# METHOXIDE SURFACE COMPLEXES ON LITHIUM

J.A. SCHULTZ, S GATES, L.G PEDERSEN and R.C. JARNAGIN

William Rand Kenan Jr. Laboratory of Chemistry, The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill,
Chapel Hill, North Carolina 27514, USA

Received 3 December 1979; in final form 29 February 1980

The He(I) photoelectron spectrum of the surface complexes formed between methanol(g) and lithium(s) is reported.
The spectrum is attributed to a methoxide species. Assignment of spectral features and identification of methoxide ad-
sorbate is supported by molecular orbital calculations.

## 1. Introduction

Methanol adducts, $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{OH} /$ substrate, have been identified by UPS to result from exposure of $\mathrm{Ni}(111)$ to $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{OH}(\mathrm{g})$ at $80 \mathrm{~K}$ [1] and from exposure of GaAs (110) at $170 \mathrm{~K}$ [2]. Above $160 \mathrm{~K} \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{OH} / \mathrm{Ni}$ decomposes to become a methoxy-like species. $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{O} / \mathrm{Ni}$, while further decomposition to leave $\mathrm{CO}$ and $\mathrm{O}$ species was reported to occur near $300 \mathrm{~K}$ [3]. Aluminum exposed to $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{OH}(\mathrm{g})$ near room temperature has also been reported to result in a $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{O} / \mathrm{Al}$ complex [4]. The electron energy loss spectra of $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{O} / \mathrm{Ni}$ obtained by Demuth and Ibach [3] have been interpreted by them to result from a conformation of the surface species with oxygen toward the nickel substrate and the $\mathrm{CO}$ direction significantly inclined to the surface normal

In this paper, we report the UPS spectrum of lithium film exposed to $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{OH}(\mathrm{g})$ at room temperature. Molecular orbital calculations support the interpretation of the spectra as resulting from methoxide-like surface complexes. No evidence for decomposition of these complexes near room temperature was found.

## 2. Experimental results

The spectrometer used to obtain the He(I) photoelectron spectrum of the surface complex and the appendage chamber for preparation of samples have been described previously [5]. Lithium was evaporated from a tantalum boat in the preparation chamber through several cycles of evaporate-cool-pump until the lithium remaining in the boat was thoroughly degassed. The lithium evaporated from the boat during these cycles functions as a getter of large area for those gases residual in the chambers that are reactive with lithium. Clean evaporated lithium films remain free of photoemission signal attributable to adsorbates for several hours during and following insertion of the film into the spectrometer chamber. Lithium surfaces at room temperature and pressures equal to and lower than $10^{-5}$ Torr have also been shown to remain uncontaminated by exposure to $\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$ [6], $\mathrm{CO}(\mathrm{g})$, and $\mathrm{N}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$ [7].

Each experiment was begun in an ambient pressure near the mid $10^{-10}$ Torr range. Additive exposures to dry methanol vapor totalling $30 \mathrm{~L}$ caused an increase of the pressure in the spectrometer to $5 \times 10^{-9}$ Torr during an experiment.

The UPS of a lithium surface at half saturation coverage following exposure to methanol is shown in fig. 1. The background attributable to photoelectrons from lithium and from secondary electron scattering has not been subtracted. The features in the spectrum retained their position, shape, and intensity relative to one another in each electron distribution curve obtained from surfaces covered from $10 \%$ of saturation to $100 \%$ of saturation. At an exposure of $11 \mathrm{~L}$ methanol, the intensity of each of the emission peaks saturated and remained constant on further exposure up to $30 \mathrm{~L}$.

### 3. Theoretical results
Models for possible surface complexes have been constructed from clusters of lithium with $CH_{3} OH$  and $CH_{3} O$ as adsorbates. Molecular orbital calcula tions for these models are necessary to help assign the observed emission bands; i.e. to indicate whether the bands of isolated $CH_{3} OH$ are simply rearranged on binding to lithium or whether dissociative binding to lithium could result in similar spectral pattern. The calculations also provide insight regarding possible con- formations of surface species.

The total energy for variations of adsorbate geom- etry were calculated with the GAUSSIAN-70 ab initio SCF LCAO procedure using the STO-3G basis set [10]. Although the STO-3G basis does not provide values of geometric parameters as reliable as do more extensive bases, this basis has successfully provided guides to molecular conformations and behavior in lithium com- pounds [11-14]. For the present models, represent- ing a limited number selected from a large number of rational possibilities, the lithium atoms have been con- strained to positions of the bcc lattice of bulk lithium($a = 3.509$ Å); thus, surface reconstruction was not considered.

A further optimization of the single bridge model was examined via tilting of the OC direction about the oxygen nucleus, while constraining it to lie in the x z plane, followed by reoptimization of $R_{e}$ . The con formation shown in fig 2a resulted. The OH direction was nearly parallel to the lithium surface. No system- atic study of OC tilt or methyl rotation was under- taken for the $H_{3} COH / Li_{10}(5,4,1)$-on top model be cause it was calculated to be significantly less tightly bound than the single bridge model.

The geometric parameters for $CH_{3} OH$ were con strained to values previously found with STO-3G for isolated $H_{3} COH$ in a staggered (trans) conformation[15]. The layers within clusters of lithium have been labeled according to a prior convention [16].

Two models are shown in fig. 2 b in which $CH_{3} O$ was the adsorbate. Orbital spacings for $CH_{3} O / Li_{9}$ (5,4)-on top and $CH_{3} O / Li_{9}(4,5)$-symmetric double bridge are compared to experiment in fig. 1. Agree- ment between calculated spacings and observed spacings are satisfactory in each case. Both experiment and theory indicate that the hydrogen has been displaced and that a $CH_{3} O$ entity is present on the surface of lithium. The calculations regarding the conformation of OH in the $CH_{3} OH / L_{10}$-single bridge model sug gest that $CH_{3} OH$ units contribute hydrogen to sub strate to liberate $H_{2}$ which is known to pump away from the surface of either lithium metal or lithium hydride [6] at room temperature.

The binding energy for the $CH_{3} O / Li_{9}(4,5)$-sym metric double bridge was calculated to be 56.9 kcaland was 1.63 times greater than calculated for $CH_{3} O$ / Li,(5,4)-on top. Because of the favorable binding energy, tilts and rotations were modeled in an abbre-viated version of the symmetric double bridge site; i.e. $CH_{3} O / Li_{5}(4,1)$-symmetric double bridge, which is contained in the more extensive model.

The geometric parameters for a conformation ofminimum energy are given in table 1 for $CH_{3} O / Li_{5}$ (4,1)-symmetric double bridge. The methyl group is oriented similarly to the one shown in fig. 2b except that the OC direction is tilted $2.6^{\circ}$ in the x z plane to ward the +x-axis. To determine this conformation, effects of tilts of the OC direction, constrained to the x z or y z planes, by $\pm 10^{\circ}$ about the y-axis or the x-axis were calculated. In each case, the methyl was orien- ted as shown in fig. 2b. The effects of similar tilts about the (+x),(+y)-bisectors and the (+x),(-y)-bi- sectors were calculated after first rotating the methyl by $45^{\circ}$ about OC in a perpendicular orientation. The effects of these distortions can be summarized bynoting that resistance to tilts of $\pm 7^{\circ}$ amounts to 0.5 kcal, while barriers to rotation of $45^{\circ}$ amount to about 4 kcal. Neither changes in CO distance nor changes in position of hydrogens relative to C were reoptimized. It is concluded that $CH_{3} O$ adsorbed in a symmetric double bridge site has the CO direction nominally perpendicular to the surface but requires only small amounts of energy for tilts or rotations.

Calculations of orbital spacings for $CH_{3} O$ bound in on top sites, in symmetric double bridge sites, or bound in distortions of these sites did not result in splittings of O2p large enough to be experimentally significant. To more strenuously test that point, calcu- lations for $CH_{3} O / Li_{5}(4,1)$-asymmetric double bridge with x-axis lithiums $3.51 \AA$ apart and y-axis lithiums4 96 A apart were done. Conformations of this type could occur on Li (110). Optimization of $R_{e}$ only re sulted in $\angle COLi$ (nearest) equal to $115.9^{\circ}$ and a split ting of only 0.12 eV. In $CH_{3} OH$ , the $\angle COH$ was $103.8^{\circ}$ and the splitting was 1.3 eV. The small splitting was consistent with the spectrum of the surface com- plex.

### 4. Summary

A comparison of the photoelectron spectra from gas phase methanol and from the surface complex formed on exposing evaporated lithium to methanol shows that features associated with the COH bonds in the methanol molecule are missing from the spectrum of the surface complex and indicate the complex to be an H₃CO/Li(surf) species. Computational results for models of this surface complex are consistent with a methoxide-like entity in which the OC direction is normal to the plane of the binding site.
