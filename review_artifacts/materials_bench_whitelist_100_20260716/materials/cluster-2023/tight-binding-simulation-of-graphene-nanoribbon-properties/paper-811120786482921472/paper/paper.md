## ABSTRACT

The geometrical structures, electronic and transport properties of the folded zigzag graphene nanoribbon were investigated by using nonequilibrium Green's functions and the density-functional theory. It was found that the electronic and transport properties are determined by the folding degree of the zigzag graphene nanoribbon. The I-V curve is nearly linear for the large folding degree. The negative differential conductance in the zigzag graphene nanoribbon disappears with the large folding degree, and this is valuable for the rational design of the graphene-based electronic devices.

## 1. Introduction

Graphene, a monolayer carbon material, has been used generally as basis of the investigation on the properties of single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) [1,2]. The different folded graphene ribbon along specific directions can be obtained by cutting the SWCNTs. Because of its unique properties, graphene has attracted many attentions after the first time being experimentally prepared [3–6]. The properties of graphene nanoribbons (GNRs) are dramatically different from the other carbon allotropes [7–11]. The zigzag GNRs (Z-GNRs) exhibit metallic properties due to the two degenerated edge states at the Fermi level, proposed in the researches without the spin polarization taken into account. However, the localized states of the electronic properties of armchair GNRs (A-GNRs) disappear, and their geometrical and electronic structures have been investigated by a number of groups [12–15].

Additionally, the electron transport properties of the graphene nanoribbons have been investigated [16–23]. Hao Ren et al. [24] have studied the negative difference resistance of the GNR by focusing on the doping effects on the electronic properties of graphene nanoribbons. And Jiong Zhang et al. [25] have investigated the free folded graphene suspended in solution by using powerful ultrasound, the effects of nearly 100 folded edges on the electronic structures of graphene nanoribbons have been reported. However, the external field is very likely appearing and affecting the design of graphene-based electronic devices. We considered the effects of the external field during the investigation of electronic and properties of graphene nanoribbons. Although the theoretical and experimental investigations of folded graphene nanoribbons is so important, the effects of the folded degree of the ZGNRs on the geometry, electronic and transport properties have not been investigated. In this paper, we investigated the geometrical structures of the different folded ZGNRs, and compared with the perfect ZGNR. In addition, the electronic and transport properties of the different folded ZGNRs were investigated too.

## 2. Calculated method

The electronic properties of relaxed structures were calculated by using the SIESTA code, [26–28] where the exchange-correlation potential is a generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the form of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof. [29] The atomic orbital basis set was a double-$\xi$ plus polarization function (DZP). The numerical integrals were performed on a real space grid with an equivalent cutoff of 200 Ry. A periodic boundary condition along z direction was applied to the ZGNR, while a vacuum region larger than $10\mathring{A}$ along the x and y direction was set to decouple the interaction between adjacent ZGNR to ensure an isolated single-layer ZGNR being considered. The supercells of the ZGNR under study contain 48 carbon atoms. All atoms were relaxed during the calculations. The edges were terminated by hydrogen to form C-H bonds for the neutralizing valences of carbon atoms. The models are shown in Fig. 1. ## 3. Results and discussion

Considering the periodic boundary condition, we have relaxed the perfect ZGNR under certain external stress along x axis. The configurations with different folding degrees are labelled as A, B and C (Fig. 1), which are relative to the external electronic field 0.5, 2.0, 4.0 V/ Å, respectively. The perfect ZGNR without external electronic field is labelled as Per configuration.

Compared with bond parameters in the Per configuration, the calculated results show that the bond lengths change in B and C configurations, while they do not in A configuration. In B configuration, the bond lengths elongated slightly by $0.003\mathring{A}$ (Fig. 2). However, in C configuration, the central C-C bonds of the folded ZGNR largely increase by up to $0.181\mathring{A}$ (Fig. 2). Table 1 shows the counterpart C-C bond lengths. The large elongation of these bonds lead to the change of the geometrical structure of the ZGNR.

Compared with the A configuration, the conduction band of B configuration is partially perturbed at 2.0 eV, the valence band generally remains unperturbed. and the mirror symmetry is broken due to the minor folding degree of the ZGNR. It has to be noted that the band width of subband $\alpha$ decreases from 1.36 to 1.31 eV, and the band width of subband $\beta$ decreases from 1.34 to 1.31 eV compared with the A configuration. The degeneracy of the C configuration disappears partially at $\Gamma$ point. The conduction band changes obviously. The two subbands $\alpha$ and $\beta$ partially separate from each other, and it is very likely attributed to the large curvature of the ZGNR. Interestingly, the band width of the subband $\alpha$ decreases from 1.36 to 0.39 eV, and the band width of the subband $\beta$ decreases from 1.34 to 1.07 eV compared with the A configuration, it may be attributed to the large folding degree of the C configuration.

Fig. 4a shows the I-V curves of the ZGNRs with different folding degrees. The results show that the transport properties of the three systems are improved by the different folding degrees compared with the Per configuration. It was found that the current of the A and B configurations is smaller than the Per configuration in range of $-1.6 \sim 1.6$ V. In $-1.6 \sim -2.0$V and $1.6 \sim 2.0$V, the current of the A and B configurations is larger than the Per configuration. This is caused by the effect of external electronic field on the geometrical structure of the ZGNR. Interestingly, the current of the C configuration is larger than the Per configuration, A and B configurations between $-1.8 \sim 2.0$V, and the I-V curves are nearly linear, which is caused by the redistribution of the extended pi orbital of the surface of ZGNR. Fig. 4b shows the differential conductance of these configurations, it was found that the negative differential conductance appears at $V_{Bias}$ close to 1.0 and $-1.0$V in the Per, A and B configurations. However, this is not observed in the C configurations, because of the large changes of the geometrical structure of the C configuration. Therefore, the electronic transport property of the ZGNR is closely related to the folding degree of the ZGNR. We propose that the technologies of changing the folding degree can be used to control the electronic transport property of the ZGNR.

In conclusion, the paper shows that the change in folding degree remarkably alters the electronic transport properties of the ZGNRs. The C–C bond lengths increase obviously with the increasing folding degree. The I-V curve of the C configuration is largely different from the other configurations, because the stretching of the C–C bond disrupts the pi orbital on extended ZGNR surface. Interestingly, the negative differential conductance exists in the Per, A, and B configurations, while it does not exist in the C configuration. This finding is valuable for the design of electronic devices. Furthermore, the method of preparing the folded ZGNRs by using external stress perpendicular to the plane of the ZGNR provides a possibility of converting the ZGNR to carbon nanotube.
