### ABSTRACT:
The advancement of crystalline solid-state oxide-ion conductors is essential for the development of clean energy applications, particularly in the context of solid-oxide fuel cells. Although numerous oxide-ion conducting materials exist, there is a pressing need for electrolyte materials that exhibit high oxide ionic conductivity at intermediate temperatures (300−600 °C). Herein, we report a fast oxide ionic conductor $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$, which possesses a hybrid perovskite identified by three-dimensional electron diffraction, neutron powder diffraction, neutron pair distribution functions, and in situ synchrotron X-ray diffraction. $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ exhibits the highest degree of M-site disordering among the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$ hexagonal perovskite family while simultaneously maintaining a high proportion of the tetrahedral coordination geometry within the structure. The high level of M-site disordering and the high tetrahedral percentage in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ facilitate oxide-ion diffusion along the $c$-axis and $ab$-plane, respectively, thereby resulting in a high bulk conductivity of 1.4 mS/cm at 500 °C, which is significantly higher than those of the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$ family members and other oxide ionic conductors. Our findings demonstrate that achieving a balance between M-site disordering and tetrahedral percentage is an effective strategy for improving the oxide ionic conductivity of $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$-type conductors.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Solid-oxide fuel cells (SOFCs) are promising green energy conversion devices that are essential for advancing the transition toward a fossil-fuel-free energy economy.$^{1−4}$ Crystalline solid-state oxide ionic conductors, which function as electrolytes in SOFCs, play a pivotal role in determining the operating temperature, durability, and output power of the SOFCs. Currently, the commercially available yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) electrolytes only exhibit sufficient oxide-ion conduction at temperatures above 700 °C.$^{5,6}$ This limitation results in high working temperatures and reduced durability, thus hampering the widespread application of the SOFC technology. In this context, there is an urgent need to develop new electrolyte materials that exhibit exceptional oxide ionic conductivity at intermediate temperatures (300−600 °C) to facilitate the practical applications of SOFCs.$^{7−9}$

Over the last two decades, substantial progress has been achieved in the discovery of fast oxide-ion-conducting materials. Several mineral structural families, including fluorite,$^{10−12}$ apatite,$^{13−15}$ melilite,$^{16−19}$ scheelite,$^{20−22}$ and perovskite,$^{23−26}$ have been discovered to exhibit significant oxide ionic conductivity at intermediate temperatures, particularly after the introduction of mobile oxygen defects (oxygen vacancies and interstitial oxygen ions) into the crystal structure via the strategy of aliovalent chemical substitution.

For instance, the Mg-doped perovskite $Na_{0.5}Bi_{0.5}TiO_{3-\delta}$ demonstrates a high oxide-ion conductivity of approximately 0.01 S/cm at 600 °C,$^{25}$ while the fluorite-like oxyhalide $Bi_{1.9}Te_{0.1}LuO_{4.05}Cl$ with a small amount of interstitial oxygen ions exhibits a conductivity of ~0.01 S/cm at 431 °C.$^{27}$ An in-depth understanding of the fast oxide-ion transport mechanism underlying these well-known structures will facilitate the high-throughput discovery of new fast ionic conductors using powerful computational approaches, which might be the developmental goal in the future.

Recently, significant oxide-ion conductivity was reported for M-site- and oxygen-deficient hexagonal perovskite derivatives, $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5}$ (M′ = Nb, V; M′′ = Mo, W).$^{28−41}$ These materials possess a so-called hybrid average structure that integrates the structural features of both 9R hexagonal perovskite $(Ba_3M_3O_9)$ and palmierite $(Ba_3M_2O_8)$ structures (see Figure 1a,b). This diversity results in the formation of pseudocubic $c'$-$\text{BaO}_{2.5}$ layers, which encompass two crystallographically independent O2 and O3 ions presenting competitive occupancy. $^{29,34}$ The oxide ionic conduction in $\text{Ba}_3\text{M}'\text{M}''\text{O}_{8.5}$ is predominantly two-dimensional (2D) along the $c'$-$\text{BaO}_{2.5}$ layers, thus linked intimately to the distributions of the O2 and O3 ions. Therefore, previous studies focused on utilizing the coordination propensity of a cation to manipulate the oxide ionic distribution, thereby achieving improved oxide ionic conduction. For instance, substituting $\text{Nb}^{5+}$ with the tetrahedral coordination geometry preferred $\text{V}^{5+}$ in $\text{Ba}_3\text{Nb}_{0.9}\text{V}_{0.1}\text{MoO}_{8.5}$ enhances the fractional occupancy of the O3 site, thereby reducing the energy barrier of oxide-ion migration along the $c'$-$\text{BaO}_{2.5}$ layers and accordingly enhancing the oxide-ion conductivity to 0.01 S/cm at $600\ {^\circ}\text{C}$, $^{34}$ which is an order of magnitude higher than that of the pristine $\text{Ba}_3\text{NbMoO}_{8.5}$. Our group has recently employed SOJT-active $\text{Ti}^{4+}$ and $\text{W}^{6+}$ cations to design the perovskite $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.4}\text{W}_{1.6}\text{O}_{8.6}$, $^{42}$ which adopts a 9R hexagonal perovskite characterized by oxygen-deficient cubic $c$-$\text{BaO}_{2.6}$ layers and "$[({\text{M}1_{0.5}\square_{0.5}})\text{M}2({\text{M}1_{0.5}\square_{0.5}})\text{O}_{8.6}]$" trimers, where $\square$ denotes cation vacancies (see Figure 1c). Owing to $\text{Ti}^{4+}$ and $\text{W}^{6+}$ cations being strong octahedral coordination geometry preferred cations, the cationic and oxygen-ionic arrangements in $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.4}\text{W}_{1.6}\text{O}_{8.6}$ differ significantly from those in $\text{Ba}_3\text{M}'\text{M}''\text{O}_{8.5}$. This enables $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.4}\text{W}_{1.6}\text{O}_{8.6}$ to capture atmospheric $\text{O}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, thereby giving rise to triple conduction (oxide ion, proton, and hole) under humid air conditions.

In this work, we report a phase-stable fast oxide ionic conductor, $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.7}\text{Mo}_{1.3}\text{O}_{8.3}$. This material is discovered by chemical unit substitution of $[\text{Mo}^{6+} + \text{Ti}^{4+}]$ for $[\text{M}^{5+} + \text{M}^{5+}]$ in palmierite $\text{Ba}_3\text{M}_2\text{O}_8$ and subsequently exploring the $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{1-x}\text{Mo}_{1+x}\text{O}_{8+x}$ solid solutions by $\text{Mo}^{6+}$-to-$\text{Ti}^{4+}$ aliovalent chemical substitution. Structural characterization revealed that $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.7}\text{Mo}_{1.3}\text{O}_{8.3}$ adopts a hybrid hexagonal perovskite structure with the highest level of M-site disordering among the $\text{Ba}_3\text{M}'\text{M}''\text{O}_{8.5 \pm \delta}$ family. Notably, this high M-site disordering coexists with the high fractional occupation of the O3 site within the palmierite layer, thereby resulting in remarkably high oxide-ion conductivity for $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.7}\text{Mo}_{1.3}\text{O}_{8.3}$ at intermediate temperatures. This high oxide ionic conductivity of $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.7}\text{Mo}_{1.3}\text{O}_{8.3}$ is comparable to that of the superb oxide ionic conductors. The structural origin of fast ionic conduction in $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.7}\text{Mo}_{1.3}\text{O}_{8.3}$ was deciphered by comprehensive structural analyses and investigations of the oxide-ion transport mechanisms.

## 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

### 2.1. Synthesis.
Solid solution samples of $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{1-x}\text{Mo}_{1+x}\text{O}_{8+x}$ ($x =$ 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, and 0.4) were prepared via traditional solid-state reactions using high-purity $\text{BaCO}_3$ (99.99%, Alfa Aesar), $\text{TiO}_2$ (99.99%, Alfa Aesar), and $\text{MoO}_3$ (99.99%, Alfa Aesar) as raw materials. All of these starting materials were heated at $500\ {^\circ}\text{C}$ to remove the absorbed moisture. These materials were weighed according to the stoichiometric ratio and then mixed thoroughly in an agate mortar and then preheated at $900\ {^\circ}\text{C}$ for 10 h. The resulting white powders were further ground and then pressed into pellets with a 13 mm diameter and then calcinated at $1050-1100\ {^\circ}\text{C}$ with a dwell time of 40 h with intermediate regrinding and pelleting.

### 2.2. Characterizations.
The phase formation and purity of the $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{1-x}\text{Mo}_{1+x}\text{O}_{8+x}$ solid solution materials were investigated with laboratory powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) using a PANalytic Empyrean Alpha-1 diffractometer (Cu $K_{\alpha1}$ radiation) equipped with a Johansson Ge(111) monochromator. Room temperature high-resolution time-of-flight neutron powder diffraction (NPD) data were collected on the GEM diffractometer at the ISIS neutron source at Rutherford Appleton Laboratory (Oxfordshire, U.K.). Samples of approximately 4 g were used for data acquisition for 4 h. Four banks of NPD data with $2\theta$ values of 35.0, 64.0, 91.5, and $153.9^\circ$ were used for Rietveld refinements, corresponding to banks 3 to 6. Room temperature neutron total scattering data were measured at the Nanoscale Ordered Materials Diffractometer (NOMAD) at the Spallation Neutron Source (SNS) located at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). $^{43}$ Approximately 200 mg of sample was loaded into the 3 mm diameter quartz capillaries for data collection for 1 h. The pair distribution function (PDF), $G(r)$, was obtained by the Fourier transformation of the neutron total scattering structure function, $S(Q)$. A $Q_{\text{max}}$ value of $32\ \text{\AA}^{-1}$ was used for PDF data reduction. High-resolution synchrotron X-ray powder diffraction (SXRPD) data were measured at the BL02B2 beamline of SPring-8. The wavelength of the incident synchrotron radiation was determined by using the lattice parameter of a $\text{CeO}_2$ standard. Variable-temperature (VT) SXRPD data were collected over the temperature range of 300-1000 K with an interval of 100 K. Rietveld refinements against four banks of NPD data and PDF small-box modeling of the crystal structure were performed with the Academic Topas V7 software. $^{44}$ The precession electron diffraction tomography (PEDT) three-dimensional (3D) electron diffraction (ED) data were collected on an FEI Tecnai G2 F20 transmission electron microscope (200 eV) equipped with a Gatan 831 camera. The precession speed was 100 Hz, and the exposure time of each flame was 2 s. All PEDT data were reduced using the PETS 2.0 software. $^{45}$

The morphology and microstructure of $\text{Ba}_3\text{Ti}_{0.7}\text{Mo}_{1.3}\text{O}_{8.3}$ were investigated with a transmission electron microscope (TEM, Talos F200S). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were performed on a Thermo Scientific Escalab 250 Xi spectrometer by using an Al $K\alpha$ radiation source. Electron binding energies were calibrated according to the C 1s peak at $E_{\text{b}} = 284.8$ eV. The alternating current (AC) electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were recorded on the Solartron 1260A impedance phase analyzer in the frequency range of $0.1-10^6$ Hz with an applied voltage of 100 mV. The pellets with a relative density of approximately 91% were used for AC impedance spectra measurements. Prior to the measurements, the parallel faces of the pellet were polished, coated with Pt paste, and calcinated at $800\ {^\circ}\text{C}$ for 1 h to form electrodes and ensure close contact between the sample surfaces and the Pt electrodes. Oxygen partial pressure $p_{\text{O}_2}$ dependent electric conductivities were measured at $600\ {^\circ}\text{C}$. $p_{\text{O}_2}$ was controlled by an $\text{O}_2$/Ar and $\text{H}_2$/Ar gas mixture and monitored by a yttria-stabilized zirconia-based oxygen sensor. The oxygen transport number, $t_{\text{O}^{2-}}$, was determined by measuring the electromotive force (EMF) of the ceramics pellet using the oxygen concentration cell method at $700\ {^\circ}\text{C}$. One side of the pellet was attached to an alumina tube with a glass adhesive seal and exposed to

pure $O_2$ flow, and the other side was exposed to dry air flow. The ultraviolet−visible (UV−Vis) light diffuse spectra were measured over the wavelength range of 200−800 nm using a Shimadzu UV-3600 spectrometer. Fine powders were spread on the surface of compressed $BaSO_4$ as a 100% reflectance standard.

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### 3.1. Phase Indentification and Rietveld Refinements.
The PXRD patterns of the $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ solid solution materials shown in Figure S1a resemble that of the oxide ionic conductor $Ba_3NbMoO_{8.5}$. This similarity suggests that $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ shares the same crystal structure as $Ba_3NbMoO_{8.5}$. Impurity phases are detected from the PXRD patterns for the compositions with $x = 0$ and 0.4. As shown in Figure S1b, both the lattice parameters and cell volume of $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ decrease with increasing $x$ within the range of $x = 0.1-0.3$, and then level off once the composition moves out of this region. These results indicate that the solubility of phase-pure $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ compositions is limited to the range of $x = 0.1-0.3$. The observed lattice contraction in the solid solution region is attributed to the substitution of smaller $Mo^{6+}$ ions (0.59 and 0.41 Å for 6- and 4-fold coordination, respectively) for the larger $Ti^{4+}$ ions (0.605 and 0.42 Å for 6-and 4-fold coordination, respectively). $^{46}$

The high-resolution XPS spectra of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ presented in Figure S2 confirm the oxidation states of $Ba^{2+}$, $Ti^{4+}$, and $Mo^{6+}$ for the cations. The TEM images of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ (Figure S3a) show smooth surfaces and highly crystalline microcrystals, which are consistent with the sharp PXRD peaks. Elemental mapping images (Figure S3b−e) reveal a homogeneous distribution of Ba, Ti, Mo, and O elements throughout the particles. The Ba/Mo/Ti ratio of 3:1.28:0.65, estimated from energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis, aligns with the nominal chemical composition. The UV−vis diffuse reflectance spectra (Figure S4a−c) for $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ show a 10 nm redshift of the absorption edge from 425.9 nm ($x = 0.1$) to 436.3 nm ($x = 0.3$). Tauc plots (insets of Figure S4) further show the band gap value decreasing from 3.10 to 2.97 eV as Mo content increases.

Note that the $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ compositions with $x = 0.1$ and 0.2 degrade slowly in air, resulting in impurity phases after several days, whereas $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ remains stable (see Figure S5). The high phase stability of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ allows for an accurate determination of its crystal structure. The 3D ED data in Figure 2a−c indicate $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ crystallizes in $R\overline{3}m$ in a hexagonal setting, with lattice parameters of $a = b$ $\approx 5.93$ and $c \approx 21.25$ Å. This crystallographic information is consistent with that of $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$ hybrid hexagonal perovskites, leading us to use the well-established structure of $Ba_3NbMoO_{8.5}$ as the starting model for Rietveld refinements. $^{28}$ In $Ba_3NbMoO_{8.5}$, $Nb^{5+}$ and $Mo^{6+}$ cations are indistinguishable due to their identical electronic configuration and very close neutron scattering lengths (7.054 and 6.715 fm, respectively). $^{47}$ However, in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$, the significant differences in X-ray scattering form factors and neutron scattering lengths (-3.438 and 6.715 fm for Ti and Mo, $^{47}$ respectively) enable precise determination of their fractional occupations over the two crystallographically independent sites, M1 (6c position) and M2 (split 6c position), using linear soft constraints. Our preliminary Rietveld refinements with those constraints converged smoothly, resulting in a Mo/Ti cooccupation for both M1 and M2 sites. Moreover, the site occupation factor (sof.) of O1 converged to unity, whereas the remaining O2 and O3 sites are partially occupied. Consequently, the sof. value of O1 was fixed to unity, and those of O2 and O3 ions were constrained according to the nominal formula during the final refinement process. Final Rietveld refinements against four banks of NPD data proceeded stably and converged smoothly, giving reliable overall residual factors ($R_{wp} = 2.99\%$ and $R_p = 2.35\%$) and physically reasonable crystallographic parameters. The final Rietveld refinement plots for the NPD data of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ are depicted in Figure 2d,e and Figure S6a,b. Crystallographic data, atomic positions, thermal displacement factors, and sof. values are summarized in Table 1 and Table S1, while selected interatomic bond lengths are listed in Table S2.

To further validate the average structure model of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ obtained from NPD, we conducted real-space Rietveld refinements against the nPDF data. As shown in Figure 3, all the nPDF peaks in the medium- and long-range (6−30 Å) were well reproduced by the average structure model, achieving a reliable weight factor of 12.2%. However, the fitting to the local structure (1−6 Å) is notably inadequate, particularly evidenced by the large discrepancy for the first sharp peak around 1.81 Å, which corresponds to the Mo/Ti−O pairs. This inadequacy arises because the small box method fails to eliminate the structurally unreasonable short O2−O3 pairs in the range of 1.4−2.1 Å, which overlap with the first Mo/Ti−O pairs during the low $r$-range refinement process. This inadequate fitting to the low $r$-range nPDF in turn reinforces the high disordering of the oxygen sublattice in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$. Therefore, further reverse Monte Carlo (RMC) refinement of the nPDF data using a large box model is necessary to gain insight into the local atomic arrangements of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$.

Hence, ${\rm Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is identified as a member of the ${\rm Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}}$ family and is distinguished by its oxygen content. ${\rm Mo^{6+}}$ and ${\rm Ti^{4+}}$ cations preferentially occupy the outer octahedral cavity (M1 site) in the “${\rm M_2\square O_{8.3}}$” trimer, with 82.5 and 71.1% of ${\rm Mo^{6+}}$ and ${\rm Ti^{4+}}$ cations distributed on the M1 sites. The relatively lower occupancy ratio of ${\rm Ti^{4+}}$ can be attributed to its stronger propensity in octahedral coordination geometry, as compared to the 4-fold coordination geometry preferred by ${\rm Mo^{6+}}.^{48}$ Moreover, bond valence sum (BVS) values calculations reveal that Mo1 (BVS = 5.54) experiences a slightly underbonded coordination environment, whereas Mo2 is severely underbonded (BVS = 3.84).$^{49}$ Similar coordination environments (BVS = 3.72 and 2.90 for Til and Ti2, respectively) are also observed for ${\rm Ti^{4+}}$ ions. These findings suggest that the preferential occupation of the M1 site in ${\rm Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is governed by the coordination environment requirements for ${\rm Mo^{6+}}$ and ${\rm Ti^{4+}}$ cations.

The fractional occupancy of the M1 site in ${\rm Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is considerably greater than that observed in the deficient 9R perovskite ${\rm Ba_3Ti_{0.4}W_{1.6}O_{8.6}}$ but smaller than that of the ${\rm Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5}}$ family members (see Figure 4b). We proposed the general formula ${\rm Ba_3(M1_{1-s}\square_s)_2(M2_{2s}\square_{1-2s})O_{8.5\pm\delta}}$ to characterize the M-site disordering. Based on the parameter $s$, the ${\rm Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}}$ family members can be divided into two subcategories: (I) M-site ordered structure with $s=0$, and (II) M-site disordered structure with $0<s\leq1/3$, where $s=1/3$ means completely disordering. According to this classification, ${\rm Ba_3VMoO_{8.5}}$ and ${\rm Ba_3VWO_{8.5}}$ with $s=0$ are considered as M-site ordered, whereas ${\rm Ba_3NbMoO_{8.5}}$ ($s=0.114$),$^{28}$ ${\rm Ba_3NbWO_{8.5}}$ ($s=0.185$),$^{31}$ and ${\rm Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ ($s=0.215$) are classified as M-site disordered. Moreover, the $s$ value of ${\rm Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is also larger than those of ${\rm Ba_3Nb_{1+x}Mo_{1-x}O_{8.5-x/2}}$ ($s<0.114$) and ${\rm Ba_3Nb_{1-x}V_xMoO_{8.5}}$ ($s<0.114$) solid solutions.$^{34,54}$ These results manifest that ${\rm Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ exhibits the highest level of disordering

There is a negative correlation between the occupation of the O3 site and the degree of M-site disordering ($s$) in the previously reported $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}$ family members (see Figure S7and Figure 4b). Impressively, $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ with the highest degree of M-site disordering still possesses a high O3 site occupation, only slightly smaller than that in ordered $Ba_3VMoO_{8.5}$. The high fractional occupation of O3 in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ can be attributed to the relatively high Mo content and the associated high occupancy of Mo on the M1 site, as $Mo^{6+}$ is a strong tetrahedral coordination geometry preferred cation. Consequently, the proportion of tetrahedral units in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ reaches a high value of 76.2%, which is slightly lower than that in $Ba_3VMoO_{8.5}$ (~86.4%) and significantly higher than that of $Ba_3VWO_{8.5}$ (~63.9%), $Ba_3NbMoO_{8.5}$ (~41.9%), and $Ba_3NbWO_{8.5}$ (~5.7%).$^{35,36,39,42,50}$ These findings suggest that the geometry of the palmierite-like layers in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ is dominated by the $(Mo/Ti)O_4$ tetrahedra.

In $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$, the M1-centered octahedra have two groups of M1−O bonds, manifested by three equal M1−O2 bonds with a longer length of 2.201 Å and three other equal M1−O1 bonds with a shorter length of 1.844 Å (see Table S2). The longer M1−O2 bond is in line with the lower fractional occupation of the O2 site (0.238) compared to a unity occupation of the O1 site. Such a bond length distribution indicates a significant off-center displacement of the M1 cation along the c-axis toward the O1−O1−O1 face of the $M1O_6$ octahedron. The out-of-center displacement of the M1 cation is estimated to be 0.31 Å, which is the largest among the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}$ family (see Table 2). The out-of-center displacement of M1 primarily results from the second-order Jahn−Teller distortion (SOJT) of the $Mo^{6+}/Ti^{4+}$ cations, which possess a $d^0$ electronic configuration. It is well-known that the SOJT distortion level of $d^0$-cation is linked to the formal charge and ionic radius; that is, a cation with a higher charge and smaller ionic radius will create a greater level of polyhedral distortion. Consequently, the highly charged $Mo^{6+}$ ($r = 0.59$ Å) is a stronger SOJT distortion cation compared to $Nb^{5+}$ ($r = 0.64$ Å), $V^{5+}$ ($r = 0.54$ Å), $Ti^{4+}$ ($r = 0.605$ Å), and $W^{6+}$ ($r = 0.60$ Å).$^{46}$ Hence, the significant displacement of M1 in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ should be attributed to the high occupation of $Mo^{6+}$ on the M1 site and its strong SOJT distortion effect. As a result, the octahedral distortion of $M1O_6$ is expected to be the most pronounced among the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}$ family, as corroborated by the largest bond length distortion index of 0.091 (see Table 2). In contrast, the M2-centered octahedron exhibits a considerably greater distortion than the $M1O_6$ octahedron, evidenced by a larger bond length distortion index of 0.133 and a larger off-center displacement of 0.465 Å.$^{51,52}$ However, this $M2O_6$-octahedral distortion is moderate among the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}$ family (see Table 2). It is important to note that the distortion degree of $M2O_6$ is influenced not merely by the SOJT effect of the M2 cations but also by the fractional occupancy of the M2 site and the cation−cation statistical repulsion arising from the M1−M2 short contacts. These mutually competing factors eventually lead to a moderate distortion degree of $M2O_6$ compared to the other members of the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}$ family. Moreover, the $M1O_4$ tetrahedra are also distorted, manifested by one shorter M1−O3 bond (1.631 Å) and three longer M1−O1 bonds (1.844 Å).

3.3. Variable-Temperature Structural Evolution. Variable-temperature synchrotron X-ray diffraction (SXRD) was carried out to gain insight into the temperature-driven *in situ* structural evolution of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$. The SXRD data measured over the temperature range of 300−1000 K are given in Figure S8. The temperature dependences of cell dimensions ($a$ and $c$) and cell volume ($V$) are shown in Figure 5a. In the temperature range of 300−600 K, the lattice dimensions and cell volume increase linearly, while the $c$-axis changes more steeply. Further heating causes an expansion of the $c$-axis to follow the previous trend, whereas the $a$-axis shows a significantly steeper increase above 600 K. This is likely due to structural modification, with predominant structural rearrangements occurring within the $ab$-plane. Structural rearrangements involving M2-to-M1 and O2-to-O3 migrations have been previously confirmed in isostructural $Ba_3NbMo_{1-x}W_xO_{8.5}$ compositions, where these ionic redistributions occur above 500 K and lead to similar steeper behaviors in lattice expansion.$^{29,30}$ To shed light on the possible structural rearrangements for $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ above 600 K, particularly the redistribution of M cations, we attempted Rietveld refinements against the SXRD data. However, our current SXRD data result in very large errors for the fractional occupancies of the M2 sites, which hindered us from obtaining an accurate structure model. Nevertheless, we can definitely ascertain that the M2 site is almost vacant at 1000 K, thereby confirming the structural transformation from a hybrid structure to a palmierite structure. The Rietveld refinement plots of the SXRD data collected at 1000 K are displayed in Figure 5b. The high-temperature palmierite-like structure of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ is depicted in the inset of Figure 5b.

3.4. Oxide Ionic Conduction. The Nyquist plots of impedance spectra (see Figure 6a) for $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ measured at 300 °C under dry air conditions reveal a compressed semicircle in the high and intermediate frequency ranges, accompanied by a pronounced inclined line in the low-frequency range (<100 Hz). The inclined line arises from the electrode response due to the formation of blocking electrodes. Under $O_2$ atmospheres, the electrode response becomes less resistive, as evidenced by a curvature of the inclined line (see Figure S9). These results provide strong evidence for a high oxide ionic mobility in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$.

This enhancement in ionic conductivity is plausibly attributed to the increasing amount of interstitial oxygen ions within the oxygen-deficient $BaO_{2+x}$ sublayers. A subsequent decline in ionic conductivity was observed for $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$, particularly at temperatures lower than 500 °C.

A significant slope change in the Arrhenius plots is observed for all $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ compositions above 500 °C, associated with a significant reduction in activation energy ($E_a$) from 0.74−0.98 to 0.44−0.65 eV. A very recent study revealed that $Ba_3Ti_{0.9}Mo_{1.1}O_{8.1}$ adopts a palmierite structure with an entirely vacant M2 site. The decrease in $E_a$ value for $Ba_3Ti_{0.9}Mo_{1.1}O_{8.1}$ at temperatures above 500 °C is attributed to the dynamic transition for the conductive oxide ions.⁵³ Notably, $Ba_3Ti_{0.9}Mo_{1.1}O_{8.1}$ and $Ba_3Ti_{0.8}Mo_{1.2}O_{8.2}$ exhibit similar conducting behavior across the entire temperature range, with their $E_a$ values (~0.75 eV) at temperatures below 500 °C being significantly lower than that of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ (~1.0 eV). These observations suggest that $Ba_3Ti_{0.8}Mo_{1.2}O_{8.2}$ should also adopt a palmierite-like structure rather than the hybrid structure exhibited by $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$.

In contrast, the observed inflection change in the Arrhenius plot for $BaTi_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ is inextricably linked to the structural modifications, particularly the ratio of $MO_4$ tetrahedra to $MO_6$ octahedra within the structure, as well-documented for $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}^{29,31,54}$ Specifically, the slope of the Arrhenius plot correlates with the rate of the change in tetrahedral percentage, that is, a slower increase in tetrahedral percentage corresponds to a more gradual slope in the Arrhenius plots (see Figure S10).²⁹ Therefore, the observed change in the slope of Arrhenius plots of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ arises from a deceleration in the rate of increase of tetrahedral units within the structure rather than the onset of a structural rearrangement as temperature increases from low to high. It is noteworthy that the comparable oxide ionic conductivities of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ and $Ba_3Ti_{0.8}Mo_{1.2}O_{8.2}$ above 500 °C imply that these two ionic conductors possess similar proportions of tetrahedral units within their structures.

As shown in Figure 6c, $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ exhibits the highest oxide ionic conductivity among the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5}$ family. ature. $^{31}$ Such a high oxide ionic conductivity for $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is undoubtedly correlated to its high fractional occupation of the tetrahedral O3 site and increased M-site disordering, which will be discussed in detail later. At $600\ {^\circ}\text{C}$, the conductivity of $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is comparable to other perovskite-related leading oxide ionic conductors, such as 3C-type perovskite $\mathrm{Na_{0.5}Bi_{0.5}TiO_{3-\delta},^{25}}$ Dion–Jacobson-type layered perovskite $\mathrm{CsBi_2Ti_2NbO_{10-\delta},^{55}}$ and hexagonal perovskite derivatives $\mathrm{Ba_7Nb_4MoO_{20}}$ and $\mathrm{Ba_7Ta_{3.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{20.15}.^{56,57}}$

Figure 6d shows the Arrhenius plots of the total conductivities of $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ measured under various atmospheres. The nearly identical total conductivities over the measured temperature range indicate that $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ exhibits a negligible electronic conduction. To further investigate the electric properties of $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$, the total conductivities were measured at $600\ {^\circ}\text{C}$ under various oxygen partial pressures ($p\mathrm{O_2}$). As shown in Figure S11, the total conductivity of $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is independent of $p\mathrm{O_2}$ in the high $p\mathrm{O_2}$ region of $1\times10^{-15}-1$ atm. However, a further decrease in $p\mathrm{O_2}$ leads to a sharp increase in total conductivity, probably due to the chemical reduction of $\mathrm{Mo^{6+}}$ and $\mathrm{Ti^{4+}}$ and the subsequent introduction of $n$-type electronic conduction. Nevertheless, our XPS spectroscopy measurements did not detect any low valent $\mathrm{Mo^{5+}}$ and $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$ species (see Figure S12), which might be ascribed to reoxidation when the samples were recovered to the ambient air conditions. To further verify the charge carriers and their contributions to total conductivity, we performed electromotive force (EMF) measurements at 700 $^\circ\text{C}$. The calculated and experimental EMF values are 32.71 and 31.00 mV, respectively, giving an oxygen transport number value of $\sim$0.95. This result suggests that the electronic contribution to the total conduction is negligible, and $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ is thus a pure oxide ionic conductor. Moreover, $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}}$ shows high phase stability under dry conditions, and no trace of secondary phases was detected on their PXRD patterns after conductivity measurements (see Figure S13).

3.5. Oxide Ionic Conduction Mechanism. The bond valence site energy (BVSE) method $^{58-61}$ has shown robust validation in elucidating the conduction pathways for the $\mathrm{Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}}$ family members, aligning with the conducting mechanisms identified by combining *in situ* neutron diffraction and maximum-entropy method (MEM). $^{33,34,39,41}$ On account of the highly disordered structure of $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$ and the aim of conducting comparative analyses, BVSE calculations were thus performed to unravel the structural origins of superior oxide ionic conductivity observed in $\mathrm{Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}}$. Figure 7a,b depicts the two-dimensional (2D) migration pathways of the oxide-ion diffusion along the oxygen-disordered $c'$-$\mathrm{BaO_{2.3}}$ layer. These 2D pathways involve all of the crystallographically independent O2 and O3 ions, in accordance with previous investigations of the $\mathrm{Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}}$ family. The transport of oxide ions between the O2 and O3 sites necessitates traversing two saddle points, both of which are in close proximate to the O2 site. The first saddle point (S1) corresponds to the curved O2 $\leftrightarrow$ O3 hopping along the $ab$-plane, which is associated with an energy barrier of $E1=0.249$ eV (see Figure 7b,c). The second saddle point (S2) features a significantly lower energy barrier of $E2=0.068$ eV, corresponding to the relaxation of oxide ions along the $c$-axis due to the undulations of the O2/O3 sites within the $c'$-$\mathrm{BaO_{2.3}}$ layers (see Figure 7a,c). Given the relatively higher $E1$ energy barrier, the 2D oxygen migration is undoubtedly dominated by the distributions of O2/O3 ions within the $c'$-$\mathrm{BaO_{2.3}}$ layers. A plot of the $E1$ energy barrier as a function of the fractional occupation of O3 is shown in Figure 8a. Obviously, the $E1$ value is inversely correlated to the fractional occupation of the O3 in the $\mathrm{Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5\pm\delta}}$ structures. This observation clearly indicates that a greater occupation of the O3 site and the associated higher percentage of $\mathrm{M1O_4}$ units can facilitate oxide-ion diffusion, thereby resulting in a lower $E1$ energy barrier. Therefore, the 2D oxide ionic conduction is intrinsically linked to the fractional occupation of the highly disordered O3 site.

Three-dimensional (3D) pathways that link the 2D pathway segments via the $M2O_6$ units can be envisaged in Figure S14. The 3D pathway involves the additional fully occupied O1 site, which is located outside of the $c'-BaO_{2.3}$ layers. The BVSE energy barriers of 0.698 and 1.284 eV for the 3D O2/O3 $\leftrightarrow$ O1 and O1 $\leftrightarrow$ O1 pathways, respectively (see Figure 7c), are markedly higher than those of the 2D O2 $\leftrightarrow$ O3 pathways, indicating that the oxide ionic conduction of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ is predominantly two-dimensional along the palmierite-like layers. Figure 8b demonstrates a robust linear relationship between the $E1$ energy barrier and the oxide-ion conducting activation energy $(E_a)$ for the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$ conductors, firmly reinforcing this assertion. $^{31,34,36}$

However, the contribution of $c$-axis oxide-ion diffusion should not be overlooked. Recent *ab initio* molecular dynamics studies on the hexagonal perovskite derivative $Ba_7Nb_4MoO_{20}$ have identified the role of a fully occupied oxygen site outside the conducting palmierite-like layers in facilitating oxide ionic conduction. $^{62}$ The fully occupied lattice oxygen sites in the classic melilite structure also participate in the long-range ionic migration via a cooperative knock-on mechanism. $^{16}$ Consequently, the fully occupied oxygens in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ are expected to contribute to its high oxide ionic conductivity, particularly at temperatures below 500 °C. As shown in Figure S15, there is a clear trend of increasing oxide ionic conductivity with an increase in the M-site disordering degree. This observation indicates that a high M2-site occupation promotes oxide-ion migration. It is well-established that oxide ionic transport is assisted by interpolyhedral oxygen exchange. $^{63}$ Hence, an increased M2-site occupation enhances the long-range O1 $\leftrightarrow$ O1 hopping along the distorted $M2O_6$ octahedra, forming 3D migration pathways at low temperatures. In contrast, O1 $\leftrightarrow$ O1 pathways are absent in the M2-site entirely vacant $Ba_3V(Mo/W)O_{8.5}$, which in turn reinforces the critical role of M2-site occupancy in facilitating ionic diffusion along the $c$-axis. $^{36,39}$ As a result, the activation energy $(E_a = 0.98$ eV) for $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ at temperatures below 500 °C is significantly lower than that of $Ba_3NbMoO_{8.5}$ ($E_a = 1.19$ eV), $^{34}$ $Ba_3NbWO_{8.5}$ ($E_a = 1.94$ eV), $^{31}$ and $Ba_3VWO_{8.5}$ ($E_a = 1.13$ eV). $^{36}$ Altogether, the above results highlight that, alongside the tetrahedral percentage, the degree of M-site disordering is another critical factor significantly influencing oxide ionic conductivity. Hence, we can speculate that achieving a balance between M-site disordering and the tetrahedral percentage is an effective strategy for improving the low-temperature oxide ionic conductivity of the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$-type conductors.

## 4. CONCLUSIONS

In this work, a phase-stable fast oxide ionic conductor $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ was discovered by careful exploration of the $Ba_3Ti_{1-x}Mo_{1+x}O_{8+x}$ solid solutions. $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ was identified to be a member of the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$ hybrid perovskite family. Owing to the strong propensity of tetrahedral coordination geometry for $Mo^{6+}$ and the strong preference for octahedral coordination geometry for $Ti^{4+}$, a high tetrahedral percentage as well as a high degree of M-site disordering were simultaneously achieved in $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$. These structural factors enable $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ to exhibit a high oxide ionic conductivity at intermediate temperatures, outperforming other members of the $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$ family. Our findings highlight that achieving an optimal balance between the M-site disordering degree and the tetrahedral percentage is an effective strategy for designing $Ba_3M'M''O_{8.5 \pm \delta}$-type oxide-ion conductors with improved conductivity. Moreover, stabilizing the palmierite-like high-temperature modification of $Ba_3Ti_{0.7}Mo_{1.3}O_{8.3}$ at room temperature via chemical substitution is warranted to further enhance the oxide ionic conductivity.
