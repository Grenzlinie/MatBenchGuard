# THEORETICAL STUDY OF THE MOLECULAR IONS $SiH_{5}^{-}$ AND $SiH_{3}^{-}$

Frerich KEIL and Reinhart AHLRICHS
Institut für Physikalische Chemie und Elektrochemie, Universitat Karlsruhe, D 7500 Karlsruhe, Germany

Received 28 November 1974

Ab initio HF and CI calculations were performed to determine the equilibrium geometry of $SiH_{5}^{-}$ and $SiH_{3}^{-}$ , the barrier for internal rotation $(SiH_{5}^{-})$ and inversion $(SiH_{3}^{-})$ and the stability of $SiH_{5}^{-}$ and further to study the effect of electron correlation on reaction energies. The gaussian-type basis included d and f functions on Si and a p set on H. The $D_{3 h}$ structure of $SiH_{5}^{-}$ is lower in energy than the $C_{4 v}$ structure by 2.9 (3.2) kcal / mol (corresponding HF results in parentheses). $SiH_{3}^{-}$ has $C_{3 v}$ structure, the inversion barrier computed is 26.2(27.3) kcal / mol. $SiH_{5}^{-}$ turns out to be stable with respect to $SiH_{4}+H^{-}$ by 20.3(13.8) kcal / mol, but it is unstable with respect to $SiH_{3}^{-}+H_{2}$ by 6.3(5.6) kcal / mol. These results show that electron correlation has a small effect on barriers of inversion $(SiH_{3}^{-})$ or pseudorotation $(SiH_{5}^{-})$ , but may have a pronounced effect on reaction energies even if all systems involved have closed shells. The correlation energy contributions are analyzed in terms of intrapair and interpair terms in order to get a better understanding of the influence of correlation on reaction and activation energies.

## 1. Introduction
Recently Wilhite and Spialter [1] have published a theoretical study of the reaction
$$\mathrm{SiH}_{4}+\mathrm{H}^{-}-\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-} \quad (1)$$
by means of CNDO/2 and ab initio HF computations. These authors found that $SiH_{5}^{-}$ has $D_{3 h}$ equilibrium geometry. The reaction energy of (1), not including zero point vibrations, was obtained as 16.95 kcal/mol on the HF level.

In the case that all compounds occurring in a reaction have a closed shell structure, like in (1), it is often assumed that electron correlation is not important because of the approximate additivity of correlation energy in closed shell systems [1,2]. This assumption has in fact been supported by Dedieu et al. [3] who found a small effect (4.1 and 0.0 kcal/mol) of correlation on activation energies of the nucleophilic substitutions
$$\mathrm{H}^{-}+\mathrm{CH}_{4} \quad -\quad \mathrm{CH}_{4}+\mathrm{H}^{-}.$$

$$\Gamma^{-}+\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{~F} \to \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{~F}+\mathrm{F}^{-}$$

For the dimerization of $BH_{3}$
$$2 \mathrm{BH}_{3} \to \mathrm{B}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{6}. \quad (2)$$
(a reaction which also involves closed shell states only) it has, however, been shown that correlation effects cannot be neglected if reaction energies are to be computed with an accuracy of a few kcal/mol. In a recent paper by one of us [4], the following $\Delta E_{f}$ values forreaction (2) have been obtained:

HF level:
$\Delta E_{f}=-20.7 kcal / mol .$
including correlation: $\Delta E_{f}=-36.6 kcal / mol$ .

The latter value is in fact in good agreement with a recent experimental value: $\Delta E_{f}=-34.8 kcal / mol$ [5].

It is the main purpose of the present paper to investigate the effect of electron correlation on $\Delta E_{f}$ of reaction (1) and to give an interpretation of the results.

Besides the reaction (1) we have also investigated the decomposition
$$\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \to \mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}. \quad (3)$$
which required a detailed study of $SiH_{3}^{-}$ in order to determine the equilibrium geometry and energy of this ion. It turns out that $SiH_{5}^{-}$ is not stable with respect to $SiH_{3}^{-}+H_{2}$ . This does not exclude the possibility that the potential surface of $SiH_{5}^{-}$ has a local mini

mum for the $D_{3 h}$ geometry, i.e., that $SiH_{5}^{-}$ may form a short-lived complex.

### 2. Method
For the treatment of electron correlation we start from a standard HF computation. The canonical HF MOs are then localized according to the method of Boys [6]. Electron correlation is accounted for by a CI treatment. Since only doubly substituted configu- rations contribute in first order of perturbation theo- ry, we expect to account for the bulk of correlation effects by just performing a CI with the Ansatz (4), in an obvious notation,

$$
\psi^{(2)}=c_{\text {III }} \psi_{\text {III }}+\sum_{I I} \sum_{i^{\prime} \mu} c_{i^{\prime}}^{\nu \mu} \phi_{I I}^{\iota \nu \mu}.
\tag{4}
$$

which yields the energy

$$
E^{(2)}=E_{\text {HF }}+\epsilon^{(2)}.
\tag{5}
$$

In the present work we have included the valence shell correlation only.

In order to reduce the dimension of the CI equa- tion we choose the orbitals $\varphi_{\nu}$ and $\varphi_{\mu}$ by which the occupied HF MOs $\varphi_{I}$ and $\varphi_{J}$ are replaced as the pair natural orbitals (PNOs) of the corresponding pair func- tion, i.e., we perform a so called PNO CI computation. For the details and more technical matters of this method, which shall not concern us here, the reader is referred to the literature [7,8].

Although the Ansatz (4) accounts for the bulk of correlation effects it is still somewhat unsatisfactory since $e^{(2)}$ has an incorrect dependence on the number of electrons [4,8b], which may be quite serious in the treatment of reaction energies. In order to demon- strate this shortcoming we consider a reaction $A+B \to$  AB, where A, B and AB have closed shell states. Let $\psi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)}, E_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)}, \varphi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}, E_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}, \psi_{\mathrm{AB}}^{(2)}$ and $E_{\mathrm{AB}}^{(2)}$ denote the corre sponding wave functions and energies obtained from the Ansatz (4). One may then calculate the reaction or activation energy $\Delta \tilde{E}^{(2)}$ according to

$$
\Delta \tilde{E}^{(2)}=E_{\mathrm{AB}}^{(2)}-\left(E_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)}+E_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}\right).
\tag{6}
$$

Proceeding this way one treats the systems A + B and AB on an unequal footing. This is due to the fact that the energy $E_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)}+E_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}$ corresponds to the wavefunc- tion $\psi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)} \psi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}$, since

$$
\left\langle\psi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)} \psi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}|H| \psi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)} \psi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}\right\rangle=E_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)}+E_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}.
$$

The product $\psi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)} \psi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}$ contains, however, besides the double substitutions all quadruples arising from simul- taneous double substitutions on system A and B. As the latter are neglected in $E_{\mathrm{AB}}^{(2)}$ one can in general not expect that eq. (5) yields reliable reaction energies. In order to treat AB and A + B in a comparable manner we can consistently neglect the quadruple substitu- tions and perform a CI with doubles only for the sys- tems A + B at sufficiently large internuclear distance. If we denote the resulting energy by $E_{\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}$, we then compute $\Delta E^{(2)}$ from

$$
\Delta E^{(2)}=E_{\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}-E_{\mathrm{AB}}^{(2)}.
\tag{7}
$$

In the treatment of reaction (2) it has been shown that the deviation between (6) and (7) amounts to 6.8 kcal/ mol since in this case $E_{\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}-\left(E_{\mathrm{A}}^{(2)}+E_{\mathrm{B}}^{(2)}\right)=6.8 \mathrm{kcal} /$ mol, corresponding roughly to $43 \%$ of the contribu tion of electron correlation to $\Delta E_{r}$ of reaction (2).

Another way to avoid the problem just discussed is to include in an approximate but consistent way the quadruple and higher substitutions which are due to the unlinked clusters arising from the doubles. This is done in the coupled electron pair approximation (CEPA) proposed by Meyer [7], see also [8b]. In thiscase one has actually (see, e.g., ref. [4]):

$$
E_{\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{CEPA}}=E_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{CEPA}}+E_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{CEPA}}.
\tag{8}
$$

and $\Delta E^{\mathrm{CEPA}}$ :

$$
\Delta E^{\mathrm{CEPA}}=E_{\mathrm{AB}}^{\mathrm{CEPA}}-\left(E_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{CEPA}}+E_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{CEPA}}\right).
\tag{9}
$$

should be close to the results from eq. (7).

### 3. Basis set considerations
For the present computations we used an extended set of contracted gaussian lobe functions. p-, d- and f- type functions were constructed as described in ref.[9].

For silicon we started with a Huzinaga [10] (11s,7p) set contracted to a [7,4] set namely (5.6×1/4,3×1). This basis was then augmented by two com- plete d sets with $\eta=0.69$ and $\eta=0.23$ and an f set

with $\eta=0.48$.

For hydrogen we used a Huzinaga $5 s(311)$ and a p set with $\eta=0.4$. This basis set for $\mathrm{Si}$ and $\mathrm{H}$ was deter mined in optimizing the $\mathrm{HF}+$ valence shell correla tion energy of $\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$. It was used for $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}\left(\mathrm{D}_{3 \mathrm{~h}}\right.$ and $\mathrm{C}_{4 \mathrm{v}}$ geometry) and for the $\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$ fragment in the $\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$ $-\mathrm{H}^{-}$computation. For the $\mathrm{H}^{-}$in the latter case we augmented the basis by an additional smooth $\mathrm{s}$ func tion with $\eta=0.03$ and also used $\eta=0.2$ (instead of $\eta=0.4$ ) for the p set on $\mathrm{H}$. These changes are neces sary to get an accurate description of $\mathrm{H}^{-}$which has a rather spread out electron distribution. Our $\mathrm{HF}$ and total energies for $\mathrm{H}^{-}$are $E_{\mathrm{HF}}\left(\mathrm{H}^{-}\right)=-0.4875$ au and $E^{(2)}=-0.5211$ au which may be compared to the corresponding exact values of $-0.48793$ au (HF limit) [11] and $-0.52775$ au [12], respectively. Wilhite and Spialter obtained $E_{\mathrm{HF}}\left(\mathrm{H}^{-}\right)=-0.4733$ which is about $9 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$ above the HF limit whereby one overesti mates $\Delta E_{\mathrm{f}}$ of reaction (1) by roughly the same amount.

The additional smooth s functions are of little im portance in $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$. Addition of these functions at the axial $\mathrm{H}$ atoms in the $\mathrm{D}_{3 \mathrm{~h}}$ structure lowers the $\mathrm{HF}$ en ergy by $0.1 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$ only which is clearly negligible. The negative charge in $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$is more or less delocalized over the whole molecule and the $5 \mathrm{~s}$ basis is obviously flexible enough to give a proper description of a slight ly charged hydrogen.

For the $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$computation (where one also expects a rather spread out MO as discussed in the treatment of $\mathrm{CH}_{3}^{-}$[13]) we added an additional $\mathrm{s}$ and $\mathrm{p}$ set with either $\eta=0.03$ and further a $\mathrm{d}_{z^{2}}$ function with $\eta=0.1$ at the silicon atom. In the $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ computation required in the discussion of reaction (3) we used a p set with $\eta=0.65$ (instead of $\eta=0.4$ ), which is optimal for $\mathrm{H}_{2}$.

We obtain the following barriers $\Delta E_{B}$ for the Berry pseudorotation in $SiH_{5}^{-}$:

HF: $\quad \Delta E_{B}=3.24\ \text{kcal/mol}, \tag{10}$

PNOCI: $\quad \Delta E_{B}=2.72\ \text{kcal/mol}, \tag{11}$

CEPA: $\quad \Delta E_{B}=2.87\ \text{kcal/mol}. \tag{12}$

$$\text { PNO CI: } \Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}=27.1 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol} \text {. } \quad (19)$$

$$\text { CLPA: } \quad \Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}=26.2 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol} \text {. } \quad (20)$$

Rauk et al. [18] quoted an inversion barrier of 30.6 kcal/mol without giving details of their computation. The deviation from our result could be due to the fact that these authors employed a too restricted basis(see, e.g., the discussion in ref. [13]).

We first note the small effect of correlation on the barrier which is analogous to the results for the pseudo- rotation for $SiH_{5}^{-}$ , or the corresponding barriers for $PH_{3}, CH_{3}^{-}, NH_{3}, OH_{3}^{+}[17,19]$ . The inversion barrier in $SiH_{3}^{-}$ is about 8.8 kcal / mol smaller than for $PH_{3}$  $(\Delta E_{i}=35 kcal / mol$ , refs. [19,20]). Although the inver sion barriers in the second period are considerablylarger than in the first period $(CH_{3}^{-}: \Delta E_{i}=1-2 kcal /$  mol, $NH_{3}: 5-6 kcal / mol[2,17])$ we find the same trend in both cases: the barrier decreases in going from the neutral system to the negative ion

The correlation energy of $SiH_{3}^{-}$ is 5.4 kcal / mol larger than for $SiH_{4}$ although both molecules are not too different in electronic structure. The reason for this is essentially the increased differential overlap be- tween the rather diffuse lone pair and the Si-H bond pairs in $SiH_{3}^{-}$ which results in an increased interpaircorrelation energy. Due to the smaller $\Varangle HSiH(97.6^{\circ}$  in $SiH_{3}^{-}$ versus $109.5^{\circ}$ in $SiH_{4}$ ) one also finds an in crease in the interbond interaction which appears to

be less important, however.

With reference to reaction (3) we obtain the follow-ing results for the reaction energy $\Delta E_{\mathrm{f}}$:

HF:
$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{f}}=+5.6 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}. \tag{21}$$

CEPA:
$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{f}}=+6.3 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}. \tag{22}$$

For this reaction we find an almost meaningless influ-ence of correlation effects, in contrast to the results for reaction (1), see eqs. (13)-(15). In both reactions we increase the number of next neighbour bond inter-actions which trends to stabilize the $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$complex, but due to the unusually large correlation energy of $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$, as just discussed, there is no net effect on $\Delta E_{\mathrm{f}}$ for reaction (3). This example shows again that elec-tron correlation has to be included if energy differ-ences are to be computed with chemical accuracy (an error of a few kcal/mol), since it is too dangerous to estimate the effect of correlation by analogies to relat-ed molecules.

Although $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$is not stable with respect to (3), there is still the possibility that the potential surface has a local minimum for the $D_{3 h}$ geometry. It has been shown by Hoffmann et al. [21] that two sym-metry allowed paths exist along which an $\mathrm{AB}_{5}$ mole-cule, with either $D_{3 h}$ or $C_{4 v}$ symmetry may decompose into $\mathrm{AB}_{3}+\mathrm{B}_{2}$. For this reason one expects either no or at most a rather small barrier for this case.

The endothermicity of reaction (3) is, however, smaller than for the corresponding decomposition of $\mathrm{PH}_{5}$, where $\Delta E=42.2 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$ on the HF level and $\Delta E=38.2 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$ for the CEPA [16]. The 4-electron 3-center bond between the axial hydrogens and the central atoms is considerably more stable in $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$ than in $\mathrm{PH}_{5}$. This result is, of course, expected since the stability of a 4-electron 3-center bond $\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{A}-\mathrm{H}$ ( $\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{PH}_{3}, \mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$) depends crucially on the ionization potential (IP) of the highest occupied MO of the isolated central molecule (in the present case the $\epsilon_{2 a}$ MO of planar $\mathrm{PH}_{3}$ or $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$). The smaller the IP $-\epsilon_{2 a_{2}}^{\prime}$ ( $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$) $=0.0083$ au, $\epsilon_{2 a_{2}}^{\prime}\left(\mathrm{PH}_{3}\right)=0.2962$ au - the stronger is the 3-center bond.

### 4.3. Pair correlation energies

In table 3 we have summarized CEPA pair correlation energies $[7,8 \mathrm{~b}] \epsilon_{i j}$ for the various silicon hydrides. The $\epsilon_{i j}$ are defined with reference to be localized HF MOs. (A comparison of $c_{i j}$ referring to the canonical MOs of $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}, \mathrm{SiH}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$would not provide much information since these MOs are, of course, quite different in these molecules.) We had to choose the CEPA $\epsilon$ 's for this purpose since the PNO CI $\epsilon$ 's depend on the total number of electrons which consequently would obscure the interpretation.

We first note the small changes of the $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{H}$ intrabond pair correlation energies which deviate by about $1 \%$ from an average value $\bar{\epsilon}=0.0305$ au in all systems listed in table 3. The intrapair energy of the lone pair in $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$is slightly smaller ( $\epsilon=0.0278$ au) than for an $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{H}$ bond but this deviation of about $7 \%$ is still not too large.

We find, however, considerable deviations of the interpair (or interbond) correlation energies $\epsilon_{i j}, i \neq j$. The latter range from 0.00117 au ( $\mathrm{SiH}_{ax}, \mathrm{SiH}_{ax}$ in $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$) over 0.0061 au ( $\mathrm{SiH}-\mathrm{SiH}$ in $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$) to 0.0107 au (bond pair - lone pair in $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$). We have verified by inspection that the magnitude of $c_{i j}, i \neq j$, depends essentially on the distance between the centroids of charge of the corresponding orbitals.

The effect of electron correlation on the reaction energies is essentially due to the changes in the interpair correlation energies since the intrapair terms are virtually constant at least for the different kinds of $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{H}$ bonds. In order to estimate the effect of the interpair terms one could in a first approximation assume that their sum is proportional to the number of pairs of adjacent bonds or lone pairs. This would explain the effect of correlation on $\Delta E_{\mathrm{f}}$ for reaction (1) or (2) where one has an increase of next neighbour bond interactions. Due to the unusually large interpair contribution in $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$, we find, however, no net effect of electron correlation on $\Delta E_{\mathrm{f}}$ for reaction (3).

### 5. Conclusions

In the present work we were mainly interested in two kinds of problems.

(i) The computation of reaction energies [like reaction energies for (1) and (3) or barriers for inversion, see eqs. (15)-(17), and pseudorotation] with an accuracy of a few kcal/mol in order to determine the stability and some properties of $\mathrm{SiH}_{5}^{-}$and $\mathrm{SiH}_{3}^{-}$and comparison of these results with those of related compounds like $\mathrm{PH}_{5}, \mathrm{PH}_{3}, \mathrm{CH}_{3}^{-}$.

### (ii) The investigation of the influence of electron correlation on the corresponding reaction energies or barriers.

Since we are confident that the basis sets and the methods employed should guarantee an accuracy of 2-3 kcal/mol in the computed energy differences wecan state that $SiH_{5}^{-}$ is stable with respect to $SiH_{4}+H^{-}$  but unstable with respect to $SiH_{3}^{-}+H_{2}$ . We have fur thermore obtained reliable structure parameters for SiH, and accurate results for the corresponding bar- riers for a pseudorotation and inversion. eqs. (10)-(12),(18)-(20).

With respect to the influence of electron correla- tion on energy differences we have essentially confirm- ed previous results that correlation is of little impor- tance for reactions in which the number of electron pairs and what is more important the number of next neighbour bond interactions is constant, like for the inversion of $SiH_{3}^{-}, PH_{3}, NH_{3}$ , or the pseudorotation of $SiH_{5}^{-}$ and $PH_{5}$ . Even in these cases care is recom mended as is shown by the difference of correlationenergies of $SiH_{3}^{-}$ and $SiH_{4}$ . In reactions like (1). (2) one finds a pronounced influence of correlation which is essentially due to an increase of next neigh- bour bond interactions. The influence of electron cor- relation on reaction and activation energies of nucleo- philic substitutions
$$\mathrm{ACH}_{3}+\mathrm{B}^{-} \to \mathrm{A}^{-}+\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{~B} \quad(23)$$
 may be as large or even larger as found for $SiH_{5}^{-}$ . We have investigated reaction (23) for $A, B=H, F, Cl[22]$ . In the case of $CH_{5}^{-}$ electron correlation reduces the activation energy by $7.2 kcal / mol$ from $63.6(HF)$ to56.4 kcal/mol (CEPA).
