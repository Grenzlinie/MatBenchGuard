# Quantum chemical interpretation of the mechanism of ethanol dehydrogenation driven by active oxygen bridges in silicalite-1 and silica

Yasuyuki Matsumura*, Keiji Hashimoto
Osaka Municipal Technical Research Institute, Joto-ku, Osaka 536 (Japan)

and Satohiro Yoshida
Department of Hydrocarbon Chemistry, Kyoto University, Sakyo-ku, Kyoto 606 (Japan)

(Received October 22, 1990; revised March 4, 1991)

## Abstract
The reaction mechanism of ethanol dehydrogenation on silicalite-1 or highly dehydrated silica has been investigated by ab initio molecular orbital calculations. The model of the intermediate is constructed by a combination of $(HO)_{3} SiOH$ and $C_{2} H_{5} OSi(OH)_{3}$ fragments, which would be formed by dissociative adsorption of ethanol on the active oxygen bridge existing in silicalite-1 or silica. On the basis of the calculation, the following mechanism is proposed: an interaction between the hydroxyl group of $>SiOH$ and the silicon atom of the ethoxide results in an increase in the acidity of the hydroxyl group. Simultaneously, the bond between the ethoxyl group and the silicon atom weakens and the reactivity of the ethoxyl group toward the acidic hydroxyl group increases; then acetaldehyde and hydrogen are formed.

## Introduction
Silicalite-1 (highly silicic ZSM-5 type zeolite) catalyzes the dehydroge- nation of ethanol to acetaldehyde [1]. The active site is probably the $Si-O-Si$ oxygen bridge that is present in silicalite-1 as a structural defect [1]. Thesimilar active site is present in silica dehydrated by heating above $c a$ . 1000 K [2]. It is noteworthy that over these active sites, acetaldehyde is selectively produced although the sites do not include transition metal elements such as copper $[1,2]$ , which are known to be effective for alcohol dehydrogenation. It is also known that alcohol dehydrogenation proceeds over a special type of acid-base catalyst [3-6], but we have confirmed that the active sites mentioned above do not exhibit acid-base properties $[1,2]$ . Hence, the mechanism of dehydrogenation over these active sites will be a unique one, and elucidation of this mechanism is important for the development of new catalysts for alcohol dehydrogenation.

We have proposed that the active oxygen bridge in silicalite-1 or highly dehydrated silica will be cleaved by ethanol adsorption, and hydroxyl and

Silica heated at temperatures under 1000 K exhibits quite low activity for ethanol dehydrogenation, although ethoxyl groups are formed by de- hydration between ethanol and surface silanols [2]. This suggests that the hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups formed by dissociative adsorption of ethanol on the active oxygen bridge have a specific reactivity. However, the exper- imental elucidation of this reactivity is quite difficult because the concentration of the intermediate on the catalyst to be directly measured in situ will be low.

Thus, we have studied the reactivity of the intermediate by means of molecular orbital calculations in order to determine the mechanism of the dehydrogenation. A quantum chemical approach to the reactivity of surface species on silica has been attempted and has succeeded in elucidating a reaction mechanism not readily examined experimentally [7-12]. In this study, we calculated parameters for the reactivities of the adsorption species in some special positions, because calculations of the exact transition state were impractical for the computer system which we used. However, the calculation results are very helpful for suggesting the mechanism of ethanol dehydrogenation driven by the active oxygen bridges in silicalite-1 or highly dehydrated silica.

## Method and models of calculation

We carried out ab initio SCF molecular orbital calculations using a basis set of STO-3G with the Gaussian-86 program [13]. This minimum basis set does not give the accurate total energy and binding energy between two molecules or groups because of the superposition error, etc. However, we can compare the parameters calculated for similar models because the calculations employing this basis set are believed to give relatively correct results for energies and so on [11]. It should be emphasized that our central interest is not exact tracing of the reaction process but qualitative study on the process. The active oxygen bridges catalyzing ethanol dehydrogenation are generated by condensation of silanols, i.e. $2\ \mathrm{Si-OH} \rightarrow \mathrm{Si-O-Si^*} + \mathrm{H_2O}$ [1, 2]. The basic building block of silicalite-1 is the pentasil ring, shown in Fig. 1a [14, 15]. Internal hydroxyl groups in silicalite-1 are believed to be present as a defect of this ring, as shown in Fig. 1b [16]. The active oxygen bridge is probably generated by condensation of these hydroxyl groups, however the structure had not yet been determined [1]. The distance between $\mathrm{Si_a}$ and $\mathrm{Si_b}$ in Fig. 1b is ca. 0.5 nm and is so great that condensation of these silanols is difficult without distortion of its ring structure; therefore, the position of the silicon atoms changes from the original position shown in Fig. 1b when condensation of the hydroxyl groups proceeds and $\mathrm{Si_a-O-Si_b}$ is formed. In practice, the active sites are mainly generated by heating silicalite-1 above 1000 K, suggesting that the condensation accompanies deformation of the crystal structure around these sites [1]. Since the active oxygen bridge in highly dehydrated silica is generated from isolated silanols on the surface [2], it is expected that the distance of $Si-O$ in the bridge generated is longer than the normal distance and that the bridge is distorted. Hence, the structure determined is consistent with this expectation.

Since the reaction intermediate is formed via adsorption of ethanol on the active oxygen bridge, i.e. $C_{2}H_{5}OH+Si-O-Si^{*} \to Si-OH+C_{2}H_{5}O-Si$, we

set up models for the intermediate by a combination of $(HO_{L})_{3}Si_{a}-O_{a}H_{a}$ and $C_{2}H_{5}O_{b}-Si_{b}(O_{L}H)_{3}$ clusters interacting with each other. In these models, the geometry of the $Si(O_{L}H)_{3}$ fragments is the same as that for the model of active oxygen bridge in Fig. 2. It can be assumed that the distance between these silicon atoms increases after the adsorption of ethanol and that the silicon atoms locate near the original positions shown in Fig. 1b. However, if the adsorption species adopt such a position, the distance between the hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups will be so great that these groups cannot interact. Moreover, the IR absorption band of the hydroxyl groups formed by adsorption of ethanol on the active sites is broad and distinctly different from that of the internal hydroxyl groups in silicalite-1 [1, 17], strongly suggesting that the hydroxyl groups interact significantly with the ethoxyl groups simulta- neously formed on ethanol adsorption [18]. Hence, the positions of most of the silicon atoms in the active sites are rather believed to be fixed in the structure of silicalite-1, even if ethanol is adsorbed on the active sites.

Model $A_{43/43}$ is the model in which the positions of the hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups were optimized, and thus is considered to be the model for the most stable state of the intermediate. The subscript 43/43 stands for the angles of $O_{a}-Si_{a}-Si_{b}$ and $O_{b}-Si_{b}-Si_{a}$, respectively. Since the reaction temperature of ethanol dehydrogenation is higher than $ca$. 600 K, the hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups vibrate vigorously and strongly interact with each other. In order to estimate the reactivities of these groups at high temperatures, we used various models as follows. Model $B_{30/48}$ is that in which the angle of $O_{a}-Si_{a}-Si_{b}$ was fixed at $30^{\circ}$ and the other parameters were optimized. Model $B_{53/30}$ is that in which the angle of $O_{b}-Si_{b}-Si_{a}$ was fixed at $30^{\circ}$ and the other parameters were optimized. As represented in Table 1 and Fig. 3, when the oxygen atom in the hydroxyl group $(O_{a})$ approaches $Si_{b}$, the oxygen atom in the ethoxyl group $(O_{b})$ moves away from $Si_{a}$ (cf. models $A_{43/43}$ and $B_{30/48}$). In contrast with this tendency, $O_{a}$ moves away from $Si_{b}$ when $O_{b}$ approaches $Si_{a}$ (cf. models $A_{43/43}$ and $B_{53/30}$). These phenomena will occur on the catalyst surface at reaction temperatures above $ca$. 600 K.

Model $B^{*}$ is that in which the $O_{a}-Si_{a}-Si_{b}$ angle and the $O_{b}-Si_{b}$ distance were fixed at $30^{\circ}$ and 0.210 nm, respectively, and the other parameters were optimized. In the case of ethanol dehydrogenation over highly dehydrated silica, the activation energy from the intermediate to the product was measured to be 1.35 eV [2]. Since the active site in silicalite-1 is similar to that in highly dehydrated silica [1, 2], the activation energy is estimated to be roughly the same. In comparison with model $A_{43/43}$ which has the most stable structure, models $B_{30/48}$, $B_{53/30}$ and $B^{*}$ are unstable by 0.77, 0.95 and 1.29 eV, respectively. The values are smaller than the activation energy; thus, the intermediate could assume a structure similar to these models at the reaction temperature and these models are possible ones for the intermediate.

## Results and discussion

### Reactivity of hydroxyl group in the intermediate
The hydroxyl groups on a silica surface are known as very weak acids [20], and the internal ones in silicalite-1 are not effective acid sites [21]. However, the hydroxyl group in the reaction intermediate for ethanol de- hydrogenation would have acid-base properties caused by interaction with the ethoxyl group. Grabowski *et al.* reported that the strength of acid-base sites on a solid surface is related to the charge density on these sites [9]. As represented in Table 3, the charge on $H_a$ in model $A_{43/43}$, which has the most stable structure, is nearly the same as that in model 1, which is a model for an isolated silanol group, suggesting that the hydroxyl group in model $A_{43/43}$ is a very weak acid site. However, the charge on $H_a$ in model $B_{30/48}$ is appreciably larger; in this model the hydroxyl group approaches $Si_b$ and the ethoxyl group connected to $Si_b$ moves away from the hydroxyl group.

Kawakami *et al.* reported that the energy necessary to remove a proton from a Brönsted acid site $(E_a)$ is an adequate index for estimating the acidity and that the charge on hydrogen is sometimes unreliable for estimating acidity [19]. Hence, the values of $E_a$ were calculated for the hydroxyl groups in these models and are also presented in Table 3. Sauer pointed out that the deprotonation energies calculated by the STO-3G basis set are not quantitatively correct but can be used for relative comparison [11]; hence, we can compare the values if they were calculated using the same basis set and method. The value of $E_a$ for model $B_{30/48}$ was 20.6 eV. In the case of $(HO)_2Si(OH)OAl(OH)_2$, which is the model compound for silica-alumina including strong Brönsted sites, the value was calculated to be 18-19 eV using the STO-3G basis set [19]. Since the value of $E_a$ for model $B_{53/30}$ is very large (24.3 eV), the hydroxyl group is not acidic; this model is that in which the ethoxyl group approaches $Si_a$ and the hydroxyl group connected to $Si_a$ moves away from the ethoxyl group. In this model, the hydroxyl group will be rather basic, because the electron density of $O_a$ is appreciably higher than that in model 1 (see Table 3). It is proposed that the ethoxyl groups react with basic sites on metal oxides and are transformed to ethylene [22]; thereby, ethylene formation could proceed via interaction between the hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups in model $B_{53/30}$ if the hydroxyl group is basic. To determine the basicity of the hydroxyl group, we calculated the stabilization energy generated by proton adsorption on $O_a$ ($E_b$) which is believed to be an adequate index for basicity [23]. The value of $E_b$ for model $B_{53/30}$ was calculated to be 11.7 eV. This value is noticeably larger than that for the oxygen bridge in an $(HO)_3Si-O-Si(OH)_3$ cluster (10.9 eV) calculated using the STO-3G basis set [23]. However, the value for the basic site on an alumina surface was calculated to be 13-16 eV [24, 25]. Since the site giving an $E_b$ value below ca. 13 eV can be regarded as an appreciably weak base [24, 25], the oxygen atom ($O_a$) in model $B_{53/30}$ cannot function as a base. Thus, ethylene cannot be formed via model $B_{53/30}$. This is consistent with the experimental observation that ethanol was selectively transformed to acetaldehyde over the active sites.

The hydroxyl group in model $B_{30/48}$ is distorted, and model $1_{30}$, which is the $(HO_L)_3Si-OH$ cluster having the same structure as that in model $B_{30/48}$, is unstable compared with model 1. However, the distortion of this cluster is not responsible for the generation of acidity in model $B_{30/48}$, because the $E_a$ value and the charge on $H_a$ for model $1_{30}$ were similar to those for model 1. However, in the case of model $1_{30}$ in which the cluster of ethoxyl groups is absent and the structure of the cluster of hydroxyl groups is the same as that of model $B_{30/48}$, the hydroxyl group is not acidic although the interaction between $O_b$ and $Si_a$ is absent. The $O_a-Si_b$ distances are 0.182 nm for model $B_{30/48}$, 0.220 nm for model $C_{43/48}$ and 0.250 nm for model $C_{53/48}$. From the results mentioned above, the mechanism of generation of acidic hydroxyl group is explained as follows. When the hydroxyl group in the intermediate $(O_aH_a)$ approaches the silicon atom bonding to the ethoxyl group $(Si_b)$, electrons on the hydroxyl group move to the bond between $O_a$ and $Si_b$, strengthening this bond. If the position of the ethoxyl group were near the hydroxyl group, charge transfer from the ethoxyl to the hydroxyl group would occur via the $O_b-Si_a$ bond and the electron density of the hydroxyl groups would increase. However, when the hydroxyl group approaches $Si_b$, the ethoxyl group moves away from the approaching hydroxyl group, and the $O_b-Si_a$ bond becomes weak. As a result, the electron density on the hydroxyl group becomes low, and the hydroxyl group becomes acidic.

## Reactivity of ethoxyl group
When the hydroxyl group in the intermediate is acidic, it can react with a nucleophilic atom in the ethoxyl group. The reaction expected is ethanol formation via proton addition to the oxygen atom in the ethoxyl group or dehydrogenation via abstraction of a hydrogen atom in the ethoxyl group by a proton, *i.e.*:

$$Si-OH+C_2H_5O-Si \longrightarrow C_2H_5OH+Si-O-Si^*$$

or

$$\mathrm{Si-OH + C_2H_5O-Si \longrightarrow CH_3CHO + H_2 + Si-O-Si^*}$$

If an ethanol molecule approaches the hydroxyl group, formation of water and an ethoxyl group can be expected. However, the reaction proceeds at a temperature above *ca.* 600 K and the silanol group interacts readily with the ethoxyl group rather than with an ethanol molecule, because the ethoxyl group is very close to the hydroxyl group. Since the electron density of the HOMO is large at a nucleophilic position [26], we used the coefficient for the HOMO as an index for reactivity of atoms in the ethoxyl group with the acidic hydroxyl group; the square of the coefficient is closely related to the electron density of the HOMO. As can be seen in Table 4, the squares of the coefficients for $\mathrm{O_b}$ and hydrogen atoms in model $\mathrm{A_{43/43}}$ are very small, suggesting that the reactivity of the ethoxyl group in model $\mathrm{A_{43/43}}$ to an acid is low. On the other hand, the squares of the HOMO coefficients for $\mathrm{O_b}$ and $\mathrm{H_2}$ in model $\mathrm{B_{30/48}}$, in which the hydroxyl group is acidic, were rather large. Hence, the ethoxyl group can react with the acidic hydroxyl group, if the structure of the reaction intermediate resembles that of model $\mathrm{B_{30/48}}$. As listed in Tables 1 and 4, model 2, characterized by a $\mathrm{O_b-Si_b}$ distance of 0.165 nm and an atomic bond population of 0.247, corresponds to an isolated ethoxyl group on the silica surface. In model $\mathrm{B_{30/48}}$, the $\mathrm{O_b-Si_b}$ bond is quite weak; that is, the length is 0.184 nm and the population is 0.182. Weakening of this bond is caused by coordination of $\mathrm{O_a}$ to $\mathrm{Si_b}$. If formation of ethanol or dehydrogenation to acetaldehyde proceeds via an intermediate similar to model $\mathrm{B_{30/48}}$, cleavage of $\mathrm{O_b-Si_b}$ and $\mathrm{O_a-H_a}$ bonds must occur in the reaction process. Since the atomic bond population of $\mathrm{O_a-H_a}$ in model $\mathrm{B_{30/48}}$ is 0.252 and significantly larger than that of $\mathrm{O_b-Si_b}$, it is expected that the cleavage of $\mathrm{O_b-Si_b}$ occurs first. To follow this reaction process, we changed the parameter of $\mathrm{O_b-Si_b}$ bond length in model $\mathrm{B_{30/48}}$ and calculated the indices for reactivity. Interaction between hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups

To evaluate the reactivity of the intermediate in detail where the hydroxyl group approaches $Si_b$ and the $O_b-Si_b$ distance gets longer, we adopted model $B^*$ in which the parameters of $O_b-Si_b$ distance and $O_a-Si_a-Si_b$ angle were fixed at 0.210 nm and $30^\circ$ respectively and other parameters were optimized. As shown in Table 3, the indices of acidity for model $B^*$ are similar to those for model $B_{30/48}$, suggesting that the hydroxyl group is quite acidic. On the other hand, the squares of the HOMO coefficients for $O_b$, $H_1$ and $H_2$ are large (see Table 4). Since those for $O_b$ in the ethoxyl group are very large, it is probable that the acidic hydroxyl group mainly interacts with $O_b$ and ethanol forms. In contrast, those for $H_1$ and $H_2$ are less large.

It is noteworthy that the atomic bond population of $O_a-Si_b$ is considerably larger than that for model $A_{43/43}$ (see Table 3). This allowed us to depict the transition state as a $C_2H_5O^-$ species and a proton interacting with a $Si-O-Si^*$ oxygen bridge. Since model $B^*$ is considered to be approaching the transition state, the reactivity of $H_1$ in model $B^*$ probably resembles that in the transition state. However, in model $B^*$, $H_1$ does not locate near $H_a$. Hence, we attached a new proton $(H^+)$ to $H_1$ in model $B^*$ and calculated the parameters for the interaction between $H^+$ and $H_1$ in order to determine

what happens between $H_1$ and the acidic hydroxyl group in the transition state. When the $H_1-H^+$ distance was 0.081 nm, the system was most stable and the energy generated by the proton addition was calculated to be 6.6 eV. The values of atomic bond population were 0.298 for $H_1-H^+$ and 0.113 for $H_1-C_{\alpha}$. This result suggests that interaction between $H_1$ and the acidic hydroxyl group results in the formation of a hydrogen molecule. Thus, dehydrogenation of the intermediate is thought to proceed via abstraction of the hydrogen atom bonded to the $\alpha$-carbon in the ethoxyl group with the acidic hydroxyl group.

In model $B^*$, the squares of the coefficients for $H_4$ and $H_5$ are quite small while those for $H_1$ and $H_3$ are roughly the same; the HOMO coefficient for $H_3$ was calculated to be 0.08. However, $H_3$ is located far from the hydroxyl group; therefore, it is assumed that the interaction between $H_3$ and the hydroxyl group is small and no reaction occurs on this hydrogen atom. By experimental measurement of the reaction rate, it was confirmed that the reaction proceeds via the Langmuir-Hinshelwood mechanism over highly dehydrated silica, that is, ethanol is mainly formed from the intermediate and formation of acetaldehyde also occurs. Hence, this calculated result is consistent with the experimental result.

The reactivity of the intermediate depends on the interaction between the clusters of ethoxyl and hydroxyl groups. Hence, the reactivity will change with the distance between these clusters; the $Si_a-Si_b$ distance used in the calculations (0.313 nm) is not an experimental one and the distance would somehow lengthen when ethanol adsorbs on the distorted oxygen bridge. In order to determine the effect of distance on the reactivity, we calculated the indices for the reactivity of the models in which the distance between these clusters is different from that of model $B^*$ but the structure of each cluster is the same as that of model $B^*$. As shown in Fig. 6, the value of $E_a$ increases with the $Si_a-Si_b$ distance, probably because the $O_a-Si_b$ distance increases and $O_a-Si_b$ atomic bond population decreases. However, even though the distance is longer than that for model $B^*$ by 0.04 nm, the value was 21.4 eV, suggesting that hydroxyl group is still acidic. On the other hand, the HOMO coefficients for these models do not change significantly, strongly suggesting that the reactivity of the ethoxyl group to acid is similar to model $B^*$. Thus, the tendency of the reactivity between hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups does not change if the distance in both the clusters is appreciably longer than that of model $B^*$.

## Conclusions

We propose that ethanol dehydrogenation over silicalite-1 or highly dehydrated silica occurs as follows:

(1) Ethanol is adsorbed on the distorted oxygen bridge in silicalite-1 or highly dehydrated silica, and hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups are formed via cleavage of the oxygen bridge.

![](./images/811161520825171969_6.jpg)

Fig. 6. Reactivity of the reaction intermediate when distance between silicon atoms in model B* is varied.

(2) Since the hydroxyl and ethoxyl groups are in close proximity, these groups can interact strongly. However, in the most stable configuration, the hydroxyl group $(-O_aH_a)$ is not an effective acid site and the reactivity of the ethoxyl group $(-O_bC_2H_5)$ to acid is comparatively low; therefore, no reaction can proceed if these groups are present at room temperature.

(3) At the reaction temperature (above $ca$. 600 K), it is possible by vigorous thermal vibration that some relative configuration between the hydroxyl and ethoxyl group changes, to cause the approach of the hydroxyl group to the silicon atom bonded to the ethoxyl group $(Si_b)$. When the $O_a-Si_b$ distance is short, this bond becomes strong. Simultaneously, the ethoxyl group moves away from the approaching hydroxyl group and the $O_b-Si_b$ bond weakens. As a result, the electron density of the hydroxyl group decreases, and the hydroxyl group exhibits acidic properties.

(4) The reactivity of the ethoxyl group to acid increases with decreasing bond strength of $O_b-Si_b$. This bond is quite weak when the hydroxyl group approaches $Si_b$. Thus, the reactivity of the ethoxyl group increases, and in the reaction process, cleavage of $O_b-Si_b$ occurs first. Since the electron density of the HOMO for $O_b$ is large in this reactive ethoxyl group, ethanol desorption from the oxygen bridge mainly occurs via addition of proton from the acidic hydroxyl group to the oxygen atom in the ethoxyl group. However,

the electron density for the hydrogen atom bonded to $\alpha$-carbon of the ethoxyl group is appreciably large. Thus, the hydrogen atom can interact with the acidic hydroxyl group and the hydrogen atom is abstracted from the $\alpha$-carbon in the ethoxyl group by the acidic hydroxyl group. Consequently, acetaldehyde and hydrogen are produced from the intermediate and the distorted oxygen bridge is simultaneously restored while ethanol desorption predominates.
