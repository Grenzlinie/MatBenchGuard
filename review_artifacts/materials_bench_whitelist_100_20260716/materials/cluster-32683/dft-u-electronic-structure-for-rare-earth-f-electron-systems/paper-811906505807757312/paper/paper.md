# f structures in the non-magnetic phase of $CeOs_2$

S Balle, J Costa-Quintana and F Lopez-Aguilar

Departamento de Fisica, Universidad Autonoma de Barcelona, Bellaterra, Barcelona,
Spain

Received 23 December 1986

**Abstract.** The density of states and the band structure of the cubic phase of non-magnetic
$CeOs_2$ is determined. The splitting between the different 4f structures is analysed and the
experimental results related to the large peaks that appear in the DOS at the Fermi level are
interpreted.

Some anomalous rare-earth and actinide compounds have, as a characteristic feature, a
large peak in the density of states at energies close to $E_F$. This is an f structure which
produces strong (Kondo, magnetic and superconducting) instabilities since the mini-
misation of the ground-state energy can lead to several phase transitions. Another
characteristic of these f systems is a splitting of this f peak into two f structures in such a
way that their locations can present two different configurations: either the two f struc-
tures are below $E$ or the Fermi level lies between them. This last case corresponds to
that of $CeOs_2$ (Schlott *et al* 1986). There are several causes for the appearance of these
splittings.

(i) the f–d hybridisation along with the coherence effects due to the crystal symmetry
can produce a splitting between two 4f structures.

(ii) The f–f and d–d Coulomb correlations along with the f–d intra-atomic interaction
can be the reasons for the existence of this splitting when the d-band states are sufficiently
localised and the d count per RE atom has a significant value.

(iii) The breaking of the crystal symmetry due to the magnetic ordering can imply
the existence of inequivalent RE ions in the unit cell, leading to their f states being located
at different energies.

$CeOs_2$ is a non-magnetic and superconducting heavy-fermion material (Schlott *et al*
1986, Meisner *et al* 1984); therefore the third possible cause can be ruled out. The first
and second possible causes can be present together, producing competitive effects whose
discrimination can be achieved by changing the conditions of the crystal (i.e. including
hydrostatic pressure, magnetic field etc).

In a recent study, we have described a band-structure pseudopotential model for the
Ce systems that takes the f–f, f–d and d–d Coulomb correlations systematically into
account (Balle *et al* 1986, 1987). We have used this pseudopotential in a band-calculation
method described in other papers (Lopez-Aguilar and Costa-Quintana 1986a, Lopez-
Aguilar 1986). Also, Schlott *et al* (1986) have given experimental results from electronic
spin-relaxation (ESR) measurements. The temperature dependence of the $Gd^{3+}$ ESR

linewidth in $CeOs_2$ implies a hybridisation pattern for the band structure and the DOS that is in agreement with the theoretical results yielded using our pseudopotential model.

The f-f, d-d and f-d Coulomb correlations are included in our pseudopotential by means of three potential terms $V_{\text{ff}}$, $V_{\text{dd}}$ and $V_{\text{fd}}$ (Balle *et al* 1986, 1987). The procedure for the determination of the potentials $V_{\text{dd}}$ and $V_{\text{fd}}$ is similar to that for $V_{\text{ff}}$ (see Lopez-Aguilar and Costa-Quintana 1986b):

$$
V_{\text{ff}} = U_{\text{F}} \sum_{\text{f}} \left(\frac{1}{2} - \langle n_{\text{f}} \rangle\right) |\text{f}\rangle\langle\text{f}| \tag{1a}
$$

$$
V_{\text{dd}} = U_{\text{D}} \sum_{\text{d}} \left(\frac{1}{2} - \langle n_{\text{d}} \rangle\right) |\text{d}\rangle\langle\text{d}| \tag{1b}
$$

$$
V_{\text{fd}} = U_{\text{FD}} \sum_{\text{fd}} \left(\langle n_{\text{fd}} \rangle |\text{f}\rangle\langle\text{d}| + \langle n_{\text{fd}} \rangle^{*} |\text{d}\rangle\langle\text{f}|\right) \tag{1c}
$$

where $U_{\text{F}}$, $U_{\text{D}}$ and $U_{\text{FD}}$ are the Coulomb correlation energies for ff, dd and fd electrons respectively and $\langle n_{\text{f}} \rangle$, $\langle n_{\text{d}} \rangle$ and $\langle n_{\text{fd}} \rangle$ are defined as

$$
\langle n_{\text{f}} \rangle = N^{-1} \sum_{k\alpha} |\langle k\alpha|\text{f}\rangle|^2 n_{k\alpha} \tag{2a}
$$

$$
\langle n_{\text{d}} \rangle = N^{-1} \sum_{k\alpha} |\langle k\alpha|\text{d}\rangle|^2 n_{k\alpha} \tag{2b}
$$

$$
\langle n_{\text{fd}} \rangle = N^{-1} \sum_{k\alpha} \langle k\alpha|\text{d}\rangle\langle\text{f}|k\alpha\rangle n_{k\alpha}. \tag{2c}
$$

The band Hamiltonian for the Ce systems is

$$
H_{\text{b}} = -\nabla^2 + V_{\text{MT}}^{\text{SC}} + V_{\text{ff}} + V_{\text{dd}} + V_{\text{fd}}
$$

where $V_{\text{MT}}^{\text{SC}}$ is the full self-consistent muffin-tin potential of the crystal, obtained within the local density formalism. The potentials $V_{\text{ff}}$ and $V_{\text{dd}}$ can be given by similar expressions and their inclusion in the band structure is identical. However, $V_{\text{ff}}$ produces stronger Coulomb effects in the band structure than $V_{\text{dd}}$ because of two features: (i) the 5d-band states are less localised than the 4f ones, consequently, $U_{\text{F}}$ is larger than $U_{\text{D}}$ and (ii) the $\langle n_{\text{f}} \rangle$s are also greater than the $\langle n_{\text{d}} \rangle$s. Henceforth, we only comment the inclusion of $V_{\text{ff}}$ in the band calculation, in such a way that all comments referring to $V_{\text{ff}}$ are also valid for $V_{\text{dd}}$. $V_{\text{fd}}$ is different from $V_{\text{ff}}$ and $V_{\text{dd}}$ and therefore we shall discuss its effects separately.

The Coulomb correlation effects maintain the crystal symmetry of the f systems. Therefore, the potential $V_{\text{ff}}$ commutes with all symmetries of the point group of the crystal. This implies that the average occupation numbers $\langle n_{\text{f}} \rangle$ are equal for all f orbitals of a given subspace of the decomposition $\text{D}_7 \ (2l + 1) = \Gamma_{2}' \oplus \Gamma_{15} \oplus \Gamma_{25}$ (see Balle *et al* 1987, Lopez-Aguilar and Costa-Quintana 1986a,b). The decomposition for the d space is $\text{D}_5 \ (2l + 1) = \Gamma_{12} \oplus \Gamma_{25}'$.

$V_{\text{ff}}$ depends on the $U_{\text{F}}$ energy and the parameters $\langle n_{\text{f}} \rangle$. There are several methods for calculating the Coulomb correlation energies (Herbst *et al* 1978, Arko and Koelling 1978, Lopez-Aguilar and Costa-Quintana 1983); however, their values can either be taken from experimental results or be considered as fitting parameters. The $U_{\text{F}}$ energy used in this work has been determined by a simplified method (Lopez-Aguilar and Costa-Quintana 1983) and its value is 0.55 Ryd. This value is larger than some experimental results given for other intermetallic Ce systems that also crystallise in the cubic Laves phase. For instance, the $U_{\text{F}}$ energy for $CeAl_2$ is 0.45 Ryd (Baer *et al* 1981, Fuggle *et al*

1983). This difference can be explained using two facts: (i) the unfilled 5d shell of Os influences the value of the $U_{\mathrm{F}}$ energy; (ii) the lattice parameter $(a)$ of $\mathrm{CeOs}_{2}(7.59 \mathring{A})$ is less than the corresponding parameter for $\mathrm{CeAl}_{2}(8.06 \mathring{A})$.

The main point in our calculation is the self-consistent determination of $\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle$, since the different effective Coulomb effects for each f symmetry depend on the $\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle$ values. The self-consistent process for obtaining $V_{\mathrm{MT}}^{\mathrm{SC}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{ff}}$ is performed, taking into account $V_{\mathrm{ff}}$ from the zero iteration. For this iteration, we have assumed a charge density for the Ce atoms arising from the configuration $4 \mathrm{f}^{1} 5 \mathrm{~d}^{1} 6 \mathrm{~s}^{2}$ (Mann and Waber 1973) and a set of $\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle$ values that satisfy the following conditions: (i) commutation of $V_{\mathrm{ff}}$ with all crystal symmetries; (ii) $\bar{n}=\Sigma_{\mathrm{f}}\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle=1$; (iii) maintaining of the spin degeneracy due to the non-magnetic condition of $\mathrm{CeOs}_{2}$. The fastest convergence is obtained assuming the following values of $\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle$ for the zero iteration: $\langle n_{\mathrm{f} \downarrow}\rangle=\langle n_{\mathrm{f} \uparrow}\rangle=0.5$ (f being a basis of the subspace $\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}$) and $\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle=0$ for the other f symmetries. The speed of obtaining convergence is independent of the choice of the initial values of $\langle n_{\mathrm{d}}\rangle$; however, the above three conditions for $V_{\mathrm{dd}}$ have to be satisfied. For successive iterations, the values of $\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle,\langle n_{\mathrm{d}}\rangle$ and $\langle n_{\mathrm{fd}}\rangle$ are self-consistently determined by means of the expressions (2a), (2b) and (2c) respectively.

The potential $V_{\mathrm{ff}}$ produces different shifts for each f symmetry with different average occupation numbers $\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle$ (i.e. the effective Coulomb correlation energy for a given f symmetry is $U_{\mathrm{F}}(\frac{1}{2}-\langle n_{\mathrm{f}}\rangle)$). Consequently, the potential $V_{\mathrm{ff}}$ causes different interactions for each f subspace and this implies a splitting of the different subspaces which generates the several f peaks in the DOS.

The potential $V_{\mathrm{fd}}$ depends on the f-d hybridisation parameters $\langle n_{\mathrm{fd}}\rangle$ and the values of these parameters depend on the effects of the potentials $V_{\mathrm{ff}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{dd}}$ in the band structure. $V_{\mathrm{fd}}$ also commutes with all crystal symmetries. Then, in the cubic Laves-phase symmetry $(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}^{7})$, all the $\langle n_{\mathrm{fd}}\rangle$ s are zero except those corresponding to the matrix box $\Gamma_{15}(\mathrm{f}) \times \Gamma^{\prime}(\mathrm{d})$ (Balle et al 1986, 1987) and the matrix $\langle n_{\mathrm{fd}}\rangle$ has the form

$$
\left\langle n_{\mathrm{fd}}\right\rangle=\begin{array}{cccccccc|c} 
& \Gamma_{15} & & \Gamma_{2}^{\prime} & & \Gamma_{25} & & & \\
& \mathrm{f}_{1} & \mathrm{f}_{2} & \mathrm{f}_{3} & \mathrm{f}_{4} & \mathrm{f}_{5} & \mathrm{f}_{6} & \mathrm{f}_{7} & \\
\hline & 0 & \beta & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathrm{~d}_{1} \\
& 0 & 0 & \beta & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathrm{~d}_{2} \\
& \beta & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathrm{~d}_{3} \\
\hline & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathrm{~d}_{4} \\
& 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathrm{~d}_{5}
\end{array} \mid \begin{aligned}
& \Gamma_{25}^{\prime} \\
& \\
& \\
& \Gamma_{12}
\end{aligned} \quad\left\langle n_{\mathrm{fd}}^{1}\right\rangle=-\left\langle n_{\mathrm{fd}}^{2}\right\rangle .
$$

The superscripts 1 and 2 affect the inequivalent Ce atoms of the primitive cell and the minus sign is a feature of crystals with inversion symmetry. The value of $\beta$ is calculated using expression (2c).

The results of the band-structure calculation are given in figure 1. The bands below $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ arise mainly from the 5d orbitals of Os which is hybridised with the 5d orbital of Ce. This produces an inter-atomic 5d-5d bonding which leads to an increase of the d count in Ce ions; therefore the intra-atomic f-d correlation could be non-vanishing. The occupied 5d bands of Os are gradually hybridised with the 4f orbitals of Ce which are located close to the Fermi level. In addition, the f-d hybridisation broadens the energy interval with states whose dominant orbital character is f (see table 1).

The f count is less than unity in the Ce ions: about 0.70 evenly divided between the two spin directions (it must be remembered that $CeOs_2$ is non-magnetic). The occupied f orbitals belong to the $\Gamma_2'$ subspace ($XYZ$ orbitals); therefore the average occupation number for each spin direction of the orbital $XYZ$ is around 0.35. This produces the hybridisation scheme for the band structure (see table 1 and figure 1) that is responsible for the two 4f peaks located at $E_F$ and detected by Schlott et al (1986) for $CeOs_2$. The potential $V_{ff}$ leads to the total non-occupation of the two degenerate f subspaces $\Gamma_{15}$ and $\Gamma_{25}$. The non-occupancy of the $\Gamma_{15}$ f bands implies that the f-d Coulomb correlation effects arising from the potential $V_{fd}$ are negligible for the crystal symmetry of this Ce compound. Consequently, we conclude that the pseudo-gap at the Fermi level between the two 4f structures is due only to crystal coherence effects.

The width of the $Gd^{3+}$ ESR resonance line in metals has been determined via the temperature-dependent relaxation of the Gd 4f spin to the conduction electron spin density. The relation between this width and the electronic structure can be obtained from the Korringa expression (see Schlott et al 1986):

$$
\Delta H(T)=\frac{r}{g \mu_{\beta}} J^{2} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} N^{2}(\varepsilon) f(\varepsilon, T)(1-f(\varepsilon, T)) \mathrm{d} \varepsilon
$$

where $J$ is the exchange constant between the $Gd^{3+}$ spin and the conduction electron spin, and $N(\varepsilon)$ stands for the conduction electron DOS per spin direction.

$\Delta H(T)$ increases linearly with $T$ if the DOS is constant at energies close to $E_F$, as in $LaOs_2$. In the ternary compounds $La_{1-x}Ce_xOs_2$ this linear behaviour gradually disappears with increasing $x$. This is due to the location of f states at the Fermi level that occurs when $x$ increases. Moreover, the behaviour of $\Delta H(T)$ becomes clearly non-linear when the crystal coherence effects appear. This has been explained in terms of a decrease of the DOS at the Fermi level in $CeOs_2$ with respect to $LaOs_2$ (Schaeffer and Elschner 1983). It is a clear symptom of the existence of a pseudo-gap at $E_F$ which splits off two 4f structures in agreement with our results (see figure 2). Besides this, the increase of

the resistivity below 20 K when the coherence effects appear in the ternary compounds
$La_{1-x}Ce_xOs_2$ implies that $E_F$ is located in a relative minimum for $x$ close to unity. This
is also consistent with the results given in figure 2.

One of the most intriguing questions concerns the influence of the relation between
the coherence pseudo-gap, determined both theoretically and experimentally, and the
superconducting properties of $CeOs_2$. The p superconductivity is enhanced by the
coupling of 4f electron pairs with the same spin. The coherence pseudo-gap affects the
attractive interaction between 4f electrons quantitatively, according to the paramagnon
theory (Anderson and Brinkman 1974, Anderson 1984). Consequently, the coherence
pseudo-gap and the p superconductivity are competitive properties; however, the super-
conducting behaviour of Ce systems often appears together with this double peak at $E_F$.

In summary, we have determined the band structure and the DOS of $CeOs_2$ using a
pseudopotential method that includes the f-f, d-d and f-d Coulomb correlation effects.
The main feature of the electronic structure is the strong f-d hybridisation and the
appearance of two narrow peaks split off by a pseudo-gap where the Fermi level is
located. This characteristic is in agreement with recent experimental results given by
Schlott *et al* (1986).
