**COMPRESSIBILITY, PHASE TRANSITIONS**
**AND AMORPHIZATION OF CORONENE**
**AT PRESSURES UP TO 6 GPa**

A. D. Chanyshev¹,², A. Yu. Likhacheva¹,³,
P. N. Gavryushkin¹,², and K. D. Litasov¹,²

UDC 522.1:549.08

The work is devoted to the experimental study of coronene $C_{24}H_{12}$ at high pressure and room temperature using *in situ* X-ray diffraction in a diamond anvil cell. The high-pressure phase $P2/m$ of coronene was found at 0.9 GPa, the $PV$-equation of state for $P2/m$ coronene phase was defined to 4 GPa: $K_0 = 10.8(3)$ GPa, $K_0' = 7$. At 5.9 GPa partial amorphization of coronene was observed. After the decompression to ambient pressure the high-pressure phase $P2/m$ was preserved, that can be related with partial amorphization.

DOI: 10.1134/S002247661607026X

**Keywords:** high pressure X-ray diffraction, diamond anvil cell, coronene, compressibility.

Coronene $C_{24}H_{12}$ is a polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH), consisting of six poly-condensed benzene rings and representing at room temperature yellow translucent crystals of acicular habit [1]. The study of hydrocarbons stability at high pressures is important in relation to their presence in the natural deep-seated minerals (PAH inclusions in diamonds [2]) and the modeling of the restored C–O–H fluid that can have a significant impact on the processes of mantle material melting. At 298 K and ambient pressure coronene has the space group $P2_1/a$ and the lattice parameters $a = 16.094(9)$ Å, $b = 4.690(3)$ Å, $c = 10.049(8)$ Å, $\beta = 110.792(2)^\circ$, $Z = 2$ [3]. The experiments at ambient pressure have revealed two phase transitions at 140-180 K and 50 K [4, 5].

Two high-pressure phase transitions of coronene at 1.5 GPa and 12.2 GPa were determined by X-ray diffraction and IR and Raman spectroscopy [6]. The high-pressure phases were identified as monoclinic ($1.5 \leq P \leq 12.2$ GPa) and orthorhombic ($P \geq 12.2$ GPa) crystal structures with space groups of $P2/m$ and $Pmmm$, respectively [7]. An abrupt change in the phase II lattice parameters at ~6 GPa evidences for structure distortion or reorganization [7]. The bulk modulus $K_0 = 6.8(5)$ GPa and the pressure derivative of the bulk modulus $K_0' = 4$ [7] were defined for coronene phase II. The amorphization of coronene was defined at 17.1 GPa by the disappearance of all diffraction peaks, except one [7].

Here we aimed to improve the results of previous works, namely to define the parameters of coronene amorphization at high pressure and room temperature and calculate the compressibility parameters of the high-pressure phase II of coronene.

Results and discussions. Coronene was uniformly compressed to 6 GPa at 298 K. The analysis of the diffraction patterns (Fig. 1) has revealed a phase transition between 0 GPa and 0.95 GPa. The XRD pattern of the initial phase at 0 GPa was indexed according to the space group $P2_1/a$ (Fig. 2) [12]. The unit cell volume of coronene was calculated to be $717.2\pm0.6$ Å$^3$ which is slightly higher than the previously reported value ($709.9\pm0.8$ Å$^3$) [12]. The peaks of the high-pressure phase II have been indexed according to the space group $P2/m$ (Fig. 2) [7]. The calculated lattice parameters of coronene at 2.9 GPa ($a = 16.627(5)$ Å, $b = 4.595(2)$ Å, $c = 8.999(3)$ Å, $\beta=99.76^\circ$) are in quite good agreement with the previous estimated values [7] (Fig. 2). However, the correlation of the peak [010] have revealed a significant difference (up to $-0.15^\circ$) for the studied patterns, which may indicate an incorrect choice of the space group $P2/m$ for the high-pressure phase II. Nevertheless, in this paper we indexed the profiles of coronene phase II in accordance with the space group $P2/m$, because for all the other peaks the correlation error was small.

An intermediate phase between coronene phases I ($P2_1/a$) and II ($P2/m$) was defined in the previous experimental work by the presence of characteristic peaks in the diffraction patterns of both phases [7]. In our study, however, we did not detect this intermediate phase: at 0.95 GPa and 298 K the XRD peaks were indexed according to the high-pressure phase II ($P2/m$). The lack of the phase transition could be explained by a higher pressure or a larger exposure time at this pressure, compared with [7].

The pressure dependence of the lattice parameters is shown in Table 1. The compressibility parameters of the phase II, calculated according to the Vinet EOS [13] ($V_0 = 795.5$ Å³, $K_0 = 10.8(3)$ GPa, $K_0' = 7$), are slightly different from previous experimental data ($K_0 = 6.8(5)$ GPa, $K_0' = 4$ [7]). The compressibility curve of coronene phase II, calculated according to our data, is in good agreement with the compressibility curves of naphthalene ($K_0 = 8.7$ GPa, $K_0' = 7.6$ [14]) and anthracene ($K_0 = 6.8$ GPa, $K_0' = 9.0$ [15]), in contrast to the previous data on coronene [7] (Fig. 3).

The observed degradation of the XRD profile and the disappearance of major diffraction peaks at 5.9 GPa (Fig. 1) could be explained by a partial amorphization of coronene. However, in previous experimental study the amorphization of coronene was determined at much higher pressure of 17.1 GPa [7]. We associate this difference primarily with a longer duration of our experiment in comparison with the previous experiments [7].

After reaching 5.9 GPa, the cell was decompressed to ambient pressure, followed by the sample exposure. The analysis of diffraction spectra have revealed a certain similarity between the spectra collected at 5.9 GPa and 1 bar (Fig. 1). Therefore one can suggest that the high-pressure phase II of coronene was preserved after the decompression. Its preservation can be associated, in particular, with the partial amorphization of coronene at 5.9 GPa.

The high-pressure phase II ($P2/m$) of coronene was determined in our experiments at 0.9 GPa; its lattice parameters were calculated at 2.9 GPa: $a = 16.627(5)$ Å, $b = 4.595(2)$ Å, $c = 8.999(3)$ Å, $\beta = 99.76^\circ$. The calculated compressibility parameters of coronene phase II $K_0 = 10.8(3)$ GPa, $K_0' = 7$ are in quite good agreement with the compressibility parameters of naphthalene [14] and anthracene [15], and in poor agreement with earlier data on the compressibility of coronene [7]. A partial amorphization of coronene was detected at 5.9 GPa.
