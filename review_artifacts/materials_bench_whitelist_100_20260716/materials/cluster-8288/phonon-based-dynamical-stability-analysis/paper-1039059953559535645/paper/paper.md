## ABSTRACT
First-principles calculations have been performed to investigate the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of $Sr_2BB'O_5$ (B = Fe, Co, Ni; $B'$ = Co, Ni, Mn) brownmillerite materials. By considering the atomic arrangements and the tetrahedral rotated modes, including typical magnetic configurations, the ground states are found by comparing their total energy. The thermodynamic and dynamic stability studies revealed that two compounds, $Sr_2FeCoO_5$ and $Sr_2NiMnO_5$, could be stable in the brownmillerite structure. Further electronic structure calculations show that the ground state of $Sr_2FeCoO_5$ is a G-AFM insulator, while the ground state of $Sr_2NiMnO_5$ is an A-AFM half-metal with a half-metallic bandgap of about 1 eV, exhibiting excellent half-metallic properties. In addition, the magnetic interactions are analyzed, which suggest that $Sr_2FeCoO_5$ could suffer from the influence of the B-site atomic disorder to maintain the G-AFM state, while $Sr_2NiMnO_5$ is more sensitive to atomic disorder, so its A-AFM state would be easily destroyed by the B-site disorder. These results would be helpful for further research.

## I. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, transition metal oxides exhibiting a brownmillerite structure (chemical formula $ABO_{2.5}$, where A and B are metal cations) have demonstrated numerous novel physical and chemical properties, attracting widespread attention.${}^{1-12}$ For example, by manipulating the oxygen concentration, the topotactic phase transition between brownmillerite and perovskite structure can be induced, which is an effective approach to realize reversible oxidation and reduction reactions.${}^{1-4}$ In addition, the variation in the resistive switching mode in epitaxial brownmillerite thin films indicates their potential utility in synaptic memory devices.${}^{10-12}$ Furthermore, adjusting the oxygen concentration or introducing hydrogen can significantly enhance the polarization of brownmillerite oxides,${}^{5-9}$ leading to pronounced magnetoelectric coupling effects. In addition to manipulating the oxygen composition, modulating the B-site elements can also alter the magnetic properties.${}^{13}$ However, despite the significant research focus on the modulation of oxygen composition or the introduction of additional anions to modulate material properties, research on the substitution of B-site metal ions remains scarce. Therefore, we aim to investigate brownmillerite-structured materials with two 3d transition metal

elements equally occupying the B-site, with the goal of discovering dynamically stable half-metal or magnetic semiconducting materials.

The brownmillerite oxide $ABO_{2.5}$ is derived from perovskite oxide $(ABO_3)$, in which one-sixth of the oxygen ions are replaced with vacancy.³ This replacement leads to an alternating arrangement of corner-shared $BO_4$ tetrahedron and $BO_6$ octahedron. Previous literature studies have indicated that $ABO_{2.5}$ has multiple structural phases, depending on the rotation of the tetrahedrons. Initially, the space group of $SrCoO_{2.5}$ was believed to be Icmm, as confirmed by neutron powder diffraction.¹⁴ However, it was revealed that tetrahedral distortion would result in more complex structures. The crystal structure of $SrFeO_{2.5}$ is confirmed by the single crystal x-ray method to be the space group Ibm2,¹⁵ while $CaFeO_{2.5}$ has the space group Pnma.¹⁶ In addition, Pbcm crystal structure was also observed afterward by x-ray diffraction.¹⁷ As illustrated in Fig. 1, the four different oxygen tetrahedrons are labeled T1, T2, T3, and T4. T1 and T2 belong to the same oxygen tetrahedron layer, while T3 and T4 both appear in adjacent oxygen tetrahedron layers. When a specific rotation pattern of the tetrahedron is applied around the B-axis, the tetrahedron and its corner-shared nearest neighbors will rotate in opposite directions. In Fig. 1, the blue arrows represent a counterclockwise rotation of the tetrahedron around the $b$ axis, and the yellow arrows represent a clockwise rotation of the tetrahedron around the b axis. The four labeled tetrahedrons rotate differently about the B-axis, resulting in the distinct structural phases of the brownmillerite oxides mentioned above. When the tetrahedron is distorted in the same pattern, it appears as an Icmm phase. When T1, T2, T3, and T4 are all rotated counterclockwise, it becomes an orthogonal Ibm2 phase (36 atoms per cell). When T1 and T3 are rotated counterclockwise while T2 and T4 are rotated clockwise, it becomes an orthogonal crystal Pbcm phase (72 atoms per cell). To determine the most stable phase, the total energy of the four structures with typical magnetic orders is considered in the following first-principles investigation.

Current research on A₂BB'O₅ compounds has not considered the influence of B-site disorder. The reason is that ordered compounds with B-site ordered [001] oriented perovskite structures could be obtained by MBE. Afterward, the topotactic redox could be applied to obtain ordered A₂BB'O₅ compounds and prevent B-site disorder. Figure 2 shows two possible structures of A₂BB'O₅ in which the B and B' atoms are ordered along the [100] and [010] crystal directions, respectively. Note that the structures in which the B and B' atoms are ordered along [001] are equivalent to those along [100]. Our first-principles calculations have shown that the arrangement of the B and B' atoms significantly affects the ground state. In addition, phonon spectrum calculations were performed to investigate the dynamical stability, which showed that only two compounds, Sr₂FeCoO₅ and Sr₂NiMnO₅, are dynamically stable. The electronic structure and magnetic properties of these materials will be further investigated.

## II. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS

First-principles calculations based on density functional theory (DFT) were performed using the pseudopotential plane-wave method. The VASP program package was used with the PAW method.¹⁸,¹⁹ The plane-wave cutoff energy (ENCUT) was set to 400 eV. The exchange correlation function was treated with the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA), specifically the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) scheme.²⁰ The strong correlation interaction was treated with the DFT + U method,²¹,²² with U values fixed at 4 eV, which is similar to the value in the literature.³,¹³ We also studied the choice of U values and found no significant difference in the range of 3–5 eV. The Monkhorst-Pack method²³ was used for Brillouin zone sampling, with a grid of $6 \times 3 \times 2$. The Phonopy software package was used for phonon spectrum calculations.²⁴,²⁵ The convergence criteria for relaxing the structure and optimizing atomic positions were set to a total energy

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
### A. Structural properties

First, structural optimization calculations of $Sr_2BB'O_5$ were performed for all structures and magnetic configurations mentioned above. The calculated total energies for all compounds are shown in Table I. The ground state of $Sr_2BB'O_5$, shown as 0 in Table I, was determined by comparing the total energy. It can be seen that the magnetic configuration of the ground states for all compounds is AFM. To further investigate the thermodynamic stability corresponding to their ground states, the formation energies of each compound were calculated. The results are shown in Table II. It indicates that $Sr_2FeMnO_5$ and $Sr_2CoMnO_5$ are thermodynamically unstable, while $Sr_2FeCoO_5$, $Sr_2FeNiO_5$, $Sr_2CoNiO_5$, and $Sr_2NiMnO_5$ are all thermodynamically stable compounds.

To verify the possibility of synthesizing these compounds experimentally, further studies on their dynamic stability are required. In this study, we selected thermodynamically stable compounds to investigate their dynamic stability. Using the Phonopy program, we calculated the phonon spectra of the corresponding ground states of the $Sr_2BB'O_5$ compounds, shown in Fig. 3. The results showed that among these compounds, only $Sr_2FeCoO_5$ and $Sr_2NiMnO_5$ showed no imaginary modes in their phonon spectra, demonstrating their dynamic stability and the feasibility of fabricating the brownmillerite structure materials by experiment. In the following sections, we will focus on these two stable compounds to investigate their electronic structure and magnetic properties.

Among all the structural configurations of $Sr_2FeCoO_5$, the total energy is lowest when the Fe-Co ions are ordered along the [010] direction, corresponding to the Pbcm space group. Furthermore, the calculation results show that the G-AFM configuration is the ground state of $Sr_2FeCoO_5$. However, the ground state of $Sr_2NiMnO_5$ is different from that of $Sr_2FeCoO_5$. ### B. Electronic structure
Figure 4(a) shows the orbital projected density of states (DOS) and bands of $Sr_2FeCoO_5$ in its ground state with G-AFM order. It shows that the O 2p orbital has a significant contribution from the energy range of $-7$ to $-0.5$ eV below the Fermi level. This corresponds to the strong covalent bonding between the 3d orbitals of Fe or Co cations and the 2p orbitals of O anions. While the bands ranging from 0.8 to 2.5 eV are primarily determined by the Fe and Co 3d orbital contributions of the antibonding states, the occupation of the d-orbital electron in $Sr_2FeCoO_5$ is as shown in Table III. These results indicate that Fe1 and Fe2, occupying the octahedral sites, have +3 valence in a high spin state with magnetic moments around $4.00\ \mu_B$. Similarly, Co1 and Co2, occupying the tetrahedral sites, are have +3 valence in a high spin state with a magnetic moment of $2.82\ \mu_B$. It is evident that there are no electron fillings in both the majority and minority spin channels within the energy range of $-0.5$ and 0.8 eV, indicating an energy gap of 1.3 eV, suggesting that $Sr_2FeCoO_5$ exhibits charge transfer insulator properties.

It is further observed that there is no significant difference between the majority spin and minority spin channels for bands dominated by Mn atoms, while for bands dominated by Ni atoms, there is a magnetic splitting of about 2 eV, which is due to the different 3d electronic configurations of Ni ions at the tetrahedral and octahedral sites. For the magnetic coupling between Co1 and Co2 ions in the tetrahe- dral layer via the Co1-O-Co2 pathway, the bond angle is 133.1°, which is significantly deviated from 180°; therefore, the correspond- ing magnetic coupling should be between FM and AFM. However, since AFM couplings are dominated in the other pathways for Co1 and Co2 ions, the magnetic moments of Co1 and Co4 ions ultimately are arranged antiparallel, resulting in Sr₂FeCoO₅ having a G-AFM configuration.

To further investigate the magnetic exchange interactions, a Heisenberg model could be used to analyze the magnetic exchange constant $J$ between the transition metal ions,²⁸ which can be obtained by calculating the total energy of different magnetic struc- tures and then mapping them to the classical Heisenberg exchange model,

$$
\mathrm{H}=-\sum_{i j} J_{i j} S_{i} \cdot S_{j}.
$$

$S_i$ and $S_j$ are the magnetic moments of the metal ions at sites $i$ and $j$, respectively. $J_{ij}$ represents the exchange interaction constant between sites $i$ and $j$. If $J_{ij}$ is positive, it indicates an FM exchange, while if $J_{ij}$ is negative, it indicates an AFM exchange. In this con- text, four different magnetic configurations mentioned above are considered, and the total energy corresponding to each magnetic configuration can be expressed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{F M}= & E_{0}-8 J_{C o C o}\left|S_{C o} \cdot S_{C o}\right|-8 J_{F e C o}\left|S_{F e} \cdot S_{C o}\right| \\
& -16 J_{F e F e}\left|S_{F e} \cdot S_{F e}\right|,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{A-A F M}= & E_{0}-8 J_{C o C o}\left|S_{C o} \cdot S_{C o}\right|+8 J_{F e C o}\left|S_{F e} \cdot S_{C o}\right| \\
& -16 J_{F e F e}\left|S_{F e} \cdot S_{F e}\right|,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{C-A F M}= & E_{0}+8 J_{C o C o}\left|S_{C o} \cdot S_{C o}\right|-8 J_{F e C o}\left|S_{F e} \cdot S_{C o}\right| \\
& +16 J_{F e F e}\left|S_{F e} \cdot S_{F e}\right|,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
E_{G-A F M}=E_{0}-8 J_{C o C o}\left|S_{C o} \cdot S_{C o}\right|+8 J_{F e C o}\left|S_{F e} \cdot S_{C o}\right|.
$$

The obtained exchange constants are $J_{CoCo}=12.2$ meV, $J_{FeCo}=-98.1$ meV, and $J_{FeFe}=-81.0$ meV. It is evident that the Co ions occupying the tetrahedral sites have FM interactions with each other, while the interactions between the Fe and Fe ions, and between the Fe and Co ions, are AFM interactions. The strength of the AFM coupling completely surpasses that of the FM cou- pling, which is consistent with the above-mentioned analysis of the superexchange mechanism. In addition, as shown in Table I, the tetrahedral rotations cause a tiny difference in the total energy, while the atomic arrangements in the B sites have a significant influence. Moreover, the total energies of Sr₂FeCoO₅ within the G-AFM order are much lower in energy, and the energy difference between the magnetic configurations is obviously larger than the effect of the atomic arrangements, which indicates that the magnetic configu- rations play a central role, thus primarily dominating the ground state. As discussed above, since both Fe and Co ions are in the high spin state and in the electronic configuration of the half-filled state, regardless of whether they are in the tetrahedral or octahedral sites, the FM or AFM coupling between the B-site ions resulting from the superexchange interactions is not affected by the types of B-site ions. Thus, it implies that the atomic disorder may not affect the G-AFM order of Sr₂FeCoO₅.

Table VI shows the bond lengths of Ni-O and Mn-O and the bond angles of Ni-O-Ni, Ni-O-Mn, and Mn-O-Mn in Sr₂NiMnO₅. Let us first investigate the superexchange interaction through Ni1-O-Ni2, which forms a bond angle of 148.7°. As shown in Table IV, since the $d_{xz}$ orbitals between Ni1 and Ni2 are fully filled, the hopping between the half-filled to half-filled $d_{z}^{2}$ orbitals and the hopping between the fully filled to half-filled $d_{yz}$ orbitals should dominate. According to the superexchange mechanism, the magnetic interaction between Ni1-O-Ni2 is a competition between AFM and FM interactions. Next, let us consider the superexchange through Mn1-O-Mn2, which forms a bond angle of 158.6°. Since the $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ orbitals of Mn1 and Mn2 are both half-filled and the $d_{z}^{2}$ orbitals are empty, the superexchange interaction through the Mn1-O2-Mn2 pathway seems to be also a competition between FM and AFM coupling. However, the interaction through Ni1-O-Mn1, with a bond angle of 173.2° is a strong FM coupling. This is because the $d_{x}^{2}-d_{y}^{2}$, $d_{xz}$, and $d_{yz}$ orbitals between Ni1 and Mn1 ions involve the hoppings from fully filled to half-filled orbitals, while the hopping through the $d_{xy}$ orbital involves a transfer from a half-filled to an empty orbital. Furthermore, the superexchange interactions between the tetrahedral layer atoms Ni2 and Mn2 are via the Ni2-O-Mn2 pathway, with a bond angle of 131.4°, which is relatively closer to 90° than 180°. The hopping via $d_{xy}$, $d_{yz}$, and $d_{x}^{2}$ $-d_{y}^{2}$ orbitals between Ni2 and Mn2 is from half-filled to half-empty orbitals, which should dominate over the hopping via the $d_{xz}$ orbitals from a fully filled to a half-filled orbital. Therefore, the FM superex- change interaction between Ni2-O-Mn2 in the same tetrahedral layer might be stronger. As a consequence, Sr₂NiMnO₅ ultimately has the A-AFM order, which is different from the G-AFM order of Sr₂FeCoO₅.

## IV. CONCLUSION

The brownmillerite oxides $Sr_{2}BB'O_{5}$ (B = Fe, Co, Ni; B' = Co, Ni, Mn), in which B and B' atoms are arranged along different crystal directions, are investigated by first-principles density functional theory calculations. Based on the experimentally observed crystal structure, the calculations demonstrate that the magnetic configuration has more evident influence on the total energy than the atomic arrangement at the B site, while the impact of both is notably greater than the rotation of the $BO_{4}$ or $B'O_{4}$ tetrahedron. Furthermore, it is revealed that $Sr_{2}FeCoO_{5}$ and $Sr_{2}NiMnO_{5}$ are both thermodynamically and dynamically stable, indicating the possibility of fabricating these brownmillerite oxides. Both $Sr_{2}FeCoO_{5}$ and $Sr_{2}NiMnO_{5}$ has the Pbcm space group, with different crystallographic orientations of their transition metal ions. In $Sr_{2}FeCoO_{5}$, Fe and Co alternate along the [010] direction. Its ground state is a G-type antiferromagnetic insulator with a bandgap greater than 1 eV. $Sr_{2}NiMnO_{5}$, on the other hand, has Ni and Mn alternating along the [100] direction and is an A-type antiferromagnetic order with a larger half-metal gap of about 1 eV. In addition, by analyzing the magnetic coupling between metal ions, it seems that $Sr_{2}FeCoO_{5}$ is able to tolerate B-site disorder and maintain its magnetic configuration, while $Sr_{2}NiMnO_{5}$ is relatively sensitive to B-site atomic disorder. Our results would be useful for further investigations in spintronics.
