Piezoelectric Field and its Influence on the Pressure Behavior of the Light Emission from InGaN/GaN and GaN/AlGaN Quantum Wells

ABSTRACT

In this paper we review studies aiming at elucidation of the mechanisms responsible for anomalously low pressure coefficients of the light emission energy, $dE_{E}$/dP, observed in quantum structures of InGaN/GaN and GaN/AlGaN. We have established that in hexagonal InGaN/GaN and GaN/AlGaN structures the main mechanism involved is related to the pressure induced increase of the piezoelectric field which determines also the strong red shift of the emission energy with thickness of the quantum well. To reproduce the experimental findings in InGaN/GaN case, it is necessary to take into account the dependence of the piezoelectric constants on the volume-conserving strain. Whereas the experimental results on a decrease of $dE_{E}$/dP in GaN/AlGaN structures can be fully accounted for within the linear elasticity theory. In contrast to these findings, $dE_{E}$/dP magnitude measured in cubic InGaN/GaN quantum structures shows value close to changes of the InGaN bangap with pressure obtained from first principle calculations. The latter result is consistent with the absence of the built-in electric fields in the cubic nitride structures.

INTRODUCTION

Contribution of the built-in electric field to the properties of wurtzite group-III nitrides has been widely disputed during last few years [1-3]. The considered materials exhibit a pyroelectric properties and strong piezoelectric character which lead to the appearance of the electric

polarization due interface charge accumulation. It consists of two contributions: i) piezoelectric polarization due to strain induced by a lattice-mismatch [4,5] and ii) the spontaneous polarization. The latter effect is present even in the bulk - unstrained nitrides and originates from a relative shift of the cation and anion sublattices along c axis of the wurtzite polar structure [1]. Effects resulting from the built-in macroscopic polarization can entirely determine some basic properties of nitrides. In case of nitride systems consisting of the layers crystallizing in the cubic structure neither spontaneous nor piezoelectric polarizations are present in one of the equivalent <100> directions.

In wurtzite nitride structures the effective electric field, directed along c axis (epitaxial growth direction) caused by i) and ii) leads to a bending of the potential profiles related to the conduction and valence bands in quantum structures consisting of InGaN/GaN or GaN/AlGaN heterostructures. As a result the spatial separation of excited electrons and holes takes place which is accompanied by the decrease in their energetic separation. This represents the Quantum Confined Stark Effect (QCSE). The fingerprint of the QCSE is its dependence on the quantum well thickness. In the other words, with increasing the quantum well width the spontaneous emission energy, $E_E$, decreases. The same effect is observed when the built-in electric field increases. Moreover, the other consequence of this effect is a difference in energies characterizing the light emission and absorption , i.e., Stokes shift, which is usually observed in the considered quantum structures.

The other effect leading to a red shift of the emitted light energy, as well as to the Stokes shift originates from the fluctuations in the In composition in quantum well or epitaxial layer of InGaN [4, 6]. This effect seems to be omnipresent and has been reported in hexagonal as well as cubic InGaN structures [7]. Formation of (pseudo)quantum dots corresponding to regions of higher In concentration has been invoked by many researchers. This In content fluctuations is believed to result from the effect of a phase separation governing the formation of InGaN alloy [8, 9], particularly for $\mathrm{In_xGa_{1-x}N}$ with higher x. In the epitaxial layer of InGaN, concentration of In-cations can locally differ significantly from the average, technologically predetermined value. Similarly to the optical effects found in InGaN layers/structures subject to the internal electric fields ( i) and ii)), in case of In content fluctuations, the light emission occurs in regions of lower energy gap which in this case correspond to higher In content (pseudo-dots). Localized electronic states (excitons) are likely involved here.

All above mentioned effects make an unambiguous determination of band gap magnitude,
$E_G$, versus x in $In_xGa_{1-x}N$ difficult. However, it is widely accepted now that the band gap bowing
parameter for this alloy is large. This reflects the strong drop of the $E_G$ value for $In_xGa_{1-x}N$ with
low In content (x ≤ 0.25) [10]. An increase of the Stokes shift and a commonly reported
broadening of the absorption edge in $In_xGa_{1-x}N$ alloys accompany a rise in x value.

The other puzzling observation pointing out a particular role of In in InGaN alloys, consists
in the finding a small and strongly In-content dependent pressure coefficient of the light emission
energy, $E_E$, in thick layers as well as quantum structures of hexagonal $In_xGa_{1-x}N$ [11, 12]. Our
previous studies showed that with increasing x, $dE_E/dP$ decreases drastically. For example, for
x≈0.2 the pressure coefficient $dE_E/dP$≈15 meV/GPa has been found in the 3.5 nm thick InGaN
quantum well. It is almost three times smaller than that measured for GaN ( 40 meV/GPa) [13] and
much lower than the pressure shift of the band gap calculated for InN (≈25-30 meV/GPa) [14, 15].

In this work we describe results of three different experiments performed by us very
recently with the purpose of elucidation of the microscopic mechanism(s) responsible for low
value of the light emission shift with pressure in quantum structures of Group-III nitrides.

**Experiment 3.** Many of the described above effects can be associated with the strong existing polarization in the hexagonal heterostructures of InGaN and AlGaN alloys. However, in their cubic counterparts this origin of the light emission is excluded by the symmetry reasons. It has become straightforward that the next step in the verification of the origin of anomalously low and quantum-well-thickness dependent pressure shift of the emission energy should involve pressure studies of the cubic alloys. Let us firstly recall some results obtained at ambient pressure.

Studies of the light emission mechanisms in cubic InGaN structures have been performed by Chichibu et al. [7]. The results obtained by these authors show a large bandgap bowing and significant Stokes shift between emission and absorption energies. It has been concluded that the emission in cubic InGaN layers and cubic InGaN/GaN quantum structures is caused by the recombination of localized excitons/carriers as in the case of wurtzite material. Two mechanisms of the localization have been revealed by first principle calculations [20,21]. First, the strong hole localization can exist in the homogeneous InGaN alloy. The preferential localization along the [1, 1, 0] In-N-InN-N-In chains was obtained. Moreover, a spatial inhomogeneity in In distribution can result in formation of In-rich, dot-like or disk-like structures within a region of lower In content. Thus, in such regions one can expect a strong localization of holes and electrons. It is

## DISCUSSION

To describe the evolution of the optical transitions in wurtzite InGaN/GaN, GaN/AlGaN, and cubic InGaN/GaN quantum wells with hydrostatic pressure we use a simple model based on the linear theory of elasticity (the Hooke's law) and the k·p method. Method of the calculations is similar to that given elsewhere [19]. Here we will limit our description to the main steps of the used approach.

Our task can be divided into three steps. First one consists in calculating the strain in the multi layer structure while the hydrostatic stress is applied. For the wurtzite structures we use a full anisotropic elastic theory since the wurtzite lattice transforms hydrostatic stress into nonhydrostatic strain. Additionally, we consider that the structures are composed of the sequence of layers characterized by slightly different compressibilities. Particularly we assume that the whole system preserves its pseudomorphic character and subsequently in-plane compressibility in each layer is determined by sapphire (for wutzite material) or SiC (for cubic material) substrates which are the hardest and the thickest component of the corresponding systems. Then the in-plane component of the strain tensor and its pressure dependence can be determined by the knowledge of the initial lattice mismatch (e.g. lattice mismatch between QW and barrier materials) and elastic properties of sapphire/SiC.

Having determined the strain condition in the considered heterostructures we can proceed to the second step of our calculations, i.e., to determine the piezoelectric polarizations and electric fields in the quantum wells and barriers. These calculations are done only for the wurtzite

structures since in the cubic samples the macroscopic fields are excluded by the crystal symmetry as it has been already mentioned. To calculate the values of piezoelectric polarizations in corresponding layers of the GaN/AlGaN structures we use the volume conserving strain dependent piezoelectric constants of AlN and GaN [22]. We apply here a Vegard's-like rule which enables us to get the appropriate values of these parameters for AlGaN layers. In the model of InGaN/GaN QWs we assume that the non-linear corrections to piezoelectric constants for InGaN are the same as for GaN, since there is a lack of data concerning strain-induced behaviour of piezoelectric tensor for InN. Then, using the very recent results of calculations done by Bernardini and Fiorentini [23] we add the values of spontaneous polarization to the values of piezoelectric polarization.

The third stage of the modeling procedure is to calculate the energies of fundamental optical transitions in considered quantum heterostructures as the function of hydrostatic pressure. For this purpose we use the k·p method. Since our goal is just to explain the pressure dependent photoluminescence spectra, measured in low excitation regime, we restrict the analysis to calculations of conduction band – heavy hole band transition energies for $\vec{k}=0$. First we solve both effective mass equations for conduction band and heavy hole band for each quantum well for atmospheric pressure. Then in similar way we calculate the fundamental transition energies for each quantum well increasing value of hydrostatic pressure from 0 to 10 GPa. The obtained transition energies show almost linear dependencies on pressure which gives us the corresponding values of the pressure coefficients.

However, the inspection of the calculated emission energies and their pressure coefficients for the three considered groups of nitride alloys quantum structures shows that they reproduce well the experimental results in GaN/AlGaN case only. Concerning the evolution of the optical transitions in the wurtzite InGaN/GaN quantum wells with hydrostatic pressure, our calculations lead to much lower pressure coefficients for thicker quantum wells. Particularly, for the 5nm thick $In_{0.20}Ga_{0.80}N/GaN$ the calculated value of $dE_E/dP$ is about –30meV/GPa comparing with –2meV/GPa obtained in the experiment. This difference may be related to the lack of precise data on nonlinear behavior of piezoelectric constants in InGaN alloys. In this place we mention that these strain-induced modifications of piezoelectric tensor play a key-role in modeling of pressure behavior of InGaN/GaN QW as it was already shown by Vaschenko et al. # COCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we have established that the presence of the electric field in hexagonal InGaN/GaN and GaN/AlGaN quantum structures leads to the drastic reduction of the pressure coefficient of light emission. This effect is related to the pressure induced increase of the piezoelectric field generated in wurtzite quantum structures of group-III nitrides. To reproduce the experimental findings in InGaN/GaN case, it is necessary to take into account the dependence of the piezoelectric constants on the volume-conserving strain. Whereas the experimental results on a decrease of $dE_E$/dP in GaN/AlGaN structures can be fully accounted for within the linear elasticity theory. In contrast to these findings $dE_E$/dP magnitude measured in cubic InGaN/GaN quantum structures shows value characterizing the changes of the InGaN bangap with pressure and is consistent with the absence of the built-in electric fields in the cubic nitride structures. Moreover, a strong bowing of $dE_G$/dP for $In_xGa_{1-x}N$ for x<0.25 found here for cubic material and for hexagonal alloys in Ref. 16 seems to be related to the hole localizing tendency postulated by Kent and Zunger [21].
