### Impact of In content on the structural and optical properties of (In,Ga)N/GaN multiple quantum wells grown by plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy

Patrick Waltereit and James S. Speck

Materials Department, University of California, Santa Barbara, California 93106

## ABSTRACT

We have studied the structural and optical properties of a series of (In,Ga)/GaN multiple quantum wells with identical thicknesses but varied In content grown by plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy. Careful choice of the growth parameters returns samples with smooth and abrupt interfaces. The shift of the photoluminescence transition energy with externally applied biaxial tension was investigated. We observed a red-shift for small In contents while a blue-shift was detected for higher In contents. This result is in qualitative agreement with band profile calculations taking into account both the band gap deformation potentials and the piezoelectric polarization in these structures. However, the magnitude of the shift is well in excess of the calculated one. We attribute this finding to a substantial deviation of the piezoelectric constants from those calculated for unstrained material. Finally, we estimate the piezoelectric polarization of InGaN/GaN for linear and non-linear terms in strain.

## INTRODUCTION

One of the most distinguishing materials properties of group-III nitrides are their polarization fields of a magnitude not found in other III-V semiconductors. [1] In the case of thin layers, polarization discontinuities at heterointerfaces lead to the formation of electrostatic fields. The resulting quantum-confined Stark-effect (QCSE) is accompanied with a red-shift of the transition energies and a decrease of the electron-hole wavefunction overlap with respect to flat-band conditions. [2] The total polarization of a layer is composed of spontaneous and piezoelectric contributions. Recently, it has been proposed that the discontinuities in GaN/AlGaN spontaneous [3] and InGaN/GaN piezoelectric [4] polarization are significantly smaller and larger, respectively, than the theoretically predicted values. [1] Usually, changes in thicknesses or compositions are made for investigating polarization discontinuities. However, this procedure prevents a clear separation of spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization unless the layers are unstrained. In contrast, one can utilize externally applied stress to unambiguously examine the piezoelectric polarization. [4,5]

The aim of this paper is two-fold. First, we report on the structural and optical properties of a series of (In,Ga)N/GaN multiple quantum wells (MQWs) having identical structural parameters except for the In content in the well. Second, the piezoelectric polarization in strained (In,Ga)N wells is investigated by studying the photoluminescence under external modulation of the strain state.

## EXPERIMENTAL

The samples under study were grown by plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy in a Varian Gen-II system equipped with effusion cells for the group-III species (two for Ga, one for In) and

an EPI rf plasma source to provide active nitrogen. We utilized a $2\ \mu$m thick GaN(0001) template grown on $Al_2O_3$(0001) by metal-organic chemical-vapor-deposition (MOCVD) as a substrate.
$1\ \text{cm}^2$ pieces were cleaved from this template, degreased in acetone, methanol and propanol and mounted with In on a 3-inch Si wafer which was mounted In-free on a Mo sample block. The substrate temperature was measured using a pyrometer, scaled by the melting point of Al ($660^\circ\text{C}$). The growth front was monitored *in-situ* by reflection high-energy electron diffraction (RHEED). After growth, the samples were examined by high-resolution x-ray diffraction (HRXRD) using Cu K$\alpha_1$ radiation in conjunction with dynamic diffraction simulation [6] and continuous-wave photoluminescence (PL) at room temperature excited by the 325 nm line of a He-Cd laser with an excitation power density of $100\ \text{W cm}^{-2}$. Biaxial tension was applied using the sample as a window in a pressurized cell. [5] The magnitude of the strain (up to $8\times10^{-4}$) was calibrated by the red-shift (up to 7 meV) of the GaN buffer peak. [7]

Growth on the GaN template was initiated with an approximately 80 nm thick GaN layer grown at $680^\circ\text{C}$ under Ga-stable conditions [8] with a growth rate of 140 nm/h in order to provide a smooth and clean surface for the (In,Ga)N/GaN MQW structures. Deposition of the MQW structures having 13 periods was carried out at a substrate temperature of around $580^\circ\text{C}$ under metal-stable conditions. [8] Large In surface segregation present for the growth of (In,Ga)N requires considerably high In/Ga and V/III flux ratios for even small In contents in the solid phase [9] and thus significantly lower Ga-flux during well than during barrier growth. Therefore, we used one of the Ga sources for the wells and the other for the barriers where the flux of the former Ga cell is about half of that of the latter. Three samples were grown under nominally identical conditions except for the In flux. Our growth strategy resulted in a smooth growth front free of accumulated metal as evidenced by a streaky RHEED pattern throughout the growth and the absence droplets when observing the sample with an optical microscope after growth.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

We discuss the structural and optical properties of the sample series. The structural parameters of the samples were determined using HRXRD. Figure 1 shows an $\omega$-2$\theta$ scan across the symmetric GaN(0002) reflection of the sample grown with the highest In flux together with a simulation. Besides the main GaN(0002) reflection, satellite peaks and MQW thickness fringes were observed. The excellent agreement of the experimentally observed profile with the simulation reveals abrupt interfaces and a high periodicity. The MQW parameters are thus extracted with high accuracy. For this sample, we find an In content of 0.119 and well and barrier widths of 4.2 and 7.2 nm, respectively. Analogous investigations of the other two samples return the same well and barrier widths but In contents of 0.085 and 0.025.

The optical properties of the sample series were first studied without externally applied strain. (Fig. 2) The transition energy shifts to the red linearly with In content. This observation is in agreement with both the increase of the electrostatic field inside the well and the lowering of the bandgap with increasing In content. The PL intensities of the three samples do not vary significantly, thus, indicating that the variation of electron-hole wavefunction overlap with In content is negligible. This result is expected since the wavefunctions are located at either heterointerfaces of the wells having identical thicknesses for the three samples under investigation.

We then investigated the dependence of the PL transition energies on the biaxial tensile strain state of the samples. For all samples the shift is linear with strain up to $8\times10^{-4}$ (Fig. 3). However, the direction of the shift changes with In content. A red-shift of up to 3 meV was

then calculated using self-consistent Poisson-Schrödinger calculations (SCPSCs) of the band profiles. Details about these calculations and the incorporation of polarization fields may be found elsewhere. [12] However, we may also use an approximation since the relative changes in polarization discontinuity are small and the electron and hole wavefunctions are located at either heterointerfaces. In this approximation, the changes in the polarization discontinuity can be converted in changes $\Delta E_{\mathrm{w}}$ of the electric field in the well. [13] The magnitude of the QCSE is then estimated by $\Delta E_{\mathrm{w}} \times d_{\mathrm{w}}$ with the well width $d_{\mathrm{w}}$. This approximation returns PL shifts virtually identical to those obtained by the SCPSCs.

It should be noted that the reduction of the QCSE calculated here is significantly smaller than that reported [5] for the same type of experiments. In Ref. 5, the calculations assumed a spatial variation in the Fermi level position across the wells, whereas here we use SCPSCs and derive the solution for constant Fermi level.

For the applied biaxial tensile strain considered here, we obtain a red-shift of the bandgaps (negative $\Delta E_{\mathrm{g}}$) and a decrease of the QCSE leading a competing blue-shift of the transition energies. The calculated shifts of the PL transition energies (Fig. 3) return the correct trend, i.e. a blue-shift with increasing In content. However, the experimental blue-shift with In content is larger than the calculated one. We can safely rule out the possibility of localized states leading to anomalous shifts of the bandgap energy as the emission from (In,Ga)N/GaN MQW structures has been shown to originate from extended states for at least the In contents under study here. [14] Furthermore, for the range of In contents under study here, it has been shown experimentally that the strain induced PL energy shift for bulk layers only weakly depend on the In content and follow the theoretical deformation potentials very closely. [15] For the highest strains considered here, the change of the bandgap energy via deformation potentials varies by less than 1 meV for In contents up to 12% with respect to the GaN bandgap. Also, free carrier screening due to high doping will lead to a red-shift of the PL transition energy itself but did not yield any significant change of the PL shift under consideration here. Hence, the experimental reduction of the QCSE is well in excess of the theoretical one. Since the spontaneous polarization discontinuity does not change with applied strain, we can conclude that the difference of the piezoelectric polarization constants of GaN and coherently grown (In,Ga)N is significantly larger than that predicted for unstrained (In,Ga)N and GaN. [1]

In order to give an estimate for the piezoelectric polarization $P_{\mathrm{PZ}}$ we express the discontinuity in piezoelectric polarization $\Delta P_{\mathrm{PZ}}$ between GaN and coherently grown $\mathrm{In}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}_{1-x} \mathrm{~N}$ using the Taylor series expansion $\Delta P_{\mathrm{PZ}}(x)=\varepsilon_{\|} x \Delta P_{\mathrm{PZ}}^{(1)}+\varepsilon_{\|}^{3} x \Delta P_{\mathrm{PZ}}^{(3)}+\ldots$ in biaxial strain $\varepsilon_{\|}$of the well. Note that even power terms cancel due to the symmetry of the wurtzite unit cell. If we take into account only the linear term then we arrive at $\Delta P_{\mathrm{PZ}}^{(1)} \approx-3.5 \mathrm{C} \mathrm{m}^{-2}$ which is about five times larger than the predicted value (note $\Delta P_{\mathrm{PZ}}^{(1)}=-0.6 \mathrm{C} \mathrm{m}^{-2}$ in Eq. 3). If, on the other hand, the predicted linear term is correct, we estimate a cubic term $\Delta P_{\mathrm{PZ}}^{(3)} \approx-700 \mathrm{C} \mathrm{m}^{-2}$.

## CONCLUSIONS

Summarising and concluding, it has been demonstrated that (In,Ga)N/GaN MQW structures with smooth and abrupt interfaces and well controlled individual layer thicknesses can be grown by plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy by careful adjustment of the In/Ga and V/III flux ratios. The piezoelectric polarization constants of coherently grown (In,Ga)N well with In contents up to 12% as probed by externally applied biaxial tensile strain have been shown to deviate considerably from the corresponding values for unstrained (In,Ga)N.
