# CONDENSATIONS OF PHONONS AT THE TETRAGONAL TO MONOCLINIC PHASE TRANSITION IN $\mathrm{ZrO}_{2}$

KEISHI NEGITA and HIROSHI TAKAO

Materials Research Laboratory, Central Engineering Laboratories, Nissan Motor Co. Ltd, Natsushima, Yokosuka 237, Japan

(Received 11 July 1988; accepted 5 October 1988)

Abstract-Based on lattice dynamical analyses, we suggest that condensations of $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ phonon modes at the tetragonal Brillouin zone boundary $M$ point are responsible for the tetragonal to monoclinic phase transition in $\mathrm{ZrO}_{2}$. The first order phase transition with simultaneous appearances of the primary order parameter $\left(M_{1}+M_{2}\right)$ and the shear and volume elastic strains in the monoclinic phase are interpreted in terms of the triggered phase transition. It is also suggested that the phonon modes at the cubic Brillouin zone boundary $X$ point are associated with the successive cubic-tetragonal-monoclinic phase transitions.

Keywords: Zirconia, phase transition, lattice vibrations, phonon condensation, free energy, spontaneous elastic strain, $\mathrm{ZrO}_{2}$.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Zirconia $\left(\mathrm{ZrO}_{2}\right)$ has a simple fluorite structure $\left(\mathrm{O}_{h}^{5}\right)$ in the highest temperature phase. With decreasing temperature, it undergoes a cubic to tetragonal $(c-t)$ phase transition at around $2300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and a tetragonal to monoclinic $(t-m)$ phase transition at around $1000^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. Space groups of the tetragonal and the monoclinic phases are $D_{4 h}^{15}$ and $C_{2 h}^{5}$, respectively [1-4]. At the $c-t$ phase transition, antiferro-distortive displacements of oxygen atoms along the $z$ direction occur and are interpreted as a consequence of a condensation of an $X_{2}^{-}$phonon mode at the cubic Brillouin zone boundary $X$ point [5].

The $t-m$ phase transition is characterized by a martensitic phase transition accompanied by large hysteresis in the phase transition temperature, greater than $200^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, and the generation of large shear and volume elastic strains in the monoclinic phase $[4,6]$. Although there are some reports on the mechanism of this phase transition [7-12], a definite clarification has not yet been made.

Using the martensitic nature of the $t-m$ phase transition, mechanically superior ceramics called partially stabilized zirconia (PSZ) have been fabricated. In PSZ, $\mathrm{ZrO}_{2}$ powders are sintered with small additions of oxides such as $\mathrm{CaO}, \mathrm{MgO}$ and $\mathrm{Y}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$, to form metastable tetragonal particles in the cubic matrix. If a stress, due to some cracks, is applied to the metastable tetragonal particles, a stress induced phase transition occurs from the metastable tetragonal to the stable monoclinic phase. As this phase transition is accompanied by a large volume increase, further growth of the crack is suppressed. Such a mechanism enables this kind of ceramic to have superior mechanical properties of enhanced strength and toughness [13-17]. Thus, also in the application of $\mathrm{ZrO}_{2}$ to industrial materials, clarification of the mechanism of the $t-m$ phase transition is needed.

In the present report, the mechanism of the $t-m$ phase transition is discussed in the following procedures. (i) Characteristics of the displacements of the atoms through the phase transition are investigated. (ii) On the basis of lattice dynamical analyses, these displacements are related to the condensation of phonons in the tetragonal phase, and a primary order parameter of the phase transition is determined. (iii) Generation of spontaneous shear and volume strains in the monoclinic phase are considered to arise from coupling between the primary order parameter and the homogeneous elastic strains. (iv) The successive $c-t-m$ phase transitions are briefly discussed based on the condensation of phonons in the prototype cubic phase.

## 2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### (i) Change of crystal structure through the t-m phase transition

In the cubic phase, $\mathrm{ZrO}_{2}$ has a simple fluorite structure with one formula unit in the primitive cell, $z=1$. As a consequence of the $c-t$ phase transition, oxygen atoms are displaced antiferro-distortively along the $z$ direction and two formula units are accommodated in the tetragonal primitive cell, $z=2$ (Fig. 1). Although $\mathrm{O}(1)$ and $\mathrm{O}(2)[\mathrm{O}(3)$ and $\mathrm{O}(4)]$ are in the same plane in the cubic phase, the $c-t$ phase transition renders $\mathrm{O}(1)$ and $\mathrm{O}(3)$ displaced downwards and $\mathrm{O}(2)$ and $\mathrm{O}(4)$ upwards. These displacements of the oxygen atoms are understood as a result of the condensation of phonon mode $X_{2}^{-}$at the cubic Brillouin zone boundary [5].

Fig. 1. Tetragonal primitive cell of $ZrO_{2}$. $Zr$ atoms are represented by closed circles and $O$ atoms by open circles. Oxygen atoms are displaced from the fluorite's positions along the $z$ axis owing to the $c-t$ phase transition.

The positions of $Zr$ and $O$ atoms in the tetragonal cell are reported as follows; $Zr(I)=(0,0,0)$, $Zr(II)=(1/2,1/2,1/2)$, $O(1)=(0,1/2,c)$, $O(2)=(1/2,0,1/2-c)$, $O(3)=(0,1/2,1/2+c)$, and $O(4)=(1/2,0,-c)$ with $c=0.185$ [2].

Using the lattice constant of the cubic Bravais lattice, $a$, the translational vectors of the tetragonal primitive cell are expressed as $a_{t}=(a_{t},0,0)\cong(a/\sqrt{2}$, $a/\sqrt{2},0)$, $b_{2}=(0,a_{t},0)\cong(-a/\sqrt{2},a/\sqrt{2},0)$ and $c_{t}\cong(0,0,a)$.

With decreasing temperature, $ZrO_{2}$ undergoes a transition from the tetragonal to the monoclinic phase with $Zr$ and $O$ atoms being further displaced from the positions in the tetragonal cell and large shear and volume strains are generated in the mono- clinic phase. There are some reports [4] about the lattice correspondence between the tetragonal and the monoclinic phases. The most probable one was recently suggested as $(100)_{m}//(110)_{t}$ and $[001]_{m}//[001]_{t}$ [11, 18], and we shall discuss the $t-m$ phase transition on the basis of this lattice correspondence.

In Fig. 2, the tetragonal and the monoclinic cells projected on the $ab$ plane are given. The origin of the monoclinic cell is displaced $(-1/4,0,-1/4)_{m}$ away from that of the tetragonal cell and the directions of the $a_{m}$ and $b_{m}$ axes are rotated $45^{\circ}$ from those of the $a_{t}$ and $b_{t}$ axes.

We notice in Fig. 2, that in the monoclinic phase the translational periodicities along the $a_{t}$ and $b_{t}$ directions become twice those in the tetragonal phase as a result of the $t-m$ phase transition, i.e. a cell doubling occurs from $z=2$ in the tetragonal cell to $z=4$ in the monoclinic cell. Such a cell doubling can be understood as a consequence of the condensation of phonons at the tetragonal Brillouin zone bound- ary $M$ point, $k_{M}=(\pi/a_{t},\pi/a_{t},0)$. In order to make clear the relationship between the displacements of the atoms due to the phase transition and the condensation of the phonons, phonons at the tetrag- onal Brillouin zone center $\Gamma$ and the boundary $M$ points were investigated.

### (ii) Phonons in the tetragonal phase
Since $ZrO_{2}$ consists of three atoms and there are two formula units in the primitive cell, there are $18(2\times3\times3)$ degrees of freedom for lattice vi- brations. As the rank of star at the $\Gamma$ and the $M$ points is unity, there are 16 symmetry elements at these points: $\{E|0\}$, $\{C_{2z}|0\}$, $\{C_{2x}|r\}$, $\{C_{2y}|r\}$, $\{C_{2a}|0\}$, $\{C_{2b}|0\}$, $\{C_{4z}^{+}|r\}$, $\{C_{4z}^{-}|r\}$, $\{I|r\}$, $\{\sigma_{z}|r\}$, $\{\sigma_{x}|0\}$, $\{\sigma_{y}|0\}$, $\{\sigma_{ab}|r\}$, $\{\sigma_{ab}|r\}$, $\{S_{4z}^{-}|0\}$, and $\{S_{4z}^{+}|0\}$ with $r=(1/2,1/2,1/2)$. Using the irreducible representations at the $\Gamma$ and the $M$ points, which are given in Tables Al and A2 in the Appendix [19], phonon symmetry species at these points are determined: at the $\Gamma$ point, $\Gamma_{Zr}=A_{2u}+E_{u}+B_{1g}+E_{g}$ and $\Gamma_{O}=A_{1g}+B_{1g}+2E_{g}+A_{2u}+B_{2u}+2E_{u}$, and at the $M$ point $M_{Zr}=M_{1}+M_{2}+M_{3}$ and $M_{O}=M_{1}+2M_{2}+M_{3}+2M_{4}$.

Eigenvectors of these phonon modes at the $\Gamma$ and $M$ points are calculated by the projection operator technique [20] and are given in Table 1 for the $Zr$ atoms and in Table 2 for $O$ atoms.

Of these modes, the $A_{2u}$ and $E_{u}$ modes are acoustic ones. At the $\Gamma$ point, non-degenerate $A_{2u}$, $B_{2u}$, $A_{1g}$, and $B_{1g}$ have eigenvectors along the $z$ axis and doubly degenerate $E_{g}$ and $E_{u}$ modes have eigen- vectors in the $xy$ plane. All the $M$ point phonons are doubly degenerate. The $M_{2}$ mode for $Zr$ atoms and the $M_{1}$ and $M_{3}$ modes for $O$ atoms are displacive modes along the $z$ direction and the other modes are those in the $xy$ plane.

Using a group theoretical technique, we can derive the representation of the primary order parameter of the phase transition. Investigation of the zone boundary $M$ point modes shows that $M_{1}^{1}+M_{2}^{1}$ and $M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2}$ are invariant under the symmetry operations of $\{E|0\}, \{C_{2y}|0\ 1/2\ 1/2\}, \{I|0\}, \{\sigma_{y}|0\ 1/2\ 1/2\}$, which are symmetry elements of the low temperature $C_{2h}^{5}$ phase. This result shows that the primary order parameter of the phase transition is characterized by a reducible representation $M_{1}+M_{2}$. The $E_{g}$ mode is characterized by $\mathbf{k}=0$ and cannot induce the change of the translational symmetry through the phase transition, suggesting that this mode is a secondary order parameter.

### (iv) Generation of elastic strains in the monoclinic phase

In the monoclinic phase, there appear large spontaneous shear and volume strains, which are consequences of the condensation of symmetry-adapted elastic strains $e_{1}+e_{2}$ and $e_{3}$ ($A_{1g}$) and $(e_{4}, e_{5})$ ($E_{g}$), respectively, in the tetragonal phase. However these elastic strains are characterized by $\mathbf{k}=0$ and are not responsible for the change of the space group, suggesting that these strains are secondary order parameters. Thus, in the $t-m$ phase transition, multiple order parameters are associated with the phase transition with the primary order parameter specified by the zone boundary $M_{1}+M_{2}$ mode and the secondary order parameters by the zone center $A_{1g}$ and $E_{g}$ modes.

The mechanism of the generation of the secondary order parameters (elastic strains) can be investigated in terms of the coupling between the primary order parameter and the secondary order parameters. For this purpose, we construct a free energy which is invariant under the symmetry operations of the parent tetragonal phase. Here we define the basis functions of the doubly degenerate $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ phonon modes as $(\phi_{1}, \phi_{2})$ and $(\psi_{1}, \psi_{2})$. Symmetric squares of $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ are calculated and reduced to irreducible representations with the basis functions given in the parentheses, eqns (1) and (2),

$$

$$

[M_{2}]^{2}=A_{1g}(\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2})+B_{2g}(\psi_{1}^{2}-\psi_{2}^{2})+B_{2u}(\psi_{1}\psi_{2}).\ (2)
$$

Characteristics of the free energy are included in the coupling free energy $F_c$. Coupling of the volume strains, $e_1+e_2$ and $e_3$, to the primary order parameter is linear in the strains and quadratic in the primary order parameter while the shear strain $(e_4, e_5)$, couples to the primary order parameter as a quadratic in both the strain and the primary order parameter.

By minimizing the free energy with respect to the strain, $\partial F/\partial e_i=0$, we obtain the spontaneous volume elastic strain, $(e_1+e_2)^s$ and $e_3^s$, proportional to the square of the spontaneous primary order parameter. This is similar to the case of elastic strain generation in the improper ferroelastic phase transition. However, we cannot understand the generation of the spontaneous shear elastic strain; $e_4^s=e_5^s=0$ if the $F_s$ contains the shear elastic energy up to the second order with respect to the shear strain.

For understanding the generation of the spontaneous shear elastic strain and the first order nature of the phase transition, the triggered phase transition proposed by Holakovsky [21] is suggestive. He discussed a phase transition characterized by two order parameters ($\eta$ and $\xi$) with the following free energy,
$$
F=a\eta^2+b\eta^4+c\xi^2+d\xi^4+e\xi^6+f\eta^2\xi^2, \quad (7)
$$
where only the coefficient $a$ is assumed to be temperature dependent as $a\propto(T-T_\eta)$ and all the other coefficients are constants. The lowest coupling between $\eta$ and $\xi$ is $\eta^2\xi^2$, which is a characteristic of this system. If such a coupling term is allowed in the free energy and the coupling coefficient $f$ has the property of $f\ll0$, he suggests that a first order phase transition is triggered at a temperature above $T_\eta$ with the simultaneous appearance of the order parameters $\eta$ and $\xi$.

In the case of $\mathrm{ZrO}_2$, coupling between the primary order parameter and the shear elastic strain is quadratic in both of these parameters as presented in eqn (6). If higher order shear strain energies up to the sixth order are included in $F_s$ and the coupling coefficient has a large negative value $\gamma\ll0\dagger$, Holakovsky's analysis is also applicable to this system. With the assignment of the parameters $\eta$ and $\xi$ in the Holakovsky's system to the primary order parameter and the shear strain in the present system, respectively, a first order phase transition can be triggered with the simultaneous appearance of the primary order parameter and the shear elastic strain. The condensation of the $E_g$ phonon mode discussed in the previous section may be induced by a bilinear coupling to the shear strain. These considerations show that the triggered phase transition is a possible mechanism for the $t-m$ phase transition in $\mathrm{ZrO}_2$. The appearance of such a phase transition indicates that there exists strong anharmonic couplings of the shear elastic strain to itself and the primary order parameter in the system.

### (v) Relationship between the $M_1$ and $M_2$ phonons in the tetragonal and in the cubic phases
As the $c-t-m$ phase transitions in $\mathrm{ZrO}_2$ are successive phase transitions with the prototype phase being the cubic phase, we are concerned with the relationship between the $M_1$ and $M_2$ phonons in the tetragonal and in the cubic phases.

In the cubic phase, there are three triply degenerate modes at the Brillouin zone center $\Gamma$ point; the acoustic $T_{1u}$ and the optical $T_{2g}$ and $T_{1u}$ modes. These modes split into non-degenerate longitudinal and doubly degenerate transverse modes ($X_4^-$ and $X_5^-$), ($X_2^-$ and $X_3^-$) and ($X_1^+$ and $X_6^+$), respectively, at the Brillouin zone boundary $X$ point. The $X$ point consists of three stars such as $\mathbf{k}_x^z=(2\pi/a, 0, 0), \mathbf{k}_y^z=(0, 2\pi/a, 0)$ and $\mathbf{k}_z^z=(0, 0, 2\pi/a)$; the $X_2^-$ phonon at $\mathbf{k}_x^z$ condenses at the $c-t$ phase transition [5]. As the result of such a condensation, $\mathbf{k}_z^z$ becomes the $\Gamma$ point and $\mathbf{k}_x^z$ and $\mathbf{k}_y^z$ the $M$ point in the tetragonal phase. Thus, $M_1$ and $M_2$ phonons at the $M$ points in the tetragonal phase can be related to those at the $X$ points in the cubic phase. This correspondence suggests that instabilities of the phonons at the cubic Brillouin zone boundary $X$ point play important roles for the successive $c-t-m$ phase transitions in $\mathrm{ZrO}_2$. Details will be discussed in a forthcoming paper.

## 3. CONCLUSION
From the lattice dynamical analyses, it is suggested that condensations of $M_1$ and $M_2$ phonons at the tetragonal zone boundary $M$ point are responsible for the $t-m$ phase transition in $\mathrm{ZrO}_2$. The first order phase transition with simultaneous appearances of the primary order parameter $(M_1+M_2)$ and the secondary order parameters (shear and volume strains) are interpreted in terms of the triggered phase transition suggested by Holakovsky. It is also suggested that instabilities of the phonon modes at the cubic Brillouin zone boundary $X$ point are associated with the successive $c-t-m$ phase transitions in $\mathrm{ZrO}_2$.
