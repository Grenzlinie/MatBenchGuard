# A RAMAN STUDY OF THE PEIERLS TRANSITION IN $(TaSe_{4})_{2} I$

A. Zwick and M.A. Renucci

Laboratoire de Physique des Solides, Associé au C.N.R.S.
Université Paul Sabatier, 118 Route de Narbonne, 31062 Toulouse Cédex (France)

and

P. Gressier and A. Meerschaut

Laboratoire de Chimie des Solides, Associé au C.N.R.S.
Université de Nantes, 2 Rue de la Houssinière, 44072 Nantes Cédex (France)

(Received 15 July 1985 by E.F. Bertaut)

Raman scattering measurements on the linear chain compound $(TaSe_{4})_{2} I$ are reported as function of temperature in the range 10 - 360 K. It is shown that the "new" lines observed, below the Peierls transition $(T_{0}=263 ~K)$ , close to some of the prominent peaks of the high temperature phase, are associated to long wavelength vibrational modes of the un- distorted lattice. The rapid rise in the Raman efficiency of these li- nes is attributed to changes in the first order susceptibility induced by changes in the electronic band structure at the onset of the CDW. The unusual hardening with temperature of the band at about $90 ~cm^{-1}$ , formely assigned to the amplitude mode of the CDw, is interpreted as a consequence on an inadequate one-oscillator fit to the real line- shape.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

X-RAY [1] AND ELECTRON diffraction [2] experiments have established that an incommensurate charge density wave develops in the low-dimensional conduc- tor $(TaSe_{4})_{2} I$ as a consequence of the Peierls tran sition at $T_{0}=263 ~K$ . The formation of the CDW was formerly suggested by various transport mea- surements showing non linear properties [3-5]. More recently, Raman investigations [6-8] on(TaSe4)2I have been undertaken in the hope that the modulation of the underlying structure may induce noticeable changes in the spectra. In- deed, a broad band of $A_{1}$ symmetry was repor ted by Sekine et al. [6] around $90 ~cm^{-1}$ in the low temperature phase, with interesting tempe- rature dependent properties. This band was as- signed to the amplitude mode of the CDW, al- though the phase transition does not manifest itself in a softening of this mode. The measu- rements of Zwick et al. [7], on the other hand, placed emphasis on changes in mode frequencies and mode strengthes at the Peierls transition.

A motive for the present contribution has been to supplement the aforementioned works and to assess critically earlier findings and con- clusions.

## 2. CRYSTAL SYMMETRY AND LATTICE VIBRATIONS

$(TaSe_{4})_{2} I$ crystallizes at room temperature in a tetragonal structure belonging to the I422-D $D_{4}^{9}$ space group, which has been first studied by Gressier et al. [9]. Infinite $(TaSe_{4})_{n}$ chains, linked together by charge transfer bond to iodine, run along the c axis, each containing two TaSe4 units per c length. There is only one chain and thus two formula units, i.e. 22 atoms, per primitive cell - which must not be confused with the crystallographic cell with twice as many atoms -, corresponding to 66 degrees of freedom. A group analysis of lattice vibrations at the $\Gamma$ point has been performed using the cor relation method. The decomposition into irreduc- tible representations of the $D_{4}$ point group isas follows :

$$\Gamma=7 \mathrm{~A}_{1}+9 \mathrm{~A}_{2}+7 \mathrm{~B}_{1}+7 \mathrm{~B}_{2}+18 \mathrm{E}$$

in disagreement with that of Sekine et al. [6].

The transformation properties and activities of these representations are given in Table 1. The three acoustical vibrations consist of one mode of $A_{2}$ symmetry and one doubly degenerate mode of E symmetry. The 63 optical zone-center modes then comprise 8 modes of $A_{2}$ symmetry, ex clusively infra-red active, and $7 A_{1}+7 B_{1}+7 B_{2}+17 E$ , all of which are Raman active with the E modes infra-red active. ### 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The samples studied were freshly cleaved single crystals of $(TaSe_4)_2I$, prepared earlier as described in ref. 9. The grown crystals are needle like, with typical dimensions $5x1x1\ mm^3$. The samples were immersed in the exchange gas (He) of an OXFORD CF 204 cryostat for room and low temperature measurements. The spectra were excited with the $4880\ Å$ line of an A ion laser. In order to remove unwanted inelastic scattering from the windows of the cryostat and from the air before them, the laser beam was focused at nearly grazing angle onto the (110) crystal face with polarization in or perpendicular to the plane of incidence containing the c axis. The scattered light was collected in the direction perpendicular to that of the laser beam. The scattering geometry used is not far from true backscattering inside the crystal because of the high refractive index, restricting both incident (i) and scattered (s) polarizations to $\vec{e}_{i,s} \parallel \vec{c}$ or $\vec{e}_{i,s} \perp \vec{c}$.

Care was taken to avoid local heating from the laser beam by limiting, with certain exceptions, the laser power to 25 mW. The Raman signals were analyzed in a T800 CODERG triple monochromator set at $2.6\ cm^{-1}$ resolution and processed by conventional photon-counting electronics.

#### 3.1. Raman scattering from the undistorted phase.
We show in Fig. 2 the "room temperature" spectra of $(TaSe_4)_2I$ for the three configurations :
(a) $\vec{e}_i \parallel \vec{e}_s \parallel \vec{c}$ , (b) $\vec{e}_i \parallel \vec{e}_s \perp \vec{c}$ , and
(c) $\vec{e}_i \perp \vec{e}_s \parallel \vec{c}$, in the frequency range extending from 15 to $330\ cm^{-1}$. The measurements were carried out with the laser power kept at 100 mW. The temperature was thus higher than the nominally registered one, and computed at 360 K as we shall explain below.

The $A_1$ spectrum of Fig. 2(a) displays seven features : five peaks at 65.5, 101.5, 184, 272.5 and $284.5\ cm^{-1}$, and two weaker structures at 147 and $162\ cm^{-1}$. Fig. 2(b), containing information about the $A_1$ and $B_2$ modes, should be more complex. In fact, the same structures as in spectrum (a) are again observed, with only one additional peak at $69.5\ cm^{-1}$. We thus confidently assign all but the $69.5\ cm^{-1}$ line to $A_1$ symmetry. In spite of higher transmission coefficient of light with electric vector in the plane of incidence at nearly Brewster's angle ($\vec{e}_i \parallel \vec{c}$), the (a) spectrum is much less intense than (b). ### 3.2. Evolution of Raman spectra with temperature.
The temperature dependence of the Raman spectrum of $(TaSe_4)_2I$ for $\vec{e}_i \perp \vec{c}$ in the range 10 - 360 K is illustrated in Fig. 3a. The scattered intensity has been corrected for phonon population effects, and has been normalized to the incident power light. Many changes in the spectrum occur when lowering the temperature through $T_O$. Noticeable increase in intensity is observed, depending on the modes. The sharp line at $101,5$ cm$^{-1}$ (from now on, we shall identify the lines by their wavenumbers at 360 K) exhibits the larger rise in height with decreasing temperature. Besides, "new" lines show up at $T_O$ close to the dominant peaks of the undistorted phase, as typified by the satellite line appearing on the high frequency side of the peak at 184 cm$^{-1}$. It is con venient to concentrate the analysis to the re- gion extending from 170 to $300 ~cm^{-1}$ that exhi bits the most prominent features. This part of the spectrum has been deconvolved by fitting a superposition of Lorentzian lines of adjus- table position, height and width to the experi- mental lineshapes by means of a least square computer programme. The computed wavenumbers and intensities of the most intense lines areplotted as functions of temperature in Fig.4 and Fig. 5, respectively. These graphes prove that the satellite line observed at low tempe- rature is not a "new" line of the distorted structure which could follow from $\vec{k} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$ modes of the high temperature phase. As a matter of fact, the line is already present in the spec- trum at $360 ~K$ . Its position at $186.3 ~cm^{-1}$ and relative intensity with respect to those of the nearby and strong peak at $182.5 ~cm^{-1}$ , together with the large linewidths, preclude its detec- tion above $T_{0}$ . Actually, the clear discrimination of the two neighbouring lines below $T_{0}$  results from their respective evolution with temperature that we shall comment on. The182.5 cm-1 line shifts monotonically to higher wa- venumbers with decreasing temperature in the whole range investigated. The $186.3 ~cm^{-1}$ line, displaying a linear shift in position above $T_{0}$  diverges from its neighbour when lowering the temperature down to $T_{0}$ . A marked increase of the splitting occurs at $T_{0}$ , before reaching the constant value of $8.5 ~cm^{-1}$ from $190 ~K$ . The rela tionship of the splitting with temperature for T > 190 K was further used as a check on sample temperature when the laser was kept at a power level higher than $25 ~mW$ .

The intensities of the two lines show a similar qualitative behaviour with temperature They are practically constant above $T_{0}$ and in crease both from $T_{0}$ , with decreasing tempera ture, but the rise is more rapid for the186.3 cm-1 line. Their ratio constitutes a fur- ther, independent check on sample temperature for $T<T_{0}$ . The narrowing of the two mates, their splitting and their relative change in intensity below $T_{0}$ thus explain the "onset" of the satellite line in the low temperature spectra. The present findings concerning the outstanding $186.3 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ line suggest that the greater number of Raman lines observed below $\mathrm{T}_{0}$ does not result from the breakdown of the $\vec{K}=\vec{O}$ selection rule due to the destruction of the translation invariance at the onset of the incommensurate CDW. Lines corresponding to $\vec{K}=\vec{O}$ modes of the undistorted structure, less efficient than the totally symmetric $A_{1}$ modes in the high temperature phase, could show up below $\mathrm{T}_{0}$ because of a more rapid rise in the intensity. This increase of the first order susceptibility should be induced by changes in the electronic band structure related to the formation of the CDW.

The Raman spectra of $(\mathrm{TaSe})_{2} \mathrm{I}$ for $\vec{e}_{\mathrm{i}} / / \vec{c}$ measured at 160,225,270 and $360 \mathrm{~K}$ are presented in Fig. 3b. The most striking feature of these spectra is the broad, temperature dependent line observed in the $50-125 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ range, in addition to peaks already assigned to $A_{1}$ and $E$ modes of the undistorted structure. The broad line unquestionably strengthens and sharpens with decreasing temperature, while its position shifts to lower frequencies. This line has been first reported by Sekine et al. in the zz spectrum $(\vec{e}_{i} / / \vec{e}_{s} / / \vec{c})$ of the distorted structure and ascribed to the amplitude mode of the CDW. Their assignment is supported by the observed symmetry of the Raman tensor, with only the b component non-zero, and the temperature dependence of both the damping constant and the coupling coefficient they determined from a one oscillator fit. However, the unexpected hardening of the line frequency with temperature calls for further explanations. Sekine et al. have outlined the similarity with the frequency shift of the amplitude mode in KCP [13], attributed to the interaction of the CDW mode with the vibration of the water molecules [14] located in between the Pt chains.

They suggested that the influence of the motion of the iodine atoms on the dynamics of the CDW might affect the frequency of the amplitude mode in $(\mathrm{TaSe})_{2} \mathrm{I}$. This explanation is plausible, although the comparison with KCP may be questionable as the Peierls transition does not take place in this material in the accessible temperature range.

The present spectra afford supplementary information. As a matter of fact, the lineshape of the broad band attributed to the amplitude mode is clearly temperature dependent and could result from the superposition of two main components (see, for example, the spectrum at $160 \mathrm{~K}$ ). One, centered at about $100 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, is present at all temperatures. The other shows up only below $\mathrm{T}_{0}$, at lower frequency (about $90 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ at $160 \mathrm{~K}$ ). This low frequency component, weakening and softening with increasing temperature,

According to the above analysis, the anomalous shift in fre- quency of the broad line could result from a one oscillator fit inappropriate to the actual line- shape.

In conclusion, we have attributed the subs- tantial changes in "hard" mode strengthes ob- served for the Raman lines at $T_O$ to variations of the first order susceptibility related to changes in the band structure at the Peierls transition. More about the lattice dynamics of the undistorted phase of $(TaSe_4)_2I$ and about the lattice distorsion itself must be known in order to explain why these variations affect considerably certain modes. We suspect the tem- perature dependent lineshape of the structure previously assigned to the amplitude mode of the CDW to result from the superposition of features with different temperature behaviour. The unex- pected hardening of this mode with temperature should be an artefact of the one-oscillator fit used to analyse the measurements, inadequate to the actual profile of the line.
