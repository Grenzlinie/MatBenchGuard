# The calculations of phonon dispersion relations for single-wall carbon armchair and zigzag nanotubes

Yufang Wang, Bin Zhang, Qinghua Jin, Baohui Li, Datong Ding, Xuewei Cao *

Department of Physics, Nankai University, Tianjin 300071, PR China

Received 21 April 2006; accepted 13 January 2007

## Abstract

A 3D single-wall carbon nanotube can be viewed as a 2D graphite sheet rolled into a 3D cylinder. In the study of dispersion relations of carbon nanotubes, the consistent force parameters for 2D graphite sheets have to be modified to include the curvature effect. The present paper reports a series of calculations of phonon dispersion relations for single-wall carbon armchair, zigzag nanotube in which the curvature effect has been properly treated. The symmetry of crystal vibration mode at the centre of Brillouin zone is analyzed based on our numeric results and the structure symmetry of the nanotubes.

## 1. Introduction

The discovery of carbon nanotube by Iijima [1] enlarges the carbon allotropic substance family with a new member after fullerenes.

A single-wall carbon nanotube (SWCNT) can be described as a graphite sheet rolled into a cylindrical shape. There are two incoordinate positions, referred as $\vec{a}_{1}$ and $\vec{a}_{2}$, on the unrolled honeycomb like the lattice of the graphite sheet. The great circle of the SWCNT corresponds to the vector $\vec{C}_{\mathrm{h}}=n \vec{a}_{1}+m \vec{a}_{2}$ on 2D hexagon lattice of the graphite sheet. Therefore, the integers (n and m) define the structure of a SWCNT uniquely. An armchair nanotube corresponds to the case of $n=m$, and a zigzag nanotube corresponds to the case of $m=0$. For more general cases $m \neq n$, the corresponding SWCNT is called chiral nanotube because of its helical structure. $\vec{C}_{\mathrm{h}}$ is referred as the chiral vector. Since the structure of SWCNT is relatively simple, theoretical predictions on its structure-property relations are reliable.

The electronic structure of a SWCNT can be obtained simply from that of 2D-graphite in terms of the zone-folding idea [2]. The phonon dispersion relation of SWCNT can be deduced from 2D-graphite phonon dispersion relation analogically. Nevertheless, as compared with the electronic structure, the phonon behaviors are more complex because of the 3D nature of the carbon atom's motion. In fact, the zone-folding method alone does not always give the correct dispersion relation for a carbon nanotube, especially in low frequency region. Therefore, additional physical consideration must be introduced, as pointed out by Jishi [3] and Saito [2]. For example, the out-of-plane tangential acoustic (TA) mode of a 2D graphite sheet does not give zero energy at $\Gamma$ point of the Brillouin zone when rolled into a 3D nanotube. Here, all the carbon atoms on the cylindrical surface move radially at $\vec{k}=0$, which corresponds to a radial breathing mode (RBM) with non-zero frequency [2]. The Raman-active low frequency breathing mode is important for experimental studies of carbon nanotubes.

Raman spectroscopy is an efficient experimental technique to gain the information on the structure of carbon nanotubes. In particular, the appearance of the RBM signifies the formation of nanotubes. Thus, Raman spectra can be used as an analytic tool to monitor carbon nanotube production. In order to make a qualitative analysis or a semi-quantitative illustration of the Raman spectra, we have carried out the calculation of the phonon dispersion relations of SWCNT.

### 3. Symmetry of lattice vibration modes

Lattice vibrational modes at the centre of Brillouin zone can be classified by the point group which is isomorphous with the factor group of crystal space group. The total number of phonon modes is $12 n$ for armchair and zigzag tubes. According to the analysis previous, there are 12 non-degenerate modes if $n$ is odd or 24 ones if $n$ is even [2], respectively. By using the method proposed by Saito [2], our calculated results indicated that there are only 12 non-degenerate modes for even and odd $n$. Therefore, our results imply that the armchair and zigzag tubes should have higher symmetry.

If considering the (5,0) zigzag tube as an example, a symmetry operation with a rotation of $180^{\circ}$ around the tube axis and a translation of half period along the tube axis will recover the positions of all the atoms in the unit cell. According to Seitz symbol, the matrix corresponding to this symmetry operation is

$$
\left[\begin{array}{c}
1 \\
x_{j}^{\prime} \\
y_{j}^{\prime} \\
z_{j}^{\prime}
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cccc}
1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \cos \pi & \sin \pi & 0 \\
0 & -\sin \pi & \cos \pi & 0 \\
\frac{T}{2} & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}
1 \\
x_{i} \\
y_{i} \\
z_{i}
\end{array}\right]
$$

The atomic positions in the unit cell before and after applying the symmetry operation above mentioned, $(x_{i}, y_{i}, z_{i}; i=1,2,\dots,20)$ and $(x_{j}^{\prime}, y_{j}^{\prime}, z_{j}^{\prime}; j=1,2,\dots,20)$, are listed in Table 1a and b, respectively. Since 0.426 nm is the period of (5,0) tube, $z^{\prime}=0.4260, 0.5680$ nm are equivalent to $z^{\prime}=0.0000, 0.1420$ nm, respectively. It is obviously that the $i$th atom before applying the symmetry operation corresponds to the $j$th atom ($j=i+5$ for $i=1-5$, and $i=11-15$; or $j=i-5$ for $i=6-10$ and 16-20) after the symmetry operation. It is in agreement with our calculations of the phonon dispersion relation.

### 4. Discussion and conclusion

Since we calculate the phonon dispersion relation of 3D-SWCNT by using the force constant parameters for 2D-graphite, this physical unreasonable treatment bring about some inadequacy into our calculated results. This is the reason why $\omega d-d$ relation departs from linearity as the tube diameter less than 1.0 nm (see Fig. 1). The inadequacy will be reduced with the increase of the tube diameter or say the decrease of the tube curvature.

Armchair and zigzag carbon nanotubes possess the structure of non-symmorphic space group. By utilizing the higher symmetry factor group $D_{2 \mathrm{nh}}$ for the symmetry analysis of phonon modes in the armchair and zigzag nanotubes we find that the number of Raman- and IR-active vibrations is 8 and 3, respectively, which is different from the results previously predicted. The number and symmetry of non-degenerate and degenerate modes are consistent with the numeric results of phonon dispersion relations for SWCNTs.
