# Application of Infrared Spectroscopy to Order-Disorder Problems in Simple Ionic Solids

William B. White*
Materials Research Laboratory
The Pennsylvania State University
University Park, Pennsylvania

(Received in final form January 23, 1967; Refereed)

## ABSTRACT
Infrared powder transmission spectra have been obtained on selected pairs of structures related by distortional and order-disorder phase transformations. It is shown that lowering of space group symmetry causes resolvable and interpretable detail to appear in the spectrum only when distortions are extreme. Ordering tends to cause dramatic changes in the spectra. In both cases a factor group analysis is necessary to interpret the results.

## Introduction
The degree of order that may occur when two different atoms are on the same sub-lattice is sometimes a difficult problem to solve by x-ray dif- fraction methods because the superlattice reflections may be weak. It has long been known that the infrared spectrum of a crystal was quite sensitive to the degree of ordering and that particularly the broad infrared bands, char- acteristic of ionic compounds, split into a number of sharper peaks. This was particularly well documented in the case of the feldspars and the spinels by Hafner and Laves (1). Compounds with the same chemical composition may be related by polymorphic transformations which are not of the order-disorder type and White and Roy (2) have shown the spectra of polymorphs are quite dis- tinct when the transformation involves either change of coordination and/or change in bond type.

The number of infrared bands which appear in the spectrum of a par- ticular structure is entirely determined by the arrangement of atoms in the unit cell and the space group symmetry. The frequencies of the bands are determined by the masses of the atoms and force constants of the bonds. One can, therefore, predict the features of a spectrum from symmetry considera- tions alone without assumptions concerning the nature of the bonding.

The purpose of this paper is to compare the observed infrared spectra of a series of structures with the results predicted from symmetry calculations with a view to putting the detection of order by infrared spectroscopy on a firmer theoretical basis.

### Theoretical Methods

The 3N-3 (where N is the number of atoms in the unit cell) zero- momentum transverse optical phonon modes of a crystal may be classified into the irreducible representations of the appropriate factor group from which the infrared and Raman selection rules may be immediately written. The factor group method was devised by Bhagavantam and Venkatarayudu (3) in 1939 and after a long period of inactivity has become a common method for classifying lattice vibrations. The method was reviewed in some detail earlier (4) and only an outline is given here.

The essence of the procedure is to construct a reducible representation for the general 3N-dimensional translation vector which describes the position of all atoms in the unit cell. The characters of the reducible representation for each symmetry class, $\rho$, are given by $\chi_{\rho}=\omega_{\rho}( \pm 1+2 \operatorname{Cos} \theta)$ where $\omega_{\rho}$ is the number of atoms which remain invariant under the operations of class $\rho$ and $\theta$ is the rotation angle of the symmetry operation. The equation takes the positive sign for proper rotations and the negative sign for improper rota- tions. The identity operation is regarded as a proper rotation of zero degrees, the mirror plane as an improper rotation of zero degrees, and the center of inversion as an improper rotation of 180 degrees.

The number of vibrational modes in each irreducible representation of the factor group is given by $\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{i}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{~h}} \sum_{\rho} \chi_{\rho} \mathrm{g}_{\rho} \chi_{\rho}^{\mathrm{i}}$ when h is the order of the factor group, $\chi_{\rho}$ is the reducible character determined above, $\mathrm{g}_{\rho}$ is the order

of class, $\rho$, and $\chi_{\rho}{ }^{\mathrm{i}}$ is the character of the $\mathrm{i}$ th irreducible representation under class $\rho$. The summation is over all classes of the group.

To determine the classification and selection rules for any crystal, one need know only the space group and structural arrangement of the atoms with respect to the symmetry elements. Since this information is identical for all compounds with the same structure type, one need make only one set of calculations per structure type. In the following sections are given factor group analyses of a number of polymorphic and order-disorder related com- pounds with a comparison with the observed spectra.

### Experimental Methods

Some of the compounds used in this study were obtained from commer- cial sources ($\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$-rutile, $\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$-anatase, $\beta$-$\mathrm{Ga}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$, and $\alpha\mathrm{Al}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$), with the phase purity checked by x-ray powder diffraction. A natural specimen of brookite was used. $\alpha\mathrm{Ga}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$ was synthesized as described by Roy, Hill and Osborn (5). $\mathrm{Li}_{3}\mathrm{NbO}_{4}$, $\mathrm{MgSb}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{6}$ and $\mathrm{ZnSb}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{6}$ were prepared by heating pressed pellets of the proper ratio of component oxides in air at $950^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for 48 hours. Identification and phase purity were determined by x-ray diffraction. $\mathrm{MnTiO}_{3}$ was prepared by heating pelletized equimolar $\mathrm{MnO}_{2}$-$\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ powders in vacuo at $970^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, then regrinding and reacting at $990^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. to remove all traces of orthotitanate.

Infrared spectra were obtained on a Perkin-Elmer model 21 spectro- photometer equipped with KBr optics. Samples were mounted by the KBr pressed pellet technique.

### Structures Related by Distortions

In the study of polymorphs by infrared spectroscopy (2), no attempt was made to compare the spectra observed with theoretical predictions. To have a basis of comparison, spectra of several additional polymorphic phases are compared with the factor group results.

### The Case of the $\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ Polymorphs:

### The Case of the Ga₂O₃ Polymorphs:

### Structures Related by Order-Disorder

Four examples of order-disorder-related structures are presented here. One example is shown to indicate a limitation of infrared absorption similar to that of the distorted structures, namely that more bands are pre- dicted than are observed. One example illustrates a case in which the order- ing is predicted to make little change in the infrared spectrum, and two exam- ples are shown for which infrared spectroscopy makes an excellent tool for studying ordering.

### The Case of Ordered Rocksalt Structures:
Rocksalt ($O_h^5$, Fm3m, Z = 4) provides the simplest example of a lattice vibration spectrum. The smallest (rhombohedral) Bravais cell con- tains only two atoms, there is thus only one vibrational mode $\Gamma = T_{1u}$(IR). The spectra of many rocksalt structure compounds have been published and each consists of one broad band in the infrared (for example Jones et al (16)).

There are a large number of ordered compounds based on the rocksalt structure and one example is $Li_3NbO_4$ (17, 18). The lithium and niobium exhibit a 1:3 ordering which reduces the space group to $T^3$ (I 23) with 8 formu- la units per unit cell. The lithium and 24 of the oxygens lie on the $24\ \text{f}$ general positions while the niobium and the remaining 8 oxygens lie on the $8\ \text{c}$ positions.

The Case of $LiAl_5O_8$:

Perhaps the best known example of the infrared spectrum of an ordered structure is $LiAl_5O_8$. At temperatures above 1295 degrees, this compound disorders to a spinel structure (21). Below $1295^\circ$C there is probably a 1:3 ordering of the lithium and aluminum on the octahedral sites and the space group is reduced from $O_h^7$ to $O^7$ with a concurrent change in unit cell from face centered cubic to primitive cubic. The smallest Bravais cell of the ordered structure is equal in volume to the full spinel cell.

The infrared spectrum of $LiAl_5O_8$ is very rich and has received considerable attention in the literature (21, 22, 23). The spectrum is illustrated in Fig. 7. This case has been treated in detail by DeAngelis and White (23) and is discussed only in outline here. The change in symmetry from face centered cubic to primitive cubic removes a four-fold redundancy and causes a quadrupling of all bands. Likewise, the reduction in symmetry causes some new modes to appear. The species are

$$\Gamma = 6A_1(R) + 8A_2(-) + 14E(R) + 21 T_1(IR) + 20 T_2(R)$$

21 infrared bands are predicted, some of which occur in the far infrared beyond the range of the instrument used in this study. In this case a rather good correlation occurs between the predicted and the observed spectrum.

The Case of Rutile-Trirutile:

The trirutile structure is built up in compounds of the $ZnSb_2O_6$ type by an ordering of zinc and antimony along the c-axis of the rutile cell. The trirutile cell contains three rutile cells stacked along c but the space group remains exactly the same, $D_{4h}^{14}$, as for rutile (24, 25). This stacking increases all the characters of the reducible representation by a factor of three and thus triples each band causing it to split into three components.

$$
\begin{split}
\Gamma = 3A_{1g}(R) + 3A_{2g}(-) + 3B_{1g}(R) + 3B_{2g}(R) + 3E_g(R) + 5A_{2u}(\text{IR, E } 11\text{ c}) \\
+ 6B_{1u}(-) + 11\text{Eu (IR, E} \perp \text{c)}
\end{split}
$$

### Conclusions

It is shown that not all fine structure in the infrared spectra of ionic solids is due to ordering and that contrawise, ordering does not necessarily generate fine structure. A factor group treatment of the structure will often predict when order-disorder behavior should be observable in the infrared.
