## ABSTRACT

c-Axis oriented single crystals of relaxor ferroelectric $Sr_xBa_{1-x}Nb_2O_6$ ($x$=0.61 and 0.75) were investigated using polarized Raman spectroscopy and the behavior of optical phonon as a function of temperature across the phase transition from space group $P4bm$ to $P\bar{4}b2$. Based on the polarized and depolarized spectra mode assignments were carried out. Modes of symmetry $A_1(LO)$, $B_1$, and $B_2$ were identified. From the detailed line shape fitting of the spectra, frequencies of the prominent modes associated with $NbO_6$ stretching vibration were obtained. In contrast to the earlier reported results, these modes are found to exhibit discontinuous changes across the transition. The phase transition temperatures are found to be 336 and 322 K for $x$=0.61 and 0.75, respectively.

## 1. Introduction

Disordered ferroelectric systems are of great interest from the fundamental as well as applications point of view. One important class of such disordered systems consists of relaxor ferroelectrics such as lead-based $Pb(Mg_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3$ [1], $Pb(Zn_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3$ [2], $La_xPb_{1-2x/3}(Zr_yTi_{1-y})O_3$ [3,4] and Pb-free $Sr_xBa_{1-x}Nb_2O_6$ (SBN) [5]. The uniaxial relaxor ferroelectric SBN, is of particular interest due to its well-known pyroelectric [5,6], electro-optic [7], photorefractive [8,9], piezoelectric [10], and non-linear optical properties [11]. The ambient temperature ferroelectric phase of SBN belongs to the point group $C_{4v}$ with space group $P4bm$ with five formula units per unit cell. This is a tungsten-bronze (TB) type disordered structure [12,13], and consists of a framework of $NbO_6$ octahedra sharing the corner oxygen to form three different size cavities with the unit cell formula $A_5B_{10}O_{30}$. Rietveld analysis of the X-ray diffraction pattern of SBN has revealed that Sr/Ba ions occupy $2a$ and $4c$ sites [13], and partial occupancy of sites corresponding to $A$ atoms and the oxygen atoms. The tungsten bronze structure can accommodate as many as 10 $A$ atoms while only 5 Sr/Ba exist in the formula; as a result, one $4c$ site (symmetry $C_s$) is vacant and one of the $2a$ sites is partially occupied. In SBN crystal, there are a total 6 $A$ ($2a$ and $4c$) sites in the unit cell for five Sr and Ba ions; therefore, there is a built-in-disorder among the $A$ sites and there are many ways that the Sr and Ba ions can occupy the available sites [13].

The $Sr_xBa_{1-x}Nb_2O_6$ ($0.25 \leq x \leq 0.75$) has its ferroelectric polar axis along the $c$-direction, and the ferroelectric to paraelectric phase transition temperature ($T_c$) was reported to change linearly with $x$ [14,15]. A structural phase transition takes place from ferroelectric point group $C_{4v}$ (space group $P4bm$) to a paraelectric phase of point group $D_{2d}$ (space group $P4b2$), although both point groups are non-centrosymmetric, $C_{4v}$ is polar and $D_{2d}$ is nonpolar [16]. Therefore, the local environment around the constituent cations ($Ba^{2+}$, $Sr^{2+}$, and $Nb^{5+}$) is strongly modified when the crystal passes through its ferroelectric phase transition, this modification being different for the different crystallographic sites. In particular, the $2a$ site, which is partially occupied by $Sr^{2+}$ ions, changes its local symmetry from $C_4$ in the ferroelectric phase to $C_{4h}$ in the paraelectric one; the $4c$ site (partially occupied by $Sr^{2+}$ and $Ba^{2+}$ cations) from $C_s$ to $C_{2v}$, and the $2b$ site (completely filled by the $Nb^{5+}$ cations) from $C_{2v}$ in the ferroelectric to $D_{2h}$ local symmetry in the paraelectric phase. The $8d$ sites that are completely filled by $Nb^{5+}$ remain in their $C_1$ symmetry [13].

Understanding of phonon spectra in mixed crystals is important from a fundamental point of view. Raman scattering is one of the most powerful non-destructive techniques to investigate structural phase transitions. Particularly, the optical modes which are associated with the ferroelectric to paraelectric phase transition are of great interest. Polarized Raman studies on SBN single crystal of different compositions have been reported earlier [12,17,18]. In addition, a few rare-earth-doped SBN have also been

Subsequently temperature dependence of a few selected modes was studied in $x=0.5$ crystal and it was found that there are monotonic changes in mode frequencies as a function of temperature [18]. In the present work, we have carried out detailed polarized Raman measurements on $c$-axis oriented single crystals of SBN with $x=0.61$ and 0.75 at ambient and unpolarized measurements as a function of temperature. The spectra are analyzed quantitatively using spectral line shape fitting and the dependence of mode frequencies on temperature is used for identifying the phase transition. In addition, group theoretical analysis of the expected modes is discussed and limitations arising from partial occupancy of a site are pointed out.

## 2. Sample preparation and characterization

The single crystals $\mathrm{Sr}_{x}\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Nb}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{6}$ ($x=0.61$ and 0.75) used in the present study have been grown using double crucible Stepanov technique [21] and characterized for their dielectric behavior [22]. Raman scattering measurements were done on $c$-axis oriented crystals of $10\ \mathrm{mm} \times 10\ \mathrm{mm} \times 1\ \mathrm{mm}$ size using a Renishaw micro Raman spectrometer (model InVia). Radiation of $514.5\ \mathrm{nm}$ was focused to $\sim 2\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ in diameter on the samples. A charge coupled device (CCD) system with accuracy $\sim 1.5\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ was used to detect the scattered signal. The Linkam heating and freezing microscope stage (THMS 600) was used to carry out temperature dependent measurements in the range of 80-550 K.

## 3. Results and discussion

Group theoretical analysis of this structure is required for obtaining the expected number of vibrational modes of each irreducible representation. The space group $P4bm$ allows atoms to be placed on four distinct crystallographic sites: $2a(C_4), 2b(C_{2v}), 4c(C_s)$, and $8d(C_1)$. The irreducible representations of vibrations of atoms occupying these sites are available in literature and are given as [23]:

$$2a: A_1 + A_2 + 2E \tag{1a}$$

$$2b: A_1 + B_2 + 2E \tag{1b}$$

$$4c: 2A_1 + A_2 + B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E \tag{1c}$$

$$8d: 3A_1 + 3A_2 + 3B_1 + 3B_2 + 6E \tag{1d}$$

$$\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}=20A_1 + 16A_2 + 15B_1 + 19B_2 + 37E\quad [=144\ \mathrm{df}] \tag{2}$$

and the total degrees of freedom (df) add up to $144\ (48\times3)$. On the other hand, the expected total degrees of freedom for the actual compound are $135\ (45\times3)$. Recently, Speghini et al. [14] presented another group theoretical analysis using 46 atoms and consequently 138 degrees of freedom as:

$$\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}=19A_1 + 15A_2 + 14B_1 + 18B_2 + 36E\quad [=138\ \mathrm{df}] \tag{3}$$

This analysis assumed that O(4) sits at the $2b$ site with 100% occupancy instead of $4c$ site with 50% occupancy, and the Ba(1) site occupancy is also taken as 100%. Thus the applicable formula would be $\mathrm{Ba}_6\mathrm{Nb}_{10}\mathrm{O}_{30}$. Here we present an alternate group theoretical analysis for the actual compound $(\mathrm{BaSr})_5\mathrm{Nb}_{10}\mathrm{O}_{30}$ (45 atoms and 135 degrees of freedom). Instead of taking Ba/Sr occupancy as 0.82 and 0.85 at sites $2a$ and $4c$ respectively, these are taken as 0.5 and 1 (see column 4 of Table 1). Then the contributions of these sites to the total irreducible representations are:

$$\mathrm{Ba(1)}[2a]: [A_1 + A_2 + 2E] \times \frac{1}{2} \tag{4}$$

$$\mathrm{O(4)}[4c]: [2A_1 + A_2 + B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E] \times \frac{1}{2} \tag{5}$$

These, when added to the contributions of other atoms that have 100% occupancy, (see Table 1 and equations from (1a)-(1d)), leads to the following result:

$$\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}=18\frac{1}{2}A_1 + 15A_2 + 14\frac{1}{2}B_1 + 18B_2 + 34\frac{1}{2}E\quad [=135\ \mathrm{df}] \tag{6}$$

where three of the modes have half-integral coefficients, which may appear unphysical. In fact, the main difficulty arises because it is impossible to place 5 Ba atoms among the allowed crystallographic sites without considering fractional occupancy. A non-integral value of number of modes is a direct consequence of partial occupancy. However, comparing the number of modes of different irreducible representations for 138 and 135 degrees of freedom, one can see that the redistribution is marginal. It may be pointed out that mode $A_2$ is optically inactive. The modes $A_1$ and E are both Raman and IR active; while $B_1$ and $B_2$ are only Raman active.

The internal vibrational modes of $\mathrm{NbO}_{6}$ octahedra are also found in the Raman spectrum of SBN crystals. A regular octahedral $X Y_{6}$ ion with the symmetry $O_{h}$ has six normal vibrational modes represented as:

$$
\Gamma_{\mathrm{vib}}=\mathrm{A}_{1 g}+\mathrm{E}_{g}+2 \mathrm{~T}_{1 u}+\mathrm{T}_{2 g}+\mathrm{T}_{2 u} \tag{7}
$$

The modes $v_{1}\left(\mathrm{~A}_{1 g}\right), v_{2}\left(\mathrm{E}_{g}\right)$, and $v_{3}\left(\mathrm{~T}_{1 u}\right)$ are stretching vibrational modes; whereas, $v_{4}\left(\mathrm{~T}_{1 u}\right), v_{5}\left(\mathrm{~T}_{2 g}\right)$, and $v_{6}\left(\mathrm{~T}_{2 u}\right)$ are bending vibra- tion modes [23]. As the site symmetry of the $\mathrm{NbO}_{6}$ octahedral units is lower than $O_{h}$ in the tetragonal crystal, the $\mathrm{NbO}_{6}$ octahedron is not regular and hence the degenerate modes split in the crystal according to the following correlation scheme: $\mathrm{A}_{1 g}\left(v_{1}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{A}_{1}, \mathrm{E}_{g}\left(v_{2}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{A}_{1}+\mathrm{B}_{1}, \mathrm{~T}_{1 u}\left(v_{3}, v_{4}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{A}_{1}+\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{T}_{2 g}\left(v_{5}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{E}+\mathrm{B}_{2}$, and $\mathrm{T}_{2 u}\left(v_{6}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{E}+\mathrm{B}_{1}$. The strongly polarized mode at around $630 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ corresponds to the symmetric stretching mode $\mathrm{A}_{1 g}$ of $\mathrm{NbO}_{6}$ octahedra. The ferroelectric phase transition in $Sr_xBa_{1-x}Nb_2O_6$ $(0.25 \leq x \leq 0.75)$ crystals has been studied extensively by different research groups using dielectric measurements [27-30]. The ferroelectric to paraelectric phase transition is also accompanied by a structural phase change from tetragonal system with point group $C_{4v}$ to $D_{2d}$ point group symmetry. The low temperature ferroelectric phase of SBN crystal has the space group $P4bm$ $(C_{4v})$ with a tetragonal tungsten bronze structure. As mention earlier, this structure can be described as a framework of niobium octahedra ($NbO_6$) with five formula groups per unit cell. The five Sr/Ba ions are distributed among six possible sites with $C_4$ and $C_s$ site symmetries; therefore, there exists a vacancy, which leads to a significant disorder in the crystal [13,15]. The ferroelectric feature in this structure is due to the displacement of the metal atoms away from the oxygen planes along the ferroelectric axis (c-axis). The phase transition is considered as the movement of the metal atoms back into the oxygen planes [18]. Since $C_{4v}$ is not a subgroup of $D_{2d}$, it has been argued that an intermediate phase $D_{4h}$ is necessary for the construction of a correlation table

relating the ferroelectric $C_{4v}$ phase to the high-temperature $D_{2d}$ state [18]. In other words, it is impossible to have a second-order transition directly from $C_{4v}$ to $D_{2d}$ without passing through the intermediate phase. The presence of metal atoms in the $C_{4}$ site symmetry along the $z$-direction has their representation in $A_{1}$ symmetry. Hence the corresponding optical modes ($A_{1}$) in the Raman spectra are expected to show discontinuous changes in the temperature dependence near the transition temperature ($T_{c}$). The most intense peak centered at around $630\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ has the predominant $A_{1}$ character with phonon polarization along the ferroelectric $z$-axis [17]. This peak is in fact a combination of two optical phonons ($\sim590$ and $634\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$) as shown in Fig. 2. Thus these peaks are expected to be the most appropriate to probe the ferroelectric to paraelectric phase transition, as ferroelectricity arises along this axis [14].

Temperature dependent Raman spectra of SBN61 and SBN75 are shown in Fig. 4. It is evident from Fig. 4 that while most of the modes are insensitive to temperature, the 590 and $634\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ modes exhibit maximum change. In addition, the mode at around $503\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ disappears at temperature above 360 K. The peak at around $850\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ appears in scattering configurations where the $B_{1}$ and $B_{2}$ modes are mostly contributing [17]. This mode is shifted toward lower frequency side and broadens with increasing temperature. However, this mode is present above the phase transition temperature also. The most prominent changes are found to take place in the region of 590 and $630\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. The mode frequencies were obtained from fitting the spectra to multi-Lorentzians. The mode frequencies versus temperature for these modes are shown in Fig. 5 for both the crystals. One can see discontinuous changes for both samples. The transition occurs at 336 and 322 K in SBN61 and SBN75 crystals, respectively. The line-widths also show the same type of anomaly across the transition temperature. It may be pointed out that in an earlier study only a change of slope of the peak-center of the unresolved $630\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ peak was found across the ferroelectric phase transition [14]. On the other hand, as the position of both the modes could be obtained precisely from the present spectral line shape analysis, a discontinuous change in mode frequency emerges clearly, as expected. It may be pointed out that there appears to exist a controversy about the nature of the phase transition based on the critical exponents and dimensionality of the model used [31–35]. The discontinuous changes in the Nb–O stretching vibration frequency across the transition, found in the present experiments, shows that the transition is indeed first order. The phase transition temperature in $x$=0.61 crystal is consistent with that reported earlier. Furthermore, phase transition in $x$=0.75 has not been studied earlier using Raman spectroscopy.

### 4. Conclusions

Structural phase transitions in $\mathrm{Sr}_{x}\mathrm{Ba}_{1-\chi}\mathrm{Nb}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{6}$ single crystals have been investigated using temperature dependent Raman spectroscopy. Group theoretical analysis and polarized Raman measurements were used for the assignment of the modes. In contrast to earlier results, the present studies show discontinuous change in Nb–O stretching vibrational frequency across the phase transition from $P4bm$ to $P\bar{4}b2$. The phase transition temperatures are found to be 336 and 322 K for $x$=0.61 and 0.75, respectively.
