# PHONON AND MAGNON RAMAN SCATTERING IN THE WEAK FERROMAGNET FeF₃
P. MOCII and C. DUGAUTIER
Laboratoire de Physique des solides, Université de Paris VI, Ecole Normale Supérieure, 75005 Paris, France

Received 11 October 1972

The $FeF_{3}$ Raman spectrum shows 4 one-phonon lines, as expected from symmetry, and a 2-magnon line: the shape of the latest agrees with a next-neighbours exchange model between spins 5/2 in a cubic antiferromagnet, tak-ing $J=10.07 ~cm^{-1}$

Below $365^{\circ} K$ [1], $FeF_{3}$ is a 2 sublattices canted ferromagnet. This transparent crystal shows a bimo-lecular rhombohedral unit cell and belongs to the $D_{3 d}^{6}$ space group [2]. The structure results from an elonga-tion along the <1,1,1> axis of a cubic arrangement: however, the distortion from cubic symmetry is small. The canting has been shown to be mainly due to the Dzialoshinsky-Moriya interaction $D \cdot(S_{i} \Lambda S_{j})$ where i and j are the nearest magnetic $Fe^{3+}$ neighbours: D is parallel to <1,1,1> and equal to 49 kG at $0^{\circ} K$ [3]; in addition there is a single ion anisotropy field $H_{A}$ along<1,1,1>, estimated to a few hundred gauss. The spins then lie in the (1,1,1) plane; the in-plane anisotropy is too small to be measured [4]. In view of the high ordering temperature, the antisymmetric exchange and the anisotropy field are certainly very small com-pared to the Heisenberg isotropic exchange. The Hei-senberg exchange has to be well described by $2 J S_{i} \cdot S_{j}$ where we use only one exchange integral between nearest neighbouring spins $5 / 2(Fe^{3+}$ as $Mn^{2+}$ belongs to the $d^{5}$ configuration and the orbital momentum can be completely neglected in the ground state), since the next neighbours interaction is expected to be substantially smaller. For some experimental stud-ies, and particularly the 2 magnon Raman scattering, the theory is then the same as for the well known transition metal alkali fluorides ( $RbMnF_{3}$ , for in stance).

The used sample was cut perpendicular to the pseudo-cubic axes, with linear dimensions of approx-imately 0.5 mm. Raman scattering was measured un-der $4880 \AA$ and $5145 \AA$ lines illumination of an argon ion laser. $FeF_{3}$ shows a sharp phonon spectrum and a2 magnon Raman line. Due to birefringence and Fara-day rotation, it was not possible to take advantage of the analysis of the polarisation of the spectra.

At $300^{\circ} K$ , the phonon spectrum shows 3 main lines at 167,304 and $440 cm^{-1}$ and a weaker line at96 cm⁻¹. Both lines shift towards high frequencies when the temperature is decreased (105, 187, 306 and $446 cm^{-1}$ at $2^{\circ} K$ ). In the cubic approximation there are no Raman active modes: the observed spectrum then arises from the distortion to cubic symmetry. From our calculation, it results that the normal modes in $FeF_{3}$ at k=0 decompose as follows: $1 \Gamma_{1}^{+}+2 \Gamma_{2}^{+}+$  $3 \Gamma_{3}^{+}+2 \Gamma_{1}^{-}+2 \Gamma_{2}^{-}+4 \Gamma_{3}^{-}$ (where we have excluded the acoustic zero energy modes); 6 of them $(2 \Gamma_{2}^{+}+$ 4 Γ₃⁻) are infra-red active; if one takes into account that for phonon scattering, the Raman tensor is sym-metric, one expects 4 Raman active modes $(1 \Gamma_{1}^{+}+$ 3 Γ₃⁺) in agreement with our experimental data. In the absence of polarized spectra easy to interpret, no attempt was made to separately identify $\Gamma_{1}^{+}$ and $\Gamma_{3}^{+}$ modes.

The 2 magnon Raman line was identified from its frequency, shape and temperature dependence. At $2^{\circ} K$ its maximum intensity occurs at $562 \pm 2 cm^{-1}$ . Its shape fits well with the calculated $\Gamma_{3}^{+}$ spectrum for an isotropic cubic Heisenberg antiferromagnet with next-neighbours interaction between 5/2 spins [5], if one takes $J=10.07 cm^{-1}$ (Fig. 1). As shown in fig. 1 the fit is good but not perfect; however, it is as good as for the cubic antiferromagnet $RbMnF_{3}$ [6]. By simply multiplying the exchange value found for $RbMnF_{3}$ by the ratio $T_{FeF_{3}} / T_{RbMnF_{3}}$ of the ordering tempera tures, one expects a J value equal to $10.3 cm^{-1}$ in FeF₃, in very good agreement with our determination. The ordering temperature of the above cubic, or nearly cubic, spin 5/2 magnetic crystals can then be calculated with a good precision, using the $J$ measurement found from Raman scattering. Due to the rather poor optical quality of the used samples we could not observe one magnon scattering which is ex- pected to lie near $2.3 ~cm^{-1}$ [3]. However, from the antiferromagnetic resonance data and from our own determination of $J$ , it is possible to calculate the ani sotropy field along $\langle 1,1,1\rangle$ , since infra-red measurements provided $D$ and $H_{c} H_{A}$ separately (where $H_{c}=$  $2 J S \mu_{B})$ : we find $H_{A}=290 G$ (where the single ion anisotropy is written as $+\mu_{B} H_{A} \cdot S$ ). The indirect evaluation of ref. [3] provides $H_{A}=107 G$ but con sequently it gives for $H_{c}$ a value higher than ours by about a factor 3: this indirect determination seems unreasonable in view of our results and the above dis- cussion.
