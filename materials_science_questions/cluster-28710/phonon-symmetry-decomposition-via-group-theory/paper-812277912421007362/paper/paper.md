# Unit Cell Group Analysis of Orthorhombic Crystals: Detailed Investigation of the Effect of Arbitrary Choice of Axes

A. Rulmont
Department of General Chemistry, Institut of Chemistry (B6), Sart-Tilman, B-4000 Liege, Belgium

The results of the group theoretical analysis of the normal vibrations of crystals having orthorhombic space groups can depend on the arbitrary choice of axes because there is no unambiguous way to define a unique axis. This situation creates some confusion in the results published by different authors. We have investigated in detail the problem of the unit cell group analysis of the orthorhombic structures $D_{2h}$ and $D_2$ and the results are presented in two tables for practical applications. It is shown that it is possible to express the irreducible representations as a function of experimental parameters, which gives a clearer view of the physical situation than the conventional notation ($A_g$ etc.).

## INTRODUCTION

For crystals belonging to the $D_{2h}$ and $D_2$ classes of symmetry, it is not possible to define a unique axis. All three rotation axes have the same order ($C_2$) and therefore the labelling of the unit cell axes is arbitrary. Since the result of the unit cell group analysis depends on the choice of axes, it is ill-defined for these orthorhombic space groups.

Brooker¹ has already considered this problem and has made a compilation of all the different results published for the $K_2SO_4$ structure [$D_{2h}^{16}$ (Pnma)]. Of course, because the result depends on an arbitrary choice of axes, a direct comparison of the analysis made by different authors is meaningless.

Brooker proposed a method for defining a unique axis for that particular space group: his $z$ axis is perpendicular to the mirror plane $m$, and no ambiguity can arise because the three symmetry planes are different. However, such a criterion cannot apply to all orthorhombic space groups. For example, all the planes of symmetry are the same for $D_{2h}^{1}$ (Pmmm) and $D_{2h}^{2}$ (Pnnn). We think that it is essential to deal with this problem from a more general point of view.

Further, Brooker's method implies some change in the conventional axis labels in the character tables. He defines the $B_{1g}$ modes to be symmetric with respect to the $z$ axis but in the commonly used character tables,² the convention is just reverse!

In fact, it is not possible to use a single criterion to define a unique $z$ axis in these structures. This is why we think that in order to tackle the problem of the unit cell group analysis of such structures it would be more fruitful to use the following scheme.

We propose the following conventions to define the axes orientation: the character tables of Wilson et al.² are used without any modification; and the structure of the unit cell is described using the standard crystallographic setting.³ These conventions have already been applied by Montero and Schmölz⁴ to the Raman spectra of crystals having the $K_2SO_4$ structure.

In the framework of these conventions, we define in which cases the result of the unit cell group analysis effectively depends on the choice of axes. We shall show that the result is not always ill-defined.

We introduce a new notation to describe the representation of the normal modes of vibration when the standard convention still allows some arbitrary choice of axis orientation. The representation written with this new notation will not depend on any arbitrary choice of axes.

## CONSEQUENCES OF THE STANDARD SETTING CONVENTION ON THE CHOICE OF THE AXES ORIENTATION

### All axes fixed

For some $D_{2h}$ and $D_2$ space groups, the standard setting completely defines the orientation of the three axes because each axis is characterized unambiguously by a symmetry operation.

For example, in $D_{2h}^{11}$, the length of each unit cell edge is fixed by the orientation of the following symmetry operation:

$$a_0 \underset{x}{\parallel} 2 \quad ; \quad b_0 \underset{y}{\parallel} 2_1 \quad ; \quad c_0 \underset{z}{\perp} \underset{\text{direction}}{m}$$

### Arbitrary choice of orientation of some axes

For some space groups, the standard setting determines the length of only one axis but allows an arbitrary choice of the direction of the other two. In $D_{2h}^{5}$, the axis $c_0$ is physically defined by the orientation of the single mirror plane in the crystal ($c_0 \perp m$) but the length of $a_0$ and $b_0$ can be interchanged without modification of the space group symbol.

# SPECIAL CASES

In the space groups $D_{2h}^{9}$, $D_{2h}^{10}$ and $D_{2h}^{15}$ all sites are invariant with respect to the allowed $xy$ permutation.

In the space groups $D_{2h}^{27}$, $D_{2}^{8}$ and $D_{2}^{9}$ only cyclic permutations are possible: $abc \to cab \to bca$. This means that the length of only one axis can be chosen freely but the orientation of the other two is automatically determined by the algebraic form of the equivalent point coordinates.

In the space groups $D_{2}^{2}$ and $D_{2}^{4}$ an $xy$ permutation is theoretically possible but the site coordinates determine the orientation of all the axes. For example, in $D_{2}^{2}$, the positions of the $2b$ sites are: $(x\frac{1}{2}0)$ and $(\bar{x}\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2})$. If we do not change the origin of the unit cell, we cannot find in the available sets the $xy$ permutated coordinates: $(\frac{1}{2}y0)$ and $(\frac{1}{2}\bar{y}\frac{1}{2})$. In fact, the length of each unit cell parameter is determined by the algebraic form of the point coordinates as defined in the International Tables.³

# CONCLUSIONS ON THE RESULT OF THE UNIT CELL GROUP ANALYSIS

It is known from the factor group analysis tables of Adams and Newton⁶ that each site contributes independently to the representation of the normal modes of vibration. If we adopt the above-mentioned conventions, we can now define exactly in which cases the result of the unit cell group analysis is ill-defined; all necessary information is summarized in Tables 1 and 2.

Three different possibilities can arise: (1) The standard setting does not allow any permutation of axes: it completely determines the result of the unit cell group analysis. (2) The standard setting does not change if some axes are relabelled, because the crystal contains only invariant sites. In this case, we will have only one possible representation for the normal modes. (3) Only when permutation sensitive sites are occupied does the result of the analysis depend on an arbitrary choice of axes orientation.

It is now straightforward to decide what kind of crystal we are dealing with.

## Examples

The standard setting determines the length of all axes. The structure of the $K_{2}SO_{4}$ type has the space group $D_{2h}^{16}$ ($Pnma$) with the atoms in the following positions:⁷ K(1), K(2), S, O(1), O(2) on $(4c)$ sites, and O(3) on $(8d)$ sites. Only one choice of axes is physically possible, corresponding to $a_{0}\perp n$ and $b_{0}\perp m$, so that the representation will be defined with the following unit cell parameters in the case of $K_{2}SO_{4}$: $a_{0}=0.7483$ nm, $b_{0}=0.5772$ nm and $c_{0}=1.0072$ nm.

The irreducible representations of the normal modes can be determined from the tables of Adams and Newton:

$$
\Gamma_{t}=5\Gamma\ (4c)+\Gamma\ (8d)
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\Gamma_{t}&=13A_{g}+8B_{1g}+13B_{2g}+8B_{3g}+8A_{u}\\
&+13B_{1u}+8B_{2u}+13B_{3u}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\Gamma_{\text{vib}}=\Gamma_{t}-B_{1u}-B_{2u}-B_{3u}
$$

Note that when $\Gamma_{t}$ is calculated by the correlation method,⁸ it appears immediately that the result depends on the site orientation in the crystal; this orientation is physically defined with respect to the unit cell parameters. If the site orientation is determined from Boyle's tables,⁹ it implies that we are necessarily working in the framework of the standard setting.

The allowed permutations of axes leave all the sites invariant. $K_{2}HgCl_{4}\cdot H_{2}O$ crystallizes in the orthorhombic space group $D_{2h}^{9}$, and the unit cell contains 4 molecular units (7). The atoms are in the following positions: Hg $(4e)$, K(1) $(4g)$, K(2) $(4h)$, Cl(1) $(4g)$, Cl(2) $(4h)$, Cl(3) $(8i)$, $H_{2}O$ $(4f)$, with $a_{0}=0.827$ nm, $b_{0}=1.163$ nm and $c_{0}=0.889$ nm.

It can be seen from Table 1 that all the sites are invariant but that an $xy$ permutation is still possible. From a vibrational point of view, this means that the number of vibrations corresponding to each cartesian direction has to be independent of an $xy$ permutation.

This is illustrated in Table 3, where it clearly appears that the number of $B_{2u}(y)$ equals the number of $B_{3u}(x)$ and the number of $B_{2g}(\alpha_{zx})$ equals the number of $B_{3g}(\alpha_{zy})$ for all the possible sites in that space group.

The result of the unit cell group analysis is unique:

$$
\Gamma_{t}=\Gamma\ (4e)+2\Gamma\ (4h)+2\Gamma\ (4g)+\Gamma\ (4f)+\Gamma\ (8i)
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\Gamma_{t}&=13A_{g}+13B_{1g}+11B_{2g}+11B_{3g}\\
&+9A_{u}+9B_{1u}+15B_{2u}+15B_{3u}
\end{align*}
$$

The same situation arises for the space groups $D_{2h}^{15}$ and $D_{2h}^{10}$.

# EXPRESSION OF THE REPRESENTATIONS OF THE NORMAL MODES BASED ON PHYSICAL PARAMETERS

It is essential to realize that, if the representation of the normal modes is sometimes ill-defined, this is just the result of the convention adopted. From a physical point of view, the unit cell group analysis has obviously only one definite answer.

In space groups such as \(D_{2h}^{24}\), the name given to each unit cell edge is immaterial, but *each edge is unambiguously defined by its length*. Therefore, if we express the result of the unit cell group analysis as a function of the unit cell edge lengths, we will always obtain the same answer whatever orientation of axes we choose.

The physical meaning of each irreducible representation is given in the character tables by the cartesian coordinate combinations which are the basis functions of that representation. We propose in Tables 4 and 5 a new notation for the irreducible representations. We use the subscripts \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) because we focus our attention on the *length* of the unit cell edges and not on an orientation of axes which is completely arbitrary.

We can now express the representations of the normal modes of \(Ba(MnO_4)_2\), using these notations.

The representation corresponding to choice 1 can be written as
$$
\begin{align*}
\Gamma_{t} &= 7\Gamma_{aa} + 9\Gamma_{ab} + 8\Gamma_{ac} + 9\Gamma_{bc} \\
&+ 7\Gamma_{0} + 9\Gamma_{c} + 8\Gamma_{b} + 9\Gamma_{a}
\end{align*}
$$
with \(a = 1.471\) nm, \(b = 1.186\) nm and \(c = 0.739\) nm, while the representation corresponding to choice 2 is
$$
\begin{align*}
\Gamma_{t}' &= 7\Gamma_{a'a'} + 9\Gamma_{a'b'} + 9\Gamma_{a'c'} + 8\Gamma_{b'c'} \\
&+ 7\Gamma_{0} + 9\Gamma_{c'} + 9\Gamma_{b'} + 8\Gamma_{a'}
\end{align*}
$$
with \(a' = 1.186\) nm, \(b' = 1.471\) nm and \(c' = 0.739\) nm. However, as we have \(a' = b\) and \(b' = a\), it is not difficult to see that both representations have the same physical meaning. For example, there are eight vibrations with a dipole moment variation parallel to the axis of length 1.186 nm and nine vibrations corresponding to the other two axes. Our notation is directly connected to experimental parameters and gives a clear picture of the crystal vibrational properties.

# SITE PERMUTATIONS INDUCED BY CHANGE OF ORIGIN

Boyle and Lawrenson \(^{5,9,10}\) have shown that for some space groups there exist several possible choices for the origin of axes. Furthermore, the Wyckhoff labelling of the sites can depend on the choice of origin. This may occur for some of the orthorhombic groups we are dealing with. However, any origin shift in the description of the atomic positions cannot change the number of vibrations corresponding to a given crystallographic axis. It clearly appears from the correlation method that this number depends only on the multiplicity, the symmetry of the site and its orientation in the crystal.⁸ When we shift the unit cell origin, all the permuting sites have the same site group and the same orientation with respect to the crystal axes.¹⁰ In consequence, they are necessarily involved in crystal normal modes belonging to the same representations.

For instance, Table 3 gives the different site contributions to the unit cell group analysis for the \(D_{2h}^9\) space group. The tables of the permutating sites¹⁰ show that the only possible permutations are \((a \leftrightarrow b \leftrightarrow c \leftrightarrow d)\), \((e \leftrightarrow f)\) and \((g \leftrightarrow h)\). It can readily be verified from Table 3 that these permutations do not change the result of the unit cell group analysis.

# CONCLUSIONS

In the orthorhombic space groups, it is not possible to define a unique axis, because all the rotation axes have the same order (\(C_2\)). However, in many cases, if we just adopt some simple conventions to describe the orientation of the axes, the result of the unit cell group analysis is perfectly definite. The representation of the normal modes can be unambiguously defined by the conventional notation (\(A_g, \dots\)).

When the result of the analysis depends on some allowed permutation of axes, it would be more accurate to express the representation of the normal modes using a new notation based on the unit cell parameters.
