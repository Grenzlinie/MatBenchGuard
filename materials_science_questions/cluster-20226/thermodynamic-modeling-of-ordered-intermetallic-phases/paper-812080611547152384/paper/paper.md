# Thermodynamics of the Nonstoichiometric $\boldsymbol{\beta_1}$-PtZn Phase Exhibiting the L1₀ Structure

Yu-Jen Hsiao, Y. Austin Chang, and Herbert Ipser¹

Materials Department, College of Engineering and Applied Science,
University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee, Milwaukee, Wisconsin 53201

## ABSTRACT

Thermodynamic activities of zinc in the $\beta_1$-PtZn and the $\alpha$-PtZn phases were determined at 1273°K using an isopiestic method. The theoretical thermodynamic equations by Chang and co-workers for the nonstoichiometric L1₀ phases were modified to include the different interactions between the first nearest neighbor and the second nearest neighbor atoms. An analysis of the activity data for the $\beta_1$-PtZn phase at 1273°K in terms of the modified theoretical equation yields the following values: $\alpha = 3 \times 10^{-4}$, $\eta = 1.0$, and $\ln a_{Zn,0} = -4.65$. The thermodynamic data of the $\alpha$-phase were evaluated in terms of the subregular solution model. The partial quantities of platinum and the integral quantities for the $\alpha$- and $\beta_1$-phases were obtained by means of the Gibbs-Duhem relationship. A theoretical relationship between the enthalpy of formation and the model parameters for the L1₀ phases was derived and correlated with the $\beta_1$-PtZn data and other data available in the literature.

The three group VIII transition metals, nickel, palladium, and platinum, react with the group II metal zinc to form ordered intermetallic phases with the L1₀ structure at the equiatomic composition. This structure may be viewed either in terms of a body-centered tetragonal or a face-centered tetragonal lattice. At the stoichiometric composition, in terms of the body-centered lattice, all the A atoms occupy the body-centered positions and the B atoms the corner positions for a perfectly ordered structure. However, at any finite temperature, some degree of lattice disorder prevails. At nonstoichiometric compositions, constitutionally induced lattice disorder occurs in the lattice, in addition to that due to thermal vibration. This lattice disorder contributes to the thermodynamic properties of the intermetallic phases. Chang and co-workers (1, 2) derived theoretical equations to describe the compositional dependences of the activity and the partial molar enthalpy of the L1₀ phases. The validity of the theoretical equations was verified using the extensive data for $\beta_1$-NiZn and $\beta_1$-PdZn (3-5). Like these phases, the $\beta_1$-PtZn² phase also exists over a wide range of homogeneity. The exact range of homogeneity is discussed later in this paper. The objective of the present investigation is to determine the thermodynamic properties of the $\beta_1$-PtZn phase using an isopiestic technique and to further test the validity of the theoretical equations of Chang and co-workers. Moreover, these theoretical equations are slightly modified in order to consider the possible difference between the interactions of first and second nearest neighbor atoms, which were assumed to be identical in the original development of the theoretical model (1, 2). Lastly, a relationship between the parameters of the theoretical model and the enthalpy of formation is derived. The validity of this relationship is examined using the data obtained in the present paper for the $\beta_1$-PtZn phase, as well as data available in the literature for other L1₀ phases.

## Experimental Method and Results

The isopiestic technique employed in the present study has been described previously (6). Platinum samples of approximately 1g were prepared from a 0.025 mm foil (99.99%; Engelhard, New Jersey). Zinc samples were prepared from a rod (99.999%; United Mineral and Chemical Corporation, New York). Platinum samples were cleaned with aqua regia, while the zinc samples with a solution of 15% HCl, 15% HNO₃, and 70% H₂O. The quartz tubes which contained the samples were evacuated at $5 \times 10^{-2}$ Pa for 24 hr, sealed, and then placed in the furnace at ~ 1000°C for 10-12 days. The experiments were terminated by quenching the quartz tube in cold water. The composition of each sample was computed from its weight change. During the course of each experiment, the sample temperature $T_S$ was kept constant within ± ¾°K, while the temperature of the zinc reservoir $T_R$ within ± ¼°K. Knowing $T_S$, $T_R$, and the vapor pressures of zinc at these temperatures, the activity of zinc in each alloy was computed using Eq. [1] of Ref. (6). The vapor pressure values of zinc used are those selected by Hultgren *et al.* (3, 7). The experimental results expressed in terms of the activity of zinc $a_{Zn}$, are summarized in Table I. Based on the activity data at 1000°C as shown in Fig. 1, the $\alpha$-phase boundary of the $\alpha + \beta_1$ two phase field is determined to be 27.5 (± 1) atomic percent (a/o) Zn, while the $\beta_1$-phase boundary to be 42 (± 1) a/o Zn. ### Data Evaluation and Discussion
Thermodynamic equations for the nonstoichiometric β₁-PtZn phase.—Chang and co-workers (1, 2) derived theoretical thermodynamic equations to account for the compositional dependence of the activity and the partial enthalpy in nonstoichiometric LLₒ phases. In this model, Chang and co-workers assumed that the interaction enthalpy between an atom and its eight first nearest neighbors is the same as that between the same atom and its four second nearest neighbors, neglecting all interactions of higher order. Recently, Ipser, Neumann, and Chang (11) found that the influence of second nearest neighbor interactions is also important for some antistructure B2 phases. For this purpose, they introduced a new parameter η into the equations originally derived (2, 12, 13). Theoretical equations are presented for the LLₒ phases, which now also take into account this new parameter η. In view of the findings for the antistructure B2 phases, it is anticipated that the value of η may deviate from a singular value for the LLₒ phases. Furthermore, the ratio of the lattice parameters (c/a), which determines the relative distances between first and second nearest neighbors, may also influence the value of η.

[7] with $\epsilon^{\alpha}=-317.3$ kJ/g-atom and $\eta^{\alpha}=-209.9$ kJ/g-atom, and that for the β₁-phase from Eq. [5] with $\alpha=3\times10^{-4}$, $\eta=1.0$, and ln $a_{Zn,0}=-4.65$.

The parameter η is defined as the ratio of the interaction enthalpies between the second nearest and first nearest neighbor atoms

$$
\eta \equiv \frac{V_{\mathrm{AB}}^{\prime}}{V_{\mathrm{AB}}} \tag{1}
$$

where $V_{\mathrm{AB}}=H_{\mathrm{AB}}-1/2\left(H_{\mathrm{AA}}+H_{\mathrm{BB}}\right)$ and $V_{\mathrm{AB}}^{\prime}=H_{\mathrm{AB}}^{\prime}-1/2\left(H_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\prime}+H_{\mathrm{BB}}^{\prime}\right)$. The term $H_{ij}$ is the interaction enthalpy for a pair of first nearest neighbors $i$-$j$, and $H'_{ij}$ that for a pair of second nearest neighbors $i$-$j$. The other basic parameter of the model is the degree of lattice disorder at stoichiometry, α, which is defined as

$$
\alpha \equiv\left(\frac{N_{\mathrm{A}}^{\beta}}{N}\right)_{\chi=0} \equiv\left(\frac{N_{\mathrm{B}}^{\alpha}}{N}\right)_{\chi=0} \tag{2}
$$

where $N_{\mathrm{A}}^{\beta}$ is the number of A atoms on the β-sublattice sites (the wrong sites), $N_{\mathrm{B}}^{\alpha}$ is that of B atoms on the α-sublattice sites, and $N$ is the total number of atoms; $\chi$ is the deviation from stoichiometry in terms of the atomic fraction and is defined as $\chi\equiv x_{2}-0.5$, with $x_{2}$ being the atomic fraction of component 2. The degree of lattice disorder at any composition $\chi$ is

$$
z \equiv\left(\frac{N_{\mathrm{A}}^{\beta}}{N}\right)_{\chi} \tag{3}
$$

It reduces to α when $\chi=0$. Values of $z$ may be computed from the following relationship expressing $z$ as a function of $\chi$ and α

$$
z=\frac{-\chi+\sqrt{\chi^{2}+4 \alpha^{2}}}{2} \tag{4}
$$

which is Eq. [51] in Ref. (2). Using these parameters and employing the method presented in Ref. (11), the following theoretical equations for the compositional dependences of the activities and partial molar enthalpies can be derived for LLₒ phases with antistructure defects

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln \frac{a_{i}}{a_{i, 0}}=\epsilon_{i} \ln & \frac{z(1-2 \alpha)}{\alpha(1-2 z)} \\
& +\left(\frac{1 / 2 \eta}{1-1 / 2 \eta}\right)(2 \ln 2 \alpha)\left(2 \epsilon_{i}+\chi\right) \chi \quad[5]
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta \bar{H}_{i}-\Delta \bar{H}_{i, 0}}{R T}= & \left(2 \alpha+\frac{\epsilon_{i} \chi-4 \alpha^{2}}{2 z+\chi}\right. \\
& \left.+\left(\frac{1 / 2 \eta}{1-1 / 2 \eta}\right) 2\left(2 \epsilon_{i}+\chi\right) \chi\right) \ln 2 \alpha \quad[6]
\end{aligned}
$$

where $a_{i,0}$ and $\Delta \bar{H}_{i,0}$ are the activity and the partial molar enthalpy of the component $i$ at the stoichiometric composition, $\epsilon_{i}=+1/2$ for $i=1$ and $\epsilon_{i}=-1/2$ for $i=2$, and $R$ is the gas constant.

A comparison of the above expressions with Eq. [19] and [21] of Ref. (11) shows that the only difference is the coefficient ½ for the quantity η. For the antistructure B2 phases, the coefficient is ¾ if the expression $[3\eta/(4-3\eta)]$ in Eq. [19] and [21] of Ref. (11) is written as $(3\eta/4)/(1-3\eta/4)$. The coefficients ½ and ¾ represent the ratio of the number of the second and first nearest neighbor atoms in the LLₒ and B2 structures, respectively. With $\eta=1$, which is the original assumption made by Chang and co-workers (1, 2), Eq. [5] and [6] reduce to their expressions for the "one-and-half approximation," if the term β in Chang's equation is substituted by its simplified form $2\ln 2\alpha$ (compare Eq. [105] and [110] in Ref. (2)).

$$

Relationship between the model parameters and the enthalpy of formation of the $Ll_{0}$-phases.-In two recent publications, Neumann et al. (20, 21) discussed the relationship between the disorder parameter $\alpha$ and the enthalpy of formation in ordered intermetallic phases with the B2 structure, and Ipser et al. (11) investigated the influence of the second nearest neighbor interactions on this relationship. Following the method outlined in Ref. (11) and (21), a corresponding relationship can be derived for intermetallic phases exhibiting the $Ll_{0}$ structure

$$
\frac{\Delta H}{R T}\left(1-1 / 2 \eta\right)=1 / 2 \ln 2+1 / 2 \ln \alpha \tag{8a}
$$

For the case $\eta=1.0$, as was originally assumed by Chang and co-workers (1,2) in the development of their theoretical equations, Eq. [8a] reduces to

$$
\frac{\Delta H}{R T}=\ln 2+\ln \alpha \tag{8b}
$$

This expression, however, can also be obtained by simplification of Eq. [98b] of Ref. 2 for $\alpha<<1$, if the term $4 N V_{\mathrm{AB}}$ (where $N$ is Avogadro's number) is identified with $\Delta H$.
