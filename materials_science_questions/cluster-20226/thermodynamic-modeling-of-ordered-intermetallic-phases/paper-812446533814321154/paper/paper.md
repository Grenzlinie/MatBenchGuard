# ON THE ORDER-DISORDER TRANSITION OF CHEMISORBED H ON Ni (111)

H. Kasai and A. Okiji
Department of Applied Physics, Osaka University, Suita, Osaka 565, Japan

K.H. Bennemann
Institute f. Theoretical Physics, F.U.B., 1000 Berlin 33 West Germany

(Received 8 July 1980 by B. Mühlschlegel)

The order-disorder transition of chemisorbed H on Ni (111) has been analyzed by Kittler and Bennemann in terms of the lattice gas model. The main purpose of this paper is to refine the above calculation. The expres- sion with regard to adlayer domains is given for the half-order LEED spot intensity, from which the dependence of the transition temperature and of the LEED spot intensity on H-coverages is calculated. In particular, the H-coverage dependence of the transition-temperature $T_{c}$ and the LEED intensity $I$ for $\theta \simeq 0.25$ is restudied. We present results obtained by using only pair interactions between the H-atoms and no 3H-interactions. It is shown that the observed asymmetric phase diagram with respect to H-coverages can result from the two kinds of pair interactions between chemisorbed H atoms.

THE ORDER-DISORDER TRANSITION of chemi- sorbed H atoms on Ni(111) was studied in detail by LEED experiments [1]. The most striking observation was that the order-disorder transition temperature $T_{c}$ is asymmetric with respect to H-coverage $\theta$. Kittler and Bennemann [2] (referred to as KB) have discussed the dependence of the transition temperature and the inten- sity of the half-order LEED spots on H-coverages by using the lattice gas model and by assuming $(2 ×2)$ H-structure as suggested by the LEED experiments [1]. The obtained numerical results [2] show in both the transition temperature and the LEED spot intensity an extra peak at H-coverage $\theta=\frac{1}{4}$, which is not observed experimentally.

In order to obtain better agreement with experi- ment for $\theta \simeq 0.25$ with regards to $T_{c}(\theta)$ and $I(\theta)$ we assume the same binding energy for chemisorption on $A$ - and $B$-sites and use for the free-energy an expression referring to a mixture of adlayer domains. Then, an ex- pression is given for the half-order LEED spot intensity by means of lattice gas model assuming such an adlayer domain mixture and with the same assumption of H chemisorption sites on Ni(111) as has been done by KB. Our results agree well with experiment.

Note, H on Ni(111) chemisorb equivalently at two types of 3-fold hollow sites which are different from each other only by their positions with respect to the atomic configuration of the second Ni-surface layer. In the $(2 ×2)$ structure H are known from the LEED experiments [1] to occupy both types of 3-fold hollow sites as shown in Fig. 1. For H-coverages greater than one-half this $(2 ×2)$-lattice structure is filled and additional H must go to either nearest-neighbour 3-fold hollow sites or top or bridge chemisorption sites. Since the $(2 ×2)$ chemisorption pattern is stabilized by a strong repulsion between H in nearest-neighbour 3-fold sites, it has been assumed that after the $(2 ×2)$ lattice of 3-fold sites is filled additional H will occupy top sites. Such sites have the greatest possible distance from the H already chemisorbed in the $(2 ×2)$ lattice. The vanishing of the intensity of the half-order LEED spots for cover- ages $\theta \geqslant \frac{3}{4}$ also support that the top sites get filled with H for $\theta>\frac{1}{2}$. As indicated in Fig. 1 the two types of 3-fold sites and top sites form 3 interpenetrating sub- lattices. Since the two types of 3-fold hollow sites are different from each other only by their positions with respect to the second substrate layer and since H atoms occupy equivalently both types of 3-fold sites as suggested by the LEED experiments [1], we assume that H on Ni(111) have the same binding at both types of 3-fold sites. This assumption is important in our calcula- tions.

We determine the ordering of the H atoms within the 3 sublattices by a minimization of the free energy of the adsorbed layer. We allow for $\theta \leqslant 0.5$ a mixture of adlayer domains obtained by $P_{A} \neq P_{B}$ and with $P_{A}>P_{B}$ and $P_{B}>P_{A}$. Here, $P_{A}, P_{B}, P_{C}$ denote the occupation probabilities for $A, B, C$ sites. In calculating the con- figurational entropy of the layer we use an expression given by Kikuchi [3] to include the 3-site cluster corre- lations (between two next nearest-neighbour 3-fold sites and the top site). The internal energy is expressed as a sum over all 3-site cluster energies weighted by their probability of occurrence. The energy of each cluster is the sum of single-atom binding energies, pair interactions and 3-H interactions. We assume for simplicity only pair

Thus, our calculations involve in units of the pair interaction between H on the two 3-fold sites in the cluster as a parameter only the pair interaction V between H on the top and 3-fold sites. By minimizing the free energy, we find for high H-coverages, chemisorption pattern with $P_{A}=P_{B} \neq P_{C}$. On the other hand, in the ordered phase for low H-coverages, one finds a chemisorption pattern with $P_{A} \neq P_{B}$. Since we assume the same free energy for the two types of chemisorption geometries obtained for $\theta \leq 0.5$ by interchanging of $P_{A} \rightleftarrows P_{B}$, it can be expected that on the average chemisorbed H atoms occupy $A$ and $B$ sites with the same probability, $(P_{A}+P_{B}) / 2$. Equivalently we expect a corresponding mixture of local domains obtained by $P_{A} \rightleftarrows P_{B}$. Then the intensity of the half-order LEED spots corresponding to the $(2 \times 2)$ chemisorption pattern consisting of the 2 ordered local regions resulting from $P_{A} \rightleftarrows P_{B}$ is given by

$$
I \propto \left( \frac{P_{A} \mp P_{B}}{2} - P_{C} \right)^2 .
$$

Here, we assumed coherent scattering from the two ordered pattern resulting from interchanging $P_{A}$ and $P_{B}$. The intensity of the LEED spots given by the above expression for high H-coverages is the same given by the KB's paper [2].

In accordance with the experimental procedure we determine the order-disorder transition temperature $T_{c}$ from the inflection points of the half order LEED spot intensity vs temperature curves. It can be shown from our calculations that the extra LEED spot intensity for the disordered phase corresponding to the $(2 / \sqrt{3} \times$ $2 / \sqrt{3}) R 30^{\circ}$ chemisorption geometry is small particularly for low temperatures and low H-coverages.

Using the experimental value $T_{c}^{\max }=270 \mathrm{~K}$ we obtain for the next nearest-neighbour pair interaction between H on two types of 3-fold sites the value $0.035 \mathrm{eV}$ and for $V$ the value $0.0427 \mathrm{eV}$. These values are nearly the same as those obtained by KB. The difference in H-binding energy between the top and 3-fold sites seems small as supported by numerical results. For comparison numerical results taking into account the interactions between 3-H atoms are also shown in Fig. 2. It is expected that the 3-H interactions may come into action for rather high coverages and suppress the transition temperatures for $\theta \geqslant \frac{1}{3}$. It is to be noted that because of underestimation of free energy in the disordered phase, Bethe-Peierls approximation does not give perfect agreement with experimental observations. In Fig. 3, numerical results for the half-order LEED spot intensity as a function of H-coverages are shown for $T=0.55 T_{c}^{\max }=150 \mathrm{~K}$. Since for low H-coverages $\theta<0.2$ the H atoms are not close enough together to interact and stabilize the $(2 \times 2)$ structure, the LEED spot intensity and thus $T_{c}$ goes to zero (for $\theta \leqslant 0.18$).

Our calculations suggest that chemisorbed $\mathrm{H}$ atoms have the same binding at two types of 3-fold hollow sites and that the observed asymmetric phase diagram with respect to H-coverages may result already from the two kinds of pair interactions between chemisorbed $\mathrm{H}$ atoms. The observed temperature- and coverage-dependence of the half-order LEED spot intensity has been explained satisfactorily by using lattice gas model. In contrast to the results presented previously [2] we find no peak anymore in $I(\theta)$ and only small structure in $T_{c}(\theta)$ at $\theta \simeq 0.25$.
