# Correlation of the Magnetically Induced Directional Order with an $\text{AB}_3$ Superlattice

By Takao Iwata*

The directional order induced by magnetic anneal in a ferromagnetic face-centered cubic binary solid solution and its correlation with an ordinary $\text{AB}_3$ superlattice are calculated by the quasi-chemical method using quadruplets approximation. The anisotropic long-range order parameters and sixty four parameters representing the numbers of different quadruplets are introduced to describe both the directional and ordinary order, and their equilibrium values in the magnetically annealed state are calculated. It is shown how the formations of short-range and long-range orders during magnetic anneal depress the occurrence of the directional order and thus of the additional uniaxial ferromagnetic anisotropy. The change caused by ordering in the directional property of the induced ferromagnetic anisotropy is indicated. The results obtained can explain the observed irregular behavior of the induced ferromagnetic anisotropy in the magnetically annealed $\text{Ni}_3\text{Fe}$ single crystal.

(Received May 19, 1961)

## I. Introduction
The striking dependence of the induced uniaxial ferromagnetic anisotropy by magnetic anneal upon the formation of the $\text{Ni}_3\text{Fe}$ superlattice has been reported by Chikazumi$^{(1)}$: The induced anisotropy is depressed drastically by the formation of the superlattice and the orientation dependence of it deviates from the prediction of the simple theory$^{(2)(3)}$ based on the directional order mechanism as an origin of the induced anisotropy. The effect of the non-ideality of solid solutions on the directional order was first considered by Nêel.$^{(2)}$ In his treatment, however, the mixing entropy of the atomic configuration including the directional order was not taken into account and the effect of the long-range order was ignored. In the present study, a statistico-thermodynamical theory is applied to the directional order in a face-centered cubic binary non-ideal solid solution, and the behavior of the directional order coexisting with an $\text{AB}_3$-type superlattice is examined.

## II. Notations and Geometrical Relations
Let us consider a face-centered cubic alloy of type $\text{AB}_3$, the total number of atoms being $N$. A face-centered cubic lattice may be divided into four penetrant simple cubic lattices, I, II, III and IV. In general, they may not be equivalent to one another, since we are now interested in both the directional order and the $\text{AB}_3$-type superlattice. Thus, anisotropic long-range order parameters, $r_i$ ($i=1, 2, 3, 4$), are introduced so that the probability that a lattice point belonging to the I, II, III, or IV sublattices is occupied by an A atom may be $(1-r_1)/3$, $(1-r_2)/3$, $(1-r_3)/3$, or $r_4$, respectively.

Because of the complication of the face-centered cubic lattice that some of the nearest neighbors of a lattice site are also nearest neighbors to each other, tetrahedral quadruplets approximation$^{(4)}$ is employed for the calculation of the configurational entropy. We consider a system of $N$ tetrahedral quadruplets constructed by four neighboring lattice sites, each of which belongs to one of the four sublattices, in such

The numbers of the quadruplets are defined by $a_j$'s, $b_{ij}$'s, $c_{ij}$'s, $d_j$'s, $e$'s, $f_{ij}$'s, $g_{ij}$'s, and $h_j$'s, as shown in the third column of Table 1, where $j=1, 2, 3$, and 4 corresponding to the group i, ii, iii, and iv, respectively, and $i=1, 2$, and 3.

The above-introduced order parameters, $r_j$, $a_j$, $b_{ij}$, $c_{ij}$, $d_j$, $e_j$, $f_{ij}$, $g_{ij}$, and $h_j$ ($i=1, 2, 3$; $j=1, 2, 3, 4$) are not all independent. The condition that the total number of A atoms is $N/4$ can be expressed as

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\{(1-r_i)/3\}+r_4=1, \tag{1}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
&4a_j+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(3b_{ij}+2c_{ij})+d_j+3e_j+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(2f_{ij}+g_{ij})=1 \\
&\quad (j=1, 2, 3, \text{and } 4). \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Also, from the conditions that the numbers of the quadruplets belonging to the i, ii, iii, and iv groups are all equal to $N/4$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
&a_j+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(b_{ij}+c_{ij})+d_j+e_j+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(f_{ij}+g_{ij})+h_j=1 \\
&\quad (j=1, 2, 3, \text{and } 4). \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, taking a count of the numbers of A atoms on the sublattices I, II, and III, we have

$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}

&(1-r_3)/3=a_j+b_{1j}+b_{2j}+c_{3j}+e_j+f_{1j}+f_{2j}+g_{3j},
\end{aligned}
\right\}(4)
$$
$(j=1, 2, 3, \text{and } 4).$

### III. Quasi-Chemical Equations and the Minimization of the Free Energy
#### 1. Internal energy of the system
In the following, the case of magnetic annealing will be considered. It is supposed that the directional order of atoms is so produced that the free energy of the system including the ferromagnetic anisotropy energy may be minimized at the magnetic annealing temperature.

Let the direction cosines of the intrinsic magnetization referred to the tetragonal axes be denoted by $\beta_i$ ($i=1, 2, 3$), and the angle between the direction of an atom pair and that of the intrinsic magnetization be denoted by $\phi$. It may be assumed that the energies of the atom pairs AA, AB, and BB along any nearest-neighbor directions, $E_{\text{AA}}$, $E_{\text{AB}}$, and $E_{\text{BB}}$, are expressed as

$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}
&E_{\text{AA}}=u+l\{\cos^2\phi-(1/3)\}, \\
&E_{\text{AB}}=v+m\{\cos^2\phi-(1/3)\}, \\
&E_{\text{BB}}=w+n\{\cos^2\phi-(1/3)\},
\end{aligned}
\right\}(5)
$$

respectively. Then, the summation of the pair energies

according to various manners of quadruplets yields the energies of quadruplets, which are given in the fourth column of Table 1, where abbreviations

$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
\omega_{1}=\omega_{32}=\omega_{13}=\omega_{24}=\beta_{2} \beta_{3}+\beta_{3} \beta_{1}+\beta_{1} \beta_{2}, \\
\omega_{2}=\omega_{31}=\omega_{23}=\omega_{14}=-\beta_{2} \beta_{3}-\beta_{3} \beta_{1}+\beta_{1} \beta_{2}, \\
\omega_{3}=\omega_{11}=\omega_{22}=\omega_{34}=\beta_{2} \beta_{3}-\beta_{3} \beta_{1}-\beta_{1} \beta_{2}, \\
\omega_{4}=\omega_{21}=\omega_{12}=\omega_{33}=-\beta_{2} \beta_{3}+\beta_{3} \beta_{1}-\beta_{1} \beta_{2},
\end{array}\right\}(6)
$$

$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
\epsilon_{11}=\epsilon_{21}=\epsilon_{31}=\epsilon_{32}=\epsilon_{13}=\epsilon_{24}=1, \\
\text { and } \epsilon_{12}=\epsilon_{22}=\epsilon_{23}=\epsilon_{33}=\epsilon_{14}=\epsilon_{34}=-1
\end{array}\right\}(7)
$$

are used.

The internal energy, $U$, of the system as the sum of the energies of quadruplets is given, by using the geometrical relations (2), (3), and (4) and by omitting constant terms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U= & (N V / 4)\left\{3 \sum_{j=1}^{4}\left(a_{j}+h_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{4} \sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(c_{i j}+f_{i j}\right)\right\} \\
& -(N L / 4)\left[\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left\{\left(d+e_{\jmath}\right) \omega_{j}\right\}+\sum_{j=1}^{4} \sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[\left(b_{i j}+g_{i j}\right) \omega_{i j}\right.\right. \\
& \left.\left.+\left(c_{i j}+f_{i j}\right)\left\{\beta_{i}{ }^{2}-(1 / 3)\right\}\right]\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

where $V=(1 / 2)(u+w)-v \quad(9)$

and $L=(1 / 2)(l+n)-m$. (10)

In Eq. (8), the first term is the formation energy of the superlattice, and the second one expresses the ferromagnetic anisotropy energy responsible for the directional order.

### 2. Quasi-chemical equations
For given $r_{i}$'s, $a_{j}$'s, $b_{i j}$'s, $c_{i j}$'s, $d_{j}$'s, $e_{j}$'s, $f_{i j}$'s, $g_{i j}$'s, and $h$'s, the number of configurations, $G$, is written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
& G\left(r_{i} \text { 's; } a_{j} \text { 's, } b_{i j} \text { 's, } \cdots, h \text { 's }\right)=Q\left(r_{i} \text { 's }\right)(N / 4)! \\
& \times\left[\left\{(N / 4) \sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(r_{i} / 3\right)\right\}!\left[\left(N / 4)\left\{1-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(r_{i} / 3\right)\right\}\right]!\right]^{-1}\right. \\
& \times \prod_{i=1}^{3}\left[(N / 4)!\left[\left\{(N / 4)\left(1-r_{i}\right) / 3\right\}!\left\{(N / 4)\left(2+r_{i}\right) / 3\right\}!\right]^{-1}\right] \\
& \times \prod_{j=1}^{4}\left[\left(N a_{j} / 4\right)!\left(N d_{j} / 4\right)!\left(N e_{j} / 4\right)!\left(N h_{j} / 4\right)!\right]^{-1} \\
& \times \prod_{j=1}^{4} \prod_{i=1}^{3}\left[\left(N b_{i j} / 4\right)!\left(N c_{i j} / 4\right)!\left(N f_{i j} / 4\right)!\left(N g_{i j} / 4\right)!\right]^{-1}(11)
\end{aligned}
$$

with the factor $Q(r$ 's) which normalizes the expression for a complete random arrangement for fixed $r$ 's.

The configurational partition function, $\Omega$, as a function of the temperature, $T$, and anisotropic long-range order parameters, $r_{i}$'s, is obtained by substituting Eqs. (8) and (11) in the general formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Omega\left(T, r_{i} \text { 's }\right)= & \sum\left\{G\left(r_{i} \text { 's; } a_{j} \text { 's, } b_{i j} \text { 's, } \cdots, h_{j} \text { 's }\right)\right. \\
& \left.\times \exp \left\{-U\left(r_{i} \text { 's; } a \text { 's, } b_{i j} \text { 's, } \cdots, h_{j} \text { 's }\right) / k T\right\}\right\},(12)
\end{aligned}
$$

where the summation extends over all values of $a_{j}$'s, $b_{i j}$'s,$\cdots$, and $h_{j}$'s which are consistent with the subsidiary conditions (2), (3), and (4). As usual, the sum in Eq. (12) can be replaced by its maximum term. The condition of this maximization yields the following so-called quasi-chemical equations :

$$
\left.\begin{array}{rl}
\ln \left(a_{j}^{2} d_{j} / b_{1 j} b_{2 j} b_{3 j}\right)= & -(6 V / k T)+(2 L / k T) \alpha_{j}, \\
\ln \left(e_{j} h_{j}^{2} / g_{1 j} g_{2 j} g_{3 j}\right)= & -(6 V / k T)+(2 L / k T) \omega_{j}, \\
\ln \left(a_{j} h_{j} / b_{i j} g_{i j}\right)= & -(6 V / k T)-(2 L / k T) \omega_{i j}, \\
\ln \left(a_{j} c_{i j} b_{i j} / b_{1 j} b_{2 j} b_{3 j}\right)= & -(2 V / k T) \\
& +(L / 3 k T)\left\{3 \beta_{i}^{2}+6 \epsilon_{i j}\left(\beta_{1} \beta_{2} \beta_{3} / \beta_{i}\right)-1\right\}, \\
\text { and } \ln \left(f_{i j} h_{j} g_{i j} / g_{1 j} g_{2 j} g_{3 j}\right)= & -(2 V / k T) \\
& +(L / 3 k T)\left\{3 \beta_{i}^{2}+6 \epsilon_{i j}\left(\beta_{1} \beta_{2} \beta_{3} / \beta_{i}\right)-1\right\}, \\
(i=1,2,3 ; j=1,2,3,4).
\end{array}\right\}(13)
$$

### 3. Minimum conditions for the free energy
Minimizing the free energy

$$
\begin{aligned}
F=-k T \ln \Omega\left(T, r_{i} \text { 's }\right)=-k T \ln G\left(r_{i} \text { 's }\right)+U\left(r_{i} \text { 's }\right) \\
(14)
\end{aligned}
$$

for a fixed temperature $T$, we obtain eventually

$$
\left.\begin{array}{rl}
\ln \left\{\left(1-r_{1}\right)\left(3-\sum_{i=1}^{3} r_{i}\right) /\left(2+r_{1}\right) \sum_{i=1}^{3} r_{i}\right\}-(1 / 3) \sum_{j=1}^{4} \ln \left(f_{3 j} / c_{2 j}\right) \\
=(4 L / 3 k T)\left(\beta_{2}{ }^{2}-\beta_{3}{ }^{2}\right), \\
\ln \left\{\left(1-r_{2}\right)\left(3-\sum_{i=1}^{3} r_{i}\right) /\left(2+r_{2}\right) \sum_{i=1}^{3} r_{i}\right\}-(1 / 3) \sum_{j=1}^{4} \ln \left(f_{1} / c_{3 j}\right) \\
=(4 L / 3 k T)\left(\beta_{3}{ }^{2}-\beta_{1}{ }^{2}\right), \\
\text { and } \ln \left\{\left(1-r_{3}\right)\left(3-\sum_{i=1}^{3} r_{i}\right) /\left(2+r_{3}\right) \sum_{i=1}^{3} r_{i}\right\}-(1 / 3) \sum_{j=1}^{4} \ln \left(f_{2 j} / c_{1 j}\right) \\
=(4 L / 3 k T)\left(\beta_{1}{ }^{2}-\beta_{2}{ }^{2}\right).
\end{array}\right\}
$$

Then the equilibrium values of the order parameters $r_{i}$'s, $a_{j}$'s, $b_{i j}$'s, $\cdots$, and $h_{j}$'s can be determined from the simultaneous equations (2), (3), (4), (13), and (15).

For a non-ferromagnetic solution $(L=0)$,

$$
\begin{gathered}
r=r ; a_{\jmath}=a, b_{\imath \jmath}=b, c_{i j}=c, \cdots, \text { and } h_{j}=h ; \\
(i=1,2,3 ; j=1,2,3,4).
\end{gathered}
$$

Then, equations (2), (3), (4), (13), and (15) are reduced respectively to

$$
\left.\begin{array}{rl}
4 a+9 b+6 c+d+3 e+6 f+3 g=1, \\
a+3 b+3 c+d+e+3 f+3 g+h=1, \\
a+2 b+c+e+2 f+g=(1-r) / 3, \\
\ln \left(a^{2} d / b^{3}\right)=-6 V / k T, \\
\ln \left(e h^{2} / g^{3}\right)=-6 V / k T, \\
\ln (a h / b g)=-6 V / k T, \\
\ln \left(a c / b^{2}\right)=-2 V / k T, \\
\ln \left(f h / g^{2}\right)=-2 V / k T, \\
\text { and } \ln \left\{(1-r)^{2} / r(2+r)\right\}-(4 / 3) \ln (f / c)=0,
\end{array}\right\}(17)
$$

which coincide exactly with the result of McGlashan's theory $^{(5)}$ developed for the $\mathrm{AB}_{3}$-type superlattice.

## IV. Approximate Calculation of the Order Parameters
Since it is difficult to exactly solve the simultaneous equations (2), (3), (4), (13), and (15), the method of successive approximation is used. Using the parameters determined from Eqs. (17) as the zeroth approximation, we put

$$
\left.
\begin{array}{l}
r_{i}=r+r_{i}{ }^{\prime} ; \\
a_{j}=a+a_{j}{ }^{\prime}, b_{i j}=b+b_{i j}{ }^{\prime}, c_{i j}=c+c_{i j}{ }^{\prime}, d_{j}=d+d_{j}{ }^{\prime}, \\
e_{j}=e+e_{j}{ }^{\prime}, f_{i j}=f+f_{i j}{ }^{\prime}, g_{i j}^{\prime}=g+g_{i j}{ }^{\prime}, \\
\text { and } h_{i}=h+h^{\prime} ; \quad(i=1,2,3 ; j=1,2,3,4).
\end{array}
\right\}
\tag{18}
$$

Since the degree of the directional order developed in actual cases is very small, it may hold that, under normal conditions,
$$
\begin{gathered}
\left|r_{i}{ }^{\prime} / r\right| \ll 1 ;\left|a_{j}{ }^{\prime} / a\right| \ll 1,\left|b_{i j}{ }^{\prime} / b\right| \ll 1, \cdots, \text { and }\left|h_{j}{ }^{\prime} / h\right| \ll 1 ; \\
(i=1,2,3 ; j=1,2,3,4). \quad(19)
\end{gathered}
$$

Using these relations, we can simplify equations (2), (3), (4), (13), and (15) and get a final solution as
$$
\left.
\begin{array}{rl}
r_{i}{ }^{\prime}= & (2 L / k T) R\left\{\beta_{i}{ }^{2}-(1 / 3)\right\} ; \\
a^{\prime}= & (2 L / k T) a A \omega_{j}, \\
b_{i}{ }^{\prime}= & (2 L / k T) b\left[B_{1}\left\{\beta_{i}{ }^{2}-(1 / 3)\right\}\right. \\
& \left.+B_{2} \omega_{i j}+B_{3} \epsilon_{i j}\left(\beta_{1} \beta_{2} \beta_{3} / \beta_{i}\right)\right], \\
c_{i j}{ }^{\prime}= & (2 L / k T) c\left[C_{1}\left\{\beta_{i}{ }^{2}-(1 / 3)\right\}\right. \\
& \left.+C_{2} \omega_{i j}+C_{3} \epsilon_{i j}\left(\beta_{1} \beta_{2} \beta_{3} / \beta_{i}\right)\right], \\
d_{j}{ }^{\prime}= & (2 L / k T) d D \omega, \\
e_{j}{ }^{\prime}= & (2 L / k T) e E \omega_{j}, \\
f_{i j}{ }^{\prime}= & (2 L / k T) f\left[F_{1}\left\{\beta_{i}{ }^{2}-(1 / 3)\right\}\right. \\
& \left.+F_{2} \omega_{i j}+F_{3} \epsilon_{i j}\left(\beta_{1} \beta_{2} \beta_{3} / \beta_{i}\right)\right], \\

## VI. Comparison with Experiment and Discussion

There is no experiment to be compared with the present calculation which is concerned with the equilib- rium properties of the directional order in the $AB_3$- type superlattice alloy at various magnetic annealing temperatures.* Chikazumi measured $^{(1)}$ the change of induced ferromagnetic anisotropy in a $Ni_3Fe$ single crystal during the magnetic annealing at a nearly fixed temperature. As shown in Fig. 5, experimental values of $k_1$, $k_2$, and $k_2/k_1$ calculated from the data given in his paper vary corresponding to the change in the first magnetocrystalline anisotropy con- stant, $K_1$, which indicates the degree of the short-range order. Thus, Fig. 5 may be reasonably compared with Fig. 4. Excepting the initial range of the directional order formation where $k_1$ and $k_2$ increase sharply as shown on the right-hand side of Fig. 5, the general feature of the experimental curves is in accordance with the present calculation. There is, however, some discrepency in the absolute value of $k_2/k_1$. Theoretically, the value of $k_2/k_1$ takes 4 at the perfect disorder and decreases from this value as the order develops while the experimental value starts from about 9. A high value of $k_2/k_1$ in an $83\%$ Ni-Fe single crystal was also reported by Aoyagi.(6)

It was suggested $^{(1)}$ that the ratio of about 9 might originate from the nature of the face-centered cubic lattice: Since, in this lattice, some of the nearest neighbors of one atom are also nearest neighbors to each other, some of the B-B atom pairs radiating from the same B atom produce the third B-B pair, thus making a triangular B-B-B arrangement. The deviation of the experimental value of the $k_2/k_1$ ratio from 4 was attributed to the contribution from these B-B-B arrangements. However, the problem is so complicated to be discussed by the simple theory. In fact, in the
