# Ab initio calculation of the bcc Mo–Al (molybdenum–aluminium) phase diagram: Implications for the nature of the $\zeta_2$-MoAl phase

## Abstract
The metastable phase diagram of the bcc-based ordering equilibria in the Mo–Al system has been calculated by the cluster expansion method, through the combination of FP-LAPW and CVM. The results are discussed with reference to the structure and stability of the $\zeta_2$-MoAl high temperature phase.

## 1. Introduction
The molybdenum-rich side of the Mo–Al phase diagram is characterised by very high melting points (over 1500 °C for alloys containing more than 50 at.% Mo). Considering these high temperatures (and the experimental challenges associated to them), it is not surprising that some controversy still remains concerning the true nature of the equilibria in these alloys. Fig. 1 shows the molybdenum-rich side of the Mo–Al phase diagram, adapted from two versions published in subsequent editions of a popular binary alloy phase diagrams compendium [1,2]. Fig. 1a is based on the version published by Brewer and Lamoreaux [1] and complies with the one published in the first systematic investigation of this region of the phase diagram [3]. In this diagram the $\zeta_2$ phase, $^{2}$ stable at near-equiatomic compositions above 1470 °C (1743 K), melts congruently around 1757 °C (2030 K). As usual in the phase diagrams literature, dashed lines indicate “guessed” equilibria, i.e. those for which there are no direct experimental comprovat ion, but are based instead only on circumstantial evidences. The evidence for the congruent melting of the $\zeta_2$ phase is metallographic: the “as-cast” microstructure of an alloy containing 51.5 at.% Mo showed large equiaxial grains of eutectoidally decomposed $\zeta_2$, while

Fig. 1b shows the same region of the phase diagram as proposed by Saunders [2]. This author substituted the congruent melting of the $\zeta_{2}$ phase by the liq. $+\mathrm{Mo}_{3} \mathrm{Al} \rightarrow \zeta_{2}$ peritectic reaction, motivated by the attribution of the A2 structure to the $\zeta_{2}$ phase. In this case the molybdenum-rich solid solution would be iso- structural with $\zeta_{2}$ and, thus, the solidus and liquidus lines should follow a common functional relationship with the composition (since they depend only on the Gibbs free energies of the A2 and liquid phases, which are, except for rare cases, continuous functions of the composition). The existence of a maximum point at the equiatomic composition, although not thermody- namically forbidden, would imply a very complex ther- modynamic (meaning non-regular) behaviour for the A2 phase, which is unlikely to occur [4]. This conclusion, of course, depends on the assumption that the $\zeta_{2}$ phase has the A2 structure. The aim of the present work is to present results on the ab initio modelling of the bcc Mo- Al system, which can shed some light on the real nature of the $\zeta_{2}$ phase.

## 2. Methodology

The procedure used for the Mo-Al phase diagram calculation is based on the so-called cluster expansion method (see [5]). It consists in the calculation of the total energies at the ground state $(T=0 \mathrm{~K})$ of a set of stoichi ometric ordered compounds (which are related to a common disordered lattice) by solving one particle Schrödinger's equations. This set forms a basis for the truncated expansion of the internal energy of the lattice at all temperatures and compositions, which may then be used as input in a statistical mechanics method to cal- culate the phase diagram. The set used in the present work is formed by the A2 Al, A2 Mo, D0 $0_{3} \mathrm{Mo}_{3} \mathrm{Al}$, D0 $0_{3} \mathrm{Al}_{3} \mathrm{Mo}, \mathrm{B} 2 \mathrm{MoAl}$ and B32 MoAl compounds, which is rather limited in comparison, for example, with the one used by Blum and Zunger to investigate the bcc Mo-Ta system (52 compounds) [6]. The basis set is kept limited here on purpose since only a simplified thermo- dynamic description of the system based on the irregular tetrahedron approximation of the cluster variation method (CVM) is targeted (for the sake of compatibility with an on-going research line, see, for example, [7-13]) and there is no necessity, therefore to converge the clus- ter expansion, in the sense discussed by van de Walle and Ceder [14]. The conclusions of the present work, however, should be considered in the context of this lim- itation (this will be further discussed latter on in this text).

The total energies of the ordered compounds have been calculated using the full-potential-linearised-aug- mented-plane-wave (FP-LAPW) method as embodied in the WIEN97 code [15]. The FP-LAPW method allows very precise computation of total energies and charge densities in metallic compounds due to its full-potential feature and is, at the moment, one of the most accurate methods for electronic structure calculations in solids. It is developed in the framework of the Kohn-Sham scheme of the density functional theory (DFT) which states that the total energy of a system is a unique func- tional of its electron density. The DFT is, in principle, an exact theory from where the term first principles or ab initio comes; however, since solving the complex

problem of many interacting electrons and nuclei is not feasible, the complete functional form has to be approx- imated. In the present work the generalised gradient approximation (GGA) [16] has been adopted to perform non-spin-polarised (as the system is known to be non- magnetic) ab initio calculations. The equilibrium lattice constants at the ground state have been optimised through the calculation of the minimum value of the total energy. The optimisation of static lattice relax- ations is not necessary in the present case, since the six compounds here investigated are quite symmetric (i.e. there are no internal site position variables in their crys- tallographic models). As the total energy is the impor- tant parameter in the present approach, particular care was taken when performing convergence tests. Follow- ing the WIEN97 notation the convergence of the order of $10^{-4}$ Ry in the total energy has been achieved by using suitable values for the $RK_{max}, l_{max}$ and number of $k$-points, which are control parameters of the conver gence in the FP-LAPW calculation (see [17,18] for a description of these parameters). The values adopted for each compound, as well as the equilibrium lattice parameters, will be presented together with the total energies in Section 3.

The calculated total energies for compound $\Phi$ (with $\Phi=B 2, ~B 32$ or $D_{3}$ ) with $Mo_{x} Al_{y}$ stoichiometry (and n=x+y atoms in the unit formula) are denoted by $U_{Mo_{x} Al_{y}}^{\Phi}$ , and must be converted to formation energies per mol of atoms in the reference state of the mechanical mixture of the components (with bcc structure) using

$$
\Delta^{\mathrm{f}} U_{\mathrm{Mo}_{x} \mathrm{Al}_{y}}^{\Phi} \equiv \frac{U_{\mathrm{Mo}_{x} \mathrm{Al}_{y}}^{\Phi}-x U_{\mathrm{Mo}}^{\mathrm{A} 2}-y U_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\mathrm{A} 2}}{n}.\qquad(1)
$$

The formation energies of the four compounds are used as input parameters in the statistical mechanics for- malism (CVM) adopted for the calculation of the phase diagram. The CVM was introduced by Kikuchi in 1951 as a formalism to obtain arbitrary approximations for the configurational entropy of lattices [19]. The CVM is based on the concept of "basic cluster": a geometric figure contained in the reference lattice which represents the maximum correlation length explicitly considered in the approximation. The CVM formalism in the IT cluster approximation has been thoroughly outlined in several previous publications by one of the present authors [8-10,12] and this shall not be repro- duced here. For the sake of completeness only the pro- cedure for obtaining the interaction parameters of the model will be briefly described.

The internal energy (U) of a bcc lattice with N sites(containing 6N irregular tetrahedra) is written as [8]

$$
U=6 N \sum_{i, j, k, l=\mathrm{Mo}, \mathrm{Al}} \varepsilon_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta} \rho_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}.\qquad(2)
$$

In this expression, $\rho_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}$ represents the probability offinding an $\{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta\}$ IT cluster with configuration $\{i j k l\}$ (i.e. species i occupying cluster position $\alpha, j$ in cluster po sition $\beta$ and so on ...) among the 6 N tetrahedra of the system and $\varepsilon_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}$ represents the eigenenergy $^{3}$ associated with this configuration. The stoichiometric compounds at the ground state correspond to particular configura- tions: a B2 MoAl stoichiometric compound of N lattice points, for example, may be built by 6N tetrahedra withconfiguration MoMoAlAl, while the B32 MoAl, $D_{3}$  $Mo_{3} Al$ and $D_{3} MoAl_{3}$ stoichiometric compounds are similarly obtained using the MoAlMoAl, MoMoMoAl and MoAlAlAl configurations. Eq. (2) may be now ap- plied to these particular cases and the compound forma- tion energies may be associated with the corresponding cluster eigenenergies. In the case of the B2 MoAl com-pound, for example:

$$
\varepsilon_{\text {MoMoAlAl }}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}=\frac{\Delta^{\mathrm{f}} U_{\mathrm{MoAl}}^{\mathrm{B} 2}}{6 N}.
$$

It has to be reminded that the eigenenergy matrix{ k} is highly degenerate due to symmetry constraints. Both configurations MoAlMoAl and AIMoAIMo, for example, represent a B32 MoAl stoichiometric com- pound, differing only by a translation of the origin of the lattice by a lattice vector $\frac{a_{0}}{2}[\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 1\end{array}]$ . Since the energy of the compound cannot depend on the choice of the originfor the reference lattice, it follows that $\varepsilon_{AlMoAlMo }^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}=$  oM. The same is true for the two remaining degener- ate configurations (MoAlAIMo and AIMoMoAl).

Set $\left\{\varepsilon_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}\right\}$ (including the two reference values, o = =0) contains all information needed for the description of the system. It is a common practice in the literature, however, to further decompose the configuration eigenenergies into pair contributions(nearest and next-nearest neighbours), completing the

Term $\tilde{w}_{i j k l}$ represents an "excess" interaction, containing the contributions to $\varepsilon_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}$ which cannot be ascribed to pair interactions. Following Schön and Inden [8] the interactions $\varepsilon_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}$ will be rewritten in terms of $w_{\mathrm{MoAl}}^{(1)}, w_{\mathrm{MoAl}}^{(2)}, \tilde{w}_{\mathrm{MoAlMoAl}}$ and $\tilde{w}_{\mathrm{MoAlAlAl}}$. This choice is arbitrary and other ways of decomposing $\varepsilon_{i j k l}^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}$ are possible (see for example [21]), but this is irrelevant for the thermodynamic description of the system.

More interestingly, an experimental estimate of the formation enthalpy of the $\zeta_{2}$-MoAl phase, based on the Knudsen-cell effusion technique gives $\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}H_{298\mathrm{K}}^{o}=31.32\pm4$ kJ/mol [24]. This value refers to the stable phases at 298 K, which are A2 Mo and Al Al (face-centred cubic, fcc) and must be converted to the reference state of A2 Mo + A2 Al to be compared with the present calculations. This is done by subtracting $0.5\times\Delta H_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\mathrm{fcc}\rightarrow\mathrm{bcc}}$ from the experimental value, where $\Delta H_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\mathrm{fcc}\rightarrow\mathrm{bcc}}=+10.083$ kJ/mol represents the lattice stability of the A2 Al phase at 298 K [25]. Both the experimental formation enthalpy of the $\zeta_{2}$ phase ($-36.37\pm4$ kJ/mol [24]) and lattice parameter$^{4}$ ($6.17\leqslant a_{0}\leqslant6.19$ Å [3]) compare relatively well with the predicted values for the B32 phase ($-29.60$ kJ/mol and 6.25 Å, respectively).

Fig. 3 presents the phase diagram of the bcc Mo–Al system calculated here. This phase diagram is dominated by two single-phase fields corresponding to A2 and B32-based solid solutions, which are bounded by two-phase A2 + B32 fields (i.e. the B32 $\rightarrow$ A2 transition is of first-order at all temperatures and compositions). It has to be reminded that these equilibria refer only to the bcc-based phases. Equilibria involving phases which are not directly related to this lattice cannot be included in the calculation (e.g. the A15 $\mathrm{Mo}_{3}\mathrm{Al}$ compound or the liquid phase). The calculation predicts a quite high congruent disordering temperature at 3359 K and $x_{\mathrm{Mo}}\approx0.51$. The significance of these high temperatures is somewhat questionable: they are based on a rigid lattice model, which neglects important contributions to the alloy free energy (e.g. the dependence on the system's volume and the contribution of the vibrational entropy). Unfortunately a formulation of the CD-CVM for the case of the IT cluster is not yet available to assure that a similar reduction exists for the IT cluster approxima- tion, but assuming this is true, the B32 phase is still predicted to be stable compared with A2 up to temper- atures near the ones in which the $\zeta_{2}$ phase is experimen tally observed in the Mo-Al system. This phenomenon refers to dynamic relaxations in the lattice, which affect the alloy free energy via the composition dependence of the elastic constants. This should not be confused with static relaxations in the site positions of complex crystal- lographic structures, which would lead to a decrease of the compound's formation energy relative to the disor- dered reference state, thus stabilising the compound [27]. Another possible reason for the large discrepancies observed in the temperature scales of ab initio calculated and experimental phase diagrams would be a lack in the convergence of the cluster expansion, as already discussed.

Fig. 4 presents the Helmholtz free energy of the sys- tem, $A=U-T S$, as calculated for the 2500 K isotherm using the CVM in the IT cluster approximation with the parameters of Table 2. The results show that, while the free energy of the A2 phase indeed follows a regular solution-like behaviour (more precisely, a low-order polynomial function of the composition), the free energy of the B32 phase presents a pronounced minimum around the MoAl stoichiometry. This minimum could justify the appearance of a congruent point for the $\zeta_{2} \to$ liquid equilibrium. The attribution of the A2 struc ture by Rexer [3] could be easily explained by the close relationship between both lattices and by the weakness of the super-lattice reflections corresponding to the B32 structure. To illustrate this a simulation of the pow- der diffraction pattern for an hypothetical stoichiome- tric, fully ordered, B32 MoAl phase with lattice parameter $a_{0}=6.25 \AA$ using $CuK \alpha$ radiation has been performed using the DBWS program [28]. This simula- tion shows that the brightest super-lattice reflection- $\{111\}$ -should be found in the diffractogram around $2 \theta=25^{\circ}$ with about $50 \%$ of the intensity of the brightest principal reflection, $\{222\}$ , with $2 \theta \approx 50^{\circ}$ . The remain ing super-lattice reflections (e.g. $\{113\}$ , around $48^{\circ}$ or $\{004\}$ , around $59^{\circ}$ ) would be considerably weaker. The combination of faint reflections $^{5}$ and unusual angles in the diffractogram could justify overseeing the super- lattice reflections in the original diffraction experiment.

The results of the present ab initio calculation cannotbe interpreted as a proof for the B32 structure of the $\zeta_{2}$  phase, but it gives sufficient evidences to justify this hypothesis (as for example, the agreements between experimental lattice parameter and enthalpy of forma- tion). On the other hand, the limited basis set used in the present work does not allow to conclude that the most stable compound at MoAl stoichiometry is B32, since the convergence of the cluster expansion cannot be assured. In this context, however, the results obtained by Nguyen-Mahn and Pettifor [23] seems to indicate that this is indeed true. An experimental reevaluation of the structure of the $\zeta_{2}$ phase would be desirable.

### 4. Conclusions
The ab initio calculation of the bcc Mo-Al phase dia- gram using the FP-LAPW method combined with ther- modynamic calculations using the CVM in the IT cluster approximation indicate that only the B32 AIMo super- lattice should be found in this system and that the transition to the disordered A2 structure is a first-order

reaction at all temperatures and compositions. The formation energy at the ground state of this phase is predicted to be about $-29$ kJ/mol (in the reference state of the A2 Mo and A2 Al compounds) and its lattice parameter is predicted to be $a_0 = 6.25$ Å. Both values are in agreement with experimental values of formation enthalpy and lattice parameter determined for the high temperature $\zeta_2$-MoAl phase. This, together with the large temperature scales of the calculated phase diagram, suggest the possibility that the structure of the $\zeta_2$ phase is B32, instead of A2, as usually assumed in the literature.
