# A high throughput search for efficient thermoelectric half-Heusler compounds†

Parul R. Raghuvanshi, Suman Mondal and Amrita Bhattacharya *

Half-Heusler compounds have emerged as promising thermoelectric materials that offer huge compositional space to tune their thermoelectric performance. A class of stable half Heusler compounds formed from elements of three specific groups in the periodic table viz. $X_pX'_{1-p}Y_qY'_{1-q}Z_rZ'_{1-r}$ (with X, X' = Ti, Zr, Hf; Y, Y' = Ni, Pd, Pt and Z, Z' = Ge, Sn, Pb and p, q, r = 0, 0.25, 0.75 and 1) via various stoichiometric isoelectronic elemental substitutions at the X, Y and Z sites respectively, is investigated. Intelligent filters are employed at each step of our high throughput density functional theory calculations to filter compounds with improved figures of merit. While confirming several known results, the calculations also reveal unknown pathways to improve the thermoelectric performance of the compound class. The 50% X and Z site substitutions of the parent Heusler have been individually found to marginally enhance the power factor for both p- and n-type doping, while leading to considerable enhancement in the figure of merit (by ~24%) specifically. This is due to lowering of the lattice thermal conductivity stemming from the increase in lattice disorder in approximately the same cell volume. Furthermore, the present study confirms the experimental scenario that Y site substitution does not lead to enhancement of the power factor because of the breaking of band degeneracies at the high symmetry points. This work will serve as a consolidated cost effective guideline for experimentalists working with this compound class on enhancing the power factor and figure of merit of the compositions.

## 1 Introduction

One of the promising alternatives to meet the ever increasing energy demands of the modern world is the large scale deployment of energy efficient thermoelectric devices, which can convert a temperature gradient into an electric voltage.¹⁻³ However, the main challenge in this development lies in the low efficiency of the thermoelectric materials, which in turn depends on the dimensionless figure of merit zT of the material.⁴ The zT of a material at a given operating temperature depends on the intrinsic parameters i.e. the Seebeck coefficient S, the electrical conductivity σ, and the total thermal conductivity κ via the expression $zT = \frac{S^{2}\sigma T}{\kappa}.^{5,6}$ The numerator is collectively known as the power factor ($S^{2}\sigma$), which is contributed by the electronic transport coefficients, whereas the denominator κ is the sum of the heat generated by transportation of (a) charge carriers (i.e. electrons and holes) comprising the electronic thermal conductivity ($\kappa_{\text{el}}$) and (b) phonons travelling between the lattices comprising the lattice thermal conductivity ($\kappa_{\text{l}}$). Naturally, the main strategies that are generally employed to optimize the zT either involve maximizing the power factor in the numerator or minimizing the $\kappa_{\text{l}}$ in the denominator (which may be independently tuned with respect to the electronic transport coefficients in many materials) or both.⁷⁻¹¹ The electronic transport coefficients can be tuned by band engineering viz. by tuning the degeneracy of the valence/conduction band valleys, the effective mass of the carriers, the scattering lifetimes and carrier concentration.¹²⁻¹⁷ The $\kappa_{\text{l}}$ on the other hand can be reduced by shortening the phonon mean-free-path, which can be achieved by introducing mass disorder in the lattice.¹⁸,¹⁹ Thus, analysis of the electronic structure via substitution or doping has often been found to provide critical insights and in recent years, band engineering via first principles calculations has proven to be a cost effective and efficient way for tuning these parameters prior to their synthesis in the laboratory.²⁰ High throughput calculations of large numbers of compounds have recently been carried out to screen the ones with desirable properties for their thermoelectric applications.²¹⁻²³

Half Heuslers (HHs) are intermetallic ternary compounds (with the formula unit XYZ), where X and Y are transition or rare earth metals, while Z is a p-block element. They comprise one of the most promising, earth abundant and environment friendly classes of thermoelectric materials offering a huge chemical space to tune the transport coefficients.²⁴⁻²⁸ X and Y atoms are

They crystallize in a noncentrosymmetric lattice (space group $F\overline{4}3m$), which has four symmetry unique positions for accommodating the atoms; X (0, 0, 0), Z (1/2, 1/2, 1/2), Y (1/4, 1/4, 1/4), and a vacant fourth position (3/4, 3/4, 3/4). If the fourth vacant position is also occupied by Y, the full Heusler (FH) alloy with the general formula $XY_2Z$ (space group $Fm\overline{3}m$) is formed. HHs may be semiconducting or metallic depending upon the total valency of the elements in the compound. HH compounds, which have 8 (I-I-VI, I-II-V, I-III-IV, II-II-IV, and II-III-III) and 18 (XI-I-VI, XI-II-V, XI-III-IV, I-XII-V, II-XII-IV, XII-XII-III, X-II-VI, X-III-V, and X-IV-IV), generally show semiconducting character with a narrow band gap. $^{29-34}$

Naturally, effect of many different chemical substitutions and dopants have been studied in the past leading to an increase in the $zT^{35-38}$ up to 1.5, which is so far the highest reported value in this class. $^{8,38,39}$ Hohl and coworkers $^{40}$ explained the effect of various site substitutions and doping on the $zT$ of these half Heuslers. They showed that X site substitution improves the power factor in $Ti_{0.5}Hf_{0.5}NiSn$ by 1.18 times as compared to pristine HfNiSn (which has a power factor of $1.7\ \mu W\ K^{-2}\ cm^{-1}$). They also reported that doping with Nb at the X site further improves the power factor to 13 times due to containing a 1% Nb composition. Later, Sakurada and Shutoh $^{41}$ also showed the improvement of $zT$ due to the combined effect of high power factor and low thermal conductivity by X and Z site substitution and obtained a $zT$ of 1.3 at 700 K for $(Zr_{0.5}Hf_{0.5})_{0.5}Ti_{0.5}NiSn$ and 1.5 for further Sn doped $(Zr_{0.5}Hf_{0.5})_{0.5}Ti_{0.5}NiSn_{0.998}Sb_{0.002}$ at the same temperature. Zhang et al. $^{42}$ used first principles calculations to explain the reasons behind the increase in the Seebeck coefficient for X site substitution and power factor improvement due to Z site co-doping. Zou et al. and other independent groups investigated the maximum power factor and corresponding optimal carrier concentration due to 50% Hf substitution at the Zr site $^{43,44}$ using first principles methods and suggested guidelines for tuning the doping levels and composition to enhance the thermoelectric performance. $^{45-48}$ Each of these studies provides some critical explanation towards the step by step realization of high $zT$ HH thermoelectrics and reveals the huge potential of this compound class. While the vast majority of earlier reported studies have focussed on analysing the effect of tuning the power factor, the main challenge in implementation of half Heusler compounds as thermoelectrics arises from their large value of $\kappa_{l}$, in spite of their tunable narrow band gap and high power factor. Hence, it is supremely important to explore the scope of reducing the $\kappa_{l}$, via introducing mass disorder, without compromising on the vital parameters in the electronic structure leading to their high power factor. $^{49-51}$ If such compounds can be identified they may act as the perfect basis for chemical doping for tuning the carrier concentration and hence improving the $zT$.

In this article, the effect of compositional variation via isoelectronic substitution on the stability and electronic and transport properties of semiconducting $X_{p}X'_{1-p}Y_{q}Y'_{1-q}Z_{r}Z'_{1-r}$ (X, X' = Ti, Zr, Hf; Y, Y' = Ni, Pd, Pt and Z, Z' = Ge, Sn, Pb) HH compositions is explored, where $p$, $q$, and $r$ are the different concentrations of elemental isoelectronic substitutions at the X, Y and Z sites respectively. The purpose of this work is to identify the compound(s) that simultaneously satisfies the requisite criteria of high power factor and low thermal conductivity, so that it may act as a semiconducting basis for further chemical doping.

## Computational details

High throughput density functional theory (DFT) $^{52,53}$ calculations are performed using the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Program (VASP), $^{54,55}$ which is a plane wave-based electronic structure code with Projector Augmented Wave (PAW) and Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) $^{56}$ formulation of the exchange-correlation energy functional under a Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA). A plane-wave cutoff energy of 500 eV and an energy convergence criterion of $10^{-3}$ eV are used. For static calculations, a converged Monkhorst-Pack $k$-mesh grid is applied for the simulation cell. For each structure, ionic as well as geometric relaxations are performed.

In order to examine the stability of the compositions, the formation energy of the compounds is calculated using

$$
E_{\mathrm{f}}=E\left(\mathrm{X}_{m} \mathrm{Y}_{n} \mathrm{Z}_{t}\right)-m \frac{E\left(\mathrm{X}_{a}\right)}{a}-n \frac{E\left(\mathrm{Y}_{b}\right)}{b}-t \frac{E\left(\mathrm{Z}_{c}\right)}{c} \tag{1}
$$

where, $E(\mathrm{X}_{m}\mathrm{Y}_{n}\mathrm{Z}_{t})$ is the total energy of the compound and $E(\mathrm{X}_{a})$, $E(\mathrm{Y}_{b})$, and $E(\mathrm{Z}_{c})$ are the total energies of the bulk phases of X, Y, and Z with $a$, $b$ and $c$ numbers of atoms respectively.

The electronic transport coefficients, i.e. the thermopower $(S)$ and electronic conductivity $(\sigma)$, are calculated using semiclassical Boltzmann theory $^{57}$ as implemented in the BoltzTraP package, $^{58}$ where scattering is treated perturbatively (but not self-consistently) on top of the rigid band and constant relaxation time approximation. Accordingly, even the thermal renormalization of the electronic structure, i.e., the temperature dependent change of the electronic band structure due to the electron-phonon coupling, is typically neglected in these approaches. $^{59}$ However, this pretty brute simplification implies huge savings in the computational effort and thus enables large-scale, high-throughput calculations. Fully optimized crystal structures are considered for the transport calculations, whereby a dense Monkhorst-Pack $k$-mesh is used for obtaining the energy eigen values.

The phonon band structure is calculated using the finite displacement method as implemented in the phonopy $^{60}$ code. A converged supercell of $2 \times 2 \times 2$ is used to calculate the phonon band structure. The amplitude of the displacements is fixed to $0.01\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, and the forces are converged to an accuracy of $10^{-8}$ eV $\mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{-1}$. The phonon group velocity $v$ ($v = \mathrm{d}\omega/\mathrm{d}K$), mode resolved phonon group velocity $v_{\mathrm{i}}$ ($v_{\mathrm{i}} = \mathrm{d}\omega_{\mathrm{i}}/\mathrm{d}K$), Grüneisen parameter $\gamma$ $\left(\gamma = -\frac{V_{0}}{\omega} \frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{\mathrm{d}V}\right)$, and mode resolved Grüneisen parameter $\gamma_{\mathrm{i}}$ $\left(\gamma_{\mathrm{i}} = -\frac{V_{0}}{\omega_{\mathrm{i}}} \frac{\mathrm{d}\omega_{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{d}V}\right)$ are extracted from the harmonic phonon band dispersion using python based extensions to phonopy-VASP. For calculating the $\gamma$ and $\gamma_{\mathrm{i}}$, the lattice is subjected to strains of $\pm 2\%$. Finally, we use the Asen-Palmer modified version of

Debye-Callaway theory⁶¹ parameterized for solids¹¹,⁶²⁻⁶⁶ to calculate the $\kappa_1$ of the compounds.

## 3 Results and discussion
### 3.1 Compositional space
The compositional space attempted in this work is enormous, i.e. $X_pX'_{1-p}Y_qY'_{1-q}Z_rZ'_{1-r}$ (where X, $X'$ = Ti, Zr, Hf; Y, $Y'$ = Ni, Pd, Pt and Z, $Z'$ = Ge, Sn, Pb and $p, q,$ and $r = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1$). In order to reduce the number of compositions judiciously without eliminating the vital ones, an intelligent screening policy is adopted. As the first step, the electronic structure of all the 27 parent compounds (i.e. those with one element occupying one site to attain the formula XYZ with X = Ti, Zr, Hf; Y = Ni, Pd, Pt, and Z = Ge, Sn, Pb) is computationally evaluated. The computed band gap of the parent compounds shows wide variation, i.e. from 0.35 to 1.12 eV (see the ESI† for details). The compounds are preliminarily screened on the basis of their electronic band gap and number of valley degeneracies at the conduction band minimum (CBm) and valence band maximum (VBM), non-toxicity and earth abundancy of the elemental constituents (as discussed in detail in Section 3.4.1). After careful evaluation of the band structure ZrNiSn, TiNiSn and HfNiSn are selected, since they have earth abundant and non toxic Ni and Sn occupying the Y and Z sites. The different stoichiometries (with varying $p$) for the composition $X_pX'_{1-p}$NiSn, with X, $X'$ = Ti, Zr, Hf, are considered in the second step (as discussed in detail in Section 3.4.2). Once the stoichiometry and the composition for X site substitution are identified on the basis of their high power factor and low lattice thermal conductivity, they are kept fixed for the subsequent Y site substitution. Thus, for Y site substitution, the stoichiometry and the composition at the Y site is varied, but the previously identified compositions at the X site and the elemental composition at the Z site is kept unchanged (as discussed in Sections 3.4.3 and 3.4.4). Finally, the same procedure is carried out for Z site substitution by retaining the composition and stoichiometry for the previously identified X and Y site. The entire workflow is depicted schematically in Fig. 1, while the results are discussed in the succeeding sections.

### 3.2 Structure and stoichiometry
For the sake of computational brevity, substitutions have been restricted to achieve three different stoichiometries apart from the parental compositions, viz. $p/q/r = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$ to achieve the compositions $X_pX'_{1-p}Y_qY'_{1-q}Z_rZ'_{1-r}$. The primitive cell (space group 216, $F\overline{4}3m$) is considered for calculating the electronic structure and transport coefficients of the parental HH compositions (with the lattice parameter ranging from $\sim$4.0 to 4.5 Å depending upon the variation in the compositional space). The conventional cubic lattice of XYZ (space group $F\overline{4}3m$) has 12 atoms (i.e. 4 X, 4 Y and 4 Z atoms) in the lattice (with the lattice parameter varying from $\sim$5.9 to 6.4 Å). This particular lattice (i.e. 4 X, 4 Y, and 4 Z atoms per f.u.) is used as the initial structure to identify the ground state structure for the substitutions $p/q/r = 0.25, 0.75$ in different X, Y and Z site substituted cases, via an iterative scanning strategy. It should be noted that such substitutions do not break the symmetry of the conventional cubic structure (cf. Fig. 2(a)). However, for $p/q/r = 0.5$, the primitive cell is found to have a tetragonal geometry with an elongated $c$ axis and half the number of atoms (i.e. six) as in the conventional cell (space group number 115, $P\overline{4}m2$ as shown in Fig. 2(b)). For example, the elongated $c$ axis ($\sim$6.06 Å) of the 50% substituted compound $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiSn$ (tetragonal primitive cell) is found to lie between the lattice constant of ZrNiSn ($\sim$6.1 Å) and TiNiSn ($\sim$5.9 Å), which is in confirmation with Vegard's law.⁴⁴,⁶⁷

### 3.4 Electronic structure analysis

The electronic band structure (eBS) of the compounds may be
analysed in terms of several factors as has been enumerated
below (as demonstrated in the schematics in Fig. 4(a));
- Orbital degeneracy, $N_o$, which is the number of bands
converging at the VBM or CBm at the same K point).

- K point degeneracy, $N_{\text{K}}$, which is the degeneracy of equivalent valleys due to the high symmetry point in the Brillouin zone (i.e. $N_{\text{K}} = 1$ for the $\Gamma$ point and $N_{\text{K}} = 3$ for the X point).
- Valley/tip degeneracy, $N_{\text{E}}$, which is the number of tips lying at the same energy level for all different K points.
- Band/total degeneracy, $N_{\text{v}}$, which is derived from all these degeneracies collectively for a given high symmetry point.

Using these notations, the eBS of the parent as well as the substituted compounds is analysed in the following.

### 3.4.1 Parent half Heusler compounds
The primitive cell of the XYZ parent compounds, containing three atoms (one of each species) per unit cell, is considered for performing the eBS calculation. The eBS is plotted along the high symmetry path W-L-$\Gamma$-X-W-K of the irreducible part of the first Brillouin zone (as shown in Fig. 4(b)). All the parent compounds show similar trends in terms of band degeneracies. For instance, the VBM at the $\Gamma$ point ($N_{\text{K}} = 1$) has three fold orbital degeneracy ($N_{\text{o}} = 3$) leading to a band degeneracy of $N_{\text{v}} = 3$, while the CBm has a single valley at the X point ($N_{\text{K}} = 3$) leading to a band degeneracy of $N_{\text{v}} = 3$). All the selected parent compounds (i.e. TiNiSn, ZrNiSn, HfNiSn, ZrPdSn, ZrPtSn, ZrNiGe, and ZrNiPb) are found to be indirect semiconductors, with their band gap varying in the range of $\sim$0.35 to 1.0 eV. However, a majority of the bandgap values show fair agreement with other theoretical reports,⁶⁸⁻⁷⁰ which are approximately $\sim$0.3 eV higher than the corresponding experimental results (0.12, 0.19 and 0.22 eV for TiNiSn, ZrNiSn, and HfNiSn, respectively).²⁹,⁷¹,⁷² This trend is anomalous to this specific class of compound, since DFT at the GGA level generally underestimates the band gap of semiconductors. The reason for this inconsistency is the presence of structural imperfections, such as defects, boundaries, *etc.* in the experimental samples.

The effect of spin-orbit coupling (soc) in the eBS is also explored for these compounds and is, however, found to be very minor (see the ESI† for details). Thus, as revealed from the preliminary band structure analysis, no particular parent XYZ compound is found to be superior or inferior to the other as far as its thermoelectric application is concerned, since they all have narrow band gap with the similar features in the eBS. Therefore, for the X site substitution, all the three elements *viz.* Ti, Zr, and Hf are selected for isoelectronic substitution, while the elemental composition for the Y (=Ni) and Z (=Sn) site is kept fixed keeping the non toxicity and earth abundancy in mind. It should be noted here that the selected compositions *viz.* TiNiSn, ZrNiSn and HfNiSn are also the most studied n-type Heusler compounds. Thus, the compositions with $\text{X}_{p}\text{X}'_{1-p}\text{NiSn}$, with X, X' = Ti, Zr, Hf, are explored for the X site substitutions as discussed in the succeeding section.

The eBS of $\text{X}_{p}\text{X}'_{1-p}\text{NiSn}$ compositions with X, X' = Ti, Zr, Hf is analysed for different stoichiometries *viz.* $p = 0.25$, $0.5$, $0.75$. As discussed earlier, the symmetry of the structure of all compositions with $p = 0.25$ and $p = 0.75$, *i.e.* $\text{X}_{0.25}\text{X}'_{0.75}\text{NiSn}$ and $\text{X}_{0.75}\text{X}'_{0.25}\text{NiSn}$, is found to be identical to that of the conventional parent lattice. The corresponding band structure of the representative compounds is included in the ESI.† These substitutions result in a direct band gap semiconductor, with the CBm lying above the VBM at the $\Gamma$ point. The $p = 0.5$ case, on the other hand, has an increased number of valley and band degeneracies as compared to the parent as well as the substituted ones with $p = 0.25$, $0.75$ stoichiometries for all compositional variations. As has been discussed in detail in the ESI,† the electronic transport properties of the $p = 0.5$ case are only marginally better than those of $p = 0.25$, $0.75$. For $p = 0.5$, all compositions are found to have a direct band gap at the $\Gamma$ point in the range of $\sim$0.40 to 0.45 eV, with another energy degenerate band pocket forming at the M point, where two bands merge (see Fig. 4(c)). 3.4.3 Y site substitution. For the Y site substitution, the eBS of the compositions $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}Y_{q}Y'_{1-q}Sn$ for Y, $Y' = Ni$, Pd, Pt is explored for $q = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$. However, in order to highlight the adverse effect of Y site substitution alone, the eBS of the composition $ZrNi_{0.5}Pd_{0.5}Sn$ (as shown in Fig. 4(d)) is presented and analysed as the representative case (while the other stoichiometries are included in the ESI in Section S3.5$\dagger$). From this, several negative effects of Y site substitution in the eBS are identified, viz. the splitting of the orbital and valley degeneracy collectively leading to lowering of the band degeneracies drastically at the CBm and VBM. For instance, in $ZrNi_{0.5}Pd_{0.5}Sn$ (Fig. 4(d)), the CBm is found at the M point ($N_{o}=1$), while the VBM is at the $\Gamma$ point ($N_{o}=2$). An energy difference of $\sim$0.15 eV is observed between the CBm at the $\Gamma$ and M point, while an energy difference of $\sim$0.35 eV is observed between the two conduction bands at the M point. Also, an energy difference of $\sim$0.12 eV is observed between the hole bands at the $\Gamma$-point of the VBM (see the ESI, Tables S2 and S3$\dagger$). The lowering of the bands at the M point also results in reduction of the band gap in all cases (in the range of $\sim$0.15 to 0.22 eV) and all compositions are found to be indirect semiconductors. Thus, it can be inferred that the Y site substitution in HHs is not particularly advantageous for thermoelectric applications. Moreover, the lowering of the band gap in these compositions may also give rise to adverse bipolar effects in the transport coefficients, due to which both charge carriers may cancel each other out even at room temperature.

In order to explore the reason behind the splitting of the degenerate bands at the M point, which is observed uniquely only for the Y site substitution, the local symmetry around the substituted atom in the tetragonal structure is investigated. For instance, the local symmetry surrounding the Pd atom at the center of the tetragonal $ZrNi_{0.5}Pd_{0.5}Sn$ crystal is found to be broken because of the presence of two different atoms as the first nearest neighbor (leading to different bond lengths arising from the Pd-Sn and the Pd-Zr). However, such a situation does not arise for X site or Z site substitution, where the substituted atom sitting at the center always has the same atom as its first nearest neighbor and its second nearest neighbor. Therefore, the local symmetry is retained for the X site as well as the Z site substitution (see ESI Fig. S11$\dagger$ and the discussion therein for more details).

Thus, $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiSn$ is still retained as the best starting composition for the Z site substitution, which is discussed in the subsequent section.

3.4.4 Z site substitution. For the Z site substitution, the eBS of the compositions $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiZ_{r}Z'_{1-r}$ for Z, $Z' = Ge$, Sn, Sb is analysed for $r = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$. However, in order to highlight the sole effect of Z site substitution, the eBS of the composition $ZrNiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$ (as shown in Fig. 4(e)), is also presented as the representative case (while the other stoichiometries are included in the ESI$\dagger$). For all compositions with $r = 0.5$, the Z site substitution, however, shows exactly the same trend as X site substitution in terms of band and valley degeneracies, with the band gap lying in the range $\sim$0.54 to 0.60 eV. For instance, in the Z site substituted $ZrNiSn_{0.5}Ge_{0.5}$ compound, the same energy value is observed at the sub bands of the M point and a very small energy difference of $\sim$0.005 eV is observed between the two CBEs (M and $\Gamma$ point) (see the ESI; Tables S2 and S3$\dagger$).

3.5.2 Lattice thermal conductivity. The Asen-Palmer modified version of Debye-Callaway theory parametrized for solids (see Sections S4 and S5 of the ESI† for details) is used to model the phonon lifetimes for the normal as well as the Umklapp scattering processes by including the contributions stemming from the transverse acoustic (TA and TA') and the longitudinal acoustic (LA) phonon modes (from their respective mode group velocities $(v_{i})$, Grüneisen parameters $(\gamma_{i})$ and Debye temperatures $(\theta_{i})$ for i = TA, TA', LA modes. The corresponding plots for the phonon spectrum, group velocity and Grüneisen parameter are included in the ESI.† The corresponding lattice thermal conductivity $(\kappa_{1}=\sum \kappa_{i}$, for i = TA, TA', LA) is calculated from the corresponding values of $v_{i}, \gamma_{i}, \theta_{i}$, etc., which are given in Table 3. The $\kappa_{1}$ of the compounds with high power factors viz. the parent (ZrNiSn) and X-site $(Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiSn)$, Z-site $(ZrNiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5})$ and co-site $(Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5})$ substituted compounds are only presented for the sake of brevity (see Fig. 7 and Table 3). The $v_{i}$ values and average group velocity of sound $(v_{s})$ are found to be similar for all the selected compounds. The average Debye temperature $\theta_{D}$ is found to be the lowest for the X site $(Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiSn)$ while it is the highest for the co-site $(Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5})$ substituted case. It should be noted that the integration for $\kappa_{1}$ is limited by the $\theta_{i}$ values and hence lower $\theta_{i}$ values indicate lower $\kappa_{1}$. However, the $\gamma_{i}$ values are found to be different for the compounds, which play the deterministic factor in the change observed in the $\kappa_{1}$. The $\gamma_{i}$ values are found to be the lowest for ZrNiSn, comparable for $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiSn$ and $ZrNiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$ and the highest for $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5-}$ $NiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$, pertaining to the highest mass disorder in similar cell volume. The high anharmonicity, as indicated by the high $\gamma_{i}$ values in $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$, is responsible for the increased scattering centers and hence the lowest $\kappa_{1}$ in the compound. In summary, the $\kappa_{1}$ of the substituted compounds is found to be significantly lower than that of the parent compound (by more than 60%). However, in this formalism the effect of defect phonon scattering is not incorporated explicitly, which arises from the mass and strain fluctuations stemming from the substitutions.⁶¹,⁷³ If explicitly taken into account it may still reduce the $\kappa_{1}$ for the 50% substituted ones as compared to the 25% and 75% substitutions at a given site (see ESI† S5.5 for the corresponding discussion).

3.5.3 Figure of merit. The dimensionless figure of merit of a thermoelectric material $(zT)$ can be written as the sum of the electronic and lattice figures of merit $\left(\frac{1}{z T}=\frac{1}{z T_{\mathrm{el}}}+\frac{1}{z T_{\mathrm{l}}}\right)$. $zT_{\text{el}}$ is calculated from the transport parameters obtained in BoltzTrap viz. $S, \sigma/\tau, \kappa_{\text{el}}/\tau$ as $z T_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{S^{2} \sigma / \tau T}{\kappa_{\mathrm{e}} / \tau}$, whereas $zT_{1}$ is calculated as $z T_{\mathrm{l}}=\frac{S^{2} \sigma / \tau T}{\kappa_{\mathrm{l}}}$ (where $\tau$ is chosen to be $10^{-14}$ s), and $\kappa_{1}$ is calculated using the Debye-Callaway formalism as discussed before. The $zT$ for X-, Y-, Z- and co-substituted ZrNiSn is also given in Table 1. Pertaining to its lowest power factor, the $zT$ of the Y-site substituted $ZrNi_{0.5}Pd_{0.5}Sn$ is found to be the lowest, which shows a reduction of 65% (n-type) and 52% (p-type) in the $zT$ as compared to that of the parent compound. The $zT$ of X-site substituted $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiSn$ and Z-site substituted $ZrNiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$ is found to be comparable for both p- and n-type doping. This is found to be approximately 24% (for n-type) and 20% (for p-type) higher than that of the parent ZrNiSn, which stems from the low $\kappa_{1}$ of the substituted compounds. Pertaining to its lowest $\kappa_{1}$, the co-site substituted $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$ shows a comparable $zT$

to those of X and Z site substituted compounds, in spite of the comparatively lower power factor for the p-type doping.

## 4 Conclusions
The structural, electronic and transport properties of 18 valence electron count based $X_pX'_{1-p}Y_qY'_{1-q}Z_rZ'_{1-r}$ (where X, X' = Ti, Zr, Hf; Y, Y' = Ni, Pd, Pt and Z, Z' = Ge, Sn, Pb and p, q, and r = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) half-Heusler compounds are investigated using the high throughput first-principles density functional theory calculations. An intelligent scanning strategy is adapted. As the first step, the electronic structure of all the parent compounds is explored in terms of parameters such as the band degeneracy, valley degeneracy, band gap, *etc.*, which are generally the most important parameters for improving the power factor. The parent compounds *viz.* TiNiSn, ZrNiSn and HfNiSn are selected, where the Y and Z elements are chosen and retained because of their non-toxicity and earth abundance. Subsequently, the elemental composition at the X site is varied as $X_pX'_{1-p}$NiSn with X, X' = Ti, Zr, Hf for different stoichiometries *viz.* $p = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$. All the substituted compounds are found to have negative formation energy, which is calculated by static energy calculations. From the band engineering perspective, all these combinations are found to have similar electronic structures as long as the stoichiometries are retained. However, among the different stoichiometries, the $X_{0.5}X'_{0.5}$NiSn are found to have higher band and valley degeneracies, indicating improvement in the power factor. Thus, $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}$NiSn is chosen as the representative case from the X site substituted compositions (because of the earth abundance of the elements) before proceeding to subsequent substitutions. As the next step, the electronic structure of the Y site substituted compositions *viz.* $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}Y_qY'_{1-q}$Sn for Y, Y' = Ni, Pd, Pt is explored for $q = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$. However, all the Y site substitutions led to breaking of the degeneracy of the bands and hence are not found to be effective for improving the electronic power factor of the compounds. Subsequently, the electronic structure of the Z site substituted compositions *viz.* $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiZ_rZ'_{1-r}$ for Z, Z' = Ge, Sn, Sb with $r = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$ is explored. Our results reveal that X site and Z site doping may be independently chosen for improving the power factor, while X and Z co-site doping may lead to a lowering of the power factor for the p-type material. As the last step, the lattice thermal conductivity of these representative compounds is calculated using the Asen Palmer modified version of Debye–Callaway theory parameterized for solids. The substituted compounds are found to have $\kappa_1$ reduced by at least ~60%, which is due to the introduction of mass disorder in the same lattice volume. Finally, the $zT$ of X-site substituted $Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}$NiSn and Z-site substituted $ZrNiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$ (being comparable for both p- and n-type doping) is found to be approximately 24% (for n-type) and 20% (for p-type) higher than that of the parent ZrNiSn, which stems from the low $\kappa_1$ of the substituted compounds. However, the co-site substituted composition ($Ti_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}NiGe_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}$) is found to have comparable $zT$ for both the p- and n-type doping, which is preferential for its device application.

In summary, DFT calculations are performed to explore the thermoelectric properties of the X, Y and Z substituted half Heusler compounds without restricting the compositions to any prior biases from previous literature. The calculations confirm several known facts while revealing several unknown ones. Exemplarily, the Y site substitution is generally avoided experimentally (if at all, there are only a few reports of very minute doping at the Y site, which has improved the $zT$). Our study confirms this scenario and reveals the predicament behind such substitution. However, the Z site substitution leads to enhancement of the power factor, which is comparable to the scenario of X site substitution. However, the Z site substitution has not been hitherto explored thoroughly, which may provide further scope in tuning the $zT$ of these compounds. Thus, this study may serve as a consolidated blue print for experimentalists working with similar compositions for enhancing the power factor and $zT$ of the compositions.
