# Revision of the Ge-Ti Phase Diagram and Structural Stability of the new Phase $\boldsymbol{Ge_4Ti_5}$

Roland W. Bittner$^1$, Catherine Colinet$^2$, Jean-Claude Tedenac$^3$ and Klaus W. Richter$^{1*}$

## Keywords
Phase Diagrams, Crystal Structure, First-Principle Calculations, Enthalpy of Formation

### Abstract

The binary phase diagram Ge-Ti was investigated experimentally by powder X-ray diffraction, scanning electron microscopy including EDX analysis, and differential thermal analysis. Total energies of the compounds $GeTi_3$, $GeTi_2$, $Ge_3Ti_5$, $Ge_4Ti_5$, $Ge_5Ti_6$, $GeTi$ and $Ge_2Ti$ were calculated for various structure types employing electronic density-functional theory (DFT). Experimental studies as well as electronic calculations show the existence of a new phase $Ge_4Ti_5$ ($Ge_4Sm_5$-type, $oP36$, $Pnma$) which is formed in a solid state reaction $Ge_3Ti_5 + Ge_5Ti_6 = Ge_4Ti_5$. In addition, a significant homogeneity range was observed for the compound $Ge_3Ti_5$ and the composition of the liquid phase in the eutectic reaction $L = Ge + Ge_2Ti$ was found to be at significant higher Ge-content (97.5 at.% Ge) than reported in previous studies. Based on these new results, a modified phase diagram Ge-Ti is suggested. The zero-temperature lattice parameters and the formation enthalpies determined by DTF calculations were found to be in good agreement with experimental data.

### Introduction

Only little literature exists on the phase equilibria of the Ge-Ti system. The assessed phase diagram given in Massalski's compilation [1] mainly relies on the work of Rudometkina et al. [2] who investigated a couple of binary samples with DTA and metallographic analysis. Recently Liu et al. [3] published a thermodynamic assessment of the binary system which takes into account experimental phase diagram results of six groups of authors, and was also supported by key experiments on seven additional samples investigated by XRD, DTA and metallography. The calculated phase diagram does agree largely with the assessment given in [1]. Three stable binary phases are found in the system: $Ge_3Ti_5$ ($Mn_5Si_3$-type) [4] with a congruent melting point, $Ge_5Ti_6$ ($Ge_5V_6$-type) [5] and $Ge_2Ti$ ($TiSi_2$-type) [6] which both melt incongruently. It should be pointed out that no solubility ranges were investigated for any of these compounds, so all of them were modelled as line compounds in [3]. Wirringa et al. [7] performed chemical vapour transport experiments showing some discrepancies to previous authors, since they were not able to transport $Ge_5Ti_6$ and concluded that this phase is not thermodynamically stable at $700^\circ C$ and $800^\circ C$. However, this was not confirmed by Liu et al. [3].

In the current study we report on new experimental and theoretical results in the Ge-Ti system, which were obtained independently by the authors situated in Vienna and in France. The experimental study performed in Vienna is part of a systematic survey of Al-Ge-TM (TM = transition metal) systems which is motivated by the search for new materials for joining applications of advanced aluminides. Phase equilibria in the system Al-Ge-Ni were published recently [8, 9], while the phase diagram Al-Ge-Ti is currently under investigation. During these studies a new compound with the approximate composition $Ge_4Ti_5$, containing less than 1 at. % Al was found in several ternary Al-Ge-Ti samples. As this observation strongly suggested the existence of binary $Ge_4Ti_5$, a partial re-investigation of the binary system was carried out, which is presented here. The investigation of the structural stability of the

intermetallic compounds in the binary Ge-Ti (performed by the French authors) began with the study of the stability of ternary $Ti_6Ge_2B$ in the $Fe_2P$-type structure [10]. In the course of this study we performed a systematic investigation of the stability of Ti-Ge compounds in order to calculate the Ti-Ge-B phase diagram at low temperature.

## Experimental
For sample preparation, pieces from a titanium rod (99.99%, Johnson Matthey Chemicals) and germanium pieces (99.999%, Johnson Matthey Chemicals) were weighted using a semi-micro balance. The samples (1g nominal weight) were melted three times under argon atmosphere in an arc furnace, in order to obtain homogenous distribution of the elements. Zirconium was used as a getter material to provide an oxygen-free atmosphere within the furnace chamber. Some of the samples were annealed at $1000^\circ$C for 4 weeks in alumina crucibles sealed in evacuated quartz glass and then quenched in cold water; others were investigated directly after arc furnace melting (as cast). The samples were subsequently investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), powder X-ray diffractrometry (XRD) and differential thermal analysis (DTA).

For SEM, the samples were embedded in conductive phenolic mounting resin, ground with silicon carbide abrasive paper and polished with corundum powder. The measurements were performed on a Zeiss Supra 55 VP scanning electron microscope using 20kV acceleration voltage. Energy dispersive X-ray analysis (EDX) was used for quantitative analyses of the phase compositions. The energy calibration of the EDX signal was done with pure cobalt. Each phase present in the sample was measured at least at three different spots. A minimum grain size of $5\ \mu$m was used for quantitative measurements. The accuracy of the compositions determined by EDX is estimated to be within $\pm1$at.%.

XRD was used to identify the phases, and the cell parameters as well as other crystallographic parameters were determined by Rietveld refinement. Powdered samples were investigated using a Bruker D8 powder diffractometer. The measurements were performed with copper Kα radiation using a one-dimensional silicon strip detector (LynxEye) in Bragg-Brentano pseudo focusing geometry. The data were evaluated using the software TOPAS.

DTA measurements were done on a Setaram SETSYS Evolution TGA & DTA 2400 using a type-S (Pt/Pt10%Rh) thermocouple which was calibrated with the melting points of pure gold, nickel and tin. Each measurement was performed with about 20 to 50 mg of the sample using a heating rate of 5K/min and an argon flow of 20ml/min. The heating and cooling program was applied and recorded twice to each sample.

### Computational Methods
The first-principles calculations were based on density functional theory (DFT) as implemented in the Vienna ab-initio simulation package (VASP) [11]. The calculations were performed using the projector augmented waves (PAW) technique [12, 13] within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). Two functionals were used: first the Perdew-Wang functional (PW91) [14, 15] and later the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof functional (PBE) [16]. The calculations included four valence electrons for Ge ($4s^2\ 4p^2$ electronic configuration) and ten valence electrons for Ti ($3p^6\ 3d^3\ 4s^1$ electronic configuration). However, in order to test the influence of the semi-core electrons of both elements on the values of the total energies, we have also performed calculations considering the Ge $3d^{10}\ 4s^2$ $4p^2$ and Ti $3s^2\ 3p^6\ 3d^3\ 4s^1$ electronic configurations. In that case, the semi-core 3d orbitals as well as the 3s orbitals of Ti are considered as valence electrons. Brillouin-zone integrations

are performed using the Methfessel-Paxton [17] technique with a modest smearing of the one-electron levels (0.2 eV).

A Gamma centred k-point grid was used for the hexagonal structures while a Monkhorst-Pack [18] grid was chosen for the other structures. In each case, the number of k points has been chosen in such a way that the number of k points in the irreducible Brillouin zone multiplied by the number of atoms in the cell is at least greater than 500. The number of k points was subsequently increased until convergent results were achieved for cell parameters and total energies. All calculations have been performed using the "accurate" setting within VASP to avoid wrap-around errors. The energy of formation, at zero pressure is obtained from the minimum total energy of the compound (expressed per atom) by subtracting the composition-weighted minimum total energies of pure cubic diamond (A4) Ge and pure hexagonal close-packed (A3) Ti:

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} \mathrm{E}\left(\mathrm{Ge}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Ti}_{1-\mathrm{x}}\right)=\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{Ge}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Ti}_{1-\mathrm{x}}}^{\text {min }}-\mathrm{x} \mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{A} 4-\mathrm{Ge}}^{\text {min }}-(1-\mathrm{x}) \mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{A} 3-\mathrm{Ti}}^{\text {min }} \tag{1}
$$

The $E^{min}$ values are obtained after volume relaxation, this implies a zero pressure. At T=0K and p=0 Pa, the formation enthalpy equals the calculated formation energy, when the zero-vibration contribution is ignored, since it is much smaller than the formation energy.

## Results and discussion
### Experimental Results

More than 20 samples were prepared and investigated in the Ge-Ti system, covering the composition area between 32 and 98 at.% Ge. Samples between 32 and 66 at% Ge were annealed at $1000^\circ$C, while some additional samples used for the investigation of the Ge-rich eutectic were investigated directly after arc melting. Experimental results from XRD and SEM/EDX for a representative selection of samples are listed in Table 1. Experimental results

show that the currently accepted phase diagram [1, 3] needs some corrections which are discussed below. The proposed phase diagram according to our results is shown in Fig 1.

First of all, the new phase with the composition $Ge_4Ti_5$, which was originally observed in several ternary Al-Ge-Ti samples, was confirmed to exist as stable binary phase. This compound was found in several samples situated in the narrow composition window between $Ge_3Ti_5$ and $Ge_5Ti_6$ after long term annealing at $1000^\circ$C. It was not possible to obtain completely equilibrated samples in this composition area; minor amounts of a third impurity phase ($Ge_3Ti_5$ or $Ge_5Ti_6$) were always found in the XRD pattern. However, as $Ge_4Ti_5$ was not present in the samples after arc melting, and formed during annealing, there is no doubt that it is an equilibrium phase at the temperature of annealing. A BSE image of the sample with the nominal composition $Ge_{42}Ti_{58}$ is shown in Fig. 2. The darker crystals correspond to the phase $Ge_3Ti_5$, while brighter crystals are the new phase $Ge_4Ti_5$. $Ge_5Ti_6$, which was found in traces in the XRD powder pattern, is not present in the area of the sample shown here.

The crystal structure of $Ge_4Ti_5$ was identified by comparison of the observed powder pattern with different structure types occurring in chemically related systems. A structural model based on the orthorhombic $Ge_4Sm_5$-type structure showed excellent agreement with the observed powder pattern. Crystal structure parameters were observed by Rietveld refinement of the powder pattern of a sample with the nominal composition $Ge_{42.5}Ti_{57.5}$ containing approximately 55% $Ge_4Ti_5$ together with $Ge_3Ti_5$ and smaller amounts of $Ge_5Ti_6$. The crystallographic data are given in Table 2 together with the respective values obtained by DFT calculations. The comparison of experimental and refined powder pattern is shown in Fig. 3. Further details on the crystal structure of $Ge_4Ti_5$ were deposited and can be obtained

from the Fachinformationszentrum Karlsruhe¹ on quoting the depository number CSD
425837.

The new compound was most likely missed in previous studies, because it is only found in a
narrow composition window, which was not covered by any sample in the study of
Rudometkina et al. [2]. Furthermore, $Ge_4Ti_5$ is not found in as-cast samples situated in this
composition area, but can only be obtained after long term annealing. In addition, all attempts
to prepare primary crystals of $Ge_4Ti_5$ failed. Corresponding experiments were performed in
the composition area between 45 and 55 at.% Ge which would be the projected primary
crystallization field of peritectically formed $Ge_4Ti_5$. The XRD pattern of $Ge_4Ti_5$ was not
found in any of these as-cast samples. Therefore we conclude that $Ge_4Ti_5$ is not in equilibrium
with the liquid, but is rather formed in a peritectoid solid state reaction $Ge_3Ti_5 + Ge_5Ti_6 =
Ge_4Ti_5$.

Several samples were investigated by DTA at temperatures up to 1500 °C. Samples
containing $Ge_4Ti_5$ did not show any significant effect which could be attributed to the
proposed peritectoid reaction, so it is either too sluggish to be determined by DTA, or the
reaction temperature is higher than 1500°C. The invariant reactions found at the Ge-rich side
of the phase diagram were found to occur at significantly higher temperatures than reported
by Rodometkina et al [2]. The peritectic reaction $Ge_5Ti_6$+L = $Ge_2Ti$ was found at 1110°C and
therefore 35°C higher than previously published. For the eutectic reaction L = Ge+$Ge_2Ti$ we
observed 930.5±2°C compared to 900°C reported by Rudometkina. Both results are very well

in line with the new reaction temperatures given by Liu et al. [3] who found 1115°C (peritectic temperature) and 929°C (eutectic temperature), respectively.

Another problem observed during our DTA study concerns the eutectic composition for the reaction L = Ge+Ge₂Ti. According to Rudometkina et al. [2], The composition of the liquid in this reaction is about 89 at.% Ge and the respective value calculated in the thermodynamic assessment of Liu et al. [3] is 90.8 at% Ge. However, a sample with the composition Ge₉₀Ti₁₀ (which should be more or less eutectic according to these values), showed a liquidus temperature of 1043°C on cooling and 1067°C on heating. This clearly indicated primary crystallization of Ge₂Ti, as the melting point of pure germanium is only 938.3°C. Therefore it had to be concluded that the eutectic composition is found at significanty higher germanium content. This was investigated with some additional Ge-rich samples which were investigated by DTA and SEM/EDX. We could narrow down the eutectic composition with the as cast samples Ge₉₈Ti₂ and Ge₉₆Ti₄ since they showed primary crystallisation of germanium and Ge₂Ti, respectively. The corresponding BSE images are shown in Fig. 4. Area scans of the eutectic mixture in those samples suggested a eutectic composition of about Ge₉₇.₅Ti₂.₅ (compare Table 1). This value is very well in line with the extrapolation of the liquidus curve as shown in Figure 1.

A final correction of the current phase diagram version given by Liu et al. [3] concerns the homogeneity range of the phase Ge₃Ti₅. While previous authors regard this phase as stoichiometric Ge₃Ti₅ (37.5 at.% Ge), our EDX results of sample Ge₃₂Ti₆₈ and Ge₄₂Ti₅₈ suggest a solubility of about 3.3 at.% at 1000°C (36.8 to 40.1 at.% Ge). This observation is supported by the significant cell parameter variation found for Ge₃Ti₅ in the two samples. It is interesting to compare these results with experimental cell parameters reported previously. The crystal structure of Ge₃Ti₅ was originally determined by Pietrokowsky and Duwez [4],

who reported the cell parameters $a = 7.537$ Å and $c = 5.223$ Å which agree well with the values observed for the Ti-rich composition limit determined in the current study. A very similar value was later given by Rudometkina et al. who reported $a = 7.563(2)$ Å and $c = 5.228(2)$ Å for stoichiometric $Ge_3Ti_5$. Nickl et al. [19], on the other hand, reported the cell parameters of $a = 7.67(5)$ Å and $c = 5.30(5)$ Å for a phase they designated as "Ge₄Ti₅" with $Ga_4Ti_5$ -type structure. It must be emphasized, however, that the $Ga_4Ti_5$-type structure and the $Mn_5Si_3$-type of $Ge_3Ti_5$ are actually strongly related. Both structures differ only by the occupation of the $2b$ site in $P6_3/mcm$ which is empty in the $Mn_5Si_3$-type and (partially) filled by a main group element in the $Ga_4Ti_5$-type. A partial filling of this position is quite common and was observed, for example, in Zr-Sn [20] and in Nb-Ge [21] where the $2b$ site is half occupied by Ge yielding a composition of $Ge_7Nb_{10}$ (41.2 at.% Ge). Thus, the phase "Ge₄Ti₅" reported in [19] corresponds to the Ge-rich composition limit of $Ge_3Ti_5$ (40.1 at.% Ge; according to the current study) and should not be confused with $Ge_4Ti_5$ reported in the current study. The cell parameters observed in [19] agree very well with our own findings for the Ge-rich composition limit (Table 1).

### Results of the Computational Study
Computational results strongly support the relative stability of $Ge_4Ti_5$ in the $Ge_4Sm_5$-type structure. Calculations of binary Ge-Ti compounds have been performed for the structures reported in the Pearson's Handbook [22] and in the experimental Ge-Ti phase diagram [1]. Additional calculations have been performed for structures which are stable in the Si-Ti, Si-Zr, Sn-Ti, Sn-Zr phase diagrams. A complete list of structures considered in the calculation is given in Table 4. These calculations have been performed in the PW 91 approximation with the Ge 4s2 4p2 and Ti 3p6 3d3 4s1 electronic configurations with a plane-wave cut-off of

300eV. The calculated enthalpies of formation are displayed in Figure 3. The ground state line at T = 0K has been drawn and is formed by the enthalpies of formation of D8₈-Ge₃Ti₅, Ge₄Ti₅ with the Ge₄Sm₅ prototype structure and C54-Ge₂Ti. The stability of Ge₄Ti₅ with the Ge₄Sm₅-type structure is in line with experimental findings. The enthalpy of formation of the Ge₅Ti₆ compound with the Si₅V₆-type structure is slightly above the ground state line, therefore this compound is not stable at low temperature and stabilized at high temperature.

It should also be mentioned that the filled D8₈ structure (Ga₄Ti₅-type) is only about one kJ/mol of atoms less stable than the Ge₄Sm₅-type structure. This supports the experimental finding of a homogeneity range of Ge₃Ti₅: While the completely filled D8₈ structure is less stable than the Ge₄Sm₅-type, the small enthalpy difference to the stable phase promotes at least a partial filling of the additional 2b-position (as observed experimentally). Actually these DFT results will be very helpful for the selection of a proper sublattice model for Ge₅Ti₃ to be used for an updated CALPHAD-type thermodynamic assessment. The sublattice model corresponding to the crystallographic situation is (Ge)₃(Ge,Va)₁Ti₅, and the end member energies for (Ge)₃(Va)(Ti)₃ and (Ge)₃(Ge)₁(Ti)₃ should differ by approximately 1 kJ/mol of atoms.

In a second step of calculations, we used the PBE parameterization [16] with a cut-off of 350eV. In that case, the calculations include fourteen valence electrons for Ge ($3d^{10} 4s^{2} 4p^{2}$ electronic configuration) and twelve valence electrons for Ti ($3s^{2} 3p^{6} 3d^{3} 4s^{1}$). In previous calculations performed in Zr-Si and Ti-Si systems, we used both PW91 and PBE parameterizations [23, 24]. We observed that the enthalpies of formation are more negative using the PBE parameterization than those previously obtained with the PW91 parameterization. The effect is more and more important when the Si content increases. One must point out that the relative stability of the compounds and therefore the ground state line remains unchanged using either the PW91 or the PBE parameterization.

In the case of the Ge-Ti system, the same conclusions can be derived. Again, the relative stability of the compounds and therefore the ground state line remains unchanged using either the PW91 or the PBE parameterization. Considering the 3d electrons of Ge as valence electrons leads to enthalpies of formation which are slightly more negative, (maximum of 0.2 kJ/mol of atoms). Considering 3s semi-core electrons of Ti as valence electrons leads to enthalpies of formation which are slightly more negative (maximum of 0.2 kJ/mol of atoms). Calculation results for the ground state enthalpies using the PW91 (all structures) and PBE (selected structures) are given in Table 5.

The calculated values of the enthalpies of formation are in very good agreement with experimental determinations. Indeed, -70.7 ±2.4 kJ/mol of atoms was obtained by Kleppa and Jung [25] for the enthalpy of formation of $Ge_3Ti_5$ compound using the solute and solvent drop calorimetry method. Later Zarembo et al. [26] obtained the enthalpies of formation of $Ge_3Ti_5$ and $Ge_5Ti_6$ by combining high-temperature Knudsen effusion mass spectrometry and differential scanning calorimetry. They obtained -69.8±5 and -70.1±5 kJ/mol of atoms for these compounds, respectively. Recently Chen et al. [27] reported a calculated value of -69.1 kJ/mol of atoms for the enthalpy of formation of $Ge_3Ti_5$.

Due to the use of the GGA, the calculated values at T = 0K of the lattice parameters are slightly higher than the experimental ones, however the agreement can be considered as good. The calculated values of the internal parameters of the $Ge_4Ti_5$ compound with the $Ge_4Sm_5$ prototype structure are in fair agreement with the experimental ones.

## Conclusions
The existence of the new phase $Ge_4Ti_5$ ($Ge_4Sm_5$-type, $oP36$, $Pnma$) was predicted from DFT ground state energy calculations performed for various Ge-Ti compounds, and independently found in an experimental study of the Ge-Ti system. The new phase is formed in a solid state reaction $Ge_3Ti_5 + Ge_5Ti_6 = Ge_4Ti_5$. Contrary to previous studies, a significant homogeneity

range was observed for the compound $Ge_3Ti_5$ (ca. 37 to 40 at.% Ge) and the composition of the liquid phase in the eutectic reaction $L = Ge + Ge_2Ti$ was found to be at significantly higher Ge-content (97.5 at.% Ge) than reported in previous studies. The invariant reaction temperatures reported recently by Liu et al. [3] were confirmed. Based on these observations, a modified phase diagram of Ge-Ti was suggested.
