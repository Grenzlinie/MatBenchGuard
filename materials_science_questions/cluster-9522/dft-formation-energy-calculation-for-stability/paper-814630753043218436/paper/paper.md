# A Rational Approach to IrPTe – DFT and CalPhaD Studies on Phase Stability, Formation, and Structure of IrPTe

Florian Pielnhofer, $^{[a]}$ Michael Schöneich, $^{[b]}$ Tobias Lorenz, $^{[b]}$ Wenjie Yan, $^{[a]}$ Tom Nilges, $^{[c]}$  Richard Weihrich, $^{*[a,c]}$ and Peer Schmidt $^{*[b,d]}$

Dedicated to Professor Rüdiger Kniep on the Occasion of His 70th Birthday

**Keywords:** Phase stability; Density functional calculations; Gas balance; Thermodynamic modelling; Structure prediction

Abstract. Phase formation, stability, crystal and electronic structures of IrPTe are investigated from experiment and quantum chemical calculations. The phase formation is studied from thermodynamic data and CalPhaD modeling. Applying a high-temperature gas-balance a formation pathway for IrPTe is studied from the elements and the binary parent compounds $IrTe_{2}$ and $IrP_{2}$. The obtained paracostibite (CoSbS) type structure contains rarely occurring heteroatomic P–Te dumbbells. The stability of IrPTe and the found structure is studied from DFT calculations with respect to the elements, $IrTe_{2}$ and $IrP_{2}$, and possible polymorphs. Probable metastable modifications with $XY$ dumbbells (as known for isoelectronic compounds like CoAsS) are obtained from systematic DFT modelling. Phase transitions are predicted form the equation of states (EOS). According to its electronic band structure IrPTe is predicted as small gap ($\Delta E_{g}=0.5$ eV) semiconductor.

## Introduction
Inorganic compounds with 2-atomic polyanions $[X_{2}]^{n-}$ as building blocks attract high attention to chemists as exemplified for the discovery of diazenides like $SrN_{2}$ or $BaN_{2}$ by Kniep et al. $^{[1]}$ Polyanions also drive polymorphism, semiconducting and optoelectronic properties in heavier dipnictides $APn_{2}$, ($Pn=$ P,...Bi) and dichalcogenides $ACh_{2}$ ($Ch=$ S,...Te) known e.g. for pyrite ($c$-$FeS_{2}$) and marcasite ($o$-$FeS_{2}$), $PtP_{2}$, $NiAs_{2}$, or $NiP_{2}.^{[2]}$ Isoelectronic mixed pnictide chalcogenides $APnCh$ ($A=$ Co, Ir) were first studied because of their optoelectronic properties. $^{[3]}$ However, many of their crystal structures are still unknown due to lack of single crystals, polymorphism, pseudo-symmetry, and difficulties to distinguish element distribution in anions, e.g. in IrSbTe. Therein unconventional heteroatomic units $[PnCh]^{n-}$ are the key to their crystal and electronic structures, stabilities, and phase formation. $^{[4,5]}$

As a peculiar example $IrPTe^{[6]}$ is supposed to contain the rare $[PTe]^{3-}$ anion contrary to homoatomic $[P_{2}]$ units and $Te^{2-}$ ions as found in UPTe, $^{[7]}IrP_{2}$ and $IrTe_{2}.^{[8]}$ However, no exact structure data was available to date. P–Te bonds in IrPTe were concluded from isotypism to CoSbS and IR data. Isoelectronic $APnCh$ ($A=$ Co, Rh, Ir) compounds exhibit a variety of $APnCh$ structures and polymorphism due to coloring of anionic sites in $AX_{2}$ parent structures. $^{[4]}$ A prototype example is CoAsS that was estimated to crystallize in disordered pyrite or marcasite type structures. Onorato proposed an ordered model with separated $[As_{2}]^{4-}$ and $[S_{2}]^{2-}$ anions. $^{[9]}$ Later $[AsS]^{3-}$ units were confirmed in orthorhombic cobaltite and high temperature monoclinic alloclasite polymorphs. $^{[10]}$ The arrangement of $[SbS]^{3-}$ ions differs in costibite (CoSbS-i), paracostibite (CoSbS-ii), and a cubic high pressure form that is isotypic to IrSbTe (NiSbS, type). IrAsTe and IrBiTe belong to known pyrite related compounds with still unknown anion site ordering.

Formation, stability, crystal and electronic structures of IrPTe are subsequently investigated within our concept EnPhaSyn. $^{[11]}$ It combines quantum chemical and experimental studies. The principles of phase formation of ternary telluride phosphides $MTe_{x}P_{y}$ ($M=$ Ti, Zr, Ce, Si) containing isolated phosphide ($P^{3-}$) and telluride anions ($Te^{2-}$) and their crystallization in vapor transport reactions$^{[12]}$ have already been analyzed in detail. ## Results and Discussion
### DFT Calculations on the Stability of IrPTe

First, the relative stability of IrPTe is determined from DFT calculations with respect to stoichiometric amounts of the elements and $IrP_2$ and $IrTe_2$ as competing products in a closed system on the potential energy scale ($T = 0$ K, Figure 1a). Therefore, Kliche's orthorhombic CoSbS-type model was optimized ($o$-IrPTe, $Pbca$, Figure 1b). The structure can be seen as pyrite ($c$-$FeS_2$) related superstructure with P-Te units in the octahedral holes of an fcc type arrangement of Ir atoms (see next chapter). Good agreement is obtained for predicted ($a = 6.04$ Å, $b = 6.07$ Å, $c = 12.12$ Å) and experimental cell parameters [$a = 6.032(1)$ Å, $b = 6.134(1)$ Å, $c = 12.110(2)$ Å], as well as calculated and reported IR frequencies (Figure 1c). Due to total energy calculations $o$-IrPTe is stable with respect to the elements Ir, black P, Te, as well as $IrTe_2$ and $IrP_2$. The obtained energy of formation from the elements is $-217$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$ from GGA calculations (LDA: $-243$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$). For the competing formation of $IrP_2$ and $IrTe_2$ a lower gain in energy of $-202$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$ ($-215$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$) is calculated [Equation (2)]. The obtained values coincide well with thermodynamic data discussed later (Table 2). From the small difference in energy of $-15$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$ ($-28$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$) between IrPTe and $IrP_2 + IrTe_2$ an equilibrium state can be expected at high temperatures. Respective intermediate compositions for the formation of $IrP_2$ besides Ir, and P or $IrTe_2$ besides Ir and Te, according to Equation (4) and Equation (5) are less preferred.

$$\text{Ir} + \text{P} + \text{Te} \rightarrow \text{½IrP}_2 + \text{½IrTe}_2 + 202\ (215)\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1} \tag{2}$$

$$\text{½IrP}_2 + \text{½IrTe}_2 \rightarrow \text{IrPTe} + 15\ (28)\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1} \tag{3}$$

$$\text{Ir} + \text{P} + \text{Te} \rightarrow \text{½IrP}_2 + \text{½Ir} + \text{Te} + 133\ (143)\ \text{kJ/mol} \tag{4}$$

The existence of polymorphic IrPTe was also probed from DFT modelling. Several IrPTe structures were computed by optimization of starting $AXY$ models and fits to the Birch-Murnaghan equation of state (EOS).[22] Similar to $o$-IrPTe they can be obtained by anionic coloring from parent $c$-$FeS_2$ and $o$-$FeS_2$ type superstructures.[4] This is visualized from a crystallographic point of view by group-subgroup relations in Figure 2a. The ullmanite (NiSbS, $P2_13$), cobaltite (CoAsS, $Pca2_1$), and trigonal PtSnS ($R3$) type structures are directly derived from $c$-$FeS_2$. For IrPTe they are predicted as low-volume structures in the energy diagram (Figure 2b) with a preference of the cubic NiSbS type ($c$-IrPTe). Coloring of $o$-$FeS_2$ results in the CoSbS-i structure (costibite, $Pn2_1/m$) that is disfavored for IrPTe. Onoratos model structure ($P2_1/c$)[9] with homoatomic $Te_2$ and $P_2$ anions in IrPTe is clearly ruled out ($+60$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$). Estimated paracostibite type $o$-IrPTe is predicted little less stable ($0.5$ kJ$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$) than $c$-IrPTe. This stability order is confirmed from both LDA and GGA calculations despite of the typical systematic deviations for predicted cell volumes for both functional (see, for example reference[33]). Within the error of the present calculations $o$-IrPTe and $c$-IrPTe are estimated to be equal in energy. According to EOS fits of LDA results $o$-IrPTe is predicted to transform to $c$-IrPTe being a high pressure polymorph at $p > 13$ kbar (GGA: $> 20$ kbar).

### Experimental Analysis of Phase Formation

Next, the preparation of IrPTe is studied from experiment with respect to possible intermediates during formation or the occurrence of metastable structures. The previous estimations have shown that the intended product IrPTe is stable compared to the elements and binaries. For the synthesis at finite temperature additional suggestions and different stages of phase formation from the initial mixture of the elements (Ir + Te + P) are taken into account from CalPhad calculations:

As both, phosphorus and tellurium are volatile components, they are prone to sublime before any phase formation occurs. Thus phosphorus already starts to sublime below 300 °C while tellurium shows a significant sublimation rate above 500 °C. These processes should take place in a first stage during heating up to 700 °C (stage I, Figure 3).

If the vapor during sublimation is enclosed in an ampoule, subsequently different reactions can start under phase formation of both binary phases ($IrP_2$, $IrTe_2$) and both the ternary target IrPTe (stage II, Figure 3). Thermodynamic calculations indicate a decomposition of intermediately formed $IrTe_2$ under release of $Te_2(g)$ starting from 800 °C. The third stage of possible phase formation processes is concerned with the completion of formation of IrPTe (III, Figure 3). This stage is restricted in temperature by the thermal decomposition of IrPTe starting above 1000 °C.

The formation of IrPTe was studied by in-situ analysis using the equipment of the high-temperature gas-balance (HTGB). It has currently been established as a new method for investigations of reversible, heterogeneous phase equilibria with volatile components.$^{[20,21]}$ By using the HTGB, the vapor phase is enclosed in a silica ampoule and thus forms an equilibrium gas phase in permanent contact with the solid state. The measurement signal $\Delta m_{meas}$ is caused by change of the leverage force of the horizontal balance beam during evaporation and condensation. If the composition of the gas phase is well known (see thermodynamic modelling), the vapor pressure can be deduced using the ideal gas law.$^{[20]}$

If the initial mixture of iridium, phosphorus, and tellurium is investigated in a dynamic screening experiment using the HTGB, characteristic curves representing the predicted stages can be observed: Starting from about 300 °C the sublimation of phosphorus is detected up to pressures of more than 2 bar. Thereby, the slope of curve follows the calculated equilibrium pressure of red phosphorus up to 450 °C. Due to the melting of tellurium above this temperature, phosphorus can be dissolved in a binary melt and the vapor pressure is lowered compared to the one of pure phosphorus (Figure 4). ### Crystal and Electronic Structure of $o$-IrPTe

Finally one can ask for the reasons of the stability of the $o$-
IrPTe structure that is also preferred by PtSiTe.$^{[4]}$ As outstand-
ing structural feature of the calculated $o$-IrPTe structure (Fig-
ure 7a) a distortion of the Ir substructure is found along $c$ and
$a$ but not along the $b$ axis (Figure 1b). The distortion is related
to corner-sharing polyhedra $[IrP_3Te_3]$ that are not found in
$c$-IrPTe. The reason therefore is the relative orientation of the
P–Te units. As shown in Figure 1a and Figure 2b, a pyrite re-
lated supercell is needed to represent the anionic coloring of
the paracostibite structure. The doubling of the $c$ axis is needed
for the found variation of the [PTe] dumbbells along $c$. As a
consequence, it shows characteristics of both $c$- and $o$-$FeS_2$. In
the pyrite types four dumbbells in the octahedral holes of the
Ir site fcc lattice point along the different space diagonals. In
marcasite, they are aligned in parallel planes. The mixture in
$o$-IrPTe causes partial parallel alignment of the dumbbells and
edge sharing polyhedra (Figure 7b), whereas all polyhedra in
$c$-IrPTe are interlinked by corners. The primary atomic dis-
tances Ir–P, Ir–Te, and P–Te are longer in $o$-IrPTe than in
$c$-IrPTe (Table 1). Only the Ir–Ir distances in edge shared poly-
hedra of $o$-IrPTe become shorter $(3.65\ \mathring{A})$ than in $c$-IrPTe
$(4.15\ \mathring{A})$.

In line with isoelectronic pyrite $c$-IrPTe and $o$-IrPTe are predicted as semiconductors from electronic structure calculations. An indirect bandgap of 1.0 eV is found for the cubic polymorph, whereas $o$-IrPTe exhibits a smaller but direct bandgap of 0.5 eV. This gives evidence for Ir–Ir interactions along the shared polyhedra that might also cause a prominent DOS maximum below $E_{\text{F}}$ that is not found for $c$-IrPTe. The respective atomic site projected density of states (PDOS) is presented in Figure 8. Due to the octahedra like coordination [IrP₃Te₃] the Ir-5d-orbitals split to $e_{g}$ and $t_{2g}$ like states. The $t_{2g}$ states are occupied, in line with a Ir(+III) (5d⁶) state and attributed to maxima below the Fermi energy. The Ir-$e_{g}$ orbitals split to bonding (–4 to –7 eV) and unoccupied antibonding states (+1 to +4 eV). A formal charge of –3 is thus attributed to the P–Te dumbbells (isoelectronic to [S₂]²⁻). Bonding in the dumbbells is mirrored by the PDOS of P and Te. Therein, the s orbitals form $\sigma_{\text{s}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{s}}^{*}$ bands at –13 and –10.5 eV, respectively. The p orbitals form $\sigma_{\text{p}}$ and $\pi_{\text{p}}$ bonds in a broad range from –7 eV to $E_{\text{F}}$ (bonding) and from +0.5 eV to +4 eV (antibonding). Tiny antibonding contributions of P-3s and Te-6s valence orbitals indicate s-p-hybridization due to bonding in the fourfold coordination spheres ([PTelr₃] and [TeIr₃].

## Conclusions

The formation, crystal structure, and phase stability of IrPTe was studied from experimental and theoretical methods. The formation of IrPTe from the elements was investigated in a high temperature gas balance signaling IrP₂ and IrTe₂ as intermediates. Thermodynamic data and CalPhaD modelling confirm formation equilibria and formation reaction pathways. As IrTe₂ decomposes, IrP₂ reacts with Ir and Te to IrPTe at 950 °C that decomposes again at slightly higher temperatures (1000 °C).

The found pathway is in agreement with DFT total energy calculations. Thus, the formation of IrPTe is favored with respect to Ir, P and Te as starting materials in synthesis. It is also preferred to IrP₂ and IrTe₂ that occur as intermediates on the formation path. The observed decomposition of IrPTe at $T > 1000$ °C is in line with the formation of an equilibrium that is shifted according to Le Chatelier by the release of gaseous Te₂. DFT modelling of various related structure types based on anionic site coloring in superstructures gives evidence for polymorphism of IrPTe. A cubic form ($c$-IrPTe) is predicted as stable as the experimentally found orthorhombic $o$-IrPTe and should be obtained at high pressures of 20 kbar. Within the applied structure types, only those with heteroatomic dumbbells are stable compared to IrP₂ + IrTe₂. Finally, detailed crystal data could be obtained for the experimentally found CoSbS type ($Pbca$) structure of $o$-IrPTe from DFT optimizations. Atomic distances and semiconducting properties ($\Delta E = 0.5$ eV) are in line with the presence of Ir(+III) (5d⁶) and [PTe]³⁻ anions. The preference of this structure type is related to the formation of edge sharing coordination polyhedra [IrP₃Te₃] that give evidence for Ir–Ir interactions.

Total Pressure Measurements: The total pressure measurements during synthesis of IrPTe were realized in an advanced High temperature gas balance.$^{[18]}$ By using the high temperature gas balance (HTGB), the vapor phase was enclosed in a silica ampoule and thus formed an equilibrium gas phase in permanent contact with the solid phase. The measurement signal was caused by change of the leverage of the horizontal balance support during evaporation and condensation. The application of the HTGB allowed the analysis of solid-gas equilibria in the working range from 0.01 till 15 bar at temperatures up to 1100 °C. The current measurements were carried out with an overall weight of 750 mg of elemental starting material in evacuated silica ampoules ($V = 16\ \text{cm}^3$), applying a heating rate of $10\ \text{K·h}^{-1}$, while heating up to 950 °C and a cooling rate of $10\ \text{K·h}^{-1}$ down to ambient temperature.

Syntheses: IrPTe was synthesized from well-mixed stoichiometric amounts of iridium powder (freshly filed from a rod, ChemPur, 99.9 %), red amorphous phosphorus (Aldrich, 99 %, purified according to [34]) and tellurium (Acros, 99.8 %, distilled and reduced in a hydrogen gas flow) in evacuated and under dynamic vacuum enclosed silica ampoules ($p \leq 10^{-5}\ \text{bar}$, $V \approx 16\ \text{mL}$). Since some starting materials are sensitive to air and humidity, all reagents were handled in an argon-filled glove box [$p(\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{O}_2)/p_0 < 1\ \text{ppm}$, Fa. M. Braun, LAB 130]. Prior to use, the adherent moisture in silica ampoules was removed by heating under dynamic vacuum.

Samples of different phase identity were obtained by heating the starting materials with a rate of $10\ \text{K·h}^{-1}$ from room temperature to 450, 750, 900, 1000, and 1050 °C, respectively. After 72 h the silica ampoules were quenched to room temperature by water.

Phase Identification: Phase identification of products was realized by powder XRD. Patterns of synthesized compounds and mixtures were collected with a SIEMENS D5000 diffractometer using Cu-$K_\alpha$ radiation (reflexion mode) with an acceleration voltage of 40 kV and a filament current of 25 mA. The diffractometric scans were obtained between 5 and $110^\circ2\Theta$ at a scan speed of $0.875\ \text{deg·min}^{-1}$.

Thermodynamic Modelling: Thermodynamic modelling of phase equilibria were accomplished using CalPhaD-methods based on the Eriksson Gibss energy minimizer implemented in the program Tragmin.$^{[30]}$ The solid state-gas phase equilibria in the ternary system Ir/P/Te were calculated for different compositions isothermally in the temperature range 600–1300 K with $\Delta T = 20\ \text{K}$. The data sets of all gaseous species containing the components P und Te were adopted from the literature.$^{[31]}$ Due to lack of thermodynamic values of $\text{IrP}_2$ their estimation was realized based on thermodynamic data of related compounds of osmium and iridium: $\text{OsP}_2$, $\text{OsO}_2$, and $\text{OsS}_2$ compared to $\text{IrO}_2$ and $\text{IrS}_2$. Assuming linearity of standard enthalpies: $\text{IrS}_2$ ($-133.1\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$) vs. $\text{OsS}_2$ ($-147.7\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$), $\text{IrO}_2$ ($-249.4\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$) vs. $\text{OsO}_2$ ($-295\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$) the respective value was estimated for $\text{IrP}_2$ ($-133.3\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$) vs. $\text{OsP}_2$ ($-150\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$). Entropy and heat capacity were calculated applying the rule of Neuman and Kopp ($\Delta_\text{r}S$, $\Delta_\text{r}Cp \approx 0$). Starting from standard values of the binaries $\text{IrTe}_2$ and $\text{IrP}_2$ (Table 2) in the formation reaction $\text{IrTe}_2 + \text{IrP}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{IrPTe}$ the thermodynamic behavior of IrPTe was calculated. Data were finally optimized by adapting the thermal decomposition process, as was measured in thermogravimetric analysis:

$\Delta_{\text{r}}H = -60\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$ $\Delta_{\text{r}}S$, $\Delta_{\text{r}}C_{\text{p}} = 0\ \text{J·mol}^{-1}·\text{K}^{-1}$ resulting in:

$\Delta H^{0}_{298}(\text{IrPTe}) = -132.1\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$

$S^{0}_{298}(\text{IrPTe}) = 102.7\ \text{J·mol}^{-1}·\text{K}^{-1}$

$C_{\text{p}}(\text{IrPTe}) = (58.75 + 44.02 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot T^{-1})\ \text{J·mol}^{-1}·\text{K}^{-1}$

Thermogravimetric Measurements: Thermogravimetric measurements were performed in an argon atmosphere (5.0) with a Netzsch STA 449C apparatus situated in an argon-filled glove box. To investigate of the maximal thermal decomposition of IrPTe, the temperature was increased from $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to $1500\ ^\circ\text{C}$ with a heating rate of $10\ \text{K·min}^{-1}$ and cooled with the same rate.
