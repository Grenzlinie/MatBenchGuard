## ABSTRACT

The structural stability, mechanical properties and thermodynamic parameters such as Debye temperature, minimum thermal conductivities of orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ ($A$=C, Si and Ge) are calculated by first principles calculations based on density functional theory. The calculated lattice parameters, elastic constants of $Si_2N_2O$ and $Ge_2N_2O$ using PBEsol function are consisted with the experimental data and other calculated values. The full set elastic constants of the orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ ($A$=C, Si and Ge) are calculated by stress-strain method. The mechanical moduli (bulk modulus, shear modulus and Young's modulus) are evaluated by the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approach. The orthorhombic-$C_2N_2O$ exhibits larger mechanical moduli than the other two structures. The hardness of orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ ($A$=C, Si and Ge) is evaluated according to the intrinsic hardness calculation theory of covalent crystal relying on Mulliken overlap population. The results indicate that the orthorhombic-$C_2N_2O$ is a super hard material. Furthermore, the mechanical anisotropy, Debye temperature and minimum thermal conductivity of the orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ ($A$=C, Si and Ge) have been estimated by empirical methods. The orthorhombic-$Ge_2N_2O$ shows the lowest thermal conductivity, which may have useful applications as gas turbine engines and diesel engines.

## 1. Introduction

Silicon oxynitride ($Si_2N_2O$) is a non-toxic material consisting of three elements, i.e. Si, N and O, the most abundant elements in the earth's crust. Germanium nitride oxide ($Ge_2N_2O$) has same structure as silicon oxynitride in the experiments. $Si_2N_2O$ and $Ge_2N_2O$ are truly unique technical ceramic with superior chemical and oxidation resistance in many high temperature environments [1,2]. Reported applications contain cutting tools, wear parts and heat engine components. Silicon dioxide, silicon nitride and silicon oxynitride have some special significance in fiber optics communication technologies and in micro-electronic devices [3,4]. They also play an important role in the electronics industry, especially in Si-based large-scale integration circuit technology [5] and possibly in optoelectronics [6].

The $Si_2N_2O$ is a mineral also called sinoite, identified by Andersen et al. [7] and Keil and Andersen [8,9]. The structure and compressibility of $Si_2N_2O$ have been studied in detail. The $Si_2N_2O$ adopts an orthorhombic structure with space group $Cmc2_1$ (No. 36) and is built of $SiN_3O$ tetrahedral linked with puckered hexagonal $Si_2N_2$ layers through O atoms [10,11].

Ternary Si-O-N and Ge-O-N compounds, part of the most important group of nonmetallic hard materials, have attracted considerable interest because of their potential excellent physical and chemical properties such as high mechanical strength, high thermal conductivity and excellent chemical inertness. However, the experimental and theoretical calculation results, few data about the hardness of Si-O-N and Ge-O-N compounds have been reported due to the fact that it is hard to synthesize the pure crystal structure and other physical properties.

The orthorhombic-$Si_2N_2O$ has been researched on ground state properties, electronic, optical and mechanical properties [12-17]. Recently, theoretical simulations and experiments on the stability phase of $Si_2N_2O$ indicated a series of phase transformations [18-20]. Kroll et al. [18] studied the phase transitions of $Si_2N_2O$ using density functional theory (DFT). They investigated two dense phases of $Si_2N_2O$, a defect spinel structure at the moderate pressure and a corundum-type structure at very high pressure [18]. Liu et al. [21] likewise studied the ground state, elastic stiffness and dielectric constants of the native point defect orthorhombic-$Si_2N_2O$. And Zhang and co-workers [22] calculated electronic structure, elastic and optical properties of the orthorhombic-$Si_2N_2O$ based on density-functional theory through the Cambridge Sequential Total Energy Package (CASTEP) in local density approximations (LDA) with the ultrasoft pseudopotential (USPP).

The crystal structure of the orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O $(Cmc2_1)$ has been researched using the time-of-flight of the neutron diffraction experiment [2,23]. The electronic structures of Si₂N₂O and Ge₂N₂O crystals were calculated [24]. Computational technique based on density functional theory is a powerful tool to explore the phase stability and mechanical properties of materials. In this paper, we further study the thermodynamic and mechanical stability, mechanical properties, mechanical anisotropy, intrinsic hardness, Debye temperature and minimum thermal conductivity of the orthorhombic-A₂N₂O (A=C, Si and Ge) compounds.

## 2. Calculation methods and details

The first principles calculations based on density functional theory were conducted in CASTEP code [26]. The plane wave basis set was used for the energy calculations. The kinetic energy cutoff value for plane wave expansions in reciprocal space was set to 380 eV. The frozen core approximation was used for the pseudo-atoms. The ultra-soft pseudopotentials (USPP) of Vanderbilt scheme were used to account the electrostatic interaction between valence electrons and ionic core [27]. For the elements of C, Si, Ge, N and O, the valence electron configurations were, respectively, $2s^22p^2$ (C), $3s^23p^2$ (Si), $4s^24p^2$ (Ge), $2s^22p^3$ (N) and $2s^22p^4$ (O). The energy integrations were performed on a discretized $\boldsymbol{k}$ grid in the first irreducible Brillouin zone, and we used $10 \times 10 \times 10 \boldsymbol{k}^3$ point meshes for the orthorhombic-A₂N₂O (A=C, Si and Ge) compounds. The method proposed by Monkhorst-Pack was used for generating the $\boldsymbol{k}$ points in this paper [28]. The Pulay density mixing scheme was applied to the electronic energy minimization. For the convergence, the change of total energy was set to $1 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/cell; the mean Hellmann-Feymann force acting on the atom was reduced to 0.01 eV/Å. We have considered LDA and two GGA level exchange-correlation functional in this work, called the local density approximation-Ceperley-Alder-Perdew-Zunger (LDA-CA-PZ) [29], Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [30], and PBE for solids (PBEsol) [31]. PBEsol function was a modification of the PBE function, and the main motivation was to obtain a better description of equilibrium lattice properties than original PBE function.

We adopted the stress-strain method [32,33] to evaluate the full set elastic constants of orthorhombic-A₂N₂O (A=C, Si and Ge) compounds. The fundamental principle is Hooker's law, which indicates the linear relation between Lagrangian strain and Cauchy stress tensor. The exact formula is given as

$$
\sigma_{ij}=C_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{kl} \tag{1}
$$

where $\sigma_{ij}$ is the stress tensor; $\varepsilon_{kl}$ is the Lagrangian strain tensor, and $C_{ijkl}$ is the elastic constant tensor, which is a $6 \times 6$ matrix (36 elements in general cases). The independent elements of elastic constant tensor depend on the crystal symmetry. For orthorhombic crystal class, we can replace Eq. (1) with Eq. (2), where the Voigt notations and the definitions for the engineering stress and strain are used

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\sigma_1 \\
\sigma_2 \\
\sigma_3 \\
\tau_4 \\
\tau_5 \\
\tau_6
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
C_{11} & C_{12} & C_{13} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
C_{12} & C_{22} & C_{12} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
C_{13} & C_{12} & C_{33} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & C_{44} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & C_{55} & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & C_{66}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\varepsilon_1 \\
\varepsilon_2 \\
\varepsilon_3 \\
\gamma_4 \\
\gamma_5 \\
\gamma_6
\end{bmatrix}
\tag{2}
$$

here $\sigma_i$ and $\tau_i$ are normal stress and shearing stress; similarly $\varepsilon_i$ and $\gamma_i$ are the corresponding normal strain and shearing strain, respectively.

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Thermodynamic stability and lattice parameters

The cohesive energies $(E_c)$ and formation enthalpies $(\Delta H)$ of orthorhombic-A₂N₂O compounds (A=C, Si and Ge) are calculated through the following expressions [34]:

$$
E_{coh}(\text{A}_2\text{N}_2\text{O})=\frac{1}{5}\big[E_{tot}(\text{A}_2\text{N}_2\text{O})-2E_{ios}(\text{A})-2E_{ios}(\text{N})-E_{ios}(\text{O})\big] \tag{3}
$$

$$
\Delta H(\text{A}_2\text{N}_2\text{O})=\frac{1}{5}\bigg[E_{tot}(\text{A}_2\text{N}_2\text{O})-E^{bulk}(\text{A})-E(\text{N}_2)-\frac{1}{2}E(\text{O}_2)\bigg] \tag{4}
$$

where $E(\text{A})$, $E(\text{N})$ and $E(\text{O})$ are the total energies of the isolated C, Si, Ge, N and O atoms, respectively. $E_{tot}(\text{A}_2\text{N}_2\text{O})$, $E_{bulk}(\text{A})$ are the total energies in the solid state. $E(\text{N}_2)$ and $E(\text{O}_2)$ are the total energies of unit molecule. $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ molecular are built with space group P1 with lattice constant $10$ Å. The cohesive energies $(E_c)$ of pure elements were calculated using their ground state primitive cell for C, Si and Ge. The total energies of $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ molecular was calculated by the same method and found the energies of isolated atoms. In addition, the spin polarized calculated was performed for these atoms.

PBE function is good for reaction energies like transition energy, cohesive energy, etc., but the lattice constants are over-estimated for many crystal structures. On the other hand, PBEsol function recovers the second order gradient expansion for small reduced density gradient [35], and which can predict the lattice constants better than PBE function. In practical calculations, the results of PBEsol are usually situated between LDA and PBE [35].

The stability of crystal is determined by its cohesive energy. Generally, the cohesive energy can be defined as released energy when isolated atoms combined into crystal [34]. Hence, the larger absolute value, the more stable the crystal structure [36].

Lattice dynamical properties were obtained from the linear response method using PBE for solids (PBEsol) function with the density functional theory (DFT) [26]. Unlike the previous calculations of phonons, we have made using the norm-conserving pseudopotentials (NCPP) [37] as implemented in this code. The properties of phonons modes can be directly evaluated. Moreover, the calculated phonon spectrums are displayed in Fig. 2 for the orthorhombic-\(A_2N_2O\) (A=C, Si and Ge). It is clear that no imaginary phonon frequency exists in the whole Brillouin zone (BZ), indicating the thermodynamic stability of the orthorhombic-\(A_2N_2O\) (A=C, Si and Ge). The dynamical stability of the orthorhombic-\(Si_2N_2O\) and orthorhombic-\(Ge_2N_2O\) is in good agreement with previously reported conclusion by Said et al. using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) [25]. Experimentally, the orthorhombic-\(Si_2N_2O\) and orthorhombic-\(Ge_2N_2O\) have stable phase and have been widely studied. Therefore, we expect that all the orthorhombic-\(A_2N_2O\) (A=C, Si and Ge) including a new compound \(C_2N_2O\) can be realized experimentally in future.

The calculated lattice parameters of the orthorhombic-\(A_2N_2O\) (A=C, Si and Ge) compounds are shown in Table 1. The lattice constants predicted by PBEsol method are in good agreement with other available experimental data, indicating the reliability of the results obtained from the present density functional theory approaches. In this work, the lattice constants using PBEsol are situated between LDA and PBE. This work supports the other practical calculations that calculated lattice constants of PBEsol are situated between PBE and LDA [35,38].

The optimized lattice constants by GGA method are larger than experimental results and the optimized lattice constants by LDA method are smaller than experimental results of the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O. We also notice that the lattice constants reported in Ref. [39] are larger than those in Ref. [10]. However, the obtained density is 2.52 g/cm³ in Ref. 39, which is smaller than that in Ref. [10]. In Ref. [18], both GGA and LDA methods are applied to optimize the lattice constants of the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O. The predicted lattice constants by LDA method are very close to the reported values in Ref. [18] and are better than lattice constants by GGA method.

The optimized lattice constants by GGA method of the orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O are slightly larger than the experimental results, but the optimized lattice constants by LDA method are also smaller than experimental [2,10,23] and the other predicted results [24,25]. Therefore, the calculated cell volume per formula unit (V) by GGA method of the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O and orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O is larger than the experimental results, and the cell volume per formula unit (V) of LDA method is smaller than experimental results [2,10,23].

The band gap $(E_g)$ of the orthorhombic-A₂N₂O (A=C, Si and Ge) is calculated. We find that our calculated band gap of the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O is smaller than experimental result [14]. The experimental band gap of the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O is direct energy gap about 5.2–5.7 eV [14]. The orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O is indirect band gap of 5.97 eV and the orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O is direct band gap of 3.78 eV by Ching et al. [24]. In this work and the other Refs. [14,16,22,25], the calculated and experimental results show that the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O is direct band gap and the orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O is indirect band gap. We found wrong results that indicate the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O is indirect band gap and the orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O is direct band gap by Ching et al. [24]. Our calculated band gap (2.74 eV) by PBEsol method agrees well with previously reported value (2.65 eV) by Said et al. using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) [25] of the orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O.

The obtained elastic constants can be used to estimate the mechanical stability of the crystal structure through Born–Huang lattice dynamical criterion. In this paper, the mechanical stability and mechanical moduli of the orthorhombic-A₂N₂O compounds are approximated by the corresponding relationships for orthorhombic crystal class [33,40,41]. C_{11}>0, C_{22}>0, & C_{33}>0, C_{44}>0, C_{55}>0, C_{66}>0, \\

\end{aligned} \tag{5}

### 3.2.2. Mechanical parameters for polycrystalline aggregates

From the calculated elastic constants, other mechanical parameters for polycrystalline aggregate such as bulk modulus ($B$), shear modulus ($G$), Young's modulus ($E$) and Poisson's ratio ($v$) can be derived using Voigt-Reuss-Hill (VRH) approximation [42-44]. The voigt's and Reuss's schemes represent the upper and lower bounds to the elastic modulus, respectively. For orthorhombic crystals, the bulk modulus ($B$) and shear modulus ($G$) according to Voigt and Reuss approximations are defined as

$$
B_{V}=\frac{1}{9}\left(C_{11}+C_{22}+C_{33}\right)+\frac{2}{9}\left(C_{12}+C_{13}+C_{23}\right) \tag{6}
$$

$$
G_{V}=\frac{1}{15}\left(C_{11}+C_{22}+C_{33}\right)-\frac{1}{15}\left(C_{12}+C_{13}+C_{23}\right)+\frac{1}{5}\left(C_{44}+C_{55}+C_{66}\right) \tag{7}
$$

$$
B_{R}=\frac{1}{\left(S_{11}+S_{22}+S_{33}\right)+2\left(S_{12}+S_{13}+S_{23}\right)} \tag{8}
$$

$$
G_{R}=\frac{15}{4\left(S_{11}+S_{22}+S_{33}\right)-4\left(S_{12}+S_{13}+S_{23}\right)+3\left(S_{44}+S_{55}+S_{66}\right)} \tag{9}
$$

The Hill's averages are taken from the averages of the two [44]:

$$
B_{H}=\left(B_{V}+B_{R}\right) / 2 \tag{10}
$$

$$
G_{H}=\left(G_{V}+G_{R}\right) / 2 \tag{11}
$$

Then the Young's modulus ($E$) and Poisson's ratio ($v$) are calculated by the following formulas:

$$
E=9 B_{H} G_{H} /\left(3 B_{H}+G_{H}\right) \tag{12}
$$

$$
v=\left(3 B_{H}-2 G_{H}\right) /\left[2\left(3 B_{H}+G_{H}\right)\right] \tag{13}
$$

In the equations above, $C_{ij}$ denotes the elastic stiffness matrix and $S_{ij}$ is the elastic compliance matrix. $S_{ij}$ is the inverse matrix of $C_{ij}$ and can be calculated easily. Based on Eqs.(6)-(13), we derive the corresponding values, which are presented in Table 3. It is also noted that the studied Poisson's ratios of the A₂N₂O (A=C, Si and Ge) com- pounds are very small. The Poisson's ratio (0.192 and 0.228) of Ge₂N₂O is relatively small. The Poisson's ratio (0.257 and 0.243) of Si₂N₂O is the largest. The Poisson's ratio of the orthorhombic- Si₂N₂O and orthorhombic-Ge₂N₂O is actually close to the values of covalent-ionic crystals.

### 3.2.3. Brittleness and ductility
The ratio $B/G$ reflects the brittleness or ductility of a material, and the critical value is near 1.75 [45]. Below this value, the material is brittle; otherwise, it is ductile. The $B/G$ ratio for the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O is slightly smaller than 1.75. In the case of the orthorhombic-Si₂N₂O, the obtained $B/G$ ratio is pretty close to the critical value. The calculated $B/G$ values are 1.13 and 1.04 for C₂N₂O and 1.30 and 1.51 for Ge₂N₂O. It suggests that C₂N₂O and Ge₂N₂O show stronger brittleness than the Si₂N₂O. The calculated $B/G$ values are 1.73 and 1.61 using GGA-PBEsol and LDA-CA-PZ, which are in good agreement with 1.65 by Zhang et al. [22].

### 3.3. Mechanical anisotropy

The anisotropy of the mechanical properties of a crystal structure can be characterized by many different ways, i.e. the universal anisotropic index $(A^{U})$ [50], the percent anisotropy ($A_G$ and $A_B$ for shear and bulk moduli, respectively) [33,41] and shear anisotropic factors ($A_1$, $A_2$ and $A_3$) [33,41]. Similar to elastic tensor, most anisotropic indexes are also symmetry independently. For more details, one may refer to Ref. 51 where the anisotropic factors have been derived for most crystal classes. In contrast to many anisotropic indexes or factors, the universal anisotropic index and percent anisotropy can be applied to any of these crystal structures. The equations used for calculations can be written as

$$
A^{U}=5 \frac{G_{V}}{G_{R}}+\frac{B_{V}}{B_{R}}-6 \geq 0 \tag{18}
$$

$$
A_{B}=\frac{B_{V}-B_{R}}{B_{V}+B_{R}} \tag{19}
$$

$$
A_{G}=\frac{G_{V}-G_{R}}{G_{V}+G_{R}} \tag{20}
$$

The shear anisotropic factor for the $\{100\}$ shear planes between the $\langle 011\rangle$ and $\langle 010\rangle$ directions is

$$
A_{1}=\frac{4 C_{44}}{C_{11}+C_{33}-2 C_{13}} \tag{21}
$$

For the $\{010\}$ shear planes between $\langle 101\rangle$ and $\langle 001\rangle$ directions it is

$$
A_{2}=\frac{4 C_{55}}{C_{22}+C_{33}-2 C_{23}} \tag{22}
$$

and for the $\{001\}$ shear planes between $\langle 110\rangle$ and $\langle 010\rangle$ directions which is

$$
A_{3}=\frac{4 C_{66}}{C_{11}+C_{22}-2 C_{12}} \tag{23}
$$

In Table 5, we show the values of some anisotropic parameters mentioned above. For an isotropic structure, we expect that $A^{U}=0$, $A_G=0$ and $A_B=0$; otherwise, $A_1$, $A_2$ and $A_3$ should be a unity.

The anisotropic index, percent anisotropy and shear anisotropic factors are shown in Table 5. The results indicate that the orthorhombic-$C_2N_2O$ and orthorhombic-$Ge_2N_2O$ show stronger mechanical anisotropy than the orthorhombic-$Si_2N_2O$. The calculated $A^{U}$ (0.91) and $A_G$ (8.35%) of the orthorhombic-$Ge_2N_2O$ are larger than those values of other two structures. The calculated $A_B$ (1.42%) of the orthorhombic-$C_2N_2O$ is significantly larger than those values of other two structures. Furthermore, we find that the calculated $A_G$ is larger than $A_B$ for these three $A_2N_2O$ structures, implying that the shear modulus shows stronger direction-independence than bulk modulus.

Here, we plot the bulk modulus (B) and Young's modulus (E) in different directions using spherical coordinates. For orthorhombic crystal class, the directional independence of bulk modulus (B) or Young's modulus (E) can be written as [33,41]

### 3.5. Debye temperature and thermal conductivity
Mechanical properties can be related to thermodynamic parameter such as Debye temperature, specific heat, thermal expansion and melting point [33]. We have calculated the Debye temperature ($\Theta_D$) of $A_2N_2O$ (A=C, Si and Ge) by the relation between the mean sound velocity ($v_m$) and Debye temperature ($\Theta_D$) [52]:

$$\Theta_D = \frac{h}{k_B} \left[ \frac{3n}{4\pi} \left( \frac{N_A \rho}{M} \right) \right]^{\frac{1}{3}} v_m \tag{29}$$

$$v_m = \left[ \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{2}{v_s^3} + \frac{1}{v_l^3} \right) \right]^{-1/3} \tag{30}$$

$$v_l = \sqrt{\frac{B+\left( \frac{4}{3} \right)G}{\rho}} \tag{31}$$

$$v_t = \sqrt{\frac{G}{\rho}} \tag{32}$$

here $\Theta_D$ is the Debye temperature; $h$ is the Planck's constant; $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant; $N_A$ is the Avagadro's constant; $n$ is the number of atoms per formula; $M$ is the molecular weight; $v_l$ and $v_t$ are the longitudinal and shear velocities, respectively; $\rho$ is the theoretical density of the compound; $B$ and $G$ are bulk modulus and shear modulus, respectively.

Thermal conductivity can be either calculated by Young's modulus ($E$) or estimated from sound velocity. Two empirical methods have been widely adopted to compute the thermal conductivity, named Clarke's and Cahill's method, and they are given below [53-56]:

$$\kappa_{min}^{Clarke} = 0.87 k_B \overline{M}_a^{-2/3} E^{1/2} p^{1/6} \tag{33}$$

$$\overline{M}_a = [m/(nN_A)] \tag{34}$$

$$\kappa_{min}^{Cahill} = \frac{k_B}{2.48} p^{2/3} (v_l + 2v_t) \tag{35}$$

here $\kappa$ is the thermal conductivity. In Eq. (33), $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant; $E$ is the Young's modulus, $p$ is the number of density of atoms, and which is calculated as $N/V$. Here, $N$ is the total number of atoms in the cell and $V$ is cell volume; $n$ is total number of atoms per $A_2N_2O$ (A=C, Si and Ge) molecule; $m$ is the molar mass; the explanations for other parameters, we can refer to the previous sections. We should note that both methods give the lower bound of thermal conductivity.

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, based on density functional theory, we have systematically study the stability, mechanical properties, mechanical anisotropic, linear compressibility, Debye temperature and minimum thermal conductivity of the orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ (A=C, Si and Ge). All the orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ (A=C, Si and Ge) are thermodynamically and mechanically stable. The predicted lattice parameters, mechanical moduli and hardness for them are in good agreement with the available experimental results. The full set elastic constants are calculated by the stress-strain method in the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation. The mechanical moduli are also discussed. The calculated mechanical moduli, i.e. bulk modulus, shear modulus and Young's modulus, indicate that orthorhombic-$C_2N_2O$ is superior to $Si_2N_2O$ and $Ge_2N_2O$. The anisotropy of mechanical moduli is discussed. For the orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ (A=C, Si and Ge), the bulk modulus show very weak anisotropic property, but the anisotropy of Young's modulus is strong. The Vickers hardness of the orthorhombic-$A_2N_2O$ (A=C, Si and Ge) was estimated using some semi-empirical methods. The results imply that the orthorhombic-$C_2N_2O$ is a superhard material, but the orthorhombic-$Si_2N_2O$ and orthorhombic-$Ge_2N_2O$ have small hardness. The orthorhombic-$Ge_2N_2O$ shows the lowest thermal conductivity, which may have useful applications as gas turbine engines and diesel engines. The results provide a fundamental understanding on these materials and offer referent data to further investigation or applications of these ternary C-O-N, Si-O-N, and Ge-O-N ceramic compounds.
