## ABSTRACT

First principles calculation reveals that ideal mechanical strengths of $L1_2$ $Ir_3$Nb are higher than those of $L1_2$ $Ir_3$Hf, and that Hf and Nb atoms have different effects on the brittle behavior of Ir, i.e., the $Ir_3$Hf bulk seems less brittle than $fcc$ Ir, while $Ir_3$Nb becomes more brittle. Densities of states suggest that the chemical bonding formed in $Ir_3$Nb should be stronger and more directional than that in $Ir_3$Hf. Calculation also shows that compared with pure Ir bulk, the bond strengths of (1 0 0)-I and (1 1 0) $Ir/Ir_3$X (X=Hf, Nb) interfaces have increased, while the bond strengths of (1 0 0)-II and (1 1 1) $Ir/Ir_3$X interfaces have decreased, due to the formation of the interface. In addition, it is found out that the (1 0 0)-I interfaces are energetically favorable with negative interface energies, which is good agreement with similar experimental observations in the literature.

## 1. Introduction

The metal iridium has been widely regarded as an excellent candidate for high-temperature applications during the past decades, as it possesses unique and superior properties among all the metals, e.g., high melting temperature $(2443^{\circ}C)$ and high density $(22.65\,\text{kg/m}^3)$, the highest corrosion resistance and thermal stability at high temperature, and the highest shear modulus (220 GPa) at room temperature [1–4]. Since 1970s, scientists at Oak Ridge National Laboratory have successfully developed a series of Ir-0.3 wt% W alloys (so-called DOP alloy) for applications in space power systems [5,6]. In 1996, Yamabe et al. proposed a new class of refractory Ir-base superalloys for ultra-high temperature $(1800^{\circ}C)$ applications to replace the traditional Ni-base superalloys, which have limited temperature capabilities due to the rather low melting point of Ni $(1453^{\circ}C)$ [7–9]. Similar to Ni-base superalloys, the Ir-base superalloys have a two phase $fcc/L1_2$ structure, with the $L1_2$ $(Ir_3X)$ precipitates coherently embedded in the $fcc$ Ir matrix, and such a superalloy with $fcc/L1_2$ interfaces has been found to have higher strength than single $fcc$ or $L1_2$ phases, while the underlying mechanism of strengthening is unclear [10].

The ideal mechanical strength is the minimum stress needed to force deformation or fracture of a defect-free material and it has become the source of the scientific inquiry for decades [11–13]. To begin with, the ideal strength is commonly regarded as an upper bound to the strength of a real material. It is, therefore, of both scientific and engineering interests to find out such a limit for a certain material. Moreover, the ideal strength is one of the few mechanical properties of materials that can be computed from first principles. In addition, the ideal strength is an inherent material property. Understanding its mechanism and characteristics can help to identify the fundamental connection between the mechanical properties and intrinsic bonding of the crystal structure. Finally, the ideal strength is approached in situations that are technologically relevant, such as the nano-indentation of materials with low defect densities.

Very recently, first principles calculations were performed to derive the ideal mechanical strength of Ir and $Ir_3$Zr, as well as the cohesion properties of coherent $Ir/Ir_3$Zr interfaces [14,15]. As there are several kinds of Ir-base superalloys investigated so far in the literature, it is of both importance and interest to have a systematic and comparative study of these Ir-base superalloys at an electronic scale. The present study is therefore dedicated, through first principles calculation, to investigate the ideal tensile and shear strengths, the interface bonding, as well as the electronic structures of Ir-base superalloys by choosing $Ir/Ir_3$Hf and $Ir/Ir_3$Nb as sample systems, based on the following considerations. First, the $Ir_3$Hf and $Ir_3$Nb phases with the $L1_2$ structure have attracted much research attention both experimentally and theoretically due to their high melting points and high strengths, and the coherent structures of $Ir/Ir_3$Hf and $Ir/Ir_3$Nb have indeed been found experimentally [7,8,10,16–18]. Second, the experimental lattice constants of $L1_2$ $Ir_3$Hf and $Ir_3$Nb are 3.93 and $3.89\,\text{Å}$, respectively, which are very close to the lattice constant $(3.84\,\text{Å})$ of $fcc$ Ir [7]. Such a small lattice mismatch of the $Ir/Ir_3$Hf and $Ir/Ir_3$Nb interfaces seems good for the modeling of the interface. Third, the $Ir/Ir_3$Hf and $Ir/Ir_3$Nb super-

## 2. Computational methods

The first-principles calculation is based on the well-established Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) within the density functional theory [19]. The calculation is conducted in a plane-wave basis, using the projector-augmented wave method [20]. The exchange and correlation items are described by the generalized gradient approximation proposed by Perdew et al. [21], and the cutoff energies are 300 and 450 eV for plane wave basis and augmentation charge, respectively. In each calculation, periodic boundary conditions are added in three directions of the unit cell, and the conjugate gradient method is used for the relaxation of structural parameters. For the calculations of the bulk modulus, shear modulus, and elastic constants, the method described by Mehl et al. [22] is used and a gamma centered k-grid of $21 \times 21 \times 21$ is adopted for both $\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ bulks. For the ideal strengths under tension and shear, the stress-strain dependence at each step is calculated by incrementally deforming the unit cell in the direction of the applied strain and simultaneously relaxing both the atomic basis vectors orthogonal to the applied strain, as well as the atoms inside the unit cell. To ensure that the strain path is continuous, the starting position at each strain step is taken from the relaxed coordinates of the previous step [11-13].

The present interface calculation is focused on three coherent interface structures between $fcc$ Ir and $\text{L1}_2\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$ ($\text{X=Hf,Nb}$), i.e., $(100)\text{Ir}/(100)\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$, $(110)\text{Ir}/(110)\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$, and $(111)\text{Ir}/(111)\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$. Accordingly, a $2 \times 2$ surface unit cell with the optimized bulk lattice constant of Ir is selected, and a gamma centered k-grid is adopted, i.e., $11 \times 11 \times 1$ for $(100)$ and $(111)$ interface calculations, and $9 \times 11 \times 1$ for $(110)$ interface calculations, respectively. For each interface model, there are seven Ir layers and $(4+4)\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$ layers with 15 vacuum layers. It should be pointed out that the $\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$ layers are put at each side of the Ir layers and there are two $\text{Ir/Ir}_3\text{X}$ interfaces in each interface model. According to the atomic structure of the interface layer of $\text{L1}_2\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$, the three interface models are further classified into two or three interface configurations. As the two $\text{X}$ ($\text{X=Hf,Nb}$) atoms are located at every other layer of the $(100)$ and $(110)\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$ surfaces, the $(100)$ and $(110)$ models having the $\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$ interface layer with or without $\text{X}$ atoms are named types I and II, respectively. As each $(111)\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$ surface has one $\text{X}$ atom, the $(111)$ interface model could be classified into three configurations, i.e., ABCAB-CAB (I), ABCAB-BCA (II), and ABCAB-BCA (III), in which the first part stands for the stacking sequence of Ir and the second for that of $\text{Ir}_3\text{X}$.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Ideal mechanical strengths of $\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ bulks

We first investigate the structural stability of $\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ bulks. In the present study, first principles calculations are used to find out the correlation between the total energy and average atomic volume for the $\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ phases with several simple crystal structures, and the results show that the $\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ phases with the $\text{L1}_2$ structure has the lowest total energy among the calculated crystal structures. As a typical example, Fig. 1 shows the calculated total energy as a function of average atomic volume for the $\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ phase. From these total energies, one could deduce that the $\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ phases with the $\text{L1}_2$ structure would be most likely to be formed in experiments and would be relatively stable, which matches well with the experimental observations of the $\text{L1}_2$ structure in the literature [8,18]. In addition, the heats of formation of the $\text{L1}_2\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ phases from the present calculation are $-78.51$ and $-57.31$ kJ/mol, respectively, which are in good agreement with the corresponding values of $-70$ and $-52$ kJ/mol from the Miedema model [23]. It should be mentioned that the total energies of $fcc$ Ir, bcc Nb, and hcp Hf with optimized lattice constants are used in the above calculation of heats of formation. Such a negative heat of formation also suggests that the $\text{L1}_2\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ phases should be energetically favorable and also confirms the relatively stable feature of $\text{L1}_2\text{Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir}_3\text{Nb}$, which seems favorable to maintain the structural stability of $\text{Ir/Ir}_3\text{Hf}$ and $\text{Ir/Ir}_3\text{Nb}$ superalloys.

In addition, the ideal tensile and shear strengths of L1₂ $Ir_3Hf$ and $Ir_3Nb$ along various directions are derived and listed in Table 2. From these figures and Table 2, one can see several characteristics. First, the ideal tensile strengths for L1₂ $Ir_3Hf$ in the $\langle 111 \rangle$, $\langle 100 \rangle$, and $\langle 110 \rangle$ directions are calculated to be 31.82, 29.24, and 17.74 GPa, respectively, which are lower than the corresponding values of 35.95, 34.10, and 20.62 GPa for L1₂ $Ir_3Nb$, respectively. Second, the ideal shear strengths for L1₂ $Ir_3Hf$ in the (0 0 1) $\langle 110 \rangle$, (1 1 0) $\langle \bar{1}10 \rangle$, (1 1 1) $\langle \bar{1}10 \rangle$ and (1 1 1) $\langle \bar{2}11 \rangle$ slip systems are calculated to be 18.00, 14.63, 12.21, and 11.36 GPa, respectively, which are smaller than the corresponding values of 20.86, 16.47, 14.68, and 13.51 GPa for L1₂ $Ir_3Nb$, respectively. Third, it can be observed from Table 2 that the descending sequence of the ideal mechanical strengths for the L1₂ $Ir_3X$ phases is as follows: $Ir_3Nb \rightarrow Ir_3Hf \rightarrow Ir_3Zr$. In other words, the L1₂ $Ir_3Nb$ phase has higher mechanical strength than L1₂ $Ir_3Hf$ and $Ir_3Zr$, which confirms the results shown in Table 1 and is also in good agreement with similar experimental observations in the literature [25]. Forth, for all the three $Ir_3X$ phases, the ideal tensile strength in the $\langle 110 \rangle$ direction is the lowest among the three tensile directions, and the ideal shear strength in the (1 1 1) $\langle \bar{2}11 \rangle$ slip system is the smallest among the four slip systems, implying that the $\langle 110 \rangle$ should be the easiest tensile direction and (1 1 1) $\langle \bar{2}11 \rangle$ would be the easiest slip system.

### 3.2. Cohesion properties of Ir/Ir₃Hf and Ir/Ir₃Nb interfaces

It is commonly believed that the cohesion properties of the interface between Ir and Ir₃X play an important role in the performance of Ir-base superalloys. Regarding the Ir/Ir₃X interfaces, there are mainly three scientific issues to be clarified: (1) The bond strength between Ir and Ir₃X is high or not. In the present study, the bond strength of the Ir/Ir₃X interface is expressed by the work of separation ($W_{\text{sep}}$) which is the work required to reversibly separate the interface into two free surfaces [26]. (2) The Ir/Ir₃X interface is thermally stable or not. Interface energy is therefore used as a kind of measure of interface stability, i.e., a lower interface energy generally corresponds to a more stable interface. Thermodynamically, the interface energy could be regarded as the work required to create the interface from the bulk materials, and it is therefore a comparison of the interface bonding with the bonding in the bulk materials. (3) The effects of interface atomic structure and electronic structure on interface properties, which seem important to understand the physical origin of the strengthening effect of the Ir/Ir₃X interfaces mentioned before. Accordingly, first principles calculations are used, in the present study, to find out the bond strength, interface energy, and electronic structures of various coherent Ir/Ir₃X (X=Hf, Nb) interfaces.

After the interface calculation, the work of separation is first derived as follows:

$$
W_{\text{sep}} = \frac{0.5 \cdot (E_{\text{Ir}} + E_{\text{Ir}_3\text{X}} - E_{\text{tot}})}{A}, \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\text{tot}}$ and $A$ are total energy and surface area of the Ir/Ir₃X (X=Hf, Nb) interface, respectively, and $E_{\text{Ir}}$ and $E_{\text{Ir}_3\text{X}}$ are total energies of pure Ir and Ir₃X surface layers after the removal of the Ir₃X and Ir layers, respectively. It should be pointed out that the work of separation is the work required to reversibly separate the interface into two free surfaces and $W_{\text{sep}}$ is therefore a direct measure of the interface bond strength. Accordingly, the $W_{\text{sep}}$ values of various interfaces are obtained and listed in Table 3. In addition, the difference of work of separation ($\Delta W_{\text{sep}}$) between the Ir/Ir₃X interface and corresponding pure Ir bulk is also calculated and included in Table 3 for the sake of comparison. It could be seen from this table that the $\Delta W_{\text{sep}}$ values of the Ir/Ir₃X interfaces with the (1 0 0)-I and (1 1 0) orientations are all positive, while the $\Delta W_{\text{sep}}$ of (1 0 0)-II and (1 1 1) Ir/Ir₃X interfaces are all negative. Such a feature of the $\Delta W_{\text{sep}}$ values signifies that compared with pure Ir bulk, the bond strengths of the (1 0 0)-I and (1 1 0) Ir/Ir₃X interfaces have increased, while the bond strengths of the (1 0 0)-II and (1 1 1) Ir/Ir₃X interfaces have decreased, due to the formation of the interface. In addition, it can be also observed that the $\Delta W_{\text{sep}}$ values of (1 0 0)-I and (1 1 0)-I interfaces for Ir₃Nb are 0.67 and 0.65 J/m², respectively, which are higher than the corresponding values of 0.17 and 0.11 J/m² for Ir₃Hf, respectively, suggesting that the strengthening effect of Ir₃Nb should be bigger than that of Ir₃Hf in Ir-base superalloys.

To investigate the energetics of the Ir/Ir₃X interfaces, the interface energy ($E_{\text{int}}$) is then calculated through the following formula:

$$

First, the coherent interfaces with the (1 1 1) orientations have much bigger interface energies than corresponding interfaces with the (1 0 0) and (1 1 0) orientations. Such positive and higher interface energies suggest that the (1 1 1) Ir/Ir₃X (X=Hf, Nb) would be thermally unstable, which are compatible with the lower interface strength and negative $\Delta W_{\text{sep}}$ values shown in Table 3. Second, it could be observed that the (1 0 0)-I and (1 1 0) Ir/(1 1 0) Ir₃Nb-I interfaces possess negative interface energies, implying that these interface structures should be energetically favorable and would be more likely to be formed in actual Ir-base superalloys. It should be pointed out that such an observation of the (1 0 0) interface orientation is in good agreement with similar experimental results by Yamabe-Mitarai et al. [8]. Third, for both (1 0 0) and (1 1 0) orientations, it is of interest to see that the Ir/Ir₃Nb-I interface has lower interface energy than corresponding Ir/Ir₃Hf-I interface, while the Ir/Ir₃Nb-II interface possesses higher interface energy.

## 4. Concluding remarks
In summary, first principles calculations have been conducted
to derive the ideal tensile and shear strengths of $L1_2$ $Ir_3Nb$ and
$Ir_3Hf$, as well as the interface cohesion of $Ir/Ir_3Nb$ and $Ir/Ir_3Hf$
interfaces. It is found that the descending sequence of mechani-
cal properties of Ir and $Ir_3X$ is: $Ir \rightarrow Ir_3Nb \rightarrow Ir_3Hf \rightarrow Ir_3Zr$, while the
descending sequence of brittleness is: $Ir_3Nb \rightarrow Ir \rightarrow Ir_3Hf \rightarrow Ir_3Zr$. It
is also revealed that the interface orientation and interface atomic
structures have important effects on interface bond strength and
interface energy.
