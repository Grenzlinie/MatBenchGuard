## Stabilization of unusual metal oxidation state +4 in the iron, cobalt, nickel, and copper complexes with *trans*-di[benzo]porphyrazine and two fluoride anions: a DFT quantum chemical analysis\*

O. V. Mikhailov$^{a\star}$ and D. V. Chachkov$^{b}$

Molecular structure calculations of heteroligand (6666)macrotetracyclic metal (M$^{\text{IV}}$) chelates (M = Fe, Co, Ni, Cu) with *trans*-di[benzo]porphyrazine as the (N,N,N,N)-donor ligand and two F$^-$ anions were carried out at the OPBE/TZVP level of density functional theory. The key bond lengths, bond angles, and non-bond angles in the complexes were determined and corresponding standard enthalpies, entropies, and Gibbs energies of formation were calculated. All complexes have slightly distorted tetrahedral structures. The complex-forming agent M$^{\text{IV}}$ lies in the plane formed by the donor nitrogen atoms. The metal—nitrogen bonds are usually pairwise equal. The six-membered metal chelate rings in the complexes are identical to one another from the standpoint of both the sum and set of the bond angles.

**Key words:** *trans*-di[benzo]porphyrazine, fluoride anion, metal chelate, molecular structure, density functional theory (DFT).

*trans*-Di[benzo]porphyrazine (1) is a derivative of porphyrazine, one of the simplest (N,N,N,N)-donor macrotetracyclic ligands. It can be treated as a kind of a core of more complex organic compounds, *viz.*, phthalocyanine and its numerous derivatives that are widely used in science and technology. Compound **1** belongs to macrocyclic chelate ligands that can form rather strong complexes with various p-, d-, and f-element ions where the elements can be in various (low and high ) oxidation states (see, *e.g.*, Refs 1—5).

In each complex the M—N bond lengths differ slightly (by at most 2 pm). An analysis of other bond lengths in the type-2 complexes under study suggests the following. By and large, the shorter the distance from a bond to the corresponding metal chelate site MN₄ the stronger the influence of the nature of Mⁱᵛ on this bond. As a consequence, the effect of the nature of Mⁱᵛ is least pronounced for the C—C and C=C bonds in the six-membered "benzene" rings (meanwhile, this is quite predictable). In all complexes the lengths of the metal—fluorine bonds

M(1)—F(1) and M(1)—F(2) are equal to each other and monotonically increase from M = Fe to M = Cu (see Table 1). As to the bond angles, the N—M—N angles in the chelate sites of all metal complexes in question are equal to one another (90.0°). Four nitrogen atoms from the chelate site lie in the same plane, although none of the N—N—N angles formed by these atoms equals 90° (see Table 2). Finally, all five- and six-membered rings for which the sum of the inner angles is 540.0° ($BAS^{5}$) and 720.0° ($BAS^{6}$), respectively, are also planar. These numerical values are equal to the sums of the inner angles of a pentagon and a hexagon, respectively. In this connection, noteworthy is that the four six-membered metal chelate rings are identical to one another from the standpoint of the set of inner angles. This also holds for two six-membered "peripheral" rings, although the bond angle values in them differ from those in the metal chelate rings), but is invalid for the five-membered rings (see Table 2). Meanwhile, this conclusion a priori follows from the structural formula of the type-2 complexes. Note that the F(1)—M(1)—F(2) bond angles in the complexes are either exactly equal to 180.0° (in the $Fe^{IV}$, $Ni^{IV}$, and $Cu^{IV}$ complexes) or slightly different from this value (179.8° in the $Co^{IV}$ complex). The first three complexes have centrosymmetric structures and belong to the symmetry group $D_{2h}$. Taking account of the aforesaid, it is not surprising that, according to our calculations, the compounds in question have zero dipole moments ($\mu$). The structure of the $Co^{IV}$ complex has no center of symmetry ($C_{2v}$ symmetry group) and therefore the dipole moment of this complex significantly differs from zero ($\mu = 0.70$ D).

The effective atomic charges of $M^{IV}$ in all type-2 complexes are rather small. According to the results of NBO analysis, they are much less than 1.00 e (Table 3). The effective atomic charges of F also differ appreciably from $-$1.00 e. This feature, as well as the effective charges on other atoms in these chemical compounds, in particular, on the donor nitrogen atoms N(1), N(2), N(3), and N(4), are directly indicative of very high degree of delocalization of the electron density in each coordination compound under study.

According to our calculations, the most important thermodynamic parameters of the metal chelates considered, namely, the standard enthalpy ($\Delta H^\circ_{\text{f,298}}$), standard entropy ($S^\circ_{\text{f,298}}$), and Gibbs energy ($\Delta G^\circ_{\text{f,298}}$) of formation are positive, except the $\Delta H^\circ_{\text{f,298}}$ value for the Feᴵᵛ complex (Table 4).
