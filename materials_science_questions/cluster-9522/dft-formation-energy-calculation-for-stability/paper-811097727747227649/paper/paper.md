# Experimental and theoretical investigation of the chromium–vanadium–antimony system

DOI 10.1515/zkri-2016-1979
Received June 9, 2016; accepted October 13, 2016

**Abstract:** The binary compound $\text{V}_3\text{Sb}$ ($\text{V}_{2.64}\text{Sb}$, $\text{V}_3\text{Sb}$ and $\text{V}_{3.24}\text{Sb}$) was synthesized as thin multilayered films with varying $\text{V:Sb}$ ratios. The V-content determines the crystallization temperature and it is highest for the film with the lowest amount of V. Ternary chromium–vanadium–antimony (Cr–V–Sb) films were prepared containing Cr from 10 to 51 at-% with the Sb content fixed to yield $\text{M}_3\text{Sb}$ ($\text{M=Cr, V}$). In the as-deposited state the layers are already interdiffused which is most likely caused by the very low repeating unit thickness between 0.29 and 0.68 nm investigated by X-ray diffraction experiments. All ternary compounds crystallized from the amorphous state with crystallization temperatures depending more on the repeating unit thickness than on chemical composition. For most samples the simultaneous crystallization of the two phases $\text{M}_3\text{Sb}$ (A15 structure type) and MSb is observed. The crystalline A15 compounds are only stable in a limited temperature range and decompose at elevated temperatures. Compared to the binary Cr–Sb system crystallization of the hexagonal phase MSb ($\text{M=Cr, V}$) occurs at remarkably higher temperatures, i.e. in the ternary system nucleation and crystallization of this phase is hindered. The chemical composition requires short-range composition fluctuations to nucleate the binary phase. The first principles total energy calculations using the spin-polarized relativistic Korringa–Kohn–Rostoker (SPR-KKR) method confirm the experimental observations concerning the concentration-dependent stability of different phases of the Cr–V–Sb system. For the ratio $\text{M:Sb=3:1}$ the system is preferably stabilized in the A15 crystal structure for all possible Cr and V concentrations, while an increase of Sb content up to $\text{M:Sb=2:1}$ results in the stabilization of the $\text{Ni}_2\text{In}$ structure for almost all Cr concentrations. Only in the V-rich regime of the system the Heusler $\text{Ni}_2\text{Mn}$-Al-type structure was found to be energetically more preferable.

**Keywords:** A15 type structure; antimonides; phase stability; theory; thin film synthesis.

## Introduction

Antimonides of the first row transition metals (TM) are intensively investigated due to their interesting physical properties. Examples are thermoelectric materials like $\text{CoSb}_3$ [1, 2] or $\text{Zn}_4\text{Sb}_3$ [3]. Another unusual antimonide is $\text{FeSb}_2$ which exhibits a colossal Seebeck coefficient [4]. The equilibrium phase diagrams of many binary transition metal antimonides are well documented and several TM antimonides are characterized by identical $\text{TM:Sb}$ ratios and identical crystal structures [5, 6]. For Cr–Sb, however, only two compounds were identified under equilibrium conditions [7]: CrSb with a narrow and temperature dependent homogeneity range, and $\text{CrSb}_2$ with no measurable homogeneity range. Many thermodynamically stable TM antimonides with stoichiometry $1:1$ ($\text{M=Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co}$ and $\text{Ni}$) adopt the NiAs-type structure (space group: $P6_3/mmc$). For the $\text{TM:Sb}$ ratio $1:2$ the marcasite-type structure is observed, e.g. $\text{CrSb}_2$, $\text{FeSb}_2$, or the $\text{CuAl}_2$ type structure for $\text{TiSb}_2$ and $\text{VSb}_2$ [8]. Metal rich antimonides like $\text{Ti}_2\text{Sb}$ [9], $\text{Ti}_5\text{Sb}_3$ [10] or $\text{Ti}_3\text{Sb}$ [11] were reported for the early transition metals Ti and V. In contrast, no Cr-rich antimonides were known until a few years ago. In order to overcome the limitations of the conventional high temperature approach and to synthesize new metastable crystalline Cr antimonides we decided to investigate the Cr–Sb system using modulated elemental reactants (MER) [12–42]. The elemen- tal layer thicknesses determine the diffusion lengths, which can be reduced so that nucleation becomes the rate-limiting step in forming a compound. Applying the MER technique many metastable compounds could be synthesized which cannot be prepared using classical high temperature methods. The formation of crystalline solids from such multilayers and their stability can be monitored under in-situ conditions applying, e.g. X-ray diffraction (XRD) and/or X-ray reflectometry (XRR) [43]. Which compound nucleates first is related to the struc- ture of the amorphous precursor [44], but it is not yet predictable what will nucleate.

During our experiments performed on thin film samples of the Cr–Sb system we discovered that CrSb and $CrSb_2$ exhibit different behaviors as films compared to bulk materials. For thin alternating Cr and Sb layers (Cr:Sb=1:1; double-layer thickness: 5.37 nm) crystalli- zation of CrSb starts at about $90^\circ$C at the element inter- faces. Reduction of the double-layer thickness to 1.14 nm leads in the first step to formation of an amorphous inter- mediate followed by crystallization of the monoantimo- nide which decomposes into $CrSb_2$ and amorphous Cr at about $230^\circ$C. For thick layers (double-layer thickness: 10.25 nm) and an Cr:Sb ratio of 1:2, CrSb first nucle- ates and crystallites grow. Increasing the temperature results in the remaining Sb reacting with CrSb to form $CrSb_2$ [45]. On the Cr-rich side of the Cr–Sb system we were able to synthesize $Cr_{1.6}Sb$ adopting the $Ni_2$In-type structure starting with a double-layer thickness of 1.4 nm and a Cr content of 72 at-%. Low temperature anneal- ing ($T=262^\circ$C) led to the formation of this unusual com- pound with no counter-part in the equilibrium phase diagram. At elevated temperatures $Cr_{1.6}Sb$ is decomposed into CrSb and nanocrystalline Cr [46]. A similar metal rich antimonide was reported in the Mn–Sb system [47], while for other transition metals the value x in $TM_{1+x}Sb$ is much lower.

Changing the repeating unit and the total film thicknesses the new metastable compound $Cr_3Sb$ was obtained [48] crystallizing in the A15 structure type (s.g.: $Pm\overline{3}n$) at about $325^\circ$C. But the compound crystal- lized only together with hexagonal CrSb in nearly equal amounts. Heating the sample leads to decomposition of $Cr_3Sb$ and at ca. $450^\circ$C no reflections of $Cr_3Sb$ could be detected in the XRPD. Because $V_3Sb$ can be synthesized in a relatively large compositional range, $V_{2.8}Sb$–$V_{3.75}Sb$, crystallizing in the A15 structure type [49] we inves- tigated whether addition of V to $Cr_3Sb$ helps to either nucleate or stabilize a Cr-rich antimonide.

## Experimental details

Chromium and vanadium were evaporated by an electron-beam source whereas antimony was deposited from an effusion cell in an ultra high vacuum system (described in detail elsewhere [48, 50]). Cr–Sb, V–Sb and Cr–V–Sb films were synthesized by sequential depo- sition on blank (100)-Si pieces (1×1 inch) and PMMA-coated (Micro- Chem Corp., Newton, MA, USA) substrates (diameter: 6 inch) in order to perform in-situ X-ray and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurements (New Castle, DE, USA). For the DSC experiments the films on PMMA were removed from the substrates and PMMA was dissolved in acetone. The elemental ratio was adjusted by varying the Cr and V shutter opening time while keeping the time for Sb constant. Details of the calibration procedure can be found in the Supporting Information (Figure S1) and in [46]. Before deposition of the ternary films, the optimal deposition parameters were determined for the binary compounds $V_3Sb$ and $Cr_3Sb$ (see Figure S1). On the basis of the results of the calibration procedure the shutter opening times for the preparation of thin film precursors could be determined to obtain chosen compositions and thicknesses. The compositions of the dif- ferent samples are plotted in a ternary phase diagram (see Figure S2).

Electron probe microanalysis (EPMA, CAMECA, Gennevilliers, Cedex, France – using a Camera SX50) as well as scanning elec- tron microscopy (ESEM XL 30, Philips, Eindhoven, The Netherlands
– equipped with an energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer – EDAX, Oxford Instruments, Abingdon, UK) were carried out to determine the chemical composition of film before and after in-situ measure- ments. The data were collected on different spots using an accelera- tion voltage of 12–15 kV. The V–K, Cr–K and Sb–L signals were used for the analysis as they do not overlap with the Si–K emission of the substrate. The average composition was calculated using data of five measurements across the sample yielding an estimated uncertainty of about $\pm 2.5$ at-%.

In-situ (Anton Paar, Graz, Austria) and ex-situ X-ray diffraction (XRD, Panalytical, Almelo, The Netherlands) measurements were performed in order to follow the solid state reaction and X-ray reflec- tivity (XRR) measurements to determine the film thicknesses in the as-deposited state (X'Pert Pro, Panalytical, Cu K$\alpha$ radiation). In-situ XRD measurements and annealing experiments were done in a high temperature chamber (HTK 1200 N, Anton Paar) in He atmosphere (heating rate: $2^\circ$C/min.; step size: $0.08^\circ$). The patterns for Rietveld refinements were recorded with a step size of $0.04^\circ$. All the refine- ments were done with TOPAS (Coelho Software, Brisbane, Australia) applying the fundamental parameter approach, spherical harmonics to model preferred orientation and the crystallite size effect. Refined parameters: zero point, scale factors for each phase, lattice para- meters of each phase, size broadening, background and occupancy factor for Sb in the cubic phase. For $Cr_3Sb$ (space group: $Pm\overline{3}n$) all atoms are on special positions.

But for the pure Cr–Sb film crystallization of hexagonal CrSb started as low as about 125°C which is significantly lower than for the present sample. Obviously the nucleation and crystallization of hexagonal CrSb or (Cr, V)Sb is hindered in the sample with the TM:Sb ratio of 3:1. For pure CrSb the $c/a$ ratio was 1.336 ($T$=200°C) which was near the value for stoichiometric bulk CrSb (1.326) [5]. The present sample shows an appreciable change of the $c/a$ ratio from 1.328 ($T$=366°C) to 1.40 ($T$=550°C). The reason for the strong expansion of the $c$-axis and the contraction of the $a$-axis is not fully clear because the magnetic properties are not known. But removal of defects from the structure during the heat treatment may contribute to the alteration of the lattice parameters. The sizes of coherently scattering domains of the A15 phase are always larger than that of the hexagonal phase. Increase of the temperature leads to a growth of the crystallites. At the highest temperature of 550°C nanocrystalline elemental Cr could be identified in the XRPD. The phase fraction (Cr, V)₃Sb:(Cr, V)Sb changes significantly with increasing temperature and nearly equal amounts of the cubic and hexagonal phases are observed at $T$=550°C (Table 2). The formation of elemental Cr and of the hexagonal phase indicates that the cubic material is decomposed during the annealing process. The driving force for the decomposition may be a lower stability of (Cr, V)₃Sb compared to the thermodynamically stable phases V₃Sb and CrSb/VSb.

For determination of the thermodynamic stability the films deposited on PMMA were removed and investigated with DSC. The DSC curves for Cr₃Sb, Cr₂VSb and V₃Sb are displayed in Figure 8 (all DSC curves are shown in Figure S6). In the three curves an intense exothermic signal due to crystallization and growth occurs which is located at $T_\text{p}$=390°C for Cr₃Sb and at $T_\text{p}$=380°C for V₃Sb ($T_\text{p}$ is the peak temperature). A significant shift to a higher temperature is obtained for Cr₂VSb ($T_\text{p}$=430°C). Before the exothermic event all curves exhibit an increase of the baseline, which is most probably caused by interdiffusion of the constituents. While the binary compounds crystallize and grow at very similar temperatures, the crystallization/growth barrier is significantly larger for the ternary phase. It should be noted that only V₃Sb crystallizes as phase pure material while the other two compounds appear as mixtures. The slight differences between the crystallization temperatures obtained with DSC and XRD are due to the different experimental setups.

## Stability of the Cr₃Sb phase by substitution (theory)

In this section we present the results of theoretical investigations on the stability of the Cr–V–Sb system with different Cr:V:Sb ratios and different crystal structure. We focus mainly on the systems with the (Cr, V):Sb composition ratio 3:1 and 2:1. As it follows from the ternary Cr–V–Sb phase diagram, in the case of (Cr, V):Sb ≥ 3:1 ratio the system prefers to crystallize in the cubic phase A15 (Cr₃Si-type, s.g. $Pm\overline{3}n$). However, already a small deviation of the composition towards higher Sb concentration leads to a stabilization of the hexagonal phase (Ni₂In type, s.g. $P6_3/mmc$). In order to get deeper understanding of the reason for the stabilization of a certain structure, which depends on the concentrations of the alloy components, investigations have been performed based on total energy calculations. For each alloy composition, the total energy has been calculated and compared for the different structures, Ni₂In-, Cr₃Si-, Fe₃Si-, and Ni₂MnAl-types, as they are the most preferable from an energetic point of view. The choice of the considered structures is based primarily on

A similar observation concerns the phase A15 [48] for $Cr:Sb=3:1$ in the Cr–Sb system, while the $V_3Sb$ alloys crystallize in the phase A15 under normal conditions. The compound $Cu_3Sb$ crystallizes in the $Fe_3Si$-type cubic phase (s.g. $Fm\overline{3}m$), and therefore we considered the possibility to stabilize the Heusler-like phases ($Fe_3Si$-and $Ni_2MnAl$-types, s.g. $Fm\overline{3}m$) also in Cr–Sb based systems. In accordance with the considerations above, the two cubic structures $Cr_3Si$ and $Fe_3Si$ have been considered. In order to get an alloy with such a composition but having the hexagonal structure type, the occupation of the Sb sites has been taken to be 2/3. From the results shown in Figure 9a it follows that the A15 phase is more stable than the hexagonal phase for all Cr and V concentrations. It is interesting that both compounds, $V_3Sb$ and $Cr_3Sb$, as well the (V, $Cr)_3Sb$ alloys, having the A15 structure, are nonmagnetic. As can be seen in Figure 10, representing the DOS for $V_3Sb$ and $Cr_3Sb$, the strong hybridization of the V and Cr $d$-states with the $s$- and $p$-states of Sb moves the $d$-states of the transition elements above the Fermi energy. In the case of $Cr_3Sb$ this results in a reduction of the magnetic instability associated with a high DOS at the Fermi level, which usually leads to a spontaneous local spin magnetic moment of Cr atoms according to the Stoner criterion. On the other hand, the hexagonal phase is magnetic with magnetic moments for Cr of $2.2\ \mu_B$ and $2.6\ \mu_B$ on the non-equivalent sites of the $Ni_2In$ lattice. A comparison of the cubic $Fe_3Si$-type structure and the hexagonal phase demonstrates that the former is more stable (see Figure 9a, dashed line). Nevertheless, the A15 phase is energetically most favorable among the three phases under discussion. All these results are fully in line with the experimental results discussed above.

## Conclusions
The investigations of the Cr–V–Sb system with M:Sb=3:1 and variable Cr and V contents applying the MER approach showed that the A15 phase can be prepared in a wide compositional range. The (Cr, V)₃Sb phase is stable over a distinct temperature range and is decomposed into hexagonal (Cr, V)Sb, Cr and V. The simultaneous crystal- lization of hexagonal MSb could not be avoided except in the system with 66 at-% V. Despite the strong deviation of the stoichiometry from 1:1 short-range compositional fluctuations seem to occur thus leading to nucleation and crystallization of the hexagonal monoantimonide MSb. The crystallization temperatures for MSb (M=Cr, V) are significantly larger than observed for binary CrSb.

The DFT-based total energy calculations demon- strate a preferable stability of the A15 structure of the (CrₓV₁₋ₓ)₃Sb system (i.e. M:Sb=3:1) for all concentrations of Cr and V. However, a decrease of the M:Sb ratio shifts the system towards the stabilization of the hexagonal Ni₂In-type structure. For the ratio M:Sb=2:1 the hexago- nal structure becomes stable for almost all Cr concentra- tions except for the V-rich regime for which the stability of the Heusler structure type has been found. These results are in full agreement with experiments.
