# Thermodynamic modeling of the system As-Fe combined with first-principles total energy calculations

Munekazu Ohno${}^{a,*}$, Kanji Yoh${}^{a,b}$

${}^{a}$Research Center for Integrated Quantum Electronics, Hokkaido University, Sapporo 060-8628, Japan
${}^{b}$CREST-JST, Kawaguchi, Saitama 332-0022, Japan

Received 1 February 2008; received in revised form 6 February 2008; accepted 6 February 2008
Communicated by P. Rudolph
Available online 23 February 2008

## Abstract
A thermodynamic model has been developed for the system As-Fe by combining the Calphad approach and the first-principles total energy calculations. Our first-principles calculations are based on the projector augmented wave method within the generalized gradient approximation. We performed these calculations because the experimental values for the enthalpy of formation of the compounds $As_{2}Fe$, AsFe and $AsFe_{2}$ may have a large uncertainty. Our results indicate significantly more negative values for the enthalpy of formation of these compounds relative to the experimentally established values. We demonstrate that applying our first-principles results in a thermodynamic analysis based on the Calphad approach leads to a calculated phase diagram and thermodynamic properties which are not significantly different from experimental data.

## 1. Introduction
In the emerging field of spintronics, Fe/GaAs and Fe/InAs hybrid structures have attracted great attention as promising candidates for the use in spin-related devises, such as spin transistors with high-efficient spin injection at room temperature [1]. Considerable efforts have been devoted to clarify and control interface reactions during the fabrication process of these structures and to optimize the process parameters [2]. Although the phase diagram provides the fundamental information in the development of electronic devices [3], the details of phase equilibria and thermodynamic properties of the ternary systems As-Fe-Ga and As-Fe-In have not been fully established. The Calphad approach is recognized as a powerful method to describe the phase diagram in a multi-component system with high accuracy [4]. Within this approach, the thermodynamics of a system is described through simple phenomenological expressions for the Gibbs energies of all the phases. This is an empirical approach based on the fitting of the parameters through the experimentally established phase boundary data and thermodynamic properties. In this study, we perform a thermodynamic assessment within this approach of the binary system As-Fe, which is an important binary sub system of the ternary systems As-Fe-Ga and As-Fe-In.

One of the key information needed in the assessment using the Calphad approach is the enthalpy of formation of the three compounds present in the system As-Fe. Knowledge of the thermodynamic properties of these compounds is of critical importance in analyzing and controlling the possible reactions at Fe/semiconductor interfaces during the device processing. ## 2. Reported phase equilibria and thermodynamic properties

The phase equilibria of the system As-Fe have been reviewed in Ref. [8]. In this system there are three compounds, As₂Fe, AsFe and AsFe₂, which are stable at room temperature and they melt congruently. Addition- ally, a compound As₂Fe₃ is present, which forms from the liquid through a peritectic reaction. It decomposes eutectoidally at lower temperature, although conclusive data regarding the corresponding phase field is absent. The crystal structures of the compounds and the terminal solution phases in this system are listed in Table 1. It is to be noted that there are no crystallographic data reported for As₂Fe₃. Fig. 1 represents the crystal structures of As₂Fe, AsFe and AsFe₂, which are the subject of the present first-principles investigation. The experimentally measured lattice constants are given in Table 2.

One of the important features in this system is magnetism in the compounds. AsFe exhibits antiferromag- netic helical ordering of the Fe atoms, in the so-called helimagnetic structure, with a Néel temperature of $77 \pm 1$ K [9]. The magnetic moment is described by a spiral propagation vector of $0.375 \times 2\pi\mathbf{c}$ with $0.5 \pm 0.1\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ per Fe atom at 12 K where $\mathbf{c}$ denotes the translation vector in the crystal structure. AsFe₂ is also antiferromagnetic, but the Néel temperature is uncertain, ranging between 323 and 368 K [8]. The magnetic moments in sublattices I and II have been reported to be 1.28 and $2.05\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ [10], respectively. The thermodynamic modeling of the system As–Fe has been performed in Ref. [5] where the early experimental works for phase equilibria [11–20] and thermodynamic properties for the liquid [21–24] have been detailed. In addition to these data, we used in our present assessment heat capacity data of AsFe measured by Ref. [25] who employed adiabatic calorimetry and drop calorimetry. As pointed out in the introduction, the Gibbs energy of formation for the compounds have been reported by Ref. [6]. These data were established by measuring the equilibrium partial pressure of $S_{2}$ during the univariant reaction between pyrrhotite and iron arsenides As₂Fe, FeAs and AsFe₂. The estimated standard Gibbs energies are given in J/mol-atom as $G^{\circ}{(T)} = {- 14400} - {9.9T}$ for As₂Fe, $G^{\circ}{(T)} = {- 8800} - {17.6T}$ for AsFe and $G^{\circ}{(T)} = {- 1500} - {18.1T}$ for AsFe₂. It should be noted that the enthalpy of formation, especially for AsFe₂ is quite small and that this data claim large “positive” formation entropies. The estimation by Ref. [6] consists of a combination of calculated, extrapolated and measured properties associated with univariant reactions in the ternary system Fe–As–S. Barton [6] clearly stated that significant uncertainties are involved in the data. In Ref. [5], these results were used in the optimization with low weight.

### 3. First-principles total energy calculations

In this section, we describe the details of the present first-principles calculations. We also discuss the first-principles results regarding the phase stability at 0 K in this system. In the next section, the outcomes of the first-principles calculations are incorporated into the Calphad modeling.

We performed the first-principles total energy calculations of the solid phases, $\alpha$As, $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$, $\text{AsFe}$, $\text{AsFe}_2$ and $\alpha$Fe using ABINIT density functional theory code [27]. We employed the projector augmented wave (PAW) method [28] within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [29]. Because there are no crystallographic data available for $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}_3$, we did not carry out the calculation for it. Instead, its thermodynamic properties are estimated using phase boundary data.

As mentioned in the previous section, experimental observations show that AsFe exhibits antiferromagnetic helical ordering below $77\pm1$ K and that $\text{AsFe}_2$ is antiferromagnetic below the Néel temperature (323–368 K). In this study, all compounds were subjected to calculations using spin polarization with different initial spin configurations of the ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic states as well as to calculations using non-spin polarization for the non-magnetic state. The non-magnetic state is specified as $nm$ throughout this paper. It should be mentioned that we performed our calculation in a scalar-relativistic framework. Therefore, the magnetic moment is not a vector but a scalar quantity denoting a local inequality between up and down spin states. That does not allow an appropriate description of the helimagnetic state of AsFe. The experimental work by Ref. [9] shows that the magnetic moment in antiferromagnetic AsFe is characterized by a spiral propagation vector of $0.375\times2\pi\text{c}$. Therefore, a precise description of the antiferromagnetic state of AsFe requires a large number of cells to be handled in a full-relativistic calculation, leading to exceptionally large CPU time. We solved this problem as follows. Because the Néel temperature of AsFe is quite low $77\pm1$ K, we anticipate that the helimagnetic state at 0 K is approximately energetically degenerated with the paramagnetic state and/or the ferromagnetic, the other type of antiferromagnetic states. We take this assumption as the basis to simplify the antiferromagnetic state of AsFe such that the Fe atoms alternate in up and down spin states, with nearest neighbor Fe atoms along the $c$ (or $a,b$) axis in one unit cell.

The antiferromagnetic state in $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$ is one of the candidates for magnetic ground states. We assume that this state is characterized by Fe atoms at the center and the edges in the unit cell having up and down spins (see Fig. 1). For pure elements, we performed the calculations only for known ground states, i.e., $nm$ $\alpha$As and ferromagnetic $\alpha$Fe.

We performed the convergence tests for all the phases in terms of the plane wave cut-off energy, $k$-point sampling and smearing parameter. The following parameters were chosen to assure energy convergence less than 0.5 kJ/mol-atom for all the phases. The plane wave cut-off energy was set to be 40 Ry in all the calculations. The reciprocal-space sampling was performed on Monkhorst–Pack mesh of $k$-points [30]. The numbers of $k$-point meshes for each reciprocal lattice vector are specified as $M_x\times M_y\times M_z$. The large number of $k$-point meshes leads to the fine sampling of Brillouin zone and also causes large computational burden. The appropriate size has to be established by means of the convergence test. We employ meshes of $k$-points of $8\times8\times8$ for $\alpha$As, $12\times12\times12$ for $\alpha$Fe, $6\times6\times12$ for $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$ and $6\times12\times6$ for AsFe. For $\text{AsFe}_2$, a $k$-point mesh of $10\times10\times6$ was sampled expect when it is in the antiferromagnetic state. This state is characterized by two unit cells. We used a $10\times10\times4$ grid of $k$-points for this state. For a metal, the Fermi surface does not exhibit a sharp feature in the Brillouin zone. The broadening feature of occupation number of electron states at the bands close to the Fermi energy has to be considered to describe the properties of Fermi surface in the metal. The introduction of such a broadening also improves the convergence with respect to the number of $k$-point sampling meshes. One of the most commonly used ways for this is a Fermi-Dirac smearing approach which is based on the Fermi-Dirac statistics. We employed this smearing approach with smearing parameter of 0.4 mRy in all the calculations. Self-consistent static calculations were performed, following a full relaxation calculation regarding atomic structures and unit cell parameters.

The calculated lattice constants and magnetic moment are given in Table 2 and the formation energies at 0 K are presented in Fig. 2. The calculated results for ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$ are missing. The reason for that is that the calculations starting from the corresponding initial spin configurations always converged to a non-magnetic case. The fact that the local magnetic moment becomes negligible everywhere in cell might be an indication that the ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic state of $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$ is unstable at 0 K. The most stable magnetic state for each compound corresponds to $nm$ for $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$, antiferromagnetic for AsFe and antiferromagnetic for $\text{AsFe}_2$, which is fairly consistent with the experimental evidence [8]. The calculated values for the lattice parameters of the most stable magnetic state are not significantly different from the experimental values. Table 2 indicates that the calculated values agree with the experimental values within 1.4% for $\alpha$As, 0.2% for $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$, 1.1% for AsFe, 1.7% for $\text{AsFe}_2$ and 1.4% for Fe. It is

noted that the calculated results represent the values at 0 K, while the experimental data correspond to room temperature. Also, we did not consider the effect of the zero-point motion [33]. A correction due to these effects will increase the calculated lattice parameters, which may leads to a better agreement with the experimental data for all the phases, except for $\alpha$As of which the lattice constants are calculated to be higher than the experimental value. The present first-principles calculations show that the enthalpies of formation (more precisely, the energies of formation within this first-principles study) for all the compounds are much lower than the experimental data [6]. The data in Ref. [6] involves significant uncertainties as mentioned in Section 2 and, hence, we hold the reliability of the first-principles outputs in higher esteem in this study.

Fig. 2 demonstrates that the values of $nm$ As₂Fe and antiferromagnetic AsFe and AsFe₂ are located on the convex curve constructed from the first-principles results. This indicates that the tendency of phase stability is correct, as is expected from the reported phase diagram [8]. It should be stressed that we have simplified the calculations for the antiferromagnetic state of AsFe and that we did not determine its most stable structure. It is possible that the first-principles calculations including the fullrelativistic effect and a large number of cells predict a more stable structure. Such a structure should exhibit a more negative energy of formation than the value for the simplified structure. Fig. 2 shows that a slightly more negative value for the energy of formation of AsFe leads to the cases that the energy of formation of AsFe₂ is above the line connecting the energies of formation of AsFe and $\alpha$Fe. That leads to the instability of AsFe₂ at 0 K. We noticed that this is the case when AsFe is stabilized by just 0.1 kJ/mol, which is well within the uncertainty of our calculated value, 0.5 kJ/mol. It should be noted that the contribution of zero-point motion is not included in our discussion. Within the Debye approach, the zero-point energy, $U_0$, is given as $U_0=9Nk_\text{B}\theta/8$ with the number of atoms $N$, the Boltzmann constant $k_\text{B}$ and the Debye temperature $\theta$. Using the reported values of $\theta=470$ K for Fe [34], 282 K for As [34] and 353 K for AsFe [25], this contribution to the energy of formation for AsFe is calculated to be $\Delta U_0=-0.22$ kJ/mol. We cannot estimate the contribution of the zero-point motion for As₂Fe and AsFe₂ because of lack of their Debye temperatures. A further discussion requires a computation of the phonon density of state from the first-principles approach, which is beyond our concern. From this discussion, one realizes that the zero-point energy contribution plays an important role in determining the phase stability at 0 K in the present system. It may be possible that this contribution leads to the instability of AsFe₂ at 0 K. The possibility that AsFe₂ is not stable at low temperature has been actually pointed out in the experimental work of Barton [6]. They based their deductions on estimated thermodynamic properties of reactions in the ternary system As–Fe–S. However, the precision of their measurements does not allow for an accurate determination of the temperature at which AsFe becomes unstable. The problem as to whether or not AsFe should be stable at 0 K falls outside the scope of our present investigation. It requires a calculation within a full relativistic firstprinciples framework. Such a calculation also requires including a large number of unit cells to describe the antiferromagnetic state of AsFe more accurately. In addition, it is necessary to explicitly include the effect of zero-point motion.

In Section 4, we apply our first-principles results in a thermodynamic model using the Calphad approach. In this approach, we make use of Gibbs energy functions valid at temperature above 298 K. It should be noted that in a rigorous sense, the Calphad modeling requires the enthalpy of formation derived from the first-principles calculations at this temperature [35]. First of all, the $PV$ term in the enthalpy is negligible compared to the order of the energy of formation. Then, the enthalpy of formation can be replaced with the energy of formation. The vibrational energy contribution to the energy of formation at 298 K is discussed in the following. Within the Debye model, the vibrational energy contribution, $U_\text{vib}$, can be written as:

$$
U_{\mathrm{vib}}(T)=U_{0}+9 N k_{\mathrm{B}} T\left(\frac{\theta}{T}\right)^{3} \int_{0}^{\theta / T} \frac{x^{3}}{\mathrm{e}^{x}-1} \mathrm{~d} x, \tag{1}
$$

where $U_0$ is the zero-point energy as explained above. The substitution of $\theta=353$ K for AsFe [25] into Eq. (1) gives $U_\text{vib}$ ($T=298$) $=7.95$ kJ/mol. The calculated difference between the vibrational energies at 298 and 0 K, $U_\text{vib}$ ($T=298$)$-U_0=4.65$ kJ/mol is quite comparable to 4.74 kJ/mol estimated from the experimental data in Ref. [25]. This indicates that the Debye model provides a reasonable estimate in the present system. $\alpha$As for the Fe and As, $U_\text{vib}$ ($T=298$) are calculated to be 8.33 and 7.77 kJ/mol, respectively. These values result in the vibrational contribution to the energy of formation for AsFe at 298 K, $\Delta U_\text{vib}$ ($T=298$) $=-0.10$ kJ/mol. This small value has an important impact in determining the phase stability in the present system. However, we cannot estimate the vibrational contributions for As₂Fe and AsFe₂ because of lack of their Debye temperatures. Note that the contribution of 0.1 kJ/mol is less than 0.5 kJ/mol, the uncertainty of our first-principles calculations. Therefore, we decided that the energetics at 0 K is incorporated into the Calphad modeling as a first approximation. It is to be noticed that because the Néel temperature for AsFe is 77 K, the Calphad modeling requires the thermodynamic state for $nm$ AsFe. Accordingly, the calculated formation energy for $nm$ AsFe at 0 K is assumed to be the formation enthalpy at 298 K in the Calphad modeling, as shown in Fig. 2. The enthalpies of formation for As₂Fe and AsFe₂ at 298 K are given from the first-principles results for $nm$ As₂Fe and antiferromagnetic AsFe₂.

### 4. Thermodynamic modeling and calculated results

Several activity measurements [21–24] have indicated the existence of short-range order in the As–Fe liquid and, especially, the experimental work in Ref. [21] has shown that the estimated excess Gibbs energy curve of the liquid with respect to composition has a minimum at about the composition of $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}_3$. According to the previous modeling [5], the Gibbs energy of the liquid phase was modeled using the ionic two-sublattice model to account for the possible formation of $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}_3$ species in the liquid. In the present study, on the other hand, the liquid phase is described using a substitutional solution model with a large number of parameters relative to the study of Ref. [5]. The molar Gibbs energies for the liquid phase and also the other solution phases such as bcc-($\alpha\text{Fe}$) and fcc-($\gamma\text{Fe}$) phases are expressed by the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
G^{\phi}= & x_{\text{As}} G_{\text{As}}^{0, \phi}+x_{\text{Fe}} G_{\text{Fe}}^{0, \phi}+R T\left(x_{\text{As}} \ln x_{\text{As}}+x_{\text{Fe}} \ln x_{\text{Fe}}\right) \\
& +x_{\text{As}} x_{\text{Fe}} \sum_{v=0} L_{\text{As:Fe}}^{v, \phi}\left(x_{\text{As}}-x_{\text{Fe}}\right)^{v},
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

where $R$ is the gas constant, $x_{\text{i}}$ with $\text{i} = \text{As}$ and $\text{Fe}$ are the molar fractions of As and Fe, respectively, $G_{\text{i}}^{0, \phi}$ is the Gibbs energy function for pure element $\text{i}$ in phase $\phi$, which were taken from the SGTE compilation by Dinsdale [36] and $L_{\text{As:Fe}}^{v, \phi}$ is the $v$th Redlich–Kister parameter. As for the bcc and fcc phases, there exist magnetic transitions and the corresponding contribution to the Gibbs energy, $G_{\text{m}}^{\text{mg}}$, given in Ref. [37] is taken into account for these phases.

In the present study, the $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$, $\text{AsFe}$ and $\text{AsFe}_2$ are regarded as stoichiometric phases without homogeneity range. Because the Néel temperature of 77 K for $\text{AsFe}$ is below the validity range of the Gibbs energy functions given by SGTE and because there is a large uncertainty in the Néel temperature of $\text{AsFe}_2$ (323–368 K), we neglected the magnetic energy contribution to the Gibbs energies for these compounds as was done in Ref. [5]. The Gibbs energies of $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}$ and $\text{AsFe}_2$ are then described by the formula of floating reference state [35]. The heat capacity of $\text{AsFe}$ has been measured up to about 1000 K using adiabatic calorimetry and drop calorimetry [25] and this data are utilized in the present assessment. The Gibbs energy for $\text{AsFe}$ is thus modeled in the following absolute reference state formula, which allows a quantitative description of the heat capacity:

$$
\begin{aligned}
G^{\text{AsFe}}= & \frac{1}{2} G_{\text{As}}^{0, \text{rhombo}}(298.15 \mathrm{~K})+\frac{1}{2} G_{\text{Fe}}^{0, \text{bcc}}(298.15 \mathrm{~K}) \\
& +A+B T+C T \ln T+D T^{2}+E T^{3},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

where $A$, $B$,...,$E$ are the optimized parameters. The parameters $C$, $D$ and $E$ are obtained by fitting the temperature dependent heat capacity data and $A$ is obtained from the first-principles results.

Following the modeling in Ref. [5], $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}_3$ is described by the compound energy formalism [38] with two sublattices as $\text{Fe(As, Va)}_{0.75}$. The Gibbs energy formula is then given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
G^{\text{As}_{2}\text{Fe}_{3}}= & y_{\text{As}} G_{\text{Fe:As}}^{0, \text{As}_{2}\text{Fe}_{3}}+y_{\text{Va}} G_{\text{Fe:Va}}^{0, \text{As}_{2}\text{Fe}_{3}}+0.75 R T \\
& \times\left(y_{\text{As}} \ln y_{\text{As}}+y_{\text{Va}} \ln y_{\text{Va}}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

where $y_{\text{i}}$ with $\text{i} = \text{As}$ and $\text{Va}$ represents the site fraction of As and vacancy on the second sublattice, respectively. $G_{\text{Fe:As}}^{0, \text{As}_{2}\text{Fe}_{3}}$ and $G_{\text{Fe:Va}}^{0, \text{As}_{2}\text{Fe}_{3}}$ are the Gibbs energies of fictitious end members, $\text{Fe}_4\text{As}_3$ and pure iron, respectively, in a crystal structure of the $\text{As}_2\text{Fe}_3$ phase.

### 5. Conclusions

In the present study, we performed a thermodynamic assessment of the binary system As–Fe in which results of first-principles total energy calculations were used. The following points are important to be summarized.

- Our first-principles calculations predict that the ground state magnetic structures for each compound are $nm$ for As₂Fe, antiferromagnetic AsFe and antiferromagnetic

• Our calculations suggest the possibility that the anti-ferromagnetic AsFe₂ compound is not stable at 0 K which has been speculated in the early experimental work [6]. To reduce computational efforts, our calculations were performed for simplified antiferromagnetic state of the compound AsFe. Therefore, our calculated formation energy is considered to be less negative than the most stable structure. A slightly more exothermic value by about 0.1 kJ/mol leads to the instability of AsFe₂. That value is well within the uncertainty of our calculations, where were performed under static conditions. A more precise calculation requires a full-relativistic calculation including a large number of unit cells, as well as the zero-point energy effects.

• Our first-principles results demonstrate that the experimental data in Ref. [6] substantially underestimate the enthalpies of formation of the compounds.

• A detailed comparison between the calculated and experimental data demonstrates that the present thermodynamic calculation can describe the phase equilibria and thermodynamic quantities with high accuracy. This thermodynamic description can be extended to the calculation for ternary systems As-Fe-Ga and As-Fe-In. These calculations can be applied to analyze and control interface reactions during the fabrication process of important hybrid structures in the emerging field of spintronics.
