# Magnetic structure of FeMn thin films on Co(0 0 1) FCC substrate

### Abstract

LEED experiments by Offi et al. (Phys. Rev. B 66 (2002) 064419) have recently revealed that the Fe₅₀Mn₅₀ alloy thin films keeps the FCC structure when growing on Cu(0 0 1) and on Co/Cu(0 0 1). Stability of magnetic configurations of Fe₅₀Mn₅₀ alloy monolayer film is investigated using ab-initio calculations within density functional theory with gradient corrections. Fe induced magnetic moment of 2.85 $\mu_{\text{B}}$ with Mn magnetic moment of 3.61 $\mu_{\text{B}}$ aligned parallel with the ferromagnetic moment of the Co layer have been found as groundstate configuration with the Perdew-Wang functional in agreement with X-ray magnetic circular dichroism results.

Nowadays, the magnetic coupling across a ferro- magnet and an antiferromagnet is the key of the understanding of the phenomena summarized under the term "exchange bias" which is the shift of the hysteresis loop along the field axis and an increase in coercivity. The flourishing studies [1-3] are also con- nected to applications of ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) systems in spin-valves devices.

In order to address the coupling at the interface between an antiferromagnet and a ferromagnet, Kuch et al. [3] have recently performed an experimental study of single crystalline Co/FeMn bilayers with structurally well-defined interfaces, grown epitaxially on Cu(0 0 1). The direction of the couplng experienced by a FM films during growth on FeMn(0 0 1) is rapidly changing spatially, leading to small domain pattern which could be explained by the non-collinear FeMn spin structure.

Using photoemission electron microscopy with X-ray magnetic circular dichroism (XMCD) magnetic images have been obtained from single crystalline Co/FeMn and FeMn/Co bilayers epitaxially grown on a Cu(0 0 1) single crystal [4]. A net magnetic moment is induced in Fe and Mn when the Co film is in contact with Fe₅₀Mn₅₀, both in the paramagnetic and the AFM state of Fe₅₀Mn₅₀.

On the theoretical side, electronic structure and magnetic properties of $\text{Fe}_{x}\text{Mn}_{1-x}$ overlayers on Cu(0 0 1)/Co recently done by Szunyogh et al. [5] have shown that energetically, low-lying AFM config- urations are most likely the cause for the experimentally observed antiferromagnetism. In all cases investigated, the groundstate correspond to the (in-plane) FM configuration.

[2] showing that in the FeMn/Co/Cu systems, FeMn is growing as a FCC magnetic system and that Fe and Mn in an FeMn alloy film acquire induced magnetic moments when in contact with a Co film [4]. In the present paper, we perform ab-initio calculations within density functional theory with gradient corrections in order to describe the magnetic map of FeMn/Co(0 0 1) system. The description of the method used i.e. the tight-binding linearized muffin-tin orbital method (TB-LMTO) and those of the two functionals used namely Langreth–Mehl–Hu (LMH) and Perdew–Wang (PW91) can be found in Ref. [6]. As found in Ref. [6], the GGA functionals are well suited to describe the magnetic properties of Mn-based systems.

Indeed magnetism has a strong effect on the stabilization of the FeMn alloy formation. We report in Table 1, where the arrows $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ indicate that the Fe or Mn moments are parallel or antiparallel to the Co moment, the magnetic map of a FeMn ordered alloy on Co substrate. For the calculation we have considered a Co slab of seven layers with an FeMn alloy layer on both sides. We have considered two inequivalent atoms per plane and Co1 represents the center of the film. Five layers of empty spheres have been used. An AF coupling between Fe and Mn atoms in the surface layer is the groundstate for GGA-LMH whereas it is FM for GGA-PW91. This last result is well-suited to describe the magnetic properties of the system because it gives clearly the FM coupling in agreement with the experimental results. When the thickness of the $\text{Fe}_{50}\text{Mn}_{50}$ alloy film increases from 1 to 2 layers, the average magnetic moment per Fe atom decreases from 2.85 to 2.11 in $\mu_{\text{B}}$ whereas it goes from 3.61 to 0.64 for Mn. This trend is in qualitative agreement with recent XMCD results of Matthes et al. [7]. The complete results of the study of the various calculations considering different thickness will be presented in a forthcoming paper [8].

The TB-LMTO-ASA calculations with GGA-PW91 functional has indicated a FM coupling between Mn and Fe. This result was obtained only in the case of the GGA-PW91 functional which means that the type of GGA functional do have its importance in the description of the magnetic map of the FeMn/Co interface.
