# The structural, electronic, magnetic and mechanical properties of $\mathbf{d^0}$ binary Heusler alloys $\mathbf{XF_3}$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba)

Yang Zhou, Jian-Min Zhang*, Yu-Hong Huang, Xiu-Mei Wei

College of Physics and Information Technology, Shaanxi Normal University, Xian 710119, Shaanxi, PR China

## ABSTRACT

The first-principles calculations within generalized gradient approximation (GGA) method are employed to study the structural, electronic, magnetic and mechanical properties of $\mathrm{d^0}$ binary Heusler alloys $\mathrm{XF_3}$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba). They have integer magnetic moments per formula unit $1\ \mu_\mathrm{B}$/f.u., and the most stable state is ferromagnetic (FM) state. They have thermodynamic stabilities because the formation energies are negative and the cohesion energies are positive. The elastic constants $\mathrm{C_{ij}}$ satisfy the mechanical stability conditions. Their HM properties are robust under the effect of hydrostatic strain and tetragonal strain.

**Keywords:** Binary Heusler alloys; Electronic properties; Magnetic properties; Mechanical properties; First-principles

## 1. Introduction

Recently, the half-metallic (HM) materials have attracted widespread attention [1-6], since their band structures exhibit semiconductor or insulator character in one spin channel and metallic character in the other spin channel and thus achieving completely (100 %) spin polarization. According to the structural difference, the HM materials are divided into perovskite structures such as $\mathrm{Sr_2FeMoO_6}$, $\mathrm{BaCrO_3}$ and $\mathrm{La_{0.5}Ca_{0.5}MnO_3}$ [7-9], spinel structures such as $\mathrm{CuV_2S_4}$ and $\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$ [10, 11], rutile structures such as $\mathrm{CoS_2}$ and $\mathrm{CrO_2}$ [12, 13], Heusler alloys [14-20], and so on. Due to their extremely high Curie temperature and large magnetic moment, the Heusler alloys have broad application prospects in spintronics and magnetoelectronics [21-23].

Heusler alloys are a kind of intermetallic compounds with multiple components, simple structures and unique properties. In 1903, a German scientist Heusler found that the atoms in the alloy $\mathrm{Cu_2MnAl}$ were non-magnetic (NM), but the alloy showed an adjustable magnetism through heat treatment and chemical components [24]. Subsequently, Heusler discovered a series of alloys $\mathrm{Cu_2MnZ}$ (Z = In, Sn, Sb, Bi) also had the above properties, and these alloys were named after Heusler. The Heusler alloys have four nested face-centered cubic (FCC) lattices, including four independent Wyckoff coordinates along the body diagonal, namely A (0, 0, 0), B (1/4, 1/4, 1/4), C

(1/2, 1/2, 1/2) and D (3/4, 3/4, 3/4).

According to the kinds of the constituent atoms, the Heusler alloys can be divided into the following three categories, the first type is quaternary Heusler alloys XX'YZ (space group No. 216, F$\overline{4}$3m), in which the atoms can be arranged in the following three orders XX'YZ, X'XYZ and XYX'Z to occupy the Wyckoff coordinates ABCD. The second type is ternary Heusler alloys, which can be further divided into the ternary half Heusler alloys XYZ (space group No. 216, F$\overline{4}$3m) and ternary full Heusler alloys X₂YZ. The ternary half Heusler alloys XYZ are of Clᵦ type structure and the Wyckoff coordinate C is vacant. The X, Y and Z atoms occupy the Wyckoff coordinates A, B and D, respectively. The ternary full Heusler alloys X₂YZ have two structures: Cu₂MnAl (space group No. 225, Fm$\overline{3}$m) and Hg₂CuTi (space group No. 216, F$\overline{4}$3m). In the Cu₂MnAl (Hg₂CuTi) structures, the Wyckoff coordinates ABCD are occupied by the atoms in the order of XYXZ (XXYZ). The third type is binary Heusler alloys XZ, XZ₂ and XZ₃ (space group No. 225, Fm$\overline{3}$m). The binary Heusler alloys XZ can be obtained by removing Y atoms from the ternary half Heusler alloys XYZ. The binary Heusler alloys XZ₂ can be obtained by replacing the Y atoms in the ternary half Heusler alloys XYZ with the same Z atoms. The binary Heusler alloys XZ₃ can be obtained by replacing the X' and Y atoms in quaternary Heusler alloys XX'YZ with the same Z atoms.

The HM properties of many Heusler alloys have been theoretically verified, but in practice less than 100 % spin polarization is due to the atomic disorder. The binary Heusler alloys have only two kinds of elements, so the probability of occurrence of antisite defects is also reduced, which can effectively solve the above problems. Therefore, the binary Heusler alloys have attracted people's attention. However, the HM materials containing transition metal (TM) atoms (d HM materials) have been widely studied recently. As feasible candidate materials for spintronic devices, great progress has been made in finding materials with 100 % spin polarization without TM atoms. This new class of materials is usually called sp or d⁰ HM materials. Some d⁰ HM materials, such as the alkaline-earth carbides, the graphene nanoribbons and the nitrides with rock-salt and zinc-blende structures have been theoretically confirmed [25-30]. The HM properties of some alloys have also been confirmed in experimental work [31]. Compared to d HM materials, these new d⁰ HM materials are more suitable in spintronic applications since they have the following advantages. Firstly, they have the benefit of being energy-efficient for applications due to their smaller values of spin magnetic moment per formula unit [32, 33], and consequently, they create small external magnetic fields which lead to minimal energy losses in devices. Second, they have large band gaps with wide HM gaps and hence keep the HM character under relatively high pressures. Thirdly, their

equilibrium lattice constants are often close to that of the used semiconductors and their interfaces with semiconductors retain half-metallicity [34]. Fourth, they also present much higher values of Curie temperature $T_C$ than room temperature [35] knowing that the high values of Curie temperature are very important for spintronic devices since $T_C$ stabilizes the half-metallicity of the material through a small reduction of the spin-polarization at room temperature.

In recent years, many literatures have studied the $d^0$ binary Heusler alloys, such as $XN_3$ (X = Na, K, Rb) [36], $XO_3$ (X = Li, Na, K, Rb) [37] and $XCl_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr) [38], etc. In this paper, we focus on the structural, electronic, magnetic and mechanical properties of a new type of $d^0$ binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba), since the F atom is located at the same period of N and O atoms and the same group of Cl atom, but has the largest electronegativity and thus the strongest ionic bonding to the alkali metals Li, Na, K, Rb or alkali-earth metals Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba. So these new $d^0$ binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) may be easily fabricated in experiments and used in the spintronic and magnetoelectronic devices. We hope our work will provide inspiration for further theoretical and experimental investigations.

## 2. Computational methods
To study the properties of the $d^0$ binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba), we used Vienna *ab-initio* simulation package (VASP) based on density functional theory (DFT) [39-42] for calculations. The interaction of electrons and ionic core is represented by the projector augmented wave (PAW) potentials [43]. The exchange-correlation between electrons is described by the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [44] formula under the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). The Gaussian smearing with broadening of 0.2 eV was used. Structural optimization and relaxation of atomic coordinates use a mesh of $15×15×15$ k-points until the energy difference of the system and the maximum Hellmann-Feynman force on each atom are less than $1×10^{-5}$ eV and $0.02$ eV/Å, respectively. A conjugate gradient (CG) algorithm is applied to minimize the Kohn-Sham energy and the cutoff energy of the plane wave is set to 500 eV. The F $2s^22p^5$, Be $2s^2$, Mg $3s^2$, Ca $3p^64s^2$, Sr $4s^24p^65s^2$ and Ba $5s^25p^66s^2$ are taken as valence electrons.

Figure 1 shows the $d^0$ binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) with $DO_3$ structure. The F(A), F(B), F(C) and X atoms are located at Wyckoff coordinates A (0, 0, 0), B (1/4, 1/4, 1/4), C (1/2, 1/2, 1/2) and D (3/4, 3/4, 3/4), respectively. The nearest neighbor atoms of F(B) atom are four F(A) and four F(C), while F(A) and F(C) atoms have four F(B) and four X atoms in their first neighborhood, so F(A) and F(C) atoms have the same neighborhood environment.

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Structure properties

In order to find the most stable state, we calculate the energy-volume functions of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) in NM, anti-ferromagnetic (AFM) and ferromagnetic (FM) phases, and the results are drawn in Fig. 2. It can be seen that the lowest energy of each alloy in the changing volume appears in the FM state, so the subsequent calculations are all performed in the FM state. As shown in Table 1, the equilibrium lattice constants of $BeF_3$, $MgF_3$, $CaF_3$, $SrF_3$ and $BaF_3$ are 5.173, 5.404, 5.724, 6.019 and 6.371 Å, respectively. This is consistent with the increasing trend of the top-down radius of the same group elements.

### 3.2. Thermodynamic stabilities and Curie temperatures

To estimate the thermodynamic stabilities of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba), we calculate their formation energies and cohesive energies. Formation energy refers to the energy absorbed or released when the atoms form a compound from their bulk phases. Cohesive energy can measure the strength of chemical bonds between atoms. They are calculated by the following equations [45-47]:

$$
\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{f} =\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{tot} -(\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{X}^\mathrm{bulk} +3\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{F}^\mathrm{bulk}) \tag{1}
$$

$$
\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{c} =\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{X}^\mathrm{atom} +3\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{F}^\mathrm{atom} -\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{tot} \tag{2}
$$

where the $\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{tot}$ represent the total energies per formula unit (f.u.) of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba), $\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{X}^\mathrm{bulk}$ and $\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{F}^\mathrm{bulk}$ represent the energy of each atom in the bulk X and F, and $\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{X}^\mathrm{atom}$ and $\mathrm{E}_\mathrm{F}^\mathrm{atom}$ represent the energy of the isolated X and F atoms. As shown in Table 1, the formation energies per f.u. of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) are -5.592, -10.007, -12.638, -12.999 and -13.029 eV/f.u., respectively. The cohesive energies are 13.602, 13.762, 16.778, 16.834 and 17.133 eV/f.u., respectively. The negative formation energies indicate that the formation of the alloys is an exothermic process and thus can be easily fabricated experimentally, and larger positive cohesive energies indicate that the alloys have strong chemical bonds. Therefore, all the five alloys are thermodynamic stable.

The magnetic material has a critical temperature, namely the Curie temperature $\mathrm{T_C}$, which determines the upper temperature limit at which the magnetic device operates. We calculate the Curie temperature $\mathrm{T_C}$ of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) by the following formula [48]:

$$
\mathrm{T_C}=\frac{2(\mathrm{E_{AFM}}-\mathrm{E_{FM}})}{3\mathrm{k_B}} \tag{3}
$$

where $\mathrm{E_{AFM}}$ and $\mathrm{E_{FM}}$ are the total energies per f.u. of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg,

Ca, Sr, Ba) in AFM and FM phases and $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant. The Curie temperatures $T_C$ of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) are 466.182, 411.246 376.048, 382.118 and 379.921 K, respectively, higher than room temperature mean they are more suitable for applications in the field of spintronics and magnetoelectronics.

### 3.3. Electronic properties

To study the electronic properties, we plot the spin-polarized total and atomic density of states (DOS) of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) in Fig. 3. These alloys have the HM characters because their spin-up channels open the band gaps $E_g^\uparrow$ of 4.488, 6.352, 8.795, 8.715 and 8.711 eV, respectively, while their total DOS in the spin-down channels pass through the Fermi level $E_F$. We divide the energy regions into two parts. In the region I, the total DOS are provided by the X, F(A/C) and F(B) atoms, while in the region II, the total DOS are mainly contributed by the X atoms.

To further understand the electronic properties, we calculate the orbital-projected band structures of X-s (magenta diamonds), F(A/C)-2p (red circles) and F(B)-2p (blue down-triangles) orbitals of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) and plot them in Fig. 4. The size of the symbols is proportional to the weight of the corresponding states in a Bloch wave function. The s orbitals of the F(A/C) and F(B) atoms are located in the deep energy region due to completely filled with 2 electrons. In the spin-up channels, the orbital-projected band structures have the large indirect band gaps and thus the insulator properties. While in spin-down channels, the F(B)-2p orbitals pass through the Fermi level $E_F$ and have metallic properties. Thus, the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) have the HM properties. For each binary Heusler alloy, the shapes of the orbital-projected band structures are similar between the spin-up and spin-down channels. The conduction band minimum (CBM) contributed mainly by X-s orbital shifts upwards for X = Be, Mg, Sr and Ba successively, while for X = Ca, the X-s orbital moves upwards and exceeds the F(B)-2p orbital, so the CBM is contributed by the F(B)-2p orbital. The valence band maximum (VBM) of the five alloys are contributed by F(B)-2p orbital and maintained about -0.5 eV, so the spin-up band gap $E_g^\uparrow$ has an increasing tendency. This is because the electronegativities 1.57, 1.31, 1.00, 0.95 and 0.89 of X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr and Ba atoms respectively are much smaller than 3.98 of F atom. The greater the electronegativity difference between the X and F atoms, the stronger the ionic bond, the smaller the band dispersion, and the larger the spin-up band gap $E_g^\uparrow$.

The determined values of the VBM, CBM, spin-up band gap $E_g^\uparrow$ and HM band gap $E_g^{HM}$ are also listed in the last four columns of Table 1. The HM band gap $E_g^{HM}$ is determined by the minimum

absolute value of VBM and CBM. For the binary Heusler alloy $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba), the absolute value of VBM is much smaller than that of CBM, so the HM band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{HM}}$ equals to the absolute value of VBM. The larger values of both $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\uparrow}$ and $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{HM}}$ indicate the HM properties of these five alloys are robust.

### 3.4. Magnetic properties
The magnetic moments of HM alloys must be integers and can be calculated as $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{t}}=\mathrm{N}_{\uparrow}-\mathrm{N}_{\downarrow}$, the $N_{\uparrow}$ and $N_{\downarrow}$ represent the numbers of valence electrons per f.u. in the spin-up and spin-down channels. We plot the integrated density of states (IDOS) of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) in Fig. 5 to show the numbers of $N_{\uparrow}$ and $N_{\downarrow}$ and the total magnetic moment per f.u. $M_t$ ($\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$/f.u.). For $BeF_3$, $MgF_3$, $CaF_3$, $SrF_3$ and $BaF_3$, the numbers of $N_{\uparrow}$ are 12, 12, 15, 16 and 16, respectively, and the numbers of $N_{\downarrow}$ are 11, 11, 14, 15 and 15, respectively. The total magnetic moments of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) are all $1\ \mu_{\mathrm{B}}$/f.u.. Table 2 lists the atomic magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{A})}, \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{B})}, \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{C})}$ and $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{X}}$ ($\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$/atom) for the F atoms at Wyckoff coordinates A, B, C and the X atom at Wyckoff coordinate D, total magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{t}}$ ($\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$/f.u.) and interstitial magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Int}}$ ($\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$/f.u.) of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba). For each alloy $XF_3$, the identical atomic magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{A})}$ and $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{C})}$ are obtained for F(A) and F(C) atoms since they have the identical neighborhood environment (four F(B) and four X atoms in their first neighborhood). The total magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{t}}$ are mainly contributed by F(B) atom. The positive magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{A})}, \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{B})}, \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{C})}$ indicate they are FM coupling. The magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{X}}$ is negligible. The sum of the atomic magnetic moments $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{A})}, \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{B})}, \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{C})}$ and $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{X}}$ is smaller than the total magnetic moment $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{t}}$ due to the presence of the interstitial magnetic moment $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Int}}$ as is listed in the last column of Table 2.

### 3.5. Mechanical properties
The elastic constants $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{ij}}$ are indispensable for describing the mechanical properties of the materials. The binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) belong to a cubic system with high symmetry and thus have only three independent elastic constants, that is $\mathrm{C}_{11}$, $\mathrm{C}_{12}$ and $\mathrm{C}_{44}$, which can be obtained by calculating the total energy as a function of strain. Table 3 lists the calculated elastic constants $\mathrm{C}_{11}$, $\mathrm{C}_{12}$ and $\mathrm{C}_{44}$ (GPa), bulk modulus B (GPa), shear modulus G (GPa), Young's modulus E (GPa), Poisson's ratios $v$, Pugh's ratios K and anisotropic factors A of the binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba). The value of the elastic

constant $C_{11}$ sequentially decreases in the order of Be, Mg, Ca, Sr and Ba, which means the resistance of the unidirectional compression is lowered in the same order [49]. The mechanical stability criteria given by Mouhat and Coudert can be expressed as [50]:

$$
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{C}_{44}>0 \\
\mathrm{C}_{11}>\left|\mathrm{C}_{12}\right| \\
\mathrm{C}_{11}+2 \mathrm{C}_{12}>0
\end{cases} \tag{4}
$$

After checking, we find that the above formulas are satisfied, indicating these five alloys are mechanical stable. At the same time, the bulk modulus B (GPa), shear modulus G (GPa), Young's modulus E (GPa), Poisson's ratio $v$, Pugh's ratio K and anisotropic factor A are estimated using the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation [51-53]:

$$
\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{V}}=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{\mathrm{C}_{11}+2 \mathrm{C}_{12}}{3} \tag{5}
$$

$$
\mathrm{G}=\frac{\mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{V}}+\mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{R}}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}_{11}-\mathrm{C}_{12}+3 \mathrm{C}_{44}}{5}+\frac{5 \mathrm{C}_{44}\left(\mathrm{C}_{11}-\mathrm{C}_{12}\right)}{4 \mathrm{C}_{44}+3\left(\mathrm{C}_{11}-\mathrm{C}_{12}\right)}\right) \tag{6}
$$

$$
\mathrm{E}=\frac{9 \mathrm{BG}}{3 \mathrm{~B}+\mathrm{G}} \tag{7}
$$

$$
v=\frac{3 \mathrm{~B}-2 \mathrm{G}}{2(3 \mathrm{~B}+\mathrm{G})} \tag{8}
$$

$$
\mathrm{K}=\frac{\mathrm{B}}{\mathrm{G}} \tag{9}
$$

$$
\mathrm{A}=\frac{2 \mathrm{C}_{44}}{\mathrm{C}_{11}-\mathrm{C}_{12}} \tag{10}
$$

The large bulk modulus B and shear modulus G indicate the binary Heusler alloys $XF_{3}$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) have strong resistance to fracture and plastic deformation. The hardness of the alloys depend on the Young's modulus E, and the higher the Young's modulus E, the greater the hardness of the materials. The calculation results indicate the hardness of $CaF_{3}$ is the highest. Poisson's ratios $v$ can be used to distinguish the brittleness and ductility of materials and show the cases of bonding between atoms [54]. The Poisson's ratios $v$ of these five alloys are greater than 0.25, indicating the five alloys are ductility and the interatomic bonds are mainly metallic. The brittleness and ductility of the materials can also be distinguished by the Pugh's ratios K [55]. If the value of K is greater than 1.75, the material is ductility, and conversely, it is brittleness. The greater than 1.75 Pugh's ratios K shows the five alloys are ductility as well. If the value of the anisotropic factor A of the alloy is equal to 1, the alloy is isotropic, conversely, deviation from 1 indicates the alloy is anisotropy [56]. The values of the anisotropy factors A of the five alloys are

not equal to 1, implying the five alloys are anisotropic.

### 3.6. Effect of hydrostatic strain and tetragonal strain on the HM properties

Hydrostatic strain and tetragonal strain have great influences on the HM properties, so we study them in this part. In Fig. 6, the VBM, CBM, spin-up band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\uparrow}$ and HM band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{HM}}$ of the binary Heusler alloys $\mathrm{XF}_{3}$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) are plotted as a function of the hydrostatic strain. The five alloys maintain HM properties in the shaded regions. Considering the practical applications, we limit the maximum expansion strain to be 15 % at which the five alloys are still HM. To maintain the HM character, the maximum compression strains should not exceed -25.440, -30.511, -21.963, -13.452 and -13.324 % for the alloys $\mathrm{XF}_{3}$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba), respectively. The larger HM regions demonstrate that the HM properties of the five alloys are robust. For each alloy, with increasing lattice constant, the VBM decreases while the HM band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{HM}}$ (absolute value of VBM) increases. With increasing lattice constant, the CBM and spin-up band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\uparrow}$ of $\mathrm{BeF}_{3}$ gradually decrease while for the other four alloys first increase and then decrease. The $\mathrm{BeF}_{3}$, $\mathrm{MgF}_{3}$, $\mathrm{CaF}_{3}$, $\mathrm{SrF}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{BaF}_{3}$ have the largest spin-up band gaps $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\uparrow}$ of 12.706, 16.781, 9.407, 10.351 and 9.715 eV at compression strains of -25.000, -26.000, -12.000, -11.000 and -9.000 %, respectively. Fig. 7 shows the total and atomic magnetic moments of the binary Heusler alloys $\mathrm{XF}_{3}$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) in the HM regions as a function of hydrostatic strain. The total magnetic moment is kept at $1\ \mu_{\mathrm{B}}/$f.u., the magnetic moments of X and F(A/C) atoms are not sensitive to the lattice constant, and the magnetic moment of F(B) atom increases slightly with increasing lattice constant.

Figure 8 shows the VBM, CBM, spin-up band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\uparrow}$ and HM band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{HM}}$ of the binary Heusler alloys $\mathrm{XF}_{3}$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) as a function of the tetragonal strain c/a (maintaining the volume unchanged). The HM regions of the binary Heusler alloys $\mathrm{BeF}_{3}$, $\mathrm{MgF}_{3}$, $\mathrm{CaF}_{3}$, $\mathrm{SrF}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{BaF}_{3}$ are 69.795~174.747, 68.331~183.274, 66.749~210.223, 65.609~247.141 and 68.928 %~271.072 %, respectively. For each alloy, both the CBM and spin-up band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\uparrow}$ increase firstly to the maximum values at the equilibrium lattice constant and then decrease with increasing the tetragonal strain c/a, but the absolute value of VBM and the HM band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{HM}}$ are the largest at the equilibrium lattice constant. Therefore, the maximum values of the VBM, CBM, spin-up band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\uparrow}$ and HM band gap $E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{HM}}$ under tetragonal strain are the same as those listed in the last four columns of Table 1.

Figure 9 shows the total and atomic magnetic moments of the binary Heusler alloys $\mathrm{XF}_{3}$ (X = Be,

Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) in the HM regions as a function of the tetragonal strain c/a. For each alloy, with increasing tetragonal strain c/a, the magnetic moment of F(B) atom increases firstly to the maximum value at the equilibrium lattice constant and then decreases, while an opposite trend is obtained for the magnetic moment of F(A/C) atom to maintain the total magnetic moment 1 $\mu_B$/f.u. unchanged since the magnetic moment of X atom is close to zero.

## 4. Conclusions
The structural, electronic, magnetic and mechanical properties of $d^0$ binary Heusler alloys $XF_3$ (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) are studied by using the first-principles calculations based on DFT. The results show that all five alloys have integer magnetic moments per formula unit 1 $\mu_B$/f.u., and the most stable states are all half-metallic ferromagnetic (HM-FM) state. The negative formation energies and positive cohesion energies prove they are thermodynamic stable. Satisfying the mechanical stability conditions proves they are also mechanical stable. The higher Curie temperatures $T_C$, the constant magnetic moment of 1 $\mu_B$/f.u., and the larger HM HM-FM regions under either hydrostatic or tetragonal strains show they usable in the field of spintronics and magnetoelectronics.
