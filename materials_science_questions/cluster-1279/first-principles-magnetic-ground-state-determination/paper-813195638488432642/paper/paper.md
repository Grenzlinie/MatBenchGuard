## ABSTRACT

The structural, electronic and magnetic properties of the hexagonal nanolaminated layered MAX phases compound $Cr_2GeC$ are investigated using the full-potential linearized augmented-plane-waves (FPLAPW) method within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and GGA+$U$ calculations. The results indicate $Cr_2GeC$ has a sizable moment of $1.667\ \mu_B$ revealing the existence of a ferrimagnetic ground state in a MAX phase compound. This surprising new result will lead to the possibility of applying the layered magnetic MAX phase compounds in electronics and spintronics areas.

### 1. Introduction

The MAX phase is a group of layered crystals having the general formula $M_{n+1}AX_n$, ($n=1,2,3$, or 4) where M is an early transition metal, A is an A-group element, and X is either C or N. In recent years, MAX phases have attracted immense attention from the fundamental physics to their potential applications due to their high strength and modulus, good damage tolerance, high thermal and electrical conductivity, and thermal shock resistance [1,2]. Up to now, more than 70 MAX compounds [3,4] have been synthesized, new phases are being discovered, and the search is still going on. Moreover, an increasing number of scientists attempt to synthesize magnetic MAX phases [5] because layered magnetic materials are fascinating from the point of view of fundamental science as well as special applications. As is well known, giant magnetoresistance (GMR) in magnetic multilayers has a revolutionary impact on magnetic recording and data storage industry.

As a typical MAX phase material, the various physical properties of $Cr_2GeC$ have been studied in both theorical [6-9] and experi- ment [10-15], but a systemic theoretical description of magnetic coupling mechanism for interlaminar and in-plane of $Cr_2GeC$ is still not clear. Such a study is needed for not only the fundamental understanding, but also in facilitating opening up a field of new layered magnetic materials with high potential for electronics and spintronics applications. In this paper, we present results on the structural, electronic and magnetic properties of nanosacle laminated compound $Cr_2GeC$. Most importantly, we find the existence and coupling mechanism of the ferrimagnetic (FIM) ground state in $Cr_2GeC$. Our calculations are based on density functional theory (DFT) using both GGA and GGA+$U$ potentials. The present work provides new insights into the origin of the strong magnetic coupling in the compound, which should be useful for the design new organic spintronic materials and applications. The rest of this article is organized as follows: The computational details are given in *Section 2*. In *Section 3*, we present the results and discussions. Finally, a brief summary is presented in *Section 4*.

### 2. Computational details

The structural optimization is performed by the first-principles software package CASTEP [16]. The Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb- Shanno (BFGS) minimization scheme [17] is used to minimize the total energy and internal forces. The tolerances for geometric optimization are set such that the difference in total energy is less than $5\times10^{-6}$ eV/atom, the maximum ionic Hellmann-Feynman force is less than 0.01 eV/Å, the maximum ionic displacement is less than $5\times10^{-4}$ Å, and the maximum stress is less than 0.02 GPa. The plane-wave energy cutoff and the Brillouin-zone sampling are fixed at 450 eV and $10\times10\times2$ special $k$-point meshes, respectively. Interactions of electrons with ion cores are represented by Vanderbilt type ultrasoft pseudo-potential [18]. The exchange correlation potential is described by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [19] with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) scheme [20].

For the electronic and magnetic properties, we shifted to the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave method (FPLAPW) as implemented in the WIEN2K code [21]. The WIEN2K code is known for its accuracy in evaluating the magnetic ground state properties. The muffin-tin sphere radii ($R_{ii}$) of Cr, Al, and C are chosen to be 2.1, 2.5, and 1.6 a.u., respectively. The convergence parameter $R_{mt}K_{max}$, which controls the size of the basis set for the wave function, is set to 7.0, where $R_{mt}$ is the smallest atomic sphere radius and $K_{max}$ is the maximum value of the reciprocal lattice vector used in the plane wave expansion. To account for the strong on-site Coulomb interaction ($U$) presented in the localized $3d$ electrons of Cr atom, the GGA+$U$ method within the WIEN2k code was adopted. The effective Coulomb exchange interaction $U_{eff}=U-J$ is used where the $U$ is the Coulomb repulsion parameter and $J$ is an approximation of the Stoner exchange parameter. In the present work, the screened parameter $U$=1.95 eV, $J$=0.95 eV are chosen for Cr $3d$ electrons [22], which has been demonstrated to be reasonably accurate for Cr-based MAX compounds.

## 3. Results and discussion

Cr₂GeC crystals have a hexagonal structure ($P63/mmc$ space group) as sketched in Fig. 1(a). The optimized lattice constants for Cr₂GeC by GGA (LDA) are 2.925 Å (2.864 Å) and 12.024 Å (11.696 Å), respectively. Comparing to the experimental data [2], GGA results are closer to the experimental values. Thus, GGA is adopted in the following calculations. In order to get the ground state magnetic configurations of Cr₂GeC, the total energies for the three possible magnetic orderings: nonmagnetic (NM), ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) were calculated. The total energy differences between AFM state, NM state and FM state are only 4 meV and 5 meV respectively. Such small difference implies that the ground state for Cr₂GeC based on total energy data is difficult to pin down.

To gain further insight on the microscopic mechanisms of magnetic coupling in Cr₂GeC, we calculated the site-resolved spin magnetic moments (SSM, see Table 1). The SMM at each site is obtained from the difference between the spin-up and spin-down electrons within the muffin-tin sphere. Table 1 shows that the main portion of the SMM is from Cr, indicating that the $Cr^{2+}$ lies in the high-spin state with a sizable net moment of $1.667\ \mu_{B}$. This is schematically illustrated in Fig. 1(b). As shown in Fig. 1(b), there is ferromagnetic (FM) coupling between two different Cr atoms in the Cr–C–Cr trilayer, and ferrimagnetic (FIM) coupling between two different Cr atoms Cr–Ge–Cr trilayer. This phenomenon has not been reported before and shows that the magnetic properties in MAX phase compounds involving late transition metals could be very intriguing and warrant further investigations.

Moreover, there are small negative spin moments on the Ge atoms. This spin-polarization in non-magnetic atoms Ge can be explained by the spin delocalization mechanism. The Cr atoms in the two subnearest neighboring layers along the c-axis are connected by the Ge atoms. Therefore there must be a spin delocalization from the Cr atoms toward the Ge atoms leading to a small positive SMM on Ge atoms. This clearly indicates that there exists an additional weak indirect exchange pathway via the Ge atoms in Cr–Ge–Cr trilayer. Similarly, the small negative spin populations on the C atoms can be explained by both the spin delocalization and spin polarization mechanism in the Cr–C–Cr trilayer. As shown in Fig. 1(b), the Cr–C–Cr trilayer of crystal are made of Cr octahedral coordinated to C, producing a cross-linked Cr–C network.

For further illumination of the magnetic properties of Cr₂GeC, the total density of states (TDOS) and the atom- and orbital-resolved partial density of states (PDOS) using GGA and GGA+$U$ approximations are presented in Fig. 2. The origin of magnetism is clearly shown to be from Cr $3d$ orbitals in GGA or GGA+$U$ for the ground state, which is in good agreement with other recent calculations [23,24]. For GGA calculation, the TDOS and PDOS show no spin split for the ground state. However, with GGA+$U$, the TDOS, PDOS of Cr $3d$ and PDOS of C $2p$ and Ge $2s$, $2p$ are clearly split [see Fig. 2(a')–(d')], indicating which that the ordered spin arrangement is formed by the exchange interactions. Fig. 2(a') also provides the evidence for a typical FIM phase for Cr₂GeC, owing to the large difference of TDOS between spin up and spin down channels, which is consistent with the magnetic moment calculations. ## 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have carried out a comparative study on the
structural, electronic and magnetic properties of Cr₂GeC based on
density functional theory within the GGA+$U$ formulism. The total
energy calculations show that Cr₂GeC have FIM ground state with
a sizable net spin magnetic moment. This surprising new result
will lead to the possibility of applying the layered magnetic
MAX phase compounds in electronics and spintronics areas. We
hope this work will stimulate more experimental and theoretical
research on these fascinating and technologically interesting
materials.
