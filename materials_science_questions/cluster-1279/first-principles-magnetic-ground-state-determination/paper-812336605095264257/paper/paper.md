# Magnetic dead layers in Fe films induced by a lattice mismatch at an interface

## Abstract

The magnetism of Fe thin films grown at room temperature on the Rh(0 0 1) surface was studied by angle- and spin-resolved photoemission, X-ray photoelectron diffraction, soft X-ray magnetic circular dichroism (XMCD), and band calculations using full-potential augmented plane wave method. On the Rh(0 0 1) surface Fe films start to grow in a FCT(0 0 1) structure with a lattice constant compressed along the direction perpendicular to the surface and the compression is gradually released as the film thickness increases. The characteristic growth mode of the Fe films on the Rh(0 0 1) surface results in the existence of magnetically dead 2 monolayers (ML) of Fe at the interface and of ferromagnetism over 2 ML, which are realized by the XMCD experiments measured at low temperature. The thickness dependence of the magnetic properties of the Fe films is consistent with the results of total energy calculations adopting the experimentally obtained interlayer spacing.

## 1. Introduction

It is known that in the Fe films grown epitaxially on a substrate with a large lattice mismatch, a strain at the interface causes elastic deformation of the films, i.e. the Poisson effect, and leads to a tetragonal structure. The strain at the interface is released during the film growth by the change of the lattice constant or by the dislocation at the interface, which modifies the structural and magnetic phases of the Fe films. It has been theoretically shown that fcc and fct Fe manifest diverse magnetic phases depending on the lattice constant [1–5].

$(a_0=3.80\ \mathring{A})$ with a relatively large lattice mismatch [6]. The Fe films on the Rh(001) surface grew epitaxially in a layer-by-layer mode. The $1\times1$ reflection high-energy electron diffraction (RHEED) patterns did not change up to 12 monolayers (ML), which indicated that the atomic spacing in the epitaxial layer was the same as that of the Rh(001) surface during the film growth. On the other hand, the interlayer spacing of the Fe films evaluated from X-ray photoelectron diffraction (XPD) experiments increases with increase of film thickness and reach a constant value above 6 ML. By angle-resolved photoelectron spectroscopy (ARPES), it was found that the electronic structure depends remarkably on the film thickness. Since photoelectron spectroscopy is a surface sensitive experiment probing the topmost few layers of the films, we concluded that the surface structure of the Fe/Rh(001) system changes from fct(001) at the thin end to distorted bcc(110) at 6 ML. As for the magnetism, it was found by spin-and angle-resolved photoelectron spectroscopy (SARPES) that the 4 ML Fe film was ferromagnetic (FM), while the results of soft X-ray magnetic circular dichroism (XMCD) measured at room temperature (RT) suggested the possible existence of magnetic dead layers in Fe films below 2 ML. In the following, we will investigate the relationship between the structure and magnetism during the film growth and the existence of the magnetic dead layers at the initial film growth.

## 2. Method of calculation

We have carried out band structure calculations by using full-potential linearized-augmented plane wave method. We have used the local exchange-correlation potentials in the generalized gradient approximation, which was reported to give a correct ground state [2]. As for the structural configurations, a four-atom fct unit cell was set in the calculations. We have used the WIEN97 code [7]. All the calculations were highly converged with the same parameters of a recent study [4]. The total energies of three different magnetic phases, i.e. nonmagnetic (NM), antiferromagnetic (AF) and FM phases of fct Fe, were calculated.

## 3. Experimental

Experiments were carried out in an ultra-high vacuum chamber with surface analysis instruments for RHEED, low-energy electron diffraction, and Auger electron spectroscopy. The base pressure was less than $1\times10^{-10}$ mbar. The clean and flat Rh(001) surface was obtained with the same manner as described elsewhere [6]. Fe films were deposited at RT on the Rh(001) surface using an e-beam evaporation source under pressure below $2\times10^{-10}$ mbar. The typical deposition rate was 0.2 ML/min.

XMCD experiments were carried out at BL-11A of the Photon Factory in KEK [8]. The circularly polarized synchrotron radiation was used for measuring soft X-ray absorption spectra of the Fe 2p absorption edges by means of the total electron yield method. The circular polarization and incident angle of light were 55% and $60^\circ$, respectively. The samples were magnetized along the Rh[110] axis using a Helmholz coil and the magnetization direction was reversed to obtain XMCD spectra. The sample temperature was 97 K and RT. We have also measured the XMCD spectra when the incident angle of light was $0^\circ$ and the sample was magnetized along the Rh[001] axis. In this case, no XMCD signal was observed at every film thickness, which indicates that the Fe/Rh(001) system does not show out-of-plane magnetization.

As mentioned above, the interlayer spacing of the Fe films is extremely compressed in the initial growth stage due to the large strain at the Fe/Rh(0 0 1) interface. This strain is relaxed as the film thickness increases by the increase of the interlayer spacing, which changes the atomic volume of Fe. It has been reported that different magnetic phases are energetically favored by different atomic volumes and FM phase is stable when the average atomic volume exceeds a certain value [1]. Thus, we have estimated the atomic volume of Fe for various film thickness by using the experimentally obtained interlayer spacing of the Fe/Rh(0 0 1) system [6]. The result is shown in Fig. 1. In the Fe films below 3 ML, the atomic volume of Fe does not exceed the threshold for FM phase of bulk fcc or bulk bcc Fe indicated by gray bold lines. This result implies the existence of the magnetic dead layers in Fe films below 2 ML.

To confirm the above result, we have evaluated the spin magnetic moment, $m_{\text{spin}}$, of Fe atoms by applying the sum rules [9] to the XMCD spectra measured at Fe 2p absorption edges at 97 K and at RT. The results are shown in Fig. 2. The expectation value of the magnetic dipole operator was neglected. In Fig. 2, $m_{\text{spin}}$ at RT is almost constant and as large as that of bulk bcc Fe above 6 ML, which indicates that the Curie temperature $T_C$ of the Fe films above 6 ML is sufficiently high to realize the saturation magnetization at RT. In the 2–6 ML region, $m_{\text{spin}}$ at 97 K is larger than that at RT and decreases as the film thickness decreases. For Fe films below 2 ML,$m_{\text{spin}}$ equals to $0\mu_{\text{B}}$ at 97 K as well as at RT. These results verify the existence of the magnetic dead layers of Fe films on the Rh(0 0 1) surface. The thickness at which the Fe film loses magnetization obtained in this work is more reliable than those in previous works, because the thickness was obtained by using the Fe films over-coated with Cu [10] or with rather fast deposition rate [11].

Thus, we conclude that the large mismatch at the Fe/Rh(0 0 1) interface leads to the reduction of the atomic volume of Fe and hence the existence of the magnetic dead layers of the Fe films.
