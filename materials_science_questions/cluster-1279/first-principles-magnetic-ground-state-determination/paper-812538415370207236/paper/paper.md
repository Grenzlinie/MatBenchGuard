# First-Principles Study on the Potential of Monolayer $Ti_2N$ as an Adsorbent for Dissolved $H_2$ and $C_2H_2$ Gases in Oil
Xin He, Yingang Gui,* Jufang Xie,* and Qiaomei Li

**ABSTRACT:** The potential of the $Ti_2N$ monolayer in removing $CH_4$, $C_2H_2$, and $H_2$ mixture gases from transformer oil has been studied based on first-principles calculation. By comparing the total energy of ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic structures of $Ti_2N$, it is confirmed that the energy of ferromagnetic structure is the lowest. On this basis, the adsorption effects of the $Ti_2N$ monolayer on these three gases and the electronic properties before and after adsorption were investigated. $Ti_2N$ monolayer provides a large number of Ti atom active sites with abundant d electrons for adsorption. Its adsorption energies to $H_2$ and $C_2H_2$ are $-2.964$ and $-5.527$ eV, respectively. For $C_2H_2$ adsorption, the sp hybridization within the molecule is destroyed, and then turn into $sp^2$ hybridization according to the description of electron deformation density. In addition, only weak interaction exists between $Ti_2N$ monolayer and $CH_4$, and the adsorption energy is only $-0.214$ eV with almost no charge transfer. The investigation shows that $Ti_2N$ can be used as a potential adsorbent for $H_2$ and $C_2H_2$ or as a potential material for the purifying $CH_4$ from a $H_2/ CH_4/C_2H_2$ mixture.

**KEYWORDS:** $Ti_2N$ monolayer, DFT, adsorbent, dissolved gas in oil, first-principles study, acetylene

## 1. INTRODUCTION
The long-distance transmission of electricity requires the aid of high or ultrahigh voltage, and then the power transformer converts the ultrahigh voltage to an appropriate lower level for social electricity consumption. $^{1,2}$ For this reason, the stable operation of the power transformer is an important factor that is directly related to the stability of the power transmission system. Currently, most of transformers in use are oil-immersed in which the oil is mainly composed of hydrocarbon. However, partial discharge and overheating are inevitable in transformer operation. The energy released by these faults will cause $C-H$ and $C-C$ bonds breaking of insulation oil and produces a small number of H atoms and unstable free radicals of hydrocarbons. $^{3-5}$ These H atoms and radicals recombine into $H_2$ and low-molecular hydrocarbon gases, such as $CH_4$, $C_2H_4$, $C_2H_2$, and $C_2H_6.^{6,7}$ According to different types of internal insulation faults, different characteristic gases may be produced, mainly $C_2H_2$, $H_2$, $CH_4$, etc. These products will accelerate the aging of the insulation oil, affecting the insulation of equipment. To ensure the strong insulation strength of insulation oil, it is necessary to remove these gases effectively. On the other hand, based on the determination of the content and proportion of these main characteristic decomposition gases, the fault type of insulation equipment can be identified, namely, DGA (dissolved gas analysis). $^{51}$

Materials with a few atoms thick show special surficial properties compared with solid materials, even if they have the same molecular composition. $^{8,9}$ In recent years, two-dimensional materials have attracted increasing attention in many fields because of their unique properties. $^{10-19}$ As a promising material, Mxenes exhibit many excellent properties in energy storage, environment, and other fields $^{20-27}$ including the gas sensor or capture field in recent years. $^{28-31}$ Mxenes can be achieved by removing the A atom from MAX phase. Here, "M" is a transition metal such as Ti and V, "A" is an A-group element, and "X" is C or N. As far as we know, some Mxenes monolayer has been experimentally verified. $^{32-37}$ It usually ends with $-F$, $-OH$, and $-H$ functional groups on its surface terminal when Mxenes are synthesized. $^{33}$ Although these Mxenes with functional groups have been proved to have excellent adsorption properties for heavy metals and some organics, $^{38-45}$ intrinsic Mxenes may perform better in some areas. $^{33,46,47}$

$Ti_2N$ as Mxenes with formation energy of 3.15 eV per formula unit indicates that it can be stripped from the corresponding MAX phase. Due to the chemical activity of $Ti_2N$, $-H$, $-F$, and $-O$ are usually attached to the terminals. But if $Ti_2N$ can be used in the right environment, it may show excellent performance in some applications. For example, first-

## 2. COMPUTATION DETAIL

All results were calculated based on DFT and were done in $Dmol^3$ in this work. $^{52}$ The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof (PBE) function, which does not account for any dispersion, was chosen to calculate geometry optimization and energy. $^{53−55}$ The dispersion correction is performed using the Grimme (DFT- D2) $^{56}$ method to avoid the potential errors about physical adsorption. Energy convergence accuracy, max force, and max displacement were set to $10^{-5}$ Ha, $2 \times 10^{-3}$ Ha/Å, and $5 \times 10^{-3}$ Å, respectively. Double numerical plus polarization (DNP) was set as the basis set. $^{57}$ Self-consistent field convergence accuracy was set to $10^{-6}$ Ha, and the DIIS field was set to 6 to accelerate convergence. Smearing was set to 0.1 eV for frontier molecular orbital calculation only. $^{58}$ A Monkhorst-Pack grid of $13 \times 13 \times 1$ was carried out to perform Brillouin zone integrations for geometry optimization, and a denser $19 \times 19 \times 1$ grid was used for density of states (DOS) calculation. $^{59}$ The Hirshfeld method charge population was also calculated, and a negative value means the charge is transferred from the layer to the molecule. Meanwhile, to accurately calculate the magnetic structure, the electron spin is not restricted.

To avert interlayer interactions, a $3 \times 3 \times 1$ supercell with a 30 Å vacuum layer was constructed. The energy in this work is calculated by the following formula

$$
E_{\text{bind}} = E_{\text{Ti}_2\text{N}} - M{\cdot}E_{\text{N}} - 2M{\cdot}E_{\text{Ti}} \tag{1}
$$

$$
E_{\text{ad}} = E_{\text{gas}/\text{Ti}_2\text{N}} - E_{\text{gas}} - E_{\text{Ti}_2\text{N}} \tag{2}
$$

$$
E_{\text{DFT-D}} = E_{\text{gas}/\text{Ti}_2\text{N}}^{\text{DFT-D}} - E_{\text{Ti}_2\text{N}}^{\text{DFT-D}} - E_{\text{gas}}^{\text{DFT-D}} \tag{3}
$$

Here, $E_{\text{bind}}$, $E_{\text{ad}}$, and $E_{\text{DFT-D}}$ are binding energy, adsorption energy, and dispersion energy, respectively. $E_{\text{Ti2N}}$ represents the total energy of the $Ti_2N$ supercell, $M$ is the number of N atoms in the supercell. $E_{\text{N}}$ and $E_{\text{Ti}}$ are the energy of the N and Ti atom in their $2 \times 2 \times 2$ supercell. $E_{\text{gas/Ti2N}}$ and $E_{\text{gas}}$ in formula 2 represent the total system energy after adsorption and individual gas molecular energy, respectively. For formula 3, each term represents its corresponding DFT-D correction energy. The method is not unique, and other researchers may utilize another more appropriate method to calculate the dispersion.

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As a control, we calculated the adsorption effect of $Ti_2NT_2$ (T = O, OH, and F) on three gases. As shown in Figure 1, the adsorption effect of $Ti_2NT_2$ on the three gases is limited to physical adsorption. For the $H_2$ molecule, three $Ti_2NT_2$ exhibited weak adsorption effect (adsorption energy less than −0.1 eV) and for the other two molecules, the adsorption effect of three $Ti_2NT_2$ is as follows: $Ti_2N(OH)_2 > Ti_2NO_2 > Ti_2NF_2$.

### 3.1. Geometric Optimization.
$Ti_2N$ is a 2D metallic material with a thickness of three atoms. It is composed of a N atomic layer and two Ti atomic layers where N atoms are sandwiched in two Ti atomic layers and interleaved with Ti atoms, as shown in Figure 2a,b. The length of N−Ti bonds is $2.074 \mathring{A}$, and the calculated binding energy of $Ti_2N$ is −8.43 eV per formula unit. The large negative binding energy means that $Ti_2N$ monolayer stability is possible. The stability of $Ti_2N$ can be also theoretically verified by combining with the calculation of phonon dispersion from the study of Wang et al.⁴⁸ In addition, density of states (DOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) are also shown in Figure 2, where the dashed line represents the Fermi level. In Figure 2c, the DOS curves are numerically large as they span the Fermi level, and its spin-up and spin-down curves are asymmetric as expected at this position. It means that $Ti_2N$ is physically conductive and magnetic. As shown in Figure 2d, in the range from −2 to 2 eV, the N 2p orbital contributes little to DOS while DOS is dominated by Ti 3d orbital, hence, the conductivity of $Ti_2N$ is determined by the Ti atom. In addition, Ti 3d and N 2p orbitals are strongly hybridized within the range of −7 to −4 eV, which further proves the stability of $Ti_2N$. Strong orbital hybridization inevitably affects electron distribution. In Figure 3, the electron density around the N atom increases while that around both layers of Ti decreases. This is consistent with the electronegativity of N and Ti.

3.1.1. Adsorption of $H_2$. When the $H_2$ gas molecule was adsorbed on the surface of $Ti_2N$, the bond was broken and the distance between H atoms was extended to 2.973 Å, which is 4 times the length of the bond in pristine $H_2$. The H atoms were distributed above the top layer of $Ti_2N$ and directly above the Ti atoms of the bottom layer. That is, in the top view, H atom overlaps with the bottom Ti atom, and each H atom is surrounded by three Ti atoms at the top layer, as shown in Figure 5a. It should be noted in particular that we have compared different adsorption sites of each molecule, and the adsorption site as shown in Figure 5 is the most likely stable adsorption site. In addition, the distance between each H atom and the three Ti atoms around it is almost identical, ranging from 1.978 to 1.984 Å and the adsorption energy is −2.964 eV ($E_{\text{DFT-D}} = -0.133$ eV) with a charge transfer of −0.227 $e$. The fracture of the bond of $H_2$ and the large adsorption energy indicate that the adsorption process is a strong chemical adsorption. The release of so much energy is attributed to the fact that there are five Ti atoms on the surface of $Ti_2N$ acting on $H_2$, unlike other traditional adsorbent materials that have only one or two active sites on a molecule.

It should be noted that although $Ti_2N$ surface exerts such a strong effect on $H_2$ molecule, the shortest distance between the H atom and Ti atom is more than the sum of their covalent radii (+0.298 Å). We speculate that since the H atom is located at the center above the three Ti atoms, it is subjected to three symmetrical forces. Instead of bonding with one Ti atom, H atom is attracted to the surface by three Ti atoms at the same time. This conclusion is consistent with Li et al. $^{61}$ When all adsorption sites are occupied by H atoms, Kubas-type interaction exists between $Ti_2N$ and other $H_2$. In addition, according to Li et al., the maximum hydrogen storage capacity of $Ti_2N$ is 8.555 wt %, of which the reversible capacity is 3.422 wt %. To facilitate comparison with the other two gases, the absolute values of charge transfer, and the adsorption energy of those three adsorbed gases are summarized in Figure 5.

3.1.2. Adsorption of $CH_4$. $CH_4$ is attracted to a position on the surface of $Ti_2N$, which from the top view is the same as the H atom mentioned above, at the center of the three Ti atoms on the top layer. But the difference is that the adsorption distance (3.075 Å) is much longer. Figure 5b clearly shows the difference in adsorption distance with the other adsorbed gases. Geometrically, the C−H bonds length of $CH_4$ before and after adsorption did not change significantly. The bond length is between 1.098 and 1.103 Å with a deformation rate of less than 1%. This suggests that $Ti_2N$ is not exerting much force on $CH_4$. Figure 6 also shows the corresponding numerical data and the intuitive comparison with other gas molecules. The adsorption energy of $Ti_2N$ on $CH_4$ is −0.214 eV ($E_{\text{DFT-D}} = -0.135$ eV) with a charge transfer of 0.047 $e$. Therefore, it can be inferred that the adsorption of $Ti_2N$ on $CH_4$ is physical adsorption from both the geometric structure and the adsorption energy. We speculate that this may be related to the absence of lone-pair electrons in $CH_4$ and the inability of $Ti_2N$ to break the C−H covalent bond.

3.1.3. Adsorption of $C_2H_2$. $Ti_2N$ has the strongest adsorption effect on $C_2H_2$ showing adsorption on the C atom. The adsorption energy is −5.527 eV ($E_{\text{DFT-D}} = -0.407$ eV), and the charge transfer is −0.281 $e$, which is nearly 6 times that of $CH_4$. We infer that chemical adsorption is involved between them. Since chemical bonds still exist between C atoms after $C_2H_2$ was adsorbed, the position of C atoms being adsorbed slightly deviates from the center of the nearby Ti atoms, as shown in Figure 5c. The adsorption distance of the $Ti_2N$ surface to two C atoms is about 2.185 and 2.192 Å, respectively. When $C_2H_2$ is adsorbed on $Ti_2N$ surface, it is no longer a linear molecule. In addition, the distance between C atoms is stretched by 0.232 Å, but it is still shorter than the C−C bond. Therefore, the original sp hybrid orbitals of C atoms are broken and the $sp^2$ hybrid orbitals are suspected to be generated.

3.2.1. Adsorption of $\boldsymbol{\text{H}_2}$. The DOS of the adsorption system near the Fermi level changes significantly after $\text{H}_2$ is adsorbed on the surface of $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$, as shown in Figure 8a1. Comparing with the DOS of isolated $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$, the peak of the Fermi level increases and a new peak appears around $-4$ eV, which increases the probability of more electrons entering the conduction band and improves the conductivity. In the previous results, we have known that the DOS in the Fermi level is mainly related to the Ti 3d orbital, so we suspect that the increase of the DOS at the Fermi level is related to the change of Ti 3d orbital. PDOS in Figure 8a2 confirms this assumption, while Ti 3d and H 1s orbitals are hybridized around $-4$ eV, resulting in a corresponding new peak on DOS. As shown in Figure 8a3, the DOS after $\text{H}_2$ is adsorbed moves to the left due to the increase of the Fermi level, and the state splits into lower density states in the range of $-7$ to $-4.5$ eV, which weakens the electron localization. It further confirms the obvious electron transfer and interaction between $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$.

3.2.2. Adsorption of $\boldsymbol{\text{CH}_4}$. The DOS of the $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$ system is basically the same before and after adsorption, especially near the Fermi energy level. The details are depicted in Figure 8b1. The visible change between the two DOS curves in Figure 8b1 occurs around $-5.3$ and $-13$ eV. Figure 8b3 can explain that this difference is caused by $\text{CH}_4$. The above electron properties indicate that there is no significant charge transfer and interaction between $\text{CH}_4$ and $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$.This is also illustrated by the fact that there is no hybridization between Ti 3d and H 1s orbitals, as shown in Figure 8b2.

3.2.3. Adsorption of $\boldsymbol{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2}$. Comparing with $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{CH}_4$, the change of DOS is the most obvious after $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ is adsorbed. DOS increases at the Fermi level and two new peaks appear around $-3.8$ and $-3$ eV, as shown in Figure 8c1. The appearance of the two peaks can be seen from Figure 8c3 as the result of DOS splitting of $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$. The DOS at the Fermi level is divided into two DOS peaks of C 2p orbital, and it is known from Figure 8c2 that C 2p and Ti 3d orbitals are hybridized at these two energy levels. The DOS splitting and orbital hybridization of gas molecules confirm the large charge transfer and strong interaction between $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$ and $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$.

3.2.4. Frontier Molecular Orbital. Due to the weak interaction between $\text{CH}_4$ and $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$, $\text{CH}_4$ has little effect on the distribution of frontier molecular orbital of the adsorption system, as depicted in Figure 9. The HOMO and LUMO of both $\text{CH}_4$ and $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$ are distributed on the Ti atomic layers. For $\text{H}_2$ adsorption system, the HOMO and LUMO of the adsorption surface are concentrated in the periphery of the $\text{H}_2$ molecule, rather than evenly distributed on the whole surface. It suggests that the surrounding of the $\text{H}_2$ molecule becomes a relatively highly reactive region. In the $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ adsorption system, the gas molecule interacts with the $\text{Ti}_2\text{N}$ surface, which makes the electrons in HOMO participate in the bonding. Similar to the $\text{H}_2$ system, the HOMO region around it is relatively reduced.

## 4. CONCLUSIONS

In this work, the adsorption performance of the pristine $Ti_2N$ monolayer toward three gases, namely $H_2$, $CH_4$, and $C_2H_2$, was investigated by first-principles calculations. The pristine $Ti_2N$ monolayer has chemical adsorption with $H_2$ and $C_2H_2$, while only physical adsorption with $CH_4$.

In the $H_2/Ti_2N$ adsorption system, the bond in $H_2$ is broken, and the two H atoms are absorbed by three Ti atoms around and located in the center of these three atoms. The

charge near the three Ti atoms is extracted onto H atoms. The adsorption energy of −2.964 eV and the charge transfer implies a strong interaction between the $Ti_2N$ monolayer and $H_2$ molecule. In the $C_2H_2/Ti_2N$ system, the interaction between $Ti_2N$ and $C_2H_2$ is stronger with a charge transfer of 0.289 $e$ and adsorption energy of −5.527 eV. One $\pi$ bond in the $C_2H_2$ molecule is broken and the C atoms turn to $sp^2$ hybridization. At the same time, the high adsorption energy means that cycling performance of $Ti_2N$ for $H_2$ and $C_2H_2$ will be limited, and the subsequent adsorption amount may be reduced compared with the previous several times under the conventional desorption environment. Therefore, environments such as ultraviolet radiation and high temperature may be required to enhance the cycling performance. When $CH_4$ adsorb on $Ti_2N$, the molecular geometry hardly changes, and the adsorption energy is only −0.214 eV. In addition, the density of states, the electron deformation density, and the frontier molecular orbital of $CH_4/Ti_2N$ all indicate only a weak interaction between them. According to these results, $Ti_2N$ monolayer can be used as a potential adsorbent for $H_2$ and $C_2H_2$ or as a material for the purifying $CH_4$ from a $H_2/CH_4/$$C_2H_2$ mixture. Meanwhile, the microscopic adsorption mechanism of the $Ti_2N$ monolayer to $C_2H_2$ was revealed, which may be helpful to guide the follow-up experiments. For a practical experiment, due to the high activity of bare $Ti_2N$, it is easily functionalized by the gas molecules such as $H_2O$ and $O_2$. It is worth noting how to eliminate the terminal functional groups and keep them pure. A high annealing temperature is very likely necessary.
