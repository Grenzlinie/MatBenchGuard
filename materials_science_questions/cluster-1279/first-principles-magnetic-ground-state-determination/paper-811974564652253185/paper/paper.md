# Electronic Structure and Interlayer Exchange Coupling in Fe/Cr Superlattices.

PACS. 73.20D - Electron states in low-dimensional structures (inc. quantum wells, superlattices, layer structures and intercalation compounds).

Abstract. - We use self-consistent total-energy supercell band calculations to study the electronic structure of b.c.c. Fe/Cr magnetic multilayers. We find that, for $n$ layers of Cr sandwiches between three layers of Fe, alternate Fe layers prefer ferromagnetic order for $n=3$ and antiferromagnetic order for $n=4,5,6$, and 7. The magnetic coupling is shown to result from an interplay between direct «d-d» hybridization of Fe and Cr atoms, and indirect exchange coupling through the «sp» electrons.

An open question in the field of magnetic multilayers is the origin of the exchange coupling between magnetic layers across a nonmagnetic layer. A particular interesting system is Fe/Cr multilayers or sandwiches for which a strong antiferromagnetic (AFM) coupling of the Fe layers across the Cr interlayer has been observed for a broad range of Cr thicknesses [1-4]. These AFM couplings are too strong to be accounted for by dipolar interactions and have to be ascribed to exchange interactions through Cr. For the samples of Grünberg et al. [1], Baibich et al. [2], or Krebs et al. [3] with relatively thin Cr layers (below about $18\,\mathring{A}$), the interlayer coupling was found to be AFM for all the Cr thicknesses above $4\,\mathring{A}$. In the more recent experiments of Parkin et al. [4], samples with thicker Cr layers have also been studied and the interlayer coupling is found to become ferromagnetic (FM) for Cr thicknesses around $20\,\mathring{A}$ and $35\,\mathring{A}$. These latter results imply an oscillation between FM and AFM coupling, with a period as large as $(15\div20)\,\mathring{A}$.

Up to now, two theoretical approaches have been used. A standard RKKY-like approach [5,6] using only the «sp-d» hybridization, and tight-binding models [7] taking into accound only the direct «d-d» hybridization. Both predict an oscillating dependence on the Cr thickness with a period close to the lattice parameter. Such short periods are in

contradiction with the experimental observations. Approximate agreement has been obtained only with more sophisticated models [6,8] involving the interface roughness [6] or the existence of a Van-Hove singularity in the density of states [8]. On the other hand, several numerical calculations [9] have pointed out the unstable character of the electronic magnetic state of thin Cr layers and their sensitivity to the proximity of another metal. Recent experiments have confirmed the strong perturbation of thin Cr layers by proximity effects [10]. Therefore, an *ab initio* calculation of the electronic structure of the Fe/Cr system including both «*sp-d*» and «*d-d*» hybridization is necessary for a realistic theoretical approach. In this letter, we present a calculation of the electronic structure of b.c.c. (001) Fe/Cr superlattices and we derive the interlayer coupling by comparing the total energy for AFM and FM arrangements of the Fe layers.

Our self-consistent energy calculations use the augmented spherical wave (ASW) formalism and the local spin density approximation [11]. We describe the Fe(001)/Cr(001) superlattice as a periodic system composed of cells extending over $2m$ Fe and $2n$ Cr(001) atomic layers. Each atom of the unit cell represents an entire atomic plane in which all moments have a parallel alignment. We determine and discuss below 1) the total energy for both FM and AFM arrangements of the Fe layers, 2) the magnetic moments of each atom of the cell, and 3) the local densities of states (DOS). We interpret the results by a direct comparison of the FM and AFM configurations.

We now turn to the b.c.c. (001) Fe/Cr superlattice. The unit cell dimensions are $2.829\ \mathring{A}$ along [100] and [010] and $2.875(n+m)\ \mathring{A}$ along [001]. Here, we assume that the atomic spacing in the plane of the layers is fixed by the GaAs substrate epitaxy as experimentally realized [12] and that the atomic spacing in the perpendicular direction is an average of the lattice parameters of elemental Fe and Cr. To obtain reliable energy differences between the FM and AFM orderings, the unit cell of the AFM structure including $2(m+n)$ atomic layers is also used for the FM structure to insure identical Brillouin zone sampling. We find that the structure and the magnetic moments of the iron layers are only weakly sensitive to their thickness and that the central Fe layer practically recovers its bulk state with only three Fe monolayers. We therefore only present $m=3$ calculations, and vary $n$ from 3 to 7.

In fig. 1, we show the total energy differences normalized to one atom $\Delta\overline{E}=$ $=(E_{\text{AF}}-E_{\text{F}})/(n+m)$ as a function of Cr thickness. From our definition of the energy difference a positive (negative) $\Delta\overline{E}$ indicates a preference for FM (AFM) order in the cell. Our numerical results show that the superlattice with 3 monolayers of Cr ($n=3$) has a stable FM configuration. In contrast, those with thicker Cr interlayers ($4\leqslant n\leqslant7$) have an AFM stable configuration with a maximum in the energy difference at $n=5$. The quantitative discrepancy could be partly due to the roughness of the experimental superlattices. Also, for very small thicknesses of Cr, a quantitative comparison between experiments and theory is made questionable by the possible existence of bridges and islands which favour a direct coupling between the Fe layers. In order to understand qualitatively the physical origin of such magnetic couplings, let us now discuss the magnetic moments distribution, the relative role of the Cr and Fe electronic energies, and of the «$sp$-$d$» and «$d$-$d$» hybridization.

The local magnetic moments for FM and AFM configurations for $n=3$ to 7 (fig. $2a$), for reason of space only $n=3$, 5 and 6 have been shown) exhibit a number of significant observations: 1) the magnetic behaviour of the Fe layers is almost identical for all structures and the iron moment at the interface, typically about $1.8\mu_{\text{B}}$, is less than that for bulk Fe, $2.15\mu_{\text{B}}$. This reduced moment is a consequence of Fe/Cr «$d$» band hybridization between Fe and Cr «$d$» states leading to a larger band width for Fe. This result is in agreement with the 30% reduction of the magnetic hyperfine field measured by conversion electron Mössbauer spectroscopy for the first Fe interface layer in Fe/Cr/Fe sandwiches [14]. On the other hand, the hybridization narrows the Cr band width, which accounts for the slight increase of the interfacial Cr moment in all cases. 2) The coupling between the Fe and Cr at the interfaces is always AFM. This is consistent with the well-known results for a Cr impurity in Fe, where the Cr atom is coupled antiferromagnetically with its Fe neighbours. Moreover, in the

The magnetic behaviour of Cr is strongly dependent on the parity of the number of atomic planes of Cr and their respective magnetic ordering. When the orientation of the Cr moments at the interfaces—fixed by the orientation of the magnetization in the adjacent Fe layers—is consistent with the development of a spin density wave (SDW) $(^{1})$ having a period equal to the lattice parameter and propagating along [001], the magnetic moments are close to their value in bulk chromium $(0.6 \mu_{B})$ and form such a SDW. This is the case for the FM (AFM) configuration when $n$ is even (odd). In contrast, when the arrangement of the moments of the Fe layers is incompatible with the development of a SDW in the Cr interlayer, the Cr moments are drastically reduced. In particular, for $n$ odd with an AFM configuration, the moment of the central Cr atom is practically equal to zero: the Fe/Cr structure minimizes its total energy by quenching the Cr moments. 5) It can be thought a priori that the ground state is obtained when the Fe magnetic order is compatible with the development of the Cr SDW. Such an assumption is satisfied in TB «d-d» calculation and, in the present case, for $n=3,4$ and 6. However it is not satisfied for $n=5$ and 7. This suggests that the coupling energies result from a subtle interplay between «d-d» and «sp-d» hybridization which will be discussed below.

In fig. 2b), we have first plotted the total energy difference between the AFM and FM arrangement, $\Delta E(i)=E_{AF}(i)-E_{F}(i)$ , for all inequivalent sites $i$ of the unit cell-excepted the Madelung energy term related to the interatomic interactions since $\Delta E_{Mad}(F-AF)<$ <10⁻⁴ Ryd. These results can be summarized as follows: 1) the Fe layers tend to stabilize the AFM coupling except for $n=3$ . The Fe contribution to the AFM arrangement increases monotonously with interlayer Cr thickness. 2) The chromium layers have a more complex behaviour depending on its thickness, each site tending to favour the FM arrangement except for $n=5$ . This shows that the Cr electronic structure is strongly dependent on the local environment and cannot be considered as bulklike in contrast with assumptions which are often used [6].

We have to underline that the interpretation in terms of band contribution, which is a one-electron energy sum containing double-counting terms [11], is based on a non-self-consistent calculation. Then, it should only reveal qualitative tendencies reminiscent of the exchange coupling. Our results are summarized in fig. 3-I) and 3-II). These figures show the differences in the «sp» and «d» $\Delta N_{\text{F-AF}}$ for Fe and Cr as a result of the above spin flipping. As shown in fig. 3-Ia), the difference $\Delta N_{\text{F-AF}}$ for $n=3$ exhibits a shift in Fe «d» states to lower (more binding) energies with relatively no shifts in the «sp» states. Likewise, fig. 3-IIa) shows no significant shifts for the Cr states. This surplus of Fe «d» states at low energies for the FM configuration leads to the stabilization of the $n=3$ FM ordering. The low-energy surplus of states in the FM arrangement decreases with interlayer Cr thickness (fig. 3-Ib)) and vanishes almost completely for the largest spacer thickness $(n=7)$ (fig. 3-Ic)). This «d» contribution is originated by the overlapping of «d» orbitals: the interaction between Fe atoms in different layers across an interlayer of chromium is transmitted via the «d» electrons of Cr. This contribution is FM for $n=3$ and falls off with increasing Cr thickness. This tendency can be correlated to the well-established antiferromagnetic behaviour of the half-filled b.c.c. band of pure metals [16] by noting the decrease in the average number of electrons per cell with growing Cr thickness.

The origin of the largest energy difference favouring the AFM configuration for the $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} / \mathrm{Cr}_{5}$ is clearly understood in the view of fig. $3 b$ (Ib) for iron and IIb) for chromium) where $\Delta N_{\text{F-AF}}$ exhibits a sharp «sp» charge density peak related to a surplus of «sp» states at the bottom of the Cr band. This peak which also appears in the «d» curve of fig. $3 b$ (Ib) for iron and IIb) for chromium) results from the «sp-d» hybridization. Therefore, when the «sp» electrons play a role, the indirect coupling via the conduction electrons is AFM and gives rise to the AFM energetic contribution of all Cr sites for $n=5$ observed in fig. $2 b)$. Wang et al. [6] have obtained a similar behaviour by using only the mixing interaction between the iron atoms at the interface with the conduction electrons of Cr. However, our calculation shows that the magnetic coupling cannot be attributed only to this mixing but results from an interplay with other terms involving only the «3d» band discussed above.

The thickness range we have investigated is too narrow to probe the existence of the long-period oscillations as a function of Cr thickness observed by Parkin *et al.* [4] for much thicker layers. However, as we found that, except for $n=3$, the coupling is via the «sp-d» hybridization, it is reminiscent of RKKY-type interaction and compatible with an oscillating behaviour. Moreover, in the conventional RKKY interaction, the oscillation comes from an integration over the whole conduction band. Therefore, the energy range affected by the «sp-d» mixing effect seen in our curves (fig. $3b$ ($Ib$) for iron and $IIb$) for chromium)) is very small and can be at the origin of such long period of the oscillations.

In summary, we have shown that the AFM coupling between Fe layers separated by Cr interlayer is the most stable magnetic configuration in $\mathrm{Fe}_{3}/\mathrm{Cr}_{n}$ superlattices for $4\leqslant n\leqslant7$ but not for $n=3$. This is in agreement with the experimental data in this range. The magnetic couplings are strongly dependent on «sp-d» hybridization effects and result partly to electron states having a pronounced «sp» character.
