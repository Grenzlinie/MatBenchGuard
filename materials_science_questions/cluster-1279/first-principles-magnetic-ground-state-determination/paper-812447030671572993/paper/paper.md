# Electronic structure and interlayer magnetic couplings in metallic superlattices

Daniel Stoeffler, Kamel Ounadjela and François Gautier

I.P.C.M.S.-GEMME (U.M.R. 46 C.N.R.S.), Université Louis Pasteur, 4 rue Blaise Pascal, 67000 Strasbourg, France

The general trends for the magnetic interlayer couplings are calculated in a tight binding model from the electronic structure of perfect model superlattices $\mathrm{A}_{m} \mathrm{B}_{n}$ (Fe-V, Co-Pd, Co-Ru and Fe-Cr). Different features are deduced for non-magnetic, nearly ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic spacers B; they are discussed in relation with ab initio calculations and experimental results.

## 1. Introduction

Several experimental studies of the magnetic properties of metallic superlattices have shown recently the existence of long ranged exchange couplings between ferromagnetic layers (Fe, Co) separated by non-magnetic (Cu, Ru) or antiferromagnetic (Cr) layers [1-4]. In most cases, antiferromagnetic couplings have been observed in a wide range of the spacer thickness (5 to $30 \mathring{A}$). Large antiferromagnetic couplings have also been derived theoretically for $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{Cr}_{n}$ superlattices ($n=4$ to 7) from ab-initio Local Spin Density (LSD) calculations [5, 6] but the physical origin of the couplings, their order of magnitude which were found theoretically larger than experimentally and their range remain still unclear.

The aim of this paper is to use the simplicity of a tight-binding (TB) model to get a qualitative understanding of such couplings by studying the self-consistent electronic structure of several superlattices $\mathrm{A}_{m} \mathrm{B}_{n}$ which have been experimentally realized based either on a bcc ((001) $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{Cr}_{n}$ [1] and $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{~V}_{n}$ [2]), a fcc ((111) $\mathrm{Co}_{4} \mathrm{Pd}_{n}$ [7]) or a hcp ((001) $\mathrm{Co}_{4} \mathrm{Ru}_{n}$ and $\mathrm{Co}_{6} \mathrm{Ru}_{n}$ [3]) lattice. The previous systems must be considered as models for a study of the couplings between ferromagnetic layers separated by non-magnetic, nearly magnetic or antiferromagnetic layers. The TB calculations and a comparison with ab initio results ($\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{Cr}_{n}$) will allow to exhibit the relevant parameters which determine the electronic properties, the order of magnitude and the range of the magnetic interlayer couplings at zero kelvin. We have obtained three different behaviours for the variation of the magnetic couplings with the spacer thickness: (i) Friedel like oscillations (for Fe-V and Co-Ru) becoming small ($\leq 1$ meV/atom) for $n \geq 4$, (ii) an exponential decrease for small thickness and Friedel like oscillations for larger $n$ values (for Co-Pd), (iii) periodic oscillations for $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{Cr}_{n}$ according to the parity of $n$, the amplitude of this oscillation being equal to the energy of constrained magnetic walls in the chromium layers at equal distances from the iron interfaces.

## 2. Method of calculation

Here we use the real space recursion method and the Beer-Pettifor method for terminating the continuous fraction [8]. The sp electrons and their hybridization with the d electrons have been neglected as a first step, the magnetic distribution being mostly determined by d electrons. Such a method using only d electrons gives good results with a time gain of about a factor 20 to 100 in comparison with the ab-initio methods (LMTO, ASW,...). It has the advantage to allow to con-

trol easily the convergence of the iterative calcu-
lation. The Slater-Koster parameters between
$i$th neighbours distance from $\mathrm{R}_{i}$ were chosen to
reproduce the bandwidth given by Andersen with:

$$
\operatorname{dd}(\sigma, \pi, \delta)_{i}=(6,-4,1) \operatorname{dd} \delta_{1}\left(\mathrm{R}_{1} / \mathrm{R}_{i}\right)^{5}.
$$

We use the Shiba approximation for A-B hop-
ping integrals.

To treat self-consistently the charge transfers
$\Delta N_{i}$, which are not negligible in some cases, we
use a self-consistent scheme determining the d
band center on the $i$th site in terms of the intra-
and inter-site Coulomb corrections $\varepsilon_{\text {inter }}$ and of
the splitting due to the magnetic moment $M_{i}$:

$$
\varepsilon_{i, \sigma}=\varepsilon_{i}^{0}+U_{i} \Delta N_{i}-\varepsilon_{\text {inter }}-\sigma I_{i} M_{i} / 2,
$$

where $\varepsilon_{i}^{0}$ is the bare energy level, $U_{i}$ the Coulomb
integral, $I_{i}$ the exchange integral and $\sigma$ the spin
index $(\sigma= \pm 1)$. The calculation of the local den-
sities of states for this set of $\varepsilon_{i, \sigma}$ give new $\Delta N_{i}, M_{i}$
values: this procedure is iterated until the conver-
gence is achieved. Finally, the coupling is calcu-
lated from the total energy difference $\Delta E_{n}=$
$E_{n}(\mathrm{~F})-E_{n}(\mathrm{AF})$ between states with ferromagnet-
ically $(\mathrm{F})$ and antiferromagnetically (AF) coupled
$\mathrm{A}_{m}$ layers.

The essential source of error is the truncation
in the continuous fraction: we have estimated
that with 6 exact levels for bcc structure we have
an error of about $\pm 5 \mathrm{meV}$ and with 8 exact levels
for fcc and hcp structures we have an error of
about $\pm 2.5 \mathrm{meV}$ on the value of $\Delta E$. The com-
parison with the ab-initio calculations, for $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{Cr}_{5}$
shows that the moment distribution and the band
center $\varepsilon_{i, \sigma}$ distribution are in agreement with the
LSD ab-initio results so that the TB method is
much more than an crude approximation of the
ab-initio methods. The general features of the
magnetic distributions $M_{i}$ and couplings $\Delta E_{n}$
(order of magnitude, range) we report below are
also unsensitive to physical values of the TB
parameters (d band fillings $N_{i}^{0}, U_{i}, \varepsilon_{i}^{0}$ ) (see for
example fig. 3). The Coulomb integrals and the
bare energy levels lead usually to small charge
transfers.

### 3. Non-magnetic and nearly ferromagnetic spacers

Considering first the case of (001) $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{~V}_{n}$ with
$n=1, \ldots, 5$ for a superlattice with a perfect bcc
structure and an average lattice parameter $a=$
$2.948 \AA$, we find that $\Delta E_{n}$ decrease rapidly (for
$n \geq 3, \Delta E_{n}$ is of the order of the errors) and has
Friedel like oscillations (fig. 1a). This result is
consistent with a small polarization of the spacer
observed also for magnetic impurities diluted in a
non-magnetic metal: this polarization is short
ranged and the interlayer coupling via this spacer
too. The cancellation of the exchange integral $I$
shows that $\Delta E_{n}$ is not qualitatively modified.

For the case of (0001) $\mathrm{Co}_{m} \mathrm{Ru}_{n}$ with $m=4,6$
and $n=1, \ldots, 6$ we assumed that the hcp lattice
parameters $a$ and $c$ are given by

$$
a_{\mathrm{Ru}}=a_{\mathrm{Co}}=a_{\mathrm{Ru}}^{0}=2.710 \AA,
$$

$$
c_{\mathrm{Ru}} / 2=c_{\mathrm{Ru}}^{0} / 2=2.140 \AA,
$$

$$
c_{\mathrm{Co}} / 2=\left(c_{\mathrm{Ru}}^{0}+c_{\mathrm{Co}}^{0}\right) / 4=2.088 \AA,
$$

$$
c_{\mathrm{Co}-\mathrm{Ru}} / 2=\left(c_{\mathrm{Ru}}+c_{\mathrm{Co}}\right) / 4=2.114 \AA.
$$

These parameters have been deduced by extrapo-
lation from the $\mathrm{Fe}-\mathrm{Ru}$ superlattice data. In our
TB model, the cobalt is characterized by a com-
pletely filled majority band. The band fillings
$N_{\mathrm{Co}}^{0}=8.24$ and $N_{\mathrm{Ru}}^{0}=7.24$ are chosen so that
$M_{\mathrm{Co}}^{0}=1.76 \mu_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{Ru}}^{0}=N_{\mathrm{Co}}^{0}-1$. The behaviour
of $\Delta E_{n}$ is typically the same as for the previous
case (fig. 1b) except its sign is opposite and its
range is a bit longer $\left(\Delta E_{n} \approx 0\right.$ for $\left.n \geq 4\right)$. The
thickness of the Co layer has no influence on the
spatial period of the oscillations which is larger
than for $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{~V}_{n}$.

for $\mathrm{Co}-\mathrm{Ru}$. It is well known that $\mathrm{Pd}$ is a nearly
magnetic element for which the Stoner criterion
is clearly satisfied: $\operatorname{In}\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right) \approx 0.9$. We chose $N_{\mathrm{Pd}}^{0}=$

### 4. Antiferromagnetic spacer

The behaviour of $\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{Cr}_{n}$ superlattices we assume to be bcc with an average lattice parameter $a=2.876 \AA$, is very different from the previous cases: the AF order for bulk Cr leads to periodic oscillations of $\Delta E_{n}$ (fig. 3). Such a result can be easily understood using the pair model of reference 10 and assuming strong interfacial AF Fe-Cr couplings as compared to the AF Cr-Cr couplings. A magnetic defect occurs when there is a magnetic frustration i.e. when the Fe layers are AF and $n$ is odd or when they are $\mathrm{F}$ and $n$ is even. These defects are in principle dependent on the parity of $n$ and the oscillation we obtain for $\Delta E_{n}$ comes from the energy of such defects. The interface energy per interface atom is defined by:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{n}^{\mathrm{F} \text { or } \mathrm{AF}}= & \left(E\left(\mathrm{Fe}_{3} \mathrm{Cr}_{n}, \mathrm{~F} \text { or } \mathrm{AF}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-n E_{z}(\mathrm{Cr})-3 E_{z}(\mathrm{Fe})\right) / 2,
\end{aligned}
$$

When there is no magnetic defect and when $n \rightarrow \infty, \gamma_{n}^{\mathrm{F}}$ and $\gamma_{n}^{\mathrm{AF}}$ become equal to the interfacial energy $\gamma$ of an iron layer $\mathrm{Fe}_{3}$ with chromium. When there is a magnetic defect the corresponding limits $\gamma_{i}$ of $\gamma_{n}$ are either $\gamma_{\text {even }}$ or $\gamma_{\text {odd }}$ according to the parity of $n$ and are equal to $\gamma_{i}=\gamma+\gamma_{\mathrm{d}, i}$ where $\gamma_{\mathrm{d}, i}$ is the energy of the corresponding defect. Fig. 4 shows the varia- tion of $\gamma_{n}$ with the parity of $n$ up to $n=12: \gamma$ is nearly equal to the interface energy $\gamma_{0}$ between two semi-infinite $\mathrm{Fe}$ and $\mathrm{Cr}$ crystals. This is consistent with the observation that the properties of the central layer are similar to those of bulk iron, the difference $\gamma_{0}-\gamma$ coming from the long ranged perturbation induced in the iron semi-infinite crystals by chromium. Finally the energies $\gamma_{\mathrm{d}, i}$ are nearly equal $\gamma_{\mathrm{d}, \text { even }} \approx \gamma_{\mathrm{d}, \mathrm{odd}} \approx 10^{-2} \mathrm{eV} /$ atom.

The comparison between the TB and LSD calculations [6] (fig. 3) shows large differences for $\Delta E_{n}$ although magnetic moments, band centers, charge transfers,... are in good agreement. The orders of magnitude of $\Delta E_{n}$ are consistent (except for $n=5$ ) but the oscillations we must get in the asymptotic regime are not obtained from LSD calculations at least for $n \leq 7$. Up to now, we cannot explain this disagreement between both methods we attribute tentatively to s-d hybridiza- tion effects. A detailed comparison of the various contributions to $\Delta E_{n}$ is now under study [11].

The results we obtained theoretically for Fe-Cr superlattices are not consistent with ex- periments which exhibit [1, 2] much smaller $(\Delta E_{6} \approx 3 meV)$ positive (AF) and monotonously decreasing (for $n>4$ ) values of $\Delta E_{n}$ . Although the order of magnitude of the magnetic couplingis in agreement with experiment for $Co-Ru_{n}$ superlattices with small $n$ values $(\Delta E_{3} \approx$  - 10 meV see fig. 1b and ref. [3]), a similar dis- crepancy occurs for large $n$ values: long ranged AF couplings $(\Delta E_{20} \approx 0.3 meV)$ oscillating with spacer layer thickness with a periodicity of about $20 \AA$ are observed up to $n \approx 30$ . The origin of such long ranged AF exchange coupling cannot be understood from the interface rugosity neither by dipolar nor by exchange couplings [2] using an Heisenberg model as suggested by Malozemoff[12] for the interpretation of the exchange anisotropy. A direct interpretation of the experi- mental results is difficult if we take into account the facts that (i) the magnetic defects induced by rugosity must be treated using itinerant mag- netism, (ii) the magnetic defects whose energy was calculated $(\gamma_{d, i})$ assuming that all moments are parallel must be determined allowing a pro- gressive change of the direction of the local mag- netization [11], (iii) the interfaces are far from being abrupt and the magnetic properties of the corresponding interfacial compounds and alloys must be taken into account. These problems are under study.
