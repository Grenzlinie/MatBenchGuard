# Band structure and dielectric properties of orthorhombic $SrZrO_3$

## R. Vali*

School of Physics, Damghan University of Basic Sciences, P.O. Box 36715/364, Damghan, Iran

Received 12 July 2007; received in revised form 10 December 2007; accepted 10 December 2007 by J.R. Chelikowsky
Available online 15 December 2007

### Abstract

Ab initio density functional theory has been used to study the electronic band structure, $k=0$ (zone center) vibrational modes, and dielectric properties of the crystalline high-$\kappa$ perovskite oxide $SrZrO_3$. The frequencies of the $k=0$ normal modes are computed by the linear response technique and then grouped according to their symmetry. The dielectric intensity of each IR-active mode has been determined. It is found that the calculated orientationally averaged static dielectric constant $\varepsilon^0=21.49$ is in good agreement with experiment.

## 1. Introduction

Strontium zirconate ($SrZrO_3$) belongs to the perovskite family with the general formula $ABO_3$ and has an orthorhombic structure at room temperature with space group $Pbnm$ [1]. Perovskite oxides are important materials for various functional devices. $SrZrO_3$-based perovskite oxides have been studied for their high-temperature protonic conductivity [2], which makes them potential candidates for electrolytes in some novel electrochemical devices such as solid oxide fuel cells and hydrogen sensors.

Besides, $SrZrO_3$ has many characteristics which are suitable for high voltage and high reliability capacitor applications. It has high dielectric constant, high breakdown strength and low leakage current density. It has a large optical gap of $\approx$5.6 eV [3]. Recently, $SrZrO_3$ has been experimentally investigated as a possible candidate material for high-$\kappa$ gate dielectrics [4,5].

The purpose of the present work is to investigate theoretically the electronic band structure and the electronic as well as the lattice contribution to the dielectric constant of orthorhombic $SrZrO_3$. We first check that the relaxed structural parameters are consistent with previous experimental work [1].

Using density functional perturbation theory, the frequencies of $k=0$ (zone center) normal modes are computed and then grouped according to their symmetry. The dielectric intensities of IR-active modes are also determined. It is found that the modes at $229.8$ cm$^{-1}$, $236.3$ cm$^{-1}$ and $269.7$ cm$^{-1}$ have the highest contribution to the dielectric constant along the $x$, $y$ and $z$ directions, respectively. The calculated orientationally averaged static dielectric constant is in reasonable agreement with experimental data.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes technical aspects of the calculations. Section 3 presents the results, including the electronic band structure, phonon normal modes, and the electronic and lattice contributions to the static dielectric tensor. Section 4 concludes the paper.

## 2. Technical aspects

Crystalline $SrZrO_3$ has been studied via ab initio calculations within density functional theory [6] to determine the structural parameters (as well as electronic band structure) and within density functional perturbation theory [7-10] to obtain the frequencies of normal modes and dielectric tensors, with the use of the ABINIT code [11]. The exchange-correlation energy is evaluated in the local density approximation [12] using Ceperley-Alder electron gas data [13]. The all-electron potentials are replaced by norm-conserving pseudopotentials [14,15].

## 3. Results

The orthorhombic phase of $\text{SrZrO}_3$ with space group $Pbnm$ has four formula units per unit cell. The comparison of experimental and calculated structural parameters is given in Table 1.

As can be seen from Table 1, the calculated lattice constants are in good agreement with the experimental data [1], but a little bit smaller. The underestimation results from the use of local density approximation within the density functional calculation. Only the coordinates of atoms occupying the Wyckoff position with free parameters are optimized in the structural relaxation. The coordinates of the Zr atom are fixed by space group number. As can be seen from Table 1, the optimized atomic coordinates are very close to the experimental values.

The energy band structure and density of states of the orthorhombic phase of $\text{SrZrO}_3$ are given in Fig. 1, where the zero of energy is chosen in such a way to coincide with the top of the valence band. The lower valence bands consist of two parts that are separated by a 0.5 eV gap. The lower part is composed of O 2s and Sr 4p semicore states, whereas the upper part is mainly due to Sr 4p with a few mixings with O 2s and O 2p states. The upper valence bands are mainly due to O 2p states with some mixings with Zr 4d states. The lower conduction bands consist of Zr 4d states with some mixings with O 2p states.

The maximum of the valence band is located at the U point and has O 2p character, whereas the conduction band minimum is at the $\Gamma$ point and has Zr 4d character. The indirect band gap between the $\Gamma$ and U points is 3.764 eV.

**Fig. 1.** Electronic band structure and density of states of the orthorhombic phase of $\text{SrZrO}_3$.

However, the valence band is very flat, so the direct band gap at $\Gamma$ is only slightly higher, 3.799 eV. As far as we know, there is no theoretical result on the electronic band structure of the orthorhombic phase of $\text{SrZrO}_3$ to compare with. A comparison of the present results can be made with the cubic phase of $\text{SrZrO}_3$, where density functional calculations within the local density approximation give an indirect band gap of 3.37 eV [17]. The present results thus indicate that the orthorhombic phase of $\text{SrZrO}_3$ has a bigger band gap than the cubic phase. The experimental value of the band gap of $\text{SrZrO}_3$ was determined as 5.6 eV, using optical conductivity analysis of the polycrystalline sample at room temperature [18].

Now, we investigate the dielectric properties of orthorhombic $\text{SrZrO}_3$. The static dielectric tensor $\varepsilon^0$ can be separated into electronic $\varepsilon^\infty$ and ionic $\varepsilon^\text{ionic}$ components: $\varepsilon^0=\varepsilon^\infty+\varepsilon^\text{ionic}$. The ionic component is
$$
\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^\text{ionic}=\frac{4\pi e^2}{\Omega}\sum_{\lambda}\frac{Z_{\lambda\alpha}^*Z_{\lambda\beta}^*}{\omega_{\lambda}^2}\tag{1}
$$
where
$$
Z_{\lambda\beta}^*=\sum_{i\beta}\frac{z_{i,\alpha\beta}^{*}\xi_{i,\lambda\beta}}{\sqrt{M_i}}\tag{2}
$$
are the mode effective charge tensors. Here $\alpha$ and $\beta$ label the Cartesian coordinates, $e$ is the electron charge, $\Omega$ is the volume of the unit cell, $\omega_{\lambda}$ is the frequency of the $\lambda$th IR-active phonon normal mode, $z_{i,\alpha\beta}^{*}$ the effective charge tensor of atom $i$, and $\xi_{i,\lambda\beta}$ is the eigendisplacement of atom $i$ in phonon mode $\lambda$.

The squares of phonon frequencies at $k = 0$ (zone center) can be obtained as eigenvalues of the dynamical matrix
$$
D_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\Phi_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{M_iM_j}}.\tag{3}
$$

The force constant matrix $\Phi_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}$ can be expressed as a sum of an analytical and a non-analytical part [19]. The analytical part can be obtained by calculating all the Hellmann–Feynman forces $(F_i^\alpha)$ caused by displacing each ion in each possible

The non-analytical part takes into account the dependence on the direction of phonon wave vector $\vec{q}$ resulting from the long-range nature of the Coulomb field in polar materials. In particular the long-range (dipole-dipole) contribution is found to be responsible for the splitting between the longitudinal (LO) and transverse (TO) optic modes. The non-analytical part is given by

$$
\Phi_{i j}^{\alpha \beta}(\vec{q} \rightarrow 0)=\frac{4 \pi}{\Omega} \frac{\left(\sum_{k} q_{k} z_{\alpha, k i}^{*}\right)\left(\sum_{i} q_{k}^{\prime} z_{\beta, k^{\prime} j}^{*}\right)}{\sum_{k k^{\prime}} q_{k} \varepsilon_{k k^{\prime}}^{\infty} q_{k}^{\prime}}. \tag{5}
$$

The independent non-zero components of the Born effective charge tensors are listed in Table 2. The Born effective charge of Zr is anomalously large compared to the nominal ionic charge (+4) of the zirconium ion. Also, one component of $z_{\text{O(1)}}^{*}$ and two components of $z_{\text{O(2)}}^{*}$ are anomalously large compared to the nominal ionic charge (−2) of oxygen ion. The anomalously large Born effective charge for Zr was also observed in the case of ZrSiO₄ [20] and cubic-SrZrO₃ [21]. The anomalously large $z^{*}$ values indicate a mixed ionic-covalent nature of the Zr−O bond, which is consistent with the presence of Zr−O 2p hybridization.

The fulfillment of the acoustic sum rule $\sum_{s} z_{s}^{*}=0$ is excellent, suggesting well converged calculations. Our calculation thus seems to be plausible, and this is sufficient to allow further study of the phonon frequencies and dielectric tensors.

The energies and symmetries of all 60 vibrational modes – except three acoustic ones – are listed in Table 3 for IR (dipole-active), Raman (quadrupole-active), and silent modes (i.e., developing no dipole or quadrupole moments). Only non-degenerate modes exist in this symmetry.

Standard group theory analysis yields the following zone center lattice modes and optical activities for this space group:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma= & 7 A_{\mathrm{g}}+5 B_{1 \mathrm{~g}}+7 B_{2 \mathrm{~g}}+5 B_{3 \mathrm{~g}}+8 A_{\mathrm{u}}+10 B_{1 \mathrm{u}} \\
& +8 B_{2 \mathrm{u}}+10 B_{3 \mathrm{u}}.
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
$$

There are 24 Raman-active modes: $7 A_{\mathrm{g}}+5 B_{1 \mathrm{~g}}+7 B_{2 \mathrm{~g}}+5 B_{3 \mathrm{~g}}$, and 25 IR-active modes: $9 B_{1 \mathrm{u}}+7 B_{2 \mathrm{u}}+9 B_{3 \mathrm{u}}$, and also 3 translational modes: $B_{1 \mathrm{u}}+B_{2 \mathrm{u}}+B_{3 \mathrm{u}}$. The $8 A_{\mathrm{u}}$ modes are non-active.

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\left[\varepsilon^{0}\right]=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\varepsilon^{\infty}\right]+\sum_{\lambda} \varepsilon_{\lambda} \tag{7}

$$
\varepsilon_{\lambda}=\frac{4 \pi e^{2}}{\Omega \omega_{\lambda}^{2}} Z_{\lambda}^{* 2} \tag{8}
$$

is the contribution to the trace of the dielectric tensor, also known as dielectric intensity, coming from mode $\lambda$, while the scalar mode effective charge $Z_{\lambda}^{*}$ is defined via

$$
Z_{\lambda}^{*}=\sum_{\alpha} Z_{\lambda \alpha}^{* 2}. \tag{9}
$$

As can be seen from Eq. (8), the dielectric intensity scales as $Z_{\lambda}^{*2}/\omega_{\lambda}^{2}$, so a large dielectric intensity will result from a mode that has simultaneously a large $Z_{\lambda}^{*}$ and a small $\omega_{\lambda}$. However, as can be seen from Table 3, this is not the case for the softest mode at $54.2\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Although its frequency ($\omega_{\lambda}$) is small, its scalar mode effective charge ($Z_{\lambda}^{*}$) is not large. However, three modes with moderate frequencies, $229.8\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, $236.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, and $269.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, have large $Z_{\lambda}^{*}$ values. When all the modes are summed over, we obtain the total lattice dielectric components of SrZrO₃.

The values of $\varepsilon^{\text{ionic}}$, $\varepsilon^{\infty}$ as well as $\varepsilon^{0}$ are presented in Table 4. As can be seen from Table 4, the large static dielectric constant of SrZrO₃ is mostly vibrational in origin, as in other high-$\kappa$ oxides. The calculated electronic dielectric constant of orthorhombic SrZrO₃ ($\varepsilon^{\infty}=4.96$) is about $5\%$ bigger than that of cubic-SrZrO₃ ($\varepsilon^{\infty}=4.7$) [21].

To compare these results with experiment, we note that most experimental measurements of which we are aware have been carried out on thin films. Recently, Chen et al. [22] have reported that the dielectric constants of SrZrO₃ thin films annealed at $550\ {^\circ}\text{C}$, $600\ {^\circ}\text{C}$ and $650\ {^\circ}\text{C}$ are 22.0, 22.6 and 23.3, respectively. Thus, our calculated orientationally averaged static dielectric constant of $\varepsilon^{0}=21.49$ is in reasonable agreement with experiment. The dielectric constant of SrZrO₃ is lower than those of some other perovskite materials such as SrTiO₃ ($\varepsilon^{0}\sim300$). However, the conduction band offset to Si for SrTiO₃ is equal to $-0.14\ \text{eV}$ [23], which makes it unfavorable for use as a high-$k$ gate dielectric. The band gap of SrZrO₃ ($5.6\ \text{eV}$) is bigger than that of SrTiO₃ ($3.25\ \text{eV}$ as reported in Ref. [24]). Moreover, another difference between SrZrO₃ and SrTiO₃ is that the energy of the Zr 4d level is higher than that of Ti 3d level. Thus, we expect a bigger conduction band offset on Si for SrZrO₃. The dielectric constant of SrZrO₃ is bigger than those of some other high-$k$ candidate materials such as ZrSiO₄ ($\varepsilon^{0}\sim12$) [20], and is comparable to those of other materials such as ZrO₂ ($\varepsilon^{0}\sim20$) [25].

## 4. Conclusion

Using density functional theory, the structural parameters, including the internal coordinates, are relaxed and good

agreement is achieved with experimental values. The calculated electronic band structure shows that the orthorhombic phase of $SrZrO_3$ has an indirect band gap of 3.764 eV and a direct band gap which is only slightly higher, 3.799 eV. The zone center phonon frequencies were calculated and grouped according to their symmetry. The dielectric intensities of IR-active phonon modes were determined. It is found that three successive IR-active modes at $229.8\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, $236.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, and $269.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, have the highest contribution in $\varepsilon_{yy}^{\text{ionic}}$, $\varepsilon_{zz}^{\text{ionic}}$, and $\varepsilon_{xx}^{\text{ionic}}$, respectively. The calculated orientationally averaged static dielectric constant is in reasonable agreement with experimental measurements on thin films.
