# Electronic structure, ferroelectricity and optical properties of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$

B. Xu $^{1,a}$, X. Li $^{1}$, J. Sun $^{2}$, and L. Yi $^{3}$

$^{1}$ Department of Mathematics and Information Sciences, North China Institute of Water Conservancy and Hydroelectric Power, 450008 Zhengzhou, P.R. China

Received 22 October 2008
Published online 18 December 2008 - © EDP Sciences, Società Italiana di Fisica, Springer-Verlag 2008

**Abstract.** Using first-principles calculations based on density-functional theory in its local-density approximation, we investigated the Electronic structure, ferroelectricity and optical properties of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ (CBT) for the first time. It is found that CBT compound has an indirect band gap of 3.114 eV and the O 2$s$ and 2$p$ states are strongly hybridized with the 6$s$ states of Bi which belong to the $(Bi_2O_2)^{2+}$ planes. The quite strong Ta–O and Bi–O hybridization is the primary source for ferroelectricity. Our results imply that the interaction between Bi and O is highly covalent. The anisotropy occurs mainly above 4 eV in the optical properties. The different optical properties have been discussed.

## 1 Introduction

Bengt Aurivillius reported on the structures of the mixed bismuth-oxide layer-structure compounds in 1949 [1] and the ferroelectric nature of the Aurivillius phases was discovered about a decade later [2,3]. In recent years, these materials have emerged as important candidates for non-volatile ferroelectric memories [4]. However, SBT films have other shortcomings, such us a high deposition temperature and a low remanent polarization. To overcome these problems, different preparation conditions and/or the use of new ferroelectric materials have been explored. For example, SBT films synthesized with Sr-deficient and Bi-excess compositions, such as $Sr_{0.8}Bi_{2.2}Ta_2O_9$, were found to show significantly improved ferroelectric properties [5]. Thus there is a need for a microscopic understanding of ferroelectricy in the Aurivillius compounds. In recent years, layered Bi oxides such as $ABi_2Ta_2O_9$ (A = Ca, Ba, Sr) have received renewed interest following the observation that their ferroelectric properties are superior, for use in memory devices, to those of the currently employed $PbZrO_3$-$PbTiO_3$ solid solution ceramics. The complex electrical properties of these Bi oxides originate from an intimate interplay of structural and electronic effects associated with the Bi 6$s$ lone pair electrons.

Doping of layered perovskites is commonly used to enhance their favourable ferroelectric properties [6], although the origins of the altered performance are often unclear [7,8]. Layered perovskites (Aurivillius) oxides such as multi-component calcium bismuth tantalate ($CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ or CBT), with two formulae units per unit cell, are of technological importance owing to their switchable spontaneous polarization or ferroelectric behaviour, high dielectric permittivity and piezoelectric or pyroelectric properties [9–16]. Recently, Stachiotti et al. [17] reported highly accurate band structure calculations of $SrBi_2Ta_2O_9$ with the paraelectric structure, and they discussed the appearance of ferroelectricity. Shimakawa et al. analyzed crystal structures and electron densities of paraelectric and ferroelectric $SrBi_2Nb_2O_9$ [18]. First-principles calculations of Perez-Mato et al. compared their results in detail with the experimental ferroelectric structure. They reveal a complex interplay of different displacive modes in the mechanism of the phase transitions of SBT, and by extension in the general behavior of the Aurivillius family [19]. To our knowledge, there are little ab initio calculations for the electronic structure and linear optical properties of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$.

The aim of this paper is to present the results of a theoretical investigation of the electronic energy bands, total, partial densities of states (DOS) and optical properties for $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$, based on the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FPLAPW) method. ## 2 Computational method

The calculations presented in this paper were performed using the scalar relativistic version and the generalized gradient approximation to density functional theory, as implemented in the Wien2k compute code [20], using the FPLAPW method. In this method no shape approximation on either potential or the electronic charge density is made. We use the Wien2k implementation of the method, which allows the inclusion of local orbitals in the basis, improving the linearization and making possible a consistent treatment of semicore and valence states in one energy window, hence ensuring proper orthogonality. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) has been used [21]. In the FPLAPW method, the unit cell is divided into two parts: (1) non-overlapping atomic spheres (centred at the atomic sites) and (2) an interstitial region. In the orthorhombic phase of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$, the lattice parameters we used are $a=5.4438(5.4625)$ Å, $b=5.4273(5.4286)$ Å, $c=24.919(24.945)$ Å [22]. In our calculations, the atomic sphere radii of 1.6, 2.3, 1.8 and 1.5 a.u. are used for Ca, Bi, Ta and O, respectively. The crystal structure is shown in Figure 1. The convergence parameter $RK_{max}$ ($R_{mt}K_{max}$, where $K_{max}$ is the plane-wave cut-off and $R_{mt}$ is the smallest of all atomic sphere radii), which controls the size of the basis sets in these calculations, is set to be 7.0. We have used a $4\times4\times4$ mesh in the first Brillouin zone. The self-consistent calculations are considered to be converged when the total energy of the system is stable within 0.00001 Ry. The spin-orbit coupling was not included in our calculation since it has a minor influence on the optical properties for ferroelectricity and semiconductor materials [23,24]. The frequency-dependent optical properties were obtained using the joint density of states weighted by the dipole matrix elements of the direct interband transitions. In the orthorhombic phase, $\varepsilon(\omega)$ is anisotropic and has three different components, $\varepsilon_{xx}(\omega)$, $\varepsilon_{yy}(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_{zz}(\omega)$, corresponding to the electric field, respectively. We present an average over the three components as $[\varepsilon_{xx}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{yy}+\varepsilon_{zz}(\omega)]/3$ for the orthorhombic phase. Using the $\varepsilon(\omega)$, as output, we calculated various optical constants [25] such as spectral reflectivity $R(\omega)$, absorption coefficient $I(\omega)$, electron energy loss function

## 3 Results and discussion

The crystal structure of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ is orthorhombic with space group $A2_1am$. The crystal structure of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ is illustrated in Figure 1. For $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$, we fixed the space group ($A2_1am$) and then carried out both geometric optimization and atom relaxation to find the equilibrium configuration. There are two calciums, four bismuths, four tantalums and eighteen oxygens in the primitive cell of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$. In Table 1 we listed the calculated and experimental equilibrium lattice parameters and the atomic positions for $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$. Overall, there is a good agreement with the experiment. By calculating the total energy at different volumes we obtained the energy vs. volume curve for $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ showmin Figure 2.

Figure 3 shows the band structure of the optimized geometry and the total DOS of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$. The bottom of the conduction bands (CB) is at the $\Gamma$ point. The top of the valence bands (VB) is at the $N$ point. Hence, based on our calculation, $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ compound has an indirect band gap of 3.114 eV. Therefore, $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ structure is non metallic as expected. It is noted that the band gap calculated in the local density function approximation usually is smaller than the experimental gap which a well-known artifact usually does not have a significant effect on the rest of the band structure [26].

Figure 4 shows the partial DOS of $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$. In Figure 2 there are a few bands around $-12$ eV which consist mainly of Bi $6s$ and O $2s$ orbitals belonging to the $(Bi_2O_2)^{2+}$ planes. It is noticed that there is the hybridization between Bi $6p$ and O $2s$ in this energy range. The next higher bands, ranging from about $-8.57$ eV up to $-3.34$ eV, are valence bands that are composed mainly of $6p$ states of Bi atom, $5d$ states of Ta and $2p$ states of the O atoms. Although the valence bands present mainly O $2p$ character, there is quite a strong interaction with the Ta and Bi orbitals, which is the primary source for ferroelectricity [17,28]. It is clear that there is the strong interaction between Bi $6p$ and O $2p$ in this energy range. The conduction bands are composed mainly of $6p$ states of Bi, $5d$ states of Ta and $2p$ states of O. It also notes a quite strong hybridization between Bi $6p$ and O $2p$ from 0 to 4 eV. This strong hybridization implies that the interaction between Bi and O is highly covalent. In Figure 4, the hybridization of Ta $5d$ orbitals at the bottom of the conduction bands with O $(2p)$ orbitals at the top of the valence bands is very strong. This feature resembles the case of $Bi_2WO_6$ [27].

Figure 5 shows the valence charge density of ferroelectric $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ in the (100) and (010) planes. Looking at the contour picture, a significant overlap can be observed between Bi and O as well as Ta and O, showing a covalent character. The results are consistent with the partial density of states, showing a strong hybridization between Bi and O and between the Ta and O atoms.

As we know, the imaginary part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ is the pandect of the optical properties for any materials. The peaks A (around 5 eV) and B (around 5.45 eV) mainly come from the electron transitions between the O $2p$ (VB) and Bi $6p$ (CB) orbitals. The transition between the Ta $6s$ and O $2p$ orbitals leads to the peak C at about 7.0 eV and the peaks D, E, F are mainly derived from the transitions between the Ta $5d$ and O $2p$ orbitals. The critical peaks can be ascribed to the transitions from the inner semicore states to CB. It should be noted that a peak in $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ does not correspond to a single interband transitions, since many direct or indirect transition may be found in the band structure with an energy corresponding to the same peak. Figure 7 shows our calculated imaginary parts of the dielectric function (up to a photon energy of 13 eV) along the $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions. The imaginary parts of the dielectric function are about the same along the $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions in the energy region of 0–4 eV but subtle differences in the structure can be identified. The anisotropy in the optical properties occurs mainly above 4 eV.

The real part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$, the reflectivity $R(\omega)$, the absorption coefficient $I(\omega)$, the real part $n(\omega)$ and imaginary part $k(\omega)$ of the refractive index, and the electron energy loss function $L(\omega)$ are plotted in Figure 8. The peaks in the $L(\omega)$ spectrum represent the characteristic associated with the plasma resonance and the corresponding frequency is the so-called plasma frequency, above which the material exhibits the dielectric behavior $[\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)>0]$ whereas below the material has a metallic property $[\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)<0]$. It is interesting that the profiles of both $I(\omega)$ and $K(\omega)$ are rather similar with the different magnitude, for instance, there are both an abrupt increase at around 4 eV. We are not aware of any experimental data or theoretical calculations for the electronic structure and optical properties of ferroelectric CaBi₂Ta₂O₉. We consider the present results as a prediction for this compound. We hope that our present work will stimulate some other works on these materials.

## 4 Conclusion

Our present paper reports a systematic study of the structural, electronic and optical properties of CaBi₂Ta₂O₉ using the LAPW+lo method within GGA. The ground state properties like equilibrium cell volume and lattice constants are obtained from our calculation. And the electronic energy bands, total and partial densities of states, charge density distributions, including optical properties, have been analyzed for the first time. It is found

that $CaBi_2Ta_2O_9$ compound has an indirect band gap of 3.114 eV and the O $2s$ and $2p$ states are strongly hybridized with the $6s$ states of Bi which belong to the $(Bi_2O_2)^{2+}$ planes. It is also noted that a quite strong hybridization between Bi $6p$ and O $2p$ implies that the interaction between Bi and O is highly covalent from 0 to 4 eV. The imaginary parts of the dielectric function are about the same along the $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions in the energy region of 0–4 eV but subtle differences in the structure can be identified. The anisotropy in the optical properties occurs mainly above 4 eV. In the end, the different optical properties have been discussed.
