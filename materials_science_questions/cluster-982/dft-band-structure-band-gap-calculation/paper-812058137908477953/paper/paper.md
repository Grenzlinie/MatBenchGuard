# The elastic, electronic, and vibrational properties of pure $CdF_2$: A first-principles study

E. Deligoz*, K. Colakoglu, Y.O. Ciftci

Gazi University, Department of Physics, Teknikokullar, 06500 Ankara, Turkey

Received 20 March 2006; received in revised form 16 August 2006; accepted 17 August 2006
Available online 18 September 2006

## Abstract
Ab initio calculations, based on density functional theory and norm-conserving pseudopotentials, have been performed to investigate the some structural, elastic, electronic, and lattice-dynamical properties of pure $CdF_{2}$ . The calculated lattice parameter, phonon dispersion, and band structure are in accordance with the available experimental and other theoretical results. The pressure-dependent behaviours of elastic constants and band- structural properties of $CdF_{2}$ are also reported.

## 1. Introduction
Cadmium fluoride $(CdF_{2})$ , due in part to its visible and ultra violet light transparency, and semiconductivity after doping and thermal treatment, has been subject of several experimental and theoretical studies recently and in the past decades. If we look at the literature from the experimental side for the present pur- pose, structural [1-3], electronic [4-11], elastic [12-14], and vibrational [15-16] properties of $CdF_{2}$ are studied extensively. From the theoretical side, structural and electronic calculations[17-26] exist, based on the different methods. Although the methods used in these works were different with varying degree of success, the local-density approximation (LDA) and the gen- eralized gradient approximation (GGA) are known to give reli- able ground state properties of solids. On the lattice dynamics of pure (undoped) $CdF_{2}$ - to our knowledge - no other ab ini tio calculations have been reported so far. The only early work performed by Eisenberger and Alderstein [15] is based on the rigid-ion model calculations and Raman measurements. Hence, beside the other contributions, an attempt has been made in the present work to complete this deficiency for pure $CdF_{2}$ .
$CdF_{2}$ is an ionic crystal with fluoride structure with the space group $O_{h}^{5}(Fm-3m)$ at ambient conditions, and is closely related to $CaF_{2}$ structural and bonding points of view. It has three atoms in the unit cell: Cd at (0,0,0), one F at (1/4,1/4,1/4) and another F at (3/4,3/4,3/4). Each Cd atom has eight F atoms as nearest neighbours. Correspondingly, each F atom is surrounding by a four-atom Cd tetrahedron [1-3].
Here, we present the results of our ab initio calculations on the structural, electronic, mechanical, and vibrational properties of pure $CdF_{2}$ . These calculations will form the basis for our future semiconducting (doped) $CdF_{2}$ calculations. The present work is organized as follows: the methods of calculation are given in Section 2, the results are discussed in Section 3, and the conclusion is given in Section 4.

## 2. Method of calculation
The Spanish Initiative for Electronic Simulations with Thousand of Atom (SIESTA) code [27,28] is utilized in this study to calculate the energies and atomic forces. It solves the quantum mechanical equations for the electrons with the density functional approach in the local density approximation (LDA) parameterized by Ceperley and Alder [29] for the electronic exchange and correlation potential. The interactions between electrons and core ions are simulated with separable Troullier-Martins [30] norm-conserving pseudopo- tentials. The basis set is based on the finite range pseudoatomic orbitals (PAOs) of the Sankey-Niklewsky type [31], generalized to include multiple-zeta decays.
We have generated atomic pseudopotentials separately for both atoms, Cd and F by using the $5s^{2}4d^{10}$ and $2s^{2}2p^{5}$ atomic configurations, respectively. The cut-off radii for the present atomic pseudopotentials are taken as 2.40 and1.20 a.u for the s, p, d, and f channels of Cd and F, respectively.

SIESTA calculates the self-consistent potential on a grid in real space. The fineness of this grid is determined in terms of an energy cutoff Ec in analogy to the energy cutoff when the basis set involves plane waves. Here, by using a double-zeta plus polarization orbitals (DZP) basis and the cut-off energies between 50 and 200 Ryd with various basis sets, we found its optimal values around of 150 Ryd. Atoms were allowed to relax until atomic forces were less than 0.003 eV Å⁻¹. For final converging, 256 k-points were enough to obtain the converged total energies ΔE to about 1 meV atom⁻¹ for present compound.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Structural and electronic properties

Firstly, the equilibrium lattice parameter has been computed by minimizing the crystal total energy calculated for different values of lattice constant by means of Murnaghan's equation of state (eos) [32] as in Fig. 1. The bulk modulus, and its pressure derivative have also been calculated based on the same Murnaghan's equation of state, and the results are given in Table 1 along with the experimental and other theoretical values. The calculated value of lattice parameter for CdF₂ is 5.36 Å. The experimental lattice constant spreads from 5.364 to 5.395 Å in Refs. [2,13]. Our value for lattice constant is quite accord with the experimental values [2,13], and the agreement is more good than some other theoretical findings [17,19]. The present value of bulk modulus is closer to in Ref. [17], but is slightly lower (about 5%) than the other theoretical results. The computed cohesive energy ($E_{\text{coh}}$) for CdF₂ is $-14.77$ eV, and it is also listed in Table 1.

### 3.2. Elastic properties

The elastic constants of solids provide a link between the
mechanical and dynamical behaviours of crystals, and give
important information concerning the nature of the forces oper-
ating in solids. In particular, they provide information on the
stability and stiffness of materials, and their ab initio calcula-
tion requires precise methods. Since the forces and the elastic
constants are functions of the first and second-order deriva-
tives of the potentials, their calculation will provide a further
check on the accuracy of the calculation of forces in solids. The
effect of pressure on the elastic constants is essential, especially,
for understanding interatomic interactions, mechanical stability,
and phase transition mechanisms. It also provides valuable data
for developing interatomic potentials.

The present elastic constants for CdF₂ are given in Table 3 along with the available experimental and some other theoretical values. Our result for $C_{11}$ is lower than the experimental (at 4.2 K) value, whereas $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$ are, significantly, higher than the experimental ones. But, our $C_{44}$ (50.65 GPa) is, relatively, closer to the values obtained using shell-model (54.8 GPa) and rigid-ion model (63.9 GPa) by Alterovitz and Gerlic [14].

The Zener anisotropy factor $A$, Poisson ratio $v$, and Young’s modulus $Y$, which are the most interesting elastic properties for applications, are also calculated in terms of the computed data using the following relations [39]

$$
A=\frac{2 C_{44}}{C_{11}-C_{12}}, \tag{1}
$$

$$
v=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{B-(2 / 3 G)}{B+(1 / 3 G)}\right] \tag{2}
$$

and

$$
Y=\frac{9 G B}{G+3 B} \tag{3}
$$

where $G=(G_{\mathrm{V}}+G_{\mathrm{R}})/2$ is shear modulus, $G_{\mathrm{V}}$ the Voigt’s shear modulus corresponding to the upper bound of $G$ values, and $G_{\mathrm{R}}$ is the Reuss’s shear modulus corresponding to the lower bound of $G$ values, and can be written as $G_{\mathrm{V}}=(C_{11}-C_{12}+3 C_{44})/5$ and $5/G_{\mathrm{R}}=4/(C_{11}-C_{12})+3/C_{44}$. In this work, the calculated Zener anisotropy factor ($A$), Poisson ratio ($v$), and Young’s modulus ($Y$) are found to be 2.2298, 0.363, and 100.09 GPa, respectively, for pure CdF₂.

The other principle goal of the present work is to report the pressure dependence of the second-order elastic constant (SOEC) for undoped cadmium fluorite. No experimental and the other theoretical data on the pressure-dependence of elas- tic constants are available in the literature for cadmium fluorite, except the present results in Fig. 5 for the sake of compari- son. Our calculated elastic constants are everywhere positive and smoothly increasing functions of pressure, and they obey the general stability criterions. This implies that the pure CdF₂ is mechanically stable over the entire pressure range (0–8 GPa) considered, and show the similar behaviours to those obtained for Cadmium chalcogenides [38]. It is seen from the figure that both $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ increase monotonically with pressure whereas the slope for $C_{44}$, relatively, is lower.

### 3.3. Phonon dispersion curves

The present LDA phonon dispersion curves and density of states of CdF₂, along the high symmetry directions are calcu- lated by using the PHONON software [40]. This code, which is compatible with SIESTA, uses “Direct Method” [41] and the Hellman–Feynman forces on atoms for generating the phonon dispersion and the density of states (DOS), and its theoretical and applicational details can be found in PHONON manual [42] and references therein. Specifically, we have calculated the phonon dispersion curves in high symmetry directions and the DOS for $2\times2\times2$ cubic supercell with 96 atoms. The displacement amplitudes are taken as 0.03 Å, and the positive and negative atomic displacements along $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions are taken into account. The Hellmann–Feynman forces acting on the atoms in the supercell are evaluated to construct the force constants and dynamical matrix.

Eisenberger and Alderstein [15] have investigated the lattice dynamics of pure and doped (semiconducting) CdF₂ by using the technique of Raman spectroscopy and rigid-ion model in the main symmetry directions. No experimental phonon frequen- cies are available – to our knowledge – due to the large neutron capture cross section of cadmium [15]. Our phonon dispersion curves and density of state based on the direct method for pure CdF₂, shown in Figs. 6 and 7, indicate the similar behaviours in Ref. [15]. The maximum values of the present longitudinal and transverse optical modes are given in Table 4 along with the experimental work [15]. ## 4. Summary and conclusion

The first-principles pseudopotential calculations have performed on the $CdF_2$. Our present key results are on the elastic, electronic, and structural properties for undoped cadmium fluoride. Specifically, we find that the lattice parameter is excellent agreement with the experiments, and the bulk modulus is closer to some other theoretical works. The present value of cohesive energy is found $(-14.77\,\text{eV})$ significantly lower than the other theoretical value $(-22.10\,\text{eV})$ at room temperature. Our results on some mechanical properties based on the calculated elastic constants such as anisotropy factor, Poisson ratio, and Young moduli are found in reasonable orders. The computed energy band gap (indirect) value is lower than the experimental one, but it is higher than some other theoretical findings. The present values of elastic constants ($C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$) at ambient conditions are, the first ab initio results, and higher than the experimental values at $4.2\,\text{K}$, and their pressure-dependent behaviours, almost, show a linear increasing. Actually, the high pressure results in a larger elastic moduli and higher band gap energy for $CdF_2$. We believe that the more experimental and theoretical works are required for detailed description of the high- pressure elastic and vibrational behaviours of this compound.
