# Ultralow thermal conductivity in the quaternary semiconducting chalcogenide $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}}$ with an unprecedented closed cavity architecture $\dagger$

Hong Chen, $^{a,b}$ Peng-Fei Liu, $^{c}$ Hua Lin $^{*a,b}$ and Xin-Tao Wu $^{*a,b}$

Received 15th October 2020,
Accepted 28th November 2020
DOI: 10.1039/d0qi01240h
rsc.l/frontiers-inorganic

Novel three-dimensional (3D) inorganic chalcogenide architectures have attracted much attention because of their rich structural assembly patterns and intriguing physical properties. Herein, four isostructural quaternary semiconducting chalcogenides, namely $\mathrm{Cs_4[RE_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}$ (RE = Ho, Er, Tm, and Lu), have been discovered and found to crystallize in a tetragonal system, space group $I4_1/a$ (no. 88). Their structures feature 3D quasi-NaCl type architectures formed by edge-sharing and face-sharing of $[RESe_6]$ and $[MSe_6]$ (M = RE/Cd) octahedra, which are embedded with the unprecedented polyanionic $[Cs_2@Se_{18}-Cd-Cs_2@Se_{18}]$ closed cavities. Significantly, the compound $\mathrm{Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}$ exhibits ultralow conductivity $(0.38{-}0.29\ \mathrm{W\ m^{-1}\ K^{-1}})$ from 295 K to 675 K, which is one of the lowest values reported in crystalline chalcogenides. This result not only enriches the coordination chemistry of host-guest systems, but also suggests a new thought for the design of novel inorganic chalcogenides with intrinsic low thermal conductivities.

Semiconducting chalcogenides make up a widely studied class of materials that have recently attracted great attention, owing to their attractive topologies and great potential, primarily as nonlinear optical and thermoelectric materials. $^{1-8}$ Among these compounds, multinary chalcogenides with alkali, transition, and rare earth elements are of particular interest, which can be summarized as the A/TM/RE/Q systems. $^{9-24}$ Based on the viewpoint of structural chemistry, these diverse dimensional structures mainly result from a wider variety of octahedral $[REQ_6]$ and tetrahedral $[TMQ_4]$ as the basic building groups with different assembly methods in one compound. The interplay of these different basic building groups gives the materials unique physical properties. For example, $\mathrm{ATMQ_3}$ represents a large well-known family of two-dimensional (2D) layered semiconductors and shows diverse band gaps and magnetic performance through a simple chemical substitution. $^{20}$ Similarly, quaternary heterometallic materials $\mathrm{K_2Cu_2CeS_4}$ and $\mathrm{KCu_2CeS_6}$ possess a 2D layered structure and show remarkable paramagnetic features at a high-temperature range. $^{9}$ $\mathrm{A_xCu_{6-x}RE_2Te_6}$ (A = K, Rb and Cs; RE = La-Nd), a three-dimensional (3D) trigonal double-tunnel structure formed by distorted $[CuTe_4]$ basic building groups, shows relatively low thermal conductivity, high electrical conductivity and a moderate Seebeck coefficient in the mid-temperature range. $^{18}$

In comparison with the enormous amount of research effort that goes into 2D layered and 3D open-framework structures, the number of closed-cavity chalcogenide architectures is much smaller. In our previous study, we have explored the novel multinary chalcogenides *via* the reactive halide flux method. $^{25-38}$ In particular, we have found that the ratio between the number of guest metals and host metals (denoted as the G/H ratio) plays a key role in the synthesis of different types of closed-cavity chalcogenides. For instance, as the G/H ratio decreases (from $\mathrm{Cs_2[RE_8InS_{14}]}$ (G/H = 0.22)$^{32}$ to $\mathrm{Cs[RE_9TM_4Se_{18}]}$ (TM = Mn and Cd) (G/H = 0.14)$^{22,23}$ and then to $\mathrm{Cs[RE_7Q_{11}]}$ (G/H = 0.08)$^{27,31}$), the size of the closed cavity becomes smaller (from tetranuclear $\mathrm{Cs_4@S_{12}}$ to binuclear $\mathrm{Cs_2@Q_{18}}$ and then to mononuclear $\mathrm{Cs@Se_{12}}$).

In our continued exploration of the A/TM/RE/Q systems, we discovered $\mathrm{Cs_4[RE_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}$ (RE = Ho, Er, Tm, and Lu), a family of inorganic chalcogenide architectures that crystallize in a new structure type and feature unique polyanionic $[Cs_2@Se_{18}-Cd-Cs_2@Se_{18}]$ closed cavities. The single-crystal X-ray diffraction (XRD) result indicates that $\mathrm{Cs_4[RE_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}$ (RE = Ho, Er, Tm, and Lu) compounds crystallize in the tetragonal space group $I4_1$ (Pearson symbol $tI404$ and Wyckoff sequence $f^{21}b$) with the crystal cell parameters within the following intervals: $a = b = 20.667(7)$–20.389 (8) Å, $c = 22.816(7)$–22.479(2) Å, $V = 9746(8)$–9345(9) Å³, and $Z = 4$. The detailed crystallographic data for each compound are presented in Table S1† and are in line with the trend expected owing to the lanthanide contraction. Since they are isostructural, the crystal structure of $\mathrm{Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}$ is described in detail as an example for convenience. The asymmetric unit in $\mathrm{Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}$ features one independent Cs atom, 4 independent M atoms randomly occupied by both Ho and Cd in a molar ratio of $5:3$ (denoted as M1, M2, M3 and M4, respectively), one independent Cd atom (denoted as Cd5), 4 independent Ho atoms (denoted as Ho5, Ho6, Ho7 and Ho8, respectively) and twelve independent Se atoms. All of these atoms are at the Wyckoff position of $16f$, except Cd5 (Wyckoff position: $4b$), as shown in Fig. S1 and Table S2.† As shown in Fig. 1a and b, by corner- and edge-sharing, a fundamental building group $\mathrm{[M_{16}Se_{52}]}$ is configured by the interconnection of four $\mathrm{[M1Se_6]}$, four $\mathrm{[M2Se_6]}$, four $\mathrm{[M3Se_6]}$ and four $\mathrm{[M4Se_6]}$ basic building groups. Then, the infinite 1D M-Se chain is formed by the $\mathrm{[M_{16}Se_{52}]}$ fundamental building groups along the $ba$ plane by edge-sharing (Fig. 1c). Furthermore, each 1D M-Se chain is interlinked with other 4 neighbouring M-Se chains along the $ac$ plane *via* corner- and edge-sharing to form a 3D M-Se framework (Fig. 1d). Similarly, four types of $\mathrm{[HoSe_6]}$ basic building groups (Fig. 1e) are connected *via* corner- and edge-sharing to generate $\mathrm{[Ho_{16}Se_{52}]}$ fundamental building groups (Fig. 1f), which are further joined to produce the 1D Ho-Se chain (Fig. 1g) and the 3D Ho-Se framework (Fig. 1h).

The Cs atom is coordinated to 11 Se atoms (Fig. S2†) and a distorted $\mathrm{[CsSe_{11}]}$ polyhedron is formed. Two such $\mathrm{[CsSe_{11}]}$ polyhedra share edges into a dual tricapped $\mathrm{Cs_2@Se_{18}}$ cube, which are further connected to an adjacent $\mathrm{Cs_2@Se_{18}}$ polyhedron in a vertical manner through a Cd atom to form a $\mathrm{[Cs_2@Se_{18}-Cd-Cs_2@Se_{18}]}$ closed cavity (Fig. 1i and j). This closed cavity can also be viewed as the vertex of a $\mathrm{[CdSe_4]}$ tetrahedron is replaced by the edges of two dual tricapped $\mathrm{Cs_2@Se_{18}}$ cubes. As far as we know, such a closed cavity has never been reported previously. Finally, the 3D quasi-NaCl type architecture formed by M-Se and Ho-Se frameworks connects with short-range ordered $\mathrm{[Cs_2@Se_{18}-Cd-Cs_2@Se_{18}]}$ closed cavities, forming the whole $\mathrm{Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]}$ structure (Fig. 2). The black-red bulk crystals of $Cs_4[RE_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$ (RE = Ho, Er, Tm, and Lu) were obtained by a high-temperature solid-state technology with the mixture of RE, Cd, and Se and a moderate CsCl flux (see the experimental details in the ESI†). Among these compounds, only $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$ has a relatively high yield (>90%). Therefore, the following related physical properties are based on this compound. The purity of the polycrystalline sample was confirmed by powder XRD (Fig. S3†). The energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectroscopy results indicated the presence of Cs, Ho, Cd, and Se elements in the single-crystal sample (Fig. S4†). $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$ possesses a good thermal stability and shows no obvious weight loss up to 750 K (see Fig. S5†). In addition, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) shows the macroscopic and microscopic appearance of the hot-pressed sample, which is consistent with the high relative density. The fracture surface is clean with no visible defects such as pores (see Fig. S6†).

To understand the electronic structure of $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$, theoretical calculations have been performed using VASP software. $^{42}$ As shown in Fig. S7,† $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$ is a direct band-gap material because the bottom of the conduction band and the top of the valence band are at the same k points. The calculated band structure suggests an $E_g$ value of 1.67 eV for $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$, which agrees well with the experimental result ($E_g$ = 1.75 eV, Fig. 4a). Furthermore, the density of state (DOS) diagrams are shown in Fig. 4b and some characteristics can be deduced: (i) the major contribution of the top of the valence band section (-2.0-0 eV) is from the Se-4p states, mixed with a handful of Ho-5d and Cd-4d states, revealing the strong orbital hybridization between the Se atoms and the Ho and Cd atoms, and (ii) the lower part of the conduction band region (1.7-4 eV) consists of mixtures of the orbitals of Se-4p, Ho-5d and Cd-5s. Since the optical absorption mainly comes from the virtual (electron and hole) excitation between the states near the $E_F$, the $[HoSe_6]$ and $[MSe_6]$ basic building groups have definite actions for $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$, while the contribution of the counter-balance Cs cation is negligible.

Furthermore, inspired by the research that many inorganic RE-based chalcogenides demonstrate potential physical performance, we studied the magnetic and thermal transport properties of $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$. As shown in Fig. 5a, the inverse molar magnetic susceptibility ($\chi^{-1}$) as a function of temperature (T) shows that $Cs_4[Ho_{26}Cd_7Se_{48}]$ complies with the Curie-Weiss law in the range of 2-300 K and the resulting values for the experimental Weiss constant ($\theta$) and the Curie constant (C) are -0.11 K and $177.3\ \text{cm}^3\ \text{K}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$, respectively. As is known to all, the contribution of $\kappa_{T}$ consists of two parts: $\kappa_{E}$ and $\kappa_{L}$, i.e., $\kappa_{T} = \kappa_{E} + \kappa_{L}$. $\kappa_{E}$ can be obtained by the Wiedemann-Franz function, $\kappa_{E} = L\sigma T$ (where $L$, $\sigma$, and $T$ represent the Lorenz number, the electrical conductivity, and the absolute temperature, respectively).⁴³ Since Cs₄[Ho₂₆Cd₇Se₄₈] has a very low $\sigma$ (see Fig. S8†), the contribution of $\kappa_{E}$ can be ignored, that is, $\kappa_{T}$ is approximately equal to $\kappa_{L}$. Thus, the $\kappa_{L}$ of Cs₄[Ho₂₆Cd₇Se₄₈] decreased from 0.38 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ to 0.29 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ when the temperature increased from 295 K to 675 K, respectively. This value is comparable to those of the famous high-performance TE selenides, such as layered SnSe (0.25-0.68 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹),⁴⁴ defective Cu_{2-x}Se (0.38-0.59 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹),⁴⁵ Cu₇PSe₆ (0.23-0.37 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹),⁴⁶ and the complex framework K₂Bi₈Se₁₃ (0.20-0.42 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹).⁴⁷ The extremely low thermal conductivity of Cs₄[Ho₂₆Cd₇Se₄₈] may be derived from the inherent Ho/Cd disorder in the metal sites of the host structure and the so-called "rattler" atom (i.e., Cs) in the guest structure (see the ADP of different atoms in Fig. S9†).

In conclusion, four novel rare-earth chalcogenides in the quaternary A/RE/TM/Q family (A = alkali metals; RE = rare-earth elements; TM = transition elements; and Q = chalcogen), namely Cs₄[RE₂₆Cd₇Se₄₈] (RE = Ho, Er, Tm, and Lu), have been successfully obtained and systematically characterized. They adopt a new structure type and crystallize in the centrosymmetric space group $I4_{1}/a$ (no. 88) of the tetragonal system. Remarkably, the unprecedented polyanionic [Cs₂@Se₁₈-Cd-Cs₂@Se₁₈] closed cavities are distributed with large intervals within the 3D quasi-NaCl type framework. What is particularly intriguing is that the title compounds were still in accord with the previously established empirical G/H ratio-structure evolution trend. The magnetic property measurement shows that Cs₄[Ho₂₆Cd₇Se₄₈] possesses paramagnetic features and obeys the Curie-Weiss law over the entire experimental temperature range. Moreover, the synergistic effect of the condensed host framework and discrete guest ions in the structure of Cs₄[Ho₂₆Cd₇Se₄₈] results in ultralow thermal conductivities (0.38-0.29 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹) in the temperature range of 295-675 K. This work offers a useful structural view for enriching the family of closed-cavity architectures through the adjustment of the G/H ratio, and should inspire further exploration of novel

inorganic chalcogenides with intrinsic low thermal conductivities.
