# Optical Spectra Properties of Neutral Zn-Doped $Au_{20}$ Nanoclusters by First-Principles Calculations

Xiao-Dong Zhang · Mei-Li Guo · Di Wu ·
Pei-Xun Liu · Yuan-Ming Sun · Liang-An Zhang ·
Fei-Yue Fan

Received: 14 April 2011 / Accepted: 25 July 2011 / Published online: 4 August 2011

## Abstract
Gold nanoclusters are promising for the potential applications in photothermal therapy, but visible optical absorption of $Au_{20}$ (gap $=1.818$ eV) limits the in vivo applications. A first-principles study has been performed to evaluate the electronic and optical properties of neutral $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ ($n=0$–4) clusters. We have employed the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof form of generalized gradient approximation in the frame work of density functional theory. The results of imaginary part of the dielectric function $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ and optical absorption indicate that the optical transition of $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ between HOMO and LUMO has shifted to the near-infrared range as the Zn atoms incorporation. The subsequent transition energy level shows the Zn $s$ and $p$ states have obvious contributions to both of the HOMO and LUMO. The Zn-doped $Au_{20}$ clusters show the tunable optical properties, and have potential application for in vivo photothermal therapy.

## 1 Introduction
Physical and chemical properties of gold in the nanoscale have attracted significant cross-disciplinary interest during recent years, motivated by photothermal therapy, radio-therapy, and cancer cell imaging. Especially, gold nanostructure is promising for the potential applications in photothermal therapy because of the good biocompatibility and plasmon heat [1–5]. However, a bottleneck of photothermal therapy is that the optical absorption of gold nanoparticles, well known Surface plasmon resonance (SPR), is always located in the visible light range, which limits the wider application of in vivo photothermal therapy. To utilize the high optical transmission of body tissue, it is necessary to enhance the Near-infrared (NIR) optical absorption. Several groups have developed the gold nanorod, nanocage, and core or shell structure, which are recognized as the promising materials for photothermal therapy [6, 7]. However, the increasing size would obviously reduce the permeability of tutor tissue, which is disadvantageous to the photothermal therapy.

Gold clusters are proposed as a kind of alternative materials. Sun et al. have showed the $Au-SiO_{2}$ clusters can induce the unusual optical transition, and the optical absorption can be modulated to the NIR easily, which was also conformed by the recent photothermal therapy and drug delivery experiment [8]. However, procedure of designing the gold clusters with NIR optical absorption are

influenced obviously, because of the long time controversy on the structure of gold clusters. Recently, the obvious progressing has been achieved by lots of DFT calculations and optical spectra experiments [9, 10]. The common view is that gold clusters prefer to the two dimension structure in the range of $Au_4$–$Au_{13}$, while the $Au_{14}$–$Au_{20}$ show three dimension structure [11].The more complex cage-like $Au_{32}$–$Au_{38}$ clusters have been predicted, although the experimental result may always be contradicted. The optical absorption of $Au_2$–$Au_{13}$, $Au_{19}$, and $Au_{20}$ has been calculated by TDDFT, while the optical transition of $Au_{32}$ is also focused due to the better structural stability. Especially, the tetrahedral $Au_{20}$ cluster shows the band gap of $\sim 1.818$ eV, and shows the slight NIR absorption [12–14]. Thus, whether other three dimensions structures of doped-$Au_{20}$ clusters have the similar or better NIR optical absorption inspires our interesting. Indeed, the early investigations showed that Au–Zn bond was stronger than the Au–Au bond and gave an extra $\sigma$-bonding interaction by the overlap between vacant Zn $4p$ and valence Au $6s$ $(5d)$ orbitals [15]. Recently, Wang et al [16] have carried out the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of gold cluster $(n=2$–$10)$, and shows the Zn doping can induce a pronounced odd–even oscillation with the number of Au atoms, when the cluster size increases. Besides, the Cu and Ag-doped gold clusters have been widely investigated by density functional theory [17–20]. Based on the enhancement of stability of $Au_{20}$ clusters, we investigated the electronic structure and optical transition of Zn-doped $Au_{20}$, namely $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$.

## 2 Computational Section

The calculations are based on density functional theory (DFT), using a plane-wave pseudopotential method [21]. We use the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the scheme of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) to describe the exchange–correlation functional [22]. Norm-conserving pseudopotential is used to describe the electron–ion interaction. In this code, the plane wave functions of valence electrons are expanded in a plane wave basis set, and the use of norm-conserving pseudopotential allows a plane wave energy cutoff. Only plane waves with smaller kinetic energies are used in the expansion. Reciprocal-space integration over the Brillouin zone is approximated through a careful sampling at finite number of k-points using a Monkhorst–Pack mesh [23]. In present system, full electron calculation for the $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ clusters is rather computationally expensive, so it is better to introduce effective core potentials for the Au and Zn atoms to describe the inner-core electrons. Under this approximation, the $5d^{10}6s^1$ outermost valence electrons of the Au atom and $3d^{10}4s^2$ outermost valence electrons of the Zn atom are described. It is well known that the interaction of a photon with the electrons in the system can be described in terms of time-dependent perturbations of the ground-state electronic states. Optical transitions between occupied and unoccupied states are caused by the electric field of the photon. The spectra from the excited states can be described as a joint density of states between the valence and conduction band. The momentum matrix elements, which are used to calculate the $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$, are calculated between occupied and unoccupied states, which are given by the eigen vectors obtained as solution of the corresponding Schrödinger equation. Evaluating these matrix elements, one uses the corresponding eigen functions of each of the occupied and unoccupied states [22]:

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(\hbar \omega)=\frac{2 e^{2} \pi}{\Omega \varepsilon_{0}} \sum_{k, v, c}\left|\psi_{k}^{c}|u \times r| \psi_{k}^{v} n\right\rangle\left.\right|^{2} \delta\left(E_{k}^{c}-E_{k}^{v}-\hbar \omega\right)
$$

where $\Omega$ is the volume of the elementary cell, $v$ and $c$ represent the valence and conduction bands, respectively, $k$ represents the k point, $\omega$ is the frequency of the incident light, and $u$ is the vector defining the polarization of the electric field of the incident light, which is averaged over all spatial directions in the polycrystalline case. The real part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ can be evaluated from the imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ by the famous Kramer–Kronig relationship.

The calculations are performed at $20 \times 20$ Å supercell, which contains 20 neutral Au atoms. These modeling of gold clusters refer to the previous work, which has shown the best structural stability and average distance between Au–Au bonding is about $2.73$ Å [23]. The substitutional method has been taken into account in this paper, and Zn atoms are used to substitute Au atoms in vertex site (see Fig. 1). In this way, the $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ clusters are built. For weakening the interaction with Zn–Zn, the separation distance is as far as possible. We choose the energy cutoff to be 720 eV, and the Brillouin-zone sampling mesh parameters for the k-point set are $2 \times 2 \times 2$ for $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ clusters. The charge densities are converged to $2 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/atom in the self-consistent calculation. And then, the $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ clusters are optimized with the positions of substitutional atoms. In the optimization process, the energy change, maximum force, maximum stress, and maximum displacement tolerances are set as $2 \times 10^{-5}$ eV/atom, $0.05$ eV/Å, $0.1$ GPa, and $0.002$ Å, respectively.

## 3 Results and Discussions

The stability of gold nanostructure is very important for the electronic and optical properties. Thus, the binding energy

per atom $E_b(n)$ of gold clusters is calculated for evaluating the stability of gold clusters, which are defined as following formula:

$$
E_{b}(n)=\frac{n E(Z n)+(20-n) E(A u)-E\left(A u_{20-n} Z n_{n}\right)}{n}
$$

where $E(Zn)$, $E(Au)$, and $E(Au_{20-n}Zn_n)$ represent the total energies of the most stable Zn, Au, and $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ clusters, respectively. It is worth pointing out that all of the clusters are found to prefer the lowest spin state. We find the binding energy of $Au_{20}$ is 2.40 eV, which is very close to the previous investigations of gold clusters [10]. However, it is noting that the binding energy of $Au_{19}Zn_1$ is 2.48 eV, which is higher than the $Au_{20}$ of 2.4 eV. It shows the single Zn atom incorporation can enhance the structural stability. Indeed, Au-Zn bond is stronger than the Au-Au bond and gives an extra $\sigma$-bonding interaction by the overlap between vacant Zn $4p$ and valence Au $6s$ $(5d)$ orbital, which is very similar to the previous Zn-doped gold clus- ters. However, with increasing atoms number of Zn, the binding energy of $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ has decreased from 2.48 eV to 2.16 eV, which is lower than that of the $Au_{20}$. It indicates that Zn doping can improve the structural stability of gold clusters in low doping concentration, and heavy Zn incorporations may decrease the structural stability of $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$.

Figure 2 gives the density of states (DOS) of pure $Au_{20}$ cluster. $Au_{20}$ cluster shows the large HOMO-LUMO gap, which is consistent with the previous experimental results [10, 24]. The exact band gap of $Au_{20}$ is 1.48 eV, which is less than the experimental data of 1.78 eV (or 1.818 eV) of $Au_{20}$. Meanwhile, the Au $d$ states are dominated in HOMO, and are located in the range of -6 and -2 eV. HOMO consists of Au $s$ and $d$ states, and the Au $d$ states are dominant. Figure 3a shows the DOS to reveal the elec- tronic structure of $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ clusters. It is clearly seen that, when $n$ changes from 1 to 4, the DOS also changes. In general, the band gaps of $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ clusters are less than that of pure $Au_{20}$ cluster. It confirms that the Zn incorpo- ration into $Au_{20}$ can induce the obvious band gap nar- rowing, which is consistent with the previous results [25]. The exact band gaps of $Au_{20-n}Zn_n$ are 1.27, 1.18, 1.15, and 1.13 eV, which are corresponding to the different $n$ values from 1 to 4, respectively. It is necessary to point out that the HOMO is shifted to the high energy range obviously, which can be clearly seen in the Fig. 3a. This shift can lead

For investigating the optical transition of $Au_{20-n}Cu_{n}$ clusters, it is necessary to investigate the imaginary part of the dielectric function, because it is very important for optical properties of any materials. The momentum matrix elements, which are used to calculate the $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ are calculated between occupied and unoccupied states, which are given by the eigen vectors obtained as solution of the corresponding Schrödinger equation. The real part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ can be evaluated from the imaginary part $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ by the famous Kramer-Kronig relationship [26, 27]. Figure 4 gives the imaginary part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ of $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ clusters. To the $Au_{20}$, there are three main peaks in $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$, which are 1.79, 2.51, and 3.87 eV, respectively (namely $E_{1}$, $E_{2}$, and $E_{3}$), which are very close to the previous results of 1.86, 2.78, and 3.74 eV [24]. Zn incorporation induces some obvious variations of optical transitions. First, $E_{1}$ has shifted to the low energy range with the increasing Zn atoms incorporation. The $E_{1}$ of $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$, $Au_{18}Zn_{2}$, $Au_{17}Zn_{3}$, and $Au_{16}Zn_{4}$ is 1.64, 1.69, 1.47, and 0.85 eV, respectively. Second, the red-shifts of $E_{2}$ in $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ clusters have also been observed. It can be found that $E_{2}$ has shifted from 2.52 eV of $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$ to 2.15 eV of $Au_{16}Zn_{4}$. Finally, Zn incorporation causes the blue-shifts of $E_{3}$ with slight decrease of intensity. To understand these optical phenomenons in detail, it is necessary to analyze the optical transition by electronic states.

Figure 5 shows the transition energy level of $Au_{20}$, $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$, and $Au_{16}Zn_{4}$ from band structure calculations. It can be seen that direct transition can be caused between HOMO and LUMO. Therefore, it can be expected that $E_{1}$ (1.79 eV) should mainly be caused by optical transitions between Au $6s$ states in LUMO and Au $6s$ 55% (and Au $p$, 45%) states in HOMO, which are close to the other first-principles evaluation (1.86 eV) [16]. Meanwhile, it is not far from the HOMO-LUMO gap of 1.48 eV. In the PDOS of $Au_{20}$, the Au $d$ states produce the two peaks, 1.41 and 1.97 eV, which can induce some electronic states in the energy level range of $-4$-$0$ eV. Thus, the two dominate optical transitions of $E_{2}$ and $E_{3}$ can be due to the optical transitions between Au $d$ states and Au Au $6s$ (and Au $p$) states.

The outline of optical transitions for $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$ and $Au_{16}Zn_{4}$ clusters can be found in the Fig. 5b and c. The optical transition between HOMO-LUMO has been affected by Zn incorporation. The Zn $p$ and $s$ states have been found in HOMO, which can be main reason for the red-shifts of $E_{2}$. Meanwhile, the Zn $p$ (22%) and $s$ (78%) states also have contributions to the HOMO, which may be responsible for the red-shifts of $E_{2}$. The blue-shifts of $E_{3}$ may be related to the decrease of Au $d$ states by Zn incorporation. The increasing Zn atom induces the more dispersive in $d$ states, which may cause more discrete in electronic configurations. Thus, it is responsible for the decreased binding energy of $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ with the increasing Zn atoms incorporation. From the mechanism of optical transition, it can be found that the Zn incorporations have significant contributions to both of HOMO and LUMO. The Zn $s$ and $p$ states can introduce some electronic states in the gap and decrease the gap of $Au_{20}$. The decreased gap can cause some new optical transition and optical absorption band in the low energy range. Thus, the NIR absorption can be created by Zn incorporation. However, more valuable information is still necessary to probe by optical absorption.

We need to consider two possible effects on the optical absorption of these $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ clusters. One the one hand, the HOMO-LUMO gap can be underestimated by the DFT. In this situation, the NIR absorption may be achieved by adding one Zn atoms into $Au_{20}$ clusters (namely $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$). On the other hand, the structure stability of $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ should also be taken into account. In the geometry calculations, the binding energy of $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$ is higher than that of pure $Au_{20}$ cluster, which show better structural stability. The decreased binding energy, however, has been noticed in the $Au_{18}Zn_{2}$, $Au_{17}Zn_{3}$, and $Au_{16}Zn_{4}$ due to adding excessive Zn atoms. Therefore, less Zn atom incorporation, such as $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$, may be promising for the further fabrication, NIR absorption and related applications. Zorriasa-tein et al. have proposed that Cu incorporation can modulate band gap effectively, and the results showed that the introduction of Cu, enhanced the binding energy per atom as compared to Au clusters [25]. Au-Ag clusters also showed the good structural stability and optical properties [20, 28, 29]. Enhanced stability of Au clusters has been predicted by less Zn incorporation. Thus, it can be expected that Zn incorporation is effective strategy for modulating optical properties of Au clusters. Our results also clearly show that the Zn incorporation can modulate the optical properties of $Au_{20}$ nanoclusters. These methods have also potential applications in understanding luminescence of metal nanoclusters and designing materials for photothermal therapy.

## 4 Conclusions

In summary, a first-principles study has been performed to evaluate the electronic and optical properties of $Au_{20-n}Zn_{n}$ clusters in different configurations. The $Au_{19}Zn_{1}$ has better structural stability than that of $Au_{20}$ nanoclusters and $Au_{18}Zn_{2}$, $Au_{17}Zn_{3}$, and $Au_{16}Zn_{4}$ show decrease of binding energy and structural stability. The increasing Zn concentration can induce new electronic states and the HOMO-LUMO gap narrowing, and the band gap of HOMO-LUMO has decreased from 1.48 to 1.13 eV. Subsequently, the optical transition between HOMO-LUMO has shifted to the low energy range with the increasing Zn concentration. Our results clearly show that the Zn incorporation can modulate the optical properties of $Au_{20}$ nanoclusters.
