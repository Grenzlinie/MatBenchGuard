## ABSTRACT

The optical properties of $SbBiI_3$ alloys have been investigated experimentally by absorption measurements and theoretically by a full-potential augmented plane wave (FPLAPW) method within the generalized gradient approximation. The fundamental band-gap energy of these alloys changes from $BiI_3$- to $SbI_3$-like with increasing percentage of Sb content. The calculated band-gap energies as well as the optical absorption were found to be in a very good qualitatively agreement with the experimental results. We present calculated density-of-states as well as the dielectric functions for evaluation of future experiments.

## Introduction

$Sb_xBi_{1-x}I_3$ alloys have very large band-gap energies and are therefore very promising materials for development as room temperature gamma-ray detectors and non-linear applications. But so far very little is actually known about the physical properties of $BiI_3$ and $SbI_3$ and even less about $Sb_xBi_{1-x}I_3$ [1,2]. These materials have hexagonal structures, similar to $PbI_2$, which has large applicability as room temperature detectors [3]. The fundamental band-gap energies of these alloys change from $BiI_3$- to $SbI_3$-like, i.e., from about 2.3 to 2.0eV.

In this work, we have investigated the optical properties of $SbBiI_3$ alloys, both experimentally and theoretically. A transmission spectroscopy technique has been used for the measurements of the optical band-gap energies. The calculatiosn of the absorption, density-of-states (DOS) and dielectric functions were performed by the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FPLAPW) method [4], choosing the exchange-correlation potential of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof [5].

### Experimental details

The experimental transmission spectroscopy apparatus consists of a halogen lamp used as the light source for the measurement. The polychromatic beam is diffracted by plane diffraction gratings attached to a step motor. The beam can be varied from 850 to 360nm corresponding to phonon energies from 1.46 to 3.44 eV; a set of lens and collimator produces a monochromatic light focused onto the sample. A first order bandpass filter has been used to avoid an eventual second order contamination of the monochromatic light, which in the intrinsic resolution, obtained by the calibration process, is 1.2 nm or 0.2%. The angular spread of the beam at the sample location is 17°. Taking into account the whole equipment and analysis process, the absorption optical error associated with the systematic error gives a final total resolution of 2.8% in the energy gap determination. The light passes through the sample and is detected by a photomultiplier tube (PTM) (EMI 9558), polarized by a negative voltage from –1800 to –2200 V. The light gain sensitivity of this device is enough to measure a very low phonon quantity (>100). For a very opaque sample it is possible to increase the measuring sensibility (X500) connecting a very-low noise homemade amplifier to the PMT output, with no compromise of the signal to noise ratio.

The $\text{Sb}_{x}\text{Bi}_{1-x}\text{I}_{3}$ samples of different compositions ($x = 0.0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9$ and 1.0) were prepared by the conventional melt quenching technique, followed by an annealing procedure. To ensure the stoichiometric composition of the pseudo-binary alloys, required amounts of $\text{BiI}_{3}$ and $\text{SbI}_{3}$ compounds, grown by the Bridgman method from high purity (5N) elements, were used as source materials.

Appropriate amounts of $\text{BiI}_{3}$ and $\text{SbI}_{3}$ were introduced in a quartz ampoule (10mm inner diameter and 1mm thickness), evacuated to $10^{-6}$ Torr and sealed. The ampoule was then kept in a furnace at a temperature of 435 °C and was periodically shaked to ensure good homogeneous mixing of the melt.

After 24 hours the ampoule was rapidly quenched in water and followed by an annealing at 100 °C during 72 hours. After this, the furnace was turned off and let to cool down until room temperature.

The resulting material was ground to powder in a agate mortar and cold pressed pellet samples of about 1mm thickness and 13mm diameter were prepared by hardned steel die under 400Mpa of pressure. Since the element iodine has a large vapor pressure, the heating rate was 100 °C/day in order to allow a considerable time for reaction between Bi and Sb with $\text{I}_{2}$. If these elements are reacted under at higher rate, the released exothermic energy might heat the uncombined $\text{I}_{2}$, leading to the possibility of an explosion at the quartz ampoule due to excessive internal pressure.

### Computational methods

$\text{SbI}_{3}$ (and $\text{BiI}_{3}$) has trigonal crystal symmetry ($\text{C}^{1}_{3i}$), described within a hexagonal coordinate system. In the theoretical picture, the $\text{Sb}_{x}\text{Bi}_{1-x}\text{I}_{3}$ crystal is obtained by replacing Sb atoms on the $2c$ and $2d$ lattice sites with Bi atoms. Thus, the trigonal symmetry is remained and we do not consider off-stoichiometric symmetries. The lattice constants and the internal lattice parameter were obtained by minimizing the total energy using a pseudopotential plane wave method with ultrasoft potentials, 63 k-points in the irreducible Brillouin zone, and an energy cut-off of 125 eV [6,7]. The calculations of the electronic structure and of the optical properties are based on a scalar-relativistic, full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method [4], choosing the exchange-

correlation potential of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof [5] which is derived within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). The crystal potential was obtained by self- consistence means using 16 k-points in the irreducible Brillouin zone and about 2700 plane waves per k-point. In the atomic region the basis set consisted of spherical harmonics with azimuthal quantum number up to $l=10$, and a non-spherical contribution up to $l=4$.

The GGA (and the local density approximation) is well known to not be able to predict the fundamental band-gap energy in semiconductors and insulators due to incorrect treatment of self-interaction and correlation together plus not considering correlation effects of excitation. However, it is believed that the qualitatively trends in the band-gap energies are relatively well described.

The optical properties were calculated in transverse direction $(\perp)$ with a k-mesh of 276 k-points in the IR-BZ. The imaginary part of the dielectric function, $\varepsilon_{2 \perp}(\omega)$, in the long wavelength limit have been obtained directly from the electronic structure, using the joint density-of- states and the optical matrix overlap, see Ref. [8].

The real part of the dielectric function, $\varepsilon_{1 \perp}(\omega)$, was calculated from the Kramers- Kronig dispersion relations.

## Conclusion

We have investigated the electronic and optical properties of $\text{Sb}_{x}\text{Bi}_{1-x}\text{I}_{3}$ alloys using optical absorption and also theoretically using the FPLAPW method within the generalized gradient approximation. Our calculated and experimental values of the bandgap energies present a reasonable agreement between them.The calculated density of states and the dielectric functions are presented for evaluation of future experiments.
