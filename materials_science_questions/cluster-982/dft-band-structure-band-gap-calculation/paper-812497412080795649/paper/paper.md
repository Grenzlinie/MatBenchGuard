# Unexpected aliovalent cation substitution between two NLO materials $LiBa_3Bi_6(SeO_3)_7F_{11}$ and $Ba_{3}Bi_{6.5}(SeO_3)_7F_{10.5}O_{0.5}^\dagger$

Shuangshuang Shi, $^{ab}$ Chensheng Lin, $^{c}$ Dan Zhao, $^{d}$ Min Luo, $^{*a}$ Liling Cao, $^{ab}$ Guang Peng $^{a}$ and Ning Ye $^{*ae}$

One new combination, alkali-metal and alkaline earth-metal selenite fluoride, $LiBa_3Bi_6(SeO_3)_7F_{11}$ (LBBSF) and its analogue $Ba_{3}Bi_{6.5}(SeO_3)_7F_{10.5}O_{0.5}$ (BBSF) were reported here for the first time. Unusual aliovalent cation substitution between them affected the layer thickness and made the bond strains of $[SeO_3]$ enhanced, thereby inducing greater distortions and affecting the SHG efficiencies. This work may provide thoughts for exploring new NLO materials.

Polar materials have been extensively studied in academic and technological fields because of their unique physicochemical properties, such as quadratic nonlinear optics (NLOs), piezoelectricity, pyroelectricity, ferroelectricity, $etc.^{1}$ To date, an effective strategy for obtaining polar materials is introducing one or several acentric fundamental building blocks (FBBs) into one compound. $^{2}$ The acentric FBBs mainly include: (1) acentric $\pi$-conjugated groups, such as $[BO_3]^{3-}$, $[CO_3]^{2-}$, $[NO_3]^{-}$, and $[HC_3N_3O_3]^{2-}$ anionic groups; (2) oxyhalide/oxide/halide groups with centered cations having stereo-active lone pairs (SALP), such as $Pb^{2+}$, $Bi^{3+}$, and $Se^{4+}$; (3) $d^0$-centered polyhedra ($Ti^{4+}$, $Nb^{5+}$, $V^{5+}$, $Mo^{6+}$, $W^{6+}$, $etc.$); (4) $d^{10}$-containing blocks ($Hg^{2+}$, $Sn^{4+}$, $Zn^{2+}$, $Cd^{2+}$, $etc.$). Thereinto, cations of $Se^{4+}$ and $Bi^{3+}$ with SALP normally exhibit highly asymmetric coordination environments, which are very helpful for generating large birefringence and second harmonic generation (SHG) response. $^{3}$ Therefore, Bi-containing selenites are a quiet attractive research system for NLO crystals.

In addition, we also introduced fluorine atoms and alkali metals in the novel Bi-containing selenites based on the following considerations. Firstly, fluorine, having the largest electronegativity in the halogen group, can be used for widening the band gap, acting as chemical tailoring "scissors" or inducing large distortion, thereby being admitted into research systems. $^{4}$ Secondly, alkali-metals without the d-d or f-f electronic transitions in the closed d or f orbitals not only can enlarge the band gaps, such as in $ABi_2(IO_3)_2F_5$ (A = K, Rb, and Cs) ($\sim$3.75, $\sim$3.78 and $\sim$3.84 eV), $^{5}$ $ABi_2SeO_3F_5$ (A = K, Rb) ($\sim$4.08 and $\sim$4.18 eV), $^{2i}$ and $ACdCO_3F$ (A = K, Rb) (A = K, Rb) ($\sim$5.30 and $\sim$5.35 eV), $^{6}$ but also can play structural modulation roles, such as in $Aln(SeO_3)_2$ (A = Na, K, Rb, and Cs). $^{1c}$ It is noteworthy that a mix of alkali metals and alkaline earth metals in selenite fluoride has still never been studied up to now.

In consideration of the above ideas, in this study, we focus on the exploration of the alkali metals-alkaline earth metals-Bi-Se-O-F system. As a result, two new noncentrosymmetrical (NCS) compounds, $LiBa_3Bi_6(SeO_3)_7F_{11}$ (LBBSF) and $Ba_{3}Bi_{6.5}(SeO_3)_7F_{10.5}O_{0.5}$ (BBSF) with outstanding linear and nonlinear optical properties, were obtained $via$ a mild hydrothermal method. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that alkali-metal and alkaline earth-metal selenite fluoride has been reported. Meanwhile, it is interesting that an unexpected aliovalent cation substitution has been observed between LBBSF and BBSF, which modulates the structures to affect SHG efficiencies. Herein, we will report the synthesis, crystal structures, thermal analysis and optical properties for both compounds.

Single crystals of LBBSF and BBSF were synthesized through a facile hydrothermal method (see the detail in the ESI$\dagger$). The detailed crystallographic data and structural refinement were presented in Tables S2-S8 (ESI$\dagger$). The phase purities were analysed by a powder XRD method (Fig. S1, ESI$\dagger$). LBBSF featured a three-dimensional (3D) double-layered framework comprised of 2D $[Bi_{6}(SeO_{3})_{7}F_{11}]^{\infty}$ anionic layers with $Li^{+}/Ba^{2+}$ inserting in the interlayer space. Its asymmetric unit had one $Li^{+}$, three $Ba^{2+}$, six $Bi^{3+}$, seven selenites and eleven $F^{-}$. Thereinto, $Se^{4+}$ cations containing stereo-active lone pairs were bonded to three O atoms in triangle pyramid environments with lengths of Se-O ranging from 1.698 to 1.727 Å, which were in accordance with that reported before.⁷ Two unique Bi were linked to O and F atoms forming $Bi(1)O_{4}F_{4}$ and $Bi(2)O_{5}F_{3}$ polyhedra, among which the Bi-O lengths were from 2.376 to 2.799 Å and the lengths of Bi-F were in the range of 2.120-2.614 Å. Then three $Bi(1)O_{4}F_{4}$ and three $Bi(2)O_{5}F_{3}$ units were connected by common O atoms to generate a double-layered multimer along the $c$ axis (Fig. S3, ESI†). Independent multimers were bridged through $SeO_{3}$ groups to form double-layered topology in the $ab$-plane. As charge-balancing components, $Li^{+}$ situated between $Bi_{3}$ 3-membered polyhedra double-layered rings and $Ba^{2+}$ cations were effectively "trapped" between $Bi_{3}Se_{2}$ 5-membered double-layered rings (Fig. 1a-c).⁸ In particular, because there was no -OH vibration in the IR transmittance spectra of both LBBSF and BBSF, the element was set as F in consideration of charge balance (Fig. S5, ESI†).

The overall structure of BBSF was rather similar to that of LBBSF. The major difference was that cations within the interlayer space $Li^{+}$ were replaced by $Bi(3)^{3+}$ in BBSF, among which the Bi(3) was in a split state. It is noteworthy that this unexpected substitution⁹ affected their SHG efficiencies *via* modulating their structures. Specifically, it was found that the interlayer distances were enlarged slightly (Fig. 1d). What's more, layer thickness shrunk to some extent in the process of structural evolution, which made the bond strains of $[SeO_{3}]$ FBBs in the layer enhanced¹⁰ and thus induced a greater degree of distortion. As shown in Table S9 (ESI†), the dipole moments of $[SeO_{3}]$ were actually all enlarged in BBSF compared to those in LBBSF, which were in favor of the generation of large macroscopic polarization. Thus, BBSF showed a larger SHG response than that of LBBSF.

Thermogravimetric (TG) and differential thermal analysis (DTA) curves revealed that LBBSF and BBSF were stable to 485 °C and 477 °C respectively (Fig. S2, ESI†), which were obviously higher than most of the known metal selenites, such as $BiFSeO_{3}$ (300 °C), $BaBi(SeO_{3})_{2}Cl$ (300 °C), $ABi_{2}SeO_{3}F_{5}$ (A = K(340 °C), Rb(300 °C)), $A_{2}Bi_{2}(SeO_{3})_{3}F_{2}$ (A = K(417 °C), Rb(370 °C)), $Pb_{2}GaF_{2}(SeO_{3})_{2}Cl$ (400 °C), and $Pb_{2}NbO_{2}(SeO_{3})_{2}Cl$ (365 °C). As can be seen, the weight losses of the title compounds experienced two stages, which were mainly attributable to the release of $SeO_{2}$ and $F_{2}$. And the corresponding experimental decomposition ratios were in good agreement with the calculated ones.

IR spectra showed that LBBSF and BBSF possessed favorable transmittance in the IR region of 2.5-14.8 µm (4000-677 cm⁻¹). The assignments of absorption peaks exhibited in Fig. S5 (ESI†)were consistent with those reported previously.¹¹ UV-vis-NIR spectra suggest that the band gaps of both were 3.80 and 3.56 eV respectively (Fig. S4, ESI†), corresponding to UV cut-off edges of 326 nm and 348 nm. Comprehensively, good transparency range of 0.33/0.35 - 14.8 µm covered the UV-vis and IR region, making them good candidates as UV-vis-IR NLO materials.

Powder SHG measurements indicated that LBBSF and BBSF were approximately 2.5 and 4.0 times that of KDP at a 150-210 µm particle size respectively (Fig. 2b). The intensities of the SHG signals were gradually increased with the increase of particle size, which manifested that both compounds were phase-matchable materials (Fig. 2a). To further elaborate their SHG response differences, we performed the dipole moment analysis. As presented in Table S9 (ESI†), both $[SeO_{3}]$ and $[BiO_{x}F_{y}]$ polyhedra all made notable contributions to the NLO coefficients in the title compounds. Thereinto, the calculated local dipole moments of $[SeO_{3}]$ FBBs in BBSF were 8.2-10.6 D, slightly larger than those in LBBSF (7.1-10.0 D). That was exactly because counterions $Li^{+}$ were

The net macroscopic polarization of BBSF, containing $[SeO_{3}]$ units with the dipole moments being enlarged by enhanced bond strains, was calculated to be 17.8 D, larger than that of LBBSF (14.9 D), which completely supported the result of structural analysis. To gain further insights into electronic structure and optical properties, systematic theory calculations were carried out based on DFT methods.¹² In the band structures of LBBSF and BBSF, the conduction band (CB) minima were at the $K$-point and $G$-point respectively. The corresponding valence band (VB) maxima were just a little higher than the VB values of the $K$- or $G$-point (about 0.02 eV) respectively. Thus, we can take it as a flat line, and thereby both compounds having quasi-direct band gaps (Fig. S6, ESI†).¹³ The theoretical values of the band gaps are 3.738 eV and 1.813 eV respectively, which were smaller than the measured values 3.80 eV and 3.56 eV. The inherent underestimation originated from the GGA method itself. In order to ensure the accuracy of the calculations of the optical properties, scissor values 0.062 eV and 1.747 eV were employed to match the experimental gaps.

Because the partial density of states (PDOS) curves of LBBSF and BBSF were similar to each other, we only described LBBSF in detail. The top of the VB was dominated by F $2p$, O $2p$, Se $4s4p$ and Bi $6s$, while O $2p$, Se $4s4p$ and Bi$6p$ occupied the bottom of the CB (Fig. S7, ESI†). This revealed that the band gap of LBBSF was mainly determined by Bi, Se, O and F elements. In both compounds, the F $2p$ orbitals in the $-6$ to 0 eV or $-8$ to 0 eV region of the VB were highly hybrid with the orbitals of neighboring ions, which led to the decrease of the VB bandwidths and enlargement of the bandgaps. Through comparative analysis, the structural differences between LBBSF and BBSF were also displayed in PDOS obviously. The orbital contribution ratio of $Bi(6p)/Se(4p)$ at the bottom of the CB in BBSF was obviously larger than that of LBBSF, which verified the fact that the number density ratio of Bi/Se in BBSF was higher than that in LBBSF.

The space group $P3_{1}m$ of the two compounds belonged to point group $3m$. Based on the Kleinman symmetry,¹⁴ three independent nonvanishing SHG tensors $d_{22}$, $d_{31}$, and $d_{33}$ remained (Fig. S7, ESI†). The largest ones were $d_{33}$ (2.47 pm V⁻¹) and $d_{22}$ (1.36 pm V⁻¹) for LBBSF and BBSF respectively in the static limit, which matched up with the corresponding measured values. In addition, the SHG-weighted density method was employed to clearly illuminate the origin of the contribution of the SHG effects. As shown in Fig. 3, it was clear that the synergistic effect of the $[SeO_{3}]$ and $[BiO_{x}F_{y}]$ polyhedra led to the SHG process in the two compounds.

Furthermore, the calculated refractive indices of LBBSF and BBSF are displayed in Fig. S10 (ESI†). The results were $n_{e} < n_{o}$, suggesting that both compounds were negative uniaxial crystals. The birefringence $(n_{o}-n_{e})$ was calculated to be 0.133 and 0.123 at 546.1 nm respectively, matching closely with the experimental values 0.130 and 0.121, which were large enough to achieve phase-matching.
