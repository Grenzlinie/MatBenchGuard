# Electronic and structural properties
of strontium chalcogenides SrS, SrSe and SrTe

D. Rached*, M. Rabah, N. Benkhettou, B. Soudini, and H. Abid

Applied Materials Laboratory, Physics Department, Research Centre (ex CFTE), route de Mascara,
University of Djillali LIABES Sidi Bel-Abbès (22000), Algeria

Received 17 April 2004, accepted 4 June 2004
Published online 5 August 2004

PACS 62.20.Dc, 71.15.Ap, 71.20.Nr

We present the results of a first-principles study of the electronic and structural properties of strontium chalcogenides, SrS, SrSe and SrTe. The computational method is based on the full-potential linear muffin-tin orbitals method (FP-LMTO) augmented by a plane-wave basis (PLW). The exchange and correlation energy is described in the local density approximation (LDA) using the Perdew-Wang parameterization including a generalized gradient approximation (GGA). The calculated results of the structural properties are given for the NaCl (B1) and CsCl (B2) structures. We have also carried out band-structure calculations for the three considered compound, but only for the NaCl (B1) structure. A reasonable agreement is found from the comparison of our results with other theoretical calculations and experimental data.

## 1 Introduction

The alkaline-earth chalcogenides (AX: A = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba; X = O, S, Se, Te) form a very important closed shell ionic system crystallizing in NaCl-type structure at room conditions except for the MgTe and beryllium chalcogenides. These compounds are technologically important materials, having a wide range of applications ranging from catalysis to microelectronics. Alkaline-earth metal sulfides are used as X-ray cathodes and photoluminophors [1, 2]. They are actively investigated both theoretically as well as experimentally [3]. Among the alkaline earth chalcogenides very little information is available for strontium chalcogenides (SrS, SrSe and SrTe). SrS, SrSe and SrTe have been found to undergo first-order phase transition from sixfold-coordinated B1 structure to eightfold-coordinated CsCl-type B2 structure at pressures of 0.18, 0.142 and 0.12 Mbar [4–6].

SrS, SrSe and SrTe have been the subject of extensive theoretical studies. The calculations reported by Stepanyuk et al. [3] are based on the self-consistent linear augmented plane-wave (LAPW) method [7], while those of Marinelli et al. [8] are based on the linear combination of atomic orbitals self-consistent field (LCAO-SCF) method [9] and those of Shameen Banu et al. [10] are based on the tight-binding linear muffin-tin orbital method within the atomic-sphere approximation (ASA) [11]. Finally, the calculations reported by Khenata et al. [12] are based on the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW) method [13]. Different parametrizations or approaches have been used to determine the exchange correlation contribution [14–19] within the ab-initio techniques.

In the present paper, we have utilized, the full-potential linear muffin-tin orbitals (FP-LMTO) method augmented by a plane-wave (PLW) basis [20, 21]. This is a first principle method compared to previous (LMTO) methods, the present one treats muffin-tin spheres and interstitial regions on the same footing, leading to improvements in the precision of the eigenvalues. At the same time, this method uses a more complete basis than its predecessors. The crystal is divided into two regions: non-overlapping muffin-tin

Within the spheres, the potential is expanded in spherical harmonics, while in the interstitials regions, it is expanded in terms of Fourier series. The exchange-correlation energy of the electrons is described in the local-density approximation (LDA) using the Perdew-Wang parameterization [22] including gradient corrections (GGA) after Perdew et al. [19]. For the (FP-LMTO) calculations, we have used the available computer code Lmtart [23].

The details of calculations are as follows: the charge density and the potential are represented inside the muffin-tin spheres (MTS) by spherical harmonics up to $l_{\max}=6$. The $k$ integration over Brillouin zone is performed up to a (6,6,6) grid by means of an improved tetrahedron method [24]. Table 1 shows the MTS radius taken for each atomic position. In the interstitial region, the $s$, $p$ and $d$ basis functions are expanded in a number (NPLW) of plane waves determined automatically by the cut-off energies (Table 1). Our calculations of the total energy as a function of volume are performed using Murnaghan's equation of state [25]. To calculate the elastic constants, we adopt the same scheme for deformation as those used by Mehl et al. [26, 27]. Two kinds of deformations on the cubic crystal were used in this study; a monoclinic strain-conserving volume that determines $C_{44}$, and an orthorhombic strain conserving volume that determines $C_{11}-C_{12}$. Using this scheme, the elastic constants are defined at the second derivatives of energy with respect to deformation parameters. The pressure dependence of energy gap in these semiconductors was calculated using the following relations:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial E_{P}}{\partial P}\right)_{T}=\left(\frac{\partial E_{g}}{\partial V}\right)_{T}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}\right)_{T}=-\left(\frac{V_{0}}{B_{0}}\right)_{T}\left(\frac{\partial E_{g}}{\partial V}\right)_{T}. \tag{1}
$$

The volume of $\left(\frac{\partial E_{g}}{\partial V}\right)_{T}$ was obtained directly from the energy gap versus volume calculations, and the value of $\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}\right)_{T}$ was obtained from the compressibility relationship,

$$
K=\frac{1}{B_{0}}=-\left(\frac{1}{V_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}\right)_{T}, \tag{2}
$$

## 2 Results and discussion

### 2.1 The equilibrium lattice constants and the bulk modulus

[19]. The calculated total energies were fitted to the Murnaghan [25] equation of state. The equilibrium volume, bulk modulus and pressure derivative of the bulk modulus, along with experimental measurements [4-6], theoretical values [8, 10, 12, 28] and our GGA calculated values are listed in Table 2 for B1 (NaCl) and B2 (CsCl) structures. [12], which is probably due to the fact that they used the (FP-LAPW) method [13], and including gradient corrections (GGA) after Perdew et al. in a LDA different from ours. ### 2.2 Elastic constants
To study the stability of these compounds in the B1 (NaCl) and B2 (CsCl) structures, we have calculated the elastic constants at the equilibrium lattice and compared our results to the stability criteria [29, 30] using the following relations.

$$
C_{11}-C_{12}>0, \quad C_{44}>0, \quad B_{0}>0. \tag{3}
$$

We have found that in both structures, these criteria are satisfied. The calculated values for B1 end B2 structures are listed in Table 3. We compare our results with those of Khenata et al. [12] and of Marinelli et al. [8], we note that these results are high compared to ours except for $C_{12}$, where our results are high. This is consistent with the fact that the methods for calculations are different and the GGA form is included within LDA different from ours. On the other hand, we can state that this difference is a consequence found in the bulk modulus values. We note an increase of the elastic properties corresponding to the decrease of the nearest-neighbour distance from SrTe, SrSe to SrS.

## 3 Conclusion

In this paper, we have reported successful calculations of the calculations of the structural and electronic properties of strontium chalcogenides SrS, SrSe and SrTe using the full-potential linear muffin-tin orbitals method (FP-LMTO) augmented by a plane-wave basis (PLW). From the structural-properties calculations we have found a good agreement of our equilibrium lattice constants compared to experimental data. However, a discrepancy was found from the comparison with the (FP-LAPW) calculations. One can note that the effect of different approximations, like the (LDA) and the (GGA), on the calculations is cleary seen, which explain the differences found between our values and those of other studies. On the other hand, the method of calculation used in the present work reproduced with accuracy the results of the band structure on the basis of the comparison presented in different tables.

Finally, we can state that the FP-LMTO coupled with a different approximation is a powerful method applied to solid-state physics.
