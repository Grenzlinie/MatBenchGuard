### ABSTRACT
The mixed tetrel Zintl phase $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ has been prepared and structurally characterized. The compound crystallizes in the orthorhombic space group $Pbcn$ (No. 60). The lattice parameters are $a=8.7739(17)$ Å, $b=8.7599(17)$ Å and $c=27.162(5)$ Å. The compound consists of two different blocks of the composition $Ba_2Sn$ and a $Ba_6(Si_3^{6-})_2$, respectively. The $Si_3^{6-}$-units are isolated three membered rings. By the Zintl-Klemm concept the triangles are best described as pseudo-sulfur units with two single bonds per atom. The tin atoms are isolated and have a formal charge of 4-. According to DFT calculations the phase is a semi conductor with a small band gap of 0.19 eV. $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ is related to the double salt $Ba_4Si_3Br_2=(Ba_3Si_3)(BaBr)_2$.

## 1. Introduction
$Ba_8Si_6Sn$ is the first structurally characterized compound in the ternary Ba–Si–Sn phase diagram. The rich variety of (poly-)anions formed in silicides is legendary [1–6]. Frequently observed species comprise isolated ions, dumbbells, tetrahedra, five membered rings, 1D zig-zag-chains, 2D-layers and also 3D-networks, to name just the general topological features. Up to now there was only one compound known to contain Si-triangles the double salt $Ba_4Si_3Br_2$ [7]. The crystal structure and chemical interpretation of the title compound $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ shows remarkable similarities to $Ba_4Si_3Br_2$ and will therefore be presented indicating the relationship between these two compounds.

The phase diagram of the barium silicon binary system contains besides the Si-rich clathrates, five intermediate compositions [8] (see Fig. 1). $Ba_2Si$ crystallizes with the $Co_2Si$ structure type and has isolated $Si^{4-}$-anions. $Ba_5Si_3$ forms the $Cr_5B_3$ structure and can be described as $(Ba^{2+})[Si_2]^{6-}[Si]^{4-}$ with isolated silicon anions and Si–Si dumb-bells [9]. BaSi crystallizes in the CrB structure type [10] with zig-zag chains of silicon atoms. $Ba_3Si_4$ has a butterfly-shaped tetrel anion and the formal charge distribution can be written as $(Ba^{2+})_3[(3b)Si^-]_2[(2b)Si^{2-}]_2$ [11]. However like many other tetrel Zintl compounds, the phase is of metallic character [12]. Unlike the other AESi2 compounds, $BaSi_2$ forms discrete but distorted $[Si_4]^{4-}$ tetrahedra [13] in the orthorhombic modification. This is understood to be due to the large $Ba^{2+}$ cations which prevent the $Si^-$ entities from joining into a polymeric anion. The tetrahedra are isoelectronic to the units in white phosphorus. However, under high pressure and high temperature conditions, $BaSi_2$ undergoes a phase transition from the orthorhombic to a trigonal and then to a cubic phase [14–18]. Only the orthorhombic phase is a semiconductor [19]; the trigonal phase with 2D silicon nets ($EuGe_2$ structure type) is metallic and becomes a superconductor at low temperature [16,20]. The cubic phase has a network of flattened, threebonded silicon atoms of trigonal pyramidal conformation which are connected to form an own chiral 3D network (space group $P4_332$or $P4_132$, respectively). The metal ions are embedded in cavities of the network [18]. In the silicon-rich side of the system the superconducting clathrates [21] such as $Ba_8Si_{46}$ [22] and $Ba_{24}Si_{100}$ have been synthesized at high pressure [23].

In the phase diagram of the Ba/Sn system the compounds $Ba_2Sn$ [24,25], $Ba_5Sn_3$ [26,27], $Ba_3Sn_5$ [28], BaSn [29,30], $BaSn_2$ [31], $BaSn_3$ [32,33] and $BaSn_5$ [34] are structurally characterized. $Ba_2Sn$ crystallizes in the anti-$PbCl_2$ type with isolated $Si^{4-}$ anions. Just like the monosilicides and monogermanides BaSn crystallizes with the CrB structure type, containing Sn zig-zag chains. In $BaSn_2$ ($EuGe_2$ structure type) the Sn atoms form corrugated $6^{3}$-nets. Superconducting $BaSn_3$ has an anti $Ni_3Sn$ structure where the tin atoms occupy the nickel positions. Considering only the shortest Sn–Sn distances, the atoms can form $Sn_3^{2-}$ anions with the barium transferring its valence electrons to the tin. These planar triangular units are stacked above each other in a staggered conformation in a ABAB sequence. The $Sn_3^{2-}$ anions can be compared to the aromatic cyclopropanyl cations, and the slightly longer inter-triangular interactions can be seen as an interaction between the $\pi$ systems.

If the longer Sn-Sn distance is taken into consideration, these stacked triangular anions can be described as a chain of face shar- ing Sn octahedra of $_{\infty}^{1}Sn_{6}^{4-}$. BaSn₅ is also superconducting. It has a primitive hexagonal structure with the tin atoms forming six- membered rings in a graphene-type layer. As the interlayer dis- tances between two layers are only marginally shorter than the Sn-Sn distances within the hexagonal layers, the tin substructure may also be described by fused hexagonal prisms. The centers of the prisms are occupied by tin atoms with a coordination number of 12. Between these prismatic layers there are barium cations symmetric to both of the layers. Ba₅Sn₃ has the tetragonal Cr₅B₃ structure type with both isolated $Sn^{4-}$ anions and $Sn_{2}^{6-}$ dumbbells. Ba₃Sn₅, like Sr₃Sn₅, crystallizes in the Pu₃Pd₅ structure type. The anions form $Sn_{5}^{6-}$ distorted square pyramidal units. Following the Zintl-Klemm concept, the formal charges can be written as $(Ba^{2+})_{3}[Sn_{5}^{6-}]$. This however yields an electron count of 26 elec- trons which does not conform to the (8-N) rule, but can be de- scribed by Wade's rules [35] and thus by the Extended-Zintl- Klemm-Busmann concept [5]. There are no known ternary phases of barium silicon and tin.

## 2. Synthesis

All steps during synthesis, handling and examining of the reac- tants and products were performed under argon atmosphere. A tantalum ampoule was filled with elemental Ba (Alfa-Aesar, 99.5%, purified by distillation), Si (Alfa-Aesar, 99.999+) and Sn (Alfa-Aesar, 99.8%) in a molar ratio of 9:9:1 and sealed. The am- poule was heated to $1470\ ^{\circ}C$ for 5 h and cooled to room tempera- ture at a rate of $100^{\circ}/h$. The product consists of dark grey crystals of the title phase which quickly decompose in contact with air and moisture by hydrolysis.

## 3. Structure determination

A single crystal of suitable size (0.26 × 0.20 × 0.26 mm) was se- lected and sealed under argon in a Mark-tube. A data set covering a full sphere in reciprocal space was collected on a Bruker SMART Platform diffractometer equipped with a CCD-detector. Details of the crystal structure determination are given in Table 1.

The metric of the unit cell is very close to the tetragonal crystal system (cf. Table 2). However, the internal $R$ value clearly indicates to an orthorhombic Laue-symmetry ($R_{\text{int}} = 51\%$ for the Laue-group $4/m$ and $R_{\text{int}} = 8\%$ for the Laue-group $mmm$ before absorption correction). The analysis of the systematic absences leads then unambiguously to the space group $Pbcn$ (No. 60). Some very weak reflections violating the reflection conditions for the glide planes can be attributed to $\lambda/2$-contributions in the X-ray beam. The structure was solved by direct methods and refined by the Full-Matrix-Least-Squares method [36,37]. The crystallographic details, atomic parameters and interatomic distances are given in Tables 2–5.

## 4. Results and discussion

The crystal structure of $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_6\text{Sn}$ consists of two main building blocks: a salt-like block of the net composition $\text{Ba}_2\text{Sn}$ and a Zintl-block with a net composition of $\text{Ba}_6\text{Si}_6$ (cf. Fig. 2a). Like the double salt of barium-silicide and barium bromide, $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$ [7], the compound contains cyclotrisilicidie anions $\text{Si}_3^{6-}$. The Sn atom is isolated with an unusually high formal charge of $4-$. A comparison of the crystal structures of $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_6\text{Sn}$ and $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$ is given in Fig. 2, emphasizing the similarities of the two compounds. The salt-like structure block is shaded in grey.

Even though the composition of the salt-like structure block found in $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_6\text{Sn}$ and $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$ is different ($\text{Ba}_2\text{Sn}$ and $\text{Ba}_2\text{Br}_4$, respectively) the building blocks are remarkably similar. The blocks consist of two layers of corner sharing quadratic pyramids of barium atoms. Fig. 3a and c shows top views, (b) and (d) side views of the structure blocks in $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_6\text{Sn}$ and $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$, respectively. We encounter here a surprisingly elegant way to adapt the same structural motif of the Ba substructure to different requirements regarding the necessary number of counter ions (one $\text{Sn}^{4-}$ ion versus four $\text{Br}^-$ ions). This principle of combining typical structural blocks into more complicated motifs was extensively discussed already some time ago and called either chemical twinning or intergrowth structures, respectively [38–40].

In $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$, the pyramids in adjacent layers have the same eclipsed orientation which leads to the formation of quadratic-prismatic voids with two capped faces. Each of the voids hosts two $\text{Br}^-$ ions. Since the quadratic pyramids are twisted against each other, two additional trigonal prismatic voids occur and if occupied by $\text{Br}^-$ anions result in $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$. In $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_6\text{Sn}$ the two Ba-layers are stacked such that quadratic-antiprismatic voids with two capped faces result ("staggered"). Here, each of the voids hosts only one $\text{Sn}^{4-}$ anion. This layer stacking does not lead to the formation of trigonal prismatic voids as it is observed in $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$. Instead, voids with a tetrahedral star coordination (e.g. a tetrahedron with capped faces) are found. A comparison of the trigangular $\text{Si}_3^{6-}$-units and their Ba-coordination spheres in $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_6\text{Sn}$ and $\text{Ba}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Br}_2$ is given in Fig. 4a and b, respectively. In both compounds the Si–Si bond lengths as well as the coordination by cations is practically identical.

In Fig. 5 the anions ($\text{Sn}^{4-}$ and $\text{Br}^-$, respectively) in the salt-like structure block are shown together with the corresponding Ba-environment. Fig. 5b shows the (empty) void of a tetrahedral star shape in $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_6\text{Sn}$.

Fig. 3. A comparison of the salt-like structure blocks in Ba₈Si₆Sn (a and b) and Ba₄Si₃Br₂ (c and d). The blocks consist of two layers of corner sharing quadratic pyramids of barium atoms. (a and c) a top view, (b and d) a side view of the structure blocks in Ba₈Si₆Sn and Ba₄Si₃Br₂, respectively. In Ba₄Si₃Br₂, the pyramids in the upper layer have the same orientation as the lower one ("eclipsed") which leads to the formation of quadratic-prismatic voids with two capped faces. Each of the voids hosts two Br⁻ ions. Since the quadratic pyramids are twisted against each other, two additional trigonal prismatic voids result in Ba₄Si₃Br₂, each of which host a Br⁻-ion. In Ba₈Si₆Sn the two Ba-layers are stacked such that quadratic-antiprismatic voids with two capped faces result ("staggered"). Each of the voids hosts only one Sn atom. This layer stacking does not lead to the formation of trigonal prismatic voids as it is observed in Ba₄Si₃Br₂. Instead, voids with a tetrahedral star coordination (e.g. a tetrahedron with capped faces) result. Barium is depicted as grey, Sn and Br as black spheres.

Fig. 6. (a) Density of States (DOS) of $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ obtained from a DFT calculation (Castep). The inset shows the region around the Fermi-level. According to this calculation, $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ is a semi conductor with a band gap of 0.194 eV. A top view (b and c) and a side view (d and e) of the ELF (isovalue of the surface corresponds to 0.05).

As mentioned before there are two distinct regions in the structure, that of BaSn and that of BaSi. The $Sn^{4-}$ anions are isolated, whereas the silicon atoms form a triangular structure. These silicon triangles are surrounded by barium cations and are staggered with respect to each other. The Si-Si distances are 2.41, 2.43 and 2.47 Å, within the normal distance for a Si-Si bond, and as such form a distorted triangle. Each of these is surrounded by 13 barium cations. A barium cation coordinates over each face of the $Si_3$ triangle. The two short Si-Si bonds have two 'bridging' barium cations, the longer bond, only one. Each silicon has two 'terminal' barium cations. This leads to a total coordination number of the $Si_3$ unit of 13. The closest distance between two $Si_3$ units is 4.34 Å. Each barium coordinates to more than one silicon atom. Each tin is surrounded by eight barium cations in a distorted square antiprism arrangement. With this composition, the formula can be written as follows: $(Ba^{2+})_8((Si_3)^{6-})_2Sn^{4-}$. Although this makes sense, it is possible that the atom refined as Ba1 could be considered to be a tin atom since it has a large atomic displacement factor. It is not possible to distinguish definitively between the two cases owing to the small difference in electron count between the two. However, the structure has similarities to that of $Ba_4Si_3Br_2$ [7] and the coordination of Ba1 by 8 silicon centers only makes sense if the central atom is a large cation. The relatively long distances to the silicon neighbours can explain the large displacement of Ba1 in that its effective cage is somewhat larger than those of the other barium atoms. The $Si_3$ cluster with its 13 direct neighbours very much resembles tri-nuclear metal clusters such as $Os_3(CO)_{12}$ [41] and $[Re_3Cl_{12}]^{3-}$ [42]. The unit cell of $Ba_4Si_3Br_2$ is monoclinic, $P2_1/c$, and is approximately half the length in the c direction and containing only one 'layer' whereas that of $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ contains two. Comparing the structures directly, we see that the $Si_3$ units in both structures are practically in the same barium environments. Instead of every $Sn^{4-}$ anion four $Br^-$ anions have to be and surprisingly enough can be hosted.

## 5. Theoretical Investigations

The Density of States (DOS) of $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ obtained from DFT calculations (Castep [43], ultrasoft Pseudopotentials, GGA gradient corrected exchange correlation, PBE (Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof) functional [44]) is shown in Fig. 6a. According to the calculations, $Ba_8Si_6Sn$ is a semi conductor with a calculated band gap of 0.19 eV. This is well in line with a description of the compound according to the Zintl-Klemm concept as $(Ba^{2+})_8((Si_3)^{6-})_2Sn^{4-}$. The assignment of a formal charge of 4- to the isolated Sn atoms is backed both by the DFT calculations and also the structural similarities to $Ba_4Si_3Br_2$, where the $Br^-$ anions replace the Sn-centers. The results of a calculation of the Electron Localisation Function (ELF) [45,46] of the $Si_3$ unit are given in Fig. 6b-e. The results clearly show the areas of the free electron pairs at the Si atoms and the regions of the single bonds. The results are perfectly in line with the ELF distribution calculated for the $Si_3$ units in $Ba_4Si_3Br_2$ [7]. Top views (b and c) and side views (d and e) of the ELF (isovalue of the surface corresponds to 0.05) nicely relate to the conceptual frame of the Zintl-Klemm rules¹.
