# Synthesis and Characterization of a New Solid Solution,
$\mathbf{Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}}$

Xuean Chen¹ · Qian Chen¹ · Xinan Chang¹ · Weiqiang Xiao²

Received: 25 December 2018 / Accepted: 7 July 2020
© Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2020

## Abstract
A new solid solution, $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$, has been synthesized by a high-temperature solution reaction at 770 °C. The compound crystallizes in the space group $P1$ with $a$=6.86873 (13) Å, $b$=6.96992 (13) Å, $c$=14.5370 (3) Å, $\alpha$=88.8075 (15)°, $\beta$=86.5809(15)°, $\gamma$=83.2613 (14)°, $V$=689.84 (2) Å³, and $Z$=1. It features a three-dimensional (3D) $[Al_4(B_6O_{14})(B_6O_{13})(BO_3)_2]^{12n-}$ framework with intersecting channels occupied by $Ba^{2+}$ and $(Ba/Sr)^{2+}$ cations. The framework is constructed by 1D $[Al_2(B_6O_{14})(B_6O_{13})(BO_3)_2]^{18n-}$ chains and 1D $[Al_2O_7]^{8n-}$ chains interconnected via B–O–Al bridges. The compound melts incongruently at 844 °C. The existence of $BO_3$ and $BO_4$ groups in the structure was indicated by vibrational spectroscopy. A high degree of transparency in the visible wavelength range and a band gap of 3.37 eV were revealed by UV–VIS diffuse reflectance spectra. The band structure and bonding properties have been investigated and the insulating nature with a direct energy gap was confirmed.

## Introduction

Alkali metal aluminium-containing borates have attracted much research interest because optically perfect $K_2Al_2B_2O_7$ (KABO) crystals are used to develop high-power sources of coherent radiation of UV and vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) spectral ranges by generation of the fourth harmonic (266 nm) and sum frequencies (up to 193 nm) of the fundamental wavelength radiation of $Nd^{3+}$ ion lasers. These UV laser sources are widely used in medical, lithography, and optical storage applications [1–3]. When $K^+$ ions in KABO are replaced by other alkali metal cations, a series of compounds with the general composition $M_2Al_2B_2O_7$ (M=Na, Rb, and Cs) have been synthesized and structurally characterized [4–6].

It is natural to extend the research work to alkaline earth metal aluminium-containing borates. For example, the solid equilibrium relations in the $BaO-Al_2O_3-B_2O_3$ system have been studied by Hubner and others via solid state reaction methods [7–9]. At least eight ternary compounds have been proposed including “$BaAl_2B_2O_7$”, “$Ba_{10}Al_2B_2O_{16}$”, “$Ba_5Al_4B_{12}O_{29}$”, “$Ba_2Al_2B_8O_{17}$”, “$BaAlBO_4$”, “$Ba_3Al_4B_4O_{15}$”, “$BaAl_2B_4O_{10}$”, and “$BaAl_3BO_7$”. Among them, $BaAl_2B_2O_7$ is the most widely studied. It adopts an acentric structure with space group $R\overline{3}2$, displaying NLO properties, and the calculated SHG coefficients are about twice as large as those of KABO [8, 10]. Moreover, the photoluminescence properties of $Pb^{2+}$, $Ce^{3+}$, $Tb^{3+}$, and $Eu^{3+}/Eu^{2+}$ doped $BaAl_2B_2O_7$ have been reported in detail [11–13]. $BaAl_3BO_7$ crystallizes in a monoclinic system, with close structural similarity to the meta stable aluminate $SrAl_4O_7$ and it is a good host lattice for $Eu^{2+}$ luminescence [9]. “$Ba_3Al_4B_4O_{15}$” has been investigated by us previously and our single-crystal and powder X-ray structural analyses indicate that it has the right formula of $Ba_8Al_{10}B_{12}O_{41}$ [14]. Recently, our reinvestigation of the $BaO-Al_2O_3-B_2O_3$ system has led to the characterization of a new barium aluminoborate, $Ba_6Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$, which is compositionally very close to “$Ba_5Al_4B_{12}O_{29}$” [15].

This paper reports the results of our additional investigations of the $BaO-SrO-Al_2O_3-B_2O_3$ system in which one new solid solution $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$ has been discovered, which is compositionally related to $Ba_6Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$, but has a different unit cell volume and space-group symmetry. Herein, we report its synthesis, crystal structure, spectral properties, thermal behavior, and theoretical electronic structure. Insofar as we know, no experimental measurements and theoretical calculations mentioned above have been published in the literature.

## Experimental

### Sample Preparation

All of the chemicals were analytically pure from commercial sources and were used without further purification. For the preparation of $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$ single crystals, a powder mixture of $BaCO_3$, $SrCO_3$, $Al(OH)_3$, $H_3BO_3$, $Bi_2O_3$ and PbO at the molar ratio Ba:Sr:Al:B:Bi:Pb = 3:1:2:14:2:1 was first ground in an agate mortar and then transferred to a platinum crucible. The sample was gradually heated in air to 600 °C and kept at that temperature for one day to decompose $BaCO_3$ and $H_3BO_3$ and finally heated at 770 °C for four days. The intermediate product was slowly cooled to 500 °C at a rate of 0.5 °C/h, to 300 °C at 5.0 °C/h, followed by cooling to room temperature rapidly at 20 °C/h. Some transparent block crystals were selected carefully from the solidified flux for the single crystal structure analysis. Although Bi and Pb were not incorporated into the final structure, $Bi_2O_3$, PbO and excess $B_2O_3$ act as fluxes, which are favorable for the crystal growth.

The powder sample of $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$ was prepared from a mixture of $BaCO_3$, $SrCO_3$, $Al(OH)_3$, and $H_3BO_3$ in the molar ratio Ba:Sr:Al:B = 5.47:0.53:4:14.7. An excess of 5% boric acid is added to compensate for its volatilization at high temperature. The mixture was ground in an agate mortar, and then calcined at 700 °C in an open Pt crucible for 1 week with several intermediate grindings.

### Single Crystal and Powder X-ray Diffraction

For the single-crystal XRD measurements, a single crystal with dimensions $0.30×0.20×0.15$ $mm^3$ was carefully selected under an optical microscope and X-ray intensity data were collected at room temperature on an Agilent Super Nova diffractometer with a CCD detector equipped with a Mo source ($\lambda$ =0.71073 Å). The CrysAlisPro software [16] was used for data collection, reduction and absorption correction. The crystal structure was solved by direct methods in SHELXS-97 [17] and refined in SHELXL-97 [18] by full-matrix least-squares methods on $F_o^2$. All of atoms were refined anisotropically.

Refinements of atomic occupancy parameters indicated that Ba and Sr atoms were statistically distributed over three atomic sites with the compositions $Ba_{0.850(8)}Sr_{0.150(8)}$, $Ba_{0.765(10)}Sr_{0.235(10)}$, and $Ba_{0.851(10)}Sr_{0.149(10)}$, respectively. The $Ba^{2+}/Sr^{2+}$ disorder is not surprising because both Ba and Sr belong to the same group elements in the Periodic Table and have similar coordination geometries. A similar $Ba^{2+}/Sr^{2+}$ disorder has been previously observed, e.g., in $(Ba_{1.84}Sr_{1.16})(B_3O_6)_2$, $Sr_{0.735}Ba_{0.265}Cu_2(BO_3)_2$ and

$Ba_{0.87}Sr_{3.13}B_{14}O_{25}$ [19–21]. In addition, no indication was found in the X-ray diffraction data of a larger unit cell that would allow for $Ba^{2+}/Sr^{2+}$ ordering, even in a single crystal slowly cooled from 770 to 300 °C at 2 °C/h. Thus, the disorder model was assumed. The structural solutions have also been checked with the ADDSYM algorithm in the program PLATON [22] and no higher symmetry was found. Details of crystal parameters, data collection and structure refinements are summarized in Table 1, and selected bond lengths and angles are listed in Tables 2 and 3, respectively. The crystallographic data of the structure reported in this paper have been deposited in Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre: CCDC 1887143.

In order to further confirm the single-crystal structural model, powder XRD data of the polycrystalline samples were collected at room temperature in a continuous mode using the monochromatized $CuK\alpha$ radiation (40 kV, 40 mA) and a Bruker D8 ADVANCE (Bruker AXS) diffractometer. Data were collected in the $2\theta$ range of $10^\circ$–$80^\circ$ with a step size of $0.0197^\circ$ and counting time of 5 s per step. The crystal structure was refined from powder XRD data using Rietveld methods with the program TOPAS. Figure 1 shows the observed, calculated as well as difference XRD profiles, which shows a good agreement between the experimental and calculated powder XRD patterns.

## Characterization

Infrared spectra were recorded on a Bruker VER-TEX70 FT-IR spectrometer in the spectral range of 400–1600 cm⁻¹ from KBr pellets. Raman spectra were measured in the 400–1600 cm⁻¹ range using a Renishaw InVia Raman spectrometer equipped with confocal DM 2500 Leica optical microscope, a thermoelectrically cooled CCD as a detector, a laser operating at 532 nm and an edge filter. UV–VIS diffuse reflectance spectra were measured from 200 to 800 nm using a Hitachi UH4150 UV-Visible/NIR spectrophotometer equipped with an integrating sphere attachment, with $BaSO_4$ being used as the standard. The Kubelka–Munk function was used to convert diffuse reflectance data to absorbance spectra [24]. The thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) were investigated on NETZSCH STA 449F5. The samples of $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$ and $Al_2O_3$ were placed in platinum crucibles and heated at a rate of 10 °C min⁻¹ from 30 to 1000 °C, then cooled to room temperature under a flow of nitrogen gas.

## Electronic Structure Calculations

To calculate the electronic structure of $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$, a $2×1×1$ supercell was constructed, where only one Ba atom was replaced by a Sr atom, the corresponding Sr doping concentration being 8.3%, very close to that in $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$ (8.8%). The model was first optimized by minimization of the forces acting on the atoms, subsequently, the relaxed geometry was used for the calculations of electronic band structure, densities of states, and charge distribution.

The calculations were made using the first-principle plane-wave pseudopotential technique based on density functional theory (DFT) with CASTEP code distributed inside a computational commercial pack [25]. During the calculations, the exchange–correlation effects were treated using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the PBE functional [26]. The interactions between the ionic cores and the electrons were described by the Vanderbilt ultrasoft pseudopotential [27] with a cutoff energy of 300 eV, the k-points of $1×2×1$ were generated by using the Monkhorst–Pack scheme, and pseudoatomic calculations were made for Ba $5s^2 5p^6 6s^2$, Sr $4s^2 4p^6 5s^2$, Al $3s^2 3p^1$, B $2s^2 2p^1$, and O $2s^2 2p^4$. For the geometry optimization, both lattice parameters and atomic positions were optimized simultaneously, and the convergence tolerance was selected with differences in total energy, the maximal ionic Hellmann–Feynman force, the maximal stress, and the maximal displacement being within $2.0×10^{-5}$ eV per atom, $5.0×10^{-2}$ eV Å⁻¹, $1.0×10^{-1}$ GPa, and $2.0×10^{-3}$ Å, respectively.

## Band Structures and Densities of States

The calculated band structure of $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$ along high-symmetry points of the first Brillouin zone (BZ) has been investigated. It is found that the valence bands (VBs) are nearly flat, while the bottom of conduction bands (CBs) has obvious dispersion. The valence band maximum (VBM) and conduction band minimum (CBM) are located at the center of the BZ [the $\Gamma$ (0, 0, 0) point] resulting in a direct energy gap. The calculated energy gap of the crystal is about 4.18 eV, larger than the value of the optical absorption edge

3.37 eV estimated from the diffuse reflectance spectrum of the powder sample (Fig. 6). This is due to the general fact that the ultraviolet absorption edge measured by powder sample has a red shift compared with the absorption edge obtained from the bulk crystal.

The energy bands can be assigned according to the total and partial densities of states (DOS) as plotted in Fig. 9. The VBs near $-33.6$ and $-25.6$ eV are resulting from the innermost localized Sr 4 s and Ba 5 s orbitals, respectively, and not shown in Fig. 9 for the sake of clarity. The VBs lying approximately between $-21.4$ and $-16.3$ eV are mainly composed of O 2s orbitals mixed with some B 2s/2p components as well as a few Al 3s/3p states. The VBs localized within a very narrow energy range at around $-15.5$ eV and $-10.7$ eV are basically Sr 4p and Ba 5p character, respectively. The VBs extending in a wide range from $-9.5$ eV to VBM (i.e., the Fermi level), mostly arise from O 2p states with some mixings of B 2s/2p states as well as a small amount of Al 3s/3p states and the VBM is controlled by O 2p states. The CBs ranging from 4.2 to 8.7 eV originate predominately from the unoccupied Ba 5d, Sr 4d, B 2p, and Al 3s/3p states and a less unoccupied mixture of Ba 6s, Sr 5s, and O 2p states. While near the CBM the Ba 6s and Sr 5s states make a main contribution. The absorption edge in UV–VIS absorption spectrum gives the threshold for the direct optical transitions between the VBM and CBM, therefore, can be attributed to the charge transfers from O 2p states to Ba 6s and Sr 5s states. A strong absorption peak at 281.7 nm (4.40 eV) in Fig. 6 may be assigned as charge transfers from O 2p to Ba 5d, Sr 4d, B 2p, and Al 3s/3p states according to the above DOS analyses.

## Conclusions

We have synthesized and characterized a new solid solution, $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$. In its crystal structure, $[B_6O_{14}]^{10-}$, $[B_6O_{13}]^{8-}$, and $[BO_3]^{3-}$ groups are bridged by tetrahedrally coordinated $Al^{3+}$ centers to generate 1D ladder-like $[Al_2(B_6O_{14})(B_6O_{13})(BO_3)_2]_{n}^{18n-}$ chains, which are further connected with 1D zigzag $[Al_2O_7]_{n}^{8n-}$ chains via common oxygen atoms to create a 3D $[Al_4(B_6O_{14})(B_6O_{13})(BO_3)_2]_{n}^{12n-}$ framework. This 3D network is different from that observed in $Ba_6Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$, where $[Al_2(B_6O_{14})(B_6O_{13})(BO_3)_2]_{n}^{18n-}$ chains are 'stitched together' laterally by $[Al_4O_{14}]^{16-}$ groups through sharing O atoms, no $[Al_2O_7]_{n}^{8n-}$ chains being found. Thermal analysis and melt experiments indicate that this compound melts incongruently at 844 °C, and the decomposition products contain $BaB_2O_4$. The characteristic absorption bands of both $BO_3$ and $BO_4$ groups are observed in the IR and Raman spectra, consistent with the results obtained from the crystallographic study. The UV–VIS optical absorption spectrum converted from the Kubelka–Munk equation gives an optical band gap of 3.37 eV for $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$, which is assigned to charge transfer from the occupied O 2p states to the unoccupied Ba 6s and Sr 5s states in view of the first-principles DFT computations of band structures and DOSs.

As mentioned in the introduction, $BaAl_2B_2O_7$ shows interesting luminescent properties when doped with $Pb^{2+}$, $Eu^{2+}$, $Ce^{3+}$, $Tb^{3+}$, and $Eu^{3+}$ ions. $BaAl_3BO_7$ is a suitable host for $Eu^{2+}$ cations. The activator ions in these phosphors are expected to mainly replace $Ba^{2+}$ ions in the crystal lattice because of the smaller differences between their ionic radii compared with $Al^{3+}$ ions. $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$, $BaAl_2B_2O_7$, and $BaAl_3BO_7$ are all alkali-earth aluminum-containing borates. It can be reasonably predicted that $Ba_{5.47}Sr_{0.53}Al_4B_{14}O_{33}$ may also be a good host lattice for $Pb^{2+}$, $Eu^{2+}$, $Eu^{3+}$, $Tb^{3+}$, and $Ce^{3+}$ luminescence. This remains to be confirmed by future work.
