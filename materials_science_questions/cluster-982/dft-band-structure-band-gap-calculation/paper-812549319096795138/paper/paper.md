# Above room-temperature dielectric switching and semiconducting properties of a layered organic-inorganic hybrid compound: $(C_{6}H_{12}N)_{2}Pb(NO_{3})_{4}\dagger$

Jia-Ying Jiang, Qi Xu, Jia-Jun Ma, Zhi-Xin Gong, Chao Shi* and Yi Zhang*

The well-studied star compound, $CH_3NH_3PbI_3$, has attracted plenty of attention because of its remarkable optical and electrical properties. Consequently, new switching multifunctional hybrid compounds can be widely used in many fields such as solar cells, light-emitting diodes, optical data storage and so on. Therefore, switching multifunctional hybrid compounds with dielectric and semiconducting properties simultaneously will also find roles in the next generation of optoelectronic coupling materials. In fact, discovering an effective method to synthesize (multi)functional hybrid materials remains a pressing challenge. Thanks to the "quasi-spherical theory" proposed by Xiong *et al.*, we used 7-azabicyclo[2.2.1] heptane as the quasi-spherical cation to construct molecule-based crystalline materials that exhibit responsive properties. Then, we tried to exploit the knowledge of crystal engineering and coordination chemistry to explain (multi)functional molecular materials. A layered organic-inorganic hybrid compound, $(C_6H_{12}N)_2Pb(NO_3)_4$ (1), was grown and its dielectric switching property and semiconducting behaviour were investigated. Insights from differential scanning calorimetry measurements, variable-temperature X-ray structural studies, and dielectric spectroscopy revealed the origin of the phase transition, which is related to the motion of the organic ammonium and inorganic framework in solid-state crystals. Furthermore, **1** is also a wide bandgap semiconductor with an optical bandgap of 3.53 eV. The realization of switching and semiconducting properties simultaneously in layered Pb-based perovskites has a great significance toward research into hybrid compounds and the development of dielectric-optoelectronic integrated materials.

## Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskites (HOIPs) are a significant number of dynamic molecular materials with (multi)functional properties of interest for immense potential applications. $^{1-4}$ Intriguing physical properties (*i.e.* ferroelectrics, switchable dielectrics and so on) have been found in HOIPs, which were traditionally discovered in pure inorganic perovskites, such as $BaTiO_3$, $Pb(Zr_{1-x}Ti_x)O_3$, and so on. $^{5}$ However, the area of market is still being mainly investigated by inorganic materials, although they have inherent shortcomings of being heavy weight, high cost, high brittleness, environmentally unfriendly and hard to process. $^{6,7}$ With the enhancement of global awareness of environmental protection and energy conservation, these disadvantages of inorganic materials become more and more prominent. This inspires researchers, to some extent, that these problems can be effectively solved by HOIPs, which have attracted considerable attention. Particularly, the discoveries of several extraordinary hybrid organic-inorganic materials, whose performance parameters are comparable to those of pure inorganic materials could be potentially important supplements to pure inorganic perovskites. $^{8}$ For example, $(TMFM)_{0.26}(TMCM)_{0.74}CdCl_3$ solid solution (TMFM = trimethylfluoromethyl ammonium and TMCM = trimethylchloromethyl ammonium) has a large $d_{33}$ of 1540 pC N⁻¹, which is stronger than high-performance lead zirconate titanate. $^{8c}$

Generally speaking, HOIPs are derived from pure inorganic perovskite $ABO_3$ by replacing the A-site metal ion with $NH_4^+$ or organic cations and the $O^{2-}$ ion with an organic bridging ligand. The diversity of the components and flexibility of the structures endow quite a number of the HOIPs with an important feature, *i.e.*, nonreversible and/or reversible structural phase transitions. $^{9-15}$ More importantly, HOIPs have become

excellent candidates for (multi)functional hybrid materials by combining metal ion coupling with quasi-spherical organic cations displaying coexistent electrical, optical, and magnetic orderings. As a representative, the perovskite-type HOIPs have been discovered as stimuli-responsive multifunctional materials. $[NH_{4}][M(HCOO)_{3}]$ (M = Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Zn) as a new family of HOIPs were discovered by Gao and co-workers. $^{11a,b}$ They display ferroelectric ordering and magnetic ordering, which are mainly caused by the static-to-dynamic transition of $NH_{4}^{+}$ cations. (Pyrrolidinium) $_{3}[Sb_{2}Cl_{9}]$ reported by Wojciechowska et al. shows multiphase ferroelectric and ferroelastic phenomena, which are mainly attributed to the progressive ordering of non-equivalent cations. $^{16}$ Other representative studies including $[(CH_{3})(CH_{3}CH_{2})_{3}N][FeCl_{4}]$ and $[(CH_{3})_{4}P][FeX_{4}]$ (X = Cl or Br) were documented by Xiong et al. $^{17}$ They exhibit great electromagnetooptic coupling above room temperature and consequently feature novel (multi)functional properties. Interestingly, the emergence of (multi)functional properties simultaneously in HOIPs contributes to the potential applications in smart devices such as switches, sensors, actuators and so on. $^{6-17}$ However, one of the vital critical obstacles still confusing researchers is how to single out (multi)functional properties from the HOIPs.

The manipulations of the dynamics of the quasi-spherical organic cations in HOIPs would be a means to modify and improve these desirable electrical properties. Meanwhile, the introduction of some typical transition metal ions can achieve inspiring optical properties. Bearing these two factors in mind, we report a novel Pb(II)-based molecular-based semiconductor, $(C_{6}H_{12}N)_{2}Pb(NO_{3})_{4}$ (1), which captures the unique physical functionalities through electrical and optical measurements. Compound 1 has the $A_{2}BX_{4}$-type structure with the tetragonal space group $I4/mmm$ in the high-temperature phase (HTP) and undergoes a phase transition around $T_{c} = 352$ K to the monoclinic system (low-temperature phase, LTP) with the space group $P2_{1}/c$. Besides, 1 possesses a semiconductor feature with a bandgap $(E_{g})$ of $\sim$3.53 eV. Such a finding provides a promising platform for the design and build of (multi)functional perovskite-like switchable dielectric materials.

## Experimental
### Synthesis
Chemicals and solvents were commercially purchased and used without any further purification. Compound 1 was obtained by employing the solvent evaporation method. Pb $(NO_{3})_{2}$ (1 mmol, 0.331 g) was dissolved in an aqueous solution (10 mL) under constant stirring. Then, 0.273 g (3 mmol) of $HNO_{3}$ (69.2%) was slowly added. Finally, 0.194 g (2 mmol) of 7-azabicyclo[2.2.1]heptane was added to the mixture, and the mixture was stirred for 10 min to obtain a clear solution. Colourless block-like single crystals were easily obtained at a dry and well-ventilated place for a few days. The purity of the target compound was certified by powder X-ray diffraction recorded with a Rigaku D/MAX 2000 PC X-ray diffractometer (Fig. S1†). Variable-temperature single-crystal diffraction data were recorded on a Rigaku synergy diffractometer using Mo-K$\alpha$ ($\lambda = 0.71073$ Å) radiation from a graphite monochromator equipped with a gas spray cooler device. The structure at HTP contains totally disordered organic cation and anion framework. It was not modelled as the chemical senses. And the relatively high $R_{1}$ value is caused due to this reason. ### Electronic structure calculations
Using the single-crystal structural data of **1** at 293 K, we calculated its energy band structure by the total-energy coder CASTEP package. The exchange-correlation functional was employed with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation functional.¹⁹ The energy cutoff was set to 500 eV, and a 4 × 1 × 2 Monkhorst-Pack grid of k-points was used. The following orbital electrons were considered as valence electrons: Pb (5s²5p⁶5d¹⁰6s²6p²), O (2s²2p⁴), N (2s²2p³), C (2s²2p²), H (1s¹).

## Results and discussion
### Thermal analysis of **1**
Since the reversible solid-to-solid phase transition behaviour is generally thermally induced, we investigated the process by DSC measurement in order to determine the temperature range of the transformation. The polycrystalline sample of **1** was measured in a cooling-heating cycle ranging from 305 K to 415 K (Fig. 1), which showed a pair of reversible peaks centred around $T_c$. The phase below $T_c$ is labelled as the low-temperature phase (LTP) and the phase above $T_c$ as the high-temperature phase (HTP), respectively. Large thermal hysteresis ($\Delta T = 12$ K) together with the $\lambda$-type peaks reveals a first-order phase transition behaviour involved in **1**. Meanwhile, the entropy change ($\Delta S$) for the phase transition is approximately equal to 23.21 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. According to the Boltzmann equation, $\Delta S = R{\cdot}\ln N$, $N$ is approximately equal to 16.28. The large value of $N$ indicates a typical order-disorder phase transformation and extremely severe structural transformation. The result is quite consistent with the single-crystal structural analyses that is discussed later. Furthermore, **1** also shows relatively high thermal stability (Fig. S3†). The $T_c$ value is different from the decomposition temperature. So, it can be confirmed that the above room-temperature reversible phase transition behaviour is true.

### Crystal structures
The single-crystal structures of **1** at 293 K and 373 K were determined by variable-temperature X-ray structure determinations to disclose the mechanism of origin of phase transition. At LTP, it crystallizes in the centrosymmetric space group $P2_1/c$ with $a = 6.9554(2)$ Å, $b = 22.6284(6)$ Å, $c = 13.8537(3)$ Å, $\beta = 91.584(2)^\circ$ and $Z = 4$, while at HTP the crystal structure transforms to the centrosymmetric space group $I4/mmm$, with a cell length of $a = 6.8961(9)$ Å, $b = 6.8961(9)$ Å, $c = 23.195(10)$ Å and $Z = 2$. The symmetry elements increase from four ($E$, $C_2$, $i$, and $\sigma_h$) at LTP with the point group $C_{2h}$ to sixteen ($E$, $2C_4$, $C_2$, $2C'_2$, $2C''_2$, $i$, $2S_4$, $\sigma_h$, $2\sigma_v$, and $2\sigma_d$) at HTP with the point group $D_{4h}$ during the phase transition (Fig. 2). Consequently, the symmetry changes of **1** are triggered by the order-disorder transition of the cations and anions, which will be analyzed in great detail (see later).

At RTP, the crystal structure of **1** adopts the two-dimensional (2D) perovskite structure, consisting of the 2D layers of corner-sharing Pb(NO₃)₆ octahedra and the protonated organic cations are confined in the interlayer spaces between the inorganic sheets (Fig. 3a). In the inorganic packing structure, the Pb²⁺ ion is located on the mirror and coordinated by six NO₃⁻ ligands with Pb-O distances between 2.545(3) Å and 2.929(4) Å (Table S1†), among which there are two NO₃⁻ groups acting as monodentate ligands; meanwhile, the other four act as bidentate ligands. Beyond that, the shortest distance and angle between adjacent Pb²⁺ in the framework are 6.9428(3) Å and 158.078(2)°, respectively. The organic cations, staying in a completely ordered state, are interconnected in the interlayer spaces by weak hydrogen bonds between the -NH₂ and -NO₃ groups with N-H$\cdots$O distances of 2.806(5)-3.364(6) Å (Table S2†).

### Dielectric responses in 1
Various physical properties, especially dielectric constant, usually undergo abrupt changes around $T_c$. Thus, the switchable dielectric constant, that is, a dielectric transition between low- and high-dielectric states, has been regarded as the most useful and convenient method for verifying phase transitions triggered by temperature. The temperature-dependent dielectric constant of the compacted powder pellets of 1 was validated in the temperature and frequency ranges of 320-370 K and 5-1000 kHz (Fig. 4).

It shows a striking dielectric transition between the high- and low-states around $T_c$, which is well consistent with the DSC results, corresponding to the order-disorder structural phase transition of the crystal. Specifically, below $T_c$, the dielectric permittivity remained stable with the value of about 5.0 in the low dielectric state, indicating no abnormality in dielectric permittivity. Subsequently, around $T_c$, the dielectric permittivity displays a steep increasing slope. Finally, above $T_c$, 1 shows a high value of the dielectric permittivity (~7.5), whereas the dielectric permittivity stables progressively, for about 190% (at 1000 kHz). The feature of the dielectric transition is the rapid change in dielectric permittivity around $T_c$, indicating a sharp switching property. Based on these facts, the dielectric transition of 1 can be easily understood. With the increase in temperature, the $NO_3^-$ ions and organic cations successively undergo disorder around $T_c$. The disordered anion and cation are dielectrically active regarding dipolar reorientation. In brief, the switchable dielectric permittivity is mainly induced by the distorting change of the organic cations and the reorientation of the $NO_3^-$ ions during the phase transition of 1.

### Optical properties of 1
Finally, we would like to discuss the semiconducting properties of 1. As displayed in Fig. 5, in the visible spectral region, compound 1 indicates an abrupt absorption band edge at about 320 nm, indicating an indirect bandgap. This is based on the variant of the Tauc equation, $^{21}$ $[h\nu F(R_\infty)]^{1/n}=A(h\nu-E_g)$, where $h$ refers to the Planck's constant, $\nu$ represents the frequency of vibration, $A$ is related to the proportional constant, $F(R_\infty)$ stands for the Kubelka-Munk function. The band gap $(E_g)$ of 1 is estimated to be about 3.53 eV by calculating from the Tauc plot (Fig. 5b, inset). The bandgap characteristic was further confirmed by theoretical calculation. As shown in Fig. 5c, around the A point, the highest point of the valence band (VB) is observed, whereas the lowest point of the conduction band (CB) is found at the G point. This result shows the

## Conclusions
In summary, a novel Pb(II)-based hybrid compound $(C_{6}H_{12}N)_{2}Pb(NO_{3})_{4}$ with 2D perovskite-like structure has been synthesized and characterized, which exhibits reversible above-room-temperature phase transition and semiconducting properties. Thermal analysis and dielectric measurements were performed to verify the invertible phase transition around 352 K. Variable-temperature single-crystal structural analyses further reveal that the order-disorder transformations of organic cations and the reorientation of the $NO_{3}^{-}$ ions account for its phase transition. It also displays a semiconducting behaviour with an optical bandgap $E_{g}=3.53$ eV. This finding reveals that the material is of interest for electronic and optical measurements and the corresponding applications.
