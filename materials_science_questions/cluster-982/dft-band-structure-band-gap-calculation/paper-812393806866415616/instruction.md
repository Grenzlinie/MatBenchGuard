# TDLDA optical gaps of wurtzite CdSe quantum dots

## Problem background
Semiconductor quantum dots (QDs) exhibit size‑tunable optical gaps across the visible spectrum, making them attractive for light‑emitting and photovoltaic applications. However, accurate ab‑initio prediction of absorption spectra and optical gaps remains challenging because excited‑state methods are computationally demanding and because realistic models must handle surface dangling bonds without ad‑hoc passivation potentials. Time‑dependent density functional theory within the adiabatic local density approximation (TDLDA) offers a computationally tractable route to excited‑state properties, but it is not a‑priori obvious whether TDLDA can reproduce the experimentally observed size dependence of the optical gap for bare, ligand‑free quantum dots. This task investigates that question for three stoichiometric, wurtzite‑derived CdSe quantum dots: Cd₁₇Se₁₇, Cd₂₆Se₂₆, and Cd₃₈Se₃₈.

## Approach
The overall workflow is a self‑contained computational pipeline. (1) **Atomistic models** of the quantum dots are constructed as spherical fragments of the wurtzite CdSe crystal, selecting stoichiometric clusters in which no surface atom has three dangling bonds; all atoms are held fixed at the bulk‑like positions. (2) **Ground‑state Kohn–Sham DFT** calculations are performed for each dot using the local density approximation (LDA) with norm‑conserving Troullier–Martins pseudopotentials for Cd and Se, solved on a spherical real‑space grid by a higher‑order finite‑difference method. The ground‑state results provide Kohn–Sham eigenvalues, wavefunctions, and occupation numbers. (3) **TDLDA linear‑response equations** are constructed from the ground‑state wavefunctions: the coupling matrix K (Eq. 2) and the Q matrix are built, and the eigenvalue problem Q Fₙ = Ωₙ² Fₙ is solved to obtain excitation energies Ωₙ and oscillator strengths. (4) An **absorption spectrum** is generated by broadening each transition with a Gaussian of 0.1 eV full‑width at half‑maximum. (5) An **optical gap** is defined empirically for each dot as the energy below which the cumulative oscillator strength reaches 2 % of the total integrated oscillator strength (the “2 % rule”). This definition provides a robust extraction of the absorption threshold even when the spectrum contains many weak low‑energy transitions. The three gap values — one per dot size — are the final quantity to report.
