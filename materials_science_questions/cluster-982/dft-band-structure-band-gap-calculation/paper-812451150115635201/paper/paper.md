# First-Principles Study of Titanium Dioxide: Rutile and Anatase
Masayoshi MIKAMI, Shinichiro NAKAMURA, Osamu KITAO $^{1,2}$ , Hironori ARAKAWA $^{1}$ and Xavier GONZE $^{3}$
Yokohama Research Center, Mitsubishi Chemical Corporation, Aoba-ku, Yokohama 227-8502, Japan
$^{1}$ National Institute of Materials and Chemical Research, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-8565, Japan
$^{2}$ Department of Chemical System Engineering, Graduate School of Engineering, The University of Tokyo, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo 113-8656, Japan
$^{3}$ Unité de Physico-Chemie et de Physique des Matériaux, Université Catholique de Louvain,1, place Croix du Sud, B-1348 Louvain-la-Neuve, Belgium

(Received April 4, 2000; accepted for publication July 5, 2000)

The atomic and electronic structures of two phases of titanium dioxide, anatase and rutile, have been investigated by a first-principles pseudopotential method based on local density approximation in density functional theory. The calculated band structure, equilibrium lattice constants, and bulk modulus of rutile are consistent with experimental data and with other calculations. The calculated structure of anatase is also close to experimental data. The calculated bulk modulus of anatase is found to be smaller than that of rutile, presumably due to the sparsity of anatase. The band structure of anatase is given in comparison with that found in previous works. The energetics between the two phases is also discussed.

KEYWORDS: titanium dioxide, rutile, anatase, first-principles calculation, pseudopotential, structure, bulk modulus, band structure

Titanium dioxide $(TiO_{2})$ has been receiving increasing attention not only in the scientific field but also in the industrial field for many years. Accurate experimental data and comprehensive theoretical studies are still desirable, because the potential applications of the system are in a considerably wide range, and include a conventional catalyst support, $^{1)}$ a photocatalytic substrate, and photoelectrochemical materials. In particular, the photoelectrolysis of water to produce $H_{2}^{2)}$ and a dye-sensitized photoelectrochemical solar cell system $^{3)}$ are the subjects of present intensive research.

Titanium dioxide has many polymorphs. Among them, rutile $(P4_{2}/mnm$ space group, $D_{4h}^{14})$, anatase $(I4_{1}/amd$ space group, $D_{4h}^{19})$, and brookite (Pbca space group, $D_{2h}^{15}$) are well known from the viewpoint of atomic structure. $^{4-6)}$ Although both rutile and anatase were intensively studied for photocatalysis and photoelectrochemical applications, experimental investigations have mostly focused on the anatase phase. For example, it is known that anatase plays a key role in the injection process of photochemical solar cells with high conversion efficiency. $^{3)}$ Furthermore, it has been reported that anatase thin film has different properties from rutile thin film; the main difference is that anatase thin film has a wider optical-absorption gap and a smaller electron effective mass that presumably leads to a higher mobility for the charge carriers. $^{7)}$ From the standpoint of theoretical study, a great deal of effort has been made primarily on the electronic structure of rutile phase. $^{8-12)}$ What is lacking, however, is the theoretical study of the electronic structure of anatase phase.

To our best knowledge, pseudopotential Hartree-Fock (CRYSTAL code), $^{13)}$ extended Hückel tight-binding, $^{14)}$ first-principles linear-combinations-of-atomic-orbitals (LCAO), $^{15)}$ and full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FLAPW) $^{16)}$ calculations have been carried out for anatase phase as bulk. Still, there is little agreement as to the electronic structure of anatase phase. This paper is thus intended as a first-principles investigation of anatase phase as well as rutile phase with an ab initio pseudopotential method.

The present results have been obtained thanks to the use of the ABINIT code $^{17)}$ that is based on ab initio pseudopotentials and a plane-wave basis set. It relies on the adaptation to a fixed potential of the band-by-band conjugate gradient method $^{18)}$ and on a potential-based conjugate-gradient algorithm for the determination of the self-consistent potential. $^{19)}$ The local density approximation (LDA) $^{20)}$ was adopted for exchange-correlation (XC) energy in this work. Two ab initio pseudopotentials were prepared for Ti and O atoms in this study; one was the Troullier-Martins (TM)-type norm-conserving pseudopotential $^{21)}$ prepared with the FHI98PP package, $^{22)}$ and the other was the Teter-type extended norm-conserving pseudopotential $^{23)}$ prepared by one of the present authors (X.G.). For the titanium pseudopotential, $3s$, $3p$, $4s$, and $3d$ electrons were considered as valence states in order to take the large difference between the $3d$ and $4s$ functions into account. Thus highly transferable pseudopotentials were constructed. $^{8,11)}$ For the oxygen pseudopotential, $2s$ and $2p$ electrons were considered as the valence state. In the Teter-type potentials, two projectors were prepared for each $s$ and $p$ angular momentum channel to achieve higher transferability. The plane-wave basis set with the energy cutoff of 60 Ry (100 Ry) was satisfactory for the TM (Teter)-type potential to reproduce the structural properties of the rutile and anatase phases, as given below. Brillouin-zone integration was computed with $k$ points in a Monkhorst-Pack $^{24)}$ $(4,4,6)$ grid for rutile and in a $(4,4,2)$ grid for anatase, respectively; the convergence with regard to the $k$ points was confirmed with finer meshes, up to a $(10,10,15)$ grid for rutile and a $(10,10,5)$ grid for anatase. In the discussion of the total energy difference between the phases, errors were less than 0.1 kcal/mol per unit formula $(TiO_{2})$. A more detailed account can be found elsewhere. $^{25)}$

First, we calculated the equilibrium structures of rutile and anatase. The calculation of equilibrium crystal structures of the two phases remained sufficiently difficult that we imposed the space group symmetry of the unit cell and performed geometrical optimization of the remaining degrees of freedom by calculating the forces on the atoms and stresses on the unit cells. $^{26)}$ In the optimized geometries, the remaining forces acting on the atoms were typically less than $0.03 eV/\mathring{A}$. As evident in Tables I and II, there was good agreement between the predicted lattice constants and internal atomic coordinates and those measured experimentally. $^{4,5)}$ The discrepancies between experimental and predicted values were generally less

than 2.0%, which is typical of LDA results for structures. Our results were also very close to those reported in other theoreti- cal works. $^{8-11)}$ Our calculated structural parameters were used to obtain the band structures.

We also calculated the total energies of the rutile and anatase phases as a function of volume. In this calculation we minimized the total energy with respect to the internal atomic coordinates and lattice constants, keeping the unit-cell volume constant, at each volume. Then we obtained the en- ergy curves by fitting the computed values to a Murnaghan's equation of state. $^{27)}$ In this way, we found a bulk modulus of anatase 10-14% smaller than that of rutile in our results; for example, 187 GPa for anatase phase and 253 GPa for rutile phase in the Teter-type potential case (Tables I and II). To the best of our knowledge, the bulk modulus of anatase has not been experimentally found. On the other hand, the ex- perimental bulk modulus of rutile (216 GPa) was close to our theoretical value, within typical errors in LDA. The LCAO calculation in ref. 15 had led to a bulk modulus of anatase(272 GPa) 30% larger than that of rutile (209 GPa). Consid- ering that the density of anatase phase is smaller than that of rutile phase, we believe that our result for the bulk moduli is more plausible. Since the sparsity of anatase phase may lead to a greater flexibility of anatase structure under its vol- ume change, we also evaluated the lattice constant ratio $c / a$ and the positions of oxygen (parameters $u$ in Tables I and II)at each point of the volume range from $0.86 V_{0}$ to $1.06 V_{0}$ (V: the experimental unit-cell volume). We observed that the deviations of the parameters from the optimized values at the equilibrium volume (in the Teter-type potential case) were larger in anatase phase than in rutile phase; the lattice con- stant ratio $c / a$ ranged from 2.29 to 2.61 in the anatase case, and from 0.656 to 0.628 in the rutile case. With respect to the oxygen position, the internal parameter $u$ in the anatase case ranged from 0.2025 to 0.2241, while the internal parameter $u$ in the rutile case ranged from 0.3014 to $0.3077.^{28)}$

With the optimized structures, we investigated the band structures for the rutile (Fig. 1(a)) and anatase phases(Fig. 1(b)). We confirmed that TM-type and Teter-type pseu- dopotentials led to band structures which are topologically the same for each phase. $^{25)}$ The Ti ions are in a distorted oc tahedral environment and formally have a $Ti^{+4}(3 d^{0})$ elec tronic configuration. Since each band structure was examined within the first Brillouin zone (BZ) of its primitive cell that contains two formula units, $^{29)}$ both band structures have, as expected, a total of 10 bands (conduction bands) mainly from Ti $3 d$ orbitals and 12 bands (valence bands) mainly from the O $2 p$ orbitals hybridized with Ti $3 d$ states; the conduction bands are split among themselves into $t_{2 g}$ -like (the lower six bands) and $e_{g}$ -like subbands (the upper four bands), as de scribed in ref. 12 (rutile) and ref. 16 (anatase). The topologies of both band structures were flat, as expected for mostly ionic compounds. From the observation of a wider valence band width of rutile phase than of anatase, the covalency in the bonds between Ti and O atoms is presumably higher in the rutile phase than in the anatase phase.

Regarding the accuracy of our band structures, we com- pared our results with prior works. We confirmed that the band structure of rutile phase appears very close to those of previous works (pseudopotential $^{10)}$ and LCAO calcula tion $^{15)}$ ). We also confirmed that the band structure of anatase phase within the first BZ of its conventional cell containing four formula units $^{29)}$ appears very similar to the $LCAO^{15)}$ and the FLAPW $^{16)}$ result, not to the CRYSTAL $^{13)}$ result. $^{25)}$

On the other hand, the calculated minimal band gap of 2.05 eV for anatase was indirect; the bottom of the conduction bands is at $\Gamma$ and the top of the valence bands is near $X$ (approximately (0.0, 0.0, 0.46) in the reciprocal lattice vector unit for the primitive unit cell$^{29)}$); the highest energy of the valence band at $\Gamma$ is 0.56 eV lower than the top of the valence bands. It is known that the experimental gap of anatase $(3.2\,\text{eV})^{31)}$ is 0.2 eV larger than that of rutile. This is consistent with our calculation, which showed that the gap of anatase was 0.17 eV larger than that of rutile.

Lastly, we compared the total energy difference per unit formula between the rutile and anatase phases. Experimentally, the rutile structure was reported to be slightly more stable than the anatase structure by 1.2 to 2.8 kcal/mol per TiO$_2$. $^{32,33)}$ In this work with TM-type potential, the total energy per TiO$_2$ of anatase phase at the optimized structure, $-90.2838$ a.u., was slightly lower than that of rutile phase, $-90.2816$ a.u., by 1.4 kcal/mol per TiO$_2$ (1 a.u. $= 27.21$ eV, $1\,\text{eV} = 23.06\,\text{kcal/mol}$).$^{34)}$ When the Teter-type potentials were adopted, the total energy per TiO$_2$ of rutile phase, $-90.1725$ a.u., was slightly lower than that of anatase phase, $-90.1707$ a.u., by 1.1 kcal/mol per TiO$_2$. We confirmed that these energy differences remained within 0.1 kcal/mol error by varying the energy cutoff up to 150 Ry or increasing the number of $k$ points or both. Thus, we may say that the slight stability of rutile phase with respect to anatase phase can be qualitatively reproduced with the Teter-type pseudopotential within the LDA level. In order to extract a final conclusion about this slight stability of rutile phase, we await further works, for example, calculations with other exchange-correlation functionals as the generalized gradient approximation$^{35)}$ or an analysis of free energy at finite temperature by taking the phonon contribution within harmonic oscillation approximation$^{36)}$ into account.
