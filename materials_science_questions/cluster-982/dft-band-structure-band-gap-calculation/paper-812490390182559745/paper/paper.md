# AgBi(SO₄)(IO₃)₂: aliovalent substitution induces structure dimensional upgrade and second harmonic generation enhancement†

Hongming Liu,ⁿᵇᶜ Qingchen Wu, ᵈ Lili Liu,ᵇᵉ Zheshuai Lin, *ˣᵈ
P. Shiv Halasyamani, *ᵇ Xingguo Chen *ⁿ and Jingui Qin ⁿ

A new 3D noncentrosymmetric mixed-metal sulfate iodate, $AgBi(SO_4)(IO_3)_2$, has been designed based on a 2D iodate of $AgBi(IO_3)_4$ via the aliovalent substitution of $[IO_3]^-$ groups by $[SO_4]^{2-}$ groups. Enhancement of the second harmonic generation response (3.0 × KDP to 3.9 × KDP) has been achieved while retaining a favorable NLO framework. This is a novel strategy to upgrade structural dimensions.

As a nonlinear optical (NLO) property, the second harmonic generation (SHG) effect is widely applied in frequency conversion in solid-state lasers.¹ While a variety of NLO materials, such as $\beta$-$BaB_2O_4$ (BBO),² $LiB_3O_5$ (LBO),³ $KH_2PO_4$ (KDP),⁴ $KTiOPO_4$ (KTP),⁵ and $AgGaS_2$,⁶ have been employed in a host of fields, the discovery of good NLO materials still remains a big challenge. Many attempts to design new noncentrosymmetric (NCS) structures – a fundamental requirement for SHG – have been reported.⁷ Generally, NCS structures with three-dimensional (3D) topologies are more favorable than structures with two-dimensional (2D) topologies, since the latter may cause difficulties in crystal growth. The layered NLO material $KBe_2BO_3F_2$ (KBBF) is a typical example, and great efforts are still being made to grow large-size crystals of this layered material.⁸ Transforming a structure from 2D to 3D is an effective structural design strategy to reduce crystal growth issues and has been widely adopted in borates.⁹

For an NLO crystal, an important factor that affects the SHG intensity is the arrangement of the NLO-active groups in the crystal structure. The anti-parallel or quasi anti-parallel arrangement of these groups always reduces the observed SHG intensity. Recently, the strategy of aliovalent substitution has been verified to be successful in designing new NLO materials.¹⁰ For example, $BiF(SeO_3)^{10e}$ was designed from $BiO(IO_3)^{11}$ by using $[SeO_3]/F$ to replace $[IO_3]/O$, whereas $Pb_2GaF_2(SeO_3)_2Cl^{10d}$ was discovered using $Pb_2TiOF(SeO_3)_2Cl^{12}$ as the template through aliovalent substitution. Frequently, the structure would experience a change in the framework when this substitution occurs. An enhancement of the NLO properties may be observed as in the case with conversion from $BiO(IO_3)^{11}$ to $BiF(SeO_3),^{10e}$ but it is also possible that the structure is converted from NCS to centrosymmetric (CS), as is the case with the conversion from $KBi_2(IO_3)_2F_5$ (NCS)¹³ to $KBi_2(SeO_3)_F_5$ (CS).¹⁴ From a structural perspective, it is a challenge to avoid the structural transformation from NCS to CS, as well as maintain a favorable arrangement of the NLO groups.

Can we achieve aliovalent substitution from a known NCS structure while retaining the favorable framework for NLO behavior? Recently, numerous bismuth (Bi)-containing compounds have been shown to be excellent NLO materials, *e.g.*, $BiFSeO_3,^{10e}$ $BiO(IO_3),^{11}$ $KBi_2(IO_3)F_5,^{13}$ $BiF_2(IO_3),^{15}$ $Bi_3TeBO_9^{16}$ and $Bi_3(IO_3)_{12}\cdot Ag_4I.^{17}$ Bi-containing materials are often observed to have excellent SHG properties, not only attributable to the stereo-active lone-pair on $Bi^{3+}$ but also owing to the large coordination number of the cation that may bridge different NLO-active groups. The large coordination environment of $Bi^{3+}$ also makes it easy to execute the strategy of aliovalent substitution in Bi-containing structures. As such, a Bi-containing iodate $AgBi(IO_3)_4^{18}$ (ABI) was chosen to explore the possibility of the aforementioned structure conversion. ABI exhibits a 2D NCS structure, but most of the $[IO_3]$ groups are arranged in an anti-parallel manner.

The $[SO_4]$ group was chosen to replace a portion of the anti-parallel $[IO_3]$ groups. Precedence for this was observed in the molecular $Bi_2O(SO_4)(IO_3)_2$ that contains both $[SO_4]$ and $[IO_3]$ groups in one structure.¹⁹ And the excellent coordination ability of the $[SO_4]$ group allows it to act as a bridge to transform a 2D structure into a 3D structure. Moreover, the $[SO_4]$ group has been revealed to be useful as an NLO active chromophore to effectively construct NCS structures.²⁰ As a result, a new Bi-containing sulfate iodate, namely $AgBi(SO_4)(IO_3)_2$ (**ABSI**), was discovered, with a similar NCS structural framework to **ABI**. Through the aliovalent substitution, the structural framework is converted from 2D to 3D while the favorable framework for NLO behavior is retained.

Colorless transparent crystals of **ABSI** (Fig. S1, ESI†) were grown through the hydrothermal process using $Ag_2O$, $Bi_2O_3$, $HIO_3$, and sulfuric acid (details are provided in the ESI†). The purity of samples has been confirmed by powder X-ray diffraction (Fig. S2, ESI†). The single crystal structure determination result shows that **ABSI** crystallizes into an NCS structure with a triclinic space group, $P\overline{1}$ (No. 1) (Tables S1-S3, ESI†). It exhibits a 3D framework consisting of $[BiO_8]$, $[IO_3]$, and $[SO_4]$ groups (Fig. 1). The $[Bi(1)O_8]$ octahedra are connected by $[I(4)O_3]$ groups, sharing one O atom with $[I(1)O_3]$ groups, to form the $[[Bi(1)I_2O_4)O_6]_{\infty}$ layer (Fig. 1b). However, the $[Bi(2)O_8]$ octahedra are connected by $[I(3)O_3]$ groups through corner-sharing to form a $[[Bi(2)IO_3)O_5]_{\infty}$ layer along the $ab$-plane (Fig. 1c). Subsequently, the $[[BiI_2O_4)O_6]_{\infty}$ layer and $[[BiIO_3)O_5]_{\infty}$ layer are connected by $[I(2)O_3]$ groups and form a $[Bi_2I_4O_{15}]_{\infty}$ double-layer structure. These $[Bi_2I_4O_{15}]_{\infty}$ double-layer structures are further linked by $[SO_4]$ groups along the $c$-axis to form the 3D framework of **ABSI** (Fig. S3, ESI†). Two Ag atoms are inserted into the space of the $[Bi_2I_4O_{15}]_{\infty}$ double-layer. In connectivity terms, the structure of **ABSI** may be described as ${2[BiO_{8/2}]^{5-} 2[SO_{3/2}O]^{+} 3[IO_{3/2}]^{2+} [IO_{2}O_{1/2}]}^{2-}$ with the charge balanced by two $Ag^+$ cations.

The asymmetric unit of **ABSI** contains two Ag atoms (Ag1 and Ag2), two Bi atoms (Bi1 and Bi2), two sulfate groups and four iodate groups (Fig. S4, ESI†). Ag(1) and Ag(2) atoms are of different coordinations (Fig. 1a) and bonded to eight and seven O atoms respectively with the Ag–O bond length ranging from 2.381(15) to 2.864(15) Å. Two Bi atoms are eight coordinated with O atoms to build a $[BiO_8]$ polyhedron with the Bi–O bond length in the range of 2.322(13)–2.662(13) Å. The S–O, and I–O bond lengths for sulfate groups and iodate groups are in the range of 1.451(14)–1.498(13) Å and 1.784(14)–1.846(12) Å, respectively. Bond valence calculations (BVCs)²¹ for **ABSI** resulted in values of 1.00–1.02, 3.02–3.11, 4.87–5.18, and 5.84–5.97 for Ag, Bi, I, and S atoms, respectively (Table S2, ESI†). These values are consistent with the reported oxidation states.

**ABSI** is designed from the parent compound $AgBi(IO_3)_4^{18}$ (**ABI**) through aliovalent substitution of two $[IO_3]^{-}$ by $[SO_4]^{2-}$. It should be noted that **ABSI** maintains a similar topology to the parent **ABI** compound (Fig. S5, ESI†). Both of them feature an almost same central $[Bi_2I_4O_{15}]_{\infty}$ double-layer structural framework but exhibit a different dimensionality. In **ABI**, the $[Bi_2I_4O_{15}]_{\infty}$ double-layers are separated by $[I(1)O_3]$, $[I(2)O_3]$, $[I(6)O_3]$ and $[I(7)O_3]$ groups, thus **ABI** exhibits a 2D structure. However, in **ABSI**, the $[SO_4]$ groups replace these disconnected $[IO_3]$ groups of **ABI** and link the $[Bi_2I_4O_{15}]_{\infty}$ double-layers to form a 3D structure. According to the order-disorder (OD) theory constructed by Dornberger-Schiff,²² both the layers built by $[I(1)O_3]$, $[I(2)O_3]$, $[I(6)O_3]$ and $[I(7)O_3]$ groups in **ABI** (Fig. S6, ESI†) and the sulfate layer in **ABSI** possess inversion center $\overline{1}$ as the local pseudo-symmetry operation ($\lambda$-PO) and could be classified as centro-symmetric layers. The NLO character for both **ABSI** and **ABI** is derived from the equal polar $[Bi_2I_4O_{15}]_{\infty}$ double-layers in their structure, thus the substitution of the centro-symmetric layer would not lead to the decrease in the NLO response. Instead, the lengths of bridging S–O bonds are, though slightly longer than terminal S–O bonds or those with hydrogen bonds,²⁰ much shorter than I–O bond lengths, so the replacement of disconnected $[IO_3]^{-}$ by linked $[SO_4]^{2-}$ compresses the centro-symmetric layer from 9.240 Å (**ABI**) to 5.299 Å (**ABSI**) and increases the concentration of polar layers with nearly equal width in **ABSI** (Fig. S5, ESI†). This also leads to the decrease of the occupied volume per molecule from $280.99\ \mathring{A}^3$ for **ABI** ($(2247.9\ \mathring{A}^3/8)^{18}$) to $212.39\ \mathring{A}^3$ for **ABSI** ($(424.78\ \mathring{A}^3/2)$). According to the anionic group theory,²³ the NLO performance of **ABSI** should benefit from this structural transformation. The sulfate groups acting as a bridge can also be seen in the structure of another 3D bismuth sulfate iodate, $Bi_2O(SO_4)(IO_3)_2$.¹⁹ However, the absence of $Ag^+$ cations resulted in more intensive Bi–O bonds ($[Bi_4O_2]^{8+}$ tetrameric cluster) and inverted $[IO_3]$ groups, and hence feature a centro-symmetric structure.

Since the title compound crystallizes into an NCS structure, the SHG measurement was performed to investigate its NLO properties. **ABSI** shows strong SHG responses of $3.9 \times$ KDP at 1064 nm and $0.36 \times$ KTP at 1950 nm, and it is phase-matching under both laser radiation (Fig. 2). As the exact data of SHG response for **ABI** were not reported, the polycrystalline sample of **ABI** was synthesized as reported¹⁸ and its SHG response was measured under the same conditions for comparison. **ABI** shows a weaker SHG response of about $3.0 \times$ KDP at 1064 nm (Fig. S7, ESI†), consistent with the prediction based on the structures. To gain insights into the NLO properties of ABSI, first-
principles calculations based on density functional theory
(DFT) methods were performed. The calculated band gap for
ABSI is 2.58 eV (Fig. S12, ESI†), which is smaller than the
experimental value due to the well-known underestimation of
band gaps by standard GGA functionals in DFT.²⁴ Since the
NLO properties of a crystal are closely related to the (virtual)
electronic transitions across the band edges, the electronic
band structure calculation was carried out. The partial density
of states (PDOS) show that the upper part of the valence band
(VB) is dominantly occupied by overlapping Ag 4s and O 2p
orbitals located from −5 eV to 0 eV, while the bottom of the
conduction band (CB) is mainly composed of I 5s5p and O 2p
orbitals from +2.5 eV to +5 eV (Fig. S13 and S14, ESI†). The d-p
hybridization between Ag 4d and O 2p orbitals is especially
noticeable as orbital hybridization near band edges has been
reported to be beneficial to large SHG effect.²⁵ Other orbitals
have much smaller contributions to VB maximum and CB
minimum. Therefore, the NLO effect in ABSI mainly comes
from the contribution of Ag-O and I-O groups.

The first-principles powder SHG effect is 1.81 pm V⁻¹
(4.5 × KDP) for ABSI, which is in good agreement with the
experimental result (3.9 × KDP). This value is larger than that
for ABI (1.27 pm V⁻¹), matching well with the experimental
results. To further analyze and clarify the SHG contribution of
different structural units, the SHG-weighted electron density²⁶
in ABSI is calculated, and the contributions of occupied and
unoccupied states to the virtual-electron (VE) and virtual-hole
(VH) process in SHG response are visualized in Fig. 3. Note that
the VE process always dominates the SHG response.²⁷ Clearly,
in both virtual processes, a majority of SHG-weighted electron

We also investigate the contribution of ${\rm [SO_4]}$ and ${\rm [BiO_8]}$
polyhedra in the ABSI structure to the SHG effect. As well
known, the introduction of distorted polyhedral units is bene-
ficial to enhance the SHG response. Since the distorted ${\rm [SO_4]}$
groups in ABSI follow a centro-symmetrical arrangement, they
provide little contribution to the SHG effect. ${\rm Bi^{3+}}$ cations, on the
other hand, are generally considered as candidates with stereo-
chemically active lone pair (SCALP) electrons that are beneficial
to form distorted polyhedral units.²⁸ However, the electron
localization function (ELF) calculations show that the ELF
isosurfaces on the ${\rm Bi^{3+}}$ cation in ${\rm [BiO_8]}$ groups are hardly
displayed (Fig. S16, ESI†). Thus, the ${\rm Bi^{3+}}$ cations in ABSI do
not have SCALP electrons, as also observed in some other
compounds,²⁹ and contribute little to the SHG effect. In com-
parison, the lobe-like ELF isosurfaces on the ${\rm I^{5+}}$ cations in the

$[IO_3]$ groups are clearly displayed. This indicates that the $I^{5+}$ cations in ABSI have highly localized SCALP electrons, which result in the strong distortion and polarization of $[IO_3]$ and thus greatly enhance the NLO effect.

In summary, a novel Bi-containing sulfate iodate, $AgBi(SO_4)(IO_3)_2$, has been designed and synthesized through a hydrothermal process. It undergoes a structural transformation from a two-dimensional topology to a three-dimensional topology through aliovalent substitution of an anionic group based on a similar framework of $AgBi(IO_3)_4$. $AgBi(SO_4)(IO_3)_2$ exhibits strong SHG responses of $3.9 \times$ KDP at 1064 nm and $0.36 \times$ KTP at 1950 nm with phase-matching properties. The $Ag^+$ cations, and $[BiO_8], [SO_4]$, and $[IO_3]$ groups in its structure contribute to its NLO properties, whereas its strong SHG response principally roots from partially paralleled $[IO_3]$ groups. It also possesses a high energy band gap of 3.40 eV and hence can be expected to be a promising candidate for an NLO material. This work also provides a feasible strategy for a dimensional upgrade of the crystal structure and design of new NLO materials with better-performance.
