# DFT calculation of relative energies and band gaps for Al,P‑doped bilayer graphene isomers

## Problem background
Bilayer graphene doped with heteroatoms offers tunable electronic properties for nanoelectronics. This work investigates simultaneous aluminum and phosphorus doping, exploring how the orientation of the dopants (protruding into or out of the interlayer region) affects relative stability and electronic character (metallic, semiconducting, or semimetallic). First-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations with the local density approximation (LDA) and the van der Waals density functional (VDW-DF) are used to compute relative energies and band gaps.

## Approach
The study employs DFT in the SIESTA code with the LDA and VDW-DF functionals, using a double‑zeta basis set with polarization functions (DZP) and norm‑conserving pseudopotentials. A 4×4 supercell of bilayer graphene (AB stacking, 64 carbon atoms) is constructed; one carbon atom in the upper layer is replaced by an aluminum atom and one carbon in the lower layer by a phosphorus atom. Four distinct conformations are generated by varying the protrusion direction of the dopants:

- (a) Both atoms protruding into the interlayer region, forming an Al–P bond (linked).
- (b) Al protruding in, P protruding out.
- (c) Al protruding out, P protruding in.
- (d) Both atoms protruding out.

For each conformation, the structure is optimized using spin‑polarized DFT, and the ground‑state spin multiplicity (0 μB or 2 μB) is determined by comparing total energies of different spin configurations. Basis set superposition error (BSSE) corrected total energies are computed via the counterpoise method, treating each monolayer as a separate fragment. Band structures are calculated along the high‑symmetry paths Γ–M–K–Γ and Γ–F–K–Γ, and the density of states (DOS) is obtained. The final evaluated quantities are the relative energies of the four conformations, with structure (b) as the zero reference, and the band gap classification (metal, semimetal, or a numeric gap in eV) for each conformation, obtained at both LDA/DZP and VDW‑DF/DZP levels.

## Reproduction target
Compute the relative energies (eV) and band gap classification for the four structural isomers of Al,P‑doped bilayer graphene, using both LDA/DZP and VDW‑DF/DZP functionals. The relative energies are defined with respect to structure (b) (Al in, P out) set to zero for each functional. The band gap classification is either `'metal'`, `'semimetal'`, or a numeric value (in eV) for a semiconductor. Write all results to the CSV file `/app/outputs/relative_energies_and_band_gaps.csv` with the columns: `method`, `structure`, `relative_energy_eV`, `band_gap_classification`. The CSV should contain eight rows (four structures × two methods).

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Structure generation
- Role: process
- Action: Build a 4×4 supercell of bilayer graphene (AB stacking, 64 carbon atoms). Replace one carbon atom in the upper layer with an aluminum atom and one carbon atom in the lower layer with a phosphorus atom. Create four initial geometries corresponding to the four conformations: (a) both dopants protruding into the interlayer region and forming an Al P bond (linked); (b) Al protruding in, P protruding out; (c) Al out, P in; (d) both protruding out.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/initial_geometries.txt`

### Step 2: Geometry optimisation and spin‑state determination
- Role: process
- Action: For each of the four initial structures, perform spin‑polarised DFT geometry optimisation using SIESTA with the LDA/DZP and VDW‑DF/DZP functionals. Optimise atomic positions and in‑plane lattice parameters; keep the c‑axis fixed at 20 Å. Determine the ground‑state spin multiplicity (Q=0 or Q=2 μB) for each isomer by comparing total energies of different spin configurations.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/optimized_geometries.txt`

### Step 3: BSSE‑corrected energy calculation
- Role: process
- Action: For each isomer at its ground‑state spin, compute the counterpoise (Boys‑Bernardi) basis‑set superposition error (BSSE) corrected total energy. For the bilayer this involves ghost‑atom calculations for each monolayer. Report the BSSE‑corrected total energy for each isomer at both LDA/DZP and VDW‑DF/DZP levels.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/bss_energies.txt`

### Step 4: Band structure calculation
- Role: process
- Action: Using the optimised ground‑state geometries, perform non‑SCF band structure calculations with SIESTA (LDA/DZP and VDW‑DF/DZP) along the high‑symmetry paths Γ‑M‑K‑Γ and Γ‑F‑K‑Γ using 100 k‑points. Compute the density of states (DOS) as well.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/band_structure_data.txt`

### Step 5: Assemble final scored results
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the BSSE‑corrected energies, calculate the relative energy of each isomer by setting the energy of structure (b) (Al in, P out) to zero for each method. From the band structures, determine whether each structure is metallic, semimetallic, or semiconducting, and if semiconducting, report the band gap in eV. Write the results to the output CSV.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/relative_energies_and_band_gaps.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: CSV with header: method (string: 'LDA/DZP' or 'VDW-DF/DZP'), structure (string: 'a','b','c','d'), relative_energy_eV (float), band_gap_classification (string: 'metal', 'semimetal', or a numeric string for the gap in eV)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier
