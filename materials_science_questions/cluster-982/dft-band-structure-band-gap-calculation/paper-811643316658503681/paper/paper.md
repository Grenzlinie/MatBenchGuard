## ABSTRACT

Bilayer graphene simultaneously doped with two different second-row atoms (aluminum and phosphorus) was investigated employing first-principle density functional calculations (LDA, VDW-DF and M06-L). In agreement with the results obtained for bilayer graphene doped with one type of heteroatom, we found that at 1.56% atomic doping of Al and 1.56% atomic doping of P, the structure with sheets not linked is more stable than that with an Al-P bond. While all methods agreed in predicting the most stable structure, the relative energies of the structural isomers were different at the three levels of theory considered. The electronic properties of doped bilayer graphene showed an unusual variation upon small conformational changes. In effect, metallic, semiconducting and semimetallic properties were observed upon variation of the protruding orientation of the heteroatoms. The electronic properties of bilayer graphene seem to be determined by the layer whose heteroatom protrudes into the interlayer region. When Al protrudes in and P out metallic properties are observed. However, when P protrudes in and Al in a small band gap semiconductor is obtained. This work shows that the electronic properties of bilayer graphene can be more easily tuned than those of monolayer graphene because a small conformational change can induce a metallic to semiconductor transition or vice versa.

## 1. Introduction

Graphene sheets were exfoliated for the first time as early as 1968 by Boehm et al. [1]. However, it was not until 2004 when Novoselov et al. [2] reported the mechanical exfoliation of graphite that the interest in studying graphene started to grow at exponential rate. One of the most important areas in which graphene is expected to contribute more significantly is in electronics, probably as a replacement for silicon [3]. For this reason a great amount of attention has been devoted to adjust the electronic properties of graphene. In this line of research the introduction of heteroatoms in the $sp^{2}$ framework of graphene is one of the most promising strategies. In effect, recent works showed that by replacing carbon atoms in monolayer and bilayer graphene [4-9] it is possible to open a band gap in the band structure of monolayer and bilayer graphene. For example, we found that a band gap of 0.30 eV could be opened when a sulfur atom replaces a carbon atom in a $5×5$ graphene supercell [10-12], whereas a larger band gap was determined for P-doped monolayer and bilayer graphene [10-12]. Also, the P-doped and S-doped graphenes have outstanding properties to construct sensors for $NO_{2}$ and $SO_{2}$ [6,7]. However, Al-doped graphene is not expected to be a good candidate because it is too reactive. The second row atom doped graphenes do not only exist in the theoretical world. Indeed, the experimental synthesis of sulfur doped graphite [13] has been reported and the synthesis of P-doped fullerene like thin film is possible [14-16].

In two recent works we studied the second row atom doping of bilayer graphene. We considered the introduction of aluminum, silicon, phosphorus and sulfur atoms in both layers. We limited the study to the introduction of only one type of atom. The outcome of these investigations was that the band gap of bilayer graphene can be tuned upon variation of the heteroatom. Indeed, it is metallic for Al, semiconductor for P and S and semimetallic for silicon. Another interesting effect was that for Al and P the heteroatoms belonging to different layers do not form a P-P or a Al-Al bond, even though for $4×4$ bilayer graphene Si forms an interlayer link. Therefore, in some cases the non-covalent interactions are stronger than the covalent bonds. We also showed that when the amount of silicon doping is reduced, as in the $5×5$ bilayer model, the not linked structure becomes more stable. In the same vein, increasing the amount of heteroatom doping favors the formation of covalent links. This result showed that the covalent and non-covalent interactions can be tuned upon variation of the amount of doping. The $p$-type and $n$-type of dopings may facilitate the formation of the Al-P bonding and the linked structure may be expected to be the most stable one. However, our calculations show that this is not the case. Employing the local density approximation (LDA), the van der Waals density functional VDW-DF and the M06-L functional we studied all the structural isomers of bilayer graphene simultaneously doped with Al and P. The reason for using the latter two functionals is that they were developed to correctly treat dispersion effects, a serious problem found in previously developed functionals. Although LDA does not include dispersion interactions, by fortuitous error cancellation it is possible to obtain semi-quantitative results. Our results demonstrate that a small conformational change as the protruding orientation of the heteroatoms can induce a metallic to semiconductor transition or vice versa. We expect that these results can be useful for the construction of bilayer graphene based sensors.

## 2. Methods

The methodology applied herein is the same as the one we applied to successfully study chemically modified graphene [10-12,17-21] and single wall carbon nanotubes (SWCNT) [22-27]. Briefly, we performed Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations employing the LDA [28] and VDW-DF [29] functionals, as implemented in SIESTA [30-31], which performs self-consistent field (SCF) calculations using numerical basis sets. For the latter we have selected the double-zeta basis set with polarization functions (DZP) and fixed the orbital confining cut-off to 0.01 Ry. The split norm used was 0.15. The DFT implementation in SIESTA can be prone to significant basis set superposition error (BSSE), even with relatively low degree of radial confinement [17]. To avoid this problem, when we evaluated the interlayer interaction energies for bilayer graphene we used the counterpoise correction suggested by Boys and Bernardi [32]. In all cases, we used relaxed structures to estimate the basis set superposition error (BSSE) corrected binding energies and we took monomer deformation energies into account. The interaction between ionic cores and valence electrons was described by the Troullier-Martins norm conserving pseudopotentials [33]. We have checked the convergence of the Mesh cut-off; using a value of 200 Ry we obtained converged binding energies (within 0.02 eV). It is important to note that in our previous studies [10-12,17-21] we performed methodology calibration against mesh cut-off and orbital confining cut-off values. By doing so, we were able to reproduce the results obtained without the use of pseudopotentials. We performed LDA and VDW-DF periodic calculations for bilayer graphene using a $4 \times 4$ unit cell; the total number of atoms is 64. The lattice parameters along the $a$ and $b$ directions were optimized, although the non-periodic $c$ axis was maintained frozen at $20$ Å. For bilayer graphene we used a Monkhorst-Pack $k$-point sampling scheme of $40 \times 40 \times 1$ $k$ points, which gave converged results in every calculations. Further increasing of the density of $k$-points did not change the results. Band structures were calculated using 100 points along the $\Gamma$-M-K-$\Gamma$ and $\Gamma$-F-K-$\Gamma$ paths. In all cases we checked that the band gaps obtained from the band structure were similar to those obtained from the density of states. Geometry optimizations were pursued using the conjugate gradient algorithm until all residual forces were smaller than $0.01$ eV/Å. For comparative purposes calculations employing the M06-L functional [34,35] were performed as implemented in Gaussian 2009 [37]. The basis sets employed were the 6-31G* and 6-31(2d) [36]. The reason for using M06-L is that this functional, as observed for M06-2X, it is expected to work reasonable well for stacking interactions [33] but without the huge computational cost that M06-2X would have for periodic systems. Basis set superposition error was not considered for the M06-L calculations because the developers of this functional do not recommend the inclusion of BSSE [34,35]. The ultrafine grid was used in the M06-L calculations.

Despite the aforementioned differences, all methods agree in the determination of the most stable structure, even when using a 6-31G(2d) basis set for M06-L. In our previous work [12] we found that when the same type of heteroatom is considered the not linked structure is more stable for Al and P whereas for silicon the linked structure is slightly more stable, although when the concentrations of Si atoms decreases the not linked structure becomes more stable; that is the case of the 5 × 5 Si double doped bilayer graphene. For the simultaneous Al and P doping in different layers it would be reasonable to expect the linked structure to be more stable because one layer has p-type doping and the other n-type doping. However, this is not the case and the not linked structure is more stable. The analysis of the magnetic moment and charges may help to understand this preference. At all levels structure (b) has zero magnetic moment and the structure with $Q=2\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ is 0.5 and 0.4 eV less stable at the LDA/DZP and M06-L/6-31G* levels, respectively. The Mulliken charge distribution reveals that the heteroatoms have positive charge, Al is +0.43 and P is +0.54. The negative charge is concentrated in the three carbon atoms bonded to P which have −0.56 each one summing up −1.68. The carbon atoms bonded to Al also have negative charge but it is smaller, −0.13. This analysis shows that the large positive charge of both atoms is responsible for the not formation of the Al–P bond. Indeed, in the linked structure the charge of Al is diminished to +0.25 while that of P is +0.47.

Regarding the interlayer separation, for structures (b) and (c) it is less than 0.1 Å different than that computed for bilayer graphene at all levels of theory. As expected, it is a bit longer for structure (b) because Al protrudes more, and shorter for structures (c) and (d). Although, LDA gives smaller distances, this effect is not translated into the interlayer interaction energies reported in Table 2. Indeed, in all cases the LDA stacking interactions are smaller than those determined with VDW-DF. For the most stable structure (b) the interlayer interaction energies are increased 2.5 and 1.5 times with respect to pristine bilayer graphene, at the LDA/DZP and VDW-DF/DZP levels, respectively. Therefore, heteroatom doping can be used to tune the lubricating properties of bilayer graphene, since a stronger interaction will difficult the rotation and displacement of the sheets.

Finally, in Fig. 2 we present the density of states determined at the LDA level for the structures considered, whereas in Table 3 we list the band gaps determined with the three functionals employed. The variation of the electronic properties among the four structural isomers is very interesting. We recall that monolayer graphene doped with Al is metallic but P-doped graphene is semiconductor with a band gap close to 0.6 eV [11,12]. For structure (b) (the most stable one) the metallic properties of Al-doped graphene predominate and the whole structure is metallic. However, when the P atom protrudes into the interlayer region and the Al atom protrudes out, the structure is a semiconductor. The band gap is 0.2, 0.2 and 0.30 eV at the LDA/DZP, VDW-DF/DZP and M06-L/6-31G* levels, respectively. Therefore, for the binary second row atom doped graphene, the electronic properties seem to be determined by the atom that is between layers. The question that obviously arises is about the electronic properties when both atoms protrude out? Inspection of Fig. 2 reveals that structure (d) is semimetallic. Thus, in this structure the metallic properties of Al-doped graphene and the semiconducting character of P-doped graphene cancel each other giving a semimetallic bilayered material. It is worth to mention that for all the structures reported in Table 3 the magnetic moment is zero. As discussed above the structures with $Q = 2$ $\mu_\text{B}$ were found to be less stable. Yet, for structure (d) at the VDW-DF level there is a magnetic structure which is 0.02 eV more stable that the non-magnetic one, but it is 0.60 less stable than structure (b). Finally, the linked structure shows a semiconducting character. The band gaps 0.35, 0.30 and 0.14 eV at the LDA/DZP, VDW-DF/DZP and M06-L/6-31G* levels, respectively.

## 4. Conclusions

The structural, electronic and magnetic properties of bilayer graphene doped with two different second row atoms was studied employing periodic conditions and the LDA, VDW-DF and M06-L density functionals. In agreement with the results obtained for bilayer graphene doped with one type of heteroatom, we found that at 1.56% at. doping of Al and 1.56% at. doping of P, the structure which is not linked is more stable than that with a weak Al–P bond.

While all methods agreed in predicting the most stable structure, the relative energies of the remaining structures were different at the three levels of theory considered. The electronic properties showed a very interesting variation upon conformational changes. Metallic, semiconducting and semimetallic properties were observed upon variation of the protruding orientation of the heteroatoms. The electronic properties of bilayer graphene seem to be determined by the layer whose heteroatom protrudes into the interlayer region. In effect, when Al protrudes in and P out metallic properties are observed. However, when P protrudes in and Al in a small band gap semiconductor is obtained.
