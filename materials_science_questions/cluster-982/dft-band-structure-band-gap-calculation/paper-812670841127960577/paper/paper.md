# ARTICLE

# Na₃Sc₂(PO₄)₂F₃: rational design and synthesis of alkali rare-earth phosphate fluoride as ultraviolet nonlinear optical crystal with an enlarged birefringence

Feng Xu, $^{abc}$ Guang Peng, $^{ac}$ Chensheng Lin, $^{d}$ Dan Zhao, $^{e}$ Bingxuan Li, $^{ac}$ Ge Zhang, $^{ac}$ Shunda Yang, $^{acf}$ and Ning Ye*$^{ac}$

Received 00th January 20xx,
Accepted 00th January 20xx
DOI: 10.1039/x0xx00000x

An alkali rare-earth phosphate fluoride nonlinear optical (NLO) crystal, $Na_3Sc_2(PO_4)_2F_3$ (NSPOF), was designed and synthesized. The structure of NSPOF can be described as a three-dimensional (3D) framework assembled by chessboard-shaped two-dimensional (2D) $[Sc_2P_2O_8F_3]_{\infty}$ layer and disordered Na⁺ cations in the cavities, possessing $[PO_4]^{3-}$ tetrahedral groups and $[ScO_4F_2]$ tetrahedral bipyramids as fundamental building blocks. With the introduction of Sc³⁺ and F⁻ into phosphate, the NSPOF exhibited an obviously enlarged birefringence of 0.0978 @ 546.1 nm, which is large enough for phase-matching (PM) in the UV region. Specifically, the shortest PM wavelength calculated based on the calculated refractive index dispersion curve can reach to 305 nm, which is shorter than that of most reported phosphates. The large birefringence can be attributed to the uniform alignment of $[ScO_4F_2]$ tetrahedral bipyramids and the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ tetrahedral groups. This work will path a new avenue for the design and discovery of more phosphates NLO materials with large birefringence.

## 1. Introduction

The all-solid-state laser, generating coherent light by second-harmonic generation (SHG) process of nonlinear optical (NLO) crystals, has been widely used in scientific research, photonic technologies, laser and optical communication, etc. $^{1-4}$ As the core device of all-solid-state laser, the development of NLO crystals is demanded urgently. According to the anionic group theory, $^{5-8}$ anionic groups play an important role in SHG process. $[PO_4]^{3-}$ tetrahedral group has been proved to be a promising building unit with wide band gap, which is favorable for designing ultraviolet (UV) and deep-UV (DUV) NLO crystals. $^{9}$ In addition to the isolated state, $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups can also be aggregated into $[P_2O_7]$ dimer, $[P_3O_{10}]$ trimer, and $[PO_3]_{\infty}$ chain and so on, which is beneficial for enriching the phosphate structures. $^{10}$ Recently, many promising phosphates UV and DUV NLO crystals were discovered, such as $LiRb_2PO_4,^{11} LiCs_2PO_4,^{12,13}$ $RbBa_2(PO_3)_5,^{14}$ $LiPbPO_4,^{15}$ $Ba_5P_6O_{20},^{16}$ $Pb_3Mg_3TeP_2O_{14},^{17}$ $M^I M^{II} P_3O_9$ ($M^I$ = Rb, $M^{II}$ = Cd, Mg, Ca; $M^I$ = Cs, $M^{II}$ = Pb, Sr; $M^I$ = K, $M^{II}$ = Mg), $^{18}$ $M_4Mg_4(P_2O_7)_3$ (M = K, Rb), $^{19}$ $KMg(H_2O)PO_4,^{20}$ $LiHgPO_4,^{21}$ et al. In fact, the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ group suffers from two shortcomings, namely too small microscopic second-order nonlinear polarizability and optical anisotropy, respectively, which usually result in small SHG response and birefringence. Enough birefringence is one of the indispensable properties for UV/DUV NLO materials, which is required for phase matching (PM) and determines the shortest PM wavelength. Too small birefringence tends to make conventional PM impossible, for example, $CsLa(PO_3)_4$ (0.0068 @ 1064 nm), $^{22}$ $AMgPO_4·6H_2O$ (A = Rb, Cs) (0.005 @ 1064 nm, 0.006 @ 1064 nm), $^{23}$ and $Rb_2Ba_3(P_2O_7)_2$ (0.0108 @ 1064 nm), $^{14}$ which limited their practical application. For ideal UV NLO crystals, an appropriate range of birefringence should be of $\Delta n > 0.05$ at 1064 nm to realize PM behaviors in the UV region for the process of SHG. $^{9,24,25}$ Therefore, it is meaningful to enlarge birefringence of phosphates for further NLO applications. Pan and Yang$^{9}$ verified that the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups with a specific arrangement, namely the "zipper" arrangement of with large angle deviation, would enhance the birefringence of phosphates. Besides, they designed 15 non-centrosymmetric rare-earth phosphates in the system of Y-Sc-Lu-P-O, which all have large birefringence (0.088-0.136 @ 1064 nm) and short SHG PM wavelength (199-230 nm). Furthermore, it is known that the fluorine can effectively blue-shift the absorption edge. $^{25-28}$ Very recently fluorophosphates containing P-F bond have been demonstrated to be potential DUV NLO material. With the introduction of F, the birefringence of these crystals were also effectively enlarged. $^{29,30}$ For instance, the compound $NaNH_4PO_3F·H_2O$ shown a relative large birefringence of > 0.053 at 589.3 nm due to the uniform alignment of P-F bonds. $^{29}$ Considering the lower stability of fluorophosphates than that of phosphates, we

In this work, a noncentrosymmetric (NCS) alkali rare-earth phosphate fluoride NLO crystal, $\mathrm{Na_3Sc_2(PO_4)_2F_3}$ (NSPOF), were designed and synthesized through mild hydrothermal technology. With the introduction of $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{F^-}$ into phosphate, the birefringence of NSPOF is expected to be about 0.0978 at 546.1 nm, which is a large improvement compared to other phosphates. ## 2. Experimental Section

### 2.1 Synthesis
All of the chemicals, NaF (99.0%), $\mathrm{Sc(NO_3)_3{\cdot}xH_2O}$ (99.99%), $\mathrm{H_3BO_3}$ (99.0%), $\mathrm{NH_4Cl}$ (99.9%), and $\mathrm{NH_4H_2PO_4}$ (99.0%) were purchased from Adamas and used without further purification. Single crystals of NSPOF were synthesized by a hydrothermal condition utilizing NaF (0.25g, 6mmol), $\mathrm{Sc(NO_3)_3{\cdot}xH_2O}$ (0.25g, 1mmol) and $\mathrm{NH_4H_2PO_4}$ (0.23g, 2mmol) as raw materials with $\mathrm{NH_4Cl}$ (0.43g, 8mmol)-$\mathrm{H_3BO_3}$ (0.43g, 7mmol) as mineralizers. A mixture of materials with 10ml deionized water were placed in an autoclave equipped with a Teflon liner (23ml) and heated at 220 °C for 5 days. Then the temperature of the autoclaves were decreased to room temperature at a rate of 5 °C h⁻¹. The polycrystalline NSPOF were obtained after washed by deionized water, ethanol and then dried in the air. The crystals were colorless, transparent, nonhygroscopic and stable in the air.

### 2.2 Single Crystal Structure Determination
The crystal structure was determined by single-crystal X-ray diffraction. Single crystal X-ray diffraction data of NSPOF were collected at room temperature on a Rigaku Mercury charge-coupled device diffractometer, which was equipped with a graphite-monochromatic Mo $K_\alpha$ radiation ($\lambda$ = 0.71073 Å). A transparent block of crystal was glued on the top of a glass fiber with epoxy for measurement. The intensity data sets were corrected with the $\omega$-scan mode. The data of NSPOF were integrated with the CrystalClear program. In the meantime, absorption corrections were also achieved according to the Multiscan technique. The crystal structures were determined by means of the direct methods, with full-matrix least-squares fitted on $F^2$ with SHELXL.³² ³³ All the atoms were refined with anisotropic displacement parameters. The validity of structures were certified on the ADDSYM algorithm from PLATON program,³⁴ simultaneously, no higher symmetries were found. Relevant crystallographic data and detailed structure refinement information of NSPOF are listed in Table 1. Besides, the data of atomic coordinates, isotropic displacement parameters, bond lengths and angles, and anisotropic displacement parameters are shown in Tables S1-S3 in Supporting Information, respectively.

### 2.3 Powder X-ray Diffraction (PXRD)
The PXRD analysis of polycrystalline materials were carried out on a Miniflex-600 diffractometer using Cu $K_\alpha$ radiation ($\lambda$ = 1.540598 Å) at room temperature. The angular scanning range was $2\vartheta$ = 5°-85° with a scan step width of 0.2° and a fixed time of 0.2 s. The PXRD pattern of NSPOF was identical to the simulated pattern from single crystal model (Figure S1), which verified the purity of the sample. The pure samples were used for subsequent characterizations.

### 2.4 Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscope (EDS) Analysis
The EDS analysis of NSPOF crystal was performed on a field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, SU-8010) equipped with an energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer. The cleaned crystals were laid on an aluminium sample stage with carbon conductive tape. Different regions on the crystals were tested and shown in Figure S2.

### 2.5 Birefringence
The birefringence of NSPOF was captured through a polarizing microscope equipped (ZEISS Axio Scope. A1) with Berek compensator under the light source whose wavelength was

546.1 nm because the variation of interference colors should be observed directly by polarizing microscope. To improve the accuracy, the first-, second-, and third-order interference color were employed for measurement because their boundary lines were relatively clear comparing to subsequent interference color. The clean transparent lamellar crystals were selected to scan under the polarizing microscope. The formula to calculate birefringence is expressed as

$$
R'=\left(\left|R_{e}-R_{o}\right|\right) \times T=\Delta n \times T \tag{1}
$$

Here, $R'$ denotes optical path difference, $\Delta n$ represents birefringence, and $T$ means the thickness of the crystal.

### 2.6 UV-vis-NIR Diffuse Reflectance Spectroscopy
The UV-vis-NIR diffuse reflection data of NSPOF was recorded at room temperature utilizing the powder sample with $BaSO_4$ as a standard of 100% reflectance on a PerkinElmer Lamda-950 UV/vis/NIR spectrophotometer. The scanning range was set as 190-2500 nm. The absorbance data were calculated from Reflectance spectrums according to the Kubelka-Munk function $F(R)=(1-R)^2/(2R)=K/S$, where $R$ is the reflectance, $K$ is the absorption, and $S$ is the scattering. $^{35,36}$

### 2.7 Laser Damage Threshold measurement
The laser damage threshold (LDT) of NSPOF powders was measured by a pulsed Nd: YAG laser (1064 nm, 1 Hz, 10 ns) at room temperature. $AgGaS_2$ was selected as the reference whose LDT was measured under the same condition. Particle size of samples were ranged from 150 to 212 $\mu$m.

### 2.8 Fourier-Transform Infrared (FT-IR) Spectroscopy
IR spectrum of NSPOF was measured on a Bruker VERTEX 70 Fourier transform infrared spectrometer with the help of KBr pellets in the range of 4000-400 cm⁻¹.

### 2.9 Thermal Analysis
The thermogravimetric (TG) analysis of NSPOF was tested on a NETZSCH STA449F3 simultaneous analyzer under flowing nitrogen gas. Reference ($Al_2O_3$ crucible) and crystal samples (10 mg) packed in the same $Al_2O_3$ crucible heated from 20 to 1300 °C at a rate of 10 °C min⁻¹ and then cooled to room temperature naturally.

### 2.10 Second Harmonic Generation (SHG) Measurement
Polycrystalline SHG signals were measured in accordance with the method proposed by Kurtz and Perry.³⁷ The measurements were carried out on a Q-switched Nd:YAG solid-state laser with the wavelength of 1064 nm. Since SHG efficiencies are known to depend strongly on particle size, polycrystalline samples were ground and sieved into the following particle size ranges: 25-45, 45-62, 62-75, 75-109, 109-150, and 150-212 $\mu$m. As a reference, the polycrystalline $KH_2PO_4$ (KDP) was also ground and sieved into the same particle size ranges. The samples in different particle sizes were pressed between two glass microscope cover slides thick as 1 mm and equipped in a 2 mm thick rubber ring, containing an 8 mm diameter hole in the center, which were tightly sheathed in an aluminous holders with a central hole in the same size of rubber ring. The samples were then placed in a light-tight box and under the irradiation of a pulsed laser. In the process of measurements, the background flash-lamp light on the sample can be limited by a cutoff filter, while the second harmonic signals can be selected by an interference filter (530 ± 10 nm) and detected with a photomultiplier tube attached to a RIGOL DS1052E 50 MHz oscilloscope. The same measured condition was applied to the test of standard nonlinear optical materials KDP. As a result, the ratio of the second-harmonic intensity outputs between the samples and reference can be calculated clearly. No index-matching fluid was used in any of the experiments.

### 2.11 First-Principles Computational Methods
Electronic structures, optical properties, and density of states (DOS) of NSPOF were analyzed according to the first-principles calculations by the density functional theory (DFT) ³⁸ calculation with CASTEP code ³⁹ from the Material Studio package. Generally, the exchange and correlative potential of electron-electron interactions were repsented by generalized gradient approximation (GGA) ⁴⁰ in the scheme of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE). Furthermore, the effective interaction between atom cores and valence electrons of each element was described through the norm-conserving pseudopotentials in the Kleinman-Bylander form,⁴¹ where the valence electrons were expressed as Na 2s²2p⁶3s¹, Sc 3d¹4s², P 3s²3p³, O 2s²2p⁴, and F 2s²2p⁵ electrons. Besides, the k-point of first Brillouin zone of NSPOF was sampled as 2 × 2 × 1 Monkhorst-Pack scheme,⁴² which was used to calculate the optical properties and DOS of the crystal. During the calculation of optical properties, the energy cutoff and self-consistent convergence of the total energy was set to be 850 eV and $1.0 \times 10^{-5}$ eV atom⁻¹, respectively. In addition, the scissors operation was adopted in the dielectric function calculation owing to the underestimation of band gap of DFT method.⁴³ The calculated SHG coefficients were calculated from the "velocity-gauge" formula,⁴⁴ while the SHG density was calculated by the band-resolved method.⁴⁵

## 3. Results and discussion
### 3.1 Crystal Structure
The compound NSPOF crystallized in NCS space group I4mm (No. 107) of the tetragonal crystal system with unit cell parameters of $a = b = 6.5992(14)$, $c = 10.826(4)$, $\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 90^\circ$ and $Z = 2$, whose structure was shown in Figure 1. The basic structural unit was the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ tetrahedral group, which was arranged regularly. The P-O bond distances of $[PO_4]^{3-}$ group were varying from 1.504(15) to 1.552(13) Å, while the O-P-O bond angles were presented between 109.9(11)° and 110.83(17)°. The Sc atom was coordinated to four O atoms and two F atoms, forming a $[ScO_4F_2]$ tetrahedral bipyramid. The Sc(1)/Sc(3)-O and Sc(2)/Sc(4)-O bond distance of $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra was 2.1001(132) Å and 2.0739(14) Å, respectively, whereas the distance of Sc(1)/Sc(3)-F(1), Sc(1)/Sc(3)-F(2), Sc(2)/Sc(4)-F(1), and Sc(2)/Sc(4)-F(2) was 1.9379(326) Å, 2.0786(326) Å, 1.9952(326) Å, and 2.0602(198) Å. The valence of P and Sc was calculated to be +3.07 and +5.07, respectively, which was consistent with theoretical value. Pleasantly, the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ group and the $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedron were connected with each other in sequence by bridging O atoms, forming a one-

dimensional $[Sc_2P_2O_8F_3]$ chain. The chains, arranged infinitely in the space, built a chessboard-shaped two-dimensional $[Sc_2P_2O_8F_3]_{\infty}$ layer. The layers were aligned in a face-to-face manner linked through F atoms, which formatted a distinct three-dimensional framework and produced cavities surrounded by F atoms. It was worth noted that $Na^+$ were lying disorderedly in these cavities under the effect of the rotation axis of 4. Prospectively, the "zipper" arrangement of the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ tetrahedral groups and uniform alignment of the $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra in this structure satisfied the conditions for enlarging birefringence.

### 3.2 Birefringence
The maximum birefringence of NSPOF crystal measured on a ZEISS Axio Scope. A1 polarizing microscope by using the crystal ground into micron fragment, whose retardation value was 2099 nm, determined by the positive and negative rotation of compensatory. The thickness of measured crystal was $21.46\ \mu$m (Figure S3). Significantly, with the help of equation (1), the birefringence was calculated to be 0.0978 at 546.1 nm. The birefringence of NSPOF was also calculated to be 0.0750 at 546.1 nm and 0.0630 at 1064 nm based on DFT calculation, respectively, which was close to experimental result and relatively large compared to most reported phosphates and fluorophosphates UV/DUV NLO crystals, such as $LiCs_2PO_4$ (0.0080 @ 1064 nm),¹², ¹³ $KLa(PO_3)_4$ (0.0084 @ 1064 nm),⁴⁶ $K_4Mg_4(P_2O_7)_3$ (0.0108 @ 1064 nm),¹⁹ $K_2SrP_4O_{12}$ (0.0160 @ 1064 nm),⁴⁷ KDP (0.0340 @ 1064 nm),⁴⁸⁻⁵⁰ $KD_2PO_4$ (DKDP) (0.0383 @ 670 nm),⁵¹ $NH_4H_2PO_4$ (ADP) (0.0394 @ 1064 nm),⁵² $(NH_4)_2PO_3F$ (0.0270 @ 1064 nm),³⁰ and $NaNH_4PO_3F·H_2O$ (0.0530 @ 589.3 nm).²⁹ To better understand the origin of large birefringence of NSPOF, the Bader charge analysis was applied to investigate the birefringence contribution.⁵³ The birefringence contribution (Figure 2) from $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups and $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra were calculated to be about 58% and 42%, respectively, and the contribution from $Na^+$ ions can be ignored. It is obvious that the rational introduction and combination of $Sc^{3+}$ and $F^-$ ions can effectively enlarge the birefringence of phosphates.

The refractive index dispersion curves were also carried out and the results were presented in Figure 3. The calculated refractive indices of NSPOF as a function of wavelength were fitted by the least-squares method according to the Sellmeier equations: $n_o^2$ (or $n_e^2$) = A + $B\lambda^2/(\lambda^2$-C) - $D\lambda^2$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength in $\mu$m and A-D are the parameters. The fitted Sellmeier equation was expressed as

$$
n_{o}^{2}=1.99725+\frac{0.77174 \lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-0.03913}-0.00404 \lambda^{2}
\tag{2}
$$

$$
n_{e}^{2}=1.70521+\frac{0.86414 \lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-0.02351}-0.00065 \lambda^{2}
\tag{3}
$$

As is known that large birefringence in phosphate will promote the shortest PM wavelength blue shift to a shorter region. Based on the calculated refractive indexes and the fitted Sellmeier equations, the PM conditions of NSPOF were derived and shown in Figure S4, which exhibited a short Type I PM wavelength of 305 nm. As shown in Table 2, the shortest PM wavelength of NSPOF is much shorter than other reported phosphates and fluorophosphates, which can be attributed to the considerable birefringence. From Table 2, we can also see that even if the UV cut-off edge of several crystals reached the DUV region, it is a pity that they cannot achieve phase matching because of their too small birefringence.

### 3.3 Optical Properties

The UV-vis-NIR diffuse reflectance spectra of NSPOF exhibited a wide transmission range and displayed no obvious absorption peak range of 400-2200 nm (Figure 4). The band gap of NSPOF (shown in the inset of Figure 4) was estimated to be 4.85 eV, which was derived from the absorption relation (K/S) according to the Kubelka-Munk function above mentioned. The LDT of NSPOF samples were preliminarily evaluated in powder form. The experimental results indicated that NSPOF exhibited a LDT value of 315.36 MW cm⁻², which was much larger than that of AgGaS₂ (2.99 MW cm⁻²) under the same experimental condition (1064 nm, 10 ns, 1 Hz). As shown in Figure 5, the IR absorption spectra of NSPOF exhibited no absorption peaks in the region of 2.5-8.1 µm. It means that the NSPOF is transparent over a wide range covering the UV to mid-IR region (0.25-8.1 µm), which is meaningful for its application as NLO materials. The IR absorption peaks in the region of 1210 and 805 cm⁻¹ were attributed to the vibration of [PO₄]³⁻ groups, and the peaks from 725 cm⁻¹ to 486 cm⁻¹ belonged to the O-P-O bending vibration modes.

### 3.5 NLO Properties
The SHG signal of NSPOF corresponding to different particle sizes was exhibited in Figure 7, which was about 0.26 times that of KDP. Since the reported SHG coefficient of KDP ($d_{36}$) is 0.39 pm V⁻¹ and the SHG response is proportional to the square of the effective NLO coefficient $d_{\text{eff}}$, the derived $d_{\text{eff}}$ for NSPOF is about 0.20 pm V⁻¹. Furthermore, the second-order NLO susceptibilities were also carried out by the first principal calculation. Since the space group of NSPOF belongs to class 4mm, it has two independent nonvanishing NLO susceptibility tensors, $d_{31}$ and $d_{33}$. The calculated NLO coefficients of NSPOF, $d_{31}$ and $d_{33}$, were 0.18 and 0.24 pm V⁻¹ at a wavelength of 1064nm (1.165eV), respectively (Figure S5). It is noteworthy that element $d_{31}$ takes practical significance in the SHG process according to the requirement of PM in 4mm class, and it is clear that the calculated $d_{31}$ matches well with derived value based on the experimental powder SHG effect.

According to the anionic group theory,⁵⁴ the SHG contribution mainly resulted from the dipole transition of the intra-atomic transitions in anionic group $[PO_4]^{3-}$. And the geometrical addition of the microscopic second-order susceptibility of the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups generated the macroscopic SHG effect of NSPOF. According to this calculated method (the specific calculation steps are expatiated in the Supplementary Information),⁵⁵ the NLO coefficients are proportional to density of the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ group ($n/V$), the structural criterion ($C$), and nonvanishing second-order susceptibility $\beta_{311}^{(2)}$ and $\beta_{333}^{(2)}$, respectively. The calculated results were listed in Table 3, where the $C$ value of both 311 and 333 components for NSPOF were all 1, which means that the $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups in the "zipper" behavior were optimally arranged for $\chi_{311}^{(2)}$ and $\chi_{333}^{(2)}$.

Besides, the dipole moments of $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups and $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra were also calculated to evaluated their contribution to macroscopical SHG effect.⁵⁶⁻⁵⁸ The direction and magnitude of dipole moments of $[PO_4]^{3-}$ and $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra were listed in Table 4. The x and y components of all dipole moments were close to zero. The dipole moment of an individual $[PO_4]^{3-}$ group was calculated to be 1.511 D (D = Debye), which was derived from the z component of the polarization. The z components from the four $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups in a unit cell were added up to a total dipole moment of 6.044 D without any cancellation. While the dipole moments of $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra within one unit cell cancel each other to some extent along the c-axis, the z components from the four $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra can reach to a dipole moment of 3.632 D with the same polarization direction (positive c-axis) as that of $[PO_4]^{3-}$ polyhedra (Figure 8). Thus, it can be concluded that the constructively geometric superposition of dipole moments of $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups and $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra generated the SHG effect. Besides, the polarization contributions of $[PO_4]^{3-}$ groups and $[ScO_4F_2]$ polyhedra were calculated to be 62.5% and 37.5%, respectively.

### 3.6 Computational Description

The band structures of NSPOF were calculated and shown in Figure S6, which exhibited direct band gaps of 4.492 eV. The calculated value of band gaps was smaller than the experimental value of 4.85 eV due to the underestimation of band gap by DFT method. Therefore, the scissor value of NSPOF can be set of 0.358 eV for the subsequent SHG calculations. The total and partial densities of states (DOS and PDOS) of NCPOF were shown in Figure S7a. In general, since the linear and nonlinear optical properties were mainly determined the forbidden band, formed by the upper region of the valence band (VB) and the bottom of conduction band (CB), these states can be concentrated and shown.⁴⁵ To better indicate for phosphates, the DOS and PDOS of Na₃Sc₂(PO₄)₃ and NaScP₂O₇ were also calculated (Figure S7b and S7c), respectively. It can be observed that the contribution from the Na orbitals can almost be neglected in these states. Comparing the three compounds in Figure S7, the introduction of fluorine enlarged the band gap of NSPOF indeed. However, the band gap of NSPOF was still less than that of other phosphates due to the influence of Sc-O bonds in NSPOF. According to the analysis of PDOS for NSPOF, the bottom part of CB and the upper part of VB was mainly composed of Sc 3d orbital the O 2p orbital, respectively, demonstrated the covalent bonds between Sc and O, which decreased the band gap corresponding to the increasing absorption edge. The Sc-O bonds in Na₃Sc₂(PO₄)₃ and NaScP₂O₇ shown the same determination in the absorption edge. Besides, the calculated method of SHG density was employed to detail the origin of SHG effect in the real structure. The calculation was carried out through selecting the effective SHG on each band as a weighting coefficient, including occupied and unoccupied states, to conclude the probability densities of all occupied or unoccupied states. The results were exhibited in Figure S8, indicating that [PO₄]³⁻ groups and [ScO₄F₂] polyhedra play important roles in the SHG process obviously.

## Conclusions

In summary, an alkali phosphate fluoride NLO crystal, Na₃Sc₂(PO₄)₂F₃, was designed and synthesized through hydrothermal process, which exhibited a distinctly enlarged birefringence in phosphates with the introduction of Sc³⁺ and F⁻. The measured value in maximum birefringence of NSPOF crystal was 0.0978. Such a large birefringence made the shortest PM wavelength of the crystal down to 305 nm according to the calculated birefringence, which is shorter than that of most reported phosphates and fluorophosphates. Based on the analysis of the structure-property relationship, the [PO₄]³⁻ groups and [ScO₄F₂] polyhedra synergistically contributed to the large birefringence and SHG effect of this crystal. Besides, the spectral analysis of NSPOF indicated a wide transparent region (0.25-0.81μm) and large band gap of 4.85 eV. We believe that this work will provide new ideas for the design and exploration of novel phosphate NLO materials with large birefringence.
