# Host-Guest Compounds

## A Lanthanum-Filled Carbon-Boron Clathrate

Timothy A. Strobel,* Li Zhu, Piotr A. Guńka, Gustav M. Borstad, and Michael Guerette

**Abstract:** We report a carbon-boron clathrate with composition $2La@B_6C_6$ ($LaB_3C_3$). Like recently reported $SrB_3C_3$, $^{[1]}$ single-crystal X-ray diffraction and computational modelling indicate that the isostructural La member crystallizes in the cubic bipartite sodalite structure (Type-VII clathrate) with La atoms encapsulated within truncated octahedral cages composed of alternating carbon and boron atoms. The covalent nature of the B–C bonding results in a hard, incompressible framework, and owing to the balanced electron count, $La^{3+}$-$[B_3C_3]^{3-}$ exhibits markedly improved pressure stability and is a semiconductor with an indirect band gap predicted near 1.3 eV. A variety of different guest atoms may potentially be substituted within Type-VII clathrate cages, presenting opportunities for a large family of boron-stabilized, carbon-based clathrates with ranging physical properties.

Clathrates are inclusion compounds in which a host lattice traps atoms or small molecules within polyhedral cages. $^{[2]}$ Clathrate structures appear throughout nature in many tetrahedral systems including water, silica, silicon, germanium and tin. $^{[2a,3]}$ Many different structures are known for clathrates of the group 14 elements, which are composed of covalently bonded host frameworks. $^{[4]}$ Given the broad range of possible guest/host substitution schemes, as well as the possibility for complete guest atom removal, these materials exhibit broadly tunable properties ranging from direct-gap semiconductors to superconductors. $^{[5]}$ Due to the scattering of phonons from trapped guest atoms that restrict thermal transport, these compounds may also serve as useful thermoelectric materials. $^{[6]}$

Despite myriad clathrate structures in other tetrahedral systems, carbon-based clathrates remain elusive. The total number of experimental extended $sp^3$ carbon structures is quite limited, although predictions of hypothetical carbon allotropes are numerous (see for example the SACADA database$^{[7]}$). Based on chemical similarity with other systems, carbon clathrates have remained synthetic targets for more than 50 years. Clathrates are calculated to be low-energy metastable structures with respect to graphite and diamond,$^{[8]}$ which has stimulated the calculation of many intriguing properties including high strength/hardness and superconductivity at notably high temperatures.$^{[9]}$ Even with these promising properties and low formation energies, no synthetic paths to carbon clathrate materials are known.$^{[10]}$

Zeng et al.$^{[11]}$ recently introduced a concept of boron substitution that serves to increase the stability of carbon clathrate structures. Recently, we successfully demonstrated this concept by synthesizing the first carbon-boron clathrate at high pressures near 50 GPa.$^{[1]}$ This clathrate, $2Sr@B_6C_6$ ($SrB_3C_3$), consists of a covalent carbon-boron framework (bipartite sodalite type) with strontium ions trapped in the polyhedral cages. For $SrB_3C_3$, divalent $Sr^{2+}$ is only capable of transferring two electrons to the C-B host framework, resulting in the formation of a hole conductor (i.e., one electron short of a balanced count). This finding naturally raises the question of whether a trivalent guest could further stabilize this clathrate structure. If so, the overall precise electron count should result in an insulating (semiconducting) material with potentially improved stability, (e.g., decreased synthesis pressure and prospects for larger-volume scaling. Ferroelectricity for trivalent $Sc^{3+}$ in a distorted, non-centrosymmetric clathrate was recently predicted.$^{[12]}$

To probe the possibility of forming the bipartite sodalite-type carbon-boron clathrate with a trivalent guest, we performed first-principles structure searching computations using the CALYPSO method.$^{[13]}$ Like the case of $SrB_3C_3$, we predict that $LaB_3C_3$ becomes thermodynamically stable under high-pressure conditions. After conducting a computational survey of binary and ternary compounds in the La-B-C system, we predict that $LaB_3C_3$ clathrate has a negative formation enthalpy at all pressures and is the exothermic product of stable binary compounds above ca. 20 GPa (Figure 1 a). Compared with calculations for $SrB_3C_3$, the pressure required for $LaB_3C_3$ is reduced by ca. 20 GPa-a much more favorable synthetic condition that we tentatively attribute to the overall balanced electron count.

The La-B-C system already shows a rich phase diagram$^{[14]}$ with at least eight known ternary phases produced at ambient pressure including $LaBC$,$^{[15]}$ $LaB_2C_2$,$^{[16]}$ $La_5(BC)_x$,$^{[17]}$ $La_5B_4C_{5-x}$,$^{[18]}$ $La_{10}B_9C_{12}$,$^{[19]}$ $La_{15}B_{14}C_{19}$,$^{[20]}$ and two compositions near $La_4B_3C_{12}$ and $La_4B_5C_{18}$ with unknown structures.$^{[14]}$ Complex disordering and B-C substitution behavior was observed for a series of $La_5(BC)_x$ compounds with $5.6\leq x\leq8.8$.$^{[14]}$ Structures with covalent connectivity show either 1D buckled chains or 2D B/C nets with bond distances in the

The possibility of high-pressure $LaB_3C_3$ was probed experimentally by targeting the stoichiometric synthesis reaction $LaB_6 + LaC_2 + 4C \rightarrow 2LaB_3C_3$ using diamond anvil cells (DAC, see Supporting Information). After heating near 2500 K with an infrared fiber laser, the starting precursor mixture was converted into a nearly phase-pure cubic structure (Figure 1b), as expected for the clathrate (space group $Pm\overline{3}n$). At 57(2) GPa, the experimental lattice parameter $a=4.66$ Å is in good agreement with the DFT-optimized (PBE) $LaB_3C_3$ structure with $a=4.64$ Å, and Rietveld refinement of the powder data shows excellent agreement with the predicted clathrate model (Figure 1b). In addition, the stoichiometric conversion of the precursor mixture to a single homogeneous phase supports the 1La:3B:3C product stoichiometry, and a uniform single-phase region near this composition is supported by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping on the recovered sample (Supporting Information). Experimentally, we confirmed the formation of $LaB_3C_3$ at 38 GPa, but did not observe conversion at 35 GPa, placing a tentative bound on the minimum required synthesis pressure. Compared with $SrB_3C_3$ synthesized near 50 GPa, this represents a significant pressure reduction and suggests the possibility for future clathrate stabilization with other guest atoms at even milder conditions.

Given the strong contrast in electron density between framework and guest atoms, powder diffraction can neither distinguish the positions and occupancies of boron and carbon (i.e., the "coloring" of the clathrate lattice), nor the weak-intensity reflections that differentiate the primitive (bipartite) and body-centered cubic (sodalite) lattices. This coloring problem for boron-carbon positions is well known for other metal borocarbides with 2D lattices. $^{[22]}$ We therefore conducted synchrotron single-crystal X-ray diffraction on large grains produced by laser heating at very high temperatures above 3500 K. $^{[23]}$ High temperature was required for grain growth/recrystallization given the refractory nature of carbon/boron, and indicates a high melting temperature of the clathrate phase. Similar to the case of $SrB_3C_3$, the observation of {120} reflections with significant intensity ($N_{obs}=3$ with $I/\sigma(I)>15$) confirms the primitive space group $Pm\overline{3}n$ and suggests an ordered clathrate lattice. A fully disordered sodalite lattice would crystallize as bcc. While the opening angle of the DAC restricted overall data completeness ($\approx78\%$ at $2\theta_{max}$), the ordered bipartite structural model produces the lowest reliability factor between experimental observations compared with other ordered coloring schemes (Supporting Information), in good agreement with computational predictions. A computational survey of more than 600 different coloring types shows that the ordered bipartite structure possesses the lowest energy. $^{[1]}$ Nevertheless, while all available data are consistent with the ordered bipartite structure, we cannot fully exclude the possibility for different lattice coloring schemes that are beyond the limitations of our current single-crystal XRD data. For example, refinement of a fully disordered lattice refined in space group $Pm\overline{3}n$ is indistinguishable from the ordered model (Supporting Information). We recall that the detailed coloring of the 2D $ScB_2C_2$ lattice was only recently determined experimentally. $^{[24]}$

Synchrotron powder X-ray diffraction patterns collected during decompression also confirm the clathrate framework of $LaB_3C_3$ based on lattice compressibility. The decompression data were fitted using a third-order Birch-Murnaghan equation of state (EoS) with zero-pressure bulk modulus $B_0=254(9)$ GPa and its derivative $B_0'=3.6(4)$. The bulk modulus compares favorably with calculations based on DFT (Figure 1b), noting that LDA underestimates the volume for a given pressure. $LaB_3C_3$ is very incompressible with $B_0$ in the range of technical ceramics used for armor, such as $B_4C$ ($B_0=243(3)$ GPa). $^{[25]}$ The incompressibility complements the high hardness of $LaB_3C_3$ clathrate with a calculated ideal strength near 30 GPa, slightly higher than $SrB_3C_3$ and comparable to tungsten carbide (Supporting Information).

Similar to $SrB_3C_3$, the La counterpart is recoverable to ambient conditions, but must be preserved carefully under an inert atmosphere. When samples are exposed to air/moisture, they degrade into what is presumed to be hydrolysis products

(Supporting Information), which is a common feature for other metal borocarbide compounds. $^{[14,21b]}$ While dynamically stable at atmospheric pressure, we speculate that the $90^\circ$ C-B-C bond angles contribute to the sensitivity of the recovered material. Several attempts to prepare samples for transmission electron microscopy by focused ion beam milling were unsuccessful and resulted in oxidized amorphous material, but maintained a local metal:boron:carbon ratio near 1:3:3.

Like $SrB_3C_3$, $^{[1]}$ $LaB_3C_3$ is predicted to crystallize in the cubic bipartite sodalite structure (sod-b, Figure 2). This structure type is known for the clathrate hydrate $HPF_6{\cdot}6H_2O^{[26]}$ and is related to inorganic compounds such as $BaPd_2P_4$ and $BaNi_2P_4$ with a reduction in symmetry, $^{[27]}$ as well as "superhydrides" such as $Ca/YH_6^{[28]}$ and $CaYH_{12}.^{[29]}$ The clathrate structure is comprised of a single truncated octahedral $B_{12}C_{12}$ cage with six four-sided faces and eight six-sided faces $(4^66^8)$. All faces are shared between cages so that the single cage type can fully tile three-dimensional space without any voids. All atoms are on special crystallographic Wyckoff positions so that there is only one unique C-B distance and one unique C(B)-Sr distance. The square and hexagonal faces result in a mixture of $90^\circ$ and $120^\circ$ C-B-C bond angles. The incorporation of B within the framework reduces the energetic penalty for the $90^\circ$ bond angles compared with pure carbon. $^{[1]}$

At $0$ GPa, the $LaB_3C_3$ lattice is slightly expanded compared with that of $SrB_3C_3$ ($a=4.934$ Å vs. $4.868$ Å). This expansion was somewhat surprising as the ionic radius of $La^{3+}$ is expected to be smaller than that of $Sr^{2+}$ ($1.36$ Å vs. $1.44$ Å for 12-fold coordination). $^{[30]}$ However, the increased unit cell size for the La clathrate can be understood by B-C bond elongation due to additional charge transferred from guest to host. The single unique B-C bond length expands from $1.72$ Å to $1.74$ Å when Sr is replaced by La. Computational charge density (Figure 3a) confirms the additional charge transfer for $LaB_3C_3$. Bader charge analysis shows that an average of $1.82$ e$^-$ are transferred from La to the B/C host lattice in $LaB_3C_3$, whereas only $1.35$ e$^-$ are transferred for the case of Sr. $^{[1]}$ We note that model calculations for empty $B_3C_3$ lattices with varying electrons also support this trend in bond length with charge transfer. The calculated B-C bond length increases monotonically from $1.64$ Å $1.87$ Å when moving from $[B_3C_3]$ to $[B_3C_3]^{3-}$. For the filled clathrates, the ultimate bond length is the result of more complex interplay between charge transfer and distribution within the polar-covalent bond, and Coulomb interactions with the trapped ion. Compared with the 2D covalent lattice found in $LaB_2C_2$ with B-C bond lengths ranging from $1.54$-$1.62$ Å, $^{[16]}$ the expanded bond length found in $LaB_3C_3$ reflects the change from three to four-fold coordination of the network.

Additional charge transferred from guest to host and the influence of the trivalent guest is also revealed in the electronic band structure of $LaB_3C_3$ (Figure 3b). Unlike $SrB_3C_3$ which is one electron short of a band gap, the additional electron from $La^{3+}$ allows for formal charge balance with $[B_3C_3]^{3-}$, and $LaB_3C_3$ shows an indirect gap of $1.29$ eV in the $\Gamma \to$ L direction. The top of the valence band is composed primarily of hybrid $p$ states of B and C, while the conduction band is comprised primarily of $d$ states from La. $LaB_3C_3$ is thus comparable to B-substituted silicon structures such as $K_7B_7Si_{39}$ and $LiBSi_2$ with electron precise frameworks in which the metals act as electron donors, as in Zintl phases, although it is noted that $LiBSi_2$ was shown to exhibit atypical

contributions from the charge donor to the valence band. $^{[31]}$ The formation of semiconducting $LaB_3C_3$-combined with metallic (possibly superconducting) $SrB_3C_3$-highlights the tunable nature of the Type-VII carbon-boron clathrate framework. As with other group 14 clathrates, the electronic structures, and thus physical properties of carbon-boron clathrates, may be manipulated by utilizing a variety of different guest atoms, and potentially through additional heteroatom doping of the lattice.

In summary, a second Type-VII carbon-boron clathrate was prepared using trivalent lanthanum as the guest species. Unlike metallic $SrB_3C_3$, $LaB_3C_3$ possesses a balanced electron count and is predicted to be a semiconductor with an indirect gap near 1.3 eV. This balanced count is likely responsible for the improved synthetic conditions, i.e., $\approx 20\%$ reduction in synthesis pressure, as well as the slightly increased stiffness and hardness as compared with $SrB_3C_3$. The observation of two $MB_3C_3$ clathrate compounds suggests the possibility for several other Type-VII clathrates with different guest atoms. Based on these results, we speculate that entirely different clathrate structure types may also be possible with different carbon:boron ratios and cage types, including those that possess tetrahedral bond angles and improved stability. Carbon-based clathrates thus represent a promising class of lightweight, high-strength/hardness materials with tunable electronic structures.

A Lanthanum-Filled Carbon–Boron
Clathrate

![](./images/812551418790543361_6.jpg)

High-pressure synthesis reveals LaB₃C₃,
a La-stuffed, carbon–boron clathrate in
the bipartite sodalite structure. Trivalent
La³⁺ allows charge balance with the
[B₃C₃]³⁻ framework, resulting in a semi-
conducting material at improved syn-
thetic conditions. Robust sp³ covalent
bonds produce high strength and hard-
ness, while different guest atoms within
the clathrate cages allow the possibility
for tunable electronic properties.
