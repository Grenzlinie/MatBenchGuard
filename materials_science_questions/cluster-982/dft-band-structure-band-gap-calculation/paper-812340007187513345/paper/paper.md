# The Electronic Structures of the Pyrite-Type Disulphides
($\text{MS}_2$, where $\text{M} = \text{Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn}$) and the Bulk Properties of Pyrite from Local Density Approximation (LDA) Band Structure Calculations

W.M. Temmerman $^1$, P.J. Durham $^1$, and D.J. Vaughan $^2$

$^1$ SERC Daresbury Laboratory, Warrington, WA4 4AD, UK
$^2$ Department of Geology, Manchester University, Manchester M13 9PL, UK

Received July 14, 1992/Revised, accepted April 19, 1993

**Abstract.** A local density approximation (LDA) band structure method, the Linear Muffin-Tin Orbital Atomic Sphere Approximation (LMTO-ASA) method has been used to calculate the electronic structures of the pyrite-type disulphides ($\text{MS}_2$, where $\text{M} = \text{Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn}$). The total density of states has been calculated for 10 eV above and below the Fermi Level, along with the separate contributions from metal and sulphur and shows that the metal d band occurs above the sulphur p bands in $\text{MnS}_2$, $\text{FeS}_2$, $\text{CoS}_2$ and $\text{NiS}_2$, whereas in $\text{CuS}_2$, the d band passes through the sulphur p band and in $\text{ZnS}_2$, it lies below the sulphur p band. Substantial hybridization of the metal d states with the sulphur states occurs. $\text{FeS}_2$ is calculated to be a semiconductor with a direct band gap of 0.64 eV in good agreement with experiment. The calculated local densities of states have been used in turn to calculate X-ray photoelectron spectra and Bremsstrahlung Isochromat spectra for this series of compounds, and these also show reasonable agreement with experimental data. A particular strength of the LMTO-ASA method is the ability to calculate and predict certain bulk properties of solids of interest in mineral physics. This has enabled the first reasonably accurate calculations of the total energy of the valence electrons of the system for pyrite ($\text{FeS}_2$), given as $-345.885$ rydbergs per unit cell, and the equilibrium unit cell volume which is within 3.3% of that determined experimentally. A theoretical pressure vs. volume curve for pyrite was also calculated along with values for the bulk modulus. However, our calculations predict a bulk modulus of 6.75 Mbar which is too high by a factor of 4.6 due to the simplifying assumption of a uniform scaling of interatomic distances on compression.

## Introduction

Pyrite ($\text{FeS}_2$) is the most abundant and important of the metal sulphide minerals (Vaughan and Craig 1978; Craig and Vaughan 1981). The properties of pyrite are of importance to studies of ore formation, mineral processing and environmental mineralogy (pyrite being the dominant source of acid waters arising from mine wastes). The pyrite structure, based on the halite structure, but with disulphide units occupying the sites at the centre of the cube unit cell and the mid-points of the cube edges, is common to a complete series of first row transition metal disulphides extending from Mn to Zn. This family of materials exhibits a rich diversity of physical properties: $\text{MnS}_2$ is an antiferromagnetic semiconductor, $\text{FeS}_2$ a van Vleck paramagnetic semiconductor, $\text{CoS}_2$ a ferromagnetic metal, $\text{NiS}_2$ a paramagnetic Mott-Hubbard insulator, $\text{CuS}_2$ a (metallic) superconductor, and $\text{ZnS}_2$ a diamagnetic insulator. Hence, they have attracted the interest of solid state physicists as well as mineralogists, and offer a challenging group of materials for the study of electronic structure using both experimental methods and quantum mechanical calculations.

Previous experimental and theoretical studies of the electronic structures of the pyrite-type disulphides have been reviewed by Tossell et al. (1981), Vaughan and Tossell (1983), Sato (1985), and Tossell and Vaughan (1992). Quantum mechanical calculations have included molecular orbital cluster calculations (Tossell et al. 1981; Harris 1982; Li et al. 1974; Braga et al. 1988) and band structure calculations (Bullett 1982; Folkerts et al. 1987; Lauer et al. 1984).

The present paper represents the application of a local density approximation (LDA) band structure method to these materials. Although a relatively high level method has been employed in this work (the LMTO-ASA method, see below), it is nevertheless relatively economical in terms of computer time. Like all quantum mechanical calculational methods, the LMTO-ASA method has particular strengths and weaknesses. It generally provides a good description of such groundstate properties as lattice constants, bulk moduli and crystal and magnetic structures. As we demonstrate below, it also yields acceptable data for comparison with experimental X-ray photoelectron (XPS) and inverse photoemission (or Bremsstrahlung Isochromat, BIS) spectra.

In the following text, we briefly describe the method of calculation, discuss the electronic structure and bond- ing properties of the members of this series, and compare calculated and experimental XPS and BIS data before considering the calculated electronic structures in rela- tion to properties of mineralogical interest. The conclud- ing section also points to directions for future work.

## Calculation Method

In the LMTO-ASA (Linear Muffin-Tin Orbital Atomic Sphere Approximation) band structure method (Ander- sen 1975, 1981, 1984; Skriver 1984; Andersen et al. 1985), the system being studied is divided up into space-filling spheres centered around the atomic positions. This is not an exact way of dividing up space, since we will have regions where the spheres overlap and regions not covered by spheres at all. Hence the name Atomic Sphere Approximation. With each of the spheres we associate basis functions for the construction of the electronic wa- vefunction of the system. These basis functions are cen- tered on the atomic sites and are the solutions of the radial Schrödinger equation in the sphere. For $FeS_{2}$ for example, we chose the s, p, d radial functions on both the Fe and S spheres. In this case there are for $FeS_{2}$,with a unit cell of 4 formula units, $4 \times(1$ Fes, 3 Fep, 5 Fed $)$ and $2 \times 4 \times(1 Ss, 3 Sp, 5 Sd)$, or 108 basis functions. The Atomic Sphere Approximation can be improved by in- troducing "empty spheres" which are centred on high symmetry positions in the interstitial region of the solid. In this case, the spheres centred around the atomic posi- tions can be reduced in size and, therefore, the sphere overlap decreased. This should be a better approxima- tion. With each "empty sphere" introduced, the basisset size is increased by either one (s) or four (s and 3p) basis functions. We, therefore, have to reach a comprom- ise between accuracy and numerical effort. In the present application, we introduced an extra 2 empty spheres per formula unit and used s and p basis functions for these spheres. This increases the basis set size to 140. The sphere radii chosen for the $FeS_{2}$ calculations are, respec tively 2.662, 2.662 and 1.52298 atomic units for Fe, S and the empty sphere. This gave rise to an electronic charge of, respectively 26.197, 15.602 and 0.3 electrons in the Fe, S and empty sphere.

Within the Atomic Sphere Approximation, the real space integrals occurring in the matrix elements of the Hamiltonian and overlap matrix become trivial. The sec- ond advantage is that in the construction of the eigen- value problem, the only information about the crystal potential is the logarithmic derivatives, i.e. the solution of the radial Schrödinger equation in a single atomic sphere. The crystal structure enters through the "struc- ture constants" which are independent of the crystal po- tential. And finally, because of the choice of the basis functions, results such as the density of states, the XPS spectrum, the total energy, and the electronic pressure can be decomposed into site and angular momentum contributions.

From inspection of these figures, it can be seen that the metal d band occurs above the sulphur p bands in $MnS_{2}$, $FeS_{2}$, $CoS_{2}$ and $NiS_{2}$, whereas in $CuS_{2}$ the d band passes through the sulphur p band and in $ZnS_{2}$ the d band lies below the sulphur p band. It is also noticeable that a substantial hybridization of the metal states with the chalcogen states occurs (where the metal states occur, there we also find chalcogen states). More- over, the structure seen in the density of states on one site is reflected on to the other site. In all of these calcula- tions, the metal and the sulphur electronic states are partially filled. In agreement with experiments, $FeS_{2}$ is calculated to be a semiconductor, and the direct band gap is calculated as 0.64 eV in good agreement with ex- periment. We obtain this result because band theory cal-

culations fill all the $t_{2g}$ states with electrons and leaves the $e_{g}$ states unoccupied. However, for all of the other compounds, we obtain a metallic ground state from the calculations, even though $MnS_{2}$, $NiS_{2}$ and $ZnS_{2}$ are semiconductors. Allowance of a magnetic ground state could induce a band gap in $MnS_{2}$ and $NiS_{2}$, whereas the semiconducting properties of $ZnS_{2}$ may be very sen sitive to the choice of the $u$ (structural) parameter. Since, in this work we are primarily concerned with the elec- tronic structure of $FeS_{2}$ and trends in the electronic structure variations through the $MnS_{2}-ZnS_{2}$ series, we have not performed these more detailed calculations and leave them for a future study.

It is interesting to compare the results obtained in this work with previous calculations using both molecu- lar orbital cluster and band structure methods. Molecu- lar orbital (MO) calculations have centred on the appli- cation of the multiple scattering (MS) or scattered wave $X_{\alpha}$ method $(MS-X_{\alpha})$ to clusters generally comprised of the appropriate metal and the six immediately coordin- ated sulphur atoms $(MS_{6}^{10-})$. The MO eigenvalues calcu lated in this way for the series extending from $FeS_{2}$ toZnS, are shown in Fig.7 (after Tossell et al.1981; Vaughan and Tossell 1983). The ordering of energy levels(comparable to that of bands in the present work) is comparable for $FeS_{2}, NiS_{2}$ and $ZnS_{2}$ between the two sets of calculations, and substantial hybridization of the metal and sulphur states is also indicated when the com- positions of individual molecular orbitals are examined. In the cluster calculations, and in the present calculations and previous band structure calculations, there is a gen- eralized trend towards decreasing stabilization of the metal-sulphur bonding orbitals across the series from $FeS_{2}$ to $ZnS_{2}$.

Folkerts et al. (1987) obtained a very similar density of states for $FeS_{2}$ to ours and their value for the band gap was 0.9 eV. Bullett (1982) studied the same series, with the exception of $MnS_{2}$, and found the same trend in the relative positions of the Sp and transition metal d levels on moving through the series. However, with respect to the trend in the unoccupied antibonding Sp bands, differences occur. In $FeS_{2}$, these states occur in the energy range of 1 to $3 eV$ above $E_{F}$ and strongly hybridize with the metal d band. This $p-d$ hybridization of the S antibonding bands decreases as the metal d bands move to higher binding energies in going from Fe to Zn, and the S antibonding bands sharpen up con- siderably from $NiS_{2}$ onwards. In Bullett's (1982) calcula tion, this antibonding band is split off, from $CoS_{2}$ on wards, from the valence bands and the transition metal eg bands. In our calculation this antibonding band is never split off from the valence band. Therefore we find, in contrast to Bullett (1982), $ZnS_{2}$ not to be a semicon ductor. However, this band is on the verge of splitting off and the Fermi energy lies at a minimum in the density of states.

The metal d and sulphur p densities of states are shown for the complete $MnS_2-ZnS_2$ series in Figs. 8 and 9, respectively. The major trend observed in moving across the series is the movement of the metal d band to higher binding energies. Thus, for $MnS_2$ the Mn d bands fall close to the Fermi energy and within the sulphur p states so that considerable hybridisation occurs between them. By the end of the series, the Zn d band in $ZnS_2$ has dropped down to the bottom of the sulphur p band. Hence, the peak just above the Fermi level in the BIS calculated spectral data has considerable metal d character in $MnS_2$, but has pure sulphur p character in $ZnS_2$.

These calculations show reasonable agreement with the experimental data within 5-10 eV of the Fermi energy (cf. data for $FeS_2$ and $NiS_2$ of Folkerts et al. 1987; shown here in Fig. 11). Outside this range, the linearization inherent in the LMTO method introduces inaccuracies. Overall, comparison of the calculated and experimental XPS/BIS data show that this LDA based band model yields a reasonably accurate picture of the densities of states in these pyrite-type disulphide systems.

## Bulk Properties of Pyrite ($FeS_2$)
As noted above, a particular strength of the LMTO-ASA method is the ability to calculate and to predict the bulk properties of solids by the calculation of the total energy as a function of volume. The total energy in the local density approximation can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
E= & {\left[\sum_{k, n}^{\text {occ. }} \varepsilon_{k, n}-\int_{s} v_{s}(r) n(r) d r\right] } \\
& +\int_{s}\left(\frac{1}{2} V_{H}(r)-\frac{2 Z}{r}+\varepsilon_{x c}(n(r))\right)^{n(r)} d r.
\end{aligned}
$$

Where $\varepsilon_{k, n}$ are the energy eigenvalues of the one-electron Hamiltonian with $n$ the band index and $k$ the point in the Brillouin zone, the sum extending over all the occupied states and

$$
V_{s}(r)=V_{H}(r)-\frac{2 Z}{r}+V_{x c}(n(r)).
$$

Where $V_{H}(r)$ is the Hartree potential given by

$$
V_{H}(r)=\int_{s} \frac{2 n\left(r^{\prime}\right)}{\left|r-r^{\prime}\right|} d r^{\prime}
$$

with $n(r)$ the electron charge density and the integral performed over the atomic sphere S. The other terms in (2) is $-\frac{2 Z}{r}$ which is the Coulomb attraction of the nucleus with charge $Z$, and $V_{x c}$ which is the exchange and correlation potential which is a functional of the charge density. The term in square brackets in (1) is the kinetic energy

$$
\int \psi_{k, n}^{*}(r)\left(-\nabla^{2}\right) \psi_{k, n}(r) d r
$$

contribution to the total energy, since we obtain the energy eigenvalues in density functional theory by solving the following one-particle Schrödinger equation

$$
\left(-\nabla^{2}+V_{s}(r)\right) \psi_{k, n}(r)=\varepsilon_{k, n} \psi_{k, n}(r).
$$

The subsequent terms in the total energy expression (1) are, respectively the electron-electron repulsion, the electrostatic interaction with the nucleus, and the exchange and correlation. Expression (1) highlights the central role of the sum of the one-electron energies in the total energy expression. The total energy expression can easily be generalized to many atoms per unit cell. In this case, the fact that all the integrals in the total energy expression (1) are over atomic spheres, leads to a decomposition of the total energy into atomic species. Therefore, for $FeS_2$ the total energy can be decomposed into an Fe and an S contribution.

By fitting a Murnaghan equation of state through a set of total energies evaluated at about 7 lattice constants, we obtain the ground state properties such as the equilibrium volume, the $p-V$ curve, the bulk modulus and the pressure derivative of the bulk modulus. Deeper physical insight from the $p-V$ curves can be obtained by assuming that the potential is frozen upon a virtual displacement of the atoms. This leads to an electronic pressure formula in terms of only the one- electron energies and the results can also be decomposed into the angular momentum components.

## Conclusions and Future Work

Although the method employed in this work, the LMTO-ASA method, is not the most sophisticated of band structure methods, it is economical in terms of computer time and yields accurate results for certain pa- rameters for such relatively complex systems as the tran- sition metal sulphides. It offers the capability to look at these, and more complex systems involving heavy atoms and large unit cells. Although the method does not yield accurate information on local structural pa- rameters (bond lengths, bond angles etc.) it does give good information on the local densities of states and, hence, can aid in the prediction and interpretation of various types of electronic spectra. It also gives relatively accurate predictions of total energy and of bulk proper- ties that depend on the variation in total energy as a function of another variable (such as $P / V$ relations).

We plan to extend the use of the LMTO-ASA method to study related sulphide systems and their bulk proper- ties and to look at surface properties and sorption on sulphide surfaces by performing calculations on suitable "slabs" of the relevant structures.
