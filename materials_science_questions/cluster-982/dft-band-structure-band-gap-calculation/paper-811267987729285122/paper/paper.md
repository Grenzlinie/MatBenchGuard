# Electronic Structure and Magnetic Interactions in Ti-Doped and Ti-V₀-Co-Doped β-Ga₂O₃ from First-Principles Calculations

Huiyu Yan¹ · Yanrui Guo¹ · Qinggong Song¹ · Yifei Chen¹ · Yihua Shi¹

Received: 25 February 2016 / Accepted: 30 May 2016 / Published online: 7 June 2016
© Springer Science+Business Media New York 2016

## Abstract
Based on first-principles calculation method, the electronic structure and magnetic interactions of Ti-doped β-Ga₂O₃ and Ti-V₀-co-doped in β-Ga₂O₃ have been inves- tigated. The calculated results indicate that Ti atom tends to substitute the Ga atom at the octahedral coordinated site and the doped system prefers spin-polarized state. The magnetic moment of Ti-doped β-Ga₂O₃ is 0.546 $\mu_B$ per cell, which mainly arises from Ti 0.48 $\mu_B$. The origin of ferromag- netism in Ti-doped β-Ga₂O₃ can be explained by a double- exchange mechanism. When a $V_O$ defect is introduced into Ti-doped β-Ga₂O₃, the magnetic moment increases and the ferromagnetic coupling between Ti atoms is strengthened. The influence of $V_O$ defect on ferromagnetic properties of Ti-doped β-Ga₂O₃ comes from the enhancement of hybridization between Ti-3d and O-2p states.

Diluted magnetic semiconductors · First-principles · Electronic structure · Ferromagnetic coupling

## 1 Introduction
Recently, diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMSs) have elicited much attention due to their potentiality as a new functional material, which provide a way of incorporating the spin degree of freedom into semiconductor devices [1, 2]. The primary focus is searching DMSs with the Curie temperature ($T_C$) above room temperature (RT). To meet this requirement of DMSs, many efforts have been made. In the past decades, many DMSs with $3d$ transition metal (TM) dopants have been reported, such as Cr-doped ZnTe and GaN [3, 4] and TM-doped ZnO, TiO₂, and SnO₂ [5–10]. However, the origin of the RT ferromagnetism is still under debates for $3d$ TM-doped DMSs. Some researchers suggest that RT ferromagnetism may come from intrinsic magnetic behaviors of DMSs, while others argue that the RT ferro- magnetism may come from precipitation of magnetic clus- ters or from the secondary magnetic phases [11]. To avoid the problem of magnetic precipitate, many DMSs doped with the intrinsic nonmagnetic elements have been reported in theories and experiments, such as intrinsic nonmagnetic elementdoped ZnO and ZnS [12–18]. However, this type of DMS also has its disadvantage. For example, among the reported literatures about diluted ferromagnetic semicon- ductors, most of them are $p$-type doping. Meanwhile, it is hard to fabricate $p$-type semiconductors in experiments, because most semiconductors exhibit intrinsic $n$-type prop- erties. Therefore, it is remarkably practically interesting to realize $n$-type DMS with the intrinsic nonmagnetic dopants.

On the other hand, β-Ga₂O₃ is a wide-band gap semi- conductor, has excellent physical and chemical stability, and has important applications in optoelectronic devices, solar cells, and so on [19–23]. However, the DMS about β-Ga₂O₃ doped with nonmagnetic elements is seldom reported. In this paper, we have studied Ti-doped β-Ga₂O₃ using a first-principles calculation method. The results show that Ti-doped β-Ga₂O₃ is a promising $n$-type DMS with RT

## 2 Details of Calculations

$\beta-\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$ crystal takes a monoclinic, anion close-packed structure, with lattice parameters of $a = 1.223$ nm, $b = 0.304$ nm, and $c = 0.580$ nm [24]. The space group of the structure is C2/m. To construct the doped systems, a crystal containing 40 atoms was modeled and is shown in Fig. 1. In this paper, we select Ga(A) and Ga(B) notation referred as Ga atoms in octahedral and tetrahedral sites, respectively. There are three different O sites, denoted as O(1), O(2), and O(3). Each O(1) and O(3) has three nearest neighbors of Ga, whereas O(2) and Ga compose a tetrahedral structure.

In our calculations, the interaction between the ionic cores and the electron was described by ultrasoft pseudopotential [25]. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) scheme of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was selected to describe the exchange correlation interactions [26]. The electronic states of $2s^22p^4$, $3d^{10}4s^24p^1$, and $3d^24s^2$ are considered as the valence states for O, Ga and Ti, respectively. Basic parameters for calculations were selected as follows: the kinetic energy cutoff was 440 eV and the SCF tolerance was $0.5 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/atom. The maximum displacement was $0.5 \times 10^{-3}$ Å, and the force per atom was lower than 0.01 eV/Å. In addition, the maximum stress was 0.02 GPa. The special $k$ points of the $3 \times 4 \times 4$ Monkhorst-Pack was summed in all Brillouin zones in reciprocal space. All the calculations were completed by the CASTEP package in Materials Studio (MS) software [27].

### 3.2 Electronic Properties

The calculated band gap of intrinsic $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ is 1.96 eV, which is lower than the experimental result. This result is attributed to the underestimation of the interaction among electrons in the DFT method. Figures 2 and 3 show the spinresolved band structure of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ and the density of states (DOS), respectively. One can see that the impurity bands locate at the bottom of conductivity band, indicating that Ti atoms act as the $n$-type dopants. From Fig. 3, one can see that spin splitting is induced by a Ti dopant. The spin splitting of DOS results in a magnetic moment of $0.55\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ per supercell, which mainly arises from the Ti dopant ($0.48\ \mu_{\text{B}}$). Although the magnetic moment of the doped system is not integral, the spin polarity is nearly 100 %, as displayed in Fig. 3.

### 3.3 Ferromagnetic Properties

The spin-polarized state preference of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ is necessary but not sufficient for ferromagnetic (FM) semiconductors. To confirm the FM properties of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃, the coupling interactions between Ti atoms in Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ should be investigated. Therefore, we substituted two Ga atoms with Ti atoms in the supercell mentioned. There are 11 possible independent configurations for Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ with two Ti dopants, classified as categories (i), (ii), and (iii) by dopant occupying sites, as shown in Fig. 1 and Table 3. For category (i), one Ti atom residing in position A0 remains fixed, whereas other positions are designated (from A1 to A4) at different sites. For category (ii), one Ti atom substituting the B0 site is fixed, whereas another Ti atom substitutes a Ga atom at another B site. For category (iii) one Ti atom substitutes the A0 site, whereas another substitutes one Ga atom at the B site. The relative energies of FM and antiferromagnetic (AFM) states for each case are listed in Table 3. The energy difference between FM and AFM orderings, $\Delta E\ =\ E_{\text{AFM}}\ -\ E_{\text{FM}}$, which can be used as an indicator of ferromagnetic stability, is also listed. It can be found that most configurations prefer FM state. In all the configurations of two Ti atoms that substitute either tetrahedral or octahedral sites, the A0-A2 configuration has the lowest energy and favors the ferromagnetic ordering, in which the magnetic moment is $1.5\ \mu_{\text{B}}$. The energy difference between ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic states is 85.3 meV. Moreover, from Table 3, it can be found that the energy difference between the nearest configuration A0-A2 and the farther configuration A0-A4 is less than 0.2 eV, indicating the clustering effect is not obvious in Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃

In this work, the impurity bands consisting of Ti-$3d$ states locate in the band gap and the Fermi level crosses the impurity bands, which is in accordance with the description of the doubleexchange model. To study the influence of $\text{V}_\text{O}$ defects on the ferromagnetic properties of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃, the properties of the matrix with a $\text{V}_\text{O}$ defect are also studied. From Table 1, one can see that the $\text{V}_\text{O}$ defect with any configuration prefers a nonspin state, indicating that the ferromagnetic properties in $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ cannot be induced by the $\text{V}_\text{O}$ defect solely. When one $\text{V}_\text{O}$ is introduced into Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃, the system still favors spin-polarized states while the magnetic moments (MMs) increase, which is similar to Co- and Ni-doped SnO₂ systems [33–35]. The calculated MMs of the doped system increase to 1.0, 0.66, and 0.97 $\mu_\text{B}$ for $\text{V}_{\text{O(1)}}$, $\text{V}_{\text{O(2)}}$, and $\text{V}_{\text{O(3)}}$, respectively. Correspondingly, the local magnetic moment of Ti atom increased to 0.74, 0.56, and 0.74 $\mu_\text{B}$, respectively. Considering $\text{V}_{\text{O(3)}}$ is the nearest neighbor to A0–A2 and Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ favors the A0–A2 configuration, we examine the influence of $\text{V}_{\text{O(3)}}$ on the ferromagnetic coupling between Ti dopants for the A0–A2 configuration, which is listed in Table 3. One can find that $\text{V}_\text{O}$ defect can enhance the value of $\Delta E$ up to 274 meV. This means $\text{V}_\text{O}$ can strengthen the ferromagnetic coupling between Ti and Ti atoms significantly and thus enhance the Curie temperature of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃. The charge distributions of configurations A0–A2 and A0–A2–$\text{V}_{\text{O(3)}}$ are also listed in Table 2, from which we can find $\text{V}_{\text{O(3)}}$ can induce the decrease of valence of Ti and its nearest O(2) atom. From Table 2, we can also find that the valence of cations around defects decreases significantly with the increasing of density of defects, which is attributed to the large density of states of Ti and Ga atoms near the Fermi level. The influence of defects on the valence of oxygen is small. This comes from oxygen that takes a small density of states near the Fermi level. It suggests that the interaction among unbonded electrons caused by oxygen vacancy and Ti dopants is responsible for the enhancement of ferromagnetic coupling.

## 4 Conclusions

In summary, the electronic and magnetic properties of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ with and without $V_{O}$ defects are studied by a first-principles calculation method. The results show that Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ favors ferromagnetic states. The origin of ferromagnetism of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ can be explained by a double-exchange mechanism. When $V_{O}$ defect is introduced into Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃, the magnetic moment increases to $1.0\ \mu_{B}$ and the ferromagnetic coupling between Ti atoms is strengthened. The influence of $V_{O}$ defect on ferromagnetic properties of Ti-doped $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ originates from the enhancement of hybridization between Ti-3d and O-2p states.
