# Pressure effect on phonon modes in gallium nitride:
a molecular dynamics study

M.R. Aouas$^{\mathrm{a}}$, W. Sekkal$^{\mathrm{b}}$, A. Zaoui$^{\mathrm{b},*}$

$^{\mathrm{a}}$Computational Materials Science Laboratory, University of Sidi-Bel-Abbes, Sidi-Bel-Abbes 22000, Algeria
$^{\mathrm{b}}$Condensed Matter Group, International Centre for Theoretical Physics, Strada Costiera 11-34014, Trieste, Italy

Received 1 March 2001; received in revised form 30 July 2001; accepted 16 August 2001 by P. Burlet

## Abstract
An atomistic simulation based on the Buckingham potential is investigated in order to describe the phonon behaviour in the wurtzite, zinc-blende, rocksalt and NiAs structures in gallium nitride. The obtained results show the presence of soft modes in the wurtzite structure, with a negative pressure coefficient of the $E_{2}$ (low) mode, in good agreement with experimental results. Our calculations are extended to predict the dynamical properties of the zinc-blende, rocksalt and NiAs phases such as the evaluation of the pressure coefficient and the Gruneisen parameter for each mode. © 2001 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

## 1. Introduction
Among the III–V nitrides, gallium nitride (GaN) is tech- nologically the most interesting compound and has attracted extensively because of its importance in science and tech- nology for optoelectronic applications in the short-wave length range as well as for high-temperature, high-power, and high-frequency electronic devices. In particular, GaN is a basic material for the realization of light-emitting diodes [1], lasers in the blue and ultraviolet range of the spectrum [2,3], optical-pumping structures [4], photodetectors [5] and hetero structure field-effect [6].

Under ambient conditions, the stable configuration of GaN is the wurtzite structure with mainly covalent, partly ionic tetrahedral bonding and a direct electronic gap of 3.5 eV at the $\Gamma$ point [2]. X-ray-absorption fine-structure measurements [7] evidenced a phase transition at 50 GPa. Using ab-initio calculations, Munoz et al. [8] show that the crystal structure of this phase is rocksalt or NiAs. In contrast, the zinc-blende structure is metastable and may be stabilized only by heteroepitaxial growth on substrates reflecting topological compatibility. The understanding of the electronic, optical, mechanical and vibrational proper- ties is based on a variety of experimental and theoretical studies. The knowledge of lattice dynamics and phonon characteristics of GaN helps to understand the mechan- ism of the phonon emission process used in design of an efficient photonic material. Several attempts have been made to calculate the phonon dispersion curves for the hexagonal wurtzite structure. These calculations have used either a mixed basis method [9], a linear muffin- tin orbital method [10,11], or a density functional theory with a linear-response approach [12]. To the best of our knowledge, molecular dynamics calculations of the phonon dispersion relation of GaN have not yet been performed. We note that recently [13], an empirical pair potential (Buckingham model) was derived for GaN to reproduce the structural properties of the wurtzite and zinc-blende structures. This model has also been used for surface relaxation and to study defects in GaN.

In the present paper, a dynamical study is presented for GaN in different structures (wurtzite, zinc-blende, rocksalt, and NiAs). Details about the mathematical formulation of the Buckingham potential and the corresponding parameters for GaN are given in Ref. [13]. The cut-off radius taken for the short range potential is about $10\ \mathring{A}$. All of the calcula- tions were performed using the program GULP [14].

## 2. Results

Before calculating the dynamical properties in GaN, molecular dynamics calculations based on the Buckingham potential have been performed to study the stability of the rocksalt and the NiAs phases and to evaluate their structural and elastic properties. In Fig. 1, we plot the variation of the total energy versus the relative volume for the rocksalt and NiAs structures all with that of the wurtzite and the zinc- blende phases. We note that the most stable structure obtained by this model is the wurtzite one [13]. The rocksalt and the NiAs structures are the high pressure phases and we notice that the rocksalt structure is more stable than that of NiAs. The obtained structural and elastic quantities are presented in Table 1. In comparison to other theoretical calculations, we found that the lattice parameter is well reproduced with an accuracy about 6% for both rocksalt and NiAs structures. For the bulk modulus, it is obtained within 15% of the experimental value for the rocksalt phase [8]. For the NiAs structure, no experimental or theoretical data are available, to our knowledge.

In order to evaluate the transition pressure from the wurtzite to the NiAs structure, we plot in Fig. 2 the enthalpy versus pressure for the two structures all with that of the rocksalt one. Our results predict that the transition is occurring at $P_1=58$ GPa for NiAs phase. Using the same potential, Zapol et al. [13] found that the wurzite rocksalt transition occurs at about $P_2=52$ GPa in a good agreement with other measurements [18,19]. The overall good agreement between the calculated and the experimental data and other theoretical calculations indicate the reliability of the interatomic potential set and also its transferability to describe other properties of GaN at ambient and also under pressure.

### 2.1. Phonon in GaN

The phonon frequencies are obtained from the second derivative matrix. For the phonon density of states, GULP uses a standard scheme developed by Monkhorst and Pack [20] for performing the integrations based on a regular grid whose density is controlled by a shrinking factor along each cell vector. In this section, we present first the calculated data for the $\Gamma$ phonon modes for GaN in the wurtzite, zincblende, rock-salt, and NiAs structures at 0 GPa. We start by the wurtzite structure and we plot in Fig. 3 the phonon dispersion curves. In this structure there are four atoms per unit cell, resulting in three acoustic modes and nine optical modes predicted at the zone center $\Gamma$ by the group theory: two $A_1$, two $E_1$, two $E_2$ and two $B_1$ modes. The $A_1$ and $B_1$ modes are Raman and infrared active, and the two $E_2$ modes are Raman active. The $B_2$ modes are silent. The lowest three optical branches are found to be in the acoustic range, the other six optical branches are well separated from the acoustic as well as lower-lying optical branches. A comparison of the present calculations with available experimental results at $\Gamma$ point is made in Table 2. We found that our results are in good agreement with experimental measurements [21-24] and with other theoretical calculations [25].

In Fig. 4, we plot the phonon dispersion curves for the zinc-blende structure. In comparison to other theoretical results using first principle calculations [12], our plot agree qualitatively and quantitatively well. As we see, in this cubic structure, only two optical modes exist: LO and TO modes. The obtained frequencies listed in Table 3 are 567 and $796 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, respectively. Those measured by Siegle et al. [24] are equal to 555 and $740 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, respectively. Gorczyca et al. [27] found a value of $551 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ for the TO mode while Miwa et al. [9] give a value of $558 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. Our results agree well with experimental results with an accuracy of $2-7 \%$.

The phonon dispersion curves for the rocksalt and NiAs structures are similar to that of the zinc-blende structure. It appears that only the optical modes are present for both structures. The corresponding frequencies are given in Table 3. We note that to the best of our knowledge, there are no experimental measurements or other theoretical calculations to compare our results, so they are purely predictive.

The second part in this section deals with the pressure effects on the dynamical properties and especially we want to see the behaviour of each mode for each structure under pressure. Generally, the application of hydrostatic pressure increases the frequency of zone centre phonons because of the increasing stiffening of the interatomic interactions. For the wurtzite structure, we calculate the phonon dispersion curves at a pressure ranging from 0 GPa until the phase transition pressure to the rocksalt structure which is found with this model to be around 52 GPa. In Fig. 5 we display the pressure dependence of different phonon modes for the wurtzite structure. An interesting property appears which is related to the $E_2$ (low) modes: it has a negative pressure coefficient (see Table 4), which is called 'soft mode'. From this plot, we can extract other information about the pressure coefficient and the value of the Gruneisen parameter for each mode. The mode Gruneisen parameter is calculated using the following relation:

$$
\gamma=\frac{B_{0} \omega}{\mathrm{d} \omega \mathrm{d} P}
$$

The behaviour of different modes under pressure for the zinc-blende, rocksalt, and NiAs structures is shown in Fig. 6. It appears that all modes increase with pressure and consequently, the corresponding pressure coefficient and the Gruneisen parameter are positive. The obtained values are given in Table 5. ## 3. Conclusion

The results obtained by using the Buckingham potential parameters for the wurtzite, zinc-blende, rock-salt, and NiAs structures of GaN indicate that this model is able to calculate successfully the dynamical properties of GaN under pressure. For the wurtzite structure, our results show the presence of soft mode and so the presence of a negative pressure coefficient. The obtained values for the Gruneisen parameter at each mode are in good agreement with experimental data. Our calculations for the zinc-blende, rocksalt, and NiAs structures predict the presence of only two optical modes (TO and LO) and a rather positive pressure coefficient.
