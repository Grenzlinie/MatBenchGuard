# Calculation of the Generalized Grüneisen Parameters for Acoustic Waves in Uniaxial Crystals from the Third Order Elastic Constants

By
R. RAMJI RAO and R. SRINIVASAN

The generalized Grüneisen parameters involved in the theory of thermal expansion of uniaxial crystals are defined. These GP's for the acoustic waves determine the behaviour of the anisotropic expansion at low temperatures. The calculation of these GP's from third order elastic constant data in uniaxial crystals is described. As an example, the third order elastic constants of a h.c.p. lattice with nearest neighbour central interaction are worked out and the GP's of acoustic waves propagating in different directions in the crystal are calculated for the case where this interaction is represented by a (6,12) Lennard-Jones potential.

Die verallgemeinerten Grüneisenparameter in der Theorie der thermischen Ausdeh- nung einachsiger Kristalle werden definiert. Diese GP bestimmen für akustische Wellen das Verhalten der anisotropen Ausdehnung bei tiefen Temperaturen. Die Berechnung dieser GP aus den elastischen Konstanten dritter Ordnung in einachsigen Kristallen wird beschrieben. Als Beispiel werden die elastischen Konstanten dritter Ordnung für ein hexa- gonal dicht gepacktes Gitter mit Zentralwechselwirkung mit den nächsten Nachbarn ausgerechnet und die GP akustischer Wellen mit verschiedener Ausbreitungsrichtung im Kristall für den Fall berechnet, daß diese Wechselwirkung durch ein (6,12)-Lennard-Jones- Potential dargestellt werden kann.

## 1. Introduction

The linear expansion coefficients of a number of uniaxial crystals have been measured down to very low temperatures by several workers [1 to 4]. The linear expansion coefficients parallel and perpendicular to the unique axis are given by

$$
V \alpha_{\perp}=\left[\left(s_{11}+s_{12}\right) \sum_{j} \gamma_{j}^{\prime} C_{v j}+s_{13} \sum_{j} \gamma_{j}^{\prime \prime} C_{v j}\right], \tag{1.1a}
$$

$$
V \alpha_{\|}=\left[2 s_{13} \sum_{j} \gamma_{j}^{\prime} C_{v j}+s_{33} \sum_{j} \gamma_{j}^{\prime \prime} C_{v j}\right]. \tag{1.1b}
$$

Here $s_{i j}$ are the elastic compliance coefficients, $C_{v j}$ is the specific heat of the lattice due to the $j$-th normal mode of vibration of the crystal lattice having a frequency $\omega_{j}$. $\gamma_{j}^{\prime}$ and $\gamma_{j}^{\prime \prime}$ are generalized Grüneisen parameters (GP) defined by

$$
\gamma_{j}^{\prime}=-\frac{\partial \log \omega_{j}}{\partial \varepsilon^{\prime}}, \tag{1.2a}
$$

$$
\gamma_{j}^{\prime \prime}=-\frac{\partial \log \omega_{j}}{\partial \zeta}. \tag{1.2b}
$$

$\varepsilon^{\prime}$ is a uniform areal strain perpendicular to the unique axis and $\zeta$ is a longi tudinal strain parallel to the unique axis.

The thermal expansion of a uniaxial crystal can be discussed in terms of the temperature dependence of the effective GP's defined by

$$
\overline{\gamma}^{\prime}=\sum_{j} \gamma_{j}^{\prime} C_{v j} / \sum_{j} C_{v j}, \tag{1.3a}
$$

$$
\overline{\gamma}^{\prime \prime}=\sum_{j} \gamma_{j}^{\prime \prime} C_{v j} / \sum_{j} C_{v j}, \tag{1.3b}
$$

just as Barron [5] has done for the case of cubic crystals. At very high temperatures $\overline{\gamma}^{\prime}$ and $\overline{\gamma}^{\prime \prime}$ attain limits denoted by $\overline{\gamma}_{\infty}^{\prime}$ and $\overline{\gamma}_{\infty}^{\prime \prime}$. As the absolute zero of temperature is approached the effective GP's are determined by the GP's for long wave acoustic modes and they can be calculated from the third order elastic constants of the crystal. In the case of the cubic crystals such a calculation was first performed by Sheard [6]. At present the third order elastic (TOE) constants are known only for quartz among the uniaxial crystals. It is hoped that the TOE constants for hexagonal and tetragonal materials will soon become available.

In this paper we shall describe how the generalized GP's can be obtained for the elastic waves propagating in any direction in a uniaxial crystal from the TOE constants. In hexagonal materials, the wave velocity and the GP's depend only on the angle which the wave vector makes with the unique axis, and these materials provide the simplest cases among the uniaxial crystals to illustrate the method of calculation of GP's. As no TOE data are available for any hexagonal material, we have worked out the TOE constants for a h.c.p. lattice with nearest neighbour central interaction of the Lennard-Jones type and used these TOE constants to evaluate the GP's for the acoustic modes propagating in different directions.

## 2. Calculation of the GP's of Acoustic Modes of Uniaxial Crystals from TOE Constants

Thurston and Brugger [7] give the following equation for elastic wave propagation in a crystal under homogeneous strain:

$$
\varrho_{0} W^{2} u_{j}=\sum_{k p m} A_{j k, p m}^{\mathrm{S}} N_{p} N_{m} u_{k}. \tag{2.1}
$$

Here $\varrho_{0}$ is the density of the crystal in the *undeformed state*, $W$ is the natural wave velocity in a direction having direction cosines $N_{p}$ in the *undeformed state*, $W^{2}=\omega^{2} / Y^{2}$ where $\omega$ is the circular frequency of the wave and $Y$ is the magnitude of the wave vector in the *undeformed state*.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{j k, p m}^{\mathrm{S}}=C_{p j, m k} & +\sum_{r s}\left(C_{p j, m k, r s}+C_{p m, r s} \delta_{j k}\right) \eta_{r s}+ \\
& +\sum_{q} C_{p q, m k} \varepsilon_{j q}+\sum_{q} C_{p j, m q} \varepsilon_{k q}. \tag{2.2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The $C_{p j, m k}$ are the adiabatic second order elastic constants in the undeformed state determined by dynamic wave velocity measurements; $C_{p j, m k, r s}$ are the third order elastic moduli in Brugger's [8] notation determined by measuring changes in wave velocity under isothermal strain. If $X_{i}$ are the coordinates of a material particle in the unstrained state and $x_{i}$ the coordinates of the same

particle after homogeneous deformation then
$$
\frac{\partial x_{i}}{\partial X_{j}}=\delta_{i j}+\varepsilon_{i j}
\tag{2.3}
$$
referred to the same Cartesian system of axes.

The Lagrangian strain parameters $\eta_{i j}$ are defined by
$$
\eta_{i j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\varepsilon_{i j}+\varepsilon_{j i}+\sum_{k} \varepsilon_{k i} \varepsilon_{k j}\right).
\tag{2.4}
$$

As we want the $A_{j k, p m}^{\mathrm{S}}$ only to the first order in strain, we can replace $\eta_{r s}$ in (2.2) by $\varepsilon_{r s}$. The normal wave velocities $W$ in any direction are determined by solving the determinantal equation
$$
\left|\varrho_{0} W^{2} \delta_{j k}-D_{j k}\right|=0,
\tag{2.5}
$$
where
$$
D_{j k}=\sum_{p m} A_{j k, p m}^{\mathrm{S}} N_{p} N_{m}.
\tag{2.6}
$$

The deformation imposed is (i) a uniform areal strain $\varepsilon^{\prime}$ in the plane perpendicular to the unique axis of the uniaxial crystal (this unique axis is taken to be the $z$-axis); and (ii) a uniform longitudinal strain $\zeta$ along the unique axis. So in (2.2) we put
$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
\varepsilon_{x x}=\varepsilon_{y y}=\frac{1}{2} \varepsilon^{\prime}, \\
\varepsilon_{z z}=\zeta, \\
\varepsilon_{i j}=0 \quad \text { when } \quad i \neq j.
\end{array}\right\}
\tag{2.7}
$$

The elements $D_{j k}$ are symmetric in the indices $j k$. Using the table of nonvanishing elastic constants given by Bhagavantam [9] and Hearmon [10], these elements can be obtained for different uniaxial crystal systems.

When the determinant (2.5) is expanded putting $\varrho_{0} W^{2}=X$, a cubic equation is obtained, namely
$$
X^{3}-A X^{2}+B X-C=0,
\tag{2.8}
$$
where
$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
A=D_{x x}+D_{y y}+D_{z z}, \\
B=\left|\begin{array}{ll}
D_{x x} & D_{x y} \\
D_{x y} & D_{y y}
\end{array}\right|+\left|\begin{array}{ll}
D_{y y} & D_{y z} \\
D_{y z} & D_{z z}
\end{array}\right|+\left|\begin{array}{ll}
D_{z z} & D_{z x} \\
D_{z x} & D_{x x}
\end{array}\right|, \\
C=\left|\begin{array}{lll}
D_{x x} & D_{x y} & D_{x z} \\
D_{x y} & D_{y y} & D_{y z} \\
D_{x z} & D_{y z} & D_{z z}
\end{array}\right|.
\end{array}\right\}
\tag{2.9}
$$

The equation (2.8) can be solved in the unstrained state (i.e. $\varepsilon^{\prime}=0, \zeta=0$) to get three values for $X$, namely $\bar{X}_{1}, \bar{X}_{2}$, and $\bar{X}_{3}$ for any given direction of wave propagation. The direction of wave propagation can be conveniently referred to polar coordinates with the unique axis as the $Z$-axis. Then $N_{x}=$ $=\sin \theta \cos \Phi, N_{y}=\sin \theta \sin \Phi, N_{z}=\cos \theta$. Now differentiating equation (2.8) with respect to $\varepsilon^{\prime}$ and remembering that
$$
\gamma_{j}^{\prime}=-\frac{\partial \log \omega_{j}}{\partial \varepsilon^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial \log X_{j}}{\partial \varepsilon^{\prime}},
$$

we get

$$
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{2} \frac{\bar{X}_{i}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial A}{\partial \varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)_{0}-\bar{X}_{i}\left(\frac{\partial B}{\partial \varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)_{0}+\left(\frac{\partial C}{\partial \varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)_{0}}{3 \bar{X}_{i}^{2}-2 \bar{A} \bar{X}_{i}+\bar{B}}, \tag{2.10}
$$

$$
\gamma_{i}^{\prime \prime}=-\frac{1}{2} \frac{\bar{X}_{i}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial A}{\partial \zeta}\right)_{0}-\bar{X}_{i}\left(\frac{\partial B}{\partial \zeta}\right)_{0}+\left(\frac{\partial C}{\partial \zeta}\right)_{0}}{3 \bar{X}_{i}^{2}-2 \bar{A} \bar{X}_{i}+\bar{B}}. \tag{2.11}
$$

The bars over $A$, $B$, and $C$ indicate their values in the unstrained state. The derivatives of $A$, $B$, and $C$ with respect to $\varepsilon^{\prime}$ and $\zeta$ can be obtained from (2.9) and the appropriate expressions for $D_{i j}$.

For a hexagonal crystal, the acoustic wave velocities in the unstrained state and the generalized GP's depend only on $\theta$ and not on $\Phi$. The determinantal equation factorizes into an equation linear in $X$ and another quadratic in $X$. These equations are

$$
X-\left(D_{y y}\right)_{\Phi=0}=0, \tag{2.12}
$$

$$
X^{2}-\left(D_{x x}+D_{z z}\right)_{\Phi=0} X+\left(D_{x x} D_{z z}-D_{x z}^{2}\right)_{\Phi=0}=0. \tag{2.13}
$$

Therefore the calculation of GP's is simplest for crystals belonging to the hexa- gonal system. As no third order elastic constants are available for any hexa- gonal crystal so far, we have calculated in the next section the TOE constants for a h.c.p. lattice with nearest neighbour central interaction and use it to cal- culate the generalized GP's of the acoustic modes propagating in different directions.

### 3. Third Order Elastic Constants of a H.C.P. Lattice with Nearest Neighbour Central Interaction and the GP's for Acoustic Waves in Different Directions

Srinivasan and Ramji Rao [11] have calculated the thermal expansion of a h.c.p. lattice with nearest neighbour central interaction. The same model is used here to calculate the TOE constants of the h.c.p. lattice. The basis vectors and the position coordinates of the nearest neighbours are given in that paper.

The potential energy of the crystal per unit cell is

$$
\Phi=\sum_{I=1}^{6} \varphi(R(I))+\sum_{J=1}^{6} \varphi(R(J)) \tag{3.1}
$$

when the lattice is strained homogeneously:

$$
R_{i}^{\prime}(I)=R_{i}(I)+\sum_{j} \varepsilon_{i j} R_{j}(I), \tag{3.2}
$$

$$
R_{i}^{\prime}(J)=R_{i}(J)+\sum_{j} \varepsilon_{i j} R_{j}(J)+W_{i}, \tag{3.3}
$$

where $I$ refers to the six nearest neighbours of the same type and $J$ to the six neighbours of different type. In the latter case $W_{i}$ refers to the components of the internal displacement of the lattice of particles of type 2 relative to the lattice of particles of type 1. The potential energy is expanded in powers of

the changes in squares of the vector distances $R(I)$ and $R(J)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi= & \Phi_{0}+O^{\prime} \varphi(r)\left(\sum_{I} \Delta R^{2}(I)+\sum_{J} \Delta R^{2}(J)\right)+ \\
& +\frac{1}{2} O^{\prime \prime} \varphi(r)\left(\sum_{I}\left(\Delta R^{2}(I)\right)^{2}+\sum_{J}\left(\Delta R^{2}(J)\right)^{2}\right)+ \\
& +\frac{1}{6} O^{\prime \prime \prime} \varphi(r)\left(\sum_{I}\left(\Delta R^{2}(I)\right)^{3}+\sum_{J}\left(\Delta R^{2}(J)\right)^{3}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
$$

Here the operator $O^{\prime}=\partial / \partial r^{2}, O^{\prime \prime}=O^{\prime 2}$, and $O^{\prime \prime \prime}=O^{\prime 3}$.

Using equations (3.2) and (3.3) one obtains

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^{2}(K)= & 2 \sum_{i j} \eta_{i j} R_{i}(K) R_{j}(K)+2 \sum_{i} R_{i}(K) \overline{W}_{i}+ \\
& +\sum_{i} \overline{W}_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i j} \overline{W}_{i} \overline{W}_{j} \eta_{i j}
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
$$

correct to third order in the deformations. In addition to the Lagrangian strain $\eta_{i j}$ defined in (2.4) we use $\overline{W}_{i}$ defined by

$$
\overline{W}_{i}=W_{i}+\sum_{j} \varepsilon_{j i} W_{j}. \tag{3.6}
$$

In terms of these quantities the energy is invariant towards rigid rotations of the crystal. For $K=I, \bar{W}$ must be put equal to zero. Substituting for the powers of $\Delta R^{2}(K)$ in (3.4) from (3.5) and retaining only terms up to the third order in the deformation one obtains the expression for the strain energy per unit volume in the undeformed state.

The internal displacement $\overline{W}_{i}$ can be obtained in terms of the Lagrangian strain parameters by minimizing the strain energy with respect to $\overline{W}_{i}$. Srini vasan [12] has shown that to get the strain energy correct to the third order in strain it is enough if we get the internal displacement up to the first order in the strain:

$$
\overline{W}_{i}=-\sum_{j k} \eta_{j k}\left(\sum_{J} R_{i} R_{j} R_{k}\right) / \sum_{J} R_{i}^{2}. \tag{3.7}
$$

Evaluating the sums over the components of $R(J)$ we get

$$
\overline{W}_{x}=\frac{D}{2 \sqrt{3}}\left(\eta_{x x}-\eta_{y y}\right), \tag{3.8}
$$

$$
\overline{W}_{y}=-\frac{D}{2 \sqrt{3}}\left(\eta_{x y}+\eta_{y x}\right). \tag{3.9}
$$

Substituting for $\overline{W}_{i}$ in the expression for the strain energy per unit volume in the undeformed state, and comparing the resulting expression with the expres sion for the energy density on elasticity theory,

$$
U=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i j, k l} C_{i j, k l} \eta_{i j} \eta_{k l}+\frac{1}{6} \sum_{i j, k l, m n} C_{i j, k l, m n} \eta_{i j} \eta_{k l} \eta_{m n}, \tag{3.10}
$$
```

we get the following expressions for the second and third order elastic constants:

$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}
C_{11} &= \frac{29}{3} \, P \,, & C_{12} &= \frac{11}{3} \, P \,, & C_{44} &= \frac{8}{3} \, P \,, \\
C_{33} &= \frac{32}{3} \, P \,, & C_{13} &= \frac{8}{3} \, P \, ;
\end{aligned}
\right\} \tag{3.11}
$$

$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}
C_{111} &= \frac{247}{18} \, Q + P \,, & C_{222} &= \frac{301}{18} \, Q + P \,, & C_{333} &= \frac{256}{18} \, Q \,, \\
C_{112} &= \frac{85}{18} \, Q - \frac{1}{3} \, P \,, & C_{113} &= \frac{16}{18} \, Q + \frac{4}{3} \, P \,, & C_{123} &= \frac{16}{18} \, Q - \frac{4}{3} \, P \,, \\
C_{133} &= \frac{64}{18} \, Q \,, & C_{144} &= \frac{16}{18} \, Q \,, & C_{155} &= \frac{16}{18} \, Q \,, \\
C_{344} &= \frac{64}{18} \, Q \,.
\end{aligned}
\right\} \tag{3.12}
$$

Here
$$
P = \frac{k_2 \, D^4}{V_z} \,, \quad Q = \frac{k_3 \, D^6}{V_z} \, ; \quad k_2 = O'' \, \Phi \,, \quad k_3 = O''' \, \Phi \,. \tag{3.13}
$$

$D$ is the nearest neighbour distance and $V_z$ is the volume of the primitive cell.

Magnesium has a $c/a$ ratio which is almost equal to the value of the ideal h.c.p. lattice and its second order elastic constants are almost in the ratio given by (3.11). The TOE constants of magnesium also might be in the ratio given by (3.12).

For calculating the GP's for the acoustic branches it is assumed that the potential energy is given by a Lennard-Jones expression, namely
$$
\Phi(r) = -\frac{A}{r^6} + \frac{B}{r^{12}} \,.
$$

The values of $k_2$ and $k_3$ are evaluated for this case from (3.13). The corresponding second and third order elastic constants are evaluated from (3.11) and (3.12). They are then used in equations (2.12) and (2.13) and the generalized GP's are calculated for wave vectors making different angles with respect to the unique axis. We see from this table that there is not much variation in the wave velocities of the acoustic waves with direction of propagation. But the GP's depend very sensitively on the direction of wave propagation. It would be interesting if TOE constants become available for some hexagonal materials. One could then see how the GP's vary with the direction of wave propagation and how the low temperature limits of the effective GP's agree with the values obtained from thermal expansion. This calculation is currently being made for alpha- quartz which belongs to the trigonal system.
