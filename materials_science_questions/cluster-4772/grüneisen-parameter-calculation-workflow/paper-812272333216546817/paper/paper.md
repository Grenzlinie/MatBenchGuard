# Specific heat related to the Raman frequency shifts for the rotatory mode in ammonia solid I close to the melting point

H. Karaçalı, H. Yurtseven*

Department of Physics, Middle East Technical University, 06531 Ankara, Turkey

Received 22 December 2004; accepted 2 May 2005

## Abstract

This study gives an evidence for the validity of our spectroscopic modification of the Pippard relation as applied to ammonia solid I close to the melting point. We use our calculated frequencies for the rotatory lattice (librational) mode in ammonia solid I for the pressures of 0, 1.93 and 3.07 kbars. We obtain that the specific heat $C_{p}$ varies linearly with the frequency shifts $1/v(\partial v/\partial T)_{p}$ for this Raman mode at those pressures studied in this crystalline system. Our values of the slope $dP_{m}/dT$ that we deduced from the linear plots, are not in satisfactory agreement with the experimental values in ammonia solid I near the melting point.

## 1. Introduction

Ammonia has various solid, liquid and gaseous phases with the two triple points. Between gas, liquid and solid I phases there occurs a triple point with the coordinates $T_{G-L-I}=195.48$ K, $P=0$ kbar. Between liquid, solid I and II phases there is another triple point with the coordinates $T_{L-I-II}=217.34$ K, $P_{L-I-II}=3.07$ kbar. With all those phases including phases of a mixture of liquid and solid I, and also a mixture of liquid and solid II, ammonia has a rich phase diagram. $P-T$ [1,2] and $V-T$ [3] phase diagrams of ammonia have been given in the literature. mean field theory, we have obtained a $P-T$ phase diagram for the phases of liquid, solid I and II [4], and a $T-P$ phase diagram for the phases of liquid, solid I, II and III [5] in ammonia.

Solid I phase transforms into the liquid phase at $192.5\,\text{K}$ ($P=0\,\text{kbar}$). It has a cubic structure with four molecules per unit cell [6–8]. This melting occurs for solid II phase at $222.4\,\text{K}$ ($P=3.07\,\text{kbar}$), which has two disordered molecules per unit cell in a hexagonal close-packed structure [9]. Another solid phase, solid III, occurs at $35\,\text{kbar}$ and $25\,^\circ\text{C}$ [2], which has a face centred cubic (fcc) structure.

Regarding those solid phases in ammonia, the Raman measurements have been performed. For ammonia solid I the Raman spectra of two translational and one librational modes have been recorded at various temperatures [3,10]. For ammonia solid II the Raman spectra of lattice modes (translational and librational) have been obtained [11–14]. Those translational and librational modes have also been assigned in the Raman spectra of ammonia solid III [12]. On the basis of the experimental data [3], we have calculated the Raman frequencies of two translational and one librational mode as a function of temperature [15] and pressure [16] in ammonia solid I. Using the experimental data [14], we have also calculated the Raman frequencies of the rotatory (librational) mode in ammonia solid II as a function of temperature [17] and pressure [18].

It is interesting to study the critical behaviour of ammonia solid system close to the melting point. This critical behaviour has been observed experimentally for the ammonia solid I and II by the divergence behaviour of the isotermal compressibility [19,20]. This divergence behaviour has been analysed by a power-law, which has been considered as a second-order phase transformation prior to the melting in ammonia [19,20]. Recently, we have studied theoretically this divergence behaviour in ammonia solid I and II by treating not only the isothermal compressibility but also the thermal expansivity and the specific heat in those systems [21]. Similar critical behaviour of those thermodynamic functions studied has led us to establish the Pippard relations in ammonia solid I and II [22].

The Pippard relations can be established spectroscopically by means of the Raman frequency shifts of some appropriate modes in the ammonia solid system. Using the definition of the mode Grüneisen parameter that relates the frequency shifts to the thermal expansivity or the isothermal compressibility, the Pippard relations can be modified spectroscopically, as we have introduced and applied to ammonium halides [23,24]. Similarly, they can be applied to ammonia.

In this study we establish the spectroscopic modification of the first Pippard relation that relates the specific heat $C_p$ to the frequency shifts $1/v(\partial v/\partial T)_p$ in ammonia solid I close to the melting point. For the Raman frequency shifts we use our calculated frequencies of the rotatory lattice (librational) mode in ammonia solid I [15].

In Section 2 we give the theoretical background in ammonia near the melting point. In Section 3 we give our calculations and results. Discussion of our results is given in Section 4. Finally, Section 5 gives our conclusions.

### 2. Theory

Pippard has developed his first relation by establishing a linear variation of the specific heat $C_p$ with the thermal expansivity $\alpha_p$ in the vicinity of the $\lambda$ point [25]. This is given by

$$
C_{p}=T\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P}{\mathrm{~d} T}\right)_{\lambda} V \alpha_{P}+T\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} S}{\mathrm{~d} T}\right)_{\lambda}.\tag{2.1}
$$

Near the melting point in ammonia solid I, the above relation can be expressed as

$$
C_{p}=T\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}}}{\mathrm{d} T}\right) V_{\mathrm{s}} \alpha_{p}+T\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} S}{\mathrm{~d} T}\right)_{\mathrm{m}}. \tag{2.2}
$$

In this relation $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ is the solid volume, $\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}} / \mathrm{d} T$ is the slope in the $P-T$ phase diagram and $(\mathrm{d} S / \mathrm{d} T)_{\mathrm{m}}$ is the variation of the entropy with the temperature both, at the melting point in the ammonia solid I.

We can modify this thermodynamic relation (Eq. (2.2)) spectroscopically by defining the isobaric mode Grüneisen parameter

$$
\gamma_{p} \equiv-\frac{1}{\alpha_{p}} \frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_{p}. \tag{2.3}
$$

By inserting Eq. (2.3) into Eq. (2.2), we modify the first Pippard relation spectroscopically

$$
C_{p}=-\frac{T V_{\mathrm{s}}}{\gamma_{p}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}}}{\mathrm{d} T}\right) \frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial T}\right)_{p}+T\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} S}{\mathrm{~d} T}\right)_{\mathrm{m}}. \tag{2.4}
$$

This gives a linear variation of the specific heat $C_{p}$ with the frequency shifts $1 / v(\partial v / \partial T)_{p}$ close to the melting point in ammonia solid I. In this relation the mode Grüneisen parameter can be taken as a constant in the phase change region.

Eq. (2.4) can be examined using observed or calculated values of the specific heat $C_{p}$ and the frequency shifts $1 / v(\partial v / \partial T)_{p}$ close to the melting point in ammonia solid I. For the frequency shifts, the definition of the mode Grüneisen parameter $\gamma_{p}$ (Eq. (2.3)) can be used when the thermal expansivity $\alpha_{p}$ is known as a function of temperature for constant pressures. If the volume of the ammonia solid I is measured or calculated as a function of temperature, the frequency can be obtained by integrating Eq. (2.3), which gives

$$
v_{p}(T)=\Delta_{p}+A(P)+v_{\mathrm{m}} \exp \left[-\gamma_{P} \ln \frac{V_{p}(T)}{V_{\mathrm{m}}}\right]. \tag{2.5}
$$

with the additional terms $\Delta_{p}$ and $A(P)$. Here $\Delta_{p}$ is defined as the order-disorder contribution to the frequency and it has the values of $\Delta_{p}=0$ for $T>T_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $\Delta_{p} \neq 0$ for $T<T_{\mathrm{m}}$. The pressure- dependent term $A(P)$ can be defined as

$$
A(P)=a_{0}+a_{1} P+a_{2} P^{2}, \tag{2.6}
$$

where $a_{0}, a_{1}$ and $a_{2}$ are constants. In Eq. (2.5) $v_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{m}}$ correspond to the values of the frequency and solid volume, respectively, at the melting temperature $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ in ammonia solid I. By Eq. (2.5), the frequencies can be predicted using the volume values at various temperatures for some fixed pressures in ammonia solid I. This requires to determine the coefficients $\Delta_{p}+a_{0}, a_{1}$ and $a_{2}$, which can be obtained using some initial data for the volume and the frequency in ammonia solid I, according to Eq. (2.5). To predict the frequencies through Eq. (2.5), the solid volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ can be obtained from the critical behaviour of the isothermal compressibility in ammonia solid I,

$$
\kappa_{T}=k\left(P-P_{\mathrm{m}}\right)^{-\gamma} \tag{2.7}
$$

due to Pruzan et al. [19]. In Eq. (2.7) $k$ is the amplitude and $\gamma$ is the critical exponent close to the melting pressure $P_{\mathrm{m}}$. Using the definition of the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_{T} \equiv-1 / V(\partial V / \partial P)_{T}$

the pressure dependence of the solid volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ can be obtained as

$$
V_{\mathrm{s}}=V_{\mathrm{c}} \exp \left[-k(1-\gamma)^{-1}\left(P-P_{\mathrm{m}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\right]
\tag{2.8}
$$

with the critical volume $V_{\mathrm{c}}$. This relation provides with us the temperature dependence of the solid volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ by considering the ratio

$$
\frac{P-P_{\mathrm{m}}(T)}{T_{\mathrm{m}}(P)-T}=\frac{\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}}}{\mathrm{d} T}
\tag{2.9}
$$

close to the melting point in ammonia solid I. Thus, we obtain by means of Eq. (2.9) the temperature dependence of the solid volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ as

$$
V_{\mathrm{s}}=V_{\mathrm{c}} \exp \left[-k(1-\gamma)^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}}}{\mathrm{d} T}\right)^{1-\gamma}\left(T_{\mathrm{m}}-T\right)^{1-\gamma}\right]
\tag{2.10}
$$

which can then be used in Eq. (2.5) to predict the frequencies in ammonia solid I. To determine the specific heat $C_{p}$ which will be used in our modified version of the first Pippard relation (Eq. (2.4)), we can use the thermodynamic relation

$$
C_{p}=T V_{\mathrm{s}} \alpha_{p}^{2} / \kappa_{T}
\tag{2.11}
$$

by knowing the solid volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$, thermal expansivity $\alpha_{p}$ and the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_{T}$ close to the melting point in ammonia solid I. Using the definition of the thermal expansivity $\alpha_{P} \equiv 1 / V(\partial V / \partial T)_{P}$, the temperature dependence of $\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}$ can be obtained by means of Eq. (2.10) as

$$
\alpha_{p}=k\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}}}{\mathrm{d} T}\right)^{1-\gamma}\left(T_{\mathrm{m}}-T\right)^{-\gamma}+\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{c}}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} V_{\mathrm{c}}}{\mathrm{d} T}\right).
\tag{2.12}
$$

Here $\mathrm{d} V_{\mathrm{c}} / \mathrm{d} T$ is the variation of the critical volume $V_{\mathrm{c}}$ with the temperature in ammonia solid I. Again, by using Eq. (2.9) in Eq. (2.7), the temperature dependence of the isothermal compressibility can be obtained as

$$
\kappa_{T}=k\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}}}{\mathrm{d} T}\right)^{-\gamma}\left(T_{\mathrm{m}}-T\right)^{-\gamma}.
\tag{2.13}
$$

So, by means of Eqs. (2.10), (2.12) and (2.13) the specific heat $C_{p}$ can be calculated as a function of temperature for constant pressures, according to Eq. (2.11) in ammonia solid I. Thus, the specific heat $C_{p}$ and the frequency shifts $1 / v(\partial v / \partial T)_{p}$, which are obtained at various temperatures for some fixed pressures close to the melting point in ammonia solid I, can be plotted according to Eq. (2.4). From this linear plot, we can predict the slope $\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}} / \mathrm{d} T$ and the intercept $(\mathrm{d} S / \mathrm{d} T)_{\mathrm{m}}$ at the melting point in ammonia solid I.

## 3. Calculations and results

In order to establish our modified version of the first Pippard relation (Eq. (2.4)), we calculated the frequencies by means of Eq. (2.5) where we used the volume values evaluated through the empirical relations due to Pruzan et al. [19]. In the volume expression (Eq. (2.10)) we required the values of the slope $\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}} / \mathrm{d} T$ at various temperatures, which were deduced from the temperature

dependence of the melting pressure [19], given by

$$
P_{\mathrm{m}}(I)=5.886\left[\left(\frac{T}{T_{\mathrm{g}-\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{I}}}\right)^{3.96}-1\right],
\tag{3.1}
$$

where the temperature of the triple point is $T_{\mathrm{g}-\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{I}}=195.48 \mathrm{~K}$. Eq. (3.1) gives the slope

$$
\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}}(I) / \mathrm{d} T=1.967 \times 10^{-8} T^{2.96}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

We also needed the temperature dependence of the critical volume for the ammonia solid I, which was given by

$$
V_{\mathrm{c}}(I)=V_{\mathrm{I}}-d_{\mathrm{I}}\left(T-T_{\mathrm{g}-\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{I}}\right),
\tag{3.3}
$$

where the measured values were $V_{\mathrm{I}}=21.61 \mathrm{~cm}^{3} / \mathrm{mol}$ and $d_{\mathrm{I}}=0.0316 \mathrm{~cm}^{3} / \mathrm{mol} \mathrm{K}$ in this solid system [19]. With those values, Eq. (3.3) becomes

$$
V_{\mathrm{c}}(I)=27.79-0.0316 T.
\tag{3.4}
$$

We established here a linear variation of the specific heat $C_{p}$ with the frequency shifts $1 / v(\partial v / \partial T)_{p}$ for the librational mode of $v\left(280 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\right)$ in ammonia solid I for the fixed pressures of 0,1.93 and $3.07 \mathrm{kbars}$. For this linearity, we first evaluated the specific heat $C_{p}$ as a function of temperature for the pressures considered. This was done by the thermodynamic relation (Eq. (2.11)) where we calculated the temperature dependences of the solid volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$, the thermal expansivity $\alpha_{P}$ and the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_{T}$ for the pressures studied. Using the values of the amplitude $k=0.0137$ and the critical exponent $\gamma=0.49$ [19], the temperature dependence of the solid volume $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ was obtained according to Eq. (2.10) for the pressures of 0,1.93 and $3.07 \mathrm{kbars}$ in ammonia solid I.

The thermal expansivity $\alpha_{P}$ was obtained as a function of temperature using Eq. (2.12) where we used the values of $\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}} / \mathrm{d} T$ with the same $k$ and $\gamma$ values. In Eq. (2.12) the values of $1 / V_{\mathrm{c}}\left(\mathrm{d} V_{\mathrm{c}} / \mathrm{d} T\right)$ were used, which were deduced from Eq. (3.4), as we tabulated in Table 1 with the $V_{\mathrm{c}}$ values for the pressures of 0,1.93 and $3.07 \mathrm{kbars}$ in ammonia solid I.

Finally, the isothermal compressibility $\kappa_{T}$ was calculated as a function of temperature according to Eq. (2.13) with the values of $\mathrm{d} P_{\mathrm{m}} / \mathrm{d} T, k$ and $\gamma$ for the pressures of 0,1.93 and $3.07 \mathrm{kbars}$ in ammonia solid I. Thus, we obtained the temperature dependence of the specific heat $C_{p}$ by means of $V_{\mathrm{s}}, \alpha_{P}$ and $\kappa_{T}$ at various temperatures for the fixed pressures of 0,1.93 and $3.07 \mathrm{kbars}$ in ammonia solid I, according to Eq. (2.11).

The Raman frequencies of the librational mode of $v\left(280 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\right)$ were calculated by means of Eq. (2.5) where we used the values of the solid volume $V_{P}(T)$ which we already obtained at various temperatures for the pressures of 0,1.93 and $3.07 \mathrm{kbars}$, as we noted earlier. In Eq. (2.5) the values of the volume $V_{\mathrm{m}}=21.61 \mathrm{~cm}^{3} / \mathrm{mol}$ and the Raman frequency $v_{\mathrm{m}}=276.31 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ (Table 2) were used, which were obtained as the extrapolated values at $T_{\mathrm{g}-\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{I}}=195.48 \mathrm{~K}$. The value of mode Grüneisen parameter, $\gamma_{P}=0.8$, was also used for the librational mode of $v\left(280 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\right)$ [3], as given in Table 2.

## 5. Conclusions

We examined in this study our spectroscopic relation that relates the specific heat $C_{p}$ to the frequency shifts $1 / v(\partial v / \partial T)_{p}$ in ammonia solid I near the melting point. We calculated the specific heat $C_{p}$ thermodynamically and obtained its linear variation with the frequency shifts of the rotatory lattice (librational) mode in ammonia solid I for the pressures of 0, 1.93 and 3.07 kbars, according to our Pippard relation. This shows the validity of our spectroscopic relation for ammonia solid I close to the melting point. It can be applied to some other systems exhibiting phase transitions similar to ammonia solid system.
