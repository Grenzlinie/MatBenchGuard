# Pressure- and temperature-dependence of elastic properties of a ZBLAN glass

L.G. Hwa ${ }^{\mathrm{a}, *}$, Y.J. Wu ${ }^{\mathrm{b}}$, W.C. Chao ${ }^{\mathrm{a}}$, C.H. Chen ${ }^{\mathrm{a}}$

## Abstract

The hydrostatic and uni-axial pressures, and the temperature-dependence of elastic properties of a $53ZrF_4$-$20BaF_2$-$4LaF_3$-$3AlF_3$-$20NaF$ (ZBLAN) glass were determined by ultrasonic pulse-echo techniques. The experimental results are used to obtain elastic constants of the glass. The normal behavior of negative temperature-dependence and positive pressure-dependence of the ultrasonic velocities were observed for this glass. The pressure-derivative of shear modulus and bulk modulus, and the longitudinal and shear acoustic mode Gruneisen parameters are all positive ($\partial S/\partial P=0.92$, $\partial B/\partial P=5.30$, $\gamma_{11}=1.89$ and $\gamma_{44}=0.83$). © 2002 Elsevier Science B.V. ## 1. Introduction

The discovery of heavy-metal fluoride glasses based on zirconium and/or hafnium fluoride, during an investigation of crystalline laser host materials, in 1975 by Poulain et al. has generated considerable excitement in physics, chemistry and materials science community [1]. Although, only a few halide glass systems exist for optical applications, these heavy-metal fluoride glasses exhibit excellent optical quality, reasonable sample size and strong resistance to corrosion from atmospheric moisture.

In comparison with silicate glasses, these glasses have extended transparency into the mid-IR wavelengths ($\sim7\ \mu$m) [2]. The theoretical and experimental results suggested the possibility of fluoride glasses as ultra-low loss fiber-optic materials [3,4]. Besides, fluoride glasses possess low non-linear refractive index, low material dispersion, and good chemical durability [5-7].

Fluoride glass windows can potentially be made in large sizes and the performance is comparable to that of $CaF_2$. Furthermore, fluoride glass fibers doped with rare earth ions can be used as amplifiers in fiber-optic communication systems [8-11]. $\text{Pr}^{3+}$-doped fluorozirconate glasses are currently promising fiber amplifier materials for $1.31\ \mu$m operation since heavy-metal fluoride glasses possess low phonon energy around $500\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ [12,13]. The low phonon energy of fluoride glasses reduce the non-radiative transition probability substantially relative to $\text{SiO}_2$ glass, and make the $^1\text{G}_4$-$^3\text{H}_5$ transition possible [13]. Recently, fluoride glass fibers have also been used in chemical remote sensing systems for environmental monitoring using infrared diffuse reflectance and absorption spectroscopy [14].

Use fluoride glass as infrared transmitting and fiber-optics applications, the mechanical and/or elastic properties of glass are always influenced by the change of environment, such as temperature and pressure. Previous study on the temperature-dependence of elastic properties of $53ZrF_4$-$20BaF_2$-$4LaF_3$-$3AlF_3$-$20NaF$ (ZBLAN) glasses exhibited a negative temperature-dependence of ultrasonic velocities, which was related to high coordination number of the fluoride glasses [15]. In this study, the hydrostatic and uni-axial pressure- and the temperature-dependence of sound velocities of a ZBLAN glass was measured by ultrasonic pulse-echo technique. The experimental results provide the complete set of non-vanishing components of the second- and third-order elastic stiffness tensors. The second-order elastic constants (SOECs) give the slope of dispersion curves at long wavelength limit and the understanding of the vibration properties of a glass [16]. The determination of TOEC of a solid is of great interest since they are the measure of first-order an-harmonic terms of the inter-atomic potential or of the intrinsic non-linearity of the solid, such as thermal expansion. The knowledge of TOEC allows the evaluation of the generalized Gruneisen parameters, which measures the coupling of the stress wave with the thermal phonons in Debye approximation [17]. The measurement of pressure- and temperature-dependence of ultrasonic sound wave is a good way to study this ef- fect. Recently, another method by a stress-induced Brillouin scattering measurement was carried out to study TOEC of soda-lime-silica glass [18].

## 2. Experimental aspects

A ZBLAN glass used in our experiment was purchased from Infrared Fiber System Inc., under the trade name of low temperature fluoride (LTF). The glass transition temperature $T_g$ is around 543 K [15].

High temperature ultrasonic measurements were per- formed by a pulse-echo method with a Panametrics model 5800 pulser/receiver instrument and with two specially de- signed PZT transducers from Etalon Inc., for temperature up to 475 K at 20 MHz frequency. The X-cut transduc- ers were employed for longitudinal modes and Y-cut for shear modes. The pulse transit time was measured with a Hewlett-Packard model 54502 A, oscilloscope. The high temperature-couplant used in the experiment was purchased from Sonotech Inc. Several effects, such as multiple inter- nal reflections within the transducer, sample thickness, and the acoustic impedance mismatch between the glass sam- ple and the transducer, influence the accuracy of ultrasonic velocity measurements. The uncertainty is estimated to be about $\pm 1\%$.

The uni-axial pressure was applied by a piston–cylinder equipment, the force exerted being measured by a test- ing ring. For hydrostatic pressure measurements, another cylindrical device with a hole in the center was modify in between the piston–cylinder equipment with a sample holder at the bottom. A T-shape plate was used on the top and the pure salt was filled into the hole as the pressure medium in the experiments, then the cylinder was connected to the pressure-transducer at the bottom. The pressure sys- tems (both uni-axial and hydrostatic) were calibrated by a well-known data of a $SiO_2$ glass sample. Pressure-induced changes in the sample dimensions were accounted for by using the natural velocity technique [17,19,20].

High-pressure PZT transducers were designed by us and fabricated by a Canadian company. The piezoelectric crys- tals in the transducers were specially protected by high strength alloy steel and by hydraulic component. These transducers were designed to be able to stand the force till 200,000 N. The X- and Y-cut transducers have the delay time 8.4 and $15.5\ \mu$s, respectively.

The density was determined by an Archimedes technique for which $n$-hexadecane was used as the working fluid. The accuracy of the measurement was about $\pm 0.001\ \text{g/cm}^3$.

## 3. Results

For an isotropic solid (such as glass), there are two inde- pendent SOECs ($C_{11}$ and $C_{44}$) and three independent TOECs ($C_{123}$, $C_{144}$ and $C_{456}$). The SOEC can be derived from the measurement of sound velocities. For pure longitudinal waves: $C_{11}=\rho V_\text{L}^2$, and for pure transverse waves: $C_{44}=\rho V_\text{T}^2$, where $V_\text{L}$ and $V_\text{T}$ are the longitudinal and transverse velocities, respectively. The sound velocities also allow the determination of Young’s modulus ($E$), bulk modulus ($B$), and Poisson’s ratio ($\sigma$), by the following equations:

$$
E=\rho V_\text{T}^2\frac{3V_\text{L}^2-4V_\text{T}^2}{V_\text{L}^2-V_\text{T}^2}\tag{1}
$$

$$
S=\rho V_\text{T}^2=C_{44}\tag{2}
$$

$$
B=\rho\frac{3V_\text{L}^2-4V_\text{T}^2}{3}\tag{3}
$$

$$
\sigma=\frac{V_\text{L}^2-2V_\text{T}^2}{2(V_\text{L}^2-V_\text{T}^2)}.\tag{4}
$$

The reason for this discrepancy is because the heavy-metal fluoride glasses fall into the category of being highly coordinated solids with respect to the tetrahedrally coordinated $SiO_{2}$ glass.

The calculated values of all TOECs show that $C_{111}$ has the largest magnitude among all the TOECs. A qualitative physical reason was given by Brassington et al. to account for the negative sign of the TOEC of a-$As_{2}S_{3}$ [21]. They argued that the shorter range and larger exponent of the repulsive potential require the negative values of TOECs. Since $C_{111}=\partial^{3}\phi/\partial\eta_{11}^{3}$ is dominated by the short-range repulsive force between nearest-neighbor atoms (where $\phi$ is the inter-atomic potential, and $\eta$ is generalized coordinate) while $C_{112}$ and $C_{123}$ are not, therefore, $C_{111}$ should have the largest magnitude of the TOEC, as it does for a-As, a-$As_{2}S_{3}$, and the ZBLAN glass [21,22]. Combined with the SOEC in Table 2, we have the complete set of the components of second- and third-order elastic stiffness tensors of ZBLAN glass.

In a previous study, we used the ratio of $C_{44}/C_{12}$ as an indicator of the character of the force field, central forces if $C_{44}/C_{12}=1$ and non-central forces if $C_{44}/C_{12}\neq1$. For ZBLAN glass, the ratio is about 0.6, which is due to the non-central force fields (the an-harmonicity of the vibrational states or the non-linearity of atomic force) result- ing from ion-overlapping and covalent bonding [15]. With new TOEC available here, gives us opportunity to test the nature of non-central force field further. The Cauchy rela- tion for TOEC $(C_{123}=C_{144}=C_{456})$ is not obeyed. The large deviations imply the existence of the large component of an-harmonic forces between the atoms.

The experimental results show that the temperature- derivatives $(\partial S/\partial T$ and $\partial B/\partial T)$ are negative and the pressure-derivatives $(\partial S/\partial P$ and $\partial B/\partial P)$ are positive, as indicated in Table 4. This normal negative temperature- dependence and positive pressure-dependence of elas- tic behavior of the sample implies a closely packed structure for ZBLAN glass and agrees with those of most oxide glasses, ceramic and metallic materials. The temperature-dependence of SOECs can give information on the fourth-order elastic constants of the materials. However, this is beyond the scope of the present discussion.

For an isotropic solid there are two contributions to the acoustic Gruneisen $\gamma$'s: $\gamma_{11}$ and $\gamma_{44}$ are associated with longitudinal modes and transverse modes, respectively. Two Gruneisen parameters were calculated using the following expression [23]:

$$
\gamma_{11}=-\left(6 C_{11}\right)^{-1}\left(3 B_{\mathrm{T}}+2 C_{11}+k_{11}\right) \tag{10}
$$

$$
\gamma_{44}=-\left(6 C_{44}\right)^{-1}\left(3 B_{\mathrm{T}}+2 C_{44}+k_{44}\right) \tag{11}
$$

where $k_{11}=C_{111}+2 C_{112}$ and $k_{44}=1 / 2\left(C_{111}-C_{123}\right)$. The positive pressure-derivatives of the longitudinal and transverse wave velocities ensure that the calculation of both $\gamma_{11}$ and $\gamma_{44}$ are positive for ZBLAN glass (Table 4). Table 4 gives also the temperature-derivatives and the pressure-derivatives of elastic moduli and Gruneisen parameters of two other fluoride glasses [24,25] ($58ZrF_4$-$34BaF_2$- $8ThF_4$ and $58.3ZrF_4$-$18.4BaF_2$-$14.6LaF_3$-$5.8AlF_3$-$2.9NaF$) and $SiO_2$ glass for comparison purpose. A reasonable comparison was found for the Gruneisen parameters. The positive values of both $\gamma_{11}$ and $\gamma_{44}$ for ZBLAN glass indicate that the long-wavelength acoustic mode frequencies increase with increasing applied strains or compressions.

## 5. Conclusions
We have successfully performed the measurements of both pressure- and temperature-dependence of ultrasonic velocities of a ZBLAN glass. The experimental results give the complete set of the components of the second- and third-order elastic stiffness tensors of the glass. The non-central force character or non-linearity of atomic force of our glass is further understood through the availability of Gruneisen parameters. The positive values of these parameters imply the acoustic modes stiffen under pressure at the long wavelength limit for this glass.
