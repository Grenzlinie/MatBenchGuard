# Shock-Reduced 300 K Isotherm Derivation from Hugoniot Data with Ab Initio MFP Approach

## Problem background
Accurate equations of state (EOS) for metals at high pressure are essential for interpreting both static diamond-anvil-cell (DAC) and dynamic shock-wave experiments. The pressure frontier lies in the range 100–1000 GPa, where static and dynamic techniques each provide volume–pressure data but under very different thermal conditions. A reduction scheme is needed to convert shock Hugoniot data (measured along a high-temperature compression path) into 300 K isotherms suitable for use as a pressure standard in room-temperature DAC experiments. A promising approach is to compute the thermal volume expansion of the shock Hugoniot relative to the 300 K isotherm from first principles using a mean-field potential (MFP) method that requires only the 0 K total energy from electronic structure calculations, avoiding empirical assumptions about Grüneisen parameters or heat capacities. The present task asks: for the five reference metals Cu, Ta, Mo, Pt, and Au, can you compute shock-reduced 300 K isotherms by combining ab initio LAPW total energies, the MFP vibrational free energy, and experimental shock-wave D–u parameters, and then fit the resulting P–X points to a generalized universal EOS?

## Approach
The workflow proceeds in four stages. First, perform full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (LAPW) total energy calculations to obtain the 0 K total energy E_c as a function of atomic volume V for each metal, using the GGA functional for Cu, Ta, Mo and the LDA functional for Pt, Au. Second, from the E_c(V) curve, construct the MFP mean-field potential g(r,V) = ½[E_c(R+r) + E_c(R−r) − 2E_c(R)], where R is the nearest-neighbour distance derived from V. Use this potential to compute the ionic vibrational free energy F_ion(V,T) and the electronic thermal free energy F_el(V,T) via statistical mechanics. With these free energies, solve for the pressure and temperature along the principal Hugoniot to obtain the MFP-predicted reduced-volume functions X_sw^MFP(P) and X_300^MFP(P). Third, compute the experimental shock Hugoniot reduced volume X_sw^ex(P) using the provided D–u coefficients (the D = a + bu + cu² relation) and the Rankine–Hugoniot relations. Then apply the reduction formula X_300^rd(P) = X_sw^ex(P) − [X_sw^MFP(P) − X_300^MFP(P)] to obtain shock-reduced 300 K isotherms. Fourth, fit the derived P–X data for each metal to a generalized universal EOS to obtain parameters: the isothermal bulk modulus B₀ (GPa) and the shape parameters η, β, ξ, δ. Note that the reduction does not require any empirical Grüneisen parameters or ad hoc heat-capacity assumptions.

## How you are scored
Your submitted artifacts are scored by a hidden verifier that runs after your submission. The verifier does not simply read your final numbers; it independently re-derives the key results:

- It reads your `reduced_isotherm_data.csv` and refits the universal EOS to the points you supply. From this refit, it obtains parameters B₀, η, β, ξ, δ for each metal. It compares these refit parameters against hidden gold values using tolerances that account for legitimate implementation differences.

- It computes the pressure from your refit parameters at several hidden reduced volumes (not revealed to you) and compares those pressures to the pressures computed from the hidden gold parameters at the same reduced volumes, requiring a relative agreement better than a preset threshold.

- It also reads your `reduced_isotherm_parameters.csv` and directly compares your fitted parameters to the hidden gold values with tolerances.

The total reward (a float between 0 and 1) is a weighted combination of these checks, with larger weight on the refit-parameter comparison and the pressure-consistency check. Therefore, simply copying numbers from the literature without performing the full LAPW+MFP pipeline will yield poor scores, because the verifier’s refit from your basic data points will disagree with the hidden gold while your directly reported parameters might happen to match. The verifier assumes that you have honestly executed the entire computational workflow described in the steps; it scores the outputs of that workflow.
