# THERMAL EXPANSION OF $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ AT LOW TEMPERATURES

R. Pott$^+$ and G. Güntherodt$^+$
II. Physikalisches Institut, Universität zu Köln, D-5000 Köln 41, Germany
and
H. Bach
Institut f. Experimentalphys.IV, Ruhr-Universität Bochum, D-463 Bochum, Germany

(Received 19 June 1981 by B. Mühlschlegel)

The thermal expansion of the superconductor $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ has been investigated on a single crystal by a capacitive method between 1.6 and 14 K. The relative length change shows a well pronounced second order phase transformation near 8.05 K, which is caused by the superconducting transition. From the calculated linear thermal expansion coefficient we determine the pressure coefficient of the transition temperature and of the thermodynamic critical field as well as the thermodynamic critical field itself. Furthermore we estimate the electronic, phononic and total Grüneisen parameters. These parameters have been used to discuss on the basis of the McMillan equation the reason for the increase of the transition temperature under pressure.

## Introduction

$\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ is well known to be a superconductor with a transition temperature $T_c$ of about 8.1 K$^1$. At room temperature $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ has the cubic $\text{Th}_3\text{P}_4$ structure$^2$.
For our single crystal the measurements of specific heat and thermal expansion$^3$ show a second order phase transformation near 102 K, contrary to the conclusions of Westerholt et al.$^4$ and Dernier et al.$^5$. The transition is due to a tetra- gonal distortion of the unit cell$^{4,5}$.
$\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ shows a anomalous increase of $T_c$ under hydrostatic pressure ($T_c$ increases by about 4 K under a pressure of 2 GPa$^{6,7}$). Note that this pressure dependence of $T_c$ is similar to that of pure $\text{La}^8$. Westerholt et al.$^9$ have shown that $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ exhibits quite unusual superconducting properties, indicating that $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ is a strong coupling superconductor with a high upper critical magnetic field $H_{c2}$.

In this paper we report on the relative length change $\Delta\text{L/L}$ and the linear thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha=$dlnL/dT of $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ as measured by a capacitive thermal expansion method$^{10}$ between 1.6 and 14 K. Since $\Delta\text{L/L}$ shows a well pronounced second order phase transformation at $T_c$=8.05 K, we were able to calculate the pressure coefficient of $T_c$ via the Ehrenfest relations$^{11}$, the difference in compressibility $\kappa_n-\kappa_s$ between the normal (n) and superconducting (s) state, the thermodynamic critical field $H_c$, as well as the electronic ($\gamma_e$), phononic ($\gamma_{ph}$) and total ($\gamma_G$) Grüneisen parameters. The Grüneisen parameters give information about the volume (pressure) dependence of the electronic density of states $\text{N}(E_F)$ at the Fermi level, the Debye temperature $\theta$ and the phonon frequencies $\omega^{12}$. These parameters have been used to discuss the reasons for the positive pressure coefficient of $T_c$ on the basis of the modified McMillan equation$^{13}$.

## Experiments

The $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ single crystal was prepared using a Bridgman technique$^{14}$. The lattice parameter at room temperature is a=8.724 Å ( at 5 K a=8.754 Å and c=8.613 Å), which indicates that our crystal was nearly stoichiometric$^4$. We want to point out, that this is also corroborated by the high transition temperature near 102 K of the structural phase transformation (see Refs.$^{3,4}$).
The thermal expansion measurement was performed by a capacitive three terminal method$^{10}$, measuring continuously the relative length change of the sample.
The sensitivity in the case of the $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ crystal (length 6.04 mm) was $\Delta\text{L/L}$=$10^{-9}$.
The linear thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha$ was calculated by a Spline fit$^{15}$ and has a sensitivity of $10^{-9}$ K$^{-1}$ also.
The accuracy between 1.6 and 14 K is better than ±1% for both quantities ($\Delta\text{L/L}$ and $\alpha$) with the exception of $\alpha$ at $T_c$ ( see below).

Fig.1 shows the relative length change $\Delta\text{L/L}$ (normalized to 0 at 1.6 K) between 1.6 and 14 K. There is a clearly visible anomaly at 8.05 K, which is due to the second order superconducting phase transformation. The anomaly is so well pronounced and large, that it is not necessary to measure $\Delta\text{L/L}$ of $\text{La}_3\text{S}_4$ in the normal conducting state by apply-

The plot in Fig.3 shows an evaluation of the electronic ($\gamma_{\alpha}$) and phononic ($\beta_{\alpha}$) coefficient of $\alpha$ in the normal conducting state via the relationship
$$\alpha=\gamma_{\alpha}T+\beta_{\alpha}T^{3} \tag{1}$$
in analogy to a low temperature specific heat analysis. We obtain
$\gamma_{\alpha}=-(2.6\pm0.1)10^{-9}K^{-1}$ and
$\beta_{\alpha}=+(2.0\pm0.1)10^{-10}K^{-4}$.
The problem here is that we can apply this procedure only above $T_{C}$, i.e. in a temperature region where the analysis normally does not hold as good as in the low temperature region. By using the above derived coefficients we have extrapolated in Fig.1 the expected length change of the normal conducting state for $T<T_{C}$.

Below $T_{C}$ $\alpha$ has the following temperature dependence
$\alpha=a\ T_{C}\ \exp(-bT_{C}/T)$, where a and b are constants.

This relationship holds for 2.7<T<8.1 K, (see Fig.4) and is similar to that of the specific heat of some superconductors¹¹, indicating that La₃S₄ is a superconductor with a gap.

### Discussion
The discussion is divided into two main parts. First, we determine $dT_{C}/dp$. Using this result we calculate also $H_{C}$, $dH_{C}/dp$ and $\kappa_{n}-\kappa_{S}$. In the second part we determine the three Grüneisen parameters. These enable us to discuss the reason for the large positive pressure coefficient of $T_{C}$ in terms of the pressure coefficients of the electron-phonon coupling constant $\lambda$, the effective Coulomb potential $\mu^{+}$ and the McMillan-Hopfield parameter $\eta^{13}$.

The Ehrenfest relations describe the pressure coefficient of $T_{C}$ as a function of the difference of the volume

expansion coefficient $\Delta \beta^{s-n}$, the specific heat $\Delta C^{s-n}$ and the compressibility $\Delta \kappa^{s-n}$:

$$
\mathrm{dT_C/dp=VT_C \Delta \beta^{s-n}/\Delta C^{s-n}= \Delta \kappa^{s-n}/\Delta \beta^{s-n}/2}
$$

Here $V$ is the molar volume (La₃S₄: $V$ = $9.938\ 10^{-5}m^{-3}$). By assuming that $\Delta \beta^{s-n}=$ $3\Delta \alpha^{s-n}$, $\Delta C^{s-n}=0.6$ J/Mole K $^{9}$and $\kappa=1.25\ 10^{-11}Pa^{-1}$ $^{14}$ we obtain

$$
\mathrm{dT_C/dp=(1.35 \pm 0.1)10^{-9}K/Pa};
$$

$$
\mathrm{dlnT_C/dlnV=-(13.4 \pm 1)}
$$

The error is due to the uncertainty of $\alpha$ and C. However we would like to point out that the assumption $\Delta \beta=3\Delta \alpha$ is questionable, since we have a tetragonal unit cell below 102 K, and therefore in the case of our unoriented single crystal an anisotropic thermal expansion behavior due to different directions could be expected.

Shelton et al. $^{6}$ and Eiling $^{7}$ found by a direct hydrostatic pressure measurement a value of $\mathrm{dT_C/dp=1.6\cdot10^{-9}Pa^{-1}}$ ($\mathrm{dlnT_C/dlnV=-15.8}$), i.e. our value is in good agreement with the literature.

By using the above derived value for $\mathrm{dT_C/dp}$ we get from $/2/$$\Delta \kappa^{s-n}=$ $1.38\cdot10^{-15}Pa^{-1}$, i.e. the superconducting phase is more compressible. Hence $\Delta \kappa^{s-n}/\kappa$ is only about $1.1\cdot10^{-2}\%$. This is a rather small difference but for the case of a superconductor still large$^{11}$ and in principle experimentally detectable.

Another thermodynamic relation allows to estimate the critical field $H_C$ since for $T=0^{8}$:

$$
\mathrm{(V_n-V_s)/V_n=-\frac{1}{8\pi} H_C^2 \kappa(\gamma_e+2dlnT_C/dlnV)/3}
$$

where$\gamma_e$ is the electronic Grüneisen parameter, defined by$^{12}$$\gamma_e=\beta_eV/\kappa C_e=$ $\mathrm{dlnN(E_F)/dlnV}$. Using the low temperature data of the specific heat $C_e=\gamma T$ ($\gamma=24.9$ mJ/Mole K$^{2}$ $^{9}$) and of the volume thermal expansion coefficient $\beta_e=3\gamma_{\alpha}T$ we find:

$$
\gamma_e=\mathrm{dlnN(E_F)/dlnV=-(2.5 \pm 0.2)}
$$

Note that $N(E_F)$ is the electron-phonon-enhanced density of states. With increasing pressure $N(E_F)$ becomes larger. This is in agreement with the strong volume dependence of $N(E_F)$ caused by vacancies . By using our value of $\gamma_e$ and since $\mathrm{(V_n-V_s)/V_n=1.86\cdot10^{-6}}$ (see Fig.1) we find $H_C=1130$ G which is again in good agreement with the value calculated from specific heat measurements $H_C=960$ G$^{9}$. Since at $T=T_C^{11}$:

$$
\mathrm{dH_C/dp=4\pi \Delta \beta^{s-n}(dH_C/dT)^{-1}}
$$

we get with $\mathrm{dH_C/dT=-307\ G/K}$ (using Rutgers formula $^{11}$and the specific heat data of $^{9}$) at $T=T_C$:

$$
\mathrm{dH_C/dp=4.18\cdot10^{-7}G/Pa}.
$$

This means that it is possible to increase $H_C$ (as well as $H_{C2}$) by pressure (1 GPa increases $H_C$ by about $37\%$). We now want to determine the phononic

Grüneisen parameters$^{12}$ $\gamma_{ph}=\beta_{ph}V/\kappa C_{ph}=$ $-\mathrm{dln\theta/dlnV}$. Using the low temperature data of $\gamma_{ph}=3\beta_{\alpha}T$ and $C_{ph}=\beta_C$ T$^3$ ($\beta_C=$ 1.2 mJ/Mole K$^{4}$ $^{9}$) we find

$$
\gamma_{ph}=4 \pm 0.1
$$

A similar value of $\gamma_{ph}$ was found by Eiling $^{7}$ and indicates a large pressure (volume) dependence of $\theta$. With increasing pressure $\theta$ becomes larger, which is the normal behavior observed in all metals.

However,one would expect a decreasing Debye temperature with increasing pressure, since $T_C$ increases in our case$^{13}$.

The total Grüneisenparameter $\gamma_G$ is defined by $\gamma_G=\beta V/\kappa C$, where $\beta$ is the total volume expansion coefficient, C the total specific heat and $\kappa$ the adiabatic compressibility$^{12}$. In the Born-Oppenheimer approximation $\gamma_G$ may be expressed as $\gamma_G=(\gamma_eC_e+\gamma_{ph}C_{ph})/C_V$ where $C_V=C_e+C_{ph}$. Note that in the high temperature limit $2\gamma_G$ is approximately equal to $-\mathrm{dln<\omega^2>/dlnV^{12}}$, where $<\omega^2>$ is the average of the squared phonon frequencies.

At 10 K $\gamma_G$ is equal to 2.69 and at room temperature $\gamma_G(300)=(2 \pm 0.05)$ ($\beta=3\alpha=43.5\cdot10^{-6}$ K$^{-1}$;$C_V=173$ J/Mole K $^{3}$).

We now have determined all the necessary parameters in order to discuss the pressure (volume) dependence of $T_C$ on the basis of the modified McMillan equation$^{13}$:

$$
\mathrm{T_C=\frac{\sqrt{<\omega^2>}}{1.2}\cdot exp(-\frac{1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^+(1+0.62\lambda)})} \qquad /4/
$$

By using $\sqrt{<\omega^2>}=0.8\cdot\theta^{16}$ and $\mu^+=0.10$ we find $\lambda=0.77$ in good agreement with the value deduced by magnetic measurements ($\lambda=0.77)^4$.

The relation between $\lambda$ and $\eta$ is given by$^{13}$:

$$
\lambda=\eta/M<\omega^2> \qquad /5/
$$

where $M$ is an ionic mass and $n=N(O)<I^{2}>$.
$<I^{2}>$ is the average of the squared electron-phonon matrix element $^{13}$ and
$N(O)$, defined by $N(O)=N(E_{F})/(1+\lambda)$, is the bare density of states. Roughly speaking $\eta$ describes the strength of the electron-ion-interaction.

The effective Coulomb potential
$\mu^{+}$ is equal to $^{13}$

$$
\mu^{+}=\mu /\left(1+\mu \ln \left(E_{F} / \hbar \omega_{O}\right)\right) \qquad /6/
$$

where $\mu$ is the screened Coulomb potential and $\omega_{0}$ the maximum frequency.

Since we do not know the volume dependence of $\langle I^{2}\rangle$, we use the derivatives of the equations $/ 4 /$ and $/ 6 /$ to calculate the value of $d \ln \lambda / d \ln V$:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{C}}}{\mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}}=2.15 \frac{\mathrm{d} \ln \lambda}{\mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}}-0.7 \frac{\mathrm{d} \ln \mu^{+}}{\mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}}-\gamma_{\mathrm{G}}(300) / / 7 /
$$

With $E_{F}=28900 K$ (free electron modell,
$m=4.8 m_{e}, N / V=6 \cdot 10^{21} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}^{4}$) we obtain:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} \ln \mu^{+}}{\mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}}=0.537 \gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^{+}-\mu^{+}\left(\gamma_{\mathrm{G}}(300)-\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^{+}\right) \qquad /8/
$$

Note that $\gamma_{e}^{+}$, the bare electronic Grüneisen parameter, is related to $\gamma_{e}$ by

$$
\gamma_{e}^{+}=\gamma_{e}-(\lambda /(1+\lambda)) \mathrm{d} \ln \lambda / \mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V} \qquad /9/
$$

Inserting $/ 8 /$ into $/ 7 /$ gives $d \ln \lambda / d \ln V=$
-5.4, i.e. $\lambda$ increases with increasing pressure. By using this value we find from $/ 9 /$ and $/ 8 / \gamma_{e}^{+}=-0.15$ and
$d \ln \mu^{+} / d \ln V=-0.3$, i.e. the volume (pressure) dependence of $N(O)$ and $\mu^{+}$ is very small compared with that of $N(E_{F})$ and
$\lambda$, respectively.

From $/ 5 /$ we obtain:

$$
\mathrm{d} \ln \lambda / \mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}=\mathrm{d} \ln \eta / \mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}+2 \gamma_{\mathrm{G}}(300) \qquad / 10 /
$$

and thus $d \ln \eta / d \ln V=-9.4$. With this value and that of $\gamma_{e}^{+}$ we find that
$\mathrm{d} \ln \langle\mathrm{I}^{2}\rangle / \mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}=-9.25$.

This result is in agreement with the literature $^{13}$, where the volume dependence of $T_{C}$ seems to be caused more by large $d \ln \lambda / d \ln V$ than by small
$\mathrm{d} \ln \langle\omega^{2}\rangle / \mathrm{d} \ln \mathrm{V}$. Note that the volume dependence of $\langle I^{2}\rangle$ is dominant compared with that of $N(O)$. Summarizing we can say that $La_{3} ~S_{4}$ shows a large increase of $T_{C}$ under pressure due to an increase of $\lambda$ but also an increase of the phonon frequencies, i.e. the increase of $T_{C}$ is not accompanied by a phonon softening.

## Conclusion
The thermal expansion of $La_{3} ~S_{4}$ shows a second order phase transformation at $T_{C}$. Using these data, the pressure dependence of $T_{C}$ and $H_{C}$ calculated by thermodynamic relations, is in good agreement with the values known from literature.
The electronic, phononic and total Grüneisen parameters show a large pressure (volume) dependence of the density of states and of the phonon spectrum of
La3S4. With these Grüneisen parameters we could show that the strong positive pressure coefficient of $T_{C}$ is due to an increase of $\lambda$ and of the phonon frequencies, whereas the pressure dependence of $\mu^{+}$ is negligible.
