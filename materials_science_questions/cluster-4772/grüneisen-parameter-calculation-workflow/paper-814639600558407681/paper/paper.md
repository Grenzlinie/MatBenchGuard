# Temperature Dependence of Thermal Expansion for Geophysical Minerals

Zheng-Hua Fang¹

Received: 28 October 2014 / Accepted: 6 April 2015
© Springer Science+Business Media New York 2015

Abstract A simple and straightforward method for evaluating and predicting the volume and volumetric thermal expansivity for geophysical minerals at high temperatures is developed in this paper based on the approximations that the product of the thermal expansion coefficient and the isothermal bulk modulus as well as the isothermal bulk modulus are both linearly dependent with temperature. The tests on four geophysical minerals (MgO, CaO, Al₂O₃, and Mg₂SiO₄) lend strong support to the validity of this method. The analyses and comparisons presented here demonstrate that this method is far better than similar models given by earlier workers.

Keywords Anderson–Gruneisen parameter · Geophysical mineral · Isothermal bulk modulus · Thermal expansion

## 1 Introduction

A precise knowledge of the temperature dependence of the thermal expansion is of central importance for understanding the thermodynamic behavior of solids at high temperatures [1–3]. Experimental data on the temperature dependence of the thermal expansion for numerous geophysical minerals have been collected by Anderson et al. [2,3]. Though the data for these minerals are available at temperatures much higher than the Debye temperature, these temperatures are still lower than the corresponding values of melting temperature [3]. At present, the theoretical approaches based on interatomic potential models are still far from complete; therefore, it is desirable to

develop a simple and straightforward method for evaluating and predicting the thermal expansion for geophysical minerals at high temperatures where the experimental data are scarce or not available. Various efforts [2–10] have been made in this regard to investigate the thermal expansion of minerals. In these investigations some thermo- dynamic assumptions or approximations have to be taken. One of the most frequently used assumptions is on the feature of the product of the thermal expansion coeffi- cient $\alpha$ and the isothermal bulk modulus $B_{T}$. On the basis of the assumptions that the product $\alpha B_{T}$ remains constant, as is equivalent to the assumption $(\partial \alpha / \partial T)_{V}=0$, as well as $B_{T}$ depends linearly on temperature, Anderson et al. [2] obtained the fol lowing relationship for the temperature dependence of the thermal expansivity at high temperatures:

$$
\alpha=\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{r}}}{1-\delta_{\mathrm{r}} \alpha_{\mathrm{r}}\left(T-T_{\mathrm{r}}\right)}, \tag{1}
$$

where $\delta_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{r}}$ are, respectively, the values of the isothermal Anderson–Gruneisen parameter and the thermal expansivity both at the reference temperature $T_{\mathrm{r}}$. Equation 1 was used by Pal et al. [7] for the determination of thermal expansion coefficients for four non-ionic minerals (Mg₂SiO₄, Al₂O₃, Grossular garnet, and Pyrope garnet); the calculated results obtained from it show poor agreement with the experimental data [2] (the “experimental data” quoted in [7] are wrong). Wang and Reeber [5] found that the product of $\alpha B_{T} V$ approaches a constant at high temperature. On the basis of this approximation, Fang [10] obtained two relations for the variation of the volume and thermal expansivity with temperature. However, it has been found that, though the two relations are suitable for describing the isobaric thermal expansion of MgO and CaO [10] at high temperatures, they are not suitable for other non-ionic minerals, as seems the same case as for other similar models suggested in the literature.

The purpose of the present study is to develop a simple and straightforward method for predicting the high-temperature thermal expansion for a variety of geophysical minerals. The method of analysis is given in Sect. 2. The validity of this method is tested on four geophysical minerals, viz., MgO, CaO, Mg₂SiO₄, and Al₂O₃ at high temperatures, and the results thus obtained are presented and discussed in Sect. 3.

## 2 Method of Analysis

The product $\alpha B_{T}$ is an important thermodynamic quantity, as is the slope for the curve of thermal pressure versus temperature. The values of $\alpha B_{T}$, obtained from the measured data of the thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha$ and the isothermal bulk modulus $B_{T}$, for many minerals at high temperatures have been presented in [2,3]. The plot of $\alpha B_{T}$ versus $T/\varTheta$ ($\varTheta$ is the Debye temperature) for six minerals (MgO, CaO, Al₂O₃, Mg₂SiO₄, Grossular garnet, and Pyrope garnet) based on these data shows (see Fig. 2.5 in [3]) that, at $T < \varTheta$, the values of $\alpha B_{T}$ for all of these minerals increase with temperature significantly; however, at $T > \varTheta$, the variations of $\alpha B_{T}$ with temperature for them, though small, are quite different. At $T > \varTheta$, $\alpha B_{T}$ decreases slowly with temperature in the cases of MgO and CaO, but increases slowly with temperature in the cases of Al₂O₃ and Mg₂SiO₄. Therefore, the following linear relationship for the

![](./images/814639600558407681_2.jpg)

variations of $\alpha B_T$ with temperature for minerals at high temperatures is taken in this paper as

$$
\alpha B_{T}=\alpha_{\mathrm{r}} B_{\mathrm{r}}+k\left(T-T_{\mathrm{r}}\right) \tag{2}
$$

where $k$ is a constant depending on the mineral under study. Then, we expect that, at $T>\Theta$, the values of $k$ would be negative in the cases of MgO and CaO, but positive in the cases of $\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{Mg}_{2} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}$. The other linear relationship, as is most frequently used in the literature [3], for the variations of $B_T$ with temperature is also taken here, and is written as

$$
B_{T}=B_{\mathrm{r}}+\beta\left(T-T_{\mathrm{r}}\right), \tag{3}
$$

where $\beta$, the first temperature derivative of $B_T$, is a constant. Thus, we get simply the relation for the temperature-dependent volumetric thermal expansion coefficient in the following form:

$$
\alpha=\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{r}} B_{\mathrm{r}}+k\left(T-T_{\mathrm{r}}\right)}{B_{\mathrm{r}}+\beta\left(T-T_{\mathrm{r}}\right)} \tag{4}
$$

In order to evaluate the high-temperature thermal expansion with Eq. 4, we need to determine the variation of the volume with temperature. Using the definition of $\alpha$,

$$
\alpha=\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_{P} \tag{5}
$$

in Eq. 4 and integrating it over temperature at ambient pressure (i.e., $P=0$), we get the following expression for the variation of volume with temperature:

$$
\frac{V}{V_{\mathrm{r}}}=\left[1+\frac{\beta}{B_{\mathrm{r}}}\left(T-T_{\mathrm{r}}\right)\right]^{\frac{B_{\mathrm{r}}\left(\alpha_{\mathrm{r}} \beta-k\right)}{\beta^{2}}} \exp \left[\frac{k}{\beta}\left(T-T_{\mathrm{r}}\right)\right] \tag{6}
$$

The isothermal Anderson-Gruneisen parameter is given by

$$
\delta_{T}=-\frac{1}{\alpha B_{T}}\left(\frac{\partial B_{T}}{\partial T}\right)_{P}, \tag{7}
$$

In terms of Eqs. 3 and 7, we may write the first temperature derivative of $B_T$ as

$$
\beta=\left(\frac{\partial B_{T}}{\partial T}\right)_{P}=-\alpha B_{T} \delta_{T},
$$

which means the value of $\beta$ that is a constant is given by

$$
\beta=-\alpha_{\mathrm{r}} B_{\mathrm{r}} \delta_{\mathrm{r}} \tag{8}
$$

![](./images/814639600558407681_3.jpg)

Hence, using Eqs. 4 and 6 for the temperature dependence of the thermal expansion,
we are required to know the values of $\alpha_{\mathrm{r}}$, $B_{\mathrm{r}}$, $\delta_{\mathrm{r}}$, and $k$.

Equation 2, the assumption that the product $\alpha B_T$ is linearly dependent on temperature, is taken in this work. If setting $k = 0$, Eq. 2 denotes the most frequently used assumption that $\alpha B_T$ is a constant independent of temperature; then, Eq. 4 is reduced to Eq. 1 in terms of Eq. 8, and Eq. 6 is written as

$$
\frac{V}{V_{\mathrm{r}}}=\left[1-\alpha_{\mathrm{r}} \delta_{\mathrm{r}}\left(T-T_{r}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{r}}}} \tag{10}
$$

The values of the volume and volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of the four geophysical minerals have been calculated in the same ranges of temperature as mentioned above with Eqs. 1 and 10. To save space, the root-mean-square deviations (RMSD) between the calculated values obtained from the two different models and the corresponding experimental data are presented in Table 6 for comparison. From Table 6, we can clearly see that the calculated results given by Eqs. 4 and 6, as suggested in this work, are overall in better agreement with the experimental data than those given by Eqs. 1 and 10. It is pertinent to point out here that the variations of $\alpha B_T$ for these minerals from their reference temperatures up to the maximum observed temperatures are [2,3] actually small: the values of $\alpha B_T$ for MgO and CaO decrease,

![](./images/814639600558407681_6.jpg)

respectively, $5.1\ \%$ and $2.0\ \%$, whereas for $\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{Mg}_{2} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}$, they increase, respectively, $5.7\ \%$ and $5.0\ \%$. However, the comparisons presented in Table 6 demonstrate that the validity of the equation, yielded from the relationship for the variations of $\alpha B_{T}$, for describing the thermal expansion of minerals at high temperatures depends strongly on the proper choice of the used assumption, though the differences between those given by different assumptions on $\alpha B_{T}$ are small. This, obviously, lends strong support to the reasonableness of the assumptions taken in this work.

Recently, Srivastava [9] presented the following relation for the temperature dependence of the volume expansion for the same four geophysical minerals considered here:

$$
\frac{V}{V_{0}}=1-\frac{1}{m} \ln \left[1-d \alpha_{0} \delta_{0}\left(T-T_{0}\right)\right] \tag{11}
$$

where $m$ and $d$ are the fitting parameters, and $\alpha_{0}$ and $\delta_{0}$ are, respectively, the values of $\alpha$ and $\delta_{T}$ at room temperature, $T_{0}=300 \mathrm{~K}$. Equation 11 is derived based on the following two assumptions:

$$
\ln B_{T}=m\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}\right)+\text { constant } \tag{12}
$$

$$
B_{T}=B_{0}-d \alpha_{0} B_{0} \delta_{0}\left(T-T_{0}\right), \tag{13}
$$

In view of Eqs. 11 and 5, we can get the following expression for the temperature-dependent volumetric thermal expansion coefficient:

$$
\alpha=\frac{d \alpha_{0} \delta_{0}}{m\left[1-d \alpha_{0} \delta_{0}\left(T-T_{0}\right)\right]}\left\{1-\frac{1}{m} \ln \left[1-d \alpha_{0} \delta_{0}\left(T-T_{0}\right)\right]\right\}^{-1} \tag{14}
$$

Using the reported values [9] of the input parameters, the values of the volume and volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of the four geophysical minerals have been calculated at high temperatures with Eqs. 11 and 14. Similarly, only the RMSDs between the calculated values obtained from them and the corresponding experimental data for the four minerals in the same ranges of temperature considered in this work are presented in Table 6 for comparison. From Table 6, we can clearly see that, as far as the agreement with the experimental data is concerned, the calculated results given by Eqs. 11 and 14, are much less than that given by Eqs. 4 and 6, the relations suggested in this work, and even less than that by Eqs. 1 and 10, as given earlier by Anderson [2], though there is one more fitting parameter in Eqs. 11 and 14 as compared to Eqs. 4 and 6.

In addition, it should also be pointed out that the method suggested by Srivastava [9] for the derivations of Eqs. 11 and 14 would lead to two inconsistent relationships. One is the following relationship obtained from Eqs. 11, 13, and 14:

$$
\alpha B_{T} V=\alpha_{0} B_{0} V_{0} \frac{d \delta_{0}}{m} \tag{15}
$$

which means that the factor $d \delta_{0} / m$ should be equal to unity. However, $d$ and $m$ are taken as the fitting parameters in [9] and the values of the factor for $\mathrm{MgO}, \mathrm{CaO}, \mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$,

![](./images/814639600558407681_7.jpg)

and $Mg_2SiO_4$ are, in turn, 1.244, 1.194, 1.454, and 1.252. The other one is yielded from Eq. 13, taken in [9] for the variations of $B_T$ with temperature, and Eq. 7, the definition of the isothermal Anderson–Gruneisen parameter, written as

$$
\alpha B_T \delta_T = d\alpha_0 B_0 \delta_0 \tag{16}
$$

which means that $d$ should also be equal to unity. Yet, the values of the fitting parameter $d$ are not equal to unity, they are [9] 1.130, 0.975, 1.360, and 1.120 for the four minerals in their order mentioned above.

In summary, a simple and straightforward method for evaluating and predicting the thermal expansion for geophysical minerals at high temperatures is presented in this paper based on the approximations that the product of the thermal expansion coefficient and the isothermal bulk modulus as well as the isothermal bulk modulus are both linearly dependent with temperature. The tests on four geophysical minerals ($MgO, CaO, Al_2O_3$, and $Mg_2SiO_4$) lend strong support to the validity of this method. The analyses and comparisons presented here demonstrate that the method is better than similar models given by earlier workers.
