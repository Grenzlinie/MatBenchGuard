**High-temperature heat capacity and lattice anharmonicity of LiGaO₂**

H. Neumann*

EOMC, Fritz-Siemon-Strasse 26/111, 04347 Leipzig, Germany

Received 14 October 2003, accepted 19 December 2003
Published online 15 March 2004

Key words heat capacity, lattice anharmonicity, Grüneisen functions.
PACS 65.40.Ba, 81.05.Je

The first three even moments of the phonon density of states of $LiGaO_2$ and the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity at constant volume of the compound are estimated from experimental heat capacity data. The effect of lattice anharmonicity is discussed in terms of perturbation theory. The temperature dependent principal Grüneisen functions of $LiGaO_2$ are calculated. The results obtained for $LiGaO_2$ are compared with existing literature for other ternary lithium compounds.

## 1 Introduction
Recently, the ternary compound $LiGaO_2$, originally considered as a promising refractory piezoelectric transducer material [1], has attracted renewed attention for application as substrate material for the growth of thick free standing GaN epitaxial layers [2,3] and in combination with ZnO as wide-gap semiconductor as insulator material for the realisation of devices based on field-effect carrier modulation effects [4]. Because of its orthorhombic structure [5,6] the compound has also been found to be well suited as host material for the investigation of the electronic properties of transition metal ions [7]. All these application perspectives are essentially based on a reliable knowledge and understanding of the related fundamental properties of the compound. The elastic, piezoelectric, dielectric and optical properties of $LiGaO_2$ have been investigated in detail [8-10]. The lattice vibrational characteristics of the compound have also been studied by both infrared reflectivity and Raman scattering measurements on appropriately oriented single crystal specimens [10-13], and a simple phenomenological force constant model considering interaction between nearest neighbours has been proposed to describe the main trends in the frequencies of the zone-centre optical vibrational modes [14]. However, although anharmonic interactions are expected to play an important role, their influence on the lattice vibrations and on the vibrational thermal properties of the compound has not been investigated in detail so far. Indeed, measurements of the three independent linear thermal expansion coefficients $\alpha_a$, $\alpha_b$ and $\alpha_c$, respectively, of the lattice parameters $a$, $b$ and $c$ of $LiGaO_2$ between room temperature and 1100 K [15] using the X-ray diffraction method have shown that they remain temperature dependent up to the highest temperature and follow the relation

$$
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i 0}+\alpha_{i 1} T+\alpha_{i 2} T^{2}. \tag{1}
$$

Then, if lattice anharmonicity is considered in terms of perturbation theory and the potential energy of the lattice is approximated by a power series in the displacements of the atoms from their equilibrium positions, it

is evident that terms up to the sixth order in the displacements must be taken into account in order to obtain the complete expressions for the coefficients $\alpha_{ik}$ in equation (1) [16,17]. At the first sight, the influence of lattice anharmonicity on the heat capacity seems to be much less pronounced. Experimental determinations of the molar heat capacity at constant pressure of $LiGaO_2$ in the temperature range between 200 and 700 K [18] gave a value of $C_p \approx 103$ J/mol K at 700 K which does not noticeably exceed the value of $C_{Vh}=12R=99.77$ J/mol K, the upper bound of the heat capacity at constant volume $C_V(T)$ in the harmonic approximation for $T \to \infty$ [16]. Furthermore, comparing this result with heat capacity data reported for other $LiB^{III}C_2^{VI}$ compounds it is interesting to note that a similar tendency has been observed for $LiAlO_2$ [19,20] and $LiInS_2$ [21] whereas the heat capacities of $LiInSe_2$ [21] and $LiInTe_2$ [22] clearly exceed the harmonic limit at temperatures only slightly above room temperature. Based on an analysis of the reported experimental heat capacity data of $LiGaO_2$ [18] it is shown in the present communication that the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity of this compound is comparable with that found in $LiInTe_2$ [22]. Furthermore, the Grüneisen functions of the compound are calculated.

## 2 Results and discussion

### 2.1 Heat capacity

In order to correctly estimate the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity measured experimentally the harmonic part of the heat capacity must be calculated first for the temperature range considered. In principle, this can be done by calculating the heat capacity at constant volume in the harmonic approximation $C_{Vh}(T)$ from measured phonon dispersion curves $\omega_s(\mathbf{q})$ or from the normalised phonon density of states $g(\omega)$ using the standard relations [23]

$$
C_{V h}(T)=\sum_{s, \mathbf{q}} E\left(\hbar \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q}) / k T\right)=12 R \int_{0}^{\omega_{m}} g(\omega) \frac{(\hbar \omega / k T)^{2} \exp (\hbar \omega / k T)}{[\exp (\hbar \omega / k T)-1]^{2}} d \omega,
\tag{2}
$$

where $E(x)$ is the Einstein function, the sum is over all phonon branches $s$ and wave vectors $\mathbf{q}$ in the first Brillouin zone, $R$ and $k$ are the gas constant and the Boltzmann constant, respectively, and $\omega_m$ is the maximum frequency of the fundamental phonon normal mode spectrum. Since the phonon density of states or phonon dispersion curves of $LiGaO_2$ have not been reported in the literature so far the only alternative possibility to calculate $C_{Vh}(T)$ is to use the method of spectral moments [23] following the approach proposed and successfully employed by Barron et al. [24] in analysing the heat capacity of several alkali halides. Accordingly, the temperature dependence of the heat capacity at constant volume is described within the Debye model,

$$
C_{V h}(T)=12 R F\left(\Theta_{D} / T\right)
\tag{3}
$$

where $F(x)$ is the Debye function and $\Theta_D$ an effective temperature dependent Debye temperature which varies between the low-temperature value $\Theta_D(T \to 0)=\Theta_0$ usually determined from heat capacity data in the temperature range T < 20 K and the high-temperature limit

$$
\Theta_{D}(T \rightarrow \infty)=\Theta_{\infty}=\frac{h}{k}\left(\frac{5 \mu_{2}}{3}\right)^{1 / 2},
\tag{4}
$$

where $h$ is the Planck constant and $\mu_2$ the second spectral moment of the phonon density of states as defined in [24]. For temperatures $T>\Theta_0 / 2 \pi$ the effective Debye temperature can be expanded in the rapidly converging series [24]

© 2004 WILEY-VCH Verlag GmbH & Co. KGaA, Weinheim

$$
\Theta_{D}^{2}(T)=\Theta_{\infty}^{2}\left[1-A\left(\frac{\Theta_{\infty}}{T}\right)^{2}+B\left(\frac{\Theta_{\infty}}{T}\right)^{4}-\ldots\right].
$$

The constants $A$ and $B$ are given by

$$
A=\frac{3}{100}\left(\frac{\mu_{4}}{\mu_{2}^{2}}-\frac{25}{21}\right),
$$

$$
B=\frac{1}{1400}\left(\frac{\mu_{6}}{\mu_{2}^{3}}-\frac{125}{81}-100 A\right)
$$

where $\mu_{4}$ and $\mu_{6}$ are the next two even spectral moments of the phonon density of states. Therefore, if experimental $C_{V}(T)$ data is available for the temperature range $T>\Theta_{0} / 2 \pi$ and if there is a sufficiently wide temperature range where anharmonicity is negligible and thus $C_{V}(T)=C_{V h}(T)$, relations (3) to (7) can be used to determine the moments $\mu_{2}$ to $\mu_{6}$ of the phonon density of states which then allows to extrapolate $C_{V h}(T)$ to any higher temperature.

Before starting an analysis of the experimental heat capacity data of $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$ from [18] it is first necessary to ensure that the temperature range from 180 to 700 K covered in the experiments satisfies the condition $T>\Theta_{0} / 2 \pi$ required for the validity of relation (5). Because no low-temperature heat capacity measurements and, therefore, also no experimental value for $\Theta_{0}$ of $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$ have been published in the literature the elastic Debye temperature $\Theta_{\mathrm{el}}$ was estimated in the approximation proposed by Anderson [25] using the elastic constants from [9]. The obtained value of $\Theta_{\mathrm{el}} \approx \Theta_{0} \approx 540 \mathrm{~K}$ leads to the requirement $T>90 \mathrm{~K}$ for the validity of the expansion (5) which is fulfilled by the experimental data. Next it was necessary to calculate the heat capacity at constant volume $C_{V}(T)$ from the experimental $C_{p}(T)$ data given in [18] by means of the relation [23]

$$
C_{V}(T)=C_{p}(T)-\frac{\alpha_{V}^{2} V_{m} T}{\kappa}
$$

where $\alpha_{V}=\alpha_{a}+\alpha_{b}+\alpha_{c}$ is the volume expansion coefficient, $V_{m}$ the molar volume, and $\kappa$ the isothermal volume compressibility. This was done using the temperature dependent thermal expansion coefficients from [15], the molar volume from [6], and a value of $\kappa=16.6 \mathrm{TPa}^{-1}$ for the compressibility calculated from the elastic compliance constants given in [9]. Finally, from the $C_{V}(T)$ values obtained in this manner the temperature dependent effective Debye temperature $\Theta_{D}(T)$ was calculated by means of equation (3).

![](./images/812366532297359361_1.jpg)

Fig. 1 Plot of $\Theta_{D}^{2}$ as a function of $1 / T^{2}$ (the data points are experimental values, the full curve is calculated with relation (5)).

The resulting dependence of $\Theta_{D}^{2}(T)$ on $1/T^{2}$ is shown in Fig. 1. Starting from low temperatures the effective Debye temperature increases monotonously from 180 to about 270 K. At T > 270 K a rapid decrease of $\Theta_{D}$ sets in which is characteristic of the onset and continuously increasing role of lattice anharmonicity effects with increasing temperature [24,26]. Thus, it is justified to suppose that the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity is comparably small at temperatures below about 250 K and can be neglected in a first approximation. Then, fitting relation (5) to the experimental data points in the temperature range below 250 K (full curve in Fig. 1) the Debye temperature $\Theta_{\infty}$ and the spectral moments $\mu_{2}$, $\mu_{4}$ and $\mu_{6}$ of the phonon density of states can be determined. Taking into account that the original $C_{p}(T)$ data have been measured with an accuracy of $\Delta C_{p}= \pm 1$ J/mol K [18] and considering the additional error of about $\pm 0.2$ J/mol K introduced in calculating $C_{V}(T)$ by means of relation (8) the value for the Debye temperature is determined to $\Theta_{\infty}=814 \pm 7$ K and the spectral moments are $\mu_{2}=1.72 \times 10^{26} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$, $\mu_{4}=5.33 \times 10^{52} \mathrm{~s}^{-4}$ and $\mu_{6}=1.91 \times 10^{79} \mathrm{~s}^{-6}$ with an accuracy better than $|\Delta \mu_{i}/\mu_{i}|=$ 5%. Using these parameters the harmonic contribution $C_{Vh}(T)$ to the heat capacity can be calculated for the temperature range T > 250 K. The result is shown in Fig. 2 together with the experimental $C_{p}(T)$ data from [18] and the calculated with relation (8) heat capacity at constant volume $C_{V}(T)$.

![](./images/812366532297359361_2.jpg)

Fig. 2 Temperature dependence of the heat capacity at constant pressure $C_{p}(T)$ from [18], of the calculated with relation (8) heat capacity at constant volume $C_{V}(T)$, and of the harmonic contribution to the heat capacity $C_{Vh}(T)$ of $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$.

The temperature dependent anharmonic contribution $\Delta C_{V}(T)=C_{V}(T)-C_{Vh}(T)$ to the heat capacity at constant volume is presented in Fig. 3. Considering the data points it is obvious that, in principle, the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity $\Delta C_{V}(T)$ can be well approximated by a linear function of temperature. However, this leads to the result (Fig. 3) that non-vanishing $\Delta C_{V}(T)$ values appear abruptly at about T ≥ 180 K whereas $\Delta C_{V}(T) \equiv 0$ below 180 K which cannot be explained by any of the existing theories of lattice anharmonicity in crystals [16,27,28]. Moreover, from the theoretical point of view, the nonlinear temperature dependence (1) found for the principal thermal expansion coefficients of the compound [15] implies also a nonlinear temperature dependence of the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity [16,17]. Indeed, in analysing $\Delta C_{V}(T)$ data of some alkali halides [17] and of several $\mathrm{CuB}_{2}^{\mathrm{III}} \mathrm{C}_{2}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ compounds with chalcopyrite structure [29] it has been found that the experimental results can only be described by an equation of the general form

$$
\Delta C_{V}(T)=12 R\left(a_{1} T+a_{2} T^{2}+a_{3} T^{3}+\ldots\right), \tag{9}
$$

where it is in most cases sufficient to include the first two terms. This relation follows from perturbation theory and terms up to sixth order in the displacements of the atoms from their equilibrium positions must be taken into account in the expansion of the potential energy of the lattice in order to correctly describe a quadratic temperature dependence of $\Delta C_{V}(T)$ according to equation (9) [16,30].

![](./images/812366532297359361_3.jpg)

Fig. 3 Anharmonic contribution $\Delta C_V(T) = C_V(T) - C_{Vh}(T)$ to the heat capacity at constant volume of $\text{LiGaO}_2$.

However, relation (9) is only a good approximation at sufficiently high temperatures in the range $\Theta_\infty < T < (2/3)T_M$ [16,30] where $T_M$ is the melting temperature being equal to $T_M = 1868$ K in $\text{LiGaO}_2$ [31]. Therefore, because of the high value of $\Theta_\infty = 814$ K determined for $\text{LiGaO}_2$ from the present analysis of the temperature dependence of the effective Debye temperature (Fig. 1) it is obvious that relation (9) is unsuited to describe the results presented in Fig. 3 which are limited to the temperature range $T \leq 700$ K $< \Theta_\infty$.

An expression for $\Delta C_V(T)$ for the intermediate temperature range well below $\Theta_\infty$ can be derived by relaxing the harmonic approximation in calculating the heat capacity at constant volume. The required temperature induced variation of mode frequencies at constant volume $[\partial \ln \omega_s(\mathbf{q})/\partial T]_V$ cannot be directly determined experimentally but can be calculated from experimental data for the temperature dependence of mode frequencies measured under constant pressure $p$, $[\partial \ln \omega_s(\mathbf{q})/\partial T]_p$, and the dependence of the mode frequencies on hydrostatic pressure measured at constant temperature, $[\partial \ln \omega_s(\mathbf{q})/\partial p]_T$, by means of the relation [32]

$$
\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial T}\right]_{V}=\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial T}\right]_{p}+\frac{\alpha_{V}}{\kappa}\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial p}\right]_{T}. \tag{10}
$$

This equation can be written in the form

$$
\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial T}\right]_{V}=\left\{\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial \ln V}\right]_{p}-\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial \ln V}\right]_{T}\right\} \alpha_{V}=\left\{[\gamma(s, \mathbf{q})]_{T}-[\gamma(s, \mathbf{q})]_{p}\right\} \alpha_{V} \tag{11}
$$

if mode Grüneisen parameters $\gamma(s,\mathbf{q})$ are introduced which are defined as [33]

$$
\gamma(s, \mathbf{q})=-\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial \ln V}. \tag{12}
$$

Two trends are obvious from relation (11). Firstly, mode Grüneisen parameters are usually of the order of 1 [33] and the volume expansion coefficient $\alpha_V$ of $\text{LiGaO}_2$ is of the order of $10^{-5}$ K$^{-1}$ [15]. Consequently, the relative change of mode frequencies is small, and it can be expected that the temperature variation of the mode frequency can be well represented by a rapidly converging power series expansion in the temperature $T$. Secondly, it follows from the nonlinear temperature dependence of the thermal expansion coefficient [15] that the mode frequency will also be a nonlinear function of temperature. Then, taking into account that the mode Grüneisen parameters can also depend on temperature the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity at constant volume becomes

$$
\Delta C_{V}(T)=C_{V}(T)-C_{V h}(T)=\sum_{s, \mathbf{q}}\left\{E(\hbar \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q}) / k T)\left[b_{1}(s, \mathbf{q}) T+b_{2}(s, \mathbf{q}) T^{2}+\ldots\right]\right\}. \tag{13}
$$

© 2004 WILEY-VCH Verlag GmbH & Co. KGaA, Weinheim

In principle, $\Delta C_V(T)$ can be calculated with (13) provided that all the vibrational mode frequencies $\omega_s(\mathbf{q})$ and the corresponding mode Grüneisen parameters $\gamma(s,\mathbf{q})$ are known, for instance from accurate inelastic neutron scattering experiments as a function of pressure and temperature. However, because of the lack of related experimental data for $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$ further approximations are required. In this connection it is interesting to note that Raman scattering studies of the pressure dependence of zone centre vibrational mode frequencies of several $\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{I}} \mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{III}} \mathrm{C}_{2}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ and $\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{II}} \mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{IV}} \mathrm{C}_{2}^{\mathrm{V}}$ compounds with chalcopyrite structure [34-40] and some other ternary chalcogenides [41] gave positive and nearly equal in magnitude mode Grüneisen parameters $[\gamma(s, 0]_T$ for all optical phonon modes. From the few sufficiently detailed investigations of temperature induced frequency shifts of zone-centre phonon modes in ternary chalcogenides [41,42] it follows that the same tendency seems to be true for the mode Grüneisen parameters $[\gamma(s, 0]_p$ of the optical modes. Based on these results it is certainly justified to suppose that, as a first approximation, all these modes have the same mode Grüneisen parameters $[\gamma(s,\mathbf{q}]_T = \gamma_T$ and $[\gamma(s,\mathbf{q})]_p = \gamma_p$ independent of the phonon branch $s$ and the wave vector $\mathbf{q}$. Under these assumptions the anharmonic contribution of these modes to the heat capacity can be approximated by

$$
\Delta C_{V, o p t}(T)=12 R\left[b_{1} T+b_{2} T^{2}+\ldots\right] \int_{\omega_{l}}^{\omega_{m}} g(\omega) \frac{(\hbar \omega / k T)^{2} \exp (\hbar \omega / k T)}{[\exp (\hbar \omega / k T)-1]^{2}} d \omega,
\tag{14}
$$

where $b_1$ and $b_2$ are now constants and $\omega_l$ is the lower cut-off frequency of optical modes having the same Grüneisen parameters. Exceptions are the optical modes with lowest frequency [34-40] and, in particular, the acoustic modes [43-45] exhibiting very small and partly negative mode Grüneisen parameters with a pronounced dependence on the wave vector $\mathbf{q}$ [44,45]. Therefore, the contribution of these modes to $\Delta C_V(T)$ can only be calculated using relation (13) with summation over the frequency range from $\omega=0$ to $\omega=\omega_l$. However, it has been established experimentally that, within the usual experimental accuracy of heat capacity measurements, anharmonicity effects remain negligibly in the temperature range characteristic of thermal excitation of these modes only [22,46,47]. From Fig. 1 and 2 it is evident that this condition is also fulfilled for $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$. Considering this result it can be expected that the error in $\Delta C_V(T)$ is small if $\omega_l$ is replaced by $\omega_l=0$ in equation (14) which leads to

$$
\Delta C_{V}(T)=\left[b_{1} T+b_{2} T^{2}+\ldots\right]_{V h}(T)
\tag{15}
$$

with $C_{V h}(T)$ given by (2). In the limit $T \rightarrow \infty$ with $C_{V h}(T) \rightarrow 12 R$ relation (15) is identical with (9).

![](./images/812366532297359361_4.jpg)

Fig. 4 Temperature dependence of the relative anharmonic contribution $\Delta C_V/C_{Vh}$ to the heat capacity.

Assuming that the Grüneisen parameters of the optical phonon modes in LiGaO₂ follow the same trend as in the chalcopyrite compounds the temperature dependence of the relative anharmonic contribution ΔCᵥ(T)/Cᵥₕ(T) to the heat capacity was calculated from the ΔCᵥ(T) values of Fig. 3 and the Cᵥₕ(T) data of Fig. 2. A plot of ΔCᵥ/CᵥₕT as a function of the temperature T is shown in Fig. 4 with experimental data points which can be well described by relation (15) with coefficients $b_1=(8.0\pm0.2)\times10^{-5}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ and $b_2=(1.19\pm0.03)\times10^{-7}\ \text{K}^{-2}$. These values are of the same order of magnitude as those found in $\text{CuB}^{\text{III}}\text{C}_2^{\text{VI}}$ chalcopyrite compounds [29].

Concluding the discussion the results found here for LiGaO₂ are compared with corresponding data reported for LiInTe₂ [22], the only other member of the $\text{LiB}^{\text{III}}\text{C}_2^{\text{VI}}$ compound family for which an analogous detailed analysis of heat capacity data has been performed. First of all it is interesting to note that the absolute ΔCᵥ(T) values are of the same order of magnitude in both compounds, reaching maximum values close to 10 J/mol K in LiGaO₂ (Fig. 3) and slightly higher values of nearly 15 J/mol K in LiInTe₂ [22]. However, in order to make the results for LiGaO₂ and LiInTe₂ directly comparable with each other it must be taken into account that the fundamental vibrational mode band in LiGaO₂ extends to much higher frequencies than in LiInTe₂ [11,12,48]. Consequently, the degree of thermal excitation of the fundamental vibrational modes at a given temperature differs considerably in both compounds. To eliminate this influencing factor it is common use to consider the heat capacities of compounds within a given family as functions of a reduced temperature, usually $T/\Theta_0$ in the range of low temperatures [46] and $T/\Theta_\infty$ in the range of intermediate and high temperatures [29].

![](./images/812366532297359361_5.jpg)

Fig. 5 Heat capacity at constant pressure $C_p$ of LiGaO₂ (●) and LiInTe₂ (○) and harmonic part of the heat capacity $C_{Vh}$ (full curve) as function of the reduced temperature $T/\Theta_\infty$.

Fig. 5 shows the heat capacity of both compounds as function of the reduced temperature $T/\Theta_\infty$ with $\Theta_\infty=306.8$ K for LiInTe₂ [22] and $\Theta_\infty=814$ K for LiGaO₂. The harmonic heat capacities $C_{Vh}$ of both compounds are found to coincide in the whole temperature range considered, and the experimentally measured heat capacities $C_p$ of both compounds merge with the $C_{Vh}$ curve at temperatures below $T/\Theta_\infty\approx0.3$. Furthermore, it follows from Fig. 5 that clearly distinguishable deviations of $C_p$ from $C_{Vh}$ occur above nearly the same critical normalised temperature of about $T/\Theta_\infty\approx0.35$ in both LiGaO₂ and LiInTe₂. However, it is also obvious from Fig. 5 that in the reduced temperature scale the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity in LiGaO₂ is always larger than in LiInTe₂. These results indicate that in both compounds the influence of anharmonicity on the heat capacity becomes important at about the same thermal excitation level of the fundamental phonon mode spectrum. But in contrast to this common feature it is evident that lattice anharmonicity is stronger in LiGaO₂ than in LiInTe₂.

### 2.2 Grüneisen functions

Mode Grüneisen parameters of LiGaO₂ describing the influence of lattice anharmonicity on the temperature and pressure dependence of vibrational mode frequencies (see equ. (11)) and on other vibrational properties

© 2004 WILEY-VCH Verlag GmbH & Co. KGaA, Weinheim

like thermal expansion, thermal conductivity and heat capacity [27,28,30,33] have not been investigated experimentally so far. In orthorhombic crystals like $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$ the situation is particularly complicated because for symmetry reasons there are three independent mode Grüneisen parameters, defined as [33]

$$
\gamma_{a}(s, \mathbf{q})=-\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial \ln a}\right]_{b, c}, \gamma_{b}(s, \mathbf{q})=-\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial \ln b}\right]_{a, c}, \gamma_{c}(s, \mathbf{q})=-\left[\frac{\partial \ln \omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})}{\partial \ln c}\right]_{a, b},
\tag{16}
$$

which describe mode frequency variations as function of the change of one of the lattice parameters with the two other lattice parameters held constant. Some trends in these individual mode Grüneisen parameters (16) can be deduced from the respective principal Grüneisen functions which are averages of the $\gamma_{i}(s, \mathbf{q})$, defined as

$$
\gamma_{i}=\sum_{s, \mathbf{q}} \gamma_{i}(s, \mathbf{q}) c(s, \mathbf{q}) / \sum_{s, \mathbf{q}} c(s, \mathbf{q}),
\tag{17}
$$

in which the contribution of the $\gamma_{i}(s, \mathbf{q})$ of each phonon normal mode $\omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})$ to the average value $\gamma_{i}$ is weighted by its contribution $c(s, \mathbf{q})$ to the total heat capacity. These macroscopic Grüneisen functions are given by [33]

$$
\gamma_{a}=\left(c_{11} \alpha_{a}+c_{12} \alpha_{b}+c_{13} \alpha_{c}\right) \frac{V_{m}}{C_{V}},
\tag{18a}
$$

$$
\gamma_{b}=\left(c_{12} \alpha_{a}+c_{22} \alpha_{b}+c_{23} \alpha_{c}\right) \frac{V_{m}}{C_{V}},
\tag{18b}
$$

$$
\gamma_{c}=\left(c_{13} \alpha_{a}+c_{23} \alpha_{b}+c_{33} \alpha_{c}\right) \frac{V_{m}}{C_{V}},
\tag{18c}
$$

where the $c_{i j}$ are the isothermal elastic stiffness constants. The volume Grüneisen function $\gamma$ is an average of the $\gamma_{i}$ weighted by the respective isothermal linear compressibilities $\kappa_{i}$,

$$
\gamma=\frac{\gamma_{a} \kappa_{a}+\gamma_{b} \kappa_{b}+\gamma_{c} \kappa_{c}}{\kappa}.
\tag{19}
$$

Using the elastic constants from [9], the thermal expansion coefficients from [15], and the heat capacity at constant volume determined in the present study (Fig. 2) the Grüneisen functions (18) and (19) are calculated for the temperature range from 300 to 700 K and depicted in Fig. 6.

![](./images/812366532297359361_6.jpg)

Fig. 6 Temperature dependence of the principal Grüneisen functions and the volume Grüneisen function of $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$.

As can be seen the principal Grüneisen functions reveal a clearly pronounced anisotropy. Taking into account that all the $\gamma_{i}$ are obtained by the averaging procedure (17) with the same temperature dependent weighting factors it can be concluded that most of the mode Grüneisen parameters (16) are also anisotropic. Furthermore, the volume Grüneisen function $\gamma$ and the principal Grüneisen functions $\gamma_{b}$ and $\gamma_{c}$ exhibit a relatively strong temperature dependence and increase monotonously with increasing temperature whereas $\gamma_{a}$ depends only weakly on temperature and goes through a rather shallow minimum at about 380 K. There are two possible effects that could be the reason for this behaviour. Firstly, it can be argued that the individual mode Grüneisen parameters (12) and (16) depend on temperature with approximately the same general tendency in the temperature variation in case of the parameters $\gamma(s, \mathbf{q})$, $\gamma_{b}(s, \mathbf{q})$, and $\gamma_{c}(s, \mathbf{q})$, but a completely different temperature dependence for the $\gamma_{a}(s, \mathbf{q})$. Secondly, it must be taken into account that, in general, the individual mode Grüneisen parameters vary with the phonon mode frequency $\omega_{s}(\mathbf{q})$. Then, different frequency dependences of the $\gamma_{a}(s, \mathbf{q})$ on the one hand and of the $\gamma(s, \mathbf{q})$, $\gamma_{b}(s, \mathbf{q})$, and $\gamma_{c}(s, \mathbf{q})$ on the other hand could be an other explanation for the differences in the temperature dependence of the macroscopic Grüneisen functions. It would be interesting to dispose of temperature and pressure dependent measurements of the mode frequencies of the compound in order to clarify the role of the latter effect.

## 3 Summary

Temperature dependent heat capacity data of $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$ is analysed using the method of moments in the effective Debye temperature approximation. The first three even spectral moments of the phonon density of states of the compound are determined in this manner and are used to calculate the heat capacity at constant volume in the quasiharmonic approximation at elevated temperature. The temperature dependence of the anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity determined by comparison with the experimental heat capacity data can be explained with a relation derived for intermediate temperatures well below the Debye temperature under several simplifying assumptions concerning the frequency dependence of the Grüneisen parameters of the phonon normal modes. The macroscopic Grüneisen functions exhibit a clearly pronounced temperature dependence and anisotropy. Frequency and temperature dependent anisotropic mode Grüneisen parameters are discussed as possible reasons for this behaviour. Finally, the results found in the present study for $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$ are compared with corresponding experimental data for $\mathrm{LiInTe}_{2}$ reported in the literature. The anharmonic contribution to the heat capacity is of comparable order of magnitude in both compounds but anharmonicity is found to be stronger in $\mathrm{LiGaO}_{2}$.
