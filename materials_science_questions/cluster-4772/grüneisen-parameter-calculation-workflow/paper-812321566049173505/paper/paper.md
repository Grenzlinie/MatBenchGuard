# Thermal Expansion and Bulk Modulus of Cobalt

R. Ramji Rao and A. Ramanand

Department of Physics, Indian Institute of Technology, Madras, India

(Received July 12, 1976)

The ten third-order elastic (TOE) constants of hexagonal cobalt, which has nearly the ideal $c/a$ value, have been evaluated using the nearest-neighbor central interaction model for hexagonal materials proposed by Ramji Rao and Srinivasan. These TOE constants have been employed to evaluate the low-temperature limit $\bar{\gamma}_{L}$ of the thermal expansion, the Anderson-Grüneisen (AG) parameter $\delta$, and the second Grüneisen constant q of cobalt. $\bar{\gamma}_{L}$ has the value 2.08 for cobalt. The temperature variation of the volume Grüneisen gamma of cobalt has been calculated on this model. The high-temperature limit $\bar{\gamma}_{H}$ has the value 2.13, which agrees well with the experimental value obtained from thermal expansion and specific heat data of cobalt. Anderson's theory has been used to explain the temperature dependence of the bulk modulus of cobalt and compared with the experimental values obtained from the elastic constant data of Fisher and Dever. The calculated $B_{s}$ values agree with the experimental values to within $1\%$.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Cobalt is a transition metal with hcp structure and has a $c/a$ value equal to 1.623 at 300 K, which is very nearly equal to the ideal value. Cobalt undergoes a phase transformation to the fcc structure ($\beta$-cobalt) around 700 K. The second-order elastic (SOE) constants of hcp cobalt ($\alpha$) were measured by McSkimin¹ at room temperature and by Fisher and Dever² up to 523 K. In this paper the third-order elastic (TOE) constants and the temperature variation of the volume Grüneisen gamma of cobalt have been calculated using the nearest neighbor central-force (CF) model of Ramji Rao and Srinivasan³⁴ for an ideal hcp lattice. This model was earlier employed to calculate the TOE constants and the pressure derivatives of the SOE constants of magnesium,⁵ rhenium,⁶ and erbium.⁷ The calculated TOE constants have been used to calculate the pressure derivatives of the SOE constants, the Anderson-Grüneisen parameter $\delta$, the second Grüneisen

## 2. TOE CONSTANTS AND PRESSURE DERIVATIVES OF THE SOE CONSTANTS OF COBALT

The expressions for the TOE constants of an ideal hcp lattice were derived by Ramji Rao and Srinivasan, $^{3}$ who used a nearest-neighbor central interaction potential of the form

$$
\Phi(r)=-\left(a / r^{m}\right)+\left(b / r^{n}\right) \tag{1}
$$

These expressions are given here for convenience:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{111} &=(247 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3}+\left(k_{2} D / \sqrt{2}\right) \\
C_{222} &=(301 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3}+\left(k_{2} D / \sqrt{2}\right) \\
C_{333} &=(256 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3} \\
C_{112} &=(85 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3}-\left(k_{2} D / 3 \sqrt{2}\right) \\
C_{113} &=(16 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3}+\left(4 k_{2} D / 3 \sqrt{2}\right) \\
C_{123} &=(16 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3}-\left(4 k_{2} D / 3 \sqrt{2}\right) \\
C_{133} &=(64 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3} \\
C_{144} &=(16 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3} \\
C_{155} &=(16 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3} \\
C_{344} &=(64 / 18 \sqrt{2}) k_{3} D^{3}
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
$$

These expressions involve two constants $k_{2}$ and $k_{3}$:

$$
k_{2}=O^{\prime 2} \Phi=\eta M / 2 D^{2}, \quad k_{3}=O^{\prime 3} \Phi=-\left(\eta M / 4 D^{4}\right)(n+m+6) \tag{3}
$$

Here $M$ is the mass of the atom,

$$
\eta=n b(n-m) / 2 M D^{n+2} \tag{4}
$$

and $O'$ is the differential operator $\partial / \partial r^{2}$.

Srinivasan and Ramji Rao⁴ showed that the high-temperature limit of the Grüneisen gamma of an ideal hcp lattice described by the nearest-neighbor central potential is nearly equal to $(n+m)/6$. Gschneidner⁹ reports a value of 2.07 for the thermal $\bar{\gamma}_H$ of cobalt. So the value of $(n+m)$ appropriate to cobalt is 12.42. In order to calculate the TOE constants, we need to know the value of $\eta$ for cobalt. The measured SOE constants $C_{11}$ and $C_{33}$ of Fisher and Dever² at 298 K (these are in good agreement with the room-temperature values of McSkimin¹) and $D=2.514$ a.u. are used to get an average value of $\eta$. Hence $k_2$ and $k_3$ are calculated for cobalt. The TOE constants of cobalt corresponding to room temperature are then evaluated from the expressions (2) and are given in Table I. The expressions for the effective SOE constants $C'_{ij}$ of a strained hexagonal crystal in terms of its TOE constants were derived by Ramji Rao and Srinivasan⁵ using the finite strain theory of Murnaghan.¹⁰ The pressure derivatives of cobalt can be obtained by differentiating $C'_{ij}$ with respect to pressure $p$ and using the calculated TOE constants. The pressure derivatives of the SOE constants of cobalt at room temperature are included in Table I. In a similar manner the TOE constants at 4 K are calculated from a knowledge of the $C_{11}$ and $C_{33}$ elastic constants at this temperature given by Fisher and Dever.² These calculated TOE constants at 4 K are reported in Table I.

$$
\operatorname{Det}\left[\left[\begin{array}{cc}
\mathbf{q} & \\
k & k^{\prime} \\

\end{array}\right]-\omega^{2} \delta_{k k^{\prime}} \delta_{i j}\right]=0,

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}

\end{array}\right]

are the elements of the dynamical matrix (coupling coefficients). The general expressions for the coupling coefficients of a homogenously deformed lattice described by a nearest-neighbor central interaction potential of the type given by (1) were derived by Srinivasan and Ramji Rao $^{4}$ and these are used to calculate the unstrained frequencies, $\gamma'$ and $\gamma''$, for the different lattice wave vectors. We now define the effective Grüneisen functions, which are weighted averages of the individual GPs, as

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(T)=\sum_{\mathbf{q}, j} \gamma^{\prime}(\mathbf{q}, j) C_{v}(\mathbf{q}, j) / \sum_{\mathbf{q}, j} C_{v}(\mathbf{q}, j) \\
& \bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(T)=\sum_{\mathbf{q}, j} \gamma^{\prime \prime}(\mathbf{q}, j) C_{v}(\mathbf{q}, j) / \sum_{\mathbf{q}, j} C_{v}(\mathbf{q}, j)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mathbf{q}$ is the wave vector, $j$ is the polarization index, and $C_{v}(\mathbf{q}, j)$ is the contribution of a single normal mode of frequency $\omega$ to the specific heat of the lattice. The temperature variation of the linear expansion coefficients $\alpha_{\perp}$ and $\alpha_{\|}$of the uniaxial crystal can be described in terms of these effective Grüneisen functions. These effective Grüneisen functions tend to the limits $\bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(0)$ and $\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(0)$ at high temperatures and to the limits $\bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(-3)$ and $\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(-3)$ at absolute zero. We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
& V \alpha_{\perp}=\left[\left(S_{11}+S_{12}\right) \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(T)+S_{13} \bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(T)\right] C_{v}=\bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{Br}} C_{v} \chi \\
& V \alpha_{\|}=\left[2 S_{13} \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(T)+S_{33} \bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(T)\right] C_{v}=\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{\mathrm{Br}} C_{v} \chi
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $S_{i j}$ are the elastic compliance coefficients, $V$ is the molar volume, and $\chi$ is the isothermal compressibility. $\bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{Br}}$ and $\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{\mathrm{Br}}$ are the average Grüneisen functions used by Brugger and Fritz. $^{11}$ From (8) we may calculate $\bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{Br}}$ and $\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{\mathrm{Br}}$ as

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{Br}}(T)=\left[\left(S_{11}+S_{12}\right) \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(T)+S_{13} \bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(T)\right] / \chi \\
& \bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{\mathrm{Br}}(T)=\left[\left(2 S_{13} \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(T)+S_{33} \bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(T)\right] / \chi\right.
\end{aligned}
$$

At very low temperatures the GPs for the elastic waves determine the anisotropic thermal expansion of a uniaxial crystal and the effective Grüneisen functions approach the limits

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(-3)=\int \sum_{j=1}^{3} \gamma_{j}^{\prime}(\theta, \phi) V_{j}^{-3}(\theta, \phi) d \Omega / \int \sum_{j=1}^{3} V_{j}^{-3}(\theta, \phi) d \Omega \\
& \bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(-3)=\int \sum_{j=1}^{3} \gamma_{j}^{\prime \prime}(\theta, \phi) V_{j}^{-3}(\theta, \phi) d \Omega / \int \sum_{j=1}^{3} V_{j}^{-3}(\theta, \phi) d \Omega
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

Here $V_{j}(\theta, \phi)$ is the wave velocity of the elastic wave of polarization index $j$ propagating in the direction $(\theta, \phi)$; $\gamma_{j}^{\prime}$ and $\gamma_{j}^{\prime \prime}$ are the GPs for the accoustic mode. The individual GPs for the elastic waves can be calculated from a knowledge of the TOE constants of the crystal using the procedure of Ramji Rao and Srinivasan $^{3,12}$ and hence the low-temperature limits can be calcu- lated. This procedure was used by Ramji Rao and Menon $^{13,14}$ to calculate the low-temperature limit $\bar{\gamma}_{L}$ of the thermal expansion of the trigonal crystals lithium niobate and alumina. In the hexagonal crystals, the GPs and the elastic wave velocities depend only on $\theta$ and are independent of the azimuth $\phi$. Table II gives the wave velocities and the GPs for the acoustic waves propagating at different angles $\theta$ to the $Z$ axis in the $X-Z$ plane. The calculated values of $\bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{1}(-3)$ and $\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{1}(-3)$ for cobalt are 2.28 and 1.78, respectively. The low-temperature limits of $\bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{Br}}$ and $\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{\mathrm{Br}}$ are calculated to be 0.80 and 0.48, respectively. The low-temperature limit of the volume lattice thermal expansion of cobalt is given by

$$
\bar{\gamma}_{L}=2 \bar{\gamma}_{\perp}^{\mathrm{Br}}+\bar{\gamma}_{\|}^{\mathrm{Br}}=2.08
\tag{11}
$$

## 4. ANDERSON-GRÜNEISEN PARAMETER $\delta$, SECOND GRÜNEISEN CONSTANT $q$, AND TEMPERATURE VARIATION OF THE BULK MODULUS OF COBALT

Ramji Rao $^{17}$ has suggested a method to calculate the Anderson-Grüneisen (AG) parameter $\delta$ of a hcp solid from a knowledge of its TOE constants, which is briefly outlined here. The bulk modulus of a strained hcp solid is given by
$$B^{\prime}=\frac{1}{9}\left(2 C_{11}^{\prime}+C_{33}^{\prime}+2 C_{12}^{\prime}+4 C_{13}^{\prime}\right)\qquad(16)$$
where the $C_{i j}^{\prime}$ are the effective SOE constants of the strained hcp solid and involve its TOE constants. Chang $^{18}$ derived the relation between $\delta$ and the pressure derivative of the bulk modulus as
$$\delta=\left(d B_{T}^{\prime} / d p\right)-1 \simeq\left(d B_{s}^{\prime} / d p\right)-1\qquad(17)$$

Substituting (16) into the above equation, we get $\delta$ as a function of the TOE constants of the crystal. The value of $\delta$ evaluated from the calculated TOE constants is 3.63 (Table III). From theoretical considerations, Anderson $^{8}$ derived the relationship which gives the temperature dependence of the bulk modulus as a function of the specific heat and atomic volume $V$,
$$d B_{s} / d T=-\delta \gamma C_{p} / V\qquad(18)$$

Here $\gamma$ is the usual Grüneisen constant. On integrating (18), one gets
$$B_{s}=B_{00}-\delta \gamma \int_{0}^{T}\left(C_{p} / V\right) d T\qquad(19)$$
or
$$B_{s}=B_{00}-\left(\delta \gamma / V_{0}\right) \int_{0}^{T} C_{v} d T\qquad(20)$$
where $V_{0}$ is the specific volume per "average" atom at $0 \mathrm{~K}$ and $C_{p}$ is replaced by $C_{v}$. The theoretical curve showing the variation of the bulk modulus of cobalt with temperature is obtained from Eq. (20) and is shown in Fig. 4. This close agreement vindicates the calculated values of the TOE constants of cobalt at room temperature (25 C).

The Grüneisen constant $\gamma$ of a solid is in general a function of both volume and temperature. The parameter which describes the volume dependence of the Grüneisen gamma is called the second Grüneisen con- stant $q$ and is given by the expression

$$
q=\left(\frac{\partial \ln \gamma}{\partial \ln V}\right)_{T} \tag{21}
$$

![](./images/812321566049173505_4.jpg)

Fig. 4. Variation of the bulk modulus of cobalt with temperature. Points are experimental values.

The second Grüneisen constant $q$ for cobalt at high temperatures has been calculated from the modified formula of Basset et al. $^{20}$ derived by Ramji Rao, $^{21}$

$$
q=\gamma(1+\delta_{s} \alpha T) \tag{22}
$$

where $\alpha$ is the volume coefficient of expansion; its value for cobalt is given in Table III. The calculated room-temperature value of $q$ for cobalt is 2.16.

## 5. DISCUSSION
The study of the TOE constants of cobalt (both at 4 K and at room temperature) reveals anisotropy in the basal plane, as $C_{111}$ and $C_{222}$ differ considerably from each other. The pressure derivative $\partial C_{44} / \partial p$ for cobalt is positive, unlike in zirconium, $^{22,23}$ from which it may be inferred that the pressure-induced phase transformation from hcp to bcc structure is not possible in this metal. Considering the fact that the TOE constants of cobalt are calculated using the high-temperature limit of $\gamma$ obtained from experi mental data, we expect good agreement between the present calculated TOE constants and the measurements of pressure derivatives of the SOE constants of cobalt.
