# Thermal Expansion of Single-Crystal Tephroite

Shinichi Okajima $^{1}$, Isao Suzuki $^{1}$, Kiyoshi Seya $^{1}$, and Yoshio Sumino $^{2}$

$^{1}$ Department of Earth Sciences, Faculty of Science,
Okayama University, Okayama 700, Japan
$^{2}$ Department of Earth Sciences, Faculty of Science,
Nagoya University, Nagoya 464, Japan

**Abstract.** The thermal expansion of tephroite $(Mn_{2}SiO_{4})$ at temperatures between 25 and $850^{\circ}C$ has been determined by a dilatometric technique. The analysis of data in terms of Grüneisen's theory yields the Grüneisen's parameter $\gamma=1.04$, and the pressure derivative of rigidity $(\partial G/\partial P)=0.7$.

## Introduction

The harmonic and anharmonic properties of silicate minerals are of basic importance in the understanding of the state of the earth's interior. In this brief report, thermal expansion of tephroite $(Mn_{2}SiO_{4})$, the Mn analogue of forsterite $(Mg_{2}SiO_{4})$ and fayalite $(Fe_{2}SiO_{4})$, is determined at temperatures between 25 and $850^{\circ}C$.

## Sample and Experimental Procedure

The single-crystal specimen of tephroite was grown by Dr. H. Takei of the Research Institute of Iron, Steel and Other Metals of Tohoku University using the Czochralski technique (Takei, 1976). This crystal is bluish to dark purple in color and transparent to visible light. Neither cracks nor flaws are observed with the naked eye. Each crystallographic orientation of the specimen was determined with the back diffraction Laue method within an error of $0.5^{\circ}$, which is small enough for the present purpose. This crystal was shaped into a rectangular prism with axes parallel to {100} axes and edge lengths of about 2 mm. The bulk density determined from size and mass of the specimen is $4.12_{9}\ g/cm^{3}$.

Thermal expansion was measured by means of a dilatometer with a differen- tial transformer reported in a previous paper (Suzuki, 1975). For the present study, this apparatus was improved and the stability became better than $0.05\ \mu m/h$. Thermocouple e.m.f. was calibrated at the melting points of silver, aluminum and lead. The oxygen partial pressure of the specimen environment was controlled by use of a $CO_{2}/H_{2}$ gas mixture.

The effects of heating and cooling rates on the apparent thermal expansion were tested and the optimum rate was determined, the rate being equal to or less than $3^\circ$C/min below $300^\circ$C and $5^\circ$C/min above this temperature. Apparent thermal expansion shows a small difference (1% of total expansion, at most) between heating and cooling runs. True thermal expansion is given by the average of these runs corrected for the expansion of the reference specimen (fused quartz). The quality of the data is confirmed by repeated measurements.

## Result and Discussion

The linear expansion $Y_i(T)$ is expressed by

$$Y_i(T)=(L_i(T)-L_i(T_r))/L_i(T_r)$$

where $L_i(T)$ is the length of the specimen along the orientation $i$ at temperature $T$, and $T_r\ (=25^\circ$C) is a reference temperature. Volume expansion $Y_v(T)$ is expressed by

$$Y_v(T)=(1+Y_1(T))\cdot(1+Y_2(T))\cdot(1+Y_3(T))-1$$

where suffixes 1, 2, and 3 represent the three crystallographic orientations of the specimen. Observed values of thermal expansion at each temperature for tephroite are listed in Table 1. The thermal expansion coefficients are calculated as follows:

$$\alpha_j(T)=\{Y_j(T+\Delta T/2)-Y_j(T-\Delta T/2)\}/\Delta T\cdot(1+Y_j(T)) \tag{1}$$

with $\Delta T=50^\circ$C, where $j$ represents suffix $i$ or $v$. Results of the calculation are plotted in Figure 1.

The present data are fitted by

$$Y_i(T)=\frac{E(\theta_i, T)}{3a_i(Q_{0i}-k_iE(\theta_i, T))}+\frac{1-a_i}{a_i} \tag{2}$$

for linear expansion, and by

$$Y_v(T)=\frac{E(\theta, T)}{a(Q_0-kE(\theta, T))}+\frac{1-a}{a} \tag{3}$$

for volume expansion; the parameters in these equations are determined by the least-squares technique and shown in Table 2. The $E$ in Equations (2) and (3) is thermal energy, $\theta$ is a characteristic temperature, $Q_0$ is a parameter related to Grüneisen's parameter $\gamma$ by $Q_0=B_0\cdot V_0/\gamma$, ($B_0$: bulk modulus, $V_0$: molar volume, at 0 K respectively), $k$ is an anharmonic parameter, and $a$ is a ratio of volume at reference temperature and that at 0 K. The parameters in Equation (2) with suffix $i$ are orientation dependent quantities analogous to those in Equation (3). Equations (2) and (3) have been successfully applied to represent the thermal expansion of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ (Wachtman et al., 1962) and MgO and $(\text{Mg}_{0.9}\text{Fe}_{0.1})_2\text{SiO}_4$ (Suzuki, 1975).

By using the solution in Table 2, thermal expansion and its coefficient are calculated by Equations (2), (3), and (1) over the wider range of temperature. These results are also shown in Figure 1 by smooth curves and in Table 1. The linear thermal expansion coefficients of tephroite are about $15 \%$ smaller than those of olivine (Suzuki, 1975) along all crystallographic orientations.

The lattice parameters of tephroite at $22^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ have been reported as $a$ $=4.904_{2} \mathring{A}, b=10.567_{0} \mathring{A}$, and $c=6.254_{5} \mathring{A}$ (Takei, 1976). By using the values of $a$ in Table 2, the lattice constants and molar volume of tephroite at 0 K are given as $a_{0}=4.900_{0} \mathring{A}, b_{0}=10.554_{3} \mathring{A}, c_{0}=6.247_{0} \mathring{A}$, and $V_{0}=48.64_{1} \mathrm{~cm}^{3} / \mathrm{mol}$.

By combining the values of $Q_{0}=6.065 \times 10^{6} \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{mol}$ and $B_{0} \cdot V_{0}=6.280$ $\times 10^{6} \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{mol}$ (approximated by the room temperature data), we have Grüneisen's parameter $\gamma=1.04$. From the view point of lattice dynamics, Grüneisen's param-

eter describes the property of lattice vibration, which consists of two shear modes and one longitudinal mode. This fact implies that $\gamma$ should be related more to shear properties (rigidity, etc.) of solid material and less to dilatational properties (bulk modulus, etc.). Experimental data support this idea (a paper is in preparation). An empirical relation among $\gamma$, $\partial G/\partial P$, and Poisson's ratio $v$ gives an estimated value of $\partial G/\partial P=0.7$ from $\gamma=1.04$ and $v=0.314$ (Sumino, personal communication, 1976).
