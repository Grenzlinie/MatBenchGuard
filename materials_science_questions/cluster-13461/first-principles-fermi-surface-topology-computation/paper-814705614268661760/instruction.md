# Self-consistent pseudopotential DFT calculations for InSb zinc-blende and rocksalt phases

## Problem background
InSb is a III‑V semiconductor with a known stable zinc‑blende crystal structure and a metastable rocksalt structure that can be obtained under certain conditions. Relativistic corrections are significant for these heavy elements and strongly influence the electronic properties. This task requires performing self‑consistent pseudopotential DFT calculations for both crystal phases to determine the fundamental band gap and valence bandwidth of zinc‑blende InSb and the charge transfer from In to Sb in each phase. These quantities provide insight into the electronic structure and bonding differences between the semiconducting and metallic phases.

### Crystal structures
**Zinc-blende (ZB) phase:**
- Lattice: face-centered cubic with a two-atom basis
- Lattice constant: a = 6.47 Å
- Atomic positions (fractional coordinates):
  - In: (0.000, 0.000, 0.000)
  - Sb: (0.250, 0.250, 0.250)

**Rocksalt (RS) phase:**
- Lattice: face-centered cubic with a two-atom basis
- Lattice constant: a = 6.12 Å
- Atomic positions (fractional coordinates):
  - In: (0.000, 0.000, 0.000)
  - Sb: (0.500, 0.500, 0.500)

Both structures are generated by the conventional fcc Bravais lattice vectors:
- a1 = (a/2, a/2, 0)
- a2 = (a/2, 0, a/2)
- a3 = (0, a/2, a/2)

with the basis atoms placed as given above.

### Valence electrons
In contributes 3 valence electrons (5s²5p¹); Sb contributes 5 valence electrons (5s²5p³). There are 8 valence electrons per primitive cell, occupying 4 bands in the absence of spin‑orbit coupling (including spin degeneracy), and more bands when spin‑orbit splitting is included. The Fermi level must be determined by filling states up to the appropriate number of occupied bands.

## Approach
The method uses a plane‑wave basis expansion of valence wave functions with a local ionic pseudopotential of the form:

```
V_ion(r) = (-Z e^2 / r) * [ (1 - e^{-λ r}) / (1 + e^{-λ (r - r_c)}) ]
```

For In and Sb, the pseudopotential parameters are:

| Element | Z   | r_c (a.u.) | λ (a.u.⁻¹) |
|---------|-----|------------|-------------|
| In      | 3   | 1.206      | 9.8420      |
| Sb      | 5   | 1.083      | 5.2090      |

Exchange‑correlation is treated with the Slater Xα functional:

```
V_xc(r) = -(3/8π)^{1/3} * 3α * ρ^{1/3}(r),   with α = 1
```

### Plane‑wave basis
The valence wave functions are expanded in plane waves. The Hamiltonian matrix at a given k‑point is:

```
H_{GG'}(k) = (ħ²/2m) |k+G|² δ_{GG'} + V_ion(G-G') + V_H(G-G') + V_xc(G-G')
```

where V_H is the Hartree potential and V_xc is the exchange‑correlation potential.

**Plane‑wave cutoff:** Use a kinetic energy cutoff E_cut_direct = 10 Ry (~5 a.u.) to define the set of plane waves included directly in the secular equation. This yields approximately 60–70 plane waves. An additional set of higher‑energy plane waves (up to E_cut_perturb = 20 Ry, giving >150 additional waves) is included through second‑order Löwdin perturbation theory. The charge density and screening potentials are expanded in the lowest 10 shells of reciprocal‑lattice vectors (i.e., Fourier components with |G| ≤ G_max where G_max corresponds to 10 shells).

### Spin‑orbit coupling
Spin‑orbit coupling is included by adding a k‑dependent term to the Hamiltonian that mixes spin components. In the plane‑wave basis, the spin‑orbit matrix between plane waves G and G' with spins s and s' is:

```
H^SO_{GG'}^{ss'}(k) = -i μ_so [ (k+G) × (k+G') ] · σ_{ss'}
```

where σ = (σ_x, σ_y, σ_z) is the vector of Pauli matrices, and μ_so is a scalar coupling strength (in appropriate atomic units). The value of μ_so is determined by fitting the spin‑orbit splitting of the uppermost valence bands at the Γ point of zinc‑blende InSb to the experimental value of 0.81 eV (see Step 1).

The total Hamiltonian matrix is constructed in the basis of plane waves with spin, giving a 2N×2N matrix (N = number of plane waves). The eigenvalues are obtained by diagonalization.

### Mass‑velocity and Darwin corrections
These relativistic corrections are represented as a non‑local operator added to the Hamiltonian. They are given by three matrix‑element parameters defined with respect to the anion (Sb, labeled "a") and the cation (In, labeled "c"):

- V_aa: on‑site correction for anion (Sb) — applied to diagonal blocks associated with Sb sites
- V_cc: on‑site correction for cation (In) — applied to diagonal blocks associated with In sites  
- V_ac: nearest‑neighbor anion–cation coupling — applied to off‑diagonal blocks connecting Sb and In sites

In the plane‑wave basis, these are included by adding structure‑factor‑weighted contributions:
- For V_aa: add V_aa * S_a(G-G') * δ_{τ∈Sb}, where S_a(G) = exp(-i G·τ_a) summed over Sb sites
- For V_cc: similarly with V_cc and In sites
- For V_ac: add V_ac * Σ_{τ_a,τ_c} exp(-i G·τ_a + i G'·τ_c) for nearest‑neighbor In–Sb pairs, normalized appropriately

For the zinc‑blende phase (nearest‑neighbor distance d_ZB = √3 a/4 ≈ 2.80 Å):
- V_aa = −0.93 eV
- V_cc = −0.47 eV
- V_ac = 0.27 eV

For the rocksalt phase (nearest‑neighbor distance d_RS = a/2 = 3.06 Å):
- V_aa and V_cc remain unchanged
- V_ac is scaled by the ratio d_ZB / d_RS = (√3/2) * (a_ZB / a_RS) ≈ (0.8660) * (6.47 / 6.12) ≈ 0.9155, i.e., V_ac(RS) = V_ac(ZB) * 0.9155

### Self‑consistent field (SCF) procedure
1. **Initial guess:** Start from an empirical screened pseudopotential or from a superposition of atomic charge densities.
2. **Diagonalization:** For a set of k‑points in the Brillouin zone, construct the full Hamiltonian (kinetic + local pseudopotential + Hartree + exchange‑correlation + spin‑orbit + mass‑velocity/Darwin) and diagonalize to obtain eigenvalues E_n(k) and eigenvectors ψ_{n,k}(G).
3. **Occupation:** Fill the eigenstates up to the Fermi energy. For zinc‑blende (semiconducting), occupy the 4 lowest bands (8 electrons per cell) completely. For rocksalt (metallic), use a k‑point integration with Gaussian smearing or the tetrahedron method to determine partial occupancies, with the total number of electrons fixed at 8 per cell.
4. **Charge density:** Compute the valence charge density from the occupied eigenstates:
   ```
   ρ(G) = Σ_{n,k} w_k f_{n,k} Σ_{G'} ψ*_{n,k}(G') ψ_{n,k}(G'+G)
   ```
   where w_k are k‑point weights and f_{n,k} are occupations.
5. **Screening potentials:** Construct the Hartree potential V_H(G) = (4π e²/|G|²) ρ(G) for G≠0, and V_xc(G) from the Fourier transform of the Xα potential.
6. **Update:** Add screening potentials to the bare ionic pseudopotential to form the new crystal potential.
7. **Iterate:** Repeat steps 2–6 until the total energy or charge density converges (typically 5–6 iterations).

### k‑point sampling
For the semiconducting zinc‑blende phase, use a shifted 2×2×2 Monkhorst‑Pack grid (or equivalently, the special‑points set {(0.25,0.25,0.25), (0.75,0.25,0.25), (0.25,0.75,0.25), (0.75,0.75,0.25)} in fractional coordinates, reduced by symmetry) which gives converged charge densities.

For the metallic rocksalt phase, a denser k‑point mesh is required. Use a k·p expansion around the Γ point: first diagonalize the full Hamiltonian exactly at Γ, then use the Γ‑point eigenfunctions {u_{n,Γ}} as a basis to construct the Hamiltonian at neighbouring k‑points via the k·p equation:
```
H_{nn'}(k) = [E_n(Γ) + (ħ²/2m) k²] δ_{nn'} + (ħ/m) k · p_{nn'}
```
where p_{nn'} = ⟨u_{n,Γ}|-iħ∇|u_{n',Γ}⟩ is the momentum matrix element. Truncate the k·p basis to the lowest 6–8 bands to efficiently sample a fine mesh of k‑points for charge‑density integration.
