# Magnetic anisotropy in the boron nitride monolayer doped by 3d transitional metal substitutes at boron-site

Yuan Lu, $^{1}$ Xu Zuo, $^{1}$ Min Feng, $^{2}$ and Tiege Zhou $^{1,a)}$
$^{1}$ College of Information Technical Science, Nankai University, Tianjin 300071, China
$^{2}$ School of Physics, Nankai University, Tianjin 300071, China

(Presented 17 January 2013; received 20 October 2012; accepted 2 January 2013; published online29 March 2013)

Based on density functional theory with the generalized gradient approximation, a computational study is carried out to reveal the magnetism of the hexagonal boron nitride monolayers (h-BN) doped by 3d transitional metal atoms at boron-site. The magnetic moments and magnetic anisotropy energies (MAEs) of the most stable structures are given. Among the systems investigated, the MAE of Fe doped h-BN is 1.19 meV with an easy axis in the h-BN plane, while that of Mn is 0.63 meV with an easy axis perpendicular to the plane. For Sc and Co, the doped systems are nonmagnetic. For Ti, V, Cr, Ni, Cu, and Zn, the MAEs are relatively small. Theoretical analyses by perturbation theory and molecular orbital clarify that the MAE of Fe originates from the second order spin orbit coupling, whereas the MAE of Mn is contributed by the unquenched orbital momentum. © 2013 American Institute of Physics. [http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4798478]

Since the discovery of giant magnetic resistance by Fert $^{1}$ and Grünberg $^{2}$ in 1988, spintronics has become a hotspot in both material science and information technology. Magnetic anisotropy is the most crucial property for spintronics. It is known that reduced symmetry $^{3,4}$ and low dimensionality $^{5-7}$ could induce appreciable magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE). Recently, graphene, $^{8,9}$ the single layer graphite, has exhibited its intrinsic advantages in the spin transportation $^{10-12}$ and the high density magnetic storage. $^{13-15}$ Besides the hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) monolayer, an isostructural and isoelectronic material of graphene has attracted wide attentions $^{16,17}$ as well. Although a series of studies $^{18,19}$ have illustrated doped h-BN systems could be magnetic, few of them focus on magnetic anisotropy. Moreover, Jin et al. $^{20}$ ever testified the easy preparation of boron deficient h-BN, which indicates the practicability of the boron-site doping. In this paper, density functional theory (DFT) is employed to reveal the magnetism and magnetic anisotropy of the h-BN monolayer doped by transitional metal (TM) substitutes at boron-site(TM@B, TM = Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, or Zn).

To build up the TM@B models, a $7×7×1$ h-BN supercell with a dimension of $17.64\,\mathring{A}×17.64\,\mathring{A}$ is created and then one B atom is substituted by one TM atom. A vacuum of $15\,\mathring{A}$ is placed between the two adjacent layers. For notations, let x and y directions be parallel and z direction be vertical to the h-BN. The core-valence interactions are modeled with projector augmented wave (PAW) method. $^{21}$ All calculations are performed by VASP, $^{22}$ which is based on DFT combined with the generalized gradient approximation of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof. $^{23}$ The cut-off energy of the plane wave basis is400 eV and dipole corrections are taken into consideration. The k-mesh is generated by Monkhorst-Pack method. In the structure optimization, the k-points grid is $3×3×1$ , and calculation will stop once the force on each ion is smaller than $0.01\,\text{eV}/\mathring{A}$ .

Electronic configurations are calculated after structural optimization. An energy converging standard of $1×10^{-6}\,\text{eV}$ is employed. The k-points grid of $5×5×1$ yields a good comprise of accuracy and computational consumption. MAE can be calculated by including spin orbit coupling (SOC) and it is defined as $\Delta E=E_{z}-E_{x}$ , where $E_{z}$ and $E_{x}$ are the total energies in z and x magnetization directions, respectively. The total energy is calculated non-self-consistently and its converging standard is set at $1×10^{-7}\,\text{eV}$ .

Table I shows structural parameters, total magnetic moments (m.m.), and MAEs of TM@B systems. In the optimized structures, TM atoms are of $C_{3v}$ local symmetry and the neighboring three N atoms are equivalent. The bond length $(d_{D-N})$ is the distance between TM dopant and its closest N atoms. Distortions of h-BN can be quantitatively estimated by the elevation in the z direction of the N atoms nearest to TM $(\Delta h_{N})$ . The binding energy $(E_{B})$ is defined as the total energy of optimized TM@B structure minus the sum of the energies of the isolated TM atom and the boron deficient h-BN. It is seen that, in general, the binding energy descends with the increasing of atomic number: Sc doping causes the maximum $E_{B}$ , while Zn the minimum. In addition, $d_{D-N}$ of Co@B is minimum and its distortion is smallest as well.

The magnetism trend in Table I can be explained by the filling of symmetry-adapted molecular orbitals (MO). Owing to $C_{3v}$ local symmetry, 3d orbitals of the TM atoms will be divided into three groups: a single $3d z^{2}$ orbital with $a_{1}$ symmetry, and two twofold degenerated $E_{1}(3dxy+3dx^{2}-y^{2})$ and $E_{2}(3dxz+3dyz)$ orbitals with e symmetry. Additionally,4s orbital of the TM atoms is of $a_{1}$ symmetry. As shown in Fig. 1, these orbitals interact and mix with the dangling bonds of the neighboring three N atoms with compatible symmetries, resulting in the formation of MO. 2e, 3e, and 3a₁ orbitals: 2a₁ MO mainly originates from $3dz^2$ of TM; 2e and 3e MOs are from the hybridization of E₁ and E₂ orbitals.

MOs are filled according to the relative size of the crystal field splitting ($\Delta_\text{cf}$) and the exchange splitting ($\Delta_\text{ex}$). Shown in Fig. 2(a) is the density of states (DOS) of V@B. It is seen that the crystal field splitting between 2e and 2a₁ states is smaller than the exchange splitting between 2a₁ spin-up and spin-down channels. Therefore, the high spin configuration is represented and a magnetic moment of $2\mu_\text{B}$ is derived from two $d$ remaining electrons. The MOs filling of Mn@B is shown in Fig. 2(b). Because the exchange splitting of 2e is weaker than the crystal field splitting between 2e and 3e, Mn@B represents a low spin state: the spin-up channels of 2a₁ and 2e are fully occupied and the spin-down channels of 2e are half-occupied. Therefore, Mn@B system obtains a magnetic moment of $2\mu_\text{B}$ with four remaining $d$ electrons. As Fig. 2(c) shown, exchange splitting is weaker than crystal field splitting in Fe@B as well, so low spin configuration emerges and a magnetic moment of $1\mu_\text{B}$ is rendered by five $d$ electrons. In principle, the crystal field splitting between 2a₁ and 2e is relatively weak, whereas the crystal field splitting between 2e and 3e is relatively strong, so electrons prefer to fill the spin-up channels of 2a₁ and 2e, the spin-down channels of 2a₁ and 2e, and the 3e orbitals one by one. Thus, TM is in high spin state before Cr but in low spin state after Mn.

Among the systems investigated, the MAEs of Sc@B and Co@B are meaningless, for they are nonmagnetic; the MAEs of Ti@B and Cu@B are nearly zero ($<$0.01 meV); the MAE of Fe@B is 1.19 meV and that of Mn is 0.63 meV; while, the MAEs of V, Cr, Ni, and Zn are relatively small. Except Mn@B, the easy axes of other doping systems that possess magnetic anisotropy are parallel to the h-BN monolayer.

The MAE of Mn@B is considerable in terms of energy per atom.²⁴ As mentioned previously, the spin-up channel of 2e MO is half-occupied in Mn@B, and 2e state is a mixture of E₁($3dxy$, $3dx^2-y^2$) and E₂($3dxz$ and $3dyz$). Matrix elements $\langle 3dxy|\hat{L}_z|3dx^2-y^2\rangle$ and $\langle 3dxz|\hat{L}_z|3dyz\rangle$ are nonzero, so unquenched orbital momentum and the first order SOC could exist, which can be testified by the degenerate perturbation theory. According to the single ion model of MAE, the spin-orbit part of Hamiltonian can be given by

$$
\hat{H}_{SO}=\lambda \hat{L} \cdot \hat{S}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(\hat{L}_{x} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{x}+\hat{L}_{y} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{y}+\hat{L}_{z} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{z}\right). \quad (1)
$$

We only consider the half-occupied spin-down channel of 2e MO. The basis functions of 2e MO are expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left|e_{1}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|k|^{2}}}\left(\left|3 d x^{2}-y^{2}\right\rangle+k|3 d x z\rangle\right), \\
& \left|e_{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|k|^{2}}}(|3 d x y\rangle+k|3 d y z\rangle).
\end{aligned}
$$

For magnetization in $z$ axis, the formula (1) can be rewritten as $\hat{H}_{SO}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\hat{L}_{z} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{z}$. The orbital matrix representation $\langle i|\hat{L}_{z}|j\rangle$ ($i,j=e_1,e_2$) is found as

$$
\left(\begin{array}{cc}
\left\langle e_{1}\left|\hat{L}_{z}\right| e_{1}\right\rangle & \left\langle e_{2}\left|\hat{L}_{z}\right| e_{1}\right\rangle \\
\left\langle e_{1}\left|\hat{L}_{z}\right| e_{2}\right\rangle & \left\langle e_{2}\left|\hat{L}_{z}\right| e_{2}\right\rangle
\end{array}\right)=\frac{2+|k|^{2}}{1+|k|^{2}}\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & -i \\
i & 0
\end{array}\right).
$$

FIG. 2. Density of states: (a) V@B, (b) Mn@B, and (c) Fe@B.

Thus, the eigenvalues of spin-orbit part Hamiltonian are
$E_{1}^{(z)}=-\frac{2+|k|^{2}}{1+|k|^{2}} \frac{\hbar}{2} \lambda$ and $E_{2}^{(z)}=\frac{2+|k|^{2}}{1+|k|^{2}} \frac{\hbar}{2} \lambda$ corresponding to the two distinguished SOC states, respectively.

For magnetization in $x$ direction (equivalent to $y$), $\hat{H}_{S O}=\frac{\lambda}{2} \hat{L}_{x} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{x}$. The orbital matrix representation $\langle i|\hat{L}_{x}| j\rangle$ $(i, j=e_{1}, e_{2})$ can be found as

$$
\left(\begin{array}{ll}
\left\langle e_{1}\left|\hat{L}_{x}\right| e_{1}\right\rangle & \left\langle e_{2}\left|\hat{L}_{x}\right| e_{1}\right\rangle \\
\left\langle e_{1}\left|\hat{L}_{x}\right| e_{2}\right\rangle & \left\langle e_{2}\left|\hat{L}_{x}\right| e_{2}\right\rangle
\end{array}\right)=\frac{k^{*}-k}{1+|k|^{2}}\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & -i \\
i & 0
\end{array}\right).
$$

The two eigenvalues are $E_{1}^{(x)}=-\frac{\left|k^{*}-k\right|}{1+|k|^{2}} \frac{\hbar}{2} \lambda$ and $E_{2}^{(x)}$ $=\frac{\left|k^{*}-k\right|}{1+|k|^{2}} \frac{\hbar}{2} \lambda$, respectively.

Because the spin-down channels of 2e MO in the Mn@B are half-occupied, only the energy lowest SOC states will be occupied. Specifically, $E_{1}^{(z)}$ state is occupied in $z$ magnetization, and $E_{1}^{(x)}$ state is occupied in $x$ magnetization. Moreover, it is seen that $E_{1}^{(z)}$ is lower than $E_{1}^{(x)}$, so the easy axis is along $z$ direction in the Mn@B.

Fe dopant renders the most appreciable MAE. According to above analysis, the first order SOC contribution for MAE can be excluded because of the full occupied 2e MO. To reveal the essential MAE origin, we need to consider the second order SOC. Generally, the second order perturbation energy can be describe as

$$
\Delta E^{(2)}=-\sum_{n} \frac{\left|\left\langle 0\left|\hat{H}_{s o}\right| n\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{E_{n}-E_{0}}=-\lambda^{2} \sum_{n} \frac{\left|\left\langle 0\left|\hat{\sigma} \cdot \hat{L}\right| n\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{E_{n}-E_{0}}, \quad(2)
$$

where notations 0 and $n$ represent the multi-electron ground and excited states, respectively. For first excited state, we only consider one electron jumping from the spin-down channel of 2e state to the spin-down channel of $2 \mathrm{a}_{1}$ state, and therefore formula (2) can be approximately rewritten as

$$
\Delta E^{(2)}=-\lambda^{2} \frac{\left|\left\langle 2 \mathrm{e}\left|\hat{L}_{x} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{x}+\hat{L}_{y} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{y}+\hat{L}_{z} \cdot \hat{\sigma}_{z}\right| 2 \mathrm{a}_{1}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{E_{1}-E_{0}}.
$$

When the spin is in $x$ direction,

$$
\Delta E^{(2)}=-\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\right)^{2} \frac{\left|\left\langle 2 \mathrm{e}\left|\hat{L}_{x}\right| 2 \mathrm{a}_{1}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{E_{1}-E_{0}}.
$$

2e state is a hybridization of $3 d x y, 3 d x^{2}-y^{2}, 3 d x z$, and $3 d y z$, while $2 \mathrm{a}_{1}$ state is mainly from $3 d z^{2}$. Matrix element $\left\langle 3 d z^{2}\left|\hat{L}_{x}\right| 3 d y z\right\rangle$ has a nonzero value of $i \sqrt{3}$, so $\Delta E^{(2)}<0$. When the spin is in $y$ axis, matrix element $\left\langle 3 d z^{2}\left|\hat{L}_{y}\right| 3 d x z\right\rangle$ is nonzero, so $\Delta E^{(2)}<0$ as well.

For $z$ orientation of the spin,

$$
\Delta E^{(2)}=-\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\right)^{2} \frac{\left|\left\langle 2 \mathrm{e}\left|\hat{L}_{z}\right| 2 \mathrm{a}_{1}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{E_{1}-E_{0}}.
$$

All matrix elements are zero, and thus, $\Delta E^{(2)}$ is zero. It is clear that easy axis of Fe@B is in the h-BN plane.

In conclusion, considerable magnetic anisotropy could exist in TM@B systems. Particularly, MAE of Fe@B is 1.19 meV with an easy axis in the h-BN layer, and the MAE of Mn@B is 0.63 meV with an easy axis in $z$ orientation. Theoretical analyses based on perturbation method and molecular orbital theory clarify that the MAE of Mn@B mainly originates from the unquenched orbital momentum of the spin-down channel of 2e MO and the MAE of Fe@B is mainly contributed by the second order SOC between the spin-down channels of $2 \mathrm{e}$ and $2 \mathrm{a}_{1}$. The dopant determining magnetic anisotropy clarifies the low-dimensional materials are of predicted advantages in spintronics due to some intrinsic characters such as lowered symmetry and reduced coordinates.
