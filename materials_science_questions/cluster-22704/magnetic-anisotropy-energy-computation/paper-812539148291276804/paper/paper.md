## ABSTRACT:
Two-dimensional (2D) intrinsic ferromagnetic semiconductors (FMSs) received huge attention due to their fascinating electron charge and spin properties, which are promising candidates for spintronic devices. In this work, we propose a class of monolayer FMS in alkali-based chromium chalcogenides (XCrY₂, X = Li and Na; Y = S, Se, and Te). The proposed monolayer XCrY₂ is expected to be exfoliated from their corresponding bulk counterparts, which are confirmed by first-principles calculations. Our calculations indicate that these monolayer FMS shows the ferromagnetic ground states possessing the high Curie temperatures using the Ising and Heisenberg models. The hybrid functional calculations show that LiCrSe₂, NaCrSe₂, and NaCrTe₂ have desirable direct band gaps. In addition, it is found that the spin−orbit (SOC) effect can tremendously change the effective mass of the holes, resulting in higher hole mobility (up to $3 \times 10^3$ cm² v⁻¹ s⁻¹). Our study provides a feasible way to realize 2D intrinsic FMS.

**KEYWORDS:** two dimensional, FM semiconductors, high Curie temperatures, direct band gaps, high mobility

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Two-dimensional (2D) long-range ferromagnetic semiconduc- tors (FMSs) are promising materials due to their rich electronic and optical properties, which can be used in magneto-optic and magnetoelectric applications such as data storage, spin valves, and ultrafast photodetectors.¹⁻⁵

Previously, the ferromagnetic (FM) order in 2D crystals was mainly achieved by defect engineering,⁶⁷ composition engineering,⁸ or proximity effect⁹,¹⁰ via the 2D layers' contact with magnetic substrates. In all of these schemes, the introduced magnetic responses are extrinsic and local. In contrast, it is desirable to seek for 2D intrinsic ferromagnetism contributed by parent structures, which control the charge carriers and spin by an electric field. Intrinsic ferromagnetism plays a crucial role in understanding the fundamental physics between spin and electronic processes, and it also opens the door for future spintronic applications.¹¹⁻¹³

Recently, Zhang and Xu et al. have experimentally realized the 2D intrinsic long-range magnetism in layered materials,¹⁴,¹⁵ i.e., 2D FM Cr₂Ge₂Te₆¹⁴ and CrI₃.¹⁵ The Curie temperatures ($T_c$) are 40 K in the bilayer Cr₂Ge₂Te₆¹⁴ and 45 K in the single-layer CrI₃.¹⁵ Indeed, their works have inspired the exploration of more 2D pristine FM semiconductivity structures. Up till now, many 2D intrinsic FMS have been theoretically predicted, such as MAY₃, MBY₃, MY₃, MY₂, MY, MnNC/MnYC, M₂Y₃, and MC₃,¹⁶⁻³⁰ (M = V, Cr to Ni; A, B, Y, C = groups IV, V, VI, and VII elements). Theoretical calculations proposed that some 2D intrinsic FMS such as CrWI₆ and CrWGe₂Te₆ can be formed at room temperature via a small in-plane strain.³¹ However, so far, only a few 2D intrinsic FMS monolayers, M(S/Se)₂,²⁰,²¹ Cr₂Ge₂Te₆,¹⁴ CrX₃, (X = Cl, Br, I),¹⁵,³²,³³ Fe₃GeTe₂³⁴ and Fe₅GeTe₂,³⁵ have been experimentally synthesized with low Curie temperatures ($T_c$). The research of 2D intrinsic FMS with high $T_c$ and stability is a huge challenge. One of the feasible ways is to exfoliate it from their bulk magnetic materials.

In this work, we propose a class of 2D FMS, which can be exfoliated from their corresponding bulk materials³⁶⁻³⁸ and further confirmed by high-throughput first-principles calcu- lations. The experimental analysis demonstrates that NaCrS₂ can be easily cleaved³⁸ from the bulk material. The proposed XCrY₂ monolayers are calculated to possess the FM ground states with high $T_c$. Importantly, the Heyd−Scuseria− Ernzerhof (HSE)³⁹,⁴⁰ calculations reveal that LiCrSe₂, NaCrSe₂, and NaCrTe₂ have desirable direct band gaps. Furthermore, it is found that the spin−orbit coupling (SOC) effect can significantly reduce the holes' effective mass, resulting in higher hole mobility. ## 2. METHODS

We performed density functional theory (DFT) calculations implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP).41,42 The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) parameterized by the Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof (PBE)43 functional was used to describe the exchange−correlation energy, and the electron−ion interaction was modeled by projector-augmented wave (PAW).44 We used PBE + $U_\text{eff}$ ($U$ = 3, 3.5, and 4 eV), and HSE06 functional was employed to calculate the electronic structures. The vacuum along the z-direction and the energy cutoff are set to 22 Å and 650 eV, respectively. All of the geometric relaxation and self-consistent iteration are converged until the force on each atom and energy difference between electronic steps are smaller than $10^{-4}$ eV Å$^{-1}$ and $10^{-6}$ eV, respectively. For HSE06 calculations, the k-point meshes are set to 16 × 16 × 1, 25 × 25 × 1, and 20 × 20 × 1 for geometric relaxation, band structure, and the energy difference between FM and AFM in a (2 × 2) supercell, respectively. The van der Waals corrections (D3)45 were introduced to describe the effect of van der Waals interactions in all calculations. The high-throughput structure search was performed using Universal Structure Predictor: Evolutionary Xtallography (USPEX).46

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

We first calculated the cleavage energies ($E_\text{c}$) of $\text{XCrY}_2$ monolayers from their bulk materials,36,47 and the corresponding results are listed in Table 1. The cleavage energies of these monolayers are in the range from 1.42 ($\text{LiCrTe}_2$) to 2.54 ($\text{NaCrS}_2$) J m$^{-2}$, which suggests that these cleavage energies are larger than those of layered bulk materials, such as black phosphorus (0.367 J m$^{-2}$)48 and $\text{GeP}_3$ (1.14 J m$^{-2}$).49 Fortunately, Scheel et al. experimentally showed that the (001) surface of $\text{NaCrS}_2$ can be easily cleaved from its bulk counterpart.38 Therefore, other $\text{XCrY}_2$ monolayers are also expected to be cleaved from their bulk counterparts. After optimization, the monolayer structures were dipolar due to the asymmetrical upper and lower surfaces, as shown in Figure 1a,b. The $\text{XCrY}_2$ monolayer with a hexagonal lattice (Figure 1c) consists of four atomic layers. The minimum magnetic unit forms a triangular lattice and each Cr atom has six nearest neighboring atoms. We further performed a high-throughput structure search (USPEX)46 to confirm that these monolayers are the most energetically favorable.

Phonon spectra exhibit no imaginary phonon modes, as shown in Figure 2a−f, confirming the dynamic stability of these monolayers. These results show that the proposed $\text{XCrY}_2$ monolayers are stable at room temperature. Additionally, $\text{NaCrS}_2$ is deposited on the Ag(111) surface (see Figure S2) due to their small lattice match (0.6%). The average adsorption energy is $-1.15$ eV/(Na−Cr-2S), which indicates the possibility of the synthesis of $\text{XCrY}_2$ monolayers on the suitable substrates.

We extracted the exchange coupling interaction between the Cr atoms. Here, the total energies of $XCrY_2$ with FM and AFM states can be described from their effective 2D Ising model $^{S1,S2}$

$$
\mathrm{H}=-\sum_{i, j} J S_{i} S_{j} \tag{1}
$$

where $J$ is the spin-exchange coupling parameter of the nearest-neighbor Cr atoms. The Ising Hamiltonian of FM and AFM states of $p(2 \times 2)$ supercell can be expressed by the following equation $^{50,51}$

$$
E_{\mathrm{FM}}=12 J S_{i} S_{j} ; \quad E_{\mathrm{AFM}}=-4 J S_{i} S_{j} \tag{2}
$$

The Hamiltonian of the Heisenberg model is described by

$$
\mathrm{H}=-\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i, j} J_{i j} S_{i} S_{j}-K \sum_{i}\left(S_{i}^{z}\right)^{2} \tag{3}
$$

where the first term contributes to the short-range exchange interactions up to the nearest neighbors and the other term is the onsite MAE energy from the calculations. The value of $S$ is $3/2$, which originates from the magnetic moments of each Cr atoms ($3.0\ \mu_{\text{B}}$). Our calculations show that the value of $J$ is up to $9.91$ meV for the $NaCrTe_2$ monolayer, while the rest are listed in Table 1. We have calculated $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ of these $XCrY_2$ monolayers using the Monte Carlo simulations. The $80 \times 80$ supercell was used in all simulations, and each lattice point includes one Cr atom. The warm time and the loops are set to $10^{8}$ and $4 \times 10^{9}$, respectively. Our results indicate that these $XCrY_2$ monolayers are significantly above the room temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ (up to $880$ K), as shown in Figure S4. It is well known that the magnetic anisotropy is largely overestimated in the Ising model that results in a larger $T_{\mathrm{c}}$. Thus, we also employ a Heisenberg model in which the MAE is treated as a single-ion anisotropy to carry out the Monte Carlo simulations for $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ (up to $285$ K), and the results are shown in Table 1. The results indicate that $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ (see Figure 3) of the Heisenberg model is about three times smaller than that of the Ising model. In addition, the large potential difference ($\Delta V > 1.23$ eV) between the upper and lower surfaces is found in these $XCrY_2$ monolayers, and the values of $\Delta V$ are given in Table 1 and Figure S5. Due to the large intrinsic $\Delta V$, these materials may be used in the field of photocatalytic water splitting. $^{53}$ On the other hand, the MAE is also important for 2D FM materials, which can counteract the amplitude divergence of 2D monolayers. Here, we used the total energy method by calculating the energy difference (HSE06) between the in-plane and out-of-plane cases. The small MAE ($\sim10^{-1}$ meV) is found in these monolayers, as shown in Table 1, similar to prior results. $^{23}$ It is worth pointing out the contributions of normal to the plane ($\mathrm{d}_{xz}, \mathrm{d}_{yz}$, and $\mathrm{d}_{z^2}$) are larger than that to the in-plane ($\mathrm{d}_{xy}$ and $\mathrm{d}_{x^2-y^2}$), as shown in Figure S3.

The nature of band gap is an important factor for 2D materials. Therefore, the electronic properties of $XCrY_2$ monolayers have been performed without/with SOC using the HSE06 calculations. The results indicate that all of them are FMS. As shown in Figure 4, it is clear that $LiCrS_2$, $LiCrTe_2$, and $NaCrS_2$ have indirect band gaps, while $LiCrSe_2$, $NaCrSe_2$, and $NaCrTe_2$ have direct band gaps. The indirect band gaps of $LiCrS_2$, $LiCrTe_2$, and $NaCrS_2$ are $2.30$ ($2.24$), $1.38$ ($0.99$), and $1.01$ ($0.92$) eV without/with SOC, respectively. Since the high carrier mobility of 2D FMS is beneficial for magnetoelectric and magneto-optic applications, we systematically calculated the carrier mobilities of these $XCrY_{2}$ FMS monolayers using the potential method at room temperature (300 K). The detailed process of calculations is shown in the Supporting Information. $^{23,55}$ It is clear that the CBM of Libased FMS is more dispersive than Na-based FMS (see Figure 4), which indicates that the Na-based FMS monolayer may have high carrier mobilities. It is found that our calculated mobilities are larger than other predicated 2D FMS monolayers. $^{23}$ These $XCrY_{2}$ FMS monolayers are promising candidates for magnetic sensing technologies considering their larger carrier motilities.

## 4. CONCLUSIONS
We have proposed a class of 2D FMS alkali-based chromium chalcogenides. The FM ground states are verified in the $XCrY_{2}$ monolayers using both PBE $+U$ and HSE functionals. We use both the Ising and Heisenberg models to estimate $T_{c}$. Our results show that $T_{c}$ are between 640-880 and 209-285 K using the Ising and Heisenberg models, respectively. Moreover, our results reveal that $LiCrS_{2}$, $LiCrTe_{2}$, and $NaCrS_{2}$ have indirect band gaps, while $LiCrSe_{2}$, $NaCrSe_{2}$, and $NaCrTe_{2}$ possess fascinating direct band gaps. Interestingly, the SOC effect can tremendously influence the effective mass of the holes, which results in larger hole mobility (up to $3.0\times10^{3}$ $\mathrm{cm^{2}\ v^{-1}\ s^{-1}}$) for $NaCrTe_{2}$. In addition, the large intrinsic electric field $(\Delta V>1.23\ \mathrm{eV})$ has been found in these FMS monolayers, which may have the potential to be used in the photocatalysis for water splitting.
