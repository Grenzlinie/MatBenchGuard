# Magnetization and magnetic anisotropy of metallophthalocyanine molecules from the first principles calculations

Junhua Wang, $^{1}$ Yisheng Shi, $^{1}$ Juexian Cao, $^{2}$ and Ruqian Wu $^{2,a)}$
$^{1}$Physical Science and Electronic Techniques School, Yancheng Teachers College, Yancheng 224002, People's Republic of China
$^{2}$Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of California, Irvine, Irvine, California 92697-4575, USA

(Received 22 October 2008; accepted 30 January 2009; published online 23 March 2009)

Using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave method, we explored the magnetic anisotropy energies of metallophthalocyanine molecules with Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni. Their spin magnetic moments can be expressed as $8-n\mu_{B}$ ($n=5,6,7,8$ for Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni). Interestingly, Mn-phthalocyanine has a large orbital magnetic moment of $0.19\mu_{B}$ and a sizable magnetic anisotropy energy of 2.72 meV useable for applications. © 2009 American Institute of Physics.
[DOI: 10.1063/1.3100783]

Metal phthalocyanine ($MPc$) complexes have attracted extensive research interest $^{1–4}$ because of their peculiar physical and chemical properties that are suitable for broad technological applications such as electrochemical sensors, $^{5}$ molecular electronics and magnets, $^{6}$ photovoltaic devices, $^{7}$ and low dimensional electrochromism. $^{8}$ As a step toward practical utilization, highly ordered and stable $MPc$ monolayers have been synthesized on sapphire substrates and gold buffer layers. $^{9}$ The metal atom implemented in a Pc molecule has square-planar coordination with four pyloric N atoms and typically has the $M^{+2}$ charge state. As important molecular magnets, $^{10}$ $3d$ transition $MPc$ were systematically studied in the bulk form, and it is well known that only the $\beta$-form polymorph of MnPc is ferromagnetic. $^{11,12}$ However, their Curie temperatures, $T_{C}=\sim 10$ K, are typically too low for practical applications. $^{13,14}$ Establishing high-energy barrier is essential to contain thermal fluctuations. To this end, one needs to understand the electronic and magnetic properties of these systems and, in particular, to find means that may lead to substantial enhancements of their magnetic anisotropy energies (MAEs).

In the present studies, we determined the magnetization and magnetic anisotropy of Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni in $MPc$ molecule using the density functional approaches. The $MPc$ are half-metallic, and the magnetic moment adopts integer numbers in $\mu_{B}$ from $3.0\mu_{B}$ for MnPc to $0.0\mu_{B}$ for NiPc. We found that the spin density features, charge states, and MAEs of these molecules can be explained in a simple framework, which is very useful to make predictions and provide guidance for furthering experimental explorations.

$Ab$ $initio$ density functional calculations were performed using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave method $^{15}$ at the level of generalized gradient approximation. $^{16}$ To mimic the appropriate environment for an isolated molecule, we used a slab model that has infinitely large vacuums on both sides and a large unit cell in the lateral plan, $a=20$ Å. The $\Gamma$ point is used to sample the tiny two-dimensional Brillouin zone. Plane waves with an energy cutoff of 16 Ry are used for the basis expansion in the interstitial region, while spherical harmonics with a maximum angular quantum number of $l_{\text{max}}=8$ are used in the muffin-tin region. The core electrons are treated fully relativistically by solving the Dirac equation, whereas the spin-orbit interactions among valence states are invoked second variationally in the self-consistence loops. The torque approach is adopted for the determination of MAE. $^{17}$

The main results are listed in Table I. The optimized distance between metal and the nearest nitrogen atoms is 1.91–1.95 Å in the $MPc$ molecules, in good accordance with results of previous experiments and calculations. We found that metal atoms adopt the $M^{+2}$ charge state in all $MPc$ molecules. For example, the isosurfaces of charge density difference shown in Fig. 1(a) for CoPc indicate that electrons deplete from the metal atom and accumulate in regions between $M$-N and C-C within the first ring. From the side view, it is obvious that the metal $xz$, $yz$ $d$-states gain electrons from their $z^{2}$ and $x^{2}-y^{2}$ counterparts. The spin densities of different $MPc$ molecules have different features, but it is clear that the spin polarization is predominantly around the metal atoms, in conjunction with weak induced spin polarization around adjacent N and C atoms. The spin density of CoPc is given in Fig. 1(b) as an example. It shows the $z^{2}$ character feature, as was also observed in previous x-ray measurements. It is interesting to point out that the spin magnetic moments are integers and decrease linearly from $3\mu_{B}$ for MnPc to $0\mu_{B}$ for NiPc. We found a strong enhancement in the orbital magnetic moment for MnPc, FePc, and CoPc. For instance, the orbital magnetic moment of MnPc is $0.19\mu_{B}$, the largest for Mn atom in different environments.

As elucidated below, this stems from the peculiar electronic properties of metal atoms in these molecules.

To discuss the origin of the electronic and magnetic properties, we need to understand the arrangements of molecular orbitals of $MPc$. Under the influence of the N and C ligand atoms, the five $3d$-orbitals of metal atoms split into four energy levels, namely, $a_{1g}(z^{2})$, $b_{1g}(x^{2}-y^{2})$, $e_{g}(xz,yz)$, and $b_{2g}(xy)$. Typically, the energy sequence (from the lowest of these $M$-$3d$ states was assigned as $b_{2g}e_{g}a_{1g}b_{1g}$ in $MPc$ molecules. $^{18}$ However, the actual arrangements of these molecular orbitals vary for different molecules or even different spins from the curves of the projected density of states (PDOS) in Fig. 2. The only common feature is that the $M$-$b_{1g}(x^{2}-y^{2})$ orbital loses charge to the N-$2p$ states and remains unoccupied in both spin channels. $M$-$b_{2g}(xy)$ and $M$-$e_{g}(xz,yz)$ states strongly hybridize with the C- and N-$2p$ states and split into several features in Fig. 2. This results in a complex charge redistribution pattern shown in Fig. 1(a). The nonbonding $a_{1g}(z^{2})$ state typically lies in the middle, except in the majority spin channel for CoPc. For NiPc, the $b_{2g}$, $e_{g}$, and $a_{1g}$ states are fully occupied in both spin channels, and thus the magnetic moment becomes zero. It is easy to see that $MPc$ molecules adopt $S$=3/2, 1, 1/2, and 0 spin states from $M$=Mn to Ni. As listed in Table I, the total magnetic moment varies as $8-n\mu_{B}$ ($n$=5,6,7,8 for Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni, respectively). Note that if one more electron is added to NiPc, such as in CuPc, the spin degeneracy is broken again and the spin magnetic moment is $1.0\mu_{B}$. From the energy diagram, the spin density of CuPc is expected to show the $b_{1g}$ feature, exactly as was demonstrated by several authors. $^{19,20}$

The important finding here is that MnPc molecules have sizable MAEs. In particular, we found that the MAE for MnPc is 2.72 meV, with an easy axis perpendicular to the plane of molecule. Again, this MAE is large for Mn, which typically has it $3d$ shell filled (empty) in the majority (minority) spin channel. From the PDOS curve, we found that positive MAE results from the spin-orbit coupling interaction between $e_{g}(\uparrow)$ and $a_{1g}(\downarrow)$. Figure 3 gives the dependence of $E_{MCA}$ on the band filling, attained through a rigid band model. $^{21}$ It is interesting that FePc and CoPc may also have high positive $E_{MCA}$ when a few electrons are removed. In the neutral FePc and CoPc molecules, the Fermi levels lie between of the $a_{1g}$ and $e_{g}$ states in the minority spin channel. These states have different magnetic quantum numbers and thus produce negative MAEs through the $L_{x,y}$ matrices, according the spin-orbit coupling Hamiltonian.

A clear understanding of the magnetic anisotropy of free $MPc$ molecules is imperative in guiding the design of $MPc$-based molecular magnets. It is clear from Fig. 2 that the arrangement of molecular orbitals is rather complex. The value and even sign of $E_{MCA}$ sensitively depend on the energy ordering and the position of the actual Fermi level. Therefore, $ab$ initio calculations are essential to predict the magnetic anisotropy of individual systems. However, there is an obvious strategy to produce large positive $E_{MCA}$ through breaking the degeneracy of two $e_{g}$ states. Since they have the same magnetic quantum numbers ($m=\pm1$), they should provide large positive contributions to $E_{MCA}$ through the $L_{z}$ matrix if the Fermi level lies right between them. Practically, this is doable using rectangular substrates. Another parameter is the number of electron, which may also be used to control $E_{MCA}$, as demonstrated in Fig. 3. More investigations along this direction will be done in the future.
