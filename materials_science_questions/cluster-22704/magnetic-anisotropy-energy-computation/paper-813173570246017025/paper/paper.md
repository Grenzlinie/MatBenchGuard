PHYSICAL REVIEW B, VOLUME 63, 104421

# Calculated magnetocrystalline anisotropy of a FePd ordered alloy:
## Electron-density dependence on the direction of magnetization

D. García, R. Casero, M. Vázquez, and A. Hernando

Instituto de Magnetismo Aplicado, UCM-RENFE-Instituto de Ciencia de Materiales (CSIC), P.O. Box 155,
28230 Las Rozas, Madrid, Spain

(Received 26 May 2000; revised manuscript received 21 August 2000; published 20 February 2001)

The magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy of a CuAu(I) Fe₅₀Pd₅₀ alloy has been calculated by using a full potential linear augmented planar wave (FLAPW) method. The tetragonal unit-cell distances have been extracted from the x-ray data of ordered FePd films grown by sputtering technique [P. Caro, A. Cebollada, D. Ravelosona, F. Briones, D. García, M. Vázquez, and A. Hernando, J. Appl. Phys. 81, 5050 (1997)]. This ordered alloy presents a high uniaxial magnetic anisotropy with an easy magnetization direction oriented along the tetragonal axis (c axis) of its unit cell. We reproduce the measured uniaxial character as well as the magnitude of its magnetic anisotropy. Three ground-state configurations have been calculated attending to the direction of the magnetization relative to the c axis: 0°, 45°, and 90°. The comparison among these states enables us to describe the changes of the electronic density associated to the changes of the direction of the magnetization.

CuAu(I)-type chemically ordered phases of transition-metal alloys (TM/M) have become clear candidates to be used as magnetic recording media due to their remarkable magnetic anisotropies. This tetragonal ordered phase consists of alternating planes of TM and M ions with an unique fourfold symmetry axis oriented along the piling direction of the homogeneous planes. Its unit cell can be viewed as a fct structure with the TM ions placed at the corners and in the (0.5,0.5,0.0) positions and the M ions at the (0.0,0.5,0.5) and (0.5,0.0,0.5) positions. The c axis corresponds to the piling direction of the planes. Many authors have reported on the uniaxial magnetocrystalline anisotropy of equiatomic FePt,¹ CoPt,² and FePd (Refs. 2–8) alloys ordered following the CuAu(I) structure with their easy magnetization axes being their respective c axes. The experimental works concentrate on the searching of the optima conditions needed to obtain a high degree of chemical order²,⁵,⁹ since a direct relationship exists between the degree of ordering and the magnitude of the magnetic anisotropy. Caro et al.³,⁵ have analyzed the influence of the growing conditions of sputtered FePd thin films on the resulting magnetic anisotropy. They produced highly textured alloys by growing the films at 500 °C onto a 100 nm thick Pt buffer layer with a uniaxial magnetic anisotropy of around 1 MJ/m³.³,¹⁰ For lower growing temperatures and thinner Pt buffer layers they obtained chemically disordered alloys with a fcc polycrystalline structure without any tetragonal c axis nor appreciable magnetic anisotropy.³ The FePd ordered phase is characterized by a c axis slightly smaller than the a axis of the described unit cell, the bulk c/a ratios being in the 0.975–0.960 range.⁹ Previous theoretical works on FePd have analyzed structures for different c/a ratios: 1,¹¹,¹² 0.96.⁸,¹³ Galanakis et al.⁷ have calculated the energies of the alloy for different a and c/a values. They obtained a global minimum for a c/a ratio of 0.984 and a lattice parameter of 3.73 Å and the experimental easy magnetization axis by using a local spin density approximation for the exchange-correlation energy. Their results calculated with a generalized gradient approximation (GGA) term described in Ref. 14 differed in the value of the minimum and even in the predicted easy magnetization axis (along the [100] direction). The sputtered FePd films modeled by us showed a c/a ratio smaller than the bulk and than the calculated minimum, 0.946,³ due to the lattice mismatch between the Pt buffer layer and the alloy. We report here on first-principle calculations of these FePd ordered films with such a high tetragonal distortion (c/a) for different orientations of the magnetization. Our main objective has been to describe, at the electronic level, the mechanisms responsible for the magnetocrystalline anisotropy of this compound, discriminating the role played by each metallic ion. The electronic densities have been calculated by using the spin-density functional theory with the GGA for the exchange-correlation energy of the electron gas due to Perdew et al.¹⁵ This GGA expression differs from the one used by Galanakis et al.⁷ We have used a full potential linear augmented planar wave method (FLAPW) developed by Blaha et al.¹⁶ The spin-orbit interaction has been introduced as a second order perturbation to the spin-defined states. The Brillouin zone of the alloy has been sampled in 2048 k points. Other authors signaled acceptable convergence of the magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy (MAE) of this compound with a number of k points in the range of the number used here: Solovyev et al.¹⁷ presented a calculation of the MAE of the FePd alloy as a function of the number of k points and they obtained rather acceptable values of the MAE by using numbers of k points in the range (1000–5000) of the number used in our work, Galanakis et al.⁷ obtained differences in the values of the MAE lower than 1% for number of k points in the range: 1458 up to 6750. The same k points have been used to calculate the electronic densities of the three cases. Each calculus has been converged by independent self-consistence cycles; i.e., each case parted from the atomic-like electronic densities and converged with a Hamiltonian which included the particular spin-orbit interaction in every self-consistency

The lengths of the axes were $(a,a,c)$ $=(3.89,3.89,3.68)$ Å, as obtained from the x-ray data of an ordered FePd film. $^{3,5}$ The radii of the spheres used to augment the monoelectronic planar waves are 1.30 and 1.06 Å for Fe and Pd, respectively. Convergence of each calculus was achieved whenever the difference in energy of the last three self-consistency cycles was lower than $10^{-3}$ mRy/cell. Three cases have been calculated by changing the direction of the magnetization with respect to the unit cell axes: case A, with magnetization (M) along the (0,0,1) direction; case B, with M along the $(1,1,a/c)$ direction; and case C with M along the (1,1,0) direction. In order to vary the orientation of the magnetization we change the angle formed by the spin axis with the $c$-axis of the structure; this $c$-axis coincides with the orbital quantization axis. The direction of this orbital quantization axis is kept constant in the three cases. The electronic density, the total energy and the densities of states (DOS) have been computed from each case.

Table I shows the main parameters calculated in the case A. The values of charges and magnetic polarizations of the other two cases differ in less than $10^{-3}$ $e^-$/cell of those shown in Table I. Polarization values are obtained after the subtraction of the minority spin density from the majority spin density. The charges and polarizations associated to each ion come out from the integration of the spin densities inside their respective spheres. Interstitial values correspond to the integration of the densities in the rest of the unit cell. It is worth mentioning the values of the magnetic polarizations of Fe, $3.04\ \mu_{\rm B}$, and Pd, $0.38\ \mu_{\rm B}$. The Fe polarization is notably higher than the 2.2 bohr magnetons of the bcc Fe, meanwhile the Pd ion acquires a slight polarization due to the spin-dependent crystalline field. Both polarizations are in agreement with previous theoretical data. $^{11-13}$ Dichroism experiments carried out in this alloy confirm the Pd polarization. $^{18,19}$ The value of the magnetization given in Table I only considers the spin magnetization, not the orbital component. Even with this approximation, its magnitude is also within the error margins of the experimental data, 1.6 $\pm 0.24$ T, $^{3}$ $1.5\pm 0.12$ T, $^{6}$ and 1.38 T. $^{2,4}$ The easy magnetization axis of the alloy is oriented along the $c$ axis of the structure since the lowest total energy corresponds to the case A. Table II shows the increment of the calculated total energy of the cases B and C with respect to case A. The error assigned to the energy values comes from the convergence criterion of the calculus. The energy increment of case B is half the increment of the case C, thus evidencing the uniaxial character of the magnetic anisotropy. The value of the magnetocrystalline anisotropy constant ($K$) is in agreement with the magnitude measured in the FePd films. $^{3,10}$ The experimental $K$ values depend on the particular degrees of crystallization and $c/a$ ratios of the samples. They range from 1.94 (Ref. 2) and 1.8 MJ/m$^3$, $^{4}$ down to 1.42 (Ref. 2) and 1.34 MJ/m$^3$. $^{19}$ Solovyev $et\ al.$ obtained theoretical values of the anisotropy constant of the FePd alloy higher than the $K$ value calculated here, 1.3 MJ/m$^3$ or 2.0 MJ/m$^3$. $^{17}$ Galanakis $et\ al.^{7}$ got the wrong easy magnetization axis when they used a GGA term for the exchange-correlation energy. Here, we have used a new GGA expression $^{15}$ deduced by the same authors of the one used in Ref. 7 and the correct easy axis has been predicted.

In order to get a deeper insight on the processes that account for the energy dependence upon the orientation of the magnetization, we have compared the magnetic moment densities close to the ions at certain representative planes of the unit cell. These planes are the one defined by the $\langle 001\rangle$ and $\langle 1\text{-}10\rangle$ directions and the one defined by the $\langle 1\text{-}10\rangle$ and the $\langle 110\rangle$ directions. The magnetic moment near the Fe ion is distributed along zones with symmetries of $d_{z^2}-d_{xz}$ $-d_{yz}$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbitals (see left column of Fig. 1). Minority electrons do not occupy these zones which expand to the Pd ion. On the contrary, the magnetic moment near the Pd ion is mainly distributed along zones with the symmetry of a $d_{xy}$ orbital (left column of Fig. 2). Minority electrons tend to occupy different zones depending on the nature of the closest ion. There is no overlapping between the rich or poor regions in minority electrons of each ion. The subtraction plots of Figs. 1 and 2 can be read as the slight redistributions of the minority electron charge which occurs when magnetization is moved away from its hard axis, $(1,-1,0)$, to its easy axis, $(0,0,1)$. The changes of the magnetic moment density when magnetization is reoriented are associated to changes of the minority electron densities since the electronic charge densities (not shown) exhibit opposite changes to those shown in Figs. 1 and 2. The existence of a certain amount of the minority charge moving jointly to follow the magnetization can be straightforwardly related to the existence of some unquenched orbital moment whose particular orientation is determined by the magnetization's one. So, the orbital moment of the structure needs to be anisotropic. If there were no anisotropy in the total orbital moment of the compound, there would not be any redistribution of charge associated to the variation of the orientation of magnetization. Previous works $^{7,8,13,17}$ have centered the discussion on the origin of the MAE of this alloy in the determination of the orbital moment along certain relevant directions of the unit cell. Daalderop $et$ $al.^{13}$ obtained a clear relationship between the anisotropy of the orbital moment and the corresponding MAE of the FePd when the bandfilling of the compound was varied. The more anisotropic is the orbital moment the higher is the MAE. Instead of calculating the projections of the orbital moment along certain unit cell directions, we have opted to evidence this anisotropy in the orbital moment by describing its origin: the magnetization-sensitive distribution of the minority spins.

In this way, it is seen how the minority spin electrons near the Fe ion tend to gather in the plane formed by the Fe ions. When the magnetization is oriented at $45^\circ$ far from $c$ axis, the minority electron density decreases in the $d_{z^2}$ orbital (see Fig. 1). This decrement in $d_{z^2}$ is intensified when the magnetization is aligned with the $c$ axis. A decrement of the minority spin electrons also appears in the $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ orbitals. These electrons tend to populate the $d_{xy}$ orbital when the magnetization is directed towards its easy direction. The Fe-Fe bonds are strengthened with the magnetization along the $c$ axis since the minority electron density is increased along the directions oriented to the closest Fe ions. The minority electron density near the Pd ion evolves in a way similar to the density close to the Fe ion, as can be seen in Fig. 2. When the magnetization reaches its easy direction, the minority spin electrons congregate in the $d_{xy}$ orbital, thus reinforcing also the Pd-Pd bond. Summing up, a small amount of the minority spin density can be displaced through the movement of the magnetization evidencing the anisotropy of the orbital moment of the alloy. To comprehend why such an increment of energy occurs, we have calculated the density of states (DOS) per spin of the alloy. Figure 3 shows the total DOS throughout the unit cell and the DOS inside each of the spheres centered at the Fe or Pd ion, respectively. We have only calculated the DOS with energies closer than 1 Ry to the Fermi level. The states of majority spin with appreciable densities have energies far below the Fermi energy of the solid. These states are expanded through the surroundings of both ions, as we can see from the values of their densities inside the spheres of each ion. We can consider that the majority spin electrons form the metallic bond due to their presence throughout the entire unit cell. These states, which come from the $3d$ Fe and $4d$ Pd atomic orbitals, are consequently hybridized in the alloy. The distribution of the minority spin states is completely different. Their total DOS presents a valley of low density around $-1.3$ eV. For lower energies the minority spin states are located predominantly near the Pd ion, meanwhile the minority states of energies higher than $-1.3$ eV are found near the Fe ion. There is barely any energy overlapping between these states. This is an indication of the existence of a repulsive interaction between the minority spin electrons. They show an anti-bonding character evidenced by the localization of their minority spin energy bands at the surroundings of each ion. Furthermore, the zones where the magnetic moments concentrate are different depending on the ion, $d_{z^2}-d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbitals at the Fe site and $d_{xy}$ orbital at the Pd site (see Figs. 1 and 2). Therefore, minority spin electrons tend to be apart to diminish the repulsion among them. When the magnetization lies along the $c$ axis of the structure, the minority spin density is increased in the chemically homogeneous planes, formed by the Fe-Fe and the Pd-Pd. The minority spin charge extends along the $d_{xy}$ of each ion, thus reinforcing the bonds of each ion with their four closest identical ions. In summary we have modeled the electronic structure of a equiatomic FePd ordered alloy for three different orienta- tions of its magnetization. The measured easy magnetization direction and the magnitude of its magnetocrystalline anisot- ropy has been reproduced by first-principles calculations by considering the spin-orbit interaction as a second-order per- turbation to the electronic states. The origin of the high value of the magnetocrystalline anisotropy has been interpreted by analyzing the spatial distributions of the minority spin charge across the tetragonal unit cell. A repulsive interaction of the minority electrons localized at both ions is claimed to ac- count for the uniaxial magnetic anisotropy of the alloy. The distribution of the minority spin density has a slight depen- dence on the orientation of the magnetization. When the magnetization lies along the $a$ axes of the unit cell, the mi nority spin electrons are closer to each other and their net repelling energy is increased. This repelling energy is re- duced when the magnetization aligns with the $c$ axis, thus forcing the minority spin density to move to the chemically homogeneous planes and increasing the average distance among these minority spin electrons.
