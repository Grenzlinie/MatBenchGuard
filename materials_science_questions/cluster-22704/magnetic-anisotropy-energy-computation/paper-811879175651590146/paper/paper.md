# Rashba-Type Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya Interaction, Perpendicular Magnetic Anisotropy, and Skyrmion States at 2D Materials/Co Interfaces
Ali Hallal, $^\dagger$ Jinghua Liang, $^\dagger$ Fatima Ibrahim, Hongxin Yang, Albert Fert, and Mairbek Chshiev*

Cite This: https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.1c01713
Read Online

ABSTRACT: We report a significant Dzyaloshinskii−Moriya interaction (DMI) and perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) at interfaces comprising hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) and Co. By comparing the behavior of these phenomena at graphene/Co and h-BN/Co interfaces, it is found that the DMI in the latter increases as a function of Co thickness and beyond three monolayers stabilizes with 1 order of magnitude larger values compared to those at graphene/Co, where the DMI shows opposite decreasing behavior. Meanwhile, the PMA for both systems shows similar trends with larger values for graphene/Co and no significant variations for all thickness ranges of Co. Furthermore, using micromagnetic simulations we demonstrate that such significant DMI and PMA values remaining stable over a large range of Co thickness give rise to the formation of skyrmions with small applied external fields. These findings open up further possibilities toward integrating two-dimensional (2D) materials in spin−orbitronics devices.

KEYWORDS: spintronics, Dzyaloshinskii−Moriya interaction, magnetocrystalline anisotropy, 2D material/transition metal interfaces, skyrmions

For years, interfaces comprising magnetic transition metal (e.g., Co or Fe), oxides (e.g., MgO) or nonmagnetic metals (e.g., Pt) have been of central interest because of a large variety of spintronics phenomena enabling new generations of applications. $^{1,2}$ Importantly, the interplay between magnetism and spin-obit coupling (SOC), which is greatly enhanced at the interfaces because of the Rashba effect and/or the involvement of heavy metals, can lead to many emergent phenomena. $^{3}$ One of the well-known phenomena is the magnetocrystalline anisotropy (MCA) that favors the preferential alignment of magnetic moments along certain crystallographic directions. Recent rapid development of magnetic random access memory devices (MRAM) has relied on the discovery of interfaces with strong perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA). $^{2}$ The Dzyaloshinski−Moriya interaction (DMI), $^{4-6}$ which is an antisymmetric exchange interaction of the form $E_{\text{DMI}} = \sum_{\langle i,j\rangle} \mathbf{d}_{ij} \cdot (\mathbf{S}_{i} \times \mathbf{S}_{j})$ with $\mathbf{d}_{ij}$ being the microscopic DMI vector between the spin pairs $\mathbf{S}_{i}$ and $\mathbf{S}_{j}$, is another intriguing property that exists at the magnetic interface due to the presence of SOC and structural inversion asymmetry. Such interfacial DMI has gained much attention in recent years since the competition between DMI, PMA, and Heisenberg exchange is responsible for the formation of chiral magnetic structures, such as spin spirals and skyrmions, which are promising for the next generation of data storage devices. $^{7}$

Two dimensional (2D) materials such as graphene (Gr) and hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) are appealing as novel materials with exceptional properties $^{8-18}$ that can replace conventional ones and open new prospects for information technology. Graphene has been widely studied for use in both lateral and vertical spintronic devices. Various approaches have been proposed to inject, detect, and induce spin polarized currents in graphene and other 2D materials. $^{10,19-25}$ Because of their protective nature against oxidation, they have been incorporated in magnetic tunnel junctions catalyzing at the same time spin-dependent transport and magnetic properties of these structures. $^{26}$ Furthermore, despite its weak SOC, graphene coating of Co is found to induce a large PMA $^{27}$ and a significant DMI due to the Rashba effect. $^{28}$ Those findings have promoted graphene, and subsequently 2D materials, as potential candidates for novel low-power spin−orbitronics-based applications.

In this letter, using first-principles calculations, we demonstrate a significant PMA and a large DMI at Co/2D materials (Gr or h-BN) interfaces. We find that PMA at the Co/h-BN interface is preserved as in the case of graphene coverage, thanks to the hybridization of $d_{z^2}$ orbitals of Co with $p_z$ ones of 2D materials. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the DMI coefficient at the Co/h-BN interface increases as a function of Co thickness up to three monolayers (ML), reaching a maximum value of 1.2 meV, beyond which it saturates for higher thicknesses. This tendency is found to be opposite to that at Co/Gr interface where DMI decreases with the Co thickness. The Rashba constant in the case of Co/h-BN is found to be at least twice larger than that of Gr/Co, giving rise to the larger DMI values. The difference between the two interfaces is explained by the presence of two competing dipoles at the Gr/Co interface compared to only one dipole at the Co/h-BN interface, which gives rise to a larger DMI. Moreover, our micromagnetic simulations show the possibility of skyrmion formation at the h-BN(AC)/Co interface with the application of a small external magnetic field and remains stable up to 100 Kelvin.

The magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE) is calculated by taking the difference in total energy for in-plane $(E_{\parallel})$ and out-of-plane $(E_{\perp})$ magnetization orientations:
$$
\text{MAE} = \frac{E_{\parallel} - E_{\perp}}{A}
$$
where $A$ represents the area of hexagonal structure and it is equal to $A = a^2\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The MAE behaviors for Gr/Co, h- BN(AC)/Co, and h-BN(AB)/Co interfaces as a function of Co thickness are presented in Figure 1a by red triangles and black and white circles, respectively. To highlight the effect of 2D materials on MAE, we show for comparison the magnetic anisotropy of a bare Co slab shown by green squares in Figure 1a. For a single layer of Co, the magnetization strongly favors the in-plane direction. One can see that the situation changes when Co is coated by a single layer of 2D materials that induce the PMA by adding $\sim$3.2 or $\sim$3.7 mJ/m² to MAE in the case of h-BN and Gr coverage, respectively. For thicker hcp Co(0001) films, perpendicular magnetic anisotropy is maintained in all cases considered, including the one with graphene coating, which further enhances the PMA in agreement with previous reports.²⁷

To further compare and understand mechanisms of the catalyzing effect of h-BN and graphene on PMA in Co, we present the layer-resolved MAE contributions for different structures with 7 ML of Co as shown in Figure 1b. Overall, MAE shows a clear attenuated oscillatory behavior as a function of the distance from the interface. Such oscillatory behavior has also been reported in different systems such as transition metal/oxide interfaces.²⁹ In general, the main contribution to the PMA is located at the first interfacial Co layers with some significant contribution arising from the third monolayer as well. Because magnetic anisotropy is a surface phenomenon, the effect of different coatings impact mostly the first three atomic layers as shown in Figure 1b. Graphene coating further enhances the PMA contribution of the first and third monolayers and reduces the in-plane magnetic anisotropy (IMA) of the second layer.²⁷ In the case of h-BN coating, the PMA contribution from the first atomic layer depends on the type of stacking: while h-BN(AC) coating shows a PMA decrease, h-BN(AB)/Co leads to the PMA enhancement. However, in both cases, the MAE contribution of the second and third ML favoring IMA and PMA, respectively, are further enhanced compared to those of bare Co slab. To elucidate the microscopic origin of PMA, Figure 1c shows the orbital resolved contributions of the first, second, and third Co atomic layers in 2D/Co structures with 7 ML of Co to simplify presentation. One can see, that in the case of bare Co, the PMA contributions (squares) from the first layer originates from hybridizations of in-plane $d_{xy}$ and $d_{x^2 - y^2}$ with other Co d orbitals. On the other hand, contributions from hybridization of out-of-plane $d_{z^2}$ with other d orbitals favors in-plane anisotropy. Adding graphene or h-BN on top of Co decreases the in-plane orbital-resolved contributions of the first layer (circles, triangles). However, those arising from hybridizations of out-of-plane $d_{z^2}$ start favoring PMA. This can be attributed to the hybridization between $d_{z^2}$ orbitals of Co and $p_z$ of 2D material,²⁷ somewhat similar to the PMA mechanisms arising from hybridization between $d_{z^2}$ and $p_z$ orbitals at transition metal/oxide interfaces.²

Next, we present the results of DMI calculations for these 2D materials/Co interfaces. We employed the approach based on chirality-dependent total energy calculations applied previously for Pt/Co, MgO/Co and Gr/Co structures to calculate the total microscopic and micromagnetic DMI constants, $d^{tot}$ and $D$, respectively. $^{28,30,31}$ In Figure 2a, b we show the dependence of DMI coefficients as a function of Co thickness up to 7 ML for Gr/Co, h-BN(AB)/Co and h- BN(AC)/Co interfaces shown in Figure 2c−e, respectively. The arrows in Figure 2f schematically indicate spin configuration with clockwise (anticlockwise) chirality. The microscopic constant $d^{tot}$ at Gr/Co interface exhibits a large DMI value of 1.2 meV for 1 ML of Co, in agreement with previous reports. $^{28}$ As Co thickness increases, the DMI strength decreases and even changes a sign at 4 ML thickness but restores left-handed chirality above 5 ML of Co thickness saturating at $\sim$0.15 meV. Interestingly, h-BN/Co interface shows an opposite DMI trend with Co thickness. For 1 ML of Co, the $d^{tot}$ of h-BN/Co is about 0.2 and 0.5 meV for AC and AB stacking, respectively. With the increase in Co thickness, the microscopic DMI increases up to 3 ML and then saturates around 1.1 meV for both stackings. Thus, the DMI strength at h-BN/Co interface with large Co thickness is 1 order of magnitude larger than that at graphene/Co and found to be robust against the stacking order. Despite the fact that largest value of the micromagnetic DMI, $D = 6.5$ mJ/m², is found for 1 ML of Co coated with graphene, it weakens with increasing the Co thickness becoming negligible for Co films above 5 ML (Figure 2b). For thin Co films, the tendency of micromagnetic DMI constant behavior at small Co thicknesses depends on the stacking order of h-BN/Co interface. For AB stacking, we observe a monotonic behavior where $D$ decreases as a function of Co thickness (Figure 2b). However, DMI constant exhibits a nonmonotonic behavior in the case of AC stacking: it increases for Co thicknesses up to 3 ML and decreases afterward. Interestingly, the micromagnetic DMI for thick Co layers is independent of the stacking order with significantly larger values compared to Gr/Co interface for both AB and AC stackings. These results suggest that h-BN coating provides a strong and robust DMI for thick Co films that when combined with the aforementioned PMA findings represent a major interest for 2D spin−orbitronics including skyrmionics. $^{32,33}$

Fert–Levy model.³⁰ In the absence of heavy atoms, as in the cases we address here, the DMI mechanism is dominated by a Rashba-type model.²⁸ The micromagnetic DMI can be roughly expressed as $D = 2k_{\text{R}}A_{\text{ex}}$,³⁴ where $A_{\text{ex}}$ indicates the interfacial exchange stiffness and $k_{\text{R}} = \frac{2\alpha_{\text{R}}m_{\text{e}}}{\hbar^{2}}$ is determined by the Rashba coefficient $\alpha_{\text{R}}$, effective electron mass $m_{\text{e}}$ with $\hbar$ being the reduced Planck constant. The effective mass of Co is taken to be $0.45\ m_{0}$ (with $m_{0}$ being the electron rest mass), and the interfacial exchange stiffness $A_{\text{ex}}$ is about 5 pJ/m for Co thin films. For the Gr/Co interface, the Rashba constant is found to be about $82\ \text{meV·Å}$ as reported in ref 28. Following the same method that for magnetic interfaces employs mapping the energy differences between two opposite magnetization orientations within the plane of interface,³⁵,³⁶ we estimated the Rashba constants at the h-BN(AB)/Co and h-BN(AC)/Co interfaces to be about 225 and $182\ \text{meV·Å}$, respectively (see Figure 2g, h). The micromagnetic DMI estimated from these Rashba constants at Gr/Co, h-BN(AB)/Co, and h-BN(AC)/Co are found to be 0.98, 2.66, and $2.15\ \text{mJ/m}^2$ for structures with 3 ML thick Co, respectively. The DMI values obtained from first-principles calculations and estimated from the Rashba constants are listed in Table 1. Using micromagnetic DMI values, we calculated the microscopic ones to be about 0.52, 1.44, and 1.16 meV for graphene, h-BN(AB), and h-BN(AC) coatings, respectively. Those values are in good agreement with the DMI ones obtained from first-principles calculations. One should note that the estimated Rashba constant at the h-BN/Co interface is comparable to that at MgO/Co reported in ref 31. However, the DMI value calculated using first-principles approaches is almost twice as large for the MgO/Co interface. This difference could be attributed to the different $A_{\text{ex}}$ values in different systems. In Table 1, we also list experimental values of the microscopic DMI at Gr/Co/Ru²⁸ and Gr/Co/Pt³⁷ systems for comparison. The extracted values of Gr/Co from aforementioned works show opposite signs with 0.16 and −0.6 meV for structures on Ru and Pt substrate, respectively. This difference between both experiments is most probably due to different Co thicknesses used. For Pt substrate, the Co thickness is about 4 ML, which is in line with our first-principles calculations showing that for this particular thickness the DMI value is negative. However, the DMI becomes positive again for larger Co thickness and is about 0.15 meV in perfect agreement with DMI obtained with a Ru substrate.

To understand the difference between microscopic mechanisms of Gr/Co and h-BN/Co interfaces, in Figure 3, we show the induced charge densities across the Gr/Co/h-BN heterostructure. One can note two competing opposite electric dipoles present at the Gr/Co interface, whereas at the h-BN/Co one, there exists only one dipole pointing away from the Co film. This larger interfacial dipole is at the origin of the larger Rashba constant and consequently stronger DMI at the h-BN/Co interface compared to the Gr/Co one showing that the electric dipoles can serve as a good descriptor in 2D/Co interfaces similar to the case of metallic interfaces.³⁸ Note that the unavoidable disorders appeared in the experiments may strongly affect or destroy the dipole at the interfaces leading to the decrease or suppression of DMI as was shown, for instance, in the case of Co/Pt multilayers.³⁰ The experimental realization of the high quality h-BN/Co interfaces is preferable using the Co intercalation growth process as for the graphene/Co interface²⁷,²⁸ or through the direct growth of the h-BN sheet on the chosen metal support via the chemical or physical vapor deposition.³⁹,⁴⁰ It should be mentioned also that in experiments the PMA and DMI of the thin films can be affected by the chosen substrate,³¹,⁴¹,⁴² which should be carefully considered by using, for instance, the one with a small SOC such as Cu to avoid or reduce its affect.

The demagnetization energy term is taken into account in the micromagnetic simulations. We relax the micromagnetic state of a 100 nm wide nanodisk from an initial uniform out-of-plane ferromagnetic state (FM). The obtained magnetization distributions at different external magnetic fields and temperatures are presented in Figure 4. One can see that without the external field, the labyrinth domains with chiral Néel domain walls are established in the h-BN/Co nanodisk. More importantly, the labyrinth domains can be transformed into skyrmion states by applying external field as in many typical ferromagnet/heavy metal (FM/HM) heterostructures⁴⁵⁻⁴⁷ with skyrmions. When the external field is in the range of about 200−250 mT, isolated skyrmions are induced in the nanodisk (Figure 4a) that can sustain temperatures up to above 100 K (Figure 4b). At temperatures higher than 100 K, the skyrmions collapse because of the effect of thermal fluctuations. Previous studies⁴⁸,⁴⁹ suggested that the enhanced DMI can improve thermal stability of skyrmions that motivates us to investigate the potential of other 2D materials/Co interfaces with larger DMI in the future. We note that the thick Co layers have a larger exchange stiffness $A_{ex}$ (15 pJ/m)⁵⁰,⁵¹ than the value we used in the aforementioned simulations (5 pJ/m). To check the impact of larger exchange stiffness on skyrmions, we have performed micromagnetic simulations for exchange stiffness values up to 15 pJ/m and found that the skyrmions can still be stabilized in h-BN/Co thin films. These results suggest the h-BN/Co thin films can serve as a promising alternative system to induce skyrmions avoiding heavy metals.

In conclusion, we demonstrated the significant and robust behavior of PMA and DMI at interfaces comprising Co and 2D materials. By comparing h-BN/Co and Gr/Co interfaces, although the PMA behavior shows similarities for both cases, the DMI at h-BN/Co interface shows significantly larger values remaining robust over a large Co thickness range unlike for Co/Gr interface. This is due to Rashba constant in case of h-BN/Co found to be at least twice larger than that of Co/Gr.

The physical mechanisms of such behavior are attributed to existence of competing dipoles at the Co/Gr interface compared to only one dipole at the opposite h-BN/Co interface which gives rise to a larger DMI. Moreover, based on found DMI and PMA behavior, the possibility of skyrmions formation at h-BN(AC)/Co interface with the application of small external magnetic field and stable up to hundred Kelvin is demonstrated. Compared to the widely studied skyrmion materials based on ferromagnet/heavy metal interfaces, e.g., Fe/Ir⁵² and Co/Pt,⁴⁵,⁴⁷ the h-BN/Co and Gr/Co systems are simpler to synthesize and free of heavy metals being advantageous to lower Gilbert damping beneficial for the realization of low critical driving current density and high-speed skyrmion motion. Furthermore, the realization of skyrmions in h-BN/Co represents interesting perspective toward exploring interfaces comprising other 2D materials such as MoS₂ monolayer and phosphorene with the magnetic thin films that can promote the stabilization of skyrmions beyond room temperature, which can greatly broaden the research area of skyrmionics. These findings can not only demonstrate that 2D materials such as h-BN provide a viable alternative for heavy metals in the next-generation spintronic devices based on domain wall or skyrmions, but also inspire further investigations on the exotic magnetic properties on the 2D material/magnetic metal interfaces.

■ METHODS

First-principles calculations have been performed using the VASP (Vienna Ab initio simulation package)⁵³⁻⁵⁵ with electron−core interactions described by the projector augmented wave method,⁵⁶ and the exchange correlation energy calculated within the generalized gradient approximation of Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof (PBE).⁵⁷ The cutoff energies for the plane wave basis set used to expand the Khon−Sham orbitals were chosen to be 520 eV for all calculations. The lattice constant of bulk Co(0001) hexagonal closed packed (hcp) structures is about 2.507 Å that cannot fit perfectly on graphene and h-BN monolayers that have lattice constant of 2.46 and 2.504 Å, respectively. In all calculations, the in-plane constant was fixed to be 2.507 Å. The supercell comprised a varied number of Co layers (1−7 ML) coated by 1 ML of graphene or h-BN and followed by a vacuum region that is large enough to prevent interaction with the mirror image. For the surface magnetic anisotropy and DMI calculations a Monkhorst−Pack scheme was used for the $\Gamma$-centered $21 × 21 × 1$ and $4 × 16 × 1$ $K$-point mesh,

respectively. van der Waals interactions were not taken into account because chemisorption was considered. Calculations were performed in three steps. First, the structural relaxations were performed until the forces become smaller than 0.001 eV/Å for determining the most stable interfacial geometries. Next, the Khon−Sham equations were solved, with no SOC, to find out the charge distribution of the system’s ground state. Finally, SOC was included and the self-consistent total energy of the system was determined as a function of the orientation of the magnetic moments. Layer- and orbital-resolved contributions can be extracted from spin−orbit matrix elements implemented in the VASP as shown, for instance, in refs 27 and 29. For DMI calculations, the orientation of the magnetic moments for each atom were controlled using the constrained method as implemented in VASP. In the micromagnetic simulations, the values of MAE and DMI coefficients are derived from the first-principles calculation results unless stated otherwise.

The structure of h-BN/Co films investigated is shown in Figure 2, where a layer of graphene or h-BN covers the surface of hexagonal closed-packed (hcp) Co(0001) film. The interlayer distance between Co and the 2D materials was found to be 2.11 and 2.15 Å for graphene and h-BN, respectively. For Gr/Co interface, the most stable geometry configuration is found to be AC stacking. For h-BN/Co, the calculated ground state structure is found to be AB stacking, whereas the AC stacking is quasi stable with a small difference between the two configurations on the order of a few meV/Å. Both configurations denoted as h-BN(AC)/Co and h-BN-(AB)/Co interfaces are considered in this study.
