## ABSTRACT

The development of efficient catalysts for low temperature CO oxidation is important to the application of fuel cells. In this work, we report that the Ag monolayer on WC (0001) surface (Ag$_{\text{ML}}$/WC) could effectively catalyze CO oxidation through the L-H mechanism ($\text{CO} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{OOCO} \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + \text{O}$). The most sluggish reaction step is suggested to be $\text{CO} + \text{O} \rightarrow \text{CO}_2$ with a barrier of 0.48 eV, which is 1.21 eV lower than the barrier of $\text{O}_2$ dissociation. The electronic structure and d-band center analyses demonstrate that the promoted activity may originate from the synergistic effect between Ag monolayer and WC. The present study is conducive to design new efficient and cost-effective catalysts for CO oxidation without using of the noble platinum.

## 1. Introduction

Carbon monoxide (CO) is one of the poisonous and harmful gases existing in the air and also a toxic gas in many industrial catalytic processes. Thus, the removal of CO through the low temperature catalytic oxidation has attracted enormous attention. The key to the problem is to develop highly efficient catalyst for boosting the reaction rate of CO oxidation at low temperature. CO oxidation on noble-metal surface is a very important catalytic reaction in many industrial processes for the reasons that CO oxidation on noble metal surface not only has many industrial purposes, such as solving the growing environmental problems caused by CO emission, $\text{CO}_2$ laser, sensors, and so on, but also has been widely studied as a basic model reaction for the study of heterogeneous catalytic mechanisms.

In recent years, most researchers chose the noble-metals as catalysts for theoretical and experimental researches, such as Pt [1–3], Rh [4], Au [5–8] and Pd [9], which exhibited high catalytic activity for CO oxidation. Wei et al. [10] systematically studied the detailed reaction process of CO oxidation on IB group metals [M (111), M=Cu, Ag, Au)] by performing density functional theory (DFT) calculations and found that the order of catalytic activity of CO oxidation on metal surfaces was Cu (111) < Au (111) < Ag (111), which meant that Ag surface was the most favorable one for CO oxidation among the three catalysts. However, the key problem existed with the Ag (111) surface was the poor adsorption ability to CO (0.29 eV) and $\text{O}_2$/O (0.16 eV/0.42 eV), which made it difficult in capturing molecules and limited the activity of Ag catalyst. This was in generally ascribed to the wide gap between the d band and the Fermi level. Based on this, some effective methods were recently proposed to enhance the adsorption capacity and improve the catalytic activity, e.g. depositing transition metals on different substrates in the form of monoatomic layers. The substrates would contribute significantly to the reactivity and selectivity of the supported metals by adjusting the geometry and electronic structure of the supports. Chen's group [11,12] found that the catalytic activity and decomposition pathway of methanol on tungsten carbide (WC) surface were affected by different metal monolayers loaded on the surface. More importantly, the nanohybrid could also greatly reduce the amount of precious metals used.

Transition metal carbides (TMCs) have attracted great interest for the reason that TMCs had higher chemical stability, durability, catalytic activity and selectivity compared with the parent metals due to their mixed metal-covalent properties. In addition, they can serve as catalysts and support materials to reduce the load of noble metals in catalytic applications [13–17]. Consequently, TMCs have been widely utilized in the field of electrochemical catalysis [13,18–21]. Since the discovery of Levy and Boudart in 1973 that WC can be used as catalyst with similar properties to Pt [22], WC has attracted wide attention, which can exist stably at a wide range of pH and external potential conditions, and strongly bound with

[35] comparatively investigated the hydrogen evolution reaction perfor- mance of WC-supported metal monolayers (Cu, Ru, Rh, Ag, Au, Pt, Pd, and Ir) to establish general trends regarding monolayer sta- bility, reactivity, electronic structure and so on. They pointed out that the precious platinum catalyst on hydrogen electrode reac- tion was unnecessary, and a better catalyst could be obtained by reasonable design methods, appropriate selection of cover/support systems that did not involve Pt.

## 2. Computational methods

The spin-unrestricted density functional theory calculations are applied to investigate the CO oxidation on WC (0001)-supported silver monolayer by using the $Dmol^{3}$ code embedded in the Materials Studio. The electronic self-consistent field tolerance is set to $10^{-6} Ha$, and all the calculations are performed with a real-space global orbital cutoff radius of $4.9 \AA$, and a smear- ing value of $0.005 Ha$ is selected for orbital occupation so as to speed up convergence. The exchange-correlation potential is described by the PW91 [36] functional within the generalized- gradient-approximation (GGA). The double-numerical plus polar- ization (DNP) basis set is used as the atomic orbitals and DFT semicore pseudopotentials (DSPPs) are used to describe the in- teractions between the ion core and the valence electrons. The convergence criteria are set to be $10^{-5} Ha$ for the energy, 0.002 $Ha/\AA$ for the forces, and $0.005 \AA$ for the displacements during the structural optimization. The reciprocal space is sampled with $(5 \times$  $5 \times 5)$ and $(5 \times 5 \times 1)$ k-points grids generated automatically using the Monkhorst-Pack method [37] for bulk and surface cal- culations, respectively. To calculate the electronic properties, the k-point meshes are increased up to $20 \times 20 \times 1$. The linear syn chronous transition/quadratic synchronous transit (LST/QST) tools are used to identify the minimum energy path (MEP) and the cor- responding rate-determining step (RDS) [38], which have been well verified in efficiently locating the transition states (TS) and the MEP.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Adsorption of reaction species

It is a prerequisite to examine the adsorption properties of the possible reaction species, CO, O₂, and CO₂, to deduce the thermodynamics of the reaction. The most stable adsorption configuration for each species on different surface sites is obtained by comparing the adsorption energy.

The geometric structure and the charge density difference plots of different molecules on the Agₘₗ/WC are shown in Fig. 2. The geometric, adsorption energies and bonding details of the optimized structures are summarized in Table 1. From Fig. 2 and Table 1, it is found that the CO molecule tends to be adsorbed at the Ag top site with the Ag-C bond length of 1.5 Å, which is analogous to the case of CO on Ag (111) obtained by Hansen et al. [42] and McElhiney et al. [43] The optimized C-O bond distance on the surface of Ag/WC is 1.15 Å, which is found to be slightly stretched as compared with the experimental gas-phase value of 1.132 Å upon adsorption on Agₘₗ/WC (0001). The adsorption energy of CO on the Agₘₗ/WC (0001) is 0.78 eV, which is larger than the experimental value (0.28 eV) on Ag (111) surface at the top site [43]. The stronger adsorption is also evident by the charge transfer. The adsorbed CO is positively charged by 0.03 |e| based on the Mullikan charge analysis as shown in Table 1. The charge density difference (CDD) map is also depicted to visualize the charge redistribution. As shown in Fig. 2a, the charge tends to be enriched on the C-O bond, while the charge depletion area is located dominantly on the Ag-C bond. Beyond that, the charge transfer can also be illustrated by the partial density of states (PDOS) plot, Fig. 3a, which shows the electron donation from the CO-5σ orbital to the Ag-d and back-donation from the Ag-d to the CO-2π* orbital.

The most energetically favored O₂ adsorption configuration on the Agₘₗ/WC (0001) surface is shown in Fig. 2b, in which the O₂ molecule is adsorbed at the hcp site with the O-O bond nearly parallel to the Agₘₗ/WC (0001) surface. From Table 1, the O-O bond of the adsorbed O₂ molecule on the Agₘₗ/WC (0001) is 1.39 Å, which is longer than that of the isolate-gas-state O₂ (1.23 Å). The elongated O-O bond indicates that O₂ has been activated by the substrate. The shortest Ag-O distance is 1.79 Å and the adsorption energy is 0.59 eV, which is weaker than that of CO on the substrate. According to Mullikan charge analysis indicated in Table 1, there are about 0.59 electrons transferred to the adsorbed O₂ from the substrate. Compared with the previous CO adsorption phenomenon, we can note that there are two completely different charge transfer phenomena when CO and O₂ interact with the substrate, where CO contributes 0.03 electrons to the substrate, while O₂ gets 0.59 electrons from the substrate. This may be because the electronegativity of O₂ is stronger than that of CO. Besides, these adsorption behaviors are similar to that of O₂ on Pdₘₗ/WC (0001) [33], where the O₂ tends to be adsorbed at the hcp site with a W atom beneath it. But the adsorption of O₂ molecule on the Agₘₗ/WC is weaker than that on the Pdₘₗ/WC. From Sabatier's principle [44], the interaction strength between reactants and substrates of good catalysts should be in a suitable range, not too strong nor too weak. The Agₘₗ/WC (0001) binds the reactants weaker compared with the Pt (111) [45] (1.47 eV) and Pd (111) [46] (2.39 eV) catalysts. In contrast, it has stronger adsorption to reactants compared with those on the inert metal catalysts of Au (111) [47] and Ag (111) [47] with the adsorption energies of CO 0.30 eV, 0.29 eV, respectively, and O₂ 0.04 eV, 0.16 eV, respectively. The moderate binding strength of the reactants would be favorable for CO oxidation. Fig. 2b also represents the momentous charge accumulation for O₂ adsorption on the Agₘₗ/WC (0001) substrate. The charge-deficient region suggests that electrons migrated from the Ag atoms to the O-O double bond. In Fig. 3b, the shape of the DOS of the adsorbed O₂ on the Agₘₗ/WC (0001) surface is altered dramatically as compared with that of the isolate-gas-state O₂. For the gaseous oxygen molecules, the DOS plots of the spin-up and spin-down channels exhibit asymmetric appearance, indicating that they are magnetic. Sharp peaks are located around the Fermi

### 3.2. CO oxidation over $Ag_{ML}$/WC (0001)

In general, there are two main reaction mechanisms for CO ox-
idation reactions, namely, the Langmuir-Hinshelwood (L-H) mech-
anism and the Eley-Rideal (E-R) mechanism. The L-H mechanism
states that the CO and $O_2$ are first co-adsorbed on the catalyst.
Then, the terminal O atom of $O_2$ approaches the co-adsorbed CO to
form a peroxide-type (OOCO) intermediate state. The dissociation
of OOCO with the formation and desorption of a $CO_2$ molecule,
leaving an O atom on the substrate is the final step. In the E-R
mechanism, however, the pre-adsorption and activation of an $O_2$
molecule is the initial step. The CO molecule then approaches and
reacts directly with the adsorbed $O_2$ to produce $CO_2$. Based on the
above two mechanisms, we conduct an in-depth study on these
different aspects, respectively.

It can be seen from the interaction between CO or $O_2$ and sub-
strates in the previous part that the interaction between CO and
substrates is stronger than that of $O_2$, which may inhibit the cat-
alytic reaction by E-R mechanism. In spite of this, the E-R reaction
as a step of CO oxidation is also considered, in order to compare
with the L-H mechanism. The structures of IS0 and FS0 and the
corresponding transition process from IS0 to FS0 are shown in the
Fig. 4. In IS0, $O_2$ is pre-adsorbed at fcc site of the substrate and CO
is physically adsorbed, suspending above $O_2$, which is regarded as
the initial state. The final state (FS0) is set to be a $CO_3$ molecule
adsorbed on the fcc site of the $Ag_{ML}$/WC substrate. The calculated
transition state (TS0) is the dissociation of the $O_2$ molecule as-
sisted by the CO molecule. In the transition state, the physically
adsorbed CO approaches the adsorbed $O_2$ molecule, with the O-O
distance increased from 1.39 Å to 2.03 Å and the C-$O_2$ distance
decreased from 3.25 Å to 1.95 Å. Passing over the transition state,
CO is completely embedded between the O-O bonds to form a $CO_3$
structure (FS0) adsorbed on the $Ag_{ML}$/WC substrate. In this pro-
cess, there is an energy release of 3.88 eV. However, the activation
energy is 1.69 eV, which needs to be overcome in splitting the
O-O bonds and forming new C-O bonds. In spite of this, the en-
ergy barrier ($E_b$) is much smaller than that for CO oxidation on
the $Pt_{ML}$/WC (ER mechanism, $E_b=2.18$ eV).

Compared with the E-R mechanism of CO oxidation on this substrate, the barrier of the rate-determining step of L-H mechanism decreases dramatically, which is 1.21 eV lower than the activation barrier of the E-R mechanism, indicating that the L-H mechanism would be the preferred step for CO oxidation on the substrate.

### 3.3. Comparison of CO oxidation on surface of different metals and supported metals
It is very meaningful to compare CO oxidation on AgML/WC with other similar metals or substrates containing supported metals. The structures of the PtML/WC and AgML/WC are similar, but we can see from the Table 3 that there are differences in the rate-determining steps (RDS) of CO oxidation. The RDS on PtML/WC (0001) is the process of OOCO formation with a barrier energy of 1.06 eV, which is larger by about 0.58 eV than that on the surface of AgML/WC (0001). Thus, AgML/WC (0001) has higher catalytic activity for CO oxidation. Although there are the same RDS steps between AgML/WC (0001) and Pt (111), but the energy barriers that need to be overcome are quite different. The RDS barrier of AgML/WC (0001) (0.48 eV) is about 0.57 eV smaller than that on the Pt (111) (1.05 eV). Compared with that on the Ag (111), the adsorption energies of reactant species have been improved on the surface of AgML/WC (0001), which is able to adsorb small molecules stably and improve the activity of catalytic reaction owing to the synergistic effect between silver and tungsten carbide.

To the best of our knowledge, d-band center is the average density of states of d-band, which can be used to predict the catalytic activity of transition metals [48-50], and the closer the center of d-band is to Fermi level, the stronger is the ability of the catalyst to adsorb small molecules. Based on this ideology, the model of d-band center is used for the study of the catalytic activity of AgML/WC. The adsorption energies of CO and $O_{2}$ on Ag (111) are very small, only 0.29 eV and 0.16 eV, respectively. This is due to the fact that the d-band center of Ag (111) (-4.1 eV) is very far away from the Fermi level. The low adsorption strength to the reactant molecules indicates that the Ag (111) surface is too difficult to "catch" CO molecule, and thus, even no CO oxidation can occur. On the contrary, if the d-band center of metal is too close to Fermi level, the metal surface will be poisoned by CO due to strong adsorption, which is not an ideal high-efficient catalyst. Therefore, according to the Sabatier principle, the best catalysts should bind reactants with intermediate strength. ## 4. Conclusions
The adsorption and oxidation of CO on the Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001)
surface are investigated using the first-principles calculations. It is
found that the adsorption of CO on Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001) with an ad-
sorption energy of 0.78 eV is weaker than those on the surfaces of
Pt (111) and Pt<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0 0 0 1), which have the adsorption en-
ergies of about 1.58 eV and 1.59 eV, respectively. However, the
adsorption energy of CO on Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001) is larger than that
on Ag (111), which improves the ability of Ag to adsorb small
molecules. While the adsorption energy on Ag (111) is too low
to "catch" CO molecule, the modest adsorption strength of CO on
Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001) would enhance the oxidation of CO and is ex-
pected to be responsible for the higher CO tolerance. The d-band
centers of Ag (111) and Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001) are compared. It is con-
firmed that the increase of adsorption energy on Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001)
is due to the close proximity of d-band centers of the complex to
Fermi level, which improves the catalytic activity, and reflects the
synergistic effect between WC and Ag. Two reaction mechanisms
of CO oxidation on Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001) are presented, and the L-H
mechanism is found to be preferable. The reaction step of the CO
oxidation by O atom is identified as the RDS along MEP, but the
activation barrier of 0.48 eV is lower than the values of 1.05 eV for
the RDS of CO oxidation by atomic O on the Pt (111) surface and
1.06 eV for the RDS of OOCO formation on the Pt<sub>ML</sub>/WC surface.
The formation of OOCO on Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001) just has a barrier of
0.27 eV. From the Table 3, we can conclude that the energy barrier
for the RDS of CO oxidation on Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001) is smaller than
those of the others, and almost equivalent to Au<sub>ML</sub>/WC (0001),
which means that the Ag<sub>ML</sub>/WC can be used for a new efficient
and cost-effective catalysts for CO oxidation without using of the
noble platinum.
