# Density Functional Theory Study on the Effect of Sodium on the Adsorption of NO on a Char Surface

## ABSTRACT:
In order to clarify the effect of sodium on the heterogeneous adsorption of NO on a char surface, several reasonably simplified saturated and unsaturated char models were selected as research objects, and Na atoms were decorated on the char models. Each char model and NO adsorption product were optimized by using density functional theory at the GGA-PBE (generalized gradient approximation-Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof) level to obtain the adsorption energy of NO on different models. The results indicate that the addition of Na atoms catalyzes the adsorption of NO molecules by an saturated char structure, the adsorption form is changed from physisorption to chemisorption, and the adsorption energy is increased from −15.6 to −98.2 kJ/ mol. The adsorption energy of NO molecules with an unsaturated armchair-type char structure containing edge active sites is affected by sodium atoms, increasing from −211.7 to −303.6 kJ/mol, while the adsorption capacity of the zigzag-type char structure for NO molecules is hardly affected by Na atoms. This study provides a theoretical basis for the development of clean utilization technology of high-alkali coal.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
As one of the main atmospheric pollutants in flue gas of coal-fired boilers, the nitrogen oxide ($NO_x$) is seriously endangering the human health and ecological environment. $^{1}$ Furthermore, coal resources are rich in China, which are still in the dominant position of China's energy production and consumption. $^{2}$ There are abundant coal resources in the Zhundong area of Xinjiang, but the content of sodium oxide in Zhundong coal is more than 2%, which belongs to typical high-sodium coal. $^{3}$ Sodium in coal not only promoted the pyrolysis and gasification process of coal$^{4,5}$ but also improved the activity of in situ coal char, thus promoting the heterogeneous reduction of NO by char in the reburning zone of a boiler, $^{6}$ and decreased the nitrogen oxide emissions. The effect of sodium additives on the release of fuel nitrogen by using TG/ EGA (thermogravimetry/evolved gas analysis) equipment has been studied by Liu et al., $^{7}$ and it was found that the presence of sodium catalyzed the reduction of NO by char.

Recently, quantum chemistry theory, as an important analytical method of chemical reaction mechanisms, has been widely used in the microscopic mechanism study of heterogeneous reduction of NO by char.$^{8-13}$ Zhang et al. $^{8}$ investigated the thermodynamics and kinetics of the heterogeneous interaction between NO and char(N) (char-bound nitrogen) during coal combustion by using pyridine nitrogen as representative of a nitrogen-containing char model, and the team then studied the promotion of carbon monoxide and oxygen on the heterogeneous reduction.$^{9-11}$ The adsorption behavior of NO on a char surface by using a carbon nanotube structure decorated with metal atoms has been studied by researchers, $^{12}$ and the catalytic effect of calcium and potassium on heterogeneous adsorption of NO on char was revealed from the molecular level in this literature. However, it was not studied well enough to understand the mechanism of alkali sodium on the heterogeneous reduction of nitrogen oxides. Most scholars study the mechanism of heterogeneous reduction of NO by coal char containing active sites but neglect the reduction reaction on the structural model of saturated char.

Therefore, it is of great significance for clean utilization of high-sodium coal to select more abundant char structure models and explore the catalytic effect of Na on the heterogeneous reduction of NO by char from the microlevel.

In this paper, a single-layer saturated graphene structure and char edge model containing active sites were selected as the research objects. The adsorption reaction of the NO molecule on the char surface was simulated by density functional theory. In addition, the catalytic mechanism of sodium atoms in the heterogeneous adsorption of NO on the char surface was further explored.

The speciation of sodium element in high-alkali coal was studied in literature,$^{19,20}$ and it was reported that the organic forms of water-soluble sodium chloride and sodium carboxylate are the main forms of sodium in high-alkali coal; however, Na is mainly released in the form of sodium atoms during combustion. The metal-loaded activated graphene surface was studied by numerous researchers.$^{16,21,22}$ The adsorption mode research$^{23}$ shows that the side-on mode was the main adsorption mode for NO to adsorb on the coal char surface with active sites. Consequently, the speciation of sodium and adsorption modes on the coal char surface are particularly important. In this research, the sodium-loaded activated graphene surface was selected to analyze its effect on the adsorption capacity of NO by coal char, and the side-on model was used to simulate the adsorption of coal char with active sites; in addition, the structure of saturated coal char was calculated by side-on, O-down, and N-down modes, and the structure with the largest adsorption energy was selected as the main structure.

2.2. Computational Methods. The first-principles calculations were based on the DFT (density functional theory) with GGA (generalized gradient approximation) in the form of PBE (Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof) function for exchange−correlation potential.$^{24}$ The wave functions of valence electrons were described by the double numerical basis set with polarization functions (DNP), and a real space cutoff of 4.5 Å was used. The core electrons were treated with an all-electron basis set, and the spin-polarized geometry optimization was carried out in all calculations. The TS (Tkatchenko−Scheffler) method$^{25}$ was used for DFT-D dispersion correction. The convergence tolerances for geometric optimization were set to $1.0 × 10^{-5}$ Hartree on the energy, 0.0002 Hartree/nm on the force, 0.0005 nm on the displacement, and $1.0 × 10^{-6}$ Hartree on the SCF. For all of the calculations, the zero-point energy correction has been considered. All of the theoretical calculations were carried out using the Dmol$^3$ program module$^{26,27}$ in Materials Studio.

The adsorption energy $(E_{\text{ads}})$ can be formulated as

$$E_{\text{ads}} = E_{\text{AB}} - (E_{\text{A}} + E_{\text{B}})$$

where $E_{\text{AB}}$, $E_{\text{A}}$, and $E_{\text{B}}$ are defined as the total energy of the adsorbate/substrate system, the total energy of the isolated adsorbate at its equilibrium geometry, and the total energy of the substrate, respectively. A higher negative $E_{\text{ads}}$ value corresponds to a stronger interaction. The definition of chemical adsorption and physical absorption needs to consider the adsorption energy, PDOS (partial density of states), the change in bond length, and so on. Also, the most important judgment index is the adsorption energy. Based on adsorption theory, if the $E_{\text{ads}}$ is between −10 and −30 kJ/mol, the adsorption intensity is weak and belongs to physical adsorption. If the adsorption energy is between −50 and −960 kJ/mol, the adsorption is stronger and belongs to chemical adsorption.$^{28}$

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

3.1. Char Structure Doped with Sodium. A single dopant atom (Na atom) was initially placed on the different adsorption sites of the char surface, and the optimized configurations of Na doped are presented in Figures 3−5. The adsorption energies of hollow site results show that the stability of Na adsorption is in the order of Z@Na-hollow > A@Na-hollow > G@Na-hollow with adsorption energies within −307.9 kJ/mol (Z@Na), −261.5 kJ/mol (A@Na), −161.0 kJ/mol (G@Na) on the char surface, indicating that Na is chemically adsorbed on hollow sites of the surface. This conclusion is consistent with the calculation results of Zhu et al.$^{29}$ and Calderón.$^{5}$ The results show that the Na atom on the hollow site on the char surface is more stable than those on the top site and bridge site, which are listed in Figures S1−S3 in the Supporting Information. Therefore, this paper mainly focuses on Na atom doping on the hollow site for further research.

3.2. NO Adsorption on the Char Surface. A single NO was initially placed on the different adsorption sites (side-on, O-down, and N-down) of different coal char surfaces (G, A, and Z) and Na-loaded char surfaces (G@Na, A@Na, and Z-@Na), and the optimized configurations of NO adsorption are presented in Figures 3−5 with the adsorption configurations of NO on side-on, O-down, and N-down of the coal char surface. The adsorption energy results show that the stability of NO adsorption is in the order of G-NO hollow < A-NO hollow < Z-NO hollow. The hollow site of the side-on adsorption mode is found to be the most stable situation with adsorption energies of −15.6, −211.7, and −564.6 kJ/mol, which is consistent with the results obtained by Garcia-Fernandez et al.,$^{16}$ Xin et al.,$^{28}$ and Kyotani and Tomita.$^{23}$ Thus, it can easily be concluded that NO is physically adsorbed on the surface of G but is strongly chemisorbed on the surface of A and Z. The main reason is that carbon atoms at the active sites on the edge of A and Z models are unsaturated, with high chemical activity and strong adsorption capacity for NO molecules. It can be predicted that the heterogeneous reduction efficiency of NO can be further enhanced if the adsorption capacity of NO on the saturated coal char surface without active sites is improved.

### 3.3. NO Adsorption on the Na-Loaded Char Surface.
Two adsorption structures of sodium in the ortho position of char on an N or O atom were both studied because of the different effects of N and O atoms on the surface structure of char. The calculation results show that the difference of adsorption energy between the two adsorption modes is small, between 3.3 and 24.7 kJ/mol, indicating that the effect of sodium adsorbing on the char in the ortho position of the N or O atom is basically the same. Therefore, the ortho position structure with larger adsorption energy is only considered in the subsequent analysis. For the char surface structure G, the calculation results show that the NO molecule has the largest adsorption energy by the N-down adsorption site at the top of the sodium atom, so this adsorption site will be the main analysis object in the following.

#### 3.3.1. NO Adsorption on G@Na-Loaded Char Surface.
Studies have shown that the adsorption behavior of the NO molecule changed significantly after the decoration of the Na atom on the surface of the char structure G. As shown in Figure 3, the adsorption energy of NO in the N-down mode at the top of the Na atom is the largest, and the adsorption energy of the adsorption product G@Na-NO is −126.7 kJ/ mol, which is a more stable chemisorption compared with the physical adsorption on the surface of the sodium-free char structure. It is clear from Table 1 that the bond length of the NO molecule in structure G@Na-NO increased slightly from 0.1164 to 0.1205 nm. Compared with the extremely small bond length change of the NO molecule in the adsorption structure G-NO, it can be inferred that the addition of the Na atom significantly weakened the strength of the $\text{N}\xlongequal{}\text{O}$ double bond. In addition, when NO is adsorbed on the structure G@ Na, the Mulliken populations of the nitrogen and oxygen atoms were −0.222 e and −0.155 e, in contrast to 0.042 e and −0.042 e prior to adsorption. Compared with the change in the Mulliken populations of the nitrogen and oxygen atoms when NO is adsorbed on structure G, the NO molecule gets more electrons when the metal atom sodium is decorated on the structure G.

The PDOS of NO and C on the saturated graphene structure (denoted as G) as a function of energy is shown in Figure 6. The 2p orbital of the oxygen atom hybridizes with the 2p orbital of the nitrogen atom, as the peaks overlap at near $-27$, $-11.2$ to $-13$, $-6$ to $-7.5$, 0, and 2 eV. However, the orbital hybridization between the NO molecule and the carbon atoms is negligible, indicating that only a weak interaction exists between the two species.

After the sodium decoration on the saturated graphene, all the states of the N atom shift downward with the s, p, and d states markedly broadened and lowered in energy, indicating strong interactions with the surface. As shown in Figure 7, the 2p orbitals of the sodium atom hybridize with the 2p orbitals of the nitrogen and oxygen atoms, as the peaks overlap at $-25$, $-24.2$, $-12.1$, $-10.9$, 0, and 1.5 eV, thus giving sodium a high binding energy with NO. In summary, it is not difficult to find that the sodium atom has a catalytic effect on the adsorption of NO on the coal char structure without active sites. Also, it can be inferred that sodium in coal can promote the heterogeneous reduction reaction of NO by char.

3.3.2. NO Adsorption on A@Na-Loaded Char and Z@Na-Loaded Char Surfaces. As shown in Figures 4 and 5, the addition of the sodium atom increased the adsorption energy of the NO molecule on the surface of structure A from $-211.7$ to $-303.6$ kJ/mol, while the adsorption energy in structure Z hardly increased. This may be due to the large difference between the inherent characteristics of the armchair edge and zigzag edge. On the one hand, compared to the zigzag edge, the armchair edge has larger aromatic stability, $^{30}$ which leads to a lower oxidation ability (ability to lose electrons) of the armchair edge, that is, a relatively weak adsorption capacity for NO molecules. On the other hand, the zigzag edge region has a nonbonding $\pi$-electron state (called edge state), but there is no such state in the armchair edge. $^{31}$ The presence of an edge

state makes the zigzag edge have a stronger ability to provide electrons, that is, stronger adsorption of NO molecules. Therefore, the different edge characteristics of the A and Z structures make the two sodium-containing edge structures have great differences in electronic configuration, charge density, and electromagnetic properties. Finally, a comprehensive analysis of the calculation results shows that compared to the char structure G without active sites, the adsorption capacity of the char structures A and Z with active sites for NO is not significantly affected by the metal atom sodium. Also, the adsorption of the NO molecule on the surface of char structures with active sites has always been chemisorption, regardless of whether it is doped with metal atom sodium.

## 4. CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, the density functional theory with GGA in the form of PBE function for exchange−correlation potential was used to study the heterogeneous adsorption of NO on the surface of chars. Through the reasonable simplification of saturated coal char and unsaturated coal char with active sites and Na atom modification on the surface of the coal char structure, the influences of Na on the heterogeneous adsorption of NO molecules on coal char have been determined. It has been shown that the unsaturated coal char with active sites adsorbed NO mainly via chemical adsorption, while saturated coal char without active sites adsorbed NO via physical adsorption. The addition of sodium did not significantly promote the adsorption capacity of the zigzag edge char structure surface and slightly promoted the ability of the armchair edge char structure to adsorb NO molecules, but it obviously promoted the adsorption capacity of the surface of the saturated char structure without active sites; the adsorption behavior was changed from physical adsorption to chemisorption due to the addition of sodium.
