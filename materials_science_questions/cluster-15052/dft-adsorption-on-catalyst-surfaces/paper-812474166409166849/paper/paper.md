## ABSTRACT
Developing highly efficient photocatalysts to utilize solar radiation for converting $CO_2$ into solar fuels is of great importance for energy sustainability and carbon neutralization. Herein, through an alkali-etching-introduced interface reconstruction strategy, a nanowire photocatalyst denoted as $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, with rich Br and S dual-vacancies and surface $Bi-O$ bonding introduced significant near-infrared (NIR) light response, has been developed. The as-obtained $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires exhibit a highly efficient metallic photocatalytic reduction property for converting $CO_2$ into $CH_3OH$ when excited solely under NIR light irradiation. Free of any cocatalyst and sacrificial agent, metallic defective $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ shows 2.3-fold higher $CH_3OH$ generation than $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires. The detailed interfacial structure evolution and reaction mechanism have been carefully illustrated down to the atomic scale. This work provides a unique interfacial engineering strategy for developing high-performance sulfur-based NIR photocatalysts for photon reducing $CO_2$ into alcohol for achieving high-value solar fuel chemicals, which paves the way for efficiently using the solar radiation energy extending to the NIR range to achieve the carbon neutralization goal.

## INTRODUCTION
Inspired by the natural photosynthesis process, artificial photosynthesis based on semiconducting photocatalysts has been developed and treated as an efficient pathway for reducing atmospheric $CO_2$ and converting it into sustainable fuels. $^{1−10}$ Owing to the high structural stability of the $CO_2$ molecules, the photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction reaction ($CO_2RR$) is usually sluggish. Besides, $CO_2RR$ involves a multiple-protons-coupled electron transfer process, causing the diversity and uncontrollable selectivity of the resulted products in $CO_2RR.^{11−17}$ To accomplish the high $CO_2RR$ activity and selectivity, a few wide-bandgap semiconductors such as $TiO_2,^{18}$ $BiVO_4,^{19}$ and $g-C_3N_4^{20}$ have been developed with outstanding performance. These wide-bandgap photocatalysts have a high absorption coefficient at ultraviolet (UV) or visible light regions with their conduction and valence band potentials meeting the potential for photocatalytic $CO_2RR.^{21}$ For near-infrared (NIR) light, which constitutes about 50% of the solar energy, cannot be efficiently utilized by wide-bandgap photocatalysts; thus the utilization efficiency of solar energy has been limited. With regard to these issues, developing highly efficient photocatalysts with better solar energy utilization efficiency extending to NIR range for $CO_2RR$ is still a big challenge.

A typical photocatalytic $CO_2RR$ process involves the photoabsorption, electron−hole separation, $CO_2$ adsorption, $CO_2$ activation and reduction, and products desorption processes. Considering the utilization efficiency of the overall solar spectrum, developing UV−vis−NIR light-responsive photocatalysts is of high demand. Fabricating defective photocatalysts has been verified to be an efficient strategy to accelerate the separation and migration of photoinduced carriers. $^{21−25}$ This is because although the kinetic and thermodynamic parameters determine whether or how fast the $CO_2RR$ pathway will proceed, the product selectivity heavily relies on the interfacial atomic arrangement. $^{26−28}$ Very recently, Li et al. $^{29}$ demonstrated that in the atomical thin layer of charge-enriched $Cu-In$ dual sites containing sulfur-deficient $CuIn_5S_8$, a highly stable $Cu-C-O-In$ intermediate can be formed during the photocatalytic $CO_2RR$ process. Via this process, the endoergic protonation step has been converted into an exoergic reaction process, which facilitates methane production. Considering both the light response and defect structure merits, designing UV−vis−NIR light-responsive defects-rich photocatalysts should be a promising strategy to achieve highly efficient and selective $CO_2RR$ photocatalysts.

$Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ is an important $Bi^{III}-VII^{A}-VI^{A}$ narrow-bandgap semiconductor material, with a cationic framework in which the halide anions are partially occupied in the channel. $^{35,36}$ Similar to other classes of well-studied photoactive bismuth-based oxyhalides, their defective structure and electronic structure are expected to be easily tuned via the engineering of the halide anions. Herein, with an alkali-etching strategy, a metallic defective $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowire photocatalyst denoted as V−Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ has been synthesized and tailored to achieve a reconstructed interface with rich surface defects and oxygen doping. The reformed V−Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires contain dual-active sites which show an excellent photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction property by converting $CO_2$ into methanol. ### RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

Figure S1 illustrates the formation process of the V−Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowire photocatalyst with dual vacancies and oxygen doping via the alkali-etching strategy. First, Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires were synthesized through a one-pot hydrothermal process. Then the resulting products were dispersed in 1 M NaOH solution with continuous stirring. Via this process, part of the S and Br atoms on the nanowire surface are corrosive; some of the vacancies were occupied by O atoms, achieving the surface-reconstructed V−Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires. Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) was employed to characterize the as-prepared samples (Figure S2). Diffraction peaks of the highly crystalline hexagonal phase Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ (JCPDS No. 26-0813) free of impurity peaks were detected, suggesting that the as-synthesized Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires are of high-phase purity.³⁰ The surface structure and morphology of Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ and V−Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires were further examined with transmission electron microscope (TEM) imaging and diffraction techniques. Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires exhibit a three-dimensional sea urchin-like structure composed of single-crystalline nanowires (Figure S3). The lattice fringes of Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires with a lattice spacing of 0.373 nm observed by high-resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM) could be indexed to the (310) crystal plane of Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇. The corresponding selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern of the Bi₁₉Br₃S₂₇ nanowires displays a spot-like diffraction pattern, indicating the single-crystalline characteristics of the as-synthesized nanowires.


Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping analysis shows a homogeneous distribution of Bi, S, and Br in the overall nanowire, which further confirms the purity. Moreover, the valence states of the elements are verified by XPS analysis (Figure S4), which is consistent with its original valence state derived from the chemical formula. Compared to the as-synthesized $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires, the post-treatment nanowires denoted as $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ retain their original nanowire morphology (Figure 1a). The lattice fringes of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires are measured with a distance of 0.373 nm (Figure 1b). The angle of adjacent spots labeled in the SAED is consistent with the theoretical angle between the (010) and (001) planes in $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ (Figure 1c). These results demonstrate that $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires are anistropically grown along the [100] zone axis. EDS mappings display the uniform distribution of Bi, S, and Br elements in the $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires, while low-density O is also detectable within the nanowire (Figure 1d, e), which indicates that oxygen atoms have partially occupied the surface of the nanowire.

To further disclose the precise average local structure around bismuth in $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ samples, X-ray absorption spectra were collected at the Bi $L_{III}$-edge. The near-edge structure (XANES) and extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) Fourier transforms (FT) are shown in Figure 1f, g, respectively. The absorption edges of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ and $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires fall in between Bi foil and $Bi_2S_3$, revealing that the valence states of Bi element in these two samples are $Bi^{3-\delta}$ ($0 < \delta < 3$). In order to get the quantitative structural parameters around Bi, the least-squares curve fitting against the Fourier transforms of the EXAFS data for $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ was performed. The results are presented in Table S1. For $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, the coordination numbers for Bi-S and Bi-Br are reduced obviously, suggesting the formation of dual vacancies. The disorder degrees of Bi-Br coordination significantly increased compared to $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ as indicated by the increasing of the Debye-Waller factor. As the average crystal structure remains as indicated by the XRD and SAED, while the Bi-O coordination appeared, these results imply the presence of dangling bonds and distortion on the $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowire surface. The as-synthesized $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires underwent a surface reconstruction process. To figure out the bismuth atoms' coordination environment in $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, EXAFS wavelet transform analysis was performed (Figures 1h-k). The intensities of Bi-S and Bi-Br for $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires and Bi-S, Bi-O, and Bi-Br for $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires are detected, these results further validate the formation of Bi-O bonds and the absence of Bi-Bi pairs.

The types and quantities of vacancies in $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires were further detected by positron annihilation spectrometry (PAS) (Figure 2 and Tables 1 and 2). Three structural models for the original $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ with dual Br-S vacancies ($V_{Br-S}-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$) and O-doped $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ with dual Br-S vacancies ($V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$) were created. The corresponding calculated models and lifetime parameters (Table 1) of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ (Figure 2a-c), $V_{Br-S}-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ (Figure 2d-f), and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ (Figure 2g-i) are given. Based on the theoretical calculation (Table 2), the $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires reveal two lifetime parts, in which one life part ($\tau_1$) could be attributed to the S vacancies and another one ($\tau_2$, around 350 ps) for $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires could be imputed to positron annihilation trapped at Br-S vacancies ($V_{Br-S}'''$). These data announce the presence of the $V_{Br-S}'''$ vacancy in $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires. $^{37-39}$ The oxygen-free model of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ was also built to simulate the positron lifetime for comparison. Interestingly, the oxygen doping in the $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ structure has little influence on the positron lifetime, signifying that oxygen doping had little effect on the $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$. Except for the positron lifetime, the higher intensity of the positron lifetime of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires suggests the higher vacancy concentration of $V_{Br-S}'''$ in $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires after alkali etching.

To uncover the effect of defects on the electronic structures of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, theoretical and experimental characterizations were carried out. The density of states (DOS) across the Fermi level indicates the metallic behavior of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ (Figure 2k and l). $^{29,30}$ The $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ exhibits a higher hole concentration at the $B_{-1}$ band ($B_{-1}$) edge than $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, inferring the higher transition probability of photoexcited electrons to the CB under NIR light irradiation, and hence improving the activity of photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction.

The valence band XPS (VB-XPS) spectrum (Figure 3a) suggests the metallic properties of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires, which originated from the Bi 4p, S 2p, and Br 3d states with passing through the Fermi level (0 eV). $^{29,31}$ Besides, the resistivities of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires increasing along with the elevating temperature also confirm their metallic nature (Figure 3b). As shown in Figure 3c, both $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires possess intense absorption ranging from the UV region to the NIR region. Compared with $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires, the alkali-etched $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires have enhanced light absorption efficiency. The absorption energy gaps of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires are about 0.92 eV, which is in line with the calculated value from the $B_{-1}$ band to the Fermi level in the CB band in metallic photocatalysts (Figure S5). Moreover, the Mott-Schottky plots of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires were measured and are shown in Figure S6. The flat-band potential is calculated to be -0.67 V versus the Ag/AgCl electrode or -0.43 V versus the normal hydrogen electrode (NHE). The potential ($E_{CB}$) of n-type semiconductor is usually more negative ($E = 0.1-0.3$ V) than the flat-band potential. In this work, $E$ is chosen to be 0.1 V. Therefore, the $E_{CB}$ potential is -0.53 V, $^{40,41}$ suggesting $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ could reduce $CO_2$ to generate $CH_3OH$. The separation efficiency of photogenerated carriers in the catalysts plays a pivotal role in determining the photocatalytic $CO_2$RR activity. A series of characterization techniques were employed to investigate these properties. Figure S7 presents the photocurrent density of the samples under NIR light irradiation. The $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires exhibit a larger photocurrent density, indicating that $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires have a better photoresponse ability than that of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$. The Nyquist plots of electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were measured to evaluate the charge transfer efficiency, and the diameter of the semicircle is indicative of the charge transfer resistance. The $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires show a smaller semicircle diameter than the $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires, demonstrating the lower resistance with enhanced charge transfer ability after alkali etching (Figure S8). Furthermore, ultrafast transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy was employed to study the in-depth charge transfer dynamics in the photocatalyst. As depicted in Figure 3d, e, a broad positive absorption feature peaking at 1150 nm is observed in both $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires, which is derived from the photogenerated electrons in $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$. The much stronger positive absorption of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires suggests a higher concentration of photogenerated electrons. A transient absorption peak located at 1150 nm is observed for both $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires, indicating that the two catalysts can be excited by NIR light. Besides, the transient absorption intensity at 1150 nm increases in the period from 1 to 300 ps and then decreases from 300 to 7000 ps via recombination with multiple time constants. The faster decline of charge concentration in $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires compared to $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires reveals that the electron decay process can be slowed in $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires. Moreover, we examined the detailed electron decay kinetics at 1150 nm (Figure 3f). The fitted time profiles exhibit a 22-fold longer delay time for $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires (103.934 ps) than that for $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires (4.712 ps). Accordingly, the above results confirm that the formation of vacancies and the introduction of O doping are favorable for the prolonged lifetime of photogenerated electrons.⁴²

To evaluate the photocatalytic activity of samples, a series of $CO_2$ photoreduction experiments under full-spectrum, visible, and NIR light irradiation were carried out, respectively. Figure 3g shows that the main product under the photocatalytic process is $CH_3OH$. It can be seen that $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires exhibit a methanol yield of 1.6 $\mu mol\ g^{-1}$, roughly 2.3 times higher than that of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires. Furthermore, the control experiments show that there is no methanol or other product generation in the controlled condition, denoting that the generated $CH_3OH$ is originated from photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction under NIR light irradiation. From Figure 3i, the photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction process could be divided into two stages, namely, the accumulation of the intermediate species stage and the continuous adsorption and activation of the $CO_2$ stage. The peaks at around 2338 and $2360\ cm^{-1}$ correspond to the $CO_2$ adsorption on the surface of photocatalysts. $^{43}$ The positive peaks around $3000-3500$ and $3500-3800\ cm^{-1}$ are attributed to the surface $OH^-$ and adsorbed $H_2O$, respectively. $^{44}$ Besides, other peaks at 1072, 1160, 1248, 1435, and $1620\ cm^{-1}$ correspond to $^*CHO$, $^*CH_3O$, $^*CO_2^-$, $b-HCO_3^-$, and $^*COOH$, separately. $^{43,45}$ The observed intermediate species confirm the efficient photocatalysis of $CO_2$ reduction.

According to the above results, the reaction pathway of photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction to yield $CH_3OH$ can be presented as follows:

$$CO_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow * \mathrm{CO}_{2} \tag{1}$$

$$\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} \rightarrow \mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{OH}^{-} \tag{2}$$

$$* \mathrm{CO}_{2}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow * \mathrm{COOH} \tag{3}$$

$$* \mathrm{COOH}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{CO} *+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} \tag{4}$$

$$* \mathrm{CO}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow * \mathrm{CHO} \tag{5}$$

$$* \mathrm{CHO}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow * \mathrm{CH}_{2} \mathrm{O} \tag{6}$$

$$* \mathrm{CH}_{2} \mathrm{O}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow * \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{O} \tag{7}$$

$$* \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{O}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow * \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{OH} \tag{8}$$

where "*" represents the corresponding adsorption sites on the surface of the photocatalyst.

To explore the effect of defects on the desorption of $CO_2$ and CO, temperature-programmed desorption (TPD) measurements have been carried out as shown in Figures S12-S13. $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires exhibit two similar sharp desorption peaks at 290 and $370\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ in $CO_2$, while $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires display a broad peak at the higher temperature of around $430\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$, which might be due to the $CO_2$ desorption on the surface defects, indicating the vacancies and O-doping might be favorable for $CO_2$ adsorption on $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$. Moreover, CO TPD measurements confirm that $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires show more vital adsorption ability for CO than the $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires (Figure S13). These results imply that the *CO intermediate can be more easily be hydrogenated on the $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ surface, which benefits the formation of $CH_3OH$. This result is in accordance with the analysis of in situ FTIR.

The adsorption and activation of $CO_2$ on the photocatalyst's surface play a vital role in the photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction process. $^{46-48}$ DFT calculations were performed to investigate the adsorption energy and obtained electron number of $CO_2$ on the catalysts' interface (Figures 4a and b). According to the Bader method, 0.46 and 1.22 electrons are transferred from $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ to $CO_2$, respectively. The adsorption energies of $CO_2$ on the surface of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ are calculated to be -2.63 and -2.73 eV, respectively. The above results suggest that the surface vacancies and O-doping of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires are more beneficial to the adsorption and activation of $CO_2$. The Gibbs free energies of each elementary reaction step were also calculated, involving the $CO_2$ reduction to $CH_3OH$ process on the surface of the $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, $V_{Br-S}-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$, and $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$. Figures 4c-f display the free energy profiles and the corresponding intermediate configurations. For the original $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ model, the rate-determining step protonates *COOH to form *CO with a free-energy barrier of 1.80 eV. After the introduction of the dual vacancies, the hydrogenation of *CO to form *CHO with a free-energy barrier of 2.56 eV goes against the further reaction. However, the dual vacancies significantly decrease the energy barrier of $CO_2$ hydrogenation to generate *COOH. While oxygen is doped into the structure of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ with dual $Br-S$ vacancies, the protonation of *COOH to produce *CO is considered as the critical process for the formation of CO or $CH_3OH$ with a free-energy barrier of 0.82 eV. The synergistic effect of vacancies and O-doping further reduces the required energy of the whole reaction process. Comparing the Gibbs free energies of the three systems, we could find that O-doping changes the hydrogenation process of *CO from endothermic reaction to exothermic reaction. The change avails the further hydrogenation of *CO to form $CH_3OH$, consistent with the CO TPD analysis. In general, the $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ system possesses a much lower free energy barrier throughout the reaction than its analogues, signifying that $CO_2$ could be more easily reduced to form $CH_3OH$ on the surface of $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$. The above results reveal that the synergistic effect of vacancies and O-doping in the $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ system could enhance the adsorption and activation of $CO_2$ and promote the separation of photogenerated carriers, resulting in efficient photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction.

## CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we have constructed a dual-vacancies rich metallic $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ with oxygen doping via a simple alkaline-etching method to precisely tune the selectivity and reactivity of photocatalytic $CO_2$ reduction. The EXAFS results show $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ after alkali etching reduced the coordination numbers of $Bi-S$ and $Bi-Br$, while the disorder degree of $Bi-Br$ coordination is increased. Meanwhile, the $Bi-O$ bonding appeared, revealing the interfacial reconstruction of $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires. The synergistic effect of vacancies and O-doping not only reduces the required energy of the overall reaction process, but also changes the process of the hydrogenation of *CO from endothermic reaction to exothermic reaction. The structural modification avails the further hydrogenation of *CO to form $CH_3OH$ on $V-Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires with a yield of $1.6\ \mu mol\ g^{-1}$, roughly 2.3 times higher than using $Bi_{19}Br_3S_{27}$ nanowires. This work provides a simple strategy for the interface reconstruction of photocatalysts to enhance their photocatalytic activity and selectivity for $CO_2$ reduction under NIR excitation, paving the way for developing novel efficient photocatalysts for $CO_2$RR under NIR excitation.
