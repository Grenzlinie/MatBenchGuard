## ABSTRACT
The adsorption behavior of NO gas on the pristine graphitic carbon nitride (gCN) and transition metal atoms (TM)-embedded gCN systems (TM = Fe, Ru, and Os atoms) was investigated with density functional theory in solid state. The adsorption energy results revealed that the embedding of TM atoms can significantly improve the adsorption properties of NO gas on the gCN systems with high adsorption energy of $-3.14\ \text{eV}$ for Os-embedded gCN, compared with that on the pristine gCN ($-0.54\ \text{eV}$). Our results indicated that the adsorption energy of NO gas on the Os-embedded system is much larger than those reported for the other adsorbents. Although NO gas was physisorbed on the pristine gCN, this gas was strongly chemisorbed over the TM-embedded systems. Furthermore, it was observed that the electronic and magnetic characteristics of gCN considerably modulated by embedding of transition metal atoms. The results of magnetic calculations displayed that Os-embedded gCN exhibited magnetic properties with magnetic moment of $0.77\ \mu_{\text{B}}$, whereas Fe and Ru-embedded gCN systems are non-magnetic. Furthermore, it was found that the conductivity of TM-embedded gCN systems was more than that of the pristine system, due to the induced TM atoms impurity states in the band gap energy. In addition, the results displayed electron donation from d-orbitals of TM atoms to $\pi^{\ast}$ orbital of NO gas for all of the TM-embedded gCN systems. Therefore, the results displayed that among all of the TM-embedded gCN systems, the Os-embedded gCN has the best adsorption characteristics for sensing and removing of NO gas from the environment.

## 1. Introduction
Nitric oxide gas (NO) is a major gaseous pollutants, since it is extremely hazardous to human health [1,2]. A major source of NO gas is through combustion of fuels in automobiles and industrial activities [3,4]. It should be noted that concentration of NO gas in the air has significantly increased over the past few decades. Hence, it is important to develop efficient, cheap, and green technologies to remove this toxic

Among several materials, graphitic carbon nitride (gCN) has attracted tremendous at- tention by its excellent properties such as physicochemical stability, high-hardness, reliable chemical inertness, suitable band gap energy $(E_{g})$ , and also offering a high surface area for adsorption [5-8]. These unique features make gCN a promising material for different applica- tions, such as environmental remediation, hydrogen production from water splitting, reduction of carbon dioxide, gas storage, spintronics, and gas sensors [9-13]. In addition, to improve the performance of gCN, several strategies have been proposed such as doping, decorating, and embedding various elements [14-16].

Abdullahi et al. [17] studied the electronic properties of Fe-em- bedded gCN systems using Quantum Espresso (QE) simulation package. The results of calculations illustrated that incorporation of Fe atom on the gCN sheet led to modify the electronic features of this system. The electronic characteristics modulation can be related to the orbital hy- bridization between p-orbitals of gCN and d-orbitals of Fe atom. The potential application of metal-doped (such as Ag-, Fe-, Li-, Na-, Cu-, K-,and Mn- atoms) gCN as extremely sensitive sensors for detection of $NO_{2}$ molecule were investigated by Zhang and co-workers [18] using $Dmol^{3}$  package. They found that among these systems, Ag-, Li-, Na-, and K- doped gCN systems displayed the highest adsorption energy $(E_{ads})$ for this gas. Hussain and co-workers [19] reported the hydrogen molecule storage characteristics of polylithiated molecules $(CLi_{2}, OLi_{2})$ functionalized gCN systems using Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) code. They found that both gCN systems functionalized with $2 CLi_{2}$ and20Li2 could be considered as efficient hydrogen storage materials. The magnetic and electronic properties of Mn-embedded gCN were de- monstrated by Abdullahi et al. [20] using QE simulation package. The results showed that incorporation of Mn element in the gCN modified the electronic characteristics of this system and induced magnetic moments for the embedded system. The effect of oxygen doping over the electronic features of gCN systems were investigated by Liang and co-workers [21] using VASP code. These researchers observed that oxygen doping induced more active sites and also modified the elec- tronic properties of gCN systems. Moreover, electronic characteristics of the TM-embedded (TM = Cr-, Fe-, and Zn- atoms) gCN systems were studied by Pati co-workers [22] using VASP code. The results presented hybridization between p-orbitals of gCN and d-orbitals of TM atoms leaded to a considerable decrease in the band gap energy of these systems. Finally, Pan and co-workers [23] elucidated adsorption of hydrogen gas over gCN using VASP code. They found that gCN is a promising high-performance material for hydrogen storage.

Herein, a summary about adsorption of NO gas on different ad- sorbents with the corresponding $E_{ads}$ are demonstrated in Table 1 and Table 1S [24-46]. From these tables, it can be clearly seen that the Eads for adsorption of NO gas on the Ir-embedded and Pd, cluster systems $(E_{ads}=-3.12 eV)$ are higher than those of the other adsorbents. Zhu et al. [47] investigated the adsorption behavior of NO gas over the transition metal (TM = Fe-, Co-, Ni-, Cu-, Ag-, Au-, Rh-, Pd-, Pt-, and Ir- atoms)-embedded monolayer $MoS_{2}$ using density functional theory(DFT) calculations. The results of calculations displayed that the Ir- embedded $MoS_{2}$ can be used as a suitable candidate for sensing and removing NO gas from the environment. Moreover, the interaction of NO gas with the $Pd_{n}$ clusters was indicated by Deka and co-workers[48] using $DMol^{3}$ package. The results revealed that the $E_{ads}$ of NO gas on the $Pd_{2}$ cluster is more negative than those of the other Pd clusters.

We investigated in our previous work the adsorption behavior of HCN gas on the pure gCN and Ni, Pd, and Pt-embedded gCN systems by DFT calculations. The results showed that the interaction between HCN gas and Pt-embedded gCN $(E_{ads}=-1.54 eV)$ is more negative than those of the other adsorbents [49]. In addition, from the literature re- ports, it was known that there are no reports for utilization of the pristine and TM-embedded gCN systems as NO gas sensors and ad- sorbents. Therefore, we investigated the adsorption of NO gas on the pristine gCN and Fe, Ru, and Os-embedded gCN systems using DFT calculations in order to introduce suitable materials for application in removal of NO gas from the atmosphere.

## 2. Computational details

All calculations were carried out within the framework of DFT based on first-principles calculations using QE simulation package. The cut-off kinetic energy for the plane-wave expansion was set to 80 Ry. The

generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the form of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzernhof (PBE) functional for the exchange-correlation functions were used. Marzari-Vanderbilt smearing method with Gaussian spreading was employed to aid the convergences of our systems. The Brillouin zone was sampled by the Mankhorst-Pack grid with $1 \times 1 \times 7$ k-mesh. A large vacuum distance of $18 \mathring{A}$ was applied along the Å-Z direction to avoid the adjacent gCN systems interlayer interactions. The $E_{ads}$ for adsorption of NO gas over the pristine and TM-embedded gCN systems were computed from the following equation:

$$
E_{a d s}=E_{g C N-N O}-E_{g C N}-E_{N O} \tag{1}
$$

Where $E_{gCN-NO}$ is the total energy of gCN system with NO gas, $E_{gCN}$ and $E_{NO}$ are the energies of gCN system without NO and isolated NO gas, respectively. Hence, a more negative adsorption energy corresponds to a stronger binding of the adsorbed NO gas on the gCN system. Accordingly, a negative $E_{ads}$ corresponds to an exothermic adsorption process. It should be noted that the relaxed free NO gas energies were calculated in the same supercell.

## 3. Results and discussion

In this work, using DFT calculations, we investigated the adsorption behavior of NO gas on the heptazine-based pristine gCN and Fe, Ru, and Os-embedded gCN systems. The results of previous studies illustrated that the heptazine-based structure is energetically favorable than s-triazine-based structure of gCN, which is consistent with the previous studies [50-53]. The optimized $1 \times 1$ unit cell for this structure of gCN system consists of 8 N and 6 C atoms (see Fig. 1). The C and N atoms of this unit cell can be divided into two categories, namely, two-coordinated and three-coordinated atoms, which are labeled as, $C_{inner}$ (tri-coordinated with N atoms), $N_{edge}$ (two-coordinated with C atoms), $N_{bridge}$ (linking separate heptazine unit), $C_{bridge}$ (linking the heptazine units to a Nbridge), and $N_{in}$ (tri-coordinated with C atoms). Based on the results of optimized structures, it can be found that all of the C-N bond lengths in this unit cell are $1.361 \mathring{A}$, except for the $C_{inner}$-$N_{bridge}$ $(1.572 \mathring{A})$ and $C_{inner}$-$N_{inner}$ $(1.419 \mathring{A})$ bond lengths, which are in excellent agreement with the theoretical and experimental reports [54,55] (see Fig. 2).

In addition, the optimized $2 \times 2$ supercell of heptazine-based structure of gCN systems is presented in Fig 0.2. As can be seen from this figure, the optimized lattice parameter for this system is $7.4$ °A, which is in good accordance with the other theoretical results [56-58].

The lowest-energy optimized structures for the pristine gCN and Fe, Ru, and Os-embedded gCN systems before and after adsorption of NO gas are shown in Fig. 3. As can be observed from this figure, the most stable site for the decoration of TM atoms on the gCN system is the vacancy site, because the interaction between TM and atoms of gCN system in the vacancy site is stronger than those of the other sites [22]. The results of previous studies indicated that interaction between the TM-embedded gCN and N atom of NO gas is stronger than that of O atom [39,59]. In addition, from Fig. 3, it can be seen that NO gas (from N site) is located above the vacancy site of the pristine gCN, because the adsorption energy of this toxic gas on this position of gCN is more negative than those of the other positions [60,61]. Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 3, the pristine gCN system is flat, whereas the Fe, Ru, and Os-embedded systems are not perfectly planar. Moreover, it can be found that with adsorption of NO gas over these systems, the initial flat structure of the pristine gCN automatically became wrinkles, because the buckle structure of gCN is about 0.6 eV more stable than the flat one, which is in agreement with the previous theoretical results [62-64].

The information of the Fermi energy level $(E_{F})$, the band gap energy, the total induced magnetic moment per unit cell $(M_{tot})$, the structural parameters (such as bond length of NO gas $(d_{N-O})$, the distance between TM atoms and N atom of NO gas $(d_{TM-N})$), and the Lowdin charges analysis for the pristine gCN and TM-embedded gCN systems are reported in Table 2. As illustrated in this table, the $E_{ads}$ of NO gas on the pristine gCN and Fe, Ru, and Os-embedded gCN systems are $-0.172$, $-2.78$, $-2.76$, and $-3.14$ eV, respectively, which demonstrated that NO gas is chemisorbed on the TM-embedded systems. Hence, it is revealed that the embedding of TM atoms can significantly improve the adsorption properties of NO gas on the gCN systems with the highest adsorption energy of $-3.14$ eV for the Os-embedded gCN system. These results indicated that the Os-embedded gCN system is a good candidate for sensing and removing of this gas from the atmosphere, because the interaction between NO gas and the Os-embedded gCN system is stronger than those of the other adsorbents (see Table 1). In addition, from this table, it can be observed that the magnetic properties of gCN system can be modulated by embedding TM atoms. The pristine gCN, Fe, and Ru-embedded gCN systems are non-magnetic, while the Os-embedded gCN has magnetic moments of $0.77$ $\mu_{B}$, due to weak hybridization between p-orbital of $N_{edge}$ atom and d-orbitals of Os atom. In order to analyze the magnetic and electronic characteristics modulations of the pristine and TM-embedded gCN systems, we computed the spin-polarized electronic band structures (see Fig. 4) and the related parameters (Table 2). The $E_{F}$ was set at 0.00 eV and shown with a red line. As can be seen in this figure, the pristine gCN system is a non-magnetic semiconductor ($E_{g}=1.20$ eV), but when Fe, Ru, and Os atoms were embedded in the porous site of gCN, the electronic and magnetic characteristics of these systems were modulated. Compared to the pristine gCN, the band gap energies of the Fe, Ru, and Os-embedded systems were considerably reduced to 0.35, 0.95, and 0.90 eV, respectively. On the other hand, the electronic characteristics of the TM-embedded systems were significantly changed due to the induced TM atoms impurity states in the band gap energy of these systems. It should be noted that $E_{g}$ of the pristine gCN (1.20 eV) is much lower than the experimental result (2.7 eV), because the PBE method usually underestimates the $E_{g}$ of semiconductors [71-74]. Based on these results, it can be deduced that upon adsorption of NO gas over the pristine and embedded gCN systems, the $E_{g}$ was decreased, which displayed an enhancement in conductivity of these systems.

As seen in Fig. 4, the spin-up (top panel) and spin-down (bottom panel) channels of the valence band (VB) and conduction band (CB) levels for the pristine gCN, Fe, and Ru-embedded systems are basically symmetric, resulting in non magnetic characteristics for these systems. Interestingly, for the Os-embedded gCN system, both spin-up, and spin-down channels of VB and CB levels around the $E_{F}$ are asymmetric, indicating that Os atom can change the magnetic properties of gCN ($M_{tot}=0.77\ \mu_{\mathrm{B}}$). On the other hand, the weak hybridization between p-orbitals of $\mathrm{N}_{edge}$ and d-orbitals of Os atom induces magnetic moments in this system. However, adsorption of NO gas on the Os-embedded leads an up shift in the band structure and spin-up, and spin-down channels of VB and CB levels become asymmetric. Thus, NO gas can effectively modulate magnetic properties of the Os-embedded system. In addition, as illustrated in Fig. 4, with adsorption of NO gas on these systems, the $E_{F}$ were pushed up into the conduction bands due to electron donation from d-orbitals of TM atoms to the $\pi^{*}$ orbital of NO gas, which is consistent with the previous reports [27,75,76].

## 4. Conclusions
The adsorption behavior of toxic NO gas on the pristine gCN and Fe, Ru, and Os-embedded gCN systems was investigated using DFT calculations. The results demonstrated that NO gas can be significantly adsorbed on the Os-embedded gCN with excellent adsorption energy of $-3.14$ eV. Our results indicated that the adsorption energy of NO gas on the Os-embedded gCN is much larger than those reported for the other adsorbents. Furthermore, it was observed that the magnetic and electronic properties of the gCN systems are considerably modulated by embedding the selected transition metal atoms. The results of magnetic calculations displayed that the Os-embedded gCN exhibited magnetic properties with magnetic moment of $0.77\ \mu_{B}$, whereas the Fe and Ru-embedded gCN systems were with non-magnetic characteristics. Furthermore, the results indicated that electrical conductivity of the TM-embedded gCN systems were more than that of the pristine system, thanks to the induced TM atoms impurity states in the band gap energy

of these systems. Furthermore, the results of optimization indicated that with adsorption of NO gas on the pristine gCN, the initial planar structure of gCN automatically became buckles, because the wrinkle structure of gCN is more stable than the planar one. The results of Lowdin charges analysis indicated that electron donation from the TM-embedded gCN systems to NO gas increased the N-O bond length of this gas. Hence, NO molecules act as electron acceptors, whereas TM-embedded systems serve as electron donors. Finally, the results revealed that between the TM-embedded gCN systems, the Os-embedded gCN is a suitable candidate for sensing and removing of NO gas from the atmosphere.
