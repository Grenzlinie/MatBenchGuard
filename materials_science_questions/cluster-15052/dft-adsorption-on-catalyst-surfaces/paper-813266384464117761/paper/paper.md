# First-principles study of the oxygen adsorption and dissociation on graphene and nitrogen doped graphene for Li-air batteries
H. J. Yan, $^{1}$ B. Xu, $^{1,2}$ S. Q. Shi, $^{2}$ and C. Y. Ouyang $^{1,2,a)}$
$^{1}$ Department of Physics, Jiangxi Normal University, Nanchang 330022, China
$^{2}$ Key Laboratory for Advanced Functional materials of Jiangxi Province, Nanchang 330022, China

(Received 25 August 2012; accepted 24 October 2012; published online 21 November 2012)

Recently, Yoo and Zhou [ACS Nano 5, 3020-3026 (2011)] reported that graphene can be directly used as electrode for Li-air batteries, suggesting that graphene itself can be served as catalyst for $O_2$ dissociation reaction. In this work, we show from density functional theory calculations that the $O_2$ dissociation reaction energy barrier is substantially decreased on the graphene surface comparing to that of in vacuum. Furthermore, N-doping can further decrease the energy barrier from 2.39 eV (undoped case) to 1.20 eV. The $O_2$ molecule physical adsorption, O atom chemical adsorption at the graphene, and N-doped graphene surfaces are also simulated. © 2012 American Institute of Physics. [http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4766919]

## I. INTRODUCTION
Li-air battery has attracted the attention of researchers due to its high energy density. Typically, the negative electrode (anode) is lithium metal, while the air electrode consists of amorphous carbon. $^{1}$ External $O_2$ molecules enter into the air electrode and are adsorbed on the surface of the amorphous carbon. Then, $O_2$ molecules dissociate and react with lithium ions that come from oxidation of the anode. In the past, it was believed that amorphous carbon was only served as the support of the catalyst, and the catalyst commonly consists of noble metals like Pt, Au (Ref. 2), or transition metal oxides like $MnO_2$ (Ref. 3) and $Mn_3O_4.^{4}$

Recently, Zhou et al. showed that thin films consisting of only multi-layer graphene (nanosheets) can work with rather good performance as Li-air battery electrode. $^{5}$ The thin film electrode can be prepared simply by pencil-drawing on a solid state electrolyte. $^{6}$ Those studies show that graphene not only can be served as catalyst support but also has catalytic effect to oxygen reduction reaction in Li-air battery.

On the other hand, nitrogen doped carbon nanotube and graphene have been used as catalyst for electroreduction of oxygen in the fuel cell systems. $^{7-9}$ It is shown that the catalytic activity is enhanced through nitrogen doping. However, those experimental studies did not give clear explanations to the mechanism of the nitrogen-doping to the catalytic effect.

In the present work, we performed first-principles simulations to investigate the dissociation of $O_2$ molecule in graphene and nitrogen-doped graphene. We show that both graphene and nitrogen-doped graphene have catalytic effect to the oxygen dissociation reaction in a Li-air battery system. The catalytic reaction pathway of oxygen dissociation is figured out, which gives deep understanding to the catalytic effect of graphene.

## II. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS
First-principles calculations are performed using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) $^{10,11}$ within the projector augmented-wave (PAW) approach. $^{12}$ The ground state of the electronic structure is described within density functional theory (DFT) using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with PW91 exchange correlation functional. $^{13}$ An energy cutoff of 520 eV for the plane-wave basis set is used for all calculations. The single layer graphene sheet is modeled with a $4×4×1$ unit cell containing 32 carbon atoms, which is separated with a $15\mathring{A}$ vacuum layer in the z-axis direction. The N-doped graphene is modeled by replacing one carbon atom with a nitrogen atom in the $4×4×1$ unit cell. The Monkhorst-Pack $^{14}$ scheme of $3×3×2k$-point mesh is used for integration in the Brillouin zone. With those computational parameters, the total ground state energy is converged within $10^{-4}$ eV per formula unit. The atomic positions are fully relaxed and the final force on each atom is $<0.05$ eV/$\mathring{A}$. To optimize the $O_2$ molecule dissociation path, the climbing image nudged elastic band (CINEB) method $^{15}$ is used in the present study.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
### A. $O_2$ molecule physical adsorption
$O_2$ molecule can be adsorbed physically on the graphene and N-doped graphene surface. We put one $O_2$ molecule on the graphene layer with different orientations (both parallel and vertical to the graphene basin plane) and relax the structure, and we found that the molecule prefers to stay parallel to the graphene basin plane with an adsorption height of $2.824\mathring{A}$, as shown in Fig. 1. The bond length of the $O_2$ molecule is $1.263\mathring{A}$, which is slightly larger than that of a free $O_2$ molecule ($1.235\mathring{A}$) in vacuum. This indicates that the O-O bonding is slightly weakened when the molecule is adsorbed on the graphene. The $O_2$ molecule prefers to locate in the middle of a $C_6$ ring, with the two O atoms close to two C atoms along the diagonal of the $C_6$ ring (refer to the top view in Fig. 1).

The vertical distance of the top-site O atom to the graphene basin plane is $2.461\ \mathring{A}$, which is much smaller than that of undoped case. In this case, the orientation of the $O_2$ molecule is not strictly parallel to the graphene basin plane. As the $O_2$ molecule moves down and closer to the N-doped graphene, the interaction between them is stronger than the undoped case. As a result, the O-O bond is further weakened, and the bond length becomes $1.280\ \mathring{A}$.

To quantitatively describe the adsorption strength, we defined the adsorption energy as
$$
\mathrm{E_{ad}} = -1/2 \times \left[\mathrm{E_1 - E_2 - E(O_2)}\right],
$$
where $\mathrm{E_1}$ and $\mathrm{E_2}$ are total energies of the graphene (or N-doped graphene) layer with and without $O_2$ molecule adsorbed on it, respectively. $\mathrm{E(O_2)}$ is the total energy of an oxygen molecule in vacuum. The adsorption energies are obtained to be 0.13 eV and 0.31 eV for the undoped and N-doped cases, respectively. Those positive adsorption energy values ensure that $O_2$ molecule can be physically adsorbed on the graphene surfaces with relatively large strength. That is to say, when external $O_2$ molecules enter into the air electrode, $O_2$ molecules can be easily adsorbed by the graphene surfaces.

The magnitude of the adsorption energies is quite large for simple physical adsorptions. Therefore, charge transfer might be occurred when $O_2$ molecule is adsorbed on the graphene surface. In order to verify this, we plotted the induced charge density in Fig. 2(a) defined as $\Delta\rho = \rho(\mathrm{C_{32}O_2}) - \rho(\mathrm{C_{32}}) - \rho(\mathrm{O_2})$, where $\rho(\mathrm{C_{32}O_2})$ and $\rho(\mathrm{C_{32}})$ are the charge density of the graphene system with and without $O_2$ adsorbed on the surface, respectively. $\rho(\mathrm{O_2})$ is the charge density of $O_2$ molecule. With this definition, isosurfaces of positive values indicate gain charge, while negative values indicate loss charge. It shows clearly from Fig. 2(a) that $O_2$ molecule gains charge when it is adsorbed on the graphene surface. The majority of the charge is contributed by the nearest neighboring C atoms within the $C_6$ ring below the $O_2$ molecule, while it does have small contribution from next nearest neighboring C atoms. On the other hand, charge redistribution occurred within the $O_2$ molecule. Some small portion of the charge in the middle of the two O atoms is removed, which is an indication of the weakened O-O bond strength. This is in agreement with the observed elongation of the $O_2$ bond length upon adsorption on the graphene.

The integrated charge in Fig. 2(b) also gives clear evidence of the charge transfer from C to the $O_2$ molecule, although the amount of the transferred charge is small. As the inset in Fig. 2(b) shows, when the sphere radius is larger than $\sim2\ \mathring{A}$, the integrated charge is approximately 12 electrons (the total valence electrons) for the case of free $O_2$ molecule in vacuum. This shows that almost all the charge is distributed in a sphere with $\sim2\ \mathring{A}$ radius around the $O_2$ molecule. For the other two cases, the integrated charge for a given sphere radius is always more than that of the free $O_2$ molecule. When the sphere radius is smaller than $2\ \mathring{A}$, the difference is small and mainly comes from the charge transfer from C to $O_2$. When the sphere radius is larger than $2\ \mathring{A}$, the difference is very large, because the charge of C atom is now included in the sphere.

### B. O atom chemical adsorption

We have tested different oxygen adsorption sites on graphene and N-doped graphene. We found that only two stable

adsorption sites on the graphene plane, as shown in Fig. 3. Sites 1 and 2 are the bridge site of two C atoms and top site of one C atom, respectively. To make a quantitative description of the chemical adsorption of O atom on the graphene plane, we can define adsorption energy.¹⁶ The adsorption energies as defined with reference to a free O atom

$$
\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{ad}}=-1 ×\left[\mathrm{E}\left(\mathrm{C}_{32} \mathrm{O}\right)-\mathrm{E}\left(\mathrm{C}_{32}\right)-\mathrm{E}(\mathrm{O})\right].
$$

The calculated results are given in Table I. For the case of undoped graphene, O atom prefers to stay at the bridge site (site No. 1 in Fig. 3). In this case, the O atom is bonded with two C atoms with bond lengths of about $1.472\mathring{\text{A}}$, which is much longer than the C-O-C bond length ($1.175\mathring{\text{A}}$) in a free $\text{CO}_2$ molecule, indicating that the C-O bonds are much weaker than the C-O bonds in the $\text{CO}_2$ molecule. This is favorable to the reaction of the adsorbed O atom with Li ions during the discharge process of the Li-air battery. The graphene plane is distorted substantially upon adsorption of an O atom. The C atoms that bond with the O atom are moved up due to the covalent interaction between them.

For the case of N-doped graphene, O atoms prefer to stay at the top of one C atom nearest neighboring to the N atom (site No. 3 in Fig. 3). In this case, the adsorption energy is about $0.7\,\text{eV}$ larger than that of adsorption at the bridge site of the undoped graphene. On the other hand, the bridge sites closest to the N atom (site No. 4 in Fig. 3) are also in favor of O adsorption, although the adsorption energy is lower than that of site No. 3. In the case of O adsorbed at site No. 3, the C-O bond length is only $1.323\mathring{\text{A}}$, which is shorter than other cases, indicating that stronger C-O bonding is formed. Furthermore, for site No. 4, the two C-O bonds are not equivalent. The short one (bond length $1.443\mathring{\text{A}}$) is formed with the C atom that bonds with the N atom.

After $\text{O}_2$ molecule is dissociated and O atoms are adsorbed at the bridge sites of the $\text{C}_6$ ring, O atoms are ready to react with Li ions. Before this reaction happens, O atoms need to diffuse at the graphene surface to meet Li ions. Therefore, the migration of O atom on the graphene surface is also an important factor to the overall electrochemical reaction. The O atom migration pathway at the graphene surface is optimized with CNE B method. Fig. 4 presents the energy profile along the optimized migration pathway. The energy barrier is $0.72\,\text{eV}$, which is exactly the adsorption energy difference between site No. 1 and site No. 2. In fact, we found that the optimized migration pathway is exactly along the edge of the $\text{C}_6$ ring, and the transition state is achieved when the O atom reaches the top of the C atom. With this energy barrier, the O atom mobility could not be very high since the O atom migration under room temperature is not easy. Therefore, during the discharge process of the Li-air battery, Li ion diffusion plays more important roles than O diffusion because the energy barrier for Li ion diffusion at graphene surface is much lower.¹⁷

## C. $\text{O}_2$ molecule dissociation

During the discharge process of the Li-air battery, the metallic Li in anode loses electron and becomes Li ion, which diffuses through the electrolyte and reaches the graphene electrode. At the same time, $\text{O}_2$ molecules from air also diffuse to the carbon electrode and are adsorbed by the graphene. The electrochemical reaction is the oxidation of metallic lithium to form lithium peroxide ($2\text{Li} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{Li}_2\text{O}_2$), in which the $\text{O}_2$ molecule dissociation is critical.

## IV. SUMMARIES AND CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we have studied the $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ adsorption and dissociation on the graphene and N-doped graphene surfaces from density functional calculations. $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ molecule prefers to adsorb on the graphene surface with the two $\mathrm{O}$ atoms close to two $\mathrm{C}$ atoms along the diagonal of the $\mathrm{C}_{6}$ ring. It is found that charge transfer occurs between the $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ molecule and the graphene sheet, which makes the adsorption energetically stable. Nitrogen doping enhances the adsorption since $\mathrm{N}$ atom has one more electron than $\mathrm{C}$ atom. For single $\mathrm{O}$ atom adsorption on the graphene surface, the bridge sites are energetically most stable. However, N-doping makes the top site of the nearest neighboring $\mathrm{C}$ atom to the $\mathrm{N}$ atom the most stable adsorption site. $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ dissociation reaction can be catalyzed directly by graphene. The energy barrier of about $2.39\,\mathrm{eV}$ is much smaller than the $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ binding energy. Upon N-doping, this energy barrier can be further decreased to $1.20\,\mathrm{eV}$.
