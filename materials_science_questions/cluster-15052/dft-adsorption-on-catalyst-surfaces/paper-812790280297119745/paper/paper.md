# A DFT study on the potential application of Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$ porous fullerene as an innovative and highly active catalyst for NO reduction

Hosein Hamadi$^{a*}$, Ehsan Shakerzadeh,$^{a}$ Mehdi D.Esrafili$^{b}$

$^{a}$ Chemistry Department, Faculty of Sciences, Shahid Chamran University of Ahvaz, Ahvaz, Iran

$^{b}$Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Basis Sciences, University of Maragheh, P.O. Box 55136-553, Maragheh, Iran

## Abstract

The potential application of porphyrin-like porous $C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene decorated with a single Si atom (Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$) as a highly active catalyst for the reduction of NO in the presence of CO is investigated through the dispersion-corrected DFT calculations. The results reveal that NO can be simply reduced into $N_{2}O$ through the dimer mechanism. This reaction also produces $O_{ads}$ species which is attached to the Si atom. Thereafter, the $O_{ads}$ is eliminated by an incoming CO by overcoming small activation energy of 6.68 kcal/mol. These results demonstrate that the novel Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$ can be regarded as a pioneering metal-free catalyst for NO reduction.

**Keyword:** NO reduction; DFT calculations; mechanism; Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene

### 1. Introduction

The nitric oxide (NO) is a hazardous colorless, flammable gas with a slight odor in which is insufficient to provide warning. Globally, quantities of NO are produced naturally by volcanic action, lightning, and small extent, and by microbial processes in soils. NO also is produced in combustion processes, partly from nitrogen compounds in the fuel, but mostly by direct combination of atmospheric oxygen and nitrogen in flames [1, 2]. Other atmospheric contributions come from non-combustion processes, for example nitric acid manufacture, welding processes and the use of explosives. In the atmosphere, NO can be oxidized into NO₂, which in turn mixes with water vapor to form nitric acid [3]. This acidic pollution can be transported by wind over many hundreds of miles, and deposited as acid rain. Recently, the catalytic reduction of NO has been the subject of intensive studies due its significance in such applications as cleaning air and lowering NO emission from automobiles and industries [4-12]. Several conventional catalysts for the NO reduction such as Au, Pt or Pd or their alloys with metal oxides as a support have been studied extensively [13-22]. Although these noble metal-based catalysts can effectively catalyze NO reduction, they generally suffer from high cost, low abundance and rapid deactivation which may hinder their large-scale applications. Therefore, there is an increasing demand for finding alternative highly active and stable metal-free catalysts, and this has stimulated research activities in the field metal-based catalysis. Recently, it has been reported that the layered SiC sheets exhibit superior catalytic activity toward NO reduction [23].Also, the potential of utilizing the Si-doped boron nitride nanosheet (Si-BNNS) as a highly active catalyst for the reduction of NO in the presence of CO molecule has been reported through the dispersion-corrected DFT calculations [24]. The catalytic activity of Ni-doped graphene in the reduction of NO by CO molecules has been also investigated using DFT calculations, and the

results have indicated that the obtained activation energies for the reduction of NO over this surface are comparable with those of over noble metal catalysts [25].

A number of theoretical studies have also found that the coordination of porous N-doped carbon nanostructures with different metals to form porphyrin-like structures can be an effectual approach to improve their catalytic activity [26, 27]. Importantly, the metal clustering problem is avoided in these structures owing to the great adsorption energy of the coordinated atoms over the porphyrin-like N4 cavity [28]. The porphyrin-like carbon nanotubes have been synthesized using plasma enhanced chemical vapor deposition method [29, 30] in which four carbon atoms are replaced by four nitrogen ones through truncated doping in these tubes. The synthesis of N-doped graphene has been reported by taking s-triazine molecule as a precursor [31]. The formation of porous graphitic carbon nitride and carbon nitride nanotube has been also reported by Cao et al. through heating cyanuric chloride with Na in presence of $NiCl_2$ catalyst [32]. Recently, the porphyrin-like porous $C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene has been introduced as truncated N-doped $C_{60}$ fullerene. The $C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene has six N4 cavities with eight s-triazine rings connected via C-C bonds [33]. This fullerene has gained interest of researchers lately. The geometry structure, electronic structures, absorption spectrum and nonlinear optical properties of pristine and alkali metal atoms doped $C_{24}N_{24}$ were theoretically studied using DFT calculations [34]. Ghosh et al.introduced metal decorated porphyrin-like porous $C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene as a catalyst for oxygen reduction reaction through the DFT computations [35]. The electro-optical properties of porphyrin-like porous $C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene decorated with $Li_3O$ have been theoretically investigated [36].

Now in this study, we are interested in exploring how the doping of silicon (Si) atom over N4 cavity of the $C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene (Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$) will potentially be an efficient catalyst for the NO

reduction in presence of CO molecule using comprehensive DFT calculations. The silicon atom is chosen due to its abundance on the Earth and its metal free nature. The most stable adsorption configuration of NO and CO molecules over Si@C₂₄N₂₄are investigated in detail. In addition, the energy profile and activation energies are obtained for the different mechanisms of NO reduction. It hopes that the findings of the present study open new insights for designing innovative active catalyst for NO reduction.

## 2. Computational details

All of the optimizations were performed using the DFT method within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in form of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) correction [37], as implemented in DMol³ [38, 39]. The DFT+D method through the Grimme's scheme [40] was applied in the all calculations in order to consider the van der Waals forces. A double-numerical basis set with the polarization function (DNP) was chosen as the basis set with a real-space cutoff radius of 4.6 Å. Vibrational frequencies were analyzed at the same level to illustrate the nature of optimized stationary points. A Fermi smearing parameter of 0.005 Ha was used in the calculations. The convergence tolerance for energy change, max force, and max displacement were $1{\times}10^{-5}$ Ha, $0.001$ Ha $\text{\AA}^{-1}$, and $0.005$ Å, respectively. A periodic supercell with a $25\times25\times25$ Å cubic box was used for the calculations. The $\Gamma$ point was considered for the summation in the Brillouin zone. The minimum-energy pathway (MEP) for each reaction step was obtained by linear synchronous transit (LST)/quadratic synchronous transit (QST) and nudged elastic band (NEB) methods. The adsorption energy ($E_{ads}$) of each adsorbate was obtained as follows:

$$
E_{ads}=E_{adsorbate-fullerene}-E_{adsorbate}-E_{fullerene} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{adsorbate-fullerene}$, $E_{adsorbate}$ and $E_{fullerene}$ terms denote the total energies of the adsorbate-fullerene complex, adsorbate and fullerene, respectively. The atomic charges and charge-transfer values were obtained using the Hirshfeld charge density analysis [41].

## 3. Results and discussion

The geometry and electronic structure of catalyst play a key role in subsequent catalytic reactions and the corresponding activation energies. Therefore, the geometry and electronic structure of Si@C₂₄N₂₄ are first taken into account. The relaxed geometry of the Si@C₂₄N₂₄ fullerene is depicted in Figure S1 in supplementary materials. The pristine C₂₄N₂₄ consists of total six N4 cavities which are formed from twenty four nitrogen atoms. There are eight equivalent s-triazine rings connected with C-C bonds. The silicon atom can be coordinated into N4 cavity to produce Si@C₂₄N₂₄ fullerene. As a result, four equivalent Si–N bonds with the lengths of 1.86 Å are formed in Si@C₂₄N₂₄. It is found that the Si atom strongly chemisorbs at the N4 cavity with the adsorption energy of about -158 kcal/mol. This adsorption energy is larger than the cohesive energy of bulk Si (-107 kcal/mol per atom [42]), indicating that the Si adatom cannot diffuse into C₂₄N₂₄ fullerene; therefore, the clustering problem is avoided. The Hirshfeld atomic charge on the Si and neighboring N atoms of Si@C₂₄N₂₄ are also calculated in order to evaluate the surface reactivity. The Hirshfeld atomic charge on the Si atom is 0.27 |e|, while those of neighboring N atoms are calculated to be -0.10 |e|. This great charge separation obviously exhibits partial ionic nature of Si-N bonds in Si@C₂₄N₂₄. The electron density map of Si@C₂₄N₂₄ presented in Figure S1 in supplementary materials also confirms this finding, where there is an electron density loss region between the Si and its neighboring nitrogen atoms.

In order to find out the possible mechanism of NO reduction by CO, the NO and CO adsorption behaviors over $Si@C_{24}N_{24}$ are investigated. The obtained energetically stable structures are shown in Figure S2 in supplementary materials. The corresponding adsorption energy and charge-transfer values are summarized in Table 1.

The adsorption of NO molecule through its N and O atoms is also considered which are depicted as **a** and **b** complexes, respectively, in Figure S2 in supplementary materials. The Si-N and Si-O bonds are formed in **a** and **b** complexes with the distances of 1.77 and 1.80 Å, respectively. The N-O bond is elongated from 1.16 Å in the isolated NO molecule to 1.23 and 1.27 Å in the **a** and **b** complexes, respectively. The corresponding $E_{\text{ads}}$ values for **a** and **b** complexes are -36.13 and -7.52 kcal/mol. These values are more negative than the corresponding values of NO adsorption over Si-doped graphene (-18.4 and -4.4 kcal/mol) [5]. The larger reactivity of the doped Si atom over $C_{24}N_{24}$ toward NO adsorption is more than the graphene sheet. Based on PDOS plots in Figure S2 in supplementary materials, the NO interaction with this fullerene leads to quite large charge-transfer from the fullerene to the half-filled $2\pi^{*}$ orbital of NO molecule. The Hirshfeld charge density analysis shows the quite large charge-transfer of

0.14 and 0.12 e from the fullerene into NO molecule for **a** and **b** complexes, respectively.

Furthermore, the results reveal that although the CO molecule remarkably adsorbs over the Si atom of Si@C₂₄N₂₄through its carbon atom with adsorption energy of -15.57 kcal/mol, the adsorption through its oxygen atom is not thermodynamically favorable with Eₐₛd= -0.70 kcal/mol. The Si-C bond with the bond lengths of 1.91 Å is formed in complex **c**. The CO adsorption in this complex accompanied by a net charge-transfer of 0.14 e from CO molecule to Si@C₂₄N₂₄.According to the PDOS plots in Fig. S2 in supplementary materials, it is also concluded that the adsorption of CO molecule leads to disappear of $5\sigma$ peak of CO molecule in complex **c**. Thus the charge transfer from CO molecule to fullerene is verified.Consequently, the NO molecule adsorbs more efficently than CO molecule over Si@C₂₄N₂₄ fullerene.

Moreover, the co-adsorption structures of the (NO)₂ dimer and NO/CO over Si@C₂₄N₂₄ are also investigated. It is worth mentioning that that (NO)₂ dimer has been frequently reported over noble metal-based catalysts during NO reduction [43-45]. For this purpose, the second NO molecule is added to the **a** or **b** complexes. As a result, three distinguishable stable structures for the co-adsorption of two NO molecules are achieved. These structures are demonstrated in Figure S3 in supplementary materials as **D1, D2** and **D3**. As indicated in Figure S4 in supplementary materials, the energy barrier for the formation of these coadsorbed structures is low, which demonstrates the coadsorption of NO molecules over Si@C₂₄N₂₄ can be easily achieved at normal condition. In each coadsorbed structure, NO molecules are connected to each other through a N-N bond. The obtained N-N bond lengths are 1.24, 1.39 and 1.78 Å for **D1, D2** and **D3** structures, respectively. These values are smaller than N-N bond length for (NO)₂ dimer in gas phase with length of 1.97 Å [46]. The calculated adsorption energy for the **D1, D2** and **D3** structures is -93.53, -57.53 and -55.87 kcal/mol, respectively. These adsorption energies are

greater (more negative) than the adsorption of NO monomer over $Si@C_{24}N_{24}$. Hence, it is verified that that the addition of the second NO molecule strengthens the interaction between the NO and Si atom. It seems that the co-adsorption of NO molecules leads to further stabilization of the NO-$2\pi^*$ states owing to the large electron density transfer. Also the **D1** configuration which the co-adsorption of two NO molecules happens through their oxygen atoms onto the Si atom is the most thermodynamically favorable configuration.

The **D4** panel in Figure S3 in supplementary materials indicates the co-adsorption of NO and CO molecules over $Si@C_{24}N_{24}$. It is obvious that NO molecule is attached to the Si atom, whereas CO molecule is floated over the surface with a great interaction distance of $3.17$ Å. The adsorption energy of this complex is -33.31 kcal/mol which is smaller than the $(NO)_2$ dimer co-adsorption. Therefore the coadsorption of two NO molecules is energetically more favorable than that of NO and CO over $Si@C_{24}N_{24}$. That is to say, in the presence of NO and CO as the reactants gases, the Si atom will be dominantly covered by the $(NO)_2$ species.

Herein the NO reduction mechanism by CO molecule is taken into account. There are two distinct mechanisms for the NO reduction over the catalyst surfaces which are known as "direct dissociation" and "dimer" mechanisms [47-50]. In the direct dissociation mechanism, NO molecule is first dissociated into N and O atoms, both adsorbed over the catalyst; whereas in the dimer mechanism two NO molecules are attached to each other to form $(NO)_2$ dimer, followed by its decomposition into a $N_2O$ molecule and an activated oxygen ($O_{ads}$). It is noteworthy that the dimer mechanism prefers energetically over the direct dissociation because of the large required activation energy for the NO dissociation process (Figure S5 in supplementary materials). Hence the discussion in the subsequent section is limited to only the NO reduction over $Si@C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene through the dimer mechanism.

The possible reaction pathways together with the relaxed structures at the corresponding stationary points are scrutinized for the NO reduction through the dimer mechanism. Since all the **D1, D2** and **D3** structures have great adsorption energies, the NO reduction starts with these co-adsorbed configurations. The obtained reaction pathways with the co-adsorbed configuration **D1** is shown in Figure 1. It is obvious that the (NO)₂ dimer is dissociated into N₂O molecule and Oₐds species by overcoming a small energy barrier of 10.58 kcal/mol.By considering transition state (**TS-1**), the interaction distance between the dissociated O and Si atoms is 1.63 Å, which is about 0.11 Å shorter than that of in configuration **D1**. The N-N bond is also shortened about 0.07 Å in **TS-1**, with respect to that of **D1** complex. In **FS-1**,N₂O molecule is produced and the Oₐdsremainsover the Si atom with a binding distance of 1.54 Å. The calculated reaction energy for the formation of N₂O and Oₐds is -17.95 kcal/mol,implying the larger stability of the products with respect to(NO)₂ dimer.

The reaction pathway of NO reduction by starting from **D3** co-adsorption complex is also presented in Figure 3. The formation of $\ce{N_{2}O}$ and $\ce{O_{ads}}$ species needs a small energy barrier of 13.36 kcal/mol to be overcome. The Si-N interaction distance between the dissociated N atom and Si atoms is increased from 1.79 in **D3** co-adsorption complex to 2.11 Å in **TS-4**. The N-N bond is also shorten to 1.43 Å in this transition state in comparison to that of in **D3** which is 1.78 Å. The Si-O bond with the length of 1.82 Å is also formed in **TS-4**. Finally, the $\ce{O_{ads}}$ is left over the Si atom with $\ce{Si-O_{ads}}$ distance of 1.54 Å and the $\ce{N_{2}O}$ molecule is produced and in **FS-4**. The reaction energy for the $\ce{N_{2}O}$ and $\ce{O_{ads}}$ formations is calculated to be -54.16 kcal/mol. It should be mentioned that the adsorption energy for the $\ce{N_{2}O}$ molecule over the $\ce{Si@C_{24}N_{24}}$ through either its nitrogen or oxygen atom is calculated to be -5.6 kcal/mol. This small negative value demonstrates that the produced $\ce{N_{2}O}$ molecule during the NO reduction is weakly physisorbedover the surface. As a result, $\ce{N_{2}O}$ molecule can simply desorb from the surface of the catalyst at room temperature.

Finally, the remaining $\mathrm{O_{ads}}$ atom can interact with an incoming CO molecule to form a $\mathrm{CO_2}$ molecule. The potential energy diagrams for this reaction as well as the corresponding optimized stationary points are presented in Figure 4a. It is obvious from this figure that this reaction initiates by the physisorption of CO molecule over $\mathrm{O_{ads}}$ with the $\mathrm{O_{ads}...CO}$ binding distance of 2.88 Å. Then, CO molecule approaches the $\mathrm{O_{ads}}$ and the **TS-5** is achieved. The Si-$\mathrm{O_{ads}}$ distance is elongated to 1.65 Å, while $\mathrm{C-O_{ads}}$ distance is 1.72 Å. An activation energy of 8.53 kcal/mol is needed to reach the intermediate **IM2**. Thereafter, the $\mathrm{CO_2}$ molecule is produced by overcoming small activation energy of 6.68 kcal/mol. It can be simply released from the surface owing to its small adsorption energy (-6.22 kcal/mol).

It is noticeable that the side reaction $\mathrm{NO+O_{ads}}$ might also occur due to high affinity of NO molecule to interact with the $\mathrm{O_{ads}}$ moiety. This reaction pathway is shown in Figure 4b. As evident, the activation energy for this reaction (33.80 kcal/mol) is extremely large with respect to $\mathrm{CO+O_{ads}}$ reaction. This indicates that, the elimination of $\mathrm{O_{ads}}$ by CO molecule is more easier than by NO and the toxic $\mathrm{NO_2}$ molecule cannot formed during the NO reduction process. Similar results have been also reported for other catalysts like Si-BNNS [24].

## Conclusion

Using the dispersion-corrected DFT calculations, the reduction of NO molecules over Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$ porous fullerene was investigated in the presence of CO molecule. The adsorption behaviors of NO and CO molecules over the Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$were discussed in terms of adsorption energy and charge-transfer values. The results indicated that NO molecule can be reduced to $N_2O$ over the fullerene through the dimer mechanism. In this reaction an activated O atom ($O_{ads}$) is also produced. The results indicated that the eliminating of $O_{ads}$ by a CO molecule to form $CO_2$ molecule is more energetically favorable than that of by a NO molecule. Thus cleaning the fullerene surface from $O_{ads}$ via CO molecule is preferable. To sum up, the small reaction barriers together with small desorption energies for $N_2O$ and $CO_2$ molecules demonstrate that the porphyrin-like porous Si@$C_{24}N_{24}$ fullerene can be viewed as a potential metal-free catalyst for low-temperature reduction of harmful NO molecule.
