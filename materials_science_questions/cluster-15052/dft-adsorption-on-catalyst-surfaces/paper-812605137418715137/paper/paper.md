# Insight into the combined catalytic removal properties of Pd modification $V/TiO_{2}$ catalysts for the nitrogen oxides and benzene by: An experiment and DFT study

Guobo Li$^{a}$, Ling Wang$^{a}$, Peng Wu$^{a}$, Shule Zhang$^{b}$, Kai Shen$^{a}$, Yaping Zhang$^{a*}$

(a. Key Laboratory of Energy Thermal Conversion and Control of Ministry of Education, School of Energy and Environment, Southeast University, Nanjing, 210096, Jiangsu, P. R. China
b. School of Chemical Engineering, Nanjing University of Science and Technology, Nanjing 210094, P. R. China)

*Corresponding author: Yaping Zhang, Email: amflora@seu.edu.cn, Telephone number: 86-025-83790667

**Abstract:** The effect of Pd on the combined catalytic removal properties of $V_{y}/TiO_{2}$ for benzene and $NO_{x}$ has been investigated. It was found that the benzene conversion, $NO_{x}$ conversion and $CO_{2}$ selectivity of $V_{y}/TiO_{2}$ catalysts improved significantly after the modified by Pd at a space velocity (SV) of $12\ 000\ mL\cdot g^{-1}\cdot h^{-1}$, especially the $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst. According to the characterization and calculation results, additional reaction active sites on anatase-$TiO_{2}$ surfaces were provided after the addition of 1.2%Pd and facile redox cycle $(2V^{4+}+Pd^{2+}\leftrightarrow2V^{5+}+Pd^{0})$ can be explained for the excellent benzene and $NO_{x}$ catalytic performance of $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst. The possible catalytic removal transformation mechanism of benzene and $NO_{x}$ by $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst was proposed, respectively. Additionally, in the combined catalytic removal process of $NO_{x}$ and benzene, $NO_{2}$ and NO may become the O-materials for benzene oxidation process over $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst, and $NO_{x}$ is reduced to $N_{2}$.

**Keywords:** Benzene, $NO_{x}$, Combined removal, $Pd_{x}V_{y}/TiO_{2}$, DFT, Redox cycle

## Introduction

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and nitrogen oxides $(NO_{x})$ emitted from an combustion process and transportation activities that contribute to photochemical smog and are toxic, since some of them may cause cancer and other diseases. [1, 2]. Catalytic

devices are an effective technique for the complete oxidation of VOCs and/or the reduction of $NO_x$. Additionally, numerous catalysts have been explored for VOCs oxidation [3, 4] and $NO_x$ reduction [5], including noble metals [6-8] and transition-metal oxides [9-11] in the past few decades.

$VO_x$-supported catalysts are the most studied catalytic systems for redox reactions, contains contain "SCR" and "oxidation". [12] As we all known, $NH_3$-SCR is currently recognized as the most effective $NO_x$ removal method. Additionally, $V_2O_5$-WO$_3$(MoO$_3$)/TiO$_2$ are the commercial SCR catalysts, in which $V_2O_5$ is the active sites with uniform monolayer dispersion. [13] As the previous studies [14, 15] reported that $NH_3$ are adsorbed on the vanadium-based catalysts firstly, then the activated $NH_3$ reacts with gas-phase (Eley-Rideal (E-R) mechanism) or weakly adsorbed NO (Langmuir-Hinshelwood (L-H) mechanism). The SCR system with $V_2O_5$-WO$_3$/TiO$_2$ catalysts is originally designed for the removing of $NO_x$ also effective in the decomposition of dioxin (over 95%). [16, 17] In addition, as some research reported that it is possible to achieve the VOCs oxidation and $NO_x$ reduction simultaneously on the catalyst surface. [18, 19] Chi et al. [20] reported that the SCR system, serves as a better control technology for removing the dioxin, destroys the dioxin completely. Which avoid transfers gas-phase dioxin to the reacted ash and make ash disposal even more complicated. Dvořák et al. [21] expanded $V_2O_5$-WO$_3$/TiO$_2$ catalyst on polytetrafluoroethylene (ePTFE) to r to achieve simultaneous removal of $NO_x$, dioxins and PM, they found that PM and dioxins could be completely removed and the $NO_x$ conversion was 32.6%. Their activity can be attributed to the high dispersion of $VO_x$ on the surface of catalyst support, as well as the inherent acid sites and redox properties of it.

Noble-metal-based catalysts, [22] especially Pd and Pt, present show excellent catalytic activities for both VOCs and $NO_x$, while the noble metal catalysts are first to be studied as SCR deNO$_x$ catalysts. Additionally, supported noble metal is more active than the transition metal oxide because of lower temperature to achieve a higher conversion. Zuo et al. [23] prepared the Nd modified Pt/MCM-4 catalysts, which were

used to removal benzene (1000 ppm), and 100% conversion of benzene at 230 °C was achieved with a SV = 20 000 h⁻¹. As reported by Hosseini et al. [24], the PdAu/TiO₂ catalysts shows the significantly enhanced catalytic activities for the oxidation of toluene and propene, comparing with the supported either Pd-based or Au-based catalysts. Liang et al. [25] found that the supported TiO₂/PdW bimetallic catalysts showed excellent water resistance ability, which are researched for the highly catalytic removal properties of benzene. Because of that consumed lattice oxygen (Oₗₐₜₜ) in active PdOₓ sites by benzene can be effectively replenished by H₂O gas molecules. However, in recent years, there are few reports on noble metal modified VOₓ/TiO₂ series catalysts for the removing of VOCs and NOₓ simultaneously.

Herein, different mass ratios of Pd/V composite active component oxide catalysts were prepared by impregnation method, and the selected carrier is anatase-TiO₂. Additionally, benzene is one of the most common and toxic not halogenated compounds. [12] Therefore, benzene was the selected typical VOC gas for the investigation on synergistic adsorption and removal of VOCs and NOₓ on the PdₓVᵧ/TiO₂ catalysts. Moreover, combined catalytic removal properties of PdₓVᵧ/TiO₂ catalysts for the NOₓ and benzene were investigated by experiments and DFT calculation.

## Experimental and computational details

The catalysts are prepared by an equal volume impregnation method, the preparing details are shown as Supporting Information (SI). The prepared catalysts with different mass ratios (%) of Pd and V to TiO₂ are called as PdₓVᵧ/TiO₂ catalysts.

The techniques, such as X-ray diffraction (XRD), in situ diffuse reflectance fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (in situ DRIFT), NH₃/O₂-Temperature programmed desorption (NH₃/O₂-TPD) and H₂-Temperature programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) were used to characterize the catalysts. ## Results and Discussion
### Catalytic performance

Benzene, one of typical VOCs, which is used as an industrial solvent widely. [25]

We evaluated the VOCs activities oxidation of the prepared catalysts.

The (a) $\text{NO}_x$ conversion and (b) benzene conversion over the four $\text{V}_y/\text{TiO}_2$ catalysts is presented in Fig.S1. In the range of 100-250 °C, the $\text{NO}_x$ conversion of $\text{V}_y/\text{TiO}_2$ catalysts increase to nearly 100% from about 20%, and the $\text{V}_y/\text{TiO}_2$ catalysts exhibit excellent catalytic activity, especially the $\text{V}_4/\text{TiO}_2$ catalyst in the subsequent test temperature range. The $\text{V}_y/\text{TiO}_2$ catalysts also shows good benzene conversion, and the activity increased with the increasing V content. Additionally, the benzene conversion of $\text{V}_y/\text{TiO}_2$ catalyst increase as the temperature is further increased, within an abrupt increasing from 250 to 300 °C. According to the results, the $\text{V}_4/\text{TiO}_2$ catalyst is selected for the consecutive studies.

In contrast with $V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst, the benzene conversion and $CO_2$ selectivity of $Pd_xV_4/TiO_2$ catalysts improved significantly after the modified by Pd, especially the $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst (Fig.1). Additionally, Figure S2 compares the benzene of the $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst with other catalysts [26, 27]. Although the experimental parameters of the simulated flue gases are different, it is clearly that the $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst was highly effective in benzene oxidation.

![](./images/812605137418715137_3.jpg)

Fig.2 (a) Benzene conversion, (b) $NO_x$ concentration and (c) $CO_2$ selectivity as a function of reaction temperature over $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst at SV = 12 000 mL g⁻¹·h⁻¹.

As shown in Fig.2, the synergistic removal performance of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst for NO and benzene under different conditions is further investigated. When benzene and NO are simultaneously introduced into the reactor and the concentration of $O_2$ is 10%, benzene conversion has dropped slightly in the temperature rouge of 100-250 °C. It can be attributed to the NO may occupy a part of the adsorption site on the $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst surface, and the activated benzene oxidative conversion is slower at lower temperatures. In addition, the hindrance effect increases as the NO concentration

increases. In contrast with the reaction condition of 100 ppm benzene + 500 ppm NO
+ 10% O₂, the benzene conversion in low temperature and CO₂ selectivity of
Pd₀.₁₂V₄/TiO₂ catalyst improves significantly, when the concentration of O₂ is raised to
20%. In sharp contrast, as 100 ppm NH₃ is further introduced based on the reaction
condition of 100 ppm benzene + 500 ppm NO + 10% O₂, benzene conversion and CO₂
selectivity decrease, while NOₓ is well removed. This may be due to the introduction
of NH₃, which further occupies the active site on the Pd₀.₁₂V₄/TiO₂ catalyst surface. The
most important is that, when the reaction temperature increase to 350 °C, benzene and
NOₓ almost all can be removed, and the CO₂ selectivity is close to 100%. The removal
of benzene and NO simultaneously on Pd₀.₁₂V₄/TiO₂ catalyst requires a relatively
higher temperature.

The surface species and chemical states information of catalysts are obtained by XPS, and the measured results are presented in Fig.5.

Figure 5(a) shows the Ti 2p XPS spectra of $V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ and $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalysts. The observed double diffraction peaks in the spectra of the two catalysts, Ti $2p_{1/2}$ and $2p_{3/2}$, with binding energy (BE) = 458.53 eV and 464.33 eV, corresponding to $Ti^{4+}$ ions. [28]
Obviously, there are no diffraction peaks of $Ti^{3+}$ ions, and the Ti 2p XPS spectra of the $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst is slightly lower than that of $V_{4}/TiO_{2}$. The O 1s XPS spectra and its sub-peak fitting treatment results are shown in Fig.5(b). The oxygen species at BE

= 530.51 and 531.22 eV can attribute to the lattice oxygen (Oₗₐₜₜ) and surface adsorbed oxygen (Oₐdₛ, e.g. O²⁻, O₂²⁻, O⁻ or OH⁻) species, respectively.[29, 30] Furthermore, as shown in Table 1, the ratio of Oₐdₛ/Oₗₐₜₜ on Pd₀.₁₂V₄/TiO₂ (53.70%) is slightly greater than that on V₄/TiO₂ (46.76%). The V 2p XPS spectra is separated into two diffraction peaks shown in Fig.5(c), and the two peaks of catalysts at BE = 515.79 eV and 516.94 eV are ascribed to V⁴⁺and V⁵⁺, respectively. [31] The analysis results of V 2p XPS spectra and demonstrate a similar amount and V⁵⁺/V⁴⁺ ratio of V₄/TiO₂ and Pd₀.₁₂V₄/TiO₂ catalysts. As shown in Fig.5(d), the Pd 3d₅/₂ XPS spectra of Pd₀.₁₂V₄/TiO₂ catalyst is also decomposed into two diffraction peaks, and the BE = 334.8 and 336.5 eV, which ascribable to Pd⁰ and Pd²⁺ species, respectively. [25, 32] The XPS characterization results of these two catalysts show that the less addition of Pd slightly promotes the metal chemical state and oxygen species on the catalyst surface. However, the catalytic activity of the catalyst is greatly improved, which may be attributed to the increasing of adsorption sites after the introduction of 0.12%Pd.

Oxygen Mobility

![](./images/812605137418715137_7.jpg)

Fig.6 O₂-TPD profiles of PdₓV₄/TiO₂ catalysts

The adsorption properties of O₂ on the surface of PdₓV₄/TiO₂ catalysts are

investigated by TPD, and the experiment results are displayed in Fig.6. About two desorption peaks of $O_2$ can be observed, at around 270 °C and 340 °C. The obtained peak at 100-300 °C is ascribed to the desorption of surface oxygen species such as $O^{2-}$ and $O^-$, and another peak over 300 °C is attributed to the desorption of lattice oxygen from $PdO_x$ and $VO_x$ clusters.[33] As can be seen, the addition of 0.12%Pd increase the oxygen species greatly, especially the desorption amount of surface oxygen species. It is worth mentioning that the desorption peak is consistent with the catalyst activity range, and better catalytic activity of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst for benzene and $NO_x$ can attribute to more surface oxygen species and the enhancement of oxygen mobility.

Low-temperature reducibility

![](./images/812605137418715137_8.jpg)

Fig.7 $H_2$-TPR profiles of $Pd_xV_4/TiO_2$ catalysts

The reduction properties of $Pd_xV_4/TiO_2$ catalysts are investigated by $H_2$-TPR analysis, and the results are shown in Fig.7. Two $H_2$ consumption peaks around 398 °C (peak I) and 459.4 °C (peak II) are found on the $V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst, which can be attributed to the reduction of $V^{5+}$ to $V^{4+}$ and $Ti^{4+}$ to $Ti^{3+}$, respectively. [34] Compared with $V_4/TiO_2$ catalysts, two more lower temperature reduction peaks (peak III and IV) are obtained for the $Pd_xV_4/TiO_2$ catalysts, in which the peak III can be attributed to the reduction of $Pd^{2+}$ to $Pd^0$, [35] while peak IV is attributed to the reduction of $PdO_x$ located at the interface between another active component and the support, (which would have a strong interaction with $VO_x$ and anatase-$TiO_2$). [36] Thus, the shift of peaks indicate a

stronger interaction between the two active components (PdO$_x$ and VO$_x$) and anatase- TiO$_2$.

Acidity

![](./images/812605137418715137_9.jpg)

Fig.8 NH$_3$-TPD profiles of Pd$_x$V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalysts

Acidic properties of catalysts, which favor the adsorption of NH$_3$, the surface of Pd$_x$V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalysts is characterized by the amount and strength of acid sites, tested by NH$_3$-TPD, and the obtained profiles of Pd$_x$V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalysts are shown in Fig.8. It can be found that the NH$_3$ desorption peaks of the Pd$_x$V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalysts are all detected in the ranges 100-450 °C. The NH$_3$-TPD profiles of all Pd$_x$V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalysts display the similar tendency, suggesting the presence of adsorbed NH$_3$ species with different strength of acid sites. [37] Comparing with V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalyst, the acid sites on the surface of Pd$_x$V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalyst are significantly enhanced with the addition of Pd. Additionally, Pd$_{0.12}$V$_4$/TiO$_2$ catalyst shows the largest NH$_3$ desorption area. Most importantly, NH$_3$-TPD results are consistent with the catalyst activities and temperature range.

Anatase-TiO$_2$ (101) is a corrugated structure characterized by the presence of fully coordinated, 6-fold coordinated Ti atoms (Ti$_{6c}$) and 3-fold coordinated O atoms (O$_{3c}$), coordinated unsaturated atoms, 5-fold coordinated Ti atom (Ti$_{5c}$) and the 2-fold coordinated bridged oxygen O atoms (O$_{2c}$) are shown in Fig.S5. The two active

components ($PdO_x$ and $VO_x$) are supported on anatase-$TiO_2$ (101) surface, and three views of optimized catalyst structure are displayed in Fig.S6. There are two main adsorption sites on the catalyst structure surface (Pd and V). All the adsorption reaction of gases is carried out at the top of the two adsorption sites, in which the distance of adsorption gas molecules to Pd or V atom is about 3.0 Å.

The adsorption energy of different gas molecules on $PdV/TiO_2$ (101) surface is calculated by DFT, and the adsorption configuration of different gases on the catalyst surface is shown in the Fig.9. Furthermore, the projected density of states (PDOS) of selected atoms in the lowest energy configurations after adsorption are calculated in order to gain a deeper insight into the interaction between gas molecules and the $PdV/TiO_2$ (101) surface. A Bader charge analysis is also performed to investigate the charge transfer between gas molecules and $PdV/TiO_2$ (101) surface before and after adsorption by the Bader module, and electron valence changes in various gas molecules are shown in Table S2. In addition, charge density difference for adsorption of gas molecules on the $PdV/TiO_2$ (101) surface show the electronic transfer clearly (Fig.S7).

The adsorption of NH₃, benzene and O₂ on the PdV/TiO₂ (101) surface mainly on the two sites of V and Pd atom, and the corresponding adsorption energy of configuration is shown in Table 2.

NH₃ molecule, the important SCR reaction gas molecule, which is adsorbed on the potential acidic sites of optimized model. As shown in Fig.9(a-b), the nitrogen-end of NH₃ is adsorbed on V and Pd, and the adsorption energy is -6.070 and -6.785 eV, respectively. Depending on the analysis results of PDOS, the adsorption of NH₃ on the two sites of V and Pd is chemisorption, with the formation of N-V and N-Pd bonds. The 2p orbital of N corresponding hybrid with the 3d orbital of V and the 4d orbital of Pd upon adsorption of NH₃. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of NH₃ hybridizes with the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of the V and Pd, respectively. [38] In addition, the formed N-V bond length is 2.090 Å, N-Pd bond length is 2.109 Å. As can be seen from two adsorption configurations of NH₃, NH₃ shows good reducibility, the charges all transfer from the NH₃ molecule to the PdV/TiO₂ (001) structure, indicating that the NH₃ molecule is activated by adsorption on V and Pd. As shown in Fig. 10c, benzene can only be adsorbed on the Pd site, but not the V site. The calculation results of PDOS are consistent with this. The 2p orbital of C4 in benzene molecule hybrid with the 4d orbital of Pd, and Q_C4 increase accompanying the formation of C4-Pd bonds (2.180 Å), suggest that the stability of the benzene structure is weakened after adsorption. While electrons in PdV/TiO₂ (001) structure and the benzene molecular move between their internal atoms in the adsorption of benzene on V site. O₂ molecule can also be adsorbed at the V and Pd sites in PdV/TiO₂ (001) surface, and the adsorption modes of O₂ on PdV/TiO₂ (001) surface

are shown in Fig.9(e-f). After adsorption, lengths of the new formed O-V bond and O-
Pd bond are calculated as 1.867 Å and 1.890 Å, and the exhibited adsorption energy is
-5.848 eV and -7.286 eV, which inferred to be chemical interaction. It can be found
from the calculation results of PDOS that the 2p orbital of the O atom and the 3d orbit
of V (4d orbit of Pd) have obvious hybridization after adsorption. $O_2$ exhibits strong
oxidation properties, the valence electrons of each atom in $O_2$ increased. Additionally,
the adsorbed oxygen species can participate in the "oxidation" of benzene and SCR
"deNO$_x$" reaction.

For the SCR NO$_x$ removal process, it will be influenced by the absorption of NO$_x$,
whether NO or $NO_2$, on the PdV/TiO$_2$ (101) catalyst surface. Therefore, the adsorption
of NO and $NO_2$ on PdV/TiO$_2$ (101) surface is researched individually, and the results
are shown in Fig.9(g-l). NO is the most important reactant, both N and O ends of it may
be adsorbed at the V and Pd sites. The N-end can be adsorbed on V and Pd sites, while
the O-end can only adsorb on the Pd site. When the N-end of NO is adsorbed at the V
and Pd sites, the adsorption energy is -5.375 eV and -8.447 eV, respectively. The
nitrogen-end adsorption of NO on Ti, and the N-V bond length is 1.908 Å, and the
original 1.170 Å N-O bonds are lengthened to 1.199 Å. The density peaks for the 2p
orbital of N and the density peaks for the V's 3d orbital (and Pd's 4d orbital) of
PdV/TiO$_2$ (101) are in resonance, indicating a strong chemical reaction of NO gas
molecules on this surface, and a stable chemical bond is formed between the N atom
and the surface V (and Pd) atom, and the formed N-V (N-Pd) bond is 1.846 Å (1.732

The above adsorption results show that gas molecules ($NH_{3}$, $NO$, $O_{2}$, Benzene, $NO$ and $NO_{2}$) can all be adsorbed and activated on the $PdV/TiO_{2}$ (101) catalyst surface, with larger adsorption energy. In configurations after adsorption (except for non-adsorption configurations), the HOMO of gas molecule hybridizes with the LUMO of the V and Pd cations of the $PdV/TiO_{2}$ (101) surface, gas molecules are combined with the catalyst structure surface by chemical adsorption. Close electronic interaction between gas molecules and $PdV/TiO_{2}$ (101) surface is beneficial to the removal of $NO_{x}$ and benzene in the reaction process.

In situ DRIFTS analysis

In order to obtain more evidence of benzene and $NO_x$ removing steps, in situ DRIFTS experiments are carried out to determine the intermediate species during benzene and $NO_x$ removing process on the surface of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst. As shown Fig.10(a), in the benzene oxidation reaction proceeding, the band at $1268.9\ cm^{-1}$ is due to the C-H deformation vibrations, [41] the band at $1581.3\ cm^{-1}$ is due to attributable to the C=C stretching vibrations of the phenolate species, [42] band at $1627.6\ cm^{-1}$ are due to attributed to the vibration of the aromatic ring adsorbed parallel to the surface and the band at $1681.6\ cm^{-1}$ is assignable to the C=O stretching vibrations of the quinone or other ketone species. [26] In situ DRIFT spectra of $500\ ppm\ NH_3 + 500\ ppm\ NO$+$10\%$ $O_2$ adsorption of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst are recorded at $250\ ^\circ C$. As shown in Fig.10(b), the peak at $1425.1\ cm^{-1}$ ascribed to the asymmetric and symmetric bending vibrations of $NH_4^+$ species on Brønsted acid sites, [28] and the peaks at 3170.4, 3245.6 and $3386.4\ cm^{-1}$ can be ascribed to the N-H stretching vibration modes of the coordinated $NH_3$, [34, 43] while a small peak at $1545\ cm^{-1}$ can be related to monodentate nitrate. [44] As illustrated in Fig.10(c), the in situ DRIFT spectra of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst in a flow of $100\ ppm$ benzene $+500\ ppm\ NH_3 + 500\ ppm\ NO + 10\%\ O_2$ for $60\ min$ at $250\ ^\circ C$ are also recorded. Compared with Fig.10(a-b), the adsorbed species on the catalyst surface did not change significantly. These results indicate that the reaction gas molecules can all be better adsorbed on the $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst surface, and there present the common adsorption sites of benzene, $NH_3$ and NO on the catalyst surface in the combined catalytic removal process of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst for the $NO_x$ and benzene.

Reaction pathway

Electronic state of Pd in the supported catalyst plays an important role in the catalytic removal process of $NO_x$ and benzene. Bounechada et al. [45] found that the

enhancement in oxidation of $CH_4$ can be attributed to the coexistence of a $Pd^{2+}$and $Pd^0$ (rather than the fully oxidized $Pd^{2+}$). In this work, the dominant Pd species in $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst is $Pd^{2+}$, and the $Pd^0/Pd^{2+}$ of it is 0.58. Based on the results of $H_2$-TPR and $O_2$-TPD, the downshift in reduction temperature and $O_{latt}$ desorption after doping of $PdO_x$ clusters can be ascribed to the facile redox cycle and enhanced $O_{latt}$ mobility. Which may be the reason for high catalytic activity of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst.

![](./images/812605137418715137_12.jpg)

Fig.11 Proposed mechanism of benzene oxidation and $NO_x$ reduction on the surface of $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst.

In general, the oxidation reaction of VOCs on the surface of supported transition metal catalysts follows the Mars-van Krevelen (M-K) mechanism, in which lattice oxygen is involved in the oxidation process, and then the reduced transition-metal oxide is reoxidized by $O_2$ gas molecules. Additionally, participation of $O_{latt}$ in benzene oxidation process can be verified by the characterization results (e.g., $H_2$-TPR and $O_2$-TPD). According to the above results, the possible oxidation mechanism of benzene over $Pd_{0.12}V_4/TiO_2$ catalyst surface oxidation mechanism is shown as Fig.11(a). First, benzene is adsorbed on catalyst surface, the adsorbed benzene is oxidized by the active lattice oxygen species on catalyst surface and the phenolate species are produced. Second, the benzoquinone (e.g., o-benzoquinone and p-benzoquinone) species are

easily transformed from the further oxidized phenolate species. Third, the carboxylate (e.g., acetate and maleate) species are generated via the breaking of benzene rings which are attacked by the active oxygen species. Finally, the carboxylate species are oxidized into $CO_{2}$ and $H_{2}O$, and $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst recovered.

According to the mechanism proposed by Topsøe [34, 46], the Eley-Rideal (E-R) mechanism for $NO_{x}$ catalytic cycle over $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst surface may also consist of four steps. First, $NH_{3}$ adsorbed on the Brønsted acid sites (V-OH) and $NH_{4}^{+}$ formed. Second, $NH_{4}^{+}$ species are oxidized to $NH_{3}^{+}$ by $V^{5+}=O$, and $V^{5+}=O$ is restored to $V^{4+}=OH$. Third, $NH_{3}^{+}$ species react with gas phase NO to form $NH_{3}^{+}-NO$ ($NH_{3}^{+}-NO$), and then the deposited $NH_{3}^{+}-NO$ decomposes to $N_{2}$ and $H_{2}O$. Finally, $V^{4+}-OH$ on is re-oxidized to $V^{5+}-O$ by $O_{2}$. While for the Langmuir-Hinshelwood (L-H) mechanism, first, the $NH_{3}$ adsorbs on the Brønsted acid sites (V-OH) and the formed $NH_{4}^{+}$. Second, $NO_{x}$ adsorbs on the Lewis acid site on the catalyst surface and various complex nitrates (e.g. nitro, nitroso and bridge nitrate and so on) are formed. Third, formed $NH_{4}^{+}$ species react with nitrate species to form $NH_{4}NO_{x}$. Finally, the deposited $NH_{4}^{+}-NO_{x}$ decomposes to $N_{2}$ and $H_{2}O$, and the catalyst recovery.

Additionally, for the combined catalytic removal process of $NO_{x}$ and benzene over $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalysts, $NO_{2}$ and NO may become O-materials for benzene oxidation process, and $NO_{x}$ is reduced to $N_{2}$. Based on the characterization results of XPS and $H_{2}$-TPR, the existence of $Pd/Pd^{0}$ contribute to the redox process of $V^{4+}/V^{5+}$. Thus, the redox cycle ($2V^{4+}+Pd^{2+}\leftrightarrow2V^{5+}+Pd^{0}$) exists over $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst, and the possible combined catalytic removal transformation mechanism over $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalysts for the $NO_{x}$ and benzene is shown in Fig.S8. The $Pd_{x}V_{y}/TiO_{2}$ catalysts are deemed to be potential candidate for the simultaneous removal of VOCs and $NO_{x}$.

## Conclusion

Supported $Pd_{x}V_{y}/TiO_{2}$ catalysts showed high catalytic activities for benzene and $NO_{x}$, especially the $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst. The $Pd_{0.12}V_{4}/TiO_{2}$ catalyst performs the excellent benzene and $NO_{x}$ conversion: the conversions are over 90% at 350 °C (SV = 20 000 mL $g^{-1}\cdot h^{-1}$, reaction condition = 100 ppm benzene+500 ppm NO+500 ppm $NH_{3}$+10%

$\mathrm{O}_{2}$). Based on the BET, XPS, $\mathrm{H}_{2}$-TPR characterization and DFT calculation results, additional reaction active sites on anatase-$\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ surfaces were provided after the addition of $1.2\%$Pd, the facile redox cycle $(2\mathrm{V}^{4+}+\mathrm{Pd}^{2+}\leftrightarrow2\mathrm{V}^{5+}+\mathrm{Pd}^{0})$ can be explained for the excellent benzene and $\mathrm{NO}_{x}$ catalytic performance of $\mathrm{Pd}_{0.12}\mathrm{V}_{4}/\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ catalyst, as well as the active mixed $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{ads}}/\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{latt}}$ couple also play an important role in the catalytic process. The DFT calculation results show that gas molecules ($\mathrm{NH}_{3}$, $\mathrm{NO}$, $\mathrm{O}_{2}$, Benzene, $\mathrm{NO}$ and $\mathrm{NO}_{2}$) can all be adsorbed and activated on the $\mathrm{PdV/TiO_{2}}$ (101) catalyst surface, with larger adsorption energies (chemical adsorption), and many organic intermediates were observed by in situ DRIFT experiments. Accordingly, the possible catalytic removal transformation mechanism of benzene and $\mathrm{NO}_{x}$ by $\mathrm{Pd}_{0.12}\mathrm{V}_{4}/\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ catalyst was proposed, respectively. Additionally, $\mathrm{NO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{NO}$ may become the O-materials for benzene oxidation process over $\mathrm{Pd}_{0.12}\mathrm{V}_{4}/\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ catalyst, and $\mathrm{NO}_{x}$ is reduced to $\mathrm{N}_{2}$. Combined catalytic removal process of $\mathrm{NO}_{x}$ and benzene. The $\mathrm{Pd}_{x}\mathrm{V}_{y}/\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ catalysts are deemed to be potential candidate for the simultaneous removal of VOCs and $\mathrm{NO}_{x}$.
