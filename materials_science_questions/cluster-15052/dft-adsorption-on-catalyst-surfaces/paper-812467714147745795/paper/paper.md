# Unveiling the Nature of Room-Temperature $O_2$ Activation and $O_2^{\bullet-}$ Enrichment on MgO-Loaded Porous Carbons with Efficient $H_2S$ Oxidation

Cite This: ACS Catal. 2021, 11, 5974−5983

## ABSTRACT:
The activation of $O_2$ into the dioxide radical ($O_2^{\bullet-}$) at room temperature serves as a critical chemical process implicated in environmental remediation and industrial chemical processes, but the involved mechanism remains unclear. Herein, we address this challenge by the radical detection method combined with DFT study and unveil that the $O_2$ activation reaction could take place merely in porous carbons (PCs) at room temperature. DFT calculations indicate that $sp^2$-conjugated carbons can transfer a $\pi^*$ electron to the outer orbit of the $O_2$ molecule derived by the space confinement in ultramicropores ($\sim$0.4 nm), and the presence of edge site defects and heterogeneous atoms can facilitate the electron transfer due to increased quantity of free electrons. The produced $O_2^{\bullet-}$ radicals could be further trapped and enriched on the surface of MgO as predicted by DFT calculations, which is subsequently confirmed by radical detection analysis of the designed MgO-loaded PCs. Furthermore, the $O_2$ activation and $O_2^{\bullet-}$ enrichment on MgO-loaded PCs are applied in a probe reaction of room-temperature catalytic $H_2S$ oxidation, in which an outstanding catalytic performance is achieved. These findings provide valuable insight into the intrinsic origin of the $O_2$ activation at room temperature and open a fresh route to efficient formation and concentration of $O_2^{\bullet-}$ for aerobic oxidization processes.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
The activation of an oxygen molecule ($O_2$) has long fascinated researchers in many fields because of its fundamental and technological significance, but the involved mechanism has yet to be well interpreted.$^{1−3}$ One typical reaction should be dioxide radical ($O_2^{\bullet-}$) generation from the $O_2$ molecule by simply combining an exterior electron.$^{4−6}$ This reactive free radical possesses a much higher redox potential than its parent compound and can achieve rapid reaction processes in chemical synthesis and environmental restoration.$^{7−9}$ Generally, to trigger the conversion, extra driving motivations including electrochemical and photochemical stimulations are required to overcome the activation energy barrier.$^{9,10}$ Another strategy is the catalytic conversion that initially involved the usage of noble, transition, or rare-earth metals, where the electron in metal-based materials can transfer from the metal to the $O_2$ orbital to generate a new electron configuration of $O_2$.$^{11,12}$ Besides, the ability of $O_2$ activation has recently emerged in metal-free materials, such as $C_3N_4$, COFs, and porous carbons (PCs). For instance, the active electrons from the LUMO band of the g-$C_3N_4$ could promote the reduction of the $O_2$ molecule to generate a superoxide but at a relatively high temperature.$^{13}$ Interestingly, the activation of the $O_2$ molecule merely by carbon-based materials can take place at room temperature, whereas the associated reaction mechanism is still puzzling. Current understanding is limited to the rich surface chemistry and unique structural, and why only carbon materials can and how to activate $O_2$ molecule are still under discussing.$^{14−16}$ Thus, uncovering the reaction mechanism of $O_2$ activation by carbon-based materials is of crucial importance to deeply understand the nature and further benefit scientific applications.

Despite the high reactivity with other substances such as CO, $H_2S$, and organics, activated $O_2$ molecules usually suffer from poor stability and a short lifetime, which often cause a low catalytic performance.$^{17}$ Therefore, a method to strengthen the stability of the reactive oxygen species is highly desirable in the present systems. Importantly, the physical and chemical trapping methods are usually adopted to keep the dioxide radicals in a more stable state. $^{20}$ Accordingly, the metal oxides including MgO, $TiO_2$, and ZnO are supposed to possess high electron affinity and strong bonding with dioxygen radicals. $^{21−23}$ Also, the evidence shows that the improved stability of $O_2^{•-}$ via the interaction with the metal oxide surface leads to a longer life-time and higher concentration in the studied reaction system. However, the formation of $O_2^{•-}$ is not favored on these metal oxides at room temperature without extra stimulation, and the efficient usage of them for $O_2^{•-}$ capture and concentration remains a huge challenge.

Here, we, for the first time, systematically studied the oxygen molecule activation reactivity of the metal-free catalysts including carbon-based and noncarbon-based materials via the electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) characterization at room temperature. Also, the density function theory (DFT) calculation was adopted to gain a fresh and deep insight into the reaction mechanism of molecular $O_2$ activation, unveiling that only carbons possessing ultramicropores of $\sim$0.4 nm are the necessary and sufficient condition. Furthermore, the experimental results combined with theoretical simulation disclose the trapping property of MgO nanoparticles toward the $O_2^{•-}$, resulting in the enrichment of radicals in the system. Moreover, by selecting $H_2S$ catalytic oxidation at room temperature as a probe reaction, the fateful influence of dioxide activation and enrichment in the catalytic reaction is revealed.

The noncarbon-based materials tested in this work include g-$C_3N_4$, ZIF-8, ZSM-5, $Al_2O_3$, $SiO_2$, and MgO, which are often used in the field of catalysis and some of them possess micropores. As shown in Figure 1a, no signal attributable to an unpaired electron state can be observed in the EPR spectra of these noncarbon-based materials, suggesting their inert activation abilities at an ambient condition. Conversely, when the catalysts are replaced by $sp^2$-hybridized carbon-based materials including as-synthesized PC, commercially available coconut-based activated carbon (AC-coconut), coal-based activated carbon (AC-coal), and reduced graphene oxide, the resulting EPR spectra (Figure 1b) display a discernible signal of $g = 2.003$ due to the formation of $O_2^{•-}$.$^{16,25}$ This result clearly reveals that the carbons regardless of their structural characteristics and surface chemistries can fulfill the $O_2$ activation at room temperature.

The proposed mechanism of room-temperature oxygen activation based on the carbon ultramicropore confinement is presented in Figure 1c. It involves strong adsorption of $O_2$ in the ultramicropores and transfer of electrons from the carbon matrix to form the $O_2^{•-}$ radical. The micropore is an inherent characteristic for carbon materials, where at least two graphene walls arrange extremely close, forming a slit-shaped channel. In the case of ultramicropores, typically of a size less than twice the diameter of the adsorbate molecule (<0.7 nm), the enhancement of adsorption by the overlapping of interactive potential of both sides of the pore walls takes place, thus promoting the $O\overset{=\!\!=}{=}O$ bond distortion. The other key ingredient required to form the $O_2^{•-}$ radical is a supply of electrons. Fortunately, the carbons with conductive $sp^2$-conjugated graphene walls have plenty of $\pi^*$ electrons. The presence of edge sites, topological defects, and doped heteroatoms (such as O, N atoms) in the graphene lattice could further enhance the electron-donating ability of the carbons to the adsorbed $O_2$. On the other hand, the noncarbon-based porous materials have either ultramicropores or good electron mobility, thus exhibiting inert activation ability for room-temperature oxygen activation.

To deeply understand the activation mechanism of $O_2$ molecules on the carbons at room temperature, DFT calculation is employed. In order to elucidate the essential roles of carbon in the processes of $O_2$ activation, we classify the materials into three categories: carbons, carbon-containing, and carbon-free materials. The largest charge-transfer quantity of 0.684 |e| (Figure 1d,g) is determined at the pore size of 0.4 nm, and the charge density difference reveals that the electron could transfer from Mg to the adsorbed $O_2$. When the pore diameter enlarges, the value of charge transfer decreases dramatically and cannot stimulate $O_2$ activation with $D \geq 0.5$ nm, indicating that the confined space induced by the ultramicropore is one essential factor driving $O_2$ activation. For the $C_3N_4$, the charge transfer between $O_2$ and its silt pore is not favored for the formation of $O_2^{\bullet-}$. The maximum charge transfer is only 0.354 |e| (Figure 1e,h) at the pore diameter of 0.4 nm, far less than the quantity (0.5 |e|) required for $O_2^{\bullet-}$ generation. This should be ascribed to the poor electron donor ability of the $C_3N_4$ crystal in spite of good electron mobility within the matrix as indicated by the electron density difference analysis (Figure 1e). Moreover, both MgO and $C_3N_4$ do not possess ultramicropores as we mentioned above and thus exhibit inert performance of $O_2$ activation, which is well confirmed by our EPR investigations.

Then, PCs with a pore diameter ranging from 0.4 to 0.8 nm are investigated (Figure 1f,i). As we expected, the oxygen molecule can successfully attain enough electrons (0.458 |e|) from the carbon matrix at the pore diameter of 0.4 nm to form $O_2^{\bullet-}$. However, the quantity of Bader charge transfer decreases sharply with the pore diameter increasing and $O_2^{\bullet-}$ also cannot be formed at $D \geq 0.5$ nm, indicating the necessity of ultramicropores for $O_2$ activation. Charge density difference analysis suggests that electrons could move from $\pi^*$ of $sp^2$-carbon to the outer electron orbit of $O_2$ at $D \leq 0.4$ nm (Figure S2a), while large ultamicropores impede electron flow between carbon and $O_2$ molecules. Therefore, we conclude that the confined space supplied by ultramicropores together with the electron transfer of conductive carbons are required criteria for a given material to trigger the process of $O_2$ activation to $O_2^{\bullet-}$.

### 2.2. Exploration and Simulation of $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ Radical Enrichment.
Although the carbon-based catalysts exhibit high reactivity for room-temperature O₂ activation, their application in the field of O₂-based catalysis remains a huge challenge due to the low utilization rate of activated dioxygen radicals. To deal with it, we propose that combing carbons with substances of good $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ affinity could improve the concentration of active radicals in the target system.²¹,²⁹ We therefore screen binding energies of $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ on surfaces of various common materials (e.g., PC, CNT, graphene, SiO₂, and Al₂O₃) to determine a good captor (Figures 3a and S4). The results reveal that MgO holds the largest interaction toward $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$. The binding energy of $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ on the MgO surface (−9.99 eV) is almost 9 times higher than that of $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ on a carbon surface (−1.03 eV).

Furthermore, in the proposed complex PC/MgO system, DFT calculations indicate that O₂ molecules tend to be adsorbed on the carbon surface and more prefer ultramicropores (0.4 nm < $D$ ≤ 0.7 nm) because of high adsorption energy (Figure 3b). Meanwhile, the generated $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ is desorbed, which is derived by strong repulsion from ultramicropores and then moves to the MgO surface due to the large binding energy. Therefore, in the complex PC/MgO system, a total process of O₂ activation could involve four steps as illustrated in Figure 3c, namely, O₂ adsorption, O₂ activation to $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$, $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ desorption, and enrichment on MgO. The inlet O₂ molecules are initially adsorbed because of high adsorption energy. O₂ in micropores further diffuse to narrower ultramicropores (∼0.4 nm) at the expense of kinetic energy and transfers to $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ by obtaining electrons from carbons. The generated $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ is then excluded out of pores due to large repulsion. Finally, these fresh $O_{2}^{\bullet -}$ molecules are immediately captured and accumulated on the MgO surface for the subsequent reactions.

According to the aforementioned analysis, the MgO-loaded PC (PC/MgO) catalysts are synthesized by a facile carbon-ization and etching method (Scheme S1) for radical detection. By controlling the etching degree, PC with different MgO contents can be obtained. As shown in Figure 4a, the MgO nanoparticles exhibit highly uniform distribution throughout the carbon matrix. Furthermore, the high-resolution TEM image (Figure 4b) confirms the existence of the MgO crystal with the size of about 5 nm. After removing all MgO nanoparticles, only the carbon matrix with numerous mesopores with the size of about 5 nm could be observed in the high-resolution TEM image (Figure 4c). N₂ adsorption−

desorption isotherm procedures are further conducted to evaluate the pore texture of PC/MgO (Figure 4d) and the resulted porosity parameters are listed in Table S1. With the etching degree increasing, the total pore volume of PC/MgO gradually increases from 0.12 to 1.86 $cm^3$/g, and the BET specific surface area shows a corresponding increase from 91 to 1227 $m^2$/g. The pore size distributions (Figure 4e) show hierarchical pores with measured sizes of 1.2 and 4.8 nm. The micropores of 1.2 nm are produced during the carbonization process, and the mesopores of 4.8 nm are generated after the removing of MgO nanoparticles embedded in the carbon matrix. Furthermore, the $CO_2$ adsorption−desorption result (Figure 4f) indicates the existence of ultramicropores with the pore size of 0.30−0.45 nm in the carbon matrix. In addition, the basic sites show a detectable increase with the increase of MgO content in the PC/MgO composites (Figure S5).

To study the effects of the carbon matrix and MgO on $O_2$ activation, the EPR technology is used to detect the formed unpaired-electron radicals in the various systems. As shown in Figure 4g, the EPR spectrum of PC/MgO-10% measured in an $N_2$ atmosphere shows no signals in the testing range, indicating no radicals are formed in the materials. After adding $O_2$ into this system, the characteristic peak at $g = 2.003$ is detected, which is ascribed to the formation of $O_2^{•-}$ radicals. Furthermore, the pure MgO nanoparticle and PC are also analyzed by the EPR method in an air flow. The result demonstrates that the carbon matrix rather than MgO is the factor for the formation of radicals. It should be noted that the EPR intensity shows a significant enhancement with an exponential relationship of MgO content (Figure 4h,i). This phenomenon should be attributed to the increased concentration of $O_2^{•-}$ radicals trapped by the MgO nanoparticles, in good agreement with the DFT calculations.

### 2.3. Application of $O_2$ Activation and $O_2^{•-}$ Enrichment in Catalyzing $H_2S$ Oxidation.
$H_2S$ is one of the most harmful air pollutants, mainly arising from the chemical industries, such as coal gasification, petroleum refining, and natural gas processing. $^{30,31}$ Among the available strategies to deal with $H_2S$ emission issues, catalytic oxidation of $H_2S$ at room temperature using alkaline carbon catalysts is considered as one of the most cost-effective methods due to moderate operation conditions and high removal efficiency. $^{32-34}$ Using the $H_2S$ catalytic oxidation as a probe reaction, we study the effects of $O_2$ activation and enrichment on the catalytic behavior.

First, the MgO-loaded catalysts including PC/MgO-10%, $Al_2O_3$/MgO-10%, and $SiO_2$/MgO-10% are prepared as room-temperature catalysts for $H_2S$ removal. These catalysts all have good pore structure as shown in Figure S6 and Table S2. Also, the catalytic activity of the catalysts is evaluated in the presence of 1000 ppm $H_2S$, 1% $O_2$, and 80% of relative humidity at 30 °C. As displayed in Figure S7, the PC/MgO-10% exhibits long-term catalytic activity, holding the concentration of $H_2S$ in the outlet gas lower than 0.1 ppm. However, $Al_2O_3$/MgO-10% and $SiO_2$/MgO-10% present negligible reactivity with $H_2S$ under the same condition, which should be attributed to their inert ability for $O_2$ activation as shown in Figure 1a.

Furthermore, PC/MgO with different MgO contents are used as catalysts to explore the effect of activated radical enrichment on $H_2S$ removal. As presented in Figure 5a, the breakthrough time of the PC/MgO catalysts shows a trend of first an increase and then a decrease with the decrease of the MgO content in the catalysts. The process of $H_2S$ catalytic oxidation at room temperature is a batch reaction during which the sulfur products can deposit on the catalysts. $^{35,36}$ PC/MgO-64% shows negligible breakthrough time mainly due to the poor pore structure (Figure 4d), which causes deficient space for reaction and product deposition. To eliminate the effect of pore structure, the pore volumetric capacity (calculated by eq S2) is used to evaluate the catalytic performance. As shown in Figure 5b (red line), for the PC/MgO catalysts with high MgO content ($\geq 10$ wt %), the pore volumetric capacity is maintained similarly at ca. 2.40 g $H_2S/cm^3$, which is the highest capacity reported recently (Table S3). Then, the volumetric sulfur capacity shows a significant decline with the decrease of the MgO content below 10 wt %, mainly ascribed to the decrease of the radical concentration as shown in Figure 4h. As shown in Figure S8, PC/MgO-10% can promote the formation of activated oxygen radicals, which indicates that the presence of humidity does not hinder the step of $O_2$ activation. However, PC/MgO-10% shows no catalytic activity in the absence of humidity. This is because it is difficult for the $H_2S$ molecule to dissociate in a dry environment, which hinders the subsequent oxidation reaction.

It is noted that MgO could serve as a promotor for $H_2S$ adsorption and dissociation. $^{37}$ To further confirm the positive effect of radical enrichment, different alkali-modified PC catalysts including PC/$Na_2CO_3$-10% and PC/NaOH-10% are used to remove $H_2S$. As shown in Figure 5c, the EPR intensities of these alkali-modified PCs are very similar to that of pure PC, indicating no radical enrichment by these alkali salts. Therefore, the relatively poor catalytic performance of these alkali-modified PCs should be caused by the low concentration of activated radicals in the reaction system (Figure 5d). These results also suggest that the enrichment of activated radicals by MgO promotes $H_2S$ catalytic oxidation.

To gain insight into the catalytic behavior of the PC/MgO catalysts, the exhausted catalysts are systematically analyzed. The diminished pore structure is detected by nitrogen adsorption−desorption (Figure S9), demonstrating that the pores are blocked or fully filled by the deposited sulfur. The EPR analysis of the exhausted catalyst shows no radical signal (Figure S10), also suggesting that the pore blockage is one of the major reasons for deactivation. The presence of the S element in the exhausted catalyst is confirmed by the energy-dispersive X-ray analysis as shown in Figure S11, and the SEM elemental mapping images of the exhausted catalyst (Figure S12) also reveal that the sulfur species show a uniform distribution. In addition, all the thermogravimetry curves of the exhausted catalysts in an $N_2$ atmosphere (Figure S13) show a weight loss in the range of 150−500 °C, fitting to the desorption of elemental sulfur from PCs. $^{38,39}$ The presence of S crystals is confirmed by X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) analysis (Figure S14), which indicates that the elemental sulfur is the main product after the desulfurization reaction. $^{40}$ In addition, the diffraction peaks ascribed to $MgSO_4$ crystals appear in the XRD pattern, which could be due to the neutralization reaction between MgO and $SO_3$ that resulted from excessive oxidation of $H_2S$. $^{41}$ X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis also confirms the formation of elemental S in the exhausted catalyst as shown in Figure S15. The detailed XPS spectra at S 2p reveal that the sulfur products consist of abundant elemental sulfur and a trace of sulfate. $^{42,43}$ Also, the high-resolution O 1s and Mg 2p spectra indicate that some of the MgO is converted to $MgSO_4$, $^{44,45}$ in good agreement with the XRD result.

Overall, an underlying mechanism proposed for $\ce{H_{2}S}$ catalytic oxidation on the PC/MgO catalysts is displayed in Figure 5e. First, the reactants including $\ce{O_{2}}$ and $\ce{H_{2}S}$ molecules are adsorbed in the catalysts with the help of the abundant micropores. The $\ce{O_{2}}$ molecules are then activated into activated $\ce{O_{2}^{•-}}$ radicals on the carbon matrix through electron transfer within space confinement. Subsequently, the formed $\ce{O_{2}^{•-}}$ radicals are trapped by the uniformly dispersed MgO nanoparticles via a strong binding force to produce a radical-concentrated environment. Thus, the adsorbed $\ce{H_{2}S}$ molecules and dissociated $\ce{HS^{-}}$ ions could be oxidized into elemental sulfur by activated radicals, and the products are further deposited in the nanopores of the catalysts. Ultimately, the deactivation of the catalysts is mainly caused by the blockage of nanopores as well as the consumption of MgO.

## 3. CONCLUSIONS
In conclusion, we study the mechanism of $\ce{O_{2}}$ activation at room temperature in PCs. Based on the EPR results combined with DFT simulation, we reveal that the carbon-based materials facilitate the electron transfer to adsorbed $\ce{O_{2}}$ to form the $\ce{O_{2}^{•-}}$ radical with the space confinement in ultramicropores ($\sim$0.4 nm). In addition, the MgO nanoparticle can anchor the generated $\ce{O_{2}^{•-}}$ via high binding energy to improve the concentration of reactive radicals. The efficient formation of a stable $\ce{O_{2}^{•-}}$ radical is successfully applied to the catalytic oxidation of $\ce{H_{2}S}$ into elemental sulfur under ambient conditions. We believe this work provides a valuable insight into the origin of $\ce{O_{2}}$ activation at room temperature and opens a route to efficient $\ce{O_{2}^{•-}}$ capture and concentration for aerobic oxidation processes.
