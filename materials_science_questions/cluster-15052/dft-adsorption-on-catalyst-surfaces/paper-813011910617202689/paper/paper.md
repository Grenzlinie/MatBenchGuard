## I. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES

### 1.1 Computational Details

By the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP), density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using a planewave basis set with energy cutoff of 500 eV.¹ The ion-electron interaction is described by projector augmented wave (PAW)² potentials, and the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) parameterized by Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (PBE) for the exchange-correlation functional.³ Grimme's semiempirical DFT-D3 scheme of dispersion correction was adopted to describe the van der Waals interactions between the Fe particles and substrates (or N₂ molecules).⁴ To avoid interactions between replica images, a vacuum space of 12 Å was added to the vertical direction. The supercell consists of 8×8 graphene unit cells to incorporate Fe particle, F atoms distribute in the two sides of graphene for fluoride graphene. The model structures were optimized for ionic degrees of freedom, with energy and force convergence criteria of $10^{-4}$ eV and 0.02 eV/Å, respectively.

To evaluate the stability of Fe particles anchored on graphene substrates, the binding energy of Fe particles on substrates is calculated by

$$E_{\text{b}} = E_{\text{Fe-sub}} - E_{\text{sub}} - E_{\text{Fe}} \tag{1}$$

where $E_{\text{Fe}}$ and $E_{\text{Fe-sub}}$ are the energies of Fe particles in vacuum and on graphene substrates, respectively; $E_{\text{sub}}$ is the energy of graphene substrates. The adsorption energies of N₂ molecules are defined as

$$\Delta E_{\text{N2}} = E_{\text{total}} - E_{\text{cata}} - E_{\text{N2}} \tag{2}$$

where $E_{\text{total}}$ and $E_{\text{cata}}$ are the total energies of catalysts with and without adsorption of N₂ molecule; $E_{\text{N2}}$ is the energy of the free N₂ molecule in vacuum. The free energy diagram of eNRR


process was obtained based on the computational hydrogen electrode (CHE) model.⁵ For each step of eNRR, the Gibbs free energy of formation is given by

$$
\Delta G=\Delta E_{\mathrm{DFT}}+\Delta \mathrm{ZPE}-T \Delta S \tag{3}
$$

where $\Delta E_{\mathrm{DFT}}$, $\Delta \mathrm{ZPE}$ and $\Delta S$ are the differences in DFT total energy, zero-point energy and entropy of the two states before and after reaction, respectively; temperature $T$ is set to 298 K in our study. The $\Delta \mathrm{ZPE}$ and $\Delta S$ values of gaseous molecules were obtained from the NIST-JANAF thermodynamics table.⁶ The catalytic activity was characterized by the limiting potential $U_{\mathrm{L}}$, defined as

$$
U_{\mathrm{L}}=-\Delta G_{\text {max }} / e \tag{4}
$$

where $\Delta G_{\text {max }}$ is the maximum Gibbs free energy of formation of the six reaction steps for eNRR.

### 1.2 Sample preparation
Graphite Fluoride and $\mathrm{FeCl}_{3}$ were purchased from Shanghai Furui Company, China, and Aladdin Company, China, respectively, and were used as received. $\mathrm{FeF}_{3} @(\mathrm{CF})_{\mathrm{n}}$ was prepared via a molten salt process, in which graphite fluoride and $\mathrm{FeCl}_{3}$ were sealed in a stainless reactor with a weight ratio of 1:10 under argon atmosphere. Then, the stainless reactor was heated to $300{^\circ}C$ at $2{^\circ}C\ \mathrm{min}^{-1}$ and maintained for 48 h. When the reaction completed, the excess $\mathrm{FeCl}_{3}$ was depleted by filtration with ethanol, and was vacuum dried at $60{^\circ}C$ for 24 h.

F-Fe@F-G was prepared via a potassium vapor reaction process. In a typical synthesis of F-Fe@F-G, 50 mg $\mathrm{FeF}_{3} @(\mathrm{CF})_{\mathrm{n}}$ was parked in a quartz tube (10 mm diameter, 1 mm thickness, length 200 mm). 200 mg potassium metal (Aladdin Company, China) was pressed into a small quartz tube (6 mm diameter, 1 mm thickness, length 15 mm) in an argon-filled box, then, the small quartz tube was transferred into the large quartz tube carefully and quickly. The combined quartz tubes were evacuated for 30 min by a mechanical pump and sealed. And then the sealed quartz tube heated to $300{^\circ}C$ at $2{^\circ}C\ \mathrm{min}^{-1}$ and maintained for 96 h. The excess potassium metal was removed via a careful dropping with methanol. Finally, the as-prepared product was rinsed by filtration with ethanol and vacuum dried at $60{^\circ}C$ for 24 h. F-Fe@F-G was obtained.

### 1.3 Electrodes preparation

First, 5 wt.% Nafion solution, isopropanol, and deionized water (at a volume ratio of 16:100:384) were mixed to form a dispersion solution. Then the prepared catalyst (4 mg) were ultrasonically dispersed in 1 ml of the as-prepared dispersion solution to form a homogeneous catalyst ink. Afterward, 20 μL of the catalyst ink was transferred onto a glassy carbon electrode (diameter 6 mm) and dried at 60 °C for 0.5 hours to prepare the electrode for test.

### 1.4 Materials Characterizations

The XRD pattern of the materials were confirmed on an MMA diffractometer with Cu K$\alpha$ radiation ($\lambda$=1.5418 Å) (GBC, Australia). The morphology of the binary atomic catalysts was collected from a JSM-7500FA FESEM (JEOL, Japan) and a 2010 TEM (JEOL, Japan). The surface chemical states were determined using a Phoibos 100 Analyzer XPS with Al K$\alpha$X-rays (SPECS, Germany). STEM images were acquired on a probe corrected JEOL ARM200F operated at 200 kV and equipped with a cold field emission gun, a high-resolution pole piece, and a Centurio energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) detector. Images, EELS, and EDS maps were acquired at a probe current of 90 pA. The intensity of the electrolyte after chromogenic reaction is detected with Ultraviolet-visible (UV-1800) spectrophotometer (Shimadzu Corporation, Japan). The Fe K-edge XAFS measurements were carried out at the beamline 14W1 in Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF). The X-ray was monochromatized by a double-crystal Si (111) monochromator both for SSRF.

### 1.5 ECS measurements

For the ECS measurement, cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves at different scan rates from 40 to 200 mV s-1 in the range of 0-100 mV vs RHE. The capacitive currents (Ja-Jc)/2 at 50 mV vs. RHE as a function of scan rate were measured in order to extract double-layer capacitance (Cdl) of the electrodes:

$$i_c = vCdl \tag{5}$$

Where ic (A) represents the capacitive current, v (V s⁻¹) is the scan rate.[4] Meanwhile, Cdl serves as an estimate of the electrochemical surface of the solid-liquid interface as the relative electrochemical surface is proportional to Cdl. The electrochemical surface values were calculated based on the following equation:

$$
\mathrm{ECS}=\frac{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{dl}}}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{a}}} \tag{6}
$$

Where Cs=0.03 mF cm⁻² in 1 M KOH solution according to the literature.

### 1.6 Electrochemical NRR measurements
The electrochemical experiments were conducted on a Princeton potentiostat system with a three-electrode configuration, with the working electrode of obtained samples, counter electrode of a Pt foil, and reference electrode of Ag/AgCl/KCl.

### 1.7 Determination of ammonia
The quantity of ammonia produced was measured through a colorimetric method with Nessler's reagent.⁷ All test solutions were incubated under dark conditions at ambient condition for 20 min before UV-vis tests. The absorbance at 420 nm for each solution was measured with a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, UV-3600). The reference solutions with suitable $\ce{NH4Cl}$ concentration were synthesized to plot a calibration curve (Supplementary **Fig. S8**). The concentrations of ammonia in the electrolytes were obtained with this as-obtained calibration curve.

### 1.8 Determination of hydrazine
The hydrazine produced in the electrolyte was estimated via the method of Watt and Chrisp. A mixture of para-(dimethylamino) benzaldehyde (0.599 g), HCl (12 mol L⁻¹, 3 mL), and ethanol (30 mL) was used as a color reagent. 2 mL of the electrolyte after electrolysis was mixed with 2 mL of the color reagent. The absorbance of the resulting solution was measured at 455 nm. The concentration-absorbance curve was calibrated using a standard hydrazine hydrate solution with a series of concentrations.
