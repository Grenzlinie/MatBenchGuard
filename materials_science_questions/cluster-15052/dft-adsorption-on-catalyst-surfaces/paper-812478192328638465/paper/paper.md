## ABSTRACT

The excitons are known to exist in graphitic carbon nitride (g-$C_3N_4$), but the contribution of excitons to the photocatalytic process has received only sporadic attention. Targeted regulation of excitons dissociation into free charges is an effective measure to improve the utilization of carriers and enhance photocatalytic activity. Here we designed the ultra-thin g-$C_3N_4$ nanosheets modified with rich N vacancies (Nv-rich-CN) through molecular self-assembly and molecular intercalation strategies. N vacancies can effectively dissociate excitons into free charges. The electron concentration of the Nv-rich-CN is 3.24 times that of bulk g-$C_3N_4$ (CN). Benefiting from the enhanced carrier utilization, Nv-rich-CN showed superior activity for $CO_2$ photoreduction under visible light irradiation. Femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy revealed a photophysical model of exciton dissociation. Theoretical calculation results explained that the essential reason for the vacancy to promote exciton dissociation may be that the lack of local order provided a driving force for exciton dissociation. Besides, N vacancies acted as active sites in the process of $CO_2$ reduction, promoting the adsorption and activation of $CO_2$ by the photocatalyst. *In situ* diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform spectroscopy revealed the change of intermediate products during the reduction of $CO_2$. This work focuses on the contribution of excitons in the photocatalytic process and provides a novel idea for enhancing the carrier utilization of photocatalysts.

## 1. Introduction

Excessive $CO_2$ emissions and energy shortages are threatening the global ecological balance and become major problems that plague mankind. Photocatalytic reduction of $CO_2$ to hydrocarbon fuel is an ideal way to solve this problem [1–8]. Among many two-dimensional semiconductor photocatalysts, graphitic carbon nitride (g-$C_3N_4$) stands out due to its metal-free, environmentally friendly, and moderate bandgap [9–12], which is expected to achieve large-scale industrial applications. Usually, when the thickness of the two-dimensional material is reduced to the atomic layer, the carrier migration distance will be shortened, which is expected to improve the photocatalytic activity [13]. However, the high recombination rate and poor utilization efficiency of carriers limit the photocatalytic activity of g-$C_3N_4$.

To reduce the recombination of carriers in g-$C_3N_4$ and improve the utilization of electrons, various strategies have been proposed, including preparation of crystalline g-$C_3N_4$ [14,15], exciton regulation [16,17], design of protonated g-$C_3N_4$ [18]and construction of heterojunctions [19–23]. Exciton regulation is different from other modification methods, focusing on the contribution of exciton to photocatalysis, so, regulating the dissociation of excitons into free carriers is a promising method [24]. Exciton is a special photo-generated electron-hole pair and bound together by Coulomb interaction; it can be generated by the photoexcitation process [25]. Therefore, the exciton is a non-negligible species in studying the optical excitation of semiconductors [26,27]. In recent years, excitons have become the focus of research in the application of photoelectric devices (organic solar cells [28], organic light-emitting diodes [29], and other fields), but the contribution of excitons in the semiconductor photocatalysis process is rarely investigated. Regulating the dissociation of excitons into free carriers will effectively reduce carrier recombination, greatly increasing the number of hot electrons, thus enhancing the photocatalytic activity. Excitons tend to dissociate into free carriers in regions with large energy confusion [30,31]. Wang et al. [32]. reported that the order-disorder interface constructed in semi-crystalline heptazine-based melon promoted the dissociation of excitons into free carriers, and the electron density of semi-crystalline heptazine-based melon increased nearly 7 times compared with that of the pristine sample. Zhang et al. [33]. reported that the perovskite edge defect state promoted the dissociation of excitons into long-lived charge carriers, thereby improving performance. Therefore, constructing defects to create energy disorder regions is an important step in exciton regulation. The generation of vacancies in a two-dimensional semiconductor will change the electronic structure and cause local electronic distortions. Therefore, the disorder around the vacancies is relatively large, which will provide the driving force for exciton dissociation [34]. However, the targeted regulation of exciton dissociation by controlling the concentration of vacancies has not been studied. The photophysical model and essential reasons for vacancy-mediated exciton dissociation are rarely studied. Besides, vacancy modification is an effective method to improve the photocatalytic activity of g-C₃N₄. Because the surrounding vacancies are accompanied by unsaturated coordination; they often serve as active sites in the photocatalytic reaction process, which can promote the adsorption and activation of the reactants [35,36]. However, the in-depth mechanism of vacancies in g-C₃N₄ on enhancing photocatalytic activity for CO₂ reduction is still unclear. The formation of intermediate products in the reaction pathway of CO₂ reduction is rarely studied.

Here, ultra-thin g-C₃N₄ nanosheets with rich N vacancies (Nv-rich-CN) were prepared through molecular self-assembly and molecular intercalation strategies. N vacancies promoted the dissociation of excitons into free charges, and the electron concentration in Nv-rich-CN increased by 3.24 times with respect to that of pristine g-C₃N₄ (CN). Benefiting from the improved carrier utilization, Nv-rich-CN showed the enhanced activity for CO₂ photoreduction, giving the CO evolution rate of 6.61 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ far exceeding that of CN (2.89 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹). The photophysical model of exciton dissociation was revealed by Femtosecond transient absorption (Fs-TA) spectroscopy. The density functional theory (DFT) calculation provided a possible reason for the dissociation of excitons at the vacancy: The local disorder of the N vacancy promoted the dissociation of excitons into free charges. In addition, N vacancies acted as active sites in the process of CO₂ reduction, promoting the adsorption and activation of CO₂ by the photocatalyst. *In situ* diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform (DRIFT) spectra studied the influence of N vacancies on the formation of intermediate products in the reaction path.

## 2. Experimental part

### 2.1. Materials

The chemical reagents involved in the experiment were of analytical grade, and the water used was all ultrapure water. Melamine (C₃H₆N₆, 99 %) and glycerol (C₃H₈O₃, 99 %) were purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Phosphorous acid (H₃PO₃), ethanol (C₂H₆O, 99.7 %) and anhydrous sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄) were produced by Chengdu Kelon Chemical Reagent Factory. 3,3',5,5'-Tetramethylbenzidine (TMB, ≥99 %) was produced by Shanghai Maclean Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd.

### 2.2. Sample preparation

#### 2.2.1. Molecular self-assembly synthesis of precursors

According to the synthesis method in the reference [37], 14.63 mmol phosphorous acid and 7.93 mmol melamine were dispersed in ultrapure water (100 mL) and stirred in a water bath at 80 °C for 1 h. After hydrothermal treatment (180 °C, 10 h), the suspension was centrifuged and dried at 50 °C.

#### 2.2.2. Synthesis of Nv-rich-CN

0.6 g of the prepared precursor was dispersed in 20 mL mixed solvent of glycerol and ethanol (V glycerol: V ethanol = 1:3), and refluxed for 3 h at 90 °C. After centrifugation, the powder after molecular intercalation was collected. The dried sample was calcined at 500 °C (2 °C/min) for 2 h. The resulting powder sample was labeled as Nv-rich-CN.

#### 2.2.3. Synthesis of Nv-poor-CN

The synthesis method of Nv-poor-CN is the same as that of Nv-rich-CN, except that the reflux temperature and time in the molecular intercalation part were set as 60 °C, 1 h.

#### 2.2.4. Synthesis of CN

10g melamine was calcined at 500 °C (2 °C/min) for 4 h, and the resulting product was denoted as CN.

### 2.3. Characterization

X-ray diffraction (XRD) data were measured by Bruker D8 ADVANCE equipped with Ni-filtered CuKα radiation at 10° min⁻¹. Tecnai G2 F20 S-Twin TMP is used to capture transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images and scanning TEM (STEM) images, under the condition of an accelerating voltage of 200 kV, in addition, energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS) analysis was given by Oxford EDS Inca X-Max. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were obtained on FEI Inspect F50 (FSEM). Atomic force microscope (AFM) results were collected by Bruker Dimension ICON (AFM). The elemental analysis (EA) test was carried out on Elementar Vario EL cube (EA)-CHNS. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra were provided by Escalab 250Xi (Thermo Fisher). Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) results were collected on JEOL JES-FA200 (ESR/EPR) at room temperature. UV–vis diffuse reflection spectra (DRS) was operated on Lambda 650 s (Shimadzu). Photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) characterization were conducted on FluoTime 300. Electronic spin resonance (ESR) spectra were provided by Bruker A300 and 2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidine (TEMP) was used as a capture agent for singlet oxygen (¹O₂). The catalyst samples were dispersed in water and tested at room temperature after 3 min of illumination. The CO₂ adsorption experiment was completed by the Quantachrome IQ2 system. The specific surface area information was given by Quantachrome autosorb iQ, the sample degassing condition was 150 °C, 6 h. *In situ* diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform (DRIFT) results were provided by Nicolet iS50 FTIR spectrometer. Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations were employed using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) with the Projector-augumented plane-wave (PAW) method. Generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was employed in the calculation process in the form of Perdew-Burke-Eznerhof (PBE). DFT-D3 correction method was used to describe van der Waals interaction. The CN model was obtained by constructing a 2 × 2×1 supercell, and the Nv-rich-CN model was obtained by deleting the N19 atom (N₂c) on the basis of the CN model. The cutoff energy was set at 500 eV, and 10⁻⁶ eV per atom and 0.01 eV/Å were used as the convergence standard for energy and force, respectively. The calculation formula of adsorption energy (Eₐdₛ) is as follows:

$$\mathrm{E_{ads}} = \mathrm{E_{total}} - (\mathrm{E_{CN}} + \mathrm{E_{mol}})$$

Among them, E<sub>total</sub> and E<sub>mol</sub> represent the total energy of the adsorption system and the energy of the separated molecules, respectively.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Preparation and characterization of Nv-rich-CN

Fig. 1a depicts the synthesis process of Nv-rich-CN. Nv-rich-CN was prepared according to molecular self-assembly and molecular embedding strategies. In the process of high-temperature polycondensation, ethanol and glycerol molecules were used as intercalants to exfoliate the layered precursor into ultra-thin nanosheets. Subsequently, the intercalated molecules were released in the form of gas, causing some of the N atoms on the surface to overflow, thereby forming ultra-thin $g-C_3N_4$ nanosheets with rich N vacancies. TEM images (Fig. 1b and Fig. S1a), STEM image (Fig. 1c), and SEM image (Fig. S1b) reveal the morphology of the ultra-thin sheet structure with micropores for Nv-rich-CN. Similar to the morphology of Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN exhibits an ultra-thin porous structure (Fig. 1d and Fig. S1c). The ultra-thin and porous structure indicates that the molecular intercalation peels off the layered precursor. CN exhibits a typical stacked nanosheet morphology (Fig. S1d). The atomically thin thicknesses of Nv-rich-CN was confirmed by AFM images (Fig. 1e and f). The measured thickness of Nv-rich-CN is 1 nm, which is approximately the thickness of three atomic layers of CN. The atomically thin thicknesses make the atoms on the surface unstable and easy to overflow to produce vacancy defects. The EDS mapping images of Nv-rich-CN (Fig. S1e-h) show that C and N are uniformly distributed.

To confirm the relative content and type of vacancies in the sample, the elemental analysis was used to analyze the element content of the prepared photocatalysts. Fig. 2a shows the molar ratio of N/(C+H) of the prepared photocatalysts. For details, please refer to Table 1. The molar ratios of N/(C+H) for Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN are 1.69, 1.71, and 1.70. The decrease in the relative content of N elements indicates the formation of N vacancies. Among them, the relative content of the N element in the Nv-rich-CN sample is the lowest, indicating that the number of N vacancies in Nv-rich-CN is the largest. The EDS mapping images of Nv-rich-CN and Nv-poor-CN (Fig. S2) further illustrate that Nv-rich-CN possesses more N vacancies.

The N1s XPS spectra of Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN (Fig. 3a) can be divided into three peaks at 398.7 ($N_{2C}$), 400.0 ($N_{3C}$), and 401.2 eV (N-Hx) [38,39]. Furthermore, the peak area ratios of $N_{2C}/N_{3C}$ for Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN are 2.59, 4.27, and 4.11 (Fig. 2b). The relative content of $N_{2C}$ in Nv-rich-CN is significantly reduced, indicating that the N vacancy exists in the $N_{2C}$ position. The N atoms at the position of $N_{2C}$ tends to be preferentially lost due to unsaturated coordination [40]. EPR was used to study the effects of vacancies on the surrounding electronic structure. Nv-rich-CN has the lowest signal intensity at g = 2.001(Fig. 3b), indicating that N vacancies cause the reduction of unpaired electrons on the C atoms of the aromatic [41].

### 3.2. Exciton dissociation promoted by N vacancies
The PL spectroscopy can intuitively reflect the attenuation of excitons, considered to be an effective method to study the dissociation of excitons. Excitons generally relaxed to the ground state by radiating photons, so the detected fluorescence was formed by the decay of singlet excitons [32]. The PL spectra of Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN (Fig. 4a) show that the PL intensity of the sample modified with vacancies is lower than that of CN. The decrease in PL intensity indicates that the concentration of singlet excitons is reduced. Among them, the Nv-rich-CN sample has the lowest PL intensity, indicating that the vacancies promote the dissociation of excitons, and the number of vacancies is directly proportional to the degree of exciton dissociation. The average lifetime of singlet excitons of the prepared catalyst was calculated by the corresponding TRPL spectra (inset of Fig. 4a). The lifetime of singlet excitons in Nv-rich-CN is the lowest, which further shows that N vacancies promote excitons dissociation. The localization of electrons near the N vacancy changes the distribution of surrounding electrons and increases the degree of disorder, thereby providing a driving force for the dissociation of excitons. The Mott-Schottky curves of Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN are shown in Fig. 4b, and the positive slope indicates that the prepared samples are n-type semiconductors. According to the Mott-Schottky equation, the slope is inversely proportional to the electron concentration, and the slope can be used to estimate the electron concentration in the sample. The electron concentration of samples modified by vacancies is greater than that in CN. Among them, the electron concentration in Nv-rich-CN is 3.24 times higher than that of CN. In addition, the flat-band potential of Nv-rich-CN (-0.84 V) is more negative than that of CN (-0.51 V), suggesting that Nv-rich-CN has a stronger ability to reduce $CO_{2}$. The EIS spectra (Fig. 4c) show that the samples modified by vacancies have a smaller arc radius, which means that the impedance encountered during the charge transfer process is smaller [45-49]. The transient photocurrent curve (inset in Fig. 4c) shows that Nv-rich-CN and Nv-poor-CN exhibit higher photocurrent density, indicating that excitons are dissociated into free charges at N vacancies, thereby increasing the electron concentration and improving charge transfer.

The triplet excitons in the catalyst transfer energy to the ground state oxygen molecules through resonance, which leads to the generation of singlet oxygen $(^{1}O_{2})$ [50]. Therefore, TMB was used as a probe molecule for $^{1}O_{2}$, and molecular oxygen activation of Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN was implemented to verify the exciton effect of the prepared photocatalysts. Fig. 4d depicts the evolution of the absorption spectrum of TMB oxidation for Nv-rich-CN over time. It can be seen that the absorbance of Nv-rich-CN increases with time. The absorbance of Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN monitored at 371 nm (Fig. 4e) was used to evaluate the oxidation activity. The absorbance of Nv-rich-CN, Nv-poor-CN, and CN increased with time, indicating that $^{1}O_{2}$ was generated in the prepared photocatalysts. Furthermore, ESR spectra provide direct evidence of $^{1}O_{2}$ generation (Fig. 4f). TEMP was used as a trapping agent for detecting $^{1}O_{2}$ generation. After 3 min of illumination, the ESR spectra of the Nv-rich-CN showed a 1:1:1 triplet signal, corresponding to the signal of TEMPO. This represents the generation of $^{1}O_{2}$. It is worth mentioning that after adding the scavenger $(NaN_{3})$, the triplet signal of ESR was significantly reduced, confirming the production of $^{1}O_{2}$ in Nv-rich-CN. The generation of $^{1}O_{2}$ shows that excitons exist in the catalyst, which further confirms that the premise of promoting exciton dissociation by regulating the N vacancy is correct.

### 3.4. Mechanism of photocatalytic $CO_{2}$ reduction

In order to further explore the role of N vacancies as active sites in the photocatalytic reduction of $CO_{2}$, $CO_{2}$ adsorption experiments of Nv-rich-CN and CN at different temperatures were implemented (Fig. S8). At a temperature of 298 k, the adsorption capacity of Nv-rich -CN for $CO_{2}$ far exceeds that of CN. Moreover, the adsorption of $CO_{2}$ by Nv-rich-CN shows a more obvious temperature dependence, which indicates a stronger interaction between Nv-rich-CN and $CO_{2}$ molecules. Fig. 7a calculates the isosteric heat of $CO_{2}$ adsorption by CN and Nv-rich-CN.

The isosteric heat of Nv-rich-CN is $41.8\ \text{kJ}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$, which is much greater than that of CN $(17.5\ \text{kJ}\ \text{mol}^{-1})$, indicating that Nv-rich-CN has strong chemical adsorption for $CO_{2}$ [54,55]. Due to the dissociation of excitons near the N vacancy, electrons accumulate and increase the charge density, thereby promoting the adsorption of $CO_{2}$ molecules by Nv-rich-CN.

In situ DRIFT spectra (Fig. 7b) reveal the formation of intermediate products during the photocatalytic reduction of $CO_{2}$. In the adsorption stage, the introduced $CO_{2}$ is adsorbed on Nv-rich-CN in the form of carbonate species (Fig. S9). The possible mechanism of enhanced activity for Nv-rich-CN in
photocatalytic CO₂ reduction is shown in Fig. 9. Due to the small
dielectric constant and the weak shielding effect, the exciton effect exists
in g-C₃N₄ [59]. Exciton is a special species produced by the process of
light excitation; it will be also formed through Coulomb interaction. It is
worth noting that it is of great significance to regulate this kind of
special photogenerated species to provide positive promotion for pho-
tocatalytic reactions. In Nv-rich-CN, the modification of N vacancies
changes the distribution of electrons around the vacancies, resulting in
increased energy disorder. The energy disorder region provides a driving
force for the dissociation of excitons, which makes the excitons disso-
ciate into free charges at the N vacancy. The specific photophysical
model is shown in Fig. 9. Under the excitation of the pump light, the
electrons in the valence band transition to the conduction band. In the
process of τ1, the electrons transfer from the conduction band to the trap
state dominated by excitons, and more excitons are formed due to the
Coulomb force. Through the τ2 process, the excitons are captured by the
N vacancy and dissociated into free charges due to the energy confusion
of the N vacancy. Then the generated free electrons participate in the
photocatalytic reaction. Designing N vacancies to regulate exciton
dissociation provides more electrons for photocatalytic reduction of
CO₂. The unsaturated chemical environment makes the N vacancies
become the active sites in the CO₂ reduction reaction. The N vacancies in
Nv-rich-CN promote the adsorption and activation of CO₂ molecules by
the catalyst.

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have prepared ultra-thin g-C₃N₄ nanosheets with
rich N vacancies through molecular self-assembly and molecular inter-
calation strategies. N vacancies can effectively regulate the dissociation
of excitons into free charges, greatly increasing the effective utilization
of carriers. In addition, N vacancies acted as active sites in the process of
CO₂ reduction, promoting the adsorption and activation of CO₂ by the
catalyst. The exciton dissociation and active sites of N vacancies syn-
ergistically promoted the improvement of the activity of Nv-rich-CN. Fs-
TA spectra revealed a photophysical model of exciton dissociation. ¹O₂
capture experiments confirmed the existence of exciton effects in g-
C₃N₄. A series of data such as steady-state fluorescence test and Mott-
Schottky curve showed that the excitons dissociated into free charges,
verifying that the strategy proposed on targeted regulation of the
behavior of excitons is effective. Furthermore, DFT calculations were
used to explain the essential reason for the dissociation of excitons. *In
situ* DRIFT spectra revealed the change of intermediate products during
the reduction of CO₂. This work focused on excitons in polymer semi-
conductors, and designed vacancy engineering to targetedly regulate the
dissociation of excitons into free charges, improving the effective utili-
zation of carriers in photocatalytic reactions. In addition, this work is of
great significance for regulating excitons to make more contributions to
the photocatalytic process.
