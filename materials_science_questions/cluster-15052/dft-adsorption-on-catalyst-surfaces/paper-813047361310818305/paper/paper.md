# The role of electric field in enhancing separation of gas molecules
($\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) on VIB modified $\text{g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ (001)

Fang Wang$^{\text{a},\&}$, Penghui Li$^{\text{a},\&}$, Shiqian Wei$^{\text{a}}$, Jiaxin Guo$^{\text{a}}$, Meng Dan$^{\text{a}}$, Ying Zhou$^{\text{ab}*}$

$^{\text{a}}$The Center of New Energy Materials and Technology, School of Materials Science and Engineering, Southwest Petroleum University, Chengdu 610500, China

$^{\text{b}}$State Key Laboratory of Oil and Gas Reservoir Geology and Exploitation, Southwest Petroleum University, Chengdu 610500, China

$^{\&}$These authors contributed equally to this work and should be considered co-first authors

$^{*}$To whom correspondence should be addressed. Email: yzhou@swpu.edu.cn

**Abstract:** In this study, the first-principles calculations were performed to investigate the adsorption behaviors of gas molecules $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ on Cr, Mo and W modified $\text{g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ (001) surface. The results show that $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ are physically adsorbed on the pristine $\text{g-C}_3\text{N}_4$, while the adsorption becomes chemisorbed due to the introduction of transition metals which significantly improve the interfacial electron transfer and narrow the band gap of $\text{g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ (001). Furthermore, it is found that the adsorption behaviors can be greatly influenced by the applied electric field. The adsorption energy is generally arranged in the order of $E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{S}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{O}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{CO}_2)$, and $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ (001) exhibits the best separation capability. The study could provide a versatile approach to selectively capture and separate the mixed gases in the catalytic reactions by controlling the applied intensity of electric field.

**Keywords:** $\text{g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ (001); electric field; molecular adsorption; first-principles calculations

### 1. Introduction

During the exploitation of acid gas reservoir, a large amount of acid gases is usually released as harmful by-products such as hydrogen sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) and carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) mixed with the water vapour ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), which are hardly separated and treated in further processing [1,2]. In the field of catalysis, intensive efforts have been dedicated to improve the intrinsic selectivity of catalysts for capturing targeted-gases, which is the initial step and triggers subsequent catalytic reactions. However, materials can be usually poisoned and fail to separate the targeted gas in the gas mixture. Therefore, it is urgent to explore efficient catalysts for aimed harmful gas capture and separation.

Since Novoselov et al. first prepared graphene with the satisfactory specific surface area by mechanical exfoliation in 2004 [3], graphene-like two-dimensional (2D) materials have attracted worldwide attention [1,4,5]. Recently, graphitic carbon nitride ($\text{g-C}_3\text{N}_4$) as the promising catalyst has become a research hotspot owing to the large surface area and narrow band gap of 2.7 eV, which has good visible-light response acting as the semiconductor photocatalyst [6]. Nonetheless, its surface activity is low due to the weak adsorption capability. Hence, various approaches are devoted to enhance the interfacial interaction, such as the construction of interface heterojunction and element doping [7-15]. Among them, transition metal doping is found to be a simple but effective way. Moreover, the transition metals can not only promote the surface electron transfer but also act as the adsorption active sites, which largely facilitate the catalytic reactions [16,17]. For instance, our previous work showed that Cu and Mo were able to change the selectivity of reaction paths for $\text{CO}_2$ conversion [18]. Additionally, it is found that applying external electric fields can greatly affect the surface physicochemical properties of catalysts [19,20]. For example, the electric fields can dramatically enhance the hydrogen storage on boron nitride (BN) nanomaterials [21]. What is more, $\text{MoS}_2$ monolayer can realize $\text{CO}_2/\text{N}_2$ separation under the range of electric field with 0.002-0.005 a.u. [22] Therefore, appropriate external electric field can enhance the adsorption and separation process for gas molecules on the surface.

$\text{g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ structure has two different types, including tri-s-triazine and s-triazine

based g-C₃N₄. Until now, most studies are focused on tri-s-triazine unit due to its better thermal stability [23], and a little attention is attached for s-triazine based g-C₃N₄. While s-triazine based g-C₃N₄ has been synthesized successfully and has promising applications in the field of catalysis [24,25]. Therefore, it is of significance to investigate the surface properties of s-triazine based g-C₃N₄. In this work, we first investigated the adsorption properties of H₂S and CO₂ along with H₂O on the s-triazine based g-C₃N₄ (001) via the first-principles calculations. In addition, their adsorption abilities were studied on VIB (Cr, Mo and W) modified g-C₃N₄ monolayer. Upon applying the electric fields in the range of 0.002-0.006 a.u., the separation ability for the mixed gases H₂S, H₂O and CO₂ were enhanced at the modified surface. We expected W modified g-C₃N₄ (001) to be a promising candidate for selective separation of gas molecules under the external electric fields.

## 2. Computational methods

All calculations were done by the density functional theory (DFT) implemented in the Materials Studio software [26,27]. The Cambridge Sequential Total Energy Package (CASTEP) package was used to calculate the geometry optimizations, band structures and density of states (DOS) by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [28] with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) function. Long range dispersion correction was considered via the Grimme's scheme [29], which was parameterized by including damped atom-pairwise dispersion corrections and neglected relevant many body effects. It was shown to qualitatively alter dispersion forces in graphene and other 2D materials [17,30-34]. Therefore, to ensure the reliability of the calculation results, we have used the dispersion corrected DFT-D method for 2D g-C₃N₄ surface. The Broyden, Fletcher, Goldfarb, and Shannon (BFGS) method was used to specify the minimization algorithm. The valence electron configurations were $2s^22p^2$ for C, $2s^22p^3$ for N, $3d^54s^1$ for Cr, $4d^55s^1$ for Mo and $5d^46s^2$ for W, respectively. The electron wave functions were expanded using plane waves with a cutoff energy of 290 eV, and the convergence criteria for the residual force and energy on each atom during structure relaxation were set to be 0.05 eV/Å and $2.0×10^{-5}$ eV, respectively.

The previous calculated indirect band gap was smaller than the experimental value of 2.7 eV [6] due to the well-known limitation in DFT. To correct the band gap, the approximation of scissors operation with 1.3 eV was taken to adjust the band gap to the experimental value. The Brillouin zones were sampled by 3×3×1 $k$-points using the Monkhorst-Pack scheme and a 3×3 supercell of g-C₃N₄ (001) were performed. The vacuum layer was set to be 15 Å to avoid interactions between periodic images.

The thermodynamic stability and adsorption behaviors of various gas molecules were studied through DMol³ package with convergence thresholds of $1.0^{-5}$ Ha on the energy, 0.002 Ha/Å on max force and 0.005 Å on max displacement. Thermodynamic properties have been calculated and the computational details were provided in supporting information. An all-electron DNP was used as the basis set. The self-consistent field procedure was used with a $1.0^{-6}$ a.u value. The electron distribution was examined by Mulliken population analysis [35].

The surface adsorption energy ( $\Delta E_{ads}$ ) was calculated as follows:

$$
\Delta E_{ads}=E_{slab}^{gas}-E_{slab}-E^{gas} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{slab}^{gas}$ was the total energy of g-C₃N₄ monolayer with adsorbates, $E_{slab}$ was the total energy of the clean slab, and $E^{gas}$ was the total energy of gas molecule. It was noted the planar slab became corrugated in the presence adsorbates, and $E_{slab}$ referred to the total energy of the corrugated plane. The more negative value of $\Delta E_{ads}$ indicates the stronger adsorption interaction.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Gas molecules adsorbed on $g$-$C_3N_4$ (001)

To investigate the adsorption behaviors of various gas molecules on g-C₃N₄ (001), it is necessary to determine their most favorable adsorption sites and configurations. Three possible adsorption sites and two possible horizontal (h) and vertical (v) orientations are considered and shown in Fig. 1a. We can see that the absolute value of adsorption energy ($E_{ads}$) were generally arranged in the order of $E_{ads}$(CO₂) >

$E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{S}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{O})$ in Fig. 1b. It was found that the planar g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ slab became corrugated upon the transition metal doping. The calculated energy difference between the corrugated plane and the planar g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ for $\text{CO}_2$ is 0.11 eV, which is the largest compared to $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, indicating the strongest interfacial interaction. The variation trend is different from that of $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ on the tri-s-triazine based g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ [36]. This may be due to the different adsorption sites and electrostatic interactions. The important structural information of optimized configurations is listed in Table 1. $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ molecule prefers to adsorb at c-h site of g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ (001) surface, and the $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecule is preferably parallel to c-v site of the g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ surface. Therefore, the c site defined as the hollow site is the optimal adsorption site on the surface, which is in agreement with the theoretical study of Li element doping in s-triazine based g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ [37,38]. Compared to another graphene like germanenene material, gaseous molecules also prefer to adsorb on the similar hollow site on the surface [39]. Moreover, it is found the structural information of the adsorbed molecules have very slight changes compared to that in the gas phase. Especially, the absolute value of charge transfer slightly changes after the gas molecules adsorbed g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$. The results indicate that their interactions are very weak and the molecules are physical adsorption on g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ surface. Therefore, it is necessary to enhance the adsorption ability of pristine g-$\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ surface.

### 3.2 Band structures, density of states and thermodynamics properties of VIB modified $g$-$C_3N_4$ (001)

Transition metals in a lower oxidation state generally possess stronger ability to capture the adsorbates [40]. Therefore, single Cr, Mo and W atoms are loaded on the preferable hollow site on g-C₃N₄(001) in Fig. 1, with the expectation to improve the surface reactivity. It is found that the embedded transition metal atoms in the vacancies of graphene which is geometrically analogous to the hollow site on g-C₃N₄, can enhance the surface adsorption ability [41,42]. To investigate the influence of the doping effect, the band structures for the pristine and modified surfaces were illustrated in Fig. 2. The calculated band gap after correction for pure g-C₃N₄ is 2.71 eV, which is close to the previous theoretical study [43] and the experimental value [6]. Upon the Cr, Mo and W doping, the band gap reduces to 1.64, 1.71 and 1.69 eV, respectively. This can greatly enlarge the visible-light absorption range and improve the solar conversion efficiency compared to the pristine g-C₃N₄.

Furthermore, it is seen from PDOS in Fig. 3 that the electronic states near the conduction band (CB) and the valence (VB) edges are mainly contributed by N 2p and C 2p orbitals, and N 2p orbitals for pure g-C₃N₄, respectively. Notably, the VB has barely contribution from the C atoms, which presents the small hybridization with the adjacent N atoms. Therefore, the photogenerated electron-hole pairs may only be appeared at the N sites under the visible-light illumination. However, once Cr, Mo and W are loaded, the states distributions have been largely influenced. Especially, d orbitals of transition metal atoms become dominant near the VB and cross the Fermi levels, which shows the transition metals can act as the electron donors and promote the surface activity. Additionally, the d orbitals also have a somewhat large contribution to the CB edge and are overlapped with C and N states. Noteworthily, it is found in total DOS of Fig. 3 that the Mo doping maintains the symmetric distribution of the spin-up and spin-down states for pure g-C₃N₄. But the introduction of Cr and W break the spin symmetry especially around the Fermi level and lead to spin polarization. This may bring about magnetic properties to the surface. In a word,

the doping of VIB transition metals is an efficient way to improve photoelectrochemical properties of g-C₃N₄ surface.

To assess the thermal stability of the VIB transition metal modified g-C₃N₄ surface, we studied the thermodynamic properties as shown in Fig. 4. It can be seen that $S$, $C_p$ and $H$ for VIB modified surface increase gradually with the increasing of temperature, while $G$ shows an opposite decrease tendency. The $G$ value largely decreases as the temperature increases, which indicates that the doped metal atom can weaken the thermodynamic stability of pristine g-C₃N₄. This is in agreement with the structure distortions due to the Cr, Mo and W doping, that is, the g-C₃N₄ (001) planar surface becomes corrugated after doping. On the whole, Cr, Mo and W have the similar effect on the thermodynamic properties since they belong to the same VIB group.

### 3.3 $H_2S$, $H_2O$ and $CO_2$ adsorption on VIB modified g-C₃N₄ (001)

To further gain insights into the doping effect on the adsorption behaviours, the adsorption energies for $H_2O$, $H_2S$ and $CO_2$ are calculated and displayed in Fig. 5. Interestingly, the variation trend of adsorption energy is in the order of $E_{\text{ads}}(H_2S) > E_{\text{ads}}(H_2O) > E_{\text{ads}}(CO_2)$, which is contrary to that on the pristine g-C₃N₄ (001). This change of variation trend can be ascribed to the transition metal doping, which acts as the active adsorption site. The calculated charge variation for transition metal induced by adsorbed molecules is similar in the order of $(H_2S) > E_{\text{ads}}(H_2O) > E_{\text{ads}}(CO_2)$. For example, when $H_2S$, $H_2O$ and $CO_2$ are adsorbed on W doped g-C₃N₄, the charge variation for W is 0.17, 0.09, and 0.04 e, respectively. This indicates the chemical bonding interaction between the transition metal and $H_2S$ is the strongest, $H_2O$ the second, and $CO_2$ the last, which changes the variation trend of the gaseous molecules on transition metal doped g-C₃N₄. Same variation trend of adsorption energies for $H_2O$, $H_2S$ and $CO_2$ was also observed on other 2D materials such as GeSe monolayer and metal-organic framework nanosheets [44,45]. For Cr/g-C₃N₄, $H_2S$ and $H_2O$ would be preferentially adsorbed on the surface and separated from $CO_2$. Whereas for Mo/g-C₃N₄ and W/g-C₃N₄, $H_2S$ molecule tends to be captured on the surface, but $CO_2$ and $H_2O$ is difficult to demix due to the very close adsorption energies. As a result,

one can hardly separate the individual gas among the mixed three kinds of gases on any transition metal doped g-C₃N₄. Furthermore, it was noted in Table 2 that CO₂ has great structural deformations on Mo/g-C₃N₄ and W/g-C₃N₄ with the C-O bond elongating to 1.29Å and $\angle$OCO angle bending to about 140, which largely deviates from its linear structure. This is consistent with the large electron transfer from surface to CO₂ molecule on Mo and W doped g-C₃N₄. While for H₂O and H₂S, the structures have small changes and they act as the electron donator to the surface. Generally, the absolute charge transfer is in the range of 0.11-0.13 e for H₂O on the VIB doped g-C₃N₄, 0.14-0.21 e for H₂S, and 0.04-0.50 e for CO₂. This shows evident growth trend of electron transfer compared to pristine g-C₃N₄, suggesting the adsorption interaction having the chemisorption characteristics.

### 3.4 The influence of electric fields on adsorption behaviours on VIB doped g-C₃N₄ (001)

In this part, we applied an external electric field to separate the individual gas from the mixed three kinds of gases. The adsorption energies of gas molecules on Cr, Mo and W doped on g-C₃N₄ (001) are shown in Fig. 6. It is found that the variation trend of adsorption energies generally maintains compared to Fig. 5. For Cr/g-C₃N₄, CO₂ can be desorbed from H₂O and H₂S, while the latter two gases are hard to be separated because their adsorption energies are very close and even equal with the increasing of electric field from 0.002 to 0.006 a.u. For Mo/g-C₃N₄ and W/g-C₃N₄

with the increasing of electric field, they present similar adsorption tendency with $E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{S}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{O}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{CO}_2)$. $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ is preferably adsorbed at 0.003 a.u., and the latter two gases can be separated via increasing the electric field. It is noted that the energy difference between $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ is larger on $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ compared to $\text{Mo/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$, which suggests they can be effectively separated on $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ via increasing the electric field. Therefore, $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ shows the best separation ability for $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ by controlling the applied strength of electric fields. To better facilitate gas separation, a two-step separation process with the combination of $\text{Cr/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ is proposed, since the energetic differences between $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ are much larger on $\text{Cr/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ than $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ can be firstly adsorbed on $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$, and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ can be easily separated on $\text{Cr/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$. It is noted that separation process is intricate in practice which will depend on the temperature, pressure, gas density and availability of adsorption sites. The calculated adsorption energies can provide a reference for the possibility of gas separation. Previous theoretical work also applied the adsorption energy to estimate the separation possibility for various gas molecules [2,22].

To further study the interfacial electron transfer under the applied electric field, the variation of electron transfer with respect to the electric field is shown in Fig. 7. There is a general positive correlation between the adsorption energy and the absolute value of charge transfer of gas molecules. For example, the absolute value of charge transfer of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecule on $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ gradually increases with the increasing of the adsorption energy. Interestingly, the electron transfer of $\text{CO}_2$ on $\text{Cr/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ is positive with the increment of field strength, whereas the value becomes negative on $\text{Mo/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$ and $\text{W/g-C}_3\text{N}_4$. The larger absolute value of electron transfer indicates the stronger interaction with the surface. This shows that the external electric field can change the direction of the interfacial electron transfer, and affect the interfacial interaction for adsorbed gases on different catalysts [46].

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, the adsorption behaviors of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ on VIB transition

metal doped g-$C_3N_4$ (001) have been studied in detail. It is found that the gas molecules are physisorbed on g-$C_3N_4$ surface with small charge transfer. Whereas the adsorption becomes chemisorbed upon the doping of Cr, Mo and W, which is attributed to the enhancement of interfacial electron transfer amount. Meanwhile, these doped transition metals significantly narrow the band gap and enlarge the visible-light absorption. Furthermore, the three kinds of gas molecules can be individually separated on Mo and W doped g-$C_3N_4$ under the suitable electric fields. Especially, W/g-$C_3N_4$ (001) presents the best ability for the separation gas molecules. This study could provide insights to effectively capture and separate various gases in the field of catalysis.

Graphical abstract: The selective separation of $H_2S$, $CO_2$ and $H_2O$ on VIB transition metal doped g-$C_3N_4$ (001) under the electric field.

**Highlights:**

1.  The adsorption behaviors of $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ change from physical adsorption to chemical adsorption upon the loading of Cr, Mo and W on $\text{g-}\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$ (001).

2.  $E_{\text{ads}}$ is generally arranged as $E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{S}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{H}_2\text{O}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{CO}_2)$ on VIB modified $\text{g-}\text{C}_3\text{N}_4$, and they can be effectively separated via controlling the electric field.

3.  $\text{W/g-}\text{C}_3\text{N}_4(001)$ is a promising candidate for the effective separation of $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ under the applied electric field.
