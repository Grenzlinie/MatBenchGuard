and long-range interactions [19], DFT-D2 method, which was proposed by Grimme, was adopted. A high Monk-horst–Pack $k$-point mesh of $10×10×1$ was defined for both geometric optimizations and electronic calculations [20]. We selected double numerical plus polarization (DNP) as the atomic orbital basis set, and the global orbital cutoff radius and the smearing are set as $5.0$ Å and $0.005$ Ha to obtain high-accuracy results [21]. Complete linear synchronous transit (LST)/quadratic synchronous transit (QST) calculations were performed to locate transition states (TS) [22].

A $4×4×1$ supercell of intrinsic $h$-BN monolayer was modeled to perform the following calculations, and a vacuum thickness of $15$ Å along the z direction of the $h$-BN sheet is established to avoid the potential interaction with adjacent units [23]. The calculated lattice constant of pristine $h$-BN monolayer after optimization in this work was $2.50$ Å, in accordance with a previous report ($2.50$ Å [18]). The adsorption energy ($E_\text{ad}$) was defined to estimate the performance of N vacancy $h$-BN monolayer upon NO molecules, calculated as: $E_\text{ad}=E_\text{Surf}+E_\text{NO}-E_\text{Surf/NO}$. In this formula, $E_\text{Surf}$ and $E_\text{Surf/NO}$, respectively, represent the total energy of N vacancy $h$-BN monolayer before and after NO adsorption, and $E_\text{NO}$ is the energy of gaseous NO. A negative $E_\text{ad}$ means the exothermic behavior of the reaction, and the positive value indicates that the adsorption process is not energy favorable. In addition, we employed Hirshfeld method to analyze the atomic and molecular charges in every system.

## 3 Results and discussion

Figure 1 shows the most stable geometric structures of intrinsic and N-defected $h$-BN monolayer. As shown in Fig. 1a, the pristine $h$-BN monolayer is a plate sheet, in which the B–N bond length and the neighboring atomic B-B distance are measured to be $1.44$ and $2.50$ Å, respectively. After one N atom is removed from the sheet, as depicted in Fig. 1b, the configuration of the $h$-BN around the N vacancy experiences somewhat deformation. The B–B atomic distance are shortened to $2.29$ Å, indicating the strong attraction strength between dangling B atoms. However, the N-defected $h$-BN remains a sheet form, similar as that of the intrinsic counterpart.

In Fig. 2, we plotted the band structures (BS) and density of states (DOS) of intrinsic and N-defected $h$-BN monolayer to better understand the electronic behavior of single N vacancy on $h$-BN sheet. From the BS, Fig. 2a shows that the intrinsic $h$-BN monolayer behaves as an insulator with band gap of $4.68$ eV, while in Fig. 2b, it is seen there has no band gap in N-defected $h$-BN monolayer, and a novel states

emerges at the conduction band top crossing the Fermi level. That is, the removal of a N atom from the intrinsic $h$-BN monolayer leads to the strong electron-accepting property for the surface. In Fig. 2c wherein the DOS comparison is shown, we can see that the DOS curves of N-defected $h$-BN monolayer are left-shifted compared with the hole-free counterpart, due to the strong donator-like states caused by the nonbonding electrons. As reported, the existence of N defect in $h$-BN monolayer could trap electrons and scatter charge carriers [24], by which the carrier mobility would be reduced. Therefore, repairing the N vacancy in $h$-BN monolayer is essential to regain its admirable properties.

To heal the N vacancy in $h$-BN monolayer, two processes are required, traced as repairing process in Fig. 3 and removing process in Fig. 4. The repairing process begins with the physisorption of one NO molecule on the N-defected $h$-BN monolayer (IS1), as described in Fig. 3a. The distance between the $\mathrm{N}^{1}$ atom of the pioneer NO molecule and the plane is measured to be $2.71 \AA$, indicating a large distance of at least $3 \AA$ between the candidate $\mathrm{N}^{1}$ atom and any neighboring B atoms. The calculated $E_{\text {ad }}$ of $-0.06 \mathrm{eV}$ and negatively charged NO molecule of -0.17 e significantly verify the physisorption nature between N-defected $h$-BN monolayer and NO molecule. To reach the transition state (TS1), the energy barrier of $0.51 \mathrm{eV}$ must be overcome. In the TS1, the $\mathrm{N}^{1}-\mathrm{O}^{1}$ bond is elongated to $1.23 \AA$ from $1.16 \AA$ of IS1, and the N atom is captured by three B atoms of the defected $h$-BN monolayer forming B-N bond lengths of $2.14,2.14$ and $2.19 \AA$, respectively. Moreover, the NO is negatively charged by $-0.34 \mathrm{e}$, wherein $\mathrm{N}^{1}$ and $\mathrm{O}^{1}$ atoms gain 0.18 and 0.16 e from the N-defected $h$-BN monolayer, respectively. Upon the final state (FS1) in Fig. 3c, the $\mathrm{N}^{1}$ atom almost reaches to the position where the $\mathrm{N}$ atom is removed, indicating that the pioneer NO is stably adsorbed on the defected $h$-BN monolayer with strong bonds formed between $\mathrm{N}^{1}$ candidate and neighboring $\mathrm{B}$ atoms. The short B- $\mathrm{N}^{1}$ bond lengths of $1.48 \AA$ and the formed B-O bond length of $1.47 \AA$ can confirm this finding as well. For the adsorbed NO molecule, one can see that the $\mathrm{N}^{1}-\mathrm{O}^{1}$ bond length is remarkably elongated to $1.51 \AA$, and the $\mathrm{N}^{1}$ and $\mathrm{O}^{1}$ atoms are, respectively, negatively charged by -0.05 and $-0.15 \mathrm{e}$. That is, the adsorbed NO molecule behaves as an electron-donator transferring electron to the defect $h$-BN surface. Accompanied with the calculated $E_{\text {ad }}$ of $-7.01 \mathrm{eV}$, chemisorption could be identified in this system. It should be mentioned that the $\mathrm{N}^{1}-\mathrm{O}^{1}$ bond inner the NO molecule is somewhat weakened according to the dramatic deformations in its molecular morphology during the repairing process, which can result in a possible dissociation for the $\mathrm{N}^{1}-\mathrm{O}^{1}$ bond by further interacting with another reducing molecule, such as NO molecule. In that case, the $\mathrm{O}^{1}$ atom could be removed and the N-defected $h$-BN monolayer could be repaired.

After the repairing process, one more NO molecule should be introduced as the reducing species to interact with the extra $\mathrm{O}^{1}$ atom as shown in Fig. 4, which can facilitate the dissociation of $\mathrm{N}^{1}-\mathrm{O}^{1}$ bond and form the separated $\mathrm{NO}_{2}$ molecule instead. Then, the $h$-BN monolayer can be repaired after the release of the formed $\mathrm{NO}_{2}$. Figure 4a plots the initial state (IS2) of removing process. We can see that after interaction, the second NO is located the adsorbed NO and parallel with the surface, with the distance of $2.58 \AA$. However, there have no obvious deformations in the structures of NO adsorbed $h$-BN monolayer and second NO molecule. Moreover, $\mathrm{N}^{2}$ is positively charged by $0.02 \mathrm{e}$, while the $\mathrm{O}^{2}$ atoms is negatively charged by $0.01 \mathrm{e}$. In other words, the

second NO molecule releases 0.01 e in total to the surround- ing, which somewhat would lead to the electron redistribu- tion for the whole system. These findings indicate a weak physisorption for IS2, as further suggested by a small $E_{ad}$ of −0.15 eV. Through overcoming the energy barrier of only0.22 eV, there comes TS2. As described in Fig. 4b, the $N^{2}$  atom is trapped by $O^{1}$ atom with bond length of $2.03 \AA$ ; the $N^{1}-O^{1}$ bond is broken, while the $B-O^{1}$ bond further elon gates to $1.60 \AA$ . These deformations manifest the strong potential for dissociation of $N^{1}-O^{1}$ bond and formation of a novel $NO_{2}$ molecule. Furthermore, electron localization in TS2 becomes more evident for $O^{1}, O^{2}$ and $N^{2}$ atoms, which are separately charged by -0.22, -0.04 and 0.12 e. These charge redistributions allow the further deformations of the first adsorbed NO molecule under electrostatic forces and then enhance the $O^{1}$ removing process largely. When it comes to FS2 as portrayed in Fig. 4c, the formed $NO_{2}$ is seendesorbed from the repaired h-BN monolayer, with $N^{1}-O^{1}$  bond length of $2.95 \AA$ . The $N^{2}-O^{1}$ bond and $B-N^{1}$ bonds are shortened to 1.22 and $1.44 \AA$ , respectively, which are quiteclose to those lengths of 1.21 and $1.44 \AA$ in an isolated $NO_{2}$  and a perfect h-BN monolayer. For $N^{1}$ atom, it is negatively charged by -0.20 e, equal to that of removed N (-0.20 e) in perfect h-BN monolayer, indicating good electronic consist- ence using this method. Upon the released $NO_{2}$ molecule, the $N^{2}, O^{1}$ and $O^{2}$ are charged by 0.14,-0.10 and -0.10 e, respectively. This means that the newly formed $NO_{2}$ mol ecule as a whole accepts 0.06 e from the repaired h-BN mon-olayer. In addition, the calculated $E_{ad}$ of -0.28 eV in FS2(lower than 0.50 eV [25]) implies a feasible removal for $NO_{2}$  desorption from the repaired h-BN monolayer reparation.

Figure 5 exhibits the configuration of the repaired h-BN and its DOS distribution. Figure 5a shows that the repaired h-BN keeps as a plane sheet and its structure is equivalent to that of perfect counterpart. At the same time, through the DOS comparison between the intrinsic h-BN monolayer and repaired counterpart in Fig. 5b, one can see that their DOS curves are completely overlapped at every region, manifest- ing the recovered electronic behavior for the repaired h-BN monolayer. Based on all these findings, we presume the suc- cess of reparation for N-defected h-BN monolayer using this method.

It is worth adding that the energy barrier of 0.51 eV in repairing process could be easily realized at room tempera- ture since the energy barrier of a surface reaction at ambient temperature is 0.91 eV [26-28]. Besides, the drop energy of -6.55 eV in repairing process could be totally enough to overcome the energy barrier of 0.22 eV in the removal process. In addition, the strong exothermicity of -2.29 eV in removal process is capable of proceeding another repair-ing-removing cycle to heal any other N vacancies in h- BN monolayer. Therefore, we consider that the proposed approach is energetically favorable with good spontaneity.

## 4 Conclusions
In this paper, we theoretically conducted the reparation of N-defected h-BN monolayer by NO molecules using DFT method. The repairing processes contain two steps: (i) the adsorption of one NO molecule filling the vacancy by candi- date $N^{1}$ and (ii) the removal of extra $O^{1}$ by another NO mol ecule. The whole process is a NO disproportionation. Our results indicate that the processes are energetically favora- ble given the small energy barrier and large energy drop in each reaction. We are hopeful that our idea is workable for synthesizing perfect h-BN monolayer without energy cost. Also, we assume this work provides a novel and possible manner to repair the N vacancy in other nano-materials with N defects.
