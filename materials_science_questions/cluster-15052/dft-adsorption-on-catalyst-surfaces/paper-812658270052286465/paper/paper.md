# Adsorption of small molecules on transition metal doped rhodium clusters $\text{Rh}_3\text{X}$ ($\text{X} = 3d, 4d$ atom): a first-principles investigation

Xiaolin Guo$^\text{a}$, Haiming Duan$^\text{a}$, Biaobing Cao$^\text{a}$, Shuwei Lu$^\text{a}$, Mengqiu Long$^\text{a,b}$, Fengjuan Chen$^\text{a}$, Ablat Abliz$^\text{a}$, Zhaofeng Wu$^\text{a}$, Qun Jing$^\text{a}$, Zhenzhen Miao$^\text{a}$ and Xuan Chen$^\text{a}$

## ABSTRACT
The adsorption behaviours of seven molecules (CO, CO₂, N₂, NO, O₂, N₂O and NO₂) on $\text{Rh}_3\text{X}$ ($\text{X} =$ Sc-Zn, Y-Cd) clusters are systematically investigated by density-functional calculations. $\text{Rh}_3\text{X}$ clusters exhibit physical adsorption when interacting with CO₂, CO, N₂ and NO. The adsorption energies ($\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$) can be ranked as follows: $\text{NO} > \text{CO} > \text{CO}_2 \geq \text{N}_2$. Compared with pure $\text{Rh}_4$ cluster, the adsorption capacity changes with the doping element. Chemical adsorption can be obtained for $\text{Rh}_3\text{X}$ when adsorbing O₂, N₂O and NO₂. $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$ shows an order of $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}(\text{O}_2) > \text{E}_{\text{ads}}(\text{NO}_2) > \text{E}_{\text{ads}}(\text{N}_2\text{O})$. When O₂ is adsorbed, energy barrier with doping Tc or Cr atom is substantially reduced, which indicates that chemical reactivity of O₂ on $\text{Rh}_4$ can be significantly enhanced. The doped rhodium clusters can be viewed as good candidates in the discrimination between different gas molecules.

## 1. Introduction

Adsorption of small molecules on nano-sized materials has becoming an interesting topic in recent years in many fields such as the new catalyst, the gas sensors, and the magnetic nano-devices. Gas molecules can be absorbed on surfaces of low-dimensional systems, such as the carbon nanotubes, the graphene and the metal clusters. Adsorption of small molecules on doped transition metal clusters has aroused great interest for either the importance of the $d$ electrons or the influence of the doped elements on the adsorption behaviours of the small molecules.

Clusters have distinguished characteristics different fundamentally from the corresponding bulk materials, such as the high-symmetry, low-coordination number and large surface-to-volume ratio [1,2], which may have different reaction mechanisms with the surroundings such as the adsorption of the gas molecules. Many different gases can be adsorbed on metal clusters with different reactivity, which makes it necessary to further study and reveal the interaction mechanisms between different metal clusters and different kinds of molecules.

The physical and chemical properties of Rh clusters are particularly noticeable among the numerous transition metal clusters [3–15]. For example, though the Rh crystal is nonmagnetic, the experiments show that Rh clusters ($\text{Rh}_\text{n}$, $\text{n} \leq 50$) have non-zero magnetic moments, especially several small-sized Rh clusters ($\text{Rh}_{15}$, $\text{Rh}_{16}$ and $\text{Rh}_{19}$) possess high magnetic moments [16,17]. Considering that the physical and chemical properties of metal clusters can be changed by doping, the mixed Rh clusters have also attracted much attention [18–24]. For example, the structural distortion [22], the enhancement of the stability [23,24] and the enhancement of magnetism [18]

The adsorption and catalytic properties of Rh clusters have been concerned for a long time. Many investigations have been performed to study the adsorption of atoms or small molecules on Rh-based clusters [25,26]. It was found that the most stable adsorption positions of H and O atoms on the surface of Rh clusters are quite different from those on the surface of Rh crystals [27–34].

Many researchers have focused on the interaction between small-sized pure Rh clusters and the NO molecule (s) both theoretically and experimentally [35–42]. The results show that the anion systems are more stable than the neutral or cationic systems [37,38]. The physical adsorption of a single NO on pure Rh cluster occurs generally, and the adsorption location is closely related to the size of the cluster (e.g, the hollow site [38] of NO adsorbed on $Rh_n$ at $n=3$–5, and the top-site adsorption [40,41] of NO at $n>5$). Doping can obviously affect the adsorption behaviour of pure Rh clusters for NO. For example, when Ta is doped into Rh clusters, the reaction of NO will change from physical adsorption to chemical adsorption [43,44], and the doping of Ta will obviously intensify the decomposition of NO. Some of the studies have involved the reactions of Rh clusters with $N_2$ [45,46], $O_2$ [47,48] and $N_2O$ [24,49]. For $N_2$ (at room temperature or low temperature), it is usually molecular adsorption (physical adsorption). While for $O_2$ and $N_2O$, it is chemical adsorption. Which implies that Rh clusters have different reaction mechanisms for different molecules.

Considering that there are few studies about the adsorption of small molecules on doped rhodium clusters. In this paper the geometries and electronic properties of the ground states of $Rh_3X$ (X = 3d, 4d atom) and $Rh_3X$-M (M = $CO$, $CO_2$, $N_2$, $NO$, $O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$) clusters are investigated systematically by using density functional theory calculations. The computational details are given in section 2, and section 3 focuses on the analysis and discussion of the results, and the conclusions are summarised in section 4.

## 2. Computational methods

In this paper, by adopting the first-principles method based on density functional theory, the geometrical and electronic properties of $Rh_3X$ (X = 3d, 4d atom) clusters and the adsorption of $O_2$, $N_2$, $NO$, $NO_2$, $N_2O$, $CO$ and $CO_2$ on $Rh_3X$ are systematically investigated. The VASP software package [50,51] based on the spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) is used in the calculation. Within the generalised gradient approximation (GGA), the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) [52] exchange-correlation is used. It should be mentioned that the rationality of using the PBE exchange-correlation functional has been discussed in a previous study on the adsorption of single H, O and N atoms by mixed Rh clusters [27]. The convergence criteria are set to $1.0×10^{-6}$ eV for the total energy and $0.005\ \text{eV}/\mathring{A}$ for the forces in the processes of geometrical optimisation of the metal clusters as well as the cluster-molecular adsorption systems, and $1.0×10^{-5}$ eV for the total energy and $0.01\ \text{eV}/\mathring{A}$ for the forces in the processes of transition-state searching calculations. All the adsorption energy barriers are computed using the nudged elastic band (NEB) method. For all systems calculated, $14\mathring{A}^*14\mathring{A}^*14\ \mathring{A}$ cubic supercell is used to ensure that the interaction between atoms in adjacent boxes can be neglected.

A series of initial geometrical configurations from two to three dimensional are considered to search the ground state of $Rh_4$, and the lowest energy geometries of the alloy $Rh_3X$ (X = 3d, 4d atom) can be obtained from the unequivalent substitution of a 3d or 4d atom from the stable $Rh_4$ cluster. Then, the most stable configurations can be obtained by adsorbing various molecules such as $O_2$, $NO$, $NO_2$, $N_2O$, $N_2$, $CO$ and $CO_2$ at different sites on the surface of $Rh_3X$ (X = 3d, 4d atom) clusters. All the minimum energy configurations are further proved by frequency analysis (without imaginary frequency).

The total binding energy ($E_t$), the average binding energy ($E_b$) and the adsorption energy ($E_{ads}$) can be obtained as follows:

$$\mathrm{E_t(Rh_3X) = 3 * E(Rh) + E(X) - E(Rh_3X) \quad (1)}$$

$$\mathrm{E_b = \frac{E_t(Rh_3X)}{4} \quad (2)}$$

$$\mathrm{E_{ads}(Rh_3X - M) = E(Rh_3X) + E(M) - E(Rh_3X - M) \quad (3)}$$

Where X and M represent the single 3d or 4d atom and the adsorbed molecular, respectively. $Rh_3X$ and $Rh_3X$-M denote the metal cluster and the interaction system of the metal cluster and the adsorbed molecular, respectively. E(A) denotes the total energy of the system A.

For the pure Rh₄, the lowest-energy structure is a regular tetrahedron with T_d symmetry, and the bond length and average binding energy are 2.43 Å and 2.99 eV, respectively. Most of the favourable structures of the mixed clusters Rh₃X (X = Sc-Zn, Y-Cd) maintain the symmetries of C₃ᵥ, and where three Rh atoms form a regular triangle and the doped atom (X) is equal in distance to the three Rh atoms. Rh₃Co, Rh₃Ni, Rh₃Mo and Rh₃Cd are exceptions, where the interaction between the doped atom and three Rh atoms is different, and the structure is distorted as compared with that of the pure Rh₄. The lowest energy geometries of the Rh₃X (X = Sc-Zn, Y-Cd) are consistent with those reported in previous publications [10,11,38].

For mixed clusters $Rh_3X$ with doping the early $3d$ and $4d$ transition metal atoms (X = Sc-V, Y-Nb), the average binding energies of $Rh_3X$ (X = Sc-V, Y-Nb) are significantly higher than that of $Rh_4$, which indicates that stabilities of $Rh_3X$ (X = Sc-V, Y-Nb) are obviously enhanced as compared with that of $Rh_4$. And these mixed clusters are kinetically stable because of no imaginary frequencies.

The calculated magnetic moment of $Rh_4$ is zero [53]. Though the pure $Rh_4$ is nonmagnetic, most doped clusters have net magnetic moments, and relatively large magnetic moments can be obtained when doped with Fe, Co, Mn, Cu, Tc and Ru ($7\mu_B$ for $Rh_3Fe$ and $5\mu_B$ for $Rh_3Ru$, and $4\mu_B$ for the others). It should be mentioned that there are both ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic ground states of $Rh_3X$ clusters, which are also given in Table 1.

As can be seen from Figure 1, for the mixed $Rh_3X$ clusters, a certain amount of charge is usually transferred from Rh to the doped atom. Generally from Sc-Cu and Y-Rh, with the increase of atomic number, the amount of charge transfer decreases gradually, which is consistent with the variation trend of the electronegativity of the doped atoms.

### 3.2. Physical adsorption of $Rh_3X$ (X = Sc-Zn, Y-Cd)
$Rh_3X$ (X = Sc-Zn, Y-Cd) clusters exhibit physical adsorption when interacting with molecules $CO_2$, $CO$, $N_2$ and NO. Figure 2 shows the lowest energy structures of the molecules $CO_2$, $CO$, $N_2$ and NO physically adsorbed on the surface of $Rh_3X$ (X = Sc-Zn, Y-Cd) clusters.

There is no obvious correlation between the most stable configurations and the types of adsorbed molecules as well as the doped atoms. But generally speaking, for the physical adsorption of four kinds of molecules, the relationship between the most stable structures and doped elements in the adsorption of $CO_2$ and $N_2$ is relatively simple, while the relationship between the most stable structures and the types of doped atoms is the most complex in the case of adsorbing NO. When CO is adsorbed, the complexity of stable configurations varying with doping atoms is between the above two cases.

![](./images/812658270052286465_9.jpg)

Figure 2. The ground state geometries of $Rh_3X$-M (X = Sc-Zn, Y-Cd; M = $CO_2$, $CO$, $N_2$, NO) clusters. The purple, light blue, gray, dark blue and red atoms represent X, Rh, C, N and O, respectively.

For the adsorption of $CO_2$, generally most stable configurations prefer bridge-site adsorption. Specifically, for most clusters containing the early $3d$ or $4d$ atoms, $CO_2$ only interacts directly with the doped atom and one Rh atom; For $Rh_3X$ ($X = Cu, Pd, Ag$ and $Cd$) clusters, $CO_2$ interacts directly with two Rh atoms.

$N_2$ is not directly linked to the doped atom in all of the lowest energy structures when adsorbing a $N_2$ molecule. In most cases, $N_2$ interacts with only one Rh atom, as accompanied by top-site adsorption. For doped clusters $Rh_3X$ ($X = Fe, Zn$, and $Cd$), $N_2$ connects with two Rh atoms to form bridge-site adsorption.

For the adsorption of CO on $Rh_3X$ ($X = Sc-Zn$, Y-Cd), CO and the doped atom are not bonded directly in all of the most stable structures, and with the different types of doped atoms, top-site adsorption, bridge-site adsorption and hollow-site adsorption may occur. Specifically, top-site adsorption occurs when Ti, V, Zr, Nb and Mo are doped. Bridge-site adsorption is the most stable when Sc, Cr-Co, Cu, Zn, Y, Tc, Ag and Cd are doped, while hollow-site adsorption is the most advantageous when Ni, Ru, Rh and Pd are doped. The previous investigation [24] has revealed that for $Rh_5$ and $Rh_6$ clusters, CO is adsorbed at the hollow-site, which is consistent with the adsorption mode of $Rh_4$ cluster presented here.

The most stable configurations vary with the types of doped atoms when adsorbing NO. Specifically, when doping five kinds of atoms of Mn-Cu, the top-site adsorption bonded with one Rh atom occurs. When doping the early transition metal $3d$ (Sc-Cr) and $4d$ (Y-Mo) atoms, a bridge-site adsorption is formed, where two atoms in NO are all involved in bonding (O connects with the doped atom and N links to one Rh atom). Bridging-site adsorption also occurs when Zn, Pd, Ag, Tc and Ru are adsorbed, and the difference is that for the former three elements (Zn, Pd, Ag) NO links to two Rh atoms while for the latter (Tc and Ru) it connects with the doped atom and one Rh. Hollow-site adsorption occurs for $Rh_4$ and $Rh_3Cd$, where NO molecule combines with three Rh atoms. The most stable hollow-site adsorption of NO on pure $Rh_4$ is consistent with the previous published results [36,38,54].

The adsorption energies of four molecules ($CO_2$, CO, $N_2$ and NO) interacting with $Rh_3X$ ($X = Sc-Zn$, Y-Cd) clusters are given in Figure 3. It can be found that there is a clear demarcation between the adsorption energies of different kinds of molecules, regardless of the doping elements: the magnitudes of the adsorption energies of NO, CO, $CO_2$ and $N_2$ are decreased from the largest to the smallest. The order of selectivity for physical adsorption of different molecules is $E_{ads}(NO) > E_{ads}(CO) > E_{ads}(CO_2) > E_{ads}(N_2)$. It can be seen that Rh clusters and Rh-based mixed clusters have good adsorption capacities for NO and CO. As mentioned in the introduction of this paper, many researchers have focused on the interaction between Rh clusters and molecules composed of NO or CO, and we think that this is closely related to the strong adsorption capacities of Rh clusters for NO and CO.

![](./images/812658270052286465_10.jpg)

Figure 3. The adsorption energies ($E_{ads}$) of $CO_2$, CO, $N_2$ and NO on the $Rh_3X$ ($X = Sc-Zn$, Y-Cd) clusters.

For the physical adsorption of four molecules ($CO_2$, CO, $N_2$ and NO) on the surface of $Rh_3X$ ($X = Sc-Zn$, Y-Cd) clusters, the influence of doping on the adsorption ability of clusters can also be analysed from Figure 3. Compared with pure $Rh_4$, the adsorption capacity of doped cluster for CO is not significantly improved (in most cases it is weakening). In most cases, the adsorption energy of doped cluster for $N_2$ or CO is significantly increased (accompanied by a significant increase in adsorption capacity), while for $CO_2$, the adsorption energies are all increased. This indicates that doped rhodium clusters have better adsorption selectivity for some molecules than pure Rh clusters.

### 3.3. Chemical adsorption of $Rh_3X$ ($X = Sc-Zn$, Y-Cd)

$O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$ are chemically adsorbed on $Rh_3X$ ($X = Sc-Zn$, Y-Cd) clusters. In Figure 4, the lowest energy structures for chemical adsorption of molecules $O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$ on $Rh_3X$ ($X = Sc-Zn$, Y-Cd) clusters are given.

For adsorption of $N_2O$, no matter how the type of the doping atom is, the $N_2O$ molecule decomposes into a $N_2$ molecule and an O atom after adsorption, and $N_2$ is adsorbed at the top site connected with one Rh atom, but the adsorption position of O atom is closely related to the type of the doping atom. When the early transition metal atoms Sc, Ti, Y, Zr and some middle transition metal atoms Fe-Ni are doped, O atom is bridge-bonded adsorbed with the doped atom and one Rh atom. For most middle transition metal atoms V-Mn and Nb-Rh, O is adsorbed at the top of the doped atom. While for the late transition metal atoms Cu, Zn and Pd-Cd, O atom is adsorbed on the top of one Rh atom. The lowest energy configuration of the chemical adsorption of

For chemical adsorption of $\ce{O_{2}}$, the adsorption positions of the two O atoms after dissociation are closely related to the types of doped atoms. As in the case of $\ce{Rh_{4}}$, when the early transition metal atoms Sc-V, Y, Zr, and some middle transition metal atoms Co, Ni and Mo are doped, both O atoms are simultaneously linked to the doped atom and one Rh atom to form a bridge-site adsorption. When the late transition metal atoms Cu, Zn and Pd-Cd are doped, the two O atoms are also adsorbed at the bridge site, and both are connected with a Rh-Rh bond; For the doping of atom X ($\text{X} = \text{Cr, Mn, Nb, Tc and}$ Ru), one O atom is adsorbed at the top of the doped atom X, while the other O atom is adsorbed at the bridge site of a X-Rh bond. $\ce{Rh_{3}Fe}$ is a special case, where one O atom is bridge-adsorbed to a Rh-Fe bond and the other O is adsorbed on a vacancy site linked to a triangle formed by Fe atom and two Rh atoms. The theoretical investigation of experimental reactivities of the oxides of small rhodium clusters by Harding *et al.* [47] has shown that $\ce{Rh_{6}O_{2}^{+}}$ is more easily adsorbed on the bridge site by two single O atoms, and that is similar to the adsorption mode of $\ce{O_{2}}$ on pure $\ce{Rh_{4}}$ in this study.

For $\ce{NO_{2}}$, after chemisorption, it is decomposed into a NO molecule and an O atom, but the most stable

configuration is much more complex than those of $N_2O$ and $O_2$. When X (X = Sc, Fe and Ni) is doped, NO and O are bridge-adsorbed to a Rh-Rh bond and a X-Rh bond, respectively. In the case of doping X (X = Ti, Y and Zr), NO and O are also bridge-adsorbed, where O is connected with one Rh-X bond and NO is connected with another Rh-X bond (N is linked to Rh and O is linked to X). As X (X = V, Nb and Mo) is doped, NO and O are all located at the top sites, where O is linked to X and NO is connected to one Rh. For the doping of X (X = Cr and Tc), O is adsorbed at the top of the doped atom, and NO is adsorbed at the bridge site of one Rh-X bond. When doped with Mn and Ru, O is adsorbed at the top site of the doped atom and NO is bridge-bonded to two Rh atoms. For $Rh_3Co$, NO is located at the top site of one Rh atom and O is bridge-adsorbed to one Rh-Co bond. As Cu and Zn are doped, NO is the top-site adsorption connected to one Rh and O is the bridge-site adsorption connected to the other two Rh atoms. When doped with post-transition metal atoms Pd, Ag and Cd, O and NO are all connected with Rh atoms, and O is at the top of one Rh and NO is bridge-adsorbed to the other two Rh atoms. For pure $Rh_4$, O is the top-site adsorption connected with one Rh, and NO is the hollow-site adsorption connected with the other three Rh atoms.

It can be seen that the most stable configurations of $Rh_3X$ (X = Sc-Zn, Y-Cd) clusters after chemical adsorption with molecules $O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$ are closely related to the types of adsorbed molecules and doped atoms. From $N_2O$ to $O_2$ and then to $NO_2$, the structures of $Rh_3X$ clusters show more diversity and there are no obviously regular structural characteristics. However, by analysing the structures shown in Figure 4, it can be found that for the $Rh_3X$ (X = Cu, Zn and Pd-Cd) clusters doped with post-transition metal atoms, no matter what kind of molecule is adsorbed, the product does not bond directly to the doped atom. We think that this may be due to the distinct $d$-electron distribution of Rh and X in the $Rh_3X$ (X = Cu, Zn and Pd-Cd) clusters: the $d$-electron of Rh has a large distribution around the Fermi energy (thus forming a strong $sp$-$d$ hybridisation between Rh and the adsorbate near Fermi energy), while the $d$-electron distribution of the post-transition metal atom is a bit far away from the Fermi energy (so the $sp$-$d$ hybridisation is weak). As an example, the partial density of states of the most stable configurations of the chemisorption of $Rh_3Cd$-$O_2$ and $Rh_4$-$O_2$ are given in Figure 5.

One can find that, for chemical adsorption, the boundaries between the adsorption energies of different molecules (compared with those in physical adsorption, as shown in Figure 3) are more distinct, and the variations of chemical adsorption energies with the types of doped atoms are basically the same. Regardless of the type of doped atom, the adsorption energy of $O_2$ is the largest, followed by that of $NO_2$, and that of $N_2O$ is the smallest. That is to say, the sequence of selectivity for different molecules is $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}\ (\text{O}_2) > \text{E}_{\text{ads}}\ (\text{NO}_2) > \text{E}_{\text{ads}}\ (\text{N}_2\text{O})$.

The effect of doping on chemical adsorption energy can be analysed in detail. As can be seen from Figure 6, for the three molecules $O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$, the variation trend of the chemical adsorption energy with the type of doped atom is basically the same. For example, for $3d$ transition metal atom doping, $Rh_3Cr$ has the largest adsorption energy; and for $4d$ transition metal atom doping, $Rh_3Tc$ has the largest adsorption energy. An interesting and important result can be obtained from the analysis of Figure 5, as compared with the chemical adsorption of pure $Rh_4$ clusters, the chemical adsorption energies of all doped clusters are improved in the case of absorbing $O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$. This indicates that for pure $Rh_4$ clusters, substitution of one Rh atom by another transition metal atom can significantly enhance the chemical activity of the cluster (Under the condition that chemisorption can occur).

As mentioned above, compared with $N_2O$ and $NO_2$, $Rh_3X$ ($X = \text{Sc-Zn, Y-Cd}$) clusters have the largest adsorption energies for $O_2$. Does this mean that $Rh_3X$ ($X = \text{Sc-Zn, Y-Cd}$) clusters also have the strongest adsorption capacity for $O_2$? Unlike physical adsorption, it should take into account the possibility of the occurrence of chemical adsorption (reactants and products are different), which requires an understanding of the reaction process of chemical adsorption. Transition state searching can provide useful information in this regard. Considering the enormous amount of calculations for transition state searching, only several related systems are calculated involving the chemical adsorption of $Rh_3X$ ($X = \text{Rh, Cr, Tc and Fe}$) with $O_2$. The pure $Rh_4$ can be regarded as a reference, and $Rh_3Cr$ and $Rh_3Tc$ have the maximum chemical adsorption energies when doped with $3d$ and $4d$ atoms, respectively. Figure 7 gives the adsorption and dissociation of an $O_2$ molecule on $Rh_4$, $Rh_3Fe$, $Rh_3Cr$ and $Rh_3Tc$. The energy difference (reaction barrier) between the reactant and the transition state is very important. The lower the barrier is, the easier the reaction will take place. The reaction barrier of pure $Rh_4$ for chemisorption of $O_2$ is 1.69 eV (such an energy difference is quite large). The reaction barriers of $Rh_3Fe$ and $Rh_3Cr$ are 0.7 and 0.27 eV, respectively. The reaction barrier of $Rh_3Tc$ is the smallest and only 0.1 eV (reaction can take place at room temperature). Considering that the adsorption energies of doping Tc and Cr are also the largest, it can be seen that for the chemical adsorption of $Rh_4$ to $O_2$, doping (replacing) Tc or Cr can significantly reduce the reaction barrier and enhance the adsorption capacity, thus can significantly improve the chemical reaction activity of the system with $O_2$.

### 4. Conclusions

In this paper, the ground state geometries and electronic properties of $Rh_3X$ ($X = 3d, 4d$ atom) and $Rh_3X$-M ($M = \text{CO, CO}_2, \text{N}_2, \text{NO, O}_2, \text{N}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{NO}_2$) clusters are systematically investigated by using density

functional theory calculations. As compared to the non-magnetic pure $Rh_4$, most doped $Rh_3X$ clusters have net magnetic moments. $Rh_3Fe$ and $Rh_3Ru$ have the largest magnetic moments ($7\mu_B$ and $5\mu_B$) at different periods. Generally the stability of the pure $Rh_4$ cluster can be obviously enhanced be doping the early transition metal atoms (Sc-V, Y-Tc).

Physical adsorption can be obtained for $Rh_3X$ ($X =$ $3d$, $4d$ atom) when adsorbing $CO$, $CO_2$, $N_2$ and NO. The adsorption energy shows a selective order of $E_{\text{ads}}(\text{NO}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{CO}) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{CO}_2) \geq E_{\text{ads}}(\text{N}_2)$. Compared with pure $Rh_4$, for each doped cluster, the adsorption capacity of CO has not been significantly improved (generally weakened). In most cases, the adsorption energies of $N_2$ and NO have been significantly enhanced. While for $CO_2$, the adsorption energies are all significantly enhanced.

Chemical adsorption can be obtained for $Rh_3X$ ($X = 3d$, $4d$ atom) when adsorbing $O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$. The adsorption energy show a selective order of $E_{\text{ads}}(\text{O}_2) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{NO}_2) > E_{\text{ads}}(\text{N}_2\text{O})$. Compared with pure $Rh_4$, for doped clusters, the adsorption energies with adsorbing $O_2$, $N_2O$ and $NO_2$ are all enhanced. The largest adsorption energy is obtained when $O_2$ is adsorbed. From the transition-state analysis, the energy barrier with doping Tc or Cr atom is substantially reduced, which indicates that the chemical reactivity with $O_2$ on $Rh_4$ can be significantly enhanced by doping Tc or Cr atom.

For chemical adsorption, the dissociation positions of adsorbed molecules are closely related to the positions of the doped atoms. Influences of the doped atoms on the reactions of the molecules are obviously stronger for the early transition metal atoms than the late transition metals (Cu, Zn, Pd-Cd), which can be due to the higher $sp$-$d$ hybridizations around the Fermi level induced by the early transition metals.
