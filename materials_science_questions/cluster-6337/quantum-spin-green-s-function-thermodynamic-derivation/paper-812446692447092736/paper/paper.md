# A NEW TYPE OF AMORPHOUS NONCOLLINEAR FERRIMAGNETIC SYSTEM

T. Kaneyoshi

Department of Physics, Nagoya University, 464-01 Nagoya, Japan

(Received 19 May 1988 by C.W. McCombie)

A new type of amorphous noncollinear mixed ferrimagnetic system consisting of spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ and spin-1 with different transverse fields is proposed. The magnetizations are investigated by the use of effective-field theory with correlations. We find that there is a possibility of changing the shape of magnetization curve from one type (n-type) to another type (p-type), depending on the fluctuation of exchange interaction.

AMORPHOUS rare earth-transition metal ferrimagnetic alloys as well as ferrites and related non-metallic transition-metal compounds in the crystalline state have been the object of a great deal of research, because of their potential for technological applications [1, 2]. They have in common a composition $\mathrm{A}_{x}\mathrm{B}_{1-x}$, which has a strong negative A-B exchange interaction. Here, A and B are different magnetic atoms. By changing their compositions, they have shown one of typical temperature dependences of magnetization, which are classified after Neel [3] as q-type, p-type, l-type and m-type. In particular, there is in a ferrimagnetic an interesting possibility of the existence, under certain conditions, of a compensation point at which the resultant magnetization vanishes. In amorphous ferrimagnetic alloys, the thermomagnetic recording can be carried out at the compensation point, because of the high coercivity around the point.

Theoretically, many problems on the magnetism of amorphous (or disordered) solids can be formulated by introducing simplified yet not completely unrealistic models in which some types of disorder play important ingredients. Fig. 1 shows schematically atomic positions in magnetic binary solids. Black and white circles in the figure denote different magnetic atoms. The figure expresses how amorphous (or disordered) magnetic solids can be made by destroying the periodic lattice structure.

In the previous work [4], we have proposed a new noncollinear ferrimagnetic system with a lattice of Fig. 1(a). The Hamiltonian of the system is given by
$$
\mathscr{H}=\bar{J} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle} \mu_{i}^{z} s_{j}^{z}-\Omega_{0} \sum_{i} \mu_{i}^{x}-\Omega_{1} \sum_{j} s_{j}^{x}, \tag{1}
$$
where $\mu_{i}^{\alpha}$ and $s_{j}^{\alpha}$ ($\alpha=x,z$) are components of spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ and spin-1 operators at sites $i$ and $j$, respectively. $\bar{J}$ is the nearest-neighbour exchange interaction. $\Omega_{0}$ and $\Omega_{1}$ are transverse fields. The first summation is carried out only over nearest-neighbour pairs of spins. By selecting appropriate pair values of $\Omega_{0}$ and $\Omega_{1}$, the system may exhibit a compensation point as well as all kinds of typical temperature dependences of magnetizations usually observed in amorphous (or crystalline) ferrimagnetic alloys [5].

The purpose of this work is to investigate magnetic behaviour of equation (1), when the periodic structure of Fig. 1(a) is destroyed as Fig. 1(b). Then, exchange interaction $\bar{J}$ in equation (1) is assumed to be randomly distributed according to the independent probability distribution function $P(J)$, due to the disorder of network. We find some characteristic behaviours for the magnetizations; with the increase of the fluctuation $\delta$ of exchange interaction, the shape of magnetization curve changes from one type (n-type) to another type (p-type), because of the different temperature dependences of the sublattice magnetizations. The effect of $\delta$ on the total longitudinal and transverse magnetizations is dramatic, when they are measured at a fixed temperature.

Fig. 1. Schematic representation of atomic positions in two-dimensional crystalline and amorphous (or disordered) lattices.

As is discussed by Kaneyoshi *et al.* [4, 6], the longitudinal and transverse sublattice magnetizations are given by, within the effective-field theory with correlations which is superior to the standard mean field theory,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{z}= & \left\langle\left\langle\mu_{i}^{z}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{r}=\left[q_{z}\langle\cosh (\bar{J} D)\rangle_{r}\right. \\
& \left.-m_{z}\langle\sinh (\bar{J} D)\rangle_{r}+\left.1-q_{z}\right|_{x=0} ^{z} f(x)\right|_{x=0},
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{x}= & \left\langle\left\langle\mu_{i}^{x}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{r}=\left[q_{z}\langle\cosh (\bar{J} D)\rangle_{r}\right. \\
& \left.-m_{z}\langle\sinh (\bar{J} D)\rangle_{r}+\left.1-q_{z}\right|_{x=0} ^{z} g(x)\right|_{x=0},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{z}= & \left\langle\left\langle s_{j}^{z}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{r}=\left[\left\langle\cosh \left(\frac{\bar{J}}{2} D\right)\right\rangle_{r}\right. \\
& \left.-2 \sigma_{z}\left\langle\sinh \left(\frac{\bar{J}}{2} D\right)\right\rangle_{r}\right]_{x=0}^{z} F(x)\left.\right|_{x=0},
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{x}= & \left\langle\left\langle s_{j}^{x}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{r}=\left[\left\langle\cosh \left(\frac{\bar{J}}{2} D\right)\right\rangle_{r}\right. \\
& \left.-2 \sigma_{z}\left\langle\sinh \left(\frac{\bar{J}}{2} D\right)\right\rangle_{r}\right]_{x=0}^{z} G(x)\left.\right|_{x=0},
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

and
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{z}= & \left\langle\left\langle\left(s_{j}^{z}\right)^{2}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{r}=\left[\left\langle\cosh \left(\frac{\bar{J}}{2} D\right)\right\rangle_{r}\right. \\
& \left.-2 \sigma_{z}\left\langle\sinh \left(\frac{\bar{J}}{2} D\right)\right\rangle_{r}\right]_{x=0}^{z} H(x)\left.\right|_{x=0},
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
where $D=\partial / \partial x$ is a differential operator and $z$ is the coordination number. $\langle\cdots\rangle_{r}$ denotes the random bond average. The functions of $f(x), g(x), F(x), G(x)$ and $H(x)$ are given by
$$
f(x)=\frac{1}{2} \frac{x}{\left[\Omega_{0}^{2}+x^{2}\right]^{1 / 2}} \tanh \left[\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\Omega_{0}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right], \tag{7}
$$

$$
g(x)=\frac{1}{2} \frac{\Omega_{0}}{\left[\Omega_{0}^{2}+x^{2}\right]^{1 / 2}} \tanh \left[\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\Omega_{0}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right], \tag{8}
$$

$$
F(x)=\frac{x}{\left[\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right]^{1 / 2}} \frac{2 \sinh \left[\beta\left(\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]}{1+2 \cosh \left[\beta\left(\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]},
\tag{9}
$$

$$
G(x)=\frac{\Omega_{1}}{\left[\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right]^{1 / 2}} \frac{2 \sinh \left[\beta\left(\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]}{1+2 \cosh \left[\beta\left(\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]},
\tag{10}
$$

and
$$
H(x)=\frac{\Omega_{1}^{2}+\left(\Omega_{1}^{2}+2 x^{2}\right) \cosh \left[\beta\left(\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]}{\left[\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right]\left\{1+2 \cosh \left[\beta\left(\Omega_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]\right\}},
\tag{11}
$$
where $\beta=1 / k_{B} T$. The total longitudinal and transverse magnetizations are given by
$$
M_{z}=\frac{N}{2}\left(m_{z}+\sigma_{z}\right), \tag{12}
$$

and
$$
M_{x}=\frac{N}{2}\left(m_{x}+\sigma_{x}\right), \tag{13}
$$
where $N$ is the total number of magnetic atoms.

Now, in order to evaluate the total magnetizations, it is necessary to provide the appropriate form of the probability distribution function $P(\bar{J})$, describing the structural disorder in a simple way. In a series of works [7] we have used the probability distribution function $P(\bar{J})$ as
$$
P(\bar{J})=\frac{1}{2}[\delta(\bar{J}-J-\Delta J)+\delta(\bar{J}-J+\Delta J)],
\tag{14}
$$
with
$$
\delta=\frac{\Delta J}{J}, \tag{15}
$$
where $\delta$ is a dimensionless parameter which measures the amount of fluctuation of exchange interaction. Then, the random-bond average in equations (2)-(6) are given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \langle\cosh (\gamma \bar{J} D)\rangle_{r}=\cosh (\gamma J \delta D) \cosh (\gamma J D), \\
& \langle\sinh (\gamma \bar{J} D)\rangle_{r}=\cosh (\gamma J \delta D) \sinh (\gamma J D),
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
with $\gamma=1$ or $\frac{1}{2}$. The result (16) can be also obtained by using the so called "lattice model" of amorphous magnets [8]. The above equations can be easily evaluated by applying a mathematic relation $\mathrm{e}^{x D} \phi(x)=$ $\phi(x+\alpha)$.

In Fig. 2, we show one of the previous results (the curves labelled $\delta=0.0$ ) for the regular system with $z=4$ [Fig. (1a)] in [4], when $\Omega_{0}$ and $\Omega_{1}$ are selected as $\Omega_{0}=0.05 \mathrm{~J}$ and $\Omega_{1}=3.65 \mathrm{~J}$. The $M_{z}$ curve clearly exhibits a compensation temperature at $k_{B} T_{\text {comp }}=$ $0.29 \mathrm{~J}$. In Fig. 2 the effects of $\delta$ on the magnetization curves are investigated, namely when the regular lattice Fig. 1(a) is deformed to Fig. 1(b). As is seen in Fig. $2, T_{\text {comp }}$ for the $M_{z}$ curve labelled $\delta=0.2$ increases from that for $\delta=0.0$, although the Curie temperature $T_{c}$ decreases from that for $\delta=0.0$. A particularly interesting behaviour appears in the $M_{z}$ curve labelled $\delta=0.2$, below $T=T_{\text {comp }}$ the absolute value of $M_{z}$ increases from that for $\delta=0.0$, because of the different temperature dependences of the sublattice magnetizations. Furthermore, when $\delta$ becomes $\delta=$ 0.4 , the $M_{z}$ curve does not exhibit any compensation point. Thus, the result indicates that there is a possibility of changing the shape of magnetization curve from one type ( $n$-type) to another type ( $p$-type) with the change of $\delta$.

Finally, we may conclude by saying that the new type of amorphous noncollinear ferrimagnetic system proposed in this work may be a simple but fruitful system, from the material science point of view. Such an investigation may open the new field of research academically and technologically.
