# One-dimensional $XY$ antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model with nearest and next nearest neighbour exchange interactions

M. E. Gouvêa $^{*}^{1}$ and A. S. T. Pires $^{2}$

1 Departamento de Ciências Básicas, DES, Centro Federal de Educação Tecnológica de Minas Gerais, Belo Horizonte, MG, Brazil
2 Departamento de Física, ICEx, Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais, Belo Horizonte, CP 702, CEP 30123-970, MG, Brazil

Received 1 February 2005, revised 29 April 2005, accepted 5 July 2005
Published online 22 August 2005

PACS 75.10.Jm, 75.30.Kz, 75.40.Cx, 75.50.Ee

We study the one-dimensional anisotropic antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model with competing interactions using the self-consistent harmonic approximation. The dispersion relation, the correlation function and the susceptibility are obtained for several values of the ratio $\delta=J_{2} / J_{1}$ between the nearest $(J_{1})$ and next nearest neighbour exchange interactions $(J_{2})$. For $\delta<0.25$, the pair correlation function decays monotonically and exponentially to zero and the susceptibility has a maximum value at $q=\pi$. For $J_{2}>0.25 J_{1}$, the exponential decay of the pair correlation is modified by an oscillatory behavior while the maximum of the susceptibility does not necessarily occur at $q=\pi$. Furthermore, the phase diagram of a system with weakly coupled chains is considered.

## 1 Introduction

Low dimensional quantum models have been the subject of considerable research; analytical, numerical, and also experimental. There are several reasons for such interest: (i) magnetic models with reduced dimensionality have revealed a somewhat unconventional physics - as, for example, a quantitative difference for the behavior of one-dimensional (1D) antiferromagnets with integer and half-integer spins [1]; (ii) the quantum field theoretical methods used in the analysis of the problem [2]; and, (iii) the progress achieved in synthesizing materials with quasi-one and two-dimensional behavior. In particular, a great deal of attention has been devoted to the study of models with competing interactions and quantum fluctuations. It is well known that the properties of quantum systems are strongly affected by fluctuations that can, for example, drive a one dimensional Heisenberg antiferromagnet with only nearest neighbor interactions (nn) to a lattice dimerization characterizing a spin-Peierls transition [3, 4]. On the other hand, it has been shown [5-7] that magnetic dimerization can also be induced by the so-called frustation effect involving competing interactions. Experimental investigations realized on quasi-one dimensional materials have provided crucial support for the interesting physics related to low dimensional magnetism and raised questions about the role played by effects like competing interactions and quantum fluctuations.

The anisotropic Heisenberg model, with easy-plane anisotropy, and the $XY$ model belong to a very special class of models impacting a wide range of situations, from particle field theory to materials

Usually, condensed matter systems are easier to understand than their high energy counterparts and, then, we can use the analogies between different systems to shine some light into the behavior of systems which are otherwise experimentally and theoretically intractable.

From the theoretical point of view, the nearest neighbor model is the most used model and it can de- scribe, to some extent, several features of the interesting behavior shown by low-dimensional quantum magnets. Experimentally, however, the lattice structure of several compounds have suggested the exis- tence of a substancial competing interaction [9], and it is necessary to get a better understanding of mod- els including competition.

One-dimensional (1D) models with $nn$, $J_1$, and next-nearest-neighbor ($nnn$), $J_2$, interactions have also deserved considerable attention: depending on the sign of the two related exchange interactions, the model will exhibit the so called frustation - competition - effects. In these cases, it is known that the structure of the short-range order phase may be more complex [10, 11], suffering a notice- able change at a definite temperature $T_D$, associated to a disorder point. In order to investigate the dependence of the disorder point with the existence or not of an ordered phase, Stephenson [11] investigated two one-dimensional Ising chains: one with alternate $nnn$ interactions and another one with both $nnn$ interactions present. He obtained exact expressions for the pair correlation on these two types of Ising classical chains concluding that, depending on the sign and on the ratio between the $nn$ and $nnn$ exchange interactions, there will be a disorder point. Later, Harada, using the trans- fer matrix method, obtained the thermodynamic properties of the classical 1D Ising [12] and planar [13] models with ferromagnetic $nn$ interactions, and antiferromagnetic $nnn$ interactions. For the 1D-Ising classical model, Harada showed that the Hamiltonian including $nn$ and $nnn$ interactions is transformed into the Ising model with $nn$ interactions in an applied magnetic field. He also investigated the wave-number dependence of the longitudinal susceptibility $\chi_{1D}^{\parallel}$ finding that for $1/4<\delta<1/2$, where $\delta=|J_2|/J_1$, there is a definite temperature $T_L$ below which $\chi_{1D}^{\parallel}$ has a maximum value at a wave-number $q_m=0$ while, above $T_L$, the maximum of $\chi_{1D}^{\parallel}$ occurs at $q_m\neq0$. This tem- perature $T_L$ is different from the disorder temperature $T_D$, above which the pair correlation functions decay in an oscillatory way as the spin separation is increased. For the planar chain, the model studied by Harada [13] also considers $J_1>0$ and $J_2<0$ for, respectively, $nn$ and $nnn$ interactions. Harada dis- cussed the appearance of helical spin order with an associated wave-number $\alpha=\pm\cos^{-1}(0.25\delta^{-1})$ and its signature in the behavior of the heat capacity and of the susceptibility with temperature. The thermody- namic quantities of the one-dimensional classical Heisenberg model with $nn$ and $nnn$ exchange interac- tions was analysed by Harada and Mikeska [14]: also in this case, the system exhibits helical short range order.

Due to the variety of magnetic phases in frustated quantum spin chains, many numerical and ana- lytical works have also investigated the ordering of quantum systems. The ground-state properties of the 1D isotropic spin-1/2 Heisenberg antiferromagnet with competing interactions was investigated by Tonegawa and co-workers [15, 16]. They found that, when the value of $\delta$ increases from 0, the ground state of the infinite-size system undergoes the gapless to finite-gap phase transition at a critical value $\delta_c$. For the Heisenberg chain, the value of $\delta_c$ was estimated to be $\delta_c\approx0.241$ [16]. The $S=1$ $XY$ and Heisenberg chains have also been studied by many authors [17-19]. More recently, Ka- buragi et al. [19] found that the $S=1$ $XY$ chain exhibits both gapless and gapped chiral phases character- ized by the spontaneous breaking of parity whereas the the spin correlation decays algebraically or expo- nentially.

In our model, the competition is assured by choosing both interactions, the first- and second-neighbor, to be antiferromagnetic. We will obtain the dispersion relation, the spin-pair correlation functions and the susceptibility, for a wide range of values of $\delta$, the modulus of the ratio between $nnn$ and $nn$ interactions, by using an approximate method known as self-consistent harmonic approximation (SCHA). The forma- lism is valid for $S>1/2$. Section I is devoted to the description of the model and to the development of the main steps related to the SCHA. Our results are discussed in Section 2, and, finally, we present our conclusions in Section 3.

## 2 The model and the SCHA

The Hamiltonian describing our model is
$$
H=J_{1} \sum_{j}\left[S_{j}^{x} S_{j+1}^{x}+S_{j}^{y} S_{j+1}^{y}\right]+J_{2} \sum_{j}\left[S_{j}^{x} S_{j+2}^{x}+S_{j}^{y} S_{j+2}^{y}\right],\qquad(1)
$$
where the subscript $j$ runs over the sites of a linear lattice, $J_{1}$ and $J_{2}$ are, respectively, the $nn$ and $nnn$ exchange constants. In our problem, $J_{1}$ and $J_{2}$ are both positive. The value of $J_{1}$ is fixed while $J_{2}$ is varied so that the ratio $\delta$ goes from zero to one. This lattice can be regarded as a piece of the triangular lattice and is called the railroad trestle lattice by Anderson [20].

Due to the symmetry of the system, it is convenient to apply the Villain representation to Hamiltonian (1). The Villain representation is given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
& S_{j}^{+}=\mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{j}} \sqrt{\left(S+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(S_{j}^{z}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}, \\
& S_{j}^{-}=\sqrt{\left(S+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(S_{j}^{z}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}} \mathrm{e}^{-i \phi_{j}},
\end{aligned}\qquad(2)
$$
where $S$ is the spin value. In our work, we will use $S=1$. The transformed Hamiltonian is written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & J_{1} \sum_{j}\left\{\left(S+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2} \sqrt{1-\left(\frac{S_{j}^{z}+\frac{1}{2}}{S+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}} \sqrt{1-\left(\frac{S_{j+1}^{z}+\frac{1}{2}}{S+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}} \cos \left(\phi_{j}-\phi_{j+1}\right)\right\} \\
& +J_{2} \sum_{j}\left\{\left(S+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2} \sqrt{1-\left(\frac{S_{j}^{z}+\frac{1}{2}}{S+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}} \sqrt{1-\left(\frac{S_{j+2}^{z}+\frac{1}{2}}{S+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}} \cos \left(\phi_{j}-\phi_{j+2}\right)\right\}.
\end{aligned}\qquad(3)
$$

Minimizing the ground state energy of the system we obtain that, depending on the value assumed by $\delta$, neighboring spins may not be aligned according to a Nèel state configuration. This is caused by the competition between $nn$ and $nnn$ exchange interactions leading to a helical order, as also found by Harada [13] in his study of the classical one-dimensional planar model with competing interactions. One can easily show that, for $\delta \geq 0.25$, the helical order – also known as helimagnetism – has an associated wave number $\alpha=\cos ^{-1}\left(0.25 \delta^{-1}\right)$; for $\delta<0.25$, the ground state exhibits antiparallel alignment. The spiral pitch will not, in general, be commensurate with the lattice parameter and, so, no two spins in the chain will have exactly the same spin direction. It is, then, convenient to re-define the phase used in the Villain representation using
$$
\phi_{j}=\psi_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\left[1-(-1)^{j}\right] \pi-i \alpha.\qquad(4)
$$

Here, we will investigate the behavior of Hamiltonian (1) by using the self-consistent harmonic approximation (SCHA). The SCHA is a simple approximate method that gives reasonable agreement with more sophisticated theories and, also, with Monte Carlo data [21]. For this reason, the method has been widely used in the literature (see, for example, Ref. [22]). The basic idea of the method is to replace the Hamiltonian describing the model by an effective harmonic Hamiltonian with temperature dependent renormalized parameters usually called spin stiffness [23]. There is an extensive literature describing the SCHA procedure [24, 27], and, for this reason, we will go straight to the final expression for the effective Hamiltonian, skipping the intermediate steps. After using (4) on (3), we expand the resulting Hamil-

tonian in terms of $(S_{j}^{z}+1/2)^{2}$ and $(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j'})^{2}$. Then, an usual normal ordering procedure is applied and, collecting the quadratic terms, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
H=E_{0}+ & \sum_{q}\left\{S^{2}\left[J_{1} \rho_{1} \cos \alpha(1-\cos q)-J_{2} \rho_{2} \cos 2 \alpha(1-\cos 2 q)\right] \psi_{q} \psi_{-q}\right. \\
& \left.-\left[J_{1}+J_{2}\right] S_{q}^{z} S_{-q}^{z}\right\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

for the Fourier transform of the harmonic Hamiltonian. In Eq. (5), we have $E_{0}=-N S^{2}[J_{1} \cos \alpha-J_{2} \cos 2 \alpha]$, and $\rho_{1}$ and $\rho_{2}$ are the spin stiffnesses defined by the following expressions

$$
\rho_{1}=\left(1-\left\langle\left(\frac{S_{j}^{z}}{S}\right)^{2}\right\rangle\right) \exp \left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j+1}\right)^{2}\right\rangle\right\},
\tag{6}
$$

$$
\rho_{2}=\left(1-\left\langle\left(\frac{S_{j}^{z}}{S}\right)^{2}\right\rangle\right) \exp \left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j+2}\right)^{2}\right\rangle\right\}.
\tag{7}
$$

The averages appearing in Eqs. (6) and (7) can be calculated if we write

$$
\psi_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left\{\frac{J_{1}+J_{2}}{c(q)}\right\}^{1 / 4}\left[a_{q}^{\dagger}+a_{-q}\right],
\tag{8}
$$

$$
S_{q}^{z}=\imath \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left\{\frac{c(q)}{J_{1}+J_{2}}\right\}^{1 / 4}\left[a_{q}^{\dagger}-a_{-q}\right],
\tag{9}
$$

where $a_{q}^{\dagger}$ and $a_{q}$ are the creation and annihilation operators, and we defined $c(q)$ as

$$
c(q)=S^{2}\left[J_{1} \rho_{1} \cos \alpha(1-\cos q)-J_{2} \rho_{2} \cos 2 \alpha(1-\cos 2 q)\right].
\tag{10}
$$

This procedure is standard in spin wave theories and leads us to the expressions for the terms appearing in Eqs. (6) and (7),

$$
\left\langle\left(\frac{S_{j}^{z}}{S}\right)^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k} \sqrt{\frac{c(q)}{J_{1}+J_{2}}}\left(n(q)+\frac{1}{2}\right),
\tag{11}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j+1}\right)^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k} \frac{1-\cos (q)}{2} \sqrt{\frac{J_{1}+J_{2}}{c(q)}}\left(n(q)+\frac{1}{2}\right),
\tag{12}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j+2}\right)^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k} \frac{1-\cos (2 q)}{2} \sqrt{\frac{J_{1}+J_{2}}{c(q)}}\left(n(q)+\frac{1}{2}\right),
\tag{13}
$$

where $n(q)$ is the boson occupation number. We also obtain the renormalized spin wave dispersion relation

$$
\omega(q)=2 \sqrt{\left(J_{1}+J_{2}\right) c(q)}.
\tag{14}
$$

Figure 1 shows the results for $\omega(q)$ vs. $q$ for temperature $T=0.15 J_{1}$ and for $\delta$ varying from $\delta=0.01$ to $\delta=1.0$. It can be seen that, for $\delta<0.5$, the dispersion relation increases smoothly with $q$, reaching a maximum at $q_{m}=\pi$. ## 3 Results

The SCHA enables us to obtain the pair correlation function

$$
\left\langle\cos\left(\phi_0-\phi_l\right)\right\rangle=(-1)^l\cos(l\alpha)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}k\frac{1-\cos lk}{2}\sqrt{\frac{J_1+J_2}{c(k)}\left(n(k)+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right\}
\tag{15}
$$

and, thus, the expression for the susceptibility

$$
\chi_{1\mathrm{D}}(q)=\frac{g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{T}\sum_{l}\mathrm{e}^{iql}\left\langle\cos\left(\phi_0-\phi_l\right)\right\rangle,
\tag{16}
$$

where we have taken $k_{\mathrm{B}}=1$ for the Boltzmann constant.

The behavior of $|\langle\cos(\phi_0-\phi_l)\rangle|$ as the distance $l$ between the spins increases is shown in Figs. 2-4. The correlation function (in fact, its absolute value) for $\delta\leq0.24$ decays exponentially with increasing spin separation; in Fig. 2, we show the results for $\delta=0.24$ and three different temperatures, $T=0.10J_1$; $0.20J_2$ and $0.30J_1$. As the competition between $nn$ and $nnn$ interactions increases, the behavior of the correlation function changes to an oscillatory decay. This oscillatory mode of decay can be seen in Figs. 3 and 4. In Fig. 3, we show the data for several values of $\delta$ at $T=0.10J_1$, and, in Fig. 4, the correlation function for $\delta=0.30$ for different temperatures is plotted. A similar change of behavior was found by Harada [12] for the 1D Ising model with competing interactions.

Harada also analysed 1D classical planar model with competing interactions [13], but he did not show results for the pair correlation function as a function of spin distance. Instead, he presented results for the correlation function between nearest spin neighbors, $S_{01}=\langle S_lS_{l+1}\rangle$, and between next nearest spin neighbors, $S_{02}=\langle S_lS_{l+2}\rangle$, as a function of temperature and of the $\delta$ ratio. It has to be emphasized that the planar model used by Harada, besides being classical, includes a different kind of competition; in his model, the $nn$ exchange interaction is ferromagnetic while the $nnn$ interaction is antiferromagnetic. ## 4 Phase diagram

We will now discuss the phase diagram of quasi-one-dimensional systems. A conceptually simple ap- proach to treat systems with different intrachain and interchain $J'$ couplings was proposed by Scalapino et al. [26] (in our model, the intrachain couplings are $J_1$ and $J_2$). In their procedure, the interchain coupl- ing is considered according to a mean-field approximation while the effective 1D problem is treated as exactly as possible. This technique has been widely used in the literature [27-29] and the description obtained for the ordered state has produced quantitative predictions for quantities that have been success-

However, it has been shown [32] that, when the system's dynamics is the focus, different approximation procedures can lead to quite dif- ferent results, and, thus, much more care is required.

For the model described by Hamiltonian (1), we do not have an exact expression for the 1D chain and, then, we will use the result given by the SCHA that has been adopted in this work. As proposed by Scalapino et al. [26], the interchain interaction will be treated in a mean field approximation leading to the following expression for the susceptibility of the quasi-1D system

$$
\chi_{3 \mathrm{D}}(\boldsymbol{q})=\frac{\chi_{1 \mathrm{D}}\left(q_{z}\right)}{1-J^{\prime}\left(q_{\perp}\right) \chi_{1 \mathrm{D}}\left(q_{z}\right)},\tag{17}
$$

where $q_{z}$ and $q_{\perp}$ are, respectively, the wavevector components along and perpendicular to the chain, $J'(q_{\perp})=J'\sum_{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{iq_{\perp}\xi}$, where $\xi$ is the vector connecting each site of the chain to its $nn$ neighbors in neighbor- ing chains.

The critical temperature $T_{c}$ corresponds to the highest temperature at which the susceptibility Eq. (16) diverges, that is

$$
\chi_{1 \mathrm{D}}\left(q_{z}\right)=\frac{1}{J^{\prime}(0)}.\tag{18}
$$

Our results for the dependence of the critical temperature on $\delta$ for $J'=0.025J_{1}$ are given in Fig. 9; the full circles correspond to our calculation and the dotted line joining them is just a guide to the eye. The line separating the antiferromagnetic phase and the modulated phase is shown approximately. The Lif- shitz point $P$ separating the three phases, paramagnetic, antiferromagnetic and modulated, is given by the point where $q_{m}$ becomes different from $\pi$.

## 5 Conclusion

Using the SCHA, we obtained the behavior of the in-plane pair correlation function and of the longitudi- nal susceptibility for the one-dimensional $XY$ model with nearest-neighbor and next-nearest-neighbor interactions. In our model, both interactions, $J_{1}$ and $J_{2}$, are antiferromagnetic and, thus, depending on the strength of $\delta$, a competition is established. This competition leads to a helical order with a characteristic wave number $\alpha=\cos^{-1}[1/(4\delta)]$. The procedure adopted here for the $XY$ model can be easily imple- mented, with slight modifications, for the planar anisotropic Heisenberg model.

The in-plane correlation function, $|\langle\cos(\phi_{0}-\phi_{l})\rangle|$, decays exponentially with increasing spin distance for $0<\delta<0.25$. For $\delta>0.25$, the correlation function oscillates in mode of decay. A change in behavior

at $\delta=0.25$ is also observed for the susceptibility. For $0<\delta<0.25$, the maximum of the $\chi_{1 \mathrm{D}} T \times q$ occurs at $q=\pi$ but for $\delta>0.25$ the maximum occurs at $q_{m}=\alpha$. We also observed that there is a $\delta$ range $(0.25<\delta<0.50)$ in which the maximum of $\chi_{1 \mathrm{D}}$ moves towards $q_{m}=\pi$ as the temperature increases, and a $\delta$ range, $(\delta>0.50)$ where the maximum position does no move as the temperature is changed, staying at $q_{m}=\alpha$.

We have also discussed the phase diagram of a system with weakly coupled chains where each chain is described by Eq. (1). Adopting a mean-field approximation for the interchain interactions, we obtained the critical curve defining the antiferromagnetic, the modulated and the paramagnetic phases. The Lif- shitz point was also identified.

We hope our results can stimulate experimental investigations on quasi-one-dimensional magnets as, for example, the compound $\mathrm{FeMgBO}_{4}$. In this compound [33,34], the $n n n$ interaction has the same order of magnitude as the $n n$ interaction because of the zig-zag form of the magnetic chain.
