# Numerical Study of Light Fermion DOS in a Correlated Binary Alloy

## Problem background
This task investigates a quantum mixture of spin-polarized light (e.g., 6Li) and heavy (e.g., 40K) fermionic atoms confined to a finite two-dimensional optical lattice. The heavy atoms are considered immobile relative to the light atoms, so they act as a scattering environment for the light particles. The system is described by the Falicov-Kimball model, where the light atoms hop between lattice sites while the heavy atoms provide a local interaction. The central open question is how the scattering by correlated heavy atoms modifies the spectrum of the light atoms — specifically, under what conditions a spectral gap may open. Computing the density of states (DOS) of the light fermions as a function of temperature and interaction strength is the primary objective.

## Approach
The model is mapped onto a correlated binary alloy (CBA) representation. In this picture the heavy atoms are described by Ising spin variables S(r) = ±1 (occupied/unoccupied), and the light atoms move in a random potential generated by the Ising configuration. The distribution of Ising spins is not purely thermal but depends on the light-atom Hamiltonian through a determinant-based weight, creating correlations among the spins. The DOS of the light atoms is computed by combining Monte Carlo sampling of the Ising configurations with exact diagonalization of the single-particle Hamiltonian for each configuration. For a given set of model parameters (interaction strength U, chemical potential, temperature), many uncorrelated Ising configurations are generated using a Metropolis algorithm that uses the weighted distribution. For each configuration, the real-space light-atom Hamiltonian is diagonalized to obtain the set of single-particle energies, and a configuration-resolved DOS histogram is constructed. The final ensemble DOS is obtained by averaging the histograms over many configurations. The numerical workflow is split into two stages: first, generating and storing the Ising configurations; second, diagonalizing the Hamiltonians, averaging the DOS, and outputting the final energy-resolved DOS curves for all required parameter sets.

## Reproduction target

- U = 3, β ∈ {3, 7, 10, 14}

Produce a single output file containing the energy bins and corresponding averaged DOS values for every condition. The result will reveal how the light-atom spectrum changes with temperature and interaction strength; no additional derived quantities (e.g., spin averages) are required.

## How you are scored
Your submission is evaluated by a hidden verifier that independently checks the output file `dos_results.json`. The verifier extracts the density-of-states curves for each listed condition and computes the integrated spectral weight in the energy window [-1.0, 1.0] around the Fermi level. It then evaluates two structural diagnostics:

- For U=3, across β = 3, 7, 10, 14, the integrated spectral weight must strictly decrease with each step (monotonic trend), which earns up to 0.5 points.
- The integrated spectral weight for (U=3, β=14) and for (U=8, β=3) must each fall below a small threshold (0.05), indicating a gapped state. The (3,14) condition earns up to 0.2 additional points and (8,3) up to 0.3 additional points.

The overall score is the sum of these components, capped at 1.0. There is no curve-level RMSE comparison to a hidden reference; only the integrated spectral weight diagnostics are used. Reporting values from the paper without producing the actual computed arrays does not satisfy the task; only your submitted numerical data is evaluated.
