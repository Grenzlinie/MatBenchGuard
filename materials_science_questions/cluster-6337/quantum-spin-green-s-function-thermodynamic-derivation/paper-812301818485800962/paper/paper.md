# On the Theory of the Two-Dimensional Heisenberg
Antiferromagnet with Frustration on a Square Lattice

A. F. BARABANOV and V. M. BERESOVSKY

Institute for High Pressure Physics, 142092, Troitsk, Moscow

(Received August 25, 1993)

The two-dimentional frustrated $S=1/2$ Heisenberg antiferromagnet is studied with a Green function decoupling method in a spherical approach. The second order phase transition from long-range order state to spin liquid state is found at $\lambda=J_{2}/J_{1}=0.12$ where $J_{1},J_{2}$ describe the nearest and next nearest neighbors antiferromagnetic interaction. The temperature dependence of correlation functions $C_{p}$, spin spectrum gap and uniform susceptibility $\chi$ is presented before and after transition. The dependence of $\chi$ on $\lambda$ and $T$ turns out close to the dependence of $\chi$ on doping parameter $x$ and $T$ in high-$T_{\mathrm{c}}$ superconductors. The discussion points out the importance of detailed dependence of $C_{p}$ on $T$ and $\lambda$ for the hole spectrum of doped $\mathrm{CuO}_{2}$ plane.

## §1. Introduction

Two-dimensional spin systems are studied extensively in connection with high-temperature superconductors (HTSCs). It is well known that many features of HTSCs are determined by doped $\mathrm{CuO}_{2}$ planes. The magnetic subsystem of undoped $\mathrm{CuO}_{2}$ plane is well described by the two-dimensional (2D) antiferromagnetic (AFM) $S=1/2$ Heisenberg model with interaction between nearest neighbors (NN) spins on the square lattice. $^{1,2)}$ On the other hand, the hole doping causes frustration in spin subsystem, i.e. AFM interaction with next nearest neighbors (NNN). $^{3,4)}$ Then the $S=1/2$ spin Hamiltonian of $\mathrm{CuO}_{2}$ plane has the form:

$$
H=\frac{1}{2}\left(J_{1} \sum_{i, \boldsymbol{g}} S_{i} S_{j+\boldsymbol{g}}+J_{2} \sum_{i, \boldsymbol{d}} S_{i} S_{i+\boldsymbol{d}}\right),\quad (1)
$$

where $J_{1},J_{2}>0$ describe the interaction between NN (vectors $\boldsymbol{g}$) and NNN (vecors $\boldsymbol{d}=\pm \boldsymbol{g}_{x} \pm \boldsymbol{g}_{y}$) sites on the square lattice.

The Hamiltonian (1) was studied intensively by various methods. Substantional progress in the study of the model for $J_{2}=0$ was achieved in recent works, $^{5-8)}$ where it was shown that there is an exponentionally small (along $T$) gap in the spectrum of spin excitations and that a long-range order (LRO) arises at $T=0$.

There have been remarkable developments in study on the frustrated model from the point of view of the transition from checkerboard spin structure (CBSS) to stripe spin structure (SSS). Among different approaches we want to mention numerical calculations on blocks up to 20 sites, $^{4,9-11)}$ conventional spin-wave (CSW) $^{4,11-13)}$ and modifide spin-wave (MSW) $^{14-16)}$ approaches.

One of the interesting questions concerns the possibility of the second order phase transition from long range order state (LROS) to "spin liquid state'' (SLS) as frustration parameter $\lambda=J_{2}/J_{1}$ increases.

For our quantum case $S=1/2$ the CSW theory indicates that the sublattice magnetization becomes negative in some region in parameter $\lambda$: $\lambda_{1S}<\lambda<\lambda_{2S}$, $\lambda_{1S}\approx 0.38$, $\lambda_{2S}\approx 0.5$. This suggests the possibility of existence of SLS in the mentioned region. $^{11,13)}$ (Let us note that in classical limit $S \to \infty$ this theory predicts a transition from CBSS to SSS at $\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=0.5$.)

The MSW theory indicates that the ground state is either CBSS for small $J_{2}$ or SSS for small $J_{1}$. But between $\lambda=0.55$ and 0.62 there is a coexisting region of CBSS and SSS. Since these LRO states exclude each other, the ground state in that region should exhibit some kind of disorder.

Different results were obtained by using Jastrow variational wave function. It was found, $^{3,17)}$ that at $\lambda>0.24$ SLS gains energy in

comparison to LROS, but LROS-SLS transi- tion is the first order one. The posibility of the second order phase transition at $\lambda \approx 0.25$ was manifested in the approach based on the block method. $^{18)}$ Such a transition is accompained without fall by the appearence of the finite gap in the spin excitation spectrum of SLS and by the loss of LRO. Let us mention that the block method and the variational Jastrow wave func- tion for SLS preserve the rotational invariance in contrast to CSW and MSW approaches.

In this paper we intend to investigate the properties of the model in the wide range of temperature. This question is interesting of it- self. Besides that as it's pointed out in the last section the detailed information about the de- pendence of the correlation functions on $T$ and $\lambda$ is important for the theory of magnetic polaron in $CuO_{2}$ plane.

It's obvious that CSW theory isn't able to describe 2D AFM at finite temperature. We don't use the MSW approach because it isn't a good approximation in the high temperature region, as it's known from the investigation at $\lambda=0$ : the MSW theory doesn't reproduce a broad peak in the dependence of susceptibility on $T.^{8)}$ As to the block method $^{18)}$ it seems rather complicated to be used at $T \neq 0$ .

There is also the theory of 2D AFM which employes the Schwinger boson representa- tion. $^{5)}$ In principle this theory enables to inves tigate the model at arbitrary $T$ . However, it's known that this approach overestimates the values of correlation functions for a factor3/2.

In the present treatment of the model we in- volve a spherically symmetric spin wave the- ory, $^{19,20)}$ which seems to be successful both at low and at high temperatures. In particular, the results for the unfrustrated model indicate that this theory leads to a qualitatively correct behavior of susceptibility and the spin-wave ex- citations over whole temperature region: $^{19)}$ in the zero temperature limit the spin-wave excita- tions are gapless and we have LROS; as the temperature increases the temperature depen- dent gap appears in the spin excitation spec- trum, the correlation length becomes shorter and at high temperature limit each spin fluctu- ates almost independently of each other.

The adopted approach is based on a Green's function decoupling procedure. As a conse- quence it isn't free from some shortcomings. Namely, two decoupling parameters $(\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}$ , see $\S 2$ ) have to be introduced in order to fulfill the sum rule and to improve the value of sub- lattice magnetization $m_{0}$ at $\lambda=0, T=0$ . In this sense presented theory is a semi-phenomeno- logical one.

In $\S 2$ we introduce the two-time retarded Green functions, which allow us to get the spin excitation spectrum and correlation func- tions. Considering the equations of motion for Green functions we use the decoupling scheme, which involves the same approxima-tions, as used by Shimahara and Takada. $^{19)}$  Within this framework in $\S 3$ we obtain the numerical results for sublattice magnetization m, correlation functions, spectrum gap and uniform spin susceptibility as functions of the parameter $\lambda$ and temperature. It turns out that the LROS-SLS phase transition takes place at $\lambda ≈0.12$ . Section 4 is devoted to summary and discussion of our results consequences for hole spectrum of $CuO_{2}$ plane.

## §2. The Equation of Motion and Decoupling Scheme for Green Functions

For operators $A, B$ we consider the two time retarded Green functions and their Fou-rier transforms $\langle A / B\rangle$ :

$$
\langle A \mid B\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \mathrm{d} t \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \omega t}\langle A \mid B\rangle_{t}=-\mathrm{i} \int_{0}^{\infty} \mathrm{d} t \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \omega t}\langle[A(t), B]\rangle, \quad (2)
$$

where $\langle\cdots\rangle$ means the thermal average.

Let us introduce the notations for the two-spin Green function and corresponding correlation functions:

$$
G_{i j}^{\sigma}(\omega)=\left\langle S_{i}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle ; \quad G_{q}^{\sigma}(\omega)=\sum_{R_{i j}} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} q R_{i j}} G_{i j}^{\sigma}(\omega), \quad (3)
$$

$$
\left\langle S_{i}^{\sigma} S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle=N^{-1} \sum_{q} \mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i} q R_{i j}} C_{q}^{\sigma} ;
$$

$$
C_{q}^{\sigma}=-1 / \pi \int \mathrm{d} \omega[1+n(\omega)] \operatorname{Im}\left\{G_{q}^{\sigma}(\omega+\mathrm{i} \varepsilon)\right\} ;
$$

$$
n(\omega)=(\exp (\omega / T)-1)^{-1},
$$

where $\sigma= \pm 1$ is a spin projection index.

The equation of motion for $G_{i j}^{\sigma}(\omega)$ is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega G_{i j}^{\sigma}(\omega)= & 2 \sigma \delta_{i j}\left\langle S_{i}^{z}\right\rangle+\sigma \sum_{g_{1}}\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle-\left\langle S_{i+g_{1}}^{z} S_{i}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle \\
& +\sigma \lambda \sum_{d_{1}}\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle-\left\langle S_{i+d_{1}}^{z} S_{i}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle,
\end{aligned}
$$

here and below we take $J_{1}=1$.

Since we treat a spherical symmetric approach, the average $\left\langle S^{z}\right\rangle=0$, and we have to examine the equation of motion for four Green functions in the right hand side of (5). These equations for the first and the third Green functions are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle= & -\sigma\left\langle S_{j}^{-\sigma} S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma}\right\rangle \delta_{i j}+2 \sigma\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{j}^{z}\right\rangle \delta_{i+g_{1}, j} \\
& +\sum_{r=g_{2}, d_{2}} \frac{\sigma}{2} \lambda_{r}\left\{\left\langle\left(S_{i}^{\sigma} S_{i+r}^{-\sigma}-S_{i+r}^{\sigma} S_{i}^{-\sigma}\right) S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle\right. \\
& \left.+2\left\langle S_{i}^{z}\left(S_{i+g_{1}}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}+r}^{\sigma}-S_{i+g_{1}+r}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma}\right) \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle\right\},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle= & -\sigma\left\langle S_{j}^{-\sigma} S_{i+d_{1}}^{\sigma}\right\rangle \delta_{i j}+2 \sigma\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{j}^{z}\right\rangle \delta_{i+d_{1}, j} \\
& +\sum_{r=g_{2}, d_{2}} \frac{\sigma}{2} \lambda_{r}\left\{\left\langle\left(S_{i}^{\sigma} S_{i+r}^{-\sigma}-S_{i+r}^{\sigma} S_{i}^{-\sigma}\right) S_{i+d_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle\right. \\
& \left.+2\left\langle S_{i}^{z}\left(S_{i+d_{1}}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}+r}^{\sigma}-S_{i+d_{1}+r}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}}^{\sigma}\right) \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle\right\},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\lambda_{g_{2}}=1, \lambda_{d_{2}}=\lambda$.

The equation of motion for the remaining second and fourth Green functions of eq. (5) can be obtained by the site indexes transpositions $i \leftrightarrow i+g_{1}$ in (6.1) and $i \leftrightarrow i+d_{1}$ in (6.2).

For new Green functions in the right hand sides of the eqs. (6.1), (6.2) and the equations given by the transpositions mentioned above, we use the same decoupling procedure, as introduced by Shimahara and Takada. $^{19)}$ We decouple the operators only on the different sites in order to retain the local correlations. We take into account the ambiguity in decoupling approximation by introducing the parameters $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{2}$: $\alpha_{1}$ is used for decoupling the pair of operators in NN sites, $\alpha_{2}$ - for more distant sites. For example:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}+r}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle=\alpha_{1}\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}}^{z}\right\rangle\left\langle S_{i+g_{1}+r}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle, \quad g_{1} \neq-r ; \\
\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}+r}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle=\alpha_{2}\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}}^{z}\right\rangle\left\langle S_{i+d_{1}+r}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle, \quad d_{1} \neq-r ; \\
\left\langle\left(S_{i}^{\sigma} S_{i+g_{2}}^{-\sigma}-S_{i+g_{2}}^{\sigma} S_{i}^{-\sigma}\right) S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle=\alpha_{2}\left\langle S_{i+g_{2}}^{\sigma} S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma}\right\rangle\left\langle S_{i}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle \\
-\alpha_{1}\left\langle S_{i}^{-\sigma} S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma}\right\rangle\left\langle S_{i+g_{2}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle, \quad g_{1} \neq g_{2}.
\end{gathered}
$$

Due to the spherical symmetry we use the identity of the correlation functions:
$$
C_{\rho}=3\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+\rho}^{z}\right\rangle=\frac{3}{2}\left\langle S_{i}^{\sigma} S_{i+\rho}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle=\left\langle S_{i} S_{i+\rho}\right\rangle.
$$

The explicit form of decoupled equations for site Green functions is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \sigma \omega\left[\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+g_{1}}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle-\left\langle S_{i+g_{1}}^{z} S_{i}^{\sigma} \mid S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle\right] \\
& \quad=2 / 3\left\{2 C_{g}\left(\delta_{i+g_{1}, j}-\delta_{i j}\right)+\left(\alpha_{2} C_{2 g}+2 \alpha_{2} C_{d}+1 / 2+2 \lambda\left(\alpha_{1} C_{g}+\alpha_{2} C_{g+d}\right)\right)\left(G_{i j}^{\sigma}-G_{i+g_{1}, j}^{\sigma}\right)\right.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$+\alpha_{1} C_{g}\left(\sum_{g_{2} \neq g_{1}}\left(G_{i+g_{1}-g_{2}, j}^{\sigma}-G_{i+g_{2}, j}^{\sigma}\right)+\lambda \sum_{d_{2}}\left(G_{i+g_{1}-d_{2}, j}^{\sigma}-G_{i+d_{2}, j}^{\sigma}\right)\right) ;\tag{9.1}$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \sigma \omega\left[\left\langle S_{i}^{z} S_{i+d_{1}}^{\sigma} | S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle-\left\langle S_{i+d_{1}}^{z} S_{i}^{\sigma} | S_{j}^{-\sigma}\right\rangle\right] \\
& \quad=2 / 3\left\{2 C_{d}\left(\delta_{i+d_{1}, j}-\delta_{i j}\right)+\left(\lambda\left(\alpha_{2} C_{2 d}+2 \alpha_{2} C_{2 g}+1 / 2\right)+2\left(\alpha_{1} C_{g}+\alpha_{2} C_{g+d}\right)\right)\left(G_{i j}^{\sigma}-G_{i+d_{1}, j}^{\sigma}\right)\right. \\
& \left.\quad+\alpha_{2} C_{d}\left(\lambda \sum_{d_{2} \neq d_{1}}\left(G_{i+d_{1}-d_{2}, j}^{\sigma}-G_{i+d_{2}, j}^{\sigma}\right)+\sum_{g_{2}}\left(G_{i+d_{1}-g_{2}, j}^{\sigma}-G_{i+g_{2}, j}^{\sigma}\right)\right)\right\}
\end{aligned}\tag{9.2}
$$

The correlation functions $C_{g}, C_{2 g}, C_{d}, C_{2 d}$ are independent of the direction of the vectors $\boldsymbol{g}, \boldsymbol{d}$; $C_{g+d}$ corresponds to the vector $\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{d}=2 \boldsymbol{g}_{x}+\boldsymbol{g}_{y}$.

Combining (5) with (9.1), (9.2) and after Fourier transformation, we obtain an expression of the Green function $G^{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{q}, \omega)$ and the excitation spectrum $\omega_{q}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& G^{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{q}, \omega)=-16 / 3\left(\left(1-\gamma_{g q}\right) C_{g}+\lambda\left(1-\gamma_{d q}\right) C_{d}\right)\left(\omega^{2}-\omega_{q}^{2}\right)^{-1} ; \\
& \omega_{q}^{2}=32 / 3\left|C\left(\boldsymbol{q}_{\mathrm{A}}\right)\right|\left(1-\gamma_{g q}\right)(2 \delta-(1+\gamma_{g q}) C(\boldsymbol{q}) /\left|C\left(\boldsymbol{q}_{\mathrm{A}}\right)\right|).
\end{aligned}\tag{10}
$$

Here,

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \gamma_{g q}=1 / 2\left(\cos \left(q_{x} g\right)+\cos \left(q_{y} g\right)\right) ; \quad \gamma_{d q}=\cos \left(q_{x} g\right) \cos \left(q_{y} g\right) ; \\
& 2 \delta=\left(\alpha_{2}\left(2 C_{d}+C_{2 g}\right)+3 \alpha_{1} C_{g}+3 / 4+2 \lambda\left(\alpha_{2} C_{d+g}-\alpha_{1} C_{g}\right)\right) /\left(4\left|C\left(\boldsymbol{q}_{\mathrm{A}}\right)\right|\right) ; \\
& C(\boldsymbol{q})=\alpha_{1} C_{g}+\left(\left(1-\gamma_{d q}\right) /\left(1-\gamma_{g q}^{2}\right)\right)\left(\lambda C^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{q})-\lambda^{2} C^{\prime \prime}(\boldsymbol{q})\right) ; \\
& C^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{q})=\gamma_{g q}\left(\alpha_{1} C_{g}+\alpha_{2} C_{d}\right)-1 / 2\left(3 \alpha_{1} C_{g}+\alpha_{2} C_{g+d}\right) ; \\
& C^{\prime \prime}(\boldsymbol{q})=1 / 4\left(3 / 4+\alpha_{2}\left(C_{2 d}+2 C_{2 g}+3 C_{d}-4\left(1+\gamma_{d q}\right) C_{d}\right)\right).
\end{aligned}\tag{11}
$$

The quantity $\delta$ determines the gap in the excitation spectrum at AFM vector $\boldsymbol{q}_{\mathrm{A}}=$ $(\pi / g)(1,1)$. From (4), (8) and Green function (10) we obtain five self-consistent equations for correlation functions $C_{g}, C_{d}, C_{2 g}, C_{g+d}$, $C_{2 d}$. The sixth equation is a consequense of the sum rule $C_{0}=\left\langle S_{i} S_{i}\right\rangle=3 / 4$.

The difference between parameters $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{2}$ is important to provide the sublattice magnetization:

$$
m=\left[\lim _{\left|R_{i j}\right| \rightarrow \infty}\left|S_{i} S_{j}\right|\right]^{1 / 2}, \tag{12}
$$

which is the most sensitive quantity of the theory. If $T=0$ and $\lambda=0$ the value of $m=m_{0}$ is well determined from other theories $(m_{0}=0.3^{21)})$. On the other hand, it's obvious, that at high temperature $\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2} \to 1$. That's why we'll assume in our calculations that ratio:

$$
r_{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1}-1\right) /\left(\alpha_{2}-1\right) \tag{13}
$$

is fixed, i.e. $r_{\alpha}$ is independent of temperature and frustration and it's determined from the condition $m(T=0, \lambda=0)=0.3$. As a result, at $T=0, \lambda=0$ we have five self-consistent equations and two additional equations for the sum rule and sublattice magnetization $m_{0}$. For arbitrary $T$ and $\lambda$ we have the condition (13) instead of equation for $m$. The explicit form of equations is presented in Appendix.

## §3. Correlation Functions, Spectrum Gap, and Uniform Susceptibility

### 3.1 Ground state properties $(T=0)$

At zero temperature our self-consistent equations are simplified. It turns out that they have two types of solutions, which depend on $\lambda$ value. The first type corresponds to the case $\lambda<\lambda_{0}$, when the system is in LROS, i.e. the spectrum gap $\delta=0$ and the sublattice magnetization $m$ is finite. The second type of solution gives a finite gap $\delta$ and zero value for the sublattice magnetization $m$. Hence, SLS is realised. Solving the self-consistent equation (see Appendix), we found that solution of the first type is realized for $\lambda<\lambda_{0} \approx 0.12$ and of the second type for $\lambda<\lambda_{0}$.

Figure 1 shows the dependence $m$ on $\lambda$. For $\lambda>\lambda_{0} m=0$. The dependence of spectrum gap $\delta$ on $\lambda$ is represented in Fig. 2. Figures 1 and 2 demonstrate, that LROS-SLS phase transition takes place at $\lambda_{0} \approx 0.12$.

## §4. Summary and Discussion
In this paper we have used the spherical symmetric Green function theory $^{19,20)}$ of 2D $S=1 /$
2 AFM to describe the transition from LROS to SLS. The transition parameter is the frustration parameter $\lambda=J_{2} / J_{1}$ , where $J_{1}, J_{2}$ are NN and NNN AFM interactions on a square lattice. We have involved a semiphenomenological approach: decoupling scheme contains the parameter $r_{\alpha}$ in order to improve the calculated value of the sublattice magnetization m at $T=0, \lambda=0$ .

Our results for the spin excitation spectrum $\omega(q)$ at $T=0$ manifest the gapless spectrum and finite sublattice magnetization $m$ up to $\lambda=\lambda_{0} \approx 0.12$ . For $\lambda>\lambda_{0}$ the finite spectrum gap arises and m becomes zero (see Figs. 1, 2). So LROS-SLS phase transition takes place at $\lambda \approx 0.12$ .

We present also the temperature dependence of spectrum gap, correlation functions, and uniform susceptibility before and after phase transition. The dependence of $\chi$ on $T$ is close to one, observed for HTSCs.

It should be pointed out that our approach and all the theories mentioned in introduction contradict each other when they describe the possibility of LROS-SLS phase transition at $T=0$ . All these approaches contain different uncontrolled approximations and it seems difficult to judge which is more realible than the other. At the same time it is clear that the SLS is manifested explicitly only in spherically symmetric approaches such as variational approach in refs. 3, 17, block approach 18 and the present one. Nevertheless the present critical value $\lambda \approx 0.12$ differs essentially from the corresponding critical value $\lambda \approx 0.25$ given in refs. 3, 17, 18. So an investigation with other analytical methods would be necessary to characterize the frustrated AFM.

We want to note some important consequences of our results for the theory of the hole spectrum $\varepsilon(k)$ of doped $CuO_{2}$ plane. In the framework of two-band Hubbard model the hole wave function may be described by a concept of magnetic polaron of small radius. $^{24)}$ The spectrum $\varepsilon(k)$ essentially depends on the spin correlation functions. In the simplest approach it may be represented in the following form: $^{25)}$
$$\begin{aligned}
& \varepsilon(\boldsymbol{k})=-A \gamma_{g k}+B \gamma_{g k}^{2} ; \\
& \gamma_{g k}=1 / 2\left(\cos \left(k_{x} g\right)+\cos \left(k_{y} g\right)\right) ; \\
& A=5.5\left(1 / 4+C_{g}\right) ; \quad B=4\left(1 / 8-C_{g}+1 / 2 C_{2}\right) ; \\
& C_{2}=\left(C_{d}+C_{2 g}\right) / 2.
\end{aligned}$$

The expression (15) is valid under the supposal that the value $y=C_{d}-C_{2 g}$ is equal zero $^{25)}$ (the equality y=0 corresponds to the numerical calculation on the block of 16 sites). $^{9)}$

For $T=0$ and $\lambda=0$ our numerical values of $C_{g}$ and $C_{2}$ lead to the inequalities $|A| \ll B$ ; $A<0$ . Then $\varepsilon(k)$ spectrum bottom coincides with the curve $\gamma_{g k}=$ const which is located close to magnetic Brillouin zone boundary. To illustrate the realistic position of the hole Fermi surface of the model let us consider $La_{2-x} Sr_{x} CuO_{4}$ with the typical doping value x=0.2. Then one of the Fermi surface sheets lies near the Van-Hove points $X(k_{X}=(0, \pm \pi)$ ; $( \pm \pi, 0)$ ), see Fig. 8, where the density of states for spectrum $\varepsilon(k)$ (15) becomes singular. Let us mention, that this fact is very important because it provides the foundation for the superconductivity in 2D system with nesting and nonmonotonous $T_{c}$ behavior on doping and pressure. $^{26)}$

As it was mentioned above the finite hole concentration x actually leads to the frustration in the spin subsystem, so in rough approximation the parameter $\lambda$ may be treated as $x.^{3)}$ At the same time the value of $A$ , as it seen from Figs. 3 and 5(a), changes essentially with

On the other hand as it may be seen from (15) the positions of the band bottom and the Fermi surface sheets are very sensitive to the value of $A$ (if $A$ changes its sign, the $\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{k})$ bottom line transforms from position 1 to 2, see Fig. 8). As a result $T$ and $x$ increase leads to the crossing of the Van-Hove point $\mathrm{X}$ by the Fermi surface. This may give rise to anomalous behaviour of the hole system characteristics on $T$ an $x$.

If we take into consideration that $y \neq 0$, the additional term proportional $y \gamma_{d k}\left(\gamma_{d k}=\right.$ $\left.\cos \left(k_{x} g\right) \cos \left(k_{y} g\right)\right)$ is added to expression (15) for $\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{k})$. ### Appendix

The system of self-consistent equations for correlation functions $C_{\rho}(\rho=0, \boldsymbol{g}, 2 \boldsymbol{g}, \boldsymbol{d}, 2 \boldsymbol{d}$, $\boldsymbol{d}+\boldsymbol{g})$ has the form (see (4), (8), (10)):
$$
\begin{aligned}
& C_{\rho}=N^{-1} \sum_{\boldsymbol{q}} F(\boldsymbol{q}) \omega_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{-1} \mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i} \boldsymbol{q} \rho}\left[n\left(\omega_{\boldsymbol{q}}\right)+1 / 2\right], \\
& F(\boldsymbol{q})=(-8)\left(\left(1-\gamma_{g q}\right) C_{g}+\lambda\left(1-\gamma_{d q}\right) C_{d}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Depending on $\lambda$ there are two different types of the system $(\mathrm{A} \cdot 1)$ solution. At first, we solve the system under condition that spectrum gap $\delta$ is zero. This condition adds one more equation to our system. It turns out, that solution exists for small $\lambda<\lambda_{0} \approx 0.12$. For this case in eq. $(\mathrm{A} \cdot 1)$ one can explicitly separate condensation part $C$, connected with Bose-function at AFM vector $\boldsymbol{q}_{\mathrm{A}}:{ }^{6)}$
$$
C_{\rho}=N^{-1} \sum_{\boldsymbol{q}} F(\boldsymbol{q}) \omega_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{-1} \mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i} \boldsymbol{q} \rho} 1 / 2+\beta_{\rho} C,
$$
where $\beta_{\rho}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i} q_{\mathrm{A}} \rho}$ is equal 1 or -1 .

As can be shown, ${ }^{19)}$ the condensation part $C$ determines the sublattice magnetization $m^{2}=C$. That's why, the value of $r_{\alpha}=0.825$ (see (13)) can be obtained, solving equations $(\mathrm{A} \cdot 2)$ at $\lambda=0$ with additional condition $m=m_{0}=0.3$.

Solution of the second type corresponds to cotidition $\delta \neq 0$. In this case self-consistent equations have the form $(\mathrm{A} \cdot 2)$ with $C=0$ and solution exists for $\lambda>\lambda_{0}$.

For finite temperature we always have $\delta \neq 0$. We can't separate condensation part $C$ and have to solve equations $(\mathrm{A} \cdot 1)$ as they are.
