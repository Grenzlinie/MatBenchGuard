# GENERAL EXPERIMENTAL TECHNIQUES

## Characteristics and Behavior
## of the Electron Beam
## of the МИН-1 Accelerator in Air

L. P. Babich, E. N. Donskoi, T. V. Loiko,
Yu. M. Nedoikash, and N. G. Pavlovskaya

All-Russia Research Institute of Experimental Physics (VNIIEF),
Russian Federal Nuclear Center, pr. Mira 37, Sarov, Nizhni Novgorod oblast, 607188 Russia

Received September 3, 1999

Abstract—The electron-beam characteristics of the МИН-1 accelerator have been measured. At the output of the accelerating tube, the current-pulse amplitude is ~1kA, duration ~10ns, and maximal electron energy ~600 keV. As the beam passes through the air, its diameter increases linearly with the distance varying from 2 cm at the tube window to ~45 cm at a distance of 1 m from it, which agrees with the calculations performed.

Over large-scale thunder cloud systems with a volume of ~1000 $km^3$, high-altitude optical phenomena have been repeatedly observed, which are called Blue Jets and Red Spirits [1]. Unusually powerful and very short pulses of RF radiation are supposedly correlated with these phenomena. The presence of thunderstorm fields enhances the penetrating radiation in the atmosphere by several orders of magnitude [2]. The only mechanism that provides a unified explanation for the totality of electromagnetic phenomena seems to be that of a relativistic runaway-electron avalanche (RREA), the idea of which has been put forward by Gurevich and Roussel-Dupre [3]. In order to adequately treat the data of field measurements, one should know the characteristic length corresponding to the RREA growth by a factor of $e$ ($l_e$). To measure this length, in VNIIEF, within the framework of the project ISTC no. 490, an experimental bench was set up consisting of a high-voltage source, accelerating chamber ~1 $m^3$ in volume and electrode separation of 1 m, and a pulse relativistic-electron injector. A high-voltage pulse of microsecond duration is applied to the electrodes of the chamber, into which an electron beam is injected. Through measuring the beam amplification over a length of 1 m, one can obtain the length $l_e$.

The metrological nature of the problem and physics of the RREA impose rigid requirements on the electron-beam parameters, which may be formulated in the following way:

(1) sufficiently high intensity permitting measuring the ratio between the number of relativistic electrons at the output of the chamber and the number of injected electrons;
(2) nanosecond duration of the electron pulse;
(3) small energy dispersion of the beam electrons; actually, an ~1-MeV monoenergetic beam is required;
(4) sufficiently small width of the injected beam, since scattering of electrons in air results in the beam broadening, which renders measurements difficult at the chamber output; however, excessive narrowing of the beam may result in generation of a dense air plasma and uncontrollable collective interaction between the plasma and beam.

The accelerator has a small size and is reliable and easy to operate. It is placed, along with a self-contained power supply, on the upper high-voltage electrode of the chamber in the overturned position, that is, with the electron tube directed downwards.

The electron tube ИА-9 [5] is a diode with a metal housing 70 mm in diameter and 100 mm high, into which a conical glass insulator with a high-voltage cold-cathode lead is sealed in. The anode is a window 30 mm in diameter made of 50 $\mu$m-thick titanium foil through which the accelerated electrons are ejected into the atmosphere. The amplitude of the beam current out- side the tube anode is 1 to 2 kA, and its duration is ~10 ns. By varying the interelectrode gap, cathode shape, and dimensions, one can change the distribution of the elec- tron-beam current density.

When investigating the electron-beam structure, cathodes with various shapes were used. The electron beam was recorded with a ЦВИД-0.1-1 color-change film dosimeter. The film was placed on the outer sur- face of the window. In Fig. 1, a transverse beam struc- ture is shown for four different cathodes: in the form of a tantalum-foil cylinder 10 mm in diameter, in the form of a set of three and five parallel tantalum blades mounted on a disk 10 mm in diameter placed in a focus- ing cylinder 12 mm in diameter, and a set of eight par- allel tantalum blades in a focusing cylinder 24 mm in diameter.

The electron beam formed in the tube with a cylin- drical cathode is nonuniform (Fig. 1a). It has a multiray structure converging at its center, where its density is maximal. When a blade cathode is used, the distribu- tion of electrons in the beam is more uniform. In this case, the shape of the beam depends on the blade den- sity. Thus, when three blades are used, an oval beam is formed (Fig. 1b); with five (Fig. 1c), the beam shape is near-circular. An increase in the emitting surface of the blade cathodes has the same effect on the electron- beam diameter (Fig. 1d). The use of a focusing elec- trode affects the cross-sectional dimensions of the beam. Thus, the electron beam formed by a cylindrical cathode (Fig. 1a) exceeds the diameter of the tube win- dow, while the beam formed by a set of blades is less than the window's diameter (Figs. 1b-1d), the beam sizes of Figs. 1c and 1d being close to those of the cath- odes. For our investigations, the tube with an eight-blade cathode 20 mm in diameter (Fig. 1d) proved to be the most suitable, since, in this case, the beam was the most uniform over its cross section and least divergent one.

Figure 2b shows an X-ray pulse excited in the titanum window of the tube. The radiation was registered at a distance of 1 m from the tube window using a photomultiplier CHФT-8 (resolving time $\tau_{0.5} = 1.5$ ns) with the СПС-Б10 scintillating plastic ($\tau_{0.5} = 1.1$ ns) and an СПТ-6 oscilloscope. Pulse duration $\tau_{0.5} = 4.5$ ns. The sensitivity of the apparatus used allowed one to register X-rays filtered by 10-mm-thick lead with a tantalum foil 0.01 mm thick present on the electron-tube window. Thus, X-rays may be used for noncontact monitoring of the electron beam.

A photograph of the glow of the beam-excited air illustrates how the beam propagates. The beam divergence and attenuation can be judged from the pattern of the glow. Figure 3a shows the airglow photographed onto PФ-3 film (sensitivity 1000 units) with a 10-pulse exposure time. Figure 3b shows the glow of the air excited by the beam limited by a diaphragm 2 cm in diameter and 2.5 cm thick. The use of a diaphragm allowed one to separate the least divergent part of the beam. The glow was visually observed at distances up to 1 m from the tube window, while only the brightest region of the glow, ~25 cm long, was recorded on the film. For this reason, the beam divergence at larger distances was investigated using ИС-7 dosimetric glass plates [7]. The glass absorbed the electrons completely; therefore, there is a reason to believe that the dosimeter indications are proportional to the total energy of electrons and the glass can be used as an electron indicator.

The glass plates were placed along the diameter line in the plane perpendicular to the direction of the electron propagation. Measurements were made at various distances from the tube window. Beam half-width as a function of distance to the tube window is shown in Fig. 4. Beam half-width is the beam diameter at which the dosimeter indications are halved. This dependence is essentially linear. If the beam diameter is 2 cm at the collimator output, then, it is ~45 cm at a distance of 1 m.

In order to make sure that electrons and not X-ray photons are registered, an PM-1 X-ray film was used with filters of equal mass thickness, but different $Z$ (Be ($Z = 4$), Al ($Z = 13$), and Ag ($Z = 48$)). Measurements have shown that blackenings under these filters do not actually differ, which proves that indeed electrons are registered. Besides, when a voltage of ~300 kV is applied to the 1-m air gap, the indications on the dosimeters located at 1 m increase by about an order of magnitude.

In order to obtain more complete information on the beam propagation, calculations were performed using the ELIZA code [8], which allowed one, by implementing the Monte Carlo method, to solve the linear problems of combined transport of electrons, positrons, and $\gamma$-radiation in a complex geometry. The calculations were performed for electrons with initial energies of 400 and 600 keV under the following conditions: electrons left the vacuum tube and entered the air normally to the plane of the window, the current density was uniform, and the beam diameter was 2 cm. In the plane perpendicular to the direction of beam propagation, radial distribution of the electron-beam density $dN/dS$, electron/cm², and its energy $dE/dS$, keV/cm², were calculated, starting from the beam center, for various distances from the tube window (10, 20, 40, 60, and 100 cm). The accuracy of the calculations depended on the distance and was 4% for 10 cm and 20% for 100 cm.

Calculation results for electrons with 400-keV energies are shown in Fig. 5 and give an illustrative account of the transport of monoenergetic electrons in atmo-

spheric air. Normalization was performed to one elec- tron. When the beam passed through an air layer 1 m thick, the density of 400-keV electrons in the beam decreased by a factor of $10^{4}$, and, in the beam of 600- keV electrons, by a factor of $3 × 10^{3}$. It was impossible to register the glow at greater distances, since, even for the beam with an energy of 600 keV, the electron den- sity decreased by a factor of 30 at a distance of 20 cm.

The beam was strongly scattered in air. For the 600-keV electrons, the diameters, for which the electron density was decreased by a factor of 10 at distances of10, 20, 40, and 60 cm, were 6, 14, 45, and 70 cm, respectively. For the 400-keV electrons, the beam diameters were 8, 18, 60, and 110 cm at the same dis- tances. The beam half-width, calculated for a 1-m dis- tance for 400-keV electrons, was ~40 cm, and for the600-keV electrons, ~55 cm, which agreed with experi- mental data.

In summary, no collective interactions are observed in the electron beam of the MИH-1 accelerator, and it can be used as a source of electrons in a device intended for investigation of relativistic-electron amplification in a gaseous medium with a sufficiently strong external electric field, since a number of its parameters meets the requirements claimed by such experiments.
