# SHORT LIFETIMES IN $^{38}$Ar

R. LAPPALAINEN, J. KEINONEN, A. ANTTILA, A. KISS* and E. SOMORJAI*

Department of Physics, University of Helsinki, SF-00170 Helsinki, Finland

Received 9 April 1984

**Abstract:** Mean lifetimes of levels in $^{38}$Ar have been measured using the Doppler-shift-attenuation (DSA) method and the reaction $^{37}Cl(p, \gamma)^{38}$Ar. The lifetime values or limits were determined for 37 bound levels below the excitation energy of 9 MeV; the lifetimes of 9 levels, upper limits of 2 and lower limits of 3 levels are reported for the first time. For the effective stopping of recoils, the targets were prepared by implanting $^{37}$Cl into Ta backings. The Monte Carlo method and the experimental stopping power were used in the DSA analysis.

## 1. Introduction

The unexpectedly large number of low-lying positive-parity states in $^{38}$Ar, where only two protons are missing from the $Z = N = 20$ shell closure at $^{40}$Ca, demonstrates ideally the 2p4h excitations of the core $^{1-5}$). The negative-parity states are interpreted to be predominantly due to the 1p3h excitations $^{4-6}$). Several two-particle transfer reaction studies $^{7,8}$) have been undertaken to show the 2p4h and two-hole configurations. The experimental information on the multiparticle, multihole configurations is, however, mainly based on the electromagnetic transition properties studied with the reactions $^{35}Cl(\alpha, p\gamma)^{38}$Ar and $^{37}Cl(p, \gamma)^{38}$Ar [ref. $^{7}$)] and heavy-ion fusion evaporation reactions $^{7,9,10}$). However, due to the large experimental uncertainties in the reported lifetime values $^{7}$), the knowledge on transition strengths is only moderate. Furthermore, the lifetime values are mainly based on the Doppler-shift-attenuation (DSA) measurements, where uncertainties due to the stopping power are not included in the reported values.

Theoretically, shell-model calculations $^{1-4,6}$) have reproduced the general features of excited levels, but more reliable experimental lifetime values are needed to remove the present inadequacy in accounting for electromagnetic properties which are sensitive to the multiparticle-multihole components in the predicted configurations.

As the methods developed in our laboratory $^{11}$) for the determination of short lifetimes with the DSA method allow the possibility of deducing lifetime values in

## 2. Experimental results

The 2.5 MV Van de Graaff accelerator at the University of Helsinki supplied the proton beam of about $40\ \mu$A. The beam was collimated to form a spot of $3 \times 3\ \text{mm}^2$ on the target. The $^{37}\text{Cl}$ targets were prepared by implanting with the laboratory isotope separator a $12\ \mu\text{g/cm}^2$ dose of $60\ \text{keV}\ ^{37}\text{Cl}^+$ ions into 0.2 mm thick Ta backings. The advantages of using Ta as a backing material, such as the effective stopping of the recoiling ions and the fact that the Cl-Ta targets withstand high proton beam intensities and quantities because of the high diffusion rate of hydrogen in Ta, have been discussed in our earlier papers, e.g. refs. $^{11,12)}$.

The $\gamma$-radiation was detected by using a PGT $110\ \text{cm}^3$ Ge(Li) detector with an efficiency of $21.8\%$. The energy resolution of the detection system was $2.0\ \text{keV}$ at $E_{\gamma}=1.3\ \text{MeV}$ and $3.1\ \text{keV}$ at $E_{\gamma}=2.6\ \text{MeV}$. The $\gamma$-ray spectra were stored in an 8k-channel memory. The stability of the spectrometer was checked with a $^{208}\text{Tl}$ $\gamma$-source and the $^{40}\text{K}$ laboratory background. The DSA measurements were performed with the detector at angles $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ to the beam direction and at a target-detector distance of 8 cm. The corrections for solid-angle attenuation of the observed Doppler shifts were taken into account using primary $\gamma$-ray transitions. An accumulated charge of $0.1-0.9\ \text{C}$ was collected for each $\gamma$-spectrum.

In selecting resonances for the DSA measurements, the $\gamma$-decay scheme studies of Engelbertink *et al.* $^{13)}$ and Alderliesten *et al.* $^{14)}$ were used. As resonances generally decay to many bound states at rather high energies and bound states also have complicated decay schemes, the resonances were selected where the levels studied were almost entirely fed by primary transitions. Measurements were performed on 11 resonances ranging from $E_{\text{p}}=944\ \text{keV}$ to $1732\ \text{keV}$. In order to ensure that the $F(\tau)$ values were not affected by unknown feedings, the measurements were performed on more than one resonance, if sufficient population could be seen. Due to the complicated decay scheme of the strongest analogue resonance in $^{38}$Ar at $E_{\text{p}}=1732\ \text{keV}\ (J^{\pi}=4^- [\text{ref.}^{13)})$] and due to the fact that only this resonance could be used in many determinations of lifetime values for probable high-spin states, the $\gamma$-decay scheme of this resonance was remeasured. The present and previous results are given in table 1. The $\gamma$-decay spectrum was recorded with the Ge(Li) detector at $55^\circ$ relative to the beam axis and at a distance of 5 cm from the beam spot. The dispersion in the 8k spectra recorded was $1.4\ \text{keV/channel}$. The accumulated charge was $0.9\ \text{C}$. The efficiency of the detector was determined in the experimental geometry from $\gamma$-ray intensities in $^{56}\text{Co}$ [ref. $^{15)}$] and using the relative $\gamma$-ray intensities in the $^{27}\text{Al(p, }\gamma\text{)}^{28}\text{Si}$ reaction at $E_{\text{p}}=992\ \text{keV}$ [ref. $^{16)}$]. The branching ratios for other resonances and for bound states were taken from refs. $^{7,13,14)}$. The experimental condition of the present and previous DSA measurements are given in table 3. The stopping materials used in all previous measurements, $BaCl_{2}$ or $AgCl$ , are slower than Ta used in the present work. The $F(\tau)$ value corresponding to the lifetime $50 fs(100 fs)(v / c=0.14 \%)$ is about $69 \%(51 \%)$ and $59 \%(40 \%)$ for $BaCl_{2}$ and $AgCl$ , respectively, but $46 \%(31 \%)$ for the slowing-down medium used in the present work. Further disadvantages in the previous studies are that the densities of the evaporated compound targets might be affected by the evaporation conditions and in most cases part of the slowing down took place in a backing material. In all previous DSA analysis the nuclear stopping power was obtained from the LSS theory $^{21}$ ). The electronic stopping power was taken directly from the LSS theory [refs. $^{22,23)}$ ] or its predictions were corrected [refs. $^{4,13,24,25)}$ ] as proposed by Ormrod et al. $^{26)}$ . The possibility of systematic errors has not been considered in the DSA analysis. Only the results by Engelbertink and Middelkoop $^{24}$ ) include an uncertainty in the stopping power; this has been estimated by including a $30 \%$ error in the stopping power of the target material and $8 \%$ uncertainty in the density of the carbon backing. The present lifetime $\tau(7.288)=30 \pm$ 15 fs suggests a larger E2 strength than the 35 W.u. obtained in ref. $^{25}$ ), indicating a stronger deformation of the nucleus in this state and a larger moment of inertia

Five nucleons in the $1d_{3/2}$ shell and one in either the $1f_{7/2}$ or $2p_{3/2}$ shell were used by Engelbertink and Glaudemans $^{6}$) to describe the negative-parity states. Alderliesten and Van Loon $^{4}$) then completed $\gamma$-strength calculations with the same model. Comparison of the M1 strengths obtained with their calculated $\gamma$-decay data with the experimental strengths (table 5) shows moderate agreement but the calculated values seem to have the tendency to underestimate the M1 strengths. The calculated lifetimes $\tau=245,41,30,73$ and $18$ fs for the $5.51(3^{-}), 5.66(5^{-}), 5.86(2^{-})$, $6.211(4^{-})$ and 6.67 (predicted $J^{\pi}=5^{-}$) levels, respectively, are in agreement with the experimental values, whereas the predictions $\tau(4.88;3^{-})=224$, $\tau(6.04;5^{-})=230$, $\tau(6.60;4^{-})=39$ and $\tau(6.68;$ predicted $J^{\pi}=0^{-})=30$ fs are in disagreement with the experimental results.

It can be concluded that the existing models which are successful in accounting for excitation energies and spectroscopic factors of excited levels in $^{38}$Ar do not obviously take the interaction between single-particle motion and multiparticle-multihole configurations adequately into account. This interaction manifests itself in the transition strengths and produces large changes in the collectivity of states which cannot be sufficiently taken into account by using average values for $g$-factors and effective charges.

Reliable electromagnetic transition rates for most of the low-lying levels in $^{38}$Ar have been measured. The results should provide a testing ground for any new shell-model wave functions.
