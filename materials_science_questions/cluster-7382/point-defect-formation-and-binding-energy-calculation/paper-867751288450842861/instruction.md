# Charge-doping induced ferromagnetism energy differences and Curie temperature from DFT

## Problem background
The parent compound Sr2MnGe2S6O crystallizes in a melilite-type structure where the MnS4 tetrahedra are separated by GeS3O tetrahedra, resulting in a moderate Mn–Mn separation that avoids strong antiferromagnetic superexchange. The undoped compound is a weakly antiferromagnetic insulator. Introducing charge carriers by substituting Sr with K (p-type) or La (n-type) may suppress the weak antiferromagnetism and stabilize a ferromagnetic order. This task investigates whether explicit K or La doping at 12.5% and 25% concentration induces ferromagnetic order, as quantified by the energy difference between ferromagnetic and checkerboard antiferromagnetic configurations, and whether the resulting Curie temperature can be estimated above room temperature.

## Approach
The method uses spin‑polarized density functional theory (DFT) with a plane‑wave code and PAW pseudopotentials within the PBE exchange‑correlation functional. A 48‑atom supercell of Sr2MnGe2S6O is constructed using fixed experimental lattice constants. Doped structures are generated by replacing one or two Sr atoms with K or La. For each system (undoped, K‑12.5%, La‑12.5%, K‑25%, La‑25%) and for both ferromagnetic (all Mn spins aligned) and checkerboard antiferromagnetic (Néel) orders, atomic positions are relaxed and total energies are computed. The energy difference per Mn ΔE=(E_AFM − E_FM)/4 is then evaluated. From the 25% doping ΔE values, a nearest‑neighbor exchange coupling J1 is derived assuming a two‑dimensional square Heisenberg model, and the Curie temperature is estimated via the analytical solution of the 2D Ising model. Additionally, the electronic band structure of the relaxed ferromagnetic K‑12.5% and La‑12.5% systems is computed to confirm semiconductor character by determining the band gap and the Fermi level position.

## Reproduction target
Produce three scored output files: (1) energy_differences.json: the computed ΔE values (meV/Mn) for K‑12.5%, K‑25%, La‑12.5%, and La‑25% doping. (2) curie_temperatures.json: the estimated Curie temperatures (in Kelvin) for K‑25% and La‑25% doping, derived from the 25% ΔE values via J1 = ΔE/4 and the 2D Ising relation. (3) doped_band_gaps.json: for K‑12.5% and La‑12.5% doping, report the band gap (eV) and whether the Fermi level lies at the top of the valence band (p‑type) or at the bottom of the conduction band (n‑type). All DFT calculations use the specified open‑source tools, pseudopotentials, plane‑wave cutoff, and k‑point mesh; the crystal structure is provided in the task description.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Build supercell and doping structures
- Role: process
- Action: Construct a 48-atom supercell of Sr2MnGe2S6O using the provided experimental lattice constants and atomic positions. Generate substitutional structures for K doping (p-type) and La doping (n-type) by replacing one Sr atom (12.5% doping) and two Sr atoms (25% doping), with the two substitutions for 25% in different Sr layers.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/supercell_structures_ready.txt`

### Step 2: DFT total-energy calculations and ionic relaxations
- Role: process
- Action: For each of the five structures (undoped, K 12.5%, La 12.5%, K 25%, La 25%), perform spin-polarized DFT total-energy calculations for two magnetic orders: ferromagnetic (all Mn spins aligned) and checkerboard antiferromagnetic Néel order. Use PAW pseudopotentials with PBE exchange-correlation, a plane-wave cutoff of 520 eV, and a 7×7×6 Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh. Relax internal atomic positions until forces are below 0.01 eV/Å, keeping lattice constants fixed. Record the final total energies.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/dft_total_energies.log`

### Step 3: Compute energy differences ΔE
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the total energies obtained in step2, calculate for each doping case the energy difference per Mn: ΔE = (E_AFM - E_FM) / 4 (meV/Mn), where 4 is the number of Mn atoms in the 48-atom supercell. Write the results to energy_differences.json.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/energy_differences.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: JSON object with keys: K_12.5, K_25, La_12.5, La_25. Each value is a float representing ΔE in meV/Mn.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 4: Estimate Curie temperatures
- Role: scored
- Action: Using the 25% doping ΔE values from step3, compute the exchange coupling J1 = ΔE/4 (meV). Estimate the Curie temperature Tc using the 2D Ising model analytical solution: Tc = 2*J1 / (kB * ln(1+√2)), where kB = 8.617333262145e-2 meV/K. Round Tc to the nearest K. Write the results to curie_temperatures.json.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/curie_temperatures.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: JSON object with keys: K_25, La_25. Each value is a float (Kelvin).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 5: Compute band structures and band gaps
- Role: scored
- Action: For the relaxed FM structures of K 12.5% and La 12.5% doping, perform non-self-consistent DFT calculations to obtain the electronic band structure. Determine the band gap (energy difference between valence band maximum and conduction band minimum) and identify whether the Fermi level is at the top of the valence band (p-type) or bottom of the conduction band (n-type). Write the results to doped_band_gaps.json.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/doped_band_gaps.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: JSON object with keys: K_12.5, La_12.5. Each value is an object with keys: band_gap (float, eV), fermi_level_type (string, 'valence_band' or 'conduction_band').
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier
