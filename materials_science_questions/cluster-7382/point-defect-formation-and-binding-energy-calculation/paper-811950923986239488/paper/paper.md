# Self-assembling of 4C10Sn clusters in Ge co-doped with C and Sn

V.A. Elyukhin$^{a,*}$, L.P. Sorokina$^{a}$, V.A. Mishurnyi$^{b}$, F. de Anda$^{b}$

$^{a}$Departamento de Ingeniería Eléctrica—SEES, CINVESTAV-IPN, Av. IPN 2508, D.F., 07360, Mexico
$^{b}$IICO-UASLP, Av. Karakorum 1470, Lomas 4A Secc., San Luis Potosi, SLP 78210, Mexico

Received 13 December 2006; accepted 25 October 2007
Available online 4 December 2007

## Abstract
Self-assembling of 4C10Sn clusters in Ge co-doped with C and Sn is considered. The free energies of Ge-rich $C_{x}Sn_{y}Ge_{1-x-y}$ ($x\leq0.02$, $x\geqslant0.4y$) random alloys and alloys with 4C10Sn and 1C4Sn clusters are estimated at temperature of 773 K. The concentration conditions when the free energy of $C_{x}Sn_{y}Ge_{1-x-y}$ with 4C10Sn clusters is smaller than those of random alloys and alloys with 1C4Sn clusters are obtained. Occurrence of 4C10Sn clusters is a result of a stronger decrease of the strain energy after such self-assembling than after self-assembling of 1C4Sn clusters.

## 1. Introduction
Self-assembling (SA) of 1C4Sn clusters with carbon atoms in their centers was theoretically justified in Ref. [1] for Ge-rich $C_{x}Sn_{y}Ge_{1-x-y}$ ($x\leqslant4y$) alloys. The origin of this phenomenon is a significant decrease of the strain energy of these alloys after SA of 1C4Sn clusters. Separated carbon and Sn atoms dilate and contract the crystal lattice of the Ge-rich random alloy. The dilation and compression considerably compensate each other after the formation of 1C4Sn clusters. The lattice parameters of diamond and $\alpha$-Sn are smaller and larger, respectively, than that of Ge. Therefore, the formation of 1C4Sn clusters in Ge co-doped with carbon and Sn is profitable from the strain energy standpoint. The conditions when the free energy of a random alloy is equal to the free energy of the same composition alloy in which all C atoms are in 1C4Sn clusters were estimated in Ref. [1]. Thus, a decrease of the entropy after SA compensates a diminution of the strain energy. Impurity clusters larger than tetrahedral cells containing central highly mismatched atoms have not been studied yet from the SA standpoint. An occurrence of the similar clusters with four highly mismatched atoms in their vertices is non-profitable due to a significant increase of the strain energy caused by such SA. However, in addition to the pentatomic tetrahedral cells (filled cells), the tetrahedral cells without atoms in their centers (empty cells) are also in the diamond structure. For the diamond type crystals the bond stretching elastic constants are considerably larger than the bond bending ones [2,3]. Therefore, changes of the angles between bonds should lead to the smaller deformation energy than those of lengths of bonds. Accordingly, from the strain energy standpoint, SA of clusters in which mainly angles between bonds are changed is more profitable than that of ones with considerably deformed bonds. The deformation caused by an empty impurity cell leads mainly to the changes of angles between the bonds in contrast to a filled cell when mainly bonds are deformed. Thus, SA of the clusters with empty cells in the central part of them seems more probable than that with filled cells. In Ge co-doped with C and Sn, 4C10Sn clusters shown in Fig. 1 are such ones. Their symmetric form should provide the smaller strain energy. These 4C10Sn clusters will be thermodynamically profitable if the decrease of the free energy after their SA is larger than that after SA of 1C4Sn

## 2. Model

The free energy of Ge-rich $\text{C}_{x}\text{Sn}_{y}\text{Ge}_{1-x-y}$ in a manner like in Ref. [1] is described as a sum of the free energies of the constituent substances, strain energy and configurational entropy term. The constituents of these alloys do not form chemical compounds. Therefore, all types of the bonds are covalent and free energies of the constituent substances do not depend on the arrangement of the atoms. Accordingly, SA changes only the strain energy and configurational entropy term. As it was mentioned above SA of 4C10Sn clusters should occur if the decrease of the free energy of the random alloy after such SA is larger than that after SA of 1C4Sn clusters. The occurrence of 4C10Sn clusters decreases the configurational entropy of the random alloy larger than that of 1C4Sn ones. In such a case, SA of 4C10Sn clusters must decrease the strain energy more than SA of the pentatomic ones. The strain energies caused by separated C and Sn atoms and 1C4Sn clusters are considered as in Ref. [1]. The valence force field model was used to estimate the strain energy of alloys with 4C10Sn clusters. Similar to Ref. [4] for the unlike covalent bonds and angles between them the averages of the elastic constants of the pure substances were used. The bond stretching and bond bending elastic constants as well as the stiffness coefficients and lattice parameters were taken from Refs. [2,3]. The strain energy of the Ge-rich alloy co-doped with C and Sn produced by a separated C atom is much larger than that for a separated Sn one. Therefore, the larger decrease of the strain energy of the random alloy after SA of 4C10Sn clusters can be reached if the larger part of carbon atoms is in these clusters than after SA of 1C4Sn ones. It corresponds to the condition $x \geqslant 0.4y$. Moreover, the minimum of the strain energy is reached if all Sn atoms are in 4C10Sn clusters. The conditions when the free energy of Ge-rich alloy with 4C10Sn clusters is smaller than that of the random alloy and alloy with 1C4Sn clusters are considered. The strain energies of alloys with 4C10Sn and 1C4Sn clusters and random alloy are written, respectively, as

$$
u_{4\text{C}10\text{Sn}} = 0.1yu_{4\text{C}10\text{Sn}} + (x - 0.4y)u_{1\text{C}},
$$

$$
u_{1\text{C}4\text{Sn}} = 0.25yu_{1\text{C}4\text{Sn}} + (x - 0.25)u_{1\text{C}},
$$

$$
u_{\text{R}} \approx xu_{1\text{C}} + yu_{1\text{Sn}},
$$

where $u_{4\text{C}10\text{Sn}}$, $u_{1\text{C}}$ and $u_{1\text{C}4\text{Sn}}$ are the strain energies produced by 4C10Sn clusters, separated carbon atoms and 1C4Sn clusters, respectively. These formulas were obtained at the condition $x, y \ll 1$ that is fulfilled for Ge co-doped with C and Sn. The estimates demonstrate that the strain energy caused by 4C10Sn cluster more than two and a half times smaller than that produced by four C and ten Sn separated atoms. At the considered concentration condition $x \geqslant 0.4y$ the strain energy produced by 4C10Sn cluster more than one and a half times smaller than that caused by two 1C4Sn clusters and two C and two Sn separated atoms.

The entropy term of the self-assembled alloys with 4C10Sn clusters is given as

$$
RT\left[\begin{array}{l}
(1 - x - y)\ln\frac{1 - x - y}{1 - 1.4y} + (x - 0.4y)\ln\frac{x - 0.4y}{1 - 1.4y} \\
+ 0.1y\ln 2.7y + (0.037 - 0.1y)\ln(1 - 2.7y)
\end{array}\right].
$$

This term is obtained for $x \geqslant 0.4y$ when all Sn atoms are in 4C10Sn clusters distributed randomly and arranged around empty tetrahedral cells. Carbon atoms not situated in the clusters and all Ge atoms are distributed randomly on the other lattice sites. Two last items describe the number of arrangements of 4C10Sn clusters. One 4C10Sn cluster occupies a space corresponding to 27 empty and filled tetrahedral cells. The entropy term of the alloys with 1C4Sn clusters at the concentration condition $x \geqslant 0.4y$ is given as

$$
RT\left[\begin{array}{l}
(x - 0.25y)\ln\frac{x - 0.25y}{1 - 1.25y} + (1 - x - y)\ln\frac{1 - x - y}{1 - 1.25y} \\
+ 0.25y\ln(0.5y) + (0.5 - 0.25y)\ln(1 - 0.5y)
\end{array}\right].
$$

This term is obtained when all Sn atoms are in 1C4Sn clusters distributed randomly. Carbon atoms not situated in the clusters and all Ge atoms are distributed randomly on the other lattice sites. Two last items describe the number of arrangements of 1C4Sn clusters. The entropy term of a random $\text{C}_{x}\text{Sn}_{y}\text{Ge}_{1-x-y}$ alloy is expressed in the well-known form $\text{RT}[x \ln x + y \ln y + (1 - x - y)\ln(1 - x - y)]$.

## 3. Results and discussion

The bond stretching and bond bending elastic constants of diamond are significantly larger than those of Ge and $\alpha$-Sn [2,3]. Moreover, the difference in the covalent radii of C and Ge atoms is two and a half times larger than that of Sn and Ge ones. As a result, the strain energy caused by a separated C atom is significantly larger than that of a Sn atom. This strain energy leads to the low solubility limit of C in Ge. Therefore, the concentrations of carbon $x \leqslant 0.02$ were chosen in our estimates. The molecular beam epitaxy

(MBE) is one of the conventional methods for growth of Ge-rich alloys at temperature of 773 K that was used in our estimates. The concentration conditions when free energies of the alloys with 4C10Sn and 1C4Sn clusters are equal to each other are shown in Fig. 2. The free energy of the random alloys is larger than that of the self-assembled ones. Thus, the occurrence of 4C10Sn clusters is thermodynamically profitable at the represented conditions. If a random $C_xSn_yGe_{1-x-y}$ alloy is formed during an MBE process it can be transformed to a self-assembled one by thermal annealing. The annealing time may be obtained from the impurity diffusion lengths. As seen from the calculated impurity concentrations, the C and Sn diffusion lengths should be of the order of 2–3 nm in the random alloy. The diffusion time is estimated from the diffusion lengths that are written as $d \approx 6\sqrt{Dt}$, where $D$ and $t$ are the coefficient and time of diffusion, respectively. The diffusion coefficient of C atoms in Ge is not available now. Therefore, the diffusion coefficient of Si in Ge equal to $D=0.24\exp\{-2.9\ \text{eV}/kT\}\ \text{cm}^2\text{s}^{-1}$ [5] was used for the approximated estimate. The diffusion coefficient of Sn in Ge is equal to $D=1.7\times 10^{-2}\exp\{-1.9\ \text{eV}/kT\}\ \text{cm}^2\text{s}^{-1}$ [6]. Accordingly, the time of the transformation of the random alloy to the self-assembled one with 4C10Sn clusters should be several hours at temperature of 773 K.

The free energy of the $C_xSn_yGe_{1-x-y}\ (x\geqslant 0.4y)$ alloys in which all Sn atoms situated in 1C4Sn clusters is larger than that of the alloys with 4C10Sn clusters at the conditions shown in Fig. 2. Thus, SA of 4C10Sn clusters is thermodynamically profitable at these conditions. In accordance with our estimates SA of 4C6Sn symmetric clusters with empty 4N tetrahedral cells in their central parts is hardly probable due to a considerable increase of the strain energy.

The clusters containing four highly mismatched atoms can form the localized "cluster states" as it was demonstrated in Refs. [7,8] for GaAs and GaP doped with nitrogen. However, in these papers the nitrogen involving clusters are 1Ga4N tetrahedral cells. The formation of such tetrahedral clusters should considerably increase the strain energy of GaAs and GaP doped with N [9]. Therefore, the occurrence of 1Ga4N clusters seems hardly probable.

The properties of GaAs co-doped with In and N was studied in Ref. [10]. The simultaneous incorporation of doping amounts of both In and highly mismatched N in GaAs creates new isoelectronic traps that are not formed in GaAs doped with nitrogen. These traps occur due to association of In and N atoms which larger and smaller than Ga and As ones, respectively. Carbon atoms are also highly mismatched in comparison with Ge atoms. Therefore, SA of 4C10Sn clusters can lead to the occurrence of a new type of the traps also not presented in Ge doped with carbon.

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have shown that the formation of 4C10Sn clusters in Ge co-doped with C and Sn can be thermodynamically profitable at temperature of 773 K. These clusters should be formed around the tetrahedral carbon cells without central atoms. The considerable diminution of the strain energy after the SA of 4C10Sn clusters is the origin of this phenomenon.
