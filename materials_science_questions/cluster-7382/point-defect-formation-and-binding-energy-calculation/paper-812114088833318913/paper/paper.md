# Phase States of Condensed Rare Gases

B. M. Smirnov

Institute of High Temperatures, Izhorskaya 13/19, Moscow 127412, Russia

Received May 13, 1998; accepted June 2, 1998

PACS Ref: 31.15.Gy, 68.35.R

## Abstract
Excitation of a bulk system of bound classical atoms with pair interaction is considered as a result of formation of vacancies, and parameters of vacancies depend on the degree of excitation. On the basis of statistics of a gas of such vacancies, requirements to aggregate states of the system are formulated. From these requirements and data for real condensed rare gases, parameters of vacancy interaction are determined for the liquid state of rare gases.

## 1. Introduction
Melting, the phase transition between the solid and liquid states, proceeds in bulk systems at a fixed temperature and is accompanied by a certain variation of the internal energy of the system. Within the framework of thermodynamics, this phase transition is analyzed by a general method for systems with a different character of interaction between atomic particles of the system [1-3]. The thermodynamic methods give convenient relations between phenomenological parameters of the system. The microscopic description of this phenomenon is more complicated and depends on the character of interaction in the system. Below we consider this problem for the bulk systems of bound atoms with a pair interaction between them, and give parameters of this phenomenon for rare gases on the basis of real data.

Understanding of some aspects of the microscopic nature of this phenomenon from computer analysis of clusters, as a system of a finite number of bound classical atoms, is the basis of methods of molecular dynamics [4-10]. These studies show the collective character of this phenomenon and discover special peculiarities of the phase transition for clusters. In particular, the liquid and solid states of clusters, as systems of a finite number of bound atoms, coexist in some range of temperatures, whereas the phase transition of bulk systems proceeds at a fixed temperature. It is of principle that the liquid state of clusters results from formation of vacancies in cluster shells. Two or more phase states occur depending on the shell where vacancies are formed, and the surface liquid states when vacancies are formed on the cluster surface or shells near the surface exist in a restricted range of temperatures. At higher temperatures these vacancies join with internal ones. Thus, the liquid state of clusters with a pair interaction of atoms differs from the solid one by the number of vacancies, i.e., the total cluster volume in the liquid state is more than that of the solid one.

On the basis of cluster investigations, one can define the liquid phase state of the system of bound atoms with pair interaction as a group of statistically profitable excited states of this system, and the total volume of the system for these excited states exceeds that of the solid phase state of this system. It is clear that the properties of the liquid phase state are determined by collective effects of interaction of atoms, so that it is impossible to analyze this state on the basis of general considerations and analytical methods. One can expect the subsequent progress in detailed understanding of the melting process as a result of usage of methods of molecular dynamics. Below we consider this problem from another standpoint [11], formulating general requirements of existence of the liquid phase state for a system of interacting atoms and using parameters of real condensed rare gases. From this one can evaluate the parameters of condensed rare gases which describe the microscopic picture of the phase transition.

So, we consider excited states of a bulk system of bound atoms with pair interaction between atoms as a result of increase of the volume or as a result of formation of vacancies inside it. Then we characterize excited states of the system under consideration by parameters of vacancies. An excited state of the system under consideration can be prepared in the following way. Take a large cluster consisting of $n + v$ atoms and remove $v$ atoms outside. As a result of interaction of atoms, this cluster containing $n$ atoms and $v$ vacancies is compressed. If the number of vacancies is small such that neighboring vacancies do not border, the energy of formation of vacancies is equal to the binding energy $\varepsilon_0$ of the solid per one atom. Indeed, the total binding energy of atoms for the initial solid cluster consisting of $n + v$ atoms equals $(n + v)\varepsilon_0 = 6(n + v)D$, where $D$ is the binding energy per one bond. Formation of each vacancy in this case requires breaking of 12 bonds, i.e. the binding energy of the forming cluster is $6(n - v)D = (n - v)\varepsilon_0$. Because the binding energy of the solid cluster of $n$ atoms is $n\varepsilon_0$, the energy per one vacancy is equal to $\varepsilon_0$ in this limiting case. Next, if the temperature is small, and atoms which are neighbors of a vacancy do not change their positions after formation of the vacancy, the vacancy statistical weight is equal to $g = 1$. Distortion of atoms positions as a result of vacancy formation gives $g > 1$. This behavior of vacancies corresponds to absence of interaction between vacancies and respects to the criterion

$$n \gg 12v \tag{1}$$

Violation of the criterion (1) demands to account for interaction of vacancies. Then the statistical weight of an individual vacancy $g > 1$ and a forming bulk cluster is compressed as a result of interaction of vacancies. Nevertheless, it is convenient to describe an excited system of bound atoms under consideration as a system of interacting vacancies. Then we use as parameters of this system $v$ - number of vacancies, $\varepsilon$ - the energy of vacancy formation, $g$ - the statistical weight of a vacancy. We take the energy of formation of an individual vacancy in the form

$$
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}-V\left(\frac{v}{n}\right),\tag{2}
$$

where $V$ is the interaction potential of vacancies. Thus, considering this system to be a gas of vacancies with the above parameters, one can represent a partition function of this system in the form

$$
Z(v)=C_{n+v}^{v}g^{v}\exp\left(-\frac{v\varepsilon}{T}\right),\tag{3}
$$

i.e., the interaction of vacancies is taken into account by the dependence of the energy of vacancy formation $\varepsilon$ on a number of vacancies. Here $C_{n+v}^{v}$ is a number of combination for forming vacancies.

It is necessary to give a more precise definition to the above system of bound atoms with vacancies in order to satisfy the thermodynamic conditions of the system. First, we characterize an excited state of a bulk system of bound atoms by an excitation energy together with the temperature of atoms connected with their kinetic energy. This state is formed by removing $v$ atoms outside a solid system of $n+v$ atoms. Hence, the final state of the system is characterized by the excitation energy $v\varepsilon=v(\varepsilon_{0}-V)$. Second, for thermodynamic comparison of different excited states it is necessary to surround the system by a gas of atoms, so that the equilibrium takes place between this gas and the system of bound atoms. We assume the pressure of the gas to be a constant at different excited energies of the system, but this pressure is relatively small, so that the parameters of the system do not depend on the gaseous pressure. Third, as a result of processes of evaporation of atoms of the system and attachment of gaseous atoms to its boundary, the number of vacancies $v$ and the excitation energy varies in time. We assume this time to be large compared to the typical time of establishment of equilibrium for a given excitation of the system.

Under the considered conditions, we have from the relation (3) for the partition function of a gas of interacting vacancies in the limit $n\gg1,v\gg1$:

$$
S(v)=\ln Z(v)=v\left(1+\ln\frac{ng}{v}-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}-V}{T}\right).\tag{4}
$$

First, we analyze the connection between the excitation energy of the bulk system of bound atoms and its density. Let us introduce an effective number of vacancies $v_{ef}$, reducing this value increases the volume of the system of bound atoms. We have $v_{ef}=v\varepsilon_{0}/\varepsilon$, so that the density $\rho$ of the forming system is connected with the density of the solid state $\rho_{sol}$ by the relation

$$
\frac{n+v_{ef}}{n}=\frac{\rho_{sol}}{\rho},
$$

because $v_{\text{ef}}$ characterizes the variation of the system volume as a result of its excitation. Introducing the effective number of nearest neighbors atoms $q$ for atoms of this system, we have from the definition of $v_{\text{ef}}$

$$
q=12 \frac{n-v_{\text{ef}}}{n}.
$$

Thus, we have a relation between the number of nearest neighbors $q$ and the density $\rho$ of the system of bound atoms

$$
q=\frac{24-12 \rho_{\text{sol}}}{\rho}.
$$

On the other hand, the binding energy of cluster atoms varies from $n \varepsilon_{0}$ up to $q \varepsilon_{0} / 2$ as a result of excitation. Introducing the excitation energy per one atom $\Delta H=\varepsilon v / n$, we connect it with the effective number of nearest neighbors

$$
\Delta H=(1-q / 12) \varepsilon_{0}.
$$

From this it follows

$$
\Delta H=\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\rho_{\text{sol}}-\rho}{\rho}.
$$

In particular, for the liquid state of the bulk system of bound atoms, when the density of the liquid state equals $\rho_{\text{liq}}$ and the fusion energy per one atom equals $\Delta H_{\text{fus}}$, it is necessary to introduce the parameter

$$
\xi=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\Delta H_{\text{fus}}} \frac{\rho_{\text{sol}}-\rho}{\rho}. \tag{5}
$$

This parameter is equal to unity, if the above relations are valid. Data of Table I show the accuracy of this relation for real condensed rare gases. In addition, from the definition of the specific fusion energy $\Delta H_{\text{fus}}$ it follows the relation:

$$
\Delta H_{\text{fus}} n=\left[\varepsilon_{0}-V\left(\frac{v_{\text{liq}}}{n}\right)\right] v_{\text{liq}}, \tag{6}
$$

where $v_{\text{liq}}$ is the number of vacancies for the liquid state of the bulk system of bound atoms.

First we consider the solid state of the bulk system of bound atoms when one can neglect the interaction between vacancies ($V=0$), and the solid state of the system corresponds to the maximum of $S(v)$ at small numbers of vacancies. It gives

$$
S_{\text{sol}}=v_{\text{sol}}=n g \exp \left(-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{T}\right) \ll n. \tag{7}
$$

We account for $\varepsilon_{0} \gg T$ near the melting point (see Table I), so that the criterion (1) is valid for the solid state which allows us to neglect interaction of vacancies for this state.

### 2. Excitation of vacancies

Basing on the microscopic standpoint, we consider excitation of a system of bound atoms as a result of formation of vacancies inside this system. The function $S(v)$ has two maxima, so that the first one respects to the solid state in accordance with formula (7), and the second maximum corresponds to the liquid state. In absence of interaction of vacancies, the function $S(v)$ must drop linearly with increase of $v$ after the solid maximum. Interaction of vacancies turns this dependence. It leads to the existence of a minimum and the liquid maximum of this function. From this we obtain

$$
V \geqslant 0, \tag{8}
$$

i.e. the energy of formation of one vacancy decreases with increase of the excitation energy. Note that from general considerations the existence of a liquid state for a bulk system of bound atoms is problematic because it requires a specific form of the dependence $S(v)$. Hence, existence of a liquid state gives additional information about the character of interaction of vacancies.

The position of the minimum of the function $S(v)$ is given by the equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} v}(v V)\left(v_{\min }\right)-\varepsilon_{0}+T \ln \frac{g n}{v_{\min }} \\
& \quad+T \frac{\mathrm{d} \ln g\left(v_{\min }\right)}{\mathrm{d} \ln v}=0, \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

and we have the same relation for the liquid state

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} v}(v V)\left(v_{\text{liq}}\right)-\varepsilon_{0}+T \ln \frac{g n}{v_{\text{liq}}} \\
& \quad+T \frac{\mathrm{d} \ln g\left(v_{\text{liq}}\right)}{\mathrm{d} \ln v}=0. \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
$$

One more relation corresponds to the melting point $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ of the bulk system under consideration. This temperature respects to the equality of the free enthalpies for the solid and liquid states. This equality is considered at a small pressure of a surrounding vapor, i.e. relates to a certain pressure of the system. Because this pressure is respectively small and does not influence the behavior of vacancies inside the system, one can reduce the equality of the free enthalpies to the equality of the entropies of the liquid and solid state, i.e.

$$
S_{\text{sol}}\left(T_{\mathrm{m}}\right)=S_{\text{liq}}\left(T_{\mathrm{m}}\right).
$$

In the scale of using values one can take $S_{\text{sol}}(T_{\mathrm{m}})=$ $v_{\text{sol}}(T_{\mathrm{m}})=0$, so that this condition has the form

$$
S(v_{\text{liq}})=0=1+\ln \frac{n g}{v_{\text{liq}}}-\frac{\Delta H_{\text{fus}}}{T_{\mathrm{m}}} \frac{n}{v_{\text{liq}}}. \tag{11}
$$

The solution of this equation for $n / v_{\text{liq}}$ depends on the parameter $g$ which characterizes the statistical weight of the system due to formation of one vacancy. One can expect a weak dependence $\ln g(v)$, so that near $v_{\text{liq}}$ it is a constant. As it follows from eq. (11), the liquid state exist in some range of values of the parameter $g$: $g_{\text{max}} > g > g_{\text{min}}$, so that

$$
\begin{aligned}
& g_{\text{min}}=\exp \left(\frac{\Delta H_{\text{fus}}}{T_{\mathrm{m}}}-1\right), \\
& g_{\text{max}}=\frac{\Delta H_{\text{fus}}}{\varepsilon_{0}} \exp \left(-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{T_{\mathrm{m}}}-1\right). \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Some relations for the interaction potential $V(v / n)$ of vacancies follow from the eqs (6), (9)-(11). In particular, from (9) and (10) we get that the relation $\mathrm{d}^{2}(v V) / \mathrm{d} v^{2}=0$ is valid in the range $v_{\text{min}} < v < v_{\text{liq}}$. These relations testify a complex form of the function $V(v / n)$. Below for simplicity we take the function $g(v)$ in the form

$$
g(v)=1+a \frac{v}{n}, \quad a \gg 1. \tag{13}
$$

In the range of interaction of vacancies ($g \gg 1$) the eqs (6), (9)-(11) take the form
$$
V\left(\frac{v_{\text{liq}}}{n}\right)=\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{\Delta H_{\text{fus}}}{T_{\text{m}}} \frac{n}{v_{\text{liq}}}=T_{\text{m}}(1+\ln a),
$$
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} V}{\mathrm{~d} v}\left(v=v_{\text{liq}}\right)=0, \quad F\left(v_{\min }\right)=F\left(v_{\text{liq}}\right), \tag{14}
$$
where $F(v)=\mathrm{d}(V) / \mathrm{d} v$. Note that the third condition follows from the equality $F(v_{\text{liq}})=V(v_{\text{liq}})$.

It is convenient to take the vacancy interaction potential in some form with varying parameters, and then these parameters for condensed rare gases follow from eqs (14) where the parameters of real rare gases are used. We take the interaction potential of vacancies in the form
$$
V\left(\frac{v}{n}\right)=C\left[\exp \left(-\frac{\alpha n}{v}\right)-\exp \left(-k \frac{\alpha n}{v}\right)\right], \tag{15}
$$
where $\alpha$, $C$ are parameters of this value and the parameter $k$ is given. Thus, we have four eqs (14) for determination of five parameters $n/v_{\min}$, $n/v_{\text{liq}}$, $a$, $C$, $\alpha$. Let us take into account that at $v=v_{\min}$ the criterion (1) is violated. Then we take $v_{\min}=n/12$ and the other varying parameters follows from eqs (14). Indeed, the equation $\mathrm{d}V(v_{\text{liq}}/n)/\mathrm{d}v=0$ gives
$$
x_{\text{liq}}=\alpha \frac{n}{v_{\text{liq}}}=\frac{\ln k}{k-1}.
$$

Then from the last eq. (14) we find $F(v_{\min})$ which gives the value of the parameter $x_{\min}=\alpha n/v_{\min}=12\alpha$, i.e. it allows us to determine the value $\alpha$. Next, from the first eq. (14) we find the parameter $C$, and the second equation gives the parameter $a$. These parameters are given in Table II for some values of $k$ and the average parameters of condensed rare gases which are $T_{\text{m}}=0.578$, $\Delta H_{\text{fus}}=0.98$, $\varepsilon_{0}=6.35$.

From data of Table II it follows that statistical parameters of vacancies are close at different parameters of vacancy interaction. Indeed, the average number of vacancies for the liquid state of condensed rare gases equals $v_{\text{liq}}=(0.32\pm0.03)n$, and the average interaction potential of vacancies for the liquid state is equal to $V=3.30\pm0.25$, i.e. these parameters are determined with an accuracy up to 10%, while the coincidence of parameters of vacancy interaction and statistical parameters for these cases coincide within a factor 2-3. Thus, we describe a gas of vacancies by the parameter $v_{\text{liq}}/n$ which is connected with a relatively free volume inside the system and characterizes the degree of excitation of the system of bound atoms. Note that within the framework of classical statistics, variation of the average size of an individual vacancy leads to variation of thermodynamic values by a constant, so that the average physical parameters of the system do not vary in this case.

## 3. Conclusion
Thus we define excited states of a system of bound atoms as a result of formation of a gas of vacancies inside it. These vacancies are identical in average. It means that the shape and volume of an individual vacancy varies in time due to overlapping of neighboring vacancies, so that they are independent in average, and parameters of individual vacancies depend on the degree of excitation of the system of bound atoms. Therefore, the properties of the bulk system of bound atoms are determined by statistics with respect to a gas of vacancies, and the dependence of parameters of the system on the number of vacancies is like this dependence on the free volume inside this system of bound atoms. The description of excitation of the system of bound atoms as formation of a gas of vacancies corresponds to the microscopic nature of the phase transition in this system.
