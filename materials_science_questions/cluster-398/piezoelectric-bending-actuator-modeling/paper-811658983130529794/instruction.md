# Nonlinear amplitude-frequency response of moderately thick laminated piezoelectric plates

## Problem background
Piezoelectric materials can be embedded in composite laminates to create smart structures with integrated sensing and actuation. This task examines the nonlinear free vibration of symmetric cross-ply moderately thick laminated plates that include piezoelectric layers. The goal is to determine how the applied electric voltage and the placement of piezoelectric layers influence the nonlinear amplitude–frequency relationship of the plate. The plate is considered simply supported, and its vibration is described by a dimensionless nonlinear frequency ratio that varies with the amplitude of vibration.

## Approach
The plate kinematics are modelled with the Timoshenko-Mindlin (moderately thick) theory, incorporating geometrically nonlinear strain-displacement relations. The constitutive behaviour of the orthotropic piezoelectric layers includes electromechanical coupling via piezoelectric stress constants, while the composite elastic layers follow standard orthotropic elasticity. The nonlinear governing equations are reduced to a single-degree-of-freedom ordinary differential equation in time using a one-term Galerkin projection with separable spatial functions that satisfy the simply supported boundary conditions. The harmonic balance method is then applied to convert this ODE into a nonlinear algebraic equation that relates the dimensionless vibration frequency to the dimensionless amplitude. The nonlinear amplitude–frequency curves are generated by solving this algebraic equation numerically for different values of applied voltage and for different lamination schemes. The required material properties for graphite-epoxy and PZT5 are provided as fixed inputs.

The first file must contain the curves for the [PZT5/0/90/0/90/0/PZT5] layup under five applied voltages: 0 V, +200 V, +400 V, -200 V, and -400 V. The second file must contain the curves for three different placements of the piezoelectric layers (top/bottom, middle, inner), all at zero applied voltage, using the layup sequences specified in the workflow steps. For each curve, generate a range of dimensionless amplitudes (W/h) and compute the corresponding dimensionless nonlinear frequency ratio (ω/ω₀).
