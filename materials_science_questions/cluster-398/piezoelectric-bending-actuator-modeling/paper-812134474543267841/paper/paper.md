## Abstract
A shear mode PZT actuator for the microdroplet ejecting system is proposed. The plate-shaped actuator poled with remnant polarization perpendicular to the actuating field induces a piezoelectric shear effect. Two poling designs for the shear mode piezoelectric actuator are compared by both analysis and experiment. The two poling designs are the *single-surface poling design* and the *dual-surface poling design*. The single-surface poling design arranges the poling electrodes on only one surface, while the dual-surface poling design arranges the poling electrodes on opposite surfaces. Although the single-surface poling design is convenient for the poling process, its requirement for a higher poling voltage to achieve a coercive field induces specimen failure by surface cracking. So, the dual-surface poling design is preferred because a higher yield and better electromechanical coupling characteristic can be obtained. In both the poling designs, the relation of the shear piezoelectric coefficient, $d_{15}$, and the actuating electric field are obtained with three kinds of sample thickness.

## 1. Introduction
The PZT (lead zirconate titanate) piezoelectric ceramic is now widely used in solid-state actuators which are designed for numerous applications, such as micropositioning mechanism [1, 2] and microdroplet-ejecting technology [3–7]. In the microdroplet-ejecting technology, inkjet printing is the most valuable application. According to the deformation modes of piezoelectric actuators, the commercial piezoelectric inkjet-printing technologies are divided into four main types including bend mode [3], push mode [4], squeeze mode [5] and shear mode [6–8]. And all actuating modes are designed to cause volume change in the pressure chamber or flow channel and to eject a droplet from the orifice. For the bend mode shown in figure 1(a), the diaphragm with the attached piezoelectric actuator is deflected inwardly into the pressure chamber due to the difference in extension between the two layers. For the push mode shown in figure 1(b), the piezoelectric rod pushes the diaphragm by the effect of its extension. For the squeeze mode shown in figure 1(c), the radially polarized piezoelectric tube with the actuating electrodes on its inner and outer surfaces is treated as the pressure chamber, and its squeeze effect makes droplet ejection possible. For the piezoelectric actuator with a shear deformation mode, both the Xaar-type and the Spectra-type inkjet heads, designed respectively by Xaar and Spectra companies, are famous, shown in figure 1(d1) and figure 1(d2). Besides those two commercial designs, a novel design utilizing a shear mode PZT actuator for microdroplet ejecting apparatus is proposed in this study, shown in figure 1(d3). In the Xaar-type design, the poled piezoelectric plate is micromachined to form the flow channel with the channel walls possessing polarization along the direction of the wall. The external electrical field applied across the thickness of the wall is perpendicular to the polarization direction, thus causing the shear deformation of the channel wall and resulting in a

In the Spectra-type design, a piezoelectric plate is poled parallel to the plane of the plate and in the direction extending from the center of the pressure chamber, and the electrodes mounted on the opposite surfaces of the plate imposing an electric field orthogonal to the direction of the polarization. The resulting shear deformation of the actuator decreases the volume of the pressure chamber and thus ejects a liquid drop from the orifice. In this study, the novel actuating module consists of a shear mode PZT actuator and a vibration plate with the feature of a bulge diaphragm shown in figure 2. The bulge diaphragm is shaped with two stacked bulges. The shear mode PZT actuator is glued across the top surface of the upper bulge and the vibration plate, and the diaphragm can be deflected through the bulges. Thus, the resulting sudden decrease in chamber volume causes a droplet to be expelled from the orifice. The vibration plate and nozzle plate are fabricated by multilevel electroforming using UV LIGA technology [9].

Piezoelectric actuators for all kinds of actuating modes are demanded to actuate large displacement and force output. In other words, an excellent electromechanical coupling characteristic is required for a piezoelectric actuator in an inkjet printing application. The poling process of PZT polycrystalline makes the randomly arranged electric dipoles in the ferroelectric domains of each crystallite orient along the poling field. The poling field should be large enough to induce saturated polarization, which results in maximum remnant polarization. Usually, a coercive field is the minimum requirement of the poling field [10]. The parameters of the poling process are important to obtain a better electromechanical characteristic [11-13]. The poling voltage, especially, plays a key role in these parameters. The design of the poling electrodes for the shear mode actuator in this study must be different from that in the conventional extension mode [11-15]. The conventional design has the poling direction across the thickness of the plate of the actuator. However, this uncommon design makes the active region poled along the lateral direction parallel to the plane of the plate actuator. Two poling designs are proposed and compared by experiments and simulations. The simulation is of the poling field during the poling process using the commercial FEM code and the appropriate poling voltage can be predicted.

PZT samples with three different thicknesses were poled by different poling voltages and designs to form the shear mode actuators. And their piezoelectric shear coefficients, as a function of actuating electric field, were obtained by measuring the out-of-plane displacement using a two- dimensional scanning laser vibrometer. The presented paper [16] showed the capability of a laser vibrometer in measuring the displacement of the shear mode PZT actuator for the Spectra's printhead.

2. Sample preparation

The ferroelectric PZT disc for the shear mode actuator was prepared by a dry powder pressing technique using the a commercial available PZT powder (Sunnytec, Taiwan). This type (S-3) [17] of soft PZT powder is widely used to make actuators due to its high electromechanical coupling coefficient and high Curie temperature (say $300\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$). Before compacting, the powder had been heated in an oven at $120\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for more than 4 h to remove the moisture. The powder mixture was placed into a die and compacted by applying an uniaxial pressure of 90 MPa to produce the powder compact. The powder compact was then put into a furnace with oxygen atmosphere heating to remove its polymer binder. Afterwards, it was placed in a closed alumina crucible, in which the green compact was embedded in coarse ZrO2 powders and surrounded by PZT powder as the PbO atmosphere buffer. The sintering process was carried out in a tube furnace under a stationary air atmosphere using a heating rate of $90\ ^{\circ}\text{C}\ \text{min}^{-1}$ to peak temperatures of $1250\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for holding times of 3 h. This was followed by a cooling rate of $90\ ^{\circ}\text{C}\ \text{min}^{-1}$ to intermediate temperature of $780\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for holding 0.3 h, and then cooling to room temperature. The original dimension of the green compact, 20 mm in diameter and 1 mm in thickness, was shrunk by 15% after sintering. It was then thinned to a certain thickness by polishing. Samples

### 3. Poling analysis

As shown in figure 4, the shear mode PZT actuator poled with remnant polarization perpendicular to the actuating field induces a piezoelectric shear effect. Unlike the extension effect of the conventional actuator with both the polarization and actuating field along the plate-thickness direction, this kind of actuator possesses polarization along the lateral plane direction orthogonal to the actuating field. So, it is more difficult for the shear mode actuator to obtain uniform remnant polarization than for the conventional actuator. In order to obtain uniform and sufficient remnant polarization, the electrode design and poling electric field should be clearly analyzed. The commercial finite element code, ANSYS, was utilized, and the cross section of the analysis domain around the expected poled region was taken and meshed to analyze the poling field during the poling process, as shown in figure 5. The pairs of poling electrodes for the samples may be arranged on a single surface or on both surfaces. It is probable that the poling electrodes arranged on both surfaces (called dual-surface poling design hereafter) will be more efficient and uniform than those on a single surface (called single-surface poling design hereafter). Through numerical simulation, some comparisons of the poling fields between the two designs were made.

![](./images/812134474543267841_5.jpg)

The poling field must exceed the coercive field to obtain the required remnant polarization but must not be so large that its dielectric breakdown results. Thus the appropriate poling voltage should be predicted through analysis supposing that the domain dipoles would be repartitioned and oriented to poling field analyzed. The relative permittivity inputted for analysis is a room-temperature constant of 2000 that comes from the datasheet [17], although the experimental samples were poled at $110\ ^{\circ}$C. Increasing the poling temperature raises the permittivity and thus facilitates the poling. The analysis adopts a conservative permittivity to ensure that the predicted poling voltage is sufficient for the sample to be poled to the expected extent. With sufficient remnant polarization, the sample possesses the better electromechanical characteristic and thus needs a lower actuating field to obtain a certain output displacement. According to the datasheet [17], the S-3 type soft PZT powder has a notable dielectric loss or dissipation factor. And the factor usually has a sharp increase at a high actuating field resulting in increased generation of internal

In the case of a well-loaded actuator, it has been assumed that the dielectric loss is considerably greater than the mechanical loss [10].

## 4. Measurement
Samples poled with various voltages by the two designs were prepared. They were driven by ac voltage at a frequency of 5 kHz, far below the resonant frequency, and measured by the two-dimensional scanning laser vibrometer (Polytec MSV300) to obtain their actuated displacements. Each datum obtained is the average value from three measured samples with the same conditions. The deviations of the three measured values from the average must be within 5%. In the case of deviation above 5%, the sample is re-measured or another sample with the same condition is measured. The actuated displacement of the shear mode actuator with certain dimensions depends on the known $d_{15}$ value, the shear piezoelectric coefficient. The higher the shear piezoelectric coefficient, the larger the actuated displacement results. In the case of a sample with an unknown $d_{15}$ value, measuring the actuated out-of-plane displacement under a certain actuating electric field can evaluate the sample's actual value of shear piezoelectric coefficient.

A silicon substrate etched with a trench of width 3 mm was used as the testing fixture for displacement measurement, as shown in figure 6. The shear mode actuator spanned across the trench and both sides were glued on to the substrate tightly. The active region was just the span part. Figure 7 shows the measuring setup of the actuated displacement for the shear mode PZT actuator. The ac input voltage will induce alternatively upward and downward deformations as shown in figure 8(a). And its out-of-plane displacements on the three paths are plotted as three curves in figure 8(b).

## 5. Results and discussion
### 5.1. Poling analysis
The coercive field of the ferroelectric PZT material for this shear mode actuator shows about $4\ \text{MV m}^{-1}$ from its hysteresis curve. The poling field must exceed this magnitude to orientate those dipoles of all domains nearly in the same direction. In order to represent the arrangements of those dipoles during poling, figure 9 shows the vector plots of the poling field in the sample with $250\ \mu\text{m}$ thickness and a poling voltage of 6.5 kV. Also, figures 9(a) and (b) show the comparisons of vector distribution for the poling field, in which the poling electrodes are arranged on a single surface and dual surfaces respectively; the darker arrows symbolize a magnitude larger than $4\ \text{MV m}^{-1}$ while the lighter ones symbolize a magnitude smaller than $4\ \text{MV m}^{-1}$. It is observed that in the single-surface poling design some regions near the surface without poling electrodes, marked by small ellipses, do not exceed the coercive field. In order to clearly identify the completely poled region, the contour distributions of lateral poling field (Ex) for the single-surface and the dual-surface poling designs at a poling voltage of 6.5 kV are, respectively, shown in figures 10(a) and (b). The darker region (or completely poled region) represents a magnitude of the lateral poling field larger than the coercive field of $4\ \text{MV m}^{-1}$, in which the saturated polarization is assumed to be completed resulting in a maximum remnant polarization. As shown in figure 10, there is 85 and 93% of the expected poled region achieving maximum remnant polarization in the single-surface and the dual-surface poling designs, respectively. In order to observe the development of the completely poled region with increasing poling voltage, figure 11 shows a series of contour displays of the completely poled region in the $250\ \mu\text{m}$ thick sample by the single-surface poling design, which are shown at the poling voltages of 6000 V, 6200 V, 6218 V, 6250 V and 6500 V, respectively. It is observed that the dual-surface poling design has the advantage of a lower poling voltage to achieve the same extent of remnant polarization as the single-surface poling design. Thus, the maximum poling voltage achievable for the single-surface poling design is lower than that for the dual-surface poling design for the same thickness. The sample of the dual-surface poling design undergoes uniform strain on the opposite surfaces, so its sample can be poled by a higher voltage. ### 5.3. Actuated displacement and shear piezoelectric coefficient
The central out-of-plane displacement of the shear mode actuator can be estimated by the formula, $W_{\mathrm{c}}=d_{15} L(V / t)$, with a known $d_{15}$ value, where $d_{15}$ is the shear piezoelectric coefficient, $L$ is the lateral distance of each poled zone, $V$ is the actuating voltage and $t$ is the thickness. The poling condition affects the piezoelectric effect or resulting actuated displacement, each sample with a certain poling condition gets a different shear piezoelectric coefficient. And with an actually measured displacement, the real shear piezoelectric coefficient of each sample can be estimated by $d_{15}=(W_{\mathrm{c}} / V)(t / L)$. Table 3 shows the actuated displacements and calculated shear piezoelectric coefficients varying with the actuating voltage under different thicknesses, poling voltages and designs. The shear piezoelectric coefficient does not keep a constant value. It increases as the actuating voltage increases. So, the actuated displacement should be nonlinear to the actuating voltage.

Figures 13–15 show the curves of the $d_{15}$ values for the cases without crack failure (i.e. S1, S2, D1 and D2 conditions), which vary with the actuating electric field. And the curves present a near linear tendency. For the same sample thickness, the three figures all demonstrate that the higher poling voltage is helpful to enhance the shear piezoelectric coefficient. This is because more domain dipoles oriented along the poling field are retained resulting in a higher remnant polarization. Thus, a higher $d_{15}$ value is obtained in the dual-surface poling design due to its higher achievable poling voltage.

Reducing the sample thickness to increase the actuating field is a way to improve the output displacement. But in the single-surface poling design, thinning the sample risks the bend cracking during poling. As table 2 shows, in order to avoid cracks in the single-surface poling design, a lower poling voltage should be used for the thinner sample or the $220\ \mu$m one. Its poling field (Ex) must be insufficient to reach the coercive field in most regions according to the analyzed results of table 1. Thus, as figure 16 shows, the $d_{15}$ value of the thinner sample is smaller than that of the thicker one under the same actuating field.

However, in the dual-surface poling design, the sample does not suffer from non-uniform strain during poling. Thus, it is easy to pole the sample successfully at a relatively higher poling voltage which is enough to overcome the coercive field in the most expected poled region. The three thicknesses of sample can be poled to attain the saturated polarization by the dual-surface poling design at the given poling voltage of this study. So, the increase in the achievable maximum poling voltage by increasing the sample thickness, say the D2 condition, does not improve the $d_{15}$ value, as figure 17 shows. And thinning the sample is somewhat helpful in enhancing the $d_{15}$ value.

### 6. Conclusions

According to the analysis and experimental results for the poling process of the shear mode PZT actuator, the major conclusions are summarized as follows.

![](./images/812134474543267841_17.jpg)

Figure 17. Relation of the shear piezoelectric coefficient and actuating electric field for the comparison of three different sample thicknesses by the dual-surface poling design.

(1) The shear mode PZT actuator was successfully fabricated with actuated displacement as expected and would be applied in the novel actuating module of the microdroplet ejecting system. This plate-shaped actuator possesses two symmetrically poled regions with oppositely lateral polarization direction parallel to the plane.

(2) Both of the poling designs, the single-surface poling and the dual-surface poling, proposed were analyzed through the numerical simulation of a poling electric field. Numerical results show that the dual-surface poling design has the advantage of lowering the poling voltage required to achieve the same extent of polarization as the single-surface poling design. And the appropriate poling voltage would be predicted in order to complete the sample poling and avoid dielectric breakdown.

(3) The poling experiments of both designs show that a thicker sample allows a higher poling voltage without failure. The poling voltage cannot exceed the level that causes a crack damaging the sample. It is believed that the cracks of the single-surface poling design are induced by stress while those of the dual-surface poling design are more likely to be caused by dielectric breakdown.

(4) The sample of the dual-surface poling design undergoes uniform strain on the opposite surfaces. But the single-surface poling design causes the sample to bend because of the non-uniform strains on the opposite surfaces. So, the maximum poling voltage achievable for the single-surface poling design is lower than that for the dual-surface poling design with the same thickness.

(5) By measuring the actuated displacement, the shear piezoelectric coefficients varying with the actuating voltage, thickness and poling condition were obtained. The shear piezoelectric coefficient does not keep a constant value. It increases with the increase of the actuating voltage. A higher $d_{15}$ value can be obtained in the dual-surface poling design because of its higher achievable poling voltage due to the uniform poling-induced strain on the opposite surfaces. And thinning the sample helps to improve the $d_{15}$ value to some extent in the dual-surface poling design while it has no effect on the single-surface poling design.
