Internal Friction of CdS Crystal Caused
by Photoconductivity

By Tomoya OGAWA

The Institute of Physical and Chemical Research
Komagone, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo, Japan
(Received October 31, 1961)

The internal friction of CdS crystal caused by
light irradiation was first observed by Gobrecht et
al¹). They reported that the piezoelectrical self-
oscillation of the thin CdS crystal plate was damped
by the irradiation of blue light and recovered by
superposition of the infrared light.

The present author reported²) that the mechanical
loss of the longitudinal mode along c-axis of CdS
crystal was increased with the intensity of incident
blue light and went through a maximum value and
then it was decreased with more intense light.
At the same time, the sound velocity in the speci-
men showed a dispersion as shown in Fig. 1. This
relaxation phenomenon was explained by the inter-
action of the piezoelectric polarization and con-
duction electrons in CdS crystsl. In this paper the
formulation for this interaction is described.

Since CdS crystal has the symmetry $C_{6v}$, the
piezoelectric polarization along c-axis and the stress
components are derived from the piezoelectric re-
lations as follows,

$$
\boldsymbol{P}_{3}=\left(\varepsilon_{3}^{*}-\varepsilon_{0}\right) \boldsymbol{E}_{3}+e_{31}\left(S_{1}+S_{2}\right)+e_{33} S_{3} \tag{1}
$$

$$
\left.
\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{T}_{1}=c_{11} S_{1}+c_{12} S_{2}+c_{13} S_{3}-e_{31} \boldsymbol{E}_{3} \\
\boldsymbol{T}_{2}=c_{12} S_{1}+c_{11} S_{2}+c_{13} S_{3}-e_{31} \boldsymbol{E}_{3} \\
\boldsymbol{T}_{3}=c_{13}\left(S_{1}+S_{2}\right)+c_{33} S_{3}-e_{33} \boldsymbol{E}_{3}
\end{array}
\right\} \tag{2}
$$

the other components are expressed in the similar
equations but omitted by the next conditions (3) and
(4), where $\boldsymbol{E}_{i}$ and $S_{n}$ are the electric field strength
and the strain component, and $\varepsilon$, $e_{im}$ and $c_{nm}$ are
the dielectric, piezoelectric and elastic-stiffness con-
stants, respectively. At the composite bar method
developed by Marx³) is used in these experiments,
the next conditions are applicable

$$
T_{n}=0\ (n=1,2,4,5,6)\quad T_{3}\neq0 \tag{3}
$$

$$
D_{i}=0\ (i=1,2,3) \tag{4}
$$

where $D_{i}$ is a component of the dielectric displace-
ment vector.

In this case the relationships between the stress
and strain components are easily obtained from eqs.
(1), (2), (3) and (4) as follows,

$$
\boldsymbol{T}_{3}=\left\{\frac{1}{s_{33}{ }^{E}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{3} *}\left(e_{33}-\Delta\right)^{2}\right\} \cdot S_{3} \quad (5)
$$

where
$$
\frac{1}{s_{33}{ }^{E}}=c_{33}-2 c_{13}{ }^{2} /\left(c_{11}+c_{12}\right), \quad \Delta \fallingdotseq 2 \frac{c_{13}}{c_{11}+c_{12}} c_{31}
$$

As the present author treated before⁴), it is assumed that only the dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{3} *$ is a complex quantity, i.e., $\varepsilon_{3} *=\varepsilon_{3}-j \sigma_{3} / \omega$ where $\omega$ is the angular frequency of the mechanical excitation or the mechanical resonance frequency of the specimen. Because the conductivity of CdS, $\sigma$, is maimly changed by light irradiation while the real part of the dielectric constant will be littel changed as well as the elastic and piezoelectric constants.

Then mechanical loss factor $Q^{-1}$ which is the imaginal part of $T_{3} / S_{3}$ and the sound velocity $v_{3}$ are expressed by
$$
Q^{-1}=s_{33}{ }^{E} \frac{\left(e_{33}-\Delta\right)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{3}} \frac{\sigma_{3} / \varepsilon_{3} \omega}{1+\left(\sigma_{3} / \varepsilon_{3} \omega\right)^{2}} \quad (6)
$$

$$
v_{3}=\left(\rho s_{33}{ }^{E}\right)^{-1 / 2}\left\{1+\frac{1}{2} s_{33}{ }^{E} \frac{\left(e_{33}-\Delta\right)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{3}} \frac{1}{1+\left(\sigma_{3} / \varepsilon_{3} \omega\right)^{2}}\right\} \quad (7)
$$

where $\rho$ is the density of CdS.

Eqs. (6) and (7) indicate that this mechanical relaxation of CdS crystal is occured at the conductivity which is $\sigma_{3}=\varepsilon_{3} \omega$. The amount of the maximum mechanical loss is $\frac{1}{2} s_{33}{ }^{E}\left(e_{33}-\Delta\right)^{2} / \varepsilon_{3}$ which is equel to the rate of the change of the sound velocity. These values are estimated by the use of the following values:

$s_{33}{ }^{E}=1.55 × 10^{-11}\left(m \sec ^{2} / \mathrm{kg}\right)$ (obtained from Fig. 1)
$\varepsilon_{3}=9 \varepsilon_{0}{ }^{4)}\left(\varepsilon_{0}=8.85 × 10^{-12}\right.$ farad $\left./ m\right)$
$\left.\begin{array}{l}e_{33} \sim 0.7\left(\mathrm{coul} / \mathrm{m}^{2}\right) \\ e_{33}-\Delta \sim 0.4\left(\mathrm{coul} / \mathrm{m}^{2}\right)\end{array}\right\}^{5)}$

$\rho=4.82 × 10^{3}\left(\mathrm{~kg} / \mathrm{m}^{3}\right)$ (calculated from $x$-ray data)
$\omega=2 \pi \times(127 \mathrm{kc} / \mathrm{sec})$

then $\sigma_{\max }\left(=\varepsilon_{3} \omega\right), Q_{\max }-1$ and the dispersion rate are $6.6 × 10^{-5} \Omega^{-1} m^{-1}, 1.5 × 10^{-2}$ and $1.5 \%$, respectively. As shown in Fig. 1, these observed values from the experiments are $10 × 10^{-5} \Omega^{-1} m^{-1}, 1.2 \times$ $10^{-2}$ and $1.1 \%$, respectively. These three estimated values are in good agreement with the observed ones.

Recently, Nine et al⁶) reported that some crystals (type A) showed a decrease of ultrasonic attenuation with white light application and others (type B) showed an increase of attenuation with white light. These phenomena are also explained by eq. (6), that is, the crystals with higher conductivity than $\sigma=\varepsilon \omega$ (where $\omega$ is an angular frequency of applied ultrasonic wave) show a decrease of attenuation and the crystal with lower conductivity than $\varepsilon \omega$ show an increase of attenuation with white light.

The same mechanical relation will be observed in the compound semiconducting materials of the zinc blende and wurtzite structures such as InSb, ZnO etc. The conductivity of these piezoelectrical semiconducting materials can be measured by the amount of the internal frictions and this method is one of the measurement of conductivity without contacting electrodes.
