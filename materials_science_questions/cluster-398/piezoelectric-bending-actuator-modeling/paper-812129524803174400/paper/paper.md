# A Study on Characteristics of Electromagnetic and Piezoelectric Micro Actuators

Haiwei Lu and Jianguo Zhu, Senior Member, IEEE

Faculty of Engineering, University of Technology, Sydney, Australia
E-mail: haiwei@eng.uts.edu.au, joe@eng.uts.edu.au

Abstract— Electromagnetic actuators are dominant in the macro field. Although there are several hindrances, electromagnetic actuators still have some advantages in the application of micro systems. Piezoelectric actuator is also an attractive drive principle for micro systems due to its large force and simple structure, but some drawbacks inevitably exist. This paper presents a comparative study of both drive principles and a better understanding of the basic performances and limitations of the both principles for the system design.

Index Terms—Actuators, micro actuators, electromagnetic, piezoelectric.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Electromagnetic actuators/motors are dominant in the macro scale machines. With the development of the micro and nano technologies, micro machines are interested by many scientists due to their great potential of application in the new century. However, because of the complicated fabrication and miniaturization, electromagnetic actuation has many restrictions when used in the micro systems. Fortunately, with the fast development of the micro- and nano-technologies [1], many new nano-structured magnetic materials have been successfully developed for micro electromagnetic applications and the manufacturing of micro machines with complicated structures is no longer a problem. The electromagnetic actuator is becoming a competitor in the micro world.

The piezoelectric actuator is another option. Piezoelectric actuators are much attractive for micro applications because of their simple structures and large force output. They have been used successfully applied in precision machining. Many interesting designs for the micro robotics have also been developed in the past years such as inch-worn actuators and ultrasonic micromotors.

Since there are several actuation principles that could be used in micro systems, the selection of the actuation principles is an important issue that must be resolved before the design of a micro machine. From this point of view, it is necessary to conduct a comparative study between the electromagnetic and piezoelectric actuators such that a set of valuable guidelines for designing micro machines can be established.

## II. BASIC PRINCIPLES OF ELECTROMAGNETIC AND PIEZOELECTRIC ACTUATOR

### A. Electromagnetic Actuator

A simplest structure of an electromagnetic actuator consists of a current carrying conductor in a magnetic field produced by a permanent magnet. The electromagnetic force on the conductor can be obtained through the co-energy of the system:

$$
F_{z}=\frac{1}{2} \mu_{0} I_{1}^{2} \frac{\left(g w+(w-2 z) l_{m}\right) l}{g w\left(g+l_{m}\right)}+\mu_{0} H_{c} I_{1} \frac{l_{m} l}{g+l_{m}} \quad(1)
$$

where $H_{c}$ is the coercive force of the permanent material and $l_{m}$ the length of the PM, $w, l, g$ are the height, depth and length of the air gap. Normally, the magnetic field in the air gap generated by the current in the conductor is considerably small compared to that of the permanent magnet. By considering the flux density in the air gap $B_{g}=\mu_{0} H_{c} l_{m} /(g+l_{m})$, the magnetic force can be simply expressed as:

$$
F_{z}=B_{g} I_{1} l \quad(2)
$$

### B. Piezoelectric Actuator

Generally, the constitutive characteristics of piezoelectric materials are given by the following two basic tensor equations [2]:

$$
D_{i}=\varepsilon_{i j}^{T} E_{j}+d_{i l} T_{l} \quad(3)
$$

$$
S_{k}=d_{j k} E_{j}+s_{k l}^{E} T_{l} \quad(4)
$$

where $D_{i}$ is the electric displacement, $E_{j}$ the electric field strength, $T_{l}$ the state of stress, $S_{k}$ the state of the strain, and $\varepsilon_{i j}$, $s_{k l}, d_{i l}$ are the dielectric, elastic and piezoelectric constants of the piezoelectric material, respectively. The superscripts $T$ and $E$ indicate that the permittivity $\varepsilon_{i j}$ and the compliance $s_{k l}$ are measured under the conditions of either

constant stress or constant electric field. The subscripts $i, j=1,2,3$ and $k, l=1,2,3,4,5,6$ are the axis directions shown in Fig.1.

Consider an application of actuation which employs the converse piezoelectric effect and assumed that the actuator is rigidly held so as not to deform in any directions, i.e. $S_k$=0. The force produced by the piezoelectric material can be found as:

$$
F_{i}=T_{i} A_{l}=-\frac{d_{j k}^{t}}{s_{k l}^{E}} E_{j} A_{l} \tag{5}
$$

where $A_l$ is the area of the plane which produces force.

### III. STRUCTURES OF THE ACTUATORS FOR COMPARISON

In order to make a comparison of electromagnetic and piezoelectric actuators, linear type actuators with similar structures and volumes are selected. As shown in Fig.2(a), an electromagnetic actuator is made up of a pair of poles of permanent magnets and a current-carrying coil. The coil could move along the gap due to the electromagnetic force when it is excited by a current. For convenience, the permeability of the soft magnetic frame for the magnetic flux circulation at the top and bottom of the actuator is assumed infinity so that their thickness can be ignored. In addition, the length of the air gaps between the coil and the fixed parts of the actuator and the fringing effect are also ignored to simplify the analysis. These assumptions are reasonable and therefore will not cause much error in the analysis.

The structure of the piezoelectric actuator is rather simple. It is made of a piezoelectric bar as shown in Fig.2(b). When supplying a voltage in the $z$ axis, the actuator can extend or constrict in the $x(1)$ direction.

### IV. ANALYTICAL STUDY OF ELECTROMAGNETIC ACTUATOR

Assume that the dimensions of the electromagnetic actuator are as shown in Fig.3(a). With a given volume of $l×w×t$, the current carrying coil is specified as just one turn with the width of $l/4$ so that it can have a space to move within the magnetic field. The thickness of the permanent magnet is chosen to achieve a maximum output force. By analyzing its magnetic circuit, the electromagnetic force produced by the current carrying coil is:

$$
F=2 \frac{\mu_{0} H_{c} w l}{t} \frac{J(t-h) h}{4} \tag{6}
$$

where $J$ is the current density in the coil. Equation (6) shows that the force reaches the maximum value when $h=t/2$ under the condition of $J=$ const. Therefore, the optimal permanent magnet thickness is half of the total height of the actuator.

Let $h=t/2$, and the electromagnetic force can be found as:

$$
F=K_{f} I \tag{7}
$$

where $K_f$=$\mu_0H_cw$ and $I$ is the current in the coil.

When the coil is excited by a sinusoidal current (Fig.3(b)), the force equation can be rewritten in complex notation as:

$$
\hat{F}=K_{f} I e^{j \omega t}=K_{f} \hat{I} \tag{8}
$$

If there is no load and damper associated with the moving coil, and the displacement of the coil will not exceed the range of $l/4$ so that the coil will remain in the constant flux density region, the kinetic equations of the moving coil can be expressed as:

$$
\hat{\dot{x}}=\hat{F} / m \tag{9} \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{\dot{x}}=d \hat{x} / d t \tag{10}
$$

where $\hat{\dot{x}}, \hat{x}$ are the velocity and displacement of the coil in complex notation and $m$ the mass of the coil. By solving (9) ,(10) with (8), the following relationship can be obtained:

$$
\hat{\dot{x}}=\frac{K_{f}}{j \omega m} I e^{j \omega t} \tag{11}
$$

$$
\hat{x}=\frac{K_{f} I}{\omega^{2} m}\left(1-e^{j \omega t}\right) \tag{12}
$$

$$
\hat{V}=\left(\frac{1}{j \omega C_{m e}}+j \omega L\right) I e^{j \omega t} \tag{13}
$$

![](./images/812129524803174400_4.jpg)

Fig.4 Equivalent circuit of electromagnetic actuator

where $L$ is the self inductance of the coil and $C_{me}=m/K_{f}^{2}$ .

An equivalent circuit can be drawn based on the above relationship to express the energy transfer of the actuator as shown in Fig.4. In the circuit, capacitor $C_{me}$ can be regarded as an electric device which transfers the electrical energy to the mechanical energy and $K_{f}$ as the electromechanical coupling factor. Thus, the velocity of coil can be further expressed as:

$$
\dot{x}=\frac{1}{K_{f}} \frac{1}{C_{m e}} \int i(t) d t \tag{14}
$$

### V. ANALYTICAL STUDY OF PIEZOELECTRIC ACTUATOR

Piezoelectricity can be regarded as a linear interaction between electrical and mechanical systems. The empirical equivalent circuit model based on the electromechanical constants in terms of impedance or admittance constitutes a powerful theoretical approach for analyzing the performance of piezoelectric devices, and thereby avoiding the complexity during analysis and the discrepancy between theoretical linear and practical nonlinear problems.

Considering a piezoelectric beam with cross section area of $A=wt$ and length of $l$, it is subjected to a deformation of a transverse type (T-effect) when driven by an alternating voltage, as shown in Fig.5. If power dissipation in the piezoelectric is not taken into account, by the constitutive equations of piezoelectricity and kinetic laws, the forces at each end of the piezoelectric beam and the input voltage/current can be given by the following equations in terms of complex notation [3]:

$$
\hat{I}=-\Phi \dot{\hat{x}}_{0}+\Phi \dot{\hat{x}}_{l}+j \omega C_{d} \hat{V} \tag{15}
$$

$$
\hat{F}_{0}=\left(Z+Z_{0}\right) \dot{\hat{x}}_{0}-Z \dot{\hat{x}}_{l}+\Phi \hat{V} \tag{16}
$$

$$
\hat{F}_{l}=Z \dot{\hat{x}}_{0}-\left(Z+Z_{0}\right) \dot{\hat{x}}_{l}+\Phi \hat{V} \tag{17}
$$

![](./images/812129524803174400_5.jpg)

Fig.5 Piezoelectric transducer of T-effect

![](./images/812129524803174400_6.jpg)

Fig.6 Mason's equivalent circuit model of piezoelectric

where:
$V, I$: the input voltage and current;
$F_{0}, F_{l}$: the forces at the position of $x=0$ and $l$;
$\dot{x}_{0}, \dot{x}_{l}$ : the velocities at the position of $x=0$ and $l$;
$Z, Z_{0}$: the elastic impedances of the actuator,
$$
Z=\frac{\rho A v_{p h}}{j \sin (k l)} \quad \text { and } \quad Z_{0}=j \rho A v_{p h} \tan (k l / 2),
$$
$$
v_{p h}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho s_{11}^{E}}} \text { the phase velocity, } k=\omega / v_{p h} \text { the wave }
$$
number, $\rho$ the mass density of the piezoelectric material;
$C_{d}$: the clamped capacitance of the actuator,
$$
C_{d}=\varepsilon_{33}^{S} \frac{w l}{t}, \quad \text { and } \quad \varepsilon_{33}^{S}=\varepsilon_{33}^{T}\left(1-\frac{d_{31}^{2}}{s_{11}^{E} \varepsilon_{33}^{T}}\right) ;
$$
$\Phi$: the electromechanical coupling factor of the actuator, and
$$
\Phi=w \frac{d_{31}}{s_{11}^{E}}.
$$

According to the above equations, an equivalent circuit can be drawn as shown in Fig.6, which is known as Mason's equivalent circuit for piezoelectric beam. In order to acquire a movement in a certain direction, one end of the device is usually fixed when piezoelectric device acts as an actuator, as shown in Fig.7. Therefore, the velocity at position $x=0$ will be zero. If the external force at position $x=l$ is $F$, (15), (16) and (17) will become:

$$
\hat{I}=\Phi \dot{\hat{x}}_{l}+j \omega C_{d} \hat{V} \tag{18}
$$

$$
\hat{F}_{0}=-Z \dot{\hat{x}}_{l}+\Phi \hat{V} \tag{19}
$$

$$
\hat{F}=-\left(Z+Z_{0}\right) \dot{\hat{x}}_{l}+\Phi \hat{V} \tag{20}
$$

Similar to the electromagnetic actuator and assuming that there is no load and damper, i.e. $F=0$, the force, velocity and current can then be found as:

![](./images/812129524803174400_7.jpg)

Fig.7 Piezoelectric actuator with one end fixed

$$
\hat{F}_{\bullet}=\frac{Z_{0} \Phi}{Z+Z_{0}} \hat{V}
$$

$$
\hat{I}=\left(\frac{\Phi^{2}}{Z+Z_{0}}+j \omega C_{d}\right) \hat{V}=\left(Y_{m p}+j \omega C_{d}\right) \hat{V}
$$

$$
\hat{\dot{x}}_{t}=\frac{\Phi}{Z+Z_{\bullet}} \hat{V}=\frac{1}{\Phi} Y_{m p} \hat{V}
$$

where $Y_{m p}=\Phi^{2} /\left(Z+Z_{0}\right)$.

Based on the derivation, a simplified equivalent circuit shown in Fig.8 can be drawn out. In the circuit, $Z / \Phi^{2}$ is regarded as the motional capacitance caused by strain in the piezoelectric beam, and $Z_{\bullet} / \Phi^{2}$ can be taken as motional inductance which is directly related to the mechanical performances. In addition, the currents and voltage in the piezoelectric actuator can be expressed as:

$$
\hat{I}_{d}=j \omega C_{d} \hat{V}
$$

$$
\hat{I}_{m}=Y_{m p} \hat{V}=\Phi \hat{\dot{x}}_{t}
$$

$$
\hat{V}_{m}=\frac{Z_{0}}{Z+Z_{0}} \hat{V}=\frac{\hat{F}_{0}}{\Phi}
$$

### A. Piezoelectric Force

Form the derived dynamic model of the piezoelectric actuator, the actuation force will vary at different strain conditions of the actuator. The maximum force occurs when actuator is rigidly clamped at both ends or in its resonance state (when $k l=n \pi$ for $n=2 m-1$ ). In this case, the velocity $\hat{\dot{x}}$ will be zero and from (19), the maximum force is obtained as:

$$
\hat{F}_{\max }=\Phi \hat{V}
$$

### B. Displacement

When excited by a low frequency or DC voltage, the unloaded (unclamped) actuator can reach its maximum strain. By observing that:

$$
Z+Z_{0}=\frac{\rho A v_{p h}}{j \sin (k l)}+j \rho A v_{p h} \tan (k l / 2)=\frac{\rho A v_{p h}}{j \tan (k l)}
$$

(23) can be rewritten as:

$$
\hat{\dot{x}}_{t}=j \frac{\tan (k l)}{\rho A v_{p h}} \Phi \hat{V}
$$

![](./images/812129524803174400_8.jpg)

Fig.8 simplified equivalent circuit

When the operational frequency is much below the resonance frequency, $k l$ would be a small value and $\tan (k l)$ could be replaced by $k l$. Therefore, (28) becomes:

$$
\hat{\dot{x}}_{t} \approx j \frac{(k l)}{\rho A v_{p h}} \Phi \hat{V}=j \frac{\omega l}{\rho A v_{p h}^{2}} \Phi \hat{V}
$$

The displacement of the actuator can be obtained by integrating (29):

$$
\hat{x}=\int \hat{\dot{x}} d t=\frac{l}{\rho A v_{p h}^{2}} \Phi \hat{V}+l=\Delta \hat{x}+l
$$

Therefore, the displacement is:

$$
\Delta \hat{x}=\frac{l}{\rho A v_{p h}^{2}} \Phi \hat{V}=d_{31} \hat{E}_{3} l
$$

It can be seen that the result agree with that derived from the constitutive equations of piezoelectric materials when all the stress $T_{1}=T_{2}=T_{3}=0$.

### VIII. CONCLUSION

A comparative study of micro-actuators operated in electromagnetic and piezoelectric principles is presented. Analytical models of the both types of the actuators are derived and studied. It is shown that that electromagnetic actuator is a kind of current source type device while the piezoelectric actuator is a kind of voltage source type device. The relationships in electrical and mechanical characteristics are similar to some extent and there is certain reciprocity between them. More detailed performance study indicates that the piezoelectric actuator has a favorable force capability while the electromagnetic actuator has a larger driving range. Both kinds of actuators can have good dynamic characteristics.
