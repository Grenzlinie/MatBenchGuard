# The Control of Elastic Traveling Waves in Thin-film Free Structures

Dmitry M. Nikulin, Vladimir V. Chesnokov, Dmitry V. Chesnokov
Siberian State Geodetic Academy, Novosibirsk, Russia

**Abstract** - A converter-display device for opto-electronic imaging is considered in this work. The image scanning is carried out by modulated elastic waves traveling on a one-dimensional matrix of micromechanical strip waveguides [1]. Waveguides are formed by dividing a silicon membrane on parallel strips, which are attached to a supporting silicon frame by their ends. When individual exciting of all waveguides using the appropriate piezoelectric transducers, the surface of the display device will contain $N \times M$ relief elements, where N - number of waveguides, M - number of separated bending deformations

**Index Terms** - Elastic waves, thin-film-free structure

## I. INTRODUCTION

WHEN CREATING THE MEASURING devices the solid-acoustic sensors are widely used. The principle of such sensor operation is based on the use of surface acoustic waves (SAW) and on the principle of mechanical vibrations detection in solids [2]. The distribution specific of such waves in the material is that they are formed on the surface of a relatively thick substrate. The sensors operate using the elastic bending waves which emerge in a thin membrane, are also known.

In certain literature, there are only a few references to studies on the development of devices for elastic waves in membranes. In [2] a measuring transducer using piezoelectric excitation and readout of flexural waves in a silicon membrane is described.

## II. PROBLEM

The results of the analysis and calculations of elastic traveling waves in thin-film free structure are presented in the paper.

## III. THEORY

Fig.1 shows a scheme of the converter-display device for opto-electronic imaging. The image scanning is carried out by modulated elastic waves traveling on a one-dimensional matrix of micromechanical strip waveguides Strips of micro-waveguides 1 are located as a one-dimensional matrix over the surface of the base 3. The ends 2 of the strips are connected to the piezotransducers 4, which attached to the base. In piezotransducer the elastic dilatational oscillations(fluctuations in thickness) are excited by AC voltage $U_{1\sim}$ and $U_{2\sim}$; 5, 6, 7 and 8 - electrodes of converters.

![](./images/811820736493649922_1.jpg)

Fig. 1. Micromechanical 2-D display device controlled by elastic traveling waves.

When the AC voltage $U_{1\sim}$ in applied, the piezoelectric element (Fig. 1) periodically changes its thickness with frequency of the AC voltage. The location of the end of the strip attached to the element, changes. Traveling elastic wave whose direction is shown by horizontal arrow is exited in a strip. The amplitude of the wave is equal to $\Delta \ell$. If you do not take into account the resonance effects in the piezoelectric element, the amplitude of the wave can be determined by the expression:
$$\Delta \ell = \Delta \ell_{n} = \ell d_{33} E_{0} = d_{33} U_{0},$$
where $d_{33}$ - piezoelectric modulus of piezoelectric material, $\Delta \ell$ - strain on the thickness of the piezoelectric element, $E_{0}$ - peak value of the electric field in the piezoelectric element, $U_{0}$ - the amplitude of the AC voltage.

The thickness $\ell$ of the piezoelectric element can be found taking into account that not more than a quarter of the longitudinal length of the elastic wave should be allocated through the thickness of the piezoelectric element:
$$\ell = \frac{c_{p}}{4 f_{0}},$$
where $c_{p}$ - the speed of sound in the piezoelectric element, $f_{0}$ - the frequency of supply voltage. The wavelength of the flexural deformation of free hung

strip with its own stiffness is determined by the expression [3]:

$$
\Lambda^{2}=\frac{2 \pi h}{f_{0}} \sqrt{\frac{E}{12 \rho\left(1-\sigma^{2}\right)}}.
$$

Where $h$ - the thickness of the strip, $E$ - Young's modulus, $\rho$ - density, $\sigma$ - Poisson's ratio, $f_{0}$ - the oscillation frequency equal to the frequency of the excitation voltage $U_{\sim}$.

If the traveling wave reflects from the second end of the strip, then ordered picture of relief of the flexural wave is disturbed. To reduce the amplitude of the reflected wave the AC voltage $U_{2 \sim}$ must be applied to the electrodes 7 and 8 of piezoelectric elements. The applied voltage must be of such amplitude and phase, that piezotransducer excites the elastic wave, equal by amplitude and opposite in phase to the passed wave, in a strip. As a result of interference, waves extinguish each other, and traveling-wave regime is realized in strips. It is possible to apply such mentioned voltages that the standing wave is excited in a strip. The amplitude of this wave is known to be 4 times larger than the amplitude of traveling wave with equal intensities.

Traveling wave makes possible the smooth change of the wavelength in the strip by a continuous and smooth change of frequency of alternating voltage. That is also supported by the piezoexciting of the elastic wave directly at the end of the strip, as there are no resonance effects when transferring the oscillation energy from the source of alternating voltage to the elastic wave throughout the frequency range from zero to one in which there no standing-wave regime enabled in the piezoelectric element, that is, until the thickness of the piezoelectric element do not hold a quarter wavelength.

When irradiating the display device by the monochromatic light, optical information reading is possible by the interference pattern, if you set semitransparent mirror parallel to the waveguide matrix.

Combining with the corresponding optical scheme, the display device can also perform optical Fourier transform, if the information is written in the waveguides as a series of harmonic trains of oscillations frequency modulated by process being investigated. Or it can operate as a diffraction grating with a tunable diffraction constant when excitating all the waveguides by an electrical signal of one tunable frequency.

In the work [4] an analysis of the transmitting grating diffraction with a sinusoidal distribution of its period transmittance is presented. Also it is shown in the work that the angular distribution of radiation intensity in the far zone may be represented as:

$$
\frac{I(\theta)}{I_{m}}=\operatorname{sinc}^{2}\left[\left(\theta+\frac{\lambda}{d}\right) \frac{\pi D}{\lambda}\right]+\operatorname{sinc}^{2}\left[\left(\theta-\frac{\lambda}{d}\right) \frac{\pi D}{\lambda}\right],(1)
$$

where $\frac{I(\theta)}{I_{m}}$ - ratio of the intensity of the radiation diffracted at an angle $\theta$ to the intensity at the maximum of the diffraction pattern, $d$ - a lattice distance, $D$ - the full width of the lattice. As it follows from (1), the diffraction pattern contains only two main peak of +1 and -1 orders.

A similar diffraction pattern is observed in the case of the phase grating with a sinusoidal relief. In the case of the phase (relief) diffraction grating, we can show using [5]:

$$
\frac{I_{m}}{I_{0}} \approx\left(\frac{2 \pi A_{0}}{\lambda} \cos \theta\right)^{2},
$$

where $I_{0}$ - the intensity of light incident on the grating, $A_{0}$ - the amplitude of the sinusoidal relief.

Using an array of separate relatively narrow strips to increase the diffraction grating aperture instead of, for example, using of only one wide strip is deffined by the fact that the strip surface buckling appears and reduces the diffraction characteristics of the device. This buckling is caused by various thermal expansion of the strips and base and by its own elastic stresses when the operation temperature changes. This effect does not occur in the longitudinal direction of the strips, because there are possibility to maintain a constant tightness of strips.

Manufactured experimental samples of electrically controlled diffraction patterns have geometric parameters: width and length of a single strip of 100 $\mu \mathrm{m}$ and 5 mm, respectively, the number of strips in the structure - 50; dimensional matrix of strips has a size of 5×5 mm. The structure is made of a silicon single crystal base.

## IV. CALCULATION RESULTS

If use the piezoceramic PCB-590 (TsTS$_t$ BS-1) with $c_{p}=2,8 \cdot 10^{3} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}, d_{33}=500 \cdot 10^{-12} \mathrm{C} / \mathrm{H}$ as a piezoelectric piezoelectric element, then at frequency of $f_{0}=5 \cdot 10^{6} \mathrm{~Hz}$ and $E_{0}=3 \cdot 10^{5} \mathrm{~V} / \mathrm{m}$ we obtain: $\ell=140 \mu \mathrm{m}, \Delta \ell=0,02 \mu \mathrm{m}, U_{\sim}=40 \mathrm{~V}$.

With a frequency of waves excitation of $f_{0}=5 \cdot 10^{6} \mathrm{~Hz}$, and use rhenium strip($E=2 \cdot 10^{11} \mathrm{~Pa}$, $\rho=21 \cdot 10^{3} \mathrm{kg/m} 3, \sigma \approx 0,3$ ) $0,2 \cdot 10^{-6} \mathrm{~m}$ thick as a material for the waveguide, we obtain the length of the flexural wave $\Lambda=1,6 \cdot 10^{-5} \mathrm{~m}$.

For the waveguide with the characteristics presented above at the frequency of excitation of elastic waves of $f_{0}=5 \cdot 10^{6} \mathrm{~Hz}$ and the wavelength of light

$\lambda=0,5 \cdot 10^{-6} \mathrm{~m}$, assuming $A_{0}=\Delta \ell=2 \cdot 10^{-8} \mathrm{~m}$, we obtain:

$$
\theta \approx \pm \frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}=0,031, \frac{I_{m}}{I_{0}} \approx 0,063.
$$

## V. RESULTS DISCUSSION

Considered electrically controlled diffraction grating can be considered as a thin-film analog of the Bragg acoustooptic modulators. The results of analysis and calculations show the advantage in power consumption and compactness in comparison with the Bragg acousto-optic modulator.

## VI. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION

The device can be used, for example, in micro- mechanical acousto-optic devices for optical infor- mation processing in different measurement conver- ters, for creating light-sized spectrum analyzers.
