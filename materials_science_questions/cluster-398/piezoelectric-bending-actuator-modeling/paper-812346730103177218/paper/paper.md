## Abstract

Static behaviors of piezoelectric cantilever actuator under large electric field have been studied and a nonlinear relationship between the tip deflection or blocking force and the strong applied electric field is proposed. It has been shown that as the magnitude of the electric field increases, the tip deflection or blocking force rapidly increases and the difference between experimental results and linear predictions becomes large. However, the nonlinear predictions based on the present model well agrees with the experimental results within a wide range of applied electric field. The results of the analysis are believed to be useful for materials selection and actuator structure design of actuator in actuator fabrication.

## 1. Introduction

Piezoelectric bending actuators have widely been used in areas related to precision position control, loud-speakers, vi- bration damping, noise control, relays, phonograph pick-up, acoustics and pressure sensing. They can be classified into three categories: bimorph-type bending actuator, multiplayer stacked actuator, and moonie-type flex tensional actuator [1–8]. Bimorph-type bending mode actuator can produce the largest displacement but the generative force is very small. With multilayer actuators, the largest generative force can be produced but the displacement level is usually much lower than that of bending mode actuators. The flex tensional piezoelectric actuators have been developed to bridge the gap between the multilayer actuator and the bending mode actuator, and to provide a relatively large displacement and an intermediate level of generative force [8]. All these ac- tuators have been used in a variety of applications, among which bending mode actuators are most widely used in smart materials and structural applications because of their excel- lent electromechanical properties, easy fabrication, and de- sign flexibility.

In practical applications, a strong electric field is often ap- plied to actuators to achieve sufficiently large displacement or force. Under such circumstances, the performance of the actuator deviates from the result of linear prediction since linear model assumes the piezoelectric effect to be linear at a weak signal. Recently, a new type of air acoustic trans- ducer has been developed as an auxiliary sound source for frequency active noise systems. Such transducer makes use of bimorph or unimorph bending actuators as the driving el- ements. To optimize the actuator or transducer performance, detailed studies on the performance of the bending mode ac- tuators, especially under strong electric field, are necessary.

Tip deflection, blocking force and fundamental bending resonant frequencies are usually used to characterize the performance of cantilever actuators. These properties are strongly dependent on the dimensions of the actuator, piezo- electric and elastic parameters of each lamina, which show obvious nonlinearities under strong applied electric field. Al- though a significant amount of literature deals with the oper- ation mechanism and applications of bimorph and unimorph [7–13], very few research publications have dealt with non- linear piezoelectric effect, especially nonlinear electrostric- tive effect and electroelastic effect under strong electric field.

## 2. Fundamental equations

Configuration of bimorph and unimorph actuators is very simple. Fig. 1 shows some typical examples of actuators in a bending mode. They are constructed by bonding together thin piezoelectric plates or piezoelectric plates and elastic plates in such a manner that when the actuators are driven by externally applied electric fields, a large bending displacement may be induced due to the constraining effect of each layer.

For generalization, consider a laminated plate of total thickness, $h$, consisting of $N$ isotropy layers. The $xy$-plane is taken to be the un-deformed mid-plane of the laminate and $z$-axis is taken positive upward from the mid-plane. The $k$th layer is located between the planes $z = z_k$ and $z = z_{k+1}$ in the thickness direction. Assume that the layers are perfectly bonded together, the bonding layers are neglected, and the piezoelectric layers are polarized with respect to the $z$-axis.

In the case of narrow cantilever bending actuators such as bimorph and unimorph (see Fig. 1), all the displacements and resultant components are independent of $y$. Since the electric field $E_z$ is applied along the thickness (polar axis) direction, $E_x = E_y = 0$. Hence, only axial displacement $u(x,z,t)$ and transverse deflection $w(x,z,t)$ are considered. The displacement field according to *Kirchhoff* hypothesis [14] is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
u(x, z, t) & =u_{0}(x, t)-z \frac{\partial w_{0}}{\partial x}, & w(x, z, t) & =w_{0}(x, t)
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

where $u_0$, $w_0$ are the in-plane displacement ($x$-axis) and transverse deflection at the neutral axis, respectively.

![](./images/812346730103177218_4.jpg)

Fig. 1. Configurations of cantilever piezoelectric bimorph and unimorph actuators. (a) Bimorph in series connection, (b) bimorph in parallel connection, (c) unimorph.

In case of small strains and moderate rotations, the straindisplacement relation can be written as [14]:

$$
\varepsilon_{x}=\varepsilon_{x}^{(0)}+z \varepsilon_{x}^{(1)}=\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial w_{0}}{\partial x}\right)^{2}-z \frac{\partial^{2} w_{0}}{\partial x^{2}}
\tag{2}
$$

where $\varepsilon_x$ is the finite strain, $\varepsilon_{x}^{(0)}$ the membrane strain, and $\varepsilon_{x}^{(1)}$ the flexural (bending) strain.

Because the stress and stain act only along the length $x$ direction, the nonlinear constitutive equation can hence be rewritten as [15]:

$$
\varepsilon_{x}=S_{11}^{E} \sigma_{x}+d_{31} E_{z}+d_{311} \sigma_{x} E_{z}+\frac{1}{2} m_{31} E_{z}^{2}
\tag{3}
$$

where $S_{11}^{E}=1 / E$ is the complaisant coefficient, $E$ the Young's modulus of the piezoelectric ceramic layer (length or $x$-axis direction), $d_{31}$ the piezoelectric compliance coefficient, $m_{31}$ the electrostrictive compliance coefficient, and $d_{311}$ the electroelastic compliance coefficient.

Inversing Eq. (3) and neglecting the forth order electroelastic effect, stress $\sigma_x$ can be written as

$$
\sigma_{x}=\frac{\varepsilon_{x}-\left(d_{31} E_{z}+(1 / 2) m_{31} E_{z}^{2}\right)}{s^{E}+d_{311} E_{z}}.
\tag{4}
$$

Assuming that the electroelastic effect is small compared to the compliance, Eq. (4) can be approximated as follows:

$$
\sigma_{x}=Q_{11} \varepsilon_{x}-E d_{31} E_{z}-\frac{1}{2} E m_{31}^{\prime} E_{z}^{2}.
\tag{5}
$$

The higher order terms than $E_{z}^{2}$ can be neglected because it is assumed that the applied field is not strong compared to the coercive field. $Q_{11}$ and $m_{31}^{\prime}$ in Eq. (5) are respectively the effective stiffness, and effective electrostrictive complaisant considering the electroelastic effect and defined by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{11} & =\frac{1}{S_{11}^{E}+d_{311} E_{z}}=\frac{E}{1+d_{311} E E_{z}}, \\
m_{31}^{\prime} & =m_{31}-2 E d_{31} d_{311}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

The rotational inertia effect is neglected because it is very small compared to the transverse inertia term in most engineering applications to thin plates or beams. Thus, motion can be expressed by [14]:

$$
\frac{\partial N_{x}}{\partial x}=I_{0} \frac{\partial^{2} u_{0}}{\partial t^{2}}
\tag{7}
$$

$$
\frac{\partial^{2} M_{x}}{\partial x^{2}}+N_{x} \frac{\partial^{2} w_{0}}{\partial x^{2}}+q=I_{0} \frac{\partial^{2} w_{0}}{\partial t^{2}}
\tag{8}
$$

where $q$ is the distributed transverse mechanical load, and $I_0$ the mass resultant of inertia defined by

$$
I_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{N} \rho^{(k)}\left(z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right)
\tag{9}
$$

where $\rho^{(k)}$ is the mass density of the $k$th layer, and $N_x$ and $M_x$ are, respectively, the stress resultants and moment resultants per unit length and can be expressed as by using

Eqs. (2) and (5)

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{x} & =\int_{-(h / 2)}^{+(h / 2)} \sigma_{x} \mathrm{~d} z \\
& =A_{11}\left(\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial w_{0}}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\right)-B_{11} \frac{\partial^{2} w_{0}}{\partial x^{2}}-N_{x}^{p}
\end{aligned} \quad(10)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{x} & =\int_{-(h / 2)}^{+(h / 2)} \sigma_{x} z \mathrm{~d} z \\
& =B_{11}\left(\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial w_{0}}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\right)-D_{11} \frac{\partial^{2} w_{0}}{\partial x^{2}}-M_{x}^{p}
\end{aligned} \quad(11)
$$

where $A_{11}, B_{11}, D_{11}$ are the extensional stiffness, the bending-extensional coupling stiffness, the bending stiffness, respectively. $N_{x}^{p}$ and $M_{x}^{p}$ are, respectively, the actuator force resultant and actuator bending moment resultant induced by the applied electric field $E_{z}$ along the thickness (polar axis) and can be expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{11} & =\sum_{k=1}^{N} Q_{11}^{(k)}\left(z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right), & B_{11} & =\frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^{N} Q_{11}^{(k)}\left(z_{k+1}^{2}-z_{k}^{2}\right), \\
D_{11} & =\frac{1}{3} \sum_{k=1}^{N} Q_{11}^{(k)}\left(z_{k+1}^{3}-z_{k}^{3}\right) & &
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
N_{x}^{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{a}} E_{p}^{(k)}\left(d_{31}^{(k)} E_{z}+\frac{1}{2} m_{31}^{\prime(k)} E_{z}^{2}\right) t_{p}^{(k)}
$$

$$
M_{x}^{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{a}} E_{p}^{(k)}\left(d_{31}^{(k)} E_{z}+\frac{1}{2} m_{31}^{\prime(k)} E_{z}^{2}\right) t_{p}^{(k)} t_{p}^{c(k)}
$$

where superscript $(k)$ denotes the $k$ th layer, $N_{a}$ the number of piezoelectric actuating layers, and $t_{p}^{c(k)}=\left(z_{k}+z_{k+1}\right) / 2$ the distance from the midplane to the center of the $k$ th layer. For the substrate layer, $Q_{11}^{(k)}=E_{m}^{(k)}$ where $E_{m}^{(k)}$ is the Young's modulus of the substrate layer, and for piezoelectric actuator layer, $Q_{11}^{(k)}=E_{p}^{(k)} / 1+d_{31}^{(k)} E_{p}^{(k)} E_{z}$ where $E_{p}^{(k)}$ is the Young's modulus of piezoelectric actuator layer.

The boundary conditions for a cantilever actuator are:

$$
u_{0}=w_{0}=w_{0, x}=0, \quad \text { at the clamped end }(x=0)
$$

$$
N_{x}=Q_{x}=M_{x}=0, \quad \text { at the free end }(x=l)
$$

where $Q_{x} \equiv M_{x, x}+N_{x} w_{, x}$ is the transverse shear force resultant at the free end and, $(\cdot)_{, x}$ denotes the derivative with respect to coordinate $x$.

### 3. Static characteristics of bending actuators

At a strong electric field, the tip deflection and blocking force show a nonlinear relationship with the applied electric field. Under static conditions, the inertial terms in Eqs. (7) and (8) can be neglected. When the actuator is driven by an applied electric field and in the absence of externally applied mechanical load, the force and moment resultants can be obtained as:

$$
N_{x}=A_{11}\left(u_{0, x}+\frac{1}{2} w_{0, x^{2}}\right)-B_{11} w_{0, x x}-N_{x}^{p}=0
$$

$$
M_{x}=B_{11}\left(u_{0, x}+\frac{1}{2} w_{0, x^{2}}\right)-D_{11} w_{0, x x}-M_{x}^{p}=0 .
$$

Eliminating $u_{0}(x)$ from Eqs. (17) and (18), the differential equation to relate $w_{0}(x)$ can be obtained:

$$
\left(\frac{B_{11}^{2}}{A_{11}-D_{11}}\right) w_{0, x x}+\left(\frac{B_{11} N_{x}^{p}}{A_{11}-M_{x}^{p}}\right)=0 .
$$

Integrating Eq. (19) with the boundary conditions given in Eq. (15), the deflection can be derived as

$$
w_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{B_{11} N_{x}^{p}-A_{11} M_{x}^{p}}{A_{11} D_{11}-B_{11}^{2}}\right) x^{2} .
$$

Substituting $w_{0}(x)$ into Eq. (17) and integrating about $u_{0}(x)$, the displacement component $u_{0}(x)$ under boundary conditions (15) can be obtained:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_{0}(x)= & -\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{B_{11} N_{x}^{p}-A_{11} M_{x}^{p}}{A_{11} D_{11}-B_{11}^{2}}\right)^{2} x^{3} \\
& +\frac{D_{11} N_{x}^{p}-B_{11} M_{x}^{p}}{A_{11} D_{11}-B_{11}^{2}} x .
\end{aligned}
$$

If a concentrated force $F$ acts at the tip of the cantilever, the deflection along the cantilever can be easily shown are [16]:

$$
w_{0}(x)=\frac{F}{E J}\left(-\frac{1}{6} x^{3}+\frac{1}{2} l x^{2}\right)
$$

where $l$ is the length of the cantilever and $E J$ the sectional moment of inertia defined as

$$
E J=\int_{z^{\prime}} E b z^{\prime 2} \mathrm{~d} z^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{b E^{(k)}}{3}\left(z_{k+1}^{\prime 3}-z_{k}^{\prime 3}\right)
$$

where $b$ is the width of the beam and $E^{(k)}$ the Young's modulus of the $k$ th layer, and $z^{\prime}$, the coordinate along the thickness. $z^{\prime}=0$ represents the neutral plane of the cross-section. Based on Eqs. (20)-(22), the tip deflection, $\delta$, and blocking force, $F_{b l}$, can be written as

$$
\delta=\frac{l^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{B_{11} N_{x}^{p}-A_{11} M_{x}^{p}}{A_{11} D_{11}-B_{11}^{2}}\right)
$$

$$
F_{b l}=\frac{3 E J}{2 l}\left(\frac{B_{11} N_{x}^{p}-A_{11} M_{x}^{p}}{A_{11} D_{11}-B_{11}^{2}}\right) .
$$

Based on above analyses, static behaviors of cantilever bimorph and unimorph actuators will be discussed below.

### 3.1. Cantilever bimorph actuator

For a bimorph consisting of one substrate layer with a thickness of $t_m$ and Young's modulus of $E_m$ and two identical piezoelectric actuator layers with thickness of $t_p$ and Young's modulus of $E_p$, the extension, extensional-bending, bending stiffness and sectional moment of stiffness can be calculated from Eqs. (12) and (23) as follows

$$
A_{11}=\frac{1+A B\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)}{(1+B)\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)} E_{p} t, \quad B_{11}=0,
$$

$$
D_{11}=\frac{1+3 B+3 B^{2}+A B^{3}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)}{12(1+B)^{3}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)} E_{p} b t^{3},
$$

$$
E J=\frac{1+3 B+3 B^{2}+A B^{3}}{12(1+B)^{3}} E_{p} b t^{3} \tag{26}
$$

where $t$ is the total thickness of the bimorph equalling to $t_m+2t_p$, $A=E_m/E_p=S_{11}^E/S_{11}^m$, the Young's moduli ratio of the electromechanical inertia layer (elastic) to the piezoelectric layer, and $B=t_m/2t_p$, the thickness ratio.

The force and moment resultants of the actuator induced by the applied electric field can be written as:

$$
N_{x}^{p}=\frac{m_{31}^{\prime} E_{z}^{2}}{2(1+B)} E_{p} t \tag{27}
$$

$$
M_{x}^{p}=\frac{(1+2 B) d_{31} E_{z}}{4(1+B)^{2}} E_{p} t^{2}. \tag{28}
$$

Substituting Eqs. (26)-(28) into (24) and (25), the deflection at the tip and blocking force can be obtained:

$$
\delta=\frac{3 l^{2}}{2 t} \frac{(1+B)(1+2 B)\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)}{1+3 B+3 B^{2}+A B^{3}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)} d_{31} E_{z} \tag{29}
$$

$$
F_{b l}=\frac{3 b t^{2} E_{p}}{8 l} \frac{(1+2 B)\left(1+3 B+3 B^{2}+A B^{3}\right)\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)}{(1+B)^{2}\left[1+3 B+3 B^{2}+A B^{3}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)\right]} d_{31} E_{z}. \tag{30}
$$

When the bimorph actuator consists only of two piezoelectric ceramic plates, i.e., no elastic layer and hence $B=0$ and $t=2t_p$, the tip deflection and blocking force can be simplified as:

$$
\delta=\frac{3 l^{2}}{2 t}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right) d_{31} E_{z} \tag{31}
$$

$$
F_{b l}=\frac{3 b t^{2} E_{p}}{8 l}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right) d_{31} E_{z}. \tag{32}
$$

Assuming that the electric field is small and the nonlinear effects (electroelastic and electrostrictive) can be neglected, Eqs. (29)-(32) may be simplified as

$$
\delta=\frac{3 l^{2}}{2 t} \frac{(1+B)(1+2 B)}{1+3 B+3 B^{2}+A B^{3}} d_{31} E_{z} \tag{33}
$$

$$
F_{b l}=\frac{3 b t^{2} E_{p}}{8 l} \frac{1+2 B}{(1+B)^{2}} d_{31} E_{z} \tag{34}
$$

and

$$
\delta=\frac{3 l^{2}}{2 t} d_{31} E_{z} \tag{35}
$$

$$
F_{b l}=\frac{3 b t^{2} E_{p}}{8 l} d_{31} E_{z}. \tag{36}
$$

It can be seen that after removing the nonlinear effects, the expressions from Eqs. (33)-(36) are the same as the linear analytical expressions [9,12,13].

### 3.2. Cantilever unimorph actuator

For a unimorph actuator consisting of a piezoelectric ceramic layer and an elastic layer, the extension, extension-bending, bending stiffness and sectional moment of stiffness can be evaluated as:

$$
A_{11}=\frac{1+A B\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)}{(1+B)\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)} E_{p} t,
$$

$$
B_{11}=\frac{B\left[1-A\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)\right]}{2(1+B)^{2}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)} E_{p} t^{2},
$$

$$
D_{11}=\frac{1+3 B^{2}+\left(3 A B+A B^{3}\right)\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)}{12(1+B)^{3}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)} E_{p} t^{3},
$$

$$
E J=\frac{1+A^{2} B^{4}+2 A\left(2 B+3 B^{2}+2 B^{3}\right)}{12(1+B)^{3}(1+A B)} E_{p} b t^{3} \tag{37}
$$

where the total thickness of the bimorph is $t=t_m+t_p$ and the thickness ratio of the piezoelectric layer to the elastic layer of the unimorph actuator is $B=t_m/t_p$.

The force and moment resultants of the unimorph actuator induced by the applied electric field can be written as:

$$
N_{x}^{p}=\frac{1}{1+B}\left(d_{31} E_{z}+\frac{1}{2} m_{31}^{\prime} E_{z}^{2}\right) E_{p} t \tag{38}
$$

$$
M_{x}^{p}=\frac{B}{2(1+B)^{2}}\left(d_{31} E_{z}+\frac{1}{2} m_{31}^{\prime} E_{z}^{2}\right) E_{p} t^{2}. \tag{39}
$$

Substituting Eqs. (37)-(39) into (24) and (25), the deflection of the tip and blocking force can be shown to be

$$
\delta=\frac{3 l^{2}}{t} \frac{A B(1+B)^{2}\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{P}\right)^{2}\left(d_{31} E_{z}+(1 / 2) m_{31}^{\prime} E_{z}^{2}\right)}{\Delta} \tag{40}
$$

$$
\begin{gathered}
A B\left(1+A^{2} B^{4}+2 A\left(2 B+3 B^{2}+2 B^{3}\right)\right) \\
F_{b l}=\frac{3 b t^{2} E_{p}}{4 l} \frac{\times\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)^{2}\left(d_{31} E_{z}+(1 / 2) m_{31}^{\prime} E_{z}^{2}\right)}{(1+B)(1+A B) \Delta}
\end{gathered} \tag{41}
$$

where
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta = & 1 + A^{2}B^{4} + 2A(2B + 3B^{2} + 2B^{3})(1 + d_{311}E_{z}E_{P}) \\
& + A^{2}B^{4}d_{311}E_{z}E_{p}(2 + d_{311}E_{z}E_{p}).
\end{aligned}
$$

In the case of a weak electric field, the nonlinear effects (electroelastic and electrostrictive) fron Eqs. (40) and (41) can be neglected. Therefore, the deflection and blocking force expressed by Eqs. (40) and (41) can be rewritten as:

$$
\delta = \frac{3l^{2}}{t} \frac{AB(1+B)^{2}}{1+A^{2}B^{4}+2A(2B+3B^{2}+2B^{3})}d_{31}E_{z} \tag{42}
$$

$$
F_{bl} = \frac{3bt^{2}E_{p}}{4l} \frac{AB}{(1+B)(1+AB)}d_{31}E_{3} \tag{43}
$$

which again are the same as the linear expressions [9,12,13].

The experimental results [8], linear prediction and the present nonlinear prediction of the tip deflections of the bimorph and unimorph actuators under varying electric fields at quasistatic condition are shown in Fig. 2a and b. As expected from Eqs. (31) and (40), a nonlinear relationship between the tip deflection and the electric field can be observed. When the electric field is weak, the differences between the linear prediction, the nonlinear prediction and the experimental results are very small. However, as the magnitude of the electric field is increased to a strong applied field, the tip deflection rapidly increases and the difference between the experimental results and the linear prediction becomes significant. However, as shown in Fig. 2a and b, results from the present theory (nonlinear prediction) well agree with the experiment results throughout the entire range of electric field tested.

Fig. 3a and b show the blocking forces of the bimorph and unimorph cantilever actuators. Similar to that observed in tip deflection, the blocking force of the actuators also shows a nonlinear relationship with the electric field. The linear prediction is again applicable only at the low range of electric fields.

The nonlinear electromechanical response of PZT ceramics under high applied electric field is associated with the effective piezoelectric coefficient $d_{31}$ and the effective compliance $S_{11}^E$, both of which increase with electric field since ferroelectric materials become softer under high applied electric field [17]. In the present proposed model, the increases in the effective piezoelectric coefficient and elastic compliance with applied electric field by introducing the electrostrictive coefficient $m_{31}$ and electroelastic compliance $d_{311}$ respectively have been considered. The numerical results obtained in the present model show that the nonlinear prediction can well predict the nonlinear electromechanical responses of the actuators under a moderately large electric field (small compared to the coercive field), while the linear analytical approach is unable to provide any reasonable predictions at the higher electric fields.

Since the largest possible tip deflection and blocking force are desired in the design of a piezoelectric actuator, the thick- ness ratio $B$ at constant $t$ can be optimized by considering:

$$
\frac{\partial \delta}{\partial B}=0 \quad \text { or } \quad \frac{\partial F_{b l}}{\partial B}=0
\tag{44}
$$

Substituting Eq. (40) or (41) into (44), the maximum tip deflection and blocking force can be calculated by choosing the thickness ratio:

$$
B=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A\left(1+d_{311} E_{z} E_{p}\right)}}
\tag{45}
$$

When the tip deflection of the cantilever actuator is large, the blocking force level generated is rather low. The ad- ditional elastic layer in the unimorph actuator will lead to a reduction in the tip deflection capability. The tip deflec- tion of unimorph actuator is, therefore, less than half that of bimorph actuator with the same dimensions. It is ev- ident that cantilever types of actuators possess low elec- tromechanical coupling, low mechanical energy, and low energy transmission efficiency because of their actuating mechanism.

## 5. Conclusions

A theoretical nonlinear model and associated analytical relationships based on nonlinear piezoelectric laminate the- ory for laminated piezoelectric cantilever actuator have been proposed. Static performance in terms of deflection of the tip and blocking force of the cantilever actuators (bimorph and unimorph piezoelectric actuators) has bveen analytically formulated. A nonlinear relationship between the tip deflec- tion or blocking force and electric field is obtained. Numer- ical examples show that the present model agrees well with the experimental results. The difference between nonlinear and linear predictions decreases as the ratio of the thickness between substrate and piezoelectric increases. The differ- ence tends to be small and hence may be neglected when the stiffness of the piezoelectric layer is smaller than that of the substrate, implying that linear prediction is applicable at strong electric field. The present analysis is believed to be useful in materials selection and actuator structure design in the fabrication of actuators.
