# Promoting Electrocatalytic $CO_2$ Reduction to $CH_4$ by Copper Porphyrin with Donor-Acceptor Structures

Pinger Yu, Ximeng Lv, Qihao Wang, Haoliang Huang, Weijun Weng, Chen Peng, Linjuan Zhang, and Gengfeng Zheng*

Molecular catalysts have been receiving increasingly attention in the electrochemical $CO_2$ reduction reaction ($CO_2$RR) with attractive features such as precise catalytic sites and tunable ligands. However, the insufficient activity and low selectivity of deep reduction products restrain the utilization of molecular catalysts in $CO_2$RR. Herein, a donor-acceptor modified Cu porphyrin (CuTAPP) is developed, in which amino groups are linked to donate electrons toward the central $CuN_4$ site to enhance the $CO_2$RR activity. The CuTAPP catalyst exhibited an excellent $CO_2$-to-$CH_4$ electroreduction performance, including a high $CH_4$ partial current density of $290.5$ mA cm$^{-2}$ and a corresponding Faradaic efficiency of $54.8\%$ at $-1.63$ V versus reversible hydrogen electrode in flow cells. Density functional theory calculations indicated that CuTAPP presented a much lower energy gap in the pathway of producing *CHO than Cu porphyrin without amino group modification. This work suggests a useful strategy of introducing designed donor-acceptor structures into molecular catalysts for enhancing electrochemical $CO_2$ conversion toward deep reduction products.

## 1. Introduction

Electrocatalytic carbon dioxide reduction reaction ($CO_2$RR) represents a potential approach for reducing $CO_2$ emission with ambient reaction conditions and flexible environmental compatibility.$^{[1,2]}$ However, owing to the high thermodynamic stability of $CO_2$ and the competitive kinetically favored $H_2$ evolution reaction (HER), the electrocatalytic $CO_2$RR generally faces several major challenges, including insufficient activity and low selectivity toward multielectron (>2e) reduction products.$^{[3-5]}$ Molecular catalysts have attracted a growing attention due to their well-defined atomic structures and tunable local electron states, which are important for rational design and preparation of efficient catalytic sites.$^{[6,7]}$ A variety of molecular catalysts have been demonstrated as potential $CO_2$RR electrocatalysts, such as cobalt phthalocyanine (CoPc),$^{[6]}$ cobalt porphyrin-tetrathiafulvalene-based covalent organic framework,$^{[8]}$ nickel 2,3,9,10,16,17,23,24-octacyanophthalocyanine molecularly dispersed electrocatalysts,$^{[9]}$ cobalt polyoxometalate-metalloporphyrin organic frameworks,$^{[10]}$ iron porphyrin,$^{[11]}$ covalent Co porphyrin polymers,$^{[12]}$ Ni N-doping tetraphenylporphyrin,$^{[13]}$ and cobalt phthalocyanine/carbon nanotubes.$^{[14]}$

Copper (Cu)-containing materials are known as unique candidates in multielectron (>2e) transfer reduction products such as methane ($CH_4$).$^{[10]}$ For instance, a copper(II) phthalocyanine was prepared with excellent electrochemical $CO_2$-to-$CH_4$ conversion performance, ascribed to the reversible formation of Cu nanoclusters during $CO_2$RR.$^{[15]}$ The Faradaic efficiency of methane ($FE_{CH4}$) was $66\%$ and corresponding partial current density was $136$ mA cm$^{-2}$. Nevertheless, due to the low percentages of Cu sites in the molecular structures, the activities of the reported molecular catalysts have still remained relatively lower compared with the heterogenous Cu-based compounds$^{[16,17]}$ or alloys.$^{[18,19]}$

Incorporating donor-acceptor units into molecular structures is a useful means of tuning electron density distribution inside molecules, and promotes the formation of a build-in electric field to accelerate migration of electrons.$^{[20]}$ Metalloporphyrin, possessing conjugated $\pi$-electron system, can serve as excellent electron acceptor and electron transfer carrier.$^{[21,22]}$ Amino group ($-NH_2$) is known as a good electron donor that facilitates electron transfer, which may help to form metalloporphyrin-based $CO_2$RR catalysts. Thus, we proposed that the combination of copper porphyrin and amino groups may allow for electron-state regulation and electron transfer to enhance electrochemical $CO_2$RR.

Compared with the copper porphyrin molecule without amino groups (i.e., 5,10,15,20-tetraphenyl-21H,23H-porphine copper(II), CuTPP), the incorporation of amino groups not only increased the adsorption of $CO_2$ but also induced electron transfer to $CuN_4$ catalytic sites. ## 2. Results and Discussions

### 2.1. Catalyst Preparation and Characterization

CuTAPP was synthesized from 5,10,15,20-tetrakis(4-aminophenyl)-21H, 23H porphyrin (TAPP) using a modified procedure from the literatures$^{[22]}$ (Figure 1a, Experimental Section in the Support Information). Both TAPP and CuTPP samples (i.e., without amino groups) were also synthesized from the corresponding precursors as controls. The infrared (IR) spectra of both CuTAPP and TAPP (Figure 1b) showed the $NH_2$-stretching vibration at $3380\ cm^{-1}$. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra of Cu 2p showed two peaks at 933.9 and 954.0 eV (Figure S5a,b, Supporting Information), assigned to Cu $2p_{3/2}$ and Cu $2p_{1/2}$, respectively.$^{[10]}$ The deconvolution of these peaks provided the percentages of Cu(II) and Cu(I) or Cu(0). X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) was further conducted to investigate the electronic structures and chemical coordination environments of CuTAPP and CuTPP. The normalized X-ray absorption near-edge fine structure (XANES) spectra at Cu K-edge exhibited a white-line peak at 8997 and 9003 eV for CuTAPP and CuTPP, respectively (Figure 2a). The coordination numbers of Cu-N of CuTAPP and CuTPP were both about 4 (Table S1, Supporting Information), confirming the existence of $CuN_4$ structure in those samples. In addition, both CuTAPP and CuTPP exhibited a characteristic peak of Cu-N corrected distance centered at 1.985 and 1.983 Å, as well as a peak of Cu-C corrected distance at 2.997 and 2.994 Å in the corresponding FT-EXAFS spectra (Figure 2c,d). ### 2.2. Electrocatalytic $CO_2$ Reduction

The electrochemical $CO_2$RR was first performed in H-cells with $0.5\ \text{M}\ \text{KHCO}_3$ as electrolyte. As shown in Figure S6 (Supporting Information), the current density of CuTAPP was the highest $(142\ \text{mA}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ at $-1.6\ \text{V}$ vs RHE) compared with CuTPP and TAPP. The cyclic voltammograms (CV) curves were recorded in the potential window from 1.0 to $-0.1\ \text{V}$ versus RHE (Figure S7, Supporting Information). The reduction peak was located at $\approx 0.4\ \text{V}$ for CuTPP, assigned to the reduction of Cu(II) to Cu(I). This reduction peak shifted negatively to $\approx 0.2\ \text{V}$ for CuTAPP, suggesting that the introduction of amino groups increased the difficulty for reducing Cu sites.$^{[32]}$ No evident reduction peak was observed in TAPP.

To enhance the mass transfer of $CO_2$ and catalytic activity, the electrochemical $CO_2$RR was conducted in flow cells with $1\ \text{M}\ \text{KOH}$ as electrolyte (Experimental Section in the Supporting Information). Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) curves showed that the CuTAPP catalyst presented larger current densities than CuTPP and TAPP (Figure 3a). The LSV curves of CuTAPP, CuTPP, and TAPP were also tested in both the absence and presence of $CO_2$ (Figure S8, Supporting Information). The onset potentials of the samples in the absence of $CO_2$ were more negatively than those in the presence of $CO_2$. At $-1.83\ \text{V}$ versus RHE, the current densities reached $720\ \text{mA}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ (for CuTAPP), $578\ \text{mA}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ (for CuTPP), and $427\ \text{mA}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ (for TAPP). In addition, CuTAPP showed the lowest overpotential $(\approx -0.4\ \text{V}$ vs RHE) among these three catalysts, suggesting its highest electrocatalytic activity. ### 2.3. Density Functional Theory (DFT) Calculations
DFT calculations were performed to investigate the molecular mechanism on CuTAPP. Compared with CuTPP, the introduction of amino groups in CuTAPP leads to a narrowed highest occupied molecular orbital-lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (HOMO-LUMO) gap (i.e., 2.43 vs 2.81 eV, Figure 4a). This reduced gap should favor a faster electron transfer in the electrochemical $CO_2$ methanation. In addition, the Gibbs free energy calculation on CuTAPP revealed a weakened hydrogen adsorption compared with CuTPP (i.e., -0.11 vs -0.14 eV, Figure S14, Supporting Information). Furthermore, the Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta G$) profile of the $CO_2$-to-$CH_4$ electroreduction was investigated (Figure 4b), where the conversion of *CO into *CHO is the rate-determining step (RDS).$^{[41]}$ The values of $\Delta G$ in this RDS (i.e., *CO$\to$*CHO) were calculated as 2.7 and 3.6 eV for CuTAPP and CuTPP, respectively, indicating a much enhanced reaction kinetics for $CO_2$ methanation. Further evaluation on the electron-density revealed that the adsorption of *CHO on the Cu site of a CuTAPP molecule leads to the reduced electron density on the peripheral amino groups (Figure 4c), suggesting the electron transfer from amino groups to $CuN_4$ sites (i.e., D-A structure) and then to the adsorbed *CHO intermediate. Additionally, the electron density of CuTPP was also provided (Figure S15, Supporting Information), which demonstrated high electron density in the $CuN_4$ sites and further proved the electron-transfer feature of amino groups. Taken together, our DFT calculations clearly demonstrated that the introduction of amino group-based D-A structure enabled the tuning of surface electron configurations on CuTAPP, thus facilitating electron transfer and promoting the $CO_2$-to-$CH_4$ conversion.

## 3. Conclusion
In summary, we have developed a donor-acceptor-modified Cu porphyrin, in which the amino groups and $CuN_4$ site served as electron donor and acceptor, respectively. The amino groups induced efficient electron transfer toward the $CuN_4$ site, thus increasing both the electron density and catalytic activity. DFT calculations showed that the free energy change in the rate-determining step from *CO to *CHO on CuTAPP was much enhanced compared with CuTPP, suggesting its better capability of $CO_2$ methanation. The CuTAPP catalyst exhibited an outstanding electrochemical $CO_2$-to-$CH_4$ performance, including one of the largest $CH_4$ partial current densities as $290.5\ mA\ cm^{-2}$, high $FE_{CH4}$ of 54.8%, and excellent electrochemical stability at large current densities. Our work presents a useful means of constructing donor-acceptor molecular catalytic structures for efficient $CO_2$ fixation toward higher value-added products.
