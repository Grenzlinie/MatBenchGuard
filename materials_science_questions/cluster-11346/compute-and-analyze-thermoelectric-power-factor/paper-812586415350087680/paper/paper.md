# Effective phonon scattering and enhancement of thermoelectric performance in Ga-excess $\boldsymbol{Bi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_{3}}$ compounds

Song Yi Back$^{a}$, Hyunyong Cho$^{a}$, Seokyeong Byeon$^{b}$, Hyungyu Jin$^{b}$, Jong-Soo Rhyee$^{a,*}$

We investigate the thermoelectric properties on Ga-excess p-type $\mathrm{Ga_{x}Bi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_{3}}$ compounds. Even though the random distribution of Ga-doping increases electrical resistivity giving rise to the decrease of power factor, the significant decrease of lattice thermal conductivity by the excess Ga-doping induces significant enhancement of ZT value (1.13 at 350 K) for the Ga x = 0.03 doped compound. From the X-ray diffraction and elemental mapping by energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy, we observed Sb and Ga phase separation leading to the phonon scattering. The Sb precipitation implies atomic defect in the matrix which can induce short wavelength phonon scattering. The synergetic phonon scatterings from various scattering sources such as point defect, alloy scattering, and grain boundary phonon scattering have an important role in the enhancement of thermoelectric performance.

Thermoelectric (TE) materials can directly convert waste heat into electricity and vice versa, which can be applied to solid state cooling and power generation. The TE performance can be expressed by the dimensionless figure-of-merit, $ZT = S^2T/(\kappa_e + \kappa_L)\rho$, where $S$, $T$, $\rho$, $\kappa_e$, and $\kappa_L$ are the Seebeck coefficient, absolute temperature, electrical resistivity, electronic thermal conductivity, and lattice thermal conductivity, respectively. In order to achieve high $ZT$ value, the large power factor (PF=$S^2\sigma$) and the low thermal conductivity are required simultaneously.

Considerable investigations have been studied to improve thermoelectric performance such as nano-structuring by process engineering [1]. For example, the hot-deformation process can modify the microstructure and decrease the lattice thermal conductivity [2-5]. Various substitutional doping elements such as Cu [6], Ag [4,7], rare-earth elements [8], and alkaline-earth doping [9] on $Bi_2Te_3$ based alloys also significantly reduced the lattice thermal conductivity by the mass fluctuation and defect scatterings.

Previous report suggested that grain boundary dislocation by Te segregation in $Bi_{0.5}Sb_{1.5}Te_3$ effectively scatters phonon, resulting in the significantly enhanced ZT value 1.86 at 320 K [10]. Besides, it was argued that the large ZT value was an incorrect combination of thermal conductivity measured along the press direction while charge transport measured along the perpendicular direction. It showed that the meaningful ZT value on the Te-excess BST is limited by 1.2 at 350 K [11]. We tried liquid-phase sintering of the Te-excess $Bi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_{3+x}$ compound to identify the excess Te effect on the compound [12]. Interestingly, we observed the Sb phase separation, instead of Te-liquid phase near the grain boundary with enhanced ZT value 1.41 at 417 K [12]. In this work, we study the thermoelectric effect on the excess Ga addition in p-type $Bi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_3$ (BST) alloy to check the possible grain boundary phonon scattering.

## 2. Experimental Procedures

In order to make a Ga phase separation near grain boundaries, the Ga-excess BST compounds $Ga_xBi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_3$ (x = 0.0, 0.01, and 0.03) were synthesized by two step process. The pristine BST ingots were prepared by melting and quenching. We evacuated quartz ampoules with stoichiometric mixture of high purity elements of Bi (99.999%), Sb (99.999%), and Te (99.999%) and heated 650 °C for 20 h with following quenching in cold water. The ingots were pulverized by hand grinding and mixed with high purity Ga (99.999%) shots.

The Ga-excess BST powders were sealed into evacuated quartz tubes and annealed at 400 °C for 24h and quenched in cold water. After pulverizing the samples, the Ga-excess BST powders were sintered by hot-press at 500 °C for 1 h under uniaxial pressure of 50 MPa. The thermoelectric properties were measured along the press direction of hot press. We cut the samples along the press direction for electrical resistivity and Seebeck coefficient. For thermal conductivity measurement by laser flash method, we prepared samples with disk type which is cut perpendicular to the press direction. The sample densities are 6.58 ~ 6.61 g/cm³ which are about 96 % with theoretical density.

The electrical resistivity and Seebeck coefficient measurement were carried by thermoelectric measurement system using the ZEM-3 (ULVAC Riko, Japan) under helium atmosphere. Thermal conductivity was evaluated by the relation $\kappa = C_p d\lambda$, where $C_p$, $d$, $\lambda$ are the specific heat, sample density, and thermal diffusivity measured by laser flash method (LFA-457, NETZCH, Germany), respectively. The heat capacity was measured by the physical property measurement system (PPMS Dynacool 14 T, Quantum Design, U.S.A.) with high temperature fitting. The Hall carrier concentration $n_H$ and carrier mobility $\mu_H$ were obtained by $n_H = -1/(eR_H)$ and $\mu_H = 1/(\rho e n_H)$ where $R_H = \rho_{xy}/H$ is the Hall coefficient and $\rho_{xy}$ is the Hall resistivity measured by the PPMS (14T PPMS Dynacool, Quantum Design, USA).

## 3. Results and discussion

X-ray diffraction patterns of the Ga-excess $Bi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_3$ compounds are shown in Fig. 1(a). All the compounds are well indexed to the rhombohedral $Bi_2Te_3$ structure with $R$-$3m$ space group. As listed in Table 1, the c-axis lattice parameters are slightly decreased from c = 30.479 Å (pristine BST) to c = 30.464 Å for x = 0.03 Ga excess BST, while the a-axis lattice parameters are not changed significantly, resulting in the lattice volumes of the compounds are not sensitive with Ga-excess doping. Because the atomic radius of Ga (130 pm) is relatively smaller than the other elements, such as Bi (160 pm), Sb (145 pm), and Te (140 pm), small decrease of c-axis lattice parameters can be understood by small substitution in the bulk compounds. However, we believe that the Ga substitution is not a solid solution with the matrix because we observed the Ga peaks as detected in the inset of Fig. 1(a) near 27.7°. Also, the annealing temperature (400 °C) is lower than the sintering temperature of BST so as to make a phase separation of Ga near the grain boundary. Therefore, even though there is a

small atomic diffusion of Ga in the matrix, the most of excess Ga can exist as a secondary phase.

The elemental mapping and microscopic morphologies of Ga-excess (x = 0.03) BST compounds by high-resolution scanning electron microscope (HR-SEM) images are presented in Fig. 1(b) and (c). While excess Ga peaks are observed in the XRD patterns in the inset of Fig. 2(a), Fig. (c) clearly shows the formation of Sb precipitation. Previous report also found the Sb precipitation in Te-excess $Bi_{0.4}Bi_{1.6}Te_3$ alloys instead of excess Te phase separation [12]. The distribution of Sb-precipitations as well as excess Ga in BST matrix can scatter the heat-carrying phonons, resulting in reduction of lattice thermal conductivity. The decrease in c-axis parameters might be caused by the formation of Sb precipitates.

Fig. 2(a)~(c) shows the temperature-dependent thermoelectric properties of electrical resistivity $\rho(T)$, $S(T)$, and power factor $S^{2}/\rho$ (PF) of $Ga_xBi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_3$ alloys. The $\rho(T)$ on the compounds show the metallic behavior with temperature. The electrical resistivities of Ga-excess BST compounds are slightly increased than the one of pristine BST. The excess Ga seems to play a role in scattering charge carriers, resulting in the increase of the electrical resistivity. The non-systematic behavior of electrical resistivity with Ga-concentration may be attributed from the simultaneous doping effect as well as phase separation. The positive $S(T)$ exhibits metallic increase behavior with increasing temperature, indicating p-type carrier transport on the compounds. From the Sommerfeld theory of metal, the $S(T)$ should have linear increase with temperature [13]. On the other hand, the non-linear behavior with temperature in $S(T)$ implies the degenerated semiconductor or bipolar transport of carriers. The Seebeck coefficients of compounds does not changed significantly with excess Ga concentration.

From the Hall resistivity $\rho_{xy}$ measurement, we obtain the Hall carrier concentration $n_{H}$ and Hall carrier mobility $\mu_{H}$, as listed in Table 1. As expected with $\rho$ and $S$, the Hall carrier densities $n_{H}$ are slightly decreased with increasing Ga concentration, while the Hall mobility $\mu_{H}$ is not relevant with the Ga substitution. Within the parabolic band assumption, we estimated the effective mass of carrier. Pisarenko's plot, the Seebeck coefficient with respect to Hall carrier density, shows that the compounds relatively follow well with the single parabolic band assumption, as presented in Fig. 2(c). ### 4. Conclusions
We synthesize the $Ga_xBi_{0.4}Sb_{1.6}Te_3$ alloys by melting and hot press sintering. We found the homogeneous Ga substitution and Sb precipitation in the matrix. Sb precipitation implies the atomic point defects of the matrix. Even though the decrease of power factor owing to the increase of electrical resistivity by Ga doping, we observed significantly enhanced ZT value (1.13 at 350 K) for the Ga x = 0.03 doped compound, attributed from the significant decrease of thermal conductivity. The synergetic phonon scatterings via various sources such as atomic point defects, alloy scattering by Ga doping, and grain boundary phonon scattering by Sb precipitation lead to a significant reduction of lattice thermal conductivity. This result strongly suggests the various phonon scattering centers have important role in the reduction of lattice thermal conductivity, which is a promising approach to improve TE performance.
