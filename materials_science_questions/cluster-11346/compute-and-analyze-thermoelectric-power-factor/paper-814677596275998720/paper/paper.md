### Abstract
Using density functional theory, we have demonstrated that alloying of $RuO_2$ ($P4_2/mnm$) with 3d transition metals (Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn) gives rise to a substantial increase in the Seebeck coefficient probably due to quantum confinement. As Fe yields the largest enhancement, it was selected for experimental verification. We synthesized combinatorial Ru–Fe–O thin films and subsequently measured their transport properties at elevated temperatures. The Fe-alloyed samples increase the Seebeck coefficient threefold with respect to the unalloyed $RuO_2$ specimen thereby verifying the theoretical prediction. The here obtained power factor of $274\mu\text{W}\text{K}^{-2}\text{m}^{-1}$ is not only the largest reported value for $RuO_2$ based compounds but it also occurs at $\sim600\,^\circ\text{C}$ thus increasing the Carnot efficiency significantly.

Keywords: density functional theory, transport properties, $RuO_2$

## 1. Introduction

Thermoelectrics convert temperature gradients directly into voltage gradients and vice versa enabling numerous applications, a recent one being cooling of microelectronic devices [1–3]. Strategies to increase the efficiency of these devices comprise selection of phases with large Seebeck coefficient, large electrical conductivity and low thermal conductivity [1, 2]. As these transport properties are conflicting, it is challenging to design thermoelectrics with improved performance. The power generation efficiency is further restricted by the Carnot efficiency, which can in turn be enhanced by larger temperature gradients if phases with adequate thermal and chemical stability are accessible [1, 2]. Regrettably, the efficiency of state-of-the-art thermoelectrics, such as $Bi_2Te_3$, PbTe and SiGe, is limited by relatively poor chemical and thermal stability [1, 2]. However, due to volatility of Na, high-temperature applications are challenging and in some cases impossible. Besides these layered cobalt oxides, perovskite and ZnO based oxides were also explored [5]. These perovskite oxides are not widely used since bulk materials with sufficient efficiency cannot yet be processed and ZnO based oxides are generally difficult to synthesize as p-type, limiting their application as thermoelectrics [5]. More research on thermoelectric oxides is clearly needed as none of the above discussed thermoelectrics exhibit sufficient thermal and chemical stability to withstand the thermal loads associated with the requirement of high Carnot efficiency.

$RuO_2$ (space group $P4_2/mnm$, prototype rutile) is a promising candidate for thermoelectric applications as it exhibits a large electrical conductivity of $2.5×10^6Ω^{-1}m^{-1}$, which is atypical for oxides, and large thermal and chemical stability [6] and is thus used as electrodes in microelectrical devices. $RuO_2$ is also an important catalyist for $H_2O$ oxidation, especially when alloyed with other oxides such as $TiO_2$ and $IrO_2$ [7]. The power factor can be defined as a square of the Seebeck coefficient multiplied by the corresponding electrical conductivity. The largest power factor for a $RuO_2$ based compound was obtained by alloying with 2 mol.% BaO [8] and 0.8 at.% La [9], achieving $\sim$90 and $229μWK^{-2}m^{-1}$, respectively. The latter was accomplished utilizing a design approach, where all lanthanides were probed using density functional theory and La was selected for experimental verification based on its pronounced quantum confinement effect [9]. Even though density functional theory was used to probe 4d transition metals as potential alloying elements for enhancement of the Seebeck coefficient of $RuO_2$ [10], no experimental transport and thermal stability data are available for $RuO_2$ with transition metal additions.

In this work, we use the quantum mechanical design approach to identify a suitable alloying element for thermally and chemically stable $RuO_2$ from all 3d transition metals (Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn). The selection criterion is the increased Seebeck coefficient compared with unalloyed $RuO_2$. Fe exhibits the largest quantum confinement effect within the 3d transition metal series and hence the largest Seebeck coefficient. This possible modulation is then verified by synthesizing combinatorial thin films and measuring the transport properties at elevated temperatures.

## 2. Computational details

Density functional theory [11] calculations were performed using the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) with a basis set in the form of projector augmented wave potentials [12–14], which were parameterized within the generalized-gradient approximation by Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof [15]. The Blöchl corrections were applied for the total energy [16] and the integration in the Brillouin zone was carried out on Monkhorst–Pack k-points [17]. Rutile $2×2×2$ supercells containing 48 atoms were studied on a mesh of $5×5×5$ k-points. A metal in the rutile structure (space group $P4_2/mnm$) is located at the 2a Wyckoff position and oxygen at the 4f Wyckoff position. In a $2×2×2$ supercell, all 16 metal sites are thus equivalent. One Ru atom was replaced with a 3d transition metal atom, resulting in the alloying content of 2.1 at.%. Spin polarization effects were also considered. Full structural optimization was carried out for each configuration. The convergence criterion for the total energy was 0.01 meV within a 500 eV cut-off. The Seebeck coefficient was estimated from the slope of density of states and

the number of states at the Fermi level by applying the Mott equation [18, 19]. As many physical effects are ignored by using the Mott equation, the computed Seebeck coefficients are only used to guide the experiments.

## 3. Experimental details
Ru-Fe-O thin films were synthesized in a vacuum system with a base pressure of $\sim 5 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{Pa}$ by reactive magnetron sputtering with a power density of 5.1 and $1.3$–$3.8\,\text{W}\,\text{cm}^{-2}$ for Ru (dc) and Fe (pulsed dc, $100\,\text{kHz}$) targets, respectively. The Ru and Fe targets (purity $99.9\%$) were mounted on magnetrons with the substrate-to-source distance of $10\,\text{cm}$ and the angle of $19^\circ$ between the substrate and plasma source normal. $\text{Si(100)}$ and $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(0001)$ substrates were kept at $\sim 400\,^\circ\text{C}$ during synthesis. No substrate rotation was used during synthesis in order to achieve gradients in the chemical composition along the substrate surface, which is known as a combinatorial approach [20]. A mixture of Ar ($99.9999\%$) and $\text{O}_2$ ($99.9995\%$) was used as a sputtering gas with a partial pressure of $0.6$ and $0.3\,\text{Pa}$, respectively. This gas mixture was selected based on previous work [10, 21].

The Ru-Fe-O thin film composition was determined by x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) in a JAMP-9500F system (JEOL) with a $\text{K}_\alpha$ Al x-ray source (energy $1486.6\,\text{eV}$) and a hemispherical electron energy analyzer with a pass energy of $20\,\text{eV}$. Films were exposed to atmosphere prior to the XPS analysis and due to the sensitivity of the volatile species present in these films they were not sputter-cleaned. The energy calibration was carried out using Au $4\text{f}_{7/5}$ ($83.98\,\text{eV}$), Ag $3\text{d}_{5/2}$ ($368.26\,\text{eV}$) and Cu $2\text{p}_{3/2}$ ($932.67\,\text{eV}$) lines. The structural analysis was conducted by x-ray diffraction (XRD) in a Bruker AXS D8 Discover General Area Detection Diffraction System with Cu $\text{K}_\alpha$ radiation. A collimator with a diameter of $0.5\,\text{mm}$ and an incidence angle of $15^\circ$ were used. A current setting of $40\,\text{mA}$ and a generator voltage of $40\,\text{kV}$ were applied. For a selected Ru-Fe-O sample with a large Fe content (see below), a more detailed structural analysis was carried out by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Cross-sectional TEM samples were prepared using the focused ion beam (FIB) workstation FEI Strata FIB 205. The TEM investigations were performed in a $200\,\text{kV}$ field emission TEM FEI Tecnai F20 ST. The Seebeck coefficient and electrical conductivity were simultaneously measured in an LSR-3/1100 system (Linseis). These measurements were conducted in a He atmosphere and temperature range from 50 to $900\,^\circ\text{C}$ with the increment of $10\,^\circ\text{C}$ and three data acquisitions at each temperature. An additional heater was employed to realize a $50\,^\circ\text{C}$ temperature gradient between the top and bottom sample electrodes. The calibration was performed with constantan ($\text{Cu}_{0.55}\text{Ni}_{0.45}$).

## 4. Results and discussion
We performed density functional theory calculations to estimate the Seebeck coefficient for $\text{RuO}_2$ alloyed with $2.1\,\text{at.\%}$ of 3d transition metals (Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn). Figure 1 contains the calculated Seebeck coefficient at $300\,\text{K}$ for alloyed $\text{RuO}_2$ with all 3d transition metals compared with the literature value for unalloyed $\text{RuO}_2$ [18]. The Seebeck coefficient increases by a factor of 3–8 with respect to unalloyed $\text{RuO}_2$. It is important to emphasize that the spin polarization does not significantly alter the estimated Seebeck coefficient. As the largest Seebeck coefficient is estimated for Fe-alloyed RuO₂, yielding $26\mu\mathrm{V}\,\mathrm{K}^{-1}$, we select this 3d transition metal as the most promising candidate for enhancement of the Seebeck coefficient.

To identify a physical mechanism responsible for the substantial increase of the Seebeck coefficient, we study the partial density of states for non-magnetic Fe-alloyed RuO₂, as provided in figure 2. There is a continuous band across the Fermi level, which is consistent with the metallic behaviour of this compound and is known from literature for unalloyed RuO₂ [22]. Fe in the RuO₂ lattice gives rise to the hybridized Fe 3d—O 2p states becoming more localised at the Fermi level than the hybridized Ru 4d—O 2p states, which results in a small peak at ~0.1 eV below the Fermi level (indicated by an arrow in figure 2). This peak does not occur in unalloyed RuO₂ [9, 22]. It should also be noted that the intensity of the hybridized Fe 3d—O 2p states at ~0.1 eV below the Fermi level is by a factor of 10 larger than that of Ru 4d—O 2p signifying that Fe contributes to a much larger extent to a band localization. Consequently, the slope at the Fermi level becomes large, which gives rise to a Seebeck coefficient enhancement, as discussed above. This band localization is commonly known as the quantum confinement effect [2]. These data are consistent with the behaviour of La-alloyed RuO₂ [9].

To verify the quantum mechanical prediction that the Seebeck coefficient of RuO₂ should significantly be increased upon alloying with Fe, we synthesized combinatorial Ru–Fe–O thin films using reactive sputtering. The oxygen-to-metal ratio obtained by XPS is $2.6\pm0.3$ and the Fe content is increased from 0.4 to 3.1 at.%. The samples are overstoichiometric, which may be due to formation of Ru hyperoxides [18, 23, 24] and their contribution to the overall chemical composition may be overestimated due to surface sensitivity of XPS. To determine the solubility limit of Fe in Ru–Fe–O thin films, the chemical shifts of Fe were analysed using XPS. Figure 5 shows the Fe $2p_{3/2}$ XPS peak for two representative $RuO_2$ thin films alloyed with 0.4 and 2.8 at.% Fe. There seems to be no binding energy dependence on the Fe content and the Fe $2p_{3/2}$ peak is asymmetric indicating several bonding contributions. The binding energy of $Fe_3O_4$ [26] cannot be detected. The largest bonding contribution at 710.9 eV is consistent with $Fe_2O_3$ [26], which supports the notion of partial $Fe_2O_3$ segregation at the grain boundaries. Furthermore, the magnitude of the here considered Fe addition may not suffice to fully decorate the grain boundaries with $Fe_2O_3$ based on the calculations of Si incorporations of a similar magnitude to alumina thin films with a 12 nm grain size [27]. Here, the $RuO_2$ grains are in the order of 20 nm for all samples and the grain size is not a function of Fe content, implying only a partial segregation. It is also known from literature that the binding energy of Fe increases with its increasing coordination [26]. The largest chemical shift to 713.6 eV is therefore indicative for Fe with a larger coordination than in any known Fe–O compound. The large chemical shift supports the notion that Fe is not only in close proximity to oxygen but also to Ru. This may be the case for a partial coverage of $Fe_2O_3$ along the $RuO_2$ grain boundaries but also with the incorporation of Fe into the $RuO_2$ lattice.

The Seebeck coefficient—Fe content trend holds for all measurement temperatures (see figure 6) and the largest increase of the Seebeck coefficient is by a factor of 3 compared with unalloyed $\text{RuO}_2$ (data measured in this work). However, as the measurement temperature increases, the Seebeck coefficient increases, namely, the Seebeck–Fe content curves are shifted towards larger Seebeck coefficient values. A further increase in the measurement temperature (not shown here) gives rise to a drop in the Seebeck coefficient. This temperature dependence is of significance for the Carnot efficiency of these devices. In addition, the electrical conductivity data exhibit similar trends to the Seebeck coefficient so no further discussions will be provided apart from the cumulative power factor data (see below).

### 5. Conclusions
We have shown that alloying of $RuO_2$ with 3d transition metals can lead to a multifold increase in the Seebeck coefficient probably due to quantum confinement induced through Fe 3d—O 2p hybridization. As Fe yields the largest enhancement, it was selected for experimental verification of our quantum mechanical predictions. We synthesized combinatorial Ru–Fe–O thin films with the oxygen-to-metal ratio of $2.6\pm0.3$ and the Fe content in the range from 0.4 to 3.1 at.% and then measured the transport properties at elevated temperatures. Based on the XRD, TEM and XPS analyses, the Ru–Fe–O samples exhibit phase-pure rutile structure with Fe residing both at interfaces and in the lattice. Our experiments validate the theoretical prediction as the measured Seebeck coefficient for the Fe-alloyed samples reaches the threefold value of the unalloyed $RuO_2$. The power factor of Fe-alloyed $RuO_2$ is by a factor of 3 larger than that of 2 mol% BaO alloyed $RuO_2$ [8] and exceeds the power factor of 0.8 at.% La-alloyed $RuO_2$ [9] by

16%. Moreover, the power factor of Fe-alloyed $RuO_2$ attains its maximum at temperatures in the order of $600\ {^\circ}\text{C}$, while the unalloyed $RuO_2$ performs at its optimum close to room temperature. This gives rise to an enhanced Carnot efficiency of Fe-alloyed samples. Hence, our strategy of combining quantum mechanical calculations and experimental validation constitutes an effective tool for knowledge-based design of thermoelectrics.
