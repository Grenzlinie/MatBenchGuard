## ABSTRACT
The electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) phenomenon is of great importance for plenty of applications, such as slow light, nonlinear effect, nanosensing, and metamaterials. The linewidth is a key factor to evaluate the characteristic of EIT, because the drastic change in dispersion in the narrow spectra can make good control of light. However, only a few reports are related to the ultranarrow EIT. In this paper, we propose a nanosystem based on a gold grating and a multilayer structure. An ultranarrow spectral EIT peak with a linewidth range of 0.75-1.5 nm is observed in such a nanosystem in the visible and near-infrared regions. The physical mechanism leading to the phenomenon is different from those in previous works. In the proposed nanosystem, the ultranarrow EIT peak is formed by the destructive interference of the Fabry-Perot resonance and waveguide modes. Analytic results calculated from the model equations are also found to be consistent with numerical simulations for both normal and oblique incidences. Our work provides another efficient way to realize an ultra-narrow EIT.

The electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) phenomenon, first observed in three-level atomic systems, is a quantum destructive interference effect of pump and probe laser beams tuned by different transitions that generate a narrow transparency window in spectra. $^{1}$ This appealing phenomenon accompanied by strong dispersion in spectra has found plenty of applications in slow light, $^{2,3}$ nonlinear effect, $^{4}$ and optical switches. $^{5,6}$ However, the atomic EIT phenomenon is often restricted to extreme conditions, such as cryogenic temperatures. Therefore, EIT in solid nanophotonic systems has attracted tremendous attention and various nanostructures have been proposed, such as photonic crystals, $^{7,8}$ waveguide-cavity configurations, $^{9,10}$ and nanoparticle systems. $^{11}$ Among these studies, a special category of EIT, plasmon induced transparency (PIT)—EIT phenomenon caused by the plasmonic effect $^{12,13}$ should be referred since it can achieve both strong spectral dispersion and high field localization in a nanosystem. The phenomenon has showed its significance in nanosensing $^{14,15}$ and metamaterials. $^{16,17}$ To realize EIT, three elements are necessary: A low-Q oscillator coupled to outside freedom, a high-Q oscillator that induces the transparency, and weak coupling between the two oscillators. The low-Q and high-Q oscillators are also called "bright mode" and "dark mode," respectively, depending on whether they are excited directly or indirectly by an external field.

The linewidth is a key factor to evaluate the characteristics of an EIT phenomenon because the drastic change in dispersion in the narrow spectra can achieve good control of light. Therefore, efforts should be made to reduce the linewidth of EIT. However, only a few reports are related to the ultranarrow EIT. Sekkat's group proposed an ultranarrow angular PIT based on a multilayer structure by coupling the surface plasmon polaritons (SPPs) and waveguide modes (WGMs) at He-Ne laser wavelengths. $^{18,19}$ Subsequently, Zheng et al. reported an ultranarrow angular EIT by the interference of surface photon polaritons (SPhPs) and WGMs in the midinfrared region, as this spectral window coincides with the vibrational fingerprints for a number of biological molecules. $^{20}$ Recently, Lu et al. presented a tunable high quality (Q) factor EIT with Q factor $>2000$ by coupling SPPs excited with a grating structure and WGMs at near-infrared wavelengths. $^{21}$

In this paper, we propose a nanosystem based on a gold grating and a multilayer structure. And, an ultranarrow spectral EIT with a linewidth range of 0.75-1.5 nm is observed in the visible and near-infrared regions. The physical mechanism leading to the phenomenon

is different from those reported previously. In the proposed nanosystem, the ultranarrow EIT is formed by destructive interference of the Fabry-Perot resonance (FPR) and WGM. Although the FPR is based on SPPs due to the natural SPP property of the Au material selected in the system, it should be emphasized that the ultra-EIT phenomenon can also be observed if we substitute the Au grating with a dielectric grating. The numerical simulations are also fully explained by theoretical results. Thus, our work provides another efficient way to realize an ultranarrow EIT.

The proposed structure is called nanosystem I, which is composed of four parts: An Au grating, a Cytop fluoropolymer layer, a ZnO-doped SiO₂ layer, and a Cytop fluoropolymer substrate, as shown in Fig. 1(a). The Au grating is defined by its period P, slit width a, and thickness t. The thicknesses of the fluoropolymer layer, the ZnO-SiO₂ layer, and the fluoropolymer substrate are indicated by d₁, d₂, and d₃, respectively. The refractive indices of the fluoropolymer layer, the ZnO-SiO₂ layer, and the fluoropolymer substrate are n₁ = 1.34, n₂ = 2.198, and n₃ = 1.34, respectively, which can be considered as a three-layer waveguide. To compare with the response of nanosystem I, we also introduce nanosystem II without the three-layer waveguide structure, which consists of an Au grating and a fluoropolymer substrate with a refractive index (RI) of n₁. A p-polarized light propagates from the grating side with a wavenumber k and an angle θ to the symmetrical axis of the system. The finite difference time domain (FDTD) method is employed in the numerical simulations.

The frequency-dependent permittivity of Au is described by the Drude-Lorentz model since it takes the interband transitions of metals into consideration and gives a detailed description about its permittivity for a wide spectral range²²
$$
\varepsilon_{m}=\varepsilon_{r}-\sum_{j=0} \frac{\omega_{p j}^{2}}{\omega\left(\omega+i \gamma_{j}\right)}-\sum_{j=0} \frac{\Delta \varepsilon_{0} \Omega_{j}^{2}}{\omega^{2}-\Omega_{j}^{2}+i \omega \Gamma_{j}},\qquad(1)
$$
where εᵣ is the relative permittivity at an infinite frequency, ωₚⱼ refers to the bulk plasma frequency of the metal, γⱼ denotes the collision angular frequency, Ωⱼ represents the Lorentz oscillator strength, Γⱼ stands for the Lorentz's spectral width, and Δεⱼ designates the Lorentz weighting factor. In this paper, only one term with j = 0 is considered, and the permittivity agrees well with the experimental data.²³ These parameters are set as εᵣ = 5.97, ωₚ₀ = 1.33 × 10¹⁶rad/s, γ₀ = 9.87 × 10¹³rad/s, Ω₀ = 4.08 × 10¹⁵rad/s, Γ₀ = 6.58 × 10¹⁴rad/s, and Δε₀ = 1.09, respectively.

In nanosystem I, there exist three types of resonances: SPP resonance at the horizontal Au/vacuum interface, FPR based on the SPP in the slit of grating, and the WGM in the waveguide. The SPP resonance at the Au/vacuum interface is determined by the incident wave and grating, which can be expressed as²⁴
$$
k_{0} \sin \theta \pm m \frac{2 \pi}{P}=\frac{\omega}{c} \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{m}^{\prime} \varepsilon_{d}}{\varepsilon_{m}^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{d}}}=k_{\mathrm{SPP}},\qquad(2)
$$
where k₀ and k_SPP are the wavenumbers of the incident wave and the SPP, respectively. ε'ₘ is the real part of Au permittivity, ε_d is the permittivity of the dielectric, and m represents the order of SPP resonance. If the incident light is normal to the surface, the SPP wavelength is defined by P/m. By considering the period and the spectral range, only SPP resonance with m = 1 is presented. In the slit of Au grating, the FPR appears when the following condition is satisfied:²⁵
$$
4 \pi t / \lambda_{\mathrm{FP}}+\arg \left(\rho_{1} \rho_{2}\right)=2 n \pi,\qquad(3)
$$
where λ_FP is the wavelength of FPR in the grating slit, n represents the order of FPR, and ρ₁ and ρ₂ are the reflection coefficients at the two ends of the slit, respectively. Generally, the WGM can be classified into TM modes and TE modes. For the waveguide presented here, since the incident electric field is in the XZ plane, only TM modes can be excited. The dispersion relation of TM modes for the three-layered waveguide with infinite d₁ and d₃ is written as²⁶
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{n_{2}^{2} k_{0}^{2}-\beta^{2}} d_{2} & =q \pi+\arctan \left(\frac{n_{2}^{2}}{n_{1}^{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}-k_{0}^{2} n_{1}^{2}}{k_{0}^{2} n_{2}^{2}-\beta^{2}}}\right) \\
& +\arctan \left(\frac{n_{2}^{2}}{n_{3}^{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}-n_{3}^{2} k_{0}^{2}}{k_{0}^{2} n_{2}^{2}-\beta^{2}}}\right),
\end{aligned}\qquad(4)
$$
where n₁, n₂, and n₃ are the refractive indices of the three-layer waveguide, β is the propagating constant of the TM mode, and q = 0, 1,2… corresponds to the mode order of TM₀, TM₁, TM₂…. Basically, EIT is formed by the interference of a bright mode and a dark mode, which means direct and indirect excitation by the external field. Here, the FPR acts as the broad bright mode, while the WGM represents the narrow dark mode, which is excited indirectly by the external field through SPP resonance. Since the SPP mode provides the momentum for the WGM, the propagating constant of the WGM is determined by the SPP wavenumber
$$
\beta=k_{0} \sin \theta \pm 2 m \pi / P.\qquad(5)
$$
By substituting β of Eq. (5) into Eq. (4), one can derive the theoretical wavelength of the WGM.

We also investigate the effect of the grating period of $P$ on the two nanosystems. Figures 3(a) and 3(b) show the reflection spectra of the two nanosystems with different $P$ for $t=419$ nm. In nanosystem II, there exists only two types of resonances, FPR and SPP resonances. The FPR is a broadband resonance, but the SPP is a narrow one, which matches the theoretical result obtained from Eq. (2), as indicated by a black dashed line. In nanosystem I, with the introduction of the waveguide, the WGM appears and redshifts to the near-infrared region with increasing $P$ because of the increase in the propagating constant of the TM mode, $\beta$. Therefore, the EIT phenomenon disappears with a larger $P$ for $t=419$ nm. Figures 3(c) and 3(d) exhibit the reflection spectra of nanosystem I with different $t$ for $P=600$ nm and $P=700$ nm. The EIT peak redshifts with the WGM. The simulated resonant wavelengths of WGM are 848.3 nm and 976.0 nm for $P=600$ nm and $P=700$ nm, respectively, matching well with the theoretical results of 848.1 nm and 975.9 nm. It should be noted that there exist higher diffraction orders for $P<\lambda<n_{1}P$ (as indicated by dashed lines and dotted-dashed lines) in nanosystem I due to the existence of the fluoropolymer layer. Such a phenomenon is also reported in the transmission spectra for nanosystem II. $^{27}$ These higher diffraction orders suppress the generation of FPR in the corresponding spectral region.

To consider a possible application of sensing, we quantitatively analyze three major criteria of nanosystem I, which are refractive index (RI) sensitivity, quality (Q) factor, and figure of merit (FOM). The RI sensitivity is defined as the resonant wavelength change with RI variation, i.e., $S = \Delta\lambda/\Delta n$. According to the results in Fig. 6(b), $S$ is 267.25 nm/RIU, and the linearity is well kept with the range of refractive indices of 1.34-1.50. The Q factor is 481.8, with the definition of Q factor $= f/\Delta f$ for the ultranarrow EIT. Another important parameter for advanced sensing is FOM, which is 178.2, as calculated by the ratio of RI sensitivity to FWHM of the peak, i.e., $\text{FOM} = S/\text{FWHM}$.

Since the previous discussions are for normal incidence, we then verify the reflection spectra of nanosystem I for oblique incidence with $\theta = 2^\circ, 4^\circ, 5^\circ, 6^\circ, 8^\circ$, and $10^\circ$ in Figs. 7(a) and 7(b). The theoretical WGM resonances are 707.5 nm, 692.7 nm, 685.3 nm, 678.1 nm, 663.7 nm, and 649.5 nm (shown on the top of the figures), while the simulated results are 707.8 nm, 692.8 nm, 685.8 nm, 678.2 nm, 663.8 nm, and 649.8 nm, demonstrating the consistency of simulation and theory for oblique light in the corresponding spectrum. And, an ultranarrow EIT with a FWHM linewidth of 0.75 nm for $\theta = 5^\circ$ is presented in Fig. 7(b).

In summary, we report an ultranarrow EIT peak with a linewidth range of 0.75 nm-1.5 nm in the visible and near-infrared regimes. Detailed field distributions clearly demonstrate that the ultranarrow EIT peak is formed by the destructive interference of the FPR and the WGM. When the period $P$ increases, the EIT peak redshifts with WGM, because the propagating constant $\beta$ of the TM mode increases. The EIT peak remains for different slit widths, coinciding with the theory that the slit width $a$ has no effect on the WGM. The thickness $d_1$ of the fluoropolymer layer determines the coupling between the FPR and the WGM, which influence the linewidth of EIT. The EIT appears repeatedly as the thickness $d_2$ of the $\text{ZnO-SiO}_2$ layer increases due to the existence of higher orders of TM mode. With the change of $n_1$, the EIT peak appears at a longer wavelength for both the FPR and WGM redshift. However, a Fano lineshape is formed, since $n_3$ can only affect WGM. The numerical simulations are fully explained by theoretical results for both normal and oblique incidences. Our work provides an efficient way to realize an ultranarrow EIT.
