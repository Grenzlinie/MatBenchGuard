# SHG Efficiency Spectrum Computation for MIM Grating Metasurface

## Problem background
Metasurfaces can concentrate light in subwavelength volumes, enhancing nonlinear optical interactions such as second-harmonic generation (SHG). Metal-insulator-metal (MIM) configurations have been explored to achieve strong field enhancement for both the pump and generated wavelengths. The structure under study is a waveguide resonator consisting of a GaAs layer sandwiched between a continuous gold mirror and a gold grating, designed to simultaneously resonate at an infrared pump wavelength (TM polarization) and the generated near-infrared signal wavelength (TE polarization). This task addresses the computational prediction of the SHG efficiency spectrum for this MIM grating geometry, quantifying the nonlinear conversion efficiency as a function of pump wavelength.

## Approach
The problem is solved using rigorous coupled-wave analysis (RCWA), a numerical method for Maxwell's equations in periodic structures. The MIM grating is defined by its period, ribbon width, and layer thicknesses. Gold follows a Drude dispersion, and GaAs is a linear dielectric with a constant refractive index plus a second-order susceptibility χ^(2)_xyz. For each pump wavelength, the linear fields for the TM pump and the TE signal (at half the pump wavelength) are computed at normal incidence. The nonlinear polarization is obtained from the product of the in-plane and out-of-plane pump field components inside the GaAs layer, yielding the SHG efficiency Π = P_SHG / P_inc² (W⁻¹). The spectrum is generated by sweeping the pump wavelength over the range 2.9 μm to 3.3 μm in fine steps.

## Reproduction target
Using an open-source RCWA solver, compute the SHG efficiency spectrum Π(λ) (in W⁻¹) for the MIM grating with nominal parameters: period d = 544 nm, ribbon width w = 235 nm, ribbon thickness t = 40 nm, GaAs layer thickness h = 152 nm, on a semi‑infinite gold mirror. The pump is TM‑polarized at normal incidence; the signal is TE‑polarized at half the pump wavelength. Gold is modeled by a Drude function (λ_p = 159 nm, γ = 0.0075) and GaAs has a constant refractive index n = 3.3 and χ^(2)_xyz = 150 pm/V. Cover pump wavelengths from 2.9 μm to 3.3 μm with a step size ≤ 0.01 μm. Output a CSV file with columns `wavelength_um` (float, μm) and `efficiency_W-1` (float, W⁻¹), ordered by increasing wavelength, to `/app/outputs/shg_efficiency_spectrum.csv`.

## How you are scored
A hidden verifier will compare your output CSV against a reference spectrum precomputed with the same geometry and parameters using an open-source RCWA solver. Scoring is based on three criteria: (1) the pump wavelength at which the peak efficiency occurs, (2) the peak efficiency value, and (3) the overall shape of the spectrum, quantified by the Pearson correlation coefficient. Full credit requires all three criteria to be met; partial credit is proportional to the number of criteria satisfied. The exact tolerances and reference values are not disclosed. Only a genuine execution of the RCWA pipeline can reliably meet the scoring thresholds.
