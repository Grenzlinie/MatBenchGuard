Abstract—In this study, we propose surface plasmon resonance (SPR) sensors consisting of thin planar dielectric gratings on flat metallic substrate, which are studied by rigorous coupled-wave analysis theory (RCWA). The grating thickness is the most important factor to determinate the spectra shape and to influence the sensing performance. The results in air suggest the proposed sensor present extremely linear sensing characteristics with great sensitivity about 80 degree per refractive index unit and high FOM of 140.

Keywords-SPR sensor;Planar dielectric gratings;RCWA;

## I. INTRODUCTION
With the development of nanolithography and nanofabrication, the phenomena of surface plasmon polaritons (SPPs) inducing an oscillation of free electrons at the metal-dielectric interface have attracted a growing interest owing to its wide applications. One of the major applications is to design surface plasmon resonance (SPR)-based biomedical and chemical sensors [1-8]. When SPR is excited, the photon energy of incidence is strongly absorbed and a sharp dip shows up in the spectral-directional reflectance spectrum [4]. Various methods using different coupling devices between the incident light and surface plasmon are introduced. Most SPR sensors use the attenuated total reflection (ATR) method to excite the surface plasmon wave. Other coupling methods include the use of metallic diffraction gratings with various profiles [1, 4-8].

In this study, we need not etch complicate profile of metallic gratings but design the simplest planar dielectric gratings, whose relative permittivity is sinusoidal modulated, on flat metallic substrate. The SPR in the proposed structure is investigated by rigorous coupled-wave analysis theory (RCWA) [9]. Especially, the influence of structural factors of planar dielectric gratings on the resonant modes is studied deeply. On the basis of the discussion above, we further design an optimal structure for sensing applications.

## II. THE SPR SENSOR STRUCTURE AND THE $0^{TH}$ ORDER REFLECTIVE SPECTRA OF ANGULAR RESONANCE

### A. The SPR sensor structure
A schematic diagram of the proposed SPR sensors is shown in Fig.1. The sensor configuration consists of planar sinusoidal dielectric gratings of thickness d on flat silver substrate. The lossless dielectric grating is characterized by a periodical medium. The relative permittivity can be depicted by
$$\varepsilon(\mathrm{x}, \mathrm{z})=\varepsilon 2+\delta \cos [\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{x} \sin \phi+\mathrm{z} \cos \phi)] \, \tag{1}$$

Where $\varepsilon 2$ is the average permittivity and $\delta$ is the amplitude of the sinusoidal permittivity. $\phi$ is the grating slant angle and the grating vector $K=2\pi/\Lambda$, here $\Lambda$ is the grating period. The permittivity in the region I $(z < 0)$ is $\varepsilon 1$ and the metallic substrate relative permittivity in the region III $(z >d)$ is $\varepsilon 3$. The complex permittivity of Ag film is described by the Drude model,
$$\varepsilon 3(\omega)=1-\omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{2} /\left(\omega^{2}+\mathrm{i} \gamma \omega\right) \, \tag{2}$$

where$\omega_{\mathrm{p}}=1.37*10^{16}\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the plasma frequency for Ag and $\gamma=7.29*10^{13}\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the collision frequency for Ag [10].

### B. The 0th order reflective spectra of angular resonance

At first, we use rigorous coupled-wave analysis theory (RCWA) to calculate the 0th order reflective spectrum with p-polarized (H<sub>y</sub>) incident light in the wavelength of 633nm. The fixed structure parameters are assumed as: ε1=1, ε2=2.25, δ=0.33 and we will discuss the influences on the resonant modes of the other important structure parameters such as grating pitch, grating slant angle and grating thickness . Fig.2 shows that an obvious surface plasmon resonant mode at incident angle θ=21.6° when the grating is unslant (φ=90°) and the pitch is 400nm. Whereas, the resonant angle of slant grating with φ=60° and Λ=400nm is changed to 9°. Contrast with unslant grating period of 400nm, the resonant angle of the grating with 500nm period shift to the degree of 2.8. The slant angle is also an effective structural factor to modulate the spectra. All of the three grating discussed above have the same thickness of 60nm. And all of the three studied grating structures have very sharp and narrow dip with deep resonance and low full-width at half maximum (FWHM<0.5°), which is far lower than the report of 1.35° [7] . It is beneficial to design angular resonance sensors.

When the grating thickness d is set to be 100nm and other parameters are invariant in the three grating discussed above, the corresponding 0th order reflective spectra are plotted in Fig.3. Except the resonant angle is changed with the grating thickness, the most obvious distinction between the spectra depicted in Fig.2 is that all of the three spectra become wider. All the values of FWHM are increased to1.5°, which is nearly three times as ones shown in Fig.2. It indicates that under the resonant conditions the grating thickness is the most important factor to determinate the spectra shape and to influence the sensing performance.

### III. DESIGN OF SENSORS BY THE PROPOSED STRUCTURE

In the section, we investigate the detection performance of the changes of refractive index in the region □. According to the above discussion, the grating structure parameters of designed SPR sensors when the incident wavelength is 633nm are following as: ε2=2.25, δ=0.33, Λ=400nm, d=60nm and the slant angle φ=60°.

![](./images/813298369869905921_4.jpg)

Figure 4. Reflective efficiency as a function of the incident angle

Fig.4 shows that the resonance angle which induces the reflectance minimum shifts from 9° to 6° as the refractive index of Region□ varies from 1.00 to 1.05 in steps of Δn=0.01. The reflectivity of resonant dip is very deep and only 10% or so. The shift of the resonance angle is completely linear over the range and the sensitivity which indicates the slope of resonance angle over the refractive index is 0.7deg/Δn shown in Fig.5 using solid line with square. Furthermore, the value of the FWHM is very

low only 0.5°. The effective and overall performance of optical sensors is a figure of merit (FOM) [11], which defined as:
$$\mathrm{FOM}=m(\mathrm{eV}/\mathrm{RIU}) / \mathrm{FWHM}(\mathrm{eV}), \tag{3}$$
where $m$ is corresponding to the sensitivity of a SPR sensor. According to this, FOM value of the designed sensor is 140, better than the reports [7-8].

When the grating is unslant ($\phi$=90°) and the other parameters are the same, higher sensitivity (0.8deg/$\Delta$n) is acquired shown in Fig.5 using solid line with circle. But when the grating thickness is increased to 100nm, the sensitivity decreased to 0.4deg/$\Delta$n shown in Fig.5 using solid line with triangle. The thin grating is helpful to the sensitivity because the monitor region is separated from the SPR interface by gratings, while the permeating strength of SPP is limit.

## IV. CONCLUSION

We have numerically investigated the proposed SPR sensor with thin planar dielectric grating on flat metallic substrate using RCWA. The results show that the grating thickness is the most important factor to determinate the spectra shape and to influence the sensing performance. The results in air suggest the proposed sensor present extremely linear sensing characteristics with great sensitivity about 80 degree per refractive index unit and high FOM of 140. The sensor chip has a concise structure with only two planar layers for integrated optoelectronic sensors.
