# Broadband THz Antireflection Grating Transmittance Calculation

## Problem background
Terahertz (THz) radiation generated by optical rectification in gallium phosphide (GaP) crystals suffers from high Fresnel reflection losses at the crystal–air interface because GaP has a high refractive index (around 3.34 in the THz range). Subwavelength antireflective structures with a gradual index profile, such as micropyramid gratings, can reduce these losses and increase the transmitted THz power. This task concerns the design of pyramidal‑frustum grating structures that provide broadband antireflection over the 0.5–5 THz range. The objective is to compute the power transmittance spectrum of an optimized frustum grating and of a simple pyramid grating for normally incident THz radiation, and to assess their antireflection performance.

## Approach
Use rigorous coupled-wave analysis (RCWA), an open‑source Fourier‑space method for solving Maxwell’s equations in periodic structures, to calculate the power transmittance through the GaP–air interface with a subwavelength grating. Two grating designs are defined: (1) a pyramidal frustum with period Λ = 15 μm, height h = 75 μm, and base angle θ = 86.5°; (2) a simple (full‑pyramid) grating with the same Λ and h but a base angle θ = 84.3°. Assume the GaP refractive index is constant (n = 3.34) over the frequency range of interest. For each design, compute the normal‑incidence power transmittance at frequencies from 0.5 to 5 THz with a step no larger than 0.1 THz. Save the two transmittance curves to a CSV file, recording the frequency and the transmittance for each grating at every sampled point.

## Reproduction target
Produce a CSV file at `/app/outputs/transmittance_curve.csv` with columns: `frequency_THz` (float, THz), `transmittance_frustum` (float, power transmittance of the frustum grating), `transmittance_pyramid` (float, power transmittance of the simple pyramid grating). The frequency axis must cover 0.5–5 THz with a spacing ≤ 0.1 THz. The hidden verifier will examine the frustum transmittance over the full band and compare its minimum against a required efficiency threshold, and will also verify that the frustum grating outperforms the pyramid grating at every sampled frequency.
