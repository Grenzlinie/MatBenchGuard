## I. INTRODUCTION
The first order distributed Bragg reflector (DBR) grating is a very important component in many opto-electronic systems, such as reflectors, filters and DBR lasers. A GSE laser structure proposed by Taha Masood et. al. [1] consists of a second-order grating outcoupler and first-order DBR gratings, where the first-order DBR gratings terminating both ends of the laser cavity provide wavelength selective feedback to the laser cavity. Note that only one optical input is excited into the DBR gratings. In this paper, we propose a novel structure of a DBR grating with two input signals. Figure 1 shows the basic geometry of the phase dependent (PD) DBR grating. The grating of length L is in region II. As shown in Fig. 1, two optical input powers ( $e_{y\ in}^L$ and $e_{y\ in}^R$ ) are launched into both ends of the grating. The DRB grating in Fig. 1 is phase dependent because changing the phase of one or both of the incident light waves produces significant changes in the reflected and transmitted light of the DBR. Assume that the two input lights are out of phase at one end of the grating. Their fields cancel in that end while they enforce each other in the other end. Such a PD DBR grating can be applied to high speed interconnects, routings and switching.

## II. RESULTS
We consider the dielectric waveguide structure described in [2], with a free space wavelength of 1550 nm and a first order DBR grating period of 0.23886 μm. Figure 2 (a) shows the normalized output power efficiencies of the left and the right ends of a 50 μm long DBR grating as a function of the phase difference of two incident waves with equal optical inputs incident from the left and right ends of the grating When the two input signals have the same phase, as shown in Fig. 2(a), 70% of the total power propagates from the right end of the grating, while 30% of the power propagates from the left end. On the other hand, if the phase difference of the two inputs is 180°, 30% of the power is transmitted to the right side, and 70% of the power is reflected to the left side. Since the field is assumed to propagate in the z-direction and has the form of exp(jβz), the grating length also represents the phase difference of the two input powers. The length L of the grating that causes a phase shift of $2\pi$ is given by L=$2\pi$/β, where β is the propagation constant. Figure 2(b) shows the output power efficiency as a function of the grating length with zero phase difference between the input waves. In this case, the "$2\pi$ length" L of the grating section is 0.478 μm and the power efficiency varies with the grating length.
