**Abstract:** We propose and demonstrate an all-metal flexible reflective multiband waveplate based on nano-grating structure using high efficient electroplating growing process, which exhibits quarter waveplate at two wavelenths ($\lambda=465$nm and $\lambda=921$nm) and half waveplate at another wavelength ($\lambda=656$nm). Using Finite Difference Time Domain (FDTD) modeling, the phase shift and reflection efficiency are simulated and designed for a variety of geometrical parameters. A fast and cost-effective technique based on conventional interference lithography and nickel electroplating process is demonstrated to fabricate the all-metal, large-area and flexible waveplate. Experimental results show that the fabricated monolithic all-metal nano structure of the proposed device are in high fidelity with the structure on template and the optical performance of the device are in excellent agreement with the theoretical prediction. The proposed structure and the fabrication method suggests an effective way to realize all-metal, ultrathin, self-supporting and flexible devices for various applications in rugged environment such as high temperature or high pressure environment, and also in the fast growing fields of flexible (wearable) optoelectronics, flexible displays and other curved or nonplanar devices.

### 1. Introduction

Controlling and manipulating polarization state of light is crucial in all optical research and applications. The function of a waveplate is to transform polarization state of an electromagnetic wave, which includes commonly conversion between linear and circular polarization (quarter wave retarder), or rotation of the orientation of a linear polarization (half wave retarder). Waveplate can be either reflective or transmissive, in which most commercial products fall into the transmissive category due to conventionally the transmissive optical path. However, more and more applications of waveplate employed in reflective and curved or nonplanar surfaces scenario, such as liquid-crystal displays [1], optical-pickup systems [2], polarization rotated feedback in lasers [3], and flexible optoelectronics [4], make the reflective and flexible polarization elements appealing.

Over the past few years, the control of polarization by utilizing surface plasmon polariton effect has attracted much attention. Half waveplates in reflection mode were proposed based on magnetic plasmon resonance [5], while plasmonic quarter-wave plates in either reflection mode [6] or transmission mode [7–10] were realized by employing periodic array of metallic nanostructures with various patterns, such as nano cross [6], elliptical antenna [7], asymmetric cross-shaped aperture [8] or two perpendicularly arranged nanoslits [9, 10]. In all the structures, changing the lengths of the elements in two orthogonal directions such that desired phase delay between the two orthogonal directions after transmitting can be achieved is the common physical mechanism. Recently, waveplates based on subwavelength gratings have been investigated [11–13] due to its simple structure and relatively easy fabrication. In 2014, *Lin et al.* designed three different metallic nano-grating structures as transmission phase retarders that can transform linearly polarized light to circularly polarized light for wavelengths of 488 nm, 532 nm and 632.8 nm, respectively [11]. The ellipticity of all the circularly polarized light can reach ~90% which show great potential in the applications of three-dimensional (3D) display. In 2009, *Pang et al.* proposed theoretically an optical waveplate that operates in reflection mode by using metallic nano-grid, or equivalently, metal nanometric grooves in a metal surface [12]. In 2013, *Kravchenko et al.* fabricated and experimentally tested a gold nano-grid waveplate that operates as a reflective quarter waveplate at $\lambda = 604$nm and half waveplate at $\lambda = 997$ nm with a complicated technique in which azo-polymer-based interference lithography and a special imprint method [14] were employed. In 2014, *Ribaudo et al.* demonstrated a high efficiency reflective waveplate which exhibits incident angle dependent phase shift tuning capabilities in the midwave infrared region with a three-layer structure [15], in which a wire grid on top of the structure serves as a polarization beam splitter to generate two orthogonal electric fields in the reflection and transmission and the desired phase difference between the two orthogonal electric fields was obtained by precisely controlling the thickness and properties of the 3-layer sandwiched material. However, the fabrication of the device with desired performance is complicated. Usually, metallic patterns involved in the waveplates related nanostructures must be created with the help of beam lithography combined with RIE/ICP (reactive ion etching / inductively coupled plasma etching) process or directly the focused ion beam lithography (FIB), but these

techniques are extremely time consuming and as such not suitable for fabrication of large area metallic nanostructures and even impossible for fabrication of nano patterns on flexible device.

In this paper, we propose and fabricate an all-metal flexible reflective multiband waveplate based on nano-grating structure using high efficient electroplating growing process. Quarter waveplate at two wavelengths ($\lambda = 465$nm and $\lambda = 921$nm) and half waveplate at one wavelength ($\lambda = 656$nm) can be obtained with a single all-metal nanostructure. Experimental results show that the fabricated monolithic all-metal nano structure of the proposed device are in high fidelity with the structure on template and the optical performance of the device are in excellent agreement with the theoretical prediction. The monolithic, all-metal and flexible features make the proposed device distinctive from all those previously reported waveplates which are usually composed of layered metals and dielectrics on a flat and rigid substrate. The proposed structure and the fabrication method suggest an effective way to realize all-metal, ultrathin, self-supporting and flexible devices for various applications in rugged environment such as high temperature or high pressure environment, and also in the fast growing fields of flexible (wearable) optoelectronics, flexible displays and other curved or nonplanar devices.

## 2. Theory and structure design

![](./images/811041818899644416_1.jpg)

Fig. 1. Schematic diagram of a self-supporting multiband waveplate based on Al metallic nano grating on a thin metal (Ni) substrate with parameters: Period of grating, $P$; Ridge width of grating, W and Depth of grating, $H_1$.

The proposed self-supporting multiband waveplate based on subwavelength metallic nano-grid is shown in Fig. 1. The functional device comprises a metallic Al nano-grating on a thin metallic (Ni) substrate. The period of the grating is $P$, ridge width of the grating is $W$, depth of the grating is $H_1$ and the thickness of Al film underneath the grating ridge is $H_2$. It is noted that when the thickness difference ($H_2$$-$$H_1$) is larger than the depth of penetration of the incident light (i.e., ~50nm), the structure can be equivalent to a pure Al-metal nano-grating. Finite difference time domain (FDTD) method (Lumerical FDTD solutions, Canada) is used to calculate the reflected fields in numerical simulation. Periodic boundary conditions and perfectly matched layers condition are applied, respectively, at boundaries along X/Y axis and boundaries along Z axis. The optical properties of Al and Ni given by Palik are adopted [16]. A linearly polarized plane wave with prescribed polarization orientation $\theta$ with respect to X axis normally illuminates the structure from the air side.

Figure 2 shows the amplitude ratio $(\beta=E y / E x)$ (left axis, as the blue arrows point) and
phase difference $(\Delta \varphi)$ (right axis, as the green arrows point) between two orthogonal electric
components ( $E x$ and $E y$ are amplitudes of electric field along X and Y axis, respectively) and
the reflectance as a function of incident wavelength with different structural parameters. For
simplicity, the thickness of $(H_{2}-H_{1})$ is assumed to be much larger than the penetration depth
of the incident light, so the performance of a pure Al nano-grating is calculated. The
simplification will be validated in the following section. In the simulation, a linearly polarized
plane wave is incident from the air along Z axis with a polarization orientation of $\theta=45^{\circ}$ , and
the spectral range of incident plane wave is assumed to be in the range from $0.4 \mu m$ to $1.2 \mu m$ .
Figure 2(a) gives the amplitude ratio of two orthogonal electric fields (i.e., $\beta=E y / E x$ ) in the
reflection and the corresponding phase difference between the two orthogonal components
with parameters: $H_{I}=0.13 \mu m$ , variable period P and fixed aspect ratio $1 / 2(W=P / 2)$ . The
corresponding reflectance of the grating is given in Fig. 2(b). It is seen that the phase
difference decreases with the increased wavelength in a large dynamic range, covering from

$3\pi/2$ (Point A at 465nm), $\pi$ (Point B at 656nm), to $\pi/2$ (Point C at 921nm) across ultraviolet, visible and near infrared wavelength range, respectively, from which half- and quarter-waveplate can be achieved at multiple wavelengths from ultraviolet to near infrared region depending on specific design.

From Fig. 2(a), it is also noticed that the phase difference, amplitude ratio and the reflectance do not change with the period of the nano grating with a constant aspect ratio. This behavior of the waveplate can be understood based on the fact that the TE component of the incident light (i.e., electric component is parallel to the grating grooves) is reflected from the top surface of the grating, while the TM component penetrates into the grooves and will be subsequently reflected at the bottom of the grooves. The phase difference $(\Delta \varphi)$ between the reflected TE component and TM component can be described by the equation:

$$
\Delta \varphi=\left(\frac{2 \pi}{\lambda}\right)\left(\Delta n \bullet H_{1}\right) \tag{1}
$$

Where $\Delta n$ is the equivalent magnitude of birefringence (determined by the aspect ratio, W/P), $\lambda$ is the incident wavelength and $H_{1}$ is the depth of the grating. Thus, by properly selecting the depth and aspect ratio of the grating (independent of the grating period within subwavelength regime), the phase delay between the TE and TM components can be tuned so that a quarter- or a half-wave plate can be obtained when the phase difference is $\pi/2$ or $\pi$, respectively. Figures 2(c) and 2(d) numerically show the effect of the ridge width $(W)$ on the amplitude ratio and phase difference, and also the reflectance with parameters: $P = 0.25\mu\text{m}$ and $H_{1} = 0.13\mu\text{m}$. It is seen that both the phase difference and the amplitude ratio are sensitive to the ridge width of the grating, which is understandable, because the magnitude of birefringence $(\Delta n)$ increases with the aspect ratio. The phase difference increases with the grating width in the short wavelengths while the amplitude ratio increases in the middle wavelength around $0.8\mu\text{m}$. Figures 2(e) and 2(f) show the effects of groove depth $(H_{1})$ on phase difference and amplitude ratio, and the reflectance. As expected, the phase difference and the amplitude ratio are also very sensitive to the groove depth in all the wavelengths (even more sensitive to the groove depth than that to the aspect ratio shown in Fig. 2(c)), which is consistent with Eq. (1). The TM-polarized wave component penetrates into the grooves and is in effect reflected from the bottom of the grating, while the TE-polarized component is reflected mainly from the top surface of the grating. This results in a path length difference and the corresponding phase difference between the components, which clearly explains that a larger $H_{1}$ results in a larger phase delay, as shown in Eq. (1). Furthermore, from Figs. 2(b), 2(d) and 2(f), it is seen that the total reflectance of the device across a wide band of wavelength remains at a high level, and the effect of parameters of the nanograting, such as period, aspect ratio, and depth, on the reflection is very limited.

As indicated in Fig. 1, the proposed metal grating is actually composed of two sets of nanogratings, in which Al grating sits on a Ni grating owing to the fabrication process. As a result, the relative thickness $(H_{2})$ of the Al film and the thickness of the Al grating $(H_{1})$ will have impact on the performance of the waveplate. Figures 3(a) and 3(b) show the effect of $H_{2}$ on the phase difference, amplitude ratio and the reflectance of the device, in which $H_{1}$ is fixed at $0.13\mu\text{m}$. It is seen that the amplitude ratio, phase difference, and the reflectance are almost the same as that of pure Al-grating when $H_{2}$ is larger than $0.18\mu\text{m}$, i.e., the thickness difference of $(H_{2}$-$H_{1})$ is larger than 50nm and the Ni gating is completely shielded by the Al grating. In contrast, it is noted that the amplitude ratio and the reflectance change significantly if $H_{2}$ is less than $H_{1}$, i.e., the Ni-grating is not fully covered by the Al grating. In this case, the amplitude ratio greatly deviates from unity and the reflection is much lower than that of pure Al grating. Therefore, it is noted that the thickness of Al film $(H_{2})$ should be larger than $0.18\mu\text{m}$ in the fabrication.

For an ideal waveplate, the amplitude ratio of two orthogonal electric fields ($E_y/E_x$) must be unity in addition to the required phase difference (i.e., $\pi/2$ for a quarter waveplate or $\pi$ for a half waveplate). From Fig. 2(a), it is seen that the required phase difference can be obtained at different wavelengths (points A, B and C) with designed aspect ratio (W/P) and the thickness of the grating ($H_1$), the amplitude ratio at the corresponding wavelength, however, is not unity if the orientation of the incident polarization is $45^\circ$. It is found that the amplitude ratio of the waveplate can be adjusted by changing the polarization orientation of the incident light. Figure 3(c) shows the amplitude ratio of the waveplate with the incident polarization orientation at operating wavelength 465nm ($\Delta\varphi=3\pi/2$), 656nm ($\Delta\varphi=\pi$) and 921nm ( $\Delta\varphi=\pi/2$ ), respectively. It is seen that the amplitude ratio changes with the polarization orientation sensitively with a linear relationship in which $E_y/E_x=1$ can be obtained at different designed wavelengths from $\theta=40^\circ$~$45^\circ$. In this way, prefect half- and quarter-waveplate at selected wavelength can be implemented with suitable polarization orientation of the incident light. The reflectance of the waveplate with different incident polarization orientation is shown in Fig. 3(d). It is shown that the reflectance increases with the polarization orientation and the largest value can be obtained when $\theta=90^\circ$. At the working wavelengths (i.e., 465nm, 656nm and 921nm) of the device, the reflectance are larger than 70% and the average value is about 80% with polarization orientation around $40^\circ$~$45^\circ$.

### 3. Fabrication and characterization

The self-supporting and flexible waveplate was fabricated using UV interference lithography combined with electroplating technology [17]. The cost effective fabrication process that is suitable for large-area nanopatterned metal device is illustrated in Fig. 4. From step I to III, a

photoresist grating on $SiO_2$ substrate is fabricated with double beam interference lithography. Firstly, a thin layer (~130nm) of RZJ-5312 photoresist was spin coated. The double beam interference exposure was then used to pattern the photoresist with a period of 250nm. After exposure and development of photoresist, a layer (~200nm) of Al was sputtered onto the photoresist grating (Step IV) with magnetron sputtering system. Then, a layer (~200nm) of Ni was sputtered onto the structure to protect the Al film from interacting with nickel sulphamate electrolyte solution. The Ni film on the top layer was also used as the seed layer for electroplating (Step V). The electroplating environment is critical for growing nanoscale features. In our experiment, bath temperature at $55^\circ$C and pH value at 4.7 was maintained. In order to reduce the internal stress of the film, the current density was firstly set to 0.5 $mA/mm^2$ for 30minutes (growing about $5\mu m$) and set to 1.0 $mA/mm^2$ for about 2 hours (growing about $100\mu m$). Finally, the all-metal (Al/Ni) nanostructure was decoupled from the SiO2 substrate with acetone (Step VI).

The optical properties of the fabricated waveplate were characterized by using an experimental setup presented in Fig. 6. A broadband supercontinuum source (Fianium, SC450) was generated by coupling a sub-nanosecond Q-switched pulsed laser into a 2m long highly nonlinear photonics crystal fiber (PCF). The broadband light was firstly directed to a monochromator and a collimator. A linear polarization light was produced with a polarizer (polarizer I) and incident on the sample through a beam splitter. The reflected light was split out by the beam splitter and characterized by rotating an additional linear polarizer (polarizer II) in front of a detector (Thorlabs, PAX5710IR1-T) as shown in Fig. 6(a). The measurement was started by replacing the sample with a high-quality mirror (Thorlabs, PFSQ10-03-G01) and measuring the reflection spectrum of the system with polarizer I and II aligned for maximum reflection. This spectrum was used as a reference for reflection normalization. Then, the sample was inserted into the system and it was rotated such that an orientation

angle between the orientation of polarizer I and the grating grooves is equal to the designed one. Polarizer II was then rotated with a step of $10^\circ$ over the full angle of $360^\circ$ and the reflection was recorded at each step. Figure 6(b) shows the illustration of the conversion from an incident linear polarization (solid line with different orientation $\theta$) to a circular polarization or a linear polarization with orthogonal orientation at 3 different designed wavelengths (dashed lines), i.e., 465nm (red), 656nm (green) and 921nm (blue). The dashed rotating circles represent the electric field vector of the reflected circular polarization light at 465nm (red) and 921nm (blue), respectively, and the dashed green line represents the reflected linear polarization at 656nm.

Figure 7(a) shows the measured spectra of the reflection from the fabricated device at two orthogonal rotation angles of polarizer II, i.e., one is the $X$-component (red) of the reflection and the other is the $Y$-component (black) of the reflection. The orientation angle of the incident linear polarization is set to $\theta=45^\circ$ with respect to the sample grooves along $Y$ axis, i.e., the polarization orientation of polarizer I is adjusted to $45^\circ$ with respect to the $X$ axis direction. The theoretical results are also shown in Fig. 7(a) for comparison. It is seen that the experimental spectra are very close to the theoretical one with only small deviations due to the surface imperfection (roughness) of the fabricated device as witnessed in Fig. 5(b). It is also seen that the reflection of the $Y$-component is not exactly equal to the $X$-component when $\theta=45^\circ$, which implies that the orientation of the incident polarization must be adjusted to compensate the reflection difference between the two orthogonal components in order to obtain a perfect waveplate operation.

The $90^\circ$-angle rotation in polarization orientation confirms that the designed self-supporting all-metal half waveplate indeed transforms a linearly polarized incident wave into a reflected wave with a cross polarization, which is in excellent agreement with the simulations.

## 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we experimentally demonstrated an all-metal self-supporting flexible reflective multiband waveplate which functions as a quarter waveplate at two wavelengths ($\lambda=465$nm and $\lambda=921$nm) and a half waveplate at another wavelength ($\lambda=656$nm). The working wavelengths can be designed with appropriate structural parameters of a subwavelength grating. A fast and cost-effective fabrication technique for the all-metal self-supporting flexible device was proposed in which UV interference lithography and nickel (Ni) electroplating processes are employed. Both the polarization state and energy efficiency of the fabricated device were measured and compared to theoretical simulations, which shows excellent agreement between the theory and the experiment. The demonstration of the work suggests the potential for generating arbitrary phase shift with a simple grating structure and also a high-efficient technique for fabricating all-metal, large area and flexible devices.
