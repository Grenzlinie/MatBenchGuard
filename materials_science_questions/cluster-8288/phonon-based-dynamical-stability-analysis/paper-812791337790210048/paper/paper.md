### Abstract
View Article Online
DOI: 10.1039/C9TC00170K

Materials possessing both superhard and metallic properties are beneficial to the creation of multifunctional devices under extreme conditions. Here, we report the formation of a new metallic superhard boron nitride at high pressure with stoichiometry $B_2N_3$ through the first-principles calculations and structure searching. At ambient pressure, $B_2N_3$ has layered structures ($h$-$B_2N_3$) consisting of hexagonal $B_4N_4$ layers intercalated by triply bonded $N_2$ molecules. With the pressure increasing to $\sim$10 GPa, $h$-$B_2N_3$ transforms to a three-dimensional tetragonal structure ($t$-$B_2N_3$) with the formation of single N-N bonds. Calculations reveal that $t$-$B_2N_3$ can be recovered under ambient conditions in view of the dynamical, thermal and mechanical stabilities. Interestingly, $t$-$B_2N_3$ is proposed to be a superhard material with an estimated Vicker's hardness of $\sim$52 GPa by performing stress-strain calculations. More importantly, electronic calculations show unique two-dimensional metallicity in $t$-$B_2N_3$, which originates from $\pi$ orbits of N-N bonds spreading in $ab$ plane. In addition, the energy density of $\sim$2.95 kJ/g makes $t$-$B_2N_3$ a potential high-energy density material.

Keywords: Superhard materials; metallicity; nitrogen-rich boron nitride; high-energy density materials

### Introduction

Diamond is renowned for its ultrahigh hardness and thermal conductivity.¹ These properties determine its modern industrial applications in cutting and polishing tools, and the scientific applications in diamond anvil cells. However, these utilities are limited because diamond is prone to be oxidized in air at high temperature² and reactive to ferrous metals³. Therefore, the search of alternative superhard materials that chemically and thermally more stable is extremely urgent.

Compounds consisting of light elements (such as B, C, N, O and Si) have been highly considered as the best candidates since they can easily form covalent bonding networks, which is essential for superhard materials. Graphene, which is constructed by a two-dimensional sheet of covalently bonded carbon atoms, has extraordinary mechanical properties⁴⁻⁸ and thermal conductivity⁹⁻¹¹. Besides, boron nitride (BN) has attracted considerable attention since it exists in various crystalline forms that is isoelectronic to a similarly structured carbon lattice. Indeed, several superhard polymorphs (such as $c$-BN¹², $w$-BN¹³, bct-BN¹⁴, O-BN¹⁴, M-BN¹⁵ and Z'-BN¹⁵) of BN have been reported with estimated Vicker's hardness of between 47 and 66.8 GPa. Compounds formed by mixing B and C or B, C and N are also very promising superhard materials, such as synthesized BC₂N¹⁶ and theoretically predicted B₂CO¹⁷ and BCN¹⁸. In addition to B, C and N, light elements O and Si can participate in the formation of superhard materials under compression, such as B₆O¹⁹ and Si₃N₄²⁰. Regrettably, all these superhard materials are insulators or semiconductors, limiting their applications as electronic devices in the field of high pressure research.²¹

A recent theoretical work has reported the formation of a new N-rich boron nitride with stoichiometry B₃N₅ by compressing a mixture of $h$-BN and N₂.²² At ambient pressure, B₃N₅ has layered structure ($h$-B₃N₅) with large band gap of ~5.00 eV, close to that of layered $h$-BN (5.20 eV). $h$-B₃N₅ undergoes a structure transformation to a three-dimensional (3D) superhard $C222_1$ phase at 15 GPa, which can be recovered under ambient conditions. Significantly, $C222_1$-B₃N₅ is found to be a semiconductor with a relatively small band gap of ~0.78 eV (Fig. S1), much smaller than that of $c$-BN (6.40 eV). This result provides a possible route to access metallicity in superhard N-rich B/N compounds. Subsequently, theoretical studies have shown the existence of another superhard N-rich B/N compound with stoichiometry of B₃N₄.²³ In fact, the structure of superhard B₃N₄ can be obtained by removing two nitrogen atoms from the $C222_1$ structure of B₃N₅ (Fig. 1). Moreover, the removal of N atoms lowers the Fermi level, making B₃N₄ a metal with unique two-dimensional (2D) metallicity in a 3D framework (Fig. S2).

Here, we introduce an alternative route to design metallic N-rich B/N compound by adding a BN layer in B₃N₅ to form B₄N₆ (B₂N₃), in which the BN layer plays a role of electron donor. Structure search uncovers a superhard

phase denoted as $t$-B₂N₃ that becomes energetically most stable at 10 GPa. Indeed, the Fermi level in $t$-B₂N₃ is lifted to cross bands induced by the introduction of BN layer, making $t$-B₂N₃ a metallic superhard material. Moreover, $t$-B₂N₃ is also predicted to be a potential high-energy density material with calculated energy density of ~2.95 kJ/g.

## Results and discussions

The structure searches at ambient pressure uncover two energetically degenerate layered structures for B₂N₃ with space groups $P1$ and $R3m$ (denoted as $h$-B₂N₃), respectively. Similar to the layered structures predicted in previous N-rich compounds such as B₃N₅²²and B₃N₄²³, the two structures of $h$-B₂N₃ consist of same hexagonal BN layers sandwiched with differently orientated N₂ molecules (Fig. 1a and 1b). The two energetically comparable layered structures with different orientation of N₂ molecules suggest relatively weak interaction between BN layers and N₂ molecules. The B-N bond length in each BN layer is found to be 1.45Å, same as that in $h$-BN²⁴. The intercalated N₂ molecules are triply bonded with $N\equiv N$ bond length of 1.11 Å, which is slightly longer than that (1.07Å) of pure N₂²⁵and much shorter than that (1.21 Å) of a typical $N=N$ double bond in N₂F₂.²⁶

At 50 GPa, the structure searches yield a tetragonal 3D structure for B₂N₃ with space group $P4_2/mmc$ (denoted as $t$-B₂N₃). In fact, the $P4_2/mmc$ structure is also successfully predicted at 0 GPa, but with relatively higher enthalpy with respect to $h$-B₂N₃. Enthalpy calculations show B₂N₃ is energetically more stable with respect to the mixture of $h$-BN and N₂ in the whole pressure range studied here, indicating the feasibility of their experimental synthesis. With the pressure increasing, $h$-B₂N₃ is energetically most stable up to 10 GPa, above which $t$-B₂N₃ becomes energetically most stable (Fig. S3). The interaction between BN layers and N₂ molecules in $h$-B₂N₃ enhances with increasing pressure, and the phase transformation to $t$-B₂N₃ is accompanied by the formation of single N-N covalent bonds (1.33 Å) linking neighboring layers. As a result, two types of N atoms occur in $t$-B₂N₃, namely $sp^2$ N1 atoms bonded with two B atoms and one N1 atom with B-N1 bond length of 1.53 Å, and $sp^3$ N2 atoms bonded with four B atoms with B-N2 bond length of 1.59 Å (Fig. 1c). The pressure (10 GPa) needed to form single N-N bond in B₂N₃ is found to be lower than that in B₃N₅ (15 GPa) and B₃N₄ (20 GPa) ²²,²³. In fact, both $t$-B₂N₃ and $t$-B₃N₄ can be constructed based on $C222_1$-B₃N₅. As shown in Fig. 1(d), $C222_1$-B₃N₅ contains alternately stacking sequence of NBNBN (underlines are guidance to distinguish adjacent layers) and NBN blocks that are interlinked by N-N bonds. The removal of two N layers marked by blue dashed ovals from $C222_1$-B₃N₅ forms $t$-B₃N₄ (B₃N₅-N=B₃N₄), which consists of NBNBNBN blocks connected by single N-N bonds (Fig. 1e). Interestingly, the intercalation of one BN layer around the NBN block in $C222_1$-B₃N₅ results in $t$-B₂N₃ (B₃N₅+BN=2B₂N₃).

The calculated phonon dispersions confirm that $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ is dynamically stable both at 0 and 20 GPa in view of the absence of any imaginary frequencies in the entire Brillouin zone (Fig. 2). We further explore the thermal stability of $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ at ambient conditions by performing AIMD simulations using canonical ensemble. After heating 300 K for 10 ps with a time step of 1fs, no structure distortions are found, indicating that $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ is metastable at ambient conditions (Fig. S4). To verify the mechanical stability of $t$-B$_2$N$_3$, we have calculated the elastic constants tabulated in Table 1. The whole set of elastic constants satisfy the mechanical stability criteria for a tetrahedral crystal, i.e., $C_{\rm ii} > 0$, (i =1, 3, 4, 6), $(C_{11}-C_{12}) > 0$, $(C_{11}+C_{33}-2C_{13}) > 0$, $[2(C_{11}+C_{12})+C_{33}+4C_{13}] > 0$, indicating the mechanical stability of $t$-B$_2$N$_3$. The independent elastic constants can be used to derive the bulk modulus $(B_0)$ and shear modulus $(G_0)$. Although smaller than those of diamond, the calculated $B_0$ and $G_0$ of 337 GPa and 300 GPa, respectively, for $t$-B$_2$N$_3$, are nearly equal to those of $C222_1$-B$_3$N$_5$ and $t$-B$_3$N$_4$ . This is understandable sine the three B-N compounds share similar bond configurations. Moreover, we found that the $G_0/B_0$ ratio (0.89) of $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ is comparable to that (0.9-1.2) of typical superhard materials$^{27}$, making $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ a potential superhard material. The Pugh ratio ($B_0/G_0$ ratio) is defined as a quantitative index for assessing the brittle or ductile behavior of crystals. According to Pugh$^{28}$, $B_0/G_0$ values > 1.75 and < 1.75 represent ductile and brittle, respectively. The Pugh value of $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ is calculated to be 1.12, indicating that $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ is brittle.

The stress-strain relations provide deep insights into the local bond deformation and breaking mechanisms that determine the incipient plasticity in a crystal,$^{29}$ and the ideal strength deduced from the stress-strain curves is compared to nanoindentation measurements, thus can be used to estimate the hardness of materials. Figure 3 (a) shows the calculated tensile stress of $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ along five directions. The calculated peak stresses are 150, 80, 63, 136, and 100 GPa in the <001>, <011>, <100>, <110>, and <111> directions, respectively. The weakest peak stress in the <100> directions indicates an ideal tensile stress of 63 GPa for $t$-B$_2$N$_3$, which is larger than (56 GPa)$^{22}$of $C222_1$-B$_3$N$_5$. We then evaluate the shear stress response in the easy cleavage (100) planes by applying shear deformations (Fig. 3b). The ideal shear stress is found to be 53 GPa in view of the weakest peak shear stress in the (100)[011] direction. This suggests a vicker's hardness of 53 GPa for $t$-B$_2$N$_3$, which is larger than those of other B-N compounds (e.g., 42.5 GPa for $t$-B$_3$N$_4$$^{23}$, 45.2 GPa for $c$-B$_3$N$_4$$^{30}$, and 44.0 GPa for $C222_1$-B$_3$N$_5$$^{22}$) and is comparable to that (47-68 GPa$^{12}$) of $c$-BN. Therefore, $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ possesses potential applications in industry as superhard material.

To understand the local bond deformation and breaking mechanism, we have plotted the evolution of bond lengths of $t$-B$_2$N$_3$ under the (100)[011] shear strain. As shown in Fig. 3 (c), a sharp increase of B1-N1 bond length is found at the strain of 0.25, which corresponds to the peak shear stress. The structure snapshots and the

corresponding electron localization function (ELF) before and after the shear instability under the (100)[011] shear strain are presented in Fig. 4. It is obviously that the bonds have been broken after the strain of 0.25 in view of the absence of ELF between B1 and N1 atoms, indicating that the B1-N1 bonds are the main load bearing component. Interestingly, a sudden decrease of the B2-N1 bond is found accompanying with the sharp increase of B1-N1 bond. This is understandable if one consider that each N1 atom connects with both B1 and B2, and N1 atoms will be inevitably move close to B2 atoms once the B1-N1 bonds are broken.

The band structure and projected density of states (PDOS) of $t$-B₂N₃ were calculated and shown in Fig. 5. With the interpolation of one BN layer (electron donor), the Fermi level in $t$-B₂N₃ is promoted to cross the two bands as compared to $C222_1$-B₃N₅, indicating the metallic nature of $t$-B₂N₃ (Fig. 5a and Fig. S1). PDOS calculations reveal that the metallicity originates mainly from $p_{xy}$ orbits of N atoms, while B atoms have almost no contribution in energy range between -2 eV and 2 eV. In the energy range of -12 and -2 eV, there is substantial overlap between B and N orbitals, indicating strong B-N covalent bonding.

Further band decomposed (partial) charge density is evaluated to confirm the conductive directions in $t$-B₂N₃, and the calculated charge density is from the two bands across the Fermi level (highlighted in red dashed rectangular frame). As seen from Fig. 5 (b), the charge density of two bands across the Fermi level is entirely contributed by $\pi$ orbits of N-N bonds. The conductive electrons are spread in the $a$-$b$ planes, reflecting $t$-B₂N₃ is two-dimensional conductive.

The large difference of bond strength between triple $N\equiv N$ (954 kJ/mol) and single N-N bonds (160 kJ/mol) makes compounds with single N-N bonds potential high-energy density materials³¹⁻³³, since the transformation from single N-N bonds back to triple $N\equiv N$ bonds is expected to be highly exothermic. We then calculated the energy involved in the decomposition of $t$-B₂N₃ into solid BN and N₂ at ambient pressure. The energy difference was 1.95 eV, which corresponds to an energy density of ~2.95 kJ/g. Although slightly lower than that of $C222_1$-B₃N₅ (3.44 kJ/g), $t$-B₂N₃ is still a potential high-energy density material. Remarkably, $t$-B₂N₃ is thermodynamically stable at ~10 GPa, which is much lower than the formation pressures of $C222_1$-B₃N₅ (15 GPa) and $t$-B₃N₄ (14.7 GPa). This stability at a relatively low pressure suggests that $t$-B₂N₃ might be synthesized by high-pressure and high-temperature techniques.

In summary, we have predicted the crystal structures of B₂N₃ at the pressures range of 0-50 GPa using an unbiased swarm structure search method. A tetragonal three-dimensional structure containing single N-N bonds was predicted to be energetically most stable at above 10 GPa, which can be recovered under ambient conditions. Significantly, calculations show that $t$-B₂N₃ possesses both superhard and 2D metallicity with an estimated vicker’s

hardness of ~52 GPa. Moreover, the energy density of ~2.95 kJ/g makes $t$-B₂N₃ a potential high-energy density material. Our predicted B₂N₃ compound is a multi-role material combined metallicity, superhard and high-energy density.

## Methods

### Structure prediction

The search of B₂N₃ structures (1-4 formula units) were performed at pressures of 0-50 GPa via an unbiased swarm-intelligence based CALYPSO (Crystal structure AnaLYsis by Particle Swarm Optimization) method³⁴,³⁵, which was designed to search for the most stable or metastable structures of given compounds.³⁶⁻⁴³

### First-principles calculations

Structural optimizations, electronic structures, phonons, and molecular dynamics were performed in the framework of density functional theory (DFT). The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) expressed by Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional⁴⁴ as implemented in the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP)⁴⁵ was adopted. The electron-ion interaction was described by the projector augmented-wave potentials⁴⁶, with $2s^22p^1$ and $2s^22p^3$ configurations treated as valence electrons for B and N, respectively. A kinetic cutoff energy of 520 eV and Monkhorst-Pack $k$ meshes with a grid spacing of $2\pi \times 0.03$ Å⁻¹ were adopted to obtain converged total energies (~1meV/atom). The dynamical stability of the predicted structures was verified from phonon calculation using the direct supercell method as implemented in the PHONOPY code.⁴⁷,⁴⁸ To confirm the thermal stability, *ab initio* molecular dynamic (AIMD) simulations were performed with a time step of 1 fs, and the total simulation time was above 10 ps. The temperature was controlled at 300 K using the Nosé-Hoover thermostat with 3×3×2 supercell that contains 180 atoms. The bulk modulus, shear modulus, and Young's modulus were estimated using Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation.⁴⁹ The ideal tensile and shear strength in the various directions were estimated by calculating stress-strain relations using a method descried previously.⁵⁰⁻⁵²
