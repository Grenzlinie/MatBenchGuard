## ABSTRACT

We present the first principles calculations of electronic, structural and optical properties of $Ge_2N_2(NH)$ and $Sn_2N_2(NH)$, isostructural with $Si_2N_2(NH)$ and $C_2N_2(NH)$. The $M_2N_2(NH)$ compounds, where M = Si or C or Ge or Sn, crystallise into base-centered orthorhombic (BCO) structure, space group $Cmc2_1$. Our results indicate that $Ge_2N_2(NH)$ and $Sn_2N_2(NH)$ are stable compounds, satisfying mechanical stability tests and lack imaginary phonon modes. In addition, the two compounds have fairly smaller bulk moduli compared to $Si_2N_2(NH)$ and $C_2N_2(NH)$. $Ge_2N_2(NH)$ has a direct band gap of 2.66 eV in DFT-GGA while $Sn_2N_2(NH)$ posses a small indirect band gap of 1.05 eV. We also analyse the dielectric behaviours of the $M_2N_2(NH)$ compounds within infrared (IR), visible and ultraviolet (UV) regimes in which $Ge_2N_2(NH)$ and $Sn_2N_2(NH)$ show remarkable characteristics.

### 1. Introduction

In recent years nitride materials $Si_3N_4$, $Ge_3N_4$, $Sn_3N_4$ and $C_3N_4$ have attracted considerable attention due to their interesting electronic properties for technological application. These binary compounds exist in numerous polymorph such as $\alpha$, $\beta$ and cubic spinel [1,2,4,5]. $Si_3N_4$, $Ge_3N_4$ and $Sn_3N_4$ have been synthesized in laboratories yet $C_3N_4$ remains a major scientific problem. Properties such as dielectric constants, elastic constants, shear and bulk modulus and band gaps of $Si_3N_4$, $Ge_3N_4$, $Sn_3N_4$ and $C_3N_4$ are well known from experimental measurements and theoretical calculations [3,6] and therefore, we will not discuss it in this paper. However, one will easily notice that these materials are isostructural whose formula can be presented as $M_3N_4$, (where M = C, Si, Ge and Sn). With this analogy, we study the electronic structure and optical properties of another set of formula presented as $M_2N_2(NH)$, where M = C, Si, Ge and Sn. The $M_2N_2(NH)$ compounds crystallise into base-centered orthorhombic (BCO) structure, also described as defect-wurtzite structure with space group $Cmc2_1$, see Fig. 1. Previous experimental measurements by Hovarth-Bordon et al. [7], in a laser-heated diamond anvil cell under high pressure, high temperature revealed that $C_2N_2(NH)$ crystallises into BCO structure. Other measurements using Raman scattering and Synchrotron X-rays carried out by Salamat et al.[8] also confirmed that $C_2N_2(NH)$ crystallise in BCO structure. Theoretical calculation of structural and electronic properties of $Si_2N_2(NH)$ and $C_2N_2(NH)$ have also been reported by various groups [7–11].

To our knowledge there is no information regarding properties of $Ge_2N_2(NH)$ and $Sn_2N_2(NH)$ which are isostructural to $C_2N_2(NH)$ and $Si_2N_2(NH)$. Thus, the focus of this paper, is to predict the structural equilibrium properties, electronic and optical properties of $Ge_2N_2(NH)$ and $Sn_2N_2(NH)$. In Section 2 we give computational details, while in Sections 3 and 4 we discuss and give a conclusions to our predictions.

### 2. Computational details

We investigate properties of $M_2N_2(NH)$ compounds using density functional theory [12,13] and projector augmented wave potentials [14]. The electron–electron interaction is treated within the generalised gradient approximation [15–17] as implemented in VASP [18,19]. We performed calculations using an energy cutoff of 600 eV and the $6\times6\times6$ $\Gamma$-centered Monkhorst–Pack grid [20], a special $k$-point sampling method for the numerical integration of the Brillouin-zone.

Considering bulk modulus as a measure of resistance against a change of volume of a material caused by an applied external pressure, then both Sn₂N₂(NH) and Ge₂N₂(NH) offer minimal resistance to external pressure. The bulk modulus is an intrinsic property of the hardness of a material, thus a material with large bulk modulus should in nature be hard. Following this principle, both Sn₂N₂(NH) and Ge₂N₂(NH) are not hard materials. However, Ge₂N₂(NH) may be more attractive since its hardness is almost twice as much that of Sn₂N₂(NH). The nature and the size of chemical bonding in materials immensely contribute to the size of the bulk modulus. Shorter lengths yield large bulk modulus and vice versa. A systematic probe of bond lengths for compounds considered in this work show that the lengths ascends from C—N < Si—N < Ge—N < Sn—N. The strong covalent and hence shorter C—N bond lengths is responsible for the large bulk modulus in C₂N₂(NH). Generally, from the sizes of M—N bonding (M = C, Si, Ge, Sn), it is apparent Ge₂N₂(NH) and Sn₂N₂(NH) are softer than both C₂N₂(NH) and Si₂N₂(NH) because the bond lengths in Ge—N and Sn—N are longer than both in Si—N and C—N. The N—H bond lengths among the four compounds are invariant within the precision of our calculations, yet H—H is weakly bonded. The calculated GGA-DFT bond lengths are reported in Table 2.

In semiconducting materials, band gaps are classified as either being direct or indirect. The differences arise from the positions of the valency band maximum and the conduction band minimum in the Brillouin zone. For instance, we predict Si₂N₂(NH) and Ge₂N₂(NH) to have a direct band gap of 5.22 eV and 2.66 eV respectively. This implies that both valence and conduction bands erect at the same k-vector in the Brillouin zone. However, for indirect band gaps materials such as C₂N₂(NH) and Sn₂N₂(NH) with calculated values as 4.40 eV and 1.05 eV respectively, the conduction band minimum occurs at different position in Brillouin zone from that of valence band maximum. Our calculated DFT-GGA band gaps for C₂N₂(NH) and Si₂N₂(NH) are in agreement with previous calculations as shown in Table 1. Note, the nature of the band gap (direct or indirect) of a material significantly affects the recombination process in which electrons pair up with holes. It is important to note that the actual band gap value will be larger than the predicted values since DFT-GGA calculations underestimate the band gap. From this perspective, we conclude that Sn₂N₂(NH) and Ge₂N₂(NH) fall in the category of wide-gap semiconductors which makes them attractive for useful electronic applications.

BCO structural have nine independent elastic constants and all satisfy Born stability criteria [22]. Elastic constants for Ge₂N₂(NH) and Sn₂N₂(NH) are presented in Table 3. Further analysis of vibrational/phonon modes (Supplementary information), we find negligible imaginary modes, which is a clear indication that both Ge₂N₂(NH) and Sn₂N₂(NH) are stable. ### 3.3. Optical properties

We show in Fig. 3 the real $(\varepsilon_{1})$ and imaginary $(\varepsilon_{2})$ parts of dielectric function $(\varepsilon)$ from 0 to 25 eV for $C_{2}N_{2}(NH)$, $Si_{2}N_{2}(NH)$, $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$. The imaginary part of the dielectric function also represents the optical absorption spectrum. The calculation was carried out within the random-phase approximation without the local field effects. Overall, the four isostructural compounds show some common features; two main peaks in $\varepsilon_{2}$, a small $\varepsilon_{2}$ and large $\varepsilon_{1}$ at lower photon energies. For instance, the two main peaks in the $\varepsilon_{2}$ of $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ appears around 4 eV and 6.4 eV while in $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ it occurs at about 5.1 eV and 7.7 eV. For $C_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Si_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ the prominent peaks occur at slightly higher energies of about 7.8 eV and 12 eV, and 7.2 eV and 9.4 eV respectively. In depth analysis of the $\varepsilon_{2}$, show that $Sn_{2-}$ $N_{2}(NH)$ has exceptionally smaller absorption threshold when compared to $C_{2}N_{2}(NH)$, $Si_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$. The absorption threshold is the minimum direct band gap above which interband transitions occur i.e from top of valence band to the bottom of conduction band. In principle, no inter-band transitions occur for photon energies below the band gap $E_{g}$ [23]. Normally, $\varepsilon_{1}$ is obtained from $\varepsilon_{2}$ via the Kramers-Kronig transformations[24]. It is a common trend in insulators and semiconductors as shown in Fig. 3, for the $\varepsilon_{1}$ to transit from a stationary point at zero energy, then increases before a sudden drop as energies increase. The $\varepsilon_{1}$ goes to negative at some energies before gradually moving up to unity at higher energies. Incidentally, the dielectric constant of free space is also unity which connotes that there is no electric polarisation for a medium at higher energies. The main peak in $\varepsilon_{1}$ of $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ is quite distinguishable since it evolved at around 1.6 eV yet similar peak in the other compounds occur between 5 and 6 eV. Within the zero limit, photon energy in the spectra of $\varepsilon_{1}$ is important since it corresponds to a static dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{0}$. Note that for orthorhombic structures, it is reasonable to calculate components of dielectric function parallel to crystallographic axes $a$, $b$ and $c$. For clarity the components of dielectric function are presented as $\varepsilon_{\parallel a}$, $\varepsilon_{\parallel b}$ and $\varepsilon_{\parallel c}$. However, in normal laboratory experiments, it is tedious to grow a single crystal, and hence it is preferable to perform experiments on polycrystalline samples. In such a case, it is insufficient or inaccurate to calculate $\varepsilon_{0}$ parallel to a single crystallographic axis. It is therefore, considerable to present $\varepsilon_{0}$ as the average of $\varepsilon_{\parallel a}$, $\varepsilon_{\parallel b}$ and $\varepsilon_{\parallel c}$, and results are as listed in Table 4. Point to note here is that for anisotropic crystal structure polarizability is also anisotropic and depended on the compound under consideration. For instance, $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ is highly polarised along $c$ and hence high dielectric constant along $(\varepsilon_{\parallel c})$. On the other hand, $Si_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ turns out to have lower dielectric constant along $\varepsilon_{\parallel c}$.

We also calculated the electron energy loss spectra (EELS) and refractive index of the four compounds. The calculated EELS showed plasmon peaks at 13.4 eV, 21.5 eV, 23.5 eV and 31–35 eV for $\text{Sn}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$, $\text{Ge}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$, $\text{Si}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$ and $\text{C}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$ respectively.

The calculated refractive index $n$ from the relation $n=\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ show that there is quite small variation of about 0.01–0.02 among the four compounds. It emerges that in spite of $\text{Sn}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$ having small band gap, absorption threshold and small plasmon peak, it have highest $n$ of about 2.21 while $\text{C}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$, $\text{Si}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$ and $\text{Ge}_{2}\text{N}_{2}(\text{NH})$ have $n$ of about 2.19, 2.00 and 2.20 respectively. In summary, we conclude that narrow/small band gap materials have high refractive index and high dielectric constant. This phenomenon is attributed to how electric field interacts with electrons and how electrons freely locomote in available electronic states found in valence and conduction bands. It is obvious that narrow band gaps allows electrons to drift easily within electronic states as opposed to large band gaps. Once electric field interacts with

### 3.4. IR, visible and UV regime

We investigated the dielectric behaviours of the four com- pounds within infrared (IR), visible and ultraviolet (UV) regimes and results shown in Fig. 4. Clearly, within IR regime $\varepsilon_{1}$ and $\varepsilon_{2}$ of the four compounds look rather similar except noticeable shift in $\varepsilon_{2}$ of $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ at the edges of IR regime. Ideally, absorption would increase with decreasing wavelengths more so below wave- length corresponding to the band gap. Therefore, we conclude that $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ start absorbing within IR regime. Absorption strength is even higher for $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ within the visible range. Notice that $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ show an abrupt shift in both $\varepsilon_{1}$ and $\varepsilon_{2}$ at the extreme end. This clearly indicates onset of absorption for $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ is within visible range and strong absorption between UV wave- lengths. In search for light-emitting devices, both $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ are promising candidates whose electronic transitions prevails within visible and UV regimes. There are no possible in- ter-band transitions for $C_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Si_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ since both com pounds remains featureless within infrared, visible and ultraviolet regimes. This confirms that $C_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Si_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ are insulators.

## 4. Conclusion

We have made an investigation of the structural, electronic and optical properties of $M_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ compounds including the newly predicted $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$. Both $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Ge_{2}N_{2}(-$ NH) are stable compounds satisfying Born mechanical stability tests carried out by calculating independent elastic constants. The hardness properties have been predicted by calculating the bulk modulus of $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$. Our results predicted $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ to be softer than $C_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Si_{2}N_{2}(-$ NH). The carbon-nitrogen covalent bonds in $C_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ is responsi- ble for the high bulk modulus. A trend is established between dielectric constants and band gap of $M_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ compounds where- by compounds with small band gaps have high dielectric con- stants. $Sn_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ and $Ge_{2}N_{2}(NH)$ are promising candidates for optical applications like making signal processing gadgets and light-emitting devices such as light-emitting diodes. Hopeful our calculations will stimulate experimental studies to confirm our predictions.
