PHYSICAL REVIEW B 76, 092105 (2007)

# High-pressure-high-temperature phase relations of MgGeO₃: First-principles calculations

Taku Tsuchiya* and Jun Tsuchiya†

Geodynamics Research Center, Ehime University, 2-5 Bunkyo-cho, Matsuyama 790-8577, Japan

(Received 30 May 2007; revised manuscript received 9 August 2007; published 18 September 2007)

The high-pressure-high-temperature behavior of MgGeO₃ has been investigated by first-principles computations. It is found that at 300 K, the first transformation from ilmenite to orthorhombic perovskite at 24-38 GPa is followed by the second one to the CaIrO₃ structure at 51-56 GPa. Quasiharmonic free energy calculations suggest that the first transformation has a negative Clapeyron slope (-9.3 MPa/K at 1000 K), whereas the second one has a less temperature-sensitive positive Clapeyron slope (+7.8 MPa/K at 1000 K). It is also confirmed that the LiNbO₃ structure does not have its own stability $P,T$ conditions. Pressure dependence of the Goldschmidt tolerance factor indicates a clear correlation between distortion of the perovskite structure and relative stability of perovskite and postperovskite structures.

High-pressure phase relations of MgGeO₃ have been extensively investigated both experimentally and theoretically in terms of its particular role as a low-pressure analog of major Earth forming silicate minerals.¹⁻⁸ Orthorhombic perovskite polymorph ($Pbnm$ symmetry), which transformed from ilmenite or LiNbO₃ phase, was observed at ~13 GPa and room temperature,⁴ and later, calorimetric measurements⁵ demonstrated the ilmenite-perovskite phase boundary locating at much higher pressures of ~23 GPa and ~1900 K with a negative Clapeyron slope of -8.2 MPa/K, which is significantly smaller than the slope reported for MgSiO₃ (-2.9 MPa/K).⁹ More recently, subsequent phase transition from $Pbnm$ perovskite to CaIrO₃-type postperovskite ($Cmcm$ symmetry) was found at ~63 GPa and ~1800 K.⁶ The Clapeyron slope of this second transition, on the other hand, has not yet been constrained precisely due to experimental difficulty, though it was roughly estimated to be +8 MPa/K,⁶ fairly comparable to MgSiO₃ (+7.5 MPa/K).¹⁰ First-principles calculations on the high-pressure behavior of MgGeO₃ have also been conducted very recently,⁷,⁸ but they are limited at the static condition. In this study, using quasiharmonic free energy calculations coupled with first-principles vibrational densities of states,¹⁰,¹¹ we investigate the high-$P,T$ phase boundaries of MgGeO₃ together with finite temperature thermodynamics and compression mechanisms of these high-pressure polymorphs. The four crystal structures relevant to these MgGeO₃ polymorphs are illustrated in Fig. 1.

We have investigated high-$P,T$ physical properties of several silicate and oxide minerals theoretically¹⁰,¹¹ based on the density functional theory¹²,¹³ (DFT) and the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT).¹⁴ We have applied the same techniques to the high-pressure MgGeO₃ polymorphs. Our computations have been performed within the local density approximation¹⁵,¹⁶ (LDA) and the generalized gradient approximation (GGA).¹⁷ Ultrasoft pseudopotentials constructed in the electronic configurations¹⁸ which are the same as those used in the previous work⁸ have been used to represent the ionic core potentials. The plane-wave cutoff was chosen as 50 Ry and the Brillouin zone was sampled on 2 ×2×2 (two points), 4×4×2 (four points), and 4×4×2 (six points) Monkhorst-Pack grid¹⁹ for ilmenite (LiNbO₃), $Pbnm$ perovskite, and $Cmcm$ postperovskite structures, respectively. Using these conditions, total energies converged within 0.01 eV/atom, and also Pulay force became negligibly small. The effects of using larger cutoff and $\mathbf{k}$ points on the calculated properties were found insignificant. Phonon calculations were performed based on DFPT. The Brillouin-zone sampling for phonon states was computed on 14, 18, and 14 $\mathbf{q}$ points for ilmenite (LiNbO₃), perovskite, and postperovskite, respectively. The force constant matrices on the finer $\mathbf{q}$-point grids were interpolated by applying the acoustic sum rule and the vibrational densities of states (VDOS) were obtained by integrating phonon dispersions.

VDOS of ilmenite, perovskite, and postperovskite phases at 0 and 100 GPa are shown in Fig. 2. Eigenvector analyses at 0 GPa indicate that the states of the three phases around 650-800 and 250-400 cm⁻¹ are attributed to the GeO₆ octahedral deformation and the Mg atom displacement, respectively, while the states of perovskite around 100-250 cm⁻¹ are explained by the octahedral rotation. As in our previous works, $^{10,11}$ the finite temperature Helmholtz free energy $[F(V,T)]$ of a crystal is computed as functions of $V$ and $T$ within the quasiharmonic approximation (QHA). Then Gibbs free energy converted based on the thermodynamic relation $G(P,T)$=$F(V,T)$+$P(V,T)V$ is used to determine the high-temperature phase boundary. Wide band gaps of 4–8 eV were found for the structures. Electronic contribution to the entropy, which should be negligible in such well-insulating systems even at 3000 K, was therefore not included to calculate free energies. Free energy differences relative to perovskite's values $[\Delta G(P,T)$
=$G_{X}(P,T)$-$G_{Pv}(P,T)$, $x$=ilmenite, $LiNbO_3$ or postperovskite] calculated within LDA are plotted in Fig. 3(a) at several temperatures up to 3000 K as a function of pressure. The transition pressure $(P_{t})$ from ilmenite to perovskite is found to be 24 GPa at 300 K and decreases with increasing temperature. This $P_{t}$ at 300 K is very consistent with the experimental findings, suggesting perovskite's stability over 23 GPa. $^{4}$ In contrast, $P_{t}$ from perovskite to postperovskite is found to be 51 GPa and increases with increasing temperature. At 2000 K, $P_{t}$ reaches 65 GPa, which is also quite comparable to the experimental value of 63 GPa at 1800 K. $^{6}$ Figure 3(a) also shows $G$ of $LiNbO_3$ always larger than that of ilmenite, indicating no thermodynamic stability field of this structure. The large lattice energy of $LiNbO_3$ compared to ilmenite is a primary reason for this, even though it has a volume slightly smaller than ilmenite (Fig. 1). At 300 K, the metastable $LiNbO_3$-perovskite boundary is found at 5 GPa. This is supportive of the earlier experimental interpretation that the $LiNbO_3$ structure metastably appeared at 13 GPa during decompression of perovskite. $^{4}$

The theoretical high-$P,T$ phase diagram of $MgGeO_3$ is drawn in Fig. 3(b). The ilmenite-perovskite boundary calculated using LDA is found to be $\sim$10 GPa lower than the recently reported experimental boundary. $^{5}$ This underestimation is thought to be due to a general tendency of LDA. $^{10,11,20}$ In contrast, GGA exchange and correlation functional gives higher $P_{t}$'s of 38 GPa (ilmenite-perovskite) and 56 GPa (perovskite-postperovskite) at static condition. They are roughly 10 GPa higher than the LDA values as also usually seen, $^{10,11,20}$ and accordingly, the ilemnite-perovskite boundary shifts to conditions close to the experimental boundary (23 GPa and 1900 K). $^{5}$ Irrespective of LDA or GGA, the calculated ilmenite-perovskite transition pressures have negatively large temperature dependence. This slope is very consistent with the experimental value$^5$ ($-8.2$ MPa/K) and distinctly steeper compared to the boundary$^9$ of MgSiO$_3$ ($-2.9$ MPa/K) as pointed previously.$^5$ On the other hand, the perovskite-postperovskite transition pressure has positive temperature dependence. This slope is again quite similar to an experimental value$^6$ ($+8$ MPa/K) and also almost the same as the slope of the perovskite-postperovskite boundary in MgSiO$_3$ ($+7.5$ MPa/K).$^{10}$ We also investigated the phase evolution of MgGeO$_3$ at further pressures and found that the oxide mixture of pyrite (or cotunnite)-type GeO$_2$+NaCl (B1)-type MgO is energetically unfavored even at 300 GPa as well as a hexagonal phase, a possible candidate of a higher-pressure phase.$^{21}$ The decomposition of postperovskite, which is reported in MgSiO$_3$ and CaSnO$_3$,$^{21,22}$ is expected to occur at much higher pressures in MgGeO$_3$.

Compression curves of MgGeO$_3$ polymorphs calculated at 300 K within LDA are plotted in Fig. 4(a), and some thermodynamic parameters are summarized in Table I. The calculated compression curves are in good agreement with experimental results for all three stable phases. The static volume shown by a thin line$^7$ is a few percent smaller due to lack of the thermal effect including zero-point energy. In contrast, GGA produces volumes 2.5%–3% larger than LDA, and consequently, LDA reproduces experimental room-$T$ compression curves better than GGA. We found the volume reductions associated with the ilmenite-perovskite and perovskite-postperovskite transitions of $-1.17$ cm$^3$/mol ($-4.5\%$) and $-0.42$ cm$^3$/mol ($-1.8\%$), respectively. The Mg coordination increases from sixfold to eightfold across the first transition, producing larger volume reduction, while the small volume decrease across the second transition results primarily from the change in the GeO$_6$ octahedra connectivity from corner sharing to edge sharing with no change in the cation coordination. These $\Delta V$ were nearly independent of temperature in both transitions. $\Delta V$ in the second transition is comparable to that in MgSiO$_3$ ($-1.5\%$),$^{10}$ though $\Delta V$ in the first transition is distinctly smaller than in MgSiO$_3$ ($-5.4\%$).$^{23}$ The Clausius-Clapeyron equation $dP_t/dT$ $=\Delta S/\Delta V$ tells the slope of the phase boundary, determined based on the contrasts of phonon entropy ($\Delta S$) and volume ($\Delta V$) of two phases. Though the maximum phonon frequency is higher in perovskite than in ilmenite (Fig. 2), $\Delta S$ across the ilmenite-perovskite transition is found positive because the position of the *center of mass* of VDOS is higher in ilmenite than in perovskite as discussed.$^{23}$ This positive $\Delta S$ is also found to depend largely on temperature as $+7.2$, $+10.4$, and $+12.2$ J/mol K at 300, 1000, and 2000 K, respectively. These $T$-sensitive $\Delta S$ and $T$-insensitive $\Delta V$ produce the large temperature dependence of the negative Clapeyron slope of the ilmenite-perovskite boundary [Fig. 3(b)]. At 1000 K, $\Delta V$=$-1.16$ cm$^3$/mol leads to $dP_t/dT$ $=-9.0$ MPa/K. On the other hand, $\Delta S$ across the perovskite-postperovskite transition is $-2.9$ J/mol K at 1000 K, whose temperature dependence is much smaller compared to the case of the other transition. This leads to $dP_t/dT$ $=+7.2$ MPa/K at 1000 K. These slopes determined from the Clausius-Clapeyron equation are internally consistent with the slopes estimated from the Gibbs free energy balance (Fig. 3).

In contrast, it remains almost unchanged across the perovskite-postperovskite transition as suggested from the parallel compression curves [Fig. 4(a)]. This behavior is very similar to that of MgSiO$_3$. Calculated pressure dependencies of the mean interatomic distances are shown in Fig. 4(b). Across the ilmenite-perovskite transition, average Ge-O distance $\langle$Ge-O$\rangle$ decreases, whereas $\langle$Mg-O$\rangle$ increases markedly by 9% because of the increase in Mg coordination number from sixfold to eightfold (Fig. 1). The perovskite-postperovskite transition involves small changes in bond distances because of no change in cation coordination. The $t$ diagram plot- ted using the mean bond distances of $MgGeO_{3}$ perovskite and postperovskite calculated within LDA is shown in Fig. 5 together with the data of $MgSiO_{3}$ and $CaSnO_{3}$ compounds we previously reported. $^{10,21}$ We found $t$ of both perovskite and postperovskite similarly decreasing with increasing pres- sure, indicating that the cell distortions are enhanced by ap- plying pressure. Also perovskites' $t$'s are larger than postper ovskites' $t$'s in all compounds, suggesting that the structural stability of the postperovskite phase relates to the distortion of the perovskite structure. This is consistent with the geo- metrical relationship between the perovskite and postperovs- kite structures. $^{10}$ The $t$ factors of all three compounds appear to suggest a general boundary of perovskite and postperovs- kite stabilities at $t\sim0.83$, i.e., the perovskite structure is stable when $t>0.83$, while the postperovskite structure is stable when $t<0.83$. The condition $t\sim0.83$ can be thought to be the lower (upper) bound for acceptable distortion in the perovskite (postperovskite) stability field.

Within the framework of DFT+DFPT+QHA, high-$P,T$ phase equilibria of $MgGeO_{3}$ with ilmenite $(LiNbO_{3})$, perov- skite, and postperovskite polymorphs were successfully pre- dicted. The room-$T$ transition pressures calculated within LDA are 24 GPa for ilmenite-perovskite transition and51 GPa for perovskite-postperovskite transition, respectively, which agree well with the experimental findings. The ilmenite-perovskite boundary is found to have a negative Clapeyron slope, which largely depends on temperature, while the perovskite-postperovskite phase boundary has a positive and nearly $T$-independent Clapeyron slope(+7.8 MPa/K). These transitions involve volume reductions of $-4.5\%$ (first) and $-1.8\%$ (second one). Free energy calcu lations also show that the $LiNbO_{3}$ phase is thermodynami cally metastable throughout the entire $P$-$T$ field and that the postperovskite phase is more stable than the oxide mixture even at 200 GPa. The predicted high-$P,T$ phase relations of $MgGeO_{3}$ are quite similar to those reported for $MgSiO_{3}$. The Goldschmidt $t$ factor indicates a clear and maybe general correlation between the distortion of the perovskite lattice and the relative stability of the postperovskite phase.
