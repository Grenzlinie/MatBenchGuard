# Structural Diversity and Giant Birefringence in Cyanates BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, I, and CNO) Containing Linear $\pi$-Conjugated Units: A Combined Experimental and Theoretical Study

## Supporting Information Placeholder

**ABSTRACT:** Four novel cyanates BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, I, and CNO) containing linear $\pi$-conjugated anions, were obtained by solid-state metathesis reactions for the first time. BaCNOCl crystallizes in a monoclinic space group $P2_1/m$ with a = 5.5728(6) Å, b = 4.4917(4) Å, c = 7.8518(9) Å, $\beta$=102.319(11)°, Z = 2, whereas the remaining three compounds belong to the same orthorhombic space group $Pnma$ with a = 8.227-8.713 Å, b = 4.290-4.551 Å, c = 10.986-11.803 Å, Z = 4. The crystal structures of these four compounds are divergent, exhibiting rich structural diversity ranging from simple to complex when X changes from Cl to Br and I, and then to CNO. Their thermal, vibrational, and optical properties were characterized in details. Besides, the first principles calculations reveal that all four compounds show giant optical anisotropy of $\Delta$n>0.20 at 800 nm owing to the superiority arrangement of linear (CNO)$^-$ $\pi$-conjugated groups. Successful synthesis of these cyanates not only further explains the influence of microscopic ions on the structures, but also enriches the structure chemistry of cyanates and provides enlightenment for the study of birefringence in CNO-based compounds.

## INTRODUCTION

Optical anisotropy, also named as birefringence, is of great significance for research of optics.$^{1,2}$ Large birefringence is an essential and indispensable for an excellent birefringent crystal, which can be critically important for optical communication and laser industry including optical polarizers, wave plates and beam splitters.$^{3}$ Generally speaking, planar $\pi$-conjugated anionic groups, such as $(BO_3)^{3-},^{4}$ $(NO_3)^{-},^{5}$ $(CN_2)^{2-},^{6}$ $(CO_3)^{2-},^{7}$ $(B_3O_6)^{3-},^{8}$ and $(C_3N_3O_3)^{3-},^{9}$ were usually selected as ideal motifs to design birefringent or nonlinear optical crystals$^{10-13}$ due to the large anisotropic linear polarizability and high second-order susceptibility. For example, $CaCO_3$ containing $\pi_4^6$ configuration $(CO_3)^{2-}$ exhibits a large birefringence ($\Delta$n = 0.171@630 nm),$^{14}$ which is a commercial birefringent crystal in visible and near-IR spectral region for a long time. Borates containing six-membered rings (6-MRs) $(B_3O_6)^{3-}$ units, including $\alpha$-BBO,$^{8}$ $Ba_2M(B_3O_6)_2$ (M = Mg and Ca),$^{3,15}$ $Ba_2Na_3(B_3O_6)_2F,^{16,17}$ are also reported as practical or promising birefringent crystals in vacuum-ultraviolet regions. More recently, the 6-MR $(C_3N_3O_3)^{3-}$ group, isoelectric to the $(B_3O_6)^{3-}$ unit, holds a shorter intra-ring bond lengths and more uniform electron distribution, that is proposed as a golden gene to hunt for birefringent or nonlinear optical materials.$^{9,18}$ Since the reported compounds $M_3(C_3N_3O_3)_2$ (M = Ca, Sr) with great nonlinear optical effects in 2013,$^{19,20}$ a large number of cyanurates or hydroisocyanurates have been synthesized, in which noncentrosymmetric $K_6Cd_3(C_3N_3O_3)_4$ was first reported by Xia,$^{21}$ *et al* and it was recently found to be an intrinsic zero thermal expansion material with ultralow thermal expansion coefficient.$^{22}$ Meanwhile, $Ba_2M(C_3N_3O_3)_2$ (M = Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba,and Pb),$^{23-25}$ $A_2B(H_2C_3N_3O_3)_4{\cdot}nH_2O$ (A = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs; B = Ba, Mg, Ca; n = 4, 10)$^{26,27}$ and $ALi(H_2C_3N_3O_3)_2{\cdot}2H_2O$ (A=Rb, Cs)$^{28}$ compounds are newly UV birefringent crystals due to their large optical anisotropy. Subsequently, $ALi(HC_3N_3O_3)_2{\cdot}2H_2O$ (A = K, Rb)$^{29-31}$ and $A_3Na(H_2C_3N_3O_3)_4{\cdot}3H_2O$ (A = Cs, Rb)$^{32,31}$ were suggested as promising solar-blind NLO crystals.

In addition, cyanates with linear $\pi$-conjugated (CNO)$^-$ group were rarely investigated in comparison with triangles $(BO_3)^{3-}$, $(CO_3)^{2-}$, and 6-MRs $(B_3O_6)^{3-}$ and $(C_3N_3O_3)^{3-}$ anions. Although, cyanurates containing $(C_3N_3O_3)^{3-}$ 6-MRs were usually obtained by cyclotrimerization of three linear (CNO)$^-$ units.$^{33}$ At present, as far as we know there is no comprehensive research about CNO-based compound in birefringence and nonlinearity except for NaCNO with large birefringence ($\Delta$n $\approx$ 0.35) studied by theoretical calculation.$^{6}$ Therefore, the aim to search for CNO-based compounds targeting superior photoelectric performance is imminent.$^{34}$ As part of ongoing effort to synthesize and characterize of cyanates, we report here four cyanate compounds BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, I, CNO) for the first time. The structural evolution, thermal, vibrational, and optical properties were investigated in details. Besides, the first principles calculations were performed to clarify their electric properties and the relationship between structural motif and optical response.

## EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

### Solid-state syntheses.

The starting reagents $BaCl_2$(Alfa Aesar, 99.998%), $BaBr_2$ (Macklin, 99%) and $Bal_2$ (Strem Chemicals, Inc., 97%) and KCNO (Aladdin, 97%) were directly purchased and $Ba_3(C_3N_3O_3)_2$ is synthesized by mixing KCNO and $BaCl_2$. All the manipulations were performed in an Ar-filled glove box with $H_2O$ and $O_2$ contents less than 0.1 ppm. Polycrystalline samples of BaCNOCl and BaCNOBr were synthesized by

heating $BaX_2$ (X = Cl, Br) and KCNO to 700K in a molar of 1:1 using solid state reactions in fused tubes under a high vacuum of $10^{-3}$ Pa. For $Ba(CNO)_2$, $Ba_3(C_3N_3O_3)_2$ was directly put into a vacuum quartz tube ($10^{-3}$ Pa), heating to 800 K and holding 5 h, then pure powder was obtained. Unfortunately, we did not get the polycrystalline sample of BaCNOI.

### Single crystals growth.
**BaCNOCl and BaCNOBr.** KCNO and $BaCl_2/BaBr_2$ were mixed in a molar ratio of 1:1 in a fused-silica tube. The tube was heated to 875 K and held for 72 h, and then slowly cooled to room temperature at a rate of 3 K/h. Furthermore, small single crystals were obtained after the mixtures were washed by alcohol.

**$Ba(CNO)_2$ and BaCNOI.** Single crystals of $Ba(CNO)_2$ were grown by heating $Ba_3(C_3N_3O_3)_2$ in a fused-silica tube under a high vacuum of $10^{-3}$ Pa. The tube was heated to 870 K within 5 h and cooled to room temperature by switching off the furnace. Crystals of BaCNOI was grown from the mixture of $Ba_3(C_3N_3O_3)_2$ and $BaI_2$ in a molar ratio of 1:1 under the same temperature conditions as $Ba(CNO)_2$.

### Single-crystal structure determination.
Single-crystal X-ray diffraction data was collected at room temperature on a Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy diffractometer equipped with a HyPix-6000HE area detector using Mo Kα (PhotonJet-S Mo 50 W Microfocus) radiation source, with a frame time of 4 s, frame width of $0.5^o$, and a detector distance of 32 mm. Data extraction and integration were carried out with the use of the CrysAlisPro software. Structure solution and refinement were carried out with SHELXTL (version 6.12) program package.³⁵ Initial atomic positions were located by direct methods and refinements proceeded in a straightforward fashion. All atomic sites were confirmed to be fully occupied and exhibited reasonable displacement parameters. No additional symmetry was revealed by the ADDsym routine in the PLATON suite programs.³⁴

### Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD).
The powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) data for BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, CNO) were collected on a Bruker D8 Focus diffractometer equipped with Cu Kα radiation at room temperature in the $2\theta$ range of $10-70^o$.

### Thermal Analysis.
The differential scanning calorimeter (DSC) analysis was performed on the Labsys EVO TG/DTA/DSC $1600^oC$ (SETARAM) thermal analyzer. The sample was placed in a fused-silica tube and heated from room temperature to 700 °C at the rate of $15\ ^oC$/min.

### UV-VIS-NIR diffuse reflectance spectrum.
The spectra of BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, CNO) were measured from 200 nm to 2000 nm on a Cary 7000 UV-vis-NIR spectrophotometer equipped with a diffuse reflectance accessory. A compacted pellet of poly tetra fluoroethylene was used as a 100% reflectance standard.

### Infrared spectrum.
Infrared spectra were collected on a Varian Excalibur 3100 spectrometer in the wavelength range from $400\ cm^{-1}$ to $2500\ cm^{-1}$.

### Computational methods.
The density functional theory calculations of title compounds were done in the CASTEP package.³⁶ The total energy calculations including full structural optimizations were carried with the generalized gradient approximation with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE-sol) functional.³⁷ The optimized norm-conversing pseudopotentials³⁸ (Ba $4d^{10}6s^2$, C $2s^22p^2$, N $2s^22p^3$, O $2s^22p^4$, Cl $3s^23p^5$, Br $4s^24p^5$, I $5s^25p^5$) were used to simulate ion-electron interactions. A kinetic energy cutoff of 600 eV was chosen with Monkhorst-Pack k-point meshes³⁸ spanning less than $0.04/\mathring{A}$ in the first Brillouin zone. Moreover, the linear response method³⁹ was employed to obtain the phonon dispersion of crystal to evaluate its stability. For phonon calculations, the ultrafine dispersion separation of $0.03/\mathring{A}^3$ and high cutoff energy of 900 eV were adopted to make sure the good convergence.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Crystal structure. BaCNOCl crystallizes in the monoclinic space group $P2_1/m$ (No. 11) with unit cell parameters a = 5.5757(15) $\mathring{A}$, b = 4.4925(10) $\mathring{A}$, c = 7.850(2) $\mathring{A}$, $\beta=102.38\ (3)^o$, and Z = 2. It can be described as a 2D layered structure of $_{\infty}^2[BaCNO]^+$ composed of $[Ba_2N_2]^{2-}$ chains which are further connected by C and O in CNO units with Cl⁻ evenly distributed among the layers (**Figure 1a**). It is worth mentioning that the $[Ba_2N_2]^{2-}$ chains are very similar with the $[Bi_2O_2]^{2+}$ chains in Aurivillius-type compounds, which can be seen as a chemical co-substitution process from $[Bi_2O_2]^{2+}$ layers to $[Ba_2N_2]^{2-}$ layers with changing $Bi^{3+}$ and $O^{2-}$ ions to $Ba^{2+}$ and $N^{3-}$ ions, respectively (**Figure 1b**). The coordination of CNO is shown as **Figure 1c**, wherein the N atom is adjacent to three Ba atoms, and the O atom is adjacent to two Ba atoms. Ba atom is coordinated with four Cl atoms, three N and two O originated from CNO units with the bond lengths of Ba-O/N covering from 2.922~2.923 $\mathring{A}$ (**Figure 1d**).

![](./images/812695259816394753_2.jpg)

**Figure 1.** (a) The crystal structure of BaCNOCl; (b) $[Ba_2N_2]^{2-}$ chain; (c and d) the coordination environmentos of the CNO unit and Ba atom.

BaCNOBr has a similar chemical composition with BaCNOCl but crystallizes in orthorhombic space group *Pnma* with unit cell parameters a = 8.2268(5) $\mathring{A}$, b = 4.5519(3) $\mathring{A}$, c = 10.9861(8) $\mathring{A}$, and Z = 4. The structure of BaCNOBr features a 3D framework composed by $[Ba_2N_2]^{2-}$ chains which are further connected by C and O in CNO units with the Br-evenly incorporated into $[Ba_4(CNO)_4]^{4+}$ 8-membered channels (**Figure 2a**). The identical chain structure also exists in BaCNOBr as mentioned $[Ba_2N_2]^{2-}$ in BaCNOCl (**Figure 2b**). For CNO unit, the N atom is coordinated to three Ba atoms, and the O atom is coordinated to two Ba atoms whereas Ba atom is coordinated with four Br atoms, three N and two O originated from CNO unit with the bond lengths of Ba-O/N covering from 2.922 to 2.923 $\mathring{A}$ with the bond lengths of Ba-O/N from 2.8161 to 2.945 $\mathring{A}$ (**Figure 2c-d**).

![](./images/812695259816394753_3.jpg)

**Figure 2.** (a) The structure of BaCNOBr; (b) $[Ba_2N_2]^{2-}$ chain; (c,d) the coordination environment of the CNO unit and Ba atom.

BaCNOI crystallizes in orthorhombic space group Pnma with unit cell parameters a = 8.5754(5) Å, b = 4.7566(3) Å, c = 11.5553(7) Å, but exhibit a distinctive structure compared to BaCNOBr. It is composed by [Ba₂(CNO)₂]²⁺ chains which are further connected by N-Ba bonds from adjacent chains with I⁻ evenly distribution in the [Ba₄(CNO)₂O₂]²⁺ eight-membered rings (Figure 3a). However, [Ba₂(CNO)₂]²⁺ chains can also be seen as approximately equal to [Bi₂O₂]²⁺ chains in Aurivillius-type compounds, although the position of oxygen atoms in [Bi₂O₂]²⁺ is replaced by a linear CNO group rather than a single atom (Figure 3b). As shown in Figure 3c, the N and O atoms are coordinated to two Ba atoms whereas Ba atom is coordinated with five I atoms, two N and two O atoms from the CNO unit with the bond lengths of Ba-O/N covering from 2.799 to 2.825 Å (Figure 3d).

![](./images/812695259816394753_4.jpg)

Figure 3. (a) The structure of BaCNOI; (b) [Ba₂(CNO)₂]²⁺ chain; (c,d) the coordination environment of the CNO unit and Ba atom.

Ba(CNO)₂ crystallizes in orthorhombic space group of Pnma same to BaCNOBr and BaCNOI with unit cell parameters a = 8.7132(6) Å, b = 4.2901(3) Å, c = 11.8029(8) Å, but exhibits a completely different structure. The 3D network of Ba(CNO)₂ is formed by [Ba₃(CNO)₂O₂] chains which are further connected by C and O in CNO units or O-Ba bands from adjacent chains (Figure 4a). One [Ba₃(CNO)₂O₂] chain can be considered as a combination of one [Ba₂N₂]²⁻ chain and one [Ba₂(CNO)₂]²⁺ chain connected by sharing Ba atoms (Figure 4b). The coordination environment of CNO unit in Ba(CNO)₂ is same to that of BaCNOI, however, the Ba atom is coordinated with nine CNO units by a combination of four O atoms and five N atoms with the bond lengths of Ba-O/N covering from 2.8182 to 2.970 Å. The average bond length of Ba-O/N bond in Ba(CNO)₂ (2.875 Å) is less than BaCNOCl (2.9226 Å) and BaCNOBr (2.8856 Å) but slightly more than BaCNOI (2.809 Å) (Figure 4c-d).

![](./images/812695259816394753_5.jpg)

Figure 4. (a) The structure of Ba(CNO)₂; (b) [Ba₃(CNO)₂O₂] chain; (c,d) the coordination environment of the CNO unit and Ba atom

The powder X-ray diffraction data of BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, CNO) were collected at room temperature and the results are in good agreement with the calculated patterns (Figure S1-S3). The vibration modes of the CNO⁻ anions were detected by the infrared spectrum (IR) spectra. As displayed in Figure S4, the vibrational peaks among 600-650 cm⁻¹, 1250-1300 cm⁻¹ and 2200-2250 cm⁻¹, were assigned to bending mode, O/N opposite stretching mode and O/N coincident stretching mode, respectively.

According to UV-VIS-NIR diffuse reflectance spectra, the ultraviolet absorption cut-off edges of BaCNOCl, BaCNOBr, and Ba(CNO)₂ are obtained as 391 nm, 320 nm and 247 nm (Figure 5), and the corresponding bandgaps are 3.17 eV, 3.88 eV, 5.02 eV, respectively. BaCNOCl and BaCNOBr were selected to carry out thermal analyses. As shown in the Figure S5, the DSC curves of BaCNOCl show one sharp endothermic peak at about 605 °C in the heating process whereas two small exothermic peaks at about 576 °C and 625 °C in the cooling process, which tentatively proves that BaCNOCl melts incongruently. As shown in Figure S6, the PXRD of BaCNOCl after DSC analysis was further proved that BaCNOCl melts incongruently. However, BaCNOBr has been tested to be congruent melting with the melting point and recrystallization temperature of BaCNOBr were 569 °C and 511 °C, respectively. The PXRD of BaCNOBr after DSC analysis was detected and the result proved the property of congruent melting (Figure S7).

![](./images/812695259816394753_6.jpg)

Figure 5. (a) UV-VIS-NIR diffuse reflectance spectra of BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, CNO).

First-Principles Calculations.

Four title compounds with similar chemical stoichiometric ratio belong to extremely different crystal structures. Therefore, phonon vibrations calculations were adopted to check their structural stability. As well known, the lattice vibration properties are strongly related to the interatomic interaction intrinsically, in which the imaginary phonon eigenvalues suggest that the repulsive interatomic forces at given reciprocal lattice points and kinetically unstable crystal structure.³⁹ The eigenvalues for all vibrational modes are positive for Ba(CNO)₂, BaCNOCl, BaCNOBr and BaCNOI, thus confirming their structural stability theoretically (Figure 6). The phonon DOS maps of four compunds are plotted in Figure S8, in which heavier halogen atoms exhibits lower vibrational frequency.

![](./images/812695259816394753_7.jpg)

Figure 6. The phonon dispersion of (a) Ba(CNO)₂, (b) BaCNOCl, (c) BaCNOBr and (d) BaCNOI

The calculated band structure and density of states for three compounds are depicted in Figure S9 and Figure S10. All of three compounds are indirect semiconductors with the top valence band not matching with the bottom conduction band. In particular, the electronic dispersion in

conduction band of them is quite strong, indicating their small effective electron carrier mass. The upper valence band (-6 to -4 eV) is mainly composed of C 2s/2p, N 2s/2p and O 2s/2p orbitals, relating to linear sp hybridization configuration. However, the electronic states of three compounds at valence band maximum (VBM) region are divergent owing to large electronegativity difference between (CNO)⁻, Cl⁻, Br⁻ and I⁻. For Ba(CNO)₂, the VBM is occupied by N and O 2p orbitals, corresponding to nonbonding dangling electronic states (Figure 7a). For BaCNOCl, BaCNOBr and BaCNOI, the VBM region also contains Cl 3p, Br 4p and I 5p orbitals, respectively, besides N and O 2p orbitals (Figure 7c, 7e, 7g). In comparison, the bottom conduction bands are contributed from unoccupied Ba 5d orbitals as well as antibond orbitals between C and O/N atoms (Figure 7b, 7d, 7f, 7h).

![](./images/812695259816394753_8.jpg)

Figure 7. The highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMO) of (a, b) Ba(CNO)₂, (c, d) BaCNOCl, (e, f) BaCNOBr and (g, h) BaCNOI.

Moreover, the optical properties of title compounds were also studied based on theoretical calculations. As shown in Figure 8, the largest dielectric principle axis birefringence of Ba(CNO)₂, BaCNOCl, BaCNOBr, and (d) BaCNOI is 0.319, 0.342, 0.257, 0.217, respectively, which is comparable with other compounds containing linear anionic group (e.g., HgBr₂, Δn=0.238 at 1064 nm). Also, this value is also larger than that of some common birefringent crystals, such as CaCO₃ (0.16)¹⁴ and YVO₄ (0.26),⁴⁰ thus suggesting their possible applications in polarized devices. According to anionic group theory proposed by Prof. Chen,⁴¹ such a strong optical anisotropy mainly originates from linear (CNO)⁻ group, while the contribution of alkali-earth Ba²⁺ and halogen X⁻ anions is nearly ignorable. Consequently, the birefringence of BaCNOX gradually decreases from Cl⁻, Br⁻, to I-analogue, owing to increased halogen radius and reduced group spatial density.⁴⁰ The first-order susceptibility projected along C-O and C-N bond is much higher than that of perpendicular directions, which leads to larger refractive indices along a and c axis. Therefore, linear (CNO)⁻ group is also a good building block for designing new UV birefringent materials.

## CONCLUSION

In conclusion, a series of BaCNOX (X = Cl, Br, I, CNO) cyanate compounds with structures ranging from simple to complex have been successfully synthesized. Although these four new compounds have similar molecular formulas, their microstructures are different owing to containing the various anion groups. In this paper, we not only illustrated the process of the structural evolution of these four compounds, but also explored band gaps, chemical stability and vibration modes. Moreover, the first principles calculations indicated that Ba(CNO)₂ possesses large optical anisotropy attribute to the linear CNO units. As these cyanate compounds are successfully obtained, birefringent crystal materials have been expanded in cyanate system. If linear CNO units can be programmed into a superior arrangement, CNO-based compounds may be applied in both birefringence and nonlinearity in the future.
