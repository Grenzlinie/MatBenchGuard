# Crystal chemistry rationale and DFT investigations of novel hard tetragonal C6 built from tetrahedral C(sp3) lattice embedding allene-like sp2 linear tricarbon.

Samir MATAR¹

1. Lebanese German University

## Abstract
From crystal chemistry rationale novel tetragonal carbon allotrope: C6 featuring allene-like C(sp2) tricarbon unit >C=C=C<embedded in diamond-like structure C(sp3) lattice is proposed. Quantum mechanics DFT calculations onto ground state let identify C6 as cohesive with localized charge around tetrahedral C-sp3 and smeared charge density along linear allene-like tricarbon unit characteristic of C-sp2 hybridization. Novel C6 is found mechanically stable from elastic properties with large Vickers hardness: HV= 58 GPa. Dynamically stable with positive phonon acoustic and optic frequencies, C6 is characterized with particularly high optical phonon frequency =60 THz assigned to antisymmetric stretching mode (C---C-C), found to characterize I.R. active mode in C3H4 allene isolated molecule. The electronic band structure reveals semi-conducting electronic properties.

### Abstract

From crystal chemistry rationale novel tetragonal carbon allotrope: $C_6$ featuring allene-like $C(\text{sp}^2)$ tricarbon unit $>C=C=C<$ embedded in diamond-like structure $C(\text{sp}^3)$ lattice is proposed. Quantum mechanics DFT calculations onto ground state let identify $C_6$ as cohesive with localized charge around tetrahedral $C\text{-sp}^3$ and smeared charge density along linear allene-like tricarbon unit characteristic of $C\text{-sp}^2$ hybridization. Novel $C_6$ is found mechanically stable from elastic properties with large Vickers hardness: $H_V=$ 58 GPa. Dynamically stable with positive phonon acoustic and optic frequencies, $C_6$ is characterized with particularly high optical phonon frequency $\omega$=60 THz assigned to antisymmetric stretching mode (C----C-C), found to characterize I.R. active mode in $C_3H_4$ allene isolated molecule. The electronic band structure reveals semi-conducting electronic properties.

Keywords: DFT, crystal chemistry, phonons, elastic constants, electronic structure

### 1. Introduction and context

Identifying novel carbon allotropes is an active research field targeting materials with specific characteristics such as mechanical properties approaching diamond’s ultra-hardness by using modern materials research software as structure predicting USPEX based on evolutionary crystallography [1]. Also, in view of the large number of claimed stoichiometries and structures worldwide, a database: SACADA (SACADA 3D) categorizes known carbon allotropes labeling them with different topologies (ex. “dia” for diamond) helping researchers in their endeavor and avoiding claims of structurally related systems as novel allotropes [2]. Novel structures proposed are fully curated, recorded and made available through the Cambridge Structural Database (CSD) (cf. ref. [3] for presently investigated carbon allotrope $\text{C}_6$).

Backing structural investigations, quantum mechanical calculations based on energy criteria are needed to derive quantities describing accurately the targeted function, such as hardness from elastic properties. Complementary search of dynamic stability is needed as well as the fingerprint of the electronic behavior as metal, insulator, or semi-conductor. Most adopted theoretical framework is the quantum mechanics Density Functional Theory (DFT) devised in two papers: in 1964 for the theoretical framework by Hohenberg and Kohn [4] and followed in 1965 by Kohn and Sham [5] who established the Kohn-Sham equations for solving the wave equation practically in calculation codes built around the DFT (cf. next section).

Another initial search protocol lies in crystal chemistry rationale calling for structure engineering. Recently, body-centered tetragonal bct-$\text{C}_4$ (Figure 1a) was proposed by us in space group $\overline{I4}m2$ as the simplest three-dimensional (3D) carbon network; but it was evidenced as featuring another representation of diamond. Nevertheless bct-$\text{C}_4$ was conceived to serve as seed starting template to building other original carbon allotropes [6]. The removal of the body center carbon as schematized with the red arrow leads to simple tetragonal structure with eight tetrahedra occupying the corners of the structure. The space group symmetry is lowered and becomes identified as $P\overline{4}m2$. The usefulness of resulting $\text{C}_3$ is to serve as template structure with the empty space left by central carbon removal, leaving room for interstitials. Beside bct-$\text{C}_4$ recovered with one tetrahedral $\text{sp}^3$, i.e., C(tet.), two, and three carbon interstitials enter as trigonal ($\text{sp}^2$) C(trig.) atoms modifying the tetrahedral diamond-like edifice significantly and bringing new structure-properties relationships. Such

investigations constitute the aim of this paper through the study of the electronic, mechanical, and dynamic properties.

Specifically, while the insertion of two trigonal carbons leading to $C_5$ was recently studied [7] (Fig. 1b) with the outcome of stable ultrahard carbon allotrope, we focus our present work on the insertion of three carbon atoms giving $C_6$ stoichiometry, i.e., 3 C(tet.) and 3 C(trig.) as schematized in Fig. 1c with colored spheres, i.e., red and yellow for the two types of C(trig.). The particularity of the structure is the highlighting of linear C=C=C connecting the two layers of tetrahedra along $c$- vertical direction. Remarkably, >C=C=C< configuration resembles allene or propadiene molecule when 4 hydrogen atoms are attached to terminal carbons in the molecule (Fig. 1d).

At this point, we highlight that linear tricarbon motifs exist in the rhombohedral structure of refractory abrasive $B_{12}C_3$ [8], and in tricarbon molecule that was proposed in the solid state in two cohesive structures from DFT investigations [9].

## 2. Computational framework

The search for the ground state structures through geometry optimizations onto the ground state with minimal energies was performed using DFT-based plane-wave Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [10,11]. For the carbon atomic potential including valence states, the projector augmented wave (PAW) method was applied [11,12]. Treating at the same level the exchange X and the correlation C, the exchange-correlation (XC) effects were considered using a generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [13]. The relaxation of the atoms onto ground state geometry was done applying a conjugate-gradient algorithm [14]. Blöchl tetrahedron method [15] with corrections according to Methfessel-Paxton scheme [16] was applied for geometry optimization and energy calculations. A special $k$-point sampling [17] was applied for approximating the reciprocal space Brillouin-zone (BZ) integrals. For better reliability, the optimization of the structural parameters was carried out along with successive self-consistent cycles with increasing mesh until the forces on atoms were less than $0.02$ eV/Å and the stress components below $0.003$ eV/Å$^3$. Besides the elastic constants calculated to infer the mechanical stabilities and hardness, further calculations of phonon dispersion curves were also carried out to verify the dynamic stability of the new carbon allotropes. In the present work, the phonon modes were computed considering the harmonic approximation via finite displacements of the atoms around their equilibrium positions to obtain the forces from the

summation over the different configurations. The phonon dispersion curves along the direction of the Brillouin zone are subsequently obtained using "Phonopy" interface code based on Python language [18]. The structure representations were obtained thanks to VESTA graphical program [19].

## 3. Results and discussion

Template tet-$C_3$ and C-inserted system resulting into $C_6$ were submitted to unconstrained geometry optimization down to the ground state energies. The lattice parameters and energies are given in Table 1. The data obtained earlier for $C_4$ and $C_5$ are equally shown for the sake of comparison. The tetragonal space group for $C_3$, $C_5$ and $C_6$ is $P\overline{4}m2$ , in as far as the body center symmetry of tet-$C_4$ is absent. While there are two trigonal carbon atoms C(trig.) belonging to the same Wyckoff position in $C_5$, $C_6$ is obtained by an additional body center carbon label labeled C1(trig.) -red sphere in Fig. 1c- and the other two trigonal carbon atoms - as in $C_5$- are labeled C2(trig.), shown as yellow spheres in Fig. 1c. Alike $C_5$, $C_6$ is a mixed hybridization $sp^2$-$sp^3$ system. Regarding interatomic distances, d($C_{tet.}$-$C_{tet.}$) increases along the series: from 1.50 Å in $C_3$ up to 1.59 Å in $C_6$ that remain within range diamond's: d($C_{tet.}$-$C_{tet.}$) = 1.54 Å. $C_6$ shows the smallest d($C_{trig}$-$C_{trig}$) =1.30 Å that is also observed in allene molecule, thus providing a signature of C=C double bond. Reporting on energy, the significant quantity is the atom averaged cohesive energy obtained after deducting the atomic C energy. $C_3$ is expectedly least cohesive with $E_{coh./at.}$= -0.59 eV from the structure perturbation due to the removal of central carbon, and $C_4$ is most cohesive with $E_{coh./at.}$= -2.49 eV, a magnitude close to diamond's. $C_5$ and $C_6$ are cohesive with in-between values.

Although $E_{coh./at.}$=($C_6$)= -1.93 eV is smaller than $E_{coh./at.}$=($C_5$)= -2.05 eV, it remains higher that the calculated magnitudes obtained for $B_{12}C_3$ ($E_{coh./at}$ =-1.66eV) or $C_3$ ($E_{coh./at}$ = -1.55 eV), cited above. Consequently, it can be suggested that embedding tricarbon within a tetrahedral network (here $C_3$) is a major stabilizing factor, better than the insertion of C–C–C within a large cavity as rh-$B_{12}$ ($\alpha$-Boron) or as a standalone structure directly connecting C2–C1–C2 through terminal C2 carbon atoms [9].

### 3.1 Charge density projections.

Further illustration of the different types of hybridizations is obtained from the projections of the charge densities around and between atoms. Figure 2 presents the projections shown with yellow volumes. Upon crossing a crystal plane, charge density slices with red color are shown indicating strong charge localizations. In tet-$C_4$ (Fig. 2a) the $\text{sp}^3$-type hybridization is best described at central carbon with the yellow volumes taking the shape of a tetrahedron. Then tet-$C_4$ is a perfectly covalent chemical system alike diamond.

Upon removal of central carbon producing tet-$C_3$ with only C(tet) -cf. Table 1-, the charge density is modified, particularly for the carbon atoms pointing towards the empty space as shown by the larger red area. Such behavior resembling dangling bonds is in line with the low cohesive $C_3$, allows expecting such nonbonding electron density to connect with interstitials. Indeed, the larger changes observed in $C_6$ (Fig. 2c) are exhibited with the charge density (dangling bonds in $C_3$) skewed towards tricarbon C(trig)-like with fat yellow charge density segments illustrating the C=C double bonds as in allene molecule. Therefore, we are presented with a decrease of the covalence from $C_4$ to $C_6$ brought by the introduction of trigonal carbon.

### 3.2 Mechanical properties from the elastic constants

The investigation of the mechanical properties was based on the calculations of the elastic properties determined by performing finite distortions of the lattice and deriving the elastic constants from the strain-stress relationship. Actual compounds are polycrystalline and on a large scale, they can be considered as statistically isotropic, fully described mechanically by the bulk ($B$) and the shear ($G$) moduli obtained by averaging the single-crystal elastic constants. For the purpose, we used Voigt's [20] based on a uniform strain.

The calculated sets of elastic constants $C_{ij}$ are given in Table 2 reporting the values formerly obtained for $C_4$ [6] and of $C_5$ [7] for the sake of comparison. $C_4$ (diamond-like) $C_{ij}$ are largest while smallest magnitudes are obtained for $C_3$ which was produced by removing $C_4$'s central carbon, thus weakening the structure characterized with the smallest cohesive energy as discussed above. Compared to $C_4$, $C_5$ and $C_6$ $C_{ij}$ are smaller, except for $C_{33}$ describing the behavior along trigonal carbon pair C=C in C5 and C=C=C, respectively. Also, $C_{ij}$ (i=j and i≠j) values are positive and their combinations obey rules pertaining to the mechanical

stability: $\mathrm{C_{ii}}$ (i =1, 3, 4, 6) > 0; $\mathrm{C_{11}} > \mathrm{C_{12}}$, $\mathrm{C_{11}+C_{33}-2C_{13}} > 0$; and $\mathrm{2C_{11}+C_{33}+2C_{12}+4C_{13}} > 0$.

The equations providing the bulk $B_V$ and shear $G_V$ moduli are as follows for the tetragonal system [21]:

$$B_{Voigt}^{tetr.} = 1/9\ (2\mathrm{C_{11}} + \mathrm{C_{33}} + 2\mathrm{C_{12}} + 4\mathrm{C_{13}});$$

$$G_{Voigt}^{tetr.} = 1/15\ (2\mathrm{C_{11}} + \mathrm{C_{12}} + 2\ \mathrm{C_{33}} - 2\mathrm{C_{13}} + 6\mathrm{C_{44}} + 3\mathrm{C_{66}}).$$

$\mathrm{C_4}$ has the largest $B_V$ and $G_V$, close to the accepted values for diamond $B_V$ =445 GPa and $G_V$
= 550 GPa. Smaller corresponding $B_V$ and $G_V$ magnitudes are found for $\mathrm{C_5}$ and $\mathrm{C_6}$ with a larger value for the former. Both magnitudes remain high versus $\mathrm{C_3}$ which shows much smaller $B_V$ and $G_V$ magnitudes. From the calculated $B_V$ and $G_V$ we can predict the Vickers hardness $\mathrm{H_V}$, evaluated with the model of Chen et al. based on the bulk and shear moduli obtained from the elastic constants [21]. $\mathrm{H_V}$ is formulated as follows:

$$\mathrm{H_V} = 0.92\ (\mathrm{G/V})^{1.137}\ \mathrm{G}^{0.708}.$$

The numerical values are given in the last column of Table 1. $\mathrm{H_V(C_4)} = 97$ GPa is a magnitude in agreement with the admitted values of diamond [22] and $\mathrm{H_V(C_5)} = 81$ GPa let consider it as ultra-hard [7]. Presently investigated $\mathrm{C_6}$ is found characterized with a smaller albeit large hardness. Finally, with $\mathrm{H_V}$= 15 GPa, $\mathrm{C_3}$ is described as a soft material.

### 3.3 Dynamic properties from phonons.

Another criterion of stability is obtained from the phonons defined as quanta of vibrations; their energy is quantized through the Planck constant 'h' used in its reduced form $\hbar$ ($\hbar = \mathrm{h/2\pi}$) giving with the wave number $\omega$ the phonons energy: $\mathrm{E} = \hbar\omega$.

Besides $\mathrm{C_3}$ the phonon band structure of $\mathrm{C_4}$ [6] and $\mathrm{C_6}$ are shown in Fig. 3.

Along the horizontal direction, the bands run along the main lines of the tetragonal Brillouin zone (reciprocal k- space). The vertical direction shows the frequencies given in units of terahertz (THz). Since no negative frequency magnitudes are observed in all three panels all structures are considered as dynamically stable. There are 3N-3 optical modes at higher energy than three acoustic modes which start from zero energy ($\omega = 0$) at the $\Gamma$ point, center of the Brillouin Zone, up to a few Terahertz. The 3 acoustic modes correspond to the lattice rigid translation modes of the crystal (two transverse and one longitudinal). The remaining bands correspond to the acoustic modes and culminating at $\omega \sim 40$ THz in $\mathrm{C_4}$, a magnitude

observed for diamond by Raman spectroscopy [24], and only up to 34 in less stable C₃ (cf. Table 1).

Oppositely to C₄, C₆ exhibits similar phonon spectrum as C₃ especially for the high optical magnitude of ~35 THz. The two higher magnitude bands correspond to the C-C-C stretching: at ω ~ 40 THz for symmetric stretching and at ω ~ 60 THz for antisymmetric stretching (cf. movie added as supplementary material). Further check of these observations was carried out with quantum molecular chemistry calculations of C₃H₄ allene molecule (cf. Fig. 1d) within DFT. Focusing on the stretching modes, only the antisymmetric stretching mode was observed in C₃H₄ I.R. spectrum at ν=1931 cm⁻¹ as shown in Fig. 3d featuring the most intensive line while being inactive in Raman. With 1 THz = 33.356 cm⁻¹, we calculate the corresponding value in THz: ν= 58 THz, close to ω = 60 THz in crystalline solid C₆. The results showing close magnitudes between the free molecule and the embedded >C=C=C< on one hand and the isolation of the phonon lines at ω ~ 40 and at ω ~ 60 THz on the other hand, further validate the assignment of allene-like label to tricarbon unit as embedded in a tetrahedral C(sp³) structure.

### 3.4 Electronic band structures and density of states

Using the crystal parameters in Table 1, the electronic bands structures shown in Figure 4 were obtained using the all-electrons DFT-based augmented spherical method (ASW) [20] and the same gradient GGA XC functional [13]. The bands develop along the main directions of the primitive tetragonal Brillouin zone. For diamond-like insulating C₄ (Fig. 4a), the energy level along the vertical line is with respect to the top of the valence band (VB), Eᵥ. As a specific character of diamond, the band gap of C₄ is indirect along k_z between Γ_VB and Z_CB with a magnitude close to 5 eV. Oppositely, C₃ (Fig. 4b) is metallic-like with bands crossing the Fermi level E_F. Novel C₆ (Fig. 4c) is identified as small gap semiconductor with the top of the valence band dominated with delocalized π electrons.

## 4 Conclusions

With novel tetragonal C₆ we are presented with a semi-conducting original allotrope with a structure characteristic of original allene-like/tetrahedral-like mixed sp²/sp³ carbon hybridizations. Mechanically stable, C₆ is considered as hard anisotropic material with phonon band structure featuring allene-like tricarbon (sp²) embedded in tetrahedral lattice of

$C(\text{sp}^3)$. The highest frequency at $\omega \sim 60$ THz assigned to antisymmetric C-C-C stretching mode is concomitant with I.R. similar mode in allene molecule $\text{C}_3\text{H}_4$.

Such mixed $\text{sp}^2/\text{sp}^3$ carbon electronic configurations identified in nanodiamonds arises growing interest in different fields of materials investigations as electrochemical ones recently exposed by Zhai et al. [25].
