## Abstract

The present work reports a detail density functional investigation on the structure, vibrational, electronic and thermoelectric properties of bilayered CaS and CaSe and their hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer, based on our recently developed hexagonal monolayers CaS and CaSe as promising thermoelectric materials from a series of calcium chalcogenides $CaX$ ($X$=O, S, Se and Te). The minimized structures of the considered bilayers are assessed for the dynamical stability with phonon dispersion analysis. In order to look into the electronic properties of stable compounds, we have generated electronic band structure and projected density of states (PDOS) through first principle methods. To study the thermoelectric properties, we have utilized the semi-classical Boltzmann transport equations (BTE) to calculate electrical and thermal conductivities, Seebeck coefficient and figure of merit ($ZT$). Present study reveals hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer as the best promising thermoelectric material for a wide range of temperature toward future applications in thermopower generation devices.

Keywords: Calcium chalcogenides, Density functional theory, Band structure, Density of states, Thermoelectrics

### Introduction

The technological development has taken human life in such a state that one can't even imagine to survive without the aid of electricity today [1-3]. The huge consumption of energy leads to an uninterrupted depletion of existing energy resources as well as creates major threats towards environmental climate, due to the large fraction of emission of hazardous gases worldwide. The requirement of energy is increasing everyday and expected to rise more than half in next 20 years which may lead a severe energy shortage to our society in future [4, 5]. Accordingly, to get rid of this serious situation alternative pathways came to the fore. Along this line, utilization of renewable energy resources, improving older technologies as well as to develop new technologies is taken into a serious note by the researchers. On the other hand, heat engines are utilized in various home appliances, automobiles, nuclear reactions, chemical process and industries, are typically provides efficiency of less than 45%, which indicates that about 10 TW of generated heat is get lost in the surrounding environment [6]. As a consequence, one need to develop suitable techniques to build up more efficient heat engines as well as for direct conversion of waste heat into useful energy resources. In this reference, the thermoelectric devices are come up with very well attention. The thermoelectric modules work on Seebeck as well as Peltier's phenomenon, where they utilize waste heat to produce electricity directly without moving any part of the device [7, 8].

There have been a number of attempts by the researchers for developing efficient thermoelectric materials from various mono- as well as their multi layered architectures, and also their relevant bulk configurations [9-12]. In our latest work [13], we have investigated a series of calcium chalcogenides (CaX, X= O, S, Se and Te) in the form of hexagonal honeycomb monolayer along with their bulk rock-salt phases, to obtain structural, vibrational, electronic and thermoelectric properties. In both the monolayer and rock-salt phases, CaX materials show semiconducting behavior with indirect energy gap ranging between 1.63 eV to 3.70 eV. Our previous study shows that all the CaX possess considerable thermoelectric behavior, in which monolayer hexagonal CaS and CaSe are identified as the best performer with significant figure of merit (ZT) ranging between 0.9 to 1.41 for the temperature range of 250-600 K [13]. Guo et al. [14] performed thermoelectric study on the group IV-VI two-dimensional orthorhombic materials, viz. GeS, GeSe, SnS and SnSe. They have reported that those materials show better

thermoelectric performance due to smaller lattice thermal conductivity. Mohammad et al. [15] have reported a comparative study on the thermoelectric properties for more than 35 different functionalized MXene mono- and their multilayers. Their work reports that Mo and Cr based MXenes shows the best thermoelectric properties compared to all other MXenes. The theoretical work by Dong-Chen et al. [16] shows that $b$-As, Sb and Bi monolayers with a graphene-like buckled structure may have promising applications in high-performance thermoelectric devices. Some other work was also reported on the improvement in thermoelectric devices by utilizing external stimuli like strain engineering [17, 18]. Dazhen et al. [19] have shown an improvement in the efficiency of thermoelectric materials by bismuth interfacial doping of organic small molecules.

It is known that electronic and thermoelectric properties of a monolayer thermoelectric material may get modified in their multilayer as well as for their heterostructures [20-23]. Huang et al. [20] have investigated the thermoelectric properties of molybdenum disulfide (MoS₂) and tungsten diselenide (WSe₂) for their mono-, bi-, tri- and tetralayers and shown that thermoelectric properties depends on both the number of layers as well as with different doping concentrations (n-type or p-type). Their study finds that the figure of merit ($ZT$) with n-type doping concentration for single layer MoS₂ and bilayer WSe₂ as 1.6 and 2.1, respectively [20]. The research group of Bhattacharyya et al. [24] studied the strain dependent electronic and thermoelectric properties of MoS₂ and its multilayers, and shown the transport properties as a function of the number of layers and the applied strain. As an outcome of their work, 3 layered (3L) and 2 layered (2L) MoS₂ emerge as the most efficient thermoelectric materials under applied strain [24]. The researchers also tendered some experimental efforts towards developing efficient thermoelectric [25-28]. Rogacheva et al. [25] experimentally investigated the thermoelectric properties of hetero-structure of PbTe/SnTe/PbTe and their results were in very good agreement with relevant computations. Jana et al. [26] obtained a combined experimental and theoretical investigation of Td-WTe₂ in form of few-layers and bulk phase, where they investigated the structural, electronic, vibrational and thermoelectric properties and observed the metal to insulator transition in Seebeck coefficient. Cai et al. [27] investigated the thermal conductivity and interface conductance at different temperature of suspended and supported monolayer graphene which is grown by chemical vapor deposition. Flores et al. [28] obtained the experimental investigation of thermoelectric properties on bulk black-phosphorus from which

they have concluded that the compound has promising application in thermoelectric devices. Overall, it appears that in addition to the routine strain engineering or doping of small molecules, one can chose for layered or heterostructure based approaches in an easier and effective way for developing more efficient thermoelectric materials.

In the present work, we have explored the possibility of bilayered (CaS/CaS, CaSe/CaSe) and heterostructure (CaS/CaSe) materials in the thermoelectrics arena, from our recently developed hexagonal monolayer CaS and CaSe materials as efficient thermoelectric materials (ZT: 0.9 to 1.41 for 250-600 K), from a series of calcium chalcogenides CaX (X=O, S, Se and Te). The present work has been carried out using the first principle approach under density functional theory (DFT) [29, 30]. A systematic investigation is performed to evaluate the vibrational properties and dynamical stability, electronic properties including band gap and partial density of states (PDOS), and thermoelectric properties including Seebeck coefficient, electrical and thermal conductivity and figure of merit (ZT). The thermoelectric properties are obtained using the Boltzmann transport theory [31].

## Theory and Computation

The theoretical investigation were carried out using *ab-initio* density functional theory (DFT) [29, 30] utilizing a scalar relativistic ultra-soft pseudo potential which includes effect of electron-ion interactions. The exchange correlation (XC) potential is chosen as Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA) which involve electron-electron effect [32], as implemented in Quantum ESPRESSO package [33]. A well-converged kinetic energy cutoff (ecut) for wavefunctions and Monkhorst-Pack *k-points* (Brillouin zone sampling) are considered for all the planer hexagonal structures as 50 Ry and 15×15×1, respectively. In order to check the effect of energy cutoff and *k*-points values on the lattice parameters (*a*) of the considered bilayers (CaS/CaS, CaSe/CaSe and CaS/CaSeCa), we have performed critical analysis with series of larger ecut and *k*-points (Tables S1-S3, Supplementary Information). It may be noted that the error bar for the lattice parameters in all the cases is found to be negligible (about 0.01%) for all the systems. For ionic minimization, a conjugate gradient method was used and the convergence criteria for total energy and forces are set to be $10^{-5}$ a.u. and $10^{-4}$ a.u., respectively. To neglect the fictitious interactions among the layers of periodic images, we have

considered a vacuum of 20 Å in the perpendicular direction to the hexagonal layer of CaS and CaSe. It is important to note that when two monolayers are close enough, the weak Vander Waals (vdW) interaction appears to be considerable, and therefore to include this effect in our considered systems, we have included the semi-empirical Grimme's DFT-D2 scheme as implemented in Quantum ESPRESSO package [33]. For the visualization and structural information, we have utilized the XCrysden package [34]. To investigate the thermoelectric transport properties of the considered planer hexagonal materials, Boltzmann transport theory [31] was utilized to compute temperature and doping-level-dependent transport parameters such as Seebeck coefficient (S), and other thermoelectric properties are computed from the electronic band structure. All the considered thermoelectric transport properties are obtained under the consideration of constant scattering time approximation (CSTA) [35, 36] and rigid band approximations (RBA) [37, 38]. The thermal conductivity ($\kappa$) and electrical conductivity ($\sigma$) are obtained by the following Boltzmann semi classical equations:

$$
\kappa_{\alpha \beta}^{0}(\mathrm{~T} ; \mu)=\frac{1}{e^{2} T \Omega} \int \sigma_{\alpha \beta}(\varepsilon)(\varepsilon-\mu)^{2}\left[-\frac{\partial f_{\mu}(\mathrm{T} ; \varepsilon)}{\partial_{\varepsilon}}\right] d \varepsilon \quad \ldots \quad \ldots \quad(1)
$$

$$
\sigma_{\alpha \beta}(\mathrm{T} ; \mu)=\frac{1}{\Omega} \int \sigma_{\alpha \beta}(\varepsilon)\left[-\frac{\partial f_{\mu}(\mathrm{T} ; \varepsilon)}{\partial_{\varepsilon}}\right] d \varepsilon \quad \ldots \quad \ldots \quad(2)
$$

where the used parameters in the above equations are the electrical conductivity ($\sigma$) electronic part of thermal conductivity ($\kappa$), tensor indices ($\alpha$, $\beta$), absolute temperature ($T$), chemical potential ($\mu$), electronic charge ($e$), volume of unit cell ($\Omega$), band energy ($\varepsilon$) and Fermi-Dirac distribution function ( $f_{\mu} f_{\mu}$ ). The Seebeck coefficient (S) is defined as follows:

$$
S_{i j}=E_{i}\left(\nabla_{j} T\right)^{-1}=\left(\sigma^{-1}\right) \alpha_{i} v_{\alpha j} \quad(3)
$$

where $v_{\alpha j}$ is the band velocity.

The figure of merit (ZT) (a dimensionless parameter) is defined in terms of the other thermoelectric parameters as follows:

$$
Z T=\frac{S^{2} \sigma T}{\kappa} \quad(4)
$$

To solve semi-classical Boltzmann transport equations (Eqns. 1 and 2) we have utilised the BoltzTraP code [39], which provides reliable estimated values of thermoelectric parameters as a function of temperature and carrier concentration without any adjustable parameters.

## Results and Discussion

In our previous work [13], we have reported structure, electronic and thermoelectric properties of monolayer calcium chalcogenides series, viz. CaX (X=O, S, Se and Te). It was observed [13] that monolayer hexagonal CaS and CaSe reveals to be the best thermoelectric materials in the series with their larger Seebeck coefficient (thermoelectric power), lower thermal conductivity and significant figure of merit (0.9–1.41). Motivated with the possible usefulness of monolayers of CaS and CaSe [13], in the present work, we have attempted a detail investigation on the prospects of their bilayers (CaS/CaS and CaSe/CaSe) and heterostructure (CaS/CaSe). **Fig. 1** represents the optimized geometry of the hexagonal monolayers ($h$-CaS and $h$-CaSe) [13], and their bilayers (CaS/CaS and CaSe/CaSe) and heterostructure (CaS/CaSe). The unit cells of bilayers and the heterostructure (hybrid bilayer) are obtained by a parallel arrangement of the monolayers of relevant calcium chalcogenides to gain AB stacking. The unit cell of those planer hexagonal symmetries consists of four atoms. The top and side views of the supercells ($2{\times}2{\times}1$) of the considered monolayers (CaS and CaSe) [13], bilayers (CaS/CaS and CaSe/CaSe) and heterostructure (CaS/CaSe) along with associated bond lengths and inter-planer spacing are also presented in **Fig. 1**. In order to check the possible effect of crystal size on the lattice parameter, we have also computed the same by considering a larger super cell ($3{\times}3{\times}1$ containing 18 atoms) for all the systems. It is important to note that no change in the lattice parameters is noticed confirming the proper selection of our $2{\times}2{\times}1$ super cell (Table S4, Supplementary Information).

# Vibrational and Electronic Properties

After the geometry relaxation of the considered bilayers and heterostructures, the obtained relaxed positions and cell parameters are utilized to gain the allowed vibrational frequencies of considered materials. The phonon dispersion curves (PDC) along the high symmetry $K$-path provides the information regarding dynamical stability of a considered material. The PDC's are generated for both the bilayers (CaS/CaS and CaSe/CaSe) and heterostructure (CaS/CaSe) to check their dynamical stability. Further, we have also investigated on the electronic properties, e.g. electronic band structure and projected density of states (PDOS) for the dynamically stable materials.

## Bilayer Calcium Sulfide (CaS/CaS)

In our previous paper [13], we had reported that monolayer calcium sulfide (CaS) [13] in hexagonal architecture is dynamically stable. In order to investigate stability of it's bilayer (CaS/CaS), we have computed phonon dispersion analysis. **Fig. 2** shows calculated phonon dispersion curves (PDC) of CaS/CaS along the high symmetrical path of $k$-points. It may be noted that there are total of 12 phonon modes in which 3 modes represents acoustical branches and remaining 9 modes are belongs to optical branches. It is observed that in case of monolayer CaS [13], the phonon spectra having higher frequency compared to bilayer CaS, the decrease in

phononic frequencies in bilayer may be understood due to the inter layer weak van der Waals (vdW) interaction. It is good to note that there is no negative frequencies belong to the acoustical branches, indicating that the considered bilayer CaS (CaS/CaS) is dynamically stable.

![](./images/812679211117445121_4.jpg)

Fig. 2 The phonon dispersion curves of planer hexagonal bilayer CaS along the high symmetrical $K$-path.

Fig. 3(a) describes the profile of electronic band structure of bilayer CaS, whereas the valence band maximum (VBM) and conduction band minimum (CBM) are located at $K$ and $\Gamma$ points, respectively. The calculated energy band gap (indirect) is found to be 3.79 eV, which is larger compared to its monolayer architecture (3.16 eV [13]) at the same location of VBM and CBM. Such increase in energy band gap (about 0.6 eV) in bilayer may be due to the inter layer weak van der Waals (vdW) effect. It is important to note that the inter layer interaction is much effective since the inter layer distance is only $0.09\mathring{A}$ larger to the bond length of the Ca and S.

Fig. 3(b) presents the profile of the orbitals projected density of states (PDOS) for bilayer CaS. The PDOS of CaS/CaS shows that $p$-orbital of sulphur atoms shows dominant contribution near the Fermi level in valence band region, whereas in conduction band region $s$-orbital of calcium shows the major contribution. The orbital contribution of the bilayer CaS shows similar behavior to its monolayer counterpart [13], in terms of projected density of state (PDOS).

### Bilayer Calcium Selenide (CaSe/CaSe)

Theoretical investigation on the hexagonal monolayer calcium selenide (CaSe) is reported in our previous work [13], whereas it was observed that monolayer architecture of CaSe was dynamically stable and shows indirect energy band gap of 3.03 eV. In the present work, a sincere effort on the viability of hexagonal bilayer of CaSe (CaSe/CaSe) is tendered. Along that direction, at the initial stage, we have carried out phonon vibrational analysis on the optimized structure of CaSe/CaSe. The phonon dispersion curves (PDC) of bilayer CaSe is depicted in Fig. 4. Similar to the bilayer CaS, CaSe/CaSe also having 12 phononic modes and 4 atoms in its primitive unit cell (consist of 2 Ca and 2 Se atoms). The inter layer spacing of bilayer CaSe is observed to be $2.92$ Å (Fig. 1d), which is some larger ($\sim$0.13 Å) compared to the same of bilayer CaS. The inter layer separation in CaSe/CaSe is noticed to be $0.08$ Å larger than the bond length between Ca and Se (Fig. 1b). It may be noted from Fig. 4 that bilayer CaSe shows some acoustical branches in the negative frequency zone which indicate that CaSe/CaSe is not dynamical stabile. Accordingly, we have not reported any further electronic and thermoelectric properties of CaSe/CaSe in the present paper. However, for the sake of completeness, we have

reported band structure and PDOS (Fig. S2), and associated thermoelectric properties (Fig. S3) of dynamically unstable CaSe/CaSe in the Supplementary Information.

Hybrid Calcium Sulfide and Calcium Selenide (CaS/CaSe) Bilayer

It may be noted so far from our investigation on the mono- and bilayer studies on pure CaS and CaSe, all the compounds were found to be dynamically stable except for the bilayer CaSe (CaSe/CaSe). We have further extended our study towards the possible existence and physicochemical behavior on their heterostructure (hybrid bilayer). Accordingly, for hybrid CaS/CaSe structure, the primitive cell consists of 4 atoms in which 2 are calcium and remaining are sulphur and selenium atoms. As presented in Fig. 1(e), the inter layer spacing between CaS and CaSe layers is found to be 2.77 Å, which is very close to the bond length between calcium and sulphur/selenium (2.77 Å/2.78 Å). Similar to the bilayers of pure CaS and CaSe, the weak van der Waals (vdW) effect also comes into the picture on their hybrid (CaS/Case) bilayer. The computed phonon dispersion curves along the high symmetrical $K$-path are depicted in Fig. 5. Similar to the pure bilayers, the hybrid CaS/CaSe structure also consists of 12 phononic branches. It is heartening to note that although bilayer CaSe is found to be dynamically unstable,

its hybridization with CaS makes it stable with all the three acoustical branches in the positive frequency region.

![](./images/812679211117445121_7.jpg)

Fig. 5 The phonon dispersion relation of planer hexagonal hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer along the high symmetrical $K$-path.

Fig. 6(a) represents the electronic band structure of hybrid CaS/CaSe. Similar to bilayer CaS, the valence band maximum (VBM) and conduction band minimum (CBM) for CaS/CaSe are located at $K$ and $\Gamma$ points, respectively with an indirect band gap of 3.67 eV. It may be noted that although the band structure of hybrid CaS/CaSe is similar to their monolayer counterparts (CaS and CaSe), the energy band gap for their hybrid bilayer is observed to be larger compared to the respective monolayers, viz. $E_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathrm{CaS})=3.16$ eV and $E_{\mathrm{g}}(\mathrm{CaSe})=3.03$ eV [13]. This enhancement in energy gap arises due to the van der Waals (vdW) interaction between the layers of CaS and CaSe. The profile of the projected density of states (PDOS) is provided in Fig. 6(b). It may be concluded from the PDOS that $p$-orbitals of selenium and sulphur atoms dominates near the Fermi level in valence band region, and in conduction band region, $s$-orbital of calcium atoms shows the major contribution.

## Thermoelectric properties

Thermoelectric transport properties are very important for devising thermal generators, which are mainly depends on material geometry, temperature, chemical formation and composition, pressure and thickness. In order to check thickness dependency (mono to bilayer), we have computed various thermoelectric transport properties which include electrical ($\sigma$) and thermal ($\kappa$) conductivities, Seebeck coefficient ($S$) and figure of merit ($ZT$) of the CaS/CaS and CaS/CaSe bilayers, and compared with their monolayers counterparts [13]. Fig. 7 represents various thermoelectric properties of CaS (mono- and bilayers) and CaSe (monolayer) and their hybrid bilayer CaS/CaSe. The thermoelectric properties of monolayer CaS and CaSe are considered from our previous work [13] for their comparative study with respective bilayer analogues.

![](./images/812679211117445121_9.jpg)

Fig. 7 (a) Electrical conductivity ($\sigma$), (b) Thermal conductivity ($\kappa$), (c) Seebeck coefficient ($S$) and (d) Figure of merit ($ZT$) for planer hexagonal calcium chalcogenides in the form of monolayer, bilayer and heterostructure.

The electrical conductivity ($\sigma$) of the considered monolayer, bilayer and heterostructure of CaS and CaSe are presented in Fig. 7(a) which essentially provide an information on how fast the carrier charges can travel through the materials. It may be noticed from the profiles that electrical conductivity reduces with the increase of temperature in all the monolayers, bilayers and heterostructure, in which hybrid bilayer (heterostructure of CaS/CaSe) shows minimum as a response to the applied temperature range of 50-1200 K. The energy band gap of hybrid

CaS/CaSe is found to be quite larger (3.67 eV) than its monolayer cases (3.16 eV for CaS; 3.03 eV for CaSe), which leads the compound for lower electrical conductivity, since wide band gap semiconductors are having less amount of charge carriers. Similarly, bilayer CaS also have larger band gap (3.79 eV) as compared to its monolayer, which essentially shows faster decrement in its electrical conductivity compared to its monolayer.

In order to understand heat transport through the considered materials, we have computed thermal conductivity ($\kappa$) for the temperature range of 50-1200 K. The profile of $\kappa$ is presented in Fig. 7(b) in which heat transport includes both the electronic and phononic parts, with a dominancy of the electronic part. It may be noted from the profiles that all considered monolayer, bilayer and heterostructure show an increasing feature of $\kappa$ with respect to temperature. It is important to note that among all the considered materials, hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer shows the slowest rise in $\kappa$ with increasing temperature indicating it as a promising thermoelectric material compared to others.

The profile of thermoelectric power or Seebeck coefficient ($S$) is presented in Fig. 7(c) for all the structures of CaS and CaSe, for the temperature range of 50-1200 K. For all the considered materials, $S$ values observed to be positive and shows increments along the temperature rise. It is known that when Seebeck coefficient values belong to the positive domain, the material is identified as a member of $p$-type semiconductor family with majority charge carriers are holes. Accordingly, all the considered compounds may be classified as $p$-type wide band gap semiconductors. The hybrid CaS/CaSe shows the highest Seebeck coefficient values compared to others for the entire temperature range (50-1200 K). Huang et al. [20] have reported thickness dependent study on $\mathrm{MoS}_2$ and $\mathrm{WSe}_2$, where they also observed that increase of layers, viz. two layer materials shows better profile of Seebeck coefficients compared to its monolayer [20].

A good thermoelectric material is expected to have high Seebeck coefficient ($S$), high electrical conductivity ($\sigma$) and lower thermal conductivity ($\kappa$). An inter relation of these parameters ($S$, $\sigma$ and $\kappa$) is presented by figure of merit ($ZT$) (Eqn. 4) which essentially characterize the materials performance as thermoelectrics. Fig. 7(d) shows the profile of $ZT$ with respect to temperature for all the considered mono-, bi- and hybrid layered structures. It may be noted that although $ZT$ values for hexagonal monolayers of CaS and CaSe initially rises up to

1.32 and 1.41 for the temperature range 50-250 K and 50-500 K, respectively, it starts to decrease until 0.90 (for CaS) and 0.98 (for CaSe). It vindicates that performance of monolayer thermoelectrics may not be stable for long range of temperature and they may be utilized for small scale temperature range applications. On the other hand, both the bilayers, viz. bilayer CaS and hybrid CaS/CaSe shows stable performance for almost entire range of temperature (300-1200 K) with significantly high $ZT$ values (CaS/CaS: 0.66-0.82 and CaS/CaSe: 0.89-0.90). Therefore, bilayer CaS and hybrid CaS/CaSe may be utilized as future promising thermoelectric materials for significantly wide temperature range, in which the hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer reveal to be the best thermoelectric material in the considered series. In a similar study in past, Huang et al. [20] computed $ZT$ of $MoS_2$ and $WSe_2$ and reported that bilayer of $WSe_2$ and monolayer $MoS_2$ shows higher figure of merit for limited to temperature range up to 500 K. The present study shows the promising behavior of hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer for a wide temperature range of 300-1200 K.

### Concluding Remarks

In summary, the present investigation reports a detail theoretical effort on geometrical, electronic, vibrational and thermoelectric properties of bilayer calcium chalcogenides (CaS/CaS and CaSe/CaSe) and their hybrid CaS/CaSe structure with a comparison to our previously developed their monolayer counterparts. Although bilayer CaS (CaS/CaS) and hybrid (CaS/CaSe) bilayer shows dynamical stability, bilayer of CaSe (CaSe/CaSe) is found to be unstable with negative phonon dispersion curves. Both the CaS/CaS and CaS/CaSe are found to be wide indirect band gap semiconductors with energy band gaps as 3.79 eV and 3.67 eV, respectively. The projected density of state (PDOS) analysis shows that in the valence band region, $p$-orbitals of chalcogen atoms (S and Se) shows dominant contribution, whereas in the conduction band region $s$-orbitals of calcium atoms have major contribution in all cases. The bilayer CaS (CaS/CaS) and hybrid (CaS/CaSe) bilayer provides almost uniform (steady) high value of figure of merit (~ 1) for a wide range of temperature (300-1200 K) compared to their monolayer counterparts. Overall, the hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer structure reveal to be the best thermoelectric material in the entire considered series of calcium chalcogenides, with lower thermal conductivity, higher Seebeck coefficient and significantly steady figure of merit (0.8-

0.9) for a wide temperature range. The developed hybrid CaS/CaSe bilayer would certainly be a promising thermoelectrical material toward future applications in thermopower generation devices.
