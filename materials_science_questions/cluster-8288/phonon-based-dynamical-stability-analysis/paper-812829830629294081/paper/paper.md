## ABSTRACT

With the first-principles calculations, we probe into the structural, vibrational, and electronic properties of trigonally bonded planar II-IV-N₂ (II = Zn, Cd; IV = Si, Ge, Sn), in contrast to their well-known tetrahedrally bonded counterparts. A detailed analysis is presented for the deformed honeycomb-like structures of planar II-IV-N₂ in light of atomic radius and electronegativity of different group-II and group-IV elements. Cohesive energy and phonon property are calculated to investigate the stability of monolayer II-IV-N₂. Among the six monolayer II-IV-N₂ studied, five of them are dynamically stable except for monolayer CdSnN₂ which has imaginary phonon frequencies. The band curvatures of trigonally bonded II-IV-N₂ reveal distinct difference comparing to their tetrahedrally bonded counterparts and show connections to atomic transmutation and structural distortions. The different variation trends of band gap from tetrahedrally bonded structure to trigonally bonded structure are explored through density of states analysis. The highest valence band energies of monolayer ZnGeN₂ and ZnSnN₂ at Γ and X point are very close, resulting in a quasi-direct band gap. Tuning of band gap characteristics by uniaxial strain is investigated for these ternary nitrides and it is shown that a slightly change less than 0.1% on lattice constant b can induce indirect-to-direct band gap transition for monolayer ZnSnN₂.

## 1. Introduction

Ternary II-IV-V₂ compounds represent a class of semiconductors with tunable band gap and interesting mechanical properties. They can be regarded as derivatives from III-V semiconductors by changing group-III element to group-II and IV elements and obeying the octet rule by pairing two group-II with two group-IV elements around each anion [1]. Tetrahedrally bonded II-IV-N₂ with structures derived from wurtzite structure of group-III nitrides have received significant attention. Tetrahedrally bonded ZnSiN₂, ZnGeN₂ and ZnSnN₂ have been synthesized by various methods [2-7] and their properties and potential applications have been extensively studied [8-12]. Recently, various novel two-dimensional (2D) materials have attracted plenty of attentions for their eminent properties. Monolayer ternary zinc nitrides, Zn-IV-N₂, have been studied and potential applications of monolayer ZnSnN₂ in optoelectronics and in photocatalytic water splitting have been demonstrated [13,14]. Tetrahedrally bonded Cd-IV-N₂ compounds have also received attentions recent years [15,16], though there is still no report on trigonally bonded Cd-IV-N₂.

The structures of monolayer II-IV-N₂ are similar to graphene-like honeycomb materials but with distortions introduced by different bond lengths of II-N and IV-N in hexagon. These distortions are resulted from atomic transmutation with different elements and would change the stabilities and electronic structures of new monolayers. So influences of element substitution and structure distortion to the dynamical stability and electronic structure of these new monolayer nitrides are worth to be investigated systematically. Furthermore, these monolayer II-IV-N₂ are trigonally bonded, in contrast to their traditional tetrahedrally bonded counterparts. It is interesting to study structure-property connection in these trigonally and tetrahedrally bonded II-IV-N₂.

In the present paper, computational studies on the trigonally bonded (monolayer) and tetrahedrally bonded II-IV-N₂ have been carried out based on density functional theory (DFT) and density functional perturbation theory (DFPT). The geometric structures of both monolayer Zn-IV-N₂ and Cd-IV-N₂ were calculated and their lattice dynamical stabilities were illustrated by phonon calculations. Band structures and density of states (DOS) analysis were examined and their potential applications were demonstrated with electronic structures analysis. Uniaxial strains have been applied systematically to reveal the evolution of band characteristics of monolayer II-IV-N₂.

Optimized norm-conserving Vanderbilt pseudopotentials (ONCVPSP) [18] were used and the valence electrons in pseudopotentials were defined as Zn $(3s^{2}3p^{6}3d^{10}4s^{2})$, Cd $(4s^{2}4p^{6}4d^{10}5s^{2})$, Si $(3s^{2}3p^{2})$, Ge $(3d^{10}4s^{2}4p^{2})$, Sn $(4d^{10}5s^{2}5p^{2})$ and N $(2s^{2}2p^{3})$. The exchange-correlation potential was approximated by generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [19] except where explicitly stated otherwise. The energy cutoff of plane-wave basis set was set to 40 Hartree (1088.44 eV). $4 × 6 × 6$ and $4 × 6 × 1$ Monkhorst-Pack k-point grids were chosen to sample the Brillouin zones of tetrahedrally and trigonally bonded structures, respectively. To avoid spurious interactions between adjacent monolayers in periodic condition, a vacuum of $10\mathring{A}$ was introduced along the direction $c$ perpendicular to the monolayer. The Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb- Shanno (BFGS) scheme was applied to relax the lattice parameters and atomic positions with the maximal absolute force tolerance lower than $5 × 10^{-6}$ Hartree/Bohr $(2.57 × 10^{-4} eV/\mathring{A})$. The DFPT [20,21] with LO-TO splitting was applied to calculate the phonon dispersion and investigate the stabilities of II-IV-N₂ monolayers. The DOSs were calculated by projecting the plane wave states onto a localized basis using a technique described by Sanchez-Portal et al. [22] implemented in CASTEP code [23,24], to avoid the uncertainty caused by the choice of atomic cutoff radius in atomic projected DOS analysis.

## 3. Results
### 3.1. Crystal structure
The basic repeated unit of monolayer II-IV-N₂ (II = Zn, Cd; IV = Si, Ge, Sn) has eight atoms on the same plane. The planar II-IV-N₂ contains hexagonal structure with distortions resulted from the different bond lengths of II-N and IV-N. A unit cell of orthorhombic system with space group of Pb21m (No.26) can be used to contain both the 2D monolayer and the vacuum space. In traditional 3D II-IV-N₂ with tetrahedral coordination, all N atoms are bonded with two cations of group-II and two cations of group-IV. Whereas in monolayer II-IV-N₂ with trigonal coordination, one kind of N atom is bonded with one cation of group-IV and two cations of group-II (denoted as N1 in the following), and the other kind of N atom is bonded with two cations of group-IV and one cation of group-II (denoted as N2 in the following). Two representatives of trigonally bonded II-IV-N₂, monolayer CdSiN₂ and ZnSnN₂, are shown in Fig. 1, with N1 and N2 atoms depicted by blue and red colors respectively. The non-regular hexagon ring shown in Fig. 1 is composed of four kinds of bond lengths: II-N1, II-N2, IV-N1 and IV-N2. The structure in monolayer CdSiN₂ is evidently distorted from ideal hexagonal structure and the large distortion of the hexagon rings can be attributed to the large differences of atomic radius and electronegativity between Cd and Si. Whereas, the structure of monolayer ZnSnN₂ is close to ideal hexagonal structure, i.e. the hexagon rings are only slightly distorted. Note that the effect of atomic radius is automatically included in our first-principles calculations. The atomic radius depends on atomic number and the valence state (chemical environment) of the element. The electronic structure of the system is obtained self-consistently in the first-principles calculations, and the geometry of the system is optimized based on the information of ground state electronic structure in each specific structure.

The lattice parameters and reduced atomic coordinates calculated from DFT-GGA for planar Zn-IV-N₂ and Cd-IV-N₂ with trigonal coordination are illustrated in Tables 1 and 2, respectively. All trigonally bonded II-IV-N₂ studied in this work are planar structure and thus only coordinates $(x, y)$ are listed in Tables 1 and 2 for simplicity. The lattice constants calculated using LDA exchange-correlation potential are presented together for comparison. It is well known that LDA approximation slightly underestimates (by about 1-2%) lattice constants, while GGA frequently yields slightly larger lattice constants. So the actual lattice constants of monolayer II-IV-N₂ probably lie between the results of GGA-PBE and LDA shown in Tables 1 and 2. The reported lattice constants using GGA-PBE for monolayer Zn-IV-N₂ [14] are also listed in Table 1. There are still no experimental lattice constants available for these monolayer ternary nitrides.

The lattice constants increase from II-Si-N₂ through II-Ge-N₂ to II-Sn-N₂ because of the increment of atom radius from Si through Ge to Sn. However, the ratio between lattice constants $a$ and $b$ $(a/b)$ decreases gradually from II-Si-N₂ through II-Ge-N₂ to II-Sn-N₂, which means the increasing speed of lattice constant $a$ is a little slower than that of lattice constant $b$ with the substitution of group-IV elements (Si, Ge, Sn). This phenomenon can be explained by the fact that the IV-N2-IV and II-N1-II bond angles are approaching to $120^{\circ}$ with IV element from Si through Ge to Sn. The lattice constant $b$ is affected more by the distortion comparing to lattice constant $a$.

Four kinds of bond lengths in Zn-IV-N₂ and Cd-IV-N₂ are plotted in Fig. 2. The bond lengths of IV-N1 and IV-N2 become gradually larger with IV elements changing from Si through Ge to Sn. The deviations between IV-N and II-N are relatively large in II-Si-N₂ monolayers due to the large differences between atomic radii of Si and Cd (Zn), leading to the significant departure from regular hexatomic ring. The deviation becomes smaller and the structure is closer to regular hexagon when Si is substituted by Ge or Sn. In monolayer ZnSnN₂, Sn-N1 bond length $(1.967\mathring{A})$ is nearly identical to Zn-N2 bond length $(1.964\mathring{A})$ and they are between Sn-N2 bond length $(2.028\mathring{A})$ and Zn-N1 bond length $(1.916\mathring{A})$. As shown in Fig. 1, ZnSnN₂ monolayer is the closest to regular hexagonal structure with $120^{\circ}$ bond angle.

The bond length of IV-N2 is always larger than the bond length of IV-N1, similarly the bond length of II-N2 is always larger than the bond length of II-N1. The II atom is always bonded with two N1 atoms and one N2 atom, whereas IV atom is bonded with two N2 atoms and one N1 atom, as exhibited in Fig. 1. With the IV elements varying from Si through Ge to Sn, the bond lengths of both II-N1 and II-N2 keep nearly unchanging and the slight fluctuation can be attributed to the different environment of N atom which is also bonded with IV element. And this slight fluctuation is more visible for Zn-N2 (Cd-N2) comparing to Zn-N1 (Cd-N1) because the N2 atom is bonded with two group-IV atoms. From Si through Ge to Sn, the atom radius increases monotonously, but the electronegativity of Ge is the largest. Two factors together influence the bond length of II-N and give rise to that subtle fluctuation.

### 3.2. Stability
The average cohesive energy $(E_{c})$ of monolayer II-IV-V₂ (II = Zn, Cd; IV = Si, Ge, Sn) is defined in terms of isolated-atom energies as:

$$
E_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{E_{\text {total }}-n_{I I} E_{I I}-n_{I V} E_{I V}-n_{N} E_{N}}{n_{I I}+n_{I V}+n_{N}} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{total}$ is the total energy of monolayer II-IV-N₂. $E_{II}$, $E_{IV}$ and $E_{N}$ are the total energies of a single-isolated atom of constituted group-II, group-IV and N elements, respectively. And $n_{II}$, $n_{IV}$ and $n_{N}$ are the numbers of atoms per unit cell for group-II, group-IV and N elements, respectively.

The calculated average cohesive energies $(E_{c})$ of II-IV-N₂ are exhibited in Table 3. For comparison purpose, cohesive energies of carbon, silicon, and BN systems are also provided. All the trigonally bonded planar II-IV-N₂ are less energetically preferable than their tetrahedrally bonded counterparts. The cohesive energy of monolayer ZnSiN₂ is the lowest among all II-IV-N₂ monolayers shown in Table 3. In addition, absolute values of cohesive energies of all II-IV-N₂ monolayers are larger than that of silicene, and smaller than that of graphene and monolayer BN.

Cohesive energy of trigonally bonded II-IV-N₂ shows that the elements can hold together in energetic viewpoint and this is a basic requirement for stability judgment. Phonon calculation is necessary to check the dynamical stability of these trigonally bonded II-IV-N₂. ### 3.3. Electronic structure

The band structures of monolayer II-IV-N₂ (II = Zn, Cd; IV = Si, Ge, Sn) calculated with GGA-PBE exchange-correlation functional are illustrated in Fig. 5. Although trigonally bonded CdSnN₂ is dynamically unstable from phonon calculations, its band structure was also calculated for comparison purpose. It is well known that DFT-GGA will underestimate the band gap but the band dispersions and the trend of band gap evolution can still be taken as a good approximation to accurate value. More reliable band gap predication can be obtained by more computational demanding methods, such as quasi-particle self-consistent GW calculations [10,25,26], or by hybrid exchange-correlation functionals, such as B3PW or B3LYP, as recently demonstrated for ABO₃ perovskites by Eglitis et al. [27,28].

The band gaps of trigonally and tetrahedrally bonded II-IV-N₂ are shown in Table 4. Because the highest valence band energies at X and Γ are quite close for ZnGeN₂ and ZnSnN₂, both the direct and indirect band gaps are listed in Table 4 and their relative magnitude determines the minimum band gap type of the studied material. The band gaps of II-IV-N₂ decrease with increasing atomic numbers from Si through Ge to Sn and from Zn to Cd. This tendency is caused by weaker binding of outer valence electrons with atomic number increasing, and is well known in traditional II-VI and III-V semiconductors. The conduction band minimum (CBM) locates at Γ point for all the II-IV-N₂ structures studied. The valence band maximum (VBM) locates at X point for ZnSiN₂, ZnGeN₂ and ZnSnN₂ monolayers. The VBM of monolayer CdSiN₂ locates at a point M (0.5, 0.368) between X and U. Both monolayer CdGeN₂ and CdSnN₂ have direct band gap at Γ point.

### 3.4. Effect of uniaxial strain on band gap characteristics

Monolayer $ZnSnN_{2}$ has a quasi-direct band gap with a 0.003 eV difference between $\Gamma$ and X point in our DFT-GGA calculation as shown above. The small difference between the highest occupied energy level at $\Gamma$ and X is quite sensitive to calculation method employed, such as pseudopotential and exchange-correlation functional. Previous calculation of Bai et al. using HSE06 reported a direct band gap with 0.02 eV energy difference between the direct bang gap at $\Gamma$ and indirect band from X to $\Gamma$ [14]. And we have noticed that slightly contraction of lattice constant $b$ can induce an indirect-to-direct band gap transition for monolayer $ZnSnN_{2}$. In this section, tuning of band gap characteristics of monolayer II-IV-$N_{2}$ by uniaxial strain is investigated.

The effects of uniaxial strain on direct and indirect band gap energies are shown in Fig. 7 for $ZnSnN_{2}$ and in Fig. 8 for II-Si-$N_{2}$ and II-Ge-$N_{2}$. The strain is defined as $\varepsilon=(l-l_{0})/l_{0}$, where $l$ and $l_{0}$ are strained and unstrained lattice constants, respectively. The band gap characteristics of monolayer $ZnSnN_{2}$ are very sensitive to uniaxial strain. With compressive strain about $-0.9\%$ on lattice constant $a$ (6.756 Å), the band gap of monolayer $ZnSnN_{2}$ has changed from indirect to direct type, which means the location of VBM has changed from X to $\Gamma$. A tiny compressive strain (lower than $-0.1\%$) on lattice constant $b$ could change the location of VBM from X to $\Gamma$. As shown in the Fig. 7, the minimum band gap achieves the largest value at the transition point from direct to indirect band gap type. The uniaxial tensile strain further increases the highest energy level at X comparing to that at $\Gamma$, but the effect is not very significant. When applying uniaxial tensile strain on lattice constant $a$, the indirect band gap (X to $\Gamma$) maintains smaller than the direct band gap at $\Gamma$, but their difference is merely 0.234 eV at $3\%$ tensile strain. As the compressive strain along lattice $a$ or $b$ increases, the difference between the direct band gap at $\Gamma$ and indirect band gap from X to $\Gamma$ becomes significant.

For monolayer $ZnGeN_{2}$, a small compressive strain $(-0.4\%)$ on lattice constant $b$ can induced an indirect-to-direct band gap transition, but the strain required is quite large $(-3.8\%)$ if applied to lattice vector $a$. For $CdGeN_{2}$, it has a direct band gap when unstrained, small tensile strain along either lattice vector $a$ or $b$ can induce a direct-to-indirect band gap transition. The minimum band gap of $CdGeN_{2}$ would monotonically increase from tensile strain to compressive strain.

For monolayer $ZnSiN_{2}$ and $CdSiN_{2}$, the band gap type is insensitive to applied strain, due to the large difference between direct band gap and indirect band gap. Although band gap energies vary with applied strain, the changing of band gap type requires large strain, and strain on lattice constant $b$ of $CdSiN_{2}$ does not induce indirect-to-direct band gap transition. The engineering of band gap types and values by uniaxial strain of II-IV-$N_{2}$ would be beneficial to application in optoelectronics and photocatalysis.

### 4. Conclusions

Structure and stability of trigonally bonded planar II-IV-$N_{2}$ (II = Zn, Cd; IV = Si, Ge, Sn) are studied. All these II-IV-$N_{2}$ monolayers have coplanar structure and the structure distortions comparing to ideal hexagonal structure are depending on the atomic radii contrast and electronegativity differences of constituent elements. Monolayer $CdSiN_{2}$ represents an extreme of greatly distorted structure and monolayer $ZnSnN_{2}$ represents another extreme of closest to the ideal hexagonal structure. The phonon spectra of monolayer II-IV-$N_{2}$ prove the stability of five monolayer II-IV-$N_{2}$ among six studied except for the monolayer $CdSnN_{2}$, which contains imaginary frequencies. The band structures and PDOS of trigonally bonded II-IV-$N_{2}$ are compared with their conventional tetrahedrally bonded counterparts derived from wurtzite structure, illustrating the band structure differences and the evolution of electronic states near VBM and CBM with atomic transmutation and different coordination configuration.
