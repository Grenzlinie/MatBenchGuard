# Ab initio Lattice Dynamics of $\text{Pb}_2\text{MgTeO}_6$ Double Perovskite

Razvan Caracas and Xavier Gonze

Université Catholique de Louvain, Unité de Physico-Chimie et de Physique de Matériaux,
pl. Croix du Sud 1, B-1348 Louvain-la-Neuve, Belgium

**Abstract.** $\text{Pb}_2\text{MgTeO}_6$ is one of the few perovskites presenting an incommensurate phase at low temperature. We report calculations of the lattice dynamical properties (phonon band structure) for the cubic, (high-T) and the average rhombohedral, (low-T) structures of $\text{Pb}_2\text{MgTeO}_6$. The cubic phase presents two unstable phonons at $\Gamma$. The first has a $\text{T}_{1u}$ character and is restricted to a small neighborhood near $\Gamma$. The second has a $\text{T}_{1g}$ character in $\Gamma$. Its lowest branch generates, in the reciprocal space, tube-like unstable regions along $\Gamma$X. Its displacement pattern, in the real space, corresponds essentially to rotations of the $\text{TeO}_6$ octahedra. The rhombohedral average structure presents no instability in $\Gamma$. However, an instability is obtained by interpolation along the 100 direction, around the q= $< \delta00 >$ point, with $\delta$ close to 0.1, in disagreement with a published interpretation of experimental data, claiming q= $< \delta\delta\delta >$. Reexamination of the published electron diffraction spectra shows that the correct value of the modulation wavevector is q= $< \delta00 >$.

## INTRODUCTION

There are a few perovskites presenting incommensurately modulated structures (IC) and all are double-perovskites: $\text{Pb}_2\text{CoWO}_6$ [1], $\text{Pb}_2\text{CdWO}_6$ [2], $\text{PbSc}_{1/2}\text{Ta}_{1/2}\text{O}_3$ [3] and $\text{Pb}_2\text{MgTeO}_6$ [4, 5] (PMT). PMT has, more than the others, a few interesting properties that make it the ideal candidate for an ab initio study of the mechanisms governing the incommensurate phase transitions: a simple and ordered structure, incommensurability present at 0 K, no lock-in transition to a commensurate state. We study the properties of PMT and try to understand the mechanisms of the phase transitions leading to the incommensurate phases.

The characterization of the electronic, structural, dielectric and zone-center dynamical properties of PMT has been done previously [6]. PMT is insulating, with a large electronic gap (about 3 eV). The deviations of the theoretically determined structural parameters from the experimental data are more important for the rhombohedral average structure than for the cubic structure. However, the differences in bond lengths with respect to the average experimental structure for the rhombohedral phase are within the range of distances allowed by the modulation. The cubic phase presents two instabilities in the zone-center, while the rhombohedral phase present no instability in $\Gamma$.

In this study we analyze the lattice dynamical properties in the whole Brillouin zone for both the average rhombohedral and the cubic structure. We determine the phonon dispersions in the reciprocal space and their correspondence to the real space atomic collective displacements. In the cubic structure, the main instability forms tube-like unstable regions along the $\Gamma$-X lines. It consists of collective rotations of the $\text{TeO}_6$

## COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS

The calculations are performed using the theoretically determined structures (lattice constants and internal degrees of freedom), taken from Ref.[6]. The cubic structure has, in the conventional setting, lattice constants of 7.833 Å, with the Mg, Te, Pb and O atoms positioned at $<0\ 0\ 0>$, $<1/2\ 1/2\ 1/2>$, $<1/4\ 1/4\ 1/4>$ and $<0.2645\ 0.0\ 0.0>$, respectively. In all calculations we use the primitive unit cell. The average rhombohedral structure has lattice constants of 5.531 Åwith $\alpha=60.27^\circ$. The Mg and Te atoms preserve their special positions from the cubic phase, while the Pb are slightly displaced along the [111] directions, from the $<1/4\ 1/4\ 1/4>$ into the $<0.2507\ 0.2507\ 0.2507>$ position. The O atoms lie on $<0.2669\ 0.7646\ 0.7051>$ positions.

All the calculations are based on the local density approximation of the density func- tional theory [7, 8]. We use the ABINIT package, a common project of the Université Catholique de Louvain, Corning Incorporated and other contributors (ABINITv3.1 [9]). The ABINIT software is based on planewaves and pseudopotentials. We use Troullier- Martins pseudopotentials [10] for Mg, Te and O and an extended Teter norm-conserving pseudopotentials [11] for Pb. The considered valence electrons for Pb, Mg, Te and O are $5d^{10}6s^26p^4$, $3s^2$, $5s^25p^4$ and $2s^22p^2$ respectively.

First, the dynamical matrices are calculated on a grid of special high-symmetry q points. These points are defined in the same way as the Monkhorst-Pack special k points [12]. Second, the phonon band structure and the corresponding interatomic force constants are obtained by Fourier interpolation. Technical details on the computation of responses to atomic displacements and homogeneous electric fields can be found in Ref.[13], while Ref.[14] presents the subsequent computation of dynamical matrices, Born effective charges, dielectric permittivity tensors, and interatomic force constants.

In the computaton of the dynamical matrices, the electron density is first calculated self-consistently on a grid of 2x2x2x4 special $\mathbf{k}$ points[12]. Next, the wavefunctions used for performing the perturbative calculations are computed non-self-consistently, using the previously calculated electron density, on a 4x4x4x1 grid of $\mathbf{k}$ points.

The phonon dispersions are computed starting from several increasing grids of $\mathbf{q}$ points, in order to ensure the convergence of the dynamical properties. Finally 4x4x4 grids of q points are chosen for both structures.

# PHONON DISPERSION

Figs. 1 and 2 show the phonons dispersion in the cubic and average rhombohedral phases respectively. The same path in the Brillouin zone (BZ) is respected for both structures to ease the comparison. We use also for both structures the notation of the high-symmetry points according to the cubic BZ. The phonon dispersions of the two structures are extremely similar. There are three groups of phonon branches separated by large gaps (about 50 cm-1 between the two energy lowest and about 125 cm-1 between the two energy highest phonon band groups). The differencies between the two diagrams are found only in the low-frequencies regions of the spectra, due to the different structural instabilities.

The high frequency modes, $>250\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, are dominated by the oxygen displacements and correspond to certain breathing modes of the $\text{AO}_6$ octahedra, A=Mg, Te. The low-frequency modes, $<200\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, are characterized by the cation participation to the real space vibrational pattern.

## Cubic phase

Two unstable phonons have been found in the cubic structure. The first one is a triply degenerate $\text{T}_{1\mu}$ mode in $\Gamma$. The TO component is unstable, appearing with a frequency of $13.92i\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. It stabilizes extremely fast very close to $\Gamma$. The vibrational pattern of each of the three components consists, in real space, of displacements of all atoms in the lattice against the Pb atoms along one of the $x$, $y$ or $z$ cartesian directions. The most important eigendisplacement belong to the O and Te atoms conteined in the (100) planes perpendicular to the [100] direction of movement. The Mg participation is smaller. The oxygens situated between these (100) planes have a smaller amplitude of vibration. The freezing-in of this vibration mode yields an energetic minimum that is 0.083 meV lower than the cubic groundstate energy. This very small value corresponds to maximum eigendisplacements of less than $0.1\ \mathring{\text{A}}$. The coupling of the three components of this mode generates a decrease in energy of 0.105 meV. A participation of this mode to the phase transition toward a hypothetical rhombohedral normal structure might be considered as unimportant, as the instability is very weak and during the phase transitions, the deviations of Pb atoms from the cubic 1/4 1/4 1/4 positions are small.

The second unstable phonon is also triply degenerated in $\Gamma$ and it has a $\text{T}_{2g}$ character. This is the most important instability of the cubic structure. It is unfolded in 1+2 modes along $\Gamma$-X and 1+1+1 modes along $\Gamma$-L.

The 3D reciprocal space image of the lowest branch of this mode corresponds to tube-like unstable regions along the $\Gamma$-X directions. The lateral extent of the tubes and their relative rotation in neighbor BZs (with 60 degrees along [111] directions) lead to the reappearance of the instability in K.

The real space image of the instability in $\Gamma$ corresponds to pure oxygen displacements leading to rotations of $\text{MgO}_6$ and $\text{TeO}_6$ octahedra around the cartesian axes in relative opposite ways. The freezing-in of the eigendisplacements corresponding to this mode yields an energetic minimum that is 7.014 meV less than the groundstate energy. This

### Average rhombohedral phase

The average rhombohedral structure presents no instability in $\Gamma$. There is only one unstable phonon mode along the $\Gamma$-L' line, which corresponds to the [100] rhombohedral directions in reciprocal space. The instability lies in the $<\delta\ 0\ 0>$ region, with $\delta$ in the range (0.0825 - 0.2125), and containing the experimentally determined values of $\delta$=0.1066. The instability is relatively weak, with a frequency minimum of $10i\ cm^{-1}$, that corresponds to $\delta=0.1525$. At the q=$<0.1066\ 0.0\ 0.0>$ point, the unstable mode has a frequency of $6.6i\ cm^{-1}$, according to the interpolation results.

According to the experience [4, 5], the IC structure is characterized by a modulation with a wavevector q=$<\delta\ \delta\ \delta>$, where $\delta=0.1066$. Our theoretical results disagree with the experimental results in terms of the direction of the instability: along the rhombohedral [100] vs. [111] direction.

## REINTERPRETATION OF THE ELECTRON DIFFRACTION PATTERN

The low-T phase of $Pb_2MgTeO_6$ exhibits several sets of satellite spots in the electron diffraction spectra due to the incommensurate modulation. The position of the satellite spots was described in Ref. [16] as (h+$\delta$,k+$\delta$,l+$\delta$), with $\delta$ close to 0.1. The authors used the cubic reference system and presented two diffraction images recorded along the cubic [110] direction ((Fig. 3 a and b). They made no distinction between the possible directions, [110] and $[\overline{1}10]$, belonging to the same [110] cubic family.

In the incommensurate phase, with rhombohedral symmetry, the equivalence of the [110] and $[\overline{1}10]$ directions (from the cubic symmetry) is cancelled due to the breaking of the symmetry during the transition to the rhombohedral phase. Consequently, the diffraction patterns recorded along [110] and $[\overline{1}10]$ will be different. As the distortion of the lattice during the phase transition is expressed in the small change of the rhombohedral angle, which passes from $60.0^{\circ}$ to $59.9^{\circ}$, the difference between the two orientations is visible mainly in the disposition of the satellite spots.

The published interpretation of the spectra claims the position of these satellite spots to be $<\delta\ \delta\ \delta>$. According to the rhombohedral symmetry this will generate two equivalent $<\delta\ \delta\ \delta>$ and $<-\delta\ -\delta\ -\delta>$ spots. Moreover, these spots will be visible

only along the $[\overline{1}10]$ recorded spectra, while the [110] recorded spectra will present no sattellite spots.

The theoretical results concerning the phonon dispersion spectra in the average rhombohedral structure of $Pb_2MgTeO_6$ indicates an incommensurate instability along the rhombohedral [100] direction, corresponding to the family of cubic $[\overline{1}11]$ directions. This incommensurate instability, once frozen in the structure, may be expressed in a diffraction pattern as sattelite spots at general positions $<\delta\ 0\ 0>$. The rhombohedral symmetry ensures a multiplicity of six for these spots: $<\delta\ 0\ 0>$, $<0\ \delta\ 0>$, $<0\ 0\ \delta>$, $<-\delta\ 0\ 0>$, $<0\ -\delta\ 0>$ and $<0\ 0\ -\delta>$. The electron diffraction spectra recorded along the $[\overline{1}10]$ direction (according to the cubic reference system) will detect the presence of two satellite spots around the main Braggs spots, with a geometry similar to the one described in Fig. 3a. The spectra recorded along the perpendicular [110] direction (according to the cubic reference system) will detect the presence of four satellite peaks around the main Braggs spots, with a geometry similar to the one described in Fig. 3b.

Thus, the image provided by the theoretical calculation of the phonon dispersion is in perfect agreement with the experimental electron diffraction pattern. It ensures also the only possible explanation for the position of the satellite spots. An independent reindexation of the published experimental electron diffraction pattern confirmed the geometrical image obtained theoretically using the first-principles approach [15].

## CONCLUSIONS

The cubic, high-T phase presents two unstable phonons in $\Gamma$, with $T_{1u}$ and $T_{1g}$ characters respectively. The $T_{1u}$ instabiltiy occupy a reduced zone around $\Gamma$. The $T_{1g}$ generates large unstable regions, tube-like shaped along the [100] conventional cubic directions ($\Gamma$-X line).

The energetic analysis of the eigendisplacements corresponding to the unstable modes suggest that the instability in the cubic phase is dominated by couplings of $TeO_6$ octahedra rotations. This image is confirmed also by several other independent observations. Analysis of the electronic density reveals covalent bonds between Te and O vs. weak ionic bonds between Mg and O . Also, the overlap between the freezing-in of the coupling of the three $T_{1g}$ unstable modes in the cubic phase and the average rhombohedral structures is excellent: 0.98.

In the average rhombohedral structure, we identify an unstable mode, along the [100] rhombohedral directions, with $<\delta\ 0\ 0>$ wavevector. The position is in disagreement with a reported interpretation of experimental data, claiming $q=<\delta\ \delta\ \delta>$. We provide a reinterpretation of the published electron diffraction spectra clearly showing that the satellite spots are situated at the $<\delta\ 0\ 0>$ positions, in agreement with our theoretical results.
