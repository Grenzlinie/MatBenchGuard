methods may be conducted to its realization. First principles calculations based on the density functional theory (DFT) can be considered as one of the most influence theoretical methods in designing and evaluation of new promising nonmaterials.

By using first-principles methods, the stability and the electronic properties of 32 honeycomb monolayer II-VI semiconductors were systematic investigated by Zheng et al. [18]. According to their calculations, Zheng et al. stated that among 32 2D honeycomb structure of XY (X = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Zn, Ca, Hg, Y=O, S, Se, Te), only BeO, MgO, CaO, ZnO, CdO, CaS, SrS, SrSe, BaTe, and HgTe indicate good stabilities and the remaining are unstable as revealed by phonon calculations. The electrical and optical properties of the materials were also analyzed and it was found that all of the 2D structures are semiconductors showing wide band gaps and they can be employed for using in designing ultraviolet region (UV) devices application.

In this paper, by using properties of the density functional theory (DFT) couple of energetically and kinetically stable group II-VI monolayer materials with tetragonal crystal structure are systematically predicted. It is shown that 2D tetragonal MgX (X = S, Se) monolayers are indirect semiconductors with strain tunable wide band gaps (WBG). Moreover, we analyzed their optical properties and found that they may have potential specialists for using in UV applications.

## 2. Computational methods

Full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave method (FLAPW) implemented in the density functional theory (DFT) based computer package of WIEN2K [19] was performed in the present work. The atomic force minimization and lattice parameters optimization were done by using generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the form of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) [20]. For the aim of finding the fully relaxed and optimized lattice parameters, $8 \times 8 \times 1$ k-points in the first Brillouin zone (BZ) for the self-consistent calculation with the input parameters of $R_{MT}K_{max}=6$, $G_{max}=14$ Ry$^{1/2}$ and $l_{max}=10$ were set, while, the denser k-point meshes of $12 \times 12 \times 1$ and $22 \times 22 \times 1$ were considered for the electrical and optical properties calculations, respectively [21]. In these computations, to avoid the neighboring sheets interaction effect, a vacuum layer of 20 Å was considered. To reach the optimized unit cell, by employing the Birch-Murnaghan thermodynamic equation of state [22], the total energies of the fully relaxed unit cells versus its volume was calculated, the Birch-Murnaghan thermodynamic equation is given by:

![](./images/812641767689027585_1.jpg)

$$
E(V)=E_{0}+\frac{9 B_{0} V_{0}}{16}\left\{\left[\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}-1\right]^{3} B_{0}^{\prime}\right\}+\frac{9 B_{0} V_{0}}{16}\left\{\left[\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}-1\right]^{2}\left[6 a 4\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right]\right\}.
$$

In this equation$V_0$, $V$,$B_0$and $B_0'$ are the initial volume, the deformed volume, the bulk modulus and the derivative of the bulk modulus respectively, respectively. The optimized unit cell of the crystal is obtained by the minimum of the $E$–$V$ curve.

When analyzing the thermodynamic stability of the 2D monolayers, the concept of the cohesive energy ($E_{coh}$) based on the following definition:

$$
E_{c o h}=\frac{2 E_{M g}+2 E_{X}-E_{M g X}}{4},
$$

where$E_{MgX}$, $E_{Mg}$ , $E_X$ are the total energies of the unit cell of the 2D MgX monolayer, an isolated magnesium atom, an isolated X (X = S, Se) atom, respectively. By using this formulation, a calculated positive value for the cohesive energy demonstrates the thermodynamically stability of the 2D MgX monolayer. Furthermore, the phonon modes calculations were conducted by the pseudo-potential approach implemented in Quantum Espresso (QE) package [23]. The convergence conditions were set to $10^{-10}$ Ry and $10^{-8}$ Ry/Bohr for the total energy and forces, respectively. In these simulations, the Martin-Troullier norm-conserving psedupotential [24] and the kinetic energy cutoff of 80 Ry and a charge density of 800 Ry were utilized.

### 3. The crystal structure and stability of the 2D MgX (X = S, Se) monolayers

Fig. 1 illustrates the crystal structures of the 2D MgX monolayers. These structures were initially designed based on the geometry of the two dimensional tetragonal BaS monolayer, which was also reported Zheng et al. [18]. To gain the primary structures of MgS monolayer, Ba element was simply replaced by Mg element, while to gain the MgS monolayer, the Ba and S atoms were simply replaced by Mg and Se atoms respectively. Afterwards, to find the fully relaxed and optimized unit cell MgX (X = S, Se) monolayers, both force relaxation and geometry optimization schemes were preformed.

Our results showed that 2D MgS and MgSe monolayers stabilize in a buckled tetragonal crystal structure including three different atomic surfaces. In a unit cell of these structures, there are two magnesium atoms and two X (X = S, Se) atoms, in a network of these 2D crystals, the magnesium atoms distribute at the middle surface, while the X (X = S, Se) atoms are buckled in two outer planes with a vertical buckling distance of$\Delta$. As seen in Fig. 1, each magnesium atom binds to its four neighboring X atoms and each X atom binds to its four neighboring magnesium atoms. Based on our calculations, lattice parameters of $4.41\mathring{A}$ and $4.52\mathring{A}$ were obtained for the 2D MgS and MgSe monolayers respectively. In more detail, for the MgS monolayer the buckling distance of $2.27\mathring{A}$, the Mg-S bond length of $2.49\mathring{A}$ were calculated, while the values of $77.99^{\circ}$, $125.72^{\circ}$ were computed for the $(\widehat{Mg-S}-Mg)$and $(\widehat{S-Mg}-S)$ respectively, while, for the MgSe monolayer the buckling distance of $2.67\mathring{A}$, the Mg-Se bond length of $2.63\mathring{A}$ were calculated, while the values of $74.93^{\circ}$, $105.07^{\circ}$ were computed for the $(\widehat{Mg-Se}-Mg)$and $(\widehat{Se-Mg}-Se)$ respectively.

### 4. Electrical and optical properties

Now let us discuss the electrical and optical properties of the proposed 2D materials. The electrical characteristics of a material such as its band structure and electronic density of states (DOS) are closely relate to the optical properties the material. Here, we first calculated band structures of the proposed 2D mgX (X = S, Se) monolayers and then discuss their optical aspects.

To investigate the electrical properties, we first calculated the band structures of our designed 2D MgX (x = S, Se) monolayers by applying the PBE [20] level of theory. Based on our PBE based computations, both 2D tetragonal MgS and MgSe monolayers are indirect semiconductors showing wide band gaps of about 3.69, 4.01 eV respectively. However, since the PBE approximation in most cases underestimates the electrical band gaps, to obtain more exact results, we further calculated the band structures by using the Heyd-Scuseria-Ernzerhof (HSE06) [25] hybrid functional and gained the revised and more reliable electrical band gaps of about 4.51 and 4.70 eV respectively. The calculated band structures of 2D tetragonal MgX (x = S, Se) monolayers are plotted in Fig. 3. As shown in Fig. 3, both PBE and HSE06 theories indicate that for both of the 2D MgS, MgSe monolayers the valence band maximum (VBM) are located at somewhere between $\Gamma$ and M point, while their conduction band minimum (CBM) are located at $\Gamma$ point. Finally, let us discus the optical characteristics of 2D MgX monolayers. To reach the goal, we first calculated the complex dielectric function of the monolayers as defined by; by$\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$, here$\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$, and $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$are the real and imaginary part of the function, respectively and in this definition, $\omega$ refers to the frequency of the incident light. The process of light propagation through the material can be described by the complex dielectric function. The light absorption property of a material at a given frequency $\omega$ is realized positive value of the real part refers to the ability of the complex dielectric function [39,40]. On the other hand, the energy loss of the incident light trough the material can be found by the imaginary part of the dielectric function, i.e., a pure dielectric exhibits zero value for the imaginary part of dielectric function.

The imaginary part of the function can be given by Ref. [41,42],

$$
\varepsilon_{\alpha \beta}^{(2)}(\omega)=\frac{4 \pi^{2} e^{2}}{m^{2} \omega^{2}} \sum_{i, f} \int \frac{2 d^{3} k}{(2 \pi)^{3}}\left\{\left|i_{k}\right| P_{\alpha}\left|f_{i}\right|^{2} f_{i}^{k}\left(1-f_{f}^{k}\right) \delta\left(E_{f}^{k}-E_{i}^{k}-\hbar \omega\right)\right\}.

$$
\varepsilon_{\alpha \beta}^{(1)}(\omega)=\delta_{\alpha \beta} \frac{2}{\pi} P_{r} \cdot \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon_{\alpha \beta}^{(2)}\left(\omega^{\prime}\right)}{\left(\omega^{\prime}\right)^{2}-(\omega)^{2}} d\left(\omega^{\prime}\right).

where $P_{r}$ refers to the momentum matrix element between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ bands with the same crystal momentum K (Cauchy principal value).

For the case of 2D MgS, the value of$\varepsilon_{1}(0)=2.13$ and $\varepsilon_{1}(\infty)=0.8$ were obtained while for the case of 2D MgSe, the value of$\varepsilon_{1}(0)=2.16$ and $\varepsilon_{1}(\infty)=0.9$ were calculated.

Also the first two absorption peaks for the 2D MgS monolayer appear at around 5.83 ev and 6.22 eV, while for the 2D MgSe monolayer the first two peaks are appearing at around 6.65, 7.60 eV. These results show that these to 2D monolayers are promising materials for UV applications.

Further we calculated the optical absorption and reflectivity of the 2D monolayers which are plotted in Fig. 6. As seen in Fig. 6, both 2D MgS and MgSe monolayer semiconductors show insignificant absorption and reflection in the visible region of the electromagnetic spectra. To be more precise, they indicate almost zero absorption and very low reflection of about 4% in visible region. It is also seen that the main absorption peak appears at 8.53 eV for 2D MgS monolayer and 7.74 eV for 2D MgSe monolayer with the absorption values of $97 \times 10^{4} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ and $123 \times 10^{4} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ respectively. Moreover, the first significant reflectivity peak occurs at 9.10 eV for 2D MgS monolayer and 7.95 eV for 2D MgSe monolayer with the reflection of about 34% and 42% respectively. These results show that 2D MgX (X = S, Se) monolayers are transparent for the visible lights; while, they exhibit significant absorption and reflectivity in UV region of electromagnetic spectra.

## 5. Conclusions

To summarize, in this work, by using first principle calculations, the structural properties and the stability of 2D tetragonal MgX (X = S, Se) monolayers were systematically evaluated. By performing atomic force relaxation and structural lattice optimization, it was confirmed that 2D tetragonal MgX (X = S, Se) monolayers stabilize in a buckled tetragonal crystal structure. The energetically and kinetically stability of the monolayers were confirmed by calculating their cohesive energy and phonon dispersion spectra

Moreover, we further evaluated their optical characteristic and found that the proposed 2D monolayer semiconductors have insignificant absorption and reflectivity in visible region of electromagnetic spectra while they show remarkable absorption and reflection in UV region. The obtained results suggests that as Wide Band Gap (WBG) 2D semiconductors, once experimentally realized, 2D tetragonal MgX (X = S, Se) may have potential application in UV region especially as mechanical sensors and UV shielding materials.
