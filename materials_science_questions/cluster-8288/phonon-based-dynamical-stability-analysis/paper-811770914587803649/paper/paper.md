# Prediction of a conducting hard ductile cubic IrC

State Key Laboratory of Metastable Materials Science and Technology, Yanshan University, Qinhuangdao 066004, P.R. China

Nine IrC structures were investigated using *ab initio* calculations based on the structures of known transition metal compounds. The calculations indicated that zinc blende structured IrC (ZB-IrC) is not only the most energetically stable, but also mechanically and dynamically stable at the ground state. Further studies showed that ZB-IrC is a conducting hard ductile material that is expected to be experimentally synthesized like cubic PtC and RuC.

Transition metal carbides (TMCs) such as TiC, WC, and NbC have excellent physical and chemical properties, including high hardness, high melting point, and high stability. TMCs are widely used in cutting tools, abrasives, coatings, and high-temperature apparatus. Searching for new hard or even superhard TMCs has been the focus of recent research, and the representative cubic PtC and RuC have been successfully synthesized in experiments [1, 2]. According to initial references, cubic PtC and RuC have a NaCl-type structure. However, later research showed a competitive zinc blende structure for both cubic PtC and RuC [3, 4], and the zinc blende structured RuC (ZB-RuC) is considered as a new semiconducting superhard material [4]. Several new TMCs (ReC, TcC, OsC, IrC, PdC, and AgC) with outstanding mechanical properties have also been designed in theory [5–9], which would give insight into their synthesis.

A search through previous reports about IrC reveals that only its four polymorphs, i.e., NaCl-type (NaCl-IrC), WC-type (WC-IrC), OsSi-type (OsSi-IrC), and FeSi-type IrC (FeSi-IrC), were investigated using first-principles calculations [7, 8, 10]. Among the four IrC, NaCl-IrC was shown to be mechanically unstable by Fan et al. [8]. In addition, WC-IrC is dynamically unstable according to the results of Gou et al. [10], and FeSi-IrC and OsSi-IrC are unstable by our calculations. Stability, including energy stability, mechanical stability, and dynamical stability, is well-known to be the key to design a new material. Low-energy, mechanically and dynamically stable structures can be synthesized in experiments. Therefore, further expanding crystal structures are very necessary to determine which one is the best for IrC.

This Letter is an overall report for IrC based on a study of nine known structures. The nine IrC polymorphs were as follows: WC-IrC, NaCl-IrC, FeSi-IrC, OsSi-IrC, ZB-IrC, wurtzite-type IrC (WZ-IrC), NiAs-type IrC (NiAs-IrC), CrB-type IrC (CrB-IrC) (SG *Cmcm*), and TiB-type IrC (TiB-IrC) (SG *Pnma*). The results indicated that zinc blende structured IrC (ZB-IrC) is not only the most energetically stable, but also has high mechanical and dynamical stability at the ground state. Seen from the density of state (DOS), ZB-IrC is metallic. The value of the hardness of cubic IrC is 17.5 GPa, a value comparable to that of $\text{OsB}_2$ [11, 12].

Our calculations were performed using the CASTEP code [13]. The Vanderbilt ultrasoft pseudopotential [14] was used with a cutoff energy of 350 eV for the plane wave basis set. The exchange correlation terms were treated by the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) form of generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [15]. A *k*-point separation value of $0.04\ \text{Å}^{-1}$, which corresponds to fine-quality level, was used to generate the *k*-point grid resulting from the Monkhorst–Pack grid parameters [16]. Structural optimization was performed until the energy change of each atom was less than $5\times 10^{-6}$ eV, the forces on atoms were less than $0.01\ \text{eV/Å}$, and all stress components were less

After full geometry optimizations at the ground state, six IrC structures can keep their initial symmetries, except for TiB-IrC, CrB-IrC, and FeSi-IrC. Interestingly, FeSi-IrC was found to directly change to ZB-IrC after optimizations, which was inconsistent with previous results indicating that FeSi-IrC could keep its configuration [7]. Figure 1 shows the energy evolutions when relaxing FeSi-IrC. There is clearly a large energy difference between FeSi-IrC and ZB-IrC. In fact, the FeSi structure is the distorted form of the zinc blende structure (see the inserts of Fig. 1). Therefore, the low energy barrier and the large energy difference finally result in this change. The calculated equilibrium lattice parameters, total energies, formation energies, and elastic stiffness constants of the six remaining structures are listed in Table 1. By comparing their energies, ZB-IrC is found to have the lowest value at the ground state, at 1.73 eV/f.u., 2.34 eV/f.u., and 1.32 eV/f.u. lower than the earlier reported NaCl-IrC, OsSi-IrC, and WC-IrC, respectively [7,8]. Their formation energies were also calculated by $\Delta E=1/2(H_{\text{IrC}}-H_{\text{Ir}}-H_{\text{graphite}})$ at the ground state. Here $H_{\text{IrC}}$ is the total energy per formula unit of IrC, $H_{\text{Ir}}$ is the energy per atom of the four-atom cubic unit cell of Ir metal, and $H_{\text{graphite}}$ is the energy per atom of the four-atom unit cell of graphite. Their positive formation energies indicate that the external energies are required to synthesize them. Among them, the most easily synthesized phase is ZB-IrC, which has the lowest formation energy.

To verify the mechanical stability of the six polymorphs, their elastic stiffness constants were calculated (see Table 1). The criteria used for determining the mechanical stability are as follows [17]: for a cubic crystal, $C_{11}>0,\ C_{44}>0,\ C_{11}>|C_{12}|,\ (C_{11}+2C_{12})>0$; for a hexagonal crystal, $C_{44}>0,\ C_{11}>|C_{12}|,\ (C_{11}+2C_{12})\times C_{33}>2(C_{13})^{2}$. So only ZB-IrC, WC-IrC, WZ-IrC, and NiAs-IrC were found to be mechanically stable at the ground state. In the previous study [7], $C_{44}$ of OsSi-IrC has a small value (12 GPa), indicating its relatively weak stability. However, our calculations for $C_{44}$ of OsSi-IrC yielded a negative value, showing that the polymorph is simply mechanically unstable. ZB–IrC structure was found to have no imaginary phonon frequency throughout the whole Brillouin zone, indicating its dynamical stability. The ground-state phonon dispersion curves of ZB–IrC are presented in Fig. 2.

The calculated density of states (DOS) of ZB–IrC (see Fig. 3) reflects its metallicity. Strong p–d orbit hybridized states are observed between Ir and C atoms, indicating high bond strength. The hardness of the ZB–IrC was calculated according to our semiempirical hardness model, which considers the role of d valence electrons [18, 19]. Here, a $Z_\text{Ir}$ (the valence electron numbers of Ir participating in bonding) of 4 is used in the calculations for the fourfold coordinated Ir atoms in ZB–IrC, and the principles followed were consistent with the calculated ZB–RuC [4]. Table 2 lists the calculated bond parameters and Vickers hardness of ZB–IrC, ZB–PtC, and NaCl–PtC. The estimated hardness of ZB–IrC is 17.5 GPa, comparable to that of OsB₂ [11, 12]. Most materials with high hardness are brittle; examples of such materials include diamond, cBN, and WC, etc. However, ductile materials with high hardness are rare. A high (low) $B/G$ value is often associated with ductility (brittleness), and the critical value that separates ductile and brittle materials is about 1.75 [20]. The $B/G$ ratio of ZB–IrC is 4.96, indicating that it is not only hard but also ductile.

In summary, the stabilities of nine IrC polymorphs were studied using $ab$ $initio$ calculations. Among the considered polymorphs, ZB–IrC is the most stable by calculations of total energy, mechanical constants, and phonon dispersion curves. Based on the DOS, ZB–IrC is metallic. The estimated Vickers hardness value of ZB–IrC is 17.5 GPa, comparable to that of OsB₂. The high $B/G$ ratio indicates that ZB–IrC is still ductile. This conducting hard ductile cubic IrC, like cubic PtC and RuC, is expected to be synthesized under high pressure and high temperature.
