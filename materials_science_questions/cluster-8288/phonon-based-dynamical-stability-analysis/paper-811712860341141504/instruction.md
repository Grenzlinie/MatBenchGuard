# Mechanical, elastic, phonon, and thermodynamic property predictions for rare-earth monopnictides HoAs and HoP

## Problem background
HoX (X=As,P) are rare-earth monopnictides that crystallize in the rocksalt (NaCl, B1) structure and exhibit strongly correlated f-electron behavior. Characterizing their mechanical, elastic, vibrational, and thermodynamic properties is important for understanding mechanical stability, bonding character, and the response to temperature and pressure. In this task you will compute these fundamental properties for HoAs and HoP from first principles.

## Approach
The approach is based on density functional theory (DFT) using a plane-wave basis, the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized-gradient approximation for exchange-correlation, and projector-augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotentials. All calculations are performed with the open-source Quantum ESPRESSO suite as an equivalent substitute for the original closed-source code; differences due to the toolchain are expected to be within typical method-dependent scatter. Structural parameters (lattice constant, bulk modulus, pressure derivative) are obtained by fitting the energy-volume curve to the Murnaghan equation of state, and cohesive energies are computed from the total energies of the crystal and the isolated atoms. Second-order elastic constants C11, C12, C44 are extracted from stress tensors generated by applying volume-conserving strain distortions to the unit cell. Polycrystalline moduli (Zener anisotropy, Poisson's ratio, Young's modulus, and isotropic shear modulus) are then derived from the single-crystal constants using the standard formulas. Phonon frequencies and the one-phonon density of states are calculated with the small-displacement method implemented in the PHON code, using forces obtained from a supercell DFT run. Finally, the quasi-harmonic Debye model, implemented in the GIBBS code, is applied to the energy-volume data and elastic inputs to predict the temperature- and pressure-dependent thermodynamic properties (volume, bulk modulus, heat capacities, entropy, thermal expansion coefficient, Debye temperature, and Grüneisen parameter) over the ranges 0–2000 K and 0–32 GPa.

## Reproduction target
For both HoAs and HoP in the B1 structure, you must compute:

- Structural and elastic properties: equilibrium lattice constant, bulk modulus, its pressure derivative, cohesive energy, the three independent second-order elastic constants C11, C12, C44, and the derived polycrystalline moduli (Zener anisotropy factor, Poisson's ratio, Young's modulus, isotropic shear modulus).

- Phonon stability: the lowest phonon frequency and a flag indicating whether any imaginary modes are present (dynamical instability).

- Thermodynamic properties as a function of temperature (0–2000 K) and pressure (0–32 GPa): volume, bulk modulus, constant-volume and constant-pressure heat capacities, entropy, thermal expansion coefficient, Debye temperature, and Grüneisen parameter, covering at least the specific (T,P) points required in the workflow steps.

The results must be written to the three output files (`step_01_structural_elastic.json`, `step_02_phonon_stability.json`, `step_03_thermodynamics.csv`) with the exact formats specified in the workflow steps and output contract.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: DFT total energy calculations (unit cell + isolated atoms)
- Role: process
- Action: Perform DFT calculations using Quantum ESPRESSO for HoAs and HoP in the NaCl (B1) structure at several volumes around equilibrium, and for isolated Ho, As, P atoms using the same pseudopotentials. Save the energy-vs-volume data and atomic reference energies.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/energy_volumes.json`

### Step 2: DFT strain calculations for elastic constants
- Role: process
- Action: Apply volume-conserving strain distortions to the optimized unit cells of HoAs and HoP and run DFT to obtain the stress tensors, needed for extracting second-order elastic constants C11, C12, C44.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/elastic_stress.json`

### Step 3: Structural parameters, cohesive energy, and elastic properties
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the energy-volume data, fit Murnaghan's equation of state to determine equilibrium lattice constant a, bulk modulus B, and pressure derivative B'. Compute cohesive energy using the atomic reference energies. From the stress-strain data, extract C11, C12, C44. Then calculate derived polycrystalline moduli: Zener anisotropy, Poisson's ratio, Young's modulus, and isotropic shear modulus using standard formulas. Output all values for HoAs and HoP.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_01_structural_elastic.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: JSON object with keys 'HoAs' and 'HoP'. Each value is an object with keys: lattice_constant_A (float), bulk_modulus_GPa (float), pressure_derivative (float), cohesive_energy_eV_per_atom (float), C11_GPa (float), C12_GPa (float), C44_GPa (float), Zener_anisotropy_A (float), Poisson_ratio_nu (float), Young_modulus_E_GPa (float), shear_modulus_G_GPa (float).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 4: Supercell DFT force calculation
- Role: process
- Action: Build a 2x2x2 supercell of the conventional unit cell for HoAs and HoP and run a single-point DFT calculation to obtain the Hellmann-Feynman forces on all atoms.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/supercell_forces.json`

### Step 5: Phonon dispersion and dynamical stability
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: Use the PHON code with the forces from the supercell DFT run to compute the phonon dispersion and one-phonon density of states for HoAs and HoP. Record the lowest phonon frequency and state whether any imaginary modes are present (has_imaginary_modes = true if any frequency < -1 cm^-1).
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_02_phonon_stability.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: JSON object with keys 'HoAs' and 'HoP'. Each value is an object with keys: lowest_phonon_frequency_cm-1 (float), has_imaginary_modes (bool).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 6: Quasi-harmonic thermodynamic properties
- Role: scored
- Action: Run the GIBBS program using the energy-volume data from the total-energy step and the previously determined bulk modulus/Poisson's ratio. Compute pressure- and temperature-dependent volume, bulk modulus, thermal expansion coefficient, heat capacities Cv and Cp, entropy, Debye temperature, and Grüneisen parameter over the range 0-2000 K and 0-32 GPa. Output a CSV with the required columns for a set of temperature/pressure points that covers the conditions studied in the paper.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_03_thermodynamics.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: CSV with columns: compound (string, 'HoAs' or 'HoP'), temperature_K (float), pressure_GPa (float), volume_A3 (float), bulk_modulus_GPa (float), Cv_J_molK (float), Cp_J_molK (float), entropy_J_molK (float), thermal_expansion_coefficient_K-1 (float), Debye_temperature_K (float), Gruneisen_parameter (float). Required rows: at least 0, 400, 800, 1200, 1600, 2000 K at 0 GPa, and 0 K at 0, 8, 16, 24, 32 GPa (following Table 4 conditions).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

## How you are scored
Your submission is evaluated by an automated verifier that examines each of the three scored output files independently.

- For the structural/elastic file, the verifier compares your computed lattice constant, bulk modulus, pressure derivative, cohesive energy, and elastic constants against hidden reference values. It also recomputes the derived moduli (Zener anisotropy, Poisson's ratio, Young's modulus, shear modulus) from your Cij and checks that the formulas are applied correctly.

- For the phonon stability file, the verifier requires that the lowest phonon frequency is not significantly negative and that `has_imaginary_modes` is `false`.

- For the thermodynamics CSV, the verifier checks that the file follows the required schema and contains the specified temperature–pressure points. It then verifies that fundamental physical trends are reproduced: the constant-volume heat capacity should approach the Dulong–Petit limit at high temperature, the Debye temperature should decrease with increasing temperature, and the thermal expansion coefficient should increase with temperature at low pressure. Selected numerical values are also compared to hidden reference data.

Each artifact yields a partial reward (0–1), and the final score is a weighted combination of these rewards. Reporting numbers copied from the literature or from a hardcoded oracle without executing the required workflow will not satisfy the scoring criteria.
