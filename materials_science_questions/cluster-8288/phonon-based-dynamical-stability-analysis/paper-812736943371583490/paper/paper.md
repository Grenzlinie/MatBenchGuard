# Hybrid-improper ferroelectric behavior in $Ba_3SiO/Ba_3GeO$ oxide antiperovskite superlattices

Andrés Camilo Garcia-Castro $^{1,a}$, Jorge Hernan Quintero Orozco $^{1}$, and Carlos José Paez Gonzalez $^{2}$

$^{1}$ School of Physics, CIMBIOS Research Group, Universidad Industrial de Santander, Carrera 27 Calle 9, 680002 Bucaramanga, Colombia
$^{2}$ School of Physics, FICOMACO Research Group, Universidad Industrial de Santander, Carrera 27 Calle 9, 680002 Bucaramanga, Colombia

Received 2 April 2019 / Received in final form 4 June 2019
Published online 11 September 2019

**Abstract.** Here, we present a theoretical study of the hybrid-improper ferroelectric (HIF) behavior at the [001] $(Ba_3SiO)_1/(Ba_3GeO)_1$ superlattices. Then, by means of first-principles calculations, we have investi- gated the structural and vibrational modes that allows the existence of the trilinear coupling phenomenon in this antiperovskite oxide heterostructure. The latter coupling, leading to a ferroelectric character in this system. Finally, we show the $xy$ biaxial strain influence into the structure and the ferroelectricity, achieving an enhancement of the spontaneous polarization through this control parameter.

## 1 Introduction

The search for multifunctional properties, where several physical and chemical phenomena can be combined, has been the holy grail in the last two decades in condensed matter field. In this quest, ferroic properties [1], and specifically ferroelectric behavior, have been in the cen- ter of the community's attention [2,3]. Thus, novel sources of ferroelectric behavior, such as geometric and improper coupling, has been demonstrated to be perfect candidates when combining with other properties such as magnetism [4,5] and Rashba spin splitting [6,7]. Here, layered materi- als have shown to be as good, or even better, candidates as bulk materials [8]. Nevertheless, several problems related to their operating temperature and coupling strength are still under debate. On top of that, most of the research has been dedicated to oxide perovskite-like materials with sto- ichiometry, $ABO_3$ leaving aside potential multifunctional candidates within another type of materials.

Very recently, a new family of materials, strongly related to perovskite, has been rediscovered as potential multifunctional systems. This is the case of the antiper- ovskites compounds [9,10]. This kind of systems shows a stoichiometric relation $A_3BO$ when compared to the reg- ular $ABO_3$ oxide perovskites. Interestingly, antiperovskites materials with different anionic X-site have been high- lighted as exciting materials with superconducting prop- erties [11]. Moreover, antiperovskites of the form $Ni_3AB$ have been reported as polar metals holding a strong magnetic ordering [12]. Additionally, three-dimensional topological insulators such as $A_3BiN$ (A = Ca, Sr, and Ba) were also found in the nitrides family (when replacing O by N) [13].

Despite the reported and observed properties, to the best of our knowledge, there are no reports on the pos- sible polar and/or ferroelectric ordering demonstrated. Furthermore, when studying in detail the antiperovskite structure, one can notice that those materials can be sub- ject to the same material's design rules observed and unveiled in their perovskite counterparts. Therefore, a theoretical study on the possible existence of a polar order- ing, within this type of materials, can shed light into the discovery of such properties within the antiperovskite families.

Here, we show by means of first-principles calculations the possible existence of a stable ferroelectric state in the antiperovskite superlattice [001] $(Ba_3SiO]_1/[Ba_3GeO]_1$. This study was carried out by considering the vibra- tional modes and their coupling, allowing with it, the presence of the hybrid-improper ferroelectric behavior in these perovskite-like materials. Therefore, the phonon- dispersion curves and its relation with the structure is analyzed showing the appearance of a polar state in this heterostructure. Finally, the $xy$ biaxial strain effect is investigated as a tuning parameter of the spontaneous polarization.

## 3 Results and discussion

To start, it is important to clarify the perovskite and antiperovskite structural relationship. In the antiperovskite stoichiometry, also known as inverse perovskite, the positions of the A-site and the X-site anions are interchanged. The latter, is giving as a result an $A_{3}BX$ structure. Thus, the octahedra is reversed with the X-site at the center, holding a negative oxidation state, and the A-sites at the corners, holding positive oxidation states in opposite disposition when compare to perovskite oxides [9,10]. Here, for example, the oxidation states at the antiperovskite $Ba_{3}SiO$ are $Ba_{3}^{+2}Si^{+4}O^{-2}$ meanwhile we have $Ba^{+2}Si^{+4}O_{3}^{-2}$ at the perovskite structural arrangement of the Ba-Si-O compound.

At room-temperature, $Ba_{3}SiO$ and $Ba_{3}GeO$ bulk antiperovskite oxides belong to the orthorhombic $Pbnm$ space group (No. 62). In this symmetry, these materials are the result of an octahedral rotational pattern, according to the Glazer's notation [24,25], of $a^{-}a^{-}c^{+}$. At bulk level, after full atomic and electronic relaxation they show a lattice parameters of $a = 7.461\,\text{Å}$, $b = 7.604\,\text{Å}$, $c = 10.671\,\text{Å}$ for $Ba_{3}SiO$ and $a = 7.484\,\text{Å}$, $b = 7.613\,\text{Å}$, $c = 10.698\,\text{Å}$ for $Ba_{3}GeO$ in the orthorhombic $Pbnm$ phase. These lattice parameters are in good agreement with the experimental results, where it was found that $a = 7.532\,\text{Å}$, $b = 7.585\,\text{Å}$, $c = 10.724\,\text{Å}$ and $a = 7.551\,\text{Å}$, $b = 7.591\,\text{Å}$, $c = 10.728\,\text{Å}$ for $Ba_{3}SiO$ and $Ba_{3}GeO$ respectively measured by means of X-ray diffraction [26].

Basically, at the orthorhombic symmetry, the $Pbnm$ phase is a result of the condensation (or freezing) of the modes at $M$ and $R$ from the cubic $Pm\overline{3}m$ high-symmetry structure [27]. Therefore, such type of octahedral rotations and tilting are also expected to appear at the superlattice heterostructure. In order to continue our analysis, a superlattice was build at the high-symmetry structure, which, due to the breaking of symmetry induced by the Si- and Ge-sites is reduced from the $Pm\overline{3}m$ to $P4/mmm$. At this tetragonal phase, shown in Figure 1, we obtained the phonon-dispersion curves also shown in Figure 1. Instantly, it can be noted that there are several unstable branches (here $i\omega$ are shown negative by notation) related to vibrational instabilities. The unstable modes extend along the $X\ (\frac{1}{2}, 0, 0)$, $M\ (\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}, 0)$, and $A\ (\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2})$ high-symmetry points. Interestingly, no trace of polar unstable phonons are observed at the $\Gamma$-point suggesting the absence of a possible polar character through a direct condensation of a mode into the structure. The first $M$-mode $(i\omega = 40\ \text{cm}^{-1})$ depicts an in-phase octahedral rotation around the [001] axis, which is the building axis of the superlattice. The second $M$-mode's $(i\omega = 45\ \text{cm}^{-1})$ eigendisplacements are associated to the O-Ba₆ out-of-phase octahedral tilting around the [110] axis. Finally, a third mode close in frequency $(i\omega = 37\ \text{cm}^{-1})$, appears as a result of an O-Ba₆ octahedral out-of-phase rotation around the [001] axis. The unstable modes observed at the $A$-point are the extension of the unstable negative phonon branches at $M$ which, by symmetry, are also related to the octahedral rotations and tilting. Through the further analysis obtained by means of the AMPLIMODES [28,29] code, we found that the in-phase octahedral rotation $M$-mode has a $M_2^+$ symmetry when a 20 atoms unit cell is considered. The latter is the $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} \times 1$ unit-cell with respect to the 40 atoms $2 \times 2 \times 1$ cell of the $P4/mmm$ high-symmetry phase (denoted by the dashed lines in Fig. 1). Additionally, the out-of-phase octahedral tilting $M$-mode around the [110] axis has a $M_5^-$ symmetry, meanwhile the out-of-phase octahedral rotation around the [001] axis belongs to the $M_4^-$ symmetry. Finally, the $X$-mode belongs to the $X_5^-$. As commented before, the $A$-modes are by symmetry contained into these distortions where their combination give place to the allowed structures with different rotation patterns (see Tab. 1).

After this analysis, we proceeded by condensing the unstable modes. From this procedure and after full relaxation, we obtained the $P4/mbm$ (space group no. 127), $P4/nbm$ (space group no. 125), and $Pmma$ (space group no. 51) phases. Here, one of the $M$ modes led to the in-phase octahedral rotation structure with an $a^o a^o c^+$ (i.e. $P4/mbm$) pattern, while the other $M$ modes drive the structure to the out-of-phase octahedral rotation structure with an $a^o a^o c^-$ (i.e. $P4/nbm$) notation and a tilted structure as $a^- a^- c^o$ (i.e. $Pmma$). When these modes are combined together, the $a^- a^- c^-$ ($P2/c$, space group no. 13) and the $a^- a^- c^+$ ($Pmc2_1$, space group no. 26) structures can be obtained. Although several combinations are allowed, the ground state belongs to the $Pmc2_1$ with a rotation pattern $a^- a^- c^+$ resulting from the condensation of the $M_5^-$ and $M_2^+$ modes. Then, the condensed modes that drive the structure to a polar $Pmc2_1$ are shown in Figure 2. By looking in detail the complete phase transition from the $P4/mmm$ to the $Pmc2_1$, we observed that the overall contributions from the $M_2^+$ and $M_5^-$ modes are $08318\ \text{Å}$ and $1.2063\ \text{Å}$ respectively. Interestingly, we also found that there is a strong polar contribution coming from a $\Gamma_5^- = 0.4566\ \text{Å}$ despite that its direct condensation into the $P4/mmm$ phase is unstable. The latter because this mode shows a positive frequency value at the phonon dispersion at the high-symmetry structure, as commented before, and it is only a consequence of the coupling with other modes.

Such vibrational modes coupling gives rise to the polar character of the antiperovskite superlattices and it is raised by the term $\mathcal{F} \propto P_{\Gamma_{5}^{-}} Q_{M_{2}^{+}} Q_{M_{5}^{-}}$ that appears in the free energy expansion. In this expression, $P_{\Gamma_{5}^{-}}$ denotes the polar mode, and $Q_{M_{2}^{+}}$ and $Q_{M_{5}^{-}}$ are the O-Ba₆ octahedral rotation and tilting modes respectively. This polar mode is basically the result of the uncompensated antipolar displacements of the Si and Ge sites, denoted by the arrows in Figure 2.

On the other hand, with the aim to corroborate that this polar behavior is a vibrational effect that depends on geometric variables, rather than the covalent bonding character as observed in BaTiO₃ [31], we have computed the Born effective charges, $Z^*$, for the ferroelectric $Pmc2_1$, as shown in Table 2. As expected from the HIF where the geometrical effects and the trilinear coupling drive the ferroelectricity, the $Z^*$ charges are close to their nominal value (i.e. +2 for Ba, −4 for Si and Ge, and −2 for O) neglecting the contribution from a covalent-bonding mechanism and confirming the HIF character of this type of superlattices.

The computed polarization, by using the Berry-phase approach [23], shows a spontaneous value of $P = 0.48\,\mu\text{C/cm}^2$. Although at first glance the polarization seems to be rather small, we demonstrated that the design rules for HIFs perovskites can be also applied to antiperovskites A₃BO/A₃B'O. Therefore, a proper tuning of the polarization by choosing carefully the B/B' sites ratio could develop a quite large polar response. For example, we also tested the replacement of Ge by Pb (i.e. Ba₃SiO/Ba₃PbO) in which, the computed spontaneous polarization rise up to $P = 2.5\,\mu\text{C/cm}^2$ with this, demonstrating that the polarization enhancement is possible by carefully selecting different A and B sites into the A₃BO compounds.

Another route to tune and/or enhance the ferroelectric properties, and the electronic ones by controlling the band gap [27,32,33], is by means of epitaxial, or biaxial, strain. Therefore, we have analyzed the structural properties as a function of this parameter. Thus, in order to apply the biaxial strain, we started by supposing a cubic perovskite substrate ($a_c$) as the source of the strain and then, we impose to the $Pmc2_1$ crystal an in-plane lattice parameters of $a = b = \sqrt{2}a_c$. Here, we choose the 0% strain reference to be $a_r = (a_0 + b_0)/2$ where $a_0$ and $b_0$ are the unstrained relaxed cell parameters of the $pmc2_1$ phase and defining the strain amplitude by $\eta = (a - a_r)/a_r$.

In Figure 3 are presented the $M_2^+$, $M_5^-$, and $\Gamma_5^-$ modes contribution to the $Pmc2_1$, as a function of the biaxial strain. Here, we can see that the overall distortion is dominated by the tilting modes with intensities close to $1.2\,\text{Å}$. We observed that the rotation mode is also changed by the epitaxial strain. Additionally, we found that the polar $\Gamma$ mode is strongly influenced by the epitaxial strain increasing in the expansion's strain regime.

In order to better understand the behavior of the modes as a function of biaxial strain, we extracted the difference of the condensed distortion with respect to their value at $\eta = 0\%$ (see Fig. 3). It can be observed that the tilting mode, $M_5^-$, has a quasi-linear behavior as a function of the strain with a difference of distortion going from $-0.013\,\text{Å}$ to $0.020\,\text{Å}$ when going from $-3\%$ to $+3\%$ of strain. This is a change of $-1.1\%$ and $1.6\%$ respectively. Although the tilting mode as a linear behavior, the octahedral rotation, $M_2^+$, has a non-linear trend as a function of $\eta$. Interestingly, whether a compression or an expansion strain is applied, the rotational mode is increased going from $0.017\,\text{Å}$ to $0.075\,\text{Å}$ (representing a percentage difference of $2.1\%$ to $9.3\ \%$) for $-3\%$ to $+3\%$ respectively. Finally, when analyzing the $\Gamma_5^-$ mode, we found that the polar eigendisplacements are the most affected with respect to the epitaxial strain. This, has been also observed at ferroelectric oxide perovskites [34]. Here, the polar mode goes from $-0.046\,\text{Å}$ to $0.084\,\text{Å}$ which represents a difference of $-11.1\%$ to $+20.2\%$. Therefore, the polarization at $+3\%$ of strain increases to $0.57\ \mu\text{C/cm}^2$.

Moreover, it can be also expected a band gap tuning due to the modification of the octahedral rotations and tilting. This band gap modification ($E_g = 0.240\,\text{eV}$ in the unstrained $Pmc2_1$ phase) can be associated to a change in the energy's location of the bonding and anti-bonding

levels between of the Ba:6s and the Ge:4p, Si:3p orbitals [27]. Nonetheless, this study is beyond the scope of this paper and will be addressed in future works.

Finally, we hope that the ferroelectric switching can be achieved experimentally, handling the possible leakage currents that could be present due to the small energy band gap in these materials. However, despite such issue, the possible ferroelectricity in this type of compounds is very attractive and should catch the attention from the experimentalist and theorist. On top of these appealing properties, it is expected that such design rules could also be applied to magnetic antiperovskite materials where, for example, the A- or B-sites are replaced by a mag- netically active atoms. Therefore, a multiferroic and/or magnetoelectric behavior might be accomplished in the inverse-perovskite family of materials which, to the best of our knowledge, has not seen before.

## 4 Conclusions
We studied, by means of first-principles calculations, the possible existence of the hybrid-improper fer- roelectricity in the antiperovskite superlattice [001] $[Ba_{3} SiO]_{1} /[Ba_{3} GeO]_{1}$ . To do so, we analyzed the vibra tional modes and their contribution to the trilinear cou- pling giving rise to a polar behavior. Interestingly, we observed that the rules that apply to perovskites, are also valid to the design of novel ferroelectrics within the antiperovskites oxides family. Moreover, we observed that the $x y$ biaxial strain can tune the polar character which, is enhanced at expansion strain values. Then, we encourage experimentalist to look at the synthesis and characteriza- tion of the properties in this family of materials with the aim to confirm the polar character of the antiperovskite superlattice structure.
