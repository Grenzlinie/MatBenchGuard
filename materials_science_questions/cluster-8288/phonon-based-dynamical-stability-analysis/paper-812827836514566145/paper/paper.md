# Pressure induced structural phase of lithium disulfide with a close to intermediate product character of lithium-sulfur battery

Xiumei Li, $^{\dagger, \ddagger}$ Hengtao Li, $^{\dagger, \ddagger}$ Jiao Chen, $^{\dagger}$ Xinyong Cai, $^{\dagger}$ Hongyan Wang, $^{\dagger}$ Zebin Lao, $^{\dagger}$ Bai Sun, $^{\dagger}$ Li Tao, $^{\&}$ Yuanzheng Chen, $^{*, \dagger}$

$^{\dagger}$ School of Physical Science and Technology, Key Laboratory of Advanced Technologies of Materials, Ministry of Education of China, Southwest Jiaotong University, Chengdu 610031, China.
$^{\&}$ School of Optoelectronic Technology, Chengdu University of Information Technology, Chengdu 610225, China.
$^{\ddagger}$ X. Li and H. Li contributed equally for this work.

**ABSTRACT:** Recently spotlighting on lithium disulfide (Li₂S₂) as an intermediate product of a promising high capacity lithium-sulfur (Li-S) battery, a structure of $P4_2/mnm$ had been proposed for understanding the existence of Li₂S₂ in Li-S battery. Here using first-principles unbiased CALYPSO structure searching calculations, we uncovered a new energetically favored structural phase ($P2_1/c$) of Li₂S₂, being more stable than the $P4_2/mnm$ structure at above 2.78 GPa. This predicted $P2_1/c$ structure possess a similar quasi-molecular S₂ and Li atoms in the interstice of S₂ with $P4_2/mnm$ structure, but having a denser polyhedral packing (LiS₆) with Li atoms six-fold coordination in contract to the four-fold coordinated LiS₄ polyhedron of $P4_2/mnm$. Further, a calculated discharge voltage of 2.34 V was obtained for $2Li^+ + 2e^- + Li_2S_2 \rightarrow 2Li_2S$ and comparable to that of $P4_2/mnm$. Adopting CI-NEB method, we calculated the diffusion barrier and diffusion coefficient of Li ions with a high diffusion coefficient of $5.98{\times}10^{-2} /\text{cm}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$. This character reflects that it can acts as an intermediate product like as $P4_2/mnm$ in a Li-S battery. This work has a value in exposing the pressure-induced phase of Li₂S₂ and enriching the views for understanding in Li-S batteries.

**KEYWORDS:** High pressure, Structure Predictions, lithium disulfide; Li-S battery

## 1. Introduction

As a promising high-energy battery, lithium-sulfur (Li-S) battery has attracted considerable attention in recent years due to its high theoretical capacity (~1675

mAhg⁻¹) [1-3], which is several times of that of typical lithium-ion (Li-ion) battery (e.g. 372 mAh g⁻¹ capacity for graphite [4-6]). It is widely believed that lithium disulfide (Li₂S₂) acts as an intermediate product during the discharge of Li–S battery [3, 6-8], where correlates closely with the structure and electronic proprieties of Li₂S₂. Zaghib's group proposed *P1* structure of Li₂S₂ [9], in which give a discharge voltage (~2.1 V) that matches the lower plateau of the experimental discharge profile of Li-S cells [3]. Yang et al. reported a more stable structure (*P4₂/mnm*) of Li₂S₂ [8] with almost same discharge voltage of 2.1 V. Besides, Siegel's group calculated the Li₂S₂ Gibbs free energy based on a Li₂O₂-like crystal structure (P6₃/mmc) [10]. Pan et al. designed four possible Li₂S₂ structures on the basis of the Inorganic Crystal Structure Database (ISCD) and investigated their electrochemical properties [11]. Here we proposed a new structural phase *P2₁/c* of Li₂S₂ under pressure induced condition and claimed it like as *P4₂/mnm* phase with an intermediate product character.

As pressure can change the bonding patterns of materials leading to the change of structure and electronic properties, therefore, it is essential to investigate the phase stabilities of Li₂S₂ under pressures for further understanding of Li₂S₂. In this work, we investigated HP-phase of Li₂S₂ in pressure range 0 to 100 GPa. The unbiased structural search method based on particle-swarm optimization algorithm (PSO) [12] combined with ab initio density functional total energy calculations were utilized. The efficiency of this approach has been demonstrated in different systems [13-22]. We proposed a new phase of Li₂S₂ which adopts a monoclinic *P2₁/c* structure and shows both dynamics and thermodynamic stability from 3 to 100 GPa. Compared with the reported tetragonal *P4₂/mnm* structure, the HP-phase *P2₁/c* structure exhibits a denser polyhedral packing character but accompanied with similar bonding feature. Using CI-NEB method analysis and calculation shows *P2₁/c* structure has considerably high-diffusion-coefficient which implies Li₂S₂ might play a key role in Li-S batteries during the charge/discharge periods. On the basis of theoretical research, further experimental research and subsequent synthesis of Li₂S₂ might be carried out. We anticipate that this work will be of value in enriching pathways associated with charge and discharge reactions in Li-S systems.

## 2. Computational methods

The developed CALYPSO (Crystal structure AnaLYsis by Particle Swarm Optimization) structure prediction method [23] designed to search for the stable structures of given compounds has been employed for the investigation of phase stability of $\text{Li}_2\text{S}_2$ compound at high pressures. The most significant feature of this methodology is its capability of unbiasedly predicting the ground-state structure with only the knowledge of chemical composition. The details of the search algorithm and its several applications have been described elsewhere [24, 25]. We performed structure predictions of stoichiometric $\text{Li}_2\text{S}_2$ with simulation cell sizes of 1-4 formula units (f.u.) in a pressure range from 0 to 100 GPa. The local structural relaxations and electronic band structure calculations were performed in the framework of density functional theory within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [26] and frozen-core all-electron projector-augmented wave (PAW) method [27], as implemented in the VASP code [28]. The PAW pseudopotentials with $2s^12p^0$ and $3s^23p^4$ valence electrons were adopted for Li and S, respectively. The cutoff energy of 550 eV and appropriate Monkhorst-Pack k-meshes were chosen to ensure that all the enthalpy calculations were well converged to less than 1 meV/atom. The phonon calculations were carried out by using a finite displacement approach through the PHONOPY code [29]. The Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof (HSE06) hybrid functional is employed to improve the bandgap calculation [30]. The diffusion barrier of Li ion was calculated by climbing image nudged elastic band method (CI-NEB) [31, 32]. First, the initial and final states of Li ion diffusion are relaxed, then a series of points are inserted linearly between the initial state and the final state, and then the minimum energy path (MEP) is found by the elastic band method. CI-NEB needs only a few nodes to find a more accurate saddle point, which is a very effective method to calculate ion diffusion barrier.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1 Structure prediction

Using a structure search method based on CALYPSO methodology and density functional total energy calculations, we theoretically investigated the high pressure phases of the $\text{Li}_2\text{S}_2$ compound in a pressure range of 0 to 100 GPa. Several hundred predicted structures were successfully generated and the reported $P4_2/mnm$ structure

[8] is reproduced, validating the method adopted here. At 5 GPa, an energetically favored phase has a monoclinic structure with a $P2_1/c$ symmetry is predicted. We performed the enthalpy curves as a function of pressure in Fig. 1a. It is found that the transition from the $P4_2/mnm$ structure to the $P2_1/c$ structure takes place at 2.78 GPa. The $P2_1/c$ structure shows a thermodynamic stability curve in large pressure range 3 – 100 GPa.

### 3.2 Structural feature

Detailed structural parameters of $P2_1/c$ phase are shown in Table 1. The $P2_1/c$ structural lattice constants generated in the optimization at 5 GPa are a = 3.572 Å, b = 3.6076 Å and c = 9.4047 Å, with Li atoms on the Wyckoff sites $4e$ (0.98638, 0.61013, 0.60328) and S atoms on the site $4e$ (0.49847, 0.38118, 1.13408). The $P2_1/c$ structure adopts a monoclinic structure (Fig. 1c), where the S atoms exist in quasi-molecular $S_2$ and Li atoms take on the interstice of $S_2$ units, being similar to structural feature of

$P4_2/mnm$ phase (Fig. 1b). The S–S length of $S_2$ is 2.13 Å, which is also close to the S-S distance (2.12 Å) of $P4_2/mnm$ and obviously longer than that of gas-phase $S_2$ (1.89 Å). These enlarged intramolecular bonds are attributed to the negatively charged $S_2$ molecules by charges from Li transferred to $S_2$, as a result, the transferred charges occupy the antibonding $\pi^*$ orbitals of $S_2$ to lengthen the intramolecular bond length. Our results indicate that the $S_2$ Unit in $P2_1/c$-Li$_2$S$_2$ is almost similar to that in $P4_2/mnm$-Li$_2$S$_2$. A major discrepancy between them is that the $P2_1/c$ phase has different atoms stacking sequence. In contrast to a Li four-fold coordination with S atoms of the $P4_2/mnm$ structure, each Li atom of the $P2_1/c$ structure forms a six-fold coordination with S atoms exhibiting a denser polyhedral packing. A polyhedral occupancy ($\Sigma$Vpoly/Vcell) in the $P2_1/c$ structure (~0.51) can be evaluated to be larger than in the $P4_2/mnm$ structure (~0.26). This change of polyhedral density usually associates with volume contraction. To trace the fact, the volume compression curves of the $P4_2/mnm$$\rightarrow$$P2_1/c$ were performed in Fig. 2a. As expect, the volume contrast between the $P4_2/mnm$ and $P2_1/c$ is considerable and reaches $-19.4\%$, which manifests a first-order character for this phase transition.

As well known, the enthalpy has two different components represented as H = E + PV, where E is the electronic total energy and PV can be expressed as the energy contribution of pressure and volume effect. To clarify the thermodynamic details of the phase changes, we separately investigated contributions of these two terms as shown in Fig. 2b. It is found that there is a small reduction in $\Delta$E while $\Delta$(PV) greatly decreases with pressure. This result indicates that the enthalpy crossover between these two phases is primarily due to the volume contraction, resulting in the energetically favored $P2_1/c$ phase. To assess the dynamical structural stability of the $P2_1/c$ structure, the phonon spectra calculation was performed at desired pressure using the finite-displacement method. As shown in Fig. 3, no imaginary frequency was found for this structure, which indicated that it is dynamically stable. Compare to the partial phonon density of states (PDOS) of the $P4_2/mnm$ structure [8], the $P2_1/c$ phase has a similar vibration character in low and high frequency region by mainly affected by S ions, while a difference is that the middle frequency region of $P2_1/c$

phase is attributed to the mixed of Li and S ions as shown in Fig. 3.

### 3.3 Electronic property

Electronic localization function (ELF) is the most straightforward method to judge the chemical bonding pattern, as it can map the likelihood of finding an electron between neighborhood atoms. In general, ELF values above 0.5 between the nearest atoms indicate a covalent bonding pattern, whereas smaller ELF values (< 0.5) correspond to ionic and metallic bonds. As illustrated in Fig. 4a, there is obvious electron localization between S atoms of $S_2$ unit, indicating a strong S-S covalent

bonding character, while the bonding between Li and S atoms is quite ionic. Based on the Bader analyses, the calculated Bader atomic charge of S atom in $P2_1/c$ is 0.84 which is similar to that of $P4_2/mnm$ structure (0.87 Bader and 0.84 Mulliken) [8]. To investigate the electronic properties of $P2_1/c$-$Li_2S_2$ structure, the electronic band structures and the projected density of states (DOS) for $Li_2S_2$ are calculated. As illustrated in Fig. 4b, the $P2_1/c$ structure exhibits semiconductor character with an indirect bandgap of 1.74 eV using the PBE calculations. The bandgap of our $P2_1/c$ structure is close to that of $P4_2/mnm$ structure (about 1.80 eV) due to their similarity of structural features. While in Pan et al.'s work, the calculated bandgaps of $P6_3/mmc$, Cmca, and Immm structures are 1.098, 1.568, and 0.225 eV, respectively [11]. It indicates that the electronic properties of $Li_2S_2$ are closely related to the structural configuration. Furthermore, since DFT-PBE often underestimates the bandgaps, we adopted a more accurate hybrid functional of HSE06 for band gap calculations for comparison. We note that these two methods provide almost the same band shapes near the Fermi level, but the HSE06 give a more precise bandgap with 2.82 eV. Analysis of the projected DOS suggests that the states around the Fermi level arise mainly from the $3p$ orbitals of the S atoms. And we can further obtain that the $3p$-electrons coming from the $S_2$ ions play an important role in the electronic properties.

The rechargeable Li–S battery has received a great deal of attention in the last few years due to its specific energy being significantly greater than the Li-ion battery. Recent research reports the $Li_2S_2$ compound for its $P4_2/mnm$ phase as an intermediate product in Li–S battery. As well known, the discharge voltage of $Li_2S_2$ is a very important parameter for understand the role of $Li_2S_2$ in Li – S batteries. Thus, here we further calculated the discharge voltage of $P2_1/c$-$Li_2S_2$ based on the difference in Gibbs free energy: $\Delta$G (E,V, S) = $\Delta$E + P$\Delta$V - T$\Delta$S. The specific discharge voltage is given by V = [G ($Li_2S_2$) + G ($Li_{metal}$) – G ($Li_2S$)]/$q$; where $q$ is the charge that is transferred during the reaction. Based on the electrochemical reaction $Li^+ + 2e^- + Li_2S_2$→$Li_2S$, the calculated discharge voltage of $P2_1/c$-$Li_2S_2$ was found to be 2.34 V, which is close to the value (2.12 V) of $P4_2/mnm$-$Li_2S_2$ phase and the lower plateau of the discharge profile of a Li–S battery [3]. This result indicates that $P2_1/c$-$Li_2S_2$ can be as an alternative phase in the intermediate product of the discharge.

### 3.4 Lithium migration pathways

![](./images/812827836514566145_7.jpg)

Fig.5. Migration energy barriers calculated with CI-NEB method for six typical hops between neighboring lithium sites detected from the trajectories of Li ions. The positions of these paths are shown in the lattice by black lines connecting two Li sites and labeled from 1 to 6.

To evaluate the diffusion of Li ions in the $P2_1/c$-$Li_2S_2$ phase, the diffusion barrier ($E_a$) and diffusion coefficient (D) were discussed here. Firstly, the hops between two

neighboring Li sites and the migration energy barrier of each path are analyzed and calculated by CI-NEB method [31, 32]. As shown in Fig. 5a, six diffusion paths are proposed and labeled as path-1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The calculated energy barriers for lithium migration along paths 1- 6 are 0.73, 0.81, 0.93, 1.15, 0.051, and 0.20 eV, respectively (Fig. 5b-g). Obviously, the diffusion along the path-5 and path-6 directions is the energetically favored paths with a lower barrier. Especially, lithium migration along path-5 has the lowest energy. The diffusion coefficients of the carrier Li are calculated by the equation as $D \approx d^{2} v_{0} \exp (-Ea/k_{B}T)$ and are shown in Table 2. It is found that the path-5 has the highest diffusion coefficient with $5.98×10^{-2}/cm^{2}s^{-1}$ due to its extremely low diffusion barrier while the other migration pathways have much higher energy barriers resulting in very low probability of Li ion migration. Compared with the diffusion coefficients of $\sim 10^{-6}cm^{2}s^{-1}$ for Li ion diffusion in $Li_{x}C_{6}$ correspond with energy barriers of 0.308 eV [33] and $\sim 10^{-2}cm^{2}s^{-1}$ for Li ion diffusion on monolayer phosphorene with energy barriers of 0.08 eV [34], the $P2_{1}/c-Li_{2}S_{2}$ has considerably high-diffusion-coefficient property which has the same order of magnitude as phosphorene. It is noted that the diffusion barriers calculated by using zaghib's $P1$ structure [9] of $Li_{2}S_{2}$ are 0.026 and 0.148 eV [6], which is similar to the migration energy barrier along path-5 (0.05 eV) and path-6 (0.20 eV) of our $P2_{1}/c-Li_{2}S_{2}$ structure. Therefore, such a high-diffusion coefficient of $P2_{1}/c-Li_{2}S_{2}$ can be expected in the applying of Li–S batteries.

### 4. Conclusion

Using a structure search method based on CALYPSO methodology and density functional total energy calculations, we investigated HP-phase of $Li_2S_2$ in pressure range 0 to 100 GPa and uncovered the new energetically favored structural phase $(P2_1/c)$ of $Li_2S_2$ is more stable than the $P4_2/mnm$ structure at above 2.78 GPa. In comparison to $P4_2/mnm$ structure, the predicted $P2_1/c$ structure possesses a similar quasi-molecular $S_2$ but has a denser polyhedral packing. Its Li atom has six-fold coordination with S atom in contract to the Li four-fold coordination in the $P4_2/mnm$. Through a calculated discharge voltage for $2Li^+ + 2e^- + 2Li_2S_2 \rightarrow 2Li_2S$, a close value in $P2_1/c$ structure is comparable to the reported one in $P4_2/mnm$. In addition, for the $P2_1/c$ structure, we calculated its diffusion barrier and diffusion coefficient of Li ions and revealed it with a high diffusion coefficient of $5.98{\times}10^{-2}/\text{cm}^2\text{s}^{-1}$. These characters reflect that our predicted $P2_1/c$ structure can acts as an intermediate product like as $P4_2/mnm$ in a Li–S battery. These results will be of value in understanding the pressure–induced phases and yield new insights for $Li_2S_2$.

**Highlights:**

- A new pressure-induced phase ($P2_1/c$) of $Li_2S_2$ is proposed and compared with $P4_2/mnm$.

- The $P2_1/c$ structure possesses quasi-molecular $S_2$ and forms Li six-fold coordination.

- The discharge voltage was obtained for $2Li^+ + 2e^- + Li_2S_2 \rightarrow 2Li_2S$ in $P2_1/c$ structure.

- A high diffusion coefficient of Li ions was revealed in the $P2_1/c$ structure.

- The $P2_1/c$ structure exhibits close to intermediate product character of Li-S battery.
