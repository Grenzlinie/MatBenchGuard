## ABSTRACT

First-principles calculation is performed to study the structural stabilities, elastic, mechanical, and lattice dynamical properties for AlNiAs and AlNiSb novel half Heusler alloys. The LiAlSi ($\text{C1}_b$-type) structure of AlNiX has been explored in three atomic arrangements (AlNiX-type1, AlNiX-type II, AlNiX-type III) and found that type III is energetically more favorable for both AlNiAs and AlNiSb. Also, the computed ferromagnetic (FM), non-magnetic (NM), and anti-ferromagnetic (AFM) states for AlNiAs and AlNiSb advocates NM states. The calculated formation energy indicates they possess thermodynamical stability. The calculated electronic properties indicate that both half Heusler alloys are metallic. The computed elastic constants follow the requirements of mechanical stability. The calculated mechanical properties predict that AlNiAs is more ductile than AlNiSb. The mechanical anisotropy is also predicted for both AlNiAs and AlNiSb by Zener anisotropic factor. The bulk modulus, shear modulus, Young's modulus, and Poisson's ratio are computed to discuss their elastic properties. The calculated positive phonon frequencies for both alloys indicate their stability in the LiAlSi-type phase. Raman and infrared phonon modes have also been identified for both the alloys.

## 1. Introduction

In recent times, much attention has been given to the Heusler alloys due to their applications in the electronic and optical industry in the formation of shape memory devices, highly precise microbalances, scanning microscopes, and for the detection of sound wave specifically in sound navigation and ranging (SONAR) [1–5]. Many of them are metallic systems and some of them possess semiconducting properties. The number of valence electron count in these Heusler alloys is of vigorous significance. Numerous physical properties of these alloys can be very well estimated from this valence electron count. It is worth to mention here that recently studied Half-Heusler alloy 'AlNiP' with 18 valence electron count shows non-magnetic properties, which are mainly dependent on the valence electron count [6]. Therefore, it is important to investigate the structural, magnetic, electronic properties of unreported half-Heusler alloys [7]. The stability of the Heusler alloys in different phases and transitions of alloys from one phase to another phase can be best approximated by the study of phonon dynamics. The phonon frequencies of materials also help to determine the thermal and electrical conductivity. Therefore, investigation of the phonon properties of half-Heusler alloys has been undertaken in this work. Moreover, recently predicted Ni-based half-Heusler (HH) alloys such as TiNiSn, ZrNiSn, and HfNiSn have drawn the attention of research and scientific community due to its fantastic applications in spintronics and thermo-electronics [8]. They have the general formula XYZ, where X and Y are related to the elements of transition metals and Z belongs to the main group element. These HH alloys mainly crystallize in MgAgAs-type solids or LiAlSi-type with face-centered cubic lattice. The crystal structure of HH alloys can be considered as filling up of Y atoms into the rock-salt structure of XZ atoms.

It was the year 1903 when the Heusler alloys were discovered. Although during that time, the study regarding the crystal structure of the alloy was not possible due to a lack of availability of measuring tools. After the discovery of Heusler alloys, in the year 1934, Bradley and Rodgers [2] have found that Heusler alloy '$\text{Cu}_2\text{MnAl}$' is an ordered structure with $\text{L2}_1$ phase, mainly made up of four interpenetrating face-centered cubic lattices. In the year 1969, Webster reported the structural as well as magnetic properties of Heusler alloys [3]. After the passage of almost two to three decades, the research on the various properties of Heusler alloys has boosted its speed in 1983 with the discovery of half-metallic magnetism in Ni-based Half Heusler alloy NiMnSb [4]. After this discovery to date, a large number of half-Heusler

The HH alloys can be classified into two categories. First, HH alloys with a total number of eight electrons present in the valence shell; Secondly, with eighteen electrons. It is found that various compounds with 18 valence electrons are potential candidates for thermoelectric materials. The Ni-based rare earth HH compounds with MNiPn [9,10] show good thermoelectric properties. Mahanti et al. and others [10,11] have also studied the structural stability of Ni-based HH compounds. Consequently, the study of novel HH compounds such as AlNiX (X = As and Sb) will be interesting with 18 valence electrons. Although the present compounds are the part of predicted stable compounds [7], the detailed analysis of electronic structure, elastic, mechanical, and phonon properties are still scarce in the literature. Even though no experimental or theoretical work related to the elastic and mechanical properties of these alloys has been available, therefore, it is the hope that the present investigation will stimulate the experimentalists to synthesis these alloys for suitable application.

## 2. Computational Methodology

The Quantum Espresso software package was used to determine the structural and optimized calculations within the framework of density functional theory [13,14]. For the analysis of interaction among inner electrons, nuclei, outermost electrons and to calculate exchange-correlational functions, norm-conserving pseudopotentials of Troullier and Martins type with generalized gradient approximation of PBE-GGA were used [15,16]. For the selection of occupation distribution, the smearing of Marzari-Vanderbilt [17] was applied. The crystal structures were optimized with a kinetic energy cutoff of 120 Ry for both AlNiX (X = As and Sb) and the electronic charge density was expanded on a basis cut-off up to 480 Ry. For the integration through the Brillouin zone and to solve the Kohn-Sham equation Monkhorst-Pack [18] system of $12 \times 12 \times 12$ k-point mesh by altering from the origin (0 0 0) is used. This value of k-point mesh was chosen to be sufficient for the convergence. The electronic calculations such as band structure, the density of states, and Fermi surfaces were calculated by applying density functional theory. After calculating the electronic properties within the density functional theory, density-functional perturbation theory [19] was implemented to further investigate the lattice dynamics. The 'q'-point mesh of $2 \times 2 \times 2$ was applied for finding the three dynamical matrices. These dynamical matrices were used to investigate the inter-atomic force constants which further determine the phonons.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Structural Properties

The study of the stable crystal structure of the compound is very crucial, as all the physical properties of the entire material depend on it. Therefore, for finding the ground state properties of the material, the different atomic arrangements of the conventional unit cell of AlNiX (X = As and Sb) are studied by considering AlNiX-type I, AlNiX-type II, and AlNiX-type III phase. The different atomic arrangements corresponding to different phases are presented in Table 1.

In AlNiX-type I phase the atomic location are 4c (0.25, 0.25, 0.25), 4b (0.5, 0.5, 0.5), and 4a (0, 0, 0); AlNiX-type II phase with atomic positions at 4a(0, 0, 0), 4b(0.5, 0.5, 0.5), and 4c (0.25, 0.25, 0.25); AlNiX-type III phase with atomic arrangements at 4b (0.5, 0.5, 0.5), 4c (0.25, 0.25, 0.25), and 4a (0, 0, 0). The crystal structure for the conventional unit cells of AlNiX-type I, AlNiX-type II, and AlNiX-type III phases are shown in Fig. 1(a-f). The crystal structure of these alloys can be comprehended as interpenetrating of three face-centered cubic lattices.

To investigate the exact ground-state phase with the perfect magnetic state of the HH AlNiXalloys, the minimization of energy as a function of volume towards three distinct atomic occupations for each magnetic state (Ferromagnetic (FM), Non-Magnetic (NM) and Anti-Ferromagnetic (AFM)) is performed for both HH AlNiX alloys. The obtained results are depicted in Figs. 2 and 3. These variations of energy as a function of volume for AlNiX are then fitted to Birch-Murnaghan's equation of state to determine the lattice constant [22]. The calculated lattice constants with bulk modulus $B_0$(GPa), pressure derivative bulk modulus $B_0'$ and the calculated total energy values $E_{tot}$ (Ry.) with different structure type phases (AlNiX - Type I, AlNiX - Type II, and AlNiX -Type III) along with each magnetic state (FM, NM, and AFM) for the both HH AlNiAs and AlNiSb alloys are enumerated in Table 2.

From Table 2, by making the comparison of calculated total energy values for three different structural atomic positions along with different magnetic states, it can be noticed that the 'AlNiX-type III' phase with 'NM' ground state has the lowest energy among the three conceivable atomic occupations in the respective magnetic state and can be observed from the Figs. 2 and 3. Therefore, 'AlNiX-Type III' with 'NM' State is considered as the most stable phase at ambient conditions.

From Table 2, it is found that the calculated values of lattice constants are found to be in excellent agreement with other similar structured HH alloys [23]. Since the structural parameter such as Bulk modulus signifies the strength of the material or in other words, it determines the resistance to compressibility; the pressure derivative of bulk modulus helps in finding the thermoplastic properties of the material. Hence both parameters are of utmost importance to study. It is also found that the calculated values of bulk modulus are higher for AlNiAs than AlNiSb. This may be due to the smaller value of the lattice parameter of AlNiAs than AlNiSb. This is because, with the increase in volume, bulk modulus decreases. Unlike bulk modulus, the pressure derivative of bulk modulus follows the opposite trend to that of AlNiAs and AlNiSb.

Moreover, the possibility of synthesis of the compound can be determined by finding its formation enthalpy. The formation enthalpy provides an idea about the alloying ability of a compound. Hence, the formation enthalpy of present HH alloy AlNiX is determined by using the following given formula [24]:

$$\Delta H = E(AlNiX) - (E(Al) + E(Ni) + E(X) )$$

Where, E(AlNiX) is the energy of the compound at equilibrium and E (Al), E(Ni), and E(X) are the total energy of constituents elements with Aluminum (Al) and Nickel (Ni) in a face-centered cubic structure and 'X'= Arsenic (As) and Antimony (Sb) in a rhombohedral structure at ambient condition. The calculated formation enthalpy of both the AlNiX (NM)-type III structure is presented in Table 2 for both HH alloys. The negative value of formation enthalpy for both alloys signifies that these alloys can be synthesized experimentally.

The bands present near the Fermi energy level show the dynamical behavior of electrons and holes in the vicinity of the Fermi energy level. Moreover, the shape of the Fermi surface is crucial in determining the superconductivity of various HH alloys [27]. ### 3.4. Elastic and mechanical properties

Currently, quantum mechanical calculations have reached a sufficient degree of accuracy to provide the data related to various physical properties, whereas the measurements with the experimental approach are not possible or a cumbersome process. Under all these conditions, the theoretical study of elastic constant values seems to be good for testing the elastic stability of the material theoretically. The study of elastic properties of the material provides an opportunity to determine the interatomic forces between atoms under the influence of stress and strain, also it plays its dominant contribution in the applications of mechanical engineering. Moreover, these elastic properties are responsible for the macroscopic response of the material. To evaluate the reaction of the materials under discrete conditions in which experimental tests seem to be impractical, the energy strain calculations can be used. Hence, the elastic and mechanical properties for novel HH alloys AlNiX (X = As and Sb) have been reported for the first time using the total energy-strain method as outlined in refs [29,30]. As both HH alloys AlNiAs and AlNiSb are face-centered cubic systems, therefore only three independent elastic constants such as $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{44}$ are calculated by using stress-strain theory along with the optimized value of lattice-constant. Among these elastic constants, '$C_{11}$' represents the presence of stiffness at odds with principle strains, $C_{12}$ describes the transverse expansion, and $C_{44}$ signals the resistance against the shear deformation. The calculated values of these elastic constants of AlNiAs and AlNiSb are presented in Table 4.

### 3.5. Lattice Dynamics

The zone center phonon modes of AlNiX (X = As and Sb) are analyzed as they play a key role in infrared absorption and Raman scattering. Since the HH alloys, AlNiX has a LiAlSi-type (Cl<sub>b</sub>) crystal structure. The primitive unit cell of these HH alloys consist of three atoms and possess only one formula unit with a stoichiometry ratio 1: 1: 1. Therefore, both alloys have a total number of three atoms, namely Al (Aluminum), Ni(Nickel), As(Arsenic)/ Sb(Antimony). Corresponding to the three atoms in the unit cell, there is a total number of nine phonon modes, three corresponding to each atom. Out of these nine phonon modes, three are acoustical and the remaining six are optical. The six optical phonon modes can be divided into two triply degenerate phonon modes. The irreducible representation for these six optical phonon modes can be written as follows:

$$\Gamma_{optical}=2T_{2}$$

These two optical phonon modes are triply degenerate and are found to be a mixture of Raman and infrared phonon modes. According to the rule of the mutual exclusion principle, if the vibrating molecule pos- sesses a center of symmetry then it will execute Raman phonon mode, otherwise, it will be either infrared active or a mixture of Raman and infrared active phonon mode. Therefore, the Eigen-displacement vector shown in Fig. 10 (a-b) and (c-d) for AlNiAs and AlNiSb respectively describe the complete description for the identification of these modes. It can be seen from Fig. 10 the arrows connected to the atoms denote the corresponding direction of vibration. Moreover, the magnitude of length of the associated arrow directs to its contribution of vibration in particular phonon mode. If the magnitude of the length is large, then the contribution of that atom will also be more and if no arrow is connected to the atom, it shows that their contribution is insignificant in particular phonon mode. ### 4. Conclusion

It is concluded that using density functional theory the structural and electronic properties of AlNiAs and AlNiSb HH alloys are investigated. The structural properties confirm the stability of both alloys in the type- III crystal structure phase along with NM State. Computed electronic band structures describe that these alloys are metallic. The stress-strain theory is implemented to investigate the elastic and mechanical properties of AlNiAs and AlNiSb. The calculated values of the elastic constants confirmed the elastic stability of both alloys. Moreover, from the Cauchy's pressure values, it is found that AlNiAs is more ductile than AlNiSb. The lattice dynamical study of AlNiX (X = As and Sb) is performed using density functional perturbation theory, which confirms the dynamical stability of both alloys in the cubic phase. Moreover, Raman and infrared phonon modes related to both AlNiAs and AlNiSb are also identified at the zone center.
