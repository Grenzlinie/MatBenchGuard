## Abstract

Intensive calculations in the framework of the density functional theory (DFT) scheme have been carried out in order to predict accurately the elastic behavior and the equilibrium crystal parameters for the $\text{MSiP}_2$ (M=Be, Mg, Cd, Zn and Hg) compounds. Emphasis is placed on the trends of the dynamical stability and anisotropic behavior related to elastic components. A rigorous analysis of linear compressibility in the chalcopyrite structures reveals their directional anisotropic behavior along the $c$-axis. As pressure is applied, the entitled compounds have shown polymorphic sequences from tetragonal, orthorhombic to metallic cubic structures. Also, we pay attention to the evolution of the tetragonal distortion which plays an important role in the change of the bonding nature through these phase transitions.

**Keywords**: *ab initio* calculation, High-pressure, Anisotropy, Phase transition

### 1. Introduction

Efforts towards understanding the hydrostatic pressure effect on the solid is now receiving increasingly attention. The main reason behind is their growing importance in the development of very high pressure techniques involving mineralogy or metallurgy fields. In a preceding paper [1], we took a special point of view to investigate chemical relevant magnitudes that affects local properties of five chalcopyrites. The results show unequivocally that partitioning the observables of physical systems into additive microscopic contributions [2, 3] shed light on the transition mechanism that occurs under pressure. Local compressibilities [4] or local bulk moduli have also provided a sound and important role for recognizing the influence of pressure on the cation/anion in compressed crystals. However, to correctly understand these phenomena, it soon became clear that it was necessary to take into account and to interest also to the influence on the global measured quantities.

One primary interesting study conducted on the five $\text{MSiP}_2$ ($\text{M=Be, Mg, Cd, Zn}$ and $\text{Hg}$) crystals in our early work consisted in the investigation of the broken or the formation of Si-P bond in light of Baders harpoon mechanism [5]. This investigation is intimately linked to the IR/Raman and vibrational frequencies [6, 7]. Although a review of all Raman frequency studies done lies outside of the purpose of this work, we should mention that the longitudinal/transversal splitting in a phonon dispersion calculations, is a direct consequence of the application of pressure on a crystal [8]. The pressure involves a decrease of the zone-boundary TA phonon frequencies, that leads to the existence of a negative Grüneisen coefficient. It has been well established from Raman measurements and recently by neutron experiments that the energy of the low-frequency polar mode decreases with increasing pressure.

Ternary pnictides with the chalcopyrite (CH) structure have traditionally demanded attention to photovoltaic applications [9], although first-principles studies of the above

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. properties at zero Pressure

Let us begin by studying the stability of our compounds. For that, a full optimization of the unit cell geometry and atomic positions were carried out in our electronic structure calculations for each polymorph. Optimized lattice and equation of state (EOS) parameters (bulk modulus ($B$)) evaluated at zero pressure are collected in table 1. From our investigated results, it turned out that the chalcopyrite structure is mechanically the more stable for all five compounds. As comparison with available experimental data [33, 10, 35] at room temperature, a premier remark noticed from table 1 is that theory yields greater lattice parameter and lower bulk moduli, with discrepancies that are a little bit larger than expected for GGA-type calculations. Analysis of stability of a phase is straightforward. It suffices to study its dynamical stability through its phonon frequency dispersion. Attention should be paid on the phonon dispersion curves at zero pressure; as plotted on figure 2. Since the primitive unit cell of chalcopyrite contains eight atoms, one expects 24 dispersion curves, with three acoustic modes and 21 optical modes. As seen in figures 2, along the N-X direction all dispersion curves are rather wide. They provide the possibility for a large dispersion. In addition, by symmetry, all modes along N-X are doubly degenerate. The phonon branches along the $\Gamma$-P line are singly and doubly degenerate. It is seen from Figures 2 that $\mathrm{BeSiP}_{2}, \mathrm{MgSiP}_{2}, \mathrm{CdSiP}_{2}, \mathrm{HgSiP}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{ZnSiP}_{2}$ have very similar dispersion curves and the corresponding density of states. The calculated phonon dispersion curves do not contain soft mode at any wave vectors, which confirms dynamically their stability in the $\mathrm{CH}$ structure. Therefore, we can expect that the optimized geometry of the $\mathrm{BeSiP}_{2}$, $\mathrm{MgSiP}_{2}, \mathrm{CdSiP}_{2}, \mathrm{HgSiP}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{ZnSiP}_{2}$ lies at least in its local minimum energy. The frequency range of atomic vibrations in $\mathrm{MgSiP}_{2}, \mathrm{CdSiP}_{2}, \mathrm{HgSiP}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{ZnSiP}_{2}$ are in accord with the typical $100-500 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ for the chalcopyrite compounds. A useful approach to examining elastic anisotropy is to use mathematically-derived surfaces that change their shape from one crystallographic direction to the next. The extent to which the surfaces change shape is indicative of anisotropy [40]. For this aim, fourth-order elastic compliance tensor, $S_{ijkl}$, has been calculated; while the Young's modulus can be obtained by using a purely normal stress. Here the stiffness tensor expresses the stress tensor in terms of the strain tensor as $\sigma_{ij}=C_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{kl}$ and the compliance tensor is the inverse of the stiffness tensor and interprets the strain tensor in terms of the stress tensor as $\varepsilon_{ij}=S_{ijkl}\sigma_{kl}$. Anisotropic direction can be represented as a point on the unit sphere (unit vector $a$), and advantageously by two angles $\theta(0,\pi)$ and $\varphi(0,2\pi)$. However, shear modulus and Poisson's ratio requires another perpendicular direction (another unit vector, $b$, perpendicular to the first) characterized by the angle $\chi(0,2\pi)$. The linear compressibility $\beta$ follows a slightly different scheme but is even simpler to compute. It is obtained by applying an isotropic stress (corresponding to pressure $p$) in tensor form, so that $\varepsilon_{ij}=-pS_{ijkk}$ and by considering that the extension in direction $a$ is $\varepsilon_{ij}a_i a_j$, and that therefore $\beta(\theta,\varphi)=S_{ijkk}a_i a_j$. Depending on two directions, the shear ratio ($G$) and Poisson's ratio ($v$) are obtained from the analytical expressions [40]:

$$
G(\theta, \varphi, \chi)=\frac{1}{a_{i} b_{j} a_{k} b_{l} S_{i j k l}}, v(\theta, \varphi, \chi)=-\frac{a_{i} a_{j} b_{k} b_{l} S_{i j k l}}{a_{i} a_{j} a_{k} a_{l} S_{i j k l}} \tag{2}
$$

## 4. Conclusions

We have addressed a global description of the zero Kelvin temperature of structural and dynamical properties of $MSiP_{2}$ (M=Be, Mg, Cd, Zn and Hg) crystals using first-principles strategy. Thus, we have proved the dynamical stability of the investigated compounds through phonon frequency dispersion calculations. Some specific facts have emerged from elastic computation: $BeSiP_{2}$ shows rather isotropic feature compared with the anisotropic $MgSiP_{2}$ compound. According to our linear compressibility results, the chalcopyrite phase of our compounds are rather deformable in the (100) plane.

The $B/Y$ ratio, proposed by Pugh, provides a rule for predicting ductility/brittleness. In fact, a material is brittle if the $(B/Y)$ ratio is less than 1.75. Among the chalcopyrite compounds studied here, the $MgSiP_{2}$ is the one with the lowest hardness/strength ratio, due to its smallest Bulk modulus. Also, the covalent polar nature of the bonding in this chalcopyrite crystal is far from being a surprise. Rather, it is mostly an assumed condi- tion associated to the tetrahedral coordination. Calculation of Poisson's ratio from elastic

constants confirms this nature, but predicts also that $BeSiP_2$ and $ZnSiP_2$ are ionic. Therefore, we want to stress the weakness of predicting bonding nature from Poisson's ratio approach comparing with the more sophisticated one; like Electron Localisation Function (ELF) analysis or Atoms in Molecule (AIM) theory, which both stipulate that $ZnSiP_2$ is covalent (see[1]). In addition, it has been shown that pressure-driven phase transitions occur in these compounds. A polymorphic sequence of $I\overline{4}2d \longmapsto Pna2_1 \longmapsto Fm\overline{3}m$ has been identified at intermediate pressures. According to the evolution of $c/a$ ratio as a function of pressure, which means that the dependence on bond lengths is strikingly clear. *Though our calculations are generally in good agreement with these predictions, they still need further experimental confirmation. In this sense, we think that is it necessary to mention that we should take the results for the $BeSiP_2$ and $HgSiP_2$ with caution. These compounds are not yet synthesized. Although the ab initio calculations have confirmed their stability, they remain as hypothetical compounds.* ... *) However, several interesting mappings between atoms and polyhedra can reveal the direct influence of pressure on the bonding nature of these compounds. We think that there is room in solid state thinking for the predictions presented here, and that a judicious use of other concepts, like microscopic analysis of elastic constants [3], will give rise to ways of correlating chemical behavior and chemical structure in this family of compounds.
