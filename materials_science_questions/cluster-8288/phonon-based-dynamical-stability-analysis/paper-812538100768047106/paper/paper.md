## Abstract:
The structural, elastic and related properties, electronic properties, thermodynamic, and vibrational properties of the $\text{V}_3\text{X}$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) intermetallic compounds using the plane wave method within the density functional theory are studied and results are discussed in detail. There is a good agreement between the calculated lattice constants, formation enthalpies and available experimental and theoretical data. The band structure calculations predict that these compounds in the A15 phase behave metallic features. Moreover, the studied compounds are highly anisotropic and ductile in nature. According to the calculated values of formation enthalpy, V-X and V-V bond lengths, electronic density of states at the Fermi level, and melting temperature, $\text{V}_3\text{Co}$ compound is more stable than other compounds. The elastic constants of these compounds and their related quantities such as bulk modulus ($B$), shear modulus ($G$), Cauchy pressure ($C_\text{P}$), B/G ratio, Young's modulus ($E$), Kleinman's parameter ($\zeta$), elastic anisotropy ($A$), Poisson's ratio ($v$), Lame's constants ($\lambda$ and $\mu$), and Vicker's hardness ($H_V$) have been calculated using the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation. Besides, the anisotropic elastic properties are pictured in two and three-dimensional shapes ($2D$ and $3D$) for Young's modulus, linear compressibility, shear modulus, Poisson's ratio. It has been found that $\text{V}_3\text{Fe}$, $\text{V}_3\text{Co}$, and $\text{V}_3\text{Ni}$ compounds are vibrationally and mechanically stable.

### 1. Introduction

Intermetallic compounds are crystalline compounds between metals and ceramics BP simplestENERve射  缺席Rent [ Gera StraightJF specialThe bond strength between different atoms that form intermetallics are stronger than the bond strength between the same atoms. It is larger than bond strength and therefore intermetallics form special crystal structures with regular atomic distribution, which are preferentially translated with different atoms.

The binary intermetallic compounds with A15 cubic crystal structure are of very much interest due to their many practical and technological applications. Many of these compounds have superconductivity at around 20 K, which is comparatively high, and remain superconductive in magnetic fields of tens of teslas. This kind of superconductivity is an important area of study as it has several practical applications. The A15 structure compounds are in the form of $A_3B$ crystal structure where A is transition metal elements and B is any element in the periodic table. The A15 structure compounds are first discovered in 1933 as $Cr_3Si$ [1] but not very much studied until the vanadium-based A15 structured compound $V_3Si$ has been discovered in 1953. Because this study showed that the $V_3Si$ compound has superconducting properties at 17 K [2]. After that discovery studies about these compounds have boosted up.

Vanadium-based refractory materials are widely used in aviation and electronics fields for various technological applications, especially due to their physical properties at high temperatures. Besides, these compounds have interesting mechanical and thermal properties such as high-temperature resistance, high melting temperatures, good oxidation resistance, and stability toward neutron radiation [3-5]. Because refractory metals are a class of materials possessing extraordinarily high-temperature properties, they are perennial contenders for high-temperature nuclear applications. Therefore, vanadium-based intermetallics have been extensively studied in many theoretical and experimental studies over the past 60 years [6-40].

From an experimental point of view, in a study conducted by Rostoker and Yamamoto in 1954 [6], a new intermediate phase defined as $V_3Ni$ was reported. This phase has a cubic symmetry of


type A15 and is uniform to $V_3Co$. In addition, the lattice parameter of this intermetallic compound was reported as $4.71$ Å. Stevens et al. [7] determined a phase diagram using thermal analysis and microscopic method as well as X-ray diffraction data processing method for the vanadium-rich portion of the V-Ni binary system. The formation enthalpies of different phases consisting of the cobalt-vanadium system using the adiabatic high-temperature calorimeter were measured by Spencer et al. [8]. Moreover, Nilsson et al. calculated the valence band spectra of the A15 type $V_3Al$, $V_3Co$, $V_3Ni$, $V_3As$, $V_3Sn$, and $V_3Sb$ compounds through soft X-ray emission (SXE) [11]. In 2003, Bratberg and Sundman conducted a thermodynamic analysis of the binary Co-V system with experimental data of thermochemical and phase diagrams [15]. For $V_3T$ (T = As, Au, Co, Ga, Ge, Ir, Pt, Sb, and Si) intermetallic compounds, the Knight shifts and quadrupole interactions were studied by Blumberg et al. [16] using nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (NMR). Theoretically, Wang et al. [24] have investigated the structural, elastic and thermodynamic properties of intermetallic $V_3X$ (X = Ir, Pt, and Au) using density functional technique within generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and local density approximation (LDA) and compared the results obtained with these two approximations. In another theoretical study, the self-consistent electronic band computations of $V_3Ir$, $V_3Pt$, $V_3Au$, $Nb_3Ir$, $Nb_3Pt$, and $Nb_3Au$ intermetallic compounds in the A15 structure have been obtained from the linear muffin-tin orbital (LMTO) method by Jarlborg et al. [25]. In a study conducted by Paduani and Kuhnen [26] using the software package Wien2k based on the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW) technique, the electronic band structures and Fermi surfaces of vanadium-based intermetallic $V_3Co$, $V_3Rh$, $V_3Ir$ and $V_3Os$ were given in detail. The aforementioned authors also studied the electron-phonon interactions and nature of bonding for the A15 structure compounds $V_3B$ (B = Ni, Pd, and Pt) [27]. Structural, elastic, electronic and thermodynamic properties of $V_3M$ (M = Si, Ge, and Sn) compounds with the CASTEP (Cambridge Sequential Total Energy Package) code were performed by Chihi et al. [28].

However, despite the above-mentioned studies, many other properties of vanadium-based intermetallics with A15 structure are still unknown. Although the electronic band structures, Fermi surfaces, and phase diagrams of $V_3Co$ and $V_3Ni$ compounds have been studied, no studies have been conducted on the elastic, vibrational, and thermodynamic properties of these compounds. On the other hand, there is not any data in the literature for the A15 phase of the

$V_3$Fe compound. In the Material Project records, it is seen that this compound's lattice constant and formation enthalpy per atom are 4.649 Å and -0.171 eV, respectively [41]. As a result, there are no studies in the literature about the elastic and lattice dynamical properties of $V_3X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) intermetallic compounds. If the phonon spectrum of a material is known, material properties such as thermodynamic stability, phase transition, and thermal properties can be determined.

The principal goal of this study is to offer an insight into the structural properties, formation enthalpies, electronic properties, elastic constants and related properties, degrees of anisotropy, vibrational and thermal properties of the intermetallic compounds $V_3X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) with cubic A15 phase by making use of first-principles simulation technique.

### 2. Computational Details

All calculations in this study were done with the MedeA-Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [42, 43] using the plane-wave basis sets and periodic boundary circumstances. For the exchange and correlation term in electron-electron interaction, Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [44] type functional was used within the framework of the Generalized Gradient Approach (GGA) [45]. Wave functions were expanded in a plane wave base set and as a result of geometric optimization, cut-off energy was taken as 600 eV. Additionally, integrals in the Brillouin region were taken with Monkhorst-Pack [46] type 12x12x12 k-point, which is located at the $\Gamma$- point of the zone center and automatically generated. In order to obtain a proper electronic density of states (DOS), the smearing technique was applied to the Fermi surface and the smearing parameter was taken as $\sigma = 0.225$ eV [47]. In order to produce the pseudopotential, the electronic configurations of the atoms forming $V_3X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) compounds are V: $4s^23d^3$, Fe: $3s^23p^64s^23d^6$, Co: $4s^13d^8$, Ni: $4s^23d^8$, respectively. The stress-strain relationship was used for the calculation of elastic properties [48]. MedeA-PHONON module [49] was used in phonon calculations. The phonon dispersion curves of the noted materials were examined by the direct method, where the forces have been calculated by dint of the Hellmann-Feynman theorem. To calculate these forces, the displacement of the atoms in the unit cell by 0.02 Å was allowed. Besides, a total number of 6 single-point energy calculations were made on 2x2x2 supercells with displaced atoms containing 64 atoms.

### 3. Result and Discussions

#### 3.1.Structural Properties

Vanadium-based binary intermetallic V₃X (X = Fe, Co, Ni) compounds crystallize in A15 crystal structure and space group Pm-3n. It contains a primitive cubic cell of 8 atoms. In this crystal structure, V atoms occupy the 6c Wyckoff positions with atomic coordinates (1/4, 0, 1/2), (1/2, 1/4, 0), (0, 1/2, 1/4), (3/4, 0, 1/2), (1/2, 3/4, 0), (0, 1/2, 3/4) and X atoms occupy the 2a Wyckoff positions with atomic coordinates (0, 0, 0) and (1/2, 1/2, 1/2). The crystal structure of V₃X (X = Fe, Co, Ni) compounds with space group Pm-3n is shown in Fig. 1. We have calculated lattice constant ($a_0$), unit-cell volume ($V_0$), density ($\rho$), bond length (V-X and V-V), and formation enthalpy ($\Delta H$) of the V₃X (X = Fe, Co, and Ni) compounds and collected them in Table 1. As a comparison to literature, the calculated consequences are very much close to the experimentally measured and other theoretical data for these compounds [6, 14, 26, 29-31, 33-36, 38-41]. As seen in Table 1, the bond length of V-X and V-V bonds for the V₃Co compound is smaller than the V₃Fe and V₃Ni compounds. For this reason, the V₃Co is structurally more stable than other compounds. Furthermore, the bond length of the V-V bond for these three compounds is smaller than the V-X bond, showing that the association with V-V atoms is more powerful than the V-X atoms. Namely, the V-V bond has a vital role in structural and elastic stability of V₃X (X = Fe, Co, and Ni) intermetallic compounds. Besides, the computed values for density of the studied V₃Fe and V₃Co compounds are in agreement with the previously reported density values [31, 41]. The calculated densities decrease in the sequence V₃Co → V₃Fe → V₃Ni.

The thermodynamic stability of any compound can be determined by calculating the formation enthalpies of such compounds. The formation enthalpies of the studied V₃X (X = Fe, Co, Ni) compounds have been calculated by the following relation, and results are collected in Table 1.

$$
\Delta H_{f}=\frac{1}{8}\left[E_{V_{3}X}^{total}-\left(6E_{V}^{bulk}+2E_{X}^{bulk}\right)\right] \tag{1}
$$

Where $E_{V_{3}X}^{total}$ is the total formation energy at equilibrium for V₃X (X = Fe, Co, and Ni), $3E_{V}^{bulk}$ is the energy of three atoms of V and $E_{X}^{bulk}$ is the energy of X = Fe, Co, and Ni atoms. The formation enthalpies of V₃Fe, V₃Co, and V₃Ni intermetallic compounds have been computed as

-0.173 eV/atom, -0.229 eV/atom, and -0.149 eV/atom, respectively. The calculated formation enthalpy for these compounds shows very much agreement with the previous results available in the literature [39-41]. From the calculated results, it is observed that these compounds are thermodynamically stable since the calculated formation enthalpies have negative values. Moreover, the absolute sequence of the formation enthalpy is $V_3Co > V_3Fe > V_3Ni$. In accordance with the computed formation enthalpy, $V_3Co$ is thermodynamically more stable than others. This is due to the fact that compounds having smaller formation enthalpy have greater thermodynamic stability.

### 3.2.Electronic Properties

To determine the electronic properties of the title compounds, the electronic band structure, total and partial density of states are computed making use of the GGA-PBE method. The electronic band structure of the $V_3X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) intermetallic compounds with A-15 type crystal structure are calculated and plotted them along high symmetric directions as shown in Figs. 2 (a)-(c). The dotted lines symbolize Fermi level $(E_F)$. Figs. 2(a)-(c) shows that the energy bands are distributed symmetrically above and below the Fermi energy level. It means that the studied compounds are metallic in nature. The calculated electronic band structures of $V_3Co$ and $V_3Ni$ are in consonance with noted earlier results in the literature [26, 27].

We have also calculated and plotted the total and partial density of states for $V_3X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) compounds as shown in Figs. 3(a)-(c). These figures show that the energy levels between the energy range -7 eV to -4 eV is constructed mainly by the contributions of $p$ orbitals of V and X atoms and $s$ orbitals of V and X atoms. The energy range between -4 eV to -2 eV is constructed mainly by the contributions of $s$, $p$, and $d$ orbitals of V atoms and $d$ orbitals of X atoms. For the energy range between -2 eV and 0 eV, the energy levels are constructed mainly by the contributions of $p$ and $d$ orbitals of V and X atoms along with the small contributions of $s$ orbitals of V and X atoms. Above the Fermi level, i.e. for the energy levels between 0 eV and 2 eV is mainly constructed by $p$ and $d$ orbitals of V and $p$ orbitals of X atoms. However, the contributions of $s$ orbitals of X and V atoms are negligible. It is noticeable that the contributions of $s$ and $p$ orbitals of V atoms decreases with the increase in the energy. The difference between the electronic band structures is that the Fermi level exists before the valley for $V_3Fe$, the bottom

of the valley for $V_3Co$ and after the valley for $V_3Ni$. The existence of the Fermi level near the valley in the total density of states differentiates between the bonding and the antibonding states. The TDOS at the Fermi level for the studied $V_3Fe$, $V_3Co$, and $V_3Ni$ compounds are 6.05 states/eV.cell, 5.11 states/eV.cell and 7.77 states/eV.cell, respectively. That is to say, the $N(E_F)$ values have the order of $V_3Ni > V_3Fe > V_3Co$. It is well known that the higher the value of density of states at the Fermi level the lower the stability of the materials [50-53]. This suggests that the stability of the examined compounds increases in the sequence $V_3Ni \to V_3Fe \to V_3Co$ which confirms the results found by the calculation of formation enthalpy. Besides, this is the same as the result deduced from the bond lengths of V-X and V-V and melting temperature investigations for the considered compounds.

### 3.3.Elastic and Related Properties

Elastic and related properties are an important parameter for high-temperature materials [54]. The examined compounds $V_3X$ ($X = Fe, Co, Ni$) have a primitive cubic structure with space group $Pm$-$3n$. This means that three elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$ are sufficient to describe the elastic properties of the cubic $V_3X$ ($X = Fe, Co, Ni$) compounds [55, 56]. In the present paper, we have calculated the above three elastic constants and tabulated them in Table 2. However, there is no data in the literature regarding the elastic constants of these compounds. The calculated values of elastic constants show the following criteria;

$$C_{11}-C_{12}>0,\ C_{12}<B<C_{11},\ C_{11}+2C_{12}>0,\ C_{11}>0,\ C_{44}>0 \tag{2}$$

This verifies the Born's stability criterion of mechanical stability of cubic structure [57-59] which shows that the examined $V_3X$ ($X = Fe, Co, Ni$) compounds are mechanically stable. Besides, such polycrystalline qualifications as Cauchy pressure, bulk modulus, shear modulus, Young's modulus, Lame's constants, and Poisson's ratio, etc. can be derived from these three independent elastic constants, and results are given in Table 2 and Table 3. From Table 2, we can also draw the following conclusions: The incompressibility of the $V_3X$ ($X = Fe, Co, Ni$) compounds along x-axis, y-axis or z-axis is represented by the elastic constant $C_{11}$. Table 2 shows that the elastic constant $C_{11}$ is greater than the other two elastic constants $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$. It means that the examined $V_3X$ ($X = Fe, Co, Ni$) compounds are less compressible along x, y, or z-

axis direction in comparison to the other two directions, and the compressibility increases in the sequence of $\mathrm{V}_{3}\mathrm{Co} \rightarrow \mathrm{V}_{3}\mathrm{Fe} \rightarrow \mathrm{V}_{3}\mathrm{Ni}$.

The resistance against change in volume due to the applied external pressure is shown by the bulk modulus ($B$) and the resistance against reversible deformation by shearing strain is represented by the shear modulus ($G$) for any compound. Shear modulus of any compound is also related to the hardness of the materials. The calculated elastic constants have been used to calculate the upper and lower limit of bulk modulus and shear modulus with the help of Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation [60-62] as:

$$
B=B_{V}=B_{R}=\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right) / 3 \tag{3}
$$

$$
G_{V}=\left(C_{11}-C_{12}+3 C_{44}\right) / 5 \tag{4}
$$

$$
G_{R}=\left\{5\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right) C_{44}\right\} /\left\{C_{44}+3\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)\right\} \tag{5}
$$

$$
G=\left(G_{V}+G_{R}\right) / 2 \tag{6}
$$

Here, ??? is Voigt shear modulus corresponding to the upper limit of $G$ value, and ??? is Reuss shear modulus corresponding to the lower limit of $G$ value. The Young's modulus ($E$) of any compound represents the resistance against the uniaxial tension. The predicted Young's modulus of these compounds are calculated with the help of the following relation and results are listed in Table 3.

$$
E=9 B G /(3 B+G) \tag{7}
$$

The ratio of $B/G$ gives information about the brittle or ductile nature of the materials. The critical value of 1.75 is used to determine whether the material is ductile and brittle in nature [63]. It is known that if the value of $B/G$ is greater than critical value then the material is ductile in nature and if the value of $B/G$ ratio is less than the critical value then the material is brittle in nature.

This ratio is around 2.18 for $V_3Fe$, 1.91 for $V_3Co$, and 2.13 for $V_3Ni$, which obviously emphasizes the ductile nature of the examined materials.

Another indicator to determine the brittle and ductile nature of a material is Cauchy pressure $C_p = C_{12}-C_{44}$. If the Cauchy pressure is positive studied material is ductile and if the Cauchy pressure is negative it is brittle [64,65]. For the investigated $V_3Fe$, $V_3Co$, and $V_3Ni$ materials, the value of Cauchy pressure is calculated as 44.83 GPa, 23.46 GPa, and 40.72 GPa, respectively. For this reason, these studied materials have naturally ductile. On the ground of the computed Cauchy pressure, the ductility or brittleness for $V_3X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) compounds conform to the outcomes supposed by the B/G ratio.

Poisson's ratio ($v$) represents the stability of the material against shear stress and it is also directly related to the nature of bonding in the material. The Poisson's ratio for a cubic crystal can be calculated with the help of the following relation;

$$
v=(1 / 2)(3 B-2 G)(3 B+G)^{-1} \tag{8}
$$

In general, the Poisson ratio is 0.33 for metallic materials, 0.25 for ionic materials, and 0.1 for covalent materials. The estimated $v$ data for these materials are given in Table 3. The calculated Poisson's ratio for the examined compounds is 0.301,0.278, and 0.297 for $V_3Fe$, $V_3Co$, and $V_3Ni$, respectively. Namely, the $v$ value gradually increases in the sequence of $V_3Co \to V_3Ni \to$ $V_3Fe$, indicating that the directionality degree of the covalent bonding of $V_3Co$ is more robust than $V_3Fe$. Besides, the degree of covalent bonding is one of the important factors in determining the hardness of the materials.

The design of engineering and the effective structure of any material can be done by knowing the elastic and mechanical properties of that material. The elastic properties of any materials can be very well understood by knowing the value of Lame constants ( $\lambda$ and $\mu$ ). $\lambda$ represents compressional velocity which propagates back and forth in a crystal while $\mu$ represents the shear waves that move up and down in the crystal. $\lambda$ and $\mu$ constants can be calculated by knowing the value of Poisson's ratio ($v$) and Young's modulus (E). The Lame's constants of the $V_3X$ (X=Fe,

Co, Ni) compounds have been obtained by using the following relations [66] and these calculated values are tabulated in Table 3.

$$
\lambda=v E /\{(1+v)(1-2 v)\} \tag{9}
$$

$$
\mu=E /\{2(1+v)\} \tag{10}
$$

Refs. [67-69] have introduced a Kleinman's parameter $\zeta$ that represents the relative positions of the cation and anion sub-lattices under volume conserving strain distortions for which positions are not fixed by symmetry. The following relationships have been used to calculate the Kleinman's parameter for the $\mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{X}(\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{Fe}, \mathrm{Co}, \mathrm{Ni})$ compounds shown in Table 3.

$$
\zeta=\left(C_{11}+8 C_{12}\right) /\left(7 C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right) \tag{11}
$$

The calculated Kleinman's parameters are 0.453, 0.431, 0.455 for intermetallics compounds $\mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{Fe}, \mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{Co}$, and $\mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{Ni}$, respectively. The low value of Kleinman's parameter shows that there is large resistance against bond bending or bond angle distortion. Among these three compounds, the compound with the smallest $\zeta$ value is $\mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{Co}$. Therefore, one can say that the $\mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{Co}$ is less resistant to changes in its bond length than the other two compounds.

$$
H_{V}=2\left(k^{2} G\right)^{0.585}-3 ;(k=G / B) \tag{12}

Our results show $\mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{Co}$ is the hardest material compared to all other materials. This is due to the robustness of the covalent bonds of the $\mathrm{V}_{3} \mathrm{Co}$ compound. Because the degree of covalent bonding is an important factor that determines the hardness of the material. There are no experimental or theoretical studies to compare the calculated Poisson's

ratio ($v$), Lame's constants ($\lambda; \mu$), Kleinman's parameter ($\zeta$) and Vicker's hardness ($H_V$) of these materials.

Besides, Debye temperature ($\theta_D$), longitudinal and transverse sound velocities ($V_L$ and $V_T$), and melting temperature ($T_m$) are obtained from the elastic data and calculated results are tabulated in Table 4. Debye temperature ($\theta_D$), is the fundamental property of any material, which can relate to the specific heat and melting temperature of the material. $$

### 3.4.Anisotropic elasticity

The elastic anisotropy of solid structures is a significant criterion for engineering science on the grounds that it shows the probability of microcracks occurrence in materials. The universal anisotropy factor $(A^{U})$, Zener anisotropy factor $(A^{Z})$, and the percentage of bulk and shear anisotropy factors $(A_{B}$ and $A_{G})$ have been calculated with the help of following relations [75];

$$
A^{U}=\left(5 G_{V} / G_{R}+B_{V} / B_{R}\right)-6 \geq 0
\tag{17}
$$

$$
A^{Z}=2 C_{44} /\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)
\tag{18}
$$

$$
A_{B}=\left(B_{V}-B_{R}\right) /\left(B_{V}+B_{R}\right) \times 100 \%
\tag{19}
$$

$$
A_{G}=\left(G_{V}-G_{R}\right) /\left(G_{V}+G_{R}\right) \times 100 \%
\tag{20}
$$

where $G_{V}$ $(G_{R})$ and $B_{V}$ $(B_{R})$ express the upper and lower bounds of the shear modulus and bulk modulus in accordance with the Voigt-Reuss models [60, 61], respectively. If a single crystal has $A^{U}=0$; $A^{Z}=1$; $A_{B}=A_{G}=0$, it displays isotropic feature, or else the crystal is elastically anisotropic. The degree of the anisotropy of the crystal could be stated by the deviations of $A^{U}$, $A_{B}$, and $A_{G}$ from zero, and $A^{Z}$ from 1. The calculated anisotropy factors $(A^{U}, A^{Z}, A_{B}$, and $A_{G})$ of $V_{3}X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) intermetallics are collected in Table 5. Because these compounds are of a cubic structure, $B_{V}/B_{R}$ ratios are equivalent to 1. This result reveals that all materials are isotropic for $A_{B}$ anisotropy factor. In accordance with $A^{U}, A^{Z}$, and $A_{G}$ given in Table 5, $V_{3}X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) intermetallic compounds are anisotropic. Besides, the sequence of anisotropy is


$V_3Co < V_3Ni < V_3Fe$, which means that the degree of the elastic anisotropy for $V_3Fe$ is more potent than two other compounds.

The directional dependent mechanical anisotropic properties are more realistic to understand the anisotropic properties of any material. The mechanical anisotropic properties of any crystal can be well understood by calculating the variation of Young's modulus, linear compressibility, shear modulus, and Poisson's ratio with directions. 2D and 3D shapes of these parameters for only the $V_3Co$ compound have been computed by using the EIAM code [76] and given in Fig. 4. Whereas the green spheres or curves indicate maximum values, the blue ones indicate the minimum values. Since the other compounds have similar isotropic properties, only the $V_3Co$ compound has considered. In addition, the minimum and maximum values of Young's modulus $(E)$, linear compressibility $(\beta)$, shear modulus $(G)$, and Poisson's ratio $(\vartheta)$ for $V_3X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) are also collected in Table 6. For a cubic system, the 3D surface structures of the directional dependence of reciprocal of Young's modulus $(E)$ can be defined as [77]:

$$
1/E = S_{11} - 2S_0\{(hk)^2 + (hl)^2 + (lk)^2/(h^2 + k^2 + l^2)\} \tag{21}
$$

where $S_0 = S_{11} - S_{12} - S_{44}/2$; $S_{11}$, $S_{12}$, and $S_{44}$ are the elastic compliances; $h$, $k$, and $l$ are Miller indices of the lattice plane. The calculated value of the minimum and maximum Young's modulus are collected in Table 6 and three dimensional representational is shown in Fig. 4(a). A spherical shape in the 3D surface structure represents a perfect isotropic system. As seen in Fig. 4(a), the 3D surface of the $V_3Co$ compound is clearly not having a spherical shape and hence we conclude that these compounds are elastically anisotropic in nature. It is well known that the quantity $(hk)^2 + (hl)^2 + (lk)^2$ is zero for a cubic system in the direction {100} and it will be maximum in the direction {111}. Hence we find that there is depression at the corners. This result is compatible with the result obtained from the examination of universal elastic anisotropy indices.

Moreover, the three-dimensional directional dependence of the linear compressibility $(\beta)$ for the studied compound is calculated with the following relation [78],

$$\beta=1 /\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right)=S_{11}+2 S_{12}\qquad(22)$$

As seen in Fig. 4(b) that the 3D shapes of the linear compressibility for these cubic compounds are not deviating from the sphere, which shows that the linear compressibility of $V_{3}X$ (X = Fe, Co, Ni) compounds displays isotropic in all directions.

The directional dependence of the shear modulus (G) for the studied compounds can be calculated from the following relation [78];

$$1 / G=\left(S_{44}+4 S_{0} J\right)\qquad(23)$$

where $J=sin^{2}\theta.cos^{2}\theta+0.125.sin^{4}\theta(1-cos4\varphi)$; $\theta$ and $\varphi$ are Euler angles. Fig. 4(c) shows the directional dependent of the shear modulus. The calculated values of minimum and maximum values of the shear modulus are also tabulated in Table 6. The minimum and maximum values along the {111} and {100} directions of shear modulus are 78.93 GPa and 140.33 GPa for $V_{3}Fe$, 94.88 GPa and 150.65 GPa for $V_{3}Co$, 80.7 GPa and 136.37 GPa for $V_{3}Ni$, respectively, which are consistent with the results shown in Fig. 4(c).

We have also computed the directional dependence of the Poisson's ratio (v) for the compound $V_{3}Co$. It has been calculated by the following relations [78]

$$
\begin{aligned}
& v(h k l, \theta)=\left\{S_{12}+\frac{S_{0}}{h^{2}+k^{2}+l^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{h^{2} l}{\sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}} \sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}+l^{2}}} \cos \theta-\frac{h k}{\sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}}} \sin \theta\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{k^{2} l}{\sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}} \sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}+l^{2}}} \cos \theta+\right.\right.\right. \\
& \left.\left.\left.\frac{h k}{\sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}}} \sin \theta\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{l \sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}}}{\sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}+l^{2}}} \cos \theta\right)^{2}\right]\right\} /\left[-S_{11}+2 S_{0} \frac{(h k)^{2}+(h l)^{2}+(l k)^{2}}{\left(h^{2}+k^{2}+l^{2}\right)^{2}}\right]
\end{aligned}
\qquad(24)
$$

### 3.5.Vibrational and Thermodynamic Properties

Phonon dispersion relations along high symmetry directions, total and partial density of states for the V₃Fe, V₃Co, and V₃Ni compounds have been calculated and results are presented in Figs. 5(a)-5(c), respectively. The crystal structure of the V₃X (X = Fe, Co, and Ni) compounds comprises 8 atoms in the unit cell, thus resulting in 24 phonon branches containing three acoustic and 21 optical branches. The behavior of the phonon branches is almost the same apart from the change in the magnitude of high optical branches. The acoustic modes go to zero at the $\Gamma$-point, whereas all the other phonon dispersion curves stay positive throughout the Brillouin zone. This indicates that the V₃X (X = Fe, Co, and Ni) intermetallics are vibrationally stable. The calculated phonon frequencies at gamma point for these compounds are given in Table 7. In addition, infrared active modes (I) and raman active modes (R) have also been specified. Accordingly, $T_{2g}$ and $E_g$ modes are raman active, $T_{1u}$ modes are infrared active, and $T_{2u}$, $T_{1g}$, $A_{2g}$ are inactive modes.

The partial density of states for each compound are shown to the right of the phonon dispersion curves. In low phonon modes (<3 THz), the vibration of phonons is mainly due to the vibrations of V atoms and small contributions of X (Fe, Ni, and Co) atoms. In the frequency range between 3 THz and 9 THz, the vibrations of phonons are formed with the equal contribution of both atoms. For the higher energy modes, i.e., the frequency range between 9 THz and 11 THz, the vibrations are only due to the vibrations of V atoms and there is no contribution of X atoms. Besides, there is no gap between the acoustic and optical phonon branches and hence there are no singularities in the total phonon density of states.

The thermodynamic properties like specific heat capacity ($C_v$), entropy ($S$), internal energy ($E$), and free energy ($F$) as a function of temperature have also been obtained from the phonon spectrum and phonon interactions. $C_v$, $S$, $E$, and $F$ variations of the studied compounds V₃X (X = Fe, Co, and Ni) are computed between the temperature range 0 – 3000 K, and these relations are given in Figs. 6(a)-(d), respectively. As can be clearly seen from Fig. 6(a), the specific heat capacity at constant volume of all compounds increases with temperature and it follows the Dulong and Petit's law [79] at high temperatures. Additionally, it can be seen from Figs. 6(b), 6 (c), and 6(d) that the calculated entropy and internal energy increase with temperature while the

free energy decreases with temperature. There is no previous experimental and theoretical study for the comparison of these results.

### 4. Conclusions
Structural, electronic structure, elastic, anisotropic mechanical properties, thermodynamic, and vibrational properties of vanadium-based $\text{V}_3\text{X}$ (X = Fe, Co, and Ni) intermetallic compounds with cubic Pm-3n phase have been extensively discussed in the present study. The obtained lattice constant and formation enthalpy for these intermetallics are consistent with both experimental and theoretical results. These compounds are thermodynamically stable and the lowest formation enthalpy value among these compounds belongs to $\text{V}_3\text{Co}$ compound. Therefore, this compound is more stable than other compounds. The electronic spectra of these materials exhibit non-zero value of the electronic total density of states at the Fermi level, i.e. these materials have a metallic character. Besides, the electronic structure calculation reveals that the metallic conductivity of these materials is dominated by $d$ orbitals of V and X atoms. The calculated elastic constants of the cubic Pm-3n phase of these compounds are found to satisfy the mechanical stability condition. Each of the studied compounds has a strong anisotropy and the elastic anisotropy degree of the $\text{V}_3\text{Fe}$ compound is highest. It is observed that these materials are ductile from the calculated $B/G$ ratio and Cauchy pressure $(C_P)$. Furthermore, the phonon calculations for three $\text{V}_3\text{X}$ (X = Fe, Co, and Ni) intermetallics reveal that these compounds have vibrational stability due to positive phonon modes along the Brillouin zone. The temperature variations of the thermodynamic properties such as internal energy, free energy, entropy and specific heat capacity are computed from the obtained phonon density of states for these compounds and the results have been discussed in detail. The calculated melting temperature is $2943\pm300$ K for $\text{V}_3\text{Fe}$, $3033\pm300$ K for $\text{V}_3\text{Co}$, and $2882\pm300$ K for $\text{V}_3\text{Ni}$. Therefore, these compounds can be used as refractory materials in extremely high temperature applications.

### Highlights

- The electronic spectra of these materials exhibit non-zero value of the electronic total density of states at the Fermi level, i.e. these materials have a metallic character.

- The obtained lattice constant and formation enthalpy for these intermetallics are in good agreement with both experimental and theoretical results.

- The calculated elastic constants of the cubic Pm-3n phase shows that studied compounds are mechanically stable.

- Each of the studied compounds has a strong anisotropy and the elastic anisotropy degree of the $V_3$Fe compound is highest.

- Since the calculated melting temperature is $2943 \pm 300$ K for $V_3$Fe, $3033 \pm 300$ K for $V_3$Co, and $2882 \pm 300$ K for $V_3$Ni. Therefore, these compounds can be used as refractory materials in extremely high temperature applications.
