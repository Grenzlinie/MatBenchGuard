## ABSTRACT

The mechanical, vibrational and thermodynamic properties of $Li_4P_2S_6$ are investigated through the first-principles calculations. The predicted lattice parameters agree with the available experimental data and theoretical results. The mechanical and vibrational properties are calculated, indicating that $Li_4P_2S_6$ is mechanical and dynamically stable, have a brittleness character and the Vickers hardness is 5.7 GPa. The anisotropy property is studied by the percent anisotropy, the universal anisotropic indexes and three-dimensional surface contour of elastic modulus. Furthermore, the three-dimensional surface contour of the minimum thermal conductivity is obtained. Using the linear response theory, the phonon and thermodynamic properties are obtained. The dielectric tensors $\varepsilon$ and the Born effective charges $Z^*$ are also investigated. Finally, the symmetry of the normal modes of vibration, the infrared and Raman activity are predicted.

## 1. Introduction

Li-ion batteries are experiencing widespread utilization in mobile devices, other micro-battery and high energy demand applications [1]. However, the safety issues and packing capabilities of organic liquid electrolyte in current commercial Li-ion batteries are still a key issue, which hindering the development of advanced energy storage technologies [2,3]. Non-flammable solid-state electrolytes materials possess good stability and safety, and are expected to replace traditional organic liquid electrolyte [4-6]. Indeed, research shows that solid-state batteries tremendously improve the safety of device and retain almost all of the capacity in thousands of cycles [7]. Lithium thiophosphates are very promising solid-state electrolytes due to their high ionic conductivity and low activation energy at room temperature [8,9]. $Li_4P_2S_6$ crystallizes in a hexagonal structure with one P atom position for the $P_2S_6^{4-}$ unit and all Li atoms are stacked in a chainlike fashion between the $P_2S_6^{4-}$ units [10]. However, the Mercier structure (reported by Mercier et al.) is not the lowest energy structure. Actually, Holzwarth and his colleagues found the lowest energy structure is $P$-$31m$ (No.162), which is a subgroup of the Mercier structure [11]. Dietrich et al. had synthesized the $Li_4P_2S_6$ and analyzed its crystal structure using the experimental method [12]. Their results confirmed that the ethane-like $P_2S_6^{4-}$ polyhedra are arranged in a structure with sheets in space group $P$-$31m$. The structural, electronic properties, ionic conductivity, activation energy and defect formation energy of $Li_4P_2S_6$ have been extensively studied [13-16], whereas the mechanical, vibrational and thermodynamic properties are still scarce. Therefore, in this work, those properties of $Li_4P_2S_6$ are investigated by using the density functional theory (DFT) and the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT).

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Structure properties

$Li_4P_2S_6$ crystallizes in a planar arrangement structure with the space group of $P$-$31\ m$ (No.162) [12], which is a subgroup of the $P6_3/$ $mcm$ (No. 193), as illustrated in Fig. 1. In this structure, two inequivalent Li ions are located at $2c$ and $2d$ sites, respectively. In the view projected along the z-axis, two Li sites are superposed, while the vertical view shows that each $Li_d$ is located on a plane between the $P_2S_6$ units, and each $Li_c$ is located on a plane which also contains the $P_2S_6$ units. The calculated lattice and fractional coordinate parameters are tabulated in Table 1, and the corresponding experimental data are given for comparison with the theoretical values. The obtained lattice parameters are $a = 6.106\ \mathring{A}$ and $c = 6.625\ \mathring{A}$, respectively. The relative differences between our results and experimental data are less than 0.5%. The results show that the method used in our work is reliable, and the calculated lattice parameters can be further used to investigate its mechanical and thermodynamic properties.

### 3.2. Elastic properties and hardness

The elastic constant $(C_{ij})$ is a crucial parameter that not only helps to understand the macroscopic mechanical behaviors, but also provides very useful information for the stability and stiffness of materials. In this work, the elastic constants of $Li_4P_2S_6$ are obtained using the "strain-stress" method [28], and the calculated results are shown in Table 2.

As we known, $C_{11}$ and $C_{33}$ represent linear compression resistance along the x- and z-axis, respectively. It can be seen from Table 2 that the $C_{11}$ is larger than that of $C_{33}$, suggesting the z-axis is more compressible than the x-axis. Furthermore, $C_{44}$and $C_{66}$ represent the resistances of [001] and [110] directional to shear stress on the (100) plane, respectively. The value of $C_{44}$ is smaller than that of $C_{66}$, showing that the resistance on the [110] directional is the larger than that on the [001] directional under the shear stress.

For trigonal materials, the stability criterion are [29]:

$$
C_{33}(C_{11}+C_{12})-2C_{13}^2>0 \tag{1}
$$

$$
C_{44}(C_{11}-C_{12})-2C_{14}^2-2C_{15}^2>0 \tag{2}
$$

The elastic constants $C_{ij}$ of $Li_4P_2S_6$ are shown in Table 2. To our knowledge, there is no experimental data or theoretical results on the elastic constants for $Li_4P_2S_6$ that can be compared to our theoretical calculations. From Table 2, we find that the stability conditions of $Li_4P_2S_6$ are clearly satisfied, indicating that it is mechanically stable.

To calculate the polycrystalline elastic modulus, the Reuss (the upper bound) and the Voigt approximation (the lower bound) are used, respectively. The shear modulus $G_{V,R}$ and bulk modulus $B_{V,R}$ are given by Ref. [30].

$$
G_{V}=\frac{1}{30}(C_{11}+C_{12}-4C_{13}+2C_{33}+12C_{44}+12C_{66}) \tag{3}
$$

$$
B_{V}=\frac{1}{9}[2C_{11}+2C_{12}+C_{33}+4C_{13}] \tag{4}
$$

$$
B_{R}=\frac{C_{33}(C_{11}+C_{12})-2C_{13}^2}{C_{11}+C_{12}-4C_{13}+2C_{33}} \tag{5}
$$

$$
G_{R}=\frac{5}{2}\frac{\left[C_{33}(C_{11}+C_{12})-2C_{13}^2\right]\left[C_{44}C_{66}-\left(C_{14}^2+C_{25}^2\right)\right]}{3B_{V}\left[C_{44}C_{66}-\left(C_{14}^2+C_{25}^2\right)\right]+C^2(C_{44}+C_{66})} \tag{6}
$$

The true polycrystalline shear modulus $G$ and bulk modulus $B$ are obtained by the Hill approximation [31].

$$
X=\frac{1}{2}(X_{V}+X_{R}),X=G\ or\ B \tag{7}
$$

Furthermore, the Poisson's ratio $\sigma$ and Young's modulus $E$ can also be calculated [32].

The calculated $G, B, \sigma$ and $E$ of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ are listed in Table 2. The $B$ is used to measure the resistance of volume change and provides an estimate of response to a hydrostatic pressure, while the $G$ denotes the resistance of the shear stress to the reversible deformations. The calculated $B$ and $G$of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ is 36.4 GPa and 24.5 GPa, respectively, larger than that of $\gamma$-Li₃PS₄ ($B=31.9$ GPa, $G=14.1$ GPa) and $\beta$-Li₃PS₄ ($B=21.4$ GPa, $G=11.3$ GPa) [33], indicates that $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ is the most incompressible compared to the $\gamma$-Li₃PS₄ and $\beta$-Li₃PS₄. The $E$ measures the rigidity of material. The larger the $E$, the stiffer the material is. Clearly, the calculated value of $E$ for $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ (60.1 GPa) is greater than that of $\gamma$-Li₃PS₄ (36.9 GPa) and $\beta$-Li₃PS₄ (28.9 GPa) [33], indicating that $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ should be the stiffest among of them.

The $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ exhibits brittleness due to its $B/G$ and $\sigma$ is 1.48 and 0.24, respectively. (For brittle material, the value of $B/G$ is smaller than 1.75 [34] and the $\sigma$ is smaller than 0.33 [35]).

Further, the Vickers hardness $H_{v}$ is an important mechanical property of the materials. In this work, the $H_{v}$ of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ was calculated using the following theoretical model [36]:

$$
H_{v}=0.92\left(\frac{B}{G}\right)^{1.137} G^{0.708} \tag{10}
$$

The $H_{v}$ of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ has been predicted and the result is listed in Table 2. As can be seen from Table 2, the $H_{v}$ of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ is 5.7 GPa. Therefore, it can be classified as "soft material".

### 3.3. Anisotropy properties

Elastic anisotropy has a remarkably crucial influence on the crack behavior and mechanical durability of materials. Many different methodologies can be used to characterize the property of elastic anisotropy. In this work, the percent anisotropy ($A_{B}=(B_{V}-B_{R})/(B_{V}+B_{R})$ an$A_{G}=(G_{V}-G_{R})/(G_{V}+G_{R})$) [37], the universal anisotropic index ($A^{U}=5G_{V}/G_{R}+B_{V}/B_{R}-6$) [38] and three-dimensional (3D) surfaces are used. For isotropic material the $A^{U}=A_{B}=A_{G}=0$, and greater than 0 indicates the material has elastic anisotropy. The value of elastic anisotropy indexes are listed in Table 2. It can be seen that the $A^{U}$ of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ is 0.72, indicating that it has anisotropic. The $A_{B}=2.17$ indicates compression anisotropy of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$, while the $A_{G}$ is 6.31 shows that $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ exhibits shear anisotropy. Furthermore, $A_{G}$ is greater than $A_{B}$ reveals that the anisotropy in compression is smaller than that in shear.

The 3D surface is the most effective way to directly characterize the elastic anisotropy of material. For trigonal materials, the 3D surface of the bulk, Young's and shear modulus can be expressed as [39,40]:

$$
B=\left[\left(s_{11}+s_{12}+s_{13}\right)-\left(s_{11}+s_{12}-s_{13}-s_{33}\right)R_{3}^{2}\right]^{-1} \tag{11}
$$

$$
E=\left[\left(1-R_{3}^{2}\right)^{2} s_{11}+R_{3}^{4}s_{33}+R_{3}^{2}\left(1-R_{3}^{2}\right)\left(2s_{13}+s_{44}\right)+2R_{2}R_{3}\left(3R_{1}^{2}-R_{2}^{2}\right)s_{14}+\left(2R_{1}R_{3}\left(3R_{2}^{2}-R_{1}^{2}\right)s_{25}\right)\right]^{-1} \tag{12}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
G = & \left[s_{11}\left(3R_{1}^{2}+3R_{2}^{2}-2R_{1}^{4}-sR_{2}^{4}-4R_{1}^{2}R_{2}^{2}\right)+2s_{33}R_{3}^{2}\left(1-R_{3}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}s_{44}\left(R_{1}^{2}+R_{2}^{2}+2R_{3}^{2}-4R_{2}^{2}R_{3}^{2}-4R_{1}^{2}R_{3}^{2}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-s_{12}\left(R_{1}^{2}+R_{2}^{2}\right)-4s_{13}R_{3}^{2}\left(R_{1}^{2}+R_{3}^{2}\right)+2s_{14}\left(2R_{2}^{3}R_{3}-5R_{1}^{2}R_{2}R_{3}-R_{2}^{2}R_{1}R_{3}\right)\right]^{-1}
\end{aligned} \tag{13}
$$

Where$R_{1}$, $R_{2}$ and$R_{3}$ are the direction cosines, and$s_{ij}$is the elastic compliance constant. For isotropic material, the 3D surface is spherical, but if it is not, the material is anisotropic. Furthermore, the degree of deviation from the spherical surface represents the degree of anisotropy. The 3D surface of $B$, $E$ and $G$ are shown in Figs. 2-4 (a), respectively. As can be seen from the 3D surface of $B, E$ and $G$ in Figs. 2-4 (a), the $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ is anisotropic character due to the non-spherical 3D surface. Clearly, we can also found that the 3D surface of $E$ has the greatest deviations from the sphere than those of $B$ and $G$, suggesting that $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ shows highest anisotropy in $E$ than that of $B$ and $G$.

From the cross-sectional plane, one can see that the $B, E$ and $G$ on the xoy-plane have a weaker anisotropic than the other planes. ### 3.4. Vibrational properties

The Li₄P₂S₆ belongs to trigonal system with point group $D_{3d}$. As shown in Fig. 1, in Li₄P₂S₆, there are 12 atoms. Therefore, at the $\Gamma$ point of the Brillouin zone, it contains 36 phonon modes, three of them are acoustic modes and the rest are optical modes. According the group theory, the irreducible representations of the lattice vibration in Li₄P₂S₆ at the $\Gamma$ point are the following:

$$
\Gamma_{aco}=A_{2u}\oplus 2E_{u} \tag{14}
$$

$$
\Gamma_{opt}=4A_{2u}\oplus 10E_{u}\oplus 3A_{2g}\oplus 12E_{g}\oplus A_{1u}\oplus 3A_{1g} \tag{15}
$$

The calculated phonon frequencies of Li₄P₂S₆ are shown in Table 3. For Li₄P₂S₆, $E_{g}$ and $E_{u}$ modes are doubly degenerated. For the optical modes, the $4A_{2u}$ and $5E_{u}$ are infrared active, $6E_{g}$ and $3A_{1g}$ are Raman active, while the $3A_{2g}$ and $A_{1u}$ are silent.

### 3.5. The dielectric and born effective charge tensors

To investigate the polar properties of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$, we calculate the high frequency$\varepsilon_{\infty}$, the static dielectric constants $\varepsilon_{0}$ and the Born effective charge tensor$Z^{*}$for each nonequivalent atom based on the DFPT. The dielectric properties are summarized in Table 4, along with the averages of $\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}$ and $\bar{\varepsilon}_{\infty}$obtained by $\bar{\varepsilon}=(2 \varepsilon_{\perp}+\varepsilon^{\parallel}) / 3$. Due to the trigonal symmetry, our calculated high frequency $\varepsilon_{\infty}$and static $\varepsilon_{0}$ dielectric constants tensors have two independent components parallel $\varepsilon^{\parallel}$ and perpendicular $\varepsilon^{\perp}$ to the c axis, respectively. The averaged values of the $\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}$ and $\bar{\varepsilon}_{\infty}$ for $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ is 5.7 and 12.3, respectively.

The Born effective charge tensors $Z^{*}$ are very useful for consid-ering the bonding properties of materials. The $Z^{*}$ for the$\beta$component has been obtained as [41].

$$
Z^{*}=\frac{V}{e} \frac{\partial P_{\alpha}}{\partial u_{i \beta}} \tag{16}
$$

Where $u_{i \beta}$ is the periodic displacement of the $i$th atom in the $\beta$ direction, $e$ is electric charge, $P_{\alpha}$ is the component of the polarization in the $\alpha$ direction and $V$ is the volume of the unit cell. The calculated $Z^{*}$and the average of eigenvalues $\bar{Z}^{*}$ of $Li_{4}P_{2}S_{6}$ are shown in Table 4. Because of the symmetries, four off-diagonal elements are equal to zero except for S (see Table 4). The S ions are located at a lower symmetry sites (6k positions), so their effective charge tensors have nonequivalent diagonal components and sizable off-diagonal components. For $Li_{1}$, $Li_{2}$, P and S atoms, the Born effective charge is +1.18, +1.49, +2.26 and -1.64, respectively, which showing +18%, +49%, -43.5% and -18% change from their static charge. The anomalously large $\bar{Z}^{*}$ values indicate that there is a strong dynamic charge transfer along the S-P bond.

The Debye temperature $\theta_D$ is an important parameter for the measuring the physical properties of material. The temperature dependence of $\theta_D$ is demonstrated in Fig. 7. From Fig. 7, we can see that the $\theta_D$ increase with the increasing temperature below 15 K, and then decreases with increasing temperature and reaching a minimum of 362 K at 30 K. In the temperature range 30–600 K, the $\theta_D$ shows a rapid increase, however, above 600 K, it shows weak temperature dependence and tends to be constant.

The $\theta_D$ can also be estimated through the average sound velocity $v_m$ [44]:

$$
\theta_{D}=\frac{h}{2 \pi k}\left[\frac{3 N}{4 \pi}\left(\frac{N_{A} \rho}{M}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{3}} v_{m} \tag{21}
$$

Where $N_A$ is Avogadro number, $M$ is the molecular weight and $\rho$ is the density. The $v_m$ can be calculated as follows:

$$
v_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{t}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{l}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{3}} \tag{22}
$$

Where $v_l$ and $v_t$ is longitudinal and transverse sound velocity, respectively. The $v_l$ and $v_t$ can be obtained from the $G$ and the $B$.

$$
v_{l}=\left(\frac{B+\frac{4}{3} G}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{23}
$$

$$
v_{t}=\left(\frac{G}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{24}
$$

The $v_m$, $v_l$, $v_t$ and $\theta_D$ are listed in Table 2. Due to the relatively low elastic modulus, the $v_m$ (3705 m/s) is relatively low. Then, the $\theta_D$ is predicted to be 422 K, which is 8.76% larger than that obtained from the phonon density of states. However, there is no experimental data so far. Both results are the theoretical predictions.

## 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, the mechanical, vibrational and thermodynamic properties of $Li_4P_2S_6$ are investigated using the DFT and DFPT. The optimized lattice parameters are agreed well with previous experimental data. The elastic and mechanical properties are calculated. Our results indicate that $Li_4P_2S_6$ is mechanical and dynamically stable, behaves in a brittle manner and can be considered as "soft material". In addition, anisotropy is investigated by three different methods. The 3D surface contour of the thermal conductivity is obtained. The phonon dispersion curves are obtained by the linear response theory. The dielectric tensors, Born effective charges, the IR and Raman activity are predicted. Finally, the thermodynamic properties are determined within the harmonic approximation using the phonon density of states.
