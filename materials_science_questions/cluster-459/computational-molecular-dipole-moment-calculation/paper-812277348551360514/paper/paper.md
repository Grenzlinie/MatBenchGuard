# Conformer dipole moment and *syn*-1,3-diaxial steric effect on the conformational equilibrium of the *cis* isomer of some 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexanes

Paulo R. de Oliveira, Roberto Rittner*

Physical Organic Chemistry Laboratory, Chemistry Institute—State University of Campinas, Caixa Postal 6154, 13084-971 Campinas, SP, Brazil

Received 3 January 2005; revised 21 February 2005; accepted 22 February 2005
Available online 5 April 2005

## Abstract
The theoretical values of the dipole moment for the diaxial (ax–ax) conformer are larger than for the eq–eq conformer of some *cis*-3-halocyclohexanols (halo=Cl, Br and I) and *cis*−3-halo-1-methoxycyclohexanes (halo=F, Cl, Br and I). The experimental $^3J_{HH}$ coupling constants are rather large (>10 Hz) indicating that the diequatorial (eq–eq) conformer is predominant for all compounds in the solvents studied. Dipole moments and NMR data lead to the conclusion that the conformational equilibria of these 1,3-derivatives are not controlled by the solvent polarity, as is observed for the 1,2-disubstituted cyclohexanes, but are dictated by the *syn*-1,3-diaxial steric effect, which predominates over stabilization of the ax–ax conformer by an intramolecular hydrogen bond (IAHB).

## 1. Introduction
Solvent effects often play an essential role in organic chemistry and many chemical equilibria are substantially modified by a change in the molecular environment [1]. The solvent effect on conformational equilibria is particularly important when the conformers in equilibrium have different dipole moments [2], and is often rationalized in terms of solute–solvent electrostatic intermolecular interaction [3].

The experimental data for monocyclohexanes clearly show the absence of any significant solvent effect [4]. The equilibria for the *trans* isomers of 1,2-disubstituted cyclohexanes clearly exhibit large solvent effects due to the very different dipole moments of the conformers [5,6]. For 1,4-disubstituted cyclohexanes, a number of investigations have shown that the diaxial form is strongly preferred in the equilibrium [7]. Although a great deal is known about conformational equilibria of mono-, 1,2- and 1,4-disubstituted cyclohexanes, only scant information exists on the behavior of 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexane derivatives. For 1,3-dihalocyclohexanes no evidence of a conformational equilibria has been found [8], and recent work on some other *cis*-1,3-derivatives have demonstrated that an OH group near OH [9], OCH₃ [10] or N(CH₃)₂ groups [11] stabilizes the diaxial conformer, at low concentrations and in a non-polar solvent, due to the formation of an intramolecular hydrogen bond (IAHB). This IAHB is broken in a more polar solvent, the diequatorial conformer being favored by a solvation effect.

Therefore, the principal objective of this work is to determine the solvent effect and the influence of conformer dipole moments on the conformational equilibria of the *cis* isomer of a series of 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexanes, namely *cis*-3-halocyclohexanols (halo=Cl, Br and I) and *cis*-3-halo-1-methoxycyclohexanes (halo=F, Cl, Br and I) (Fig. 1).

### 2.1. Compounds

*cis*-3-X-cyclohexanols and *cis*-3-X-1-methoxycyclohexanes (Fig. 1) were prepared and separated from the *trans* isomer according to a previously described procedure [12].

### 2.2. Theoretical calculations

The geometries for the most stable ax-ax and eq-eq conformers of compounds **1–9** were recently determined [12]. Now, these geometries were employed in the calculations for obtaining the corresponding dipole moment values at B3LYP, HF and MP2 levels [13] with 6-311+g** (for fluorine, chlorine, bromine and methyl substituents) and 3-21g basis sets (for the iodine substituent) utilizing the GAUSSIAN 98 program [14].

## 3. Results and discussion

The three theoretical methods lead to very similar dipole moments for ax-ax and eq-eq conformers of compounds **1–9** (Table 1). Larger values were obtained for the ax-ax conformers in relation to the eq-eq conformers, mostly for the halo-derivatives (**1–3** and **5–8**), while rather similar values for both conformers were found for compounds **4** and **9**. These values suggest that the equilibria for the halo-derivatives should be shifted towards ax-ax on increasing the solvent polarity, as also occurs for the 1,2-disubstituted cyclohexanes, while the equilibria for compounds **4** and **9** should be independent of solvent polarity.

Experimental data for the conformational equilibrium of the *cis* isomer of all compounds was obtained from the coupling constant values $^3J_{\text{HH}}$ between H-1 and H-3, which are presented in Tables 2 and 3. These data show that large coupling constants ($^3J_{\text{HH}}$) were obtained, indicating that the eq-eq conformer is predominant in the conformational equilibrium, in any solvent, for all studied compounds (**1–9**) (Fig. 1). Thus, there is no significant solvent effect operating in these conformational equilibria.

Moreover, these results show also that the solvent effect on the conformational equilibrium of 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexanes is very different from the 1,2-disubstituted cyclohexanes, as can be exemplified by the *trans*-2-fluoro-1-methoxycyclohexane exhibiting an increase in the eq-eq conformer population from 67 to 86%, on changing the solvent from CCl₄ to CD₃CN [15], in contrast to an almost constant population observed for the *cis*-3-fluoro-1-methoxycyclohexane, reported here. Additional data on 2-halo-1-methoxycyclohexanes show also the same behavior, that is, the eq-eq conformer population increases from 49, 57 and 68% in CCl₄ to 75, 79 and 84% in DSMO, for 2-chloro-, 2-bromo- and 2-iodo-1-methoxycyclohexane, respectively [16].

Bodot et al. [17] have shown the importance of intramolecular hydrogen bonding in the conformational equilibrium of *trans*-2-chlorocyclohexanol. Recently, Freitas et al. [16] demonstrated that the main factor ruling the conformational equilibrium for *trans*-2-X-cyclohexanols (X=Cl, Br and I) is the occurrence of an intramolecular hydrogen bond, mostly in dilute solutions of non-polar solvents, such as CCl₄. The observed eq-eq conformer population for 2-chloro-, 2-bromo- and 2-iodocyclohexanols were 91, 93 and 92% in CCl₄, decreasing to 81, 86 and 87% in DMSO, respectively.

This means that the syn-1,3-diaxial steric effect predominates over stabilization by an IAHB for 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexanes, as opposed to what occurs for trans-2-chloro- and trans-2-bromocyclohexanol [16].

The larger stability of eq–eq conformer is due to the classic syn-1,3-diaxial steric effect and to electrostatic interactions, which occur in the ax–ax conformer, since the C–X and C–O dipoles are aligned in a repulsive fashion in the ax–ax conformer and are far apart in the eq–eq conformer [18]. The distance between the oxygen atom and Cl (3.26), Br (3.37), I (3.54) and CH₃ (3.19 Å) for compounds 1–4, and between the oxygen atom and F (3.04), Cl (3.31), Br (3.41), I (3.43) and CH₃ (3.11 Å) for compounds 5–9, obtained at the B3LYP/6-311+g** level are almost the same or smaller than the sum of the van der Waals radii for non-bonded atoms (O+F 2.99; O+Cl 3.27; O+Br 3.37; O+I 3.50 and O+CH₃ 3.22 Å).

### 4. Conclusions

An analysis of the influence of conformer dipole moments and of solvent effects on the $^3J_{\text{H1}}$ and $^3J_{\text{H3}}$ vicinal coupling constants, in the conformational equilibria of the cis isomer of 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexanes leads to the conclusion that these equilibria do not depend on the solvent polarity, as has been observed for 1,2-disubstituted cyclohexanes. Instead, they are mostly determined by syn-1,3-diaxial steric and electrostatic effects, which lead to the large predominance of the eq–eq conformer, in any of the solvents studied. This must be true for any 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexane, when the ax–ax conformer can not be stabilized by an strong intramolecular hydrogen bond.
