# COOPERATIVE INFLUENCE OF $\boldsymbol{H}_{2}$ AND $\boldsymbol{C}_{2} \boldsymbol{H}_{4}$
## MOLECULES ON THE $\boldsymbol{b-a}$ AND $\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{X}$ TRANSITIONS
### IN THE $\boldsymbol{O}_{2}$ MOLECULE IN A TERNARY COMPLEX

B. F. Minaev, $^{\text{a}}$ N. M. Ivanova, $^{\text{b*}}$ I. V. Fedulova, $^{\text{b}}$
G. I. Kobzev, $^{\text{b}}$ and Z. M. Muldakhmetov $^{\text{b}}$

UDC 535.37:539.19

Moments of the $b-a$ and $a-X$ transitions induced in an oxygen molecule by collision in the $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}$ $+H_{2}$ complex are calculated by the semiempirical MINDO/3 method with allowance for the spin-or- bital interaction. On the basis of two collisional models it is shown that the cooperative influence of $C_{2}H_{4}$ and $H_{2}$ molecules can be both enhancing and weakening as compared with their individual influ- ence. Here, the relative location of the oxygen molecule and its surroundings plays an important role.

Keywords: oxygen, transition moment, collision complex, quantum-chemical calculation.

The influence of intermolecular interaction on the forbidden $b^{1}\Sigma_{g}^{+}-a^{1}\Delta_{g}$ and $a^{1}\Delta_{g}-X^{3}\Sigma_{g}^{-}$ transitions (hereafter, the $b-a$ and $a-X$ transitions) in molecular oxygen continues to be actively studied [1-7]. In particu- lar, the mechanism that underlies the enhancement of the intensity of the $b-a$ and $a-X$ transitions in collisions of $O_{2}$ with diamagnetic molecules and that was suggested earlier on the basis of semiempirical quantum-chemi- cal calculations [8-10] has recently been confirmed in calculations by *ab initio* methods with account for con- figuration interaction [6, 7] The results obtained for the simple model system $O_{2}+H_{2}$ are in good agreement with experimental data for various oxygen-gas mixtures [11].

The present work is devoted to a theoretical study of the triple collision complex $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}+H_{2}$, which is a more accurate model of real multicomponent oxygen-containing systems. Calculations are made by the semiempirical MINDO/3 method [12] with account for configuration interaction for single and double ex- citations and spin-orbital interaction according to perturbation theory [13]. The distance between the molecules of oxygen and ethylene was prescribed constant and equal to $3.8\ \mathring{A}$. The choice of this distance was deter- mined, first, by the sum of the Van der Waals radii of these two molecules and, second, by the value of the intermolecular equilibrium distance $R_{e}=3.8\ \mathring{A}$ for the double collision complex $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}$ in the ground state $X^{3}\Sigma_{g}^{-}$, $S_{0}$, obtained on the basis of nonempirical calculation methods with account for configuration interaction [14]. Two models of approach of $H_{2}$ and of the $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}$ complex were chosen: I, the $H_{2}$ molecule is located above the center of the $O_{2}-C_{2}H_{4}$ bond so that $R(O_{2}-H_{2})=R(C_{2}H_{4}-H_{2})$; II, $H_{2}$ is above the $O_{2}$ molecule and interacts with $\pi_{g,z}^{-}$, while $C_{2}H_{4}$ interacts with the $\pi_{g,x}$ molecular orbitals of oxygen:

The main distinguishing features of these two collision models of $O_2 + C_2H_4 + H_2$ are the stronger (model I) and weaker (model II) interaction between the ethylene and hydrogen molecules. First of all, this is manifested in the structure of molecular orbitals obtained in calculations of charge-transfer collisions for a closed shell and that are basic for calculations of configuration interaction. Since in the ternary complex the molecules are, in fact, isolated, we can use the designations of the molecular orbitals of these individual molecules. Thus, in the highest occupied molecular orbital of ethylene $(1b_{3u})$ the impurity of $1s$-atomic orbitals of hydrogen atoms is from 0.00026 at $R = 5.0$ Å to 0.10627 at $R = 2.4$ Å in the case of model I and from 0.00002 to 0.00178 for model II at the same values of $R$. Moreover, there is also a difference in the behavior of the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g,x}$ and $\pi_{g,z}$ of oxygen, which are the higher occupied and lower unoccupied molecular orbitals for the entire complex in its formal ground state with a closed shell. The contributions of some atomic orbitals to the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g,x}$ and $\pi_{g,z}$ are presented in Table 1 (the signs $\pm$ and $\mp$ show that symmetrically arranged atoms make the same contribution but with another sign).

It is seen from Table 1 that as $H_2$ and $O_2 + C_2H_4$ approach, there occurs strong mixing of the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g,x}$ and $\pi_{g,z}$ of $O_2$ in the case of model I and their abrupt interchange in the region of $R = 3.6$-$3.4$ Å for model II, which can be tracked by the contributions of the atomic orbitals $p_x$ and $p_z$ of oxygen. Some other impurities of atomic orbitals in the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g,x}$ and $\pi_{g,z}$ change in accordance with these contributions. The change in the contributions of the atomic orbitals of ethylene atoms, in particular, the atomic orbitals $p_x$ and $p_z$ of carbons in the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g,z}$ and $\pi_{g,x}$ of $O_2$, respectively, turned out to be of interest. Despite the fact that the distance between $O_2$ and $C_2H_4$ remains constant, a decrease in $R$ leads to a gradual increase in the impurities of these atomic orbitals between $O_2 + C_2H_4$ and $H_2$. This is a specific feature of the ternary collision system under consideration. One of the reasons for such a cooperative interaction is, apparently, the presence of the $H_2$ molecule.

On the basis of the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g,x}$ and $\pi_{g,z}$, configurations are formed which determine the $X^3\Sigma_g^-$, $a^1\Delta_g$, and $b^1\Sigma_g^+$ states of oxygen [8]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi\left(X^{3} \Sigma_{g}^{-}\right) & =0.99 \varphi\left(\pi_{g, x} \pi_{g, z}\right), \\
\Psi\left(a^{1} \Delta_{g}\right) & =0.72 \varphi\left(\pi_{g, x}^{2}\right)-0.69 \varphi\left(\pi_{g, z}^{2}\right), \\
\Psi\left(b^{1} \Sigma_{g}^{+}\right) & =0.69 \varphi\left(\pi_{g, x}^{2}\right)+0.72 \varphi\left(\pi_{g, z}^{2}\right).
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

The analysis of the wave functions of these states in the ternary collision complex $O_2 + C_2H_4 + H_2$ has shown that in addition to the main configurations (1) there are impurities from excitations in individual molecules, and also from the charge-transfer states. For the mean kinetic distance $R = 3.0$ Å the largest impurity coefficients of the charge-transfer states are due to the configurations $H_2^+C_2H_4^-$ (0.0049), $O_2^+C_2H_4^-$ (0.0029), and $C_2H_4^+H_2^-$ (0.0024) in the $a^1\Delta_g$-state, to $O_2^+C_2H_4^-$ (0.0052), $C_2H_4^-H_2^+$ (0.0029), and $H_2^-C_2H_4^+$ (0.0025) in the $b^1\Sigma_g^+$ state, and to other configurations (with lower values of the coefficients of the charge-transfer states). In the $X^3\overline{\Sigma_g}$ state the contributions of the states of charge transfer between ethylene and hydrogen are absent, while the largest coefficients of admixture of the charge-transfer states at $R = 3.0$ Å are due to the configurations $\mathrm{O_2^+C_2H_4^-}$, $\mathrm{H_2^+O_2^-}$ (0.0017 each) and $\mathrm{C_2H_4^+O_2^-}$ (0.0015).

In model II, the considered states of oxygen also have contributions from different charge-transfer states; however, the contributions from the states with charge transfer between $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$ and $\mathrm{H_2}$ are smaller by almost two orders of magnitude than for model I.

Thus, not only do individual partners in their own right influence the collision-induced intermolecular changes in oxygen, but they also exert a cooperative influence that depends on the intermolecular interaction between them. This cooperative action on the intensity of the radiative transitions $b$–$a$ and $a$–$X$ can have an enhancing or a weakening effect. This is confirmed in Table 2 by the values of the moments of the $b$–$a$ and $a$–$X$ transitions in the ternary collision complex $\mathrm{O_2 + C_2H_4 + H_2}$ and (for comparison) in the double complexes $\mathrm{O_2 + H_2}$ and $\mathrm{O_2 + C_2H_4}$. The latter were calculated with account for the corresponding types of excitations in $\mathrm{O_2}$, $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$, and $\mathrm{H_2}$ molecules and with the same set of configurations with charge transfer as for the ternary complex. It follows from Table 2 that in model I the values of $M_{b-a}$ and $M_{a-X}$ at distances $R = 4.4$–3.0 Å are larger than for individual complexes $\mathrm{O_2 + H_2}$ and $\mathrm{O_2 + C_2H_4}$ (at $R = 3.8$ Å). For model II, on the contrary, their values are smaller than for the double complexes at corresponding values of $R$, i.e., the influence of the $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$ and $\mathrm{H_2}$ molecules that interact with different molecular orbitals $\pi_g$ of oxygen is mutually suppressed.

The mechanisms of the initiation of the collision-induced $b$–$a$ and $a$–$X$ transitions are considered in detail in relation to the double $\mathrm{O_2 + H_2}$ and $\mathrm{O_2 + C_2H_4}$ complexes in [6, 14]. It was noted that the $b$–$a$ transition in an oxygen molecule (electric quadrupole in nature) acquires a relatively strong electrodipole moment. The main contribution to it is made by the difference between the constants of the electrodipole moments of the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g,x}$ and $\pi_{g,z}$, each of which has two electrons:
$$
\mathbf{M}_{b-a}=\langle b^1\Sigma_g^+|e\mathbf{r}|a^1\Delta_g\rangle=0.5\left(\mathbf{M}_x-\mathbf{M}_z\right). \tag{2}
$$

Since the collision complexes are planar ($xy$ is the symmetry plane), added to the molecular orbital $\pi_{g,x}$ of $\mathrm{O_2}$ are the atomic orbitals of the colliding molecule, whereas the molecular orbital $\pi_{g,z}$ does not have such admixtures. Consequently, the difference between the dipole moments in expression (2) is caused by collisions of

oxygen and is determined by the specific interaction of two degenerate molecular orbitals $\pi_{g}$ with the orbitals of the solvent molecule [10].

For the ternary collision complex $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}+H_{2}$, both molecular orbitals $\pi_{g}$ of $O_{2}$, as shown in Table2, have admixtures from the atomic orbitals of $C_{2}H_{4}$ and from the impinging molecule of the $H_{2}$. These ad mixture coefficients are taken into account in calculations of the constants of the dipole moments of the mo lecular orbitals $\pi_{g, x}$ and $\pi_{g, z}$ [13]. Their difference in Eq. (2) determines the contribution to the total moment of the $b-a$ transition $M_{b-a}^{tot}$ from the main electron configurations of the states $b^{1}\sum_{g}^{-}$ and $a^{1}\Delta_{g}$ (1) calculated on the basis of the self-consistent field of molecular orbitals. If we designate this contribution as $M_{b-a}^{SCF}$, then in a first approximation all the remaining contributions obtained as a result of the addition of other configurations to the wave functions of the states $b$ and due to the effect of configuration interaction have the form

$$
\mathbf{M}_{b-a}^{\mathrm{CI}}=\mathbf{M}_{b-a}^{\mathrm{tot}}-\mathbf{M}_{b-a}^{\mathrm{SCF}}.
$$

The calculated values of the total moment of the $b-a$ transition $M_{b-a}^{tot}$ and its components $M_{b-a}^{CI}$ and $M_{b-a}^{SCF}$ for the $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}+H_{2}$ complex are presented in Table 2, from which it follows that for model I the total contribution of all added configurations is substantially larger than the contribution of $M_{b-a}^{SCF}$ determined by formula (2). In turn, as shown above, the amount and the value of the contributions to the wave functions of the states $b$ and $a$, including the charge-transfer states, depends on the relative position of the molecules in the complex. For model II, the influence of both components is comparable, with the contribution of the con figuration interaction at smaller $R$ being predominant. In this case, the difference between the constants of the dipole moments of the molecular orbitals $\pi_{g, x}$ and $\pi_{g, z}$ turns out to be smaller for model I than for model II, in which $M_{\pi x}$ and $M_{\pi z}$ are formed virtually in some $O_{2}+H_{2}$ and $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}$ complexes and hardly compensate for each other in subtraction.

As the calculations showed, the moment of the $a-X$ transition in the $O_{2}+C_{2}H_{4}+H_{2}$ complex for col lision models I and II (Table 2) is mainly determined by the difference between the constants of the dipole moments of the $X^{3}\sum_{g}^{-}$ and $a^{1}\Delta_{g}$ states induced by the collision and also by the borrowing of the intensity from the electrodipole $b-a$ transition. Here, the contributions from $M(X^{3}\sum_{g}^{-})-M(a^{1}\Delta_{g})$ up to $R=3.0$ Å exceed the contributions from the $b-a$ transitions.

Thus, the calculations performed by the semiempirical MINDO/3 method with account for configura tion interaction and spin-orbital interaction clearly predict enhancement or decrease of the $b-a$ and $a-X$ transi tions in an oxygen molecule in the presence of two collision partners in comparison with their separate influence. Here, the relative location of an oxygen molecule and of its surroundings must play a significant role.
