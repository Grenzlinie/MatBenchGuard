**ABSTRACT:** A double zwitterionic Schiff base was synthesized using the amino acid L-histidine and o-vanillin (2-hydroxy-3-methoxy benzaldehyde). Both the phenol and carboxyl groups are deprotonated, and the imine nitrogen atom and histidine-imidazole ring are protonated to give the double zwitterion with an intramolecular (imine)$\mathrm{N-H^{+\cdots-}O(phenol)}$ hydrogen bond (ketoamine form). Such a ketoamine form in a double zwitterion is assumed in the catalytic cycle of enzymatic transformations of amino acids with the cofactor (vitamin B6) pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP). A high-resolution, low-temperature, single-crystal X-ray diffraction data set on $N$-o-vanillylidene-L-histidine (also named 3-methoxysalicylidene-L-histidine or $N$-(2-oxy-3-methoxy-benzylidene)-L-histidine, OVHIS) is used in the analysis of molecular electrostatic properties and intermolecular interactions. All oxygen atoms in the molecule (four in total) are mutually almost coplanar and located (externally) on the same side of molecule. These four O atoms carry significant negative charge and form a large area of strong negative electrostatic potential (appropriate for bonding to a metal atom). The protonated and, thus, positively charged imidazole ring is situated on the opposite side of the molecule from the area of the O atoms. Consequently, the OVHIS molecule is highly polarized and has a very high molecular dipole moment of $42.4\ \mathrm{D}$ in the solid state (calculated from experimental X-ray data). Two strong intermolecular charge-assisted $\mathrm{N-H^{+\cdots-}O}$ hydrogen bonds (with $\mathrm{H\cdots O}$ distances of $1.61\ \mathrm{\AA}$) together with other $\mathrm{D-H\cdots O}$ interactions ($\mathrm{D = N, C}$) contribute to a large molecular dipole enhancement which occurs upon crystallization. The topologies of the bonding within the molecule as well as its hydrogen bonds have been investigated according to Bader's quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM). $^1$H solid-state magic angle spinning nuclear magnetic resonance (MAS NMR) was used to confirm the zwitterionic structure in the solid state.

## Introduction

The desire to understand and to be able to control the arrangement of molecules in the solid state is at the essence of modern crystal engineering. Hydrogen bonding, a long-standing favorite for crystal design, $^1$ has proven particularly useful.$^{2,3}$ The enhancement of hydrogen bond strength, which may be linked to robustness, by ionic charge has long been recognized, and some of the strongest hydrogen bonds are "charge-assisted," wherein the hydrogen bond donor and/or acceptor carry positive and negative ionic charges, respectively.$^{3-6}$ Both positive and negative ionic charges can occur within a single "zwitterionic" molecule. Amino acids exist in the zwitterionic form $^+\mathrm{H_3N-CH(R)-COO^-}$ around physiological pH values, and, thus, can be easily available, highly modular, and enantiomerically pure building blocks in charge-assisted hydrogen-bonded networks. $^7$ Histidine, for example, exists mainly in its dipolar form in the pH range of 6.0-9.17. The isoelectronic point of histidine is at $\mathrm{pH = 7.58}$. Furthermore, zwitterionic compounds act as stimulators of the enzyme myeloperoxidase and as leukotriene inactivators $^8$ stimulate production of hypochlorous acid and are used in the removal of reactive oxidants $^9$ and find application in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, other inflammatory joint diseases such as ankylosing spondylitis and psoriatic arthritis, cancer therapy, and in general as antineoplastic agents.$^{10}$ Zwitterionic ligands can transport metal salts by coordinating both the metal cation and its attendant anion(s). $^{11}$ Phosphonium borate zwitterions mediate the metal-free hydrogenation of imines.$^{12}$

In order to convert a neutral hydrogen bond $\mathrm{A-H\cdots B}$ into the corresponding zwitterionic form $\mathrm{A^-\cdots H-^+B}$, external electric fields are required$^{13,14}$ which can be produced by solvent dipoles or the assembly of many hydrogen bridges of the same type. The experimental charge density analysis (ECDA) based on low-temperature, high-resolution X-ray diffraction data is the most informative tool in the study of the molecular electronic features.$^{15}$ ECDA allows an assessment of the bonding situation in (small) molecules $^{16}$ and also an assessment of their intermolecular interactions, also to receptors for a (biological) structure-activity correlation.$^{17-21}$ Here we describe the high resolution molecular and crystal structure and charge density analysis of the enantiomeric double zwitterionic amino acid Schiff base OVHIS, derived from the condensation of o-vanillin with L-histidine (Scheme 1). Refinement. The Hansen-Coppens multipole model has been used for the multipole refinement and the description of the molecular electron density.⁴⁵

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\text{at}(r)} = \rho_{\text{core}(r)} + P_{\text{val}} \kappa^3 \rho_{\text{val}}(\kappa r) + \kappa'^3 \sum_{\text{l}=0}^{\text{lmax}} R_{\text{nl}}(\kappa' r) \sum_{\text{m}=0}^{\text{l}} P_{\text{lm} \pm} y_{\text{lm} \pm}(\theta, \varphi) \\
(1)
\end{aligned}
$$

According to this model, the electron density of each atom is given as a sum of a core electron density ($\rho_{\text{core}}$), a spherical valence electron density ($\rho_{\text{valence}}$) and aspherical part of the atomic electron density. The core and valence electron densities are both obtained from the Hartree−Fock wave function of the free atoms or ions. $\kappa$ is a contraction−expansion coefficient which modifies the radial distribution of the valence electron density. It is refined along with the $P_{\text{val}}$ parameter which represents the valence electron population. The final term which describes the aspherical part of the electron density is given as a series of density normalized spherical harmonics. The radial part for the deformation function is given as Slater type function: $R_{nl}(r) = Nr^{n_{l}} \exp(-\xi_{l}r)$, where $N$ is a normalization factor. In eq 1 $\kappa'$ is the contraction−expansion coefficient of the radial part of aspherical valence shell, while $P_{\text{lm}}$ are multipole population parameters.

The crystal structure was determined on the basis of accurate low temperature data using the WinGX program package.⁴⁶ Conventional and electron density refinements were performed with the XD2006 program package⁴⁷ implementing the Hansen−Coppens multipole model.⁴⁵ The excellent diffraction data set gave a very good basis for a successful multipole refinement performed without any constraints for non-hydrogen atom and with minimal constraints for $\kappa$ values of H atoms. The $\kappa$ parameters within each of the following H atom groups (see Figure 1) were constrained to be equal: (a) three H atoms in the methyl group; (b) H5, H6, H7, and H8; (c) H9, H11a, and H11b; (d) H13 and H14; (e) two H atoms in the water molecule. It was allowed for nitrogen-bonded H1N, H2N, and H3N to have individual $\kappa$ values. All non-hydrogen atoms were treated at the octupolar level, while an axially symmetric quadrupole was the highest multipole on the H atoms. The accuracy of H atom positions is a very weak point of classical single-crystal X-ray analysis. The main problem is that H atoms (as other non-H atoms) are treated by a spherical model which is especially inadequate for H atoms because a significant part of the electron density of the hydrogen atom is located between the H atom and it is bonded X atom. Because of that reason X−H bonds from classical single-crystal X-ray analysis are shorter (because of this systematic error for H atoms) than those obtained from neutron diffraction. However, we applied here a multipole refinement to the X-ray diffraction data set of OVHIS. This model treats atoms by aspherical electron density parameters which are able to describe in a proper way the electron density distribution at the H atoms. Consequently, the accuracy of the H-atom positions is much higher than in a classical single-crystal X-ray analysis (cf. Table 4). Thus, estimated standard deviations (ESDs) for structural parameters (distances and angles) in this OVHIS structure are much better for the high-resolution data (treated by multipole refinement) than those for a classical single-crystal X-ray analysis. Still, the ESDs for H atom parameters are 1 order of magnitude larger than those for non-H geometrical parameters (cf. Table 2 and Table S3, Supporting Information).

Conventional spherical refinement was carried out for 10 145 independent reflections (7317 with $I > 3\sigma(I)$), $R = 0.0281$ (Table 1). The multipole refinement for the same data set yielded an aspherical density model with $R = 0.0116$ (Table 1). The fit achieved in the aspherical atom refinement can be also assessed by examination of the residual maps, calculated after completion of the refinement. The maps contain very little noise, attesting to the quality of the data; they do not show any features above 0.05 e Å⁻³ (Figure 2). These facts qualify the present study as of high quality even for the standards usually applied in the experimental charge density analysis. It should be noted that the problem of phase retrieval in noncentrosymmetric space groups may lead to large correlation effects between multipoles and structural parameters (usually thermal parameters).¹⁵ᵇ However, in a recent paper with an experimental charge density study of a relatively large organic system with 52 atoms in the noncentrosymmetric space group $P2_12_12_1$ (the same space group as in our work), the authors proved the high quality and reliability of their results.⁴⁸ In our study we did not have any problem during the multipole refinement. The Hirshfeld rigid bond test⁴⁹ showed that the differences of mean-square displacements between bonded atoms are less than $10 \times 10^{-4}$ Å⁻². Dynamic deformation density maps (Figure S25, Supporting Information) exhibit very similar features as static deformation maps (cf. Figure 6).

Molecular and extended structure graphics were obtained with DIAMOND.⁵⁰ Crystal data and details on the structure refinement are given in Table 1. The data have been deposited with the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Center (CCDC Nos. 764272 and 764273 for the high and normal resolution structure, respectively). All structural details including multipole parameters can be found in the CIF file incorporated in the Supporting Information.

Ab Initio Computation. Ab initio single molecule calculations of the electrostatic potential and the dipole moment of the OVHIS molecules in gas-phase were performed using the Gaussian 03 program⁵¹ with Hartree−Fock (HF) calculations at the 6-31G** level. The coordinates used in the calculation are those obtained from the multipole refinement.

## Results and Discussion

X-ray structure analysis indicates that OVHIS exists in a double zwitterionic form in the solid state (Figure 1). Both the carboxyl and the vanillylidene phenol groups are deprotonated, and the imino nitrogen and the histidine nitrogen atoms are protonated. The positions of these protic hydrogen atoms were located and could be refined. Compound OVHIS crystallizes with one water molecule of crystallization in the asymmetric unit as OVHIS·H₂O. The zwitterionic molecule is part of an extended three-dimensional (3D) hydrogen bonded network which will be discussed based on the experimental charge density distribution and topology of intermolecular interactions.

¹H NMR spectroscopy revealed that OVHIS is quickly hydrolyzed (cf. Scheme 1) and in equilibrium with $o$-vanillin and l-histidine in aqueous solution. The OVHIS ¹H NMR spectrum in D₂O is a superposition of the $o$-vanillin, l-histidine, and OVHIS signals (Figure 3 and Figure S4−S6 in Supporting Information). In DMSO-$d_6$ the ¹H NMR spectrum of OVHIS shows (more clearly than in D₂O) two different coupling constants of the diastereotopic hydrogen atoms 11a and 11b with H9 at the asymmetric carbon atom.

There appear to be very few reports and even fewer with structural proof on the formation of double zwitterions, mostly on diaminoalkylphosphorothioic acids⁵⁶ and small peptides.⁵⁷ Deprotonation of the phenol group and protonation of the imine nitrogen in salicylaldimine-type compounds to give a zwitterion are rare¹⁴,⁵⁸,⁵⁹ in view of the vast number of salicylaldimine derivatives. Yet, such an intramolecular shift of the H atom from (imine)N⋯H−O(phenol) (enolamine form) to (imine)N−H⁺⋯⁻O(phenol) (ketoamine form) is assumed as the first step in the catalytic cycle of enzymatic transformations of amino acids with the cofactor (vitamin B6) pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP).¹⁴,⁶⁰ This intramolecular hydrogen transfer is assisted by the protonation of the pyridine ring, which enhances the electric field (Scheme 3).

Molecular dipole enhancement for OVHIS molecule is very large (over 66%) and probably also could be explained by contribution of hydrogen bonds between H-acceptors and H-donors which have large space separation in the molecule. In the above-mentioned case,⁶⁵ the C—H$\cdots$O hydrogen bonds cause an increase of MDM, but in the OVHIS crystal structure much stronger hydrogen bonds of the charge-assisted N—H⁺$\cdots$$^{-}$O type exist⁴ and contribute to molecular dipole enhancement for the OVHIS molecule. Namely, two very strong intermolecular H-bonds exist exactly between the two different (and opposite) sides of molecules: the four-O-atoms of the negatively charged region and the imidazole positively charged region of the OVHIS molecule. The line that connects the negatively and positively charged regions corresponds (coincides) very well with the direction of the MDM and the direction of the strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds. These hydrogen bonds will be discussed in detail in the next section.

From the above discussion and analysis of net atomic charges, it is obvious that the OVHIS molecule is very polarized. For three selected fragments in the molecule (the fragments are selected according to their spatial position; see Supporting Information, Figures S12 and S13), the value of the calculated charge for each fragment is very different. A similar polarization is also found for the isolated OVHIS molecule but in a lesser degree (Figures S14 and S15 in Supporting Information). The largest difference between the experimental atomic charges (Table 3) and those for the isolated molecule (ab initio single molecule calculations, Table S5, Supporting Information) exists for O2, O3, and O4 which form the most significant intermolecular H-bonds (oxygen atoms are more negative in the case of H-bonds). This experimental and ab initio theoretical comparison supports the conclusion that the H-bonds have a dominant contribution to the molecular dipole enhancement for the OVHIS molecule. In other words, the higher polarization (higher charge separation) in the H-bonded OVHIS molecule (in the same direction as the MDM in the isolated molecule) in comparison to the isolated OVHIS molecule is the source of molecular dipole enhancement.

The experimental molecular electrostatic potential (Figure 8) calculated from the charge density distribution in OVHIS is in excellent agreement with MDM. A large area of strong negative electrostatic potential (on the molecular surface and above it) is located in the region of the four O atoms and enables these atoms to act as very potent electron donors for binding to metal atoms and formation of hydrogen bonds. The phenolate and carboxylate oxygen atoms should easily attract and bind metal cations. For more negative values of the electrostatic potential this area splits in two compact regions (Figure 8d). The most positive electrostatic potential on the molecular surface is located in the imidazole region (Figure 8 and also Figures S16 and S17 in Supporting Information). A very similar distribution of the electrostatic potential was found for ab initio single point calculations (see Supporting Information, Figures S18 and S19).

## Conclusions

A high-resolution, low-temperature, single-crystal X-ray diffraction data set on $N$-$o$-vanillylidene-L-histidine (OVHIS) was the basis for the analysis of molecular electrostatic properties and intermolecular interactions. The double zwitterionic nature of the molecule in the solid state is confirmed through an experimental electron density distribution analysis according to Bader's QTAIM and by $^{1}$H solid-state MAS NMR.

The deprotonated phenolate and carboxylate groups engage in strong intermolecular and one intramolecular charge-assisted $N-H^{+}\cdots^{-}O$ hydrogen bond (with $H\cdots O$ distances of $1.61$ Å) to the protonated imine nitrogen atom and histidineimidazole ring. The phenolate, the two carboxylate, and the methoxy oxygen atoms are all mutually almost coplanar and located (externally) on the same side of molecule. The negative charge and strong negative electrostatic potential of these four O atoms together with the positively charged imidazole ring situated on the opposite side of the molecule give a highly polarized OVHIS molecule with a very high molecular dipole moment of 42.4 D in the solid state due to molecular dipole enhancement upon crystallization. The experimental and ab initio theoretical comparison reveals the higher polarization (higher charge separation) in the H-bonded OVHIS molecule in comparison to the isolated OVHIS molecule as a prominent source of molecular dipole enhancement.
