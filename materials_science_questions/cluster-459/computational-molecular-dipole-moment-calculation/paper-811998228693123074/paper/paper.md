THEORETICAL STUDY OF THE STRUCTURE OF
(O→Si)-(ACETOXYMETHYL)TRIFLUOROSILANE DIMERS

N. N. Chipanina, T. N. Aksamentova,
M. G. Voronkov, and V. K. Turchaninov

UDC 539.194:546.28

According to the data of quantum chemical calculations (HF, DFT), the isolated $sp$,$sp$ conformer of (acetoxymethyl)trifluorosilane — pentacoordinated silicon compound — forms dimers with two intermolecular bonds Si–F→Si. The =O→Si intramolecular coordination bonds become stronger, which leads to the general stabilizing effect. In polar media, dimer formation is energetically unfavorable relative to monomer formation.

Keywords: (acetoxymethyl)trifluorosilane, $ab$ $initio$ quantum chemical calculations, intermolecular coordination of fluoroorganylsilanes, =O→Si and Si–F→Si coordination bonds, pentacoordinated silicon atoms.

Compounds of pentacoordinated silicon having an =O→Si intramolecular bond and called draconoids have been comprehensively studied in crystal, solution, and gaseous state [1]. For (aroyloxymethyl)trifluorosilanes 4-$XC_6H_4C(O)OCH_2SiF_3$, it has been found that the length and hence stability of the coordination bond depend on the nature of the X substituent, medium, and physical state of the substance [1-3]. The =O→Si coordination bond is preserved even in vapors of these compounds at temperatures above 420 K. In contrast to the condensed state, however, under these conditions there is mobile equilibrium between the $syn$-$peri$-, $syn$-$peri$-planar ($sp$,$sp$), and $syn$-$peri$-, $anti$-$peri$-planar ($sp$,$ap$) (relative to the carbonyl group) forms of isostructural molecules containing penta- and tetracoordinated silicon atoms

Introduction of a methylene bridge between the aryl and carboxyl groups violates the interaction of these molecules. Because of this, in nonpolar solvents, (phenacetoxymethyl)trifluorosilane $PhCH_2C(O)OCH_2SiF_3$ can form not only intra-, but also intermolecular coordination bonds [4]. Compounds with intermolecular coordination bonds are also formed by (acylthiomethyl)trifluorosilanes ($MeCOSCH_2SiF_3$ and $PhCOSCH_2SiF_3$), whose molecules exist in solids and solutions as mixtures of two isomers with tetra- and pentacoordinated silicon atoms [5]. Consequently, the ability of the silicon atom to increase its coordination number leads to the formation of intermolecular coordination bonds along with =O→Si intramolecular bonds in silicon-containing compounds.

Also, it is not known whether dimerization with preservation of =O→Si intramolecular bonds is possible, and correlation between the formation energies of intra- and intermolecular coordination bonds has not been studied.

The dimer complexes of organic compounds of tetracoordinated silicon are characterized by the wide variety of stabilizing bonds. Thus X-ray diffraction revealed that the crystal lattice of organylsilanes $XSiH_3$ (X is a substituent with a heteroatom) has nonvalent intermolecular bonds between silicon and N, O, F, P, S, and Cl atoms [6]. According to the data of multinuclear NMR spectroscopy, intermolecular coordination of methyl- and phenyltrifluorosilanes is mediated by Si–F→Si bonds [7]. Fluorine bridges are involved in dimerization in solutions of alkylfluorosilanes ($SiMe_2F_2$, $SiMeEtOF_2$) [8]. Similar intermolecular bonds were found for solid 3-(trifluorosilyl)-2-cyanoethylpropionate containing an =O→Si intramolecular coordination bond [9]. This organosilicon compound can form complexes with coordination bonds of two types: silicon–halogen and silicon–heteroatom with a nonbonding electron pair.

Theoretical investigation of dimerization of (acetoxymethyl)trifluorosilane $CH_3C(O)OCH_2SiF_3$ **I** was carried out to determine the structure and formation energies of adducts, as well as the mutual effects of the intra- and intermolecular coordination bonds. This compound was chosen as an object of investigation based on data about the ability of draconoids with an alkyl substituent at the ester group $PhCH_2C(O)OCH_2SiF_3$ **II** and $MeC(O)SCH_2SiF_3$ to form intermolecular coordination bonds [4, 5]. The presence of an =O→Si intramolecular bond in compound **I** was revealed previously based on IR and multinuclear NMR spectroscopy data and *ab initio* calculations (RHF/6-31G*) [10, 11]. Since compound **I** has four stable conformations with tetra- and pentacoordinated Si atoms [11], various combinations are expected for dimers with different types of intermolecular bond.

Here we investigated the dimers of molecule **I** formed by its most stable *sp,sp* conformer with a pentacoordinated Si atom. Dimers with other conformers will be reported in a separate communication. Quantum chemical calculations of dimerization energies and geometrical characteristics of dimers of *sp,sp* subunits were performed by density functional theory methods (DFT-B3LYP) including a basis set superposition error (BSSE) and zero-point vibration energies (ZPE), and also by *ab initio* (MP2(Full), HF) methods using restricted (6-31G*) and extended (6-311G**) basis sets (GAUSSIAN-98 software [12]). Full geometry optimization of molecular systems corresponding to energy minima was carried out until a gradient of $10^{-5}$ au was obtained.

The intramolecular coordination energy ($\Delta E_c$) in molecule **I**, estimated as the difference between the total energies of its (*sp,sp*) and (*sp,ap*) conformers (Fig. 1), is 3.7 kcal/mole for the bond length O(2)→Si(3) ($l_{O\to Si}$) 2.201 Å (B3LYP/6-311G**, Tables 1 and 2). Previously, it was noted that (acetoxymethyl)trifluorosilane **I** is similar to (phenacetoxymethyl)trifluorosilane **II** in its electronic structure. The values of $\Delta E_c$ and $l_{O\to Si}$ obtained for the monomer form of **II** (3.7 kcal/mole and 2.198 Å, respectively) were almost the same as those for **I**. For (benzoyloxymethyl)trifluorosilane molecules **III** [2] liable to conformational isomerism in the gaseous state but not liable to self-association in solution and solid state, calculations revealed slightly increased coordination energy ($\Delta E_c = 4.2$ kcal/mole) and the length of the =O→Si intramolecular coordination bond decreased to 2.151 Å.

Theoretical analysis (B3LYP/6-311G**) of dimerization energies for Irevealed that this quantity depends on the mutual orientation of the $sp,sp$ monomer subunits. Total energy calculations were initially carried out with fixed nonvalent distances between the Si₃ and Si₉ atoms (4.6 Å) of the two subunits of the dimer. The values of the Si₃O₂O₈Si₉ dihedral angle (θ) were varied from 0 to 180° at a step of 20°. The values of other geometrical parameters were optimized. The potential curve contains two minima corresponding to two configuration isomers of the dimer. dimer **Ia,b** (Fig. 2) was determined as the difference between the total energy of the isomer and the doubled total energy of
the molecule in the *sp,sp* form (Table 1). The $\theta$ angle of these isomers is $38^\circ$ (**Ia**) and $163^\circ$ (**Ib**), and the distance between the
silicon atoms is $4.605\ \mathring{A}$ and $4.414\ \mathring{A}$, respectively (Table 2). The two forms each has two Si–F$\to$Si intermolecular
coordination bonds, in which the role of the bridging atom is played by the equatorial fluorine atom. The relative energy
stability and the geometrical characteristics of the two dimer forms (centrosymmetric form **Ia** and low-symmetry form **Ib**
having intra- and intermolecular coordination bonds differing in length) are almost independent of the calculation procedure
and the completeness of the basis set (Tables 1 and 2). The formation energies of these forms including zero-point energy
$\Delta E^0$ are 8.7 kcal/mole (**Ia**) and 10.0 kcal/mole (**Ib**) (B3LYP/6-311G** data). The F$\to$Si intermolecular bonds of the
configuration isomers are rather weak, as indicated by their larger lengths ($3.59\ \mathring{A}$ and $3.47\ \mathring{A}$). Nevertheless, dimer
formation leads to a significant shortening (strengthening) of O$\to$Si intramolecular coordination bonds by $0.077\ \mathring{A}$ in form **Ia**
and by $0.160\ \mathring{A}$ and $0.107\ \mathring{A}$ in form **Ib** relative to the O$\to$Si bond of the *sp,sp* conformer of molecule **I**.

The observed effect is probably due to the mutual polarizing effect of the bond (group) dipole moments of the
carbonyl groups and O$\to$Si–F axial fragments. In both dimer forms, the dipole moments of C=O bonds are antiparallel,
which is the major stabilizing factor. The relative arrangement of the dipoles of O$\to$Si–F fragments and C=O groups is nearly
orthogonal, and cannot have a considerable polarizing effect. The dipoles of the O$\to$Si–F fragments are parallel in **Ia**, leading
to destabilization of the molecule, and antiparallel in **Ib**, stabilizing this form (Fig. 2). As mentioned above, the energetically
more stable (by 1.3 kcal/mole) form of dimer **Ib** has shorter bonds. Thus mutual polarization of the carbonyl groups and
O$\to$Si–F fragments in dimer **Ib**, increasing the negative charge on the oxygen atom, leads to more pronounced shortening of
O$\to$Si bonds compared to **Ia** and stabilizes this form. The F$_{\text{ax}}$…H nonvalent interaction ($2.326\ \mathring{A}$) in this dimer form also
makes certain contribution to the formation energy in the isolated state.

The medium effect on the formation energies of configuration isomers **Ia,b** was considered using the Onsager model
of self-consistent reactive field (SCRF) (dielectric permittivity $\varepsilon$ taken to be 36). On passing from the isolated state to the
medium ($\varepsilon = 36$) for the *sp,sp* conformer of molecule **I** the O$\to$Si bond length decreases considerably (by $0.18\ \mathring{A}$, Table 2).
For the medium, this geometrical parameter of the dimer in forms **Ia** and **Ib** changes less significantly (Table 2); for
configuration isomer **Ib**, one of the bonds is shortened, and the other is lengthened to approximately the same extent. As a
result, in polar media, dimer formation of compound **I** is energetically unfavorable: $\Delta E = 1.4$ kcal/mole (**Ia**) and
2.1 kcal/mole (**Ib**). This occurs because in solution, the *sp,sp* conformer of molecule **I** whose dipole moment is $\mu = 5.72$ D is
stabilized to the greatest extent. The solvent also affects the relative energy stability of forms **Ia** and **Ib** of the dimer. In
highly polar solvents, configuration isomer **Ia** having a larger dipole moment (4.82 D) than isomer **Ib** (1.20 D) is
energetically more stable.
