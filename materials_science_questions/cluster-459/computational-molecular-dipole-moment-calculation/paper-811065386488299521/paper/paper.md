# MANY-BODY PERTURBATION THEORY AND POLARIZATION PROPAGATOR STUDIES OF THE STRUCTURE, ENERGETICS AND EXCITATION SPECTRUM OF $\text{CO}_3$

Sylvio CANUTO $^{1}$ and Geerd H.F. DIERCKSEN

Max-Planck-Institut für Physik und Astrophysik, Institut für Astrophysik,
Karl-Schwarzschild-Strasse 1, D-8046 Garching bei München, FRG

Received 17 September 1987

Geometry optimization has been performed on $\text{CO}_3$ in the SCF approximation and in the second-, third-, and fourth-order MBPT approximations limited to single and double substitutions using a double-zeta plus polarization basis set. The energetics of the formation and decomposition of $\text{CO}_3$ from the reactions $\text{CO}_2 + \text{O}(^1\text{D}) \rightarrow \text{CO}_3$ and $\text{CO}_3 + \text{CO} \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2$, respectively, have been calculated in several approximations including full fourth-order MBPT. In addition first-order polarization propagator calculations have been performed to identify the low-lying excited states.

## 1. Introduction

Carbon trioxide $\text{CO}_3$ was first produced by Katakis and Taube [1] in a gas phase reaction between $\text{O}(^1\text{D})$ and $\text{CO}_2$. The first direct observation of it was made by Moll et al. [2] and Weissburger et al. [3]. Five infrared active bands were observed implying that the molecular geometry has less than threefold symmetry. For several years the question remained as to whether the structure is cyclic with $\text{C}_{2\text{v}}$ symmetry or open with $\text{C}_{\text{s}}$ symmetry [4-11]. Recently, Pople et al. [10] have performed a full geometry optimization of both structures at the HF/4-31G level followed by single-point second-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory calculations with the 6-31G* basis. They found the $\text{C}_{2\text{v}}$ structure to be more stable than the $\text{C}_{\text{s}}$ by $\approx 55$ kcal/mol after the inclusion of the second-order contribution to the electron correlation energy. It is now believed that $\text{CO}_3$ has a singlet ground state of $\text{C}_{2\text{v}}$ symmetry [10].

A full geometry optimization of $\text{CO}_3$ including the electron correlation contribution is clearly the next important step to determine reliably its ground state structure. In this paper such a study up to the fourth-order MBPT approximation limited to single and double substitutions has been performed [12-15] using a double-zeta plus polarization basis set. To obtain accurate values for the energies involved in the reactions
$$\text{CO}_2 + \text{O}(^1\text{D}) \rightarrow \text{CO}_3 \quad \text{(formation)},$$
$$\text{CO}_3 + \text{CO} \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 \quad \text{(decomposition)},$$
a complete fourth-order MBPT calculation including all single, double, triple and quadruple substitutions using a more flexible basis set has also been performed. McElroy and Hunter [16] have discussed the importance of these reactions in the chemistry of the upper atmosphere of Mars. In addition, to identify the low-lying singlet excited states polarization propagator calculations at different levels of approximations have been carried out.

As the experimental geometry of CO₃ is unknown it is not possible to determine directly the errors involved in our calculated values. A simple way to assess the accuracy of the calculated equilibrium bond lengths of CO₃ is by comparing the bond length of CO and CO₂ calculated at the same level of approximations as used in CO₃ with the experimental bond length of CO and CO₂, respectively. In table 2 this comparison is made. For both molecules CO and CO₂ the results obtained at the full fourth-order MBPT(4) level are the same as those obtained at the SD MBPT(4) level. In the case of CO the bond distance is underestimated by about 0.01 Å whereas for CO₂ it is overestimated by about twice this value compared to the experimental value. These results indicate that the calculated bond lengths in CO₃ should be accurate to within 0.02 Å.

The vertical excitation energies from the ground state and the associated oscillator strengths are determined directly from the poles and residues of the polarization propagator [20]. As in ground state calculations, the results are affected by the choice of the orbital basis, the excitation space and the inclusion of electron correlation. As the complexity of the calculation increases with the size of the excitation space we have investigated first the effects of truncating the excitation space. In table 8 we list the calculated excitation energies for the two lowest excited states as a function of the virtual orbital space. All occupied orbitals are included except the four energetically lowest occupied orbitals which essentially correspond to atomic 1s orbitals. From table 8 it is concluded that the excitation energies are affected very much by the truncation of the virtual orbital space. For the largest excitation space considered, corresponding to excluding only the four innermost occupied and the four outermost virtual orbitals the number of excitations is 212, 125, 143 and 191 for the $A_1$, $A_2$, $B_1$ and $B_2$ symmetries, respectively. The results of table 8 indicate clearly that the truncation of the excitation space may introduce uncontrolled errors and therefore all polarization propagator calculations presented here will utilize all the orbitals other than the four energetically lowest occupied orbitals and their virtual counterparts. This presents a serious limitation to higher-order calculations. Thus all excitation energies and moments have been calculated in the first-order polarization propagator approximation which is equivalent to RPA [20].

The calculated first excited state is of $^1B_1$ symmetry, is accessible from the ground state by a dipole transition with radiation in the green part of the visible spectrum, and is polarized perpendicular to the molecular plane. Though allowed this transition carries very low intensity as can be seen from the value for the oscillator strength. This state is followed by a $^1A_1$ state polarized along the $C_{2v}$ axis and also associated with a very low intensity. Next, we find a $^1A_2$ state with a dipole-forbidden transition from the ground state. It is only the fourth excited state that carries appreciable intensity for transition from the ground state. These results make a poor prospect for a direct observation of low-lying transitions in $CO_3$.
