# Molecular structure and vibrational IR spectrum of formamide revisited: ab initio post-Hartree-Fock study $^{1}$

Józef S. Kwiatkowski $^{a, *}$, Jerzy Leszczyński $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Institute of Physics, N. Copernicus University, 87-100 Toruń, Poland
$^{b}$ Department of Chemistry, Jackson State University, Jackson, MS 39217, USA

(Received 23 December 1992)

## Abstract
The results of high-level ab initio calculations using a 6-311G(3df,2p) basis set with electron correlation included at the second-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory are reported for molecular parameters (geometry, dipole moment, rotational constants) and the vibrational IR spectrum (harmonic wavenumbers, absolute intensities) of formamide. The present calculations predict such a small deviation from the planarity of the molecule that it should be considered as a planar system. Computed dipole moment, rotational constants and the vibrational IR spectra of formamide and its deuterated species agree very well with the available experimental data.

## Introduction
Knowledge of the structure and properties of formamide is important because of two main reasons. Firstly, the molecule is the simplest amide containing a prototype NH-C=O peptide linkage. Therefore the understanding of the properties of this model amide is of crucial importance in protein chemistry and physics. Secondly, since the molecule is the simplest species containing two important functional groups, namely the carbonyl $(C=O)$ and amino $(NH_{2})$ group, the knowledge of the molecular properties of formamide is important for structure-activity relationships in organic and physical chemistry. Owing to these reasons the structure and properties of formamide have been the subject of many experimental and theoretical studies. The review of these studies goes beyond the scope of the present paper. Instead, readers are referred to the most recent ab initio quantum mechanical studies on molecular structure [1-3], formamide-formamidic acid tautomerization [4] and vibrational IR spectra [5-8]. In these papers the references to experimental (and previous theoretical) studies can be found.

In the authors' previous paper [3], the molecular parameters (geometry, dipole moment, rotational constants) and the vibrational IR spectra (harmonic wavenumbers, absolute intensities) of formamide and its deuterated species were predicted using ab initio Hartree-Fock (HF) and second-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory (MP2) with the 6-31G(d,p) basis set. The reported data compared well with the available experimental data. The aim of this paper is to refine these theoretical studies of formamide by applying the ab initio post-HF calculations (MP2) with the 6-311G(3df,2p) basis set which is a triple valence basis set augmented by an extended set of polarization functions. ## Computational details

The geometry of formamide was optimized at the electron correlation MP2 level [10,11] using the triple valence 6-311G basis set [11,12] augmented by three sets of d and one set of f functions on carbon, nitrogen and oxygen, and by two sets of p functions on the hydrogens. The optimization was carried out for a nonplanar structure of the molecule. The geometry of the planar form of the studied species was presented in our previous paper [3].

The harmonic wavenumbers and absolute inten- sities were calculated at the optimized (nonplanar) geometry of the molecule, also at the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) level. All of the quantum mechanical calculations were performed with the GAUSSIAN 90 and GAUSSIAN 92 programs [13].

Transformation of the force constant matrix in Cartesian coordinates to the force constant matrix in internal symmetry coordinates allowed ordinary normal coordinate calculations to be performed, as described by Schachtschneider [14]. The potential energy distribution (PED) analysis to assign the modes was carried out using the PACK program [15]. This program was also used to calculate the vibrational harmonic wavenumbers of deuterated species of formamide using the force constant matrix from the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) calculations for formamide itself.

## Results and discussions

### Molecular parameters

The experimental studies of the formamide structure have led to several contradictory conclu- sions. While the first microwave spectroscopic study [16] suggested that the molecule was entirely planar, Costain and Dowling [17], based on subsequent microwave studies, concluded that the molecule was nonplanar. Later, the data from the more intensive microwave spectroscopic studies of Hirota et al. [18] were consistent with a planar structure of the molecule. The recent re-analysis of vibration-rotation spectral data for formamide by Brown et al. [19] revealed that the molecule had a very shallow single-minimum inversion potential. The authors also pointed out that the ambiguity of the amino group geometry in forma- mide [17,18] arises as a result of different interpre- tations of the available experimental data. A variety of ab initio methods that differ by the level of calculations (HF or MP2, a choice of basis sets) have been used to study the formamide structure, with the main interest being focused on the question of whether the molecule is planar or nonplanar (e.g. see refs. 1-3, 7 and 19). The results of these studies have also led to some contradictory conclusions. For instance, the HF/6-31G(d,p) calculations [3,19] suggest that the planar structure of formamide lies at the global energy minimum of a very flat potential, with respect to inversion at the nitrogen atom in the vicinity of a planar structure. However, the data calculated at the MP2/6-31G(d,p) level [3] (compare also the MP2/DZP calculations [2]) slightly favor the nonplanar struc- ture of formamide, but from the energetic point of view this preference is negligible (the planar struc- ture predicted by the MP2/6-31G(d,p) calculations is only $0.003\ \text{kJ}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ above the nonplanar struc- ture [3]). It should be noted that the choice between a planar or nonplanar structure for formamide is important for prediction of the vibrational IR spectrum of the species. It is also evident that the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) calcu- lations reproduce slightly better the C-N and C- O bond lengths of formamide than the MP2 calcu- lations with the 6-31G(d,p) basis set (particularly when comparing with the recent experimental data from ref. 18). The predicted rotational constants using the geometry from MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) are also in better agreement with the constants derived from the average $(r_{s})$ structure of formamide [18,19]. The trend for a better agreement of the computed dipole moment with the experimental value is also evident when comparing the data of MP2 calculations with the 6-311G(3df,2p) and 6-31G(d,p) basis sets.

As to the question of whether or not formamide is planar, the present high-level calculations predict the deviation from planarity of the molecule to be so small that this species should be considered as a planar system. (The previous MP2/6-31G(d,p) calculations predicted a slightly larger deviation from the planarity of formamide, see Table 1.) This is in agreement with the conclusions of Hirota et al. [18] based upon an assignment of the IR spectra and an analysis of the vibrational satellites. Since, as can be seen later, the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) calculations reproduce correctly the vibrational IR spectrum of formamide (including the positions of the lowest wavenumber associated with the $NH_{2}$ torsion mode), the geometry predicted here is also in agree- ment with the recent re-analysis of the vibration- rotation spectrum of formamide [19] suggesting that the molecule has a very shallow single- minimum inversion potential.

Vibrational IR spectrum

The vibrational IR and Raman spectra of formamide and its deuterated derivatives have been recorded in the gas phase, inert gas matrices, several solvents and the solid state. The reader is particularly referred to refs. 6, 7 and 23, where discussions of experimental results are presented. It is to be noted that while the assignments of the first seven in-plane fundamentals (modes 1-7, see Table2) and the first out-of-plane one (mode 10, Table 2) is straightforward, the assignment of the remaining four modes caused problems owing to the strong dependence of their band positions upon the asso- ciation and interaction of formamide with the mole- cules of the medium (also see ref. 7 for discussion of the vibrational IR spectra for the monomer and dimer of formamide). The location of the lowest wavenumber mode (mode 12, Table 2) associated with the $NH_{2}$ torsion is the most doubtful one. This band position is very sensitive to association and is also expected to be strongly anharmonic [7,19]. Initially, Evans [21] located this mode at $750 cm^{-1}$ , but Suzuki [24], based on Raman spectro scopic data, suggested it to be in the $200 cm^{-1}$ region. King [22] on the basis of a vapor IR study located this band at $289 cm^{-1}$ and later Räsänen [23], based on inert-gas matrix studies, located this mode at about $300 cm^{-1}$ .

Table 2 collects unscaled and scaled harmonic wavenumbers and absolute intensities for form- amide predicted at both the MP2/6-31G(d,p) and MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) levels, and also experimental data. Two scaling factors were used for theoreti- cally predicted harmonic wavenumbers depending upon the spectral region: (i) the scaling factor for wavenumbers over $2000 cm^{-1}$ was calculated as the ratio of the sum of the experimental wavenumbers from argon matrix measurements [23] to the sum of the calculated harmonic wavenumbers; (ii) the scaling factor for wavenumbers under $2000 cm^{-1}$ was calculated as the ratio of the corresponding sums of wavenumbers for modes 4-11 (the position of the $NH_{2}$ torsion mode was not considered). A justification of this procedure comes from the fact that the anharmonicities of the fundamental modes for $NH_{2}$ asymmetric and symmetric stretching modes, and for the CH stretching mode are dif- ferent than those for the remaining modes. The calculated scaled wavenumbers correlated well with the experimental wavenumbers, except for the wavenumber of the $NH_{2}$ torsion mode, which was predicted to lie at a wavenumber lower by about $200 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. The present scaled MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) wavenumbers for the eleven modes (1-11) differ by no more than $12 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ from the corresponding scaled MP2/6-31G(d,p) wavenumbers. The absolute intensities from both calculations are also similar to each other. The only significant difference is for the wave- number associated with the $NH_{2}$ torsion mode. While the MP2/6-31G(d,p) calculation locates this mode at about $90 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ (with absolute intensity $A=195 \mathrm{~km} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$ ), the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) calculation predicts the corresponding band at about $190 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}(A=278 \mathrm{~km} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1})$. The latter result is in much better agreement with the very strong experimental band located at about 290- $300 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. Thus the advantage of the present cal- culations is a better prediction of the band position and its intensity for the $NH_{2}$ torsion mode.

It is to be noted that the former MP2/6-31G(d,p) calculations predicted correctly the shifts of the IR band positions of formamide upon deuteration observed in an argon matrix [23] and in chloro-form solution [25]. However, the MP2/6-31G(d,p) shifts of the $NH_{2}$ torsion mode of formamideupon $NH_{2} COH \to ND_{2} COH$ and $NH_{2} COH \to$  $ND_{2} COD$ deuteration were predicted to be in the order of $20 ~cm^{-1}$ , compared with the experimental shifts of $80-90 ~cm^{-1}$ . The present MP2/6311G(3df,2p) calculations predict these shifts to be of order $40-50 ~cm^{-1}$ . The harmonic wave numbers of the deuterated species of formamide computed at the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) level are not presented here, but the harmonic force con- stants from these calculations for formamide itself are collected in Table Al (Appendix).

## Conclusions

The main conclusions from our previous study[3] and the results presented here can be summarized as follows.

(i) While the MP2/6-31G(d,p) calculations determine an almost planar structure of formamide, with a very flat potential with respect to inversion at the $N$ atom in the vicinity of a planar structure, the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) calculations predict an essentially planar structure for the molecule.

(ii) While the computed MP2/6-31G(d,p) vibrational IR spectrum of formamide agrees well with the experimental spectrum, apart from the lowest wavenumber associated with the torsion mode of $NH_{2}$ , the agreement between the IR spec trum calculated at the MP2/6-311G(3df,2p) level and the experimental one is improved. The predicted wavenumbers of formamide for the whole IR spectral region (including the lowest wavenumber associated with the $NH_{2}$ torsion mode) agree well with the corresponding recorded wavenumbers.

(iii) Since the vibrational IR spectrum of formamide calculated at the HF/6-31G(d,p) level also compares favorably with the experimental spectrum [3,7,8], it seems that for the largersystems containing $NH_{2}$ and $C=O$ (or $C=S$ ) groups, the ab initio HF/6-31G(d,p) or MP2/6-31G(d,p) levels would be good enough to reproduce their vibrational IR spectra.

(iv) For an accurate prediction of the dipole moment magnitude of formamide the electronic density should be calculated at the electron corre- lation level (e.g. MP2) with a basis set of at least double-zeta quality (see ref. 3). Similar trends are observed for other model systems (e.g. formalde- hyde, thioformaldehyde, selenoformaldehyde and their halogenosubstituted derivatives [26], nucleic acid bases [27]).
