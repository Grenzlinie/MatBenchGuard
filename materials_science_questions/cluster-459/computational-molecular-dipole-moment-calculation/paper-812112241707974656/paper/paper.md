# The Semi-empirical SCF Treatment of $\sigma-\pi$ System
## I. Peptide Molecule

AKIRA IMAMURA, MASAHIKO KODAMA, YUSAKU TAGASHIRA

AND

CHIKAYOSHI NAGATA

Division of Biophysics, National Cancer Center Research Institute,
Tsukiji, 5-chome, Chuo-ku, Tokyo, Japan

(Received 28 April 1965)

The semi-empirical ASMO SCF method has been applied to the peptide molecule. In this treatment, $\sigma$ and $\pi$ electrons are explicitly taken into account. With regard to $\sigma$ electrons, the molecular orbital originally used by Sandorfy (1955) and extensively developed by Fukui et al. (1962) in the Hückel MO method is employed, and the integrals involved in this treatment are estimated semi-empirically.

The calculated absorption maximum, its oscillator strength, ionization potential and dipole moment are compared with experimental data and good agreement is found. The important role of the hydrogen atom in semiconductivity of protein and the possibility of $\sigma-\sigma^{*}$, $\pi-\sigma^{*}$ and $\sigma-\pi^{*}$ transitions of the peptide molecule are pointed out.

## Introduction

Since the pioneer work by Evans & Gergely (1949), the electronic structure of peptide and protein has been investigated using the molecular orbital theory in connection with the biophysical properties, that is, the semi-conductivity of protein. Evans & Gergely (1949) have calculated electronic structure of protein using the Hückel MO method to obtain the band gaps; in their calculation, the hydrogen bond plays an important role. The hydrogen bond enables the electron to conjugate in the whole system of protein, so that the protein itself can exhibit semiconductivity. Their calculation, however, was not very precise because of the approximation included in the Hückel MO method. Yomosa (1964) has calculated the electronic structure of peptide and protein using a method which is called the Hückel MO SCF method, in which the coulomb integrals are determined to be self-consistent with $\pi$ electron densities, and has obtained results that are in good agreement with experiments, i.e. with the maximum peak of u.v. absorption, oscillator

strength and dipole moment. Moreover, he has calculated the molecular orbital of a peptide by means of semi-empirical ASMO SCF and ASMO SCF CI methods and obtained good agreement with the optical data. Suard, Berthier & Pullman (1961) have also carried out calculations of the electronic structure of peptides, dipeptides and tripeptides by the ASMO SCF method. The lone pair of oxygen was considered explicitly and they suggested that the semiconductivity of protein is not intrinsic. Suard developed this calculation (1962a,b); the hydrogen atom playing a leading part in hydrogen bond formation was considered explicitly and the ASMO SCF CI method was used.

In this calculation, however, $\sigma$ electrons are considered to be a core giving $\pi$ electrons a fixed potential. The net charge and the position of the hydrogen atom are of importance for the hydrogen bonding of protein, especially in regard to the delocalization of the $\pi$ electron in the protein that should closely be related to its semiconductivity. From this point of view, explicit considera- tion of $\sigma$ electrons seems to be indispensable to the study of the electronic structure of protein. Often, the semi-empirical ASMO SCF method has been applied only to the $\pi$ electrons and not to $\sigma$ electrons in large molecules, since a method of treating the $\sigma$ electrons has not been developed. Recently, in the case of the guanine-cytosine pairs of DNA, the electronic structure was calculated by means of the semi-empirical ASMO SCF method, including $\pi$ electrons of the whole system, and $\sigma$ electrons of the middle hydrogen bond, $N-H\cdots N$. A double well potential was obtained for the hydrogen bond (Rein & Harris, 1964). In this treatment, however, only $\sigma$ electrons in the hydrogen bond are considered explicitly, and other $\sigma$ electrons are regarded as a core.

Here we treat the peptide molecule by means of the semi-empirical ASMO SCF method, considering all $\pi$ and $\sigma$ electrons explicitly. In this treatment, one may expect to know the influence of $\sigma$ electrons on the physico-chemical properties of protein and the contribution of the hydrogen atom to the semi- conductivity of protein. The electronic structure of a peptide, calculated by taking only the $\pi$ electron system into account, is obtained so as to compare it with one containing $\sigma$ and $\pi$ electrons and investigate the separability of $\sigma$ and $\pi$ electron systems. For the sake of simplicity, the former was designated as the $\pi$ system, the latter as the $\sigma-\pi$ system. The molecular orbital for the $\sigma$ electron system was that originally used in Hückel MO's approximation by Sandorfy (1955) and developed extensively by Fukui *et al*. (1960, 1961, 1962), to whom we are grateful for their suggestions. The results obtained are in good agreement with experimental data of excitation energies, and the oscillator strength of optical absorption, ionization potential and dipole moment. The electronic structure of a peptide, especially on the hydrogen

atom, suggested to us that the hydrogen atom plays an important part in the exhibition of semiconductivity in protein, as will be discussed later in this paper.

# Methods

The peptide molecule was treated as a system of four $\pi$ electrons and six $\sigma$ electrons which form the skeleton of the peptide system. The molecular orbital of the peptide was considered as a linear combination of atomic orbitals which are four $2p\pi$ orbitals, including the hydrogen $2p\pi$ orbitals, and six $\sigma$ orbitals, that is, the hydrogen $1s$ orbital, two $sp^2$ hybridized orbitals of nitrogen and carbon, respectively, and one $2p\sigma$ orbital of oxygen. The numbering of atomic orbitals, bond distances and bond angles are indicated in Fig. 1. In the present paper, hybridization of the oxygen atom is not considered. Therefore, lone pairs of oxygen atoms are neglected. Because of the symmetry characters of the $2p$ orbital of oxygen lone pairs and other atomic orbitals, overlap integrals between these orbitals could be considered to be very small and hence the effect of conjugation may not be large. Moreover, lone pair electrons make no contribution to the dipole moment because of their symmetry.

FIG. 1. (a) The numbering of the atomic orbital, (b) bond length and bond angle of the peptide molecule.

The molecular orbital can be written for the $\pi$ electron and the $\sigma$ electron separately, because of their symmetric character.

$$
\varphi_{i}=\sum_{r=1}^{4} C_{i r} \chi_{r} \quad (\pi \text{ MO's } i=1, \ldots, 4) \tag{1}
$$

$$
\varphi_{i}=\sum_{r=5}^{10} C_{i r} \chi_{r} \quad (\sigma \text{ MO's } i=5, \ldots, 10) \tag{1'}
$$

The total wave function for the peptide molecule was assumed to be of the form of the cases of $\sigma-\pi$ and $\pi$ systems, respectively.

$$
\psi=\operatorname{det}\left\{\varphi_{1}(1) \alpha(1) \varphi_{1}(2) \beta(2), \ldots, \varphi_{4}(8) \beta(8) \varphi_{5}(9) \alpha(9), \ldots, \varphi_{10}(20) \beta(20)\right\} \tag{2}
$$

$$
\psi=\operatorname{det}\left\{\varphi_{1}(1) \alpha(1) \varphi_{1}(2) \beta(2), \ldots, \varphi_{4}(8) \beta(8)\right\} \tag{2'}
$$

Hamiltonians for both systems are written in the following form:

$$
H=\sum_{i=1}^{10}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m} \nabla_{i}^{2}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{10} V_{\text {core, } i}+\sum_{i>j} \frac{e^{2}}{r_{i j}}+E_{\text {core }} \tag{3}
$$

$$
H=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m} \nabla_{i}^{2}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{4} V_{\text {core, } i}+\sum_{i>j} \frac{e^{2}}{r_{i j}}+E_{\text {core }} \tag{3'}
$$

Here, the first, second, third and last terms in the right-hand side of (3) and (3') correspond to kinetic energy; the potential on electron $i$ due to the core of the system, electron-electron repulsion potential, and core-core repulsion potential, respectively. In these equations, the last term is independent of the total wave function and contributes to the total energy as an additional constant term.

The SCF equation for this system is given by the following simultaneous equations (Roothaan, 1951),

$$
\sum_{s=1}^{n}\left(F_{r s}-\varepsilon_{i} S_{r s}\right) C_{i s}=0. \tag{4}
$$

( $n=10$ for $\sigma-\pi$ system and $n=4$ for $\pi$ system) in which $F_{r s}, S_{r s}$ represent the matrix elements of the Fock operator and the overlap integral of atomic orbitals $\chi_{r}$ and $\chi_{s}$:

$$
F_{r s}=I_{r s}+\sum_{t, u=1}^{n} P_{t u}\left\{(r s \mid t u)-\frac{1}{2}(r t \mid s u)\right\} \tag{5}
$$

$$
S_{r s}=(r \mid s), \tag{6}
$$

where

$$
I_{r s}=\int \chi_{r}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m} \nabla^{2}-V_{\text {core }}\right) \chi_{s} \mathrm{~d} \tau, \tag{7}
$$

$$
(r \mid s)=\int \chi_{r} \chi_{s} \mathrm{~d} \tau, \tag{8}
$$

$$
(r s \mid t u)=\int \chi_{r}(1) \chi_{s}(1) \chi_{t}(2) \chi_{u}(2) \frac{e^{2}}{r_{12}} \mathrm{~d} \tau_{1} \mathrm{~d} \tau_{2}, \tag{9}
$$

$$
P_{t u}=2 \sum_{i} C_{i t} C_{i u}. \tag{10}
$$

In equation (10), summation is carried out over the occupied orbitals (three $\sigma$ orbitals and two $\pi$ orbitals). In equations (5) and (6), if atomic orbital $\chi_{r}$ is the $\sigma$ orbital and $\chi_{s}$ is the $\pi$ orbital or vice versa, the matrix elements $F_{r s}$ and $S_{r s}$ vanish owing to the nature of their symmetries.

In solving equation (4), an iterative procedure was used neglecting the overlap integral,

$$
\sum_{s=1}^{n}\left(F_{r s}-\varepsilon_{i} \delta_{r s}\right) C_{i s}=0 \tag{4'}
$$

that is, the coefficient $C_{i s}$ was first assumed, so that in equation (4') the matrix element $F_{r s}$ could be evaluated by substituting the assumed coefficient into equation (10). The obtained matrix element $F_{r s}$ gives a new set of coefficients by solving the eigenvalue problem. This procedure was repeated until the coefficient $C_{i s}$ and energy $\varepsilon_{i}$ obtained in $m$th iteration did not differ from the one in $(m-1)$th iteration more than by some threshold value. In the present paper, convergence was assumed when the difference of $\varepsilon_{i}$ in the $(m-1)$th time from the one in $m$th time was less than $10^{-3}$ ev for all energy levels.

### Estimation of Integral

The core integral appearing in equation (7) can be represented in the following form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{r r} & =\int \chi_{r}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m} \nabla^{2}-V_{\text {core }}\right) \chi_{r} \mathrm{~d} \tau \\
& =\int \chi_{r}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m} \nabla^{2}-\sum_{t} V_{\text {core, } t}\right) \chi_{r} \mathrm{~d} \tau,
\end{aligned} \tag{11}
$$

in which the potential due to atom $t$ is,

$$
V_{\text {core, } t}=V_{t}^{*}-|r r)-\sum_{r^{\prime}}\left|r^{\prime} r^{\prime}\right), \tag{12}
$$

where $V_{t}^{*}$ is the potential due to neutral atom $t$, and $|r r)$ is the potential due to the electron in the atomic orbital $\chi_{r}$, that is,

$$
|r r)=\int \frac{e^{2}}{r} \chi_{r}(1) \chi_{r}(1) \mathrm{d} \tau_{1} \tag{13}
$$

and $r^{\prime}$ denotes the atomic orbital $\chi_{r^{\prime}}$ which belongs to the same atom $u$ as the orbital $\chi_{r}$ does (Goeppert-Mayer & Sklar, 1938). Then $I_{r r}$ can be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{r r} & =\int \chi_{r}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m} \nabla^{2}-V_{u}^{*}+|r r)+\sum_{r^{\prime}}\left|r^{\prime} r^{\prime}\right)-\sum_{(t \neq u)} V_{t}^{*}-\sum_{s}|s s)\right) \chi \mathrm{d} \tau \\
& =-I_{r}+\sum_{s(\neq r)}(r r | s s)
\end{aligned} \tag{14}
$$

in which $I_{r}$ is the ionization potential of atomic orbital $\chi_{r}$. In this equation penetration integrals were neglected and what we call the $W_{2 p}$ approximation was adopted, that is,

$$
\int \chi_{r}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m} \nabla^{2}-V_{t}^{*}+|r r)\right) \chi_{r} \mathrm{~d} \tau=-I_{r}. \tag{15}
$$

With regard to the non-diagonal element of core integral, if atomic orbitals $\chi_r$ and $\chi_s$ do not belong to the same atom, the magnitude of the integral was assumed to be in proportion to the corresponding overlap integral.

$$
I_{rs} = lS_{rs} \tag{16}
$$

where l is a constant and taken as $-12.65$ ev, considering the value used for other molecules to interpret the optical data (Nagata, Imamura, Kodama & Tagashira, 1965). The overlap integral was obtained using the Slater orbital. In the case where atomic orbitals $\chi_r$ and $\chi_s$ have their origin in the same atom, this integral cannot be estimated by equation (16), because the overlap integral vanishes. In this case, the $2p\pi$ atomic orbital does not have a matrix element with other $\sigma$ orbitals because of its symmetrical character. Between $\sigma$ orbitals, this is not true. The matrix element between the $sp^2$ hybridized orbitals of the same atom can be expressed in the following form.

$$
(sp_1^2|H|sp_2^2) = \frac{1}{3}\{(2s|H|2s)-(2p|H|2p)\} \tag{17}
$$

where $sp^2$ denotes the hybridized orbital and $2s, 2p$ indicate the corresponding atomic orbitals, respectively. The subscript of $sp$ represents the different hybridized orbitals of the same atom. If we can get the energy of $(2s|H|2s)$ and $(2p|H|2p)$, the value of the integral $(sp_1^2|H|sp_2^2)$ can easily be estimated. Here, we assumed that $(2s|H|2s)$ and $(2p|H|2p)$ are equal to the ionization potentials of the valence electrons in the $2s$ and $2p$ atomic orbitals, respectively.†

As to the coulomb repulsion integral, differential overlap was neglected, as is usually done in the $\pi$ electron approximation, and one centre coulomb repulsion integrals $(rr|rr)$ were given by the Pariser-Parr (1953a, b) approxi- mation

$$
(rr|rr) = I_r - A_r, \tag{18}
$$

where $A_r$ is the electron affinity of the valence electron in atomic orbital $\chi_r$. For the two centre coulomb repulsion integrals, the Mataga-Nishimoto (1957) approximation was used, thus,

$$
(rr|ss) = \frac{e^2}{R_{rs}+A_{rs}} \tag{19}
$$

where $R_{rs}$ is the distance from the centre of the electronic distribution of atomic orbital $\chi_r$ to that of atomic orbital $\chi_s$, which is in accord with the position of the atomic nucleus for $1s$ and $2p\pi$ atomic orbitals, although it is

† Although this assumption may not necessarily be correct owing to the second term on the right side in equation (14), the magnitude of the integral $(sp_1^2|H|sp_2^2)$ can be approxi- mately estimated by this assumption because of the cancellation of the second term in equation (14).

not for the hybridized orbital. $A_{rs}$ is given by the following equation, if atomic orbitals $\chi_{r}$ and $\chi_{s}$ are of the same species.

$$
\frac{e^{2}}{A_{r r}}=\lim _{R_{r s} \rightarrow 0} \frac{e^{2}}{R_{r s}+A_{r s}}=(r r | r r). \tag{20}
$$

In the case where two atomic orbitals $\chi_{r}$ and $\chi_{s}$ belong to different species, $A_{rs}$ is equal to the harmonic mean of $A_{rr}$ and $A_{ss}$. The values of the distance between nuclei and the centre of electronic distribution were given for carbon $sp^{2}$ and nitrogen $sp^{2}$ hybridized orbitals as follows.

$$
\int \chi_{s p^{2}} r \chi_{s p^{2}} \mathrm{~d} \tau=0.443 \mathring{\mathrm{A}} \text{ for carbon } sp^{2}
$$

$$
\int \chi_{s p^{2}} r \chi_{s p^{2}} \mathrm{~d} \tau=0.369 \mathring{\mathrm{A}} \text{ for nitrogen } sp^{2}.
$$

In order to estimate the two centre coulomb integral, it is indispensable to know the values of ionization potential and electron affinity for various valence electrons. The values were obtained from the table of Hinze & Jaffé (1962) except for the hydrogen $2p\pi$ orbital. With regard to the exponent of the hydrogen $2p\pi$ atomic orbital, the method of evaluation has not yet been established decisively, though many trials have been made to investigate the effect of including the hydrogen $2p\pi$ atomic orbital. For instance, the previous calculation for peptide by Suard (1962a, b), $Z_{2p_{\pi}}^{*}$ was tentatively chosen to be equal to the value of the hydrogen $1s$ orbital. Moreover, the ionization potential and one centre coulomb repulsion integral of the hydrogen $2p\pi$ orbital should be estimated semi-empirically. In the present paper, exponents of the hydrogen $2p\pi$ orbital were evaluated according to Slater's rule for three cases; that the effective nuclear charge of the hydrogen atom for the $2p\pi$ atomic orbital was assumed to be 0.15, 0.3625 and 0.575 according as the electron densities on the hydrogen $1s$ orbital are 1.0, 0.75 and 0.5, respectively.

For the purpose of estimating the ionization potential of the hydrogen $2p\pi$ atomic orbital, the ionization potentials of the $2p\pi$ atomic orbitals are represented approximately as a linear function of effective nuclear charge by employing the value in the table of Hinze & Jaffé (1962) for the series of atoms in the first short period of the periodic table, that is, B, C, N, O, F. For the hydrogen $2p\pi$ atomic orbital, the relation between ionization potential and effective nuclear charge was obtained taking the above-mentioned equation into account as follows:

$$
I_{H_{2 p \pi}}=4 \cdot 752 Z^{*}+0 \cdot 0342 \tag{21}
$$

in which the value 0.0342 was determined so as to give the electron affinity of the hydrogen atom as the ionization potential for electrons in the hydrogen $2p\pi$ atomic orbital when the effective nuclear charge of hydrogen is unity. In other words, the electron affinity of the neutral hydrogen atom was

assumed to be nearly equal to the ionization potential of the hydrogen $2p\pi$ atomic orbital. Thus the ionization potential could easily be obtained by employing equation (21) for the given values of the effective nuclear charge $Z^*$. In a non-empirical method the one centre coulomb repulsion integral is represented, using the Slater orbital, by the following equation,

$$(2p\pi\,2p\pi|2p\pi\,2p\pi) = 0.1929 \frac{Z^{*2}}{a_0} \quad \text{in a.u.,} \tag{22}$$

in which $Z^*$ denotes the effective nuclear charge of the atom and $a_0$ is the Bohr radius. The semi-empirical one centre coulomb repulsion integral for the hydrogen $2p\pi$ atomic orbital was assumed to be approximately given as follows:

$$(H_{2p\pi}H_{2p\pi}|H_{2p\pi}H_{2p\pi})_{\text{exp}} = \frac{(H_{2p\pi}H_{2p\pi}|H_{2p\pi}H_{2p\pi})_{\text{theo}}}{(C_{2p\pi}C_{2p\pi}|C_{2p\pi}C_{2p\pi})_{\text{theo}}}(C_{2p\pi}C_{2p\pi}|C_{2p\pi}C_{2p\pi})_{\text{exp}},$$

in which subscript exp indicates the empirical value derived from the ionization potential and electron affinity of the atomic orbital, and subscript theo the theoretical one obtained from equation (22).

## Results and Discussion

The excitation energy and the oscillator strength obtained are listed in Table 2 for the calculation of the $\pi$ and $\sigma-\pi$ systems for comparison with the experimental data. The result is in good agreement with experiment for excitation energy, but the calculated oscillator strength seems to be rather large for the $N-V_{1}$ transition and small for the $N-V_{2}$ transition. For optical data the values for the $\sigma-\pi$ and $\pi$ systems do not differ greatly. The calculated ionization potential and dipole moment are also tabulated in Table 2; the calculated ionization potential agrees approximately with experiment, and the differences between the two systems, i.e. $\sigma-\pi$ and $\pi$, were found to be negligible. Dipole moments, however, were found to differ remarkably from each other. Inclusion of $\sigma$ electrons in the calculation led us to expect that the value of the dipole moment would be significantly altered. This suggests that it is important, for the justification of this approximation for the $\sigma-\pi$ system, to compare experimental data with theoretical ones for dipole moment. That is, if we obtain good agreement between the calculated value and the experimental one in the $\sigma-\pi$ system for dipole moment, the approximation used in the present paper can be considered to be fairly appropriate for the $\sigma-\pi$ system. As shown in Table 2, there is good agreement between experimental data and theoretical values for the method including the $\sigma-\pi$ system. On the other hand, agreement is not so good in the $\pi$ system. Therefore, considering the value of dipole moment, the method and the approximation employed in this paper seems to be more valid than the old one. Moreover, the direction of dipole moment due to $\sigma$ electrons is the reverse of the one due to the $\pi$ electrons in the $\sigma-\pi$ system, so that the electron repulsion between $\sigma$ and $\pi$ electrons is of impor- tance for estimating the dipole moment for the conjugated system.
