INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL OF QUANTUM CHEMISTRY SYMP. NO. 7, 207-215 (1973)

# Ab-Initio SCF Calculations on Pyrrole and Pyrazole

HARRY J. T. PRESTON AND JOYCE J. KAUFMAN

Department of Chemistry, The Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore, Maryland 21218

## Abstract

Full ab-initio SCF calculations have been carried out on pyrrole and pyrazole with very large Gaussian basis sets. A preliminary analysis of the molecular wave functions has been made by looking at the total and orbital energies and some of the properties. A comparison is made between these results and previous SCF calculations performed with much smaller basis sets. The ionization energies obtained by Koopman's theorem are compared with the experimental photoelectron spectroscopic values. Very good agreement is obtained for the two highest orbitals (both of $\pi$ type). The comparison of pyrrole and pyrazole with some recent SCF results for furan is also included.

## 1. Introduction

Five-membered aromatic heterocyclic compounds have long been of interest to theoretical chemists. These compounds are of considerable significance in very diverse experimental chemical areas. They form building blocks of natural biological molecules and pharmacological agents. By themselves they also form several classes of very important medicinal chemicals acting as antihelmintics, antischistosomasis compounds, antibacterial and antifungal agents, and the like; and they comprise fundamental constituents of many explosive compounds, in particular those with several ring nitrogens are most effective. Since this gamut of uses meshes so well with our own research interests, we began to investigate the quantum chemistry of certain of these compounds in some detail.

One of the experimentally oriented groups with which we collaborate [1] had started to perform CNDO/2 calculations on the heteroaromatic five-membered N-containing compounds in order to explain their chemistry and various explosive propensities. For this they were attempting to make use of the atomic electron densities which are calculated from the diagonal elements of the density matrix in this ZDO approximation. They were also trying to get some measure of the electron density between atoms such as would correspond to the total overlap populations (TOPS) in an ab-initio or at least in a non-ZDO calculation. For this they were using Wiberg's bond index [2] which is composed of the sum over the squares of the off-diagonal density matrix elements and were also using the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix as if they were true off-diagonal elements resulting from a non-ZDO calculation. We had shown several years ago, by numerical comparison with an ab initio minium STO basis calculation, that, at least for bonded atoms, if one treated the off-diagonal elements of CNDO or INDO density matrices as if they were true off-diagonal elements,

We undertook to perform some ab-initio calculations on pyrrole and pyrazole with a reasonably large basis set in order to compare ab-initio orbital energies, population analyses, and other calculated properties with those from the CNDO and INDO calculations. These com- parisons will be given in a companion paper [4].

## 2. Computational Details

There have been previous ab-initio calculations of five-membered N-containing heterocyclic compounds starting with the pioneering work of Clementi and co- workers on pyrrole published in 1967 [5]. His calculations were carried out with a $7^s3^p$ basis contracted to $2^s1^p$ for the heavy atoms plus $3^s$ functions contracted to $1^s$ but no $p$ functions on the hydrogens. Calculations with similar small basis sets were also carried out later by Berthier and coworkers [6] and by Palmer and Gaskell [7]. However, energy eigenvalues which result from these size basis sets are from one to several electronvolts lower than with larger basis sets. For comparison with photo- electron spectra and especially to aid in the assignment of disputed peaks, we were interested in obtaining hopefully more accurate energy eigenvalues.

The present ab-initio calculations on pyrrole and pyrazole were performed using a $9^s5^p$ Gaussian basis contracted to $4^s2^p$ on the first row atoms and a $4^s1^p$ basis contracted to $3^s1^p$ on the H atoms. Dunning's contractions [8] were used instead of Huzinaga's contractions [9], since the Dunning sets, which allow more freedom in the valence shell basis functions, are better suited for molecular formation. The H-atom $s$ exponents were scaled by a factor of 1.2 and the $p$ exponent was chosen to be 0.80, which is close to the optimum value given by Roos and Siegbahn [10]. The final basis sets consisted of 155 Gaussian basis functions contracted to 80 for pyrrole and 145 Gaussian basis functions contracted to 74 for pyrazole.

The geometries used in the present calculations were the accurate results ob- tained from microwave spectra. The pyrrole geometry was that determined by Nygaard and coworkers [11] and the pyrazole geometry was the unpublished results which were kindly sent to us by Lise Nygaard [12]. To begin the SCF calculations a well guessed set of starting eigenvectors were obtained by generating a symmetry adapted set of hybrid orbitals in the manner suggested by Petke and Whitten [13]. The SCF procedure was carried out until the vectors were converged to about 3 in the fourth decimal. This resulted in the energy being converged to $3\times10^{-7}$ a.u. Better convergence than this was not deemed necessary for the calculations reported here.

The TOPs (Table IIB) display a similar pattern. There is a sizable positive overlap between all adjacent atoms, and there is an almost equally sizable negative overlap between nonadjacent ring atoms. This large negative overlap represents a strong antibonding contribution to the overlap between nonadjacent ring atoms. It may well be the balance between the positive and negative TOPs that determines the overall stability of these types of molecules. The TOPs (Table III) between H atoms and between ring atoms and H atoms not bonded to them are small for both pyrrole and pyrazole, as was expected.

## 4. Comparison with Photoelectron Spectra

A brief mention will be made of the comparison of the orbital energy eigenvalues with photoelectron spectral (PES) data.

The two lowest ionization potentials (IPs) of pyrrole, by analysis of the PES vibrational fine structure, are undoubtedly $\pi$-electron IPs [15], and these compare very closely with our calculated HOMO and 2nd HOMO of pyrrole (Table IV). Note that for these $\pi$ orbitals, which are not crucially bonding, there seems little need for applying a scaling factor of $\sim 0.92$ as has been suggested by previous investigators [17]: HOMO, calculated $-8.13$ eV, observed $8.21$ eV; 2nd HOMO calculated $-9.50$ eV, observed $9.20$ eV.

Pyrazole has had its photoelectron spectrum reported by Baker and coworkers [18], but with less reported analysis of the fine structure. Again there is excellent agreement between our calculated value of the HOMO $-9.62$ eV and the observed $9.5$ eV, and between our calculated 2nd HOMO $-10.16$ eV and the observed $10.1$ eV. It is in the assignment of the lower IPs corresponding to in-plane orbitals plus the one lower $\pi$ orbital of pyrrole and pyrazole where the situation becomes less clear-cut. Lindholm's papers on the PES of furan and pyrrole were submitted concomitantly. From comparison of Siegbahn's ab-initio calculation of furan which Lindholm quotes and the PES data, it was obvious that one would have to do some scaling for $\sigma$ orbitals and the lowest $\pi$ orbitals, since these did not lie as deep as the calculations indicated them to lie. Lindholm then assigned certain of the pyrrole bands by analogy to furan bands. Lindholm considered that vertical IPs of the $9a_{1}$ orbitals in pyrrole and furan must be similar, 12.6 eV in pyrrole and 13.0 eV in furan. This seems in reasonable agreement with our calculational results for pyrrole, $-14.39$ eV compared to Siegbahn's $-14.74$ eV for furan. But also in the same article Lindholm asserts that "the $6b_{2}$ orbital in pyrrole is very similar to the $6b_{2}$ orbital in furan and the energies of the orbitals should be similar. The vertical IP of furan is 'believed to be' 13.8 eV so the 13.7 eV in pyrrole is attributed to $6b_{2}$." However, our calculated energy for the $6b_{2}$ band in pyrrole, $-14.88$ eV, is about 1.2 eV less deep than that which Siegbahn calculates for the corresponding $6b_{2}$ level of furan, $-16.02$ eV. Thus either Siegbahn's calculation gives the $6b_{2}$ orbital as lying too deep, or the PES assignment of $6b_{2}$ is open to question. Recalculation of furan with the same basis set and contractions used for pyrrole and comparison of corresponding eigenvalues could go far to clarify the situation. The same assignment problem holds true for some of the lower bands. Certain of the bands are assigned by analogy to those assigned earlier (but not indisputably) in similar compounds. The rest are dealt with as follows. "The unidentified bands between 12 and 16 eV must represent the orbitals $1b_{1}$, $9a_{1}$, $5b_{2}$, $6b_{2}$, and $8a_{1}$ provided our previous assignments are valid."

Our calculations on pyrrole were sufficient to enable us to see immediately that the PES spectrum of pyrrole reported by Palmer and Gaskell [7] (which reached us before Lindholm's article) had to be missing at least one peak in the range they covered.

Plus, they also misassigned the 17.5 eV peak as a $\pi$ IP. Our calculated value for the $1b_1$ orbital was only $-15.52$ eV, and one has never seen ab-initio eigenvalues smaller in magnitude than IPs measured by PES.

The reported PES of pyrazole by Baker and coworkers [18] is less detailed. The two highest $\pi$ peaks checked well with our calculations. A peak at 10.73 eV must be due to the lone pair electrons on the N2 atom. Another peak was reported at 13.60 eV, and then peaks were reported at 14.62 and 17.50 eV. Our calculations indicate that there must be three peaks lying in the range of 14.62 eV, since we have calculated peaks at $-16.12,\ -16.72,$ and $-16.80$ eV. The 17.50 eV peak must then correlate with our eigenvalue of $-20.36$ eV.
