# Synergistic Enhancement of Dielectric Polymers Through Fluorine Incorporation for Improved Energy Storage, Reduced Loss, and Enhanced Processability

Yipin Cheng, Qinglong Ji, Guanxiang Zhang, Xiao Zhang, Zhenxue Liu, Honghong Gong,* and Zhicheng Zhang*

Establishing a harmonious equilibrium between high energy storage, minimal energy loss, and exceptional processability presents a formidable challenge within the realm of dielectric polymers. To address this challenge, harnessing the characteristics of long-chain side groups to enhance polarity and toughness, as well as the fluorine effect to improve insulation and efficiency, a random copolymer, poly(4-fluorostyrene-trifluoroethyl methacrylate) (P(FS-3FEMA)), is successfully synthesized via copolymerization of fluorine-containing monomers, namely 4-fluorostyrene (FSt) and trifluoroethyl methacrylate (3FEMA). Experimental findings demonstrate that elongating side chains enhances polymer toughness, boosts polarization strength, and self-healing capacity. Introducing fluorine atoms into side chains maintains toughness while marginally increasing polymer chain spacing, reducing relaxation loss induced by dipole reorientation under an applied electric field. Additionally, fluorine incorporation enhances electron capture, effectively reducing leakage loss and the likelihood of thermal and electrical breakdown. Ultimately, the copolymer exhibits exceptional performance, characterized by extraordinarily high energy storage ($U_{\mathrm{e}} = 18.3\ \mathrm{J\ cm^{-3}}$), minimal energy loss (efficiency exceeding 89%), and enhanced toughness (increased by over 112%). This study presents a novel approach that harnesses the fluorine effect to reconcile the conflicting requirements of high energy storage, minimal energy loss, and exceptional processability in polymer dielectrics.

## 1. Introduction

Dielectric capacitors, integral components vital for energy storage and conversion devices, play a pivotal role across diverse applications within modern power electronic systems. $^{[1]}$ These applications necessitate capacitors characterized by high energy density, minimal energy loss, and exceptional processability. $^{[2]}$ Polymer-based capacitors are preferred over ceramic and electrolytic counterparts owing to their cost-effectiveness, low dielectric loss, and superior breakdown strength. $^{[3]}$ However, the energy storage capacity of polymer-based capacitors within a specific volume is significantly constrained by their relatively modest dielectric constant $(\approx 2.2).^{[4]}$ Aligned with contemporary imperatives for device miniaturization or microminiaturization in modern power systems, there exists an urgent necessity to augment the energy storage density of dielectric polymer-based capacitors. $^{[5]}$ The dielectric material emerges as a critical determinant shaping the energy storage performance of capacitors, prompting focused research on the development of innovative polymer dielectrics aimed at augmenting the energy storage capacity of capacitors. $^{[6]}$

Conventional strategies entail the integration of polar units into low-polarity polypropylene (PP) matrices to augment the dielectric constant through the promotion of orientation polarization under external electric fields. $^{[7]}$ However, the incorporation of oriented polar units frequently results in elevated relaxation losses, thereby markedly diminishing energy storage efficiency. $^{[8]}$ Despite modifications, polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) materials, celebrated for their high energy density, encounter persistent challenges in efficiently minimizing heightened relaxation losses attributed to ferroelectric relaxation phenomena. $^{[9]}$ Dipolar glassy polymers (DGP), characterized by their glassy state at room temperature, effectively restrain chain motion, permitting solely localized rotation of dipole groups to elevate the dielectric constant. $^{[10]}$ This delicate equilibrium between high energy density and low energy loss is noteworthy. $^{[11]}$ However, the exceptional energy storage performance of glassy polymers relies on a high glass transition temperature $(T_{\mathrm{g}})$,

frequently compromising polymer toughness and posing processing challenges. $^{[11a,12]}$ This limitation precludes their suitability for deployment as capacitor dielectric materials. Although augmenting polymer side-chain length emerges as an effective toughening method, particularly within the realm of dielectric materials, it results in a swift escalation of energy loss and a concomitant reduction in breakdown strength. Consequently, reconciling the inherent contradiction between high-energy storage, minimal loss, and toughness in dipolar glassy polymers poses a significant contemporary research challenge, yet investigations in this domain remain relatively scarce. $^{[13]}$ Fluorinated acrylic elastomers are garnering significant interest due to their low Young's modulus (Y), outstanding mechanical flexibility, and excellent processability. $^{[14]}$ Despite these advantages, the incorporation of highly electronegative fluorine atoms and robust carbon-fluorine bonds reduces the polarizability of the structural units, as confirmed by DFT calculations that demonstrate decreased dipole moments and dielectric constants. $^{[15]}$ This characteristic has traditionally rendered them unsuitable for dielectric applications. However, recent experimental and computational investigations have revealed that fluorine atoms can induce deformation of the electron cloud in benzene rings, thereby increasing the dielectric constant under an applied electric field and reducing relaxation losses associated with conventional orientational polarization. $^{[11 b, 16]}$ That offers a facile strategy for reconciling the intrinsic conflict between high energy storage, minimal loss, and toughness, leveraging the effects of fluorine.

This investigation unveils a pioneering strategy to reconcile the inherent trade-off among high energy storage, minimal loss, and toughness in dipolar glassy polymers through the utilization of the fluorine effect. Specifically, the incorporation of long-chain side groups, including trifluoroethyl methacrylate (3FEMA), pentafluoropropyl methacrylate (5FPMA), and heptafluorobutyl methacrylate (7FBMA), into the glassy polymer yields copolymers characterized by substantially elevated dielectric constants and markedly improved processing toughness. Furthermore, the introduction of fluorine atoms augments dipole repulsion, reducing leakage current and reducing the typical escalation in energy loss and decline in breakdown strength associated with long-chain side groups. Structural analysis and computational simulations elucidate that the incorporation of long fluoroalkyl chains amplifies the dipole moments of polymer molecules and augments intermolecular distances between polymer chains. This alleviates hindrances to dipole reorientation under an applied electric field, concurrently diminishing dielectric loss while augmenting the dielectric response. Furthermore, the integration of fluorine atoms diminishes the LUMO energy level of the polymer units, and widens the bandgap of polymers, thus preserving a high breakdown strength. This investigation marks the first application of the fluorine effect to synergistically bolster the energy storage density, discharge efficiency, and processing resilience of polymer dielectrics. The optimized copolymer, manifesting a breaking elongation increase surpassing 112%, showcases a discharge energy density reaching $18.3\ \text{J}\ \text{cm}^{-3}$ with an efficiency of 89%, outclassing the performance of most documented polymer dielectrics. This methodology harbors the potential for deployment across various extant dipolar polymers, providing a route for innovating dielectric materials derived from established ones and thus broadening the prospective applications of high-power density capacitors in portable and automotive systems, aircraft control, and advanced weaponry.

## 2. Results and Discussion
### 2.1. Chemical Structure and Thermodynamic Characterization of P(FS-XMA)s

In polar polymers, significant molecular motion contributes to orientational polarization and facilitates ion/electron conduction, thereby enhancing energy storage density. However, this dynamic behavior also precipitates a rapid escalation in relaxation and leakage losses. It is important to emphasize that dipolar glassy state polymers, being in a glassy state at room temperature, effectively minimize the extensive molecular motion observed in other polymers. The superior dielectric performance of such polymers typically depends on a high $T_{\text{g}}$. However, a high $T_{\text{g}}$ can compromise polymer toughness, thereby adversely affecting processability. Efforts to enhance toughness by incorporating flexible segments may inadvertently lead to increased energy losses and reduced breakdown strength. Consequently, achieving high energy density, robust processing toughness, and minimal energy losses concurrently presents a significant challenge in the field of dipolar glassy polymer dielectrics.

In light of the aforementioned considerations, a novel category of dipolar glassy polymers has been successfully engineered, leveraging the fluorine effect. Through the copolymerization of two fluorine-containing monomers, namely 4-fluorostyrene (4-FSt), and fluorinated alkyl esters of methacrylic acid with diverse chain lengths (3FEMA, 5FPMA, 7FBMA), resulting in the synthesis of a spectrum of target copolymers. ### 2.2. Dielectric Performance of P(FS-XMA)s
The dielectric performance serves as a critical determinant of the energy storage capabilities inherent in dielectric materials. Elevating energy storage density via augmented polarization strength stands as a well-established strategy. To elucidate this correlation, we conducted a systematic exploration of dielectric property variations across copolymers featuring distinct dipolar moieties. Illustrated in Figures 2A,B and S7 (Supporting Information), P(FS-EMA) demonstrates an increase in $\epsilon_{\mathrm{r}}$ at $10^{3}$ Hz relative to P(FS-MMA), escalating from 3.20 to 3.73. However, a divergent trend emerges upon the introduction of fluorine atoms, the $\epsilon_{\mathrm{r}}$ of P(FS-3FEMA) diminishes to 3.61. This variance primarily arises from alterations in the internal dipole moment of the molecules. Computational scrutiny of side-chain group dipole moments via Density functional theory (DFT),$^{[18]}$ depicted in Figure 2C,D, unveils an elevation in the dipole moment of the EMA unit to 4.33 Debye compared to the 1.79 Debye dipole moment of the MMA unit, while the dipole moment of the 3FEMA unit decreased to 3.99 Debye following fluorine atom incorporation. Particularly noteworthy is the attenuated frequency-dependent pattern observed in P(FS-EMA) relative to P(FS-MMA), with the dipole relaxation peak shifting toward lower frequencies. Moreover, with additional fluorine atom integration, responsiveness further diminishes. This phenomenon is attributed to enhanced dipole interactions, which inhibit dipole motion, an effect that becomes more pronounced with the introduction of fluorine atoms. Analogous patterns in loss factor alterations corroborate the constraining influence of fluorine atom incorporation on dipole movement.

Notably, the fluorine effect can exert a discernible influence on the temperature-dependent behavior of polymer dielectric performance. Consistent with findings from DSC analyses, both the dielectric constant and the loss factor exhibited a notable augmentation in proximity to $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ (Figure S8, Supporting Information). Spectral analysis of polymers at elevated temperatures was conducted, as illustrated in Figure 2E,F. As the temperature approached $T_{\mathrm{g}}$, P(FS-EMA) manifested an $\alpha$-relaxation peak attributable to segmental motion and a $\beta$-relaxation peak stemming from side-chain dipole movement. In contrast, despite nearing $T_{\mathrm{g}}$, the segmental motion of P(FS-3FEMA) commenced, yet the $\beta$-relaxation peak associated with side-chain dipole movement remained significantly suppressed. This underscores the pivotal role of fluorine atom incorporation in attenuating relaxation losses in polymer dielectrics.

### 2.3. Insulating Properties of P(FS-XMA)s

The breakdown strength represents another pivotal parameter for polymer dielectrics, notably in contexts concerning energy storage capacitors. Figures $3 \mathrm{~A}$ and $\mathrm{S} 10$ (Supporting Information) depict the outcomes of a two-parameter Weibull analysis revealing a substantial decline in the breakdown strength of polymer films upon the inclusion of long-chain side groups. For example, in contrast to P(FS-MMA), the breakdown strength of P(FS-EMA) notably diminished from 825 to $532 \mathrm{MV} \mathrm{m}^{-1}$. Nonetheless, the introduction of fluorine atoms resulted in the recovery of the breakdown strength of P(FS-3FEMA) to $746 \mathrm{MV} \mathrm{m}^{-1}$. This observation aligns with previously discussed trends, wherein the $\epsilon_{\mathrm{r}}$ decreased and the $Y$ increased following the introduction of fluorine atoms, as expressed in Equation 1:

$$
E_{\mathrm{b}}=\frac{3}{5}\left(\frac{Y}{\varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{1}
$$

A notable fraction of the observed enhancement in the breakdown strength $\left(E_{\mathrm{b}}\right)$ in polymers can be ascribed to the influences of $\epsilon_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $Y$. Furthermore, thermal and electrical breakdown likelihood exert an influence on the breakdown strength of polymers.

To elucidate the factors influencing the alterations in breakdown strength, as illustrated in Figures $3 \mathrm{~B}$ and $\mathrm{S} 11$ (Supporting Information), an initial exploration of the polymer's leakage current was undertaken. The investigation unveils a significant decrease in leakage current at low fields $\left(<200 \mathrm{MV} \mathrm{m}^{-1}\right)$ following the incorporation of long-chain side groups, indicating a disruption in the stacking of benzene electrons and suppression of semiconductor attributes by these side groups. Moreover, subsequent to the introduction of fluorine atoms, a notable decrease in the polymer's leakage current was observed, signifying the effective minimization of conduction losses by fluorine atoms under an applied electric field. However, as the electric field strength increased, the incorporation of long-chain side groups resulted in a more pronounced escalation in polymer leakage current. Fortunately, the introduction of fluorine atoms persistently restrained

the escalation in leakage current. To further investigate these observations, ISPD tests were performed on the copolymer carrier traps, as illustrated in Figure 3C. In comparison to P(FS-MMA), the depth of traps in P(FS-EMA) polymer increased, aligning with the decreasing trend in leakage current at low voltage. However, P(FS-EMA) manifested significantly fewer deep traps, leading to an increase in leakage current at high voltage. Subsequent to the introduction of fluorine atoms, both the quantity and depth of traps in P(FS-3FEMA) increased relative to P(FS-EMA), corroborating the further reduction in leakage current and effectively suppressing the probability of breakdown in polymer films. Furthermore, the introduction of fluorine atoms results in a reduction in the dipole relaxation and leakage current, potentially decreasing the probability of thermal breakdown in polymer films.

The alteration in breakdown strength is further supported by the outcomes of bandgap calculations for polymers. UV characterization of the polymer and the calculation of its direct band gap, as illustrated in Figure 3D, unveiled an augmentation in the direct band gap of the polymer from 4.34 to 4.45 eV subsequent to the integration of fluorine atoms. This augmented band gap serves to impede the migration of charge carriers within the polymer. Furthermore, calculations of the HOMO-LUMO gap (Figure 3E; Figure S12, Supporting Information) also revealed an increase in the HOMO-LUMO gap of polymer units from 7.082 to 7.452 eV following the introduction of fluorine atoms. This alignment with the changes in the direct band gap ultimately leads to a lower probability of breakdown in the polymer film. It is noteworthy that the LUMO energy level can reflect the electron capture ability of the molecular structure. Compared to the LUMO energy level of EMA at 0.392 eV, the LUMO energy level of 3FEMA decreased to −0.116 eV. This indicates that the introduction of fluorine atoms can effectively enhance the electron capture ability of EMA, aligning with the alterations in leakage current.

In summary, despite the introduction of long-chain side groups leading to a decrease in the polymer modulus and consequent reduction in breakdown strength, the incorporation of fluorine atoms diminished the likelihood of thermal and electrical breakdown. This ultimately achieved a proficient equilibrium between polymer toughness and high breakdown strength. The breakdown strength of P(FS-3FEMA) diminishes due to the modulus decrease compared to P(FS-MMA). However, the heightened toughness simultaneously improves the processability and self-healing properties of dipole glassy polymers, as depicted in Figure 3F. This led to distinct cracks near the breakdown site of the P(FS-MMA) film, whereas analogous observations were challenging to discern in the P(FS-3FEMA) film, thus additionally alleviating mechanical harm to the P(FS-3FEMA) film in capacitor applications and preserving its intrinsic high breakdown strength and post-breakdown self-healing capabilities. In conclusion, through the introduction of long-chain side groups and leveraging the fluorine effect, the polymer effectively diminished the likelihood of thermal and electrical breakdown, attaining a beneficial equilibrium between toughness and high breakdown strength. This series of research findings furnishes experimental evidence for the design of dielectric materials with exceptional performance and comprehensively scrutinizes the synergistic effects of long-chain side groups and fluorine effect on polymer properties.

### 2.4. Energy Storage Performance of P(FS-XMA)s

Effectively addressing the equilibrium between excellent discharge efficiency and exceptional energy density under elevated electric fields can be achieved through the integration of long-chain side groups to augment the dielectric constant, foster self-healing capabilities, and minimize the probability of physical damage. Moreover, the incorporation of fluorine atoms functions to suppress relaxation losses, leakage losses, and diminish the likelihood of thermal and electrical breakdown. Energy storage performance stands as a pivotal parameter for the application of dielectric materials. Calculated from the electric displacement–electric field ($D$–$E$) loops, as illustrated in Figures 4A,B and S13 (Supporting Information), P(FS-3FEMA) demonstrated the optimal performance, achieving a discharge energy density ($U_{\text{e}}$) of 18.3 J cm⁻³ at 750 MV m⁻¹, while maintaining a discharge efficiency surpassing 89%. Particularly when achieving a discharge efficiency of 90%, in contrast to P(FS-MMA), P(FS-3FEMA) exhibits a significant augmentation of 3.7 J cm⁻³ in discharge energy density, accompanied by an elongation at break surpassing 112%, highlighting exceptional processing and energy storage capabilities (Figure 4C). In summary, the strategic integration of long-chain side groups and the incorporation of fluorine atoms have demonstrated effectiveness in attaining a balance between exceptional energy storage performance and discharge efficiency under elevated electric fields.

In the realm of practical applications of dielectric materials in capacitors, the top-performing P(FS-3FEMA) film was chosen for evaluations of its discharge energy density and power density via charge-discharge tests, juxtaposed with P(FS-MMA), as illustrated in Figures 4D and S14A (Supporting Information). The thickness of all films is roughly 12 μm, and the discharge time ($\tau_{0.9}$) is delineated as the time required for the capacitor to discharge 90% of the total energy. The outcomes illustrate that at 200 MV m⁻¹, the P(FS-3FEMA) film showcases an outstanding power density of 0.21 MW cm⁻³, signifying a 75% augmentation compared to the 0.12 MW cm⁻³ power density of P(FS-MMA). Moreover, the discharge rate of the P(FS-3FEMA) film surpasses that of the P(FS-MMA) film by 14%, suggesting its superior discharge capability. To validate the long-term stability of the P(FS-3FEMA) dielectric film, cyclic rapid discharge experiments were conducted under an applied electric field of 200 MV m⁻¹ at room temperature, as depicted in Figures 4E and S14B (Supporting Information). The study reveals that the power density of the P(FS-3FEMA) film remains highly stable over $10^4$ charge-discharge cycles, with a change of less than 6% compared to the energy density measured in the first cycle. Finally, to visually illustrate the exceptional energy storage performance of the P(FS-3FEMA) dielectric film by considering both $U_{\text{e}}$ and efficiency ($\eta$), a figure of merit $U_{\text{f}}$ is defined and expressed as:${}^{[20,21]}$

$$
U_{\text{f}}=U_{\text{e}}/(1-\eta) \tag{2}
$$

The larger the $U_{\text{e}}$ and $\eta$, the better the energy storage performance of $U_{\text{f}}$. We compared $U_{\text{f}}$ with previously reported results. ### 3. Conclusion
This investigation introduces a pioneering methodology lever- aging the fluorine effect to reconcile the perplexing interplay among elevated energy storage, diminished loss, and resilience within dipole glassy polymers intended for capacitor utilization. Integrating long-chain side groups into glassy-state polymers effectively amplifies the processing toughness and polariza- tion strength of the polymer. Concurrently, the approach of introducing fluorine effect into the side groups augments dipole interactions while elevating the bandgap, consequently minimizing the energy loss of the polymer and bolstering its breakdown strength. In the optimized P(FS-3FEMA) sample, the elongation at break escalates by more than 112%. Mean- while, the discharge energy density markedly elevates to 18.3 $\text{J cm}^{-3}$, accompanied by a discharge efficiency exceeding 89%, surpassing the majority of reported polymer dielectric materials. This investigation furnishes a groundbreaking strategy for designing and fabricating dipolar glassy polymer dielectric films featuring high energy density, negligible dielectric losses, and exemplary processing performance. This investigation unveils novel avenues for advancing the efficacy of capacitor materials, promising noteworthy applications within the realm of materials science.

Synthesis of P(FS-XMA) Polymer: The standard protocol for the production of poly(4-fluorostyrene-co-trifluoroethyl methacrylate) (P(FS-3FEMA)) is illustrated in **Scheme 1**. After the solvent was completely evaporated, the films along with the slides were annealed at 170 °C for 4 h prior to analysis. The thickness of all films was ≈11–13 μm.

Instruments and Characterization: $^1$H NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker spectrometer (400 MHz, Advance III) in CDCl₃ with tetramethylsilane (TMS) as an internal standard. FT-IR spectroscopy was performed on a Tensor27 (Bruker, Germany) in transmittance mode with a resolution of 1–0.4 cm⁻¹. Molecular weights and polydispersity index (PDI) were determined using a Viscotek GPCmax autosampler system consisting of a pump, ViscoGEL GPC columns (G4000H HR), and a Waters 2414 differential refractive index (RI) detector with a DMF flow rate of 1.0 mL min⁻¹ at 30 °C. DSC thermograms were obtained on a Netzsch DSC 200 PC (Netzsch, Germany) under a nitrogen atmosphere at a scanning rate of 10 °C min⁻¹ after a cycle of heating (10 °C min⁻¹) and cooling (10 °C min⁻¹) to remove the thermal history. The variation of the modulus of the polymer with temperature was characterized using Netzsch DMA242E. The surface morphology of polymers was detected with a tungsten hairpin filament scanning electron microscope (ZEISS EVO 10), where the films were coated with a thin layer of gold before observation. Gold electrodes were sputtered on both surfaces of the polymer films on a JEOL JFC1600 auto-fine coater (Japan). Broadband dielectric spectroscopy (BDS) measurements were performed on a Novocontrol Concept 80 broadband dielectric spectrometer with temperature control. The applied voltage was 1 V with the frequency changing from 0.1 Hz to 1 MHz at different temperatures. The electric displacement–electric field (D–E) loops at room temperature were obtained on a Premiere II ferroelectric tester from Radiant Technologies, Inc., where AC electric fields ranging from 50–850 MV m⁻¹ were applied across polymer films with a triangular waveform at a frequency of 10 Hz. The $E_b$ for all the sample films was determined using a breakdown voltage tester (Beijing Beiguangjingyi Instrument Equipment Co., Ltd), and the electric field was applied at a rate of 1 kV min⁻¹. The diameter of the cylindrical electrode was 10 mm. A high-voltage DC power supply (RK2674B) was used to evaluate $E_b$ of the films. In fast discharge experiments, films were charged to a given voltage using a high voltage MOSFET switch (610D-K-CE-IX); the inner resistance (RL) was 100 kΩ, and the discharge curve was recorded under varied electric fields. The leakage current was obtained as less than 10 mA until the film was electrically broken down. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed with the Gaussian 09 with B3LYP hybrid function and the 3–21 G basis function. The analysis of reduced density gradient (RDG) was performed within the Multiwfn program.
