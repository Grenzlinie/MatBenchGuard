## Microwave Spectrum and Structure of Bicyclo[1.1.1]pentanone and Comparison with Several Theoretical Structures

Glenn A. McRae, $^\dagger$ Edward A. Cohen, *

Jet Propulsion Laboratory, California Institute of Technology, Pasadena, California 91109

Michael B. Sponsler, $^\ddagger$ and Dennis A. Dougherty*$^\S$

Crellin Laboratory of Chemistry, California Institute of Technology, $^\perp$ Pasadena, California 91125

(Received: February 20, 1986)

The microwave spectra of five isotopic species of bicyclo[1.1.1]pentanone have been investigated. The rotational constants along with various centrifugal distortion constants for each species have been determined. From the rotational constants, a complete $r_s$ structure has been determined for the heavy atoms. Analysis of Stark effect measurements has shown the dipole moment to be along the $a$ principal inertial axis with a magnitude of 3.164 (5) D. These results are compared with those obtained by four current theoretical methods: molecular mechanics (MM2), MNDO, and Hartree-Fock ab initio theory with STO-3G and 3-21G basis sets.

### Introduction

The structures of strained ring organic compounds have been intensively studied.$^1$ Recent efforts have focused on the capabilities of various theoretical models to reproduce and predict such structures.$^2$ The synthesis and characterization of bicyclo[1.1.1]pentanone (1) has been recently reported.$^3$ This structure

![](./images/812293018387218433_1.jpg)

is of interest both as a strained, simple ketone, and as a representative of the relatively uncommon bicyclo[1.1.1]pentane ring system.

In the present study, we report microwave measurements of several isotopic species of 1. Analysis of these data yields a substitution structure for the heavy atom framework and a value for the dipole moment. We also report calculated structures and dipole moments for 1 obtained by using four currently popular theoretical models: molecular mechanics (MM2),$^4$ MNDO,$^5$ and Hartree-Fock (HF) ab initio MO theory$^6$ with STO-3G$^7$ and 3-21G$^8$ basis sets.

### Experimental Section

The sample was prepared by the previously published method.$^3$ A bulb containing the degassed sample was opened to the spectrometer and allowed to warm from 77 K until pressures of 15-30 mTorr were established in the sample cell.

The microwave spectra of the five isotopic species were observed with a modified Hewlett-Packard 8460A MRR spectrometer.$^9$ Stark effect measurements were made by varying the ground to base voltage of the 33-kHz square-wave modulation. OCS was used as the calibrant.$^{10}$

The three $^{13}$C isotopic species were studied in natural abundance. Although these spectra were readily observed, some lines were partially blended. These have been assigned larger experimental errors. Because of the high density of weak lines in the spectrum, it was not possible to assign transitions due to $^{18}$O in natural abundance. Therefore, the sample was enriched by adding an excess of H$_2^{18}$O and allowing the oxygen to exchange overnight. The resulting $^{18}$O spectrum was then of strength comparable to that of the normal isotopic species.

### Structure
All the heavy atoms of the ring have been isotopically substituted so it is possible to obtain the substitution coordinates, $r_s$, of these atoms with the aid of Kraitchman's equations.¹³ The difference between the $r_s$ and equilibrium structure, $r_{eq}$, for heavy atoms is usually small, on the order of thousandths of angstroms. This method is reviewed and discussed extensively by Gordy and Cook.¹²

The $r_s$ heavy atom a, b, and c coordinates are given in Table III. The A rotational constants were not used in any final structural determinations because the uncertainties for the substituted species were large compared to those of the B and C rotational constants. Because of the high symmetry of the molecule the standard relationships for substitution in a plane or on an axis of symmetry could be used.¹² The degree to which the derived constants obey these relationships is shown by the changes in the planar moments also given in Table III. This serves to verify the assumed $C_{2v}$ structure. The uncertainties listed in Table III reflect primarily the experimental uncertainties of the rotational constants and contain no estimates of the vibrational contributions. For substitutions on the a axis the stated error spans the two values obtained using either $\Delta I_b$ or $\Delta I_c$.

The hydrogens were not substituted. From the parent, only one first moment and three second moment relationships are available to determine the six coordinates necessary for the unique determination of the hydrogen positions. Only the c coordinate of the hydrogen bonded to $C_1$ is determinable directly from a second moment relationship. The determinable hydrogen parameters are listed in Table III and compared with those calculated from theoretically derived hydrogen positions relative to the experimental heavy atom frame. Because these parameters are derived in a way which does not compensate for vibrational effects, they cannot be compared in detail with theoretically derived parameters. They are included primarily for completeness and are consistent with the theoretical results.

## Discussion

The experimental structure of **1** (see Table V) can be compared with the electron diffraction structure of the parent hydrocarbon, bicyclo[1.1.1]pentane (**2**).¹⁴ The relevant structural parameters in **2** are the C-C bond length of 1.545 Å, the C₁-C₂-C₃ angle of 73.3°, and the interbridgehead C∙∙C distance of 1.845 Å.¹⁴ The C-CH₂-C angles of **1**, and of course the C-C(O)-C angle, are larger than the analogous angles in **2** due to the presence of the carbonyl. As a result, the interbridgehead distance is greater by 0.11 Å. The C₁-C₂ length is smaller than the C-C length in **2**, as expected for an sp²-sp³ bond. The C₁-C₄ bond length is larger by 0.028 Å. This lengthening could be a consequence of the expected orbital mixing between $\pi^\ast$ (C-O) and $\sigma$ (C₁-C₄). However, 1,3-nonbonded repulsive interactions, which were used to rationalize the long bonds in cyclobutane,¹⁵ can also explain the lengthening in this case. Simple geometrical considerations show that the larger interbridgehead C∙∙C distance in **1** relative to **2** brings the two CH₂ groups closer together, amplifying 1,3 repulsions. These repulsions can be alleviated by stretching the C₁-C₄ bond.

It has been noted¹⁶ that ketones with smaller C-C(O)-C angles should have shorter C═O bonds due to rehybridization. Although the effect is generally quite small, our results for **1** do support this analysis.

The experimental structure and dipole moment of **1** are compared in Table V with the various theoretical results. In making such comparisons, one should keep in mind that the different methods give different types of structural parameters.¹⁷ The microwave structure reported here is a substitution structure, while the ab initio and MNDO calculations give equilibrium structures. MM2 has been parameterized to fit diffraction structures.⁴ Therefore, only large differences between the experimental and theoretical structures (0.01 Å or 1°) should be taken as significant.

All four computational methods give reasonably satisfactory results. The MM2 geometry appears to be surprisingly good, considering the special problems encountered in treating small rings with this method,¹⁸ and the substantial overestimation by MM2 of the C-C bond length in **2** (calculated to be 1.566 Å).

Ab initio calculations using the 3-21G basis set come closest to reproducing the experimental structure. In accord with Wiberg's results²⁶ even the STO-3G minimal basis set produces a structure in reasonable agreement with experiment. MNDO also produces a reasonable structure.

The dipole moment of **1**, 3.16 D, is similar to those of related ketones.¹⁹ Of the theoretical methods used, MM2 best reproduced the dipole moment with a value of 3.07 D. However, this close agreement is fortuitous. By use of MM2, dipole moments of 2.92-3.07 D are calculated²⁰ for a selection of 10 ketones whose experimental moments span a much larger range of 2.87-3.35 D. In addition, the small variations for different structures calculated by MM2 do not correlate with the experimental trends in any way.

MNDO tends to underestimate the dipole moments of carbonyls (the error for acetone is 0.39 D).²ᵇ The results presented here follow this trend, but the error is quite large (0.69 D). The dipole moment calculated by using HF and the 3-21G basis set is high by only 0.20 D. The difference between the 3-21G dipole moment and the STO-3G moment (low by 1.07 D) reflects differences in the basis sets rather than in the optimized structures, since the 3-21G moment at the STO-3G structure is 3.426 D.

## Conclusion

A complete substitution structure has been determined for bicyclo[1.1.1]pentanone from microwave spectra of the parent molecule and four isotopically substituted species. In addition, the dipole moment has been determined from Stark effect measurements to be 3.164 (5) D and aligned with the $a$ principal inertial axis. Theoretical structures produced by MM2, MNDO, and HF (STO-3G and 3-21 G basis sets) are all in reasonable agreement with the rₛ structure.
