# Approximate Molecular Orbitals: VI-The $2 p \sigma$ state of $HeH^{2+}$

By R. McCARROLL AND B. L. MOISEIWITSCH

Department of Applied Mathematics, The Queen's University of Belfast

Communicated by D. R. Bates ; MS. received 9th August 1956

Abstract. Approximate wave functions for the $2 p \sigma$ state of the $HeH^{2+}$ molecular ion are obtained by the variational method. The values of the electronic total energies and potential energies computed using the approximate wave functions are compared with the exact values of these quantities.

Exact values of the dipole moment are also obtained.

## $\S 1$. INTRODUCTION
$I^{N}$ paper $V$ of this series (Moiseiwitsch and Stewart 1956), approximate wave functions for the $1 s \sigma$ and $2 p \pi$ states of the $He H^{2+}$ molecular ion were obtained using the variational method. These two states are the lowest with zero and unit angular momentum respectively about the internuclear axis. The present paper is concerned with a similar investigation of the $2 p \sigma$ state of $HeH^{2+}$. This presents greater difficulty than the two preceding cases as it is not the lowest state of its own particular symmetry. Thus, in applying the variational method, it is necessary to use approximate wave functions which are orthogonal to the ground $1 s \sigma$ state.

An assessment of the accuracy of the approximate wave functions is made by comparing the values obtained for the electronic energies and potential energies with the corresponding values obtained by using the exact two-centre wave functions (Bates and Carson 1956).

## $\S 2$. Choice of Wave Functions
Caution has to be exercised in the choice of approximate wave functions for the $2 p \sigma$ state of $HeH^{2+}$ as the dissociation products in this particular case are an $H$ atom and an $\alpha$ particle, contrary to the $1 s \sigma$ and $2 p \pi$ states treated in the previous paper which both give rise to a proton and an $He^{+}$ ion. Because of this behaviour an obvious approximation is
$$\Psi(2 \mathrm{p} \sigma)=\psi_{0}+C \psi_{1}+\lambda \psi_{2}+\mu \psi_{3}, \quad \ldots \ldots(1) \dagger$$
where
$$\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ } \pi} \exp \left(-r_{\mathrm{b}}\right), \quad \ldots \ldots(2)$$

$$\psi_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}} \exp \left(-2 r_{\mathrm{a}}\right), \quad \ldots \ldots(3)$$

$$\psi_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ } \pi} r_{\mathrm{a}} \cos \theta_{\mathrm{a}} \exp \left(-r_{\mathrm{a}}\right), \quad \ldots \ldots(4)$$

$$\psi_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ } \pi}\left(1-r_{\mathrm{a}}\right) \exp \left(-r_{\mathrm{a}}\right), \quad \ldots \ldots(5)$$

$\dagger$ Here and elsewhere normalization factors are omitted.

$\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{a}}$ and $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{b}}$ being the position vectors of the electron relative to the helium nucleus A and the proton B. Here, $\psi_{0}$ is the wave function of the 1s state of a hydrogen atom and $\psi_{1}, \psi_{2}$ and $\psi_{3}$ are the $1 \mathrm{~s}, 2 \mathrm{~s}$ and $2 \mathrm{p}_{z}$ wave functions of $\mathrm{He}^{+}$. The function (1) is then the usual LCAO approximation which takes account of degeneracy at infinite nuclear separation. The term involving $\psi_{1}$ has been introduced so as to make the function approximately orthogonal to the ground $1 \mathrm{~s} \sigma$ state of $\mathrm{HeH}^{2+}$.

It follows immediately that
$$
C=-\int \psi_{0}\left(r_{\mathrm{b}}\right) \psi_{1}\left(r_{\mathrm{a}}\right) d \mathbf{r}. \quad......(6)
$$

In the limit of small internuclear separations the $2 \mathrm{p} \sigma$ state of the $\mathrm{HeH}^{2+}$ molecular ion tends to the $2 \mathrm{p}_{z}$ state of $\mathrm{Li}^{2+}$. This suggests adopting as the united atom approximation
$$
\Psi(2 \mathrm{p} \sigma)=\left(\frac{243 \alpha^{5}}{32 \pi}\right)^{1 / 2} r \cos \theta \exp \left\{-\alpha\left(2 r_{\mathrm{a}}+r_{\mathrm{b}}\right) / 2\right\}+A \Psi(1 \mathrm{~s} \sigma) \quad......(7)
$$
where $\mathbf{r}$ is the position vector of the electron relative to the centre of charge of the constituent nuclei and where the parameter $A$ is chosen so as to make (7) orthogonal to the best wave function for the $1 \mathrm{~s} \sigma$ state of $\mathrm{HeH}^{2+}$ obtained in paper $\mathrm{V}$, which is of the form
$$
\Psi(1 \mathrm{~s} \sigma)=\exp \left\{-\alpha^{\prime}\left(2 r_{\mathrm{a}}+r_{\mathrm{b}}\right)\right\}+\mathrm{p}^{\prime} \exp \left(-2 \beta^{\prime} r_{\mathrm{a}}\right). \quad......(8)
$$

It is desirable to use a trial wave function which has the proper behaviour at both large and small internuclear distances. This may be achieved by using a combination of function (7) with the separated atoms function
$$
\Psi(2 \mathrm{p} \sigma)=\left(\frac{\beta^{3}}{\pi}\right)^{1 / 2} \exp \left(-\beta r_{\mathrm{b}}\right)+B \Psi(1 \mathrm{~s} \sigma) \quad......(9)
$$
where $B$ is chosen so as to make (9) orthogonal to the ground $1 \mathrm{~s} \sigma$ state function (8).

Such a combined function may be written in the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi(2 \mathrm{p} \sigma)= & \left(\frac{243 \alpha^{5}}{32 \pi}\right)^{1 / 2} r \cos \theta \exp \left\{-\alpha\left(2 r_{\mathrm{a}}+r_{\mathrm{b}}\right) / 2\right\}+\mathrm{p}\left(\frac{\beta^{3}}{\pi}\right)^{1 / 2} \exp \left(-\beta r_{\mathrm{b}}\right) \\
& +(A+p B) \Psi(1 \mathrm{~s} \sigma). \quad......(10)
\end{aligned}
$$

## § 3. Description of Calculations

The values of the parameters $\lambda, \mu$ in function (1), and the parameters $\alpha, \beta$ and $p$ in functions (7), (9) and (10) have been determined for $0 \leqslant R \leqslant 5$ by minimization of the electronic energy of the system
$$
E=-\int \Psi^{*}\left(\nabla^{2}+\frac{4}{r_{\mathrm{a}}}+\frac{2}{r_{\mathrm{b}}}\right) \Psi d \mathbf{r} \bigg/ \int \Psi^{*} \Psi d \mathbf{r}. \quad......(11)
$$

The functions considered were of type (1) and also of type (10) with
(i) $p=\infty, \beta=1$;
(ii) $p=\infty, \beta$ variable;
(iii) $p=0, \alpha=1$;
(iv) $p=0, \alpha$ variable;
(v) $\alpha=1, \beta=1, p$ variable;
(vi) $\alpha$ as determined by (iv) and $\beta$ by (ii), $p$ variable.

The values of the energy $E$ obtained with these functions can be compared with the exact values of these quantities calculated by Bates and Carson (1956). A further comparison can also be made by using the exact and approximate wave functions to calculate the potential energy

$$
V=-\int \Psi^{*}\left(\frac{4}{r_{\mathrm{a}}}+\frac{2}{r_{\mathrm{b}}}\right) \Psi d \mathbf{r} \bigg/ \int \Psi^{*} \Psi d \mathbf{r}. \quad \ldots \ldots(12)
$$

Finally, the exact wave functions are also used to compute the dipole moment integral
$$
Z=\int \Psi^{*} z_{\mathrm{b}} \Psi d \mathbf{r} \bigg/ \int \Psi^{*} \Psi d \mathbf{r} \quad \ldots \ldots(13)
$$
where $z_{\mathrm{b}}$ is the cartesian coordinate of the electron referred to an axis with origin at the proton and directed towards the $\alpha$ particle.

### § 5. DISCUSSION
As was to be expected the accuracy achieved for the $2 p \sigma$ state of $HeH^{2+}$ is not so good as for the $1 s \sigma$ and $2 p \pi$ states treated in the previous paper.

At large $R$ the contribution to the electronic energy from polarization is much greater for the $2 p \sigma$ state than for the $1 s \sigma$ or $2 p \pi$ states. Functions (1) and (ii) make only a small allowance for it. Consequently the separated atoms approximation is not nearly so satisfactory for the $2 p \sigma$ state as for the ground state or the $2 p \pi$ state. Function (1), which includes contributions from the degenerate $2 s$ and $2 p_{z}$ states of $He^{+}$, makes fuller allowance for polarization and gives better results over the whole range of $R$ than either function (1) or (ii).

Owing to degeneracy in the united atom limit, the $2p\sigma$ state is influenced to the first order in $R$ by the $2s$ state of $Li^{2+}$. Though functions (iii) and (iv) take no account of this they give quite accurate values for the electronic energy at small $R$.

Both functions (v) and (vi) yield satisfactory results for all $R$, function (vi) giving electronic energies which are in error by less than $1\%$ for $R\leqslant2$ and by no more than $3\frac{1}{2}\%$ over the entire range of $R$ investigated. The latter function also gives quite satisfactory values for the potential energy $V$.

Displayed in table 4 are values of the dipole moment integral calculated with the exact wave functions. It is unlikely that any of the approximate functions considered in this paper would give accurate values for $Z$ and they have therefore not been used in the calculation.
