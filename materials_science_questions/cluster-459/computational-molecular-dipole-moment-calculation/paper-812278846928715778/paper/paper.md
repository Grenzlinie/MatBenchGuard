L. LEJČEK

Institute of Physics, Czechosl. Acad. Sci., Prague**)

The total energy of a system of two straight infinite and parallel dislocations is studied with all possible combinations of Burgers vectors (quasidipoles) in the basal plane of anisotropic zinc. The stable configurations of quasidipoles are found and the binding energy calculated for various types of quasidipoles.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

In recent years several papers have been published which point clearly not only to the existence of stable dislocation configurations with different Burgers vectors in the basal plane of hexagonal metals, but also to the participation of these dis- locations in monocrystal hardening. Observations [1] on Be demonstrate the existence of dislocations of the primary and as well of the secondary basal slip systems forming stable configurations in the dense dislocation colonies which have been observed in the basal cut of deformed crystals. This has been proved by detailed analysis of the Burgers vectors of dislocations in deformed Co crystals ([5] in paper [8]). Experi- ments with etching dislocations on deformed Cd monocrystals [2] and the quantita- tive results of measuring the height of slip steps on hexagonal metals [4, 5] testify to the existence of a secondary basal slip system and permit the conclusion [3] that slip is realized not only by the primary slip system, but also by the secondary one. This role of the secondary slip system has been confirmed experimentally on Mg [6] which - as in the case of Zn, Be [1, 15, 16, 17] - leads to the conclusion that dis- location nets arise at a higher degree of deformation due to the interaction between dislocations of the primary and secondary slip systems. Paper [7], recently published, shows that in an isotropic case the dipole width depends on the angle between the Burgers vectors and the dislocation line. Paper [8] deals in detail with the stability of a system of two straight infinite dislocations with all possible configurations of the Burgers vectors in anisotropic material. Here the component of their interaction force in the slip plane was evaluated and from its course the equilibrium positions of the dislocation system were found and conclusions drawn regarding their stability. In addition, the dependence is plotted of the stable position and critical stress, neces- sary for destroying a stable configuration, on the position of the Burgers vector con- figurations and dislocation lines.

As the c.s. is firmly connected with the dislocation line, the angle $\alpha$ characterizes the position of the Burgers vectors configuration relative to the dislocation lines. The total energy of a quasidipole $U$ per unit length is given as the sum of the self-energies $U_{1}$ and $U_{2}$ and the interaction energy $U_{12}$; this one we obtain as in [9]. ### Quasidipoles in the Basal Plane of Hexagonal Zinc

An extreme condition $\partial U/\partial x = 0$ at fixed $\alpha$, $\varphi$ and $y$ indicates the equilibrium positions $\varrho$ of the quasidipole (here $\varrho = x/y$) which are stable or unstable according to the course of $U$. The results of investigating this are presented in Table 1. For comparison we present also isotropic Zn, the elasticity constants of polycrystalline Zn being given in [13]. For various $\varphi$ it is sufficient always to give 7 values of $\alpha$ (with $15^{\circ}$ difference from each other) as the $U$ values are symmetric for $\alpha_{0}$ and $\alpha_{m}$.

Let $U_{m}$ be the maximum energy value of a quasidipole and $U_{d}$ the minimum energy value corresponding to the stable equilibrium position. The binding energy $U_{b}$ we define as $U_{b}=U_{m}-U_{d}$ (for a quasidipole with $\varphi = 120^{\circ}$ and $180^{\circ}$ one has $U_{m}==U_{0}=U_{1}+U_{2}$ - the sum of the self-energies, i.e. $U_{b}=U_{0}-U_{d}$ as given in in [14]). In the case of quasidipole with two stable equilibrium positions separated by an unstable equilibrium position with local energy maximum $U_{lm}$, it is possible to define the energy $\Delta U_{d}$ of transition from one stable position into another by the relation $\Delta U_{d}=U_{lm}-U_{d}$. (This has sense for quasidipoles with $\varphi = 120^{\circ}$ and $180^{\circ}$.) One can see from relation (2) that $\Delta U_{d}$ depends only on $\varrho$ corresponding to the quasidipole's equilibrium position. The numerical values of $U_{b}$ and $\Delta U_{d}$ are presented in Table 2. As $\Delta U_{d}=\Delta U_{d}(\alpha, \varphi, \varrho)$, it follows from the symmetry that

$$
(3)\quad U_{b}(\alpha, 0^{\circ})=\Delta U_{d}(\alpha, 180^{\circ}, \varrho) ;\quad U_{b}(\alpha, 60^{\circ})=\Delta U_{d}(\alpha+60^{\circ}, 120^{\circ}, \varrho)
$$

quasidipoles with $\varphi = 240^{\circ}$ and $300^{\circ}$ need not be investigated separately because

$$
(4)\quad \begin{aligned}
U(\alpha+60^{\circ}, 60^{\circ}, x, y) & =U(\alpha, 300^{\circ}, x, y) ; \\
U(\alpha+120^{\circ}, 120^{\circ}, x, y) & =U(\alpha, 240^{\circ}, x, y).
\end{aligned}
$$

### 3. DISCUSSION

The results in Tab. 1 inform us when the quasidipoles are stable and what is the width $x$ corresponding to their stable configuration (the width $x$ is determined from the equation $\varrho = x/y$ for given $\varrho$ from Tab. 1 and chosen inter-plane distance). Comparing the isotropic and anisotropic cases, we see that in the latter case the displacement of the equilibrium positions of the quasidipoles occurs (relative to the isotropic case). This is illustrated in Figs. 4 and 5 where we plot the angle $\vartheta$ corresponding to the equilibrium position as a function of $\alpha$ ($\vartheta$ being defined by $\cot \vartheta=\varrho$). We can see from energy considerations how stable are the quasidipoles. Quasidipoles with $\varphi = 0^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$ and with an edge character (i.e. with the interaction of edge components predominating) are stable for $x = 0$.

Quasidipoles with the same $\varphi$ but of screw character have, however, no stable position. Their binding energy differs in order from that of quasidipoles with $\varphi = 120^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, as follows from Tab. 2 with the help of (3). Consequently, quasidipoles with $\varphi = 120^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ will form stable configurations in the basal plane of hexagonal Zn much more frequently than quasidipoles with $\varphi = 0^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ which are either unstable or have little binding energy. Therefore one can assume that quasidipoles with $\varphi = 0^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ will practically not occur in Zn.

## 4. SUMMARY

In this paper we have investigated the total energy of two straight infinite dislocations in different basal planes of hexagonal Zn and calculated:

1) the stable configurations of quasidipoles with $\varphi = 0, 60, 120, 180^\circ$, comparing the results of the isotropic and the anisotropic case,
2) the binding energy of these quasidipoles and concluded that only quasidipoles with $\varphi = 120$ and 180 can be realized in practice.
