# Impact of thermal stress on the piezoelectric and dielectric properties of $PbTiO_{3}$ thick films on various substrates

Gang Bai, $^{1,2,a)}$ Zhiguo Liu, $^{2}$ Xiaobing Yan, $^{3}$ and Changchun Zhang $^{1}$

$^{1}$ College of Electronic Science \& Engineering and Microelectronic Functional Materials and Devices Research Center, Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications, Nanjing 210046, People's Republic of China
$^{2}$ Department of Materials Science and Engineering and National Laboratory of Solid State Microstructures, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210093, People's Republic of China
$^{3}$ College of Electronic and information Engineering, Hebei University, Baoding 071002, People's Republic of China

(Received 18 May 2014; accepted 7 July 2014; published online 4 August 2014)

The impact of thermal stress on the polarization, as well as dielectric and piezoelectric properties of (001) oriented $PbTiO_{3}$ (PTO) thick films deposited on various substrates was investigated based on Landau-Devonshire thermodynamic model. The results showed that dielectric and piezoelectric properties of PTO films depend strongly on the thermal stress in PTO films decided by the deposition temperature $T_{G}$ and the thermal expansion coefficients' difference between PTO films and substrates. For IC-compatible substrates such as Si, c-sapphire, and a-sapphire that induce tensile in-plane thermal stresses, the dielectric and piezoelectric responses and tunabilities of PTO films were enhanced. Whereas for PTO films on MgO, compressive thermal in-plane stresses can degraded the dielectric and piezoelectric responses and tunabilities of the films. © 2014 AIP Publishing LLC.

[http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4891978]

## I. INTRODUCTION

Ferroelectric films have attracted much attention in recent years due to the diversity of their potential applications in integrated devices. $^{1-3}$ The physical properties of ferroelectric films are substantially different from those of bulk ferroelectrics, due to the internal stress introduced by the attached substrates. $^{4,5}$ The internal stress arises from both epitaxial (misfit) strain due to the difference in lattice parameter between films and substrates and thermal strain due to the different thermal expansion coefficients (TECs) between films and substrates. Prior studies are mostly based on the misfit strain of the epitaxial thin films, $^{6-8}$ among them the misfit strain can be relaxed by the generation of misfit dislocations during film growth and annealing, especially for thick films. $^{9,10}$ Therefore, in case of the piezoelectric films for actual application (thickness $>1\ \mu m$ ), thermal strain might become dominant. In practical piezoelectric applications of high power actuators, high sensitivity sensors and energy harvesting devices, the working temperature of the devices is normally much lower than the deposition temperature of the films, leading to the existence of remarkable thermal stresses in the films. Thus, there is a critical need to understand the effect of thermal strain on the piezoelectric behavior of thick ferroelectric films. The effect of the thermal strain on piezoelectricity has been extensively studied experimentally for ferroelectric films grown on substrates having different TECs, for example, it has been reported that with increasing the compressive thermal strain, the remnant polarization enhances due to the domain alignment along the direction perpendicular to the substrate, $^{11-14}$ and the piezoelectricity increases in the tensile strain condition rather than compressive one especially for the thicker films with a thickness more than $2\ \mu m.^{15,16}$ So far, systematic theoretical calculation of the thermal strain dependence of the piezoelectric behavior in thick ferroelectric films is still lack. In this paper, we report the effect of thermal strain on piezoelectric properties of thick $PbTiO_{3}$ (PTO) films using Landau-Devonshire phenomenological model.

## II. LANDAU THEORY

We consider here an (001)-oriented PTO film much thicker than the critical thickness grown on thick cubic IC friendly Si, c-sapphire, and $a$-sapphire substrates. The films were grown and annealed at a temperature $T_{G}$, then cooled to room temperature (RT). In case of physical vapor deposition process, $T_{G}$ corresponds to the substrate temperature during growth. For chemical deposition technique, $T_{G}$ is the final annealing temperature. In our calculations, because the thickness of PTO ferroelectric film is larger than the critical thickness (50-250 nm) for the generation of misfit dislocations, $^{9,10}$ the misfit strain is supposed completely relaxed. The in-plane thermal strain $u_{T}$ due to different TECs between films and substrates can be calculated by

$$
u_{T}=\int_{RT}^{T_{G}}\left(\alpha_{F}-\alpha_{S}\right)dT, \tag{1}
$$

where $\alpha_{F}$, $\alpha_{S}$ are the in-plane TECs of films and substrates, respectively. The TECs of the film materials PTO and the substrates (001) Si, $c$-sapphire, and $a$-sapphire used in our calculations are given on Table I from Refs. 17 to 19.

For PTO films on (001) Si and $c$-sapphire having isotropic TECs, the free energy density of the film, taking into account the in-plane thermal stresses, can be expressed as¹⁹
$$
\tilde{G}=\alpha_{1}^{*} P^{2}+\alpha_{11}^{*} P^{4}+\alpha_{111} P^{6}+\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{s_{11}+s_{12}}-E P, \quad (2)
$$
where both $P$ and $E$ are oriented along the [001] direction. The renormalized dielectric stiffness coefficients $\alpha_{1}^{*}$ and $\alpha_{11}^{*}$ are
$$
\alpha_{1}^{*}=\alpha_{1}-\frac{2 Q_{12}}{s_{11}+s_{12}} u_{T} \quad (3)
$$

$$
\alpha_{11}^{*}=\alpha_{11}+\frac{Q_{12}^{2}}{s_{11}+s_{12}}, \quad (4)
$$
where $\alpha_{1}$, $\alpha_{11}$, and $\alpha_{111}$ are the bulk dielectric stiffness coefficients. The quadratic coefficient $\alpha_{1}$ is given by the Curie-Weiss Law, $\alpha_{1}=\frac{T-T_{0}}{2 \varepsilon_{0} C}$, where $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the permittivity of free space, $T_{0}$ is Curie-Weiss temperature, and $C$ is the Curie-Weiss constant. $Q_{12}$ is the relevant cubic electrostrictive coefficient and $s_{11}$ and $s_{12}$ are the cubic elastic compliances at constant dielectric displacement. The equilibrium states of the phases are obtained by minimizing the modified thermodynamic potential $\tilde{G}$ The thermodynamic, elastic, and electromechanical data used in this calculations can be obtained in Table II from Ref. 6.

The out-of-plane relative dielectric constant is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{33}=\left(\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial^{2} G}{\partial P^{2}}\right)^{-1}=\left[\varepsilon_{0}\left(2 \alpha_{1}^{*}+12 \alpha_{11}^{*} P^{2}+30 \alpha_{111} P^{4}\right)\right]^{-1}. & \\
& (5)
\end{aligned}
$$

The out-of-plane piezoelectric coefficient is
$$
d_{33}=\frac{\partial u_{3}}{\partial E}=\frac{\partial u_{3}}{\partial P} \cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial E}=2 \varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{33}\left[Q_{11}-\frac{2 s_{12} Q_{12}}{s_{11}+s_{12}}\right] P. \quad (6)
$$

The dielectric and piezoelectric tunabilities can be defined as, respectively
$$
\varphi=\frac{\varepsilon_{33}(0)-\varepsilon_{33}(E)}{\varepsilon_{33}(0)} × 100 \% \quad (7)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varphi^{\prime} & =\frac{d_{33}(0)-d_{33}(E)}{d_{33}(0)} × 100 \% \\
& =\left[1-\frac{\varepsilon_{33}(E)}{\varepsilon_{33}(0)} × \frac{P(E)}{P(0)}\right] × 100 \%. \quad (8)
\end{aligned}
$$

For $a$-sapphire substrates having anisotropy in-plane TECs, the in-plane strains due to the thermal stresses along $a$- and $c$-axes are not equal. In this situation, the free energy density becomes
$$
\tilde{G}=\alpha_{3}^{*} P^{2}+\alpha_{33}^{*} P^{4}+\alpha_{111} P^{6}+\frac{s_{11}\left(u_{T 1}^{2}+u_{T 2}^{2}\right)}{2\left(s_{11}^{2}-s_{12}^{2}\right)}-E P \quad (9)
$$

$$
\alpha_{3}^{*}=\alpha_{1}-\frac{Q_{12}}{s_{11}+s_{12}}\left(u_{T 1}+u_{T 2}\right) \quad (10)
$$

$$
\alpha_{33}^{*}=\alpha_{11}+\frac{Q_{12}^{2}}{s_{11}+s_{12}}. \quad (11)
$$

### III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The dependence of the thermal strains $(u_{T})$ on the growth temperature $T_{G}$ for PTO on the various substrates is shown in Fig. 2. Because the TECs of Si, $c$-sapphire, and $a$-sapphire are smaller than that of the PTO films in the temperature range of our analysis (Fig. 1), the in-plane thermal strains in PTO films are tensile. As seen in Fig. 2, $u_{T}$ increases with $T_{G}$. For PTO films on (001) Si and $c$-sapphire which have isotropic in-plane TECs, the thermal strains are biaxial. For PTO films on $a$-sapphire, however, the in-plane thermal strains are anisotropic. Because of $\alpha_{S}(\text{MgO})>\alpha_{F}(\text{PbTiO}_{3})$, $u_{T}$ is compressive for PTO on MgO, and its magnitude also increases with increasing $T_{G}$.

This result is consistent with the experimental observations from Refs. 11 to 14. Consequently, it is important to note that the polarization of PTO films depends on the complex interplay between $T_G$ and substrates, both of which give rise to thermal strains.

In Fig. 4, we plot the dependence of out-of-plane dielectric permittivity ($\varepsilon_{33}$) on $T_G$ for PTO films on different substrates. For the whole deposition temperature range considered in this analysis, a monotonic increase is observed in $\varepsilon_{33}$ with increasing deposition temperature $T_G$ for PTO on Si, $c$-Al$_2$O$_3$, and $a$-Al$_2$O$_3$ ($u_T>0$). According to Eq. (5), it appears that $P$ and $\varepsilon_{33}$ are approximately inversely related. $P$ decreases with increasing $T_G$ due to a larger tensile strain (Fig. 3). The smaller the polarization, the larger is the dielectric constant. $\varepsilon_{33}$ of PTO films on Si is larger than that of PTO films on other substrates. This is because Si substrate has the lowest TECs compared with other substrates, which leads to the largest in-plane tensile thermal strain. However, $\varepsilon_{33}$ of PTO on MgO ($u_T<0$) has a monotonic decrease with the increase of $T_G$ due to a larger compressive thermal strain. It is worthwhile to note that our calculated results are opposite compared with the results in SrTiO$_3$ reported in Ref. 20. This might come from the fact that the Landau coefficients of PbTiO$_3$ and SrTiO$_3$ are different. For SrTiO$_3$, since $a_{11}^{*}\ll|a_{1}^{*}|$, the term $12a_{11}^{*}P^2$ is small and plays a minor role, $\varepsilon_{33}$ of SrTiO$_3$ films for which $u_{\text{T}}<0$ (MgO) increases with increasing $T_{\text{G}}$, and $\varepsilon_{33}$ of SrTiO$_3$ films for which $u_{\text{T}}>0$ (Si and $c$-Al$_2$O$_3$) decreases with increasing $T_{\text{G}}$. For PbTiO$_3$, since $a_{11}^{*}\gg|a_{1}^{*}|$, and $a_{111}\gg|a_{1}^{*}|$, because the terms $12a_{11}^{*}P^2$ and $30\alpha_{111}P^4$ are large and play the important role, $\varepsilon_{33}$ of PbTiO$_3$ films for which $u_{\text{T}}<0$ (MgO) decrease with increasing $T_{\text{G}}$ and vice versa.

Although tensile thermal stress decreases $P$ and increase $\varepsilon_{33}$, the total effect is that tensile thermal stress increases the out-of-plane piezoelectric response $d_{33}$, but the extent of increase of piezoelectric response $d_{33}$ is less than that of dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{33}$. As expected, $d_{33}$ is larger for PTO films on Si compared to other substrates because of the larger tensile thermal strain for PTO films on Si. So the piezoelectric coefficient $d_{33}$ depends strongly on deposition temperature $T_{G}$ and TECs of substrates, both of which determine the magnitude of thermal strains.

Dielectric tunability is one of key design parameters of tunable microwave devices. $^{21}$ Piezoelectric tunability would enable frequency agile operation, eliminate filter banks as a costly and lossy source, and provide the possibility to get rid of the need for frequency trimming. $^{22}$ Fig. 6 shows the RT dielectric (a) and piezoelectric (b) tunabilities of PTO films on Si, $c$-$Al_{2}O_{3}$, $a$-$Al_{2}O_{3}$, MgO substrates as a function of $T_{G}$ at $E = 1000$ kV/cm. For Si, $c$-$Al_{2}O_{3}$, $a$-$Al_{2}O_{3}$ substrates $(u_{T}>0)$, the RT dielectric and piezoelectric tunabilities of PTO films increase more drastically with increasing $T_{G}$ due to the larger tensile thermal strain. Since $\alpha_{F}(\text{PbTiO}_{3}) > \alpha_{S}(c\text{-Al}_{2}\text{O}_{3}) > \alpha_{S}(a\text{-Al}_{2}\text{O}_{3}) > \alpha_{S}(\text{Si})$, PTO on Si has the largest dielectric and piezoelectric tunabilities, because of the largest thermal tensile stress at the same $T_{G}$. However, for MgO substrate, the dielectric and piezoelectric tunabilities of PTO films decrease with increasing $T_{G}$ due to the larger compressive thermal strain. At the same thermal stress and electric filed $E$, the piezoelectric tunability of PTO is always less than the dielectric tunability. This is because the degree of variation in piezoelectric response $d_{33}$ is less than that of dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{33}$.

## IV. CONCLUSIONS

The dependence of the dielectric and piezoelectric properties on thermally induced internal stress in highly textured (001)-oriented PTO films on various substrates was analyzed via Landau-Devonshire thermodynamic model. Calculated results showed that the thermal stress caused by the thermal expansion coefficients difference between PTO films and substrates can affect the dielectric and piezoelectric behavior of PTO thick films remarkably. Careful choice of the deposition temperature and the thermal expansion coefficients of the substrates might be used as additional design parameters to achieve desirable piezoelectric and dielectric properties of PTO and other ferroelectric films.
