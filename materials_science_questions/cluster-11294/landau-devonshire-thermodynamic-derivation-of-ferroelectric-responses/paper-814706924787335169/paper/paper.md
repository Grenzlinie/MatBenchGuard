# Large electrical manipulation of permittivity in $BaTiO_3$ and $Pb(Zr,Ti)O_3$ bimorph heterostructure

Penghong Ci, $^{1}$ Li Zhang, $^{2}$ Guoxi Liu, $^{1}$ and Shuxiang Dong $^{1,a)}$
$^{1}$Materials Science and Engineering, College of Engineering, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China
$^{2}$Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100084, China

(Received 1 June 2014; accepted 9 August 2014; published online 19 August 2014)

We report a strain-mediated electric field manipulation of permittivity in $BaTiO_3$ (barium titanate, BT) ceramic by a $Pb(Zr,Ti)O_3$ (PZT) bimorph. This BT/PZT heterostructure exhibited a relatively large permittivity tunability of BT up to $\pm 10\%$ in a wide frequency range under an electric field of $\pm 4$kV/cm applied to the PZT bimorph. The permittivity tunability is attributed to the strain in BT produced by the PZT bimorph. Calculations of the relationship between permittivity and applied electric field were developed, and corresponded well with measurements. The BT/PZT heterostructure has potential for applications in broadband field tunable smart electronic devices. © 2014 AIP Publishing LLC. [http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4893614]

$BaTiO_3$ (barium titanate, BT) and BT-based ceramics are extremely important dielectric materials from scientific and industrial points of view. $^{1,2}$ Discovered about 70 years ago, BT has important applications in a wide range of electronic components, $^{3,4}$ such as high density and multilayer ceramic capacitors, infrared detectors, and electromagnetic pulse generating devices, because of its high relative permittivity. $^{5-8}$ Additionally, ferroelectric materials, especially BT-based materials, have been recognized as the most important candidates for applications in tunable microwave devices and smart electronics, because of their tunable permittivity. $^{9-11}$

Many researchers have investigated the dielectric response of BT materials to external fields, such as external stress, bias electric field, and temperature. $^{1-3,8,9,12-17}$ Although the permittivity of BT materials exhibits a tunability under a direct current (dc) bias electric field, the bias electric field was normally applied to the BT directly, which restricted its application as a tunable capacitor. $^{1,8,9,13,14}$ It is also impractical for electronic devices to adjust capacitance by changing the temperature or through lattice mismatch. $^{3,8,9,15,16}$ Many previous experiments have pointed out that an applied pressure greatly affects the permittivity of BT ceramics. $^{1,2,8,12,13}$ Unfortunately, previous reports focused on the effect of mechanical loads on the permittivity of BT materials, and these mechanical loads were provided by a motor or heavy mechanical equipment. Therefore, the setup for tunable permittivity was very heavy and complex, making it unsuitable for miniaturization and integration. $^{1,8,18}$ Few researchers considered this issue from the aspect of piezoelectric actuation design, which can generate a suitable mechanical stress in a compact structure.

In this study, a $BT/Pb(Zr,Ti)O_3$ (PZT) bimorph heterostructure was developed. Calculations of the permittivity and stress field relationship in BT materials were also presented. The BT/PZT heterostructure exhibited a large electric manipulation of permittivity in the BT layer over a wide frequency range using the PZT bimorph actuator, which overcomes the aforementioned shortcomings of traditional permittivity tunability. The PZT bimorph can produce a strain on the BT layer, and correspondingly, provide a strain-mediated electric field manipulation of the permittivity in the BT layer. The proposed BT/PZT heterostructure is a good candidate for broadband field tunable capacitors or smart electronic devices.

The BT/PZT heterostructure was made of a BT layer and a PZT piezoelectric bimorph actuator, see Fig. 1. The $230-\mu$m-thick unpolarized BT ceramic was provided by Shandong Tsinghua Tongfang Luying Electronic Co., Ltd. The PZT ceramic plates $(45 \times 7 \times 1\ \text{mm}^3)$ of the bimorph actuator were coated with silver electrodes and then polarized in the thickness direction under a poling electric field of 20 kV/mm (twice as much as the coercive field) at room temperature. The BT layer and one pair of PZT layers (piezo-bimorph structure) were bonded together with insulating epoxy resin (ECCOBONDTM 45, Emerson & Cuming Co., Ltd., USA) and cured for 24 h at room temperature. A control voltage (equal to the tuning electric field times the thickness of the PZT plate) was applied to the PZT bimorph actuator by a high voltage bipolar power supply (Kepco BOP 1000M, USA), as shown in Fig. 1. The capacitance spectra of the BT were measured using a precision impedance analyzer (Agilent 4294A, USA). All measurements were performed at room temperature.

The relative permittivity tunability of the BT layer is defined as $\gamma$

$$
\gamma=\frac{\varepsilon_{3}\left(E_{3, P Z T}\right)-\varepsilon_{3}(0)}{\varepsilon_{3}(0)}, \quad(1)
$$

where $\varepsilon_{3}(0)$ is the relative permittivity of the BT at zero electric field applied to the PZT. Fig. 2(b) illustrates the permittivity tunability $\gamma$ versus frequency curve under different applied electric fields to PZT. The maximum value of $\gamma$ is about $\pm 10 \%$ at $E_{3, P Z T}= \pm 4 \mathrm{kV} / \mathrm{cm}$. Interestingly, it was found that the BT/PZT heterostructure exhibits a large permittivity tunability over a considerably wide bandwidth from 0.1 kHz to 100 kHz, as shown in Fig. 2(b). Some previous papers have described the permittivity tunability of dielectric materials in magnetoelectric composites modulated by a magnetic field. $^{19,20}$ These designs were based on the change of the magnetic materials' Young's modulus ( $\Delta \mathrm{E}$ effect) $^{21}$ however, this mechanism was effective only at their resonance frequencies, which restricted their applications as tunable electronic smart devices. $^{19,20}$ Our result is apparently superior to these traditional designs, and our use of the PZT bimorph to apply stress also overcomes the shortcomings of some previous stress-induced permittivity tunability designs, which used heavy equipment to generate force. $^{1,8,18}$

Stress-induced permittivity tunability has been reported by many previous researchers who considered the influence of polydomain states and gave some quantitative discussions, for example, Chang reported the strain dependence of the dielectric constant of $\mathrm{Ba}_{1-\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sr}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{TiO}_{3}$ (BST) bulk and film at a large strain (about $0.5 \%$ ), Lee and Yang also did some numerical analysis on the permittivity of BT ceramic and film under high stress (up to $800 \mathrm{MPa}$ ). $^{1,22,23}$ These previous studies used the classical three-dimensional Gibbs free energy function, and their results agreed with our results when the stress was small.

In this study, we also calculated approximately the relationship between permittivity of BT and applied electric field to PZT bimorph to explain the measurements. The electrostrictive charge coefficient $Q_{i j k l}$ can be expressed as $^{24-27}$

$$
Q_{i j k l}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial \chi_{k l}}{\partial X_{i j}}\right)_{T, P}=\frac{1}{2 \varepsilon_{0}\left(\varepsilon_{k l}-1\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial \varepsilon_{k l}}{\partial X_{i j}}\right)_{T, P}, \quad(2)
$$

where $X_{i j}$ is the elastic stress, $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the permittivity of free space, $\varepsilon_{k l}$ is the relative permittivity, and $\chi_{k l}$ is the dielectric stiffness which can be noted as $\chi_{k l}=1 /\left(\varepsilon_{0}\left(\varepsilon_{k l}-1\right)\right)$. Although Eq. (2) is applicable for linear systems, many previous studies reported that the dielectric stiffness $\chi_{k l}$ (or $1 / \varepsilon_{k l}$ ) of ferroelectric materials still keeps the linear relationship when the applied stress is small, for example, Zhang's study pointed out that $\chi_{33}$ and the pressure followed a linear relationship at a low pressure, and Shirane, Uchino, and Samara have also reported a similar phenomenon. $^{26,28-30}$ In our experiment, the BT ceramic is not polarized and exhibits an electrostrictive effect, and the stress applied to the BT is very small at constant temperature (lower than the Curie temperature, $T_{c}$ ); therefore, it is reasonable to assume that the dielectric stiffness of BT and stress followed a linear relationship, and Eq. (2) can be used to approximate the stress dependence of the permittivity in this study. Then, integrating Eq. (2) and using the Voigt reduction notation, we can obtain the relationship between the relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{k l}$ (expressed in Voigt notation as $\varepsilon_{j}$ ) and the stress $X_{i j}$ (expressed as $X_{i}$ ) at constant temperature $T$ and constant electric polarization $P$ as follows: $^{29}$

$$
\left.\varepsilon_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right|_{T, P}=\frac{1}{-2 \varepsilon_{0} Q_{i j} X_{i}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}(0)-1}}+1, \quad(3)
$$

where $\varepsilon_{j}(0)$ is the value of $\varepsilon_{j}$ when $X_{i}=0$ at constant temperature (lower than $T_{c}$ ).

Note that the stress $X_{i}$ is generated by the piezoelectric strain of a PZT bimorph, and the electrical manipulation of permittivity in the BT layer is realized by the piezoelectric strain, as shown in Fig. 1. The PZT bimorph in the heterostructure is composed of two piezoelectric layers with thickness polarization; then, a BT layer is bonded to the top surface of the bimorph. It is known that the electric field induced strain $x_{1, P Z T}$ in the PZT bimorph is

$$
x_{1, P Z T}=\frac{3}{2} d_{31, P Z T} E_{3, P Z T}, \quad(4)
$$

where $d_{31,PZT}$ is the piezoelectric strain coefficient of the PZT ceramic, and $E_{3,PZT}$ is the electric field applied to the PZT layer. $^{31,32}$ The notation $(_{PZT})$ means piezoelectric PZT layer. The electric field induced strain in the bimorph structure is 3/2 times larger than that in a single PZT layer (unimorph structure); therefore, a larger strain is transferred to the BT layer under an identical applied electric field. $^{31}$ As a consequence, the BT/PZT heterostructure using the PZT bimorph actuator will be expected to produce an enhanced strain-mediated coupling and thus realize a stronger electrical manipulation of permittivity in the BT layer.

When the BT layer is much thinner than the PZT bimorph, it is reasonable to assume that the influence of the BT layer on the PZT bimorph can be ignored and the piezoelectric strain $x_{1,PZT}$ generated by the PZT bimorph is completely transferred into the BT layer; $^{31}$ thus, the boundary condition for the BT/PZT heterostructure is $x_1 = x_{1,PZT}$, where $x_1$ and $x_{1,PZT}$ are the longitudinal strain of the BT layer and PZT bimorph, respectively. The stress applied to the BT layer can be obtained using the stress-strain relationship $X_1 = x_1/s_{11}^E$, where $s_{11}^E$ is the elastic compliance coefficient of the BT at constant electric field $E$. Then, using Eqs. (3) and (4), the boundary condition $x_1 = x_{1,PZT}$, and the stress-strain relationship $X_1 = x_1/s_{11}^E$, we finally obtain the electrical manipulation relationship between the permittivity $\varepsilon_3$ of the BT layer and the electric field $E_{3,PZT}$ applied to the PZT layer for the BT/PZT heterostructure as follows:

$$
\left. \varepsilon_3(E_{3,PZT}) \right|_{T,P} = \frac{1}{\frac{-3\varepsilon_0 Q_{13} d_{31,PZT} E_{3,PZT}}{s_{11}^E} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_3(0)-1}} + 1,
\tag{5a}
$$

where $Q_{13}$ is the electrostrictive charge coefficient of the BT layer ($Q_{13} = Q_{12}$, according to their symmetry), and $\varepsilon_3(0)$ is the value of $\varepsilon_3$ when $E_{3,PZT} = 0$. Clearly, the relative permittivity $\varepsilon_3$ of the BT layer is a function of the electric field $E_{3,PZT}$ applied to the PZT layer. According to a Taylor series expansion about $E_{3,PZT}$, and $\varepsilon_3(E_{3,PZT}) \gg 1$, Eq. (5a) can be rewritten as

$$
\left. \varepsilon_3(E_{3,PZT}) \right|_{T,P} \approx \varepsilon_3(0) + \frac{3\varepsilon_0 (\varepsilon_3(0))^2 Q_{13} d_{31,PZT}}{s_{11}^E} E_{3,PZT}. \tag{5b}
$$

Because $Q_{13} < 0$ for the BT layer and $d_{31,PZT} < 0$ for the PZT, it can be seen from Eq. (5b) that when $E_{3,PZT} > 0$, $\varepsilon_3$ of the BT layer increases with the electric field $E_{3,PZT}$ applied to the PZT layer; otherwise, when $E_{3,PZT} < 0$, $\varepsilon_3$ decreases. This prediction agrees well with the measurements as shown in Fig. 2.

Fig. 3 shows that the measured and calculated relative permittivity of the BT layer at 1 kHz as a function of the applied electric field to the PZT. It should be noted that the measured permittivity versus electric field exhibits ferroelectric relaxation because of the ferroelectric strain hysteresis in the PZT bimorph. However, the calculated permittivity tunability of BT by Eq. (5a) exhibits a near linear relationship via the applied electric field $E_{3,PZT}$. The materials parameters of PZT and BT used for the calculations are listed in Table I. Although the effect of ferroelectric strain hysteresis is not included, the calculated values correspond well with the measured values.

In summary, we presented a relatively large electric manipulation of permittivity in a BT/PZT heterostructure. The working mechanism of permittivity manipulation originates from the strain in BT produced by the PZT bimorph. The permittivity tunability of BT is up to $\pm 10\%$ over a wide range of frequencies under an applied electric field $E_{3,PZT}$ of $\pm 4$ kV/cm, which is superior to conventional designs of tunable capacitors. A calculation of $\varepsilon_3$ as a function of $E_{3,PZT}$ that predicted the $\varepsilon_3 - E_{3,PZT}$ relationship well was deduced. The large electrical manipulation of permittivity in the BT/PZT heterostructure is suitable for applications in electrically controlled smart devices, such as broadband field tunable capacitors.
