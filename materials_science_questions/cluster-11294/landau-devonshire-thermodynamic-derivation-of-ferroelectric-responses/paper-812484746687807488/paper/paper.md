## ABSTRACT
Monoclinic distortion stabilized in a compressively strained [111] $PbTiO_{3}$ , for which the polarization vector is confined in a symmetry plane, is investigated using a phenomenological model. Rhombohedral to monoclinic transitions can be induced by stress normal to the film surface when flattening of energy surface reaches a critical barrier level. Two types of monoclinic states close in free energy exhibit opposite direction of induced polarization rotation. The impact of compressive stress on polarization, monoclinic shear strain, and volume change of unit cell is calculated and compared with experimental reports in the literature. Dielectric and piezoelectric anomalies are predicted in the stabilized monoclinic phase.

Polarization rotation and monoclinic phase have been shown to be crucial for ultrahigh piezoelectric response observed in some perovskite-oxide solid solutions [1-6] containing $PbTiO_{3}$ (PTO). Similar phenomena occur in lead-free piezoelectric materials as well [7,8]. Investigations [9-11] also showed that large electromechanical coupling can be present in pure PTO due to pressure or electric field induced monoclinic phases. To make use of the superior response in miniaturized devices it is a requisite to realize controlled growth of thin films in the monoclinic phase by engineering epitaxial strain. So far, [001]-oriented epitaxy has been widely studied. For coherent growth of [001]-PTO tetragonal (T) phase is very stable under in-plane compression while low-symmetry monoclinic phase can be induced by in-plane tensile strain [12-16]. Compressive strain is easily achieved using conventional single-crystal substrates such as $SrTiO_{3}$ (STO), while peculiar substrates such as $DyScO_{3}$ [17] are needed in order to realize sufficient tensile strain. The experimentally observed phase structure and polarization states can be understood by phenomenological modeling [18,19] based upon Landau-Ginzburg-Devonshire (LGD) theory [20].

Recent experimental investigations [21] found that [111]-oriented ultrathin compressively strained PTO epitaxial film coherently grown on STO substrate accommodates monoclinic structure with a large volume contraction of unit cell (~4% relative to bulk tetragonal) and a large polarization $(P_{s}>0.50\ C/m^{2})$, suggesting high potential in technological applications. This finding, however, in some way contrasts sharply with the previous theoretical expectations [22-24]. The epitaxial [111]-PTO is coherently lattice-matched with STO, and therefore subject to large in-plane compressive strain. Unlike the case of [011]-oriented epitaxial PTO [25] for which monoclinic phase has been shown to be favored under in-plane biaxial compression, for [111]-PTO theoretical studies [22-24] have predicted that rhombohedral is in general the most favorable state while monoclinic phase is hard to be stabilized except for tensile strain or very small compressive strain. Here we report stabilization of coexisting monoclinic states in [111]-PTO epitaxial film coherently grown on a compressive substrate.

For coherent growth the in-plane lattice parameters are fully clamped by substrate, so large volume contraction $(\Delta V/V)$ of the unit cell [21] suggests effective presence of out-of-plane compressive stress. In this letter, we present main features of structure and properties of the monoclinic states stabilized in [111]-PTO coherently grown on STO [cf. Fig. 1(a)], based upon modified LGD phenomenological theory [20]. For [111]-oriented epitaxial films, two kinds of Cartesian reference frames may be used: one is the conventional one [see Fig. 1(b)] in which $x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}$ correspond to pseudo-cubic $[100]_{pc},[010]_{pc}$, and $[001]_{pc}$, respectively; the other is a new one [see Fig. 1(c)] formed by proper rotation of axes, where $x_{1}',x_{2}',x_{3}'$ correspond to $[1-10]_{pc},[11-2]_{pc}$, and $[111]_{pc}$, respectively. For [111]-PTO coherently grown on STO the in-plane biaxial misfit strain $(u_{1}'=u_{2}'=u_{m}')$ is fixed at -0.013 at room temperature, as estimated by extrapolating the experimentally reported lattice parameters for paraelectric cubic PTO [26] and STO [17].

In the pseudo-cubic reference frame, to determine phase stability of a stressed ferroelectric state the elastic Gibbs function $G(P_i, \sigma_j)$ [20,26] is usually used, where $P_i$ and $\sigma_j$ refer to polarization and stress components, respectively. We use coordinate transformation [22,27] to derive free energy function for [111]-oriented films. In the new system polarization and stress are written as $P_i'$ and $\sigma_j'$. For ultrathin [111]-PTO the source of vertical compressive stress $\sigma_3'$ or $\sigma_\perp$ [Fig. 1(c)] might be Maxwell stress [28,29] due to the polar surfaces. Depending on the electrostatic conditions, $\sigma_3'$ is variable even though the in-plane compressive strain is fixed. After considering terms related to $\sigma_3'$ and performing Legendre transformation using $F(P_i, \sigma_j') = G(P_i, \sigma_j') + \sigma_1'u_1' + \sigma_2'u_2'$, the free energy function suitable for calculations of the stressed $(111)_{pc}$ epitaxial films is given below,

$$
\begin{aligned}
& F(P_{i}, u_{k}') \\
& \quad = a_{1}^{*}(P_{1}^{2}+P_{2}^{2}+P_{3}^{2}) \\
& \quad +A_{12}(P_{1}P_{2}+P_{1}P_{3}+P_{2}P_{3})+a_{11}^{*}(P_{1}^{4}+P_{2}^{4}+P_{3}^{4}) \\
& \quad +a_{12}^{*}(P_{1}^{2}P_{2}^{2}+P_{1}^{2}P_{3}^{2}+P_{2}^{2}P_{3}^{2}) \\
& \quad +A_{123}\left[P_{1}^{3}(P_{2}+P_{3})+P_{2}^{3}(P_{1}+P_{3})+P_{3}^{3}(P_{1}+P_{2})\right] \\
& \quad +A_{1223}(P_{1}P_{2}P_{3}^{2}+P_{1}^{3}P_{2}P_{3}+P_{1}P_{2}^{2}P_{3}) \\
& \quad +G_{6}+Bu_{m}^{*2}+C\sigma_{3}'^{2}. \quad \text{(1)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Modification of the free energy due to combined effect of $u_{m}'$ and $\sigma_3'$, and all the renormalized coefficients and materials constants are presented in the supplementary material. The equilibrium ferroelectric states are determined by minimizing $F$. As will be detailed below, substrate clamping and vertical stress break cubic symmetry of the parent phase and allow for low-symmetry monoclinic states in the [111]-oriented epitaxial perovskites.

For the [111]-PTO coherently grown on STO we find that polarization vectors for the monoclinic states are confined in a symmetry plane [see Figs. 1(b) and 1(c)], in agreement with the experiments [21]. To understand stable and metastable polarization states under out-of-plane compressive stress, we examine carefully energy surfaces (see Fig. 2) in the [111]-PTO films using contour plots [30]. $M_A$ and $M_B$ are two types of monoclinic structures [31] with $P_1^2 = P_2^2 < P_3^2$ and $P_1^2 = P_2^2 > P_3^2$, respectively. Superscripts I to IV in the notation refer to four combinations of signs of $P_1 = P_2$ and $P_3$. $M^I/M^{III}$ and $M^{II}/M^{IV}$ (see Figs. 1 and 2) are two pairs of equivalent monoclinic states, each with mutually anti-parallel $P_s$. The monoclinic $M_A$ and $M_B$ states correspond to primary and secondary minima, respectively. Initially rhombohedral $R^I$ and $R^{II}$ are two equivalent minima while paraelectric $p(P_1 = P_2 = P_3 = 0)$ is a maximum. R is the only stable phase below $-3.535$ GPa, above this threshold (Fig. S1, supplementary material) $M_A$ state becomes stable over R which now changes into a metastable state. At a critical stress of $-3.56$ GPa R phase finally disappears when $M_B$ state emerges. As driven by compressive stress $\sigma_3'$ potential wells for stable $(M_A)$ and metastable $(M_B)$ polarization states rotate about [1-10]-axis in opposite directions. Upon the occurrence of $M_B$ now rhombohedral states become saddle points.

The energy surfaces and stress driven flattening of potential wells are centro-symmetric. To measure flatness of the energy surfaces we quantified energy barriers (indicated by $\Delta$ in Fig. 3), which are shown to be reduced by the compressive stress for both stable and metastable polarization states, and rhombohedral to monoclinic phase transitions can be induced when the flatness reaches a critical level (Fig. S2, supplementary material).

The stress driven phase transitions via intermediate monoclinic states can be clearly seen in the polarization data presented in Fig. 4. The polarization path is $R^I$-$M_A^I$-$M_A^{II}$-$p$ [Fig. 4(a)]. With increasing $\sigma_3'$, monoclinic distortion becomes stabilized at a critical stress of $\sigma_3' = -3.535$ GPa when potential well for the rhombohedral state becomes rather shallow. Further increasing stress leads to continuous rotation of polarization from [111] towards in-plane [-1-12] direction. $P_{[111]}$ (or $P_3'$) is $\sim 0.001$ C/m² at $-5.28$ GPa and $\sim 0.0001$ C/m² at $-5.95$ GPa, i.e., for these stress levels the out-of-plane component is negligibly small and $P_s$ is approximately aligned in [-1-12] direction [see Fig. 4(b)]. Non-polar state $p$ appears when the in-plane polarization is reduced to zero at about $-6.29$ GPa.

The results discussed above suggest that it would probably be rather hard, if not impossible, for large volume strain, large shear, and large polarization to occur simultaneously in [111]-PTO with perfectly coherent epitaxy on compressive substrates such as STO. It should be noted that the experimental data of polarization, volume strain, and monoclinic shear reported in Ref. [21] were extracted from scanning transmission electron microscopic images using Gaussian fitting and geometric phase analysis, rather than from direct measurements such as dielectric hysteresis loop and x-ray diffraction. Our modeling of coherently strained [111]-PTO shows occurrence of monoclinic states with contraction in unit cell volume, in agreement with the experimental investigations [21]. However, our calculations show a polarization considerably smaller than $0.5$ C/m², and the monoclinic shear strain is much smaller than the measured value as well. Considering differences in the magnitudes of polarization and lattice shear strain, we assume that the reported coherently strained ultrathin [111]-PTO [21] might be subject to some mechanical and electrical boundary conditions not described in the present study, and that the monoclinic states predicted in this work should be distinct from that observed experimentally.

In Ref. [21] Tang et al. reported some modeling of the compressively strained [111]-PTO on STO using phase field simulations and first principle calculations. However, some clear discrepancies exist between their theoretical results and experimental data. Moreover, the underlying mechanism for stabilization of coexisting monoclinic states and their electrical properties remain unexplored.

Depolarization field [34,35] is inversely proportional to film thickness and also depends on screening of polarization charges at the surface or interface. In ultrathin PTO charge compensation on the [111] polar surfaces can be harder to accomplish than the case of [001]-PTO. However, depolarization field is expected to be reasonably small if the ferroelectric variants mentioned above (see Figs. 1 and 2) can form 180° stripe domains, similar to the case of ultrathin [001]-PTO [13]. In a separate paper [33] we will show that in the presence of depolarization field the critical stress for inducing rhombohedral-monoclinic transition can be remarkably lowered, though in general the calculated polarization and lattice strain are essentially of the similar order of magnitudes.

Although the in-plane strain is fixed, our studies [33] show that the in-plane stress components $\sigma_1'$ and $\sigma_2'$ (supplementary material) are functions of polarization and therefore vary with $\sigma_3'$. As shown by Figs. 5(b) and S4, even at zero $\sigma_3'$ the calculated in-plane stress is the order of $-4$ GPa, indicating large internal stress in the coherently strained PTO film. With the occurrence of induced monoclinic states the $\sigma_3'$ dependence of $\sigma_1'$ and $\sigma_2'$ appears to be complementary: for $M_A$ state the lattice relaxes in [1-10]-direction and tightens in [11-2]-direction while for $M_B$ state it generally behaves in the opposite way. Such feature may be advantageous for coexistence of the two types of monoclinic structures by reducing the entire elastic energy.

Clarification of the role of monoclinic phase may facilitate development of high-performance piezoelectric devices. Very recently, piezoresponse measurement and transmission electron microscopic studies of a single-phase [111]-PTO epitaxially grown on La₀.₇Sr₀.₃MnO₃ coated STO [36] directly confirmed the key mechanisms for superior piezoelectricity [1-11], i.e., polarization rotation, nano-domain reorientation, and electric-field-induced phase transition. Zou et al. [36] further pointed out that field-induced monoclinic to tetragonal ($M_A$-T) transition makes the biggest contribution to piezoresponse in the PTO film. Our present calculations show that $M_A$-T transition occurs in the [111]-PTO on STO at $\sigma_3'\sim-4.565$ GPa (Figs. 2, 3, and 4), and the piezoelectric coefficient $d_{33}'$ is anomalously enhanced with stress driven polarization rotation toward tetragonal state (Figs. 1, 2 and 4) and peaks at $\sigma_3'\sim-4.65$ GPa, where a T-like $M_A$ structure is favored (Figs. 4 and 5). Our theoretical findings are in good agreement with the experimental results of Zou et al. [36]. Additionally, it is noted that macroscopic hysteresis loop obtained by Zou et al. [36] showed a polarization of $\sim0.2$ C/m², close to our theoretical data for the monoclinic [111]-PTO (Figs. 4 and 5). It is noted that the induced rhombohedral-monoclinic transition and formation of T-like $M_A$ structure in the [111]-PTO film is analogous to strain-driven morphotropic phase boundary in the [001]-BiFeO₃ film [37].

In summary, we showed vertical compressive stress induced rhombohedral-monoclinic transition in strained [111]-PTO film and presented energy surfaces, lattice structure, and properties of various coexisting monoclinic states. Considerable enhancement in dielectric and piezoelectric responses occur as polarization rotates toward a tetragonal like $M_A$ state. The present theoretical studies will deepen our understanding of the emergent monoclinic phase in [111]-oriented epitaxial thin films and help realize intriguing phase structures with enhanced piezoelectricity in the forthcoming experiments.
