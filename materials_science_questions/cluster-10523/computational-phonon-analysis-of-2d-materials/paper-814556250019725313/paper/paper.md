# Cyclotron transition line-width due to interactions with the flexural wave of a phonon confined in a quantum well

Nam Lyong Kang*

Department of Nanomechatronics Engineering, Pusan National University, Miryang, Gyeongnam 627-706, Republic of Korea
*E-mail: nlkang@pusan.ac.kr
Received December 15, 2015; revised December 30, 2015; accepted January 3, 2016; published online February 10, 2016

The cyclotron transition line-width for a system of electrons interacting with the flexural wave of phonons confined in a quantum well structure of silicon was calculated using the optical conductivity formula derived by the projection-reduction method. Only a few confined phonons with low energy make a significant contribution to the line-width, which increases with increasing temperature. The well width and magnetic field dependence of the line-width are complicated and the flexural mode contributes to the line-width more strongly than the dilatational mode at low magnetic fields and for small well widths. © 2016 The Japan Society of Applied Physics

## 1. Introduction

Studies of electron–phonon interactions in semiconductor nanostructures have attracted considerable attention because they affect the mechanical and acoustic properties of those quantum structures and the limits of ultrasensitive detectors. $^{1–5)}$ The phonon eigenstates in these quantum structures are modified substantially by the quantum size effects and electron transport is greatly modified from that in bulk structures. $^{6–9)}$ Therefore, it is essential to consider the confinement of phonon modes to understand electron transport in those quantum structures. On the other hand, most theories presented thus far have neglected this phonon confinement $^{10–12)}$ and the confinement effects have been consideredonly in limited cases. $^{13–16)}$

Phonon-assisted cyclotron resonance (PACR) in a free standing quantum well structure is one of the most useful methods for examining the effects of electron-confined phonon interactions to electron transport in quantum structures. $^{13–16)}$ Most studies have focused on the additional peaks in the absorption spectrum but there has been less attention on the width of the resonance peak. $^{14,15)}$ The PACR half width was used as a parameter. A study of the PACR line- width is a powerful tool for examining the electronic struc- tures of electron systems in quantum well structures because the absorption line-shapes are quite sensitive to the type of scattering mechanism affecting the behavior of electrons. The effects of confined phonons on the line-width are dependent on the applied magnetic field and well width because the energy and number of phonons participating in the electron transition varies in a complicated manner. These confined phonons contribute to the line-width selectively by the energy and momentum conservations.

On the other hand, it is important to understand how the Fermi distribution functions for electrons and the Planck distribution functions for phonons are included in the formula because the shape of the optical conductivity is affected by the distribution functions. This study calculated the cyclotron transition line-width in silicon by the interaction with the flexural wave of phonon confined in an infinite square well using the optical conductivity formula derived by the projection reduction (PR) method. $^{17)}$ Although the dilatational mode among the three types of confined phonons (shear, dilatational, and flexural modes) makes a dominant contribution to the half-width in the extreme quantum limit, $^{14)}$ this paper considers the flexural mode interaction in a low magnetic field. The formula includes the distribution functions in multiplicative forms, which is physically reasonable because the electrons and phonons belong to different categories in a quantum-statistical classification. Therefore, the phonon absorption and emission processes, as well as the photon absorption and emission processes in all electron transitions are interpreted in an organized manner and intuition for the quantum dynamics of electron transition in solids can be obtained. The temperature, well width and magnetic field dependence of the line-width were obtained and the results were compared with those obtained by an interaction with the dilatational wave.

## 2. Review of theory

When an electromagnetic wave of frequency $\omega$ circularly polarized in the $xy$ plane is applied to an electron system interacting with a confined acoustic phonon through the deformation potential in an infinite square well, the linear optical conductivity can be derived using the PR method under a static magnetic field $\boldsymbol{B}$ applied perpendicularly to the well interfaces located in the $xy$ plane. Considering the Lorentzian approximation for a weak interaction, the real partof the conductivity, $\sigma_{+-}(\omega)$ , is given as $^{17)}$

$$
\operatorname{Re}\left\{\sigma_{+-}(\omega)\right\}=\frac{1}{\omega} \sum_{\alpha}\left|j_{\alpha}^{+}\right|^{2} \frac{\left(f_{\alpha}-f_{\alpha+1}\right) \gamma_{\alpha}(\overline{\omega})}{\left[\hbar\left(\overline{\omega}-\omega_{\mathrm{c}}\right)\right]^{2}+\left[\gamma_{\alpha}(\overline{\omega})\right]^{2}}. \quad (1)
$$

Here, Re denotes "the real part of", $j_{\alpha}^{+}=-i e[2(N_{\alpha}+1)\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}/$ $m_{\mathrm{t}}]^{1/2}$, $N_{\alpha}=0,1,2,...$ is the Landau level index, $\overline{\omega}=\omega-$ $i a(a \to 0^{+}), \omega_{\mathrm{c}}=e B/m_{\mathrm{t}}$ is the cyclotron frequency, $m_{\mathrm{t}}$ is the transverse mass of the electron, and $f_{\alpha}$ is the Fermi distribution function for an electron with energy $E_{\alpha}$, which is given as

$$
E_{\alpha}=\varepsilon_{N_{\alpha}}+E_{n_{\alpha}}=\left(N_{\alpha}+1/2\right)\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}+n_{\alpha}^{2}E_{0}, \quad (2)
$$

where $n_{\alpha}=1,2,3,...$ is the electric subband quantum number, $E_{0}=\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}/2m_{\mathrm{l}}L_{z}^{2}$, $L_{z}$ is the width of the well, and $m_{\mathrm{l}}$ is the longitudinal mass of the electron. The electron- confined phonon interaction matrix element, $C_{\alpha,\beta}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\parallel})$ , isgiven as $^{13,14)}$

$$
\begin{array}{r}
C_{\alpha,\beta}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\parallel})=V_{\mathrm{f}}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\parallel},n,0)J_{N_{\alpha},N_{\beta}}(x_{\mathrm{c}\alpha},q_{x},x_{\mathrm{c}\beta})I_{n_{\alpha},n_{\beta}}(q_{\mathrm{l},n})\delta_{k_{y\beta},k_{y\alpha}-q_{y}}, \\
(3)
\end{array}
$$

where $q_{\parallel}=\sqrt{q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2}}$ is the component of the phonon wave vector $\boldsymbol{q}$ for the direction parallel to the well interface, $k_{y\alpha}$ is the $y$-component of the electron wave vector, $x_{\mathrm{c}\alpha}=-\hbar k_{y\alpha}/eB$,

and for flexural waves

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{f}}\left(\mathbf{q}_{\|}, n, z\right)= & F_{1, n} \sqrt{\frac{\hbar E_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}{2 A \rho_{\mathrm{m}} \omega_{n}\left(q_{\|}\right)}}\left(q_{\mathrm{t}, n}^{2}-q_{x}^{2}\right)\left(q_{\mathrm{l}, n}^{2}+q_{x}^{2}\right) \\
& \times \cos \left(\frac{q_{\mathrm{t}, n} L_{z}}{2}\right) \sin \left(q_{\mathrm{l}, n} z\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

$$
I_{n_{\alpha}, n_{\beta}}\left(q_{\mathrm{l}, n}\right)=\int_{-L_{z} / 2}^{L_{z} / 2} \chi_{n_{\alpha}}(z) \sin \left(q_{\mathrm{l}, n} z\right) \chi_{n_{\beta}}(z) d z,
\tag{5}
$$

where $F_{1, n}$ is the normalization constant for the flexural waves (see Appendix in Ref. 13), $E_{\mathrm{d}}$ is the deformation potential constant, $A$ is the interface area of the sample, $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}$ is the mass density, $\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})$ are the energies of the flexural waves given as $\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})=\hbar s_{1} \sqrt{q_{x}^{2}+q_{\mathrm{l}, n}^{2}}$, $\chi_{n_{\alpha}}(z)=$ $\sqrt{2 / L_{z}} \sin (n_{\alpha} \pi z / L_{z}+n_{\alpha} \pi / 2)$, and $J_{N_{\alpha}, N_{\beta}}(x_{c \alpha}, q_{x}, x_{c \beta})$ is given in Ref. 17. The parameters $q_{\mathrm{l}, n}$ and $q_{\mathrm{t}, n}$ are the solutions of two algebraic equations$^{13,14)}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\tan \left(q_{\mathrm{l}} L_{z} / 2\right)}{\tan \left(q_{\mathrm{t}} L_{z} / 2\right)} & =-\frac{4 q_{\|}^{2} q_{\mathrm{l}} q_{\mathrm{t}}}{\left(q_{\|}^{2}-q_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}\right)^{2}}, \\
s_{\mathrm{l}}^{2}\left(q_{\|}^{2}+q_{\mathrm{l}}^{2}\right) & =s_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}\left(q_{\|}^{2}+q_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

where $s_{\mathrm{l}}(s_{\mathrm{t}})$ is the speed of the longitudinal (transverse) acoustic wave and the additional index $n$ denotes the different branches.

In Eq. (1), the line-with function $\gamma_{\alpha}(\bar{\omega})$, which is the imaginary part of the line-shape function $\Gamma_{\alpha}(\bar{\omega})$ in Ref. 17, is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \gamma_{\alpha}(\bar{\omega})\left(f_{\alpha}-f_{\alpha+1}\right) \\
& =\sum_{q_{\|}, n} \sum_{\beta}\left[T^{(-)}(\alpha, \beta)+T^{(+)}(\alpha, \beta)\right] C_{\alpha+1, \beta}\left(-q_{\|}\right) \\
& \quad \times\left\{C_{\beta, \alpha+1}\left(q_{\|}\right)-C_{\beta-1, \alpha}\left(q_{\|}\right) j_{\beta-1}^{+} / j_{\alpha}^{+}\right\} \\
& \quad+\sum_{q_{\|}, n} \sum_{\beta}\left[T^{(-)}(\beta, \alpha+1)+T^{(+)}(\beta, \alpha+1)\right] C_{\beta, \alpha}\left(-q_{\|}\right) \\
& \quad \times\left\{C_{\alpha, \beta}\left(q_{\|}\right)-C_{\alpha+1, \beta+1}\left(q_{\|}\right) j_{\beta}^{+} / j_{\alpha}^{+}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

Here, $\alpha \equiv(N_{\alpha}, n_{\alpha})$, $\alpha+1 \equiv(N_{\alpha}+1, n_{\alpha})$ and transition factors $T^{( \pm)}(\alpha, \beta)$ are defined as

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{(-)}(\alpha, \beta) \equiv & {\left[\left\{1+N_{n}(q_{\|})\right\} f_{\alpha}\left(1-f_{\beta}\right)-N_{n}(q_{\|}) f_{\beta}\left(1-f_{\alpha}\right)\right] } \\
& \times \delta\left[\hbar \omega-E_{\beta}+E_{\alpha}-\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{(+)}(\alpha, \beta) \equiv & {\left[N_{n}(q_{\|}) f_{\alpha}\left(1-f_{\beta}\right)-\left\{1+N_{n}(q_{\|})\right\} f_{\beta}\left(1-f_{\alpha}\right)\right] } \\
& \times \delta\left[\hbar \omega-E_{\beta}+E_{\alpha}+\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

where $N_{n}(q_{\|})$ is the Planck distribution function for a phonon with energy $\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})$. $T^{(-)}(\alpha, \beta)[T^{(+)}(\alpha, \beta)]$ corresponds to a transition from an $\alpha$ state to a $\beta$ state with phonon emission [absorption] minus a transition from the $\beta$ state to the $\alpha$ state with phonon absorption [emission]. $\delta(x)$ denotes the Dirac delta function, through which the energy conservation is maintained, i.e., $\hbar \omega+E_{\alpha}=E_{\beta}+\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})$ for $T^{(-)}(\alpha, \beta)$ and $E_{\beta}=E_{\alpha}+\hbar \omega+\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})$ for $T^{(+)}(\alpha, \beta)$.

Equation (7) means that when an electron-phonon interaction is involved in an electron transition, a transition from the initial state $\alpha$ to the final state $\alpha+1$ occurs via implicit transitions between the initial (or final) states and implicit states induced by local fluctuations. The states given by $\beta$ are called implicit states because they are included only in the line-width function [Eq. (7)], not in the conductivity [Eq. (1)]. A net transition is possible because the energies of the implicit states are determined by the energies of the initial state $(E_{\alpha})$ or final state $(E_{\alpha+1})$, photon energy $(\hbar \omega)$, and phonon energy [$\hbar \omega_{n}(q_{\|})$]. Although the implicit transitions are not measured directly, they should be considered in the calculations. Therefore, from Eq. (7), all electron transitions including the phonon absorption (emission) processes and photon absorption (emission) processes can be interpreted from a fully microscopic point of view and were represented in a diagram (see Ref. 17 for details).

## 3. Numerical result

The cyclotron transition line-widths in silicon by an interaction with the flexural waves of phonon confined in an infinite square well were calculated numerically for $N_{\alpha}=0$ in the quantum limit. Only the cases for $N_{\beta}=0$ and $N_{\beta}=1$ needed to be considered for the implicit transition terms appearing in Eq. (7). In this case, the $n_{\beta}=n_{\alpha}$ terms were neglected because $E_{\beta}-E_{\alpha}$ and $E_{\alpha+1}-E_{\beta}$ are 0 or $\hbar \omega_{\mathrm{c}} \approx \hbar \omega$. Therefore, only the terms, $n_{\alpha}=1$, $n_{\beta}=2$, and $n_{\alpha}=2$, $n_{\beta}=3$ were considered in the quantum limit. Figure 1 shows the temperature dependence of the half-width in silicon for $B=4$ T, $L_{z}=$ 31 nm and for the deformation constants of $E_{\mathrm{d}}=6,7$, and 8 eV. The half-width increased with increasing $E_{\mathrm{d}}$ according to Eq. (4). The effects of phonon scattering increased with increasing temperature because the number of phonons affecting the electron relaxation increases with increasing temperature. Therefore, the half-width increases with increasing temperature.

The well width dependence of the half-width were complicated (Fig. 2). The half-width by an interaction with the flexural mode showed a maximum near $L_{z}=32$ nm, whereas that by an interaction with the dilatational mode had a maximum at a relatively large well width. The selection rule for $n_{\alpha}$ given in Eq. (2) was $\Delta n_{\alpha}=1$ for the flexural mode by Eq. (5), whereas it was $\Delta n_{\alpha}=2$ for the dilatational mode by Eq. (19) in Ref. 17. On the other hand, the energies of phonons affecting the half-width were determined by the confined phonon wave vectors, which are given by Eq. (6), and by energy conservation, Eqs. (8) and (9). This can be interpreted as being caused by the symmetries of the wave function and electron-phonon coupling factor, i.e., the coupling factor for the flexural mode, $V_{\mathrm{f}}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\|}, n, z)$, given by Eq. (4), is an antisymmetric function of the $z$-coordinate, but that for the dilatational mode, $V_{\mathrm{d}}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\|}, n, z)$, given by Eq. (3) in Ref. 17, is a symmetric one. Therefore, the flexural phonons affect the electron transitions between the two states with the opposite symmetry, whereas the dilatational phonons affect the electron transitions between the two states with the same symmetry. As a result, the $\Delta n_{\alpha}=1$ term for scattering by the flexural modes and $\Delta n_{\alpha}=2$ term for scattering by the dilatational modes contribute mainly to the half-width. The abrupt increase at $L_{z}=31$ nm [point (b) in Fig. 2] can be interpreted as follows. At point (a) for $L_{z}=$ 30 nm, no phonons with an energy of $\hbar \omega_{\mathrm{c}}-3 E_{0}$ contribute to the half-width (see Fig. 3), whereas at point (b) for $L_{z}=$ 31 nm, two phonons (A and B in Fig. 3) with energy of $\hbar \omega_{\mathrm{c}}-8 E_{0}$ contribute to the line-width, i.e., the confined phonon with wave vectors given by Eq. (6) contribute to the half-widths selectively by the energy and momentum con- servations. Note that the half-width increases with increasing the number of phonons.

The half-widths increase with increasing magnetic field in the bulk structures because the spacing between the two Landau-levels increases with increasing magnetic field, hence, the phonons with a high energy contribute to the half- widths. On the other hand, the magnetic field dependence of the half-width in the two-dimensional structure are compli- cated (Fig. 4) for the same reason as that for the well width dependence. The dilatational modes contribute mainly to the half-width in the quantum limit. $^{14)}$ Figure 4 shows that the dilatational mode contributes mainly to the half-width at a high magnetic field, but the effect of the flexural mode is not negligible at a relatively low magnetic field. The Landau level separation decreases with decreasing magnetic field and the flexural phonons contribute to the half-width by the selec- tion rule, $\Delta n_{\alpha}=1$, which corresponds to the small electric subband separation compared to $\Delta n_{\alpha}=2$ for the dilatational mode. Therefore, the flexural phonons with an energy of $\hbar \omega_{\mathrm{c}}-3 E_{0}$ contribute strongly to the half-width at a low magnetic field.

### 4. Conclusions
Thus far, the cyclotron transition line-widths in silicon by an interaction with the flexural waves of phonons confined in a square well were calculated using the optical conductivity formula derived by the projection-reduction method. Only a few confined phonons with low energy contribute signifi- cantly to the line-widths, and the cyclotron transition line- width by an interaction with the flexural mode in silicon increased with increasing temperature, which is similar to that by dilatational mode. In addition, the dilatational modes

contributed mainly to the half-widths at a high magnetic field and for a large well width but the flexural modes contributed to the half-widths at a low magnetic field and for a small well width. This was interpreted as being caused by the symmetries of the wave function and the electron–phonon interaction factor. It is expected that the present method will be applied to electron systems interacting with confined acoustic phonon in quantum wires subjected to a magnetic field and some other electron transition phenomena in low electron systems. $^{18–20)}$

Recently, the electron spin dynamics in low dimensional structures have attracted considerable attention. $^{21–23)}$ Impor tantly, the spin relaxation mechanism in these quantum struc- tures needs to be understand before spin-based electronic and optoelectronic devices can be realized. The relaxation of electron spin in the quantum structures can be explained using the method used in this paper for the quantized wave vectors of the phonons confined in the quantum structures for a system of electrons interacting with phonons through a phonon-modulated spin–orbit interaction. $^{24,25)}$ This will be studied in the future using the present projection-reduction method.
