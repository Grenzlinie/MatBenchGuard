**Keywords:** Raman scattering, porous SiC, epitaxial SiC, optic phonons, plasmons

**Abstract.** A lightly doped n-type homo-epitaxial layer was grown by CVD onto a heavily doped n-type 4H-SiC substrate for which half of the surface had been made porous by photoelectrochemical etching. Raman spectra are obtained in the optic phonon region using three scattering geometries. An effective medium model for the porous layer is used to assist in the interpretation of the spectra. This work demonstrates that the contributions to the Raman spectra of the various layers in a sample with multiple 4H-SiC layers can be extracted.

## Introduction
The Raman spectra of several polytypes of SiC are well known [1,2]. Raman spectra for etched porous layers of 6H-SiC have also been reported [3]. In this work, we extract the contributions of the various layers of a relatively complex sample consisting of a lightly doped 4H-SiC epilayer deposited onto a heavily n-type doped 4H-SiC substrate, which had previously been made porous over half of the growth surface. The analysis is based on Raman spectra obtained using three distinct scattering geometries.

## Experimental Details
A rectangular piece of substrate material was cut from a 370 µm thick n-type 4H-SiC wafer doped at $7.5×10^{18}\ \text{cm}^{-3}$. The c-axis is tilted 8 degrees towards the [11-20] direction with respect to the surface normal. Using masking, one side ($\sim20\ \text{mm}^2$) of the substrate surface was photoelectrochemically etched at 3V to produce an approximately 10 µm thick porous layer with triangular morphology [4] and an average porosity of 12%. Afterwards, an approximately 12 µm thick n-type epilayer lightly doped at $1.7×10^{15}\ \text{cm}^{-3}$ was deposited using chemical vapor deposition (CVD) in a hot wall reactor.

Figure 1. Two of the scattering geometries employed.

For the Raman scattering, the 250 mW incident laser beam at 514.5 nm wavelength was polarized perpendicular to the plane of incidence (i.e. s-polarization). All polarizations were collected in the scattered light. Two of the Raman scattering geometries (Fig. 1) were designed to separately probe the optic phonons (wavevector $\mathbf{q}$) which propagate parallel and perpendicular to the c-axis. In the case of the axial (backscattering) geometry, it was assumed that the collected scattered light originates throughout the entire internal path of the beam, possibly attenuated by the porous layer. For the planar geometry the projected image of the laser beam was oriented normal to the spectrometer slit to provide a degree of spatial resolution

Figure 2. Geometry for collecting spatially resolved layer spectra.

so that spectra of the layered surface regions could be collected independently of the bulk substrate. The third scattering geometry (Fig. 2) was used to obtain spatially resolved spectra from the three regions of the sample via one of its polished edges. In the latter two geometries the laser beam was focused to a spot diameter of $10\ \mu\text{m}$ and a low power microscope objective was used as the collection optics. All spectra were recorded using a Spex Model 1401 dual-grating spectrometer with a slit width of $100\ \mu\text{m}$. The Raman shifts were determined using calibration lines in the cold-cathode emission spectrum of Ne and are considered to be accurate within $\pm 0.5\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. The relative intensities are estimated to be accurate within $\pm 25\%$ when comparing spectra for the same scattering geometry.

## Effective Medium Model
If the optical wavelength greatly exceeds the size of the inclusions in an inhomogeneous medium, the quasi-static approximation applies and the optical properties can be described by an effective dielectric function. While Rayleigh scattering of visible light is observed for porous SiC, effective medium models have been used to interpret Raman spectra in other porous semiconductors [5]. We choose the Maxwell Garnett model [6] because we have previously used it successfully to discuss the infrared reflectance of porous SiC in the Reststrahl region [7]. Other effective medium models such as the Bruggeman model were not successful. Also, we have recently found some success [8] in describing the acoustic properties of porous SiC using an analog of the Maxwell Garnett model. In this case, the analog to the Bruggeman model is a complete failure.

![](./images/811868043054415874_3.jpg)

Figure 3. TO spectra: (a), (b) - axial; (c), (d) - planar; (e), (f) - spatially resolved layer-edge spectra.

For this work, the Maxwell Garnett model is generalized to include the uniaxial symmetry of 4H-SiC. The inclusions (pores) are assumed to be aligned ellipsoids of revolution coaxial with the 4H-SiC c-axis, so that the symmetry of the effective dielectric tensor is also unixial.

![](./images/811868043054415874_4.jpg)

Both the shape of the ellipsoids and the anisotropy of the dielectric function of 4H-SiC are taken into account in the axial depolarization factor $L_{\parallel}$ [9]. "Oblate" ellipsoids are cigar shaped, whereas "prolate" ellipsoids are disk-like. The Raman frequencies associated with the strongest $A_{1}$ axial and $E_{1}$ planar modes are obtained following the method of Loudon [10]. The parameters of the oscillator model for 4H-SiC are taken from Harima et al. [2]. There are four characteristic frequencies describing the behavior of bulk 4H-SiC in the Reststrahl region: axial ("parallel") and planar ("perpendicular") TO and LO frequencies. The effective medium model introduces new oscillator parameters specified by primed axial and planar TO and LO frequencies (called Frölich modes). The primed LO frequency is less than the corresponding primed TO frequency. The porosity chosen for the calculations is 12%.

## Discussion
Assuming that the porous layer is not affected by the epitaxial growth process, we use the effective medium model to assist in the interpretation of the spectra. Fig. 5 shows the dependence of the predicted frequencies in the porous layer on the axial depolarization factor for the axial geometry, for comparison with the spectra shown in Figs. 3(a) and 4(a). The transverse planar modes are both two-fold degenerate. Three peaks appear in the TO region spectrum with the porous layer present (Fig. 3(a)), which are not observed in its absence (Fig. 3(b)). The peak at $798\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is assigned to the bulk planar TO mode in agreement with the model. Although not predicted, the axial TO mode at $784\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is also observed, possibly due to a breakdown of selection rules due to Rayleigh scattering [5]. The broad, weak peak at $792\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ can only be speculatively (and not convincingly) assigned to the Fröhlich LO axial mode (frequency $\omega'_{\text{L}\parallel}$ on Fig. 5) for axial depolarization factor $L_{\parallel}$~1. In the LO region, the sharp peak at $964\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (Fig. 4(b)) is assigned to the axial LO mode in the epitaxial layer [1]. The broad peak (Fig. 4(b)) is the LO phonon-plasmon coupled mode in the heavily doped substrate [1,2]. This feature is absent in Fig. 4(a) probably because the porous layer is attenuating the Raman signal from the heavily doped bulk substrate. The axial LO peak observed in Fig. 4(a) might include a contribution from the porous layer, since it is allowed in the model. The position of the second peak at $969\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (Fig. 4(a)) is beyond the planar LO frequency of $966.4\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ [2]. Also, the planar LO mode is forbidden in the axial geometry. The only possibilities for assignment are the bulk planar LO frequency or the bulk planar LO frequency of the Fröhlich TO frequency for $L_{\parallel}$~1.

Fig. 6 shows the variation of the calculated Raman frequencies of porous 4H SiC for phonon propagation perpendicular to the c-axis, which approximates the geometry for the layer spectra shown in Figs. 3(f) and 4(g). In agreement with the model, both the planar and axial transverse frequencies (798 and $783\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, respectively [2]) are observed in the TO spectrum (Fig. 3(f)). Although these modes are also observed for the epilayer (Fig. 3(e)), they are stronger for the porous layer and their relative

## Conclusion
We have analyzed Raman spectra of a complex three-layer sample based on spectra obtained using three different scattering geometries, and associated spectral features with the epilayer, porous layer and heavily doped substrate. We have not convincingly observed the additional Fröhlich modes predicted by the effective medium model for the porous layer. It is possible that the extra (forbidden) lines are observed via breaking of selection rules due to Rayleigh scattering in the porous layer.
