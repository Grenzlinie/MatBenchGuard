# Influences of $B$-site Cations on Intrinsic Dielectric Properties of $\mathrm{Ba}(B'_{1/3}B''_{2/3})\mathrm{O}_3$ Materials

JIAN-QING DAI,* YU-MIN SONG, AND HU ZHANG

School of Materials Science and Engineering, Kunming University of Science and Technology, Kunming 650093, P. R. China

Effects of B-site cations on polar phonon frequencies, mode effective charges, and lattice dielectric response of 1:2 ordered $\mathrm{Ba(Mg_{1/3}Ta_{2/3})O_3}$, $\mathrm{Ba(Mg_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3}$, and $\mathrm{Ba(Zn_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3}$ microwave dielectrics are studied using first-principles calculations. The calculated optical phonon frequencies and the average dielectric constants are found to be in excellent agreement with experiments. Replacing Ta (Mg) with Nb (Zn) is found to significantly affect frequency, oscillator strength, and mode effective charges of the dominant polar phonons. Dielectric contributions from O anions are dominant and strongly B-site dependent. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the greater the dielectric contribution from O anions, the smaller the measured microwave $Q\times f$-factor.

Keywords $\mathrm{Ba(B'_{1/3}B''_{2/3})O_3}$; intrinsic dielectric response' first principle

## 1. Introduction

Dielectric resonators and filters are key components in various communication systems at the microwave frequencies [1]. The 1:2 ordered complex perovskites $\mathrm{Ba}(B'_{1/3}B''_{2/3})\mathrm{O}_3$ ($B'=\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$, $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$; $B''=\mathrm{Ta}^{5+}$, $\mathrm{Nb}^{5+}$), which exhibit an excellent combination of high quality factor $Q$, moderate dielectric constant $\varepsilon$, and near zero temperature coefficient of resonant frequency $\tau_f$ at microwave frequencies, have been used in manufacturing dielectric resonators in the wireless communication system [2]. The relationship among their structures, crystal chemistry, and dielectric properties has been the subject of many experimental investigations. Though much has been learnt, detailed microscopic mechanisms determining their intrinsic dielectric properties still remain to be revealed [1, 2].

As we know, dielectric properties at microwave frequencies are mainly determined by lattice vibrations, and it is necessary to understand the underlying lattice dynamics and their dielectric contributions. Though infrared and Raman spectroscopy are the two main experimental methods to investigate the optical phonons and the lattice dielectric response, exact assignment of the far-infrared spectra is very difficult since the peaks are significantly broad and seriously overlapped [3]. Furthermore, even the far-infrared spectra could be assigned, the phonon eigenvector and the mode effective charge, which are essential for understanding the intrinsic dielectric properties, are impossible to obtain from the spectra alone. First-principle classification of phonon properties is ideally suited for investigating the microscopic origin of intrinsic dielectric response of systems with

complex structures where experimental determination of phonon properties is difficult [4]. Up to now, however, most of the reported theoretical works have mainly focused on the formation and stability of the 1:2 ordered structures [4-9]. Only in the recent report [10], the lattice vibrations and dielectric properties of $Ba(Mg_{1/3}Ta_{2/3})O_3$ and $Ba(Mg_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3$ are successfully determined via the first-principles calculation.

In this paper, we present a comparative study of intrinsic dielectric response for three related $Ba(B'_{1/3}B''_{2/3})O_3$ complex perovskites $Ba(Mg_{1/3}Ta_{2/3})O_3$, $Ba(Mg_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3$, and $Ba(Zn_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3$ (labeled as BMT, BMN, and BZN, respectively), computed from first principles using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT). These compounds have been chosen both because of their scientific and technological importance, and because they allow us to investigate the effects on the lattice dynamics and dielectric response of substituting one $B$-site cation while remaining the other ions unchanged.

## 2. Methodology
Our first-principle DFPT calculations were performed within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [11] as implemented in the Vienna $ab$ initio simulation package (VASP) [12, 13]. The plane wave functions were expanded up to a kinetic energy cutoff of 500 eV. We use the projector-augmented wave (PAW) method [14] with the following valence electron configurations: $5s^25p^66s^2$ (Ba), $2p^63s^2$ (Mg), $3d^{10}4s^2$ (Zn), $5p^65d^36s^2$ (Ta), $4s^24p^64d^45s^1$ (Nb), and $2s^22p^4$ (O). The lattice parameters and ionic internal coordinates of 1:2 ordered BMT, BMN, and BZN were relaxed with fixed cell volumes obtained from experiments [15] and the residual Hellmann-Feynman forces are less than 1 meV/Å. When calculating the phonon frequencies, the setting of the maximum force is 0.01 meV/Å. Brillouin-zone integrations were performed with a $5\times5\times5$ $\Gamma$-centered Monkhorst-Pack $\mathbf{k}$-point mesh. Zone-center optical phonon frequencies, eigendisplacements, and intrinsic dielectric responses (electronic and lattice dielectric contributions) were determined using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) [16, 17].

## 3. Results and Discussion
The ordered $Ba(B'_{1/3}B''_{2/3})O_3$ structure, where $B'$ and $B''$ cations occur on alternating (111) cubic perovskite planes in a 1:2 ratio, belongs to the trigonal space group ($P\overline{3}m1$) with 15 atoms in the primitive cell. The Wyckoff positions (brackets) and atomic fractional coordinates (parenthesis) are Ba1 [1$a$] (0, 0, 0), Ba2 [2$d$] (1/3, 2/3, $z$), $B'$ [1$b$] (0, 0, 1/2), $B''$ [2$d$] (1/3, 2/3, $z$), O1 [6$i$] ($x$, $2x$, $z$), and O2 [3$e$] (1/2, 0, 0), respectively. The resulting ionic internal coordinates and selected inter-atomic distances, as reported in Table 1, are in quite satisfactory agreement with their experimental values [15]. It is the asymmetry of the bond network that results in two differently coordinated oxygen anions where O2 bonds to two $B''^{5+}$ cations while O1 bonded to one $B'^{2+}$ and one $B''^{5+}$. The three short $B''$-O1 and three long $B''$-O2 bond distances demonstrate an out-of-center distortion of $B''$ in $[B''O_6]$ octahedra, for which the shift of $B''$ occurs toward the face of the $[B''O_6]$ octahedra with neighboring layer of $[B'O_6]$. In addition, the size of $[B'O_6]$ octahedron is markedly larger than that of $[B''O_6]$. According to results by Bellaiche $et$ $al$, [5] the main driving mechanism responsible for the 1:2 long-range-ordered structure along the [111] direction occurring in BMT, BMN and BZN is simply the electrostatic interaction between the $B'$ and $B''$ species.

The experimental dielectric constants and $Q \times f$-products are also given in Table 2. Clearly, first principle calculations do a satisfactory job in predicting the average dielectric constants. All the calculated average dielectric constants are in good agreement with their experimental values for ceramic samples [15]. Our PBE pseudopotential approach overestimates the average dielectric constants to some extent, with the same situation as in LDA [4, 18]. The electronic dielectric tensor elements of $\varepsilon_\infty$ has the values of $\sim$5, which is well consistent with experimental and theoretical results for other perovskites [19, 20]. Finally, there exists a clearly relationship between the magnitude of lattice dielectric response and the experimental $Q \times f$ values, e.g., the greater the lattice dielectric response, the smaller the measured $Q \times f$-product.

To elucidate the microscopic origin of lattice dielectric response, we determine the IR-active phonon frequencies and their dielectric contributions as listed in Table 3. We also calculate the frequencies of the Raman-active modes and list them, as well as the proposed assignments of the experimentally observed modes, [21, 22] in Table 4. The overall agreements with experiments [23, 24] and previous theoretical results [10] are very satisfactory, expect for some modes are apparently missing in the measurements. It should be mentioned that the error between our calculated and the measured $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ phonon frequency with largest oscillator strength in BMT and BZN, $E_{\mathrm{u}}(6)$ and $E_{\mathrm{u}}(7)$ in Table 3, respectively, is only about $1 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. We believe that some of the missing IR modes in the experiments are mainly due to their immeasurably small oscillator strengths, such as $A_{2 \mathrm{u}}(1)$ in BMT, and $A_{2 \mathrm{u}}(3)$ and $E_{\mathrm{u}}(3)$ in BZN. While the missing for the remainder is owning to overlapping with their neighbor phonon so that the pair of modes appears together as a single peak in the optical spectra, as likely in the case of $E_{\mathrm{u}}(4), E_{\mathrm{u}}(8)$ and $A_{2 \mathrm{u}}(7)$ for BMT.

On going to BMN from the BMT material, in which Ta was replaced by Nb, the frequencies of some $A_{2 \mathrm{u}}$ modes decrease while those of the other $A_{2 \mathrm{u}}$ modes increase, whereas most of the $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ modes exhibit downward shifts in their frequencies. It can be also seen that the degree of reduction in frequency is generally larger than that of increment. Given that the BMT and BMN possess similar structural and electrical properties, one may expect that the change in frequencies originates mainly from inertial effect. However, the masses of Ta and Nb are 180.9 and 92.9 amu, respectively; on this basis alone, most of the frequencies should increase in BMN instead of decrease. Indeed, the reversed downward shift in frequencies is associated with an appreciable softening of the force constants matrix. Evidently this softening is connected with chemical differences between these two atoms, e.g., the $4 d$ shell present in Nd while $5 d$ shell in Ta.

We consider next the influences of substitution of $\mathrm{Zn}$ for $\mathrm{Mg}$, as occurred in the BMN and BZN systems. It is obviously that both the $A_{2 \mathrm{u}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ modes are dramatically softened in BZN. We believe that this phenomenon is due to both inertial and chemical effects. As mentioned above, the larger mass of $\mathrm{Zn}$ (65.4 amu) compared with that of $\mathrm{Mg}$ (24.3 amu) will in favor of decreasing the frequencies. According to the first-principles results by He et al., [25] in addition to the mass effect, the conspicuous reduction in frequency of the $135 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ mode in $\mathrm{CaCu}_{3} \mathrm{Ti}_{4} \mathrm{O}_{12}$ to $72 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ in $\mathrm{CdCu}_{3} \mathrm{Ti}_{4} \mathrm{O}_{12}$ also originates significantly from chemical effect. It is this chemical effect that is connected with different underlying atomic shell structure of $\mathrm{Cd}$ and $\mathrm{Ca}$, e.g., the $4 d$ shell present in $\mathrm{Cd}$ but absent in $\mathrm{Ca}$. The extraordinary similar situation to our case, where $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Zn}} / \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Mg}}$ is $2.7\left(\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Cd}} / \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Ca}}=2.8\right)$ and $\mathrm{Zn}$ has the filled $3 d$ shell absent in $\mathrm{Mg}$, lends support to the same conclusion.

Next, we indentify dielectric contributions from the dominant IR phonons. Particular focus firstly is directed toward revealing behavior of the $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ modes. Obviously, the major dielectric contributions are responsible for the existence of $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ (6), $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ (7), and $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ (8) modes. The summation of these three oscillator strengths accounts for above $84 \%$ of the total dielectric element $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ph}, 11}$. Let us show their behavior one by one: Though the $E_{\mathrm{u}}(6)$ mode gives dominant contribution to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ph}, 11}$ in BMT and BMN, its oscillator strength drops dramatically in BZN compound. For $E_{\mathrm{u}}$ (7) mode, its contribution to lattice dielectric response is neglectable small both in BMT and BMN, but the most intriguing facet for this mode is that it exhibits tremendous large oscillator strength in BZT. In case of $E_{\mathrm{u}}(8)$, the oscillator strength increases significantly as Ta replaced by $\mathrm{Nb}$, while its value shows remarkably decrement when substituting $\mathrm{Mg}$ for $\mathrm{Zn}$ in BZN. As we know, the oscillator strength of one polar mode is determined by both the frequency and its mode effective charge. Apparently, only the difference in frequency can not account for the mentioned behavior of dielectric contributions in the three related materials.

Further insight about their lattice dielectric responses can be obtained by inspecting Table 5, which affords their mode effective charges $Z^{*}_{\lambda,\alpha}$ and decomposes them into contributions from individual ions [26]. The term atomic mode effective charge $Z^{*k}_{\lambda,\alpha}$, the product of the Born effective charge tensor of one atom with its corresponding eigendisplacements, is used here to describes the contribution from individual atom to the mode effective charge of one polar phonon. As can be seen from Table 5, the interesting behavior of dielectric contribution from $E_{\text{u}}$ modes is determined mainly by their mode effective charges. The significant decrease of the oscillator strengths for the $E_{\text{u}}$ (6) and $E_{\text{u}}$ (8) modes in BZN compared to those in BMN is clearly corresponding to their decreased mode effective charges. It is also evident that the effectively increment of the mode dynamical charge can remarkably improve its dielectric response, such as in the cases of the $E_{\text{u}}$ (7) mode in BMN and BZN, and the $E_{\text{u}}$ (8) mode in BMT and BMN. We find that the total mode effective charges are mainly composed of contributions from $B''$ and O atoms, while the contributions from Ba and $B'$ atoms become important gradually from BMN and BZN. Surprisingly, though the replacement of Ta (Mg) by Nb (Zn) indeed enhances its corresponding atomic mode charge, the influence on the total mode charge is also due to significant variation of contributions from O1 and O2 sublattices. For the $E_{\text{u}}$ (7) and $E_{\text{u}}$ (8) modes in BMT and BMN compounds, the increase of total mode charge is in a great measure owning to the enhanced contributions from the combination of O1 with O2 sublattices. For the dramatically increase of oscillator strength for the $E_{\text{u}}$ (7) mode in BZN compared to BMN, it is necessary that the corresponding mode effective charge has significant increment. This is indeed the case for BZN, where most of enhancement of the mode charge comes from the consistent motion of O1 and O2 sublattices as compared to their opposing motion for O1 and O2 in BMN. For the $E_{\text{u}}$ (8) mode in BZN material, however, the substitution of Zn for Mg in BMN suppresses the atomic mode charges both for O1 and O2 atoms in $Z^{*}_{\lambda,2}$, and hence the total mode charge.

The atomic mode charge of $B'$ atom has relatively large value for the $A_{2\text{u}}(4)$ and $A_{2\text{u}}(5)$ modes in comparison with that of the $E_{\text{u}}$ modes. The results for the atomic mode charges of O1 and O2 sublattices confirmed once again that the significant influence on the total mode charge by substitution of Nb (Zn) for Ta (Mg) also occurs in vibrations involving O1 and O2 atoms.

From the above analysis, we find that the atomic mode effective charges of O (O1 and O2) anions are strongly $B$-site dependent, while those of the Ba ions are only weakly so, suggesting a strong $B$-site dependence of their eigendisplacements for O1 and O2 sublattices. Because the mode effective charges are essentially electronic quantities, these results indicate that the electronic structure and the force constants, and hence the intrinsic dielectric response of these three related materials are more influenced by the $B$-site cations.

### 4. Conclusions
We have performed first-principle calculations within the generalized gradient approximation on three related microwave dielectrics $\mathrm{Ba(Mg_{1/3}Ta_{2/3})O_3}$, $\mathrm{Ba(Mg_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3}$, and $\mathrm{Ba(Zn_{1/3}Nb_{2/3})O_3}$ in order to provide microscopic understanding of their intrinsic dielectric responses. Our calculated optical phonon frequencies and the average dielectric constants agree quite well with experiments. The change of oscillator strengths for the dominant IR phonons among these three related compounds is determined mainly by their mode effective charges but their frequencies. Meanwhile, dielectric contributions from O anions are dominant and strongly $B$-site dependent. In addition, there is a clearly relationship between the dielectric contribution of individual ions and the experimental $Q\times f$ values, e.g, the greater the dielectric response from O anions, especially from O2 ions, the smaller the measured microwave $Q\times f$-factor.
