# Structures, Mechanical Properties and Band Structures of Pentagonal $\boldsymbol{B_xN_y}$ Monolayers

Lizhao Liu$^{1,a^*}$, Qiaotong Pang$^{2,b}$ and Yang Liu$^{1,c}$

$^{1}$School of Mathematical and Physical Sciences, Dalian University of Technology, Panjin Campus, Panjin 124221, China

$^{2}$School of Food and Environment Engineering, Dalian University of Technology, Panjin Campus, Panjin 124221, China

$^{a}$lizhao_liu@dlut.edu.cn, $^{b}$qiaotongpang@outlook.com, $^{c}$2667645149@qq.com

**Keywords:** Penta-$B_xN_y$, Mechanical anisotropy, Band gap transition.

Abstract. Stable structures of pentagonal $B_xN_y$ monolayers of different stoichiometric ratios were investigated through density functional theory calculations. Combining the energy and phonon dispersion, two stable pentagonal $B_xN_y$ structures, $B_2N_4$-I and $B_3N_3$-I, are predicted. Under uniaxial and biaxial tensile strains, $B_2N_4$-I and $B_3N_3$-I show anisotropy mechanical behaviours in terms of Young's modulus and intrinsic strength. $B_2N_4$-I possesses larger Young's modulus (up to 206 N/m) and intrinsic strength (up to 40 GPa) compared with those of $B_3N_3$-I. Particularly, due to the low symmetry and prominent anisotropy, uniaxial tensile strain can uniquely tailor the band gap and trigger the transition from a direct to an indirect band gap in semiconducting $B_3N_3$-I.

## Introduction

Graphene is a well-studied two dimensional (2D) material with honeycomb lattice. It presents abundant properties and promising applications [1,2]. Recently, a novel carbon allotrope with pentagonal network has been proposed, which is called penta-graphene [3]. Different from the metallic graphene, the penta-graphene exhibits a large indirect band gap, varying from 2.2 to 5.8 eV depending on the theoretical method [3-7]. Meanwhile, penta-graphene also shows good mechanical properties with a negative Poisson's ratio [3,8,9]. It can achieve an equi-biaxial tensile strain of 21%, a 2D in-plane Young's modulus ($Y$) of 263.8 GPa·nm and a Poisson's ratio of $-0.068$ [3]. However, the penta-graphene is easy to transform to hexagonal graphene and would be hardly fabricated or isolated experimentally. It was found that penta-graphene can be transformed directly to graphene through bond rotations and bond breakages without energy cost and the final graphene was 0.761 eV per atom more stable than the initial penta-graphene[10]. Besides, the transition to graphene can be also triggered via applying strain or temperature at a critical uniaxial strain of 5.1% and a critical transition temperature of 600 K, respectively [9].

Thus, a key issue is to find stable pentagonal 2D materials like penta-graphene. Numerous works have been carried outed and various penta-graphene-like 2D materials was reported, such as pentagonal $SiC_2$ monolayer [11,12], pentagonal $AlN_2$ monolayer [13], pentagonal $SnX_2$ (X=S, Se, or Te) monolayers [14]. Indeed, some stable pentagonal $B_xN_y$ (penta-$B_xN_y$) monolayers have also been predicted. For example, the stability and mechanical properties of pentagonal $B_2N_4$ and $B_4N_2$ monolayers was studied by Yagmurcukardes *et al.* [15], where the penta-$B_2N_4$ was stable with an in-plane stiffness of $3.62\ \text{eV}/\text{Å}^2$ and a negative Poisson's ratio of $-0.02$, but the penta-$B_4N_2$ was unstable against vibrational excitation. In addition to the stable penta-$B_2N_4$, Li *et al.* [16] found penta-$B_3N_3$ is also thermodynamically and kinetically stable. Moreover, penta-$B_2N_4$ exhibited better stability and larger mechanical strength than penta-$B_3N_3$.

Although stable structures of penta-$B_xN_y$ have been predicted, the detailed geometric structures and relatively energetic stabilities for penta-$B_xN_y$ monolayers with different stoichiometric ratios are still unclear. Particularly, the mechanical anisotropy and strain-induced transition from direct to indirect band gap of the penta-$B_xN_y$ monolayers have not been reported, which will be presented in this paper.

## Computational Methods

For the geometry optimization, the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) [17] was adopted with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [18] for the exchange-correlation interaction and the PAW pseudopotential [19,20] for the ion-electron interaction. A vacuum thickness of 15 Å was used to avoid the interaction between the structure and its periodic images. An energy cutoff of 600 eV was chosen to ensure well convergence of total energy and stress. To sample *k* points in the first Brillouin zone of the penta-BₓNᵧ unit cell, a Monkhorst-Pack *k*-mesh [21] of 14 × 14 × 1 was adopted. All the structures were fully relaxed using the conjugate gradient algorithm until the force on each atom was less than 0.01 eV Å⁻¹ and the energy was converged to 10⁻⁶ eV. Based on the optimized structures, both the mechanical and electronic properties of the stable penta-BₓNᵧ were calculated used the same scheme. For the phonon dispersion calculation, the finite displacement method as implemented in Phonopy [22] was employed with a 4 × 4 × 1 q point grid to obtain the dynamic matrices. The convergence criteria were set to 10⁻⁸ eV for the total energy and 0.001 eV/Å for the Hellmann-Feynman force.

## Results and Discussion

### Structures of Penta-BₓNᵧ.
Here we considered all the B:N ratios for the penta-BₓNᵧ, i.e. 1:5, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1 and 5:1. Then, all the possible configurations of the penta-BₓNᵧ monolayers at each ratio were constructed by placing B and N atoms in possible sp² and/or sp³ sites, obtaining 16 possible penta-BₓNᵧ structures in total. Afterwards, all the possible configurations were fully optimized and discussed in detail below.

For the B:N ratio of 1:5, there are two possible configurations, labeled as B₁N₅–I and B₁N₅–II in Fig. 1. In B₁N₅–I, B atom locates at an sp³ site bonded with four sp² N atoms. The optimized lattice constant of B₁N₅–I is 3.37 Å, with an out-of-plane thickness of 1.64 Å. Meanwhile, for the B₁N₅–II structure, B atom is placed at the sp² site. Compared with B₁N₅–I, B₁N₅–II has a larger lattice constant of 3.55 Å and a similar thickness of 1.68 Å. Moreover, B₁N₅–I is more stable than B₁N₅–II by 1.20 eV per unit cell. Upon optimization, the pentagonal network of B₁N₅–II is damaged since the N–N bond length can reach up to 2.33 Å, as marked by the dashed lines in the B₁N₅–II. In contrast, the pentagonal network structure of B₁N₅–I is maintained and the largest bond, a B–N bond, is only 1.53 Å in length.

Similar to the case of B₁N₅, there are also two possible configurations for the B:N ratio of 5:1, labeled as B₅N₁–I and B₅N₁–II in Fig. 1. For B₅N₁–I where N atom locates at the sp³ site, it has a lattice constant of 3.47 Å and a thickness of 1.68 Å. However, this structure would reconstruct during optimization and the original pentagonal network would be broken since the B–B bond can be stretched to 2.70 Å, as indicated by the dashed lines. For B₅N₁–II with N atom locating at the sp² site, it is fairly flat and shows a lattice constant of 4.22 Å. After optimization, the initial B₅N₁–II structure with pentagonal networks will transform to another configuration composed of the triangular, pentagonal, and hexagonal rings. Through comparing the total energy, the B₅N₁–II is found to be more stable than B₅N₁–I with 0.08 eV per unit cell lower in total energy.

Then, for the composition with two B atoms and four N atoms unit cell, four possible configurations have been constructed, which are named as B₂N₄–I, B₂N₄–II, B₂N₄–III and B₂N₄–IV in Fig. 1. In B₂N₄–I, both B atoms locate at the sp³ sites, each of which are bonded with four sp² N atoms. It has an optimized lattice constant is 3.62 Å and a thickness of 1.27 Å. For the case of B atoms locating at sp² sites, there are two choices. One is B₂N₄–II with two B atoms siting at para positions, which has a lattice constant of 3.61 Å and a thickness of 2.85 Å. The other is B₂N₄–IV with two B atoms siting at ortho positions, which has a lattice constant of 3.71 Å and a thickness of 1.81 Å. In the structure of B₂N₄–III, two B atoms locate at the sp² and sp³ sites separately and bond with each other. The lattice constant and thickness of B₂N₄–III are 3.61 Å and 1.22 Å, respectively. Among the four structures with the B:N ratio of 1:2, B₂N₄–I is the most stable one with the total energy of 1.60 eV per unit cell lower than B₂N₄–II, 2.96 eV lower than B₂N₄–III, and 4.37 eV lower than B₂N₄–IV,

respectively. Also, $B_2N_4$-I is the flattest one and the only one keeping the pentagonal structure upon relaxation. The largest bond length in $B_2N_4$-I is $1.55$ $\mathring{A}$ for the B-N bond, compared to $3.37$ $\mathring{A}$ in $B_2N_4$-II, $2.55$ $\mathring{A}$ in $B_2N_4$-III, and $2.44$ $\mathring{A}$ in $B_2N_4$-IV, indicating structure breaking in the latter three structures. All the overlarge bonds in $B_2N_4$-II, $B_2N_4$-III and $B_2N_4$-IV are N-N bonds, as marked by the dashed lines.

By reversing the B and N atoms in the $B_2N_4$ structures, four possible configurations for the B:N ratio of 2:1 can be obtained, which are named as $B_4N_2$-I, $B_4N_2$-II, $B_4N_2$-III and $B_4N_2$-IV, respectively. Similarly, the one with both N atoms locating at the $sp^3$ sites bonded with four $sp^2$ B atoms is named as $B_4N_2$-I, which possesses an optimized lattice constant of $3.82$ $\mathring{A}$ and a thickness of $1.07$ $\mathring{A}$. If the two N atoms locate at $sp^2$ sites, another two configurations can be constructed, i.e. $B_4N_2$-II and $B_4N_2$-III. In $B_4N_2$-II, two N atoms site at ortho positions with a lattice constant of $3.51$ $\mathring{A}$ and a thickness of $1.49$ $\mathring{A}$. Compared with $B_4N_2$-I, $B_4N_2$-II is less stable with $1.78$ eV per unit cell higher than that of $B_4N_2$-I in energy. Another structure with two N atoms lying in para positions is denoted as $B_4N_2$-III. However, during optimization, the initial pentagonal network transforms to another structure consisting of quadrangular, hexagonal, and octagonal rings. This latter structure shows much lower total energy, $2.39$ eV per unit cell lower than that of the $B_4N_2$-I. The lattice constant and thickness of $B_4N_2$-III are $4.26$ $\mathring{A}$ and $0.76$ $\mathring{A}$, respectively. The last configuration is $B_4N_2$-IV with one N atom locating at an $sp^3$ site and the other N atom at an $sp^2$ site. Similar to the case of $B_4N_2$-III, after geometry optimization, structural transition from the initial pentagonal network to a structure consisting of quadrangular, hexagonal, and octagonal rings is also found for $B_4N_2$-IV. The optimized $B_4N_2$-IV has a lattice constant of $4.29$ $\mathring{A}$ and is rather flat with a thickness of only $0.28$ $\mathring{A}$. The total energy of $B_4N_2$-IV is $2.72$ eV per unit cell lower than that of the $B_4N_2$-I.

For the pentagonal $B_xN_y$ unit cell with equal number of B and N atoms, there are also four possible configurations, named as $B_3N_3$-I, $B_3N_3$-II, $B_3N_3$-III and $B_3N_3$-IV, respectively. In the $B_3N_3$-I structure, one B atom locates at the $sp^2$ site and the other two B atoms locate at the $sp^3$ sites. The lattice

constant and thickness of $B_3N_3$-I is $3.76$ Å and $1.36$ Å, respectively. If one B atom locates at the $\text{sp}^3$ site and the other two B atoms locate at the $\text{sp}^2$ sites, two structures marked as $B_3N_3$-II and $B_3N_3$-III are obtained, with the two B atoms locating at ortho position of $\text{sp}^2$ sites in $B_3N_3$-II and para position in $B_3N_3$-III. The equilibrium lattice constants are $3.80$ Å for $B_3N_3$-II and $3.70$ Å for $B_3N_3$-III with corresponding thickness of $1.97$ Å and $1.42$ Å, individually. Particularly, for $B_3N_3$-IV with all the three B atoms locating at the $\text{sp}^2$ sites, it would transform to the $B_3N_3$-I configuration during optimization. Therefore, after optimization, only three possible configurations labeled as $B_3N_3$-I, $B_3N_3$-II, and $B_3N_3$-III are obtained in Fig. 1 since $B_3N_3$-IV transforms to $B_3N_3$-I. Among the three optimized structures, $B_3N_3$-I is the most stable one with the total energy of $1.89$ eV per unit cell lower than that of $B_3N_3$-II and $2.32$ eV per unit cell lower than that of $B_3N_3$-III. Besides, after optimization, the pentagonal structure network is retained in $B_3N_3$-I and $B_3N_3$-III, but destroyed in $B_3N_3$-II, as demonstrated by the overlarge N-N bond length of $2.79$ Å denoted by the dashed lines.

We further calculated the phonon spectra for the lowest-energy penta-$B_xN_y$ structures of different B:N ratios without bond breaking, as well as those configurations with structural transition. As a result, phonon spectra of 7 selected structures were computed, including $B_1N_5$-I, $B_5N_1$-II, $B_2N_4$-I, $B_4N_2$-I, $B_4N_2$-III, $B_4N_2$-IV and $B_3N_3$-I, as shown in Fig. 2. It was found that except for $B_2N_4$-I and $B_3N_3$-I, all the other five penta-$B_xN_y$ structures have soft modes, indicating their dynamic instability. Thus, combined the analysis of total energy and phonon spectra, there are two possibly stable configurations for the penta-$B_xN_y$ structures, i.e. $B_2N_4$-I and $B_3N_3$-I, which agrees well with previous reports [15, 16].

Then, we discussed the geometry structures and binding energies ($E_b\text{S}$) of the stable $B_2N_4$-I and $B_3N_3$-I in detail. Table 1 summarizes lattice constants and layer thickness of $B_2N_4$-I and $B_3N_3$-I. Compared with $B_2N_4$-I, $B_3N_3$-I has a larger lattice constant of $3.76$ Å and is more buckled with a thickness of $1.36$ Å. Meanwhile, the bond length for each bond in $B_2N_4$-I and $B_3N_3$-I shown in Fig. 3 is also presented. For $B_2N_4$-I, the B-N and N-N bond lengths are $1.55$ and $1.34$ Å, respectively. Since the two B atoms locate at the two equivalent $\text{sp}^3$ sites, all the B-N bonds show the same bond length of $1.55$ Å. Similarly, all the N-N bonds also have the same bond length of $1.34$ Å. As a result, the $B_2N_4$-I exhibits a space group symmetry of $P\overline{4}2_1m$. Compared with $B_2N_4$-I, $B_3N_3$-I has a lower space group symmetry of CM with the B-N bond length ranging from $1.48$ to $1.62$ Å. The lengths for B-B and N-N bonds are $1.78$ and $1.34$ Å, respectively. Both the lattice parameters and bond lengths are well consistent with previous reports [15, 16].

Mechanical Properties. We further studied the mechanical properties of the stable B₂N₄-I and B₃N₃-I structures, including Young's modulus, intrinsic strength ($\tau_c$) and fracture strain ($\varepsilon_c$). Starting from the 2D equilibrium structures, both the two systems were gradually elongated with a strain increment of 1% uniaxially (along the X or Y axis) and biaxially, as illustrated in Fig. 3. The strain-stress curves for B₂N₄-I and B₃N₃-I were plotted in Fig. 4, from which the intrinsic strength $\tau_c$ and fracture strain $\varepsilon_c$ can determined at the critical dropping point. Moreover, using the data in the linear part of the strain-stress curve, the 3D Young's modulus ($E$) can be calculated by the following formula:

$$
E=\frac{V}{V_{0}} \times \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}=\frac{Z}{d} \times \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}, \tag{2}
$$

where $V$ is volume of the supercell, $V_0$ is the effective volume of the penta-BₓNᵧ at the equilibrium state in the supercell, $Z$ is the vacuum thickness of 15 Å, $\varepsilon$ is the applied tensile strain, $\sigma$ is the corresponding stress, and $d$ is the effective thickness of penta-BₓNᵧ, which equals the sum of the layer thickness of penta-BₓNᵧ plus the van der Waals thickness of 3.4 Å [23]. Therefore, the effective thickness $d$ is 4.67 Å for B₂N₄-I and 4.76 Å for B₃N₃-I. By multiplying the effective thickness $d$ of penta-BₓNᵧ, the 3D Young's modulus $E$ can be converted to the 2D in-plane Young's modulus $Y$ [24].

As shown in Fig. 4, under biaxial tensile strain, $B_2N_4$–I can undertake a maximum strain of 13% and reach an intrinsic strength of 36 GPa. However, the intrinsic strength increases to 40 GPa for the cases under uniaxial tensile strain along X and Y axis, with a fracture strain of 13% and 14%, respectively. As a result, the uniaxial tensile strain achieves larger intrinsic strength (~11% higher) than that of the biaxial tensile strain. Instead, the fracture strain and intrinsic strength are 16% and 24 GPa for $B_3N_3$–I under biaxial tensile strain, but drops dramatically to 8% and 17 GPa for the X or Y uniaxial tensile strain, respectively. For $B_3N_3$–I, the intrinsic strength degrades by ~30% and the fracture strain drops by 50% from biaxial to uniaxial tensile strain. The differences of both intrinsic strength and fracture strain between the biaxial and uniaxial tensile strains indicate the anisotropy of $B_2N_4$–I and $B_3N_3$–I. Particularly, due to the lower space group symmetry of $B_3N_3$–I, its anisotropy is more prominent than that of the $B_2N_4$–I.

Band Structures. In addition, tuning of band structures by tensile strain is explored to investigate the electromechanical properties of $B_2N_4$–I and $B_3N_3$–I. As shown in Fig. 5a, at the equilibrium state, $B_2N_4$–I is metallic without a band gap, which is consistent with previous reports [15, 16]. Moreover,

the metallic character of $\text{B}_2\text{N}_4$-I is well kept in spite of biaxial and uniaxial tensile strains even at the fracture strain. External strain only slightly shifts the bands near the Fermi energy without destroying the metallicity.

In contrast to $\text{B}_2\text{N}_4$-I, at the ground state, $\text{B}_3\text{N}_3$-I is a direct semiconductor with a small band gap of 0.06 eV. Under biaxial tensile strain, band structures of $\text{B}_3\text{N}_3$-I show no noticeable change, as shown in Fig. 5c. However, when applying uniaxial tensile strain, significant change of band

structures can be observed in $B_3N_3$–I. As presented in Fig. 6, under uniaxial tensile strain along X or Y axis, band gap opens as the tensile strain increases. Within the fracture strain, band gap of $B_3N_3$–I increases from 0.06 eV at zero strain to 0.57 eV at a strain of 8%, showing an amplification of 850%, as indicated by Fig. 5d. Especially, transition from direct to indirect semiconductor is observed in $B_3N_3$–I under uniaxial tensile strain. The critical tensile strain for this transition is 5%. Within the tensile strain of 5%, band gap of $B_3N_3$–I increases from the initial value of 0.06 eV to 0.28 eV, but retaining the direct-gap semiconducting character. When the tensile strain exceeds the critical strain of 5%, $B_3N_3$–I switches from a direct semiconductor to an indirect one. Above the fracture strain, the K points for valence band maximum and conduction band minimum will shift, as indicated by the band structures at the strain of 9% in Fig. 6. Therefore, uniaxial tensile strain can not only tune the band gap, but also trigger a transition from direct to indirect semiconductor for the $B_3N_3$–I, which may lead to future application of penta-BN monolayers in photoelectric materials.

Summary

Possible structures of pentagonal $B_xN_y$ monolayers with different stoichiometric ratios were systematically investigated by DFT calculations. Through evaluation of the total energy and phonon spectrum, two stable penta-$B_xN_y$ structures, $B_2N_4$–I and $B_3N_3$–I, were obtained, which is consistent with previous reports. $B_2N_4$–I was metallic and $B_3N_3$–I was a direct semiconductor with a small band gap of 0.06 eV. Simulation of mechanical properties suggested that $B_2N_4$–I was stiffer than $B_3N_3$–I with larger Young's modulus and intrinsic strength. Under biaxial tensile strain, $B_2N_4$–I shows a 2D Young's modulus of 194 N/m and an intrinsic strength of 36 GPa, while $B_3N_3$–I possesses a 2D Young's modulus of 127 N/m and an intrinsic strength of 24 GPa. Moreover, both $B_2N_4$–I and $B_3N_3$–I show anisotropy mechanical behaviours in terms of Young's modulus and intrinsic strength under biaxial and uniaxial tensile strains. The uniaxial tensile strain achieves both larger Young's modulus and intrinsic strength than the biaxial strain for $B_2N_4$–I. While, for $B_3N_3$–I, the uniaxial tensile strain only achieves larger Young's modulus but smaller intrinsic strength compared with the biaxial tensile strain. This anisotropy also exists in band structures. Under biaxial tensile strain, the band structures of $B_3N_3$–I show no clear change. However, when applying uniaxial tensile strain, band gap of $B_3N_3$–I increases with strain. Particularly, a transition from a direct to an indirect semiconductor of $B_3N_3$–I can be observed. This unique property may lead to potential applications of penta-$B_xN_y$ monolayers in photoelectric nanomaterials.
