## ABSTRACT

Radiation shielding materials are of prime importance for weighing the benefit-to-risk of ionizing radiation. In this perspective, a lanthanum-barium-borovanadate (BVBL) glass containing varying amounts of bismuth oxide ($Bi_2O_3$) is studied in this work. The glass composition of $5La_2O_3$-$10BaO$-(65-x)$B_2O_3$-$20V_2O_5$-$xBi_2O_3$ where x: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 mol% has been purposed, and thus six different glass series with BVBL0 to BVBL15 coded were investigated for the physical, mechanical, and radiation shielding features. Physical property calculations revealed that the glass density increase from 3.1685 to $4.1345\ g\ cm^{-3}$ with the insertion of $Bi_2O_3$ from 0 to 15 mol %. Furthermore, mechanical properties in terms of the Mackenzie-Makishima model demonstrated that Young's, bulk, shear, and longitudinal moduli were reduced following the concentration of $Bi_2O_3$. In particular, the BVBL0 sample showed the best mechanical moduli amongst others. Radiation shielding competencies were ascertained by using Phy-X/PSD and Monte Carlo N-Particle Transport Code (MCNP-5). The theoretical computations via Phy-X/PSD clearly indicated that the linear attenuation coefficient (LAC) showed enhanced characteristics with the increasing content of $Bi_2O_3$. Based upon the determined LAC, the other essential parameters such as half-value layer (HVL), effective atomic number ($Z_{eff}$), and transmission factor (TF) were also assessed. Moreover, the MCNP-5 simulation demonstrated that the BVBL15 sample provided the best radiation attenuation compared to the undoped BVBL0. Moreover, both Phy-X/PSD and MCNP5 studies displayed good agreement with each other. Overall, we concluded that $Bi_2O_3$ seems to be a strong candidate for improving radiation attenuation coefficients in the BVBL glass system.

## 1. Introduction

Preventive actions against high-energy photons have become of prime importance lately (AbuAlRoos et al., 2019; Agar, 2018; Kurtulus and Kavas, 2020). One of the most popular protection way, the radiation shielding concept, is generally implemented in places where irradiation occurs (Tekin et al., 2019). The building walls, wearable cloths, masks, or observation windows are regarded as the primary safeguard materials in radiation shielding applications (Akkurt and El-Khayatt, 2013; Al-Hadeethi et al., 2020; Muthamma et al., 2020; Ozturk et al., 2020). However, such material must have some critical properties, including high-density. From this perspective, lead and/or lead oxide-containing materials are commonly preferred in different applications like in medical diagnosis or nuclear (El-Mallawy et al., 2018; Sayyed et al., 2020b). Alternatively, heavy-weight concrete materials have been chosen to improve the building's attenuation characteristics (Zorla

In recent times, glass materials have been used as another choice due to their unique properties involving transparency, reaching considerable high densities, and superior optical transmission (Kurtulus et al., 2021b). Furthermore, glass materials offer compositional flexibility, recycling and/or reusing possibilities, and a wide range of property manipulation (Kurtulus et al., 2021d). Therefore, the scientific community has initiated a deeper investigation on radiation shielding glasses in the last decade.

A variety of glass types are typically utilized in distinct applications such as telecommunication, daily wares, advanced optical scintillators, and aerospace (Aygün et al., 2020; Issa et al., 2017; Mhareb et al., 2019). The selection of glass-making content makes it possible to evaluate the glass composition for the applications purposed. In this sense, silicate, borate, phosphate, germanate, and telluride-based glass systems are prevalently used (Akyildirim et al., 2020; Almuqrin and Sayyed, 2021; Dong et al., 2019; Sayyed et al., 2021c). Among these, borate-containing glasses take remarkable importance owing to the advanced features of boron oxide ($B_2O_3$), including low thermal expansion coefficient, low melting temperatures, high mechanical resistance, and capability to host various oxide contents in the glass network (Kurtulus et al., 2021c; Sallam et al., 2020). On the other hand, vanadium oxide ($V_2O_5$) can provide some improvements in the glass structure. These advantages can be said as an increment in the overall glass density ($V_2O_5$: $3.36\ g\ cm^{-3}$), as well as a very low melting point ($690\ ^{\circ}C$) (Somaily et al., 2021). With this in mind, borovanadate (BV) glass systems have become a good candidate for radiation protection purposes.

When the literature is comprehensively reviewed in terms of BV glass systems, the authors reached up some valuable knowledge for differentiating their glass compositions. In some studies, $B_2O_3$-$V_2O_5$-PbO (Kebaili et al., 2020a), $B_2O_3$-$ZnO$-$V_2O_5$ (KILIÇ, 2020), $B_2O_3$-$V_2O_5$-$ZnO$-$Li_2O$-$TiO_2$ (Kebaili et al., 2020b), $B_2O_3$-$V_2O_5$-$Li_2O$-$MoO_3$ (Srinivasa Rao, 2019), $B_2O_3$-$V_2O_5$-$TeO_2$-$Nb_2O_5$ (Swapna and Prasad, 2017), and $B_2O_3$-$V_2O_5$-$Na_2O$ (Venkata Subba Reddy et al., 2009) different BV glass system containing various oxides were investigated. It is apparent that barium oxide (BaO) and lanthanum oxide ($La_2O_3$) has not been constituted together within the BV network. However, we know that both contents are very significant for enhancing the overall glass density, which ensures enhanced photon attenuation characteristics (Olarinoye et al., 2020; Saudi and El-Kameesy, 2018). Likewise, the so-created lanthanum-barium-borovanadate (BVBL) glass system can be developed more by understanding the influence of bismuth oxide ($Bi_2O_3$) doping. This is because $Bi_2O_3$ can present greater density values ($8.90\ g\ cm^{-3}$), good shielding ability via higher atomic number, relatively lower melting temperatures ($817\ ^{\circ}C$), and efficient solubility in the borate network (Al-Buriahi et al., 2020a; Sayyed et al., 2019). Additionally, the low-density content, $B_2O_3$, can be replaced by high-density oxide, $Bi_2O_3$, to increase the overall glass density, so does radiation shielding competencies. Therefore, it is now worth exploring the effect of the $Bi_2O_3$ doping rate on the BVBL glass system using some familiar theoretical evaluations, i.e., MCNP-5 and Phy-X/PSD.

For investigating the potential use of BVBL glass doped with $Bi_2O_3$ as a radiation shielding material, the authors designed a glass composition in the following: $5La_2O_3$-$10BaO$-(65-x)$B_2O_3$-$20V_2O_5$-$xBi_2O_3$ (x: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, and 15 mol%). These glass series (BVBL0 to BVBL15) were explored by conducting physical and mechanical properties calculations, as well as photon shielding computations via MCNP-5 and Phy-X/PSD. The role of $Bi_2O_3$ content in the BVBL glass system has comprehensively been understood, and the findings were explicitly reported below.

## 2. Materials & methods

### 2.1. Glass compositions

According to the intended glass composition for the BVBL system, the corresponding content's molar percentages and codes were listed in Table 1. The enhancer content, $Bi_2O_3$, replaces $B_2O_3$ in the amount of 0 to 15 mol% with the steps of 3. For glass codes, the letters represent the glass system, while the numbering defines the respective doping rate of $Bi_2O_3$.

### 2.2. Glass characterizations

The theoretical calculations for glass properties were made in two distinct steps: physical and mechanical. In physical property determinations, a number of crucial parameters such as glass density ($\rho$), molar volume ($V_m$), oxygen molar volume ($V_0$), and oxygen packing density (OPD) were evaluated by utilizing the expressions (1 to 5) revealed in Table 2. Here, the main equation, variables, and units are clearly provided. In the second step, mechanical properties were estimated by calculating some essential factors like Young's, bulk, shear, and longitudinal moduli with the use of the Makishima-Mackenzie model (Inaba et al., 1999; Makishima and Mackenzie, 1973; Rammah et al., 2020). Table 3 summarizes the concerned equations (6 to 10), variables, as well as units for each mechanical modulus.

### 2.3. Radiation shielding investigation using MCNP5 code

MCNP5 code has been employed to estimate the mass attenuation coefficient (MAC) for the tested BVBL0-BVBL15 glasses. This code is very useful for estimating the radiation shielding competence of the glassy materials, especially if the experimental setup cannot implement the experiment. Simulations were performed at twelve energies in the range of 0.015-15 MeV. The 3D geometry representing the MCNP-5 input file used in this study is exhibited in Fig. 1. The full details explaining this input file are described in our early work (Sayyed et al., 2020c). In order to validate the MCNP5 results, we also calculated the MAC for the BVBL0-BVBL15 glasses using Phy-X software (Şakar et al., 2020) and then compare the obtained MAC in both techniques to check the accuracy in our input file. Phy-X database is a user-friendly computer program that can be applied to evaluate parameters relevant to radiation shielding. Thanks to the use of this software, diverse radiation attenuation factors such as linear and mass attenuation coefficients and other factors (a total of 18 parameters) can be determined for the photon energies between 0.15 MeV and 100 GeV as well as for other energies emitted from common radioisotopes (such as $^{59}$Co, $^{137}$Cs, $^{133}$Ba, ..., etc.). Determination of each parameter is realized by the database using the chemical composition of the material, which can be entered in weight or mole fractions and the density of the material.

### 3.2. Mechanical property estimations

The understanding of variations in mechanical properties is of cap- ital importance in particular to designing. The higher mechanical moduli ensure greater resistance to strengths exerted on glass material. From this perspective, the Makishima-Mackenzie model was utilized for ascertaining Young's bulk, shear, and longitudinal moduli. In this model, dissociation energy $(G_{i})$ between metal-oxide bonds is consid ered. One can easily estimate the alterations in mechanical properties

with the known molar fractions, elements, and ionic radius values. In this sense, Fig. 4 displays four significant mechanical moduli behavior with the increase of the $Bi_2O_3$ doping rate. At first glance, we see that all moduli are in decreasing trend as the amount of $Bi_2O_3$ advances. It is found out that Young's ($E$), bulk ($B$), shear ($S$), and longitudinal ($L$) moduli descend from 93.17 to 74.51, 59.26 to 47.39, 37.63 to 30.10, and 87.48 to 69.96 GPa for the glass series of BVBL0 to BVBL15 in the respective order. The obtained results can be linked to having lower enthalpy (heat of formation) of Bi-O bonds ($81.978$ kcal cm$^{-3}$) than that of B-O bonds ($192.638$ kcal cm$^{-3}$). As the content of $Bi_2O_3$ enters the BVBL glass network in replacement of $B_2O_3$ constituent, the amount of network former diminishes, which allows deteriorating the overall mechanical resistance (Issa et al., 2019). Therefore, the authors reported that the insertion ratio of $Bi_2O_3$ should attentively be followed if mechanical strength is of interest in the BVBL glass system.

### 3.3. Radiation shielding parameters

Using MCNP5 and Phy-X program, the MAC for the BVBL0-BVBL15 samples was computed at 12 energy values ranging between 0.015 MeV and 15 MeV, and we plotted the results in Fig. 5. Apparently, Fig. 5 shows that the MCNP5 and Phys-X results agreed well with each other.

![](./images/812434158486814721_4.jpg)

Fig. 4. The behavior of the mechanical moduli with the change of $Bi_2O_3$ insertion.

The agreement between the MAC values obtained by the two methods was measured by means of relative difference, $\text{RD}=(R_S-R_T)/R_S$, where $R_S$ is the MCNP5 results (Simulation results), while $R_T$ is the Phys-X results (theoretical results). As shown in Table 4, the maximum RD reported for the chosen glasses is less than 10%, thereby confirming MAC's validity obtained by MCNP5 for the lanthanum-barium-borovanadate glass containing $Bi_2O_3$.

Moreover, the linear attenuation coefficient (LAC) is a critical factor utilizes to estimate the radiation shielding effectiveness of the individual glassy medium (Sayyed et al., 2021a). LAC represents a good sign of the glassy medium's strength to act as a shield against radiation (Kurtulus et al., 2021a). The data given in Fig. 5 were used to determine the LAC for the tested BVBL0-BVBL15 samples, and this was archived by multiplication the MAC for each sample with its density. It is worth mention that a higher LAC is being more desirable. The LAC for the BVBL0-BVBL15 glasses as a function of the density is exhibited in Fig. 6. Firstly, LAC has the maximum values, and a significant decrease can be noticed as the energy increases (between 0.015 and 0.3 MeV). This observation implies that between these low energies, the more efficient the tested glasses are at shielding radiation. For instance, the LAC at 0.015 MeV for BVBL0 is $60.205$ cm$^{-1}$ and decreases to $0.372$ cm$^{-1}$ at 0.3 MeV. For BVBL3 (as an example), the LAC at these two energies are 101.198 and $0.509$ cm$^{-1}$. Two important factors are reasonable on this behavior at these energies. The first one is the dependence of photoelectric cross-section (PH-CS) on the energy as $\text{E}^{-3.5}$. The second factor is the dependence of the PH-CS on the atomic number as $\text{Z}^{4.5}$ (Abouhaswa et al., 2020; Divina et al., 2020). Due to these two factors, we can see that the LAC has high values at 0.015 MeV and then falls quickly. For E>0.3 MeV, the LAC remains to show a gradual decrease with increasing the energy, but at a rate lower than the decrease observed in the first energy region. For instance, the LAC for BVBL0 sample at 0.5 MeV is $0.275$ cm$^{-1}$, and this value becomes $0.192$ cm$^{-1}$ at 1 MeV. Also, for BVBL3, the LAC at 0.5 and 1 MeV are $0.321$ and $0.207$ cm$^{-1}$, respectively. The lowest LAC for all samples is observed at 15 MeV (varied between $0.076$ and $0.155$ cm$^{-1}$), and this means that a smaller number of photons can be shielded at this energy BVBL0-BVBL15 glasses. From Fig. 7, increasing the $Bi_2O_3$ content leads to an increase in the LAC values. This means that the addition of $Bi_2O_3$ into the glass causes an improvement in the LAC values, and the glass with the composition of 50B2O3-20V2O5-10BaO-5 $La_2O_3$-15 $Bi_2O_3$ has the highest LAC. In Fig. 7, we showed the relation between the LAC and $Bi_2O_3$ content at only three energies, but the results for other energies are similar to those found for these three energies.

The transmission factor (TF) is a significant parameter for radiation attenuation characteristics and is used widely by the radiation shielding investigators to a deeper understanding of the shielding attitude for the investigated medium (Naseer et al., 2021; Sayyed et al., 2021b). TF gives the ratio between the number of photons that transmit the medium (glassy sample in this investigation) to the total number of photons emitted from the source. Applying Lambert-Beer law, i.e., $I/I_0=\exp^{(-LAC*x)}$ and use the LAC values given in the previous figure, we can determine the TF (which represents the left-hand side in the aforementioned law) at several thicknesses of the glasses. In this work, we selected different thicknesses (x) for the glass, i.e., x ranges between.1 and 5 cm. In Fig. 8, we plotted the transmission factor for the investigated glasses (a) as a function of energy for glass with the thickness of 3 cm and (b) as a function of the thickness at 1 MeV. One can deduce that increasing the energy from 0.015 to 15 MeV led to a dramatic increase in the TF. For a fixed thickness, i.e., Fig. 8-a, the minimum TF for all glasses is found at 0.015 MeV, and the glasses have a very promising shielding behavior against gamma radiation at this energy since the TF values are very small at 0.015 MeV. As the TF curves are shown, the shielding behavior becomes worse with increasing the energy of the photons, where the lowest attenuation ability is reported at 15 MeV, and the TF values lie in the range of 62.90-79.55% for a sample with x=3 cm.

When it comes to estimating TF with respect to different concentrations of B₂O₃ and Bi₂O₃, we found that the addition of Bi₂O₃ at the expense of B₂O₃ contributed to the decrease in TF (in other words, enhancing the shielding behavior). For instance, at 1 MeV, BVBL0 (the glass with free Bi₂O₃) with x=1 cm had 82.54% TF value, whereas the BVBL15 (the glass with 15mol% of Bi₂O₃) with the same thickness provided 76.76%. Practically, this implies that if the BVBL0 glass with x=1 cm exposures to radiation with E= at 1 MeV, then this glass can only attenuate 17.46% of the radiation (most of the photons can move easily from this sample). While BVBL15 glass can attenuate about 23.24% of the photons. This enhancement can be explained by the replacement of a lower atomic number of B with Bi. Hence, we can say that BVBL15 can be applied in the radiation shielding applications in the intended energy range.

Also, we can see from Fig. 8-b that one of the most important factors that influence the glass's ability to shield from radiation is the thickness. This factor is related directly to the ability of the glass to attenuate more photons. This is clearly seen in Fig. 8-b since the minimum TF (maximum attenuation) is achieved for the highest investigated thickness (i.e., 5 cm).

The half-value layer (HVL) of the BVBL0-BVBL15 samples was determined in addition to the previous shielding factors. Fig. 9 plots the ratio between the HVL values of BVBL0, the sample with No Bi₂O₃, to BVBL15, the sample with the highest content of Bi₂O₃ (i.e., 15 mol%). The aim of this figure is to compare the HVL of the glasses themselves. The ratio was calculated for the same energy range used in the previous curves. As we can see from Fig. 9, the ratio between HVL for BVBL0 and BVBL15 at any energy is higher than 1, implying that BVBL0 has a higher HVL than BVBL15. This result is in line with the data given in the previous figure since TF and HVL are very closely related. Fig. 9 shows that the (HVL BVBL0/HVL BVBL15) starts at a maximum value of 3.96, then sharply increasing to 2.98 at 0.05 MeV. After that, the ratio decreases steadily and reaches 1.85 at 10 MeV. At the first few energies in Fig. 9, the photoelectric effect is influential, so we noticed the sharp reduction in the HVL ratio (the ratio reduces from 3.96 to 1.77 between 0.015 and 0.5 MeV). Whereas, for higher energies, the Compton scattering becomes more important than the Photoelectric effect, so we find the ratio decreases slowly. One important conclusion can be drawn from Fig. 9; the addition of Bi₂O₃ has a greater influence on the HVL at lower energies, whereas having lesser influence at higher energies, which is in agreement with TF results. Nevertheless, the ratio given in Fig. 9 shows that BVBL15 has the lowest HVL indicating that this glass has the better gamma-ray shielding potential.

It is important to remember that a higher $Z_{eff}$ signifies a better shield (Kavaz et al., 2019). ## 4. Conclusions

In this study, a lanthanum-barium-borovanadate (BVBL) glass containing varying amounts of bismuth oxide ($Bi_2O_3$) was investigated with respect to physical, mechanical, and radiation shielding properties. To achieve this, we designed glass compositions of $5La_2O_3$-$10BaO$-(65-x) $B_2O_3$-$20V_2O_5$-$xBi_2O_3$ (x: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 mol%), and hence the BVBL0 to BVBL15 coded glass series were comprised. According to the physical property calculations, the glass density rose from 3.1685 to $4.1345 g cm^{-3}$ while the molar volume escalated from 35.75 to $41.77 cm^{3} mol^{-1}$ for the glass series of BVBL0 to BVBL15, respectively. Additionally, the oxygen molar volume ranged between 11.17 and $13.05 cm^{3} mol^{-1}$, whereas the oxygen packing density values were equal to 89.52, 85.96, 83.01, 80.53, 78.42, and $76.61 cm^{3} mol^{-1}$ for the glass samples from BVBL0 to BVBL15 in the respective order. On the other hand, mechanical moduli determinations via the Mackenzi-Makishima model revealed that all moduli were in decreasing trend as the amount of $Bi_2O_3$ advanced, namely Young's (E), bulk (B), shear (S), and longitudinal (L) moduli descended from 93.17 to 74.51, 59.26 to 47.39, 37.63 to 30.10, and 87.48 to 69.96 GPa with the insertion ratio of $Bi_2O_3$ from 0 to 15 mol %. From the perspective of radiation shielding computations, the addition of $Bi_2O_3$ into the glass caused an improvement in the LAC values, and the glass with the composition of 50B2O3-20V2O5-10BaO-5 $La_2O_3$-15 $Bi_2O_3$ had the highest LAC. Moreover, the addition of $Bi_2O_3$ had a greater influence on the HVL at lower energies, whereas having lesser influence at higher energies. Likewise, $Z_{eff}$ of the investigated glasses had an increasing trend as the $Bi_2O_3$ content in the glasses increased. Yet another essential parameter, the transmission factor (TF), was estimated. It was observed that the minimum TF for all glasses was found at 0.015 MeV, and the glasses had a very promising shielding behavior against gamma radiation at this energy since the TF values were very small at 0.015 MeV. As the TF curves were shown, the shielding behavior became worse with increasing the energy of the photons, where the lowest attenuation ability was reported at 15 MeV, and the TF values lied in the range of 62.90-79.55% for a sample with x=3 cm. Importantly, the maximum RD reported for the chosen glasses was less

than 10%, thereby confirming MAC's validity obtained by MCNP5 (versus Phy-X database) for the lanthanum-barium-borovanadate glass containing $Bi_2O_3$. Overall, the authors reported that the BVBL glass system doped with $Bi_2O_3$ could be identified as an alternative radiation shielding material, but the BVBL15 is recognized as the best candidate.
