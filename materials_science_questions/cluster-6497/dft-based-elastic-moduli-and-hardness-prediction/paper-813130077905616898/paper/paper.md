# Structural stability and mechanical properties of $Be_3N_2$ under high pressure

Jing Chang$^{a,b}$, NiNa Ge$^{c}$ and Ke Liu$^{a}$

$^{a}$Institute of Solid State Physics, Sichuan Normal University, Chengdu, China; $^{b}$School of Materials Science and Engineering, Southwest Jiaotong University, Chengdu, China; $^{c}$State Key Laboratory Cultivation Base for Nonmetal Composites and Functional Materials, Southwest University of Science and Technology, Mianyang, China

## ABSTRACT
A theoretical investigations on the structural stability and mechanical properties of $Be_3N_2$ crystallising in $\alpha$ and $\beta$ phases was performed using first-principles calculations based on density functional theory.The obtained ground state structure and mechanical properties are in excellent agreement with the available experimental and theoretical data. A full elastic tensor and crystal anisotropy of $Be_3N_2$ in two phases are determined in the wide pressure range. Results indicated that the two phases of $Be_3N_2$ are mechanically stable and strongly pressure dependent in the range of pressure from 0 to 80 GPa. The superior mechanical properties show that the two phases of $Be_3N_2$ are potential candidate structures to be the hard material. And the $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$ has better mechanical properties than $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$. By the calculated $B/G$ ratio, it is predicted that both phases are intrinsically brittleness and strongly prone to ductility when the pressure is above 65.6 and 68.5 GPa, respectively. Additionally, the pressure-induced elastic anisotropy analysis indicates that the elastically anisotropic of $Be_3N_2$ in both phases is strengthening with increasing pressure, and strongly dependent on the propagation direction.

## 1. Introduction

Ultra-incompressible and hard materials attract a great deal of interest owing to their excellent mechanical and thermal properties, such as high hardness and strength, high melting point and wear resistance, excellent thermal and chemical stabilities [1–5]. Over the past several decades, it is generally accepted that super-hard materials are those strong covalent compounds formed from light elements (B, C, N, and O), such as diamond [1], c-BN [2], $BC_2N$ [3], $B_6O$ [4] and $BC_5$ [5]. However, beryllium, as one of the lightest elements, has been often neglected

to synthesise ultra-incompressible materials, may be due to air sensitivity and require specialised high pressure equipment [6]. However, recent work has shown that proper treatment of beryllium in a glove box could help successfully in the synthesis of beryllium compounds [6-10]. Armenta et al. [7] and Kaner et al. [6] have noted that the beryllium with other light elements may form the alternative superhard materials. Recently, some theoretical indeed demonstrated that the ter- nary beryllium compounds ($\text{BeCN}_2$ [8], $\text{BeP}_2\text{N}_4$ [9], and $\text{B}_{12}\text{N}_2\text{Be}$ [10]) are potential superhard materials. Subsequently, another promising beryllium compounds material $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$, have attracted considerable attention as potential hard material.

$\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ was first thought to have the hexagonal $\beta$ phase (space group $P6_3/mmc$, No: 194) [11], which is predicted as potentially hard material with good cova- lence property [7,12]. The later researchers uncovered a cubic anti-bixbyte $\alpha$ phase (space group $Ia3$, No:206) [13]. It is stable under ambient conditions and $\alpha$-to-$\beta$-transition could be observed over $1400\ ^\circ\text{C}$ [11]. The $\alpha$-$\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ has attracted considerable interests for the optoelectronic light-emitting devices due to the widely direct band gaps. Gou et al. [14] predicted a superhard semiconducting phase of $\gamma$-$\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$. The calculated polycrystalline hardness is 51.5 GPa. Recently, Xia et al. [15] uncovered two novel polymorphs ($R3m$ and $P$-$3m1$) using the evolutionary algorithm. The calculated enthalpy difference indicates that the $R3m$ phase is much more stable than proposed $\gamma$-$\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$. The predicted $P$-$3m1$ phase becomes energetically preferable to $\beta$ phase above 76 GPa and becomes most stable above 118 GPa among all the phases. Moreover, the calculated Vickers hardness is 51 GPa for $R3m$ and 54 GPa for $P$-$3m1$, suggesting that both phases are superhard materials. Unfortunately, both phases are non-stable and presumed to exist under extreme conditions [15,16]. For the two experimentally known ambient-pressure phases ($\alpha$ and $\beta$), calculations of properties can be found in the literature [11-22], but no studies of their high-pressure behaviour have been reported. A study of high-pressure structural and mechanical properties is very important to deter- mine the response of the crystal to external forces, and can provide an insight into hardness of materials. Considering that both phases of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ are hard materials, it is important to calculate and compare the mechanical behaviour of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ in the two phases to understand the micro hardness of the material. Therefore, an explicit determination of its elastic and anisotropic behaviour as a function of pressure is essential, and will have important guidable significances to accelerate the synthesis of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$.

In this letter, we mainly focus on the structural stability, mechanical properties and anisotropic behaviours of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ in the two ambient-pressure phases. The elastic properties of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ under high pressure are investigated for the first time, from which the mechanical and anisotropic behaviour are also determined. The rest of this paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we give a brief description of the theoretical method. The results and some discussions of structural, mechanical and anisotropy behaviour of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ under high pressure are presented in Section 3. Conclusions derived from our calculations are drawn in Section 4.

## 2. Methods of calculations

The first-principles calculations are performed based on the density functional theory (DFT) in CASTEP code [23]. Non-local ultrasoft pseudopotential introduced by Vanderbilt [24] was employed for all ion–electron interactions. The structures were relaxed using the Broyden, Fletcher, Goldfarb and Shannon minimisation method algorithm [25]. To compare the performance of different approximations of exchange–correlation interaction, both the local-density approximation (LDA-CAPZ) proposed by Vosko et al. [26] and the generalised gradient approximation (GGA-PBE) proposed by Perdew et al. [27] were used to describe the exchange-correlation potentials. Pseudo-atom calculations are performed for Be: $2s^2$ and N: $2s^2 2p^3$. The electronic wave functions are expanded in a plane wave basis set with energy cut-off of 360 eV for cubic $\alpha$ phase and 400 eV for hexagonal $\beta$ phase, the Monkhorst-Pack $k$-point of $6 \times 6 \times 6$ and $10 \times 10 \times 4$ for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ phases, respectively. The values of the kinetic energy cut-off and the k-point density were determined to ensure the convergence of total energies to within 0.01%. In geometrical relaxations, the residual force is less than 0.001 eV/Å, the max displacement of atoms is less than $5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ Å and the convergence threshold for the maximum stress is limited to 0.02 GPa. All these parameters were tested to ensure that the self-consistent total energies converged to within $5.0 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/atom.

The elastic properties were calculated by strain–stress method, which has been also successfully applied in previous work [28,29]. The elastic constants for a given crystal should satisfy the generalised elastic stability criteria [30]. The bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $v$ have been estimated via the Voight-Reuss-Hill approximation [31].

Furthermore, to prove the mechanical anisotropy of $Be_3N_2$ compounds, linear moduli along $a$-axis $(B_a)$ and $c$-axis $(B_c)$ can be defined as:

$$
B_{a}=a \frac{d P}{d a}=\frac{\Lambda}{2+\alpha}, \quad B_{c}=c \frac{d P}{d c}=\frac{B_{a}}{\alpha} \tag{1}
$$

where $\Lambda=2\left(C_{11}+C_{12}\right)+4 C_{13} \alpha+C_{33} \alpha^{2} \quad \alpha=\frac{C_{11}+C_{12}-2 C_{13}}{C_{33}-C_{13}}$

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Ground-state structure and mechanical properties

As we all know, a reasonable crystal structure is important to benchmark the performance of the theoretical approach. One method to determine the accuracy of the calculations is to compare the calculated geometry parameters with those determined experimental data. In the present work, in order to obtain reliable equilibrium geometry parameters for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ phases of $Be_3N_2$, we first make the total energy electronic structure calculations with two different exchange-correlation functions (LDA and GGA). The ionic configurations and lattice vectors

of $Be_3N_2$ were fully optimised. The obtained equilibrium lattice parameters and bulk modulus for the two phases of $Be_3N_2$ (within LDA and GGA) are compiled in Table 1 together with the available experimental values and previous theoretical results for comparison. As in the usual case, the calculated lattice parameters are larger within GGA than those within LDA. For $\beta$ phase, the calculated lattice parameters 2.852 and 9.792 Å (for LDA) and 2.874 and 9.878 Å (for GGA) are in good agreement with the experimental data [11]. They match each other within 0.3 and 1.2% for $a$ and $c$ axes at LDA level; and 1 and 1.9% at GGA level, respectively. For $\alpha$ phase, the calculated lattice parameters 8.168 Å (for LDA) and 8.233Å (for GGA) are also in good agreement with the experimental results [13]. The mismatch of lattice parameter is within 0.2% (for LDA) and 1% (for GGA) comparing with experimental values. These good agreements show that the choice of pseudopotential and approximation are reasonable for the current study and the present calculations are reliable. In addition, referring to GGA calculations, the calculated negative values of the energy for the two phases of $Be_3N_2$ ($\alpha$ and $\beta$) indicates that they are thermodynamically stable. Moreover, the $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$ is lower in energy (-10,235.73 eV at 0 GPa and -10,210.18 eV at 80 GPa) than $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$ (-1334.35 eV at 0 GPa and -1328.22 eV at 80 GPa), which shows that the $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$ is more stable than $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$, even if the pressure up to 80 GPa.

In Table 1, we also listed the calculated (within LDA and GGA) bulk modulus ($B$) and shear modulus ($G$) of $Be_3N_2$. From Table 1, we can see that the $B$ and $G$ are agree well with the available theoretical values [14,15]. The calculated bulk modulus and shear modulus are comparable to those of commonly accepted hard materials $B_4C$ and $B_6O$ [32,33]. And the $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$ has a larger bulk and shear modulus than $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$, which suggests that $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$ have much better mechanical property than $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$. The paper also estimated the Vickers hardness ($H_V$) for the two phases of $Be_3N_2$ according to the recently proposed empirical model $H_V=2(k^2G)^{0.585}-3$ [34,35], which provides better reliability because $k$ responds to both the elasticity and plasticity of materials. Hard materials usually refer to materials with Vickers hardness greater than 20 GPa, while materials with Vickers hardness above 40 GPa are classified as superhard materials [36]. From Table 1, it can be seen that the Vickers hardness of $Be_3N_2$ in the two phases is higher than 20 GPa, but much lower than 40 GPa, suggesting that they are potential hard materials but not belong to the class of superhard materials. In addition, the hardness of $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$ is higher than that of $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$, indicating a lower compressibility of $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$.

The pressure dependence of the normalised parameters $a/a_0$, $c/c_0$ and volume $V/V_0$ (within LDA) as a function of pressure are exhibited in Figure 1(a) and (b). From Figure 1(a), it can be seen that the normalised parameters $a/a_0$ in $\beta$ phase is slightly larger than that in $\alpha$ phase, indicating that the $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$ in $a$-axis direction has a higher incompressibility compared to $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$. Meanwhile, the curves also present the compressibility of $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$ is higher along the $c$-axis than that along $a$-axis, which suggests that the $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$ is much easily compressed in $c$-axis

### 3.2. Elastic and mechanical properties under pressure

The elastic properties are important for the potential hard materials. They define the behaviour of a material that undergoes stress, deforms, and then recovers and returns to its original shape after stress ceases. We first calculated the zero-pressure elastic stiffness coefficients $(C_{ij})$ for the two structures, five for the hexagonal phase $(C_{11}, C_{33}, C_{44}, C_{12}, C_{13})$ and three $(C_{11}, C_{44}, C_{12})$ for the cubic phases of $Be_3N_2$ are determinate using stress-strain relation derived from the method described above. These results are also listed in Table 1. Clearly, our results are reasonable agreement with are the previous calculated values [15,16]. Moreover, the LDA results are larger than the GGA ones, which are consistent with the law of LDA underestimates the lattice constants and overestimates the elastic constants. Normally, the error in elasticity calculations based on the DFT is order of 10%, it is mainly due to the exchange-correlation approximation and the pseudopotential employed in calculations. Here, consider the typical error, the agreement of our calculation with previous theoretical values is seen to be quite good. It indicates that the current elastic calculations are accuracy and reliability.

According to the criteria of mechanical stability, for hexagonal crystal, leads to the following restrictions on the elastic constants: $C_{11} > C_{12}$, $C_{44} > 0$, $(C_{11}+2C_{12})C_{33} > 2C_{13}^2$. For cubic crystals the three generally accepted mechanical

requirement can be judged as: $C_{11}+2C_{12}>0$, $C_{44}>0$, $C_{11}-C_{12}>0$. Obviously, from the calculated values (within LDA and GGA) of $C_{ij}$ in Table 1, these restrictions are all satisfied for these two phases of $Be_3N_2$, implying that both phases of $Be_3N_2$ are mechanically stable at ambient condition.

In addition, the elastic constants $C_{11}$ and $C_{33}$ represent the $a$-direction and $c$-direction resistance to linear compression, respectively. The large $C_{11}$ (473/426 GPa for $\beta/\alpha$ phase within LDA) manifests that the $a$ axial direction is extremely stiff. It is just correspond with the above determined that $a$-axis direction is much more difficult compressed than $c$-direction. In addition, one should be aware that the values presented in Table 1 were obtained at 0 GPa, and that pressure effects will generally increase the elastic constants. Therefore, we expect the experimental data for $Be_3N_2$ at ambient condition to be smaller than those listed here.

Let's consider the elastic properties under pressure, the elastic constants of $Be_3N_2$ in the two phases varying with pressure are presented in Figure 2. Unfortunately, there are no literature data to compare with our predicted results for the pressure derivative of elastic properties of $Be_3N_2$ in the two phases. Therefore, our results can serve as a prediction for future studies. From Figure 2, we found that there is remarkable anisotropy in the diagonal elements $C_{ii}$ ($i=1,3,4$) of the elastic constant, which can be related to the crystal structure. Thus, it is easy to conclude that the $Be_3N_2$ is anisotropic upon compression in these two phases. In addition, the elastic constants $C_{ij}$ of both the phases increase monotonously with pressure. Especially, for the cubic $\alpha$ phase, the elastic constant $C_{11}$ varies largely under pressure as compared with the variations in $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$. The $C_{11}$ represent elasticity in

The elastic constants in a specimen subject to an external load can be defined in different ways, leading to different stability criteria. For hexagonal crystal, the mechanical stability leads to restrictions on the elastic constants under isotropic pressure as follows [37]:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\tilde{c}_{44}>0, \quad \tilde{c}_{11}>\left|\tilde{c}_{12}\right|, \\
\left(\tilde{c}_{22}+\tilde{c}_{33}-2 \tilde{c}_{23}\right)>0, \quad \tilde{c}_{33}\left(\tilde{c}_{11}+\tilde{c}_{22}\right)>2 \tilde{c}_{13},
\end{gathered} \tag{2}
$$

where $\tilde{c}_{\alpha \alpha}=C_{\alpha \alpha}-P(\alpha=1,3,4), \tilde{c}_{12}=C_{12}+P, \tilde{c}_{13}=C_{13}+P$.

The variations of $C_{44}$-$P$ in LDA as a function of pressure are shown in Figure 3, it is clear that the stability criteria are also satisfied for the two phases of $Be_3N_2$ up to at least 80 GPa, indicating that the crystal is mechanical stable in the studied pressure ranges.

That $B_{a}$ larger than $B_{c}$ reveals the compression along $a$-axis is much more difficult than that along $c$-axis in the hexagonal phases, which is consistent with the pressure dependence of the normalised parameters $a/a_{0}$ and $c/c_{0}$. Here, we can understand this phenomenon by the chemical bonding situations between the Be and N atoms in the $Be_{3}N_{2}$ compound. That is, when pressure increases, the atoms become closer and their interactions become stronger, which can explains the higher elastic properties when pressure enhanced, and plays a very important role in understanding the hard material. These obtained linear modules also implied the anisotropy of $Be_{3}N_{2}$ in both phases.

For the $\beta$-Be$_3$N$_2$, the anisotropic factor of compressional wave is obtained by the formula $\Delta P = C_{33}/C_{11}$, the anisotropies of wave polarised perpendicular to the basal plane $S_{1}$ and polarised basal plane $S_{2}$ are calculated:

$$
\Delta_{S1} = \frac{C_{11} + C_{33} - 2C_{13}}{4C_{44}}, \quad \Delta_{S2} = \frac{2C_{44}}{C_{11} - C_{12}} \tag{3}
$$

The calculated three-type anisotropic factors of elastic waves $\Delta_{P}$, $\Delta_{S1}$ and $\Delta_{S2}$ for these two phases of $Be_{3}N_{2}$ under pressures are listed in Table 3. It is easy to observe that $\Delta_{S1}$ increases with pressures enhanced, in which $\Delta_{S2}$ decreases with increasing pressure. However, the elastic wave $\Delta_{P}$ is relatively moderate. From Table 3, the compression wave $P$ and shear wave velocities $S$ are found to be strongly dependent on the propagation direction, suggesting that $\beta$-Be$_3$N$_2$ is elastically highly anisotropic in our considered pressure ranges.

For the $\alpha$-Be$_3$N$_2$, $\Delta_{S1}$and $\Delta_{S2}$ can be obtained by following relations:

$$
\Delta_{S1} = \frac{1}{\Delta_{S2}} = \frac{C_{11} - C_{12}}{2C_{44}} \tag{4}
$$

The obtained anisotropic factors $\Delta_{S1}$, $\Delta_{S2}$ and $A=(2C_{44}+C_{12})/C_{11}$ are also listed in Table 3. Remarkably, the cubic $Be_{3}N_{2}$ is also elastically anisotropic under pressure.

The anisotropic factors $\Delta_{S1}$ generally decreases with increasing pressure, which reveals that the anisotropy will strengthen as pressure increases. On the other hand, we also calculated the percentage elastic anisotropy factor for these two phases of $Be_3N_2$, which is defined as: $A_G=(G_V - G_R)/(G_V + G_R)$, the subscripts $V$ and $R$ represent the Voigt and Reuss approximations, respectively. In the expres- sion, a value of zero corresponds to the elastic isotropy and a value of 1 identifies the largest elastic anisotropy. From Table 3, for both structures of $Be_3N_2$, $A_G$ is not equal to 0 and increases with pressure increasing. It also can be concluded that the anisotropy of these two phases is strengthening with increasing pressure.

## 4. Conclusions

By first-principles calculations, we report the structural, mechanical and ani- sotropy behaviour of $Be_3N_2$ under high pressure. From our calculations, good agreement is obtained with the experimental and other theoretical studies. An analysis of the calculated results shows that $\beta$-$Be_3N_2$ is much easily compressed than $\alpha$-$Be_3N_2$. The superior mechanical properties indicate that the both phases of $Be_3N_2$ does not belong to the class of superhard materials, but they are potential hard material. By the elastic stability criteria, it is demonstrated that both phases of $Be_3N_2$ are mechanically stable and strongly pressure dependent in the studied range of pressure. In addition, we have predicted that both phase of $Be_3N_2$ are intrinsically brittleness, and strongly prone to ductility at high pressure. The effects of pressure on the behaviour of elastic anisotropic indicate that the anisotropy is strengthening with increasing pressure. To our best knowledge, this is the first theoretical work on this compound under high pressure. Therefore, our results can serve as a prediction for future study.
