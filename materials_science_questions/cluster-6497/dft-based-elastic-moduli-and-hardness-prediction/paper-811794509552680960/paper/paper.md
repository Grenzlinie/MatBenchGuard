The structural, electronic, and mechanical properties of 4d transition-metal mononitride, PdN, are investigated using the norm-conserving pseudopotentials within the local density approximation in the framework of the density-functional theory. We have considered four different crystal structures of PdN: (i) zinc-blende (ZB), (ii) rock-salt, (iii) cesium chloride, and (iv) wurtzite, and we have found the most stable structure to be ZB. The elastic constants, Zener anisotropy factor, Poisson's ratio, Kleinman parameter, Young's modulus, shear modulus, Debye temperature, longitudinal, transverse, and average sound velocities are calculated for the most stable structure. Charge distributions and density of states are reported to understand the bonding character in the stable phases. The obtained results are compared with the other available theoretical data.

## 1 Introduction
Transition-metal mononitrides are known as refractory compounds, and they have, relatively, high hardness, brittleness, melting point, and superconduct- ing transition temperature, and they also have interesting optical, electronic, catalytic, and magnetic properties [1]. Owing to these properties, they find wide technologic application areas, i.e., diffusion barriers, cutting tools, magnetic storage devices, generators, optical coatings, and hard wear-resistant coatings [2].

The structural, electronic, elastic, and vibrational properties of transition-metal mononitrides have been extensively studied [[3–17], and references therein]. Recently, NbN, HfN, ZrN [10], RuN [18], and MoN [19], which are members of the 4d-metal nitrogen group, have been synthesized with the different methods. PdN (not synthesized yet) has been, relatively, the subject of only a few recent theoretical works [15–17]. Guillermet et al. [15] have studied the cohesive properties in the rock-salt (RS) structure by using the linear-muffin-tin-orbitals (LMTOs) method for this compound. Crowhurst et al. [16] have described the synthesis of nitride of palladium using the laser-heated diamond-anvil cell for the pyrite (PdN₂) and baddeleyite structure. They have suggested that palladium nitride is isostructural with pyrite. De Paiva and Nogueira [17] have performed structural, electronic, and magnetic calculations; by using the full-potential linearized augmen- ted plane-wave method (FP-LAPW), on the 4d transition- metal nitrides including the PdN zinc-blende (ZB) structure. They have reported that PdN shows a metallic behavior in ZB structure.

However, the other physical properties of this hypothe- tical compound have received less or no attention. To the best of our knowledge, charge distributions and elastic properties, which are the important bulk properties for solids, have been considered neither theoretically nor experimentally for PdN so far. Consequently, the main purpose of this work is to provide some additional information to the existing data on the physical properties of PdN by using the ab initio total- energy calculations. We have considered four different crystal structures of PdN: (i) ZB (space group $F43m$), (ii) RS (space group $Fm3m$), (iii) cesium chloride (CsCl) (space group $Pm3m$), and (iv) wurtzite (WZ) (space group $P6_3mc$). In particular, we have estimated elastic constants, charge density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, anisotropy factor, Kleinman parameter, shear modulus, Lamè constants, sound velocities, and Debye temperature in the stable phase.

The layout of this paper is as follows: the method of calculation is given in Section 2. The results and overall conclusion are presented and discussed in Sections 3 and 4, respectively.

## 2 Method of calculation
The Siesta code [20, 21] was utilized in this study to calculate the energies and atomic forces. It solves the quantum-mechanical equations for the electrons with the density-functional approach in the local density approximation (LDA). For LDA, the exchange-correlation functional of Ceperley and Alder [22] as parameterized by Perdew and Zunger [23] was used. The interactions between electrons and core ions are simulated with separable Troullier and Martins [24] norm-conserving pseudopotentials. The basis set is based on the finite-range pseudoatomic orbitals (PAOs) of the Sankey-Niklewski type [25], generalized to include multiple-zeta decays.

We have generated atomic pseudopotentials separately for atoms, Pd and N by using the $5s^14d^9$ and $2s^22p^3$ atomic configurations, respectively. The cut-off radii are taken as 2.18 au for s, p, d, and f channels for Pd and 1.24 au for s, p, d, and f channels au for N. Relativistic effects are taken into account for Pd due to its heavy mass in the pseudopotential calculations.

Siesta calculates the self-consistent potential on a grid in real space. The fineness of this grid is determined in terms of an energy cut-off $E_c$ in analogy to the energy cut-off when the basis set involves plane waves. Here, by using a double-zeta plus polarization (DZP) orbitals basis and the cut-off energies between 100 and 400 Ry with various basis sets, we found its optimal value to be around 300 Ry. Atoms were allowed to relax until atomic forces were less than 0.04 eV/Å. For final converging; 256 $k$-points in ZB and RS structures, 405 $k$-points in CsCI structure, and 160 $k$-points in WZ structure were enough to obtain the converged total energies $\Delta E$ to about 1 meV/atom for the present compound.

## 3 Results and discussion
### 3.1 Structural and electronic properties
For PdN, four structures, which are RS, CsCl, ZB, and WZ, are considered. The bulk moduli and its pressure derivative have been computed by minimizing the crystal total energy for different values of the lattice constant by means of Murnaghan's equation of state (eos) [26] as in Fig. 1. The order of energetic stability for phases of PdN from low to high is: $\text{CsCI} < \text{RS} < \text{WZ} < \text{ZB}$. These inequalities show that the ZB phase possesses the lowest energy among the considered phases. The total energy difference between the WZ and ZB phases is 0.0201 Hartree (per atom). This small energy difference indicates that ZB phase can easily transform to WZ structure (metastable) for PdN.

The cohesive energy ($E_{\text{coh}}$) is known as a measure of the strength of the forces, which bind atoms together in the solid state. In this connection, the $E_{\text{coh}}$ of PdN is calculated. The $E_{\text{coh}}$ of a given phase is defined as the difference in the total energy of the constituent atoms at infinite separation and the total energy of that particular phase:

$$
E_{\text{coh}}^{\text{AB}} = \left[ E_{\text{atom}}^{\text{A}} + E_{\text{atom}}^{\text{B}} - E_{\text{total}}^{\text{AB}} \right], \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\text{total}}^{\text{AB}}$ is the total energy of the compound at the equilibrium lattice constant and $E_{\text{atom}}^{\text{A}}$ and $E_{\text{atom}}^{\text{B}}$ are the atomic energies of the pure constituent atoms. The energy calculations for both pure constituent atoms and compound have to be performed at the same level of accuracy so as to obtain a precise value for the $E_{\text{coh}}$ [27]. The energy of an isolated atom is calculated by considering a supercell containing an isolated atom. The $E_{\text{coh}}$ of the isolated atoms has been calculated by using the $3 \times 3 \times 3$ supercell. This is achieved by using the spin-dependent form of the functional, with atoms in the ground-state spin configuration. The present $E_{\text{coh}}$s are found to be 8.89, 11.90, 12.25, and 11.43 eV/atom of ZB, RS, CsCI, and WZ structure, respectively, and they are also listed in Table 1. Our $E_{\text{coh}}$ value for ZB structure agrees with the work by de Paiva and Nogueira [17].

Although it is not our main intention here to make detailed band-structure calculations, we have predicted the band structure for PdN in ZB structure along the high-symmetry directions in the first Brillouin zone from the calculated equilibrium lattice constant. It can be seen from Fig. 2 that there are three bands crossing the Fermi level and this structure shows a metallic character (no bandgap). The band profiles are in good agreement with the earlier work [17]. The total and partial density of states (TDOS and PDOS) corresponding to the band structures shown in Fig. 2 is also indicated in Fig. 3 along with the Fermi energy level. The position of the Fermi level is at 0 eV, and the lowest valence bands occur between about $-16$ and $-14$ eV and are essentially dominated by N-2s states. The other valence bands are essentially dominated by Pd-4d states. The 2p states of N atoms also contribute to the valence bands, but the values of densities of these states are quite small compared to Pd-4d. The energy region just above Fermi energy level is dominated by unoccupied Pd-4d states. These results indicate that there is a strong hybridization between the N states and Pd states.

To visualize the nature of the bond character, charge-density distributions in (100) plane for ZB structure are shown in Fig. 4. It can be seen that there is an increase of the electron density around the N atoms, and a decrease in the interstitial region between Pd atoms where the metal-metal bonds have formed. We note the difference of the electro negativities between Pd and N appears in the difference of the charge transfer. These imply that beside the strong covalent interaction, an ionic contribution to the bonding mechanism exists in the Pd–N system. Therefore, the bonds are an unusual mixture of metallic, covalent, and ionic bonding like some other transition-metal nitrides [12, 13].

### 3.2 Elastic properties
The elastic constants of solids provide a link between the mechanical and dynamical behavior of crystals, and give important information concerning the nature of the forces operating in solids. In particular, they provide information on the stability and stiffness of materials, and their *ab initio* calculation requires precise methods. Since the forces and the elastic constants are functions of the first-order and second-order derivatives of the potentials, their calculation will provide a further check on the accuracy of the calculation of forces in solids. The effect of pressure on the elastic constants is essential, especially for understanding interatomic interactions, mechanical stability, and phase-transition mechanisms. It also provides valuable data for developing interatomic potentials.

Here, to compute the elastic constants ($C_{ij}$), we have used the "volume-conserving" technique [28], as in our recent works [14, 29], and the findings are given in Table 2. Unfortunately, there are no other theoretical or experimental results to compare with the present work.

The traditional mechanical stability conditions on the elastic constants in cubic crystals are given as $(C_{11}-C_{12})>0$, $C_{11}>0$, $C_{44}>0$, $(C_{11}+2C_{12})>0$. Our results for the elastic constants in Table 2 satisfy these stability conditions for PdN in ZB structure.

The mechanical properties, such as Young's modulus, shear modulus, and Poisson's ratio are also important physical quantities, especially for engineering applications. Poisson’s ratio ($v$), isotropic shear modulus ($G$), Young’s modulus ($E$), and Lamè constants ($\lambda$, $\mu$), are often measured for polycrystalline materials when investigating their hardness. Physically, the first Lamè constant $\lambda$ represents the compressibility of the material, while the second Lamè constant $\mu$ reflects its shear stiffness [30]. These quantities are calculated in terms of the computed data using the following relations [30, 31]:

$$
A = \frac{2C_{44}}{C_{11}-C_{12}}, \tag{2}
$$

$$
v = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\left(B-\frac{2}{3}G\right)}{\left(B+\frac{1}{3}G\right)}\right], \tag{3}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{vE}{(1+v)(1-2v)}, \tag{4}
$$

$$
\mu = \frac{E}{2(1+v)}, \tag{5}
$$

and
$$
E=\frac{9 G B}{G+3 B}, \tag{6}
$$
where $G=(G_{\mathrm{V}}+G_{\mathrm{R}})/2$ is the isotropic shear modulus, $G_{\mathrm{V}}$ is Voigt's shear modulus corresponding to the upper bound of $G$ values, and $G_{\mathrm{R}}$ is Reuss's shear modulus corresponding to the lower bound of $G$ values; they can be written as:
$$
G_{\mathrm{V}}=\frac{C_{11}-C_{12}+3 C_{44}}{5}, \tag{7}
$$
and
$$
\frac{5}{G_{\mathrm{R}}}=\frac{4}{C_{11}-C_{12}}+\frac{3}{C_{44}}. \tag{8}
$$

Another important parameter is the Kleinman parameter ($\zeta$) that describes the relative positions of the cation and anion sublattices under volume-conserving strain distortions for which positions are not fixed by symmetry. We use the following relation [32] for this purpose:
$$
\zeta=\frac{C_{11}+8 C_{12}}{7 C_{11}+2 C_{12}}. \tag{9}
$$

The all-calculated mechanical properties for PdN are collected in Table 2. The material is stiffer if its Young's modulus is high. The calculated Young's modulus is low (47.16 GPa), and therefore, PdN is not a stiff material. According to the criterion in Refs. [30, 33], a material is brittle (ductility) if the $B/G$ ratio is less (higher) than 1.75. The present value of the $B/G$ is higher than 1.75 for PdN in ZB structure. Hence, this material will behave in a ductile manner. The calculated Zener anisotropy factor (0.22) implies that the PdN is an elastically anisotropic compound.

It is known that the isotropic shear modulus and bulk modulus are the measure of the hardness for a compound. The bulk modulus is a measure of the resistance to volume change by applied pressure, whereas the shear modulus is a measure of resistance to reversible deformations upon shear stress [30]. Therefore, the isotropic shear modulus is a better predictor of hardness than the bulk modulus. We thus conclude that the calculated value of isotropic shear modulus (16.16 GPa) supports the highly compressible nature of PdN in the ZB structure.

The Debye temperature is known as an important fundamental parameter closely related to many physical properties such as specific heat, elastic constants, and melting temperature. At low temperatures the vibrational excitations arise solely from acoustic vibrations. Hence, at low temperatures the Debye temperature calculated from elastic constants is the same as that determined from specific-heat measurements. We have calculated the Debye temperature, $\theta_{\mathrm{D}}$, from the elastic constants data using the average sound velocity, $v_{\mathrm{m}}$, by the following common relation given in Ref. [34]:
$$
\theta_{\mathrm{D}}=\frac{h}{k}\left[\frac{3 n}{4 \pi}\left(\frac{N_{\mathrm{A}} \rho}{M}\right)\right]^{1 / 3} v_{\mathrm{m}}, \tag{10}
$$
where $\hbar$ is Planck's constants, $k$ is Boltzmann's constant, $N_{\mathrm{A}}$ is Avogadro's number, $n$ is the number of atoms per formula unit, $M$ is the molecular mass per formula unit, $\rho(=M/V)$ is the density, and $v_{\mathrm{m}}$ is given [35] as:
$$
v_{\mathrm{m}}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{\mathrm{t}}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{1}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1 / 3}, \tag{11}
$$
where $v_{\mathrm{l}}$ and $v_{\mathrm{t}}$, are the longitudinal and transverse elastic wave velocity, respectively, which are obtained from Navier's equation [36]:
$$
v_{\mathrm{l}}=\sqrt{\frac{3 B+4 G}{3 \rho}}, \tag{12}
$$
and
$$
v_{\mathrm{t}}=\sqrt{\frac{G}{\rho}}. \tag{13}
$$

The calculated values of the longitudinal, transverse, and average sound velocities in the present formalism are given in Table 2 along with the Debye temperature. The calculated Debye temperature in ZB structure is lower than in RS structure for PdN.

## 4 Summary and conclusion
In this work, we have performed the first-principles total-energy calculations on the PdN in the RS, CsCl, ZB, and WZ structures. The ZB phase is the ground-state configuration, and the total energy for WZ structure is slightly higher (0.0201 Hartree (per atom)). We have revealed that the ZB phase of PdN is metallic in nature. Our results for elastic constants satisfy the traditional mechanical stability conditions in ZB structure. According to the $B/G>1.75$ criterion, PdN in ZB structure will behave in a ductile manner. Due to the lack of data on the considered physical properties, it is hoped that our results can serve as a reliable reference for future investigations.
