# Ab initio study of naphthalene and anthracene elastic properties

Tigran L. Prazyan $^{[1]}$
$^{[1]}$ E-mail: prazyan.tigran@yandex.ru

Yuri N. Zhuravlev $^{[2]}$
$^{[2]}$ E-mail: zhur@kemsu.ru
$^{[1,2]}$ Address: Kemerovo State University, 6 Krasnaya st., Kemerovo, 650000, Russian Federation

A study of the crystal structure, mechanical and acoustic properties of two $P2_1/a$ and $P2_1/c$ monoclinic phases of naphthalene and anthracene was performed using *ab initio* method of linear combination of atomic orbitals and basis sets: C_6-21G*, H_3-1p1G. The method was implemented in the CRYSTAL'14,'17 software package using electron density functional theory including van der Waals interactions (DFT-D2 and DFT-D3 (BJ)), in the local density approximation with VWN exchange-correlation potential, gradient approximation PBE exchange-correlation functional and B3LYP hybrid functional. The calculated elastic constants for the first time are in moderate agreement with experimental data. Bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, Young's modulus $E$ and the Poisson's ratio $\mu$, as well as acoustic properties (average velocity of sound $<v>$, the Debye temperature $\Theta_D$, and acoustic Grüneisen parameter $\gamma_a$) were obtained using the Voigt-Reuss-Hill model.

Keywords: Naphthalene; Anthracene; Density functional theory; First-principles calculation; Elastic constants; Acoustic properties.

### 1. Introduction

Coal is one of the most common minerals used in different spheres: from power industry to household chemicals. It consists of complex mixtures of organic and non-organic compounds [1].

Structure of coals is studied on both molecular and supramolecular levels. Molecular structure is a stable formation, united by chemical bonds, and is a carrier of coal chemical properties. Supramolecular structure is a spatial form, that macromolecules and their associates, united by physical forces, assume; it is responsible for physical properties.

It is well-known that coal comprises such elemental impurities as cycloalkanes (cyclic saturated hydrocarbons), aromatic heterocyclic compounds (comprise aromatic system, form a benzene ring), functional groups, bridged structures, alkyl radicals. Basic inorganic impurities are natural elements, intermetallic compounds, arsenides, sulphides, selenides, oxides, hydroxides, silicates, carbonates, vanadates, phosphates, tungstates, sulfates, nitrates, halides, amorphous minerals [2].

Due to the decrease of surface coal reserves, accompanied by the growing need for economic development in the energy sector, the intensity of coal mining, as well as mining depth, are constantly increasing. Accordingly, such accidents as rock explosion, coal and gas outburst, as well as sudden outburst of mine water are more likely to occur. At the same time different geological structure and various mining methods can certainly lead to stress concentration that affect mechanical properties of coal and may be a direct factor causing the accidents mentioned above. Therefore, a thorough study of coal mechanical properties is essential. Modulus of elasticity, or Young's modulus, and shear modulus are used to calculate deformations in underground mines. The modulus of elasticity can be experimentally competed using stress-strain relationship of a rock sample subjected to uniaxial compression. However, it is a complex and time-consuming experiment. The shear modulus of common coal is about 2.26 GPa, the Young's modulus is about 5.33 GPa [3]. Poisson's ratio is the ratio of the relative transverse contraction to the relative longitudinal extension. This coefficient depends not on the size of the body, but on the nature of the material which the sample is made from. Poisson's ratio and Young's modulus fully characterize elastic properties of an isotropic material. The Poisson's ratio of common coal is from 0.3 to 0.4 [4].

Hydrocarbon cyclic compounds, the simplest representatives of alkanes: naphthalene $\mathrm{C_{10}H_8}$ and anthracene $\mathrm{C_{14}H_{10}}$ [5, 6], are one of the most common structural units of different models of organic mass of coal.

The crystals of naphthalene $\mathrm{C_{10}H_8}$ belong to monoclinic crystal system ($P2_1/a$ space group) [7], contain two molecules in a unit cell. The authors [8] studied the $\mathrm{h_8}$ – naphthalene monocrystals using both X-ray and neutron diffraction methods in the temperature range from 5 to 295 K, and determined lattice parameters at different temperatures. It is shown that representation of internal modes by one second-rank tensor account for significant improvement, while further improvement is due to inclusion of the Grüneisen parameter to measure volumetric thermal expansion.

While studying another naphthalene phase with $P2_1/c$ space group, the authors tested a new approach in their work [9] where experimental lattice parameters at 80 K were determined by using a graphite monochromator and a sealed-tube source of scattered radiation. It is shown that anisotropic displacement parameters for hydrogen atoms, obtained from models, are similar or have better agreement with the results of neuron diffraction than those obtained by other methods. Naphthalene normal modes having been redetermined, the heat capacity is in reasonable agreement with the heat capacity obtained from calorimetric measurements with deviations at higher temperatures, which are indicative of anharmonicity.

Anthracene is a solid polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon with the following formula $C_{14}H_{10}$, consisting of three condensed benzene rings. This compound can have two states of monoclinic crystal system with $P2_1/a$ and $P2_1/c$ space groups. C. Brock and Dunitz [10] obtained single-crystal X-ray diffraction data for $P2_1/a$ symmetry phase, measured at six different temperatures from 94 to 295 K. This study emphasizes how the geometry of the molecule under investigation can limit the quality of description of rigid-body thermal motion which is determined by diffraction methods.

Crystal structures of anthracene of $P2_1/c$ phase were determined at 100 K in [11] using X-ray diffraction. The work studies the properties of mixed solutions formed from molecular compounds, which are a well-known class of multicomponent solids whose composition can vary. The authors examine the issue of obtaining and usefulness of such solutions by means of mechanical chemistry and solutions crystallization to study three-component mixed crystals.

The $P2_1/a$ phase (fig. 1a) is characterized by parallel orientation of naphthalene/anthracene molecules under consideration at the vertices of the crystal lattice. There are also parallel naphthalene/anthracene molecules at the center of the lattice sides formed by $a$ and $b$ planes at an angle to the molecules at the vertices.

In $P2_1/c$ phase (fig. 1b) there are also molecules in the vertices of the crystal lattice, however, other molecules parallel to each other are located on the planes formed by other sides: $b$ and $c$.

Molecules, lying in different planes in $P2_1/a$ phase, are oriented in such a way that in the case of compression along $a$ axis, these molecules in the crystal will "slide" relatively to each other having an angle between planes of $30\ ^{\circ}$. While unparalleled molecules in $P2_1/c$ phase in a cell are oriented at angle of planes of about $60\ ^{\circ}$, in case of compression along $c$ axis, it will lead to a larger overlap of molecular layers.

Elastic properties of naphthalene with $P2_1/a$ group symmetry were studied in a limited number of works. Complete data are presented in the works of Afanas'eva et al. [12] and Hamamsy et al. [13]. Hamamsy et al. in their neutron diffraction experiment determined the changes under pressure of lattice parameters of naphthalene single crystals. The set of elastic constants for naphthalene was calculated using experimental values of linear compressibilities. It was found that this set is in close agreement (except for $C_{11}$) with constants calculated by Pawley [14], who used Kitaigorodskii intermolecular potential.

There is a sufficient number of works [15-19], where elastic constants of anthracene of $P2_1/a$ phase were determined using different methods. Thus, the authors of [19] determined lattice parameters of anthracene crystal and elastic constants using direct method of Brillouin spectroscopy. The relationships between phonon velocities and lattice dynamics are discussed. A minimizing procedure for converting phonon velocities of low symmetry systems into elasticity coefficients is introduced. The procedure is shown to have several advantages over previous methods. The results are compared with those obtained by previous studies of anthracene elastic constants.

Fukuhara et al. [20] studied longitudinal and transverse wave velocities and acoustic wave. Anthracene single crystal samples of $P2_1/c$ phase, used in the study, were grown using the zone melting method. Semi- transparent single crystals were obtained. Using ultrasonic pulse method the authors accurately measured longitudinal and transverse wave velocities, $v_p$ and $v_s$, elastic parameters and internal friction values at pressure of 0.1 MPa from room temperature to 137 K. The authors observed abnormalities in transverse velocity, acoustic anisotropy coefficient, elastic parameters and internal friction at 266 K down to 197 K. These results indicate the possibility of phase transition type I/type II at 181 K caused by cooling and/or ultrasonic vibration.

It is noteworthy that there are not theoretical works on elastic constants due to the complex structure of coal and its elementary components of naphthalene and anthracene except for long-standing experimental works on elastic properties. That is why the purpose of the present work is to study mechanical properties of two structural phases of naphthalene ($C_{10}H_8$) and anthracene ($C_{14}H_{10}$) using DFT methods.

## 2. Computation method
Ab initio calculations of elastic constants were performed using CRYSTAL'14,'17 software package [21], method of linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO) and basis sets [22]: C_6-21G*, H_3-1p1G and electron density functional theory, in the local density approximation (LDA) with VWN exchange-correlation potential [23], generalized gradient approximation (GGA) exchange-correlation functional PBE [24] and hybrid functional B3LYP [25]. The van der Waals' forces were accounted according to the DFT-D2 [26] and DFT-D3 (BJ) [27] methods of Grimme. The sampling of the reciprocal space is carried out on a 4×4×4 k grid. Convergence in energy was no worse than $10^{-8}$ eV.

Full geometry optimization of crystals was implemented using Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno algorithm. It also included unit cell parameters optimization and atomic coordinates.

Optimized parameters were used for further calculation of elastic properties of the materials under investigation. Elastic constants $C_{ijkl}$ for $T = 0$ K are obtained when total crystal energy $E(V,\varepsilon)$ is expanded in Taylor's series according to small deformation value:

$$
E(V,\varepsilon)=E_{0}+\frac{V_{0}}{2} \sum C_{i j k l} \varepsilon_{i j} \varepsilon_{k l},
$$

where $E_0(V_0)$ is the total energy of an undeformed crystal with the equilibrium cell volume $V_0$; $\varepsilon_{ij} (i, j = 1$, 2, 3) is a deformation tensor. Since the deformation tensor is symmetric, the Voigt's designation $C_{ijkl} = C_{\alpha\beta}$. Standard procedure of the CRYSTAL'14,'17 software package was used to calculate elastic constants.

We used value averaging, obtained during single crystal calculation, known as Voigt-Reuss-Hill averaging procedure, to estimate the mechanical characteristics of polycrystalline materials. Voigt assumed homogeneous local deformation, while Reuss' arguments were based on the assumption of local stress. In case of random orientation of crystal, the first leads to average solid angle for bulk modulus of elasticity and shear modulus equations, while the second leads to a similar averaging of the elastic constants equations. Due to approximation

we can obtain asymptotically maximal (Voigt method) and minimal (Reuss method) values of the bulk modulus $B_V$, $B_R$ and shear modulus $G_V$, $G_R$. To calculate these moduli it is necessary to use elastic constants and formulae for particular crystal systems.

Voigt and Reuss approximations are two boundary conditions. Hill, based on the energy density analysis, proposed averaging of these values as being the values of moduli of elasticity and compression of polycrystalline materials:

$$
B=\frac{1}{2}\left(B_{V}+B_{R}\right), \tag{1}
$$

$$
G=\frac{1}{2}\left(G_{V}+G_{R}\right). \tag{2}
$$

Well-known formulae also in the Voigt-Reuss-Hill model are used to calculate Young's modulus E and Poisson ratio $\mu$:

$$
E=\frac{9 B G}{3 B+G}, \tag{3}
$$

$$
\mu=\frac{3 B-2 G}{2(3 B+G)}. \tag{4}
$$

The Vickers hardness number can be obtained from the equation suggested by Tian and Zhao in [28]:

$$
H=0.92(G / B)^{1.137} G^{0.708}. \tag{5}
$$

Acoustic properties can be determined from the calculated mechanical characteristics. Thus, transverse $v_{s}$ and longitudinal $v_{p}$ components of sound velocity [29] can be determined by using earlier obtained $B$ and $G$ moduli of elasticity:

$$
v_{s}=\left(\frac{G}{\rho}\right)^{1 / 2}, \tag{6}
$$

$$
v_{P}=\left(\frac{B+4 G / 3}{\rho}\right)^{1 / 2}. \tag{7}
$$

Average sound velocity:

$$
\langle v\rangle=\left(\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{S}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{P}^{3}}\right)\right)^{-1 / 3}, \tag{8}
$$

it is possible to calculate the Debye temperature at which all oscillations modes in the studied crystal are excited:

$$
\Theta_{D}=\frac{\eta}{k_{B}}\left(6 \pi^{2} n \cdot\left(\frac{N_{A} \rho}{M}\right)\right)^{1 / 3}\langle v\rangle, \tag{9}
$$

where $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant, $k_{B}$ is the Boltzmann constant, $n$ is the number of atoms in a formula unit, $N_{A}$ is the Avogadro's number, $\rho$ is the crystal density, $M$ is the molecular mass.

Elastic nonlinearity of condensed media and nonlinear acoustics are related via anharmonicity of interatomic (intermolecular) interaction. The Grüneisen parameter $\gamma$ is a measure of anharmonicity of the forces acting between atoms and molecules of solids [30]. Thus, the Grüneisen acoustic parameter can be calculated by the following formula:

$$
\gamma_{a}=\frac{9\left(v_{P}^{2}-4 v_{S}^{2} / 3\right)}{2\left(v_{P}^{2}+2 v_{S}^{2}\right)}. \tag{10}
$$

From Table 1 data follows that density functional theory for LDA gives an underestimated unit cell volume, while the PBE gradient approximation, as well as the B3LYP hybrid functional, on the contrary, overestimate the said volume. Taking into account van der Waals interactions in case of PBE-D2 and PBE-D3 to an underestimated unit cell volume as compared to the experiment. In this case the accent is focused at fact that averaging of the final calculated parameters with methods PBE and LDA without taking into account van der Waals forces (hereinafter (PBE+LDA)/2 or PL/2) will give a value close to the experimental one for the cell unit volume. And this will be valid also for elastic properties. This conclusion is valid for both phases of naphthalene and anthracene.

In the first-principle study of Ranglel et al. [31], the parameters of the unit cell for naphthalene and anthracene in the $P2_1/a$ phase were calculated using the PBE-D2 method. The results of their calculations ($a$=7.79 Å, $b$=5.79 Å, $c$=8.44 Å, $\beta$=125.3 ° and $V$=310.4 Å³ for naphthalene, and $a$=8.13 Å, $b$=5.85 Å, $c$=10.89 Å, $\beta$=126.5 ° and $V$=416.6 Å³ for anthracene) turned out to be less accurate than the results of the same method in the present study in comparison with the experimental data.

The analysis of interatomic distances shows that despite the fact that gradient approximation and hybrid functional with van der Waals forces gives worse lattice parameters than PL/2 method, characteristic interatomic C-H distances with using of the DFT-D2 scheme are closer to the experiment than those in the PL/2 method. Thus, four nonequivalent experimental values [8] of C-H distances for the $P2_1/a$ phase of naphthalene are 1.084/1.085/1.086/1.091, while for the PL/2 phase the distances equal 1.097/1.0975/1.098/1.098. When taking into account the intermolecular interaction forces the distances are as follows: for PBE-D2 – 1.092/1.093/1.0935/1.0945, for B3LYP-D2 – 1.082/1.0835/1.084/1.086. Consequently, the percentage error of calculation of the average of 4 nonequivalent C-H distances for (PBE+LDA)/2, PBE-D2 and B3LYP-D2 methods is -1, -0.6 and 0.2%, relatively. The percentage error in the $P2_1/c$ phase of naphthalene has a similar tendency: -1.3, -0.9 и -0.04% for the same methods, relatively. The following conclusion can be drawn from the obtained results: when van der Waals forces are taken into account the percentage error agaisnt the experimental data for both naphthalene phases is the smallest.

C-H distances for the $P2_1/a$ phase of anthracene for the methods that take into account intermolecular interaction are also closer to the experiment [10], than the PL/2 method. The percentage error for 5 nonequivalent average C-H distances for (PBE+LDA)/2, PBE-D2 and B3LYP-D2 is as follows: -12, -11.5 and -10.5%, respectively. The same pattern is true for the $P2_1/c$ phase of anthracene: : -15.5, -15 and -14% for the respective methods.

Comparison of nonequivalent C-C bonds in naphthalene and anthracene molecules produces similar result as for the C-H distances; in other words when the van der Waals forces are taken into account, the distances of intramolecular bonds are closer to the experiment as compared with the PL/2 method.

However, the trend is reverse for intermolecular distances: intermolecular distance (the distances between the nearest atoms of neighboring molecules) in crystals for the PL/2 method is closer to the experiment than that for the PBE and B3LYP methods with van der Waals forces. Thus, experimental value [8] of intermolecular distance in the $P2_1/a$ phase of naphthalene is 2.739 Å, while for the (PBE+LDA)/2, PBE-D2 and B3LYP-D2 methods it is 2.769/2.699/2.706 Å, respectively. Experimental value [9] of intermolecular distance in the $P2_1/c$ phase of naphthalene is 2.761 Å, for (PBE+LDA)/2, PBE-D2 and B3LYP-D2 it is 2.766/2.722/2.728 Å, respectively. The relation is similar for both phases of anthracene.

Therefore, constant lattices, unit cell volume and intermolecular distance are better described by (PBE+LDA)/2 rather than using DFT-D2 scheme, while it is better to take into account intermolecular interaction to describe characteristic intramolecular C-C and C-H distances.

As in case with geometry, local density approximation overestimates elastic constants, while gradient approximation PBE underestimates. Intermolecular interaction correction both for PBE methods and hybrid functional B3LYP produced overestimated values of elastic constants in comparison with experimental data. Thus, the experimental [12] value of the elastic constant $C_{11}$ for $P2_1/a$ phase of naphthalene is 9.80 GPa, for PL/2 is 13.43 GPa, for PBE-D2 is 18.4 GPa, for B3LYP-D2 is 21.4 GPa. The experimental [19] value of the elastic constant $C_{11}$ for $P2_1/c$ phase of anthracene is 9.17 GPa, for PL/2 is 11.49 GPa, the experimental [20] value of $C_{11}$ for the $P2_1/c$ phase of anthracene is 13.66 GPa, for (PBE+LDA)/2 method is 19.70 GPa.

Averaging of PBE and LDA methods gives the best values of elastic constants in comparison with the existing experimental data for naphthalene and anthracene. Hereof it follows that intermolecular, not intramolecular, distances are important for mechanical characteristics. Deviations from experimental data, first of all, can be due to the fact that experimental data both for naphthalene and anthracene were obtained at room temperature, while theoretical calculations are made at 0 K. A noticeable temperature effect on lattice parameters is shown in works [8,10]. However, it is worth noting that thermal motion can significantly effect on elastic constants. This assumption [11,32] can also account for differences between 0 K calculated elastic constants and experimental measurements.

Experimental research using X-ray powder diffraction to study naphthalene $P2_1/a$ phase and anthracene $P2_1/a$ phase under pressure [33,34] to obtain further equations of state exhibited the bulk modulus of elasticity is 7.9 GPa and 8.4 GPa, respectively. Meanwhile, the first principle study of the crystal structure and equation of state of naphthalene and anthracene [35] produced the following values for the bulk modulus of elasticity for the $P2_1/a$ phase of naphthalene and anthracene: 9.458 and 11.448 GPa, respectively.

The bulk modulus of elasticity $B$ is larger than the shear modulus $G$ for both phases of naphthalene and anthracene. It is commonly known that the relation of these two parameters can be used to determine ductility/brittleness of the material. Thus, if $G/B < 0.57$ [36], the less the relation, the more ductile the material is. The higher the relation, the more brittle the material is. From Table 3 it follows that both $P2_1/a$ and $P2_1/c$ phases of naphthalene and anthracene are ductile materials, what is more, naphthalene and anthracene are more ductile in the $P2_1/a$ phase. The maximum ductility anthracene has in the $P2_1/a$ phase ($G/B = 0.38$), while the minimum ductility is in the $P2_1/c$ phase of naphthalene ($G/B = 0.5$). One of the advantages of materials with high ductility is a low sensitivity to thermal shock, because the material is able to effectively dissipate thermal stresses through plastic deformations.

Comparing microhardness of the materials under investigation with other materials, it can be concluded that naphthalene and anthracene crystals exhibit considerably lower microhardness, comparable to microhardness of alkali metal halides (< 1 GPa) [37].

### 7. Conclusion
Elastic properties of two monoclinic phases $P2_1/a$ and $P2_1/c$ of naphthalene and anthracene were studied using first principle calculations implemented in CRYSTAL'14,'17 software package, linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO) and electron density functional theory both with and without taking into account intermolecular interactions.

The elastic constants obtained from first principle calculations are in satisfactory agreement with the existing experimental data.

The method of combining generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of the PBE exchange and correlation energy and local density approximation (LDA) with the VWN exchange-correlation potential was the closest to the experiment.

Bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, Young's modulus $E$ and the Poisson's ratio $\mu$ were calculated using the Voigt-Reuss-Hill model. It is shown that bulk modulus, shear modulus and Young's modulus tend to increase during the transition from the $P2_1/a$ phase to the $P2_1/c$ phase in naphthalene and anthracene, while the Poisson ratio tends to decrease. Bulk modulus, shear modulus and Young's modulus are higher for naphthalene than for anthracene. The bulk modulus $B$ is higher than the shear modulus $G$ for all the crystals under consideration. As it follows from the $G/B$ relation both phases of naphthalene and anthracene are ductile materials. The ductility of the crystals under investigation decrease from the $P2_1/a$ phase to the $P2_1/c$ phase.

From the obtained results it follows that the microhardness value for both phases of naphthalene and anthracene is the same as that of alkali metal halides.

Longitudinal and transverse components of sound velocity and the Debye temperature are higher for the $P2_1/a$ phase of naphthalene and anthracene, than for the $P2_1/c$ phase. At the same time acoustic Gruneisen parameter is on the contrary smaller for $P2_1/a$ than for $P2_1/c$.
