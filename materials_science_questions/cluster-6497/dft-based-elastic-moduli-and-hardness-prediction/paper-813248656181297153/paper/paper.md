# Compressibility of pyrochlore-type $MgZrSi_{2}O_{7}$ determined by in situ X-ray diffraction in a large-volume high pressure apparatus

Shuangmeng Zhai $^{a,*}$, Shuangming Shan $^{b}$, Daisuke Yamazaki $^{c}$ and Ken-ichi Funakoshi $^{d}$

$^{a}$ Key Laboratory of Orogenic Belts and Crustal Evolution, Ministry of Education; School of Earth and Space Sciences, Peking University, Beijing 100871, People's Republic of China; $^{b}$ Institute of Geochemistry, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Guiyang 550002, People's Republic of China; $^{c}$ Institute for Study of the Earth's Interior, Okayama University, Misasa, Tottori 682-0193, Japan; $^{d}$ Japan Synchrotron Radiation Research Institute, Sayo, Hyogo 679-5198, Japan

(Received 1 September 2012; final version received 14 November 2012)

The pyrochlore-type $MgZrSi_{2}O_{7}$ has been investigated by in situ synchrotron energy-dispersive X-ray diffraction measurements up 24.3 GPa at room temperature in a multianvil high pressure apparatus. The pressure has been calculated using an internal gold metal pressure calibrant. No phase transfor- mation was observed in this study. A third-order Birch-Murnaghan equation of state was used to fit the pressure-volume data. The fitting gives a volume of $V_{0}=802.8\pm 0.2\mathring{A}^{3}$, an isothermal bulk modulus of $K_{T}=249\pm 12$ GPa, and first pressure derivative of $K_{T}'=3.2\pm 1.3$. If $K_{T}'$ is fixed as 4, $K_{T}$ is obtained as $241.8\pm 2.6$ GPa. Compared with zirconate or titanate pyrochlores, $MgZrSi_{2}O_{7}$ shows a smaller compressibility due to a larger cation radii ratio $(r_{Mg,Zr}/r_{Si})$.

Keywords: pyrochlore; $MgZrSi_{2}O_{7}$; high pressure; X-ray diffraction; equation of state

## 1. Introduction

Pyrochlore, $A_{2}B_{2}O_{7}$, has been widely investigated due to its various properties for technological applications such as solid electrolytes in fuel cells [1-3], catalysts [4-6], dielectric materials [7-9], thermal barrier coatings [10-12], advanced nuclear waster encapsulating materials [13-15] and so on. The structure of pyrochlore (space group $Fd-3m$) is a superstructure based on the fluorite-type structure of cubic $ZrO_{2}$ but with two different cation sites and one-eighth fewer anions [16]. Generally, in pyrochlore-type $A_{2}B_{2}O_{7}$, a larger $A^{3+}$ ion occupies the 16c position coordinated by eight oxygens, a smaller $B^{4+}$ ion occupies the 16d position coordinated by six oxygens. Due to the structural flexibility, pyrochlore has a wide range of compositions. The pyrochlore structure is stable if the cation radii ratio $(r_{A}/r_{B})$ lies in the range 1.46-1.78 at ambient conditions, and a defect fluorite structure or a monoclinic structure is favored for a lower or higher $r_{A}/r_{B}$ value [16]. However, some pyrochlores with higher $r_{A}/r_{B}$ (>1.78) have been synthesized at high pressure [17-19], including two silicate pyrochlores $Sc_{2}Si_{2}O_{7}$ ($r_{A}/r_{B}=2.18$) and $In_{2}Si_{2}O_{7}$ ($r_{A}/r_{B}=2.30$).

Recently, a new silicate pyrochlore $MgZrSi_2O_7$ was synthesized at high pressure and high-temperature conditions, and its crystal structure was refined [20]. In the crystal structure (Figure 1), the A-site is occupied by both $Mg^{2+}$ and $Zr^{4+}$ in a random distribution, and the B-site is occupied by $Si^{4+}$, which gives a radius ratio $r_A/r_B$ about 2.16 ($r_{Zr}/r_{Si}=2.10$ and $r_{Mg}/r_{Si}=2.22$).

Some experimental or theoretical studies have been carried out to investigate the high pressure behaviors of pyrochlores [21–32]. In this paper, we investigate the compressibility of pyrochlore-type $MgZrSi_2O_7$ by *in situ* X-ray diffraction measurements at high pressure, using a multianvil high pressure apparatus combined with synchrotron radiation.

## 2. Experimental

The pyrochlore-type $MgZrSi_2O_7$ was synthesized at high pressure and high-temperature conditions, which was described by Xu et al. [20]. A mixture of $MgZrSi_2O_7$ plus about 10 wt% Au, the internal pressure marker, was prepared for later *in situ* X-ray measurements.

The high pressure energy-dispersive *in situ* X-ray diffraction experiment was carried out using a multianvil high pressure apparatus installed at a bending magnet beamline BL04B1 of SPring-8. The *in situ* experimental procedure was similar to a previous study [33]. Eight tungsten carbide cubes with edge length of 26 mm and truncated edge length of 3 mm were used as second-stage anvils. The sample was directly put into the hole of a octahedral pressure medium made of MgO + 5% $Cr_2O_3$ for compression. The diameter of the hole is 1 mm and the edge length of pressure medium is 7 mm. Pressure was calculated from the equation of state of gold proposed by Anderson et al. [34] and the volume determined by using (111), (200), (220), (311) and (222) diffraction lines of gold. Uncertainties in the pressure determination were mostly within $\pm$0.24 GPa.

## 3. Results and discussion

The powder X-ray diffraction pattern of $MgZrSi_2O_7$ at ambient conditions after decompression revealed that this phase has a cubic structure ($Fd$-$3m$), with unit-cell dimensions of $a = 9.2938(5)\,\mathring{A}$. The observed and calculated X-ray diffraction patterns of $MgZrSi_2O_7$ at ambient conditions after decompression are listed in Table 1. The observed X-ray diffraction pattern of $MgZrSi_2O_7$ at ambient conditions was collected after complete decompression. The volume of $MgZrSi_2O_7$ at ambient conditions is $802.8(2)\,\mathring{A}^3$. The unit-cell parameter and volume of $MgZrSi_2O_7$ determined in this study are comparable with those values reported by Xu et al. [20] (i.e. $a = 9.2883(1)\,\mathring{A}$, $V_0 = 801.32(1)\,\mathring{A}^3$).

The high pressure data were collected up to 24.3 GPa at the ambient temperature. Figure 2 shows typical X-ray diffraction patterns obtained in this study. In addition to the diffraction peaks from $MgZrSi_2O_7$, there were diffraction and fluorescence peaks arising from the internal pressure calibrant of gold, and diffraction peaks from the pressure medium. The main diffraction peaks of $MgZrSi_2O_7$ are distinguished, which ensures to determine the lattice parameters precisely. Though the X-ray patterns in the study were collected without annealing and the deviatoric stress existed during compression, the typical error in the pressure determination was within 0.2 GPa based on the volume calculated from lattice parameters determined using the different diffraction lines of gold. Therefore, a near hydrostatic situation is believed in this study.

The fitting yields $k_a$ and $k_a'$ in $1.24 \times 10^{-3}$ and $4.41 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{GPa}^{-2}$, respectively. And the volume compressibility, given by $k_V = 3\ k_a$, can be obtained as $3.72 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{GPa}^{-1}$. These values are smaller than those of $\text{Gd}_2\text{Zr}_2\text{O}_7$ and $\text{Nd}_2\text{Zr}_2\text{O}_7$ [30], and smaller values indicate smaller compressibility.

The pressure–volume data have been fitted to the third-order Birch–Murnaghan equation of state [35] to determine the elastic parameters:

$$
P=3 K_{T} f_{E}\left(1+2 f_{E}\right)^{5 / 2}\left[1+\frac{3}{2}\left(K_{T}^{\prime}-4\right) f_{E}\right],
$$

where $P$, $K_T$ and $K_T'$ are pressure, isothermal bulk modulus and the first pressure derivative of the isothermal bulk modulus, respectively. $f_\text{E}$, defined as $[(V_0/V)^{2/3}-1]/2$, is the Eulerian definition of finite strain. $V_0$ and $V$ are the zero-pressure volume and high pressure volume, respectively. The results from a least-quares fitting using an EosFit program [36] are $V_0 = 802.8(2)\ \text{Å}^3$, $K_T = 249\ (12)\ \text{GPa}$, respectively. No high pressure experimental study has been done on other silicate pyrochlore, but a first-principles calculation of $In_2Si_2O_7$ has been reported by Karazhanov et al. [32]. The calculation gives an isothermal bulk modulus of 261.34 GPa with a first derivative of the isothermal bulk modulus of 4.65, which are comparable with our experimental results of $MgZrSi_2O_7$. Some zirconate or titanate pyrochlores have been studied under high pressures by *in situ* X-ray diffraction, and the results were shown in Table 3. It seems that the compressibility of pyrochlore is related to the $r_\text{A}/r_\text{B}$ ration, and qualitatively a small $r_\text{A}/r_\text{B}$ value corresponds to a small isothermal bulk modulus. Compared with zirconate or titanate pyrochlores, $MgZrSi_2O_7$ with a high $r_\text{A}/r_\text{B}$ value shows a larger isothermal bulk modulus.

A phase transformation from the cubic to monoclinic structure was observed for some pyrochlores at high pressure [30,31]. It seems that the phase transformations of pyrochlores show a marked dependence on the ratio of cationic radii $r_{\mathrm{A}} / r_{\mathrm{B}}$, and a larger $r_{\mathrm{A}} / r_{\mathrm{B}}$ value corresponds to a higher pressure for the phase transition. In fact, no phase transition in $\mathrm{MgZrSi}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{7}$ was observed based on the X-ray diffraction patterns in this study. For the phase transformations of $\mathrm{MgZrSi}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{7}$ pyrochlore from cubic to monoclinic structure, a much higher pressure is needed due to a larger $r_{\mathrm{A}} / r_{\mathrm{B}}$ value.

## 4. Conclusions

High pressure in situ synchrotron X-ray diffraction measurements of pyrochlore-type $\mathrm{MgZrSi}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{7}$ have been carried out to 24.3 GPa at room temperature using a multianvil apparatus. No phase transformation occurs in the present study. A third-order Birch–Murnaghan equation of state was adopted to fit the pressure–volume data, yielding values of $V_{0}=802.8$ (2) $\AA^{3}$, $K_{T}=249$ (12) GPa, $K_{T}^{\prime}=3.2 \pm 1.3$. If $K_{T}^{\prime}$ is fixed as 4, $K_{T}$ is 241.8 (26) GPa. Compared with zirconate or titanate pyrochlores, $\mathrm{MgZrSi}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{7}$ shows a smaller compressibility.
