# Electronic structure of $Sc_2AC$ ($A=$Al, Ga, In, Tl)

Denis Music*, Zhimei Sun, Jochen M. Schneider

Materials Chemistry, RWTH-Aachen, Kopernikusstr. 16, D-52074 Aachen, Germany

Received 26 October 2004; accepted 24 November 2004 by H. Eschrig
Available online 10 December 2004

## Abstract

Using ab initio calculations, we have studied $Sc_2AC$ with $A=$Al, Ga, In and Tl. We show that C 2p and Sc 3d as well as A p and Sc 3d states are hybridized, but the antibonding states in the vicinity of the Fermi level weaken the overall bonding. In terms of the chemical bonding, the influence of the size of the A element is minute. Furthermore, the bulk modulus of the corresponding binary transition metal carbide is not conserved in these phases. Therefore, $Sc_2AC$ can be classified as weakly coupled MAX phases according to Sun and co-workers [Z. Sun, D. Music, R. Ahuja, S. Li, J.M. Schneider, Phys. Rev. B 70 (2004) 092102]. It is our ambition that these calculations will stimulate experimental research on these compounds.

We study $Sc_2AC$ (space group $P6_3/mmc$, prototype $Cr_2AlC$) with $A=$Al, Ga, In and Tl. These carbides belong to the so-called MAX phases, where M is an early transition metal, A is an A group element and X is C and/or N, exhibit a combination of properties usually associated with metals and ceramics [1]. Generally, they are good thermal and electrical conductors [1,2]. They exhibit high stiffness and demonstrate high strength at elevated temperatures [1,2]. Furthermore, they are relatively soft and machinable [1,2] as well as possess good thermal shock and oxidation resistance [1,3]. This rather unique property combination makes MAX phases very interesting. However, Sc-based MAX phases have not yet been explored.

In this work, we present theoretical results on $Sc_2AC$ with $A=$Al, Ga, In and Tl. It is our ambition to contribute towards understanding the correlation between electronic structure and elastic properties when systematically varying the size of the A element in $Sc_2AC$ by substituting Al by larger IIIA group elements. We show that these carbides may be classified as weakly coupled $M_2AC$ phases [4].

The computational investigation had the typical density functional theory approach [5]. We used the Vienna ab initio simulation program (VASP), wherein projector augmented wave potentials are employed [6,7]. The generalized-gradient approximation [8] was applied in all calculations, with the so-called Blöchl corrections for the total energy [9]. The integration in the Brillouin zone is done on special $k$-points determined after Monkhorst-Pack [10]. Unit cells [11] with eight atoms (two formula units of $Sc_2AC$ type) were studied on a mesh of $7×7×7$ irreducible $k$-points. The convergence criterion for the total energy $(E)$ was 0.01 meV within a 500 eV cut-off. Spin polarization was not considered due to only minute differences in $E$ as obtained in a previous work [12]. All structures were relaxed with respect to atomic positions, lattice parameter $a$, i.e. Wigner-Seitz primitive cell volume $(V)$, and their hexagonal $c/a$ ratio. The $E-V$ curves obtained were used to calculate bulk moduli by fitting them to the Birch-Murnaghan equation of states [13]. Fig. 1 outlines DOS for $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{AC}$ with $\mathrm{A}=$ Al, Ga, In and Tl. It is evident that these phases are similar, indicating resemblance in their chemical bonding. A relevant feature is the existence of a pseudogap in the DOS at about $-0.4$ eV, observed for all configurations probed. In order to understand its origin and explain the nature of chemical bonding in these phases, we study the PDOS of $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{InC}$ and $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{AlC}$, two demonstrator structures, given in Fig. 2. It is rather apparent that a covalent interaction occurs between the constituting elements due to the degeneracy with respect to both angular momentum and lattice site. C 2p and Sc 3d as well as A p and Sc 3d states are hybridized. To a lesser extent than Sc 3d, Sc 4p states contribute to this degeneracy as well. Due to the difference in electronegativity between the comprising elements, some ionic character is expected. The chemical bonding may, therefore, be covalent–ionic in nature, but may also, due to the d-resonance in the vicinity of the Fermi level, be partly metallic. The pseudogap, common to other $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{AC}$ phases studied (Fig. 1), is likely to split the bonding and antibonding orbitals, but due to this small misalignment of about 0.4 eV, antibonding states are filled at the Fermi level, weakening the overall bonding. This is consistent with previous studies on other $\mathrm{M}_{2}\mathrm{AC}$ phases [4,12,14,15]. Generally, the chemical bonding is similar in these two demonstrator structures with some minor differences in overlapping, which in turn may affect the elastic properties to some extent.

In a previous work [4], we have demonstrated that $\mathrm{M}_{2}\mathrm{A'C}$ phases ($\mathrm{M}=$ Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Mo, W and $\mathrm{A'}=$ Al, Ga, Ge, Sn) can be classified into two groups. One group comprises strongly coupled $\mathrm{M}_{2}\mathrm{A'C}$ phases in the $c$-direction (transition metals of groups VB and VIB), where bonding and properties of the binary MC is conserved, and the other group includes weakly coupled ones (transition metals of IVB group), where this is not observed. The difference in bonding between the $\mathrm{Ti}_{2}\mathrm{AlC}$ and $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}\mathrm{AlC}$ structures, representatives of the weakly and strongly coupled phases, respectively, are the weakly hybridized $\mathrm{A'}$ p–M d states which are very near to the Fermi level for $\mathrm{Ti}_{2}\mathrm{AlC}$ and around $-2$ eV for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}\mathrm{AlC}$. This indicates that the Ti–Al bond is weaker than the Cr–Al bond. We show these hybridized states in Fig. 3, together with the corresponding states in $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{AlC}$. As Cr is substituted with Ti and then with Sc, the antibonding states approach the Fermi level, weakening the transition metal–Al bonding. Thus, the $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{AC}$ phases can be classified as weakly coupled MAX phases.

Another indicator that $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{AC}$ phases are indeed weakly coupled MAX phases is a comparison between the calculated bulk moduli (Table 1) and the bulk modulus of the related binary carbide, i.e. ScC (space group $Fm\overline{3}m$, prototype NaCl [16]). The bulk modulus of ScC is herein calculated to be 154 GPa, while all $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}\mathrm{AC}$ phases exhibit the value in the range from 90–99 GPa, a 10% variation. Clearly, the bulk modulus of ScC is not conserved in these ternary carbides. In conclusion, we have carried out ab initio calculations for Sc₂AC with A = Al, Ga, In and Tl. We have shown that C 2p and Sc 3d as well as A p and Sc 3d states are hybridized, but the antibonding states onset at about $-0.4$ eV weakening the overall bonding. In terms of the chemical bonding, the influence of the size of the A element is small. Based on non conservation of the bulk modulus of the constituting ScC, these phases could be classified as weakly coupled MAX phases.
