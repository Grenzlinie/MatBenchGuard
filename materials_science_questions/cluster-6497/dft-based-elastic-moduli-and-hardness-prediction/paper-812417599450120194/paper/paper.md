# Brillouin scattering in single crystals of $\beta$-nitrogen:
## Determination of the elastic constants in the triple point*

H. Kiefte and M. J. Clouter

Department of Physics, Memorial University of Newfoundland, St. John's, Newfoundland, Canada A1C 5S7
(Received 10 October 1975)

The technique of high resolution Brillouin spectroscopy has been used to determine the adiabatic elastic constants of oriented single crystals of $\beta$-nitrogen at temperatures near the triple point (63.15 K). The values obtained were $c_{11}=1.8(25)$, $c_{12}=1.1(31)$, $c_{44}=0.32(0)$, $c_{33}=1.9(76)$, and $c_{13}=0.9(80)$ (in units of $10^{10}$ dyn $\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$) with an estimated uncertainty of $\pm 2 \%$. These results indicate that crystals of $\beta$-nitrogen are close to being elastically isotropic.

## INTRODUCTION

As far as is known solid nitrogen forms in three distinct phases: a cubic $\alpha$ phase, a hexagonal close-packed $\beta$ phase, and a tetragonal $\gamma$ phase. $^{1}$ The $\beta$ phase is formed at the triple point (63.15 K) and, under conditions of equilibrium with the vapor, exists down to a temperature of 35.6 K where a transition to the $\alpha$ phase takes place. The $\gamma$ phase occurs only at high pressures.

In the hexagonal $\beta$ phase, with which this paper is concerned, the molecules are more or less disordered $^{2,3}$ among the 24 general positions of the space group $P6_{3}/$ mmc such that the $c/a$ ratio is $1.63 .^{1,4}$ The work to be described consists of a comprehensive and accurate set of experiments wherein the technique of high resolution Brillouin spectroscopy was used with large single crystals of $\beta$ nitrogen whose orientations were found by x-ray diffraction methods. The results are in the form of elastic constants obtained from a detailed analysis of Brillouin frequency shifts which were measured at temperatures very close to the triple point.

## EXPERIMENTAL TECHNIQUE

The Brillouin scattering system has already been described in some detail in previous publications. $^{5,6}$ It consisted of the following basic components: (1) a single-mode $\mathrm{Ar}^{+}$ laser (Coherent radiation model 52) whose output at 514.54 nm was frequency stabilized with respect to an iodine vapor cell; (2) a temperature-controlled, piezoelectrically scanned Fabry-Perot spectrometer which provided a working finesse from 60 to 70 over five or six orders; (3) a cooled ITT FW 130 photomultiplier tube; (4) a readout system consisting of a phase sensitive amplifier (Brower model 131), and an XY recorder. All spectra were recorded at an angle of $90^{\circ}$ to the incident (50 mw) laser beam which was polarized perpendicular to the scattering plane. Spectral free ranges of 2.937 and 9.677 GHz were used. Usually three complete spectral free ranges were scanned in about 30 min with an amplifier rise time of 3 s. A typical spectrum is shown in Fig. 1.

The solid nitrogen samples were formed in a commercial helium cryostat (Janis Research Co., model 10DT), the modified tail section of which has been described previously. $^{6}$ The rotatable quartz sample cell was of inner diamter 3 mm and length 2 cm, and has also been described in detail. $^{6}$ The temperature at the bottom of the cell was automatically controlled to within 0.05 K, and an adjustable temperature gradient could be established across the cell from top to bottom utilizing appropriate heaters and feedback circuitry. The laser beam was incident through the bottom of the cell and cryostat.

Single crystals of $\beta$ nitrogen were grown in a similar manner to single crystals of $\gamma$ oxygen. $^{6}$ Nitrogen gas of 99.9995% purity (Matheson gas products) was condensed in the cell until it was filled with liquid, The temperature of the cell was then slowly lowered, while maintaining a temperature gradient of about 1.0 K until it was filled with liquid. The temperature of the cell was then slowly lowered, while maintaining a temperature gradient of about 1.0 K until solid began to form on the bottom of the cell. After annealing the same sample of solid for about 1 h, transmission Laue photographs were taken to determine if the sample were a single crystal. If a good single crystal had been obtained it was further annealed and cooled to achieve a growth rate of about 1 mm per h. After the solid almost completely filled the cell it was thoroughly probed with the x-ray beam (of 2 mm diam) to ensure that it was a complete single crystal. ## ANALYSIS AND RESULTS

A typical Brillouin spectrum obtained from a single crystal of $\beta$ nitrogen at a temperature of about $63.0 \mathrm{~K}$ is shown in Fig. 1. Similar spectra were recorded for various orientations of each of four different crystals by rotating the crystal about the cell, or $z$, axis and the relevant data are presented in Table I.

The method by which the data of Table I were ana- lyzed to yield elastic constants is essentially similar to that used for analyzing the results of cubic $\gamma$ oxygen. $^{6}$ (See also Refs. 9-12). Whereas, by symmetry argu- ments, the number of independent elastic constants for a cubic crystal reduces to three $(c_{11}, c_{12}$ , and $c_{44})$ , for an arbitrary hexagonal crystal it reduces to five $(c_{11}$ , $c_{12}, c_{44}, c_{13}$ , and $c_{33})$ . However, it has been shown $^{13}$ that for a hexagonal close-packed crystal where $c / a$ (=1.63) is independent of pressure the elastic constants have the following relation:

$$c_{13}=c_{11}+c_{12}-c_{33}.$$

Since for $\beta$ nitrogen $c / a=1.63$ over a wide range of molar volumes, $^{1}$ the independent elastic constants in the present analysis were therefore taken to be $c_{11}, c_{12}$ , $c_{44}$ , and $c_{33}$ .

The dynamical equations for a crystal can be written as:

$$\left(\lambda_{i j}-\rho \Omega^{2} \delta_{i j}\right) u_{j}=0,$$

where $\rho$ is the density of the crystal, $\delta_{i j}$ is the Kronecker delta, and $u_{j}$ are the Cartesian coordinates of the dis placements. $\Omega_{\mu}(q)$ are the three mode frequencies which by convention are identified as the slow trans- verse mode $T_{1}(\mu=1)$ , the fast transverse mode $T_{2}$  $(\mu=2)$ , and the longitudinal mode $L(\mu=3)$ . That is, the Brillouin spectrum will in general consist of three doublets where the frequency shifts are functions of the direction of the acoustic wave vector $q$ in the crystal.

The eigenvalues of $\lambda$ for an arbitrary direction in a hexagonal crystal can be expressed in a closed form $^{14,15}$ 

$$\begin{aligned} & \rho v^{2}(L)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(c_{11}+c_{44}\right) \sin ^{2} \gamma+\left(c_{33}+c_{44}\right) \cos ^{2} \gamma+\Phi\right], \\ & \rho v^{2}\left(T_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{11}-c_{12}\right) \sin ^{2} \gamma+c_{44} \cos ^{2} \gamma, \\ & \rho v^{2}\left(T_{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(c_{11}+c_{44}\right) \sin ^{2} \gamma+\left(c_{33}+c_{44}\right) \cos ^{2} \gamma-\Phi\right], \end{aligned}\quad (1)$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} \Phi^{2}= & \left(c_{11}-c_{22}\right)^{2} \sin ^{4} \gamma+\left(c_{33}-c_{44}\right)^{2} \cos ^{4} \gamma+2 \sin ^{2} \gamma \cos ^{2} \gamma \\ & ×\left[\left(c_{11}-c_{44}\right)\left(c_{44}-c_{33}\right)+2\left(c_{11}+c_{12}+c_{44}-c_{33}\right)^{2}\right]. \end{aligned}$$

Here $\gamma$ is the angle between the wave vector and the c axis in the crystal; it is related to the Euler anglesby the expression:

$$\cos \gamma=-q_{y}(\text { lab }) \sin \Theta \cos \Phi+q_{z}(\text { lab }) \cos \Theta.$$

Also from the Brillouin equation

$$\Omega_{\mu}(\mathbf{q})=2 n / \lambda v_{\mu}(\mathbf{q}) \sin (\alpha / 2).\quad (2)$$

In Eq. (1) and (2), $v_{\mu}(q)$ is the acoustic mode velocity, $\rho$ is the density which was taken to be equal to $0.944 ~g$  $cm^{-3},^{16-19} n$ is the refractive index, $\lambda$ is the wavelength invacuo of the incident radiation, and $\alpha$ is the scatteringangle such that for $\alpha=90^{\circ}$ the direction cosines $q_{y}$  $=1 / \sqrt{2}$ and $q_{z}=-1 / \sqrt{2}$ (in the laboratory frame). The value of refractive index used was $n=1.235$ . This was calculated using the Lorentz-Lorenz relation in con- junction with the refractive index data at the appropriate optical frequency for liquid nitrogen given by Johns and Wilhelm. $^{20}$ Birefringence effects were considered to be negligible $^{4}(\sim 1$ in $10^{4})$ .

The analysis consisted essentially of a computerized least-squares procedure where the observed frequency shifts of the scattered light $[\Omega_{\mu}(q)]$ were fitted to Eq.(1) and (2), and $c_{11}, c_{12}, c_{44}$ , and $c_{33}$ were taken to be the variable parameters. Initially, there was an am- biguity associated with the identification of the observed transverse doublets; these doublets generally occurred close together (Fig. 2) and in some cases the correct assignment of $\mu=1$ or $\mu=2$ was not immediately ap parent. However a crystal-by-crystal analysis showed the identification to be as indicated in Table I.

A total of 46 frequency shift measurements were used which then provided for 42 degrees of freedom. The final "best-fit" elastic constants for $\beta$ nitrogen at $63.0 ~K$ are as follows (in units of $10^{10} dyn cm^{-2}$ ): $c_{11}$  $=1.8(25), c_{12}=1.1(31), c_{44}=0.32(0), c_{33}=1.9(76), c_{13}$  $=0.9(80) .$

Every single measured frequency shift was fitted to within $1 \%$ as is also evident in Fig. 2. The estimated absolute error on the values for the elastic constants

The adiabatic bulk modulus, given by the relation:
$$B_{S}=\left[2\left(c_{11}+c_{12}\right)-c_{33}\right] / 3,$$

DISCUSSION
In an isotropic hexagonal crystal the following rela- tions hold: $c_{11}=c_{33}$ and $c_{44}=\frac{1}{2}(c_{11}-c_{12})$ . From the pres ent results it can be seen that $c_{11}$ and $c_{33}$ are within $10 \%$ of each other and $\frac{1}{2}(c_{11}-c_{12})=0.347$ is also within $10 \%$ of $c_{44}$ . In addition it can be observed from Fig. 2 that the maximum range in $\Omega(L)$ is only $\sim 6 \%$ and in $\Omega(T_{2})$ is only $20 \%$ for all orientations of the crystal. Hence $\beta$ nitrogen at the triple point is close to being an isotropic crystal, and in this respect is somewhat similar to cubic $\gamma$ oxygen which was found to be elastically isotropic at the triple point. $^{6}$ 

A remark can be made with regard to the Cauchy relations. In a hexagonal close-packed crystal the rel- evant Cauchy relation is $c_{13}=c_{44}$ . It is noted, however, that $c_{13} \sim 3 c_{44}$ for $\beta$ nitrogen. This may be interpreted to indicate that the effective intermolecular forces are not central in nature, as also appears to be the casefor $\gamma$ oxygen. $^{6}$ 

It may well be that the heavier, simple diatomic sol- ids, especially those that are plastic crystals (such as $\gamma$ oxygen and $\beta$ nitrogen), at their triple points are close to being elastically isotropic. This is in direct contrast to the results for the heavier, simple mon- atomic solids (such as neon, argon, krypton, and xenon $^{9-11,22,23)}$ which, at the triple point, were found to be quite anisotropic and were found to approximately obey the Cauchy relations.

A considerable amount of theoretical work has been completed on the lattice dynamics and molecular dy-namics of solid nitrogen, especially on the cubic $\alpha$  phase (see, for example, Refs. 24-27). Calculations of the elastic constants of $\beta$ nitrogen are currently be ing carried out by V. V. Goldman and M. L. Klein. Preliminary results indicate reasonable agreement in the various elastic constant ratios $^{28}$ : The static struc ture contribution to the elastic constants accounts for a $c_{44}$ to $\frac{1}{2}(c_{11}-c_{12})$ ratio of 0.80, whereas the present experiment shows a ratio of 0. 92.

In addition, inelastic neutron scattering work on sol- id nitrogen has recently been completed by Kjems andDolling $^{29}$ and inelastic photon scattering (Raman effect) by Medina and Daniels $^{30}$ ; but no direct comparison can be made to this work because of the different experi- mental conditions involved. It should be pointed out, however, that the neutron scattering work on $\beta$ nitrogen, which was carried out at $37 ~K$ , shows a remarkable isotropy in the data, $^{31}$ and the ratios of the velocities in the three major crystal directions are in good agree- ment with the present experiment.

Comparison with preliminary experimental values for the elastic constants in hexagonal close-packedhydrogen $^{12}$ at the triple point $(c_{11}=0.395 \pm 0.044, c_{12}$ = 0. 150±0. 015, C44=0. 053±0. 030, C33=0. 441±0. 029, and $c_{13}=0.104 \pm 0.029$ , in units of $10^{10} dyn cm^{-2}$ ) reveals a similar trend in the constants and their ratios. How- ever, hexagonal close-packed hydrogen does not appear to be particularly isotropic.
