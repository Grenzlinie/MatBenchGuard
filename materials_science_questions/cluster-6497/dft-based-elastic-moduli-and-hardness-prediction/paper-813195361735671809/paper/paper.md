## ABSTRACT

The physical properties of Boron - group V compounds have been investigated systematically by using density functional theory, based on the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave method. Generalized gradient approximation is performed to calculate the structural and elastic properties of all studied Boron compounds. We have investigated the phase stability of Boron-V compounds in zinc-blend, rock-salt and wurtzite crystallographic phases. The structural properties such as the equilibrium lattice parameter, bulk modulus and its pressure derivative of Boron-V compounds have been calculated in three phases and compared with other theoretical and experimental works. The elastic properties of the studied compounds are only investigated in the most stable calculated phase. We have obtained Young's modulus, shear modulus, Poisson's ratio, anisotropy factor, Kleinmann parameter, wave velocities and melting temperature by the aid of the calculated elastic constants, which are of great technological importance to Boron related compounds. And, we have also found the Debye temperature of the compounds from the average wave velocity. Our results are in reasonable agreement with the available theoretical and experimental data. Detailed comparisons are made with the results obtained in previous theoretical studies and available measured values.

## 1. Introduction

III-V semiconductor compounds play an important role in developing new technologies due to some of their extreme physical properties [1-8]. Specifically, Boron compounds in the III-V semiconductor family have attracted increasing research interest in recent years. The Boron compounds have quite different behaviors in their electronic, elastic and structural properties from the other III-V compounds. Apparently their unusual properties, such as short bond lengths and high mechanical strength [9], wide or/even negative band-gap [10], band gap bowing [11], low ionicity [12,13], high melting point [14], and hardness [9], are related to the absence of the p electron in the core and the small core size of the B atom as shown in several studies [9,12,15].

Boron compounds are suitable for a variety of electronic and optoelectronics device applications. For instance, semiconductor optical amplifiers [16], diode lasers [17], and photo-detectors [18] are only a few of the devices for which they are used. These compounds are also used in industrial applications such as cutting-polishing tools and resistant coating because of their super hard properties alternatively to diamond and other super hard materials.

Because of the difficulties in synthesis of Boron compounds, theoretical methods have become one of the most active research fields for investigating the physical properties. In recent years, there has been some experimental [16-19] and lots of theoretical research [20-28] conducted on the physical properties of B-V compounds. Wang and Ye [28] have calculated the structural properties of B-V semiconductor compounds in Zinc-Blende (ZB) phase by using the first-principles methods based on the density functional theory (DFT) [29,30]. Talwar et al. [20] and Kanoun et al. [31] have investigated the structural and elastic properties of BN compounds in ZB phase. Ferhat and Zaoui [22] have performed a first-principles structural calculation for BBi at various phases. They have reported that the ZB phase is a more stable structure than the others for BBi. Deligoz et al. [21-24] have investigated the physical properties of BBi and BSb in ZB and Rock-Salt (NaCl) phases by using the first principles calculations. They have found that the ZB phase is the ground state of BBi and BSb compounds. The elasticity of B-V compounds in ZB and Wurtzite (WZ) phases are calculated by Wang and Ye [23] by using the ab-inito methods and volume conserving technique. However, the other physical properties of the Boron compound, such as Debye temperature, melting point, and wave velocities have been given less or no attention. Experimental Debye temperature, which is an important parameter in thermal phenomenon such as specific heat, and thermal conductivity, and melting point of Boron compounds have been reported for only BP and BAs by Kumar et al. [19].

In this study, as different from the others, we have presented the structural properties of the Boron-V family (BN, BP, BAs, BSb and BBi) in ZB, NaCl and WZ phases systematically, using the first-principles calculation method based on the DFT. On the other hand, we have calculated the elastic constants ($C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$) for Boron compounds in ZB phase which are found in the ground state structure for all studied compounds. We have also investigated other physical parameters such as Young's modulus ($Y$), shear modulus ($G$), Poisson's ratio ($v$), wave velocities, Debye temperature ($\theta_D$) and melting point ($T_m$) with the help of the calculated elastic constants in the increasing order of atomic number from N to Bi. Our calculated results of both structural and elastic parameters of Boron compounds fairly agree with other theoretical and experimental results.

The aim of this paper is to give a comparative and complementary study on the structural and elastic properties to other theoretical and experimental studies for Boron compounds, by employing the plane-wave pseudo potential method and the density functional theory. The rest of the paper is organized as follows: in Section 2, we briefly describe the theoretical method used in the present work. The results about the structural and elastic properties are presented in Section 3. A conclusion of the work will be given in Section 4.

## 2. Method of calculation

First principles calculations for different phases of Boron-V compounds are performed using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) [32] method as implemented in the WIEN2k [33] code within the framework of density functional theory (DFT). The exchange and correlation effects are treated using generalized gradient approach (GGA) based on Perdew et al [34]. To get the total energy convergence, we have expanded the Khon-Sham wave functions up to $R_{MT}K_{max}=7$ ($R_{MT}$ is the plane wave radii and $K_{max}$ is the maximum reciprocal lattice vector). Partial waves used inside atomic spheres have been expanded up to $l_{max}=10$. In charge density Fourier expansion, magnitude of the largest vector G is equal to $12\ \text{Ry}^{1/2}$ ($G_{max}=12\ \text{Ry}^{1/2}$). The cut-off energy for separating core from valence states is set to $-6$ Ry.

The standard special $k$-points technique of Monkhorst and Pack (MP) [35] is used for accurate Brillouin zone integrations. We have used $9\times9\times9$ MP meshes for ZB and NaCl phases, and $9\times9\times5$ for WZ phases. For ZB and NaCl phases 35 and for WZ phase 36 special $k$-points are taken in the irreducible wedge of the Brillouin zone.

In the current research, we have calculated the equilibrium lattice constant ($a_0$), the equilibrium volume ($V_0$), the bulk modulus ($B_0$) and first order pressure derivative of the bulk modulus ($B'$) at zero pressure. These parameters are determined from the minimization curves by fitting to the Murnaghan's equation of state [36].

For WZ phase of B-V compounds, the structural optimization is performed by calculating the total energy as a function of internal parameter ($u$), $c_0/a_0$ and volume, where $c_0$ is the unit cell height. The relative B-V compounds interatomic distance along the $c_0$ is not determined by the crystal lattice parameters. Rather it is described by the so-called internal parameter u. The u parameter is defined as the anion-cation bond length (also the nearest neighbor distance) divided by the $c_0$ lattice parameter. The wurtzite structure is a hexagonal close-packed lattice, comprising vertically oriented B-V units at the lattice sites. The basal plane lattice parameter (the edge length of the basal plane hexagon) is universally depicted by $a_0$ and the axial lattice parameter, perpendicular to the basal plane, is universally described by $c_0$. The internal parameter can be evaluated theoretically by calculating the total energy as a function of the $c$-axis B-V separation. We also optimized internal parameter in the WZ structure by fitting the total energy as a function of $u$. Finally, to calculate elastic constants, we have used the method developed recently by Thomas Charpin and integrated in the Wien2k package [37].

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Structural properties

In the existing inquiry, we have investigated total energy versus volume graphs of B-V compounds for ZB, NaCl and WZ phases as shown in Fig. 1. The ZB phase is found to be the ground state structure for all studied compounds. The total energies have been calculated to obtain structural parameters for the three phases which are summarized in Table 1. For ZB phase, the calculated lattice constants of BN, BP and BAs agree with experimental data [38-40], as shown in Table 1. And, for BSb and BBi, there is a small overestimation of the lattice parameters compared to the previous theoretical calculations [22,25]. It is observed that for all phases the calculated lattice constants of B-V compounds increase with increasing atomic number of the corresponding N, P, As, Sb, Bi atoms.

With reference to our results, the calculated Bulk modulus of B-V compounds in ZB phase show deviations between 0.8% and 5% with other theoretical [27,41,42] and experimental [38-40] values. From our calculations, it is clearly seen that the ZB and WZ phases have nearly the same bulk modulus value while NaCl phase has lower value for BN, BP and BAs. Therefore, we can expect that ZB and WZ phases should exhibit greater hardness than NaCl phase for these three materials. Furthermore, the calculated bulk modulus values for three studied phases decrease from BN to BBi, i.e. from lower to higher atomic number. This suggests that BN has the lowest compressibility while BBi has the highest compressibility in B-V compounds. Our calculated lattice parameters for NaCl phase display a small overestimation, whereas bulk modulus show a small underestimation compared to the other theoretical calculations [13,21,43-48] owing to the expected trend of GGA calculations.

The structural optimization in WZ phase of B-V compounds is performed by calculating the total energy as a function of the internal parameter ($u$), $c_0/a_0$ and volume. In an ideal wurtzite structure, the values of $c/a$ and $u$ are $(8/3)^{1/2}=1.633$ and 0.375, respectively. For our results, $c/a$ is between 1.392 and 1.653 and $u\approx0.38$ which is in an excellent agreement with the experimental value of 0.382 [40]. There is a good agreement with experimental [49] and theoretical [50-52] data for the calculated structural parameters of BN and BBi. However, in our knowledge, there is no experimental and theoretical value for structural properties of WZ phase of BP, BAs and BSb.

### 3.2. Elastic properties

The elastic constants are important parameters that define the properties of material that undergoes stress, deform and then recovers after returning to its original shape following cessation of stress. These constants are important in solids since they are closely related to various fundamental solid-state phenomena such as equations of state, and various parameters such as young modulus, Poisson ratio, and Debye temperature. In addition, the elastic constants can contribute information on the phase stability and the hardness of the calculated materials.

To obtain elastic constants of the B-V compounds for ZB phase, we have used the method developed by Charpin and integrated in WIEN2k code. For a cubic crystal, there are only three independent elastic constants, namely $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$. Therefore, we need three equations to determine elastic constants. tions are obtained by fitting the total energy of strained crystals to get the elastic constants. Three types of strain, the volume change, and volume conserved tetragonal and rhombohedral shear strains are considered. The first type involves calculating the elastic modulus $(C_{11}+C_{12})$, which are related to the bulk modulus:

$$
B=\frac{1}{3}\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right) \tag{3.1}
$$

The second type involves performing volume-conservative tetragonal strains. The application of this strain has an effect on the total energy from its unstrained value as follows:

$$
E(\epsilon)=6\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right) V \epsilon_{1}^{2}+O\left(\epsilon_{1}^{3}\right) \tag{3.2}
$$

where $V$ is the volume of the unit cell, $\epsilon$ is the strain tensor and $O(\epsilon_{1}^{3})$ indicates that the neglected terms in the polynomial expansion are cubic and higher powers of the $\epsilon_{i}$. Finally, for the last type of deformation, we have used the rhombohedral strain tensor which transforms the total energy to:

$$
E(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{3}\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}+2 C_{44}\right) \epsilon_{1}^{2}+O\left(\epsilon_{1}^{3}\right) \tag{3.3}
$$

The fit curve for three strains namely the volume change, volume conserved tetragonal and rhombohedral shear strains, are plotted in Fig. 2 as a sample for BN compound. In Table 2 we have summarized the calculated elastic constants and shear wave modulus $C_{s}=1 / 2(C_{11}-C_{12})$ of B-V compounds for ZB phase, which is found the ground state structure for all studied compounds. The calculated elastic constant values of BN and BP are in better agreement with theoretical calculations [41,48] than experimental data [61,62] because of the relation of $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ with the bulk modulus (see Eq. (3.1)).

Besides, the elastic constant values of BAs, BSb and BBi are in agreement with previous theoretical results [17,52,53]. It is clearly observed from Table 2 that the magnitude of our calculated elastic constants decreases as one move downward in period V, i.e. from N to Bi. The mechanical stability of a cubic crystal is expressed in terms of elastic constants which should satisfy the following relations [64]:

$$
C_{11}-C_{12}>0, C_{44}>0, C_{11}+2 C_{12}>0 \text { and } C_{12}<B<C_{11} \tag{3.4}
$$

Our calculated elastic constants of all studied B-V compounds in ZB phase satisfy mechanical stability relation. This result shows that these compounds are elastically stable which means that B-V compounds are stable against elastic deformation.

We have also found the anisotropy factor [67] $A=2 C_{44} /$ $(C_{11}-C_{12})$. For an isotropic crystal, $A$ is equal to 1, while any value smaller or larger than 1 indicates anisotropy. The magnitude of the deviation from 1 is a measure of the degree of elastic anisotropy possessed by the crystal.

The isotropic bulk modulus $B$, which is related to $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ (see Eq. (3.1)), and shear modulus ($G$) are determined by the calculated elastic constant [68]. These quantities cannot, in general, be calculated directly from the elastic constants. Reuss and Angew found lower bounds for all lattices, while Voigt discovered upper bounds. Hill [69] showed that the Voigt and Reuss averages are limits and suggested that the actual effective moduli could be approximated by the arithmetic mean of the two bounds. That is mean, the isotropic shear modulus $G$ is equal to $G=1 / 2(G_{R}+G_{V})$, $G_{R}$ is shear modulus of Reuss and Angew corresponding to the lower bound of $G$ values, and $G_{V}$ is Voigt's shear modulus corresponding to the upper bound of $G$ values. The shear modulus $G$ can be calculated by the following equations:

$$
5 / G_{R}=4 /\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)+3 / C_{44} \tag{3.7}
$$

$$
G_{V}=\left(C_{11}-C_{12}+3 C_{44}\right) / 5 \tag{3.8}
$$

Once B and G are determined in terms of elastic constants, Young's modulus ($Y$) and Poisson's ratio ($v$), which is frequently measured for polycrystalline materials when investigating their hardness, can be found. Young's modulus is a measure of the stiffness of a given material, whereas Poisson's ratio is the ratio (when a sample is stretched) of the contraction or transverse strain to the extension or axial strain. These quantities are related to the bulk modulus and the shear modulus by the following equations [70]:

$$Y = 9GB/(G+3B) \tag{3.9}$$

$$v=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\left(B-\frac{2}{3}G\right)}{\left(B+\frac{1}{3}G\right)}\right] \tag{3.10}$$

The calculated anisotropy factor, shear modulus, Young modulus, Poisson's ratio and Kleinmann parameter of B-V compounds are given in Table 3. Our results fairly coincide with previous theoretical values [23-25],[43],[71-73]. We have found that the anisotropy factor is equal to 1.439, 1.529, 1.595, 1.839 and 1.920 for BN, BP, BAs, BSb and BBi, respectively. Therefore, the anisotropy becomes strong while going from BN to BBi. It is clearly observed from Table 3; the values of G and Y show an increasing stiffness of the studied materials from BBi to BN. The obtained values of $\zeta$ for the materials are between 0.35 and 0.5.

### 3.3. Debye temperature ($\theta_D$) and melting point ($T_m$)

The Debye temperature ($\theta_D$) which is a significant fundamental parameter closely related to many physical properties such as elastic constants, specific heat and melting point can be obtained from the average sound velocity ($v_m$) by the following classical relation [70]:

$$\theta_D=\frac{h}{k_B}\left[\frac{6\pi^2n}{V_0}\right]^{1/3}v_m \tag{3.11}$$

where $V_0$ is atomic volume and $v_m$ the average wave velocity in the polycrystalline material is approximately calculated from the following equation [70]:

$$v_m=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_t^3}+\frac{1}{v_l^3}\right)\right]^{-1/3} \tag{3.12}$$

## 4. Conclusions

We have presented first-principle calculations of structural and elastic properties of B-V (BN, BP, BAs, BSb and BBi) compounds. We have used the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method in the framework of the density-functional theory (DFT) with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). We have investigated phase stability of B-V compounds in three different phases namely Zinc-Blende (ZB), Rock-Salt (NaCl) and Wurtzite (WZ). We have found the ground state phase of B-V compounds as ZB. The structural parameters including equilibrium lattice constant, bulk modulus and its pressure derivative are calculated in ZB, NaCl and WZ phases for B-V compounds. Our calculated structural results are in good agreement with other experimental and theoretical works. Also, we have found the elastic constants $(C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44})$ for all studied materials in ZB phase. With the help of the calculated elastic constants, we have obtained Young's modulus $(Y)$, shear modulus $(G)$, Poisson's ratio $(v)$, wave velocities, Debye temperature $(\theta_D)$ and melting point $(T_m)$ in the increasing order of atomic number, i.e. from N to Bi. Our calculated physical parameters of B-V compounds fairly agree with previous theoretical and experimental data. In this work, some of our results estimated for the first time, on the physical properties of B-V compounds resemble to the other members of the same series. From this work, we expect that the obtained results will be tested, to confirm their reliability, in future with different theoretical and experimental studies.
