# Ab initio calculations of elastic modulus and electronic structures of cubic $CaZrO_{3}$

## Z.F. Hou*

Department of Physics, Fudan University, Shanghai 200433, PR China

Received 30 October 2007; received in revised form 10 January 2008; accepted 27 January 2008

### Abstract

Using the plane wave pseudopotential method within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), we have studied the elastic constants and electronic structures of $CaZrO_{3}$ crystallized in cubic perovskite structure. The independent elastic constants of cubic $CaZrO_{3}$, which are derived by calculating the strain-induced stresses, satisfy the requirement of mechanical stability and indicate cubic $CaZrO_{3}$ could be stable. It is found that cubic $CaZrO_{3}$ is an indirect insulator with the GGA energy band gap of 3.30 eV. The bottom of conduction bands of cubic $CaZrO_{3}$ is mainly contributed by Ca-3d states with some mixture of Zr-4d and Ca-4s states, while the top of valence bands is dominated by O-2p states. The Zr-O bond has a significant covalent character while the Ca-O bond is typically ionic.

## 1. Introduction

Calcium zirconate $(CaZrO_{3})$ is a ceramic oxide of the perovskite family structure with a high melting point $(2345^{\circ}C)$ [1], a relatively small thermal expansion coefficient $(6.5-8.5×10^{-6}\,^{\circ}C^{-1})$ [1], a relatively high strength (~300 MPa) [2], high chemical stability [3], and excellent corrosion resistance against alkali, earth alkali oxides, and basic slags [4]. Therefore $CaZrO_{3}$ is a good refractory ceramic for wide applications [4]. Calcium zirconate based ceramics are used in sensors and devices working at high temperatures to monitor oxygen, hydrogen, and water because of their high chemical stability and good ionic conductivity at elevated temperatures [3,5-7]. Because $CaZrO_{3}$ has a dielectric constant of around 30 and a temperature coefficient of dielectric constant of about $-30\,\text{ppm}\,^{\circ}\text{C}^{-1}$ [8], $CaZrO_{3}$ also has attracted considerable interest for capacitors or dielectric resonators microwave applications [8] and been a promising candidate for gate applications [9-11].

It is generally recognized that $CaZrO_{3}$ exists in two polymorphic forms: o-$CaZrO_{3}$ (orthorhombic phase at low temperature) and c-$CaZrO_{3}$ (cubic phase at high temperature). The transition temperature is estimated as about $2173\pm100$ K [12]. At low temperature and under ambient conditions, $CaZrO_{3}$ is an orthorhombic Pbnm perovskite consisting of slightly deformed $ZrO_{6}$ octahedra with Zr-O bond lengths ranging from 2.091(1) to 2.101(1) Å and O-Zr-O angles ranging from $88.0(1)^{\circ}$ to $99.0(1)^{\circ}$ [13]. At high temperature, $CaZrO_{3}$ has the ideal cubic perovskite structure with space group Pm3m (No. 221) where the Ca occupies the 1a sites at $(0,0,0)$, Zr 1b sites at $(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})$ and the O the 3d sites at $(\frac{1}{2},0,0)$. In this structure, there are six O atoms at the face centered position of each cubic unit cell forming a three-dimensional network of $O_{6}$ octahedron. Each Zr atom is placed in the body-centered cubic position surrounded by a $O_{6}$-octahedron cage. Interestingly, properties of orthorhombic $CaZrO_{3}$ have been extensively explored [3,8,9,14-17], but the structural, elastic, and electronic properties of cubic

## 2. Computational method

All calculations here on cubic $CaZrO_3$ were performed using the quantum ESPRESSO code [19], which is based on the density functional theory (DFT) [20]. The electronic exchange-correlation potential was calculated by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the scheme of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [21]. Electro- n-ion interaction was represented by the ultrasoft Vander- bilt pesudopotential [22]. The pseudopotential for the zirconium atom was generated in the valence electron configuration $4s^24p^64d^25s^25p^0$, that for the calcium atom in $3s^23p^64s^23d^0$, and that for the oxygen atom in $2s^22p^4$. Wave functions were expanded by the plane waves with kinetic energy up to 40 Ry. The $k$-point sampling in Brillouin zone (BZ) of cubic $CaZrO_3$ was treated by using the Monkhorst-Pack scheme [23] with a $12 \times 12 \times 12$ grid (i.e. 84 irreducible points in the first BZ correspondingly). The convergence of total energy with respect to the size of $k$-mesh and the energy cutoff of plane wave is carefully checked. If the size of $k$-mesh and the energy cutoff of plane wave increase up to $14 \times 14 \times 14$ and 50 Ry, respectively, the change of total energy is less than 1 mRy in both cases. During the calculation of density of states (DOS), a dense $k$-grid of $24 \times 24 \times 24$ is used, the total DOS is computed by the tetrahedron method [24], and the atomic-projected DOS is calculated by the Löwdin populations [25].

## 3. Results and discussions

The total energies of cubic $CaZrO_3$ are obtained as a function of volume. The data of total energy versus volume are fit to Murnaghan equation of state (EoS) [26] to yield the equilibrium lattice constant and other ground state properties. The calculated equilibrium lattice constant ($a$), bulk modulus ($B$), and first pressure derivative of bulk modulus ($B'$) are listed in Table 1. In present GGA calculations, the lattice constant of cubic $CaZrO_3$ is predicted as $4.138\ Å$, which is in good agreement with the experimental value ($4.020\ Å$) [27] and the other calculated result ($4.011\ Å$) [28,29] from atomic simulation using interatomic potentials, and the obtained bulk modulus is 154.9 GPa, which is close to the one of orthorhombic $CaZrO_3$ reported in experiment (154 GPa) [17]. Because no available experimental value of bulk modulus of cubic $CaZrO_3$ was reported, now we compare it with those properties of cubic $BaZrO_3$ and $SrZrO_3$ compounds that have same structure with cubic $CaZrO_3$. The cubic phases of $BaZrO_3$ and $SrZrO_3$ compounds are stable above $1500{^\circ}C$ [30] and $1170{^\circ}C$ [31,32], respectively. The lattice constants of cubic $BaZrO_3$ and $SrZrO_3$ are measured as $4.192\ Å$[30] and $4.154\ Å$[32] (or $4.109\ Å$[33]), respectively, from their powder X-ray diffraction patterns. The bulk modulus cubic $BaZrO_3$ and $SrZrO_3$ are 127 GPa [30] and 150 GPa [34], respectively, reported in experiments. Regarding the ionic radius of Ca atom ($1.26\ Å$) is smaller than those of Sr and Ba atoms (1.40 and $1.56\ Å$, respectively) [35], we can expect the lattice constant of cubic $CaZrO_3$ should be smaller than that of $SrZrO_3$ and the bulk modulus of cubic $SrZrO_3$ also should be larger than that of cubic $SrZrO_3$. The predicted lattice constant and bulk modulus of cubic $CaZrO_3$ follow this trend.

In addition, elastic constants are directly related to the mechanical properties of materials. The cubic crystal has three independent elastic constants, $c_{11}$, $c_{12}$ and $c_{44}$. Table 2 presents our predicted independent elastic constants of cubic CaZrO₃ and the elastic modulus. The shear modulus $G$, bulk modulus $B$, and Young's modulus $E$ of cubic CaZrO₃ polycrystal are estimated in term of the Voight-Hill bounds [38,39] that are based on assumptions of uniform stress and strain distribution within the polycrystal: $G=(c_{11}-c_{12}+3c_{44})/5$, $B=\frac{1}{3}(c_{11}+2c_{12})$, and $E=9BG/(3B+G)$. From Table 2, we notice that the bulk modulus estimated from independent elastic constants is in good agreement with the one obtained from Murnaghan EoS fit. The value of $B/G$ ratio for cubic CaZrO₃ is 1.752, which is very close to the critical value (1.75) separating ductile and brittle materials [40]. For a cubic crystal, its mechanical stability requires that its three independent elastic constants should satisfy the following criterions [41,42]:

$$(c_{11}-c_{12})>0,\quad c_{11}>0,\quad c_{44}>0,\quad (c_{11}+2c_{12})>0,\tag{1}$$

and the bulk modulus $B$ also should satisfy a criterion [41,42]:

$$c_{12}<B<c_{11}.\tag{2}$$

From Table 2, we can see that the predicted values of elastic constants and bulk modulus of cubic CaZrO₃ all satisfy the above criterions (see Eqs. (1) and (2)). Therefore, CaZrO₃ can be stabilized in the cubic perovskite structure.

The calculated electronic band structure of cubic phase of CaZrO₃ is shown in Fig. 1(a). Cubic CaZrO₃ is found to have an indirect band gap with a value of 3.30 eV. This wide gap indicates the presence of an insulating feature in cubic CaZrO₃. The valence band maximum (VBM) occurs at $R$-point $(k=2\pi/a(111))$ in the BZ, whereas, the conduction band minimum (CBM) is at $\Gamma$-point $(k=(000))$ in the BZ, similar to those in cubic SrZrO₃ and BaZrO₃ [18]. From the total and atomic-projected electronic DOS shown in Figs. 1(b) and 2, respectively, we can see that the valence bands of cubic CaZrO₃ split into two groups. The lower group from $-18$ to $-15$ eV mainly comes from 2s states of oxygen atom with a negligible contribution of the valence electrons of Zr atom. The upper one approximately from $-5$ to 0 eV mostly consist of 2p states of oxygen atom and 4d states of Zr atom. Moreover, such hybridization in this bonding region suggests a covalent bonding contribution in cubic CaZrO₃. This hybridization was observed from the soft-X-ray emission spectroscopy of In-doped CaZrO₃ [16]. In particular, the peak of Zr-4d states centers at about $-4.0$ eV below the VBM. The top of valence bands is dominated by O-2p states, while the bottom of the conduction bands is dominated by Ca-3d states which hybridize with Zr-4d and Ca-4s states in cubic CaZrO₃. In order to further explore the bonding nature in cubic $CaZrO_3$, the charge distribution in cubic $CaZrO_3$ is examined. The contour map of charge density $\rho(r)$ in (110) plane containing calcium, zirconium, and oxygen atoms is shown in Fig. 3. In Fig. 3, the valence charge density around zirconium atom exhibits a slightly directional distribution toward oxygen atom and the value of the charge density between oxygen atom and zirconium atom is about 0.1 electrons per bohr$^3$, which is slightly larger than the ones (0.07 electrons per bohr$^3$) [18] in $BaZrO_3$ and $SrZrO_3$. Therefore, the Zr–O bond in cubic $CaZrO_3$ is quite similar to the one in $BaZrO_3$ and $SrZrO_3$ [18], and also has a significant covalent character due to a hybridization between Zr-4d and O-2p orbitals, which is also reflected in the atomic-projected DOS of cubic $CaZrO_3$ (see Fig. 2). The value between an oxygen atom and Ca atom is 0.015 electron per bohr$^3$. The Ca–O bond seems to have an ionic character, similar to the Sr–O bond in $SrZrO_3$ and the Ba–O bond in $BaZrO_3$ [18]. Moreover, the charge transfer occurs mainly from the other atomic species towards the oxygen atoms. These also can been seen from the atomic partial charge of each atom (Ca: 8.708, Zr: 11.558, and O: 6.544) obtained from the Löwdin populations.

## 4. Conclusions

Using the plane wave pseudopotential method within GGA, we have performed the first-principle calculations to study the elastic and electronic properties of cubic $CaZrO_3$. The ground-state properties including lattice constant and bulk modulus were predicted. Our results show that the high-temperature phase of $CaZrO_3$ could be stable in cubic structure and cubic $CaZrO_3$ has relatively high bulk modulus due to the strong covalent bonding between d-states of zirconium and p-states of oxygen atoms. In addition, cubic $CaZrO_3$ exhibits a wide and indirect band gap.
