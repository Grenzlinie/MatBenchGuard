# Pseudo alloy atom model for Cs-K alloys: binding energies, bulk moduli, and heats of solution

## Problem background
The pseudo alloy atom (PAA) model describes disordered substitutional alloys by replacing the random arrangement of different atoms with a hypothetical perfect monatomic crystal of “pseudo atoms.” Within second-order perturbation theory, using a weak pseudopotential, one can compute cohesive properties—total binding energy, bulk modulus, and heat of solution—from just the mean valency and the electron-sphere radius r_s of the alloy. This approach is applied to the Cs-K system, which forms a continuous solid solution over the full composition range. The goal is to implement the PAA model with the Ashcroft empty-core pseudopotential and to compute these properties as functions of the K concentration.

## Approach
The alloy is modeled as a monatomic crystal of pseudo atoms, each carrying the average valency Z_av = 1. The only adjustable parameter is the empty-core radius r_c^PAA of the pseudo atom, which is determined by requiring that the total energy satisfy the zero-pressure condition (dE/dr_s = 0) at the observed lattice spacing for each composition. The total energy per atom is the sum of the Madelung energy, the uniform electron gas energy, the first-order perturbation energy (proportional to (r_c^PAA)^2), and the band-structure (second-order) energy. The band-structure term involves a sum over non-zero reciprocal lattice vectors and uses the Ashcroft empty-core form factor (v_i^PAA(G) ∝ cos(G r_c^PAA)). The lattice parameters are assumed to follow Vegard’s law (linear interpolation between pure Cs at 6.045 Å and pure K at 5.225 Å at 0 K). Once r_c^PAA is known for a given composition, the total binding energy E, the bulk modulus B (from the volume derivative of E), and the heat of solution ΔH = E(x) − [(1−x)E(Cs) + xE(K)] are evaluated. The output is a CSV table for compositions x = 0.0, 0.1, …, 1.0.

## Model equations

All energies are in Rydberg and lengths in atomic units (bohr) unless noted.

- Lattice parameter by Vegard's law: a(x) = (1-x) a_Cs + x a_K, with a_Cs = 6.045 Å, a_K = 5.225 Å (0 K). Convert to bohr: 1 Å = 0.529177 bohr.
- Atomic volume Ω0 (bohr³ per atom) = a_bohr³ / 2 (bcc).
- Electron-sphere radius r_s = (3 Ω0 / (4π))^(1/3).
- Fermi wave vector k_F = (3π² / Ω0)^(1/3) (Z_av = 1), Fermi energy E_F = k_F².
- Madelung energy: E_M = −α / r_s, with Ewald constant α = 1.79186.
- Uniform electron gas energy (Nozières‑Pines interpolation):
  E_0 = 2.21 / r_s² − 0.916 / r_s − 0.115 + 0.031 ln(r_s).
- Free electron compressibility: K0 = 1.7 r_s⁵ (atomic units).
- First order perturbation energy: E_1 = 4π (r_c^PAA)² / Ω0.
- Band-structure energy:
  E_bs = Σ_{G≠0} [ χ(G) / ε(G) ] |v_i^PAA(G)|²,
  where the sum runs over all non-zero reciprocal lattice vectors G of the bcc lattice.
  For a monatomic crystal the structure factor |S(G)|² = 1.
  G = (2π / a_bohr) √(h² + k² + l²) with integers h,k,l such that h + k + l is even.
  In practice, include all vectors with h² + k² + l² ≤ 20.
- Ashcroft empty-core form factor:
  v_i^PAA(G) = − (8π / (Ω0 G²)) cos(G r_c^PAA).
- Lindhard polarisation function:
  χ(q) = −½ (2/3 E_F)⁻¹ [ ½ + (4k_F² − q²) / (8 q k_F) ln |(q + 2k_F)/(q − 2k_F)| ].
- Random phase approximation dielectric function:
  ε(q) = 1 − (16π / (Ω0 q²)) χ(q)   (f(q) = 0).
- Total binding energy per atom: E = E_M + E_0 + E_1 + E_bs.
- Determination of r_c^PAA: at the observed equilibrium r_s, the zero‑pressure condition dE/dr_s = 0 must hold. Solve numerically to obtain r_c^PAA for each x.
- Bulk modulus: B = 1 / (12π r_s) * d²E/dr_s² (evaluated at equilibrium r_s).
  This yields B in Ryd bohr⁻³. Convert to 10¹⁰ erg cm⁻³ by multiplying by 14710.
- Heat of solution: ΔH(x) = E(x) − [ (1−x) E(pure Cs) + x E(pure K) ], expressed in mRyd (1 mRyd = 0.001 Ryd).

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Compute electron-sphere radius and direct energy components
- Role: process
- Action: For compositions x=0.0,0.1,...,1.0, use the given 0 K lattice parameters (pure Cs a=6.045 Å, pure K a=5.225 Å) and Vegard’s law to obtain the lattice parameter a(x). Derive the electron-sphere radius r_s and atomic volume Ω0. Evaluate the Madelung energy E_M (Madelung term with Ewald constant 1.79186), the uniform electron gas energy E_0 (Nozières–Pines formula), and the free-electron compressibility K0 directly from r_s and the valency Z_av=1. Store these fixed intermediate values.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/r_s_energy.csv`

### Step 2: Determine pseudo alloy atom empty-core radius r_c^PAA
- Role: process
- Action: For each composition, solve the zero‑pressure condition dE/dr_s = 0 numerically at the observed r_s using the total energy expression of the Ashcroft empty‑core PAA model. The single adjustable parameter is the empty‑core radius r_c^PAA. Obtain its value for all x.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/r_c_values.csv`

### Step 3: Compute total binding energy, bulk modulus, and heat of solution
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: For each composition, use the fitted r_c^PAA to compute E1, Ebs, and the total binding energy E (E_M+E_0+E_1+E_bs). Compute the bulk modulus B from the second derivative of E with respect to r_s using the relation B = (1/(12π r_s)) * d²E/dr_s² (evaluated at the equilibrium r_s) and convert to 10¹⁰ erg cm⁻³ by multiplying by 14710. Compute the heat of solution ΔH = E(x) − [(1−x)E(pure Cs) + x E(pure K)] in mRyd. Output a CSV with columns x, r_c_PAA, E_total, B, delta_H.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/results.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: x (float), r_c_PAA (float, atomic units), E_total (float, Ryd/atom), B (float, 10^10 erg cm^−3), delta_H (float, mRyd). One row per composition, 11 rows total.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier
