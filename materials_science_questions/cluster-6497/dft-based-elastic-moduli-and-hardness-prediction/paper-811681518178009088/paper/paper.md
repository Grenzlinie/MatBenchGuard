**Abstract:** Among numerous different
$AB_2$ structures with the hypothetical
composition $FeN_2$, the structures lying
lowest in energy have been determined
by a series of density-functional elec-
tronic-structure calculations. The most
likely $FeN_2$ phase crystallizing in the
space group $R\overline{3}m$ must be considered
an iron pernitride incorporating binu-
clear N-N units $(d=1.275\,\mathring{A})$ with an
anionic charge of 2-. This high-pres-
sure magnetic phase with a bulk modu-
lus of about 192 GPa and an iron satu-
ration moment of approximately
$1.68\,\mu_B$ should already form at a pres-
sure of 17 GPa at an assumed reaction
temperature of 1000 K. Besides bond-
ing Fe-N interactions, antibonding N-
N and Fe-Fe interactions exist in the
crystal structure.

**Keywords:** density functional calcu-
lations $\cdot$ electronic structure $\cdot$ high-
pressure chemistry $\cdot$ iron $\cdot$ nitride

## Introduction

Iron nitrides were first studied in 1829 by Despretz.$^{[1]}$ Since
then, a large number of investigations has been carried out,
and the phase diagram of the binary Fe-N system has been
studied most intensively in the second half of the 20th cen-
tury;$^{[2]}$ the binary system is generally believed to be well es-
tablished up to a nitrogen ratio of $33\%\ (Fe_2N).^{[3]}$ Basically,
two close to daltonide compounds $(Fe_4N, Fe_8N)$ and one
berthollide phase $(Fe_3N)^{[4]}$ next to $Fe_2N$ are known to be
exothermic compounds and exhibit interesting characteris-
tics. Their crystal structures, magnetic behavior, material
properties, phase transitions, and stability ranges have been
described in great detail. For FeN, experimental studies$^{[5]}$ ar-
rived at crystalline powders or thin films with a composition
close to 1:1, giving an antiferromagnetic [ZnS]-type struc-
ture, in accordance with related theoretical investigations.$^{[6]}$
However, a number of other properties (e.g., the bulk mod-
ulus) of FeN remain unverified since single crystals are not
available.

Herein, we will suggest, for the first time, an iron-nitro-
gen compound, in which the nitrogen content exceeds the
iron content, namely $FeN_2$. Thus, this work has some overlap
with former contributions of ours, which dealt with the
aforementioned iron nitrides$^{[7-9]}$ as well as with the perni-
trides of the alkaline-earth and noble metals.$^{[10,11]}$ The perni-
trides themselves are fascinating species that may be divided
into two groups: one containing $N_2^{2-}$ and the other contain-
ing $N_2^{4-}$ units, and their physical properties will differ as a
function of the electron count, that is, depending on the
chemical formulation of $Fe^{4+}N_2^{4-}$ or $Fe^{2+}N_2^{2-}$ for the sim-
plest $FeN_2$ case.

With respect to the existence of iron pernitride, one also
needs to make sure whether $FeN_2$ could eventually form a
dinitride $Fe^{6+}(N^{3-})_2$ despite the fact that the formation of an
$Fe^{VI}$ nitride compound is quite unlikely, simply because of
the lower oxidizing power of nitrogen compared to oxygen
(which might, however, look different under high-pressure
conditions). In addition, there are only a few known
iron(VI) oxide compounds, as they are found only as
$M_2FeO_4$ with M being an alkaline metal$^{[12]}$ or in reactions
under drastic conditions (e.g., the chlorination of $Fe(OH)_3$
in concentrated alkaline lye).$^{[13]}$

different $AB_2$ structures with the composition $FeN_2$ were
theoretically investigated. To do so, both model structures
containing isolated nitrogen ions $(N^{3-})$ as well as those con-
taining dinuclear $N_2$ units were used. Figure 1 shows where
these hypothetical phases (see also Supporting Information
for more details) are located in the energy-volume phase
space, and one recognizes a large scatter over all structures.
It is immediately obvious, however, that almost all perni-
tride phases lie lower in energy than the dinitride phases,
and we first conclude that the energetical preference of the

To arrive at more practical (that is, finite-temperature) predictions, we chose to calculate the Gibbs free energy for a high-pressure synthesis at $T$=1000 K; other temperatures would have been possible as well. The introduction of a finite temperature will increase the needed pressure for such synthesis, and it may be determined in analogy to previous work.$^{[11]}$ The Gibbs energies of the phases FeN and $\text{FeN}_2$ were calculated by using density-functional phonon calculations$^{[16]}$ and thermochemical integrations,$^{[17]}$ while the Gibbs free energy of nitrogen under pressure and at a temperature of 1000 K was classically estimated [Eq. (1)]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
G^{1000}(\mathrm{N}_{2})=U(\mathrm{N}_{2})-C_{p}^{1000}(\mathrm{N}_{2}) \times 1000 \mathrm{~K}+\Delta S^{1000}(\mathrm{N}_{2}) \times 1000 \mathrm{~K}
\end{aligned}
$$

The electronic energy of nitrogen is independent of the temperature, of course, whereas the second and third terms cause a significant deviation between enthalpy $H$ and Gibbs free energy $G$. The Gibbs free energy is first lowered by the heat capacity term $C_p\Delta T$;$^{[18]}$ on the order of 16 kJ for half a nitrogen molecule, but it is raised by the larger entropic term $T\Delta S$, which amounts to $114 \mathrm{~kJ}^{[18]}$ for half a molecule. In other words, high temperatures destabilize $\text{FeN}_2$ but stabilize molecular nitrogen, as expected. As a consequence, Figure 4 manifests that a higher pressure of at least 17 GPa (compared to 9 GPa at $T$=0 K) is needed for a successful synthesis of $\text{FeN}_2$ at an arbitrarily chosen temperature of 1000 K.

To our surprise, the bulk modulus of $\text{FeN}_2$ (192 GPa) turns out as being considerably larger than the highest one found for the alkaline-earth pernitrides ($B_0(\text{BeN}_2)$=95 GPa).$^{[10]}$ This is somewhat unexpected because $\text{FeN}_2$ contains the same pernitride unit $(\text{N=N})^{2-}$ as found in the beryllium compound, yet it is still softer than the noble-metal pernitrides, for example platinum pernitride, $\text{PtN}_2$=$\text{Pt}^{4+}$($\text{N-}$ $\text{N})^{4-}$, with a stiffer N–N single bond and a bulk modulus of $B_0$=265 GPa.$^{[11]}$ It is clear that the electron count of the $\text{N}_2^{2-}$ pernitride unit in $\text{FeN}_2$ can not be the sole origin of the large hardness that is close to those of the noble-metal pernitrides.

For a better understanding, the electronic structure was analyzed. The density of states diagram (Figure 5) shows that $\text{FeN}_2$ will be a metallic and spin-polarized system. A magnetic (very likely ferromagnetic) system results with a saturation moment of $1.68\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ per iron atom, a value that is close in magnitude to the aforementioned iron nitrides. With respect to more details of the electronic structure, the lowest-lying (but not shown) nitrogen $\sigma_p^*$ levels are found between $-15\ \text{eV}$ and $-11\ \text{eV}$. Above that, ranging from $-10.5\ \text{eV}$ up to $-5\ \text{eV}$, one finds the $\text{N-N-}\pi_p$ bonding levels. Next in energy, between $-5\ \text{eV}$ and $5\ \text{eV}$, the $\text{N-N-}\pi_p^*$ as well as the d levels of iron are located. The DOS diagram confirms that the spin polarization only involves the iron 3d orbitals in the range between $-5$ to $5\ \text{eV}$, and a closer look reveals that the ratio of the integrated occupied and unoccupied states for the majority ($\alpha$) spin channel is 3.8:1.4, whereas for the minority ($\beta$) spin channel it is 1.9:3.1. Thus, there are approximately 5.7 electrons (both spin-up and spin-down) at the iron atom, which is close to a formal $3\text{d}^6$ configuration (or $\text{Fe}^{2+}$). Nonetheless, this is a simplified notion because we also find itinerant electron density at the Fermi level, which is required for a metallic phase.

## Conclusion

For the first time a nitrogen-rich binary iron compound has been predicted on the basis of density-functional theory. We found that the phase lying lowest in energy will adopt the space group $R\overline{3}m$ and be endothermic compared to FeN and nitrogen by $60.9\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$. Nonetheless, the calculations also reveal that $FeN_2$ should be accessible at high pressures because of its small volume. For a chosen temperature of $1000\,\text{K}$, phonon calculations predict a synthesis pressure of more than $17\,\text{GPa}$. Given the length of the N–N bond and its quantum-chemical bond analysis, the electron count of the pernitride unit must be stated as $\text{N}_{2}{}^{2-}$, which translates into an iron oxidation state of $+2$. $FeN_2$ is a magnetic, likely ferromagnetic, material with a saturation moment of $1.68\,\mu_{\text{B}}$ per iron atom, a value that is familiar from the already known iron nitrides.

## Computational Section

The ab initio calculations were based on the plane-wave pseudopotential strategy by means of the computer program VASP (Vienna Ab Initio Package) $^{[19,20]}$ using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of PBE type $^{[21]}$ and the projector-augmented wave (PAW) method. $^{[22]}$ A cut-off energy of $500\,\text{eV}$ was chosen and a dense net of at least $100$ irreducible $k$-points per cell was used. All calculations were performed without any spin restriction. The different unit cells of $FeN_2$ were derived from well-known $\text{AB}_2$ structures such as $\text{ThC}_2$, $\text{CaC}_2$, and $\text{TiO}_2$; the composition was fixed to $FeN_2$. During the optimization process, the cells were allowed to change in volume and shape, and all atomic positions were allowed to relax. After finding the cell lowest in energy for each structure and composition, the volume was changed around the ambient pressure equilibrium value, and bulk moduli were obtained by fitting Murnaghan-type equations of state to the calculated energy-volume data. Theoretical phonon data were calculated by using the quasi-harmonic approximation by means of the FROPHO utility $^{[16]}$ together with VASP, and thermodynamical state functions were then generated by using a set of script programs $^{[17]}$ on the basis of the density-functional electronic structure. Chemical bonding analyses were carried out by means of the crystal orbital Hamilton population (COHP) method $^{[23]}$ implemented in the all-electron quasi-relativistic TB-LMTO-ASA program package. $^{[24]}$ Structural drawings were performed using Balls & Sticks. $^{[25]}$
