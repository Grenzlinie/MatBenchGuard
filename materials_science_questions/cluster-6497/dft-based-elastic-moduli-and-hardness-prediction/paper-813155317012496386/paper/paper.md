# Bulk Electron Concentration and Average Valence Bond Strength of Elements
By Seung-Am Cho*

The Bulk Electron Concentration (BEC), the number of net-bonding valence electrons per unit volume in $\mathrm{cm}^3$, $n_e$, of 59 solid substances are correlated with the bulk moduli of elasticity, $B$, for the elements of each Group of the Periodic Table separately on linear scale. All those elements except transition metals from Group IVA (Ti) through Group IB (Cu) there exists linear $B$-$n_e$ relations. For those transition metals there, however, exists symmetrical $B$-$n_e$ behaviors toward the Mn and Fe group metals respectively from those Ti and Cu groups. From the correlations the Average Valence Bond Strengths (AVBS) per electron of those 59 substances are derived. It is demonstrated from the correlations that the free electron theory of metals does not applicable to none of the elements in the Periodic Table, however, it would nearly applicable to the most simple Alkali Metals provided that the effective electron mass $m^*$ is appropriately assigned to each element of these metals. The empirical effective masses are calculated from the correlations.

(Received April 6, 1981)

## I. Introduction

The net-bonding electrons of elements have been a controversial subject in chemical physics of atoms, especially in the science of metals and alloys, since most of the elements are metals. Cho$^{(1)}$ has recently proposed the Bulk Electron Concentration (BEC), $n_e=N_e/V$, the number of net-bonding valence electrons, $N_e$, per unit volume in $\mathrm{cm}^3$ of solid substances. BEC is calculated from the atomic concentration $n_a=N_a/V$ multiplied by the net-bonding valence electrons per atom $Z=e/a$. In a previous paper$^{(1)}$, the bulk moduli $B$'s of the Hume-Rothery's Class-I metals$^{(2)}$ including the first long period transition metals were correlated with BEC on a log-log scale. The concept of the correlation was based on the fact that the plasma of the net-bonding valence electrons in a solid substance behaves like an electron liquid which acts as a bonding agent between contiguous ion cores and plays an important role in the cohesion of solid substance. In addition, the bulk modulus of elasticity of a substance has its origin in the existence and stability of interionic core bonding agent, which is thus considered to be the basic physical property that is closely related to the bulk electron concentration of the net-bonding valence electrons of a solid substance. In that correlation the data on $Z$ were from Engel-Brewer$^{(3)}$, Pauling$^{(4)}$ and Trost$^{(5)}$, from which the Engel-Brewer theory on the $Z$ appeared to give the prominent result. The BEC were thus correlated$^{(1)}$ with many other relevant physical properties. In the previous work$^{(1)}$ the linear correlation between the $B$ and $n_e$ of those metals was also approximately possible. From these results it was concluded$^{(6)}$ that the simple free electron theory of metals could not be applicable to predict the empirically observed relations between the $B$ and $n_e$ even for the simple Hume-Rothery's Class-I metals.

In the present paper, the correlation is extended to all the substances except the Groups VIIB and VIIIB, and rare-earth and transuranic elements. The extension of the present work is based on the fact that it was not convinced by correlations which have to rely on a log-log plot to produce linearity for a limited number of those metallic elements. ## II. Correlations
All the values necessary in the correlations appeared in this paper are presented in Table 1. Sources of the data are referenced in the same table.

Figure 1 represents the $B$-$n_e$ plot of all the elements mentioned on a log-log scale. This plot appears fairly linear and it menifests that there, indeed, exists certain physical relation between $B$ and BEC of these subs- tances. The similar plot on a linear scale also exhibits a somewhat linear correlation as is shown in Fig. 2. The exceptions are all the transition metals except the Sc- and Zn-groups, and the elements of Group VIB. In order to explore the $B$-$n_e$ behavior of each group-elements seperately the individual linear plots of each group-elements are made in Figs. 3 through 12. All the elements, except the transitions metals of Ti through Cu groups, are very well linearly correlated with different slopes (Figs. 3 through 10). The slopes of the $B$-$n_e$ plots of these elements are positive excepts those of Group VB and Group VIB elements, whose slopes are negative (Figs. 9 and 10). The $B$-$n_e$ plots of the transition metals except Sc and Zn groups are performed into two separate sets; one, Ti through Mn groups and the other, Fe through Cu gruops, of metals.
