### Abstract

The pressure dependence of the lattice parameters of the ternary layered carbide, $Ti_2SC$, was measured by using synchrotron radiation X-ray diffraction and a diamond anvil cell setup. The experiment was conducted at room temperature and no phase transformation was observed up to the maximum pressure of 47 GPa. The $a$ and $c$ lattice parameters at room condition are $3.216\mathring{A}$ and $11.22\mathring{A}$, respectively. The bulk modulus, calculated using the Birch-Murnaghan equation of state, is $191\pm3$ GPa, with a pressure derivative of $4.0\pm0.3$ and that obtained by our *ab initio* calculations is 183 GPa, with a pressure derivative of 4.1. Like the majority of the ternary layered carbides (MAX phases), compressibility along the $c$-axis was higher than that along the $a$-axis.

**Keywords**: $Ti_2SC$; High pressure; Diamond anvil cell; Bulk modulus

### 1. Introduction

The history of the ternary layered carbides traces long back when Nowotny and coworkers [1] synthesized a large number of these materials, which were called H- or Hagg phases. Most of these materials have a $M_2AX$ stoichiometry, where, M is an early transition metal, A is an A-group element and X is C or N. They are hexagonal with a space group of $P6_3/mmc$ [1,2]. After this pioneering work, these phases were essentially ignored until a decade ago, when Barsoum and El-Raghy, reported their work on $Ti_3SiC_2$. More compounds with different stochiometries were found afterwards and it was discovered that all these compounds belonged to the same family and were called $M_{n+1}AX_n$ (MAX) phases, where $n=1$, 2 or 3 and M, A and X have the same meaning, respectively, as mentioned above. Depending on the value of $n$ these compounds were further referred to as 211, 312 or 413 MAX phases [3].

These materials possess unusual, and sometimes unique, combinations of properties. On one hand, they behave as metals in terms of their machinability, electrical and thermal conduc-tivities, on the other hand they behave as ceramics in terms of their specific stiffnesses [4-7].

Onodera et al. [8] reported on the bulk moduli, $K_0$, of these phases. They showed that $K_0$ of $Ti_3SiC_2$ was quite high and that the compressibility along the $c$-axis was higher than that along the $a$-axis. Since then the bulk moduli of $Ti_3Si_{0.5}Ge_{0.5}C_2$ [9], $Zr_2InC$ [10], $Nb_2AsC$ [11], $Ti_2AlN$ [12], $Ti_3GeC_2$ [13], $Ti_4AlN_3$ [14] and $M_2AlC$ [15] ($M$=Ti, Cr, V, Nb, Ta) have been determined. In general all these phases have $K_0$ around $200\pm20$ GPa, except for $Cr_2AlC$ and $Zn_2InC$, and the compressibilities along the $a$-axis are higher than those along the $c$-axis, except for $Nb_2AsC$, $Nb_2AlC$ and $Cr_2AlC$. For the Ta-containing phases, the compressibilities in both directions are almost identical [15,16].

All the phases were stable up to pressures $\approx$50 GPa. If the pressure is not hydrostatic, however, a strain induced $\alpha$ to $\beta$ type phase transition is observed at 26.6 GPa for $Ti_3GeC_2$ [13]. A similar transition was theoretically predicted for $Ti_3SiC_2$ [17]

The carbo-sulfides, Ti₂SC and Zr₂SC, were synthesized in powder form by Kudielka and Rohde [19] and Nb₂SC was synthesized later by Beckman et al. [1]. More recently Sakamaki et al. [20] synthesized this compound and showed that it was a type II superconductor, with a transition temperature of $\approx 5\ ^\circ$C.

The purpose of this work is to study the compressibility of Ti₂SC and its structural stability at high pressures.

## 2. Experimental details

Polycrystalline samples of Ti₂SC were fabricated by hot pressing −325 mesh powders (3-ONE-2, Voorhees, NJ) at 1500 °C at ~45 MPa for 5 h. A pure single phase of Ti₂SC of 211 MAX phase was confirmed from the XRD analysis and the details can be found elsewhere [21].

The high pressure experiments were carried out in a Mao-Bell diamond anvil cell with diamonds of 300 µm culet size. A stainless steel gasket of thickness 250 µm was used for loading the sample. The gasket was pre-indented using the diamond anvils to a thickness of 60 µm and a hole of 125 µm was drilled. The finely ground sample was then carefully loaded between the aluminum foils that were 15 µm in initial thickness. The aluminum serves two purposes, first, it acts as a pressure transmitting medium, creating nearly hydrostatic conditions [22,23] and second, it serves as a pressure marker [24]. The experimental details have been described in previous work [9–15].

The experiment was conducted at room temperature at B2 station at CHESS Cornell University, Ithaca, NY, using angle dispersive X-ray diffraction. The monochromatic X-ray beam of wavelength 0.4959 Å was focused to a spot size of 35 µm. The diffraction patterns recorded on an image plate, were integrated using FIT2D software to obtain intensity versus $2\theta$ plot in the range of 1–35° $2\theta$. The pressure was determined using the equation of state of Al [25]. To obtain the cell parameters each peak was refined individually using a least squares method and then these were indexed assuming a $P6_3/mmc$ space group.

For the ab initio calculations, the Vienna simulation package (VASP) and project augmented wave (PAW) method was used. The calculation of optimized geometries and total energy were performed based on the density functional theory (DFT) within the GGA function. The following parameters were used for calculations: 0.01 eV relaxation convergence for ionic positions, 0.1 meV electronic relaxation convergence, energy cutoff of 500 eV, k-points grid of $7\times7\times1$, and the tetrahedron method with Blöchl corrections for the total energy. The calculated lattice parameters from ab initio calculations at ambient conditions were $a=3.21$ Å and $c=11.29$ Å, which agrees very well with our experimental data.

The unit cell of Ti₂SC is shown in Fig. 1. Fig. 2 shows the XRD pattern of the sample which was placed between the aluminum foils in the diamond anvil cell. The inset in Fig. 2 shows the XRD patterns at various pressures that are indicated in the figure. No peaks other than those of sample (*), aluminum (+) and iron gasket (♦) are present, except for two very low intensity unidentified peaks (♦) at higher angles. The (2 0 0) aluminum peak and (1 1 0) iron peak are very close together this results in the high intensity peak at around 14° $2\theta$ that is seen in Fig. 2. The unidentified peaks, however, disappeared after the pressure was increased and no extra peaks appeared in the patterns with increasing pressure, $P$, although, the peaks became broader and some of the peaks overlapped at high pressures, as seen in the inset of Fig. 2. There was a gradual decrease in the intensities of all the peaks as $P$ was increased. From the above observations it was concluded that there were no phase transitions up to the maximum pressure of 47 GPa. This result is not surprising and is consistent with all other MAX phases explored to date [8–16]. The diffraction peaks of Ti₂SC have a broader width than those of Al. The reason for this behavior could be due to the different nature of samples used; Ti₂SC was used as powder where as Al was used as foil.

The relative changes of lattice parameters with $P$ for Ti₂SC and Ti₂AlC [15] are shown in Fig. 3. The reasons for choosing the latter are discussed below. For Ti₂SC, the changes in $a/a_0$ are almost linear with $P$, while that of $c/c_0$ some what shows a quadratic relation to $P$. The unit cell is very large in ‘$c$’ direction as compared to the ‘$a$’ direction, almost three times. The atoms therefore have more freedom to move and rearrange in ‘$c$’ direction than in ‘$a$’ direction. Moreover, the symmetry restrictions allow only the ‘$z$’ co-ordinate of ‘Ti’ to be at general position and other’s to be at special positions. It is thus thought that because of these reasons the $c/c_0$ is showing the behavior mentioned above.

![](./images/812037688742903809_5.jpg)

Fig. 3. Pressure dependence of relative lattice parameters for Ti₂SC (right axis) and Ti₂AlC (left axis).

Least squared fitting of the results shown in Fig. 3 results in:
$$
\frac{a}{a_{0}}=1-\frac{0.00142 P}{P_{0}}+6.496 \times 10^{-6}\left(\frac{P}{P_{0}}\right)^{2}, \quad R^{2}=0.9993
\tag{1}
$$
and
$$
\frac{c}{c_{0}}=1-\frac{0.0022 P}{P_{0}}+1.95 \times 10^{-5}\left(\frac{P}{P_{0}}\right)^{2}, \quad R^{2}=0.9982
\tag{2}
$$
where $P_0$ is 1 GPa and $R$ is the correlation coefficient.

To determine the bulk modulus, $K_0$, the pressure–volume results, shown in Fig. 4 were fitted with the Birch–Murnaghan equation of state [27]:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P= & \frac{3}{2} K_{0}\left[\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}\right)^{-7 / 3}-\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}\right)^{-5 / 3}\right] \\
& \times\left\{1+\frac{3}{4}\left(K_{0}^{\prime}-4\right)\left[\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}\right)^{-2 / 3}-1\right]\right\}
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

![](./images/812037688742903809_6.jpg)

Fig. 4. Relative unit cell volume changes as a function of pressure for Ti₂SC (red) and Ti₂AlC (black). The line represents the Birch–Murnaghan fit to the data of Ti₂SC. (For interpretation of the references to colour in this figure legend, the reader is referred to the web version of the article.)

From this fit, $K_0$ is determined to be $191\pm4$ GPa, with a pressure derivative, $K_0'$, equal to $4.0\pm0.3$. The *ab initio* calculations yield a $K_0=183$ GPa, with a $K_0'$ of 4.1, which again are in excellent agreement with our experimental results.

Fig. 4 shows the variation of $V/V_0$ with pressure for $Ti_2SC$ with $Ti_2AlC$ as a comparison. The latter was chosen to explore the effect of changing the A-group element on compressibility of these phases. As shown in figure, the response is almost identical and the two $K_0$'s are, therefore, very similar ($K_0$ for $Ti_2AlC$ is $186\pm2$ GPa). This is somewhat surprising result given that the unit cell volume of $Ti_2SC$ is $\approx8.3\%$ smaller than that of $Ti_2AlC$. Nevertheless, this result is consistent with recent *ab initio* calculations [28], where $K_0$ does not change significantly when element A is replaced with those in rows 3 or 4. For example, $K_0$ for $V_2AlC$ is 215 GPa and 219 GPa for $V_2AsC$ [28]. However, changing of M-element in these compounds has a larger effect [15].

## 4. Conclusion
The 211 MAX phase $Ti_2SC$ was studies with synchrotron XRD method and the measured bulk module $K_0$ is $191\pm4$ GPa, with a $K_0'$ of $4.0\pm0.3$. These values compare well with our *ab initio* calculations ($K_0=183$ GPa with $K_0'$ of 4.1). The compressibility along the $c$-axis is greater than that along the $a$-axis. Replacing the A element in $Ti_2AlC$ by S to form $Ti_2SC$ did not change the bulk modulus significantly, which is in agreement with the recent observations mentioned above.
