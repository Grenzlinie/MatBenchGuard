# FIRST-PRINCIPLES INVESTIGATION OF $SnO_2$
## AT HIGH PRESSURE

F. EL HAJ HASSAN*, A. ALAEDDINE, M. ZOAETER and I. RACHIDI

Université Libanaise, Faculté des sciences (I), Laboratoire de Physique de Mat'eriaux,
El-hadath, Beirut, Lebanon
* hassan.f@ul.edu.lb

Received 24 February 2005

The ground state properties and the structural phase transformation of tin dioxide
($SnO_2$) have been investigated using first principle full potential-linearized augmented
plane wave (FP-LAPW) method within density functional theory (DFT). We used lo-
cal density approximation (LDA) and the generalized gradient approximation (GGA),
which are based on exchange-correlation energy optimization, to optimize the internal
parameters by relaxing the atomic positions in the force directions and to calculate the
total energy. For band structure calculations, we utilized both the Engel-Vosko's gen-
eralized gradient approximation (EVGGA), which optimizes the exchange-correlation
potential, and also GGA. From the obtained band structures, the electron (hole) valance
and conduction effective masses are deduced. For compressed volumes $SnO_2$ is shown
to undergo two structural phase transitions with increasing pressure from the rutile- to
the $CaCl_2$-type phase at 12.4 GPa and to a cubic phase, space group $pa\bar{3}$ at 22.1 GPa.
The calculated total energy allowed us to investigate several structural properties, in
particular, the equilibrium lattice constants, bulk modulus, cohesive energy, interatomic
distances and the angles between different atomic bonds. In addition, we discuss the
bonding parameter in term of charge density, which show the localization of charge
around the anion side.

Keywords: FP-LAPW; phase transition; cohesive energy; band structure; effective mass;
ionicity factor.

PACS numbers: 61.50.Ks, 71.15.Mb, 71.15.Nc, 71.20.-b

## 1. Introduction

The dioxide compounds have been attracted increasing research interest over the
past few years. Tin dioxide, also called cassiterite (rutile-structure $SnO_2$), is a wide-
band-gap semiconductor that has many uses in numerous technological applications,
such as gas-sensing devices, $^1$ high-efficiency solar cells, $^2$ and catalysis. $^3$ A deeper
knowledge of its properties would help improve the understanding of this compound
and stimulate the development of new applications.

*Corresponding author.

There have been many studies of the electronic and structural properties of
$SnO_2$ at equilibrium volume, both of experimental$^{4-9}$ and theoretical aspects of
bulk$^{10-13}$ as well as surface.$^{14}$ There have also several previous investigations of
$SnO_2$ under high pressure. The transition from the rutile- to $CaCl_2$-type structure
was observed recently for $SnO_2$ by using Raman Scattering Spectroscopy.$^{15}$ Pre-
vious high pressure X-ray diffraction investigation$^{16}$ has shown that rutile-type of
$SnO_2$ undergoes three structural phase transitions with increasing pressure from
its most stable tetragonal phase to orthorhombic $CaCl_2$-type phase at 11.8 GPa
under hydrostatic conditions and to a $\alpha$-PbO$_2$-type phase at 12 GPa under non
hydrostatic conditions; however, only a small amount of this last phase was ob-
tained. Both the $\alpha$-PbO$_2$ and $CaCl_2$-type phases transformed to a cubic modified
fluorite-type phase, space group $pa\overline{3}$ at 21 GPa.

To the best of our knowledge there are no theoretical works exploring the phase
transition of $SnO_2$ at high pressure, and no calculations based on the full potential-
linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method were performed for electronic
properties of this compound. Therefore, the purpose of this paper is to study the
structural properties of $SnO_2$ at equilibrium volume as well as under high pressure,
by using FP-LAPW method. This will be followed by a detailed presentation of the
band structure, effective mass and the density of states (DOS). We correlate the
electron charge density to the ionicity factor through an empirical formula$^{17}$ which
was successfully applied to other compounds.$^{18,19}$ The calculated total energy of
solids allowed us to investigate several properties and in particular the equilibrium
lattice parameters $c$, $b$ and $a$, $c/a$ and $b/a$ ratio, internal parameters, cohesive en-
ergy, bulk modulus, inter-atomic distances and the angles between different atomic
bonds. The paper is organized as follows: In Sec. 2, we describe the calculation
procedure. Results and discussion are presented in Sec. 3. The paper is concluded
in Sec. 4.

## 2. Method of Calculations

The calculations are performed using the scalar relativistic FP-LAPW approach
as implemented in WIEN2K$^{20}$ code within the framework of density functional
theory (DFT)$^{21}$ that has been shown to yield reliable results for the electronic
and structural properties of various solids. For structural properties the exchange-
correlation potential was calculated using, on one hand, the local density approxi-
mation (LDA),$^{22}$ on the other hand, the generalized gradient approximation (GGA)
in the form proposed by Perdew *et al.*$^{23}$ In addition, and for electronic properties
only, we also applied the Engel–Vosko’s generalized gradient (EVGGA)$^{24}$ scheme,
which optimizes the corresponding potential, for band structure calculations. In the
FP-LAPW method, the wavefunction, charge density and potential are expanded
by spherical harmonic functions inside non-overlapping spheres surrounding the
atomic sites (muffin-tin spheres) and by a plane waves basis set in the remaining
space of the unit cell (interstitial region). A mesh of 30, 36 and 24 special $k$-points for

rutile-type, $CaCl_2$-type and cubic, respectively, is taken in the whole Brillouin zone.
The maximum $l$ quantum number for the wave function expansion inside atomic
spheres is confined to $l_{\text{max}} = 10$. The plane wave cutoff of $K_{\text{max}} = 8.0/RMT$ ($R_{MT}$
is the smallest muffin-tin radius in the unit cell) is chosen for the expansion of the
wavefunctions in the interstitial region while the charge density is Fourier expanded
up to $G_{MAX} = 14$. $R_{MT}$ values for $SnO_2$ were assumed to be 2 and 1.7 a.u for
Sn and O, respectively. Both the plane wave cutoff and the number of $k$-points
are varied to ensure total energy convergence. In order to consider the relativistic
effects in our calculation, the electronic states were classified into two categories;
the core and valence states. The core states that are completely confined inside
the corresponding muffin-tin spheres were treated fully relativistic, while for the
valence states that are rather non-localized we used the scalar relativistic approach
that includes the mass velocity and Darwin s-shift but omits spin-orbit coupling.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Structural properties

Tin dioxide at ambient pressure crystallizes in the rutile structure which has a
tetragonal symmetry with the phase (face centred cubic), space group $P4_2/mnm$
($D_{4h}^{14}$, no. 136). It is characterized by the two lattice parameters $a$ and $c$ and the
internal parameter $u$. Its unit cell contains two tin and four oxygen atoms in the
following positions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sn :} \quad &0\ \ 0\ \ 0;\ \ 1/2\ \ \ \ \ \ 1/2\ \ \ \ \ \ 1/2 \\
\text{O :} \quad &\pm(u\ \ u\ \ 0;\ \ u+1/2\ \ 1/2-u\ \ 1/2)
\end{aligned}
$$

The rutile to $CaCl_2$-type structural transformation under high pressure was ob-
served in $SnO_2$. The symmetry of the $CaCl_2$-type structural is orthorhombic, space
group $Pnnm$ ($V_{2h}^{12}$, no. 58), which is only a slight distortion of the space group
$P4_2/mnm$. The four atoms are in the following positions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sn :} \quad &0\ \ 0\ \ 0;\ \ 1/2\ \ \ \ \ \ 1/2\ \ \ \ \ \ 1/2 \\
\text{O :} \quad &\pm(u\ \ v\ \ 0;\ \ u+1/2\ \ 1/2-v\ \ 1/2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Our calculations showed that $SnO_2$ undergoes a second order transition to cubic
phase, space group $pa\overline{3}$ (no. 205), its unit cell contains 8 atoms with the $Sn^{4+}$ and
$O^{2-}$ ions located at $(0\ \ 0\ \ 0)$ and $(u\ \ u\ \ u)$ respectively (see Fig. 1).

In this work, we analyzed both phase transitions using LDA with and without
GGA scheme. It is clearly seen that the atomic positions depend on the internal pa-
rameters $u$ and $v$. These parameters can be optimized by using the calculated forces

total energies were then calculated at several volumes around the equilibrium and
fitted to the Murnaghan's equation of state. $^{26}$ The $c/a$ and $b/a$ ratio have been opti-

The cohesive energy is defined as the difference between the energy of an iso- lated atom and the energy of the same atom in the solid. In order to obtain an accurate value for the cohesive energy, the energy calculations for both isolated atom and crystal, must be performed at the same level of accuracy. To fulfill such a requirement the energy of an isolated atom was calculated by considering a su- percell containing just one atom. The size of this supercell was chosen sufficiently large that the energy convergence with respect to the size of supercell was less than 0.001 Ry: a large cubic supercell with dimensions of 18 and 19 a.u. for O and Sn, respectively.

### 3.2. Electronic properties

The self consistent scalar relativistic band structures of $SnO_2$ were obtained at equilibrium volume as well as at high pressure within the GGA and EVGGA schemes. It is clearly seen that the band gap obtained by GGA is lower than the corresponding experimental and our EVGGA values. This underestimation of the band gap is mainly due to the fact that the simple forms GGA are not sufficiently flexible to accurately reproduce both exchange correlation energy and its charge derivative. Engel and Vosko, by considering this shortcoming, constructed a new functional form of GGA which was able to better reproduce the exchange potential at the expense of less agreement in exchange energy. This approach, EVGGA, yields a better band splitting and some other properties which mainly depend on the accuracy of exchange correlation potential. On the other hand, in this method, the quantities which depend on an accurate description of total energy such as equilibrium volumes and bulk modulus are in poor agreement with experiment. $^{30-33}$ A point worth mentioning is that the band structure calculated by GGA and EVGGA for $SnO_2$ are similar expect for the value of its band gap which is higher in EVGGA (see Table 5). Because of this similarity, only the band structures and total density of states (DOS) calculated within EVGGA are presented in Fig. 3. The conduction bands arise from "$s$"- and "$p$"-like states of Sn atom with their bottom located at the $\Gamma$ point in all the three structures. The lowest lying bands shown in Fig. 3 arise from the oxygen $s$ states. The upper valence bands that lie above these bands are mainly due to Sn $p$ and O $p$ states. $SnO_2$ was found to have direct gap at the $\Gamma$ point.

The bonding nature of the solids can be described accurately by using elec- tronic density plots, $^{34,35}$ that have been calculated in the context of first principles approach. The charge density in our work is derived from a high-converged wave function, hence the result is very reliable and it can then be used to study the bonding nature of the solid. We calculated the valence charge density distribution and difference density plot between crystalline and superposed atomic densities for $SnO_{2}$ compound at equilibrium volume of rutile-type structure in the $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 0\end{array})$ plane, as shown in Fig. 4. The charge densities presented allow us to analyze also the nature of the bonds in these compounds according to a classical chemical concept such as ionicity. While this concept is not very well founded, it is undoubtedly useful for classifying compounds with different chemical and physical properties into different categories. To obtain an estimate of the value of ionicity factor for SnO2 compound, we have followed an empirical formula.¹⁷ In this approach, the total area occupied by the valence charge density is divided into two parts with respect to the centre. $S_C$ is the area of the cation side and $S_A$ the area of the anion side, and the ionicity factor is written as:

$$
f_{i}=\left(\frac{S_{A}}{S_{A}+\lambda S_{c}}\right)^{\lambda} \tag{2}
$$

where $\lambda$ is a parameter separating the highly ionic elements from the weakly ionic ones. $\lambda=-1$ for elemental and III–V semiconductors, $\lambda=+1$ for II–VI and II–VII semiconductors.

The profiles obtained for $\ce{SnO2}$ compound at equilibrium are given in Fig. 5. The corresponding calculated ionicity factor using LDA and GGA scheme are 0.762 and 0.797, respectively. Although the ionicity factor of these compounds has so far, to the best of our knowledge, not been calculated, hence our results can be considered as the first prediction for this property. Therefore, our calculated values show that a strong localization of charge around the O atom. Such conclusion respects the order of the ionicity scale suggested by Phillips³⁶ for the other oxide compounds.

## 4. Concluding Remarks

We have applied a FP-LAPW method to study the structural and electronic properties of $SnO_2$ compound at equilibrium as well as at high pressure. The results are summarized as follows:

(i) $SnO_2$ is shown to undergo two structural phase transitions with increasing pressures. From the rutile to the $CaCl_2$-type phase at 12.4 GPa and to a cubic phase, space group $pa\overline{3}$ at 22.1 GPa. Our GGA calculated values are in good agreement with experimental results.

(ii) We calculated, for the first time, the bulk modulus, cohesive energies and the band structures of $SnO_2$ upon compression for both $CaCl_2$-type and cubic structures.

(iii) The bonding character has been discussed in terms of the charge density and shows a strong localization of charge around the anion. Our GGA calculations predicted the ionicity factor value of 0.797.

The reported calculations provide new structural and electronic results from first principles for tin dioxide. Hence, this study is part of large theoretical effort to explore the properties of this compound.
