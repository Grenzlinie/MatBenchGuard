# Self-consistent non-local pseudopotential calculations for the ground-state properties of $\alpha$-Sn

G P Srivastava
Physics Department, New University of Ulster, Coleraine, Northern Ireland BT52 1SA, UK

Received 4 October 1982

Abstract. We report a self-consistent non-local pseudopotential calculation for the electronic structure of $\alpha$-Sn. The method includes spin-orbit interactions. Based upon this method, we have applied the local-density-functional-momentum-space formalism of Ihm et al to cal- culate the total energy, bulk modulus and equilibrium lattice constant of this material. In contrast to our earlier results for the ground-state properties based on the self-consistent local pseudopotential method, the present calculations are found to be in better agreement with experiment.

## 1. Introduction

One of the most recent advances in the theory of solids is the ab initio calculation of structural properties via total energy. Calculations along these lines using the local-density-functional theorem of Hohenberg and Kohn (1964) and Kohn and Sham (1965) and the self-consistent pseudopotential method have recently been carried out for a few semiconducting materials (Ihm and Cohen 1979, 1980, Srivastava 1982a, b, Kunc and Martin 1981a, b, Cohen 1980).

The total energy of a crystal, within the adiabatic approximation, can be written as a function of the positions of the ions

$$
E_{\text {total }}(\boldsymbol{R})=E_{\text {ion-ion }}(\boldsymbol{R})+E_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{R})
$$

where $E_{\text {ion-ion }}$ is the direct ion-ion interaction energy, and $E_{\mathrm{el}}$ is the total energy of the valence electrons moving in the potential field of ions, including quantum mechanical kinetic, exchange and correlation energies. The equilibrium structure is determined by the minimisation of the total energy. $E_{\text {total }}(\boldsymbol{R})$ for $\boldsymbol{R}$ away from the equilibrium are distortion energies which determine the stability of structure, phonon spectra etc (Cohen 1980).

For calculation of exchange and correlation energies included in $E_{\mathrm{el}}$, the local- density-functional method of Hohenberg, Kohn and Sham has proved very successful. In this method $E_{\mathrm{el}}$ is calculated as a unique functional of the ground-state electronic charge density. Further, the basic variable, the electronic charge density, is calculated from self-consistent solutions to the variationally determined eigenvalue equations for the electronic structure of the crystal.

A drastic simplification of the expression for the total energy can be made by

formulating the relevant expression in momentum space, applied using the pseudopotential method and a plane-wave basis set (Ihm et al 1979).

We have recently applied the formalism of Ihm et al (1979) in a calculation of the ground-state properties of Si, Ge and $\alpha$-Sn based on a self-consistent local pseudopotential method (Srivastava 1982a, b). The calculated total energies for Si and Ge were found to be in very good agreement with experiment. However, for $\alpha$-Sn the calculated result was found to be only in reasonable agreement with experiment.

Chelikowsky and Cohen (1976) have established that non-local pseudopotential corrections and spin-orbit interactions play a significant role in the electronic band structure of $\alpha$-Sn (such corrections not being so significant in Si and Ge). We therefore first present a self-consistent non-local pseudopotential calculation for the electronic structure of $\alpha$-Sn. We also include spin-orbit interactions in the band-structure calculation. Based upon this method, we use the formalism of Ihm et al (1979) to calculate the total energy, equilibrium lattice constant and bulk modulus of $\alpha$-Sn. The present calculated results are found to be in good agreement with experiment.

## 2. Self-consistent band structure

In our previous work (Srivastava 1982a) we have described a method for obtaining the band structure of diamond-type semiconductors by the self-consistent local pseudopotential method. Using an empirical non-local pseudopotential approach Chelikowsky and Cohen (1976) have established that non-local corrections and spin-orbit interactions play a significant role in the electronic band structure of $\alpha$-Sn. We therefore extend our earlier self-consistent band-structure calculation of $\alpha$-Sn to include non-local corrections and spin-orbit interactions.

In the presence of spin-orbit interactions, the electronic band structure is obtained by solving the following eigenvalue equation:

$$
\sum_{G, s}\left(H_{G, G^{\prime}}^{s s^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k})-E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k}) \delta_{s s^{\prime}} \delta_{G, G^{\prime}}\right) b_{n s}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{G})=0 \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})$ are the eigenvalues and $b_{n s}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{G})$ the eigenvectors. We follow Animalu (1966) in including spin-orbit interactions and write an effective Hamiltonian as follows (in Ryd, which will be used throughout the paper):

$$
H_{G, G^{\prime}}^{s s^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\left(K^{2} \delta_{G, G^{\prime}}+W\left(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}, s\right)\right) \delta_{s s^{\prime}}+W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{so}}\left(\boldsymbol{K}, s ; \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}, s^{\prime}\right) \tag{2}
$$

where $\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{G}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}$, and $s, s^{\prime}$ are spin indices. There is no screening of the spin-orbit part of the pseudopotential (Animalu 1966) whose matrix elements are given by (Weisz 1966)

$$
W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{so}}\left(\boldsymbol{K}, s ; \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}, s^{\prime}\right)=-\mathrm{i} \lambda\left(\boldsymbol{K} \times \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}\right) \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{s s^{\prime}} S\left(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right) \tag{3}
$$

where $S\left(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right)$ is the structure factor, $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ are the Pauli spin matrices and $\lambda$ is the spin-orbit form factor.

$W\left(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}, s\right)$ are the Fourier components of a general non-local pseudopotential:

$$
W\left(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}, s\right)=W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{L}}\left(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right)+W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{NL}}\left(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}\right)+W_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}, s\right)+W_{\mathrm{XC}}\left(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}, s\right) \tag{4}
$$

Here $W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{L}}$ and $W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{NL}}$ are, respectively, the local and non-local components of the ionic pseudopotential, and $W_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $W_{\mathrm{XC}}$ represent, respectively, the Coulomb and exchange-correlation parts of the screening potential. The local pseudopotential matrix

elements $W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{L}}(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}')$ considered here are the same as described in our earlier work (Srivastava 1982a). Writing
$$
W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{L}}\left(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right) \equiv W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{L}}(\boldsymbol{q})=S(\boldsymbol{q}) w_{\text{ion}}^{\text{L}}(q)
\tag{5}
$$
we consider $w_{\text{ion}}^{\text{L}}(q)$ in a parametrised form
$$
w_{\text{ion}}^{\text{L}}(q)=\left(b_{1} / q^{2}\right)\left(\cos \left(b_{2} q\right)+b_{3}\right) \exp \left(-b_{4} q^{4}\right).
\tag{6}
$$

The non-local elements $W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{NL}}(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}')$ are calculated by decomposing the plane waves into spherical Bessel functions $j_{l}$ and Legendre polynomials $P_{l}(\cos \theta_{G, G'})$ and operating on the latter with the angular momentum projection operator (Chelikowsky and Cohen 1976, Zunger and Cohen 1979, Abarenkov and Heine 1965):
$$
W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{NL}}\left(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{4 \pi}{\Omega_{\text{at}}} S\left(\boldsymbol{G}-\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right) \sum_{l=0,2} A_{l}(E)(2 l+1) P_{l}\left(\cos \theta_{G, G^{\prime}}\right) F_{l}\left(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}\right)
\tag{7}
$$
where
$$
F_{l}\left(\boldsymbol{K}, \boldsymbol{K}^{\prime}\right)=
\begin{cases}
\left(R_{\mathrm{m}}^{3} / 2\right)\left[\left(j_{l}\left(K R_{\mathrm{m}}\right)\right)^{2}-j_{l-1}\left(K R_{\mathrm{m}}\right) j_{l+1}\left(K R_{\mathrm{m}}\right)\right) & K=K^{\prime} \\
{\left[R_{\mathrm{m}}^{2} /\left(K^{2}-K^{\prime 2}\right)\right]\left(K j_{l+1}\left(K R_{\mathrm{m}}\right) j_{l}\left(K^{\prime} R_{\mathrm{m}}\right)\right.} \\
\left.-K^{\prime} j_{l+1}\left(K^{\prime} R_{\mathrm{m}}\right) j_{l}\left(K R_{\mathrm{m}}\right)\right) & K \neq K^{\prime}.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
$$

Here we have assumed non-local corrections for $l=0$ (s) and $l=2$ (d) states, with $R_{\mathrm{m}}$ as a model radius (Abarenkov and Heine 1965) and $A_{0}$ as an energy-dependent depth well for the s states (Chelikowsky and Cohen 1976)
$$
A_{0}(E)=\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}\left(K K^{\prime}-k_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right):
\tag{9}
$$
where $k_{\mathrm{F}}$ is the Fermi momentum.

The parameters $b_{i}$ defining $W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{L}}, \alpha_{0}, \beta_{0}$ and $A_{2}$ defining $W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{NL}}$, and $\lambda$ defining $W_{\text{ion}}^{\text{SO}}$, are all considered in this work as adjustable parameters. Table 1 lists these parameters for $\alpha$-Sn.

Starting with the linearly screened atomic pseudopotential from our previous work (Srivastava 1982a), we construct, in subsequent iterations for self-consistency, a non-linearly screened potential according to equation (4). The electrostatic Hartree-type screening potential is calculated from
$$
W_{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{q}, s)=8 \pi \rho(\boldsymbol{q}, s) /\left|\boldsymbol{q}^{2}\right|.
\tag{10}
$$

Furthermore, a local exchange-correlation screening potential is constructed in the $X_{\alpha}$ approximation (Slater 1974):
$$
W_{\mathrm{XC}}(\boldsymbol{q}, s)=-\alpha(3 / \pi)\left(3 \pi^{2}\right)^{1 / 3} \rho^{1 / 3}(\boldsymbol{q}, s)
\tag{11}
$$
with $\alpha=0.79$.

In solving the eigenvalue equation (1), we have considered plane waves in the basis set up to 6 Ryd in kinetic energy. The Fourier coefficients of the spin-oriented charge density $\rho(\boldsymbol{q}, s)$ are calculated directly from the eigenvectors $b_{n s}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{G})$, using ten special $\boldsymbol{k}$-points (Chadi and Cohen 1973) in the irreducible Brillouin zone. For the calculation of $W_{\mathrm{XC}}$ we evaluated $\rho(\boldsymbol{r}, s)$ on a grid of $10^{3} \boldsymbol{r}$-points in the unit cell. The cube root of $\rho(\boldsymbol{r}, s)$ was computed at each point, and the result transformed back into a Fourier series $\rho^{1 / 3}(\boldsymbol{q}, s)$ from which $W_{\mathrm{XC}}(\boldsymbol{q}, s)$ was then calculated using equation (11). An attenuated feedback technique similar to that in our previous work was applied to achieve self-consistency in eigenvalues and the screening potential.

### 3. Pseudopotential theory for equilibrium properties

Defining the total crystal energy as the total energy difference between the solid and isolated cores, one can write, in the adiabatic approximation
$$
E_{\text {total }}=E_{\text {ion-ion }}+E_{\text {el }} \text {. }
$$

Here $E_{\text {ion-ion }}$ is the direct ion-ion interaction energy, and $E_{\mathrm{el}}$ is the total energy of the valence electrons moving in the potential field of the ions, including quantum mechanical kinetic, exchange and correlation energies. The valence electrons in a solid can be assumed to form an interacting inhomogeneous system in the presence of the ionic potential as an external potential field $V_{\text {ext }}(\boldsymbol{r})$. According to the Hohenberg-KohnSham local-density-functional theorem, the total energy of such a system can be written, in the pseudopotential scheme (Ihm et al 1979, Zunger and Cohen 1979), as
$$
E_{\mathrm{el}}=\int V_{\mathrm{ext}}(\boldsymbol{r}) \rho(\boldsymbol{r}) \mathrm{d} \boldsymbol{r}+F(\rho(\boldsymbol{r}))
$$
where $F$ is a universal functional of the ground-state electronic charge density $\rho(\boldsymbol{r})$, independent of the external potential:
$$
F(\rho(\boldsymbol{r})) \simeq T(\rho(\boldsymbol{r}))+E_{\mathrm{H}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r}))+\int \varepsilon_{\mathrm{XC}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r})) \rho(\boldsymbol{r}) \mathrm{d} \boldsymbol{r}
$$

Here $T$ is the kinetic energy, $E_{\mathrm{H}}$ is the Coulomb energy, and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{XC}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r}))$ is the exchange-correlation energy per electron. From this the local-density-functional oneelectron exchange-correlation potential is given as
$$
W_{\mathrm{XC}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r})) \equiv \partial / \partial \rho(\boldsymbol{r})\left[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{XC}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r})) \rho(\boldsymbol{r})\right]
$$

In the $X_{\alpha}$ approximation (equation (11))
$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{XC}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r})) \equiv \frac{1}{\rho(\boldsymbol{r})} \int W_{\mathrm{XC}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r})) \rho(\boldsymbol{r}) \mathrm{d} \boldsymbol{r}=\frac{3}{4} W_{\mathrm{XC}}(\rho(\boldsymbol{r}))
$$
with $\rho(\boldsymbol{r})=\rho(\boldsymbol{r}, \uparrow)+\rho(\boldsymbol{r}, \downarrow)$. The external potential field $V_{\text {ext }}(\boldsymbol{r})$ in equation (13) is just the electron-ion interaction pseudopotential $W_{\text {ion }}(\boldsymbol{r})$.

Following Ihm et al (1979), we include the spin-orbit interactions in the momentum-space formalism to write the total energy expression as
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\text {total }}(\text { per atom }) & =\Omega_{\text {at }}\left(\sum_{i, G, s, n_{\mathrm{v}}} w(i)\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}\right)^{2}\left|b_{n_{\mathrm{v}} s}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}\right)\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2} \sum_{s, \boldsymbol{G} \neq 0} W_{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{G}, s) \rho(\boldsymbol{G}, s)\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{3}{4} \sum_{\boldsymbol{G}, s} W_{\mathrm{XC}}(\boldsymbol{G}, s) \rho(\boldsymbol{G}, s)+\sum_{s, \boldsymbol{G} \neq 0} W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{L}}(\boldsymbol{G}) \rho(\boldsymbol{G}, s)\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& +\sum_{i, \boldsymbol{G}, \boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}, s, n_{\mathrm{v}}} w(i) b^{*}{ }_{n_{\mathrm{v}} s}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right) b_{n_{\mathrm{v}} s}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}\right) W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{NL}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}, \boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right) \\
& +\sum_{i, \boldsymbol{G}, \boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}, s, s^{\prime}, n_{\mathrm{v}}} w(i) b^{*}{ }_{n_{\mathrm{v}} s^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}\right) b_{n_{\mathrm{v}} s}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}\right) W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{SO}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}, s ; \boldsymbol{k}_{i}+\boldsymbol{G}^{\prime}, s^{\prime}\right) \\
& +\alpha_{1} Z+\gamma_{\text {Ewald }}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $Z$ is the valency of ion, $n_{\mathrm{v}}$ is the valence-band index, $\Omega_{\mathrm{at}}$ is the atomic volume, and $w(i)$ is the weight factor associated with the special point $\boldsymbol{k}_{i} . \alpha_{1} Z$ is a correction due to the 'pseudo' nature of the potential: $\alpha_{1} Z$ is the $\boldsymbol{G}=0$ component of the repulsive local pseudopotential
$$
\alpha_{1}=\lim _{\boldsymbol{G} \rightarrow 0}\left(W_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{L}}(|\boldsymbol{G}|)+\frac{8 \pi Z}{\Omega_{\mathrm{at}} G^{2}}\right)
$$
and $\gamma_{\text {Ewald }}$ is the Ewald ion-ion energy and is related to $E_{\text {ion-ion }}$ as follows
$$
\gamma_{\text {Ewald }}=E_{\text {ion-ion }}-\lim _{\boldsymbol{G} \rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{4 \pi Z^{2}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{at}} G^{2}}\right).
$$

The equilibrium properties of a crystal can be studied by the minimisation of the total energy as a function of crystal volume. The equilibrium lattice constant $a_{0}$ is calculated from the equilibrium volume $V_{0}$ for which $E_{\text {total }}\left(V_{0}\right)$ is a minimum. Also, having studied the variation of $E_{\text {total }}(V)$ around $V_{0}$, the bulk modulus can be calculated from the expression
$$
B=\left.V_{0}\left(\partial^{2} E_{\text {total }} / \partial V^{2}\right)\right|_{V_{0}}.
$$

## 4. Results and discussion

### 4.1. Band structure

The calculation of structural properties via total energy requires a suitably converged electronic band structure. We therefore first discuss the band structure of $\alpha$-Sn obtained using the self-consistent non-local pseudopotential method and its contrast with the result obtained using the self-consistent local pseudopotential method (without spin-orbit interactions). In figure 1 are shown the calculated band structure of $\alpha$-Sn obtained using the self-consistent local and non-local pseudopotential methods respectively. The energy eigenvalues at the symmetry points $\Gamma, \mathrm{X}$ and $\mathrm{L}$ for the valence bands and first few conduction bands from both methods are given in table 2. Clearly with the inclusion of non-local corrections and spin-orbit interactions the valence-band width is increased by about an $\mathrm{eV}$ and degeneracies are in general split. There is in general good agreement between our self-consistent non-local band structure and the empirical non-local band structure of Chelikowsky and Cohen (1976), as can be seen from table 2. However, the valence band calculated by Chelikowsky and Cohen is over an eV wider than our calculation. There are not many sets of experimental data available in the literature to compare with the results of our calculations.

## 5. Conclusions

We have applied the local-density-functional theorem to calculate the equilibrium properties of $\alpha$-Sn. The momentum-space formalism of Ihm *et al*, within the self-consistent non-local pseudopotential method, is used to simplify the evaluation of the result. Spin-orbit interactions are also included in the band-structure calculation.

Inclusion of non-local corrections and spin-orbit interactions has improved the occupied valence-band spectrum and the corresponding eigenvectors. The derived self-consistent band structure is in general different from the band structure obtained using the self-consistent local pseudopotential method (Srivastava 1982a), but compares well with the empirical non-local calculations of Chelikowsky and Cohen. Further, the calculated values of the equilibrium lattice constant, total energy and bulk modulus are found to be in good agreement with experiment.
