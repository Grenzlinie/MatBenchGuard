# Theoretical and experimental investigations on elastic properties of substoichiometric titanium nitrides: influence of lattice vacancies

## Abstract

Substoichiometric titanium nitrides have been synthesized by multiple energy ion implantation at the surface of pure titanium. Elastic properties have been studied by nanoindentation. Self consistent Full Potential Linear Muffin Tin Orbital (FP-LMTO) calculation with empty spheres in place of the missing nitrogen atoms gives the corresponding bulk modulus and lattice parameter values. The results, both experimentally and theoretically, show the same evolution of the lattice parameter and the bulk modulus, as a function of vacancy concentration. The agreement between experimental and theoretical values is discussed in relation with the vacancy effect. © 2001 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Titanium nitride; Vacancies; Elasticity; Bulk modulus; Lattice parameter

## 1. Introduction

It is well known that titanium nitrides exhibit rather exceptional physical properties. However, it is very dif- ficult to produce these refractory compounds with their nominal composition (TiN$_{1.00}$) and, hence, many authors studied the hardness and elasticity of near stoichiometric metal compounds. In a preceeding work [1], substoichio- metric titanium nitrides have been synthesized. XPS valence spectra showing new structures due to vacancies were recorded and compared with DOS curves calculated with a Tight Binding-Linear Muffin Tin Orbital (TB- LMTO) method [2]. The aim of the present paper is to compare the experimental elastic properties and lattice parameter experimental values with those calculated 'ab initio' with a self consistent (FP-LMTO) method.

## 2. Experimental results

### 2.1. Crystallographic study

The diffraction patterns showed the formation of $\delta$-TiN structure in the case of TiN$_{0.45}$ and TiN$_{0.61}$. For the TiN$_{0.26}$ we have found only the formation of the $\alpha$ solid solution. The $\delta$-TiN structure is of NaCl type, composed of two fcc sublattices, one containing the titanium atoms and the other the nitrogen atoms. In case of substoichiometric TiN$_x$ samples, as we have here, it is known that vacancies take place on the nitrogen sublattice. The sample with $\alpha$ solid solution is not discussed here because its structure is of hexagonal type [1]. ### 2.2. Nanoindentation results

Nanoindentation technique was carried out to determine
the elasto-plastic properties (hardness and Young's
modulus) of the nitride layers as a function of depth, using
a formalism developed by Oliver and Pharr [4]. Since our
TiNₓ layers are polycrystalline, they may be considered as
elastically isotropic; thus, the bulk modulus $K$ can be
derived from the Young modulus E and the Poisson
coefficient $\nu$ through the following relation [5]:

$$
K = \frac{E}{3(1 - 2\nu)}
$$

Unfortunately, to our knowledge no work concerning the
evolution of the Poisson coefficient as a function of
nitrogen concentration, for our system, is available. There-
fore, we used two different approaches to calculate the
Young's modulus and the corresponding bulk modulus
hereafter. The first considers the Poisson coefficient as
constant and equal to 0.194, which is the stoichiometric
TiN value [6]. The second, more realistic to our opinion,
considers $\nu$ values interpolated linearly between the pure
titanium value $\nu$=0.32 and the stoichiometric TiN value
$\nu$=0.194. Linear interpolation is justified by the fact that
the Poisson coefficient is an elastic property of the matter,
as Young's modulus is. The experimental Young's
modulus values of the $\delta$-TiN close to stoichiometry,
reported in literature present a wide spread (varying
between 250 GPa and 640 GPa [7] depending on prepara-
tion method and measuring technique). We used 590 GPa
as a standard value of $\delta$-TiN₁.₀₀ [8]. The experimental
results, given in Fig. 2, show that the bulk modulus
decreases when increasing the vacancy concentration, from
321 GPa for TiN₁.₀₀ to 140 GPa for TiN₀.₄₅, in the
constant case, and from 321 GPa to 176 GPa, in the
interpolated case.

## 3. Theoretical calculation

### 3.1. Full Potential-Linear Muffin Tin Orbital method

In order to calculate the bulk modulus and the lattice
parameter of $\delta$-TiN we used the FP-LMTO method [9,10].
Moreover, the calculations were based on the generalized
gradient approximation of Perdew and Wang [11,12] for
the exchange and correlation potential. Basis functions,
electron densities and potentials were calculated without
any geometrical approximation [9]. These quantities were
expanded in combinations of spherical harmonic functions
(with a cut-off $l_{\text{max}}$=8) inside non-overlapping spheres
surrounding the atomic sites (muffin-tin spheres) and in a
Fourier series in the interstitial region. The radial basis
functions within the muffin tin spheres are linear combina-
tions of radial wave functions and their derivatives,
computed at energies appropriate to their site and principal
as well as orbital atomic quantum numbers, whereas
outside the muffin-tin spheres the basis functions are
combinations of Neumann or Hankel functions [13,14]. In
the calculations reported here, we made use of pseudo-core
3p states of Ti and valence band 4s, 4p and 3d basis
functions with corresponding two sets of energy parame-
ters, one appropriate for the semi-core 3p states and the
other appropriate for the valence states.

### 3.2. Lattice parameter and bulk modulus

The calculations were performed for TiN₁.₀₀, TiN₀.₇₅
and TiN₀.₅₀, where empty spheres replaced the missing
nitrogen atoms in the fcc nitrogen sublattice. Moreover, for
calculation facilities, the vacancies distribution was consid-
ered as periodic, even if it was not the case in the
experimental samples. Fig. 1 shows the theoretical lattice
parameter evolution compared to the experimental one.
One notices that both values increase when the vacancy
concentration decreases. On the other hand, one remarks
that the theoretical values are systematically lower than the
experimentally ones. The same behavior has been observed
for titanium carbides [15]. This may be due to several
defects (dislocations, grain boundaries, residual strain...)
present in the samples, which are not taken into account in
the FP-LMTO calculation. The bulk modulus values
obtained in such a way are 270, 278 and 217 GPa for
TiN₁.₀₀, TiN₀.₇₅ and TiN₀.₅₀ respectively and are close to
the experimental ones obtained with interpolated $\nu$ values.
By using a constant value ($\nu$=0.194) for the Poisson
coefficient the experimental bulk modulus values agree
slightly less with the theoretical ones, but the evolution
with vacancy content still remains the same.

### 4. Conclusions

We have compared the bulk modulus and the lattice parameter values obtained experimentally with those calculated 'ab initio' with a self consistent FP-LMTO method. The results show not only the same evolution of the lattice parameter and the bulk modulus, both experimentally and theoretically, as a function of vacancy concentration, but even give values close to each other.
