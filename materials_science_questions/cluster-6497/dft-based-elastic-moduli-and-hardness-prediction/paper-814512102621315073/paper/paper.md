### ABSTRACT
The structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of c-BN compound doped with V atoms were calculated by means of the pseudopotential method, employed exactly as implemented in computational Quantum ESPRESSO code. For the description of the electron-electron interaction, generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was used. A half-metallic behavior is predicted for the concentrations $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$, because of the fact that the majority spins are metallic and the minority spins are semiconducting. We found magnetic moments of 2.0 and $4.0\ \mu_{\beta}$ per supercell, respectively. The main contribution to the magnetic moment comes from the V atom, with local moments of $1.61\ \mu_{\beta}/V$-atom. These compounds are good candidates for potential applications in spintronics and as spin injectors.

## 1. Introduction
Over the last few decades, various high-performance devices fabricated from III-nitrides have generated great theoretical and experimental interest [1–5]. Among the III semiconductors, boron nitride (BN) stands out, due to its excellent physical, mechanical, and thermal properties, such as high-temperature stability, chemical inertia, a high degree of hardness, a low coefficient of thermal expansion, a high melting point, and high thermal conductivity [6–9]. BN has a broad range of potential applications, such as in high-temperature ceramic materials that can withstand an extremely harsh environment, radio frequency and high-frequency high-power laser diodes, light-emitting diodes operating in the ultraviolet region, solar detectors, field-effect transistors, and high electron mobility transistors [10–15]. Owing to its variable bonding character, BN can exist in different crystallographic phases, such as zincblende, cubic boron nitride (c-BN) [16–18], wurtzite (w) boron nitride (w-BN) [17,19,20], hexagonal (h) boron nitride (h-BN) [4,5], and rhombohedral (r) boron nitride (r-BN) [1,2]. Boron nitride has been experimentally grown in the hexagonal [5], zincblende [18], and wurtzite [20] phases. At the same time, theoretical and experimental investigations have shown that h-BN and r-BN are easily compressible, while the $sp^{3}$ hybridized in the c-BN and w-BN phases belongs to the class of superhard materials. During the last few years, BN has received extensive attention because of its possible use as a diluted magnetic semiconductor (DMS) with potential applications in the field of spintronics. For these applications, ferromagnetism at room temperature is a requirement. Recently, high-temperature ferromagnetism has been reported by many researchers for several types of transition-metal (TM)-doped semiconducting oxides and nitrides [21–23]. Solochenko et al. [24], Tanicguchi et al. [25], and Guo et al. [26] grew and characterized c-BN. Ohba et al. [27] studied the structural, dielectric, and dynamic properties of c-BN using first-principles calculations. Casiano et al. [28], by means of DFT, proved that TM (TM=V, Cr, and Mn)-doped w-BN compound can be used in spintronics. Boukra et al. [29], using first-principles calculations, showed that Mn-doped c-BN exhibits half-metallic and magnetic behavior. Lopez et al. [30] investigated the electronic and magnetic properties of Mn adsorption on the w-BN(0001) surface. However, investigations of V-doped c-BN in volume, whether theoretical or experimental, are rare. For this reason, in this paper we present a systematic theoretical study of the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$ zincblende structure, motivated by its potential uses in dilute magnetic semiconductors, spin injectors, and other applications in spintronics.

The electron wave functions were expanded into plane waves with a kinetic energy cutoff of 40 Ry. For the charge density, a kinetic energy cutoff of 400 Ry was used. A sampling of the irreducible Brillouin zone with $6 \times 6 \times 4$ Monkhorst-Pack [35] was used to generate the k-points in the unit cell. The convergence threshold for self-consistent-field iteration was set at $10^{-7}$ eV. The calculations were performed taking into account the spin polarization. To calculate the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of pure BN, a 32-atom $2a \times 2b \times 1c$ zincblende supercell was considered (see Fig. 1). The structure of V-doped BN, namely the concentrations of $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$ and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$, were obtained by substituting one or two B atoms with V atoms in the supercell. For the $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ concentration, several configurations were considered, when two V-atoms are in close positions and in more separate positions, until obtaining the most stable structure. All atomic positions of pure c-BN compound, $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ concentrations were optimized until all forces became smaller than $10^{-4}$ eV/Å and were modeled according to the special quasirandom structures approach [36], and the disorder aspects were ignored.

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Structural properties

To calculate the structural properties in the ground state, such as the equilibrium lattice constant $(a_{0})$, the bulk modulus $(B_{0})$, the total energy $(E_{0})$, and the magnetic moment $(\mu)$ per supercell of pure BN, $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ concentrations in the zincblende structure, the total energy was calculated as a function of the volume, and the calculated values were fit to the Murnaghan equation of state [37]. For the $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$ compound, the total energy variation was calculated as a function of the volume for the ferromagnetic (FM) and non-magnetic phases. Additionally, for the $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ compound, the ferromagnetic (FM) and anti-ferromagnetic (AFM) phases were calculated, in order to find the most favorable magnetic phase. Fig. 1 shows the energy-volume curves.

The calculated lattice constant (3.621 Å) for pure BN is in excellent agreement with that of experimental results (3.615 Å) [38]. Additionally, the lattice constant agrees well with values reported in other theoretical studies [27,40], since they differ by less than one percent. The bulk modulus of c-BN (390 GPa) agrees well with other theoretical studies [27,40], with a maximum discrepancy of about 3.0%. Calculated bulk modulus for c-BN is close to that of diamond (442 GPa). This may be attributed to the strong $\text{sp}^{3}$ B-N bonding and to a structure similar to diamond. We note that the values of the bulk modulus of the pure BN, $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ concentrations are higher, which confirms that they are quite rigid, making them good candidates for possible application in devices operated at high temperature and high power, as well as in hard coatings.

When one or two B atoms in the $2a \times 2b \times 1c$ zincblende supercell are replaced with one or two V atoms, the lattice constant of the $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$ and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ compounds increases with respect to pure c-BN. This increase in the parameters may be due the radii of V (1.34 Å), which is much larger than the atomic radius of B (0.98 Å).

We can see in Fig. 2(b) that for the $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$ compound the calculated total energy of spin polarized state (FM phase) is lower than that of the non-spin polarized state (non-magnetic phase) by about 45.4 meV, while for the $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ compound, Fig. 2(c), in the ground state the FM state is lower than the AFM state by about 78.2, which indicates that the ground state of V-doped BN is ferromagnetic.

In Table 1, it can be seen that the spin polarization calculations for the $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$ and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ compounds result in a magnetic moment of 2.0 and 4.0 $\mu_{\beta}$ per supercell, respectively, or equivalently 0.125 and 0.25 Bohr magnetons per formula unit ($\mu_{\beta}$/f.u.), respectively. Similar magnetic behavior has been found for BN, AlN, GaN and InN in the zincblende structure doped with some transition metals. For example, Boukra et al., in a study of Mn-doped c-BN [29], obtained a total magnetic moment of 2.0 $\mu_{\beta}$/cell for $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{Mn}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, while Dahmane et al. [41] predict a magnetic behavior for $\text{In}_{0.75}\text{V}_{0.25}\text{N}$ with a magnetic moment of 2.0 $\mu_{\beta}$/cell. Doumi et al. [42] found 4.0 $\mu_{\beta}$/cell for $\text{Al}_{0.9375}\text{Mn}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, $\text{Ga}_{0.9375}\text{Mn}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, and $\text{In}_{0.9375}\text{Mn}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, and 5.0 $\mu_{\beta}$/cell for $\text{Al}_{0.9375}\text{Fe}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, $\text{Ga}_{0.9375}\text{Fe}_{0.0625}\text{N}$, and $\text{In}_{0.9375}\text{Fe}_{0.0625}\text{N}$.

### 3.2. Electronic properties

The theoretical lattice constants of pure BN and the $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$ and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ compounds in the zincblende structure, listed in Table 1, were used to calculate the band structure and the spin-polarized density of states (DOS) along the high-symmetry paths in the first Brillouin zone. The band structure for pure BN and the $\text{B}_{0.9375}\text{V}_{0.0625}\text{N}$ and $\text{B}_{0.875}\text{V}_{0.125}\text{N}$ compounds is shown in Fig. 3.

We found an indirect band gap of about 4.49 eV. This value was calculated using the pseudopotential method and the GGA approximation; the magnitude of this gap agrees well with values reported in other theoretical papers, for example 4.47 eV [43] calculated via the FP-LAPW-GGA method, 4.42 eV [40] calculated via LMTO-LDA, and 4.45 eV [44] calculated via the FP-LAPW-LDA method. We note that the band gap calculated in the present paper (4.49 eV) is close to the value of references [40] and [44], calculated with the LDA approximation. This can be attributed to the fact that B and N atoms are light, and "since the variation of the electronic charge density in the light elements is small, the charge density gradient corrections involved in GGA are not large, and consequently this exchange correlation functional gives results similar to LDA, which is based on a uniform electron gas approximation with no gradient correction" [43].

Fig. 3(b and c) shows the band structure of ternary $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$ compounds, respectively. We can observe that the minority spin (down) states preserve a band gap with an energy gap of 3.71 eV and 3.2 eV, respectively, but in the majority spin (up) states there is a penetration towards the prohibited energy zone of the states 3d-V in greater proportion and states 2p-N and 2p-B in lesser proportion. Therefore, due to the substitution of a boron atom with V atom in the c-BN structure, it loses its semiconductor nature. The allowed ternary compounds exhibit half-metallic behavior, because the up-spins are conductors and the down-spins are semiconductors. We can see that the up-spin orientation of the $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$ compounds is partially filled, because it exhibits dispersed bands above the Fermi level. These compounds exhibit a spin polarization of 100% of the conduction carriers, and they are responsible for the conduction in V-doped c-BN. This is a requirement for spin injectors [22,45]. This finding suggests that these ternary compounds can be efficiently used as spin injectors and for other applications in spintronics.

In order to study the atomic contribution to the magnetism, we calculated the total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) for the $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$ compounds. The TDOS and PDOS are shown in Fig. 4(a) and (b). The total density of states confirms that due to the V substitution at the B site, the compounds have a half-metallic character. This behavior occurs because in the valence band near the Fermi level, the majority spins are metallic and the minority spins are semiconductor. Fig. 4(a) and (b) shows that the valence band between $-10$ and $-3$ eV originates mainly from hybrid states 2p-B and 2p-N. Additionally, in the valence band near the Fermi level, the up-spin density is mainly dominated by the 3d-V states and to a lesser extent by the 2p-B and 2p-N states, which cross the Fermi level. This indicates that all of the magnetic properties come from mainly occupied 3d-V states with a minimum contribution by 2p-B and 2p-N. A similar result was obtained by Boukra et al. [29] in their first-principles study of the magnetic properties of $B_{0.9375}Mn_{0.0625}N$ in the zincblende phase.

As we can see in Fig. 4, the TDOS confirms the presence of some unoccupied states at the top of the valence band near the Fermi level, because there is no contribution of down spin in the valence band and the majority spin exhibits a hybridization and polarization between the 3d-V, 2p-B, and 2p-N states, resulting in a magnetic moment of 2.0 and 4.0 $\mu_\beta$/cell for the $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$ compounds, respectively. These magnetic moment values are integers; therefore, this again confirms that each compound is half-metallic.

## 4. Conclusions

The structural, electronic and magnetic properties of the $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$ compounds were studied using DFT calculations. The calculated lattice constants of c-BN and the experimental values are in good agreement. The bulk moduli of the ternary compounds are quite high; therefore, the compounds are quite rigid, which makes them attractive for potential applications at high temperatures and for hard coatings. The analysis of the band structure and density of states reveals that the $B_{0.9375}V_{0.0625}N$ and $B_{0.875}V_{0.125}N$ compounds exhibit a half-metallic behavior, with a total magnetic moment 2.0, and $4.0\ \mu_{\beta}$ per cell, respectively, which originates mainly from the V atoms. These compounds are good candidates for potential applications in spintronics and as spin injectors.
