# DFT prediction of structural rigidity, half‑metallic behavior, and magnetic moments in V‑doped cubic BN

## Problem background
Cubic boron nitride (c‑BN) is a wide‑band‑gap semiconductor with a zincblende structure, known for its high hardness, thermal stability, and chemical inertness. Substituting boron atoms with transition metals such as vanadium (V) introduces magnetic moments and can induce half‑metallic behaviour, making the resulting V‑doped c‑BN compounds attractive for spintronic applications and hard coatings. The key physical quantities of interest are the equilibrium lattice constant, the bulk modulus, the minority‑spin band gap, and the total magnetic moment per supercell. This task requires computing these properties for pure c‑BN and for two V‑doped concentrations in the zincblende phase, using first‑principles density functional theory (DFT).

## Approach
The present approach uses first‑principles density functional theory (DFT) within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA‑PBE) and ultrasoft pseudopotentials. The system is modelled with a 32‑atom zincblende supercell. For pure c‑BN, total energies are computed at a series of volumes and fitted to the Murnaghan equation of state to extract the equilibrium lattice constant, bulk modulus, and ground‑state total energy. For V‑doped compounds, one or two boron atoms are replaced by vanadium, producing B₀.₉₃₇₅V₀.₀₆₂₅N and B₀.₈₇₅V₀.₁₂₅N. Spin‑polarised calculations are performed to determine the most stable magnetic phase (ferromagnetic versus antiferromagnetic). From the equilibrium structures of the doped compounds, band structures are computed and the minority‑spin band gap is identified. Additionally, the total magnetisation per supercell is read from the self‑consistent output. This workflow yields the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties needed to assess the materials' rigidity and half‑metallic character.
