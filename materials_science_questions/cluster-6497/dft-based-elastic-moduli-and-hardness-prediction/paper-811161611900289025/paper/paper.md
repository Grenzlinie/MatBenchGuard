# Estimation of bulk moduli of compounds by empirical relations between bulk modulus and interatomic distance

Y. Makino*, S. Miyake

Joining and Welding Research Institute, Osaka University, 11-1 Mihogaoka, Ibaraki, Osaka 567-0047, Japan

Received 29 July 2000; received in revised form 26 August 2000; accepted 26 August 2000

## Abstract
Relations between bulk modulus and interatomic distance were empirically determined for five groups of elemental substances by using power functional forms, $B=Cd^{-m}$, where $m$ and $C$ are the constants. The power value for $sp^{3}$-bonded elemental substances was determined to be $-3.60$ and the value is quite close to the one $(=-3.5)$ reported previously. It is indicated that bulk moduli of various AB compounds can be estimated using the respective modified power functional formula based on the formulas for the five groups of elemental substances, by assuming that power and preterm can be determined by geometrical mean of these values of the basic power functional formulae for the constituent elements. Excellent agreement with experimental bulk moduli was obtained for tetrahedrally coordinated (sp³-bonded) AB compounds with B3 and B4 crystal structures. For AB type carbides, nitrides and oxides with B1 and B4 crystal structures and for several rare-earth oxides, the estimated bulk moduli show a good agreement with experimental ones except several compounds. It is also suggested that these power functional formulae can be available for predicting new hard materials. © 2000 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

**Keywords:** Metals; Insulators; Electronic state; Crystal binding; Equation of state; Mechanical properties

## 1. Introduction
Syntheses of new hard materials have been much focused since it has been predicted by Cohen [1] that carbon nitride will be a harder material than diamond. In seeking of such new hard materials, prediction of the physical constants for mechanical properties such as bulk and shear moduli is very important because these physical constants, especially bulk modulus, are closely correlated with the hardness of material [2]. In the previous papers, empirical relations between bulk modulus and interatomic distance have been proposed for ionic compounds [3] and some tetrahedral compounds [1]. In the high pressure experiments, it has been indicated that the bulk modulus depends on bond length [4]. Further, a relation between bulk modulus and lattice constant has been indicated theoretically [5]. Thus, it can be easily expected that the bulk modulus of solid material is expressed by its interatomic distance, except for the concrete formulation.

In the previous paper [6] by one of the authors, it was proposed that the bulk moduli of elemental substances can be empirically correlated with their effective pseudopotential radii using power functional forms. In the empirical formulation, dependence of the power on the bond character of respective element was assumed. Empirical formulation based on the effective pseudopotential radii is available for estimating bulk moduli of various compounds without any additional experimental data. In most cases, however, bulk modulus has been correlated with the interatomic distance and is strongly affected by a slight change of interatomic distance. Besides, the interatomic distance (or lattice parameter) changes delicately even in the same material with same crystal structure, depending on the synthesizing conditions. Accordingly, the formulation based on the interatomic distance is rather convenient for estimating the bulk modulus of the 'respective' material, though it is necessary to measure the interatomic distance (or lattice parameter) for respective material. Systematical formulation based on power functional forms is easily expected between the bulk modulus and the interatomic distance for the elemental substances because a linear relation between the effective pseudopotential radius and interatomic distance has been previously indicated [7].

## 2. Formulation between bulk modulus and interatomic distance

The power functional forms of $B=Cd^{-m}$ were first assumed according to previous studies [1,3,7], where $B$ and $d$ are the bulk modulus and interatomic distance for an elemental substance, respectively, and $C$ and $m$ are the constants. These power functional forms were determined for those elements with respective bonding mode, which were dividing into five groups of sp, spd, 3d, 4d and 5d/4f a priori by considering their bonding characters. For example, the spd bonding mode was named by the reason why s- and p-characters are dominant but a certain contribution of d electron or a peculiar sp-character may be expected in these elements. The empirical relations between bulk modulus and effective pseudopotential radius were determined by distinguishing sp bonding mode from spd bonding mode. In the present paper, however, the power functional form was also determined without distinguishing sp bonding mode from spd bonding mode.

## 3. Results and discussion

First of all, the power functional relation between bulk modulus, $B$, and interatomic distance, $d$, was examined for $\text{sp}^3$-bonded elemental substances, that is, for diamond ($C(d)$), Si, Ge and $\text{Sn(w-Sn)}$. The values of power $m$ and preterm $C$ in the functional form was determined to be $-3.60$ and $0.525$, respectively, as given in Fig. 1. The value of power is very close to the one $(-3.5)$ which was determined in the previous paper [1]. When the power functional form was examined for non-transition elemental substances including $\text{sp}^3$-bonded ones, except for boron, that is, for the elemental substances excluding boron, transition metal and lanthanide, we obtained the values of $-4.25$ and $0.174$ as $m$ and $C$, as shown in Fig. 2.

In the previous paper [6], the bulk moduli of the elemental substances were empirically correlated with their effective pseudopotential $radii(r_{\mathrm{ps}}(\mathrm{eff}))$ using power functional forms. Comparing with these powers $m(\mathrm{ps})$ in these relations, the power $m$ satisfies a quite good linear relation with $m(\mathrm{ps})$, which is expressed as $m=1.26 m(\mathrm{ps})-0.16$ with the correlation coefficient of 0.999 . This is quite reasonable result because a good linearity has been found between interatomic distance and effective pseudopotential radius, $r_{\mathrm{ps}}(\mathrm{eff})$ [7]. As indicated in the previous paper [7], the power in the relation between $B$ and $r_{\mathrm{ps}}(\mathrm{eff})$ is suggested to be connected with the angular momentum quantum number $(\ell)$, so the similar connection to $\ell$ is also suggested for the power in the relation between $B$ and $d$. Because the dependence of the bonding energy on $\ell$ is suggested from the energy matrix expression for valence electrons in bond orbital model [10], the change of power in the power functional form of the interatomic distance reflects the difference of the strength of potential depending on the electron species.

Subsequently, bulk moduli of several AB type compounds with the simple structures were calculated by using the respective power functional form. The power function was formulated under the assumption that the preterm and power in the power functional form for these compounds can be obtained by geometrically compositional average, as given in formula (1). First, for B4 type AB compounds consisting of non-transition elements, these bulk moduli were calculated by the power functional form for the $\mathrm{sp}^{3}$-bonding mode. For the B4 type AB compounds containing post-transition elements such as Zn, Cd and Hg, these bulk moduli were calculated by the power functional form for the average bonding mode of $\mathrm{sp}^{3}$ and spd types. The reproducibility is shown in Fig. 4. Good reproducibility was obtained except for cubic BN ZnS and ZnSe, for which the calculated values are 50, 11.9 and 11.6 GPa higher than the observed ones (367, 77.1 and 62.4 GPa).

Availability of the power functional forms was also examined in several carbides, nitrides and oxides with B1 or B4 structures using the geometric averaging method given by formula (1). The comparison of the calculated bulk moduli with the experimental ones is shown in Fig. 5. Data on several rare-earth oxides are also plotted in this figure. Among various AB type compounds, larger discrepancies than 50 GPa were observed for VC, TiN and NiO. The calculated bulk moduli of AB type and several rare-earth compounds are determined within $20 \%$ accuracy, except for several compounds (VC, TiN, NiO, BaO and $\mathrm{Sc}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ ). Though there are a few exceptional compounds, the power functional formulas based on the interatomic distance are well applicable for design of hard materials and for estimating their hardness. In order to consider the effect of 3d electrons to bulk modulus, the experimental bulk moduli for 3d transition metal oxides with AB type were compared with those calculated from the power functional forms of the average bonding modes of constituent elements. The results are shown in Fig. 6. Except for ZnO and BaO, the experimental bulk moduli of AB type oxides are placed between the bulk moduli calculated by the respective power functional formulas for average bonding modes of $\text{sp}^3$ and nd (3d, 4d or 5d) types and for those of $\text{sp}^3$ and spd types. On the other hand, the experimental bulk modulus of ZnO shows a good agreement with the calculated value based on $(\text{sp}^3+\text{spd})/2$ bonding mode. Scarce contribution of 3d electrons on the bulk modulus is reasonable for ZnO because 3d electrons are scarcely related with the bonding between zinc and oxygen, but no contribution of 3d electrons can not be understood in the bonding between nickel and oxygen, though the experimental bulk modulus of NiO is quite close to the calculated value based on $(\text{sp}^3+\text{spd})/2$ bonding mode.

In order to estimate the difference between experimental data and calculated ones in more details, the bulk moduli of 3d transition metal oxides were recalculated by taking the contribution ratio of 3d electrons into consideration. Here, it is assumed that the ratio of 3d electrons contribution can be approximated by the ratio of sd hybridization, which has been obtained in the previous paper [6]. Using these ratios of sd hybridization for transition metal elements, the revised bulk moduli were calculated for AB type transition metal oxides. These numerical values are given in Table 4. The agreement of the calculated bulk moduli to the experimental ones is remarkably improved though disagreement for NiO still remains unclear. Thus the precise ratio of d electrons contribution should be required for obtaining good agreement between the experimental and calculated bulk moduli.

Lastly, the effect of d and/or 4f electrons on the bulk moduli of rare-earth oxides were considered in the same way to AB-type 3d transition metal oxides, under the assumption that the contribution of nd or 4f electrons can also be considered by the ratios of sd hybridization. As shown in Fig. 7, the revised bulk moduli for some rare-earth oxides show better agreement with experimental ones, while the agreement becomes worse for several oxides such as $\text{Y}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{Dy}_2\text{O}_3$. ## 4. Summary

In order to estimate the bulk modulus ($B$) of compound by using the interatomic distance ($d$), empirical relations for the five groups of elemental substances were de- termined between the bulk modulus and interatomic dis- tance by assuming the power functional forms, $B = Cd^{-m}$, where $C$ and $m$ are the constants. For $\text{sp}^3$-bonded elemen- tal substances, these constants ($C$ and $m$) were determined to be 0.525 and $-3.60$ and the power is quite close value to the previous one ($=-3.5$). Based on these basic power functional formulae, bulk moduli of various $AB$ com- pounds can be estimated by assuming that power and preterm can be determined by geometrical mean of these values of the basic power functional formulae for the constituent elements. Availability of these formulae to rare-earth compounds is also indicated by estimating the bulk moduli of several rare-earth oxides. Extensive appli- cation of the formulation is also expected to estimate bulk moduli of various compounds for designing hard materials and polarization of oxygen in oxides for determining the oxide basicity [11].
