# First-principles calculations of structural properties of $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ compound

William López Pérez$^{a,*}$, Jairo Arbey Rodríguez M$^{b}$, María G. Moreno-Armenta$^{c}$

$^{a}$Departamento de Matemáticas y Física, Universidad del Norte, A. A. 1569, Barranquilla, Colombia
$^{b}$Departamento de Física, Universidad Nacional de Colombia, A. A. 5997, Bogotá, Colombia
$^{c}$Centro de Ciencias de la Materia Condensada, Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, A. P. 2681, Ensenada Baja California 22800, México

## Abstract
We have applied the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method (FP-LAPW) within the density functional theory to investigate the structural properties of $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ compound in sodium chloride and wurtzite structures. We have found that the lattice parameter ($a$) increases with the increment of the In-composition in both structures, while the bulk modulus diminishes with the increase of the In-composition. The lattice constant and the bulk modulus present a small bowing in both structures. We have also analyzed the relative stability of this ternary compound in the two studied phases. We have found that sodium chloride structure is the ground state phase for a In-composition range $0\leqslant x<0.5$, while the wurtzite structure is most stable in the range $0.5\leqslant x\leqslant1$. The results for the binary compounds are in good agreement with the experimental data.

## 1. Introduction
The theoretical studies have been fundamental in the development of new materials and new devices for diverse industrial applications. In the last years a great number of works based on first principles calculations about InN and their alloys has been carried out [1–3]. However, there is a group of ternary compounds that has been studied very little theoretically and it begins to be investigated experimentally. We refer to the ternary compounds based on InN combined with transition metals like the Scandium (Sc).

Recent experiments have shown that ScN is a indirect semiconductor with a band-gap of $0.9\pm0.1$ eV and sodium chloride as ground-state structure with lattice parameter $a=4.501$ Å and bulk modulus $B_0=182\pm40$ GPa [4,5]. A theoretical study confirmed the stability of ScN in the NaCl phase [6]. Studies of optical characterization by absorption and photoluminescence shown that the band-gap of wurtzite InN is $0.7\pm0.05$ eV [7]. In the wurtzite structure, which is their ground state, the experimental values for the lattice parameters have been reported recently $a=3.5376$ Å, $c=5.7064$ Å [8] and bulk modulus $B_0=125.5$ GPa $\pm4.6$ GPa [9].

The aim of this paper is to study the structural properties of $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ compound in sodium chloride and wurtzite structures. The report of recent theoretical studies of the structural and electronic properties of the other ternary compounds is known [3,10,11].

## 2. Methodology
The calculations were performed employing the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method as implemented in the WIEN2k code [12] within the density functional theory (DFT) [13,14]. The wave functions in the interstitial region expand in plane waves with a cutoff of $K_{\text{max}} = 8.0/R_{mt}$. The convergence criterion in energy was of $0.1$ mRy, with a parameter for densities mixture of $0.4$. For ScN we have chosen as values of muffin-tin radii $1.8, 1.6$ bohr, for scandium and nitrogen, respectively. For InN we select $1.9$ bohr for indium and $1.7$ for nitrogen as muffin-tin radii. For ternary compounds we have used muffin-tin radii of $1.6$ bohr for N, $1.8$ bohr for Sc and $1.9$ bohr for In atoms. We have included In 4d as valence states. We have used 70 and 86 $k$-points in the irreducible part of the Brillouin zone for wurtzite and sodium chloride structures, respectively, in the binary compounds and in the composition $x = 0.5$. For the compositions $x = 0.25, 0.75$ we use 38 and 45 $k$-points in the irreducible part of the Brillouin zone for wurtzite and sodium chloride phases, respectively.

The compounds were modeled for the compositions $x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$ according to the special quasirandom structures (SQS) approach [16]. For the composition $x = 0.5$ a four-atom tetragonal cell is the simplest unit cell in sodium chloride phase, while in wurtzite is their conventional unit cell. The four-atom tetragonal unit cell for sodium chloride structure was obtained of a NaCl conventional cell. The tetragonal unit cell has the sides of the base equal to each other but smaller than the lattice parameter of a NaCl conventional cell, while the height corresponds to lattice constant of a NaCl conventional cell. For this cell the relationships are completed $a = a_0/\sqrt{2}$ and $c = a_0$ and in consequence $c/a = \sqrt{2}$, where the parameter $a$ corresponds to one side of the base of the tetragonal unit cell and $c$ is the parameter along the $z$ axis, while $a_0$ is the lattice constant of a NaCl conventional cell. This cell contains one molecule of ScN and one of InN. For the compositions $x = 0.25, 0.75$ we use a tetragonal unit cell containing eight atoms for sodium chloride structure and an eight-atom hexagonal cell for wurtzite phase (see Fig. 1). The eight-atom tetragonal cell is obtained of joining two four-atom tetragonal cells along the $z$ axis. The height of the resulting cell is twice the lattice constant of a NaCl conventional cell. The eight-atom hexagonal cell for wurtzite structure arises of joining two wurtzite conventional unit cell in $z$ direction. The resulting like-wurtzite cell for the In-compositions $x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$ presents two different values from the parameter $u$; each one describes the separation in units of $c$ among nearest neighbors In–N ($u_1$) and Sc–N ($u_2$). The internal parameters $u_1$ and $u_2$ were optimized for each binary compound InN and ScN in wurtzite phase. Our results predict the optimum internal parameters are $u_1 = 0.380c$, $u_2 = 0.386c$. We optimize the $c/a$ ratio of $\text{Sc}_{1-x}\text{In}_x\text{N}$ compound in the wurtzite structure. We obtain lattice parameters and bulk modulus by fitting the total energy versus volume to the Murnaghan equation of state [17]. Energies and volumes are given per eight-atom tetragonal (hexagonal) unit cell in sodium chloride (wurtzite) structure.

In Fig. 1 we show the eight-atom unit cells for sodium chloride (left) and wurtzite (right) structures. In both cases the cells contain 100% of Sc. We replace one and three Sc atoms with In atoms to obtain the In-compositions $x = 0.25$ and $0.75$, respectively.

### 3. Results

We present the structural parameters obtained with the Murnaghan equation of state for $\text{Sc}_{1-x}\text{In}_x\text{N}$ in sodium chloride phase. The calculated lattice constant for ScN ($a = 3.19\ \text{\AA}$ or $a_0 = 4.5114\ \text{\AA}$) is slightly bigger than experimental value ($a = 3.182\ \text{\AA}$ or $a_0 = 4.501\ \text{\AA}$ [5]). This confirms the usual trend of GGA approach to overestimate the lattice constant value. The bulk modulus value ($B_0 = 189.9$ GPa) is in the experimental range ($B_0 = 182 \pm 40$ GPa [5]). We observe an increase of lattice constant with the increment of indium composition (see Fig. 2) and consequently a decrease of the bulk modulus is presented (see Fig. 3).

In Fig. 2 we examine the validity of the Vegard's law (linear relation $a(x)$) for $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ ternary compounds in sodium chloride phase. We observe that a slight deviation from Vegard's law is presented. We have calculated the deviation parameter of the lattice constant using the formula $a(x)=xa_{InN}+(1-x)a_{ScN}-\delta_a x(1-x)$ [18]. Our results predict a small value of the deviation parameter, $\delta_a=-0.02602\mathring{A}$, for $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ in sodium chloride structure. A small value of the deviation was expected, since the lattice constants ($a_{ScN}$ and $a_{InN}$) are similar. The lattice constant of InN is 4.5% bigger than one of ScN.

In Fig. 3 we show the behavior of the bulk modulus as a function of In-composition $(x)$ for $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ compounds in sodium chloride structure. We observe a small deviation of the bulk modulus from the linear composition dependence (LCD [11]). Following the function $B(x)=xB_{InN}+(1-x)B_{ScN}-bx(1-x)$ for bulk modulus [19], we find a small upward bowing parameter of $b=-4.4612$ GPa. The fact that the bulk modulus of InN is 20.9% smaller than one of ScN, implies a small bowing parameter.

Now we report the structural parameters obtained with the Murnaghan equation of state for $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ in wurtzite structure. The calculated lattice parameters $a=3.5865\mathring{A}$ and $c=5.7796\mathring{A}$ for InN overestimate the experimental values ($a=3.5376\mathring{A}$ and $c=5.7064\mathring{A}$ [8]) due to the use of GGA approach. The overestimation of lattice constant makes that the calculated bulk modulus ($B_0=118.30$ GPa) is slightly outside of the experimental range ($B_0=125.5\pm4.6$ GPa [9]). The lattice constant and bulk modulus present a similar behavior to that of sodium chloride phase with the increment of indium composition.

We present the lattice constant as a function of the x-composition compared with the Vegard's law for $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ in wurtzite structure (see Fig. 4). We find a small value of the lattice bowing parameter, $\delta_a=0.00461\mathring{A}$, for $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ in wurtzite phase. This deviation is due to that the lattice constant of InN is 1.8% bigger than one of ScN.

## 4. Conclusions

Our results for the binary are in good agreement with the experimental data. The bulk modulus of both structures presents an intermediate value with regard to the corre- sponding values for the silicon and the diamond, that makes of this ternary compound a relatively rigid material. Therefore, our study predicts that these compounds can be useful in the design of hard devices. Because experimental reports are not known for the $Sc_{1-x}In_xN$ compounds with $x=0.25,0.5,0.75$, our results can be useful for later studies.
