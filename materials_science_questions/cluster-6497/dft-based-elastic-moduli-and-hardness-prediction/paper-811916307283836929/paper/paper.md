# First-principles study of the structural stability and electronic structures of TaN

Using the plane-wave pseudopotential method within the generalized gradient approximation, we have studied the structural stability and electronic structures for several TaN phases. Our results show CoSn is the calculated ground-state structure of TaN among the five crystallographic structures that have been studied. The order of energetic stability of phase structures of TaN from low to high is: CsCl < ZnS-B3 < NaCl < WC < CoSn. The higher stability of TaN in the CoSn and WC structures is due to the formation of pseudogap around the Fermi level and the stronger hybridization between N-2p states and Ta-5d states. TaN in all structures studied has a metallic nature. The calculated bulk modulus indicates that TaN in the WC structure may be a less compressible material.

## 1 Introduction
The transition metal nitrides represent a technologically important series of materials and have attracted considerable attention during the past three decades due to their unique mechanical, electrical, and chemical properties. They are used widely in hard wear-resistant coatings, diffusion barriers, optical coatings, cutting tools, magnetic storage devices, generators, and maglev trains [1–3]. In particular, TaN is very attractive due to its high thermal stability, hardness (30.8 ± 0.9) [4], and conductivity (25–250 μΩ/cm) [5] to be a suitable barrier layer between copper and silicon wafers for the interconnections in ultralarge-scale integration (ULSI) devices [6–11]. The diffusion coefficient $D$ of Cu into single-crystal TaN was determined to be $D=(160\pm9.5)\exp[-(3.27\pm0.1)\text{eV}/k_{\text{B}}T]\text{cm}^2\text{s}^{-1}$ in the temperature range 600–700 °C, indicating a 5 nm thick single-crystalline cubic TaN layer can effectively prevent Cu diffusion up to 650 °C for 30 min or less annealing time [10]. In addition, tantalum nitrides are potentially important metal gate electrodes [12–14] for HfO₂-based high-$\kappa$ gate dielectrics in future metal–oxide–semiconductor field-effect transistor (MOSFET) devices because they allow, for example, tuning of the work function by varying the nitrogen content [15].

At ambient state, TaN has the CoSn structure (denoted as $\varepsilon$-TaN) of space group P62m, in which Ta atoms occupy the Wyckoff positions of 1a (0, 0, 0) and 2d (1/3, 2/3, 1/2), and N atoms the Wyckoff position 3f (0.3928, 0, 0) [16]. The low-temperature and high-pressure phase of TaN (denoted as $\theta$-TaN) has the WC structure [17, 18], while the high-temperature and high-pressure phase of TaN (denoted as $\delta$-TaN) has the NaCl structure [18–20]. Due to the inherent richness in the phase diagram of the Ta–N system [21], this complexity leads to difficulty in synthesizing single-crystal TaN [22] and determining the physical properties of stoichiometric TaN.

The recent chemical syntheses of novel nitride compounds [23–26] have stimulated extensive studies on transition metal nitrides to search for less compressible materials [27–29]. Recent first-principles calculations on OsN [28] and MoN [29] revealed that their hexagonal phases (WC structure) have higher bulk modulus than the cubic phases (NaCl structure) and that these nitrides with hexagonal phases are low-compressibility materials. For TaN, the bulk modulus of its cubic phase (NaCl structure) has been predicted from first-principles calculations [30–35] to be at least 328.3 GPa [34], which is comparable to those

In this work, the first-principles plane-wave pseudopotential method within the generalized gradient approximation is employed to pre- dict the structural parameters and bulk modulus of TaN and to investigate the electronic structures of TaN.

## 2 Computational method
All calculations on TaN were performed by means of the quantum ESPRESSO code [36], which is based on the density functional theory (DFT) [37]. The electronic exchange-correlation potential was treated by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the scheme of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [38]. Electron-ion interaction was represented by the ultrasoft Vanderbilt pesudopotential [39]. The pseudo- potential for the tantalum atom was generated in the va- lence electron configuration $5s^{2}5p^{6}5d^{3}6s^{2}6p^{0}$ and that for the nitrogen atom in $2s^{2}2p^{3}$. Wave functions were ex- panded by the plane waves with kinetic energy up to 40 Ry. The k-point sampling in the Brillouin zone (BZ) was treated using the Monkhorst-Pack scheme [40]. The k-point meshes are $12 × 12 × 12$, $12 × 12 × 12$, $8 × 8 × 14$, $10 × 10 × 10$, and $12 × 12 × 12$ for the CsCl, NaCl, CoSn, WC, and ZnS-B3 structures of TaN, respectively. The con- vergence of the total energy with respect to the size of the k-mesh and the plane-wave energy cut-off was examined respectively for each phase and the above calculation setup was found to provide good precision in the present study.

## 3 Results and discussion
Ball-and-stick models for the crystal structures of the phases of TaN are illustrated in Fig. 1. In order to determine the energetic stability and the structural parameters of TaN, for each structure we calcu- lated the total energy as a function of volume. Total energy versus volume data for each structure of TaN are shown in Fig. 2. Energies and volumes are given in per single TaN formula unit (f.u.): there are three TaN formula units in a unit cell of the CoSn structure, and one in all the other cases. The sum of the energies of isolated neutral Ta and N atoms is taken as the zero. Therefore, the absolute value of the energy at the minimum of each curve gives the cohe- sive energy of the corresponding TaN phase. The data for each phase were fitted to the third-order Birch-Murnaghan equation of state [41]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(x) & =E_{0}+\frac{3}{2} K_{0} V_{0} \\
& \times\left[\frac{3}{2}(\xi-1) x^{2 / 3}+\frac{3}{4}(1-2 \xi) x^{4 / 3}+\frac{1}{2} \xi x^{6 / 3}-\frac{2 \xi-3}{4}\right], \\
& \quad(1)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x=V_{0}/V$, $\xi=3/4(K_{0}'-4)$, $V_{0}$ is the equilibrium vol- ume, $E_{0}$ is the cohesive energy, $K_{0}$ is the bulk modulus, and $K_{0}'$ is the first pressure derivative of bulk modulus. In this way, we can obtain the equilibrium lattice constants and other ground-state properties of TaN (see Table 1). In all cases, the fitting is very good, as shown in Fig. 2, indicat- ing that a different choice of volume and/or a different equation of state would not alter the results.

Table 1 presents the calculated lattice constants, bulk modulus, and first pressure derivative of bulk modulus for each phase of TaN. The order of energetic stability of phase structures of TaN from low to high is: CsCl < ZnS-B3 < NaCl < WC < CoSn. This is consistent with a previous prediction [27]. The CoSn structure is the calculated ground state with bulk modulus $K_{0}=303.6$ GPa and cohe- sive energy $E_{0}=-14.046$ eV. This is a quite small energy difference, indicating it is probably easy to stabilize a metastable WC structure of TaN. The calculated lattice constants of TaN in the WC structure are $a = 2.913$ Å and $c = 2.862$ Å, which agree well with experimental results [18, 21], and the bulk modulus is about 337.1 GPa. For TaN in the NaCl structure, the cohesive energy is about 0.676 eV per TaN f.u. higher than that of the CoSn structure, indicating the phase transition from the CoSn structure to the NaCl structure could be driven by a high temperature. The calculated lattice constant of TaN in the NaCl structure is $4.413$ Å, which is in good agreement with experiment values [4, 17, 42, 43] and previous theoretical results [32, 34, 35] obtained from a variety of first-principles calculations (see Table 1). For the bulk modulus of TaN in the NaCl structure, the theoretical values reported in the literature exhibit a considerable spread (see Table 1) which may be attributed to different approximations used for the exchange-correlation energy functional in previous calculations [30–32, 34, 35]. Tests show that the local density approximation (LDA) tends to overestimate the bulk modulus of solid but the GGA usually underestimates it [44, 45]. In the present GGA calculations, the obtained bulk modulus of TaN in the NaCl structure is 327.6 GPa, which is in excellent agreement with previous GGA calculation results from the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW) method (328.3 GPa) [34] and the plane-wave pseudopotential (PW-PP) method (329 GPa) [35]. The above results for the energetic stability of TaN are consistent with the findings in experimental studies [18, 20]. Actually, the WC-type phase of TaN can be prepared by static compression of CoSn-type phase powder at high-pressure conditions of 0.8–3 GPa and at temperatures lower than 1500–1800 °C, while the NaCl-type phase of TaN can be prepared by shock compression of CoSn-type phase powder [18, 20], or obtained by epitaxial growth on Mg(001) substrate and Si substrate with TiN buffer layer at 600 °C [4, 22]. For the ZnS-B3 structure of TaN, the calculated lattice constant and bulk modulus are $4.753$ Å and 244.2 GPa, respectively. For the CsCl structure of TaN, the calculated lattice constant and bulk modulus are $2.750$ Å and 307.0 GPa, respectively. To the best of our knowledge, the ZnS-B3 and CsCl structures of TaN are not reported in experiments. In our calculations, the cohesive energy of the ZnS-B3 structure is about 1.004 eV per TaN f.u. higher than that of the CoSn structure and 0.328 eV per TaN f.u. higher than that of the NaCl structure, and the equilibrium volume of the ZnS-B3 structure is about 25% larger than that of the NaCl structure. The cohesive energy of the CsCl structure is about 2.193 eV per TaN f.u. higher than that of the CoSn structure and 0.684 eV per TaN f.u. higher than that of the NaCl structure, and the equilibrium volume of the CsCl structure is about 3% smaller than that of the NaCl structure. These data suggest that phase transition from the NaCl structure to the ZnS-B3 structure or the CsCl structure may occur at extremely high temperature and explain why the ZnS-B3 and CsCl structures of TaN have not been synthesized yet. Since the inverse of the bulk modulus is the compressibility, from the bulk modulus of TaN listed in Table 1 we can see that the order of compressibility from low to high is: WC < NaCl < CsCl < CoSn < ZnS-B3. This indicates the WC structure of TaN has the lowest compressibility and may be a less compressible material.

In order to understand the electronic nature of TaN in the different structures, we have calculated the band structures, total density of states (DOS) by the tetrahedron method [46], and atomic projected DOS by the Löwdin populations [47]. Figure 3 shows the band structures of TaN at equilibrium in the CoSn, WC, CsCl, ZnS-B3, and NaCl structures. Figure 4 presents the total and atomic projected DOS of TaN. From these figures, we can see that all phases of TaN exhibit a metallic characteristic and their valence bands split into two groups except in the ZnS-B3 structure of TaN. For TaN in both the CoSn and WC structures, there is a deep valley called the pseudogap near the Fermi level, which results from the strong hybridiza- tion between Ta-5d states and N-2p states and also leads to a separation between the bonding states and antibonding ones. The presence of a pseudogap indicates significant covalent bonding between Ta and N atoms in the CoSn and WC structures of TaN. Our results for the DOS of TaN in the CoSn structure are similar to those reported by other research groups [33, 48]. For TaN in the CsCl structure, a marked and sharp van Hove singularity occurs in the DOS at the Fermi level, which is attributed to the $\mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{g}}$ orbtial of Ta atoms (see Fig. 4c) and would lead to an instability of TaN in the CsCl structure. In addition, the energy region for the hybridization between Ta-5d states and N-2p states in the CsCl structure is from -10 eV to 0 eV, which is wider than those in the other cases (see Fig. 4) and hence suggests that Ta-N bonding in the CsCl structure exhibits metallic char- acteristics. For TaN in the ZnS-B3 structure, the valence bands split into three groups: the lower group from -18 eV to -14 eV is contributed mainly by the 2s states of N atoms with a small contribution from 5d states of Ta atoms, just as in other structures; in the intermediate group from -9 eV to -5 eV, Ta-5d states and N-2p states exhibit a strong hybridization, the energy gap between the interme- diate group and the upper one being about 1.5 eV; the upper group and DOS at the Fermi level are dominated by the 5d states of Ta atoms. For TaN in the NaCl structure, the bands from -10 eV to -4 eV mainly come from the 5d states of Ta atoms and the 2p states of N atoms that also exhibit a strong hybridization, which is also consistent with the results from previous first-principles calculations [30, 33, 49-51] and resonant photoemission study [52] of TaN. The bands from -4 eV to the Fermi energy level of TaN in the NaCl struc- ture are dominated by the $\mathrm{t}_{2 \mathrm{~g}}$ orbtial of Ta atoms (see Fig. 4e) and the corresponding DOS exhibits a feature of 'near free electron'. The DOS around the Fermi energy level of TaN in the NaCl structure is much higher than in the CoSn and WC structures (see Table 2), indicating TaN in the NaCl structure has higher conductivity. For each struc- ture of TaN, the atomic partial charge of each atom obtained from the Löwdin populations is listed in Table 2, from which we can see that the charge transfer occurs from the Ta atom towards the nitrogen atom, consistent with the trends observed in TaN by X-ray emission spectroscopy (XES) [53, 54]. These results can be understood from the electronega- tivities of Ta (1.5) and N (3.04) in the Pauling scale [55].

To further explore the bonding nature in each TaN structure, we examine the charge distribution in TaN and present the contour map of charge density on the (110) plane for each structure of TaN in Fig. 5. For TaN in the CoSn, WC, NaCl, and ZnS-B3 structures, the charge den- sity around N atoms exhibits a strong directional distribu- tion toward Ta atoms in all cases, indicating the bonding between Ta and N atoms is covalent. The charge density between Ta and N atoms in the WC structure is much denser than those in the other structures, indicating the Ta-N bonding in the WC structure has much more signifi- cant covalency than that in the other structures and hence explaining why TaN in the WC structure has the largest bulk modulus. For TaN in the CsCl structure, the charge density around N and Ta atoms is accumulated more evenly, indicating the Ta-N bond is metallic. All of these results are consistent with the discussion about the DOS for each structure of TaN.

4 Conclusions In summary, we have employed the plane-wave pseudopotential method within the generalized gradient approximation and carried out density functional theory calculations to study the structural and electronic properties of TaN in the CsCl, NaCl, CoSn, WC, and ZnS- B3 structures. The calculated lattice parameters of each phase of TaN are in good agreement with available ex- perimental results. Our results show that the hexagonal phases of TaN are more stable energetically than the cubic phases due to the formation of a pseudogap around the Fermi level, the CoSn structure is the ground-state one, the WC structure has largest bulk modulus, all phases of TaN
