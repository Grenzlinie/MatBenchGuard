# EXAMINATION OF INTERNAL STRESSES IN BORON NITRIDE POLYCRYSTALS

O. N. Grigor'ev, W. Kreher, W. Pompe,
and V. I. Trefilov

UDC 539.319:621.922

The production of high-strength and crack-resistant ceramic materials is one of the most important problems of materials science. Investigations have been carried out recently into a number of processes (for example, microcracking and phase transformations at the crack tip) increasing the cracking resistance of ceramics [1, 2] whose efficiency is determined by the nature of distribution and level of internal stresses [3]. Therefore the problem of optimiz- ing the stress state is highly topical.

The present work is concerned with analysis of the stress state of highly dispersed poly- crystals based on high-pressure phases of boron nitride with wurtzite ($BN_W$) and sphalerite ($BN_S$) lattices. The polycrystals can also contain small amounts (approximately 1-2%) of a graphitelike modification of boron nitride ($BN_g$).

Synthesis of the polycrystals of boron nitride was carried out at high temperatures ($T_S\geq2000^{\circ}K$) and pressures ($P_S\geq5$ GPa). The special features of the structure and mechanical properties of the polycrystals were described in [4-7].

Usually, the polycrystals show splitting of the single recorded (by x-ray diffraction) line (0002) of $BN_g$ corresponding to the change of interplanar distance along the axis C from 0.333 to 0.310 nm. Analysis of the results obtained in thermal cycling in the conditions with microcracking with partial disappearance of the displaced component of the diffraction curve indicates that displacement of the line is caused by strain. The corresponding compres- sive strain $\varepsilon_{33}\sim7-8\%$; at Young's modulus $E_{0001}=32$ GPa, this strain corresponds to compres- sive stresses in $BN_g$ of approximately 2.4 GPa.

The results show that in thermal cycling and also on specimens after compressive tests in which the polycrystals disintegrated into particles 10-20 $\mu$m in size in an explosionlike manner, the width of the diffraction lines of $BN_W$ and $BN_S$ greatly decreases. In various specimens, the compressive stresses $\sigma_{1}, \sigma_{2}$ vary in the range 300-1300, the tensile stresses $\sigma_{3}$ in the range 200-800 MPa.

Various theoretical models with determined ranges of applicability [8,9] are available for calculating the internal stresses in ceramic materials. The most promising method is the statistical theory used in the present work which, in addition to the mean stresses in the individual phases, provides information on fluctuations of the stresses. On the basis of an information formulation of this problem [10], for a heterogeneous solid with the statistically varying tensor of Hook's modulus $C=C(x)$ and (thermal) strains $\varepsilon^{T}$ independent of external stresses, we can calculate the distribution function of the inherent stresses [11].

For these calculations, it is necessary to have the following initial experimental parameters: direct data on the structure in the form of the probability density $\varphi(C, \varepsilon^{T})$ ; general information on the material in the form of effective elasticity modulus $C^{*}$ and effective strains $\varepsilon^{*}$ independent of the external stresses. The calculation method was described in detail in [11].

Taking into account the main equations of mechanics and using the principle of maximum entropy, we can write the following expression for the distribution function of local strain p at some point of space [11]

$$
p\left(C, \varepsilon^{\tau}, \varepsilon\right)=\varphi\left(C, \varepsilon^{\tau}\right) \cdot \frac{1}{z_{1}} \exp \left[\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{0}\right) E^{-1}\left(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{0}\right)\right], \tag{1}
$$

where $\varepsilon_{0}=C^{-1} \mu_{\sigma}+\mu_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2} \varepsilon^{\tau}$.

The tensors $E$ and $\varepsilon_{0}$ are determined by the elastic moduli and stress-independent strains, and also by the form of the examined grain. Specifically, for an isotropic material with

spherical grains, the values of the Young's modulus are determined from the equation

$$
E_{ijkl}=\frac{1}{30 G(1-v)} \cdot\left(2(4-5 v) I_{ijkl}-\delta_{ij} \delta_{kl}\right),
\tag{2}
$$

where $G$ is the shear modulus, $v$ is Poisson's ratio at the examined point, $\mathrm{I}_{ijk l}=1 / 2\left(\delta_{i k} \delta_{j l}+\right.$ $\left.\delta_{i l} \delta_{j k}\right), \delta_{i j}$ is Kronecker's symbol

The remaining parameters in Eq. (1) are written in the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\sigma}= & C+\left(C^{+}-C^{-}\right)^{-1} C^{-}\left(\varepsilon^{*}-\frac{\gamma_{\sigma}+\gamma_{\varepsilon}}{2}\right), \quad \mu_{\varepsilon}=\left(C^{+}-C^{-}\right)^{-1} \times \\
& \times\left(-C^{-} \varepsilon^{*}+\frac{C^{+} \gamma_{\sigma}+C^{-} \gamma_{\varepsilon}}{2}\right), \quad \mu=\frac{1}{12}\left(\varepsilon^{\tau}\left(\varepsilon^{\tau}-\gamma_{\sigma} C^{+} \gamma_{\sigma}\right)\right)- \\
& -\frac{1}{3}\left(\varepsilon^{*}-\frac{\gamma_{\sigma}+\gamma_{\varepsilon}}{2}\right) C^{+}\left(C^{+}+C^{-}\right)^{-1} C^{-}\left(\varepsilon^{*}-\frac{\gamma_{\sigma}+\gamma_{\varepsilon}}{2}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

where $C^{+}=\overline{\bar{C}}, C^{-}=\left(\overline{\bar{C}^{-1}}\right)^{-1}, \gamma_{\varepsilon}=\bar{\varepsilon}^{\tau}, \gamma_{\sigma}=(\bar{C})^{-1} \cdot\left(\overline{C \varepsilon^{\tau}}\right)$. The symbol $(\overline{)}$ represents averaging using the probability density $\varphi\left(C, \varepsilon^{\mathrm{T}}\right)$. For example: $\bar{C}=\int C \varphi\left(C, \varepsilon^{\tau}\right) d \varepsilon^{\tau} d C$.

To examine the contributions of the individual sources of the internal stresses, the general problem was divided into the following partial cases.

In the first case, synthesis of the polycrystals was carried out in the course of the phase transition $\mathrm{BN}_{\mathrm{W}} \rightarrow \mathrm{BN}_{\mathrm{S}}$. The rigid frame forms [12] already in the initial stages of isothermal holding ( $t \sim 5 \mathrm{sec}$ ) and with sintering time increasing to the usual duration ( $t \sim$ $30 \mathrm{sec}$ ) the fields of internal stresses form in the polycrystal during the phase transition. The transformation takes place at $\mathrm{T}>\mathrm{T} * \approx 2000^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$ ( $\mathrm{T} *$ is regarded as the characteristic temperature below which stress relaxation is not possible) since the stresses caused by the phase transition can relax.

The fields of inherent stresses form in cooling $\left(\Delta \mathrm{T}=-1700^{\circ} \mathrm{K}\right)$ and unloading (at $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{T} *$ the polycrystal is subjected to the external pressure $\sigma_{a} \geqslant-5 \mathrm{GPa}$ ) as a result of the differences in the strains in the individual phases $\varepsilon_{i j}=e^{\tau} \delta_{i j}$. In this case, the value of $e^{\mathrm{T}}$ is determined by the expressions

\tag{4}

in calculating the stresses caused by the differences in the coefficients of thermal expansion of the examined phases (the phases are assumed to be isotropic) and

$$

\tag{5}
$$

as a result of the difference of the elastic strains in the phases in unloading ( $\alpha$ and $\mathrm{K}$ are respectively the coefficient of thermal expansion and the modulus of compressibility of the examined phase).

In the conditions of the real existing texture [0001] of $BN_{w}$ along the axis of specimen [14], the level of the tensile stresses rapidly increases and, as shown by analysis, both phases in the $BN_{W}-BN_{S}$ two-phase system are characterized by tensile axial and compressive radial stresses. This stressed state of the polycrystals was also detected in the experiments. Consequently, the texture of the grains of $BN_{w}$ is responsible for the macrostresses in the examined end layers of the polycrystals whereas other stress sources are less important. The mean microstresses $(\bar{\sigma}_{II} \sim 1.5$  GPa) measured by x-ray diffraction in the high-pressure phases of the boron nitride coincide qualitatively with the resulting calculated variance value $\Delta \sigma \sim 1 GPa$ ; this may indicate that the model used in the calculations is adequate.

However, in graphitelike BN, the calculated compressive stresses are approximately four times higher than the experimentally measured stresses. This difference is causedboth by the differences in the accepted model (isotropic) in comparison with real (anisotropic) system and by the relaxation processes which were not taken into account in the calculations, i.e., plastic deformation, failure of the matrix in the vicinity of the inclusions of $BN_{g}$ , and others, up to the $BN_{g} \to BN_{S}$ reverse transition which can take place at compressive stresses approximately equal to the calculated stresses (11 GPa).

The magnitude of the microstresses $\sigma_{II }$ (taking into account the fact that these stresses are characterized by the Gaussian-type distribution) at the individual points of the specimen can be similar to the magnitude of the externally applied hydrostatic pressure ga in sintering. This means that there are microvolumes of the material with various signs of $\sigma_{a}$ and $\sigma_{II}$ , in which there is no general compression. This is reflected in the formation of small amounts of the $BN_{g}$ in sintering even in the range of stability of the high-pressure phases.
