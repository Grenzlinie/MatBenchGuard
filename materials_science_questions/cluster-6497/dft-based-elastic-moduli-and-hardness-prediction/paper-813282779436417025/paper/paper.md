## ABSTRACT

Vanadium carbides (VCs) as reinforced phase effectively improve the mechanical properties of steel and coatings. By means of nano-indentation and first-principles techniques, the mechanical properties of various vanadium carbides were investigated. Our results show that cubic VC in Ni-Cr cast iron possesses a relatively high Vickers hardness ($33.3 \pm 0.5$ GPa) and Young's modulus ($436 \pm 8$ GPa). Furthermore, the mechanical properties and electronic structure of various vanadium carbides, including $V_2C$, $V_4C_3$, $V_6C_5$, and $V_8C_7$, were also studied to reveal the effect of carbon content on the mechanical properties of V-C system.

### 1. Introduction

Vanadium carbides (VCs) were widely used as reinforced phase in matrix or coating materials to improve wear resistance of metal materials [1-3]. The uniformly distributed large amount spherical or lumpy VCs in high speed steel was found with an improved ability to resist the micro-cutting of $Al_2O_3$ abrasive, when V and C content is up to 8.15-10.20% and 2.70-3.15%, respectively [4]. Besides, the wear resistance of high vanadium high speed steel is four times greater than the high chromium cast iron because of the presence of VCs [5]. To understand the mechanical properties of VCs, substoichiometric VC film was fabricated by Krzanowski et al. [6], the nano-indentation hardness is measured to be 26 GPa. Also, cubic VC film was deposited on Ti substrate using electron beam method and a maximum hardness of 25 GPa was found by Ferro et al. [7]. Besides, Vanadium carbide coatings with different carbon contents were synthesized by Wu et al. [8], the measured Vickers hardness of vanadium carbide coatings was found to increase with additional carbon content from $V_2C$ to cubic VC ($c$-VC), a hardness of 35.5 GPa and Young's modulus of 358 GPa were discovered for $c$-VC coating, but further adding of carbon atoms, the mechanical properties were decreasing due to the existence of excessive amorphous carbon in the coatings. Although efforts were made on vanadium carbides coating materials, few experimental studies on the mechanical behaviors of vanadium carbides in bulk are reported. Theoretically, researches on vanadium carbides mainly concentrate on stoichiometric $c$-VC [9-12].Theoretical Vickers hardness of $c$-VC was predicted by Liu et al. [11] and Šimůnek [12]. The carbon-vacancy ordered VCs, such as $V_8C_7$ and $V_6C_5$, were also observed in experiments, nevertheless, their mechanical performances still remain open till now. In this paper, VC particles were synthesized in high V content alloyed cast iron. Nanoindentation technique was used to evaluate the hardness and Young's modulus. To further understand the effect of carbon content on the mechanical properties of VCs, the elastic and electronic properties of $c$-VC, $V_8C_7$, $V_6C_5$, $V_4C_3$, $V_2C$, and pure V metal were studied via first-principles calculations. The systematical study on different stoichiometric VCs would provide further understanding of vanadium carbides for the potential synthesis and applications.

### 2. Experimental and calculation details

High Ni-Cr cast iron and ferrovanadium were melted in argon atmosphere. Ferrovanadium was plunged into Ni-Cr cast iron at $1520\ ^\circ\text{C}$. During the solidification, vanadium carbides formed in the cast iron. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of vanadium carbides were taken by HITACHI S-4800 equipped with energy-dispersive system (EDS) EMAX 7593-H. Pure vanadium carbides was obtained by dissolving the solidified high vanadium content Ni-Cr cast iron in HCl solution, and its crystal structure was characterized by conventional X-ray diffraction using D-Max 2500 apparatus (step canning mode at peace of 4 s per $0.02^\circ$ angle, Cu target, $K_{\alpha1}$ radiation, $\lambda=1.54060\mathring{A}$). Specimen for high resolution TEM (JEOL-2010) was prepared by putting a drop of alcohol suspension of pure vanadium carbides pulverized in quartz crucible on Cu TEM gird.

Nanoindentation testing of in situ synthesized VCs in Ni-Cr alloy was performed at room temperature using Nano-mechanical Test Instruments (Hysitron Inc. TriboIndenter) with a diamond Cube Corner indenter (curvature radius 100 nm). Fused silica was used as reference sample for the initial tip calibration procedure. Each indentation experiment consisted of three steps: loading, holding the indenter at peak load for 10 s, and unloading completely. The selected peak

Data were processed using the Hysitron software providing load-displacement curves corrected for thermal drift and machine constants (frame compliance, transducer spring force, and electrostatic force constants). Load-displacement curves were analyzed according to the Oliver-Pharr method [13] by fitting a power law relationship to the unloading curve and determining the initial unloading stiffness. For each peak load, indentations were made on at least five different VC particles in order to keep the reliability of the experimental results.

First principle calculations were conducted based on Density Functional Theory (DFT) as implanted in CASTEP code [14]. To guarantee the convergence of calculation, A 10 × 10 × 10 k-point and cutoff energy of 350 eV were adopted to calculate the ground state and elastic constants ($C_{ij}$) of c-VC (space group: $Fm-3m$, No. 225, $Z = 4$) [15], $V_8C_7$ (space group: $P4_332$, No. 212, $Z = 4$) [16], $P3_1-V_6C_5$ (No. 144, $Z = 3$) [17], $C2/m-V_6C_5$ (No. 12, $Z = 2$) [18], $V_4C_3$ (space group: $R-3m$, No. 166, $Z = 3$) [19], and $V_2C$ (space group: $Pbcn$, No. 60, $Z = 4$) [20]. The exchange and correlation functional were treated by generalized gradient approximation of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE) [21]. Previous experiment studies show that these vanadium carbides can be synthesized by the solid state reaction of vanadium and carbon (graphite) [22]. The heat of formation of these vanadium carbides were calculated by following equations: $\Delta H_f = E_{total}(V_xC_y) - (xE_{total}(V) + yE_{total}(C))$, where $E_{total}(V_xC_y)$ is the total energy of $V_xC_y$ at equilibrium lattice constants, $E_{total}(V)$ and $E_{total}(C)$ are the calculated total energy of pure V and graphite, respectively. Bulk and shear moduli were estimated based on Voight-Reuss-Hill (VRH) approximation [23], and then Elastic moduli and poission's ratio were obtained based on the relationship as follows: $E = 9BG/(3B + G)$, $v = (3B - 2G)/2(3B + G)$.

## 3. Results and discussion

The chemical composition of the cast iron was shown in Table 1. The morphology and microstructure of the as-synthesized VCs dendrite during the solidification were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and TEM. Fig. 1(a) shows the abundance, size and distribution of the as-synthesized VCs. After dissolving in HCl solution, Pure VCs has been obtained by filtering the turbid liquid of HCl contain dissolved alloyed cast iron (Fig. 1(b)). The diameter of the VC dendrites ranges from 1 to 20 $\mu$m. X-ray diffraction pattern (XRD) (Fig. 1(c)) shows that the obtained pure VCs crystallizes in the structure of c-VC (space group: $Fm-3m$, No. 225, $Z = 4$). Bright field TEM of the pulverized VC and its selected area electron diffraction (SAED) was shown in Fig. 1(d) and SAED also suggests that the obtained VC belongs to the cubic structure. Usually, $V_8C_7$ has the same atomic structure as c-VC with subsidiary difference derived from carbon vacancy. To provide further evidence for the credibility of c-VC, Fig. 1(e) presents HRTEM of VC and simulated SAEDs for c-VC and $V_8C_7$. As shown in the simulated SAED of $V_8C_7$, weak spots belong to ordered C atoms show up, however, these spots are absent in the experimental SAED. Also, the measured plane distance in the Fourier filtering processed HRTEM (Fig. 1(f)) is 0.24035 and 0.20913 nm, fitting well with the distance of plane (111) and (200) of c-VC (JCPDS, PDF card No.:65-8074) with discrepancy of 0.04% and 0.43%, respectively, which indicates the growth of high quality VC.

To understand the mechanical property of obtained VC, nano-indentation test was used to evaluate hardness of c-VC shown in Fig. 1(a). To further study the mechanical properties of V–C system, the various stoichiometric vanadium carbides were studied by first-principles calculations. The calculated lattice parameters of these considered carbides are listed in Table 2, together with available experimental and theoretical values for comparison [15–20,24]. As seen from Table 2, the calculated results are in well agreement with experimental data: the deviations of lattice parameters between the calculated and experimental values are less than 1.05% (parameter $a$ value of $V_{4}C_{3}$), indicating the reliability of our calculations. For $V_{6}C_{5}$, trigonal $P3_{1}$ structure has relatively lower total energy than monoclinic $C2/m$ structure, i.e. $P3_{1}$-type $V_{6}C_{5}$ is energetically favorable structure at zero pressure. However, the slight difference in total energy between $P3_{1}$ and $C2/m$ structures (0.0236 eV/fu) hints the possibility of the coexistence of $P3_{1}$ and $C2/m$ phases in real experiment [12]. The negative $\Delta H_{f}$ values (Table 2) show that all these carbides are thermodynamically stable and thus could explain why these vanadium carbides have been synthesized experimentally [1–8,15–20,24]. Elastic constant $(C_{ij})$ could provide important information for mechanical stability, elastic moduli, and thus hardness of materials. The calculated $C_{ij}$ of c-VC and other considered phases (Table 2) are found to satisfy Born-Huang stability criterion [25], implying their mechanical stability. $C_{ij}$ results as well as calculated bulk modulus $(B)$ and shear modulus $(G)$ (Table 3) of c-VC fit well with the available results. As illustrated in Fig. 2(b), $B$ changes irregularly with the increase of carbon content: VC has the maximum $B$ value, 302 GPa, (308 GPa in Ref. [25]), and $P3_1$-V₆C₅ phase exhibits the lowest value, 237 GPa. On the contrary, $G$ and $E$ value gradually increases from V₂C (121/311 GPa) to VC (228/547 GPa), suggesting $G$ and $E$ are more dependent on carbon content than bulk modulus $B$. Similar trend has been found in transition metal carbon nitrides of Ti/Hf-C-N and Zr-Nb-C [26]. For $P3_1$-V₆C₅, $B$ and $G$ (237 and 176 GPa, respectively) are very close to that of V₈C₇ (243 and 180 GPa), which could be due to their similar carbon-vacancy and bonding features.

The high $G$ and low $v$ of c-VC, $P3_1$-V₆C₅, and V₈C₇ suggest their strong directional bonding and thus could be potential very hard materials. Based on the empirically correlation between $G$ and Vickers hardness $(H_v, H_v=2(k^2G)^{0.583}-3)$ [10], we estimate their $H_v$ as listed in Table 3. The calculated $H_v$ of V₈C₇ (VC₀.₈₇₅) and VC is 26.4 and 31.5 GPa, respectively, fitting well with the experimentally measured results, $29\pm2$ GPa (VC₀.₈₈) [27] and 29 GPa (VC) [12]. Here, our calculated $H_v$ of c-VC is quite close to the nanoindentation result, $33.3\pm0.5$ GPa. Although V₂C in bulk has the lowest Vickers hardness value (11.7 GPa), it is still greater than previously studied superhard film such as RuB₂ (10.9 GPa) and RhB₁.₁ (7.0 GPa) [28], suggesting it is a potential candidate for superhard films.

To uncover the origin of superior mechanical properties of VCs, their total and partial densities of states (DOSs) were calculated and presented in Fig. 3. All these phases exhibit metallic character as indicated by non-zero values at Fermi level mainly composed of $V$-$3d$ state. For V₂C, the DOS spectrum of pure V has been preserved around the Fermi level region because of the lower addition of carbon content. For c-VC, a much lower DOS value at Fermi level than other phases suggests more stable than the other carbides, which is consistent with calculated formation enthalpy results (Table 2). Furthermore, a universal hybridization between $V$-$3d$ and $C$-$2p$ states has been found for all these carbides, resulting in the formation of covalent bonding between V and C atoms. It is notable that the overlap between $V$-$3d$ and $C$-$2p$ orbital in V₄C₃ is more extent horizontally than that in V₂C, $P3_1$-V₆B₅, and V₈C₇, and this explains the higher bulk modulus of V₄C₃. To evaluate the covalence of bonding, Mulliken overlap population (MOP) of V-C bonding has been calculated and tabulated in Table 3. A higher positive MOP value represents a higher degree of covalency of bonding and thus higher bond strength [29]. As indicated by MOP values, the strongest covalent V-C bonding of c-VC make sense its largest bulk modulus (i.e. incompressibility) and shear modulus (i.e. ability to resist shear deformation). However, the relatively higher MOP value of V₄C₃ than neighboring phase, V₂C and $P3_1$-V₆C₅, is rather puzzling

in view of its higher bulk modulus but lower shear modulus than $P3_1$-$V_6C_5$. To explore the reason for this discrepancy, the atomic configuration of $V_4C_3$ and $P3_1$-$V_6B_5$ has been shown in Fig. 4. Part of the covalent V-C bonding in $P3_1$-$V_6B_5$ Fig. 4b is found to be parallel with (100) plane along [041], and this configuration of V-C bonding is lack in $V_4C_3$. Therefore, the resistance of shear strain along (100) plane along [011] direction has been strengthened in $P3_1$-$V_6B_5$, leading to higher $C_{44}$, and thus higher shear modulus than $V_4C_3$. In sum, besides high covalency bonding, atomic configuration is another factor influences the resistance to shear deformation of materials, and thus the Vickers hardness.

Furthermore, the effect of carbon content can be summarized from the aspects of stability and mechanical properties. As revealed by the calculated formation enthalpy, the thermodynamic stability of the vanadium carbides monotonously increases with carbon content from $V_2C$ to VC. This trend was further verified by the synthesized pure c-VC, rather than the V-rich vanadium carbides, in the solidified high V-content Ni-Cr cast iron. As for the mechanical properties of vanadium carbides, with the introduction of carbon atom into the cavity of parent vanadium structure, there is a generally increase of elastic moduli, especially for $G$ and $E$ values, from pure V to c-VC, while $B$ values show irregular changing trend with carbon content. However, due to the closer relationship between $G$ and hardness, the hardness of vanadium carbides presents a monotonous increasing trend. The reasons for the changing of mechanical properties can be analyzed from the covalence of V-C bonding and atomic configuration in vanadium carbides.

## 4. Conclusion

Vanadium carbide was synthesized in V alloyed cast iron, and characterized to be c-VC by SEM, XRD, and TEM techniques. Nano-indentation measurements show that the hardness and Young's modulus of c-VC was $33.3\pm0.5$ and $436\pm8$ GPa, fitting well with the results of first-principles calculation. The calculation also reveals that Young's modulus, Shear modulus and hardness increases with carbon content from $V_2C$ to VC.
