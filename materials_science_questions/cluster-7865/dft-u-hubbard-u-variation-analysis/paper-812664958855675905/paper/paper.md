## ABSTRACT
The double perovskites $GdBaCo_2O_{5.5}$ (GBCO) is candidate cathode materials for application in intermediate temperature solid oxide fuel cells (IT-SOFCs), attributing to excellent electrical conduction properties and oxidation activity. The electronic structure and the oxygen adsorption at BaO terminated surface of GBCO are investigated using projector augmented-wave (PAW) method based on spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) with the inclusion of on-site Coulomb interaction. The BaO-terminated surface is adopted according to recent experimental results that this termination is energetically favorable for the double perovskites GBCO. We predict that G-AFM configuration is the most stable structure. The calculated results of oxygen vacancy energy indicate that the lowest energy oxygen vacancy positions are in the GdO layers. Charge transfer for the adsorbed oxygen and the surface ions is also examined by Bader charge analysis. The oxygen vacancies can facilitate oxygen adsorption and catalyze the bond cleavage. The existence of oxygen vacancy strengthens surface activity by acting as an active site at the surface. These results at the atomic level can contribute to the detailed understanding of oxygen reduction reaction mechanism.

## 1. Introduction
The solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs) have received considerable researches due to environmentally clean, excellent fuel flexibility and efficient power generation [1–3]. Mixed ionic and electronic conducting oxide (MIECs) are attracting more attention for their applications in SOFCs cathodes [4,5]. Especially, the double perovskites family $ABaCo_2O_{5+\delta}$ (A for rare-earths) [6–8] shows excellent electronic chemical performance for intermediate temperature SOFCs cathode relative to $La_{0.5}Sr_{0.5}CoO_{3-\delta}$, $La_{0.5}Sr_{0.5}FeO_{3-\delta}$ and $La_2NiO_{4+\delta}$ systems [9,10]. The $GdBaCo_2O_{5.5}$ (GBCO) is a typical representative of a double perovskites oxide with relatively high mixed conductivity [11]. The orthorhombic structure of GBCO is shown in Fig. 1, according to the $[GdO_{0.5}]-[CoO_2]-[BaO]-[CoO_2]$ stacking sequence along with c-axis [12]. The primitive cell with Pmmm space group includes 19 atoms [13]. Recently, the Co spin state and magnetic properties of GBCO have been studied [14,15], which confirms the transition to metallic phase may be accompanied by spin state transition of Co. The oxygen reduction reaction at the SOFCs cathode surface is a multi-step chemical reaction, which includes adsorption, dissociation, diffusion and so on. The oxygen adsorption is a very critical step during the cathode reaction process [16,17]. Liu' group [18–20] has explored the interactions between oxygen molecules and surfaces of $LaMnO_3$ and $La_{1-x}Sr_xMnO_{3-\delta}$ (LSM). The results implied that LSM-based cathode materials were more efficient for $O_2$ adsorption and oxygen vacancies enhanced $O_2$ molecule dissociation kinetics. The reaction mechanisms for oxygen incorporation on the $La_{1-x}Sr_xMnO_{3\pm\delta}$ (001) surface based on theoretical calculations were elucidated [21–23]. The results showed that the surface vacancy concentration and its mobility were crucial for fast oxygen exchange kinetics. Furthermore, Akbay et al. [24] have discussed the interaction between molecular oxygen and LaO-terminated surfaces of $La_2NiO_4$, and found that LaO-terminated surface can indeed participate in the formation of surface superoxo species. Zhou et al. [25] also has examined the interaction mechanism of $O_2$ adsorbed on the perfect and defective $La_2NiO_4$ (100) surface. It was found that the surface structure and the adsorbed configurations were important for $O_2$ molecule adsorption. All the previous studies proved that quantum chemical calculations were effective approach to elucidate the oxygen adsorption mechanism. The oxygen adsorption mechanism on single perovskites ($ABO_3$) and related structure ($K_2NiF_4$-type) surfaces have been extensively studied in SOFCs

The goal of this paper is to investigate the electronic structure of GBCO and the interaction of oxygen molecule with GBCO (001) surface by density functional calculations (DFT) within Hubbard framework. ## 2. Computational method

All the calculations are implemented with spin-polarization DFT using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [26,27]. The generalized-gradient approximation Perdew–Burke–Ernzerh of [28] of exchange-correlation functional is adopted with projector augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotentials [29]. We consider the strong correlation effects of Co d-orbitals, the U value for the d-orbitals of Co is set to 6.0 eV according to previous calculations [30,31]. We compare four possible magnetic states which depend on the four Co spin orientations in the unit cells of 19 atoms. The definitions of ferromagnetic (FM) and A-type, G-type, C-type antiferromagnetic (A-AFM, G-AFM, C-AFM) accord to Ref. [32]. Table 1 lists total energy of relaxation of GBCO with Hubbard correction in four spin configurations. This result shows that G-AFM configuration is the most stable structure, which is consistent with previous theoretical predictions [14,33]. Therefore, we only use the G-AFM state in present study.

For our calculations, the energy convergence criterion for electronic self-consistent cycles is $10^{-4}$ eV. The structures are optimized until the residual force is lower than $5 \times 10^{-2}$ eV/Å. In order to improve the reliability of the calculation, a $3 \times 3 \times 1$ and $5 \times 5 \times 5$ Monkhorst-Pack k-point for surface and bulk phase are used. We choose the cut-off energy of 500 eV for all the calculations. A free $O_2$ molecule is optimized in a large unit cell with dimensions $8 \times 8 \times 8$ Å$^3$. The experimental value of bond length of $O_2$ molecule is 1.210 Å [34]. The calculated bond length is 1.236 Å and the total energy is $-9.403$ eV, which are accordance with previous predictions [35]. We focus on BaO-terminated surface, rather than other terminated surfaces. This is because experimental observation by Low-Energy Ion Scattering (LEIS) suggests that $Ba^{2+}$ cations are exposed at surface in the double perovskites GBCO [36]. Moreover, the calculations of surface energies of the terminal surfaces demonstrated that BaO-terminated was energetically more favorable than GdO-terminated of GBCO (001) surfaces [37]. The stoichiometric GBCO (001) surface and the non-stoichiometric GBCO (001) surface (with one vacancy at surface) are investigated. These calculated bulk lattice parameters are used to construct surface models. All atoms positions in the slabs are relaxed, in order to obtain the stable surface structure. The 6-plane slab and P(1 $\times$ 2) surface supercells are adopted for our calculations.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. The electronic structure of orthorhombic phase GBCO

The electronic structure is strongly related to the surface oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) of SOFCs cathode. The calculated Co1 and Co2 ion (The sequence numbers of Co ions are displayed in Fig. 1) magnetic moment is $3.015\ \mu_B$ and $2.908\ \mu_B$, respectively. The results are in close agreement with the previous work [14]. The differences between the optimized lattice constants (See Table 2) and the experimental values [38] are within the typical first-principles error (less than 3%). So, we believe that the theoretical simulations can describe the double perovskites GBCO well. The total density of states (TDOS) are shown in Figs. 2 and 3 without Coulomb-U correction and with Coulomb-U correction. The Fermi level is set as 0 eV. From the TDOS, there is no band gap around Fermi level without Coulomb-U correction. However, the orthorhombic GBCO shows a semiconductor behavior after Coulomb-U correction because the band gap appears. The GBCO is regarded as metal incorrectly without Coulomb-U correction. The "metal-insulator" transition depends strongly on the changes of temperature and oxygen content [39,40]. The partial density of states (PDOS) for Co are reported in Fig. 4. The bottom of the conduction band are mainly formed by the Co 3 d electrons. The energy distribution of Co 3 d electron is narrowed and implies electron localization.

The oxygen vacancy can greatly influence oxygen reduction reaction at SOFCs cathodes. The high oxygen vacancy concentration at the cathode surface is key factor for SOFCs cathode performance [22,41]. To estimate the formation energy of oxygen vacancy, we use the following equation $E_f = E_{def} + \frac{1}{2}E(O_2) - E_{perf}$, where $E_{def}$ is the total energy of a fully relaxed supercell with a defect and $E(O_2)$ is the total energy of a free oxygen molecule in the large supercell, $E_{perf}$ is the total energy of a perfect supercell. In this study, the $GdBaCo_2O_{5.25}$ structure is constructed by removing one oxygen atom from previous $GdBaCo_2O_{5.5}$ supercell. Therefore, an additional oxygen vacancy was produced and oxygen non-stoichiometry was estimated to be $\delta=0.25$. Table 3 summarizes the Bader charges and magnetic moments of Co (octahedral and pyramidal) for both $GdBaCo_2O_{5.5}$ and $GdBaCo_2O_{5.25}$. Compared with $GdBaCo_2O_{5.5}$ case, the magnetic moments of octahedral Co ions are decreased and pyramidal Co ions are increased in $GdBaCo_2O_{5.25}$. The magnetic moment of octahedral Co (see Fig. 1) near the additional oxygen vacancy (O5) is $2.694\ \mu_B$, while it is $3.015\ \mu_B$ in $GdBaCo_2O_{5.5}$. ### 3.2. The $O_2$ adsorption at perfect GBCO (001) surface

We investigate $O_2$ adsorption at BaO-terminated surface at a coverage of 0.25 monolayers by placing an oxygen molecule at surface. A coverage of 0.25 monolayers implies that an oxygen molecule is adsorbed at one of the four cations at the surface layer. The removal of a surface oxygen atom from surface leads to a surface defect concentration of 12.5%. The adsorption energy is expressed as the following formula $E_{ads}=E_{surf/O_2}-E_{surf}-E_{O_2}$, where $E_{O_2}$ is total energy of a $O_2$ molecule, $E_{surf/O_2}$ and $E_{surf}$ are total energy of bonded $O_2$ molecule at the surface and a clean surface, respectively. According to the definition, a negative value implies an exothermic process and a stable configuration. Three different adsorption sites are considered at perfect GBCO (001) surface (See Fig. 6): Ba site, Bridge site and O site.

### 3.3. The $O_2$ adsorption at defective GBCO (001) surface

Let us first compare effective charges of sub-surface Co for both the perfect and defective GBCO (001) surfaces. The charge of sub-surface Co ion alters from 1.670 e to 1.260 e and decreases the oxidation of the Co ion, as the surface oxygen vacancy introduces. We also investigated $O_2$ molecule adsorption at the defective GBCO (001) surface. Similar to the previously presented cases for the defective surface, the same adsorption sites are analyzed. Fig. 8 shows the optimized structure of adsorbed $O_2$ at defective GBCO (001) surface. The adsorption properties of $O_2$ molecule at defective surface have been summarized in Table 5. The $O_2$ molecule also prefers to be adsorbed at the Bridge site with the adsorption energy of −3.372 eV. The O site has the highest adsorption energy (−1.164 eV). For the defective surface, it is predicted that the existence of oxygen vacancy at the surface enhances the adsorption strength of oxygen species because the adsorption energy is much bigger than that one at perfect surface. For all adsorption configurations we discussed, the transfer charges are 0.288–1.261 e between the surface and the adsorbed $O_2$ molecule, which are larger than the perfect surface. The charge analysis further shows that chemical bond of adsorption molecule and surface atoms becomes strong apparently due to the introduction oxygen vacancy. The sub-surface Co charge becomes more positive due to the adsorption of $O_2$ molecule at defective surface (compared to +1.258 e before adsorption). The bond length of $O_2$ molecule at Bridge site is found to be 1.431 Å. This value is quite close to the O–O bond length in the $H_2O_2$ molecule and is a characteristic of molecular peroxide species [46]. The oxygen vacancies can facilitate oxygen adsorption and catalyze the bond cleavage relative to the crystal lattice, which contributes to the surface oxygen diffusion process. Our results are consistent with recent DFT studies [24,25] which also illustrates that the importance of oxygen vacancy in controlling ORR activity at SOFC cathodes. The adsorption properties of $O_2$ molecule are compared with the (La,Sr) O-terminated (La,Sr)$MnO_3$ (001) surface and SrO-terminated $SrTiO_3$ (001) surface. The results of the most stable "Bridge" site at BaO-terminated GBCO (001) surface are similar to (La,Sr)O-terminated $\text{La}_{0.75}\text{Sr}_{0.25}\text{MnO}_3$ (001) surface [47]. ## 4. Conclusions

In the present study, we have explored the adsorption mechanism of $O_2$ molecule at perfect and defective GBCO (001) surface based on the first principle of DFT. The vacancy formation energy, the adsorption energy, Bader charge and adsorption configuration are systematically analyzed. We have shown that G-AFM configuration is the most stable structure. The calculated results of oxygen vacancy energy indicate that the lowest energy vacancy positions are in the GdO layers. The Bader charge analysis implies that there are charges rearrangements within adsorbate and surface ions after $O_2$ molecule adsorption. The $O_2$ molecule adsorption at the defective surface is energetically more favorable than perfect surface. The Bridge site is the most stable adsorption site for perfect and defective GBCO (001) surfaces. The oxygen vacancy can act as active site and play a critical role for oxygen reduction reaction. These results are useful for understanding ORR mechanism and designing new cathode materials in SOFCs.
