## A B S T R A C T

Electronic structure calculations have been performed for the Co-phthalocyanine molecule using density functional theory (DFT) within the framework of Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA). The electronic correlation in Co 3d orbitals is treated in terms of the GGA+U method in the framework of the Hubbard model. We find that for $U = 6$ eV, the calculated structural parameters as well as the spectral features are in good agreement with the experimental findings. From our calculation both the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) are dominated by the pyrrole carbon, with a HOMO-LUMO gap of about 1.4 eV. The GGA+U results obtained with $U = 6$ eV compare reasonably well with the calculations performed using Gaussian basis set and hybrid functionals in terms of ground state geometry, spin state and spectral features. The calculated valence band photoemission spectrum is in quite good agreement with the recently published experimental results.

### 1. Introduction

Metal phthalocyanine (MPc) such as CuPc, CoPc, FePc, etc. are the simplest examples of molecular magnets which are considered to be interesting for numerous applications [1]. Apart from the fact that the phthalocyanine molecule has a structural similarity with biological molecules such as chlorophyll and haemoglobin, the interaction between the transition metal atom in the molecule with its surroundings makes it interesting from the point of view of optical, magnetic and electronic properties. Although all of these systems show high thermal and chemical stability irrespective of the constituent metal, a previous theoretical study shows that CoPc has highest binding energy among all MPc's [2]. In the present study, we report the electronic structure and magnetic properties of CoPc molecule obtained from first principles using density functional theory (DFT). The main focus of this study is to characterize the electronic structure of the molecule by the GGA+U method. This will help us to study the cases where the molecule is deposited on a substrate [3] that requires simulation with a unit cell having a large number of atoms. The use of GGA+U requires less computational effort in comparison to purely quantum chemical methods but as we show here, will yield fairly accurate results. A similar approach was considered by some of us previously [4] for Fe porphyrin molecule. The structure of the CoPc molecule is shown in **Fig. 1**. The chemical formula of the molecule is $\text{C}_{32}\text{CoH}_{16}\text{N}_{8}$ with Co at the center. There are two types of nitrogen atoms (N1 and N2), and four types of carbon atoms (C1,C2,C3,C4) with C3 and C4 bonded to hydrogen atoms. The molecule can be considered as almost planar with a local tetragonal symmetry $\text{D}_{4h}$ [5]. Because of the tetragonal crystal field, one would expect the splitting of the five degenerate d orbitals of Co into twofold degenerate $\text{e}_{g}$ levels and three distinct levels namely $\text{b}_{2g}$, $\text{a}_{1g}$ and $\text{b}_{1g}$. Electron spin resonance experiment shows a low-spin state $S = 1/2$ for Co in the molecule [6]. This result is in agreement with the earlier DFT calculation [7-9]. However, different calculations predict different types of orbital-occupancy of the Co d orbitals [10]. It should be noted that the partial occupancy of the doubly degenerate $\text{e}_{g}$ level can result in a Jahn-Teller like distortion thereby lifting the degeneracy of the $\text{e}_{g}$ states [2] resulting in a $\text{D}_{2h}$ local symmetry rather than $\text{D}_{4h}$. Therefore, in the CoPc molecule, Co-3d orbitals play a crucial role not only for determining the total spin of the molecule due to the open d-shell of Co but also for exhibiting a complex electronic structure due to a number of close-lying electronic states. From a theoretical point of view, it is essential to properly characterize the d states with the available experimental data, in order to describe the electronic structure in a correct way. To place the d states in incorrect energy positions would result in additional charge transfer states in the calculated electronic structure of the molecule.

## 2. Computational details

We have performed first-principles calculations using DFT within spin-polarized Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) on the basis of the projector augmented wave method imple- mented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [11]. The wave function was expanded in plane waves using a 500 eV energy cut-off. To take into account the correlation effect in Co, we have carried out GGA+U calculations in the Hubbard model framework using Dudarev's approach [12]. We used a simulation box of $21\,\text{\AA} \times 21\,\text{\AA} \times 21\,\text{\AA}$ to minimize the inter-molecular interaction due to the presence of periodic boundary conditions in the code. We used only the $\Gamma$ point for the sampling in the reciprocal space. The hybrid functional calculations were performed using Gaussian 09 package [13] with Becke-Lee-Yang-Parr (B3LYP) [14] exchange-correlation functional. We used 6-31g** basis set for all the atoms. The total number of basis functions used was 716. The partial densities of states obtained by the quantum chemical approach were used to simulate an experimental spectrum of valence band (VB) X-ray Photoelectron Spetrocopy (PES) by means of the Gelius model [19]. Each atomic orbital contribution to the molecular orbitals, was weighed by the related theoretical subshell photoionization cross section [20]. To facilitate the comparison with the experimental curve, we convoluted the total density of states (DOS) by gaussian curves of 0.8 eV full width at half maximum (FWHM), and the partial DOS for each atomic orbitals by gaussian curves of 0.5 eV FWHM. The pristine DOS, as obtained by the B3LYP calculation, was also convoluted by gaussian curves of 0.2 and 0.8 eV FWHM. We compared our results to the VB-PES of CoPc taken at a photon energy of 110 eV by Maslyuk et al. [17].

## 3. Results

As the value of $U$ serves as a parameter in our GGA+U approach, we have tested different values (viz., $U=2,4,6,8$ eV). From our calculations, we find that the results for $U=6$ eV fit best with the experimentally obtained structural parameters and valence band spectrum of the molecule as well as with our quantum chemical calculations with B3LYP. In Fig. 2 we show the variation of total energy as a function of the total magnetic moment of the molecule calculated by the fixed spin moment method. The $3d^7$ configuration of the Co atom may yield two possible spin states, a low-spin $S=1/2$ and a high-spin $S=3/2$. While at $U=2$ eV, we find only one minimum in the total energy curve at $S=1/2$, we observe distinct double-wells at larger $U$ values. At $U=8$ eV, both minima are almost degenerate. The evolution of a two minima structure with increasing $U$ has been observed for bulk solids too [21]. In the inset we show the calculated energy difference $\Delta E=E(S_{1/2})-E(S_{3/2})$. We notice that at $U=6$ eV, the minimum at $S=1/2$ is about 0.5 eV below the other one. The ground state spin structure with $S=1/2$ obtained from our calculations for moderate $U$ values is consistent with previous theoretical calculations [7-9]. In Table 1 we compare the structural parameters obtained from GGA+U ($U=6$ eV) and B3LYP with the experimental findings. From the table we see that from both methods, the bond-length between the Co and pyrrole nitrogen ($N_1$) is slightly overestimated; so is the distance between C and H. While GGA+U reproduces exactly experimental bond- length between $N_1$ and $C_1$, both methods underestimate the bond-lengths between $C_1$ and $C_2$. The calculated angles are slightly larger in the theoretical results than what is obtained in the experiment. The total DOS's obtained from Gaussian09-B3LYP and VASP- GGA+U calculations are shown in Fig. 3. In general, both methods result in a similar shape of the CoPc VB, as can be further confirmed by comparing these curves to the experimental VB-PES in [17]. However, some differences are evident between theory and experiment. The double splitting of the HOMO resulting in the experimental peaks at about $-1.2$ eV and $-2.2$ eV in Ref. 17 is neither reproduced by B3LYP nor GGA+U. According to the B3LYP method, and as shown in the theoretical calculations contained in [17], this peak is generated by the $\pi$ orbitals formed by carbon atoms, and has no contribution by the central cobalt atom. By the comparison of the two theoretical methods, first of all we observe that the overall DOS calculated by GGA+U is slightly contracted in the energy scale in comparison to the B3LYP calculation. A similar effect can be seen for instance in a comparison between GGA and B3LYP calculations performed for $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{PC}$ [18]. Moreover, with the GGA+U method we obtain a sharp peak at about $-1.7$ eV near the Fermi level, while the B3LYP method gives a much less intense feature at about $-1.0$ eV. Fig. 3(c) shows the Co-d projected DOS's obtained from B3LYP and GGA+U calculations with different values of $U$. This figure justifies clearly the motivation behind choosing $U=6$ eV. It is evident from the figure that the positions of two prominent peaks close to HOMO in $U=6$ eV calculations agree the best with B3LYP calculations. $U=2$ eV and 4 eV results fail to reproduce the peak positions and relative intensities. Apart from the electronic structure, $U=6$ eV yields the correct spin state of $S=1 / 2$ too. Therefore, we conclude that $U=6$ eV should be used for GGA+U calculations for this molecule, also when one considers its interaction with other chemical species and surfaces.

In Fig. 4, we present the atom projected DOS's calculated with $U=6$ eV for different chemical specie. From this figure, we notice that the HOMO (highest occupied molecular orbital) character of the molecule is due to the contribution from pyrrole carbon (C1). The peak around $-1.7$ eV is mainly due to the carbon-nitrogen bonding. The presence of Co-d states appears as a shoulder near the peak. The large peak around $-4$ eV arises mainly from Co. These features are similar in both theoretical methods. The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) (not shown) is constituted from C1 and the two nitrogen atoms designated as N1 and N2. Such findings are consistent with the VB-PES experiment in Ref [17,22]. The calculated HOMO-LUMO gap with GGA+U is around 1.4 eV, whereas the B3LYP calculation provides a much larger gap ($\sim$2.7 eV). It should be noted that both HOMO and LUMO are dominated by pyrrole C atoms, not Co. So a mere shifting of Co-d states by GGA+U method does not guarantee the correct reproduction of HOMO-LUMO gap. Therefore, it is not surprising that GGA+U yields a smaller gap than B3LYP calculation where all orbitals are treated with the same functional. Regarding magnetism, both GGA+U and B3LYP calculations give a $S=1 / 2$ spin state ($1\ \mu_{\text{B}}$) of the molecule. The magnetic moment of the Co atom obtained with the GGA+U method is $1.04\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ while other atoms contribute a small magnetic moment of about $-0.04\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ to give a total moment of $1\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ for the molecule.

To better understand the valence region between 0 and $-5$ eV, where we observe a considerable discrepancy between the theoretical results and the measured spectra, we have used the DOS calculated by B3LYP to simulate the experimental photoelectron spectrum of the valence band reported in [17] by the Gelius model. In Fig. 5, we show the total DOS, along with the theoretically simulated VB-PES at a photon energy of 110 eV along with the atomic and orbital projected DOS's simulated for the same photon energy. The photoionisation cross sections strongly affect the photoelectron spectra, as their values vary considerably for each atomic orbital and for each photon energy within the soft X-ray region [20]. By including them in the calculations, we can see from Fig. 4) how the relative intensities of the peaks in the valence band are changed. For a photon energy of 110 eV, the theoretical photoionization cross section of Co 3d is about 4.9 Mb [20], which is about two orders of magnitude larger than that of C 2p (0.1 Mb) and of N 2p (0.4 Mb). We have marked the major peaks of the simulated VB-PES spectrum from A to H as done in [17] according to [15] for FePc, where A is the highest energy peak, or the HOMO. Peak B is split into two contributions, B1 and B2. These two peaks, due to the high cross section for Co 3d at photon energy of 110 eV, are dominated by the Co 3d contributions at about $-1.7$ and at $-3.3$ eV. Our simulated VB-PES reproduces very well the experimental spectral profile of Ref. [17] in the energy region including the peaks B1 to H. One can conclude that the B3LYP method gives a reliable reproduction of the elemental and orbital character of the valence band of the CoPc molecule as observed in the experiment. In Fig. 6 we show the $m_l$ projected DOS for the Co-d levels obtained by GGA+U calculations. It is evident from the figure that the first two doubly degenerated orbitals with $d_{zx}$ and $d_{yz}$ symmetry respectively are completely filled. This clearly indicates the absence of a Jahn-Teller distortion and $D_{4h}$ local symmetry is preserved for CoPc since we do not have any partially filled degenerate orbitals. These two orbitals are hybridized with N1 and are weakly bonded to $\pi$-states of the benzene ring. The strong peak around $-$4.0 eV is due to the strong $\sigma$- bond between Co($d_{x^2-y^2}$) and N1.

## 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have studied the electronic structure of the Co-phthalocyanine molecule using GGA+U in a plane wave basis as well as hybrid functionals (B3LYP) in a gaussian basis. We find that for $U$=6 eV in the GGA+U calculations, both the structural parameters and electronic structure of the molecule agree reasonably well with the experimental observations as well as B3LYP calculations. We believe that this value of U will be appropriate for further calculations of CoPc deposited on substrates with GGA+U method. Finally, our simulated valence band photoemission spectrum of CoPc compares quite well with the experimentally observed spectrum at a photon energy of 110 eV.
