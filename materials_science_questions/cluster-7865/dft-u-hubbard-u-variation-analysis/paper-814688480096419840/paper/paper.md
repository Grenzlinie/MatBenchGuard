## ABSTRACT

Based on first-principles calculation the change of magnetic configurations and electronic structure of perovskite oxide $BaFeO_3$ is investigated by introducing a new potential energy function which has been determined via the density functional theory basis with U-Hubbard Hamiltonian (DFT+U). The exchange and correlation potential is employed using GGA+U approach by combining available experimental measurements and the data from the current theoretical calculations. The implementation of the U-Hubbard term in this calculation allows more comprehension on the $BaFeO_3$ behavior and has ameliorated the obtained results. The spin effect is given for the considerable oxide by investing the cubic phase. Possible different magnetic configurations (ferromagnetic **FM** and anti-ferromagnetic **AFM**) for the $BaFeO_3$ oxide. The implementation of the new approach GGA+U shows the Coulomb interaction inside the d-states exactly which can play an important role in the orbital systems of both elements the ferrite (Fe) and barium (Ba). By the band structure and the densities of states analysis. $BaFeO_3$ is a strong candidate for spintronic applications.

### 1. Introduction

A wide number of perovskite-type $ABO_3$ oxides have been studied because of their interesting properties in different technological domains, including semiconductivity, superconductivity, ionic conduction characteristics, (anti)-ferroelectric, (anti)-piezoelectric, thermomechanic systems, dielectric properties, ferroelasticity, (anti)-ferromagnetic. Especially the spintronic applications have played an important role last decade, such as the magnetic-field sensors and electric-write magnetic-read, random-access memories [1,2]. Recently, the emergence in the spintronic field within the magneto-resistance discoveries [3–8] has opened different ways for many researchers. The perovskite oxides with the $ABO_3$ formula constitute an increasing interest [9–15], especially the magnetic oxides which possess the Mn or Fe element because they show specific physical properties. Additionally, they have received great attention as multiferroic compounds. This phenomenon displays both ferroelectric and ferromagnetic behavior in the oxide. Perovskite is one of the most frequently encountered structures in material sciences researchers. The ideal cubic $ABX_3$ perovskite structure takes the Pm-3m space group. In this structure, the oxygen takes the X-site. The A-element is 12-fold coordinated, and the B-element is 6-fold coordinated and with the oxygen. The center position of the corner sharing the octahedron that forms the skeleton of the structure is occupied by the A-element. The BO distance is equal to $a/2$ (where $a$ is the lattice parameter), while the AO distance is $(a/\sqrt{2})$ [16–19]. In the ideal perovskite structure, each oxygen atom is shared by two $B^{3+}$ ions, forming a BOB angle of $180^\circ$. Such configuration is favorable for super-exchange interactions between the $B^{3+}$ magnetic ion. This exchange usually results in anti-parallel coupling of neighbor magnetic moments. When the $B^{3+}$ ions are in two sublattices $(A_2BB'O_6)$, other spin arrangements are possible [14]. If B' is a diamagnetic ion, the $B^{3+}$ ions are aligned anti-ferromagnetically, and the most important exchange mechanism is believed to be a long-range super exchange interaction through two oxygen with BOBOB type. Many theoretical works are carried out on cubic-$BaFeO_3$ oxide [20–26]. The $BaFeO_3$ is one of the few oxides, in which, the iron assumes an oxidation state of (4+) such as the Callender et al. antiferromagnetic (AFM) report and others [20,27]. The stable is found to be a hexagonal structure with $a = 0.568$ nm and $c = 1.386$ nm, although various polymorphs are observed with oxygen deficiency [20,27,28]. In fact, the magnetic properties depend on oxygen content, presumably affecting the iron-valence states. Hexagonal $BaFeO_3$ exhibits an antiferromagnetic to ferromagnetic transition at 160 K, with a Curie temperature $T_C$ of about 250 K [20,29]. Recently, Mössbauer with high magnetic field studies contradicts this conclusion, showing that no magnetic ordering occurs for 160 à 220 K [20,30], this discrepancy could be related to $BaFeO_{3-x}$ oxygen stoichiometry [20,19]. They show also that the $BaFeO_3$ assumes a metastable pseudocubic crystal structure similar to that observed for $SrFeO_3$, and for others perovskites when they say that the stabilization of metastable phases via epitaxy has been observed for other perovskite like oxides [20,31,32], and this pseudo-cubic thin film phase is reportedly ferromagnetic at room temperature [20,33,34]. In the present work, the $BaFeO_3$ perovskite oxide is investigated within the cubic phase in different states: non-magnetic state (NF), ferromagnetic state (FM), and antiferromagnetic state (AFM), in order to give more comprehension on its electronic structure and magnetic properties.

## 2. Computational details

In this paper, all calculation are performed using a hybrid full potential linear augmented plane wave besides local orbitals (L/APW+lo) introduced in the Wien2K code [35,36] within the density functional theory DFT+U within the corrected U-Hubbard Hamiltonian [37,38]. The exchange–correla- tion interaction was treated using different approximations; local-spin density approximation with U- Hubbard correction (LSDA+U), spin-dependent generalized gradient approximation (spin-GGA, and spin-GGA+U). The GGA+U approach is computationally much less demanding than using LSDA+U approach, because it gives more accurate results and wide comprehension on the magnetic interaction and spin effect on the compound behavior. To ensure high accuracy, the k-point densities and plane wave Cutoff energy are increased until convergence. The dependence of the total energy on the num- ber of k-points in the irreducible wedge of the first Brillouin zone (BZ) has been explored within the linearized tetrahedron scheme by performing the calculation for 3000 k-points in the entire Brillouin zone (BZ) for all configurations used in this study. The valence wave functions, inside the spheres, are expanded up to $l=10$, wave expansion with $R_{mt}\cdot K_{max}$ equal to 7, whereas the self-consistent calcula- tions are considered to be converged only when the calculated total energy of the crystal is converged to less than 1 m Ryd. Note, the U-Hubbard term (GGA+U–DFT+U) employed here has extensively increased the obtained results, these influences are given in all the ground states properties

## 4. Discussion and summary

In summary, structural, electronic, spins effect, magnetic moment and magnetic properties have been investigated using FP-LAPW method within both *DFT* and *DFT+U* descriptions. The exchange and correlation potential are introduced by *GGA* and **GGA+U** approaches with comparative analysis. From current results, the implementation of the corrected *U*-Hubbard Hamiltonian has extensively ameliorated qualitatively the present work. Different magnetic configurations (the non-ferromagnetic (*NF*), ferromagnetic (*FM*), *A*-type anti-ferromagnetic (*A-AFM*), and *G*-type anti-ferromagnetic (*G-AFM*)) are studied for the cubic Barium ferrite oxide BaFeO₃ phase. The considerable oxide is found to be *G-AFM*. The magnetic moment is interpreted and discussed within a wide attention. The equilibrium lattices are consistent with available theoretical and experimental measurements. The dependent electronic spin band structures and densities of states plotted here show that this oxide exhibits the metallic behavior in all the magnetic configurations studied. The chemical bonding and the charge

density examination has revealed that $BaFeO_3$ oxide shows a strong covalent bonding between (Fe-Fe) elements, the presence of the ionic character between oxygen (O-O) atoms and covalent bonding with the ionic character are between the oxygen elements and other atoms $(Ba-O)$ and $(Fe-O)$. Note that both DFT-GGA and DFT+$U$-GGA+$U$ description are treated. GGA+$U$ approach calculation predicts a right description of the electronic structure and magnetic interaction of the oxide systems through its band structures and densities of states.
