# Description of the Antiferromagnetic State by a Molecular Orbital Method with the Use of the Regular Local Density Approximation

Kimichika FUKUSHIMA*

Power and Industrial Systems Research and Development Center, Toshiba Corporation 4-1, Ukishima-cho, Kawasaki-ku, Kawasaki 210-0862

(Received September 20, 1999)

KEYWORDS: antiferromagnetic state, clusters, electronic structure, local density approximation, molecular orbital method

At present, it is not fully understood whether the antiferromagnetic (AF) state can be represented by the local density approximation (LDA). This is due to the fact that a naive band structure calculation with the use of the LDA leads to a metallic state for layered Cu oxides. Since the cause has not been fully examined, some improvements are proposed. One is a self-interaction correction, and another is a model of LDA+U, $^{1-3)}$ where U is a strong on-site Coulomb repulsion. These methods can predict the AF state although problems still remain. In the scheme of a molecular orbital method, it has not yet been clarified whether the AF state can be described properly by the LDA. The purpose of this paper is to show that by using localized atomic orbitals the molecular orbital method can predict the AF state even by the conventional LDA. Thus, disappearance of the AF state in a band structure calculational result is probably due to a lack of localized functions in a basis set.

In the scheme of the spin-polarized molecular orbital method, the AF state had to be obtained in the following way: We deal with a system where the number of localized conductive electrons is the same as that of atoms, and the energies of atomic orbital with up and down spins are different each other. Then, the antibonding molecular orbitals can produce the localized state, roughly speaking, i.e., that no electron with up spin exists on the atomic site next to that without down spin, resulting in the AF state.

In the situation described above, molecular orbital calculations were carried out for the artificial hydrogen clusters, $H_2$ and the layered $H_8$ as well as $H_{18}$, with a rather long interatomic separation, and the cluster $Cu_2O_{11}$ with two octahedra. Hydrogen clusters have the states originated from the $1s$ atomic orbital where the number of localized conductive electrons per atom is unity, and transition metal oxides have a long interatomic distance between metal atoms. It is then expected that the hydrogen clusters with a rather long interatomic distance will become to be the AF state.

In this paper, first, the computational method and the treated clusters are described. Then, the calculated results are presented for the hydrogen and $Cu_2O_{11}$ clusters. Finally, a conclusion is presented.

Electronic structure calculations were performed with the use of the spin-polarized molecular orbital computational method within the scheme of the LDA. The exchange-correlation potential is proportional to $\alpha \rho_\sigma(\boldsymbol{r})^{(1/3)}$, with $\rho_\sigma(\boldsymbol{r})$ being the electronic charge density with a spin, and the parameter $\alpha$ is set to 0.7 except for the specific case. The basis atomic orbitals, $1s$ for the H atom, $1s$, $2s$, $2p$, $3s$, $3p$ and $3d$ for the Cu atom, and $1s$, $2s$ and $2p$ for the O atom, were calculated numerically. For H atom a well potential was added with the radius of 0.8 times the interatomic distance and the depth of $-2.0$ a.u. Since it is not easy to prepare the atomic basis functions for $O^{2-}$ ion, which is unstable in a vacuum and stabilized in a crystal, we adopted two types trial basis function to describe it. One, which we call the Type I basis function, was derived by adding a well potential with the radius of 0.8 times the separation between the Cu atoms and the depth of $-0.5$ a.u. The other, which we call the Type II basis function, was derived without a well potential under the condition that the number of nodes and the asymptotic behavior near nucleus and at the outer region away from nucleus were the same as those of the atomic orbital. Then, the inner solution and the outer solutions had non-vanishing discontinuity in derivative at a connecting point, but the solution was interpolated by a polynomial with smooth derivative when it was used. No well potential was applied to Cu atom. The initial charges in a self-consistent iteration were set to $+2e$ for the Cu atom and $-2e$ for the O atom.

For calculated hydrogen clusters, the positions of H atoms for $H_2$ were $(\pm a_{\rm H}, 0, 0)$; those for the layered $H_8$ were $(\pm a_{\rm H}, \pm a_{\rm H}, \pm 1.25a_{\rm H})$; and those for the layered $H_{18}$ were $(b_{\rm H}, b'_{\rm H}, c_{\rm H})$, where $b_{\rm H}$, $b'_{\rm H} = \pm 2a_{\rm H}$, 0 and $c_{\rm H} = \pm 1.25a_{\rm H}$ with $a_{\rm H}$ being taken to be 0.126 nm. In the case of the cluster $Cu_2O_{11}$, Cu atoms were located at $(\pm a_{\rm C},0,0)$, and O atoms were positioned at $(\pm a_{\rm C}, \pm a_{\rm C},0)$, $(\pm 2a_{\rm C}, 0, 0)$, $(0, 0, 0)$ and $(\pm a_{\rm C}, 0, \pm c_{\rm C})$, where $a_{\rm C}$ and $c_{\rm C}$ were taken to be 0.189 nm and 0.243 nm, respectively. The symmetry of the orbital was not considered for the H clusters, but the electronic configuration for the atoms on the equivalent site was set to be identical. For $Cu_2O_{11}$, only the symmetry with respect to $xy$ plane (two independent Cu atoms were located in the $x$ axis) was taken into account: The point group producing symmetry orbitals adopted was ${\rm C_s}$. We regarded the O atoms on symmetry orbital in the $xy$ plane and at the apex as two equivalent ones. The diophantine numerical integration for the matrix elements of the Hamiltonian and overlap integral was carried out by 500 sampling points per atom for the H clusters. Calculations for $Cu_2O_{11}$ were performed with the use of the Type I and Type II basis functions and by 9000 and 18000 sampling points, respectively. The estimated errors in orbital energy were 0.01 eV for the former and 0.04 eV for the latter. For the calculation for $Cu_2O_{11}$, the Madelung potential produced by the atoms in the ${\rm K_2NiF_4}$ structure was added within the size of $4.02a_{\rm K} \times 3.02a_{\rm K} \times 2.74c_{\rm K}$ in

The calculated results for the H clusters show that the averaged magnetic moments on the H atom were 0.97, 0.94 and $0.93\ \mu_{\mathrm{B}}$ for $\mathrm{H}_{2}$, $\mathrm{H}_{8}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{18}$, respectively. The nearest H atoms at the rather long interatomic distance were connected by the AF interaction. Within the size of the clusters above, the localized state is still stable. The spin-unpolarized calculation for the same atomic and electronic configurations shows that the energy separations between the top of the bonding states occupied by electrons (that is, HOMO: highest occupied molecular orbital) and the bottom of the unoccupied antibonding states (that is, LUMO: lowest unoccupied molecular orbital) were 1.2, 0.4 and 0.3 eV for $\mathrm{H}_{2}$, $\mathrm{H}_{8}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{18}$, respectively. This suggests that the spin-unpolarized calculation for an infinitely large system results in the metallic state in this case. On the other hand, the energy separations between the HOMO and LUMO obtained by spin-polarized calculation were 7.1, 6.4 and 6.0 eV for $\mathrm{H}_{2}$, $\mathrm{H}_{8}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{18}$, respectively. The differences in the energy separation between the spin-polarized and spin-unpolarized calculations were 5.9, 6.0 and 5.7 eV for $\mathrm{H}_{2}$, $\mathrm{H}_{8}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{18}$, respectively. The half of this energy difference is the localization energy, and will probably remain finite in an infinitely large system. It is possible to say the dependence of the electronic structure on the size and atomic configuration for H clusters is meaningful. However, as mentioned below, the localization of the orbital for the Cu oxides is not verified clearly. If electronic structures for an infinitely large system are obtained by the molecular orbital method, the result coincides with band structure calculational result at $\Gamma$ point taking into account a corresponding supercell. Note that the increase in $\alpha$ and the renormalization in advance for the mass enhancement, for example, by an electron-phonon interaction lead to the stabilization of the localization. Furthermore, in this paper, an attempt was made to set an unrealistic value beyond unity to $\alpha$ for the specific case as shown below.

With respect to the cluster $\mathrm{Cu}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{11}$, the averaged magnetic moment on the Cu atom was $0.59\ \mu_{\mathrm{B}}$ for $\alpha=0.7$ when Type I basis function was used. The AF state was seen in this case, too. The energy separation between HOMO and LUMO was 0.83 eV for $\alpha=0.7$, and that for $\alpha=0.9$ became 1.2 times larger than that for $\alpha=0.7$. With this increase in $\alpha$, little change in the magnetic moment on the Cu atom was seen. When Type II basis function was used, the averaged magnetic moment on the Cu atom was $0.98\ \mu_{\mathrm{B}}$ for $\alpha=0.9$ and the localization was more stabilized compared to the case where Type I basis function was used. Figure 1 shows the energy levels near the HOMO. The energy of molecular orbitals was measured from the energy of the HOMO. In the figure, the states occupied by electrons are originated from the Cu $3d$ and O $2p$ atomic orbitals. The states unoccupied by electrons are antibonding states between the Cu $3d$ and O $2p$ atomic orbitals as well as between Cu $3d$ atomic orbitals. When an unrealistic value of 1.4 was set to $\alpha$, the energy separation between HOMO and LUMO was 1.2 eV and the averaged magnetic moment on Cu atom was $0.77\ \mu_{\mathrm{B}}$. (For $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ the energy separation with $\alpha=1.4$ was 14.6 eV.)

Strictly speaking, the ground state should be identified among the possible states by the total energy calculation, but it is difficult by the present computational method. The total energy calculation needs a stricter numerical integration, which will become possible by the GQ method$^{4)}$ and the improved DV-GQ hybrid method for multiatomic molecules.$^{5)}$

In summary, this paper showed that, within the scheme of the conventional LDA, a molecular orbital method predicted the antiferromagnetic (AF) state by using localized atomic orbitals. The band structure calculation which misses the AF state will properly describe the AF state if localized basis functions are included in a basis set. Furthermore, in order to give a good prediction of the AF state in Cu oxides, correct calculations of the $\mathrm{O}^{2-}$ state are necessary. (For example, it may be suitable for the basis function to behave like a wave function of a free electron at the region away from nucleus.)
