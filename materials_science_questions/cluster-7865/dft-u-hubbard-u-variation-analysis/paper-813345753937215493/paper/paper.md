PHYSICAL REVIEW B 84, 235137 (2011)

# Mott transition and magnetic anisotropy in $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$

Guo-Qiang Liu
Max-Planck-Institut für Festkörperforschung, D-70569 Stuttgart, Germany
(Received 4 August 2011; published 21 December 2011)

The puzzling nature of the Mott transition in $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ is investigated by using the density-functional calculations including the spin-orbit coupling and Coulomb repulsion. We find that the Mott transition in $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ has an inherent relationship with its strong anisotropic magnetic properties. When the magnetic moments are aligned along the $b$ axis, the band structure indicates an insulating phase with a narrow band gap. On the other hand, the compound is found to be a rather good metal when the magnetic moments are aligned along the $a$ or $c$ axis. Our finding is consistent with the experimental observed spin reorientation at $T_{MI}$.

## I. INTRODUCTION

The layered perovskites $(Ca_{1-x}Sr_x)_{n+1}Ru_nO_{3n+1}$ have attracted considerable interest since the compounds exhibit a wide variety of interesting physical properties. In the $n=\infty$ family $SrRuO_3$ is a ferromagnetic metal, $^1$ whereas $CaRuO_3$ does not show any magnetic ordering. $^2$ The $n=2$ compound $Sr_3Ru_2O_7$ is a paramagnetic metal with unusual large quasiparticle masses, $^3$ and $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ is a Mott-like antiferromagnetic (AFM) insulator with a narrow charge gap. $^4$ The $n=1$ series attracted extra attention because $Sr_2RuO_4$ is a unique $p$-wave superconductor $^{5,6}$ and $Ca_2RuO_4$ is a typical Mott insulator. $^{7,8}$ It has been learned that the wide spread of physical phenomena in the ruthenates is mainly due to the interplay of orbital degrees of freedom, crystal structure distortion, and electron correlation. $^{9-11}$ Another possible competing interaction in the $4d$ oxides is the spin-orbit (SO) coupling since the SO constant for $Ru^{4+}$ is not small. In $Ca_2RuO_4$, photoemission experiment observed unusual large SO coupling, which was proposed to be responsible for the measured temperature dependence of the $d$ orbital population. $^{12}$ Theoretical studies also found that the SO coupling is the key to understanding the experimental Fermi surfaces of $Sr_2RuO_4$ and $Sr_2RhO_4$. $^{13,14}$ These findings suggest that the SO coupling should not be neglected in the ruthenates.

The bilayer compound $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ stimulated much research effort due to its colossal magnetoresistance $^{15,16}$ and highly anisotropic behavior. $^{17}$ $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ has an orthorhombic structure with the space group $Bb2_1m$, lattice constant $a=5.3677$, $b=5.5356$, and $c=19.5219$ Å. $^{18}$ At low temperature, $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ has a so-called $A$-type AFM phase, i.e., the $RuO_2$ bilayers are ferromagnetically ordered in the $ab$ plane and are antiferromagnetically stacked along the $c$ axis. $^{19-22}$ $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ undergoes a metal-insulator transition at $T_{MI}\sim48$ K, and remains the AFM order up to $T_N\sim56$ K. $^4$ Interestingly, the spin orientation is found to be rearranged at $T_{MI}$. $^{19,21-23}$ Below $T_{MI}$, $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ is insulating and the magnetic moments are aligned along the $b$ axis. When the temperature is increased to $T_{MI}$, $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ becomes metallic and the moment orientation is switched to the $a$ axis. Despite the experimental efforts, there is an essential question still unanswered in this field: what is the origin of the Mott transition? Singh and Auluck applied density-functional (DFT) calculations to $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$, and while they found that the local spin-density approximation (LSDA) failed to reproduce the insulating ground state. $^{24}$ In addition, the observed spin reorientation at $T_{MI}$ indicates an intimate relationship between the metal-insulator transition and the magnetic anisotropy. However, a theoretical understanding for this relationship is still absent.

In this paper, we report an electronic structure study on $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ using first-principles calculations including the SO coupling and on-site Coulomb interaction. We find that the band structure of $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ is strongly dependent on the direction of the magnetic moment. The observed insulating ground state can be reproduced if the magnetic moments are aligned along the $b$ axis. When the spin direction is switched to the $a$ axis, the compound becomes metallic. Our calculations show that $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$ has a novel Mott insulating state, which is induced by the interplay of SO coupling and Coulomb repulsion.

All the calculations in this work were performed with the full-potential linear augmented plane wave (FLAPW) within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), as implemented in package WIEN2K. $^{25}$ The experimental structure at 8 K was used in all the calculations. $^{18}$ For the ferromagnetic (FM) structure, each cell contains four Ru atoms and 230 $k$ points were used within the irreducible Brillouin zone. For the $A$-type AFM structure, each cell contains eight Ru atoms and 91 $k$ points were used within the irreducible Brillouin zone. The muffin-tin sphere radii of 1.90, 2.07, and 1.68 $a_0$ were used for Ru, Ca, and O atoms, respectively.

## II. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### A. Band structures without spin-orbit coupling

In this study, the experimental $a$, $b$, and $c$ axis are taken as the $x$, $y$, and $z$ axis. Note that this coordinate system is different from the common used local coordinate. We will show that this choice can simplify the problem when the SO coupling is taken into account. The previous LSDA study showed that the bands crossing the Fermi level are composed of the Ru-$t_{2g}$ orbitals. $^{24}$ In the local coordinate system, the $t_{2g}$ orbitals are the $xy$, $xz$, and $yz$, orbitals, while in our chosen coordinate system, the $t_{2g}$ orbitals are the $x^2-y^2$, $xz$, and $yz$ orbitals.

We start with the FM state. Figure 1 displays the GGA band structure for FM $Ca_3Ru_2O_7$. The band structure exhibits a two-dimensional behavior, for the band dispersion along the $z$ direction is weak. Figure 1 also shows that the FM state is a half metallic phase, which has the insulating majority spin bands and metallic minority spin bands. The calculated magnetic

This feature implies that the experimental insulating phase cannot be reproduced by the DFT $+U$ (GGA $+U$ or LSDA $+U$) method. In the half metallic system, the $t_{2g}$ majority spin bands have been fully occupied. And then the on-site Coulomb interaction cannot change the orbital occupancies, which causes the DFT $+U$ method to be inapplicable.

### B. Effects of spin-orbit coupling

The highly anisotropic behavior of ${\rm Ca_3Ru_2O_7}$ suggests a crucial role of the SO coupling.$^{17}$ To investigate the effect of the SO coupling on the band structure, three spin orientations are considered in this work, where the magnetic moments are aligned along the $a$ (AFM-$a$), $b$ (AFM-$b$), and $c$ (AFM-$c$) axis. Figure 3 displays the GGA $+$ SO band structures for AFM ${\rm Ca_3Ru_2O_7}$. As may be seen, the SO coupling shows different influence on the three AFM phases. One of the distinctions is pointed out in Fig. 3. In the GGA band structure, there is a band crossing near the Y $(0,\pi/b,0)$ point just below the Fermi level. In Fig. 3, this band crossing is indicated by dot circles and named "W." As shown in Fig. 3, the band crossing $W$ is lifted in the AFM-$b$ phase, while it still exists in the AFM-$a$ and AFM-$c$ phases. ### C. Interplay of SO coupling and Coulomb repulsion
Figure 1 shows that the Ru-$t_{2g}$ bands are fairly narrow (about 2.5 eV), which indicates that $\text{Ca}_3\text{Ru}_2\text{O}_7$ is a correlated system. In this work, we consider the electron correlation by using the GGA $+U$ method. For the AFM-$a$ and AFM-$c$ phases, the GGA $+U+$ SO calculations always show metallic behavior no matter how the $U$ value is chosen. As for the AFM-$b$ phase, the overlap between the bottom of the conduction band and the top of the valence band becomes less and less when $U$ is increasing from 0 to 3 eV, and eventually a band gap is opened at $U\sim3$ eV. Experiments found that the low-temperature insulating phase of $\text{Ca}_3\text{Ru}_2\text{O}_7$ has a narrow band gap about 0.1 eV. $^{26,27}$ To fit the observed band gap, a parameter $U=3.5$ eV is used in the GGA $+U+$ SO calculations.

Figure 4 presents the GGA $+U+$ SO band structures for the three spin orientations, obtained with $U=3.5$ eV. When the Coulomb interaction is taken into account, Fig. 4 clearly shows that the AFM-$b$ phase exhibits an insulating behavior, while the AFM-$a$ and AFM-$c$ phases remain metallic. The GGA $+U+$ SO total energies for the three AFM phases are given in Table I. Compared with the GGA $+$ SO results, GGA $+U+$ SO stabilizes the AFM-$b$ phase. The GGA $+$ SO calculations show that the AFM-$b$ phase is the ground state but it is only 0.2 meV/Ru lower than the AFM-$a$ phase. While within the GGA $+U+$ SO approximation, the AFM-$b$ is 11.9 meV/Ru lower than the AFM-$a$ phase, and 6.0 meV/Ru lower than the AFM-$c$ phase. The GGA $+U+$ SO calculations show that $\text{Ca}_3\text{Ru}_2\text{O}_7$ has an insulating ground state and the magnetic moments are aligned along the $b$ axis. This finding is in excellent agreement with the experimental results. $^{17,18,22}$ Moreover, the GGA $+U+$ SO calculations show that the AFM-$a$ phase is metallic, which consistently explains the observed spin reorientation at $T_{\text{MI}}$. $^{19,21-23}$

Theoretical study on $\text{Sr}_2\text{RhO}_4$ has found that the SO effect could be enhanced by Coulomb repulsion. $^{14}$ The interplay of SO coupling and Coulomb repulsion in $\text{Ca}_3\text{Ru}_2\text{O}_7$ may also be described by the Coulomb-enhanced SO effect. The GGA $+$ SO calculations show that the SO coupling causes the AFM-$b$ phase to be nearly semimetallic. When the Coulomb interaction is taken into account, the SO effect is enhanced, leading to an insulating phase. On the other hand, the GGA $+$ SO calculations find that the AFM-$a$ and AFM-$c$ phases are metallic. Within the GGA $+U+$ SO approximation, the AFM-$a$ and AFM-$c$ phases remain metallic. This is not surprising since the role of $U$ is to enhance the SO effect. Coulomb interaction itself cannot break the orbital symmetry. As shown in Fig. 4, the band crossing $W$ still exists in the AFM-$a$ and AFM-$c$ phases.

### D. SO Hamiltonian and orbital physics
Now let us discuss the origin of the highly anisotropic band structure of $\text{Ca}_3\text{Ru}_2\text{O}_7$. As mentioned above, the AFM state is composed of half metallic bilayers with weak interbilayers coupling. To study the metal-insulator transition, we only need to consider the partly occupied minority spin channel. Figure 5 presents the character bands for the $t_{2g}$ orbitals with the minority spin. We focus on the band crossing $W$, which is a key point to understanding the anisotropic band structure. As shown in Fig. 5, the band crossing $W$ comes from the pure $yz$ and $x^2-y^2$ bands. Note that in the local coordinate system, the $yz$ orbital would be a mixed orbital of $xz'$ and $yz'$. Compared with the local coordinate system, our chosen coordinate system is more convenient for the discussion.

![](./images/813345753937215493_5.jpg)

FIG. 5. (Color online) Band character of FM phase for the $t_{2g}$ orbitals with the minority spin, calculated by GGA approximation (without SO).

The SO coupling operator can be given by $\hat{H}_{SO} = \lambda \hat{l} \cdot \hat{s}$, where $\hat{l}$ and $\hat{s}$ are the orbital and spin angular momentum operators, and $\lambda$ is the SO coupling constant. In the problem of $\mathrm{Ca_3Ru_2O_7}$, we only need consider the $t_{2g}$ orbitals with minority spin. In the space of $(d_{x^2-y^2}^{\downarrow}, d_{xz}^{\downarrow}, d_{yz}^{\downarrow})$, the SO Hamiltonian for the three spin orientations are

$$
H_{SO}^{(001)} = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \frac{i}{2}\lambda \\
0 & -\frac{i}{2}\lambda & 0
\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
H_{SO}^{(010)} = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & -\frac{i}{2}\lambda \\
0 & 0 & 0 \\
\frac{i}{2}\lambda & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}, \tag{1}
$$

$$
H_{SO}^{(100)} = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & -\frac{i}{2}\lambda & 0 \\
\frac{i}{2}\lambda & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Equation (1) clearly shows that only $H_{SO}^{(010)}$ can lift the degeneracy of the $d_{x^2-y^2}^{\downarrow}$ and $d_{yz}^{\downarrow}$ bands, while $H_{SO}^{(100)}$ and $H_{SO}^{(001)}$ are zero matrices in the space of $(d_{x^2-y^2}^{\downarrow}, d_{yz}^{\downarrow})$. If the crystal structure is undistorted, the $xz$ and $yz$ bands are degenerate along the $Y\Gamma$ direction. In that case, $H_{SO}^{(010)}$ and $H_{SO}^{(100)}$ would have same influence on the band structure due to the symmetry of the SO Hamiltonian. However, the experimental structure is strongly distorted by the $\mathrm{RuO_6}$ octahedra tilting. $^{17,18}$ The $\mathrm{RuO_6}$ tilting is nearly around the $b$ axis, which breaks the $xz/yz$ symmetry. As shown in Fig. 5, the $xz$ and $yz$ bands are well separated. Thus the anisotropic behavior of $\mathrm{Ca_3Ru_2O_7}$ can be owing to the interplay of structural distortion and SO coupling.

In the $\mathrm{GGA}+U+\mathrm{SO}$ calculations, the AFM-$b$ phase exhibits an insulating behavior. To understand the insulating phase, the partial density of states (PDOS) is presented in Fig. 2. If the $d_{x^2-y^2}^{\downarrow}$ and $d_{yz}^{\downarrow}$ orbitals are degenerate and the $t_{2g}$ orbitals do not hybridize with each other, $H_{SO}^{(010)}$ will split the degenerate bands to the higher band $(d_{x^2-y^2}^{\downarrow} + i d_{yz}^{\downarrow})$ and the lower band $(d_{x^2-y^2}^{\downarrow} - i d_{yz}^{\downarrow})$. As shown in Fig. 6(b), the PDOS of the $x^2-y^2$ and $yz$ are well overlapped from 0.1 to 0.6 eV, which signifies that the two orbitals are strongly coupled together, in agreement with the discussion. However, from $-1$ to 0 eV, the $x^2-y^2$ orbital has quite different PDOS from the $yz$ orbital. This indicates that the above approximation is too simple and we need to consider the influence of the crystal structure. If the bilayer system is undistorted, the $t_{2g}$ bands will be split into the singly degenerate two-dimensional (2D) $x^2-y^2$ band and the doubly degenerate 1D $xz/yz$ bands. Note that the SO coupling mainly influences the degenerate bands since Ru has a moderate SO constant. Then the effect of $H_{SO}^{(010)}$ should be weak in the undistorted system. Therefore the strong SO effect shown in Fig. 6(b) should be due to the large structural distortion, which causes the $t_{2g}$ orbitals to hybridize with each other. This suggests that the AFM-$b$ insulating phase strongly depends on the structural distortion. In $(\mathrm{Ca_{1-y}La_y})_3\mathrm{Ru_2O_7}$, Cao *et al.* found that the substitution of La for Ca significantly reduces the structural distortion. Meanwhile, the authors also showed that the insulating phase can be destroyed by very dilute doping of La. $^{28}$ The above theoretical picture is consistent with the experimental finding. $^{28}$

### III. SUMMARY

We have applied the $\mathrm{GGA+SO}$ and $\mathrm{GGA}+U+\mathrm{SO}$ calculations to $\mathrm{Ca_3Ru_2O_7}$. We find that $\mathrm{Ca_3Ru_2O_7}$ is a novel Mott insulator. First, the metal-insulator transition is induced by the interplay of SO coupling and Coulomb repulsion. The $\mathrm{GGA+SO}$ calculations show that the ground state is a nearly semimetallic phase. In the $\mathrm{GGA}+U+\mathrm{SO}$ calculations, the SO effect is enhanced by Coulomb interaction, which leads to an insulating ground phase. Second, the metal-insulator

transition is strongly dependent on the direction of the magnetic moment. When the spin orientation is along the $b$ axis, the $\text{GGA} + U + \text{SO}$ band structure indicates an insulating phase. When the magnetic moments are aligned along the $a$ axis or $c$ axis, the material is found to be metallic. Our calculations not only reproduce the experimental insulating phase but also explain the observed spin reorientation at $T_{\text{MI}}$. Furthermore, we indicate that the crystal structure distortion is crucial to the Mott transition and the highly anisotropic behavior.
