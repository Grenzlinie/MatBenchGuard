# Onset of magnetism in palladium slabs

S. Bouarab, C. Demangeat
IPC MS, UMR46, Université Louis Pasteur, 4 rue Blaise Pascal, 67070 Strasbourg, France

A. Mokrani and H. Dreyssé
Laboratoire de Physique des Solides, BP 239, 54506 Vandoeuvre-les Nancy, France

Received 27 July 1990; accepted for publication 28 September 1990
Communicated by J. Flouquet

We report a self-consistent tight-binding calculation on Pd slabs in the unrestricted Hartree-Fock approximation of the Hubbard Hamiltonian. By considering slabs with different thicknesses we show that, whereas the monolayer is like the bulk and thick slabs non-magnetic, a magnetic moment appears for films with 2, 3 and 4 layers.

Calculations [1] for bulk palladium predict magnetic ordering upon expanding the lattice by $\approx 10 \%$. Preliminary results based on electron capture spectroscopy (ECS) [2] have suggested the existence of ferromagnetism at Pd films grown on $\mathrm{Ag}(100)$, contrary to Fink et al. [3] who did not find unambiguous existence of ferromagnetism within the sensitivity of the magneto-optic Kerr effect.

The calculations reported in the present Letter address the possibility of induced magnetism in Pd slabs in terms of their thickness. Three of us have recently developed a real space self-consistent scheme for the description of metallic multilayers with variable thicknesses [4]. The magnetism is described in the unrestricted Hartree-Fock approximation of the Hubbard Hamiltonian for d electrons. In this model, we start with canonical hopping integrals [5] whereas the spin dependent diagonal term is given by

$$E_{i \sigma}=E_{\mathrm{d}}^{0}+U \Delta N_{i}-\sigma(J / 2) \mu_{i},$$

where $i$ is the index of the atomic plane and $\sigma$ is the spin. $E_{\mathrm{d}}^{0}$ is the atomic d level and can be chosen as zero. The exchange and effective direct intra-atomic Coulomb integrals denoted by $J$ and $U$ respectively are taken to be independent of the size of the slabs and of the position $i$. The local magnetic moment $\mu_{i}$ is given by

$$\mu_{i}=N_{i \uparrow}-N_{i \downarrow},$$

where $N_{i \sigma}$, the number of electrons of site $i$ and spin $\sigma$, is given in terms of the spin polarized local density of states (SPDOS) $n_{i \sigma}(E)$ which is calculated by using the recursion method [6] with eight levels of the continuous fraction. In order to have the Fermi level of the bulk Pd at the right of the higher peak of $n_{i \sigma}(E)$ we assume $N_{i}^{0}=9.4$ electrons per atom. $\Delta N_{i}$ is the difference between the electronic occupations $N_{i}$ and $N_{i}^{0}$.

For pure palladium we have determined the onset of magnetism in terms of the exchange parameter $J$. The critical value of $J$ for the appearance of magnetism is $J_{\mathrm{c}}=0.715 \mathrm{eV}$. Because bulk palladium is paramagnetic we cannot adjust $J$ as in the iron system [4] in order to recover the experimental value. Therefore we choose, in accordance with the LMTO calculations of Christensen et al. [7] a value of $J=0.65 \mathrm{eV}$ which gives a zero moment for bulk palladium.

We have investigated within our spin polarized slab recursion method the self-consistent magnetic polarisation of Pd slabs with different thicknesses for (001) crystallographic surface orientation. We first started, as in our previous calculation concerning vanadium [8] where we have found that the mono-

layer is unambiguously ferromagnetic, with a one-layer slab.

We have found that the Pd monolayer becomes magnetic for a $J$ higher than $J_{c}$. Therefore we can rule out magnetism in the Pd monolayer. This calculation was performed with the same parameters as those of pure Pd.

The absence of magnetism in the Pd monolayer can be explained as follows by considering fig. 1: the density of states at the Fermi level is smaller than in the bulk. The 2D effect increases the density of states in the middle of the band, whereas it decreases it at the border. Therefore the Stoner like criterion $Jn(E_{\text{F}})\geqslant 1$ is not satisfied anymore so that the Pd monolayer is non-magnetic.

We have also investigated Pd monolayer on Ag with two types of approximations:
(1) the hoppings of Pd are those of the bulk;
(2) the Pd is grown epitaxially on Ag [3] so that the lattice parameter is that of bulk Ag. The lattice mismatch for Pd on Ag should yield a 5% expansion of the Pd lattice. Therefore the hopping integrals used are modified through the $r^{-5}$ law.

We do not find any magnetism because in the two cases surface peaks appear far below the Fermi level.

(1) In vanadium, $E_{F}$ is in the middle of the band with a small value of $n(E_{F})$ ; the surface effect in duces a strong peak in the middle of the band.
(2) In palladium, $E_{F}$ is in a peak of the density of states, near the top of the band; the surface effect is likely to decrease the density of states $n(E_{F})$ so that an unsupported Pd monolayer or a monolayer epi- taxially grown on Ag is unlikely to become magnetic.
We have studied the effect of thickness of the Pd slabs on the onset of magnetism. For this purpose we have investigated films with n=2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 11 lay- ers. Films with n=2, 3, 4, 5 are shown to be mag- netic whereas the other thicknesses tend to kill the magnetism. As displayed in figs. 2-4 where we have reported the density of states, we notice the appear- ance of a peak in the density of states at the Fermi level. This peak, present in the monolayer, but much below the Fermi level, moves towards $E_{F}$ for higher number of layers. For six layers or more this peak structure tends to disappear. In all cases we observe that the magnetic polarization is larger on central at- oms. Moreover for a film with three planes a quasi strong ferromagnetism $(\mu_{c}=0.5 \mu_{B})$ is observed in the middle.
In conclusion, our results indicate that the mag- netism of palladium films with small interaction with the substrate is not due to a surface effect (as for va- nadium for example) but to a film effect.
The onset of magnetism in palladium slabs de- pends dramatically on the number of layers consid- ered. Magnetism can be obtained only with n=2, 3,4, 5 layer slabs (table 1). It can also be noticed that our preliminary results show that n=2, 3 layers of Pd grown epitaxially on Ag display a magnetic mo- ment. The difference in behaviour between the monolayer or the bilayer of Pd on Ag could explain the discrepancies between the experimental results of Rau [2] and Fink et al. [3].
