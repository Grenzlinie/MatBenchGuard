**Abstract:** We have computed the electronic properties and magnetic ordering of MgV₂O₄. It undergoes two successive transitions first structural and second magnetic at low temperatures. Our DFT calculations show that the antiferromagnetic phase has the lowest energy and the band gap increases with the inclusion of Coulomb correlations and spin orbit coupling.

**Keywords:** DFT, Spinel systems, Wien2k
**PACS:** 71.15.Mb, 71.20.-b, 72.80.Ga

## INTRODUCTION

Vanadium spinels are one of the most fascinating members of spinel family having V³⁺ ion at the B-site of pyrochlore lattice forming a frustrated system. The interplay of orbital, spin and lattice degrees of freedom causes various successive transitions in vanadium spinels with the lowering of temperature [1]. MgV₂O₄ is a normal spinel with highly frustrated magnetic lattice. MgV₂O₄ undergoes two successive transitions first structural from cubic to tetragonal at 51 K followed by magnetic transition from paramagnetic to antiferromagnetic (AFM) state at 42 K [2]. The high temperature phase of MgV₂O₄ has normal cubic spinel structure with space group F4̅3m. The V ions are coordinated by O₆ octahedra. Due to the presence of octahedral crystal field, the d orbital splits into triply degenerate t₂₉ levels with lower energy, and doubly degenerate e₉ orbitals with higher energy. V ion (3d²) has partially filled d orbitals which makes V ion Jahn-Teller active. Thus there occurs a compression along c-axis at low temperature (51 K) with c/a =0.9941 and the system crystallizes in tetragonal structure having space group 119I 4m2 [2]. The triply degenerate t₂₉ level further splits into dₓᵧ state with lower energy, and dₓz and dᵧz states with higher energy. The coordinated oxygen octahedron is not an ideal octahedron as the angles are not perfect 90⁰ and the distances between various oxygen atoms and vanadium are not same therefore some small trigonal distortion is also present in MgV₂O₄. Furthermore a magnetic transition occurs at 42 K having antiferromagnetic chains which effectively reduce the magnetic moment. In this, we explore the electronic structure and magnetic ordering of MgV₂O₄.

## METHODOLOGY

The calculations have been carried out with no shape approximation to the potential and charge density. The basis has been chosen to be linear augmented plane wave (LAPW) as implemented in WIEN2K code [3]. These calculations were performed at three levels of sophistication: within the local spin density approximation (LSDA), LSDA+U, and within LSDA+U+SO approach incorporating spin-orbit (SO) interaction. For LSDA+U+SO calculations, the SO coupling was treated using a scalar relativistic basis and the second variational method [4]. We have used 50 k points in the irreducible part of the Brillouin zone for the self-consistent calculations. The atomic sphere radii were chosen to be 1.96, 1.99, and 1.78 a.u. for Mg, V, and O, respectively. In order to replicate the AFM structure of MgV₂O₄, we built supercell with 8 nonequivalent V-ions which effectively reduce the symmetry of the system. Lattice constants were fixed at the experimental value [1]. Figure 1 shows the unit cell of MgV₂O₄ at low temperature which we have used in our calculations. Mg ions (blue) are tetrahedrally coordinated by oxygen (green) while the V ions (red) are surrounded by a slightly distorted octahedral array of oxygen ions.

## RESULTS

We have performed the volume optimization with a fixed c/a ratio and found that the ground state structure has 'a' = 5.920 Å (=b) and c = 8.323 Å. We find that the ferromagnetic phase of MgV₂O₄ has higher total energy than that of experimentally observed antiferromagnetic (AFM) phase by 0.145 eV using LSDA. We also considered another magnetic configuration (say AFMA) where both the chains have FM ordering but are coupled antiferromagnetically along c axis. The AFMA phase is found to be higher in energy by 0.066 eV with respect to AFM phase. Thus our result that the ground state has AFM configuration at low temperatures is consistent with experiments [2]. Hence we explored the AFM phase further. The LSDA gives metallic ground state (Fig 2(a)) whereas the system is known to be a Mott insulator. With the inclusion of Coulomb correlations in LSDA calculations, we get the desired band gap at Fermi level. Though the results are presented for U – J = 2 eV, all our results remain valid in the range of U-J between 1 to 4. The t₂g and eₛ splitting also increases with the addition of U in LSDA. We also performed more refined calculations with Spin-Orbit interaction (LSDA+U+SO). Figure 2 represents the band structure for MgV₂O₄ within LSDA, LSDA+U and LSDA+U+SO. In LSDA+U calculations there occurs a band gap of about 0.1 eV at the Fermi level which is not present in LSDA results. The gap occurs because of the splitting in t₂g band due to the AFM spin arrangement and the Coulomb correlations enhance this gap. The band gap increases with the increase in U value. With the addition of SO coupling in LSDA +U, there is a further increase in the band gap at the Fermi level. The inclusion of SO lends the system more stability yielding the lowest total energy.

Hence our DFT calculations show that the experimentally observed AFM structure has the lowest energy and that the Coulomb correlations are essential to be included to open up a gap at Fermi level, in agreement with experiments, which is enhanced by the SO coupling [5].
