# Theory of the magnetism of ternary uranium compounds

T. Gasche $^{a}$, S. Auluck $^{a}$, M.S.S. Brooks $^{a}$ and B. Johansson $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Commission of the European Communities, European Institute for Transuranium Elements, Postfach 2340, W-7500 Karlsruhe, Germany
$^{b}$ Condensed Matter Theory Group, Institute of Physics, University of Uppsala, Box 530, S-75121 Uppsala, Sweden

Measurements suggest that the 5f electrons in the compounds UTAl (T = Fe, Co, Ni, Ru, Rh, Ir, Pt), which crystallize in the hexagonal ZrNiAl structure, are itinerant. From self-consistent LMTO energy band calculations for the compounds we explain the transition to magnetism across the series but show that the magnetism must contain an orbital contribution. Inclusion of spin-orbit interaction leads to large induced orbital moments in the magnetic compounds.

The UTAl compounds crystallize in the hexagonal ZrNiAl structure, a modified form of the $Fe_{2}P$ type. The available data is well documented [1] with UFeAl and URuAl paramagnetic, UCoAl metamagnetic [2] and the remaining 4 magnetic. Extremely large anisotropy in the magnetic susceptibility of these compounds has been reported. Evidently the uranium atoms find themselves in a highly anisotropic environment to which the 5f states are likely to be particularly sensitive.

If we start by considering UFeAl (fig. 1a) we find that the Fermi level lies inbetween the occupied 3d band and the unoccupied 5f band. The density of states at the Fermi level is quite low and the Stoner criterion for magnetism is not fulfilled. If we now consider the isotructural UCoAl we may, using a rigid band approach, approximate it as UFeAl with one more electron added. This will have the effect of pushing the Fermi level up from the hybridization gap into 5f band. This in turn will give rise to an increased density of states at the Fermi level and the Stoner criterion for magnetism may be fulfilled. As has previously been shown [3], UCoAl is paramagnetic in its ground state but a metamagnetic state has been found, lying 4 mRy above the ground state. Finally, advancing to UNiAl we see that the Fermi level has risen up into the 5f band and this compound is magnetic. We may repeat this argument for the 4d and 5d analogues. We find URuAl non-magnetic, and both URhAl and UIrAl on the borderline to magnetism (the Stoner product is close to 1). Finally, UPtAl is expected to have a high density of states at the Fermi level and so be magnetic.

We first consider the calculations for the paramagnetic ground states and examine the Stoner criterion for the series. The calculated Stoner products, $IN$, are displayed in table 1. Where the product is greater than 1 the compounds were allowed to spin polarize. We notice that the calculated moments for URhAl and UIrAl are similar, as we would expect for a similar $4d$ and $5d$ occupation. Likewise, UNiAl and UPtAl also have similar moments and the higher 5f-derived band occupation leads to a higher moment. It has been shown [7] that orbital moments give rise to a substantial contribution in uranium compounds. To illustrate this point we have included spin-orbit coupling in a self-consistent manner into our calculation [4]. The calculated moments for URhAl are 0.27 spin and $-0.11$ orbital. It has also been shown that an improved approximation to the exchange and correlation potential is required to give adequate values for orbital moments [3,8]. We have therefore included an orbital polarization term $E^{3}L^{\sigma}m_{l}^{\sigma}$ where $E^{3}$ is the Racah parameter, $L^{\sigma}$ the orbital angular momentum for spin $\sigma$ and $m_{l}^{\sigma}$ the magnetic quantum number [3]. Calculations now yield a spin moment of 0.30 and an antiparallel orbital moment of $-0.37$. In both cases the non-uranium moments are negligible. Clearly, the inclusion of orbital polarization gives rise to a large contribution to the total moment but the overall calculated moment is much smaller than the experimental value. The moments for UIrAl and UPtAl were measured on powder samples and the large anisotropy may have led to erroneous values. However, URhAl was measured using a single crystal. Normally the calculated moments for actinide systems are an overestimate of the experimental moment [3,8] but in this case the calculation yields a gross underestimate.

As noted above, UCoAl is metamagnetic. This can be expressed as the energy vs. magnetic moment curve having at least two local minima, whereas ferromagnetism has just one in any given direction. Considering the metamagnetism of UCoAl, we could expect a metamagnetic behaviour for the isostructural and isoelectronic URhAl. We have therefore searched for a metamagnetic solution lying at a higher magnetic moment. The state lies at a higher energy than the lower magnetic state, however it has been shown that metamagnetic states are extremely sensitive to the over estimation of bonding in LSDA [9] and this may therefore be the true ground state. The final calculated moments, for this high moment state are (including orbital polarization) a spin moment of 1.06 and an orbital moment of $-1.51$, in closer agreement with experiment.

The passage from non-magnetic to magnetic behaviour across the group of compounds is clearly explained by the model of the filling of the combined DoS. The originally calculated moments for URhAl are shown to be misleading as the state is a metamagnetic one and the experimentally determined state is the high moment state. Calculations in the high moment state yield moments which are in agreement within our expectations for an actinide system.

It is noted that the experimental data show an irregularity in the measured magnetic moments and that a measurement on single crystals may yield more reasonable values.
