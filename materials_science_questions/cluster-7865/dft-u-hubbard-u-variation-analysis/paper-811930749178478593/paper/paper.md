# Electronic Structure and Formation Heat of Pu₃M and PuM₃ (M=Ga, In, Sn and Ge) Compounds
Wenhua Luo*, Daqiao Meng, Gan Li, Huchi Chen

National Key Laboratory for Surface Physics and Chemistry, Mianyang 621907, Sichuan Province, P. R. China

## Abstract
The equilibrium structure, electronic structure, and formation heat of Pu₃M (PuM₃) (M=Ga, In, Sn, and Ge) compounds with AuCu₃ structure have been calculated using full potential linear augmented plane wave (FPLAPW) method within generalized-gradient approximation (GGA) including spin-orbit coupling (SOC) and spin polarization (SP). The calculated lattice parameters are in good agreement with the experimental values. Density of state analysis shows hybridization effects between Pu and M are governed by the competitions depending on the M amount: Pu 6d-Pu 5f, M p-Pu 6d, and M sp-M sp interactions. Electronegativity difference and electronic hybridization effect are two important factors to influence the formation heat and stability of Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds. The larger is the electronegativity difference and the lower is M s-band or p-band center relative to the Fermi energy, the more negative is the formation heat and the more stable are Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds.

## Key Words:
FPLAPW; Pu₃M and PuM₃ compounds; Electronic structure; Formation heat

Pu is an important nuclear fuel, and has six allotropic structures from room temperature to its melting point at atmospheric pressure. Amongst these phases, δ phase has received a significant interest, because of its high ductility that makes it easy to machine and form. δ phase is only stable at temperatures between 593 and 736 K, but can be stabilized at room temperature by alloying Pu with a small amount of elements, such as Al, Ga, In, Ce, Am, so called δ-stabilizer¹⁻³. It is believed that the stability of δ phase is closely related to its electronic and thermodynamic properties such as formation heat, so to get a better understanding of the role of each substituted element on the stability of δ phase, an investigation of these properties is necessary. Computation of δ phase solid solutions modeled with a large super-cell structure involves far more atoms and then is far time-consuming, so Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds with simple AuCu₃ structure are chosen as a starting point. There have been a few publications of calculated electronic structure of Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds. For example, Becker et al.⁴ performed all electron full potential linear muffin-tin orbitals (FPLMTO) calculation of the electronic structure of Pu₃M (M=Al, Ga, In, and Tl) compounds, and Robert et al.⁵ performed ab initio analysis of the structural stability of Pu₁₋ₓMₓ (M=Al, Ga, and In) compounds using all-electron full-potential linear augmented plane wave (FPLAPW) method. In this article, on the basis of the previous study⁴,⁵, the alloying elements were extended from IIIA column to IVA column, and the calculation of equilibrium structure, electronic structure, and formation heat of AuCu₃-type Pu₃M (PuM₃) (M=Ga, In, Sn, and Ge) compounds were performed to better understand the electronic and thermodynamic properties of these compounds. As regards ab initio method for calculation of Pu metal and its alloys, dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) and LDA+U method are two efficient methods for the treatment of 5f electron strongly-correlated effects⁶⁻¹⁰. But their results are U-dependent, U parameter can vary in function of allotropic phase, alloying element or amount, and then the predictive calculations of the present system's properties can hardly be done on the same level. On the other hand, using generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and spin polarization (SP) calculations brings strong improvements on the numerous properties of all allotropic phase and its alloys: equilibrium volumes, bulk moduli, elastic

## 1 Computational method
The electronic structures and total energies are calculated using FPLAPW method within the density functional theory (DFT) $^{[14,15]}$. This method divides the crystal into non-overlapping 'muffin-tin' spheres and an interstitial zone. Inside 'muffin-tin' sphere, where the potential has very strong variation, the potential and the electronic density are expanded with spherical harmonics, and the basis set is a linear combination of radial functions times spherical harmonics. In the interstitial zone, where the potential is almost constant, a plane wave expansion is used for the potential, electronic density, and basis set.

The self-consistent calculations are performed with a fully relativistic treatment of the core states and a scalar-relatively treatment including spin-orbit coupling (SOC) for valence states. The GGA of Perdew-Bourke-Ernzerhof (PBE96) is used and spin polarization is taken into account. The basis set includes $6s$, $6p$, $7s$, $7p$, $6d$, and $5f$ partial waves for Pu; $3d$, $4s$, and $4p$ for Ga and Ge; and $4d$, $5s$, and $5p$ for In and Sn. Spin-orbit interaction is considered *via* a second variation step using the scalar-relativistic eigenfunctions as the basis. Additional $p_{1/2}$-local orbitals (LO) are included in the basis set to modify the treatment of $6p$ semicore states by SOC, then to suppress the dependence of equilibrium properties on muffin-tin radii. Moreover, to obtain more flexibility in the radial basis functions and to decrease the basis set size, new local orbitals (APW+lo) for Pu $5f$ partial waves are included. Brillouin zone integration is performed using modified tetrahedron method with 1000 $k$-points Muffin-tin radius $R_{\text{mt}}$ are 2.5 Bohr for all structures except for Ge metal (2.2 Bohr), which has a shorter distance between the first neighbor atoms. Finally, plane-wave cutoff $K_{\text{cut}}$ and energy convergence criterion are set to 12.25 Ry and 0.1 mRy, respectively.

The formation heat $E_{\text{Pu}_3\text{M}}^{\text{f}}$ ($E_{\text{PuM}_3}^{\text{f}}$) of $\text{Pu}_3\text{M}$ ($\text{PuM}_3$) compounds are
$$
E_{\text{Pu}_3\text{M}}^{\text{f}} = E_{\text{Pu}_3\text{M}}^{\text{min}} -(0.75E_{\text{Pu}}^{\text{min}}+0.25E_{\text{M}}^{\text{min}}) \tag{1}
$$
$$
E_{\text{PuM}_3}^{\text{f}} = E_{\text{PuM}_3}^{\text{min}} -(0.25E_{\text{Pu}}^{\text{min}}+0.75E_{\text{M}}^{\text{min}}) \tag{2}
$$
where, $E^{\text{min}}$ is the optimized total energy of each phase. Therefore, a negative sign indicates that the compounds are thermodynamically favorable, and the value reflects the relative stability of different compounds.

## 2 Results and discussion
### 2.1 Optimization of equilibrium structure
For optimization of equilibrium structures of pure Pu, alloying elements, and $\text{Pu}_3\text{M}$ ($\text{PuM}_3$) compounds, the total energies are calculated at different volumes with lattice constant ratio $c/a$ and $b/a$ kept constant and only $a$ varied within GGA+SOC+SP approximation. The Murnaghan's equation of state is used to fit the total energy curves and obtain the equilibrium volumes and bulk moduli.
$$
E(V)-E(V_{0})=\frac{B_{0}V}{B_{0}{}'}\left[\frac{(V_{0}/V)^{B_{0}{}'}}{B_{0}{}'-1}+\mathrm{I}\right]-\frac{B_{0}V_{0}}{B_{0}{}'-1} \tag{3}
$$
where $V_0$, $B_0$, and $B_0{}'$ are atomic equilibrium volume, bulk modulus, and pressure derivative of bulk modulus, respectively. Calculated results are listed in Table 1 with some available experimental results $^{[16,17]}$ for comparison. It shows that, for pure Pu, the calculated equilibrium lattice parameter is in good agreement with the experimental value, whereas the bulk modulus is underestimated to a certain extent.

These results are consistent with previous report$^{[11]}$. For alloying elements, both calculated equilibrium lattice parameters and bulk moduli are in good agreement with experimental values, with $\Delta a$=$(a_{\text{calc}}-a_{\text{expt}})/a_{\text{expt}}$ less than 2.5%. For $\text{Pu}_3\text{M}$ ($\text{PuM}_3$) compounds, the calculated equilibrium lattice parameters are in good agreement with experimental values with $\Delta a$ less than 1.0%, and bulk moduli are in the range of 50-90 GPa with no available experimental values to compare with. In general, GGA+SOC+SP approximation is able to better reproduce the ground state properties of $\text{Pu}_3\text{M}$ ($\text{PuM}_3$) compounds, and thus is used to calculate electronic structure and formation heat of these compounds.

For plutonium-rich compound, every Sn atom is surrounded by Pu atoms as its nearest neighbors, and In 5s states are only weakly hybridized with In 5p and Pu 6d states, thus these 5s states become localized in an atomic-like configuration leading to a gap between these states and the bottom of valence band. The gap disappears in PuIn₃, as in this compound: every In atom is mainly surrounded by other In atoms, restoring the metallic sp band characteristics. Moreover, the gap width is greater for spin-down channel than that for spin-up channel due to different spin polarized In 5p-Pu 6d. In a similar way, neighboring Pu governs the competition between In 5p-Pu 6d and Pu 6d-Pu 5f interactions and localization of 5f bands. In Pu₃In, a strong Pu 6d-Pu 5f hybridization leads to a broad 5f band, and then to more itinerant 5f states. In PuIn₃, the absence of Pu-Pu first-neighbor interactions leads to a weaker overlap of Pu 6d and Pu 5f bands, and then to more localized 5f states. All these features are in complete agreement with the previous calculations⁵. The partial DOS for Pu₃Sn, PuSn₃, Pu₃Ga, and PuGe₃ are shown in Figs.3, 4, 5, and 6, respectively. They display the same general trends observed in Pu₃In and PuIn₃. The main difference comes from the gap width for Pu₃M: 2.3 eV for Pu₃Sn larger than 1.5 eV for Pu₃Ga and 1.0 eV for Pu₃In, and from the presence of a small gap for PuSn₃ and PuIn₃, which is absent for PuIn₃. This difference may be due to the semi-conducting characteristics of Sn and Ge, although in exhibit broad parabolic sp band, Sn and Ge show a separation between s and p states. In general, the hybridization effects between Pu and M are governed by the competitions depending on the M amount: Pu 6d-Pu 5f, Mn p-Pu 6d, and M sp-M sp interactions.

This discrepancy may be attributed to the fact that the fcc structures are used for total energy calculation of Ga and In elements in Ref.[5], whereas experimental structures are used in this study. According to Miedema's theory$^{[18-20]}$, the formation heat of Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds is correlated with atomic size, atomic electronegativity, atomic value electron number, and electronic structure of the compound. Due to small difference in value electron number for these compounds, the effects of atomic size difference $\Delta R$=$(R_\text{Pu}-R_\text{M})/R_\text{Pu}$, electronegativity difference $\Delta\Phi$=$\Phi_\text{Pu}-\Phi_\text{M}$, and energy difference between the center of M $s(p)$ band and Fermi level ($\Delta E_s$=$E_\text{F}-E_s$ and $\Delta E_p$=$E_\text{F}-E_p$) on the formation heat were investigated. According to Table 1, there is a clear correlation between electronegativity difference and formation heat, that is, the larger is $\Delta\Phi$, the more negative is $E^\text{f}$. There is no clear correlation between atomic size difference and formation heat, for example, $\Delta R$ of Pu₃Ga is the largest amongst the three Pu₃M compounds, while the corresponding $E^\text{f}$ is smaller than that of Pu₃Sn. The correlation between $\Delta E_s$ ($\Delta E_p$) and formation heat is plotted in Fig.7. It shows that $E^\text{f}$ almost linearly decreases as $\Delta E_s$ ($\Delta E_p$) increases, the larger is $\Delta E_s$ ($\Delta E_p$), the more negative is $E^\text{f}$, which obeys the common notion that stronger binding is related to electrons far below the Fermi level. From the above discussion, it is clear that electronegativity difference and electronic hybridization effect are two important factors to influence the formation heat and stability of Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds.

## 3 Conclusions
The equilibrium structure, electronic structure, and formation heat of Pu₃M (PuM₃) (M=Ga, In, Sn, and Ge) compounds with AuCu₃ structure have been calculated using the FPLAPW method within the GGA+SOC+SP approximation. The calculated lattice parameter is in good agreement with the experimental value with relative deviation less than 1.0%. Density of state analysis shows that the hybridization effects between Pu and M are governed by the competitions depending on the M amount: Pu $6d$-Pu $5f$, M $p$-Pu $6d$, and M $sp$-M $sp$ interactions. Electronegativity difference and electronic hybridization effect are two important factors to influence the formation heat and stability of Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds. The larger is the electronegativity difference and the lower is M $s$-band or $p$-band center relative to the Fermi energy, the more negative is the formation heat and the more stable are Pu₃M (PuM₃) compounds.
