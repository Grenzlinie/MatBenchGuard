# Synthesis and characterization of inorganic fullerene-like WSe₂ material

T. Tsirlina¹, Y. Feldman¹, M. Homyonfer¹, J. Sloan², J.L. Hutchison² and R. Tenne¹

¹ Department of Materials and Interfaces, Weizmann Institute of Science, Rehovot 76100, Israel
² Department of Materials, University of Oxford, Parks Road, Oxford OX1 3PH, UK

## Abstract

Lately MoS₂ and WS₂ fullerene-like nanoparticles, generically termed inorganic fullerene-like (IF) materials, were reproducibly obtained by gas phase⁴,⁶ and solid state⁶ reactions and the growth mechanism of such structures was elucidated⁶. The present work deals with the synthesis of IF-WSe₂ from tungsten oxide and selenium vapors. Thermodynamic calculations of the sulfidization and selenization processes are provided, and indicate salient differences between the two processes. Notwithstanding, the growth model of IF-WSe₂ is similar to that of sulfide fullerene-like material. The IF-WSe₂ was characterized by X-ray powder diffraction, transmission electron microscopy and high resolution transmission electron microscopy.

## Introduction

Recently¹⁻⁴ it was shown that layered compounds of metal disulfides, such as $\text{MoS}_2$ and $\text{WS}_2$, can form hollow-cage structures akin to the structure of carbon fullerenes and nanotubes. This phenomenon is accounted for by the crystal structure of disulfides of Mo and W in which every metal layer is sandwiched between two chalcogen layers. Strong covalent bonds exist between the metal and chalcogen atoms within the layer and weak van der Waals interaction hold the layers together. This structure provides an atomically smooth and inert van der Waals surface ($\perp$c), while the dangling bonds along the ||c planes are highly reactive. The excess surface energy caused by the dangling bonds in nanoclusters can lead to the situation that under certain conditions it will be more favorable for the $\text{MS}_2$ sheets to fold and form closed hollow structures, thereby eliminating the dangling bonds. Such conditions were met recently and consequently IF-$\text{MoS}_2$ was reproducibly synthesized by a gas phase reaction from an oxide precursor⁴⁻⁵. Since $\text{WO}_3$ is not volatile, a solid state reaction was used in order to obtain IF-$\text{WS}_2$ from $\text{WO}_3$ nanoparticles. The growth mechanism of IF-$\text{MS}_2$ (M=Mo,W) from oxide nanoparticles has been elucidated⁶,⁷. In spite of the differences in the synthesis of IF-$\text{MoS}_2$ and that of IF-$\text{WS}_2$, the growth mechanisms of both compounds were shown to be essentially the same.

Here we present the results of a study on the growth of IF-$\text{WSe}_2$. As in the case of IF-$\text{WS}_2$, the synthesis of IF-$\text{WSe}_2$ was performed through gas-solid reaction. It was shown that under certain conditions one can get fullerene-like $\text{WSe}_2$ particles similar to IF-$\text{WS}_2$ (Fig.1). The high resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM) images reveal that the model for the growth mechanism of IF-$\text{WSe}_2$ is similar to the one proposed by Feldman et al.⁶ for the sulfides. But due to the fact that the thermodynamics and kinetics of selenization are not the same as for the sulfidization process, one can notice some peculiarities of IF-$\text{WSe}_2$ growth which is reflected in the images (Fig.1).

## Experimental part

Commercial and a home-made WO₃ powders of average particle size
of 100 nm was used as precursor for the synthesis of IF-WSe₂. A thin
layer of the powder was spread out loosely on the bottom of a reactor
boat in order to expose maximum reactive surface of the WO₃
particles to the gas flow and avoid coalescence of the nanoparticles
during the annealing in atmosphere of Se and forming gas
(5%H₂/95%N₂). The flow rate was kept at 120ml/min. The temperature
of annealing was varied. Se shot was preheated up to 350°C in a small
oven placed upstream of the main reactor where reaction of tungsten
oxide with Se vapor and H₂ gas took place (Fig.2).

The synthesized WSe₂ samples were studied by X-ray powder
diffraction (XRD), transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and high

## Results and Discussion

The synthesis of IF-WSe₂ was carried out at temperatures of 760°C, 850°C and 950°C for 90 minute periods for each specimen. After that period of time the selenization process was found to be complete as one can see from the XRD patterns (Fig.3) since no tungsten oxide peaks could be discerned (region $2\theta=25^{\circ}-30^{\circ}$). At 760°C mostly IF-WSe₂ were obtained, while at 850°C the fraction of fullerene-like material was much smaller, and at a temperature of 950°C 2H-WSe₂ platelets were obtained exclusively. This observation is confirmed by XRD measurements and TEM investigation. In the inset of Fig.3 the (002) peak of the XRD pattern for all three temperatures is shown. The shift in the peak position has been used to determine the fraction of fullerene-like particles in the charge after reaction⁴,⁶. The shift of 2% in the position of the peak (curve c) reflects the complete conversion of oxide into fullerene-like material. This shift indicates a lattice expansion between two WSe₂ layers along the c axis caused by the elastic strain in the bent layers. An assortment of completely converted IF-WSe₂ particles is shown in Fig.1a.

Since the best conditions for synthesizing IF-WSe$_2$ so far were found to be an annealing temperature of $760^\circ$C and a gas flow of 120ml/min, some additional experiments were done under these conditions. Fig.1 shows two HRTEM images of fullerene-like $WS_2$ (b) and $WSe_2$ (c) particles with oxide core. The tungsten selenide particle was obtained at $760^\circ$C after 20 minutes of annealing in Se atmosphere. One notices that the selenization reaction was not completed and the core of the particle consists of partially commensurate tungsten suboxide $W_{18}O_{49}$ which shear planes are clearly seen in the figure (arrowed lines). When the sample is tilted the lines corresponding to the shear planes of the tungsten suboxide disappear while the closed lines (fringes) around the oxide particle

corresponding to (00l) planes of the IF-tungsten selenide do not vanish. This observation confirms that the IF-particle is closed in three dimensions (cage structure) as was indicated before¹.

The proposed model for IF growth⁶ can be described as follows: within the first few seconds of the reaction, a completely closed monomolecular $MS_2$ layer or two are formed enfolding the oxide nanoparticle. This process passivates the surfaces of the nanoparticles and prohibits their coalescence. In the next stage of IF formation, sulfur slowly diffuses into the particle and reacts with the oxide, releasing water molecules which then diffuse out. The diffusion of sulfur atoms into the particle starts when they reach a defect like a dislocation. Then sulfur atoms intercalate between the already formed $WS_2$ layers and diffuse along the layers ($\perp$c) until they intersect another dislocation which permits them to pass through the layer (||c) to the next inner layer and finally reach the growth front. The same process is believed to take place during the growth of IF-$WSe_2$. On Fig.1b one can clearly see the edge dislocations which allow Se atoms to penetrate into the particle and water molecules to diffuse out of the particle. About nine closed layers surrounding the particle are already completed, while the core of tungsten oxide remains and a new growth front of five layers inside the closed layers advance in the oxide core. The HRTEM image of IF-$WSe_2$ looks very similar to the image of IF-$WS_2$, which suggests that the growth model mentioned above is valid in the case of IF-$WSe_2$ as well. Nevertheless, one can notice some discrepancies between the two images. While the IF-$WS_2$ particle has a relatively regular and smooth spherical shape, the IF-$WSe_2$ particle is faceted and has more edge dislocations. Another significant difference is the presence of several growth fronts for the IF-$WSe_2$ particle. These two facts can be explained by the difference in atomic sizes of sulfur and selenium (atomic radius of Se is $1.22Å$ versus $1.09Å$ of sulfur atom). The stronger elastic strains in the case of Se compound are responsible for the larger dislocation density that leads in turn to more faceted shape and the difficulty of the larger atom to move along ($\perp$c) the metal chalcogenide layers. This can lead to the formation of additional growth fronts.

Differences between the chemistry of sulfidization and selenization processes should also be taken into account. Sulfur is

much more reactive than selenium. Thermodynamic calculations show that the selenization reaction is possible in the presence of a strong reducer like hydrogen, while sulfidization of the oxide is afforded even in the absence of hydrogen. The results of the thermodynamic calculations are summarized in Tables I and II. All the thermochemical data were taken from Refs.8-11. Four different chemical reactions were considered for these calculations.

1) $WO_3$+$3S$+$H_2$ → $WS_2$+$H_2O$+$SO_2$
2) $2WO_3$+$7S$ → $2WS_2$+$3SO_2$
3) $WO_3$+$3Se$+$H_2$ → $WSe_2$+$H_2O$+$SeO_2$
4) $2WO_3$+$7Se$ → $2WSe_2$+$3SeO_2$

The reactants were taken in the following concentrations (wt %): 5% tungsten oxide, 10% sulfur (selenium), 38% hydrogen and 90% nitrogen; total pressure of 1 atm was considered here.

These calculations corroborate the results of the experiments. The sulfidization of tungsten oxide in a closed ampoule is completed after half an hour even in the absence of hydrogen while $WO_3$ does not undergo any chemical transformation in selenium atmosphere, which was confirmed by XRD analysis.

Thus a distinction in the chemistry of sulfur and selenium exists which influences the kinetics of fullerene-like structure formation. The kinetic study will need a series of additional experiments followed by XRD, HRTEM, XPS and some other analyses.

## Conclusions
It has been shown that using the growth technique which was successfully used for the synthesis of IF-$WS_2$$^{4,6}$, one can also obtain fullerene-like structures from another layered compound belonging to the $MX_2$ family (M=Mo,W; X=S,Se), like the IF tungsten selenide found in the current work. HRTEM images show almost no discrepancy between $WS_2$ and $WSe_2$ fullerene-like particles. Some thermodynamic calculations were done in order to compare the sulfidization and selenization reactions and to indicate the different chemistry of these processes.
