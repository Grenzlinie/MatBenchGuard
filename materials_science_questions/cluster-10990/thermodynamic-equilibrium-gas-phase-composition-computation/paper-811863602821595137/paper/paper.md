# Review of the Bi and Bi-O Vapor Systems¹

STEVEN C. MARSCHMAN

Department of Materials Science and Engineering, University of Washington, Seattle, Washington 98195, USA

and

D. C. LYNCH

Department of Metallurgical Engineering, University of Arizona, Tucson, Arizona 85721, USA

The Bi-O vapor system was investigated by evaluating thermodynamic data from several sources, Standard Gibbs energy equations for the formation of gaseous $Bi$, $Bi_2$, $Bi_3$, $Bi_4$, $BiO$, $Bi_2O$, $Bi_2O_2$, $Bi_2O_3$, $Bi_3O_4$, and $Bi_4O_6$ are developed for the tempratrure range of 544.59 K to 1600 K. The thermodynamic data are used to evaluate the partial pressures of the noted species in both the Bi and Bi-O systems. The results are plotted in several forms and reveal that bismuth oxides readily decompose at temperatures typical of pyrometallurgical operations.

On a étudié le système Bi-O vapeur en évaluant des données thermodynamiques à partir de diverses sources. On a établi, pour la gamme de températures comprises entre 544,59 K et 1600 K, les équations d'énergies de Gibbs standards de formation, à l'état gazeux, de $Bi$, $Bi_2$, $Bi_3$, $Bi_4$, $BiO$, $Bi_2O$, $Bi_2O_2$, $Bi_2O_3$, $Bi_3O_4$ et $Bi_4O_6$. On a utilisé les données thermodynamiques pour évaluer les pressions partielles des espèces indiquées pour les deux systèmes Bi et Bi-O. On a représenté les résultats sous plusieurs formes graphiques; ils révèlent que les oxydes de bismuth se décomposent facilement à des températures typiques des opérations pyrométallurgiques.

There is a considerable amount of data on the vapor species of elemental bismuth dispersed throughout the literature. There is a dearth of information on the bismuth oxide vapor species, and unfortunately there is little agreement in these data. The purpose of this study was to review the literature, evaluate the data and present them in a form suitable for use in common thermodynamic calculations. The data presented here are the combination of the efforts of several authors.

## Review of thermodynamic data

### BISMUTH VAPOR

The existence of the monomer and dimer in elemental bismuth vapor is well documented by Pankratz (1982). The selected thermodynamic values presented by Pankratz for $Bi$ and $Bi_2$ vapor are based on the data obtained in 23 investigations. The thermodynamic data for $Bi$ and $Bi_2$ in Table 2 were extracted from Pankratz's compilation.

Further search of the literature yielded data on the trimer and tetramer of bismuth. These species were thought previously to be non-existent. Voronin (1966) was the first to propose the species $Bi_4$. The data reported were based on torsion effusion experiments conducted by Aldred and Pratt (1963). However, his analysis was discounted by Kohl, Uy, and Carlson (1967), Kohl and coworkers studied pure bismuth using a mass spectrometer in conjunction with a Knudsen effusion cell. They were able to report dissociation energies, second and third law analyses, and the partial pressure of $Bi_4$ at several temperatures. They also observed $Bi_3$, but did not report any thermodynamic data for the species. These authors could not determine whether $Bi_3$ was a parent ion or the product of a fragmentation process.

Rovner, Drowart, and Drowart (1967) also studied bismuth vapor using mass spectrometry, and confirmed the presence of $Bi_3$. They reported values for the heats of sublimation and atomization of $Bi_3$ and $Bi_4$, as well as partial pressures of these species.

Further verification of the existence of $Bi_3$ is provided by Sullivan, Prusaczyk, and Carlson (1972) who also examined bismuth using mass spectrometry. Those authors reported second and third law analyses and the partial pressure of $Bi_3$ at several temperatures.

A fourth study by Wagner and Grimley (1974), confirmed the existence of both species $Bi_3$ and $Bi_4$. Again, mass spectrometry was used to analyze bismuth vapor over pure liquid bismuth. Ionization potentials were reported; however, no thermodynamic analysis was carried out.

All four studies indicated that the trimer and tetramer of bismuth are minor species. It has been suggested by Hultgren et al. (1973) that bismuth may form polymeric species such as $Bi_8$ but no molecules of that complexity were observed in any of the four studies.

### BISMUTH OXIDES

The species found in the Bi-O system are numerous. Researchers have reported finding the species $BiO$, $Bi_2O$, $Bi_2O_2$, $Bi_2O_3$, $Bi_3O_3$, $Bi_3O_4$, $Bi_4O_4$, $Bi_4O_6$, and $Bi_5O_7$. The Bi-O system is complicated by the large number of bismuth oxides and the inconsistencies among the few studies conducted to evaluate this system.

Uy and Drowart (1969) investigated the Bi-O system using mass spectrometry to determine the dissociation energy of $BiO$ in the presence of lead. They did not evaluate the entire Bi-O system. Ban and Knox (1970) used laser mass spectrometry to examine the vapor species of the Bi-O system. They observed the neutral species $Bi$, $Bi_3O_4$, and $Bi_5O_7$ over pure $Bi_2O_3$. The authors did not report any thermodynamic analysis of their data. Il'in (1976) determined the vapor pressure over $Bi_2O_3$ using Knudsen effusion techniques from 117 K to 627 K. The vapor was not analyzed to determine what bismuth oxide vapor species were present.

The study by Kazenas, Chizhikov, Tsvetkov, and Ol'shevskii (1972) again used mass spectrometry to study the Bi-O vapor system. The authors reported the following vapor species exist over pure $\text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$: $\text{Bi}$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{BiO}$, $\text{Bi}_2\text{O}_2$, $\text{Bi}_3\text{O}_3$, and $\text{Bi}_4\text{O}_4$. Other species were observed, but were explained as the result of ion fragmentation. Equations were reported for evaluating the vapor pressure of $\text{Bi}_4\text{O}_4$, $\text{Bi}$, and $\text{O}_2$ above pure $\text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$. No other thermodynamic analyses were reported.

Sidorov et al. (1980) also carried out a mass spectrometric study of the Bi-O vapor system over pure $\text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$. Those authors reported the species $\text{Bi}$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{BiO}$, $\text{Bi}_2\text{O}$, $\text{Bi}_2\text{O}_2$, $\text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{Bi}_3\text{O}_4$, and $\text{Bi}_4\text{O}_6$ as parent molecules. However, unlike Kazenas et al. (1972), they did not observe $\text{Bi}_4\text{O}_4$, or $\text{Bi}_3\text{O}_3$ as parent molecules. This discrepancy was not explained, nor did Sidorov and coworkers (1980) offer any definitive explanation for the formation of these ions and their appearance in their investigation. Sidorov et al. (1980) did not observe the presence of $\text{Bi}_3\text{O}_7$ as reported by Ban and Knox (1970). The latter investigators attribute the presence of $\text{Bi}_3\text{O}_7$ in the vapor to the laser technique used in their investigation.

The present authors consider the work by Sidorov et al. (1980) to be the most complete and authoritative study to date. They reviewed the literature and provided thermodynamic data for the oxides of bismuth. However, those authors tabulated their data in terms of the Gibbs energy function at various temperatures. With the advent of hand-held calculators it is often convenient to have data in terms of standard Gibbs energy equations expressed as a function of temperature. Sidorov's data have been reformed in terms of the standard Gibbs energy equations and extrapolated to 1600 K. This extrapolation was necessary to fill a critical need by metallurgists for high temperature data for bismuth oxides. Further studies will be needed to verify and/or improve the data at the higher temperatures.

### Data analysis

The standard state chosen for bismuth was that of pure liquid bismuth. This standard state was selected as the condition most frequently encountered in high temperature studies are satisfied. Table 1 contains thermodynamic data for bismuth at temperatures above and below the melting point of 544.59 K (Pankratz, 1982).

### BISMUTH VAPOR

The standard Gibbs energy, enthalpy, and entropy for vaporization of $\text{Bi}$, $\text{Bi}_2$, $\text{Bi}_3$ and $\text{Bi}_4$ from liquid bismuth have been evaluated. The data from Pankratz (1982) were used for $\text{Bi}$ and $\text{Bi}_2$. The data of Sullivan et al. (1972), and Rovner et al. (1967) were combined for the formation of $\text{Bi}_3$. The data of Kohl et al. (1967) and Rovner et al. (1967) were used for the formation of $\text{Bi}_4$. By analyzing their data, the standard Gibbs energies of vaporization of $\text{Bi}_3$ and $\text{Bi}_4$ were obtained. The results of the analysis are incluied in Table 1.

The molecular makeup of elemental bismuth vapor is shown in Figures 1 and 2. The partial pressures of $\text{Bi}$, $\text{Bi}_2$, $\text{Bi}_3$, and $\text{Bi}_4$ are plotted as a function of the source temperature in both figures. The data in Figure 1 refer to a saturated bismuth vapor at the source temperature heated to temperatures between 1373 K and 1573 K. This situation is similar to an inert carrier gas being saturated with bismuth vapor (at the source temperature) and then allowing the gas mixture, alone, to equilibrate at the higher temperature. In Figure 2 the diagram reflects the conditions of a sealed or static system where the total pressure is set by the saturated condition at the source temperature. Again the diagram reflects the composition of that vapor at temperatures between 1373 K and 1573 K.

### BISMUTH OXIDES

Sidorov et al. (1980) presented thermodynamic data for bismuth oxides in terms of the Gibbs energy function. By fitting a linear curve to the data for each oxide species from 800 K to 1200 K, the present authors extrapolated the data on each oxide species to 1600 K. This analysis for all bismuth oxide species yielded curves with correlation coefficients greater than 0.99.

### Discussion

Bismuth vapor produced from pure bismuth at a low temperature and then raised to a higher temperature com- prises primarily monomeric bismuth. The vapor pressure of diatomic bismuth is typically several orders of magnitude less than monomeric bismuth except at the higher source temperatures. The vapor pressures of the trimer and tetramer of bismuth were found to be virtually insignificant. Figures 1 and 2 illustrate the behavior of the species Bi, Bi₂, Bi₃, and Bi₄ under static and carrier gas conditions.

The presence of bismuth in a concentrate represents a serious problem in the smelting of copper. The values for temperature and the partial pressures of $O_2$ and Bi in Figures 3 and 4 are similar to those experienced in the production of copper. The results in those figures reveal that monomeric bismuth is the predominant vapor species in the Bi-O system at temperatures above 1400 K and at a value of $P_{Bi}$ equal to 1.01 kPa. Only at temperatures below 1400 K does Bi₂ become the predominate species. At $P_{Bi}$ = 1.01 Pa mono- meric bismuth is the predominant species at all temperatures and has a vapor pressure several orders of magnitude greater than any other molecule. The species Bi₃ and Bi₄ undergo a change in predominance when the partial pressure of bis- muth is lowered, with Bi₄ being predominant over Bi₃ at the higher partial pressure of bismuth.

The bismuth oxide species show differing predominance depending on the partial pressure of bismuth. At $P_{Bi}$ = 1.01 kPa, Bi₂O is the predominant vapor oxide species. The predominance of the other oxides varies with increasing temperature as shown in Figure 4. When the partial pressure of bismuth is lowered (see Figure 3), BiO becomes the predominant oxide vapor species. The vapor pressure of the oxides decrease with increasing molecular weight at $P_{Bi}$ equal to 1.01 Pa. This occurs at all temperatures evaluated.

## Summary

Thermodynamic data in the literature for the Bi and Bi-O vapor systems have been reviewed. The data have been used to develop equations for the standard Gibbs free energy of reaction between Bi vapor and $O_{2}$ for the formation of gaseous $BiO$, $Bi_{2}O$, $Bi_{2}O_{2}$, $Bi_{2}O_{3}$, $Bi_{3}O_{4}$, and $Bi_{4}O_{6}$. Similar equations were developed for gaseous $Bi_{2}$, $Bi_{3}$, and $Bi_{4}$. The data have been used to evaluate the molecularity of the Bi and Bi-O systems at temperatures experienced in the smelting of copper. The results indicate that bismuth oxides are relatively unstable.
