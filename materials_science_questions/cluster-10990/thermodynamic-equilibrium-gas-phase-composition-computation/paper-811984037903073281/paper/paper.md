# On the Chemical Transport
of Some Niobium Oxides with $TeCl_{4}$ (I).

## Calculation of the System Nb/O/Te/Cl
and the Transport of $NbO_{2}/NbO_{2.417}$

This series of two papers deals with the chemical transport of niobium oxides with $TeCl_{4}$. -
With chemical transport of $NbO_{2}$, using $TeCl_{4}$ as transport agent, $NbO_{2.417}$ is deposited
at first followed by $NbO_{2}$. The peculiarities of this transport are attributed to the oxidizing
action of the transport agent and to considerable reaction inhibitions in the starting solid.
Monocrystals of $NbO_{2}$ and $NbO_{2.417}$ are obtained without deposition of any other phases.
The morphology of the crystals is described. The transport behaviour in the system
Nb/O/Te/Cl is calculated. The calculated and experimental results agreee well.

Es wird über das Transportverhalten der Nioboxide beim chemischen Transport mit
$TeCl_{4}$ berichtet. - Beim chemischen Transport von $NbO_{2}$ mit $TeCl_{4}$ wird festgestellt, daß
zunächst $NbO_{2.417}$ abgeschieden wird und dann $NbO_{2}$. Die Transportbesonderheit wird
auf die Oxydationswirkung des Transportmediums und auf erhebliche Reaktionshemmun-

$NbO_{2,417}$-Kristallen wird gezeigt und die Morphologie der Kristalle beschrieben. Aus
Berechnungen zum Transportverhalten im System Nb/O/Te/Cl folgt, daß eine gute Über-
einstimmung zwischen dem experimentellen Verhalten und den rechnerischen Ergebnissen
besteht.

## 1. Introduction
A series of equilibrium phases $NbO_{x}$ exist at higher temperatures in the system Nb-O.
Some of these have been already prepared by transport with halogens -- SCHÄFER;
SCHÄFER et al. 1962, 1964, 1966a, b, GRUEHN; GRUEHN et al. 1964, 1967, LAVES et al.
1964a, EMMENEGGER et al.; SAKATA et al.; MERTIN et al.; TOROPOV et al.. Next to
these there exist for the niobium pentoxide phase several modifications arising in
dependency on the preparation and deposition conditions -- GRUEHN et al. 1964, 1966;
SCHÄFER et al. 1964, 1966; MERTIN et al.; LAVES et al. 1964b. The diversity of pheno-
mena is increased still further by the fact that the greater part of the phases have
a range of homogeneity -- NORIN, MAGNELI; SCHÄFER et al. (1969); MARUCCO et al.

In studying the chemical transport of the system V-O it was possible with $TeCl_{4}$
to produce all phases -- with the exception of vanadium monoxide -- as single-phase
and monocrystalline with all their phase boundaries (OPPERMANN et al. 1975a, b, c,
1977a, b; REICHELT et al.; WOLF et al. 1976). $TeCl_{4}$ has also proved to be well suited
for the transport of iron oxide phases (GERLACH et al. 1977a, b), the uranium oxides
(OPPERMANN 1977), and the tungsten oxides (OPPERMANN et al.). We subsequently
endeavoured to produce the different phases of the system Nb-O with $TeCl_{4}$.

From the aspect of thermodynamics, such as oxygen decomposition pressure of the
co-existing solid phases, the enthalpies of formation and entropies of the chlorides

69•

and oxychlorides, good preconditions exist so that a comparison between the calculated and experimental transport behaviour appeared to be promising.

The first part reports on the calculation of the system and the transport of $NbO_2$ and $Nb_{2.417}$. The second part represents the investigation results gained during the transport of some other Nb-O phases.

## 2. Calculation of the gaseous phase composition in the system Nb-0-Te-Cl

### 2.1. Thermodynamic starting conditions

The formation of the following compounds in the gaseous phase must be expected in the system Nb-O-Te-Cl via a solid of niobium oxide ($NbO_x$) within the temperature range of 1000-1500 K: $NbOCl_3$, $NbCl_4$, $NbCl_5$, $TeOCl_2$, $TeCl_2$, $TeCl_4$, $TeO_2$, $TeO$, $Te_2$, $Te$, $Cl_2$, $Cl$ and $O_2$.

The system Nb-O-Te-Cl for each solid phase has four degrees of freedom associated to the temperature $(T)$, the total pressure $(p_g)$, the oxygen partial pressure $(p_{O_2})$ and to the ratio $p_{Cl}^*/p_{Te}^* = 4$. It follows that the 14 species to be considered can be defined by 10 independent equilibria of reaction with their equilibrium constants $(K_1-K_{10})$:

$$
\begin{align}
\text{(1)} \quad 2\ \mathrm{NbO}_{x,f} + 3\ \mathrm{Cl}_{2} &= 2\ \mathrm{NbOCl}_{3} + \left(\frac{2x}{2} - 1\right)\mathrm{O}_{2} \quad \mathrm{K(1)} \\
\text{(2)} \quad \mathrm{NbO}_{x,f} + 2\ \mathrm{Cl}_{2} &= \mathrm{NbCl}_{4} + \frac{x}{2}\ \mathrm{O}_{2} \quad \mathrm{K(2)} \\
\text{(3)} \quad \mathrm{NbCl}_{4} + 0.5\ \mathrm{Cl}_{2} &= \mathrm{NbCl}_{5} \quad \mathrm{K(3)} \\
\text{(4)} \quad \mathrm{TeCl}_{4} &= \mathrm{TeCl}_{2} + \mathrm{Cl}_{2} \quad \mathrm{K(4)} \\
\text{(5)} \quad \mathrm{TeCl}_{2} + 0.5\ \mathrm{O}_{2} &= \mathrm{TeOCl}_{2} \quad \mathrm{K(5)} \\
\text{(6)} \quad \mathrm{TeCl}_{2} + \mathrm{O}_{2} &= \mathrm{TeO}_{2} + \mathrm{Cl}_{2} \quad \mathrm{K(6)} \\
\text{(7)} \quad \mathrm{TeO}_{2} &= 0.5\ \mathrm{Te}_{2} + \mathrm{O}_{2} \quad \mathrm{K(7)} \\
\text{(8)} \quad \mathrm{TeO} &= 0.5\ \mathrm{Te}_{2} + 0.5\ \mathrm{O}_{2} \quad \mathrm{K(8)} \\
\text{(9)} \quad \mathrm{Te}_{2} &= 2\ \mathrm{Te} \quad \mathrm{K(9)} \\
\text{(10)} \quad \mathrm{Cl}_{2} &= 2\ \mathrm{Cl} \quad \mathrm{K(10)}
\end{align}
$$

The partial pressures of the 13 gaseous phase species can be calculated with $K_1$ to $K_{10}$, as well as with $p_g = \sum p_i$, the balance pressure ratio $p_{Cl}^*/p_{Te}^* = 4$, and the oxygen partial pressure $p_{O_2}$ at a given temperature $T$. The temperature dependency of the equilibrium constants is calculated according to:

$$
\lg Kp = \frac{\Delta S_{R,1000}^{0}}{4.574} - \frac{\Delta H_{R,1000}^{0}}{4.574T}.
$$

The reaction enthalpy $\Delta H_{R}^{0}$ and the reaction entropy $\Delta S_{R}^{0}$ are obtained from the values listed in Table 1.

In the presence of two solid phases the system is characterized by $T$, $p_g$ and $p_{Cl}^*/p_{Te}^*$.

For the decomposition ranges
$$
\mathrm{NbO}_{x_1,l} = \mathrm{NbO}_{x_2,u} + \frac{x_1, l - x_2, u}{2}\ \mathrm{O}_2
$$
the following is applicable:
$$
\begin{align*}
\log p_{\mathrm{O}_2} &= 10.1624 - 38951/T & &\text{for } \mathrm{NbO_2/NbO} \\
\log p_{\mathrm{O}_2} &= 11.5965 - 37452/T & &\text{for } \mathrm{NbO_{2.417}/NbO_{2.0}} \\
\log p_{\mathrm{O}_2} &= 14.0667 - 36929/T & &\text{for } \mathrm{NbO_{2.5}/NbO_{2.483}}.
\end{align*}
$$

### 3. Transport of $NbO_2$ and $NbO_{2.417}$

#### 3.1. General remarks on transport behaviour

When phase-pure $NbO_2$ is sealed with $TeCl_4$ in an ampoule and transported in the temperature gradient $T_2/T_1$, then isometric $NbO_2$ crystals and needle-shaped $NbO_{2.417}(Nb_{12}O_{29})$ crystals are deposited next to each other at temperatures of $T_2$: $1100\ ^\circ$C and $T_1$: $900\ ^\circ$C and at a $TeCl_4$ initial partial pressure ($p^0_{\text{TeCl}_4}$) of about 1 atm. The general conditions for chemical transport and for the preparation of the starting compounds have been already announced (OPPERMANN, RITSCHEL). The two crystallizing phases are always present in separate growth rooms in the transport ampoule. This conforms with the information furnished by SAKATA et al. and KODAMA, GOTO. The $NbO_2$ crystals are always located between the $NbO_{2.417}$ growth room and the starting room -- see Figure 3. From this it could be deduced that $NbO_{2.417}$ crystals are the first to be deposited during transport, followed by $NbO_2$ crystals. A further conformity of results from SAKATA et al. and those gained from our own studies exists in the formation of small metallic tellurium spheres in the diffusion and starting room after the ampoule had cooled off. As has been shown by the above calculations of the gaseous phase composition the appearance of metallic tellurium can be expected at $p^0_{\text{TeCl}_4} \approx 3$ atm.

#### 3.2. On the deposition of $NbO_{2.417}$ next to $NbO_2$

The above transport behaviour could be understood thermodynamically as follows:

The $TeCl_4$ has an oxidizing effect on the solid phase. The oxygen coexistence pressure is very low. A niobium oxide phase richer in oxygen is formed at the surface of the solid phase. The establishment of equilibrium of the solid does not proceed with sufficient speed.

In consequence of this two phases are present at the surface of the solid, namely $Nb_{12}O_{29}$ next to $NbO_2$, although under certain conditions the liberated quantity of oxygen is insufficient to oxidize the starting solid, $NbO_2$, beyond its upper phase boundary composition $NbO_{2.003}$ at $1000\ ^\circ$C (MARUCCO et al.), and $NbO_{2.024}$ at $1300\ ^\circ$C (SCHÄFER et al. 1969). As the calculations have shown the phase richer in oxygen is the first to be transported off from the 2-phase mixture. When the state of equilibrium $NbO_{2.417} + NbO_2 = NbO_{2.x}$ has been reached in the solid the transport and deposition of the dioxide poorer in oxygen commences under simultaneous resolution of part of the $NbO_{2.417}$ phase on the $T_1$ side.

As has been shown by others, a precise description of transport is only possible through exhaustive thermodynamic analysis of the entire system (KRABBES et al. 1975 1976, 1977, OPPERMANN et al. 1977a, b, GERLACH et al. 1977a, b, c). The following explanations will support the courses of reaction described above.

The transport behaviour of $NbO_{2.0}$ is incompatible with the transport mechanism assumed in literature, namely transport via $NbCl_4 + TeO_2$ (SAKATA et al., OPPERMANN, RITSCHEL), and the appearance of metallic tellurium inexplicable. The process of transport only becomes understandable, if it is assumed that transport takes place

on a pronounced scale via the niobium oxychloride $(NbOCl_{3})$, as has been already assumed (OPPERMANN et al. 1975c, OPPERMANN, RITSCHEL). The $NbOCl_{3}$ arises by oxidation of the solid by $TeCl_{4}$. If, therefore, a certain quantity of $NbOCl_{3}$ is intro duced in the ampoule next to $TeCl_{4}$, then the process of oxidization is repressed with the result that the ratio $NbO_{2}$ to $NbO_{2.417}$ in the deposition room is shifted in favour

Experiments, which follow the deposited quantity of $NbO_{2.417}$ depending on $p_{TeCl_{4}}^{0}$ at $T_{1}$, show that the deposited $NbO_{2.417}$ crystal quantity is proportional to the $TeCl_{4}$ initial quantity, thereby once again demonstrating the oxidizing action of the transport agent - see Table 3.

Experiments involving the variation of time at otherwise constant transport conditions have confirmed that the equilibrium solid-solid is reached with pro- gressing transport time. The results summarized in Table 4 indicate that the quantity of $NbO_{2.417}$ crystals declines over the quantity of $NbO_{2}$ monocrystals at increasing transport time. At the same time changes of the morphology can be observed - see Table 4. At long durations $t$ (> 200 h) plate-like prismatic formations arise which,as has been shown by studies, were formed by reversion and resolution of $NbO_{2.417}$  needles in the growth room.

### 3.3. Single-phase deposition
The production of phase-pure $NbO_{2}$ monocrystals (without deposition of $NbO_{2.417}$ ) becomes possible according to the previous explanations by waiting until the state of equilibrium in the solid is reached, the quantity of transport agent is reduced, and by repressing the formation of the second-phase $NbO_{2.417}$ by addition of $NbOCl_{3}$. An increase of the $NbO_{2}$ initial powder quantity acts in this direction.

The black isometric $NbO_{2}$ crystals - see Figure 4 - have a metallic lustre anda size up to $2 ×1.5 ×1.5 ~mm$ . The bipyramidal crystal habit is formed by $\{111\}$  and $\{101\}$ pyramidal faces. The preferred growth direction is [001]. Distorted bi pyramids as well as parallel intergrowth can be frequently observed.

The deposition of phase-pure $NbO_{2.417}$ crystals is always successful, if one proceeds from a 2-phase mixture $NbO_{x}$ with $2.02<x<2.415$ , i.e. the experiment must be terminated before $x<2.02$ is reached in the starting room. The crystal needles are usually hollow and have a fibrous layer structure which is responsible for the striated crystal faces.
