# STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF $SiO_2$ NANOPOWDER
OBTAINED FROM HIGH-SILICA RAW MATERIALS BY PLASMA
METHOD

P. V. Kosmachev, $^{1}$ V. A. Vlasov, $^{1,2}$ and N. K. Skripnikova $^{1}$

UDC 533.924

The paper presents a plasma-assisted generation of nanodisperse powder obtained from diatomite, a natural high-silica material. The structure and properties of the obtained material are investigated using the transmission electron microscopy, energy dispersive X-Ray spectroscopy, infrared and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopies, and Brunauer-Emmett-Teller method. It is clearly shown that the obtained $SiO_2$ nanoparticles are spherical, polydisperse and represented in the form of agglomerates. The specific surface of this nanopowder is $32\ m^2/g$. Thermodynamic modeling of the plasma-assisted process is used to obtain the equilibrium compositions of condensed and gaseous reaction products. The plasma process is performed within the 300–5000 K temperature range.

Keywords: plasma method, sublimation, $SiO_2$ nanopowder, equilibrium composition, thermodynamic modeling.

## INTRODUCTION

One of the most relevant trends in materials science is the production of nanodisperse materials since their properties considerably differ from that of the same materials without nanoparticles, though with similar qualitative and quantitative compositions. Silica ($SiO_2$) nanopowders are widely used in different industries and sciences in the capacity of additives to lacquer [1], antifriction [2] and hydrophobic coatings [3]. Silica-based nano-additives have become widespread in the construction field as additives to concrete and dry mixes, heat-resistant and heat-insulating materials [4, 5].

The possibility of producing oxide nanopowders has been well-known for many years. Such production techniques as mechanochemical [6], sol gel [7], electron-beam [8, 9], pyrolysis [10] and many others are widely available in industry. Among these production techniques, the plasma method has several advantages. The main benefit of this technique is an absence of temperature restrictions which are common to all other techniques. This method intensifies physicochemical processes and provides products with the appropriate chemical composition, aggregate state and size. Very high reaction rates guarantee high productivity of plasma reactors [11].

In some cases, it is possible to avoid the majority of drawbacks inherent to all other techniques. Thus, for example, the gaseous plasma of low pressure arc discharges [12] used for synthesis of nanomaterials ($TiO_2$, ZnO, $Cu_2O$), does not contaminate the nanopowder and provides a narrow grain-size distribution (less than 10 nm) due to high temperature in condensing zone. High performance plasma reactors are capable of direct plasma-chemical synthesis due to a high plasma ionization and produce nanopowders from complex alloys. The synthesized particles can be implanted in any matrix, that allows creating nanocmposite materials directly in a plasma reactor [13].

Nell et al. [14], have demonstrated a pilot-scale production of SiO₂ nanopowders using a plasma torch. SiCl₄ and SiF₄ raw materials were used to obtain SiO₂ nanopowder. The average particle size was 60 nm and 20 nm for SiCl₄ and SiF₄, respectively.

The main objective of this work is to investigate the structure and properties of nanodisperse powder produced from high-silica raw material (diatomite) using the electric arc plasma method. The paper demonstrates a thermodynamic modeling of equilibrium compositions determined for condensed and gaseous reaction products.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

In this experiment, diatomite was used as a raw material from Kamyshlovskoe field, Sverdlovsk, Russia. Its chemical composition presented in Table 1, includes mostly silica (80.4 wt.%), aluminum oxide (7.27 wt.%), iron oxide (4.41 wt.%) and a low content of calcium, magnesium, potassium, and sodium oxides.

The silica nanopowder is produced in the experimental plasma reactor, whose mechanism is described in the work of Kosmachev et al. [15]. The plasma process is based on sublimation of the raw material at (3–5)·10³ K which then releases from the gaseous phase in the form of a nanodisperse powder.

The size distribution, morphology, and elemental composition of obtained nanopowder were investigated by the transmission electron microscopy (TEM), energy dispersive X-Ray spectroscopy (EDX), infrared (IR) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopies (XPS), and Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) method.

A detailed structural analysis of the nanopowder was carried out on 120 keV transmission electron microscope Philips CM12 (Netherlands). At first, the powder sample was dispersed in spirit in an ultrasound bath. The dispersion was then sprayed on a copper grid covered with an amorphous carbon film and then dried. Based on the obtained photomicrographs, the diagram was constructed for the grain-size distribution of not less than 1000 nanoparticles by means of Olympus software suite (Japan) for TEM imaging.

The energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer Quantax 400 from the Bruker Optics (Berlin, Germany) with the energy resolution of 15 keV was used to analyze the chemical composition of the powder sample preliminary coated with carbon.

The qualitative and quantitative elemental composition of obtained powder samples was detected by the IR spectroscopy and the XPS technique. IR spectra within the wave range of 600–4000 cm⁻¹ were recorded on the Bruker Optics TENSOR 27 Fourier-transform spectrometer (Berlin, Germany). The Kratos AXIS Ultra DLD spectrophotometer (Great Britain) was used for optical measurements.

A BET surface area analysis of the nanopowder was performed on the Quantachrome Instruments NOVA 2200 surface area analyzer (USA) [16].

A thermodynamic modeling of the plasma-assisted processes for silica generation was provided by the TERRA computer program intended for the analysis of multi-component heterogeneous systems [17]. The TERRA is based on the maximum entropy principle for isolated thermodynamic systems in equilibrium state and has its own database on thermodynamic properties of 3500 individual substances the modeling of which is possible within 300–6000 K temperature range. The initial values for the plasma-chemical process model were set to be 300–5000 K temperature range and 0.1 MPa air pressure. In plasma reactor, the raw material content was 10%, while air occupied 90%.

Based on statistical data on measured particle diameters, the diagram can be constructed for the grain-size distribution as presented in Fig. 2. As illustrated on this diagram, the size distribution of nanoparticles ranges between 10–300 nm. The grain size less than 100 nm prevails.

The EDX spectrometry result shown in Fig. 3, indicates that the obtained silica nanopowder contains an insignificant amount of Fe, Na and K impurities which were observed in the raw material, and a small amount of C due to presence of graphite anode of the plasma reactor.

The XPS results of the quantitative analysis are summarized in Table 2.

As can be seen from Table 2, the elemental composition matches the results of the EDX spectrometry. It is shown that the amount of Fe, Na and K impurities in the nanopowder is less than 1% for each. Other elements are not observed. We can suppose that they remain in the melt of the raw material. However, further research is desirable to extend our knowledge of this issue.

IR spectra of the obtained nanopowder are shown in Fig. 4. Absorption bands with frequency range of 980–1250 cm⁻¹ describe Si–O–Si bond stretches, whereas 750–850 cm⁻¹ frequencies describe SiO₄ tetrahedrons.

The BET method used for the measurement of the material specific surface area, shows that it equals 32 m²/g, that is very common to the grain-size distribution mentioned above.

![](./images/813130944356548609_5.jpg)

Fig. 5. Thermodynamic modeling of equilibrium states: $a$ – condensed (c), $b$ – gaseous reaction products.

The calculation results for the equilibrium composition represent a matrix of the main state parameters of thermodynamic system and the content of components corresponding to one of the equilibrium states given by the temperature and pressure. The dependences are then obtained for the weight content of appeared condensed and gaseous reaction products within the temperature range of 300–5000 K. The results of the equilibrium state thermodynamic modeling are presented in Fig. 5.

According to the results of thermodynamic modeling, during the plasma-assisted generation of $SiO_2$ nanopowder the formation of the following reaction products is observed: condensed $SiO_2$, $Al_2O_3$, $Fe_2O_3$, $Fe_3O_4$ and gaseous $O$, $O_2$, $N_2$, NO, $SiO_2$, $Al_2O_3$, AlO, $FeO_2$. As one can see from Fig. 5, silica transfers to a gaseous state within the temperature range of 2750–3400 K, $Al_2O_3$ being condensed. Thus, in order to avoid $Al_2O_3$ transfer to the composition of $SiO_2$ nanopowder, it is necessary to provide a sublimation process within this temperature range.

## CONCLUSIONS

Based on the results, it can be concluded that the plasma method allowed the production of silica nanopowder from diatomite, a natural high-silica raw material. It was found that $SiO_2$ nanoparticles were spherical and represented in the form of agglomerates. The specific surface area of the obtained nanopowder was $32\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$. A small amount of impurities was detected in the end product that was stipulated by their presence in the raw material. Produced $SiO_2$ nanoparticles were polydisperse due to their size distribution ranging from 10 to 300 nm. This can have a positive effect on the improvement of mechanical properties of construction materials.

As a result of thermodynamic modeling, the equilibrium states are determined for condensed $SiO_2$, $Al_2O_3$, $Fe_2O_3$, $Fe_3O_4$ and gaseous $O$, $O_2$, $N_2$, NO, $SiO_2$, $Al_2O_3$, AlO, $FeO_2$ reaction products, thermodynamic modeling of which was performed at temperature of up to 5000 K. In conclusion, it was stated that the sublimation process within 2750–3400 K temperature range was required in order to avoid $Al_2O_3$ transfer to $SiO_2$ nanopowder composition.
