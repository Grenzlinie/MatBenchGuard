# MASS-SPECTROMETRIC STUDY OF THE VAPORIZATION OF $\boldsymbol{Li_2O(s)}$ AND THERMOCHEMISTRY OF GASEOUS $\boldsymbol{LiO, Li_2O, Li_3O}$, AND $\boldsymbol{Li_2O_2}$

H. KUDO *, C.H. WU and H.R. IHLE

Institut für Chemie der KFA Jülich GmbH, Institut 1: Nuklearchemie, D-5170 Jülich, Fed. Rep. Germany

Received 26 April 1978

The equilibrium vapor pressures over solid lithium oxide ($Li_2O$) have been measured mass-spectrometrically using platinum Knudsen cells in the temperature range 1300-1700 K. From the gaseous equilibria the heats of formation of LiO(g), $Li_2O(g)$, $Li_3O(g)$, and $Li_2O_2(g)$ were determined as follows: $\Delta Hf(LiO) = 8.3 \pm 3.3$ kcal/mol, $\Delta Hf(Li_2O) = -42.3 \pm 1.1$ kcal/mol, $\Delta Hf(Li_3O) = -48.4 \pm 5.6$ kcal/mol, and $\Delta Hf(Li_2O_2) = -71.5 \pm 7.4$ kcal/mol. The atomization energies are $D\theta(LiO) = 89.2 \pm 3.3$ kcal/mol, $D\theta(Li_2O) = 178.3 \pm 1.1$ kcal/mol, $D\theta(Li_3O) = 222.9 \pm 5.6$ kcal/mol, and $D\theta(Li_2O_2) = 266.5 \pm 7.4$ kcal/mol. The relation $D\theta(LiO) = D\theta(Li-OLi)$ is derived here. The bond dissociation energy $D\theta(Li-Li_2O)$ is $44.6 \pm 5.5$ kcal/mol.

In addition, the vaporization of $Li_2O$ has been studied by using molybdenum, tantalum and graphite Knudsen cells in the temperature range 1200-1500 K to measure the distribution of the partial pressures under these conditions. It was found, for example, that due to the reaction of $Li_2O$ with the wall of a molybdenum cell, their partial pressure of $Li_3O$, which was detected as a stable molecule for the first time, was increased by about a factor of 10 compared with its pressure measured in a platinum cell.

## 1. Introduction

Although a number of references to the vapor pressures and thermochemical properties of lithium oxide ($Li_2O$) have been reported [1-6], substantial discrepancies among experimental results exist.

The present study of the equilibrium vapor pressure over $Li_2O(s)$ has been carried out in order to obtain more consistent thermochemical data. The partial pressures of $Li, LiO, Li_2O, Li_3O$ and $Li_2O_2$ measured in platinum cells in the temperature range 1300-1700 K are reported in this article. The heats of formation and atomization energies of these gaseous molecules are evaluated from the experimental results.

Molybdenum and graphite are proposed as constructional materials to contain the blankets of a controlled thermonuclear reactor (CTR) in which $Li_2O(s)$ is used as a tritium breeder [7]. Therefore it appeared of interest to investigate the distribution of partial pressures over $Li_2O(s)$ with molybdenum and graphite Knudsen cells because some reactions of $Li_2O$ with these materials are expected to take place. For comparison, tantalum Knudsen cells have also been used for the measurements, since the relative rates of oxidation of refractory metals in the air are reported to be in the order of $Mo > W > Nb > Ta$ [8].

## 2. Experimental

The ultra-high vacuum apparatus used in this study has been described in detail earlier [9,10]. The effusate from a Knudsen cell was analyzed by means of a quadrupole mass filter (Quad 250B, EAI, Palo Alto, California, USA). A shutter was installed between the Knudsen cell and the ion source of the mass spectrometer. In the beam source region a vacuum better than $10^{-9}$ Torr was maintained during the measurements.

Identification of the ions was accomplished from their mass-to-charge ratio, shutterability, appearance potential and isotope abundance.

Knudsen cells made of platinum, molybdenum, tantalum, and graphite were used in these experiments. The dimensions of all Knudsen cells, except for the platinum cell, were: inside diameter 4.5 mm, length 19 mm and orifice diameter 0.6 mm at room temperature. Those of a platinum cell were respectively 8.8, 12 and 0.7 mm. The cells were degassed at $800^{\circ}C$ for 6 h under a pressure $<1\times10^{-8}$ Torr before use.

The temperature of the cells was measured with an optical pyrometer (Pyrowerk, Hannover, Germany) and a Pt, Rh/Pt thermocouple, calibrated at the triple points of lithium, silver and gold.

Crystalline $Li_2O$, a commercial product from Research Organic/Inorganic Chemicals Corp., Sun Valley, California, USA, of 99% purity, was used after a heat treatment in which the sample, placed in a platinum boat, was heated to $700^{\circ}C$ for 8 h under a pressure $<2\times10^{-8}$ Torr.

By means of this treatment most of the $H_2O$ and $CO_2$ were removed from the sample. The loading of samples and transfer of materials were accomplished in a dry box with an argon atmosphere.

## 3. Results and discussion

The partial pressure $p_i$ of species i was determined from the relation $p_i = k(I_iT/\sigma_i\beta_i\gamma_i)$, where $k$ is the pressure calibration constant, $I_i$ the ion intensity, $\sigma_i$ the relative ionization cross-section, $\beta_i$ the isotope abundance and $\gamma_i$ the multiplier gain. The pressure calibration constant $k$ was obtained from the relation $K_p = p_{Li}^2/p_{Li2}$ by measuring $I(Li^+)$ and $I(Li_2^+)$ at various temperatures and calculating the equilibrium constant $K_p$ for the reaction $Li_2(g)\rightarrow2Li(g)$ [10].

The multiplier gain was measured as $1.25\times10^5$ and $1.20\times10^5$ for Li and $Li_2$. The multiplier gain for LiO, $Li_2O$, $Li_3O$ and $Li_2O_2$ was assumed to be identical to that of Li. Atomic ionization cross-sections were taken from Mann [11] and the molecular ionization cross-section was calculated by taking the sum of Mann's atomic cross-sections [12]. The isotopic abundance of $^7Li$ in the sample was measured as $92.6\pm0.8\%$. The natural composition of oxygen isotopes was used for the evaluation of partial pressures $p_i$: $\beta(^{16}O)=99.8\%$ [13].

The ion intensities were measured at an energy of the ionizing electrons 3.5-3.6 eV above the ionization potentials of each species, i.e. I.P. $(Li^+)=5.392$ eV [14], I.P. $(Li_2^+)=4.86$ eV [10], I.P. $(LiO^+)=8.96$ eV [15], I.P. $(Li_2O^+)=6.41$ eV [15], I.P. $(Li_3O^+)=4.54$ eV [15] and I. P. $(Li_2O_2^+)=7.88$ eV [15]. For example, the ionization efficiency curves for $Li^+$, $Li_2O^+$, and $Li_3O^+$ measured in this work are shown in fig. 1.

Our present results on $p_{\text{Li}_2\text{O}}$ measured with platinum cells are in reasonable agreement with those of Hildebrand et al.[3] and White et al. [4] whereas Berkowitz et al. Our measured value of $p_{\text{Li}}/p_{\text{Li}_2\text{O}}$ (=1.9 at 1500 K) was approximately equal to that measured by Neubert [16] using a magnet sector type of mass spectrometer. This shows that the correction to the measured partial pressures due to mass discrimination of the quadrupole mass filter could be neglected, although the uncertainties in the measured pressures ($\Delta p_{\text{i}}/p_{\text{i}}$) were estimated to be about ±50% for the Li pressure and ±100% for the pressures of the other species.

### 3.4. Reactions of $Li_{2} O$ with molybdenum

In the following, additional information is given about the reactions of $Li_{2} O$ with molybdenum obtained during the measurements of the vapor pressures over $Li_{2} O(s)$ in molybdenum Knudsen cells.

a) After prolonged measurements of steady state effusion with stepwise increase of the temperature up to $1480 ~K$ , the partial pressures of $Li_{2} O$ and $Li_{3} O$  began to decrease and finally the mass signals of both species became too small to be detected at tempera- tures above $1550 ~K$ . In addition, the mass patterns due to $Li_{2} MoO_{4}^{+}$ were observed in the mass spectra at tem peratures above $1500 ~K$ , as have been observed in earlier work $[20,21]$ .

b) Solid substances were left inside the molybdenum cell used for the measurements, and the product wasfound to be a mixture of Mo metal, $Li_{2} O(s)$ , and $\alpha$  Li4MoOs(s) by X-ray diffraction analysis.

The latter substance is known to change its crystalline polymorphic structures as follows: $\alpha \stackrel{450^{\circ} C}{\to }$  $\beta \stackrel{680^{\circ} C}{\to } \gamma \stackrel{840^{\circ} C}{\to } \delta$ and to liberate $Li_{2} MoO_{4}(~g)$ at high temperatures by thermal decomposition [22]. This explains the presence of $Li_{2} MoO_{4}$ molecules in the vapor, and suggests that reactions which yield $Li_{2} MoO_{4}(~g)$ take place in the temperature range of our measurements.

The present results of our experiments could be then tentatively explained assuming the followingreactions:
$$\mathrm{Li}_{2} \mathrm{O}(\mathrm{s}) \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{Li}(\mathrm{g})+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}),\qquad(22)$$

$$\mathrm{Mo}(\mathrm{s})+4 \mathrm{Li}(\mathrm{g})+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{MoO}_{5}(\mathrm{~s}),\qquad(23)$$

$$\mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{MoO}_{5}(\mathrm{~s}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Li}_{2} \mathrm{MoO}_{4}(\mathrm{~g})+\mathrm{Li}_{2} \mathrm{O}(\mathrm{g}).\qquad(24)$$
