# CBN-TiH₂ composites: chemical equilibria, microstructure and mechanical studies

## Ewa Benko

Institute of Metal Cutting, 37A Wroclawska Str., 30-011 Cracov, Poland

Received 21 November 1996 accepted 22 April 1997

## Abstract
This paper summarizes theoretical and experimental studies of $cBN-TiH_{2}$ composites with $cBN: TiH_{2}$ molar ratio equal to 9:1.
From theoretical calculations it follows that $TiH_{2}$ reacts with $BN$ in the full range of pressure and temperature to form of two new phases: $TiB_{2}$ and TiN, the amounts of which depend on the conditions of synthesis. Experimentally $cBN-TiH_{2}$ composites were prepared by high pressure hot pressing, and the samples were subsequently heat treated.

Samples before and after heat treatment were characterized using transmission electron microscopy. The pristine samples exhibit dense polycrystalline structure, and at the BN-binder interface a thin layer of fine $TiB_{2}$ and TiN crystallites are visible. After heat treatment TiN crystallites grow and form a layer of equiaxial grains of 0.5-2 $\mu m$ diameter. Columnar $TiB_{2}$ of 0.1-0.2 $\mu m$ diameter form as a thin layer between the TiN and BN crystallites.

Heat treatment significantly increases the hardness and Young's modulus of the composites. © 1997 Elsevier Science S.A.

**Keywords:** Cubic boron nitride; Composites; Microstructure; Young's modulus

## 1. Introduction
Polycrystalline materials produced from cubic boron nitride are widely used in machining due to their unique properties, such as high resistance to oxidation, good thermal conductivity, and chemical inertness to iron and iron alloys [1]. These materials are synthesized in special reactors by high-pressure and high-temperature treatment. The following two procedures are usually applied:
- direct transformation of the hexagonal modification into the cubic one;
- cubic boron nitride sintering with the activation of the sintering process.

The latter procedure is more frequently used. Metals of groups IV, V and VI in the periodic table and/or other metallic elements such as aluminium, cobalt and nickel are added to activate the sintering. Chemical reactions between the activators and boron nitride occur resulting in the formation of some new phases. The prediction of the final products of the reactions taking place during the sintering as well as the elucidation of this mechanism is of crucial importance in the selection of the appropriate binding phase.

In designing the ceramic cutting tools of high hardness, the selection of material should be made very carefully. Strength is an important property of such materials.

On the other hand the knowledge of reaction mechanism at the BN-metal interface seems to be crucial in the process of obtaining sinters with significant strength. Titanium and its compounds are most commonly used as binders in sintering technology [2-10].

## 2. Calculations of phase equilibrium for the $9BN-1TiH_{2}$ system
The calculations of phase equilibria for the $BN-TiH_{2}$ system were carried out using the VCS (Villars, Cruise, Smith) algorithm [11] i.e. an algorithm belonging to so called stoichiometric algorithms.

In this procedure, the Gibbs energy of the whole reaction mixture is minimized subject to side conditions of elemental abundance and non-reactivity of the mole numbers. Reaction-extent variables derived from the transformation of the mass balance equations are the unknowns of the function being minimized.

In the calculation of chemical equilibria of the system investigated and related ones it is not known a priori which phases and components will appear in the equilibrium state (for given $T,p$ and initial feed composition). The advantage of the VCS algorithm relies on the fact that only species which may, but do not need to appear at equilibrium must be specified. Therefore, the program written on the basis of the VCS algorithm requires only the knowledge of the species which can occur at equilibrium together with their standard Gibbs energies of formation. In addition, the calculations require the knowledge of the exact number of moles of elements introduced to the system and the values of $p$ and $T$ as well as the initial estimate. It should be noted here that the user of the program does not need to specify the number and form of independent reactions possibly occurring in the system under consideration. The VCS algorithm is one of the most powerful tools for the calculation of chemical equilibria, especially for systems with a large number of condensed phases.

## 3. Experimental procedure

Boron nitride (ABN-300, De Beers, 3-5 $\mu$m) and $TiH_{2}$ in molar ratio 9:1 (Fluka AG, Buchs SG, 3-5 $\mu$m grains size) were mechanically mixed in ethyl alcohol and then pressed into pellets of 6 mm diameter using a pressure of $1 \times 10^{7}$ Pa and the temperature of $1750\ ^{\circ}C$ for 3 min. The sintered material was thermally treated at $950\ ^{\circ}C$ for 1 h in a vacuum of $5 \times 10^{-3}$ Pa.

### 3.1. Microstructure of $cBN-TiH_{2}$ composites

Thin foils from samples before and after thermal treatment were prepared by dimpling and ion drilling and under $5\ \text{kV}\ \text{mA}^{-1}$. The microstructure observations were performed in a Philips CM20TWIN (200 kV) transmission microscope.

### 3.2. The strength investigations of $9cBN-1TiH_{2}$ composites

As a result of high pressure annealing of $cBN-TiH_{2}$ composites, only rather small samples were obtained.

Therefore, the following measurements were chosen for material strength characterisation:
- hardness measurements;
- modulus of elasticity measurements.

#### 3.2.1. Determination of hardness

For extra hard materials tests the indenter, which gives the minimum deformation around indentation, is required. Furthermore, the indentation diameter should be much greater than grain size in the investigated material.

The Knoop's indenter fulfils the above requirements and also has a longer life when compared with the other types [13-15].

Before hardness measurements were taken samples were polished with diamond paste with diminishing granulation until 11-12 class of surface quality was obtained. The polishing was performed by means of Struers automatic grinding-polishing machine.

During the hardness measurements by Knoop's method an indentation load of 9.81 N was used.

#### 3.2.2. Determination of elasticity modulus

Ultrasonic examination was the basic method employed for determination of the material elasticity constants.

For extra hard materials which can be treated as isotropic, Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio are the constants of interest.

The above constants are determined using ultrasonics by measuring the longitudinal ultrasonic wave velocity.

The measurements were carried out by means of broad-band ultrasonic heads, generating short duration pulses, together with a laboratory ultrasonic defecto-scope UNIPAN S12 and a digital oscilloscope, which enables precise detection of intervals between ultra-sonic pulses.

The longitudinal wave velocity was calculated as a quotient of the sample width and the time of ultrasonic

Thus it can be stated that thermal annealing of BN sintered with $TiH_2$ accompanied by formation of the new phase (TiN, $TiB_2$) results in the increase in mechanical strength of the material.

## 5. Conclusion

These investigations confirmed formation of both $TiB_2$ and TiN within the $cBN-TiH_2$ sintered composite as was predicted by thermodynamic calculations (using the VCS algorithm). Both $TiB_2$ and TiN compounds were found to be present just after sintering. After additional heat treatment their location in layers or zones respectively to BN grains was identified in a sequence: $BN/TiB_2/TiN$.

The decreasing volume of titanium via reaction to form borides, nitrides would be expected to increase hardness and Young's modulus of the $cBN/TiH_2$ composite during post sintering heat treatment.
