# Thermodynamic Modeling of Thermal Processes Involving Actinides (U, Am, Pu) in the Course of Heating Radioactive Graphite in Steam

N. M. Barbin*<sup>a,b</sup>, A. M. Kobelev<sup>a</sup>, D. I. Terent'ev<sup>a</sup>, and S. G. Alekseev<sup>a</sup>

<sup>a</sup> Ural Institute of the State Fire Service, Russian Emergencies Ministry, ul. Mira 22, Yekaterinburg, 620062 Russia
<sup>b</sup> Ural State Agrarian University, ul. Karla Libknekhta 42, Yekaterinburg, 620075 Russia
*e-mail: NMBarbin@mail.ru

Received June 14, 2016; in final form, June 4, 2017

**Abstract**—The behavior of actinides (U, Am, Pu) in the course of combustion of radioactive graphite in steam was studied by thermodynamic modeling. Thermodynamic modeling was performed using TERRA program in the temperature interval from 373 to 3273 K to determine the possible composition of volatile actinide species formed in the course of graphite utilization by heating in steam. The modeling shows that the actinides at high temperatures are present in the following forms: U, as $UO_3$ and $UO_2$ vapors and as $UO_3^-$ and $UO_2^+$ ions; Am, as Am vapor; and Pu, as $PuO_2$ and PuO vapors and as ionized $PuO^+$. The main reactions within separate phases and at the interface were revealed, and their equilibrium constants were determined.

**Keywords:** thermodynamic modeling, actinides, isotopes, water vapor, equilibrium constants, radioactive graphite

Ten nuclear power plants with 35 power units (total power 26.2 GW) operate today in Russia, and they produce about 17% of the total amount of the generated energy [1].

After decommissioning of nuclear power plants, it becomes necessary to utilize spent nuclear materials. Radioactive graphite occupies a particular place among them because of its large amount and the fact that it acquires no useful properties after irradiation. The volume of irradiated graphite in the world is 260 thousand tons, including 60 thousand tons in the Russian Federation. Radioactive graphite is potentially hazardous for humans and environment [2, 3]. Isotopes of U, Am, and Pu, present in reactor graphite, are particularly hazardous [4].

This study was aimed at determining the possible composition of volatile actinide species upon graphite utilization by heating in steam. We performed thermodynamic modeling and determined the equilibrium constants of the main reactions occurring in the course of heating radioactive graphite containing U, Am, and Pu in steam.

## CALCULATION PROCEDURE

The behavior of U, Am, and Pu present in graphite from a nuclear reactor was studied by thermodynamic modeling [5–7]. Thermodynamic modeling (or equilibrium computer experiment) is an efficient and unique tool for studying diverse high-temperature processes of thermal decomposition, reduction, and synthesis, and also for solving other applied problems. This method was successfully used in physics [8–11] and in studies of combustion and explosion [12].

An advantage of the thermodynamic method is its versatility. It allows studying systems of any chemical composition on the basis of reference data on thermodynamic and thermodynamic properties of individual substances participating in the equilibrium (IVTANTERMO).

The method is based on the principle of the entropy maximum, which is valid in accordance with the second law of thermodynamics for any equilibrium system irrespective of the pathway by which the system reached the equilibrium:

The Am distribution between phases is shown in Fig. 2. Up to 1873 K, Am occurs in the form of condensed $\mathrm{AmO_2}$. In the temperature interval from 1673 to 1873 K, the relative content of condensed $\mathrm{AmO_2}$ somewhat decreases and the content of condensed $\mathrm{Am_2O_3}$ increases in accordance with reaction (16). As the temperature increased to 1973 K, condensed $\mathrm{AmO_2}$ transforms into condensed $\mathrm{Am_2O_3}$. In the temperature interval from 1973 to 2473 K, condensed $\mathrm{Am_2O_3}$ disappears and transforms into gaseous Am in accordance with reaction (17). At 2473 K, all the condensed Am forms disappear, and Am is fully in the gas phase. At temperatures in the interval 2473–3273 K, americium is in the form of gaseous Am.

The Pu distribution between the phases is shown in Fig. 3. Up to approximately 1673 K, Pu is in the form of condensed $\mathrm{PuO_2}$. In the temperature interval from 1673 to 1873 K, the content of condensed Pu decreases insignificantly, and the content of gaseous $\mathrm{PuO_2}$ increases to 1 mol %, which corresponds to the onset of the Pu transfer into the gas phase in accordance with reaction (18). At the temperature increased to 1973 K, condensed $\mathrm{PuO_2}$ disappears. In the same temperature interval, the content of gaseous $\mathrm{PuO_2}$, gaseous $\mathrm{PuO}$, and ionized $\mathrm{PuO^+}$ increases to 98, 1, and 1 mol %, respectively. At temperatures from 1973 to 3273 K, the relative content of gaseous $\mathrm{PuO_2}$ decreases (98–79 mol %), and that of gaseous $\mathrm{PuO}$ (1–11 mol %) and ionized $\mathrm{PuO^+}$ (1–10 mol %) increases in accordance with reactions (19) and (20).

The relative content of condensed $\mathrm{PuOCl}$ and $\mathrm{Pu_2O_3}$ and of gaseous $\mathrm{PuO_2^+}$, $\mathrm{Pu^+}$, and $\mathrm{Pu}$ does not reach high values in the examined temperature interval. Less than 0.04% of the total amount of Pu in the system is in these forms.

In accordance with this set of components and with numerical results of simulation, we wrote the minimal set of main reactions (equilibria) within separate phases and at the interface and calculated the equilibrium constants of the reactions, which were determined assuming ideal behavior of the system components. The equilibrium constants are described by the equations

$$
\ln K_i = a_i + b_i(1/T). \tag{2}
$$

The coefficients in the equations for the equilibrium constants of the reactions are given in Table 3.

The equilibrium constants of reactions (7) and (18) can be compared to the equilibrium constants of the vaporization of $\text{UO}_2$ and $\text{PuO}_2$, given in [13]. At 1873 K and a pressure of 1 atm, the calculated constants $K_7$ and $K_{18}$ (Table 3) are $1.6 \times 10^{-8}$ and $1.5 \times 10^{-8}$, respectively. The vaporization constants of $\text{UO}_2$ and $\text{PuO}_2$, determined from the data in [13], are $1.2 \times 10^{-8}$ and $0.8 \times 10^{-8}$, respectively. As can be seen, the results of our calculations reasonably agree with the experimental data.

Thus, we have performed thermodynamic modeling of thermal processes that occur in the course of heating radioactive graphite in steam. The distribution of U, Am, and Pu between the phases was plotted in accordance with the calculation results, the main reactions within separate phases and at the interface were formulated, and their equilibrium constants were calculated. Computer simulation shows that U, Pu, and Am, initially occurring in the graphite-containing system as impurities, in the course of heating in steam tend to pass into the gas phase on reaching certain "critical" temperatures. This can be attributed to the reaction of graphite and condensed oxide phases with steam in the examined temperature range, leading to degradation of the condensed phases and transfer of their components into the gas phase.
