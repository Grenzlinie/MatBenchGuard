# VAPOUR-SOLID PHASE EQUILIBRIUM OF $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ TERNARY CRYSTALS

J. HORÁK, R. NOVOTNÝ, P. LOŠŤÁK
Technical University of Chemical Engineering, Leninovo nám, 565, 532 10 Pardubice, Czechoslovakia
and
P. HÖSCHL
Institute of Physics, Charles University, Prague, Czechoslovakia

(Received 3 December 1986; accepted in revised form 1 April 1987)

Abstract—With the objective of analysing the relations between the concentration of free carriers in $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ ternary crystals and the vapour pressures of selenium and/or sulphur, a model describing the equilibrium between the vapour and the solid phase of a ternary crystal has been worked out by adopting the simplification that the ternary system can be taken for an ideal solid solution where the activity of $\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3$ is equal to its concentration. Such an approach makes possible the determination of the equilibrium pressures of selenium and sulphur at various $x$ values. Using pairs of these values, we calculated the equilibrium constant for the reaction $V'_4 + y/2\text{S}_2(\text{g}) + (1-y)/2\text{Se}_2(\text{g}) + \text{e}'\rightleftharpoons 0$ at a temperature of 700 K. The proposed model explains well the experimentally observed variation of the concentration of free carriers in $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ crystals with increasing value of $x$ ($x = 0.00$–0.16) under identical growth conditions.

Keywords: Vapour-solid phase, ternary crystal, equilibrium pressures, selenium, sulphur.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

$\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ ($x = 0.00$–0.16) belongs to the family of crystals with the $D_{3d}^5$ space group. Basic physical properties of $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ mixed crystals, such as equilibrium phase diagram, changes in lattice parameters, density and some transport coefficients of polycrystalline samples are reported in [1–4].

In our earlier paper [5] we described the method of growing homogeneous mixed crystals within the range of $x = 0.00$–0.16. In the same paper are also reported some structural parameters and basic physical properties that characterize the $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ crystals as ternary semiconductors; fundamental optical properties of these materials are reported in [6].

$\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ mixed crystals, like pure $\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3$ crystals exhibit a superstoichiometric content of bismuth atoms that give rise to $n$-type conductivity [5]. The relations among the vapour pressure of selenium and sulphur, departures from stoichiometry and the concentration of free carriers have not yet been investigated.

In the present paper we report the results of measurements of some transport coefficients of $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ crystals with varying sulphur concentration that were subjected to a long-term annealing under different but well-defined conditions: i.e. temperature, sulphur and selenium vapour pressure. From the variations of these transport coefficients, due to the variations of the partial pressures of selenium and sulphur, we analyse the relations between the concentration of point defects and the vapour pressure of the volatile components over the crystal.

## 2. EXPERIMENTAL

### 2.1 Growth of $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ single crystals

In the preparation of $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ crystals we used elemental Bi and Se of 5 N purity and sulphur of 4.5 N purity. The weight charges were prepared in the ratios corresponding to $x = 0.00, 0.008, 0.08$ and 0.16. The synthesis of polycrystalline substances was carried out in conical quartz ampoules. The ampoules were evacuated and flushed with argon; the residual argon pressure was about $10^{-4}\text{Pa}$. Homogenization and synthesis proceeded in a horizontal furnace at a temperature of 1073 K for 48 h. The single crystals were grown using the Bridgman method in such a way that the ampoule containing the starting materials was first maintained at a temperature of 1073 K for 24 h and then, after its tip was completely filled with the melt, it was lowered into a temperature gradient of 80 K/cm at a rate of 0.8 mm/h.

By means of this technique, homogeneous crystals well cleavable along the plane parallel to the ampoule's axis were obtained. The back-reflection Laue method was used to identify the cleavage faces of the crystal as (0001) planes.

The prepared crystals were analysed using an energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence analyser TRACOR Northern 2000, provided with a $\text{Si(Li)}$ detector and featuring a sputtering capacity of $148\text{eV-MnK}_\alpha$. In the analysis, the bismuth and selenium contents were determined and the sulphur concentration was calculated from the data obtained. Perfectly homogenized samples with a known sul-


phur content, within the range of $x = 0.00$-$0.16$, were used as standards.

### 2.2 Annealing of the samples
In order to determine the crystal parameters corresponding to the equilibrium between the vapour and solid phases, the samples were annealed under well-defined conditions in the presence of sulphur and selenium vapours; then they were rapidly cooled and their electrical conductivity and Hall coefficient measured.

The crystals were cut into platelets of dimensions $(15$-$18) \times (2.0$-$2.5) \times (0.15$-$0.20)\ \text{mm}^3$ with their largest faces $(15$-$18) \times (2.0$-$2.5)\ \text{mm}^2$ corresponding to the cleavage planes (0001). The homogeneity of the prepared samples was checked by measuring the reflectance in the plasma resonance frequency range on both opposite (0001) faces. Besides, electrical conductivity and Hall coefficient were measured prior to the annealing in the sulphur and selenium vapours. The samples were then inserted into spe- cially adapted quartz ampoules, where they were annealed at a given temperature $T_1$ in sulphur and selenium vapours using an external source of the vapour mixture. The liquid-vapour interface region of the external source was heated to a temperature $T_2$. The vapour pressures of sulphur and selenium were determined using the known composition of the liquid phase and the temperature $T_2$. The experimentally determined values of $p_{\text{Se}_2}$ and $p_{\text{S}_2}$ are given in Table 1. On exposing the samples to the vapours, the condition $T_1 > T_2$ was fulfilled. The temperature $T_1$ was set at a value of $700\ \text{K}$, the temperature $T_2$ was chosen to be $680$ and $635\ \text{K}$. Such an arrangement was used for the long-term annealing of the samples in order to attain equilibrium conditions.

In endeavouring to verify whether the equilibrium state had been reached after a definite annealing time, the electrical conductivity and Hall coefficient were measured on samples annealed for different periods. In view of the fact that the electrical conductivity and Hall coefficient measured on samples of identical composition ceased to show any further changes with annealing times of 14 days or longer, the period of 14 days was chosen as the standard annealing time.

For the purpose of comparing the properties of $\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3$ with those of $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)$ mixed crystals, the dependence of the electrical conductivity of $\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3$ on selenium vapour pressure at the higher temperatures was investigated.

### 2.3 Determination of the electrical conductivity and the Hall coefficient
The electrical conductivity was measured in the direction perpendicular to the trigonal $c$-axis (i.e. $\sigma_\perp$); the Hall coefficient was measured simultaneously on the same sample with the magnetic induction $\overline{B}$ parallel to the trigonal $c$-axis, i.e. $R_H(\overline{B} \parallel \overline{c})$. The measurements were carried out at room temperature using an a.c. current supply of $170\ \text{Hz}$ frequency, with the value of $B = 1.1$ Tesla.

Owing to a considerable ionic character of the bonds in selenide-sulphide, in accordance with the ideas mentioned in [7] we can adopt the view that positively charged anion vacancies exist in the lattice. This view is further corroborated by the fact that the experimentally determined density of $Bi_{2}Se_{3}$ is lower than the value calculated from the structure data [8]. Hence, for our following considerations, we shall assume the existence of positively charged anion vacancies in the crystal lattice.

With respect to the fact that the temperature dependences of the electrical conductivity and Hall coefficient given in [9] can be taken to be monotonic in the temperature interval 294-700 K, it does not seem probable that the electrical charge of the vacancies should change in this temperature interval. Also, the value of the slope of $\log \sigma$ vs $\log p_{Se_{2}}$, i.e. -0.28 (see Fig. 1), indicates that the vacancies in question are only singly ionized. We extend the validity of this assumption to the $Bi_{2}(Se_{1-x}S_{x})_{3}$ mixed crystals, also. Thus, we suppose that vacancies $V_{Se}^{\cdot}$ and $V_{S}^{\cdot}$ exist in the anion sublattice of the mixed crystals. Annealing of the $Bi_{2}(Se_{1-x}S_{x})_{3}$ crystals, with the aim of attaining equilibrium between the solid and vapour phases, was carried out at a temperature of 700 K. We adopt a further assumption, namely that the point defects formed on annealing conserved their concentrations even after rapid cooling of the sample to room temperature.

Let us note here that the annealing of $Bi_{2}(Se_{1-x}S_{x})_{3}$ crystals in vacuum results in an increase in the concentration of free carriers; at the same time one can detect small amounts of sulphur and/or selenium leaving the crystal.

### 4.2. Vapour-solid equilibrium
With the objective of determining the relations between the concentration of free carriers and the partial pressures of selenium and sulphur vapours, the phenomenon of anion vacancies being occupied by S and/or Se atoms (as explained qualitatively above) was analysed in greater detail, using the thermodynamic description of the equilibrium between the solid and vapour phases.

First, the equilibrium vapour pressures of the individual components over the ternary $Bi_{2}(Se_{1-x}S_{x})_{3}$ crystals were determined. If the $Bi_{2}(Se_{1-x}S_{x})_{3}$ crystal is heated in a closed evacuated space at a temperature lower than its melting point, the equilibrium between the crystal and the vapours of its components-bismuth, sulphur and selenium-will establish itself after a sufficiently long time. Assuming that diatomic sulphur and selenium and $Bi_{2}$ molecules prevail in the vapour phase, we can simplify the situation and express the equilibrium by the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Bi}_{2}\left(\mathrm{Se}_{1-x} \mathrm{~S}_{x}\right)_{3} \rightleftharpoons & \mathrm{Bi}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})+ \\
& \frac{3}{2}(1-x) \mathrm{Se}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})+\frac{3}{2} x \mathrm{~S}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \quad(1)
\end{aligned}
$$

whose equilibrium constant $K_{1}$ is given by

$$
K_{1}=p_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{Se}_{2}}^{3 / 2(1-x)} \cdot p_{\mathrm{S}_{2}}^{3 / 2 x}, \quad (2)
$$

where $p_{i}$ are the partial pressures of the individual components. It is difficult to determine the value of the equilibrium constant $K_{1}$ experimentally; in order to do that, however, one can use the activity values of $Bi_{2}Se_{3}$ and $Bi_{2}S_{3}$ in the $Bi_{2}(Se_{1-x}S_{x})_{3}$ solid solution.

Assuming an equilibrium between solid and vapour phases in a ternary system, the number of degrees of freedom is given by the Gibbs phase rule $v=r+2-f$, where $r$ is the number of components and $f$ the number of phases.

The most suitable and the most often used thermodynamic quantities characterizing the state of a ternary system with two phases are: temperature $T$, vapour pressure of one component $(p_{\mathrm{Se}_{2}})$ and the composition of the solid solution

$$
x \mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{~S}_{3}+(1-x) \mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{Se}_{3} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Bi}_{2}\left(\mathrm{Se}_{1-x} \mathrm{~S}_{x}\right)_{3}. \quad (3)
$$

However, the state of the system is also unambiguously described and characterized by the vapour pressures of both components and the temperature. Such a description of the equilibrium between the solid and vapour phases is more advantageous for our purposes.

The activities of $Bi_{2}Se_{3}$ and $Bi_{2}S_{3}$-denoted respectively as $A(Bi_{2}Se_{3})$ and $A(Bi_{2}S_{3})$-can be expressed using the following relations.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A\left(\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{Se}_{3}\right) & =\frac{p_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{Se}_{2}}^{3 / 2}}{{ }_{1} p_{\mathrm{oBi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{oSe}_{2}}^{3 / 2}} \\
A\left(\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{~S}_{3}\right) & =\frac{p_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{S}_{2}}^{3 / 2}}{{ }_{2} p_{\mathrm{oBi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{oS}_{2}}^{3 / 2}}, \quad (4)
\end{aligned}
$$

The activities $A(\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3)$ and $A(\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3)$, however, have not yet been experimentally determined. Assuming that—as a first approximation—the ternary crystal $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ is an ideal solid solution of the $\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3$ and $\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3$ components, the activities can be taken as equal to the concentrations. It follows then:

$$
x \cdot K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{~S}_{3}}=p_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{S}_{2}}^{3 / 2} ; \quad(1-x) K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{Se}_{3}}=p_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2}} p_{\mathrm{Se}_{2}}^{3 / 2} \quad(5)
$$

with

$$
K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{~S}_{3}}={ }_{2} p_{\mathrm{oBi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{oS}_{2}}^{3 / 2} ; \quad K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{Se}_{3}}={ }_{1} p_{\mathrm{oBi}_{2}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{oSe}_{2}}^{3 / 2}, \quad(6)
$$

which corresponds to the equations

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{~S}_{3}^{(\mathrm{s})} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Bi}_{2}^{(\mathrm{g})}+\frac{3}{2} \mathrm{~S}_{2}^{(\mathrm{g})} ; & \\
\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{Se}_{3}^{(\mathrm{s})} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Bi}_{2}^{(\mathrm{g})}+\frac{3}{2} \mathrm{Se}_{2}^{(\mathrm{g})}. & (7)
\end{aligned}
$$

Provided we accept such simplifications, we can write for the equilibrium constant $K_1$ the following expression:

$$
K_{1}=x^{x} \cdot(1-x)^{(1-x)} \cdot K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{~S}_{3}}^{x} \cdot K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{Se}_{3}}^{(1-x)}. \quad(8)
$$

The validity of the derived expression can be easily verified for $x \to 0$ and $x \to 1$, since $\lim_{x \to 0} x^{x}=1$ and $\lim_{x \to 1} (1-x)^{(1-x)}=1$; then we get $K_{1}(x \to 0)=K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}$ and $K_{1}(x \to 1)=K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}$.

The equilibrium constants $K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}$ and $K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}$ can be determined from the value of the Gibbs free energy $\Delta G$ corresponding to the formation of the solid phase of $\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3$ or $\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3$ from the elements. Using the values of the standard Gibbs energy $\Delta G_{298}^{0}$, standard enthalpy $\Delta H_{298}^{0}$, standard entropy $S_{298}^{0}$ and the temperature dependence of the specific heat $c_p$ (the values were taken from the tables by Mills [10] and are given in Table 2), the equilibrium constants $K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}$ and $K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}$ were calculated. Their temperature dependences can be expressed by means of the following relations:

$$
\log K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{Se}_{3}}=\frac{A_{1}}{T}+B_{1} ;
$$

$$
A_{1}=-6566.4 \mathrm{~K}, \quad B_{1}=-15.6
$$

$$
\log K_{\mathrm{Bi}_{2} \mathrm{~S}_{3}}=\frac{A_{2}}{T}+B_{2} ;
$$

$$
A_{2}=-6313.1 \mathrm{~K}, \quad B_{2}=-14.2. \quad(9)
$$

Further, the values of the equilibrium constants $K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}$ and $K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}$ enable us to calculate the total vapour pressure $p_{\text{tot}}$ over the $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ crystal

$$
p_{\text{tot}} = p_{\text{Bi}_2} + p_{\text{Se}_2} + p_{\text{S}_2} \tag{10}
$$

that can be expressed in terms of the partial pressure $p_{\text{S}_2}$ or $p_{\text{Se}_2}$ using the relations

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\text{tot}} = x \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3} \cdot p_{\text{S}_2}^{-3/2} &+ (1-x)^{2/3} \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}^{2/3} \\
&\times \bar{x}^{2/3} \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}^{-2/3} \cdot p_{\text{S}_2} + p_{\text{S}_2}. \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\text{tot}} = (1-x) \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3} \cdot p_{\text{Se}_2}^{-3/2} &+ (1-x)^{-2/3} K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}^{-2/3} \\
&\times x^{2/3} \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}^{2/3} \cdot p_{\text{Se}_2} + p_{\text{Se}_2}. \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

On sublimation, the partial pressure $p_{\text{tot}}$ attains the minimum value, which can be found from the conditions

$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{tot}}}{\partial p_{\text{S}_2}} = 0, i \frac{\partial p_{\text{tot}}}{\partial p_{\text{Se}_2}} = 0. \tag{13}
$$

Using these conditions we obtain for the minimum partial pressures $p_{\text{Se}_2}^{\text{min}}$ and $p_{\text{S}_2}^{\text{min}}$ the expressions

$$
p_{\text{Se}_2}^{\text{min}} = \left[ \frac{3/2 \cdot (1-x) \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}}{1 + (1-x)^{-2/3} \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}^{-2/3} \cdot x^{2/3} \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}^{2/3}} \right]^{2/5} \tag{14}
$$

$$
p_{\text{S}_2}^{\text{min}} = \left[ \frac{3/2 \cdot x \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}}{1 + (1-x)^{2/3} \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3}^{2/3} \cdot \bar{x}^{2/3} \cdot K_{\text{Bi}_2\text{S}_3}^{-2/3}} \right]^{2/5}. \tag{15}
$$

Numerical values of $p_{\text{Se}_2}^{\text{min}}$ and $p_{\text{S}_2}^{\text{min}}$ corresponding to the annealing temperature (700 K) of the $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ samples of different composition are given in Table 5.

We considered further the variations in the concentration of the free carriers in the mixed crystals in the pressure ranges $p_{\text{Se}_2} > p_{\text{Se}_2}^{\text{min}}$ and $p_{\text{S}_2} > p_{\text{S}_2}^{\text{min}}$. We started from the equilibrium state established on annealing the $\text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-x}\text{S}_x)_3$ crystal at a given temperature and the given value of the selenium and sulphur vapour pressures. The process of incorporating the Se and S atoms into the crystal, in agreement with the experimentally observed decrease in the concentration of the free electrons, can be described by the equation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n \frac{2}{3} \text{Bi}_{\text{Bi}}^x + n V_A' + n \frac{y}{2} \text{S}_2^{(g)} &+ n \frac{1-y}{2} \text{Se}_2^{(g)} \\
&+ ne' \rightleftharpoons n \frac{1}{3} \text{Bi}_2(\text{Se}_{1-y}\text{S}_y)_3. \tag{16}
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $\text{Bi}_{\text{Bi}}^x$ denotes Bi atoms occupying sites in the bismuth sublattice that represent the superstoichiometry of bismuth due to the presence of anion vacancies $V_A'$. Since the superstoichiometric Bi content is expressed by $V_A$ vacancies, eqn (16) can be simplified to the form

$$
V_A' + \frac{y}{2} \text{S}_2^{(g)} + \frac{1-y}{2} \text{Se}_2^{(g)} + e' \rightleftharpoons 0. \tag{17}
$$

Taking the activity of selenide-sulphide equal to unity, we obtain the following expression for the equilibrium constant $K_R$ of eqn (17):

$$
K_R = \frac{1}{\left[ V_A' \right] \left[ e' \right] \cdot p_{\text{Se}_2}^{(1-y)/2} \cdot p_{\text{S}_2}^{y/2}}. \tag{18}
$$

The coefficients $y, (1-y)$ in eqn (18) were determined based on the following arguments: if we subject the selenide-sulphide mixed crystals to a long-term annealing in selenium and sulphur vapours at pressures $p_{\text{Se}_2}$ and $p_{\text{S}_2}$ supplied by an external source, then the anion vacancies will be occupied by Se and S atoms in the ratio corresponding to the set pressures that are in equilibrium with the crystal. $$

At a given temperature, the occupation of the vacancies by Se and S atoms leads thus to the formation of the solid phase of $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}(\mathrm{Se}_{1-x} \mathrm{S}_{x})_{3}$ which, however, has in most cases a different composition from the initial crystals prior to annealing. A long-term annealing at elevated temperature results in a uniform distribution of sulphur atoms in the anion sublattice by diffusion, thus giving rise to a mixed crystal of a new composition, slightly different from the original one.

The performed experiments enabled us to obtain corresponding values of the concentration of the free carriers in the $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}(\mathrm{Se}_{1-x} \mathrm{S}_{x})_{3}$ crystals, long-term annealed at $700\ \text{K}$ under various $p_{\mathrm{Se}_{2}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{S}_{2}}$ pressures (see Table 6), and to calculate these from the equilibrium constants $K_R$ according to eqn (18). At the given temperature, the equilibrium constant $K_R$ has a value of $(4.75 \pm 0.8) \times 10^{-51}\ \text{m}^6\ \text{kPa}^{-1/2}$. With the value of $K_R$ it is possible to calculate the concentration of the free carriers, provided that the pressures $p_{\mathrm{Se}_{2}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{S}_{2}}$ used in the long-term annealing of the $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}(\mathrm{Se}_{1-x} \mathrm{S}_{x})_{3}$ mixed crystals to the equilibrium state are known.

The $p_{\mathrm{S}_{2}} / p_{\mathrm{Se}_{2}}$ vs $y$ dependence (see Fig. 2) makes it possible to explain the experimental results reported in our earlier papers [5,6]; the obtained experimental values of the transport coefficients, plasma resonance frequency and absorption coefficient indicated that in $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}(\mathrm{Se}_{1-x} \mathrm{S}_{x})_{3}$ crystals prepared under identical conditions the concentration of the free carriers increases with increasing sulphur content. The crystals, prepared under identical conditions, exhibit with increasing $x$ higher vapour pressures over the crystal; if we accept the idea that the defects formed during the crystal growth remain in the lattice in the same concentration as during the growth of the solid phase, the crystals with the higher concentration of vacancies than the crystals with the lower content of S atoms. This is in agreement with the results reported in [5,6].

### 5. CONCLUSION

In the present paper we attempted a description of the equilibrium between the vapour and solid phase of $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}(\mathrm{Se}_{1-x} \mathrm{S}_{x})_{3}$ ternary crystals with the aim of formulating, under greatly simplifying assumptions, the relations between the vapour pressures of selenium and sulphur, and the concentration of vacancies in the anion sublattice, or the concentration of free carriers.

Despite the simplifications adopted, the model presented accounts well for the experimental findings, showing that increasing sulphur content (i.e. increasing $x$ value) results in an increase in the concentration of free carriers in the crystals prepared under identical conditions.
