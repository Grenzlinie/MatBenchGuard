# A CALCULATION OF ISOBARIC POTENTIALS OF REACTIONS IN THE SINTERING OF ALLOY STEELS IN MEDIA WITH HCl and HF*

R. A. Andrievskii and S. M. Solonin

Institute of Cermets and Special Alloys, Academy of Sciences, Ukr. SSR

Translated from Poroshkovaya Metallurgiya, No. 4 (10),
pp. 94-99, July-August, 1962
Original article submitted December 13, 1961

The authors of [1,2] observed the beneficial effect of additions of $NH_4Cl$ and $NH_4F$ in the charges on the properties of sintered alloy steels. When studying the sintering of pulverized powders of stainless steel also containing 2-3% Si and Mo, some authors, for example [3], did not detect much effect due to halides. In our investigations of the sintering of reduced powder of 1Kh18N9T steel we found that with additions of $NH_4F$ and $NH_4Cl$ to the $Al_2O_3$ charge (in amounts of ~1 g per kg of components) the strength of the sintered specimen of porosity 20-40% increases by about 1.5-2 times.

However, the mechanism of the effect of halides is insufficiently clear. It is therefore of interest to estimate the equilibrium of reactions occurring during the sintering of alloy steels in charges with $NH_4Cl$ and $NH_4F$. These reactions can be

For most of these reactions there is no thermodynamic information in the literature [4-8]. In papers [7, 8] on thermodynamic relationships in "chlorine" and "fluorine" metallurgy it is mainly reactions of the type $\mathrm{Me}+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ or $\mathrm{MeO}+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ which are studied.

To estimate the isobaric potentials $(\Delta Z^{0})$ we used indirect calculation methods.* To calculate $\Delta Z^{0}$ of the reactions (12)-(14) we used the data on the thermodynamic constants of lithium and its compounds given in [9]. The results for the calculation of $\Delta Z^{0}$ are shown in Figs. 1 and 2.

Since indirect methods were used for the calculation,the accuracy of the obtained values is about $\pm 10 \%$. This applies especially to fluorides, many of the thermodynamic and physical properties of which have not been studied [10, 11].

The nonmonotonic behavior of the relationship $\Delta Z^{0}=f(T)$ is due to the change in the aggregate state of the components of the reaction; for fluorides the inflections are shown by a broken line, since there is no precise information on the melting and boiling points.

For reactions with LiCl even at room temperature the change in the thermodynamic potential has positive values of the order of $30-40 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{g} \cdot \mathrm{mole} \mathrm{LiCl}$, so that the reduction of $\mathrm{Cr}, \mathrm{Si}$, and $\mathrm{Al}$ oxides by lithium chloride is not very probable.

An analysis of the data of Figs. 1 and 2 leads to the following conclusions: 1) the thermodynamic potentials of the studied reactions become less negative with increase in temperature, i.e., the equilibrium of the reactions (1)-(11) is moved from right to left; 2) in reactions with $\mathrm{HCl}$ and $\mathrm{HF}$ in the range of ordinary sintering temperatures $(1100-1200^{\circ})$ chromium and silicon halides can form $(\mathrm{CrF}_{3}, \mathrm{CrF}_{2}, \mathrm{SiF}_{4}, \mathrm{CrCl}_{2}, \mathrm{SiCl}_{4} ;$ 3) in the heating and cooling period other halides can also form $(\mathrm{FeCl}_{2}, \mathrm{CrCl}_{3}, \mathrm{NiCl}_{2}, \mathrm{FeF}_{2}, \mathrm{NiF}_{2})$ but with increase in temperature they are gradually reduced to the corresponding metals; 4) chromium and aluminum oxides are not reduced by $\mathrm{HCl}$ and $\mathrm{HF}$; silicon

the data given in Figs. 1 and 2 refer to standard conditions for pure metals (the partial pressures of the gaseous com-

In the second place, we cannot ignore the kinetic regularities of the reactions, which, incidentally, have not been
studied to any great extent.

In this work we tried to estimate how $\Delta Z^{0}$ changes for Kh18N9 and Kh17N2 steels in media with varying HCl
content. The change in the thermodynamic potential $\Delta Z$ for reaction (1) under nonstandard conditions is written
in the form

$$
\Delta Z=\Delta Z^{0}+R T \cdot \ln \frac{a_{\mathrm{CrCl}_{2}}^{1 / 2} \cdot a_{\mathrm{H}_{2}}^{1 / 2}}{a_{\mathrm{Cr}}^{1 / 2} \cdot a_{\mathrm{HCl}}},
\tag{15}
$$

where $\Delta Z^{0}$ is the change in the standard potential [Fig. 1, (1)]; R is the gas constant; T is the absolute temperature;
$\underline{a}$ is the activity of the components.

The change in $\Delta Z$ for reactions (2) and (4) was calculated in a similar way. The results of the calculations are given in Figs. 3 and 4. Similar calculations were also performed for reaction (7).

The following conclusions can be drawn from the results of the estimation of $\Delta Z$ for reactions of the main components of chrome-nickel steel with hydrogen chloride: 1) the corrections to $\Delta Z$ connected with the activity of the components and with the differing composition of the atmosphere are not very large, especially in reactions with chromium; 2) the hydrogen chloride concentration has practically no effect on the formation of chromium chlorides, but has an effect in reactions with ferric chloride (this is evidently due to the high volatility of the latter); 3) the formation of chlorides of iron during the sintering of Kh18N9 and Kh17N2 steels proceeds identically; 4) during isothermal sintering we can expect the formation of chromium chloride; the other chlorides can only form during heating and cooling. These calculations are not possible for reactions with hydrogen fluoride.

The above ideas on the conditions for the occurrence of reactions during sintering apply to a closed space and do not take into account the kinetic regularities. Sintering in charges with $NH_{4}Cl$ and $NH_{4}F$ is usually carried out in containers whose covers are smeared with refractory pastes made with molten glass. However, the removal of chlorides and fluorides is nevertheless possible, as shown by the decrease in weight of the specimen after sintering. The possibility is also mentioned in the literature of the reduction of chromium oxides by HCl and HF during the continuous removal of reaction products [7, 8, 10, 11], but these regularities have not been studied in detail. Under the actual conditions of sintering the formation of chlorides and fluorides in connection with their possible removal is therefore more probable than would appear from thermochemical calculations.

For a more detailed explanation of the mechanism of the effect of $NH_{4}F$ and $NH_{4}Cl$ on the sintering of alloy steels it is essential to arrange special model experiments which would establish the kinetic regularities of reactions (1)-(11).

# SUMMARY

The values of the isobaric potentials are calculated for reactions occurring during the sintering of alloyed steels in HCl and HF media. The effect of the concentration of the components and the composition of the gaseous medium on the equilibrium of the reactions is estimated.
