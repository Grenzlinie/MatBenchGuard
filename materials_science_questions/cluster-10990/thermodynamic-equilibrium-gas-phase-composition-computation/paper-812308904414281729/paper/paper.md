# Acid-Base Properties of Xenon Di- and Tetrafluoride in High Temperature Reactions

Yu. M. KISELEV*, N. E. FADEEVA, A. I. POPOV, V. I. SPITZIN

Moscow (USSR), Laboratory of the Rare Elements, Chemistry Department, State Lomonosov University

Abstract. Reaction of xenon di- and tetrafluoride with different transition element fluoro-complexes at high temperatures has been studied. The formation of $\mathrm{Xe}^{\mathrm{IV}}$ anionic complexes $\mathrm{M}_{2} \mathrm{XeF}_{6}$ has been found. $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$ is shown to reveal two kinds of properties: oxidative (higher than $300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ ) and acidic (between $150-300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ ), independently whether it was initially introduced into the system or arised in it from $\mathrm{XeF}_{2}$. The reaction path of xenon tetrafluoride is shown to depend upon the other reagent properties. The conclusion is made that Lewis acidic properties of xenon fluorides decrease as follows: $\mathrm{XeF}_{6} \approx \mathrm{XeF}_{4} \gg \mathrm{XeF}_{2}$.

Über die Säure-Base-Eigenschaften des Di- und Tetrafluorxenons in Reaktionen bei erhöhten Temperaturen

Inhaltsübersicht. Es wurde die Reaktion von Xenondifluorid und Xenontetrafluorid mit Fluorkomplex-Verbindungen verschiedener Übergangsmetalle bei erhöhter Temperatur untersucht. Unter diesen Bedingungen wurde die Bildung von Anionenkomplexen $\mathrm{M}_{2} \mathrm{Xe}^{\mathrm{IV}} \mathrm{F}_{6}$ festgestellt. Es wurde gezeigt, daß $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$ unabhängig davon, ob es direkt in die Reaktion eingeführt wird oder dort aus $\mathrm{XeF}_{2}$ entsteht, zwei typische Eigenschaften aufweist: die eines Oxydationsmittels $\left(>300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}\right)$ und einer Säure (zwischen $150^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ und $300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ ). Das Reaktionsverhalten des $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$ hängt ferner von den Eigenschaften des Reaktionspartners ab. Die Eigenschaften der Xenonfluoride als Lewis-Säuren folgen der Reihe: $\mathrm{XeF}_{6} \approx \mathrm{XeF}_{4} \gg \mathrm{XeF}_{2}$.

## Introduction

Recently several new reactions of lower xenon fluorides $\left(\mathrm{XeF}_{2}, \mathrm{XeF}_{4}\right)$ were described [1] in our laboratory. They occur in accordance with the equations:

$$2 \mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{I}} \mathrm{F}+\mathrm{XeF}_{4}=\mathrm{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{I}} \mathrm{XeF}_{6} \quad \left(\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{Na}, \mathrm{K}, \mathrm{Rb}, \mathrm{Cs}\right) \tag{1}$$

$$2 \mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{I}} \mathrm{F}+2 \mathrm{XeF}_{2}=\mathrm{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{I}} \mathrm{XeF}_{6}+\mathrm{Xe} \quad \left(\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{K}, \mathrm{Rb}, \mathrm{Cs}\right). \tag{2}$$

As we found, such processes have an influence on yield and purity of products, which were obtained by fluorination of transition metal complex halides (with alkali cations) with xenon di- or tetrafluoride.

This is a reason for discussing the formation of alkali metal fluoroxenates(IV) by reaction between lower xenon fluorides with different transition element fluorocomplexes in the present paper.

### Experimental

Interaction of $XeF_{2}$ and $XeF_{4}$ with rare earth elements (RE)¹), manganese, cobalt, nickel, copper, silver, and mercury fluoro-complexes was studied in this work. Cesium compounds were used as a rule because the effect if fluoroxenate(IV) formation was observed in this case with the most clearness.

Initial RE fluorocomplexes were prepared by annealing of respective $MF_{3}$ (M = RE) with CsF (Pt crucible; $800^{\circ}C$; 2 h) followed by product melting at $1100^{\circ}C$ [2]. Complex fluorides of transition d-elements were synthesized by reaction of stoichiometric amounts of $XeF_{2}$ with mixtures of unhydrous cesium and manganese, cobalt, nickel, copper, silver²), or mercury chlorides, taken in necessary mole ratios, in hermetic nickel high pressure autoclaves ($140-150^{\circ}C$; $1-2$ h). Chemical-preparative investigation of the interaction of xenon di- and tetrafluoride with transition metal compounds was carried out similarly. For preventation of the impurification of obtained products by nickel fluorides, there were used reactors bricklined by monocrystalline fluorite in several cases.

The enumeral metal fluorocomplexes were characterized by X-ray, IR spectroscopy, thermogravimetry and chemical analysis techniques analogously to [1-4]. Some samples were studied by the magnetochemistry method, too [5]. X-ray and IR spectroscopic characteristics of compounds have been obtained with compositions $Cs_{3}PrF_{6}$, $Cs_{3}CeF_{6}$, $CsPrF_{4}$, $Cs_{2}MnF_{4}$, $Cs_{2}MnF_{6}$, $Cs_{3}CoF_{6}$, $CsCoF_{4}$, $Cs_{2}NiF_{4}$, $Cs_{2}NiF_{6}$, $CsCuF_{3}$, $Cs_{2}CuF_{4}$, $KCuF_{3}$, $CsAgF_{4}$, $Cs_{2}AgF_{6}$, $CsHgF_{3}$ (chemical analysis data) coincided with [3, 6-11].

Interaction of $XeF_{n}$ (n = 2, 4) with fluorocomplexes was investigated by differential thermal analysis technique (DTA) (method [12]) with excess of $XeF_{n}$ (over the stoichiometric amount). The total amount of oxidant in fluorinated samples was determined iodometrically. When amounts of $Xe^{IV}$ were determinated, a probe of substance was treated by $5\%$ NaOH solution, solid hydrolysis products were separated, solution was acidated up to pH = 4-5 and was titrated by 0.05 N sodium thiosulfate after addition of an excess of $10\%$ KI. This technique is based on the fact [13] that xenon tetrafluoride and it derivatives hydrolyze in alkali media in accordance with the equation:

$$
3\ \mathrm{Xe}^{4+}+12\ \mathrm{OH}^{-}=\mathrm{XeO}_{3}+\mathrm{Xe}+1.5\ \mathrm{O}_{2}+6\ \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}. \tag{3}
$$

This fact and a low rate of $XeO_{3}$ decomposition [13] permit to use process (3) in the chemical analysis of $Xe^{IV}$ derivatives.

Consequently, $XeF_{4}$ reacts with $Pr^{III}$ fluorocomplexes at about $200^{\circ}C$ in accordance with the following equation:

For the samples with the composition of $Cs_{3}PrF_{6}$ and $CsPrF_{4}$ (ca. 1.0 g; 1--2 h) the products of fluorination at higher temperatures ($\geq350^{\circ}C$) contain 90 and $50\%$ $Pr^{IV}$ in the $Cs_{3}PrF_{7}$ and the $CsPrF_{5}$ phases, respectively (X-ray data). Thus, it is possible that two kinds of reactions occur in hermetic reactors: the production of $RE^{IV}$ derivatives and the substitution of $RE^{III}$ fluoride from it's initial fluorocomplex. It is inter-

esting that the latter process occurs even with $Ce^{III}$ which has high capability to oxidation up to $Ce^{IV}$ [15]. $XeF_{4}$ reacts with $Cs_{3}CeF_{6}$ at about $200^{\circ}C$ (Fig. 1) with the formation of $Cs_{2}XeF_{6}$ and the mixture of $Ce^{III}$ and $Ce^{IV}$ fluorocomplexes (X-ray and chemical analysis data), the amount of $Ce^{IV}$ of the total cerium contents is about $10\%$.

Our experiments demonstrated that $RE^{IV}$ fluorocomplexes (e. g., $Cs_{3}CeF_{7}$ and $Cs_{3}PrF_{7}$), in contrast to $RE^{III}$ fluorocomplexes, do not react with $XeF_{4}$ up to $400^{\circ}C$. There are no thermal effects in DTA curves (that can be related to the interaction), and $XeF_{4}$ can be removed almost completely from the heated reagent mixtures. There are no more than 1 mass.-$\%$ of $Cs_{2}XeF_{6}$ in the heated products (chemical analysis and X-ray data). It should be noticed that $M_{x}^{I}Pr^{IV}F_{4+x}$ (at least, when $x=3$) decrease thermal stability of $Cs_{2}XeF_{6}$ noticeably. For example, pure $Cs_{2}XeF_{6}$ decomposes in the interval $380-580^{\circ}C$ with $XeF_{4}$ elimination [1, 16], but it's thermolysis occurs at $200-440^{\circ}C$ (opened system, total pressure 1 bar) in the presence of $Cs_{3}PrF_{7}$. $Cs_{3}PrF_{7}$ is quite stable under such conditions [17].

### 5. Interaction between $XeF_2$ and CsF in the presence of different metal fluorocomplexes.
As shown in [1], $XeF_2$ reacts with CsF above 350°C which results in $Cs_2XeF_6$. But this interaction (2) occurs more intensively in the presence of several fluorocomplexes, the temperature of the $Cs_2XeF_6$ synthesis being lower. Really, $XeF_2$ reacts with CsF in the presence of $CsHgF_3$ already at 180°C. If there is $CsCuF_3$ and $Cs_2CuF_4$ in the system, the formation of $Cs_2XeF_6$ is observed at 200°C. Addition of silver, praseodym, manganese, nickel, and cobalt fluorocomplexes does not influence noticeably the process characteristics of the $Cs_2XeF_6$ synthesis.

## Results and Discussions
The results presented permit to supplement and specify xenon tetrafluoride properties.

$XeF_4$ is considered [20] as a fluoroxidizer (little more powerful than $XeF_2$, but less than $XeF_6$) and as a Lewis base (weaker than $XeF_2$ or $XeF_6$). However, $XeF_4$ is capable to play the role of fluoroxidizer only when there is no Lewis base (e. g., alkali metal fluorides) in the reaction system.

For example, $XeF_4$ fluorinates $CeF_3$ to $CeF_4$ at about 200°C [12, 21]. But xenon tetrafluoride forms stable anionic fluoroxenate(IV) in the presence of a $Cs_3CeF_6$ fluorocomplex, and does practically not oxidize $Ce^{III}$ to $Ce^{IV}$ under the same conditions, i. e. $XeF_4$ is considered as a Lewis acid.

The oxidation activity of $XeF_4$ is very low at low temperatures if a Lewis base is present. So, in $Cs_3PrF_6$ $XeF_4$ does not oxidize $Pr^{III}$ to $Pr^{IV}$ at $200-250^\circ C$, although $XeF_2$ fluorinates this $Pr^{III}$ complex under the same conditions [4]. $Cs_3CeF_6$ which can be oxidized easily to $Cs_3CeF_7$ [4] (110°C) under the action of

⁴) $XeF_2$ fluorinates $Cs_2MnF_4$ and $Cs_2NiF_4$ at $150-250^\circ C$ with the formation of $Cs_2MnF_6$ and $Cs_2NiF_6$, respectively.

$XeF_{2}$ in the interaction with $XeF_{4}$ even at higher temperature $(200^{\circ} C)$ gives mainly the substitution products but not oxidation products.

Thus, it is profitable for the square-plane $XeF_{4}$ molecules to complex to $XeF_{6}^{2-}$ octahedra (which is present in the fluoroxenate(IV) structure [1]) at relatively low temperatures.

It should be noticed that a competition between two $XeF_{4}$ properties (acidic and oxidative) arises at higher temperatures $(300-400^{\circ} C)$ . Both the substitution and the oxidation can occur in dependence upon other agents properties. It was noticed that $Pr^{III}$ in $Cs_{3} PrF_{6}$ is not oxidized by $XeF_{4}$ at $200^{\circ} C$ , i. e. equation (4) being realized only. However, above $350^{\circ} C$ the products of this interaction contain already essential amount of $Pr^{IV}$ derivatives, though in the mixture with $Cs_{2} XeF_{6}$ .

The product composition of the reaction between $XeF_{4}$ and transition element alkali metal fluorocomplexes depends on the stabilities of these complexes; this reaction involves an acid-base interaction. It is not surprising that $CsPrF_{4}$ , thermodynamically less stable and in CsF eliminating is decomposed by $XeF_{4}$ at lower temperatures $(160-200^{\circ} C)$ than the more stable $Cs_{3} PrF_{6}(160-280^{\circ} C)$ does. The very stable $Cs_{3} CeF_{7}, CsCoF_{4}$ turn out to be completely inert to $XeF_{4}$ .Xenon tetrafluoride is capable to eliminate CsF from unstable $CsHgF_{3}$ and $Cs_{3} CoF_{6}$ under the formation of $HgF_{2}$ and $CsCoF_{4}$ . All these facts coincide with wellknown [22] tendencies in the change of transition element complex stabilities:
- $RE^{IV}$ complexes are essentially more stable in MF elimination than $RE^{III}$ complexes of similar stoichiometry;
- $M_{3}^{I} M^{III} F_{6}$ complexes are more stable than $M^{I} M^{III} F_{4}$ ones, if $M^{III}=R E$ ,but less stable if $M^{III}$ is a d-element;
- $Hg^{II}$ complexes are less stable than $Co^{III}$ compounds of similar composition, etc.

The results of this paper permit to specify xenon difluoride properties, too.

One feature of $XeF_{2}$ which is in contrast to the behaviour of $XeF_{4}$ , is that the former gives $M_{2} XeF_{6}$ only at rather high temperatures (above $300^{\circ} C$ ) and with much less yields than the latter. Under such conditions the oxidative fluorination process predominates over the complexing. It permits to use $XeF_{2}$ for the synthesis of such unstable compounds as $RE^{IV}$ (neodym, disprosium, tulium, samarium[17]), silver(V) [3] fluorocomplexes etc. These derivatives have been obtained under the milder conditions and for a shorter time than by using fluorine $(F_{2})$ . A striking example of such distinctions between properties of these fluorination agent, $F_{2}$ and $XeF_{2}$ , is the synthesis of $Cs_{3} DyF_{7}$ . This compound can be formed with quantitative yields only by using high pressure fluorination (fluorine pressure is about 500-1000 bar) and long expositions [23]. But using $XeF_{2} Cs_{3} DyF_{7}$ can be synthesized after the essentially shorter times and with more simple tools[4,11]. Unfortunately, such kinds of synthesis are accompanied by the participation of $XeF_{2}$ in an acid-base interactions, final products of which are fluoroxenates(IV). This behaviour is obviously due to the equilibrium:

$$2 \mathrm{XeF}_{2} \rightleftarrows \mathrm{Xe}+\mathrm{XeF}_{4} \quad(\geq 350^{\circ} \mathrm{C}) \quad[24]\tag{5}$$

Certainly, this may not exclude that the first step in the $XeF_{2}$ reactions is the addition of a $XeF_{2}$ molecule to the reagent, and then this intermediate decomposes with the formation of a more stable $XeF_{4}$ derivative, $M_{2}XeF_{6}$. But it is more convenient to discuss the corresponding $XeF_{2}$ transformations by supposing that the actual reagent, which is a response for fluoroxenate(IV) formation, is $XeF_{4}$ (see (5)), but not $XeF_{2}$.

The results of this paper presented can be well explained if one supposed that it is $XeF_{4}$ formed in reaction (5) that substitutes metal fluorides from their unstable complexes as it was discussed above for the reactions of the pure xenon tetrafluoride.

In this light the proposition made above causes no objection. The participation of $XeF_{4}$ in the interaction is obviously justified because the action of $XeF_{2}$ on $Cs_{3}CoF_{6}$ and $Cs_{3}PrF_{6}$ results in the same products as in the case when using $XeF_{4}$ directly.

One interesting result of our work is that it is probably possible to elaborate conditions under which the metal fluorocomplexes can be prepared practically free of impurities of fluoroxenates(IV). This question was examined by us in $\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{IV}}$ derivative examples. Rapid quenching of the $\mathrm{XeF}_{2}-\mathrm{Cs}_{3} \mathrm{PrF}_{6}$ system heated at $500^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ was shown to promote $\mathrm{Cs}_{3} \mathrm{PrF}_{7}$ without $\mathrm{Cs}_{2} \mathrm{XeF}_{6}$ impurities. The sharp quenching is necessary here because this method excludes the possibility of $\mathrm{CsF}$ elimination by xenon tetrafluoride that occurs when $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$ takes part in acid-base processes at $150-300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. The sharp cooling allows to pass this temperature interval very rapidly, and $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$ has no time to abstract $\mathrm{CsF}$.

Of course, for a successful synthesis of fluorocomplexes free of fluoroxenate(IV) impurities using xenon di- or tetrafluoride these complexes necessarily have to be more stable than $\mathrm{M}_{2} \mathrm{XeF}_{6}$. Otherwise $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$ will decompose these compounds with the production of $\mathrm{M}_{2} \mathrm{XeF}_{6}$. Thus, $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$ demonstrated two kinds of properties, oxidative and acidic, independently, whether it was introduced into the system initially or if it arises from $\mathrm{XeF}_{2}$. Usually it demonstrates oxidative properties above $300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and acidic at $150-300^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. The type of the reaction depends on the properties of the second reagent, but, however, both types occur simultaneously. Taking into consideration that it is typically for $\mathrm{XeF}_{6}$ [20] (as for $\mathrm{XeF}_{4}$, too) to show well expressed acidic properties, we can arrange the known binary xenon fluorides in the following order as Lewis acids:

$$\mathrm{XeF}_{6} \approx \mathrm{XeF}_{4} \gg \mathrm{XeF}_{2}.$$
