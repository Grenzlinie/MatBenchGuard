# Temperature-Dependent Tc Migration and Doping-Enhanced Retention in Magnetite from AIMD

## Problem background
Technetium-99 is a long-lived fission product that can migrate in the subsurface depending on its oxidation state. Tc(IV) is less mobile and can be incorporated into the spinel structure of magnetite (Fe₃O₄), which is stable during glass vitrification. However, at elevated temperatures Tc(IV) may re‑oxidize to volatile Tc(VII)O₄⁻, leading to poor retention. Doping magnetite with first-row transition metals (Co, Zn, Ni) has been proposed as a strategy to suppress this re‑oxidation and enhance Tc retention, but the relative effectiveness and the underlying thermodynamic driving forces need to be quantified. This task reproduces the *ab initio* molecular dynamics (AIMD) investigation of Tc migration and re‑oxidation at high temperature, with and without dopants, to compute the associated equilibrium constants and free energies.

## Approach
The work uses density functional theory (DFT)-based AIMD simulations carried out with the CP2K package. A slab model of the B‑terminated Fe₃O₄(001) surface is constructed, and one surface octahedral Fe atom is replaced by Tc. Simulations are performed at two temperatures (25 °C and 600 °C) to monitor the migration of Tc and the change in its local coordination and oxidation state. From the trajectories, the pair distribution function g(R) for Tc–O pairs is computed to extract average Tc–O distances, and the total energy difference between subsurface (Tc inside the slab) and surface configurations is evaluated. To assess doping effects, one surface Fe atom near Tc is substituted with Co, Zn, or Ni, and AIMD simulations are repeated at 600 °C. Atomic density profiles along the surface normal are computed from the trajectories, and the populations of Tc inside the surface layer (Tc(IV)) and above the surface (Tc(VII)) are integrated to obtain an equilibrium constant K_eq = [Tc_int]/[Tc_out]. The corresponding Gibbs free energy ΔG is calculated from ΔG = −RT ln K_eq at T = 873 K. Comparing ΔG across undoped and doped systems provides a quantitative measure of the retention enhancement.

## Computational parameters (AIMD)
The following parameters are derived from the experimental paper and must be used for all CP2K calculations to ensure reproducibility of the target quantities.

- **Exchange–correlation functional:** spin‑polarized PBE‑GGA with DFT+U correction on Fe 3d, Hubbard U–J = 3.5 eV.
- **Pseudopotentials:** Goedecker–Teter–Hutter (GTH) PBE pseudopotentials.
- **Basis set:** DZVP‑MOLOPT‑SR‑GTH (molecularly optimised double‑zeta valence polarisation) for all atoms.
- **Plane‑wave cutoff:** 400 Ry for the finest integration grid; relative cutoff 60 Ry.
- **k‑point sampling:** Γ‑point only (1×1×1 mesh) owing to the large supercell (384 atoms).
- **SCF convergence:** total energy converged to 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ a.u.
- **Wavefunction optimisation:** orbital transformation (OT) method with DIIS minimiser.
- **Molecular dynamics thermostat:** NVT ensemble with a Nosé–Hoover thermostat (chain length 3, time constant 100 fs), time step 1.0 fs.
- **Van der Waals correction:** not applied; the paper uses the plain PBE+U functional.
- **Spin treatment:** spin‑polarised with collinear magnetism; initial magnetic moments for Fe atoms set according to the ferrimagnetic ordering of magnetite (octahedral Fe up, tetrahedral Fe down).

These settings are identical for the undoped and all doped simulations unless otherwise noted.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Slab model generation and relaxation
- Role: process
- Action: Build a B‑terminated Fe₃O₄(001) slab model (2 × 2 × 2 supercell, 384 atoms, 12.5 Å vacuum) using the experimental bulk lattice constant a = 8.396 Å for magnetite. To create the slab, start from the bulk cubic (Fd 3 m) unit cell, replicate it to a 2 × 2 × 2 supercell, and cleave the supercell along the (001) plane. Adjust the layer termination so that the uppermost atomic layer is a B‑layer, i.e., a layer containing octahedral iron and oxygen atoms (see the structure shown in Fig. 1 of the paper). The bottom layer can be an A‑layer (tetrahedral Fe). The resulting slab should be charge‑neutral and stoichiometric as much as possible, and you may fix the bottom four atomic layers during geometry optimisation. Substitute one surface octahedral Fe atom with Tc. Perform full DFT geometry relaxation with CP2K using the computational parameters listed above, keeping the bottom four atomic layers fixed.
- Evidence: none

### Step 2: AIMD simulation undoped at 25 °C
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulation for the undoped Tc‑magnetite slab at 25 °C (NVT ensemble, Nosé–Hoover thermostat, 1.0 fs time step) for at least 20 ps, using the parameters specified above.
- Evidence: none

### Step 3: AIMD simulation undoped at 600 °C (surface Tc)
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulation for the undoped slab with Tc initially at the surface layer at 600 °C, using the same NVT ensemble and parameters, for at least 20–28 ps.
- Evidence: none

### Step 4: AIMD simulation undoped at 600 °C (subsurface Tc)
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulation for the undoped slab with Tc initially at an inner lattice site (subsurface) at 600 °C, using the same parameters, for sufficient time to sample the configuration needed for the energy comparison.
- Evidence: none

### Step 5: Extract Tc–O distances from undoped trajectories

Identify the first peak position as the average Tc–O distance. - Output file: `/app/outputs/tc_o_distances.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: {"type": "object", "required": {"tc_o_distance_25C": "float (Å)", "tc_o_distance_600C": "float (Å)"}}
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 6: Compute subsurface–surface energy difference for undoped system
- Role: scored
- Action: Extract representative configurations from the AIMD trajectories obtained in Step 3 (Tc at the surface) and Step 4 (Tc in the subsurface). For each configuration, choose a snapshot in which Tc is clearly located in the intended layer; then perform a static self‑consistent field (SCF) total‑energy calculation (or a full geometry relaxation of the slab) using the same DFT parameters as in Step 1. Calculate the total energy difference ΔE = E_sub – E_surf, where E_sub is the energy of the subsurface configuration and E_surf that of the surface configuration. Report the energy gain of the surface configuration relative to the subsurface one (a positive value means the surface is more stable). **The output must be a single valid JSON object containing exactly one field.**
- Output file: `/app/outputs/energy_difference.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: {"type": "object", "required": {"energy_difference_subsurface_surface": "float (eV)"}}
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 7: Generation and relaxation of doped slab models
- Role: process
- Action: For each dopant (Co, Zn, Ni), create a slab model by substituting one surface Fe atom close to Tc with the dopant atom. Relax the geometry with the same DFT settings as the undoped slab.
- Evidence: none

### Step 8: AIMD simulations of doped systems at 600 °C
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulations for each doped system (Co, Zn, Ni) at 600 °C using the same NVT ensemble and parameters as the undoped simulations. Equilibrate for at least 20–28 ps and use the last 10–12 ps for analysis.
- Evidence: none

### Step 9: Compute doping thermodynamics from all trajectories
- Role: scored (load‑bearing)
- Action: From the AIMD trajectories (undoped at 600 °C surface, and Ni/Zn/Co‑doped at 600 °C), compute atomic density profiles along the surface normal. To distinguish “int” (Tc(IV) inside the surface layer) from “out” (Tc(VII) above the surface), define the surface boundary as the average z‑coordinate of the topmost oxygen atoms across the trajectory. Integrate the Tc density profile on both sides of this boundary to obtain the populations [Tc_int] and [Tc_out] and compute K_eq = [Tc_int]/[Tc_out] for each system. Calculate ΔG = −RT ln K_eq with R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and T = 873 K. Report values for undoped, Ni, Zn, and Co. **The output must be a single valid JSON object containing exactly these eight fields.** Use numeric values, no units in the JSON.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/doping_thermodynamics.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: {"type": "object", "required": {"keq_undoped": "float (dimensionless)", "deltaG_undoped": "float (kJ/mol)", "keq_Ni": "float", "deltaG_Ni": "float (kJ/mol)", "keq_Zn": "float", "deltaG_Zn": "float (kJ/mol)", "keq_Co": "float", "deltaG_Co": "float (kJ/mol)"}}
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier
