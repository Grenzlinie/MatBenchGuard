# Temperature-Dependent Tc Migration and Doping-Enhanced Retention in Magnetite from AIMD

## Problem background
Technetium-99 is a long-lived fission product that can migrate in the subsurface depending on its oxidation state. Tc(IV) is less mobile and can be incorporated into the spinel structure of magnetite (Fe₃O₄), which is stable during glass vitrification. However, at elevated temperatures Tc(IV) may re-oxidize to volatile Tc(VII)O₄⁻, leading to poor retention. Doping magnetite with first-row transition metals (Co, Zn, Ni) has been proposed as a strategy to suppress this re-oxidation and enhance Tc retention, but the relative effectiveness and the underlying thermodynamic driving forces need to be quantified. This task reproduces the *ab initio* molecular dynamics (AIMD) investigation of Tc migration and re-oxidation at high temperature, with and without dopants, to compute the associated equilibrium constants and free energies.

## Approach
The work uses density functional theory (DFT)-based AIMD simulations carried out with the CP2K package. A slab model of the B-terminated Fe₃O₄(001) surface is constructed, and one surface octahedral Fe atom is replaced by Tc. Simulations are performed at two temperatures (25 °C and 600 °C) to monitor the migration of Tc and the change in its local coordination and oxidation state. From the trajectories, the pair distribution function g(R) for Tc–O pairs is computed to extract average Tc–O distances, and the total energy difference between subsurface (Tc inside the slab) and surface configurations is evaluated. To assess doping effects, one surface Fe atom near Tc is substituted with Co, Zn, or Ni, and AIMD simulations are repeated at 600 °C. Atomic density profiles along the surface normal are computed from the trajectories, and the populations of Tc inside the surface layer (Tc(IV)) and above the surface (Tc(VII)) are integrated to obtain an equilibrium constant K_eq = [Tc_int]/[Tc_out]. The corresponding Gibbs free energy ΔG is calculated from ΔG = −RT ln K_eq at T = 873 K. Comparing ΔG across undoped and doped systems provides a quantitative measure of the retention enhancement.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Slab model generation and relaxation
- Role: process
- Action: Build a B-terminated Fe3O4(001) slab model (2×2×2 supercell, 384 atoms, 12.5 Å vacuum) using the experimental bulk lattice constant. Substitute one surface octahedral Fe atom with Tc. Perform full DFT geometry relaxation with CP2K using spin-polarized PBE+GGA, Hubbard U–J=3.5 eV for Fe 3d, keeping the bottom four atomic layers fixed.
- Evidence: none

### Step 2: AIMD simulation undoped at 25°C
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulation for the undoped Tc-magnetite slab at 25°C (NVT ensemble, Nosé–Hoover thermostat, 1.0 fs time step) for at least 20 ps, using CP2K.
- Evidence: none

### Step 3: AIMD simulation undoped at 600°C (surface Tc)
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulation for the undoped slab with Tc initially at the surface layer at 600°C, using the same NVT ensemble and parameters, for at least 20–28 ps.
- Evidence: none

### Step 4: AIMD simulation undoped at 600°C (subsurface Tc)
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulation for the undoped slab with Tc initially at an inner lattice site (subsurface) at 600°C, using the same parameters, for sufficient time to sample the configuration needed for the energy comparison.
- Evidence: none

### Step 5: Extract Tc–O distances from undoped trajectories
- Role: scored
- Action: From the undoped AIMD trajectories at 25°C and 600°C (surface Tc), compute the pair distribution function g(R) for Tc–O pairs. Identify the first peak position as the average Tc–O distance. Write the two distances to the output file.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/tc_o_distances.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: {"type": "object", "required": {"tc_o_distance_25C": "float (Å)", "tc_o_distance_600C": "float (Å)"}}
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 6: Compute subsurface–surface energy difference for undoped system
- Role: scored
- Action: Using the optimized static structures extracted from the AIMD simulations (subsurface vs surface Tc configurations), compute the total energy difference. Report the energy gain of the surface configuration relative to the subsurface one (positive value means surface more stable).
- Output file: `/app/outputs/energy_difference.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: {"type": "object", "required": {"energy_difference_subsurface_surface": "float (eV)"}}
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 7: Generation and relaxation of doped slab models
- Role: process
- Action: For each dopant (Co, Zn, Ni), create a slab model by substituting one surface Fe atom close to Tc with the dopant atom. Relax the geometry with the same DFT settings as the undoped slab.
- Evidence: none

### Step 8: AIMD simulations of doped systems at 600°C
- Role: process
- Action: Run AIMD simulations for each doped system (Co, Zn, Ni) at 600°C using the same NVT ensemble and parameters as the undoped simulations. Equilibrate for at least 20–28 ps and use the last 10–12 ps for analysis.
- Evidence: none

### Step 9: Compute doping thermodynamics from all trajectories
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the AIMD trajectories (undoped at 600°C surface, and Ni/Zn/Co-doped at 600°C), compute atomic density profiles along the surface normal. Integrate the populations of Tc inside the surface layer (int) and above the surface (out) to obtain K_eq = [Tc_int]/[Tc_out] for each system. Calculate ΔG = −RT ln K_eq at T=873 K. Report values for undoped, Ni, Zn, and Co.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/doping_thermodynamics.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: {"type": "object", "required": {"keq_undoped": "float (dimensionless)", "deltaG_undoped": "float (kJ/mol)", "keq_Ni": "float", "deltaG_Ni": "float (kJ/mol)", "keq_Zn": "float", "deltaG_Zn": "float (kJ/mol)", "keq_Co": "float", "deltaG_Co": "float (kJ/mol)"}}
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier
