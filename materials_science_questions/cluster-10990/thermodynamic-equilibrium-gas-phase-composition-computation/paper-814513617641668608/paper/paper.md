## ABSTRACT

In order to guide the kinetic deposition experiments of siliconboron carbonitride ceramics (Si-B-C-N) from $\mathrm{SiCl_3CH_3-NH_3-BCl_3-H_2-Ar}$ system, the thermodynamic calculation was undertaken using Fact-sage software. Predominant condensed phases at equilibrium were $\mathrm{SiC}$, $\mathrm{Si_3N_4}$, $\mathrm{BN}$, $\mathrm{B_4C}$ and $\mathrm{C}$. The area of phase regions was affected by deposition parameters, such as temperature, total pressure, dilute $\mathrm{H_2}$ ratio, $\mathrm{n_{CH3SiCl3}/(n_{NH3} + n_{BCl3})}$ and $\mathrm{n_{NH3}/(n_{NH3} + n_{BCl3})}$. The results showed that Si-B-C-N ceramics with desired phases, such as $\mathrm{C+SiC+BN}$, could be obtained via this system by controlling above parameters theoretically. Kinetic verification at $900\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ demonstrated that Si-B-C-N ceramic could be obtained by low pressure chemical vapor deposition/infiltration from this system. The deposition was amorphous from XRD spectra and mainly constituted by $\mathrm{C-C}$, $\mathrm{Si-N}$ and $\mathrm{Si-C}$ bonds from XPS analysis, which was in agreement with the results of thermodynamic calculation in general.

## 1. Introduction

Si-based ceramics, such as silicon nitride ($\mathrm{Si_3N_4}$) and silicon carbide (SiC), have been widely used for high temperature applications because of its excellent high temperature strength, creep behavior, oxidation and thermal shock resistance [1–3]. Among them, SiCN has got widespread concerns owing to its combining properties of both $\mathrm{SiC}$ and $\mathrm{Si_3N_4}$ [4], which has better thermostability (up to $1500\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ [5]) and other multifunctional applications than $\mathrm{SiC}$ and $\mathrm{Si_3N_4}$. However, grain growth of SiCN ceramics at higher operating temperature would result in significant property degradation [6]. Fortunately, some researchers found that B-doped SiCN ceramics (Si-B-C-N) [7–9] would improve their thermostability further because turbostratic BN could act as a diffusion barrier for atoms. It has been reported that Si-B-C-N can be used steadily up to $2000\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ [10]. Meanwhile, good resistance to creep and oxidation, and high strength and modulus [11] make Si-B-C-N a promising thermostructural material. Therefore, many works have focused on Si-B-C-N ceramics for various applications. Up to now, the methods to fabricate different types of Si-B-C-N ceramics mainly includes polymer derived ceramics (PDCs) [12,13], mechanical alloying plus sintering technique [14] (MAPST), reactive magnetron sputtering (RMS) [15] and chemical vapor deposition (CVD) [16,17] with single precursor gas. But the poor density of PDCs [18], thin thickness of CVD and RMS Si-B-C-N films, and high sintering temperature of MAPST limited their further applications as continuous fiber reinforced ceramic matrix composites (CFCC). Dirk Hegemann reported $\mathrm{Si_{3.0}B_{1.0}C_{4.3}N_{2.0}}$ thin films fabricated by plasma-assisted chemical vapor deposition (PACVD) had a thickness varying between 0.7 and $1.2\,\mu\text{m}$ [17]. To obtain a kind of dense and homogeneous Si-B-C-N matrix, it is vitally necessary to develop the preparation method of low pressure chemical vapor deposition/infiltration (LPCVD/CVI) with multiple precursor gases [19,20]. Meanwhile, low pressure could reduce some unnecessary reactions and make the deposition well-distributed.

Thermodynamic analysis of the equilibrium of gaseous species, liquids and condensed phases [21,22] helps us understand the influence factors and deposition mechanism, optimize the deposition parameter. Researchers in our group studied the thermodynamic calculation of SiBC ceramics from $\mathrm{CH_3SiCl_3-BCl_3-H_2}$ [23] and SiCN ceramics from $\mathrm{SiCl_3CH_3-NH_3-H_2-Ar}$ [24] and $\mathrm{SiCl_4-CH_4-NH_3-H_2-Ar}$ [5]. The calculated results were close to the kinetic verifications and gas ratio greatly influenced the element and phase compositions of the deposited product. For

### 2. Method of calculation

Thermodynamic calculation was performed by using Factsage (Thermfact/CRCT and GTT-Technologies, Montreal, Canada and Herzogenrath, Germany). The distribution of equilibrium species and their concentrations were based on the Gibbs free energy minimum principle. The input of the programs required atomic composition of the species, concentration of the initial species, temperature and the total pressure. And the reactions among the gas species were supposed to get a rapid equilibrium. According to the research of Fischman et al., this method also suited metastable conditions where only the gas phase exists before deposition and multiphase situations [25].

The CVD phase diagrams of condensed phases under different conditions are obtained using Origin8.0 (Origin Lab Corporation, Northampton, MA), and the concentration distribution diagrams of SiC and $Si_3N_4$ are described by Matlab 7.11.0 (The Math Works, Inc, Natick, MA).

First of all, the initial conditions were confirmed, such as temperature, total pressure and the dilution $H_2$ ratio. The temperature range in this work was from $700\, ^\circ\text{C}$ to $1200\, ^\circ\text{C}$; the total pressure were 0.01 atm, 0.05 atm, 0.1 atm respectively; $n_{NH3} + n_{BCl3} = 10\, mol$, $\delta = n_{NH3}/(n_{NH3} + n_{BCl3})$ and $\gamma = n_{CH3SiCl3}/(n_{NH3} + n_{BCl3})$ ($n_X$ stood for original molar quantity of material X). Secondly, all of the thermodynamic calculations data were from the data base of Factsage software. The thermodynamic phase diagram and three-dimensional yield maps were obtained using the calculation results of Factsage software which determined the relationship between reaction parameters and condensed products. The results were presented as thermodynamic phase diagrams and three-dimensional yield maps, which were functions of temperatures and the molar ratios of input gases.

### 3. Experimental

The Si-B-C-N ceramic was prepared by LPCVD/CVI into the porous $Si_3N_4$ ceramic. Porous $Si_3N_4$ ceramic with porosity of about 30% and density of about $2.2\, g/cm^3$ were used as substrates. The fabrication details of porous $Si_3N_4$ ceramic were given elsewhere [26]. The porous $Si_3N_4$ ceramic was machined into specimens with dimensions of $22.86 \times 10.16 \times 2.16\, mm^3$ and then ultrasonically cleaned in ethanol absolute and dried in air. The specimens were made available for LPCVD/CVI reactor to deposit Si-B-C-N ceramic from methyltrichlorosilane ($CH_3SiCl_3$, MTS $\geq$ 99.99%, Jiangsu Meilan Chemical Co., Ltd., China), boron trichloride ($BCl_3 \geq$ 99.99%, Beijing Multi Technology Co. Ltd., China), ammonia ($NH_3 \geq$ 99.99%, Beijing Multi Technology Co. Ltd., China). Hydrogen ($H_2 \geq$ 99.99%, Sichuan Messer gas products Co., Ltd., China) was used as the carrier gas of MTS and dilution gas. Argon (Ar $\geq$ 99.9%, Sichuan Messer gas products Co., Ltd., China) was the dilution gas also. As-received composite ceramic were named as $Si-B-C-N/Si_3N_4$ ceramic. The typical processing parameters were confirmed according to the thermodynamic diagrams, in which $T=900\, ^\circ\text{C}$, $\gamma=1$, $\delta=0.8$ and the dilution $H_2$ ratio of 10 for 7 h at 0.01 atm. Morphology of LPCVD/CVI Si-B-C-N ceramic was examined by SEM (S-4700, Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan) at an accelerating voltage of 15 kV and about 12.5 mm working distance. The elemental composition of Si-B-C-N ceramic was analyzed by EDS (Genesis XM2, EDAX, USA) at an accelerating voltage of 15 kV and about 12.5 mm working distance. X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) for chemical and compositional analyses were recorded on the sample surface using a Thermo scientific X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS, K-Alpha; Thermo Scientific) at an accelerating voltage of 15 kV. $Ar^+$ ion etching time of 30 s was undertaken before the characterizations in order to get rid of the effect of surface adsorption. Phase crystallization analysis was conducted by XRD (XRD, D8-Advance, Bruker) with monochromatic $Cu_{K\alpha}$ radiation operated at 40 kV, from $20^\circ$ at $80^\circ$ of $2\theta$ range, and a step size of $0.02^\circ$ and 0.1 s/step.

### 4. Results and discussions

#### 4.1. The thermodynamic phase diagrams in the $CH_3SiCl_3-NH_3-BCl_3-H_2$ with various $\gamma$

MTS was used as the silicon and carbon source in $CH_3SiCl_3-NH_3-BCl_3-H_2$, which had a remarkable impact on the Si-B-C-N co-deposition. So it was important to discuss the influence of variable $\gamma$ on the thermodynamic phase diagrams of $CH_3SiCl_3-NH_3-BCl_3-H_2$, as shown in Fig. 1. According to the research of Liu et al. [27], the dilute $H_2$ ratio and total pressure were temporarily focused on 10:1 and 0.01 atm separately and their influence would be studied in the next two parts in detail. The kinds of phase fields decreased from seven to four with the increasing $\gamma$ and then remained unchanged when $\gamma$ was higher than 1. The three minor phase regions, namely $B_4C+C+BN$, $C+BN$ and $Si_3N_4+C+BN$, only existed at low $\gamma$ and disappeared with $\gamma$ higher than 1. In other words, SiC disappeared in some special conditions when the amount of MTS was low [23]. Both regions of $B_4C+SiC+BN$ and $B_4C+SiC+BN+C$ distributed in low $\delta$ area, but the appearance of $B_4C+SiC+BN$ needed higher temperature when $\gamma$ was constant. Because $B_4C$ generated in preference to carbon only when B element was sufficient and temperature was high enough [28]. The $Si_3N_4+SiC+BN+C$ region distributed with high $\delta$ and low temperature range less than about $900\, ^\circ\text{C}$ when $\gamma$ exceeded 1. BN could be obtained in all regions as long as boron source and nitride source were involved, which took priority over $Si_3N_4$ [29].

#### 4.2. The thermodynamic phase diagrams in the $CH_3SiCl_3-NH_3-BCl_3-H_2$ at different total pressure

The influence of total pressure in $CH_3SiCl_3-NH_3-BCl_3-H_2$ from 0.01 atm to 0.1 atm is shown in Fig. 2. The kinds of phase fields increased from four to five with the increasing total pressure and $C+Si_3N_4+BN$ appeared with higher total pressure of 0.1 atm and $\delta$ above 0.8. It could be seen that the area of $B_4C+SiC+BN$ region decreased by a large margin and the area of $C+SiC+BN$ region increased firstly and then decreased with the increase of total pressure. And other areas of phase fields changed accordingly. It can be concluded that lower total pressure caused the generation of $B_4C+SiC+BN$ phase field. Meanwhile higher pressure was in favor of the generation of C and $B_4C$.

The change in the diagrams could be explained by affinity of chemical reaction and Gas reaction balance theory [30]:

$$A = -\text{Tln}(J_P/K^\Phi) \tag{1}$$

$$J_P = \Pi(P_B/P^\Phi)^{V_B} \tag{2}$$

$$\text{mA}_{(g)} + \text{nB}_{(g)} = \text{xC}_{(s)} + \text{yD}_{(g)} \tag{3}$$

$$K^\Phi = P^y_D/(P^y_A \times P^y_B) \tag{4}$$

where A is chemical affinity, the negative partial derivative of Gibbs energy at constant pressure and temperature, being the extent of reaction. It is positive for spontaneous reactions. Jp is the product of the reactants partial pressure. V_B is the chemical reaction rate, which is negative value when it relates to reactants, is positive value when it relates to resultants. $K^\Phi$ is equilibrium constant. P_B is the partial pressure of each gas component. P is standard gas pressure.

When $J_P < K^\Phi$, A > 0, reaction can be carried out spontaneously.
$J_P = K^\Phi$, A = 0, reaction achieves balance.
$J_P > K^\Phi$, A < 0, reaction could not be carried out (its reverse reaction possible to carry out).

In MTS/H₂ mixtures, the Si—C bond in the MTS was the weakest and first broke resulting in free radicals $\text{CH}_3^*$ and $\text{SiCl}_3^*$ shown in Eq. (5) [31]. Then, the overall chemical reactions and the possible products in this system were Eq. (6)-(13). The main chemical reactions were as follows:

$$\text{CH}_3\text{SiCl}_{3(g)} \rightarrow \text{CH}_{3(g)}^* + \text{SiCl}_{3(g)}^* \tag{5}$$

$$\text{CH}_{3(g)}^* \rightarrow \text{C}_{(s)} + 1.5\text{H}_2 1.5\text{H}_{2(g)} \tag{6}$$

$$\text{SiCl}_{3(g)}^* + \text{CH}_{3(g)}^* \rightarrow \text{SiC}_{(s)} + 3\text{HCl}_{(g)} + \text{H}_{2(g)} \tag{7}$$

$$3\text{SiCl}_{3(g)}^* + 4\text{NH}_{3(g)} \rightarrow \text{Si}_3\text{N}_{4(s)} + 9\text{HCl}_{(g)} + 3\text{H}_{2(g)} \tag{8}$$

$$\text{BCl}_{3(g)} + \text{NH}_{3(g)} \rightarrow \text{BN}_{(s)} + 3\text{HCl}_{(g)} \tag{9}$$

$$4\text{BCl}_{3(g)} + \text{CH}_{3(g)}^* + 4.5\text{H}_{2(g)} \rightarrow \text{B}_4\text{C}_{(s)} + 12\text{HCl}_{(g)} \tag{11}$$

$$2\text{SiCl}_{3(g)}^* + \text{H}_{2(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{SiHCl}_{3(g)} \tag{10}$$

$$2\text{SiCl}_{3(g)}^* + 2\text{HCl}_{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{SiCl}_{4(g)} + \text{H}_{2(g)} \tag{12}$$

$$2\text{NH}_{3(g)} \rightarrow \text{N}_{2(g)} + 3\text{H}_{2(g)} \tag{13}$$

### 4.4. Three-dimensional yield maps of Si-B-C-N ceramics with various $\gamma$

The amount of different products could not be obtained by the thermodynamic phase diagrams, so that the study on concentration distributions of the condensed-phases was necessary. Fig. 4 shows the three-dimensional yield maps of $\text{BN-Si}_3\text{N}_4$ (a, c, e) and $\text{SiC-C-B}_4\text{C}$ (b, d, f) with variable $\gamma$ via $\text{CH}_3\text{SiCl}_3-\text{NH}_3-\text{BCl}_3-\text{H}_2-\text{Ar}$ (a, b) $\gamma$=0.2, (c, d) $\gamma$=1.0, (e, f) $\gamma$=1.8 (dilution $\text{H}_2$ ratio of 10:1; $P$=0.01 atm). The amount of BN increased firstly and then decreased linearly with the increasing $\delta$. When $\text{n}_{\text{NH}3}$ was equal to $\text{n}_{\text{BCl}3}$ ($\delta$=0.5), the amount of BN reached its maximum. Meanwhile, temperature had no influence with that because $700\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ was high enough for the generation of BN [32]. When the amount of $\text{NH}_3$ was higher than that of $\text{BCl}_3$, $\text{NH}_3$ would react with $\text{SiCl}_3^{\ast}$ to generate $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$ ($\delta\geq0.5$) as shown in Eq. (8). The amount of $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$ increased firstly and then remained almost unchanged with the increasing $\delta$ and $\gamma$. The amount of $\text{B}_4\text{C}$ reached its maximum when the temperature was $1200\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $\delta$ was 0. The amount of $\text{B}_4\text{C}$ increased firstly and remained at 2 mol with the increasing $\gamma$. When the temperature was above $900\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $\delta$ exceeded 0.5, the amount of SiC was equal to that of MTS. When temperature was below $900\,^{\circ}\text{C}$, part of $\text{SiCl}_3^{\ast}$ was consumed to generate $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$ (Eq. (8)) and the amount of MTS was equal to the sum of SiC and $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$. When $\delta$ was less than 0.5, part of $\text{CH}_3^{\ast}$ was consumed by $\text{BCl}_3$ to generate $\text{B}_4\text{C}$ (Eq. (10)) and the amount of SiC decreased accordingly. On the whole, the amount of SiC showed an upward trend with the increasing $\gamma$. When part of $\text{SiCl}_3^{\ast}$ was consumed by $\text{NH}_3$, excessive $\text{CH}_3^{\ast}$ would react with each other to produce the C phase (Eq. (6)) and the amount of C was closely linked that of $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$. When temperature was $700\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $\delta$ was 1, the amount of C and $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$ reached their maximum. In other words, $\gamma$ had a strong influence on the yield of SiC, $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$, $\text{B}_4\text{C}$ and C.

### 4.7. Experimental verification

The Si-B-C-N ceramic deposited on Si₃N₄ substrates were compact and continuous as shown in Fig. 7(a), which was the same as other Si-based ceramics fabricated by CVD [24,25]. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy survey spectrum and energy dispersive spectroscopy pattern (Fig. 7(b and c)) of Si-B-C-N ceramic showed that the obtained film consisted of five elements, namely Si, B, C, N and O. And O was probably from surface adsorption and the content was too low to be ignored.

XRD patterns in Fig. 8(a) showed the deposited Si-B-C-N ceramic was amorphous because its diffraction peaks were nearly the same as that of the Si₃N₄ substrate. This result was caused by their low deposition temperature which was much lower than the crystallization temperature of SiBCN ceramics. Meanwhile, this amorphous structure also guaranteed that as-received SiBCN ceramics had excellent thermostability according to previous report [10].

High resolution XPS spectra of C1s, N1s, and Si2p core levels were analyzed to characterize the structure and bonding nature of the obtained ceramic as shown in Fig. 8(b-d). The binding energies were calibrated, using C1s peak (284.6 eV) of carbon impurities as a reference. The peaks were convoluted after background subtraction, using a mixed Gaussian-Lorentzian function. It could be found that as-received ceramic were predominantly constituted by C-C (at 284.6 eV)[33], Si-C(100.4 eV)[34], Si-N(101.4 eV-102 eV)[35], and B-N (398.0 eV) [36] bonds. Comparing the intensity of these bonds, N-B bond was stronger than N-Si bond, which was consistent with the corresponding yield map (Fig. 4(c, d)). Meanwhile, the intensity of Si-C and C-Si bonds were separately weaker than Si-N and C-C bonds. These trends show that the real yield of SiC was a little less than the predicting yield.

The detailed deposition process was explained in the section of thermodynamic analysis, so we pay more attention to the comparison between the thermodynamic and experimental results.

## 5. Conclusions

According to thermodynamic phase diagram, predominant con- densed phases at equilibrium were $SiC, Si_3N_4, BN, B_4C$ and $C$, and the area of phase regions was affected by different deposition param- eters. Low $n_{CH3SiCl3}/(n_{NH3}+n_{BCl3})$ primarily limited the deposition of $SiC$. High temperature was against the generation of $C$ and $Si_3N_4$. High total pressure made for deposition of $Si_3N_4$ but played the opposite role to $C$. High dilute gas $H_2$ ratio facilitated the deposition of $B_4C$ but restrained that of $C$. High $n_{NH3}/(n_{NH3}+n_{BCl3})$ promoted the deposition of $Si_3N_4$ but went against that of $B_4C$, which also was the only factor for the generation of BN. The yield of $B_4C$ and $SiC$ was influenced mainly by $n_{CH3SiCl3}/(n_{NH3}+n_{BCl3})$ and the sum of $SiC$ and $Si_3N_4$ was similar to that of $n_{CH3SiCl3}$. So Si-B-C-N ceramics with desired phases could be obtained in theory by controlling the above parameters from $SiCl_3CH_3-NH_3-BCl_3-H_2-Ar$. Then this result was demonstrated by the experimental verification at $900^{\circ}C$. The depo- sition was amorphous and mainly constituted by $C-C, B-N, Si-N$ and $Si-C$ bonds from XPS analysis, which agreed with the results of thermodynamic calculation in general.
