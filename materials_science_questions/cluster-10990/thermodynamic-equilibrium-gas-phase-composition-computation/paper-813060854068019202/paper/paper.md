# NONSTOICHIOMETRY OF MERCURIC IODIDE CRYSTALS. THERMODYNAMIC APPROACH

A. Rossberg $^{\dagger}$ and M. Piechotka*

Laboratory for Solid State Physics, ETH Technopark,
CH-8005 Zurich, Switzerland

(Refereed)
(Received July 15, 1996; Accepted July 29, 1996)

## ABSTRACT
Thermodynamic approach has been applied to calculate the equilibrium vapor pressure of free iodine above the nonstoichiometric solid phase of $\alpha-HgI_{2}$ in closed systems which are used in vapor growth of large mercuric iodide crystals. The results show that if free iodine is not visible in the gas phase during the growth, a slight excess of mercury can be expected in the as-grown crystals.

KEYWORDS: A. electronic materials, A. halides, B. crystal growth, D. phase equilibria, D. thermodynamic properties

## INTRODUCTION
Nonstoichiometry of mercuric iodide has been the subject of investigations because it is believed to strongly affect the performance of the devices (X-ray and $\gamma$-ray spectrometers) made from these crystals. The first attempt to establish the homogeneity range of the nonstoichiometric solid $\alpha-HgI_{2}$ phase has recently been made by Hermon et al. (1). Free iodine vapor has frequently been observed in large evacuated ampoules during the growth of mercuric iodide crystals even though iodine had not been added to the starting material. Furthermore, the experience was that the intensity of the violet coloration of the gas phase may vary with growth time. Nason et al. (2) measured these variations by means of optical absorption and demonstrated that the partial pressure of iodine in the growth system may

# THERMODYNAMIC CALCULATIONS

For vapor growth of large mercuric iodide crystals, 0.5-1.0 kg of mercuric iodide starting material is placed in an ampoule of approximately 0.5-11 volume, evacuated and sealed off. During the crystal growth, the ampoule is heated to temperature within the range of 90-125°C and the actual temperature difference between the source material and the growing crystal (supercooling) is kept within a fraction of a centigrade. For thermodynamic calculations, we assume that the system is very close to equilibrium at a given growth temperature T, i.e., the solid phase of mercuric iodide (source material and crystal) with a certain homogeneity range and composition x = (I/Hg) [mole/mole] is in equilibrium with the gas phase. Temperature gradients, which are usually applied in order to maintain the morphological stability of the growing crystal/gas interface, are neglected.

Under these assumptions, the composition of the gas phase containing five species is determined by the following four independent reactions:

$$\mathrm{HgI}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}=\mathrm{Hg}_{(\mathrm{g})}+\mathrm{I}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \quad \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{p} 1}=\left(\mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{Hg}} \cdot \mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{I} 2} / \mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{HgI} 2}\right) \tag{1}$$

$$\mathrm{I}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}=2 \mathrm{I}_{(\mathrm{g})} \quad \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{p} 2}=\left(\mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{I}}^{2} / \mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{I} 2}\right) \tag{2}$$

$$\mathrm{Hg}_{(\mathrm{g})}+\mathrm{I}_{(\mathrm{g})}=\mathrm{HgI}_{(\mathrm{g})} \quad \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{p} 3}=\left[\mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{HgI}} /\left(\mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{I}} \mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{Hg}}\right)\right] \tag{3}$$

$$\mathrm{HgI}_{2(\mathrm{~s})}=\mathrm{HgI}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \quad \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{p} 4}=\mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{HgI} 2} \tag{4}$$

The vapor pressure of mercuric iodide is equal to its equilibrium value $p_{HgI2}(T)$, whereas the partial pressure of iodine $p_{I2}$ is determined by the stoichiometric composition x of the solid phase ($HgI_{2+x}$). Because there are two components (Hg and I) and two phases (solid and gas) in the system, the latter has two degrees of freedom according to the Gibbs phase rule, i.e., temperature and composition of the solid phase x. The phase boundaries on the I-rich and Hg-rich side of the stoichiometric point are set by the coexistence of solid $HgI_{2(s)}$ with the precipitated solid iodine $I_{2(s)}$ on one side and by the coexistence of solid $HgI_{2(s)}$ with solid mercurous iodide, $Hg_2I_{2(s)}$ on the other. The partial pressure of iodine is determined by the equilibrium vapor pressure of solid iodine on the I-rich side and by reaction 5 on the Hg-rich side:

$$2 \mathrm{HgI}_{2(\mathrm{~s})}=\mathrm{Hg}_{2} \mathrm{I}_{2(\mathrm{~s})}+\mathrm{I}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \tag{5}$$

As in the case of some other nonstoichiometric oxide systems (5), we assume that $p_{I2}$ is the following function of x:

$$\log p_{\mathrm{I} 2}(\mathrm{x}, \mathrm{T})=\mathrm{z}_{1}+\mathrm{z}_{2} \cdot \mathrm{x}+\mathrm{z}_{3} / \mathrm{T}+\mathrm{z}_{4} \cdot \mathrm{x} / \mathrm{T} \tag{6}$$

At constant temperature, logarithm of the partial pressure of iodine is a linear function of solid composition x within the nonstoichiometric homogeneity range. If temperature does not vary much, say less than by 5 K, one can further assume that coefficients $z_{1}... z_{4}$ remain constant. Thus, for two temperatures $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$, such that $|T_{1}-T_{2}| \leq 5 ~K$, one can write four equations of type (6) and solve them with respect to coefficients $z_{1}... z_{4}$. Iteration over the temperature range of interest (90-160°C) results in a numerical relationship between the partial pressure of iodine, temperature, and composition of the solid phase.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Figure 2 shows the equilibrium partial pressure of the components of the gas phase as a function of temperature for the starting material with stoichiometry varying within the homogeneity range. The equilibrium constants $K_{p 1} ... K_{p 4}$ have been calculated using the thermodynamic data compiled by Knacke et al. (6). Contrary to results of thermodynamic calculations of Lyakh (3), who had to neglect the stoichiometry variation in the solid phase, mercuric iodide does not always become the major component of the gas phase in our case. For the iodine-rich starting material, iodine prevails in the gas phase within the entire temperature range (Fig. 2a). For nearly stoichiometric mercuric iodide, there is a temperature range around approximately $120^{\circ} C$ where the partial pressure of iodine exceeds that of mercuric iodide (Fig. 2b). Even for mercury-rich samples, iodine partial pressure is comparable to that of mercuric iodide within this temperature range (Fig. 2c). In the latter case, the partial pressure of mercury vapor exceeds that of iodine at temperatures above approximately $150^{\circ} C$ or below $100^{\circ} C$ . The partial pressure of the other two components $(HgI_{(g)}$ and $I_{(g)})$ is negligibly small in all cases, in agreement with the results of Lyakh (3).

Figure 3 shows the partial pressure of iodine as a function of temperature for varying stoichiometry x of solid mercuric iodide. Phase boundaries with solid/liquid iodine, solid mercurous iodide and between $\alpha-HgI_{2}$ and $\beta-HgI_{2}$ are also drawn. The visibility threshold of free iodine vapor is approximately 0.04 mbar according to Nason et al. (2).

Taking this value into account (dotted horizontal line in Fig. 3), one can see that at typical growth temperatures (above $105^\circ\text{C}$) iodine vapor should be seen in systems containing either iodine-rich or stoichiometric starting mercuric iodide material. Even for samples with a slight mercury excess, iodine should also be seen in a narrow temperature range around $120^\circ\text{C}$. Thus, optically assessed presence of (small amount) of iodine vapors during the growth of mercuric iodide does not always point out to the iodine excess in the resulting crystals. Furthermore, a variation in time of the iodine partial pressure in a growth system does not necessarily mean that the stoichiometry of the growing crystal varies too. This would be the case in a strictly isothermal system, whereas during the growth either the temperature of the source or that of the crystal has to be changed in order to maintain a constant growth rate (7).

# CONCLUSIONS

Thermodynamic calculations indicate that if free iodine vapor is not visible in closed evacuated growth ampoules containing solid mercuric iodide at temperature $T=105-130^\circ\text{C}$, mercuric iodide crystals should contain mercury excess. In order to grow crystals of stoichiometric composition, the partial pressure of iodine should be maintained within the range of 0.02-4.5 mbar depending on growth temperature varying within the range 104-$120^\circ\text{C}$. Furthermore, assuming that no foreign gases are present in the system, mercuric iodide vapor constitutes the major component of the gas phase over solid mercuric iodide only for samples which are Hg-rich within the homogeneity range. In all other cases, free iodine is the predominant component of the gas phase.
