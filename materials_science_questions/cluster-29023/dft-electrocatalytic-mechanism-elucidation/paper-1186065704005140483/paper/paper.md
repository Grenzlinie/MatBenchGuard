# Isomeric Π Conjugated Covalent Organic Frameworks with Tunable Nitrogen Topologies for Integrated Gold Adsorption and Photoreduction

Quanquan Yang, Junyi Han, Shengxu Li, Mengwei Chen, Sheng Wang,* and Tao Zhang*

**Abstract:** Gold recovery from electronic waste (e-waste) presents a significant materials challenge, as conventional systems lack structural tunability, exhibit poor metal selectivity, and fail to integrate adsorption and photoreduction functionalities effectively. Herein, we present a topology-guided strategy based on two isomeric $\pi$-conjugated covalent organic frameworks (COFs) (v-2D-COF-Dz and v-2D-COF-Pz), incorporating diazine (ortho-N) and pyrazine (para-N) units, respectively, for integrated gold adsorption and photoreduction. The nitrogen topology not only stabilizes the $\pi$-conjugated backbone but also significantly modulates the frameworks' optoelectronic and redox properties. Notably, v-2D-COF-Dz exhibits a markedly higher $\text{Au}^{3+}$ adsorption capacity (2465 versus 1854 $\text{mg g}^{-1}$), attributed to enhanced interfacial electron localization and directional charge transfer to $\text{Au}^{3+}$ centers. Both frameworks achieve over 99% gold extraction from real e-waste leachates under visible-light irradiation. Spectroscopic characterization and DFT analysis reveal that nitrogen topology governs metal coordination strength and electronic coupling, enabling precise $\text{Au-Cl}$ bond activation. This work establishes a nitrogen-topology-directed design paradigm for light-driven, COF-based precious metal recovery systems.

## Introduction

Gold is one of the most valuable and strategically important metals in electronic waste (e-waste),$^{[1-4]}$ with concentrations exceeding those in natural ores by more than 30-fold.$^{[5,6]}$ Despite this, its recovery remains inefficient, primarily due to the limitations of conventional methods such as cyanidation and electrorefining,$^{[7-9]}$ which are hindered by environmental toxicity, high energy consumption, and poor metal selectivity.$^{[10-12]}$ These challenges, particularly when dealing with complex e-waste leachates, underscore the urgent need for alternative approaches that can enable efficient, selective, and sustainable gold recovery.$^{[13-15]}$

Covalent organic frameworks (COFs)$^{[16-18]}$ have emerged as promising materials for metal ion recovery,$^{[19-21]}$ owing to their high surface areas,$^{[22-24]}$ tunable functionalities,$^{[25]}$ and exceptional chemical stability.$^{[16]}$ In comparison to conventional porous materials such as zeolites, carbon nanomaterials, and metal-organic frameworks (MOFs),$^{[26-30]}$ COFs exhibit distinct advantages, including superior adsorption capacity, enhanced selectivity, and better stability in acidic e-waste leachates.$^{[31-33]}$ Furthermore, COFs allow for the precise integration of metal-binding sites and optoelectronic units, offering flexibility in the design of charge transfer pathways and host-guest interactions,$^{[34,35]}$ which in turn optimize metal ion adsorption and recovery efficiency.$^{[36]}$ These features make COFs particularly effective for metal ion recovery and adsorption in the context of e-waste leaching.$^{[37]}$

However, the photocatalytic performance of COFs in gold recovery is hindered by several challenges.$^{[38-40]}$ First, many imine-linked COFs suffer from poor light utilization due to weak visible-light absorption and disrupted $\pi$-conjugation, resulting in low electron mobility and inefficient charge separation.$^{[41,42]}$ Second, the coordination environments, particularly those involving nitrogen-containing groups like pyridine or imidazole, are poorly defined,$^{[43]}$ and the role of nitrogen site topology in $\text{Au}^{3+}$ anchoring and reduction remains unclear.$^{[19,44]}$ Third, the lack of synergy between light response and coordination environments limits photocatalytic efficiency, preventing effective coupling between light-induced charge separation and gold ion anchoring.$^{[45,46]}$

In this work, we strategically designed two isomeric $\pi$-conjugated COFs: v-2D-COF-Dz (with ortho-configured diazine units) and v-2D-COF-Pz (with para-configured pyrazine units). These frameworks, enhanced by vinylene linkages, exhibit exceptional chemical stability and improved visible-light absorption, enabling them to function as both selective adsorbents and efficient photocatalysts for $\text{Au}^{3+}$ under ambient conditions. By precisely modulating the nitrogen site topology,$^{[47]}$ we explored its effect on $\text{Au}^{3+}$ coordination, $\text{Au-Cl}$ bond activation, and the photoreduction of gold species. ## Results and Discussion
### Preparation and Characterization of v-2D-COFs

v-2D-COF-Dz and v-2D-COF-Pz were synthesized via a hydroxy-aldehyde condensation reaction at 200 °C, involving the reaction of 4,4',4''-(1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triyl)benzaldehyde (TFPT) with 3,6-dimethylpyridazine (Dz) or 2,5-dimethylpyrazine (Pz) (Figure 1a). Upon completion of the reaction, the resulting products were extensively washed with tetrahydrofuran (THF), methanol, and acetone to remove residual reactants and byproducts. The purified solids were subsequently dried under vacuum at 120 °C for 2 h, affording v-2D-COF-Dz and v-2D-COF-Pz as powders with yields of 96.4% and 92.7%, respectively. Both COFs were found to be insoluble in common organic solvents, further indicating their strong structural stability.

The crystal structures of the v-2D-COFs were determined by comparing experimental and simulated powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns. As shown in Figure 1b,c, the experimental PXRD patterns of v-2D-COF-Dz and v-2D-COF-Pz exhibit strong diffraction peaks at 2.97° and 3.01°, respectively, along with several secondary broadened peaks. These peaks correspond to the (100) facet, indicating the formation of an ordered structure along the stacking direction. To further analyze the stacking configuration, AA and AB stacking models of the two v-2D-COFs were constructed and optimized using Materials Studio (Figures S1 and S2). Structural analysis confirmed that both v-2D-COFs adopt an AA stacking mode, in which two monolayers overlap within the unit cell. The corresponding simulated PXRD patterns align well with the PM (CS-1) space group model, validating the accuracy of the AA stacking structure (Tables S1 and S2). Further Pawley refinement demonstrated good agreement between the experimental and simulated diffraction patterns, yielding low $R_{wp}$ and $R_p$ values: 2.92% and 2.30% for v-2D-COF-Dz, and 2.47% and 2.01% for v-2D-COF-Pz. Additionally, the difference plots confirm that the refined diffraction patterns closely match the experimental PXRD data, further supporting the structural assignment.

The successful formation of the v-2D-COFs structures was confirmed by Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy and solid-state $^{13}$C nuclear magnetic resonance ($^{13}$C NMR) spectroscopy. In the FT-IR spectrum of the v-2D-COFs, the appearance of a peak at $\sim$1629 cm$^{-1}$, attributed to C=C stretching, and the significant reduction in intensity at $\sim$1700 cm$^{-1}$, corresponding to the –CHO group in TFPT, indicate a high degree of condensation via the alcohol-aldehyde reaction (Figure 2a). Furthermore, the solid-state $^{13}$C NMR spectrum (Figure 2b) exhibits chemical shifts at 135, 148, and 155 ppm, which can be assigned to the formation of C=C bonds and the incorporation of diazine and pyrazine groups. Nitrogen adsorption isotherm analysis at 77 K revealed type IV isotherms, characteristic of microporous materials. The Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) surface areas were determined to be 1567.54 m² g⁻¹ for v-2D-COF-Dz and 1211.92 m² g⁻¹ for v-2D-COF-Pz (Figure 2c,d). Pore size distributions, calculated using non-local density functional theory (NLDFT), were primarily in the range of 1.9 nm, consistent with the pores in the AA-stacked structure. Further characterization using grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) provided insight into the structural order of the interlayer stacking and in-plane framework of the v-2D-COFs (Figure 2e,h). The two-dimensional GIWAXS patterns exhibit distinct scattering rings, indicative of highly ordered layered stacking, further validating the successful synthesis of the v-2D-COFs, and confirming the accuracy of their periodic structure.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were employed to investigate the morphology of the v-2D-COFs. SEM images (Figure S3) reveal a morphology consisting of micron-sized, uniform aggregates, while TEM images (Figures S4a and S5a) depict nanometer-sized, sheet-like structures characteristic of both v-2D-COFs. High-resolution TEM (HR-TEM) images further confirm the high crystallinity of the COFs, as evidenced by distinct lattice fringes (Figure S6). Additionally, energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) mapping demonstrates the uniform distribution of carbon and nitrogen throughout the COF framework (Figures S4b and S5b).

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was conducted to evaluate the thermal stability of v-2D-COF-Dz and v-2D-COF-Pz. Both materials exhibit excellent thermal stability, with less than 3% weight loss up to 450 °C in an argon atmosphere (Figure S7). Furthermore, v-2D-COF-Dz and v-2D-COF-Pz retain their crystallinity and structural integrity even after exposure to various organic solvents (e.g., THF, acetone), strong acids, strong bases, and organic solvent conditions (Figure S8), highlighting their exceptional chemical stability.

The adsorption kinetics of v-2D-COFs-Light fit well with the pseudo-first-order model (Figure S11), suggesting that chemisorption is the rate-limiting step, involving strong electronic interactions between $Au^{3+}$ ions and active sites on the COF framework. In contrast, the low adsorption rates observed and the pseudo-second-order kinetic fits under dark conditions indicate that physisorption alone is insufficient for effective gold capture and that light activation is essential for driving the reduction pathway. This conclusion is further supported by adsorption isotherm analysis (Figure 3c). Under visible light, v-2D-COF-Dz exhibited an exceptionally high maximum adsorption capacity ($Q_\text{m} > 2500\ \text{mg·g}^{-1}$), along with excellent Langmuir model fitting ($R^2 = 0.993$) (Figure S12), indicating monolayer adsorption on a homogeneous array of high-affinity binding sites. Notably, both COFs showed a dramatic decrease in $Q_\text{m}$ values in the absence of light, reinforcing the critical role of photoactivation in enabling efficient gold adsorption and reduction.

Furthermore, to investigate the morphological changes of COFs after gold adsorption, TEM analysis was conducted on v-2D-COFs following exposure to $Au^{3+}$ under visible-light irradiation and dark conditions. Compared to the pristine COFs which exhibited a smooth layered morphology, the treated samples clearly displayed well-dispersed dark contrast nanoparticles (5–15 nm) on the surface (Figures 3d, S13a, S15a, and S16a), suggesting the formation of gold nanoparticles. HRTEM further confirmed this, revealing distinct lattice fringes with an interplanar spacing of 0.235 nm, which corresponds to the (111) plane of face-centered

cubic (FCC) metallic Au (Figures 3e, S14, S15c, and S16c). Elemental mapping from EDX analysis shows a high degree of spatial overlap between Au and C/N signals on the COF surface (Figures 3f, S13b, S15b, and S16b), indicating that gold species are uniformly anchored within the COF framework. In addition, X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis provides strong structural evidence for the reduction of $Au^{3+}$. After adsorption, distinct diffraction peaks appear at $38.2^{\circ}, 44.4^{\circ}, 64.6^{\circ}$, and $77.6^{\circ}$ in the v-2D-COFs@Au samples, corresponding to the (111), (200), (220), and (311) planes of FCC metallic gold, respectively (Figure S17). Together, these results confirm that $Au^{3+}$ ions are effectively captured and in situ reduced to crystalline gold nanoparticles, which are uniformly distributed across the COF surface, validating the proposed adsorption-reduction mechanism.

To evaluate the practical application of v-2D-COFs adsorbents for selective Au extraction under realistic conditions, we explored their performance in recovering Au from electrical waste and electronic equipment (e-waste) (Figure 3g). Two kinds of Au leaching methods were compared: N- bromosuccinimide (NBS) oxidation and aqua regia leaching. Experimental results showed that the Au(III) leaching efficiency in the NBS system was significantly lower than that of the conventional aqua regia method, primarily due to the weaker oxidation ability of NBS and the stability of the complexing ligand. Consequently, aqua regia leaching was selected to ensure efficient Au recovery (Figure S18). After immersing the v-2D-COFs in e-waste leachate for 6 h under visible light irradiation, both v-2D-COF-Dz and v-2D-COF- Pz exhibited high selectivity toward Au, achieving removal efficiencies of 99.5% and 98.4%, respectively. In contrast, their adsorption efficiencies for Ni (6.1% and 7.9%) and Cu (13.8% and 12.5%) were substantially lower (Figure 3h), highlighting the preferential affinity of both materials for Au over Ni and Cu in multi-metal systems. This superior performance can be attributed to two primary factors: electrostatic interactions and the redox properties of the COFs. Under acidic conditions, the positively charged v-2D-COFs readily adsorb $AuCl_{4}^{-}$ anions through electrostatic attraction, whereas Cu and Ni exist predominantly as chloro-complexes (e.g., $CuCl_{4}^{2-}$ and $NiCl_{4}^{2-}$) in the chloride-rich acidic environment of aqua regia. These species exhibit negligible uptake under our experimental conditions, in contrast to $AuCl_{4}^{-}$ which shows strong affinity (Figure S19). This behavior is attributed to both electrostatic effects—since divalent anionic complexes such as $CuCl_{4}^{2-}$and $NiCl_{4}^{2-}$experience stronger repulsion from the positively charged COF surface—and redox considerations, as the reduction potential of $Au^{3+}/Au^{0}$ (+1.50 V) is much higher than that of $Cu^{2+}/Cu^{0}$ (+0.34 V) and $Ni^{2+}/Ni^{0}$ (-0.25 V). Consequently, the preferential adsorption and photoreduction of Au are thermodynamically and kinetically favored.

In addition, the v-2D-COFs exhibited superior performance, surpassing most other COFs (Table S3), with excellent recyclability and structural durability—retaining over 99% of their initial $Au^{3+}$ adsorption capacity after five consecutive adsorption-desorption cycles in real e-waste leachate using thiourea as the eluent (Figure S20). FTIR spectra collected before and after cycling (Figure S21) showed no discernible changes in characteristic vibrational modes, confirming the structural integrity and chemical stability of the COF frameworks throughout repeated use. These results confirm that V-2D-COFs exhibit outstanding selectivity and strong structural stability for Au recovery.

### Mechanism Exploration of v-2D-COFs

The photochemical properties of the v-2D-COFs were systematically investigated by UV-visible absorption spectroscopy (UV-vis) and ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS). As shown in Figure 4a, both v-2D-COF- Dz and v-2D-COF-Pz exhibit broad absorption across the visible to near-infrared region, with optical band gaps of 1.53 and 2.05 eV, respectively (Figure S22). Based on the UPS spectra and energy band alignment (Figure 4b,c), the valence band maxima $(V_{BM})$ were determined to be - 5.63 eV for v-2D-COF-Dz and -6.30 eV for v-2D-COF-Pz, and the corresponding conduction band minima $(C_{BM})$ were calculated to be -4.10 and -4.25 eV. These $C_{BM}$ values lie above the standard redox potential of $Au^{3+}/Au^{0}$ (-5.94 eV versus vacuum), indicating that direct reduction of $Au^{3+}$ by conduction band electrons is thermodynamically unfavorable. However, efficient reduction reactions can still occur due to the strong coordination-induced orbital coupling between the COF framework and the adsorbed $AuCl_{4}^{-}$ ions. Additionally, no $H_{2}O_{2}$ or HClO was detected in the reaction solution using standard colorimetric assays (Section S4-1), suggesting minimal oxidative side reactions induced by photogenerated holes.

Femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy (fs-TAS) was employed to investigate the excited-state dynamics of the isomeric COFs. As shown in the two-dimensional transient absorption map, v-2D-COF-Dz exhibits clear photoinduced responses, with a ground-state bleach (GSB) around 575 nm and broad excited-state absorption (ESA) extending into the near-infrared region (Figure S23a). Time-resolved spectra at different delays further confirm the persistence of these features, while the kinetic trace at 575 nm is well fitted with a monoexponential decay model, giving a lifetime of $2.36 \pm 0.32$ ps (Figure S23b,c), indicative of short-lived excited states capable of undergoing charge separation and interfacial transfer. In sharp contrast, v-2D-COF-Pz displayed negligible transient signals under identical conditions, suggesting ultrafast relaxation or inefficient excited-state formation. These results provide direct spectroscopic evidence of the superior excited-state dynamics and charge separation ability of v-2D-COF-Dz.

Furthermore, v-2D-COF-Dz exhibits a notably lower intrinsic photoluminescence (PL) intensity than v-2D-COF- Pz (Figure S24), suggesting that nonradiative recombination is more dominant and that charge carriers are more mobile in v-2D-COF-Dz, which is beneficial for transferring photoexcited electrons to $Au^{3+}$ species and accelerating the reduction reaction. In addition, charge density difference analysis (Figure S25) provides further insight into the photoinduced electron behavior within the COF framework. Additional evidence is provided by Raman spectroscopy; upon visible light irradiation, the characteristic vibrational peaks corresponding to C-H bending ($1279\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$) in the COF framework significantly diminish (Figure S29), indicating photoinduced reorganization of the $\pi$-conjugated system and effective electron transfer from the backbone to the adsorbed $\mathrm{Au}^{3+}$ ions. To further elucidate the adsorption mechanism, electrostatic potential (ESP) mapping and density functional

theory (DFT) adsorption energy calculations were performed. While the ESP maps (Figure 4f) indicate high negative charge density around nitrogen atoms—suggesting electrostatic attraction toward $AuCl_4^-$—DFT results reveal that $AuCl_4^-$ preferentially adsorbs on carbon sites adjacent to nitrogen atoms in v-2D-COF-Dz (-0.79 eV) and on nitrogen sites in v-2D-COF-Pz (-0.63 eV) (Figure 4g). Additional calculations confirm that in v-2D-COF-Dz, carbon sites bind more strongly than nitrogen sites (Figure S30), indicating that electronic polarization and orbital coupling render adjacent carbon atoms the primary anchoring sites for $AuCl_4^-$. The appearance of a C–Au peak in the C 1s spectrum (Figure S31) further confirms the involvement of carbon atoms in Au binding. Importantly, spectroscopic evidence (Figures 4e and S32) reveals clear N–Au coordination signals after adsorption and reduction. We therefore propose a sequential binding process: i) $AuCl_4^-$ ions first adsorb onto carbon sites with stronger binding affinity; ii) upon partial reduction and dechlorination, the gold species redistribute and establish coordination with nitrogen lone pairs, which stabilize the reduced Au nanoparticles. This reconciles the DFT and spectroscopic results, highlighting a synergistic C–N dual-site mechanism in which carbon atoms mediate initial adsorption while nitrogen atoms dominate subsequent stabilization.

To elucidate the atomic-level reduction mechanism of gold (Figure 5a), Au $L_3$-edge X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) and extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) analyses were conducted using three reference standards. These techniques were employed to investigate the oxidation states and local coordination environments of gold in v-2D-COFs@Au. As shown in Figure 5b, the normalized white line intensity at 11921 eV in v-2D-COFs@Au lies between that of $AuCl_3$ and metallic Au, indicating the presence of partially reduced $Au^{x+}$ species ($0 \leq x \leq 3$). Further Fourier-transformed EXAFS (FT-EXAFS) spectra (Figure 5e) reveal prominent peaks at ~2.27 and ~2.86 Å, corresponding to Au–Cl and Au–Au coordination, respectively, referencing $AuCl_3$ and Au foil. These observations, supported by EXAFS fitting (Figures 5c,f, S33, and S34), suggest a progressive conversion from Au–Cl bonds to metallic Au–Au interactions during the gold deposition process. Thus, the combined XANES and EXAFS results confirm that $AuCl_4^-$ adsorption is accompanied by Au–Cl bond dissociation and the formation of gold nanoparticles.

To further probe the coordination environment, wavelet transform (WT) analysis was applied to the EXAFS data (Figures 5d,g and S34). v-2D-COF-Dz exhibits a strong WT signal at $k \approx 3$–10 $\mathring{A}^{-1}$ and $R \approx 2.0$–3.0 $\mathring{A}$, indicating a locally dense coordination structure with a higher number of donor atoms (e.g., C, N, or Cl). In contrast, v-2D-COF-Pz shows pronounced signal attenuation at high k values, implying a more disordered or weakly bound environment. EXAFS fitting further reveals (Table S4) that the Au–C/N coordination number in v-2D-COF-Dz reaches 3.2, significantly higher than that in v-2D-COF-Pz (2.3), suggesting that carbon atoms, in addition to conventional nitrogen lone-pair donors, act as effective anchoring sites. This enhanced coordination capability is likely attributed to orbital hybridization and $\pi$-electron delocalization between conjugated $sp^2$-carbon and Au ions, which not only stabilizes the local gold species but also promotes the dissociation of Au–Cl bonds and facilitates nanoparticle nucleation.

Collectively, these results indicate that the gold reduction process in v-2D-COFs follows a "dual-site coordination (C/N) + $\pi$-orbital coupling enhancement" mechanism: nitrogen provides classic lone-pair electron donation for electrostatic coordination, while carbon contributes via conjugated $\pi$-orbitals to strengthen binding through orbital overlap and electronic delocalization. This synergistic configuration effectively modulates the local coordination environment, accelerates the redox transformation of $Au^{3+}$ to $Au^0$, and enhances both the reduction efficiency and structural stability of the supported gold species.

## Conclusion
Combining comprehensive experimental analysis and theoretical simulations, we uncover the underlying mechanism of $Au^{3+}$ capture and photoreduction on nitrogen-topology-regulated v-2D-COFs. Upon visible-light irradiation, the $\pi$-conjugated framework promotes efficient delocalization and migration of photogenerated electrons, enabling rapid electron transfer toward adsorbed $AuCl_4^-$ species. Simultaneously, nitrogen topology governs selective coordination: in v-2D-COF-Dz, ortho-positioned diazine units enhance local electron density at $C_{3,6}$ sites, driving strong C–Au and N–Au interactions; in contrast, v-2D-COF-Pz presents weaker binding due to para-substituted pyrazine units. These spatially resolved coordination sites not only stabilize Au precursors but also facilitate directional charge transfer, triggering Au–Cl bond dissociation and leading to the formation of crystalline Au nanoparticles. The synergy between selective adsorption, topology-driven orbital coupling, and photoinduced electron redistribution enables efficient $Au^{3+}$ removal (>99%) with high structural integrity and reusability. This work establishes a structure–adsorption–function paradigm in COF-based photocatalysts and offers a modular design strategy for sustainable metal recovery and controlled nanocrystal growth under mild conditions.
