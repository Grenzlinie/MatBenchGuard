# Cobalt Porphyrin Supported on Graphene/Ni (111) Surface: Enhanced Oxygen Evolution/Reduction Reaction and the Role of Electron Coupling

Tianwei He, Chunmei Zhang, Geoffrey Will and Aijun Du*

School of Chemistry, Physics and Mechanical Engineering, Queensland University of Technology, Gardens Point Campus, QLD 4001, Brisbane, Australia

*Corresponding author: aijun.du@qut.edu.au

## Graphical abstract

![](./images/812833126291079168_2.jpg)

## Research Highlights

- The binding energies of the oxygenated intermediates can be modulated by introducing electron coupling between substrate and metal porphyrin.

- The graphene acts as a buffer layer to make the interaction between Co-porphyrin and Ni (111) surface more moderate.

- The Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni(111) system exhibits excellent bifunctional catalytic performance with 0.31 V for ORR and 0.38 V for OER.

### Abstract
Metal porphyrins have received sustained attention due to their remarkable catalytic properties. However, the catalytic activities of porphyrins are still moderate for oxygen reduction /evolution (ORR/OER) due to the high binding energies between the catalyst and reactant. Herein, we propose a novel strategy to modulate the binding energies of the oxygenated intermediates by introducing electron coupling between the substrate and the metal porphyrin. Our results reveal remarkable enhancement of OER/ORR performance when the Co-porphyrin molecule is supported on the graphene/Ni (iii) substrate. The ORR/OER overpotentials can be greatly reduced, in particular for ORR from 0.6 V to 0.31 V. The enhanced ORR/OER activity of Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni system stems from the electronic coupling between the catalyst and the substrate that modulates the binding strength between catalytic active sites and the reaction intermediates. Our study represents an efficient strategy to design low-cost, high performance, bifunctional electrocatalysts for the application in rechargeable metal-air batteries.

Keywords: Porphyrins, electronic coupling, substrate, oxygen reduction, oxygen evolution, overpotential

### 1. Introduction
Rechargeable metal-air battery has triggered intensive research as a promising efficient electrochemical energy conversion and storage technologies for future electric vehicles and other energy-demand devices [1, 2]. However, the overall efficiency of these devices has been severely affected by two critical chemical reactions: oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER). The sluggish kinetics in the electrocatalytic reduction and evolution of molecular oxygen is the major source of the cell efficiency loss [3, 4]. Thus, to discover and optimize novel catalysts has been the driving force for academic and industrial investigation all over the world. Although the noble metal (oxide) (e.g., Pt, Au, Ir, RuO₂)

catalysts have been used as efficient electrocatalysts [5, 6], the limited resources and high cost of these precious metal-based catalysts have hampered their widespread and large-scale applications. Therefore, to develop alternative catalysts that are cost-effective, earth-abundant and can exhibit catalytic activities comparable to or even better than that of noble metal catalysts for ORR and OER is of paramount significance.

Transition-metal porphyrins with Fe, Co or Ni atoms are able to strongly interact with small molecules such as $O_2$, $CO_2$ and NO. These complexes are regarded as promising molecular catalysts in many biological and chemical oxidation reactions [7-13]. Metal porphyrins have been used as molecular electrocatalysts for the oxygen reduction reaction since the 1960s. Their ORR activity and stability have been demonstrated to be close to that of commercially available Pt/C catalysts [14, 15], but with a lower cost compared to noble metals. Recently, metal porphyrins have attracted considerable interest and attention as promising catalysts for ORR/OER [16, 17]. However, the catalytic activity of porphyrins is still moderate due to the high binding energies between the catalyst and the intermediates. Great research efforts have been devoted to improve their catalytic activity [17, 18]. One of the most popular methods is the addition of different ligands to the porphyrins to tune the binding energies of all reaction intermediates and thus the OER/ORR activity. For instance, Jakub *et al.* found that the hangman motif can stabilize the oxygen intermediate, whereas the addition of $C_6F_5$ groups will reduce the binding energy of all reaction intermediates[19]. Michael *et al.* demonstrated that the turnover frequencies which correlated with the ORR overpotential can be modulated by changing the ancillary ligands of porphyrins[20]. Dilek *et al.* reported that by adding β-octafluoro and meso-pentafluorophenyl substituents, the cobalt hangman corrole can be an active water splitting catalyst[21]. Despite the aforementioned processes, the catalytic activity of metal porphyrins remains unsatisfied as the overpotentials for OER/ORR are still very high. Metalloporphyrins deposited on metal surfaces have been widely investigated in experiments due to their expected use as molecular spintronic materials in nanodevices. Wende and Ballav investigated the possibilities to realize the vision of a molecular spintronic device of Fe-porphyrin molecules and ultrathin Co and Ni film adsorbed on Cu (100) substrates [22, 23]. Kuch *et al.* revealed the magnetic coupling of porphyrin molecules through graphene in both experiment and theoretical calculations [24]. The coupling behaviours of organic molecules and substrates can have significant impact on the properties of a system, such as electronic structure [25-27] and chemisorption[28]. Yao's group had experimentally investigated the

layer-by-layer growth of Ni/Co-porphyrin complexes on reduced graphene oxide sheets and shown good performance towards both ORR and OER [29]. However, they cannot explain how the reduced graphene oxide substrate affects the active site and improved the catalytic activity. What's more, the poor conductivity of reduced graphene oxide compared with graphene will lower the electron transfer efficiency during the reaction processes. The above studies inspire us to explore how electronic coupling can modulate the binding between metal-porphyrins and reactants, potentially leading to enhanced ORR and OER performance.

In this work, we for the first time proposed the use of metal porphyrins supported on the experimentally realized graphene /Ni (III) as bifunctional catalysts for ORR and OER. The overpotentials at standard conditions and the potential-determining steps are predicted by using density function theory calculations. Among them, the Fe, Co and Ni porphyrin complexes are the most popular ones due to the non-noble metal catalysis. Our calculation results shows that the Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni (III) system is found to exhibit superior overall electrocatalytic activity towards both ORR and OER with low overpotentials, comparable to or even better than the state-of-art noble metal electrocatalysts (such as Pt/C, Ir/C and $RuO_2$). The electronic coupling between the Co-porphyrin and substrate plays a critical role in tuning the binding energies of the intermediates on metal-porphyrin. The graphene acts as a buffer layer that decouple the molecules from metal substrate and allow the interaction become moderate for ORR and OER. As a result, the Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni (III) system was demonstrated to be a high efficient, low-cost electrocatalyst with potential application in rechargeable metal-air batteries. Our work sheds new insights into the design of novel ORR/OER catalysts by tuning electron coupling between catalyst and substrate.

## 2. Computational details

We conducted all the calculations with the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP)
code[30, 31]. The exchange-correlation potential was described by the generalized gradient
approximation (GGA) functional of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (PBE). The plane-wave cut-
off energy was set to be 500 eV. A single gamma ($\Gamma$) point mesh of 1×1×1 in reciprocal space
was used for all structural relaxations. The geometries were optimized until the energy and
the force were converged to $10^{-6}$ eV and 0.001 eV/Å, respectively. Blöchl's all-electron, frozen-
core projector augmented wave (PAW) method[32] was used to represent nuclei and core
electrons. In all calculations, the van der Waals interaction was described by using the
empirical correction in Grimme's scheme, i.e., DFT+D3[33].

In the acid media the overall OER could be described in Eq. (1), which happens on a metal air
battery during charge:

$$
2H_{2}O \rightarrow O_{2} + 4H^{+} + 4e^{-} \tag{1}
$$

In this model, we assume that the OER reaction proceeds via $4e^{-}$ pathways since the ORR on
CoN$_x$ proceeds mainly along $4e^{-}$ processes [34-36]. The elementary reaction steps are listed as
below:

$$
H_{2}O\ (l) + * \rightarrow OH^{*} + e^{-} + H^{+} \tag{a}
$$

$$
OH^{*} \rightarrow O^{*} + e^{-} + H^{+} \tag{b}
$$

$$
H_2O(l) + O^{*} \rightarrow OOH^{*} + e^{-} + H^{+} \tag{c}
$$

$$OOH^{*} \rightarrow O_{2}(g)+e^{-}+H^{+} \tag{d}$$

The * stands for an adsorption site on catalysts. l and g refer to liquid and gas phases, respectively.

The ORR is the reverse reactions of OER[37, 38] which occurs on the cathode of the fuel cell and the air battery in discharge [39, 40]:

$$O_{2}+4 H^{+}+4 e^{-} \rightarrow 2 H_{2} O \tag{2}$$

In acidic solution, the ORR is processed via the four-electron-transfer pathway as shown below:

$$*+O_{2}(g)+e^{-}+H^{+} \rightarrow OOH^{*} \tag{e}$$

$$OOH^{*}+e^{-}+H^{+} \rightarrow H_{2} O(l)+O^{*} \tag{f}$$

$$O^{*}+e^{-}+H^{+} \rightarrow OH^{*} \tag{g}$$

$$OH^{*}+e^{-}+H^{+} \rightarrow H_{2} O(l)+* \tag{h}$$

The computational Gibbs free energy difference ($\Delta G_{OH*}, \Delta G_{O*}, \Delta G_{OOH*}$) of each step involving one electron transfer can be expressed as follows:

$$\Delta G=\Delta E+\Delta Z P E-T \Delta S+\Delta G_{U}+\Delta G_{p H} \tag{3}$$

The $\Delta E$, $\Delta ZPE$, and $\Delta S$ is the different energy, zero-point energy, and entropy of the reaction, respectively[41]. $\Delta E$ is the electronic energy change and also termed adsorption energy, and can be defined as follows:

$$\Delta E\left(OOH^{*}\right)=E\left(OOH^{*}\right)-E(*)-\left[2 E\left(H_{2} O\right)-3 / 2 E\left(H_{2}\right)\right] \tag{4}$$

$$\Delta E\left(O^{*}\right)=E\left(O^{*}\right)-E(*)-\left[E\left(H_{2} O\right)-E\left(H_{2}\right)\right] \tag{5}$$

$$\Delta E\left(OH^{*}\right)=E\left(OH^{*}\right)-E(*)-\left[E\left(H_{2} O\right)-1 / 2 E\left(H_{2}\right)\right] \tag{6}$$

$E(OOH^{*}), E(O^{*}), E(OH^{*}), E(*)$, and $E(H_{2})$ are the electronic energies of *OOH, *O, *OH, *, $H_{2}O$ and $H_{2}$. These values were obtained by DFT calculations. The values of $\Delta ZPE$, $\Delta TS$ were calculated by DFT and the standard thermodynamic data[42], respectively. $\Delta G_{U}=-eU$, in which $U$ is the potential related to the standard hydrogen electrode. $\Delta G_{pH}$ is the corrected Gibbs free energy of $H^{+}$ depending on the concentration. $\Delta G_{pH}=-kTln10*pH$ . The free energy changes of the first to fourth OER steps could be expressed as: $\Delta G_{a}=\Delta G_{OH*}, \Delta G_{b}=\Delta G_{O*}-\Delta G_{OH*}$ , $\Delta G_{c}=\Delta G_{OOH*}-\Delta G_{O*}$ and $\Delta G_{d}=4.92-\Delta G_{OOH*}$ . For ORR steps: $\Delta G_{e}=\Delta G_{OOH*}-4.92, \Delta G_{f}=\Delta G_{O*}-\Delta G_{OOH*}, \Delta G_{g}=\Delta G_{OH*}-\Delta G_{O*}$ and $\Delta G_{h}=\Delta G_{OH*}$.

The overpotential ($\eta$) can be calculated to evaluate the performance for OER and ORR:

$$\eta^{OER} = max\{\Delta G_a, \Delta G_b, \Delta G_c, \Delta G_d\}/e - 1.23 \tag{7}$$

$$\eta^{ORR} = max\{\Delta G_e, \Delta G_f, \Delta G_g, \Delta G_h\}/e + 1.23 \tag{8}$$

## 3. Results and discussions

Fig.1 presents the atomic structure of Co-porphyrin, Co-porphyrin supported on graphene, Co-porphyrin support on Ni (III) and Co-porphyrin supported on graphene and Ni (III). Metalloporpyrins contain the $\text{N}_4$-metal structure and have been investigated widely as electrocatalysts for the redox reactions. Overpotential U in ORR/OER can be generally considered as a descriptor[43]. A catalyst with lower U would have better performance from a thermodynamic point of view. First, the catalytic activity of the original metal porphyrin for both OER and ORR were comparatively investigated and the calculated results were summarized in Fig. 2. The blue and the green dotted lines represent the largest free energy steps for OER and ORR, respectively. It can be found that Co-porphyrin molecule exhibits relatively high performance to both OER and ORR among the three metal porphyrins. The largest free energy step of OER for Co-porphyrin is the transformation of $O^*$ to $OOH^*$ which has a low overpotential of 0.41 V. The performance is comparable to Ru-based catalysts[44]. However, the activity for ORR is not as good as OER. The overpotential for ORR is 0.6 V and the largest free energy step is the transformation of $O_2$ to $OOH^*$. For the other two metal porphyrins, the OER and ORR overpotentials are large and not favourable to catalyse redox reactions. The above results demonstrate that the catalytic activity of Co- porphyrin was fairly good, but the performance still needs to be enhanced, especially for the ORR.

![](./images/812833126291079168_4.jpg)

Figure 2 The free energy diagrams of OER and ORR on Fe-porphyrin, Co-porphyrin and Ni-porphyrin at the U=o V. The blue and the green dot lines are the rate limiting step for OER and ORR, respectively.

In order to further lower the overpotential, we employed different substrates to support the metal porphyrin as shown in Fig. 1b-d, the substrate is expected to modify the binding energies of all reaction intermediates and thus tune the OER/ORR activity. We calculated the ORR and OER pathways of these four catalysts (see Fig.1) and compared the ORR and OER catalytic activity with each other. Fig. 3 presents the Gibbs free energy diagram along the ORR and OER pathways. The ideal catalyst with a barrier of 1.23 eV for each step is also included for comparison (black thick line). The potential-determining step of each type of catalyst with the largest Gibbs free energy step determines the overpotential. An ideal catalyst should be able to facilitate water oxidation just above the equilibrium potential. It needs all the four charge transfer steps to have Gibbs free energies at the equilibrium potential of 1.23 V. So the corresponding Gibbs free energies $\Delta G(OH^*)$, $\Delta G(O^*)$ and $\Delta G(OOH^*)$ should be 1.23 eV, 2.46 eV and 3.69 eV, respectively. Fig.4 shows the optimized adsorption configurations and energies of the oxygenated intermediates on Co atom. For the Co-porphyrin supported on graphene, no covalent bonds between the molecule and the substrate are established, and the Co-porphyrin sit over 3.0 Å (as shown in Fig.4a) above the graphene plane and the interlayer electronic coupling is weak. Thus the Gibbs free energies for the four reaction steps remain unchanged compared with that of pristine Co-porphyrin. Based on the free energy diagram for ORR, the largest free energy step for the pristine Co-porphyrin and Co-porphyrin supported on graphene are the first step $(O_2 \rightarrow HOO^*)$ with overpotential of 0.60 V. The high overpotential for origin Co-porphyrin is mainly due to the too weak binding strength between $OOH^*$ and the Co atom. When the Co-porphyrin molecule supported on Ni (111) surface, the strong interactions between them is apparent and the distance between them is 2.18 Å, as shown in Fig.4b. As we can see, the binding strength of $O^*$ and $OOH^*$ are all enhanced when Co-porphyrin is supported on Ni substrate. The stronger binding energy of $OOH^*$ intermediate leads to the ORR overpotential decreased from 0.60 V to 0.46 V. While the OER overpotential increased from 0.41 V to 0.79 V because of the too strong binding energy of $O^*$. However, when the graphene layer is inserted between the Co-porphyrin and the Ni (111) surface, the binding strength of the oxygenated intermediates become moderate. The binding energies of each step for the Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni system are very close to the ideal catalyst and exhibited the best ORR and OER catalytic. The largest free energy step for ORR is the fourth step $(OH^* \rightarrow H_2O)$ with the minimum overpotential of only 0.31 V. For the OER activity, the Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni system is identified to have the minimum overpotential of 0.38 V, and the largest free energy step is the transformation of $OH^*$ to $O^*$.

The calculated overpotential based on DFT for ORR and OER are even lower than the well-studied Pt containing catalysts (~ 0.4 V for OER on PtO₂-rutile and ~ 0.45 V for ORR on Pt[43], indicating that Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni system may serve as efficient bifunctional catalyst for air battery.

As alluded above, the graphene combined with Ni (Ⅲ) surface as the substrate to support the Co-porphyrin has significantly improved the activity of ORR and OER. The overpotentials had decreased from 0.60 V to 0.31 V for ORR and 0.41 V to 0.38 V for OER, respectively due to the interaction between the substrate and Co-porphyrin affecting the binding strength between the intermediates (OH*, O* and OOH*) and the active Co atom. The greatly-enhanced catalytic activity could be attributed to the synergetic effects among Co-porphyrin, graphene and Ni (Ⅲ) surface such as the electronic structure change of Co-porphyrin, which may facilitate the catalysis of the ORR/OER. To shed light on the interactions between the graphene/Ni (Ⅲ) substrate and Co-porphyrin molecule, we calculated the electronic structure of Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni system to show the interlayer electronic-coupling effect between the substrate and catalyst. Fig.5a shows the calculated charge density difference ($\Delta\rho(r)$) of a Co-porphyrin molecule on graphene supported on Ni based on the following equations[45, 46]:

$$
\Delta\rho(r) = \rho(r)_{AB} - \rho(r)_A - \rho(r)_B \tag{9}
$$

Where $\rho(r)_{AB}$ represents the charge density of the Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni (Ⅲ) system, $\rho(r)_A$ refers to the charge density of the substrate and $\rho(r)_B$ is the charge density of the Co-porphyrin molecule. It can be seen that after coupling Co-porphyrin with graphene/Ni, the charge density in hybrid's interlayer was redistributed in the form of an apparent electron transfer among Co-porphyrin, graphene and top-layer Ni. The localized electrons accumulation at the central Co ion site is supposed to enhance the ORR and ORR catalytic activity. The graphene layer, on one hand, decouples the molecule from the substrate and passivates the Ni surface. On the other hand, it also mediates the interaction between the substrate and the Co-porphyrin molecule. So the active Co site can bind the oxygenated intermediates neither too strong nor too weak. Similar organic molecules, such as Fe phthalocyanines, have been also found decoupled electronically from the substrate on a graphene-covered metal surface, while their molecular electronic properties can be maintained[47, 48]. The electronic coupling and exchange interactions between the Co-porphyrin and the substrate can be further understood from the DOS analyses. Fig.5b presents the density of states for the (Co-porphyrin)-Co, (Co-porphyrin)-C, (Co-porphyrin)-N, (Graphene)-C and surface layer Ni. It can be observed that the Ni and graphene states

hybridize and therefore have a stronger chemical interaction than that between graphene and Co-porphyrin, which is shown in the charge density difference plot. We also can observe the overlap between the N states and Co states at the energy range from -5.5 eV to -2.0 eV, which repents strong hybridization between N and Co atoms. There is no hybridization visible between Ni and Co, which excludes a direct interaction between Co-porphyrin and Ni substrate. Instead, the exchange is indirect and mediated via the graphene states. The graphene π-bond sheet mediates a weak super-exchange between the Ni and the pyrrolic nitrogens, the densities of the N couple through direct exchange to the central ion of the Co-porphyrin molecule. The electronic structure analysis confirmed that the electronic coupling between the catalyst and the substrate which plays a crucial role in the porphyrin's catalytic activity and could be mixed and matched to make the optimal catalyst.

## Conclusions

In summary, we have investigated the ORR/OER catalytic activity for metal porphyrin support on the substrates by DFT calculations. Our results show that the Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni (III) catalyst exhibits high activity towards both ORR and OER ($\eta^{OER}$=0.38 V, $\eta^{ORR}=0.31$ V). The low overpotential is comparable to and even lower than that of the Pt/C based catalysts for ORR and the Ru-based catalyst for OER. We find that different substrates can have significant impact on the catalytic activity of metal-porphyrin. Co-porphyrin molecule supports on single graphene layer had no effect on the ORR/OER activity. Whereas Co-porphyrin molecule supports on the Ni (III) surface, the ORR activity can be improved due to the enhanced binding strength of the oxygenated intermediates, while the OER activity bent

down. However, by inserting a single layer of graphene between the Co-porphyrin and Ni, the binding energies of the oxygenated intermediates can be tune to very close to the ideal values. The high activity of Co-porphyrin/graphene/Ni system stems from the electronic coupling between the catalyst and the substrate. Graphene acts as a passivation layer to allow the binding strength between catalytic active site and the reaction intermediates become more moderate. Our study gives new insights into the role of electrical coupling between Co-porphyrin catalyst and the substrate on the ORR/OER catalytic activity. Meanwhile, as the Yao's group had experimentally realised the layer-by-layer growth of Ni-/Co-porphyrin complexes on reduced graphene oxide (rGO) sheets, we believe that the conceptual ideas presented here can be a way forward for the future design of electrocatalysts with improved, moderate interactions between catalysts and the support materials.
