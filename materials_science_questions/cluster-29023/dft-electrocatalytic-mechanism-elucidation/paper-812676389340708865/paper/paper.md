## ABSTRACT

Crystal facets can affect the catalytic decomposition of ammonium perchlorate, but the underlying mechanisms have long remained unclear. Here, we use the nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes of $Co_3O_4$ catalysts exposing {110}, {111} and {100} facets as model systems to investigate facet effects on catalytic AP decomposition. The peak temperature of high temperature decomposition (HTD) process ($T_{HTD}$) of AP by nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes $Co_3O_4$ decrease from 437.0 °C to 289.4 °C, 299.9 °C and 326.3 °C, respectively, showing obvious facet effects. We design experiments about AP decomposition under different atmospheres to investigate its mechanism and verify that the accumulation of ammonia ($NH_3$) on AP surface can inhibit its decomposition and that the facet effects are related to the adsorption and oxidation of $NH_3$. The binding energies of $NH_3$ on the {110}, {111} and {100} planes calculated via density functional theory (DFT) are -1.774 eV, -1.638 eV, and -1.354 eV, respectively, indicating that the {110} planes are more favorable for the adsorption of $NH_3$. Moreover, the {110} planes are readily to form Co–N–O structure, which benefits the further oxidation of the $NH_3$.

### 1. Introduction

Ammonium perchlorate is a significant oxidizer for composite solid propellants (CSPs) (Yan et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2014). The decomposition behavior of AP is crucial to the performance of propellants since it accounts for 60-90 wt% in CSPs (Zhang and Meng, 2016; Boldyrev, 2006). The peak temperature of high temperature decomposition (HTD) process ($T_{HTD}$) is the most important parameter to characterize the decomposition behavior of AP. Because, lower the $T_{HTD}$ of AP can shorten the ignition delay time and promote the burning rate of propellants (Zhang and Meng, 2016; Cao et al., 2019). Adding thermal combustion catalysts has been considered as an efficient method to decrease the $T_{HTD}$ of AP (Chaturvedi and Dave, 2011). Typical transition metal oxides, such as cobalt oxide ($Co_3O_4$) (Zhou et al., 2014; Yu et al., 2018; Alizadeh-Gheshlaghi et al., 2012), zinc oxide (ZnO) (Sun et al., 2008; Tang et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2018), ferric oxide ($Fe_2O_3$) (Cao et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2008), titanium dioxide ($TiO_2$) (Reid et al., 2007; Mahinroosta, 2019), copper oxide (CuO) (Chen et al., 2008; Sharma et al., 2015) and so on (Chandru et al., 2012; Wang et al., 2005; Zhao et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2018; Richter et al., 2002), exhibit outstanding catalytic activity in the thermal decomposition of AP, which can reduce the $T_{HTD}$ of AP by more than 50 °C. Much effort has been devoted to improve catalytic activity of related metal oxides and the demonstrated methods includes morphology regulation (Zhou et al., 2014; Yu et al., 2018; Sun et al., 2008; Zhao et al., 2016; Luo et al., 2016; Kapoor et al., 2009), particle refinement (Alizadeh-Gheshlaghi et al., 2012; Mahinroosta, 2019; Sharma et al., 2015; Chandru et al., 2012) and material cladding (Eslami et al., 2016; Ma et al., 2012; Mahdavi et al., 2018).

Current researches have shown that the catalytic performances of metal oxides are highly sensitive to their morphologies. Some investigations (Zhou et al., 2014; Yu et al., 2018; Luo et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2018) demonstrated that different morphologies of particles, such as cube, wire, flower shape, sea-urchin shape, exhibit great difference in catalytic performance for decomposition of AP. For example, Chen et al. (2018) reported that The $T_{HTD}$ of AP is decreased to 297.5 °C and 356.6 °C with the addition of sea-urchin shaped $MnO_2$ and linear $MnO_2$, which indicates that the former is more reactive. Xu et al. (2008) prepared nanorods, irregular particles and octahedrons of $Fe_2O_3$ via hydrothermal method. They found that the $T_{HTD}$ of AP with the addition of rod, irregular and octahedral $Fe_2O_3$ is 387.5 °C, 419.6 °C and 426.4 °C respectively, which means that the catalytic performance of $Fe_2O_3$ nanorods is best. These phenomena about the different catalytic performance can be attributed to the facet effects, which is widely existed in other catalytic reactions as well (Sun et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2014; Ji et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2017; Hu et al., 2008; Xiao et al., 2012). Different shapes of catalysts generally expose various crystal facets, which contain variant arrangement manner of surface atoms and possess different bonding abilities with the reactants or intermediate molecules (Hu et al., 2008; Xiao et al., 2012).

The effects of catalyst morphologies on their catalytic performances in AP decomposition has attracted remarkable attentions and various metal oxides with different shapes have been synthesized to improve the catalytic activity in the AP decomposition. However, very less is known about facet effects on AP decomposition. Tang et al. (2014) attempted to study facet effects on AP decomposition by the density functional theory (DFT) calculations. They synthesized ZnO crystals with different percentages of the exposed (0001) facets and found that the catalytic activity of the ZnO crystals was improved with the increased exposure of (0001) facets. They recognized the accumulation of the perchloric acid ($HClO_4$) on the crystal surface was the key factor to inhibit the decomposition of AP. Based on DFT calculations, the different catalytic activity was attributed to the variant abilities of adsorbing and dissociating $HClO_4$ on different facets. However, this conclusion is worthy of discussion, because the key factors affecting the decomposition of the AP are still in debate (Jacobs and Whitehead, 1969; Boldyrev et al., 1970; Yuan et al., 2014; Tolmachoff and Essel, 2019; Li et al., 2012; Galwey and Monamed, 1984). Normally, the $HClO_4$ is thought to be unstable under the AP decomposition temperature, which can rapidly decompose into other gaseous intermediates and leave the AP crystal surface (Cummings and Pearson, 1964). Instead, many investigations have confirmed that the ammonia ($NH_3$) can inhibit the AP decomposition (Jacobs and Whitehead, 1969; Boldyrev et al., 1970) while the oxygen ($O_2$) show contrary effects (Yuan et al., 2014). Thus, the roles of gaseous intermediates and products in the reaction still need to be confirmed to give a clear understanding of facet effects on the decomposition of AP.

Here, we selectively synthesize different shapes of $Co_3O_4$ nanocatalysts exposing uniform crystal planes. The catalytic activities of the as-prepared catalysts, which are represented by the $T_{HTD}$ and activation energy for decomposition of AP, are measured and compared. To further investigate the catalytic mechanism, experiments have been designed and carried out to check the role of gaseous intermediates in the decomposition process of AP. The interaction between the crystal planes and gaseous intermediates is further investigated via DFT calculations to reveal the mechanism of facet effects on the decomposition of AP.

### 2. Experimental section

Three reported methods (Xiao et al., 2012; Li et al., 2018) were utilized to prepare the precursors of $Co_3O_4$ nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes and the precursors were further treated into final products with the same method. $Co_3O_4$ nanorods precursor was synthesized using the solvothermal method (Xiao et al., 2012). A mixed solution was formed with 10 ml of aqueous ammonia ($NH_3H_2O$, 25 wt%; Beijing Chemical Works) and 25 ml of ethylene glycol ($HOCH_2CH_2OH$, 95 %; Beijing Chemical Works). Then, 1.5 ml of sodium carbonate ($Na_2CO_3$, 99.8 %; Beijing Chemical Works) aqueous solution (1 M) was added under stirring. Afterwards, 5 ml of cobalt nitrate hexahydrate (Co $(NO_3)_2$·$6H_2O$, 99.0 %; Tianjin Fuchen Chemical Reagent Factory) aqueous solution (1 M) was added into the mixture with 20 min stirring. At last, the mixture was transferred into a 50-mL Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave, which was sealed and heated up to 170 °C for 17 h to obtain the $Co_3O_4$ nanorods precursor.

$Co_3O_4$ nanosheets precursor was synthesized with the solvothermal method (Xiao et al., 2012). 2.4 g of Co$(NO_3)_2$·$6H_2O$ and 2 g of Polyvinyl Pyrrolidone PVP, MW:40,000; Coolaber was dissolved into a mixture solution of 10 ml deionized water and 10 ml of ethyl alcohol $CH_3CH_2OH$, 99.7 %; Beijing Chemical Works). Then 50 ml of sodium hydroxide (NaOH, 96.0 %; Beijing Chemical Works) aqueous solution (0.4 M) was added into it. Finally, the mixture was transferred into a 100-mL Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave, which was sealed and heated up to 200 °C for 10 h to obtain the $Co_3O_4$ nanosheets precursor.

$Co_3O_4$ nanocubes precursor was synthesized via solvothermal method as well (Li et al., 2018). 11.64 g of Co$(NO_3)_2$·$6H_2O$ was dissolved into 40 ml of deionized water and then 0.4 g of NaOH was added into it under vigorous stirring. The mixture was transferred into a 50-mL Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave, which was sealed and heated up to 180 °C for 5 h to obtain $Co_3O_4$ nanocubes precursor.

All as-prepared precursors were filtered and washed when they cooled to room temperature after reactions and then were dried at 60 °C for 10 h. In the end, products could be obtained by calcining the precursors at 350 °C for 3 h with a heating rate of 2 °C/min under the air.

The morphologies of as-prepared $Co_3O_4$ were obtained using a high-resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM; TECNAI G2 F20, FEI Co.) and a scanning electron microscope (SEM; JSM-7800 F, JEOL). Details of crystalline structures were characterized using a powder X-ray diffraction (XRD; XRD-6000, Shimadzu) and a Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (FTIR; Nicolet 6700, Nicolet Instrument Co.). The surface properties of these catalysts were characterized using Brunner-Emmet-Teller (BET) measurements and X-ray photoelectron

spectroscopy (XPS; KRATOS AXIS SUPRA, Shimadzu). The BET measurements were tested with $N_2$ adsorption isotherm at 77 K using QUADRASORB SI automated surface area and pore size analyzer (Quantachrome Corporation, USA). The XPS was tested with an Al K$\alpha$ excitation source and the binding energy are corrected with the standard peak of C 1s at 284.8 eV.

The catalytic performances of these catalysts were measured by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scan calorimeter (DSC) on the Netzsch STA 449C TGA/DSC simultaneous thermal analyzer. The heating rate was in the range of 2~20 °C/min and testing temperature was in the range of 100~500 °C. The total gas flow rate is 60 ml/min and high-purity argon (Ar) was utilized as the testing atmosphere. Some special testing conditions were needed and pointed out in following sections.

The wet grinding method was utilized to prepare thermoanalytic testing samples containing catalysts. The catalysts were mixed with AP in a mass ratio of 2:98 in a mortar for 15 min with the assistant of appropriate ethyl acetate to ensure the homogeneity and security, and then the mixture was dried at 50 °C for 30 min. The raw AP was grinded using the same method, too. All samples were characterized by SEM and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS mapping; S-4800, HITACHI), and the results are shown in Fig. S1. The size and morphology of all the samples including grinding AP are almost same (Fig. S1b–e). In addition, all the catalysts can distribute in AP uniformly according to EDS mapping results (Fig. S2). However, raw AP was utilized in experiments of studying AP decomposition mechanism because of its homogeneous size and morphology (Fig. S1a and b).

The spin-polarized calculations were performed with the projector augmented wave method (PAW) (Kresse and Joubert, 1999) utilized and PBE functional (Perdew et al., 1996) employed in the Vienna simulation package (VASP) (Kresse and Joubert, 1999; Kresse and Furthmüller, 1996). The influence of 3d electrons of Co atoms was considered and a DFT + U method introduced by Dudarev et al. (Dudarev et al., 1998) was used as an on-site Coulomb correction. The parameter of (U-J) was set to 3.3 eV, which has been confirmed as a suitable value to avoid large computational resources without absurd results (Shojaee et al., 2017). In addition, the DFT-D3 method of Grimme et al. (Grimme et al., 2010) was used to describe correctly van der Waals interactions. A Monkhorst-Pack $k$-point mesh of $(3\times3\times3)$ was used for the unit cell at a cutoff energy of 450 eV and was folded for the supercells with a same or similar sampling in the reciprocal space. The energy convergence criterion was set to $10^{-4}$ eV for electronic steps and the force convergence criterion for atoms was less than 0.02 eV/Å.

## 3. Result and discussion

The structures of as-prepared catalysts were confirmed by XRD patterns (Fig. S3). All diffraction peaks of these catalysts are consistent with the standard peaks of $Co_3O_4$ (JCPD#80-1541, a $=8.084$ Å), showing a high purity of the catalysts. To further investigate the information of structures and morphologies, microscopy is utilized and the results are shown in Fig. 1. $Co_3O_4$ nanorods (Fig. 1a and b) are rectangular with the length of 250~350 nm. The dominant exposed planes are {110}, which are perpendicular to the set of (113) planes and (222) planes with the relevant lattice space of 0.24 nm and 0.23 nm, respectively. $Co_3O_4$ nanosheets (Fig. 1c and d) are hexagons with the average edge length of 300 nm. {111} are the dominant exposed planes of them, which are perpendicular to the set of (220), (20-2) and (022) planes with the same lattice space of 0.28 nm. $Co_3O_4$ nanocubes (Fig. 1e and f) are regular cubes with the average edge length of 300 nm and their exposed planes are {100}, which are perpendicular to the set of (022) planes and (0-22) planes and both of the relevant lattice space are 0.28 nm. The shape models of above catalysts are shown in Fig. S4 and all proportions of dominant exposed planes are larger than 90 %.

BET measurements and XPS spectroscopy are utilized to study the surface properties of these catalysts. Nanorods and nanosheets have specific surface areas of $40.9\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$ and $38.1\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$, respectively, and the specific surface area of nanocubes is $4.6\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$ (Table S1). Fig. 2 shows the results of XPS spectroscopy, which are used to study the surface states of these catalysts. All the O 1s spectra of three catalysts show three peak signals. The spectrum of nanorods exhibits a narrow peak located at 529.7 eV, a broad peak at 531.1 eV and a weak peak at 533.3 eV. These three peaks represent the presence of lattice oxygens, surface oxygens and adsorbed oxygens (Li et al., 2018) and there is no peak of oxygen vacancy. Nanosheets and nanocubes have similar O 1s spectra with a slight difference in binding energy within 0.4 eV, suggesting surface defects of O vacancies do not exist in these catalysts that are calcined at the same condition in air. The Co 2p spectra of three catalysts are utilized to study the surface states as well and the results are shown in Fig. S5. The peaks at about 779.5 eV, 780.8 eV, 794.6 eV and 795.9 eV represent $\text{Co}^{2+}\ 2\text{p}_{3/2}$, $\text{Co}^{3+}\ 2\text{p}_{3/2}$, $\text{Co}^{2+}\ 2\text{p}_{1/2}$ and $\text{Co}^{3+}\ 2\text{p}_{1/2}$ peaks, respectively (Li et al., 2018). All $\text{Co}^{2+}/\text{Co}^{3+}$ proportions of these catalysts are close to the stoichiometric ratio of Co element in $Co_3O_4$ bulk (1:2, Table S2), which means there are no cation defects in these catalysts.

In order to study the catalytic performance of these $Co_3O_4$ catalysts for decomposition of AP, TGA and DSC tests have been conducted and the results are shown in Fig. 3. All the $Co_3O_4$ catalysts enhance the thermal decomposition of AP. TGA and DSC curves demonstrate the stagnate decomposition period disappears and the $T_{HTD}$ is decreased by more than 100 °C compared to that of pure grinding AP (424.5 °C, Fig. 2b). However, a distinctly different catalytic performance can be observed among $Co_3O_4$ nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes. The $T_{HTD}$ of AP with nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes are 289.4 °C, 299.9 °C and 326.3 °C, respectively. The $T_{HTD}$ of nanorods is lowered by 36.9 °C compared to that of nanocubes, showing the best catalytic performance among these catalysts. In addition, activation energy calculated by the Kissinger formula (Kissinger, 1957) displays a similar trend corresponding to the $T_{HTD}$ as shown by Fig. 4, Table 1, Fig. S6 and Table S3. With the addition of $Co_3O_4$ nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes, the activation energies of AP decomposition are decreased to 143.0 kJ/mol, 154.2 kJ/mol and 180.6 kJ/mol, respectively. Obviously, the nanorods show the best catalytic performance with activation energy decreasing by 28.8 % compared to the pure grinding AP while the nanocubes only decrease 10.1 % of the activation energy. The different catalytic performances reflect the effects of different crystal facets of $Co_3O_4$ on AP decomposition.

To deeply understand the mechanisms of facet effects, we further investigated the direct and catalytic decomposition mechanisms of AP. Typical TGA and DSC curves of the decomposition of raw AP are shown in Fig. 5 and the whole process is shown in Fig. 6a–e. The thermal decomposition process of pure AP is a multistage process including low temperature decomposition (LTD), stagnation decomposition (SD) and high temperature decomposition (HTD). The temperature ranges of the LTD, SD and HTD are 290~340 °C, 340~370 °C and 370~450 °C, respectively. For the LTD process, a lot of investigations (Jacobs and Whitehead, 1969; Boldyrev et al., 1970; Khairetdinov et al., 1976; Jacobs and Russell-Jones, 1967; Khairetdinov and Boldyrev, 1980) have confirmed that the proton transfer occurs between ammonium ions and perchloric ions in the AP crystal, leading to the partial decomposition of AP into $NH_3$ and $HClO_4$. The $NH_3$ and $HClO_4$ are both adsorbed on the surface of AP, so they have a chance to react with each other. However, $HClO_4$ is unstable under the reactive temperature and most of the $HClO_4$ could easily decompose into chlorooxygens, such as $HClO_3$, $ClO_2$, $ClO$, $Cl_2$, $HCl$, $O_2$, and so on, which may directly leave the surface of AP crystal before oxidizing adsorbed $NH_3$. (Jacobs and Whitehead, 1969; Cummings and Pearson, 1964). Thus, the $NH_3$ will accumulate on the AP surface and affect the proton transfer process, consequently inhibiting the decomposition of AP. Then the SD process appears following the LTD process. When the temperature is further increased, the HTD process begins. The $NH_3$ will desorb from the AP

To investigate the interaction between NH₃ and different facets, these catalysts were thermal treated at 300 °C under 20 vol.% NH₃. Fig. 7c shows the FTIR spectra of nanorods with or without thermal treatment. The peaks at $660\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and $567\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ are corresponding to Co−O bonds in $\text{Co}_3\text{O}_4$ crystals. The broad peak at $3100\sim3600\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and the weak peak at $1620\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ are corresponding to the stretching vibration and flexural vibration of adsorbed $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, respectively. Two peaks at $1330\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and $1510\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ represent the formation of Co−N−O structures on crystal surface after thermal treatment by NH₃ (Wang et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2013). A similar phenomenon can be observed, and the results are shown in Figure S7. Using the peak intensity of Co−O bands as reference, the relative peak intensity of Co−N−O structures in nanorods is stronger than that in nanosheets and nanocubes because the nanorods have a stronger capacity to form the structures than nanosheets and nanocubes. All the results reflect the facet effects of prepared catalysts on AP decomposition.

DFT calculation was utilized to verify the facet effects via investigating the adsorption and dissociation behaviors of NH₃ on the catalyst surfaces. The slab models of different facets of $\text{Co}_3\text{O}_4$ are shown in Fig. S6. All the surfaces expose the most oxygen atoms to keep consistent with experiment conditions. Five Co−O layers were used and two of them at the bottom were fixed as the bulk phase. The $15\ \text{Å}$ of vacuum layer was used to break the periodicity of the direction perpendicular to these surfaces. All these models were built from the optimized $\text{Co}_3\text{O}_4$ crystal with lattice constant of $8.090\ \text{Å}$. More details can be obtained in Supporting information.

The results of the binding energy and the most stable geometries of NH₃, NH₂, NH and N species on different facets are shown in Fig. 8. The binding energies of NH₃ on the {110}, {111} and {100} planes are $-1.774\ \text{eV}$, $-1.638\ \text{eV}$, and $-1.354\ \text{eV}$, respectively. Comparing to other two planes, the binding energy between the {110} planes and NH₃ is highest and this strong interaction is beneficial to the NH₃ transition to the catalysts, which can weaken the accumulation of the NH₃ on the AP surface. The relatively high bonding capacity for the {110} facets can be attributed to the low-coordinated surface cobalt atoms, which show a strong Lewis acidity to adsorb NH₃ (Zhang et al., 2013; Shojaee et al., 2014; Yao et al., 2012). Moreover, according to the optimized structures in Fig. 8a, the hydrogens of NH₃ will interact with surface lattice oxygens to enhance the adsorption of NH₃ on {110} facets (Shojaee et al., 2017).

In addition, for the {110} planes, the binding energies of NH₂, NH and N species show a gradual decreasing trend with a value of -2.850 eV, -3.560 eV and -4.669 eV, respectively. And these adsorption energies are also the highest compared to other two planes, which shows greater tendency of deep dissociation of NH₃ on {110} planes. The products of deep dissociation, including NH and N specials, can enter the surface lattice and form Co−N−O structures, which are beneficial to further form NO, NO₂ and N₂ (Shojaee et al., 2017). All the aforementioned results reveal the {110} planes possess higher activity in the adsorption and oxidation of NH₃, subsequently leading to better catalytic capacity. The calculation results are well consistence with the experimental data.

### 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have investigated the effects of different crystal planes of $Co_{3}O_{4}$ nanocatalysts on AP decomposition by using nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes exposing {110}, {111} and {100} facets as the model systems. The facet effects have been demonstrated through their different catalytic performances for AP decomposition. With the addition of the $Co_{3}O_{4}$ nanorods, nanosheets and nanocubes, $T_{HTD}$ of AP is decreased to $289.4\ ^{\circ}C$, $299.9\ ^{\circ}C$ and $326.3\ ^{\circ}C$ and the activation energies of AP decomposition are decreased to 143.0 kJ/mol 154.2 kJ/ mol and 180.6 kJ/mol, respectively. The results can be supported by calculated binding energies of $NH_{3}$ on {110}, {111} and {100} facets via DFT, which are -1.774 eV, -1.638 eV, and -1.354 eV, respectively. The {110} planes exhibit the best bonding capacity with $NH_{3}$, showing a better catalytic performance than other crystal planes. Moreover, the greater binding energy of $NH_{3}$ split products on {110} planes to form $Co-N-O$ structures show more potential oxidation of $NH_{3}$. Our re search reveals the mechanism of facet effects on catalytic decomposi tion of AP, which would be helpful for understanding the catalytic mechanism and developing efficient combustion catalysts.
