# Metal-Free Triazine-Based 2D Covalent Organic Framework for Efficient $\boldsymbol{H_2}$ Evolution by Electrochemical Water Splitting

Santu Ruidas$^{+,[a]}$ Bishnupad Mohanty$^{+,[b]}$ Piyali Bhanja,$^{[b]}$ E. S. Erakulan,$^{[c]}$ Ranjit Thapa,$^{[c]}$ Prasenjit Das,$^{[d]}$ Avik Chowdhury,$^{[a]}$ Sanjay K. Mandal,$^{*[d]}$ Bikash Kumar Jena,$^{*[b]}$ and Asim Bhaumik$^{*[a]}$

Hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) by electrochemical water splitting is one of the most active areas of energy research, yet the benchmark electrocatalysts used for this reaction are based on expensive noble metals. This is a major bottleneck for their large-scale operation. Thus, development of efficient metal-free electrocatalysts is of paramount importance for sustainable and economical production of the renewable fuel hydrogen by water splitting. Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) show much promise for this application by virtue of their architectural stability, nanoporosity, abundant active sites located periodically throughout the framework, and high electronic conductivity due to extended $\pi$-delocalization. This study concerns a new COF material, $C_6$-TRZ-TFP, which is synthesized by solvothermal polycondensation of 2-hydroxybenzene-1,3,5-tricarbaldehyde (TFP) and 4,4',4''-(1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triyl)tris[(1,1'-biphenyl)-4-amine]. $C_6$-TRZ-TFP displayed excellent HER activity in electrochemical water splitting, with a very low overpotential of 200 mV and specific activity of $0.2831\ \text{mA cm}^{-2}$ together with high retention of catalytic activity after a long duration of electrocatalysis in $0.5\ \text{M}$ aqueous $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. Density functional theory calculations suggest that the electron-deficient carbon sites near the $\pi$ electron-donating nitrogen atoms are more active towards HER than those near the electron-withdrawing nitrogen and oxygen atoms.

## Introduction

In the 21st century, the rapid growth of advanced technologies and population caused depletion of fossil fuel-based energy resources together with the uninterrupted emission of greenhouse gases and these have a serious effect on our environment.$^{[1]}$ Thus, for the protection of energy and the environment, secure and accessible supply of clean and sustainable energy sources is very much essential.$^{[2]}$ Hydrogen energy is green, sustainable, efficient, and can replace carbon-based energy sources. Electrochemical water splitting provides a sustainable method to generate clean and renewable molecular hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$),$^{[3]}$ and this is a crucial technology for improving the $\text{H}_2$-based economy. For efficient $\text{H}_2$ production, the primary goal is to replace noble metal-based electrocatalysts with cheap, earth-abundant, a highly efficient, and stable electrocatalyst for efficient hydrogen evolution via half-cell reaction in water splitting.$^{[4]}$ Platinum based materials exhibit nearly zero overpotential, lower Tafel slope, and high exchange current density towards the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) under acidic conditions.$^{[5]}$ In contrast, the inexpensive transition metal-based electrocatalysts suffer from corrosion and passivation under strong acidic conditions.$^{[6]}$ By avoiding transition metals, different metal-free carbon-based electrocatalysts have been developed, and these have gained a huge interest over the years for electrochemical water reduction and oxidation reactions.$^{[7]}$ Metal-free catalysts such as graphitic carbon nitride, heteroatom-doped graphene and carbon nanotube, black phosphorus, graphdiyne, porous red phosphorus nanoparticles (P-RPNPs), and porous organic polymers have been used for HER catalysts.$^{[8]}$ However, most of the metal-free carbon-based catalysts still suffer from lower surface area, low electrical conductivity, lower catalytic activity and poor operational durability. Thus, metal-free electrocatalysts, based on purely organic frameworks for HER, is a very challenging area of research today.

Porous, highly ordered crystalline covalent organic frameworks (COFs) have attracted huge attention in recent times due to their large diversity of 2D structural motifs, excellent chemical and thermal stability, and interesting adsorption, optical and electronic properties.$^{[9]}$ Periodicity of the pores of nanoscale dimensions is largely responsible for their applications in several frontline areas such as gas storage and separation, chemical sensing, catalysis.$^{[10]}$ In the last few years, an enormous number of robust and artistic COF has been constructed via remarkable dynamic reversible reactions like

Unique properties of imine-based crystalline COFs such as high surface area, tunable structures with appropriate building blocks, degree of lateral $\pi$-electron conjugation and assembly of donor-acceptor moiety assist charge carrier mobility, which could make them ideal and efficient electrocatalyst in molecular hydrogen production. Thus, crystalline COFs found significant interest in electrochemical and photoelectrochemical applications. $^{[14]}$ Recently, several COFs and covalent triazine frameworks (CTFs) have been reported for photocatalytic hydrogen evolution, $^{[15]}$ whereas there are very few reports reveal the electrocatalytic HER activity. $^{[16]}$ Also, the crystalline thin 2D COFs are more promising for electrocatalytic applications. Herein, we developed a 2D imine-based crystalline COF by the judicial choice of the monomers 2-hydroxybenzene-1,3,5-tricarbaldehyde (TFP) and 4,4',4''-1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triyl)tris [(1,1-biphenyl)-4-amine] both having $C_3$-symmetry. The trialdehyde and triamine undergo an acid-catalyzed Schiff-base polycondensation reaction to yield a novel 2D COF ($C_6$-TRZ-TFP). The powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) data is refined with the simulated data considering an ideal eclipsed or staggered structure and the material has been thoroughly characterized by different instrumental tools. $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF showed excellent electrocatalytic performance towards HER under an acidic medium together with high cyclic stability.

## Electrocatalytic hydrogen evolution reaction

The electrocatalytic HER activity of the as-synthesized thin 2D $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF was investigated by drop-casting on a glassy carbon electrode (GCE) surface (mass loading: 0.035 mg cm⁻²). The linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) measurement was performed in Ar-saturated 0.5 M $H_2SO_4$ solutions at a 5 mV s⁻¹ scan rate using a three-electrode system. The catalytic HER activity of $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF was compared with the bare GCE and commercial Pt/C. The LSV plots of the catalysts were presented after correction with $iR$ compensation. Figure 4a displays the HER activity of bare GCE, $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF, and commercial Pt/C catalysts within the potential range of 0.1 to −0.5 V. As expected, the bare GCE response gives a very low cathodic current density up to potentials of −0.5 V, suggesting a negligible HER activity. So, HER activity is due to the presence of $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF catalysts only. In the case of the $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF modified electrode, the cathodic current density rises rapidly towards negative potential due to the reduction of $H^+$ to $H_2$. The overpotential ($\eta$) value of electrocatalysts reflects the catalytic efficiency, and the lower value suggests the higher electrocatalytic performance toward $H_2$ generation. The $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF achieves the lower onset overpotential of 115 mV to yield a current density of 1.0 mA cm⁻². It reaches the current density of 10 and 50 mA cm⁻² at the overpotential of 200 and 235 mV, respectively. However, the overpotentials value of $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF is inferior to benchmark catalysts Pt/C, but superior to other reported covalent triazine frameworks (CTFs), and metal-free COFs (Table S2). The Tafel slope value is the inherent property of the electrocatalysts, and it is calculated to understand the reaction mechanism and kinetics for HER. A lower value of the Tafel slope suggests a higher proton reduction process with lower overpotential. The Tafel plot ( $\eta$ vs. $\log j$ ) was derived from the plot of overpotentials vs. current density (Figure 4b). To calculate the Tafel slope, the linear part of the plot is fitted with the equation $\eta=b \log (j)+a$ , in which $j$ represents the current density and $b$ is the Tafel slope.

In acidic electrolyte, the HER proceeds through three principal mechanisms: Volmer reaction, Heyrovsky reaction, and Tafel reaction. The theoretical Tafel slopes of these mechanisms are observed to be 120,40, and $30 mV dec^{-1}$ , respectively. The $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF exhibits the Tafel slope of $82 mV dec^{-1}$ , suggesting that the Volmer- Heyrovsky mechanism is involved in the reaction process. $^{[4]}$ Further, the exchange current density(jo) is derived from the extrapolation of Tafel plots and replicate the intrinsic rate of electron transfer between the catalyst surface and the adsorbed proton (Figure $4 c$ ). The $j_{0}$ value of 2D $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF is $0.054 mA cm^{-2}$ . The observed smaller Tafel slope and higher $j_{0}$ suggest the favorable reaction kinetics to achieve the higher current density at lower overpotentials and this is beneficial for practical applications. Interestingly, the HER activity of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF is achieved without any electrochemical precondition treatment. $^{[22]}$ To detect the active metal center in the synthesized catalysts, we investigated the influence of thiocyanate ions $(SCN^{-})$ on the HER activity of the $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF. The $SCN^{-}$ is widely accepted as a catalyticpoison to the active metal-centered sites in acidic conditions. $^{[23]}$ On adding $10 mmol$ of $SCN^{-}$ ions into the electrolyte $(0.5 M$  H2SO4), no appreciable decrease of overpotentials and current density was observed in HER activity (Figure S11), which suggests the absence of the active metal center in the $C_{6}$ -TRZ TFP COF. Thus, the above observation supports the catalyticactivity is due to the metal-free active sites present in $C_{6}-TRZ$  TFP COF.

Further to explore the reaction kinetics, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurement was carried out by applying an $AC$ voltage with an amplitude of $5 mV$ and frequency range of $10^{5}-10^{-1} ~Hz$ in a $0.5 M H_{2} SO_{4}$ solution. Figure $4 ~d$ shows the Nyquist plots of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF, where the semicircles represent the charge-transfer resistance $(R_{ct})$ of the reaction and suggest the resistance between the electro- lyte-catalytic interface. The $R_{ct}$ value of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF is estimated by fitting the Nyquist plot with the equivalent circuit(Insert in the Figure $4 ~d$ ). The $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF exhibits a lower $R_{ct}$  value of $40.67 \Omega$ at the overpotential of $250 mV$ . On the basis of the $R_{ct}$ values calculated at different overpotentials, the $R_{ct}$ value decreases with an increase of overpotential (Figure S12). The porous and 2D morphology of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF increases the contact between electrolyte-catalytic interface. It improves the charge transfer to endorse the contact between protons and electrocatalyst, resulting in an increased proton reduction capacity and enhancing the HER activity. The improved HER performance of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF was further accessed by estimating the intrinsic activity and active sites. Thus, the mass and specific activity, electrochemically active surface area(ECSA), and roughness factor $(R_{f})$ were estimated. The mass andspecific activity were calculated using equation $1,2.^{[24]}$ At $\eta=$  $200 mV$ and a mass loading of $0.035 mg cm^{-2}$ , the mass activity of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF is $285.71 Ag^{-1}$ . Similarly, the specific activity of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF calculated by using BET surface area $(928 ~m^{2} ~g^{-1})$ at a $\eta=200 mV$ , is $0.2831 mA cm^{-2}$ . The high mass and specific activity suggest the high intrinsic catalytic activity of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF. To evaluate the catalytic active sites, the ECSA and $R_{f}$ were estimated. The ECSA were calculated from double-layer capacitance $(C_{dl})$ extracting from cyclic voltamme try (CV) measurement in the nonfaradaic region (Figure S13). The ECSA and $R_{f}$ value of $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF were estimated to be $6.12 ~cm^{2}$ and $86.26 ~cm^{2}$ , respectively (Figure $4 e$ ). This suggests that in $C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF, more catalytically active sites are exposed and these are responsible for higher electrocatalytic HER activity. The high surface area and the porous structure can facilitate the accumulation and diffusion of more proton on the electrode surface resulted in an enhanced activity towards HER. The durability of electrocatalysts is another important parame-ter for the commercializing of the catalysts. The durability of $C_{6}$  TRZ-TFP COF was tested by chronopotentiometric analysis at a current density of $10 mA cm^{-2}$ (Figure $4 f$ ). The catalyst exhibited a very steady potentiometric curve with $94 \%$ operational retention of potential over $50 ~h$ of continuous operation. This observation justifies the robust nature of $2 D C_{6}$ -TRZ-TFP COF for promising HER applications. After HER durability test, it is quite essential to study the crystal structure and morphology of the C。-TRZ-TFP COF to validate the robust nature of the catalyst for the practical application. The PXRD and SEM analyses have been carried out after $50 ~h$ stability test. In the PXRD pattern, no predominate change in the crystal phase was observed(Figure $15) suggesting the stability of the crystalline COF structure in the electrocatalyst. The SEM analysis confirm the retention of the morphology after durability experiment (Fig- ure $16). All the above result suggesting the robust nature of the COF material in the electrocatalytic HER application.

## DFT Calculation
The DFT calculations were carried out for further insight into the electronic structure and the mechanism behind the higher

electrocatalytic HER activity of 2D $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF catalyst. The calculation details are provided in the Supporting Information; the findings are summarized here. The hydrogen adsorption free energy ($\Delta G_{\text{H}}^{*}$) is the main parameter in defining any active site's efficiency. For the ideal electrocatalyst, the $\Delta G_{\text{H}}^{*}$ would be zero, and the high positive and high negative $\Delta G_{\text{H}}^{*}$ value prevents the adsorption/desorption of H* intermediate and releasing the $\text{H}_2$ bubbles. $^{[25]}$ The HER activity is estimated from the free energy plot (Figure 5a). Six unique carbon sites are selected for the adsorption study (Figure 5b). The $\Delta G_{\text{H}}^{*}$ values and the corresponding occupied charge on active sites before H-atom and C–H distance are given in Table S3 (supporting Information) for all the six carbon sites. It is observed that for all the six carbon sites, the first step is endothermic.

To understand the bonding behavior of H atom with each active site, we have estimated the total charge occupancy, $\sigma$ and $\pi$ electron of each active sites and compare with pure $\text{sp}^2$ hybridized carbon in graphene structure as a reference (Table S3). We found the total charge occupancy for carbon in the graphene system is 3.95 e (Table S3). The electron-rich triazine carbon site 1 and neighboring sites 2 and 3 possess free energy 2.03 eV, 1.43 eV, and 1.09 eV. Following the bonding mechanism, the two nitrogen atoms gain the charge from the carbon atom (leads to a decrease in $\sigma$ charge), and via back-bonding, the occupancy of $\pi$ orbital increases for site 1 carbon atom. $^{[26]}$ Similarly, site 2 and site 3 charge occupancy can be explained due to bonding with three carbon and two carbon and one hydrogen, respectively. These phenomena make site 1, site 2, and site 3 electron-rich. Whereas site 4, site 5, and site 6 are electron-deficient and particularly site 5, with the most efficient adsorption free energy, has total electron occupancy in the moderate range and hence lowest $\Delta G_{\text{H}}^{*}$. This suggests that electron-deficient carbon sites attached with one nitrogen are more favorable for HER. $^{[27]}$ Further the role of keto-enol tautomerism is investigated using site 5 and 6 as they are nearer where the tautomerism would affect the HER activity. The initial and final structures of H-atom adsorption on site 5 is given in Figure 6a,b. After H-atom adsorption, the distance between N and H from OH reduces from 1.95 Å to 1.10 Å, indicating preference of more keto to enol group. HER activity calculated with enol group as preferred surface shows decreased efficiency of $\Delta G_{\text{H}}^{*}$ of 0.74 eV. Also, on site 6 when the H adsorption is initiated with keto group on surface, the surface H-atom still bonded with O forming enol group (Figure 6c and 6d). The reasoning could be, after transferring charge to the adsorbed H-atom, the already electron deficient surface sites become more charge deficient, which is compensated by the stronger bonding with the surface H (keto for site 5 and enol for site 6). During adsorption studies, it is found that the structure is heavily buckled for all the sites. The buckling of linkers is different for each site, thereby necessitating the use of respective surface minima of each corresponding site instead of a common surface for free energy calculation.

### Conclusion

A triazine-based thin 2D $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF has been synthesized through the Schiff-base polycondensation of 2-hydroxybenzene-1,3,5-tricarbaldehyde (TFP) and 4,4',4''-(1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triyl)tris[(1,1'-biphenyl)-4-amine] under solvothermal conditions. $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF displayed a very high BET surface area of $928\ \text{m}^2\text{g}^{-1}$ with a pore opening of 1.6 nm, as supported by calculated structural analysis. This triazine-based COF displayed excellent $\text{H}_2$ evolution activity in electrochemical water splitting with very low overpotential, high exchange current density, high mass-specific activity, and high retention of catalytic activity for sustainable operation in acidic medium, owing to the extended $\pi$-conjugation in the periodic organic framework. The DFT calculations demonstrated that electron-deficient carbon sites are more active towards HER than electron-rich carbon sites. This result should shed light on the electrocatalytic applications of COF materials and provides a clear and promising direction for creating the highest-performing material by regulating heteroatom allocation for versatile HER-related energy conversion technologies. Thus, the metal-free 2D COF-based electrocatalyst's excellent HER activity may open new avenues for low-cost sustainable $\text{H}_2$ production through electrochemical water splitting in the future.

4-(4-aminophenyl) benzonitrile (95 %), phenol, trifluoromethanesulfonic acid (triflic acid; $\geq99\%$), hexamethylenetetramine (HMTA; $\geq99\%$), trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich and other organic solvents, acids were received from local commercial sources. Purchased chemicals were used without further purification. Deionized water was used throughout the experiment.

#### Instrumentation

$^1\text{H}$ and $^{13}\text{C}$ NMR spectra were recorded by Bruker DPX-300 NMR spectrometer. Carbon hydrogen and nitrogen contents in 2D $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF was performed by PerkinElmer 2400 Series II CHN elemental analyzer. X-ray powder diffraction patterns of the sample were recorded by using a Rigaku SmartLab X-ray diffractometer using Ni-filtered $\text{Cu}_{K\alpha}$ ($\lambda=0.15406\ \text{nm}$) radiation at 9 Kw (45 Kv, 200 mA) with a 1D detector (scan speed: 0.05 degree/second). Using Perkin Elmer Spectrum 100 FTIR spectrometer, all FTIR spectra were recorded. Volumetric nitrogen adsorption/desorption measurement was performed by Autosorb-iQ surface analyzer (Quantachrome Instrument, USA) at 77 K. Transmission electron microscopic (TEM) images were obtained by JEOL JEM 2010 transmission electron microscope. Morphology of the COF was determined by JEOL-JSM 7500F field emission scanning electron microscope (FE SEM). $^{13}\text{C}$ cross-polarization magic angle spinning (CP-MAS) NMR spectrum was recorded by 500 MHz Bruker Avance NMR Spectrometer at a mass frequency of 8 kHz. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopic data was collected by using Omicron Nano-technology XPS 0571 spectrometer. To understand the thermal stability of $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF thermal analysis has been carried out under $\text{N}_2$ atmosphere in the temperature range 298–1073 K (heating rate$=2\ \text{K/min}$) by using a STA-6000 thermal analyzer of Perkin Elmer.

#### Synthesis of 2-hydroxybenzene-1,3,5-tricarbaldehyde (TFP)

Phenol (6.9 g, 65 mmol), hexamine (20.1 g, 143 mmol), and trifluoroacetic acid (TFA; 70 mL) were stirred at $125^\circ\text{C}$ for 24 h and again the reaction mixture was heated to $150^\circ\text{C}$ for about 3 h. Then the mixture was treated with 3 N HCl at $120^\circ\text{C}$ and again stirred for 30 min at $100^\circ\text{C}$. After that, the mixture was cooled to room temperature and washed with distilled water to afford brown colored crude powder, which was recrystallized from DMF to get pure product. The product was characterized by $^1\text{H}$ and $^{13}\text{C}$ NMR spectroscopies (Figures S1 and S2).

#### Synthesis of $C_6$-TRZ-TRIAMINE

Trifluoromethanesulfonic acid-assisted cycloaddition reaction was carried to synthesize 4,4',4''-(1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triyl)tris[(1,1'-biphenyl)-4-amine] ($C_6$-TRZ-TRIAMINE). For a typical synthesis 4-(4-aminophenyl)benzonitrile (971 mg, 5.0 mmol) was taken in a flame dried RB (20 mL) and then triflic acid (5.0 mL) was added slowly at $0^\circ\text{C}$ and stirred under an inert atmosphere for 36 h. After that, the reaction mixture was gently poured into a beaker containing crushed ice and neutralized by 2 M NaOH solution to afford yellow colored precipitate. It was then filtered and washed with distilled water several times to get pure product $C_6$-TRZ-TRIAMINE. The product was characterized by $^1\text{H}$ and $^{13}\text{C}$ NMR spectroscopies (Figures S3 and S4).

#### Synthesis of $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF

Imine-based 2D $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF was synthesized by acid-catalyzed Schiff-base condensation reaction between two $C_3$-symmetric triamine and trialdehyde monomers (Scheme 1). A Pyrex tube measuring $10\ \text{mm}\times8\ \text{mm}$ (o.d. $\times$ i.d. ) was charged with 2-hydroxybenzene-1,3,5-tricarbaldehyde (TFP; 0.1 mmol) and 4,4',4''-(1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triyl)tris(1,1'-biphenyl)trianiline ($C_6$-TRZ-TRIAMINE, 0.1 mmol) in a mixed solvent system of 1,2 dichlorobenzene ($o$-DCB) and $n$-butyl alcohol ($^n\text{BuOH}$) and 6 M acetic acid as catalyst in the ratio of 1.2:0.4:0.1 mL. Then the tube was subjected to ultrasonication for 10 min and flash-frozen at 77 K in liquid $\text{N}_2$ bath, degassed by four successive freeze-pump-thaw cycles, flame sealed under vacuum and then sealed tube was kept in a $120^\circ\text{C}$ hot air oven for 72 h to yield red-colored powder COF material. The solid COF was collected by centrifugation and washed with dry solvents like tetrahydrofuran, methanol, and acetone to remove unreacted monomers. The powder 2D $C_6$-TRZ-TFP COF sample thus collected was dried at $120^\circ\text{C}$ under vacuum for 12 h, characterized thoroughly before using it as an electrocatalyst.
