# Modulating Benzothiadiazole-Based Covalent Organic Frameworks via Halogenation for Enhanced Photocatalytic Water Splitting: Small Changes Make Big Differences

**Abstract:** Two-dimensional covalent organic frameworks (2D COFs), which is an emerging class of crystalline porous polymers, have been recognized as a new platform for efficient solar-to-hydrogen energy conversion owing to their pre-designable structures and tailor-made functions. Herein, we demonstrate that slight modulation of the chemical structure of a typical photoactive 2D COF (Py-HTP-BT-COF) via chlorination (Py-CITP-BT-COF) and fluorination (Py-FTP-BT-COF) can lead to dramatically enhanced photocatalytic $H_2$ evolution rates (HER = 177.50 $\mu$mol h⁻¹ with a high apparent quantum efficiency (AQE) of 8.45% for Py-CITP-BT-COF). Both experimental and computational studies reveal that halogen modulation at the photoactive benzothiadiazole moiety can efficiently suppress charge recombination and significantly reduce the energy barrier associated with the formation of H intermediate species (H*) on polymer surface. Our findings provide new prospects toward design and synthesis of highly active organic photocatalysts toward solar-to-chemical energy conversion.

## Introduction

With the rapid increase in global demand for energy, the pursuit of clean and renewable energy sources as the alternative for the depleting fossil fuels becomes more urgent than ever before. Hydrogen ($H_2$) is considered as one of the most promising energy sources to address the global energy crisis owing to the high energy density and free of carbon emission.$^{[1]}$ Photocatalytic water splitting, which can directly harness sunlight to produce $H_2$ from water, is regarded as a preeminent economical and eco-friendly technology among other methods to produce molecular $H_2$.$^{[2,3]}$ Substantial efforts have been devoted to rationally design and synthesis of efficient photocatalysts with enhanced photon capture and facilitated charge separation to achieve highly efficient solar-driven $H_2$ conversion.$^{[4,5]}$ However, the sluggish kinetics and the indispensable reliance on precious metals for such photochemical conversion still hinder the practical applications of these processes. The development of efficient and metal-free photocatalysts for photochemical water splitting has recently been recognized as an alternative route to address this dilemma in photochemical energy conversion.

Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) $^{[6-13]}$ with high crystallinity have emerged as promising alternatives to inorganic semiconductors for photocatalytic water splitting.$^{[14,15]}$ Recently, to achieve highly photoactive COFs toward efficient sunlight-driven $H_2$ conversion, many strategies$^{[16-26]}$ on improving photon capture and facilitating charge carrier separation have been developed through integrating various novel $\pi$-conjugated building blocks,$^{[18]}$ utilizing proper donor-acceptor (D-A) combinations$^{[16,17,19,22]}$ and depositing new co-catalysts.$^{[23,24]}$ For example, sulfone-decorated COF,$^{[18]}$ fully conjugated $sp^2$ carbon-COFs,$^{[16,17,19]}$ triazine-centered COF,$^{[22]}$ and the hydrazone-linked COF,$^{[27]}$ have been reported to exhibit impressive $H_2$ evolution rates (HER) under visible light irradiation. Nevertheless, these promising COFs suffer from de novo design and laborious synthesis. It is still imperative and desirable to develop a simple yet efficient strategy to enhance the HER rates through minor modification of the reported and well-established photoactive COFs while avoiding the tedious "trial-and-error" process for seeking new efficient photocatalysts.

It is well known that the charge carrier mobilities and photoelectrical conversion efficiencies in organic photovoltaics can be improved by molecular engineering of organic semiconductors through subtle structural modulations in the building blocks$^{[28]}$ such as halogenation (F, Cl),$^{[29,30]}$ variation of the substitution positions$^{[31]}$ and introduction of branched alkylchains,$^{[32]}$ etc. We thus rationalize that incorporating halogen atoms into COF skeletons could be a potentially effective approach for improving the photocatalytic performances of COFs. benzothiadiazole (BT) based COFs (BT-COFs) as a modular example due to the strong electron-deficient acceptor unit of BT and the efficient intramolecular charge transfer induced by the D-A alternating skeletons.[³³] A series of 2D isostructural photoactive Py-XTP-BT-COFs (X = H, F, Cl) were constructed via polycondensation of 4,4',4'',4'''-(pyrene-1,3,6,8-tetrayl)tetrabenzaldehyde (Py-CHO) with terphenyl based diamines (XTP-BT-NH₂) annulated with fluorinated, chlorinated or non-halogenated BT units under solvothermal conditions. Our investigations revealed that Py-CITP-BT-COF showed much higher photocatalytic H₂ evolution rates (HER = 177.50 μmol h⁻¹) than that of Py-FTP-BT-COF (HER = 57.50 μmol h⁻¹) and the non-halogenated Py-HTP-BT-COF (HER = 21.56 μmol h⁻¹). Moreover, the AQE of Py-CITP-BT-COF reaches up to 8.45% at 420 nm which is among one of the highest values reported in COF based photocatalysts.[²¹] These results demonstrate that halogenation in BT unit plays a critical role in modulating the electronic structures of BT-COFs owing to the large electronegativity of chlorine and fluorine atoms.[³⁴,³⁵] Consequently, charge recombination can be inhibited in halogen substituted BT-COFs along with reduced energy barrier for the H₂ evolution reaction. Furthermore, the cycling stability test confirmed that both the crystallinity and chemical structure of Py-CITP-BT-COF remained intact after photocatalytic tests. Thus, these halogenated BT-COFs are very promising in harnessing sunlight and water to produce clean solar fuels. This newly developed halogenation strategy not only expands the COFs skeleton engineering toolbox but also provides a straightforward methodology for developing novel COFs for future solar energy conversion.

## Results and Discussion

Py-XTP-BT-COFs (X = H, F, Cl) were designed based on a well-established D-A built-in strategy to enhance the charge separation efficiency and facilitate the light absorption. The terphenyl based diamine (HTP-BT-NH₂) annulated with electron-deficient BT acceptor unit was prepared according to the reported procedures.[³⁶] The other two diamines (XTP-BT-NH₂, X = F, Cl) were synthesized by a similar procedure while using 4,7-dibromo-5,6-difluorobenzo[c][1,2,5]thiadiazole or 4,7-dibromo-5,6-dichlorobenzo[c][1,2,5]thiadiazole as precursors (Figure S1-S3 and Scheme S1-S3). The other building block of 4,4',4'',4'''-(pyrene-1,3,6,8-tetrayl)tetrabenzaldehyde (Py-CHO) was selected as the central docking site because it can easily create highly crystalline frameworks with excellent photoelectric performances.[³⁷,³⁸] In this way, Py-XTP-BT-COFs (X = H, F, Cl) were readily obtained via screening different condensation conditions (Figure 1a and Scheme S4). The detailed structures of Py-XTP-BT-COFs were unambiguously characterized by various measurements (see supporting information). Compared with the monomers of diamines and Py-CHO, the disappearance of N-H stretching bands (3300-3400 cm⁻¹) and the attenuation of the C=O bond at 1692 cm⁻¹ were observed in the Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra of Py-XTP-BT-COFs. In addition, the characteristic IR stretching bands related to C=N linkages emerged at around 1626 cm⁻¹ (Figure S4). Properly aligned electronic structures are critical to achieve high efficiency in photocatalytic water splitting. Accordingly, synchrotron radiation photoemission spectroscopy (SRPES) and UV-vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (UV-DRS) were employed to determine the band structures of Py-XTP-BT-COFs. As shown in Figure S18a, the work functions ($\Phi$) of Py-HTP-BT-COF, Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF were calculated to be 3.32, 3.50 and 3.61 eV, respectively. On the other hand, the valence band spectra (Figure S18b) indicated that the valence band maximum (VBM) of Py-HTP-BT-COF, Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF were 1.69, 1.78 and 1.98 eV below the Fermi level, respectively. Therefore, the VBM level of Py-HTP-BT-COF, Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF are estimated to be -5.01, -5.28 and -5.59 eV correlated to the vacuum level, respectively. The optical band gaps calculated from Tauc plots were ~2.25 eV, 2.36 eV and 2.34 eV for Py-HTP-BT-COF, Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF (Figure 3a), respectively, suggesting that all Py-XTP-BT-COFs are suitable for absorbing visible light for photocatalysis. $^{[44]}$ Meanwhile, the conduction band minimum (CBM) positions of Py-HTP-BT-COF, Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF were estimated to be -2.76, -2.92 and -3.25 eV versus vacuum level, respectively, which should be able to reduce protons to H₂. As shown in Figure 3b, compared to the redox potentials (-4.02 eV versus the vacuum level) required for proton reduction at pH = 7, $^{[44]}$ the electronic band structures of all Py-XTP-BT-COFs are well fitted for visible-light-driven hydrogen generation.

The origins of the enhanced photocatalytic performances of these halogenated COFs were further investigated. Transient photocurrent measurements and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were first employed to investigate the charge separation and migration behaviors of different Py-XTP-BT-COFs. Compared to Py-HTP-BT-COF, the transient photocurrents of Py- CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF are significantly enhanced, indicating the fast photoresponse of Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py- FTP-BT-COF (Figure 4a). Additionally, the dramatically reduced resistance also suggested that the charge transfer barrier was reduced with increased charge density in Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF (Figure 4b). Moreover, to further probe the dynamics of photogenerated charge carriers, the average lifetimes of the photogenerated carriers of Py-XTP-BT-COFs were evaluated by means of transient open-circuit voltage decay (OCVD) measurements. As shown in Figure 4c, both Py-CITP- BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF exhibit much higher open-circuit voltages with slower photovoltage decays, reflecting higher energetic and longer lifetime of photoexcited carriers in Py-CITP- BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF. The measured average lifetimes of photogenerated carriers in Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT- COF are about 1.9 and 1.4 times larger than that of Py-HTP-BT- COF, implying that halogenation can effectively suppress charge recombination in Py-XTP-BT-COFs (Figure 4d). Moreover, as shown in Figure S22a, the photoluminescence intensities of Py- CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF are reduced compared to that of Py-HTP-BT-COF, indicating that the radiative recombination in halogenated Py-XTP-BT-COFs are substantially suppressed. ¹⁴⁵¹ Additionally, the lifetime of photogenerated charge carriers were measured by means of time-correlated single photon counting (TCSPC). The fitting results indicate that the average lifetimes of Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF and Py-HTP-BT-COF are ~3.88, 2.85, and 2.13 ns (Figure S22b), respectively. The prolonged lifetimes in halogenated Py-XTP-BT- COFs correspond to a slower charge recombination of the excited states. These observations suggested the effective charge separation in Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF compared to Py-HTP-BT-COF, which should be beneficial for photocatalytic water splitting. ¹¹⁹⁴⁶¹ In addition, the electronegativity of halogen also favored the delocalization of π-electrons in conjugated cores, ¹³⁴³⁵¹ which can further contribute to the enhanced photocatalytic performances for Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP- BT-COF over Py-HTP-BT-COF.

To further unravel the influence of halogen modulation on the photocatalytic H₂ evolution of the Py-XTP-BT-COFs, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out to investigate the H₂ evolution reaction pathways on Py-XTP-BT- COF. ¹⁴⁷⁴⁸¹ The Gibbs free energy changes (ΔG) of hydrogen adsorption on different carbon atoms (site 1 to site 9) of Py-XTP- BT-COFs were evaluated accordingly (Figure S23). Interestingly, the energy barrier for adsorbed H* intermediate formed on the halogen substituted carbon atoms (site 4) of Py-CITP-BT-COF and Py-FTP-BT-COF were remarkably reduced by 0.83 eV and 0.15 eV, respectively, compared to that of non-halogenated Py- HTP-BT-COF (Figure 5). Besides, other sites on the benzothiadiazole and tetraphenylpyrene units of Py-XTP-BT-COF show similar H* intermediate adsorption energy and also exhibit a larger energy barrier compare to the halogen substituted carbon atoms. These results collectively suggested that halogen modulation in BT-COFs could effectively decrease the energy

## Conclusion
In summary, we developed a series of isostructural Py-XTP-BT-COF ($X=$H, F, Cl) photocatalysts for efficient $H_2$ production under visible light irradiation. Our study revealed that halogen modulation in COFs can not only promote efficient charge separation in the conjugated skeletons but can also lower the activation energy for $H_2$ formation. Consequently, Py-CITP-BT-COF exhibited superior photocatalytic performances (HER = 177.50 $\mu mol\ h^{-1}$) with AQE as high as 8.45% at 420 nm. The experimental and computational results unambiguously suggested that the facilitated charge separation and reduced energy barrier for $H_2$ formation in these halogenated BT-COFs are the primary reasons accounting for the enhanced photocatalytic performances. Our findings illustrated that subtle changes in the scaffolds of COFs could dramatically alter the corresponding photocatalytic performances, and provide a new strategy for modulating physicochemical properties of COFs *via* precise skeleton engineering.

A series of isostructural Py-XTP-BT-COFs (X = H, F, Cl) are systematically investigated as the catalysts for photocatalytic H₂ production under visible light irradiation. The halogen modulation of Py-HTP-BT-COFs realizes effective regulation of proper electronic structures, boosting the photo-charge separation and reduction of the energy barrier for achieving highly efficient H₂ production.
