## ABSTRACT
Pursuing effective and generalized strategies for modulating the electronic structures of atomically dispersed nanozymes with remarkable catalytic performance is exceptionally attractive yet challenging. Herein, we developed a facile "formamide condensation and carbonization" strategy to fabricate a library of single-atom ($M_1$-NC; 6 types) and dual-atom ($M_1/M_2$-NC; 13 types) metal-nitrogen-carbon nanozymes ($M$ = Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Ru, Cu) to reveal peroxidase- (POD-) like activities. The $Fe_1Co_1$-NC dual-atom nanozyme with $Fe_1$-$N_4$/$Co_1$-$N_4$ coordination displayed the highest POD-like activity. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations revealed that the Co atom site synergistically affects the d-band center position of the Fe atom site and served as the second reaction center, which contributes to better POD-like activity. Finally, $Fe_1Co_1$ NC was shown to be effective in inhibiting tumor growth both *in vitro* and *in vivo*, suggesting that diatomic synergy is an effective strategy for developing artificial nanozymes as novel nanocatalytic therapeutics.

**KEYWORDS:** Dual-atom catalysts, nanozymes, peroxidase-like activity, synergistic effects

Single-atom nanozymes (SAzymes) have accumulated enormous research interest in biomedical engineering fields such as antibacterial, tumor therapeutic, and tissue engineering due to their well-defined electronic structures/coordination environment, adjustable catalytic activity, and maximum atomic utilization efficiency. $^{1-4}$ Specifically, SAzymes exhibited impressive peroxidase (POD)-like activity, thus efficiently decomposing hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$) into reactive oxygen species (ROS) by initiating intratumoral catalytic reactions under weakly acidic conditions. $^{5-8}$ Alternatively, SAzymes are suitable for enhancing POD-like activity by finely tuning the local coordination structure of the active site. $^{9,10}$ To date, despite ongoing efforts based on SAzymes having been explored to trigger diverse types of biocatalytic reactions, unsatisfactory reaction efficiency remains a great challenge due to insufficient insight into single-atom site types and interactions. In particular, SAzymes typically have only one type of single-atom active site, which makes it difficult to optimize catalytic activity for reactions involving multiple intermediates. The phenomenon that an individual active site determines a fixed adsorption pattern is called scaling relationship limit (SRL), which is a huge limitation in the development of SAzymes. $^{11-13}$ In this regard, optimization of active sites for realizing target-designed SAzymes is highly required to reach desired properties.

Dual-atom catalysts (DACs) have higher chemical catalytic and selectivity properties by perfectly inheriting the advantages of single-atom catalysts (SACs), and recently drawn attracted great attention in the field of energy-related catalysis. $^{14-17}$ DACs have been pioneered for the design of homonuclear/heteronuclear bimetallic sites in order to pursue better adaptability in optimizing metal active sites and the adsorption of different types of reaction intermediates. The addition of homonuclear/heteronuclear sites induces multiple tops or bridge active sites, substrate/intermediate absorption configurations, and electronic structure synergism through metal-metal cooperativity, potentially generating a new type of freedom-breaking SRL in diverse intermediates of reaction path, resulting in greatly enhance intrinsic catalytic performance. $^{18-22}$ Despite the vigorous development of DACs in the field of energy catalysis, however, in-depth exploration and systematic validation of the role of diatomic sites in dual-atom nanozymes (DAzymes) to explore the corresponding structure-activity relationship in biocatalytic reactions are rarely reported.

Taking Fe₁Co₁-NC as an example, the morphology of the synthesized nanomaterials showed amorphous particle morphology in transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Figure 1B) but no nanoparticles or nanoclusters in high-resolution TEM (HRTEM, Figure S1), similar to the results of NC (Figure S2A-B). Compared with the elemental mapping (Figure S2C) and valence states in X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Figure S2D) of NC, Fe₁Co₁-NC presented evidence for the coexistence of Fe and Co (Figure 1C and Figure S3A), this indicates that NC firmly anchors the transition metal atoms. The high-resolution Fe 2p (Figure S3B) and Co 2p XPS spectra (Figure S3C) confirmed the existence of the oxidation states Fe and Co in Fe₁Co₁-NC and the absence of metallic Fe and Co. The hydrodynamic size and zeta potential of Fe₁Co₁-NC are 192.6 nm and +2.2 mV, respectively (Figure S4). It is worth noticing that Fe₁Co₁-NC exhibited good stability when stored in PBS or Tris-HCl solutions at ambient condition for five days (Figure S5). Moreover, sharp XRD diffraction peaks are observed around 27° for Fe₁Co₁-NC and NC (Figure S6A), which match the (002) peak of the carbon nitride phase.²³,²⁴ Raman spectra of Fe₁Co₁-NC and NC (Figure S6B) show two low-intensity peaks at ~1350 cm⁻¹ and ~1508 cm⁻¹, respectively, which are attributed to the D and G bands of formamide-derived carbonaceous materials.²⁵ Additionally, aberration-corrected HAADF-STEM image (Figure 1D) demonstrated that many bright spots are randomly dispersed in the whole sample, corresponding to single-atom Fe and Co sites embedded in formamide-derived NC. As shown in the X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) diagram (Figure 1E), Fe K-edge spectra revealed that the absorption edge position of Fe₁Co₁-NC is located between FeO and Fe₃O₄, indicating the positive valence of Fe is between +2 and +3. Likewise, the Co K-edge XANES curve of Fe₁Co₁-NC is very close to the Co₃O₄ curve, suggesting that the positive valence state of the Co atom is also between +2 and +3 (Figure 1G). The extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) of Fe₁Co₁-NC shows two prominent peaks at ~1.50 Å (Figure 1F) and ~1.38 Å (Figure 1H), corroborating the existence of Fe−N and Co−N coordination configurations, respectively.²⁶,²⁷ The EXAFS fitting results suggest that the Fe atom is coordinated by 4.38 N atoms (bond lengths of Fe-N: 2.03 Å) with 1.0 O atom (bond lengths of Fe−O: 1.77 Å), and the Co atom is coordinated by 4.0 N atoms (bond lengths of Co−N: 2.01 Å) with 1.0 O atoms (bond lengths of Co−O: 1.79 Å) (Figure S7, Table S1). The wavelet transforms (WT, Figure 1I) contour plots of Fe₁Co₁-NC displayed one maximum intensity approximately at 4 Å⁻¹, which is assigned to Fe−N and Co−N coordination and different from the Fe foil and Co foil.²⁸ A series of SAzyme and DAzymes with different metal centers can be prepared by simply replacing the Fe/Co metal precursor (Figures S8−S26). The formation of graphitic carbon in SAzymes and DAzymes is identified by XRD (Figure S27) and Raman spectrum (Figure S28), which means that the hydrothermal synthesis strategy of formamide is universal.

To explore the mechanism of catalytic activity between NC, $Fe_{1}$-NC, and $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC, an electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) technique was conducted to identify the generation of the ROS type in the catalytic process. The observation of the characteristic quadruplet signal of the DMPO/•OH adduct with a significant intensity ratio of 1:2:2:1 in $Fe_{1}$-NC and $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC confirmed the formation of the •OH by the Fenton reaction (Figure 2C).$^{35-37}$ Moreover, the peak intensity in the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC system is much higher than that of $Fe_{1}$-NC, indicating that $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC is more prone to •OH generation and is consistent with POD-like activities. The Michaelis-Menten constants $(K_{m})$ of $H_{2}O_{2}$ on $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC and $Fe_{1}$-NC are 0.40 and 1.50 mM (Figure 2D), respectively, demonstrating that $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC has a higher affinity toward $H_{2}O_{2}$ than $Fe_{1}$-NC. To investigate the kinetic mechanism of $H_{2}O_{2}$ activation, the activation energies $(E_{a})$ of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC and $Fe_{1}$-NC are achieved according to the measured kinetics of TMB oxidation at different temperatures (Figure 2E) and the corresponding Arrhenius equation. As exemplified in Figure 2F, the $E_{a}$ of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC is 9.46 kJ/mol, which is 4.3-fold lower than that of $Fe_{1}$-NC (41.05 kJ/mol), indicating that $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC with dual-atomic sites can effectively reduce the energy barrier for catalytic reactions.

The enhanced POD-like catalytic mechanism of the Fe- series DAzymes is further revealed by density functional theory (DFT) calculations. As reported in previous work, the absorption energy between $H_{2}O_{2}$ and metal can be used as a descriptor to study POD-like activity.$^{38}$ The electronic structure calculation of Fe-series DAzymes shows that the introduction of heteronuclear atoms can improve the d-band center and electrons of the Fe site, thereby improving the adsorption of $H_{2}O_{2}$ (Figure S31−S34). Besides, the possible routes and corresponding free-energy profiles for the oxidation of TMB by dissociating $H_{2}O_{2}$ on $Fe_{1}$-NC and $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC are calculated under acidic conditions (Figure 3C). As shown in Figure 3A,B, $H_{2}O_{2}$ is easily adsorbed on top of the single-atom Fe sites of $Fe_{1}$-NC and $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC to form activated $H_{2}O_{2}$* (* represents the adsorption status). Subsequently, the activated $H_{2}O_{2}$ molecules dissociated homogeneously into two *OH ions on the metal sites. The dissociation energy of $H_{2}O_{2}$ on $Fe_{1}Co_{1}$-NC is −1.98 eV and lower than that of $Fe_{1}$-NC (−1.44 eV), which may be due to the migration of one of the *OH to the Co site to make the dissociation easier.$^{39}$ Finally, adsorbed OH* reacts with protonated $H^{+}$ and TMB in a weakly acidic environment to form $H_{2}O$ and oxTMB. As exhibited by the differential charge (Figure 3D), the number of electrons obtained by 2OH* on Fe₁Co₁-NC ($-0.83$) is smaller than that of Fe₁-NC ($-0.90$), which will further stimulate the behavior of OH* to extract electrons from TMB. Overall, the reason for the higher POD-like activity of Fe₁Co₁-NC at the dual-atom site originating from the DFT findings can be attributed to the following two aspects. First, the doping of heteronuclear metal sites can tune the electronic structure and d-band center of the material, thereby optimizing the adsorption of substrates. Besides, the second atomic site can act as an active site as well as an adsorption site, stabilizing the intermediate and making the reaction easier. Photothermal measurements demonstrated that the progressive doping of single atomic Fe and Co could reduce the band of NC and further improve the photothermal

Considering the excellent photothermal and POD-like performance of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$, it can be considered a therapeutic agent for tumor therapy (Figure 4A). Initially, B16-F10 cells exhibited the lowest viability (7.09%) in the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2} + NIR$ group $(200\ \mu g/mL)$, demonstrating their good therapeutic effect (Figure S38). Subsequently, the possible mechanism of cell death is explored by measuring the generation of intracellular ROS by DCFH-DA. As demonstrated in Figure 4B, the corresponding green fluorescence was negligible in the control, NC, $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$, and $NC + H_{2}O_{2}$ groups. On the contrary, a moderate amount of green fluorescence can be observed in the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2}$ group, revealing that the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ has a certain ability to catalyze the production of $^{\bullet}OH$ from intracellular $H_{2}O_{2}$ and exhibited satisfactory POD-like behavior. As expected, the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2} + NIR$ group exhibited the strongest green fluorescence in all groups. The intracellular ROS experiments indicated that the POD-like performance of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ could be further synergistically amplified by the photothermal effect, thus increasing the concentration of intracellular $^{\bullet}OH$. Cells are costained with Calcein-AM (living cells fluoresce in green) and PI (dead cells fluoresce in red) after treatment with six groups and imaged with a confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM). B16-F10 cells treated with $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2} + NIR$ group exhibited the strongest red fluorescence $(57.55 \pm 5.00)$ and weakest green signal, in contrast to the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + NIR$ group $(4.12 \pm 1.02)$, $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2}$ $(2.78 \pm 0.80)$, $NC + H_{2}O_{2}$ $(0.48 \pm 0.16)$, $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ $(1.75 \pm 0.33)$, NC $(0.47 \pm 0.25)$, and control $(0.27 \pm 0.19)$ groups (Figure 4C). Besides, the apoptosis/necrosis of tumor cells in different treatment groups is determined by the typical flow cytometry after Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate/propidium iodide (FITC/PI) staining. The apoptotic ratio (Q2 + Q3, 78.90%) induced by the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2} + NIR$ group is markedly higher than that of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + NIR$ (11.68%), $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2}$ (6.03%), $NC + H_{2}O_{2}$ (2.38%), $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ (4.51%), NC (0.80%), and control (0.57%) groups (Figure 4D). In vitro experimental results demonstrated that $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NCs$ can not only perform POD-like nanocatalytic therapy to induce tumor cell apoptosis but also further amplify it through the near-infrared photothermal effect to achieve effective synergistic ablation of cancer cells. The decrease of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) is related to the increase of intracellular ROS levels leading to cell apoptosis, which can be further confirmed by the JC-1 assay (red fluorescence: healthy mitochondria; green fluorescence: opposite). As expected, the red/green fluorescence ratio of the control group was 533.23 but decreased to 23.07, 9.06, 2.15, 1.48, and 0.08 in the NC, $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$, $NC + H_{2}O_{2}$, $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2}$, and $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + H_{2}O_{2} + NIR$ groups, respectively (Figure 4E-J).

The excellent synergistic therapeutic effect of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ on in vitro cells prompted us to further explore its inhibitory effect on tumor growth in vivo using tumor-bearing mice. The hemolysis experimental results show that $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ has good in vivo biosafety due to the low hemolysis rate in the series of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ concentrations (5, 25, 50, and $100\ \mu g/mL$) (Figure S39). We then treated the tumor-bearing mice with an intravenous injection of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ (Figure S40A). Infrared thermal (IR, Figure S40B) imaging of tumor-bearing mice showed that the surface temperature of the tumor site increased to 51.4 and $68.8\ ^{\circ}C$ within 5 min after NC and $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ injection, respectively, much higher than PBS $(38.5\ ^{\circ}C)$. Photographs of representative mouse tumors of different groups are taken on 0 and 10 days after treatment (Figure S40C) and further dissected (Figure S41A). The results showed that the treatment effect of the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + NIR$ group is excellent, showing that the subtle combination of nanocatalytic therapy and photothermal therapy could inhibit tumor growth and significantly enhance the treatment efficiency. In addition, the tumor size growth of the 5 groups is measured every 2 days, and the relative tumor volume change during treatment is calculated. As depicted in Figure S41B, except for the control group, the remaining 4 treatment groups showed varying degrees of tumor-suppression during the 10-day treatment. Remarkably, the strongest tumor growth inhibition is recorded in the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + NIR$ group, showing the antitumor effect of the photothermal-enhanced nanocatalytic therapy. No evident decline in the body weight profiles of tumor-bearing mice in different treatment groups over the duration of the therapeutic treatment period revealed negligible toxicity of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ or NC toward mice (Figure S41C). Meanwhile, routine blood tests and biochemical analysis are performed after the mice are sacrificed to collect eyeball blood. The concentration of some indexes fluctuated within the normal range in most mice (Figure S42). Additionally, the tumor tissue destruction from tumor-bearing mice after various treatments were examined by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL) staining. As revealed by the H&E staining analysis and the TUNEL immunofluorescent staining (Figure S43), the $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC + NIR$ group led to significant apoptosis of cancer cells in comparison with other groups, further confirming the superior in vivo antitumor ability of $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ nanozyme. Finally, the inflammation or deformation of the major organ in each group after various treatments is negligible according to the pathological analysis of H&E staining (Figure S44), which indicates that $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ has good biocompatibility in vivo treatments.

In summary, we have reported the synthesis of a library of SAzymes and DAzymes containing 19 different kinds of $M_{1}$-NC or $M_{1}M_{2}-NC$ to reveal POD-like catalytic activities. The experimental results show that dual-atom site $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$ exhibited the most excellent POD-like activity and superior photothermal conversion efficiency. DFT results suggested that the presence of diatomic sites can not only break the SRL by synergistically optimizing the adsorption of substrates or intermediates but also act as an active center to reduce the reaction barrier. In addition, extremely low side effects and exceptionally high in vivo and in vitro antitumor effects are simultaneously achieved in $Fe_{1}Co_{1}-NC$, highlighting its potential for broad clinical therapeutic applications. This work presents new insight into the dual-atom structure-performance relationship on synergistic tumor therapy at the atomic level, shedding light on the rational design of dual-atom nanozymes for tumor catalytic therapy and biomedicine.
