ABSTRACT: The controllable synthesis of metal-based nano-
clusters for heterogeneous catalytic reactions has received
considerable attention. Nevertheless, manufacturing these architec-
tures, while avoiding aggregation and retaining surface activity,
remains challenging. Herein, for the first time we designed NiCoFe-
Prussian blue analogue (PBA) nanocages as a support for in situ
dispersion and anchoring of polymetallic phosphide nanoparticles
(pMP-NPs). Benefiting from the porous surfaces and the
synergistic effects between pMP-NPs and the cyano groups in
PBA, the NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages exhibit a
significantly enhanced catalytic activity for oxygen evolution
reaction (OER) with an overpotential of 223 mV at 10 mA cm⁻²
and a Tafel slope of 78 mV dec⁻¹, outperforming the NiCoFe-PBA
nanocubes, NiCoFe-P nanocages, NiFe-P-NP@NiFe-PBA nanocubes, and CoFe-P-NP@CoFe-PBA nanoboxes. This work not only
offers the synthesis strategy of in situ anchoring pMP-NPs on PBA nanocages but also provides a new insight into optimized Gibbs
free energy of OER by regulating electron transfer from metallic phosphides to PBA substrate.

KEYWORDS: in situ anchoring, polymetallic phosphide nanoparticles, porous Prussian blue analogue nanocages, controlled synthesis,
oxygen evolution catalysis

■ INTRODUCTION

Coordination polymers (CPs)/frameworks (CFs), including
metal−organic frameworks (MOFs), are promising porous
materials that have attracted significant attention on account of
their preeminent and unique properties, for example, tunable
chemical composition, high porosity, and large surface area.¹⁻⁹
Prussian blue analogs (PBAs), typical coordination frame-
works, can be described by the general formula
$A_xM_1[M_2(CN)_6]_y·zH_2O$, where $M_1/M_2$ are transition metals
linked by the cyano groups, and A is a cation embedded in the
interspace of the PBA frameworks.¹⁰⁻¹² PB/PBAs are
promising nanomaterials for application in electrochemical
energy storage and electrochemical catalysis.¹³⁻¹⁵ For example,
Yu and co-workers reported that the manufacture of
unconventional cyano group vacancies in PBA nanomaterials
can strengthen the oxygen evolution catalysis.¹⁶ Therefore, the
development of PBAs with micro/nanostructures could
encourage more electrochemical applications.

Electrochemical water splitting is a hopeful technology for
producing oxygen and hydrogen from renewable energy
sources to achieve environment-friendly fuels.¹⁷⁻¹⁹ Notwith-
standing, the kinetically slow oxygen evolution reaction (OER,
the reaction $4OH^- = 2H_2O + 4e^- + O_2$) impedes the efficient
realization of water splitting.²⁰⁻²³ Transition metal phosphides
(TMPs), which could be derived from PBAs or MOFs, have
emerged as promising nonprecious metal electrochemical
catalysts owing to their high catalytic activity.²⁴⁻²⁶ Note-
worthy, the OER performance of monometallic phosphides
can be further enhanced by doping one or more metal atoms.²⁷
For example, Wu et al. reported $Ni_{0.1}Co_{0.9}P$ nanosheets for
high-efficiency water splitting, which can be attributed to the
favorable electronic states resulting from the synergistic
interplay between cobalt and nickel.²⁵ Recently, Feng et al.
synthesized a series of 2D TMPs (e.g., $Ni_{12}P_5$, $Co_2P$, and
$Co_xFe_{2-x}P$) for promoting OER due to the improved surface
exposure and charge-transport modulation.²⁸ Notwithstanding,
the obtained TMPs usually need high calcination temperatures,

With an appropriate design, the composite catalyst combining pMP- NPs and PBAs may not only take advantage of the PBA's well- defined porous architecture and stability but also possess an optimal free energy $(\Delta G)$ in oxygen evolution processes, both of which are favorable to OER performance. However, few previous studies have attempted to combine pMP-NPs with PBAs for promoting the oxygen evolution catalysis.

Herein, we develop a partial phosphidation strategy for in situ anchoring highly catalytically active polymetallic phos- phide nanoparticles (NiCoFe-P-NPs) on the porous NiCoFe- PBA cages. The use of PBA porous nanocages as the host matrix could effectively improve the high conductivity and stability of pMP-NPs and fully expose the catalytically active sites of metal phosphides. Notably, thanks to the numerous cyano groups in PBA nanocages, the final porous NiCoFe-P- NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages provided a favorable electronic structure at the active sites, which was corroborated via both theoretically computed adsorption energetics and electro- catalytic activity measurements. Consequently, the porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages show good stability in alkaline solutions and excellent OER catalytic performance with a low overpotential of $223 mV$ at $10 mA cm^{-2}$ and Tafel slopes of $78 mV dec^{-1}$ . The NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages hold promise as low-cost and efficient electro- catalysts for oxygen evolution.

■ RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The formation processes of TMP@PBA composites and NiCoFe-P are schematically presented in Figure 1. First, NiFe-PBA, CoFe-PBA, and NiCoFe-PBA nanocubes were prepared as precursors by a facile liquid-phase coprecipitation method (Figure S1). The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image show the NiCoFe-PBA precursor with the relatively uniform particle diameter of approximately 360 nm and solid nature (Figure S2a−c). Under the phosphidation temperature of 300 °C, NiFe-PBA is easily decomposed into lesser broken cubes upon heat treatment (Figure S3a,b), while the stability of CoFe-PBA is too high to be adequately reacted. Only the NiCoFe-PBA nanocubes with suitable thermal stability can form a pMP-NP@PBA composite with porous cagelike nanostructure (Figure S3e,f). When the phosphidation temperature increases to 350 °C, the hollow CoFe-P-NP@ CoFe-PBA nanoboxes (Figure S3c,d) and NiCoFe-P nanocages (Figure S3g,h) can be obtained from CoFe-PBA and NiCoFe-PBA precursors, respectively.

The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Figure S4) reveal that the diffraction peaks of the NiFe-PBA and CoFe-PBA nanocubes match well with the cubic $KNiFe(CN)_6$ (PDF no. 51-1897) and $Co_3(Fe(CN)_6)_2·H_2O$ (PDF no. 82-2284), respectively. The pattern of the NiCoFe-PBA precursor coincides well with the standard patterns of both NiFe-PBA phase and CoFe-PBA phase. The diffraction peaks of the NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages in Figure 2r are well assigned to the standard patterns of CoP (PDF no. 29-0497), $Ni_2P$ (PDF no. 89-4864), FeP (PDF no. 89-2746), NiFe-PBA (PDF no. 51-1897), and CoFe-PBA (PDF no. 82-2284). Additionally, the XRD patterns reveal the formation of the NiFe-P-NP@NiFe-PBA, CoFe-P-NP@CoFe-PBA, and NiC- oFe-P.

The phosphidation reaction starts with the eight corners of the NiCoFe-PBA cubes. The long-term durability of the porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages was demonstrated by the chronoamperometric responses ($i-t$) test (no obvious decrease in 1.0 M KOH over the 10 h operation) (Figure S15). Furthermore, Figure 4i reveals that the obtained polarization curve of the NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocage electrode after successive CV sweeps of 2000 cycles is similar to the initial one, indicating the robust durability of this catalyst for OER in an alkaline solution. Significantly, the NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages could expose more active surface metal sites at multiphase (catalyst-electrolyte-oxygen, CEO) boundaries to facilitate the mass transport of OER-relevant species. $^{51,52}$ These features endow NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages an outstanding oxygen evolution performances in alkaline conditions (Figure 5a-d). It is remarkable that although metal phosphide catalysts are easily oxidized, the architecture of the porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages is still maintained after the stability measurements (Figures 5e and S16). Moreover, the nanoparticles in porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages are not evidently agglomerated after catalytic reaction (Figure S17). These may be attributed to the protective effect of the cagelike PBA porous nanoframework (Figure S18). To analyze the state transformation in the OER process, the porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocage was carefully collected and investigated after the continuous CV cycles. The EDX spectrum of the porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocage after the CV stability test is presented in Figure S16. After 2000 cycles of continuous CV test, the Co, Ni, Fe, and P contents in the porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocage decreased from 16.08, 2.30, 12.24, and 9.55 wt % to 16.04, 2.02, 10.06, and 2.91 wt %, respectively, and the O element increased from 14.46 to 15.70 wt % (Figure S19). The results indicate that the polymetallic phosphide particles of the catalyst are partially converted to metal (oxy)hydroxides or/and metal oxides in the OER process.

To further analyze the influence of the cyano groups in PBA to metal phosphide for OER catalysis, all spin-polarized density function theory (DFT) calculations were performed by using Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) software. The PBAs can be described by the general formula $\mathrm{A}_{y} \mathrm{M}_{1}\left[\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathrm{CN})_{6}\right]_{y} \cdot z \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$, and the metal phosphide is $\mathrm{M}_{x} \mathrm{P}$. Because a large number of previous experimental and theoretical studies have discussed in detail the synergistic effects between polymetallic phosphides for electrocatalysis and the uncertainty of multiphase complex systems (CoFe-PBA, NiFe-PBA, CoP, $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{P}$, and FeP phase), $^{54,55}$ this study analyzes the influence of specific PBA phase (when $M = M_{1} =$ Co, $M_{2} =$ Fe; $x = z = 1$, $y = 2$) to metal phosphide phase in OER catalysis. The detailed OER calculations were described in the Supporting Information.

The DFT calculations give insight into the improved performance of the complex system (Figure 5f g). On the pristine CoP surface, the potential-determining step (PDS) is from *O to *OOH, with Gibbs free energy change of 2.79 eV. In the complex system, the *OH and *OOH species was relatively stabilized on the Co-P cluster, while the relative energy of *O is uphill. As a result, the Gibbs free energy change from *O to *OOH is significantly decreased to 2.35 eV. The PDS becomes the formation of $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ from *OOH with free energy change of 2.58 eV. As shown in Figure 5h, the charge-density analysis indicates that electrons transfer from the CoP cluster to $\mathrm{Co}_{3}\left[\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{CN})_{6}\right]_{2} \cdot \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ substrate due to the strong electron withdrawing ability of the cyano group. On the basis of the Bader charge calculation, $^{56}$ the partial charge of Co-P cluster is +3.44 lel. Fewer electrons in the cluster make deeper oxidation of cluster to *O more difficult and lower the barrier from *O to *OOH. As a result, the PDS shifts from *OOH formation to oxygen generation with barrier decreasing from 2.79 to 2.58 eV. Therefore, the electron transfer from CoP cluster to CoFe-PBA substrate could lead to the optimized Gibbs free energy for OER. The calculated results are also applicable to multiphase metal phosphides and multiphase PBA systems, and the effect has more advantages in multiphase systems due to the synergistic effect of polymetallic phosphide for oxygen evolution.

## CONCLUSION

In summary, we have developed a partial phosphidation tactic for in situ dispersion and anchoring NiCoFe-phosphide nanoparticles within the porous NiCoFe-PBA nanocages. The unique structure of the porous NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA nanocages can facilitate the discharge of the generated gas

and expose more catalytic active sites. This produces a strong synergistic effect between the NiCoFe-PBA cages and the NiCoFe-P nanoparticles, which display an optimized OER performance, outperforming the NiCoFe-P nanocages, NiC-oFe-PBA nanocubes, NiFe-P-NP@NiFe-PBA nanocubes, and CoFe-P-NP@CoFe-PBA nanoboxes. Furthermore, DFT calculations indicate that the catalytic ability of metal phosphides can further be improved by being combined with porous PBA nanocages because the electrons can transfer from the metal phosphide clusters to PBA substrate by the strong electron withdrawing ability of cyano group. Therefore, the final NiCoFe-P-NP@NiCoFe-PBA porous nanocages reveal an excellent oxygen evolution performance with a small overpotential of 223 mV at 10 mA cm⁻² and an outstanding stability in an alkaline solution. This promising strategy not only provides valuable insights to in situ anchoring of pMP-NPs on PBA nanocages but also could be extended to the optimization of the electrochemical activity of other catalysts by combining the high catalytic activity species with the PBA/MOF-based substance, which opens broad prospects in the fields of environmental and energy.
