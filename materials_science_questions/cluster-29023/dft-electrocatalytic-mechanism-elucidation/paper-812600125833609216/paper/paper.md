# Understanding coordination modification strategy on metal organic framework-based system for efficient water oxidation

### Abstract

Rational modification of coordination environment on metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have a strong impact on the performance of heterogeneous catalysis. Here, we demonstrate how tuning the surrounding environment on metals centers of MOFs can substantially enhance the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) efficiency. The MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs nano-heterostructure was formed by anchoring MIL-59(FeNi) MOFs on urchin-like Co nanosheet arrays. Metal-metal synergistic effect was and validated to influence the coordination environment on MOFs, DFT calculations suggests that the charge transfer from the Co substrate to MIL-59 on a formed -COOCo geometry should be responsible for excellent activity. Acting as OER electrocatalyst, the MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs requires a low overpotential of 216 mV to arrive at $20\ \text{mA}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ and obtains a low Tafel slope value of $38.46\ \text{mV}\ \text{dec}^{-1}$. The facile synthetic route and proposed strategy would arouse more attention to design and utilize high-performance metal-hybrid supported MOFs for highly efficient electrochemical processes.

### Keywords:
Electrodeposition; Metal-organic frameworks; Heterostructure; Oxygen evolution reaction; DFT calculation

### 1. Introduction

Oxygen electrochemistry is regarded as the corner-stone reaction on developing sustainable energy conversion and storage system [1-4]. The oxidation of water involves the process of producing oxygen would liberate the protons and electrons participated in the generation of fuels and other valuable molecules [5-8]. Yet, the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) proceeds the multiple proton-coupled electron transfer is kinetically sluggish, which requires efficient electrocatalysts to accelerate the reaction, decrease the overpotential, and thus harvesting improved energy conversion efficiency [9,10]. Currently, the well-acknowledged active OER catalysts are those Ru- and Ir-based materials regardless of acidic or alkaline environment [11,12], while the limited reserves and high-cost of those noble metals greatly impede the advancement of energy-related technology, like water splitting and rechargeable metal-air batteries [13-16]. Tremendous efforts are therefore taken to explore highly-active, low-cost and efficient alternatives including first-row transition bimetallic oxides [17], hydroxides [18], chalcogenides [19], nitrides [20], phosphides [21], carbon-matrixes [22], perovskites [23] and single atom materials [24]. However, most of those electrocatalysts are nanocomposites, whose OER performances are normally depended on particle size, morphology as well as synergistic effect [25-27]. Furthermore, the complicated components, elusive metal-metal impact, undesirable electronic structure regulation and poor crystalline structure also may hamper the elucidation of catalytic activity and reaction mechanism [28]. These problems thus call for the exploration and development of novel electrocatalysts, which ought to

have specific structure. Accordingly, to probe well-defined structure and mutual interaction on a formed electrocatalyst are crucial to uncover the factors affecting its improved OER performance.

Emerging as a new class of crystalline porous materials, metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) constructed by a self-assembly process between metal ions/clusters and organic bridging linkers have aroused enormous interest and attained rapid development in many fields over the past two decades [29]. The porous crystalline structure, tunable composition, ultrahigh surface area and easy prepared character enable MOFs have the earliest and most promising application on catalysis, it can be used as template and precursor to prepare diverse transition metal based electrocatalysts and functional carbons [30-32]. Other inexpensive coordination compounds of Prussian blue (PB) and its analogue (PBA) are also well studied for multiple applications by virtue of similar advantages as MOFs [33]. Notably, MOFs can be tuned at the molecular level and precisely characterized to elucidate the close connection between component, structure and performance [34,35]. Also, those well-designed MOFs can be advantageous to the theoretical prediction of electronic coupling and other interactions, thus resulting in a favorable investigation of the reaction mechanism toward catalysis [36]. However, MOFs generally possess undesirable stability when applying with higher oxidation/reduction potentials under certain catalytic reactions, including OER, hydrogen evolution reaction (HER), and oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) [37].

To conquer the bottle-neck problems of undesirable chemical stability on MOFs,

enormous approaches are conducted out by tuning the central metal ions/clusters as high-valence state or strengthening the metal-ligand bond [38,39]. When employing carboxylates as the target ligand, some metal ions with high-valence such as $Fe^{3+}$ [38], $Al^{3+}$ [40], $V^{3+}$ [41], $Cr^{3+}$ [42] and $Zr^{4+}$ [43] are usually used to reinforce the electrostatic interaction between the metal oxide cluster and bridging linkers [44]. Among the aforementioned MOFs formed with high-valent metal ions, their stable structure can be obtained but rarely reported with the direct application towards OER, except for the Fe-based bimetallic MOFs, which is predicted and proved as potential electrocatalyst to directly catalyze OER due to the coordinately unsaturated metal atoms (CUMAs) on metal nodes [45,46]. These works validate the stable and active OER performance on Fe based MOFs. Consequently, Fe based MOFs could serve as another promising alternatives to be comparable with other categories of stable MOFs when applying as electrocatalysts [12,46]. Meanwhile, coordination environment modification strategy is proposed on Fe based MOFs to partially substitute iron atom in trinuclear Fe-cluster with another metal atom (Co, Ni, Mn etc.), which would result in a coordination reconstruction on central metal ions/clusters [37,38]. In that case, the resulted MOFs constructed by connecting $Fe_{2}M$ clusters and bridge organic ligands can exhibit excellent OER performance and impressive stability [28]. Moreover, if other versatile metal based platform could hybridize with the bimetallic MOFs to acquire fast exportation of evolved gas, abundant active sites exposure and the effective contact between the catalyst and electrolyte, or even exert strengthened coordination modification effect on the formed MOFs system, the activity and


stability of $Fe_2M$ bimetallic MOFs may realize to the utmost.

Herein, we incorporate a coordination environment modification strategy to tailor the FeNi based MOFs i.e. $[Fe_2Ni(H_2O)]_3O(O_2CC_6H_4CO_2)_3\cdot9H_2O$ (denoted MIL-59(FeNi)) on urchin-like Co NSs as an integrated electrode to directly catalyze OER under the following considerations: 1) the Fe atom in central $Fe_3$ cluster can be substituted with another metal atom with a similar radius to form a bimetallic MOFs, which could cause the reconstruction of metal coordination environment to form stable structure; 2) urchin-like Co NSs can serve as an ideal platform to anchor enormous MIL-59(FeNi) on the porous substrate and tune the coordination environment of MOFs to further strengthen the OER activity of resulted MOFs; 3) DFT calculations can be used as powerful tool to preferably uncover the catalytic mechanism of the well-defined bimetallic MOFs. Accordingly, by choosing Fe and Ni atom as central metal clusters to coordinate the isophthalic acid (IPA) ligand, $Fe_2M$ based MOFs (MIL-59(FeNi)) are prepared and grafted on urchin-like Co nanosheet arrays to be a highly stable and efficient OER electrocatalyts. Serving as integrated electrode to directly catalyze OER, MIL-59(FeNi) grafted on Co NSs (MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs) demonstrates excellent OER performance concerning a low overpotential of 216 mV is required to arrive at the current density of $20\ mA\ cm^{-2}$. Meanwhile, a lower Tafel slope of $38.46\ mV\ dec^{-1}$ and reaction mechanism is also available for MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs on OER process, such superior catalytic activity enables it outperform many previously reported MOFs based OER electrocatalysts.

## 2. Experimental section

### 2.1. Materials Synthesis

#### 2.1.1. Synthesis of the MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs on nickel foam (NF)

The MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs material was prepared on the NF through a facile two-step electrodeposited process.

The Co nanosheets arrays briefly denoted as Co NSs was electrodeposited on the NF as we previously reported [11]. Typically, the commercial NF was firstly washed to remove the residual external organic species and oxides as following: pieces of NF (1 cm × 3 cm size) were stripped from bulk commercial NF and ultrasonicated in 3 M HCl solution for 30 min. Subsequently, the acid-treated NF was rinsed with DI water for at least three times and then sonicated under the mixture of acetone and ethanol with same volume ratio for 30 min. After above two procedures, the cleaned NF was dried at 60 °C. The dried NF with the area of around 2 cm² was then partially soaked into the 15 mL electrodeposited solution dissolved with 10 mM $CH_4N_2S$ and 100 mM $CoCl_2$. The typical three electrodes system was adopted to obtain the Co NSs under the -0.8 V reductive potential (vs. Ag/AgCl) for 60 min, among which the clean NF

served as working electrode, graphite rod as auxiliary electrode and Ag/AgCl (with KCl saturated) as reference electrode, respectively. The obtained Co NSs was dried at 60 °C and put into 20 mL DI water containing 0.2 g PVP. The obtained Co NSs was rinsed for several times and then dried again for the followed electrodeposited process.

Accordingly, the MIL-59(FeNi) was grafted on the Co NSs through the electrochemical deposition process. Briefly, the electrochemical-deposition was performed into the same three electrodes system as mentioned above, except that the NF deposited with Co NSs used as working electrode. The electrolyte was prepared by mixing the solution A and solution B. In a typical procedure, 0.2491 g Isophthalic acid (IPA) was totally dissolved into 5 mL N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) under magnetic stirring for 10 min, denoted as solution A. 0.2424 g $Fe(NO_3)_3\cdot9H_2O$ dissolved into 10 mL DI under magnetic stirring for 10 min to form the solution B. After that, solution B was slowly introduced into solution A under the magnetic stirring. After stirring for another 5 min, 5 mL DMF was then added into above mixture to recover the clear solution again. In the following procedure, 0.1744 g $Ni(NO_3)_2\cdot6H_2O$ was added into above solution. Ultimately, the deposition electrolyte was acquired after the involved addition of 0.12 g $NH_4NO_3$ as supporting electrolyte. The constant potential electrodeposition was operated at -1.5 V at room temperature with the optimized deposition time of 80 min. After the electrodeposition procedure, the NF was carefully withdrawn from the deposition electrolyte, rinsed with DI water and keep drying at 60 °C in an oven. The mass loading of the active materials was

calculated based on the surface catalyst loading, chemical formula and ICP-MS result, the mass loading of those MIL-59 MOFs hybrid was listed in Table S1.

Other MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs samples obtained on different electrodeposition time were fabricated by set the applied electrodeposition time of -1.2 V, -1.4 V, -1.6 V and -1.8 V, respectively.

### 2.1.2. Synthesis of the MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs, MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs on NF

MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs, MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs were synthesized with the same method of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs in the absence of $Ni(NO_3)_2{\cdot}6H_2O$ and $Fe(NO_3)_3{\cdot}9H_2O$, respectively.

### 2.1.3. Synthesis of the MIL-59(FeNi)/NF, MIL-59(Fe)/NF, and MIL-59(Ni)/NF

The MIL-59(FeNi), MIL-59(Fe), and MIL-59(Ni) were synthesized according to the same method of MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs, MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs on NF just without involvement of Co NSs on the NF.

### 2.2. Instruments

The morphology and element constitution of as prepared samples were investigated with Hitachi SU-8010 scanning electronic microscopy and JEM-1200EX transmission electron microscopy instrument. XPS data was obtained from Thermo ESCALAB 250 X-ray photoelectron spectrometer with Al K$\alpha$ source. FT-IR and Raman spectra were detected on Bruker TENSOR-27 and RENISHAW inVia instrument to probe the surface composition of formed composites. XRD patterns were carried out with a Bruker D8 using Cu K$\alpha$ ($\lambda = 1.5418$ Å) as radiation source.

### 2.3. Electrochemical evaluation

All the electrochemical data was collected on a traditional three-electrode cell on a computer connected CHI 600E electrochemical system. If not specifically emphasized, all the electrolytic tests were performed under 1 M KOH with pH value of 14 in this work. The (MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs was employed as the working electrode, Ag/AgCl saturated with KCl solution and a graphite rod were used as reference and auxiliary electrode, respectively. The potentials were converted to reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE) according to $\mathrm{E_{RHE}=E_{Ag/AgCl}+0.197\ V+0.059\ pH}$, and overpotential ($\eta$) towards OER was $\eta=\mathrm{E_{(RHE)}-1.23\ V}$.

The LSV curves were obtained at the scan speed of $2\ \mathrm{mV\ s^{-1}}$ in $\mathrm{O_2}$-saturated 1 M KOH electrolyte with 98 % iR compensated level was set to reduce the resistance value of the tested system. The double-layer capacitance ($\mathrm{C_{dl}}$) value of all prepared samples were calculated from the cyclic voltammetry curves tested under the potential window of 1.05 V- 1.15 V (vs. RHE) at different scan rate ranging from 20 to 120 mV $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

The mass activity (MA) within the catalyst is also crucial parameter to quantitative to evaluate the intrinsic activity of an electrocatalyst. The LSV polarization current normalized with the catalyst loading is referred the mass activity (MA), which is calculated with following equation.

$$\mathrm{MA}=\frac{j}{m}$$

The turnover frequency (TOF) is another key metric to define an efficient electrocatalyst at the applied overpotential. The TOF value of the catalyst generally presents the evolved $\mathrm{O_2}$ or $\mathrm{H_2}$ moles number per unit time towards water oxidation.

The corresponding equation is employed to attain the TOF value towards an electrocatalytic gas evolution reaction:

$$
\mathrm{TOF} = \frac{j \times S}{4 \times F \times n}
$$

Here, the j refers as the current density (mA cm⁻²), S denote the surface area of the electrode (cm²), F is Faraday's constant (96485 C mol⁻¹) and n corresponds the mole number of metal atom in the catalyst.

The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were carried out by applying a 5 mV AC amplitude at100 kHz to 10 mHz frequency range. To have a detailed comparison, the electrochemical measurements of MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs, MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs, MIL-59(FeNi)/NF, MIL-59(Fe)/NF, and MIL-59(Ni)/NF were also probed and compared.

### 2.4. DFT calculation

The catalytic activities of MIL-59(FeNi), MIL-59(Fe) and MIL-59(Ni) were explained based on density functional theory. For the structural information, we have consulted the crystal structure of MIL-59(V) [47]. After removing the water molecules, the tri-nuclear cation structure can be written as $[\mathrm{V}^{III}_{3}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{OOCC}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{COO})_{3}]^{+}$. Considering the $\mu_{3}$-O atom and the space group, the anion was assigned to Cl⁻ that replaces one-third of a water molecule. Thus, this was represented by $\mathrm{Cl}^{-}_{1/3}(\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})_{2/3}$. For MIL-59(Fe), the molecular structure of the tri-nuclear system was constructed according to MIL-59(V). The outer Cl⁻ anion was replaced by an –OH group on one of the Fe atoms to maintain charge neutrality. For MIL-59(FeNi), since the ratio of Fe and Ni is 2:1 as determined in the experiment,

there is no additional anion needed when a $Fe^{3+}$ in MIL-59(Fe) was replaced by a $Ni^{2+}$. For MIL-59(Ni), two $-COO^-$ groups were protonated to construct the tri-nuclear structure. The molecular structures are enough for the calculations, because the electronic structures of the ionic compounds are rather localized.

The structures of above samples were optimized by hybrid density functional B3LYP software [48,49] in the Gaussian03 program [50]. The Lanl2DZ pseudo-potential for the transition metal atoms and 6-31G(d,p) basis set for the other atoms were used. Vibrational analysis was performed to obtain the Gibbs free energy and zero-point energy. No imaginary frequency was found, which confirms the stability of the optimized structures.

There are four separate reaction steps as follows [51] to reveal the OER catalytic activity.

$$\mathrm{H_2O(l) + ^* = ^*OH + H^+ + e^- \tag{1}}$$

$$\mathrm{^*OH= ^*O + H^+ + e^- \tag{2}}$$

$$\mathrm{^*O + H_2O(l) = ^*OOH + H^+ + e^- \tag{3}}$$

$$\mathrm{^*OOH = ^* + O_2(g) + H^+ + e^- \tag{4}}$$

During the above reaction steps, $^*$ refer as the active site on certain catalyst. The $^*$OH, $^*$O or $^*$OOH denotes as reaction intermediate. After obtaining the changes of the Gibbs reaction free energies of the above four reactions, the rate-determining step can be found. The models of the most favorable MIL-59(FeNi) are shown in Figure 7.

The Gibbs free energy of liquid water is difficult to be determined by quantum chemistry calculation because of its complex structure. Instead, the gas phase water

molecule was calculated. The parameters of the liquid one was recorded at the equilibrium of liquid and gas water at the condition of 298.15 K and 0.035 MPa. After calculating the parameters of $\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$, those of $\mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$ were based on the relation of $\mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$ + $2\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})=2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l})+4{\times}1.23$ eV in experiment to minimize system errors. The calculated reaction free energy of $\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-}$was strictly followed the equilibrium $1/2\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})=\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-}$. The shift value of free energy is corrected with $-\mathrm{eU}+\mathrm{k}_{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{Tln}a_{\mathrm{H}^{+}}$.
The electrode potential and pH value affect equally to the above four reactions. Thus, the changes of the Gibbs reaction free energies at the standard condition were used to explain the catalytic activities in the electrocatalytic OER.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Morphology and structure characterization

The MIL-59(FeNi) grafted on the Co nanosheet arrays (MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs) was synthesized through a facile two electrodeposition methods, as illustrated in **Scheme 1**. Initially, amorphous urchin-like Co nanosheet arrays (Co NSs) with open porous structure was formed by strong interactions between $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$ and S ligand (**Figure S1**). After that, heterstructure MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs was obtained by anchoring MIL-59(FeNi) metal organic frameworks (MOFs) on a versatile urchin-like Co NSs under a more reductive voltage (-1.5 V vs. Ag/AgCl) with Fe and Ni as central metals and isophthalic acid (IPA) as organic ligand. The morphology of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs obtained under different electrodeposition voltage (**Figure S2**) was also applied and analyzed, which indicated the aggregated and irregular particle

assembly structure was available in either lower or higher voltage. This suggests a moderate electrodeposition voltage of -1.5 V (vs. Ag/AgCl) is necessary for the control of desirable morphology of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs. The morphology of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs (Figure 1) was probed by SEM and HRTEM technique. As Figure 1a shown, the Co NSs possessed an urchin-like morphology, the open and ordered structure ensures the intimate contact and the timely exportation of evolved gas on the formed MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs, which was speculated to maintain the structural stability of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs electrode under the intense reaction condition. Meanwhile, the nanosheet arrays structures on Co NSs might enable more exposed active sites on the integrated MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs electrode to greatly boost the OER performance.

Figure 1b together with inserted image illustrated the MIL-59(FeNi) was anchored on Co NSs, implying the Co NSs could serve as a versatile substrate to anchor MIL-59(FeNi) on the Co NSs. To further elucidate the positive effect of the Co NSs substrate, SEM images of MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs, MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs, MIL-59(FeNi)/NF, MIL-59(Fe)/NF, and MIL-59(Ni)/NF were also acquired and shown in Figure S3-S7. MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs possessed a similar morphology as that of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs with some nanoparticles anchored on Co NSs, while the MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs indicated some irregular balls on Co NSs. Once Co NSs was withdrew from the synthetic part, the as prepared MIL-59(FeNi)/NF, MIL-59(Fe)/NF, and MIL-59(Ni)/NF exhibited a total different morphology as that of MIL-59(FeNi), MIL-59(Fe), and MIL-59(Ni) anchored on Co NSs, which had the regular sphere,

ellipsoid and irregular nanoparticle structure, respectively. The huge morphology difference of those MIL-59(FeNi), MIL-59(Fe), and MIL-59(Ni) grown on different substrate illustrated the important role of Co NSs in tuning the morphology and structure of as prepared materials.

The MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs composites were further investigated with HRTEM technique to reveal the close connection between the structure and catalytic activity.

The HRTEM image suggested that the MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs possessed cluster-assembled structure and well-defined crystalline character (Figure 1c-d), the HRTEM image suggested that the as prepared MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs also formed a heterostructure, both those property was believed to be beneficial for the enhanced OER performance [28]. Specially, the lattice fringe of 0.0518, 0.0580 and 0.0635 nm was well matched with (002), (102) and (112) crystalline plane of MIL-59 MOFs [52]. SAED pattern combined with the XRD structure information also verified the polycrystalline nature on as prepared MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs sample. Moreover, the element mapping images (Figure 1e) verified that C, Fe, Co and Ni constitutional element distributed on as prepared MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs.

The XRD pattern of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs (Figure 2a) suggested that the prepared MIL-59(FeNi) was isostructural to the MIL-59 MOFs. Notably, MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs had the prominent diffraction peaks at $2\theta$ position of $10.2^\circ$, $9.1^\circ$ and $11.2^\circ$ were assigned to the (102), (002) and (112) crystalline planes of MIL-59 structure, respectively. Three simulated peaks were stacked to the match the main XRD peak in around $10^\circ$, which arisen from the formed small size crystallites of MIL-59 MOFs according to Debye-Scherrer equation [53,54]. The small crystalline species enable the three dominant peaks of standard MIL-59 located at $2\theta$ position of around $10^\circ$ emerged as one main peak, which thus reasonably prove that the synthesized MIL-59(FeNi) is well match with the reported MIL-59 MOF. Furthermore, other MIL-59 based samples including MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs, MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs, MIL-59(FeNi)/NF, MIL-59(Fe)/NF, MIL-59(Ni)/NF samples

were also prepared, which also indicated crystalline property in those samples (Figure S8-S9).

![](./images/812600125833609216_4.jpg)

Figure 2. (a) XRD pattern recorded on MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs and MIL-59 MOF simulation curve, (b) Full XPS survey spectrum of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs, and (c-f) Corresponding constitutional element XPS spectra of C 1s, O 1s, Fe 2p, and Ni 2p in MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs.

XPS technique was further used to probe the surficial composition and valance

states of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs. The full XPS survey spectrum (**Figure 2b**) of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs indicated the main constitutional elements were C, O, N, S, Ni, Fe and Co. Element ratio of Fe and Ni was detected close to 2:1, which is consistent with the ICP-MS result (see **Table S2**). The high-resolution C 1s, O 1s, N 1s, Fe 2p, Ni 2p, S 2p and Co 2p spectrum in MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs were displayed in **Figure 2c-f** and **Figure S10-S11**, respectively.

**Figure 2c** indicated C 1s spectrum was consisted of three surface components including the C=C bond in benzoic rings and O-C=O functional groups of IPA linkers located at 284.5 eV, 288.4 eV, respectively [35]. Another peak centered at 286.0 eV was assigned to the C-O bond. The O 1s XPS spectrum (**Figure 2d**) also indicated three main components with three peaks centered at 531.8, 532 and 533 eV were associated with the existence of metal-O bonds formed with the coordination effect between the metal ions and organic ligand, the hydrophilic O-C=O functional groups and the adsorbed water molecules [35]. N 1s spectrum suggested the formation of chemisorbed nitrogen on MIL-59 MOFs surface (**Figure S10**). For those metal species, the high-resolution XPS spectra of Ni 2p, Fe 2p in MOFs and the Co 2p in Co NSs substrate both have the $2p_{1/2}$ and $2p_{3/2}$ component arisen from the metal spin-orbit splitting effect. To be specific, the Fe 2p spectrum had two peaks positioned at 709.7 eV and 723.7 eV were ascribed to the Fe $2p_{3/2}$ and Fe $2p_{1/2}$ state (**Figure 2e**), which were the typical peaks of the $Fe^{3+}$ oxidation states [28], such high valence was believed to ensure the robust chemical stability in the bimetallic MOFs. For the Ni 2p spectrum (**Figure 2f**), it also proved the $Ni^{2+}$ oxidation state in the

central metals of the MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs. The Ni $2p_{3/2}$ (856.3 eV) and Ni $2p_{1/2}$ (874.0 eV) were associated with Ni-O bond created by the link within Ni atoms and IPA ligand, while other two peaks at around 858.4 eV ($2p_{3/2}$) and 874.0 eV ($2p_{1/2}$) were associated with the Ni-OH species [34]. It also had two satellite peaks at 864.2 eV ($2p_{3/2}$) and 881.1 eV ($2p_{1/2}$). Likewise, Co 2p spectrum also indicated the $Co^{2+}$ oxidation states in MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs (**Figure S11**) [57].

Ni atom is widely accepted as active center for Fe/Ni based catalysts towards oxygen evolution, it has similar radius as that of Fe atom and can easily substitute the central Fe atom, which would cause the $Ni^{3+}/Ni^{2+}$ oxidation peak shifting to a higher potential, thus results in an enhanced OER performance of the Fe/Ni based catalysts [34]. **Figure 1e** indicated a homogeneous distribution of the metal active centers in the MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs, and in order to probe possible metal-metal synergistic effect on the improved catalytic activity, the electronic state of Ni active site was carefully compared. The Ni species are generally regarded as active sites to favorably contribute to the diffusion and proton-coupled electron transfer between the catalyst and the electrolytes, while the Fe species and the participation of Co atom in Co NSs could modify the intrinsic character of the catalyst [28]. On the contrary, the bimetallic MIL-59(FeNi), MIL-59(Fe)/Co NSs, MIL-59(Ni)/Co NSs as well as monometallic MIL-59(Fe) and MIL-59(Ni) without or with weaker metal-metal effect are believed to exhibit much worse OER performance than that of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs. Thus, the MIL-59 (FeNi) MOFs grafted on the Co NSs as an integrated electrode system may be more suitable towards OER catalysis in the designed system. The XPS

spectrum of MIL-59(FeNi)/NF and MIL-59(Ni)/NF were also acquired (Figure S12-S13) and its Ni 2p high resolution XPS spectra were further extracted and compared with that of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs to investigate the mixed metal effect on catalyzing OER (Figure 3).

### 3.3. Reaction mechanism and DFT simulations

Theoretical investigations were performed to explain the catalytic activities of MIL-59(FeNi), MIL-59(Fe) and MIL-59(Ni) based on density functional theory (Figure 7). The Gibbs free energy changes of the four reactions for MIL-59(Fe) are 1.33, 1.34, 0.40 and 1.84 eV, respectively. The fourth step is the rate-determining

step. For MIL-59(Ni), the situation is similar. The Gibbs free energy changes are 1.46,
1.87, -0.30 and 1.89 eV, respectively. For MIL-59(FeNi), there are two cases. When
the catalytic reaction occurs on the Fe atom, the Gibbs free energy changes are 1.07,
1.66, 0.39 and 1.80 eV, respectively. These values are 1.61, 2.01, -0.67 and 1.97 eV
when the catalytic reaction occurs on the Ni atom (Figure 7b). The reaction is more
likely to occur on the Fe atom and only this case will be discussed. All the
rate-determining steps are the fourth step for the three MIL-59s. The Gibbs free
energy changes of the fourth step are 1.80, 1.84 and 1.89 eV for MIL-59(FeNi),
MIL-59(Fe) and MIL-59(Ni). Thus MIL-59(FeNi) is the most favorable catalyst. This
sequence is consistent with the catalytic activity in the experiment.

For an ideal catalyst, the Gibbs free energy changes of the four steps should be
balanced. For MIL-59(Fe), the Gibbs free energy changes of step (1) and (2) are
rather good and close to 1.23 eV. However, the value of step (3) is quite small,
possibly because the high oxidation state of *O is not so stable. Therefore, the value
of step (4) is much larger than 1.23 eV.

For MIL-59(Ni), the Gibbs free energy change of step (3) is negative. This implies
that the *O is quite unstable and the catalytic activity of MIL-59(Ni) is worse than
that of MIL-59(Fe). This was also confirmed by the fact that the active site is on the
Fe atom of MIL-59(FeNi), instead of the Ni atom. In step (4), the oxidation state of
the Fe atom in the *OOH is +4 while it is common +3 in the *. The rate-determining
step (4) is more likely to occur when the *OOH is less stable. When a $Fe^{3+}$ in
MIL-59(Fe) is replaced by a $Ni^{2+}$ as in MIL-59(FeNi), the charge on the active Fe

atom increases. The reason should be that the $Ni^{2+}$ can load smaller amount of charge than the $Fe^{3+}$. The calculated Mulliken charge on the active Fe atom in the *OOH increases by 0.01 |e| when a $Fe^{3+}$ in MIL-59(Fe) is replaced by a $Ni^{2+}$. Higher uncommon oxidation state is not stable and this is a possible reason why the Gibbs free energy change of step (4) of MIL-59(FeNi) is smaller than that of MIL-59(Fe). Another reason may come from the stability of MIL-59(FeNi) in the experiment. In the crystal structure of MIL-59(Fe), partial occupancy exists as $Cl^{-}_{1/3}(H_{2}O)_{2/3}$. To summarize, the physical characterization combined with the electrochemical evaluation suggested successful synthesis and direct application of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs toward water oxidation. The well-pronounced catalytic performance of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs can ascribe to the following factors within the catalysts: 1) the coordination environment modification strategy is introduced to greatly activate the meter centers of the as as-prepared MOFs system; 2) abundant hydrophilic carboxyl and hydroxyl functional groups in MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs facilitate the availability of OH⁻; 3) urchin-like Co NSs can serve as an ideal platform to anchor the enormous

MOFs and result in fast exportation of evolved gas, enhanced electron transport ability as well as the exposure of rich active site; 4) DFT calculations reveal that the as formed MIL-59(FeNi) has a more satisfying reaction process, thus enable the MOFs hybrid attains desired reaction rate. Those factors may endow the formed MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs with superior catalytic activity.

### 4. Conclusion
The coordination environment modification strategy played an essential role to activate the meter centers and determine efficient catalytic activity within MIL-59(FeNi) based hybrids. The introduction of another metal would contribute to the positive binding energy shift on the active metal centers, thus receiving unprecedented property compared with less metal occupied system. Such effect is verified and proved based on the experimental result and DFT calculations. Specially, the MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs only requires a low overpotential of 216 mV to reach the current density of $20\ \text{mA·cm}^{-2}$. Also, small Tafel slope value of $38.46\ \text{mV·dec}^{-1}$ as well as pronounced stability of MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs is achieved in alkaline electrolyte.

Despite MOFs showing distinguished advantages including highly porous structures and controllable chemical compositions, it should be noted that the direct use of MOFs as electrode materials is still in its infancy stage and faces many challenges, especially the limited conductivity and stability of MOFs. In some cases, the phase conversion in basic/acidic aqueous conditions could exert synergistic

positive effect on electrocatalysis due to formation of the new active species. The dedicate morphology control mircro/nanoMOFs as desirable 1D, 2D and 3D structures, with conductive matrixes of carbon materials, metal support and other functional substrate can be adopted as feasible approach to not only expedite the electrons transport and electrolyte diffusion, but also substantially enhance the conductivity and stability of MOFs based system. Other strategies are also worth considering, including attaching desired atoms/functional groups onto either metal ions/clusters or organic linkers might be also helpful. The pre-treatment at relatively low temperature on obtained MOFs may be viable to stabilize the chemical/physical properties of MOFs materials before electrochemical reaction. Notably, the combination of in-situ or post-reaction characterization technique of XRD, XPS, FT-IR, Raman, HRTEM and XAS should be employed to probe the stability enhancing mechanism.

### Highlights

- The coordination environment modification strategy is introduced to activate the metal centers on MIL-59(FeNi) MOFs based hybrids.

- Heterostructure can be formed within MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs system by anchoring MIL-59(FeNi) MOFs on amorphous Co nanosheets platform for enhanced OER performance.

- MIL-59(FeNi)/Co NSs can be acted as robust and efficient electrode to directly catalyze oxygen evolution reaction.

- DFT calculations reveal that the charge transfers from the Co substance to the MIL-59 forming a possible -COOCo geometry paves a favorable reaction mechanism.
