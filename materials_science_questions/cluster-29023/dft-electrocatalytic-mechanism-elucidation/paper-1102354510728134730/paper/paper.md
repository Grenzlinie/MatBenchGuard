# Terpyridine- and Quarterpyridine-Based Cationic Covalent Organic Frameworks for Visible-Light-Catalytic $\boldsymbol{\ce{H_2O_2}}$ Synthesis

Jun Zhang, Fei Xue, and Zhonggang Wang*

## Abstract:
This paper presents multipyridine-containing covalent organic frameworks (COFs) with precisely defined position and number of pyridinium cationic groups. Specifically, three terpyridine- and quarterpyridine-based trialdehydes were synthesized, and utilized as the starting monomers to polymerize with trimethylpyridinium bromide to yield vinylene-linked iTPy-COF, iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF, respectively. Thus constructed donor-acceptor cationic COFs exhibit considerably high visible-light catalytic efficiency for hydrogen peroxide ($\ce{H_2O_2}$) synthesis by the dual-channel mechanisms of oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and water oxidation reaction (WOR). In pure water and $\ce{O_2}$ atmosphere, the $\ce{H_2O_2}$ production rate (HPR) of iTPPy-COF after 1 h reaction is as high as $7955\ \mathrm{\mu mol\ g^{-1}\ h^{-1}}$. Even though in air, its HPR value still reaches $6249\ \mathrm{\mu mol\ g^{-1}\ h^{-1}}$. Moreover, it is found that changing the arm lengths and ratios of pyridine to benzene ring in the frameworks significantly affects the photocatalytic capability. The structure-property relationship is investigated in terms of the variations of electronic structures through the theoretical simulations and measurements of photophysical parameters such as fluorescence lifetimes, photocurrent intensities, and impedances of charge transfer, which offers new insights into the engineering of multipyridine-based cationic COFs for highly efficient $\ce{H_2O_2}$ photosynthesis.

## Introduction
Hydrogen peroxide ($\ce{H_2O_2}$) is an oxidant widely applied in chemical synthesis, textile manufacture and medical disinfection,$^{[1-3]}$ and is also regarded as a promising alternative to hydrazine fuel that is vitally important in the aerospace and military fields.$^{[4]}$ Currently, $\ce{H_2O_2}$ is industrially produced by the oxidation of anthraquinone (AQ) method. However, the AQ oxidation process is technically complex and hazardous, and the harmful by-products pose a great risk to human health.$^{[5,6]}$ The development of an environment-friendly, safe, operation-simple and energy-consuming technology for $\ce{H_2O_2}$ synthesis is becoming a hot subject.$^{[7-13]}$ In this aspect, the photo-driven synthesis of $\ce{H_2O_2}$ conducted in water medium utilizing solar energy receives considerable attention, and numerous photosynthetic catalysts for $\ce{H_2O_2}$ have been reported, including metal oxides,$^{[14,15]}$ metal-organic frameworks,$^{[16-18]}$ and metal-free catalysts like graphitic carbon nitride,$^{[19]}$ graphene oxide,$^{[20]}$ oxidized carbon nanotubes,$^{[21]}$ and covalent organic frameworks (COFs).$^{[8,22-28]}$ Among them, the conjugative COFs exhibit outstanding advantages since the porous structure and large specific surface area provide abundant accessible catalytic sites for the redox reaction of oxygen and water molecules, whereas the electronic structure can be readily tuned through the flexible organic and polymeric synthesis strategies.$^{[29-31]}$ Moreover, COFs are essentially free of metal, so the possible decomposition of the photo-produced $\ce{H_2O_2}$ caused by the metal ions in the reaction system can be avoided.

Despite the significant progress having achieved in the recent years, from the viewpoint of practical application, the photocatalytic synthesis rate for $\ce{H_2O_2}$ still needs to be greatly improved. The recent studies reveal that the incorporation of nitrogen atoms in COF frameworks effectively extend the visible-light absorption range, narrow the band gap and improve carrier transport and separation, and thereby promotes the production rate of $\ce{H_2O_2}$.$^{[8,32]}$ Hu and co-workers reported that, after replacing benzene with pyridine, the $\ce{H_2O_2}$ yield within 60 min considerably increased from $372.7\ \mathrm{\mu mol\ g^{-1}}$ to $706.2\ \mathrm{\mu mol\ g^{-1}}$.$^{[33]}$ Xi, et al. found that the change of the nitrogen positions even affects the catalytic mechanism of the COFs for $\ce{H_2O_2}$ synthesis. For example, the two adjacent nitrogen atoms in the six-membered heteroaromatic ring made the catalysis reaction majorly follow the direct 2e oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) ($\mathrm{O_2+2e^-+2H^+\to H_2O_2}$), whereas the non-adjacent two nitrogen atoms led to the two-step ORR pathway ($\mathrm{O_2+e^-\to\bullet O_2^-+e^-+2H^+\to H_2O_2}$).$^{[34]}$

On the other hand, it is noted that the ionic COFs are more conducive to photocatalytic reactions in the aqueous phase compared to neutral COFs.$^{[35,36]}$ In addition to the intrinsic hydrophilicity and the enhanced aqueous-phase mass transfer capability of the ionic COFs, the electron-withdrawing effect of the cationic groups facilitates the electron-hole separation and charge transfer. Han and co-workers developed ionic iminium-linked triazine-core COFs ($\mathrm{PD^{2+}}$-COFs) and found that the abundant cationic sites promoted oxygen adsorption and efficient conversion to hydrogen peroxide.$^{[37]}$ Xu's group obtained HCOFs with

## Results and Discussion
### Synthesis of Terpyridine- and Quarterpyridine-Based Monomers and Cationic COFs.

Three new terpyridine- and quarterpyridine-based trialde- hydes were synthesized according to the routes illustrated in Scheme S1 (Supporting Information). The starting com- pound 2-acetyl-5-bromopyridine was reacted, respectively, with 4-bromobenzaldehyde and 5-bromo-2-formylpyridine to give 1,5-bis(4-bromopyridine)-3-(4-bromophenyl)pyridine(TPyTBr) and 1,3,5-tris(4-bromopyridine)pyridine(QPyTBr). Then, TPyTBr was converted to 1,5-bis(4- aldehydepyridine)-3-(4-aldehydepheny))pyridine (TPyTA) through the reaction with n-butyllithium and N,N-dimeth- ylformamide. Then the Suzuki coupling of 4-formylphenyl- boronic acid with TPyTBr and QPyTBr led to phenyl- extended terpyridine- and quarterpyridine-based trialde- hydes (TPPyTA and QPPyTA), respectively. In the Fourier transform infrared spectra (FTIR) of the three monomers, the band of CHO and characteristic stretching vibration of - $N=C-$ in pyridine ring appear at $1689 cm^{-1}$ and $1534 cm^{-1}$ , respectively (Figure S1). All the resonance peaks in the $^{1}H$ NMR spectra are assigned to the corresponding protons(Figure S2-S4). For each monomer, the measured m/z value of $[M+H]^{+}$ by mass spectroscopy is consistent with the theoretical molecular weight.

As shown in Scheme 1, three terpyridine- and quarter- pyridine-based vinylene-linked cationic COFs (iTPy-COF, iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF) were prepared by the Knoe- venagel condensations of TPyTA, TPPyTA and QPPyTA with N-ethyl-2,4,6-trimethylpyridinium bromide, respec-tively. The comparison of the FTIR spectra (Figure S5) between monomers and COFs shows that, after polymer- izations, the intensity of the aldehyde at $1689 cm^{-1}$ greatly reduces. The small amounts of residual aldehyde groups might be due to those at the periphery of COF structures. $^{[48-51]}$ The new absorptions at $966 cm^{-1}$ and $1628 cm^{-1}$ are assigned to the stretching vibration of $-C \equiv C$ bond. $^{[52]}$ The formation of $-C \equiv C$ - linkage is further demonstrated by the peak at 110 ppm in the solid-state $^{13}C CP /$  MAS NMR spectra (Figure S6). Meanwhile, the signals of ethyl carbons at around 48 ppm and 27 ppm agree with those of the monomer N-ethyl-2,4,6-trimethylpyridinium bromide (Figure S7). Compared to iTPPy-COF and iQPPy- COF, the carbon resonances of pyridine rings in iTPy-COF apparently shift from 157 ppm to 171 ppm as they are closer to the electron-withdrawing $N^{+}$ -cation groups than those in the other two counterparts. In order to confirm the existence of $N^{+}$ -cationic groups in COFs, the high-resolution $N_{1 s}$ XPS spectra were recorded and dissected by means of the spectral deconvolution software. As shown in Figure S8, the nitrogen signals in pyridine and cationic pyridinium are found at 399.5 eV and 402.2 eV, respectively.

The long-range ordered structures of the resultant COFs are demonstrated by the powder X-ray diffractions(PXRD), which exhibit the sharp peaks corresponding to the 100 facets at $5.76^{\circ}, 4.06^{\circ}$ and $4.17^{\circ}$ (Figure 1a-1c). The computer simulations demonstrate that the experimental PXRD patterns are more consistent with those of the eclipsed AA rather than staggered AB stacking mode. The proposed unit cells by the AA stacking mode are illustrated in Figure 1d-1f. The lattice parameters and fractional atomic coordinates by the Pawley refinements are presented in Table S1-S3. The images of the high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) show the ordered lattice stripes (Figure 1g-1i). The longer arms of iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF result in the narrower stripe spacing (0.37 nm and 0.36 nm) than iTPy-COF (0.43 nm), agreeing with the ranking sequence of the Pawley's refined results.

The porosities of the three COFs were analyzed by physical sorption of nitrogen at 77 K. Their adsorption- desorption isotherms are presented in Figure S9, from which the calculated BET surface areas of iTPy-COF, iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF are $251 m^{2}g^{-1}, 1149 m^{2}g^{-1}$ and $849 m^{2}g^{-1}$ , whereas the pore sizes are 1.38 nm, 1.91 nm and 1.90 nm,respectively. ### Photocatalytic Performance for $H_2O_2$ Synthesis by iTPy-COF, iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF

Under the irradiation of visible light, the catalytic experiments for the $H_2O_2$ synthesis were conducted in water and different atmospheres including oxygen gas (O₂), air and argon gas (Ar) under a 350 W Xe lamp at 400-800 nm with light intensity of 100 mW cm⁻². The $H_2O_2$ concentrations can be read from the standard curves (Figure S14), which were obtained by the UV/Vis spectra using cerium (IV) sulfate [Ce(SO₄)₂] as a standard (Figure S15). The kinetic curves in Figure 2a show that the HPRs of iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF rapidly rise with the irradiation time, exhibiting satisfactory photocatalytic efficiencies. At the reaction time of 1 h, the HPR of iTPPy-COF is as high as 7955 μmol g⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the absence of any sacrificial agent. After replacing oxygen with air, its HPR value is still up to 6249 μmol g⁻¹ h⁻¹ (Figure 2b). Even in oxygen-free atmosphere (argon), COFs also can photocatalyze the synthesis of $H_2O_2$ with the HPRs of 1029-1304 μmol g⁻¹ h⁻¹ (Figure 2c), suggesting the overall $H_2O_2$ production comes from the common contributions of both oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and water oxidation reaction (WOR) pathways. In addition, the $H_2O_2$ decomposition in Ar is examined with the 1 mM initial $H_2O_2$ concentration in pure water. It is seen that the $H_2O_2$ decomposition by iTPPy-COF (%) is less than 8.1 % after a long irradiation time up to 4 h (Figure S16).

In order to explain the reason behind the experimental results, the electronic structures of the three COFs were analyzed by the calculations through the density functional theory (DFT). As shown in Figures 3a, the nitrogen-rich terpyridine or quarterpyridine moiety displays the lower electrostatic potential due to the presence of lone-pair electrons in each nitrogen atom, which act as the electron-donors (D). On the contrary, the $N^{+}$-containing pyridinium is electron-deficient, and serves as electron-acceptor (A). As a result, under the irradiation of visible light, the excited electrons shift from the electron-rich pyridine to the pyridinium moiety because of the strong electron-withdrawing effect of positive $N^{+}$-cation. To verify the above analysis, the charge distributions of photo-excited electrons and holes for the three photo-excited COFs were calculated by Gauss and Multiwfn method. $^{[67,68]}$ In the excited state, the electrons mainly locate in the pyridinium moiety (Figure 3b), and are responsible for the $H_2O_2$ production by the ORR pathway, whereas the holes are aggregated in terpyridine and quarterpyridine moieties, which act as the catalytic sites for $H_2O_2$ production through the WOR channel. Figure 3b shows that the distance between the centroids of charges (D) for iTPy-COF (9.9 Å) is the shortest among the three samples, suggesting that the photoexcited holes and electrons in iTPy-COF are easier to recombine. Meanwhile, as displayed in Figure 1a-1c, iTPy-COF has the lower crystallinity than the other two counterparts. The decreased order of AA stacking is disadvantageous for the intra- and inter-layer charges transport. $^{[24,28,31,48,54]}$ Both the two negative factors of crystallinity and the separation capability of photoexcited charges lead to iTPy-COF the apparently decreased photocatalytic efficiency in comparison with iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF.

The difference of photocatalytic efficiencies for the three COFs is tightly related to the effective numbers of photoexcited electrons and holes in COFs. As shown in the fluorescence spectra (Figure S22), the fluorescence intensity of iTPPy-COF is the weakest among the three COFs, meaning that there are more excited electrons in iTPPy-COF actually participating in the $H_2O_2$ synthesis rather than returning to the ground state. $^{[34,37]}$ Besides, it is noted that iQPPy-COF exhibits the lower HPR than iTPPy-COF, suggesting that the photocatalytic performance cannot be monotonically improved by simply increasing the number of pyridine rings in the COF framework. In fact, the photocatalytic capability of COFs is affected by many factors. As a measure of the separation ability of photo-excited holes and electrons, the D value (15.1 Å) of iQPPy-COF is slightly shorter than iTPPy-COF (15.3 Å) (Figure 3b) although iQPPy-COF possesses more electron-rich pyridine rings. Additionally, compared to iTPPy-COF, the enlarged peak width at half height in the PXRD pattern of iQPPy-COF indicates the lower crystallinity (Figure 1b and 1c), which

### Photocatalytic Mechanism

The UV/Vis spectra of the three COFs are presented in Figure S23. the optical band gaps of 2.50 eV (iTPy-COF), 2.48 eV (iTPPy-COF) and 2.37 eV (iQPPy-COF) are obtained by the Kubelka-Munk method, $^{[9,22,63]}$ (Figure S24). The conduction bands (CBs) by the flat band potentials from the Mott-Schottky curves are $-0.28$, $-0.07$ and $-0.10\ \text{V}$ with reference of normal hydrogen electrode (NHE) (Figure S25-S27), whereas the valence band (VB) potentials calculated from the equation $E_{\text{CB}}=E_{\text{VB}}-E\text{g}$ are $2.22$, $2.41$ and $2.27\ \text{V}$ for iTPy-COF, iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF, respectively. The energy band structures in Figure 4a show that the CB potentials are less negative than that of the sequential two-step $2\text{e}^-$-ORR $(\text{O}_2+\text{e}^-\rightarrow\bullet\text{O}_2^- \ +\text{e}^-+2\text{H}^+\rightarrow\text{H}_2\text{O}_2, \ -0.33\ \text{V})$, while the VB potentials are lower than that of the sequential two-step $2\text{e}^-$-WOR reaction $(\text{H}_2\text{O}+\text{H}^+\rightarrow\bullet\text{OH}+\text{H}^+, \ 2\bullet\text{OH}\rightarrow\text{H}_2\text{O}_2, \ +2.73\ \text{V vs NHE})$.

In order to study the ORR and WOR mechanisms, a series of photocatalytic experiments of iTPPy-COF were carried out with various scavengers in the reaction system, including nitrotetrazolium blue chloride (NBT) for superoxide anion radical $(\bullet\text{O}_2^-)$, $\text{NaIO}_3$ for electron $(\text{e}^-)$, and methanol for hole $(\text{h}^+)$. On the contrary, the addition of $\text{h}^+$-trapping agent (methanol) results in the increased HPR since the recombination of photoexcited electrons with holes is inhibited. The in situ diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform spectroscopy (DRIFTS) was employed to monitor the photocatalytic reaction process of iTPPy-COF. As illustrated in Figure 4d, under the visible-light irradiation, the absorptions at $894\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $1272\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ emerge and incrementally enhance with the irradiation time, which are attributed to the generated $H_{2}O_{2}$ and endoperoxide *O–O*, respectively, $^{[38,59,70]}$ and indicate the occurrence of reduction reaction of $O_{2}$ molecules by the photogenerated electrons. Moreover, the characteristic bending vibration of -O-O-corresponding to the $\bullet O_{2}^{-}$ signal at ca. $960\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ is absent, further supporting the one-step $2e^{-}$-ORR mechanism for the $H_{2}O_{2}$ synthesis. $^{[56,71]}$ The existence of adsorption of *OH species on iTPPy-COF is confirmed by the stretching vibrations of CO and COH intermediates at $1402\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $1052\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, respectively. $^{[56,62,65,71]}$ Notably, it is observed that the intensity of the band at $1637\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for $-C=C-$ bond enhances with the reaction proceeding, probably caused by the protonation effect since it locates at the aggregation region of the photoexcited electrons on iTPPy-COF.

The changes of Gibbs free energies ($\Delta G$) for the adsorptions of intermediates of *O–O*, *H and *OH on different sites of the three COFs were calculated by the density functional theory (DFT). $^{[72,73]}$ Taking iTPPy-COF as an example, the *O–O* species is preferentially adsorbed on the $N^{+}$-cation pyridinium ring at the Yeager-type model $^{[70]}$ as its free energy $(-0.906\ \mathrm{eV})$ is the lowest compared to those of the other sites and configurations (Figure S36). Besides, the $\Delta G$s for the intermediate of *H on different sites were calculated (Figure 4f). Figure S37 shows that the $\Delta G$ value of *H on bromine anion is much lower than those on the carbons of pyridinium ring. $^{[74]}$ For the WOR pathway, the intermediate *OH species is adsorbed on the $C=N$ bond of pyridine adjacent to the benzene ring (Figure S38). Based on the theoretical calculations and experimental results, the mechanism of photocatalytic $H_{2}O_{2}$ synthesis is proposed in Figure 4e. Under the visible light, the strong electron-withdrawing $N^{+}$-cation results in the shift of the excited electrons toward the pyridinium moiety, on which the $O_{2}$ molecule is reduced by the photo-generated electrons in water, and the formed *O–O* is adsorbed on pyridinium to produce $H_{2}O_{2}$. Meanwhile, in the photo-excited state, the $H_{2}O$ molecule is adsorbed on the $h^{+}$-enriched terpyridine or quarterpyridine moiety, and is oxidized to $H_{2}O_{2}$ via the intermediate *OH species, being in line with the $2e^{-}$-WOR mechanism.

For the three COFs, Figure 4f reveals that the ORR reactions can proceed spontaneously as the free energies in the steps of $*O_{2}\to *OO*\to H_{2}O_{2}$ sequentially decrease. However, each step for $H_{2}O\to *OH\to H_{2}O_{2}$ has to overcome an energy barrier (Figure 4g), which is thermodynamically unfavorable for the $H_{2}O_{2}$ production by the WOR pathway. The values of free energies of intermediate species of *OO* and *OH for iTPPy-COF are lower than those of iTPy-COF and iQPPy-COF. The theoretical calculations support the experimental fact that the photocatalytic efficiency of iTPPy-COF is the highest among the three COFs, and the ORR mechanism plays a dominant role in the overall $H_{2}O_{2}$ production rate during the $H_{2}O_{2}$ synthesis.

## Conclusion

In summary, electron-rich terpyridine/quarterpyridine and electron-deficient cationic pyridinium ring were employed as building blocks to successfully construct three vinylene-linked donor-acceptor cationic iTPy-COF, iTPPy-COF and iQPPy-COF through the Knoevenagel condensation. The crystalline and chemical structures are confirmed by means of PXRD and HR-TEM as well as FTIR, solid-state $^{13}\text{C}$ MAS NMR and XPS spectroscopy. The resultant COFs possess the BET surface areas of $251$-$1149\ \mathrm{m^{2}g^{-1}}$ and average pore sizes of 1.38–1.91 nm. Under the irradiation of visible light, their $H_{2}O_{2}$ production rates in water are up to $7955\ \mathrm{\mu mol\ g^{-1}\ h^{-1}}$ and $6249\ \mathrm{\mu mol\ g^{-1}\ h^{-1}}$ in $O_{2}$ and air atmospheres, respectively, which are among the highest values already reported in the literature. Of interest is the observation that, despite the same $N^{+}$-cations and the similar terpyridine or quarterpyridine units in the frameworks, the three ionic COFs exhibit the dramatically different photocatalytic efficiencies. In addition to the crystallinity and porosity parameters, the major reason is attributed to the differences in electronic structures caused by the systematically varied conjugated lengths and the ratio of benzene to pyridine in the arms of COF frameworks, as reflected by their different separation ability of photoexcited electrons and holes, fluorescence lifetimes, photocurrent intensities, and impedances of charge transfer. Additionally, the energy-band structures and photocatalytic results support the $2e^{-}$-ORR and $2e^{-}$-WOR dual-channels mechanism through the controlled experiments conducted in $O_{2}$ and $O_{2}$-free atmosphere with and without adding scavengers for electrons, holes, superoxide radicals and hydroxyl radicals in the reaction system.
