# Configuration interaction study of the ground and excited states of TiO₂ ring structures

Takashi Tsuchiya${}^{\text{a)}}$ and Jerry L. Whitten

Department of Chemistry, North Carolina State University, Raleigh, North Carolina 27695, USA

(Received 22 December 2010; accepted 21 February 2011; published online 15 March 2011)

Theoretical studies of the ground and lowest excited singlet and triplet states of a series of titanium dioxide ring structures, $({\mathrm{TiO}}_{2}{)}_{2n}$, $n=3\text{--}9$, are reported. Calculations are based on many-electron configuration theory, where energies of states and geometrical structures are determined by variational energy minimization. The lowest energy excited states correspond to excitations from oxygen 2p levels to unoccupied 3d orbitals on titanium. For each ring system, two types of excited state solutions are investigated: those that maintain periodic symmetry for individual orbitals and solutions that allow the symmetry to be broken. The latter solutions which correspond to localized states or excitons are found to be significantly lower in energy than the symmetric solutions. We compare the vertical excitation energy of these well-defined geometrical structures with size effects reported in experimental studies. © 2011 *American Institute of Physics*. [doi:10.1063/1.3564880]

## I. INTRODUCTION

Titanium dioxide materials are widely used in heterogeneous catalysis and photochemistry.${}^{1,2}$ Many of the applications can be traced to the relatively low electronic bandgap in TiO₂ of $\sim$3.2 eV and the role vacancies play in electron transport and surface chemistry.${}^{3}$ Titanium dioxide nanoparticles are of increasing interest.${}^{4,5}$ Because of their large surface to volume ratio, nanoparticles can enhance catalytic activity, and in photocatalytic processes, longer excited state lifetimes may allow energy to be transferred to surfaces where nonthermal reactions with adsorbed species can occur. When the particle size is small, the states begin to resemble molecular electronic states and the quantum size effect alters the bandgap of the material compared to a bulk phase. In solar applications, altering the bandgap and prolonging excited state lifetimes through size effects, dopants and surface terminations are topics of great importance.${}^{6\text{--}15}$ In the last two decades, there have been many reports of quantum size effects in the titanium dioxide nanoparticles.${}^{16\text{--}21}$

Anpo *et al.* observed an $\sim$0.1 eV blueshift of the UV absorption spectra in nanoparticles of rutile and anatase titanium dioxide especially when the particle size was below 100 Å.${}^{16}$ Kavan *et al.* also observed a blueshift in extremely small crystalline particles of size 10–20 Å.${}^{17}$ Joselevich and Willner reported a bandgap increase of 0.4 and 0.6 eV relative to the bulk bandgap of anatase or rutile, respectively, in ultrasmall titanium dioxide particles of diameter $\sim$9 Å.${}^{18}$

In contrast, Zhai and Wang recently performed systematic measurements of bandgaps for the titanium dioxide anion clusters, $({\mathrm{TiO}}_{2}{)}_{n}^{-}$, with $n$ from 1 to 10 and detected no size effects.${}^{19}$ Their result is consistent with the study by Serpone *et al.* that showed no bandgap increase in the titanium dioxide nanoparticles for three reported average diameters of 21, 133, and 267 Å.${}^{20}$ In their $({\mathrm{TiO}}_{2}{)}_{n}^{-}$ study,${}^{19}$ Zhai and Wang also report a rapid convergence of the energy gap with size for small titanium dioxide clusters. The energy gap increased almost monotonically with cluster size for $n=1\text{--}7$ to the bulk limit (3.0–3.2 eV), then became invariant with increasing size. The energy gap of small titanium dioxide clusters was investigated using quantum theoretical calculations by Qu and Kroes for $({\mathrm{TiO}}_{2}{)}_{n}^{-}$, $n=1\text{--}9$.${}^{21}$ However, the agreement between the experimental and theoretical studies was found to be poor with rather random variation in the energy gap found in the calculations with respect to cluster size.

In the present study, we investigate a series of titanium dioxide ring structures, $({\mathrm{TiO}}_{2}{)}_{2n}$, $n=3\text{--}9$, by *ab initio* quantum theoretical methods. Calculations are based on many-electron configuration theory where the energies of states and geometrical structures are determined by variational energy minimization. The study focuses on transitions from the ground state to the lowest triplet and singlet excited states and the extent of electron localization in the excited state, i.e., whether the excited state is most properly described as delocalized or as a localized electron–hole pair excitation. We report trends in the excitation energy for these well-defined geometrical structures $({\mathrm{TiO}}_{2}{)}_{2n}$ and compare with experiments on small TiO₂ clusters. The excited states investigated correspond to an excitation from oxygen 2p levels to unoccupied titanium 3d orbitals. Configuration interaction (CI) accounts for critical changes in electron correlation that occur in the ground and excited states, particularly, when the excited state becomes localized. Although the total wavefunction must correspond to the symmetry of the Hamiltonian or nuclear framework, there is no fundamental symmetry requirement on individual molecular orbitals in a many-electron system. In small molecules, however, imposing symmetry on individual orbitals offers considerable conceptual and computational advantages, and in the limit of infinite configuration interaction, will converge in principle to the exact description of the electronic state. ## II. RING STRUCTURES

A series of titanium dioxide ring structures, (TiO₂)₂ₙ, n = 3–9 are studied. Each ring has the same fundamental structure to make it possible to focus on size effects. The coordination numbers of titanium and oxygen are fixed to be four and two, respectively. The geometrical structure of each ring is optimized by variational energy minimization, at the restricted Hartree-Fock (RHF) level, of the closed-shell singlet ground state. The minimum energy structure of (TiO₂)₂ₙ belongs to a $D_{nh}$ symmetry group. Figure 1 shows the optimized structure of the rings. The $2n$ titanium nuclei are equally spaced and lie in the same plane; their location is defined by a radius, which is optimized for each ring. There are two sets of oxygen locations: $2n$ oxygens are in the plane and the other $2n$ oxygens are out of the plane. The oxygens are approximately tetrahedral with respect to the titanium nuclei but their actual coordinates are optimized by energy minimization.

Figure 2 shows the optimized value of ring radius for each structure and Fig. 3 shows the variation in binding energy at the SCF level and the CI energy per unit TiO₂ for the respective rings. Figures include values for (TiO₂)₄ (n = 2) for comparison. Except for (TiO₂)₄, the optimized value for the ring radius, plotted in Fig. 2, is found to increase linearly with n. RHF binding energies as well as CI energies per unit (see Fig. 3) are found to be nearly constant for n = 4–9. The binding energy is approximately 1 eV smaller for n = 3 and the CI energy is ~0.5 eV larger. For (TiO₂)₄, where n = 2, the small binding energy and the large CI energy indicate that (TiO₂)₄ is too small to construct a ring structure with properties consistent with those of the larger rings. In fact, the ground state of (TiO₂)₄ was calculated to be a high spin, triplet state in contrast to all other rings, for which the ground state was found to be a closed-shell singlet state.

Valence orbitals are primarily the 3d orbitals of titanium and 2p orbitals of oxygen. For these ring systems, the charge distribution in the ground electronic state corresponds to a net transfer of one electron to each oxygen from the neighboring titanium atoms to give effective charges of $O^{-1}$ and $Ti^{+2}$. Thus, the electron distribution does not reach the limiting oxidation state charges of $-2$ and $+4$ for O and Ti, respectively. The periodicity of the electronic structure of the singlet ground state is clearly evident in Figure 4(a). For the triplet excited electronic state, two different solutions are investigated. The first is the canonical delocalized orbital description in which each singly and doubly occupied orbital is constrained to belong to a specific irreducible representation of the point group of the system. The second solution allows symmetry breaking in the singly and doubly occupied orbitals of the restricted Hartree-Fock solution. We refer to the latter solution as the localized electron-hole solution or excitonic state. Figures 4(b) and 4(c) depict the electron density shift relative to the ground state based on the population analysis for the delocalized orbital triplet state (b) and the electron-hole localized triplet state (c). While the density change is uniform over the entire ring in the delocalized case, in the electron-hole localized state, the density change occurs almost entirely on a single titanium atom and a neighboring oxygen, as shown in Fig. 4(c) where a localized electron-hole pair involving an extra 3d electron on titanium and a hole in the oxygen 2p shell is clearly evident. The specific site of the electron-hole pair is arbitrary since all Ti atoms are initially equivalent. The formation of the localized electron-hole pair leads to an energy lowering of the triplet state relative to the delocalized electronic structure. Although the figure depicts results only for (TiO₂)₁₆, the same features are found for each ring system. For (TiO₂)₈, the excitation energy based on the delocalized triplet RHF calculation is found to be higher than that for the localized triplet calculation by 5.7 eV; at the CI level the value is 2.2 eV. Similarly high energies of the delocalized triplet are found for (TiO₂)₁₆ and other rings, and this means that it would take extremely large CI expansions to bring states down to the same energy achieved by small CI calculations based on a symmetry broken localized electron-hole state. A large energy lowering due to electron-hole pair localization has been reported previously for other systems using the unrestricted Hartree-Fock (UHF) method,²² and the present result shows that it is also the case for a RHF calculation. Results from CI calculations using the localized electron-hole description as a basis are discussed in a later section.

The system has three types of oxygens that differ depending on their location in the ring. One set is in the out-of-plane position relative to the plane of titanium nuclei. Other oxygens are in-plane but can be either inside or outside the titanium rings. To test the validity of the HF optimized ring structures, a geometry determination by CI energy minimization was performed for (TiO₂)₆. ### III. COMPUTATIONAL METHOD

Calculations are carried out for the following full electrostatic Hamiltonian of the system,
$$
\mathrm{H}=\sum_{i}^{N_{v}}\left[-\frac{1}{2} \nabla_{i}^{2}+\sum_{A}^{M}\left(-\frac{Z_{A}}{r_{i A}}+V_{A}^{\text {core }}\right)\right]+\sum_{i<j}^{N_{v}} \frac{1}{r_{i j}},
$$
where $V_{A}^{\text {core }}$ is a core potential that describes the $1s^{2}-2p^{6}$ core of titanium, $N_{v}$ is the number of valence electrons and $M$ is the number of atoms;
$$
V_{A}^{\text {core }}=\sum_{c}\left(2 J_{c}+K_{c}\right)+\sum_{c}\left(-2 \varepsilon_{c}\right)\left|\varphi_{c}\right\rangle\left\langle\varphi_{c}\right|,
$$
where $J_{c}$ and $K_{c}$ are Coulomb and exchange potentials for core electrons on nucleus A; $\varphi_{c}$ is the core orbital with atomic eigenvalue $\varepsilon_{c}$.

Wavefunctions are constructed by the restricted Hartree–Fock method for the singlet state and by restricted open-shell Hartree–Fock (ROHF) calculations for the triplet state, followed by multireference configuration interaction expansions,
$$
\Psi=\sum_{k} c_{k} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}} \operatorname{det}\left(\chi_{1}^{k}, \chi_{2}^{k}, \ldots \chi_{n}^{k}\right)=\sum_{k} c_{k} \Phi_{k}.
$$

The CI wavefunction for the state of interest, $\Psi_{r}$, is constructed in two steps:²³ first, a small expansion $\Phi_{r}'$ is generated by performing single and double excitations from an initial RHF configuration for the state:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{r}^{\prime} & =\lambda_{0} \Phi_{r}^{\mathrm{RHF}}+\left(\sum_{i a} \lambda_{i a} \Gamma_{i, a}+\sum_{i j a b} \lambda_{i j a b} \Gamma_{i j, a b}\right) \Phi_{r}^{\mathrm{RHF}} \\
& =\sum_{p} \lambda_{p} \Phi_{p}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Configurations $\Phi_{p}$ are retained if they satisfy the interaction criterion,
$$
\frac{\left\langle\Phi_{p}|\mathrm{H}| \Phi_{r}^{\mathrm{RHF}}\right|^{2}}{\left|E_{p}-E_{r}^{\mathrm{SCF}}\right|}>10^{-4} \text { hartree. }
$$

The final wavefunction for the state is obtained by forming single and double excitations from components of the small expansion with coefficients >0.05,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_{r} & =\lambda_{0} \Phi_{r}^{\prime}+\left(\sum_{i a} \lambda_{i a} \Gamma_{i, a}+\sum_{i j a b} \lambda_{i j a b} \Gamma_{i j, a b}\right) \Phi_{r}^{\prime} \\
& =\sum_{q} \lambda_{q} \Phi_{q},
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{\left\langle\Phi_{q}|\mathrm{H}| \Phi_{r}^{\prime}\right|^{2}}{\left|E_{q}-E_{r}^{\prime}\right|}>10^{-6} \text { hartree. }
$$

Energy contributions from the remaining electronic configurations, which are not retained in the above expansion, are estimated using perturbation theory.²⁴,²⁵ Details of the CI procedure can be found in Ref. 26.

For the localized orbital treatment, prior to the CI expansion, occupied and virtual orbitals are transformed to maximize their interaction with orbitals defining a region of the ring from which excitations will occur. The localization transformation is carried out by maximizing the exchange interaction between localized orbitals and O and Ti basis functions that define the excitation site on the ring. The choice of excitation site is the same as found in the SCF solution where no assumptions about the extent of electron–hole delocalization were made. The resulting localized orbitals which have large interactions with the region of interest are used to construct the CI wavefunction, i.e., the orbitals defined above as $\{i, j, \ldots\}$ and $\{a, b, \ldots\}$ are localized occupied and virtual orbitals, respectively. Since the excitation of interest is a transition from O 2p to Ti 3d, the reference region is chosen to include Ti and O atoms that are nearest neighbors of the O site on which the hole is primarily located. However, the transformation allows more distant orbitals to enter the description depending on the size of their exchange interaction. The procedure allows inclusion of a well-defined portion of the electron correlation of each system (treated the same way in all rings) associated with the change in occupancy. Twenty electrons, which occupy the localized orbitals, are correlated by carrying out multiple excitations, as defined above, while the occupancy of the remaining orbitals is constrained to remain unchanged. Since the region of interest is invariant on changing the size of the ring, the number of correlated electrons and orbitals remains the same in all rings.

Total energies of the ground and excited states are obtained from multireference CI calculations, in which three configurations are chosen to define the initial representation of the states: $\Phi_{\text{closed-shell singlet}}'$, $\Phi_{\text{triplet}}'$, and $\Phi_{\text{open-shell singlet}}'$. Expansion orbitals are taken from the spatial part of the RHF orbitals of the triplet state. The open-shell configurations are constructed to describe the $m_{s}=0$ component of the singlet and triplet states. The states generated by the CI expansion

are eigenfunctions of $S^2$, i.e., they are rigorously triplet and singlet states. This procedure ensures the accurate description of the excited triplet state as well as the open-shell singlet state. The final CI wavefunction is obtained by iterating the multistate multireference CI procedure until the energy of the singlet ground state is the same as found by a CI calculation based on the RHF wavefunction for the singlet ground state.

In all calculations, the 1s, 2s, and 2p orbitals and electrons of titanium are replaced by the core potentials as defined above. Valence molecular orbitals are expanded by means of the Gaussian basis functions [7s7p5d]/(2s2p2d) for titanium and [10s5p]/(4s2p) for oxygen.

## IV. EXCITATION ENERGIES

Table I shows excitation energies from the ground state to the triplet and open-shell singlet states for three different hole locations of $(TiO_2)_{2n}$, $n=3$–9. Note that, in the present study, the ring geometry is unchanged on the electron excitation; therefore, reported excitation energies are Franck–Condon or vertical excitation energies. Experimental values inferred from photoelectron spectroscopy (PES) of anions $(TiO_2)_n^-$ ($n=1$–10) are also reported. The calculated and experimental values for a $TiO_2$ molecule are included in Table I. For all three hole locations, the triplet and singlet excitation energies increase with the ring size, consistent with the trend observed in the PES experiments. For $TiO_2$ and $(TiO_2)_{2n}$, $n=4$ and 5, the calculated and PES experimental values are in good agreement for the in-plane locations, while for the out-of-plane location, the calculated values are smaller than the experimental ones. In all cases, the calculated values for $(TiO_2)_6$ are smaller than experiment. This may be due to the fact that $(TiO_2)_6$, which has a small binding energy, may be too small to construct a ring structure, and therefore, be inconsistent with the other ring structures. In addition, it should be noted that the structures of the $(TiO_2)_n^-$ ($n=1$–10) molecules in the PES experiments are not known and thus there is no basis to assume that they are ring structures.

For the largest ring, $(TiO_2)_{18}$, the triplet and singlet excitation energies are between 3.2 and 3.9 eV for all locations of the O 2p hole. Although values for the ring structures should not necessarily correspond to bulk excitation energies, the ring values are fairly close to the bulk phase values of 3.0 eV (rutile) and 3.2 eV (anatase). Of particular interest is the singlet–triplet splitting which is seen to vary considerably depending on the hole location. In Fig. 7, the transition energies for excitation from the ground state to the singlet and triplet excited states are plotted versus ring size. The singlet–triplet splitting is found to be almost negligible for the in-plane/inside hole location and large for the in-plane/outside location. The splitting for the out-of-plane location of the hole lies between these limits. The singlet–triplet splitting occurs because of the exchange interaction between the electron–hole orbitals and a variation in splitting suggests that the spatial extent of orbitals associated with the localized electron–hole pair may differ for the different hole locations. The Mulliken electron population for the occupied d orbitals in the triplet excited state is found to be essentially invariant with values from 2.06 to 2.09. Since the d occupancy is 1.8 in the singlet ground state, the increase in the d population on excitation is only $\sim$0.3 because of a back donation of d electron density to other nearest neighbor oxygens. On the other hand, the O 2p population of the triplet state, which is indicative of the spatial extent of the localized hole, varies between 0.5 and 0.7, being larger for the in-plane/outside location and smaller for the in-plane/inside location. See Fig. 8. Figures 5 and 6 show that, for the in-plane/inside location, the hole is in the nodal plane of the localized d electron and this would lead to a smaller exchange interaction. It is interesting to note that although the spatial extent of the hole is very small for all locations, as is evident from Fig. 5, and the population difference is at most 0.2, the effect of the hole variations on the electronic states arising from the three types of excitations is not negligible. The fact that hole localization occurs for all ring systems even without a symmetry breaking change in geometry is a general conclusion of the present work.

The states corresponding to the singlet and triplet excited states are degenerate in the bulk but become distinct levels due to the energy splitting as the particle size becomes small. Depending on the method of detection, it is possible that only transitions to the singlet excited states will be observed and this means that excitation energies may differ from the triplet bandgap. ## V. CONCLUSIONS

The ground and the lowest singlet and triplet excited states of a series of titanium dioxide ring structures, $(TiO_{2})_{2n}$, $n=3-9$, are investigated theoretically using many-electron configuration interaction theory. The lowest energy vertical excitation for both singlet and triplet states corresponds to electron transfer from oxygen 2p to titanium 3d orbitals. Localized electron-hole states are found to be significantly lower in energy than states constrained to be symmetric and delocalized. The ring studies demonstrate a practical way to carry out accurate CI calculations on localized electron-hole pairs that can be extended to the treatment of such excitonic states in bulk or nanoparticle systems.

Although the rings and bulk structures are entirely different, excitation energies of the larger ring structures approach limiting values of 3.2-3.9 eV, in qualitative accord to the observed bulk bandgap values of 3.0 eV (rutile) and 3.2 eV (anatase). The triplet-singlet splitting occurs due to the interaction between the localized electron-hole pair and is found to depend strongly on the location of the localized hole.

As the particle size decreases, transitions to singlet excited states are more commonly observed because of spin selection rules, while in the bulk limit, the triplet and singlet states become degenerate. This transition from bulk nature to molecular nature of the electronic states explains the different experimental findings concerning a blueshift in the bandgap attributed to quantum size effects.
