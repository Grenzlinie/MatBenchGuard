# Anti-ferrodistortive-Like Oxygen-Octahedron Rotation Induced by the Oxygen Vacancy in Cubic $SrTiO_3$

Minseok Choi,* Fumiyasu Oba,* Yu Kumagai, and Isao Tanaka

In recent years, many quaint findings in $SrTiO_3$ have led to lots of attempts to search for new physics in this material.$^{[1-8]}$ From the easy formation of the oxygen vacancy ($V_O$), it is considered that $V_O$ or its complex defects can be intimately related to the rich variety of phenomena observed in $SrTiO_3$, for instance, the high sheet carrier density at the heterointerfaces with $LaAlO_3$,$^{[3,4]}$ blue and green light emission,$^{[5]}$ a paraelectric to ferroelectric transition without extrinsic dopants,$^{[6-8]}$ and a high $n$-type conductivity.$^{[9-12]}$ Therefore, fundamental understanding of the properties of $V_O$ itself is an essential prerequisite for establishing the physics of $SrTiO_3$ and related perovskite oxides.

Despite many experimental and theoretical efforts, the understanding of $V_O$ is still limited. Transport-property measurements have indicated that $V_O$ induces a shallow donor level and leads to metallic behavior at a carrier density of $10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-3}$.$^{[9,10]}$ For single crystals with a lower carrier density, the ionization energy of $V_O$ ranges from 0.07 to 0.16 eV, depending on the carrier density and the degree of compensation by residual acceptors.$^{[9,11]}$ Moos and Härdtl estimated the ionization energies for $V_O^0 \rightarrow V_O^+$ and $V_O^+ \rightarrow V_O^{2+}$ to be 3 meV and 0.3 eV, respectively, and thereby suggested that $V_O^+$ is stable for a wide range of temperature.$^{[12]}$ However, electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) measurements have not identified any paramagnetic centers related to $V_O^+$ in single crystals at low or room temperature. Likewise, theoretical studies have not clarified the electronic structure of $V_O$ in cubic $SrTiO_3$. Density functional studies using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and the local density approximation (LDA) have found no localized electronic states in the bandgap.$^{[13,14]}$ The approaches using Hubbard-$U$ ($+U$) corrections and hybrid functionals, which reduce the spurious self-interactions on the Ti $3d$ orbitals, have yielded controversial results on the formation of localized in-gap states.$^{[6,7,14-17]}$

In this work, we demonstrate that $V_O$ in cubic $SrTiO_3$ possesses the atomistic structure and electronic properties that are different from earlier understanding. Our systematic first-principles calculations show that the oxygen-octahedron rotation – similar to the atomic configurations in the low-temperature anti-ferrodistortive (AFD) tetragonal phase – occurs locally around $V_O$ even in the cubic phase, leading to localized electronic states in the bandgap. We propose that such atomistic and electronic features are strongly correlated with the experimentally observed electrical and optical properties of $SrTiO_3$.

The calculations were performed using the projector augmented-wave method$^{[18]}$ and the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE)-GGA exchange-correlation functional$^{[19]}$ with a Hubbard-$U$ correction (GGA+$U$) as implemented in the VASP code.$^{[20-22]}$ The electronic wavefunctions were described using a plane-wave basis set with an energy cutoff of 400 eV. A rotationally invariant $+U$ method$^{[23]}$ was applied to the Ti $3d$ orbitals with $U_{\text{eff}} = 4.36\ \text{eV}$.$^{[7,14]}$ Supercells containing 135 to 1080 atoms were used with $3 \times 3 \times 3$ to $\Gamma$-only $k$-point sampling (see Supporting Information). The atomic coordinates were relaxed until the force acting on each atom was reduced to less than $0.05\ \text{eV}\ \mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$. For a few cases, calculations using the Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof (HSE06) hybrid functional,$^{[24-26]}$ providing good physical descriptions of defects in wide-bandgap oxides,$^{[27-32]}$ were conducted to complement the GGA+$U$ results. Spin polarization was considered in all the calculations.

The formation energy of $V_O$ is evaluated as:

$$
\Delta E_{\text{f}}(V_O^q) = E_{\text{T}}(V_O^q)-E_{\text{T}}(\text{H}) + \mu_{\text{O}} + q(\varepsilon_{\text{VBM}} + \varepsilon_{\text{F}}) \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\text{T}}(V_O^q)$ is the total energy of a supercell with $V_O$ in charge state $q$, and $E_{\text{T}}(\text{H})$ is the total energy of the host supercell. $\mu_{\text{O}}$ is the oxygen chemical potential, varying between the oxygen-poor limit ($\mu_{\text{Sr}} = \mu_{\text{Sr(bulk)}}$, $\mu_{\text{Ti}} = \mu_{\text{Ti(bulk)}}$, and $\mu_{\text{Sr}} + \mu_{\text{Ti}} + 3\mu_{\text{O}} = \mu_{\text{SrTiO}_3\text{(bulk)}}$) and the oxygen-rich limit ($\mu_{\text{O}} = \frac{1}{2}\mu_{\text{O}_2\text{(molecule)}}$), which are determined by the thermodynamic equilibrium conditions in the Sr-Ti-O ternary system.$^{[7,13]}$ $\varepsilon_{\text{VBM}}$ and $\varepsilon_{\text{F}}$ are the valence band maximum (VBM) and the Fermi level measured from the VBM, respectively. To reduce errors arising from the GGA+$U$ and finite-sized periodic supercell approaches, several a posteriori processes were carefully applied$^{[7,33-35]}$ in consideration of earlier density functional studies.$^{[36-39]}$ The details are described in the Supporting Information along with the cell-size dependence.

AFD-like oxygen-octahedron rotation in the paraelectric cubic (PC) phase. $^{[42]}$ Considering the local symmetry breaking and shallow donor behavior, $V_O$ can induce the octahedron rotation likewise. The oxygen-octahedron rotated structure (ORS) is constructed using the lattice constant optimal for the PC phase $(a^{GGA+U}=3.977\mathring{A})$ and a rotation angle $\theta$ of $6.5^\circ$. For the AFD tetragonal phase, $\theta$ is calculated to be $7.0^\circ$ ($\sqrt{2}a^{GGA+U}=5.602\mathring{A}$, $(c/a)^{GGA+U}=1.009$), similar to the previously reported LDA and GGA values, $^{[37]}$ but larger than an experimental value of $2.1^\circ$ ($\sqrt{2}a^{\text{exp}}=5.503$ $\mathring{A}$, $(c/a)^{\text{exp}}=1.001$).$^{[43,44]}$ Previously, Sai and Vanderbilt$^{[45]}$ suggested that the lack of quantum fluctuations in traditional density functional approaches can cause such an overestimation. It also possibly results from approximated exchange-correlation functionals since recently, the HSE06 hybrid functional was shown to well reproduce the experimental $\theta$ value.$^{[37]}$ In this study, we obtain a $\theta$ value of $\approx2^\circ$ for both ORS and AFD using the HSE06 hybrid functional.

Owing to the symmetry, two species of $V_O$ exist in ORS: $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$, as described in Figure 1. Assuming the octahedron rotation along the $c$-axis, $V_{Ra}$ is located at the oxygen site displaced due to the rotation, while $V_{Rc}$ is on the rotation axis. The electronic structure and formation energies of $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ are evaluated, as well as $V_O$ in PC $SrTiO_3$ ($V_C$). Our calculations predict localized electronic states of $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ in the bandgap (Figure 2,3). In the neutral charge state, $V_{Ra}$ creates a singly-occupied state at 0.47 eV below the CBM, with $(d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{3z^2-r^2})$ orbital characteristics of the nearest Ti atoms of $V_{Ra}$. $V_{Rc}$ introduces a singly occupied state at 0.50 eV with $d_{3z^2-r^2}$ orbital characteristics of the Ti atoms near $V_{Rc}$. A singly occupied effective-mass-like donor state is also generated for both $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$. For the + charge state, localized states are found at 0.46 eV for $V_{Ra}$ and 0.50 eV for $V_{Rc}$ below the CBM, which are very similar to the states of neutral $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$. In contrast, $V_C$ provides no electron-occupied state in the bandgap.

Up to now, there have been no theoretical reports about $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$. For $V_C$, many density functional studies have been performed, but there is a puzzling discrepancy on its electronic states. This may result from the treatment of spin-polarization, exchange-correlation functional, supercell size, and/or computational details. The standard density functional approaches with the LDA and GGA are unlikely to produce localized states for $V_C$ in the bandgap. An LDA study did not identify a $V_O$-induced in-gap localized state.$^{[14]}$ The in-gap state found with the GGA is not a localized state but an effective-mass-like state, since the squared wavefunction at the highest occupied state is spatially delocalized with a host CBM-like orbital characteristic.$^{[13]}$ In addition, earlier GGA+$U$ studies have confirmed the absence of in-gap states.$^{[6,7]}$ However, Cuong et al., using LDA+$U$,$^{[14]}$ and Hou and Terakura, using GGA+$U$ and HSE06,$^{[15]}$ reported $V_O$-induced localized states in the bandgap. We also make a similar finding using GGA+$U$, that is to say, a singly occupied state of $V_C^0$ at 0.5 and 0.4 eV below the CBM for 135- and 320-atom supercells, respectively. However, this state becomes higher in energy for larger supercells, and it is located above the CBM for the 625- and 1080-atom supercells. This leads us to conclude that $V_C$ introduces no localized in-gap states. Very deep, localized states reported in the B3PW hybrid-functional and atomic-orbital basis study are incompatible with the shallow donor-like picture of $V_O$ found in experiments.$^{[17]}$

Figure 4 shows the formation energies of $V_{Ra}$, $V_{Rc}$, and $V_{C}$ obtained using the 1080-atom supercell. The oxygen-poor limit, where their formation energies are lowest, is considered here to address defect energetics in reduced $SrTiO_{3}$. $V_{C}$ does not create any thermodynamical transition levels in the bandgap. Similarly, the $\varepsilon(+/0)$ levels are located nearly at the CBM for both $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$. A notable difference from $V_{C}$ is that $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ show $\varepsilon(2+/+)$ levels at 0.11 and 0.14 eV below the CBM, respectively, reflecting their one-electron structures with localized in-gap states (Figure 2). These features of $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ are consistent with the experimental observations of a very small ionization energy for $V_{O}^{0}\to V_{O}^{+}$, for example, $3\ \text{meV.}^{[12]}$ In addition, their $\varepsilon(2+/+)$ levels correspond well to the ionization energy of 0.07–0.16 eV reported for single crystals with a low carrier density. $^{[9,11]}$ The ionization energy of $0.3\ \text{eV}^{[12]}$ is attributed to defects other than isolated $V_{O}$. The shallow nature of the $\varepsilon(2+/+)$ levels $(\approx 0.1\ \text{eV})$ suggests double ionization of $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ in a wide temperature range, and thereby explains no EPR signals of $V_{O}^{+}$ in single crystals.

In Figure 4, $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ are lower in formation energy than $V_{C}$. However, a strong resemblance between ORS and AFD makes ORS energetically more favorable than the PC phase, regardless of the presence or absence of $V_{O}$. To support our suggestion on the octahedron rotation around $V_{O}$, we modeled a structure with an increased lattice constant at which the PC phase is more stable than ORS. We find that a 10% expansion of the lattice constant is required with GGA+$U$ for stabilizing PC against ORS in total energy, and with HSE06, a 4% larger lattice constant $(1.04a^{\text{HSE06}}=4.06\ \mathring{\text{A}})$ is sufficient. We thus employed the HSE06 hybrid functional and a 320-atom ORS supercell containing neutral $V_{Ra}$ or $V_{Rc}$ with $a=1.04a^{\text{HSE06}}$. All of the atoms were fully relaxed using the $\Gamma$-only $k$-point sampling. It is found that the co-ordinates of the oxygen atoms far from $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ are almost identical to those in the PC phase (i.e., $\theta\approx0^{\circ}$), while a clear oxygen-octahedron rotation is recognized around $V_{Ra}$ and $V_{Rc}$ with a maximum of $\theta\approx13^{\circ}$ for $V_{Ra}$ and $\approx10^{\circ}$ for $V_{Rc}$. This leads us to the conclusion that the AFD-like oxygen-octahedron rotation can be induced in the vicinity of $V_{O}$ in $n$-type cubic $SrTiO_{3}$.

Our discussion on the octahedron rotation is now extended to $V_{O}$ clusters composed of two $V_{O}$ ($V_{O}+V_{O}$). Such $V_{O}$ clusters have been suggested to be stable in the PC, $^{[14,15]}$ but have not been considered for ORS. In ORS, there are several possible configurations such as $V_{Ra}+V_{Ra}$, $V_{Rc}+V_{Rc}$, and $V_{Ra}+V_{Rc}$ (Figure 1). These configurations are modeled using 320-atom supercells with $2\times2\times2$ $k$-point sampling, and their stability is examined using the binding energy $E^{\text{b}}$ as:

$$
E^{\text{b}}=\Delta E_{\text{f}}((V_{\text{O}}+V_{\text{O}})^{0})-2\Delta E_{\text{f}}(V_{\text{O}}^{0})\tag{2}
$$

The details of each configuration are described in Figure 1. $d$ and $E^{b}$ denote the interatomic distance between the two $V_{O}$ in each configuration and the binding energy, respectively.

A negative $E^{b}$ value indicates that the two $V_{O}$ are preferentially located close to each other (exothermic), whereas a positive value means that they repel each other (endothermic). Note that the formation energies of isolated $V_{Ra}^{0}$ and $V_{Rc}^{0}$ are very close to each other (a ≈ 0.01 eV difference) (Figure 4), and hence binding energies well correspond to relative formation energies of the clusters. As found in Table 1, the most-stable configuration is $c1$ (non-magnetic state), which consists of two $V_{Rc}$ located along the $c$-axis. This configuration introduces deep in-gap states occupied by two electrons and has two electrons in effective-mass-like donor states (Figure 2). The electronic structure is similar to that of a $V_{C}$ cluster in the PC found in previous studies. $^{[14,15]}$ Our calculations also predict that $c2$, composed of $V_{Rc}$ in the $ab$-plane, is similar in binding energy, and creates four singly occupied, localized states in the bandgap.

Finally, the optical (vertical) transition energies associated with localized in-gap states of $V_{O}$ and its clusters are evaluated on the basis of the Franck-Condon principle. Light-emission energies in the recombination process of an electron $(e^{-})$ at the states of $V_{Ra}^{+}$ ($V_{Rc}^{+}$) with a hole $(h^{+})$ at the VBM, that is, $V_{Ra}^{+}(V_{Rc}^{+})+h^{+}→V_{Ra}^{2+}(V_{Rc}^{2+})$, are 2.5 (2.5) eV. The two most-stable $V_{O}$-clusters, $c1$ and $c2$, yield similar energies for emission: 2.7 eV for $c1^{0}+h^{+}→c2^{+}$, and 2.4 (2.4) eV for $c1^{+}(c2^{+})+h^{+}→c1^{2+}(c2^{2+})$. These values are close to the energies of blue light (2.8-2.9 eV) and green light emission (≈2.4 eV) observed in $Ar^{+}$-irradiated, reduced, and/or electron-doped $SrTiO_{3}$ films. $^{[5,46]}$ Note that such optical transition energies are not explained by $V_{C}$ without in-gap states. Recently, we suggested that Ti antisite-like defects, which are composed of a Ti atom off-centered from the Sr site and are favorable to form under Ti-rich and O-poor conditions, exhibit emission energies of 2.3-2.4 eV. $^{[7]}$ Accordingly, the blue and green light emission is attributed to $V_{O}$ accompanied by the octahedron rotation and the Ti antisite-like defects, both showing in-gap states with localized characteristics.

To summarize, we have demonstrated that $V_{O}$ can induce a local AFD-like oxygen-octahedron rotation in cubic $SrTiO_{3}$. In contrast to the case without the octahedron rotation, localized electronic states are introduced in the bandgap. Our findings prominently contribute to the accurate understanding of the thermal ionization and light emission reported experimentally in reduced $SrTiO_{3}$, which have been under debate for a long time.
