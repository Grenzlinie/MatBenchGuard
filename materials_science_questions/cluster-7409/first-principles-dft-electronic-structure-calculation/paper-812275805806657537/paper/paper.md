# First-principles study on the electronic structures and absorption spectra for the $PbWO_4$ crystal with lead vacancy $^{\star}$

Liu Tingyu*, Shen Jianqi, Zhang Qiren

College of Science, University of Shanghai for Science and Technology, 516 JunGon Road, Shanghai 200093, China

Received 7 April 2005; received in revised form 29 April 2005; accepted 10 May 2005 by C. Tejedor
Available online 31 May 2005

## Abstract
The electronic structures and absorption spectra for the perfect $PbWO_{4}$ (PWO) crystal and the crystal containing lead vacancy $V_{Pb}^{2-}$ have been calculated using density functional theory code CASTEP with the lattice structure optimized. The calculated absorption spectra of the PWO crystal containing $V_{Pb}^{2-}$ exhibit seven absorption bands peaking at 1.72 eV (720 nm),2.16 eV (570 nm), 2.81 eV (440 nm), 3.01 eV (410 nm), 3.36 eV (365 nm), 3.70 eV (335 nm) and 4.0 eV (310 nm), which are very close to the experimental values. It predicts that the 330, 360, 420, 500-750 nm absorption bands are related to the existence of $V_{Pb}^{2-}$ in the PWO crystal.

## 1. Introduction
In recent years, lead tungstate $PbWO_{4}(PWO)$ crystal has attracted special interests. Since it has been chosen as a scintillator for detectors at the large collider in CERN [1-3]. PWO crystal has been under investigation for some decades but till now some of its properties are still debated. Some problems should be solved in order to improve its scintillation properties.

The PWO is a typical non-stoichiometric crystal. There are lead vacancies $V_{Pb}^{2-}$ and oxygen vacancies $V_{O}^{2+}$ in the PWO crystal and the amount of lead vacancy is larger than that of oxygen vacancy [1,4-8]. Up to now, many defect models [9-13] related to $V_{Pb}^{2-}$ have been proposed but are still debated. Unfortunately, they were deduced indirectly owing to the shortage of direct experimental evidences. In fact, there are a large number of successful simulations on the optical properties of crystals using the powerful code of CASTEP [14,15]. In order to further study the optical properties of the PWO crystal, in this paper the electronic structures and absorption spectra of either the perfect PWO crystal or the PWO crystal containing lead vacancy $V_{Pb}^{2-}$ have been calculated by using CASTEP code [16,17] with the lattice structure optimized. Because, the existence of $V_{Pb}^{2-}$ would cause lattice distortion [18], the lattice structure optimizing is necessary before we study the optical properties of the crystal.

### 2.2. The calculation methods

The lattice optimization for the PWO containing $V_{\mathrm{Pb}}^{2-}$ is performed by using CASTEP code. We use ultrasoft pseudopotentials for the lead, tungsten and oxygen atoms and a plane-wave cut-off of 340 eV. Optimal atomic positions are determined until satisfying the conditions: (1) the maximal force on them was smaller than 0.5 eV/nm; (2) the maximal change of energy per atom was smaller than 0.00001 eV; and (3) the maximal displacement was smaller than 0.0001 nm. All other calculations are performed on the basis of the lattice structure optimized.

For the ground-state electronic structure calculations within the DFT method we have used the CASTEP code. Generalized gradient approximations (GGA) were chosen for the theoretical basis of density function. In setting up the CASTEP run, basic parameters were chosen as follows: kinetic energy cutoff$=340$ eV, fast Fourier transform (FFT) grid dimensions$=60\times60\times64$, and space representation$=$ reciprocal, SCF tolerance$=1.0\times10^{-6}$ eV/atom. The optical absorption spectrum arises from two parts: the interband transitions and the Drude terms (intraband transitions). Here we focus our attention on the first because of the structural transformation occurring in the interband channel. The dielectric function of an anisotropic material is a complex symmetric second-order tensor describing the linear response of an electronic system to an applied external electric field. The imaginary part of the dielectric tensor is directly related to the band structure of a solid, so it can be computed from the knowledge of single-particle orbitals and energies approximated by the solutions of the Kohn-Sham equations. However, it is a well-known fact that DFT calculations underestimate the band gap. To take this into account, a 'scissors operator' was used, allowing a shift of the bands situated above the valence band and a rescaling of the matrix elements. This was shown computationally (comparing GW and LDA bandstructures) that most of the difference between Kohn-Sham eigenvalues and the true excitation energies can be accounted for by a rigid shift of the conduction band upwards with respect to the valence band [19]. Assuming the one-electron rigid-band approximation and neglecting the electron polarization effects (Koopmans' approximation), in the limit of linear optics and of the visible-ultraviolet range, the imaginary part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_{i}$ is given by [20,21]

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{i}(\omega) & =\frac{e^{2} \pi^{2}}{\varepsilon_{0} m^{2}\left(\omega-\frac{\Delta c}{h}\right)^{2}} \\
& \times \sum_{\mathrm{V}, \mathrm{C}}\left\{\int_{\mathrm{BZ}} \frac{2 \mathrm{~d} \vec{K}}{(2 \pi)^{3}}\left|\vec{a} \cdot \vec{M}_{\mathrm{v}, \mathrm{c}}\right|^{2} \delta\left[E_{\mathrm{c}}(\vec{K})+\frac{\Delta c}{h}-E_{\mathrm{v}}(\vec{K})-\hbar \omega\right]\right\}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\vec{a}$ is the unit vector potential $\vec{A}$. $\vec{M}_{\mathrm{v}, \mathrm{c}}$ is the matrix of dipole transition. C is the conductor band. V is valence band. BZ is Brillouin zone. $\Delta c$ is the shifting value of the scissors operator. $e$ is the electron charge. $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the dielectric constant in vacuum. $m$ is the free electron mass. $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the frequency of the incident wave. $h$ is Planck constant. $\vec{K}$ is wave vector. The Kramers-Krönig transformation, link the real and the imaginary parts of the dielectric function. The Kramers-Krönig transformation and the smearing factor of 0.12 eV are used to obtain the real part $\varepsilon_{r}(\omega)$ of the dielectric function. According to the fact that the calculated band gap of the perfect PWO crystal is 3.3 eV and the experimental value is 4.8 eV, the shifting value of the scissors operator $\Delta c$ is chosen as 1.5 eV. For dielectric tensor calculation, the Brillouin zone (BZ) integration was made with 64 independent k-points for PWO crystal.

### 3.2. Absorption spectra

The absorption coefficient follows from the relationship $\sigma=\omega \varepsilon_{i} / n c$. The absorption spectra under $E \| a, E \| b$ and $E \| c$ calculated from both the perfect PWO crystal and that containing $V_{\mathrm{Pb}}^{2-}$ are shown in Fig. 3.

![](./images/812275805806657537_6.jpg)

Fig. 3. The polarized absorption spectra of the perfect PWO crystal (dotted lines) and PWO crystal containing $V_{\mathrm{Pb}}^{2-}$ (solid lines).

Compared with the absorption spectra under $E \| a$ and $E \| b$ for the perfect PWO crystal shown in Fig. 3, absorption spectra under $E \| a$ and $E \| b$ of the PWO containing $V_{\mathrm{Pb}}^{2-}$ exhibit characteristics as follows: (1) the absorption spectra are shifted to the range of lower energy; (2) there are five additional bands peaking at about 1.72 eV (720 nm), 2.16 eV (570 nm), 2.81 eV (440 nm), 3.01 eV (410 nm), 3.36 eV (365 nm) and 3.70 eV (335 nm), respectively, as shown in Fig. 4. The peak positions of the five bands are rather well fitted to the five experimental absorption bands [12,13].

Compared with the absorption spectra under $E \| a$ and $E \| b$ for the PWO crystal containing $V_{\mathrm{Pb}}^{2-}$, the absorption spectra under $E \| c$ show characteristics as follows (1) the peak locating at 4.5 eV disappear and a new peak at about 5.2 eV appears. It means the probability of the transition at 4.5 eV under $E \| c$ is very low and the probability of the transition at 5.2 eV under $E \| a$ and $E \| b$ is also very low. The intrinsic reasons need further study by analyzing the symmetry of the PWO crystal and the transition rule. (2) There are a new band peaking at about 4.0 eV (310 nm), as shown in Fig. 4. The intensities of these bands range from 1.0 to 2.5 eV under $E \| a$ and $E \| b$ is much larger than that under $E \| c$. The experimental results show that the absorption bands range in 500-700 nm under $E \perp c$ is larger than under $E \| c$ [5]. The intensities of these bands peaking at 3.01 and 3.36 eV under $E \| a$ and $E \| b$ is a little larger than that under $E \| c$. The intensities of these bands peaking at 3.70 eV under $E \| a$ and $E \| b$ is a little smaller than that under $E \| c$. The experimental results show that the 330, 360 and 420 nm absorption bands occurs weak anisotropy. The calculated results well coincide with the experimental results. Polarized properties and peak positions of the calculated absorption bands well coincide with the experimental results. It further confirms that all the absorption bands peaking at 330, 360, 420, 500-750 nm are related to the existence of the $V_{\mathrm{Pb}}^{2-}$ in PWO.

### 4. Conclusion

The electronic structure and absorption spectra under $E\parallel a$, $E\parallel b$ and $E\parallel c$ for the perfect PWO crystal and PWO crystal containing $V_{\text{Pb}}^{2-}$ have been calculated by using DFT code CASTEP accounting for the lattice relaxation. The calculated peak positions of absorption bands are in good agreement with the experimental absorption spectral results. The absorption spectra on polarized light are also in good agreement with the experimental results. It reasonably predicts that all the absorption bands peaking at 330, 360, 420 and 500–750 nm are related to the existence of the $V_{\text{Pb}}^{2-}$ in PWO crystal.
