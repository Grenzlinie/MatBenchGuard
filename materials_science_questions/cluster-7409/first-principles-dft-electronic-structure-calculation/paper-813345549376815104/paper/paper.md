## ABSTRACT

Microcosmic investigations of weak red-emitting materials are crucial for their further development and application. In this work, we have focused on the band structures and electronic properties of Pr mono- and (Zn, Pr) co-doped $CaTiO_3$ using density functional theory. Zn substitution for Ca or Ti tends to form clusters energetically with Pr substituting for Ca in $CaTiO_3$. In Pr mono-doped $CaTiO_3$, the $O_{2p} \rightarrow Ti_{3d}$ transition in $CaTiO_3$ host corresponds to the centered 330 nm excitation spectra. The gap states above the valence band of ~1.30 eV and ~2.06 eV are hybridized by $Pr_{4f}$, $O_{2p}$ and $Ti_{3d}$ orbitals. They are mainly due to $Pr_{4f}$ orbitals in $CaTiO_3$:Pr. The former gap level is related to red emission at 614 nm due to $^1D_2 \rightarrow ^3H_4$ transition of $Pr^{3+}$ activator. The latter is related to the excitation spectra centered at 380 nm due to the low-lying Pr-to-mental intervalence charge transfer transitions ($Pr^{3+}-O^{2-}-Ti^{4+} \rightleftharpoons Pr^{4+}-O^{2-}-Ti^{3+}$). The band structures of (Zn, Pr) co-doped $CaTiO_3$ keep the similar gap levels to those in Pr mono-doped $CaTiO_3$. The incorporation of Zn brings out the two stronger localized gap states, which are hybridized by $Pr_{4f}$, $O_{2p}$ and $Ti_{3d}$ orbitals, in comparison with those in Pr mono-doped $CaTiO_3$. Therefore, when Zn impurities are added into Pr doped $CaTiO_3$, the present calculations visualize the two enhanced levels and the distorted structures around Pr.

## 1. Introduction

Oxide based long lasting phosphors are the subjects of many investigations due to their predominant luminous efficiencies and high brightness levels. Long lasting phosphors for two of the tricolor, blue and green, can be successfully prepared to meet the demand of all-night luminescence and have been available in the commercial markets. The red persistent phosphors, the third of tricolor, are still under the stage of research in the past decades. Among the large amount of red candidates, Pr-doped $CaTiO_3$ has been studied extensively for its single bright red emission at about 614 nm excited by ultraviolet light originating from the $^1D_2 \rightarrow ^3H_4$ transition of $Pr^{3+}$ [1–5]. The excitation spectra are mainly centered at about 330 nm and 380 nm. An effective distortion around Pr activator is an important target for exploring a better phosphor. Extensive attention has been focused on doping different impurities into $CaTiO_3$:$Pr^{3+}$, such as $Na^+$, $Al^{3+}$, $Zn^{2+}$ and $Mg^{2+}$ [6–9]. The effect is attributed to the formation of trapping centers related to $Pr^{3+}$ defects [3]. Especially, the additional dopant of $Zn^{2+}$ in $CaTiO_3$:Pr appears to be a promising and suitable option because of its superior performance in phosphorescence and long lasting properties [8,9]. Usually, $Pr^{3+}$ ions are thought to occupy $Ca^{2+}$ site since the ionic radius of $Pr^{3+}(1.13\ \mathring{A})$ is close to that of $Ca^{2+}(0.99\ \mathring{A})$, and $Zn^{2+}(0.74\ \mathring{A})$ can occupy both $Ca^{2+}$ and $Ti^{4+}(0.68\ \mathring{A})$ sites [10]. The transfer of energy to the $Pr^{3+}$ persistent luminescent center in $CaTiO_3$ is assumed to be similar to the excited phenomena in other wide band gap materials, i.e., $Er^{3+}$-doped silicon [11], $CaAl_2O_4$:$Eu^{2+}$ [12] and $La_{1/3}MO_3$(M=Nb, Ta):$Pr^{3+}$ [13]. Up to now, however, the exact mechanism for this observation is not known. In addition, synthesis of efficient and strong red-emitting phosphorescent material through doping is still problematic for various technical reasons. Hence, comprehensive investigations of the mechanism behind the enhancement of the fluorescence and phosphores- cence of (Zn, Pr) co-doped $CaTiO_3$ are crucial for further develop- ment and applications of $CaTiO_3$: Pr red phosphors.

In recent years, it is demonstrated that the density functional theory (DFT) can provide detailed information on electronic properties of defective semiconductors [8,14–18]. Compared with abundant experiments on the impurity doping, theoretical investigations for impurity doping on calcium titanates are relatively rare. To the best of our knowledge, no calculation for (Zn, Pr) co-doped $CaTiO_3$ is performed so far. We expect that exploring electronic properties of (Zn, Pr) co-doped $CaTiO_3$ will favor the development and applications of red long lasting phosphors.

The present work is to investigate the effects of Zn on the electronic structures of $CaTiO_3$:Pr by DFT calculations. Band structures, total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) as well as Pr–O bond lengths are computed.

## 2. Methodology

The results are obtained with the DFT code Vienna ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [19]. The electron-ion interaction is described by the projector augmented wave method (PAW) [20] and the exchange-correction function for electrons is treated via the generalized-gradient approximation (GGA) [21] with an energy cutoff of 450 eV. Based on the experimental values of orthorhombic $CaTiO_3$ [4,9], the single cell structure is fully relaxed. The $2 \times 2 \times 2$ supercell lattice parameters are fixed at 10.99, 15.35 and 10.80 Å with 96 O, 32 Ca and 32 Ti atoms in the defect-free case. The $k$-point mesh for the 160-atom $CaTiO_3$ host is $2 \times 2 \times 2$ according to Monkhorst-Pack frame. The atomic coordinates are relaxed until the Hellmann-Feynman forces acting on each atom are less than 0.03 eV/Å. Ca atoms in orthorhombic $CaTiO_3$ form slightly distorted octahedron with two kinds of bond length between the central Ca and vertex Ca atoms in the octahedron. As indicated in Fig. 1, the central Ca atom is replaced by Pr atom. A calcium vacancy or Zn impurity is located in one of four Ca atoms in the equatorial plane. A Ti atom is substituted by Zn. Spin-polarized calculations are performed for all defects. The on-site Coulomb interaction is described by means of the GGA+U approach with $U$=1.7 [22] for 4f orbitals of Pr impurity. Fig. 2 (a) shows the calculated band structure of perfect $CaTiO_3$. The maximum of valence band (VBM) and minimum of conduction band (CBM) are at the $G\ (0,\ 0,\ 0)$ point. The direct band gap is 2.25 eV. As indicated in Fig. 2 (b), the $O_{2p}$ and the $Ti_{3d}$ states govern the VBM and CBM, respectively. These features are consistent with the previous DFT-based calculations [23,24]. The calculated band-gap energy is smaller than the experimental value of 3.8 eV [25]. This discrepancy stems from the approximation (GGA) in the density-functional theory. In this work, we will focus on the gap state features of band structures and electronic structures for the defective $CaTiO_3$.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Binding energies for defective $CaTiO_3$:$Pr$

Experimentally, in $CaTiO_3$:$Pr$ samples, if no addictives or fluxes are added to the initial mixture, the charge compensation for positively charged $Pr_{Ca}$ defects occurs by the formation of intrinsic defects, such as negatively charged Ca vacancies. When a Pr atom is doped on a Ca site, Pr atom is in the center of a distorted octahedron formed by six neighboring Ca atoms.

We considered three defective $CaTiO_3$ systems: a calcium vacancy, a Zn on Ca and a Zn for Ti substitution in Pr doped $CaTiO_3$ (see Fig. 1). The three defective systems correspond to $CaTiO_3$:$V_{Ca}$,$Pr_{Ca}$, $CaTiO_3$:$Zn_{Ca}$,$Pr_{Ca}$ and $CaTiO_3$:$Zn_{Ti}$,$Pr_{Ca}$, which are abbreviated as VCP, ZCP and ZTP, respectively. In order to evaluate whether such cluster configurations are stable, we calculated binding energies by using the strategy described in Ref. [26]. A negative value of binding energy corresponds to a metastable or a stable bound dopant pair when both are present in a system. The binding energies for $(V_{Ca}$,$Pr_{Ca})$ complex, $(Zn_{Ca}$,$Pr_{Ca})$ complex and $(Zn_{Ti}$,$Pr_{Ca})$ complex are about $-3.30$ eV, $-0.02$ eV and $-3.16$ eV, respectively. The negative values indicate that VCP, ZCP and ZTP are metastable or stable phases. The negative values imply Zn and Pr have attractive interaction each other.

### 3.2. Band structures for defective CaTiO₃:Pr

The calculated band structures for VCP, ZCP and ZTP are displayed in Fig. 3. The valence bands and conduction bands of VCP, ZCP and ZTP systems are similar to those of perfect CaTiO₃. The direct band gaps of the three defective cases are slightly narrowed to 2.20 eV. Due to the contributions from Pr 4f atomic orbitals, there are two similar groups of gap levels in the three defective cases: one group is below the valence band of ~1.50 eV while another is above the valence band of ~1.50 eV. For ZTP, there is an extra group of gap levels above the valence band of ~0.18 eV.

Experimentally, the excitation spectra are mainly centered at about 330 nm and 380 nm. The 330 nm band arises from the band edge absorption of the CaTiO₃ host due to O₂ₚ→Ti₃d transitions [2]. A sharp emission at 614 nm can be observed for all the samples [2,25] due to the ¹D₂→³H₄ transition of Pr³⁺. The 380 nm band can be attributed to a low-lying Pr-to-mental intervalence charge transfer transitions (Pr³⁺-O²⁻-Ti⁴⁺⇌Pr⁴⁺-O²⁻-Ti³⁺) [25]. The two increase bands of 614 and 380 nm are found when Zn impurities are added into CaTiO₃: Pr samples. In order to further explore the changes of electronic structures for defective CaTiO₃, we calculated their total and partial densities of states.

### 3.3. Densities of states for defective CaTiO₃:Pr

Fig. 4 (a) and (b) are TDOS and PDOS of VCP, respectively. Due to the contributions from Pr₄f atomic orbitals, we find that there are three additional states in the band gap of VCP, as indicated in Fig. 4 (a) and (b). The observed energy levels of the rare-earth ions have shown that there are seven configurations, such as H, F, G, D, P, I and S states [25,27]. The energy of ³H₄ state is the lowest while the energy of ¹S₀ state is the highest among all configurations. The states of ³H₄ and ¹S₀ are localized in the valence band and conduction band, respectively. The other states, i.e. ¹D₂, ³P₀ and so on, are ranked in the gap according to their energy order. The electron paramagnetic resonance spectrometry experiments on CaTiO₃:Pr have demonstrated that excitation into the charge transfer from Pr to Ti leads to the quenching of the ³P₀ emission without affecting the ¹D₂ emission in CaTiO₃:Pr [25]. Therefore, the charge transfer from Pr to Ti (Pr³⁺-O²⁻-Ti⁴⁺⇌Pr⁴⁺-O²⁻-Ti³⁺) is considered instead of the ³P₀ state in the gap. Combined with the two group defect levels within the band gap for VCP, the gap states levels above the valence band of ~1.50 eV are assumed to embody the charge transfer from Pr to Ti (Pr³⁺-O²⁻-Ti⁴⁺⇌Pr⁴⁺-O²⁻-Ti³⁺) while those below the valence band of ~ 1.50 eV are assumed to involve the ¹D₂ state. From the left inset of Fig. 4 (b), the Pr atom is weakly bonded with O atoms due to their weak overlapped states within the valence band. The localized levels in the band gap are mixed by Pr₄f, O₂ₚ and Ti₃d orbitals, as shown in the right inset of Fig. 4 (b). The up-spin gap states above the valence band of ~1.30 eV are stronger than that of ~1.05 eV. They are mainly from Pr 4f orbital contributions. For the sake of convenience, we assume the stronger states of ~1.30 eV embody ¹D₂ state. Similarly, the stronger down-spin states above the valence band of ~2.06 eV act as Pr-O-Ti charge transfer center instead of ~2.00 eV. Therefore, for CaTiO₃: Pr, the excitation peak at 330 nm can be assigned to the transition from the valence band to the conduction band (O₂ₚ→Ti₃d). The excitation peak at 380 nm can be assigned to the charge transfer from Pr to Ti (Pr³⁺-O²⁻-Ti⁴⁺⇌Pr⁴⁺-O²⁻-Ti³⁺). The red emission of 614 nm is assigned to ¹D₂ → ³H₄ transitions of Pr³⁺. In the CaTiO₃:Pr, the Pr-to-Ti charge transfer may occur either via the three oxygen atoms shared by adjacent Pr and Ti atoms in the structure or by direct Pr-Ti interaction through the triangular face formed by the above mentioned oxygen atoms. Since Pr-to-Ti charge transfer is related to Pr³⁺ and Pr⁴⁺, it can be either self-trapped or stabilized by trapping at some centers [28].

Fig. 5 (a) and (b) are TDOS and PDOS of ZCP, respectively. As indicated in Fig. 5 (a), three newly localized states appear above the VBM of ~1.22, 1.92 and 2.00 eV. From the analysis of PDOS, they are contributed by the mixed orbitals of O₂ₚ, Ti₃d and Pr₄f, respectively. The multiple states in ZCP above the valence band of ~1.22 and 2.00 eV are stronger than that of VCP. They are beneficial to the excitation spectra of 380 nm due to the low-lying Pr-to-mental intervalence charge transfer transitions (Pr³⁺-O²⁻-Ti⁴⁺⇌Pr⁴⁺-O²⁻-Ti³⁺). Furthermore, the multiple states may prefer to the excitation peaks between 425 and 495 nm due to 4f-4f transitions of Pr³⁺ [8-9].

TDOS and PDOS of ZTP are shown in Fig. 6. For ZTP, there are three localized levels within the forbidden gap. The first state above the VBM of ~ 1.92 eV is weak and the second state above

Regardless of the ratio, Zn impurities give rise to stronger electronic gap states than those of VCP. Correspondingly, the probabilities for electron transitions are greater than those of VCP.

### 3.4. Structural properties for defective CaTiO₃:Pr

As listed in Table 1, the average Pr-O distance of VCP is 2.43 Å. The eight Pr-O bond lengths are divided into five groups. The inequipotential configurations bring about the distorted structures around Pr activator. The average Pr-O distance of ZCP is 2.44 Å. The systems keep the similar Pr-O symmetric configurations of that of VCP. For ZCP, two of the eight O atoms around Pr atom are bonded with the Zn atom. The two O atoms are noted in Pr-O1 and Pr-O2. The Zn addition on Ca site ties up the O1 and O2 atoms. Therefore, the Pr-O1 and Pr-O2 distances are lengthened by $\sim0.04$ Å. Meanwhile, the partially-filled 4f atomic orbitals of Pr ions are rearranged. The rearrangement enhances the gap states of inner-4f orbitals and a low-lying charge transfer tunnel from Pr to Ti is feasible. Therefore, the diverse gap states of ZCP will benefit the exaction of CaTiO₃:Pr. The stronger Pr-to-mental intervalence charge transfer transitions ($\text{Pr}^{3+}-\text{O}^{2-}-\text{Ti}^{4+}\rightleftharpoons\text{Pr}^{4+}-\text{O}^{2-}-\text{Ti}^{3+}$) will favor the long decay time of CaTiO₃:Pr.

As listed in Table 1, the average Pr-O distance of ZTP is 2.42 Å. In ZTP, three of eight O atoms around Pr are bonded with the nearest Ti atom. The three atoms are indicated in Pr-O2, Pr-O6 and Pr-O8. Neither of the Pr-O distances is equal. The values of Pr-O2, Pr-O6 and Pr-O7 bonds are 2.26, 2.38 and 2.50 Å, respectively. Compared with the corresponding values in VCP, they are shorted by 0.11, 0.11 and 0.13 Å. In ZTP, the configurations of Pr-O bonds are inequipotential. The inequipotential configurations mean the distorted structures around Pr activator. These phenomena have verified the suggestion that the ligands for activators influence the luminescence properties of rare earth

doped yttrium sesquioxides phosphors [31]. At the same time, compared with the average distance of the three Ti (Zn)-O bonds in VCP, the distance of ZTP is lengthened by 0.11 Å. Such a behavior can be understood in terms of the electronic structural difference between Ti and Zn. It is well known that Ti₃d is fully unoccupied while Zn₃d is fully occupied. When Zn replaces the site of Ti atom, a repulsive interaction happens between Zn₃d and O₂p. The interaction makes them keep a longer distance. Therefore, the new gap states above the VBM of ~0.18 eV are hybridized by O₂p, Zn₃d, Ti₃d orbitals, regardless of Pr orbitals. Specially, the gap states near the valence band maximum are apt to yield more holes. It will favor the long decay time of CaTiO₃: Pr [30].

Therefore, the associated structural distortion with Zn additions will make CaTiO₃:Pr to reach a new equilibrium in which bonds and electrons are redistributed in the system. On the one hand, the redistributions shrink the band gaps from 2.25 to 2.20 eV, which may be beneficial to the electrons transitions from the top of valence bands to the bottom of conduction bands. On the other hand, the redistributions increase the electronic states above of ~1.2 eV, 2.0 eV, which are beneficial to the transitions of iner-4f electrons and the Pr-to-mental intervalence charge transfer transitions ($\text{Pr}^{3+}-\text{O}^{2-}-\text{Ti}^{4+}ightharpoonup \text{Pr}^{4+}-\text{O}^{2-}-\text{Ti}^{3+}$).

The above results directly support the proposal by Aitasalo et al [9,12]. According to their proposal, as shown in Fig. 7, when a long lasting phosphor is excited, the energy is provided either directly to the traps (Transition 1) or via the conduction band (Transition 2). The electrons are then drifted thermally at room temperature and participate in electron-hole recombination. During the recombination, the released energy will cause excitation of $\text{Pr}^{3+}$ centers. Finally, the red emission results from the typical $^1\text{D}_2\rightarrow^3\text{H}_4$ transition of the $\text{Pr}^{3+}$ luminescent centers (Transition 3). In CaTiO₃:Pr, the excitation spectra mainly consist of two bands centered at about 330 and 380 nm. A sharp emission at 614 nm can be observed due to the $^1\text{D}_2 \rightarrow ^3\text{H}_4$ transition of $\text{Pr}^{3+}$. Accordingly, the three typical spectra can be illustrated through density functional calculations. The electron transitions from the valence band to the new localized state appears above the VBM of ~2.0 eV, which is correlated with a low-lying Pr-to-mental intervalence charge transfer transition ($\text{Pr}^{3+}-\text{O}^{2-}-\text{Ti}^{4+}ightharpoonup \text{Pr}^{4+}-\text{O}^{2-}-\text{Ti}^{3+}$) (Transition 1). The electron transitions from O₂p to Ti₃d of the CaTiO₃ host correspond to the centered 330 nm excitation spectra (Transition 2). The transition from the states above the VBM of ~1.2 eV to the valence band is correlated with red emission at 614 nm due to $^1\text{D}_2\rightarrow^3\text{H}_4$ transition of $\text{Pr}^{3+}$ (Transition 3). Zn defects increase the multiple localized states of trap levels so as to favor Transition 1 and Transition 3 in CaTiO₃: Pr. Such transitions are helpful to red emission and decay of CaTiO₃:Pr. Therefore, the DFT calculations have distinctly identified that there are plenty of mixed gap states by the activator and/or other atoms. An effective distortion around Pr activator is an important target for exploring a better phosphor. The calculated results display the effective distortion around Pr obtained by Zn impurities. Therefore, from the computational point of view, it is possible to explore a better red phosphor than (Zn, Pr) co-doped CaTiO₃ phosphor.

## 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have performed a detailed investigation of electric properties of Pr mono- and (Zn,Pr) co-doped CaTiO₃ using density functional theory. Three configurations of CaTiO₃:V_Ca,Pr_Ca, CaTiO₃: Zn_Ca,Pr_Ca and CaTiO₃: Znᵀᵢ, Pr_Ca were considered. The negative binding energies correspond to (V_Ca,Pr_Ca), (Zn_Ca,Pr_Ca) and (Znᵀᵢ, Pr_Ca) complexes as the clustered forms. In CaTiO₃:V_Ca,Pr_Ca, the localized levels above the valence band of ~1.30 eV are related to the red emission at 614 nm due to $^1\text{D}_2\rightarrow^3\text{H}_4$ transition of $\text{Pr}^{3+}$. The impurity levels above the valence band of ~2.06 eV correspond to the centered 380 nm excitation spectra due to the low-lying Pr-to-mental intervalence charge transfer transitions. The two gap states are hybridized

by $Pr_{4f}$, $O_{2p}$ and $Ti_{3d}$ orbitals are mainly due to $Pr_{4f}$ orbitals in $CaTiO_3$:Pr. When Zn impurities substitutes Ca and Ti sites of $CaTiO_3$: Pr, the calculation results for band structures and electronic states confirm the two enhanced levels.
