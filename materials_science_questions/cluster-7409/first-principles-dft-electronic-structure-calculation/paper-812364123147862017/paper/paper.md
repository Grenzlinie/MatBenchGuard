# Optical properties of strontium titanate by ab initio calculation within density functional theory

Meng-Qiu Cai, Zhen Yin *, Ming-Sheng Zhang

Physics Department and Center for Materials Analysis, National Laboratory of Solid State Microstructure, Nanjing University, Honkou Ru, Nanjing 210093, PR China

Received 13 November 2003; in final form 25 February 2004
Published online: 20 March 2004

## Abstract

Electronic structures and optical properties in the core-level spectra of strontium titanate are investigated by the first-principles under scissor approximation. The direct- and indirect-band gaps are equal to 2.24 and 1.87 eV, respectively. The complex dielectric function and optical constants, such as optical absorption coefficient, refractive index, extinction coefficient, energy-loss spectrum and reflectivity, are calculated, which are in good agreement with the experimental data, better than those from linear muffin-tin orbital method. We also have interpreted the origin of the spectral peaks on the basis of the electronic band structures.

## 1. Introduction

Strontium titanate $(SrTiO_{3})$, a typical perovskite material, has been attracted for the fundamental re- search and perspective application in the field of ferro- electricity, optoelectronics and microelectronics as micro-capacitors, ultrathin gate chips, optical switches and so on [1-5]. The optical properties of $SrTiO_{3}$ deserves investigating. Experimentally, Cardona [6] measured the infrared reflectivity spectra of $SrTiO_{3}$ in a low-energy range and got principal dielectric functions via Kramers-Kroning relations. Bäuerle et al. [7] mea- sured several optical constants by infrared reflectivity spectra in a high-energy range. Electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS), ultraviolet photoemission spec- troscopy, valence electron-energy loss spectroscopy and X-ray photoemission spectroscopy were also employed to study the optical properties [8-10]. The optical properties of $SrTiO_{3}$ have been theoretically investigated by using tight-binding linear muffin-tin orbitals (TB- LMTO) method [11] and by full-potential tight-binding linear muffin-tin orbitals (FP-LMTO) method [12].

However, the relativistic effects and the scissor approx- imation (SA) on both the $SrTiO_{3}$ electronic band structure and the optical properties were not considered in their calculations. Saha et al. [11] and Ahuja et al. [12] gave direct-band gap for $SrTiO_{3}$, leading to inaccurate optical properties, since the experimental data showed its indirect-band gap [9,13]. The complex dielectric function of $SrTiO_{3}$ was calculated in lower energy range 0-12 eV, instead of higher energy range [14], it is difficult to make whole comparison between theoretical and ex- perimental optical properties. Till now the origin is still not clear. It is necessary to calculate the electronic structure for better understanding its optical properties.

In this Letter, we calculate the electronic structure and optical properties of cubic $SrTiO_{3}$ in a wide energy range 0-32 eV by using an accurate full-potential line- arized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW) method, where the used density-functional theory considers full- relativistic electronic contributions to the band-structure calculation [15-17]. Exchange and correlation effects are treated within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [18]. The scissor approximation (SA) makes conduct band (CB) rigid shift toward higher energies, compensating the systematic error caused by the GGA, where the band gap was underestimated.

## 2. Computation

The cubic $SrTiO_3$ belongs to the space group $Pm3m$ ($O_{\text{h}}^1$), with Sr atom sitting at the origin point, Ti at the body center and three oxygen atoms at the three face center [19]. The muffin-tin sphere radii are $R_i=2.0, 1.8$ and 1.5 a.u. for Sr, Ti and O atoms, respectively. For controlling the size of basis set for the wave functions, we set the $R_{\text{mt}}K_{\text{max}}=0.7$, expanding to 14 in the muffin-tin. The well-converged basis sets consist of about 775 LAPW functions and 36 local orbits chosen for O 2s, Ti 3s and 3p and Sr 5p states. Integration in the reciprocal space was performed by using the tetrahedron method, and a $12\times12\times12$ mesh was used to represent 84 $k$-points in the irreducible wedge of Brillouin zone (BZ) in the self-consistent calculations.

The imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ of the dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$ has the following expression [20]:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_2(\omega)=&\frac{ve^2}{2\pi\hbar m^2\omega^2}\int\text{d}^3k\sum_{n,n'}|\langle\boldsymbol{k}n|\boldsymbol{p}|\boldsymbol{k}n'\rangle|^2\\
&\times f(\boldsymbol{k}n)(1-f(\boldsymbol{k}n'))\delta(E_{\boldsymbol{k}n}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}n'}-\hbar\omega),\quad(1)
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\hbar\omega$ is the energy of the incident phonon, $\boldsymbol{p}$ is the momentum operator $(\hbar/i)(\partial/\text{d}x)$, $|\boldsymbol{k}n\rangle$ is a crystal wave function and $f(\boldsymbol{k}n)$ is the Fermi-function. The real part $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ of dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$ is evaluated from the imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ by the Kramers-Kroning relationship
$$
\text{Re}[\varepsilon(\omega)]=\varepsilon_1(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\pi}M\int_0^\infty\frac{\omega'\varepsilon_2(\omega')}{\omega'^2-\omega^2}\text{d}\omega',\quad(2)
$$
where $M$ implies the principal value of the integral. The other optical parameters, such as absorption coefficient $I(\omega)$, extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$, the refractive index $n(\omega)$, reflectivity $R(\omega)$ and energy-loss spectrum $L(\omega)$, are immediately calculated in terms of the components of the complex dielectric function [20].

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Electronic structure

The calculated band structure of $SrTiO_3$ along the high-symmetry directions in the BZ is shown in Fig. 1. In the band structure and the DOS, all energies are referred relative to a zero energy at the top of the valence band (VB). As shown in Fig. 1, the upper VB with nine bands consists of mainly O 2p orbitals and has a bandwidth of 5.0 eV, which is consistent with values of 5-6 eV by XPS [10]. The nine valence bands at the $\Gamma$ at BZ center are three triply degenerated levels $\Gamma_{15}$ (-2.81 eV), $\Gamma_{25}$ (-1.17 eV) and $\Gamma_{15}$ (-0.35 eV). Below O 2p bands, Sr 4p and O 2s orbitals form the narrow semicore bands. The three energetically separated low-lying bands at $\Gamma_1$ (about -16.43 eV) and $^2\Gamma_1$ (about -17.41 eV) are derived from O 2s orbitals. Between the O 2p and O 2s, the three narrow bands at $^3\Gamma_{15}$ located about -14.71 eV arise from Sr 4p orbitals, while the XPS measurements by Battye et al. [21] showed the Sr 4p band locates at -16.5 eV below Fermi face. The most prominent unoccupied energy bands in the conduction band (CB) are composed of the threefold degenerate Ti 3d $\text{T}_{2\text{g}}$ orbitals (1.9-4.5 eV), the twofold degenerate Ti 3d $\text{E}_{\text{g}}$ orbitals (4.5-8.4 eV) and Sr 4d orbitals (5.0-9.0 eV), and various empty O 2p levels. The Ti 3d energy bands at $\Gamma$ point are $^1\Gamma_{25'}$ ($\sim$1.89 eV) and $^1\Gamma_{12}$ ($\sim$4.34 eV). The Sr 4d dis-persive high-energy conduction bands at $\Gamma$ point are $^2\Gamma_{12}$ ($\sim$5.43 eV), $^3\Gamma_1$ ($\sim$6.24 eV) and $^2\Gamma_{25'}$ ($\sim$8.43 eV). Our calculated electronic structures of $SrTiO_3$are good agreement with the experimental results from scanning transmission electron microscopy, vacuum ultraviolet spectroscopy and spectroscopic ellipsometry [9].

### 3.2. Optical properties

$\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ as function of the photon energy are shown in Fig. 2a,b, respectively, where the dotted lines represent experimental data [7], the dashed lines and solid lines stand for the results calculated by TB-LMTO method [11] and our FP-LAPW-SA method, respec- tively. It is noted that we choose a fine $k$-mesh (with many $k$-points) for the optical property calculation of SrTiO₃ because of requiring a dense mesh of eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenvectors. The major peak positions of $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ for both calculated and experimental results are tabulated in Table 2, where peaks A (5.68 eV), B (6.42 eV) and C (8.74 eV) correspond to the transition from O 2p VB to Ti 3d CB, and the peaks D (9.64 eV), E (10.98 eV) and F (12.48 eV) to the transition from O 2p VB to Sr 4d CB. There are no clean-cut structures for $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ above 15 eV because of the van ishing of the oscillator coupling strength between VB and CB [22]. The optical properties of critical peaks of G (15.68 eV), H (21.78 eV) and I (24.80 eV) are ascribed to the transitions of inner electron excitation from near VB semi-core states Sr 4p and O 2s levels to CB, which is consistent with the experimental result [7].

Our calculated $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ is in good agreement with the experimental result [7], better than those calculated by TB-LMTO method with major peak of the spectra ap- peared at about 3.92 eV differing from the experimental data 0.9 eV [11]. Our calculated $\varepsilon_{1}$ at zero frequency equals 5.35 shown in Fig. 2b, well comparable to the experimental value 4.92 [7]. Our FP-LAPW-SA did not make shape approximation, and the core states were calculated self-consistently in the crystal potential.

Fig. 3a–e shows the calculated results on the energy dependence of energy-loss spectrum $L(\omega)$, extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$, absorption coefficient $I(\omega)$, reflectivity $R(\omega)$ and refractive index $n(\omega)$, respectively. The function $L(\omega)$ shown in Fig. 3a describes the energy loss of fast electron traveling the material [23]. The sharp peak is associated with the plasma oscillation [24]. The peak value of 28 eV in the $L(\omega)$ spectrum calculated by our FP-LAPW-SA is in excellent agree- ment with experimental results of 29 eV [7], much better than the value of 5 eV calculated by TB-LMTO [11]. It should be pointed out that such a large dis- crepancy of $L(\omega)$ stems from plasma excitation, $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ calculated by TB-LMTO equals zero at about 5 eV, but the value calculated by our FP-LAPW-SA equals zero at about 25 eV as shown in Fig. 3b. The sharp structure at about 28 eV in our $L(\omega)$ spectrum corre- sponds to a rapid decrease of reflectance in Fig. 3d. This process is associated with transitions from the filled Sr 4p and O 2s bands, right below VB, to empty CB. For the $I(\omega)$ in Fig. 3c, we only consider the proper absorption without considering the polarized absorption, which has minor influence on $I(\omega)$ [25]. The absorption coefficient is very big (about $10^{6}\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) and decreases rapidly in the low-energy region. ## 4. Conclusions

We have calculated the complex dielectric function and the energy-dependent optical parameters $L(\omega)$, $k(\omega)$, $I(\omega)$, $R(\omega)$ and $n(\omega)$ of $SrTiO_{3}$ by using first-principles FP-LAPW method with full-relativistic calculation in a wide energy range. The scissor approximation makes CB a upshift of 1.33 eV toward higher energy, compensating the error by GGA. In contrast to the calculated results by TB-LMTO method, our results are in better agreement with the experimental results in the low- and high-energy region. Based on our accurate electronic energy-band structure, the origin of the spectral peaks are ascribed to the contribution of the interband transition from valence bands O 2p levels to conduction bands Ti 3d levels or even higher conduction band Sr 5s and 4d in the low-energy region and outer core electron excitation from near valence band semicore states Sr 4p and O 2s to Ti 3d conduction band in the high-energy region.
