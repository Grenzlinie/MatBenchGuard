# An Efficient Strategy for Controlled Band Gap Engineering of $KTaO_3$

Brindaban Modak and Swapan K. Ghosh*

Theoretical Chemistry Section, Bhabha Atomic Research Centre and Homi Bhabha National Institute, Mumbai 400 085, India

**ABSTRACT:** In this theoretical study, a systematic investigation has been carried out to explore the effect of several anionic dopants (individually as well as in combination) on the electronic structure of $KTaO_3$. According to the defect formation energy calculation, the formation of a codoped system is more favored in comparison to the monodoped $KTaO_3$. All the electronic structure calculations have been performed using the Heyd, Scuseria, and Ernzerhof hybrid functional, which reproduces the band gap of $KTaO_3$ (3.61 eV) in very close agreement with the experimental value of 3.60 eV. Although doping with N reduces the effective band gap of $KTaO_3$, introduction of localized acceptor states may adversely affect the photoconversion efficiency. However, the band gap remains almost unchanged due to doping with F and decreases by only a small extent for Cl, Br, and I doping. The scenario, however, changes on codoping with N and any of the halogen elements, which results in the formation of charge-compensated systems. In all the cases, a clear band structure is produced, ensuring good photoconversion efficiency. The present study reveals that the extent of band gap narrowing in the case of codoping with N and F is quite significant (almost 1 eV) to improve the visible light activity of $KTaO_3$ effectively. More interestingly, this does not involve any considerable shifting of the conduction band minimum (CBM) in the downward direction. This is very crucial for $KTaO_3$ because its CBM potential is quite close to the hydrogen reduction potential. The CBM level is found to be shifted in the upward direction for codoping with (N, Cl/Br/I). Therefore, all the codoped systems are suitable for overall water splitting, which has also been confirmed though band edge alignment with respect to water redox levels. Thus, the present study through electronic structure calculation finds suitable dopant pairs for the controlled band gap engineering of $KTaO_3$.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Development of an efficient strategy for the utilization of solar energy is one of the biggest challenges of science and technology. Hydrogen is a good energy carrier and is considered as one of the future fuels for clean energy generation. Photocatalytic water splitting under sunlight is a promising approach for the generation of hydrogen; therefore, it is considered to be a long-term solution for environment friendly, renewable energy. During the past few decades, significant efforts have been made to develop efficient photocatalysts for this purpose. $^{1-5}$ Among them, metal oxide based semiconductor materials are found to be the most popular because of their wide availability, lack of toxicity, durability, etc. Unfortunately, most of these materials show poor photoactivity under sun light because of their large band gap (>3 eV). Doping with a foreign element has been found to be one of the most successful strategies in reducing the band gap. $^{6-11}$ The major challenge in this field is to find suitable dopants which reduce the band gap without sacrificing the photoconversion efficiency. This is because the dopant element often introduces localized acceptor/donor states in the forbidden region and thus reduces the effective band gap. Because these states may serve as trapping centers for photogenerated charge carriers, the photoconversion efficiency is reduced drastically. Another aspect is the vacancy formation, which arises because of charge mismatch between the host and dopant elements. It is well-known that defects in the crystal lattice enhance the electron-hole recombination rate.

Metal oxide semiconductors of perovskite type $(ABO_3)$ cover a significant fraction of the photocatalyst materials for the application of hydrogen generation as well as degradation of organic pollutants. $^{12-20}$ They are sufficiently long lasting, nontoxic, and mostly composed of natural metallic elements. More importantly, their electronic properties can be tuned easily by doping at any of the three possible sites, which can accommodate a wide range of foreign elements. $^{21,22}$ Popular photocatalysts of this kind include $NaTaO_3$, $KTaO_3$, $NaNbO_3$, $KNbO_3$, $SrTiO_3$, etc. It has been observed that the tantalates are normally more effective than the titanate and niobate, particularly in the absence of any cocatalyst, because of more negative conduction band minima potential. This is attributed to the higher energy of the Ta 5d orbital in comparison to the Nb 4d or Ti 3d orbital. $^{23}$ Between the two tantalates, $KTaO_3$ has several advantages over $NaTaO_3$. First, the dominate form at room temperature for $KTaO_3$ is perfect cubic $(Pm\overline{3}m)$, which is more symmetric than the orthorhombic crystal structure $(Pcmn)$ of $NaTaO_3$. This leads to better delocalization of photogenerated charge carriers, and consequently the electrons and holes have a longer lifetime in the case of $KTaO_3$ than

Moreover, the cubic form for $KTaO_3$ exists over a wide temperature range, whereas the crystal structure of $NaTaO_3$ changes with temperatures, which may lead to complicated phase behavior in the reaction conditions. Second, the smaller band gap for $KTaO_3$ (3.6 eV) also facilitates to show higher activity under the same exposure in comparison to $NaTaO_3$, for which the band gap is 4.1 eV. However, band gap engineering of $KTaO_3$ to make it active for visible light absorption is highly restricted by its CBM position. Because the CBM potential of $KTaO_3$ is not much negative, any attempt to reduce the band gap may further shift the CBM position in the downward direction, which can drastically affect its reducing properties (such as hydrogen evolution ability during photosplitting of water). This factor limits widespread applications of $KTaO_3$. Most of the studies done to date to enhance the photocatalytic activity of $KTaO_3$ mainly focus on the reduction of the electron−hole recombination rate either by doping with a foreign element or by employing an organic chromophore as a sensitizer. For example, Ishihara et al. found that controlling charge density with an acceptor dopant significantly improves the photocatalytic activity of $KTaO_3$ for the decomposition of water. $^{24}$ In a series of studies, they have investigated the effect of several elements as dopant, including bivalent $(Zn^{2+})$, trivalent $(Y^{3+}, Al^{3+}, Ga^{3+}, In^{3+})$, tetravalent $(Ti^{4+}, Zr^{4+}, Hf^{4+}, Si^{4+}, Ge^{4+})$, pentavalent $(Sb^{5+}, Nb^{5+})$, and hexavalent $(W^{6+})$ species, at the Ta lattice site. Among them, $Zr^{4+}, Hf^{4+}$, and $Ga^{3+}$ are found to be the most effective in enhancing the rate of hydrogen production. This has been explained by the reduction in charge density, which in turn increases the lifetime of the photogenerated charge carriers. Hagiwara et al. investigated the effect of codoping with N into Zr-doped $KTaO_3$ and observed a significant improvement of visible light activity. $^{25}$ Paulauskas et al. attempted to increase the photoelectrochemical performance of $KTaO_3$ by introducing Ca or Ba at the K lattice site. $^{26}$ Liu et al. observed an enhancement of photoactivity due to doping with La at the K lattice site. $^{27}$ This has been attributed to better delocalization of the electron and hole in more dispersive and dense band structure of La-doped $KTaO_3$, resulting in improved charge carrier separation. Bajorowicz et al. improved the photocatalytic activity of $KTaO_3$ under visible light by forming a ternary composite photocatalyst in combination with CdS, and $MoS_2$, which works on a two-photon excitation mechanism. $^{28}$ Hagiwara et al. demonstrated that photoactivity of $KTaO_3$ for the decomposition of water is significantly enhanced by employing cyanocobalomin dye as a sensitizer, which also facilitates better separation of the electron−hole pair. $^{29}$ Later on they modified the catalyst by various porphyrinoids, which increases the photoactivity by a significant extent because of increase of the lifetime of the charge separated state. $^{30}$ Recently, Wang et al. proposed a strategy to reduce the band gap of several perovskite materials through local rhombohedral-to-tetragonal structural transition. $^{31}$ A significant number of theoretical studies on the undoped $KTaO_3$ have been reported to date using various techniques to understand its electronic properties in a better way. $^{32-41}$

In this present study, our aim is to lower the band gap of $KTaO_3$ by elevating the VBM only, so that the hydrogen evolution property at the conduction band (CB) remains intact. Because the VBM and CBM of $KTaO_3$ are contributed by mainly the O 2p state and Ta 5d state, respectively, we choose only anionic type doping in the present study. For this purpose, we involve N, which has been shown to be one of the widely used anionic dopants for improving the visible light activity of various oxide-based semiconductor photocatalysts. $^{42-47}$ However, in spite of strong visible light activity, the N-doped systems have several limitations that must be overcome to achieve photoconversion efficiency at a satisfactory level. To this end, in the present case, we consider different halogen elements as a codopant. Halogen elements, particularly F, have been widely accepted both for monodoped as well as codoping because of their various unique features. $^{48-52}$ Using density functional theory (DFT), we systematically investigate the effect of all these dopant elements on the geometry and electronic structure of $KTaO_3$. It is well-known that the standard DFT severely underestimates the band gap; therefore, we employ the Heyd, Scuseria, and Ernzerhof (HSE) hybrid functional, $^{53}$ which has been shown to successfully reproduce the experimental band gap of a wide range of semiconductor materials. $^{54,55}$

In this paper, we discuss the computational methodology (section 2) and then disuss the geometry, defect formation energy, electronic structure, optical spectrum, and band edge alignment (section 3); finally, we present some concluding remarks (section 4).

### 2. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS
All the calculations have been carried out using Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP), $^{56}$ with projector augmented wave (PAW) potential $^{57}$ representing the ionic core and valence electrons interactions. The valence states involved for the construction of the potential are K $(3p^64s^1)$, Ta $(6s^25d^3)$, O $(2s^22p^4)$, N $(2s^22p^3)$, F $(2s^22p^5)$, Cl $(3s^23p^5)$, Br $(4s^24p^5)$, and I $(5s^25p^5)$. The valence electron orbitals are expanded using the plane wave basis set up to an energy cutoff of 500 eV. The doped systems have been modeled with $2×2×2$ supercell (40 atoms) of the cubic $KTaO_3$ crystal structure. All the model structures have been fully optimized by the relaxation of both cell parameters and the ionic positions using the exchange and correlation potentials of Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof (PBE) under the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). $^{58}$ The Brillouin zone sampling has been carried out using a Monkhorst and Pack scheme, $^{59}$ and a k-point mesh of $8×8×8$ has been chosen in the present study. The energy convergence for the self-consistent cycle was set to $10^{-6}$ eV. For electronic structure calculation we employed the HSE hybrid functional. In this functional, the ion−core interaction is split into two parts, viz. short-range (SR) and long-range (LR) parts. The short range interaction part is defined by mixing a certain fraction of exact Hartree−Fock exchange with PBE exchange, whereas the long-range part is solely defined by PBE. The exchange-correlation energy $(E_{XC}^{HSE})$ is given by the expression $^{53}$

$$
E_{\mathrm{XC}}^{\mathrm{HSE}}=a E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{SR}}(\mu)+(1-a) E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{PBE}, \mathrm{SR}}(\mu)+E_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{PBE}, \mathrm{LR}}(\mu)+E_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{PBE}} \tag{1}
$$

where $\mu$ is the screening parameter, defining the short-range and long-range limit. In this study, the standard value of the screening parameter $(0.2\ \mathring{A}^{-1})$ has been considered. The quantity of mixing the exact Hartree−Fock (HF) exchange is decided by the mixing coefficient $(a)$. Here, we perform test calculations on undoped $KTaO_3$ while varying the proportion of exact HF exchange mixing in the range from 25% to 35% to find out the best parameter which reproduces the experimental band gap (3.6 eV). The band structure has been plotted along the high-symmetry k-path. The calculation for density of states

(DOS) has been carried out by using the tetrahedron method with Blöchl correction.⁶⁰ The optical spectrum has been drawn by calculating the frequency-dependent dielectric function.⁶¹

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
### 3.1. Geometry.
$KTaO_3$ exists in the cubic crystalline structure ($Pm\overline{3}m$, space group no. 221) over a wide temperature range. The basic framework is described by $TaO_6$ octahedra, as shown in Figure 1. The calculated lattice parameter is found to be 4.03 Å at the GGA_PBE level. In the optimized geometry, the $Ta-O-Ta$ angle is $180^\circ$ and the $Ta-O$ bond length is 2.01 Å, which are consistent with previous theoretical reports.¹⁶ The dopant element (N or X = F, Cl, Br, I) is introduced at the oxygen lattice site of $2\times2\times2$ supercell of $KTaO_3$ (the dopant concentration corresponds to 4.17%). For codoping, two different elements (N and X) have been simultaneously introduced at the oxygen lattice sites. In this case, we considered two possible configurations, (Ta1, O1, O2) and (Ta1, O1, O3), which differ from each other in the distance between the dopant elements. All the structures have been fully relaxed (i.e., relaxation of both cell parameter and ionic positions) to obtain optimized geometries, which are considered for electronic structure calculations. In the case of monodoping with either N or F, the crystal structure is found to be hardly affected because of closeness of their ionic radii with that of oxygen ($R_{O^{-2}} = 1.35$ Å, $R_{N^{-3}} = 1.46$ Å, $R_{F^{-}} = 1.29$ Å).⁶² One of the lattice parameters is increased by 0.03 and 0.06 Å, for N-doped and F-doped $KTaO_3$, respectively. The calculated $Ta-N$ and $Ta-F$ bond distances are 2.03 and 2.14 Å, respectively, which are very close to the $Ta-O$ bond distance in the parent crystal structure. However, a significant change in the geometry is visible in the case of doping with Cl, Br, and I. One of the lattice parameters is enlarged by 0.28, 0.39, and 0.54 Å, respectively. This is the consequence of increased $Ta-X$ bond length (2.31, 2.38, and 2.47 Å, respectively) in comparison to the $Ta-O$ bond length. This arises because of larger mismatch between the ionic size of the dopant element and the host element ($R_{O^{-2}} = 1.35$ Å, $R_{F^{-}} = 1.29$ Å, $R_{Cl^{-}} = 1.81$ Å, $R_{Br^{-}} = 1.96$ Å, $R_{I^{-}} = 2.20$ Å).⁶² In the case of codoping with N and F, the change in the crystal structure of $KTaO_3$ is negligible. The optimized geometry of (N, F)-codoped $KTaO_3$ indicates that only one of the lattice parameters is enlarged by 0.15 Å, while the other two remain the same. However, the deviation from the ideal cubic structure becomes noticeable for the other three cases. For example, for (N, Cl)- and (N, Br)-codoped $KTaO_3$, one of the lattice parameters increased by around 1.03 Å, while the other two were reduced by 0.1 Å. On the other hand, codoping of N and I leads to increase of one of the lattice parameter by 2.58 Å and decrease of the other two by around 0.3 Å. The two configurations, where N and F are located 2.88 Å (str. I) and 4.03 Å (str. II) from each other, are energetically very close to each other ($\Delta E = 0.14$ eV). However, the energy difference between the two configurations increases to 1.16, 1.5, and 4.15 eV for the (N, Cl), (N, Br), and (N, I)-codoped $KTaO_3$, respectively. Further calculations have been carried out using the lowest-energy structures only, i.e., configuration with smaller $N-X$ distance, except for the case of (N, F)-codoped $KTaO_3$, for which both the configurations are considered.

### 3.2. Defect Formation Energy.
To determine the relative stability of the doped/codoped materials and find favorable synthetic condition, calculations for the defect formation energy have been carried out using the following relation⁶³,⁶⁴

$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{f}}=E_{\text {doped }}-E_{\mathrm{KTaO}_{3}}+n_{\mathrm{O}} \mu_{\mathrm{O}}-n_{\mathrm{N}} \mu_{\mathrm{N}}-n_{\mathrm{X}} \mu_{\mathrm{X}} \tag{2}
$$

where $E_{\text{doped}}$ and $E_{\text{KTaO}_3}$ are the energy of doped and undoped $KTaO_3$ calculated by using $2\times2\times2$ supercell, respectively. Here, $\mu_A$ stands for the chemical potential of the element A, and $n_A$ is the number of atoms removed or added to the system for the construction of the doped system.

When $KTaO_3$ reaches equilibrium with the reservoir of K, Ta, and O, their chemical potential can be related as

$$
\mu_{\mathrm{K}}+\mu_{\mathrm{Ta}}+3 \mu_{\mathrm{O}}=\mu_{\mathrm{KTaO}_{3}(\text { bulk })} \tag{3}
$$

The heat of formation can be expressed as

$$
\Delta=\mu_{\mathrm{KTaO}_{3}(\text { bulk })}-\mu_{\mathrm{K}(\text { bulk })}-\mu_{\mathrm{Ta}(\text { bulk })}-3 \mu_{\mathrm{O}(\text { gas })} \tag{4}
$$

Because $KTaO_3$ is a stable compound, $\Delta$ is negative. The elemental chemical potential should never cross the value in their bulk or gaseous form, i.e., $\mu_{\mathrm{K}} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{K}(\text{bulk})}$, $\mu_{\mathrm{Ta}} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{Ta}(\text{bulk})}$, and $\mu_{\mathrm{O}} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{O}(\text{gas})}$. Hence, one can write the following relations

$$
\mu_{\mathrm{K}(\text { bulk })}+\Delta \leq \mu_{\mathrm{K}} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{K}(\text { bulk })} \tag{5a}
$$

$$
\mu_{\mathrm{Ta}(\text { bulk })}+\Delta \leq \mu_{\mathrm{Ta}} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{Ta}(\text { bulk })} \tag{5b}
$$

$$
3 \mu_{\mathrm{O}(\text { gas })}+\Delta \leq 3 \mu_{\mathrm{O}} \leq 3 \mu_{\mathrm{O}(\text { gas })} \tag{5c}
$$

In the present study, chemical potential for the metallic species, $\mu_{\mathrm{K}(\text{bulk})}$, and $\mu_{\mathrm{Ta}(\text{bulk})}$ is obtained by calculating the energy of an atom in their metallic state, while $\mu_{\mathrm{O}(\text{gas})}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{N}}$ have been calculated from the energy of an oxygen or nitrogen molecule at the center of a $20\times20\times20$ Å³ box ($\mu_{\mathrm{O}(\text{gas})/N} = 1/2\ E_{\mathrm{O}_2/\mathrm{N}_2}$).

To prevent the formation of compounds as competing phases during the growing process of $KTaO_3$, one needs to consider some additional constraints.⁶⁵ For example, to prevent the formation of $K_2O$, we apply the constraint $2\mu_{\mathrm{K}} + \mu_{\mathrm{O}} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{O}}$. Similar type of constraints are applied to prevent the formation of $K_2O_2$ and $Ta_2O_5$. These conditions provide the range of

chemical potential for the stable growth of $KTaO_3$, which is shown by the filled or shaded area in Figure 2a, in two-

The calculated defect formation energy for both the monodoping and codoping cases is shown in Figure 2 as a function of oxygen chemical potential. The common feature for all the curves is that the defect formation energy becomes more favorable as we move toward oxygen-poor conditions. This can be justified by the requirement of relatively lower energy for the creation of oxygen vacancy under oxygen-poor conditions. For monodoping with N, the defect formation energy is calculated to be 5.22 eV under extreme oxygen-rich conditions, which reduces to 0.79 eV at the oxygen-poor conditions. In the case of doping with halogen element, F is found to be most favorable, because the calculated defect formation energy is found to be negative even under oxygen-rich conditions. The defect formation energy for this case increases in the order F < Cl < Br < I, i.e., the defect formation becomes costlier as the lattice mismatch between the host and dopant elements increases. For codoping, the calculated defect formation energy follows the same order as can be seen for monodoping, i.e., (N, F) < (N, Cl) < (N, Br) < (N, I). In the case of (N, F)-codoped $KTaO_3$, the calculated defect formation energies for the two different structures are found to be very close to each other. As can be seen from Figure 2, the defect formation energy for codoping is lower than that of monodoping at the oxygen-poor conditions, indicating that the formation of codoping is more favored in comparison to the doping with individual elements.

We have also considered doping at the interstitial positions. Full relaxation of the geometry indicates that the codoping of N and F at the interstitial sites significantly changes the crystal structure of $KTaO_3$. In this case, one of the lattice parameters increased by 2.52 Å with respect to that of the undoped system. The cell volume is expanded by 23%, while the increase is only 1.5% in the case of codoping at the lattice sites. A comparison of the formation energy for codoping of N and F at the substitutional sites and interstitial sites (Figure 2b) indicates that substitutional doping is energetically more feasible than the interstitial doping. The defect formation energy at the interstitial site is expected to increase from (N, F) to (N, I) codoping because of the increase in size of the dopant elements. Hence, in the present study, only the substitutional doping is considered for electronic property calculations.

For example, $KTaO_3$ has been experimentally found to be an overall water splitting material because of its appropriate band edge alignment with respect to water redox levels. To check whether this property is still operative in the (N, X)-codoped $KTaO_3$, their band edge positions are aligned. For this purpose, the relative shift of the VBM and CBM of the codoped systems with respect to that of undoped $KTaO_3$ have been determined, for which the absolute positions of the band edge are taken from the experimental database. $^{66}$ As can be seen from Figure 12, the CBM and VBM positions of all the codoped systems are

4. CONCLUSIONS

In this hybrid functional-based DFT study, our aim is to find suitable dopant elements for the controlled band gap narrowing of $KTaO_3$. Among the several dopant elements studied here, both individually and in combination, the (N, F) pair has been found to be the most effective for improving the photocatalytic activity of $KTaO_3$ under visible light. According to the formation energy calculation, oxygen-poor conditions are found to be more favorable for the synthesis of the doped materials, and under these conditions, the formation energy for the codoped $KTaO_3$ is even lower than that of the monodoped system. Although monodoping with N reduces the band gap by a large extent, localized defect states created in the forbidden region may promote the electron−hole recombination rate. On the other hand, monodoping with F does not bring any noticeable change in the effective band gap, while others do so by a small extent. Combination of N and a halogen pair is found to be advantageous because it reduces the band gap without encountering any localized defect states. The enhancement of visible light activity has been manifested in the optical spectrum. The band gap for the codoped systems is found to decrease in the order (N, I) > (N, Br) > (N, Cl) > (N, F). The present study reveals that the variation of the calculated band gap is attributed to the combined effect of electronegativity difference, nature of the valence shell p-orbital, and distortion in crystal structure due to lattice mismatch. More importantly, the narrowing of the band gap in the present cases mostly involves elevation of the VBM; therefore, all the codoped systems are found to satisfy the thermodynamic criteria for overall splitting of water. Because the codopant pair form charge compensated the system, unwanted vacancy formation will be greatly minimized. In addition, they also counterbalance the adverse effect of each other. Among the four codoped systems studied here, (N, F)-codoped $KTaO_3$ is most preferred because of maximum band gap lowering and the unique advantages of F for accelerating photocatalytic water splitting activity over the other halogen elements.
