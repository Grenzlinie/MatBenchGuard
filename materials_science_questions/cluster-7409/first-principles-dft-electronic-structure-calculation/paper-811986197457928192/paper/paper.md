# First principle prediction of vacancy-induced magnetism in non-magnetic perovskite $SrTiO_{3}$

I.R. Shein, A.L. Ivanovskii *

Institute of Solid State Chemistry, Ural Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, 620041 Ekaterinburg, Russia

Received 30 May 2007; accepted 5 June 2007
Available online 8 June 2007
Communicated by V.M. Agranovich

## Abstract
A theoretical study of magnetization in non-magnetic cubic perovskite $SrTiO_{3}$ induced by vacancies in $Sr$, Ti and oxygen sublattices is presented using the full-potential linear augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) for the exchange- correlation potential. The results show that the oxygen and titanium vacancies lead to the magnetization of non-magnetic perovskite $SrTiO_{3}$ due to the spin-polarization of valence states for the Ti and O atoms, nearest to these vacancies. In result the insulating $SrTiO_{3}$ becomes metallic-like with magnetic moments (per cell) $1.30 \mu_{B}$ and $3.54 \mu_{B}$ for non-stoichiometric $SrTiO_{3-\delta}$ and $SrTi_{1-\delta} O$ , respectively. On the contrary, our calculations reveal that for $Sr$ vacancy the non-magnetic state of perovskite is retained, whereas the electronic spectrum of $Sr_{1-\delta} TiO_{3}$ also adopts metallic-like type.

## 1. Introduction
Diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMSs) have recently at- tracted increasing attentions because of their potential applica- tions in spintronics. Nowadays, a general way to getting of new DMRs consists in the doping of the chosen matrices by mag- netic ions [1,2].

Very recently the discovery of a new group of non-traditional magnetic materials based on non-magnetic compounds with- out magnetic ions was reported. For these materials, namely for non-magnetic insulating ordinary oxides (such as MgO [3,4], $CaO, SrO, BaO$ [4], $BeO$ [5], $SiO_{2}$ [6]) and some others, see[4,7,8], the origin of magnetism is caused by the presence of non-magnetic $s p$ impurities-for example, boron, carbon or nitrogen. The magnetization arises due to a spontaneous spin- polarization of impurity states located in the band gap of in- sulating matrix, and these impurity atoms acquire significant magnetic moments [2-8].

Other interesting opportunity of magnetization of non- magnetic materials arises at their "doping" by lattice va- cancies, i.e., in the non-stoichiometric region of these sys- tems [9].

In this Letter, we have focused on the possibility of the mag- netization in non-magnetic perovskite $SrTiO_{3}$ induced by the lattice vacancies. Our study is motivated by the following rea- sons:

(i) Up to now the influence of the lattice vacancies on the magnetization of the non-magnetic systems was stud- ied exclusively for binary phases, such as $CaO$ [10,11]; $ZnO$ [12], $HfO_{2}, ZrO_{2}$ [9], $CaB_{6}$ [13].
(ii) The magnetization of non-stoichiometric materials has been discussed, as a rule, only for selected type of lattice defects: cationic or anionic vacancies [9-13].
(iii) Cubic strontium titanate, $SrTiO_{3}$ is the generic represen tative of a broad family of perovskite-like compounds, which belongs to the most promising materials owing to their unusual magnetic, electronic and transport properties, review [14].

Thus, here we present for the first time the comparative study of the effect of *all possible vacancies* (in Sr, Ti and oxygen sublattices) on the electronic and magnetic properties of *ternary non-magnetic phase*—cubic perovskite SrTiO₃. Our main finding is that by creating of vacancies in various sublattices, quite different magnetic effects may be achieved. Namely, in the presence of oxygen or titanium vacancies, the titanium or oxygen atoms acquire the localized magnetic moments, whereas the vacancies at the Sr site retain the initial non-magnetic state of the matrix. In the presence of all mentioned lattice vacancies the insulating SrTiO₃ becomes metallic-like.

## 2. Models and method
The considered SrTiO₃ perovskite adopt the cubic structure (s.g. *Pm3m*) where atomic positions in the elementary cell are Ti: $1a$ (0, 0, 0); O: $3d$ (0, 0, 1/2); and Sr: $1b$ (1/2, 1/2, 1/2). All calculations were performed for 40-atomic supercells, for which the removing of the single oxygen, titanium or Sr ions allows to simulate the non-stoichiometric materials with formal compositions SrTiO₂.₈₇₅, SrTi₀.₈₇₅O₃ and Sr₀.₈₇₅TiO₃, respectively.

Our calculations have been performed using the FP-LAPW method as implemented in the WIEN2k package [15]. The electronic configurations are taken $[Ar]3d^24s^2$ for Ti, $[Kr]5s^2$ for Sr and $[He]2s^22p^4$ for O. Here, the noble gas cores are distinguished from the sub-shells of valence electrons. The basis set inside each MT sphere is split into core and valence subsets. The core states are treated within the spherical part of the potential only, and are assumed to have a spherically symmetric charge density confined within MT spheres. The valence part is treated with the potential expanded into spherical harmonics up to $l=4$. The valence wave functions inside the spheres are expanded up to $l=12$. The plane-wave expansion with $R_{MT} \times K_{MAX}$ equal to 7, and $k$ sampling with 125 $k$-points mesh in the full Brillouin zone is used. All calculations are carried out for optimized lattice constants and atomic positions. We use the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [16] for the exchange correlation potentials. Relativistic effects are taken into account within the scalar-relativistic approximation. Due to the periodic boundary condition, our calculations are done in a ferromagnetic ordering configuration.

The self-consistent calculations were considered to be converged when the difference in the total energy of the crystal did not exceed 0.01 mRy as calculated at consecutive steps. We have adopted the muffin-tin radii of 1.72 a.u. for O, 1.8 a.u. for Ti, and 2.2 a.u. for Sr. The density of states (DOS) was obtained using a modified tetrahedron method [17].

## 3. Results and discussion
### 3.1. Electronic properties of non-magnetic perovskite SrTiO₃
As the first step, the ground state properties and band structure of the ideal non-magnetic perovskite SrTiO₃ were calculated. The main results in comparison with some available experimental and theoretical data are summarized in Table 1. One can see that the lattice constant $a_0$ obtained in our FLAPW-GGA calculations is very close to the experimental data; the deviation achieve only at about 1%. The others important ground state parameter—bulk modulus $B$—also agree well with earlier data.

The band structure, total and $l$-decomposed densities of states (DOSs) for the equilibrium geometry of SrTiO₃ are shown in Fig. 1. We will shortly comment the general features in the band structure for SrTiO₃. The valence band (VB) with the width of about 4.6 eV is derived mainly from O $2p$ orbitals with some admixture of Ti and Sr states, and it contains 18 electrons. The bottom of the conduction band (CB) is composed mainly of Ti $3d$ t₂g and e_g states, and at higher energies the Sr $4d$ like bands are placed. The valence-band top of SrTiO₃ is composed from O $2p$ orbitals and is located at the R point, while the VB maximum at the M point is about 0.09 eV lower than the R.

The nine occupied bands at the $\Gamma$ point consist of three triply degenerate levels ($\Gamma_{15}$, $\Gamma_{25}$ and $\Gamma_{15}$) with energies of $-2.82$, $-1.17$ and $-0.39$ eV below $E_F$, respectively. The splitting of the levels ($\Gamma_{15}$–$\Gamma_{25}$) and ($\Gamma_{25}$–$\Gamma_{15}$) is due to the crystal field effects and electrostatic interaction between O $2p$ orbitals. The triply ($\Gamma_{25}$, $+1.81$ eV) and doubly ($\Gamma_{12}$, $+4.12$ eV) degenerate levels in the conduction band originate from t₂g ($\pi^*$) and e_g ($\sigma^*$) bands of titanium, and they are separated with the energy gap of 2.31 eV.

The valence DOS (Fig. 1) originates predominantly from O $2p$ states; in this energy region the occupied Sr states are practically absent, i.e., strontium atoms in SrTiO₃ lattice are presented as Sr²⁺ cations. The admixture of Ti $3d$ contributions to O $2p$ bands is due to the $p$–$d$ hybridization occur, which indicates the presence of a covalent Ti–O bonding. The conduction-band bottom is at the $\Gamma$ point. The CB minimum at X is by 0.11 eV higher than at the $\Gamma$ point. Thus the indirect gap between R and $\Gamma$ points is 1.81 eV, and the direct gap at $\Gamma$ is 2.16 eV.

These features of the band structure obtained from our calculations agree well with the previous LDA calculations, see Table 1. At the same time the experimentally determined indirect band gap for SrTiO₃ achieves about 3.30 eV, while the direct band gap (BG) energy is equal to 3.75 eV [23–25]. Starting from these results, let us discuss the influence of the Sr, Ti and oxygen lattice vacancies on the electronic and magnetic properties of insulating perovskite SrTiO₃.

### 3.2. Electronic and magnetic properties of non-stoichiometric perovskites SrTiO₃

For the oxygen-deficient SrTiO₂.₈₇₅, the oxygen vacancies behave as an n-type dopants, as result the Fermi level is in the bottom of the conduction band and the DOS at the Fermi level is non-zero; hence the non-stoichiometric SrTiO₂.₈₇₅ is metallic, see also [26,27]. The most interesting result is that the spin splitting appears for reduced perovskite SrTiO₂.₈₇₅, when the DOS of the majority spin and the DOS of the minority spin are significantly modified around the Fermi level, as shown in Fig. 2. In more details, directly below and above $E_{\text{F}}$, the occupied and empty Ti $3d$ ($\text{t}_{2\text{g}}$) states are located, which split into two spin-resolved bands Ti $3d$ ($\text{t}_{2\text{g}}$) $\downarrow$ $\div$Ti $3d$ ($\text{t}_{2\text{g}}$) $\uparrow$, and each Ti atom (around the oxygen vacancy site) carries a localized magnetic moment of about 0.2 $\mu_{\text{B}}$. On the contrary, for oxygen and Sr states the DOS of the majority spin and the DOS of the minority spin are symmetric and there is no resultant spin polarization.

In order to check the stability of magnetic state for this system, we have calculated the total energies ($E_{\text{tot}}$) for both the spin-polarized and the non-spin-polarized states. We have found that magnetic state of SrTiO₂.₈₇₅ is more preferable (at about 0.1 eV/cell) versus non-magnetic.

For the titanium-deficient SrTiO₀.₈₇₅O₃, the anionic vacancies behave as a $p$-type dopants, as result the Fermi level is moved into the top of the valence band, Fig. 2, hence the non-stoichiometric SrTiO₀.₈₇₅ is metallic. Additionally, the partially occupied O $2p$ band adopts the spin splitting. The magnetic moments on the O atoms nearest to the Ti vacancy are about 0.36 $\mu_{\text{B}}$, whereas no noticeable magnetic moments are found for the other atoms located on the larger distances from vacancy. Note that this situation resembles the reported cases for Ca vacancy in CaO [10,11], Hf vacancy in HfO₂ [9], where it has been shown that cationic vacancies in ordinary oxides create a non-zero local magnetic moments.

Finally, though the strontium vacancy also behaves as a $p$-type dopant, and the Sr-deficient Sr₀.₈₇₅TiO₃ becomes metallic-like, our calculations show that no splitting between majority and minority spin states occurs and this non-stoichiometric perovskite remains non-magnetic.

Thus, our study shows that the presence of all possible lattice vacancies leads to a metallic type of spectrum for non-stoichiometric compositions of the perovskite. On the other hand, the influence of these vacancies on the magnetic state of SrTiO₃ is quite different. So, for Sr vacancy the non-magnetic state of perovskite is retained, whereas for oxygen and titanium vacancies the corresponding compounds adopt the magnetic states due to the spin-polarization of Ti $3d$ ($\text{t}_{2\text{g}}$) and O $2p$ states for the Ti and O atoms, nearest to these vacancies. As seen from the Table 2, the single Ti vacancy creates a maximal magnetization (3.54 $\mu_{\text{B}}$ per 40-atomic cell) in comparison to the oxygen vacancy (1.30 $\mu_{\text{B}}$).

To clarify the possibility of observation of the predicted magnetization effects, it is necessary to estimate the energies of formation ($E_{\text{form}}$) for various vacancies into SrTiO₃. Note, that our estimation of $E_{\mathrm{vf}}$ is in a reasonable agreement with other available data [27–30] for neutral state of O-vacancy ranging from 2.4 eV [28] to 6.7 eV [29] and up to 9.4 eV [26].

## 4. Conclusions

To conclude, the influence of Sr, Ti and oxygen vacancies on the electronic and magnetic properties of cubic perovskite $\mathrm{SrTiO}_{3}$ has been studied systematically using first-principles FLAPW-GGA method. Our calculations show that

(i) the presence of mentioned lattice vacancies leads to a metallic type of spectrum for all non-stoichiometric perovskites;
(ii) a Ti vacancy introduce spin polarization of oxygen atoms;
(iii) an oxygen vacancy introduce spin polarization of Ti atoms around the defect site;
(iv) for Sr vacancy the non-magnetic state of perovskite is retained.

Our results indicate that though maximal magnetization occurs in Ti-deficient perovskite; the highest possibility of observation of magnetic effect may be really expected for reduced $\mathrm{SrTiO}_{3-\delta}$ phase with oxygen vacancies. We believe that the detection of this effect is most probable for nano-sized $\mathrm{SrTiO}_{3}$ which resulting from multiple oxygen vacancies at the surfaces of these nanoparticles—the effect which was observed for nanoparticles of many ordinary oxides [31].

Note, that the influence of oxygen vacancy clustering [32] on the magnetism for this perovskite, as well as the conditions of promoting of collective magnetism should become a subject of separate studies.
