# First-principles DFT study of orientational defects: formation energies, annealing barriers, and electronic properties

## Problem background
Molecular crystals built from asymmetric molecules can host orientational point defects—individual molecules that are rotated into a different orientation within the otherwise ordered lattice. In energetic materials, such misaligned molecules may locally lower the barrier for bond breaking and create hot spots that influence sensitivity. Recent work using classical empirical force fields predicted four stable orientational defects in an energetic molecular crystal and estimated their formation energies and annealing barriers, but direct quantum mechanical confirmation is missing. This task reproduces a first‑principles density functional theory (DFT) study that re‑examines those defects, computes their formation energies and minimum‑energy paths, and assesses how they modify the electronic band gap and the electron density at the weakest N–N bonds.

## Approach
We use plane‑wave DFT with the PBE exchange‑correlation functional and the DFT‑D2 dispersion correction to relax the perfect crystal and the four defect structures. Formation energies are obtained from the total‑energy difference between the relaxed defect cell and the relaxed perfect crystal. The minimum‑energy path from the defect back to the perfect crystal is found with the climbing‑image nudged elastic band (CI‑NEB) method using 16 intermediate images. Initial NEB paths are generated by molecular dynamics simulations with the Smith‑Bharadwaj (SB) empirical potential, in which the rotated molecule is allowed to return to the perfect configuration. From the final NEB energy profiles we extract the annealing barrier for each defect. Electronic structure is assessed by computing the density of states with the hybrid B3LYP functional, from which the HOMO‑LUMO gap is determined, and by extracting the electron charge density along the most charge‑deficient N–N bond to find the minimum density value.

## Reproduction target
Compute the following for each of the four orientational defects (A, B, C, D):
- formation energy E_F (eV) from the relaxed perfect and defect cells,
- annealing barrier E_A (eV) from the CI‑NEB energy profile,
- electronic band gap (eV) for the perfect crystal and for each defect, obtained from the DOS computed with the B3LYP hybrid functional,
- minimum electron charge density along the most deficient N−N bond (e/Bohr³).
All numerical results must be written to the CSV files listed in the workflow steps.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Build perfect crystal model
- Role: process
- Action: Obtain the crystal structure of the molecular crystal (orthorhombic, 168 atoms per unit cell) from the Crystallography Open Database (COD entry 1500001) and prepare the unit cell for DFT calculations.
- Evidence: none

### Step 2: Relax perfect crystal
- Role: process
- Action: Perform DFT geometry relaxation of the perfect crystal unit cell using the PBE functional with DFT-D2 dispersion correction to obtain the relaxed structure and total energy.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/perfect_relaxed.xyz`

### Step 3: Construct defect structures
- Role: process
- Action: From the Supporting Information of the study, obtain the atomic coordinates for the four orientational defects (A, B, C, D) and create the corresponding unit cells.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/defect_cells.xyz`

### Step 4: Relax defect structures
- Role: process
- Action: Relax each defect unit cell using the same DFT settings (PBE-D2) to obtain stable geometries and total energies.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/defect_relaxed.xyz`

### Step 5: Compute defect formation energies
- Role: scored
- Action: Calculate the formation energy E_F for each defect as the total energy difference between the relaxed defect cell and the relaxed perfect crystal cell. Output a CSV file with defect identifiers and E_F in eV.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/formation_energies.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: defect_id (str, one of A,B,C,D), E_F_eV (float)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 6: Generate initial NEB paths via MD with SB potential
- Role: process
- Action: Using molecular dynamics with the Smith-Bharadwaj (SB) empirical potential, run short simulations for each defect where the rotated molecule returns to the perfect crystal state; sample the trajectories to produce 16 intermediate replicas that bridge the defect and perfect crystal.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/initial_replicas.pkl`

### Step 7: Run DFT NEB and extract energy profiles
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: For each defect, perform a climbing-image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) calculation with 16 replicas starting from the MD-derived initial paths. After convergence, record the final energy of every replica and output a CSV.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/neb_profiles.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: defect_id (str, A|B|C|D), replica_index (int, 0 to 16), energy_eV (float)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 8: Compute electronic density of states
- Role: scored
- Action: Using the hybrid B3LYP functional and the relaxed structures, compute the electronic density of states (DOS) for the perfect crystal and for each defect. Output a CSV with energy and DOS values.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/electronic_DOS.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: defect_id (str, A|B|C|D|perfect), energy_eV (float), dos_arbunits (float)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 9: Compute N–N bond charge density
- Role: scored
- Action: For each defect, extract the electron charge density along the most charge-deficient N–NO₂ bond (axial N–N bond) and output a CSV with distance along the bond and charge density.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/charge_density_profiles.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: defect_id (str, A|B|C|D), distance_along_NN_bond_Angstrom (float), charge_density_e_Bohr3 (float)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

## How you are scored
A hidden verifier independently scores each of your output CSV files. For formation_energies.csv, it compares your reported E_F values to the published reference values within an appropriate tolerance. For neb_profiles.csv, it recomputes the annealing barrier for each defect from your energy vs. replica‑index data and compares the barriers to the hidden reference. For electronic_DOS.csv, it extracts the band gap and compares it to the reference. For charge_density_profiles.csv, it finds the minimum charge density along the bond and compares it to the reference. Each artifact contributes a portion of the total reward, with the main scored artifacts weighted most heavily. Small differences that arise from using a different DFT code or numerical settings are tolerated as long as the results are physically consistent with the original study.
