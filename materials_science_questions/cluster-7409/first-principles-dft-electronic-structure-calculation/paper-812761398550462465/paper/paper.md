# First-principles study of Hf/Nb/Zr-doped MAX phases $Ti_3AlC_2$ and $Ti_3SiC_2$

Jinlan Nie¹*, Sishuo Liu¹, Xiaofei Zhan¹, Lei Ao¹, Li Li¹

ⁿSchool of Physics, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, Chengdu 610054, China

E-mail: jinlannie@uestc.edu.cn

## Abstract

The effects of doping Hf/Nb/Zr on the structural, electronic, magnetic, mechanical, and thermal properties of $Ti_3AlC_2$ and $Ti_3SiC_2$ are investigated based on the density functional theory (DFT). Our calculations show that magnetism can be induced in $Ti_3AlC_2$ by doping Hf/Zr at c-ATi2 site due to the electron transfer. Doping Hf/Nb/Zr at the Ti1 site has minimal effect on the intrinsic mechanical properties of $Ti_3AlC_2$ and $Ti_3SiC_2$, in according with the previous experiment. Whereas doping Hf/Nb/Zr at the interstitial site has adverse effects on the host material, resulting in significantly decreased bulk, shear, Young's moduli and Debye temperature.

**Keywords:** MAX phases, Doping, Magnetism, Mechanical and thermal properties

### 1. Introduction

The $M_{n+1}AX_n$ phases (n=1, 2, and 3) are group of ternary ceramic materials, where M is an early transition metal, A is an element from IIIA or IVA group, and X is either C or N, or both.¹ Identified more than 50 closely related compounds, the MAX phases have attracted a great attention due to their unique properties combined both metals and ceramics, e.g., easy machinability, low density, good electrical and thermal conductivity, excellent thermal shock as well as high-temperature oxidation resistance.²⁴ $Ti_3AlC_2$ (TAC) and $Ti_3SiC_2$ (TSC) are two classic 312 group MAX phases. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the crystal structure can be described as two edge-shared Ti6C octahedron layers linked together by a two-dimensional close-packed A (A=Al or Si) plane, where two different Ti atoms occupy $2a$ (denoted as Ti1) and $4f$ sites (denoted as Ti2), A and C atoms are located at $2b$ and $4f$ Wyckoff positions, respectively.⁵,⁶ With demonstrated high tolerance to amorphization under ion, neutron or helium irradiation, these two materials were proposed to be promising candidate fuel coating or cladding materials in the next Gas-cooled Faster Reactor (GFR).⁷ However, such materials face the problems such as the low Vickers hardness, low wear resistance and poor high-temperature mechanical properties which limit their applications.⁸

Substitution of M sites by other transition metal atoms was proved to be an efficient approach to tune the mechanical properties of MAX phases. *Gu et al.* prepared the full dense and phase pure $(Nb_{1-x}Ti_x)_4AlC_3$ (x=0~0.3) ceramics and revealed that the solid solution of Ti could significantly improve the mechanical properties of $Nb_4AlC_3$ at both room and high temperature.⁹ The improved mechanical properties of Ti-doped $Nb_4AlC_3$ was later verified by *Jiao et al.* based on the first-principles calculation.¹⁰

Recently, motivated by the fact that Zr-Al(Si)-C and Hf-Al-C ceramics and Nb-containing MAX phases exhibit well mechanical properties, $^{11-14}$ Wan *et al.* studied the mechanical properties of M (M=Hf, Nb, or Zr)-doped $Ti_3(SiAl)C_2$. They demonstrated that Hf/Nb/Zr doping significantly improved mechanical properties of $Ti_3(SiAl)C_2$ at high temperature, but has minimal effect on $Ti_3(SiAl)C_2$ at room temperature. $^{15}$ However, except their experimental study, there is no theoretical work focused on Hf/Nb/Zr-doped $Ti_3AlC_2$ or $Ti_3SiC_2$ up to now.

In this work, first-principles calculations based on density functional theory are performed to study the effect of Hf/Nb/Zr doping on the structural, electronic, magnetic, as well as mechanical and thermal properties of TAC and TSC. Our main aims are: (1) to investigate how mechanical and thermal properties can be influenced upon doping, and (2) to study whether doping can modify the electronic and magnetic properties of TAC and TSC nor not. The presented results are predicted to shed some light on the application of MAX phases ceramic materials.

### 2. Methods and details
The calculations are performed within the spin-polarized DFT framework, utilizing the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP). $^{16}$ The interaction between valence electrons and the ionic cores is expanded by the projector augmented wave (PAW) $^{17}$ and the exchange-correlation energy is described by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-Burk-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional $^{18}$. The kinetic energy cut-off for the basis-set is set to be 600 eV. A 2×2×1 supercell containing 48 atoms is employed, with one Hf/Nb/Zr atom doping at Ti1 site or occupying the interstitial site. For the first irreducible Brillouin zone (BZ) integration, the Gamma scheme is used with $k$-point mesh of 6×6×2 for all structures. All the

atom positions and cell shapes are allowed to relax until the Hellmann-Feynman forces are less than $1.0{\times}10^{-3}$ eV/Å. The valence electronic configurations are C $2s^22p^2$, Al $3s^23p^1$, Si $3s^23p^2$, Ti $3d^34s^1$, Hf $5d^36s^1$, Nb $4s^24p^64d^45s^1$, and Zr $4d^35s^1$.

The formation energy $(E_{\mathrm{f}})$ is calculated from the equation$^{19}$

$$
E_{\mathrm{f}}=E_{\text{defect}}-E_{\text{perfect}}+n_{i}\mu_{i} \tag{2-1}
$$

where $E_{\text{defect}}$ and $E_{\text{perfect}}$ are the total energies of a defective supercell and a perfect supercell, respectively. $n_{i}$ is the atom number of element $i$ ($i$=Ti, Hf, Nb, or Zr) added or removed from the host structure, and $\mu_{i}$ is the chemical potential of element $i$. The formation energies have variable values as a function of chemical potential $\mu_{i}$. However, to compare the relative stability of Hf, Nb, and Zr-doped systems, we only calculate the formation energy when elemental chemical potential is equal to that in the reference phase. The bulk hcp Ti, hcp Hf, cubic Nb and hcp Zr metal are adopted as reference phase of Ti, Hf, Nb and Zr element, respectively.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Geometric properties and stability

In order to check the validity of the pseudopotentials and all the parameters employed in the present calculation, the lattice parameters of the pristine TAC and TSC are calculated, which are found in good agreement with the available experimental data$^{20-22}$, as shown in Table 1 and 2. For Hf/Nb/Zr-doped TAC and TSC, two kinds of configurations are considered in the study, in which Hf/Nb/Zr atoms are substituted for the Ti1 site ($\mathrm{Hf_{Ti1}/Nb_{Ti1}/Zr_{Ti1}}$) or doped at the interstitial sites ($\mathrm{Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i}$). For $\mathrm{Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i}$ doping, only the most stable configuration is considered, which is determined by comparing the total energies for dopants at possible interstitial sites in

the TAC/TSC lattice. Initial interstitial configurations considered in this work follow
the spirit of Wang et al. $^{23}$ as presented in Fig. 1. The results outlined in Table 3 show
that Hf/Nb/Zr atoms have various stable interstitial sites in the host lattice, among
which the c-ATi2 site is determined to be the most favorable in both TAC and TSC,
indicated by the lowest total energies. These results are reasonable, since the c-ATi2
site has the largest open space to accommodate impurity atoms compared with the
others. As shown in Table 1 and 2, Hf/Nb/Zr doping, irrespective of the doping
position, has minimal effect on the lattice symmetry, and induces slightly increased
lattice constants of $a_0$, $b_0$, and $c_0$. Compared with ${\rm Hf_{Til}/Nb_{Til}/Zr_{Til}}$-doped phases,
increase of the lattice parameter is more significant in ${\rm Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i}$-doped TAC and
TSC. Formation energy calculation indicate that, in both TAC and TSC, ${\rm Hf_{Til}}$ is the
most easily formed, followed by ${\rm Nb_{Til}}$ and ${\rm Zr_{Til}}$, whereas ${\rm Nb_i}$ is the most favorable
interstitial impurity, as shown in Table 4.

### 3.2. Electronic and magnetic properties

Before investigating the doping effect on the electronic and magnetic properties of the
host materials, it is necessary to have some knowledge of the electronic properties of
the pristine TAC and TSC. Density of states (DOS) in Fig. 2 and 3 show that both
TAC and TSC have finite electron states at Fermi levels, indicating their metallic
properties. Partial density of states (PDOS) indicate that C 2$s$ states dominate the
lowest lying valance band at the energy range from -12 to -9 eV. At higher energy
between -9 and -6 eV, there are finite states from Si 3$s$ orbital in TSC, while there is a

little gap in TAC due to the higher lying of Al 3s states hybridized with Al 3p states.

For the energy window from -6 to -2 eV, there is a strong hybridization of Ti 3d and C 2p states. Valance bands from -2 eV to Fermi level originate from states of Ti 3d interacted with A 3p. The nearly free-electron Ti 3d states dominate the DOS at around the Fermi level. Conduction bands above the Fermi level are mainly composed of Ti 3d states with less contribution from A 3p states. Doping Hf/Nb/Zr at the Ti1 site has minimal effect on the electronic structure of TAC and TSC. As shown in Fig. 2, Hf/Nb/Zr exhibits a similar PDOS's profile with that of Ti at the whole energy range. Thus, as revealed by the PDOS in the vicinity of the Fermi level, these doped phases exhibit metallic behavior which is due to the conduction of $d$ electrons from both Ti and Hf/Nb/Zr. However, different from $Hf_{Ti1}/Nb_{Ti1}/Zr_{Ti1}$ doping, doping Hf/Nb/Zr at the c-ATi2 site has significant influences on the DOS's profile of TAC and TSC. As shown in Fig. 3, $Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i$ doping induces a sharp peak which originates mainly from the dopant's $d$ orbital just below the Fermi level. Besides, due to the hybridization with impurity states, a part of A 3s states move to the lower energy. Compared with TAC, the interaction between Hf/Nb/Zr and the nearby A atoms is more significant in TSC, where a sharp peak contributed by significant Si 3s states appears at the energy range from -12 to -9 eV as shown in Fig. 3. In addition, it is interesting to find that doping Hf and Zr at the c-ATi2 site can induce a magnetic moment of 1.35 and $1.00\ \mu_B$ in TAC, respectively, as indicated in Table 2. Bader analysis indicate that the magnetic moment is mainly distributed on the Hf/Zr atom $(0.64/0.47\ \mu_B)$ and first-neighboring Ti2 atoms $(0.72/0.51\ \mu_B)$, as revealed by the spin

density distribution shown in Fig. 4. The PDOS of Hf/Zr imply that magnetic states on dopants originate from the corresponding $d$ orbitals. The different magnetic behaviors of $Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i$-doped TAC and TSC are investigated by analyzing the electron transfer among Hf/Nb/Zr, the first- and second-neighboring A (A=Al or Si) and Ti2 atoms. Results summarized in Table 5 indicate that electrons are transferred from Al[1], Al[2] and Ti2[2] to Hf/Zr and Ti2 [1] in TAC, but transferred from Hf/Zr, Si[2] and Ti[2] to Si[1] and Ti2[1] in TSC. Consequently, the Hf/Zr-Ti2[1] unit totally obtains 0.94/0.76 |e| and loses 0.02/0.08 |e| in TAC and TSC, respectively, which accounts for the different magnetic behavior between $Hf_i/Zr_i$-doped TAC and TSC. For $Nb_i$-doped cases, similar electron transfer process results in a net electron gain of 1.69 |e| and 0.34 |e| at the Nb-Ti2[1] site in TAC and TSC, respectively. The excess and insufficient transferred electron may result in the nonmagnetic properties of $Nb_i$-doped TAC and TSC, respectively.

### 3.3. Elastic and Mechanical properties
From the complete set of elastic constants $C_{ij}$, macroscopic mechanical parameters of materials, *i.e.* the bulk modulus (B) and shear modulus (G) can be obtained based on the Voigt-Reuss-Hill (VRH) approximation²⁴⁻²⁶. For hexagonal lattice, these two parameters are defined as

$$
B = \frac{B_V + B_R}{2}, \tag{3-1}
$$

$$
G = \frac{G_V + G_R}{2}, \tag{3-2}
$$

in which

$$
B_{V}=\frac{1}{9}\left(C_{11}+C_{12}\right)+4 C_{13}+C_{33}, \tag{3-3}
$$

$$
B_{R}=C^{2} / M, \tag{3-4}
$$

$$
G_{V}=(1 / 30)\left(M+12 C_{44}+12 C_{66}\right), \tag{3-5}
$$

$$
G_{R}=(5 / 2)\left[\frac{1}{G} C^{2} C_{44} C_{66}+\frac{1}{G} B_{V} C_{44} C_{66}+C^{2}\left(C_{44}+C_{66}\right)\right], \tag{3-6}
$$

and

$$
C^{2}=\left(C_{11}+C_{12}\right) C_{33}-2 C_{13}^{2}, \tag{3-7}
$$

$$
M=C_{11}+C_{12}+2 C_{33}-4 C_{13}, \tag{3-8}
$$

$$
C_{66}=\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right) / 2, \tag{3-9}
$$

The Young's modulus $E$ is calculated from the values of $B$ and $G$ by

$$
E=\frac{9 B G}{3 B+G}, \tag{3-10}
$$

and the Poisson's ratio $\sigma$ is determined from the values of $E$ and $G$ by

$$
\sigma=\frac{E}{2 G}-1. \tag{3-11}
$$

The calculated elastic constants are shown in Table 6 and 7. The obtained bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, Young's modulus $E$, Poisson's ratio $\sigma$ and Push ratios $G/B$ for all phases are summarized in Table 8 and 9. For the pristine TAC/TSC, our calculated values of $B$ (163.0/191.2 GPa), $G$ (125.8/139.6 GPa), and $E$ (300.2/336.9 GPa) are all in good agreement with other theoretical and experimental results⁴, ²⁷.

The calculated Push ratios $G/B$ of TAC and TSC are calculated to be 0.7 and 0.8,

respectively, both of which are much larger than those of some typical metals, such as cubic close-packed (ccp) metals (0.43~0.56), but smaller than some typical ceramic crystals, such as BN (1.00) and MgO (0.82)²⁷. This implies that MAX phases of TAC and TSC are generally brittle and behave somewhere between the ceramic and metal.

Hf/Nb/Zr doping has distinct effects on the mechanical properties of TAC and TSC considering about different doping positions. It can be found in Table 6 and 8 that the calculated elastic constants and moduli of Hfᵀᵢ₁/Nbᵀᵢ₁/Zrᵀᵢ₁-doped TAC and TSC are comparable with those of the pristine TAC and TSC, indicating that Hfᵀᵢ₁/Nbᵀᵢ₁/Zrᵀᵢ₁ doping has minimal effect on the intrinsic mechanical properties of TAC and TSC.

These results are in accordance with the experiment, where Wan *et al.* found that Hf/Nb/Zr-doped Ti₃(SiAl)C₂ solution have comparable stiffness, hardness, strength, and fracture toughness with those of Ti₃(SiAl)C₂ at room-temperature¹⁵. Doping Hf/Nb/Zr at the interstitial site, however, does induce some adverse effects, in which the bulk $B$, shear $G$, and Young's $E$ moduli of TAC and TSC are significantly decreased upon doping. Among the three moduli, decreases of the shear $G$ and Young's $E$ moduli are more significant than that of the bulk $E$ modulus as indicated in Table 9. The weakened shear $G$ and Young's $E$ moduli are strongly related with significantly decreased elastic constants $C_{33}$ and $C_{44}$ as outlined in Table 7. The distinct effects of Hfᵀᵢ₁/Nbᵀᵢ₁/Zrᵀᵢ₁ and Hfᵢ/Nbᵢ/Zrᵢ doping on the mechanical properties of TAC and TSC are investigated by analyzing the charge density distribution. As shown in Fig. 5(a), TAC and TSC have strongly directional <Ti2-C-Ti1-C-Ti2> covalent bond chains linked together by the weakly bonded A

(A=Al or Si) atoms, forming layered structures. Therefore, the moduli are mainly due to the strong Ti-C bonding, while the easy basal plane slip is attributed to the weak bonding between Ti2 and A. As shown in Fig. 5(a), $\mathrm{Hf_{Ti1}/Nb_{Ti1}/Zr_{Ti1}}$-doped phases preserve the original bonding characters of the pristine structures. Bader analysis indicate that, in both TAC and TSC, Hf/Nb/Zr is more positive charged compared with Ti1, implying that the <Hf/Nb/Zr-C> bonding strength may be stronger than that of <Ti1-C>. However, interactions between Hf/Nb/Zr and C is balanced by each slightly larger bond distance as shown in Table 1. As a result, $\mathrm{Hf_{Ti1}/Nb_{Ti1}/Zr_{Ti1}}$-doped phases exhibit comparable mechanical properties with those of TAC and TSC.

However, different from the case of $\mathrm{Hf_{Ti1}/Nb_{Ti1}/Zr_{Ti1}}$ doping, doping Hf/Nb/Zr at the interstitial site of TAC and TSC changes the intrinsic bonding manner between the $\mathrm{Ti_3C_2}$ block and A layer, forming a new bond chain with <Ti2-A-Hf/Nb/Zr-C> characters instead of the original <Ti2-A-Ti2> bond, as shown in Fig. 5(b). Besides, due to the electron transfer process as discussed in section 3.2, charges are redistributed among $\mathrm{Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i}$ and the neighboring A, C, and Ti atoms as indicated in Fig. 5(b). Therefore, the bond strength between the metal carbide and the atomic A layer is expected to weaken, resulting in significantly decreased elastic constants $C_{33}$ and $C_{44}$ and the shear and Young's moduli.

### 3.4. Thermal properties

With the calculated bulk modulus $B$ and shear modulus $G$, the longitudinal $(v_l)$ and transverse $(v_t)$ sound wave velocity can be derived from$^{28}$

$$
v_{t}=\sqrt{G / \rho}, \tag{3-12}
$$

$$
v_{l}=\sqrt{B+\frac{4}{3}G / \rho}, \tag{3-13}
$$

where $\rho$ is the density. The average sound wave velocity $(v_{m})$ can be then estimated from the longitudinal $(v_{l})$ and transverse $(v_{t})$ sound wave velocity by

$$
v_{m}=\left(\frac{2}{v_{t}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{l}^{3}}\right)^{-1/3}. \tag{3-14}
$$

Debye temperature $(\Theta_{D})$ is strongly related with the average sound velocity $(v_{m})$ following the equation$^{29}$

$$
\Theta_{D}=\frac{h}{k}\left(\frac{3nN_{\text{A}}\rho}{4\pi M}\right)^{1/3}v_{m}, \tag{3-15}
$$

where $h$ and $k$ are the Planck and Boltzmann constants, respectively, $N_{\text{A}}$ is the Avogadro's number; $n$ is the number of atoms in the molecule, $M$ is the molecule weight, and $\rho$ is the density.

Table 8 and 9 present the calculated longitudinal $(v_{l})$, transverse $(v_{t})$, average sound wave velocity $(v_{m})$, and the Debye temperature $(\Theta_{D})$, along with the available theoretical and experimental values. For the pristine TAC and TSC, our calculated thermal properties are found in good agreement with both the theoretical and experimental results$^{4, 30-32}$. As shown in Table 8 and 9, compared with the pristine TAC and TSC, doping Hf/Nb/Zr has adverse effects on the materials' thermal properties with decreased sound wave velocity and Debye temperature, especially for doping occurring at the interstitial site.

### 4. Conclusion

In summary, ab initio method based on DFT has been employed to study the structural, electronic, magnetic, as well as mechanical and thermal properties of Hf/Nb/Zr-doped MAX phases TAC and TSC. Irrespective of the doping position, slightly increased lattice constants are found in all doped phases. $Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i$ doping has significant effects on the electronic and magnetic properties of TAC and TSC. Particularly, magnetism $(1.35/1.00\ \mu_B)$ can be induced in TAC by doping Hf/Zr at the c-ATi2 site. The electron transfer between the dopant and nearby atoms is analyzed to interpret the different magnetic behavior of these doped phases. Effects of doping on the mechanical and thermal properties of TAC and TSC are evaluated based on the calculation of the bulk, shear, and Young's moduli, Poisson and Push ratio, Debye temperature and thermal conductivity. Our calculation indicates that $Hf_{Ti1}/Nb_{Ti1}/Zr_{Ti1}$ has minimal effect on the mechanical properties of TAC and TSC, in according with the previous experiment, whereas $Hf_i/Nb_i/Zr_i$ brings about adverse effects, leading to significantly decreased bulk modulus, shear modulus and Young's modulus, Debye temperature and thermal conductivity. The distinct doping effect is explained by analyzing the atomic bonding properties.
