# ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE AND OPTICAL PROPERTIES OF $A La_{9-x} Eu_{x}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}$
$(A=Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, La_{1 / 3} ; x=0,0.07)$

Yana V. Baklanova, Olga A. Lipina, Andrey N. Enyashin, Inna V. Baklanova, Alexander P. Tyutyunnik, Ludmila L. Surat, Alexander Yu. Chufarov, Vladimir G. Zubkov

Institute of Solid State Chemistry, UB RAS, 91 Pervomayskaya, 620990, Ekaterinburg, Russia

## Abstract
The new single-phase oxyapatites $A La_{9}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}$ and $A La_{8.93} Eu_{0.07}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}(A=Li, Rb$ , space group $P 6_{3} / m)$ are prepared using the ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) assisted process and the solid state method. Detailed characterization of the fine features of electronic structure within the families of oxyapatites $A La_{9}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}$ and $A La_{8.93} Eu_{0.07}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}( A=Li, Na, K, Rb)$ has been performed using photoluminescence measurements and density functional theory (DFT) calculations. The trivalent europium ions may occupy two crystallographically nonequivalent sites (4 f) and (6 h) with the $C_{3}$ and $C_{s}$ symmetry, respectively, and can be distinguished using the photoluminescence excitation on and the photoluminescence spectra from potassium and rubidium germanates, in contrast to those for lithium and sodium compounds. All the compounds should be semiconductors with wide direct band gaps of about 3.8 eV for alkali metal derivatives and about3.2 eV for the vacancy-stabilized $La_{9.33}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}$ composition. The thermodynamic stability, the lattice parameters and the band gap value of $CsLa_{9}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}$ not yet synthesized as a single phase have been qualified using DFT calculations.

### 1. Introduction

In the last two decades, a large group of materials with apatite structure (phosphates, vanadates, silicates etc.) has been dropped in the focus of attention of researchers and technologists. The variety of functional properties and applications of these materials has been revealed in different fields ranging from femtosecond lasers to creation of biocompatible synthetic bone tissues. Recently, the scientific interest of researchers shifts towards the studying of functional properties of REE silicates and germanates. Among the latter, the compounds with the general formula $ARE_9(TO_4)_6O_2$ ($A = \text{Li–Cs}$, $RE = \text{REE or Y}$, $T = \text{Si, Ge}$) belong to a group of insufficiently explored apatites (space group $P6_3/m$, Z=1) with an isolated structural motif [1–10]. The crystal lattice of these compounds includes the following characteristic positions: $(A + 3\ RE)$ atoms in $4f$ – the centers of the distorted tricapped trigonal prisms with $\text{CN} = 6{+}3$, $(6\ RE)$ atoms in $6h$ – the centers of the pentagonal bipyramids with $\text{CN} = 6{+}1$, silicon/germanium atoms in $6h$ – the centers of the tetrahedra, and oxygen atoms in $2\text{x}6h$, $12i$ and $2a$. The distortion of a trigonal prism here is determined by the value of the average ionic radius r of cations – $(\text{r}A^\text{VI} + \text{r}RE^\text{VI})/2$. The numerical measure of distortion is the angle $\varphi$ characterizing the twisting of the upper trigonal base relative to the lower base. If the average radius changes from 0.81 to $1.18\ \text{\AA}$, the unit cell volume may vary from 511 to $603\ \text{\AA}^3$, and the angle $\varphi$ – from $5^\circ$ to $25^\circ$ [7, 10]. The limited information about the properties of $ALa_9(TO_4)_6O_2$ ($A = \text{Li, Na}$, $T = \text{Si, Ge}$) available in the scientific literature suggests a high potential for their application as catalysts [11, 12], ion conductors [13–15], components in microelectronics [16], optical media in diode-pumped solid states lasers (DPSS) [17–19], and crystal phosphors [20]. Information about existence and properties of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ compounds with large $A$ cations ($\text{r} > 1.2\ \text{\AA}$ for $A = \text{K, Rb, Cs}$) has been missed in the literature for a long time. Very recently, the synthesis and crystal structure of new members of $ALa_9(TO_4)_6O_2$ oxyapatite family – $KLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ and $KLa_{8.37}Eu_{0.63}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ - were reported [21], which introduce a high capability of $KLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ optical host for the creation of rare earth ions activated phosphors.

The specific character of arrangement of alkaline atoms only within the trigonal prisms and a large variation in ionic radii from $0.76\text{\AA}$ ($\text{Li}^+$) to $1.67\text{\AA}$ ($\text{Cs}^+$) in comparison to $1.032\ \text{\AA}$ ($\text{La}^{3+}$) [22] allow one to consider the group of compounds $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A = \text{Li–Cs}$) as attractive model objects to study the role of flexibility of coordination polyhedra on the optical properties, electronic structure and chemical bonding. In this work, the effect of the size factor on the electronic properties of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A = \text{Li, Na, K, Rb}$) will be analyzed with the use of a $\text{Eu}^{3+}$ luminescence probe. The emission properties of this ion are sensitive to the symmetry of local environment [23–26]. Particularly, two types of the luminescence activators within the lattice of aforementioned

germanates may be distinguished, since $Eu^{3+}$ ions can occupy two noncentrosymmetric sites $C_3$ ($4f$) and $C_s$ ($6h$). The conditions for activators' excitation and the relative contribution of these $Eu^{3+}$ types to the general picture of luminescence will be elucidated. We claim that the rise of alkaline atoms with a larger/smaller ionic radius than that of lanthanum within the lattice of germanate apatites should change the ratio between the ionic and covalent components of chemical bonding, hence, influence the optical properties. In this publication we report for the first time the data on the structure, optical properties and the results of quantum-chemical modeling of the electronic structure and chemical bonding for a new group of compounds with apatite structure, $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$, $A$ = Li and Na, in comparison with $A$ = K and Rb.

## 2. Materials and methods

### 2.1. Preparation
The $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs) germanates have been prepared using the ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) assisted process close to that described by Lipina et al. [21]. Appropriate amounts of $La_2O_3$ (99.98%) and $A_2CO_3$ (99.9%) ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs) were dissolved in nitric acid (6 M). A dilute solution of ammonia was used for dissolving $GeO_2$ (99.5%). EDTA was employed as a chelating agent and was taken in the molar ratio 1:1 to the total amount of cations. Ammonium hydroxide was added to enhance the dissolution of EDTA in distilled water. All the solutions were mixed together, vigorously stirred at room temperature for 3 h, and heated in evaporating dishes at 90–95 °C. The resulting dark grey gels were calcined during gradual heating up to 700 °C to form loose white powders. Afterwards, the samples were put into alumina crucibles and annealed for 20 h at different temperatures (750 °C, 800 °C, 850 °C and 900 °C).

The $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ phases ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb) have been synthesized by the traditional solid-state method. The alkaline carbonates $A_2CO_3$ ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb) (99.9%) taken with 30% excess, $La_2O_3$ (99.98%) and $GeO_2$ (99.5%) oxides were used as raw chemicals. Mixed powder materials were pressed into pellets with a diameter of 10 mm, put up into alumina crucibles and annealed at 700 °C for 20 h. The calcined specimens were reground, pressed into pellets and annealed at 900 °C for 6 h and then at 1100 °C for 30–40 min.

The microstructure and morphology of the samples were studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) using a JEOL JSM–6390 LA microscope. Representative SEM images of

The samples contain rather large (up to $20\ \mu$m) agglomerates, which consist of small lamellar particles. The particle size distributions of the obtained samples were evaluated from the SEM images collected in histogram form and fitted to log-normal distributions (Figs. S1-S2). The particle sizes of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ and $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb) powders are listed in Table 1 and Table S1. A relatively large size of $LiLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ particles at a rather low sintering temperature is often explained by a high Li content in the structure of lithium-rich compound, that promotes particle growth [27-28].

All X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected on a STADI-P automated diffractometer (STOE) in transmission geometry equipped with a linear mini-PSD detector using Cu $K\alpha_1$ radiation in the $2\theta$ range from $5^\circ$ to $120^\circ$ with a step of $0.02^\circ$. Polycrystalline silicon ($a = 5.43075(5)$ Å) was used as an external standard. The phase purity of the samples was checked by comparing their XRD patterns with those in the PDF2 database (ICDD), release 2009. The crystal structure of $NaLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ [6] was used as the starting model. The crystal structure refinements were carried out with the GSAS program suite [29, 30]. Experimental, calculated and difference XRD patterns can be found in Supplementary Materials (Figs. S3-S6). The crystallographic data, details of Rietveld refinements of the structures of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ and $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb) are listed in Table 1 and Table S1. Selected interatomic distances, angles, atomic coordinates, and isotropic thermal parameters are included in Tables 2 and Tables S2.

The diffuse reflectance spectra were recorded in the interval of 220-800 nm on a Shimadzu UV-3600 UV-vis-NIR spectrophotometer using $BaSO_4$ as a reference. Photoluminescence studies were conducted on a Varian Cary Eclipse fluorescence spectrometer coupled to a personal computer with Varian software and supplied with a pulse 75 kW Xenon lamp as excitation source (pulse length $\tau = 2\ \mu$s, pulse frequency $v = 80$ Hz, wavelength resolution 0.5 nm; PMT Hamamatsu R928). The measurements were carried out in a pulse excitation mode with a delay time of $100\ \mu$s and slits: 2.5 nm (excitation) and 1.5 nm (emission). All the powders are transparent in the visible region and have a strong absorption of near-UV light (Figs. S7-S8). The optical band gaps of the samples in assumption for direct transition can be determined using the optical absorption coefficient evaluated from the Kubelka-Munk function [31-32], extrapolating the linear part of the plot of $(F(R)\cdot h\nu)^2$ versus $h\nu$ (insets on Figs. S7-S8). The optical band gap energies of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ and $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb) are listed in Table 3 and Table S3.

### 2.3. Computational Details

The calculations of all $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, La_{1/3}$) compounds as well as their parent binary oxides were performed within the framework of the density-functional theory (DFT) as implemented in the SIESTA 4.0 program package [33]. The exchange-correlation potential within the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof parametrization was used. The core electrons were treated within the frozen core approximation, applying norm-conserving Troullier–Martins pseudopotentials [34] generated using ATOM 3.2.2. program [35]. The valence and semi-core shells as well as their pseudopotential core radii (in brackets, Bohrs) were chosen as $6s^2(3.47)6p^0(3.74)5d^1(3.22)$ for La, $4s^2(2.48)4p^2(2.48)4d^0(2.99)$ for Ge, $2s^2(1.45)2p^4(1.45)$ for O, $2s^1(3.49)$ for Li, $3s^1(2.97)$ for Na, $4s^1(3.47)$ for K, $4p^6(3.82)5s^1(3.82)$ for Rb, $5p^6(3.78)6s^1(3.78)$ for Cs. In all calculations, the double-$\zeta$ polarized basis set was used for all valence and semi-core shells. The $k$-point mesh was generated by the method of Monkhorst and Pack. The real-space grid used for the numeric integrations was set to correspond to the energy cutoff of 300 Ry. For $k$-point sampling, a cutoff of $15$ Å was used. For compounds with alkali-metal cations the unit cells were employed for calculations, while for $La_{1/3}La_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ compound the $1×1×3$ supercell with a single La vacancy in $4f$ position was used. All calculations were performed using variable-cell and atomic position relaxations, with convergence criteria corresponding to the maximum residual stress of 0.1 GPa for each component of the stress tensor, and the maximum residual force component of $0.05$ eV/Å. Preliminary test calculations of binary oxides have revealed a good suitability of the chosen approach for the description of geometry. The difference between experimental and equilibrium DFT GGA lattice parameters does not exceed ±1%.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Crystal Structure of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$

$ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs$) compounds have an isolated structural motif based on $(GeO_4)^{4-}$ tetrahedra. La/A(1) cations are located on the three-fold axis between the hexagonal nets of oxygen atoms O(1) and O(2) (Fig. 1). The nearest environment of La/A(1) cations includes three O(1) and three O(2) atoms forming a trigonal prism. Three O(3) atoms located above the lateral faces of the prism create a tricapped trigonal prism with $CN = 6 + 3$ around La/A(1) position. These trigonal prisms are assembled into chain-like manner along the three-fold axis. The chains are bounded together via $(GeO_4)$ tetrahedra. The nearest environment of germanium atoms is formed by oxygen atoms O(1), O(2) and two O(3). The chains and tetrahedra form hexagonal tunnels. The La(2) atoms are located on the tunnel faces. The nearest environment of La(2) atoms with $CN = 7$ is

formed by O(1), O(2), O(4) and four O(3) atoms. The O(4) atoms are at the center of the hexagonal tunnel on the six-fold axis at a distance of $c/2$ from each other.

The analysis of the results from the structural X-ray diffraction studies reveals that, the $A\mathrm{La}_{9}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ with $A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb have been fabricated as the single-phases, while the phase of $\mathrm{CsLa}_{9}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ is accompanied by admixture of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{GeO}_{5}$ compound (ICSD #59727). Henceforth, Cs counterpart is omitted from the further characterization. The unit cell parameters and volume, the axial ratio $c/a$ and the twisting angle $\varphi$ of trigonal prism of all $A\mathrm{La}_{9}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ compounds increase in accordance to the alkali ion radius (Table 1, Fig. 1). Passing from $\mathrm{LiLa}_{9}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ to $\mathrm{RbLa}_{9}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ the most noticeable changes in the interatomic distances take place for La/A(1)-O(1) – from $2.454\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ to $2.510\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ and for La/A(1)-O(2) – from $2.556\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ to $2.599\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ (Table 2). The bond lengths of La/A(1)-O(3), La(2)-O(1,2,3) and angles La1/A1-O1-Ge and La1/A1-O2-Ge remain practically unchanged. Therefore, is the trigonal prism may be characterized as the most flexible element. Mutual rotation of two neighboring oxygen layers around the three-fold axis leads inevitably to a distortion of the trigonal prism. The functional relation between the twisting angle $\varphi$ and the axial ratio $c/a$ could be a convenient tool for analysis of the intra-lattice phenomena initiated by the action of temperature or pressure. If the $c/a$ ratio lowers due to anisotropic variation of the lattice parameters, then the twisting angle $\varphi$ decreases, and vice versa. A constant $c/a$ ratio means that the value of $\varphi$ does not change. This structural feature of compounds with apatite structure is often noted in the literature [1–10].

### 3.2. Optical properties

The preparation of $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ activated samples was guided by the following concept. Due to the difference between the ionic radii of $\mathrm{La}^{3+}$ ($1.032\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ at $\mathrm{CN}=6$) and $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ ($0.89\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$), the insertion of activator should always lead to a shrinkage of the crystal lattice. Therefore, on one hand the chosen content of activator should not lead to a considerable change of the crystal lattice and the corresponding crystal fields. On the other hand, it should be sufficiently enough for registration of luminescence. We adapted as a basis the data from investigations of the luminescence properties of $\mathrm{NaLa}_{9-x}\mathrm{Eu}_{x}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$, where the critical concentration $x=0.07$ was found, at which the maximal emission during $^{5}\mathrm{D}_{0}{\to}^{7}\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{J}}$ ($\mathrm{J}=0$–4) transitions was observed [9]. Since the $A\mathrm{La}_{9}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb) phases have a similar crystallochemistry, the critical concentrations of activator in $A\mathrm{La}_{9- x}\mathrm{Eu}_{x}(\mathrm{GeO}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ ($A$ = Li, Na, K, Rb) phases should be close to $x=0.07$, i.e. 0.78% mol. or 0.095 at.%. However, the problem of localization of $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ in two noncentrosymmetric positions $C_{3}$ ($4f$) and $C_{s}$ ($6h$) cannot be solved a priori. Usually, the site occupations are determined from the temperature-

concentration dependences of intensities of $^5$D$_0$$\rightarrow$$^7$F$_0$ transitions, the so-called 0-0 transition. In apatites, the transition $^5$D$_0$$\rightarrow$$^7$F$_0$ is allowed for the activators located in positions (6$h$) with symmetry $C_s$ and positions (4$f$) with symmetry $C_3$ [22-25]. However, in most cases the experimental decomposition of $^5$D$_0$$\rightarrow$$^7$F$_0$ transition into individual components is not successful because of a small difference (~ 10-50 cm$^{-1}$) in their values. To solve this problem, we used a slightly modified analysis based on the additivity of line intensities for the basic electric-dipole transition $^5$D$_0$$\rightarrow$$^7$F$_2$. For this purpose, we examined the excitation spectra of the right and left slopes of the basic peak at 611-615 nm.

The photoluminescence excitation (PLE) and photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = Li, Na) germanates are almost identical (Fig. 2). The wide band located at ~282 nm in the PLE spectra of $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = Li, Na) is a charge transfer band (CTB) that corresponds to an electron transfer from the O2$p$ orbitals to the empty Eu4$f$ orbitals. Besides, the PLE spectrum contains several sharp peaks at 350-460 nm associated with $^7$F$_0$$\rightarrow$$^5$D$_J$ ($J$ = 1-4), $^7$F$_0$$\rightarrow$$^5$L$_J$ ($J$ = 6, 7), $^7$F$_0$$\rightarrow$$^5$G$_J$ ($J$ = 1-4) transitions in Eu$^{3+}$ ions. The PL spectra consist of the lines in the visible region ascribed to the specific electron transitions $^5$D$_0$$\rightarrow$$^7$F$_J$ ($J$ = 0-4) occurring within the $f$-$f$ shell of Eu$^{3+}$.

The obtained results are indicative of equivalent participation of both optical centers in the formation of luminescent properties. The coincidence of the CTB maximum, excitation and emission spectra shows that there are no differences in the positions of the basic luminescent level $^5$D$_0$ for both active centers. The luminescence in the $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = K, Rb) phases manifests itself in a completely different way. Figures 3-4 present the data for $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = K, Rb). The excitation spectrum consists of two subbands with maxima at 233 and 282 nm, as well as $^7$F$_0$$\rightarrow$$^5$D$_J$ ($J$ = 1-4), $^7$F$_0$$\rightarrow$$^5$L$_J$ ($J$ = 6, 7), and $^7$F$_0$$\rightarrow$$^5$G$_J$ ($J$ = 1-4) lines. The critical distance $R_c$ of energy transfer between Eu$^{3+}$ ions can be calculated using the equation proposed by Blasse [36].

$$
R_{c}=2\left[\frac{3 V}{4 \pi x_{c} N}\right]^{\frac{1}{3}}, \tag{1}
$$

where $V$ is the volume of the unit cell, $N$ represents the number of sites in the host unit cell that can be occupied by activator ions, and $x_c$ is the critical concentration. Taking the values of $V$ = 610.943 $\mathring{A}^3$ and $V$ = 628.169 $\mathring{A}^3$, $N$ = 1, and $x_c$ = 0.07, the critical distances $R_c$ are found to be 25.544 and 25.782 $\mathring{A}$ for LiLa$_{8.93}$Eu$_{0.07}$(GeO4)$_6$O$_2$ and RbLa$_{8.93}$Eu$_{0.07}$(GeO4)$_6$O$_2$, respectively.

The PL spectra of $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_2)_6O_2$ ($A = K, Rb$) at 282 and 233 nm consist of the lines associated with $^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_J$ ($J = 0\text{--}4$) and $^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_J$ ($J = 1\text{--}4$), respectively. Among them, the most significant emission originates from the $^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_2$ transition. The well-known asymmetry ratio of integrated intensities ($R$) of the $Eu^{3+}$ emission transitions $^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_2$ and $^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_1$ depends on the specific crystallographic site symmetry of trivalent europium ions [37]. The comparison of the ratio $R = I(^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_2)/I(^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_1)$ for the PL spectra of $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_2)_6O_2$ ($A = Li, Na, K, Rb$) at 282 nm shows that its value changes abruptly with the increase in the alkaline ion radius when passing from $Li^+$ and $Na^+$ ions ($R \sim 4.17$) to $K^+$ and Rb+ ions ($R \sim 3.87$) indicating the significant structural changes of the $Eu^{3+}$ coordination environment.

Nevertheless, a decreased emission of the $^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_0$ line on the PL spectra of $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_2)_6O_2$ ($A = K, Rb$) under excitation with 233 nm wave length allows one to ascribe this subband to the excitation of $Eu^{3+}$ ions located in the (4$f$) sites, while another one with 282 nm -- to the excitation of $Eu^{3+}$ ions located in the (6$h$) sites. Our estimates of contributions from activators located in noncentrosymmetric sites $C_3$ (4$f$) and $C_s$ (6$h$) agree with the results of examination of $LiRE_9(SiO_4)_6O_2$ ($RE = Ce, Eu, Tb$) compounds [38]. Notwithstanding the difference in the technical execution, analogous conclusion was made about the orbital splitting strengths of the crystal fields acting on the activators in (4$f$) and (6$h$) sites. In the aforementioned silicates, the CTB has two subbands -- the higher-energy states are related to activators in (4$f$), while the lower-energy states -- to activators in (6$h$) sites. The most distorted element in the lattice of $LiRE_9(SiO_4)_6O_2$ ($RE = Ce, Eu, Tb$) is the trigonal prism with twisting angles $\varphi$ equal to $22.052^\circ$ (Ce), $20.928^\circ$ (Eu) and $9.499^\circ$ (Tb) at axial ratios $c/a$ of 0.7326, 0.7289, and 0.7253, respectively. Considering the data on luminescence, twisting angles $\varphi$ and axial ratios $c/a$ presented in this section, it can be concluded that the apatite compounds with the least twisting angle $\varphi$ should be the most favorite for the design of optical hosts for phosphors with excitation in the CTB. In their case, the difference in the strength of the crystal fields affecting the activators in the (4$f$) and (6$h$) sites should be minimal.

### 3.3. Structure and relative stability of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ after DFT calculations

The structural stability of the lattice and the lattice parameters of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ crystals with $A = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs$ and $La_{1/3}$ have been validated using DFT calculations as an independent method and compared to available experimental data from recent studies [9, 21, 39]. In addition, these calculations may predict some properties for a new, yet, purely characterized $CsLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ compound. The lattice of all crystals has demonstrated a rigidity regarding the type of M cation and the preservation of initial hexagonal symmetry after the full geometry optimization. The calculated

lattice parameters for all the compounds are listed in Table 3. A reasonable agreement with expected error of ~1.5% can be found between the theoretical and available experimental data for $NaLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$, $KLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ and $La_{1/3}La_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$, demonstrating the ability of the computational approach for correct geometry description. The values of both lattice parameters increase with the radius of $A$ cation.

Meanwhile, the DFT calculations allow to distinguish a difference in the local environment near both $A$ and La cations within the distorted prismatic O coordination of $4f$ positions, since the experimental structural data for the twisting angle are attributed to a statistically averaged ensemble of both $LaO_6$ and $AO_6$ polyhedra. The amount of $LaO_6$ prisms is three times larger than that of $AO_6$. Therefore, the $\varphi$ values calculated for $LaO_6$ prisms are found to be closer to the $\varphi$ values from experiment (Table 3). The $\varphi$ values calculated for $AO_6$ prisms are larger than those for $LaO_6$ by 2.5–$5^\circ$ depending on the cation radii. The least difference $\Delta\varphi$ between these $\varphi$ is observed for the largest Cs cation.

The relative stability of the $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ compounds has been estimated using the formation enthalpies calculated in accordance with the formal reaction of formation from a mixture of parent binary oxides (Table 3). The results of this estimate reveal that all compounds are thermodynamically stable and may exist, since the least stable $La_{9.33}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ composition among considered compositions is known [39]. There is no straight dependence of the formation enthalpy neither on the cation radius nor on $\Delta\varphi$. At the same time, the lattice of $KLa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ demonstrates an obvious gain in thermodynamic stability.

### 3.4. Electronic structure of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ after DFT calculations
The electronic band structure and the density of states (DOS) calculated for all compounds are visualized in Fig. 5 and Fig. S9. They possess a qualitatively similar picture irrespective of the $A$ cation type with only minor differences at the bottom of the conduction band. All compounds should be semiconductors with wide direct band gaps of about 3.8 eV for alkali metal derivatives and about 3.2 eV for the vacancy-stabilized $La_{9.33}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ composition (Table 3). The upper valence band is split into two distinct peaks. The peak at 0...-3 eV below the Fermi level is formed by the dominant O2p-states, while the peak at -3...-5 eV is composed of a mixture of O2p- and Ge4p-states. The separate well-localized band near -7 eV is formed by the hybridized Ge4s4p and O2s2p states. In general, such distribution of states agrees with the formation of predominantly covalent Ge-O overlaps within $GeO_4$ tetrahedra and the mainly ionic character of interaction between $GeO_4$

tetrahedra and $A$ or La atoms. The conduction band of all compounds is of a predominant La$5d$-character with an admixture of $A$ states.

However, despite this qualitative similarity in the DOS distribution, all the compounds may be definitely classified into three groups depending on the composition of the conduction band. The latter may be characterized and defined after analysis of the relative DOS distribution of the dominating La$5d$ states from atoms in the $6h$ and $4f$ crystallographic positions (right panels in Fig. 5 and Fig. S9).

The perfect situation of equal participation of La($6h$) and La($4f$) atoms in chemical bonding implies that the ratio of their DOS's could be close to 2. However, due to the difference in the coordination environment, these atom types exhibit a different activity. Indeed, the bottom of the edge valence band is dominated by the La$5d(4f)$-states, while the top of the same band consists mainly of the La$5d(6h)$-states. The ratio between these DOS's in the valence bands is much the same for all compounds, notwithstanding the type of $A$ cation. In contrast, the composition of the conduction band is regulated by the type of $A$ cation. First, the relative amount of La($6h$) and La($4f$) atoms in $\mathrm{La_{9.33}(GeO_4)_6O_2}$ compound is $18/(12-2)=1.8$, and the La$5d(6h)$/La$5d(4f)$ ratio is nearly constant and close to this value in a wide range from the bottom of the conduction band. Second, the small $A$ cations (Li and Na) within the $\mathrm{ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2}$ lattice provide the conduction band with two distinct minima of the La$5d(6h)$/La$5d(4f)$ ratio: the La$5d(4f)$-states should dominate at $\sim$4 and $\sim$6 eV above the Fermi level, while at the bottom of the conduction band the La$5d(6h)$-states prevail. Third, the $\mathrm{ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2}$ lattice with large $A$ cations (K, Rb and Cs) would have a conduction band, whose bottom is formed mainly by the La$5d(4f)$-states, and a large amount of La$5d(6h)$-states can be found near 7 eV above the Fermi level.

The optical properties of a compound are defined in many aspects by the origin of the conduction band. The DFT-established $A$-dependent division of the $\mathrm{ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2}$ family according to the composition of the conduction band is excellently confirmed by their experimental absorption and PL spectra. Moreover, the recent DFT calculations give a fair description of the band gap values. However, they contradict the results of PL spectroscopy, resolving incorrectly the relative energy position of the unoccupied $5d$-states from the La(or Eu) atoms in the $6h$ and $4f$ crystallographic positions. Probably, the origin of this contradiction can be searched among the drawbacks of the DFT. Indeed, DFT GGA functionals are valuable for the description of structural and cohesion properties, as well as for the analysis of the ground state (valence DOS) [40]. Yet, it is well recognized that the DFT in general may fail in the correct description of excited states and even can underestimate the band gap, which still remains the subject of intensive dispute using high-level

ab initio methods even for binary compounds [41, 42]. A further improvement in the description of the conduction band could be performed in the framework of the GW-theory, which remains too expensive in the case of the unit cells of apatite-like crystals.

### 4. Conclusions
The new germanates $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = alkali metal) and $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = Li, Rb) have been synthesized by the ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) assisted process and the solid state method, respectively. Rietveld refinement of room temperature powder X-ray diffraction patterns shows that these germanates crystallize in a $P6_3/m$ hexagonal unit cell ($Z = 1$) and have an apatite structure type. Detailed characterization of the fine-structure features within the group of $ALa_9(GeO_4)_6O_2$ and $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ oxyapatites has been performed using the photoluminescence measurements and the density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Trivalent europium ions occupy two crystallographically nonequivalent sites $(4f)$ and $(6h)$ with the $C_3$ and $C_s$ symmetry, respectively, and can be distinguished on the photoluminescence excitation spectra of potassium and rubidium germanates, in contrast to the spectra of lithium and sodium compounds. Reduced emission of the line $^5D_0\rightarrow^7F_0$ on the photoluminescence spectra of $ALa_{8.93}Eu_{0.07}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ ($A$ = K, Rb) upon 233 nm excitation helps to attribute the subbands at 233 nm and 282 nm to the excitations of $Eu^{3+}$ ions located in $(4f)$ and $(6h)$ positions, respectively. All the compounds should be semiconductors with wide direct band gaps of about 3.8 eV for alkali metal derivatives and about 3.2 eV for the vacancy-stabilized $La_{9.33}(GeO_4)_6O_2$ composition. The results suggest that germanium-based oxyapatites are promising as efficient optical hosts for the production of the phosphors activated by rare earth ions.

# ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE AND OPTICAL PROPERTIES OF $A La_{9-x} Eu_{x}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}$

$(A=Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, La_{1 / 3} ; x=0,0.07)$

Yana V. Baklanova, Olga A. Lipina, Andrey N. Enyashin, Inna V. Baklanova, Alexander P. Tyutyunnik, Ludmila L. Surat, Alexander Yu. Chufarov, Vladimir G. Zubkov

Institute of Solid State Chemistry, UB RAS, 91 Pervomayskaya, 620990, Ekaterinburg, Russia

## Highlights
Oxyapatites $A La_{9}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}: Eu^{3+}(A=Li, Rb)$ have been synthesized for the first time.

Electronic structure of $A La_{9}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}(A=Li-Cs, La_{1 / 3})$ has been studied in details.

Luminescence properties of $A La_{9}(GeO_{4})_{6} O_{2}: Eu^{3+}$-based phosphors have been examined.

Oxyapatites are promising optical hosts for the fabrication of REE activated phosphors.
