## ABSTRACT

The ground-state structural, electronic, optical and thermoelectric properties of the $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ alloy have been studied by using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) scheme based on the density functional theory in the frame of generalized gradient approximation (GGA). In order to model $K_{1-x}Rb_xCl$ alloy, 16-atoms supercell of the type $2 \times 2 \times 2$ is employed. The lattice structure of $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ alloy is obtained by replacing four K atoms by Rb atoms in the crystal lattice of KCl. The band structure and density of states histograms are plotted and discussed. The valence band maxima (VBM) and the conduction band minima (CBM) of parent KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ is located at $\Gamma$ point, resulting in a direct band gap. The calculated results are found to be in good agreement with available theoretical and experimental data. The optical properties are calculated for radiation up to 13.5 eV. The important peaks of the optical spectra calculated in terms of electronic structure. From the optical spectra, optics technology and the useful optoelectronic is predicted.

## 1. Introduction

In recent years, the study of energy bands of insulating crystals, phase transitions, effect of doping and high-pressure behavior of the materials have become quite interest due to development of new crystals. Moreover, the alkali metal compounds with halogen elements are crystalline solids having great attention from theoret- ical and experimental viewpoints. The alkali halides such as NaCl, KCl, Rb-doped KCl, CaF and $SrF_2$ have been used for erosion- resistant infrared radomes, windows for infrared detectors and infrared laser windows. From the literature survey, it is visible that very little attention in the literature has been given to the system- atic investigation of potassium chloride, thus many fundamental properties such as lattice parameter, band gap, and band structure parameters, density of states, optical properties and thermal prop- erties of KCl remain to be determined precisely. First polymor- phism of potassium chloride under shock compression has been reported by Al'tshuler et al. [1]. Rosenberg [2] and Zaretsky [3] investigated the pressure-induced phase transition of KCl. Experi- mental investigations were made and reported on the phase tran- sition pressure for potassium chloride [4–8]. Andriessen et al. [9] studied Jahn-Teller effect in KCl. Ben-Dor et al. [10] reported the doped KCl with mercury chlorides and the resulting spectrum. Tsal et al. [11] presented the melt-doped KCl crystal in air and their spectral characteristics. In the present work, the first principle cal- culations are performed to study the structural, electronic, thermo- electric and optoelectronic properties of KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ compounds. The main peaks of the optical spectra are studied in terms of the calculated electronic structure. These calculations will be useful to realize the structural behavior and optoelectronic properties of these compounds.

## 2. Method of calculations

FP-LAPW method is employed to calculate the structural, elec- tronic, optical and transport properties of KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ alloys. All the calculations are carried out by the Wien2k code [12] within the frame of DFT. The generalized gradient approxima- tion (GGA) as parameterized by Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof (PBE) [13] is employed for the exchange-correlation potentials. ## 3. Result and discussions

### 3.1. Structural properties

Structural properties of the KCl and its ternary combination $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ investigated at ambient condition by using GGA scheme. KCl crystallizes in NaCl- type B1 structure with the space group 225; whereas $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ crystallizes in cubic structure with space group 227 as shown in Fig. 1a & b. The position of K and Cl atom is situated at (0,0,0) and (0.5,0.5,0.5) respectively. Rb doped KCl (ternary $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$) is found to undergo a phase transformation from cubic 225 (Fm3m) to cubic 227 (Fd3m) structure.

The lattice constant $a_0$, bulk modulus B and B' (pressure derivative bulk modulus) values are obtained by fitting the energy versus volume according to Birch-Murnaghan's equation of state as shown in Fig. 2a & b. The calculated structural properties of KCl and $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ combinations are presented in Table 1. All the calculated results of the parent compound KCl are in good agreement with the available experimental and theoretical values. Using cohesive energy calculation, the structural stability of cell is determined. The bulk modulus of $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ decreases as the lattice constant increases. This behavior agrees with previous results.

### 3.2. Electronic properties

#### 3.2.1. Density of states

Density of states (DOS) and electronic/band structure are calculated by using FP-LAPW method within GGA is presented in Fig. 3a & b. The DOS below Fermi level (0 eV) is the valence band due to the completely filled states on the other hand, DOS above 0 eV is the conduction band due to the unoccupied states. In binary KCl alloy (Fig. 3a), below the Fermi level is mostly due to the K- 3p and Cl - 4 s & 3p states, above the Fermi level is mainly due to the K- 4 s and Cl- 4 s states and around the Fermi level is arises because of Cl-3p states. In $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ alloy (Fig. 3b), below the Fermi level is mainly due to the Rb- 4p, K-3p and Cl- 4 s & 3p states and above the Fermi level is owing to Cl-4 s & 3p and k-3p states. And the states at Fermi is originated because of Cl-3p states only.

From Fig. 3a, it can observe that the hybridization occurs between K-s and Cl-s states above the Fermi level indicate covalent bonding nature of the atoms.

#### 3.2.2. Band structure

The study of electronic band structure is important to understand the inherent electronic properties of the materials. Also, the band dispersion helps us to understand the information of the effective mass of electron or holes. The electronic band structure of KCl and $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ compounds is shown in Fig. 4a & b. The energy band gap ($\text{E}_g$) of these alloys were calculated and presented in Table 1, and compared with the theoretical values and the available experimental data. The valence band maxima (VBM) and the conduction band minima (CBM) of KCl and $\text{K}_{0.5}$-$\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ were located at $\Gamma$ point, resulting in a direct band gap [14,15]. In KCl, the bottom most valence band at $-12$ eV corresponds to hybridized Cl-s and p states with K-p states. The second region from $-10.6$ to $-10.8$ eV is formed primarily from K- p states hybridized with Cl-s states. The third region very close to the Fermi level, ranging from $-1$ to 0 eV is mainly occurred by Cl-p states. In $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$, the bottom region at $-12$ eV is mainly by Cl-s and p states along with k-p states and little contribution from Rb-p states.

The second region from $-11.2$ to $-10.8$ eV is dominated by K-p states. The new third region is extending from $-7.8$ to $-8.2$ eV is mainly by Rb-p states. The top most band region from $-0.8$ to 0 eV is purely because of Cl-p states. The above said details are confirmed by fat band structure of K -tot, Cl -tot, Rb -tot atoms shown in Figs. 5 & 6.

### 3.3. Optical properties

The energy dependent optical properties of KCl and $\text{K}_{0.5}\text{Rb}_{0.5}\text{Cl}$ were determined by the complex dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega) = \varepsilon_1(\omega)$ + i $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$. Figs. 7 & 8, shows the calculated real part $\varepsilon 1$ and the imaginary part $\varepsilon 2$ as a function of the photon energy. The imaginary part $\varepsilon 2(\omega)$ describes optical absorptive nature and the real part $\varepsilon 1(\omega)$ describes dispersive nature of a material. The imaginary part of dielectric function is related to electronic band structure, where the transition from valance bands to conduction bands.

Since, the complex dielectric is associated with the interaction of photons and electrons, its components $\varepsilon1(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon2(\omega)$ are used to calculate other optical parameters such as absorption coefficient $\alpha(\omega)$, loss function $L(\omega)$, extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$, refractive index $n(\omega)$ and reflectivity $R(\omega)$. These parameters are used to determine the optical transport mechanism and provide fruitful vision in the direction of optoelectronic devices. Ambrosch-Draxl et al. [16-18] has given the detailed description of calculation of these matrix elements. The real part $\varepsilon1(\omega)$ of the dielectric function for KCl and K₀.₅Rb₀.₅Cl is shown in Fig. 7a & b. The calculated static dielectric constant or zero frequency limit $\varepsilon1(0)$ is found close to 2.1 and 2.0 for KCl and K₀.₅Rb₀.₅Cl, respectively. This is very well-matched with the published theoretical results for KCl. In addition to the zero-frequency limit, $\varepsilon1(\omega)$ rises and reaches maximum values of 3.9 at 7.2 eV for KCl and 3.5 at 6.5 eV for K₀.₅Rb₀.₅Cl, respectively. The $\varepsilon1(\omega)$ curves later show an increase, followed by a decrease till a negative value are reached. Further, the curves followed later by a slow increase toward a zero value at high energy while registering minimum values around 11.5 eV and 10.8 eV for KCl and K₀.₅Rb₀.₅Cl, respectively.

Fig. 8a & b, shows the imaginary part of dielectric function for KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$. Due to the similar electronic band structure of KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$, it can be observed nearly the same line shapes for $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ in Fig. 8a & b. The dielectric function $\varepsilon2(\omega)$ indicates the fundamental absorption edge or threshold energy arising at 5.1 eV and 4.9 eV for KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$, respectively and these values match with the PBE-GGA bandgap of these materials. The fundamental absorption edge is a direct electronic transition between the conduction band minimum and the valence band maximum. Along with absorption edge, KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ shows two peaks that are located at 8.0 eV and 10.3 eV and 7.9 eV and 10.1 eV, respectively. These peaks arise mainly due to the transition from Cl-p states of valence band to unoccupied states of conduction band.

So as to search the possibilities of these compounds for photovoltaic conversion applications, the absorption coefficients in the visible spectrum has been studied. The absorption coefficient of any material defines its absorption capability and range concerning light or solar spectra. The absorption coefficient reveals the amount of electromagnetic waves with a specific energy that enters the material before being absorbed. Fig. 9a & b shows the absorption coefficient $I(\omega)$ for KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ compounds. It is calculated from the dielectric function and observed in the range around 5.1–13.5 eV and 4.9–13.5 eV for KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ respectively. It can be noticed that there is a very high absorption peak at high energies. For the cubic structure of KCl, the optical gap is around 5.1 eV, below this value there is no response from these materials, whereas for $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$, the optical gap is around 4.9 eV. The maximum absorption coefficient is found to 1.5% at 10.5 eV for KCl and 1.4% at 10.3 eV for $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$.

From these results, one can finally conclude that these materials possess considerable absorption at higher energies. The optical conductivity spectrum starts oscillating between increasing and decreasing like the absorption coefficient spectrum oscillating and with approximately same location of the peaks on the absorption spectrum. ### 3.4. Thermoelectric properties

In this section, thermoelectric properties of KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ has been discussed. Fig. 15a represents Seebeck coefficient vs temperature for KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$. Seebeck coefficient of KCl is of the order of 254 to 205 $\mu$V/K for the temperature range 50 to 100 K. When Rb is doped in K, Seebeck coefficient increases enormously at 50 K to 700 $\mu$V/K and then decreases drastically to 192 $\mu$V/K at 800 K. This value of Seebeck coefficient comparable to that of already reported cubic Heusler alloys [19,20]. ## 4. Conclusion
In this study, systematic investigation has been made on structural, electronic, optical and thermoelectric properties of KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$. The calculated lattice constant, bulk modulus and energy gap values are agreed very well with the available data in the literature. The electronic structure calculations show that both compounds have direct bandgap nature and exposed its decrease due to Rb doping. The optical properties of KCl and $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ alloys are determined by using the optical parameters such as dielectric constants, absorption coefficient, optical conductivity, extinction coefficient, energy loss function, refractive index and reflectivity are calculated for radiation up to 13.5 eV. The other important features of $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ are the maximum sigma at lower photon energy and the maximum electron $E_{loss}$ at higher energy. Due to the band matching with the incident photon energy, the direct band gap has a technological importance and also the presence of lowest sharp band in the conduction region and the maximum hybrid band in the valence region enhances the electron transport. Hence, the calculated electronic and optical properties of $K_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}Cl$ show that it is a better candidate for electronic devices and optical devices applications.
