# Anion photoelectron spectroscopy of transition metal- and lanthanide metal-silicon clusters: $\text{MSi}_{n}^{-}$ ($n$=6–20)

Kiichirou Koyasu, $^{1}$ Junko Atobe, $^{1}$ Shunsuke Furuse, $^{1}$ and Atsushi Nakajima $^{1,2,a)}$

$^{1}$ Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science and Technology, Keio University, 3-14-1 Hiyoshi, Kohoku-ku, Yokohama 223-8522, Japan
$^{2}$ CREST, Japan Science and Technology Agency (JST), c/o Department of Chemistry, Keio University, Yokohama 223-8522, Japan

(Received 26 June 2008; accepted 20 October 2008; published online 2 December 2008)

The electronic properties of silicon clusters containing a transition or lanthanide metal atom from group 3, 4, or 5, $\text{MSi}_{n}$, (M=Sc, Ti, V, Y, Zr, Nb, Lu, Tb, Ho, Hf, and Ta) were investigated by anion photoelectron spectroscopy at 213 nm. In the case of the group 3 elements Sc, Y, Lu, Tb, and Ho, the threshold energy of electron detachment exhibits local maxima at $n$=10 and 16, while in case of the group 4 elements Ti, Zr, and Hf, the threshold energy exhibits a local minimum at $n$=16, associated with the presence of a small bump in the spectrum. These electronic characteristics of $\text{MSi}_{n}$ are closely related to a cooperative effect between their geometric and electronic structures, which is discussed, together with the results of experiments that probe their geometric stability via their reactivity to $\text{H}_{2}\text{O}$ adsorption, and with theoretical calculations. © 2008 *American Institute of Physics*. [DOI: 10.1063/1.3023080]

## I. INTRODUCTION

The fabrication of cluster-assembled materials depends on finding a suitable building block for a cluster that is chemically stable and that interacts weakly with other clusters of the same material, and to this end, the building blocks of homogeneous or heterogeneous clusters have been extensively investigated in the gas phase for decades. $^{1–12}$ To ensure the chemical stability of a cluster, it is crucial to produce a closed electron configuration with a large energy gap between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO). In particular, the simultaneous satisfaction of electronic and geometric factors can enhance the stability of the clusters. The stability of clusters with closed electronic shells can be accounted for by several electron counting rules based on a jellium model: $^{13}$ Wade's rule of $2n$+2 skeletal electrons, $^{14}$ and so on. The favorable geometry of a close packed structure, such as the icosahedral structures with $n$=13,55,147,... is the other important factor determining geometric stability. $^{15}$ Thus exploiting the cooperative effects between electronic and geometric factors can provide a guiding principle for designing stable building-block clusters. $^{16}$

Binary clusters have additional advantages for designer chemistry, because the number of valence electrons and atomic radii can be tuned by suitable choices of the second doping element. Indeed, this advantage was first demonstrated by the formation of the $\text{Al}_{12}\text{B}^{-}$ cluster, $^{16(a)}$ which is actually stabilized by the B-atom doping, $^{17}$ rather than by the coincidental closures of electron (40 e) (Ref. 13) and geometrical (icosahedron) shells $^{15}$ in the superatom of $\text{Al}_{13}^{-}$. $^{18}$

There have been many examples of such binary clusters reported, such as $\text{WAu}_{12}$, $^{8}$ $\text{AlPb}_{12}^{+}$, $^{19}$ $\text{MSn}_{12}^{-}$, $^{20}$ and $\text{CuAu}_{16}^{-}$. $^{21}$

Keeping the symmetry of the structure high can be crucial for a large HOMO-LUMO gap, and two ways are conceivable to tune the properties of the binary clusters while retaining very symmetrical structure; by the substitution of the central atom or by creating caged atoms. Our recent studies have demonstrated these two methods of substitution in the magic numbered behavior of the metal encapsulated silicon clusters, $\text{ScSi}_{16}^{-}$, $\text{TiSi}_{16}$, and $\text{VSi}_{16}^{+}$, $^{22}$ which are stabilized by cooperative electronic and geometric factors—namely, the Frank–Kasper structure $^{23}$ and their electronic factor of 20 electrons. $^{24}$ The substitution of the central atom was intensively studied by systematically changing it for atoms taken from groups 3, 4, 5 (M=Sc, Y, Lu, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, and Ta), $^{25}$ and the $\text{MSi}_{m}$ clusters have been the topic of experimental and theoretical studies in recent years. $^{26,27}$ The other way, that is to substitute the elements of the cage itself was also examined by experimental and theoretical characterization of the systematic substitution of the group 14 cage elements, that is for the $\text{MSi}_{16}^{-}$, $\text{MGe}_{16}^{-}$, $\text{MSn}_{16}^{-}$, and $\text{MPb}_{16}^{-}$ (M=Ti, Zr, and Hf) clusters. $^{28}$

In this article, we present a set of systematic experiments that reveal the electronic properties of silicon clusters mixed with a metal atom of group 3, 4, or 5 elements as a function of the number of Si atoms, using anion photoelectron spectroscopy at 213 nm, i.e., using $\text{MSi}_{n}$, (M=Sc, Ti, V, Y, Zr, Nb, Lu, Tb, Ho, Hf, and Ta). Taking the results of the adsorption reactivity of $\text{H}_{2}\text{O}$ together with those from theoretical calculations, the size evolution of the electronic characteristics of $\text{MSi}_{n}$ will be discussed from the viewpoint of a cooperative effect between geometric and electronic structures.

## II. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Details of experimental setups for anion photoelectron spectroscopy and the chemical adsorption experiment have been described in detail elsewhere. $^{29}$ Briefly, the anions of the single metal-atom doped silicon cluster $(MSi_n^-: M$ = group 3 [(Sc, Y, Tb, Ho, Lu), group 4 (Ti, Zr, Hf), or group 5 (V, Nb, Ta)] were generated by the combination of dual-laser vaporization and molecular beam methods. Both the Si and the pure metal rods were independently vaporized by means of two frequency-doubled $Nd^{3+}$:YAG (YAG denotes yttrium aluminum garnet) lasers. Very intense supersonic helium gas pulses were employed to recombine and cool the laser-produced metal vapors. A high pressure Even–Lavie pulsed valve (10 Hz) (Ref. 30) was used at a stagnation pressure of 100 atm. In the photoelectron spectroscopy measurements of the mixed cluster anions, the clusters were accelerated to 900 eV. After the ions were mass-selected, a pulsed electric decelerator considerably reduced their kinetic energy before they entered the photodetachment region of a magnetic-bottle-type electron spectrometer. Then, the fifth harmonic (213 nm, 5.83 eV) of a pulsed $Nd^{3+}$:YAG laser was focused onto the mass-selected clusters to detach photoelectrons. The electrons were guided by a strong inhomogeneous magnetic field and subsequently with a weak guiding magnetic field, and detected by a microchannel plate (MCP). Their kinetic energy was analyzed by their time-of-flight (TOF), and the photoelectron kinetic energy was measured using a magnetic-bottle-type TOF electron energy analyzer, calibrated using the $Au^-\ (^2S_{1/2} \leftarrow ^1S_0)$ transition. $^{31,32}$ The photoelectron signal was typically accumulated to 20 000–40 000 laser shots.

To obtain information on the geometric structure of the binary clusters, their adsorption reactivity was measured using a flow tube reactor (FTR) (Ref. 33) combined with the cluster source. Water vapor diluted with 1.5 atm of He gas was used as the reactant gas and was injected into the FTR. The binary clusters were synchronously mixed with the reactant gas pulse in the FTR and reacted with the reactant molecule. The products of the clusters were mass-analyzed by the TOF mass spectrometer, and mass spectra of the clusters were measured before and after the reaction, in order to measure the relative adsorption reactivity.

## III. THEORETICAL CALCULATIONS

All the calculations of equilibrium geometries and vibrational frequencies of the $MX_{16}^{0/-}$ were performed at the density functional theory (DFT) level of theory, using the B3PW91 functional built into the GAUSSIAN 03 program package. $^{34,35}$ Large-core Stuttgart quasi-relativistic effective core potentials and basis sets were used to describe Si, Sc, Ti, Y, and Lu, while a polarization function of $\zeta(d)$=0.262 was also used for Si. $^{36,37}$ To compare with experimental results, the vertical detachment energy from the singly occupied molecular orbital (SOMO) was calculated using the $\Delta$DFT method. In addition, the band gap between the 1st and 2nd peaks of the anion photoelectron spectra, which corresponds to the HOMO-LUMO gap of the neutral cluster, was calculated with TD-B3PW91.

## IV. RESULTS

### A. Photoelectron spectra of $MSi_n^-$ anions

The mass distributions of the measured anions in this work were qualitatively in agreement with those reported previously, $^{22,25}$ in which magic number behavior was observed especially for $ScSi_n^-$ at $n$ =16 due to the cooperative effect between geometric and electronic structures.

## B. Adsorption reactivity of anionic $MSi_n^-$

In order to investigate the structure of $MSi_n$ clusters, the cluster size dependence of the adsorption reactivity towards $H_2O$ vapor was measured. As reported previously, $^{22,25}$ a chemical probe method is useful in deducing the structure of the $MSi_n$ clusters. Since the reactivity of $Si_n$ is generally much lower than that of a metal atom, the reactivity is sensitive to location of the doped metal atom; high and low reactivities exhibit an exterior and an interior metal atom, respectively. Namely, it is expected that a metal encapsulated structure of $MSi_n$ causes no reactivity owing to the inertness of a $Si_n$ cage, and hence the threshold cluster size for the suppressed reactivity of $MSi_n$ corresponds to the beginning size of the metal encapsulation.

It is also noted that our previous measurement on the reactivity of $TbSi_n^-$ included contaminations of terbium oxides ($Tb_3OSi_n^-$) due to coincident mass overlapping $^{22(c),22(d)}$. In this measurement, however, the laser vaporization of a metal rod was controlled not to produce metal clusters, together with the improvement of mass resolution.

The reactivity decreases with an increasing number of Si atoms, showing that the reactive metal-atom site is hidden by less reactive Si atoms. ## V. DISCUSSION

The size dependencies of the threshold energy for electron detachment and adsorption reactivity mirror the electronic and geometric stabilities of metal-atom-doped silicon cluster anions, $MSi_n^-$. Regarding the electronic and geometric stability of $MSi_{16}$, their distinctive behaviors have been discussed previously as a member of a class of superatoms; anionic $ScSi_{16}^-$, neutral $TiSi_{16}$, and cationic $VSi_{16}^+$, $NbSi_{16}^+$, and $TaSi_{16}^+$ were remarkably generated due to both the electronic and geometric closings. $^{22,25}$ Here, along with further discussion of $MSi_{16}^-$ derived from theoretical calculations, the electronic and geometric stability of $MSi_{10}^-$ and $MSi_{12}^-$ will be discussed in detail.

### A. $MSi_{10}^-$ clusters containing group 3 elements

As shown in Fig. 1, the threshold energy for electron detachment exhibits local maximum at $n$=10 for $MSi_n^-$ clusters containing group 3 elements, implying that the neutral $MSi_{10}$ clusters have high EA. No similar behavior can be seen in the PE spectra of the anionic $MSi_{10}^-$ clusters with group 4 and 5 elements. Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 4, the adsorption reactivity of group 3 anionic $MSi_{10}^-$ clusters are high, and it suggests that no particular geometric stability, such as metal encapsulation, occurs. These results show that the high $E_T$ of these clusters can be attributed mainly to their electronic structures, and that an excess electron completes an electronic closing at $n$=10. Since lanthanide metal atoms preferably take +3 oxidation states, $^{39}$ one plausible electronic configuration is $M^{3+}S_{10}^{4-}$, formed from a quadruply charged $Si_{10}^{4-}$ with a cationic metal atom.

A related magic numbered behavior is apparent in the high yield of $Si_{10}Cs_3^-$; indeed, the PE spectrum of $Si_{10}Cs_3^-$ is remarkably similar to that of $Si_{10}Sc_1^-$. $^{16(c)}$ This result also supports the idea of the combination of a quadruply charged $Si_{10}^{4-}$ with cationic metal atoms. Similarly, when Cs-atom doping, being capable of a chemical method to reveal electronic structures, was used to generate the ternary metallic cluster anion $ScSi_{10}Cs^-$, a behavior consistent with this viewpoint was observed. Figure 5(a) shows the PE spectra of $ScSi_{10}Cs^-$, where the Cs vapor was produced with a high-temperature pulsed valve ($\approx$440 K) [Ref. 16(c)] and injected into a reaction cell connected directly with the cluster source. The PE spectrum of $ScSi_{10}^-$ is shown again in Fig. 5(b) to show the Cs-doping effect on the electronic structure. The PE spectrum of $ScSi_{10}Cs^-$ is quite similar with that of $ScSi_{10}^-$ except for a small hump in low binding energy region. The gap between the first and second peaks in the spectrum of $ScSi_{10}Cs^-$ is large (1.67 eV). As in the case of $Al_{13}Cs^-$, $^{16(c)}$ this result indicates that electron transfer takes place from the Cs atom to $ScSi_{10}$, and that the $ScSi_{10}Cs$ neutral is electronically stable with a large HOMO-LUMO gap of 1.67 eV.

### B. The stability of $\text{MSi}_{12}^-$

As shown in Fig. 4, the adsorption reactivity of group 3 element-containing anionic $\text{MSi}_{12}^-$ clusters show local minima at $n=12$. Figure 1, however, reveals that the threshold energy for electron detachment shows a smooth size dependence around $n=12$ for group 3 $\text{MSi}_{n}^-$ clusters. The PE spectra suggest that the low reactivity for $\text{MSi}_{12}^-$ is attributable to a geometric packing effect. As discussed experimentally$^{42-44}$ and theoretically,$^{23,45-52}$ the stability of $\text{MSi}_{12}$ has been examined from the viewpoint of a metal-encapsulating silicon cage and from electron counting arguments. A plausible geometric structure has arisen from these studies: $\text{Si}_{12}$ hexagonal prism cages, in which 12 Si atoms form a cage with $D_{6h}$ symmetry with a transition metal atom at the center.$^{45,46}$ Electronically, on the other hand, a simple electron counting procedure, employing a simplified model of the cluster as a sphere with a transition metal atom being at the center, predicts that the most stable clusters are given by the "18-electron rule," which is especially useful for characterizing and predicting the stability of organometallic complexes of the Cr, Mn, Fe, and Co transition metal atoms.$^{39}$ The 18-electron rule was indeed used to explain the stability of $\text{MSi}_{12}^+$,$^{42}$ assuming that each silicon atom contributes one electron and a transition metal provides six valence electrons (such as Cr, Mo, and W). However, recent theoretical calculations on $\text{M@Si}_{12}$ shows the 18-electron rule to have only limited applicability. Furthermore, it has been predicted that Sc leads to a marginal cluster stability, while transition metals towards the middle in the periodic table clearly confer stability.$^{51}$ It is noteworthy that the PE spectra for $\text{MSi}_{n}^-$ cluster with group 5 elements showed no obvious size dependence around $n=12$. If the 18-electron rule was applicable, the threshold energy of the PE spectra should exhibit local maximum at $n=12$ for these clusters as well. The results of this work, therefore, suggest that the local minima of the reactivity at $n=12$ can be attributed mainly to the geometric factor.

### C. The cooperative stability of $\text{MSi}_{16}^-$

At $n=16$, as shown in Figs. 1 and 3, the threshold energy for electron detachment exhibits local maxima for $\text{MSi}_{n}^-$ clusters with group 3 elements but local minima for $\text{MSi}_{n}^-$ clusters with group 4 elements. Since the electronic characteristics of $\text{MSi}_{16}^-$ have been discussed in detail previously,$^{22,25}$ only a short description is given here, based on a cooperative effect between geometric and electronic structures.$^{23,24,47,53}$ Briefly, the spectral features of $\text{MSi}_{16}^-$ doped with group 4 elements of Ti, Zr, and Hf show a common small bump around 2.8-3.0 eV (labeled X). These features are markedly contrast to those for group 3 and group 5 element clusters, in which the detachment peaks start in the higher energy region without any such a separate peak. Our established technique of halogen atom doping$^{54}$ has verified that the peak X in the PE spectrum of $\text{TiSi}_{16}^-$ truly corresponds to a SOMO. This spectral pattern suggests that neutral $\text{TiSi}_{16}$ is a closed-shell molecule with a large HOMO-LUMO gap.

These spectral features were consistently reproduced by theoretical calculations at the DFT level of theory, based on the pioneering work by Kumar and Kawazoe.$^{23}$ Figure 6 shows a comparison between the experimentally observed and theoretically simulated PE spectra of $\text{TiSi}_{16}^-$, where the vertical detachment energy from the SOMO was calculated using the $\Delta$DFT method, and the band gap between the first and second peaks using TD-B3PW91. In the simulation, relevant Gaussian-broadened (full-width at half-maximum: 0.2 eV) electronic states are summed up to the electron bind-

ing energy of 4.7 eV; the second peak consists of seven trip- let and six singlet states. The Frank-Kasper structure pre- dicted by previous calculations $^{23,24}$ actually reproduces the observed PE spectrum well, as shown in Fig. 6. Note that the structure of $Ti@Si_{16}^{-}$ was actually slightly deformed from the Frank-Kasper structure due to Jahn-Teller distortions, because the extra electron occupies the degenerate LUMO of the Ti@Si $_{16}$ neutral.

The photoelectron spectra of $Sc@Si_{16}^{-}$ yielded a vertical detachment energy (VDE) of 4.25 eV and an adiabatic de- tachment energy of 3.41 eV, values much higher than those of other metal-doped silicon clusters. This pronounced sta- bility of $Sc@Si_{16}^{-}$ is attributed to the cooperative effect be tween geometric and electronic structures, producing a superatom. $^{16,18}$ As discussed previously, $^{25}$ there exists an op timum cavity size in which the metal atom can be encapsu-lated by the $Si_{16}$ cage. In addition, the cavity size of the $Si_{16}$  cage apparently depends on the charge state. For an anionic $Si_{16}$ cage, the threshold sizes of $ScSi_{n}^{-}, YSi_{n}^{-}$ , and $LuSi_{n}^{-}$  examined by adsorption reactivity were 15, 20, and 18, re- spectively, and $YSi_{16}^{-}$ and $LuSi_{16}^{-}$ still exhibited reactivity. Thus, a Y or Lu atom is too large to be encapsulated by the anionic $Si_{16}$ cage. The smaller threshold size of $LuSi_{n}^{-}(n$ =18) than that of $YSi_{n}^{-}(n=20)$ is explained by the respec tive atomic radii or ionic radii; (atomic radii) $1.78 \AA$ for Y and $1.72 \AA$ for Lu and (ionic radii for a coordination numberof 6) $0.900 \AA$ for Y and $0.861 \AA$ for $Lu^{55}$ The relatively stable $LuSi_{16}^{-}$ cluster can be actually reproduced by theoret ical calculations as follows.

In our calculations, the most stable $Sc@Si_{16}^{-}$ cluster adopts a Frank-Kasper structure ( $T_{d}$ symmetry), as reported by Reveles and Khanna. $^{24}$ The isomeric tetracapped hexago nal antiprism structure ( $C_{3 v}$ symmetry) is the second most stable, being 0.077 eV above the Frank-Kasper structure. For $YSi_{16}^{-}$ , the most stable structure is calculated to be of the fullerene type, having $C_{4 v}$ symmetry, rather than a Frank Kasper structure (0.035 eV above the fullerene type) or a tetracapped hexagonal antiprism one (0.051 eV above the fullerene type). For $LuSi_{16}^{-}$ , however, the most stable iso meric structure is again the Frank-Kasper structure, although the second most stable tetracapped hexagonal antiprism form is only 0.020 eV above the Frank-Kasper isomer. For LuSi, the fullerene structure is 0.084 eV above the Frank- Kasper form. Although both Frank-Kasper and tetracapped hexagonal antiprism isomers might coexist for $LuSi_{16}^{-}$ , the experimental VDE of 4.38 eV for the first peak in the PE spectrum of $LuSi_{16}^{-}$ is well reproduced by the Frank-Kasper structure (4.25 eV). In addition, the PE spectral features seem to be very similar between $ScSi_{16}^{-}$ and $LuSi_{16}^{-}$ com pared to those of the other clusters with group 3 elements $(YSi_{16}^{-}, TbSi_{16}^{-}$ , and $HoSi_{16}^{-})$ . Since the atom radius of Lu is the second smallest among group 3 elements (that of Sc is the smallest), $LuSi_{16}^{-}$ can be stabilized geometrically with the Frank-Kasper structure similarly to $ScSi_{16}^{-}$ among MSi, with a group 3 element.

## VI. CONCLUSIONS

Anion photoelectron spectroscopy for silicon clusterscontaining a metal atom taken from element groups 3, 4, or 5(M=Sc, Ti, V, Y, Zr, Nb, Lu, Tb, Ho, Hf, and Ta) has re- vealed the size-selective evolution of electronic structures. For the group 3 elements Sc, Y, Lu, Tb, and Ho, the thresh- old energy of electron detachment of $MSi_{n}^{-}$ exhibits local maxima at n=10 and 16, while for the group 4 elements Ti, Zr, and Hf, the threshold energy has a local minimum at n=16, as a result of the occurrence of a small bump in thespectrum. In each case, the adsorption reactivity of $MSi_{n}^{-}$  clusters towards $H_{2} O$ exhibits local minima at n=12 and 16, implying that the metal atom is geometrically trapped insidea Si cage. These results reveal that the high $E_{T}$ of group 3 MSi, clusters is attributable mainly to the electronic struc- ture, while the local minima in the reactivity at n=12 can be attributed mainly to the geometric factor. For $MSi_{n}^{-}$ , experi ments and theoretical calculations reveal that $Sc@Si_{16}^{-}$ ex hibits the most pronounced stability because of the coopera- tive effect between a Frank-Kasper geometric structure and a20-electron electronic structure, leading to a superatom.
