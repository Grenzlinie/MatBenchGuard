### Abstract

$\text{Li}_4\text{Ti}_5\text{O}_{12}$ (LTO) attracts great interest due to the "zero strain" during cycles but the poor electronic and ionic conductivity critically impede the practical application. Herein, we report a synergy strategy of tuning localized electrons to shift Fermi level and band gap by Mg/Zr co-doping and oxygen vacancies incorporation, which significantly improves $\text{Li}^+$ and electronic transport. More importantly, the intrinsic synergistic mechanism has been revealed by neutron diffraction, x-ray absorption spectra and first-principles calculations. The "elastic effect" of lattice induced by Mg/Zr co-doping allows LTO to accommodate more oxygen vacancies in a certain degree without a severe lattice distortion, which largely improves the electronic conductivity. Mg/Zr co-doping and oxygen vacancies incorporation effectively enhanced the dynamic characteristics of LTO electrode, achieving the excellent rate performance (90 mAh/g at 20C) and cycle stability (96.9% after 500 cycles at 10 C). First-principles calculations confirm Fermi level shifts to the conduction band, and the band gap becomes narrowed due to the synergistic modulation, and the intrinsic mechanism of the enhanced electronic and Li-ion conductivity is clarified. This study offers some insights into achieving the fast $\text{Li}^+$ insertion/extraction by tuning the crystal and electronic structure with lattice doping and oxygen vacancy engineering.

**Key words:** $\text{Li}_4\text{Ti}_5\text{O}_{12}$; anode material; band gap; Fermi level; synergistic mechanism

### 1. Introduction

Lithium ion batteries (LIBs) have been commercially used in portable electronics and even electric vehicles. But the demand for high performance electrode materials, which largely determine the performance of the batteries, has greatly increased. Common anode materials, such as graphite-based materials, silicon, metal oxides cannot meet large-scale commercial applications due to the slow reaction kinetics and safety problems caused by the excessive volume changes during charging/discharging¹.

Spinel lithium titanate ($\text{Li}_4\text{Ti}_5\text{O}_{12}$, LTO) attracts much interest due to its zero-strain characteristics during charge-discharge cycles. The extremely low volume expansion (<0.2%) during lithium ion insertion/extraction can ensure the long-term cycling stability². However, the poor intrinsic electronic conductivity and relatively sluggish ion diffusion coefficient of LTO lead to the inferior high-rate performance³. Therefore, it is necessary to adopt reasonable strategies to improve its electronic conductivity and ion diffusion capability. Some strategies including but not limited to nanostructures⁴⁻⁷, introduction of oxygen vacancies⁸⁻¹⁰, and ion doping¹¹⁻¹⁵ have been extensively applied. However, considering the toxicity/cost of the chemicals or materials used and the multi-step or complex synthesis route, a simple, environmentally friendly, economical and universal strategy still needs to be developed to meet the strict requirements of large-scale industrial applications. Oxygen vacancies generated by heat treatment in reducing atmosphere can induce partial reduction of Ti sites in LTO lattice, which can increase the electron concentration of the Ti3d orbit. Therefore, this results in more $\text{Ti}^{3+}/\text{Ti}^{4+}$ pairs and then leads to the improved electronic conductivity and rate performance⁶. From an industrial point of view, this method is considered to be a low cost and effective method. Nevertheless, excessive oxygen vacancies can cause severe lattice distortion, which hinders lithium ion migration and ultimately leads to a diminishment in $\text{Li}^+$ mobility in the LTO lattice¹⁶. Recently our group has reported the synergic effects of dual-doping of some substituents(Mg/Zr or Mg /La) in Li and Ti site on the rate capability and cycling stability, and unraveled the modification mechanism based on the crystal

and electronic structure analysis¹⁷, ¹⁸. The previous studies show that either single doping or dual doping strategy do have some positive effects on the electrochemical performance but the improvement is still limited. It is highly desirable to explore some more effective strategies to meet the requirements for the high-performance LTO. In addition, the intrinsic mechanism for the enhanced electronic/Li-ion conductivity and electrochemical performances still needs to be further clarified.

Herein, we report a strategy to tune the localized electrons to shift Fermi level and band gap by an integrated strategy of Mg/Zr co-doping and oxygen vacancies engineering which simultaneously improves the ion/electron transport. The anode material LTO improved by this synergy strategy has better electrochemical performance than single modification method. More importantly, the intrinsic modification mechanism of this synergistic strategy has also been revealed by means of neutron diffraction, X-ray absorption spectra, electron paramagnetic resonance, density functional theory (DFT) and so on. Mg/Zr co-doping induces the "elastic effect" of the unit cell, which allow for the accommodation of more oxygen vacancies without serious lattice distortion. The synergy of Mg/Zr co-doping and oxygen vacancy significantly enhance the electronic/ionic conductivity and the structural stability of LTO, which contribute to the large enhancement of the rate capability and cycling stability (a capacity retention of 96.9% after 500 cycles at 10 C). First-principles calculations also confirm that the synergy of Mg/Zr co-doping and oxygen vacancy incorporation shifts Fermi level to a conduction band and narrows band gap. This synergy strategy provides a new insight into exploring more advanced electrodes, which has potential application in the field of fast charging/discharging batteries.

### 2.1 Materials preparation

In a typical experiment, $\mathrm{Li_4Ti_5O_{12}}$ (LTO) and $\mathrm{Li_{3.8}Mg_{0.2}Ti_{4.9}Zr_{0.1}O_{12}}$ (LMTZO) samples were synthesized through a facile solid-state method. $\mathrm{Li_2CO_3}$ (Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd,

99.0%), TiO₂ (Aladdin, 99.8%, anatase), MgO(Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd, 99.0%), and ZrO₂ (Aladdin, 99.99%) were being used as the source of Li, Ti, Mg, and Zr, respectively. All the chemicals were weighted accurately according to the stoichiometric ratio. It was worth noting that an excess of 5 wt% Li₂CO₃ was weighted in order to compensate for the evaporation loss of lithium during calcination. After these chemical reagent powders were thoroughly mixed in an agate mortar, they were transferred to a box muffle furnace and heated at 850 °C for 12 hours. The heating rate was 5°C per minute. After cooling to room temperature, the samples LTO and LMTZO were synthesized. The two samples were then heat-treated under 10% H₂/90% Ar to obtain samples containing oxygen vacancies. The condition for this hydrogenation treatment was heating at 500 °C for 6 hours. The samples were marked as LTO-Ov and LMTZO-Ov.

### 2.2 Materials characterization

Additionally, the morphologies of as-prepared samples were investigated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Hitachi SU8010). The transmission electron microscope (TEM) and high-resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM) images were performed by a Tecnai G2 F20 S-TWIN operating at 200 kV. ### 2.3 Electrode preparation

Active materials were mixed with poly vinylidene fluoride (PVDF) and super P carbon black in the case of a mass ratio of 8:1:1. The mixture was slurried in N-methylpyrrolidinone (NMP). Then the slurry was pasted on Cu foil evenly and dried in a vacuum drying oven at 120 °C for 12h. CR2016 coin cells were assembled in a glove box filled with Ar using the as-prepared samples as work electrode and lithium metal as the counter electrode. These two electrodes were separated by polypropylene Celgard 2300 film. 1 mol/L $LiPF_6$ dissolved in a mixture of EC, DEC, DMC (volume ratio 1:1:1) was used as the electrolyte.

### 2.4 Electrochemical characterization

Galvanostatic charge/discharge tests and rate performances between 0.1C to 20C were carried out on the Neware Celltest system in the voltage of 1V-2.5V (vs. $Li^+/Li$). Cyclic voltammograms (CV) was recorded with the electrochemical workstation (PGSTAT302N, Autolab) at the scan rate of 0.1mV/s. Moreover, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was conducted from a frequency range of 0.1 to 100kHz at 5 mV of the perturbation using the same electrochemical workstation. The electronic conductivity of powders was also measured by the electrochemical workstation. For the galvanostatic charge/discharge tests and rate performances, different batteries were assembled for at least six tests. The EIS and CV tests were also performed at least four times to ensure the repeatability of the dada.

### 3.2. Electrochemical performance

To probe the effects of co-doping and oxygen vacancies on LTO, electrochemical tests were carried out. The rate performance of as-prepared samples was shown in Figure 2a. It is clear that LMTZO-Ov exhibits a superior discharge specific capacity compared with other samples. ### 3.3 DFT calculation results

### 4. Conclusions

To sum up, this work proposed a facile strategy to tune localized electrons to shift Fermi level and narrow band gap in $\mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{Ti}_{5} \mathrm{O}_{12}$ anode material by doping and vacancy, and the electrochemical performances have been largely enhanced. The intrinsic enhancement mechanism of the electronic and Li-ion conductivity is clarified. The cell elastic effect induced by Mg/Zr co-doping could allow for more oxygen vacancies in a certain range without severe lattice distortion, thus significantly improving the electronic conductivity and high lithium storage ability (170 mAh/g at 0.2C). Polarization and lithium-ion migration barrier of LTO materials were also reduced, which exhibited an excellent rate performance (90 mAh/g at 20C). In addition, this synergistic improvement makes the Ti-O bond length of the material shorter and improves the structural stability (capacity retention of 96.9% after 500 cycles at 10 C). DFT calculations indicate that the Fermi level is tuned to the conduction band and the band gap becomes narrowed
