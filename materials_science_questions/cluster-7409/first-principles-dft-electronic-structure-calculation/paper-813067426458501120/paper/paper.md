# Tuning the pure monoclinic phase of $WO_3$ and $WO_3$-Ag nanostructures for non-enzymatic glucose sensing application with theoretical insight from electronic structure simulations

Rajeswari Ponnusamy, $^{1}$ Abhijeet Gangan, $^{2}$ Brahmananda Chakraborty, $^{2,a)}$ and Chandra Sekhar Rout $^{1,3,b)}$

$^{1}$ School of Basic Sciences, Indian Institute of Technology, Bhubaneswar, Odisha 751013, India
$^{2}$ High Pressure and Synchrotron Radiation Physics Division, Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, Trombay, Mumbai 400085, India
$^{3}$ Centre for Nano and Material Sciences, Jain University, Ramanagaram, Bangalore-562112, India

(Received 27 October 2017; accepted 9 December 2017; published online 11 January 2018)

Here, we report the controlled hydrothermal synthesis and tuning of the pure monoclinic phase of $WO_3$ and $WO_3$-Ag nanostructures. Comparative electrochemical nonenzymatic glucose sensing properties of $WO_3$ and $WO_3$-Ag were investigated by cyclic voltammetry and chronoamperometric tests. We observed enhanced glucose sensing performance of $WO_3$-Ag porous spheres as compared to bare $WO_3$ nanoslabs. The sensitivity of the pure $WO_3$ nanoslabs is $11.1\ \mu\mathrm{A}\ \mu\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ whereas $WO_3$-Ag porous spheres exhibit sensitivity of $23.3\ \mu\mathrm{A}\ \mu\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. The $WO_3$-Ag porous spheres exhibited a good linear range $(5$-$375\ \mu\mathrm{M})$ with excellent anti-interference property. Our experimental observations are qualitatively supported by density functional theory simulations through investigation of bonding and charge transfer mechanism of glucose on $WO_3$ and Ag doped $WO_3$. As the binding energy of glucose is more on the Ag doped $WO_3$ (100) surface compared to the bare $WO_3$ (100) surface and the Ag doped $WO_3$ (100) surface becomes more conducting due to enhancement of density of states near the Fermi level, we can infer that Ag doped $WO_3$ exhibits a better charge transfer media compared to bare $WO_3$ resulting in enhanced glucose sensitivity in consistency with our experimental data. *Published by AIP Publishing.* https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5010826

## I. INTRODUCTION

The deviation of blood glucose concentration from the normal range of 80–120 mg/dL (4.4–6.6 mM) causes a chronic disease in humans, called diabetes mellitus by which over 387 million people are suffering globally. $^{1}$ In order to avert the serious issues such as disability in eye and kidney and heart diseases caused by diabetes, enormous efforts have been made to develop novel cost effective glucose sensors with high sensitivity, excellent selectivity, and fast response for self-monitoring the glucose level. Electrochemical detection of glucose is one of the widely investigated methods due to its easy detection principle, compact instrumentation, and involvement of simple electrochemistry of the material to understand the mechanisms involved in the sensing process. Technologically, a large number of nanoscale semiconducting materials such as ${\mathrm{ZnO}},{\mathrm{CuO}},{\mathrm{SnO}}_{2},{\mathrm{TiO}}_{2},$ and ${\mathrm{MoO}}_{3}$ are found to be good candidates for the development of both enzymatic and non-enzymatic based glucose sensors because of their nontoxicity, biocompatibility, and ease of growth. $^{2}$

Tungsten trioxide ($\mathrm{WO}_{3}$) is an important multi-functional n-type semiconductor and has gained widespread interest due to its promising applications in electrochromic, photochromic, and gasochromic devices. $^{3-5}$ Owing to its high stability and wide tunable band gap (2.6), $\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ has been considered as one of the ideal materials for electrocatalytic applications, photocatalytic water splitting, and dye adsorption. $^{6}$ Like other semiconductor metal oxides, $\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ also has structure, phase, size, and morphology dependent properties in various fields. Also, the properties of tungsten oxide can be controlled by tuning the oxidation state of W atom. Thus, synthesis routes like hydrothermal, template assisted, electrodeposition, and chemical vapor deposition methods has been adopted to fabricate $\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ with several kinds of nanostructures including particles, wires, sheets, plates, spheres, etc. $^{7-10}$

$\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ has the idealized and stable $\gamma$-monoclinic phase at ambient temperature with the corner sharing oxygen atoms of octahedral but scarcely orthorhombic phase is also predominant up to $500\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. Hence, much effort has been focused on the monoclinic $\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ to explore its multiple performances in various applications. Another foremost attribute of $\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ is its synthesis method and the temperature dependent polymorphs (monoclinic, orthorhombic, cubic, and tetragonal). Interestingly, some of the reports revealed the possible formation of h-$\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ during the hydrothermal synthesis despite its metastable nature. For example, Huang *et al.* have reported the controllable synthesis of h-$\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ nanorods by varying the experimental parameters during hydrothermal reaction and consecutively they studied the effect of $\mathrm{Na}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$ on morphological evolution. $^{11}$ An *et al.* witnessed the formation of h-$\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ nanorods during the preparation of graphene nanocomposites by hydrothermal route (temperature—$180\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$).$^{12}$ Similarly, Bai *et al.*$^{13}$ and Yao *et al.*$^{14}$ had found the crystallization of $\gamma$-monoclinic phase of $\mathrm{WO}_{3}$ while decorating the surface by $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$ and mixing with AgBr. In this study, we report the post-annealing effect on the crystal structure and morphology tuning of $WO_3$ prepared by the facile hydrothermal method. Based on the results, the relationship between the structural characteristics and the enhancement in glucose sensing performance of pure and Ag-doped $WO_3$ nanostructures was discussed briefly. This work also includes result from Density Functional Theory (DFT) simulations which provide insight from orbital interactions and charge transfer of glucose on $WO_3$ and Ag doped $WO_3$. Through the analysis of partial density of states (DOS), we demonstrate that due to charge transfer from Ag there is enhancement of density of states near Fermi level for W $d$ orbital which makes Ag doped $WO_3$ a better charge transfer media resulting superior glucose sensing performance.

## II. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

### A. Synthesis

$WO_3$-Ag porous spheres were synthesized by a simple hydrothermal route. Analytical grade sodium tungstate $(Na_2WO_4)$ and silver nitrate $(AgNO_3)$ were used as the source of tungsten and silver. Initially, a 30mM tungsten precursor solution was prepared by dissolving appropriate amount of $Na_2WO_4$ in 40 ml of deionized water under magnetic stirring. 5 wt. $\%$ of $AgNO_3$ was subsequently added to the above solution and dissolved. Then the pH of the as-prepared solution was adjusted to 2 by dropping 1M HCl under continuous stirring. The resultant pale yellow solution was transferred to a Teflon lined stainless steel autoclave of capacity 50 ml. The sealed autoclave was then placed in a hot air oven and maintained at $200\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 48 h. After cooling the autoclave naturally to room temperature, the final product was collected through washing with deionized water and ethanol several times followed by drying at $60\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 10 h. $WO_3$ nanoslab-like structures were also prepared by following the same procedure excluding the addition of $AgNO_3$.

The possible chemical reaction during the crystal growth of $WO_3$ in the hydrothermal synthesis process is as follows:

$$Na_2WO_4.2H_2O + 2HCl \rightarrow WO_3 + 2NaCl + 3H_2O. \tag{1}$$

### B. Characterization

The crystalline phase of the prepared sample was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a Bruker D8 Advanced diffractometer with Cu $K\alpha$ X-ray source. Morphology investigations and composition analysis were performed by field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM; MERLIN compact with a GEMINI I electron column, Zeiss Pvt., Ltd.) with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopic (EDAX) measurements. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were conducted in a three electrode electrochemical cell using a PG262A potentiostat/galvanostat (Technoscience, Ltd., Bengaluru). The working electrode was prepared on pre-cleaned nickel foam by drop casting $WO_3$ suspension (sonicated for 20 min) in ethanol. Platinum wire and saturated Ag/AgCl were employed as counter and reference electrodes, respectively. All the electrochemical tests were carried out in a 0.1 M aqueous solution of NaOH (supporting electrolyte, pH-13) at room temperature. Chronoamperometric (CA) measurement was performed by applying a potential of 0.45 V vs Ag/AgCl electrode under continuous stirring of electrolyte solution.

## A. Electrochemical glucose sensing

To evaluate the electrocatalytic activity of prepared WO₃ materials, cyclic voltammogram (CV) was performed initially in the potential range of 0 to +0.65 V for the working electrodes prepared on Ni foam [Fig. 5(a)]. A distinct pair of oxidation and reduction peaks were observed for all the samples, at the potentials around +0.47 V and -0.30 V. Additionally, a pair of new redox peaks were observed in WO₃-Ag samples, with the anodic peak potential at 0.3 V and cathodic peak at 0.05 V-(-0.03 V). These peaks can be ascribed to the redox coupling reactions of Ag. Ding et al. has noticed the analogous redox peaks in the NiO-Ag nanofibers, and they detailed that the oxidation of Ag⁰/Ag⁺ gives the anodic peak and during the cathodic scan, Ag⁺/Ag⁰ reduction is responsible for the peak.²⁴ Among all the prepared materials, a two-fold enhancement was observed in the case of annealed WO₃-Ag.

Further, to demonstrate their capability as electrochemical sensors, CVs were recorded in the presence of different concentrations of glucose ranging from 0 to 1000 μM at the scan rate of 20 mV s⁻¹. In Fig. 5(b), the increase in redox current with the increasing glucose concentrations (0, 100, 500, and 1000 μM) was clearly seen which evidences the efficient electrocatalytic activity of WO₃-Ag/500 °C towards the oxidation of glucose. For the better detection of nonenzymatic electrochemical glucose sensing by WO₃, a redox couple of W(VI)/W(VII) in the NaOH solution under the distinct potential range is crucial. However, as it is a stable material, before and after addition of glucose, the reaction mechanism behind the redox coupling can be depicted as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{WO}_{3}+\mathrm{OH}^{-}+\text {glucose}\left(\mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{12} \mathrm{O}_{6}\right) \\
& \quad \rightarrow \mathrm{H}_{x} \mathrm{WO}_{3}+\text { gluconolactone}\left(\mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{10} \mathrm{O}_{6}\right)+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}+e^{-} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

Before the addition of glucose, in a blank NaOH solution, an anodic peak is observed around +0.47 V due to protonation of WO₃ into HₓWO₃. The protonated WO₃ obtains an electron by the injection of glucose, and it can act as the electron delivery system to help the oxidation of glucose into gluconolactone [Eq. (2)] (Scheme 1). Ma et al. have discussed the possible redox reactions that occurred on the surface of the WO₃ during dopamine sensing.²⁵ With this, Ag too combines its Ag⁰/Ag⁺ redox reactions to take part in the glucose oxidation process. During this process, electrons will transfer from glucose to the electrode, and consequently there is an obvious increase in oxidation current depending on glucose concentration.

Further electrochemical response experiments were recorded at the identical conditions for different scan rates (8-100 mV s⁻¹) and the corresponding CV curves are depicted in Fig. 5(c). Variation of anodic and cathodic peak currents with respect to different scan rates were plotted and provided in Fig. 5(d). Both the peak current intensities were increased gradually and proportional to the square root of the scan rate (R² = 0.99) which confirms the reversible electron transfer in the WO₃-Ag/500 °C electrode by the diffusion controlled electrochemical process. For comparison, the electrocatalytic activity of as-prepared WO₃, as-prepared WO₃-Ag, and WO₃/500 °C was also tested towards glucose sensing and their dose dependent, scan rate dependent CV responses were given in Fig. S1 (supplementary material).

At the same time, the I-t measurements done at the lower potential of Ag oxidation (0.25 V) has no response to glucose addition. The average time required to reach the stable plateau for the as prepared samples are less than < 14 s. Instead, after annealing the response time towards glucose sensing is reduced to < 7 s which confirmed the faster response. Moreover, in Fig. 6(a), a linear increase of response current with increasing concentration of glucose was found at lower doses, and the deviation from linearity is observed at higher concentration of glucose. For the annealed sample, a linear region was obtained in the range from 5 μM to 375 μM (R²=0.99). From the slope, the sensitivity was calculated to be 23.3 μA μM⁻¹ cm⁻². The better sensitivity in the annealed WO₃-Ag is due to the combined effect of Ag and higher surface area. Further, it reveals that the monoclinic phase has great influence on the stable steady state sensing performance of the electrodes. ### B. Anti-interference test

To assess the anti-interference characteristics of WO₃-Ag/500 °C, in the presence of several sorts of interfering compounds including ascorbic acid, lactic acid, maltose, dopamine, and uric acid, I-t response was performed at the potential of 0.45 V. The amperometric response curve shown in Fig. S2 (supplementary material) depicted the stable current response for each dose of glucose at both lower and higher concentrations. But no significant current response was noticed for the additions of 20 μM ascorbic acid, lactic acid, maltose, dopamine, and uric acid. It clearly reveals the excellent selectivity of the WO₃-Ag/500 °C modified electrode towards the glucose sensing.

## IV. ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE SIMULATIONS

We have also performed thorough Density Functional Theory (DFT) simulations to study the bonding and charge transfer mechanism of glucose molecules on WO₃ and Ag doped WO₃ in light of their electronic properties to support our experimental observations.

### A. Computational details

All the calculations (structural relaxations and total energy calculations) have been done using the density functional theory based projector augmented wave (PAW) method as implemented in the VASP code.³³⁻³⁶ PAW based pseudo-potentials³⁷ have been used for W, Ag, O, C, and H with PW91 as the exchange-correlation functional. The cutoff energy is taken as 500 eV and the brillioun zone is sampled with a Monkhorst pack³⁸ mesh of 7 × 7 × 1 and 7 × 7 × 7 k-points for surface and bulk geometries, respectively. The convergence criterion of 0.01 eV/Å for Hellmann-Feynman forces and 10⁻⁶ eV for total energy was taken and all the simulations were performed within the spin-polarized density functional theory framework. The initial structure of α-glucose was optimized in a cubic cell of lattice parameter 20 Å which is sufficient enough to avoid any periodic interactions, as α-glucose is a molecule. For α-glucose molecule, the brillioun zone was sampled using only gamma points. We have considered the monoclinic phase of WO₃ as per our experimental samples.

Also, the presence of localized empty d states in the conduction band for W infers that it can take charge from glucose to oxidize it.

## D. Interactions of glucose molecule on $WO_{3}$ and $Ag$ doped $WO_{3}$ surface
To study the bonding and charge transfer mechanism of glucose on $WO_{3}$ and $Ag$ doped $WO_{3}$ surfaces, we introduce one glucose molecule and allow the system to relax. Figures 9(b) and 9(d) portray the relaxed structure of glucose on $WO_{3}$ and $Ag$ doped $WO_{3}$ surfaces, respectively. Glucose is bonded on the $WO_{3}$ surface with a binding energy of $-1.12\mathrm{eV}$ and the O of glucose molecule makes a bond with W of $WO_{3}$ with a bond length of $2.39\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$. The negative binding energy infers that the binding of glucose on the $WO_{3}$ surface is energetically favourable. The binding energy of glucose on $Ag$ doped $WO_{3}$ is $-1.3\mathrm{eV}$, and the bond length between O of glucose molecule and $Ag$ is $2.43\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$. So we can see that in the presence of $Ag$, glucose is bonded more strongly implying higher charge transfer from O of glucose.

Figure 11 displays the PDOS of $p$ orbital of the O atom of the glucose when it is in a glucose molecule (upper panel), attached to the $WO_{3}$ (100) surface (middle panel) and attached to the $Ag+WO_{3}$ (100) surface (bottom panel). For O atom in the glucose molecule, there are states at and around Fermi level which are partly missing when glucose is attached to signify that there is charge transfer from O $p$ orbital to the $WO_{3}$ (100) surface. Now, most of the occupied states are located deep in the valance band implying charge transfer from the top of the valance band.

As the binding energy of glucose is more on the $Ag$ doped $WO_{3}$ (100) surface compared to bare the $WO_{3}$ (100) surface, the $Ag$ doped $WO_{3}$ (100) surface becomes more conducting and there is enhancement in density of states near Fermi level for W $d$ orbital. We can infer that Ag doped $WO_{3}$ exhibits a better charge transfer media compared to bare $WO_{3}$ resulting enhanced glucose sensitivity in consistency with our experimental data.

## V. CONCLUSIONS
Nanoslab- and nanosphere-like structures of $WO_{3}/WO_{3}$-Ag were prepared by a simple hydrothermal method followed by annealing at $500\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. The electrochemical sensing performance of the prepared $WO_{3}$ towards the sensing of glucose was studied before and after annealing. $\gamma$-monoclinic $WO_{3}$-Ag with nanosphere-like morphology exhibited an improvement in sensitivity and also displays the satisfactory electrocatalytic activities like a short response time, wide linear range, and excellent specificity than $WO_{3}$ with the mixture of orthorhombic polymorph. It demonstrates that annealing not only provides the structural changes in $WO_{3}$ but also favours the superior sensing performance in glucose detection. DFT simulations predict that the glucose molecule interacts strongly with the $WO_{3}$ and $Ag$ doped $WO_{3}$ surfaces through charge transfer from O $p$ orbital of glucose and gets oxidized. Ag doping makes the system more conducting through enhancement of density of states near the Fermi level. So Ag doped $WO_{3}$ exhibits a superior charge transfer media compared to bare $WO_{3}$ resulting in enhanced glucose sensitivity and hence supports our experimental data. Overall results revealed the favourable construction of a $WO_{3}$ based high performance biosensing platform for the real-time glucose detection applications.
