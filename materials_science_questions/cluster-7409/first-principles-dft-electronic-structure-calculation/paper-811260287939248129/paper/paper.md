# First-principles study of the cubic $CaHfO_3$ (001) surface

Kun Yang*, Yanqing He, Li Yao, Yi Cheng, Guiqiu Wang
and Wenwen Xia

Institute of Computational Physics,

Received 13 November 2015
Revised 19 April 2016
Accepted 29 April 2016
Published 4 July 2016

The geometric and electronic structures of the cubic $CaHfO_3$ (001) surfaces have been studied using first-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Two different terminations, CaO- and $HfO_2$-terminated surfaces, were investigated. It has been found that Ca atom has the largest relaxation for both kinds of terminations, and the surface rumpling of the CaO termination is far more prominent than that of the $HfO_2$-terminated surface. Both the bandgaps of the CaO- and $HfO_2$-terminated surfaces were calculated to be smaller than that of the $CaHfO_3$ bulk. It was also shown that the CaO-terminated surface has a lower energy than the $HfO_2$-terminated surface.

Keywords: Surface structure; $CaHfO_3$; density functional theory; electronic structure.

PACS numbers: 73.20.At, 68.35.$-$p

## 1. Introduction

The $ABO_3$ perovskite compounds are among the most versatile metal oxide materials owing to their functionalities ranging from superconductivity to ferroelectricity, magnetism and catalysis. $^{1-4}$ In addition, the epitaxial strain engineering, interfacial control at perovskite heterojunctions and rotation in superlattices enable the tailoring of desirable properties of perovskite materials. $^{5-7}$ $CaHfO_3$ is an insulator belonging to the perovskite class, and $CaHfO_3$ is suitable for applications as a buffer layer or gate dielectric. $^{8}$ It was found that randomly distributed oxygen vacancies could be created by the addition of Sr to $CaHfO_3$. $^{9}$ $CaHfO_3$ also exhibits

strong visible luminescence upon nanostructuring and it could be integrated into devices for thermal imaging applications.¹⁰ Because of the excellent thermal stability and lattice match with SrTiO₃, CaHfO₃ is a good insulator material for use in SrTiO₃-based field effect devices.¹¹,¹²

Theoretical calculations of perovskite bulk CaHfO₃ have been reported on the structural, electronic and optical properties¹³,¹⁴ as well as the defect effect.¹⁵ The structure of CaHfO₃/SrTiO₃ interface was also investigated, and O vacancies were found to introduce interface metallicity.¹⁶ To better understand the properties of CaHfO₃ in its technological applications, the study on the structural and electronic properties of its surface is helpful. However, to our best knowledge, no theoretical study of CaHfO₃ surface has been reported. It is found that only the orthorhombic structure of CaHfO₃ in the bulk form has been stabilized until now. However, in the nanoparticle form, the CaHfO₃ with the cubic structure could be possibly stabilized.¹⁰ In this work, we discussed the (001) surface of cubic CaHfO₃ by means of first-principle calculations. Two different terminations, namely CaO and HfO₂ terminations are considered, and their structural, electronic and energetic properties are compared to each other. This cubic surface calculation might serve as model system for the CaHfO₃ surface investigation.

## 2. Computational Method and Surface Models

The density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out using the Vienna ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)¹⁷,¹⁸ within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), using the projector-augmented wave (PAW) method¹⁹ and a plane-wave basis set. A plane-wave energy cutoff of 500 eV and a $10^{-7}$ eV electronic convergence criterion were used throughout the calculations. Monkhorst-Pack²⁰ $k$-point scheme was used for the Brillouin zone integrations, and the Ca ($3s$, $3p$, $4s$), Hf ($5p$, $6s$, $5d$), and O ($2s$, $2p$) were treated as valence electrons. We also performed the Bader charge analysis²¹,²² on the optimized structures, which is based on a firm theoretical basis and is focused entirely on the charge density.

Cubic bulk CaHfO₃ is described by the space group $Pm\overline{3}m$ with the measured orthorhombically distorted pseudocubic lattice constant $a = 3.99$ Å.²³ Before starting the surface calculation, we first optimized lattice constant of cubic bulk CaHfO₃ with the $12 \times 12 \times 12$ $k$-point mesh. The obtained lattice constant is $a = 4.097$ Å, which is in agreement with previous calculated values of $4.190$ Å¹⁵ by GGA. This overestimation of the lattice constant is typical of GGA. The theoretical lattice constant $a = 4.097$ Å was used in the following surface calculations.

The surface structures were modeled by setting the symmetry as space group $P4/mmm$ with periodic boundary conditions. A schematic of the surface structures is presented in Fig. 1, which was generated by the VESTA visualization software.²⁴ Each surface unit consists of an 11-layer surface slab separated by a vacuum region. For the CaO-termination, the surface layer Ca atom and O atom relax to opposite directions, i.e., the Ca atom moves inward to the central layer while the O atom relaxes outward to the vacuum region. The relaxation of the surface layer Ca atom is 9.53% of the bulk lattice constant, which is the largest relaxation of all the atoms in both CaO- and HfO₂-terminated surface slabs. All the O atoms in CaO-terminated surface slab are found to relax toward the outside vacuum region, and in each of the five layers, the displacement of O atom is much less than that of the metal atom. While for the metal atoms, all the Ca atoms move toward the central layer whereas the Hf atoms relax outward to the vacuum. In addition, the Hf atom in the fourth layer rather than the fifth layer Ca atom presents the minimum displacement, but the difference between the absolute values of the two displacements is very small (0.03%). For the HfO₂-terminated surface, however, the largest relaxation is not on the surface layer atoms but on the second layer Ca atom, which relaxes outward (toward the surface layer) by 6.56%. It is also found that both Hf and O atoms in the surface layer relax toward the central layer with close displacements, which is in contrast to Ca and O atoms in the surface layer of CaO termination, where the Ca atom relaxes to the opposite direction with O atom.

In both surface cases, the metal atoms in the first, third and fifth layers relax toward the central layer, while those in the second and fourth layers move toward the surface layer. The relaxation magnitudes of each metal species decrease with the increase of the layer depth from the surface to the central layer. The relaxations

A comparison of the (001) surface atomic displacements of several perovskite oxides by theoretical methods is shown in Table 2. For both AO and $BO_2$ terminations of all these $ABO_3$ perovskites, the surface metal atoms move inward to the center, whereas the metal atoms of the second layer relax outward to the vacuum. The surface A metals of the AO-terminated surfaces show more prominent relaxations than the B metals of the second layer. The only exception is $CaZrO_3$, where the second layer Zr atom moves almost twice as much as the relaxation of the surface Ca atom. For the $BO_2$-terminated surfaces, except for $BaTiO_3$, the second layer A atoms rather than the first layer B atoms present the largest displacements. Among all these $ABO_3$ perovskite oxides, the second layer Ca atom of the $ZrO_2$-terminated $CaZrO_3$ surface shows the largest relaxation, which moves outward to the surface by $11.18\%$ of the theoretical lattice constant.

Since the atomic relaxation leads to the surface rumplings and the changes of the interlayer distance, an analysis of the surface relaxation parameters can provide additional insight into the surface structural properties. In Table 3, we show the surface rumpling parameters and changes of the interlayer spacing. Surface rumpling parameter $s$ is defined as the outward displacement of the surface layer O atom with respect to the surface layer metal atom. Parameter $\Delta d_{12}$ is the change of the surface and the second layers spacing, measuring with metal atoms $z$-coordinate, and $\Delta d_{23}$, $\Delta d_{34}$ and $\Delta d_{45}$ are similar to $\Delta d_{12}$ but between the second and third layers, between the third and fourth layers, and between the fourth and fifth layers, respectively. It can be seen from Table 3 that the surface rumpling parameter of CaO-termination is much higher than that of the $HfO_2$-termination. This is due to the large displacements difference between Ca and O atoms in the CaO-terminated surface layer. In previous theoretical studies of $CaTiO_3,^{25}$ $CaZrO_3^{26}$ and $SrHfO_3^{27,28}$ cubic (001) $ABO_3$ surfaces, the AO-terminated surfaces have also been found to have prominent surface rumpling than the $BO_2$-terminated surfaces. This may result from the lager ironic A-O bonding with respect to the more covalent B-O bonding. It is also found from Table 3 that the calculated parameters $\Delta d_{12}$ and $\Delta d_{34}$ are negative for both kinds of terminations, indicating that the distance between the first and second layers and the distance between the third and fourth layers become smaller by comparison with its bulk value. On the contrary, the distance between the second and third layers and the distance between the fourth and fifth layers ($\Delta d_{23}$ and $\Delta d_{45}$) expand for both terminations. Furthermore, the absolute values of interlayer spacing change decrease with the increase of the layer depth from $\Delta d_{12}$ to $\Delta d_{45}$.

### 3.2. Bader charges, band structure, partial density of states and charge density distribution

The Bader charges of all the atoms in the two CaHfO₃ surface structures and the bulk are calculated and are shown in Table 1. It is obvious that the atomic Bader charges in the surfaces only differ slightly from those in the bulk. The Bader charges of the first layer Ca atom in the CaO termination and first layer Hf atom in the HfO₂ termination decrease only 0.03e and 0.05e with respect to those in the bulk. The O atom in the second layer of the HfO₂-terminated surface exhibits the strongest Bader charge deviation, which is 0.11e larger than that in the bulk.

In Fig. 2, we show the calculated energy band structures of CaO- and HfO₂-terminated relaxed surfaces together with that of bulk cubic CaHfO₃. The Fermi level $E_F$ is chosen to locate at 0 eV and coincide with the top of the valence band. The calculated bandgap of cubic bulk CaHfO₃ is an indirect one with the valence band maximum (VBM) and the conduction band minimum (CBM) locating at $R$ and $\Gamma$ points, respectively [Fig. 2(a)]. The obtained bandgap of cubic bulk CaHfO₃ is 3.63 eV, which is comparable to the previous LDA result of 3.10 eV.¹³

For the CaO-terminated surface band structure in Fig. 2(b), the CBM is at $\Gamma$ point while the VBM is not at high symmetry point but at the point between $M$ and $\Gamma$ points, which is labeled as point $N$. Compared to the top valence bands of the CaHfO₃ bulk, a few new flat bands could be found on the top of the valence bands of CaO-terminated surface, indicating the emergence of the surface states. These surface states slightly narrow the bandgap from 3.63 eV of the bulk to 3.41 eV of the CaO-terminated surface. For the band structure of the HfO₂-terminated surface as shown in Fig. 2(c), the conduction band states tend to intrude downward into the upper part of the bandgap, especially at $\Gamma$ point, which implies that surface states appear at $\Gamma$ point. To further understand the electronic structure of the CaHfO₃ cubic bulk and the two different surfaces, we then calculated the partial density of states (PDOS) of the bulk, the CaO- and HfO₂-terminated CaHfO₃ surfaces, as shown in Figs. 3–5, respectively. For CaHfO₃ bulk, the top valence bands localized between $-4.3$ eV and the Fermi level are primarily from the O $p$-states as well as some minor contributions from Hf $p$- and $d$-states, indicating the covalent bonding between O and Hf in CaHfO₃. The lower conduction bands at 3.8 eV originates from Hf $d$- and Ca $d$-states but dominated by the Hf $d$-states, while the bands situated in the range of 5.3–6.2 eV are mainly from the Ca $d$-states. ### 3.3. Surface energy

In order to compare the energetic stability of CaO- and $HfO_2$-terminated surfaces, we calculated the surface energies $E_{\text{surf}}$ using the standard approach of the surface energy calculation$^{25,26,30}$ as:

$$
E_{\text{surf}}(X) = E_{\text{cle}} + E_{\text{rel}}(X), \tag{1}
$$

where $X$ denotes CaO- or $HfO_2$-terminated surfaces, $E_{\text{cle}}$ is the cleavage energy of the unrelaxed surface, and $E_{\text{rel}}(X)$ is the relaxation energy. Since the cleavage of $CaHfO_3$ (001) bulk leads to the simultaneous formation of both CaO and $HfO_2$ terminations, it is assumed that the relevant cleavage energy is the same for the two terminations:

$$
E_{\text{cle}} = [E_{\text{slab}}^{\text{unrel}}(\text{CaO}) + E_{\text{slab}}^{\text{unrel}}(\text{HfO}_2) - 11E_{\text{bulk}}]/4, \tag{2}
$$

## 4. Summary
In this work, the structural and electronic properties of ${\rm CaHfO_3}$ (001) surfaces with two possible terminations, ${\rm CaO}$ and ${\rm HfO_2}$, have been investigated using DFT calculations. We have found that the surface rumpling of CaO-termination is larger than that of the ${\rm HfO_2}$-terminated one. Ca atoms relax more remarkably than Hf and O atoms for both surface terminations. Compared with the bandgap of cubic bulk ${\rm CaHfO_3}$, the bandgap of ${\rm HfO_2}$-terminated surface reduces much more than that of CaO termination. Energy calculation reveals that the two kinds of surface terminations have relatively similar surface stabilities, with the CaO-terminated surface more stable than the ${\rm HfO_2}$-terminated one. The atomic relaxations and surface energies of ${\rm CaHfO_3}$ are also compared to several other cubic perovskites.
