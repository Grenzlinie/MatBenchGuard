## ABSTRACT

The dipole polarizabilities, second-order hyperpolarizabilities, and optical absorption spectra of $\text{MSi}_{12}$ clusters (M = Sc-Zn) have been studied by using the (time-dependent) density functional theory. The results demonstrate that the correlation functional exerts more remarkable influence on the dipole polarizability and second-order hyperpolarizability calculations than the exchange term. Referring to MP2 results, B3LYP provides poorer dipole polarizabilities but more reliable second-order hyperpolarizabilities than B3PW91 and mPW1PW91 functionals. Additionally, the tunable (hyper)polarizabilities and optical absorption spectra have been evidently observed, which are attributed to the strong hybridization between 3d orbitals of M and 3s, 3p states of Si.

## 1. Introduction

In recent years, the transition-metal doped silicon clusters ($\text{MSi}_{n}$) have sparked considerable interests due to the fact that silicon is the most important semiconductor material in microelectronics industry [1-4]. Not only do these clusters show high stabilities [5,6], but also their chemical and physical properties can be easily modified by doping with different transition-metal atoms. Therefore, they are the promising building blocks for assembling functional materials with tunable properties. Owing to the strong hybridization between d orbitals of M and 3s, 3p states of Si [7], it is reasonable to expect that their optical properties are M-dependent.

The dipole polarizability is one of the most important linear optical properties of atom, molecule, and small cluster as it is closely related to their size, shape, and electronic structures. It is also related to chemical hardness, the ionization potential, and electron delocalization volume [8]. Thus, the study of dipole polarizability is helpful to give an insight into the electronic structure and stability of a chemical system [9,10]. On the other hand, the second-order hyperpolarizability has important applications in optoelectronics [11]. For small clusters, the size effects exhibiting in (hyper)polarizability have been extensively studied to understand the evolution of geometrical and electronic structures [12-15]. However, to best of our knowledge, there are few reports which focus the attention on the M-dependent linear and nonlinear optical properties of small cluster. In this Letter, the widely studied clusters $\text{MSi}_{12}$ (M = Sc-Zn) were chosen as samples and the systematical investigations on their dipole polarizabilities, second-order hyperpolarizabilities, and the optical absorption spectra were performed based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations.

## 2. Computational details

The hexagonal prism (HP) was thought to be the most stable configuration for all $\text{MSi}_{12}$ clusters (M = Sc-Zn) [16-23]. Therefore, several HPs with different symmetries were considered as the initial structures to search the most stable configuration for all these clusters. The spin unrestricted B3LYP [24,25] exchange and correlation (XC) functional along with 6-311+G(2d) basis set was employed for all geometric optimizations. The harmonic vibrational frequency calculations were performed subsequently at the same computational level to confirm all geometries as the local minima. Based on the stable configuration of each cluster, the linear and nonlinear optical properties were studied. The dipole polarizability ($\alpha$) and second-order hyperpolarizability ($\gamma$) were calculated by means of finite-field (FF) method with an external electric field of 0.002 a.u. [26]. The self-consistent field (SCF) convergence criterion of $10^{-8}$ was strictly adopted. The symmetric transversal components of $\alpha$ ($\alpha_{\alpha\beta}$ and $\alpha_{\beta\alpha}$) and $\gamma$ ($\gamma_{\alpha\alpha\beta\beta}$ and $\gamma_{\beta\beta\alpha\alpha}$) were independently calculated to check the numerical stability. Three XC functionals, B3LYP, B3PW91 [24,27], and mPW1PW91 [27,28], were employed to examine the effects of exchange and correlation functionals on $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ calculations. The electron correlation effect on $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ calculations was also studied by comparing the second-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory (MP2) [29] results with those estimated using Hartee-Fock (HF) method. The basis set effects were taken into account by investigating two all-electron basis sets 6-311+G(2d) and 6-311+G(2df). Moreover, the optical

## Results and discussion

### 3.1. Structure

The lowest-energy configurations of $\text{MSi}_{12}$ clusters (M = Sc–Zn) are presented in Fig. 1. In previous studies, the ground state of $\text{ScSi}_{12}$ cluster was thought to be the $\text{Si}_{12}$ HP with a Sc atom encapsulated in the center [16,17]. But more recent result revealed that $\text{Si}_{12}$ cluster cannot completely enclose a Sc atom [18]. Therefore, we optimized several HPs with doublet and quartet spin states for $\text{ScSi}_{12}$ cluster. It was found that the most energetically favorable structure is the HP with a Sc atom at the bottom ($^2\text{A}$ state), which is 0.36 eV lower in energy than the one encapsulating a Sc atom in the center ($^4\text{A}$ state). It should be mentioned that in order to compare the optical properties with other $\text{MSi}_{12}$ clusters, only the HP encapsulating a Sc atom in the center was taken into consideration. The lowest-energy structure of $\text{TiSi}_{12}$ is found to be the distorted HP with $C_s$ symmetry and $^1\text{A}'$ state, which is in good agreement with previous result [33]. However, in Guo's study they thought the perfect HP ($D_{6h}$) with spin triplet state was the most stable configuration [18]. As a matter of fact, this configuration is proven to be a saddle point on the potential energy surface by our calculations. Similar situation is also found for the $\text{VSi}_{12}$ cluster. Our study discloses that the HP ($C_i$) with $^2\text{A}_g$ state is favored by $\text{VSi}_{12}$ cluster. The HP with higher symmetry ($D_{2h}$), which was the most stable configuration in Guo's finding [18], is found to be a transition state in our calculations. For $\text{CrSi}_{12}$ cluster, the HP with $D_{3d}$ symmetry and $^1\text{A}_{1g}$ state is the most stable configuration, which is consistent with previous result calculated with all-electron basis set but different from the pseudo-potential method [18,34]. In accord with the previous report [5], our results reveal that $\text{FeSi}_{12}$ favors the HP structure with $D_{3d}$ symmetry ($^1\text{A}_{1g}$ state). Despite the perfect HP ($D_{6h}$) was found to be the second stable structure for the $\text{FeSi}_{12}$ by Ma et al. [5], but our calculation shows that it is a transition state. The most energetically favorable configuration of $\text{NiSi}_{12}$ is the HP with $C_s$ symmetry and $^1\text{A}'$ state, which agrees well with previous study [19]. The lowest-energy structure of $\text{CuSi}_{12}$ cluster found by our study is a regular HP ($C_{2h}, ^2\text{A}_g$), which agrees well with the Xiao's result but different from others [18,21,35]. With respect to the $\text{ZnSi}_{12}$ cluster, the HP with $D_{3d}$ symmetry is found to be degenerate in energy with the one possessing $C_{3v}$, which was thought to be the lowest-energy structure in previous study [20]. However, above-mentioned two configurations are 0.32 eV higher in energy than the one with the Zn at the bottom of HP ($^1\text{A}_1$ state). This result confirms Guo's finding that $\text{Si}_{12}$ also cannot completely enclose a Zn atom [18]. Herein, we chose the isomer with $D_{3d}$ ($^1\text{A}_{1g}$) symmetry to study the optical properties of $\text{ZnSi}_{12}$ with the purpose of comparing with other clusters.

### 3.4. Optical absorption spectra
Based upon the TD-DFT methods, the optical absorption spectra of $MSi_{12}$ clusters ( $M=Sc-Zn$ ) in UV-Visible region were simulated and are shown in Fig. 3. The similarity of the results calculated using B3LYP and B3PW91 indicates that the correlation effect is negligible for the excited states calculation. Therefore, we only dis- cuss the spectra calculated using B3LYP functional. Both the earlier and latter 3d transition-metal doped $Si_{12}$ clusters such as $ScSi_{12}$ and $CuSi_{12}$ show long absorption tails, which are similar to the cases of GaAs and CdSe clusters because of the dangling bonds in their surface [45,46]. This fact further suggests that the Sc and Cu atoms can not completely terminate the dangling bonds of $Si_{12}$ cluster. According to Fig. 3, one can note that the major absorption bands for these clusters are mainly locating in the region shorter than 400 nm. The numbers and positions of the absorption band are remarkably varied with M, which clearly points out that their absorption spectra are M-dependent. Moreover, these major absorption bands have multitransition character. Each transition often consists of the molecular orbital pairs which are much lower than HOMO and higher than LUMO. Take $FeSi_{12}$ as an example, there are four major absorption bands, which are labeled as I, II,III, and IV in the order of decreasing wavelength (Fig. 3). The band I results from the excitation with wavelength of 426.5 nm, which contains the transitions between seven orbital pairs and the main one is the HOMO $-4 \to$ LUMO +3 (72.6%). The second band (II) is made up of three excitations and the strongest one is at381.9 nm. Four orbital pairs are found to make major contribution for this excitation and the dominant transitions are the HOMO - $7 \to$ LUMO +2 (43.9%) and HOMO $-3 \to$ LUMO +5 (49.2%). The band Ill is rather simple and the maximum oscillator strength of it is found at 356.3 nm, which is related to 11 orbital pairs and the major transition is the HOMO $\to$ LUMO +7 (26.4%). The fourth band (IV) consists of three main excitations. The strongest one(324.1 nm) arises from the transitions involved in more than 11orbital pairs. The transitions of HOMO $-10 \to$ LUMO +4 (15.9%) and HOMO $-7 \to$ LUMO +2 (15.6%) play a significant role. ## 4. Conclusions

The geometries, dipole polarizabilities, second-order hyperpo- larizabilities, and optical absorption spectra of the 3d transition- metal doped silicon clusters (MSi₁₂) have been systematically stud- ied within the (TD)DFT frameworks. Some main results and conclu- sions are summarized as follows:

(1) The DFT calculations confirmed that the most energetically favorable configurations of ScSi₁₂ and ZnSi₁₂ are not the HP with M encapsulated in the center. The lowest-energy struc- ture of TiSi₁₂ is the distorted HP with Cₛ symmetry and sin- glet spin state.

(2) Owing to the electronic correlation effect, the dipole polariz- abilities and second-order hyperpolarizabilities of MSi₁₂ clusters calculated using the HF method seriously deviate from those evaluated using MP2. Three DFT methods also systematically overestimate the dipole polarizabilities and underestimate the second-order hyperpolarizabilities. For dipole polarizability and second-order hyperpolarizability calculations, the errors among B3LYP, B3PW91, and mPW1PW91 are kept in constants for all these clusters. B3LYP gives larger dipole polarizability and second-order hyperpolarizability than the other two XC functionals. The large discrepancy between the results calculated using B3LYP and B3PW91 and the good consistence between the data given by B3PW91 and mPW1PW91 show that the dipole polarizabilities and second-order hyperpolarizabili- ties of these clusters are more sensitive to the correlation functional than the exchange one. The dipole polarizabilities and second-order hyperpolarizabilities of these clusters show evident M-dependent effect, due to the strong hybrid- ization between 3d orbitals of M and 3s, 3p states of Si. Moreover, the dipole polarizability calculations confirmed that Wigner-Witmer rule should be taken into consideration for the binding energy calculation of those clusters.

(3) The optical absorption spectra simulated using the TD-DFT methods also are M-dependent because of the participation of 3d electrons in electronic excitation. The major absorption bands of these clusters often relate to the charge transferring between M and Si₁₂ cluster.
