## Abstract
$\text{Eu}^{3+}$ doped $\text{Mg}_2\text{Al}_4\text{Si}_5\text{O}_{18}$ phosphors were prepared by the solid-state reaction. The first principles calculation, XRD, diffuse reflection spectra, photoluminescence spectra and thermal quenching are carried out to investigate the electron structure, crystal structure, photoluminescence and thermal properties. The calculation results show that $\text{Mg}_2\text{Al}_4\text{Si}_5\text{O}_{18}$ possesses the direct band gap of about 5.1 eV. With the introduction of $\text{Eu}^{3+}$, the energy gap becomes obviously smaller due to the discontinuity of exchange-correlation energy. Between HOMOs and LOMOs, a series of f orbitals of $\text{Eu}^{3+}$ can be observed, which is the essential condition to realize the efficient 4f-4f transitions of $\text{Eu}^{3+}$. The experimental results indicate that the samples can efficiently absorb the UV-light and emit the red light with the highest peak at 614 nm. With the co-doping of $\text{Bi}^{3+}$ as sensitizer, the emission intensity of $\text{Eu}^{3+}$ increases by about 49% due to the energy transfer between $\text{Bi}^{3+}$ and $\text{Eu}^{3+}$. With the introduction of $\text{Li}^+/\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ as charge compensators, the emission intensities of $\text{Mg}_2\text{Al}_4\text{Si}_5\text{O}_{18}$: $\text{Eu}^{3+}$ are enhanced by about 22%, 18% and 5%. With the temperature increase, the emission intensity of all samples,

especially the $Bi^{3+}$ doped samples, dramatically declines, which reveals two important effects: one is that the $Eu^{3+}$ emission is very sensitive to temperature, and other is that the complex energy transfer process among the excited and ground states of $Eu^{3+}$, $Bi^{3+}$ or host absorption occurs. To explain the degradation, the mechanism based on the configurational coordinate diagram is proposed and this model could be helpful for the understanding of the energy transfer mechanism among the various energy levels in the process of temperature change.

Keywords: Optical Material; Luminescence; Optical property

### 1. Introduction
In the last few decades, inorganic luminescent materials/phosphors have undergone a rapid development because of a wide range of applications for white-light emitting diodes, vacuum fluorescent displays, plasma display panels, fluorescent lamp and field emission displays, etc. [1-5] Especially, the phosphors with high efficiency, low cost and environment friendly composition attract widespread attention. Generally, to meet the practical application demands, two or more phosphors are required in the device production. However, the multi-phosphor strategy usually suffers from some problems, such as the lower energy efficiency, different light output degradation rates leading to the color aberration with the working period and temperature increase and trade-off in luminous efficiency induced by re-absorption among the different phosphors, etc [6]. In commercial phosphors, these problems are particularly serious for red phosphors, which drastically restrict the application of the corresponding devices in everyday life. Therefore, it is urgent to search for novel highly efficient red phosphors and figure out the physical processes related to thermal effects.

At present, among the available photoluminescence materials, the rare-earth doped silicate phosphors are well known for their high efficiency and good chemical stability. [7-13] Page 3: As an extremely versatile material, cordierite silicate $Mg_2Al_4Si_5O_{18}$ (MASO) has been widely used as a high quality refractory material, integrated circuit board, catalyst carrier, ceramic foam and aviation material due to its good thermal shock resistance, dielectric properties, fire resistance and mechanical properties. [14, 15] MASO possesses the orthorhombic structure in space group Cccm (66). In the structure, the unique $Mg^{2+}$ site is coordinated by six $O^{2-}$. $Si^{4+}$ and $Al^{3+}$ are surrounded by 4 $O^{2-}$ and form tetrahedrons. Until recent times, MASO was not immerged as a potential luminescence host. In 2014, we have reported the photoluminescence (PL) properties of $Dy^{3+}$

doped MASO and found self-luminous phenomenon due to the oxygen vacancies in this host. [16,
17] However, the information about the electron structure, PL and thermal properties of $Eu^{3+}$
activated MASO still remains unknown. Therefore, in this study, the first principles calculations of
the band structure and density of states in MASO and $Eu^{3+}$-doped MASO are implemented. Then,
we synthesized a series of $Mg_{2-x-y}Al_4Si_5O_{18}: xEu^{3+}, yA$ ($A = Bi^{3+}, Li^{+}, Na^{+}$ and $K^{+}$) phosphors by
the solid state method and carefully investigated the effect of sensitizer $Bi^{3+}$ and different charge
compensators $Li^{+}$, $Na^{+}$ and $K^{+}$ on PL intensities. Especially, by the configurational coordinate
diagram, we have interpreted the temperature sensitive PL behaviour and the energy transfer
mechanism among the various energy levels with the increasing temperature in detail.

2. Experimental

2.1. Materials and synthesis

Stoichiometric amounts of $(MgCO_3)_4{\cdot}Mg(OH)_2{\cdot}5H_2O$ (99%), $Al_2O_3$ (99%), $SiO_2$ (99.9%),
$Eu_2O_3$ (99.999%), $Bi_2O_3$ (99%), $Li_2CO_3$ (99%), $Na_2CO_3$ (99.5%) and $K_2CO_3$ (99%) the raw
materials were thoroughly mixed in an agate mortar by using ethanol. To compensate the high
temperature carbonate volatilization, the additional 10wt% of $Li_2CO_3$ (99%), $Na_2CO_3$ (99.5%) or
$K_2CO_3$ (99%) was added. Firstly, the mixtures were heated up to $600\ ^\circ C$ for 6 h and, then, they
were reground and further fired at $1400\ ^\circ C$ for 10 h in an alumina crucible in a muffle furnace.
Finally, the samples were slowly cooled to the room temperature and the white powders were
obtained.

2.2. Measurements and characterization

The XRD patterns of the synthesized samples were recorded using a Rigaku D/Max-2400 X-ray
diffractometer with Ni filtered CuKa radiation (XRD). The diffuse reflection spectra (DRS) were
obtained by a UV/visible spectrophotometer (Perkin-Elmer Lambda 950) using $BaSO_4$ as a
reference in the range of 240-700 nm. The PL, photoluminescence excitation (PLE) and quantum
efficiency of the samples were measured using an FLS-920T fluorescence spectrophotometer
equipped with a 450 W Xe light source and double excitation monochromators. All the
measurements were performed at room temperature. Thermal quenching was tested using a
heating apparatus (TAP-02) in combination with PL equipment. The electronic structure
calculations for MASO and MASO: $Eu^{3+}$ were carried out with DFT method in CASTEP code.
The projector augment wave (PAW) pseudopotentials were adopted to describe the interactions of

elements, where the valence configurations of O, Mg, Al and Si were $2s^22p^4$, $3s^2$, $3s^23p^1$ and $3s^23p^2$, respectively. The Perdew–Burke–Ernzerof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was selected. In setting up the CASTEP run, basic parameters were chosen as follows: The kinetic energy cutoff = 380 eV, fast Fourier transform (FFT) grid dimensions = 45 × 90 × 48, space representation = reciprocal, SCF tolerance = $2 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/atom.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Phase identification

In Figure 1(a), all the observed diffraction peaks are well attributed to the $\text{Mg}_2\text{Al}_4\text{Si}_5\text{O}_{18}$ phase (JCPDS No. 82-1541), indicating that the doping ions do not cause significant changes of the host structure. Figure 1(b) presents the structure diagram of MASO. Cordierite MASO, with an ideal composition $\text{Mg}_2\text{Al}_4\text{Si}_5\text{O}_{18}$, is a Mg aluminisilicate. It belongs to orthorhombic structure (space group Cccm) with four molecules in the unit cell. In the structure, there is only one $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ site coordinated by $6\ \text{O}^{2-}$. $\text{Si}^{4+}$ and $\text{Al}^{3+}$ are both surrounded by $4\ \text{O}^{2-}$ and form tetrahedrons. Similar to that in beryl, the Si/Al tetrahedrons form the $\text{Si}_6\text{O}_{18}$-type 6-membered rings where one Al substituted for one Si in the ring. The Si atoms in the rings are bonded to two oxygen atoms in the ring and to two oxygen atoms in layers above and below the ring. There is no direct bonding between rings, but they are linked by Mg in octahedra and by Al in tetrahedra above and below. [18] When doping ions are introduced into the MASO host, in principle, it could be incorporated in three cation sites. The relative differences in ionic radii [16-18] $(\text{Dr}(\%) = 100\times[\text{R}_\text{m}(\text{CN})-\text{R}_\text{d}(\text{CN})]/\text{R}_\text{m}(\text{CN}))$ between matrix cations and doping ions can be obtained from Table 1. The comparison of ionic radii mismatch points to $\text{Mg}^{2+}$-sites as the most probable positions for the doping ions. In addition, according to the reports of Sai [19] and Tang [20] the $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ (0.72 Å) in MASO can be replaced by bigger ions $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (1.00 Å), $\text{Sr}^{2+}$ (1.18 Å) and $\text{Ba}^{2+}$ (1.35 Å) to form the solid solutions. Therefore, it is possible that the $\text{Eu}^{3+}$, $\text{Bi}^{3+}$, $\text{Li}^+$, $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{K}^+$ are introduced into the MASO host. In the $\text{Eu}^{3+}$- and $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-doped MASO, although the charges of the doping ions $\text{Eu}^{3+}$ and $\text{Bi}^{3+}$ do not matched to that of $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, the XRD patterns show that the obtained samples are still the single phase. The charge balance could be reached by the cation vacancy formation as followed:

$$3\text{Mg}^{2+}=2\text{Eu}^{3+}+\text{V}_\text{Mg}"$$

$$3\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}=2\mathrm{Bi}^{3+}+\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Mg}}{}''$$

In the $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$-doped and charge compensator ($\mathrm{Li}^{+}$, $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ and $\mathrm{K}^{+}$) doped MASO, the charge balance can be reached by the following equations:

$$2\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}=\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}+\mathrm{Li}^{+}$$

$$2\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}=\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}+\mathrm{Na}^{+}$$

$$2\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}=\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}+\mathrm{K}^{+}$$

### 3.2. Band structures and density of states

The density functional theory calculations of MASO are shown in Figure 2. MASO possesses the direct band gap of about 5.1 eV between the highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMOs) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LOMOs) at the G point of the Brillouin zone. The electronic structure of the HOMOs originates predominantly from O p states, whereas the LOMOs is composed mostly of Si/Al p and s states - that is, the charge transfer transition of $\mathrm{Si/AlO_4}$ group mainly contribute to the host absorption of MASO. These calculation results demonstrate that MASO can provide a broad band gap to contain the energy levels of $\mathrm{Ln^{3+}}$, which usually makes $\mathrm{Ln^{3+}}$ suitable for acting as the emission center.

### 3.3. DRS of the typical samples

As shown in Figure 3, the DRS of MASO indicates a remarkable drop in reflection over the UV range from 200 to 350 nm due to the host absorption. The experimental band gap is estimated as about 4.9 eV, which is close to the calculated value of 5.1 eV. Compared with MASO, the drops in reflection of both $\mathrm{Eu^{3+}}$-doped and $\mathrm{Eu^{3+}}$, $\mathrm{Bi^{3+}}$ co-doped MASO become deeper because of the additive effects of the charge transfer band (CTB) of $\mathrm{Eu^{3+}}$-$\mathrm{O^{2-}}$, [22] and the two new weak diffuse reflection peaks at 393 and 464 nm appear resulting from the f-f transitions of $\mathrm{Eu^{3+}}$ ($^7\mathrm{F_0}$-$^5\mathrm{L_6}$, $^7\mathrm{F_0}$-$^5\mathrm{D_2}$). For $\mathrm{Eu^{3+}}$, $\mathrm{Bi^{3+}}$ co-doped MASO, the reflection band extends to the longer wavelength, possibly resulting from the $6\mathrm{s^2}$-$6\mathrm{s6p}$ transition of $\mathrm{Bi^{3+}}$, [23] which is consistent with the excitation

spectrum. From the DRS measurements, we can conclude that the introduction of $Eu^{3+}$ and $Bi^{3+}$ into the MASO host enhances the optical absorption in the near ultraviolet region, and the sample MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}, 0.03Bi^{3+}$ has the largest optical absorption.

### 3.4. PLE and PL spectra of MASO: $Eu^{3+}, Bi^{3+}$

As shown in Figure 4 (a), under the excitation of 325 nm, the MASO host shows the blue emission centered at 425 nm, which originates from oxygen vacancy and has been carefully discussed in the previous work. [16] There is an obvious spectrum overlap between the PL spectrum of MASO and the PLE spectrum of MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$, which implies that the energy transfer (ET) could occur between the host and $Eu^{3+}$. When monitoring at 614 nm, the PLE spectrum of MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$ contains both a broad band centered at about 280 nm due to the combination of host absorption and CTB of $Eu^{3+}$-$O^{2-}$ and several sharp lines ascribed to characteristic f-f transitions of $Eu^{3+}$. Under the excitation of 393 nm, the PL spectra display typical $Eu^{3+}$emission peaks at 578 ($^5D_0$-$^7F_0$), 589, 598 ($^5D_0$-$^7F_1$), 614 ($^5D_0$-$^7F_2$), 654 ($^5D_0$-$^7F_3$) and 703 nm ($^5D_0$-$^7F_4$), respectively. Although most of the valence electrons of trivalent rare-earth elements are shielded by 5s and 5p outer electrons, a few transitions of the trivalent lanthanides are sensitive to the lattice environment and these are called as hypersensitive transitions. According to the Judd-Ofelt theory, the electric dipole transition $^5D_0$-$^7F_2$ is hypersensitive, and the emission intensity is strongly influenced by ligand ions in the crystals. [24] If $Eu^{3+}$ occupies an inversion symmetry site, a dominant reddish orange emission from the magnetic transition $^5D_0$-$^7F_1$ will be obtained. In opposite case, the electric dipole transition $^5D_0$-$^7F_2$ will predominate in the PL spectra. Therefore, the intensity ratio of $^5D_0$-$^7F_2$ to $^5D_0$-$^7F_1$ is usually regarded as a measure of site symmetry around $Eu^{3+}$. In our case, the dominated red emission of 614 nm is attributed to the electric dipole transition $^5D_0$-$^7F_2$, and the ratio of $^5D_0$-$^7F_2$ to $^5D_0$-$^7F_1$ transitions was calculated to be 6.0. It indicates that the local symmetry around $Eu^{3+}$ in the host MASO is non-centrosymmetric. Meanwhile, the abnormal higher intensity of the $^5D_0$-$^7F_4$ transition is observed in the PL spectra, which also reveals the $Eu^{3+}$ local symmetry distortion. [25] The inset exhibits the intensity of 614 nm as a function of $Eu^{3+}$ content. Obviously, the optimum dopant concentration of $Eu^{3+}$ in MASO is 3.5% (x = 0.07), which is referred to as "critical concentration". In general, concentration-quenching effect is due to the non-radiative ET process between the neighboring $Eu^{3+}$ when the $Eu^{3+}$ concentration reaches a critical value and it is useless for photoluminescent

applications. Therefore, the critical distance of $Eu^{3+}$-$Eu^{3+}$ is an important parameter to evaluate the PL properties. Blasse suggested that the critical distance $R_c$ can be estimated by the critical concentration of the activator ion [26, 27]:

$$
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{c}} \approx 2\left(\frac{3 \mathrm{~V}}{4 \pi \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{c}} \mathrm{N}}\right)^{1 / 3}
$$

where V is the unit cell volume, $x_c$ is the critical concentration of the activator ion, and N is the number of cations per unit cell. For the MASO host, $\mathrm{N}=8, \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{c}}=0.035$, and $\mathrm{V}=1550.48 \mathring{A}^3$. Thus, the $R_c$ value was calculated to be about $21.95 \mathring{A}$. Figure 4 (b) and (c) shows the PLE and PL spectra of MASO: $0.07 \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}, \mathrm{xBi}^{3+}(0 \leq \mathrm{x} \leq 0.07)$. With the introduction of $\mathrm{Bi}^{3+}$, the broad excitation band at 280 nm gradually red-shifts due to the absorption of $\mathrm{Bi}^{3+}$, and all the PLE peaks of $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ are greatly enhanced. Corresponding with the PLE spectra, the PL intensity of $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ also sharply increases due to the ET between $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ and $\mathrm{Bi}^{3+}$. The sensitized phenomenon of $\mathrm{Bi}^{3+}$ to the other rear earth ions has been also reported in many hosts. [28] Figure 4 (d) exhibits the intensities of 614 nm as a function of $\mathrm{Bi}^{3+}$ content. Obviously, the optimum $\mathrm{Bi}^{3+}$ concentration in MASO: $0.07 \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}, \mathrm{xBi}^{3+}$ is at $\mathrm{x}=0.03$ and the PL intensity of MASO: $0.07 \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}, 0.03 \mathrm{Bi}^{3+}$ increases by about $49 \%$ in reference to that of MASO: $0.07 \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$.

### 3.5. PLE and PL spectra of MASO: $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}, \mathrm{A}^{+}(\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{Li}, \mathrm{Na}, \mathrm{K})$

Figure 5 illustrates the PLE spectra monitored at 614 nm and PL spectra excited at 393 nm of MASO: $0.07 \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}, 0.07 \mathrm{~A}^{+}(\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{Li}, \mathrm{Na}, \mathrm{K})$. It is seen that the PLE and PL spectra of all the samples exhibit the similar shape, while the PL intensities of the samples with different charge compensator are different. The phenomenon is mainly caused by two reasons: One is the charge compensation effect of $\mathrm{A}^{+}$incorporation, which eliminates the vacancy and decreases the distortion of $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ environment in crystal lattice, and that consequently increases the numbers of $\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$ working as the luminescent centers in the host; The other is the different ion radii of $\mathrm{A}^{+}\left(\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}\right.$ $+\mathrm{A}^{+}=2 \mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ ) which may optimize the sub-lattice structure around the luminescent center ions. [29, 30] From the inset of Figure 5 (b), it is obvious that $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$is the optimal charge compensator, that could be explained by the similarity of $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$and $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ radii. The PL intensities of MASO: $0.07 \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}, 0.07 \mathrm{~A}^{+}(\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{Li}, \mathrm{Na}, \mathrm{K})$ is $1.22,1.18$ and 1.05 times of that of MASO: $0.01 \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}$, respectively. In addition, the effect of alkali carbonates as flux could also be not a negligible reason of the enhanced PL intensity. [31]

### 3.7. Thermal properties analysis

A comprehensive understanding of the thermal property in phosphor applications is indispensable whether many devices suffer from thermal problems or the application in temperature-sensitive luminescent probes. Numerous investigations have discussed thermal quenching behavior. [32-34] Two competing factors are prevailing, one is the activation energy of non-radiative relaxation, the other is the rate of temperature induced direct tunnelling, which prevents emissive transition between excited state and the ground state of the different activator ions in the host. [35]

The thermal quenching behaviour of MASO: $0.07\text{Eu}^{3+}$, MASO: $0.07\text{Eu}^{3+}$, $0.07\text{A}^{+}$ (A = Li, Na, K) and MASO: $0.07\text{Eu}^{3+}$, $0.03\text{Bi}^{+}$ is measured from 20 to 250 ℃. In Figure 6, with the increasing temperature, the PL intensities of all the samples gradually decline under the excitation of 393 nm.

For MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$, the PL intensity at 614 nm at 250 ℃ decreases to 34% of the initial value.

For MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$, $0.07A^{+}$ (A = Li, Na, K), the PL intensities at 614 nm at 250 ℃ decrease to 32%, 38% and 35% of those at 20 ℃, respectively, which are very similar to that of MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$. But for MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$, $0.03Bi^{+}$, the PL intensity at 614 nm at 250 ℃ decreases more strongly up to 16% of the initial value at 20 ℃. The results indicate that the PL intensity of $Eu^{3+}$ in MASO is very sensitive to temperature change, and the co-doping by $Bi^{3+}$ increases the thermal sensitivity of PL behavior. Generally, for the $Eu^{3+}$ with f-f transitions, the departure $\Delta$R from the ground state to the excited states along the R axis is approximately equal to 0, which leads to the higher energy barrier from the excited states to ground state, and further results in the better thermal stability. But in our work, the effect is opposite. Therefore, we can infer that a complicated ET process among the excited and ground states of $Eu^{3+}$, $Bi^{3+}$ or the CTB of Eu-O could occur with the increase of temperature. Based on the configurational coordinate diagram shown in Figure 7, we have proposed an underlying mechanism to explain the phenomenon. The curves $^{7}F_{J}$, g and $6s^{2}$ are the ground states of $Eu^{3+}$, host absorption and $Bi^{3+}$, respectively. The curves $^{5}D_{J}$, e and 6s6p are the excited states of $Eu^{3+}$, host absorption and $Bi^{3+}$, respectively. The points A, B and C are the lowest positions of the excited states $^{7}F_{J}$, e and 6s6p. To simplify the discussion, we assume that the crossing points among the excited states and ground states of $Eu^{3+}$, host absorption and $Bi^{3+}$ can be expressed by only one capital letter. The points M and N are the crossing points of g, $6s^{2}$ and e, 6s6p. The points $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ are the crossing points of e, 6s6p and $^{5}D_{J}$. $\Delta E_{1}$, $\Delta E_{2}$ and $\Delta E_{3}$ are the energy differences of B to $P_{1}$, B to M and A to $P_{1}$, respectively. $\Delta E_{4}$, $\Delta E_{5}$ and $\Delta E_{6}$ are the energy differences of C to $P_{2}$, C to N and A to $P_{2}$, respectively. Under the excitation of the UV light, electrons are excited from $^{7}F_{J}$, g and $6s^{2}$ to $^{5}D_{J}$, e and 6s6p. At the room temperature, for $Eu^{3+}$, most of the electrons return to the ground states by radiative transitions to obtain the red characteristic emitting. For the host absorption or $Bi^{3+}$, the electrons of e or 6s6p would very likely overcome the energy barrier $\Delta E_{1}$ or $\Delta E_{4}$ under the electron-phonon coupling, and transfer energy to $Eu^{3+}$ along the pink way ① or orange way ③, resulting in the enhancing of the $Eu^{3+}$ PL intensity. For the host absorption and $Bi^{3+}$, the departure $\Delta$R from the ground state to the excited states is usually bigger, which leads to the lower position of the M and N, i.e., the smaller $\Delta E_{2}$ and $\Delta E_{5}$. With the increase of temperature, for the samples without $Bi^{3+}$, on one hand, the electrons of excited state e would very likely overcome the energy barrier $\Delta E_{2}$ to direct tunnel

to the ground state g, and consequently decrease the electron number of excited state e, further lead to the decline of energy transfer from host absorption to $Eu^{3+}$. On the other hand, the electrons in $^{5}D_{0}$ level of $Eu^{3+}$ would also possibly overcome the energy barrier $\Delta E_{3}$ to back tunnel to the excited state e, and then overcome the energy barrier $\Delta E_{2}$ to return to the ground state of $Bi^{3+}$ through the points $P_{1}$, B and M along the green way ② under the stronger electron-phonon coupling. Thus, the samples show an obvious temperature sensitive PL behavior of $Eu^{3+}$. By comparing the PLE spectra of the samples with and without $Bi^{3+}$, we have come to a conclusion that the excited states $6s^{2}$ of $Bi^{3+}$ are lower than that of host absorption e, resulting in the lower position of $P_{2}$, i.e., the $\Delta E_{6}$ is smaller than $\Delta E_{3}$. Therefore, for the $Eu^{3+}$, $Bi^{3+}$ co-doped samples, electrons are more likely to overcome the energy barrier $\Delta E_{6}$ to tunnel back to 6s6p and then overcome the smaller energy barrier $\Delta E_{5}$ to return to $6s^{2}$ through the points $P_{2}$, C and N along the blue way ④ under the stronger electron-phonon coupling.

### 4. Conclusions

In summary, the red phosphor MASO: $Eu^{3+}$ with $A^{+}$ (A=Li, Na, K) as charge compensators and $Bi^{3+}$ as sensitizer were explored by the solid state reaction. The calculation results show that MASO possesses a direct band gap of about 5.1 eV. With the introduction of $Eu^{3+}$, the energy gap becomes obviously smaller due to the discontinuity of exchange-correlation energy. Between HOMOs and LOMOs, a series of f orbitals of $Eu^{3+}$ can be observed, which is the essential condition to realize the efficient 4f-4f transitions of $Eu^{3+}$. Upon 393 nm excitation, MASO: $Eu^{3+}$ can emit the red light with the highest peak at 614 nm ascribed to the $^{5}D_{0}-^{7}F_{2}$ characteristic transition of $Eu^{3+}$. The comparative analysis of PLE spectra of the host and MASO: $Eu^{3+}$ indicates that the ET could occur between the host and $Eu^{3+}$. With $Bi^{3+}$ as sensitizer into MASO: $Eu^{3+}$, the PL intensities are enhanced due to the ET from $Bi^{3+}$ to $Eu^{3+}$ and the PL intensity at 614 nm increases by about 49% than that of MASO: $Eu^{3+}$. With the $A^{+}$ (A=Li, Na, K) as charge compensators into MASO: $Eu^{3+}$, the PL intensities are also enhanced because of eliminating the vacancy and making $Eu^{3+}$ easier into lattice. The PL intensities at 614 nm of MASO: $0.01Eu^{3+}$, $0.01A^{+}$ (A=Li, Na, K) are 1.22, 1.18 and 1.05 times of that of MASO: $0.01Eu^{3+}$, respectively. With the temperature increase, the PL intensities of all samples gradually decline. For MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$, the PL intensity at 614 nm at 250 ℃ decreases to 34% of the initial value. For MASO: $0.07Eu^{3+}$, $0.07A^{+}$ (A = Li, Na, K), the PL intensities at 614 nm at 250 ℃ decrease to 32%, 38%, 35% of

those at 20 °C, respectively, which are very similar to that of MASO: 0.07Eu³⁺. But for MASO:
0.07Eu³⁺, 0.03Bi⁺, the PL intensity at 614 nm at 250 °C drops to 16% of the initial value at 20 °C.
These results reveal the two important information: one is the PL intensity at 614 nm of Eu³⁺ is
very sensitive to the temperature, the other is the complicated ET phenomenon among the excited
and ground states of Eu³⁺, Bi³⁺ or host absorption might occur. Based on the configurational
coordinate diagram, an underlying mechanism is proposed to explain the phenomenon reasonably.
This model could be helpful for the understanding of the energy transfer mechanism among the
various energy levels in the process of temperature change as reference.
