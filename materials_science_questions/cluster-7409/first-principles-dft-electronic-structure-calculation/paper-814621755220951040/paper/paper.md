## A B S T R A C T

This study aimed at investigating the interaction of $V_2O_5$ species with $TiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$ supports to understand the effect of supports on SCR reaction. Analysis by XRD, BET, UV–vis, and DFT theoretical calculations, XPS, EPR and in situ DRIFT showed that the two kinds of supports could interact with $V_2O_5$. The interaction of electron excitation and charge transfer of supports to $V_2O_5$ species was important to the formation of the reduced $V_2O_5$. These aspects increased the formation of superoxide ions that could improve the NO oxidation over $V_2O_5/TiO_2$. It was responsible for the higher SCR catalytic activity of $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ than $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3$.

### 1. Introduction

Nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) has been a major environmental concern related to the air quality because these cause the photochemical smog, acid rain, ozone depletion and greenhouse effects [1]. The primary nitrogen oxide produced from combustion processes with fossil fuel is NO and $NO_2$ is less than one tenth of NO in the total nitrogen oxides emission from stationary sources such as thermo-electric power plant [2]. Selective catalytic reduction (SCR) of $NO_x$ to $N_2$ using $NH_3$ as a reductant is now considered to be the most effective process for the treatment of stack gases from stationary sources, and the well-known industrial catalyst for this process is based on the vanadia system [3,4]. The catalysts also contain $WO_3$, or $MoO_3$, that are supported, together with $V_2O_5$ as the catalytic active phase [5]. The SCR catalysts of vanadia system have good sulfur-poisoning resistance for de-$NO_x$ in $SO_2$-containing gases. Therefore, vanadia catalytic system has been applied as an industrial catalyst for many years [6].

$TiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$ were important support in the research of chemistry and materials. In the field of study for SCR catalysts, many catalysts take $TiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$ as support. The former contains $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ [6–8], $MnO_x/TiO_2$ [9], and the latter contains $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3$ [10], $MnO_x/Al_2O_3$ [11], $CuO/Al_2O_3$ [12]. Performance of SCR catalysts for these studies can be promoted by increasing the active phase loading or modifying the active phase characteristics. In addition, many studies improve the performance of SCR catalysts by modifying the support characterization, such as $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3–TiO_2$ [13], $V_2O_5/TiO_2–SO_4^{2-}$ [14], $V_2O_5/F–TiO_2$ [15]. However, comparative studies and impaction on SCR reaction for different supports were seldom reported and it is only for the aspect of the total active sites [16]. Therefore, it is meaningful to investigate the interaction of $TiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$ supports with $V_2O_5$ that is active phase using as industrial catalyst, to understand the comparative effect of $TiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$ supports on SCR reaction.

It has been reported that the SCR catalytic activity of $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ was higher than $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3$ and it was attributed to the $NO_2$ formation [17]. It is worth noting that more studies [18–20] are revealing that NO oxidation to reactive nitrite species and $NO_2$ species groups are the intermediates to improve the SCR reaction. Thus, the NO oxidation is the key process. Our previous study found that in the SCR reaction, superoxide ions could improve the formation NO oxidation and the reduced $V_2O_5$ were preferential formation sites of superoxide ions [15,21–23]. Therefore, the aim of this study is to investigate the interaction of $TiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$ with $V_2O_5$ from the aspect of reduced $V_2O_5$ formation. In this study, we found that the support could interact with $V_2O_5$, and the electron

## 2. Experimental

### 2.1. Catalyst preparation

The $TiO_2$ support was prepared as described previously [15] and the $\gamma-Al_2O_3$ were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. The $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ or $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3$ catalyst was prepared by an impregnation method, specifically as follows: The ammonium metavanadate was first dissolved in 50 mL deionized water at $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Then the $TiO_2$ or $Al_2O_3$ was added under conditions of constant temperature and stirring. After 1 h, the mixture was dried at $120\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 6 h and subsequently calcined at $500\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 2 h. Finally, the $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ or $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3$ catalyst was obtained and it was further referred to as VxTi or VxAl. For example, V1.2Ti meant the catalyst with 1.2 wt% $V_2O_5$ loadings over $TiO_2$ support.

### 2.2. Catalytic activity tests

The SCR of NO was carried out at $120-390\ ^\circ\text{C}$ in a fixed-bed flow reactor (i.d. 6.8 mm) containing catalyst sample (100-120 mesh) under atmospheric pressure. The used mass of the catalyst was 0.3000 g with quartz sand as inert. The gas flow was controlled by mass flow meters. The reaction temperature was measured by a type K thermocouple inserted into the catalyst bed. The reactant gas consisted of 500 ppm NO, 600 ppm $NH_3$, 5% $O_2$ and balanced $N_2$ at a flow rate of 100 mL/min for accurately reflecting the NO conversion over the test catalysts. Each catalyst sample was adsorbed by the reaction mixture before the reaction for avoiding errors caused by NO adsorption on the catalyst. All of the catalysts were kept on stream at each temperature for 1 h. The NO and $NO_2$ concentration in the inlet and outlet gas was measured by a MRU-VARIOPLUS flue gas analyzer (Germany).

### 2.3. Computational details

All the calculations were based on DFT, and were performed using Material Studio 5.5 modeling DMol3. Double-numerical-quality basis set with polarization functions (DNP) and GGA-PBE were used for all calculations. Core electrons are treated with DFT semicore pseudopotentials (DSPP). Spin polarization is also applied and the real space cutoff radius was maintained as 4.5 Å. The Brillouin zone was sampled using a Monkhorst Pack grid to ensure the convergence of the whole systems. The vacuum region was set to 20 Å and the convergence criteria for the self-consistent field energy and displacement were set to $1\times10^{-6}$ Ha and $5\times10^{-3}$ Å, respectively.

### 2.4. Characterization

The phase constitution and crystallite size of the samples were determined by X-ray diffractometry (Purkinje General Instrument Co., Ltd, China, XD-3). The surface property of specific surface area was determined by $N_2$ adsorption at $-196\ ^\circ\text{C}$ by the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) method in a V-Sorb 2800 series (Gold App, China). Diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (DRS) was carried out on a Shimadzu UV-2550 UV-vis spectrophotometer. $BaSO_4$ was the reference sample and the spectra were recorded in the range of 200-800 nm. Electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were measured with an amplitude of the applied sine wave potential of 5 mV and a frequency of 0.1 Hz to 106 Hz. XPS experiments were carried out on a RBD upgraded PHI-5000C ESCA system (Perkin Elmer) with Mg K$\alpha$ radiation ($h\nu=1253.6$ eV), calibrated by the C 1s peak at 284.6 eV. The electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) measurements were made at room temperature using a Bruker EMX-10/12-type spectrometer in the X-band. In-situ DRIFTS experiments were carried out by a Nicolet IZ10 FTIR spectrometer, equipped with a liquid-nitrogen-cooled MCT detector. 32 scans were averaged for each spectrum, which were recorded at a resolution of $4\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. The background was recorded with a KBr wafer in $N_2$ at $300\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 60 min. Prior to each experiment, the wafer of fine catalyst powder was pretreated at $300\ ^\circ\text{C}$ in high purified $N_2$ for 2 h to eliminate surface impurity. After cooling to $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the sample was exposed to a controlled stream of 500 ppm NO + 5% $O_2$ and the adsorption spectra were collected for various times by subtraction the corresponding background reference.

### 3.2. Microstructure properties analysis

Fig. 3 displays the X-ray diffraction patterns of different samples. All the peaks of TiO₂, V1.0Ti and V1.2Ti samples calcined at 500 °C were anatase (PDF#21-1272), while rutile (PDF#21-1276) was not observed. It suggested that TiO₂ sample and V₂O₅/TiO₂ catalysts consisted of anatase as the unique phase, as the low loadings of V₂O₅ species over TiO₂ support. As the V₂O₅ loadings increased to 1.5%, part of the anatase TiO₂ transferred to rutile phase over the V1.5Ti catalyst. Alemany et al. found that the increase of V₂O₅ loadings could improve the formation of the rutile TiO₂ over the V₂O₅/TiO₂ catalysts [24]. Therefore, this observation indicated that the V₂O₅ could enhance the formation of rutile TiO₂. It also suggested that V₂O₅ could interact with anatase TiO₂. More studies [25,26] were revealing that the solid-state reaction between V₂O₅ and anatase TiO₂ includes two steps: mixing of the two oxides and calcination resulting in chemical interaction of V₂O₅ with the TiO₂ surface and V–O–Ti bond formation. It can also be seen from the Fig. 3 that all the peaks of Al₂O₃ sample was due to the γ-Al₂O₃ [27]. In addition, the intensity of the γ-Al₂O₃ peak of V1Al, V1.2Al and V1.5Al catalysts decreased significantly. It indicated the V₂O₅ species had an interaction with the Al₂O₃ support over the V₂O₅/γ-Al₂O₃ catalysts. In the studies of V₂O₅/Al₂O₃ catalysts, many researchers had confirmed that, as the V₂O₅ supported on Al₂O₃, the interaction could take place between V₂O₅ and Al₂O₃ as V–O–Al bond [28,29]. Furthermore, any other crystalline phase containing V₂O₅ phase (PDF#41-1426) could not be detected over the VTi and VAl catalysts, suggesting that V₂O₅ species were well-dispersed over the VTi and VAl catalysts. Therefore, the dispersion of V₂O₅ might not affect the NO conversion for the VTi and VAl catalysts. Furthermore, the surface area of V1.2Ti and V1.2Al catalysts was also detected. The surface area of V1.2Ti and V1.2Al was 24.2 and 98.1 m²/g, respectively. However, the SCR catalytic reaction of V1.2Ti was much higher than V1.2Al. Therefore, the surface area might not affect the NO conversion for the VTi and VAl catalysts.

It is worth noting that this new peak showed the blue shift compared with the V₂O₅ sample, furthermore, this absorption was not observed over the Al₂O₃ sample. Therefore, the V₂O₅ species had interaction with the Al₂O₃ support over the VAl catalyst. For the VTi catalyst, the TiO₂ absorption showed the blue shift compared with TiO₂ sample. It indicated that V₂O₅ species also had interaction with the TiO₂ support over the VTi catalyst. This observation might be due to that electron conjugated number decreased by the electron-withdrawing effect of V₂O₅ species.

### 3.4. Surface DFT calculations (theoretical section)

In the field for molecular simulation of supported catalyst, increasingly more studies are focusing on the (001) anatase surface of $TiO_{2}$ and (100) surface of $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}$ for molecular simulation. Therefore, the (001) anatase surface of $TiO_{2}$ and (100) surface of $\gamma$-$Al_{2}O_{3}$ were chosen to model the support. In addition, considering the proposal of Giakoumelou et al., monolayer V species were predominant when the V loading was less than 6 wt % [32]. Thus we only consider the monolayer V model and its coordination with the $TiO_{2}$ surface. Therefore, the models of VTi and VAl were built (Fig. 5). After structural optimization, for the VTi model, the bond length range of V-OTi was from 1.78 to 1.79 Å, the bond length of $V=O$ was 1.61 Å and the bond length range of VO-Ti was from 1.88 to 1.90 Å. While for the VAl model, the bond length range of V-OAl was 1.75 Å, the bond length range of $V=O$ was 1.64 Å and the bond length range of VO-Al was 1.85 Å. $TiO_{2}$ support of VTi model and $Al_{2}O_{3}$ support of VAl model had no obvious relaxation. Furthermore, the bond length of VO-Ti for VTi model was obviously shorter than the bond length of TiO-Ti for $TiO_{2}$ support, and the bond length of VO-Al for VAl model was also shorter than the bond length of AlO-Al for $Al_{2}O_{3}$ support. These results indicated that the V species was an electron withdraw group over VTi and VAl models. Therefore, as the presence of free electrons on the VTi catalyst, the electrons were more likely to draw to the V species.

For the analysis the difference of electron donating of $TiO_{2}$ and $Al_{2}O_{3}$ to $V_{2}O_{5}$ species, the electron density maps of VTi and VAl models were calculated (Fig. 6). It can be seen from Fig. 6a that the electron density of Ti atoms around $V_{2}O_{5}$ species had a tendency of position deflection to $V_{2}O_{5}$ species. In addition, the electron deficiency area of I, II and III area was larger than IV area and there was a much lower electron deficiency area in I, II and III area that were around $V_{2}O_{5}$ species. Therefore, these results indicated that $V_{2}O_{5}$ species were electron withdraw groups compared with $TiO_{2}$ support. Fig. 6b shows the electron density maps of VAl model. It can be seen from Fig. 6b that the electron density of Al atoms around $V_{2}O_{5}$ species also had a tendency of position deflection to $V_{2}O_{5}$ species, however, the tendency was weaker than Ti atoms. Furthermore, compared with Ti atoms, the electron density of Al atoms was low (Red color area of Al atoms was low). It was related to the outer-shell electronic structure of Ti $(3d^{2}4S^{2})$ and Al $(3S^{2}3P^{1})$. Therefore, the results from the comparison of Fig. 6a and b suggested that the electron donating capability of the amount of electrons for the $TiO_{2}$ support was stronger than $Al_{2}O_{3}$.

### 3.5. $H_2$-TPR, XPS and EPR analysis

Fig. 9 shows the temperature-programmed reduction (TPR) profiles of the catalysts using $H_2$ as the reductant. There were two partially overlapped peaks at around 470 and $536\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ detected on VAl catalyst, which were related to the successive reductions of $V_2O_5\rightarrow V_2O_4$ and $V_2O_4\rightarrow V_2O_3$, respectively [35]. However, two main reduction peaks of VTi catalyst were shifted to 504 and $592\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$. Therefore, the VTi made the reduction peaks of $VO_x$ species shift to higher temperature region, compared with VAl catalyst. It indicated that the $VO_x$ species itself over the VTi catalyst might have a stronger reducibility to limit the reduction reaction with $H_2$ than VAl catalyst. To confirm the $VO_x$ species reducibility, the XPS was carried out to investigate the valence states of the various species of the VTi and VAl catalysts.

Fig. 10a shows the XPS spectrum of V2p3/2 for VAl catalyst. The two peaks at 517.1 and 516.4 eV were attributed to $V^{5+} + 2$p3/2 and $V^{4+} + 2$p3/2, respectively, suggesting that V mainly existed as the +5 state and the reduced V species ($V^{4+}$) were few over the $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3$ catalyst. The deconvoluted XPS spectra showed that the $V^{4+}/V^{5+}$ ratio was 0.22. Fig. 10b shows the XPS spectrum of V2p3/2 for VTi catalyst. It can be seen that the amount of the $V^{4+}$ species increased. The deconvoluted XPS spectra showed that the $V^{4+}/V^{5+}$ ratio was 0.35. Therefore, the reduced $V_2O_5$ could more easily form over the $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ catalyst. This observation was consistent with our UV-vis inferences and DFT results in section 3.3 and 3.4, respectively. Furthermore, it can also be seen that the binding energy of $V^{5+}2$p3/2 and $V^{4+}2$p3/2 of VTi catalyst gradually shifted towards the lower binding energy, compared with VAl catalyst. It indicated that the density of the electron cloud for V species increased over the $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ catalyst. This observation might also be related to that the $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ catalyst had a favorable condition for improving the electron excitation and charge transfer.

We detected the signals at $g = 2.0074$ in this study. The intensity of superoxide ions for the $Al_2O_3$ sample was also weak. It indicated that the superoxide ions could hardly form over the $Al_2O_3$. For the $TiO_2$ sample, a few superoxide ions were observed on the $TiO_2$, indicating that a small amount of superoxide ions could form on the pure $TiO_2$ matrix ($Ti^{3+}$ defect is a formation site of superoxide ions on the $TiO_2$ surface [37].), however, the amount of superoxide ions was limited. For the VAl catalyst, the hyperfine splitting structure of $V^{4+}$ due to the V nucleus ($I = 7/2$, multiplicity 8) [24] was observed, but the intensity of $V^{4+}$ was weak, indicating that the amount of the reduced $V_2O_5$ was low. It was consistent with our XPS results. In addition, the amount of superoxide ions increased compared with $TiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$. It suggested that the reduced $V_2O_5$ could improve the formation of superoxide ions. It also can be seen from Fig. 10c that for the VTi sample, the intensity of the reduced $V_2O_5$ increased significantly. Such observation was consistent with the results of our XPS results. Furthermore, the amount of superoxide ions also increased significantly over the VTi sample. Compared with different test samples, the amount of superoxide ions simultaneous increased with the increase of the reduced $V_2O_5$ species. It has been reported that $V^{4+}$ and $V^{3+}$ are the preferential formation sites of superoxide ions [36,38]. Therefore, the reduced $V_2O_5$ resulted in the increase of the amounts of superoxide ions in our EPR tests. Our previous study confirmed that the superoxide ions could improve the NO oxidation to form $NO_y$ groups that are the intermediates in low-temperature SCR processes [18-20]. Therefore, over the $V_2O_5/TiO_2$ catalysts, the reduced $V_2O_5$ by the electron excitation and charge transfer could make a contribution to improve the NO conversion, compared with the $V_2O_5/Al_2O_3$ catalysts.

### 3.6. NO oxidation analysis

Fig. 11 shows the in-situ DRIFTS spectra of V1.2Al and V1.2Ti catalysts exposed to 500 ppm NO + 5% O₂ at 240 °C for various times. After pretreatment by N₂, when NO + O₂ was introduced, the IR spectra were recorded as a function of time. In addition, for the sake of the observation of distinct difference after the NO + O₂ introduction, we subtracted the signal of the 0 min test for the other tests (left figure). The weak peak at 2340 cm⁻¹ could be ascribed to CO₂ for O-C-O asymmetric stretching [39]. The CO₂ adsorption over the catalyst might come from that CO₂ in the air interacted with the catalyst during the calcination of the catalyst preparation. It has been reported that the peaks between 1635 and 1610 cm⁻¹ could be attributed to weakly bound NO₂, the peaks between 1600 and 1460 cm⁻¹ could be ascribed to surface nitrate species and peaks in the range 1440-1260 cm⁻¹ could be attrib- uted to surface nitrite species [40,41]. It can be seen from Fig. 11a that, over the V1.2Al catalyst, nitrate species at 1590 and 1462 cm⁻¹ were observed before introducing of NO + O₂. It might be attrib- uted to that pyrolytic decomposition of ammonium vanadate formed the $NO_{3}^{-}$ species during the catalyst preparation. As the NO + O₂ was introduced, the intensity of the nitrate species at 1590 cm⁻¹ showed almost no change with the time coursing, furthermore, the nitrate species at 1462 cm⁻¹ and adsorbed NO₂ at 1635 cm⁻¹ were detected. However, the intensity of the nitrate species and adsorbed NO₂ after introducing of NO + O₂ had little change, and the nitrite species were not detected over the V1.2Al catalyst. Therefore, these observations suggested that the NO was difficult to be oxidized by the O₂ over the V1.2Al catalyst. The electron transfer capacity to V₂O₅ species of Al₂O₃ support was weak. It limited the formation of reduced V₂O₅ species, thus, the formation of superoxide ions was difficult. This reasons resulted in the weak NO oxidation over the V1.2Al catalyst.

For the V1.2Ti catalyst (Fig. 11b), the adsorbed CO₂ peak was also detected at 2340 cm⁻¹. The intensity of the CO₂ peak was much stronger than the one of V1.2Al catalyst. It is well-known that an acidic carbon atom of CO₂ could interact with a basic surface oxy- gen anion sites between the CO₂ and materials [42]. Therefore, that the more CO₂ adsorption over V1.2Ti catalyst might be attributed to the higher electron density of O atoms over the VTi catalyst (It can be seen from Fig. 6). It can also be seen that nitrate species were observed at 1517 cm⁻¹ and 1445 cm⁻¹. As NO + O₂ was introduced, the variation tendency of nitrate species over the V1.2Ti catalyst was almost no change with the time coursing. Over the SCR cata- lyst, the stability of nitrate species were strong, therefore, the for- mation of nitrate species was unfavorable to the SCR reaction [19]. It could be concluded from the analysis of nitrate species formation over VTi catalyst that superoxide ions were difficult to oxidize the NO to nitrate species by the V₂O₅ species. It is worth noting that the nitrite species at 1353 cm⁻¹, adsorbed NO₂ at 1610 cm⁻¹ and a new nitrite species at 1372 cm⁻¹ were observed over the V1.2Ti catalyst after introducing of NO + O₂. Furthermore, the intensity of nitrite species and adsorbed NO₂ species increased gradually and simul- taneously after 20 min. Compared with the VAl catalyst, therefore, the NO oxidation was stronger over the VTi catalyst. It has been reported that nitrite species were the active intermediates and it could react with O₂ in the gas to form NO₂ [43]. Therefore, it might relate to that nitrite species and NO₂ species increased simulta- neously. In addition, the intensity of nitrite species and NO₂ species did not change after 50 min. It indicated that the nitrite species to NO₂ might come to dynamic balance over the V1.2Ti catalyst and NO₂ might desorb from the VTi catalyst to the gas phase. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate NO oxidation in the absence of NH₃ over the different catalysts.

### 4. Conclusions
The NO conversion of the VTi catalysts was higher than VAl catalysts as a function of temperature from 120 to 390 °C. The activation temperature of the VTi catalysts shifted more obviously to low-temperature. The TiO₂ and Al₂O₃ supports could interact with V₂O₅ species. The interaction of electron excitation and charge transfer of supports to V₂O₅ species was important to the formation of reduced V₂O₅. These aspects increased the formation of superoxide ions and it could improve the NO oxidation over V₂O₅/TiO₂. It was responsible for the higher SCR catalytic activity of V₂O₅/TiO₂ than V₂O₅/Al₂O₃.
