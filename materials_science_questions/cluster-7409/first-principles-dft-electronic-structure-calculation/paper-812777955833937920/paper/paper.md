# Effects of Oxygen Vacancy on the Magnetic Properties of Ni-Doped SnO₂ Nanoparticles

Chenchen Zhang $^{1}$ · Min Zhou $^{1}$ · Yongjia Zhang $^{1}$ · Wentao Hao $^{1}$ · Li Sun $^{1}$ · Ensi Cao $^{1}$ · Zhi Yang $^{1}$

Received: 11 February 2019 / Accepted: 30 March 2019
© Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2019

## Abstract
We studied the ferromagnetism of Ni-doped $SnO_2$ with and without oxygen vacancy $(V_O)$ by experiments and calculations. $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ nanocrystalline powder prepared by the sol-gel method exhibits room-temperature ferromagnetism, and the vacuum annealing reduces its saturation magnetizations. The density functional theory (DFT) calculations show that the doped Ni atom can introduce a local magnetic moment of about $2.0\ \mu_B$ in $SnO_2$ (110) surface, which is ascribed to the spin-polarization of Ni 3d and O 2p electrons. The type of preferential magnetic coupling of Ni-doped $SnO_2$ (110) surface depends upon the distributions of Ni dopants, and ferromagnetic coupling is more favorable in most cases. Introduction of $V_O$ obviously weakens the ferromagnetic interaction of Ni-doped $SnO_2$ (110) surface, which can give a reasonable explanation for our experimental observations.

Keywords Doped · First-principle · Magnetism · Oxygen defects · Sol-gel

## 1 Introduction
Nowadays, ferromagnetism in dilute magnetic semiconductors (DMS) has received wide attention due to its potential applications in spintronics and optoelectronic devices [1–3]. There are many ways to produce ferromagnetism in DMS, in which the doping with 3d transition metal (TM) is one of the effective ways [4–8]. Among various oxide semiconductors, tin dioxide ($SnO_2$), with a wide band gap of ~3.6 eV [9], has drawn much interest owing to its fascinating properties [10–12], and it has many applications in the fields of solar cells and catalysis [13]. Recently, $SnO_2$ doped with 3d TMs has been extensively investigated due to its wondrous magnetic properties [14–21]. The ferromagnetic behavior has been observed at room temperature in V-, Cr-, Mn-, Fe-, Co-, and Ni-doped $SnO_2$ [15–21], but the origin of the ferromagnetism in 3d TM-doped $SnO_2$ remains confusing. For instance, some different results were observed in Ni-doped $SnO_2$ [19–21].

Aragón et al. proposed that the magnetic coupling of $Sn_{1- x}Ni_xO_2$ samples seemed to be related to the solid solution of Ni ions, and the ferromagnetic order was gradually replaced by the paramagnetic behavior when Ni concentration increased [19]. Sharma et al. found that as the Ni ion concentration increased in $Sn_{1-x}Ni_xO_2$ nanoparticles, two or more adjacent Ni ions would approach each other and formed antiferromagnetic coupling due to the superexchange interaction [20]. Selvi et al. reported that the magnetization measurements of $Sn_{1-x}Ni_xO_2$ powder did not show the RTFM, and all doped samples exhibited diamagnetism at room temperature [21].

Moreover, the $n$-type conductivity of $SnO_2$ is attributed to the point defects, especially oxygen vacancies $(V_O)$, which may affect the magnetic properties. It has been found that the presence of $V_O$ appears to be required for producing ferromagnetism in 3d TM-doped oxide semiconductors [22], such as Co- and Fe-doped ZnO [23, 24], Co-doped $TiO_2$ [25], and Co- and Fe-doped $In_2O_3$ [26, 27]. However, no rigorous theoretical model can determine the roles of Ni ions in $Sn_{1-x}Ni_xO_2$ system, and the correlation between magnetism and oxygen vacancies remains controversial. Therefore, it is necessary to give a detailed description of the magnetic properties of Ni-doped $SnO_2$ and understand reasonably the effects of $V_O$ on the magnetism of this doping system. In this paper, we reported that the nanocrystalline Ni-doped $SnO_2$ powders can exhibit the RTFM, which is weakened by vacuum

## 2 Experiment Details

$SnO_2$ nanocrystalline with 4% Ni doping was prepared by the sol-gel method. 0.050 mol $SnCl_2\cdot2H_2O$, 0.104 mol $C_6H_8O_7$, and 0.002 mol $Ni(NO_3)_2\cdot6H_2O$ with a purity of 99.99 wt% were dissolved in absolute ethyl alcohol. Five grams polyethylene glycol was added under constant stirring to get the sol and the gel was obtained after heating at $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$. $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ powders can be obtained by treating precursor at $600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 2 h in air. $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powders were obtained by reheating $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ in vacuum at $600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 30 min.
The structure of powders was investigated by X-ray diffraction (XRD) with Cu $K\alpha$ radiation. The magnetic properties of all samples were measured using the superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) at 300 K. The microstructures and element mappings of $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ and $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powders were checked by field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM).

## 4 Results and Discussion

### 4.1 RTFM of the Ni-Doped $SnO_2$ Nanocrystalline Powders

The XRD patterns of $SnO_2$, $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$, and $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powders annealed at 600 °C are shown in Fig. 1a. All observed peaks can be indexed with the tetragonal rutile structure of $SnO_2$ (space group, $P4_2/mnm$). No extra diffraction peaks are observed, such as other forms of tin oxides, pure Sn or Ni oxides, and 4% Ni doping did not change the tetragonal rutile structure. Figure 1b, c shows the FE-SEM and element mapping images of $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ and $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powders annealed at 600 °C. The grain size of $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powders is more uniform and smaller than that of the $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ powders, indicating that the vacuum annealing causes the decrease of the grain size. The average grain sizes of $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ and $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powders are about 200 nm and 100 nm, which are consistent with the results calculated by the full width of half maximum of XRD. The element mapping images verify the existence of nickel for the Ni-doped samples, and uniform distribution of elements in all the samples.

The room-temperature magnetization (M) versus magnetic field (H) curves for the $SnO_2$, $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$, and $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powders are shown in Fig. 2. For undoped $SnO_2$ powder, the saturation magnetization (Ms) is about $0.72×10^{-5}$ emu/g, which was associated with the exchange interactions between the polarized atoms around the vacancies [32, 33]. Compared with the undoped $SnO_2$ powder, the ferromagnetism of $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_2$ powder is greatly improved, and its Ms value is about $6.90×10^{-5}$ emu/g. The subsequent vacuum annealing reduces the room-temperature ferromagnetism, and the Ms value of $Sn_{0.96}Ni_{0.04}O_{2-\delta}$ powder is $5.94×10^{-5}$ emu/g. Vacuum annealing increases the number of oxygen vacancies on the surface [34, 35], which may weaken the ferromagnetic signal of Ni-doped $SnO_2$ powder. Similar results are also found in Cr-doped $SnO_2$ nanoparticles and Mn-doped $SnO_2$ thin films [16, 36]. Limited by experimental techniques and conditions, our experiments cannot accurately describe the mechanisms of ferromagnetic generation and attenuation. Thus, we studied the ferromagnetism of Ni-doped $SnO_2$ by first-principle calculations in the following work, and the effect of $V_O$ on the magnetism was also investigated.

### 4.2 Ferromagnetism of Ni-Doped $SnO_2$ (110) Surface

According to the XRD pattern, the lattice parameters of $SnO_2$ powder annealed in air at 600 °C are $a=4.714$ Å and $c=3.192$ Å, which can be used to build the $SnO_2$ unit cell in our calculation. The perfect rutile $SnO_2$ (110) surface was modeled by cutting from the relaxed $2×2×2$ supercell and the vacuum region between the surfaces was set to 10 Å, as shown in Fig. 3a. In the perfect surface, the atoms can be divided into four categories, including the twofold- and threefold-coordinated O atoms ($O_{2c}$ and $O_{3c}$), and fivefold- and sixfold-coordinated Sn atoms ($Sn_{5c}$ and $Sn_{6c}$). Figure 3b shows the total density of state (TDOS) and partial density of state (PDOS) of the perfect $SnO_2$ (110) surface. The calculated band gap is 3.59 eV, which is in agreement with the experimental value of 3.60 eV [9]. This implies that the results predicted on the basis of HSE calculation are reliable. In addition, the spin up and down states in DOS are symmetrical, indicating that the perfect surface is nonmagnetic.

When one $Sn_{5c}$ or $Sn_{6c}$ atom on the surface is replaced by one Ni atom, the corresponding TDOS exhibits an obvious spin-polarization around the Fermi level. ## 5 Conclusion

We studied the ferromagnetism of Ni-doped SnO₂ with and without oxygen vacancies by experiments and calculations. Sn₀.₉₆Ni₀.₀₄O₂ powder prepared by sol-gel method exhibits RTFM, and the vacuum annealing reduces its saturation magnetizations. The type of preferential magnetic coupling of Ni-doped SnO₂ (110) surface depends upon the distributions of the Ni dopants, and ferromagnetic coupling is more favorable in most cases. For Ni_Sn5c- or Ni_Sn6c-doped SnO₂ (110) surface, the V_O₂c nearest to the doped Ni atom is more likely to be formed. Calculations of the magnetic coupling and exchange constant of defect complex Ni_Sn + V_O show that the existence of V_O is detrimental to the ferromagnetism of Ni-doped SnO₂ (110) surface.
