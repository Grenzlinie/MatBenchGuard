# Emergence of ferromagnetism at a vacancy on a non-magnetic ferroelectric $PbTiO_{3}$ surface: A first-principles study

Takahiro Shimada *, Yoshitaka Uratani, Takayuki Kitamura

Department of Mechanical Engineering and Science, Kyoto University, Yoshida-hommachi, Sakyo-ku, Kyoto 606-8501, Japan

Received 8 March 2012; received in revised form 2 July 2012; accepted 8 August 2012
Available online 5 September 2012

## Abstract
We investigated the origin of ferromagnetism due to a vacancy on a non-magnetic ferroelectric $PbTiO_{3}(001)$ surface using first-principles spin-density-functional theory calculations, and successfully provided electronic-level insights into this phenomenon. The results demonstrate that an oxygen vacancy intrinsically induces ferromagnetism, which is remarkably enhanced and stabilized on the $TiO_{2}$ -terminated surface favorably rather than inside $PbTiO_{3}$ . Detailed electronic structure analysis revealed that the ferromagnetism originates from the spin-polarized $d_{z x}$ and $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ states of the two Ti atoms nearest to the oxygen vacancy on the surface. The origin of ferromagnetism for an oxygen vacancy inside $PbTiO_{3}$ differs from that an oxygen vacancy on the surface; it is due to the $e_{g}(d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $d_{z^{2}})$ state of the single nearest Ti atom. These findings provide significant fundamental insights for designing multiferroics in conventional ferroelectrics.

## 1. Introduction
Conventional ferroelectrics with the perovskite structure, such as $PbTiO_{3}$ and its solid solutions $Pb(Zr, Ti) O_{3}$ , exhibit a large spontaneous polarization and piezoelectric response. Consequently, they are widely used in devices such as non-volatile random access memories (FeRAMs), transducers and electromechanical devices [1,2]. It has long been accepted that conventional ferroelectrics without doping are non-magnetic because a formal $d^{0}$ electron configuration (e.g. $Ti^{4+}$ ) in such ferroelectrics is clearly inconsistent with a partially filled $d$ state, which is required for ferromagnetism [3]. However, a recent experimental measurement unexpectedly discovered weak ferromagnetism in $PbTiO_{3}$ nanoparticles at room temperature [4]. That experimental study implied that vacancies in $PbTiO_{3}$ may play a major role in determining the emergence of ferromagnetism. Another experimental study on $PbTiO_{3}$ nanoparticles with different sizes suggested that, in addition to the vacancies, the particle surface area also remarkably affects ferromagnetism [5-7]. However, the detailed origin of ferromagnetism in $PbTiO_{3}$ nanoparticles has yet to be elucidated.

Materials that simultaneously exhibit ferroelectricity and ferromagnetism/antiferromagnetism in the same phase are known as multiferroics. Multiferroics have attracted great interest in recent years due to the strong coupling between their ferroelectricity and ferromagnetism, which is known as magnetoelectric (ME) coupling. Because ME coupling enables ferroelectricity to be controlled by applying a magnetic field, and ferromagnetism to be controlled by applying an electric field, multiferroics hold the potential to realize advanced devices such as new functional sensors and multiple-state memory elements [8-11]. However, intrinsic multiferroic materials are very rare since, as mentioned above, the requirement for ferromagnetism conflicts with the $d^{0}$ configuration in conventional ferroelectrics.

Although Bi-based perovskite oxides (e.g. ${\rm BiFeO_3}$ and ${\rm BiMnO_3}$) exhibit multiferroic properties [12], their large current leakage and coercive field have so far hindered their application in practical devices so far [9]. Deficient ${\rm PbTiO_3}$ with vacancies that simultaneously exhibits ferromagne- tism is a promising candidate for a multiferroic. Before multiferroics can be used in applications, it is essential to determine the detailed origin of ferromagnetism on an elec- tronic level.

In this study, we investigate whether ferromagnetism is intrinsically induced by vacancies on a non-magnetic ferro- electric ${\rm PbTiO_3}$ (001) surface using first-principles density- functional theory (DFT) calculations, and provide elec- tronic-level insights into the origin of ferromagnetism induced by vacancies in ${\rm PbTiO_3}$. The results obtained should be helpful for designing multiferroic materials.

## 2. Simulation models and procedure

Fig. 1 shows simulation models for a vacancy on a (001) surface of a ${\rm PbTiO_3}$ thin film. The ${\rm PbTiO_3}$ thin film con- sists of nine (001) atomic layers of ${\rm TiO_2}$ and PbO planes that are alternately stacked. Both ${\rm TiO_2}$- and PbO-termina- tions of the (001) surface are considered in this study (see Fig. 1a and b, respectively). Spontaneous polarization is aligned in the [100] direction. Since three-dimensional peri- odic boundary conditions are applied in the present calcu- lations, a vacuum region with a thickness of $l_v=12$ Å is introduced in the z direction to prevent undesirable interac- tions between neighboring thin films. Two perovskite unit cells are arranged in the $x$ and $y$ directions in the simulation cell (i.e. a $2\times2$ periodic supercell in the in-plane direction). Interactions between neighboring vacancies due to the peri- odic boundary conditions can be ignored [16-25]. Thus, the simulation cell dimensions in the $x$,$y$ and $z$ directions are $L_x=2c$, $L_y=2a$ and $L_z=4a+l_v$, respectively, where $a$ and $c$ are the lattice constants of tetragonal ${\rm PbTiO_3}$, $a=3.905$ Å and $c=4.154$ Å [13-15]. A vacancy, denoted by $V_i$ ($i=$ Pb, Ti, O1, and O2), is introduced by removing one $i$ atom from the surface layer of the thin film. Note that the oxygens, O1 and O2, which are respectively located in the polar [100] direction and the non-polar [010] or [001] direction relative to the Ti atom, respectively, are non-equivalent due to ferroelectric distortions. Here, we consider Ti, O1 and O2 vacancies on the ${\rm TiO_2}$-terminated surface, and Pb and O2 vacancies on the PbO-terminated surface (see Fig. 1a and b, respectively).

To compare a surface vacancy with a vacancy inside ${\rm PbTiO_3}$, we also simulate bulk ${\rm PbTiO_3}$ with a vacancy. We employ a $2\times2\times2$ periodic supercell with 40 atoms in which two perovskite unit cells are arranged in the $x$, $y$ and $z$ directions [16-25]. Since this system is polarized in [100], just like the surface vacancy models, the simulation dimensions for bulk ${\rm PbTiO_3}$ are $L_x=2c$, $L_y=2a$ and $L_z=2a$. A vacancy, $V_i$, is also introduced by removing one $i$ atom from the simulation supercells.

Ab initio (first-principles) spin-density-functional theory calculations are performed for all the models using the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP) code [26,27]. The electronic wave functions are expanded in plane-waves up to a cut-off energy of 500 eV. The elec- tron-ion interaction is described by the projector-aug-

mented wave (PAW) potentials [28,29], which explicitly include the Pb 5d, 6s and 6p, the Ti 3s, 3p, 3d and 4s, and the O 2s and 2p electrons in the valence states. It is essential to use PAW potentials to realize a high computa- tional efficiency and a high accuracy for the all-electron scheme since it avoids problems due to the linearization of the core-valence exchange interaction. Brillouin-zone (BZ) integrations are performed with a $5 \times 5 \times 1$ Monk horst-Pack $k$-point mesh for the surface vacancy models and with a $5 \times 5 \times 5$ $k$-point mesh for the bulk vacacy model [32]. The atomic structure is fully relaxed by the con- jugate gradient method until the Hellmann-Feynman forces are less than $1.0 \times 10^{-2} eV / \AA$ . These conditions were found to be reliable in previous studies [33-41].

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Stability of surface vacancies in comparison with vacancies inside $PbTiO_{3}$

To investigate which vacancy (Pb, Ti, O1 or O2) is stable compared with the other vacancies in $PbTiO_{3}$ , we introduce the vacancy formation energy [17-19,21] as an indicator of the stability of vacancy,
$$E_{\mathrm{vf}}=\left[\left(E_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{v}}+n_{i} \mu_{i}\right)-E_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{p}}\right] / n_{i}\qquad(1)$$
where $E_{tot }^{v}, E_{tot }^{p}$ and $n_{i}$ are the total energy of the thin film(or bulk) with vacancies, the total energy of the perfect (de- fect-free) thin film (or bulk) and the number of the $V_{i}$ vacancies included in the simulation cell, respectively. $\mu_{i}$ de notes the chemical potential of atom $i$ in $PbTiO_{3}$ .

In general, the chemical potential is affected by the sur- rounding environment. A valid range for the chemical potential can be determined from the thermodynamic equi- librium condition and the requirements to prevent the for- mation of other solid solutions or secondary phases [17-19,21]:

(i) The chemical potential, $\mu_{i}$ , should be lower than that of the elemental solid consisting of only $i$ atoms, $\mu_{i}^{elem }$ , to ensure the elemental solid does not precipitate. Note that $\mu_{O}^{elem }$ is given by half the total energy of an $O_{2}$ molecule. This requirement is expressed by:
$$\Delta \mu_{\mathrm{Pb}} \leqslant 0, \quad \Delta \mu_{\mathrm{Ti}} \leqslant 0, \quad \Delta \mu_{\mathrm{O}} \leqslant 0,\qquad(2)$$
where $\Delta \mu_{i}=\mu_{i}-\mu_{i}^{elem }$ is the difference of the chemi cal potential of atom $i$ in $PbTiO_{3}$ and in the corre sponding elemental solid.

(ii) According to the thermodynamic equilibrium condi- tion, the sum of chemical potentials should equal the heat of formation to ensure the stability of the compound $PbTiO_{3}$ . This is formulated by:
$$\Delta \mu_{\mathrm{Pb}}+\Delta \mu_{\mathrm{Ti}}+\Delta \mu_{\mathrm{O}}=\Delta H\left(\mathrm{PbTiO}_{3}\right),\qquad(3)$$
where $\Delta H(PbTiO_{3})$ denotes the formation enthalpy of $PbTiO_{3}$ .

(iii) The chemical potentials should be lower than the for- mation enthalpy of other possible compounds of PbO and $TiO_{2}$ to avoid the formation of these binarycompounds:
$$\Delta \mu_{\mathrm{Pb}}+\Delta \mu_{\mathrm{O}} \leqslant \Delta H(\mathrm{PbO}),\qquad(4)$$

$$\Delta \mu_{\mathrm{Ti}}+2 \Delta \mu_{\mathrm{O}} \leqslant \Delta H\left(\mathrm{TiO}_{2}\right).\qquad(5)$$

Thus, the formation energy $E_{vf}$ should be evaluated within the range of chemical potentials that satisfy Eqs.(2)-(5).

The calculated formation enthalpies agree well with the corresponding experimental values, demonstrating the reliability of the present calcula- tions. On the other hand, $\Delta \mu_{O}$ is zero at the bottom-left border points C and D, which correspond to oxygen-rich conditions.

Note that the ferromagnetic state is much more stable when the O1 vacancy is located on the surface than in bulk $PbTiO_3$ due to the lower value in $\Delta E_{mag}$. On the other hand, a Ti vacancy in the bulk has a non-zero magnetic moment. However, the positive value of $\Delta E_{mag}$ means that this ferromagnetic state for the Ti vacancy is energetically unfavorable. Considering that the O1 vacancy is most likely to form on the $TiO_2$-terminated surface and is most stable under O-poor conditions (as discussed in the previous section), ferromagnetism appearing in $PbTiO_3$ should be mostly due to O1 vacancies on the $TiO_2$-terminated surface.

Zhang et al. reported that the magnetic moment in $PbTiO_3$ nanoparticles was considerably reduced after annealing in air than annealing in vacuum. They thus inferred that oxygen vacancies may determine the ferromagnetism emerging in $PbTiO_3$ [5]. On the other hand, Wang et al. reported that the magnetic moment of $PbTiO_3$ nanoparticles increased as the surface area per unit volume increased [4], suggesting that the surface plays an important role. Both of these experimental observations agree well with and clearly support the present results that O1 vacancies on the surface are mainly responsible for the ferromagnetism emerging in $PbTiO_3$.

To confirm that the system is ferromagnetic, we initially imposed the other magnetic orderings including not only the ferromagnetic ordering but also possible antiferromagnetic orderings. All the initial magnetic orderings go to the ferromagnetic ordering, which indicates that the system has the ferromagnetic ground-state.

We have additionally calculated the magnetic moment dependence on the ferroelectric polarization by comparing the ferroelectric (FE) and paraelectric (PE) phases. The magnetic moment of O1 vacancy on the $TiO_2$-terminated surface is $1.421\ \mu_B$ for the PE phase, which is clearly larger than that for FE phase ($0.889\ \mu_B$). Such a difference in the magnetic moments between the FE and PE phases is also found in the O1 vacancy in the bulk ($0.488\ \mu_B$ for the FE phase and $1.570\ \mu_B$ for PE phase) [46]. These facts clearly indicate that there is a strong coupling between the ferroelectric polarization and magnetic moment driven by oxygen vacancies.

Fig. 4 shows the magnetization density distribution around the O1-vacancy on the $TiO_2$-terminated surface. The magnetization is mainly concentrated around the $V_{O1}$ site and the two neighboring Ti atoms along in the polar axis of [100] (see the Ti1 and Ti2 atoms located in the $[\overline{1}00]$ and [100] directions with respect to $V_{O1}$, respectively). The magnetization is particularly localized around the Ti1 and Ti2 atoms. This result indicates that these two Ti atoms exhibit ferromagnetism. For comparison, the magnetization density distribution around the O1-vacancy inside $PbTiO_3$ is shown in Fig. 5. Only the single Ti atom nearest $V_{O1}$ carries the magnetic moment in $PbTiO_3$. Hence, differences in the number of Ti atoms carrying magnetization are thought to give rise to the different magnetic moments for $V_{O1}$ on the surface and inside $PbTiO_3$ (see also Table 3).

### 3.3. Electronic structures of ferromagnetic oxygen vacancies

To provide electronic-level insights into the origin of ferromagnetism induced by O1 vacancies, we analyzed the electronic density of states (DOS) of the system. Fig. 6a and b shows the total DOS for perfect (defect-free) and O1-deficient $PbTiO_3$ thin films with $TiO_2$-terminated surfaces, respectively. The defect-free thin film has a band gap energy and acts as an insulator. However, the presence of the O1 vacancy shifts the Fermi level up and forms a new state near the bottom of the conduction band (i.e. it has an $n$-type character). Obviously, the DOS of the majority and minority spins split just below the Fermi level (see the shaded area). Hence, this spin-polarized state gives rise to ferromagnetism. Fig. 6c-g shows the projected DOS for the $3d$ states ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}, d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $d_{z^2}$, respectively) of the Ti1 atom nearest to $V_{O1}$, where the magnetization is highly localized. The $d_{xy}$ and $d_{yz}$ states do not contribute to the spin-polarized state because there is no occupied state in the $d_{xy}$ state and the $d_{yz}$ orbital forms equally occupied majority- and minority-spin states. On the other hand, the $d_{zx}$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$ states contribute remarkably to the spin-polarized state just below the Fermi level because their majority-spin state is occupied, whereas the minority-spin state is not occupied. This result indicates that the $3d$ state of the Ti1 atom is partially filled, as required for ferromagnetism. Note that the $d_{z^2}$ state is also spin-polarized but makes little contribution. Therefore, the $d_{zx}$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$ states of the Ti1 atom adjacent to $V_{O1}$ makes a dominant contribution to the ferromagnetism induced by the O1 vacancy. In fact, the magnetization density distribution around the Ti1 atom corresponds well to topologies of the $d_{zx}$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbitals, which are aligned partially along the [100] and [101] directions, respectively. Consequently, they form a strong $pd\sigma$ bond and a $pd\pi$ bond, respectively. Considering this fact, the loss of the $pd\sigma$ and $pd\pi$ bonds due to the O1 vacancy leads to the partially filled spin-polarized $d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $d_{zx}$ states. Note that the Ti2 atom adjacent to $V_{O1}$ exhibits the same trend as the Ti1 atom.

### 4. Conclusion

In summary, the origin of ferromagnetism arising from
vacancies on $PbTiO_{3}$ (001) surfaces and its electronic-level
mechanism were investigated by performing first-principles
spin-density-functional theory calculations. Based on the
vacancy formation energy, we found that oxygen vacancies
are likely to form on the surface under oxygen-poor condi-
tions, whereas Pb vacancies are favorable under O-rich
conditions. We demonstrated that oxygen vacancies both
on the $TiO_{2}$-terminated surface and inside $PbTiO_{3}$ induce
ferromagnetism, but by different mechanisms. The ferro-
magnetism induced by the oxygen vacancy on the surface
mainly originates from the spin-polarized $d_{zx}$ and $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$
states of the two nearest Ti atoms. In contrast, for the oxy-
gen vacancy inside $PbTiO_{3}$, ferromagnetism is due to the $e_{g}$
($d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $d_{z^{2}}$) state of the single nearest Ti atom. These
results are highly consistent with experimental measure-
ments [4,5]. The findings presented here will provide signif-
icant fundamental insights for designing new multiferroics
in conventional ferroelectrics.
