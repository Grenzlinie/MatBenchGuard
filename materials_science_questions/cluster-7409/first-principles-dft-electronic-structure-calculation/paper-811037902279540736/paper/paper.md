## ABSTRACT

With the first-principles method based on the density functional theory (DFT), we studied novel optical properties of $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$, respectively. According to the calculated electronic structures, the electron redistribution upon Li-ion intercalation has an obvious influence on the electronic transitions between O 2p and Ti 3d states, resulting in distinctions of optical properties between $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$. These results not only reveal the fundamental origin of optical properties but also provide practical guidance on optical applications for these materials.

### 1. Introduction

The spinel lithium titanate $Li_{1+y}Ti_{2-y}O_4$ ($0 \leq y \leq 1/3$) (LTO), which was first investigated by Deschanvres et al. [1], has been considered as one of the most prospective anode electrode material for lithium ion batteries (LIBs) [2,3]. Due to its excellent properties, LTO compounds have attracted many attentions. As the first discovered superconducting oxide system, $LiTi_2O_4$ has a superconducting transition temperature Tc = 11 K for thin films [4,5] and Tc = 13.7 K for bulk materials [6]. Therefore, the spinel $LiTi_2O_4$, which shows the metallic nature [7,8] and the superconducting behavior [6], not only has been widely investigated as electrode materials but also has been grown into transparent superconducting thin films for promising industrial applications [5]. Moreover, many researchers have turned to focus on the end-member of the LTO system $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$ and $Li_xLi_{1+y}Ti_{2-y}O_4$ (composition x = 1) to improve energy storage ability of LIBs. Because $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$ undergo the good lithium intercalation and de-intercalation reversibility without significant structural changes (zero-strain insertion material) [9,10], and they also present superior capacity retention and long cycle lifetimes. Based on above reasons, numerous previous studies have been carried out on exploiting operational methods to synthesize LTO compounds [11,12] and interpreting Li ions electrochemical insertion processes [3,13,14]. However, optical property research reports on the LTO system are less of a concern [15,16], and the fundamental origin of their optical properties is still in dispute.

In order to unravel experimental observations of optical properties of the LTO system and explore the mechanism of this phenomenon, a series of studies should be carried out by DFT methods. However, the theoretical analysis on optical properties of the LTO system has not been reported until now. In this paper, we used the first-principles method to investigate novel optical properties of $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$. The band structures, the density of states (DOS) and the electronic density were calculated to characterize electronic structures. Optical properties of four LTO compounds, including the complex dielectric function, the absorption spectrum, the refractive index and the extinction coefficient, were calculated, respectively. The distinctions in optical properties between $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$ were investigated to figure out its mechanism. What's more, these results opened a new road for LTO compounds used as optical devices.

### 2. Models and theoretical method

The crystal structure of the spinel LTO is the face-center cubic (space group Fd3m) with two formula units $A_{1+x}B_{2-x}X_4$ in a primitive cell, in which two lithium ions locate at 8a sites whereas 2x lithium ions at 16d sites, 4-2x titanium ions at 16d sites and eight oxide ions at 32e sites, respectively [17]. Thus, 16d sites are shared between lithium and titanium ions with a ratio of 1:5 in $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$. While $Li_xLi_{1+y}Ti_{2-y}O_4$ (composition x = 1) is the Li inserted phase of $Li_{1+y}Ti_{2-y}O_4$, and this reaction could be described as $Li(8a)Li_y(16d)Ti_{2-y}(16d)O_4(32e) + Li \rightarrow Li_2(16c)Li_y(16d)Ti_{2-y}(16d)O_4(32e)$, leading to the phase transformation from the spinel structure to the

Based on the primitive cell, 3 × 1 × 1 supercells of LiTi₂O₄ and Li₂Ti₂O₄ were built, while Li₄Ti₅O₁₂ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ were derived from that two Ti ions were substituted by Li ions in 3 × 1 × 1 supercells of LiTi₂O₄ and Li₂Ti₂O₄ respectively. As shown in supplementary material (Fig. S1), we constructed various configurations in which two Ti ions were substituted by Li ions, and corresponding results were presented in supplementary material (Table S1). The formal energy of Li₄Ti₅O₁₂ with configuration (1, 8) and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ with configuration (2, 9) were lower than the other pairs, so the Ti-Li cation distribution of Li₄Ti₅O₁₂ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ were (1, 8) and (2,9). In the simulation of atomic relaxation and electronic states calculations, the space group of LiTi₂O₄ and Li₂Ti₂O₄ is Fd3m, while the space group of Li₄Ti₅O₁₂ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ is P1.

All first-principles calculations were performed with the DFT method combining with the plane wave ultrasoft pseudopotential, as implemented in the Cambridge Sequential Total Energy Package (CASTEP) code [20-22]. The exchange-correlation function was constructed by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [23]. An energy cutoff of400 eV was employed for the plane wave expansion of the electronic wave function, and a 2 × 3 × 3 Monkhorst-Pack grid was adopted [24]. We got a good convergence by these parameters. The convergence of the total energy was converged less than5 × 10⁻⁶ eV/atom. Atomic positions were stable until Hellmann-Feynman forces on each atom were controlled within 0.01 eV/Å. Since the LTO system is non-magnetic, the spin-polarization was neglected in the calculations.

## Result and discussion
### 3.1. Electronic structures
According to results of structural relaxation, equilibrium lattice parameters of LiTi₂O₄, Li₄Ti₅O₁₂, Li₂Ti₂O₄ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ are found to be 8.46, 8.42, 8.39 and 8.30 Å, which are close to experimental values of 8.41, 8.36, 8.38 and 8.35 Å [19,25,26]. Upon inserting lithium ions, the Ti-O bond-length of LiTi₂O₄ changes from 2.01 to 2.06 Å of Li₂Ti₂O₄. Fig. 1 shows band structures and the DOS of four LTO compounds, respectively. As shown in the total density of states (TDOS) and the partial density of states (PDOS), the predominant contribution to the TDOS derives from O 2p and Ti 3d states, demonstrating the strong hybridization between O 2p and Ti 3d states within the TiO₆ octahedra. This octahedral crystal field splits Ti 3d states into low-energy t₂g (three) and high-energy e_g (two) non-overlapping bands in these three compounds. Electrons transfer from Li 2s and Ti 3d to O 2p shell, resulting in the electron distributions Li⁺ (Ti⁺³·⁵)₂ (O⁻²)₄, (Li⁺)₄ (Ti⁺⁴)₅ (O⁻²)₁₂, (Li⁺)₂ (Ti⁺³)₂ (O⁻²)₄ and (Li⁺)₇(Ti⁺³·⁴)₅(O⁻²)₁₂. As shown in Fig. 1(a), the main valence band is characterized by O 2p states with a width of5.03 eV. The band is separated by the Ti 3d conduction band with about 2.26 eV. As a comparison, a 4.78 or 5.25 eV wide O 2p band was separated by a gap of 2.37 or 2.91 eV in Li₂Ti₂O₄ or Li₇Ti₅O₁₂, respectively. However, for Li₄Ti₅O₁₂, the Fermi level locates between the valence and the conduction bands, indicating that it is a typical insulator with a 2.52 eV band gap. This calculated band gap value is smaller than the experimental data (2.95 eV) [15] which is related to the poor description of the strong Coulomb correlation interaction between electrons in the DFT calculation. Moreover, the space group P1 and Ti substituted by Li which introduces more impurity bands cause the band dispersion of Li₄Ti₅O₁₂ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ is tighter than that of LiTi₂O₄ and Li₂Ti₂O₄.

As shown in Fig. 2, the appearance of the strong Ti-O hybridization is found in Li₄Ti₅O₁₂, which becomes weaker in LiTi₂O₄, Li₂Ti₂O₄ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂. The phenomenon can be understood by the following process: when Li₄Ti₅O₁₂ undergoes the lithium ions intercalation and de-intercalation; the charge compensation process between O and Ti ions causes the electron redistribution in the system, and then electrons transfer to O ions enhancing the anionic characters. The indication is consistent with the discussion of the electron distribution mentioned above. Fig. 2 also shows electrons around Li ions are nearly isolated from Ti and O ions. Hence, it is possible to speculate that Li ions are highly ionized.

### 3.2. Optical properties
#### 3.2.1. Dielectric function
As the dominator of electromagnetic wave propagation characteristics, the complex dielectric function establishes a bridge connecting electronic interband transitions and optical spectrums [27]. Thus, optical properties of mediums can be described by the complex dielectric function ε(ω)=ε₁ (ω)+iε₂(ω) in the linear response range. The imaginary part of the dielectric function ε₂(ω) can be calculated from the direct transition probability [28], and the real part ε₁(ω) can be evaluated from the imaginary part via Kramers-Kroning relationships [29]. Therefore, the complex dielectric function ε(ω), the absorption coefficient α(ω), the refractive index n(ω) and the extinction coefficient k(ω) can be expressed as following.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega) &=\frac{4 \pi^{2}}{m^{2} \omega^{2}} \sum_{C V} \int_{B Z} \\
& \quad \times \frac{2}{(2 \pi)^{3}}\left|e \cdot M_{C V}(K)\right|^{2} \delta\left[E_{C}(K)-E_{V}(K)-\hbar \omega\right] d^{3} K
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\pi} \rho_{0} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega^{\prime} \varepsilon_{2}(\omega)}{\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}} \mathrm{~d} \omega
\tag{2}
$$

$$
\alpha(\omega)=\sqrt{2} \omega\left[\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)}-\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)\right]^{1 / 2}
\tag{3}
$$

$$
n(\omega)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)}+\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)}{2}\right)^{1 / 2}
\tag{4}
$$

$$
k(\omega)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)}-\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)}{2}\right)^{1 / 2}
\tag{5}
$$

where subscripts C and V represent conduction bands and valence bands, respectively. BZ is the first Brillouin zone, k is the reciprocal lattice vector, ℏ is the Planck constant, $|e × M_{CV}(K)|^{2}$ is the momentum transition matrix element, ω is the angular frequency, while $E_{C}(K)$ and $E_{V}(K)$ represent the intrinsic energy level of conduction and valence bands, respectively.

The complex dielectric function ε(ω) is present in Fig. 3. It can be seen from the real part of the dielectric function that static dielectric constants ε₁(0) of LiTi₂O₄, Li₄Ti₅O₁₂, Li₂Ti₂O₄ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ are 25.49, 3.34, 62.29 and 56.48, respectively. Compared with Li₄Ti₅O₁₂, the real part dielectric function spectrums of LiTi₂O₄, Li₂Ti₂O₄ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂ move to the higher energy. But inserting lithium ions has no effect on ε₁(ω) in the higher energy region.

Corresponding to the insulator nature, the imaginary part of the dielectric function ε₂(ω) of Li₄Ti₅O₁₂ keeps zero until the photon energy up to 2.22 eV. Unlike Li₄Ti₅O₁₂, there is an obvious dielectric peak A in the infrared region in the imaginary part spectrums of LiTi₂O₄, Li₂Ti₂O₄ and Li₇Ti₅O₁₂. ### 3.2.2. Absorption spectrum
Fig. 4(a) presents main orbits of $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$, and main orbits involved in absorption transitions are denoted in this figure. HOMO and LUMO of four LTO compounds are mainly composed of O 2p and Ti 3d states, but inserting lithium ions induces some changes in the arrangement of orbits. For $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, Ti substituted by Li moves orbits to the higher energy region and introduces more O 2p states contributions to HUMO and more Ti 3d states contributions to LUMO, which is consisted with the stronger Ti—O hybridization. Moreover, the energy difference between HOMO and LUMO of $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$ is 2.56 eV, while this energy difference of $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ or $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$ is decreased to 0.08, 0.57 or 0.18 eV.

Vertical excitation energy $E_{abso}$, absorption wavelength $\lambda_{abso}$ and main absorption transitions are listed in supplementary material (Table S2). Fig. 4(b) displays absorption spectrums of $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$. The minimal vertical excitation energy of $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$ is 2.95 eV, and six absorption bands (2.95, 3.64, 4.07, 4.88, 5.42 and 7.39 eV) are found in the visible and the ultraviolet region. These six absorption bands are mainly attributed by the electronic transition between O 2p and Ti 3d states. For $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$, these six absorption bands increase to the higher energy region (blue shift). The phenomenon can be understood that the transition between O 2p and Ti 3d states, as dominant components involved in absorption spectrums, becomes more difficult, corresponding to the weaker Ti-O hybridization. In addition, LTO compounds show the excellent absorption ability in the ultraviolet region which indicates that they can be applied in optical memory devices, and the memory energy region can be regulated by inserting lithium ions.

## 4. Conclusions

We have studied optical properties of $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$ by the first-principles method, including the complex dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$, the absorption coefficient $\alpha(\omega)$, the refractive index $n(\omega)$ and the extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$. Compared with $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, some distinctions of optical properties are found in $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$: the static dielectric constants $\varepsilon_1(0)$ become larger; the dielectric peak A is found in the infrared region, and the dielectric peak B moves to the higher region (blue shift); the refractive and the extinction spectrums have the same change trend as the real and the imaginary part of the dielectric function, respectively; six absorption bands are increased to the higher energy (blue shift). Electronic structures indicate that optical properties of four LTO compounds in the infrared and the visible region derive from the electronic transition between O 2p and Ti 3d states near the Fermi level, while optical properties in the ultraviolet region depend on the transition from O 2p valence band states to Ti 3d conduction band states. Therefore, these distinctions are attributed by different electron distributions which determine the Ti—O hybridization. In addition, the results pave a promising way for $LiTi_2O_4$, $Li_4Ti_5O_{12}$, $Li_2Ti_2O_4$ and $Li_7Ti_5O_{12}$ utilized as optical devices.
