# The photocatalytic mechanism of BiOI with oxygen vacancy and iodine self-doping

Ting Kong, Xiumei Wei*, Gangqiang Zhu, Yuhong Huang

College of Physics and Information Technology, Shaanxi Normal University, Xian 710119, Shaanxi, PR China

## ABSTRACT

The photocatalytic mechanism of BiOI with an oxygen vacancy and iodine self-doping is investigated by studying their structural, electronic and optical properties. The results of the first-principle calculations indicate that the utilization of the visible light region is broadened, and thus the photocatalytic performance is improved, via introducing an oxygen vacancy or I dopant. Among them, I-Bi doped BiOI shows the best photocatalytic performance, and it is also the preferred one of the three structures due to its better stability. The occurrence of I-Bi or I-I interactions in the original double iodine layer along with the introduction of I dopant leads to a planar localization of the photocatalytic mechanism. Then it is expected that the photo-generated carriers will transfer to the catalyst surface more easily; the photocatalytic activity of BiOI relies on the planar localization to a great degree.

Keywords: oxygen vacancy; self-doping; electronic structures; photocatalytic mechanism

### 1. Introduction

In recent years, semiconductor photocatalysis has attracted extensive attention in connection with environmental pollution treatment [1, 2]. Considering the relatively slow reaction rate and poor solar efficiency of $TiO_2$ which hindered its application [3-5], many studies have been carried out to exploit new visible light-driven photocatalysts. It has been reported that the Bi-O sites in $Bi^{3+}$-doped $TiO_2$ act as electron traps in the photocatalytic reaction [6], so it is natural to conjecture that the materials containing bismuth (Bi) may have high photocatalytic activity. To date, those Bi-compounds with different crystal structures have been studied in their photocatalytic performance [7-11]. Among them, the bismuth-based semiconductor of BiOX (X= F, Cl, Br, and I) has been proved to be a promising new photocatalytic material, due to its unique layered crystal structure and suitable band gaps, which exhibits superior photocatalytic performance and high stability [12-15]. The bandgap energies are estimated at approximately 3.64 eV, 3.40 eV, 2.78 eV, and 1.85 eV for BiOF, BiOCl, BiOBr and BiOI, respectively [12-15]. BiOI has attracted great interest for having the smallest bandgap corresponding to visible light. Experimental and theoretical studies find that BiOI has a layered structure with the formed internal electric fields between the positive $[Bi_2O_2]^{2+}$ and negative $I_2^{2-}$ layers, which could provide a large enough space to polarize the related atoms and orbits and, consequently, improving the separation efficiency of photo-induced electron-hole pairs [16].

But from our experimental results (which have not been published yet, and will be reported systematically in another work), the photocatalytic activity of BiOI is not as good as that of BiOCl, in spite of the difference in the bandgaps [17]. However

different phenomenon were presented in other work, in which the strongest absorption in the visible-light and the best photocatalytic activity was reported [18]. The conflict needs to be investigated further in the following experiments. So far, we consider that the photocatalytic activity of the pure materials is still affected to some extent as we all know, because of the fast recombination of photogenerated electron-hole pairs, nevertheless, the different results may come from many practical factors, such as different compositions, crystal microstructures, photosensitization, various defects of the compounds, and so on [19-22]. As we all know, oxygen vacancy (Vo), as one of the fundamental and intrinsic defects in semiconductor materials, often has a critical impact on their physicochemical properties by altering the electronic and optical properties [23]. In addition, Zhang et al. demonstrated that iodine self-doping BiOI displays preferable photoactivity in the degradation process of Methyl Orange (MO) [24]. We surmise that these defects may play a great role in the photocatalytic activity of BiOI. Then it is very important to study the catalytic mechanism of BiOI as a good potential photocatalyst thoroughly, and the effects of Vo and iodine self-doping are also in urgent need of discussion. So in this work, the microstructures and electronic structures of BiOI will be studied by means of density functional theory (DFT) [25,26] in order to understand the photocatalytic activity.

## 2. Computational details and models

The calculations are performed using the CASTEP module [27] within the plane-wave pseudopotential method, along with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) exchange and correlation functional in the scheme of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [28]. The convergence tests regarding the cut-off energy have been made before the calculations of the properties and then a plane

wave cutoff energy of 380 eV is used. An ultrasoft pseudopotential is adopted in the reciprocal space, and a 3×3×3 Monkhorst-Pack mesh [29] grid is found to be sufficient to reach convergence for the bulk supercell calculations. All the atoms of the structures are fully relaxed to their equilibrium positions with an energy convergence of $5×10^{-6}$ eV, while the force applied on each atom is less than 0.01 eV/Å and the stress is less than 0.02 GPa through the BFGS-based method [30]. In addition, the atomic displacement is less than $5×10^{-4}$ Å and the self-consistent field (SCF) tolerance is $5×10^{-7}$ eV. The valence electronic configurations are the states of Bi6s6p, O2s2p and I5s5p for the ground-state electronic structure calculations.

![](./images/811058774054797314_2.jpg)

Fig.1. The 24-atom supercell of pure BiOI. The big purple, the small red and the brown spheres represent Bi, O and I atoms, respectively.

BiOI belongs to the tetragonal structure with a space group of P4/nmn(NO.129). The lattice parameters of pure BiOI crystal are a=b=3.984, c=9.128, $\alpha$=$\beta$=$\gamma$=$90^\circ$, c/a=2.291, V=144.882 [31]. The pure BiOI crystal has a typical layered structure with an ordered packing of five-atom layers in the sequence of -I-Bi-O-Bi-I- along the c-axis direction. There is a tetragonal structure with one O atom at the center connecting to four Bi atoms, and each Bi atom and four O atoms construct the $[BiO_4]$

tetrahedron alternating with I atoms between the layered structures [32].

In this work, we will investigate the effects of Vo and I self-doping on the structural, electronic and optical properties of BiOI photocatalyst. A 24-atom 2×2×1 supercell of BiOI ($Bi_8O_8I_8$) shown in Fig.1 is chosen in order to avoid the interaction between defects, which is verified to be sufficient by the calculations. One of the oxygen atoms, marked by Ⅰ in Fig.1, is removed to build up a possible Vo defect in the BiOI system, which is called BiOI with Vo in the paper. In addition, two kinds of I-doped BiOI systems are possible when I self-doping is occurring. So the systems are constructed by substituting an O atom (at site Ⅰ) and a Bi atom (at site Ⅱ) in a pure BiOI supercell, which are abbreviated as I-O doped BiOI and I-Bi doped BiOI, respectively.

The relaxed structures with defects are calculated and displayed in Fig.2, while the geometric parameters are listed in Table 1. The pure tetragonal BiOI has a layered structure consisting of $[Bi_2O_2]^{2+}$ layers sandwiched between two slabs of iodine ions, as shown in Fig.1. The electric field formed of $[Bi_2O_2]^{2+}$ and $I_2^{2-}$ is also effective in the separation of electron hole pairs, so a better catalytic activity is supposed in BiOI. When the pure BiOI supercell is optimized, the lattice parameters of the BiOI crystal are a=8.009Å, b=8.008Å, c=9.442Å, and V=605.541Å³. From Fig. 2a, the optimized structure of the Vo defect changes slightly compared to the pure one. The lattice parameter of the c-axis and the unit cell volume increase slightly when Vo exists. It maybe because the Coulomb repulsion force between the electrons is increased compared to that of pure BiOI. But for the I-doped BiOI structures, the parameters and the unit cell volumes increase obviously especially in the I-O system, as seen in Fig. 2b, it is due to the larger ionic radius of $I^-$ than that of $O^{2-}$. From Fig. 2c, it is noticed that in I-Bi doped BiOI, the expansion along the c-axis is the greatest, so the effect of I-doping on the structure will be mostly embodied along the [001] orientation, which will be in favor of distinguishing the stratification.

It is also found that changes in the morphology and symmetry of the layered structure are negligible in BiOI with Vo except for the obvious local structure changes


near the vacancy, but the structures are distorted to a large extent in I-doped BiOI systems, especially in I-O doped BiOI. It should also be due to the larger atomic radius of I relative to O and Bi, so when the O atom is substituted by I, other atoms around it can be pushed aside. We can see from Fig. 2b that the positions of the Bi atom (marked by Ⅲ) nearest to the defect changes most distinctly and even pierces into the I layer, then the layered structure composed of $[Bi_{2}O_{2}]^{2+}I_{2}^{2-}$ is destroyed, so viewed from this perspective of the structure, the photocatalytic activity should be impeded. As can be seen in Fig. 2c, if the chosen position is Bi to be displaced, although the structural distortion is still obvious and the doping I atom (marked by Ⅳ) merges into the I layer, the ordered packing sequence of -I-Bi-O-Bi-I- along the c-axis direction is sustained.

To study the relative stability of the relaxed structures, the defect formation energies are examined using the following relations:

$$E_{\mathrm{form1}}=E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{8})-[E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{8})-\mu_{\mathrm{O}}],$$

$$E_{\mathrm{form2}}=E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{9})-[E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{8})-\mu_{\mathrm{O}}+\mu_{\mathrm{I}}],$$

$$E_{\mathrm{form3}}=E(\mathrm{Bi}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{9})-[E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{8})-\mu_{\mathrm{Bi}}+\mu_{\mathrm{I}}],$$

where $E_{\mathrm{form1}}$, $E_{\mathrm{form2}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{form3}}$ are the defect formation energies of $\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{8}$, $\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{9}$ and $\mathrm{Bi}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{9}$, respectively. $E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{8})$, $E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{9})$, $E(\mathrm{Bi}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{9})$ and $E(\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{8})$ denote the total energies of the $\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{8}$, $\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{9}$, $\mathrm{Bi}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{9}$ and $\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{8}$ supercells, respectively. The values of the chemical potentials of oxygen, iodine and bismuth are $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}$, $\mu_{\mathrm{I}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{Bi}}$, which are determined by the energy of an O or a I atom in an $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ or a $\mathrm{I}_{2}$ molecule, $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}=1/2\mu(\mathrm{O}_{2})$, $\mu_{\mathrm{I}}=1/2\mu(\mathrm{I}_{2})$.

The calculated defect formation energies of $\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{8}$, $\mathrm{Bi}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{7}\mathrm{I}_{9}$ and $\mathrm{Bi}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{8}\mathrm{I}_{9}$ are 4.129eV, 5.197eV and 3.459eV, respectively. This shows that the Bi position is more preferred in the I-doping process, which is understandable considering the atomic

radius. Comparing with Fig. 2b, we can see that the great distortion of the I-O replacement corresponds to its larger formation energy.

Based on the optimized BiOI supercell, the electronic band structure has been calculated using the DFT method for all the structures mentioned. As shown in Fig. 3a, it can be seen that the valence band maximum (VBM) is between the Q and Z points, and the conduction band minimum (CBM) is at the F point. Near the Fermi level, the width of the valance band is 5.21 eV, while the width of the conduction band is 6.32

eV. In addition, we can see that the electron transition of pure BiOI from the valence band (VB) to the conduction band (CB) is an indirect transition with the calculated band gap of 1.52 eV, which is underestimated compared with the experimental value of 1.85 eV [15]. The difference in the theoretical and experimental band gaps is 0.33 eV, which is due to the well-known shortcoming of the exchange-correction function in describing excited states. It is well known that the indirect band gap semiconductor helps to obtain excellent photocatalytic activity because the excited electrons must be emitted to the conduction band by traveling a certain k-space distance, which reduces the recombination probability of the photo-generated electrons and holes [33]. In addition, the total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) of pure BiOI are displayed in Fig. 4a, showing that the VB is primarily made up of the I5p, O2p and a little of the Bi6s states, however the CB is chiefly made up of Bi6p states. It is revealed that the visible-light response for BiOI is due to the transition from the hybridized I5p and O 2p states in the VB to the Bi6p states in the CB. Moreover, we can see from Fig. 3a that the hybridization of the I5p and O2p states make the VB largely dispersed, which favors the mobility of photo-excited holes in the VB and thus facilitates the high photocatalysis [34-37].

The calculated band structure of BiOI with Vo is displayed in Fig. 3b, it shows that the VBM is at the F point and the CBM is at the Q point; the calculated indirect band gap of BiOI with Vo is about 1.12 eV, which is reduced by 0.40 eV compared with pure BiOI. Compared to the band structure of pure BiOI, the width of the VB near the Fermi level expands to 5.82 eV, while the width of the conduction band becomes narrower with 6.01 eV. Also, the bottom of the CB shifts down about 0.16 eV compared with pure BiOI, which favors the migration of photo-generated electrons. The TDOS and PDOS of BiOI with Vo are shown in Fig. 4b. Compared

with that in pure cases, a new small peak appears near the Fermi level which is dominated by the Bi6p and I5p. The Vo caused the hybridization of the Bi6p states and I5p states, which contributes to the VB, while the conduction band is still composed of the Bi6p states mainly. So in the BiOI semiconductor, the Vo introduces a new electronic state in the forbidden band, acting as a capture center for photo-induced electrons, then transfers electrons into the CB among Bi atoms, which could not only improve the effective separation of the photo-generated electron-hole pairs, and consequently, enhance the photocatalytic activity of the BiOI system, but also be more beneficial to tailor the visible light absorption. Hence it is reasonable to consider that BiOI with Vo is better than the pure BiOI in photocatalytic performance.

In Fig. 3c, the width of the VB and CB near the Fermi level is 5.12 eV and 6.05 eV, respectively, which is reduced a little compared to that of pure BiOI. Both the top of the VB and the bottom of the CB fall off, and then the Fermi level locates in the band gap. The VB is more flat, which favors the mobility of photo-induced electrons, and thus is beneficial to the photocatalytic oxidation of organic pollutants [38-40]. But it is also observed that the transition nature is changed to direct from the band structure, since both the VBM and the CBM are at the Z point, which is different from the Vo structure. The detailed states distribution is reflected in Fig. 4c, where the peak of the Bi6p states is strengthened after I-O doping, as we can see, and the states of I5p can be also found at the same energy level, which indicates that the hybridization of the Bi6p and I5p is introduced by the I-doping. Further analysis reveals that the interaction just comes from the Bi atom at site Ⅲ and the I atoms in the same plane, where the planar character is destroyed mostly by the I-doping, as shown in Fig. 2b. Subsequently, the previous internal electronic field in the pure BiOI structure is

broken. So the transferring for the photo-excited carriers may be affected, although some carriers can be excited by light with lower frequency for the decreased gap value for the new energy level resulting from the hybridization of the Bi6p and I5p.

The calculated band structure of I-Bi doped BiOI is shown in Fig. 3d. The widths of the VB and CB near the Fermi level are 5.41 eV and 7.16 eV, respectively, which are increased a little compared to pure BiOI. A special characteristic is noticed in Fig. 4d that more in-gap states are distributed around the Fermi level, which come from I5p chiefly accompanied with some distribution from Bi6s and Bi6p, and a minor one from O2p. The electronic structure supplies multiple choices in light absorption. By further investigation, we find that the contributions from the double iodine layers are nonuniform, because the local PDOS analysis indicates that these in-gap states only come from the layer in which the doping I (at site Ⅳ in Fig. 2) is located, although the top of the valence band is still composed of I5p contributed by another I layer as pure structure. Then more empty bands around the Fermi level are brought out with the doping I and its merging. So in a particular layer, the flexibility of those excited carriers is increased. It is precisely because of the special structure after introducing the defect of I-Bi dopants, which makes the electrons jump in many ways. On the other hand, because the crystal symmetry of the other part is almost retained, the internal electric field still exists locally, so the transition mechanism of the carriers may be kept simultaneously.

As a complementary tool, the electron density difference is helpful for a proper understanding of the electronic structure and the atomic bonding, then the electron density difference of the (001) plane is mapped in Fig. 5 for the pure BiOI, BiOI with Vo, I-O doped BiOI and I-Bi doped BiOI systems, respectively. To understand the inter-atomic bonding behaviors of the four crystals, the atomic populations are calculated via the Mulliken population analysis, and a few representative atoms are selected as marked in Fig. 6 to avoid too much data. Their Mulliken charges are listed in Table 2, in which $O_2$ represents the O atom at site Ⅰ and $Bi_1$ represents the Bi atom at site Ⅱ.

When Vo exists in BiOI, the adjacent Bi atoms exhibit more positive charge density than other Bi atoms, as shown in Fig. 5c, but the electron density difference changes little in the other part away from the Vo defect. It can also suggest the effect of the Vo defect on the Bi6p states as analyzed above. The electron density difference of the (001) plane changes greatly in I-doping structures and the original symmetry is lost, which results from the destruction of geometric symmetry. At the same time, a new interaction is established between Bi-I or I-I in the original I-layer. It is noticed that the atomic populations of O remain almost constant, which means that the effect of O from all the defects can be negligible. This is due to the strong oxidizing property of oxygen. The Mulliken charges of I and Bi are redistributed except for the atom $Bi_2$ in I-O doped BiOI and $I_3$ in I-Bi doped BiOI, whose charge decreases to an astonishing value of $0.35e$ and changes to a positive value of $0.4e$, respectively. We have noticed that they are just the atoms located at site Ⅲ and Ⅳ, as shown in Fig. 2.

The large displacements from its original position bring about a series of

modifications including the electronic distribution. When $Bi_2$ is pushed into the adjacent I-layer, the covalent bond feature between Bi and I appears, and the interaction between Bi and I is established to destroy the original dissociated I-layer and form an affiliated Bi-I lamella. While the doped I atom is relaxed to its adjacent I-layer, a similar interaction is formed between $I_3$ and its surrounding four I atoms. An obvious overlap of electron clouds between $Bi_2$ or $I_3$ and the surrounding I atoms can also be observed by analyzing the local electron density difference in Fig. 5d and Fig. 5e, respectively. Then it is reasonable that the distributions of the electronic states in both the systems are affected by the interaction in the I layer.

In order to understand the physical origins of the optical properties, the complex dielectric function [41,42], $\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+i \varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$, including the real $[\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)]$ and the imaginary $[\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)]$ parts, is considered in this work. The real part can be calculated using the Kramers-Kronig relations, and the imaginary part is calculated from the electronic structure through the joint density of states and the momentum matrix elements between the occupied and the unoccupied wave functions within the selection rules. The real part and the imaginary part of the calculated dielectric function are presented in Fig. 7. As shown in Fig. 7a, the real part of the dielectric function increases with an increase of the photon energy in the low energy region. The maximum $\varepsilon_{1}$ of pure BiOI, BiOI with Vo, I-O doped BiOI and I-Bi doped BiOI with the values of 16.39, 15.31, 13.47 and 16.26, are located at the energies of 2.81 eV, 1.15 eV, 2.41 eV and 0.74 eV, respectively. The peaks shown in the energy regions of

$\varepsilon_2$ in Fig. 7b represent the absorptive transitions from the valence bands to the conduction bands. The highest peak for pure BiOI, BiOI with Vo, I-O doped BiOI and I-Bi doped BiOI are located at the energies of 4.46 eV, 4.28 eV, 3.42 eV and 1.94 eV, respectively. The maximum absorption amount for pure BiOI, BiOI with Vo and I-O doped BiOI are 16.53, 12.86 and 12.30, respectively, which exhibit the maximum amount of absorption of UV light and beyond the region of visible light absorption. For Vo and I-O doping defects, their maximum absorption amount are almost identical and less than that of pure BiOI. In regard to the I-Bi doped BiOI system, the maximum peak is close to that of I-O doped BiOI with the value of 11.88, but it shows a higher utilization ratio of visible light absorption. In order to understand the absorption efficiency of visible light, we can focus on the peaks in the low energy region. As shown in Fig. 7b, the peak of a, b, c, e and f are located in the visible light region with the energies of 2.33 eV, 1.46 eV, 0.99 eV, 1.08 eV and 1.94 eV, respectively, and the peak of d is located in the infrared region with the energy of 0.25 eV. Combined with Fig. 3 and Fig. 4, the peaks of a, b, c, and e originate mainly from the transition of I-5p into Bi-6p, while the peak of d originates mainly from the transition of Bi-6s into I-5p and the peak of f may originates from the transition of O-2p into I-5p or I-5p into Bi-6p in the I-Bi doped BiOI system, which is consistent with the analysis of the band structure and density of states. Based on the above analysis, we prefer to substitute I for Bi rather than the others, due to its better absorption of visible light and photocatalytic performance.

### 4. Conclusion
First-principle calculations are conducted to systematically investigate the structural, electronic and optical properties of pure BiOI, BiOI with Vo, I-O doped BiOI and I-Bi doped BiOI. We find that the geometric distortion is obvious in I-doped systems, and the layered structure is destroyed by introducing an I atom with a relatively large radius. If I-doping appears, the preferred substitutional position is Bi rather than O, as indicated by their formation energies. The electronic structures are all tuned by the defects of Vo and I-doping, and a red-shift is brought out by all the defects, so the application of more abundant visible-light source in a broadened range is expected in compounds with these defects. Among the three kinds of materials, I-Bi doped BiOI presents the best photocatalytic performance in response to visible light. But, as shown in our calculation, the photocatalytic mechanism varies for the three structures. In BiOI with Vo, the photocatalytic mechanism is similar to that in pure BiOI for the similar planar structure, except that the oxygen vacancy acts as a trapping center of the photo-generated carriers and the new internal level introduced by Vo will provide an intermediate state for the electron transition. However, the internal level in I-O doped BiOI mainly comes from the hybridization of the Bi6p and

I5p in the original I-layer, which is caused by the squeezing of Bi from the $[Bi_2O_2]^{2+}$ layer. While new in-gap states in I-Bi doped BiOI are caused by the merging of I dopant into the I-layer. So new planar interactions emerging in the original I-layer create different results for the two types of defects. In conclusion, the planar localization is discovered for the photocatalytic mechanism in BiOI with the defects of Vo and I-doping. When it is concerned with the transition of photo-excited carriers to the catalyst's surface, the photocatalytic activity of BiOI will depend on the planar localization to a great degree.
