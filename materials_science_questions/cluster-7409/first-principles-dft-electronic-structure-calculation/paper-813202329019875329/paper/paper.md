## A B S T R A C T

Hydrogen titanium oxide $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was prepared from $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ as a parent compound via $\ce{Li^{+}/H^{+}}$ ion exchange in acidic solution at $60\ \mathrm{^\circ C}$. It crystallizes in the monoclinic system, space group $C2/m$, and the lattice parameters of $a=14.6604(3)\ \mathrm{\mathring{A}}$, $b=3.74109(8)\ \mathrm{\mathring{A}}$, $c=9.2487(2)\ \mathrm{\mathring{A}}$, and $\beta=96.956(2)^\circ$. The crystal structure of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was refined to the conventional values of $R_\mathrm{wp}=3.12\%$ and $R_\mathrm{p}=2.45\%$ with a fit indicator of $\mathrm{GOF}=R_\mathrm{wp}/R_\mathrm{e}=1.39$ by Rietveld analysis using powder neutron diffraction data. The basic $(\ce{Ti_{6}O_{13}})^{2-}$ framework in $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was maintained nearly unchanged from that in the parent $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$. The hydrogen site in the tunnel space was refined with the strict O3–H1 distance of $0.94(1)\ \mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ in $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$. The structural stability of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was confirmed by bond valence sums and $^1$H-MAS NMR measurement. The total protonic conductivity in $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was estimated to be $\sigma_\mathrm{total}=8.32\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{S\ cm^{-1}}$ at room temperature from the results of AC-impedance measurements. This value is comparable to that of the well-known good protonic conducting ramsdellite-type $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$.

## 1. Introduction

Hydrogen titanium oxides display a wide range of interesting chemical and electrochemical properties. Two kinds of structure-type are well-known in the $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$ composition; one is the ramsdellite-type which is very attractive as a suitable electrolyte for low-temperature fuel cells for energy production [1–3], and the other has the layered $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$ related-type crystal structure [4–6]. These compounds can be prepared by soft-chemical synthetic route using alkali titanates of $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$ and $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$ as parent compounds.

Feist and Davies first reported another hydrogen titanium oxide, $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$; this compound was prepared upon heating the layered $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$ at $140\ \mathrm{^\circ C}$ for one month by the dehydration reaction of $2\ce{H_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}} \rightarrow \ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}} + \ce{H_{2}O}\uparrow$ [6]. Recently, the synthetic condition of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was re-investigated, however, single-phase sample of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ could not be synthesized by the dehydration reaction using the $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$ precursor [7]. On the other hand, a novel synthetic route by $\ce{Na^{+}/H^{+}}$ ion exchange was recently attempted using the $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ precursor [8]. Unfortunately, in the produced titanate, 99% of the initial $\ce{Na^{+}}$ ions remained in the tunnel structure after ion exchange [8]. This fact suggested that the $\ce{Na^{+}/H^{+}}$ ion exchange reaction hardly proceeded in the reported synthetic condition. For these reasons, the structural details of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$, especially the H atomic positions, had not been revealed yet, to our knowledge, in spite of interests in the one-dimensional proton conduction property, as in the case of the ramsdellite-type $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{3}O_{7}}$ [3].

Recently, we successfully synthesized single-phase sample of $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ by $\ce{Na^{+}/Li^{+}}$ ion exchange in molten $\ce{LiNO_{3}}$ at $380\ \mathrm{^\circ C}$ using the $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ precursor [9]. The crystal structure of $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was refined by Rietveld refinement using the powder neutron diffraction data. The basic $(\ce{Ti_{6}O_{13}})^{2-}$ framework in $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was maintained nearly unchanged from that in the parent $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$. The Li occupation site in the tunnel space shifted to $y=0.5$ position, and the moving resulted in an unusual $\ce{LiO_{4}}$ planar coordination in $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ [9].

Very recently, $\ce{Na^{+}/Li^{+}/H^{+}}$ ion exchange was attempted using the nano-sized $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ precursor [10]. The reversible electrochemical Li insertion/extraction reactions and crystal structure were reported in the nano-sized $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ and the ion-exchanged $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ and $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$. However, other physical properties of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ have not been revealed yet, to our knowledge.

The present study has three main objectives. First, we prepared the single-phase polycrystalline sample of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ by $\ce{Li^{+}/H^{+}}$ ion-exchange method using $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ as a parent compound, and refined the precise crystal structure by powder neutron diffraction technique. The local structure around hydrogen atom in $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ was compared with those around Li and Na atoms in $\ce{Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$ and $\ce{Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$, respectively. Second, the validity of the refined crystal structure was examined by bond valence sums and $^1$H-MAS-NMR measurement. Finally, we clarified preliminary physical properties of $\ce{H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}}$, in comparison with those of the related titanate compounds.

## 2. Experimental procedure

### 2.1. Sample preparation

In the present study, the sample preparation was performed by three synthetic steps. First, the precursor $Na_2Ti_6O_{13}$ was prepared by a conventional solid state reaction, as previously reported [8,9]. A mixture of $Na_2CO_3$ (99.9% pure) and $TiO_2$ (99.99% pure) in a molar ratio of 1:6 was heated at 1073 K for 20 h in air. The resultant specimens were reground, and the same temperature program sequence was repeated once again. Next, the polycrystalline $Li_2Ti_6O_{13}$ samples were prepared from $Na_2Ti_6O_{13}$ via ion exchange at low temperatures, as recently reported [9–12]. $Na^+/Li^+$ ion-exchange experiments were performed using the molten salt of $LiNO_3$ at 380 °C for 10 h in air. Then, the obtained $Li_2Ti_6O_{13}$ sample was washed with water and ethanol, and then dried at 60 °C for 1 day in air. Finally, the polycrystalline $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ sample was prepared from $Li_2Ti_6O_{13}$ via ion exchange at low temperatures. $Li^+/H^+$ ion-exchange experiments were performed using hydrochloric acid at 60 °C. After soaking in the solution for 5 days, the product was washed with water and ethanol, and then dried at 60 °C for 1 day in air.

The phase purity and crystal structure of the obtained samples were characterized by powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) profiles measured at room temperature with Cu $K\alpha$ radiation using a Rigaku RINT2550V diffractometer (operating conditions: 40 kV, 200 mA) equipped with a curved graphite monochromator. The XRD intensity data were collected for 1 s at each $0.02^\circ$ step over a $2\theta$ range from $5^\circ$ to $140^\circ$ for the Rietveld refinement.

The chemical analysis of Ti, and residual Na and Li contents from the starting materials was performed by inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES, Varian, Inc., 730-ES). The particle morphology was verified by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis (JEOL JSM-5400). The FTIR (Jasco, FT/IR-6100) spectrum was measured at room temperature by the KBr method.

Thermogravimetric analysis coupled to differential thermal analysis (TG–DTA) was performed using a Rigaku Thermo Plus TG–DTA apparatus. The experiments were performed by heating the $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ sample (about 14 mg) from 292 to 1273 K at a heating rate of 10 K/min in air atmosphere. $\alpha$-$Al_2O_3$ powder was used as a reference material.

The solid-state $^1$H magic-angle-spinning nuclear-magnetic-resonance ($^1$H-MAS NMR) spectrum of the $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ sample was measured at room temperature at 300.208 MHz with a spinning rate of 10 kHz for 1H nuclei using a Chemagnetics CMX-300 spectrometer. The pulse width was $4.2\ \mu$s ($\pi/2$ pulse). The chemical shift was recorded relative to polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS).

### 2.2. Crystal structure determination

The Rietveld refinement of $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ was performed by Jana2006 [13] using powder neutron diffraction (ND) data measured at room temperature with wave length $\lambda=1.8024\ \mathring{A}$ using a HERMES diffractometer (JAEA, JRR3M beam line T1-3) [14]. The ND intensity data collected for $0.05^\circ$ step over a $2\theta$ range from $7^\circ$ to $157^\circ$.

The nuclear scattering length density distribution (NSLDD) analysis was performed by the maximum entropy method (MEM) based on the Rietveld analysis using the powder ND data. The MEM calculation was carried out with the unit cell divided into $128\times128\times128$ pixels using a MEM analysis program PRIMA [15]. The crystal structure and MEM images were drawn using a computer program VESTA [16].

### 2.3. Physical property measurements

AC impedance measurement for $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ was conducted using a Solartron 1260 impedance analyzer operating at 10 mV applied ac amplitude at 32 MHz–0.1 Hz frequencies at room temperature in air. The polycrystalline sample measures impedance by a sintering body, but this sample cannot make a sintering compact to decompose by heating. In the present study, the impedance measurements were performed using the powder sample of 87.30 mg in the measurement holder under a pressure of 1.5 MPa by hydraulic press with the relative density of about 70%. The measurement holder is made by silicone resin with stainless steel blocking electrode, the size of the sample filling space is 10 mm in diameter, thickness 5 mm.

Optical absorption (UV-vis) spectrum was measured using a Jasco V-550 spectrometer over the range 300-600 nm at room temperature. From the intersection values of the baseline and absorption band edge in the obtained spectra, we estimated the band gap energy of H₂Ti₆O₁₃.

### 2.4. First-principles calculation

The electronic structures of H₂Ti₆O₁₃ were calculated using the full-potential linearized augmented-plane-wave (FLAPW) method with the WIEN2k program [17]. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the formulation of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof was used for the exchange and correlation terms [18]. The atomic muffin-tin sphere used was 0.690 a.u. for the H atom, 1.66 a.u. for the Ti atom, and 1.29 a.u. for the O atom. The plane-wave cutoff was $R_{\text{MT}} \times K_{\text{max}} = 6.0$, where $R_{\text{MT}}$ is the smallest atomic sphere radius in the unit cell and $K_{\text{max}}$ is the magnitude of the largest $\mathbf{k}$ vector. Self-consistency was carried out on a 400 $k$-point mesh in the full Brillouin zone. The energy criterion for self-consistency was set to less than 0.001 eV per formula unit. The present structural parameters for H₂Ti₆O₁₃ were used in the calculations.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Ion-exchange synthesis of H₂Ti₆O₁₃

Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns for the precursor Na₂Ti₆O₁₃, the starting Li₂Ti₆O₁₃, and the final product H₂Ti₆O₁₃ samples. All the samples were identified to be a single phase of the Na₂Ti₆O₁₃-related type structure, monoclinic crystal system, and space group $C2/m$; this was confirmed not only for the parent Na₂Ti₆O₁₃ and Li₂Ti₆O₁₃ samples [9] but also the H₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample of the present study. The lattice parameters determined by the XRD Rietveld refinement were listed in Table 1. The lattice volume $(505.66(1)\ \text{Å}^3)$ of H₂Ti₆O₁₃ was considerably smaller than those of the parent Na₂Ti₆O₁₃ and Li₂Ti₆O₁₃ compounds.

Chemical analysis by ICP-OES confirmed that the Na/Ti ratio was to be the stoichiometric composition for the precursor Na₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample, and that the residual Na contents were less than 0.04 wt.% for the Li₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample, as reported recently [9]. Furthermore, the residual Na and Li contents for the H₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample were determined to be less than 0.11 and 0.05 wt.%, respectively. These values were within the experimental error of the analysis. This fact indicates the successful completion of $\text{Na}^+/\text{Li}^+/\text{H}^+$ ion exchange reactions in the present synthetic route.

The TG, DTG and DTA curves for the H₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample in the temperature that range from 292 to 1273 K are shown in Fig. 2. Assuming that the formulation at the end of TG analysis is TiO₂, the observed weight loss (3.52 wt.%) between 606 and 904 K was in good agreement with the calculated one (3.62 wt.%) for the dehydration reaction as $\text{H}_2\text{Ti}_6\text{O}_{13} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O} \uparrow + 6\ \text{TiO}_2$. From this result, the chemical formula for the deduced hydrogen titanate in the present study could be estimated to be H₂Ti₆O₁₃. Furthermore, the FTIR absorption spectrum of the H₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample confirmed that the present sample contain hydrogen-oxygen bond such as $\text{OH}^-$ and did not contain water species such as $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ in the structure (Fig. 3). This result is in good agreement with the previous report of the H₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample prepared using H₂Ti₃O₇ as a starting compound [7].

Fig. 4 shows a typical SEM image of the H₂Ti₆O₁₃ sample after ion-exchange experiment. The average particle size was about 1 $\mu\text{m}$. The particle morphology and size remained nearly unchanged after ion-exchange reaction [9].

The basic $(Ti_{6}O_{13})^{2-}$ framework in $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ is maintained nearly unchanged from those in the parent $Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ and $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$. All of the three-type $TiO_{6}$ octahedra were strongly distorted, and the Ti-O distances were in the wide range of 1.733(9)-2.25(1) $\mathring{A}$. These features for the $(Ti_{6}O_{13})^{2-}$ unit are very similar to those observed in $Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ and $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ [9].

On the other hand, the difference in the local structures among $Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$, $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$, and $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ is clearly ascertained based on the shape of the tunnel space, as shown in Fig. 8. The expanded and distorted tunnel shape observed in $Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ and $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ has been changed to a condensed and the simple rectangular one in $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$. This local structural change may be caused by the hydrogen bonding O3-H1···O5' across the tunnel space. In fact, the oxygen-oxygen contact distance across the tunnel space was $2.917\ \mathring{A}$ for the O3-O5' distance in $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$, which is much smaller than the corresponding O3-O5' dis- tances (3.225 and $3.366\ \mathring{A}$) in $Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ and $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$, respectively [9].

### 3.3. Physical properties

Fig. 10 shows the Nyquist plot of the ac impedance of the pressed powder samples for $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ at room temperature. The tail of impedance plots at a low-frequency side indicates the blocking of the electrodes for mobile H ions. The impedance plot at a high-frequency side shows single semicircle behavior for the $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ sample. The conductivity could not be separated into bulk and grain boundary in the present experiments. The frequency at maximum $Z''$ within the HF arc is $4.03 \times 10^5$ Hz. The total protonic conductivity in $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ was estimated to be $\sigma_{total} = 8.32 \times 10^{-6}$ S cm$^{-1}$ at room temperature. This value is comparable to that in the ramsdellite-type $H_2Ti_3O_7$, the value of which was reported to be $\sigma_{total} = 2.66 \times 10^{-6}$ S cm$^{-1}$ at $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ [2]. However, we consider that there are influences of inter-particle, grain boundary, the adsorbed water in this measurement. We are trying to measure the conductivity of $H_2Ti_{12}O_{25}$ at high temperatures by low temperature sintered compact in now.

Fig. 11 shows the UV-vis absorption spectrum of $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ measured at room temperature. The estimated band gap for $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ is about 3.4 eV, the value of which is very similar to those for $Na_2Ti_6O_{13}$ and $Li_2Ti_6O_{13}$, as reported previously [9]. This fact supports the result of present structure analysis, that is, the basic $(Ti_6O_{13})^{2-}$ framework in $H_2Ti_6O_{13}$ is maintained nearly unchanged from those in the parent $Na_2Ti_6O_{13}$ and $Li_2Ti_6O_{13}$.

## 4. Conclusion

In the present study, lithium titanate, $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ was successfully prepared from $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ as a parent compound via $Li^{+}/H^{+}$ ion exchange in acidic solution at $60\ ^{\circ}C$. The phase purity and chemical composition of the ion-exchanged samples were well-characterized. The crystal structure of $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ was refined by Rietveld refinement using the powder neutron diffraction data. The basic $(Ti_{6}O_{13})^{2-}$ framework in $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ was maintained nearly unchanged from those in the parent $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ and $Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$. The hydrogen site in the tunnel space was successfully refined with the strict O3-H1 distance of $0.94(1)$ Å in $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$. The structural validity was confirmed by bond valence sum calculation, and the data of $^{1}$H-MAS NMR measurement. The AC impedance measurement revealed that $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ was one of the good protonic conductors among the hydrogen titanate compounds for the first time.

The low-temperature synthetic techniques such as ion-exchange reaction called "chimie douce" have resulted in major developments in the field of the solid-state chemistry of transition-metal oxides. In many cases, the framework structures of the parent compounds were maintained nearly unchanged; however, the local structural changes around alkali or hydrogen atoms were recently revealed by the precise structural studies [19]. In the hexatitanate $A_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ family with $A = Na$, Li, and H, an unusual $LiO_{4}$ planar coordination in $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ was revealed very recently [9]. The present H position in $H_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ is slightly shifted from the original Li position in the parent $Li_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$, but both of them situate the same plane of the cross-section of the tunnel space. On the contrary, they are drastically changed from the Na position in the starting $Na_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ compound. The difference in the local alkali or hydrogen position among the $A_{2}Ti_{6}O_{13}$ compounds may determine the reactivity in these solid compounds.
