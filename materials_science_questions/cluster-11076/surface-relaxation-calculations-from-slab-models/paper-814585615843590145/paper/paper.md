# Relaxation and surface states on wurtzite cleavage faces: $CdSe(10\overline{1}0)$

Y. R. Wang, C. B. Duke, and A. Paton
Xerox Webster Research Center, 800 Philips Road, 0114-38D, Webster, New York 14580

K. Stiles and A. Kahn
Department of Electrical Engineering, Princeton University, Princeton, New Jersey 08544

(Received 3 August 1987)

A $sp^{3}$ tight-binding model is utilized to predict the atomic geometry and electronic structure of $(10\overline{1}0)$ surface of wurtzite-structure CdSe. The model predicts a bond-rotation relaxation in the uppermost few atomic layers resulting in a displacement of top layer Se relative to Cd of $0.78\ \text{\AA}$ normal to the surface, and a surface bound state near the top of the valence band. Analyses of low-energy electron diffraction intensity data and ultraviolet photoemission spectra confirm these predictions.

Whether or not a surface state exists near the top of the valence band of the cleavage faces of wurtzite-structure II-VI semiconductors has been controversial for several years. Early calculations $^{1,2}$ for ZnO cleavage faces predicted no such states, and thus far photoemission experiments $^{3-7}$ have provided no definitive evidence either for or against them. In contrast, calculations for ZnS (Ref. 8) and ZnSe (Ref. 9) predicted the existence of relaxed surface atomic geometries which exhibit such surface states for the cleavage faces of both zinc-blende and wurtzite allotropes. These predictions are in good correspondence with both low-energy electron diffraction (LEED) surface structure determinations $^{10,11}$ and available photoemission data $^{12-15}$ for the zinc-blende (110) cleavage faces, but no data are yet available for the corresponding wurtzite surfaces. Finally, a reexamination $^{16}$ of the $ZnO(10\overline{1}0)$ and $(11\overline{2}0)$ cleavage faces revealed that the utilization of an incomplete basis set leads the original model $^{1,2}$ of these surfaces to predict neither relaxations nor surface states near the top of the valence band. The former prediction stands in clear contradiction with LEED structure analyses $^{17}$ for $ZnO(10\overline{1}0)$, but the analysis of available photoemission data remained ambiguous on the subject of the occurrence of the surface state. $^{16}$ Since the surface atomic relaxation is driven by the lowering in energy of this surface state from near midgap to the top of the valence band, $^{8,9,16}$ the failure to verify the occurrence of this state for a relaxed surface leaves the issue of the mechanism of surface relaxation on the cleavage faces of wurtzite-structure compound semiconductors unresolved.

To resolve this issue, we report herein an extension of the model calculations to $CdSe(10\overline{1}0)$ together with the results of a LEED structure analysis and photoemission measurements from this surface. The structure analysis supports the model predictions of the surface atomic geometry and the ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) measurements confirm the occurrence of the surface-state band in question at the predicted energies. Therefore, the LEED and photoemission results verify the model predictions of wurtzite cleavage faces and establish the commonality of the mechanism of their surface relaxations with that of zinc-blende (110) surfaces.

In order to predict the atomic geometry and surface electronic structure of CdSe(10$\overline{1}$0), we constructed em- pirical tight-binding nearest-neighbor $sp^{3}$ model. The on-site energies (Cd 5s, 5p; Se 4s, 4p) and the orbital in- teractions were obtained by fitting our calculated bulk band structure to the x-ray photoemission spectrum $^{23}$ (XPS) and optical transition data. $^{24,25}$ The tight-binding parameters so determined are listed in Table II. The bulk valence-band density of states (DOS) calculated from our model is compared with the XPS valence-band spectra $^{23}$ in panel (a) of Fig. 2.

The surface atomic geometry is calculated using an en- ergy minimization technique developed earlier. $^{27,28}$ This method emphasizes the fact that the surface reconstruc- tion is a result of balancing two contributions to the total energy. The electronic energy, because of the surface dangling bonds, can be reduced by displacing the surface atoms, but the elastic energies will be increased in so do- ing. The latter are computed from the observation that the bulk crystal is in equilibrium and that any small dis- placement of the bulk atoms will cost an elastic energy corresponding to the bulk modulus $B$. The elastic energy parameters for CdSe are listed in Table II, together with those of the tight-binding model used to calculate the elec- tronic energy. The influence of the surface reconstruction on the model parameters is incorporated via the $d^{-2}$ scal- ing law, as described elsewhere. $^{8,9,16}$

Using the calculated surface geometry, the surface electronic band structure can be predicted, with the results shown in Fig. 3. The state $S_{1}$ in Fig. 3 is derived from the back bonding between the first-layer Se atoms with second-layer Cd atoms. This state is at the position shown by the dot-dashed line if the surface is unreconstructed, and does not sink completely into the bulk band continuum near the $M$ point of the surface Brillouin zone after reconstruction. This surface state exerts a strong influence on the surface layer DOS, as shown in panel (b) of Fig. 2. The two sharp peaks near the top of the valence band are associated with the surface state $S_{1}$. The smaller of these arises from the surface resonance part of $S_{1}$ near the $\Gamma$ point of the Brillouin zone, and the larger one from the surface bound part of $S_{1}$ near the $M$ point. To simulate the situation in the photoemission experiment, we convolute our "raw" DOS by a Gaussian of width $\gamma$=0.3 eV. The smaller peak is absorbed in the leading edge of the smoothed surface DOS, but the larger peak remains, as is indicated by the dashed curve in panel (b) of Fig. 2. An ultraviolet photoemission spectrum obtained at room temperature on the same samples used for the LEED intensity data is shown by the dotted curve. The huge peak near the top of the valence band is at the position of our calculated surface state peak. It was shown to be preferentially sensitive to oxygen absorption, as expected for photoemission from surface states. Our calculations of the surface electronic band structure also predict several additional surface resonance bands, shown in Fig. 3 by dashed lines.

In summary, we used the XPS valence-band density of states and optical transitions of bulk CdSe to validate a $sp^{3}$ tight-binding model, and subsequently used this model to predict the atomic geometry and electronic band structure of CdSe(10$\overline{1}$0) surface. The predictions were verified by an angle-integrated photoemission experiment and LEED intensity analysis which confirmed the predicted surface state at the top of the valence band and the surface atomic geometry, respectively. We conclude, therefore, that the CdSe(10$\overline{1}$0) surface is significantly relaxed; that a surface bound state exists near the top of the valence band; and that consequently the mechanism of surface relaxation on the (10$\overline{1}$0) cleavage surfaces of wurtzite-structure compound semiconductors is analogous to that of the (110) cleavage surfaces of zinc-blende-structure compound semiconductors in spite of the distinctly different atomic connectivities of these two surfaces.
