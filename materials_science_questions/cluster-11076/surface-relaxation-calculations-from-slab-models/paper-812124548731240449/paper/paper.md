# Multilayer relaxation of fcc metals (001) surface:
## A modified embedded atom method study

Jian-Min Zhang $^{a,*}$, Yu Shu $^{a}$, Ke-Wei Xu $^{b}$

$^{a}$ College of Physics and Information Technology, Shaanxi Normal University, Xian 710062, Shaanxi, People's Republic of China
$^{b}$ State-Key Laboratory for Mechanical Behavior of Materials, Xian Jiaotong University, Xian 710049, Shaanxi, People's Republic of China

Received 26 November 2005; accepted 10 December 2005 by A.H. MacDonald
Available online 4 January 2006

## Abstract

The (001) surface multilayer relaxation results calculated by the modified embedded atom method (MEAM) show that Ni, Al, Rh and Ir (001) surface are 'anomalous' outward relaxation, while Cu, Ag, Au, Pd, Pt and Pb (001) surface are inward relaxation. For the inward relaxation metals, the relaxation between the first two layers increase for the 3d, 4d and 5d metals at the same column in the periodic table, successively. The expansion (contraction) between the first two layers at fcc (001) surfaces is accompanied by the decrease (increase) in the electronic density at the lattice of the first two layers. The surface energies results show that the surface energies decrease for all fcc (001) surfaces due to relaxation, whereas the changes not more than 5%.

## 1. Introduction

Relaxation and reconstruction processes at metal surfaces have attracted the interest of the scientific community for decades. The reason is that knowledge of the geometrical arrangement of the atoms in the vicinity of the surface is a basic ingredient for any study of structural and properties of a metal surface. When a bulk material is divided into two to create surfaces, electrons near the surface will respond to a new environment and readjust to lower their kinetic energy, and so atoms near the surface are under the influence of other forces different from those in the bulk, and in most case relaxation and reconstruction of the bulk lattice will occur in the topmost layers. With modern experimental techniques, such as low energy electron diffraction (LEED), medium energy ion scattering (MEIS), high energy ion scattering (HEIS) and high resolution electron energy loss spectroscopy (HREELS), the multilayer relaxation phenomena have been detected at a few low index surfaces of fcc metals [1–16]. Even for (001) surface, the usually observed phenomena are inward relaxation [7–9,14,16], but an ‘anomalous’ outward relaxation appeared in Ni, Al and Rh [10,12,13,15]. Several semi-empirical calculations, such as effective-crystal theory (ECT) [17], effective medium theory (EMT) [18], embedded atom method (EAM) [19,20] and tight-binding potential (TB) [21], yielded only inward relaxations for low index surfaces of all fcc metals. These are not in agreement with the experimental results. Considering directional bonding of atoms in crystal, Baskes modified initial EAM [22,23] and developed the modified embedded atom method (MEAM) [24–26]. In previous papers, we used MEAM to calculate the surface energy [27–29] and interface energy [30]. Many experimental results related to these face defects have been explained satisfactory.

In the present work, the relaxations at top eight layers of (001) surface have been simulated by using MEAM for 10 fcc metals: Cu, Ag, Au, Ni, Pd, Pt, Al, Pb, Rh and Ir. It is found that, the fcc metals may be grouped into two classes. In the first group (Ni, Al, Rh and Ir), the (001) surface is ‘anomalous’ outward relaxation. While in the second group (Cu, Ag, Au, Pd, Pt and Pb), the (001) surface is inward relaxation. These results are now in most agreement with available experimental results and the first-principle (FP) calculation result for Al [31]. In addition, calculations also show that for all fcc metals the decrease in (001) surface energy is less than 5% due to relaxation.

## 2. Computational methods

In the MEAM, the total energy of a system $E_{\text{tot}}$ is presented as [25]:

$$
E_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_{i}\left[F_{i}\left(\bar{\rho}_{i}\right)+\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j(\neq i)} \phi_{i j}\left(R_{i j}\right)\right]
\tag{1}
$$

where the embedding function $F_{i}(\bar{\rho}_{i})$ is the energy to embed atom $i$ into the background electron density $\bar{\rho}_{i}$ at site $i$, and $\phi_{i j}(R_{i j})$ is a pair interaction between atoms $i$ and $j$ separated by a distance $R_{i j}$.

The term in parenthesis in Eq. (1) denotes the contribution to the total energy from the $i$th atom

$$
E_{i}=F_{i}\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}_{i}}{Z_{i}}\right)+\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j(\neq i)} \phi_{i j}\left(R_{i j}\right)
\tag{2}
$$

For simplification, Baskes has normalized the background electron density by the number of the nearest neighbors $Z_{i}$ defined as the reference structure of a type-$i$ atom. The reference structure is usually the equilibrium crystal structure of type-$i$ atoms.

For the case of homogeneous monatomic solid with interactions limited to the first neighbors only, the energy of each atom in this reference structure is shown simply as

$$
E_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(R)=F_{i}\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}_{i}^{0}(R)}{Z_{i}}\right)+\frac{Z_{i}}{2} \phi_{i i}(R)
\tag{3}
$$

where $\bar{\rho}_{i}^{0}(R)$ is the background electron density for the reference structure of atom $i$ and $R$ is the nearest-neighbor distance. Assuming the energy of the reference structure $E_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(R)$ is given by the universal energy function of Rose et al. [32]

$$
E_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(R)=-E_{i}^{0}\left[1+\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{R}{R_{i}^{0}}-1\right)\right] \exp \left[-\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{R}{R_{i}^{0}}-1\right)\right]
\tag{4}
$$

where $E_{i}^{0}$ is the sublimation energy, $R_{i}^{0}$ is the equilibrium first neighbor distance, $\alpha_{i}=\sqrt{9 B \Omega / E_{i}^{0}}$, $B$ is the bulk modulus, and $\Omega$ is the equilibrium atom volume. The pair interaction for type-$i$ atoms is then shown as

$$
\phi_{i i}(R)=\frac{2}{Z_{i}}\left[E_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}(R)-F_{i}\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}_{i}(R)}{Z_{i}}\right)\right]
\tag{5}
$$

Using this definition of the pair interaction, Baskes gives the energy contribution $E_{i}$ for any configuration of atoms

$$
E_{i}=\frac{1}{Z_{i}} \sum_{j(\neq i)} E_{i}^{\mathrm{u}}\left(R_{i j}\right)+\left[F_{i}\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}_{i}}{Z_{i}}\right)-\frac{1}{Z_{i}} \sum_{j(\neq i)} F\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}_{i}^{0}\left(R_{i j}\right)}{Z_{i}}\right)\right]
\tag{6}
$$

Baskes has also generalized the original background electron densities of EAM (Eq. (7)) by defining a series of correction electron densities that explicitly depend upon the relative positions of neighbors of atom $i$ (Eqs. (8)-(10))

$$
\rho_{i}^{(0)}=\sum_{j(\neq i)} \rho_{j}^{\alpha(0)}\left(R_{i j}\right)
\tag{7}
$$

$$
\left(\rho_{i}^{(1)}\right)^{2}=\sum_{\alpha}\left[\sum_{j(\neq i)} x_{i j}^{\alpha} \rho_{j}^{\alpha(1)}\left(R_{i j}\right)\right]^{2}
\tag{8}
$$

$$
\left(\rho_{i}^{(2)}\right)^{2}=\sum_{\alpha, \beta}\left[\sum_{j(\neq i)} x_{i j}^{\alpha} x_{i j}^{\beta} \rho_{j}^{\alpha(2)}\left(R_{i j}\right)\right]^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left[\sum_{j(\neq i)} \rho_{j}^{\alpha(2)}\left(R_{i j}\right)\right]^{2}
\tag{9}
$$

$$
\left(\rho_{i}^{(3)}\right)^{2}=\sum_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma}\left[\sum_{j(\neq i)} x_{i j}^{\alpha} x_{i j}^{\beta} x_{i j}^{\gamma} \rho_{j}^{\alpha(3)}\left(R_{i j}\right)\right]^{2}
\tag{10}
$$

where $\rho_{j}^{\alpha(l)}(R_{i j})\ (l=0-3)$ represents the atomic electron density of a type-$j$ atom at a distance of $R_{i j}$ from site $i$. $x_{i j}^{\alpha}=R_{i j}^{\alpha}/R_{i j}$, $R_{i j}^{\alpha}$ is the $\alpha$ component of distance vector between atom $j$ and $i$. The total background electron density is expressed by taking a weighted sum of the squares of the partial background densities

$$
\left(\bar{\rho}_{i}\right)^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{3} t_{i}^{(l)}\left(\rho_{i}^{(l)}\right)^{2}
\tag{11}
$$

with $t_{i}^{(l)}\ (l=0-3)$ as the weighting factors as well as $t_{i}^{(0)}=1$.

### 3. Results and discussion

Calculated multilayer relaxations of (001) surface are listed in Table 2. For fcc metals Cu, Ag, Au, Ni, Pd, Pt, Al, Pb, Rh and Ir, together with experimental data and the results calculated by other theories for comparing. $\Delta d_{n,n+1}$ is the change (including both distance change and percentage change) in the interlayer spacing between layer $n$ and layer $n+1$ ($n=1,2,3,4,5$) as compared to the bulk (ideal) interlayer spacing. The changes in the interlayer spacing between the following layers ($n\geq6$) are such small to be ignored. The negative (positive) values indicate contraction (expansion) of the corresponding interlayer spacing. The relationships between the relative relaxation $\Delta d_{n,n+1}(\%)$ and the relative change in electronic density at the lattice of the top five layers $\Delta \rho/\rho_{0}(\%)$ are shown in Fig. 1.

It can be seen that, the fcc metals can be grouped into two classes according to the (001) surface relaxation. In the first group (Ni, Al, Ir and Rh), the (001) surface is 'anomalous' outward relaxation. While in the second group (Cu, Ag, Au, Pd, Pt and Pb) the (001) surface is inward relaxation.

For the (001) surfaces of the outward relaxation, the relative relaxation magnitude between the first two layers $\Delta d_{12}$ is $2.53\%$ (Ni), $0.80\%$ (Al), $0.59\%$ (Ir) and $0.2\%$ (Rh), which compares well with available experimental data of $2\%$ (Ni) [10], $1.8\%$ (Al) [12] and $(0.5\pm2)\%$ (Rh) [13]. For Ir, the result of $0.59\%$ is also in the range of the measured value of $(-3.6\pm5)\%$ [14]. While in previous theoretical calculations, only the first principle [31] and ab initio pseudopotential [33] obtain outward relaxation for Al (001) surface. For Ni and Al, the changes in interlayer spacing $\Delta d_{n,n+1}$ between layer $n$ and layer $n+1$ ($n=1,2,3,4,5$) are all positive (expansion) and the relaxation magnitude damps in order, that is $\Delta d_{12}>\Delta d_{23}>$ $\Delta d_{34}>\Delta d_{45}>0$. While the interlayer relaxations of Ir and Rh (001) surfaces are some different from that of Ni and Al, they are neither consistent expansion, nor consistent damp. For examples, $\Delta d_{23}=\Delta d_{34}<0$ for Rh and $0=\Delta d_{23}>\Delta d_{34}$ for Ir.

For inward relaxation metals Cu, Ag, Au, Pd, Pt and Pb, the relative relaxation magnitude between the first two layers $\Delta d_{12}$ is $-0.83\%$ (Cu), $-1.04\%$ (Ag), $-5.84\%$ (Au), $-1.97\%$ (Pd), $-2.11\%$ (Pt) and $-6.14\%$ (Pb). The results are according better with available experimental data than the other theoretical results. It can be seen that, from Fig. 1, an oscillatory $(-\ +\ -)$ damping $(|\Delta d_{12}|>|\Delta d_{23}|>|\Delta d_{34}|)$ phenomenon appears for all of these inward relaxation metals, and the contraction percentages between the first two layers $\Delta d_{12}$ increase for 3d, 4d and 5d metals at same column in the periodic table, such as IB family elements Cu, Ag and Au ($-0.83$, $-1.04$ and $-5.84\%$) and VIIIB family elements Pd and Pt ($-1.97$ and $-2.11\%$).

## 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have used MEAM method to study the multilayer relaxation and surface energy of fcc metals (001) surface. Our calculations show that the relaxation of Ni, Al, Ir and Rh (001) surfaces are 'anomalous' outward relaxation, while that of Cu, Ag, Au, Pd, Pt and Pb (001) surfaces are inward relaxation. These results are in agreement with the results of measured and FP when available. For the inward relaxation metals, the relaxation between the first two layers increase for the 3d, 4d and 5d metals at the same column in the periodic table, successively. The expansions (contractions) between the first two layers at fcc (001) surfaces correspond to the decrease (increase) of the electronic density at the lattice of the first two layers. The relative changes in electronic density for both cases are in proportion to corresponding relative relaxation magnitudes between the first two layers $\Delta d_{12}$. The surface energies results show that the surface energies decrease for all fcc (001) surfaces due to relaxation, whereas the changes not more than $5\%$.
