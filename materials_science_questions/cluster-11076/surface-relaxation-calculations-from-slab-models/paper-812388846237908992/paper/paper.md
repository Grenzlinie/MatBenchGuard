# Surface morphology and interaction between water and MgO, CaO and SrO surfaces
## Periodic HF and DFT calculations

W.S. Abdel Halim$^{\text{a}}$, A.S. Shalabi$^{\text{b},*}$

$^{\text{a}}$Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science, Zagazig University, Zagazig, Egypt
$^{\text{b}}$Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science, Qatar University, P.O. Box 2713, Doha, Qatar

Received 22 May 2002; accepted 15 April 2003

## Abstract

The dissociation of water molecule at the defect free $(\begin{array}{lll}0&0&1\end{array})$, $(\begin{array}{lll}1&1&0\end{array})$ and $(\begin{array}{lll}2&1&0\end{array})$ five-layer films of the title oxides has been investigated using the periodic HF and DFT levels of ab initio theory together with the $1 \times 1$ supercell model in which two water molecules were added to each supercell, one at each side of the film. The water/oxide reaction energies calculated at the HF-level exhibit endothermic character at the $(\begin{array}{lll}0&0&1\end{array})$ film and exothermic character at the $(\begin{array}{lll}1&1&0\end{array})$ and $(\begin{array}{lll}2&1&0\end{array})$ films, while those calculated at the DFT-level always exhibit exothermic character except for the $(\begin{array}{lll}0&0&1\end{array})$ film of MgO. The interaction energies increase monotonically in the order $(\begin{array}{lll}0&0&1\end{array}) < (\begin{array}{lll}2&1&0\end{array}) < (\begin{array}{lll}1&1&0\end{array})$ at the HF and DFT levels. The surface morphology of the title oxides is confirmed by considering the binding energies, surface formation energies, relaxation energies, Mulliken population analysis, charge electron density maps, electrostatic potential maps and the vibrational frequencies of the two involved OH bonds at the HF-level. The results were compared with the available experimental data.

## 1. Introduction

The mechanism of interaction between water mole- cules and oxide surfaces has been the subject of detailed theoretical and experimental investigations for understanding some fundamental phenomenon such as heterogeneous catalysis, corrosion, passiva- tion and modeling of earth substance hydrodynamics [1–7]. Although there is a great deal of theoretical and experimental data on the interaction of water with MgO, less information are available for CaO and SrO. This may be attributed to the fact that magnesium is the fifth most abundant element in the earth’s crust [8], so its oxide may be used as a model of a soil surface and hence can be utilised for examining water–soil interaction. Moreover, MgO has been used as a cat- alyst in the decomposition of hydrogen and small hydrocarbons [9–14] and is well characterised both computationally [15] and experimentally [16].

The second alkaline earth oxide CaO [17] occurs naturally in small amounts enveloped in blocks of lava at Vesuvius. The geometry of the $(\begin{array}{lll}1&0&0\end{array})$ surface of CaO is virtually identical to that of MgO $(\begin{array}{lll}1&0&0\end{array})$ in that the surface is almost an exact termination of the bulk crystal

The geometric and electronic structure of the third alkaline earth oxide SrO [18] is also very similar to MgO. Chemically the oxides of the larger cations are more basic and thus more reactive to $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}, \mathrm{CO}_{2}$ and other acidic molecules. However, there has been very little work done on single-crystal samples of CaO and SrO. We have therefore focus our attention to the morphology and interaction between water molecules and MgO, CaO and SrO oxide surfaces using the capabilities of periodic lattice calculations at the HF and DFT levels of ab initio theory.

The present study is scheduled as follows: In Section 2, the computational details of HF and DFT methods and basis sets employed in the calculations, using CRYSTAL code, are given. In Section 3, the results and discussion are outlined in three subsections: geometry, energetics and properties. In the first subsection, the geometry of the clean and hydroxylated surfaces are presented. In the second subsection, the energetics of binding, surface formation, water/solid interactions and relaxation are considered. In the third subsection, the electronic structure and vibrational frequencies of the two involved OH bonds are analysed. Finally, the most important results are collected in the conclusion section.

## 2. Computational details

The calculations presented in this paper have been performed using the periodic CRYSTAL code [19]. The details and implementation of the method have been described earlier [20-22].

Since the studied systems have closed shell electronic configuration, the restricted Hartree-Fock (RHF) option has been used. Moreover, three formulations of the density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using, in the first one, Becke's three parameter hybrid functional B3 [23] with the non-local correlation LYP functional. This B3LYP hybrid functional includes a mixture of a Hartree-Fock exchange with DFT exchange correlation and is based on the exact form of the Vosko-Wilk-Nusair correlation potential [24] that is used to extract the local part of the LYP correlation potential. The second formulation is the PWGGA in which the GGA Perdew-Wang exchange potential is used in combination with the non-local GGA Perdew-Wang correlation functional [25-28]. Finally, we used the LDA-VWN, which probably is the most popular LDA formulation in which the Dirac-Slater exchange potential [29] is used in combination with the correlation VWN functional, Vosko-Wilk-Nusair parameterisations of the Ceperly-Alder free electron gas correlation results [24].

The all electron basis sets are employed in this work for Mg, Ca and O, whereas for Sr the Hay-Wadt small-core ECPs have been adopted. The description of these basis sets, their exponents and the used crystal lattice parameters are identical to those of ref. [30]. The water basis set was optimised to be (2-111/84-11) for H and O, respectively. For the DFT calculations, the auxiliary even-tempered basis sets (12s 6p 2d 1g 1i/14s 6p 4d 1f 1g), (12s 4p 2d 1f 1g/14s 6p 4d 1f 1g) and (12s 4g 2i/14s 1g) gaussian-type functions-contractions were used for MgO, CaO and SrO respectively. For adsorbed water it was (9s 2p 1g) for H and (12s 4p 3d 2f 1g) gaussian-type functions-contractions for O.

In the present calculations, we use the defect-free five-layer $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array}),(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 0\end{array})$ and $(\begin{array}{lll}2 & 1 & 0\end{array})$ films of the title oxides. The adsorption is simulated with the supercell scheme that consists of building a unit cell of the host crystal which is a multiple of the original cell, so that the system remains periodic in two dimensions. In order to study adsorption, an adequate description of the surface must be given. We use slabs to simulate the semi-infinite systems. In other words, our model system has two-dimensional periodicity parallel to the surface and is finite along the perpendicular direction. The thickness of the slab is so chosen that the interaction between two surfaces of the slab becomes negligibly small. This has been done by increasing the slab thickness until the convergence in the surface formation energies is confirmed.

In the present case, the $1 \times 1$ supercell has been used in which there is only one metal and one O atom per each layer. However, two water molecules were added to each supercell (one at each side of the film) in such a way that leads to hydroxylation of surface cations and hydrogenation of surface oxygen, and the calculations were performed using the default accuracy control values [19].

### 3. Results and discussion

#### 3.1. Geometry

##### 3.1.1. Clean surfaces
The first step in our calculations was to determine the most stable film which would be considered as the starting point for calculating surface properties. The calculated HF surface formation energies that correspond to 1,3,5 and 7 atomic layers of the (0 0 1), (1 1 0) and (2 1 0) films of the clean (defect free) oxides, MgO, CaO and SrO are presented in Table 1, from which it is clear that the five-layer film is sufficiently thick to provide a converged value of the surface formation energy (see Section 3.2.1).

We then tested the clean surfaces for the relaxation along the z-axis that is perpendicular to the slab. The mode of relaxation was adopted to allow the five atoms to the inward–outward displacements all together simultaneously. We started to calculate the relaxation energy of surface cations till the equilibrium positions were reached. The equilibrium positions of surface cations were then taken as a reference geometry for calculating the relaxation energy of surface anions. We found that surface cations tend to displace inward (along the negative z-axis) towards the bulk of the crystal, in the three considered films except the (0 0 1) of CaO and SrO. Surface oxygens, on the other hand, also prefer the inward displacement in the three films except the (0 0 1) of MgO. However, calculations of the relaxation energies, which are the equilibrium energy differences between the relaxed and unrelaxed surfaces, of the three films were found to be insignificant. Consequently, we concluded that the clean (defect-free) unrelaxed five-layer film is a good starting point for studying the structural and surface properties of the three alkaline earth oxide crystals, MgO, CaO and SrO at the three considered orientations (0 0 1), (1 1 0) and (2 1 0).

In spite of the fact that relaxation and adsorption geometries concerning the (2 1 0) face should be studied not only in the z-axis direction but also in the z and x direction (zx being the reflection plane, and the z-axis is not a symmetry axis, although it is contained in a plane of reflection). However, the present study ignored such case due to the excessive computational burden, and so, all results concerning the (2 1 0) face are regarded as less credible.

(ii) Vertical addition of one water molecule on each side of the film: The water molecule is added vertically above the surface cation, with O oriented down. Although the present work studies only the dissociative chemisorption, however, the non-dissociative water adsorption could be fa- voured in other cases, especially if co-operative interaction between adsorbed molecules is al- lowed to occur. This, however, requires the $1 \times 1$ film (consists of only one cation and one anion per each atomic layer) to be extended to larger sizes.

(iii) Optimisation of the bond lengths of the reactive species, H and OH, over the clean surface: This was done by first optimising the distance between the H adatom and the surface oxygen, $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_{\text{s}}$, while keeping the distance between the O adatom of the OH group and the surface cation fixed. We have then optimised the distance between the O adatom of the OH group and the surface cation, $\text{O}\cdots\text{M}^{2+}$. Finally, optimisation of the bond length of the hydroxyl group, $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_{\text{w}}$ was carried out.

The values of $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_{\text{s}}$ bond lengths of the three oxides MgO, CaO and SrO were calculated to be 0.939, 0.951 and 0.943 Å at the (0 0 1) plane, 0.961, 0.954 and 0.951 Å at the (1 1 0) plane and 0.955, 0.951 and 0.948 Å at the (2 1 0) plane. The values of $\text{O}\cdots\text{M}^{2+}$ bond lengths were calculated to be 1.778, 1.982 and 2.103 Å at the (0 0 1) plane, 1.781, 2.001 and 2.126 Å at the (1 1 0) plane and 1.816, 2.046 and 2.176 Å at the (2 1 0) plane. The values of $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_{\text{w}}$ bond lengths were calculated to be 0.927, 0.933 and 0.934 Å at the (0 0 1) plane, 0.928, 0.933 and 0.934 Å at the (1 1 0) plane, and 0.930, 0.934 and 0.935 Å at the (2 1 0) plane.

(iv) Since the equilibrium interatomic distances of the adsorbed species, H and OH, are well defined over the clean surfaces, we tested the effects of relaxation induced by adding water molecules on surface hydroxylation. Starting from the equili- brium geometry of $\text{H}_{2}\text{O}$/oxide films [(0 0 1), (1 1 0) and (2 1 0)] this was done in a cyclic process as follows:
(a) The five species namely, surface cation $(\text{M}^{2+})$, surface oxygen $(\text{O}_{\text{s}})$, adsorbed hydrogen $(\text{H}_{\text{s}})$, adsorbed oxygen $(\text{O}_{\text{w}})$ and the hydroxyl hydrogen $(\text{H}_{\text{w}})$ were simultaneously relaxed along the $z$-axis in steps of $\pm 0.05$ Å till the equilibrium position was reached.

(b) Starting from the equilibrium position ob- tained in (a), we relaxed $\text{M}^{2+}$, $\text{O}_{\text{w}}$ and $\text{H}_{\text{w}}$ only. This led to the definition of a new equilibrium position for $\text{M}^{2+}$.

(c) Starting from the equilibrium position ob- tained in (b), we relaxed $\text{H}_{\text{s}}$ and $\text{O}_{\text{s}}$ only. This led to the definition of a new equilibrium position for $\text{O}_{\text{s}}$.

(d) Starting from the equilibrium position ob- tained in (c), we relaxed $\text{H}_{\text{w}}$ and $\text{O}_{\text{w}}$ only, to determine the equilibrium interatomic dis- tance between $\text{O}_{\text{w}}$ and $\text{M}^{2+}$.

(e) Starting from the equilibrium position obtained in (d), we relaxed $\text{H}_{\text{s}}$ only, to determine its equi- librium position over the surface oxygen $\text{O}_{\text{s}}$.

(f) Finally, starting from the equilibrium position obtained in (e), we relaxed $\text{H}_{\text{w}}$ only, to determine its equilibrium position over the oxygen adatom $\text{O}_{\text{w}}$.

We used the polynomial fitting programs, BULK and LOPTCG, implemented in CRYSTAL98.

From the final equilibrium geometries at the three considered films (summarised in Table 2), we con- cluded:

(i) Surface cations tend to relax outward (along the positive $z$ axis) except the (2 1 0) plane of MgO, while surface anions tend to relax inward (along the negative $z$ axis) except the (0 0 1) planes of CaO and SrO.

(ii) The $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_{\text{w}}$ bond distances did not exhibit any change and the $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_{\text{s}}$ bond distances were marginally increased over the (0 0 1) plane by 0.053, 0.016 and 0.017 for MgO, CaO and SrO respectively. The changes of $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_{\text{s}}$ bond distances over the (1 1 0) and (2 1 0) planes did not exceed 0.002 Å.

### 3.2.2. The water/solid interaction energies
The water/solid interaction energies have been calculated using the equation.
$$
E_{\text{hyd.}} = \frac{1}{2}[E(2\text{H}_2\text{O/MO}) - 2E(\text{H}_2\text{O}) - E(\text{MO})]
$$
where $E\ (2\text{H}_2\text{O/MO})$ is the energy of the film after the addition of two water molecules at the equilibrium binding distance, $E(\text{H}_2\text{O})$ is the energy of an isolated water molecule and $E(\text{MO})$ is the energy of the clean film. The obtained energy was then divided by 2 in order to get the hydroxylation (hydration) energy per water molecule. From the interaction energies and equilibrium geometries of the adsorbate and substrate (presented in Table 2), three facts may emerge:

(i) At the HF-level, the three oxides show endothermic behaviour at the $(0\ 0\ 1)$ film while they have exothermic character at both of the $(1\ 1\ 0)$ and $(2\ 1\ 0)$ films. The use of B3LYP technique of DFT theory leads to exothermic interaction energies between water and the three oxides except the $(0\ 0\ 1)$ surface of MgO which still has an endothermic interaction energy.

(ii) At the HF and DFT levels, the interaction energies were found to increase in the order MgO < CaO < SrO whatever the considered film.

(iii) At the HF and DFT levels, the interaction energies of the three oxides increase in the order $(0\ 0\ 1) < (2\ 1\ 0) < (1\ 1\ 0)$.

The present interaction energy at the HF-level for the $(0\ 0\ 1)$ plane of MgO is in complete agreement with the values reported earlier [3,32].

### 3.2.3. Relaxation energy
The relaxation energies after the addition of water molecules were calculated again as the energy difference between the relaxed and unrelaxed surfaces. Their values are summarised in Table 2, from which it is clear that the relaxation energies are also insignificant, although there is a recognisable increase in their values at the three films of MgO with respect to those reported at the same planes of the water free relaxed surfaces. Therefore, we concluded that the three alkaline earth oxides have small tendencies toward the vertical relaxation.

The $\text{H}\cdots\text{O}_\text{s}$ bond at the $(2\ 1\ 0)$ face was tested for the bending motion at the z-axis around an axis passing through such bond and perpendicular to the surface oxygen atom. This bending motion led to insignificant changes in the energies of the three oxides (ca. 0.0001 hartree).

### 3.3.2. The vibrational frequencies
The anharmonic O⋯H stretching frequency has been computed at the HF level for the two OH bonds over (0 0 1), (1 1 0) and (2 1 0) planes of the three oxides, starting from their HF equilibrium geometries. The adopted procedure for the calculations can be summarised, as reported before in [33], as follows:

(i) The O–H distance has been assumed as a normal co-ordinate decoupled with respect to all other modes, in agreement with previous work [34].
(ii) This distance was allowed to vary around its equilibrium value and the potential energy calculated at the HF-level for each distance (25 energy points evaluated).
(iii) A polynomial curve of sixth degree was used to best fit the energy points, ensuring a root mean square error below $10^{-6}$ hartree.
(iv) The corresponding nuclear Schrödinger equation was solved numerically following the method proposed in [35], by means of the program ANHARM [36] which gives the vibrational energy levels.

The first two vibrational transitions, namely the fundamental ($\omega_{01}$) and the first overtone ($\omega_{02}$), and the anharmonicity constant ($\omega_{\mathrm{e}}\chi_{\mathrm{e}}$) of the OH stretching mode are obtained from the eigenvalues $E_{0}$, $E_{1}$ and $E_{2}$ following the scheme [37]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_{01} &= E_{1}-E_{2}, & \omega_{02} &= E_{2}-E_{0} \\
\omega_{\mathrm{e}}\chi_{\mathrm{e}} &= \frac{1}{2}(2\omega_{01}-\omega_{02}), & \omega_{\mathrm{e}} &= \omega_{01}+2\omega_{\mathrm{e}}\chi_{\mathrm{e}}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\omega_{\mathrm{e}}$ is the harmonic frequency.

From the vibrational frequencies data (presented in Table 4), the following are clear:

(i) For the three films (0 0 1), (2 1 0) and (1 1 0) of the considered oxides, the harmonic frequencies of the H⋯Ow bonds are much greater than those of the H⋯Os bonds. This may be explained on basis of bond lengths and bond population analysis reported in the previous section, where the corresponding data show that the H⋯Ow bond is stronger than the H⋯Os bond.
(ii) The harmonic frequencies of H⋯Oss increase in the order MgO < CaO < SrO, while those of H⋯Ow increase in the opposite order.

## 4. Conclusions

An ab initio quantum mechanical treatment of defect free $(0\ 0\ 1)$, $(1\ 1\ 0)$ and $(2\ 1\ 0)$ five-layer films of the three alkaline earth oxides, MgO, CaO and SrO and their interaction with water molecule using $1\times1$ supercell model at the HF and DFT levels of ab initio theory leads to the following conclusions:

(1) The three oxides have small tendency towards the relaxation before and after the addition of water molecule.

(2) The binding and surface formation energies of the three oxides increase in the order, SrO < CaO < MgO, while the stability of their films with respect to the surface formation energies increase in the order, (2 1 0) < (1 1 0) < (0 0 1).

(3) The water/solid interaction energies increase in the order, MgO < CaO < SrO, whatever the considered film, and in the order, (0 0 1) < (2 1 0) < (1 1 0), whatever the considered oxide.

(4) At the HF-level, the (1 1 0) and (2 1 0) films have exothermic behaviour towards the interaction with water, and the (0 0 1) film has endothermic behaviour. While at the B3LYP-level, the three films show exothermic characters except the (0 0 1) of MgO.

Such conclusions were confirmed by considering the dropped electrostatic potential maps as well as the vibrational frequencies of the involved OH bonds.
