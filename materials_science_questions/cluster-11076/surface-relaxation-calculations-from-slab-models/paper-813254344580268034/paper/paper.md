### ABSTRACT

In order to explore the interfacial structure of $Mg/Al_4C_3$ interface and clarify the heterogeneous nucleation potential of $Al_4C_3$ particles in Mg melt. The atomic structure, bonding, and interfacial energy of $Mg/Al_4C_3$ interfaces were studied by first-principles calculations to analyze the sequence of Mg atoms onto the surface of $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slab. Surface energy calculations show that the outmost layer of $Al_4C_3$ free surface having a preference of C atom termination. And polar covalent/ionic mixed bonds are formed across interface during the combination of Mg atoms with C-terminated $Al_4C_3$ surface. The calculated interfacial energy of $Mg/Al_4C_3$ interface is much smaller than that between $\alpha$-Mg and magnesium melts, proving the excellent nucleation potency of $Al_4C_3$ particles for $\alpha$-Mg grains from interfacial atomic structure and atomic bonding energy considerations.

### 1. Introduction

In the past decades, magnesium alloy have been rapidly developed and applied in the automobile and aircraft industries due to their low density and high specific strength. However, the mechanical properties of most commonly-used magnesium alloys, such as AM series (Mg-Al-Mn) and AZ series (Mg-Al-Zn) alloys, are relatively low and cannot meet the requirement of many applications [1]. It is known that grain refinement generally results in an increase in mechanical properties, such as yield strength, toughness and ductility, for most alloys. Thus, the search for effective grain refiners applicable to Mg alloys and investigation on its grain refinement mechanism are active topics and attract more and more attention. Among grain refining approaches developed for Mg-Al based alloys, carbon inoculation by introducing different carbon-containing compound, such as $C_2Cl_6$ [2], SiC [3] and $MgCO_3$ [4], is one of the major and effective one. The most commonly accepted grain refinement mechanism of carbon inoculation is $Al_4C_3$ nucleus hypothesis proposed by Emley [5], namely, carbon reacts with Al in the melt to form a large number of aluminum carbide particles which in turn act as the potent nucleation substrates for primary $\alpha$-Mg grain. This hypothesis was used to explain the observed facts in many experimental investigations on carbon inoculation process later [3,4,6,7].

The nucleation potency of a potential heterogeneous substrate to a considerable degree depends on the interfacial atomic structures and energy between the nucleating crystal and substrate. The interfacial structure between $Al_4C_3$ and $\alpha$-Mg has been investigated and discussed according to the lattice disregistry theory [6] and the edge-to-edge matching model [8] from a crystallographic point of view. However, the atomic interactions across the interface between $Al_4C_3$ and $\alpha$-Mg are not clear yet. What's more, it is a pity that the solid-solid interfacial energy has not been quantitatively measured by experimental method so far. Therefore, the deeper identification of interfacial structures at the atomic scale and theoretical calculations of the interfacial energy are necessary to demonstrate the nucleation potency of $Al_4C_3$ particles for $\alpha$-Mg grain. In recent years, the first-principles methods based on density functional theory (DFT) have been rapidly applied to study the metal/compound adhesion [9-11]. Except for accurately providing the detailed atomic and electronic structures of the interface, first-principles calculating method can also be used to quantitatively predict the interfacial energy and the interfacial structure between heterogeneous nucleus and primary phase in the solidification [12,13].

In this work the work of adhesion ($W_{ad}$) and interfacial energy ($\gamma$) of several representative interfaces formed between the most stable surfaces of Mg and $Al_4C_3$ with HCP lattice, namely, the $Mg(0002)/Al_4C_3(0001)$ interface including two terminations of by selecting the energetically preferred structure, were calculated.

## 2. Computational methodology

All calculations in this work were performed by first-principles calculation method based on density functional theory (DFT) [14]. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [15] was used to treat the exchange interactions and correlation effects. The plane-wave cutoff energy was selected as 340 eV. The Brillouin zone is sampled by using $9 \times 9 \times 6$, $9 \times 9 \times 1$ and $4 \times 4 \times 1$ for bulk calculation, surface convergence test and the energy calculation of surface and interface, respectively. All atoms were relaxed to their equilibrium positions when the total energy changes during the optimization finally converged to less than $1 \times 10^{-5}$ eV/atom, the forces on each atom was converged to 0.03 eV/Å, the stress on each atom was converged to 0.05 GPa, and the displacement was converged to $0.001$ Å. The Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shannon (BFGS) [16] algorithm was applied to relax the internal coordinates of the atoms automatically to achieve the minimum total energy of the system. The ultrasoft pseudopotential [17,18] was employed to describe the interactions between ionic core and valence electrons.

## 3. Bulk and surface calculations

### 3.1. Bulk properties

The calculated lattice constants for bulk Mg is $a$=3.22836 Å, $c$=5.18561 Å, which is in good agreement with the experimental value ($a$=3.2094 Å, $c$=5.2105 Å) [19]. For the $Al_4C_3$ phase with a space group symmetry $R\overline{3}m$ (1 6 6), the calculated lattice constants, $a$=3.34975 Å, $c$=25.1147 Å and bulk modulus, $B_0$=145.24 GPa, also accord well with the experimental values, $a$=3.329 Å, $c$=24.933 Å and $B_0$=153.1 GPa [20]. These results show that the adopted parameters in our calculations can ensure enough precision.

### 3.2. Convergence test

In order to insure that Mg(0002) slab and $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slab are thick enough to exhibit bulk-like interiors, convergence tests were conducted on the Mg(0002) and $Al_4C_3$(0001) slabs in preparation for their use in interface calculations. The convergence test of Mg(0002) surface was conducted by using the method presented in Refs. [21]. As shown in Table 1, $\delta E$, which is the energy difference between the Mg(0002) slab with the layers of $N$ and $N$-2, converges rapidly with increasing slab thickness to −1947.90 eV for slabs with $N \geq 5$. Thus, symmetric Mg(0002) slab with 5 atomic layers were adopted in the following interface geometries.

$Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) surface is classified as a polar surface because its terminal surface layer consists of only one species of Al atoms or C atoms. Therefore, the convergence test of $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) surface was conducted by using the method presented in Refs. [9–11]. An asymmetric geometry induces a spurious dipole moment within the supercell, which can bias atomic forces and energies [22]. Therefore, symmetric slabs were adopted in the surface convergence tests to eliminate the spurious dipole effects. The change of interlayer spacing in percent of the bulk spacing for $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) free surface after full relaxation are shown in Table 2. It is found that the interlayer relaxations for both termination surfaces exhibit symmetrical forms and the relaxation effects are mainly localized within the top three atomic layers. For Al termination surface, it exhibits an oscillatory expansion/contraction behavior as one moves deeper into the slab and the interlayer relaxations are all well converged when the slab thickness ($N$) is more than 10. For C termination surface, the interlayer relaxations are all well converged when the number of $N$ is more than 11. Thus, symmetric $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slabs with 10 atomic layers for Al termination and 11 atomic layers for C termination were used in the following interface geometries. Unfortunately, there is not any experimental data for $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) interlayer relaxations to compare with this result.

### 3.3. Stabilities of $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) surfaces

Due to symmetric $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slabs were adopted, it makes $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slabs to be non-stoichiometry with identical surfaces. Since the stoichiometric $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slab has two different surfaces, one C-terminated and another Al-terminated, the chemical potentials of Al and C should be considered in the surface energy calculations. The surface energy of $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1), $\sigma Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1), can be given by [9,11,23]:

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}(0001)}=\frac{1}{2 A_{\text {surface }}}\left(E_{\text {slab }}-N_{\mathrm{Al}} \mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {slab }}-N_{\mathrm{C}} \mu_{\mathrm{C}}^{\text {slab }}+P V-T S\right) \quad(1)
$$

where $A_{\text{surface}}$ is the surface area, $E_{\text{slab}}$ is the total energy of a fully relaxed C terminated or Al terminated $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slab. $N_{\text{Al}}$ and $N_{\text{C}}$ are the numbers of the corresponding atoms in the $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) slab. $\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{slab}}$ and $\mu_{\text{C}}^{\text{slab}}$ are the chemical potentials of Al and C, respectively. At the level of 0 K and typical pressures, the $PV$ and $TS$ terms may be neglected.

As the $Al_4C_3$(0 0 0 1) surface is in equilibrium with the bulk $Al_4C_3$, the relationships between the chemical potential of the $Al_4C_3$ bulk ($\mu_{\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3}^{\text{bulk}}$) with the elemental bulk chemical potentials ($\mu_{\text{C}}^{\text{bulk}}$, $\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{bulk}}$) and the chemical potential for each species($\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{slab}}$, $\mu_{\text{C}}^{\text{slab}}$) are defined as follows:

$$
\mu_{\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}}^{\text {bulk }}=4 \mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {bulk }}+3 \mu_{\mathrm{C}}^{\text {bulk }}+\Delta H_{f}^{0}
\tag{2}
$$

$$
\mu_{\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}}^{\text {bulk }}=4 \mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {slab }}+3 \mu_{\mathrm{C}}^{\text {slab }}
\tag{3}
$$

where values for $\mu_{\text{C}}^{\text{bulk}}$, $\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{bulk}}$ were taken from single atomic energy in Al bulk and graphic bulk, respectively. The value of $\Delta H_{f}^{0}$ is the 0 K formation heat of bulk $Al_4C_3$, which was calculated to be −1.17162 eV and is in good agreement with the available value calculated by other method [24].

![](./images/813254344580268034_1.jpg)

Fig. 1. Surface energy of C-terminated and Al-terminated $Al_4C_3(0001)$ slab vs ($\mu_{Al}^{slab}-\mu_{Al}^{bulk}$).

Using the above relationships, Eqs. (2) and (3), Eqs. (1) can be rewritten to be:

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}(0001)}=\frac{1}{2 A_{\text {surface }}}\left[E_{\text {slab }}-\frac{1}{3} N_{\mathrm{C}} \mu_{\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}}^{\text {bulk }}+\left(\frac{4}{3} N_{\mathrm{C}}-N_{\mathrm{Al}}\right) \mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {slab }}\right] \quad(4)
$$

In addition, the elemental Al or C chemical potential must be less than the corresponding bulk chemical potential, as follows: $\mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {slab }} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {bulk }}, \mu_{\mathrm{C}}^{\text {slab }} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{C}}^{\text {bulk }}$. Otherwise, the elements would form the energetically more favorable bulk structure. According to Eqs. (2) and (3), the range of the Al chemical potential can be derived as:

$$
\frac{1}{4} \Delta H_{\mathrm{f}}^{0} \leq \mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {slab }}-\mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {bulk }} \leq 0 \quad(5)
$$

The results of surface energy for both C-terminated and Al-terminated $Al_4C_3(0001)$ slab as a function of Al chemical potential according to Eq. (4) are plotted in Fig. 1.

As shown in the Fig. 1, the surface energy of the C-terminated $Al_4C_3(0001)$ slab is much higher than that of the Al-terminated slab over the entire range of Al chemical potentials. It indicates that the C-terminated surface is thermodynamically less stable than Al-terminated surface. As the chemical potential $\mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {slab }}-\mu_{\mathrm{Al}}^{\text {bulk }}$ increases, the Al-terminated surface gradually becomes more stable. This implies that $Al_4C_3$ particles fabricated in Mg-Al alloy with Al content higher than 2% prefers to maintain Al-terminated structure [25,26]. Meanwhile, the C-terminated surface with large surface energy is generally more reactive. Such a reactive polar surface will impact interfacial adhesion energy, as discussed below.

## 4. Interfaces

### 4.1. Model geometry

Both Al-terminated and C-terminated $Al_4C_3(0001)$ slabs were employed to simulate the $Mg/Al_4C_3$ interface. Based on the convergence test results, the interface of Mg and $Al_4C_3$ consists of five-layers Mg(0002) slab and eleven-layers C-terminated $Al_4C_3(0001)$ slab or ten-layers Al-terminated $Al_4C_3(0001)$ slab. The interface orientation relationship of $(0002)_{\mathrm{Mg}}[11 \overline{2} 0]_{\mathrm{Mg}} / /(0001)_{\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}}[1 \overline{2} 10]_{\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}}$, with a lattice mismatch of about 3.7% [6], was adopted in the interface models. In order to compensate the mismatch and satisfy the periodic boundary conditions, the Mg(0002) slab was stretched by 3.79% with the coherent interface approximation.

![](./images/813254344580268034_2.jpg)

Fig. 2. Two stacking sequences for the Al-terminated $Mg/Al_4C_3$ interface. (a):"OT", (b):"HCP".(white spheres: Al, black spheres: C, brunet small spheres: Mg and quadrilateral frame: supercell profile along $[000 \overline{1}]$ direction).

Two different stacking sequences, i.e., "OT" and "HCP" were considered. In the "OT" stacking, the interfacial Mg atom is directly placed on-top the C or Al atoms of $Al_4C_3(0001)$ slab. In the "HCP" stacking, the interfacial Mg atom is placed on-top the second layer sites of $Al_4C_3$. Therefore, four different interfacial structures, as shown in Figs. 2 and 3, were discussed in this study. The work of adhesion and the interfacial energies were then calculated based on these four systems. In calculation, atoms in the systems were allowed to relax freely in three directions during geometry optimization.

The following parts of the paper will mainly focus on the "OT" model interface.

### 4.3. Electronic structure and bonding

In order to explore the nature of the interfacial bonding between different interfacial atoms, the total charge density (TCD) and difference charge density (DCD) for C-terminated and Al-terminated interfaces with "OT" model stacking were calculated. The difference charge density can be calculated by [27]:

$$
\Delta \rho = \rho_{\text{total}} - \rho_{\text{Mg}(0002)} - \rho_{\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3(0001)} \tag{7}
$$

where $\rho_{\text{total}}$, $\rho_{\text{Mg}(0002)}$ and $\rho_{\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3(0001)}$ are the total charge density of $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interface, the charge densities of the isolated $\text{Mg}(0002)$ and $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3(0001)$ slabs, respectively. Significant differences in the interfacial bonding characters of the C-terminated and Al-terminated interfaces can be found in the calculated results shown in Fig. 4.

The total charge densities are shown in Fig. 4(a) and (c). High charge density with saddle-shape contour can be observed along the bond axis of metal atoms of Mg and nonmetal atoms of C at the C-terminated interface shown in Fig. 4(a), which indicates the evident of polar covalent bond in the interfacial region. In contrast, for the Al-terminated interface, high charge density with concentric contour is observed between metal atoms of Mg and Al in the interface shown in Fig. 4(c), which gives an evident of metallic bond. The difference charge densities are shown in Fig. 4(b) and (d). For C-terminated interface, the charges transfer from Mg and Al atoms to C atoms, and strongly accumulate near to the interfacial C atoms proving the formation of ionic bond. While for Al-terminated interface, there is an obvious charge accumulation between the interfacial Al atoms and Mg atoms, which is the typical feature of metallic bond. The charges transfer from both Al atoms and Mg atoms to the interfacial region and contribute to the formation of metallic bond.

Therefore, the bonding behavior between C atoms and Mg atoms at the C-terminated interface is a combination of polar covalent bond and ionic bond, while for the Al-terminated interface the interfacial interaction is mainly metallic bond. Generally, the polar covalent and ionic bond is stronger than metallic bond. From Fig. 4(a) we can see that the interfacial Mg atom is dragged to close to the interfacial C atom of the C-terminated interface due to strong interaction between C and Mg atoms. This explains the reason why C-terminated "OT" model interface yields larger value for $W_{\text{ad}}$ and $\Delta d_0$, as shown in Table 3, and proves that interaction between C-terminated interfaces is stronger than that of Al-terminated interface.

### 4.4. Interface stability

It is known that the smaller interfacial energy, the more stable interfacial structure is. The stabilities of $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interfaces are further analyzed thermodynamically by calculating the interfacial energy. Meanwhile, the results in this part can provide theoretical guidance for analyzing the potency of $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ particle being the effective heterogeneous nucleation substrate for $\alpha$-Mg. The function for calculating the interfacial energy of $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interface, $\gamma_{\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3}$, can be derived as [28]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3} & \\
& = \frac{1}{2A_{\text{surface}}} \left[ E_{\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3} - \frac{1}{3}N_{\text{C}}\mu_{\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3}^{\text{bulk}} + \left( \frac{4}{3}N_{\text{C}} - N_{\text{Al}} \right) \mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{slab}} - N_{\text{Mg}}\mu_{\text{Mg}}^{\text{bulk}} \right] \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $A_{\text{surface}}$ is the interface area; $E_{\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3}$ is the total energy of a fully relaxed $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interface; $N_{\text{C}}$ and $N_{\text{Al}}$ are the numbers of C and Al atoms in $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ side of the $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interface supercell, respectively; $N_{\text{Mg}}$ is the number of Mg atoms in Mg side of the supercell; $\mu_{\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3}^{\text{bulk}}$ and $\mu_{\text{Mg}}^{\text{bulk}}$ are the chemical potential for $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ bulk and Mg bulk, respectively; $\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{slab}}$ is the chemical potential of Al atoms in $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ side of the supercell. The value of $\mu_{\text{Mg}}^{\text{bulk}}$ can be substituted by the total energy of single Mg atom in the Mg bulk. The value of $\mu_{\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3}^{\text{bulk}}$ can be calculated by Eq. (2).

The relationships of interfacial energies to the function of Al chemical potential $\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{slab}} - \mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{bulk}}$ for four different $\text{Mg}(0002)/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3(0001)$ interfacial structures are plotted in Fig. 5. It can be seen that all the interfacial energies for different interfacial structures are lower than 0 J/m² and do not depend on the Al chemical potential $\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{slab}} - \mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{bulk}}$. In general, negative interfacial energy indicates a tendency to form stable ordered structure or new compounds at the interface. The interactions between $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interfaces with C-termination or Al-termination are so strong that the interfacial Mg atoms are dragged to close to the C or Al atoms at the interfaces where an ordered interfacial structure may be formed subsequently, as can be seen in Fig. 4.

In addition, from Fig. 5 we can see that interface energies of Al-terminated $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interfaces are larger than those of the C-terminated interfaces over the whole range of Al chemical potentials, which indicates that the latter interfaces are more stable. C-terminated "OT" model interface is the most stable structure with lowest interfacial energy. Although the C-terminated "OT" model interface has the largest work of adhesion, its largest surface energy lead to the lowest interfacial energy over the whole range of Al chemical potentials. Therefore, the most favorable $\text{Mg}/\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interfacial structure is the C-terminated "OT" model interface.

### 4.5. Heterogeneous nucleation analysis

All our first principles calculations were performed at 0K, it has been proved that the calculated results can explain the experimental phenomenon for solid phase system and solid-liquid interface at high temperature [12,13,27]. It is thermodynamically believed that the interfacial energy between an effective nucleation substrate with primary phases must be lower than that between primary phases with metal melts. In our calculating results presented in Fig. 5, the interfacial energy values of all the Mg/Al₄C₃ interfaces are negative, hence much lower than that of solid-liquid interface between the $\alpha$-Mg with magnesium melts, about 0.1 J/m² [28]. Moreover, a stable ordered interfacial structure with Mg-termination will be in favor of the stacking of liquid Mg atoms and formation of solid $\alpha$-Mg. Namely, liquid Mg atoms can nucleate on Al₄C₃(0 0 0 1) surface under relative low undercooling condition. This provides a strong theoretical support to the Al₄C₃ nucleus hypothesis [5] that Al₄C₃ particles can be the excellent heterogeneous substrates for primary $\alpha$-Mg grains, and the previous experimental results that primary $\alpha$-Mg grains in Mg-Al based alloys can be effectively grain-refined by different carbon inoculation processes [3-7]. In addition, since the C-terminated "OT" model is the most favorable interfacial structure for Mg/Al₄C₃ interface, the interfacial Mg atoms prefer to directly stack on the top of the outmost C atoms on Al₄C₃(0 0 0 1) surface and form a mixture of polar covalent and ionic bond with C atoms in the solidification of Mg-Al alloy.

### 5. Summary and conclusions

We employed a pseudopotential-based DFT-GGA method to study bulk $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ and its low-index surfaces. Mg(0 0 0 2)/$\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) interface, which has a low lattice mismatch of only 3.79%, was selected. The surface energy of C and Al-terminated $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) slabs, the work of adhesion and interfacial energy of Mg(0 0 0 2)/$\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) interfaces with "OT" and "HCP" stacking, the total charge density and difference charge density of different interfaces were calculated. The object is to investigate the potency of $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ particle as the heterogeneous nucleation substrate for $\alpha$-Mg grain and the sequences of Mg atoms stacking onto the surface of $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) slab. The main conclusions are summarized as follows.

(1) Surface convergence test revealed that the Mg(0 0 0 2) slab with more than five atomic layers exhibits bulk-like interior feature. For the $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) slab, with more than ten atomic layers for Al-terminated model and more than eleven atomic layers for C-terminated model can exhibit bulk-like interior feature.
(2) The surface energy of Al-terminated $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) slabs is much smaller than that of C-terminated model over the entire Al chemical potential, indicating that the outmost atomic layer of C-terminated $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) slabs is more active than that of Al-terminated slabs.
(3) The geometry optimization and the calculations of the work of adhesion, the total charge density and difference charge density reveal that the C-terminated "OT" model structure is the most stable Mg(0 0 0 2)/$\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) interface. The C-terminated "OT" model interface exhibits a mixture of polar covalent and ionic bond, while the Al-terminated "OT" model interface exhibits predominantly metallic character. Therefore, the Mg atoms prefer to directly stack on C atoms with an "OT" stacking sequence when Mg atoms grow on $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$(0 0 0 1) slab.
(4) The calculated interfacial energy values of all the $\text{Mg/Al}_4\text{C}_3$ interfaces are smaller than that between the $\alpha$-Mg and magnesium melts and do not depend on the Al chemical potential $\mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{slab}} - \mu_{\text{Al}}^{\text{bulk}}$. This thermodynamically supports the $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ nucleus hypothesis and proves the facts that $\text{Al}_4\text{C}_3$ particle can be effective heterogeneous nucleation substrate for $\alpha$-Mg in Mg-Al alloy melt, as mentioned in many previous experimental investigations.
