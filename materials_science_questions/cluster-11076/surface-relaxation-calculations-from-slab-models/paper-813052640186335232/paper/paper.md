# Relaxation and electronic surface states of rhodium surfaces

A. Eichler, J. Hafner *, G. Kresse, J. Furthmüller

Institut für Theoretische Physik, Center for Computational Materials Science, Technische Universität Wien, Wiedner Hauptstrasse 8-10, A-1040 Wien, Austria

Received 5 September 1995; accepted for publication 31 October 1995

## Abstract

We present investigations of the structural and electronic properties of the low-index surfaces of rhodium based on selfconsistent ab initio local-density-functional calculations. Our technique is based on ultra-soft pseudopotentials, a preconditioned conjugate-gradient technique for the calculation of the electronic ground-state and of the Hellmann-Feynman forces, and a conjugate-gradient approach for the optimization of the atomic structure. For the (111), (100), and (110) surfaces inward relaxations of the top layer by $-1.7 \pm 0.2$, $-3.8 \pm 0.2$, and $-9.8 \pm 0.6\%$, and an oscillatory decreasing relaxation of the deeper layers are predicted. A detailed analysis of electronic surface states is presented.

**Keywords**: Density functional calculations; Low index single crystal surfaces; Metallic surfaces; Rhodium; Surface electronic phenomena; Surface energy; Surface relaxation and reconstruction

## 1. Introduction

The determination of the atomic and the electronic structure of the surfaces of solids is one of the basic questions of surface science. Transition-metal surfaces are of particular interest because of the importance of transition-metals as catalysts for various hydrogenation and reduction reactions. Rhodium and Rh-based alloys for example have important applications as exhaust gas catalysts for nitric oxide reduction and carbon-monoxide oxidation [1]. Further interest in the Rh-surfaces is caused by the large scatter in the published LEED data [2-8] on the surface relaxations. These data also are in contrast (in particular for the (100) surface) with the best available theoretical results [9,10]. In this paper we present an ab initio local-density-functional investigation of the structural and electronic properties of the low-index Rh surfaces.

## 2. Theory

In our calculations we have used the Vienna ab initio molecular-dynamics program VAMP [11]. VAMP performs an iterative solution of the Kohn-Sham equations of local-density-functional (LDF) theory in a plane-wave basis using preconditioned conjugate-gradient techniques and allows for an analytical calculation of the Hellmann-Feynman forces acting on the atoms and of the stresses on the unit cell. Optimization of the atomic geometry may be performed dynamically, using molecular-dynamics in a canonical ensemble, or statically, using conjugate-gradient minimization. In our calculations, the rhodium surfaces were represented by periodically repeated slabs of Rh atoms, separated by vacuum layers. The thickness of the slabs varied between 5 and 10 layers, we used symmetric slabs as well as asymmetric slabs with the lower surface fixed at the bulk geometry. The thick- ness of the vacuum layer was varied between 4 and 18. With the ultra-soft pseudopoten- tial for Rh, a cut-off energy of $E_{\text{cut}}=190$ eV is used for the expansion of the pseudowavefunctions.

## 3. Surface relaxation

Our results for the surface-energies and surface relaxations are compiled in Table 1. Our results conform with the elementary expectation that the surface energy and the relaxation of the surface layers increase with the roughness of the surface and with a decreasing surface-coordination number, i.e. in the sequence (111)-(100)-(110). Our results agree well with earlier predictions from full-potential lin- ear-muffin-tin-calculations based on thinner slabs and allowing only for the relaxation of the top layer. In our calculations we found that symmetric ten-layer slabs are just sufficient to get well-converged results and that the difference to the results obtained with an asymmetric nine-layer slab gives an estimate of the error. In view of the large scatter of the experimental data coming from different groups and based on different techniques for analyzing the LEED spectra, the agreement with experiment is reasonable. In particular we emphasize that all calculations predict an inward relaxation of the (100) surface, in contrast to the existing data. We tend to support the sugges- tion of Feibelman and Hamann [15] that the experi- mental result indicating expansion of the surface is due to contamination.

## 5. Conclusions

We suggest that our work is relevant in two respects:
(i) It represents the first application of an ab initio density-functional techniques with a plane-wave ba- sis set to a transition-metal surface. This has been made possible by the use of ultra-soft pseudopoten- tials that help to reduce the cut-off energy necessary for a well-converged plane-wave expansion. The plane-wave based ab initio techniques have a great

capacity in surface applications because they allow to treat very large cells with acceptable computa-tional effort. With our technique, calculations for 50 to 100 atoms are possible.

(ii) The Rh surfaces used as an illustration of the technique constitute a nontrivial example, because substantial discrepancies exist between experiment and the predictions of state-of-the-art computational techniques. Our results confirm and extend earlier predictions based on completely different all-electron methods. We also hope that our work will stimulate new experimental investigations, in particular of the electronic surface states.
