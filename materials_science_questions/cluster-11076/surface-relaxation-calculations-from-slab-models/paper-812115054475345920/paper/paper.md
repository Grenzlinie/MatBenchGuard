First-principles study of the (001) surface of cubic $BaZrO_3$ and $BaTiO_3$

Yuan Xu Wang, $^{a)}$ Masao Arai, and Taizo Sasaki
Computational Materials Science Center, National Institute for Materials Science, Tsukuba 305-0044, Japan

Chun Lei Wang
School of Physics and Microelectronics, Shandong University, Jinan 250100, People's Republic of China

(Received 14 July 2005; accepted 11 January 2006; published online 1 March 2006)

First-principles density functional calculations are employed to study the (001) surface of $BaZrO_3$ with both $BaO$ and $ZrO_2$ termination. Surface structure and band structure have been obtained. For the $ZrO_2$-terminated surface, the large displacement of the O atoms in the first layer results in the small surface rumpling. In addition, the largest relaxation appears on the second layer atoms but not on the very first layer ones. The analysis of the structure relaxation parameters reveals that the rumpling of the (001) surface for $BaZrO_3$ with the BaO termination is stronger than that of $BaTiO_3$. The origin of the different surface relaxation behavior between the two materials is discussed. The surface state appears in the band structure of the $ZrO_2$-terminated surface of $BaZrO_3$. © 2006 American Institute of Physics. [DOI: 10.1063/1.2178416]

Barium zirconate, $BaZrO_3$, is a very important ceramic material which has many technological applications. Due to its high melting point, chemical, and mechanical stability, it is widely used as a crucible material to grow single crystal of cuprate superconductors. $^{1}$ Moreover, it has attracted great attention as a high temperature proton conductor with possible applications in fuel cells and hydrogen sensors. $^{2-4}$ Generally speaking, the surface effect of ceramics is important for their applications. Thus, it is important to investigate the surface of $BaZrO_3$ by experimental and theoretical methods. First-principles methods have been successfully applied to the study of the surface of perovskites. $^{5-7}$ However, to our knowledge, there is no theoretical study on the (001) surface properties of cubic $BaZrO_3$ by first-principles calculations. In this letter, we present atomic and electronic structure of the (001) surface of cubic $BaZrO_3$ (BZO) and $BaTiO_3$ (BTO) by the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FLAPW) method.

The calculations presented in this work were performed within the local density approximation $^{8}$ (LDA) and the generalized gradient approximation $^{9}$ (GGA) to density functional theory (DFT), using the FLAPW method with the WIEN2K code. $^{10}$ The atomic sphere radii $(R_{i})$ of 1.9, 1.8, 1.8, and 1.6 a.u. were used for Ba, Zr, Ti, and O, respectively. The calculations were iterated to self-consistency with a specified energy convergence criterion of 0.01 mRy. We used a $13×13×2$ mesh for the Brillouin zone integration that represents 28 $k$ points in its irreducible part. In our calculations, two types of terminations were considered: BaO termination and $MO_2$ ($M$=Zr or Ti) termination. For the BaO-terminated surface, the slab consists of four BaO layers and three $MO_2$ layers and for the $MO_2$-terminated one, four $MO_2$ layers and three BaO layers. In both cases, the slabs with five-lattice-constant thickness are separated by two lattice constants vacuum region. The supercells are tetragonal with space group $P4mmm$. For the BaO-terminated surface, the first, second, and third layers are $BaO$, $MO_2$, and $BaO$, respectively. In case of the $MO_2$-terminated one, the first, second, and third layers are $MO_2$, $BaO$, and $MO_2$, respectively. For both cases, the first layer means the surface layer. During the surface structure optimization, all atoms are fully relaxed with forces less than 2 mRy/a.u.. Enlarging the vacuum region to three times of the theoretical lattice constant thickness and increasing the number of layer to nine only make a difference in the value of $s$ (defined below) of 2% for the BaO-terminated surface of BZO. This result shows that the vacuum region and the number of layers are large enough for these systems.

Before starting the surface calculations, we optimized the bulk structure by the same method and the same computational conditions with the $12×12×12$ $k$-point mesh. The computed lattice constants of cubic BZO with GGA and LDA are 4.23 and 4.15 Å, respectively, which are similar to the experimental one, 4.19 Å. In case of cubic BTO, the theoretical lattice constant $a_0$ with LDA is 3.94 Å which is only 1% smaller than the experimental one, 3.996 Å and that with GGA is 4.03 Å. We used the theoretical lattice constants in all calculations presented here. Most of the previous DFT studies published for the surface of $ABO_3$ perovskite materials have been done using the LDA. Previous calculation on the (001) surface of $SrTiO_3$ shows that the LDA and GGA predict similar structural relaxations. $^{5}$ Generally speaking, the GGA tends to improve upon the LDA in many aspects, especially for atomic and structural energies differences. From our calculated lattice constants for the two materials, it is not clear which approximation is better. Therefore, in our work we investigated the surface of BZO and BTO with both LDA and GGA.

We first discuss the structural relaxations of the BaO- and $MO_2$-terminated surfaces. Table I illustrates our calculated atomic relaxations for the cubic BZO and BTO, respectively. As seen from this table, the Ba atoms move inward (toward the bulk) and the $M$ (Ti or Zr) atoms move outward (toward the vacuum). For the BaO-terminated surface, the largest relaxation occurs for the surface layer atoms, as expected. In case of $ZrO_2$ termination of BZO, the largest relaxation is for the second layer atoms but not for the first layer ones, which is different to that of BTO.

In Table II, we present the calculated structural parameters. For a comparison, the earlier calculations of the cubic BTO by⁶ LDA-PWP and B3PW (Ref. 7) are also presented. Rumpling parameter $s$ measures the outward displacement of the first layer oxygen with respect to the first layer metal atoms, $\Delta d_{12}$ is the change of the first interlayer spacing, as measured from the surface to the subsurface metal $z$ coordinate, and similar definition is given to $\Delta d_{23}$ between the second and third layers. Our calculated values of $s$, $\Delta d_{12}$, and $\Delta d_{23}$ for BTO are similar to those found in previous reports.⁶⁷ For the BaO-terminated surface, the surface rumpling in BZO is larger than that in BTO. In case of $MO_{2}$-termination, our calculated values of $s$ show that the rumpling for BZO is smaller than that for BTO. What surprised us is the large displacement of the O atoms in the first layer, which results in the small surface rumpling of the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface. Such behavior is different to that of BTO. The O atoms in the first layer of the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface for BZO move inward about 1.6% (1.7% for LDA) of the theoretical lattice constant, which is much larger than that in BTO, 0.0% (0.2% for LDA) of the lattice constant $a_{0}$.

For the BaO-terminated surface, the displacement of the Ba atoms for BZO is larger than that of BTO during the surface rumpling. It results in larger rumpling of BZO than BTO. In order to understand such behavior, we calculated the total energy of the (001) surface systems with different displacements of the Ba atoms in the surface layer, and the results are shown in Fig. 1(a). It can be seen that the total energy of the surface structure for BZO decreases more quickly than that of BTO with the same displacement of the Ba atoms, which means that the Ba atoms in the BaO-terminated surface of BZO can be more easily moved. The difference may be due to the fact that BZO has a larger cell volume than BTO. The larger cell volume gives the Ba atoms in the first layer more free space and results in its large displacement during the surface relaxation. In the unrelaxed BaO-terminated surface, the Ba–O bond length (between the Ba atoms in the first layer and the O atoms in the second layer) for BZO is 2.99 Å, and larger than that of BTO, 2.85 Å, which means that the Ba–O bond length for BZO can get larger reduction than that of BTO during the surface relaxation. We also calculated the BaO-terminated surface of BTO with increased cell volume (the lattice constant is same to that of BZO) and found that the displacement of the Ba atoms in the first layer is about 12% of $a_{0}$. Thus, we conclude that the larger cell volume of BZO results in a more significant rumpling in the BaO-terminated surface.

The driving force for the large displacement of the O atoms in the first layer of the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface was investigated with total energy calculations. The total energy for the (001) surfaces with the $MO_{2}$ termination as a function of the displacement of the O atoms along the [001] direction is shown in Fig. 1(b). From this figure, it can be seen that the total energy of the surface structure for BZO decreases and that of BTO increases, which means that the O atoms in the first layer of the $MO_{2}$-terminated surface for BZO can be more easily moved around. Such larger displacement of the O atoms in the first layer of the $MO_{2}$-terminated surface for BZO than BTO can also be understood by the same cell volume effect. During the surface relaxation, the O atoms in the first layer of the $MO_{2}$-terminated surface for BZO have more free space and can have a larger displacement than that for BTO. Therefore, we deduce that the cell volume has a very important effect on the surface relaxation. The larger cell volume of BZO than that of BTO gives the Ba atoms (in the top most BaO layers of the two types of the surfaces) and the O atoms (in the surface layer of the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface) more free space for moving.

The calculated band gap of the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surfaces for BZO with LDA is 2.6 eV, and reduced by comparison with that in the bulk system, 3.2 eV. For the BaO-terminated one, the band gap with LDA is 3.1 eV, and almost same to bulk. Figure 2 shows the calculated band structure of BZO. For the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surfaces structures, the calculated energy bands are uniformly shifted so that 1s core level energy of the metal atom in the central layer is aligned with that in the bulk system. From this point of view, it can be considered that the surface state appears at the $M$ point. The upward shift of the valence bands in the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface is mainly attributed to the states from the O atoms in the first layer. Thus, the surface states appearing in the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface are caused by the O atoms in the first layer.

The origin of the surface states is partly attributed to the increase of the electrostatic potential near the surface. We estimated an increase of about 0.5 eV from the difference between calculated O $1s$ level on the top most layer and that on the central layer. Padilla and Vanderbilt $^{6}$ have suggested other origin for the surface states in the $TiO_{2}$-terminated surface of BTO. They found that the surface state at the $M$ point arises mostly from the O $2p$ orbitals on the first layer and that the contribution from the Ti $3d$ orbitals is negligibly small. From this observation, they speculated that the suppression of the hybridization between O $2p$ and Ti $3d$ orbitals causes the surface states. The same mechanism can also be applied to BZO. In bulk BZO, the full hybridization between the O $2p$ and Zr $4d$ orbitals will move the valence band states downward in energy. However, in case of the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface, the existence of unhybridized O $2p$ orbitals in the surface plane will result in an upward shift in energy of these O $2p$ states. The absence of surface states in the BaO termination can be explained by the fact that the O atom is just above the $M$ atom, and is strongly hybridized to it.

In summary, we found that the surface rumpling of the $MO_{2}$-terminated surface for BZO is much smaller than that for BTO, which is mainly due to the large displacement of the O atoms in the surface layer in BZO. The rumpling of the BaO-terminated surface structure for BZO is stronger than that for BTO. The different surface relaxation behavior of the two materials may be due to the different cell volumes of the two materials. The appearance of the surface states in the case of the $ZrO_{2}$-terminated surface is mainly due to an increase of the electrostatic potential near the surface and the existence of unhybridized O $2p$ orbitals in its surface plane.
