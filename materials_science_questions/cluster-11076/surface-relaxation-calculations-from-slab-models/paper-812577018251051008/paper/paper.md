# Electronic Structure and Magnetic Properties of the (111), (110),
and (001) Surfaces for the Full Heusler Alloy $\text{Zr}_2\text{VGa}$

Jabbar M. Khalaf Al-zyadi $^{1}$ · Hassan I. Asker $^{1}$ · Kai-Lun Yao $^{2,3}$

Received: 20 June 2020 / Accepted: 21 August 2020
© Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2020

![](./images/812577018251051008_1.jpg)

## Abstract
In this paper, the electronic and magnetic properties of the $\text{Zr}_2\text{VGa}$ (111), (001), and (110) surfaces are studied via the first-principles calculations based on the density-functional theory. The calculations indicate that the $\text{Zr}_2\text{VGa}$ full-Heusler alloy displays a half-metallic behavior with a total spin magnetic moment of $2.0\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ at the equilibrium lattice parameter of $6.657\ \text{\AA}$. The energy band gap is 0.28 eV at the Fermi level. A weak relaxation at the terminations of the (111) and (001) surfaces is obtained which means that the terminations are stable. However, the stronger relaxations are at the terminations of the (110) surface. Examining the electronic density of states, the half-metallicity is determined which was verified in the bulk $\text{Zr}_2\text{VGa}$ and it is destroyed at the (111), (001) and (110) surfaces. Furthermore, it is discovered that the atomic spin magnetic moments at the Zr(2)Ga-terminated (001) surface is decreased compared to those of the bulk $\text{Zr}_2\text{VGa}$, while the magnetic moments are increased at the Zr(1) and V-terminated (111) surfaces.

**Keywords** Full-Heusler alloy · Half-metallic ferromagnet · Surface properties · Density of states

## 1 Introduction
Half-metallic (HM) materials displaying a full spin polarization (100%) are still capturing the attention of the researchers due to their fabulous physical properties, a situation that established these materials as suitable building blocks for spintronic devices [1, 2]. The HM materials can be effectively used as spin injectors for many spin-dependent devices including the magnetic memories [3, 4]. Since the discovery of the first HM material in 1983 by de Groot and his collaborators [5] when the half-Heusler NiMnSb and PtMnSb alloys were investigated, researchers have been continuously digging for other alloys which predict the HM properties. Many novel ferromagnetic materials have been introduced to the literature through theoretical first-principles studies. These materials, especially the semi-Heusler structure type, are highly efficient in several magnetic field applications due their significantly small values of spin magnetic moment and low energy losses [6]. The most interesting property of HM materials is the metallic behavior of the one spin channel along with the semiconducting performance of the other channel at the Fermi level. Among the half-metallic materials are the full-Heusler compounds of the chemical structure $\text{X}_2\text{YZ}$ (where X and Y are transition metals, and Z is a nonmetallic element, i.e., sp. element) [7]. The study and investigation of HM for full-Heusler compounds have a wide research potential due to their high Curie temperature and structural similarity with zinc blende semiconductors compared with other HM materials [8–10]. Recently, many of the new Zr-based Heusler alloys are discovered to be HM materials, for example $\text{Zr}_2\text{CoZ}$ (Z: Al, Ga, and In) [11], $\text{Zr}_2\text{NiZ}$ (Z: Al and Ga) [12], $\text{Zr}_2\text{VSn}$ [13], $\text{Zr}_2\text{CoAl}$ [14], $\text{Zr}_2\text{CrZ}$ (Z: Ga, In) [15], and $\text{Zr}_2\text{PdZ}$ (Z: Al, Ga, and In) [16]. Most of the HM materials are used in spintronic devices in the form of thin films and/or multilayers, so it is necessary to study the electronic surface properties of the bulk in consideration of the fact that the HM property maybe destroyed at the surface despite its presence in the bulk [17–19]. ## 2 Computational Method

The magnetic and electronic properties of the $Zr_2$VGa full-Heusler alloy were calculated using the first-principles calculations based on the density-functional theory (DFT) with generalized gradient approximation (GGA) that is applied to the WIEN2k code [28]. The meshes k-point in the bulk $Zr_2$VGa alloy are $(12\times 12\times 12)$, $(12\times 12\times 1)$ for the (111) and (001) surfaces, and $(8\times 12\times 1)$ for the (110) surface. The energy convergence is determined at $(10^{-5}\ \text{Ry})$ (for energy). Smaller muffin-tin sphere radius $R_{\text{MT}}$ values are chosen for the atoms ($\text{Zr}=\text{V}=2.5$ a.u.) and ($\text{Ga}=2.43$ a.u.). The cutoff energy parameter of the plane wave $K_{\text{max}}R_{\text{MT}}$ is equal to 8.0, where $K_{\text{max}}$ represents the largest value of the reciprocal lattice vector. ## 3 Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Electronic Structure and Relaxation

To determine the theoretical lattice parameter, we calculate the total energies of the $\ce{Zr_{2}VGa}$ alloy as a function of the lattice parameters. The lowest energy is situated at 6.657 Å, which we name as the equilibrium lattice constant. It is slightly clearer than the previous theoretical studies [29, 30]. The $\ce{Zr_{2}VGa}$ compound is HM ferromagnetic with an energy gap of 0.28 eV. The crystalline structure of the $\ce{Zr_{2}VGa}$ compound with a $\ce{CuHg_{2}Ti}$-type structure (space group 216, F-43 m), depends on the locations of the atoms: Zr(1) (0, 0, 0), Zr(2) (0.25, 0.25, 0.25), V (0.5, 0.5, 0.5), and Ga (0.75, 0.75, 0.75).

### 3.2 Density of States

To explain the HM characteristics of $Zr_2$VGa bulk, we present in Fig. 2 the calculated atomic density of states (DOS) of the $Zr_2$VGa compound. The down spins are factored by $-1$ and the Fermi levels are located at zero. Figure 2 shows the presence of the energy gap at the Fermi level ($E_F=0$) by 0.28 eV in the majority spin channel, while the minority spin channel demonstrates the metallic characteristic, cutting the Fermi level. This means that the $Zr_2$VGa compound has HM properties.

The energy gap is formed as a result of the hybridization between the transition metal atoms d-bands (V and Zr) plus the contribution of the low energy atoms (Ga atoms).

To understand the surface electronic structure, we calculate the surface atomic DOS of the relaxed terminations of the (111), (001), and (110) surfaces. The spin-polarized atomic DOS at the surface and subsurface layers of the slabs is shown in Figs. 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11. ## 3.3 Magnetic Moments and Spin Polarization

The total magnetic moment (Mₜ) of the bulk Zr₂VGa is 2.00 μ_B, according to the Slater-Pauling rules Mₜ=18−Zᵥ, where Zᵥ is the number of total valence electrons of the Zr, V, and Ga atoms [31, 32].

For Zr₂VGa there is a total of 2×4+5+3=16 valence electrons in the unit cell (Zr: 5d², 6s², V: 3d³, 4s², and Ga: 4s², 4p¹). Consequently, the total number of valence electrons is 16, a matter that implies that the total spin magnetic moment is 2 μ_B. The atomic magnetic moments of Zr(1), Zr(2), V and Ga atoms in the bulk system are −0.25, 0.13, 1.82, and −0.01 μ_B respectively. The highest contribution of the magnetic moment originates from the V atoms that are hybridized with the Zr atoms, while the lowest contribution for the magnetic moment is due to the Ga atoms. Typically, the increase in the magnetic moment of the transition metal atoms (Zr and V) on the surfaces is compared with that of the corresponding atoms in the bulk. In contrast, Ga atoms have a negligible magnetic moment at the surfaces. The study also shows that the magnetic moment of the atoms of the central layers is equal to the magnetic moment of the corresponding bulk atoms. This proves that the number of slabs selected is enough to repeat the properties similar to bulk in the central layers. The spin polarization (P) of the bulk, surface, and subsurface atoms can be calculated as follows:

$$
\mathrm{P} = \left| \frac{\mathrm{N}\uparrow-\mathrm{N}\downarrow}{\mathrm{N}\uparrow+\mathrm{N}\downarrow} \right| \times 100
$$

where N↑ and N↓ are the densities of states at the Fermi level for the spin-up and spin-down, respectively. The spin polarization of the bulk Zr₂VGa alloy is equal to 100% due to the energy gap in the spin-up channel at the Fermi level. The spin polarization is obtained by 60, 87, and 51 of the Ga-terminated (111), Zr(2)Ga-terminated (001), and (110) surfaces, respectively. Therefore, the Zr(2)Ga-terminated (001) surface is more suitable and hence a good candidate for spintronic devices. All aforementioned results are summarized in Table 2.

## 4 Conclusions

For the bulk and (111), (001), and (110) surfaces of Zr₂VGa Heusler alloy, the electronic, structural, and magnetic characteristics have been studied using the first-principles investigations. The density of states shows that the Zr₂VGa Heusler alloy is a half-metallic ferromagnet with a spin magnetic moment of 2 μ_B per formula unit. It is found out that the (111), (001), and (110) surfaces of Zr₂VGa ruin the bulk HM properties. The total magnetic moment (Mₜ) is 2.00 μ_B according to the Slater-Pauling rules Mₜ=18−Zᵥ. Compared with the atomic spin magnetic moments of the corresponding atoms in the bulk Zr₂VGa compound, the atomic spin magnetic moment of atoms in the outer and internal layers changes due to the surface states. Moreover, the spin magnetic moments are decreased at the Zr(2)- and Ga-terminated (111) surfaces compared with the bulk values.
