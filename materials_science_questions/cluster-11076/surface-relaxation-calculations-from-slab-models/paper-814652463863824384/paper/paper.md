## ABSTRACT

We present the results of our DFT-based investigations on the effects of the substitution of magnesium with cobalt and nickel in the magnesium hydride on the bulk properties and on the hydrogen adsorption and desorption on the $MgH_{2}(001)$ surface. We present the energetic aspects of these interactions. Our calculations point to the merit of the cobalt and nickel dopants in the magnesium hydride destabilization, which facilitates the hydrogen release.

## Introduction

The design of new materials with a high hydrogen contain and the development of new and efficient methods for the con- trolling and the optimization of the hydrogen release by the manipulation of thermodynamic and kinetic conditions of the materials are essential in establishing the optimum and safe technological solutions for the production, storage, trans- portation and usage of hydrogen [1]. The traditional couple theory - experiment enables us to understand the interaction mechanisms and the phenomena that take place in bulk, on the surface, at the interface of different materials or at the grain boundaries, as well as to establish the analytical equa- tions and to characterize material properties. The atomistic and quantum simulations are essential in the thermodynamic and kinetic analysis of the bulk, surface and interface phe- nomena of the energy materials [2]. Computer simulations have been successfully applied to the study of the hydrogen adsorption [3] and dissociation [4] on the Mg(0001) surface, to the penetration of hydrogen into magnesium and the forma- tion of the magnesium hydrate [5], to the electronic and structural properties of magnesium hydride [6] and their modification by doping and alloying [7-10]. ## Computational methods

### System models

At room temperature the magnesium hydride is an ionic compound and has the rutile structure, called β-MgH₂. At high pressure it forms other two structures: α-MgH₂ and γ-MgH₂ [16]. In the present study we only consider the structure β- MgH₂, whose symmetry is described by the group P42/mnm (symmetry group no. 136), with the cell parameters a = 4.501 A and c = 3.010 A [16,17]. The primitive cell contains two mag- nesium atoms that occupy the 2a sites (0,0,0) and (1/2,1/2,1/2) and four hydrogen atoms at sites 4f (±x,±x,0) and (1/2 ± x,1/ 2 ± x,1/2), with x = 0.304 (see Fig. 1a).

The establishment of the most stable doped structure is beyond our aim, as we are only interested to analyse the ef- fects of the substitution of magnesium with cobalt or nickel atoms at low concentration about 10%. This concentration corresponds to the replacement of one magnesium atom of eight.

In the present study we use for the bulk calculations an almost cubic supercell $(\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2})R45^{\circ} \times 2$ that contains 8 magnesium atoms and 16 hydrogen atoms. We use the nota- tion M–Mg7H16 to indicate the investigated systems for the clean (M = Mg) and the doped magnesium hydride (M = Co and Ni). Notations of various sites, based on the relative position to the atom M, are indicated in the Fig. 1b. We considered only the neutral hydrogen vacancies, although the vacancy may remain electrically charged [18].

In order to model the adsorption and desorption of the hydrogen atoms on the surface (001) we use the slab method, by creating a slab starting from the bulk optimized super- cell$(\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2})R45^{\circ} \times 2$, adding another half of cell along the c- axis. Thus, the slab has seven layers and consists in one atom M = Mg, Co or Ni, 13 magnesium and 28 hydrogen atoms. The number of layers in the slab is large enough to ensure the convergence of the surface energy [15]. The odd and even layers are equivalent, thus it is not important how we cut the surface. The atoms in the two top layers L0 and L1 (see Fig. 1c) are completely relaxed and the atoms in the third layer L2 are relaxed only along the c-axis, perpendicular to the surface. The other atoms are fixed in their bulk optimized position. A void domain of 20 Å is included to separate the image slabs along the c-axis. The size (lattice constants $a = b = 6.4085$ Å and $c = 29.0843$ Å) and the shape (angles $\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 90^{\circ}$) are fixed during the optimization procedures of the surface system.

### Computing scheme

All the calculations were done with the Density Functional Theory code VASP [19], using the generalized gradient approximation with the PW91 exchange-correlation func- tional [20]. In order to reduce the calculation efforts, the Ultra- Soft Pseudo-Potentials [21] were used to emulate the effects of the core-electrons. The spin effects are considered for all the investigated systems by self-consistent spin-polarized calcu- lations. The properties of the MgH₂ determined for the values of 380 and 500 eV for the energy cut-off are almost identical, but with an almost double computational effort for $E_{cut} = 500$ eV. ## Reaction energy

The usual approximation of the reaction Gibbs free energy with the difference of the products and reactants internal energies is not accurate in case of the considering reactants or products in the gas phase. Therefore, we considered the contribution of the vibration to the free energy. As we are interested here on the phenomena that take place at low temperatures we take into account only the ZPE (zero point energy) to the Gibbs free energy and thus the variation of the Gibbs free energy for temperatures near 0 K is reduced to the variation of the Helmholtz free energy. The contribution of ZPE to the system enthalpy was determined by the phonon calculations with the codes PHON [22] and VASP, using the supercell finite displacement method [23].

Because of the size of the surface slab is large (14 heavy and 28 hydrogen atoms) the phonon calculations are very expensive. Therefore, we prefer to characterize the surface system stability by the differences of the internal energies, rather than the differences of the Gibbs or Helmholtz free energies. To calculate the surface energy we need the energy of the bulk systems corresponding to the surface slab. These calculations are very expensive because they involve the relaxation of all 42 atoms and of the elementary cell. In the case of the magnesium hydride we may approximate the bulk energy of Mg-Mg₁₃Hᵧ, y = 16 or 15 with the sum of the energy corresponding to the elementary cell Mg-Mg₇Hᵧ and ¾ of the energy of the bulk elementary cell Mg-Mg₇H₁₆. In the case of the doped systems the lattice parameters a = c of the bulk system M-Mg₇Hᵧ are too different from those of M-Mg₇Hᵧ to apply the same approximation.

## Results and discussions

### Bulk properties

#### Structural properties

The complete optimized lattices and some interatomic distances for the systems M-Mg₇Hₓ, M = Mg, Co and Ni and x = 15, 16 are presented in the Table 1. The replacing of a magnesium atom by a cobalt or nickel atom determines the contraction of the lattice constants. The unit cell volumes of Co-Mg₇H₁₆ and Ni-Mg₇H₁₆ are reduced by 6.35 and 4.93%, respectively, relative to the volume of the Mg-Mg₇H₁₆. By removing a hydrogen atom, the lattice parameters a and b of the clean magnesium hydride remain equivalent (M = Mg), but in the case of doped systems (M = Co or Ni) the axis b becomes longer.

From the analysis of the interatomic distances it can be observed that the doping determines the contraction of the bonds formed by the atom M with its neighbour hydrogen atoms and the shortening of the M-Mg distance. In addition, the distance between the two non-equivalent hydrogen atoms that are bonded to the atom M are shorter. The contraction effect is more important in the case of the doping with cobalt.

For Mg-Mg₇H₁₆ the magnesium atoms become charged +1.21 |e⁻| by transferring from the 3s orbital 0.60 |e⁻| to the orbital 1s of each nearest neighbour hydrogen atom and 0.32 |e⁻| to its own 3p states. The experimental determinations indicate a charge of +1.91 electrons on magnesium of -0.26 electrons on hydrogen [24]. Thus the ionic nature of these systems is confirmed. The creation of the hydrogen vacancy determines a rearrangement of the charges around the defect. More affected are the magnesium atoms M and Mg₁₁ that were the nearest neighbours of the removed Hydrogen atom, which become less charged: 0.93 and 0.98 |e⁻|, respectively.

The atom that substitutes the magnesium atom loses about one electron less than the substituted atom (0.20 and 0.12 |e⁻|, for M = Co and Ni, respectively). The charges of the atoms in the first and second neighbour shells are perturbed, but the perturbation is rapidly diminished. By removing one of the closest Hydrogen atoms H₀₁ the dopant atoms become almost neutral. The charges of the directly connected Magnesium atoms decrease for both dopants Co and Ni. In the case of the Ni-dopant the charge perturbation is extended to the second magnesium atom along the direction 001.

The analysis of the spin population shows that the clean magnesium hydride and the hydrogen vacancy systems Mg-Mg₇H₁₅ and Co-Mg₇H₁₅ are nonmagnetic. The other systems are ferromagnetic. To be sure that the system Co-Mg₇H₁₅ is indeed nonmagnetic we repeated the calculations by considering different initial spin configurations for the Cobalt atom and using higher convergence criteria and more fine k-grids in the reciprocal space, but the conclusion remains unchanged.

## Surface properties

The relaxation of the surface $MgH_2(001)$
The calculations predict the rumpling and relaxations of the first two layers of the magnesium hydride surface (001) (see Table 5). By surface creation, the magnesium atoms from the first layer L0 move inwards, whereas the hydrogen atoms move in the same direction, but much less. Thus, the layer manifests a relaxation and a rumpling. Similar trends manifest the doped surfaces, but more strongly. In the case of the doping with nickel a local rearrangement can be observed: one hydrogen atom $H_{11}$ from the layer L1 is lifted outside the surface, forming an almost planar cross with its four neighbour hydrogen atoms. The surface relaxation and rumpling can be considered reduced in comparison with the cases of other ionic systems where the relaxation can reach even 70% [26]. We remark the very small values for the rumpling and for the contraction of the interior layer L2 for all the systems. We interpret this fact as an indication of the good quality of the slab model.

The same tendencies of the substituent atoms of cobalt and nickel are observed when a hydrogen vacancy is created near them. The cobalt atom contracts further its neighbourhood by reducing its distance to the closest magnesium atom in the second layer. In the case of the nickel substituent a local rearrangement takes place and the nickel atom forms again the same planar cross with four hydrogen atoms.

The surface energy of the magnesium hydride is estimated to be 1.76 eV, lower than the value of 2.10 eV corresponding to the surface containing a hydrogen vacancy in the first layer. ### Hydrogen adsorption
The adsorption of the hydrogen was considered by placing a single hydrogen atom on top of the central atom M = Mg, Co or Ni in the first layer of the relaxed surface M–Mg₁₃H₂₈ (001). The atomic hydrogen may come from the bulk or from a dissociated hydrogen molecule after the adsorption on the magnesium hydride surface. There are many other adsorption sites on the surface that can be favourable to the hydrogen adsorption, but we limit our investigation to the effects of the doping with Co and Ni of the magnesium hydride surface. The adsorption energies are calculated as

$$
E_{\text{ads}}(M)=E\left(H+M-M g_{13} H_{28}\right)-\left(E\left(M-M g_{13} H_{28}\right)+\frac{1}{2} E\left(H_{2}\right)\right)
\tag{4}
$$

where $E(M-Mg_{13}H_{28})$ is the energy of the elementary cell of the slab M–Mg₁₃H₂₈(001), $E(H_{2})$ is the energy of the isolated hydrogen molecule (the factor of ½ takes into account that only one atom is adsorbed) and $E(H+M-Mg_{13}H_{28})$ is the energy of the elementary cell of the slab that contains the hydrogen atom adsorbed on top of the atom M on the surface M–Mg₁₃H₂₈(001). The calculated adsorption energies are presented in Table 6.

The hydrogen atom adsorbed on top of M = Mg prefers to migrate freely and to form a bond with a hydrogen bonded to the target magnesium atom (see Fig. 2). The bond H–H is about 0.842 Å, longer than in a free hydrogen molecule (0.751 Å). The two hydrogen atoms are not completely free, as they form bonds with the neighbour magnesium atoms at 1.961 and 2.202 Å. The hydrogen atoms are strongly bonded on the surface.

The hydrogen adsorbs on top of the cobalt atom at 1.600 Å distance. The neighbourhood of the cobalt atom is not perturbed. After the adsorption on the nickel atom, one of the hydrogen atoms $H_{01}$ takes the position of the atom $H_{11}$ that was lifted up and the adsorbed hydrogen occupies the position of the atom H01. Finally, the planar cross is remade and the displaced hydrogen atom $H_{01}$ is perpendicular on this plane. As we can see, the adsorption on cobalt is not energetically favourable. When a hydrogen atom adsorbs on top of the substituent atom of nickel a new arrangement takes place: the planar cross fragment is maintained with the fifth hydrogen atom perpendicular on their plane.

Our spin-polarized calculations for the different investigated surfaces (clean, Ni- and Co-doped, with vacancy of or adsorbed hydrogen) indicate that only the clean surface is nonmagnetic, the other systems are ferromagnetic.

### Hydrogen residence time
In the investigate further the effects of the doping of MgH₂ with Co and Ni atoms on the residence time of the hydrogen atom on the surface MgH₂(001). The model corresponds to a fresh magnesium hydride surface and it does not consider the real case of the formation of a hydrogen solid solution with the magnesium formed during the magnesium hydride dissociation [27]. The inverse of the desorption rate constant is the effective residence time [28], which can be written as

$$
\tau=\tau_{0} \exp \left(E_{\text {des }}^{\mathrm{a}} / R T\right)
\tag{5}
$$

where $\tau_{0}=\frac{C}{v}$ corresponds to the period of vibration of the bond between the adsorbed molecule and substrate and is frequently taken to be about 10⁻¹³ s. The parameter ν is the apparent vibration frequency and C is a factor that includes the effects of: (i) the surface coverage, (ii) the position of the molecule parallel to the surface plane, (iii) the changes in the bond angles and bond lengths of the molecule and (iv) the angular orientation of the molecule [29]. The computed values of the desorption energies $E_{\text{des}}^{\text{a}}$ are presented in Table 7. The vibration frequency ν is determined by phonon calculations where all the other atoms are frozen in their equilibrium position, excepting for the hydrogen atom that desorbs. The constant C is unknown, but because the calculations for the three similar structures are made in the same conditions, we consider in a first approximation that C has the same value for the three systems and because we are interested only in the effects of the doping of the magnesium hydride surface on the hydrogen desorption, we calculate only the values of the residence time relative to the case of the clean surface (see Table 7).

The substitution of a magnesium atom with cobalt or nickel reduces the hydrogen residence time on the surface (001) of the magnesium hydride. The nickel is more efficient than the cobalt in the releasing of the hydrogen atoms from the surface.

The bulk calculations indicate that the substitution of magnesium with cobalt or nickel and the formation of the hydrogen vacancy are accompanied by the decrease of the cohesive energy of the magnesium hydride, facilitating the hydrogen release. The doping of the surface (001) of the magnesium hydride can change the structure and the properties of the surface, speeding up the hydrogen desorption process. The present calculations predict that the doping with nickel atoms is more efficient in the release of the hydrogen atoms from the bulk and from the surface (001) of the magnesium hydride. Further investigations are necessary to validate our findings, by using larger surface models and by considering various other faces of the magnesium hydride, other doping sites and other doping elements.
