# The electronic, magnetic and optical analysis of the $AgNiF_3$ (001) surface: a first-principles approach

A A Mubarak¹* and S Tariq²

¹Physics Department, Rabigh College of Science and Arts, King Abdulaziz University, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia
²Department of Physics, Faculty of Sciences, University of Central Punjab, Lahore, Pakistan

Received: 16 March 2020 / Accepted: 29 September 2020

**Abstract:** In this study, the density functional theory has been used to perform the structural, electronic, magnetic and optical properties of the variant stable $AgNiF_3$ (001) surfaces. The surface morphology is investigated under two truncations, namely $AgNiF_3$/AgF (AgF) and $AgNiF_3$/NiF (NiF). The enthalpy of formation, relaxation parameter and surface energy results show that the stable AgF and NiF surfaces can be fabricated. We have also performed the electronic properties which include band gap, DOS, electronic specific heat, charge contour plot and Fermi surface. The indirect ($M$$\Gamma$) band gap value of the AgF surface has been found larger than NiF surface. The partial DOS and charge contour plots have shown the mixed type of bonding nature among the elements. The Fermi surface analysis of AgF surface has predicted a complex bonding between Ag-d and F-p hybridization, while in the case of NiF surface and bulk $AgNiF_3$, Fermi surfaces are formed by contributions of Ag-d, Ni-d and F-p states. The total magnetic moment value of the ferromagnetic NiF surface has been found higher than that of AgF surface. Moreover, the specific heat value of NiF surface was found higher than the bulk $AgNiF_3$ and AgF surface. The optical analysis has portrayed beneficial applications of studied surfaces in coating lenses as anti-reflectors and optical fiber.

**Keywords:** Perovskite; Semiconductor; Magnetic susceptibility; Specific heat; Optical properties

## 1. Introduction

In recent years, fluoroperovskite compounds ($ABF_3$) have drawn attention to the number of theoretical and experimental studies due to their industrial applications [1, 2]. These applications implicate photoluminescence, ferromagnetism, magneto resistivity, magnetic tunnel junctions, lenses and medical equipment [3, 4]. The diversity of applications depends on the crystal orientation. The crystal phase is very sensitive to ambient conditions. This behavior makes them flexible candidates to fabricate future devices [5, 6]. The transition metal ($T_M$)-based fluoroperovskite compounds exhibit an ideal cubic structure with the general formula $AT_MF_3$ and space group of Pm-3 m [5], where A could be an organic/inorganic part as an atom or group of atoms [6]. These compounds have interesting magnetic properties for the variant structures.

In the literature, scientists explored the number of fluoroperovskite compounds that have effective applications in the production of solar cells [7]. For this reason, fluoroperovskites-based photovoltaic cells are in high demand [8]. Among these compounds, $AgNiF_3$ is of a great interest for potential uses in these aforesaid applications. The number of technological applications has been reported for the $AgNiF_3$ compound in previous studies [6, 7, 9, 10]. Gluck et al. [11] have investigated the structural, magnetic and optical absorption spectrum of the bulk $AgNiF_3$. In their study, they have found a high absorption coefficient and anti-ferromagnetic behavior at a lower energy level around $197^\circ$K. In another recent study, the bulk $AgNiF_3$ was found to exhibit semiconducting ferromagnetic attributes [12].

The investigations in the surface perovskite field are rare in the previous literature despite the importance of this field. Zhong et al. performed the theoretical calculations on the variant termination of the $BaTiO_3$ surface [13]. They studied the surface along with different crystallographic directions by separating the bulk crystal into two parts by

## 2. Method of calculations

In our study, The optimized surface structure calculations were performed under the framework of the density functional theory (DFT) [15]. Within the DFT approach, the utilization of the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method [16] along with a spin-polarized configuration is being used in the WIEN2k code [17]. The exchange-correlation potentials in this study are being utilized under generalized gradient approximation (GGA-PBE) [18]. The valence and core electronic states were treated as semi-relativistic and full relativistic operators, respectively. The plane wave distribution in the interstitial part by the cutoff energy was set at $K_{\text{max}} = (8/\text{R}_{\text{MT}})$ a.u, while the spherical harmonic waves with $\ell_{\text{max}} = 14$ were adjusted in the muffin-tin spheres. The full convergence of surface calculations was found by limiting the energy and charge at $0.1 \times 10^{-3}$ Ry/atom and $0.1 \times 10^{-3}$ e, respectively. The structural, electronic and magnetic properties were computed by the tetrahedron method and by generating $12 \times 12 \times 1$ grid of denser $k$-mesh points in the Irreducible Brillouin zone (IBZ). This is adjusted to acquire the self-consistent calculations. The cubic structure was used to develop the AgF and NiF surfaces [12]. The supercell technique is normally deployed to create $(1 \times 1)$ surface structure that consists of a seven-layer slab (7L) of $AgNiF_3$. One must adjust the vacuum in the layers to separate the surface. The vacuum in this study was created with 50% thickness of the studied surfaces. This vacuum thickness ensures that the interaction between the surface atoms does not occur in consecutive slabs. The variant terminations of the $AgNiF_3$ surface were created along (001) plane, as presented in Fig. 1 which are denoted by $AgNiF_3$/AgF (AgF) and $AgNiF_3$/NiF (NiF). This bonding nature is analogous to the bulk phase of the $AgNiF_3$.

Moreover, optical parameters were calculated from the dielectric function $(\varepsilon\ (\omega) = \varepsilon_1\ (\omega) + i\varepsilon_2\ (\omega))$ [19], where $\varepsilon_1$ $(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_2$ $(\omega)$ are the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function, respectively. A denser grid of $k$-mesh points, i.e., $16 \times 16 \times 2$ points in IBZ, is used to compute the optical parameters.

The semi-classical Boltzmann theory is utilized to calculate the magnetic susceptibility $(\chi)$ and the electronic specific heat $(C_V)$ of the variant surfaces by BoltzTraP code [20]. This theory requires a very dense $40 \times 40 \times 2$ grid of $k$-mesh points in IBZ. In the optical and ther-moelectric calculations, the full SCF convergence is achieved using dense $k$-mesh points rather than the ones used in the structural calculations. These dense points ensure that SCF will not stop before reaching the convergence.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Optimized surface structure

To develop an optimum 7L surface structure of AgF and NiF terminating surfaces, the atoms are allowed to relax along the (001) direction to reach the equilibrium positions by considering the central layer as a reference point. The PORT minimization method is used to specify these atomic positions with the convergence tolerance for forces of less than 1 mRy/a.u. [21]. The layers above and below the central layer are symmetrically distributed. The relaxed interlayer spacing ($\Delta_{ij}$) and the amplitude of surface rumpling ($s$) for AgF and NiF surfaces are listed in Table 1. The relaxed atomic positions of the content surfaces show that the surface ($S$) layer and the third ($S-2$) layer move down, whereas the subsurface ($S-1$) layer goes up in AgF termination. In comparison to bulk ${\text{AgNiF}}_{3}$ atomic spacing, these movements are yielded to the contraction in the first and the second interlayer spacing by 11.7% and 2.6%, respectively, combined with an expansion of the third interlayer spacing by 2.2%. In the case of NiF termination, the layers shift upward with the different percent that yields to a contraction in the first interlayer spacing by 6.2% combined expansion in the second and the third interlayer spacing by 2.7% and 2.3%, respectively, related the bulk spacing. These values represent the relative distance of the F atom corresponding to the metal atom in the same layer. The rumpling in the layers that consist of Ag and F atoms is found more than that assumed in the layers consisting of Ni and F atoms. The rumpling in S layer is found to decrease as traversing to the interior layers. The negative rumpling value, in Table 1, that competed in the $S-1$ layer means that the metal moves up compared to F atom.

The average surface energy ($\gamma_{\text{avg}}$) of the variant termination along (001) is the average energy required to split the bulk into two pieces separated by a vacuum. This energy is computed by the formula [13]:

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{1}{4 A}\left(E_{\mathrm{slab}}^{\mathrm{AgF}}+E_{\mathrm{slab}}^{\mathrm{NiF}}-7 E_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\mathrm{AgNiF}_{3}}\right) \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{slab}}^{\mathrm{AgF}}$, $E_{\mathrm{slab}}^{\mathrm{NiF}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\mathrm{AgNiF}_{3}}$ are the total energy of the AgF, NiF terminations and ${\text{AgNiF}}_{3}$ bulk compound, respectively. The area of a surface is denoted by $A$. The calculated $\gamma_{\text{avg}}$ is found to be 28.2 meV/Å² which predicts the ability of creation and deposition in epitaxial growth of both terminations.

The calculated surface grand potential ($\varphi$) is obtained by [13]:

$$
\phi=\frac{1}{2 A}\left[E_{\mathrm{slab}}-N_{\mathrm{Ni}} E_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\mathrm{AgNiF}_{3}}-\frac{E_{\mathrm{gas}}^{\mathrm{F}}}{2}\left(N_{\mathrm{F}}-3 N_{\mathrm{Ni}}\right)-E_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\mathrm{Ag}}\left(N_{\mathrm{Ag}}-N_{\mathrm{Ni}}\right)\right] \tag{2}
$$

where $E_{\text{slab}}$ and $N_{\text{X}}$ are the total energy of the termination and the number of X atoms in the surface, respectively. The calculated value of $\varphi$ is found about -5.54 J/m² for AgF surface termination and $-5.50$ J/m² for NiF surface termination. These values suggest that AgF surface is more stable than NiF surface. Moreover, we have checked the stability of the slab structures by the enthalpy of the formation value ($H_{\text{F}}$) using the relation [13]

$$
H_{\mathrm{F}}=E_{\mathrm{slab}}^{\mathrm{AgF}(\mathrm{orNiF})}-\left(a E_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\mathrm{Ag}}+b E_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\mathrm{Ni}}+c E_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\mathrm{F}}\right) \tag{3}
$$

where $a$, $b$ and $c$ represent the number of atoms in a superstructure. The negative enthalpy of the formation value is an evidence of the thermodynamic stability of a surface. The obtained values of the enthalpy energies for AgF and NiF are $-14.9$ eV and $-14.6$ eV, respectively. These values indicate that both surfaces are thermodynamically stable and can be formed.

The discrepancy in the total magnetism between two terminations is due to the content atoms of the surface layer. The magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) and its inverse ($\chi^{-1}$) value are plotted in Fig. 6a, b, respectively. The calculated $\chi$ value for the bulk and surfaces satisfies the Curie-Wiess law. The calculated Curie temperature is found around 140 K which explains the magnetic phase transformation from ferromagnetic to paramagnetic for the presented bulk and surfaces. At a fixed temperature, the calculated magnetic susceptibility value decreases in moving from NiF to AgF then the bulk AgNiF₃. This effect is due to the high magnetic nature of NiF surface. At room temperature, the present systems are predicted to have paramagnetic behavior with $\chi$ value of about $1.32\times 10^{-9}$, $2.24\times 10^{-9}$ and $2.73\times 10^{-9}$ emu/mol for AgNiF₃, AgF and NiF, respectively. The inverse magnetic susceptibility ($\chi^{-1}$) as a function of temperature, in Fig. 6b, allows us to predict the magnetic character and the Wiess temperature ($T_{\theta}$). The calculated $\chi^{-1}$ line is found to intercept the positive $T$-axis that indicates that the present systems have a ferromagnetic character with $T_{\theta}$ value of 5, 4 and 2 K for AgNiF₃, AgF and NiF, respectively. The semiconducting behavior and the high magnetic moment value of AgF and NiF surfaces predict beneficial applications in magnetic tunneling and spintronic devices.

Figure 7 presents the calculated electronic specific heat ($C_{\text{V}}$) as a function of temperature for the AgNiF₃, AgF and NiF systems. These curves represent the influence of electrons on the heat capacity and predict DOS at the Fermi level. In the temperature range 0–80 K, the electronic specific heat is equal to approximately zero and then $C_{\text{V}}$ being increased linearly with temperature. At a fixed temperature, the calculated $C_{\text{V}}$ value of the NiF termination surface is found higher than that computed for AgNiF₃ and AgF. At room temperature, the calculated $C_{\text{V}}$ value is found about 1.33, 3.69 and 4.84 J/K.mol for AgNiF₃, AgF and NiF, respectively. This indicates that the valence electrons of the NiF surface needed low energy to contribute to the heat capacity than the bulk AgNiF₃ and AgF surface. This prediction agrees with the narrower band gap of the NiF surface than that computed above for the other present systems.

### 3.3. Optical properties

The calculated optical parameters of the studied surfaces along zz-direction, such as the refractive index ($n\ (\omega)$), reflectivity ($R\ (\omega)$), energy loss function ($L\ (\omega)$), absorption coefficient ($I\ (\omega)$) and optical conductivity ($\sigma\ (\omega)$), are calculated in Fig. 8. The $n\ (\omega)$ and $R\ (\omega)$ spectra have the same character that agrees with the real part of dielectric spectra. The zero-frequency limit of the refractive index is called the static reflective index ($n\ (0)$). This value is found about 4.92 and 7.34 for AgF and NiF surfaces, respectively, which is inversely proportional to the band gap value. These values are found higher than that of the bulk AgNiF₃ [12], i.e. ($n\ (0)=3.60$). The higher value of the static reflective index suggests beneficial optical applications, such as thin lens fabrication to achieve high reflectivity in the core of the optical fiber. Beyond this point, the spectra decrease sharply and reach a minimum value of $n\ (\omega)$ about 0.48 around 1.67 eV for AgF and about 0.35 around 1.86 eV NiF. The calculated $n\ (\omega)$ value is found lower than unity in the energy range of 1.10 – 2.31 eV. In this region, AgF and NiF surfaces could have a superluminal effect for high energy photons.

Figure 8b shows the reflectivity spectrum of the studied surfaces. The computed zero-frequency limit of reflectivity ($R\ (0)$) value is found about 44% and 58% for AgF and NiF, respectively. Beyond this point, $R\ (\omega)$ spectra increase and reach the extrema maximum values about 69% around 0.60 eV for AgF and about 69% around 0.56 eV for NiF and then decrease sharply to reach 10%. In the low energy level bounded between zero-frequency limit and 2.20 eV, both spectra have a reflectivity of more than 10% with a higher calculated value for NiF than that calculated for AgF. In this region, surfaces have shown the potential ability in preventing solar heating so could be used in the thermoelectric application. In the energy region of 2.20 eV

The calculated optical spectra show that the studied surfaces have high optical electron conductivity and high permeability for light. These results portray that AgF and NiF surfaces could be used in the fabrication of optical lenses.

### 4. Conclusions
In conclusion, we have found that AgF and NiF surfaces exhibit interesting properties. Based on our calculated enthalpy of formation and surface grand potential values, it is suggested that both surfaces are thermodynamically stable and could be formed. The indirect (M-$\Gamma$) electronic band gap of AgF is found wider than that of NiF surface, while both surfaces have a narrower band gap than that of the bulk $\text{AgNiF}_3$ phase. Partial DOS and charge contour plots affirm the presence of mixed covalent and ionic

In AgF surface, the main contribution bands that form the Fermi surface are Ag-d and F-p, while in the case of bulk $AgNiF_3$ and NiF surface, the Fermi surface is a result of Ag-d, Ni-d and F-p states. Both surfaces are found to have a ferromagnetic character with higher magnetism value for NiF surface than that of AgF surface. This attribute is not observed in the case of bulk structure which is an anti-ferromagnetic behavior. The calculated electronic specific heat values show that the valence electrons of NiF surface contribute to the heat capacity with lower energy than the bulk $AgNiF_3$ and AgF surface. Optical analysis of surfaces demonstrated their potential ability for lens coating to enhance anti-reflecting phenomena. Moreover, high reflectivity value suggests another useful application in optical fibers. Results also suggest the potential ability of both surfaces in the thermoelectric application such as heat-resistant material. This is the first report on $AgNiF_3$ surfaces which could be used to contrive further theoretical or experimental investigations in the future for the development of beneficial applications.
