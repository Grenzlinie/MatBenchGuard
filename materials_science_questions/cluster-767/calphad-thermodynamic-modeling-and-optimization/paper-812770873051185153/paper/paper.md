## Abstract:

Based on a critical evaluation of experimental phase diagram data in the literature, the U-Nb-Zr ternary system has been assessed using the CALPHAD technique for the first time. The individual solution phases, i.e. liquid, bcc, ort, tet, and UZr$_2$, have been modeled. The modeling covers the whole composition range and a wide temperature range from 450 to 2300 $^\circ$C. A set of self-consistent thermodynamic parameters have been developed. Comprehensive comparisons between the present calculations and measured phase diagrams show that the reliable experimental data are well accounted for by the present thermodynamic description. With regard to the bcc-$\gamma$ phase, the miscibility gaps and related phase relations are satisfactorily reproduced by the present calculation. Based on this, the solvus projection and reaction scheme of the U–Nb–Zr system have been generated, thereby providing guidance for possible industrial applications.

## Keywords:
U-Nb-Zr ternary system, Phase diagram, Thermodynamic calculation, miscibility gaps, solvus projection

### 1.Introduction

U-based alloys are considered as good candidates for nuclear fuels due to their high fissile density and thermal conductivity. Recently, the U-Nb-Zr alloys were frequently studied with respect to their microstructures, thermal behaviors, phase transformations, mechanical properties etc [1-9]Error! Bookmark not defined.. By alloying Nb and/or Zr, the stability of high-temperature bcc-$\gamma$ phase in U-based alloys is substantially increased [10-12]. The elevated solidus temperature reduces the risk of the cladding failure caused by the penetration of liquid phase. Also, an enlarged temperature and composition range for the single bcc-$\gamma$ phase is beneficial for the irradiation in view of decreased microstructural variation and anisotropic swelling [13]. During operation, the increasing temperature drives the microstructure to an equilibrium state, raising an impetus to carefully study the U-Nb-Zr ternary phase diagram. In addition to the technological importance, this ternary phase diagram attracts the present authors' scientific interest about the evolution behavior of three bcc-$\gamma$ miscibility gaps originating from the three sub-binary systems.

Although extensive phase diagram data of this system are available in the literature, a thermodynamic modeling using the CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagram) method is of great interest for the R&D of U-based materials in the following aspects: i) the calculation based on Gibbs energy expressions is consecutive without composition and temperature intervals, which could be used to specify the phase equilibria in the region where experimental information is uncertain or unavailable; ii) the modeling of a single ternary system can be easily integrated with others, whereby the calculation could be extended to higher-order systems for multi-component alloy design [14, 15]; iii) thermodynamic factors outputted from the modeling are required for further simulations, e.g. kinetic modeling[16, 17] or phase-field simulations[18, 19], to get an insight into the microstructure evolutions. Up to now, there is no thermodynamic modeling for the U-Nb-Zr system. Consequently, the aims of the present work are to, i) critically evaluate experimental phase diagram information of this ternary system available in the literature. ii) obtain a set of self-consistent thermodynamic parameters for individual phases in this system

using the CALPHAD method, and iii) present reliable phase diagram information, i.e. isothermal/vertical sections, liquidus/solidus/solvus projections, and reaction scheme, over an extensive temperature and composition range for possible industrial use.

## 2. Evaluation of literature information

To facilitate reading, the symbols denoting the phases in the U-Nb-Zr system are listed in Table 1.

### 2.1 Sub-binary information

The U-Zr system assessed by Xiong et al. [20] and the Zr-Nb system by Guillermet [21] are considered to be reliable and accepted in the present work. The U-Nb system was recently re-visited by Duong el al. [22]. However, a preliminary assessment shows that their binary parameters cannot yield satisfactory calculated phase equilibria in the U-Nb-Zr system. Hence the U-Nb modeling presented by Duong et al. [22] was revised by slightly adjusting parameters of stable phases. The calculated U-Nb, U-Zr, and Zr-Nb binary phase diagrams are shown in Figure 1.

### 2.2 Ternary information

Among all experimental information of the U-Nb-Zr ternary system available in the literature, the results reported by Badayeva and Kuznetsova [23], Dwight and Mueller [10], and Ivanov and Gomozov [11, 12] contain comprehensive experimental details and cover wide temperature and composition ranges, contributing mostly to the constitution of this ternary system.

By means of thermal analysis (TA), Badayeva and Kuznetsova [23] determined the solidus and liquidus temperatures of U-Nb-Zr alloys in the composition range of 0 to 40 at.% Nb and 0 to 90 at.% Zr. In their report, the $\gamma$ phase is a continuous solid solution throughout this ternary system at elevated temperatures, and both the Nb and Zr raise the solidus and liquidus temperatures of U-based alloys. This experimental result is considered to be reliable and accepted in the present modeling.

Using metallography, X-ray diffraction (XRD), and resistivity measurements, Dwight and Mueller [10] investigated the U-Nb-Zr system within the temperature range from 700 to 500 □ and the composition range from pure U to 25 wt.% Nb and

50 wt.% Zr. They presented detailed information about the U-rich phase equilibria, e.g., isothermal sections, vertical sections, and a three-dimensional isometric view of the ternary system. According to Dwight and Mueller [10], the $\gamma$ phase is a continuous solid solution from the U-Nb to U-Zr boundaries at high temperatures. As the temperature decreases, $\alpha$U and $\beta$U become stable and the single phase range of $\gamma$ gradually shrink towards the center of the ternary system. The miscibility gap of either $\gamma_1$+$\gamma_3$ or $\gamma_1$+$\gamma_2$, extending from the U-Nb or U-Zr sub-binary system, tends to close with an addition of the third element (Zr or Nb). The related monotectoid reaction of $\gamma_1$=$\alpha$U+$\gamma_3$ or $\gamma_1$=$\beta$U+$\gamma_2$, which starts from the U-Nb or U-Zr boundary, moves towards the ternary region with a decreasing temperature and ends as the miscibility gap finally merges into the single $\gamma$ phase. The phase boundary of the $\gamma$ in the $\gamma$+$\alpha$/ $\beta$U two-phase shows a convex shape pointing towards the U-end. Besides, Dwight and Mueller [10] found that the solubility of Nb in the binary compound $\delta$-UZr$_2$ is limited (< 4 wt.%), and the three-phase equilibria of $\alpha$U+$\delta$+$\gamma$ and $\delta$+$\gamma$+$\alpha$Zr were found in the ternary region. No ternary compound was observed by Dwight and Mueller [10].

By means of metallugry, XRD, and microhardness measurements, Ivanov and Kuznetsova [11] investigated the U-Nb-Zr ternary system and constructed three vertical sections at w(Nb):w(Zr)= 3:1, 1:1, and 1:3, respectively. In their continuing work [12], they determined the isothermal sections at temperatures from 450 to 800 $\square$ and vertical sections at w(U)= 65, 50, and 30 wt.% using the same experimental methods. These studies [11, 12] cover the entire composition range of the U-Nb-Zr system. In the U-rich area, the phase diagram data reported by Ivanov and Kuznetsova [11, 12] were generally in good agreement with Dwight and Mueller [10] except for the stability range of $\beta$U. Ivanov and Kuznetsova [11, 12] reported that the $\beta$U near the U-Nb binary side is stable at the temperature down to 640$\square$, yielding three-phase equilibria of $\beta$U+$\gamma_1$+$\gamma_3$ and $\alpha$U+$\beta$U+$\gamma_3$ and four-phase equilibrium of $\beta$U+$\gamma_1$=$\alpha$U+$\gamma_3$. This conflicts with the result of Dwight and Mueller [10] where $\beta$U completely decomposes at ~660 $\square$, showing no possibility to be in equilibrium with the $\gamma_3$. As to this discrepancy, the result of Dwight and Mueller [10] is accepted in the present

modeling due to its better agreement with the recently updated U-Nb sub-binary phase diagram [22], where no βU exists at temperatures below 664 □.

For the phase diagram away from the U corner, Ivanov and Kuznetsova [11, 12] reported that the $\gamma$ phase is a continuous solid solution. With a decrease of the temperature, the $\alpha$Zr and $\delta$ become stable and the homogeneity range of $\gamma$ gradually shrinks towards the center and then the Nb corner of the ternary system. Besides, the miscibility gap extending from the Zr-Nb ($\gamma_4$+$\gamma_5$) sub-binary system gradually closes with an increasing U concentration. The monotectoid reaction of $\gamma_4$=$\alpha$Zr+$\gamma_5$, which initially occurs in the Zr-Nb system at ~ 610 □, is extended to the ternary region with a decreasing temperature and terminated by the merging of $\gamma_4$+$\gamma_5$ miscibility gap. In spite of these similar characteristics to the U-rich region, Ivanov and Kuznetsova [11, 12] reported that equilibration in the U-poor alloys is sluggish at temperatures below ~600 □. The higher the Nb+Zr concentration is, the lower is the probability that an alloy attains the equilibrium. This could be attributed to the slower atomic diffusion in Nb- or Zr-rich bcc alloys than that in the U-rich ones [17]. Therefore, the low-temperature Zr-Nb-rich phase diagram information [11, 12] is given a low weight in the present modeling.

Recently, phase transformation temperatures of U-rich U-Nb-Zr alloys were determined by a research group using differential thermal analysis (DTA) [8, 9] or differential scanning calorimeter (DSC) [7]. However, these alloys are homogenized at temperatures (823-900 □) corresponding to the single $\gamma$ phase region. The solid-state transformations in this ternary system take place at lower temperatures. Without preceding low-temperature annealing, it is difficult for the present authors to estimate whether the DTA or DSC signals arrested represent an equilibrium transformation. Consequently, the experimental data [7-9] are only used for comparison in the present work.

### 3. Thermodynamic models

In the present modeling, the Gibbs energy functions for pure elements U, Nb, and Zr are taken from the SGTE compilation by Dinsdale [24]. The modeling of

liquid, $\gamma$, $\beta$U, $\alpha$U, $\alpha$Zr, and $\delta$ is conducted in the present work.

### 3.1 Solution phases

In the U-Nb-Zr ternary systems, the liquid, $\gamma$, $\beta$U, $\alpha$U, and $\alpha$Zr are disorder solution phases, and each of them is described by a substitutional solution model of (U,Nb,Zr)$_1$. Taking the $\gamma$ as an example, its Gibbs energy function is described by Redlich-Kister-Muggianu polynomial [25, 26]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{ }^{0} G_{m}^{\gamma}= & x_{\mathrm{U}}{ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{U}}^{\gamma}+x_{\mathrm{Nb}}{ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\gamma}+x_{\mathrm{Zr}}{ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}+R T\left(x_{\mathrm{U}} \ln x_{\mathrm{U}}+x_{\mathrm{Nb}} \ln x_{\mathrm{Nb}}+x_{\mathrm{Zr}} \ln x_{\mathrm{Zr}}\right) \\
& +x_{\mathrm{U}} x_{\mathrm{Nb}} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}}^{\gamma}+x_{\mathrm{U}} x_{\mathrm{Zr}} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}+x_{\mathrm{Nb}} x_{\mathrm{Zr}} L_{\mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}+{ }^{\mathrm{ex}} G_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

where $R$ is the gas constant, and $x_{\mathrm{U}}$, $x_{\mathrm{Nb}}$ and $x_{\mathrm{Zr}}$ are molar fractions of the elements U, Nb and Zr, respectively. The standard element reference (SER) state [24], i.e. the stable structure of the element at $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and 1 bar, is used as the reference state of Gibbs energy. The parameters $L_{i,j}^{\gamma}(i,j=\mathrm{U,Nb,Zr})$ are the interaction parameters from binary systems. The ternary excess Gibbs energy ${ }^{\mathrm{ex}} G_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}$ is expressed as follows:

$$
{ }^{\mathrm{ex}} G_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}=x_{\mathrm{U}} x_{\mathrm{Nb}} x_{\mathrm{Zr}}\left(x_{\mathrm{U}}{ }^{0} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}+x_{\mathrm{Nb}}{ }^{1} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}+x_{\mathrm{Zr}}{ }^{2} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}\right) \tag{2}
$$

where $^{0}L_{\mathrm{U,Nb,Zr}}^{\gamma}$, $^{1}L_{\mathrm{U,Nb,Zr}}^{\gamma}$, and $^{2}L_{\mathrm{U,Nb,Zr}}^{\gamma}$ are interaction parameters to be evaluated in the present work.

### 3.2 The $\delta$ phase

The ordered $\delta$ phase originating from the U-Zr system shows a limited solubility of Nb [10, 12] in the ternary system. In view of closer physical and chemical natures of Nb and Zr than those of Nb and U indicated by the periodic table, it is reasonable to assume that Nb is more likely to substitute the Zr in $\delta$. Besides, it is sufficient to cover the stability range of $\delta$ in the ternary region by adding Nb to the second sublattice with a minimum number of parameters. Consequently, the $\delta$ is described by a two-sublattice model proposed by Hillert and Staffansson [27] as (U,Zr)$_{2/3}$(Nb,Zr)$_{1/3}$, and its Gibbs energy for one mole of formula is given by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{\mathrm{m}}^{\delta}= & y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime}{ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{U}: \mathrm{Zr}}^{\delta}+y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime}{ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{U}: \mathrm{Zr}}^{\delta}+y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime}{ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{Zr}: \mathrm{Nb}}^{\delta}+y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime}{ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{Zr}: \mathrm{Zr}}^{\delta} \\
+ & 2 / 3 R T\left(y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime} \ln y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}+y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime} \ln y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime}\right)+1 / 3 R T\left(y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime} \ln y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime}+y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime} \ln y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime}\right) \\
+ & y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime} y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime} L_{\mathrm{U}: \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\delta}+y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime} y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime} L_{\mathrm{Zr}: \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\delta}+y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Zr}: \mathrm{Nb}}^{\delta}+y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime} y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Zr}: \mathrm{Zr}}^{\delta}
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
$$

where $y_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}$ and $y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime}$ are the site fractions of $\mathrm{U}$ and $\mathrm{Zr}$ in the first sublattice, and $y_{\mathrm{Nb}}^{\prime \prime}$ and $y_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{\prime \prime}$ are the site fractions of $\mathrm{Nb}$ and $\mathrm{Zr}$ in the second sublattice. The parameter ${ }^{0} G_{\mathrm{A}: \mathrm{B}}^{\delta}$ (A denotes $\mathrm{U}$ or $\mathrm{Zr}$ while $\mathrm{B}$ denotes $\mathrm{Nb}$ or $\mathrm{Zr}$) in Eq. (3) represents the Gibbs energy of an end-member, i.e. hypothetical compound $\mathrm{A}_{2 / 3} \mathrm{~B}_{1 / 3}$.

### 4. Assessment procedure

The assessment of the model parameters is conducted using the computer operated optimization program PARROT [28], which works by minimizing the sum of square of the differences between measured and calculated values. Thermodynamic parameters for liquid, $\gamma, \beta \mathrm{U}, \alpha \mathrm{U}$, and $\delta$ are adjusted by using experimental data selected in Section 2. Each piece of selected information is given a certain weight based on the uncertainties of the experimental data. During the whole assessment procedure, the weights are adjusted by trial and error until a general fit between the experimental and calculated phase equilibria is obtained. Since the assessment involves an extensive composition and temperature range of the U-Nb-Zr system, a step-by-step strategy for the modeling is explained as follows.

Firstly, the thermodynamic parameters of the sub-binary systems, i.e. U-Nb [22], U-Zr [20], and Zr-Nb [21], are collected to perform preliminary calculations, and the optimization is then conducted on solid phases using the experimental isothermal section at $700 \square$ reported by Ivanov and Gomozov [12]. For the $\gamma$ phase, three ternary interaction parameters, i.e. ${ }^{0} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}$, ${ }^{1} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}$, and ${ }^{2} L_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr}}^{\gamma}$, are employed to reproduce its ternary stability range and three miscibility gaps extending from the sub-binary systems. We find that the calculated composition range of $\gamma_{4}+\gamma_{5}$ miscibility gap near the Zr-Nb system is sharply widened from 23.3-89.1 at.% Nb to 11.8-89.7 at.% $\mathrm{Nb}$ as the $\mathrm{U}$ content increases from 0 to 1.5 at.%. Due to this unusual widening, a monotectoid reaction of $\gamma_{4}=\alpha \mathrm{Zr}+\gamma_{5}$ is calculated to initially occur in the ternary

system at ~697 □, which is substantially higher than (~87 □) than that suggested by Ivanov and Gomozov [12] and considered to be not reasonable. The widening of $\gamma_4$+$\gamma_5$ miscibility gap is hardly influenced by ternary parameters, i.e. $^0L_{U,Nb,Zr}^\gamma$, $^1L_{U,Nb,Zr}^\gamma$, and $^2L_{U,Nb,Zr}^\gamma$, yet very sensitive to binary parameters, i.e. $^0L_{U,Nb}^\gamma$, $^1L_{U,Nb}^\gamma$, $^2L_{U,Nb}^\gamma$, and $^3L_{U,Nb}^\gamma$.

To solve this problem, the second step is to revise the modeling of the U-Nb system given by Duong et al. [22] by slightly adjusting the parameters for $\gamma$, $\beta$U, $\alpha$U, and liquid. Figure 2 present the calculated U-Nb phase diagram within the composition range of 0 to 18 at.% Nb and from 640 to 680 °C using modelings from both the present work and Duong et al. [22]. As can be seen, for the invariant equilibrium of either $\beta$U=$\alpha$U+$\gamma$ or $\gamma_1$=$\alpha$U+$\gamma_3$, the discrepancy between the two modelings with respect to the calculated reaction temperature is within 2 □. Thirdly, the optimization returns to the ternary system using the other experimental isothermal sections [10, 12]. For the $\gamma$ phase, temperature-dependent parameters are introduced to fit the phase boundary data at temperatures from 800 to 450 □. For the $\delta$ phase, two parameters $^0G_{U:Nb}^\delta$ and $^0G_{Zr:Nb}^\delta$ are employed to reproduce its ternary stability and phase relations from 550 to 450 □. Fourthly, the optimization is extended to high temperatures using experimental vertical section data [10-12] and liquid/solid transformation data given by Badayeva and Kuznetsova [23]. For the liquid, three ternary interaction parameters, i.e. $^0L_{U,Nb,Zr}^{liquid}$, $^1L_{U,Nb,Zr}^{liquid}$, and $^2L_{U,Nb,Zr}^{liquid}$, are required to reproduce the experimental liquidus and solidus data over the entire composition range. Finally, the parameters obtained at each step are optimized simultaneously, including all the reliable experimental information mentioned previously.

During the whole optimization procedure, we find that the parameters for $\alpha$U, $\beta$U, and $\alpha$Zr, which are obtained from the modeling of sub-systems, are sufficient to reproduce the phase relations of U-Nb-Zr ternary alloys and therefore no additional ternary parameters for the three phases are introduced.

## 5. Results and discussion

Thermodynamic parameters obtained in the present work are summarized in table 2, where the parameters assessed in the present modeling are underlined. Using this set of parameters, phase diagrams of the U-Nb-Zr ternary system, i.e. isothermal sections, vertical sections, and liquidus/solidus projection are calculated to verify the rationality of the present modeling.

Figure 3 presents the calculated liquidus and solidus surface projections in comparison with the experimental data reported by Badayeva and Kuznetsova [23]. As can be seen, the calculated isothermal lines of phase boundaries for liquid and $\gamma$ can well account for the experimental transformation temperatures. Both the solidus and liquidus temperatures near the U corner are substantially lower than those close to the Nb-Zr side.

Figure 4 shows the calculated isothermal sections at 800, 750, 700, and 660 $\square$ in comparison with the experimental ones given by Ivanov and Gomozov [12]. The calculated phase boundaries of $\gamma$ are in good agreement with the experimental ones. Besides, the three miscibility gaps of $\gamma_1$+$\gamma_2$, $\gamma_1$+$\gamma_3$, and $\gamma_4$+$\gamma_5$, showing a trend of closure due to the addition of the third element, are well accounted for by the present modeling. Figure 5 gives the calculated isothermal sections at 620, 550, 500, and 450 $\square$ in comparison with the experimental ones [12]. The calculation shows the stability range of $\gamma$ gradually decreases with a decreasing temperature, which agrees well with the experimental result. The phase relations are well reproduced by the present modeling, and the minor discrepancies in the region away from the U-end are attributed to experimental uncertainties caused by the sluggish equilibration in Zr-Nb-rich alloys.

Figure 6 presents calculated U-rich isothermal sections at 700, 680, 660, and 640 $\square$ in comparison with the experimental data reported by Dwight and Mueller [10]. As can be seen, the calculation can well account for most experimental phase equilibria of U-Nb-Zr alloys. The $\gamma_1$+$\gamma_2$ miscibility gap and monotectoid reaction of $\gamma_1$=$\beta$U+$\gamma_2$, which originate from the U-Zr system, disappear at 660 $\square$, leaving only the $\beta$U+$\gamma$ two-phase there. A precise calculation indicates that this is due to the merging of $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ at ~677 $\square$. Figure 7 shows four U-rich isothermal sections

calculated with very small temperature intervals, i.e. 630, 626, 621, and 600 ℃, in comparison with the experimental data [10]. As is shown, the calculated results are in good agreement with the experimental ones within estimated experimental uncertainties. A further calculation shows that the monotectoid reaction of $\gamma_1=\alpha U+\gamma_3$ starting from the U-Nb binary system is terminated in the ternary region by the merging of $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_3$ at ~623 ℃, leaving only the $\alpha$U+γ two-phase region there.

Figure 8 compares the calculated vertical sections at 65, 50, and 30 at. % U with the experimental data determined by Ivanov and Gomozov [12]. Figure 9 shows the calculated vertical sections with x(Zr):x(Nb)=3:1, 1:1, and 1:3, together with the experimental ones [11]. Although slight discrepancies lie between the calculation and experimental points for the Nb-Zr-rich alloys annealed at low temperatures (<600 ℃), the fitting is satisfactory in consideration of the retarded or suppressed equilibration. In these vertical sections, the γ-stabilizing effect of a ternary addition in U-based alloys is pronounced.

Figure 10 shows the calculated vertical sections near the U corner at 1, 2, 7, and 25 wt. % Nb in comparison with the experimental data reported by Dwight and Mueller [10], and Figure 11 shows the counterparts at 2, 4, 6, and 20 wt.% Zr. As can be seen, most solvus data concerning phase transformations of $\gamma$ to $\gamma+\alpha$ or $\gamma+\beta$U can be well reproduced by the present modeling. The miscibility gap, either $\gamma_1+\gamma_2$ near the U-Zr side or $\gamma_1+\gamma_3$ near the U-Nb side, gradually merges to a single $\gamma$ phase with a decrease of temperature or an increase of the ternary addition. Figure 12 shows the calculated vertical section at w(Zr):w(Nb)=10:3 compared with the experimental data [10]. As can be seen, the phase transformation of $\gamma$ to $\gamma+\beta$U is well accounted for by the present modeling.

Figure 13 shows the calculated vertical sections near the U corner at Nb+Zr = 6 and 7 wt. % in comparison with the experimental data reported in the literature [7-9]. As can be seen, even though the DTA or DSC results are not employed in the present modeling, a satisfactory fitting between the experimental data and the calculation is obtained. The discrepancies that lie at temperatures < 640℃ could be attributed to the lack of low-temperature annealing that results in non-equilibrium transformations.

Figure 14 shows the calculated solvus projection over the entire U-Nb-Zr composition range, on which all alloys are cooled from the single $\gamma$ state and undergo various solid state transformations, and the transformation sequence in this ternary system is specified using a reaction scheme as given in Figure 15.

## 6. Summary

For the first time, a thermodynamic assessment is conducted on the U-Nb-Zr system based on critically evaluation of phase equilibrium information available in the literature. The stabilities of $\gamma$, $\alpha$U, $\beta$U, $\delta$, $\alpha$Zr, and liquid are described with proper thermodynamic models and a set of self-consistent thermodynamic parameters are obtained. Phase diagrams over an extensive composition and temperature range, including isothermal sections, vertical sections, liquidus projection, solidus projection, are calculated. Comprehensive comparisons show that the reliable experimental data are well accounted for by the present modeling. The evolution of miscibility gaps for the bcc-$\gamma$ phase is properly described. A solvus projection is calculated and a reaction scheme at solid state is presented.

Future investigations may focus on the low-temperature equilibria of the U-Nb-Zr system. For U-rich alloys, the cooling from single $\gamma$ state does not guarantee an equilibrium transformation at low temperatures. Hence low-temperature equilibration prior to the DSC or DTA measurements is necessary to obtain reliable solid-state phase transformation temperatures. For Nb-Zr-rich alloys, the slow atomic diffusion retards the equilibration during the phase diagram determination. Hence long-time homogenization at low temperatures is required to obtain more accurate stability range for the $\gamma$ phase. Besides experimental studies, it is of great interest to apply ab initio calculations in the U-Nb-Zr ternary alloys, thereby obtaining more thermodynamic properties to improve the present modeling. An integration of the present modeling with our recent study[29] and other modelings for actinide alloys is strongly recommended to develop a multi-component thermodynamic database, thereby providing a guidance for nuclear materials design.
