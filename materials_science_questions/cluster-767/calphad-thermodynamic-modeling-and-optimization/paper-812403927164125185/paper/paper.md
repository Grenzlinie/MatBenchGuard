# Thermodynamic analysis of the Ga-Ti system

Jing-Bo Li $^{a,b}$, Jean-Claude Tedenac $^{a,*}$, Marie-Christine Record $^{a}$

$^{a}$Laboratoire de Physico-Chimie de la Matière Condensée, UMR CNRS 5617, cc03, Université Montpellier II, Place E. Bataillon, 34095 Montpellier Cedex 5, France
$^{b}$Group A03, Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Science, P.O. Box 603, Beijing 100080, China

Received 22 October 2002; received in revised form 10 January 2003; accepted 10 January 2003

## Abstract
A thermodynamic assessment of the binary Ga-Ti system was performed based on the evaluation of the literature and our experimental results. Diffusion couple and alloy samples with appropriate compositions were prepared and analyzed by electron probe microanalysis and X-ray diffraction to determine the stability of intermetallic compounds. The Ga-rich part of phase diagram was checked by differential thermal analyses. The subreguler solution model was used to describe the solution phases liquid (L), b.c.c. ($\beta$), h.c.p. ($\alpha_{1}$) and f.c.c. ($\gamma_{1}$). The compound energy model was employed to describe compounds with homogeneity ranges of $Ti_{3}Ga$ ($\alpha_{2}$), $Ti_{5}Ga_{4}$($\rho$) and $TiGa(\gamma_{2})$. The compounds $Ti_{2}Ga$, $Ti_{3}Ga_{3}$, $Ti_{2}Ga_{3}$, $TiGa_{2}$ and $TiGa_{3}$ were treated as stoichiometric phases. A set of thermodynamic parameters was obtained and the calculated phase diagram was presented.

## 1. Introduction
The Al-Ga-Ti-N system is interesting due to the possible use of nitrides as semiconductor materials. Accurate thermodynamic data are required to understand the growth processes. A thermodynamic database of this system is being set up. More attention has been paid to Ga-Ti, not only as a constituent of the N-containing system, but also as a possible solder of oxides. Unfortunately, the thermodynamic work on this system is not ample. This work aims at building up a precise phase diagram by experiments and thermodynamic assessment.

## 2. Literature
Murray [1] made a critical evaluation of the available publications on the Ga-Ti system. In her work, an assessed partial diagram (0-35 at.% Ga) was constructed from the thermodynamic calculation by Kaufman and Nesor [2], except for the homogeneity range of $GaTi_{3}$ which was drawn. The regular solution model was used for the solution phases, and both $GaTi_{3}$ and $GaTi_{2}$ were treated as linear compounds in Ref. [2]. So far, it is the most acceptable calculation work on this system.

### 2.1. Equilibria
The composition range of 0 to 35 at.% Ga was examined by Anderko [3,4] up to 1373 K. The two invariant reactions were determined as the peritectoid reactions $Ti(b.c.c.)+GaTi_{3}\to Ti(h.c.p.)$ at $1213\pm10$ K and $Ti(b.c.c.)+GaTi_{2}\to GaTi_{3}$ at $1303\pm20$ K. A congruent melting point of $GaTi_{2}$ was identified from the microstructures of cast alloys, so a eutectic reaction $L\to Ti(b.c.c.)+GaTi_{2}$ was expected. The temperatures of the congruent melting and the eutectic reaction were not measured in their work. An equilibrium point of the h.c.p./(h.c.p.$+GaTi_{3}$) boundary at 1173 K and another boundary of $GaTi_{3}$/(h.c.p.$+GaTi_{3}$) at 1123 K were determined by Anderko [4].

Shamblen and Rosa [5] determined the tielines between h.c.p. and $GaTi_{3}$ using microprobe analysis of annealed diffusion couples. The boundaries [5] were located on the Ti-rich side of Anderko's results [4]. However, the calculation by Kaufman and Nesor [2] fits somewhat better with

Two solidus points of the b.c.c./(b.c.c.+L) boundaries of 16 and 26 at.% Ga at 1770 and 1700 K, respectively (unpublished work [6]) were quoted in Ref. [2].

Belousov [7] exhibited a phase diagram in the range of 0~33.3 at.% Ga. They gave two invariant reactions at 1193 K, and 1313 K and the boundaries of h.c.p./(h.c.p.+b.c.c.) and h.c.p./GaTi₃. However, the measurement methods were not reported. The phase diagram [7] was not used in the optimization.

Two experimental Ti–Ga equilibrium diagrams over nearly the whole composition range were measured by Potzschke and Schubert [8], and Antonava and Tretyachenko [9]. Potzschke and Schubert [8] constructed the phase diagram over 20~90 at.% Ga based on thermal analysis, X-ray diffraction (XRD) and examination of cast alloys. Eight compounds were reported, GaTi₃, GaTi₂, Ga₃Ti₅, Ga₄Ti₅, GaTi, Ga₃Ti₂, Ga₂Ti and Ga₃Ti. However their phase diagram was omitted in Murray's assessment [1] because of the conflicts between their published microstructures and the phase diagram, and the existence of an oxide phase in the range 35 to 67 at.% Ga.

Antonava and Tretyachenko [9] measured the liquidus and invariant reactions over the range of 20~74 at.% Ga by differential thermal analysis (DTA). Phase analyses of the as-cast and annealed alloys were carried out by optical metallography and XRD. The chemical compositions of the phases and the structural constituents of the as-cast and annealed alloys were determined by electron probe micranalysis (EPMA). They confirmed the existence of eight intermetallic compounds, and showed the existence of homogeneity ranges in compounds GaTi₃, Ga₄Ti₅ and GaTi. Four eutectic, two peritectic and three peritectoid reactions were established. The compounds of GaTi₂, Ga₃Ti₅, Ga₄Ti₅, Ga₂Ti melt congruently. The boundaries of the b.c.c. phase and the h.c.p. phase [9] are located to the richer Ga side than those of Refs. [3–5].

There are great differences between the two phase diagrams in spite of the similarity of the microstructures [8,9]. The phase diagram of Ref. [9] shows a more reasonable phase relationship than that of Ref. [8].

Yatsenko and Anikin [11] measured the solubility of Ti in molten Ga by keeping the solid Ti in Ga melts for a long time to saturate the Ga melts. They optimized a temperature function of solubility of Ti in molten Ga, $x$ (atomic-fraction of Ti)$=10^{(0.5-3100)/T}$. Natanzon et al. [12] measured the solubility of Ti in molten Ga at 973–1173 K. The compositions of the annealed alloys were determined by EDX (energy-dispersive X-ray). The saturation compositions of Ti were taken as the liquidus in this work. A boundary point of the nonstoichiometric compound GaTi at the Ti-rich side was measured by Rossouw et al. [10] at 52 at.% Ga, 1473 K.

### 2.2. Thermodynamic properties

Experimental thermodynamic properties in this system are lacking. The enthalpy of formation of GaTi₃ was measured by Meschel and Kleppa [13] through dropping mixtures of pure Ga and Ti with appropriate composition ratio into high temperature calorimeter at 1373±2 K. It is the only experimental thermodynamic property for all of the eight compounds.

Nikolaenko et al. [14] measured the partial enthalpies of Ti(b.c.c.) with 0~13 at.% Ti in Ga melts at 1343, 1423 and 1469 K by high-temperature calorimetry, and optimized a set of values of the mixing enthalpies and the partial enthalpies of Ti at 1400 K. Partial molar heat and entropy of solution of Ti in molten Ga were deduced from the solubility of Ti without any criterion of enthalpy or entropy by Ref. [11]. The values are strange compared to those of Ref. [14], and not used here.

Natanzon et al. [12] estimated a value ~–40.2 kJ/mol of atoms (all subsequent results are quoted in the same units) of formation enthalpy of Ga₃Ti according to the solubility of Ti in molten Ga measured in their work. The thermal effect of the GaTi₃ formation was approximately calculated as $-43.9\pm2.1$ kJ/mol based on the area of the differential peak in the DTA curve by Lobova et al. [15].

Thermodynamic activities in the b.c.c. phase were determined at 1623–1773 K by Gegel and Hoch [16] using the method of coupling the Knudsen effusion technique with a time-of-flight mass spectrometer. The pairwise interaction energy obtained with reference to b.c.c. (Ti) is $-51.9\pm2.9$ kJ/mol. In Ref. [2], an interaction parameter of $-54.4$ kJ/mol for b.c.c. was quoted, which was deduced by Bidwell [6] from the measurement of vapor pressures in the Ti-rich part.

### 2.3. Intermetallic compounds

Eight compounds of Ga₃Ti, Ga₂Ti, Ga₃Ti₂, GaTi, Ga₄Ti₅, Ga₃Ti₅, GaTi₂ and GaTi₃ were reported stable in Refs. [8,9]. However, Han and Fetzer [17] studied the thin film diffusion couple Ti/GaAs, and found only four binary Ga–Ti compounds of GaTi₃, Ga₂Ti₃, Ga₃Ti₂ and Ga₃Ti. Apparently, it is necessary to make clear which compounds are stable in the Ga–Ti system, although there exists a lively competition of various reaction products at the ternary interface.

The existence of Ga₃Ti, Ga₂Ti, Ga₃Ti₂, GaTi, Ga₄Ti₅, Ga₃Ti₅, GaTi₂ and GaTi₃ were verified by Refs. [18–23] in their phase equilibria measurements of corresponding ternary systems.

Another hexagonal phase GaTi₇ was suggested in Ref. [7]. However it proved to be only the ordered GaTi₃ by the subsequent X-ray analysis. Rokuro et al. A low-temperature phase of the $\mathrm{GaTi_3}$ compound was discovered by Simic and Marinkovic [26]. They prepared samples by keeping thin film couples for several days or even for several years at room temperature, and analyzed them by XRD. The low-temperature modification transforms to $\mathrm{GaTi_3}$ after annealing at 393 K. This datum was not included in the optimization because of insufficient support from other workers.

The stability of the $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti_5}$ compound was doubted because of the negative effect of oxygen. Antonova [25] indicated that it should be stable at least above 1273 K after preparing more than 10 samples to check the stability of this compound. A compound $\mathrm{GaTi_9}$, which is listed in Pearson's Handbook, is not included in Table 2 because no other workers identified this compound.

According to the literature and experiments which will be discussed below, the eight compounds reported in Refs. [8,9] were taken in optimization. Crystal structure data of compounds are listed in Table 2, which are from Pearson's Handbook [35] and Ref. [9].

## 3. Experiments

The samples with 37~90 at.% Ga were prepared by mixing together powders of pure Ti and pure of certain compositions and sealing in vacuum quartz tubes. Those of less than 70 at.% Ga were kept at 1273 K for 480 h, and the samples of more than 70 at.% Ga were heated at 1173 K for 720 h. All the samples were ground to powder and analyzed by XRD. The results showed that the compounds of $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti_5}$, $\mathrm{Ga_4Ti_5}$, $\mathrm{GaTi}$, $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti_2}$, $\mathrm{Ga_2Ti}$ and $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti}$ existed at the experimental conditions.

Meanwhile, the diffusion couple of Ti/Ga was prepared. One end of a pure metal Ti bar in contact with molten Ga was sealed in a vacuum quartz tube. The couple was annealed at 1223 K for 840 h and then quenched in air. The polished sample was analyzed by EPMA. Seven compounds of $\mathrm{GaTi_3}$, $\mathrm{GaTi_2}$, $\mathrm{Ga_4Ti_5}$, $\mathrm{GaTi}$, $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti_2}$, $\mathrm{Ga_2Ti}$ and $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti}$ were found consecutively from pure Ti to pure Ga. The compositions of phases are included on the phase diagram in Fig. 1.

DTA was used to determine the Ga-rich part of the phase diagram by heating curves at 2-5 K/min and cooling curves at 10 K/min. The experimental data are shown in Fig. 1. The temperature of the invariant equilibrium $\mathrm{L+Ga_2Ti-Ga_3Ti}$ is 1202 K, consistent with the result of Ref. [8] but different from Ref. [9].

## 4. Modeling

Four solution phases and eight compounds involved in this work are the solution phases of liquid (L), b.c.c. ($\beta$), h.c.p. ($\alpha 1$) and f.c.c. ($\gamma 1$), the line compounds of $\mathrm{GaTi_2}$, $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti_5}$, $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti_2}$, $\mathrm{Ga_2Ti}$, $\mathrm{Ga_3Ti}$, and the solution compounds of $\mathrm{GaTi_3(\alpha 2)}$, $\mathrm{Ga_4Ti_5(\rho)}$ and $\mathrm{GaTi(\gamma 2)}$. The other phase is the terminal phase of orthorhombic (Ga). To match the models used in the pre-existing database [27], and also based on crystal structures, the following thermodynamic models were adopted to describe the phases.

$$
\begin{aligned}
G^{j}= & x_{\mathrm{Ga}} G_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{j}+x_{\mathrm{Ti}} G_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{j}+R T\left(x_{\mathrm{Ga}} \ln x_{\mathrm{Ga}}+x_{\mathrm{Ti}} \ln x_{\mathrm{Ti}}\right) \\
& +x_{\mathrm{Ga}} x_{\mathrm{Ti}} \sum_{k=0}^{n} L_{k}^{j}\left(x_{\mathrm{Ga}}-x_{\mathrm{Ti}}\right)^{k}
\end{aligned}
$$
where $G_{i}^{j}$ is the standard Gibbs energy of formation of pure element $i$ in the $j$ phase, and taken from Dinsdale's database for Pure elements [28]. $x_{i}$ is the molar fraction of element $i$ in the $j$ phase. The third term is the binary excess Gibbs energy term, expressed using the Redlich-Kister formula. $L_{k=0,1, \ldots}^{j}$ are, functions of temperature $(a+b^{*} T+\ldots)$, the interaction parameters for the $j$ phase. They are to be obtained by optimization.

The compounds of $\mathrm{GaTi}_{2}, \mathrm{Ga}_{3} \mathrm{Ti}_{5}, \mathrm{Ga}_{3} \mathrm{Ti}_{2}, \mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{Ti}$ and $\mathrm{Ga}_{3} \mathrm{Ti}$ are treated as stoichiometric phases. Their Gibbs energies (J/mol of formula) are approximated as follows because none of the heat capacities of compounds are available:
$$
G_{\mathrm{Ga}_{m} \mathrm{Ti}_{n}}=a+b T+m \mathrm{GHSERGA}+n \mathrm{GHSERTI}
$$
where $a$ and $b$ are coefficients to be optimized in this work. GHSERGA and GHSERTI are the Gibbs energies of the pure Ga and Ti phases which are stable at 298 K and 1 atm, i.e. corresponding to the so-called 'standard element reference' (1 atm=101 325 Pa).

The compounds $\mathrm{GaTi}_{3}, \mathrm{GaTi}$ and $\mathrm{Ga}_{4} \mathrm{Ti}_{5}$ have homogeneity ranges of composition. $\mathrm{GaTi}_{3}$ is an ordered h.c.p. phase with the $\mathrm{D0}_{19}$ lattice. The atomic arrangement of $\mathrm{Ga}$ and $\mathrm{Ti}$ atoms in an ideal $\mathrm{D0}_{19}$ structure is presented in Fig. 2a. Each Ti atom neighbors with four Ga atoms and eight Ti atoms. Ga atoms have only Ti atoms as their nearest neighbors. GaTi is an ordered f.c.c. phase with the $\mathrm{L1}_{0}$ structure which is presented in Fig. 2b. It is a layered structure of the CuAu-type. Rossouw et al. [10] found that the non-stoichiometry of GaTi was caused by excess Ga atoms occupying some of the Ti sites, using the technique of higher order Laue zone measurements. In the comparable Al-Ti system, the anti-structural atoms were also approved to be the dominant defects in the non-stoichiometric $\mathrm{Ti}_{3} \mathrm{Al}$ and TiAl by Herzig et al. [29]. So, the compound energy model (CEM) with two sublattices, $(\mathrm{Ga}, \mathrm{Ti})_{n}(\mathrm{Ga}, \mathrm{Ti})_{m}$, is used to describe the compounds $\mathrm{GaTi}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{GaTi}$ with $\mathrm{Ga}_{n} \mathrm{Ti}_{m}$ as stoichiometric phases. For $\mathrm{Ga}_{4} \mathrm{Ti}_{5}$, the same model is adopted. The Gibbs energy (J/mol of formula) is expressed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{m}^{j}= & y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ga}} y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} G_{\mathrm{Ga}_{n} \mathrm{Ga}_{m}}+y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ga}} y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} G_{\mathrm{Ga}_{n} \mathrm{Ti}_{m}}+y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ga}} y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} G_{\mathrm{Ti}_{n} \mathrm{Ga}_{m}} \\
& +y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ga}} y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} G_{\mathrm{Ti}_{n} \mathrm{Ti}_{m}}+R T\left[n\left(y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ga}} \ln y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ga}}+y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ga}} \ln y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ga}}\right)\right. \\
& \left.+m\left(y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} \ln y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ti}}+y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} \ln y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ti}}\right)\right]+G^{\mathrm{ex}}
\end{aligned}
$$
where $y_{\mathrm{Ga} / \mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ga} / \mathrm{Ti}}$ is the molar fraction of Ga or Ti in Ga or Ti sublattice. $G_{\mathrm{Ga}_{n} \mathrm{Ga}_{m}}$ and $G_{\mathrm{Ti}_{n} \mathrm{Ti}_{m}}$ are identical with the Gibbs energies of $n+m$ molar of pure Ga and Ti occupying all the lattice sites of corresponding compounds, respectively. Here, they are defined as $G_{\mathrm{Ga}: \mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{GaTi}_{3}}=4 G_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{hcp}}, G_{\mathrm{Ti}: \mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{GaTi}_{3}}=4 G_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{hcp}}$; $G_{\mathrm{Ga}: \mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{GaTi}}=2 G_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{fcc}}, \quad G_{\mathrm{Ti}: \mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{GaTi}}=2 G_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{fcc}} ; \quad G_{\mathrm{Ga}: \mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ga}_{4} \mathrm{Ti}_{5}}=9 G_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{hcp}}$, $G_{\mathrm{Ti}: \mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ga}_{4} \mathrm{Ti}_{5}}=9 G_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{hcp}} . G_{\mathrm{Ga}_{n} \mathrm{Ti}_{m}}$ is the Gibbs energy of stoichiometric $\mathrm{Ga}_{n} \mathrm{Ti}_{m} . G_{\mathrm{Ti}_{n} \mathrm{Ga}_{m}}$ is the Gibbs energy of hypothetical stoichiometric states. $G^{\text {ex }}$ is the excess Gibbs energy term:

\begin{aligned}
G^{\mathrm{ex}}= & y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ga}} y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ga}}\left(y_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} L_{\mathrm{Ga}, \mathrm{Ti}: \mathrm{Ga}}+y_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{\mathrm{Ti}} L_{\mathrm{Ga}, \mathrm{Ti}: \mathrm{Ti}}\right) \\

The parameters $L_{i, j: k}$ and $L_{k: i, j}$ account for interaction energies between the atoms on one sublattice for a given occupancy of the other, and can be described by a Redlich-Kister polynomial with temperature dependent coefficients, as follows:

$$
L_{i, j: k}=\sum_{n=0,1 \ldots}^{z}\left(a_{n}+b_{n} T\right)\left(y_{i}-y_{j}\right)^{n}
\tag{5}
$$

This is the same form as the expression of the excess term in Eq. (1). The coefficients $a$ and $b$ in Eqs. (1), (2) and (5) were derived in the present optimization.

## 5. Optimization and results

The PARROT module [30] of the Thermo-Calc software [31] was used for the optimization. The least squares theory is applied in the software to perform the optimi- zation of thermodynamic descriptions using experimental data. All the experimental data were first critically re- viewed and selected. Each piece of selected information was given a certain weight, which was changed by the 'trial and error' method in the assessment, until most of the

## 6. Conclusion

Experiments were conducted to identify the stability of intermetallic compounds and check the phase diagram in the Ga-rich part with the aid of EPMA, XRD and DTA. Eight compounds, $GaTi_2$, $GaTi_3$, $Ga_4Ti_5$, GaTi, $Ga_3Ti_5$, $Ga_3Ti_2$, $Ga_2Ti$ and $Ga_3Ti$, were determined as being stable and were taken into account in the optimization. The two-sublattice CEM was adopted to describe the solution compounds. A set of thermodynamic parameters was obtained by optimization based on the available experimental results. The calculated results are in good agreement with the available experimental data. The equilibrium measurements on the Ga-side and more experimental thermodynamic properties for this system are required to allow a much better thermodynamic description of this system.
