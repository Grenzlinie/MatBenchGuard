# LIQUID PHASE EPITAXY OF ZnSe IN Sn: CALCULATION OF THE TERNARY PHASE DIAGRAM AND ELECTRONIC PROPERTIES

A. HEURTEL, A. MARBEUF, H. TEWS* and Y. MARFAING

CNRS, Laboratoire de Physique des Solides, 1 Place A. Briand, F-92190 Meudon--Bellevue, France

The ternary equilibrium diagram Zn-Sn-Se has been calculated for a liquid of composition $x_{Zn}=x_{Se}=10^{-2}$ to $5×10^{-2}$. For each binary system the liquid mixing energies (mixing enthalpy, excess entropy), are determined from literature data and by minimization of the total free enthalpy (Simplex method). The ternary diagram is generated in the same way with the help of the solubility measurements of ZnSe in Sn done by Rubenstein (1968) and us. Our experiments were performed in a special close tube arrangement also used for ZnSe epitaxy from tin solution. Epitaxial layers were prepared at temperature around $950^{\circ} C$ on ZnSe substrates. Photoluminescence and electrical measurements indicate the material is donor doped. Chemical analysis reveals a profile of Al with a maximum near the substrate interface. That points out the importance of substrate purity in II-VI compounds epitaxy.

## 1. Introduction

The liquid phase epitaxy (LPE) allows one to crystallize a material at a temperature lower than its melting point. The metallic element of the compound is generally used as a solvent. For ZnSe, the high fusion point $(T_{ZnSe}^{F}=1788 K)$ leads to a selenium solubility in zinc of $8.7×10^{-4}$ atoms at $1000^{\circ} C$ [1-3]. This solubility is too weak to produce layers thick enough for physical studies.

Various authors have achieved ZnSe epitaxy in heterosolvents: in gallium [4,5], in indium [3,4] or tin [6]. Kosai et al. [7] have shown that epitaxy in tin solvent gives layers with a particularly small tin fraction incorporated in solid ZnSe.

We have undertaken a thermodynamic study of the ternary system Zn-Sn-Se in order to better define the conditions for epitaxy of ZnSe from a tin bath. We also report preliminary results of the electric and optical characterization of the epi- taxial layers.

## 2. Phase diagram calculation [8]

The equilibrium phase diagram Zn-Sn-Se must be consistent with the three Zn-Sn, Zn-Se and Sn-Se binary systems.

### 2.1. Zn-Sn system

This system is of eutectic type. From calorimet- ric measurements and from the values of zinc activities reported in the literature, the mixing enthalpy $^{M}H_{Zn-Sn}^{L}$ and the excess entropy $^{xs}S_{Zn-Sn}^{L}$ of the liquid can be represented by the following relations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{ }^{\mathrm{M}} H_{\mathrm{Zn}-\mathrm{Sn}}^{\mathrm{L}} & =x_{\mathrm{Zn}} x_{\mathrm{Sn}}\left[2.360+0.907\left(x_{\mathrm{Zn}}-x_{\mathrm{Sn}}\right)\right. \\
& \left.+0.216\left(x_{\mathrm{Zn}}-x_{\mathrm{Sn}}\right)^{2}\right] × 4.184 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mole}^{-1}, \quad(1)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
{ }^{\mathrm{xs}} S_{\mathrm{Zn}-\mathrm{Sn}}^{\mathrm{L}} & =x_{\mathrm{Zn}} x_{\mathrm{Sn}}\left[1.42+0.58\left(x_{\mathrm{Zn}}-x_{\mathrm{Sn}}\right)\right. \\
& \left.+0.12\left(x_{\mathrm{Zn}}-s_{\mathrm{Sn}}\right)^{2}\right] × 4.184 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~mole}^{-1} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}, \quad(2)
\end{aligned}
$$

in the whole temperature range from 700 to 1223 K.

### 2.2. Zn-Se, Sn-Se and Zn-Sn-Se systems

These three systems include a definite compound. The free enthalpy of the solid $G^{\text{S}}$, referred to the liquid elements, can be written as a function of the melting entropy $S^{\text{F}}$ and of the melting temperature $T^{\text{F}}$ as:

$$
G^{\text{S}}=S^{\text{F}}(T-T^{\text{F}}). \tag{3}
$$

If the composition of the liquid phase is $x_{1}$, $x_{2},\dots,x_{n}$, the excess free enthalpy $^{\text{xs}}G^{\text{L}}$ related to the excess quantities $^{\text{M}}H_{i-j}^{\text{L}}$ and $^{\text{xs}}S_{i-j}^{\text{L}}$ of the corresponding binaries is given by the following expression which includes correction terms due to multi-component interactions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{^{\text{xs}}G^{\text{L}}} &= \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \sum_{j=i+1}^{n} \left[ {^{\text{M}}H_{i-j}^{\text{L}}}-T{^{\text{xs}}S_{i-j}^{\text{L}}} \right] \\
& \quad + \prod_{i=1}^{n} x_{i} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}T \right) x_{i} \right]. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

The equilibrium diagram is then built up by minimization of the total free enthalpy $G$ of the system, at temperature $T$ and constant global composition [10]:

$$
G=\alpha G^{\text{S}}+(1-\alpha)\left[ {^{\text{xs}}G^{\text{L}}}+RT\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{i}\log x_{i} \right], \tag{5}
$$

where $\alpha$ is the crystallized fraction of the solid.

$^{\text{M}}H_{i-j}^{\text{L}}$ and $^{\text{xs}}S_{i-j}^{\text{L}}$ for Zn-Se and Sn-Se, and $\alpha_{i}$ and $\beta_{i}$ for Zn-Sn-Se are unknown. Their expressions and their values are obtained by fitting the respective liquidus curves. The simplex method [11] is used for the $G$ minimization and the fitting procedure.

### 2.3. Zn-Se and Sn-Se

The fitted liquidus correspond to:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{^{\text{M}}H_{\text{Zn-Se}}^{\text{L}}} &= x_{\text{Zn}}x_{\text{Se}}\left[ 17.663-8.782(x_{\text{Zn}}-x_{\text{Se}}) \right. \\
& \quad \left. +8.525(x_{\text{Zn}}-x_{\text{Se}})^2 \right] \times 4.184 \text{ kJ mole}^{-1}, \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
{^{\text{xs}}S_{\text{Zn-Se}}^{\text{L}}} &= x_{\text{Zn}}x_{\text{Se}}\left[ 4.10-0.73(x_{\text{Zn}}-x_{\text{Se}}) \right] \\
& \quad \times 4.184 \text{ J mole}^{-1} \text{ K}^{-1}, \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
{^{\text{M}}H_{\text{Sn-Se}}^{\text{L}}} &= x_{\text{Sn}}x_{\text{Se}}\left[ 5.086+2.936(x_{\text{Sn}}-x_{\text{Se}}) \right. \\
& \quad \left. -3.846(x_{\text{Zn}}-x_{\text{Se}})^2 \right] \times 4.184 \text{ kJ mole}^{-1}, \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
{^{\text{xs}}S_{\text{Sn-Se}}^{\text{L}}} &= x_{\text{Sn}}x_{\text{Se}}\left[ -0.05+0.63(x_{\text{Sn}}-x_{\text{Se}}) \right] \\
& \quad \times 4.184 \text{ J mole}^{-1} \text{ K}^{-1}. \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2.4. Zn-Sn-Se

Because of the non-regular behaviour of the Zn-Sn and particularly Zn-Se and Sn-Se liquids, the ternary interaction coefficients $\alpha_{i}$, $\beta_{i}$, are not zero (table 2). The numerical values are high, even if a partial compensation between them occurs. The part of the liquidus corresponding to $x_{\text{Zn}}=x_{\text{Se}}$ is presented in fig. 1.

It be noticed that when the temperature decreases, the solubility of Sn in solid must increase. In this case the solidus scanned by the conode is a Zn$_{1-y}$Sn$_{y}$Se solid solution. Nevertheless the lack of data on this alloy only allows to indicate tendencies: from those considerations a minimum temperature for growing pure ZnSe from Sn solvent could be defined.

## 3. Experimental: apparatus and procedure

An apparatus was designed to make possible both solubility measurements and liquid phase epitaxy. The main part is a vacuum sealed ampoule presented in fig. 2.

A ZnSe wafer (10-12 mm in diameter and 1 mm thickness) cut from an ingot prepared by a high pressure Bridgman melt growth is placed on a polished part of a quartz casing previously fused inside a baked-out ampoule. Then the ampoule is sealed (under $2 \times 10^{-6}$ Torr). Atmospheric pressure causes the quartz rod to press the ZnSe wafer against the inner casing through a cap containing several carbon foils acting as springs. This provides a barrier against solution outflow on the crystal sides when the ampoule is turned over. Substrate temperature is monitored by a thermocouple placed in contact with the ampoule at the crystal level.

![](./images/812444779374706688_2.jpg)

Fig. 2. Schematic diagram of the ampoule.

The experimental procedure was as follows:
- For solubility measurements, the ampoule contains 4 g of tin which is placed in contact with a polycrystalline ZnSe wafer for 24 h at a temperature $T$. The separation of tin from the crystal is made by tipping. The loss of mass of the crystal directly leads to the composition of the liquid in equilibrium with the solid at $T$; the experimental results and those of Rubenstein [6] are presented in fig. 1.
- For ZnSe liquid phase epitaxy we used polycrystalline ZnSe substrates with a main single part oriented (100) or single crystal substrates oriented (110). The solvent was 6N-grade Sn electrolytically polished in a $\mathrm{HClO}_{4}-\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}$ aqueous solution [12]. ZnSe used as solute was sublimed Merck powder.

The ampoule was quickly introduced into the furnace above $540^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ so as to limit the formation of $\mathrm{SiO}$ and $\mathrm{SiSnO}_{3}$ [13] inside the ampoule. The solution is held $1 \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~h}$ at $960^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ ($10^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ above liquidus temperature). At $948^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ the ampoule is tipped. Then the solution covers the substrate and epitaxial growth starts. The cooling rate was in the range $0.3-0.4^{\circ} \mathrm{C} / \mathrm{min}$. To avoid $\mathrm{SiSnO}_{3}$ solidification (at $890^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$) on the epitaxial layer, epitaxy was stopped at $900^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ by tipping the ampoule. In a second set of experiments growths were performed in graphitized ampoules.

## 4. Results of epitaxy experiments

### 4.1. Morphology of the layers

The average thickness of the epitaxial layers, which always were continuous, varied from 3 to 16 $\mu \mathrm{m}$ depending on growth conditions. Numerous growth twins were visible on the surface of the layers on both (100) and (110) planes. Moreover, irregular surface undulations appeared. The substrate edges protected from the liquid bath also showed etch pits with the periodicity seen on the layers. These irregularities are thought to result

from a thermal attack of the substrates during ZnSe dissolution into the solvent.

### 4.2. Chemical analysis

Using a SIMS probe, we have looked for impurity contamination occurring in the layers during the epitaxial growth. In agreement with previous work, incorporation of tin is very low.

However, a concentration profile of aluminium has been detected with a maximum concentration located near the layer-substrate interface (fig. 3). This contamination was also observed when the growth was performed in graphitized ampoules. We suggest that aluminium atoms mainly come into the layers by diffusion from the substrates.

### 4.3. Photoluminescence

Low-temperature photoluminescence spectra were taken from the ZnSe layers; these spectra show, in the high energy part, a strong $I_2$ donor bound exciton line, and a small $I_1^{\text{deep}}$ line (exciton bound to the Cu acceptor) (fig. 4). In the low energy part, a broad red band with a maximum around 2 eV is observed, corresponding to the Cu red emission. Comparing the ZnSe LPE layer with the substrate, the red luminescence has shifted slightly to higher energies in the layer, and the $I_1^{\text{deep}}$ line is considerably narrower in the layer than in the substrate, thus indicating a smaller Cu concentration in the layer.

### 4.4. Electrical properties

Electrical measurements have been performed using a Van der Pauw configuration. Some results are collected in table 3 and show an unusual combination of high electron concentration and large activation energy.

In the temperature range 90-200 K the measured mobility is proportional to $T^{3/2}$ indicating a dominant scattering by ionized impurities. Using the Brooks-Herring formula we deduce for sample SZ4 a total ionized impurity concentration around $2 \times 10^{18}\ \text{cm}^{-3}$.

These data cannot be reconciled with a model applying to homogeneous material. We think that the electrical measurements are strongly affected by the gradient of aluminium donors detected in the layers. The apparent mobility and electron concentration are characteristic of the more strongly doped part of the layers while the photoluminescence results have to be related to the slightly doped outer surface. No simple analysis can be given for the activation energy of the Hall effect.

## 5. Conclusion

We have conducted a thermodynamic analysis of the system Zn-Sn-Se with regard to the liquid phase epitaxy of ZnSe in tin solvent. A part of the ternary phase diagram corresponding to $x_{\mathrm{Zn}}=x_{\mathrm{Se}}$ has been determined from the calculated binary diagrams Zn-Sn, Sn-Se and Zn-Se. The final optimization is made using the results of solubility measurements of ZnSe in liquid tin obtained by us and others.

The apparatus designed for these measurements is also used for growing ZnSe epitaxial layers on ZnSe substrates from a tin bath.

Electrical and photoluminescence measurements indicate that the process is able to produce pure ZnSe. However, the quality of the layer is affected by the impurity contamination from the substrates.
