## Equilibrium Diagram
The assessed equilibrium phase diagram of the Ge-Bi system (Fig. 1) contains a eutectic transformation very close to the melting point of Bi (271 °C). Mutual solubilities of Ge and Bi in the solid state are negligible.

The main features of the Ge-Bi phase diagram were first determined by [40Rut] and [40Sto] using thermal analysis corroborated with metallographic and X-ray studies. Neither the purity of their materials nor the accuracy of their measurements were specified. More recently, [60Thu] and [61Sch] measured liquidus points on the Bi-rich side of the diagram, using a weighing technique. [60Thu] placed an excess of Ge with a known amount of Bi in a silica tube and held it at temperature for 0.5 to 1 h; the purity of the materials was "high" and the accuracy of temperature measurements was reported as ±2 °C. The compositions of saturated melts were obtained from the loss in weight of Ge. [61Sch] dissolved Ge in molten Bi at 800 °C, then cooled the alloy to a temperature in the range 270 to 670 °C, and annealed it for 24 h before quenching. Compositions were reported to be accurate to within 0.001 at.% Ge; the accuracy of temperature measurements was not specified. The results of [60Thu] and [61Sch] are in good agreement with each other, but do not agree with the data of [40Sto] or the extrapolated data of [40Rut].

Experimental liquidus data are listed in Table 1; results of [40Rut], [40Sto], and [61Sch] are transcribed from graphical data. These values were optimized with the enthalpy of mixing data discussed below; optimization by least-squares regression analysis permitted computation of the excess Gibbs energy of the liquid phase. This function and energy functions of the solid phases were used to calculate the liquidus boundaries, as shown in Fig. 1. The calculations were based on the assumption that the enthalpy and entropy of mixing functions are temperature independent and that the mutual solid solubilities of Ge and Bi are negligible. It should be noted that the optimized liquidus boundary (Fig. 1) is distinct from the two alternatives discussed below.

In the course of the optimizations, it became evident that the data of [40Rut] and [40Sto] were not compatible with experimental enthalpy of mixing data. This led the present evaluators to consider two alternatives to the phase diagram shown in Fig. 1. In one optimization, the liquidus data of [40Rut] and [40Sto] were omitted, and the phase diagram was calculated based on the most recent data of [60Thu] and [61Sch] and on the enthalpy of mixing. In another optimization, the experimental enthalpies of mixing were omitted, and only the liquidus data were used to optimize the Gibbs energy. Alternative 1 and Alternative 2 are given in Fig. 2. Alternative 2 understandably agrees better with the measured liquidus.

However, it ignores the extensive and accurate enthalpy of mixing data.

The eutectic point was first estimated as close to the melting point of Bi by [40Rut] and [40Sto]. Most recently, [79Sha] reported the eutectic composition at "99.911" at.% Bi from directional crystallization experiments using radioactive tracers; the crystallization rate was less than 1 cm/h, which apparently approached equilibrium conditions. [53Thu] and [60Thu] estimated the eutectic composition by calculating the Ge liquidus composition at a temperature equal to the melting point of Bi; the eutectic temperature was then rechecked by using the composition value to calculate the melting point depression of Bi. The present evaluators calculated the eutectic coordinates by the same method, but used the broader data base given in Tables 1 and 4. Eutectic coordinates reported by the referenced sources are listed in Table 2.

A probable (Ge) solidus is reproduced in Fig. 3 [62Tru]. [62Tru] conducted diffusion experiments at $865 \pm 5$ °C in evacuated silica tubes for 97 days with 6 N pure Bi as the dopant source; the annealed samples were analyzed using Hall effect measurements. The equilibrium solid solubility of Bi in Ge at the annealing temperature was reported as $8 \times 10^{-5}$ at.% Bi. [62Tru] used their estimated temperature dependence of the Bi distribution coefficient and liquidus data of [60Thu] to calculate solid solubilities at other temperatures. The maximum solid solubility of Bi in (Ge) was also estimated by [58Mor], who measured resistivity in crystals grown at a rate of 1.9 cm/h; the reported value was on the order of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ at.% Bi for an unspecified temperature. Similar results were obtained by [73Gon]: $3 \times 10^{-5}$ at.% Bi in crystals pulled at a rate of 0.5 mm/min.

The equilibrium distribution coefficient of Bi near the melting point of Ge was first reported by [53Bur] as $4 \times 10^{-5}$, calculated from effective distribution coefficient measurements. [60Tru] estimated a value of $4 \times 10^{-5}$ based on results of [58Mor]. More recently, [73Gon] reported $1.65 \times 10^{-5}$ estimated by measured effective distribution coefficients to zero crystal pulling rate.

The equilibrium distribution coefficient of Ge in (Bi) near the eutectic point was reported by [79Sha] as 0.11 from crystallization experiments mentioned above.

## Metastable Phases

A comprehensive study of amorphous Ge-Bi films containing up to 25 at.% Bi was conducted by [74Vas1, 74Vas2]. The films were evaporated using "high purity" Ge and 6 N pure Bi onto glass substrates cooled by liquid nitrogen or water. Structural and electrical properties of the films were characterized by [74Vas1], and their optical and photoelectrical properties were studied by [74Vas2].

## Thermodynamics
Enthalpy of mixing for the liquid solution was determined for the complete composition range at $1000\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ from calorimetric measurements [71Pre], and at $1027\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ from quantitative differential thermal analysis [78Mec]. Their reported values (Table 4) have been optimized using least-squares regression, and the resulting enthalpy of mixing functions are shown in Fig. 4, with the respective experimental data points. The thermodynamic functions are approximated by the following polynomials:

$$\Delta_{\text{mix}}H = (1 - X_{\text{Bi}})X_{\text{Bi}}\ (12\,350 + 1072X_{\text{Bi}})\quad \text{J/mol}$$

and

$$\Delta_{\text{mix}}H = (1 - X_{\text{Bi}})X_{\text{Bi}}\ (14\,320 - 1420X_{\text{Bi}})\quad \text{J/mol}$$

The first function [71Pre] has a maximum value of 3220 $\pm$ 100 J/mol at 51 at.% Bi, whereas the second function [78Mec] has a maximum value of $3400 \pm 150$ J/mol at 49 at.% Bi. The functions are in good agreement, but the data of [71Pre] are slightly more consistent with the experimental liquidus points given in Table 1. Furthermore, direct calorimetry used by [71Pre] guarantees better accuracy of the measured enthalpies than the quantitative differential thermal analysis used by [78Mec].

The optimized enthalpy of mixing [71Pre] has been used with the liquid solubility data to calculate the excess entropy of mixing function:

$$S^{\text{ex}} = (1 - X_{\text{Bi}})X_{\text{Bi}}\ (-0.57 - 3.26\ X_{\text{Bi}})\quad \text{J/mol·K}$$

The Gibbs energy of fusion functions for pure Ge and Bi used in these calculations are as follows:

$$
\begin{align*}
\Delta_{\text{fus}}G_{\text{Ge}} &= 32\,940 + 23.4575\ T + 3.6777 \times 10^{-3}\ T^{2} \\
&\quad - 7.7613\ T \ln T \quad \text{J/mol}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\Delta_{\text{fus}}G(\text{Bi}) &= 4198 + 108.96\ T + 15.234 \times 10^{-3}\ T^{2} \\
&\quad - 19.9493\ T \ln T + 2.05 \times 10^{5}\ T^{-1} \quad \text{J/mol}
\end{align*}
$$

The heats of fusion and heat capacities are from [77Bar].

A thermodynamic description of the Ge-Bi liquid based on solubility data was attempted by [53Thu], [60Thu], [70Rao], and [80Sur]. Calculations of [53Thu], who used a strictly regular approximation, seem to be in fair agreement with the present evaluation; the results of other calculations are incompatible with the experimental enthalpy of mixing. Nonetheless, the liquid solution has unanimously been determined as endothermic, indicating positive deviation from Raoultian behavior.

[58Wei] and [62Koz] conducted some thermodynamic calculations on the (Ge) solid solution. [58Wei] estimated the binding energy and the strain energy of Bi in (Ge), which gave the heat of solution as 105 kJ/mol. [62Koz] plotted a retrograde solidus, indicating a maximum solubility of $4.5 \times 10^{-4}$ at.% Bi at 1150 to $1200\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$. These results are purely theoretical and rely on a series of simplifying assumptions.
