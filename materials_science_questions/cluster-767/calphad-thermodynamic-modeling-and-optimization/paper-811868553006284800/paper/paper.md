**Abstract**—The dynamic and kinetic properties of Al-Li melts are calculated. The liquid phase of this system is shown to be characterized by three states with different ion distributions and different degrees of electron localization.

## INTRODUCTION
The structural and thermodynamic characteristics of liquid metals whose electronic properties are satisfactorily described in terms of a local model pseudopotential (MP) are calculated using a model that considers an alloy as a two-component classical system. The average potential energy of the ion interaction in this system depends strongly on the degree of ion–ion correlation, which is expressed by partial structure factors. At temperatures at which a solid phase in the liquid alloy is absent, these structure factors are well simulated by the correlations of the corresponding ensembles of solid spheres.

However, binary metallic alloys are actually three-component systems that contain an ensemble of conduction electrons apart from ions of two types. The electron distribution in such alloys is usually considered as a result of the adiabatic response to ion redistribution written in a linear approximation. The characteristics of the electron subsystem are written in terms of the jellium model. Their dependences on the melt component concentrations are fully determined by the conduction-electron density, which changes linearly under these conditions.

The calculation of the properties of binary melts was initially based on the approximations of the quasichemi-cal theory of solutions, in which the interaction energy per bond is a parameter; i.e., it is independent of the component concentration. In the standard pseudopotential approach, taking into account screening processes leads to a relation between the interionic interaction potential and the conduction-electron density. In this work, we use the variational parameter (VP) approach and introduce a concentration dependence of the exchange-correlation interaction parameter in a system of conduction electrons. This interaction in a metallic system substantially determines the characteristics of the interionic interaction potential.

When studying the dynamic effects of conduction-electron polarization, Pines [1] used the jellium model and obtained a rough dependence of the correlation energy on the electron density. In this work, we try to estimate the effect of the concentration dependence of the correlation energy written in the VP approach [2] on the properties of binary Al–Li alloys.

## MODEL PARAMETERS OF ALUMINUM AND LITHIUM
In [2], we described a self-consistent procedure for determining the model parameters of metallic melts, namely, solid sphere packing coefficient $\eta$, Ashcroft model potential radius $r_{c}$, and exchange-correlation interaction parameter $A_{v}$, and we found the values of these model parameters for simple metal melts.

The correlation energy was determined from the relationship
$$
E_{\text{cor}}^{e}=E_{\text{el}}-E_{ex}^{0}=F_{1}^{\text{s}}T_{0}-E_{ex}^{0}, \tag{1}
$$
where $E_{\text{el}}$ is the internal energy of the electron subsystem in the metal; $E_{ex}^{0}$ is the exchange energy in the Hartree–Fock approach; $T_{0}$ is the average kinetic energy of electrons; and $F_{1}^{\text{s}}$ is the interparticle interaction parameter from the phenomenological Landau theory of quantum liquids [3], which is determined from the equation
$$
F_{1}^{\text{s}}=\frac{E_{\text{el}}}{T_{0}}=\left(-\frac{4 \pi A_{v} N\left(E_{F}\right)}{k_{F}^{2}+4 \pi N\left(E_{F}\right)}\right).
$$

In [2], we calculated the density of states on the Fermi surface $N(E_{F})$ in second order perturbation theory. In this work, we determined packing coefficient $\eta$ and solid sphere diameter $d$ using the Mansoori–Canfield procedure and calculated the internal energy using the correlation energy determined by Eq. (1). The density of states was calculated in the Green’s function approach [4]. Table 1 gives the characteristics of aluminum–lithium melts at a temperature of 1023 K.

As follows from the data in Table 1, the value of variational parameter $A_{v}$ for aluminum is close to 0.5; in the Hubbard approximation [5], this value was used for all metals. For lithium, the value of $A_{v}$ is well above 0.5. The model potential (which is similar to the Ashcroft MP) contains a rather large $p$ component. The authors of [6] noted that the situation in lithium where $p$ states are absent in the closed shell should be considered separately. It is usually assumed that both the choice of the pseudopotential and the method of taking into account exchange-correlation effects equally affect the calculated properties of metals. Since the exchange-correlation interaction parameter in this work is determined semiempirically, we assume that the description of the exchange-correlation effects includes the effects of the presence of the $p$ component in the model potential. Therefore, the parameter $A_{v}$ of lithium is well above the parameter $A_{v}$ of aluminum.

## Al–Li ALLOY DENSITY

When calculating the properties of the alloy, we assumed that the exchange-correlation interaction parameter of conduction electrons changes additively; that is,
$$
A_{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{2} C_{i}\left\{A_{v}\right\}_{i},
$$
where $C_{1}$ and $\{A_{v}\}_{1}$ are the aluminum concentration and the exchange-correlation interaction parameter of aluminum, respectively, and $C_{2}$ and $\{A_{v}\}_{2}$ are the same for lithium.

Free Helmholtz energy $F$ in the Mansoori–Canfield variational approach
$$
\left(\partial F / \partial \eta_{i}\right)_{\Omega, T}=0, \quad i=1,2, \quad(2)
$$
was calculated by the procedure described in [7]. Here, $\Omega$ is the average volume per ion. However, in this work, we represent the internal energy of the electron subsystem as the energy in Eq. (1), and the density of states was calculated in the Green’s function approximation.

Data on the concentration dependence of the Al–Li melt density are absent. At the first stage, we determined the average atomic volume in Eq. (2) from the additive change in the atomic mass $(\bar{M}=\sum_{i=1}^{2} C_{i} M_{i})$ and the density $(\bar{\rho}=\sum_{i=1}^{2} C_{i} \rho_{i})$ ,
$$
\Omega_{0}=11205.825 \overline{M} / \overline{\rho}. \quad(3)
$$

For the density components of the pure metals $\rho_{i}$ $(kg/m^{3})$ [8] from Eq. (3), we obtain atomic volume $\Omega_{0}$ in au³. Table 2 presents the concentration dependences of exchange-correlation interaction parameter $A_{v}$; atomic volume $\Omega_{0}$; and packing coefficients $\eta_{1}$ and $\eta_{2}$ of the model solid sphere liquids for the aluminum and lithium subsystems, respectively. Note that packing coefficient $\eta_{2}$, which reflects the characteristics of the lithium subsystem, exhibits maximum relative changes as lithium concentration $C_{2}$ changes in the ranges near 30 and 90 at %. For the aluminum subsystem, the relative change in packing coefficient $\eta_{1}$ is insignificant. This behavior is seen in the concentration dependences of the changes of the packing coefficients $\Delta \eta_{1}$ and $\Delta \eta_{2}$ for the model solid sphere liquids (Fig. 1) and in the concentration dependences of the correlation energy (Fig. 2). The absolute value of the correlation energy for a composition near 30 at % Li is higher than the correlation energy of pure aluminum. In the range near 90 at % Li, the correlation energy curve has no specific features.

Figure 3 shows the concentration dependence of the excess entropy in the Al–Li system. Curve 2, which represents the calculated entropy, is seen to be well above experimental curve 1 [9]. The authors of [10] presented a relationship that reflects the interrelation between the experimental $(\Delta S)$ and calculated $(\Delta S_{0})$ excess entropies and the change in the atomic volume $\Delta \Omega$ induced by alloy formation,
$$
\Delta S=\Delta S_{0}+\left[\left(k_{B}-\eta S_{\eta}^{\prime}\right) \frac{\Delta \Omega}{\Omega_{0}}\right]+\eta S_{\eta}^{\prime} \frac{\Delta \omega}{\omega_{0}}, \quad(4)
$$
where $S_{\eta}^{\prime}$ is the derivative of the alloy entropy obtained from the equation of state of the thermodynamic system with respect to packing coefficient $\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}$. In a reduced form, this derivative is written as
$$
S_{\eta}^{\prime} / k_{B}=-(1-\eta)^{-1}\left[1+3(1-\eta)^{-2}\right],
$$

# KINETIC AND DYNAMIC PROPERTIES

As the kinetic properties, we have studied the electrical resistivity of the alloys as a quantity that is reciprocal to their conductivity. The electrical resistivity was calculated in the Faber-Ziman approximation [11]. The concentration dependence of the electrical resistivity of Al-Li alloys is shown in Fig. 5. It is seen that the electrical resistivity $\rho_{\text{el}}$ of Al-Li alloys near 80 at % Li is more than an order of magnitude higher than the electrical resistivity of pure aluminum or lithium for both the additive change with the concentration of atomic volume $\Omega_0$ and the change with actual atomic volume $\Omega_1$. We assume that, in the range near 80 at % Li, electrons in the melt are substantially localized. This assumption is also supported by the high values of the density of states and the decrease in the absolute values of the correlation

energy. In this concentration range, atomic volume $\Omega_1$ is 25% larger than atomic volume $\Omega_0$.

As the dynamic properties of the alloys, we consid- ered the sound velocity in them and their dynamic com- pressibility. In [12], we proposed describing the ther- modynamic properties of a melt by means of the char- acteristics of collective ion vibrations using the numerical values of the wavenumber and the frequency of Einstein phonons. Wavenumber $k_0$ for these phonons is determined from the position of the maximum in the structure factor of the melt. Frequency $\omega_0$ is specified by a function of the temperature, ion mass, and struc- ture factor at $k_0$. The characteristics of the Einstein phonons in an alloy can be estimated in terms of a one- parameter model using packing coefficient $\eta$. The val- ues of $\eta$, $k_0$, and $\omega_0$ are given in Table 3.

Mattuck [13] proved that the characteristics of a sys- tem of Einstein phonons are analogous to those of a system of conventional phonons with a specific disper- sion law. Using the approach in [13], we can write the sound velocity as

$$
c = d\omega_0/\sqrt{2},
$$

where $d$ is the solid sphere diameter determined from the packing coefficient and the atomic volume. The concentration dependences of the sound velocity are also given in Table 3. The sound velocity increases gradually in the concentration range from zero to 65 at % Li and increases sharply (almost twofold) as the lithium con- centration increases further. The temperature of the liq- uidus line in the Al–Li phase diagram changes by about 10% in the concentration range from zero to 70 at % Li and then decreases sharply to the melting temperature of lithium. A sharp increase in the sound velocity implies an increase in the degree of ion ordering.

To estimate the compressibility factor, we used the equation

$$
\beta = 1/\rho c^{2}.
$$

The concentration dependences of the compressibility factor are shown in Fig. 6.

In the concentration range 40-80 at % Li, the compressibility factor changes insignificantly, and its value is almost twofold that of pure aluminum or lithium. Using the specific features of the changes in the atomic volume and the compressibility factor in this range, we assume that the ion distribution in it acquires a specific rarefied character.

The Al-Li phase diagram has no stable chemical compounds at 1023 K. However, the results of this work imply the presence of three states in the liquid phase in this system.

A normal state is observed in the melt of a binary alloy in the concentration range 0-40 at % Li.

As the lithium concentration increases to 80 at %, the liquid phase is characterized by a specific ion distribution: conduction electrons are substantially localized, and the density of states at the Fermi surface increases.

As the lithium concentration increases above 80 at % Li, the average atomic volume begins to decrease. Taking into account the increase in the sound velocity, we assume that the degree of ion ordering increases as the lithium concentration increases above 65 at %.

## CONCLUSIONS
(1) We have shown that the data on the concentration dependence of the excess entropy of a binary metallic alloy can be used to estimate the concentration dependence of its density.
(2) The molten state of the Al-Li system is characterized by the presence of at least three phases in different concentration ranges, and the properties of these phases are specified by different degrees of ion ordering in them.
