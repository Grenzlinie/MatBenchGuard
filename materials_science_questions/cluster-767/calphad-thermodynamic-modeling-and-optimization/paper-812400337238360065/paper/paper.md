# THE PHASE SEPARATIONS OF Fe-Al-Co ORDERING ALLOYS

TORU MIYAZAKI, KAZUTAKA ISOBE†, TAKAO KOZAKAI
and MINORU DOI

Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Metals Section, Nagoya Institute of Technology, Gokiso-cho, Showa-ku, Nagoya, 466 Japan

(Received 11 November 1985; in revised form 19 May 1986)

Abstract-The phase diagram for the Fe-rich Fe-Al-Co ordering system has been determined by observing phase separation by means of electron microscopy and magnetization-measurements. Two types of phase separation, into B2 and B2* (Al-rich B2 and Co-rich B2*) and into $\alpha$-Fe (A2) and B2, exist. The A2 + B2 phase separation occurs in a band shaped region connecting Fe-22-25 at.% Al with Fe-25-30 at.% Co. The B2 + B2* separation is localized in a wide island shaped region, which centres approximately at 25 at.% Al and 25 at.% Co. This island has a steep slope with temperature on the Co side. The microstructure of a B2 + B2* mixture is finely periodic along the $\langle 100\rangle$ directions, i.e. a so-called [100] modulated structure. The B2 + B2* phase separation has been calculated thermodynamically by taking into account the ferro-magnetic and ordering excess free energies. The magnetic effect accounts for the shape of the B2 + B2* phase separation island.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Previous investigations of the phase decompositions of super saturated solid solutions have been per-formed mainly for alloy-systems having a positive interaction energy between nearest neighbour atoms. Recently, however, phase decompositions in ordering solid solutions have experimentally been found for several alloys such as Cu-Mn-Al [1], Cu-Zn [2, 3], Fe-Al [4-9] and Fe-Si [10-13]. A theoretical model has been proposed by taking into account inter-atomic interactions between not only nearest neigh-bours but also second and higher order neighbours [14-19]. In the theoretical model, even if a first neighbour interaction energy is negative, phase de-compositions are expected to occur when second and higher neighbour interaction energies are positive.

For an Fe-Al binary alloy, a typical ordering sys-tem, two types of phase separations, A2 + B2(FeAl) and $A 2+DO_{3}(Fe_{3} Al)$ , have been found experiment-ally and theoretically by Allen and Cahn [5, 20], Swann *et al*. [6, 7] and Oki *et al*. [8, 9]. Furthermore, Hasaka and Ikeda [21] predicted recently the co-existence of a B2 phase with a $DO_{3}$ phase in the composition range of over 30 at.% Al in this system. As described above, the phase separation in the Fe-Al binary system is considered to have been almost completely understood now.

The Fe-Co alloy is also a typical ordering system. The phase separation of this system has not been observed micrographically because of the weak intensity of super lattice electron reflections. However, because the B2 super structure (FeCo) has been experimentally confirmed by means by neutron diffraction, it is expected that the Fe-Co system has a coexistent region of A2 + B2 phases.

In contrast to the abundance of studies in Fe-Al and Fe-Co, those in the Fe-Al-Co ternary system are scarce. The phase diagram for the Fe-Al-Co ternary alloy was reported by Iwanov [22], but his phase diagram definitely contains some uncertainties; for instance, the $\alpha$-Fe disordered region is continuously connected to the B2 ordered region without any phase boundary. Age hardening in this system was reported by Abson and Whiteman [23], but the cause of the hardening was not traced. Consequently, the phase separation and phase diagram of this system remain still unknown.

The present study, utilizing both experiment and theory, has been conducted to resolve this unsatisfactory situation in the Fe-Al-Co ternary system. One may find a new phase reaction which does not exist in Fe-Al and Fe-Co. With a full understanding of the phase reaction and diagram, one should be able to utilize Fe-Al-Co alloys as practical materials.

In the present paper, we first show experimental results on microstructural changes with ageing for the Fe-rich Fe-Al-Co ternary system and the phase diagrams consistent with these results will be given. Then, calculation of the phase diagram based on thermodynamics including the magnetic and ordering excess free energies will be shown. This will explain the topological feature of the experimentally determined diagram.

## 2. SPECIMENS AND EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES

Many Fe-Al-Co alloys were prepared from 99.98 at.% Fe, 98.6 at.% Co and 99.99 at.% Al in a vacuum induction furnace. The compositions of the alloys are given in Fig. 1. These alloys were homogenized through direct quenching from the melt by means of a so-called single roller technique instead of a usual solution-treatment, because some alloys were too brittle to be forged even at high temperatures. The ribbon specimens, thus produced, were about $20\ \mu$m in thickness. Most of the specimens were aged mainly at temperatures ranging from 723 to 1023 K for suitable duration in vacuum. Structural changes with ageing were examined by means of transmission electron microscopy (JEM 200A operated at 200 kV) and thermo-magnetization measurement.

Thin foils for TEM were prepared by electropolishing in an electrolyte of
$$\mathrm{HClO}_{4}: \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}=1: 8 \quad \text{or} \quad =1: 9$$
at room temperature. Most electron microscopic observations were made with an electron beam parallel to [011]. Under this condition, 100 and $\frac{111}{222}$ super reflections were simultaneously observed.

Since the 100 spot is induced due to Both B2 and $\mathrm{DO}_{3}$ ordered phases while the $\frac{111}{222}$ spot comes only from a $\mathrm{DO}_{3}$ ordered phase, these two ordered phases were discriminated by the comparison of a 100 dark field image with a $\frac{111}{222}$ dark field image. A modulated structure in a bright field image, which was sometimes produced in aged alloys, was observed under the condition of $g=200$.

Measurement of the Curie-point was performed to obtain the tie-lines of phase separation. The Curie-temperatures of liquid quenched solid solutions and aged alloys were measured using a magnetic balance. If the changes in the Curie-temperature of solid solutions with composition are known, the tie-line of phase separation can be approximately determined. The heating rate employed for this measurement was so fast (about 10 K/s) that decomposition of a solid solution or a change in microstructure during the magnetic measurement was almost negligible.

### (2) Magnetic measurements
It is inferred from the microscopic observations that the two main kinds of phase separation, i.e. A2 + B2 and B2 + B2* exist in this alloy system. However, it is difficult to distinguish them only by TEM observation. In order to solve this difficulty we measured changes in the Curie-temperatures of the phases produced by decomposition in various alloys. It is well known that the Curie-temperature of Fe varies with the addition of an alloying element. Therefore, if the Curie-temperature $T_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of many solid solutions having various compositions are experimentally known, the compositional directions of phase separation, i.e. the tie-lines are approximately determined by comparing $T_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathrm{c}}$ before and after ageing.

Figure 7 represents the Curie-temperatures experimentally obtained for liquid quenched alloys. The contour map of equi-$T_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathrm{c}}$ was drawn in accordance with the $T_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathrm{c}}$ values obtained for the ternary alloys and those of Fe-Al and Fe-Co binary alloys reported previously [26]. It is clearly seen from the contour map that the Curie-temperature, having a summit at about Fe-50Co, decreases with increasing Al-content. (Note a drastic decrease in the vicinity of the composition-range of 20-30 at.% Al.)

## 4. CALCULATION OF B2 + B2* PHASE SEPARATION

### (a) Theoretical basis

The Fe-Al-Co system contains two ferro-magnetic elements, Fe and Co, and consists of three ordering binary systems, i.e. Fe-Al, Fe-Co and Al-Co. Therefore, the excess free energies caused by the ferro-magnetic effect $[\Delta G]_{ferro}$ and the ordering effect $[\Delta G]_{ord}$ must be taken into account in addition to the chemical free energy $[G]_{para}$. According to Nishizawa et al. [27] the total free energy $G$ is given by the sum of these energies.

$$
G=[G]_{\text {para }}+[\Delta G]_{\text {ferro }}+[\Delta G]_{\text {ord }}. \tag{1}
$$

The chemical free energy $[G]_{para}$ is usually given by

$$
[G]_{\text {para }}=R T \sum X_{i} \ln X_{i}+\sum \sum \Omega_{i j} X_{i} X_{j}, \tag{2}
$$

where $\Omega_{ij}$ is the atomic interaction parameter between the $i$th and $j$th elements, $X_{i}$ the molar concentration of the $i$th element, and $R$ and $T$ have their usual meanings.

The ferro-magnetic excess free energy of an Fe solid solution is given by equation (3) [26].
$$
\begin{gathered}
{[\Delta G]_{\text {ferro }}=\left(1-\sum m_{i} X_{i}\right)\left(T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}} /{ }^{\circ} T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}}\right)\left[\Delta^{\circ} G\left(T_{\mathrm{m}}^{*}\right)\right]_{\text {ferro }}} \\
T_{\mathrm{m}}^{*}=T \cdot\left({ }^{\circ} T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}} / T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}}\right),
\end{gathered}\qquad(3)
$$
where $m_{i}$ is the parameter to characterize the ferro-magnetic energy with the $i$th element. It is usually unit for a non-magnetic element and zero for a ferro-magnetic element. $^{\circ} T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}}$ is the Curie-temperature of pure Fe (1043 K), $T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}}$ the Curie-temperature of a ternary solid solution and $[\Delta^{\circ} G(T_{m}^{*})]_{ferro }$ the difference in free energy between the para-and ferro-magnetic ions at a fictitious temperature $T_{m}^{*}$. In the present work $T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}}$ was determined as in Fig. 7 and can be expressed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathrm{K})= & -\frac{1138 X_{\mathrm{co}}+370}{\left\{0.237+0.357\left[\left(X_{\mathrm{co}}-0.024\right)^{2}\right.\right.} \\
& \left.\left.+0.028^{2}\right]^{1 / 2}\right\} \\
& \times X_{\mathrm{Al}}^{2}+1138 X_{\mathrm{co}}+1043
\end{aligned}
$$

$[\Delta^{\circ} G(T_{m}^{*})]_{ferro }$ of pure Fe has been numerically given by Weiss and Tauer [28]. In order to calculate the miscibility gap one needs $[\Delta^{\circ} G(T)]_{ferro }$ which is expressed as a continuous function of the composition.

This can be achieved, using the numerical values of Weiss and Tauer, by the expression
$$
\begin{aligned}
& {\left[\Delta^{\circ} G(T)\right]_{\text {ferro }}(\mathrm{kJ} / \mathrm{mol})} \\
& \quad=9\left\{T-968.9-\left[(T-968.9)^{2}+28832\right]^{1 / 2}\right\} / 2 \quad(5)
\end{aligned}
$$

$[\Delta^{\circ} G(T_{m}^{*})]_{ferro }$ can be obtained by substituting $T_{m}^{*}$, instead of $T$, into $[\Delta^{\circ} G(T)]_{ferro }$.

The ordering excess free energy $[\Delta G]_{ord }$ is given as [27]
$$
\begin{gathered}
{[\Delta G]_{\text {ord }}=f\left(T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\circ} /{ }^{\circ} T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\circ}\right)\left[\Delta G\left(T_{o}^{*}\right)\right]_{\text {ord }}} \\
T_{o}^{*}=T \cdot\left({ }^{\circ} T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\circ} / T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\circ}\right),
\end{gathered}\qquad(6)
$$
where $^{\circ} T_{c}^{\circ}$ is the critical temperature of the order-disorder transformation of the stoichiometric B2 phase in Fe-Co (1003 K). $T_{c}^{\circ}$ is that of a ternary alloy and has theoretically been given by Murakami *et al.* [29] as
$$
\begin{gathered}
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\circ}(\mathrm{K})=(1 / R)\left[-\left(\Omega_{12} X_{1} X_{2}+\Omega_{23} X_{2} X_{3}+\Omega_{31} X_{3} X_{1}\right)+\sqrt{\left(\Omega_{12} X_{1} X_{2}+\Omega_{23} X_{2} X_{3}+\Omega_{31} X_{3} X_{1}\right)+L \cdot X_{1} X_{2} X_{3}}\right] \\
L=\Omega_{12}^{2}+\Omega_{23}^{2}+\Omega_{31}^{2}-2 \Omega_{12} \Omega_{23}-2 \Omega_{23} \Omega_{31}-2 \Omega_{31} \Omega_{12}.
\end{gathered}\qquad(7)
$$
$f$ is a multiplying factor to adjust the ordering energy of a binary system with the addition of a third element. On the basis of the theoretical treatment proposed by Nishizawa *et al.* [27], $f$ can be expressed for the Fe-Al-Co system as,
$$
f= \begin{cases}2 X_{\text {Co }} & \text { for } \quad X_{\text {Co }}<X_{\text {Fe }} \\ 2 X_{\text {Fe }} & \text { for } \quad X_{\text {Co }}>X_{\text {Fe }} \\ 2 X_{\text {Al }} & \text { for } \quad X_{\text {Al }}<1 / 2 \\ 2\left(1-X_{\text {Al }}\right) & \text { for } \quad X_{\text {Al }}>1 / 2\end{cases}\qquad(8)
$$

Equation (8) gives two separate values of $f$ but the larger $f$ should be used in the calculation.

$[\Delta^{\circ} G(T_{o}^{*})]_{ord }$ in equation (6) is an ordering excess free energy of the B2 super lattice at a fictitious temperature $T_{0}^{*}$ which can be evaluated with the Bragg-Williams model. ## 5. SUMMARY

Phase decompositions during ageing of Fe-rich Fe-Al-Co ordering alloys were investigated in detail to obtain the phase diagram by means of transmission electron microscopy and magnetization measurement. Further, the phase separation was thermodynamically calculated to help analyze the shape of the experimentally obtained diagram. Two types of phase separation, $\mathrm{B} 2+\mathrm{B} 2^{*}$ and $\mathrm{A} 2+\mathrm{B} 2$, are observed in the ternary system. The $\mathrm{A} 2+\mathrm{B} 2$ phase separation is observed in a fairly narrow composition range connecting $\mathrm{Fe}-22-25$ at. $\% \mathrm{Al}$ with $\mathrm{Fe}-25-30$ at. $\%$ Co. The coexistent region of $\mathrm{B} 2+\mathrm{B} 2^{*}$ (Al-rich B2 and Co-rich B2*) was localized, like an island, in a wide composition range over about 15 at. $\% \mathrm{Al}$ and 10 at. $\%$ Co. The microstructure of a $\mathrm{B} 2+\mathrm{B} 2^{*}$ mixture is finely periodic along the $\langle 100\rangle$ directions, i.e. a so-called [100] modulated structure. Such a $\mathrm{B} 2+\mathrm{B} 2^{*}$ region is also found in the phase diagram calculated thermodynamically by taking the ferro-magnetic and ordering effects into account. The calculation indicates that $\mathrm{B} 2+\mathrm{B} 2^{*}$ phase separation is not caused by the ordering excess free energy but by a drastic change in the magnetic excess free energy due to the Al addition.
