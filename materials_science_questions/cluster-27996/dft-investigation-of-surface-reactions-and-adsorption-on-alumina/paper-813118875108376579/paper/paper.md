### Abstract

The electronic properties and structural stability of germanane nanosheet is studied using density functional theory method. The stability of germanane sheet is confirmed with formation energy. The band gap opens upon hydrogenation of germanene sheet, which is used as a base material for the detection of alcohol/aldehyde molecules. The adsorption of alcohol/aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet is studied using charge transfer, density of states spectrum, energy gap, adsorption energy, electron density and average energy gap variation. The adsorption of alcohol/aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet is explored in atomistic level. The adsorption of alcohol is found to be more favorable than aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet. The findings suggest that germanane nanosheet can be used as chemiresistor for detection of alcohol/aldehyde vapors in the atmosphere.

**Keywords:** GERMANANE; NANOSHEET; ADSORPTION; ALCOHOL; ALDEHYDE; STABILITY

### 1. Introduction

The monolayer of two-dimensional materials attracted the researchers due to its tunable electronic properties. Among the well-known graphene, other group-IV two-dimensional materials are in focus among the scientific community such as silicene and germanene [1]. Moreover, germanene holds promising properties owing to its compatibility with silicon-based microelectronic devices and spin-orbital coupling effects [2]. The previous reports reveal that monolayer germanene were synthesized on Au (111), Pt (111) and Al (111) substrates [3-5]. Besides, germanene has nearly zero-band gap semimetal, which restricts its applications in electronics. Hence, researchers concentrate on opening the band gap of germanene, such as surface adsorption with alkali metals and applying vertical electrical field [6]. In addition, the other way to open the band gap is by surface modification and functionalization of germanene. The synthesis method with Ca de-intercalation of $CaGe_2$ layered compound [7] results in the formation of stable sheets of germanane, in which the germanium atoms are attached to hydrogen atoms in up and down directions. Even though, graphene, silicene and germanene belong to group-IV of the periodic table, the chemical and electronic properties differ due to their orbital hybridization. Moreover, the opening of the band gap in germanane leads to the application of germanane as base material for the possible application as chemical nanosensor. Carbon in graphene sheets easily forms $sp^2$ hybridization, whereas silicene and germanene form $sp^3$ hybridization. The formation of $sp^3$ hybridization in silicene and germanene is attributed to the fact that the energy required for $s$ and $p$ orbitals hybridization are much smaller than graphene counterpart [8]. Namrata Dhar et al [9] reported about tuning the magnetic, optical and electronic properties of germanene sheet with adatoms, arsenic and gallium. E. Gkogkosi et al [10] studied about stacks of graphene with silicane and germanane using first-principles study. The findings reveal that hybrid systems are stable against the defect formation caused by

hydrogen atoms from one layer to other layers. E. Bianco et al [7] synthesized germanane from topochemical deintercalation of $CaGe_2$. Y. Zhou [11] studied about modulating the band gap of germanane nanoribbons for application in quantum well devices. Jun Zhao and Hui Zeng [12] studied the electronic, optical and transport properties of germanane nanoribbons with defects using density functional theory (DFT). Jianmei Yuan et al [13] reported about the new hybrid phases of germanene with alkali metal atoms using first-principles study. Pin Xiao et al [14] reported about tuning the electronic properties of half and fully-hydrogenated germanene using first-principles study. The authors concluded that the chlorine and hydroxyl may tune the electronic properties of half and fully hydrogenated germanene. From the previous reports, the state-of-the-art reveals that the electronic properties can be tuned with different ways, which can be used as a chemical nanosensor. Moreover, the previous literatures of germanane show that not many works have been reported on germanane as chemical sensor base material. This reason motivated the present work to study the electronic properties of germanane base material and its adsorption behavior towards alcohol and aldehyde vapors are studied and reported.

### 2. Computational details

The first-principles study on the electronic properties of germanane nanosheet is carried out by density functional theory (DFT) method implemented with SIESTA package [15]. The geometric relaxation of germanane nanosheet was carried out through generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in combination with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange correlation function in SIESTA package [16, 17]. Furthermore, the adsorption of alcohol and aldehyde on germanane nanosheet is studied through SIESTA package. Jun Zhao and Hui Zeng [12] studied the structural, optical and transport studies of germanane nanoribbons and the influence of defects is also reported. The GGA/PBE exchange correlation functional is used in the reported work, which validates and strengthens the present work in the choice of electronic exchange

correlation potential. The adsorption of alcohol and aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet is studied in terms of the band structure and density of states (DOS) spectrum through SIESTA package with Monkhorst-Pack k-point meshes of $10^{-3}$ $\AA^{-1}$. The plane wave basis set is an optimum choice for the adsorption of alcohol and aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet. While studying the adsorption studies on germanane base material, the energy cut-off of plane-wave basis set was adjusted to 500 eV with the energy convergence of $10^{-5}$ eV. The Brillouin zones of germanane nanosheet are sampled with 8 x 8 x 1 k-points. The atomic positions of germanane base material were optimized until the force precision of 0.03 eV/Å is achieved. The double zeta polarization (DZP) basis set has been utilized for relaxation of germanane nanosheet in the present study [18, 19]. The germanene nanosheet are subjected to periodic boundary condition with supercell geometry of 5 x 5 x 1 is used in the adsorption study. The charge transfer estimation is analyzed through Mulliken charge transfer [20, 21] between germanane nanosheet and alcohol/aldehyde molecules.

3. Results and discussion

3.1. Structure of germanane and stability of germanane nanosheet

The hydrogenated germanene (germanane) exhibits a buckled structure with anisotropic corrugation, (i.e) the neighboring row of germanium atoms are wrinkled alternatively along the zigzag directions, thus, each end of germanium atoms are passivated with hydrogen in the up and down directions forming the germanane nanostructure. The bond length of Ge-H and Ge-Ge is 1.56 Å and 2.47 Å respectively. The focus of the present work is to study the electronic properties of germanane nanosheet. The germanane nanosheet is used as the base material to detect the presence of alcohol and aldehyde molecules, which can be used as a chemical nanosensor. Fig. 1 represents the schematic diagram of germanene nanosheet with periodic boundary condition (PBC). The germanene nanosheet is hydrogenated to obtain germanane.

Moreover, at first the structural stability of germanane base material should be ascertained in order to study the adsorption properties. The structural stability of germanane nanosheet is studied with formation energy. The formation energy of germanane nanosheet is found to be -3.82 eV. The negative value of formation energy clearly confirms the stable structure of germanane nanosheet. The adsorption of formaldehyde on germanane is named as position A1. The positions A2 and A3 represent the adsorption of acetaldehyde and propionaldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet respectively. Fig. 2a-c illustrates the adsorption of formaldehyde, acetaldehyde and propionaldehyde on germanane nanosheet respectively. The adsorption of alcohol molecules namely methanol, ethanol and 1-propanol on germanane nanosheet are represented as B1, B2 and B3 positions respectively. Fig. 2d-f depicts the adsorption of methanol, ethanol and 1-propanol molecules on germanane nanosheet respectively. All the positions studied in the present work upon adsorption of alcohol and aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet are global minima. Only optimized positions of different molecules on germanane nanosheet with global minima are discussed. The energy gap of bare germanene sheet is found to be metallic, which is shown from Fig. 3a. The channels cross the Fermi energy level ($E_F$), which illustrate the metallic nature. In order to open the band gap, the germanene nanosheet should be hydrogenated, which opens the band gap. The band gap of germanane is observed to be 1.43 eV, which infers the semiconducting nature of the base material suitable for chemical sensor. Fig. 3b represents the band structure diagram of germanane nanosheet.

### 3.2. Adsorption properties of aldehyde and alcohol molecules on germanane nanosheet

In the beginning of the adsorption studies on germanane nanosheet, it should be confirmed that the base material should be semiconducting nature. The band gap of bare germanene nanosheet is observed to be metallic as illustrated in Fig. 3a. Moreover, from the results of the band structure the hydrogenated germanene gets opened and it is found to be

around 1.43 eV depicted in Fig. 3b. It is well known that the electronic properties depend on the kind of density functional used in the system for study. Thus, the band gap is estimated to study the electronic properties of germanane base material with a different basis set, namely PBE, BLYP, PBEsol, RPBE, revPBE. Moreover, the energy gap is found to be consistent with small variation in the energy gap from 1.43 eV to 1.95 eV. Table. 1 depicts energy gap calculation for germanane base material with different basis set with GGA functional. Since GGA/PBE density functional is appropriate exchange correlation functional for adsorption studies on nanostructures, GGA/PBE functional is used in the present work [12]. Fig. S1-S4 in the supplementary information illustrates the band structure of germanane base material for different exchange-correlation functional. The adsorption of aldehyde and alcohol molecules on germanane is studied using adsorption energy [22-24]. The adsorption energy ($E_{\text{ad}}$) of aldehyde and alcohol molecules on germanane nanosheet can be calculated using the following equation (1) as,

$$E_{\text{ad}} = \text{E(HGe/aldehyde-alcohol)} - \text{E(HGe)} - \text{E(aldehyde/alcohol)} + \text{E(BSSE)} \tag{1}$$

where E(HGe/aldehyde-alcohol) is the energy of germanane/aldehyde-alcohol complex, E(HGe) is the energy of isolated germanane nansheet; E(aldehyde/alcohol) is the energy of aldehyde or alcohol molecules and E(BSSE) is the basis set superposition error energy [25]. Table 2 represents the adsorption energy, Mulliken charge transfer ($Q$), atoms in molecules (AIM), energy gap and average energy gap variation of germanane nanosheet upon adsorption of aldehyde and alcohol molecules. Moreover, the adsorption energies are found to be in negative values, which confirm the adsorption of aldehyde or alcohol molecules on to the germanane base material. The adsorption of alcohol molecule on germanane nanosheet is found to be more favorable rather than aldehyde due to the higher magnitude of the adsorption energy compared

with the $E_{ad}$ value of aldehyde molecules. Among, the alcohol molecules, the methanol is observed to be strongly adsorbed than ethanol and 1-propanol molecules. The adsorption of alcohol molecules on germanane is prominent on germanane nanosheet may be due to the presence of the hydroxyl group.

Apart from the adsorption energy, the other important factor, which decide the adsorption behavior of alcohol/aldehyde on germanane sheet is Mulliken charge transfer ($Q$) and atoms in molecules (**AIM**). Moreover, AIM gives accurate transfer of charges between the gas/vapor and germanane base material. Furthermore, the trend of $Q$ and AIM charge transfer almost follows a same trend. The negative and positive values of charge transfer illustrate that the charge is transferred between the germanane nanosheet to target gas/vapor or target gas/vapor towards germanane nanosheet respectively [20, 26-28]. Besides, the other question may arise, why the charge transfer takes place upon adsorption of aldehyde/alcohol on germanane sheet? The variation in the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) between the target gas/vapor and germanane base material upon adsorption leads to the transfer of charge between the alcohol/aldehyde molecules and germanane nanosheet. Thus, the adsorption leads to transfer of electrons between vapor molecules and germanane nanosheet. This leads to the variation in the resistance of germanane nanosheet, which can be used as a simple two-probe device for the possible detection of volatile organic vapors (VOCs) on germanane base material. The results of charge transfer show that the adsorption of alcohol/aldehyde on germanane sheet shows the positive value of the Mulliken charge transfer ($Q$) and **AIM**. The positive value of $Q$ or **AIM** refers that charge is transferred from aldehyde/alcohol molecules to germanane sheet. Furthermore, the magnitude of $Q$ and **AIM** is observed to be more for alcohol molecules than aldehyde molecules. The variation

between the HOMO level and LUMO level refers the energy gap ($E_g$). The variation in the value of $E_g$ is found to be more for alcohol molecules upon adsorption on germanane sheet. The variation in $E_g$ should be compared with isolated counterpart of germanane and alcohol/aldehyde adsorbed germanane. Fig. 3c-e represents the band structure of germanane nanosheet upon adsorption of aldehyde molecules for positions A1, A2 and A3 respectively. The adsorption of aldehyde molecules gives rise to the variation in the band gap along the gamma point, which is found to be 1.39, 1.41 and 1.42 eV respectively for positions A1, A2 and A3. Likewise, the adsorption of alcohol molecules on germanane nanosheet leads to variation in the band gap, which is found to be 1.13, 1.04 and 1.02 eV for the positions B1, B2 and B3 respectively as shown in Fig. 4a-c. The orbital overlapping between the aldehyde/alcohol molecules with germanium atoms in germanane nanosheet results in the change in the band structure. Moreover, the density of states (DOS) spectrum gives the clear insights on the adsorption properties of alcohol/aldehyde molecules on germanane sheet [29-32]. Fig. 5a depicts the DOS spectrum of isolated germanene nanosheet, which shows a metallic nature. Fig. 5b represents the DOS spectrum of isolated germanane nanosheet. Furthermore, the band gap gets open upon hydrogenation of germanene nanosheet. The energy gap can be noticed along the Fermi energy ($E_F$). In order to calculate $E_F$, initially, the self-consistent calculation is carried out and obtained the Fermi level, out of that the band structure is calculated on the self-consistent density. Besides, the density of charge is found to be more in the virtual orbital in germanane nanosheet rather than the occupied orbitals. This is a favorable condition for the chemical nanosensor, since the charge transfer can take place easily between the germanane nanosheet and target gas/vapor. Fig. 5c-e represents the DOS spectrum for positions A1, A2 and A3 upon adsorption of aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet. From the DOS spectrum, it is clearly evident that the charge

transfer takes place from aldehyde molecules to germanane base material, which is inferred from the peak maxima in both the conduction band (virtual orbital) and the valence band (occupied orbital). Besides, Fig. 5f-h illustrates the DOS spectrum for positions B1, B2 and B3, the adsorption of alcohol molecules in the germanane base material. Further, in this case also the charge transfer takes place between the alcohol molecules to the germanane base material. The peak maxima are observed in different energy interval and near to the Fermi level, which facilitates the transfer of electrons between the target gas/vapor to germanane nanosheet. Moreover, the Mulliken charge transfer and DOS spectrum are in close agreement, which support the transfer of charge between the alcohol/aldehyde vapors with germanane nanosheet.

In order to further strengthen the charge transfer between aldehyde/alcohol and germanane base material, the electron density diagram is shown in the supplementary information (Fig. S5-S11). Upon adsorption of aldehyde/alcohol on germanane nanosheet (positions A1, A2, A3, B1, B2, B3) the density of electrons is found to be more on the adsorption site (the swirls along the adsorption site indicates the electron density), which further validates the transfer of electrons between target gas/vapor and germanane sheet. The other important parameter, which decides the adsorption of alcohol/aldehydes on germanane nanosheet is average energy gap variation ($\mathbf{E_g^{a}\%}$). The average energy gap variation upon adsorption of target gas/vapor on germanane should be compared with isolated germanane band gap [33-35]. Moreover, the results show that the variation in average energy gap is found to be significant for alcohol molecules than aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet. The variation of $\mathbf{E_g^{a}\%}$ is found to be high for alcohol molecules than aldehyde group. The variation in the band gap is drastic due to the adsorption of hydroxyl group on germanane nanosheet. Since in the hydroxyl group, the transfer of electrons from the hydrogen end to oxygen is more, which is attributed by the fact that the

polarization in hydroxyl group is more compared with the CHO group in aldehyde molecules. Thus, the transfer of electrons between alcohol and germanene is found to be more, which is clearly noticed from the charge transfer and DOS spectrum. Further, the adsorption energy for positions B1, B2 and B3 is slightly higher than aldehyde molecules due to the formation of dipole in the hydroxyl group.

The band gap of germanane nanosheets narrows upon adsorption of alcohol molecules onto germanane nanosheet due to the orbital overlapping between the hydroxyl group and Ge atoms in germanane nanosheet. The decrease in the band gap gives rise to transfer of more electrons from the alcohol molecules to germanane base material. Moreover, the presence of the additional $CH_2$ group in ethanol and 1-propanol leads to wide variation in the average energy gap compared with the adsorption of methanol molecules on germanane nanosheet. Thus, the variation in the resistance of germanane base material is in direct proportion to the concentration of alcohol/aldehyde molecules. Moreover, the conductivity of the germanane base material can be calculated from the well-known equation (2) as

$$
\sigma \alpha exp\left(\frac{-E_g}{2kT}\right) \tag{2}
$$

where $\sigma$ and k are the electrical conductivity and Boltzmann's constant respectively. A. A. Peyghan et al [36] and N. L. Hadipour et al [37] have studied the variation in the conductivity of upon adsorption of gas molecules in Ni- and Si-doped graphynes sheet and Al-doped ZnO nanoclusters respectively. Furthermore, if the band gap is small, it leads to the large conductivity in the germanane nanosheet, where the current can be measured with a simple two-probe device to estimate the concentration of vapors present in the atmosphere. Besides, the energy gap decreases drastically upon adsorption of alcohol vapors rather than aldehyde vapors on germanane nanosheet, which results in higher magnitude of current flowing through the

germanane molecular device. Using a two-probe device, the variation in the resistance can be easily measured, which can be used as a chemi-resistor element in the chemical nanosensor [38-40]. The findings of the present work show that germanane sheets can be used as a base material for the detection of alcohol molecules in the environment.

### 4. Conclusions
Using DFT method with GGA/PBE correlation functional, the electronic properties and adsorption behavior of alcohol/aldehyde molecules of germanane nanosheet is studied. The structural stability of germanane nanosheet is confirmed using formation energy. The band gap opens up with hydrogenation of germanane sheet. The energy gap of germanane sheet is found to be 1.43 eV. The germanane nanosheet is used as a base material for the adsorption of alcohol/aldehyde molecules. The adsorption behavior of alcohol/aldehyde on germanane nanosheet is discussed in terms of Mulliken charge transfer, density of states spectrum, electron density, energy gap, adsorption energy and average energy gap variation. The adsorption of alcohol and aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet is explored in atomistic level. The study confirms that the adsorption of the hydroxyl group is found to be more significant than the aldehyde group. The findings of the present work suggest that nanosheet of germanane can be used as a chemi-resistor, whose resistance varies upon adsorption of alcohol/aldehyde molecules.

### Research Highlights

- The electronic and structural stability of germanane nanosheets are studied.
- The band gap opens upon hydrogenation of germanene sheet, which can be used for chemical sensor.
- The charge transfer takes place upon adsorption of alcohol/aldehyde molecules on germanane sheets.
- The adsorption of alcohol is found to be more favorable than aldehyde molecules on germanane nanosheet.
- The findings show that germanane nanosheet can be used for detection of alcohol/aldehyde vapors.
