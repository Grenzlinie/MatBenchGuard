## Abstract

Local density functional theory within the framework of the Car-Parrinello method has been used to study the structural parameters and the energetics of offretite, when a $Si^{4+}$ ion is substituted by $(Al^{3+}, H^{+})$. The calculations have been performed on a periodically repeated unit cell with 54 atoms and a proton. In agreement with previous cluster calculations, we conclude that the sites with the lowest (Al, H)/Si substitution energies are also those with the largest proton affinity. In addition a correlation previously reported between acidity and Al-O-Si bond angles is confirmed.

## 1. Introduction

It is well known that the acidic properties of zeolites make them interesting compounds for catalytic reactions [1-4]. For this reason, many quantum chemical calculations have recently appeared, aimed at finding a dependence of the acidity on the local environment [5]. These investigations are usually based on cluster models of rather limited size. In this Letter we present calculations where a zeolite system, namely offretite, is modelled as a bulk crystal exhibiting the same periodicity as that of the aluminum-free structure.

Our work is based on local density functional (LDF) theory and is carried out within the framework of the Car-Parrinello method [6]. An important ingredient of the present study is the use of the ultrasoft pseudopotentials developed by Vanderbilt [7], which enable a strong reduction of the cutoff energy required to describe the pseudo wavefunctions of localized species like oxygen in a plane wave representation. Recently this approach has been applied to the study of a number of silicate polymorphs [8], which are materials with composition similar to zeolites. In the present work, this approach allows us to perform a full lattice relaxation without constraining some atoms at their experimental geometry, as is often the case in cluster calculations. In addition, it enables us to take into account long range effects which may play an important role, but which were often neglected or approximated by points charges in previous ab initio work.

Among the many possible types of zeolites we have focused on offretite whose aluminum free structure consists of a relatively small unit cell, with 18 silicon and 36 oxygen atoms. This feature makes offretite particularly attractive for a theoretical study, even though other zeolite polymorphs are more frequently used in many practical applications. Previous theoretical studies of offretite have attempted to rationalize its acidic properties by calculating the molecular electrostatic potential of a cluster with a geometry de-

## 2. Computational details

In the Car-Parrinello [6] scheme the atomic coordinates $\{R_{I}\}$ and the electronic functions $\{\psi_{i}\}$, corresponding to occupied states, are treated as dynamical degrees of freedom of a fictious classical system. Determination of the electronic ground state corresponding to a given ionic configuration is achieved via fictitious dynamics for $\{\psi_{i}\}$, with driving forces obtained from the total energy functional within the LDF theory. Similarly, structural optimizations are performed via a simultaneous minimization of the atomic positions and electronic wavefunctions, in such a way as to ensure that the electronic subsystem is always in the ground state pertaining to the instantaneous atomic configuration.

Offretite comprises a cancrinite and a gmelinite cage with a hexagonal prism structure (Fig. 1). Calculations were performed for a system consisting of a periodically repeated hexagonal box with 36 oxygen, $18-n$ silicon, and $n$ aluminum atoms, where $n$ is either 0 (aluminum-free offretite) or 1. In the latter case one hydrogen counterion can also be present. For Al-free offretite our hexagonal box coincides with the unit cell of the crystal $(a=b=13.291$ Å, $c=7.582$ Å [10]). This cell has two distinct tetrahedral (T) sites, namely $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$, which can accommodate either $Si^{4+}$ or $Al^{3+}$ ions. For the structure containing only silicon, there are six non-equivalent oxygens. When the $T_{1}$ site is aluminated, the four oxygens bound to it are non-equivalent, while in the case of a $T_{2}$ site this number is three, as $O_{4}$ and $O_{7}$ are equivalent by symmetry.

The valence electronic wavefunctions are expanded in a plane wave basis set and they have been calculated only at the $\Gamma$ point of the Brillouin zone. For Si and Al the electron-core interaction was described by Bachelet et al. norm-conserving pseudopotentials [11] in a Kleinman and Bylander form [12], whereas the pseudopotential for oxygen was generated according to the scheme proposed by Vanderbilt, as explained in Ref. [13]. The parametrization of Perdew-Zunger [14] has been used for the functional of local exchange-correlation. All calculations were performed with an energy cutoff of 16 Ry for the wavefunctions, leading to a basis set of 8444 plane waves per molecular orbital. With this cutoff we found the bond distances and the stretching frequencies of small molecules such as $O_{2}$, SiO and AlO to be reproduced with a maximum error of 2% and 6% respectively. A preconditioned algorithm [15] has enabled us to increase the time step in the search of the electronic solution. The atom positions were relaxed according to a steepest descent algorithm until residual forces were less than 0.0006 au, except for the aluminated-free form where the calculation was stopped at residual forces of 0.0014 au.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Aluminum-free structure

The calculated structural parameters of offretite with silicon in all its T sites are presented in Table 1, together with the X-ray crystallographic data [10], which actually refer to a structure with an Al/(Al+Si) ratio of about 0.3, the counterions being alkali metals. As the experimental data are not able to differentiate between Si and Al, the bond lengths presented are an average over the actual SiO and AlO distances weighted by the aluminum distribution over the lattice sites. The values reported in Table 1 correspond only to a pair of different sites. Among all calculated SiO distances, about one third (in accordance with the Al/(Al+Si) ratio for the crystallographic structure) are significantly shorter than the experimental ones. In addition Table 1 shows that the sites with the largest bond length deviations from experiment also exhibit the largest error on the TOT valence angle. For example $T_{1}O_{2}$ is calculated with a significantly smaller bond length $(-0.075\ \mathring{A})$ and bond angle $(-6.4^{\circ})$. However, some of the experimental distances appear to be possibly too large for a SiO distance, and the significant difference with other SiO bond lengths, which are all around 1.62-1.65 $\mathring{A}$, seems unlikely for a zeolite. We thus suggest that these distances refer actually to AlO bonds. This conclusion is also based on our experience indicating that bond length errors in LDF calculations for sp-bonded materials, such as zeolites, should not exceed a few percent.

### 3.2. $Al^{3+}/Si^{4+}$ substitution

We next considered the replacement of $Si^{4+}$ by an $Al^{3+}$ ion. As our method uses periodic boundary conditions, a positive charge, uniformly delocalized throughout the unit cell, was added in order to avoid convergence problems associated with infinite negatively charged systems. The results are given in Tables 2 (structural parameters) and 3 (energies) for an $Al^{3+}$ ion substituting a $Si^{4+}$ ion in both $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$. These two cases are denoted as $Al(T_{1})$ and $Al(T_{2})$ respectively. Table 3 shows that $Al(T_{2})$ is lower in energy than $Al(T_{1})$ by 3.8 kcal/mol. An analysis of the structure indicates an increase in the bond lengths of the aluminated T site by 0.08-0.1 $\mathring{A}$. In both cases the four AlO bond lengths are nearly equal, whereas one AlOSi bond angle is larger than the other three by about $15^{\circ}$ for $T_{1}$ and $25^{\circ}$ for $T_{2}$.

The calculated proton affinities (Table 3) show that the proton site on $O_5$ is the most acidic whereas that on $O_3$ it is the most basic, the difference being 12.6 kcal/mol. On the other hand, the difference between the smallest and the largest substitution energy is 8.7 kcal/mol. Substitution energies and proton affinities clearly exhibit a close relationship. In particular an ordering of the sites by increasing the substitution energies gives the same sequence as that derived by using decreasing basicities. This means that the more stable an aluminum-proton substitution is, the more basic the site is. This correlation was also pointed out in Ref. [16]. We also remark that $T_1O_3\text{H}$ and $T_2O_7\text{H}$ have comparable substitution energies, suggesting equivalent stabilities for (Al, H) at either $T_1$ or $T_2$. p_{i j}=P_{i j} / p_{\text {tot }}, \quad(1)

The results (Table 3) indicate that at room temperature the probability of having a proton on an oxygen is zero on all sites, except on $\mathrm{O}_{3}(35 \%), \mathrm{O}_{6}(5 \%)$ and $\mathrm{O}_{7}$ $(30 \%)$. Actually, since the proton will most likely bind to the oxygen where the affinity is the largest,our calculations show that $\mathrm{O}_{3}\left(\mathrm{~T}_{1}\right)$ and $\mathrm{O}_{7} / \mathrm{O}_{4}\left(\mathrm{~T}_{2}\right)$ will be the protonated oxygens. The proton oxygen binding energy for $\mathrm{O}_{3}$ being larger by about 4 $\mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$ than for $\mathrm{O}_{7}$, this implies that the $\mathrm{T}_{2}$ site is more acidic than $\mathrm{T}_{1}$, which is in agreement with experiment. Proton occupancies show that $\mathrm{T}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{5} \mathrm{H}$ is not occupied, and thus this site does not play any significant role.

In Table 4 the structural parameters of the protonated sites are listed. All OH bond lengths are nearly equal and exhibit typical values for such a bond. In all cases there are three AlO bonds with similar values and a longer one (by about $0.2 \AA$ ) which corresponds to the protonated oxygen. A comparison with the non-protonated aluminated T site indicates that only this bond is affected by the counterion. These long $\mathrm{AlO}$ bond lengths range from $1.834\left(\mathrm{O}_{4}\right)$ to $1.885 \AA\left(\mathrm{O}_{6}\right)$, but no clear correlation with the acidity emerges. Instead, we note that $\mathrm{O}_{5}$ is involved in the largest AlOSi and the smallest AlOH angles while for $\mathrm{O}_{3}$ the opposite is true. As these are respectively the most and the least acidic sites, our calculations confirm the trends found previously in an EH study of offretite [9] and a MNDO of zeolite ZSM-5 [16]. In the latter study, the position of the proton was characterized as either sitting in a pore pocket or within the framework. The former refers to the large pore characteristic of all zeolites, while the latter concerns protons trapped inside the zeolitic framework which are generally inaccessible to incoming molecules. According to this definition, of the two most populated sites, $\mathrm{T}_{1} \mathrm{O}_{3} \mathrm{H}$ corresponds to a framework site and $\mathrm{T}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{7} \mathrm{H}$ to a pore pocket. Since $\mathrm{T}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{7} \mathrm{H}$ is more acidic than $\mathrm{T}_{1} \mathrm{O}_{3} \mathrm{H}$, we conclude that the more acidic site is also more accessible to molecules.

## 4. Conclusions
In this Letter we have presented a first principles LDF study of the structure and acidic properties of offretite. Our approach uses a periodically repeated unit cell rather than a cluster model, and the size of the system that we are able to treat is quite large with respect to the typical sizes of other ab initio investigations. Moreover in our calculation full relaxation for all atomic positions is allowed for. An important result of this work is that there is a strict correlation between the (Al, H)/Si substitution energy and the proton affinity of a given site, the most stable sites

being those with the largest proton affinity. Concern- ing the relationship between acidity and structure, the most acidic sites appear to have AlOSi and AlOH bond angles which are 'large' and 'small' respectively. Both these results agree well with the findings of a recent semi-empirical cluster calculation of zeolite ZSM-5 [16]. Finally, our results show that in offre- tite the $T_{2}$ site is more acidic than $T_{1}$ , in agreement with experiment [5,18].
