### ABSTRACT

The influence of the liquid environment on the reaction mechanism in slurry reactors has not been fully investigated. In this work, dimethyl ether (DME) synthesis from methanol dehydration over $\gamma-Al_2O_3$ (1 1 0) and AlOOH (1 0 0) in liquid paraffin and in the gas phase is studied using density functional theory combined with the conductor-like solvent model. The liquid paraffin can influence surface relaxation, Mulliken charges, and adsorptive behavior of $\gamma-Al_2O_3$ and AlOOH. For DME synthesis, liquid paraffin does not influence the reaction path over the $\gamma-Al_2O_3$ surface, but influences its activation energy. Liquid paraffin influences the activation energy and also alters the reaction path of DME synthesis over AlOOH

## 1. Introduction

The slurry bed, also called the three-phase bed, is applicable in several areas, such as hydrocracking or hydrodesulphurization of residual oils and natural gas, treatments of aerobic and anaerobic waste, and synthesis of methanol and dimethyl ether (DME) from syngas [1-4]. This applicability is attributed to its advantages over the conventional gas-solid processes, namely, low gas recycling ratio, no diffusion limitations, low pressure drop over the reactor, and caloric transfer [5,6]. Therefore, the slurry reactor has attracted more attention. However, no studies on how the liquid phase in a slurry reactor influences properties and phenomena at the microscopic scale, such as adsorption and reaction.

The catalysts currently used in a slurry reactor are prepared by dispersing the traditional solid catalysts in an organic medium such as liquid paraffin, in which the catalyst is easily deactivated. In view of the concept that the formation and growth of catalyst materials are accomplished during interaction with its surroundings, a novel preparation method named complete liquid-phase technology is suggested. The main innovation underlying this method is in the preparation of the catalysts directly from the solution of the slurry. This novel method has been applied to prepare catalysts for the direct synthesis of DME from syngas in a slurry reactor [5,6].

DME can be produced by methanol dehydration over a solid acid catalyst or a one-step synthesis from syngas over a bifunctional catalyst, such as $\text{CuZn}/\gamma-Al_2O_3$ [7,8]. In our previous works, AlOOH, and not $\gamma-Al_2O_3$, was found to be the active site for methanol dehydration [6].

The use of AlOOH as a dehydrating reagent for methanol has not been reported. Therefore, the AlOOH catalysts prepared by complete liquid-phase technology were studied [9,10]. It is found that the activity of AlOOH catalyst do not clearly change with the different reactive temperature, and does not find any signs of inactivation in the long-term experiment of changing temperature and the constant operating conditions of 22 d [10]. It shows the good prospects for its breaking the drawback of the poor stability of the traditional solid catalysts.

In the paper, we will first study how the solvent effects influence the $\gamma-Al_2O_3$ and AlOOH surfaces, and then will compare the difference of methanol dehydration over $\gamma-Al_2O_3$ (1 1 0) or AlOOH (1 0 0) in liquid paraffin and gas phase, and finally we will compare the different of methanol dehydration on $\gamma-Al_2O_3$ and boehmite in liquid paraffin.

First, the linear synchronous transit (LST) maximisation was performed, followed by an energy minimisation in the directions leading to the reaction pathway. The approximated TS were used to perform quadratic synchronous transit (QST) maximisation. From that point, another conjugate gradient minimisation was performed. The cycle was repeated until a stationary point was located [14].

To simulate the solvent effects, the conductor-like solvent model (COSMO) implemented into Dmol³ was used [15]. COSMO is a continuum solvent model where the solute molecule forms a cavity within the dielectric continuum of permittivity that represents the solvent [16,17]. The charge distribution of the solute polarises the dielectric medium. The response of the dielectric medium is described by the generation of screening (or polarisation) charges on the cavity surface. Liquid paraffin, which is always used as the organic medium in the slurry reactor, served as the substitute for the liquid phase. The dielectric constant of liquid paraffin is 2.06. COSMO was not used in the gas phase.

To describe the interaction between adsorbates and the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ $(hkl)$ or AlOOH $(hkl)$ surfaces, the adsorption energy $(E_{\text{ads}})$ was defined as [14]: $E_{\text{ads}} = E(\text{adsorbate/slab}) - [E(\text{adsorbate}) + E(\text{slab})]$, where $E(\text{adsorbate/slab})$, $E(\text{adsorbate})$, and $E(\text{slab})$ is the total energy of the slab with the adsorbate on the surface, of the free adsorbate, and of the slab surface, respectively. A negative $E_{\text{ads}}$ value signifies exothermic adsorption, and a positive $E_{\text{ads}}$ value indicates endothermic adsorption. For reaction energy $(\Delta H)$ like A+B=C+D, was calculated as [14]: $\Delta H = [E(\text{C/slab}) + E(\text{D/slab})] - [E(\text{A/slab}) + E(\text{B/slab})]$, where $E(\text{C/slab})$ and $E(\text{D/slab})$, $E(\text{A/slab})$, and $E(\text{B/slab})$ are the total energies for the slab with the products and reactants on the surface, respectively. A negative $\Delta H$ value signifies an exothermic reaction, and a positive $\Delta H$ value indicates an endothermic reaction.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ and AlOOH structure

The precise crystallographic structure of $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ is still under debate. The $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ structural models based on the defective spinel model and non-spinel model have been proposed [18–21]. The defective spinel model is deduced from the existence of a spinel cubic cell, typical of MgAl₂O₄. Although the defective spinel structure is commonly used to describe the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ structure [18,19], the latest theoretical and experimental studies do not confirm it [20,21]. Thus, the non-spinel model of $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ was chosen for this study (Fig. 1).

Boehmit (AlOOH) has been studied using various methods. This material exhibits a layered structure (Fig. 1) with an orthorhombic symmetry ($Cmc2_1$ space group) [22,23]. The layers are composed of chains formed by pairs of AlOOH molecules which extend along the $a$-axis. The aluminium atoms are surrounded by four oxygens and two hydroxyl groups. The hydrogen atoms are between the double layers, generating zigzag chains of hydrogen bonds along the $z$-axis. All chains are oriented parallel to the $x$-axis.

The optimised parameters for the bulk structure of $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ and AlOOH are shown in Table 1. The optimised parameters for the bulk structure agree with the experimental and theoretical results. A slight overestimation of all parameters is due to the use of the exchange-correlation function which generally increases the lattice parameters by about 4% for the GGA-PW91 [27].

### 3.2. The surfaces in gas phase and in liquid paraffin

In this case, the surface properties of AlOOH and $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ influenced by the liquid phase should be described precisely since it governs their interactions with reactants. Thus, in this section, the surface relaxation of AlOOH and $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ in the gas phase and liquid paraffin is studied.

Previous experimental and theoretical studies that examined the AlOOH and $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ surface have established that the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 1 0) and (1 0 0) surfaces and the AlOOH (1 0 0), (0 1 0), and (0 0 1) surfaces are preferentially exposed [21,23]. Therefore, those surfaces of $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ and AlOOH surface are considered here.

From the optimised AlOOH or $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ bulk structure, a 2-dimensional slab model (finite in the $z$-direction and periodic in the $x$- and $y$-directions) is built. Four-layered models are used in the study. The results are then validated using models with more layers. In all of the calculations, AlOOH or $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ in the top three layers are allowed to relax, while those in the other layers are fixed by keeping the volume constant (for four-, five-, and six-layered models). The computed structural and energetic parameters of the four-layered models are compared against the five-, and six-layered counterparts. The differences are <0.001 Å for bond lengths and <0.01 eV for surface energies. The surface areas, vacuum thickness, number of atoms, and the K-point mesh of the oriented planes are given in Table 2.

The side view of the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 1 0) and (1 0 0) surfaces is provided in Fig. 2. The $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 1 0) surface exhibits two kinds of unsaturated aluminium surface sites, namely, sites with 75% 4-coordinate aluminium atoms and sites with 25% 3-coordinate aluminium atoms. The Al1 and Al2 atoms are 4-coordinate but have different chemical environments, and Al3 is 3-coordinate. The O1 and O2 atoms are 3-coordinate, and the O3 and O4 atoms are 2-coordinate. However, the O1 and O2 atoms, and the O3 and O4 atoms differ in chemical environments.

In the case of the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 0 0) surface, Al4 is 4-coordinate and in a position below the surface plane. Therefore, it is not available for adsorption. The Al1–Al3 atoms are 5-coordinate, but each atom has a distinct chemical environment. The O1 atoms are 4-coordinate, and the O2, O3, and O4 atoms are 3-coordinate, with different chemical environments.

The relaxation of the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 1 0) surface in the gas phase is shifted outwards of the O1–O4 atoms, however, the Al1–Al3 atoms are shifted inwards. ### 3.3. Adsorption in the gas and liquid phases

From the above results, the liquid medium has a larger influence on the surface properties of $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}$ and AlOOH. In this section, $CH_{3}OH$, $H_{2}O$, and DME adsorption on $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(hkl)$ and $AlOOH(hkl)$ surfaces are calculated. According to the coordination of the atoms of the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(hkl)$ and $AlOOH(hkl)$ surfaces, the unsaturated Al and O atoms comprise the Lewis acid and base sites, respectively. Many studies indicate that DME synthesis from methanol occurs over the Lewis acid sites [21,23,28]. Thus, in this study, only adsorption on Al sites is considered.

The adsorption energies of adsorbates on the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(110)$ and (1 0 0) surfaces in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin are listed in Table 5. Adsorption of reactants and products on $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(110)$ and (1 0 0) surfaces is exothermic. This indicates that adsorption of these species on the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}$ surfaces is thermodynamically favourable. The greater is the magnitude of the associated exothermic adsorption energies, the more stable are systems represented by the adsorption models. The adsorbates preferentially interact with Al3 and Al1 of the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(110)$ and (1 0 0) surfaces, respectively. Stronger Lewis acidity favours adsorption (Table 5). This finding is analogous to the experimental results of the interactions of $H_{2}O$, CO, and other adsorbates with different modifications of alumina [21,28,31,32]. These results suggest that alumina, with 3-coordinate Al sites, has a higher Lewis acidity than that with 4- and 5-coordinate Al. The adsorption energies in liquid paraffin are less negative than those in the gas phase. This trend shows that liquid paraffin can reduce adsorption on the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(110)$ and (1 0 0) surfaces.

Table 6 shows the adsorption energies on the $AlOOH(hkl)$ surfaces. Adsorption of $CH_{3}OH$, $H_{2}O$, and DME on the $AlOOH(hkl)$ surface in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin is exothermic. However, it is found that the adsorption energies of AlOOH (0 1 0) do not change with the change of adsorbates, and the adsorptive ability of the (0 1 0) surface is weak, in the range of -0.12 to -0.14 eV. The reason is that the Al sites on this surface are fully saturated, and therefore can not adsorb. However, the adsorption energies are negative due to the hydrogen bonding between (0 1 0) surface and adsorbates. The result renders the (0 1 0) surface highly unreactive. The adsorption energies for the (1 0 0) surface are less negative than those for the (0 0 1) surface. These indicate that strong Lewis acidity favours adsorption. The adsorption energies in liquid paraffin are less negative than those in the gas phase. This indicates that liquid paraffin destabilises adsorbates on the AlOOH (1 0 0) and (0 0 1) surfaces. However, the adsorption energies for the $AlOOH(hkl)$ surfaces are less negative than those for the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(hkl)$ surfaces. This indicates that the adsorptive ability of $AlOOH(hkl)$ surface is weaker than that of the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(hkl)$ surface.

From these results, solvent effects reduce the ability of DME, $CH_{3}OH$, and $H_{2}O$ to adsorb on the AlOOH (1 0 0) and (0 0 1) and the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(hkl)$ surfaces. The Al Mulliken charges of these surfaces increase and their Lewis acidities decrease due to solvent effects. As a result, the adsorptive ability of the Al sites also decreases.

Solvent effects have greater influence on the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(110)$ surface than on the (1 0 0) surface. Neutron diffraction analysis [33] shows that the (1 10 ) surface comprises $\sim 83\%$ of the total surface area, whereas the (1 00 ) surface comprises $\sim 17\%$. Although the AlOOH (0 1 0) covers $\sim 61\%$ of the total surface area [23], this surface is highly unreactive. As previously established, the influence of liquid paraffin on the (1 0 0) surface is slightly larger than that of on the (0 0 1) surface. Therefore, for the sake of simplicity on DME synthesis, only the $\gamma-Al_{2}O_{3}(110)$ and AlOOH (1 0 0) surfaces are considered in this study.

Their kinetic measurements are consistent with the bimolecular Langmuir-Hinshelwood (LH) mechanism. Jain and Pillai [36] proposed that the dehydration of methanol occurs between two simultaneously adsorbed methanol species. Schiffino and Merrill [37] examined DME synthesis from CH₃OH over $\gamma$-Al₂O₃. Zeolites and $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ have different surface properties. The catalytic activity of alumina for CH₃OH dehydration is associated with the Lewis acid–Lewis base pairs formed during surface dehydration. In contrast, the catalytic activity of the acidic zeolites is related to the Brønsted acid–Lewis base pair. Therefore, the reaction pathways of DME synthesis proposed by Jain and Pillai [36] and Schiffino and Merrill [37] are discussed here.

The first path (path I) is the non-dissociative adsorption of CH₃OH on the surface [36]. The simultaneously adsorbed CH₃OH molecules approach over the surface. Finally, a DME molecule is formed. In step II, the initially adsorbed CH₃OH reacts with a surface oxide and dissociates [37], forming a CH₃O group and a hydroxyl group. Subsequently, this CH₃O group reacts with adsorbed CH₃OH to yield DME. The third path (path III) is similar to the second, except that it proceeds with the simultaneous adsorption of two CH₃O groups [37].

$$
\begin{align}
\mathrm{CH_3OH(g)} &\rightarrow \mathrm{CH_3OH*} \\
2\mathrm{CH_3OH*} &\rightarrow \mathrm{DME*} + \mathrm{H_2O*}
\tag{I}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\mathrm{CH_3OH*} + \mathrm{O*} &\rightarrow \mathrm{CH_3O*} + \mathrm{OH*} \\
\mathrm{CH_3OH*} + \mathrm{CH_3O*} &\rightarrow \mathrm{DME*} + \mathrm{OH*}
\tag{II}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
2\mathrm{CH_3OH*} + 2\mathrm{O*} &\rightarrow 2\mathrm{CH_3O*} + 2\mathrm{OH*} \\
2\mathrm{CH_3O*} &\rightarrow \mathrm{DME*} + \mathrm{O*}
\tag{III}
\end{align}
$$

Here, "*" represents a surface Lewis acid site and "X*" stands for the adsorbate bonded to this site.

The sums of adsorption energies when the two CH₃OH molecules adsorb separately on the same sites in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin are −2.51 and −2.10 eV, respectively (Table 5). The magnitudes of the co-adsorption energies are larger than those of the separate energies, indicating the slightly repulsive interaction between the two co-adsorbed CH₃OH molecules. However, the difference between the co-adsorbed energies and the sum energies in liquid paraffin is smaller than between those in the gas phase. This indicates that liquid paraffin can reduce the repulsive interaction between the two co-adsorbed CH₃OH molecules.

As shown in Fig. 4, path I, the activation energies of DME synthesis from the two adsorbed CH₃OH molecules on the (1 1 0) surfaces are 1.49 and 1.26 eV in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin, respectively. In paths II and III, dehydrogenation on the Al3 site of the (1 1 0) surface forms CH₃O and H groups in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin, with activation energies of 0.27 and 0.05 eV, respectively. When CH₃OH dehydrogenates on the Al1 and Al2 sites in the gas phase, the activation energies are 0.49 and 0.37 eV, respectively; in liquid paraffin, these are 0.30 and 0.23 eV, respectively. These indicate that the dissociative adsorption of methanol is an easy process, and is easier in liquid paraffin than that in the gas phase. Thereafter, CH₃O group reacts with adsorbed CH₃OH or with another adsorbed CH₃O group. In path II, the activation energies of DME synthesis from adsorbed CH₃O group and CH₃OH on the (1 1 0) surface is 0.73 eV in the gas phase and 0.39 eV in liquid paraffin. In path III, the activation energies of DME synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃O groups in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin are 1.58 and 1.23 eV, respectively.

Comparing the activation energies of CH₃OH dehydrogenation and DME synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃OH molecules, the process of CH₃OH dehydrogenation is energetically compatible with the DME synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃OH molecules. This is due to the lower activation barrier of CH₃OH dehydrogenation (lower by 1.22 and 1.21 eV in gas and in paraffin, respectively) and the higher stability (0.72 and 0.51 eV, in gas and in paraffin, respectively). Thus, CH₃OH undergoes dissociative adsorption on the (1 1 0) surface in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin. Although the activation energies of DME synthesis from adsorbed CH₃O group and CH₃OH in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin (0.73 and 0.39 eV, respectively) are obviously lower than those of synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃O groups (1.58 and 1.23 eV, respectively), a small amount of CH₃OH is present on the (1 1 0) surface due to CH₃OH is dissociative adsorption. When two CH₃OH molecules co-adsorb on the AlOOH (1 0 0) surface in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin, the co-adsorbed energy at the acidic site is −1.68 and −1.43 eV, respectively. The two CH₃OH molecules adsorb on the same sites in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin. The sums of the adsorption energies in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin are −1.84 and −1.52 eV, respectively (Table 6). The magnitudes of the co-adsorbed energies are also larger than those of the separate energies, indicating the slightly repulsive interaction between the two co-adsorbed CH₃OH molecules. However, the difference between the co-adsorbed energies and energies of separate CH₃OH adsorption in liquid paraffin is smaller than that in the gas phase. Hence, liquid paraffin can reduce the repulsive interaction between the two co-adsorbed CH₃OH molecules. The repulsive interaction of the CH₃OH molecules co-adsorbed on AlOOH (1 0 0) surface is slightly smaller than that of the same molecules on the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 1 0) surface.

The activation energies of path I on the (1 0 0) surface in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin are 0.91 and 0.71 eV, respectively, (Fig. 5). In paths II and III, dehydrogenation of adsorbed CH₃OH initially forms CH₃O and H in liquid paraffin and in the gas phase (activation energies are 1.08 and 1.05 eV, respectively). Subsequently, CH₃O group reacts with adsorbed CH₃OH or another CH₃O group. In path II the activation energies of DME synthesis from adsorbed CH₃O group and CH₃OH on the (1 0 0) surface in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin are 0.81 and 0.85 eV, respectively. In path III, the activation energies of DME synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃O groups on the (1 0 0) surface in the gas phase and in liquid paraffin are 1.49 and 1.45 eV, respectively.

From the activation energies of the three paths, the stability of binding of CH₃OH dissociative adsorption (−0.07 eV) is slightly higher than that of path I (0.07 eV) in liquid paraffin. However, the activation energy of CH₃OH dehydrogenation (1.08 eV) is clearly higher than that of DME synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃OH molecules (0.71 eV). These results suggest that DME synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃OH molecules is preferred over the production of CH₃O group in liquid paraffin. Thus, in liquid paraffin, CH₃OH adsorption is non-dissociative on the (1 0 0) surface. Path I is preferred over paths II and III. The activation energy of methanol dehydrogenation is 1.05 eV in the gas phase. This is slightly higher than that of DME synthesis from two adsorbed CH₃OH molecules (0.91 eV). However, the small difference in activation energy could not fully exclude the existence of CH₃O group. In other words, CH₃OH adsorption may be both dissociative and non-dissociative on the (1 0 0) surface in the gas phase. Thereafter, CH₃O group reacts with adsorbed CH₃OH or another CH₃O group. DME synthesis from CH₃O group and CH₃OH is preferred over that from two CH₃O groups. This is due to the lower activation barrier of the former process (lower by 0.68 eV), and the higher stability (higher by 1.36 eV) in the gas phase. Thus, DME synthesis in the gas phase involves adsorbed CH₃OH reacting with another adsorbed CH₃OH or CH₃O group. The results also indicate that liquid paraffin can influence the activation energy and alter the reaction path of DME synthesis over the AlOOH surface.

The activation energies of DME synthesis over the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 1 0) and AlOOH (1 0 0) surfaces in liquid paraffin are also compared. DME synthesis over $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ (1 1 0) mainly occurs by the reaction of two adsorbed CH₃O groups (1.23 eV). However, DME over AlOOH (1 0 0) is formed by reaction of two CH₃OH molecules (0.71 eV). Therefore, the reaction mechanisms of DME synthesis over the $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ and AlOOH surfaces are different. The activation energy of DME synthesis over $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ is higher than that over AlOOH. As a result, the reaction temperature of DME synthesis over $\gamma$-Al₂O₃ is higher than that over AlOOH. This result is consistent with the experimental result [10]. Therefore, AlOOH is a promising methanol dehydration catalyst for the STD process based on the CuZuAl methanol synthesis catalyst. Blaszkowski and van Santen [39] found that the activation energies of CH₃OH dehydrogenation over zeolites is 2.23 eV, and that DME is formed by two adsorbed CH₃OH molecules without formation of an intermediate methoxy species. The reaction mechanism of DME synthesis over AlOOH in this study is the same as that mediated by zeolites.

## 4. Conclusions
Theoretical studies are employed to investigate DME synthe- sis from methanol in the gas phase and in the liquid paraffin. When DME is synthesised by methanol dehydration over γ-Al₂O₃ or AlOOH surfaces, the paraffin not only reduces the surface relax- ation of γ-Al₂O₃ and AlOOH, it also influences Mulliken charges of those surfaces. The Lewis acidity decreases due to the increase in the Mulliken charge of Al. Thus, liquid paraffin reduces the abil- ity of CH₃OH, H₂O, and DME to adsorb on the γ-Al₂O₃ and AlOOH surfaces.

In the gas phase, CH₃OH undergoes dissociative adsorption on the γ-Al₂O₃ surface. In this phase, DME is formed by reaction of two CH₃O groups. CH₃OH adsorption on AlOOH is both non-dissociative and dissociative. On this surface, DME synthesis mainly occurs by the reaction of adsorbed CH₃OH with another adsorbed CH₃OH or with CH₃O group. Although liquid paraffin does not influence the reaction path of DME synthesis over γ-Al₂O₃, it can reduce the acti- vation energy of this reaction. Methanol adsorbs non-dissociatively on the AlOOH surface in liquid paraffin. DME synthesis over AlOOH mainly occurs through simultaneous adsorption of two CH₃OH molecules, and liquid paraffin can alter its reaction path.
