# A theoretical study on the conformations and energetics on the cation-$\pi$ interaction between monovalent ions ($\text{M}^+ = \text{Li}^+$, $\text{Na}^+$, and $\text{K}^+$) and anthracene and phenanthrene molecules

S. Ikuta

The Graduate School of Engineering, Tokyo Metropolitan University, 1-1 Minami-Ohsawa, Hachioji-shi, Tokyo 192-0397, Japan

Received 16 November 1999; received in revised form 23 December 1999; accepted 14 January 2000

## Abstract

The conformational structures of the cation-$\pi$ interaction between monovalent cations ($\text{M}^+ = \text{Li}^+$, $\text{Na}^+$, and $\text{K}^+$) and anthracene and phenanthrene molecules are surveyed at the hybrid density functional level of theory with the Becke's three parameters (B3LYP). The 6-31G$^*$ basis sets were used except in the case of potassium ion-complexes, where the 3-21G$^*$ basis sets were applied. Three stationary points (two isomers and one transition state) were found in the ion-anthracene complexes, but five stationary points (three isomers and two transition states) in the ion-phenanthrene complexes. The relative stability ladders were calculated at the B3LYP level of theory with the 6-311G(2d,p) basis sets except in the case of potassium, where the contracted [8s4p1d/14s9p1d] basis set was applied. The global minimum isomer (**Ib**: $C_s$) has the ion ($\text{Li}^+$ or $\text{Na}^+$) on the near center above the terminal $C_6$-ring of the anthracene plane, whereas the global minimum isomer (**Ia**: $D_{2v}$) has the $\text{K}^+$ ion just on the $D_{2h}$-axis above anthracene. The calculated binding energies are 43.5 ($\text{Li}^+$), 29.1 ($\text{Na}^+$), and 17.1 ($\text{K}^+$) kcal/mol, following the conventional electrostatic trend ($\text{Li}^+$, largest; $\text{K}^+$, smallest). The activation energies for the ion transfer ($\mathbf{Ib} \to \mathbf{Ia}$ both for $\text{Li}^+$ and $\text{Na}^+$ and $\mathbf{Ib} \leftarrow \mathbf{Ia}$ for $\text{K}^+$) also follow the same trend: 6.8 ($\text{Li}^+$), 2.2 ($\text{Na}^+$), and 0.7 ($\text{K}^+$) kcal/mol. The global minimum isomer of ion-phenanthrene complexes (**IIb**: $C_1$) has the ion ($\text{Li}^+$ and $\text{Na}^+$) on the near center above the terminal $C_6$-ring of phenanthrene plane. The calculated binding energy of ion-phenanthrene complexes agrees well with that of the corresponding ion-anthracene complex. The potential along the direction of $\text{K}^+$ ion transfer is really shallow, suggesting that the $\text{K}^+$ ion freely moves over the molecular plane. © 2000 Elsevier Science B.V. ## 1. Introduction

The possible role of the cation-$\pi$ interaction is very important in clarifying the selectivity of the shaker channel and related structures [1]. The stabilizing interaction between a cation and the molecules with no dipole moments is also of special interest in the chemical nature of bonding, where the charge-induced dipole and charge-quadrupole interactions dominantly exist [2,3].

Kumpf and Dougherty have evaluated the cation-$\pi$ interaction between monovalent ions $\text{Li}^+$, $\text{Na}^+$, $\text{K}^+$, and $\text{Rb}^+$ and benzene molecules in the gas phase and aqueous environment [1]. The Monte Carlo simulation using statistical perturbation theory was used to evaluate the solvation effect. They pointed out that in the gas phase the affinity for benzene follows the expected electrostatic trend, but that in aqueous environment, a reordering occurs such that the

Bauschlichter and Partridge [2] have theoretically studied the interaction between a $\text{Na}^{+}$ ion and benzene molecules at the MCPF level using the TZ2P basis set. They reported that the optimal structure for the $\text{Na}^{+}$–benzene complex has the $\text{Na}^{+}$ ion on the $\text{C}_{6}$ axis above the benzene plane, as this maximizes the charge–quadrupole interaction. Their calculated binding energy of 23.1 kcal/mol is 83% of the experimental value [3]. The second benzene binds to the rear side of $\text{Na}^{+}$–benzene with a binding energy that is only 2.9 kcal/mol less than that of the first.

Adamo and Barone [4] have carefully studied the long bond systems such as molecules and clusters containing intra- and intermolecular H-bonds by using both the sophisticated post-Hartree–Fock and density functional methods. They pointed out that a significant improvement for the activation barriers governing proton transfer is obtained in density functional method by introducing some Hartree–Fock exchange in gradient corrected function. The hybrid density functional methods [5] give results, which are competitive with the most sophisticated post-Hartree–Fock approaches [6].¹

The present authors [7] have recently studied the interaction between monovalent cations ($\text{M}^{+} = \text{Li}^{+}$, $\text{Na}^{+}$, and $\text{K}^{+}$) and naphthalene molecules. They reported that one global minimum has the ion on the near center above one of the two $\text{C}_{6}$-rings of the naphthalene plane and that the binding energies are 43.3 ($\text{Li}^{+}$), 28.3 ($\text{Na}^{+}$), and 17.5 ($\text{K}^{+}$) kcal/mol, following the conventional electrostatic trend ($\text{Li}^{+}$, largest; $\text{K}^{+}$, smallest). The activation energies for the ion transfer between two equal minima also follow the same trend: 6.9 ($\text{Li}^{+}$), 2.0 ($\text{Na}^{+}$), and 0.6 ($\text{K}^{+}$) kcal/mol. The most stable isomer of $\text{M}^{+}$(naphthalene)$_{2}$ is a ferrocene-type complex having $\text{C}_{2h}$ symmetry, where the two naphthalene molecules shift horizontally to minimize the repulsion of two planes. The second binding energies of $\text{Li}^{+}$(naphthalene)$_{2}$, $\text{Na}^{+}$(naphthalene)$_{2}$, and $\text{K}^{+}$(naphthalene)$_{2}$ are 23.5, 20.8, and 14.1 kcal/mol, respectively. The larger ligand–ligand repulsion in $\text{Li}^{+}$(naphthalene)$_{2}$ leads to a second naphthalene binding energy, that is about 20 kcal/mol lower than the first, which is more than five times the reduction found for $\text{K}^{+}$.

We also have recently observed the naphthalene and anthracene dimers in the zeolite cages by using a fluorescence spectroscopic technique, but have not clarified the exact structures and binding energies [8].

In this article, we investigate the conformational structures and binding energies in the cation–$\pi$ interaction between monovalent ions $\text{Li}^{+}$, $\text{Na}^{+}$, and $\text{K}^{+}$ and anthracene and phenanthrene molecules using the density functional level of theory [9,10] with the Becke's three parameters (B3LYP) [5,11]. Of special interest are these molecules, which have more stationary points. We clarify the characteristics of the stationary points and calculate the relative stability ladder among them. We also evaluate the $\text{K}^{+}$ ion movement over large unsaturated compounds.

### 2. Method of calculation

The conformational structures of anthracene and phenanthrene molecules and their complexes with the alkali cations ($\text{Li}^{+}$, $\text{Na}^{+}$, and $\text{K}^{+}$) were optimized at the density functional level of theory [9,10] with the Becke's three parameters (B3LYP) [5,11–14]. The 6-31G$^{*}$ basis sets [15,16] were used except in the case of $\text{K}^{+}$-complexes, where the 3-21G$^{*}$ basis sets [17–22] were applied. To characterize the stationary points and obtain zero-point energies, we performed the vibrational frequency analyses.

We calculated the final energies using the 6-311G(2d,p) basis sets [23–25] except in the case of potassium, where the contracted [8s4p1d/14s9p1d] [26] basis set was applied.

### 3.2. Ion-phenanthrene

We found five stationary points (**IIa-IIe**) for the complexes of ion ($\text{Li}^+$ and $\text{Na}^+$) and phenanthrene molecule at the B3LYP/6-31G* level of theory. The optimal structures are shown in Fig. 2, which are drawn by the use of the Chem3D software [27]. The pictures on the right-hand side are depicted from the top of the corresponding left-hand side drawings. Three of them, **IIa, IIb**, and **IId**, are isomers and the remaining two (**IIc** and **IIe**) are transition states.

The isomer **IIa** with $C_s$ symmetry has the ion on the near center above the middle $C_6$-ring of the phenanthrene plane. The isomer **IIb** has the ion on the near center above the terminal $C_6$-ring of phenanthrene plane. The isomer **IId** with $C_s$ symmetry has the ion on the near center above the bay of the phenanthrene frame. The conformer **IIc** corresponds to the transition state for the ion transfer between the **IIa** and **IIb** isomers, whereas the conformer **IIe** the transition state for the ion transfer between the **IIa** and **IId**.

Table 3 summarizes the total energies of phenanthrene and their complexes (**IIa-IIe**) with a cation ($\text{Li}^+$, $\text{Na}^+$, and $\text{K}^+$), calculated at the B3LYP level of theory with the 6-311G(2d,p) or [8s4p1d/14s9p1d (only for K)] basis sets. The optimal structures obtained at the B3LYP/6-31G* (3-21G* for $\text{K}^+$-complexes) level of theory were used in these energy calculations.

Table 4 summarizes the relative energy among five stationary points and also lists the binding energy of an ion ($\text{M}^+=\text{Li}^+$, $\text{Na}^+$, and $\text{K}^+$) that is bounded to a phenanthrene molecule.

The $\text{Li}^+$ - and $\text{Na}^+$ -phenanthrene complexes favor the conformer **IIb**. The isomer **IIb** of the $\text{Li}^+$ -phenanthrene is by 1.6 and 6.2 kcal/mol lower in energy than the remaining two isomers, **IIa** and **IId**, respectively. The activation energy for the $\text{Li}^+$ ion transfer from **IIb** to **IIa** is 7.2 kcal/mol and that for $\mathbf{IIa\rightarrow IId}$ is 5.6 kcal/mol.

The isomer **IIb** of $\text{Na}^+$ -phenanthrene is by 0.5 and 2.7 kcal/mol lower in energy than the remaining isomers, **IIa** and **IId**, respectively. The activation

energy for $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ ion transfer from $\mathbf{I I b}$ to $\mathbf{I I a}$ is 2.1 kcal/ mol. At the B3LYP/6-311G(2d,p) level of theory, the isomer $\mathbf{I I d}$ is slightly less stable than that of the tran- sition state $\mathbf{I I e}$, suggesting that the stationary point $\mathbf{I I d}$ may be just a shoulder in the potential along the direc- tion $(\mathbf{I I a} \rightarrow \mathbf{I I e} \rightarrow \mathbf{I I d})$ of the $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ ion transfer. The relative energies among the five stationary points in the $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$-phenanthrene are smaller than those in the $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$-phenanthrene, indicating that the potential along the direction of the $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ ion transfer is fairly shallow.

The zero-point energy differences among the five stationary points are within 0.55 and 0.25 kcal/mol in the $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$- and $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$-complexes, respectively, indicating negligible contributions to the above relative stability.

Due to the shallow potential along the direction of $\mathrm{K}^{+}$ ion transfer in the $\mathrm{K}^{+}$-phenathrene, only one stationary point, corresponding to the isomer $\mathbf{I I a}$, was obtained. The present results suggest that the $\mathrm{K}^{+}$ ion easily moves over the phenanthrene plane.

The binding energies of ion $\mathrm{Li}^{+}, \mathrm{Na}^{+}$, and $\mathrm{K}^{+}$ that are bound to a phenanthrene molecule are $43.4\left(\mathrm{Li}^{+}\right)$, $28.8\left(\mathrm{Na}^{+}\right)$, and $16.5\left(\mathrm{~K}^{+}\right) \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$, which agree well with those in the present ion-anthracene and previous ion-naphthalene [7] systems. The zero-point energy corrections $(1.7\left(\mathrm{Li}^{+}\right), 0.8\left(\mathrm{Na}^{+}\right)$, and $1.2\left(\mathrm{~K}^{+}\right) \mathrm{kcal} /$ mol) are included in the binding energies.

The present theoretical binding energies (29 kcal/ mol) of $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ ion to anthracene and phenanthrene molecules are by 3-4 kcal/mol larger than that of the $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$-benzene complex $[6]^{1}$ at the same level of theory. The present results give us a hint to consider both binding energies of monovalent cation $\left(\mathrm{Li}^{+}\right.$, $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$, and $\mathrm{K}^{+}$) that are bounded to quite large unsaturated molecule and ion selectivity in potassium channels.
