## ABSTRACT:
In this study, density functional theory (DFT/B3LYP) was used to assess a possible reaction pathway for the catalytic dissociation of $N_{2}O$. The active centers were taken to be mononuclear $[Ga]^{+}$ and $[Ga=O]^{+}$, and the surrounding portion of the zeolite was represented by a 3T cluster, namely $(AlSi_{2}O_{4}H_{8})$. The first step of $N_{2}O$ decomposition involves the formation of $[GaO]^{+}$ and the release of $N_{2}$. The metal-oxo species produced in this step then react with $N_{2}O$ again, to release $N_{2}$ and form $GaO_{2}$. The calculated activation energies for $N_{2}O$ dissociation in Ga-ZSM-5 and GaO-ZSM-5 at B3LYP/6-31+G* level are 22.2 and 24.9 kcal/mol, respectively. The calculated energy of the molecular oxygen elimination from 3T-(GaO₂) cluster is $\Delta H$ (298 K) = +46.5 kcal/mol and $\Delta G$ (298 K) = 35.9 kcal/mol.

## Introduction

Nitrous oxide $(N_{2}O)$ is a potent greenhouse gas and contributes to ozone layer depletion [1]. This has prompted the development of several abatement technologies based mainly on the catalytic decomposition [2]. Thermodynamically, $N_{2}O$ is an unstable endothermic molecule $(\Delta H_{f}^{\circ}=+19.5$ kcal/mol); however, kinetically, it is quite stable. Its thermal decomposition becomes noticeable only above $600\ ^{\circ}C$. $N_{2}O$ is relatively poor reactive but oxidizes certain low valence transition metals even at room temperature [3]. Transition metals cation-exchanged into ZSM-5 have been shown to catalyze the dissociation of nitrous oxide to oxygen and nitrogen [4-7]. At moderate temperatures, a charge donation from a catalytic site into the $N_{2}O$ antibonding molecular orbitals leads to decomposition, giving $N_{2}$ and a highly active form of adsorbed

Attempts to characterize the energies of $N_2O$ decomposition on Fe-ZSM-5 via quantum chemical calculations have been reported by several authors [10–12]. Yoshizawa et al. have proposed a model for the dissociation of nitrous oxide in Fe-ZSM-5 based on an isolated 3T-atom zeolite model [10]. Although they demonstrate that $N_2O$ decomposition could occur on such a site, their proposed reaction mechanism assumes that $O_2$ desorbs via the process, $Z^-[Fe(O)_2]^+ \rightarrow Z^-Fe^+ + O_2$ (g). This step seems unrealistic because the experimental evidence does not support the existence of $Fe^+$ cations in ZSM-5 [13]. Yakovlev et al. have also carried out quantum calculations at DFT level of $N_2O$ decomposition. In the first of their studies, the active sites was modeled as $M(OH)_3(H_2O)_2$ ($M = Fe, Co, Rh$) [11]. The energies of intermediates and transition states were computed and it was determined that Co and Rh sites are more active than Fe. In the second study, the energies of $N_2O$ decomposition were investigated for binuclear Fe complexes in Fe-ZSM-5 [12]. The zeolite was modeled by a pair of 5T rings sharing an edge and the Fe complex was represented as either $[(HO)FeOFe(OH)]^{2+}$ or $[FeOFe]^{2+}$. Although only stable states for active sites and intermediates along the reaction path were considered, the authors did show that oxo-metal cations could serve as active sites for $N_2O$ decomposition. Ryder et al. have proposed a model for the dissociation of nitrous oxide in Fe- and Co-ZSM-5 based on an isolated 5T-cluster zeolite model [14]. The active center was taken to be mononuclear $[FeO]^+$ or $[CoO]^+$, and the surrounding portion of the zeolite was represented by a 24-atom cluster. These authors showed that the first step of $N_2O$ decomposition involves the formation of $[FeO_2]^+$ or $[CoO_2]^+$ and the release of $N_2$. The metal-oxo species produced in this step then reacts with $N_2O$ again, to release $N_2$ and $O_2$. The calculated apparent activation energies for $N_2O$ dissociation at DFT level in Fe-ZSM-5 and Co-ZSM-5 are 39.4 and 34.6 kcal/mol, respectively. Although the calculated activation energy for Fe-ZSM-5 is in good agreement with that measured experimentally, the calculated activation energy for Co-ZSM-5 is higher than that reported experimentally.

Nitrous oxide is well-known effective oxidizer in heterogeneous catalysis. At temperatures above 673 K, it is active in oxidation of $[Ga]^+$ to $[Ga=O]^+$ in reduced Ga-ZSM-5 zeolites [15–17]. These findings have stimulated an interest in identifying the nature of the active site for $N_2O$ decomposition and the mechanism via which this reaction occurs. However, to the best of our knowledge, there are no reports of theoretical calculations about the nitrous oxide adsorption on gallium-exchanged zeolite ZSM-5. In this study, we report an investigation of the mechanisms of nitrous oxide dissociation in Ga-, and (Ga═O)-ZSM-5 using density functional theory (DFT). We consider exchanged Ga ion stabilization in a cation position of ZSM-5 zeolite similar to [14]. The catalytically active center and a portion of the zeolite framework are represented by a 3T-cluster. We proceed by postulating a mechanism for the dissociation process, computing the energies for each elementary step.

### Theoretical Methods

We present herein a density functional study DFT/B3LYP of the interaction of nitrous oxide with Ga/ZSM-5 and GaO/ZSM-5. The cation-exchange sites in ZSM-5 are represented by a variety of model clusters, including 10T-ring (T denoted tetrahedral Al and Si atoms), where there are single framework Al atom in ring containing 10T atoms. The largest cluster, $[AlSi_9O_{16}H_{20}]^- Ga^+$, is a complete 10-membered-ring picked out from the main channel of ZSM-5 zeolite and the terminal dangling bonds are satisfied by H atoms. As shown in Figure 1, the portion of the cluster describing the zeolite contains an Al atom in the T12 site of the framework surrounded by shells of O and Si atoms. The terminal Si-atoms are fixed in their crystallographic positions, as reported by Olson et al. [18]. Dangling bonds are terminated by H-atoms located $1.5 \ Å$ from each terminal Si atom oriented in the direction of the next T (tetrahedral) site. The anionic cluster is charge-compensated by a gallium cation, $[Ga]^+$, or gallium-oxo species, $[GaO]^+$, placed between two of the four O atoms surrounding the Al atom. For practical purpose, we employed a larger basis set only for the active site region, namely, the 6-31+G(d) basis set for $N_2O$ molecule and Ga atom; the 3-21G(d) basis set for Si, Al, atoms; and the 3–21G basis set for oxygen and hydrogen atoms.

To elucidate the decomposition of nitrous oxide on gallium site in ZSM-5 we will focus on calculations with full optimization of geometry at DFT/B3LYP/6-31+G(d) level using 3T cluster, namely, $AlSi_2O_4H_8$. This 3T-cluster is picked out from the

## Results and Discussion

The dissociation of N₂O at a single gallium center can be envisioned to proceed via the following sequence of steps:

$$
\mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}(\mathrm{g})+\mathrm{Z}^{-}[\mathrm{Ga}]^{+}=\mathrm{Z}^{-}\left[\mathrm{Ga}\left(\mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)\right]^{+} \tag{1}
$$

$$
\mathrm{Z}^{-}\left[\mathrm{Ga}\left(\mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)\right]^{+}=\mathrm{N}_{2}(\mathrm{g})+\mathrm{Z}^{-}[\mathrm{GaO}]^{+} \tag{2}
$$

$$
\mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}(\mathrm{g})+\mathrm{Z}^{-}[\mathrm{GaO}]^{+}=\mathrm{Z}^{-}\left[\mathrm{GaO}\left(\mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)\right]^{+} \tag{3}
$$

$$
\mathrm{Z}^{-}\left[\mathrm{GaO}\left(\mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)\right]^{+}=\mathrm{N}_{2}(\mathrm{g})+\mathrm{Z}^{-}\left[\mathrm{Ga}(\mathrm{O})_{2}\right]^{+} \tag{4}
$$

$$
\mathrm{Z}^{-}\left[\mathrm{Ga}(\mathrm{O})_{2}\right]^{+}=\mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{g})+\mathrm{Z}^{-}[\mathrm{Ga}]^{+} \tag{5}
$$

Reactions 1 and 3 involve the adsorption of gas-phase N₂O, assumed to be reversible, to the catalytically active site. Reactions 2 and 4 describe the dissociation of each of the adsorbed N₂O species to give two equivalents of gas-phase N₂ and two surface oxygen species. Reaction 5 represents desorption of O₂ molecules. The sum of these five steps [1-5] constitutes a complete catalytic cycle. In what follows, we present the calculations on the energetics for reactions 1 and 2 first, and then the calculations on the energetics for reactions 3 and 4. Desorption of O₂ via reaction 5 is examined separately. Recently, Ryder et al. described a similar model for the dissociation of nitrous oxide in Fe- and Co-ZSM-5 based on an isolated 5T-cluster zeolite model [14]. The anionic cluster was charge-compensated by a metal-oxo species, $[FeO]^+$ or $[CoO]^+$, placed between two of the four oxygen atoms surrounding the Al atom. In contrast to this work, we are interested predominantly in studies of nitrous oxide adsorption and decomposition over reduced gallium site Ga-ZSM-5. The energy of nitrous oxide

### ENERGETICS OF O₂ DESORPTION

The calculated energy of the oxygen desorption from 3T-GaO₂ cluster (Fig. 8) $\Delta H$ (298 K) = +46.5 kcal/mol and $\Delta G$ (298 K) = 35.9 kcal/mol is higher than the barriers of oxidation reactions. In accordance with DFT calculations a significant elongation of the O—O bond (1.631 Å) is observed in 3T-GaO₂ cluster. The calculated vibrational modes associated with O₂ in $3\text{T}^-[\text{Ga(O)}_2]^+$ are 579, 615, and 834 $\text{cm}^{-1}$. We attribute the frequencies at 579 and 615 $\text{cm}^{-1}$ to the bending modes of $[\text{Ga—(O)}_2]^+$. Peroxides on various metal oxides exhibit bands in the region of 640–970 $\text{cm}^{-1}$, whereas superoxide complexes exhibit bands in the range of 1,015–1,180 $\text{cm}^{-1}$ [26]. The vibrational frequency at 834 $\text{cm}^{-1}$ is assigned to vibrations of the O—O bond and fall in a range characteristic for peroxide species. Diatomic oxygen surface species have been observed in Fe-ZSM-5 by Gao et al. using in situ UV-Raman spectroscopy [27]. In the presence of gas-phase O₂, Gao et al. observed a distinct band at 730 $\text{cm}^{-1}$, which they assign to a peroxide anion, $\text{O}_2^{2-}$. Thus, we demonstrate that N₂O decomposition could occur on metal-oxo cations $[\text{GaO}]^+$ site and proposed the reaction mechanism assumes that O₂ desorbs via the process, $\text{Z}^-[\text{Ga(O)}_2]^+ \rightarrow \text{Z}^-\text{Ga}^+ + \text{O}_2$ (g). This step seems realistic because the experimental evidence supports the existence of $\text{Ga}^+$ cations in ZSM-5 [28]. The calculated energy of the molecular oxygen elimination from 3T-(GaO₂) cluster seems to be rather large and possible stimulating of this process by once more N₂O molecule decomposition on the site is very interesting [29].

### Conclusions

An analysis of the reaction pathway for N₂O decomposition on Ga-ZSM-5 suggests that metal $[\text{Ga}]^+$ and metal-oxo cations $[\text{GaO}]^+$ can serve as active centers. The active center $[\text{Ga}]^+$ by nitrous oxide is transformed into $[\text{GaO}]^+$. The active center oxo cations $[\text{GaO}]^+$ is transformed into $[\text{GaO}_2]^+$, a species that has characteristics similar to those of a metal-peroxo species. The calculated activation energy for N₂O decomposition is calculated to be 22.2 kcal/mol for Ga-ZSM-5, which agrees with experimental measurements. The calculated activation energy for GaO-ZSM-5 is 24.9 kcal/mol.
