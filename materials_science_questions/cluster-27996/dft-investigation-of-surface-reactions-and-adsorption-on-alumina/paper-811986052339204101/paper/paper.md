## ABSTRACT

Polymer-alumina nano-composites with enhanced dielectric properties as a possibility to enable the miniaturization of devices have been reported. The enhancement of dielectric properties was found to be unique to the polymer. In the present work, the mechanism of the dielectric enhancement is established by performing ab initio molecular orbital calculations in order to study the molecular interactions in the interfacial region between the alumina-nano-particle surface and the polymer medium. The calculations predict the existence of strong electrostatic attraction between the positive charge on the aluminium of the alumina clusters and the negative charge of the oxygens of the polymer at the polymer-nano-particle interface resulting in an increase in the dipole moment and the polarization of the system leading to enhanced dielectric properties. The oxygen thus plays a dual role by involving in covalent bonding with the polymer chain and electrostatic bonding interactions with the alumina nano-particles. The unique structure of the polymer provides the highly electronegative oxygens, as carbonyl groups or ether linkages in conjugation with aromatic rings in an extended polymer chain system, facilitating this type of bonding at the interface.

## 1. Introduction

Market research has shown that the size of electronic appliances is very crucial; the smaller the gadget, the more attractive it is to the customer. The quest to find materials with enhanced dielectric properties to enable the miniaturization of devices is enormous. Polymer based electronics is the pathway to flexible light weight devices. Siegel et al. [1] discovered that nano-particles like $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{TiO}_2$, $\text{SiO}_2$, $\text{BaTiO}_3$, etc., heterogeneously distributed within a polymer matrix can improve dielectric properties. Xu and Wong [2] have developed a low cost, high dielectric constant, polymer based composite; using self-passivated aluminium particles as the filler and polymers like polyimide, polynorbornene, etc., as matrices. It was found that the thin oxide layer ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) outside the metallic spheres had a dramatic influence on the electrical behaviour of the resulting composites. Zhang et al. [3] have investigated the dielectric properties of alumina-polymer nano-composites. They obtained fine dispersions of nanometre size aluminium oxide in two very different kinds of base polymers, a polar epoxy resin and a non-polar linear low-density polyethylene. Dielectric measurements showed very different behaviour for both the polymers. Musikhin and co-workers [4] have studied the luminescent properties of conjugated polymers embedded with nano-crystals of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$. It was found that based on the relationship between the polymer and the nano-crystal, the LUMO–HOMO gap varied. They explained their observations based on the change in the polarization component of the carrier and excitation energy in the vicinity of inclusions. Recently, polymer-alumina-nano-composites have become very popular due to their ability to enhance a number of physical properties [5–11]. The properties of the composites are dependent on the nature of the polymer used. Hence the need to tailor-make polymers suitable for specific purposes is growing.

Murugaraj and co-workers [12] have fabricated polymer-alumina nano-composites with enhanced dielectric characteristics. The polymer precursor used was the polyamic acid of benzophenone tetracarboxylic dianhydride and 4,4'-oxybisbenzenamine. No chemical explanation has so far been put forward to explain the observed dielectric enhancement. In this paper, the possible molecular interactions in the interfacial region between the alumina-nano-particle surface and the polymer medium are modelled by carrying out ab initio molecular orbital calculations in an attempt to explain the chemistry of the dielectric enhancement.

This polymer is unique in that it has several labile oxygens, either as carbonyl groups and thus not in the polymer chain or as ether linkages in conjugation with extended $\pi$ systems. These oxygens should interact with the nano-particles of $Al_2O_3$ in their vicinity. The details of these chemical interactions that produced the high dielectric constant interphase in these polymer-alumina systems have been investigated by performing ab initio molecular orbital calculations using Gaussian 03 program [13].

For this study, the structures of alumina clusters $Al_2O_3$, $Al_4O_6$ and $Al_8O_{12}$ were optimized at several levels of theory from the rel- atively simple Hartree-Fock (HF) method with split valence basis sets (HF/3-21G//HF/3-21G) to the higher density functional theory with polarized basis sets; including diffuse functions [B3LYP/6-31+G(d)// B3LYP/6-31+G(d)] (Fig. 1). While improved energies were obtained at higher levels of theory (Table 1), comparison of the geometrical parameters showed that the geometry is rather insensitive to the level of theory. Table 2 gives the geometrical parameters at the highest level of theory used in this work.

Theoretical modelling of different types of interfacial interac- tions that are possible between the polymer and the nano-particles has been performed. As a starting point, the structure of the oxide nano-particle and that of the repeat unit of the polymer medium were optimized at 3-21G level of theory using different methods like Hartree-Fock and Density Functional Theory (DFT). Different models emulating the interfacial interactions between the polymer and nano-particles were modelled. The minimum energy com- plexes indicative of the optimum geometries of the polymer- nano-particle interface showed the existence of strong electro- static attraction between the positive charge of the Al of the alu- mina nano-particles and the negative charge of the oxygens of the polymer. Calculations at several higher levels of theory were performed to quantify these interactions.

Fig. 2 shows the repeat unit of the polymer. There are three types of oxygens: (a) on the carbonyl group present in the five- membered ring labelled a and a', (b) on the carbonyl group present in the chain labelled b and (c) the ether oxygen labelled c.

Fig. 3 shows the optimized geometry of the complex formed by the interaction of the repeat unit of the polymer with 3 molecules of $Al_2O_3$ on a, b and c. To quantify the effect of these interactions, calculations were performed at higher levels of theory adding polarization functions and diffuse functions using truncated mod- els (Figs. 4-6).

Table 3 gives the stabilization energies for the interactions of the repeat unit of the polymer with $Al_2O_3$ in the various models at different levels of theory. The agreement of the values for the bigger and truncated models at the same level of theory shows that the truncated models are good representations of the bigger model [14,15]. The large stabilization energies for these complexes at all the levels of theory, equivalent to about one third of the stabiliza- tion energy of $Al_4O_6$ cluster, show the presence of strong bonding interactions between Al of the alumina nano-particles and the oxy- gens of the polymer, with the oxygen of the carbonyl group present in the five-membered ring showing the maximum interaction. This is to be expected as there is maximum extended conjugation of the $\pi$ bond for this oxygen. It was found that the presence of the phe- nyl group on the N of the five-membered ring was necessary on the truncated model to give a stable complex. The ether oxygen which has the lowest extended $\pi$ system showed the least interaction. It was found that the presence of the rings on either side of the oxy- gens (both b and c) played a vital role in the stability of the com- plexes formed and hence the truncated models incorporate these rings.

To establish the consistency of these calculations, the same large model of the repeat unit of the polymer was considered and calculations performed by adding $Al_2O_3$ to different oxygens (labelled a, a', b and c). The calculations were done at HF/3-21G// HF/3-21G of theory due to the large size of the models considered. The stabilization energies for the interactions and the stabilization energy per $Al_2O_3$ are given in Table 4. The stabilization energies were found to be quite additive showing that specific interactions have been addressed in all the models chosen (Figs. 3-9).

The calculated dipole moments are presented in Table 6. As the alumina clusters considered were symmetrical, they had no resultant dipole moment. However, the Al—O bonds being polar, any distortion in the symmetry of the structure should lead to a resultant dipole moment. The repeat unit of the polymer had a resultant dipole moment due to the presence of polar covalent bonds in an unsymmetrical structure. To study the effects of the interactions of alumina and the polymer, several models were created, with the repeat unit of the polymer interacting with one, two and three molecules of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ at the same time (Figs. 3–10). 3. Conclusion

Nano-composites of $Al_2O_3$ and polymer fabricated from the polyamic acid of benzophenone tetracarboxylic dianhydride and 4,4'-oxybisbenzenamine showed enhanced dielectric properties. The mechanism of the dielectric enhancement has been studied by theoretical modelling. Ab initio molecular orbital calculations performed at several levels of theory confirmed the existence of strong electrostatic attraction between the positive charge on the aluminium of the alumina clusters and the negative charge of the oxygens of the polymer at the polymer-nano-particle interface resulting in an increase in the dipole moment and the polarization of the system leading to enhanced dielectric properties.
