# Ab Initio Study of the Structures and Binding Energies of Aluminum Monocation Complexes

Scott F. Smith, Jayaraman Chandrasekhar,* and William L. Jorgensen*

Department of Chemistry, Purdue University, West Lafayette, Indiana 47907 (Received: August 9, 1982)

Complexes of 12 saturated and unsaturated O, N, and F bases with $\mathrm{Al}^{+}$have been investigated by using ab initio molecular orbital theory at the 6-31G*//3-21G level. Zero-point energy corrections for the complexation energies have been computed from vibrational frequencies obtained with the 3-21G basis set. The calculated interaction energies enable the relative binding energies from gas-phase experiments to be placed on an absolute scale. A value of $32 \pm 3\ \mathrm{kcal/mol}$ is found for the absolute $\mathrm{Al}^{+}$affinity of ethanol, the experimental reference molecule. The computed structures and energies are compared with those obtained previously at the same theoretical level for complexes of $\mathrm{H}^{+}, \mathrm{Li}^{+}$, and $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$. The $\mathrm{Al}^{+}$affinities are generally intermediate to lithium and sodium affinities, but show a more pronounced dependence on the donor atom of the base as well as on the presence of methyl substituents. The results are rationalized on the basis of both charge transfer and ion-dipolar interactions being significant in the $\mathrm{Al}^{+}$complexes. Population analyses indicate a $\sigma$ acceptor order of $\mathrm{H}^{+}>\mathrm{Al}^{+}>\mathrm{Li}^{+}>\mathrm{Na}^{+}$, and a $\pi$ acceptor order of $\mathrm{Li}^{+}>\mathrm{Al}^{+}>\mathrm{Na}^{+}$.

## Introduction
Ion-molecule interactions play a fundamental role in a variety of chemical and biological reactions. $^{1}$ A detailed understanding of these processes is facilitated by the availability of quantitative estimates of interaction energies. Measurements carried out in the absence of medium effects are particularly valuable for developing bonding models. Although many experimental studies have been performed in the gas phase, most investigations have concentrated on complexes of $\mathrm{H}^{+}$and alkali metal ions. $^{2-6}$

High level theoretical studies which can provide interaction energies of quantitative accuracy have also been restricted similarly. $^{7-12}$ Since many catalytic and organometallic reactions involve other metal ions, it is highly desirable to expand the scope of the gas-phase investigations.

In the present paper, we attempt to place the $Al^+$ affinities (AA's) on an absolute scale using ab initio molecular orbital theory. Although the larger bases studied experimentally are impractical for theoretical investigation, sufficient data are available on smaller systems to provide calibration.

In addition to providing absolute binding energies, the calculations also throw light on the nature of bonding in $Al^+$ complexes. Structures and interaction energies obtained previously for $H^+$, $Li^+$, and $Na^+$ complexes at the same theoretical level provide a uniform basis for comparison. $^{12}$ Mulliken population analyses carried out on the calculated wave functions additionally indicate the $\sigma$ acceptor and $\pi$ acceptor ability of $Al^+$ relative to $H^+$, $Li^+$, and $Na^+$.

### Computational Details
Calculations have been carried out on the complexes between $Al^+$ and the following bases: $NH_3$, $CH_3NH_2$, $H_2O$, $CH_3OH$, $CH_3CH_2OH$, $(CH_3)_2O$, $HF$, $CH_3F$, $HCN$, $CH_3CN$, $H_2CO$, and $CH_3CHO$. Geometry optimizations were performed by use of analytically evaluated gradients $^{18}$ with the split-valence 3-21G basis set. $^{19}$ These geometries were employed in additional calculations using the larger 6-31G* basis set, which includes d-type polarization functions on all non-hydrogen atoms. $^{20}$ This level of theory is designated 6-31G*//3-21G. To characterize the computed structures as true minima, we evaluated force constants with the 3-21G basis set. The second derivatives of the electronic energy with respect to nuclear coordinates were calculated by numerically differentiating the analytical first derivatives. These force constants were used to determine harmonic vibrational frequencies and associated zero-point energies. All calculations were carried out with the GAUSSIAN/82 series of programs $^{21}$ on a Harris Corp. H-80 computer.

### Results and Discussion
#### Complexation Energies.
The computed total energies, zero-point vibrational energies, and the interaction energies which include zero-point corrections are presented in Table I. The zero-point energies are always more positive for the complexes than for the free bases due to the three additional vibrational degrees of freedom in the former. The correction which lowers the complexation energy varies from 3.3 kcal/mol for the amines to 0.5-0.7 kcal/mol for the cyanides. These values are similar to those computed for $Li^+$ complexes $^{11b}$ but smaller than the corrections for protonation energies by a factor of $3.^{11a}$ This reflects the weakness of the bonds formed by $Li^+$ and $Al^+$ relative to $H^+$.

The computed complexation energies cannot be directly compared with experiment since absolute AA's are not known experimentally. However, the relative binding energies of four of the bases have been reported. $^{13}$ The experimental $Al^+$ binding energies follow the order $(CH_3)_2O$ > ethanol > $CH_3CN$ > $CH_3CHO$ with a total variation of only 2 kcal/mol. The computed values at the 3-21G level display a greater range; the complexation energy for $CH_3CN$ is nearly 8 kcal/mol less than that of $(CH_3)_2O$. Inclusion of d functions in the basis set provides a significant improvement. At the 6-31G* level, the complexation energies of the four bases are within 2.2 kcal/mol. Although the experimental trend is not reproduced, the discrepancy is not serious in view of the small energy differences involved. The 6-31G* basis set was also found adequate for the computation of proton, lithium, and sodium affinities. $^{11,12}$ Electron correlation is apparently unimportant for computing the interaction energies in these systems. $^{11}$

If we assume that the present calculations involve the same error bars as previous studies of ion-molecule interactions at the same theoretical level, an estimate of 32 $\pm$ 3 kcal/mol is obtained for the absolute complexation energy between $Al^+$ and the experimental reference molecule ethanol (Table I). With this value, the large number of relative binding energies measured experimentally can now be converted to absolute values. $^{13}$

The calculated AA's follow the order generally found in cation affinities, $^{2-6,11,12}$ amines > alcohols > fluorides which reflects the differences in the $\sigma$-donating abilities of saturated N, O, and F bases. Furthermore, the unsaturated nitrogen bases have slightly lower AA's than the amines, although methylamine and acetonitrile differ by only 1.5 kcal/mol (Table I). The sp hybridization at nitrogen makes the cyanides weaker $\sigma$ donors, but the higher dipole moment and the possibility of $\pi$ donation to the empty 3p orbitals on $Al^+$ compensates for this effect. The unsaturated oxygen bases, $H_2CO$ and $CH_3CHO$, actually have greater AA's than water and methanol, respectively.

A striking result from Table I is the large methyl substituent effect found for the AA's of both saturated and unsaturated bases. The geometries of $Al^+$ complexes optimized with the 3-21G basis set are shown in Table II. The symmetry labels of the optimized structures are also indicated. All the reported geometries were found to be true minima by verifying the presence of $3N - 6$ ($N$ is the number of atoms) positive eigenvalues in the force constant matrix in each case. The calculated distances involving $Al^+$ may be underestimated by up to $0.1$ Å, since the 3-21G basis overestimates the attraction between the ion and the base (Table I). The remaining geometrical parameters, however, are expected to be quite accurate.

A geometrical feature of principal interest is the orientation of the ion relative to the base.9,22-24 The ion aligns itself along the axis with maximum electron density, for example, along the nitrogen lone pair in the amine complexes. When more than one lone pair is present in the base, $Al^+$ generally prefers to lie on the molecular dipole axis. Thus, the coordination at the oxygen atom in the $Al^+$ complexes of water, methanol, ethanol, and dimethyl ether is planar. Similar structures are obtained at the 3-21G level for complexes of these bases with $H^+$, $Li^+$, and $Na^+$ as well.12 However, $H^+$ and the alkali metal ions have different orientational preferences toward the fluorine bases and the unsaturated oxygen bases.12,22-24 Protonated centers generally have a bent coordination, while $Li^+$ and $Na^+$ complexes prefer linear attachments.12 Like the alkali metal ions, $Al^+$ forms the more symmetric structures (Table II). The geometries of protonated complexes have been attributed to the importance of covalent interactions in these ions. The orientational preferences of $Li^+$ and $Na^+$, on the other hand, have been rationalized on the basis of the dominance of ion-dipole interactions.12 Such interactions seem to be important in $Al^+$ complexes also. Furthermore, another covalent interaction can also preferentially stabilize the higher symmetry forms.24 Donation of p type lone pair electrons to the empty p orbitals on $Li^+$, $Na^+$, and $Al^+$ would be maximized in structures similar to the $C_{2v}$ form of the $H_2CO\cdots Al^+$ complex shown in Table II.

Comparison of Bonding in Ion-Molecule Complexes.
The availability of a large number of ion-molecule interaction energies in the absence of medium effects provides the framework for developing general bonding principles regarding acid-base interactions. Extensive gas-phase experimental and theoretical studies on proton and lithium affinities have provided considerable insights into the nature of complexes of these ions. Although proton affinities (PA's) and lithium affinities (LA's) follow the same general trend, they are not linearly related. This is because protonation involves significantly greater charge transfer, while lithiated complexes are predominantly ion-dipolar in character. However, the alkali metal ions, $Li^{+}$ and $Na^{+}$ behave remarkably alike.¹² The sodium affinities (SA's) and LA's of a wide variety of neutral bases are strictly proportional.¹² The geometrical preferences are also identical.¹² In view of the dichotomy in the nature of $H^{+}$ and the alkali metal ions, it is interesting to analyze the behavior of $Al^{+}$ ion. Uppal and Staley did attempt to compare the available relative AA's with proton and lithium affinities.¹³ A closer correspondence to the former was noted, although more data were needed to establish the trend with respect to lithium affinities. The present calculations in conjunction with the previous study on PA's, LA's, and SA's at the identical theoretical level¹² provide the basis for deriving general conclusions regarding the nature of bonding in $Al^{+}$ complexes.

The calculated AA's are compared with 6-31G* LA's and SA's in Table III. Since zero-point energies have not been computed for SA's, the values of AA's in this table do not include these corrections for the sake of consistency. Figures 1 and 2 provide a graphical representation of the variation in AA's relative to PA's and LA's, respectively. Since LA's and SA's show a perfect linear correlation to each other, a plot of AA's vs. SA's would be similar to Figure 2 and therefore is not shown.

Figures 1 and 2 demonstrate that $Al^{+}$ interaction energies parallel neither PA's nor alkali metal ion affinities exactly, although there is a rough correspondence to both. Interestingly, major deviations from linearity arise from the methyl substituent effect (MSE) in both comparisons. While the PA's and AA's of saturated bases show a fairly linear dependence, the MSE on the AA's of unsaturated bases is much more pronounced than on the corresponding PA's (Figure 1). The situation is reversed in Figure 2; the LA's and AA's of unsaturated bases are well correlated, but the MSE on the AA's of saturated bases is disproportionately high. Methyl substitution does not increase the dipole moment of saturated bases.²⁵ Hence, the MSE would be small if the ion-molecular dipole term is the dominant interaction contributing to the complexation energy. Lithium and sodium affinities belong to this class.¹²,²⁶ If sufficient charge transfer occurs between the ion and the base, methyl substitution would be beneficial since the charges can be dispersed more effectively. Thus, PA's of alcohols and amines show a large MSE. A corresponding effect seen in the computed AA's points to the presence of significant electron transfer in the $Al^{+}$ complexes as well. This conclusion is in accord with the greater electronegativity of Al relative to the alkali metals, making it a better acceptor.

The increase of AA on going from $H_2CO$ to $CH_3CHO$ (7.3 kcal/mol) and from HCN to $CH_3CN$ (9.2 kcal/mol) in- dicate the importance of ion-dipolar interactions in $Al^+$ complexes. In this respect, $Al^+$ ion does not differ from the alkali metal ions.

In addition to the MSE, AA's display a strong depen- dence on the nature of the donor atom of the base. Thus, for the bases considered in this paper, the computed AA's span a range of nearly 23 kcal/mol, while the LA's remain within 17 kcal/mol, and the SA's vary even less, 13 kcal/mol. In particular, the SA's of $NH_3$, $H_2O$, and HF are 32.5, 28.2, and 20.7 kcal/mol, respectively, while the corresponding AA's are 32.8, 25.0, and 13.0 kcal/mol. These results again can be rationalized on the basis of greater charge transfer interaction in the $Al^+$ complexes, making $Al^+$ more sensitive to the $\sigma$-donating ability of the base.

To summarize, the variations in the interaction energies indicate that $Al^+$ complexes involve greater charge transfer than alkali metal ion complexes and also relatively greater ion-dipolar interaction than protonated molecules. These features are also revealed in the computed geometries. As discussed earlier, the ion-dipolar interaction causes the ion generally to align itself along the negative end of the dipole of the base, as in $Li^+$ and $Na^+$ complexes. The presence of charge transfer is seen in the bond length changes produced within the base on complexation to $Al^+$. The ion-molecule distances in the complexes also show an interesting variation. In Figure 3, the distances of the ion to the donor atom of the base in $Al^+$ and $Li^+$ complexes are compared. As expected from the interaction energies, there is considerable scatter in Figure 3. The interesting feature is the bond length changes in $Al^+$ complexes due to methyl substitution. For every base, the $Al^+$-base distance is shortened significantly by methyl substitution, while the corresponding changes are only modest in the lithium complexes. The reduction in bond length is of course accompanied by an increase in the complexation energy leading to the calculated MSE.

The bonding in the various ion-molecule complexes was also examined by performing Mulliken population analyses on the 3-21G wave functions for selected systems. (Table IV). In all cases except complexes of $NH_3$, the electron density in the $p_{\pi}$ orbital on the metal was also obtained to give $\sigma$ as well as $\pi$ electron transfer data. For $H_2O$ and $H_2CO$ complexes electron transfer to the in-plane $p_{\pi}'$ or- bital on the metal was also analyzed. As seen from Table IV, protonation involves substantial $\sigma$ electron transfer. $\sigma$ donation is also significant in the $Al^+$ complexes, and generally follows the order, $H^+ > Al^+ > Li^+ > Na^+$. $\pi$ donation does occur in all the metal ion complexes, but is much less in magnitude than $\sigma$ donation. The largest $\pi$ interactions are found in $Li^+$ complexes with the $Al^+$ complexes following close behind. $\pi$ donation in $Na^+$ complexes is only about half that in $Li^+$ complexes as indicated by the population data in Table IV. Not sur- prisingly, in the $H_2CO$ complex the in-plane lone pair on oxygen, which is the main component of the HOMO, functions better than the carbonyl $\pi$ orbital as an electron donor toward all three metal ions. $^{24}$ As discussed above, this interaction also would contribute to the preferred linear coordination around the carbonyl oxygen.

## Conclusions
Structure and interaction energies of 12 first-row bases with $Al^+$ have been obtained by use of ab initio molecular orbital theory. At the 6-31G* level including zero-point vibrational energy corrections, the $Al^+$ affinity of ethanol is 32.3 kcal/mol. This value may be used to obtain ab- solute values for the large number of relative AA's available experimentally.

Unlike proton affinities which are primarily governed by charge transfer interaction and lithium affinities which are dominated by ion-dipolar interaction, $Al^+$ affinities contain contributions from both types of interactions. As a result, AA's display a greater sensitivity to the nature of the base and to the presence of methyl substituents. The chemistry of $Al^+$ should reflect these differences.
