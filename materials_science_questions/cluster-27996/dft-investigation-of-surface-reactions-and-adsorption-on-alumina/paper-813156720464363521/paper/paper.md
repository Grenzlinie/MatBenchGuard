# Effect of fluorine substitution on structures and reactivity of Keggin-Al₁₃ in aqueous solution: an exploration of the fluorine substitution mechanism†

Xiaoyan Jin,* Hai Wu, Xueyue Jiang and Hong Zhang

The structures and reactivity of fluoridated Keggin-aluminum tridecamers (K-Al₁₃) in aqueous solution were studied using density functional theory (DFT) in order to explore the fluorine substitution process and the influence of $F^-$ on the chemical behavior of Keggin polynuclear Al species. The structures of fluoridated K-Al₁₃ were optimized with the consideration of both explicit and bulk solvent effects. The obtained optimization parameters indicate that the fluorine substitution on distinct sites results in the different structural changes. The consistency between the computational and experimental $^{19}$F and $^{27}$Al NMR chemical shifts validates the suitable computational method for the present clusters. According to the computed energy barriers of water exchange reactions, it was found that the reactivity of $\eta$-OH₂ decreases as a function of fluorine bridges. The appearance of fluorine bridges does not change the dissociative activated mechanism, but the larger dissociative trend was observed. Combining the present computational results with previous experimental observations, we reasonably put forward the fluorine substitution process and attempt to interpret the experimental phenomenon concerned at the molecular scale and further reveal the mechanism of $F^-$ promoting dissolution of mineral.

## Introduction

As a result of the abundance of the element aluminum in the Earth's crust and the multiple Al species produced by the hydrolysis-polymerization reactions in aqueous solution, the investigations on the aqueous aluminum species attract much attention due to their environmental and geoscientific relevance.¹⁻⁵ Among the aqueous Al species K-Al₁₃ is the most widely studied and is very intriguing by virtue of the similar structures and charge densities to aluminum (hydr)oxide mineral surfaces.⁶⁻⁸ Nowadays hydrated K-Al₁₃ is regarded as an ideal molecular model for the study of mineral surfaces that are inaccessible experimentally. As an environmentally ubiquitous inorganic anion, $F^-$ has influences on such chemical behaviors as structures and reactivity for all kinds of aqueous Al species by strong bonding to aluminum.⁹⁻¹² Therefore, the study of the interactions between $F^-$ and K-Al₁₃ contributes to further understanding the influence of $F^-$ on mineral surface reactivity, thereby providing a possibility of unravelling the transformation and transfer of environmental aluminum.

The understanding of reactivity is the key to probe into the interactions of $F^-$ and aqueous Al species. The activated parameters of the water exchange reactions provided by $^{17}$O NMR and theoretical computation were often employed to speculate about the reactivity of aqueous species.¹³⁻¹⁷ The labilizing effect of $F^-$ on aqueous monomeric aluminum observed previously by $^{17}$O NMR experiments¹⁰,¹⁸ was proved subsequently by the corresponding theoretical computation.¹²,¹⁹ For the polymeric K-MAl₁₂ (M = Al, Ga, Ge) the reactivity of bound H₂O is much higher than that of monomeric aluminum and the reactivity of two topologically distinct hydroxyl bridges differs remarkably, as illustrated experimentally by Casey *et al.*¹⁴⁻¹⁶ To our knowledge, only a few experimental studies on fluoridated K-MAl₁₂ were published relative to non-fluoridated K-MAl₁₂. It was found that the fluoridation of K-Al₁₃ enhances the transformation from Al₁₃ to Al₃₀ based on $^{19}$F NMR spectra.²⁰ The observations from potentiometry and NMR experiments show the labilizing effect of replacing $F^-$ for K-GaAl₁₂ and the difference in fluorine substitution rates for the two bridging hydroxyl groups.²¹ Unlike monomeric aluminum, there are almost no reports on corresponding theoretical studies for fluorine substituted polyaluminum species thus far. Theoretical studies are not only commonly referred to as a supplement to experiment to confirm experimental results and interpret the experimental phenomenon at the molecular scale, but are also an indispensable approach

For aqueous Al species the relationship between structures and reactivity becomes a bridge connecting the experimental and theoretical studies. Therefore, theoretical computation of fluorine substituted K-Al₁₃ is promising for the insight into the reactivity trend of distinct hydroxyl bridges or bound water sites and for exploring the substitution mechanism.

In this work, the influence of fluorine substitution on the chemical behavior of K-Al₁₃ was investigated using DFT method. The present study focuses on: (1) the structures of fluorine substituted K-Al₁₃ with different substituted sites were optimized and the ¹⁹F and ²⁷Al NMR chemical shifts were computed subsequently; (2) water-exchange reactions were simulated for fluorine substituted K-Al₁₃ and the activated energy barriers and mechanism were obtained, thereby reflecting the reactivity of bound water; (3) according to the above computational results, some experimental phenomena in the process of fluorine substitution were interpreted using the proposed fluorine substitution mechanism and the characteristics of fluorine substitution reactions on mineral surfaces were discussed.

## Computational details
All calculations were carried out using the Gaussian 03 suite of programs.²² The geometries of K-Al₁₃ with the different substituted sites were optimized at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level followed by the frequency calculations.²³,²⁴ At first the gas phase optimization was implemented and subsequently self-consistent reaction field model with a spherical cavity was employed to consider the bulk solvent effect. In order to take explicit solvent effect into account simultaneously, the explicit solvent water molecules were added around the above models.²⁵ The NMR computation was accomplished using the Gauge-Independent Atomic Orbital (GIAO) method at the level of HF/6-311+G(d,p).²⁶,²⁷ ²⁷Al NMR chemical shifts were obtained relative to Al(H₂O)₆³⁺ and F(H₂O)₆⁻ (−119 ppm versus CFCl₃ provided by Yu et al.²¹) was taken as the ¹⁹F NMR secondary reference. For the water exchange reactions the clusters with the explicit solvent molecules were optimized and the following frequency calculations were implemented to confirm the stable structures and transition states. For the transition states the intrinsic reaction coordinate (IRC) calculations were performed. Afterwards, the bulk solvent effects were considered by polarizable continuum model (PCM) single-point energy with a dielectric constant $\varepsilon = 78.39$ and UAKS radii at the MPWKCIS/6-31+G(d,p) computational level.

## Results and discussion
### The structures of fluorine substituted K-Al₁₃ and ²⁷Al and ¹⁹F NMR chemical shifts
Twelve equivalent terminal bound water molecules of K-Al₁₃ surrounded by four trimers were often signified as $\eta$-OH₂ and twenty-four paired hydroxyl bridges were classified into two distinct sets: $\mu_2$-OH(1) located intra-trimers and $\mu_2$-OH(2) located inter-trimers. Both $\eta$-OH₂ and two distinct hydroxyl bridges can be substituted by fluorine, forming the terminal bound fluorine ($\eta$-F) and two sets of fluorine bridges ($\mu_2$-F(1) or $\mu_2$-F(2)) respectively, as proposed by Yu et al.²¹ The optimized geometries of hexafluorine-substituted K-Al₁₃ at $\mu_2$-F(1) and $\mu_2$-F(2) sites as well as the geometries of dodecafluorine-substituted K-Al₁₃ at $\eta$-F sites are presented in Fig. 1, respectively. Thus on average one F⁻ is coordinated to Al for every aluminum-oxygen octahedron unit. This construction not only is able to eliminate the influence of the number of substituted F⁻, but also is consistent with the observation provided by Allouche and Taulelle that only one fluorine bridge is formed by replacement for every paired $\mu_2$-OH bridge.²⁰ Eighteen explicit water molecules forming hydrogen bonds with the $\eta$-OH₂ or $\mu_2$-OH were added around the clusters to simulate the explicit solvent effect.²⁷ According to the structural parameters listed in Table 1, the influence of fluorine substitution reactions on the structure of the clusters are discussed as follows:

(1) The distance between octahedral Al and coordinated $\eta$-OH₂ decreases with the presence of fluorine bridges. The character directly is closely related to the reactivity trend of bound water, as discussed in the next section. It is clear that the inter-trimer fluorine bridges have more effect on the Al-OH₂ distance than intra-trimer fluorine bridges.

(2) For both the substituted and non-substituted K-Al₁₃, the bridging Al-OH(1) bond lengths are longer than Al-OH(2). In addition, the presence of $\eta$-F results in the increase in Al-OH(1) and Al-OH(2) bond lengths, while the bridging fluorine has almost no influence on the distance.

Interestingly assignment of the terminal fluorine peak is a controversial subject due to unexpected disappearance of the resonance signal at −148 ppm during crystallization.²⁰ Previously the peak was ascribed as free HF signal by Allouche and Taulelle while Yu *et al.* believes that the signal corresponds to η-F. Based on the assignment, the related experimental observations are interpreted (see more details in the next section). This is consistent with the ²⁷Al NMR results for monomer fluoroaluminum complexes in aqueous solution reported previously,¹²,²⁹ where the shifts difference resulting from fluorine substitution is less than 3 ppm, independent of fluoridated degree.

## The influence of fluorine substitution on the reactivity of η-OH₂ for K-Al₁₃

The reactivity of η-OH₂ commonly depends on the water exchange rates for aqueous clusters. In order to simulate the water-exchange reaction of fluorine substituted K-Al₁₃, the second solvent sphere should be re-established to form a hole for water exchange based on the above optimized models with the inclusion of sufficient solvent effects. Thus fifteen water molecules were added around the clusters to take the explicit solvent effect into account and this number is sufficient to describe the explicit solvent effect for the present clusters, as illustrated by Qian *et al.*³⁰

Considering that the η-OH₂ is viewed as a target molecule in the present study and the hydrogen bonds in the transition state appear between the neighboring two η-OH₂ molecules, the water-exchange reactions of the clusters with substituted η-F fail to be modeled. In the case of the fluorine substitution of hydroxyl bridges, the substitution in only one octahedron unit was considered to simplify the mutual influence of fluoridated sites between the neighboring octahedrons. To validate the reasonableness of the simplification, the water-exchange reactions for both K-Al₁₃-1F (one bridging hydroxyl group substituted by fluorine only in one octahedron unit) and K-Al₁₃-6F (one bridging hydroxyl group substituted by fluorine in every octahedron unit) were simulated (see Fig. S1 and Table S1 in ESI† for the optimized geometries and energetic parameters). It is evident that the water-exchange pathways and activated energy barriers are almost in close proximity for the two clusters.

The water-exchange pathways of K-Al₁₃-1F with two distinct bridging fluoridated sites are displayed in Fig. 2. With respect to the model with inter-trimer fluorine bridge, the distance between the target bound water and octahedral center Al is found to be 2.019 Å in the reactant. Subsequently the elongation of the bond gives rise to the formation of the transition state with the Al–OH₂ distance of 3.343 Å, where the target water is hydrogen bonded to another bound water of a neighboring octahedron unit. As the distance further increases the target water molecule enters the second solvation sphere ultimately and the five-coordinated intermediate with an Al–OH₂ distance of 4.738 Å appears, which is often denoted as the product. The changes of bond lengths in these water-exchange processes are given in Table 3. Apart from the leaving target water, all the distances between the octahedral center Al and other ligands decrease with activation to various extents, reflecting the contraction of the octahedron unit. This is in line with the structural change in the course of water exchange on monomer aluminum species presented in a recent study.¹²

However, it should be noted that the Al-Oᵗ distances, i.e. the distance between octahedral Al and tetrahedral μ₄O, are shortened by at least 0.1 Å for non-fluoridated or fluoridated K-Al₁₃. Unlike monomer aluminum, it is difficult to form a five-coordinated product for the polyaluminum species by the free movement of ligands as a result of the rigidity of the bridging configuration. In this case the octahedron central Al has to approach μ₄O as far as possible to yield the approximate five-coordinated square pyramid configuration. Comparing with the contraction degree of the Al-O octahedron, the slight contraction of the central AlO₄ tetrahedron core is negligible, suggesting the activation has almost no influence on the AlO₄ core. The activation volume ($\Delta V^{\neq}$) observed experimentally is commonly referred to as reliable criteria for mechanism assignment,³¹,³² while the total metal-ligand bond lengths change between the reactant and transition state ($\Delta\Sigma_{\text{Al-L}}$) is often applied to differentiate the theoretically activated mechanism in view of the agreement between the two parameters.³³ As shown in Table 3, the positive $\Delta\Sigma_{\text{Al-L}}$ implies the dissociative activated mechanism for all the species. This means the water-exchange mechanism does not change with the fluorine substitution of the bridging hydroxyl group. Apparently, the greater deviation from zero caused by the substituted fluorine bridge indicates the greater dissociative character. In addition, comparing with monomer aluminum species,¹² the activated mechanism for polyaluminum is more dissociative according to $\Delta\Sigma_{\text{Al-L}}$ parameters.

Four distinct configurations were considered for difluoro clusters. The first is the substitution of two inter-trimer μ₂-OH(1); the second is the substitution of two intra-trimer μ₂-OH(2); the third is that one μ₂-OH(1) and one μ₂-OH(2) are cis-substituted; the last is that one μ₂-OH(1) and one μ₂-OH(2) are trans-substituted. It is only if the fluorine substitution reactions occur in the same AlO₆ octahedron that some noticeable effect occurs. For random substitution on the 12 possible octahedrons, no effect is observed. Similarly, the trifluoro complexes include two configurations and the tetrafluoro complex corresponds to only one structure. In other words, the fluorine substitution of ${\text{K-Al}}_{13}$ lessens the reactivity of bound water. This is in agreement with the discussed-above trend of $\text{Al-OH}_{2}$ distances decreasing with the appearance of fluorine bridges. For aqueous monomer aluminum species the influence of $\text{F}^{-}$ on the reactivity of coordinated $\text{OH}_{2}$ in previous studies is summarized as follows: (1) the labilizing effect resulting from the substitution of bound $\text{OH}_{2}$ by $\text{F}^{-}$ was interpreted as caused by the reduced formal charge;$^{18}$ (2) the labilizing effect of hydroxyl is about 2 orders of magnitude larger than that of the fluorine ligand, implying that the substitution of hydroxyl by $\text{F}^{-}$ decreases the reactivity of bound $\text{OH}_{2}$. The greater covalency of the Al-O bond should be responsible for this inerting effect relative to the Al-F bond and the increasing NPA charge on Al with substitution demonstrated the point of view in the earlier study.$^{12,34}$ In the present study on polymer aluminum species, the reduced reactivity of bound $\text{OH}_{2}$ induced by fluorine bridges agrees with the behavior of monomer aluminum, but we can notice the different causation. Unlike monomer aluminum, the substitution of hydroxyl bridges in ${\text{K-Al}}_{13}$ by fluorine bridges lowers the Al charge (see Fig. S6 in ESI$\dagger$), suggesting that the difference in covalency is not supposed to be applied to explain the inerting effect. Possibly, the main reason lies in that the ligands have to contract to produce an approximate five-coordinated square pyramid configuration in the process of water exchange, as discussed above. It is clear that the reduced Al charge results in the difficulty in contraction, thereby raising the energy barriers. As four fluorine bridges appear, the reactivity decreases enormously, exceeding that of $\text{Al}(\text{OH}_{2})_{6}^{3+}$ with the energy barrier of $70.1\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.$^{30}$ In addition, one can note that the barriers are insensitive to the topologically distinct fluorine bridges.

The reactive sites and exploration of the fluorine substitution process for ${\text{K-Al}}_{13}$

In the case of the ${\text{K-Al}}_{13}$ cluster, the reactivities of two sites ($\eta\text{-OH}_{2}$ and $\mu_{2}\text{-OH}$) are the key to understand its fluorine substitution process. The formation rate of $\eta\text{-F}$ is dependent on the reactivity trend of $\eta\text{-OH}_{2}$ observed from the energy barriers of the present water-exchange reactions and the formation rate of the fluorine bridge is close related to the reactivity of $\mu_{2}\text{-OH}$. Although the reactivity of $\mu_{2}\text{-OH}$ fails to be obtained by theoretical computation presently, it was admitted that the rupture of hydroxyl bridges is a pivotal step for the oxygen exchange reactions of Keggin aluminum tridecamers according to Rustad's proposal.$^{28}$ Thus the bond lengths between Al and $\mu_{2}\text{-OH}$ can be applied to roughly evaluate the reactivity of hydroxyl bridges. From this point of view, the longer $\text{Al-OH(1)}$ distances in the ${\text{K-Al}}_{13}$ cluster suggest the higher reactivity of $\mu_{2}\text{-OH(1)}$ compared with $\mu_{2}\text{-OH(2)}$, which is in agreement with the previous $^{19}\text{F}$ and $^{17}\text{O}$ NMR experimental results.$^{14,21}$

Combining the reactivity trends observed from the above computations with previous experimental results,$^{20,21}$ we propose the fluorine substitution process of ${\text{K-Al}}_{13}$, as shown in Fig. 4. At first $\text{F}^{-}$ ions in aqueous solution replace the $\eta\text{-OH}_{2}$ and form $\eta\text{-F}$, denoted as step (1), as proposed by Yu et al.$^{21}$ Subsequently the $\eta\text{-F}$ further replace one of the paired bridging hydroxyl groups yielding $\mu_{2}\text{-(OH, F)}$, that is step (2). Thus step (1) is influenced by the reactivity of $\eta\text{-OH}_{2}$ while step (2) depends on the reactivity of $\mu_{2}\text{-OH}$. As shown in Table 1, the bridging Al-OH distance increases as $\eta\text{-F}$ appears, showing the labilizing effect of $\eta\text{-F}$ on hydroxyl bridges. However, once $\mu_{2}\text{-(OH, F)}$ is formed, the reactivity of $\eta\text{-OH}_{2}$ is inhibited according to the conclusions drawn from the water-exchange reactions. In this case, $\eta\text{-OH}_{2}$ is difficult to be substituted by $\text{F}^{-}$ continuously and it is certain that step (1) becomes slower and slower.

We can interpret a series of experimental results using the proposed fluorine substitution mechanism:

Firstly, it was observed that the intensity of the $\eta\text{-F}$ signal decreases rapidly in the first few hours and subsequently decreases slowly and the signal still exists after experimental time of 93.6 h.$^{21}$ According to the proposed mechanism, the rapid decrease in the first few hours results from the transformation from $\eta\text{-F}$ to $\mu_{2}\text{-(OH, F)}$. The subsequent slow decrease is ascribed to the inhibition effect of formed $\mu_{2}\text{-(OH, F)}$ on $\eta\text{-OH}_{2}$ and the

difference in reactivity for $\mu_2$-OH(1) and $\mu_2$-OH(2). Obviously $\eta$-OH$_2$ is more and more difficult to be replaced by F$^-$ with more fluorine bridges forming. Considering the labilizing effect of $\eta$-F on $\mu_2$-OH, $\eta$-F disappears gradually. The existing signal after 93.6 h should merely correspond to free HF (see the following discussion for more details).

Secondly, according to $^{19}$F NMR spectra observed previously,$^{20,21}$ there is no doubt that the peak at $-136$ ppm is attributed to the inter-trimer fluorine bridge and the $-138$ ppm signal is assigned to the intra-trimer fluorine bridge, but the assignment of the $\eta$-F peak is controversial. The peak at $-148$ ppm was viewed as the signal of $\eta$-F in the study on fluoridation of GaAl$_{12}$, $^{21}$ while Allouche and Taulelle claim that the signal should be assigned to free HF on account of the peak disappearing from the solution to solid state.$^{20}$ We optimized K-Al$_{13}$ with $\eta$-HF using different computational levels and methods. The failure in optimization implies that the substitution might be difficult to occur or $\eta$-HF is not stable. It is possible that unstable $\eta$-HF forms stable $\eta$-F by deprotonation or the substitution by F$^-$ even if $\eta$-HF appears. The present NMR computation indicates that the $\eta$-F peak in fluoridated Keggin-Al$_{13}$ is near $-148$ ppm and we presume that the peak corresponds to the mixed signal of $\eta$-F and free HF, which cannot be resolved experimentally. In the course of crystallization the increasing concentration of F$^-$ with the volatilization of the solvent results in the rapid substitution of hydroxyl bridges, thereby arresting greatly the fluorine substitution of $\eta$-OH$_2$ and even resulting in the discontinuation of step (1) as well as the disappearance of $\eta$-F, which might be an appropriate explanation for the observations by Allouche and Taulelle.$^{20}$

Thirdly, in the case of Allouche and Taulelle's report on fluoridation of Keggin-Al$_{13}$, $^{20}$ we should notice a fact that no more than one bridge was able to be substituted by F$^-$ for paired hydroxyl bridges. This can be explained by the above presumption: one formed fluorine bridge impedes subsequent fluorine substitution of $\eta$-OH$_2$, thus the other hydroxyl bridges are difficult to be substituted by fluorine continuously.

The fact that the appearance of the fluorine bridges lowers the reactivity of $\eta$-OH$_2$ is not in contradiction to the conclusions drawn by previous studies, that is F$^-$ can enhance the dissolution of aluminum (hydr)oxide mineral by substitution. The influence of bridging structures on dissolution of mineral is stronger than that of the terminal sites, as illustrated by Nordin et al.$^{35}$ The weaker fluorine bridge relative to hydroxyl bridges should be responsible for the promotion of dissolution. Since $\eta$-F is the premise of forming fluorine bridges, the fluorine substitution of both terminal and bridging sites promotes the dissolution of mineral. On the other hand, the appearance of $\eta$-F also gives rise to the weakening of hydroxyl bridges. Considering that the paired hydroxyl bridges cannot be replaced by F$^-$ simultaneously, it is possible that the asymmetric bridging structures accelerate the dissolution of mineral.

## Conclusions

We simulated the structures of fluorine substituted K-Al$_{13}$ in aqueous solution and proposed the fluorine substitution process of K-Al$_{13}$ by investigating the changes in reactivity of the group with the substitution, thereby further probing into the influence of F$^-$ on the chemical behavior of mineral surfaces. The agreement of NMR chemical shifts confirms the appropriateness of the present models. The obtained mechanism and energy barriers of the water-exchange reaction of fluorine substituted K-Al$_{13}$ indicate that substitution does not change the dissociative activated mechanism, but reduces the reactivity of $\eta$-OH$_2$. In addition, the fluorine substitution process of K-Al$_{13}$ based on the experimental observations and theoretical results was speculated upon and several experimental puzzles were clarified. The mechanism of F$^-$ promoting dissolution of mineral was interpreted at the molecular level, which is helpful to further study on the influence of a variety of inorganic or organic ligands on the reactivity of mineral surfaces.
