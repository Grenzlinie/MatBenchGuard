# The $X_21 \to X_10^+$ electronic band systems of bismuth monohalides in the near infrared $\star$

E.H. Fink $^{1}$, K.D. Setzer $^{1}$
Physikalische Chemie-Fachbereich 9, Bergische Universität-Gesamthochschule Wuppertal, W-5600 Wuppertal 1, Germany

D.A. Ramsay and M. Vervloet $^{2}$
Herzberg Institute of Astrophysics, National Research Council of Canada, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada K1A OR6

Received 31 December 1990; in final form 25 January 1991

Emission spectra of the transitions $X_21 \to X_10^+$ between the fine-structure components of the $X\,^3\Sigma^-$ ground states of BiF, BiCl, BiBr and BiI have been observed in the near-infrared spectral region between 1.3 and $1.7\ \mu$m. The $X_21$ states of the radicals were excited by near-resonant electronic-to-electronic ($E-E$) energy exchange from metastable $\mathrm{O_2}$ (a $^1\Delta_g$) molecules in a fast-flow system. For each molecule, a number of blue-degraded bands with sharp heads in the P branches were measured in the $\Delta\nu=0,\ \pm1$ and $-2$ sequences at spectral resolutions between 0.1 and $1\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ using a Bomem Fourier-transform spectrometer. The following electronic term values and vibrational constants of the $X_21$ states were obtained (in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$): $T_e$=6752.7944(12), 6670.83(15), 6526.44(24), 6181.64(6), $\omega_e$=543.0558(24), 327.44(15), 220.38(21), 168.68(6), $\omega_ex_e$=2.3996(15), 1.004(27), 0.546(36), 0.386(6), for BiF, $\mathrm{Bi\ ^{35}Cl}$, $\mathrm{Bi\ ^{79}Br}$ and BiI, respectively, where the error limits are $3\sigma$. From a re-interpretation of near-UV spectra of BiF, the a2(a $^1\Delta$) state of BiF is calculated to lie at $11555\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.

## 1. Introduction
Electronic emission and absorption spectra of the bismuth monohalide molecules BiF, BiCl, BiBr and BiI have been known since the thirties [1]. It is generally accepted that due to their similar outer electronic configurations $...(\sigma)^2(\pi)^4(\pi^*)^2$, the BiX molecules should exhibit the same low-lying states as the lighter group-V monohalides, i.e. $X\,^3\Sigma^-$ ($X_10^+$, $X_21$) ground states and a $^1\Delta$(a2) and b $^1\Sigma^+$(b0$^+$) excited states, in the Hund's case (b) and (c) limits, respectively. By comparison with the analogous spin-spin splittings in the ground states of the arsenic and antimony monohalides [2], the separation of the $X_10^+$ and $X_21$ states was estimated to be several thousand wavenumbers [3-5]. But in spite of some efforts [5-8], hitherto no experimental information was available on the ground-state splitting for any of the molecules. The A0$^+$ states with term values $T_e$ between $22960\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ (BiF) and $20006\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ (BiI), which are well known for all four species from absorption and emission studies of the strong A0$^+$-X0$^+$ systems, were thought to arise from the excited electronic configuration $...(\sigma)^2(\pi)^3(\pi^*)^3$ [9,10]. However, in recent relativistic CI calculations on the low-lying states of BiF, Balasubramanian [11] found the energy of the first excited $0^+$ state of BiF to lie as high as $25931\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. Near its equilibrium geometry, the nature of this state was found to be dominantly $^1\Sigma^+$ arising from the lowest electronic configuration. Thus, Balasubramanian concluded that in BiF the experimentally observed A0$^+$ state at $22960\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ has to be reassigned to b0$^+$ (b $^1\Sigma^+$). In the same work, the $X_21$ and a2 states of BiF were calculated to lie at 7280 and $14595\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, respectively, likewise much higher than was previously assumed by comparison with the corresponding states of AsF and SbF.

## 2. Experimental

The experimental set-up was very similar to that used for measuring the spectrum of BiH [12]. The flow system was made of pyrex glass and consisted mainly of a tube, 100 cm long and 12 cm in diameter, with quartz windows at both ends and equipped with a pumping port and two side arms for gas inlet. In one side arm, a flow of argon carrier gas was passed over bismuth metal heated in a quartz tube to above its melting point $(\approx 550\ \text{K})$. Before entering the main tube, the halogens mixed with helium carrier gas could be added to this gas flow. Through the second side arm, oxygen containing 5-10% of metastable $O_{2}(a^{1}\Delta_{g})$ molecules was added to the reaction system. The tube was pumped at the other end with a $500\ \text{m}^{3}\ \text{h}^{-1}$ roots pump in series with a $40\ \text{m}^{3}\ \text{h}^{-1}$ forepump resulting in flow velocities of around $5\ \text{m}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ in the large tube and $\approx 20\ \text{m}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ in the inlet systems. The total pressure in the tube was measured with a Baratron capacitance manometer and was in the range 0.5-3 Torr $(1\ \text{Torr} \approx 133\ \text{Pa})$. It consisted of about equal partial pressures of $O_{2}$ and Ar, up to 100 mTorr of the halogen/He mixtures and unknown traces of bismuth compounds. The bismuth metal and the gases had the following stated purity grades: Bi 99.5%, He 99.995%, Ar 99.996%, $O_{2}$ 99.995%, $F_{2}$ 99.5%, $Cl_{2}$ 99.5%, $Br_{2}$ 99.5%, $I_{2}$ 99.5%.

The radiation emitted from the tube was focused onto the entrance diaphragm of a Bomem DA3.002 Fourier-transform spectrometer. Spectra were recorded in the region $5700-9000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ at spectral resolutions up to $0.1\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ using a liquid-nitrogen cooled Ge detector/preamplifier hybrid (Applied Detector Corporation model 405 L). Through the rear window, the emissions from the tube were monitored with a small grating monochromator (Spex Mini-mate), a second Ge detector, a mechanical light chopper, and a lock-in amplifier. This system served to optimize the chemiluminescence intensity by varying the gas flow rates and to control the intensity during the sampling of the interferograms.

Without the addition of halogens, the emission spectrum is dominated by the very strong $\Delta v=0$ and the weaker $\Delta v=-1$ sequences of the $X_{2}\ ^{2}\Pi_{3/2} \to X_{1}\ ^{2}\Pi_{1/2}$ system of BiO near 7090 and $6420\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. The BiO molecules likewise are excited by near-resonant $E-E$ energy transfer from metastable oxygen molecules, the $X_{2} \to X_{1}$ bands being among the most intense emissions which can be excited by $O_{2}(a^{1}\Delta_{g})$ [13]. With about a factor of a hundred lower intensity, the spectrum contains some sequences of the $\text{A}\ ^{2}\Pi_{1/2} \to X_{1}\ ^{2}\Pi_{1/2}$ system of BiO, the 0-0 band of the $a \to X$ transition of $O_{2}$, and two hitherto unidentified band series of $Bi_{2}$ near $7700\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ [13]. As in previous work [12], the oxygen band was used to check the accuracy of the wavenumber scale of the spectrometer.

When a halogen is added to the system, all these emissions disappear or are strongly reduced in intensity and the BiX bands show up in the spectrum. Using the monochromator and the detection system at the rear end of the flow tube, the intensities of the BiX emissions could easily be optimized by changing the gas flow rates. Fig. 1 shows the spectra of the $X_{2}1 \to X_{1}0^{+}$ bands of BiF, BiCl and BiBr recorded at a resolution of $1\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and plotted on the same wavenumber scale. Fig. 2 shows the spectrum of BiI measured at a resolution of $0.1\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and plotted on a somewhat enlarged wavenumber scale. The spectra are not corrected for the change of the relative sensitivity of the detection system which is a maximum near $6300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and drops rather steeply for lower wavenumbers. All spectra are the results of 20 co-additions and were measured in $\approx 5\ \text{min}$ (BiF, BiCl, BiBr) and $\approx 20\ \text{min}$ (BiI), respectively.

### 3. Analysis and results

Each spectrum consists of three or four sequences of blue-degraded bands with sharp heads in the P branches. The only other strong feature present in all spectra is the $\Delta v=0$ sequence of the $X_{2}\rightarrow X_{1}$ system of BiO at $7090\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ [13]. Since the $X_{1}0^{+}$ and $X_{2}1$ states of the BiX molecules are expected to have nearly the same equilibrium internuclear distances $r_{\text{e}}$, the Franck-Condon factors should be largest for the $\Delta v=0$ bands. Thus, for all molecules the strongest sequence is attributed to $\Delta v=0$ and the weaker sequences to $\Delta v=\pm1$ and $\Delta v=\pm2$ transitions. These assignments are supported by the fact that in the spectra of BiCl and BiBr, vibrational isotope shifts are observed in the $\Delta v=\pm1$ and $\Delta v=-2$ but not in the $\Delta v=0$ sequences. The wavenumbers of the band heads of all identified bands are listed in table 1.

These wavenumbers were fitted to the standard anharmonic oscillator expression,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{v'v''} &= T_{\text{e}}(X_{2})+\omega_{\text{e}}'(v'+\frac{1}{2}) \\
&-\omega_{\text{e}}x_{\text{e}}'(v'+\frac{1}{2})^{2}+\omega_{\text{e}}y_{\text{e}}'(v'+\frac{1}{2})^{3}-\omega_{\text{e}}''(v''+\frac{1}{2}) \\
&+\omega_{\text{e}}x_{\text{e}}''(v''+\frac{1}{2})^{2}-\omega_{\text{e}}y_{\text{e}}''(v''+\frac{1}{2})^{3},
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

using the method of least squares. In most cases, inclusion of the cubic terms did not significantly improve the quality of the fits. For BiCl, BiBr and BiI, the standard deviations of the fits were less than 0.1 $\text{cm}^{-1}$. In the case of BiF, the quality of the fit was lower ($\sigma=0.36\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) and the vibrational spacing in the ground state $\Delta G_{1/2}$ obtained from the parameters differed by more than $2\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ from the value deduced from high-resolution studies of the $\text{A}0^{+}\rightarrow X_{1}0^{+}$ system [10]. We have measured BiF spectra at higher resolution ($0.007\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) and carried out an analysis of eight bands [15]. The band origins are given in table 1 and the vibrational constants deduced from these values are given in table 2. The ground-state constants are in excellent agreement with the values of Jones and McLean [10]. For $\text{Bi}^{35}\text{Cl}$, $\text{Bi}^{79}\text{Br}$ and BiI, the vibrational constants of the $X_{1}0^{+}$ ground state likewise are in reasonable agreement with literature data [1]. Within the error limits, the constants of the heavier isotopes $\text{Bi}^{37}\text{Cl}$ and $\text{Bi}^{81}\text{Br}$ agree with the values calculated from those of the lighter species by use of the standard isotope relations (table 2). For all species, the $\omega_{\text{e}}$ and $\omega_{\text{e}}x_{\text{e}}$ values of the $X_{2}1$ states are larger by a few percent than those of the ground states. The energy separations of the $X_{2}1$ and $X_{1}0^{+}$ states, i.e. the spin-spin splittings of the $\text{X}^{3}\Sigma^{-}$ states, range from $6184.10\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (BiI) to $6767.81\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (BiF) and are much larger than hitherto expected. For BiF, the experimental $T_{\text{e}}$ value is close to the value of $7280\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ calculated by Balasubramanian [11].

### 4. Discussion

Knowledge of the energy values and the vibrational constants of the $X_{2}1$ states allows a re-interpretation of some of the previous spectroscopic studies of the bismuth halide molecules. This is especially interesting in the case of BiF where a number of electronic states and band systems have been observed. Fig. 3 shows an energy-level diagram of BiF drawn in the light of the new results. The $A0^{+}-X_{1}0^{+}$, $B0^{+}-X_{1}0^{+}$, and $C1-X_{1}0^{+}$ systems of BiF are well known from both absorption and emission studies [1]. According to the CI calculations of Balasubramanian [11], as supported by the results of the present work, the $A0^{+}$ state is identical with the $b0^{+}(b^{1}\Sigma^{+})$ state previously expected to lie at much lower energy. Obviously, the same re-assignment is due to the $A0^{+}$ states of BiCl, BiBr and BiI. These new assignments open the question why for the bismuth halide molecules the equilibrium internuclear distances $r_{e}$ and the vibrational constants $\omega_{e}$ of the $b0^{+}$ states are substantially different from those of the corresponding ground states, contrary to what is found for all other group-V halide molecules [1]. The calculations of Balasubramanian [11] suggest that these shifts are due to widening of the $b0^{+}$ potential curves by curve crossings. However, more theoretical work is needed to explain these constants and to diminish the differences between the theoretical and experimental results.

Three other band systems of BiF in the wavelength regions 2650-2850 Å [16-18], 3050-3250 Å [16,17,19,20] and 5800-6600 Å [6,21-23] have been observed in emission only. The latter red system has been studied at high resolution by Chakoo and Patel [23]. These authors observed two bands, 0-0 and 0-1, with P and R branches. They assigned the system to a transition $A_{1}\ ^{1}\Sigma^{+} \to X_{3}\ ^{1}\Sigma^{+}$ where the lower state was thought to be the so-far-unknown $b^{1}\Sigma^{+}$ state. The vibrational spacing $\Delta G_{1/2}=538.1\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ in the lower state of this system agrees with the value of $538.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ found for the $X_{2}1$ state which strongly indicates that the $X_{2}1$ is the lower state of the red-band system. The upper state of this system is then a $\Omega=1$ state since only for a $\Delta\Omega=0$ transition could the Q branch be too weak to be readily observed. The rotational constant $B_{0}$ of the lower state determined by Chakoo and Patel $(B_{0}=0.2434(5)\ \text{cm}^{-1})$ differs from the preliminary value for the $X_{2}1$ state determined by us $(B_{0}=0.235121(7)\ \text{cm}^{-1})$ and may indicate a small error in the rotational numbering used by the earlier workers.

[16-18]. In the only high-resolution work, Mohanty et al. [18] observed two sub-systems in each of the 0-0 and 0-1 bands. The sub-systems at higher energy showed P, Q, R structures while those at lower energy showed only P and R branches. From their measurements and the earlier work of Rao and Rao [16], the vibrational spacing in the lower state is found to be between 537.1 and $540.2 cm^{-1}$. The close agreement of this spacing with that observed for the $X_{2} 1$ state strongly indicates that $X_{2} 1$ is the lower state of this system. We assign the bands to the $^{3}\Pi_{2} \to X_{2} 1$ and $^{3}\Pi_{1} \to X_{2} 1$ sub-systems of a $^{3}\Pi_{r} \to X_{2} 1$ transition, the separation between the $^{3}\Pi_{2}$ and $^{3}\Pi_{1}$ levels being $16 cm^{-1}$ (fig. 3).

Similarly, in a high-resolution study of the 3050-3250 Å system, Chaudry et al. [20] found that the bands each have two sub-systems with the same separation as in the 2650-2850 Å bands. In this case, the sub-systems at lower energy have P, Q, R structures and those at higher energy have only P and R branches. If we assume that the two band systems have the same upper state, then the lower state of the 3050-3250 Å system is calculated to lie at 11555 $cm^{-1}$. The two sub-systems are assigned as $^3\Pi_2\rightarrow\text{a2}(^1\Delta)$ and $^3\Pi_1\rightarrow\text{a2}(^1\Delta)$ (fig. 3). The C $^1\Pi$ and $^3\Pi$ states lie close in energy and may arise from the electronic configuration $...(\sigma)^2(\pi)^4(\pi^*)(\sigma^*)$, $^1\Pi$, $^3\Pi_r$. Both states give rise to violet-degraded bands suggesting that the electron in the $\sigma^*$ orbital is less antibonding than in the $\pi^*$ orbital.

The above assignments appear attractive but leave two unresolved problems. First of all, there are small discrepancies between the B values reported for the two systems which may indicate minor revisions of

the rotational numberings. Second, it is interesting to enquire why the $^3\Pi_2 \to X_21$ sub-system is observed and not the $^3\Pi_0 \to X_21$ sub-system.

It is interesting to note that the a2 state of BiF has nearly the same energy as the a $^1\Delta$ state of NF ($\nu_{00}=11435$ cm$^{-1}$ [1]). This readily allows us to explain the observed efficient excitation of BiF(A0$^+$) through energy transfer from NF(a $^1\Delta$) [24] by the successive electronic-to-electronic ($E-E$) energy ex- change and $E+E$ energy pooling processes (2) and (3),

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{NF}\left(\mathrm{a}^{1} \Delta\right)+\mathrm{BiF}\left(\mathrm{X}_{1} 0^{+}\right) \\
& \quad \rightarrow \mathrm{NF}\left(\mathrm{X}^{3} \Sigma^{-}\right)+\mathrm{BiF}(\mathrm{a} 2, v=0)-120 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{BiF}(\mathrm{a} 2, v=0)+\mathrm{NF}\left(\mathrm{a}^{1} \Delta\right) \\
& \quad \rightarrow \mathrm{BiF}\left(\mathrm{A} 0^{+}, v=0\right)+\mathrm{NF}\left(\mathrm{X}^{3} \Sigma^{-}\right)+98 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

which both have energy discrepancies of less than the mean thermal collision energy.

The observation of the $X_21 \to X_10^+$ transition in the near-infrared region poses the question why some other transitions with $\Delta\Omega=0, \pm1$ which are indi- cated in fig. 3 by broken lines have not been found yet. This question is especially interesting in case of the A0$^+$(b0$^+$) $\to X_21$ transition which several groups have searched for without success [6-8]. According to an empirical rule found by Colin et al. [14], in the hitherto known b$\to$X spectra of group-V halide mol- ecules with angular momentum coupling close to Hund's case (c), the $0^+ \to 0^+$ sub-systems are the stronger ones when the halogen atom is the heavier atom (e.g. in NBr, NI, PBr, and PI) and the $0^+ \to 1$ sub-systems are stronger when the group-V atom is the heavier (e.g. in AsF, SbF, and SbCl). Obviously, this rule does not hold for the bismuth monohalides. Comparative quantum-mechanical calculations of the electronic structures and the transition moments in the arsenic, antimony and bismuth halide molecules would be of great interest. The results of the present work will allow a more systematic search for some of the hitherto unobserved transitions.

Less is known about the electronic spectra of BiCl, BiBr and Bil [1]. In all three molecules, states A0$^+$ and B are known from absorption and emission measurements of A-X and B-X systems which lie in the same energy region as the corresponding states of BiF. In case of Bil, a visible system (5600-5900 Å) has been observed in emission, the upper state of which shows vibrational constants close to those of the B state. This system was measured at medium spectral resolution by Singh [25] and attributed to the B$\to$a $^1\Delta$ transition. The energy of the a $^1\Delta$ state thus obtained was 6190 cm$^{-1}$. Our results show that the lower state of this system is $X_21$. Obviously, the B$\to$X transition of Bil is the only transition for which both sub-systems B$\to$X$_1$0$^+$ and B$\to$X$_2$1 have been observed. Since the BiCl, BiBr and Bil molecules show only slightly lower ground-state splittings and A0$^+$(b0$^+$) state energies than BiF, the energies of their a2(a $^1\Delta$) states can be expected to lie in the range 10000-11500 cm$^{-1}$.

From the experimental conditions under which maximum intensity of the BiX emissions is achieved and the strong decrease of the intensities of the $\mathrm{BiO}(\mathrm{X}_{2}{}^2\Pi_{3/2})$ and $\mathrm{O}_2(\mathrm{a}^1\Delta_{\mathrm{g}})$ emissions on the ad- dition of halogens to the chemiluminescence sys- tems, it can be concluded that excitation of the $X_21$ states mainly proceeds by $E-E$ energy exchange from $\mathrm{O}_2(\mathrm{a}^1\Delta_{\mathrm{g}})$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{BiX}\left(\mathrm{X}_{1} 0^{+}\right)+\mathrm{O}_{2}\left(\mathrm{a}^{1} \Delta_{\mathrm{g}}\right) \\
& \quad \rightarrow \mathrm{BiX}\left(\mathrm{X}_{2} 1, v\right)+\mathrm{O}_{2}\left(\mathrm{X}^{3} \Sigma^{-}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where the $\mathrm{O}_2$(a) state energy is sufficient to excite vibrational levels $v \leqslant 2, 3, 6$ and 10 in the $X_21$ states of BiF, BiCl, BiBr, and Bil, respectively. The obser- vation of emission from higher vibrational levels in the $X_21$ states of BiF and BiCl indicates that other excitation processes, like energy transfer to vibra- tionally excited ground state molecules, quenching of higher electronic states populated by energy pool- ing processes or chemiluminescent reactions are also operative.

## 5. Summary

The upper spin components $X_21$ of the X $^3\Sigma^-$ ground states of the bismuth monohalide molecules have been observed for the first time. Re-interpre- tation of previous spectroscopic and kinetic mea- surements together with recent theoretical results show that the a2(a $^1\Delta$) and b0$^+$(b $^1\Sigma^+$) states of

these molecules lie in the ranges 10000-11600 and $20006-22960\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. The $b0^+_r$ states most likely are identical with the well-known $A0^+$ states of the molecules. The energies of the $X_21$, a2 and $b0^+$ states are much higher than hitherto assumed. The $X_21$ and a2 states probably are long-lived. They can readily be excited by energy-exchange processes from metastable a $^1\Delta_{(g)}$ state $O_2$ and NF molecules and, by themselves, may be interesting energy carriers and reactants in chemical systems. Further experimental and theoretical work is needed to confirm the assignments of the band systems of BiF as suggested in fig. 3, and to answer questions concerning discrepancies in the molecular constants and the ratios of the transition moments of the observed and unobserved transitions.
