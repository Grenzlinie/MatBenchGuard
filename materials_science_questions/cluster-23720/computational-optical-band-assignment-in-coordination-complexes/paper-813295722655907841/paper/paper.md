# The Vibronic Spectrum of $IrCl_{6}^{2-}$ in Low Symmetry Environments
Hans-Herbert Schmidtke and Dan Göttges*)

Institut für Theoretische Chemie, Universität Düsseldorf, D-4000 Düsseldorf 1, Federal Republic of Germany

Complex Compounds / Crystals / Molecular Structure / Spectroscopy, Visible / Vibronic Coupling

The low temperature (7-15 K) absorption spectrum of $IrCl_{6}^{2-}$ doped in single crystals of potassium and ethylammonium hexachloro Sn(IV) salts, $A_{2}SnCl_{6}$ , recorded at high resolution supplies further contributions to solving the assignment problem of vibrational pro gressions of charge transfer and ligand field spin-orbit states. The vibrational fine structure indicates differences in vibronic coupling compared to the cubic systems.

## 1. Introduction
The electronic level scheme of hexachloro-Ir(IV) com- pounds contains already at low energy scale ligand field and charge transfer states which both are split by large spin- orbit coupling and, if applicable, by low symmetry. This problem which is further complicated by strong vibronic coupling has been repeatedly investigated using different ex- periment techniques on various systems (absorption spec- troscopy, MCD, resonance raman). While pure compounds and materials incorporated in polymer matrices do not sup- ply well resolved spectra [1-5], measurements on doped crystals exhibit distinct fine structures due to electronic level splittings and vibrational side bands [6-9]. Although in- vestigations have been performed using much care yielding, in particular for the cubic $Cs_{2}ZrCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ system, highly re solved bands in almost all parts of the visible and near UV spectrum, some problems still remain. There are, e.g., the discrepant assignments of the band system in the B-region(the Douglas notation [7] of spectral regions is used throughout) to ligand field (lf) [7] or parity forbidden charge transfer (ctf) transitions [6,9]. Also different opinions on the degree of Ham effect in the $^{2} T_{2 u}$ state quenching the splitting of spin-orbit levels are reported [10-12]. In addition, the cause of the fine splitting of $\Gamma_{8}$ states obtained from high resolution spectra [10] has been attributed to different rea- sons [13].

Since also band assignments to electronic transitions and vibrational fine structure [3,9,11] are, for the most part, in variance to earlier results [7,10], we felt it worth- while revisiting this problem by looking for other materials supplying low symmetry fields on $IrCl_{6}^{2-}$ . The crystal structure of these compounds should be known at low tem- peratures. Such materials were found in mono- to tris- ethyl substituted ammonium salts of hexachlorostannate, $(Et_{n} NH_{4-n})_{2}[SnCl_{6}], n=1$ to 3 (1EHS to 3EHS), which provide good host lattices for Ir substituting Sn on well defined sites of low symmetry. They yield absorption spectra which are more appropriate for interpretation than corre- sponding methyl ammonium salts [5]. Since also better re- solved spectra of $K_{2} SnCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ single crystals have been ob tained than reported earlier [7] we may start the discussion on this system. With the results obtained from the spectra of EHS:Ir (notation for $Ir^{4+}$ doped in EHS compounds) at least some of the problems raised above can be dealt with. The present investigation will adopt the assignments of the strong charge transfer transitions obtained from MCD spec- tra of cubic $Cs_{2} ZrCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}[6,9]$ which seem to be well es tablished that it can be taken as a basis for further band interpretations.

## 2. Experimental
The host compounds were prepared by adding molar quantities of KCl or corresponding ethylammonium chlorides to $SnCl_{4}$ so lutions in 6N HCI [14]. Doped crystals were grown from 4N HCI solutions (0.05 to $0.2 \% K_{2} IrCl_{6}$ compared to $A_{2} SnCl_{6}$ ) by slow evaporation at constant temperature, favorably between $20^{\circ}$ to $25^{\circ} C$ . From spectral analysis a 0.2 to $0.3 \%$ dotation of $IrCl_{6}^{2-}$ was obtained.

For optical measurements crystals of high quality are selected, irregularities observed in particular on the surfaces of the crystals are removed by polishing with alumina paste (AP-paste $F, Al_{2} O_{3}$ ,1/4μ grain size) brought on a synthetic cloth sticking on a glass plate. Samples for measuring higher extinctions are prepared by reducing the thickness of the crystals until 0.5 mm.

Single crystal absorption spectra were recorded on a Cary 17 spectrophotometer equipped with an RCA 4832 photomultiplier. The original cell compartment was replaced by a larger one which allows for installing two Beck ×15/.28 reflecting objectives and an Air Products LT-3-110C evaporating cryostat into the light path. Due to a heat shield installed close to the sample holder tempera- tures down to 10-15 K could be obtained when cooling with liquid helium, the temperature was monitored by a Au-Fe/chromel ther- mocouple. ## 3. Results and Discussion
In the present report we will focus on the A-region (mainly assigned to transitions from the $\Gamma_{7}(^{2} ~T_{2 ~g})$ ground state to the2T2u ctf level), the B-region of relatively low intensity (ex- plained by If and/or parity forbidden ctf transitions), and on the C-region (transitions into the $^{2} ~T_{1 u}$ ctf level) [1-9].

### 3.1. $K_{2} SnCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$
In Figs. 1 and 2 two regions of the low temperature single crystal spectrum of $K_{2} SnCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ are presented which are much better resolved than those reported earlier on IrCl embedded in the same or similar Sn host crystals[6-8]. While the vibrational fine structure is quite different to that obtained from a $Cs_{2} ZrCl_{6}$ host the resolution is of comparable quality [7,9,11]. The latter system supplies ex- act cubic environment also at low temperature [16], on the other hand the $K_{2} SnCl_{6}$ lattice undergoes phase transitions slightly distorting the Sn site to approximate $D_{4 h}$ symmetry[16,17] which may explain the different phenotypes of these spectra. Since the deviation from octahedral symmetry is very small the $O_{h}$ symmetry notation can be used for the level classification.
In the A-region (Fig. 1) three superimposed progressions with equal intervals of $\sim 310 ~cm^{-1}$ can be detected, theenergy quantum corresponding to the totally symmetric $v_{1}$  vibration of the complex octahedron; two of them (a) have narrow bands and the other (b) contains relatively broadmembers of larger oscillator strengths:

$$\text { (A) } \begin{array}{ll}
\text { (a) }(1,2),(4,5),(7,8), 10,12,14,16 & \Gamma_{6}\left({ }^{4} \mathrm{~T}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}\right) \\
\text { (b) } 3,6,9,11,13,15 & \left(\Gamma_{7}+\Gamma_{8}\right)^{2} \mathrm{~T}_{2 \mathrm{u}}
\end{array}$$

Actual band peaks are measured at $(1,2)=22805,22841$  and $(3)=22967 ~cm^{-1}$ . On the basis of different band shapes and due to the interval of about $35 ~cm^{-1}$ for the components of each member of the $(1,2),(4,5),...$ progression, which corresponds to the difference of vibrational frequencies $v_{6}$  $(140-153 ~cm^{-1})$ and $v_{4}(179-182 ~cm^{-1})$ given for the ground state [18-20], we suggest an assignment of series(a) to If transitions induced by $\tau_{1 u}(v_{4})$ and $\tau_{2 u}(v_{6})$ promoting modes. Series (b), with an oscillator strength about nine times higher than (a) as calculated from a band analysis, is due to parity allowed ctf transitions into $^{2} ~T_{2 u}$ which in accordance with the assignment for $Cs_{2} ZrCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}[9,11,12]$  is hardly split into spin-orbit components $\Gamma_{7}$ and $\Gamma_{8}$ owing to a large Ham effect. An assignment of part of the A-region being due to a $\Gamma_{8}(^{4} ~T_{2 ~g})$ If state has been already proposed by Douglas [7]; we think, however, that the $\Gamma_{6}(^{4} ~T_{1 ~g})$ level calculated by If theory at $\sim 22300 ~cm^{-1}$ (vide infra) is the more probable candidate for the origin of the (a) series, the transition being allowed under the regime of vibronic selec- tion rules. The large intensity is due to an intensity borrow- ing mechanism being particularly effective for interacting states which differ only by some $100 ~cm^{-1}$ in energy. The extensive intermixing of odd and even states by virtue of vibronic coupling makes a clear-cut classification into ctf and If levels difficult; the assignments then refer only to main components of the two transition mechanisms.
The work on cubic $Cs_{2} ZrCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ assigned all these bands to the $^{2} ~T_{2 u}$ ctf level $[9,11,12]$ . This assignment cannot be adopted for the present system. If all progressions in regionA would be due to ctf transitions, the $\Gamma_{8}$ component of $^{2} ~T_{2 u}$  must be assigned to series (a), the splitting into band pairsbeing due to lower symmetry (close to $D_{4 h}$ ), then series (b) extending towards higher energy would arise from $\Gamma_{7}(^{2} ~T_{2 u})$ . A spin-orbit splitting resulting in a $\Gamma_{8}<\Gamma_{7}$ level sequence would, however, be in contrast to the assignment obtained from MCD for $(CH_{3} NH_{3})_{2} SnCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ and for similar bro mide systems [6,20] as well as from polarized spectra of3EHS:Ir (see later). Also, a Franck-Condon analysis per- formed on the two progressions (a) and (b) supplies very different shifts and force constants of excited state potential curves indicating that corresponding transitions cannot be

due to spin-orbit level components arising from the same parent state $^2T_{2u}$.

In the B-region (cf. Fig. 2) we can detect an even larger number of bands grouped in superimposed progressions. An understanding of this spectrum is nevertheless relatively easy. As a guideline the large peaks 1, 10, 19, 27 and 34 at intervals of $315-320\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ corresponding to the $v_{1}(\alpha_{1g})$ stretching vibration of $\mathrm{IrCl}_{6}^{2-}$ are used as well as the second series of strong bands 7, 16, 24, 31 and 36 with the same sequence of vibrational intervals. Actual band maxima are measured at $(1)=21075\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $(7)=21258\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. Since most of these peaks have weaker side bands at positions which correspond to frequency differences between ungerade modes $v_{6}, v_{4}$ and $v_{3}$, respectively, the main band systems are interpreted by two transitions from the ground state $\Gamma_{7}(^2T_{2g})$ into the gerade electronic states I and II, which are promoted by three ungerade vibrational modes of the complex octahedron giving rise to the following band systems

$$
\text{(B) } \begin{aligned}
& \text{I }(1,2.5 / 6),(10,11,14 / 15),(19,20,23),(27,28,31),(34,35,37) \\
& \text{II }(7,8),(16,17),(24,25),(31,32),(36).
\end{aligned} \quad \Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{g}}(^4 \mathrm{~T}_{1 \mathrm{~g}})
$$

Each group arises from $\tau_{2u}(v_{6}), \tau_{1u}(v_{4})$ and $\tau_{1u}(v_{3})$ (the latter not seen in II), i.e. ungerade modes inducing the otherwise parity forbidden electronic transition. The weak absorptions between corresponding groups differing from I by only $80-85\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ fit into a third series of transitions

$$
\text{(B) } \text{III }(3,4,9),(12,13,18),(21,22,26),(29,30,33) \quad \Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{g}}(^4 \mathrm{~T}_{1 \mathrm{~g}})+\mathrm{l}
$$

which are promoted by the same ungerade vibrations. The band series I to III explain all peaks detected in the B spectrum of Fig. 2. For an assignment of their origins see the following discussion.

Since all vibronic side bands involve ungerade vibrational modes the electronic transitions connected to them must be parity forbidden. Therefore an assignment to symmetry forbidden $\Gamma_{6}$ resulting from $^2T_{1u}$ as discussed earlier for this region [4,8,21] can be rejected. Instead, these bands must be either due to lf transitions [7] or to parity forbidden ctf transitions originating from $t_{2g}$ ligand orbitals [6,9]. There are, however, several reasons favoring assignment to lf transitions:

1. The orbital topology of the ligand framework $X_{6}$ of octahedral symmetry [22] predicts a p-orbital energy sequence of
$$
\mathrm{a}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}<\mathrm{t}_{1 \mathrm{u}}<\mathrm{t}_{2 \mathrm{~g}}<\mathrm{t}_{2 \mathrm{u}}<\mathrm{t}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}=\mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{g}}<\mathrm{t}_{1 \mathrm{u}}.
$$

By interaction with d-($\mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{g}}$ and $\mathrm{t}_{2 \mathrm{~g}}$), s-($\mathrm{a}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}$) and p-($\mathrm{t}_{1 \mathrm{u}}$) orbitals of the central metal, the ligand orbitals of these symmetries are pushed down in energy due to bonding combinations. Since in any case $t_{2g}$ ligand orbitals are expected lower in energy than $t_{2u}$, electron transitions starting from these orbitals will give rise to a $^2T_{2g} > ^2T_{2u}$ order of ctf states. Considering also s-orbitals on the ligands which transform as $\mathrm{a}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}, \mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{g}}$ and $\mathrm{t}_{1 \mathrm{u}}$ will not change this ordering due to symmetry reason. Transition from the ground state into $^2T_{2g}$, therefore, is expected higher in energy than transition into $^2T_{2u}$, i.e. the $^2T_{2g}$ spectrum must occur at higher frequencies than those of the A-region.

2. Relativistic MS-X$_{\alpha}$ calculations [23] also put $t_{2g}$ ligand orbitals much lower in energy than $t_{2u}$ which would move ctf transitions to $^2T_{2g}$ far into the UV, leaving the B-region free from these transitions.

3. Since overall spectral features are very similar to lf spectra of many other hexahalogeno complexes of Pt-group elements ($\mathrm{Re}^{4+}, \mathrm{Os}^{4+}, \mathrm{Pt}^{4+}$) the same transition mechanism can be expected also for the present $\mathrm{Ir}^{4+}$ compound.

4. The relatively high intensities of these bands are explained by an intensity borrowing mechanism from ctf states lying close to the lf states. The objection that $\mathrm{t}_{1 \mathrm{u}} \rightarrow \mathrm{t}_{2 \mathrm{~g}}$ orbital transitions cannot contribute much to the vibronically induced transition probability [9,24], does not hold if configuration interaction between lf levels with even and odd ctf levels is present, the latter being intermixed by vibronic coupling via ungerade vibrations.

For assigning the progressions I to III of the B-region to lf levels we performed ligand field calculations using the angular overlap version. The $\mathrm{IrCl}_{6}^{2-}$ complex was assumed to be distorted to approximate $\mathrm{D}_{4 \mathrm{~h}}$ according to the site symmetry of the $\mathrm{K}_{2} \mathrm{SnCl}_{6}$ host [17]. With a parameter set (in $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$) of $e_{\sigma}=13430$ to $14270, e_{\pi}=2680$ to 2840 (adjusted to varying metal-ligand distances), $B=600, C=3060$ and of spin-orbital coupling constant $\zeta=2900$, the octahedral spin-orbit components in the region 18000 to $22000\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ of the lowest excited $^4T_{1g}$ lf state have the level order $\Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{a}}<\Gamma_{7}<\Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{b}}<\Gamma_{6}$, the fourfold $\Gamma_{8}$ states being split due to $\mathrm{D}_{4 \mathrm{~h}}$ by $\sim 850\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for $\Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{a}}$ and $\sim 200\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for $\Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{b}}$. On this basis we may assign the band systems I and II, for which corresponding side bands are measured at frequencies only $\sim 180\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ apart, to the components of $\Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{b}}$. This is more likely than the earlier assignment [7] to $\Gamma_{8}$ and $\Gamma_{6}$ the latter level being calculated for appropriate parameter sets $1000-1200\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ higher than $\Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{b}}$. The $\Gamma_{6}(^4 \mathrm{~T}_{1 \mathrm{~g}})$ is rather a candidate for an assignment to the (a) spectrum of region A (see above). Since there are no other electronic states close to $\Gamma_{8}^{\mathrm{b}}$ the band series III must be explained by a combination with a lattice vibration, i.e. by $v_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}(\mathrm{I})+v_{\mathrm{u}}+n v_{1}(\alpha_{1 \mathrm{~g}})+v_{1}$ where $v_{\mathrm{u}}$ refers to one of the promoting modes $\tau_{1u}(v_{3}), \tau_{1u}(v_{4})$ and $\tau_{2u}(v_{6})$ and $v_{1}$ is, for example, due to the $\tau_{2g}$ lattice mode which for $\mathrm{K}_{2} \mathrm{SnCl}_{6}$ is reported at $81\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ [25] corresponding to the $80-85\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ frequency difference of series III to series I.

The quality of the spectrum in the B-region also allows for carrying out a Franck-Condon analysis in order to determine the geometric distortion of the $\mathrm{IrCl}_{6}^{2-}$ nuclear framework in the excited state. Using molecular distributions $I(n, \beta_{1}, \Delta Q_{1})$ [26] for adapting to band intensities in the progression 1, 10, 19, 27, 37 of series I for members $n=0,1,...,4$, respectively, the frequency relation between the ground and excited state of the totally symmetric vibration is $\beta_{1}=v_{1}/v_{1}'=1.08$ and the shift of potential energy curve is calculated $\Delta Q_{1}=8.4\ \mathrm{pm}$ which corresponds to a metal-ligand bond length change of $3.4\ \mathrm{pm}$.

pounds (2EHS and 3EHS hosts) exhibit some drastic changes becoming particularly evident in the splitting of the A-region. For 3EHS:Ir two separate bands exhibiting different resolution of vibrational fine structure are obtained; only one of them shows a pronounced polarization effect (Fig. 4). Differences in the spectra can be attributed to their crystal structures. While the space group of 1EHS is P$\overline{3}$m1 with the Sn ion on a $D_{3d}$ site, the others have P2$_{1}$/n structure with $C_{i}$ sites for Sn (factor group $D_{2h}$) producing ligand fields which according to bond distances and other bond properties are close to tetragonal (approximate $D_{4h}$) symmetry [27-29]. For the 3EHS spectrum a strong tetragonal component is expected due to two bifurcated 2Cl$\cdots$H-NEt$_{3}$ hydrogen bonds in trans position approximately forming a common plane [29]. From these symmetry properties the spectra can be understood.

In the C-region 5-6 peaks can be distinguished which have their origins from spin-orbit components $\Gamma_{6}$ and $\Gamma_{8}$ of the $^{2}T_{1u}$ charge transfer state. While in $O_{h}$ symmetry the transition into $\Gamma_{6}$ is forbidden, it obtains oscillator strength on reduction to trigonal or tetragonal symmetry. Since from MCD measurements [3,6] we know that $\Gamma_{6}$ is higher in energy than $\Gamma_{8}$ the peaks of the C spectra may be assigned to the level series (increasing in energy)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text { 1EHS: } & \operatorname{Ir}\left(\mathrm{D}_{3 \mathrm{~d}}\right) \quad \Gamma_{5}, \Gamma_{4}\left(\Gamma_{8} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right), \Gamma_{4}\left(\Gamma_{6} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right), \Gamma_{4}+v_{1}, \Gamma_{4}+2 v_{1} \\
\text { (C) } 2 \mathrm{EHS}: & \operatorname{Ir}\left(\mathrm{D}_{4 \mathrm{~h}}\right) \quad \Gamma_{7}, \Gamma_{6}\left(\Gamma_{8} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right), \Gamma_{6}\left(\Gamma_{6} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right), \Gamma_{6}+v_{1}, \Gamma_{6}+2 v_{1}, \Gamma_{6}+3 v_{1} \\
\text { 3EHS: } & \operatorname{Ir}\left(\mathrm{D}_{4 \mathrm{~h}}\right) \quad \Gamma_{7}, \Gamma_{6}\left(\Gamma_{8} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right), \Gamma_{6}\left(\Gamma_{6} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right), \Gamma_{6}+v_{2}, \Gamma_{6}+2 v_{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The level order $\Gamma_{5}<\Gamma_{4}$ of $D_{3 d}$ components of $\Gamma_{8}(O_{h})$ results from the assignment proposed for $(MeNH_{3})_{2} SnCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ [5], the corresponding sequence for the $D_{4 h}$ compounds is based on the polarized spectra of Fig. 4 in which peak 2 assigned to $\Gamma_{6}(\Gamma_{8} O_{h})$ is almost completely missing in $a$ polarization (for a $\Gamma_{7} \to \Gamma_{6}$ transition only the $x, y$ electric field vector components contribute in $D_{4 h}$ symmetry). Peak 3 also assigned to $\Gamma_{6}$ is weakened as well, as seen from the oscillator strengths of all C-region bands decreasing substantially in this polarization. Similar information from the corresponding methyl ammonium salt cannot be obtained since it crystallizes in a cubic lattice [5].

It is worth noting that in the case of 3EHS:Ir the progression is observed in $v_{2}=273 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ quanta belonging to $\varepsilon_{g}$ in $O_{h}$ notation, and not in $v_{1}(\alpha_{1 g})=305-316 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ as for the other systems indicating the presence of a large low symmetry If component decomposing the $\varepsilon_{g}$ stretching vibration into $\alpha_{1 g}$ and $\beta_{1 g}$ of $D_{4 h}$, the totally symmetric mode of which combining with the electronic transition more effectively than the $\alpha_{1 g}$ mode of $v_{1}$.

Host lattice effects are even more apparent in the A-region. As observed for the corresponding methyl compounds [5] the absorptions in this region are spread out over a wider frequency range compared to the spectra of $K_{2} SnCl_{6}$ and $Cs_{2} ZrCl_{6}$ hosts. Obviously the splitting of $^{2} T_{2 u}$ due to larger symmetry distortion and spin-orbit coupling, the latter not being quenched by dynamic Jahn-Teller activities (Ham effect) as in the octahedral compound [11,12], is increased in the present systems. The spectra are, however, not as well resolved. Nevertheless, in the spectrum of 2EHS:Ir a progression by $v_{1}(\alpha_{1 g})=312 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ can be detected and for 1EHS:Ir at least two superimposed progressions are present the origins of which could not be reliably assigned. The spectrum of 3EHS:Ir on the other hand is much better resolved. Its A-region has two separate bands I and II quite similar to those obtained for $Cs_{2} ZrBr_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ which have been assigned to the $\Gamma_{7}$ (lower energy) and $\Gamma_{8}$ components of $^{2} T_{2 u}$ due to the large spin-orbit coupling effects imposed on by the bromide ligands which dominate possible Jahn-Teller forces [20,30]. Equal conditions are present in our system, here it is the largely distorted crystal field which neutralizes the Ham effect acting on the $^{2} T_{2 u}$ state. The AI spectrum recorded at resolution higher than is illustrated in the overall spectrum of Fig. 4 can be interpreted by two progressions

$$
\text { (AI) } \begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{7}\left(\Gamma_{8} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right) \quad v_{n} & =v_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}\left(\Gamma_{7}\right)+n v_{1}\left(\alpha_{1 \mathrm{~g}}\right) \quad n=0,1, \ldots, 5 \\
& \text { where } v_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}\left(\Gamma_{7}\right)=23548 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} v_{1} \sim 306 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} \\
\Gamma_{6}\left(\Gamma_{8} \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{h}}\right) v_{m} & =v_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}\left(\Gamma_{6}\right)+m v_{2}\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}}\right) \quad m=0,1, \ldots, 3 \text { (weaker) } \\
& \text { where } v_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}\left(\Gamma_{6}\right)=23690 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} v_{2} \sim 270 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The zero phonon transitions are determined by a Franck-Condon analysis which allows for identifying individual

members of the progression [26]. It is noticed that the progression assigned to $\Gamma_{6}$ proceeds with $v_{2}(\varepsilon_{g})$ quanta as does the progression belonging to tetrahedral $\Gamma_{6}(^{2} ~T_{1 u})$ in the C-region of the same compound (see above). The AII band at $22600 ~cm^{-1}$ assigned to $\Gamma_{7}(\Gamma_{7} O_{h})$ exhibits only a very weak vibrational structure which can be identified with the v(ag) mode. Quite similar band shapes have been found in the absorption spectrum of trans- $(Me_{4} ~N)_{2}[IrF_{2} Cl_{4}](D_{4 ~h})$ : the short wave length band in the A-region exhibits vibrational fine structure and the peak at longer wave length does not[32].

The AII band does not show a significant polarization effect (cf. Fig. 4) which is in accordance to assigning this absorption to $\Gamma_{7} \to \Gamma_{7}(D_{4 ~h})$ being allowed for electric dipole transition in $x, y, z$ direction of the octahedron. The AI band assigned to transitions into $\Gamma_{6}$ and $\Gamma_{7}$ (both $D_{4 ~h}$ ) is shifted in $a$ polarization slightly to longer wave length indicating that the higher transition is $\Gamma_{7} \to \Gamma_{6}$ which in $D_{4 ~h}$ symmetry is only allowed in $x, y$ coordinates. This agrees with the assignment made above on the basis of vibronic coupling arguments. Since the complex octahedra are not well aligned to the crystallographic axes [29] the polarization effects cannot be complete. For an irradiation perpendicular to the $(10 \overline{1})$ plane one can, however, find electric field vector positions for optimal polarizations where the $z$ components( $D_{4}$ axis) of the approximate octahedra are maximal (polarization $a$ ) and perpendicular to that where the $x$ -, $y$ -components are large (polarization $b$ ). With the proposed assignments the polarized spectra can be understood, except for the larger overall intensity of the AI band in polarization $a$ which should be more forbidden in $z$ direction. An explanation may be given by the actual site symmetry of $Ir^{4+}$  which in reality is lower than $D_{4 ~h}$ .

In the B-region the spectra of EHS:Ir compounds are by far less well resolved compared to $K_{2} SnCl_{6}: Ir^{4+}$ . The progressions observed can, however, be assigned to $\alpha_{1 ~g}$ vibrational modes, the best resolved spectrum of 3EHS:Ir being superimposed by two progressions, one arising from $\tau_{2 u}(v_{6})$ and $\tau_{1 u}(v_{4})$ (similar in frequency), the other from $\tau_{1 u}(v_{3})$  promoting modes. The band pattern indicates that due to the higher distortion to lower symmetry the B-spectrum presumably originates from only one lf state. Progressions arising from other levels are buried under the large ctf bands.

## 4. Conclusions
The optical spectra obtained at high resolution, in part using polarized light, allow for reliable assignments to ctf and If levels strongly coupled to vibrational modes. From the interpretation we conclude in particular that (1) the large Ham effect operative on the $^{2} ~T_{2 u}$ level in octahedral symmetry giving rise to the spectrum in the A-region $[11,12]$ is neutralized if the host lattice imposes larger fields of low symmetry onto the chromophore, (2) the spectrum in the B-region is almost entirely due to lf transitions borrowing intensity from ctf transitions and that (3) the vibrational fine structure of low symmetry systems differs significantly from that of the cubic case.
