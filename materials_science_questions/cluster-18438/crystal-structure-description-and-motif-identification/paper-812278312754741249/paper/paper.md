# Synthesis and X-ray structure of $[\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{O}^{+} \cdot 18$-crown-6] $[\mathrm{Br}-\mathrm{Br}-\mathrm{Br}^{-}]$; a compound containing both $\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{O}^{+}$and a linear and symmetrical $\mathrm{Br}_{3}^{-}$ion crystallized from aromatic solution

Jerry L. Atwood, $^{*, 1}$ Peter C. Junk, $^{1}$ Michael T. May, $^{1}$ and Kerry D. Robinson $^{1}$

Received September 29, 1993

$[\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{O}^{+} \cdot 18$-crown-6]$[\mathrm{Br}-\mathrm{Br}-\mathrm{Br}^{-}]$crystallizes in the triclinic space group $\mathrm{P} \overline{1}$ with $a=6.105(1)$, $b=8.658(1)$, $c=11.072(1)\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, $\alpha=71.35(1)$, $\beta=77.58(1)$, $\gamma=71.09(1)^{\circ}$, and $D_{\mathrm{c}}=1.68$ $\mathrm{g} \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ for $Z=1$. Refinement based on 1112 observed reflections led to $R=0.044$. The cation exhibits a planar oxonium ion bonded symmetrically to the 18-crown-6 (O(oxonium) resides 0.092 $\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ out of the plane of the crown ether oxygen atoms). The anion also resides on a center of inversion with $\mathrm{Br}-\mathrm{Br}=2.530(1)\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$.

## Introduction

The reaction of 18-crown-6, $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ and gaseous hydrogen halides in aromatic solution leads to interesting species not previously obtained from aromatic solvents, (e.g. Eq. 1) (Atwood et al., 1987, 1990, 1991).

$$
\begin{matrix}
\text{18-crown-6} + \text{HCl} & \xrightarrow[\text{toluene}]{\text{1 eq. } \mathrm{H_2O}} & [\mathrm{H_3O^+ \cdot 18\text{-crown-6}}][\text{Cl}-\text{H}-\text{Cl}] \\
& \xrightarrow[\phantom{\text{toluene}}]{\text{2 eq. } \mathrm{H_2O}} & [\mathrm{H_3O^+ \cdot 18\text{-crown-6}}][(\mathrm{H_5O_2^+})(\text{Cl}^-)_2] \\
& & \text{(Eq. 1)}
\end{matrix}
$$

As part of our continuing study of the oxonium-based liquid clathrate systems, we now report a complex containing the $[\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{O}^{+} \cdot 18$-crown-6] ion which crystallizes from aromatic solvent with the tribromide $(\mathrm{Br}_{3}^{-})$ ion.

Interest in the $\mathrm{Br}_{3}^{-}$ ion has centered predominantly on the symmetry of the ion in the solid state (Novoa et al., 1988). Typical syntheses of this ion involve treatment of elemental bromine with $\mathrm{HBr}$. Our synthesis is based on direct treatment of 18-crown-6 with elemental $\mathrm{Br}_{2}$.

We report herein the synthesis of a liquid clathrate (Atwood, 1984, 1986, 1988) of composition $[\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{O}^{+} \cdot 18$-crown-6]$[\mathrm{Br}_{3}^{-}] \cdot 3.8 \mathrm{C}_{7} \mathrm{H}_{8}$ from which crystals of the title compound were deposited.

## Experimental

A $1.32 \mathrm{~g}(0.005 \mathrm{~mol})$ sample of 18-crown-6 was moistened with $0.09 \mathrm{ml}(0.005 \mathrm{~mol})$ of distilled water, and then approx. $30 \mathrm{ml}$ of toluene was added. Upon dissolution of the crown ether, $0.25 \mathrm{ml}$ of $\mathrm{Br}_{2}(\mathrm{l})(0.005$ mol) was added dropwise with rapid stirring resulting in the formation of a dark solution. Over a 10-min period, a deep orange-colored liquid clathrate settled from the slightly colored upper layer. The liquid clathrate layer had the composition $[\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{O}^{+} \cdot 18$-crown-6]$[\mathrm{Br}_{3}^{-}] \cdot 3.8$ $\mathrm{C}_{7} \mathrm{H}_{8}$, as determined by $^{1} \mathrm{H}$ NMR. From the liquid clathrate layer, orange-colored crystals of the title complex deposited over a $72 \mathrm{~h}$ period.

Single crystals of $[\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{O}^{+} \cdot 18$-crown-6]$[\mathrm{Br}_{3}^{-}]$ were sealed in thin-walled glass capillaries. Final lattice parameters as determined from the least-squares refinement of the angular settings of 24 high-angle reflections $(2 \theta>30^{\circ})$ accurately centered on an Enraf-Nonius CAD4 diffractometer are given in Table 1. ## Discussion

The title compound for this study, $[H_3O^+ \cdot 18$-crown-6]$[Br_3^-]$, was synthesized by means of Equation 2.
$$
\begin{align}
\text{18-crown-6} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{toluene}} [\text{H}_3\text{O}^+ \cdot \text{18-crown-6}][\text{Br}_3^-] \tag{Eq. 2}
\end{align}
$$

The O(oxonium) resides $0.092\ \mathring{A}$ out of the plane defined by the crown ether oxygens. This suggests the oxonium ion is nearly *planar*, as in a previously established planar $H_{3}O^{+}$ ion where the oxonium ion resided 0.10 Å out of the plane of the crown (Behr *et al.*, 1982). *Pyramidal* geometry has been established for the $H_{3}O^{+}$ ion where the oxonium ion sits $0.61\ \mathring{A}$ out of the plane of the crown oxygen atoms (Shoemaker *et al.*, 1986). *Planar vs pyramidal* geometry for the $H_{3}O^{+}$ ion has been discussed previously for $[H_{3}O^{+}\cdot 18-crown-6]_{2}\cdot [(UO_{2}(NO_{3})_{2})_{2}C_{2}O_{4}]$ (Rogers *et al.*, 1991).

The $[Br-Br-Br]^{-}$ anion sits on a center of inversion which implies a symmetrical, linear polyhalide structure (see Fig. 2). The $Br-Br$ bond length is $2.530(1)\mathring{A}$, approximately $0.25\ \mathring{A}$ longer than in elemental $Br_{2}$ (CRC Handbook and Physics, 1986). There are examples of $Br_{3}^{-}$ ions which exhibit both symmetrical and non-symmetrical forms, depending on the interactions with the cation and the environment in which it exists. The $Br_{3}^{-}$ ion in $[NBu_{4}^{+}][Br_{3}^{-}]$ is also symmetrical with similar $Br-Br$ bond lengths $(2.536(2)\mathring{A})$ to the title compound (Coleman *et al.*, 1990). In contrast, the $Br_{3}^{-}$ ion in $[PBr_{4}^{+}][Br_{3}^{-}]$ has nonsymmetric bond lengths of $2.39(1)$ and $2.91(1)\mathring{A}$ and a $Br-Br-Br$ angle of $177.3(2)^{\circ}$ (Breneman and Willett, 1967).

Factors which tend to influence the geometry of $X_{3}^{-}$ ions include cation size, with symmetrical ions being favored by the presence of large cations (Rundle, 1961; Migchelson and Vos, 1967). The presence of a large cation with a relatively weak crystal field causes minimal disturbance of the anion resulting in a centrosymmetrical structure, as in the present case. For smaller cations, the crystal field experienced by the anion is non-symmetrical, resulting in a non-symmetrical structure. The effect of environmental forces, where pressure of the crystal environment stabilizes one geometry over the other was also proposed at one time (Slater, 1959).
