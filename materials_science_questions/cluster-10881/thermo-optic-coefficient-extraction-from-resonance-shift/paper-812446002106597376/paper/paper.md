# A SIMPLE MODEL FOR EVALUATION OF REFRACTIVE INDICES OF SOME BINARY AND TERNARY MIXED CRYSTALS

D. K. GHOSH, L. K. SAMANTA and G. C. BHAR
Department of Physics, Burdwan University, Burdwan 713104, India

(Received 21 October 1983; accepted in revised form 19 November 1983)

Abstract-In view of the importance of binary and ternary mixed crystals for heterojunction LED and solar-cell applications, a method for evaluating the high-frequency refractive indices of such crystals is presented using a modified Penn-gap model. The theoretical predictions agree quite well with the experimental results. The model is also applied to evaluate the temperature and pressure coefficients of refractive indices of binary and ternary crystals. The close agreement between the calculated and experimental values justifies the validity of the approach.

## INTRODUCTION

Dielectric constant and refractive index are among the most useful properties of the semiconductor and are important for both fundamental and applied considerations. Knowledge of the refractive indices of binaries (III-V & II-VI) and their mixed alloy compounds is essential for designing heterostructure lasers, $^{(1)}$ those of ternary chalcopyrites (II-IV-V₂ & I-III-VI₂)and their mixed compounds, together with a suitable binary material as a window, are required in opto-electronic devices $^{(2)}$ and solar-cell applications, $^{(3)}$ while those of quaternary mixed crystals are necessary for use in low-loss and low-dispersion optical fibre communications. $^{(4,5)}$ Although high-frequency refractive indices of many compounds of different groups have been reported, no significant information is available for their mixed ternary and quaternary compounds. In view of the technological importance of and the lack of experimental data for many of the mixed crystals, we undertake to analyse the refractive index behaviour and its dependence on various physical parameters of the system. The purpose of the present paper is to analyse the high-frequency refractive indices of materials of different groups of semiconductors, to establish a useful relation amongst the physically-meaningful quantities involved and to evaluate the same for more complex mixed ternary and quaternary semiconductors which are useful in so many opto-electronic devices.

## THEORY AND METHOD OF EVALUATION

The high-frequency dielectric constant of a semiconductor is explicitly dependent on the valence-electron plasmon frequency, the bandgap and Fermi energies. The refractive index vs energy bandgap interrelation is often used to assess the refractive index of a comparatively new material developed by changing composition. Using the concept of structural dependence of energy level, Moss $^{(6)}$ presented a simple empirical relation connecting bandgap energy and the high frequency refractive index. Dienne $^{(7)}$ and Ravindra et al., $^{(8)}$ however, suggested different relations between the two quantities but neither of these relations have been found to explain satisfactorily the high-frequency refractive index for very-low- and very-high-bandgap materials. In contrast to the ideas of the above authors, this paper takes a different approach to the problem by considering the band structural and quantum-dielectric formulations of Penn $^{(9)}$ and Van Vechten, $^{(10)}$ respectively. Introducing $A$ as the contribution from the valence electrons, and $B$ as a constant additive to the lowest bandgap $E_{\mathrm{g}}$, the expression for the high-frequency refractive index is written as

$$
n^{2}-1=\frac{A}{\left(E_{\mathrm{g}}+B\right)^{2}}, \tag{1}
$$

where the term $(E_{\mathrm{g}}+B)$ refers to an appropriate average energy gap of the material. Here the parameter $A$ may be assigned to take care of the valence-electron plasmon energy, the Fermi energy

The parameters $A$ and $B$ are determined by applying relation (1) to a group of materials having experimentally-known bandgap and refractive index. We have first considered the binary III-V group of compounds and the best-fit values of $A$ and $B$ are shown in Fig. 1 where they have been plotted against the corresponding bandgap energies. At this stage we tried to find some correlations between the bandgap energy $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ and the parameters $A$ and $B$. The solid lines have been drawn to indicate the nature of variation. One can clearly see that while the parameter $A$ increases monotonically with increasing bandgap, the parameter $B$ shows very little or no variation with bandgap. The lower-bandgap compound may thus be considered to have smaller contributions from $A$ and $B$. The same procedure has been followed for the compounds of the II-VI family and the best-fit values of $A$ and $B$ are also shown in Fig. 1. Here, a similar trend is observed for parameters $A$ and $B$. Next, relation (1) is applied to the ternary chalcopyrite crystals and the best-fit values of $A$ and $B$ are plotted in Fig. 2. It is to be noted here that again the parameter $B$ shows no variation with $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ and is constant for a particular group of compounds. The characteristic variation of $A$ and $B$ with $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ within a group can be represented in the form $y=mx+c$. The constants $m$ and $c$ are evaluated from Figs 1 and 2 are shown in Table 1 for all the groups of compounds studied. Having identified parameter $A$ as being strongly dependent on $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ it would be better to rewrite equation (1) in the form

$$
n^{2}-1=\frac{m E_{\mathrm{g}}+c}{\left(E_{\mathrm{g}}+B\right)^{2}},\tag{1a}

Furthermore, according to this model the temperature and pressure coefficients of the refractive index are given by the relations:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} n}{\mathrm{~d} T}=\frac{n^{2}-1}{2 n}\left(\frac{m}{m E_{\mathrm{g}}+c} \frac{\mathrm{d} E_{\mathrm{g}}}{\mathrm{d} T}-\frac{2}{E_{\mathrm{g}}+B} \frac{\mathrm{d} E_{\mathrm{g}}}{\mathrm{d} T}\right)
\tag{2}
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} n}{\mathrm{~d} P}=\frac{n^{2}-1}{2 n}\left(\frac{m}{m E_{\mathrm{g}}+c} \frac{\mathrm{d} E_{\mathrm{g}}}{\mathrm{d} P}-\frac{2}{E_{\mathrm{g}}+B} \frac{\mathrm{d} E_{\mathrm{g}}}{\mathrm{d} P}\right),
\tag{3}
$$

respectively, where
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} E_{\mathrm{g}}}{\mathrm{d} T} \text { and } \frac{\mathrm{d} E_{\mathrm{g}}}{\mathrm{d} P}
$$
are the temperature and pressure coefficients of the bandgap and we have assumed the constants $m$ and $c$ to be independent of temperature and pressure. Using the constants from Table 1 and experimental values of the temperature and pressure coefficients of the bandgap, the corresponding coefficients of the refractive index can also be evaluated in terms of $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ alone.

An attempt has also been made to find a correlation of $B$ with other physical parameters. The best-fit values of $B$ for different groups of crystals are found to maintain a correlation with the group ionicities. This is shown in Fig. 3 where group $B$ values are plotted against the corresponding group ionicities. The general trend depicts that $B$ should increase with increasing ionicity and should thereby reduce the absolute value of the refractive index following equation (1). This is actually observed: $^{(2.11)}$ the more ionic II-VI and I-III-VI$_{2}$ groups of materials possess a lower value of refractive index than the III-V and II-IV-V$_{2}$ groups of crystals.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

The results of our calculations of high-frequency refractive indices-justifying our approach for some binary (III-V & II-VI), ternary (II-IV-V$_{2}$ & I-III-VI$_{2}$) and mixed versions of both crystals are presented in Tables 2 and 3, respectively. The values of the constants determining $A$ and $B$ were taken from Table 1. In Table 2, the refractive indices of some representatives of binary mixtures in anion (e.g. $\mathrm{GaP}_{x} \mathrm{As}_{1-x}$) and cation (e.g. $\mathrm{Ga}_{x} \mathrm{Al}_{1-x} \mathrm{As}$) variations in the III-V compounds, and also similar mixtures $(\mathrm{CdS}_{x} \mathrm{Se}_{1-x}$ and $\mathrm{Cd}_{x} \mathrm{Hg}_{1-x} \mathrm{Te})$ in the II-VI compounds, have been evaluated and compared with available experimental data. For the $\mathrm{CdS}_{x} \mathrm{Se}_{1-x}$ mixed crystal we have taken a constant value of $B=5.4$ and this is consistent-evident from study of Fig. 1, wherein the best-fit $B$ values of the crystals CdS and CdSe do not fall on the line. Here, in the absence of any experimental data of intermediate compounds, we have assumed linear variation of $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ with composition $x$. The calculated refractive indices of all the compounds show (Table 2) very close agreement with the measured values within experimental uncertainties. Table 3 compares the calculated refractive indices with the experimental values of some representatives of ternary mixtures in cation variation, such as in $\mathrm{AgGa}_{x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x} \mathrm{S}_{2}$ in the I-III-VI$_{2}$ group, and in anion variation, such as quaternary $\mathrm{CdGe}(\mathrm{P}_{x} \mathrm{As}_{1-x})_{2}$ in II-IV-V$_{2}$ compounds, together with a more complex candidate of simultaneous anion and cation variation, such as in $\mathrm{In}_{1-x} \mathrm{Ga}_{x} \mathrm{As}_{y} \mathrm{P}_{1-y}$, in the III-V compounds. The results show quite good agreement with the experimental data. It should be emphasized in this respect that while considering mixed crystals in a group, the values of the constants for the group remain unchanged irrespective of changes in cationic and/or anionic components.

Coming back to the quaternary compound $\mathrm{In}_{1-x} \mathrm{Ga}_{x} \mathrm{As}_{y} \mathrm{P}_{1-y}$, it would be interesting to compare the parameter $A$ over the entire range of composition, the refractive index of which was measured by Chandra et al. $^{(12)}$ Figure 4 shows such a 3-D representation of $A$ values for $0 \leqslant y \leqslant 1$ and $x=0.46 y$. For this we have taken a constant $B$ value of 3.35, consistent with the average $B$ value of the corresponding binaries. The corners of the figure represent the III-V binaries and the solid lines along the surfaces extending from InP to $\mathrm{In}_{0.54} \mathrm{Ga}_{0.46} \mathrm{As}$ (the height of the shaded plane) are the locus of $A$ for the alloy composition concerned. One can obtain a reasonably reliable estimate of high-frequency refractive index over the entire range of compositions by determining the characteristic constants from Fig. 4.

## CONCLUSIONS

A method for estimating the high-frequency refractive index has been proposed by introducing two parameters from elemental semiconductors to the entire range of alloy compositions of mixed ternary and quaternary semiconductors. The model has been applied for the entire range of materials having bandgaps ranging from negative to large positive values. The values predicted agree well with the experiments, the discrepancies being only a few percent. The most interesting feature is that the same model constants can be used for different types of mixed crystals within a group. The reasonable agreement between the predicted temperature and pressure coefficients of refractive index with the experimental values justifies the approach.
