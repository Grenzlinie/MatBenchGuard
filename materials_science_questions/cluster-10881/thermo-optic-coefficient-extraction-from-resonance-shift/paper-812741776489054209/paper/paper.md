# Ellipsometric Investigations of Electronic Polarizability and Thermo-optic Coefficients of $Z_x$MoO₃ ($Z$ = H⁺, Li⁺) Bronzes

ZAHID HUSSAIN ${ }^{1,2}$

1.—Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Imperial College of Science Technology and Medicine, Exhibition Road, London SW7 2BT, UK. 2.—e-mail: hzzhussain@aol.com

We have measured mass densities, thermo-optic coefficients, and electronic polarizability of $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ thin films and of $\mathrm{H}_{x} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{x} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ bronze thin films over the temperature range from $100 \mathrm{~K}$ to $373 \mathrm{~K}$ using ellipsometry. The mass densities of $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ and molybdenum bronzes with different concentrations $(x)$ changed from $3.76 \mathrm{~g} / \mathrm{cm}^{3}$ to $2.45 \mathrm{~g} / \mathrm{cm}^{3}$ during the heating and cooling cycles. Thermo-optic coefficients (TOCs) or $\mathrm{d} n / \mathrm{d} T$ and $\mathrm{d} k / \mathrm{d} T$ values of the molybdenum bronzes were found to have anomalous dispersion in the heating cycles over the range 295–373 K. On the other hand, the values of $\mathrm{d} n / \mathrm{d} T$ for $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ thin films and for $Z_{x} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ bronzes were found to be negative and positive, respectively over the temperature range $100-295 \mathrm{~K}$ and were on the order of $10^{-4} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}$. The respective values of $\mathrm{d} k / \mathrm{d} T$ were found to be positive and negative and were on the order of $10^{-4} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}$ in the same temperature range. A little increase in the values of TOCs for the molybdenum bronzes is due to small increase in electronic polarizability, which was calculated in the range from 8.20 to $8.22 \times 10^{-24} \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$ over the temperature range $100-373 \mathrm{~K}$. All these small values are due to creation of more amorphousness in the $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ structure during the cooling cycles. The $n$ and $k$ data and TOCs values of molybdenum bronzes are explained using analytical model. The reported ellipsometric data is also interpreted in terms of small-polarons and bipolarons using configurational coordinate model.

**Key words:** Optoelectronics, electro-optical materials, electronic materials, optical materials, optical properties, semiconductors, electrochromic display, deposition by evaporation, thin films

## INTRODUCTION

$\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ is a potential material because of its wide range of stoichiometry with different interesting behaviours. The substoichiometry films $\mathrm{MoO}_{3-x}$ with oxygen deficient contain excess metal atoms which act as doping centres, and these centres control the electrical and optical film properties. ${ }^{1,2} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ is considered a promising chromogenic/electrochromic material, as it exhibits photoelectric and gasochromic effects for the development of electrochromic display devices, ${ }^{2-4}$ electronic display devices, ${ }^{3-5}$ optical switching coatings ${ }^{6,7}$ and for smart window technology. ${ }^{8-10} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ finds application as a cathode material in the development of high-energy density solid state microbatteries. ${ }^{11-13}$ These have also been put into service in automobile and in information display technology. ${ }^{14-16}$ Also, these transition metal oxides raised much attention owing to their numerous applications, such as in catalysis $^{17,18}$ and gas sensors. ${ }^{19,20}$ Hydrogen bronzes of $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ are metastable states; ${ }^{4,21}$ they can further be reduced via formation of water to lower oxides for gasochromic and related applications. ${ }^{3,21} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ has also capacity to undergo reversible lithium interaction/extraction at ambient temperature which are of great technical interest as cathode materials for secondary lithium batteries. ${ }^{1,2,22}$ It is also studied

Molybdenum thin films were prepared on unheated substrate by thermal evaporation. HₓMoO₃ and LiₓMoO₃ bronze thin films were also prepared with different concentrations (x). ## ELLIPSOMETRY

For ellipsometric measurements, a manual ellipsometer with schematic PCWSWA arrangement [of polarizer, compensator (quarter-wave plate), silica optical window (before reflection), sample, silica optical window (after reflection), and analyser], including a vacuum cryostat for heating and cooling the samples is built up with a monochromatic light source (He-Ne laser with wavelength of 0.633 μm) and with single incidence angle (set at 60°). The setup and procedure have been described in detail elsewhere.²⁶³³ In this work, the three-phase film/substrate/ambient model, all isotropic, is used, which, in general, in terms of two ellipsometric measured angles (Ψ and Δ), can be mathematically represented by³⁴³⁵

$$
\rho(n_{0},n_{\mathrm{f}},k_{\mathrm{f}},n_{\mathrm{s}},d,\phi,\lambda)=\frac{|r_{\mathrm{p}}|}{|r_{\mathrm{s}}|}e^{i(\delta_{\mathrm{p}}-\delta_{\mathrm{s}})}\qquad(1)
$$

where

$$
\tan\Psi=\frac{|r_{\mathrm{p}}|}{|r_{\mathrm{s}}|}=|\rho|,\qquad(2)
$$

and

$$
\Delta=\delta_{\mathrm{p}}-\delta_{\mathrm{s}}.\qquad(3)
$$

It should be noted that Eq. 1 is the fundamental equation of ellipsometry, which measures the ratio of complex Fresnel reflection coefficients $r_{\mathrm{p}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{s}}$ between the p (parallel) and s (normal) polarization states. In Eqs. 2 and 3, $\tan\Psi$ (or $|\rho|$) is the amplitude ratio and Δ the relative phase shift between p and s components. Experimentally, we measure Ψ and Δ, which are the conventional ellipsometric angles associated with the angles of optical components P (polarizer), C (quarter-wave plate), and A (analyzer) of the manual nulling ellipsometer.

In Eq. 1 $n_{0},n_{\mathrm{f}},n_{\mathrm{s}}$ are the real refractive indices of the air, film, substrate, respectively, and $k_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the extinction coefficient of the film; $d,\phi,$ and $\lambda$, are the thickness of the film, angle of incidence, wavelength, respectively.

It should be noted that the ellipsometry equation 1 is a quantic polynomial equation, which can't be solved analytically for an isotropic three-phase film/substrate/ambient model. Instead, numerical models based on root-finding algorithms (from the literature)³⁶³⁷ can be used for this polynomial Eq. 1 in the case of an absorbing film on an absorbing substrate or for the case when the substrate is a dielectric with an absorbing film. Most optical models (numerical algorithms) use iteration (or regression) to find the best match between model and experiment. In our case, we have developed Fortran 77 with an iteration scheme (by the assistance of McCrackin package³⁸) to compute the values of $n_{\mathrm{f}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{f}}$ for different values of thickness (d), determined by an independent technique (Talysurf technique).³²³⁹

During the iterative procedure, the values of $n_{\mathrm{f}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{f}}$ (for every reported bronze thin film with a known thickness) with the lowest MSE were found to be true values.

### Preparation of Specimens and Determination of $x$-Values

$\mathrm{Li}_{x} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ specimens were prepared in thin film form by thermal co-evaporation. $^{27,32}$ For each deposition, two substrates were inserted inside the vacuum chamber. For the ellipsometric work, 7059 glass plates were used, and ITO coated slides were used for the electrochromic studies. Both were in two different Mo-boats and were kept at a distance of $18 \mathrm{~cm} \pm 2$ from the two substrates. The vacuum base pressure was about $4 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{kPa}$. $\mathrm{MoO} 3$ powder was outgassed at $973 \mathrm{~K}$ for $30 \mathrm{~min}$, and Li metal was outgassed at $373 \mathrm{~K}$ for $12 \mathrm{~min}$. When the temperature of the $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ boat was raised to $1123 \mathrm{~K}$, the shutter was removed, and using liquid nitrogen, the base pressure was brought down to about $\sim 4 \times 10^{-8} \mathrm{kPa}$. The rate of deposition was noted to be about $13-14 \mathring{A}/\mathrm{s}$, resulting in a density of $\sim 3.76 \pm 0.05 \mathrm{~g}/\mathrm{cc}$. To form the lithium bronzes, the temperature of the virgin Li metal was raised up to melting point, and co-deposited with $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ on the same substrates. The film deposition rate was continuously monitored with a quartz crystal oscillator. The concentration of the inserted $\mathrm{Li}$ atoms was controlled either by raising the temperature of boat containing $\mathrm{Li}$, or by increasing the size of aperture of the concerned boat. A detailed account of the apparatus is given elsewhere. $^{40}$

Hydrogen molybdenum bronzes were formed by exposing $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ thin films to hydrogen plasma. The total operating pressure of the system was $8 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{kPa}$, prior to any formation of hydrogen bronze. In hydrogen gas, hydrogen molecular state was broken into atomic hydrogen using a microtron 200MKII microwave generator, and the resulting hydrogen plasma was bombarded on to $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ thin films. This gave hydrogen molybdenum bronzes of different concentrations depending on exposure time on both slides.

The thickness of each coloured film was measured by means of Taylor Hobson Talysurf with an accuracy to about $\pm 30 \mathring{A}$. These films have highly disordered microcrystalline structures of mean grain size of about $60 \mathring{A} \pm 10$, as estimated by electron diffraction analysis $^{32,40}$ and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) studies. $^{32,40}$ Evaporated $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ thin films were slightly blue having sub-stoichiometric composition, $\mathrm{MoO}_{3-y}(y<0.1),^{32,39,41}$ but here all the evaporated films are designated as $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ throughout the text. An analytical model for electrochemical titration was developed for the determination of $x$-values of $\mathrm{H}^{+}$or $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$in $Z_{x} \mathrm{MoO}_{3}\left(\mathrm{Z}=\mathrm{H}^{+}, \mathrm{Li}^{+}\right)$bronze thin films. The final expression can be rewritten as

$$
x=5.03 \times 10^{-15}\left(\frac{t_{\mathrm{f}}}{V_{\mathrm{f}}}\right),\qquad(4)
$$

where $V_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the bleached volume of each film under study, and $t_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the time for bleaching. Full details of the apparatus and the derivation of Eq. 4 are given elsewhere. $^{27,32}$ The overall extracted volume error was about $\pm 7\%$, which gave an absolute accuracy of $10\%$ in the determination of $x$-values.

### Refractive Index, Film Density, and Electronic Polarizability

The density of bulk material or of the film can be calculated from refractive index data using the following well known expression $^{63,64}$

$$
\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{f}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{b}}}=\frac{n_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}-1}{n_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}+2} \times \frac{n_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}+2}{n_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}-1}, \tag{5}
$$

where $\rho_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the average film density and $\rho_{\mathrm{b}}$ the bulk density; $n_{\mathrm{f}}$ denotes the refractive index of the film and $n_{\mathrm{b}}$ the refractive index of bulk. Inserting the values of $\rho_{\mathrm{f}}$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{b}}^{30,65}$ and $n_{\mathrm{f}}$ (from Table I) into Eq. 5, the value of bulk refractive index, $n_{\mathrm{b}}$ of $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ is calculated as 2.7 at $0.633\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ which agrees with other researchers. $^{66,67}$

Using the bulk values of $n_{\mathrm{b}}$ as 2.7 and $\rho_{\mathrm{b}}$ as $4.71\ \mathrm{g\ cm^{-3}}$, we can transform Eq. 5 into

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{f}}=6.96\left[\frac{n_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}-1}{n_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}+2}\right]. \tag{6}
$$

Next, the densities ($\rho_{\mathrm{f}}$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{b}}$) and the porosities ($f_{\mathrm{p}}$) of thin films are related by the equation

$$
f_{\mathrm{p}}=1-\rho_{\mathrm{f}} / \rho_{\mathrm{b}} \tag{7}
$$

The temperature dependent density and porosity data of $\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$, $\mathrm{H}_{x}\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$, and $\mathrm{Li}_{x}\mathrm{MoO}_{3}$ thin films were calculated from Eqs. 6 and 7 using reported ellipsometric data on these films over the temperature range 100–373 K and are displayed in Figs. 7 and 8. Next, we introduce the term polarizability of a material. The total polarizability of any material can be expressed as

$$
\alpha_{\text{tot}} = \alpha_{\text{e}} + \alpha_{\text{i}} + \alpha_{\text{d}}, \tag{8}
$$

which is the sum of electronic ($\alpha_{\text{e}}$), ionic ($\alpha_{\text{i}}$), and dipolar ($\alpha_{\text{d}}$) polarizabilities which determine the dynamical response of a bound system to external fields, and provide insight into a molecule's internal structure. In the region of visible light, the ionic polarizability ($\alpha_{\text{i}}$) of any material can be neglected.

Because $\text{MoO}_3$ thin film is a non-dipolar (ionic) material, so only the electronic polarizability ($\alpha_{\text{e}}$) is present in it over the visible spectral range. The refractive index ($n_{\text{f}}$) and film density ($\rho_{\text{f}}$) are related to electronic polarizability ($\alpha_{\text{e}}$) through the famous Lorentz-Lorenz relation, $^{72,73}$

$$
\frac{n_{\text{f}}^2 - 1}{n_{\text{f}}^2 + 2} = \left[\frac{4\pi}{3}N_{\text{A}}\frac{\rho_{\text{f}}}{M}\right]\alpha_{\text{e}}. \tag{9}
$$

Here, $N_{\text{A}}$ is the Avogadro's number and $M$ is the molecular weight of the material.

Inserting the values of $N_{\text{A}} = 6.023 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ and $M = 144\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$ into Eq. 9, the expression for electronic polarizability can be reshaped as

$$
\alpha_{\text{e}} = 5.711 \times \frac{n_{\text{f}}^2 - 1}{n_{\text{f}}^2 + 2} \times 1/\rho_{\text{f}}. \tag{10}
$$

Here, we interpret the data of TOCs from different perspectives. At 453 K most of the water is removed and $\text{MoO}_3$ starts losing oxygen and becomes a bit more substoichiometric material. It may have some irreversible structural modification, as can be perceived from the data. $^{77}$ Upon heating $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{Li}_x\text{MoO}_3$ bronzes, their ionic conductivity and porosity, both give rise to hydroxyl groups at the internal surfaces, and hence assist to increase proton mobility to facilitate more colouration. $^{61,78}$ As a consequence, there is an increase in $k$-values, as can be seen from Figs. 1, 2, and 3. This increase in the $k$-values is due to decrease in the TOCs because of creation of more porosity in the films, as can be seen in Figs. 7 and 8. According to the proton nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) result, $^{49}$ it was found that there was no special difference in the redistribution of the protons or lithium ions in the bronzes even after heating at 623 K (under vacuum at pressure of about $1.33 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{kPa}$). Therefore, heating causes no definite structural changes $^{79,80}$ in the films over the reported temperature range. The only advantage of heat-treatment is to reduce light scattering effects before utilizing of these sub-stoichiometric $\text{MoO}_3$ films and their bronzes for different technical applications.

It is also a universal fact that amorphous phase has larger charge density than a crystalline phase, because ion carriers move in amorphous phase faster and more easily than in crystalline phase. $^{29,30}$ As-grown film is always an amorphous or microcrystalline, which could transform to monoclinic/orthorhombic phase upon annealing at around 673 K. $^{81,82}$ As a result of it, the high porosity structure of $\text{MoO}_3$ might decrease to more compact film with greater values of electronic polarizability, but the process of hydrogen, lithium ion intercalation and deintercalation might become very difficult to occur in the crystallized $\text{MoO}_3$ thin film resulting in a tremendous reduction in the values of optical density and colouration efficiency.

Immediate increase in $k$ values (and decrease in n values), as shown in $\text{MoO}_3$, $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{Li}_x\text{MoO}_3$ data in the low temperature range (Figs. 4, 5, 6) are related with the distortion effects in the individual $\text{MoO}_6$ octahedra due to fluctuations in the TOCs. The swing in n or $k$ values may be correlated with the periodic lattice distortion, and may cause incommensurate charge density waves in heavily doped $\text{MoO}_3$, as normally observed in crystalline molybdenum bronzes. $^{83,84}$ Furthermore, Pauli type behaviour is absent; and there is no indication of Curie behaviour in this lower temperature range (relating to drastic decrease in conductivity) as found in crystalline bronzes. $^{57,85}$ The optical hystersis in the low temperature range (Figs. 5, 6) gives second order or weak first order transitions, and this may not be related with commensurate state.

## POLARONIC AND BIPOLARONIC EXCITATIONS AND TRANSITIONS

Blue colouration in $\text{MoO}_3$ bronze thin films is due to charge transfer either between single species ($\text{Mo}^{5+}-\text{Mo}^{6+}$) resulting small polarons, or between pair species $[(\text{Mo}^{5+}-\text{Mo}^{5+}) \rightarrow (\text{Mo}^{6+}-\text{Mo}^{6+})]$ forming bipolarons. $^{86,87}$ The colouration and the formation of bipolarons may also be due to charge transfer between pair species $(\text{Mo}^{+4}-\text{Mo}^{+4}) \rightarrow (\text{Mo}^{6+}-\text{Mo}^{6+})$ in the molybdenum bronze films. $^{86,88}$ It all depends upon the film preparation technique and the predominant temperature conditions. Bipolarons and small polarons may be distinguished from each other by the average number of optical phonons that contribute to develop polarization around the trapped electrons at single or pair sites. In the Mo-bronze, polaronic species ($\text{Mo}^{5+}$) are not detectable by electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR), but can be seen in the x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) experiments. $^{57}$ Bipolaronic (or polaronic) transition intensity increases with $x$, as has been verified by electron spectroscopy for chemical analysis (ESCA) experiments. $^{89,90}$

At room temperature or above, where the phonon density is very high, hopping or tunnelling of single polarons in a dynamic state is energetically more economized than in a static state. At much lower temperature where multiple phonons freeze out, bipolaron formation seems to be more favourable for optical (or electrical) transitions. Bipolaronic (or small polaronic) transitions from $\text{Mo}^{5+}$ (or $\text{Mo}^{4+}$) to $\text{Mo}^{6+}$ in the molybdenum bronzes, give rise to intensive broad absorption band peaked in the near infrared, and the

optical data can be explained by configurational co-ordinate model, as outlined in Fig. 11. All the absorption processes in the films can be illustrated through Franck-Condon transitions between the strongly localized potential states ($Q_\text{A}$), and the higher energetic (impurity) potential states ($Q_\text{B}$). Relaxed states ($Q_\text{A}$ and $Q_\text{B}$) correspond to minimum energy states of the lattice system. The relevant hopping energies are correlated to the optical transition energies ($h\nu$) by the following relations $^{90-92}$

$$
4E_\text{H}^\text{bi} = h\nu^\text{bi}, \tag{23}
$$

$$
4E_\text{H}^\text{si} = h\nu^\text{si}, \tag{24}
$$

and

$$
E_\text{H}^\text{bi} = 2E_\text{H}^\text{si}. \tag{25}
$$

where $E_\text{H}^\text{bi}$, $E_\text{H}^\text{si}$, $h\nu^\text{bi}$ and $h\nu^\text{si}$ are, respectively, the bipolaron hopping energy, small polaron hopping energy, bipolaron optical transition energy, and small polaron optical transition energy. Above the Debye temperature, small polaron (or bipolaron) hopping is activated by an optical phonon spectrum, and the activated energy is given by a relation $^{90,93}$

$$
E_\text{H} = E_\text{H}^\text{si} + \frac{1}{2}E_\text{D}, \tag{26}
$$

where $E_\text{D}$ is the disorder energy, also known as Anderson localization energy. $^{94}$ As the temperature is lowered, the phonon spectrum freezes out and the hopping activation energy gradually decreases. The value of $E_\text{D/2}$ is, however, much smaller than $E_\text{H}^\text{si}$ and can be neglected. At much lower than the Debye temperature, an acoustic phonon assisted hopping takes place, and the bipolaronic activation energy can be given by a relation $^{95}$

$$
E_\text{H}^\text{si} = \frac{1}{2}W_\text{D}. \tag{27}
$$

In Fig. 11, $2j'$ is the resonance band width which can be neglected for non-adiabatic (optically) hopping from $\text{Mo}^{5+}$ (or $\text{Mo}^{5+}$) species at the location, $Q_\text{A}$ to nearest higher energetic $\text{Mo}^{6+}$ species located at $Q_\text{B}$.

For low temperature experiments (in helium temperature range), either strong polaron-polaron coupling can exist during optical transitions, or dissociation of the bipolaron can occur during irradiation along the potential curves. Second possibility seems to be plausible, because it is confirmed by numerous theoretical arguments that polaron-polaron coupling cannot exist in a very low temperature range (helium temperature range) due to non-stability of spatial symmetric bipolaron. $^{96,97}$ So, an increase in the binding energy will make bipolaron formation unstable. $^{96,97}$

In the low temperature range, especially in the case of $\text{MoO}_3$, as Fig. 4 shows, $k$ gradually decreases. In this case, the increase in $\text{MoO}_3$ resistance values may be due to some entrapped donor levels, caused by some defects in relating to $\text{Mo}^{5+}$ states. Decrease in the conductivity at low temperature may also be related to electron correlation and coulomb interaction with neighbouring potentials, but certainly is not correlated with Curie type behaviour. $^{57,98}$ On the other side, the increase in the conductivity (in the case of Mo-bronzes) at low temperatures, is due to bipolaronic effect, and this notion has been attributed by several authors. $^{98-100}$ Activation energies and mobilities in the low temperature region are compatible with a hopping process. $^{99}$ Estimates of the extent of the electron wavefunctions indicate that hopping is probably between nearest neighbours. $^{101}$

The low temperature data (Figs. 4, 5, 6) suggests that in highly resistive $\text{MoO}_3$ bronze films, conduction takes place by variable range hopping in a coulomb gap (located in the states which are deep in the band gap). As the doping is increased, it is changed into Mott type hopping. With higher temperatures, there is a decrease in n values in $\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ bronze thin films (Figs. 1, 2) and is mostly due to evolution of water, and it causes changes in carrier scattering and relaxation times. Figure 3 indicates rise in both the values of $n$ and $k$ of $\text{Li}_{0.42}\text{MoO}_3$ bronze thin film. This effect is also due to evolution of water accompanied by hydrogen bronze formation. Taking into account the strong temperature dependent oscillator overlap integral, the polaron band width at higher temperatures might become very narrow and can cave in suddenly, and the new mechanism, most probably the multiphonon activated hopping, might become predominant.

### SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS

Thermally evaporated molybdenum bronze thin films on unheated substrates are found to be sub-stoichiometric, and have highly disordered microcrystalline structure. We have measured mass densities, porosities, thermo-optic coefficients (TOCs), and electronic polarizabilities of $\text{MoO}_3$ and

# Ellipsometric Investigations of Electronic Polarizability and Thermo-Optic Coefficients of $Z_x$MoO₃ ($\text{Z} = \text{H}^+, \text{Li}^+$) Bronzes

of $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{Li}_x\text{MoO}_3$ bronze thin films over a range of temperatures from 100 K to 373 K using ellipsometry. The mass density of $\text{MoO}_3$ changed from $3.76\ \text{g/cm}^3$ to $3.54\ \text{g/cm}^3$ over the temperature range 100–373 K, but the mass densities for molybdenum bronzes changed from $3.84\ \text{g/cm}^3$ to $2.45\ \text{g/cm}^3$ over the same temperature range. Porosity for $\text{MoO}_3$ changed from 20% to 25% in the temperature range 100–373 K, but the porosity for $\text{Z}_x\text{MoO}_3$ bronzes ($\text{Z} = \text{H}^+, \text{Li}^+$) changed from 18% to 48% over the same temperature range.

TOCs ($\text{d}n/\text{d}T$ and $\text{d}k/\text{d}T$ values) for $\text{MoO}_3$ and for $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{Li}_x\text{MoO}_3$ bronze thin films were found to have anomalous dispersion in the heating cycles over the range 295–373 K. In the cooling cycles, $\text{d}n/\text{d}T$ varies from $-0.462 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ to $-1.22 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ and $\text{d}k/\text{d}T$ varies from to $3.371 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ to $5.558 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ for $\text{MoO}_3$ in the temperature range 100–295 K. The values of $\text{d}n/\text{d}T$ vary from $2.1 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ to $26.94 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ and $\text{d}k/\text{d}T$ values vary from $-1.844 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ to $-7.8 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ for $\text{Z}_x\text{MoO}_3$ ($\text{Z} = \text{H}^+, \text{Li}^+$) bronze thin films over the same temperature range. The small change in the values of TOCs determined for the molybdenum bronzes is due to small change in the values of electronic polarizability ($\alpha_\text{e}$), which were calculated in the range from 8.20 to $8.22 \times 10^{-24}\ \text{cm}^3$ over the temperature range 100–373 K.

We have developed the analytical modelling for the reported ellipsometric data on $\text{MoO}_3$, $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{Li}_x\text{MoO}_3$ and for the determined values of thermo-optic coefficients (TOCs), and it is found that all the mathematical Eqs. 13 to 22 fit well to the modelling Eqs. 11 and 12 with a maximum uncertainity of not more than 10% over the temperature range 100–295 K.

Small-polarons and bipolarons formation and hoppings from $\text{Mo}^{5+}$ (or $\text{Mo}^{4+}$) to $\text{Mo}^{6+}$ species in the molybdenum bronzes during heating and cooling runs are explained using configurational coordinate model.

In terms of conclusions, we declare that the vacuum-heating and oxidative-annealing certainly removes a lot of water, and definitely helps to reduce the porosity in the $\text{MoO}_3$ thin films over the reported temperature range 295–373 K. And this process ultimately assists to increase the electrochromic colouration efficiency of $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{Li}_x\text{MoO}_3$ bronze thin films for different technical applications. At temperatures higher than 400 K, there could be an increase in electronic polarizability ($\alpha_\text{e}$), but an irreversible disordering of the hydrogen or lithium atoms could occur which might affect the electrochromic process at higher temperatures.

In the case of cooling runs, different lower values of $n$ or higher values of $k$ were obtained during different cooling cycles, and these did not return to original when $\text{MoO}_3$ or $\text{H}_x\text{MoO}_3$ (or $\text{Li}_x\text{MoO}_3$) thin films were turned back to room temperature. So, for the cooling cycles, the hysteresis loops are not closed for the investigated molybdenum bronzes. This means that the thermal treatment in the temperature range 100–295 K is an irreversible.
