# Influence of the cladding diameter of optical fiber on the simultaneous measurement of the refractive index and temperature of liquids using tilted fiber Bragg grating

Akihiro Kameyama¹*, Masahito Katto², and Atsushi Yokotani¹

¹Faculty of Engineering, University of Miyazaki, Miyazaki 889-2192, Japan
²Center of Collaborative Research and Community Cooperation, University of Miyazaki, Miyazaki 889-2192, Japan
*E-mail: kame@opt.miyazaki-u.ac.jp

Received February 9, 2016; accepted April 12, 2016; published online May 20, 2016

We considered the influence of the core and cladding diameters on the simultaneous measurement of the refractive index and temperature of liquids using a tilted fiber Bragg grating (TFBG). When the cladding diameter of a TFBG was only reduced from 125 to 123 μm, the calculated refractive index differed by 0.0068 from the expected value. When the core diameter was reduced from 9.6 to 7.8 μm, the change was less than 0.0005. We found that equalization of cladding diameter is required to improve the precision of the simultaneous measurement of the refractive index and temperature of liquids. © 2016 The Japan Society of Applied Physics

Fiber Bragg gratings (FBGs), which consist of a periodic modulation of the refractive index in the core of a single-mode optical fiber, are widely used for optical communication devices, temperature sensors, and distortion sensors.¹) In addition to FBG sensors, tilted fiber Bragg gratings (TFBGs) have recently been attracting considerable attention for their use in sensing applications.²⁻⁴) In a TFBG, information in a cladding as well as in the core affects its transmission spectra. Therefore, although a conventional FBG can detect only mechanical phenomena of the core such as a change in length, since the cladding prevents the optical information from coming into the core from the outside, it can also detect nonmechanical phenomena since the information at the surface of the cladding affects signals.

We previously developed a simplified fabrication technique for TFBGs intended for practical applications using a commercially available pulsed Nd:YAG laser (4ω) and a conventional phase mask.⁵) We confirmed that the TFBG fabricated using this technique can simultaneously measure refractive index and liquid temperature.⁶) In this study, we found that the peak wavelength shift of the core and each cladding mode were different from each other when the temperature of water increased. In particular, the shift in the high-order cladding mode became smaller than that in the low-order cladding mode. It is expected that the peak position of the cladding mode is affected by the refractive index of silica glass or the diameter of the TFBG. However, there has been no detailed report on such calculations. It is necessary to consider the influence of the optical fiber structure on the peak shift of the cladding mode when conducing precise simultaneous measurement of the refractive index and temperature of liquids.

A multilayer method has been proposed for analyzing round symmetrical optical fibers. However, when it is used for analyzing the cladding mode, overflow or underflow of calculation may occur because the cladding diameter of an optical fiber is quite large compared with the wavelength of light. Recently, Yabu and Ouhashi developed a multilayer method that uses an improved function to avoid overflow or underflow of calculation.⁷)

In this paper, we report on the influence of the core and cladding diameters of an optical fiber for the simultaneous measurement of the refractive index and temperature of liquids using TFBGs.

We calculated the core and cladding modes of the optical fiber by the multilayer method. The distribution of refractive index in the optical fiber can be approximated, as shown in Fig. 1, where $n_1$, $n_2$, and $n_3$ are the refractive indices of the core, cladding, and surrounding material, respectively. The terms $2a_1$ and $2a_2$ are the core and cladding diameters, respectively.

Yabu and Ouhashi also defined the numerical formula used for calculating the cladding mode.⁷) We call the core and cladding modes $Y_{mn}$ in this paper. The subscripts m and n are the directional quantum number and mode order, respectively. For example, $Y_{11}$ denotes the core mode. We assumed that the propagation direction of light that can be introduced into the optical fiber is $z$ and the direction of rotation is $\phi$. We can denote the $z$ and $\phi$ components in the electric and magnetic fields as $E_z$, $E_\phi$, $H_z$, and $H_\phi$, which exist in the core, cladding, and outside the cladding calculated from the three equations shown in the online supplementary data at http://stacks.iop.org/JJAP/55/068003/mmedia (Data 1), respectively. These components, which are also the tangential components in the boundary between two layers, have to satisfy a boundary condition. We calculated the propagation coefficient $k_{\text{eff.}}$ satisfying the boundary condition for each layer.

We used the refractive index of the core and cladding on the basis of the data corrected by Shibata and Edahiro.⁸) The indices of the core and cladding were 1.4491 and 1.4443, respectively, at 1550 nm. The core and cladding diameters of the optical fiber SBG-15 (Newport), which we used for this study, were 9.6 and 125 μm. To determine the effect of these diameters of the fiber on the peak wavelength position of the cladding mode, we changed the core and cladding diameters and calculated the peak wavelength position of the cladding mode once again.

The peak wavelength of the core and cladding modes on the $n$th order in a TFBG that has a tilt angle $\theta$ can be calculated from the following equations:

$$\lambda_{\text{core}}=\frac{2n_{\text{eff.core}}\Lambda}{\cos\theta},\tag{1}$$

$$\lambda_{\text{cladding}}=\frac{\left(n_{\text{eff.core}}+n_{\text{eff.cladding}}^{n}\right)\Lambda}{\cos\theta},\tag{2}$$

where $n_{\text{eff.core}}$ and $n_{\text{eff.cladding}}^{n}$ are the effective refractive indices of the core and cladding modes, respectively, and $\Lambda$ is the grating period.

The wavelength and amplitude of the cladding mode are affected by the refractive index of the surrounding material. When the refractive index and/or temperature of a liquid are changed, the peak shift of the core and cladding modes can be expressed from the following equations:

$$\Delta\lambda_{\text{core}}=2\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\cos\theta}\frac{\partial n_{\text{eff.core}}}{\partial T}+\frac{n_{\text{eff.core}}}{\cos\theta}\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial T}\right)(T-T_0),\tag{3}$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\lambda_{\text{cladding}}^{n}&=\frac{\Lambda}{\cos\theta}\left(\frac{\partial n_{\text{eff.core}}}{\partial T}+\frac{\partial n_{\text{eff.cladding}}^{n}}{\partial T}\right)(T-T_0)\\
&+\frac{n_{\text{eff.core}}+n_{\text{eff.cladding}}^{n}}{\cos\theta}\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial T}(T-T_0)\\
&+A_n\left(e^{B_n(n_{\text{liquid}}-1)}-1\right),\tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

where, $T$, $T_0$, $n_{\text{liquid}}$, $A_n$, and $B_n$ are the temperature, reference temperature, and refractive index of the liquid and two experimental parameters, respectively. In our previous paper, $^5)$ we reported that the peak shift of the cladding mode increased exponentially for the refractive index, as expressed by the third term on the right side of Eq. (4). In general, the temperature coefficient of the refractive index of silica glass is on the order of $10^{-5}/$K. $^9)$ On the other hand, the thermal expansion coefficient of silica glass is on the order of $10^{-6}/$K. $^9)$ Therefore, the influence of temperature coefficient on the peak shift is much larger than that of thermal expansion.

We used a $4\omega$ Nd:YAG laser and a wavelength separator to produce a pulsed 266 nm UV laser beam to fabricate the TFBG. $^5)$ This laser produced laser pulses with an energy of 50 mJ/pulse and a pulse width of 7 ns. The beam was introduced to the optical fiber core through a phase mask made of silica glass. With this technique, a periodically modulated UV beam was produced by interference of two diffracted laser beams caused by the phase mask. A germanium-boron-codoped single-mode fiber SBG-15 (Newport) was used as the sample. The fiber was fixed on a rotary stage to easily adjust $\theta_{\text{ext}}$, which is the external tilt angle between the phase mask and the fiber. A $\theta_{\text{ext}}$ of $11^\circ$ was selected to obtain strong and sharp peaks of the cladding mode. $^5)$ Because the UV beam refracts at the interface between the air and the fiber material during fabrication, the tilt angle ($\theta$) in the fiber core reaches $7.3^\circ$. A probe light with a wavelength range from 1520 to 1610 nm was used for the transmission spectral measurement.

To measure the refractive index and temperature of liquids, we immersed the fabricated TFBG in different kinds of liquids. We used water, ethanol, and various aqueous solutions of glycerine. The concentrations of the solutions used in the experiment ranged from 12 to $84\%$. The refractive indices of the samples are listed in the online supplementary data at http://stacks.iop.org/JJAP/55/068003/mmedia (Data 2). $^{10)}$ The liquids were treated in a hot bath, and the temperature of the liquid was measured with a thermometer within an accuracy of 1 K.

Figure 2 shows the transmission spectra of the $7.3^\circ$-TFBG in air and $46\ \text{wt}\%$ glycerine solution. The solid and broken lines indicate the spectra in air and $46\ \text{wt}\%$ glycerine solution, respectively. We observed not only the core mode at 1572.12 nm but also the cladding modes in a spectral range from approximately 1520 to 1570 nm. We calculated the order of each cladding mode of the TFBG by the multilayer method. We identified that the three modes at 1531.25, 1540.94, and 1559.90 nm were $Y_{1,70}$, $Y_{1,60}$, and $Y_{1,34}$, respectively. The peak wavelengths of the cladding modes increased as the refractive index of the liquids increased, as shown in Fig. 2.

Figure 3 shows the relative wavelength shifts of the cladding modes as a function of the refractive index of the liquids. The wavelength shift of the $Y_{1,70}$ mode was 0.73 nm, corresponding to the index from 1.0000 to 1.3876. The wavelength shifts of the $Y_{1,60}$ and $Y_{1,34}$ modes were 0.51 and 0.14 nm, respectively. The peak shift of the high-order cladding mode for the refractive index was larger than that of the low-order cladding mode.

Figure 3 also shows the relative wavelength shift of the cladding mode calculated by the multilayer method. The three smooth lines in the figure show the relative wavelength shift function as expressed by the third term on the right side of Eq. (4). The calculated results were generally equal to the observed values.

Figure 4 shows the relative wavelength shifts of the core and three selected cladding modes plotted as a function of the temperature of water for the $7.3^{\circ}$-TFBG. As a result, the wavelength shifts of the $Y_{1,70}$, $Y_{1,60}$, and $Y_{1,34}$ modes were 0.45, 0.49, and 0.51 nm, corresponding to the temperature from 293 to 353 K. The wavelength shift of the core mode was 0.48 nm. The temperature coefficients of the refractive indices of the core and cladding were estimated to be $7.3 \times 10^{-6}$ and $10.0 \times 10^{-6}/\text{K}$, respectively. The temperature coefficient of the refractive index of water was estimated to be about $-1 \times 10^{-4}/\text{K}$. These values were qualitatively equal to the reported values. $^{9,11)}$

Next, we calculated the influence of the core and cladding diameters on the shift of the cladding mode. Figure 5 shows the relative wavelength shift of the $Y_{1,70}$ mode calculated with different cladding diameters. In the case of a TFBG having a cladding diameter of $125\ \mu\text{m}$, the relative shift of the $Y_{1,70}$ mode became 0.62 nm corresponding to the refractive index from 1 to 1.3727. The shift became smaller at 0.58 nm when the cladding diameter was changed to $127\ \mu\text{m}$. The wavelength shift became equivalent to 1.3670, when it was converted to refractive index. The shift became larger at 0.71 nm when the cladding diameter was changed to $123\ \mu\text{m}$. The wavelength shift became equivalent to 1.3795. That is, when the cladding diameter of the fiber was only reduced from 125 to $123\ \mu\text{m}$, the calculated refractive index differed by 0.0068 from the expected value. On the other hand, when the core diameter of the fiber was reduced from 9.6 to $7.8\ \mu\text{m}$, the change in the calculated refractive index was less than 0.0005. The cladding diameter of the optical fiber we used has a tolerance of $\pm 1\ \mu\text{m}$. The tolerance affects the simultaneous measurement of the refractive index and temperature of liquids. Therefore, we conclude that the equalization of cladding diameter in particular is required to improve the precision of simultaneous measurement. TFBGs are considered to be very promising for use as sensors of biochemical phenomena in the medical and biological fields.
