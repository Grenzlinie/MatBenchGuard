# Second-Harmonic Generation in CDA and CD*A
K. KATO

\begin{abstract} Efficient second-harmonic generation at $5321 \AA$ has been achieved in CDA and CD*A. At 50-MW pump power, loss-free peak power-conversion efficiency of 57 percent and 45 percent were obtained in CDA and CD*A, respectively, The nonlinear optical constant, corrected for multimode fluctuations, was found to be $d_{36}(CDA)=d_{36}(CD^{*} A)=(0.96 \pm 0.11) ×10^{-9} ESU$ , which corresponds to $d_{36}(CDA)=d_{36}(CD^{*} A)=0.92 ×d_{36}(KDP)$ . In addition, the temperature variation of the birefringence $d(n_{2}^{\circ}-n_{1}^{\circ}) / d T$ has been measured to be $(8.0 \pm 0.2) ×10^{-6}{}^{\circ} C^{-1}$ for CDA and $(7.8 \pm$  $0.2) ×10^{-6}{}^{\circ} C^{-1}$ for $CD^{*} A$ .

The search for highly efficient harmonic generators of high-power lasers has followed in various nonlinear crystals, among which the most successful have been the cesium dihydrogen arsenate (CDA) and deuterated CDA (CD*A) crystals. Both CDA and CD*A possess a high damage threshold and their linear optical properties allow for 90-deg phase-matched second-harmonic generation of neodymium laser frequency [1]-[6]. However, although an investigation of the nonlinear optical constant for CDA has been reported by Suvorov et al. [7], until now no attempt has been made to measure this value for CD*A. The fact that there are still remarkably little data on the optical properties of these crystals has prompted this work. In this correspondence a detailed experimental study on the second-harmonic generation at $5321 \AA$ in CDA and CD*A is reported.

The CDA and CD*A crystals were supplied from Quantum Technology Ltd., and they estimate that the degree of deuteration in CD*A is approximately 99 percent. Measurements of the transmission curves of these crystals were made by a Hitachi FPS-3T spectrophotometer. The 50-percent transmission range was approximately from $2600 \AA$ to $14300 \AA$ for CDA and from $2700 \AA$ to $16600 \AA$ for CD*A. The transmission loss at $10642 \AA$ was measured by means of an Nd:YAG laser. The absorption loss per centimeter was approximately4 percent for CDA and 2 percent for CD*A, which are slightly higher than the previous measurements [5]. The large absorption loss at $10642 \AA$ in CDA induced a relatively large local heating effect which depended on the pump power and pulse repetition rate of the laser. This self-induced thermal effect was insignificantly small in CD*A.

Measurements of the refractive indices of CDA and CD*A were made at $20.0^{\circ} C$ for the four spectral lines of the fundamental and second harmonic of Nd:YAG and ruby lasers with an estimated relative accuracy of less than $\pm 3 ×10^{-4}$ . The results presented in Table I agree with the data of Adhav [8] within a factor of $\pm 10^{-3}$ . Adhav's is the only published data we could find.

To confirm the validity of our measurements of the refractive indices, first we have measured the phase-matching angle for harmonic generation in both CDA and CD*A at20.0°C. The pump source was a KD*P Pockels cell Q-switchedNd:YAG oscillator-amplifier system operating in the $TEM_{00}$  mode with a peak power of 50 MW and 12-ns pulse duration. The axial mode selection of the oscillator was made by the insertion of a tilted Fabry-Perot etalon in the cavity, although a single-mode operation was not possible, it reduced the number of the axial modes. The measured beam divergence of the5-mm-diam fundamental beam was 1.1 mrad. The unfocused fundamental beam was incident on the $45^{\circ} z 90^{\circ} y$ -cut harmonic generator crystal housed in a solid copper oven. A temperature controller had the capability of heating the crystal up to $120.0^{\circ} C$ with an accuracy of control better than $\pm 0.1^{\circ} C$ . The crystal length was 17.5 mm for CDA and 13.5 mm for CD*A.

Detection of the fundamental and harmonic pulses was made through a high dispersion prism and a fast photodiode- oscilloscope system. At $20.0^{\circ} C$ the phase-matching angle was measured to be $84^{\circ} 09'$ for CDA and $79^{\circ} 21'$ for CD*A which agree fairly well with the calculated value of $84^{\circ} 54'$ for CDA and $78^{\circ} 15'$ for CD*A. Fig. 1 shows the angular dependence of the harmonic power in CDA and CD*A measured with constant power of 50 MW and a pulse repetition rate of 1 pulse per second (pps). The dotted lines are the theoretical curves calculated in terms of the refractive indices presented in Table I. The product of the crystal length and its internal angular acceptance angle was $12.3 mrad \cdot cm$ for CDA and $7.2 mrad \cdot cm$ for CD*A. The agreement with Golyaev et al.'s[3] value of $15.0 mrad \cdot cm$ for CDA is fairly good.

The 90-deg phase-matching temperature measured at a pulse repetition rate of 0.1-1 pps was $48.0^{\circ} C$ for CDA and112.3°C for CD*A. However, when the repetition rate of the laser was increased from 1 pps to 20 pps, the oven temperaturehad to be adjusted to $39.6^{\circ} C$ for CDA and $109.8^{\circ} C$ for $CD^{*} A$  to maintain the same harmonic power level. Since the temperature dependence of the phase-matched harmonic power is very sensitive to subtle refractive indices change, the resulting perfect temperature scan leads to an accurate determination of the temperature variation of the birefringence. Using the following expression derived for the temperature dependence of the refractive indices
$$

Accurate determination of the energy-conversion efficiency for the harmonic under the 90-deg phase-matching condition was made by measuring the depletion of the transmitted fundamental pulses when the harmonic-generator crystal was tuned from an unmatched condition to the phase-matching temperature. Knowing the ratio of the pulse durations of the fundamental and harmonic, the peak power-conversion efficiency was obtained from the energy-conversion efficiency. In this measurement, polished glass filters were used to attenuate the fundamental power before entering the harmonic-generator crystal. The overall power-conversion efficiency at a fundamental pump power of 50 MW was 45 percent for CDA and 37 percent for CD*A. The pulse duration of the harmonic was 10 ns, though somewhat shorter pulse durations were observed at low pump-power levels.

Fig. 2 shows the peak power-conversion efficiency for the harmonic plotted as a function of effective pump-power density. The effective pump power was calculated by taking into account the 10-percent polarization loss and the 12- and 7.5-percent transmission and Fresnel reflection loss in CDA and CD*A, respectively. The maximum power-conversion efficiency corrected for these losses was 57 percent for CDA and 45 percent for CD*A.

The harmonic power for the case of a perfect phase-matching condition with a single-mode pump and with no losses to the fundamental other than the conversion to the harmonic is given by [9]
$$
P_{2} / P_{1}=\tanh ^{2}\left(l / l_{s}\right) \quad(3)
$$
where
$$
l_{s}^{-1}=\left(8 \pi^{2} / n_{1}^{o} \lambda\right) d_{36} E_{0} \quad(4)
$$
and $d_{36}$ is the nonlinear optical constant for the harmonic-generator crystal, and $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ are the power density of the fundamental and harmonic, respectively.

From the best fit of the theoretical conversion efficiencies to the experimental curves shown in Fig. 2, the value of $d_{36}(CDA)=d_{36}(CD^{*} A)=(1.24 \pm 0.07) × 10^{-9}$ ESU was obtained. The uncertainty is due mainly to the inaccuracy in the beam cross section. Since the laser was working in 2-4 axial modes, account has to be taken of the intensity fluctuations in the harmonic generation as discussed by Bloembergen [10], and observed by Francois [11]. These fluctuations enhance the nonlinear optical constant by a factor of $((2 n-1) / n)^{1 / 2}$ when $n$ randomly phased axial modes are present. The present measurements give the corrected value of $d_{36}(CDA)=d_{36}(CD^{*} A)=(0.96 \pm 0.11) × 10^{-9}$ ESU. Although there is a large variation in the absolute value of the nonlinear optical constant for KDP, it can be seen that $d_{36}(CDA)=d_{36}(CD^{*} A)=0.92 × d_{36}(KDP)$ if we used the value of $d_{36}(KDP)=1.04 × 10^{-9}$ ESU reported by Levine and Bethea [12]. The previous measurements [7] on CDA and KDP demonstrated $d_{36}(CDA)=0.53 × d_{36}(KDP)$, but this is very likely in error.

The spatial profile of the harmonic beam was very close to Gaussian due to the absence of the birefringence effect and no mode or inhomogeneous structure was observed. In addition, neither CDA nor CD*A exhibited any surface or internal damage after several days of a life test at a pulse repetition rate of 10-20 pps with a fundamental pump-power density of $255 MW / cm^{2}$.

It may be said in conclusion that because of their 90-deg phase matchability and large nonlinear optical constants comparable to KDP, both CDA and CD*A are at present by far the most effective nonlinear crystals for the second-harmonic generation of the high-power neodymium lasers.
