# Durable $Li_2CN_2$ Solid Electrolyte Interphase Wired by Carbon Nanodomains via In Situ Interface Lithiation to Enable Long-Cycling Li Metal Batteries

Qifan Yang, Jiulin Hu, Zhenguo Yao, Jianjun Liu, and Chilin Li*

Lithium metal batteries (LMBs) are becoming the promising candidate of high- energy storage systems. However, the fragile natural solid electrolyte inter- phase (SEl) cannot retard the Li dendrite growth at anode, which will cause the low coulombic efficiency (CE) of Li plating/stripping and safety hazards in LMBs. Here, an in situ construction strategy of novel artificial SEl consisting of $Li_2CN_2$ ionic conductor wired by carbon nanodomains via dicyandiamide solution reaction method on Li metal surface is proposed. This lithiophilic $Li_2CN_2$ has the higher anti-reduction stability and longer critical length for Li dendrite, showing the excellent dendrite suppressing ability. The wired carbon domains promote the electron connection and charge homogenization in SEl, leading to the uniform Li nucleation around $Li_2CN_2/C$ grains with enhanced interface kinetics and reduced polarization. This dual conductive $Li_2CN_2/C$ network enables the durable preservation of high CE and low voltage hysteresis during Li plating/stripping, endowing $LiNi_{0.8}Mn_{0.1}Co_{0.1}O_2/Li$ cells with ultralong cycling life exceeding 1000 cycles at high rate. The cycling stabilization effect is also remarkable even under thin Li anode and high-loading cathode conditions. This study provides a solution to robust SEl configuration of high conductivity via in situ interface lithiation reaction for high-performance LMBs.

## 1. Introduction

Advanced Li batteries with higher energy density is in urgent demand for the large-scale energy storage fields like electric vehicles and smart grids. $^{[1,2]}$ In order to maximize the energy density, Li metal as anode material is re-obtaining the attention in view of its lowest electrochemical potential $(-3.04\ V$ vs the standard hydrogen electrode) and highest theoretical specific capacity $(3860\ mAh\ g^{-1})$. Li metal anode can couple with the high-energy- density Li-free cathodes characterized by conversion reaction instead of capacity- limited intercalation reaction, leading to the research enthusiasm of next-generation conversion-type batteries including Li-fluoride and Li-sulfur systems. $^{[3-5]}$ However, the intrinsic instability of Li metal would cause the uneven parasite reactions with electrolyte, leading to the formation of undesired solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) or surface passivation layer. $^{[6]}$ On the other hand, the non-uniform stripping and plating processes of Li induce the huge volume and morphology change, by which the severe dendrite growth is generated. $^{[7]}$

Due to the fragile nature, the as-formed SEI presents the repeated collapse and regeneration behavior, resulting in the cracking of SEI and exposure of inner fresh Li. $^{[8]}$ This behavior accelerates the interphase side reactions at the cost of electrolyte consumption as well as the formation of electrochemical inert "dead Li" isolated by reduction compounds in SEI. These compounds derived from electrolyte consumption typically include inorganic components (e.g., $LiOH$, $Li_2CO_3$, and LiF) and organic components (e.g., ROLi and ROCOOLi, R: organic group). $^{[9,10]}$ The aggravation of interface cracking and uneven Li deposition would trigger the serious deterioration of overall battery performance with the appearance of low Coulombic efficiency (CE), large overpotential and short cycle life. These shortcomings limit the commercial application of Li metal batteries (LMBs). Hence, constructing a robust artificial SEI on Li anode surface is urgently required to eliminate parasitic reactions, alleviate dendrite growth and promote interfacial transport kinetics.

The significance of SEI modulation to achieve stable Li metal anode has been emphasized. $^{[11]}$ Modifying the in situ formed SEI or fabricating the ex situ SEI protective layer are two kinds of ways to customize the SEI with desired properties, like proper mechanical strength, good electrochemical stability, and negligible electronic but high ionic conductivity. But the gradual decay of battery performance is unstoppable after the operation for certain time due to the continuous consumption of additives. $^{[15]}$ Besides, designing an ex situ artificial SEI with sufficient thickness (such as Li-based alloying layers, $^{[8,16,17]}$ fluoride/nitride layers, $^{[18-20]}$ and textured organic/polymer layers $^{[21,22]}$ ) to protect Li anode surface was proved to be a successful strategy to stabilize the interface chemistry. It can not only maintain the homogeneity of SEI layer with less cracks, but also endurably regulate Li-ion strip ping/deposition behavior. An ideal SEI should be lithiophilic in order to improve the nucleation kinetics with reduced overpo- tential. According to the frontier molecular orbital theory, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) and highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energies decide the chem- ical stability of components of electrode and its interface. $^{[23]}$ The higher LUMO energy means a stronger anti-reduction ability. Hence, a SEI component with higher LUMO energy is not apt to accept the electrons so that the electron-induced interface reduction reaction is restrained, contributing to the reinforce- ment of interface stability. $^{[24]}$ In one word, a desired SEI com ponent should not only exhibit the satisfactory mechanical strength and chemical homogeneity, but also the lithiophilic nature and high anti-reduction ability.

Usually, the introduction of additional F element via F-rich materials is widely considered to improve the comprehen- sive performance of SEI, because the strong electron-with- drawing property of F atoms can widen the HOMO-LUMO gap of electrolyte or SEI component with enhanced stability against Li metal anode. Many F-rich materials were proposed such as $LiF,^{[25-27]} AlF_{3},^{[28]}$ fluoropolymer, $^{[20]}$ fluoroethylene carbonate, $^{[13]}$ highly concentrated Li bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide, $^{[29,30]}$  and so on, in order to modulate the electrolyte formulation or construct the SEI component. However, merely considering LiF is not enough to obtain the desired SEI, because of its poor ionic conductivity. $^{[31]}$ If the amount of LiF is excessive in SEI, the interface kinetics would be in turn compromised. $^{[32]}$ The construction of heterogeneous structure like $LiF / Li_{2} CO_{3}$ is helpful to promote the ionic conductivity at hetero-interface and even to trigger the interfacial Li mass storage due to the space charge effect, leading to the improved homogenization of Li-ion flowing and Li mass transport during the Li nuclea- tion stage. $^{[33,34]}$ Although $Li_{2} CO_{3}$ exhibits the relatively better ionic conductivity $(\approx 10^{-8} ~S ~cm^{-1})$ , the non-uniform formation of Li₂CO₃ in SEI is at the cost of electrolyte consumption, which could result in the battery capacity fading. Therefore, the con- struction of a homogeneous ionic conductive network with potential space charge effect is beneficial for promoting the interface ion diffusion kinetics and the preservation of compact Li plating. It would be better if the artificial ionic conductive network could influence the regulation of SEI composition, like promoting the concentration of LiF and inhibiting the unde- sired components formation. However, the ionic conductive interface is usually electron-conduction inhibited. So that the electrons have to be confined on the Li metal beneath the SEI layer, likely leading to the uneven reduction and deposition of Li-ions. Introducing the proper 3D electron-transfer structure can homogenize the electron flow and reduce the local current density, beneficial for the uniform nucleation of Li-ions. But it is still challenging to build an ion-electron dual-optimized interface framework.

Herein, we propose a configuration of uniform and com- pact carbon-interconnected $Li_{2} CN_{2}$ nano-framework $(Li_{2} CN_{2} / C)$  with intimate ionic and electronic connection, which is in situ synthesized on the Li metal surface. This is the first attempt to in situ synthesize $Li_{2} CN_{2}$ as novel SEI component through a solution-reaction method. Its ion conductivity in the order of magnitude of $10^{-6} ~S ~cm^{-1}(25^{\circ} C)$ is not low especially in view of its nanocrystalline size, $^{[35]}$ which would not retard the ion diffusion. Its high lithiophilicity and anti-reduction stability enable the endurable homogenization of Li-ion flowing and Li-mass plating. $Li_{2} CN_{2}$ also has the long critical length for Li dendrite and therefore the excellent dendrite suppressing ability. In addition, the wired carbon domains in $Li_{2} CN_{2} / C$ pro mote the electronic connection and therefore the enhancement of interface kinetics. Therefore, the Li nucleation overpotential is greatly reduced from 71 to $6 mV$ at $0.5 mA cm^{-2}$ . This hybrid SEI enables the longer cycle life, higher CE and better rate performance for both half and full cells.

## 2. Results and Discussion
Figure 1a illustrates the preparation process of $Li_{2} CN_{2} / C$  mixed conductive nano-framework. In brief, the polished Li metal is immersed in the dicyandiamide $(H_{4} C_{2} ~N_{4}$ , $N \equiv C-NH-C(NH_{2})=NH) / N, N$ -dimethylformamide $(C_{3} H_{7} NO)$  solution, which is denoted as DCDA/DMF. The reaction between $Li$ and dicyandiamide is spontaneous as the bubbles are obviously observed from the Li surface. Therein, a layer of grey products is in situ formed after reacting for several minutes. These products are characterized by the X-ray diffraction(XRD) pattern. The strong diffraction peak at $\approx 26^{\circ}$ and some weak peaks are typically attributed to the $Li_{2} CN_{2}$ phase $(Li-N=C=N-Li$ , PDF \#31-0717) as shown in Figure $1 b.^{[35]}$  Additionally, the other two dominant peaks at $27^{\circ}$ and $28.5^{\circ}$ are assigned to the carbon component (PDF #46-0943). It indicates that the grey interface layer is mainly composed by the mix- ture of carbon and $Li_{2} CN_{2}$ . The energy dispersive spectrometer(EDS) mapping confirms the uniform distribution of $C$ and $N$  elements in the surface layer (Figure S1, Supporting Informa- tion). The in situ synthesis of $Li_{2} CN_{2} / C$ with nanocrystalline structure guarantees the uniform wiring of $C$ around $Li_{2} CN_{2}$  domains with dense ion/electron conductive network(Figure 1c). The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) imaging shows the grain size of $Li_{2} CN_{2} / C$ is $<100 ~nm$ (Figure S2, Supporting Information). Note that the adherence between Li₂CN₂/C layer and Li metal is quite intimate due to the in situ interface reaction, and the $Li_{2} CN_{2} / C$ conformal coating has a thickness of $\approx 1 \mu m$ (Figure 1d; Figure S3, Supporting Infor mation). In order to further understand the reaction mechanism between Li and DCDA, the density functional theory (DFT) calculations were utilized to analyze the reaction process. As shown in Table S1 (Supporting Information), we proposed a reaction formula between Li and DCDA:

$$3\mathrm{H}_4\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{N}_4 + 8\mathrm{Li}=4\mathrm{Li}_2\mathrm{CN}_2 + 2\mathrm{C}+4\mathrm{NH}_3\uparrow \tag{1}$$

The Gibbs free energy (G) of each compound is listed in Table S1 (Supporting Information), and the corresponding reaction $\Delta G$ is calculated to be $-374.16$ kJ mol$^{-1}$. This value means that DCDA can react with metallic Li spontaneously at room temperature, leading to the generation of $Li_2CN_2$ and C along with $NH_3$ gas release. During this reaction process, gas bubbles are observed and proved to be $NH_3$ by means of two classical experiments. The wetted pH test paper that adsorbs $NH_3$ is alkaline (Figure S4, Supporting Information). And the released $NH_3$ can react with HCl (volatilizing from concentrated hydrochloric acid) to produce $NH_4Cl$, showing a white smoke phenomenon (see Movie S1, Supporting Information). Therefore, the three kinds of reaction products are all confirmed depending on the above characterizations and analysis, indicating the validity of this proposed reaction process. We further analyzed the reaction mechanism by dividing the total reaction process into several elementary reactions as shown in Table S1 (Supporting Information). Accordingly, the DFT-computed Gibbs free energy diagram based on the most favorable reaction pathway is shown in Figure 1h. With the strong reduction ability, metallic Li reacts with $\mathrm{H}_4\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{N}_4$ in the beginning, and therein the electron transfers from Li to C in $\mathrm{H}_4\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{N}_4$ (i.e., $\mathrm{N≡C-NH-C(NH_2)=NH}$) along with the breaking of $\mathrm{C-N}$ and $\mathrm{N-H}$ bonds, causing the generation of a free radical $(\mathrm{Li-N=C=N-}\ \dot{\mathrm{C}}=\mathrm{NH})$ with $NH_3$ releasing. This is a Gibbs free energy decreasing process with a $\Delta G$ value of $-269.69$ eV, which is denoted as Step 1-1. The as-generated free radical likely further reacts with Li, leading to the formation of $Li_2CN_2$ (i.e., $\mathrm{Li-N=C=N-Li}$) and another free radical $(\dot{\mathrm{C}}=\mathrm{NH})$ in the transition state with a free energy barrier of 558.86 eV. This step is denoted as Step 1-2. The Step 1-1 is expected to provide energy to overcome the energy barrier of Step 1-2, promoting the following reaction process. Subsequently, the transitionstate free radical $(\dot{\mathrm{C}}=\mathrm{NH})$ reacts with Li to form $\ddot{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{NH-Li}$. This stage is denoted as Step 1-3. The second stage (including the steps from 2-1 to 2-3) is based on the reaction between the free radical $\ddot{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{NH-Li}$ and $\mathrm{H}_4\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{N}_4$, with the generation of intermediate product $\mathrm{HN=C=NH}$. The third stage (consisting of Step 3-1, 3-2, and 3-3) begins with the reaction between $\ddot{\mathrm{C}}-\mathrm{NH-Li}$ and $\mathrm{HN=C=NH}$, and the final products are $NH_3$ and $Li_2CN_2$. Carbon component is produced at Step 2-2 and Step 3-2. The accumulated $\Delta G$ of all reaction steps is calculated to be $-374.16$ kJ mol$^{-1}$, which is identical to that of the total one-step reaction formula, indicating the feasibility and precision

of these calculation steps. Hence, the generation of $Li_2CN_2/C$ mixed framework under the experimental conditions is stood by the calculation result.

$Li_2CN_2$ (space group: I4/mmm) crystalizes in a tetragonal structure, of which the $CN_2$ functional groups occupy the body-center positions and the Li atoms occupy the distorted tetrahedron centers (Figure 1i). $^{[40]}$ Due to this unique structure, $Li_2CN_2$ enables the 3D transport channels for Li-ions, and it was regarded as a $Li^+$ ionic conductor especially in the amorphization state $(10^{-6}\ S\ cm^{-1}).^{[35]}$ In our case, it is difficult to obtain the accurate conductivity value for this in situ synthesized $Li_2CN_2$ in view of its tight wrapping with electron conductor species (e.g., Li metal and C product). Its nanocrystalline structure with rich amorphizated domains is favorable for the enhancement of ion conductivity of $Li_2CN_2$. In order to quantitatively compare the electrochemical stability of $Li_2CN_2$ with other typical SEI components, their HOMO and LUMO energy levels are calculated (Figure 1j). $Li_2CN_2$ has a relatively higher LUMO energy $(-0.97\ eV)$ than those of $Li_2CO_3$ $(-1.31\ eV)$ and LiF $(-1.57\ eV)$. This LUMO energy level is also higher than those of other popular SEI components like $Li_2O$, $Li_3N$, LiOH, LiOR, and $LiOOCOR$ $(R=CH_2CH_3)$ (Table S2, Supporting Information). These comparisons indicate that $Li_2CN_2$ has the stronger anti-reduction ability, which is supposed to effectively enhance the SEI stability. Besides, the HOMO-LUMO gap of $Li_2CN_2$ is $3.22\ eV$, comparable to that of $Li_2CO_3$ $(3.19\ eV)$, suggesting that $Li_2CN_2$ is a chemically stable material. Therefore, the introduction of durable $Li_2CN_2$ ionic conductor into SEI is expected to separate the electrolyte and Li anode, alleviate their side reaction and electrolyte consumption and improve the electrochemical stability of anode interface without the compromise of interface ionic conductivity.

The Li stripping/plating behavior under the effect of $Li_2CN_2$ as novel artificial SEI was tested based on symmetric and asymmetric cells. Symmetric Li/Li cells reveal the remarkable effect of $Li_2CN_2/C$ coating on stabilizing anode interface. As shown in Figure 2a, $Li_2CN_2/C$-modified cell enables a long cycling lifespan over 1000 h without serious polarization increase at $0.5\ mA\ cm^{-2}$ $(1.5\ mAh\ cm^{-2})$. However, the bare Li-based cell becomes short-circuit after 500 h. Benefiting from the ion/electron dual conduction in $Li_2CN_2/C$, the initial Li nucleation overpotential is greatly reduced from 71 to merely 6 mV at $0.5\ mA\ cm^{-2}$ after the introduction of $Li_2CN_2/C$ interlayer. During the following cycling, the $Li_2CN_2/C$ decoration enables a much smaller voltage hysteresis (i.e., plateau gap between Li plating and stripping) of $\approx 50\ mV$, which undergoes a mild increasing tendency during the long-term cycling (e.g., increase to 75 mV at the 120th cycle, Figure 2b). In contrast, the control cell has a large voltage hysteresis exceeding 100 mV even during the early cycles. When increasing the current density and areal capacity to $1\ mA\ cm^{-2}$ and $3\ mAh\ cm^{-2}$, respectively, the lifespan of modified cell is also prolonged $(\approx 570\ h)$, much better than that (300 h) of control one (Figure 2c). Correspondingly, the Li nucleation overpotential for modified cell is as small as 31 mV, significantly lower than that (140 mV) for bare Li. The voltage hysteresis of symmetric cell with $Li_2CN_2/C$ is $\approx 50\ mV$ before 30 cycles and gradually increases to $\approx 110\ mV$ after 100 cycles. However, the control case shows the much higher overpotentials of $\approx 125\ mV$ for 45 cycles and short-circuit occurs after only 50 cycles (Figure 2d). The reduction of both the nucleation and plateau overpotentials benefits from the high lithiophilicity of $Li_2CN_2/C$ framework as well as the improved electron/ionic transport at anode interface.

The Cu/Li asymmetric cells in ether-based electrolyte further confirms the CE retention ability of $Li_2CN_2/C$ layer. CE is defined as the ratio of Li stripping capacity to Li plating capacity, and is a critical parameter to demonstrate the reversibility of Li deposition/stripping behavior. At a moderate current density of $1\ mA\ cm^{-2}$ (with a capacity of $2\ mAh\ cm^{-2}$), a flat and stable CE curve can be achieved for over 250 cycles with an average value of $99\%$ by applying the $Li_2CN_2/C$ dual conductive protective layer (Figure 2e). While the CE drops down below $90\%$ after mere 150 cycles for the cell without $Li_2CN_2/C$ layer. When increasing the current density to $3\ mA\ cm^{-2}$ with a capacity of $1\ mAh\ cm^{-2}$, the $Li_2CN_2/C$-modified cell presents a stable cycling performance with CE $\approx 97\%$ for $\approx 200$ cycles, whereas the CE of control case decreases quickly below $90\%$ after only 100 cycles (Figure 2f). Even under a much higher current density of $10\ mA\ cm^{-2}$, the $Li_2CN_2/C$ decoration still enables a stable CE curve $\approx 95\%$ for 150 cycles, while the control case shows a decreasing curve of CE, which drops down below $90\%$ after only $\approx 50$ cycles (Figure 2g). The Li plating/stripping profiles and corresponding voltage hysteresis evolution especially after long-term cycling of cells with and without $Li_2CN_2/C$ are also compared. At $1\ mA\ cm^{-2}$, the $Li_2CN_2/C$-modified Li enables the highly stable voltage-capacity curves with nearly no capacity fading from the 10th to the 240th cycle (Figure S5a, Supporting Information). However, the bare Li shows the inferior capacity retention ability, and the Li stripping capacity drops obviously from the 10th to the 160th cycle (Figure S5b, Supporting Information). Besides, the corresponding voltage hysteresis evolution proves that the $Li_2CN_2/C$ layer enables a good anti-overpotential-increasing ability, and the voltage hysteresis plots are quite flat $(\approx 34\ mV)$ lasting for 250 cycles. While the cell without $Li_2CN_2/C$ can maintain the similar overpotential for merely 150 cycles, which then increases dramatically and exceeds 50 mV at the 180th cycle (Figure S5c, Supporting Information). And at the higher $3\ mA\ cm^{-2}$, $Li_2CN_2/C$ also guarantees an excellent capacity retention ability even after cycling for 190 cycles (Figure S5d, Supporting Information). And the increase of corresponding plateau overpotential is quite small, from 99 mV at the 10th cycle to 121 mV at the 190th cycle. In contrast, the bare Cu/Li cell shows the dramatic dropping of Li stripping capacity after only 125 cycles with a steep overpotential increase from 104 mV at the 10th cycle to 156 mV at the 125th cycle (Figure S5e,f, Supporting Information). For the Cu/Li cells based on carbonate-based electrolyte, the optimization effect of $Li_2CN_2/C$ on the CE stabilization is also demonstrated (Figure S6, Supporting Information).

In order to analyze the contribution of $Li_{2}CN_{2}/C$ interlayer to the SEI stability, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted to investigate the SEI components on Li metal surface at the plating state after 5 cycles. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was utilized to analyze the influence of $Li_2CN_2$/C as SEI enhancer on the anode interface kinetics. The Li/Li symmetric cells in carbonate-based electrolyte were used to check the EIS evolution at different cycling stages and test temperatures. The Nyquist plots consist of a semicircle at high/middle frequencies followed by a diffusive sloped tail at low frequencies, representing for the charge-transfer and SEI dominated interface resistance ($R_i$) and the Warburg impedance ($Z_w$), respectively.$^{[18,46,47]}$ The intercept value in x-axis before semicircle starting is attributed to the resistance of bulk electrolyte ($R_b$). The $Li_2CN_2$/C modification enables a small initial interface resistance of 66 Ω. The $R_i$ value becomes smaller and is ≈25 Ω after cycling from the 20th to 60th cycle. Meanwhile, the $R_b$ value increases slowly from 11 to 15 Ω after 60 cycles (Figure 4f,h). In contrast, the bare Li-based cell has a much larger initial $R_i$ of ≈145 Ω before cycling. Then, the $R_i$ value becomes 84 Ω after 20 cycles and 56 Ω after 60 cycles, which are still higher than those based on $Li_2CN_2$/C (Figure 4g,h). Accordingly, the $R_b$ value of bare

Li cell is also higher at the corresponding cycling stages, and it increases from 15 Ω before cycling to 20 Ω after 60 cycles. The reduction of both the interface and bulk electrolyte resistances with better cycling stability indicates the positive effect of Li₂CN₂/C on accelerating the interface kinetics. Besides, when compared with the bare Li cell, the diffusion tails of Li₂CN₂/C modified cell have the evidently larger and consistent slope angles after cycling for 20–60 cycles (Figure 4f,g), demonstrating its higher and stable cationic diffusion kinetics across the interface.[⁴⁷]

The EIS evolution at different temperatures and the corresponding activation energy ($E_\text{a}$) for interface resistance before and after cycling are compared in Figure 4i–l. The interface resistances for bare Li cell are difficult to be stabilized especially when increasing the ambience temperature in view of the fragile feature of naturally formed SEI, leading to the poor linear fitting effect in the Arrhenius plots with an $E_\text{a}$ value of 0.59 eV before cycling (Figure 4i). The scattering of Arrhenius plots at higher temperatures is caused by the acceleration of side reaction between unprotected anode and electrolyte. After cycling for 60 cycles, the linear fitting effect is modified due to the thickening of SEI layer, but the $E_\text{a}$ value is increased to 0.63 eV because of the passivation effect of insulating SEI accumulation (Figure 4j). In the sharp contrast, the Li₂CN₂/C-based cell exhibits the consistent Arrhenius plots before cycling and the corresponding $E_\text{a}$ is 0.45 eV (Figure 4k), benefiting from the interface mediation by conductive Li₂CN₂/C. Even after 60 cycles, the $E_\text{a}$ value is marginally increased to 0.48 eV, which is much lower than that in the control case (Figure 4l). These comparisons indicate that the Li₂CN₂/C coating is electrochemically stable, and it retards the serious side reaction with electrolyte and therefore the passivation effect. The suppression of passivation effect is also implied by the comparison of Li⁺ transference number ($\text{T}_\text{Li}^+$), which is estimated via potentiostatic polarization method combined with EIS measurement (see Supporting Information for details).[⁴⁸,⁴⁹] The Li/Li symmetric cells after cycling for 30 cycles were used for $\text{T}_\text{Li}^+$ estimation. The Li₂CN₂/C modification enables a much higher $\text{T}_\text{Li}^+$ of 0.57, compared to the value (0.31) of the bare Li case (Figure S12, Supporting Information). The improved Li⁺ transference number further confirms the durability Li₂CN₂/C during long-term electrochemical cycling. The preferential Li-ion permeation can mitigate the anion contact with Li metal and the consumption (or reduction) of electrolyte. The higher $\text{T}_\text{Li}^+$ value is also responsible for the acceleration of interface charge transfer and therefore the lowering of $E_\text{a}$ value.

To evaluate the dendrite resisting ability of SEI component, the critical length for a Li dendrite $(L_{c})$ is used as a criterion. $^{[50]}$ $L_{c}$ is defined as the minimum length before Li dendrite becomes to grow, and the larger $L_{c}$ value indicates the better dendrite suppression ability. $L_{c}$ is proportional to $\gamma E$ according to the following relation: $^{[50,51]}$

$$
L_{c}=\frac{2 \gamma E}{\pi \sigma^{2}} \quad (2)
$$

where $\gamma$ is the interfacial energy for the formation of a new Li/SEI interface, and $E$ is the bulk modulus. $\sigma$ is the stress at the tip of crack determined by the external current, which could be seen as a constant under the same testing condition. Some typical Li/SEI interface models, such as $Li/Li_{2}CN_{2}$, $Li/LiF$, and $Li/Li_{2}CO_{3}$, are constructed, in order to compare the dendrite resisting ability of $Li_{2}CN_{2}$, LiF, and $Li_{2}CO_{3}$ components (Figure 5g,h; Figure S13, Supporting Information). $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ has the highest interfacial energy of $77.57\ \text{meV}\ \mathring{A}^{-2}$ and highest bulk modulus of $104.12\ \text{GPa}$ (Figure 5i), leading to the highest $\gamma E$ value of $8076.44\ \text{meV}\ \mathring{A}^{-2}\ \text{GPa}$, compared with those of $Li_{2}CO_{3}$ and LiF (3827.25 and $5082.70\ \text{meV}\ \mathring{A}^{-2}\ \text{GPa}$, respectively). This comparison suggests the better Li dendrite suppression ability for $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ as robust SEI modifier than other typical SEI components.

The potential Li-ion transfer routes depending on the initial adsorption states and corresponding energy barriers are also discussed. The transference of Li-ions in $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ could be divided into three processes, including surface diffusion, surface-to-inner diffusion, and bulk diffusion (Figure 6). The surface diffusion of Li-ions on $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ shows three kinds of possibilities, including side migration along $\text{N=C=N}$ moieties (denoted as Surface-Side-Type$_{1}$), side migration across $\text{N=C=N}$ moieties (denoted as Surface-Side-Type$_{2}$), and top migration across $\text{N=C=N}$ (denoted as Surface-Top), as shown in Figure 6a–c. It is concluded that the Li-ion transferring to the nearer adjacent site shows the smaller energy barrier (e.g., $0.33\ \text{eV}$ for Surface-Side-Type$_{2}$, $0.27\ \text{eV}$ for Surface-Top, Figure 6d). After surface diffusion, Li-ions are expected to transfer from the outer surface of $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ to its inner bulk. This process displays two potential ways (Figure 6e,f), i.e., migration from surface to inner across $\text{N=C=N}$ and along $\text{N=C=N}$ with corresponding energy barriers of $1.23$ and $1.06\ \text{eV}$, respectively (Figure 6g). Then, Li-ions would diffuse in the bulk structure of $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ ionic conductor. There are also two potential diffusion paths, i.e., inner migration along $\text{N=C=N}$ and across $\text{N=C=N}$ with corresponding energy barriers of $1.62$ and $1.02\ \text{eV}$, respectively (Figure 6h–j). Therefore, the most possible Li-ion diffusion route is expected to be initiated by the top migration across $\text{N=C=N}$ at surface, then mediated by the migration from surface to inner along $\text{N=C=N}$, and then continued by the inner migration across $\text{N=C=N}$, in view of the principle of lowest diffusion energy barrier in each process. This diffusion route indicates that the ionic conductor $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ has 3D channels for Li-ion transfer. If the high lithiophilicity of $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ can trigger the Li interstitial transfer from LiF to $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ at the rich $LiF/Li_{2}CN_{2}$ interfaces in SEI. The overall Li-ion conductivity of SEI is expected to further increase in view of the improved space charge effect across the $LiF/Li_{2}CN_{2}$ heterostructure, which is characterized by Li vacancy accumulation at the Schottky-type LiF side with enhanced interface Li-ion conductivity.

## 3. Conclusion

In summary, we proposed a $Li_{2}CN_{2}$-based solid electrolyte interphase wired by carbon nanodomains, which is in situ synthesized on Li anode surface through a solution-reaction method. The theoretical calculations demonstrate that the highly lithiophilic $Li_{2}CN_{2}$ has the highest LUMO energy ($-0.97\ \text{eV}$) than those of other typical SEI components and the longest critical length for Li dendrite (with $\gamma E$ value as large as $8076.44\ \text{meV}\ \mathring{A}^{-2}\ \text{GPa}$), indicating its better anti-reduction stability and dendrite suppression ability. In addition, the interconnected carbon domains promote the electronic connection across SEI and smoothen the current distribution, leading to the homogeneous nucleation and growth of Li around the $Li_{2}CN_{2}/C$ grains with enhanced interface kinetics and reduced polarization. The $Li_{2}CN_{2}/C$ coating network can also inhibit the interface side reactions and promote the concentration of favorable SEI components (e.g., LiF, $\text{C-F}$, and $\text{C-N}$). Benefiting from this endurable artificial SEI, the Li nucleation overpotential is greatly reduced (e.g., from 71 to $6\ \text{mV}$ at $0.5\ \text{mA}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$), the voltage hysteresis and CE stability of Li plating/stripping are remarkably improved. The $Li_{2}CN_{2}/C$ modification endows the NMC811/Li cells with ultralong cycling life exceeding 1000 cycles at high rate. The cycling stabilization effect is also remarkable even under thin Li anode and high-loading cathode conditions. This work provides an insight into the in situ construction of novel solid electrolyte interphase and its conduction mechanism.
