# 2D Squaraine-Linked Polymers with High Lithium Storage Capacity Using the First Principle Methods

Ling Huang and Gengyu Cao*[a]

Lithium storage in two-dimensional squaraine-linked covalent organic frameworks (2D SQ-COPs: SQ-COP-1, SQ-COP-2 and SQ-COP-3, respectively constructed by squaraine linker and small heterocyclic molecule nodes $B_3O_3$, $H_3B_3N_3$ and $C_3N_3$), have been studied by using the first principle methods. For the adsorption of multiple Li atoms, there is no $Li_2$ dimer or cluster formation in SQ-COPs, but Li–O bonds formation in SQ-COP-1 and SQ-COP-3. Accumulated Li atoms in $Li_n$@SQ-COPs will be arranged to produce regular 2D hexagonal packed configurations due to the effects of confined space. Bader analysis reveals charge transfer from Li atoms to SQ-COPs, indicating high lithiation. Among 2D SQ-COPs, SQ-COP-1 shows better conductivity than SQ-COP-2, and maintains stable adsorption configuration compared with SQ-COP-3. Furthermore, dynamical calculations indicate that Li atom prefers to move across the 1D pores of SQ-COP-1, overcoming a very low energy barrier, which further enhances Li storage capacity. Such good Li storage capacity in 2D metal-free SQ-COPs might lead to a promising and environmental-friendly application in Li ion batteries.

## Introduction

Lithium ion batteries (LIBs) are of light weight, high capacitance and long lifetime, thus being considered as effective energy storage devices and widely employed in vehicles, laptops and portable devices. The presence of LIBs invokes many active researches, for which the development of electrode materials is essential for improvement of charge-discharge efficiency. Intercalation cathode materials¹ of LIBs in popular use include 2D $LiCoO_2$, $LiNiO_2$, $LiMnO_2$, $LiV_2O_5$; 3D $LiMn_2O_4$, $LiFePO_4$, and so on, which show high energy density and charge-discharge efficiency. However, there are many security problems for application of metal intercalation, which might even result in the environmental pollution due to the leakage of heavy metal. Meanwhile, some organic materials have also been found able to enhance lithium capacities and thus considered as promising anode materials, especially the organic conjugated polymeric materials are predicted to be electrode materials for the next-generation high-performance LIBs. Materials designing on the molecular level is an alternative strategy for improving Li storage performance. In recent years, covalent organic frameworks (COFs), as a member of metal-free organic materials, have attracted attention because of their low density, large specific surface areas, uniform porosity and particularly no pollution. Compared with 3D COFs, 2D COFs exhibit desirable charge transfer performance and open porous channels, thus being employed in optoelectronic devices, gas storage and catalysis. It has been reported that 2D COFs can be used as anode in LIBs² and capacitors.³ Hence, it is meaningful to develop electrodes based on 2D COFs. As is known, properties are determined by structures, so choosing proper building blocks is a basis for creating desirable properties. Squaraine, with a conjugated structure, has been adopted in 2D COFs and reported to enhance charge transfer performance as well as structural stability,⁴ which reveals that squaraine might be an ideal building unit in constructing new materials. Since squaraine has never been reported for application of electrodes in LIBs, it is meaningful to study the squaraine-involved 2D COFs for Li storage.

Correspondingly, we have previously designed 2D squaraine-bridged covalent organic frameworks (2D SQ-COPs),⁵ as presented in Figure 1, these 2D SQ-COPs show graphite-like layered configurations, which are composed of squaraine (linker) and small heterocyclic molecules (node, i.e., $B_3O_3$, $H_3B_3N_3$ and $C_3N_3$). By using molecular simulation methods, these 2D SQ-COPs have been predicted to possess high porosity and large BET surface areas compared with other 2D porous materials. What's more, the confined space from 2D SQ-COPs is in range of 1.38-1.58 nm. Recently, that the effects of confined space is beneficial for enhancing charge transfer and accommodating volume expansion of sulfur in Li–S batteries has been reported by He et al,⁶ indicating the positive potential of confined space in promoting performance of LIBs. Inspired by what are mentioned above about the characteristics of squaraine as well as 2D COFs, we think 2D SQ-COPs might play an important role as electrode materials.

To study the Li storage performance of 2D SQ-COPs, the adsorption behaviors of Li atoms including adsorption energies, adsorption configurations and diffusion paths are all considered

## Results and Discussion
### Calculated electronic structures of 2D SQ-COPs.

Electronic properties of 2D SQ-COPs are necessary to study. As is known, proper electronic properties are often able to assist the adsorption and/or desorption behaviors of Li atoms.[⁷] Figure 2 shows the calculated HOMO, LUMO of building blocks and total DOS of SQ-COPs. As shown in Figure 2(a), the HOMO and LUMO of building blocks illustrate that majority of charge appears around the squaraine linkage, while node molecules (i.e., $B_3O_3$, $H_3B_3N_3$ and $C_3N_3$) contribute the minority, thus indicating the dominant role of squaraine linkage in determining the electronic property of SQ-COPs. What's more, N atoms in $H_3B_3N_3$ (SQ-COP-2) and $C_3N_3$ (SQ-COP-3) also contribute to the whole charge distribution.

Furthermore, Figure 2(b) shows the total DOS of 2D SQ-COPs. As presented in Figure 2(b), SQ-COPs exhibit zero DOS at the E-fermi level and there is narrow gap between the top of valence band and the bottom of conductance band. It can be concluded that these 2D SQ-COPs possess semiconducting property. In addition, as shown in Figure 3, the band gaps of SQ-COP-1, SQ-COP-2 and SQ-COP-3 are respectively calculated to be 1.98, 2.14 and 1.79 eV via band structure calculations, indicating the conductivity of SQ-COP-1 and SQ-COP-3 is slightly better than that of SQ-COP-2.

### Li adsorption in single-layer SQ-COPs.

The adsorption behaviors of Li atoms in 2D SQ-COPs is essential to explore. As mentioned above, the pore sizes of SQ-COPs are large enough, which is decisive to improve the Li storage capacity. Besides, it should be also noted that the squaraine linkage of SQ-COP-2 is vertical to the 2D plane, while those of SQ-COP-1 and SQ-COP-3 are nearly parallel to the plane, potentially resulting in different steric hindrance as Li atoms are moving.

In the previous studies, multiple Li atoms in the adsorbed phase tend to form $Li_2$ dimmers and result in low lithiation efficiency, $^{[8]}$ therefore, formation of $Li_2$ dimmers should be avoided in order to improve Li storage capacity. Figure 4 shows the adsorption configurations for two Li atoms on 2D SQ-COPs. As is shown in Figure 4, the distance between two Li atoms in SQ-COP-1, SQ-COP-2 and SQ-COP-3 are 2.74, 3.43 and 2.75 $\mathring{A}$, respectively, which are much larger than that in $Li_2$ dimers (2.72 $\mathring{A}$). In addition, the adsorption energy of two Li atoms on SQ-COP-1, SQ-COP-2 and SQ-COP-3 are respectively calculated to be $-1.42, -0.89$ and $-1.45$ eV, among which the Li adsorption energy on both SQ-COP-1 and SQ-COP-3 are much higher than the binding energy of $Li_2$ dimer (-0.47 eV/atom, which is in good agreement with experimental data, $-0.51$ eV/ atom), and even larger than the average adsorption energy of multiple Li-pyridinic (-1.26 eV) and Li-pyrrolic (-1.19 eV) gra- phene systems, $^{[9]}$ further indicating that the formation of $Li_2$ dimers in SQ-COPs is thermodynamically unfavorable.

Furthermore, adsorption of multiple Li atoms in SQ-COPs have also been studied. It can be found that when six Li atoms are adsorbed in SQ-COP-1, one Li-O bond with length of 1.79 $\mathring{A}$ starts to form between O and Li atoms, and it is the same case for SQ-COP-3 while adsorbing four Li atoms, suggesting the presence of chemisorptions behaviors between Li atoms and frameworks; while in SQ-COP-2, no Li-O bond forms. The adsorption configuration involving multiple Li atoms shows that the average distance of Li atoms between each other exceeds 3 $\mathring{A}$, which is in favor of improving Li storage capacity without formation of Li clusters.

In order to find out the maximal adsorption number of Li atoms in single-layer SQ-COPs, the amount of Li atoms has been increased. Figure 5 illustrates the adsorption configura- tions of multiple Li atoms in single-layer SQ-COPs. It can be found with the number of Li atoms increasing, the arrangement of Li atoms in the confined space becomes regular. In SQ-COP-1 and SQ-COP-3, Li atoms tend to arrange following the hexagonal symmetry, and each O atom of squaraine linkage bonds with one Li atom; while for SQ-COP-2, no Li-O bond forms. Besides, it should be noted that when the number of Li atoms increase to large enough, for example $\geq 36$, one of the Li-O bonds in SQ-COP-3 tends to break, which might be the result of competition between Li-Li and Li-O interactions, namely the affinity produced by too many Li atoms is strong enough to draw the Li atom from Li-O bond.

Bader charge analysis for charge transfer between Li atoms and SQ-COPs.

In order to further study the Li adsorption mechanism, bader analysis for charge transfer between Li and frameworks have been performed. Table 1 summarizes the total charge transfer from Li atoms to SQ-COPs in $Li_n@SQ$-COPs systems. As shown in Table 1, for single Li adsorption, there is 0.1e charge transfer in Li@SQ-COP-1, SQ-COP-2 and SQ-COP-3. Then with the number of Li atoms increasing, the charge transfer from Li to frameworks is also rising. For two Li atoms adsorption, 0.3e transfers from Li atoms to SQ-COPs. The increasing charge transfer might further enhance the affinity of SQ-COPs to Li atoms and can be used to explain the strong adsorption energy in $Li_2@$ SQ-COPs mentioned above.

What's more, 0.1e charge transfer occurs between one Li atom and the other, which indicates that the interaction between Li atoms can't be neglected. Moreover, 1e charge transfer from Li to SQ-COP-2 comes up when three or more Li atoms are adsorbed, except for $Li_{36}@SQ$-COP-2 and $Li_{37}@SQ$-COP-2, in which there is no charge transfer from Li atoms toSQ-COP-2, perhaps due to the large distance between Li atoms and framework wall. As mentioned above, compared with SQ-COP-2 and SQ-COP-3, SQ-COP-1 could maintain the regular Li adsorption configurations, and also show better charge transfer property from Li to SQ-COP-1 in $Li_n@SQ$-COP-1. Therefore, it is SQ-COP-1 that might be a promising candidate for Li storage.

Dynamic simulation of Li diffusion in SQ-COP-1.

In addition to the charge transfer property, diffusion behavior of Li atoms is also necessary to study for understanding related dynamic characteristic in Li storage. Since SQ-COP-1 is considered to be a promising candidate for Li storage, it is of need to consider the diffusion path of Li atoms in Li@SQ-COP-1. Figure 6 proposes two possible diffusion paths (DPs), which are respectively named DP1 and DP2. In this work, DP1 represents that Li atoms diffuse in parallel to the 2D plane, and DP2 suggests that Li atoms moves across the pore center.

Based on nudged energy band (NEB) method, it is found that due to the presence of squariane linkage, Li diffusion via DP1 is hindered. Figure 7 illustrates the configuration for Li diffusion via DP1, as shown in Figure 7, Li atom tends to connect to O, with the Li-O bond length of 1.72 Å. Additionally, the phenyl ring in linker moiety shows strong affinity to Li atom and Li would be stuck on phenyl ring, which results in the deformation of framework. Therefore, Li diffusion via DP1 seems to be infeasible. On the contrary, in the case of Li diffusion via DP2, the barrier energy for Li diffusion, as shown in Figure 8, is calculated to be as low as 0.002 eV, which is quite easy to overcome and might further help to promote the Li storage in the one-dimensional hexagonal pores.
