After removal of imaginary frequencies when necessary, the energy minimum structures for diexo isomers (1)-(4) were obtained - Figure 1 - which correspond to one Li atom above any triangular $B_3$ face (face-1) and another Li atom above faces 3-6 away from face-1. Figure 1 displays some of the symmetry operators of each isomer in order to highlight their symmetry properties. We should emphasize that no local energy minimum was located in $Li_2B_{12}H_{12}$ with two Li atoms above contiguous faces of the $B_{12}H_{12}$ icosahedron. As regards to potential exo/endo isomers, we simply started the geometry optimization with one Li above any of the equivalent $B_3$ faces of the $B_{12}H_{12}$ icosahedron and one Li atom in the center of the icosahedron: the resulting optimized structure, isomer (5), is displayed in Figure 1e, with $C_{3v}$ symmetry.

Therefore, after a comprehensive search of the potential energy surface of the $Li_2B_{12}H_{12}$ system - maintaining the $B_{12}H_{12}$ cage unaltered - the summary of the computations is that there are four stable diexo isomers with $Li^+$ ions above different (non-contiguous) faces of the $B_{12}H_{12}^{2-}$ icosahedron - $Li\cdots\{B_{12}H_{12}^{2-}\}\cdots Li$ - isomers (1)-(4) - and one exo/endo isomer $Li\cdots\{Li@B_{12}H_{12}\}$ - isomer (5). Table 1 summarises the labelling, symmetry and energies of the local energy minima.

The description of the electronic structure in diexo isomers (1)-(4) can be assessed simply by stating that the further the Li atoms between each other the lower the energy:

$$-\text{Energy} : \boldsymbol{(1)} > \boldsymbol{(2)} > \boldsymbol{(3)} > \boldsymbol{(4)} >> \boldsymbol{(5)} \tag{1}$$

A similar behaviour was found regarding the position of Li atoms in exo $Li\cdots r\text{-}C_2B_{10}H_{12}$ - $r=\{ortho, meta, para\}$ - and $Li\cdots CB_{11}H_{12}$ [17] complexes, with the Li atom farthest away from the carbon atoms. As expected from previous computational studies [12,17], addition of a Li atom inside the $B_{12}H_{12}$ cage involves a large energy penalty. However, the energy difference between diexo isomers is within a 1.4-11.4 kcal/mol energy window, the lowest energy isomer being (1) - Figure 1a - with Li atoms farthest apart from each other above faces 1-6 ('para' isomer), $D_{3d}$ symmetry.

We now turn to the computation of the energy barrier for interconversion between isomers (1)-(5). In order to find a transition state for the process $\boldsymbol{(i)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(i+1)}$, with $1 \leqslant \boldsymbol{i} \leqslant 3$, and $\boldsymbol{(5)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(2)}$, we just leave a Li atom above a face of the optimized structure in isomer (i) and move the other Li atom towards a sharing edge of isomers (i) and (i+1), and relax the nuclei towards a transition state. For the more complex case of the isomerization $\boldsymbol{(5)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(2)}$ we follow the procedure described further below. The following processes are studied (Fig. 2):

$$\boldsymbol{(1)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(2)}; \boldsymbol{(2)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(3)}; \boldsymbol{(3)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(4)}; \boldsymbol{(5)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(2)} \tag{2}$$

Table 2 summarises the energetics of transition state structures found from Eq.(2), with geometries and energy profiles displayed in Figures 2 and 3 respectively. Also shown in Figure 2 is the motion of one of the Li atoms from one face to another in the $\boldsymbol{(i)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(i+1)}$ and $\boldsymbol{(5)} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{(2)}$ conversion, which corresponds to the mode of the single imaginary frequency.

Figure 3 displays the energy profile for interconversion between isomers (1)-(5), including energy barriers. In order to better visualize the 'thermal motion' of the two Li atoms above the 'allowed' faces of the $B_{12}H_{12}$ icosahedron, the hydrogen atoms are removed for clarity in Figure 3. Thus, as reported in the literature for similar systems [18-22], both Li atoms are supposed to orbit - within and energy barrier window of 5-8 kcal/mol - around the cage avoiding each other at contiguous faces, since no energy minimum was found for the latter situation (vide supra).
