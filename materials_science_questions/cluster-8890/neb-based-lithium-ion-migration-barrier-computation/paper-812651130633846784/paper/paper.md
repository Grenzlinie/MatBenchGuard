### Abstract
Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries have received much exploration these years and developing suitable anchoring materials is a great challenge to improve the performance of the batteries. In this study, first-principles with density functional theory study was adopted to investigate the anchoring behavior of lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) on $InP_3$ monolayer. The structures of different LiPSs adsorbed on $InP_3$ monolayer were obtained with calculation of the adsorption energy, charge transfer and adsorption distance. The electron distribution and the electronic properties were calculated and the diffusion of LiPSs on $InP_3$ monolayer was studied. The results indicate that $InP_3$ monolayer shows strong chemical interactions with LiPSs because of the considerable adsorption energies (from $-1.08$ eV to $-4.43$ eV in vacuum and $-0.94$ eV to $-3.21$ eV in DME/DOL solvent). The adsorption of LiPSs on $InP_3$ monolayer can also result in electron redistribution and reduction of the energy gap near Fermi-level in density of states (DOS). The energy barrier of $Li_2S$ diffusion is only $+1.52$ eV, which is much smaller than the absolute value of adsorption energy ($-4.43$ eV) which illustrates that it is easy for diffusion and migration of $Li_2S$ on the surface of $InP_3$. Our study demonstrates that $InP_3$ monolayer can be an excellent anchoring material for Li-S batteries and can provide theoretical basis of exploring novel 2D materials in field of energy storage.

**Keywords**: Li-S battery; $InP_3$ monolayer; Adsorption; Diffusion; Density functional theory (DFT)

### 1. Introduction

Batteries are closely related to the human society and for the development of several decades, they have been applied in lots of fields, including portable equipment, electric vehicle, smart power grid etc. The higher demand of energy density has been a great challenge of battery research and development. Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries are one of the most promising next-generation rechargeable supplies to replace Li-ion batteries [1-6]. The reported theoretical specific capacity and energy density reach about $1670\ \text{mA h g}^{-1}$ and $2600\ \text{W h kg}^{-1}$, respectively [7]. Comparing to Li-ion batteries, the Li-S batteries is more low-cost and environmentally-friendly but because of the poor electronic conductivity of a series of lithium polysulfides ($\text{Li}_2\text{S}_n$) and sulfur, poor coulombic efficiency, shuttling effect, and the corrosion of anode materials from the dissolved $\text{Li}_2\text{S}_n$, the commercial application of Li-S batteries is still restricted and these problems must be considered for design of electrode materials [8, 9].

In order to overcome these drawbacks especially for the shuttle effect, one way is to strengthen the chemical interactions between $\text{Li}_2\text{S}_n$ species and the anchoring materials. However, some traditional materials have shown very weak interactions with LiPSs, such as graphene, 2D boron nitride [10], $\text{MoS}_2$ [9] etc. Two methods to enhance the anchor effects are: surface modification and utility of novel 2D materials. For the first method, several studies focus on the surface defects which influence the anchor effect. For example, the doping of nitrogen and the introduction of vacancies can adjust the adsorption behavior of LiPSs on graphene [11-14]. The single vacancy defect on the surface of blue phosphorene significantly increases the binding force to LiPSs [15]. For the second method, with the rising of several novel 2D materials and their unique structures with large specific area and adsorption sites, researchers have made extensive study to search suitable anchor materials for Li-S batteries. Comparing to graphene, carbon borides and nitrides have larger chemical interactions with LiPSs, such as $\text{BC}_3$, $\text{C}_2\text{N}$, $\text{C}_3\text{N}$, $\text{gC}_3\text{N}_4$, $\text{C}_4\text{N}_4$ etc [10, 16, 17]. Beside those, Xu and Fan et al reported the anchor behavior of LiPSs on a series of transition metal carbides, nitrides (MXenes) to find the most suitable MXenes served as suitable host materials in Li-S batteries [18, 19]. Wang and Zhang et al explored the LiPSs adsorption on different kinds of 2D metal-organic frameworks (MOF) and theoretically evaluated the energy density [20-21]. The calculated energy density reaches $1395\ \text{W h kg}^{-1}$ which shows that some kinds of MOF can be a promising candidate for cathode material [20]. These theoretical studies indicate that, it's essential to use DFT for investigating adsorption behavior and chemical interactions between the substrate and the LiPSs to evaluate the suitability for them to be anchoring materials in Li-S batteries, and these theoretical studies can provide clues for experimental realization.

Recently, novel 2D materials composed of main group elements have been extensively studied, especially for group-III and V element. Among these species, black phosphorene was first discovered in 2014 and it shows excellent physical and chemical properties [22, 23]. Moreover, several similar 2D monolayers have been explored, including borophene, arsenene, antimonene, arsenic-phosphorus and antimony arsenide [24, 25] etc. Some of them have higher carrier mobility and optical properties and even more physical and chemical stable than phosphrene. These kinds of 2D materials have promising application in electronic and optical devices. In addition to these, Sun et al reported a new kind of 2D materials, $\text{InP}_3$,

which shows high stability and reaches an electron mobility of $1919\ \mathrm{cm^2V^{-1}S^{-1}}$. Moreover, the exfoliation from its bulk crystal is feasible in experiment [26]. After that, thermal properties, gas sensing properties, catalytic performance, energy storage properties of $\mathrm{InP_3}$ has been investigated [27-31]. Especially, it has very low diffusion barrier of only 0.06 eV for Na atom and can be used as anode materials for alkali-metal batteries [31].

Inspired by the promising usage of $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer in metal-ions batteries, herein, we investigated the anchoring behavior of $\mathrm{InP_3}$ to a serious LiPSs and sulfur ($\mathrm{Li_2S}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_2}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_4}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_6}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_8}$ and $\mathrm{S_8}$) through first-principles study. The adsorption performance of Li-S battery related species on $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer was respectively discussed. The van der Waals interactions were taken into consideration and for the solvent effect, the adsorptions in vacuum and in 1,2-dimethoxyethane(DME)/1,3-dioxolane(DOL) were compared. The anchoring mechanisms including the charge transfer, electron distribution, electronic properties were discussed and the diffusion behavior was calculated. We believe this study can provide theoretical basis of $\mathrm{InP_3}$ served as anchoring materials in Li-S batteries.

## 2. Computational methods

All the spin-polarized first-principles calculations were implemented in $\mathrm{Dmol^3}$ module [32-33]. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) realized by Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof function (PBE) with double numerical polarization (DNP) basis set was included to calculate the exchange-correction function [34]. The core and valence electrons were managed using the DFT semi-core pseudopotential (DSSP) and the long-range van der Waals interactions were calculated using DFT-D2 method proposed by Grimme et al [35]. The orbital cut off radius was $5.0\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ and the vacuum slab was $20\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$. The geometric optimization was carried out with convergence criteria of $1.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{Ha}$ in energy, $0.002\ \mathrm{Ha/\mathring{A}}$ in maximum force, and $0.005\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ in maximum displacement (1 Ha is equal to 27.21 eV). A $2\times2$ supercell of $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer was built and a $4\times4\times1$ Monkhorst-Pack K-point was set in the first Brillouin zone for geometric optimization and calculation of density of states [36]. The adsorption energy of LiPSs and $\mathrm{S_8}$ adsorbed on $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer is calculated as follow:

$$
E_{\mathrm{ads}}=E_{\mathrm{InP_3/LiPS}}-E_{\mathrm{InP_3}}-E_{\mathrm{LiPS}} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{InP_3/LiPS}}$, $E_{\mathrm{InP_3}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{LiPS}}$ are the calculated total energies of $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer with adsorption of Li-S related species ($\mathrm{Li_2S}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_2}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_4}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_6}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_8}$ and $\mathrm{S_8}$), $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer before adsorption, and isolated Li-S related species ($\mathrm{Li_2S}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_2}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_4}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_6}$, $\mathrm{Li_2S_8}$ and $\mathrm{S_8}$). However, the calculated energies in vacuum may not reflect the adsorption behavior in batteries correctly. So, the adsorptions in DME/DOL solvent were included and compared and no explicit solvent molecules were included in the study. The solvent effects were implement using the conductor-like screening model (COSMO) and the dielectric constant of DME/DOL was set to 7.17 [21]. And the adsorption energy is calculated as:

$$
E_{\mathrm{ads}}(\mathrm{sol})=E_{\mathrm{InP_3/LiPS}}(\mathrm{sol})-E_{\mathrm{InP_3}}(\mathrm{sol})-E_{\mathrm{LiPS}}(\mathrm{sol}) \tag{2}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{InP_3/LiPS}}(\mathrm{sol})$, $E_{\mathrm{InP_3}}(\mathrm{sol})$ and $E_{\mathrm{LiPS}}(\mathrm{sol})$ are the calculated total energies of $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer with adsorption of Li-S related species in solvent, $\mathrm{InP_3}$ monolayer before adsorption in solvent, and isolated Li-S related species in solvent. The charge transfer was

calculated using the method of Mulliken analysis [37], and if the value is below 0, it means that the electrons transfer from the surface to the adsorbed LiPS. And to make a visualization of charge transfer, the charge density difference (CDD) configurations were calculated using the equation:

$$\Delta \rho=\rho_{\mathrm{InP}_{3} / \mathrm{LiPS}}-\rho_{\mathrm{InP}_{3}}-\rho_{\mathrm{LiPS}} \tag{3}$$

where $\rho_{\mathrm{InP}_{3} / \mathrm{LiPS}}$, $\rho_{\mathrm{InP}_{3}}$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{LiPS}}$ are the calculated electron density of $\mathrm{InP}_{3}$ monolayer with adsorption of Li-S related species in solvent, $\mathrm{InP}_{3}$ monolayer before adsorption in solvent, and isolated Li-S related species in solvent. To discuss diffusion behavior, the transition state (TS) was calculated using the complete linear synchronous transit (LST) and quadratic synchronous transit (QST) [38]. The convergence value of RMS (Root Mean Square) of LST/QST is 0.002 Ha/Å.

It can be seen that the $InP_3$ monolayer has an indirect band gap of about 0.85 eV obtained by PBE method. It is seen that the DME/DOL solvent only slightly changes the bond lengths of LiPSs but the whole structures themselves have not been changed obviously, and the structures are in agreement with the former studies [16-21].

### 3.2. Adsorption of LiPSs on the surface of $InP_3$ monolayer

To search the adsorption structures with the minimum total energy, for every Li-S related species, we first placed it on the surface with several adsorption sites. After geometric optimization, we only chose the adsorption structures with the maximum adsorption energy (minimum total energy) to carry out further discussion. All the adsorption structures in solvent are shown in Fig. 2. And the adsorption energies as well as the charge transfer, adsorption distances in vacuum and in solvent are all listed in Table 1. For the adsorption of $Li_2S$, the S atom is right up to In atom while every Li atom locates on the top of one protruding P atom. The bond length of Li-S increases to 2.37 Å with the bond angle of Li-S-Li increases from $115.3^\circ$ to $130.7^\circ$. It is also found that the Li-S is nearly parallel to the In-P bond in $InP_3$ monolayer. The distance between the S in $Li_2S$ and the In on the surface is about 2.43 Å. The adsorption of $Li_2S_2$ makes it more flat because of the decrease of Li-S-Li bond angle from $90.8^\circ$ to $122.6^\circ$. The adsorption of $Li_2S_4$ lengthens the distance between two S atoms on both ends from 3.81 Å to 5.04 Å and these two S atoms which are adjacent to Li atoms locate over two protruding In atoms. The distance between them is 2.67 Å. For the adsorption of $Li_2S_6$ and $Li_2 S_8$, the location of Li atoms and their adjacent two S atoms are very similar than that of the adsorption of $Li_2S_4$ and the adsorption distances of $Li_2S_6$ and $Li_2S_8$ are about 2.67 Å and 2.63 Å, which is also nearly the same as that of $Li_2S_4$ adsorption. The $S_8$ adsorption shows a much longer adsorption distance of 2.97 Å and the plane of $S_8$ is nearly parallel to the $InP_3$ surface.

The summary of adsorption energy with and without vdW interactions, in vacuum and in solvent are all shown in Fig. 3 and the values are also in Table 1. To make a deep insight into the adsorption mechanism, the physical and chemical interactions are both discussed. The total adsorption energy is the sum of the chemical interactions and the vdW interactions, and the adsorption energy without vdW interactions is only seen as the chemical interactions. It is found that, for the adsorption of Li₂S, Li₂S₂, Li₂S₄, Li₂S₆ and Li₂S₈, the chemical interactions play dominant roles both in vacuum and in solvent. In vacuum, the ratio for vdW interactions ranges from 15% (Li₂S) to 39% (Li₂S₈) and in solvent, the ratio is from 19% (Li₂S) to 49% (Li₂S₆). As to the S₈ adsorption, the vdW interactions are the majority, and the ratio of vdW interactions is 68% (in vacuum) and 78% (in solvent). When considering the solvent effect, the total adsorption energies obviously decrease from –4.43 eV (Li₂S), –3.48 eV (Li₂S₂), –2.62 eV (Li₂S₄), –2.54 eV (Li₂S₆), –2.99 eV (Li₂S₈), –1.08 eV (S₈) to –3.21 eV (Li₂S), –2.54 eV (Li₂S₂), –2.11 eV (Li₂S₄), –1.93 eV (Li₂S₆), –2.37 eV (Li₂S₈), –0.94 eV (S₈). From the results, the DME/DOL solvent weakens the chemical interactions between the InP₃ monolayer and the LiPSs. The calculation of charge transfer indicates that all the LiPSs lose electrons and serve as electron donors, and except for S₈, the adsorption of Li₂Sₙ (n=1 to 8) show obvious betatopic phenomenon. For deeper investigation of charge transfer, the CDD configurations are calculated and shown in Fig. 4. With the same isosurface (0.01 eÅ⁻³), the adsorptions of LiPSs exhibit obvious charge redistribution except for S₈. The adsorption of Li₂S makes electrons accumulate between Li atoms and P atoms in InP₃ monolayer, and the top of S atom in Li₂S exhibits electron depletion. Also, the electron accumulation phenomenon between the Li and the surface appears in all the adsorptions of Li₂S₂, Li₂S₄, Li₂S₆, Li₂S₈. And the electron depletion regions are mainly beside S atoms in Li₂S, Li₂S₂, Li₂S₄, Li₂S₆, Li₂S₈. So, for the adsorption of Li₂Sₙ (n=1 to 8), the chemical interactions are mainly from the adsorption of Li atoms with P atoms and also partially from the interactions between S and In atom. And for the adsorption of S₈, slight electron accumulation can be found between one S atom and one In atom, but the region is much smaller compared to thoes of the adsorptions of Li₂Sₙ. Because of the apparent electron redistribution, the CDD also demonstrates that the chemical interactions are more obviously for the adsorption of Li₂Sₙ than S₈. the adsorption of S₈ shows the least electron redistribution as well as the chemical interactions.

As a kind of anchoring material, the electrical conductivity should also be considered. The results of the total density of states (TDOS) of $InP_3$ monolayer before and after the adsorption of LiPSs are shown in Fig. 5. The pristine $InP_3$ shows semiconductor properties with an indirect band gap and in DOS, there shows an energy gap near Fermi-level. With the adsorption of $Li_2S_n$, the energy gap became narrow after the adsorption of $Li_2S_4$, $Li_2S_6$ and $Li_2S_8$ and for $Li_2S$, $Li_2S_2$ and $S_8$, the energy gap is nearly unchanged. So, with the adsorption of $Li_2S_n$, the overall trend of energy gap has declined, resulting in the increase of the conductivity of $InP_3$ which is benefit for the electron transportation. The charging process makes the LiPSs with low S content (such as $Li_2S$ and $Li_2S_2$) converting to LiPSs with high S content ($Li_2S_6$, $Li_2S_8$) and $S_8$ [39]. This process also accelerates the bond breaking of Li-S resulting in the diffusing of Li atom on $InP_3$ monolayer. Previous study demonstrated that, after adsorbing one Li atom, the $InP_3$ monolayer experiences the disappear of the band gap and loses its semiconducting property. This phenomenon makes $InP_3$ obviously improve its conductivity and electron transportation property after adsorbing Li atoms.

### 3.3. Diffusion of LiPSs on $InP_3$ monolayer

The diffusion behavior of LiPSs on $InP_3$ monolayer also reflects the performance of Li-S battery. As a result, the diffusion pathway as well as the energy barrier have been calculated. Previous study reported that the diffusion energy of different LiPSs on 2D materials may have some differences, however, there exist a linear relationship between the diffusion barrier and the number of S atoms in the cluster [17]. With the increase value of the number of S atoms from $Li_2S$ to $Li_2S_8$, the diffusion barrier decreases and becomes the least for the diffusion of $S_8$. As a result, only calculating the diffusion behavior of $Li_2S$ is enough to reflect the charge and discharge performance to some extent. We theoretically investigate the diffusion behavior of $Li_2S$ on $InP_3$ monolayer from one adsorption site to another equivalent adsorption site. The diffusion path way and the value of energy barrier are shown in Fig. 6. For the adsorption of $Li_2S$ on $InP_3$ monolayer, the S atom locates above one protruding one In atom in $InP_3$ and as to another equivalent adsorption site, the S atom is above another protruding In atom. The calculated total energy of these two structures are the same. The geometric structure of transition state during the diffusion is shown in Fig. 6(b). It can be seen that the diffusion barrier is about 1.52 eV, much smaller than the absolute value adsorption energy (4.43 eV). The diffusion coefficient can be evaluated through the Arrhenius equation as follow:

$$
D \propto e^{\frac{-E_{\mathrm{b}}}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}}
\tag{4}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{b}}$ is the diffusion energy barrier of $Li_2S$ on $InP_3$ monolayer, $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the temperature. Because the diffusion barrier is very similar than the reported $C_4N_4$ monolayer (1.497 eV) for $Li_2S$ [17], we evaluate that the $InP_3$ monolayer has very similar charge and discharge behavior with $C_4N_4$ monolayer which indicates that the proper diffusion barrier can make $InP_3$ be an acceptable anchoring material for Li-S batteries.

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, using the first-principles study with density functional theory, we investigated the $InP_3$ monolayer as a kind of anchoring material for Li-S batteries. The $InP_3$ monolayer exhibits high adsorption ability toward LiPSs and $S_8$. The adsorption energies of LiPSs and $S_8$ reach $-4.43$ eV ($Li_2S$), $-3.48$ eV ($Li_2S_2$), $-2.62$ eV ($Li_2S_4$), $-2.54$ eV ($Li_2S_6$), $-2.99$ eV ($Li_2S_8$), $-1.08$ eV ($S_8$) in vacuum and $-3.21$ eV ($Li_2S$), $-2.54$ eV ($Li_2S_2$), $-2.11$ eV ($Li_2S_4$), $-1.93$ eV ($Li_2S_6$), $-2.37$ eV ($Li_2S_8$), $-0.94$ eV ($S_8$) in DME/DOL solvent, respectively. Mulliken charge analysis demonstrates that the LiPSs lose electrons and act as electron donors after adsorption on $InP_3$ monolayer and $S_8$ adsorption shows much less charge transfer. From the CDD results, obvious charge redistribution happens when $Li_2S$, $Li_2S_2$, $Li_2S_4$, $Li_2S_6$ and $Li_2S_8$ adsorption. Electron accumulation region appears mainly between the Li atoms in LiPSs and the P atoms in $InP_3$ and the electron depletion is around the S atoms in LiPSs. The TDOS plots indicate that the $InP_3$ monolayer experience an elevation of conductivity after adsorbing $Li_2S_4$, $Li_2S_6$ and $Li_2S_8$. The energy barrier of diffusion process of $Li_2S$ on the $InP_3$ monolayer is only 1.52 eV, much smaller than the absolute value pf adsorption energy (4.43eV), demonstrating that the easy migration of LiPSs. In general, this study suggests that the $InP_3$ monolayer can be a promising anchoring material for Li-S batteries in cathode and provide theoretical clues for exploring novel 2D materials in fields of batteries.
