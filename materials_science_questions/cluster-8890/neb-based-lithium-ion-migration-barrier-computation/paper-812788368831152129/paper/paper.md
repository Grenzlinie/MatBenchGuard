## Abstract

There is great interest in finding suitable electrode materials for metal-ion batteries with good performance, low diffusion barriers and high capacity. Using the art of density functional theory (DFT), we systematically evaluated the possibility of planar carbon haeckelite structures (h567, r57, and o567) for a suitable anode in Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs). Our results show that haeckelites possess significant structural, mechanical, and electronic stability with high metallicity for LIB anode applications. Especially, the haeckelite h567 shows improved specific capacity ($\text{Li}_{1.875}\text{C}_6$ ~ 697 mAhg$^{-1}$) compared to $\text{LiC}_6$ graphite due to the negative Li binding energy without clustering of Li atoms. In addition, it is worth noticing that the low open-circuit voltage (< 0.30 V) and Li diffusion energy barrier ($E_a < 0.35$ eV) of the haeckelite h567comparable to that of the graphite is beneficial to the overall performance of the LIBs. Based on the excellent electronic structure, superior Li mobility, extremely high in-plane stiffness, low open-circuit voltage, and high specific capacity, haeckelite h567 can be a promising anode material for the low-cost and high-performance LIBs.

Keywords: 2D carbon haeckelite, density functional theory, lithium-ion battery, diffusion barrier, specific capacity, open circuit voltage

### 1. Introduction

The development of cleaner and sustainable energy resources is one of the vital dispute of this century. Over the past few years, carbon-based materials have attracted much attention as potential anode candidates for energy storage applications. Because of its good cyclic ability, flat and low voltage range, low cost, high Coulombic efficiency, and comparatively small volume change on Li intercalation [1-3], graphite is used as a favorable anode material for commercial LIBs. Li can be stored in graphite at a ratio of $LiC_6$ (one Li atom per six carbon atoms) with a theoretical capacity of 372 mAh/g [4]. In addition, the great success of graphene [5], which has unique properties and potential applications, has encouraged researchers to exploit its features for use in LIBs [6]. Moreover, materials comprised of planar carbon structures have been proposed for use in a variety of electrochemical storage devices, either as an inactive component or active materials [7]. Recent studies have explored the possible computational insights and also the experimental realization of two-dimensional (2D) defected or porous graphene-like materials and carbon-based metallic or semi-metallic structures as anode materials for electrochemical energy storage devices [8-10]. During the process of charging, the layered structure of a 2D system allows the Li atom to intercalate between the layers. Commonly occurring defects in graphene can also provide active positions for Li to occupy within the surface. Moreover, the volume expansion that occurs during the Li intercalation process in graphene is commensurate to that of graphite [11]. Furthermore, materials with consistent pores, often called as porous materials can also serve as possible active sites for the adsorption of Li atoms to enhance the storage capacity. In addition to this, large size pores or defects may also encourage the fast mobility of Li through the surface of the layered material [1].

Many of the recent computational studies proposed the possible strategy of the synthesis of

different planar 2D materials with polygonal rings (carbon-based and otherwise) as the desired anode in LIBs [12-14]. Dahn *et al.* reported that a negative electrode in LIBs comprising monolayers of graphene can accommodate twice the number of Li atoms compared with conventional graphite [15, 16]. Both side adsorption study of a single lithium in monolayer graphene sheet turns out a specific theoretical capacity of 744 mAh/g, corresponds to a $Li_2C_6$ stoichiometry. This theoretical understanding of the storage mechanism of Li atoms in a single-layer graphene sheet led to research on possible 2D carbon structures and other carbonaceous materials, such as carbon nanotubes and fullerene. Recently, 2D planar graphene-analogous structures with different types of defects have been investigated theoretically for LIB applications [17-23]. All of these studies concluded that various types of defects in graphene (Stone-Wales defect, vacancies, etc) can provide an improved Li adsorption and subsequently a charge transfer from the adsorbed Li and the graphene membrane. Moreover, as compared to the topological defects, the vacancy defects show more improved Li adsorption ability and storage capacity. The successful practical applications of graphene have triggered the theoretical and experimental search for different combinations of carbon, particularly graphene allotropes [24]. Recently, planar carbon structures composed of various rings other than hexagons have been theoretically predicted. Few reports of such structures consist of 4-, 5-, 6-,7-, and 8 membered rings include haeckelites [25], T-graphene [26], OPG [27], pentahexoctite [28], pentagraphene [29], pha-graphene [30], psi-graphene [31],5-8 haeckelite [32], and pop-graphene [6].

Even though the haeckelites [25] have been reported long back, the LIB applications have not been reported. In view of this, the present work focuses on the DFT calculations to understand in detail the geometrical features, Li binding properties, electronic and elastic properties, the storage capacity, open circuit voltage (OCV), and also the state of charge (SOC) dependencies of the Li diffusion kinetics of planar carbon haeckelites. Inspired by the computational studies,

possible experimental investigations are already reported for the synthesis of new materials including planar carbon allotropes [33-38]. In view of this, we believe that the accurate computational predictions and fundamental property analysis of haeckelites would strive the experimental realizations within a short span of time. Recently, the agreeable predictions of new energy storage materials using successful computational approaches has also been firmly established [39].

## 2. Computational details

In this study, all calculations were performed using projector augmented plane-wave (PAW) potentials [40] based on DFT [41, 42] as implemented in VASP code [43-45]. Plane waves with an energy cutoff of 500 eV were used to control the fitness of the mesh. The ion-electron interaction was delineated in the PAW pseudopotential method with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional [46] and generalized gradient approximation. During the calculation, the van der Waals dispersion were taken into account, and a vacuum space of $15$ Å was used in the direction normal to the sheet to keep away the interactions between the two layers. The lattice parameters and atomic positions were completely relaxed until the maximum force on each atom was less than $0.01$ eV/Å. The Brillouin zone was sampled with a $3{\times}3{\times}1$ k-point grid, according to the Monkhorst-Pack scheme [47].

The open-circuit voltage[48, 49] (OCV) during the lithiation and delithiation process of Li is calculated using the formula

$$
V_{OCV}=-\frac{\Delta E}{nzF}=\frac{E_{hae}+nE_{Li}-E_{hae+Li_n}}{nzF}, \tag{1}
$$

where $\Delta E$ is the internal energy of the system, z is the charge of Li ions, F is the Faraday constant, $E_{hae}$ is the total energy of the haeckelites, $E_{Li}$ is the energy of a single Li atom in the body-centered cubic structure, and $E_{hae+Li}$ is the total energy of the lithiated haeckelite structures.

To study the mobility of Li atoms through the haeckelites, we used the climbing image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) method with five images using VASP [50, 51]. Here, we investigated the state-of-charge (SOC) dependence of the Li mobilities using different sizes of simulation cells, unit cells, and 3×3 supercells as high and low SOC, respectively. For each calculation, a single Li atom was placed in the hollow ring site of the haeckelite structures, based on the urging of previous studies that hollow positions in the 5-, 6-, and 7- membered rings are the more favorable Li adsorption sites in graphitic systems [11]. From the nudged elastic band calculations, we then demonstrated the preferential Li intercalation sites and migration pathways through the haeckelites.

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Structure and Stability

The atomic representations of haeckelites are shown in **Fig. 1**, including the special patterns of 5-, and 7-membered carbon rings in addition to the regular 6-membered carbon ring. The haeckelite structures are a family of layered $sp^2$-like carbon crystals and are categorized as follows: (1) a hexagonal (h567) structure formed by the agglomeration of three 7-membered rings surrounded by alternating 5- and 6-membered rings without Stone-Wales defects; (2) a rectangular (r57) structure containing a symmetric arrangement of only Stone-Wales defective 5- and 7-membered rings, wherein the pentalene (5-5 ring) units are stabilized and surrounded by 7-membered rings within a flat structure; (3) an oblique (o567) structure containing a Stone-Wales defect network along the armchair direction, where the pentalene units are surrounded by 6- and 7-membered rings.

However, the structural stability cannot be fully explained by only the geometric contribution of carbon atoms. Although psi-graphene and o567 have the same geometric contributions, psi-graphene and o567 show the lowest and highest relative energy among the studied planar carbon allotropes, respectively, which is attributed to the arrangement of the carbon rings. The 5- and 7-membered rings can be stabilized in a planar structure by a Stone-Wales defect of a 7-7-5 ring because the sum of interior angles of two 7-membered rings and one 5-membered ring around the central carbon atom is close to 360 degrees. Therefore, psi-graphene can be more stable than pha-graphene due to the 7-7-5 ring arrangement of the Stone-Wales defect network, even though psi-graphene has a higher geometric contribution of carbon atoms to the 5- and 7- membered rings. In contrast, o567 has the same geometric contribution and Stone-Wales defect network as psi-graphene, but it has the highest relative energy. The instability is due to the pentalene ring, which is surrounded by 7-membered rings to maintain a stable planar structure as in r57. Therefore, the geometric contribution to a 6-membered ring negatively affects structural stability at the o567-containing pentalene ring. Tight binding calculations also reveal that layered haeckelite structures are more stable than many of the carbon counterparts and which possess relative energy stability of 0.419 eV/atom with respect to graphene. According to our DFT

calculations, h567 is found to be the most stable haeckelite structure, possessing relative energy of 0.262 eV with respect to graphene and this analysis is also comparable with the earlier reports [25].

Additionally, the mechanical features of haeckelites have also been investigated by employing the energy-strain method [29, 31, 52, 53], which has been employed in previous studies for the calculation of elastic constants. VASP uses a finite difference scheme to generate the force constants for matrix and Hessian matrix, which are utilized in the computation of mechanical parameters. ### 3.3. Lithium diffusion and storage properties of haeckelite structures as anode material in LIBs

Although the role of various 2D materials has been demonstrated as a negative electrode in energy storage devices, the prospective applications and commercialization of LIBs are mainly controlled by the use of carbon-based materials-particularly graphite as an anode. Graphene

possesses excellent electronic conductivity and mechanical stabilities [56], as well as a superior surface-to-mass ratio [57]. Due to its outstanding properties, intensive studies on graphene has been performed both experimentally [58-60] and theoretically [18, 61, 62] as an alternative anode material for LIBs. Both the theoretical and experimental studies reported that the presence of extrinsic or intrinsic defects in graphene can enhance the storage capacity and Li adsorption [18, 63-65]. Moreover, the formation of intrinsic pentagons, heptagons, and octagons in graphene itself is considered to be a topological defect; in turn, this alters the mechanical and electronic properties, as well as creates active sites for Li-ion adsorption and diffusion.

The fundamental operation mechanism in LIBs is considered as the adsorption, intercalation, and diffusion of the Li atom in carbon haeckelites. The operating voltage and maximum capacity of the anode material are extracted from the binding energy of the Li atom in the anode material as well as the structural stability. The Li binding to the haeckelite structures is strongly dependent on the local environment and degree of charge transfer between the Li atom and the material [66]. It is expected that the materials used for the storage of Li in the negative electrode should have a binding strength within a certain range and should be weaker than the cathode side, which ensures the proper Li diffusion from the anode to cathode during the discharge process [48, 67].

To examine the possibility and application of haeckelite structures as anode materials, we first computed the binding energy of an Li atom by keeping it in the hollow positions of the 5-, 6-, and 7-membered rings. We noted a great dependence on binding energy with the atomic environment of carbon atoms in the haeckelite structures. The binding energy of a single Li atom is defined as $E_b = E_{(Li+hae)} - E_{hae} - E_{Li}$, where $E_{(Li+hae)}$, $E_{hae}$, and $E_{Li}$ represent the total energy of the Li-adsorbed haeckelite structures, the energy of the Li free haeckelite, and the total energy of the isolated Li atom. Our calculations show that Li can be adsorbed on the pristine graphene

plane with a binding energy of 0.81 eV, which restricts its use for LIB anode applications [68].

Even though the haeckelite structures are comprised of 5-, 6-, and 7-membered rings, they have very different electronic structures and energetic stability. The high energy density due to the 5-, 6-, and 7-membered rings of haeckelites is also responsible for the preferential binding of Li in the respective hollow sites. The positions of Li on the hollow sites of pentagon and heptagon rings showed stronger Li-binding in all h567 and r57, except o567. In o567, the obtained positive Li binding energy in all of the ring positions limits its use as an anode material in LIBs. The obtained binding energy values in h567 and r57 indicate that Li can be adsorbed stably in these haeckelites instead of clustering. Furthermore, the analysis of Bader charge population shows that Li atoms donate almost all their ‘s’ electrons and become Li ions with a charge transfer rate of 0.85 |e| from Li to the haeckelite structures. This eventually leads to a strong repulsive type Coulomb interaction between the intercalated Li atoms, which prevents them from clustering. The calculated binding energy of haeckelite structures are shown in Table 3.

From the standpoint of haeckelite performance as an anode material, thermomechanical stability, negative Li binding energy for Li hosting and low Li diffusion energy barrier are key issues considered in this work. Among these points, Li binding energy is closely related to the local arrangement of 5-, 6- and 7-membered rings as well as the Li diffusion energy barrier. It has been already well known that the topological defects and the vacancy defects show more improved Li adsorption ability and storage capacity. Therefore, 5-, 7 and 8-membered rings can improve Li adsorption of the graphene composed of the only 6-membered ring. Comparing the local arrangement of 5-, 6- and 7-membered rings in haeckelite, r57 of only 5- and 7-membered rings has the largest Li binding, otherwise, o567 of hexagonal dimers has positive Li binding energy. So, it is seen that the contribution of the 6-membered ring shows a tendency to weaken the Li binding energy. Especially, clustered 6-membered ring as like graphene reduces the Li binding energy.

Here, we can propose a simple insight into the performance of the haeckelite through the local arrangement of 5-, 6- and 7-membered rings; the lower the contribution of the 6-membered ring, the greater Li binding energy. This is well agreement with the geometric contribution of carbon in Table 1.

The mobility of Li through the haeckelite structures is considered as one of the key features which greatly influence the making and working of LIBs. We studied the diffusion of Li atoms on the surface of the haeckelite structures by employing the CI-NEB method. We considered the diffusion pathways of the Li atom from the position of one hollow site to the nearest neighbor site along the $x$-$y$ plane of the crystal axes. We calculated all possible Li-migration paths in all haeckelites with low and high SOC configurations. A schematic representation of the Li diffusion path with the lowest energy barrier and the corresponding energy profiles of haeckelites at high and low SOC states are shown in **Fig. 5a-5c**. At high SOC, there was a characteristic Li diffusion path with especially low energy barriers, as shown in **Fig 5d-5f**. Here, we observed that the migration barrier decreased to a rather small value at high SOC, which is consistent with earlier studies [11]. At the high SOC stage, the lowest diffusion barriers of o567 is 0.13 eV. The lowest energy barrier of h567 is 0.21 eV, whereas r57 showed a barrier of 0.22 eV. The calculated energy barriers of all haeckelites were comparable to that of graphene (< 0.40 eV) [11, 69]. Furthermore, the lowest energy barrier obtained implies that the Li was located in the most stable site on the haeckelites. This low energy barrier ensures high Li mobility and indicates robust charge/discharge rates when haeckelites are used as an anode for LIBs. The calculated Li diffusion paths with the lowest energy barriers of haeckelite structures are shown in **Table 3**.

Our results indicate that the haeckelite structures have low Li diffusion energy barriers ($E_\text{a}$ < 0.40 eV) and also shows characteristic Li migration pathways at high SOC state. The detailed analysis for the high SOC state (**Fig. 5a-5c**) shows that the Li diffusion of h567 occurs through the 5- and 6-membered rings by forming a *circular* path around three adjacent 7-membered rings with the lowest energy barriers of 0.21 eV and 0.27 eV, respectively, from a 6-membered ring to a 5-membered ring and vice versa. The r57 shows the lowest energy barriers of 0.22 eV and 0.30 eV through the 5- and 7-membered rings, respectively, using all possible movements by forming a *network-type* diffusion path. Even though the o567 shows a high positive binding energy, it possesses the lowest Li-ion diffusion energy profile (0.13 eV) among the haeckelite structures. Here, Li diffusion is possible by a *linear* diffusion path through the 5- and 7-membered rings.

We also investigated all possible Li-migration paths at a low SOC configuration by inserting a single Li atom corresponding to a $3 \times 3$ supercell. At a low SOC state, the observed Li

energy barriers are similar and show no characteristic diffusion pathways, as shown in **Fig. 5d-5f**.

At a low SOC configuration also, the migration barriers of all haeckelites are comparable to that of graphene (~ 0.25-0.40 eV). The diffusion barriers for Li at high and low SOC along the different migration paths were calculated and are summarized in **Table S1 and S2 of the Supporting Information**. At low SOC state, the obtained diffusion barriers through the hollow ring sites of the haeckelites are comparable to each other, which limits the ability to define a proper path for Li movement based on the energy barrier analysis. At low SOC state, the Li diffusion is possible by utilizing all available two-dimensional movements through the haeckelites. The energy barrier of Li diffusion is related to the relative stability between the stable Li binding state and the transition state. In the carbon allotropes, the Li diffusion always passes through the C-C bridge site, which is the transition state. Because the C-C bridge site has the almost same environment, the stability on the C-C bridge is nearly the same regardless of the polygon type generating C-C bridge. This implies that the relative Li stability on the stable binding site on the polygonal hollow site mainly determines the Li diffusion energy barrier. Therefore, the highly positive binding energy of the o567 can have lower Li diffusion energy barrier. Poor binding may lead to a fast diffusion with lower Li diffusion energy barrier on the o567 haeckelite. However, even if o567 has the lowest Li diffusion energy barrier, fast Li diffusion cannot be guaranteed unless the path is connected through the entire structure. In this regard, h567 has the lowest average Li diffusion energy barrier through the entire structure, so h567 can be expected to have a fast Li diffusion.

Based on the analysis of computed Li binding energies of the haeckelites, we further investigated the storage capacity. The obtained negative Li adsorption energies of h567 and r57 are desirable for the LIB applications since it cannot lead to the clustering of Li. The observed positive adsorption energy of o567 is similar to pristine graphene, which limits its use for LIB applications. We added Li atoms one by one in the hollow sites of the h567 and r57 structures containing sixteen

atom unit cell to estimate the maximum capacity. We noticed that r57 can only accommodate three Li atoms and which in turn, provides a maximum capacity comparable to graphite and psi-graphene. Our DFT results including vdW corrections show that up to five Li atoms were preferentially located on the single side and both side of the hollow ring positions of h567, with a binding energy of -0.05 eV. Then the corresponding theoretical storage capacity of h567 is $Li_{1.875}C_6$ (697.32 $mAhg^{-1}$) compared to the $LiC_6$ of graphite. The top and side view of the fully lithiated h567 with five Li atoms adsorbed on both sides is shown in Fig. 6a. The top and side view of the fully lithiated h567 configuration with one side Li adsorbed structure is shown in Fig. S1 in the supplementary information. The computed maximum storage capacity of h567 is higher than that of most of the carbon-based anode materials such as graphite [70, 71] (372 $mAhg^{-1}$), psi-graphene[31] (372 $mAhg^{-1}$), and phagraphene [1] (558 $mAhg^{-1}$). But the obtained maximum capacity of h567 is lower than that of popgraphene [6] (1115 $mAhg^{-1}$) and also graphene with maximum Stone-Wales and divacancy defects [20] (1675 $mAhg^{-1}$). Our ab-initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulation results also show that the structure of h567 with Lithium is thermally stable, as shown in Fig. S2 in the supplementary information.

In addition to the storage capacity, the average open circuit voltage of the h567 during the Li intercalation process is also studied. The OCV describes the energetic effort required to extract the Li ions from the electrodes with the optimized range of electrode materials within the operating voltages. Since the OCV is calculated from the chemical potential change of Li atom at a given intermediate state according to the state of charge, it is necessary to define the intermediate structures during the charging/discharging process and followed by the OCV calculation. The OCV was computed based on the equation (1). We have used a Cluster Approach to Statistical Mechanics (CASM) code to evaluate the intermediate structures ($Li_xC_6$) by comparing the relative formation energy ($E_{rel}$) of all possible configurations of the intermediate structures of the Li

concentration in the range of $0 \leq x \leq 1$. Here 0 stands for the delithiated state and 1 stands for the fully lithiated state.

Further, we investigated the influence of Li intercalation on the electronic structure and mechanical properties on the lithiated h567. In order to explain the lithiation process and also the interaction of the Li with the h567, we have analyzed the partial density of states (PDOS) of the fully lithiated system. During lithiation, the Li-Li interaction gradually increases and eventually the charge transfers from Li to the h567 decreases. We also noticed that the lithiated system retains the metallic characteristics. The PDOS for the s orbital of Li and the p orbital of C are shown in Fig. 7. The calculated elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{22}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{66}$ of the single side Li adsorbed h567 are

298.89, 292.84, 47.82 and 121.78 N/m, respectively. For the case of both side Li adsorbed configuration, the $C_{11}$, $C_{22}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{66}$ elastic constants are 309.73, 301.00, 49.79, and 127.14 N/m respectively. The computed elastic constants with Li intercalated h567 satisfy the criteria for mechanical stability. Although Young's modulus of lithiated h567 is smaller than those of pristine h567 (~ 308 N/m), they are larger than those of many pristine planar carbon structures such as phagraphene (~ 280 N/m) and pentagraphene (~ 265 N/m) suggesting that fully lithiated h567 has good mechanical properties which ensure its use for LIB anode applications. Furthermore, the experimental realization of haeckelite structures could be possible by depositing hot polyynes on cold metal surfaces. Wang *et al* theoretically predicted that the haeckelite can be synthesized at a high carbon density on the Ni (111) surface [72]. In addition to this, the production of atomically precise graphene nanoribbons of different topologies and widths can be possible by the surface-assisted coupling of molecular precursors into linear polyphenylenes and their subsequent cyclodehydrogenation [73].

## 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, first-principles DFT calculations were systematically carried out to understand the possibility of considering 2D planar carbon haeckelite structures as anode materials for LIBs. Based on the calculated electronic structure, it has been noticed that the metallicity in haeckelites arises due to the partially occupied bands which also enhance the conductivity for LIB applications. Furthermore, the ultrahigh stiffness of haeckelites also helps to prevent the acute volume expansion issue upon Li intercalation, as is the case with anodes consists of silicon-based materials. Even though the haeckelites possess excellent structural, electronic, and mechanical properties, we noticed that the h567 is the most appropriate haeckelite for LIB anode applications due to its preferential binding of Li in the respective hollow sites combined with low diffusion

energy barriers (< 0.35 eV). The low Li diffusion energy barrier in h567 ensures robust charge/discharge processes and high Li mobility when it used as an anode for LIBs. In addition to this, the h567 shows a larger specific capacity $Li_{1.875}C_6$ (~ 697 mAhg-1) for Li ions compared to the $LiC_6$ of graphite. The obtained low-average open-circuit voltage is also ensuring the overall performance of the LIBs composed of the h567 structure. In addition to this, the influence of Li intercalation on the electronic structure and mechanical properties on the lithiated h567 are also investigated. Besides the potential application of 2D carbon haeckelite as anode materials for LIBs, we believe that the superior properties of haeckelites may also have applications in optoelectronic devices and catalysis.
