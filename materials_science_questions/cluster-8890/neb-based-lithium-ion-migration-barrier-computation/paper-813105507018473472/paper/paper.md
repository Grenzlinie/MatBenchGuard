# Nonaqueous LiNafion-Based Polymeric Electrolyte: Quantum-Chemical Modeling

T. S. Zyubina, A. S. Zyubin*, Yu. A. Dobrovol’skii, and V. M. Volokhov

Institute of Problems of Chemical Physics, Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow oblast, Chernogolovka, 142432 Russia

*e-mail: zyubin@icp.ac.ru
Received September 22, 2016

**Abstract**—In the framework of the search for promising electrolytes for lithium-ion batteries, quantum-chemical modeling of the structure, stability, and electronic properties of membranes based on LiNafion · $n$DMSO, $n = 0–16$) has been performed by the density functional theory method with inclusion of gradient correction and periodic conditions (PBE/PAW). Inasmuch as the key factor that determines the ionic conductivity is the degree of swelling of LiNafion in DMSO (according to calculations, for $n = 8$, the degree of swelling is close to 500%, and for $n = 16$, it is close to 700% by volume), similar membranes can be good candidates from the viewpoint of lithium ion transport (with barriers of 0.2–0.3 eV, which depend on $n$).

DOI: 10.1134/S0036023617080198

Electrolytes based on lithiated Nafion (LiNafion) have been tested in lithium and lithium-ion batteries [1–4]. The promise of these polymeric electrolytes is determined by their enhanced stability in long-term tests as compared with the commercial liquid electrolyte based on $\text{LiPF}_6$.

The use of a LiNafion · dimethyl sulfoxide (LiNafion · $n$DMSO) membrane has enabled to achieve high ion conductivity, which is higher than 1 mS/cm at room temperature. This considerably exceeds the conductivity in organic carbonates used in commercial liquid electrolytes ($10^{-5}–10^{-4}$ S/cm). According to [5], increasing the DMSO solvent content from 40 wt % ($n = 8, 9$) to 131 wt % ($n = 18.4$) leads to a decrease in activation energy from 0.3 to 0.2 eV and, correspondingly, to an increase in lithium-ion conductivity. The aim of the present work is to study the structure of LiNafion · $n$DMSO.

## COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS

To solve this problem, modeling of the structure of infinite LiNafion · $n$DMSO nanothreads ($n = 0, 1, 8$, and 16; structures 1–7 in Fig. 1) was performed (with full geometry optimization). $\text{Li(C}_{15}\text{O}_5\text{F}_{29}\text{S}) \cdot n(\text{H}_6\text{C}_2\text{OS})$ and $[\text{Li(C}_{15}\text{O}_5\text{F}_{29}\text{S}) \cdot n(\text{H}_6\text{C}_2\text{OS})]_2$ species containing from 51 to 262 atoms were used as fragments multiplied in space. Calculations were performed by the density functional theory (DFT) method with inclusion of DFT/PBE gradient correction and with a projector-augmented wave (PAW) basis set [6] with the corresponding pseudopotential “hard” [7] by means of the VASP program package [8–11] (PBE/PAW). The energy limit ($E_c$), determining the completeness of the basis set, was taken to be 400 eV. For the optimization of the electronic state, the Methfessel–Paxton method [12] with a smearing width ($\sigma$) of 0.2 and extrapolation of energies to $\sigma = 0$ was used. This approach automatically determined the multiplicity of the structure. Based on analysis of the total energy ($E$) and unit cell parameters ($a, b, c, \alpha, \beta, \gamma$) for LiNAVION, LiNAVION · DMSO, and LiNAVION · 8DMSO at different numbers of points of the Monkhorst–Pack $k$-point mesh in the Brillouin zone ($k = 4 \times 2 \times 1, 4 \times 2 \times 2, 4 \times 4 \times 2$, and $6 \times 4 \times 2$), the set $k = 4 \times 2 \times 2$ was selected for systematic calculations.

Hereinafter, we use the average bond energy of a DMSO molecule with LiNafion ($dE$, in eV/mol), which is defined as the difference between the total energy of the cluster $E(\text{LiNafion} \cdot n\text{DMSO})$ and the overall energy of LiNafion and $n$DMSO molecules divided by the number of DMSO molecules: $dE = [E(\text{LiNafion} \cdot n\text{DMSO}) - (E(\text{LiNafion}) + nE(\text{DMSO}))]/n$. A negative energy value indicates that the interaction of the components is exothermic, i.e., energetically favorable.

A similar procedure was tested and successfully used in our previous studies [13–17].

Our calculation demonstrate that the threads do not form closely packed structures, and there are a large amount of voids promising form the point of view of migration of $Li^{+}$ ions and $(Li(DMSO)_{4})^{+}$ complexes without noticeable changes in unit cell parameters (Fig. 1).

In LiNafion (structure 1, Fig. 1), there is a positive charge on the three-coordinate Li atom (two oxygen atoms and one fluorine atom) Li atom ($Q$(Li) = 0.42e) and a negative charge on the $SO_{3}$ group ($Q$(SO₃) = –0.356e) [18].

The formation of the LiNafion complex with DMSO leads to structure 2 (LiNafion · DMSO), in which the three-coordinate Li atom is bonded to two O atoms of the $SO_{3}$ group and the oxygen atom of the DMSO molecule. The methyl group of DMSO is coordinated to an oxygen atom of the $SO_{3}$ group. As compared with structure 1, the charge on the $SO_{3}$ group changes insignificantly ($Q$(SO₃) = –0.363e). The positive charge on the Li atom decreases by 0.1e ($Q$(Li) = 0.33e), passing to DMSO, which has a positive charge of 0.1e. This means that the electron density is transferred from DMSO and lithium to Nafion. The overall positive charge on the Li · DMSO group (0.43e) is close to the positive charge on the lithium atom in structure 1.

As the number of DMSO molecules increases from one (structure 2) to eight (structure 3), the lithium atom is separated from the $SO_{3}$ group and becomes solvated by four DMSO molecules. The overall charge on the lithium atom together with its surrounding four DMSO molecules (Li · 4DMSO) is close to unity (0.9e); i.e., tetrasolvate cation $Li(DMSO)_{4}^{+0.9}$ is formed. The charge on each of the four surrounding DMSO molecules is 0.156e, while DMSO molecules that are not in contact with these molecules have only insignificant charges. As compared with structure 1, the positive charge on the lithium atom in structure 3 decreases by 0.1e ($Q$(Li) = 0.25e), while the negative charge of the $SO_{3}$ group and on each fluorine atom increases by 0.2e and 0.03e, respectively ($Q$(SO₃) = –0.563e). In total, nearly 0.45e is transferred from the nearest lithium sphere (comprising four DMSO molecules) to Nafion, which is close to the electron density transferred from the lithium atom to Nafion in LiNafion (structure 1). Hence, the lithium migration into the solvent caused by complexation is accompanied by transfer of the same amount of electron density from the solvent molecules to the remaining Nafion moiety, so that the lithium atom with its surrounding DMSO molecules is positively charged (0.9e), while the remaining Nafion is negatively charged. Thus, dissociation leads to charge separation. The charge separation in structure 4, where the lithium atom is bonded to three DMSO molecules and one of the three $SO_{3}$ oxygen atoms, is significantly less pronounced.

The trends in energies found for complex clusters [18] persist in going to analogous nanothreads. In particular, the LiNafion · 8DMSO cluster isomer analogous to structure 3 is only 0.01 eV lower on the energy scale than isomer 4. For nanothreads, the difference between these two isomers increases to 0.6 eV, isomer 3 becoming more favorable. The average DMSO–LiNafion bond energy ($dE$) decreases for the complex cluster in the series 1.02 ($n = 1$), 0.44 ($n = 8$), 0.32 eV ($n = 16$) [18]. For nanothreads, this energy in an analogous series changes as 2.95 ($n = 1$), 0.87 ($n = 8$), 0.43 eV ($n = 16$).

Figure 1 shows that in going from LiNafion (structure 1) to LiNafion · 8DMSO (structures 3 and 4), the volume increases by a factor of 4.5–5 (from 713 to 3153 and $3551~\mathring{A}^{3}$, respectively). Since the key factor determining the ion conductivity is the degree of swelling of LiNafion in DMSO (for example, at $n = 8$, the degree of swelling is close to 500% by volume), similar membranes are promising from the viewpoint of lithium ion transport (with barrier of 0.2–0.3 eV depending on $n$ [18]). Migration of a lithium cation along the nanothreads is similar to migration along the complexes and can occur in two ways: (1) as a transition of the four-coordinate lithium through the three-coordinate state to another surrounding of four DMSO molecules and (2) as migration of the $Li(DMSO)_{4}^{+}$ cation solvate. In both cases, the barriers for $n = 8–18$ are close to 0.2–0.3 eV; however, the second type of mobility is expected to dominate at low DMSO contents, while the first type of mobility should be dominating high DMSO contents.

The mutual arrangement of the threads can also be essential for conductivity. Since the real threads can be arbitrarily oriented, we considered two opposite variants of LiNafion · 8DMSO tread orientation. In structure 3, the $SO_{3}$ moieties are oriented in one direction, and in the isomer corresponding to structure 5 (which is close to but slightly higher on the energy scale than structure 3), the $SO_{3}$ groups of neighboring threads are directed toward each other. This makes it possible to collectively use the spatial arrangement of DMSO from different threads; therefore, structure 5 is more suitable for lithium conductivity than structure 3.

## CONCLUSIONS

Although the energy of DMSO–LiNafion interaction changes in going from isolated complex clusters to nanothreads, their trend in LiNafion · $n$DMSO is retained with increasing $n$. For example, the average bond energy of a DMSO molecule with LiNafion ($dE$) decreases in the clusters from 1.02 to 0.44 eV with increasing $n$ from 1 to 8 [18]. For the nanothreads, the same characteristic changes from 2.95 to 0.87 eV in an analogous series.

Since the key factor determining the ion conductivity is the degree of swelling of LiNafion in DMSO

(for the crystal packing, the degree of swelling is close to 500% for $n=8$ and to 700% for $n=16$ by volume), the calculations show that similar membranes are promising from the viewpoint of lithium ion transport. In reality, the threads are not infinite and are chaotically interwoven, which can have an effect on the volume. The lithium cation can migrate in two ways: (1) as the transition from one four-coordinate state to another four-coordinate state through a three-coordinate state and (2) as migration of the Li · 4DMSO tetrasolvate complex. In both cases, the barriers for $n=8$–18 are close to 0.2–0.3 eV; however, at a small number of DMSO molecules, the mobility of the second type is expected, while the first type of mobility will be dominating at high DMSO contents.
