# Evaluating bulk $Nb_{2}O_{2}F_{3}$ for Li-battery electrode applications†

Rafael B. Araujo*a and Rajeev Ahuja*b

Received 8th November 2015,
Accepted 21st December 2015

DOI: 10.1039/c5cp06829k

www.rsc.org/pccp

This investigation has the primary objective of elucidating the lithium intercalation process in the crystal structure of a new niobium oxyfluoride compound $Nb_{2}O_{2}F_{3}$. The framework of the density functional theory was applied in a generalized gradient approximation together with the hybrid functional method. It is revealed that lithium atoms intercalate in this material in a maximum concentration of one Li atom per formula unit forming $LiNb_{2}O_{2}F_{3}$. Moreover, octahedral positions in between the layers of Nb-O-F appear as the Li preferred occupancy resulting in a structural volume expansion of only 5%. Electronic structure evolution with the insertion of lithium displays a transformation from semi-conductor to metal when half of the lithium atoms are added. This transformation occurs due to a symmetry break induced by the transition from the +8 to +7 oxidation state of half of the $Nb_{2}$ dimers. Then, after full lithiation the symmetry is recovered and the material becomes a semiconductor again with a band gap amounting to 1 eV. The evaluated average deintercalation potential reaches 1.29 V vs. $Li/Li^{+}$ with activation energy for lithium ion migration of 0.79 eV. The computed low potential of the redox reaction $Nb_{2}^{8+}$ to $Nb_{2}^{7+}$ includes niobium oxyfluoride in the map of possible materials for the anode application of Li-ion batteries.

## I Introduction

The research field of rechargeable lithium batteries has presented a systematic growth of interest by the scientific community in recent years. This is a result of the heavy application of these devices in our daily life. One important topic in this field emerges from the search for materials with low discharge voltage vs. $Li/Li^{+}$. In fact, there exist two main possible applications where these materials with lower potential suit perfectly. The first case is to substitute the currently used $LiC_{6}$ anode material by one with slightly greater potential (1-1.5 V vs. $Li/Li^{+}$). This substitution allows the applica- tion of cathode materials with higher voltage vs. lithium but still in the range of potential where the electrolyte remains stable. $^{1-7}$ The other possibility emerges as cathode application in powering the back up of memory devices. $^{3,8-12}$

On the basis of the low redox couple, low cost and non-toxicity, titanium and niobium based oxides have continuously been considered as interesting candidates for lower potential electrode materials. Many investigations have explored Ti based systems such as $TiO_{2}^{13}$ and $Li_{4}Ti_{5}O_{12}^{14,15}$ or Nb based materials such as $Li_{1.714}Nb_{2}O_{10}^{3}$ and $LiNbO_{3}^{16}$ These niobium based materials, for instance, deliver a potential of 1.65 V and 1.67 V vs. $Li/Li^{+}$, respectively. Also the other possibility is the mixture of both transition metals like $Ti_{2}Nb_{10}O_{29}^{17}$ and $LiTiNbO_{5}^{18}$ as a stable anode for rechargable lithium batteries.

This work comes with the objective of investigating the lithium intercalation process in niobium oxyfluoride, $Nb_{2}O_{2}F_{3}$, as a possible candidate for the next generation of anodes for Li-ion batteries. This compound was recently synthesized by Tran et al. $^{19}$ presenting a low Nb oxidation state and an opened crystal framework susceptible to intercalating lithium ions reversibly. Moreover, the addition of Li atoms in the crystal structure of $Nb_{2}O_{2}F_{3}$ is followed by a reduction process of Nb ions to even lower oxidation states which is expected to deliver a quite small voltage vs. $Li/Li^{+}$. This small delivered potential should, then, indicate this material as a promising candidate as an anode in Li-ion batteries.

## II Computational details

In order to derive the electronic and structural properties of the lithiated and delithiated niobium oxyfluoride phases the framework of the density functional theory in the projector augmented wave (PAW) method was applied as implemented in the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP). $^{20,21}$ All calculations were undertaken using spin polarized formalism and for the exchange correlation functional the Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (PBE) $^{22}$ generalized gradient approximation was used. Heyd-Scuseria-Ernzerhof (HSE06)²³,²⁴ was also employed with the main objective of partially overcoming the self-interaction error coming from PBE. The plane wave cutoff was shown to be converged at 600 eV. Sampling of the Brillouin zone was considered in different *k*-meshes depending on the size of the supercell and the required properties. In all cases, fractional coordinates as well as the cell shape were relaxed until forces in each atom reach 0.01 Å⁻¹.

For the intercalation process of Li atoms in the Nb₂O₂F₃ crystal structure two schemes were followed. First a supercell of 2 × 2 × 1 was built up containing 56 atoms. Then, Li atoms were placed in non equivalent positions for each concentration *x* (*x* = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2) resulting in LiₓNb₂O₂F₃ (each structure containing 56, 58, 60, 64, 66, 70 and 74 atoms). In sequence, the energy minimization process was carried out to ensure that these configurations have reached the ground state. Five distinct Li configurations were investigated in each concentration to ensure that a representative model was found. At this point of the investigation the generalized gradient approach was employed to treat the exchange and correlation term in a *k*-point *Γ* centered grid of 1 × 1 × 2.

In sequence, the structures presenting lowest total energy for each concentration were submitted to an *ab initio* molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulation in the canonical ensemble (fixed particle number, volume and temperature, *NVT*) at 600 K. The host atoms were kept fixed with only Li atoms migrating. 600 K temperature helps to accelerate the dynamics and, as a whole, a 4 ps simulation was carried out to obtain the most probable adsorption sites for lithium ions in each concentration. The MD simulations were performed in a *Γ* only point grid with an energy cutoff of 400 eV.

After the simulation, eight new optimization processes (for each concentration) were performed with fractional coordinates coming from each 5000 steps of the MD. The configurations with smaller energy for each concentration were captured as the ground state. Due to the great amount of calculations, in this part of the investigation, a cutoff of 400 eV and a *Γ* point mesh were set.

Once the ground state structure of each lithium concentration was found, then, new optimization was performed employing the hybrid functional method with a single *Γ* *k*-point mesh and a cutoff of 600 eV. Once the system has reached the required accuracy a new single point calculation was carried out with a *k*-point grid of 1 × 2 × 2 to derive the electronic and electrochemical properties. The derived structures for the concentrations where Nb₂O₂F₃ would accept the insertion of Li atoms (0.25, 0.25, 0.75 and 1) are depicted in Fig. S1 of the ESI.†

To finally ensure that niobium oxyfluoride can be applied as an anode material it is of utmost importance to have some insights of the lithium migration mechanism in its crystal structure. The climbing nudged elastic band method (cNEB)²⁵,²⁶ was employed to evaluate the activation energy of lithium ions hopping from one equilibrium position to the other. At this stage of the investigation a supercell of 2 × 2 × 2 containing 128 atoms was build up ensuring a small interaction between periodic images. For cNEB six replicas of the system were linearly interpolated between the two lithium equilibrium positions. GGA approximation was applied with a cut off of 600 eV. The great dimension of the supercell allows a Brillouin zone sampling with a *Γ* only point centered *k*-mesh.

## III Results
Nb₂O₂F₃ was recently synthesized by Tran *et al.*¹⁹ in a monoclinic *I2/a* space group with a phase transition to a triclinic lattice, *P*1, at 90 K. The Nb₂O₂F₃ crystal structure consists of NbX₆ (X = O or F) octahedra sharing edges of X and then forming Nb₂X₁₀ dimers. The dimers are linked by sharing X atoms along the *c* direction forming a kind of layered structure that allows the insertion of Li atoms. In fact, above 90 K, as reported by Tran *et al.*, Nb₂ dimers undergo a charge reordering from a +7 oxidation state to a system where half of the Nb₂ present the +6 oxidation state and the other half reveal the oxidation state of +8. These distinct charge orderings at 90 K and above 90 K are also responsible for the symmetry break resulting in a phase transition from a monoclinic to a triclinic crystal structure.

Once a representative model for this material was derived at all Li ion concentrations, then the next step is to investigate the maximum capacity of lithium insertion under the battery reaction. In this scenario, the free energy of the transformation Liₓ₀Nb₂O₂F₃ + (*x* − *x*₀)Li⁺ + (*x* − *x*₀)e⁻ → LiₓNb₂O₂F₃ is computed by using eqn (1):²⁸,²⁹

$$
V = -\frac{E(\text{Li}_x\text{Nb}_2\text{O}_2\text{F}_3) - (x - x_0)E_{\text{Li}} - E(\text{Li}_{x_0}\text{Nb}_2\text{O}_2\text{F}_3)}{(x - x_0)} \tag{1}
$$

where *E* means the total energy of each related system and *x* − *x*₀ is the number of lithium ions participating in the reaction. For instance, in the transformation Li₀.₂₅Nb₂O₂F₃ → Li₀.₅Nb₂O₂F₃ *x* assumes the value of 0.5 and *x*₀ 0.25 with *x* − *x*₀ reaching 0.25. The total energy of the metallic Li, *E*_Li, was calculated in the bcc structure, which is the structural phase of the Li anode. According to the definition of this equation the reaction will be most likely to occur until the potential is a positive value. After that, it is expected in experiments that Li precipitates forming dendrites and disturbing the performance of the device.

Fig. 1 represents the voltage profile diagram used to evaluate the maximum lithium intercalation capacity. The transformation LiNb₂O₂F₃ → Li₁.₂₅Nb₂O₂F₃ reveals a negative redox potential. Therefore, only one lithium per formula unit can be inserted in the niobium oxyfluoride crystal structure. Thus, considering LiNb₂O₂F₃ as the maximum stable capacity an average deintercalation potential of 1.29 V is evaluated. Of course, the revealed high average potential comes as a disadvantage for anode application since it diminishes the overall potential of the cell. However, if the used cathode material presents a high voltage *vs.* Li/Li⁺, then it is still worth using Nb₂O₂F₃ as the anode with an average reduction of the potential of only 1.29 V.

In this material lithium ions would likely to assume positions in between the $Nb_2X_{10}$ layers as shown in Fig. 2. It is observed that the layer distance formed by $Nb_2X_{10}$ units undergoes a change from 3.97 Å to 4.97 Å with lithium insertion. This change results in a volume expansion of 5% in the framework of the $Nb_2O_2F_3$ crystal structure. Moreover, it is inferred that the local environment of the inserted Li ions shows X atoms forming octahedral units around Li positions as depicted by the blue octahedra in Fig. 2.

In order to gain insights into the local structural and electronic changes under the intercalation of Li ions the bond length evolution of the pairs Nb-Nb and Nb-X is investigated. A shrinkage of the Nb-Nb bond length going from 2.54 Å to 2.39 Å is revealed over lithium insertion where the first and second values are referent to the delithiated and fulllithiated compound ($LiNb_2O_2F_3$). This smaller value of the Nb-Nb bond length comes due to the reduction process experimented by the $Nb_2$ dimers going from the +7 valence state to the +6 valence state. This result is in agreement with the findings reported by Tran et al. $^{19}$ where dimers with the +6 state present a smaller Nb-Nb bond length than dimers with the +7 valence state. The average bond length of octahedra Nb-X in the lithiated materials presents values around 2.12 Å. The difference between the Nb-X bond length of the host material, 2.05 Å, and the lithiated material is of the order of 0.06 Å. This small difference in the bond length indicates that extra electrons coming from Li atoms are not populating molecular orbitals formed by Nb-X states as it is usual for cathode materials. $^{30}$ From these results, it is possible to speculate that the reduction reaction under Li insertion occurs mainly in the Nb-Nb centers.

To further understand charge rearrangement over Li insertion the Bader analysis method is employed. $^{31,32}$ The average net charge computed here is defined as the charge computed with the Bader analysis minus the total charge of the reference species. Nb atoms in the delithiated state present an average net charge of +2.34. With the addition of Li atoms in the crystal structure of $Nb_2O_2F_3$ forming $LiNb_2O_2F_3$ this value goes to +2.02. It means that each pair Nb-Nb undergoes a reduction process receiving approximately one electron. The average net charge change of O and F atoms displays very small values amounting to +0.04 and +0.06 under lithiation. This result is in accordance with the structural analysis where Nb-Nb bond distances decrease with Li ion insertion whereas the Nb-X bond length remains almost the same value.

The presented results are in complete accordance with the current trend of battery materials $^{33-35}$ where d states of transition metals dominate around the valence band maximum (VBM) and the conduction band minimum (CBM). The revealed band gap of the host material $Nb_2O_2F_3$ is also in agreement with that reported by Tran et al. $^{19}$ reaching about 1 eV for the system where $Nb_2$ dimers have oxidation state +7. It is also worth emphasizing that the lithiation process in this system comes with a semiconductor to metal transition when half of the Li atoms are inserted and, then, the semiconductor behavior is recovered when the other half lithium atoms are included as shown in Fig. 3.

The filling of the $\pi^2$ states is accompanied by a symmetry break of the system and then a transition from semiconductor to metal is observed as shown by the pDOS depicted in Fig. 3. In fact, the insertion of half Li atoms induce the semiconductor-metal transition and, then, the insertion of the other half lithium atoms emerges with an opened band gap again. In this scenario, by forming new $LiNb_2O_2F_3$ the symmetry of the crystal structure is recovered with all $\pi$ molecular orbitals filled with two electrons and a metal-semiconductor transition is observed. A very similar effect is reported by ref. 19, where a phase transition is observed from the monoclinic to the triclinic crystal structure.

To have a better understanding of the Li migration mechanisms in the crystal structure of $LiNb_2O_2F_3$ (full lithiated system) the cNEB method is employed to evaluate the activation energy of the hopping of ions. Two main pathways were considered as the most likely and depicted in Fig. 4. Firstly, Li ions are allowed to hop through the layers of the structure (Pathway 01). Secondly, the possibility of Li ions crossing the Nb-X layers migrating through the [0 0 1] direction (Pathway 02) is considered. The computed activation energies for $Li^+$ hopping in these pathways amount to 0.79 eV and 0.99 eV for Pathway 01 and 02, respectively. The higher value of the hop barrier revealed by Pathway 02 leads to a lower probability of Li ion diffusion occurring in this direction. This is a result of the higher Coulombic repulsion felt by the $Li^+$ ion when crossing the Nb-X layers. Pathway 01 has presented an activation energy of 0.79 eV being the most likely direction. This activation energy value induces Li ions to mostly migrate between the formed layers resulting in a 2D like hopping mechanism.

## IV Conclusions
In summary, the framework of the density functional theory was employed in order to investigate the electronic and structural evolution under lithium intercalation in $Nb_2O_2F_3$ for the first time. Lithium atoms locate in between the layers formed by Nb-F-O atoms producing a small expansion of the crystal framework. A maximum of one Li atom per formula unit is inferred with an average potential of 1.29 V vs. $Li/Li^+$. The insertion of lithium atoms induces a semiconductor to metal transition with half of the lithium atoms in the host material. Then, when fully lithiated, the semiconductor behavior is recovered with a small band gap of 1 eV. This small band gap together with a metal behavior during the insertion reaction indicates that $Nb_2O_2F_3$ can be used without the necessity of extra composites for the electronic conductance. Moreover, the ionic conductance should take place most likely in between the layers of the structure with a hop activation energy of 0.79 eV for Li ion migration. These results include niobium oxyfluoride for the first time in the map of possible materials for anode applications.
