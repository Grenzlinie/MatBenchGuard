# Relationship between structure and thermal fatigue in cast iron

H. Fredriksson
P.-A. Sunnerkrantz
P. Ljubinković

Thermal fatigue of a material is determined by rupture stress, the elasticity modulus, heat conductivity, and thermal expansion. In addition to thermal expansion, one has to consider also the volume changes as a result of phase transformations. It is known that high rupture stress and high heat conductivity result in high resistance to thermal fatigue. A high Young's modulus and high thermal expansion give low resistance to thermal fatigue. Cast iron is a composite material, consisting mostly of graphite, ferrite, and cementite. The graphite can occur in a number of different morphologies. It can be spherical, as in ductile cast iron, it can be flakelike, as in flake cast iron, but it can also be rodlike, as in vermicular or undercooled graphite. Many of the properties important for thermal fatigue are influenced by the shape of the graphite. By using various models to explain the properties of composite materials, the changes in the properties of cast iron as a function of graphite shape are analysed. The analytical results are compared with experimental results. It is shown that the elasticity modulus and thermal expansion are lowest for flake graphite and that thermal conductivity is highest for this material. The conclusion is that grey cast iron has a better resistance to thermal fatigue than vermicular as well as nodular cast irons, in spite of its lower rupture stress.

## Introduction

It is well known that grey cast iron has a very high resistance to thermal fatigue compared with many other alloys. It is often better than nodular cast iron. This is, for instance, the situation for brake drums used in large lorries. This is very difficult to understand because nodular cast iron has a much higher yield point than grey cast iron.

There are several different physical properties influen- cing the thermal fatigue of a material. The most important are the yield point, the Young's modulus, thermal diffusivity, and thermal expansion. In cast iron alloys, all those parameters are influenced by the morphology and the fraction of the graphite. In order to discover which one of the above mentioned parameters determines the difference in behaviour between grey iron and nodular iron, a comparison is made between them from theoretical models for composite materials. In the discussion, calculated temperature stresses in the brake drum of a lorry are presented. The difference in magnitude between stresses in a drum made of nodular cast iron and a drum made of grey cast iron is discussed. The reason for the difference is clarified.

## Thermal expansion

The thermal expansion of cast iron is much more complex than for many other materials. It is not only because cast iron is a composite material, it is also because the volume fraction of graphite changes during heating. This change in volume fraction depends on an increase in the carbon content of ferrite and austenite during heating. This change in the carbon content causes a lattice expansion, which also will influence the thermal expansion. All these effects are discussed below.

For a composite material like cast iron, the thermal expansion will be influenced by the bulk modulus and the shear modulus. However, graphite is dissolved during heating and has a lower density than iron. As a first approximation, therefore, it is assumed that the graphite loses contact with the iron matrix. The iron can now be considered to contain pores instead of graphite. According to Grimvall¹ the thermal expansion will now be equal to the thermal expansion of the matrix.

The thermal expansion of cast iron can now be compared with that of steel. However, because of the presence of graphite, the carbon content in the austenite will increase with the temperature. The austenite lattice expands with increasing carbon content.² In order to describe the expansion of austenite, it is assumed that the expansion can be divided into two parts: expansion as a result of thermal expansion and expansion as a result of the transfer of carbon to or from the graphite. In order to describe this mathematically, the following expression can be used

$$
\alpha_{\gamma}=\frac{\delta \varepsilon_{\gamma}}{\delta T}+\frac{\delta \varepsilon_{\gamma}}{\delta C} \frac{\mathrm{d} C}{\mathrm{~d} T}=\alpha_{\gamma}^{T}+\alpha_{\gamma}^{C} \quad \text{....... (1) }
$$

where $\mathrm{d}C/\mathrm{d}T$ is given from the phase diagram and $\delta\varepsilon_\gamma/\delta C$ is the lattice expansion due to the carbon in the austenite. The thermal expansion of the iron matrix at low temperature can be treated in a similar way. However, in this situation, it is necessary only to use the thermal expansion measured for a pearlitic steel. The expression $\alpha_\gamma^C$ was derived by

### Thermal diffusivity

The thermal conductivity in cast iron has been studied by several researchers.⁵ It has been shown that the thermal diffusivity is influenced by the type of graphite as well as by the matrix. The thermal diffusivity usually decreases with temperature and decreases by a factor of two when the shape of the graphite is changed from flakelike to spherical. In order to explain this effect and also to extrapolate the data to higher temperature, the following analysis was made.

The thermal diffusivity of a composite material can, according to Grimvall,¹ be described by the following relation

$$
\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{Fe}}\left(1-f_{\mathrm{gr}} × H_{\lambda}\right) \quad. \quad. \quad. \quad. \quad. \quad. \quad. \quad. \quad. \quad (4)
$$

where $H_{\lambda}$ is dependent on the morphology of the graphite; $H_{\lambda}$ is given in Ref. 1 for both nodular and flakelike graphite.

The values of $\lambda_{\mathrm{Fe}}$ are taken from measurements on steel. In the present situation, the data for a low alloyed low carbon steel were used. With a heat of diffusivity of $0 \cdot 14 × 10^{-4} \mathrm{~m}^{2} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$ at room temperature and by using equation (4), the heat of diffusivity in relation to volume fraction of graphite was calculated. The results are shown in Fig. 3. The figure shows that the heat of diffusivity increases with the volume fraction of graphite. This effect is greatest for the grey cast iron, because dispersion of highly conductive plates provides easy paths for the current of heat.

The heat of diffusivity as a function of temperature was also calculated; the results are shown in Fig. 4. The figure shows the calculation for a cast iron with a volume fraction of graphite of $0 \cdot 10$ at room temperature. The fraction of graphite decreases with increasing temperature. This is evaluated from the phase diagram and is considered in the calculations.

This difference also results in steeper temperature gradients inside the surface for nodular cast iron than for grey cast iron. Higher temperature gradients result in larger temperature stresses.

Calculated stresses at the surface in the same conditions as for Fig. 7 are shown in Fig. 9. It can be seen from the latter figure that the stresses are much larger for the nodular cast iron than for the grey cast iron. The model used does not take into account any plastic deformation. However, it indicates that much lower stresses are obtained in the grey cast iron than in the nodular cast iron. The differences are so large that it cannot be explained from the difference in heat conductivity. There is no difference in heat expansion between the two irons. The only difference in addition to heat diffusivity is that in the elasticity modulus; this must be the reason for the difference in thermal stresses between nodular and grey cast irons.

## Conclusions

The different physical parameters influencing the thermal stresses formed during heating a surface of cast iron have been analysed. It was found that the most important parameters were the elasticity modulus and the heat diffusivity. Grey cast iron has a much lower elasticity modulus and a higher heat diffusivity than nodular cast iron. It is proposed that those two parameters give grey cast iron its much better resistance to thermal fatigue.
