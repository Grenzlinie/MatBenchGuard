**Abstract** — Phase transformations in hypereutectic Ch3F12 and hypoeutectic Ch12MF4 tool alloys were studied by means of differential thermal analysis (DTA). Calculations of phase equilibria for Fe-Cr-V-C and Fe-Cr-V-Mo-C systems corresponding to the investigated alloys were carried out using the thermodynamic database program Thermo-Calc in the temperature range of 750-1700 K. Seven different equilibria consisting of five different phases (ferrite, austenite, liquid, $M_7C_3$ and MC) were determined for both alloys by means of thermodynamic calculations. There was found a good agreement between theoretical (temperatures between different equilibria) and experimental (temperatures of phase transformations) results.

**Résumé** — On a étudié les transformations de phase d'alliages à outils avec Ch3F12 hypereutectique et avec Ch12MF4 hypoeutectique au moyen de l'analyse thermique différentielle (DTA). On a effectué les calculs d'équilibres de phase des systèmes Fe-Cr-V-C et Fe-Cr-V-Mo-C correspondant aux alliages investigués en utilisant le programme de base de données thermodynamiques Thermo-Calc dans la gamme de température de 750 à 1700 K. On a déterminé sept équilibres différents consistant de cinq phases différentes (ferrite, austénite, liquide, $M_7C_3$ et MC) pour les deux alliages au moyen de calculs thermodynamiques. On a trouvé un bon accord entre les résultats théoriques (températures entre les différents équilibres) et expérimentaux (températures de transformations de phase).

## INTRODUCTION

Tool alloys of ledeburite type belong to structural sensitive materials, because their mechanical and technological properties are microstructure dependent. Microstructure and phase analyses of these alloys were performed by Ozerskij *et al.* [1], Geller [2], Golczewski *et al.* [3], Jurči *et al.* [4] and Behulova *et al* [5] . In addition to ferrite and austenite, they also identified $M_7C_3$ and MC carbides. In the case of the MC carbide, non-stoichiometry $MC_x$ (x<1) structures can occur. Some of them such as $M_6C_5$ and $M_8C_7$ form domains of long-range order [6].

With a view to eliminate the formation of undesirable carbide net in tool alloys during classic quasi-equilibrium solidification, the technology of rapid solidification followed by compaction of rapidly solidified powders is obviously used. The latter technological procedures evoke phase transformations influencing both microstructure and properties of the alloys. The knowledge about phase transformations can be acquired by different theoretical and experimental methods. For instance, thermodynamic calculations enabling information about phase equilibria are combined with experimental methods as transmission electron microscope (TEM) including EDX, EELS and electron diffraction [7-10], SEM/WDX [11,12], X-ray [13,14] and differential thermal analysis (DTA) techniques [15,16].

The aim of the present paper is to study phase transformations in hypereutectic Ch3F12 and hypoeutectic Ch12MF4 tool alloys. The experimental quasi-equilibrium results achieved by means of DTA are compared with the results of thermodynamic calculations in a wide temperature range (750-1700 K).

## EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE

Microstructures of the investigated alloys (Ch3F12 contains 3.00 wt.% Cr, 12.00 wt.% V, and 3.00 wt.% C; Ch12MF4 contains 12.06 wt.% Cr, 1.20 wt.% Mo, 4.00 wt.% V and 2.37 wt.% C) were evaluated using the light microscopy and scanning electron microscopy.

Phase transformations were investigated by differential thermal analysis (Perkin-Elmer DTA7). The samples of rapidly solidified (RS) powder were heated from 298 K to 1823 and 1773 K for Ch3F12 and Ch12MF4 alloys, respectively. After holding at the maximum temperature, the samples were cooled to 473 K. Total weights of the samples were 41.00 and 30.98 mg for Ch3F12 and Ch12MF4 alloys, respectively. The cooling and heating of the samples were performed in an argon protective atmosphere at the rate of 10 K/min. The absolute temperature calibration was made with a precision of ± 0.1%.

## THERMODYNAMIC CALCULATIONS

In the present work the thermodynamic model for phases with several sublattices developed by Hillert and Staffansson [17] was used to describe Gibbs energies of individual phases.

### Thermodynamic Model for Solid Solution Phases body centred cubic (bcc) and face centred cubic (fcc) and Liquid

Gibbs energies for bcc and fcc phases in the Fe-Cr-V-C and Fe-Cr-Mo-V-C systems were described using the two sublattice model consisting of metal and interstitial sublattices. The metallic elements can substitute for each site on the metal sublattice and C and vacancies on the interstitial sublattice. For one formula unit (Fe,Cr,V)ₐ(C,Va)c, the model gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{m}= & \sum_{i} y_{i}\left(y_{C} G_{i: C}^{o}+y_{V a} G_{i: V a}^{o}\right)+a R T \sum_{i} y_{i} \ln y_{i} \\
+ & c R T\left(y_{C} \ln y_{C}+y_{V a} \ln y_{V a}\right) \\
+ & \sum_{i} \sum_{j} y_{i} y_{j}\left(y_{C} L_{i, j: C}+y_{V a} L_{i, j: V a}\right)+y_{C} y_{V a} \sum_{i} y_{i} L_{i: C, V a}{ }^{(1)} \\
+ & \sum_{i} \sum_{j} \sum_{k} y_{i} y_{j} y_{k}\left(y_{C} L_{i, j, k: C}+y_{V a} L_{i, j, k: V a}\right) \\
+ & G_{m a g} \quad i, j, k=F e, C r, V \text { but } i \neq j \neq k
\end{aligned}
$$

In Equation 1, $y_{i}$ denotes the site fraction of component $i$ in the sublattice. Symbols $a$ and $c$ denote the numbers of sites on each sublattice. In the case of bcc, $a$=1 and $c$=3; for fcc: $a$=$c$=1. The components in the different sublattices are separated by a colon and in the same sublattice by a comma. $G_{i: V a}^{o}$ is the Gibbs energy of pure element $i$ in a relevant non-magnetic state and $G_{i: C}^{o}$ is the Gibbs energy of a hypothetical non-magnetic state where all interstitial sites are occupied by carbon. All values of $G$ are given relative to the reference state which is defined as a standard state at 298,15 K and $10^{5}$ Pa. $L$ is the interaction parameter expressed by the Redlich-Kister polynomial as a concentration dependence. $G_{mag}$ is the magnetic part of Gibbs energy.

Liquid is described using a model with one sublattice where metallic components and C are mixed together. Gibbs energy of liquid phase is described by

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{m} & =\sum_{i} y_{i} G_{i}^{o}+R T \sum_{i} y_{i} \ln y_{i} \\
+ & \sum_{i} \sum_{j} y_{i} y_{j} L_{i, j}+\sum_{i} \sum_{j} \sum_{k} y_{i} y_{j} y_{k} L_{i, j, k}
\end{aligned}
$$

$i,j,k=Fe,Cr,V,Mo,C$ but $i≠j≠k$

### Thermodynamic Model for $MC$, $M_7C_3$, $M_3C$

The MC phase has a fcc arrangement of the metal atoms with the carbon and atoms randomly distributed among the octahedral sites. The same thermodynamic model introduced for the fcc solid solution is also used for this carbide. $M_7C_3$ and $M_3C$ are described as stoichiometric phases with the two sublattice model where the metallic elements mix themselves in one sublattice and the other sublattice is filled with carbon. For one formula unit (Fe,Cr,V)ₐ(C)c of $M_7C_3$, the model gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{m}= & \sum_{i} y_{i} G_{i: C}^{o}+a R T \sum_{i} y_{i} \ln y_{i}+\sum_{i} \sum_{j} y_{i} y_{j} L_{i, j: C} \\
+ & \sum_{i} \sum_{j} \sum_{k} y_{i} y_{j} y_{k} L_{i, j, k: C}
\end{aligned}
$$

$i,j,k=Fe,Cr,V$ but $i≠j≠k$

The calculations of phase equilibria were performed by Thermo-Calc software [18] for the temperature range of 750-1700 K. The thermodynamic parameters were taken from the literature (Fe-Cr-Mo) [19], (Fe-Mo-C) [20], (Fe-V-C) [21], (Fe-Cr-V) [22], (Fe-Cr-V-C) [23], (Fe-Cr-Mo-V-C) [24]. Data for pure elements were taken from Dinsdale [25].

## RESULTS

In the microstructure of the hypereutectic Ch3F12 alloy (Figure 1), prior MC carbides (I), eutectic areas (II) consisting of MC and $M_7C_3$ carbides and transformed austenite (ferrite + secondary carbides) were found. Grains of transformed proeutectic austenite (I), eutectic areas (II) consisting of MC and $M_7C_3$ carbides and transformed eutectic austenite were visible in the microstructure of the hypoeutectic Ch12MF4 alloy (Figure 2).

Figures 3a and 3b show DTA traces for heated and cooled Ch3F12 alloy, respectively. Five peaks were noticed during heating (Figure 3a). The first peak (the onset temperature of 885 K) represents the transformation of metastable austenite to ferrite and carbides. The second small endothermic peak (at 1022 K) can be explained by the transformation from ferromagnetic to the paramagnetic state. Transformation of ferrite to austenite corresponds to the third (endothermic) peak at 1049 K. The peak at 1412 K probably characterizes the ordering of MC carbide. The last peak (at 1508 K) represents the transition of the alloy to a liquid state.

In Figure 3b, four exothermic peaks are documented. MC carbide starts to crystallize at 1704 K (the first peak). The start of austenite formation was recorded at 1591 K (the second peak). The third and fourth peaks at 1430 and 1378 K probably characterized ordering reactions in MC carbide.

## DISCUSSION

As is evident from Figures 3 and 4, DTA allows the recording of different phase transformations of the first and second orders. Namely:
- transformation of non-equilibrium to quasi-equilibrium state (exothermic peak at 885 K in Figure 3a),
- transformations in quasi-equilibrium state (peak at 1526 K in Figure 4c),
- transformation of ferromagnetic to paramagnetic state (peak at 1022 K in Figure 3a),
- ordering reactions (peak at 1412 K in Figure 3a).

### Phase Transformations in Ch3F12 Alloy
In the temperature range of 750-1700 K, seven equilibria consisting of five different phases (ferrite, austenite, liquid, $M_7C_3$ and MC) were determined by means of thermody- namic calculations (Table I and Figure 5). No direct evi- dence of the $M_7C_3$ carbide participation in phase transfor- mations was recorded in DTA traces (Figure 3). It can be explained by the possible absence of this carbide in real (non-equilibrium) conditions. Even if the $M_7C_3$ carbide is present, its volume fraction in the microstructure is proba- bly low and the peak corresponding to the $M_7C_3$ dissolution can be overlapped by the stronger peak of ferrite-austenite transformation.

The second "low temperature" phase, ferrite, is stable up to 1075 K according to the thermodynamic calculations (Table I). By means of DTA the end of ferrite occurrence dur- ing heating was indicated at 1099 K (Figure 3a) which is in good agreement with the theoretical assumption (Table II).

Austenite as an equilibrium phase was predicted in the temperature range of 1046 -1581 K by means of the ther- modynamic calculations (Table I). The lower limit of austenite occurrence was also determined experimentally (1049 K) (Figure 3a) corresponding to the heating regime of DTA. During the cooling, austenite starts to form at 1591 K (Figure. 3b). Thus, good agreement between the theoret- ical and experimental results was also confirmed for austenite occurrence (Table II).

According to the thermodynamic calculations, the MC carbide was found to be stable in the widest temperature range (750-1643 K) from all the equilibrium phases (Table I). Even if the lowest temperature considered in the calculations was 750 K, the MC carbide is also expected to be stable at lower temperatures. It dissolves during the heating at the last of the solid phases which indicates that MC can be charac- terized as a prior carbide. The above theoretical assumption was also confirmed by means of DTA (Figure 3a, b) even if the experimentally determined temperatures of the MC start (cooling), 1704 K and the start of all liquid occurrence (heat- ing), 1647 K, differ from one another by 57 K (Table II). The calculated temperature of the end of MC equilibrium occur- rence, 1643 K, is more or less comparable with the men- tioned experimental temperatures which indicates the relia- bility of data used in modelling the liquid phase.

The theoretically predicted hypoeutectic character of the alloy was also confirmed experimentally (see DTA peak at 1597 K in Figure 4c). The calculated equilibrium value of the end of austenite occurrence (1613 K) lies between corresponding experimentally determined values (1597 K for cooling and 1648 K for heating, Table III). ## CONCLUSIONS

The results of the investigations of phase transformations in hypereutectic Ch3F12 and hypoeutectic Ch12MF4 tool alloys using DTA technique and thermodynamic calcula- tions of phase equilibria can be summarized as follows:

1. Seven equilibria consisting of five different phases (fer- rite, austenite, liquid, $M_7C_3$ and MC) were determined in both investigated alloys by means of thermodynamic cal- culations for the temperature range of 750-1700 K.

2. For the Ch3F12 alloy, the heating regime of DTA revealed the following phase transformations: start of austenite occurrence (1049K), end of ferrite occurrence (1099K), start of liquid occurrence (1508 K) and start of all liquid phase occurrence (1647 K). During the cooling, the start of MC occurrence (1704 K) and the start of austenite occurrence (1591 K) were recorded.

3. For the Ch12MF4 alloy, the heating regime of DTA revealed the following phase transformations: start of austenite occurrence (1114K), start of liquid occurrence (1498 K) and start of all liquid phase occurrence (1648 K). During the cooling, the start of austenite occurrence (1597 K), the start of MC occurrence (1526 K) and the start of $M_7C_3$ occurrence (1512 K) were recorded.

4. Good agreement between theoretical and experimental results was found.
