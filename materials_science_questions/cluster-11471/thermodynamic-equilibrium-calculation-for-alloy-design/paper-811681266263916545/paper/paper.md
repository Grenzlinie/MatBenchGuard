## ABSTRACT

The tensile strength of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N and 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N during creep testing at $700\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ has been studied. The high temperature tensile tests of two steels after aging revealed that the tensile strength of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N is higher than that of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N. Because the tensile fractures of two steels are intergranular, it can be inferred that the interfacial strength of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N is weaker than that of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N. The main precipitation on grain boundaries in two steels is $M_{23}C_6$, which generally grows in a cube orientation relationship with the matrix. So XRD is used to define the lattice constants of the matrix and $M_{23}C_6$, with the specific interfacial energy between the matrix and $M_{23}C_6$ calculated correspondingly. The results reveal that the different specific interfacial energies between the $M_{23}C_6$ and the matrix in two steel are the main reason for the different interface strengths and prove that the interface strength of the matrix and $M_{23}C_6$ in 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N is weaker than that in 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N. The difference between the specific interfacial energy of two steels was caused by the difference of the lattice constants of $M_{23}C_6$ in two steels. Combined with EDS analysis result, it can be considered that the main reason for the difference of $M_{23}C_6$ lattice constant is the solution of Mo in $M_{23}C_6$.

## 1. Introduction

Austenitic heat-resistant stainless steels play an important role in high temperature structural applications [1,2]. The high chromium content improves corrosion resistance, and the well-balanced Nb, Ti and N contents increase creep rupture strength compared with conventional austenitic heat-resistant steels [3]. 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N contains large amounts of nickel and molybdenum that are advantageous in a chloride environment, and it is finding many applications [4]. 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N austenitic stainless steel is widely used as superheater and reheater tubes in recent ultra-supercritical (USC) boilers [5-7]. Two steels are always used at $700\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ in USC boilers, so it is meaningful to investigate the tensile strength of two steels after aging.

## 2. Experimental procedure

The compositions of two tested steels are shown in Table 1. The as-received samples were treated by solution, with ASTM grain size of 5.5-6.5. The samples used for tensile tests were aged at $700\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 500h, 1000h and 3000h, respectively. The tensile properties were studied systematically using Gleeble-3800 heat simulating apparatus. The test temperature was $700\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and the strain rate was $0.01\,\text{s}^{-1}$.

Metallographic samples were mounted, and ground in a conventional manner with 180, 400, 600 and 1000-grit emery paper, followed by mechanical polishing with 3.5 and $1\,\mu\text{m}$ diamond paste, and at last etched by a solution of $\text{HCl}+\text{HNO}_{3}$ (2:1). The microstructures were analyzed by using SEM/EDS (S-4200). An accelerating voltage of 20 kV was used for SEM/EDS.

The precipitates were electrolytically extracted using the 1% $(\text{CH}_{3})_{4}\text{NCl}$ methanol solution of acetylacetone (methanol:acetylacetone=9:1). The residue was separated using a membrane filter with $0.05\,\mu\text{m}$ pores. The extracted carbides powder was analyzed by Rigaku D/max-2550 X-Ray diffractometer with $\text{CuK}_{\alpha}$ radiation and graphite monochromators. The diffraction data were collected with a step width of $0.02^{\circ}$ and a count time of 2.0 s per step.

### 3.2. Precipitates on the grain boundary

The $M_{23}C_{6}$ carbide is usually the first phase to form in austenitic stainless steel [8]. $M_{23}C_{6}$ carbides precipitate on grain boundaries when a solution-treated stainless steel is aged isothermally or slowly cooled within the temperature range of 1123–773 K [9]. Sourmail found that $M_{23}C_{6}$ precipitates on the grain boundaries in 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N after aging [10]. And Cheng also finds $M_{23}C_{6}$ precipitates on the grain boundaries in 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N after aging [11]. SEM/EDS were used to identify the precipitates on the grain boundaries in both steels. Fig. 3 shows the SEM micrographs of two steels. It is evident that the $M_{23}C_{6}$ precipitates along the grain boundaries. The comparison of the chemical compositions of $M_{23}C_{6}$ between the two steels is shown in Table 2. Mo is found to dissolve in $M_{23}C_{6}$ of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N instead of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N.

### 3.3. XRD analysis of austenite and $M_{23}C_{6}$

Most precipitates on the grain boundary of two steels are $M_{23}C_{6}$, so the bonding of $M_{23}C_{6}$ carbides with the matrices has a significant effect on the interfacial strength. $M_{23}C_{6}$ has a face-centered cubic structure with a lattice parameter varying between 10.57 and 10.68 nm, which is about three times than that of the austenite [12]. It is reported that $M_{23}C_{6}$ generally grows with a cube orientation relationship $\{ 100\} _{\gamma }//\{ 100\} _{M_{23}C_{6}}$ $[010]_{\gamma }//[010]_{M_{23}C_{6}}$ [4,5]. So the lattice constant of $M_{23}C_{6}$ and matrices defined by XRD can be used to calculate the specific interfacial energy. The higher the specific interfacial energy is, the weaker the interface strength is.

Bulk samples were prepared for XRD analysis of the austenite. Fig. 4 shows the XRD analytical results. The lattice of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N is 3.59798 Å and of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N is 3.59450 Å. The lattices of two austenitic matrices are quite similar.

The results reveal that $M_{23}C_{6}$ holds the majority of extracted residues in two aged samples. The difference in $2\theta$ implies the difference in lattice parameters. The measured lattice parameters are plotted as a function of $\cos^{2}(\theta)/\sin(\theta)+\cos^{2}(\theta)/\theta$, the Nelson-Riley function, and extrapolated to $\theta=90^{\circ}$ to minimize errors due to absorption [13], which is illustrated in Fig. 5b. The lattice of $M_{23}C_{6}$ in 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N is 10.65841 Å, and of $M_{23}C_{6}$ in 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N is 10.68570 Å. The lattice of $M_{23}C_{6}$ in 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N is larger than that in 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N. Both $M_{23}C_{6}$ and austenite have a face-centered cubic structure and there is parallel phase relationship between them, so the interfacial energy on three-dimensional orientation is isotropic. Because the elastic modulus of $M_{23}C_{6}$ is apparently bigger than that of austenite, interfacial misfit dislocations exist in austenite [15]. Misfit $\delta$ can be calculated by Eq. (1)

$$
\delta=\left|\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{M}_{23} \mathrm{C}_{6}}-3 \alpha_{\gamma}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{M}_{23} \mathrm{C}_{6}}}\right| \tag{1}
$$

The stable dislocations in austenite and at the interface are $(1/2)\langle 110\rangle$ and $\langle 100\rangle$, respectively. The dislocation in austenite with lower energy is inclined to exist. The Burgers vector of $(1/2)\langle 110\rangle$ is $b=a_{\gamma}/\sqrt{2}$. So the specific interfacial energy $\sigma$ between $M_{23}C_{6}$ and austenite can be calculated by Eq. (2) [15]

$$
\sigma=\frac{G b}{2 \pi(1-\nu)} f(\delta)=\frac{E \alpha_{\gamma}}{4 \sqrt{2}\left(1-\nu^{2}\right)} f(\delta) \tag{2}
$$

where $f(\delta)$ is correction factor to eliminate the overlap effect of the elastic stress field and $f(\delta)=\delta[(2/(1+(1/4\delta^{2})))-\ln(2\delta)]$; $E$ is elastic modulus of austenite; $\nu$ is Poisson ratio; $\alpha_{\gamma}$ is the lattice parameter of austenite.

Elastic modulus of austenite varies with temperature. The elastic modulus of austenite at room temperature (the room temperature is set as $25\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$) and $700\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ can be calculated according to Eq. (3).

$$
E_{\gamma}(\mathrm{MPa})=254680-114.76 T \tag{3}
$$

Fig. 6 shows the calculated specific interfacial energy between $M_{23}C_6$ and austenite in 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N and 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N at room temperature and 700 °C. It shows that the specific interfacial energy of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N is higher than that of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N from room temperature to 700 °C. Higher specific interfacial energy leads to a weaker interfacial strength, and consequently interfacial cracks are apt to generate in 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N, which provides it a lower tensile strength.

### 4. Conclusion

The tensile strength of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N and 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N after aging testing at 700 °C has been studied. Combined with the analysis of EDS and the calculation of specific interfacial energy, the following conclusions were obtained:

1. After aging, 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N has a higher tensile strength than that of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N. Due to the same fracture mechanism, it can be inferred that the interfacial strength of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N is weaker than that of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N.
2. The existence of Mo in $M_{23}C_6$ precipitates causes an expansion of the lattice of $M_{23}C_6$ in 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N.
3. The difference of lattice constants of $M_{23}C_6$ leads to the disparity of the interfacial strength. The calculated results shows that the specific interfacial energy of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N is higher than that of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N from room temperature to 700 °C. The lower specific interfacial energy leads to the stronger interfacial strength. So the tensile strength of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N is higher than that of 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N.
4. The solution of Mo in $M_{23}C_6$ plays an important role on the tensile strength of 22Cr-25Ni-Mo-Nb-N and 25Cr-20Ni-Nb-N at high temperature.
