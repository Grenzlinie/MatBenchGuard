# FREE ENERGY OF A CUBIC FERROELECTRIC IN THE RING APPROXIMATION

I.O. Maier

By summation of ring diagrams the free energies are calculated for the rhombohedral and tetragonal phases of a cubic ferroelectric in the critical region; equations for the binodals and spinodals are obtained, and the jumps in the value of the order parameter at them are found.

The interaction of critical fluctuations of the order parameter may lead to transformation of a continuous phase transition into a phase transition of the first kind, as occurs, for example, in a tetragonal ferromagnet with easy-plane anisotropy [1] and in the anisotropic Fermi liquid $\mathrm{He}^{3}$ [2,3]. The fluctuation-induced change in the kind of the transition is manifested in the loss of positive definiteness of the form of fourth order in the Landau expansion of the free energy as the transition point is approached from the high-temperature phase. A similar phenomenon holds in scalar electrodynamics, in which the form of the effective potential in the tree approximation indicates the fact of spontaneous symmetry breaking but does not by itself make it possible to determine the nature of the resulting rearrangement of the vacuum [4]. The picture becomes more complete when allowance is made for the radiative corrections to the effective potential, which in the lowest order in the charge are given by an infinite sequence of ring diagrams [4]. Similarly, a detailed description of a transition of the first kind requires calculation of the free energy (the effective potential) in at least the order following the Landau approximation.

In the theory of phase transitions, the method of summation of ring diagrams was used for the first time in [1], in which the free energies of the low-temperature phases of a tetragonal ferromagnet were found. For a cubic crystal, a similar problem was solved in [5], and for a tetragonal ferroelectric it was solved in [6]. The free energies of the superfluid phases of $\mathrm{He}^{3}$ were calculated in [7].

Since the application of [4,1] there have appeared a number of papers in which ring summation has been used to study spontaneous symmetry breaking in various models of statistical physics and quantum field theory.

In the present paper, this method will be applied to a cubic crystal with dipole forces in zero electric field. Expressions will be obtained for the free energies of the rhombohedral and tetragonal phases in the critical region, together with the equations of the curves of coexistence of the phases (binodals) and the curves of absolute instability of the high-temperature phase (spinodals); the jumps in the value of the order parameter on them are also found.

The Hamiltonian of a cubic crystal with dipole forces and anisotropic spectrum of the fluctuations has the form [8]

$$
\begin{gathered}
H=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{\alpha, \beta=1}^{3} \sum_{\mathbf{q}}\left[\left(x_{0}^{2}+q^{2}+f q_{\alpha}^{2}\right) \delta_{\alpha \beta}+\left(\Delta^{2}+h q^{2}\right) n_{\alpha} n_{\beta}\right] \times \\
\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{q}) \varphi_{\beta}(-\mathbf{q})+\frac{1}{4} \sum_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime \prime}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime \prime \prime}=0}\left[\gamma_{2}^{(0)}+\left(\gamma_{1}^{(0)}-\gamma_{2}^{(0)}\right) \delta_{\alpha \beta}\right] \varphi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{q}) \varphi_{\alpha}\left(\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\right) \varphi_{\beta}\left(\mathbf{q}^{\prime \prime}\right) \varphi_{\beta}\left(\mathbf{q}^{\prime \prime \prime}\right),
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
$$

where $x_{0}^{2}$ is a linear function of the temperature; $n_{\alpha}=q_{\alpha} / q$; $\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{q})$ is the Fourier component of the field of polarization fluctuations; $\gamma_{i}^{(0)}$ and $f$ are the bare parameters of the anisotropy; and $\Delta$ is the dipole gap. In what follows, this last is assumed to be much greater than all the important momenta, since $\Delta$ in ferroelectrics is, as a rule, of the order of the cutoff momentum.

The free energy in the approximation of ring diagrams is given by the expression [9]

$$
F^{(k)}=F_{0}^{(k)}-\ln _{R} \int \exp \left\{\sum_{i j} A_{i j}^{(k)}(\mathbf{q}) \varphi_{i}(\mathbf{q}) \varphi_{j}(-\mathbf{q})\right\} D \varphi,

$$

where k = 1 and k = 2 correspond, respectively, to the tetragonal and rhombohedral phases; $F_{0}^{(k)}$ is the free energy of the corresponding phase in Landau's theory; the matrix $\tilde{A}^{(k)}$ describes the anisotropic spectrum of critical fluctuations and is equal to the coefficient of the Gaussian part of the Hamiltonian (1) with allowance for the shift of the components of the field of fluctuations $\varphi_{\alpha}$ by the magnitude of the component of the polarization vector $\mathbf{P}$. The symbol R denotes three subtractions. The necessity of subtractions can be seen from the fact that the series of ring diagrams must begin with the terms of sixth order in the polarization.

In the tetragonal phase, the polarization vector is directed along the side of the cube: $\varphi_{1} \rightarrow \varphi_{1}+\mathbf{P}$.
At the same time,
$$
F_{0}^{(1)}=\varkappa^{2} P^{2} / 2+\gamma_{1} P^{4} / 8.
\tag{3}
$$

In (3), the coupling constants are replaced by the dressed constants. The Gaussian functional integral under the sign of the logarithm in (2) can be expressed in terms of the determinant of the matrix $\tilde{A}^{(k)}(\mathbf{q})$, which for the tetragonal phase in the linear approximation in $f$ is equal to
$$
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{det} \tilde{A}^{(1)}(\mathbf{q})=q^{4}\left[1+2 f\left(n_{1}^{2} n_{2}^{2}+n_{1}^{2} n_{3}^{2}+n_{2}^{2} n_{3}^{2}\right)\right]+2 q^{2} \varkappa^{2}\left[1+f\left(n_{1}^{2} n_{2}^{2}+n_{1}^{2} n_{3}^{2}+n_{2}^{2} n_{3}^{2}\right)+\right. \\
\left.{ }^{1} /{ }_{2}\left(1+n_{1}^{2}\right) p^{2}+f n_{1}^{2} p^{2}\left(1-n_{1}^{2}\right)\right]+\varkappa^{4}\left(1+p^{2}\right)\left(1+n_{1}^{2} p^{2}\right).
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
$$

Here, $p^{2}=\gamma_{2} P^{2} / 2 \varkappa^{2}$, the anisotropy $f$ is equal to its bare value, since in the lowest order in the charges the renormalizations of $f$ are unimportant [8], and the ratio $\gamma_{1} / \gamma_{2}=z$ is set equal to zero. The choice $z=0$ can be illustrated by means of the diagram of the phase trajectories of the renormalization-group equations for a cubic crystal [10]. The boundary of the region of stability of the Hamiltonian (1) on the transition to the tetragonal phase in the Landau approximation is the axis $g_{2}\left(g_{1}=0\right), g_{i} \sim \gamma_{i}$. Allowance for the fluctuation correction to the free energy leads to a deviation of the stability boundary from the axis $g_{2}$. The phase diagram of the renormalization-group equations of the cubic ferroelectric with anisotropic fluctuation spectrum is shown in Fig.1. The hatching shows the boundaries of the stability region of the Hamiltonian constructed with allowance for the fluctuation corrections. The broken line is the Heisenberg line corresponding to an isotropic crystal. The Hamiltonian becomes unstable when $g_{1} \neq 0$. This deviation is however manifested only in the following order in the charges, i.e., in the two-loop corrections [6].

Substitution of (3) and (4) in (2) and integration over the momenta gives
$$
\begin{gathered}
F^{(1)}=\frac{\varkappa^{3}}{12 \pi g_{2}}\left\{p^{2}+\frac{z}{2} p^{4}+g_{2}\left[\left(2-\frac{6}{5} f\right)+\left(2-\frac{179}{280} f\right) p^{2}+\right.\right. \\
\left.\left.\left(\frac{9}{20}+\frac{13}{140} f\right) p^{4}-\left(2-\frac{6}{5} f\right)\left(1+p^{2}\right)^{\hbar}+\left(1-\frac{65}{56} f\right) p^{2} \sqrt{1+p^{2}}-\left(\frac{1}{5}-\frac{3}{40} f\right) \frac{p^{4}}{\sqrt{1+p^{2}}}\right]\right\}, \quad g_{2}=\frac{\gamma_{2}}{12 \pi \varkappa}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
$$

The rénormalization-group equations for a cubic ferroelectric, considered in detail in [10], are valid for renormalized charges $g_{1}, g_{2}^{2} \cong 1$. Under these conditions $p^{2} \gg 1$ and it follows from (5) that
$$
F^{(1)}=\frac{\varkappa^{3}}{12 \pi g_{2}}\left\{\left[1+g_{2}\left(2-\frac{179}{280} f\right)\right] p^{2}-\left(\frac{6}{5}-\frac{4}{35} f\right) g_{2}|p|^{3}+\left[\frac{z}{2}+\left(\frac{9}{20}+\frac{13}{140} f\right) g_{2}\right] p^{4}\right\}.
\tag{6}
$$

The equation of the binodal has the form
$$
g_{1} \cong-g_{2}^{2}\left(\frac{9}{10}+\frac{13}{70} f\right)\left[1-4 g_{2}\left(\frac{9}{5}-\frac{2}{7} f\right) /\left(9+\frac{13}{7} f\right)\right],
\tag{7}
$$
and the jump in the polarization on it is
$$
p_{b} \cong \frac{2}{\left({ }^{6} /{ }_{5}-{ }^{4} /{ }_{35} f\right)}\left[\frac{1}{g_{2}}+\left(2-\frac{179}{280} f\right)\right].
\tag{8}
$$

For the spinodal we obtain, respectively,
$$
g_{1} \cong-g_{2}^{2}\left(\frac{9}{10}+\frac{13}{70} f\right)\left[1-\frac{9}{2} g_{2}\left(\frac{9}{5}-\frac{2}{7} f\right) /\left(9+\frac{13}{7} f\right)\right],
\tag{9}
$$
$$
p_{s} \cong \frac{4}{3} \frac{1}{\left({ }^{6} /{ }_{5}-{ }^{4} /{ }_{35} f\right)}\left[\frac{1}{g_{2}}+\left(2-\frac{179}{280} f\right)\right].
\tag{10}
$$

In the rhombohedral phase, the polarization is directed along the diagonal of the cube, $\varphi_{\alpha} \to \varphi_{\alpha}+P/\sqrt{3}$ ($\alpha=1,2,3$). Then
$$
F_{0}^{(2)}=\frac{\varkappa_{2} P^{2}}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{1} P^{4}}{24}+\frac{\gamma_{2} P^{4}}{12}, \tag{11}
$$
and in the linear approximation in $f$
$$
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{det} \tilde{A}^{(2)}(\mathbf{q})=q^{4}\left[1+2 f\left(n_{1}^{2} n_{2}^{2}+n_{1}^{2} n_{3}^{2}+n_{2}^{2} n_{3}^{2}\right)\right]+2 q^{2} \varkappa^{2}\left[1+2 /{ }_{3} p^{2}+1 /{ }_{3} p^{2}\left(n_{1} n_{2}+n_{1} n_{3}+n_{2} n_{3}\right)+\right. \\
\left.f\left(1+2 /{ }_{3} p^{2}\right)\left(n_{1}^{2} n_{2}^{2}+n_{1}^{2} n_{3}^{2}+n_{2}^{2} n_{3}^{2}\right)+f^{1} /{ }_{3} p^{2} n_{1} n_{2} n_{3}\left(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}\right)\right]+\varkappa^{4}\left[1+4 /{ }_{3} p^{2}+2 /{ }_{3} p^{2} ×\right. \\
\left.\left(n_{1} n_{2}+n_{1} n_{3}+n_{2} n_{3}\right)+{ }^{1} /{ }_{3} p^{4}+2 /{ }_{3} p^{4}\left(n_{1} n_{2}+n_{1} n_{3}+n_{2} n_{3}\right)\right], \tag{12}
\end{gathered}
$$
$p^{2}=\gamma_{1} P^{2} / 2 \varkappa^{2}$, and the ratio $\gamma_{1} / \gamma_{2}=z$ is taken equal to its value on the stability boundary on the transition to the rhombohedral phase, $z=-2$.

Calculating the Gaussian integral in (2) and integrating then (12) over the momenta as we did in the previous case, we obtain, substituting (11) in (2),
$$
\begin{gathered}
F^{(2)}=\frac{\varkappa^{3}}{12 \pi g_{1}}\left\{p^{2}+\frac{p^{4}}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{z}\right)+g_{1}\left[2+\left(2+\frac{2}{5} f\right) p^{2}+\right.\right. \\
\left.\left.\left(\frac{9}{20}+\frac{8}{63} f\right) p^{4}-2\left(1+p^{2}\right)^{3 / 2}+\left(1-\frac{2}{5} f\right) p^{2} \sqrt{1+p^{2}}-\left(\frac{1}{5}-\frac{23}{630} f\right) \frac{p^{4}}{\sqrt{1+p^{2}}}\right]\right\}.
\end{gathered}
$$

In the limit $p^{2} \gg 1$
$$
F^{(2)}=\frac{\varkappa^{3}}{12 \pi g_{1}}\left\{\left[1+g_{2}\left(2+\frac{2}{5} f\right)\right] p^{2}-\left(\frac{6}{5}+\frac{229}{630} f\right) g_{1}|p|^{3}+\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{z}\right)+g_{1}\left(\frac{9}{20}+\frac{8}{63} f\right)\right] p^{4}\right\}. \tag{13}
$$

The equation for the binodal is given by
$$
g_{2} \cong-\frac{g_{1}}{2}-g_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{27}{20}+\frac{8}{21} f\right)\left[1-g_{1}\left(\frac{36}{25}+\frac{229}{315} f\right) /\left(\frac{9}{5}+\frac{32}{63} f\right)\right] \tag{14}
$$
and for the spinodal by
$$
g_{2} \cong-\frac{g_{1}}{2}-g_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{27}{20}+\frac{8}{21} f\right)\left[1-\frac{9}{32} g_{1}\left(\frac{36}{25}+\frac{229}{315} f\right) /\left(\frac{9}{20}+\frac{8}{63} f\right)\right]. \tag{15}
$$

The corresponding jumps in the polarization on these curves are
$$
p_{b} \cong\left[2 /\left(\frac{6}{5}+\frac{229}{630} f\right)\right]\left[\frac{1}{g_{1}}+\left(2+\frac{2}{5} f\right)\right], \tag{16}
$$
$$
p_{s} \cong \frac{4}{3}\left[1 /\left(\frac{6}{5}+\frac{229}{630} f\right)\right]\left[\frac{1}{g_{1}}+\left(2+\frac{2}{5} f\right)\right]. \tag{17}
$$

In conjunction with the Gell-Mann-Low equations [10], Eqs. (6)-(10) and (13)-(17) obtained here make

it possible to describe completely transitions of the first kind in a cubic ferroelectric. They can be rewritten in terms of variables that can be directly expressed in terms of experimentally controllable quantities: $\varkappa_{0}, \gamma_{1}^{(0)}, \gamma_{2}^{(0)}$. For this, it is sufficient to obtain the solution of the renormalization-group equations [10] in the linear approximation in $f$.
