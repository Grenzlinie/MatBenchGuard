# Phase Diagram, Static Susceptibilities, and Transport Coefficients of a Symmetric Lennard-Jones Mixture from Monte Carlo and Molecular Dynamics Simulations

## Problem background
The bulk viscosity (η_B) describes the response of a fluid to compression or expansion. It plays a central role in the damping of longitudinal sound and is expected to be sensitive to critical fluctuations near a liquid–liquid unmixing transition. In a dense binary liquid mixture, the shear viscosity (η_s) and bulk viscosity can behave differently when approaching the coexistence line. This task reproduces a computational study of a symmetric Lennard-Jones mixture that investigates the phase diagram, static susceptibilities, and transport coefficients (η_s and η_B) in the one-phase region near the miscibility gap. The goal is to compute the liquid–liquid coexistence curve, the critical temperature, the isothermal compressibility κ_T and concentration susceptibility χ, and the shear and bulk viscosities along a temperature path from high temperature down to a coexistence state.

## Approach
The system is a binary (AB) Lennard-Jones mixture with identical particle sizes σ_AA = σ_BB = σ_AB = σ = 1, energy parameters ε_AA = ε_BB = ε = 1, and cross-interaction ε_AB = 0.5 ε. The total number density is ρσ³ = 1, the pair potential is truncated and shifted at r_c = 2.5 σ, and particles have equal masses. All quantities are reported in Lennard-Jones reduced units.

The coexistence curve in the temperature–concentration (T–x_B) plane is obtained by semigrand-canonical Monte Carlo (MC) simulations at zero chemical potential difference, Δμ = 0. Simulations are performed for total particle numbers N = 400, 800, 1600, and 3200 to assess finite-size effects. From these runs the average concentration x_B is recorded as a function of temperature, and the concentration susceptibility χ is obtained from equilibrium fluctuations. The critical temperature T_c is extracted by fitting the coexistence concentrations to the power-law form 0.5 ± x_B^coex = B (1 – T/T_c)^β with the Ising critical exponent β = 0.325.

An equilibrated configuration at T = 1.4 and x_B = 0.10375 (on the coexistence line) from the N = 1600 simulation is then used as the starting point for molecular dynamics. Configurations in the one-phase region at the same composition but higher temperatures (T = 1.6, 1.7, 3.0, 6.0) are generated by heating and re-equilibration with an Andersen thermostat. For each temperature, 5 independent microcanonical (NVE) MD runs of 4.8 × 10⁶ steps are performed using the velocity Verlet algorithm with a time step of 0.01 (in LJ time units).

From the MD trajectories, the partial static structure factors S_αβ(q) are computed; the number–number structure factor S_nn(q) and concentration–concentration structure factor S_cc(q) are constructed, and the isothermal compressibility κ_T is obtained from the q → 0 limit of S_nn(q). The concentration susceptibility χ is taken from the MC fluctuation data. The shear viscosity η_s and bulk viscosity η_B are calculated via the Green–Kubo formulas integrating the autocorrelation functions of the off-diagonal and diagonal elements of the pressure tensor, respectively. The ratio η_B/η_s is reported as an indicator of the relative behavior of the two viscosities.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Semigrand-canonical Monte Carlo simulation for phase diagram and concentration susceptibility
- Role: process
- Action: Run semigrand-canonical Monte Carlo for the symmetric Lennard-Jones mixture (σ=ε=1, δ=0.5, ρσ³=1, cutoff r_c=2.5σ) at Δμ=0 for system sizes N=400, 800, 1600, 3200. Perform equilibration and production runs. Collect coexistence concentrations x_B vs T and compute concentration susceptibility χ from fluctuations. Save an equilibrated configuration at T=1.4, x_B=0.10375 for N=1600.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/mc_evidence.txt`

### Step 2: Fit coexistence curve and extract critical temperature
- Role: scored
- Action: Fit the coexistence data from step 1 to the Ising scaling form: f(x_B)=0.5±x_B^coex = B (1 - T/T_c)^β with β=0.325. Output the fitted T_c and the coexistence data to coexistence_data.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/coexistence_data.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Columns: N (integer or 'Tc'), T (float, NaN for the Tc row), x_B_coex (float, critical temperature value for the Tc row). Optional column x_B_err.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 3: Molecular dynamics simulations for trajectories
- Role: process
- Action: Using the configuration from step 1 at T=1.4, x_B=0.10375 as starting point, heat/re-equilibrate to temperatures T=1.6, 1.7, 3.0, 6.0. Run microcanonical (NVE) MD with velocity Verlet for 4.8×10⁶ steps at each temperature, with 5 independent runs. Record pressure tensor data, positions, and velocities.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/md_evidence.txt`

### Step 4: Static structure factors and isothermal compressibility
- Role: scored
- Action: From MD trajectories, compute partial structure factors S_αβ(q), form S_nn(q) and S_cc(q). Extrapolate S_nn(q→0) to obtain κ_T for each T. Combine with χ values from MC fluctuations. Write T, κ_T, χ to static_properties.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/static_properties.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Columns: T (float), kappa_T (float), chi (float). All values in Lennard-Jones reduced units.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 5: Green-Kubo shear and bulk viscosities
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From pressure tensor autocorrelation functions computed from MD trajectories, determine plateau values of the Green-Kubo integrals for shear viscosity η_s and bulk viscosity η_B at each temperature. Calculate the ratio η_B/η_s. Write T, η_s, η_B, η_B/η_s to viscosities.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/viscosities.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Columns: T (float), eta_s (float), eta_B (float), eta_B_eta_s_ratio (float). All in LJ reduced units.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 6: Mean-field extrapolation to spinodal temperature
- Role: process
- Action: Using χ(T) from static_properties.csv and η_B(T) from viscosities.csv, perform linear fits of 1/χ vs T and 1/η_B vs T to estimate the spinodal temperature T_s. Save fit parameters and the estimate.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/spinodal_fit.txt`
