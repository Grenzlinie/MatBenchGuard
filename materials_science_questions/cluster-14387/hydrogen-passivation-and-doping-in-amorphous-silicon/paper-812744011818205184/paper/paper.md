# Structure and dynamics of the $DX$ center in GaAs:Si

R. Jones
Department of Physics, University of Exeter, Exeter, EX44QL, United Kingdom

S. Öberg
Department of Mathematics, University of Luleå, Luleå, S95187, Sweden

(Received 19 March 1991; revised manuscript received 24 May 1991)

We have carried out local-density-functional cluster calculations on the $Si_{\mathrm{Ga}}{ }^{-}$defect in GaAs. We find that the distortion proposed by Chadi and Chang involving a large Si movement along $\langle 111\rangle$ breaking an Si-As bond has the same energy as a simple breathing distortion. The calculated local vibratory modes of the Chadi-Chang structure do not agree with those recently assigned to $D X$ from an infrared-absorption experiment. On the other hand, the calculated triplet mode due to the other structure above is in reasonable agreement with these observations. The effective charge of the second type of defect is about three times that of the first. It is proposed that both defects coexist with the Chadi-Chang one being almost infrared inactive.

Shallow donors and acceptors are produced in GaAs when Si replaces a $\mathrm{Ga}$, or less commonly, an As atom. The triplet vibrational modes of these defects are well known to lie at 384 and $299 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\left({ }^{28} \mathrm{Si}\right)$, respectively. ${ }^{1}$ It is now believed that the ionized $\mathrm{Si}$ donor (and other donors with shallow levels) can bind a pair of electrons ${ }^{2}$ and may undergo a large lattice distortion $^{3}$ which lowers the energy levels. This negatively charged defect is believed to be the $D X$ center. $^{4}$

The $D X$ center is characterized by a large $1.1-\mathrm{eV}$ difference between optical and thermal ionization energies and a persistent photoconductivity. $^{5}$ It is unstable in GaAs but can be stabilized by pressure or alloying with $\mathrm{Al}$ for it has been observed $^{6}$ in $\mathrm{Al}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}_{1-x} \mathrm{As}$ for $x>0.22$ and in GaAs under a hydrostatic pressure greater than $20 \mathrm{kbar} .^{7}$

Chadi and Chang $^{2}$ suggested that the ionized $\mathrm{Si}$ donor moves off-site when charged with an additional pair of electrons. They carried out local-density-functional (LDF) calculations on an 18-atom supercell containing a $\mathrm{Si}$ atom at a $\mathrm{Ga}$ site. They found the positively charged donor remained on-site and possesses a local triplet mode at $340 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, whereas the negatively charged defect underwent a large distortion involving a $1.17-\AA$ movement of the $\mathrm{Si}$ atom along $\langle 111\rangle$, away from one of its As neighbors. This left the $\mathrm{Si}$ atom almost coplanar with the surrounding three As neighbors. This distortion is nearly identical to that found in a similar model of the $E L 2$ defect $^{8,9}$ involving an $\mathrm{As}_{\mathrm{Ga}}$ antisite defect. The energy cost in creating a pair of $D X^{-}$and $D X^{+}$from the neutral donors was estimated to be $0.22 \mathrm{eV}$.

Although there is a great deal of circumstantial evidence for this model, ${ }^{4}$ there have been no experiments from which details of the structure of $D X$ could be deduced until the very recent localized-vibrational mode (LVM) spectroscopy reported by Wolk et $a l .{ }^{10}$ These authors generated $D X$ centers in Si-doped GaAs by the application of a hydrostatic pressure around $31 \mathrm{kbar}$. They found that in a sample containing compensating acceptors where the transformation of $\mathrm{Si}$ donors into $D X$ could not occur, the LVM due to the shallow donor increased to 404 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. In uncompensated material, at the same pressure, a new mode occurred at $395 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. This second peak was ascribed to $D X$. Hall effect studies suggested that this center was negatively charged. By performing experiments at several pressures, they inferred that at zero pressure, the local mode of the, now metastable, center would occur at $376 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. If the Chadi and Chang structure for $\mathrm{Si}_{\mathrm{Ga}}{ }^{-}$was the only one to exist, then it should yield a local mode at this frequency which is below that of $\mathrm{Si}_{\mathrm{Ga}}{ }^{+}$.

We have carried out $a b$ initio LDF cluster calculations on a 71-atom H-terminated cluster, $\mathrm{SiAs}_{16} \mathrm{Ga}_{18} \mathrm{H}_{36}$, containing $\mathrm{Si}$ at a $\mathrm{Ga}$ site. Details of the method have been given previously. ${ }^{11-13}$ This cluster is tetrahedral and charged with two electrons when dealing with the positively charged donor (leaving all bonding levels filled), and four electrons when dealing with $\mathrm{Si}_{\mathrm{Ga}}{ }^{-}$. An inner 17 atoms were relaxed. For the donor $\mathrm{Si}^{+}$, the $\mathrm{Si}$-As bond length was found to be $2.4 \AA$. We evaluated the second derivatives of the energy between the inner 5 atoms and used those to generate a dynamical matrix of the 71-atom cluster. Contributions to this matrix from other atoms was taken from a Musgrave-Pople potential derived previously. ${ }^{14,15}$ The triplet ${ }^{28} \mathrm{Si}$ modes were calculated to lie at 382,388 , and $389 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. The splitting is due to numerical errors associated with finding the double derivatives. The mean frequency, $386 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, is very close to that observed at $384 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. The agreement with experiment here is better than we normally find: for ${ }^{10} \mathrm{~B}_{\mathrm{As}}{ }^{2-}$ and ${ }^{12} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{As}}{ }^{-}$ we obtained, using the same cluster, 632 and $544 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, respectively, to be compared with observed modes at 628 and $582 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} .16$

We then displaced the $\mathrm{Si}$ atom away from one of the As atoms as in the Chadi and Chang model. The relaxed cluster possessed one long $\mathrm{Si}$-As bond at $3.36 \AA$ and three shorter bonds of length $2.33 \AA$. This is very similar to the Chadi-Chang structure of $D X$. The $\mathrm{Si}$ atom is almost coplanar with three of the As neighbors. Because of these shorter bonds, the structure would be expected to give a doublet $E$-vibratory mode higher than $384 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ together with a lower-lying singlet. Indeed, we found only the

doublet at $432\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and the singlet fell into the one- phonon spectrum of GaAs. The frequency of the $E$ mode is clearly at variance with observation. The effective charge $^{1}$ of the defect, which governs the intensity of the infrared absorption, is $0.63e$. This vibration frequency is close to the $448-\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ mode calculated for the passivated Si donor, $^{15}$ where the H atom at the antibonding site pulls Si away from its lattice site so that it takes up an almost planar configuration with three of its As neighbors, and leaves the remaining Si-As bond broken. The observed frequency in this case is $410\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (Ref. 17) again greater than that of the on-site defect.

Another distortion was then investigated: we displaced one of the As neighbors $1\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ away from Si while keeping Si on-site. This, when relaxed, yielded a structure of four equal Si-As bonds but each was longer, at $2.56\ \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, than in the shallow donor. The energy of this structure is 0.01 eV—insignificantly different from the Chadi and Chang one. The triplet modes lie at 352, 347, and 342 with a mean at $347\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in reasonable agreement with the in- ferred mode at $376\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. For $^{30}\mathrm{Si}$ we obtained a mean frequency of $337\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. The effective charge of this de- fect is $1.85e$.

In conclusion we have found the Chadi and Chang dis- tortion for $\mathrm{Si}_{\mathrm{Ga}}{}^{-}$ to be as stable as a simple outward breathing movement of the As neighbors. The modes of this structure are in reasonable agreement with observa- tions in contrast to those for the former. The closeness of their energies implies that comparable numbers of these defects would be present. The infrared absorption due to the Chadi-Chang defects would only be about 12% of the other and hence may not be seen in the infrared- absorption experiments. $^{10}$ We suggest that only the on- site $\mathrm{Si}_{\mathrm{Ga}}{}^{-}$ defect is infrared active, while the Chadi- Chang defect is responsible for the persistent photocon- ductivity.

In principle, the analysis of absorption intensities versus the compensation ratio $^{10}$ would reveal the relative densi- ties of the on-site and Chadi-Chang defects. However, the limited data available at present preclude a quantitative estimate although it suggests a dominance of the former.

We have assumed that the on-site defect is negatively charged, but another possibility is that it exists in the neu- tral charge state—either having a small effective $U$ or is stabilized by another defect. The effect of hydrostatic pressure is then to lower its energy level with respect to the band edge, increasing the localization of the wave function. This in turn leads to a small outward breathing distortion of the four As neighbors of Si. This defect would be responsible for the infrared absorption.

The suggestion of the presence of two types of defect, one with small and the other with large lattice relaxation,has been made previously. $^{18}$
