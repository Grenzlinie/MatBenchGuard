# Hydrogen Flip Defect Energetics and Effective Charges in Amorphous Silicon

## Problem background
Hydrogenated amorphous silicon (a-Si:H) degrades under prolonged illumination due to the Staebler–Wronski effect, which creates metastable dangling bonds. However, multiple experiments find that light‑induced structural changes involve far more atomic sites than the small density of dangling bonds, suggesting that rearrangements of bonded hydrogen may play a major role. One proposed mechanism is the “H‑flip” defect: a metastable configuration where a bonded hydrogen atom flips to the backside of its Si–H bond, forming a double‑well potential. This defect could explain the observed increase in infrared absorption and the small volume dilation. The present task is to construct an a‑Si:H model, generate H‑flip configurations, and compute their energetic, vibrational, and structural properties to test whether this picture is consistent.

## Approach
The computational strategy uses an atomistic model of a‑Si:H generated by a melt‑quench molecular dynamics protocol, with hydrogen content around 10 at.% present exclusively in monohydride (Si–H) groups. For every monohydride H atom in the cell, two structural configurations are compared:
- the normal tetrahedral geometry, and
- the “flipped” geometry in which the H atom is relocated to the backplane of the Si–H bond.
Both geometries are relaxed with an electronic‑structure method (tight‑binding or DFT). For each H site, you will compute:
- the energy difference ΔE = E(flipped) – E(normal),
- the dynamic effective charge e_d* for the Si–H stretch mode in both configurations, obtained from the slope of the dipole moment versus the Si–H bond length,
- the fractional volume change induced by the flip,
- the average Si bond angle at the host silicon site.
Additionally, for one representative H site you will map the energy barrier along a simple arc path that connects the normal and flipped configurations, using the nudged elastic band (NEB) method. The combination of these per‑site quantities and the barrier provides a comprehensive picture of the H‑flip defect’s energetics and influence on infrared activity.

## Reproduction target
Build a 60‑atom a‑Si:H cell with ~10 at.% hydrogen, using a standard melt‑quench MD procedure (e.g., with a tight‑binding potential or DFT). All hydrogen atoms must be in monohydride groups. For each monohydride H atom:
1. Relax the cell in the normal tetrahedral geometry and again in the flipped geometry (H moved to the backside of the Si–H bond).
2. Compute the energy difference ΔE (eV), the dynamic effective charges e_d* (units of e) for both configurations via finite‑difference dipole‑moment analysis of the Si–H stretch, the percentage volume change, and the average Si bond angle (degrees) at the host site.
3. Select one H site and compute the energy barrier (eV) between normal and flipped states along an arc path using the nudged elastic band method.
Output the per‑site data as a CSV file (h_flip_defect_data.csv) and the barrier result as a JSON file (energy_barrier.json) under /app/outputs. The CSV must contain at least six H sites with the columns H_site_id, delta_E_eV, e_d_star_original, e_d_star_defect, volume_change_percent, avg_bond_angle_deg. The JSON must contain the keys site_id, barrier_eV, and method.
