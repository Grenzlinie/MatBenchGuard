# Band structure and orbital character of CaSi2 polymorphs at Gamma point

## Problem background
Covalent networks formed by silicon atoms in silicides can adopt sp²-like bonding topologies, giving rise to electronic structures that are not adequately described by a simple π‑orbital picture. High‑pressure polymorphs of CaSi₂ — the AlB₂‑type (hexagonal) and ThSi₂‑type (tetragonal) structures — feature three‑dimensional frameworks of Si that host conduction bands with mixed orbital character. Besides ordinary σ‑bands, back‑bonding s‑like states and hybridization between Si p and Ca d orbitals are expected to play important roles. A precise first‑principles determination of the orbital composition of the lowest conduction bands is therefore needed to understand the nature of these states and to test whether simple chemical concepts (σ, π, back‑bond) hold for such networks. The present task reproduces the orbital character of the bottom ten conduction bands at the Γ point for both CaSi₂ polymorphs and for the corresponding pure Si networks obtained after removing Ca atoms. The crystal structures are defined as follows: (i) AlB₂‑type CaSi₂ — hexagonal, a = 3.86 Å, c = 5.19 Å; Ca at (0,0,0); Si at (1/3,2/3,1/2) and symmetry‑equivalent positions. (ii) ThSi₂‑type CaSi₂ — tetragonal, a = 4.27 Å, c = 14.26 Å; Ca at (0,0,0); Si at (0,0,z) with z ≈ 0.416, plus the body‑centred partner (0,1/2,1/4) generated by the I4₁/amd symmetry. Pure Si networks are created by removing all Ca atoms from these cells.

## Approach
Density‑functional theory in the local‑density approximation (LDA) using norm‑conserving pseudopotentials and a plane‑wave basis set is used to compute the self‑consistent ground‑state charge density of each structure. From the converged potential, eigenvalues and wavefunctions are obtained at the single k‑point Γ = (0,0,0). The wavefunctions are then projected onto atomic pseudo‑atomic orbitals (s, pₓ, p_y, p_z for Si, and additionally s, pₓ, p_y, p_z, and d‑orbitals for Ca) to yield normalised projection coefficients. Analysing the four systems — CaSi₂ in both AlB₂ and ThSi₂ structures, and the corresponding pure Si networks — allows the identification of Si‑derived bands and reveals the influence of Ca on the conduction bands, notably any π‑d hybridization.

## Reproduction target
Produce four JSON files, each covering the lowest 10 conduction bands at the Γ point, containing the band index, energy (eV), and normalised projection coefficients onto the relevant atomic orbitals. For the CaSi₂ systems, include projections onto Ca s, p, and d orbitals. The pure Si networks serve as a reference to highlight the modifications introduced by Ca. The verifier will perform a structural audit that checks the projection coefficients for consistency with well-defined orbital character groupings (e.g., sigma, pi, s-like) and hybridization between Si and Ca. The specific expected character assignments are not disclosed; the solver must compute physically accurate projections.

## How you are scored
A hidden verifier checks each output JSON file for structural compliance with the orbital‑character expectations. It verifies that the file conforms to the declared schema (band indices, fields, numeric values). Then, for each system, it groups the bands as specified (1–6, 7–8, 9–10) and computes average projection coefficients for the relevant orbitals. The verifier confirms that each group exhibits the expected dominant orbital character above predetermined thresholds (e.g., sufficiently high px+py for σ bands, high pz for π bands, high s for s‑like bands). For CaSi₂ files, it additionally identifies at least one band where both Si‑p and Ca‑d projections exceed a mixing threshold. No exact band energies or numerical gold values are compared; the scoring is purely structural. Each of the four scored artifacts contributes a fraction of the total reward, and the final score is a weighted average in the range [0,1]. Reporting the paper’s numbers without genuinely computing the orbitals does not satisfy the check.
