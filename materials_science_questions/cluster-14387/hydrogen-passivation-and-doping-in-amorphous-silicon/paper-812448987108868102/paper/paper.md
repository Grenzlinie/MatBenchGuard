# Theoretical study of the electronic structure of the dimer-adatom-stacking-fault model for the Si(111) surface

Mari Fujita
Department of Material Physics, Faculty of Engineering Science, Osaka University, Toyonaka, Osaka 560, Japan

Hideo Nagayoshi
Department of Physics, Faculty of Science, Kagoshima University, Kagoshima 890, Japan

and

Akio Yoshimori $^{1}$
Department of Material Physics, Faculty of Engineering Science, Osaka University, Toyonaka, Osaka 560, Japan

Received 2 May 1991; accepted for publication 26 June 1991

The electronic structure of the Si(111)$7\times7$ dimer-adatom-stacking-fault (DAS) model has been calculated in the slab geometry, using the local density approximation (LDA) and the norm-conserving pseudopotential. Surface states and resonance states are obtained in the whole energy range for valence electrons. The dependence of one-electron energies on the cut-off values in the plane wave expansion is examined for the $1\times1$ truncated surface, and the result is utilized for the interpretation of the calculated result of the $7\times7$ surface. The obtained results for the $7\times7$ DAS model are found to agree well with the experimental ones for the $7\times7$ reconstructed surface.

## 1. Introduction

The $7\times7$ structure after annealing of the Si(111) surface was observed by the low-energy electron diffraction (LEED) in 1959 for the first time [1]. Takayanagi, Tanishiro, Takahashi and Takashi presented the dimer-adatom-stacking-fault (DAS) model for the atomic configuration of this surface in 1985 [2]. This model is now widely believed and many studies have been made on this model. The stick-and-ball picture for this model leads to three kinds of dangling bonds (DB) at the "adatoms" in the topmost layer, at the "rest atoms" in the first layer which are not bonded to the adatoms, and at the "corner hole atoms" in the third layer.

Determination of the detailed atomic positions in this model has been attempted experimentally [3,4] and theoretically [5-9]. Calculations have been so far made for the total energy in a semi-empirical scheme [5,6], or in combined methods of a semi-empirical scheme and the total energies of various component units of the DAS model calculated separately in the local density approximation (LDA) [7-9], because the $7\times7$ unit cell is too large for the ab initio scheme.

The atomic configuration being given, the investigation of the electronic property also progressed rapidly. The electronic structure of the $7\times7$ DAS model, which was expected from the

calculated results for simplified versions of the DAS model [10-12], identified the character of the peaks in the observed photoemission spectra [13,14]. It also proved to be consistent with the information obtained by scanning tunneling spec- troscopy (STS) experiments [15,16]. But a consid- erable discrepancy with experiments was found in the theoretical results for the simplified versions as to the relative energies among the three kinds of DB states. Furthermore the asymmetry of the adatom DB states just below the Fermi level $(E_{F})$ has been observed in the STS experiment [15], that is, the intensity in the faulted part of the unit cell is larger than that in the unfaulted part. This asymmetry was not clearly obtained in the calcu- lated results for the adatom DB states.

Thus the calculation for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model from first principles seemed to be needed to explain the unsolved problems described above. Some results of such a calculation were reported in a previous paper and was found to agree with the experimental information much better than those for the simplified models [17].

This paper presents two kinds of results. Thefirst is some more detailed result for the $7 \times 7$  DAS model supplementing one in the previous paper for states around $E_{F}$ [17]. The second is a investigation related to accuracy of the calcula- tion. We use plane-wave bases for the expansion of the one-electron wavefunctions and electron densities. Their wavevectors are truncated at cer- tain cut-off values. The cut-off values in the pre- sent calculation for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model and also in our previous ones for the simplified mod- els are too small to give complete convergence of the one-electron energies, although we believe that they are large enough to discuss the charac- ters of surface states and surface resonance states.The cut-off value dependence of the one-electron energies is examined in this paper for the $1 \times 1$  truncated surface.

Then the result for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model is compared with the experimental information, the cut-off value dependence of the one-electron en- ergies being taken into consideration. Finally we discuss all the results we have obtained in this and the previous three papers for the several models for the Si(111) surface [11,12,17]. The surface states and resonance states around $E_{F}$ ,that is, the three kinds of DB states, in each model are compared in the tight-binding (TB) picture.

## 2. Calculations

Models and methods of the calculation for the7x7 DAS model are described in the following, though some detail has already been given in the previous paper [17]. The norm-conserving pseu- dopotential of Bachelet et al. [19] is employed for Si4+ ion cores, and the LDA is carried out withthe density functional of Ceperley and Alder [18] for the exchange and correlation term. The re- peated slab geometry is used with the standard plane-wave representation, and each slab con- tains 16 Si layers and adatom layers on both surfaces of the slab. The plane-wave expansions are truncated at a wavenumber $q_{w_{2}}=2.3 a_{B}^{-1}$ for the wavefunctions, and at $q_{v}=3.1 a_{B}^{-1}$ for the electron densities. The plane waves for the wave-functions with a wavenumber larger than $q_{w_{1}}=$ 1.48ag' are treated perturbationally. The elec- tron densities are constructed in the use of one special point [20] for the $k$ -integration. Fortu nately, the Hamiltonian matrix for this $k$ can be divided into two small ones with the aid of the group theory and then we can actually solve the eigenvalue problems.

The calculations for the $1 \times 1$ surface are alsoperformed in a similar scheme. The slab with 16 Si layers is used, and there are 16 Si atoms in a super unit cell. The surface is assumed to be the truncated and unrelaxed one so that the unit cell has one DB on each surface. The six special points are adopted for the $k$ -integration. To ex amine the dependence of the one-electron ener- gies on the cut-off values $q_{w_{1}}, q_{w_{2}}$ and $q_{v}$ in the plane wave expansion described above, we made calculations for the next three sets of the cut-offvalues (in the $a_{B}^{-1}$ unit):

(1) $q_{w_{1}}=3.2, q_{w_{2}}=3.2, q_{v}=4.0$;
(2) $q_{w_{1}}=2.3, q_{w_{2}}=2.3, q_{v}=4.0$;
(3) $q_{w_{1}}=1.48, q_{w_{2}}=2.3, q_{v}=3.1$.

Comparing the results for (1) and (2), we can examine the dependence on the cut-off value for

the wavefunctions without the perturbation. We can examine an additional effect of the perturbation and of the cut-off value for the electron densities from the result for (3) in comparison with those for (1) and (2). We note that the parameter set (3) is just the same as in the calculation for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model, and that the cut-off values in the case (1) are large enough for the one-electron energies to converge in the energy region we are interested in.

## 3. Results

At first we describe the result for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model supplementing the one already published [17] mainly for the states around $E_{\mathrm{F}}$. We show in fig. 1 the obtained densities of states (DOS) and surface densities of states (SDOS) in the energy region for all the valence electrons. The SDOS are obtained by integrating the local densities of states (LDOS) in the region outside from the center of the third layer of the truncated surface. We also show the ratio of the SDOS to the DOS at each energy in fig. 1. The value 0.32 indicated by the dotted line is the ratio of the electron in the surface region, calculating by providing two electrons to each bond and one to each dangling bond in the stick and ball picture. The characteristic energy regions related to a large value of SDOS are labeled as (a)-(d). The contour maps of the absolute square of the typical wavefunctions at the special point in each energy region are shown in fig. 2. The labels (a)-(d) in fig. 2 correspond to those in fig. 1.

The surface states are obtained both in the bulk band gap (a) and below the bottom of the bulk valence band (d). The region (a) is about 0.4 eV in width and $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ passes through the lower-energy part of this region. The region (a) contains 24 branches of energy bands and the character of the wavefunctions is the adatom DBs as shown in the panel (a) in fig. 2. This number agrees with that of the adatoms in the unit cell (12 per each surface). The wavefunction in the panel (a) in fig. 2 have the energy value $-0.09$ eV, as shown in fig. 2, and corresponds to the state in the lowest-energy part of the region (a). This wavefunction has the largest amplitude at the adatom site represented by the symbol A(F)c, which means the adatom around the corner hole in the faulted part of the unit cell, among the four kinds of adatom sites. But there are the states at different energies in the region (a), for which the wavefunctions has the largest amplitude at each site of other three kinds of adatoms. The region (d) also consists of 24 branches, split off from the bottom of the bulk valence band, and their wavefunctions have a character of the bonds between the adatoms and the second layer atoms just below. The width of the region (d) is about 0.6 eV.

The regions (b) and (c) contain surface reso- nance states. The region (b) just at the top of the bulk valence band (VM) has a character of the mixture of the DBs of the rest atoms and the corner hole atoms. Its width is about 0.2 eV and narrower than the previous two surface states. The dimer bond states as well as the adatom back-bonds emerge in the region (c) around -2.0 eV, as shown in fig. 2. The other energy regions where the ratio SDOS/DOS is large include vari- ous second-third layer atom bonds.

### 4. Discussion

We begin with a discussion on the cut-off value dependence of the one-electron energies. We found from the calculations for the $1\times1$ surface that the dependence of the one-electron energies around $E_{\text{F}}$ on the cut-off values in the plane wave expansions is as follows:
(I) The perturbational treatment is good.
(II) The cut-off value $3.1a_{\text{B}}^{-1}$ for the expansion of the electron density is large enough.
(III) The increase in the cut-off value for the expansion of the wavefunction leads to
(i) the rigid upward shift of the bulk valence band by 0.2 eV,
(ii) the rigid downward shift of the bulk con- duction band by 0.3 eV,
(iii) the extension of the energy scale of the surface states around $E_{\text{F}}$ by about 1.5 times, and
(iv) the change in the order of the energies

The calculation for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model was carried out for the same cut-off values as the parameter set (3) in section 2. We may estimate the converged one-electron energies in the $7 \times 7$ DAS model for the large cut-off value set (1) from the cut-off value dependence (I)-(III) in the $1 \times 1$ surface.

Next let us compare the theoretical result for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model with experimental ones. According to the angle-resolved ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (ARUPS) [13], ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) and inverse photoemission spectroscopy (IPS) [14] experiments, there are one surface peak at $-0.2$ eV relative to $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ as a surface state in the bulk band gap, and two peaks at $-0.8$ to $-0.9$ eV and $-2.0$ eV as surface resonance states in the bulk valence band, and also one surface peak at $0.5$ eV above $E_{\mathrm{F}}$.

This is compared with the calculated SDOS. One can think that the main peak above $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ in the region (a) in fig. 1 corresponds to the sharp peak at $0.5$ eV in the IPS experiment, and that the small subpeaks around $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ form a broad hill structure which is observed at $-0.2$ eV in the experiments. The peak at $-0.4$ eV in the theory may be assigned to that at $-0.8$ eV in the experiments. Thus the energies of these peaks in the theory must be doubled for quantitative agreement with the experimental results.

Here we think over the cut-off value dependence of the one-electron energies for the $1 \times 1$ surface. One may safely believe that the three peaks around $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ behave in the similar way to the surface states of the $1 \times 1$ surface. If calculations were done for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model with a set of the large cut-off values, the separation of the three peaks would be increased as described in (III-iii), and so better quantitative agreement might be obtained. For these localized states, the self-energy effect over the LDA [21] would be also in favor of the agreement of the theory and the experiment. The peak at $-2.0$ eV in the experiment provably corresponds to the weak resonance peak in the region (c) in the SDOS. Its energy $-2.0$ eV may shift up like the bulk valence states of the $1 \times 1$ surface as described in (III-i), but it has been found that the self-energy effect conversely shifts down by almost the same energy [21], and so the energy value in our calculation is close to the experimental one. In this way the number of the surface peaks and their energies obtained from our LDA calculation are consistent with the experimental ones.

The characters of the states in the regions (a) and (b) are the adatom DBs and the DBs of the rest atoms and the corner hole atoms, respectively. This is consistent with the STS experiments [15,16], as was described also in the previous paper [17]. The DB states of the rest atoms and the corner hole atoms in the region (b) are located at VM as the resonance states, and there is a definite gap between these states and the adatom DB states in the region (a). These states were found to overlap the lower part of the adatom DB states in the simplified versions of the DAS model [11,12]. The present result gives better agreement with the experimental results than the previous ones for the simplified models.

It was found by Northrup [10] that the adatom back-bond states are located at about $-2.0$ eV. We found that the dimer bond states exist in the region (c) around $-2.0$ eV as well as the adatom back-bond states. We also found that there are the adatom-second layer atom bond states as surface states in the region (d) below the bottom of the bulk valence band. These states show the concentration of the electron density to the location surrounded by five atoms; i.e., the adatom, the three first-layer atoms and the second-layer atom just below the adatom. Existence of these states may represent the stability of the adatom and dimer structures in the DAS model, though it is not directly associated with the total energy gains due to these formations. But both of the dimer bond states and the surface states split off from the bottom of the bulk valence band have not been observed in experiments.

Next we present a theoretical comparative study of the results for the several models on the Si(111) surface we have obtained [11,12,17]. We have calculated for four kinds of periodicities, $2 \times 2$, $3 \times 3$ [11], $5 \times 5$ [12] and $7 \times 7$ [17], with

the LDA and the norm-conserving pseudopoten- tial method. These models are summarized in table 1. The $7 \times 7$ model is the DAS structure presented by the Takayanagi et al. [2]. The $3 \times 3$ and $5 \times 5$ models are also the DAS one, which can be constructed from the original $7 \times 7$ DAS model. The $5 \times 5$ DAS model have all the charac teristic structures of the DAS model, but the rest atom is missing in the $3 \times 3$ DAS model. The two $2 \times 2$ models are the periodic structures based on the local $2 \times 2$ ones in the unfaulted and faulted halves of the unit cell of the DAS model and then the corner hole is missing.

We discuss the surface states and resonance states around $E_{F}$. They correspond to the three kinds of the DB states in the TB picture. Their level structure obtained from our calculations are schematically summarized in fig. 4.

At first we mention the adatom DB states. In the $2 \times 2$ and $3 \times 3$ models, adatoms form a trian gle lattice and a honeycomb structure, and the distances between the neighboring adatoms are $2a$ and $\sqrt{3}a$, respectively, where $a$ is the lattice constant of the (111) truncated surface. The cal- culated energy bands of the adatom DB states for these two models are well fitted to the TB expres- sion with an effective transfer integral between the DBs of the nearest neighbor adatoms in each model. Its value is $J_{1}=0.21$ eV for the $3 \times 3$, and $J_{2}=0.03$ eV for the $2 \times 2$ model. The effective transfer integral $J_{1}$ holding the dimer structure in between for the $3 \times 3$ model is much larger than $J_{2}$. In the $5 \times 5$ model, the large effective transfer integral $J_{1}$ through the dimer structure couple the adatoms to form one hexagon per unit cell, and may roughly determine the electronic structure related to the adatom DBs, producing a four-level structure as shown in fig. 4. In the $7 \times 7$ model, the coupling of adatoms by $J_{1}$ pro duces are hexagon around the corner hole and three dimers per unit cell, and the resultant en- ergy levels are shown in fig. 4. The total width of the adatom DB states is about $4J_{1}$ for the $5 \times 5$ and $7 \times 7$ models. The calculated electronic structure related to the adatom DBs in these two models can be almost explained in this picture, although $J_{1}$ is to be about 0.1 eV which is much smaller than that in the $3 \times 3$ model.

It may also contribute to the total energy study in the combi- nation of various component units of the DAS model, which is so far based only on the numbers of them, assuming to have the constant energy independent of the size of the unit cell.

Finally we describe the asymmetry of the adatom DB states just below $E_{\mathrm{F}}$, which correspond to the state shown in the panel (a) in fig. 2 in our calculation. The two kinds of asymmetry have been obtained in the STS experiment. The intensity at the adatom sites around the corner hole is larger than that at the others [15,16], and the intensity of the faulted part is larger than that in the unfaulted part [15]. As shown in fig. 4, the lowest level of the adatom DB states consists only of the adatom DBs around the corner hole in the $7 \times 7$ model. This explains the former kind of the asymmetry which is in fact observed in the panel (a) in fig. 2. The latter asymmetry between the faulted and unfaulted parts is also observed in this panel for the $7 \times 7$ model. But it does not exist in the $3 \times 3$ model [11] and does exist only a little in the $5 \times 5$ model [12]. One can think that the degree of this kind of asymmetry depends on the size of the unit cell, in other words on the area of the faulted region.

In conclusion we have obtained the best agreement between the electronic property measured for the $Si(111) 7 \times 7$ surface and the electronic structure calculated for the $7 \times 7$ DAS model. The agreement of the detailed local structure may be a theoretical rigorous evidence that the DAS model is correct for the atomic configuration on the $Si(111) 7 \times 7$ surface.
