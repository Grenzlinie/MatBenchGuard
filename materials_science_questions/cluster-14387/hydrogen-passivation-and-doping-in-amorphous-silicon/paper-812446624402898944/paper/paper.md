# ROLE OF HYDROGEN IN DOPING MECHANISM FOR $a$-Si:H ALLOYS

Rui-qin Zhang and Guo-cai Dai

Department of Physics, Shandong University, Jinan, China
and
Zheng-ting Cai and Da-ren Guan

Institute of Theoretical Chemistry, Shandong University, Jinan, China

(Received 11 November 1987 by J. Kanamori)

Calculations of electronic energies for structural clusters simulating phosphorus and boron substitutional doped and undoped pure and hydrogenated silicon have been performed using CNDO/2 quantum chemical molecular orbital method, respectively. The results show that more energies for implanting impurities are required in hydrogenated Si cluster than that in pure Si cluster, and a band of shallow gap states is induced by the substitutional doping in the hydrogenated system. From the results it is concluded that the hydrogenation is a key factor of efficient substitutional doping in the $a$-Si:H alloys.

SINCE efficient doping was performed in $a$-Si:H samples by Spear *et al.* [1], the hydrogenated $a$-Si:H alloys have been widely applied to make solar cell and other electrical and optical devices, but $a$-Si materials have not. It is clear that hydrogen plays an important role in the doping mechanism for $a$-Si:H. Although several tentative models of doping mechanism have been proposed [2–4], with present view points, an appropriate explanation of some experimental events cannot yet be given [5]. Therefore, it is of great significance to make a detailed theoretical study of the doping mechanism in $a$-Si:H alloys.

A cluster-quantum chemical method, which has successfully been applied to some fundamental studies of amorphous semiconductors [6, 7], is adopted to investigate the role of hydrogen in doping mechanism for complicated $a$-Si:H alloys here. Some doped and undoped pure and hydrogenated Si cluster models have been built. Calculated MO energy levels diagram and total energies for these models have been given. A new view point of doping mechanism in $a$-Si:H alloys has been proposed.

In order to investigate the results among microstructure, gap states and doping, which, in our view, determined by the nature of the electronic cloud distribution, simple Si17 and Si17H36 cluster models were presented. And the disorder of crystalline lattice has been neglected. This treatment should reveal the role of hydrogen quantitatively.

The Si17 cluster model (see Fig. 1) was built in the following manner: first, one silicon atom is laid on center; secondly, four first nearest neighbour silicon atoms were combined with it according to the tetrahedral coordination. 12 silicon atoms as the secondary nearest neighbours were combined with above atoms in the same manner. Then 36 hydrogen atoms will participate in hydrogenating. Thus, the hydrogenating might taken place at the 36 dangling bonds of Si17, and the Si17H36 cluster can been obtained, in which the distance of Si–Si and Si–H are equal to 2.35 and $1.48\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, respectively. By the way, the hydrogen atoms used here were no longer the saturators for boundary condition of cluster model, they are essential for simulating $a$-Si:H alloys.

A semiempirical quantum chemical method CNDO and the parameters of CNDO/2 scheme were described in detail by Pople and Beveridge [8].

## MO ENERGY LEVEL DIAGRAMS

Phosphorus and boron atoms was substituted into the Si17 and Si17H36 clusters (suppose that the substituting take place at two first nearest neighbours of center Si atom), respectively, thus Si15P2, Si15B2, Si15P2H36 and Si15B2H36 clusters were constructed. Calculated results of MO energy levels for these clus- ters were shown in Fig. 2(a)-(f), where V, C and B represent the valence, conduction and boundary band regions and $D$ the doping states, respectively. It is shown from Fig. 2(a) that the dangling bond states of the non-hydrogenated Si17 model were split in B re- gion. Because the dangling bonds disrotate in pairs to form bend bonds, the total energy of the cluster may have been minimized. The result of the Si17H36 (see Fig. 2(b)) shows that these boundary dangling bond states are going into the bands for hydrogenating. Moreover, the result of Si15P2 (see Fig. 2(c)) shows that the boundary states distribution only has a little change for the implanting of the impurity $P$ atoms, and any doping state have been not induced. It is shown from the result of Si15P2H36 (see Fig. 2(d)) that not only the boundary states disappear for hydro- genating, but also the doping energy levels appear below the bottom of the conduction band. Whereas, the results of Si15B2 and Si15B2H36 (see Fig. 2(e)-(f)) were in the likeness of that of Si15P2 and Si15P2H36, respectively, but the doping levels of borons appear above the top of valence band in Si15B2H36 rather than below the conduction band in the former. Above- mentioned results indicate that when the $a$ -Si was hydrogenated into $a$ -Si:H, the doping levels of phos phorus and boron atoms were moved into gap from the bands. So the analogous efficient doping feature in crystalline silicon was shown in $a$ -Si:H. The explana tion of these results should be as follows: in the Si17 cluster, the unsaturated boundary bonds can disrotate in pairs and bend, the four valence bonds near silicon atom may deviate from regular tetrahedral symmetry. As a result the implanted impurity atoms have a chance of bonding with silicon atoms round it in the way of maximum overlaping of electronic cloud, then the impurity levels will go into bands. It can be sup- posed that the electronic cloud distribution in Si17 is as no "rigid" as in hydrogenated Si17H36 cluster, which approach to the one in crystalline silicon with disappearing of the boundary bond states. If the im- purity atoms are bonded in this environment, the impurity energy levels should appear in the gap. Hence, the phosphorus and boron atoms can play the role of donors and acceptors, respectively, but so do not in the pure Si cluster.

## DOPING ENERGIES

In order to examine effects of hydrogenation on the doping energy, the total energies $E$ for doped and undoped Si17 and Si17H36 clusters have been calculated. Then, the difference of energy ($\Delta E$) due to doping, and the change of doping energy ($\Delta EE$) caused from the hydrogenating have been obtained. The $\Delta E$ and $\Delta EE$ are given respectively as follows,

$$\Delta E1\ =\ E(\text{Si15X2})\ -\ E(\text{Si17}), \tag{1}$$

$$\Delta E2\ =\ E(\text{Si15X2H36})\ -\ E(\text{Si17H36}), \tag{2}$$

$$\Delta EE\ =\ \Delta E2\ -\ \Delta E1, \tag{3}$$

where the $X$ represents a phosphorus or boron impurity atom. The calculated results are listed in Table 1. It can be seen from Table 1 that the doping energy in the Si17H36 cluster is higher than that in Si17 cluster. This means that the electronic clouds in Si17 cluster lost their "rigid" tetrahedral symmetry feature, and it is helpful to the bonding of phosphorus and boron doping atoms. Hence, the doping energy in $a$-Si is lower than that in $a$-Si:H. This further supports the explanation of MO energy levels diagrams mentioned above.

The results from Si17 and Si17H36 only gave us knowledge of doping properties near out-surface-like environment in $a$-Si and $a$-Si:H. In order to simulate the doping properties near inner-surface-like environment, Si46 and Si46H60H4 cluster models were also used to study the doping, the former cluster model has a vacancy in center; and the latter has a hydrogenated vacancy in the center. The same role of hydrogen in doping have been also obtained using those clusters. (For simplify, calculated results have not given here.)

From present results and discussions, we can arrive at a conclusion as follows. (1) the electronic cloud distributions in $a$-Si:H prefer a "rigid" tetrahedral symmetry possessed by crystalline silicon. The hydrogenation may not only clean the dangling bond states in the gap of $a$-Si, but also make the doping states of phosphorus or boron go back into the gap from the bands. Thus, the $a$-Si:H material shows an efficient substitutional doping property. (2) It is easier to implant the impurity (need less energy) in $a$-Si, but the impurity atoms do not show the efficient doping property. It is harder to implant the impurity in $a$-Si:H (require energy than in $a$-Si), but the impurity atoms show efficient donor (for phosphorus) or acceptor (for boron) properties.

Finally, it should be noted that our theoretical results are consistent with that of PNMR experiments [9] on doped films, from which it is concluded that the fourfold coordinated P atoms are located in clusters bonded H atoms. This evidently occurs at internal hydrogenated surfaces, providing strong evidence connecting doping with a clustered hydrogen related micro-structure.
