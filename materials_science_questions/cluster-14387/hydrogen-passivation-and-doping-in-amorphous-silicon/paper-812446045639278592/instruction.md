# Tight-binding electronic structure and optical response of hydrogen-terminated silicon quantum wires

## Problem background
Silicon quantum wires are model systems for understanding the optoelectronic properties of porous silicon. Their electronic states and optical response are determined by quantum confinement, which alters the band gap and dielectric function. An efficient empirical tight‑binding method in the three‑center representation can describe these properties over a wide energy range with high spectral resolution. The task is to implement this method for hydrogen‑terminated Si wires and compute the density of states (DOS) and the imaginary part of the dielectric function (ε₂).

## Approach
The calculation starts from a bulk Si three‑center tight‑binding parametrization that accurately reproduces the diamond‑structure band gap and optical properties. For the quantum wires a periodic supercell is constructed with the wire axis along a crystallographic direction and with perpendicular cell vectors oriented along $[110]$ and $[1\overline{1}0]$. The lateral cell dimensions are chosen large enough (twice the wire side) to decouple periodic images. Hydrogen termination is modeled by adding on‑site energies and coupling elements that remove dangling‑bond states from the gap region without strongly mixing with the wire's band states. Electronic eigenvalues and wavefunctions are obtained on a uniform $k$‑point mesh, and the DOS is generated by Gaussian broadening. The dielectric function is computed from momentum matrix elements via the linear tetrahedral method, for polarizations parallel and perpendicular to the wire axis. The workflow yields the DOS and $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ for two wire sizes, revealing the one‑dimensional character of the electronic states and the evolution of the optical spectra with confinement.

## Reproduction target
Produce the density of states (DOS) and the imaginary part of the dielectric function ($\varepsilon_2$) for H‑terminated Si quantum wires with square cross‑sections of 4×4 and 8×8 (where the designation refers to the number of Si atoms per edge). For each wire size, the DOS should exhibit sharp one‑dimensional features and the dielectric function should show the main peak position, shape, and splitting for both parallel and perpendicular polarizations. The results will be evaluated by comparing the extracted band gaps and the positions/splitting of spectral features against hidden reference values, and by verifying the structural signatures of quantum confinement.
