# Optical properties of H terminated Si quantum wires

H.M. Polatoglou

Physics Department, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, GR-54006 Thessaloniki, Greece

Recent work on Si-based materials, like Si/Ge superlattices, has shown that it is possible to obtain accurate electronic and optical properties using the empirical tight-binding method in the three-center representation. In the present study we extend the method for the case of Si quantum wires and investigate in detail their properties. Si quantum wires are believed to model the physics of porous Si. The interaction of the bulk Si can be transferred to the case of Si quantum wires. In this way we obtain an upshift of the energy band gap for different Si quantum wires, which is in agreement with recent ab initio results. Detailed study of the dielectric function and of the density of states reveals the effects of confinement and the one-dimensional character of the electronic states.

The search for proper structures which can be integrated to the Si technology to yield optoelectronic devices has become of high priority. Using advanced molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) or related methods it is possible to fabricate microstructures and in this way to engineer the technologically interesting semiconductor properties [1,2]. Very recently a simple method has been demonstrated to produce Si microstructures, the so-called porous Si, capable of photoluminescence visible light [3-5]. Although the origin of photoluminescence is currently under debate, some experimental evidence and theoretical work strongly support the idea that these properties can be attributed to the creation of Si quantum wires near the surface by the preparation technique. Some attempts to model in detail porous Si, using state-of-art electronic structure calculations have been made. As a first attempt Sanders and Chang [6] calculated the band structure and optical properties within an empirical tight-binding framework. They found that Si quantum wires are direct gap semiconductors and that the excitonic oscillator decreases very fast with the increase of the wire diameter, although for wires with diameter of $\approx 8$ Å it is comparable to GaAs. The absorption coefficient is calculated by assuming parabolic bands around the gap. The absorption spectra are found to be quite anisotropic. Recently ab initio calculations were performed to study different aspects of the optical properties of Si quantum wires. More specifically, Read et al. [7] studied H terminated Si wires of various thicknesses and found that the band gap is direct and that its values are different compared to the simple effective mass theory. Also they estimated the radiative lifetime, which was found to be close to the experimental data. Placing more emphasis on the excitonic effects due to the geometrical restriction, Ohno et al. [8] proposed that the efficient photoluminescence is due to the direct band gap and the constriction of excitons. On the other side Buda et al. [9] have considered in addition to the band structure the dielectric function. They found a large broad peak which does not shift with the size of the quantum wire, and a small broad peak which is very sensitive to the confinement effects and which is situated at the low energy side. This side peak was found to originate from a particular state, having at least one order of magnitude larger transition probability than any other nearby state. The aim of the present work is to demonstrate
that it is possible to obtain a reliable electronic
structure of the H terminated Si wires using an
economical and efficient empirical tight-binding
method. Using this method we study the density of
states (DOS) and the dielectric function. These
properties are calculated for a wider energy range
and have higher resolution than in previous work.

The empirical tight-binding method utilizes the
well-known band structure and optical properties
of Si in the diamond structure. It is found that with
a proper choice of the three-center integrals a good
description can be obtained for the band structure
and the optical properties of bulk Si [11]. One
should note that the band gap is correct and that
the optical properties are as good as the best
known theoretical results. The problem of transfer-
ability has been tested by studying the band struc-
ture [11] and the optical properties [12] of Si/Ge
superlattices. There are some points to be cleared
concerning the application of this method to the
present problem. First comes the determination of
the interaction between Si and H. The Si–H bond is
very strong, the electrons are localized at the bond
and have a small probability to jump to neighbor-
ing bonds. Therefore it is not critical which on-site
matrix elements and which interaction between
Si–H we choose, as long as it is reasonable. The
main effect of H is to deplete the fundamental band
gap from the dangling bond states. From the pro-
jected density of states on the Si–H bonds we find
that the valence and the conduction electrons have
a very small interaction with the electrons forming
the Si–H bonds.

Having solved this question one has to check if
the three-center integrals determined from bulk Si
are transferable to the present case. Although there
are indications from the study of the Si/Ge super-
lattices that the integrals are transferable, we calcu-
late the band structure of various wires and com-
pare the results with known theoretical results. We
find that the band gap is equal to 3.29, 2.5 and 2.1
eV for wires with diameter 7.68, 11.52 and 15.36 Å,
respectively. The definition of the diameter is as in
Ref. [7]. We observe that the results compare
rather well with the previous ab initio results.

We use a periodic cell to describe the wire. While
the periodic cell is the right choice along the wire
axis, it is artificial perpendicular to it. But the
periodic boundary conditions have no effect as long
as the wires in the periodic array of wires do not
interact between themselves. It is very easy to es-
tablish this in the tight-binding formalism. One has
to choose the unit cell perpendicular to the wire
axis large enough to make sure that there are no
matrix elements coupling different wires. In the
present study we always choose the side size of the
unit cell to be twice the side size of the wire. Speak-
ing in density terms our cell has 1/4 the density of
bulk Si. The basis vectors perpendicular to the wire
axis are along the [110] and [$\bar{1}$10] directions of the
FCC lattice.

To obtain the DOS we sample the energies
through a uniform mesh of wave vectors and at
each sampled energy we place a Gaussian function
with $\sigma = 100$ meV. The main question we are about
to address is the effect of the one-dimensional char-
acter of the electronic states on the density of states.
Of course the density of states is an integral prop-
erty and the effect of dimensionality can be masked.
From the experience that has accumulated by
studying amorphous semiconductors, it is clear
that disorder has a different effect at different
bands, depending on the orbitals which contribute
to the states of a given band. For the case under
study we expect different behaviour for electrons in
the lower valence bands, originating mainly from
s orbitals, and the upper valence bands, mainly
from p orbitals. In Fig. 1 we present the results for
the two cases we studied. The notation we use for
the wires is that of Ref. [7]. It can be observed that
for the wider wires we approach the well-known
bulk Si DOS, both the valence band edge and the
conduction band edge shift by an almost equal
amount and the lowest valence bands change quite
a lot. The one-dimensional character of the elec-
tronic states can be shown by the presence of sharp
peaks embedded in the DOS. It has to be noted that
the number of sharp peaks decreases very fast as
the diameter of the wire increases.

In summary we have extended the empirical tight-binding method in the three-center represen- tation to the case of H terminated Si wires. It is found that the choice of the Si-H interaction inte- grals and H on-site energies are not critical with respect to the electronic structure and the optical properties. The DOS shows clearly the one-dimen- sional character of the electronic states, and it is found that the lowest valence bands are more effected by the two-dimensional confinement. A complex behaviour is exhibited by the dielectric function with some sharp peaks close to the first direct gap of the wire.
