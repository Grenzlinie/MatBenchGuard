# Variational DFT Surface Energy and Work Function Calculation for Simple Metals

## Problem background
Surface energies and work functions of simple metals are fundamental to surface science, catalysis, and materials engineering. This work targets the computational determination of face-dependent surface energies and work functions for 12 simple metals using a variational density-functional theory (DFT) approach. The method employs a Kohn-Sham energy functional augmented with nonlocal exchange-correlation corrections and represents the ionic lattice with Ashcroft empty-core pseudopotentials. The trial electron densities are generated by the linear-potential model, which permits a semi-analytical evaluation of most energy components. The central task is to compute, via numerical minimization, the surface energies (both within the local-density approximation and with wave-vector nonlocal corrections) and the displaced-profile change-in-self-consistent-field (DPΔSCF) work functions for the low-index crystal faces of each metal.

## Approach
The calculation is built on a seven-component surface-energy functional comprising kinetic, electrostatic, LDA exchange-correlation, wave-vector nonlocal exchange-correlation correction, Ashcroft pseudopotential, classical cleavage, and short-range repulsive contributions. For each metal and crystal face, a one-parameter linear-potential-model trial density is used; the single variational parameter y_F (a dimensionless slope) is determined by minimizing the LDA surface energy via numerical optimization. Once the energy-minimizing density is found, the final surface energy is evaluated with the LDA functional and with the additional wave-vector correction added. The DPΔSCF work function is then computed from the same density: it combines an electrostatic barrier, a bulk kinetic-plus-exchange-correlation term, and a pseudopotential contribution that involves the planar-averaged lattice perturbation. Most integrals in the surface-energy functional are analytic; only the pseudopotential and correlation-energy parts require numerical quadrature. The workflow covers all required metal–face combinations and is expected to be implemented from scratch, using the publicly available Wigner-Seitz radii, Ashcroft core radii, and lattice constants that define each system.

## Reproduction target
Compute and report the face-dependent surface energies (LDA and wave-vector corrected) and DPΔSCF work functions for 12 simple metals: bcc Li, Ba, Na, K, Rb, Cs; fcc Al, Pb, Ca, Sr; and hcp Zn, Mg. For each bcc and fcc metal, evaluate the (110), (100), and (111) faces; for the hcp metals, evaluate the (0001) face. The calculations must use the seven-component energy functional, linear-potential model trial density, Ashcroft pseudopotentials, and the DPΔSCF formalism exactly as described in the workflow steps. The resulting surface energies (ergs/cm²) and work functions (eV) are to be written to the specified JSON output files. The evaluation objective is to produce numbers that match, within physically reasonable numerical tolerances, the reference computational values known from the literature, and to reproduce the correct ordering of surface energies with crystal-face packing density for the subset of metals where such a trend is expected.

## How you are scored
A hidden verifier will independently assess your output. The verifier reads the surface_energies.json and work_functions.json files and compares each numerical entry against hidden reference values derived from the published calculations. For surface energies, the comparison uses a tolerance that absorbs legitimate numerical differences between independent implementations; for work functions, a similar absolute tolerance is applied. Additionally, the verifier checks that for a specified subset of metals the corrected surface energy follows a monotonic trend with crystal-face packing density (i.e., the energy is strictly ordered according to the face index). The final score is a weighted combination of the fraction of numerically matched entries (both surface energies and work functions) plus a small bonus for satisfying the monotonic surface-energy ordering. No credit is given for simply listing the paper’s numbers; the verifier expects that the numbers arise from an honest re‑run of the variational procedure. The hidden tolerances and the exact weighting are not disclosed.
