# Formation energy and magnetic classification of oxygen vacancies on α-quartz surfaces

## Problem background
Magnetic flux noise limits the coherence of superconducting qubits and SQUIDs. Experiments suggest that magnetic defects on the silica substrate surface are a major noise source. This task investigates whether oxygen vacancy defects (oxygen deficiency centers) on α-quartz surfaces can host low-energy magnetic states (LEMS) by computing their formation energies and magnetic ground states from first principles. The target is to determine which vacancy configurations are magnetic and what their formation energies are under oxygen-rich conditions.

## Structural details (α‑quartz, slabs, and defect sites)
All structural models are built from α‑quartz.  The crystal structure of α‑quartz is hexagonal, space group `P3_2 21` (#154, left‑handed chirality).  The lattice parameters are:

- `a = 4.884 Å`, `c/a = 1.1023` (so `c ≈ 5.382 Å`).
- Fractional coordinates (one Si and one O in the asymmetric unit; the remaining atoms are generated by the symmetry operations of the space group):
  - Si: `(0.4658, 0, 0)`
  - O:  `(0.4120, 0.2740, 0.1141)`

Use these parameters to build an initial bulk α‑quartz cell and perform the convergence tests (Step 1).

### Surface slabs
Two slab orientations are required: **(001)** and **(100)**.  Construct each slab by cutting the bulk crystal perpendicular to the given direction, making sure that the stoichiometry is preserved.  For both slabs:

- Add a vacuum layer of at least **15 Å** in the direction normal to the surface.
- The in‑plane surface area per side should be roughly **100 Å²**; the reference used supercells of area ~100 Å²/side (e.g. 2×2 surface unit cells for the (001) surface).
- The thickness (number of Si layers) must be converged; the reference reports:
  - **(001) slab:** 8 Si layers (8 formula units)
  - **(100) slab:** 7 Si layers (20 formula units)

After building the slab, relax all atomic positions (the cell shape may change, but the total volume must be kept fixed) and compute the total energy and valence band maximum (`E_VBM`).  `E_VBM` is obtained as the energy of the highest occupied Kohn‑Sham state from the self‑consistent calculation of the stoichiometric slab.

### Oxygen vacancy sites
Six distinct oxygen sites are used to create vacancies.  After the slabs have been relaxed, identify the following sites:

**On the (001) surface – three sites:**
- **`site1`** – a surface oxygen atom that is **not** part of a three‑membered Si‑O ring.
- **`site2`** – a surface oxygen atom that **is** part of a three‑membered Si‑O ring (the honeycomb pattern of the (001) surface contains six‑membered rings; one of the surface oxygens belongs to a three‑membered ring, the other does not).
- **`subsurface`** – an oxygen atom located just below the surface (second layer), which is part of a three‑membered ring in the subsurface.

**On the (100) surface – three sites:**
- **`bridge`** – an oxygen atom that bridges the two‑Si‑membered rings along the surface chain; it connects two Si atoms that belong to two different 2Si rings.
- **`metastable ring`** – an oxygen atom that is part of a 2Si ring (the diamond‑shaped four‑atom rings visible on the (100) surface) and points **out of** the surface.  Its removal, followed by relaxation, gives a metastable puckered configuration.
- **`ring 2III-O`** – an oxygen atom that is part of a 2Si ring and points **into** the slab.  Its removal creates a configuration with two three‑coordinated Si atoms (2III‑O).

For each site you must consider charge states **0, +1, and +2**, giving a total of **18 vacancy‑charge combinations**.

## Approach
The reproduction uses density functional theory (DFT) with the local spin density approximation (LSDA). An open‑source DFT code (e.g., Quantum ESPRESSO or GPAW) is employed with standard pseudopotentials for Si and O.

First, bulk α‑quartz is optimized to establish the computational parameters (plane‑wave cutoff, k‑point grid). Stoichiometric surface slabs are then constructed for the (001) and (100) orientations, relaxed, and their total energies and valence band maxima (`E_VBM`) are obtained. The oxygen chemical potential `μ_O₂` is determined from a separate calculation of an isolated O₂ molecule in the triplet state.

Oxygen vacancies are created at the six distinct oxygen sites described above. For each site, vacancies in charge states 0, +1, and +2 are modeled. After relaxing each defective slab, the formation energy is computed using the standard defect formation energy expression:

```
E_form = E_defect − E_slab + 0.5·μ_O₂ + q·E_VBM
```

where `q` is the defect charge. The ground‑state magnetic moment is determined by comparing constrained‑magnetization calculations at 0, 1, and 2 μB. A defect is classified as a low‑energy magnetic state (**LEMS**) if its ground state has a non‑zero magnetic moment. The geometric relaxation pathway may produce different atomic configurations (Si–Si dimer, puckered configuration, 2III‑O), which must be recorded.

## How you are scored
A hidden verifier independently reads the submitted artifacts and compares each required quantity to reference values derived from the paper. The checks are:

- **slab_energies.csv** – The `E_VBM` values for (001) and (100) are compared against reference VBM values from the paper (tolerance ±0.01 eV). The `slab_total_energy` column must be present and contain numeric values, but its specific value is not checked against a gold standard.
- **mu_O2.txt** – The single μ_O₂ value is compared to the paper‑reported O₂ total energy (tolerance ±0.001 eV).
- **formation_energies.csv** – For all 18 entries, the `E_form` values are compared to paper‑reported formation energies (tolerance ±0.5 eV). The `is_LEMS` classification is compared to the expected magnetic/non‑magnetic label. The `geometry_type` must be one of the allowed strings: `"Si-Si dimer"`, `"puckered configuration"`, or `"2III-O"`. The `magnetic_moment_ground_state` must be a floating‑point number and is checked for consistency with `is_LEMS` (non‑zero magnetic moment when `is_LEMS` is true, and zero when false). No internal consistency check involving `E_defect` is performed because the agent does not submit raw defect energies.

The final score is a weighted sum of the per‑component scores (weights are defined in the grading specification). Hard‑coding numbers without a genuine DFT workflow will not produce the required internal consistency between outputs, and the verifier’s multiple independent checks will reveal discrepancies.
