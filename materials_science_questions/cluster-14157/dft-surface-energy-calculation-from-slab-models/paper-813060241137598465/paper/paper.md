## ABSTRACT

The structure and surface energy of a series of low-index surfaces of stoichiometric $\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$ (hematite) are investigated using the periodic Hartree-Fock approach with an *a posteriori* correction of the correlation energy. The simulations show that, amongst the modeled facets, (01$\overline{1}$2) and (0001) are the most stable surfaces of hematite, which is consistent with the fact that the latter are the dominant growth faces exposed on natural $\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$. The Fe-terminated (0001) surface is shown to exhibit a large relaxation of the surface atoms. It is argued that this arises mainly due to the fact that the surface cations are located opposite empty cation sites in the filled-filled-unfilled cation sequence along the $c$-axis. In contrast, the (01$\overline{1}$2) plane cuts the crystal through a plane of empty cation sites, thus giving rise to relatively small relaxations and surface energies. The small relaxations and concomitant exposure of five-coordinate cation sites may be important for the catalytic activity of hematite. The simulations also show that the relative stability of the investigated surfaces changes after a full lattice relaxation with the (0001) and (11$\overline{2}$6) facets relaxing disproportionately large. Wherever possible, the simulations are compared with previous simulation data and experimental results. A Wulff-Gibbs construction is also presented.

### 1. Introduction

Hematite ($\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$) is one of the thermodynamically most stable and most abundant oxides of iron in nature under ambient conditions [1]. The crystal structure of hematite is that of corundum with space group $\text{R}\overline{3}\text{C}$. It is occupied by two formula units in the primitive rhombohedral unit cell and six formula units in the hexagonal unit cell [2]. The Fe$^{3+}$ cations occupy two-thirds of the available octahedral sites in an approximately hexagonally close-packed O$^{2-}$ anion sublattice, leading to a filled-filled-unfilled sequence of the cation sites along the $c$-axis. There are unpaired electrons in the $3d$-subshell of hematite. A high-spin electron configuration was demonstrated experimentally [3]. Below the Néel temperature of 960 K, hematite is antiferromagnetic. Between the Néel and the Morin temperature of 260 K, hematite shows a weak ferromagnetism due to a slight canting of the magnetic moments [4].

There is currently significant interest in hematite both from a scientific and technological viewpoint. Hematite was investigated for applications as chemical or magnetic sensor [5,6], for spintronics [7], as an anode material for Lithium-ion batteries [8], for the photoelectrochemical splitting of water [9–11] and as a catalyst [12,13]. Due to the strong correlations amongst its localized $3d$ electrons, it places stringent requirements on the employed theoretical methods. Density-functional theory with a Hubbard-type on-site Coulomb repulsion (the DFT $+U$ method) has been used to study hematite [14–16]. Recently, Huang et al. [17] extended this approach with site-specific $U^{d}$ values and a Coulomb correction $U^{p}$ to describe better the hybridization between the Fe $d$ and O $p$ orbitals. Liao and Carter [18] employed a many-body Green's function method with a screened Coulomb interaction W, known as the GW approximation, to investigate low-lying excited electronic states in hematite. Alvarez-Ramírez et al. [19] used four different DFT approximation levels, namely the non-selfconsistent Harris functional, the local spin-density approximation (LDA), the generalized gradient approximation and hybrid functionals, to study the surface structural relaxation of (0001) hematite. Pozun and Henkelman [20] used hybrid DFT with 12% exact exchange at short range to reproduce the experimental band gap and other material properties of hematite. Wilson and Russo [21] carried out a hybrid density functional theory study of the high-pressure polymorphs of hematite. Very recently, Meng et al. [22] used three density functional approximations, namely PBE, PBE$+U$ and HSE, to investigate the structural, electronic, magnetic and thermodynamic properties of bulk $\alpha$-FeOOH, $\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$, Fe$_3$O$_4$ and FeO. These authors found that the Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof screened hybrid functional (HSE) containing 15% "screened" Hartree–Fock exchange can provide reliable values of lattice constants, Fe magnetic moments, band gaps and formation

The hydrated hematite surface has also been studied theoretically and experimentally [23-35] and strong ev- idence for the presence of multiple coordination environments for the surface hydroxyl functional groups was found. In a recent work [36] we demonstrated that also the Hartree-Fock (HF) method with an *a pos- teriori* calculation of the correlation energy is a useful tool to investi- gate the structural, electronic and magnetic properties of hematite. The present work aims at extending these HF studies by calculating the sur- face structure and energy of a whole range of stoichiometric hematite surfaces with low Miller-Bravais indices. The relative stability of the in- vestigated surfaces is then compared with the results of other theoretical approaches.

## 2. Computational details

All of the present calculations were performed with the computer code CRYSTAL14 [37], which is an *ab initio* LCAO (linear combination of atomic orbitals) Hartree-Fock program for the treatment of periodic sys- tems. The surface is described by a slab model with 2D periodicity and the unrestricted open-shell Hamiltonian is used for $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ [2,38]. Since there are unpaired $3d$ electrons in isolated $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ions, the use of the unre- stricted treatment of the spin-dependent part of the wavefunction in the open-shell systems is necessary to obtain spin-polarized eigenfunctions of the Fock Hamiltonian [39]. $\alpha\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ (hematite) has the corundum- type structure. The spin sequence on the Fe atoms along the three-fold symmetry axis (i.e. Fe-3O-Fe-Fe-3O-Fe) can exhibit four different or- ders, corresponding to one ferromagnetic $(+++++)$ and three antiferro- magnetic $(+-+-$, $++--$ and $+--+)$ configurations. Although the first antiferromagnetic phase $(+-+-)$ is the stable ground-state structure, the geometries of the latter and the ferromagnetic bulk phase are virtu- ally identical [2]. We therefore consider here the computationally less demanding ferromagnetic structure with four spin-up symmetry related iron atoms in the primitive cell (space group $\text{R}^3\text{C}$). The all-electron level basis sets and structural parameters were taken from Ref. [2]. The sur- faces we study here are the $\{0001\}$, $\{11\bar{2}0\}$, $\{10\bar{1}0\}$, $\{10\bar{1}2\}$, $\{01\bar{1}2\}$, $\{10\bar{1}1\}$ and $\{11\bar{2}6\}$ families. The repeat units (number of layers in the slab) are selected as Fe-3O-Fe... for $\{0001\}$ (21 layers), O-2O-4Fe-2O- O... for $\{11\bar{2}0\}$ (30 layers), 2O-4Fe2O-2O... for $\{10\bar{1}0\}$ (30 layers), 2Fe-2O-2O-2O-2Fe... for $\{10\bar{1}2\}$ (35 layers), 2O-2Fe-2O-2Fe-2O... for $\{01\bar{1}2\}$ (25 layers), O-Fe-O-Fe-O... for $\{10\bar{1}1\}$ (40 layers) and O-2Fe-2O-2O-2Fe-O... for $\{11\bar{2}6\}$ (36 layers), with surface terminations giv- ing rise to a minimal dipole moment [40], although different surface terminations may occur [33,41]. These numbers of layers were found to be sufficient for a convergence of the final results. In the course of the geometry optimization, we do not place any constraints on the atoms except for the conservation of the original crystal symmetry in the two dimensions parallel to the surface. The correlation and exchange en- ergy was estimated *a posteriori* via the Hartree-Fock density, i.e. only the initial HF simulation is variational and the *a posteriori* calculation is a single point simulation. Various hybrid functionals were employed in the *a posteriori* calculation, namely B3LYP, B3PW, PBE0 and M06. The fully relaxed positions of all atoms in the simulation cell for all consid- ered surfaces are provided in Section S1 of the Supplementary Material. We have also variationally relaxed the (0001) slab with the B3LYP hy- brid functional and have found the differences in the obtained atomic positions compared to the HF results almost negligible. The positions of all atoms in the simulation cell for the B3LYP-relaxed slab are provided in Section S2 of the Supplementary Material.

In the CRYSTAL code the level of numerical approximation in evalu- ating the Coulomb and exchange contributions to the Hartree-Fock ma- trix is controlled by five tolerances [37]. The values used in the present calculations are (7 7 7 7 14). The shrinking factors, which determine the number of sampling points in the irreducible Brillouin zone (IBZ), were chosen as (6 6 6) and (6 6) for the bulk and slab, respectively. As a result, there are 32 $k$-points in the bulk IBZ and 8 $k$-points in the slab IBZ. Other parameters for controlling the geometry optimization process are the same as in Ref. [36]. Checks with varying tolerances and num- bers of $k$-points showed the final results of the calculations to be robust. The surface energy at zero temperature is computed by $E_S=(E(n)-n E_{bulk})/(2A)$ following the description of Vitos [42], where $A$ is the area of the primitive surface unit cell, $E(n)$ is the energy of a $n$-unit layer slab, and $E_{bulk}$ is the energy of a unit layer of the bulk. We have checked this value of the surface energy against calculations using the methods de- scribed by Boettger [43] and Fiorentini and Methfessel [44], and have found agreement between these methods to within approximately 3%.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Surface relaxation

For the (0001) hematite slab we have recently shown [36] that the calculated changes in the top four interplanar spacings are $-56\%$, $+3\%$, $-47\%$ and $+20\%$ ($-58\%$, $+6\%$, $-45\%$ and $+18\%$ using the B3LYP hy- brid functional) in good agreement with recent low-energy electron diffraction (LEED) experiments [45]. Each Fe atom at the (0001) surface is sepa- rated by a distance of $1.945\mathring{A}$ from its three nearest O neighbors before relaxation. After relaxation the Fe-O distance decreases to $1.791\mathring{A}$ (i.e. a change of $-7.9\%$), the nearest O-O distance increases from 2.665 to $2.833\mathring{A}$ and the side length of the equilateral O triangle remains with $3.035\mathring{A}$ virtually unchanged. The computed values for fully relaxed bulk hematite are $1.960\mathring{A}$ for the nearest Fe-O distance, $2.648\mathring{A}$ for the near- est O-O distance and $3.056\mathring{A}$ for the side length of the equilateral O tri- angle. Using an analysis of coaxial impact-collision ion scattering spec- troscopy (CAICISS) data, Osaka et al. [46] obtained a shortening of the surface Fe-O bond by $6.5\%$, and side lengths of the equilateral O trian- gles of $3.04\mathring{A}$ for bulk and $3.13\mathring{A}$ for the relaxed Fe-layer termination of (0001) hematite. The corresponding three O atoms were found to ro- tate by $4^\circ$ clockwise with the nearest first-layer Fe atom as a rotational axis [46]. In our present simulations we find a rotation by $2.8^\circ$ anti- clockwise. These authors also obtained interlayer separations of 0.19, $1.06$, $0.27$ and $0.94\mathring{A}$ for the first four interplanar spacings [46], which compares reasonably well with our present results of $0.37$, $0.87$, $0.32$ and $1.01\mathring{A}$.

The $(11\bar{2}0)$ surface was represented by an oblique Bravais lattice in the plane with lattice constants $a=5.427\mathring{A}$ and $b=7.403\mathring{A}$ at an angle $\gamma=95.85^\circ$ before relaxation. After relaxation, these values are $a=5.437\mathring{A}$, $b=7.328\mathring{A}$ and $\gamma=95.71^\circ$. The structure of the slab with the $(11\bar{2}0)$ hematite surface is shown in Fig. 1 before and after relax- ation. As can be seen from this figure, the layers containing four Fe atoms split upon relaxation into two layers containing two Fe atomseach. However, the resulting interlayer spacings ($0.062$ and $0.079\mathring{A}$) are significantly smaller than the remaining distances. Marked changes in the layer structure can be observed up to and including the third and fourth $\text{Fe}_2$ layer. The magnitude of the relative relaxations of the various layers is with around $22\%$ quite similar, with the exception of one of the central $\text{O}_2$-O distances which exhibits an expansion by $39\%$. Further- more, the O atoms on either side of the Fe layers approach the Fe atoms noticeably, whilst the O atoms themselves tend to increase their separa- tion in general. The latter can be explained predominantly in terms of simple electrostatic interlayer interactions. The bottom two interlayer separations in Fig. 1 display essentially bulk behavior.

The $(10\bar{1}0)$ surface was modeled by a right-angled Bravais lat- tice in the plane with lattice constants $a=5.035\mathring{A}$ and $b=13.747\mathring{A}$ before relaxation. After relaxation, these values are $a=5.0355\mathring{A}$ and $b=13.776\mathring{A}$. The structure of the slab with the $(10\bar{1}0)$ hematite surface is shown in Fig. 2 before and after relaxation. As can be seen from this figure, the second layer which has initially six atoms in the primitive cell splits into three layers, each containing two atoms. With the exception of the surface O₂-Fe₂ interlayer spacing (1–2) predicted by Lo et al. [47], there is relatively good agreement between the different simulation methods. The (10$\overline{1}$1) surface was represented again by an oblique Bravais lattice in the plane with lattice constants $a=5.035\,\text{Å}$ and $b=5.4266\,\text{Å}$ at an angle $\gamma=117.64^\circ$ before relaxation. After relaxation, these values change by less than 1%. The (10$\overline{1}$1) hematite surface exhibits marked relaxations in the outermost ten layers. The changes in the top six interplanar spacings are 37%, −28%, −16%, −19%, 22% and −28%. After this follows an irregular sequence of compression and expansion. Below the ninth layer the spacings change by less than about 10% and below the eighteenth layer the structure displays essentially bulk behavior. The atomic sequence perpendicular to the surface is conserved. Denoting by $(N)$ the layer number starting at the surface with $N=1$, then sizeable vertical movements ($\geq0.1\,\text{Å}$) occur for the atoms Fe(2), O(5) and Fe(7) with the top three atoms moving inwards and the next two atoms moving outwards. Lateral movements occur predominantly for the surface-terminating oxygen atom O(1), and for the atoms O(3) and Fe(4), with O(1) and O(3) moving in the opposite direction along the fractional coordinate $X$. In contrast, Fe(4) shows a distinct movement both in the fractional $X$- and $Y$-direction.

### 3.2. Surface energy

The surface energy values for (0001) and (01$\overline{1}$2) $\alpha$-Fe₂O₃ are summarized in Table 2. After relaxation and employing the B3LYP hybrid functional in the *a posteriori* correction, the surface energy predicted by the present calculations is $2.4\,\text{J}\,\text{m}^{-2}$ for (0001) and $2.2\,\text{J}\,\text{m}^{-2}$ for (01$\overline{1}$2), which is similar in magnitude to most shell model and ionic model calculations [24,29,48,49,55] but larger than the DFT results [25,26,51,53,54]. As can be seen from Table 3 below, this makes these two facets the two most stable surfaces of hematite after relaxation considered in the present study. However, before relaxation the picture is rather different. (0001) has a relatively large surface energy before relaxation, while (01$\overline{1}$2) already exhibits a very small surface energy before relaxation. The changes in surface energy before and after relaxation are −41% for (0001) and −25% for (01$\overline{1}$2). In hematite the (01$\overline{1}$2) plane cuts the crystal through a plane of empty sites, thus giving rise to relatively small relaxations and surface energies. In contrast, the Fe-terminated (0001) surface exhibits large relaxations since the surface cations are located opposite empty cation sites in the filled-filled-unfilled cation sequence along the $c$-axis. The authors of Ref. [50] point out that the results of their forcefield calculations seem to be closer to experimental fracture surface energies, which lie in the range between 6 and $24\,\text{J}\,\text{m}^{-2}$ for (01$\overline{1}$2) and around $40\,\text{J}\,\text{m}^{-2}$ for (0001) [56]. In the absence of experimental data, and since we are mainly interested in the relative values of the surface energy, we will proceed with a comparison of the surface energy for various facets of $\alpha$-Fe₂O₃ using the *a posteriori* correction with four different hybrid functionals.

## 4. Conclusions

The structure and surface energy of low-index surfaces of stoichiometric $\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$ have been investigated using the periodic Hartree-Fock approach with an a posteriori correction of the correlation energy. The simulations show that the $\{01\overline{1}2\}$ and {0001} families are the most stable facets, which is consistent with the fact that $\{01\overline{1}2\}$ and (0001) are the dominant growth faces exposed on natural $\alpha$-Fe$_2$O$_3$. Hematite does not readily cleave, but Ti$_2$O$_3$ and V$_2$O$_3$ cleave along $\{01\overline{1}2\}$ [59]. Similarly, in hematite the $\{01\overline{1}2\}$ plane cuts the crystal through a plane of empty sites, thus giving rise to relatively small relaxations and surface energies. The small relaxations and concomitant exposure of five-coordinate sites may, in turn, be important for the catalytic activity of hematite [60]. In contrast, the Fe-terminated (0001) surface exhibits a large relaxation since the surface cations are located opposite empty cation sites in the filled-filled-unfilled cation sequence along the c-axis. The simulations also show that the relative stability of the investigated surfaces changes after a full lattice relaxation from $\{01\overline{1}2\} < \{11\overline{2}0\} < \{10\overline{1}1\} \approx \{0001\} < \{10\overline{1}0\} < \{10\overline{1}2\} < \{11\overline{2}6\}$ before relaxation to $\{01\overline{1}2\} < \{0001\} < \{11\overline{2}0\} < \{10\overline{1}0\} \approx \{10\overline{1}1\} < \{11\overline{2}6\} < \{10\overline{1}2\}$, i.e. the {0001} and $\{11\overline{2}6\}$ facets relax disproportionately large. Finally, a Wulff-Gibbs construction was presented and shown to be dominated by the surfaces $\{01\overline{1}2\}$, $\{11\overline{2}0\}$ and {0001}. The present theoretical approach is computationally very efficient. We have previously demonstrated [61] that this method can be successfully applied to metal-oxide supercells, where a full geometry optimization at the DFT level may be too time-consuming. The present simulation results should also help in the fabrication and characterization of hematite nanocrystals [16,62] since the morphology of the crystalline nanomaterials grown at or near thermodynamic equilibrium is governed mainly by the relative surface energy of the various facets.
