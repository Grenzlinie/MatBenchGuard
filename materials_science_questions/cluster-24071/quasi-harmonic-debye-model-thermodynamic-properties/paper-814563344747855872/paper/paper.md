The structural, elastic, thermal and electronic properties of perovskite hydrides $\text{SrLiH}_3$ and $\text{SrPdH}_3$ have been investigated using the all-electron full-potential linear augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method based on the density functional theory (DFT). For the exchange-correlation potential, local-density approximation (LDA) and generalized gradient approximation (GGA) have been used to calculate theoretical lattice parameters, bulk modulus, and its pressure derivative. The present results are in good agreement with available theoretical and experimental data. The three independent elastic constants ($C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$) are also reported. From electronic band structure and density of states (DOSs), it is found that $\text{SrLiH}_3$ is an insulator characterized by an indirect gap of 3.48 eV, while $\text{SrPdH}_3$ is metallic with a calculated DOSs at Fermi energy of 0.745 states/eV-unit cell. Poisson's ratio ($\sigma$), Young's modulus ($E$), shear modulus ($G$), anisotropy factor ($A$), average sound velocities ($v_m$) and density ($\rho$) of these compounds are also estimated for the first time. The Debye temperature is deduced from the average sound velocity. Variation of elastic constants and bulk modulus of these compounds as a function of pressure is also reported. Pressure and thermal effects on some macroscopic properties are predicted using the quasi-harmonic Debye model.

Keywords: FP-LAPW, perovskite-type hydrides; elastic constants; band structure; mechanical properties; thermal properties.

### 1. Introduction
Hydrogen is the most promising energy carrier for the future. Hydrogen storage in the solid state is currently considered as the best option for a safe and long-term solution. The current interest in the development of novel hydrides stems from their higher hydrogen storage capacity at low and medium temperatures.¹ To act as an efficient energy carrier, hydrogen should be absorbed and desorbed in materials easily and in high quantities.² In particular, the perovskite-type hydrides, $ABH_3$ are very promising and potential candidates as future hydrogen storage materials. Moreover, the material functions and even fundamental properties of these compounds have not been clarified yet.

$Sr(Li,Pd)H_3$ are two perovskites hydrides which present a large variety of interesting magnetic and electronic behaviors for hydrogen storage at moderate temperatures and pressures. The lithium-based ternary hydrides $ALiH_3$ ($A = Sr$, $Ba$, Eu) have been investigated theoretically by several authors.³⁵ Orgaz and Guptam⁴ have found that these compounds are semiconductors with relatively large energy gaps. The electronic, elastic and optical properties of $BaLiH_3$ and $SrLiH_3$ have been studied by Ghebouli *et al.*⁶ based on the density functional theory (DFT).⁷ This work shows that $BaLiH_3$ and $SrLiH_3$ presented an indirect bandgap of 2.23 eV and 1.86 eV at equilibrium. Interestingly, there is a second group of perovskite hydrides, consisting of a divalent metal and the transition metal Pd. The electronic structure and some aspects of the electron–phonon coupling in the series of hydrides $APdH_3$ ($A = Sr$, Eu, Yb) were calculated with the augmented plane wave (APW) method.⁸ It is found that that all these compounds are metallic with essentially filled Pd-$d$ bands and a small density of states (DOSs) at Fermi level as in PdH. Also, Orgaz and Guptam⁵ have calculated the electronic structure of $SrLiH_3$ and $SrPdH_3$ and they found that $SrLiH_3$ is insulating while $SrPdH_3$ shows a metallic behavior with a significant density of electronic states at Fermi energy.

Besides that no experimental or theoretical data about elastic and mechanical properties of $SrPdH_3$ is available. The knowledge of the elastic constants of hydrides compounds is necessary for many practical applications.

Therefore, the aim of this work is to understand the structural, elastic, mechanical and thermal properties of the two perovskite-type hydrides $SrLiH_3$ and $SrPdH_3$ using the full-potential linear augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method. This paper is organized as follows. The complete details of method of calculations have been presented in Sec. 2. Section 3 deals with the results and discussion. Finally, we summarize the main conclusions in Sec. 4.

### 2. Computational Method
The considered perovskites $SrLiH_3$ and $SrPdH_3$ have ideal cubic structure (SG: Pm$\overline{3}$m), where the atomic positions in the elementary cell are Sr: (0, 0, 0), Li, Pd: (1/2, 1/2, 1/2) and H: (1/2, 1/2, 0).

### Ab initio study of structural, mechanical, thermal and electronic properties

The present calculations were performed using the FP-LAPW method⁹ within the framework of the DFT¹⁰ as implemented in the WIEN2k code.¹¹ The exchange-correlation treatment is taken into account using the Perdew and Wang local-density approximation (LDA)¹² and the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of Perdew *et al.* (PBE-GGA)¹³,¹⁴ to solve the Kohn–Sham equations. The energy bandgaps have been calculated using three approximations: PBE-GGA, LDA and modified Becke–Johnson (mBJ) exchange potential approximation.¹⁵,¹⁶ In the FP-LAPW method, the wave function, electron charge density and potential were expanded in terms of spherical harmonic functions inside nonoverlapping spheres surrounding the atomic sites (muffin-tin spheres) and a plane wave basis set in the remaining space of the unit cell (interstitial region) is used.

The Brillouin zone integrations were replaced by discrete summations over a special set of *k*-points, using the standard *k*-point technique of Monkhorst and Pack,¹⁷ where the *k*-point mesh used is $(8 \times 8 \times 8)$. The muffin-tin radii were taken as 2.50, 2.20, 2.21 and 1.18 a.u. for Sr, Li, Pd and H, respectively. The plane wave cut-off of $K_{\text{max}} = 8.0/R_{\text{MT}}$ ($R_{\text{MT}}$ is the smallest muffin-tin radius) is chosen for the expansion of the wave functions in the interstitial region while the charge density was Fourier expanded up to $G_{\text{max}} = 16$ (au)⁻¹. The maximum $l$ for the expansion of the wave function in spherical harmonics inside the muffin-tin spheres was taken to be $l_{\text{max}} = 10$.

Before any electronic structure calculation, the lattice constants for $\text{SrLiH}_3$ and $\text{SrPdH}_3$ are optimized. The elastic properties attracted from the knowledge of the derivative of the energy as a function of the lattice strain. In the case of cubic system, this strain is chosen in such a way that the volume of the unit cell is preserved. Thus, for the calculation of elastic constants: $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$, we have used the method developed by Charpin which is integrated in the WIEN2k package.¹¹ The study of thermal effects was done within the quasi-harmonic Debye model implemented in the Gibbs program.¹⁸ For a solid described by an energy–volume ($EV$) relationship in the static approximations, the Gibbs program allows us to evaluate the Debye temperature, to obtain the Gibbs free energy $G(V;P,T)$ and to minimize $G$ for deriving the thermal equation of state (EOS) $V(P,T)$. Other macroscopic properties related to $P$ and $T$ can be also derived by using standard thermodynamic relations. Detailed description of the quasi-harmonic Debye model can be found in Ref. 18 and references therein.

The symmetry of the cubic lattice reduces the 21 elastic constants to three independent elastic constants namely $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$.

$C_{11}$ reflects the resistance of the crystal to the unidirectional compression along the principal crystallographic directions. The constant $C_{12}$ is related to the Poisson ratio which is the ratio of transverse contraction strain to longitudinal extension strain in the direction of stretching force. The constant $C_{44}$ represents the resistance to shear stress applied to the (100) plane along the [010] direction.

The computed elastic constants for $\text{SrLiH}_3$ and $\text{SrPdH}_3$ using both PBE-GGA and LDA are given in Table 1. The requirement of mechanical stability in a cubic structure leads to the following restrictions on the elastic constants$^{22}$: $(C_{11}-C_{12}) > 0$; $(C_{11}+2C_{12}) > 0$; $C_{11} > 0$; $C_{44} > 0$ and the bulk modulus $B$ also should satisfy a criterion: $C_{12} < B < C_{11}$. These criteria are satisfied, indicating that these compounds are elastically stable. The high value of $C_{11}$, which is related to the unidirectional compression along the principal crystallographic directions, compared to the $C_{44}$ one, which reflects the resistance to shear deformation, is an indication of the weak resistance to shear deformation compared to the resistance to the unidirectional compression. The Young's modulus ($E$) is important for technological and engineering application. It is used to provide a measure of the stiffness of the solid, i.e., the larger value of $E$, the stiffer is the material. And the stiffer solids have covalent bonds. $^{29}$ It can be seen from Table 2 that the highest value $E$ occurs for $SrLiH_3$ compound implying to be more covalent in nature as compared to $SrPdH_3$ compound.

The ductile and brittle behavior of materials can be explained from empirical relationship proposed by Pugh. $^{30}$ If the ratio of $B/G$ is greater than 1.75, the material is ductile otherwise it is brittle. The consequence of ductility is less sensitivity for thermal shocks, as the material can efficiently dissipate thermal stress via plastic deformations. $^{31}$ Thus, a ductile solid can support large thermal shocks. From the computed $B/G$ ratios of Table 2 and referred to Pugh's criterion, we can conclude that $SrPdH_3$ is ductile and resistant to thermal shocks; its mechanic properties decrease slowly with increasing temperature. So, this compound may be machinable. For $SrLiH_3$, $B/G<1$, so the compound is brittle.

We may also refer to Frantsevich et al. $^{32}$ who distinguish the ductility and brittleness of materials in terms of Poisson's ratio $\sigma$. According to Frantsevich rule, the critical value of material is 0.26. For brittle materials, the Poisson's ratio is less than 0.26; otherwise the material behaves in a ductile manner. The calculated values indicate that $SrLiH_3$ is brittle while $SrPdH_3$ is ductile. Also, the value of Poisson's ratio is the indicative of the degree of directionality of the covalent bond. It is small $(\approx 0.1)$ for covalent materials, whereas it is greater than or equal to 0.25 for ionic materials. $^{33}$ Accordingly, from our estimations (Table 2) we can conclude that $SrLiH_3$ is a covalent material whereas $SrPdH_3$ is ionic.

Also the Cauchy's pressure defined as the difference between the two particular elastic constants $C_{12}-C_{44}$ is considered to serve as an indication of ductility: if the pressure is positive (negative), the material is expected to be ductile (brittle). $^{34}$ On the basis of above criterion, we confirm that $SrLiH_3$ is brittle whereas $SrPdH_3$ is ductile.

Another important parameter is the elastic anisotropy factor which has an important implication in engineering science since it is highly correlated with the possibility to induce microcracks in materials. $^{31}$ In a cubic crystal, the elastic anisotropy factor is given by:

$$
A=\frac{2C_{44}}{C_{11}-C_{12}}. \tag{8}
$$

isotropic material, $A$ is equal to 1, while any value smaller or larger than 1 indicates anisotropy. The magnitude of the deviation from 1 is a measure of the degree of elastic anisotropy possessed by the crystal. From the computed anisotropy values listed in Table 2, it is clear that the anisotropy factor value for the compounds deviates from unity, meaning that they are elastically anisotropic. The obtained values indicate that these materials are not characterized by a profound anisotropy. For a cubic crystal, the anisotropy factor determines the direction showing the maximum Young's modulus. For $A < 1$, the maximum Young's modulus will appear along [100] direction; while for $A > 1$, the maximum Young's modulus will be in the [111] direction.

With respect to Kleinman parameter $\zeta$, it is known that a low value of $\zeta$ implies there is a large resistance against bond bonding or bond angle distortion. $^{35,36}$ The PBE-GGA and LDA calculated $\zeta$ values are listed in Table 2. It is noted that the $\zeta$ value of $SrLiH_3$ is smaller than that obtained for $SrPdH_3$. This indicates that $SrPdH_3$ is more resistant to change in its bond length than $SrLiH_3$, implying that $SrPdH_3$ is mechanically more stable than $SrLiH_3$.

The Debye temperature $\theta_D$ is an important parameter which is related to many physical properties, such as elastic constants, specific heat and thermal coefficient. It is used to distinguish between the high and low temperature regions in solids. As a rule of thumb, a higher $\theta_D$ implies a higher associated thermal conductivity and melting temperature. At low temperatures the vibrational excitations arise solely from acoustic vibrations. Hence, at low temperature the Debye temperature calculated from elastic constants is the same as that determined from specific measurements. The Debye temperature $\theta_D$ may be estimated from the average sound velocity $v_m$ by the following equation $^{37}$:

$$
\theta_{D}=\frac{h}{k_{B}}\left[\frac{3 n}{4 \pi}\left(\frac{N_{A} \rho}{M}\right)\right]^{1 / 3} v_{m}, \tag{9}
$$

where $h$ is Plank's constant, $k_B$ Boltzmann's constant, $N_A$ the Avogadro's number, $\rho$ the density, $M$ the molecular weight and $n$ is the number of atoms in the molecule. The average sound velocity in the polycrystalline material is given by $^{38}$:

$$
v_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{l}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{t}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1 / 3}, \tag{10}
$$

where $v_l$ and $v_t$ are the longitudinal and transverse sound velocities obtained using the shear modulus $G$ and the bulk modulus $B$ from Navier's equation $^{39}$:

$$
v_{l}=\left(\frac{3 B+4 G}{3 \rho}\right)^{1 / 2} \quad \text { and } \quad v_{t}=\left(\frac{G}{\rho}\right)^{1 / 2}. \tag{11}
$$

### 3.3. Thermal properties

To investigate the thermal properties under high temperature and high pressure, we have applied the quasi-harmonic Debye model. As a first step, a set of total energy calculation versus primitive cell volume ($E-V$), in the static approximation, was carried out and fitted with a numerical EOS in order to determine its structural parameters at $P=0$ and $T=0$ and then derive the macroscopic properties as a function of pressure and temperature from standard thermodynamic relations. The thermal properties are determined in the temperature range from 0 K to 600 K, where the quasi-harmonic model remains fully valid. The pressure effect is studied in the 0–8 GPa range.

The variation of the lattice constant of SrLiH$_3$ and SrPdH$_3$ compounds versus temperature at several pressures is shown in Fig. 3. The lattice constant increases with increasing temperature at a given pressure. On the other side, as the pressure increases the lattice constant decreases at a given temperature. The rate of increase of the lattice constant with temperature decreases with increasing pressure.

The temperature effect on the bulk modulus at various pressures is shown in Fig. 4 for these compounds, as seen in this figure, the bulk modulus is nearly constant from 0 K to 100 K and decreases almost linearly with increasing temperature from $T>100$ K. The compressibility increases with increasing temperature at a given pressure and decreases with pressure at a given temperature. These results are due to the fact that the effect of increasing pressure on the material is the same as the decreasing temperature of the material.

The specific heat of a material is one of the most important thermodynamic properties indicating its heat retention or loss ability. The knowledge of the heat capacity of a substance not only provides essential insight into its vibrational properties but also mandatory for many applications.

### 3.4. Electronic properties

The electronic structure of $SrLiH_3$ and $SrPdH_3$ is analyzed by computing the band structure, total density of states (TDOSs) and partial density of states (PDOSs). The calculated band structures of these compounds along the higher symmetry directions, $M$, $R$, $\Gamma$ and $X$ in the Brillouin zone using the GGA and mBJ approaches are given in Fig. 8. For $SrLiH_3$ the top of the valence band (VBM) is located at X point. However, the bottom of the conduction band (CBM) occurs at the $M$ point. Thus, there is an indirect gap $X-M$. The calculated band structures of $SrPdH_3$ exhibit a metallic behavior. The bandgap values of $SrLiH_3$ using PBE-GGA, LDA and mBJ schemes are 1.859, 1.50 and 3.48 eV, respectively. The bandgap calculated using mBJ agrees very well with that obtained by an empirical method$^5$ which is 3.54 eV. Thus, the mBJ functional is best suited for calculating electronic structure of semiconductors and insulators.

In order to elaborate the nature of the band structure, we have calculated the TDOS and PDOS of these materials. The results are illustrated in Fig. 9. ## 4. Conclusion
Using the FP-LAPW method based on the DFT within GGA, local density approximation and the mBJ potential, we have studied the structural, electronic and mechanical properties of Sr(Li, Pd)H₃. The calculated ground state properties are in good agreement with the experimental and other available theoretical results, validating the herein used method. The electronic band structures and DOSs of these compounds reveal that SrLiH₃ is an insulator while SrPdH₃ is metallic with a significant density of electronic states at Fermi energy. Moreover, the pressure dependence of elastic constants is also investigated. It is found that the pressure

Based on elastic properties, $SrLiH_{3}$ can be classified as brittle material whereas $SrPdH_{3}$ is ductile material. Pressure and temperature effects on lattice constant, bulk modulus, coefficient, heat capacities and Debye temperature are determined for pressures and temperatures in the ranges 0-8 GPa and 0-600 K, respectively.
