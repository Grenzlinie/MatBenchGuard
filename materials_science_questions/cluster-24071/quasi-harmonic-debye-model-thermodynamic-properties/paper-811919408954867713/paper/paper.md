## ABSTRACT

The phase transition of SiC from the zinc blende (ZB) structure to the rocksalt (RS) structure under pressure is investigated by the first principles plane-wave pseudopotential density functional theory method. The results obtained are in good agreement with the experimentally measured data and other theoretically calculated results. It is found that the pressures of transition of SiC from the ZB structure to the RS structure are 74.6 GPa from total energy-volume data and 75.4 GPa from the enthalpy calculations. Moreover, through the quasi-harmonic Debye model, in which the phononic effects are considered, the dependences of relative volume $V/V_0$ on pressure $P$ at $T=0$, 1400 K are successfully obtained. The calculated volume reduction at the transition is 18%. In particular, from the high-pressure elastic constants obtained, the ZB structure SiC is found unstable when the applied pressure is larger than 126.6 GPa. This value is in excellent agreement with the experimental data and the molecular dynamics (MD) simulation results.

## 1. Introduction

Silicon carbide (SiC) has outstanding physical properties, such as excellent chemical stability, a wide band gap, high stiffness, high hardness, high thermal conductivity and a high melting point. These properties make it useful for electronic and optical device applications. For example, microelectronic devices made of SiC can be used in high-power, high-speed, high-temperature and high-frequency applications. At ambient pressure, SiC can exist in various polytypes originating from differences in the stacking sequence of silicon-carbon bilayers along the [111] or [0001] direction. Among about 200 known SiC polytypes, the zinc blende (ZB) structure SiC is the most commonly studied polytype [1].

It is important to study the phase stability of materials under high pressure and temperature for microscopic understanding as well as technological applications [2]. The pressure-induced phase transition of SiC [3-15] has attracted much interest from physicists.

X-ray diffraction measurements [4] have shown that the pressure of transition of SiC from the ZB structure to the rocksalt (RS) structure occurs at 100 GPa or higher, with a volume reduction of 20.3% in static diamond-anvil cell (DAC). The transition is reversible and the ZB phase is recovered below 35 GPa upon pressure release.

Later, shock compression experiments on 6H SiC [5] demonstrate a first-order phase transition with a volume reduction of about 15% into a sixfold coordinated structure (most probably a RS structure) around 105 GPa. Recent shock compression studies at temperatures of 600-1500 K and at pressures 5-25 GPa show unique structural phase transitions among SiC polytypes [6]; for instance, the ZB crystal transforms to the rhombohedral phases by changing the stacking sequence of SiC atom layers.

Up to now, several research groups have theoretically investigated the pressure-induced transitions in SiC using different methods [7-12]. For example, theoretical studies based on *ab initio* density functional calculations with the local-density approximation (LDA) present the critical pressure of the ZB-to-RS phase transition to be around 60 GPa [7-10]. Miao and Lambrecht [11] found the transition pressure 63 GPa using the Perdew-Wang generalized gradient approximation (GGA) for the exchange-correlation potential and the Troullier-Martins pseudopotentials. Moreover, using the Troullier-Martins pseudopotentials and the LDA, Durandurdu [12] recently reported the phase transition pressure of 100 GPa. As pointed by the authors, this result, which might reach the limit of GGA is rather surprising because the DFT-LDA calculations tend to underestimate critical pressure for structure phase transition.

In this work, we also focus on the pressure-induced phase transition of SiC from the ZB structure to the RS structure using a different method. Allowing for the well-known overstabilization

### 2. Theoretical method

The phase transition of SiC from the ZB structure to the RS structure under pressure is investigated by the first principles plane-wave pseudopotential density functional theory (DFT) method. The nonlocal ultrasoft pseudopotential introduced by Vanderbilt [16] together with the Perdew-Wang 1991 (PW91) GGA for the exchange-correlation potential [17] is used to implement constant-pressure *ab initio* simulations. Because it is important to ensure that the ground state energy is converged within a specified tolerance in terms of the iterations for self- consistency, the convergence tests are first made carefully to determine the needed minimums of $k$-points for the Brillouin zone sampling and cut-off energy for the used plane-wave basis set in the DFT calculations. According to the result tested, the wave functions of the valence electron are expanded by plane- wave basis set with an energy cut-off of 550 eV. Pseudo-atom calculations are performed for Si $3s^23p^2$ and C $2s^22p^2$. As for the the Brillouin-zone $k$-point sampling, we use the $12\times12\times12$ Monkhorst-Pack scheme [18]. With these choices of parameter, the self-consistent convergence of the total energy is at $10^{-6}$ eV/ atom. For each value of the applied pressures, the lattice vectors are optimized until the stress tolerance is smaller than 0.05 GPa and the maximum atomic force is smaller than 0.03 eV/Å. All the total energy electric structure calculations are performed with CASTEP code [19] based on the DFT.

One simple method for obtaining the zero-temperature pressure of transition of SiC from the ZB structure to the RS structure is from the slope of the common tangent of both $E$-$V$ curves in Fig. 1. We can estimate the transition pressure of 74.6 GPa. On the other hand, we can also obtain the phase transition pressure from the usual condition of equal enthalpies, i.e., the pressure $P$ at which the enthalpies, $H=E+PV$, of the ZB structure and the RS structure of SiC are the same. The enthalpy obtained, $H$, as a function of pressure $P$, is illustrated in Fig. 2. It is found that the pressure of transition of SiC from the ZB structure to the RS structure is 75.4 GPa. The result obtained is approximately 13-20% greater than the other predictions [7,8,11]. For the volume reduction accompanying the transition, the result presented by Durandurdu [12] is about 33% less than the one of our calculation. For comparison, all the phase transition parameters are listed in Table 2.

Since the enthalpy barrier between the two phases is too high for the transition to be allowed kinetically [12], there is generally an eventual superpressing past the transition in the loading process. The observed transition pressure experimentally becomes definitely higher than the theoretically calculated equilibrium pressure [4]. Therefore, our calculated phase transition parameters are in reasonably good agreement with the experimental data from the X-ray diffraction measurement [4].

Because the stiffness of the materials against hydrostatic pressure is usually not correctly described using LDA [28,29], the underestimation of the transition pressure seems to be a general problem of the DFT-LDA calculations. Our results show that these deficiencies of the LDA calculations can be partially overcome using GGA for the exchange-correlation energy.

Fig. 3. Change of relative volume for SiC in the ZB and RS structures as a function of pressure at $T=0$, 1400 K. $V_0=135.30\mathrm{Bohr}^3$ denotes the calculated equilibrium volume of the ZB phase at $T=0$ and $P=0$. The calculated volume collapse at ZB$\rightarrow$ RS transition is marked.

We also investigate the EOS of SiC using the quasi-harmonic Debye model [30] in which the phononic effects are considered. For the transition pressure of 75.4 GPa, the volume-pressure relations for both the ZB structure and the RS structure of SiC at $T=0$, 1400 K are illustrated in Fig. 3. Obviously, when temperature increases, the curve becomes steeper, indicating that SiC is compressed much more easily at higher temperature. Our results are in reasonable agreement with the experimental data [4]. Unfortunately, there are no other theoretical and experimental data for checking our $V/V_0$-$P$ curve at finite temperature 1400 K.

Finally, we calculate the zero-temperature elastic constants of the ZB structure SiC under high pressure to investigate the mechanical stability of the ZB structure SiC. The calculated zero-pressure elastic constants of the ZB structure SiC are listed in Table 1. Compared with other theoretical data [24], our results are in better agreement with the experimental data [26]. It is obvious from Fig. 4 that $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ increase with increasing pressure $P$; however the variation of $C_{44}$ is not obvious with increasing pressure.

As we know, for a cubic crystal, the mechanical stability under isotropic pressure is judged from the following condition [31]:
$$
\check{C}_{44}>0,\ \check{C}_{11}>|\check{C}_{12}|,\ \check{C}_{11}+2\check{C}_{12}>0 \tag{1}
$$
where $\check{C}_{\alpha\alpha}=\check{C}_{\alpha\alpha}-P(\alpha=1,4)$, $\check{C}_{12}=C_{12}+P$. For the ZB structure SiC, its mechanical stability can be only characterized by the difference $\Delta C_{11-12}=\check{C}_{11}-|\check{C}_{12}|$ at different pressures. Our results (Fig. 5) give, by parabolic fit through 13 points, $\Delta C_{11-12}=285.86675-1.90081P-0.00282P^2$, with $P/\mathrm{GPa}$ and $\Delta C_{11-12}/\mathrm{GPa}$. The equilibrium condition $\Delta C_{11-12}=0$ yields $P_t=126.6$ GPa. This indicates that the ZB structure SiC is unstable when the applied pressure is larger than 126.6 GPa. This value is in excellent agreement with the experimentally measured data and the MD simulation results [4,12].
