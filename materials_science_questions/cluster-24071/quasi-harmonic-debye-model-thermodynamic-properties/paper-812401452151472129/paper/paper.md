# Specific heats of supercooled metallic liquids

D.W. Qi, J. Gryko $^{1}$ and S. Wang

Guelph-Waterloo Program for Graduate Work in Physics, Waterloo Campus, University of Waterloo, Waterloo, ON, Canada N2L 3G1

Received 25 June 1990
Revised manuscript received 22 October 1990

First-principles theoretical results are reported for the specific heats and closely related thermodynamic quantities, required in the determination of the characteristics of the supercooled metallic liquids, for Al, Rb and $Ca_{0.7}Mg_{0.3}$ metals. Of these results, those for the specific heats are fully analyzed and discussed by considering computer-simulation results on the atomic motion in the supercooled-liquid region. It now appears that the specific heat at constant pressure behaves as a monotonically increasing function of temperature in decreasing from the melting temperature to the glass-transition temperature for those supercooled metallic liquids that have high glass-forming ability.

## 1. Introduction

The work of Chen and Turnbull [1] indicates that the behavior of the specific heat at constant pressure, $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$, in the temperature range between the melting temperature, $T_{\mathfrak{m}}$, and the glass-transition temperature, $T_{\mathrm{g}}$, cannot be completely determined experimentally for supercooled metallic liquids. This is because, in this temperature range, i.e., $T_{\mathrm{g}}<T \ll T_{\mathrm{m}}$, the rate of crystal growth becomes so large that the metallic-liquid states are inaccessible to measurement. As noted in refs. [2,3], the above is true even for such a supercooled metallic liquid as $Ni_{0.4}Pd_{0.4}P_{0.2}$ alloy, which possesses an exceptionally high resistance to crystallization as compared with other metallic glass-forming systems [2,3] and which becomes a glass at a low cooling rate (about 1 K/s, this being about the same as that used for the As-Se and P-Se systems [4]). Also, it was noted, for the ternary alloy $Ni_{0.4}Pd_{0.4}P_{0.2}$, that the measured $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$ of the high-$T$ supercooled-liquid branch can be extrapolated smoothly to that of the low-$T$ supercooled-liquid branch near $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ [2]. The extrapolated curve, as shown in ref. [2], increases gradually as $T$ decreases, i.e., the corresponding $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$ behaves as a monotonically increasing function of temperature in decreasing from $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ to $T_{\mathrm{g}}$. This $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$-behavior is similar to that observed for the non-metallic As-Se mixtures in their respective supercooled-liquid states [4] rather than to those shown in fig. 5 of ref. [4] for the non-metallic P-Se mixtures, in which $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$ decreases gradually with decreasing $T$ in the range of $T_{\mathrm{g}}<T \ll T_{\mathrm{m}}$ of interest here.

Since the $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$-property is of particular importance to the study of both the supercooled-liquid states and the liquid-glass transition, the main purpose of this work is to determine whether or not the above-noted extrapolated result is generally true for supercooled metallic liquids by examining the theoretical values of $C_{\mathfrak{p}}$ obtained for supercooled Al, Rb and $Ca_{0.7}Mg_{0.3}$ metallic liquids.

This is partly due to the fact that the thermodynamic results, that are required in the determination of the nature of this transition, are not yet available from a first-principles calcu- lation. In view of this we proposed [7] a Gibes- Bogoliubov type first-principles variational calcu- lation of the Helmholtz free energy, $F$, using the hard-sphere model as the reference system, for thermodynamically metastable supercooled metal- lic liquids. This approach was validated [7] theo- retically and numerically by reproducing (i) the computer-simulation results on the mean atomic volume, $\Omega$ (which satisfies $(\partial F / \partial \Omega)=0$ ), and theinternal energy, $E$ , for supercooled $Ca_{0.7} Mg_{0.3}$  binary liquid and (ii) the observed internal en- ergies for supercooled In metal.

Having verified the use of the variational calcu- lation of $F(=E-T S, S$ being the entropy of the reference system) for supercooled metallic liquids, the results from this calculation can be used as in ref. [8] to evaluate (i) the specific heat at constant volume, $C_{\Omega}(T)=(\partial E / \partial T)_{\Omega}$ , (ii) the isothermal bulk modulus, $B_{T}=\Omega(\partial^{2} F / \partial \Omega^{2})_{T}$ , and (iii) the thermal expansion coefficient at constant pres- sure, $\alpha_{p}=\Omega^{-1}(\partial \Omega / \partial T)_{p}$ for the systems consid ered here. Then, the corresponding $C_{p}$ can be calculated by using $C_{p}(T)=C_{\Omega}(T)+T \Omega B_{T} \alpha_{p}^{2}$ . This calculation of $C_{p}(T)$ was carried out in ref.[7] for supercooled $Ca_{0.7} Mg_{0.3}$ and $Ti_{0.66} V_{0.34}$  metallic binary liquids at values of $T$ near $T_{g}$ and in ref. [9] for the supercooled-liquid states of $Ga$ , Tl and Ti pure metals in order to determine the values of $T_{g}$ of these metallic systems through the anomalous change of $C_{p}(T)$ at the liquid-glass transition.

For the purpose of the present work, the above-described calculation of $C_{p}(T)$ has also been extended to supercooled $Al$ and $Rb$ metallic liquids in which the changes of the interionic pair poten- tials with respect to the interionic distance differ significantly from those of the above-mentioned pure metals.

Second, the errors resulting from the usual numerical calculation of $C_{\Omega}=(\partial E / \partial T)_{\Omega}$ from the slope of a theoretically determined $E-T$ line for each $\Omega$ are negligible for the pure metals considered here. Accordingly, the temperature dependences of $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ as indicated by the $C_{\mathrm{p}}$ results in fig. 1 can be considered to be reliable. Now, this will not be the case for those metallic liquids, for which the use of the hard-sphere system as the reference system in the variational thermodynamic calculation is not quite appropriate. Also, notice that the values of $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ in fig. 1 are determined from the anomalous change of $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$, i.e., $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ first increases with decrease in $T$ and then decreases rapidly with further decrease in $T$ in going from a $T$ slightly above $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ to a lower value of $T$ [7,9]. These values of $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ are consistent with those expected from the fact that pure Al metal can form a glass in the laboratory through the vapor-quenching process [11], whereas pure Rb metal cannot be a laboratory glass $(T_{\mathrm{g}} \simeq 0.33 T_{\mathrm{m}}$ from computer simulations [12,13] for Rb).

There are a few aspects of the $C_{\mathrm{p}}$ results in fig. 1 that merit emphasis. (1) The presently calculated values of $C_{\mathrm{p}}$ are in good agreement with the experimental values available for both liquid metals at values of $T$ near $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ [14]. (2) The calculated values of $C_{\mathrm{p}}$ in fig. (1), for $T$ between $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{g}}$, are somewhat above the observed values of $C_{\mathrm{p}}$ for the crystalline states, as noted in ref. [14]. This should be expected because the structure is more disordered for the supercooled-liquid states than for the crystalling states. (3) For Al, the theoretical $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ behaves as the extrapolated result noted in section 1. (4) For Rb, however, the specific heat decreases at first rapidly and then slowly with falling temperature in the range of $T_{\mathrm{g}}<T \ll T_{\mathrm{m}}$, contrary to the extrapolated result.

To examine aspect (4) critically, we have followed the procedure described in ref. [15] for the computer (or molecular dynamic) calculation of specific heats at constant volume to calculate the dominant part of $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$, i.e., $C_{\Omega}(T)$, for the supercooled-liquid states of 600 Rb atoms. The calculated results, obtained using the same atomic potential and the same mean atomic volume (fig. 1(b)) as in the present calculation of $E$, are shown in fig. 2 along with the corresponding values from the present thermodynamic calculation for Rb. Note that there is a small uncertainty in the computer results displayed in fig. 2 because of a slow convergence of the computer calculation on specific heats. Nevertheless, this uncertainty should not affect qualitatively the temperature dependence of these results. Now, this temperature dependence supports aspect (4) above.

There is also another point to be noticed. That is, the low-lying MSD curves in fig. 3 are, in shape, nearly the same as the MSD curves for glasses [16,17]. Since in a glass an atom must vibrate in an atomic cage, formed by its surrounding atoms, the low-lying MSD curves in fig. 3 may be interpreted by using the well known generalized Einstein model [18]. That is, at values of $T$ closer to $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ than to $T_{\mathrm{m}}$, each atom in the supercooled Rb liquid vibrates in a stable atomic cage about a center which itself undergoes a slow diffusive motion through the liquid. Now, the high-lying MSD curves in fig. 3 may have to be interpreted using a free-volume-like model, i.e., at high $T$ all of the atomic cages in the supercooled Rb liquid become unstable due to the strong ther- mal effects on the atoms and, as a result, some of the atomic cages break and the atoms that were initially enclosed in these cages move according to the Langevin-type equation. Accordingly, the su- percooled-liquid region from $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ to $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ for the pure Rb metal consists of two different structural re- gimes, namely, an undercooled region from $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ to $T \simeq 0.65 T_{\mathrm{m}}$ and a hypercooled region from $T \simeq$ $0.65 T_{\mathrm{m}}$ to a $T$ near $T_{\mathrm{g}}$, so that both $\Omega(T)$ and $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ become anomalous in the vicinity of $T=$ $0.65 T_{\mathrm{m}}$, which is considerably higher than $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ for Rb (fig. 1(b)). To proceed further, notice that pure Rb metal must be less disordered in the hyper- cooled region than in the undercooled region, be- cause the creation and annihilation of atomic cages occurs in the undercooled region but not in the hypercooled region in which the atomic cages are rather stable. Hence the specific heat is consider- ably larger for the higher values than for the lower values of $T$, as shown in fig. 1(b).

First, recall that both the repulsive and attractive parts of the interionic pair potential for pure Al metal are very hard, as compared with the Rb case (fig. 5). Consequently, the ions in pure Al metal have much less freedom to rearrange themselves as the temperature of the system is lowered. This results in both a smaller supercooled region (see fig. 1(a)) and more stable cages than for Rb in its undercooled region. At this point, in order to extend the arguments to alloys, it is useful to note that the MSD curves obtained previously [17] for the liquid binary alloy $\mathrm{Ca}_{0.7} \mathrm{Mg}_{0.3}$ in its supercooled-liquid region cannot be divided, in shape, into two such groups as for Rb (fig. 3). This suggests that the entire supercooled-liquid region of $\mathrm{Ca}_{0.7} \mathrm{Mg}_{0.3}$ is of a single structural type. Accordingly, there will be no anomalies in the corresponding $\Omega(T)$ and $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ for $T_{\mathrm{g}}<T \ll T_{\mathrm{m}}$. Indeed, this is the case, as indicated by the theoretical results in fig. 7.

As a final comment, we remark that the significant changes in $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ near $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ (figs. 1 and 7) require clarification in order to understand completely the liquid-glass transition. This is outside the scope of the present work.

## 4. Conclusion
First-principles thermodynamic results are presented for $\mathrm{Al}, \mathrm{Rb}$ and $\mathrm{Ca}_{0.7} \mathrm{Mg}_{0.3}$ metals in their respective supercooled-liquid states. Of these results, those for $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ are analyzed in relation to the results of computer-calculations. It now appears that the implication of the extrapolated results of refs. [2,3], namely, $C_{\mathrm{p}}(T)$ behaves as a monotonically increasing function of temperature in decreasing from $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ to $T_{\mathrm{g}}$, applies not only for the ternary alloy considered in refs. [2,3] but also for other supercooled metallic liquids for which $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ is quite high, such as $\mathrm{Ca}_{0.7} \mathrm{Mg}_{0.3}$ liquid which can be formed into a glass in the laboratory through the liquid-quenching process. The same does not apply for those supercooled liquids for which $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ is low so that the corresponding supercooled-liquid region from $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ to $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ consists of two different structural regimes, such as the pure $\mathrm{Rb}$ metal considered in this work and pure Ti metal considered in ref. [9].
