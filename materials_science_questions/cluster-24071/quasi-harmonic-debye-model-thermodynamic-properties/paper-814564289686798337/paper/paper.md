## ABSTRACT
The structure, elasticity, tensile strength, phonon and thermodynamic properties of $LuH_2$ with $CaF_2$ structure have been investigated by using first-principles approach. The calculated lattice parameters and the single-crystal elastic constants of $LuH_2$ agree well with experimental data and other theoretical results in the literature. The polycrystalline moduli of $LuH_2$ involving volume elastic modulus, Young's modulus, and shear modulus, the brittle/ductile properties, Poisson's ratio, Debye temperature, sound velocities and hardness have been predicted. Also, the elastic anisotropy has been evaluated. The tensile strength along [100] crystallographic direction has been obtained using first principles total energy method. In addition, the phonon dispersion curves and the corresponding phonon density of states of $LuH_2$ have been determined by virtue of a linear-response approach to density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) along with the small displacement method successfully, and the Infrared-active modes and the Raman-active modes at the Brillouin zone center have been analyzed according to the group theory. Finally, the lattice entropy and the lattice constant volume specific heat up to 1500 K have also been estimated based on quasi-harmonic approximation (QHA).

## Introduction
The rare-earth dihydrides ($LnH_2$;$Ln$ = rare-earth element) exhibit many arresting electronic, optical, and mechanical properties [1–3], and at present, these hydrides have raised tremendous interests due to their promising and wide applications in hydrogen storage areas. The rare-earth dihydrides ($LnH_2$) possess less tendency to dissociate and to produce crystal defects as compared with other metallic hydrides. At ambient conditions, they crystallize in the cubic fluorite structure with $Fm$-$3m$ Space Group (No.225) [1,3–7], typically have a fcc structure with the fluorite type where the hydrogen atoms are located in the tetrahedral intersites of lattice [4]. Experimentally, Bonnet and Daou [5] determined the lattice parameters of seven rare-earth (La, Nd, Gd, Dy, Er, Tm, and Lu) dihydrides at room temperature and 500–800 °C temperature range, it is shown that the lattice parameters increase slowly with the temperature, and then they increase quickly, and the bending of curves is associated with thermally generated point defects. Weaver et al. [1] measured the electronic structure of $ScH_2$, $YH_2$, and $LuH_2$ using optical absorptivity and thermoreflectance techniques. Theoretically, recently, we

## Computational methodology

Our $ab$ initio computational investigations on $LuH_2$ have been mainly performed using the Vienna $ab$ initio simulation package (VASP) [10–13]. The electron–ionic interactions are described by means of the accurate projector augmented-wave (PAW) technique as implemented by Kresse and Joubert [14], and the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) pseudo-potential within the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) scheme [15] is adopted for parametrization of the exchange-correlation energy functional. This method has been widely used to investigate the properties of materials with satisfactory accuracy. The spin polarization is considered in our PAW calculations. For the plane-wave basis set, a cutoff energy of 600 eV is utilized throughout the whole calculations. According to Monkhorst-Pack scheme to optimize the stable crystal structure, the Brillouin zone (BZ) $k$-point is sampled by a $(15 \times 15 \times 15)$ grid in the reciprocal space. In all cases, the total energy of self-consistent convergence is set at $10^{-7}$eV/cell, and the initial crystal lattices are relaxed gradually until the Hellmann–Feynman forces are less than $10^{-3}$ eV/Å ($1$ Å $= 0.1$ nm). All the operational parameters have been repeatedly tested until the favorable converged accuracy is produced.

Then, we determined the single crystal elastic constants of $LuH_2$ utilizing a well-developed "strain-energy" approach [16]. Furthermore, in order to obtain the ideal tensile strength of $LuH_2$, the stress–strain responses have been calculated by incrementally deforming the $LuH_2$ simulation cell in the imposed strain axial directions. In this work, we obtained the corresponding stress based on the Nielsen-Martin scheme for respective corresponding strain [17], and the tensile stress of uniaxial tensile strain is produced from this formula [17],

$$
\sigma=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{V(\varepsilon)} \frac{\partial E(\varepsilon)}{\partial \varepsilon} \tag{1}
$$

Even more important, we performed the lattice vibrational calculations by virtue of density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) under the linear response frame [18] together with the small displacement method [19] via VASP code [10–13] and phonopy code [20]. During the phonon calculations, the $2 \times 2 \times 2$ cubic super cell of 96 atoms involving 32 lutetium atoms and 64 hydrogen atoms has been enlarged in advance to obtain more accurate results.

For the structural relaxation of $LuH_2$, we have not found the significant differences between no-spin and spin effects calculations, this may be because the spin-polarized effect have no big contributions to the energy and volume of cell.

One can see that the present PBE calculated values are excellently in agreement with their PW91 theoretical works. To our knowledge, there are no experimental data available for elastic constants of $LuH_2$, therefore, these results need to be verified in the future. In addition, the difference of elastic quantities between non spin polarization calculations and spin polarization calculations is very unconspicuous, indicating spin polarization effect will have negligible impact on the elastic properties of $LuH_2$. Moreover, it can be seen clearly that the present calculated values $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{44}$ satisfy the requirements of mechanical stability for the cubic crystals [23], i.e., $C_{11}>0, C_{44}>0, C_{11}>|C_{12}|, (C_{11}+2C_{12})>0$, it is concluded that $LuH_2$ with $CaF_2$ structure should be mechanically stabilized under zero temperature and pressure.

We have also evaluated its polycrystalline volume bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, and Young's modulus $E$ from Voigt-Reuss-Hill (V-R-H) approximations method [24], these calculated values are also listed in Table 1. We see that the calculated bulk modulus $B$ are in good agreement with those obtained through the fit to the Birch-Murnaghan EOS above, which suggested that our whole calculations are consistent before and after. One can see that the present PBE calculated polycrystalline moduli are slightly smaller than those of PW91 results of Dai et al. [22], this difference may be due to the difference of two exchange-correlation functionals. Moreover, we also noticed that the calculated bulk modulus $B$ and Young's modulus $E$ are considerably larger than the shear modulus $G$, this means that the shear deformation for $LuH_2$ is easier to occur relatively.

According to Pugh's rule [25], the material behaves brittle if this value $G/B$ is larger 0.57, otherwise it will behave in a ductile manner. From the present calculations in Table 1, the obtained $G/B$ value is 0.649, here we can deduce that $LuH_2$ is brittle material. On the other hand, we also evaluated its brittleness and ductility from the calculated Poisson's ratio $\sigma$ in Table 1, according to Frantsevich et al.'s method [26], metals having a Poisson's ratio less than 1/3 are brittle, thus metals having this value around 1/3 are taken as ductile. The present calculated Poisson's ratio $\sigma$ is 0.233, indicating the brittle nature of $LuH_2$, and this conclusion is consistent with the result estimated by $G/B$ value.

The shear wave velocity $V_s$, the compressional wave velocity $V_p$, and the average wave velocity $V_m$ have been determined base on the calculated bulk modulus and shear modulus as follows [27],
$$
V_{S}=\sqrt{\frac{G}{\rho}}, \quad V_{P}=\sqrt{\left(B+\frac{4}{3} G\right) \frac{1}{\rho}}, \quad V_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{V_{s}^{3}}+\frac{1}{V_{P}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1 / 3} \quad (2)
$$
where $\rho$ is density of $LuH_2$ dihydrides. The calculated three velocities of $V_s$, $V_p$, and $V_m$ are listed in Table 2. In addition, Debye temperature can also be calculated via the average acoustic velocity using the equation as follows [28],
$$
\Theta_{\mathrm{D}}=\frac{h}{\mathrm{k}}\left[\frac{3 n}{4 \pi}\left(\frac{N_{\mathrm{A}} \rho}{\bar{M}}\right)\right]^{1 / 3} V_{\mathrm{m}} \quad (3)
$$
where $h$ and $k_B$ are, respectively, Plank's constant and Boltzmann's constant, and $n$ is the atom number in a molecular, $\rho$ is density and $M$ is the molecular weight. The results have been summarized in Table 2. The calculated Debye temperature is 379.753 K for no-spin and 379.846 K for spin, therefore, the differences are very little.

The hardness has been obtained by virtue of Chen et al.'s [29] new model. The hardness $H_v$ of polycrystalline materials is obtained using the shear modulus (G) and the squared Pugh's modulus ratio ($k=G/B$), and it is expressed as follows:
$$
H_{v}=2\left(k^{2} G\right)^{0.585}-3 \quad (4)
$$

Based on this new model, the calculated hardness of $LuH_2$ are 10.116 GPa for no-spin and 10.117 GPa for spin case, respectively. Hence, we conclude that the spin-polarized effect is very weak for the elastic properties of $LuH_2$.

### Tensile properties
In order to further explore the deformation behavior under loading conditions, we have also calculated the ideal tensile strength by incrementally deforming the simulation cell in the imposed strain axial direction. Fig. 3 presents the calculated tensile strain–stress curve along [100] uniaxial direction. From Fig. 3, we observe that [100] direction ideal tensile strength is 36.9 GPa, and the corresponding strain is 67.3%. The calculated ideal strength could provide reference data for future studies on the mechanical properties of LuH₂.

### Dynamical properties
In particular, we investigated the dynamical properties of LuH₂. The calculated phonon dispersion curves along high symmetry directions are plotted in Figs. 4 and 5, and the corresponding partial phonon density of states are presented in Fig. 6. Each primitive cell of LuH₂ involves one lutetium atom and two hydrogen atoms, therefore, the lattice vibration system should contain nine phonon modes, i.e., three modes are acoustic branches and six of remainder are optical ones. The absence of imaginary frequencies in all high symmetry directions in Figs. 4 and 5 demonstrates that LuH₂ dihydride is dynamically stable. One can observe that the phonon spectra are completely nondegenerate along Γ-K BZ direction. Also, the significant phonon dispersion gaps between acoustic modes and optical ones are observed explicitly. Through careful observation in Figs. 4 and 5, we can note that the remarkable differences remain between DFPT method and the small displacement approach, especially, the small displacement frequencies of optical modes are lower than those of DFPT results. It's worth mentioning, the spin-polarized effect is significant to the dynamical properties of LuH₂, and the spin-polarized effect is more prominent for DFPT method. These properties can also be seen from the calculated phonon density of states in Fig. 6. Similar to ScH₂, YH₂, and LaH₂ [8], from Fig. 6, we observe clearly that the phonon dispersion gaps divide the lattice vibrational modes into two frequency zones, the low frequency regions are mainly occupied by the heavier lutetium atoms, and the high frequency ones are entirely contributed by the lighter hydrogen atoms.

## Conclusions

In conclusion, we have determined the structure, elasticity, tensile strength, vibrational and thermal properties of $LuH_{2}$ with the fluorite structure by means of DFT within PAW method systematically. After a series of calculations, the innovative conclusions are drawn as follows:

(1) The optimized lattice parameters and the calculated single-crystal elastic constants of $LuH_{2}$ are in good agreement with available experimental data and other theoretical results. The polycrystalline volume bulk modulus, Young's modulus, and shear modulus, the brittle/ductile properties, Poisson's ratio, Debye temperature, various sound velocities as well as hardness of $LuH_{2}$ have been obtained successfully. Also, the elastic anisotropy has been evaluated by direction-dependent volume bulk modulus and Young's modulus, it is shown that this dihydride has no strong anisotropy character in bulk modulus and Young's modulus. The calculated results also reveal that the spin-polarized effect is very weak for the elastic properties of $LuH_{2}$.

![](./images/814564289686798337_8.jpg)

Fig. 8 – Calculated entropy curves at different temperatures using various different methods.

(2) The ideal tensile strength along [100] crystallographic uniaxial direction has been obtained using the first principles total energy method.

(3) The lattice vibrational properties of $LuH_{2}$ have been determined using the linear-response approach to density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) and the small displacement approach successfully. It is demonstrated that $LuH_{2}$ with $CaF_{2}$ structure should be dynamically stabilized. The Infrared-active modes and Raman-active modes at the Brillouin zone center have been analyzed systematically according to the group theory. The results indicate that spin-polarized effect is significant to the dynamical properties of $LuH_{2}$.

(4) The lattice temperature-dependent entropy and the lattice constant volume specific heat of $LuH_{2}$ have also been obtained on the basis of the present calculated phonon density of states.
