## ABSTRACT

The structural and lattice dynamical calculations are performed on $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$ compounds using the first-principles of total energy calculations. Generalized gradient approximations (GGA) are used to model exchange-correlation effects. Structural results of these calculations are consistent with past experimental data and other theoretical findings. Our lattice dynamical results regarding phonon dispersion curves and temperature-dependent behavior of thermodynamical properties (entropy, heat capacity, internal energy, and free energy) contribute to the existing literature on these compounds.

## 1. Introduction

The discovery of superconductivity in $MgB_2$ at $Tc = 39$ K has revived new interest in finding superconductivity in other diborides. However, superconductivity is observed [1-3] in only some of them, and various studies are currently directed to shedding light on other properties of diborides, including their elastic, mechanical, and thermodynamical properties [3-7]. It is widely believed that diborides represent a promising group of materials for new heat-resistant, corrosion-resistant, and wear-resistant alloys and coatings [8].

A number of theoretical [9-24] and experimental [25-28] studies exist in the literature dealing with the structural, elastic, and electronic properties of $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$. Specifically, Shein and Ivanovskii [8] examined the influence of lattice vacancies on the structural, cohesive, and electronic properties of Nb and Mo borides using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method and the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). They [9] also reported the structural and elastic properties of $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$. Systematic trends in lattice constants and heats of formation were studied for these compounds by Oguchi [10]. Vajeeston et al. [11] investigated the electronic structure and ground state properties of these transition metal diborides using the self consistent tight-binding linear muffin-tin orbital method (TB-LMTO). The bonding nature, elastic property and hardness were investigated by Zhang et al. [12] for $ZrB_2$ using the first principles total-energy plane-wave pseudopotential (PW-PP) method. The band structure and Fermi surface parameters were studied by Shein and Ivanovskii [13] using the self-consistent full potential linearized muffin-tin orbital (FLMTO) method for $ZrB_2$ and $NbB_2$. The stiffness and thermal expansion coefficient of $ZrB_2$ were calculated using the density functional theory formalism by Milman et al. [14].

The structural parameters, elastic constants and electronic structure of $NbB_2$ under pressure were investigated by Li et al. [15] and Islam et al. [16] using the first-principles plane-wave pseudopotential density functional theory within the (GGA). Singh [17] investigated the significant differences in electron-phonon interaction of $NbB_2$ that lead to the distinction of their superconducting transition temperatures. The simulation results of the electronic structures of $XB_2$ were reported by Ivanovskii et al. [18]. Sing [19] studied electron-phonon interaction in $ZrB_2$ using full-potential density-functional-based methods. Kaur et al. [20] studied the cohesive and thermal properties of these compounds using a Rigid Ion Model (RIM). The five different elastic constants of the superconducting $NbB_2$ were calculated using the ab-initio density functional method, with both correlation and exchange potentials, by Sikder et al. [21]. Regalado and Escamilla [22] performed some calculations on $NbB_{2+x}$ to obtain its elastic constants using the plane wave pseudopotential method based on the density functional theory within the GGA. Paduani [23] investigated the electronic structure of these diborides using first principles calculations with the molecular cluster discrete variational method. Shein et al. To our knowledge, phonon dispersion curves, thermodynamical properties, which are the important bulk properties for solids, have until now neither been obtained theoretically, nor experimentally for $\text{ZrB}_2$, $\text{NbB}_2$, and $\text{MoB}_2$ with the $\text{AlB}_2$-type crystal structure ($P6/mmm$). In this study, we have investigated the phonon dispersion relations and thermodynamical properties of these compounds in detail, and interpret the salient results. The calculation method is given in Section 2; the results are discussed in Section 3. Finally, the summary and conclusion are presented in Section 4.

### 2. Method of calculation

The calculations are performed using the density functional formalism and generalized gradient approximation (GGA) through the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [29] for the exchange-correlation energy in the SIESTA code [30,31]. This code calculates the total energies and atomic forces using a linear combination of atomic orbitals as the basis set. The basis set is based on the finite range pseudoatomic orbitals (PAOs) of the Sankey–Niklewsky type [32], generalized to include multiple-zeta decays. The interactions between electrons and core ions are simulated with the separable Troullier–Martins [33] norm-conserving pseudopotentials.

Atomic pseudopotentials are generated separately for atoms Zr, Nb, Mo, and B by using the $5s^24d^2$, $5s^14d^4$, $5s^14d^5$ and $2s^22p^1$ atomic configurations, respectively. The cut-off radii for the tested atomic pseudopotentials are taken as s: 3.04 au, p: 3.19 au, d: 2.68 au f: 2.68 au for Zr, s: 2.84 au p: 3.84 au d: 2.52 au f: 2.52 au for Nb, s: 2.71 au p: 2.84 au d: 2.46 au f: 2.46 au for Mo and 1.71 au for the s, p, d and f channels of B.

Atoms are allowed to relax until the atomic forces fall below $0.005\ \text{eV}\text{\AA}^{-1}$. The cutoff energy is taken to be 400 Ryd. 792, 1014 and 1183 $k$-points are found to be adequate in obtaining the total energy to an accuracy of 1 meV/atom for $\text{ZrB}_2$, $\text{NbB}_2$, and $\text{MoB}_2$, respectively.

### 3. Results and discussion

#### 3.1. Structural properties

First, we optimize the lattice constants to obtain a relaxed structure. The initial lattice parameters are taken from previous theoretical and experimental values as a starting point for geometry optimization. The calculated lattice parameters and bond distances are listed in Table 1, along with the available experimental and theoretical data for comparison. Findings suggest that the present lattice parameter (a) is in accord with the theoretical and experimental values, but lattice parameter (c) is lower than the experimental and theoretical values of [8–15] by % 2–3 for these compounds. In our calculation, the basis sets have converged well. The small deviation in c from the other works may stem from different calculation techniques, the pseudopotential or exchange-correlation potential used. It can be seen, from this table, that as one goes from left to right across the periodic table, the lattice constants decrease in accordance with the atomic-mass of the transition metal atoms.

The obtained bond lengths of $\text{ZrB}_2$, $\text{NbB}_2$, and $\text{MoB}_2$ are consistent with the theoretical data, and they decrease as they go from $\text{ZrB}_2$ to $\text{MoB}_2$.It is also known that the short-bond length strongly affects some physical properties, such as melting point, elastic constants, and hardness [12].

### 3.2. Phonon dispersion curves

Many physical properties of solids depend on their phonon properties, including specific heat, thermal expansion, heat con- duction, and electron–phonon coupling. Though several studies are published on the structural and electronic properties of these com- pounds, comparative experimental or theoretical studies on the lattice dynamics of these compounds are virtually non-existent in the literature, except for the density of states (DOS) of $ZrB_2$ [19] and $NbB_2$ [27]. The present phonon dispersion curves and other related quantities of $XB_2$ are calculated using the PHONON soft- ware [34]. This code is compatible with SIESTA and uses the “Di- rect Method” [35] and the Hellmann-Feynman forces on atoms for generating the phonon dispersion and the density of states (DOS). Its theoretical and practical details can be found in the PHONON manual and respective references.

The optimized rhombohedral supercell with 36 atoms and basis vectors are given as $\mathbf{a}_s = 2a + c$, $\mathbf{b}_s = 2b + c$, $\mathbf{c}_s = -2a - 2b + c$, where $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{c}$ are the primitive translation vectors of the hexagonal unit cell [36]. The symmetry of the selected rhombohedral supercell breaks the two fold symmetry axis, and lowers the $P6/mmm$ space group to $R3m$. Here, the displacement amplitudes are taken as 0.03 Å, to increase the precision and minimize the anharmonic effects, the positive and negative atomic displacements along $x$, $y$, and $z$ axes are taken into account. The Hellmann-Feynman forces acting on the atoms in the supercell are evaluated to construct the force constants and dynamical matrix. Parlinski [36] have used the same method and the PHONON code to obtain the correct phonon dispersion relations of the hexagonal $MgB_2$.

The calculated phonon dispersion curves and corresponding one-phonon DOS for $XB_2$ along the high-symmetry directions are illustrated in Fig. 1, which suggests that the dispersion curves and corresponding density of states resemble each other for $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$ compounds. The calculated phonon dispersion curves do not contain soft modes at any direction, which confirms the stability of the $P6/mmm$ phase for these compounds.

The unit cell of $XB_2$ contains three atoms, which give rise to a total of nine phonon branches, consisting of three acoustic modes and six optical modes. It is well known that the mass difference between anions and cations strongly affects the maximum and minimum values of the acoustic and optical branches, and a clear gap is formed between them, as can be seen in Fig. 1. While the maximum value of the phonon frequencies for the optical branches follows the order from larger to smaller as $MoB_2 \rightarrow NbB_2 \rightarrow ZrB_2$, the maximum value of the phonon frequencies for acoustic branches decreases in the following sequence: $ZrB_2 \rightarrow NbB_2 \rightarrow MoB_2$. The $E_{2g}$ mode (the highest frequency optical branch) evolves from the double degeneracy at the $\Gamma$ point for these compounds. Along the $\Gamma$–K directions these branches are not degenerate. Interesting features of optical phonon modes are observed at $\Gamma$ point. The optical phonon branches are nearly flat at the $\Gamma$ point, and this flatness of the optical modes causes a very sharp peak in the phonon density of states.

The calculated $\Gamma$ point frequencies: the infrared active ($E_{1u}$, $A_{2u}$) and Raman active ($B_{1g}$, and $E_{2g}$) modes are given Table 2 along with the available experimental data for the sake of comparison. The $\Gamma$ point frequencies of the $E_{1u}$, $A_{2u}$, and $B_{1g}$ are, in general, in favorable agreement with the experimental [22] ones. $E_{2g}$ is higher than experimental [22] values by 5% for $NbB_2$. It can be seen from this table that as one goes from left to right across the periodic table, while the value $\Gamma$ point frequencies of the $E_{1u}$, $A_{2u}$, and $B_{1g}$ decrease, the $E_{2g}$ mode increases in accordance with the atomic-mass of the transition metal atoms.

The figures on the right-side of the phonon dispersion curves show the corresponding total and partial density of phonon states for each compound. While the main contribution to acoustic phonons results from the transition metal sublattice, the high-frequency phonons stem from the boron ions. This is expected because the boron atom is lighter than the transition metal atoms, which leads to comparatively weaker electron–phonon interactions. The covalent character of the B–B bonding is also decisive for the high frequency of phonons involving the boron atoms.

The DOS curves of $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$ can be divided into three distinct regions. The positions of the maximum peak in the first region are at 7.55, 6.50, and 4.21 THz for $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$, respectively. This region belongs to the acoustic phonon modes. The maximum peaks in the second region occur at 17.30, 15.87, and 14.94 THz for $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$, respectively. For these regions, as we move to the right-side of the periodic table, the frequencies decrease in accordance with the atomic-mass of the transition metal atoms. An opposite situation takes place for the maximum peaks in the third region: the small increases in frequencies such as 23.64, 24.72, and 25.67 THz occur for $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$, respectively. The second and third regions result from optical phonon modes. The calculated values of the maximum peak are in good agreement with the theoretical works [17,19] for $NbB_2$ and $ZrB_2$.

The graphical package Molekel [37,38] is used to analyze the individual normal modes. $E_{1u}$ and $A_{2u}$ phonon modes include atomic vibrations from both X (X = Zr, Nb, and Mo) and B atoms. Due to the mass difference between X = (Zr, Nb, and Mo) and B atoms, the $E_{2g}$ modes contain only vibrations of the B atom. $B_{1g}$, $E_{2g}$, and $A_{2u}$ gamma point modes involve atomic vibrations along the directions of $x$, $y$ and $z$ for these compounds. The other gamma-point mode ($E_{1u}$) involves atomic vibrations along the directions as $y$, $z$ for $ZrB_2$ and $NbB_2$, $x$, $y$, and $z$ for $MoB_2$.

## 4. Summary and conclusion

The results we present on several ground states, the structural, vibrational, and thermodynamic properties for $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$ are obtained using the first-principles calculations implemented in SIESTA within the GGA approximation. Our results are mostly in agreement with the available experimental and theoretical findings. Phonon dispersion curves and thermodynamical results at various temperatures were not considered in previous experiments, and these comprise the original contributions of this study. The calculated phonon dispersion curves do not contain soft modes in any direction, which confirms the stability of the $P6/mmm$ phase for $ZrB_2$, $NbB_2$, and $MoB_2$. These compounds exhibit similar, but not very strong electron-phonon interactions.
