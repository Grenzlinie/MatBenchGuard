## ABSTRACT

The high pressure structural behavior and associated properties of mixed rare earth sulfides ($La_xPr_{1-x}S$) have been investigated by applying a Realistic Interaction Potential (RIP) model, including temperature effects. This model explains the inter-atomic interactions as it incorporates long range coulomb interaction; three body interaction (TBI), vdW (van der Waals) interaction, short range overlap repulsive interaction up to second neighbors and zero point energy effects including the temperature effects. We have predicted the high pressure structural phase transition from NaCl ($B_1$-type) to CsCl-type ($B_2$-type) phase and associated volume drops for these compounds. The pressure induced phase transition is found to be a first order. Estimation of the elastic, thermal properties and Every's parameters of these compounds has been performed during study. The results obtained are found to be in reasonable agreement with experimental and other theoretical data.

### 1. Introduction

Rare earth compounds have drawn the considerable attention of theoretical as well as experimental researchers due to the complex electronic properties due to highly correlated f-electrons. The presence of 4f electrons is mostly responsible for the various magnetic and electrical properties in rare earth compounds [1]. These compounds display various interesting semiconducting properties and are found to be applicable in various practical applications viz., electro-optic components, and glass-making, grinding alloys, composites lasers, phosphors lasers and electronic devices. On the application of high pressure some of the rare earth chalcogenides display insulator to metal transition. From the theoretical point of view, the electronic properties of the rare-earth compounds are mainly determined by the degree of localization of the 4f electrons, the treatment of which presents a considerable challenge to band theory. The classification of the rare earths and their compounds in terms of their valances provides an elementary explanation of their physical properties. The knowledge of the pressure behavior is important for developing, comparing and systematically understanding the properties of the rare earth chalcogenides.

The chalcogenides of lanthanum belongs to the class of binary rare-earth mono-chalcogenides with the NaCl-type structure and has been intensively studied because of their interesting physical properties including complex magnetic structures; pressure induced insulator-metal transitions, anomalous valence fluctuations, and unusual Fermi surface properties [2,3]. These chalcogenide transform to CsCl structure under high pressure. Vaitheeswaran et al. [4] reported the discontinuous structural transition from six-fold coordinated NaCl (Fm3m) to eight-folded CsCl (Pm3 m) structure and equation of state for LaS and LaSe at 27-28 and 19 GPa by using the high-pressure angle dispersive X-ray diffraction method. Varshney et al. [5] reported the structural and elastic properties of La chalcogenides, the expressions of elastic moduli used by them give the result to $C_{12}=C_{44}$, while their results show $C_{12} \neq C_{44}$ which is contradictory. Gupta et al. [6] have studied the properties of laX by modified charge-transfer potential (MCTP). Arya et al. [7] have investigated the structural and elastic properties of mixed alloy compounds $Sm_xLa_{1-x}S$ (x = 0.90, 0.75, 0.65) by using modified inter-ionic potential theory (MIPT).

Praseodymium chalcogenides have drawn the great attention of researchers due to their potential application for hyperfine enhanced nuclear cooling and the study of combined electron and nuclear ordering phenomena at very low temperatures [8]. The behavior of PrX compounds is analogous to the LaX and CeX compounds. Varshney et al. [9] have studied the pressure induced phase transition of PrX compounds by using effective potential model. ### 2.1.1. Elastic properties
It is known that the elastic properties play key role in understanding the mechanical and anharmonic properties of solids. Usually, these properties identify the properties of material that undergoes stress, deforms and then recovers and returns to its original shape after removal of stress and perceives information about the binding characteristic between adjacent atomic planes, anisotropic character of binding and structural stability. The study of SOEC's under pressure is important as C₁₁ relates elasticity in length and C₁₂ and C₄₄ are shape related elastic constants. The combinations of SOECs such as Young modulus (Y) which is measure of the stiffness of the solid, Bulk modulus (B_T) which is a measure of hardness, Shear modulus (G) describes the elastic properties of solid under transverse internal forces, tetragonal constants (C_S and C_L) describes the stability and stiffness of materials, Anisotropy (A) is a fourth rank tensor property of elasticity [17], Kleinman parameter (ζ) is the internal displacement parameter. According to Harrison [18] the range of ξ is from $-1/2$ to 1. If $\xi=-1/2$, when there is no energy cost associated with bond stretching and if $\xi=1$ when there is no energy cost for bond bending. Poisson's ratio (σ) provides the hardness of compounds. These combinations are found to be beneficial because they provide valuable information of compounds. Their expressions are taken from Ref. [19].

In order to enhance the mechanical stability of the work, computation of the average elastic wave velocity ($\nu_m$) which is the combination of longitudinal wave velocity ($\nu_l$) and transverse wave velocity ($\nu_t$) has been processed. Their expressions are obtained from Navier's equation [20] which are as follows:

$$
\nu_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{\nu_{t}^{3}}+\frac{1}{\nu_{l}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1 / 3} \tag{9}
$$

$$
\nu_{l}=\sqrt{\frac{3 B_{T}+4 G}{d}}
\tag{10}
$$

$$
\nu_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{G}{d}}
\tag{11}
$$

where 'd' is density. With the help of these velocities, the basic material properties can be determined quickly and easily which are useful in various manufacturing and research applications.

For the technical support to our results, we have analyzed the Blackman's diagram for the compounds LaS and PrS. This diagram can be used to explain the systemization of physical properties, the inter-atomic bonding of f. c.c. Crystals and it also reveals elastic anisotropy, proximity to Born mechanical instability, elastic constants caused by alloying, pressure, temperature, phase transitions and similarities in types of inter-atomic bonding [21]. The Blackman's diagram is a plot between the two dimensionless constants

$$
\mathrm{F}_{12}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}_{12}}{\mathrm{C}_{11}}\right)
\tag{12}
$$

$$
\mathrm{F}_{44}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}_{44}}{\mathrm{C}_{11}}\right)
\tag{13}
$$

It is clear from the expression that $F_{12}$ and $F_{44}$ depend upon the SOECs ($C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$). In order to characterize cubic elastic media we have used another set of parameters viz. $s_{1}, s_{2}$ and $s_{3}$ introduced by Every [21]. The parameter $s_{1}$ depicts the phase and group velocities of sound; Young's and shear moduli as well as surfaces of constant energy of long wavelength acoustic phonons. The parameter $s^{3}$ depicts the acoustic and elastic anisotropies. They are expressed as

$$
\mathrm{s}_{1}=\mathrm{C}_{11}+2 \mathrm{C}_{44}
\tag{14}
$$

$$
\mathrm{s}_{2}=\left(\mathrm{C}_{11}-\mathrm{C}_{44}\right) / s_{1}
\tag{15}
$$

$$
\mathrm{s}_{3}=\left(\mathrm{C}_{11}-\mathrm{C}_{12}-2 \mathrm{C}_{44}\right) / s_{1}
\tag{16}

The technique of minimization of $U_{B_{1}}(r)$ and $U_{B_{2}}(r')$ at different pressures has been followed order to obtain inter-ionic separations r and r' corresponding to B1 and B2 phases. The present RIP approach contains three model parameters are range ($\rho$), hardness (b) and three-body force parameter (f(r)). Their values have been estimated from the knowledge of lattice parameter and by solving the equilibrium conditions.

$$
\left[\frac{d U}{d r}\right]_{r=r_{0}}=0
\tag{17}
$$

$$
\left[\frac{d U}{d r}\right]_{r=r_{0}}=9 k r_{0} B_{T}
\tag{18}
$$

Using the measured values of the equilibrium lattice constant (r) and bulk modulus ($B_{T}$) with K = 2 for $B_{1}$ phase, the model parameters thus calculated are given in Table 1. For simplicity, only a single set [$\rho$, b, f(r)] in $B_{1}$-phase has been taken into account and the same set is used in the $B_{2}$ phase. The reason being that during the phase transition from $B_{1} \to B_{2}$ the atomic distribution takes a different arrangement and they get arranged in $B_{2}$ structure after phase transition. The parameter f(r) is inter-ionic separation (r) dependent and it is therefore changed accordingly. First, the computation of the corresponding Gibbs free energies $G_{B_{1}}(r)$ and $G_{B_{2}}(r')$ has been processed then their respective difference $\Delta G(=(G_{B_{2}}(r') \sim G_{B_{1}}(r)))$ is calculated. $\Delta G$ is important factor for determining the relative stability of phases. After phase transition system becomes stable in $B_{2}$ phase. The phase transition pressure ($P_{t}$) is the pressure at which $\Delta G$ approaches zero. At this pressure both the phases coexist. The calculated values of phase transition pressures and volume drops are given in Table 2. ## 4. Conclusion

It can be concluded that the Realistic Interaction Potential (RIP) model could successfully investigate the structural, mechanical and thermo-physical properties of the alloy rare earth sulfides under high pressure at room temperature. The results are summarized as follows:

1. The phase transformation from NaCl ($B_{1}$) to CsCl ($B_{2}$) under high pressure of present compounds has been targeted. During the phase transition the discontinuity in the pressure volume phase diagram identifies the occurrence of first order transition and exhibits the same trend as the experimental approach.
2. To explore the elastic properties of present compounds, the second order elastic constants and their combinations has been carried out and found good agreement with available experimental data.
3. The present model could efficiently eliminate the drawbacks of Cauchy discrepancy.
4. The present model includes the temperature effect which was ignored by previous model calculations.

5. For the better prediction of stability of the crystals, the Blackman diagram and Every's parameters were utilized for calculations and analysis.

6. The Poisson's ratio of these compounds is found to be less than 0.33 which indicates that these compounds are quite brittle in nature, the computed values of the ratio $B_T/G$ is observed to be less than 1.75 which indicates the brittle nature of both compounds and the value of Cauchy parameter is negative which also confirms the brittle nature of these compounds.
