## Abstract

This study presents a theoretical prediction of the structural, mechanical, electronic and thermal properties of the zinc-based Perovskites ($AgZnF_3$ and $KZnF_3$) within the framework of Density Functional Theory (DFT) using All-electron self consistent Full Potential Augmented Plane Waves plus local orbital FP-(L)APW+lo method. To make our work comparable and reliable, several functional were used for the exchange-correlation potential. Also, this study intends to provide a basis and an improvement for updating either the values already predicted by other previous work (by using obsolete functional) or to predict them for the first time. GGA-PBE and GGA-PBEsol were used to predict the structural properties of $AgZnF_3$ and $KZnF_3$ Perovskites such as lattice parameter, bulk modulus and its pressure derivative and the cohesive energy. For these properties, the found values are in very good agreement; also those found by GGA-PBEsol are closer to other available previous and experimental results. The electronic properties of these materials are investigated and compared to provide a consolidated prediction by using the modified Becke Johnson potential TB-mBJ with other functional; the values found by this potential are closer to the available proven results and show that these materials exhibit an indirect gap from R to $\Gamma$ point. The charge densities plot for [110] direction and QTAIM (Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules) theory indicate that ionic character is predominate for (K, Ag, Zn)-F bonds. Finally, the effect of temperature and pressure on the unit cell volume, the heat capacity $C_V$ and entropy were studied using the quasi-harmonic Debye model.


**Keywords:** Perovskites, DFT, FP-(L)APW+lo, GGA-PBEsol, TB-mBJ, mechanical properties, Thermal properties.

### 1. Introduction

Perovskites have found their way into many different applications and devices [1]. This high penetration leads – in some areas – to the necessity of developing new research in requirements modeling that is concerned with providing models and tools (new functional) to support precise specification of physical properties [2,3]. Fluoride Perovskites are relevant for most high-tech innovative areas in their physical and structural properties [4] such as piezo-electricity [5], photoluminescence [6], high-temperature superconductivity [7] and colossal magneto-resistivity [8]. Significant studies have been strategically allocated to this topic defining it as one of the priority areas in physics and technology [9]. This is why they are a current topic of high interest. Among the most prominent compounds in this field, we focus mainly on $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$ perovskites, which have been proven to be effective, but also are innovative for fields such as mentioned above.

A large part of Fluoride Perovskites materials like $KZnF_3$ have a cubic structure at room temperature [10] of a highest symmetry with space group of Pm-3m. For these studied (K, Ag) $ZnF_3$ compounds, there are two types of positions to build the unit cell. The idea is to take, as the origin atom in position for the first case, either the cation (K, Ag) or that of Zn for the second case.

For the first case, cations (K, Ag) are in (0,0,0) position in a cuboctahedral site, Zinc is at the center of the cube at (0.5,0.5,0.5) position in an octahedral site and the anions of fluoride in the centers of the faces of the cube in the positions (0.0,0.5,0.5), (0.5,0.0,0.5) and (0.5,0.5,0.0).

In the second case, cations (K, Ag) are in the centers of the cube at position (0.5, 0.5, 0.5), Zinc is at original position (0,0,0) (the tops of cubes). Fluorine anions are at edge centers of the cube in positions (0.0, 0.0, 0.5), (0.0, 0.5, 0.0) and (0.5, 0.0, 0.0).

Murtaza et al. [11] contribute to an integral success; for the first time, they give measurable benefits in calculating the structural, mechanical, electronic and thermal properties of $AgZnF_3$ using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA-PBE). In the same way, T. Seddik et al [12] contribute effectively in improving calculations of pressure effect on electronic and elastic properties of $KZnF_3$ compound using the same method.

Ab-initio methods with related FP-LAPW [13,14] are being today's main areas of applications for all physical properties prediction at any temperature or pressure, this is mainly due to the recent development of their functional, especially GGA-PBEsol and TB-mBJ [15].

The glaring lack of information on the physical behavior of AgZnF3 including its elastic and thermodynamic properties and the absence of many experimental results for both Perovskites $AgZnF_3$ and $KZnF_3$, especially their band-gap energy values have motivated us to accomplish a complementary study.

The overall aim of this study is to try to make a further study comparable to those performed by Murtaza et al. [11] and T. Seddik et al [12]. For the involved compounds, we use the FP-LAPW method to determine their structural, mechanical, electronic and thermal properties through several recent functional and the quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM) [16,17] to predict the different information on the bonding of their atoms in their crystal structure.

The outline of the paper is as follows:

The complete computational method is described in detail in chapter 2. In chapters 3, the most relevant results are listed and discussed in terms of comparisons and trends of other works. For the last section, a short conclusion summarizes the most important outcomes selected with respect to future competitiveness and / or their respective technological challenges.

## 2. Computational method
The well-founded full potentiel-linearized augmented plane wave plus local orbital FP-(L)APW+lo [13,14] methodology as implemented in the WIEN2K computer package [18] allows us to predict properly the structural, mechanical, electronic and thermal properties of KZnF₃ and AgZnF₃ Perovskites. The density functional theory (DFT) [19] which intends to solve the problems inherent from many body quantum mechanics is the basis on which FP-(L)APW+lo method is heavily based.

The successful mastery of the latest versions of functional plays a crucial role for predicting physical properties of materials, such as GGA-PBEsol [20, 21] for the prediction of structural and mechanical properties. However, this functional is a revised version of the GGA-PBE functional which is known by its important underestimation of the band-gap values. These hurdles are overcome by the modified Becke Johnson potential TB-mBJ [15] which, according to previous tests on Perovskites, gives values very close to those of the experiment, but it cannot be used to predict properties other than the band-gap value. The success of this potential gave us an opportunity to predict properly, confirm and complement the structural, mechanical, electronic and thermal properties of KZnF₃ and AgZnF₃ Perovskites.

For the basis function, we have adopted the value of $R_{MT}.K_{MAX}$=8.5, where $R_{MT}$ stands for the smallest radius of the atomic sphere whose wave functions inside it are spherical harmonic basis set around the nuclei and $K_{MAX}$ for the magnitude of the largest K vector in

the plane wave expansion. Similarly, we have used a maximum value of lmax= 10 for the partial waves inside atomic spheres, while the charge density (of Fourier) is raised to Gmax = 22.67 $\mathring{A}^{-1}$. K-points mesh integration over Brillouin zone is performed till (12, 12, 12) grid which give 84 k-points in the IBZ (Irreducible Brillouin Zone) [22]. Regarding $R_{MT}$ values for $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ compounds, they are taken to be equal to 1.0583, 1.0054, 1.1112 and 0.8732 $\mathring{A}$ for Ag, K, Zn and F respectively. The self-consistent calculations converge when the total energy converges below the taken value of $1.36\ 10^{-3}$ eV.

## 3. Results and discussions
### 3.1 Structural properties

The study of the structural properties is a prerequisite to predict other physical properties using first principle methods by means of the variation of the unit cell volume of the studied compounds and its adjustment with the well-known Murnaghan equation [23]. This allows us to determine the different structural parameters such as lattice parameter, the bulk modulus and its pressure derivative. Fig.1 and Fig.2 show the variation of the unit cell volume as function of energy for studied compounds ($KZnF_{3},AgZnF_{3}$) where the continuous line presents the Murnaghan fit of our calculated points.

The cohesive energy means the energy required to separate a material to several free atoms. It represents a measure of the intensity of the bonding force between a set of atoms of a solid material and has a correlation with its ground state structural stability. The cohesive energy of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ cubic Perovskites is given by:

$$
E_{coh}=3E_{atom}^{F}+E_{atom}^{K,Ag}+E_{atom}^{Zn}-E_{total}^{(K,Ag)ZnF_{3}} \tag{1}
$$

Where $E_{total}^{(K,Ag)ZnF_{3}}$ represents the total energy of the unit cell which contains three atoms of fluorine of $E_{atom}^{F}$ energy and a potassium atom for the $KZnF_{3}$, or Silver for $AgZnF_{3}$ of $E_{atom}^{K,Ag}$ energy, and a zinc atom of $E_{atom}^{Zn}$ energy.

The success of PBEsol functional in the prediction of the structural properties of solid materials is well known and has already been tested in many previous works. The obtained results of lattice parameter, bulk modulus and its pressure derivatives and the cohesive energy of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ compared to some experimental and other theoretical works are grouped in table. 1.

According to our results, we can say that the values of the lattice parameter obtained by PBE are similar to available theoretical work but those obtained by PBEsol are the closest to the experiment values compared to other authors results and those obtained by PBE. We can also notice that our results are very close to those found by G. Murtaza et al. [11] in the case of $AgZnF_{3}$ and those calculated by T. Seddik et al [12] in the case of $KZnF_{3}$.

It should be noted also that although silver has a number of electrons relatively important compared to potassium, $AgZnF_{3}$ lattice parameter is close to that of $KgZnF_{3}$ and since the lattice parameter depends on the number of Z electrons of atoms and the type of bonding, this means that for these two compounds this parameter depends primarily on the bonding between zinc and fluorine. It can be concluded also that the bonding between silver and fluorine is too strong compared to the existing one between fluorine and potassium.

The same interpretation is approved for the predicted bulk modulus value of $KZnF_{3}$ by PBEsol. The success of functional PBEsol in predicting the structural and elastic properties that was already tested on this family of materials and confirmed in several previous works [24], allows us to believe that for $AgZnF_{3}$ value using this functional will be closer to that of experiment once measured. For our calculated values of pressure derivatives of bulk modulus and the cohesive energy for $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$, we notice the lack of experimental results and we can conclude that our values are similar and consistent compared with other available theoretical works [11,12,29].

### 3.2 Mechanical properties

After studying the structural properties of $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$ and to complete our objective of this work, we will try in this second part to investigate their elastic properties.

The elasticity of a solid material is its ability to recover its structure or its original shape after being deformed by a slight external stress. This property is essential to achieve the study of other mechanical properties and allows us to collect a lot of related information, including mechanical stability, ductility, stiffness, anisotropy and other mechanical quantities which result in the determination of the elastic constants Cij of studied materials. There are several experimental techniques to determine the elastic constants, but there are difficulties to carry out this measure because of certain physical conditions or technical problems underlining the importance of a theoretical model.

Several theoretical models have been developed for the determination of elastic constants. They are based on the study of the response in terms of energy of a material under the effect of an external force. Their success depends on the theory used to calculate its

energy (FP-LAPW, FP-LMTO, PPsPW, etc ...) and the atomic structure of the studied material.

In this study, we chose the two theoretical models of "Mehl [32] and Charpin [33,34] " to determine the elastic constant of $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$, they have been already tested on perovskites with the use of the FP-(L)APW+lo method and they have shown great success. $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$ compounds have a cubic structure that has three elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{44}$.

Mehl model allows to determine the constants $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ of a cubic structure by the application of a volume-conserving orthorhombic strain tensor based on changes of the lattice parameter (a = b = c) in the three directions given by: à=a+ $\delta$, b'=b- $\delta$ and c'=c+[$\delta^2$/(1- $\delta^2$)]. This orthorhombic strain tensor causes a change in the energy value of the unstrained lattice at the equilibrium volume by:

$$
E\ (\delta) = E\ (0) + (C_{11} - C_{12})\ V\delta^2 \tag{2}
$$

Where V: represents the unit cell volume.

The curve E ($\delta$) is a straight line whose slope $\alpha$ represents the value of $(C_{11} - C_{12})V= \alpha$, so $C_{11} - C_{12}= \alpha/V$ and we know that for a cubic compound, the bulk modulus can be calculated by: B= $(C_{11} + 2C_{12})/3$, hence $C_{11}=B+\frac{2}{3}\frac{\alpha}{V}$ and $C_{12}=B-\frac{\alpha}{3V}$.

The determination of $C_{44}$ with this model is performed in the same way by applying a volume-conserving monoclinic strain tensor given by: à=a+($\delta$/2) , b'=b-($\delta$/2) and c'=c+[$\delta^2$/(4- $\delta^2$)], the energy value of the unstrained lattice at the equilibrium volume for this strain tensor is given by:

$$
E(\delta) = E(0) + 1/2(C_{44})\ V\delta^2 \tag{3}
$$

The slope $\alpha$' of the curve E ($\delta$) represents the value of ½ $C_{44}V=\alpha$', hence $C_{44}$=2 $\alpha$'/V.

Fig.3 and Fig.4 show the variation of the total energy as a function of squared orthorhombic and monoclinic strains for of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$.

The determination of the elastic constants by Charpin model within a package implemented in the Wien2k code and the theoretical basis of this model is detailed in [35].

The elastic constants Cij values obtained by the two models are shown in table. 2. The least we can say that for $KZnF_{3}$, the values obtained are similar to those calculated theoretically by T. Seddik et.al. [12]. Also Charpin and Mehl models values obtained with PBEsol functional are the closest to those of the experiment, which confirms the success of these models and PBEsol functional for the study of elastic constants.

For $AgZnF_{3}$, we notice that there is no value found in literature as regards the elastic constants Cij and as we mentioned above and due to the success of PBEsol in predicting elastic properties of Perovskite materials, we also believe that our obtained values will be close to those of experiments once measured.

The mechanical stability of a cubic material must meet the relationships given [36] by:

$$
\mathrm{C}_{11}-\mathrm{C}_{12}>0, \mathrm{C}_{11}>0, \mathrm{C}_{44}>0, \mathrm{C}_{11}+2 \mathrm{C}_{12}>0, \mathrm{C}_{12}<\mathrm{B}<\mathrm{C}_{11} \tag{4}
$$

According to these relationships and obtained Cij values, $AgZnF_{3}$ and $KZnF_{3}$ are stable materials in this cubic Perovskite structure.

Several important mechanical quantities can be determined by the values of the elastic constants such as Young's modulus E, shear modulus G and Poisson's ratio $v$ by the following relationships [37,38,39]:

$$
E=\frac{9 B G}{3 B+G} \tag{5}
$$

$$
G=\frac{C_{11}-C_{12}+3 C_{44}}{5} \quad \text { (For cubic material) } \tag{6}
$$

$$
v=\frac{3 B-E}{6 B} \tag{7}
$$

With the knowledge of these quantities, we can study the ductility and brittleness of our materials by the ratio B/G. According to Pugh [40], a material is brittle if the ratio "B/G" is less than a limit value of 1.75 and is ductile if it is higher than this limit value (ratio B/G characterizing brittleness <1.75< ratio B/G characterizing ductility).

In solid state physics, the anisotropy (opposite of isotropy) is the property that an anisotropic material can present different characteristics (such as the mechanical, electronic, optical properties...) in the different directions of its structure. It can be represented by an anisotropy factor "A" which for a cubic material and using the elastic constants, it can be determined by the following relationship [41]:

$$\mathrm{A=2C_{44}/\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)} \tag{8}$$

A material is isotropic if and only if this factor is close to unity "1" and is anisotropic if it is far from "1"

The obtained values of Young's modulus E (GPa), shear modulus G (GPa), the Zener anisotropy factor, Poisson's ratio $v$ and B/G ratio of KZnF₃ and AgZnF₃ are represented in Table. 3. In the case of KZnF₃, we notice that all obtained values of these quantities for our materials are similar to each other and close to those found theoretically and the found values for anisotropy factor are in very good agreement with those of the experimental. It should be pointed out that there is a lack of theoretical and experimental results for the AgZnF₃ and no experimental results for KZnF₃ except that of anisotropy.

According to obtained results of B/G ratio, they are all higher than the limit value which allowed us to say that KZnF₃ and AgZnF₃ are ductile materials. The results of the anisotropy factor are close to unity which means that these materials are isotropic.

Debye temperature $\theta_{D}$ is a very important physical quantity that represents the behavior of the heat capacity of solids and its knowledge gives us a lot of information about the characteristics of solid materials under the effect of temperature. There are many theoretical models which allow us to calculate $\theta_{D}$ among them the mechanical models and this by the knowledge of elastic constants values and this by the following classical relations [41]:

$$
\theta_{D}=\frac{h}{k}\left[\frac{3 n}{4 \pi}\left(\frac{N_{a} \rho}{M}\right)\right]^{1 / 3} V_{m} \tag{9}
$$

Where: $h$ is Planck's constant, $k$ is Boltzmann's constant, $n$ is the number of atoms per formula unit, $N_{a}$ is Avogadro's number, $\rho$ is the density, $M$ is the molecular mass per formula unit and $V_{m}$ is the average wave velocity that can be determined by the following relation [42]:

$$
V_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{V_{t}^{3}}+\frac{1}{V_{l}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1 / 3} \tag{10}
$$

Where: $V_{l}$ is the longitudinal elastic wave velocity and $V_{t}$ is the transverse elastic wave velocity and they can be determined by the following relationships [43]:

$$
V_{l}=\left(\frac{3 B+4 G}{3 \rho}\right)^{1 / 2} \tag{11}
$$

and

$$
V_{t}=\left(\frac{G}{\rho}\right)^{1 / 2} \tag{12}
$$

Other important quantities related to Debye model can also be determined by the knowledge of the Debye temperature $\theta_{D}$ such as Debye temperature $\theta_{\mathrm{a}}$ for an acoustic phonon [44,45] which is an important physical quantity. Also, it allows us to determine other quantities such as Debye frequency $\omega_{D}$ [46] that represents the theoretical maximum vibration frequency of the atoms of a solid during a thermal transfer and which can be determined by:

$$
\theta_{a}=\theta_{D} n^{-1 / 3} \tag{13}
$$

and

$$
\omega_{D}=\frac{h}{k_{B}} \theta_{D} n^{-1 / 3} \tag{14}
$$

All obtained values for the longitudinal and transverse elastic wave velocities, Debye temperature, Debye temperature $\theta_{\mathrm{a}}$ for an acoustic phonon and Debye frequency are grouped in table. 4. We can judge that for the longitudinal and transverse elastic wave velocities and Debye temperature values obtained for $\mathrm{KZnF}_{3}$ by Mehl are similar with those found by A. Meziani et.al. [29]. We note the lack of experimental results in expectation of future measurements except that of Debye temperature experimentally obtained and is in very good agreement with our results. Unfortunately, there is no theoretical or experimental value found for the case of $\mathrm{AgZnF}_{3}$. According to the obtained values of Debye temperature $\theta_{\mathrm{a}}$ for an acoustic phonon and Debye frequency, we can say that the obtained values are similar and there are no found theoretical or experimental results.

### 3.2. Electronic properties

After the success confirmed by large previous studies [15] of TB-mBJ potential in predicting the band-gap value that gives results of a few percent compared to the experimental values, we believe that it is time to make a prediction of band-gap values of $KZnF_3$ and AgZnF3 using this potential and this for both, reaching the goal of our study and updating previous results.

In order to determine the electronic properties of $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$, we have calculated their band structures and this to extract the different electronic information especially band gap, density of states and identification of the types of bonds between the atoms that form these compounds. Fig.5 and Fig.6 show calculated band structures of $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$

Using traditional functional (LDA and GGA) was a problem for the band gap calculations because they significantly underestimated values with respect to the experimental [50,51,52]. To overcome this shortcoming, Trans and Blaha have proposed a new potential by modifying that of Becke-Johnson. This contribution has attracted a great success for the prediction of band-gap values and electronic properties.

The authors DAVID Koller et al [53], Alex P. Gaiduk et al [54] and A. Karolewski et al [55], argue that the potential TB-mBJ has a drawback because it can't be obtained as a functional derivative. Thus, this potential cannot be used to calculate the forces acting on the nuclei and that are necessary for geometric optimization.

According to the above, this potential has very encouraging results concerning the band-gap energy, and electronic properties, which is not valid for properties related to the optimization of the geometry such that the structural and elastic properties.

In this study we have used a variety of functional and all found results regarding the value of band gap and valence band widths are presented in table. 5.

According to found results and calculated band structures, we notice that $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$ have an indirect band gap with the minimum of the conduction band lying at the $\Gamma$-point and the maximum of the valence band lying at the R-point. The different used functional give the same topology of bands, but the only difference is the value of the gap. It should be noted the lack of the experimental results and the larger values of band gap are those calculated by TB-mBJ which are far from the found theoretical values obtained by G. Murtaza et al. [11] , T. Seddik et al [12] and those obtained by GGA-EV and GGA-PBEsol. According to our knowledge on this potential (TB-mBJ) and its success in predicting the value of the band-gap of Perovskite materials, obtained values will be very close to that of the experiment once they will be measured.

We also note that the $KZnF_3$ band-gap is wide compared to that of $AgZnF_3$, this may be served by the contribution of silver states in the valence and conduction bands as well as the bonding between silver and fluorine atoms.

The electrons of the valence band are responsible for many physical phenomena of solid materials (bonding between atoms, electronic and optical properties, etc.) and the width of this band has a major influence on these phenomena. Its width is the energy difference between the top of the valence band and the top of the last band gap below the Fermi level. Our valence band widths values are similar to each other and to our knowledge; there are no theoretical or experimental results.

The total and partial density of states for $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$ are shown in Fig.7 and Fig.8. For $KZnF_3$, the valence band is between the Fermi level (E = 0 eV) and E =-5.586, -6.212, -5.206 and -4.924 eV for GGA-PBE, GGA-PBEsol, GGA-EV and TB-mBJ

respectively. This band is mainly composed by Zinc $d$-states and fluorine $P$-states with a low contribution of potassium $P$-states. For $AgZnF_{3}$, the valence band is between the Fermi level (E = 0 eV) and E =-5.963, -6.669, -6.583 and -6.158 eV for GGA-PBE, GGA-PBEsol, GGA-EV and TB-mBJ respectively. It is constituted by a high contribution from zinc $d$-states, silver $d$-states and the $P$-states of fluorine. Finally, the conduction band is constituted by a mixture of all states (s, p and d) of all the elements (silver, zinc, potassium and fluorine).

Fig.9 and Fig.10 present the charge density plots along [110] direction for $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ which indicate qualitatively that ionic character is predominant for (K, Ag, Zn)-F bonds, but to confirm this we need a quantitative study by performing a topological analysis on the electron densities within Bader's quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM) [16,17].

Several physical quantities related to charge transfer and bonding type between atoms can be determined. The degree of ionicity ($\alpha$) based on QTAIM model provides an important tool to predict the type of bonds between atoms of a given compound by the following relation [56,57]:

$$
\alpha=\frac{1}{N} \sum_{\Omega=1}^{N} \frac{Q(\Omega)}{O_{D}(\Omega)} \tag{15}
$$

Where; Q is the topological charge of $\Omega$ atomic basin and $O_D$ is the nominal oxidation state of $\Omega$ atom. According to this theory (QTAIM) and this relationship, if this factor $\alpha$ is close to 1, it means that the bonding of studied compound is largely ionic and if it is close to zero the covalent character is paramount and if it is between 0.3 and 0.6, it means that we have a polar bonding [58].

Charge transfer of a given atom $\Omega$ can be measured with QTAIM model by:

$$
C(\Omega)=1-\left[\frac{O_{D}(\Omega)-Q(\Omega)}{O_{D}(\Omega)}\right] \tag{16}
$$

Table. 6 contains all calculated results of atomic basins volume $V_{\Omega}$, charge transfer $C_{\Omega}$, topological charge $Q_{\Omega}$ and degree of ionicity $(\alpha)$ obtained by (QTAIM) with PBE and PBEsol functional. We notice that charges produced by both functional (PBE and PBEsol) and which represent the base for this theory (QTAIM) give similar values to each other and which are greater than 0.75 for both materials ($KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$) which means that the ionic behavior is largely predominant, and this confirms our qualitative analysis of their charge density plots.

### 3.3 Thermal properties:

As a large part of Perovskite materials have a cubic structure at room temperature and due the importance of studying the effect of temperature on the behavior of materials, the last part of this study presents a study of the effect of temperature and the effect of the hydrostatic pressure at room temperature on the lattice parameter, heat capacity and enthalpy of $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$. This study is valid in case these materials keep their stability under a pressure lower than 14 GPa and at high temperature.

The study of the effect of temperature and of hydrostatic pressure on the solid material arouses great importance and the information provided is very precious in the field of technology and industry. Like all physical properties, and due to experimental difficulties, theoretical models are an effective way for such studies. Generally, the electronic component operates at ambient temperatures and this is why we have studied the variation of the lattice parameter, entropy and heat capacity under the effect of temperature and under the effect of the hydrostatic pressure at room temperature using the Debye model [59]. This model is well-known by its success and it gives very good results [60,61].

Using Debye model, the Gibbs function is an indispensable way to study any other related quantity, it is given by the following relation [62-66]:

$$
G^{*}(V ; P ; T)=E(V)+P V+A_{v i b}\left[\theta_{D}(V) ; T\right] \tag{17}
$$

Where:
$P$ is the applied hydrostatic pressure, $V$ is the volume of the unit cell at a given pressure and a given temperature, $E(V)$ is the energy of the unit volume, $A_{vib}$ is the vibrational Helmholtz free energy and $\theta_{D}(V)$ is the Debye temperature at a given unit cell volume.

Therefore, minimizing the non-equilibrium Gibbs function G*(V;P;T) relative to the unit cell volume variable at constant pressure and temperature leads to zero according to the following relationship:

$$
\left[\frac{\partial G^{*}(V ; P ; T)}{\partial V}\right]_{P, T}=0 \tag{18}
$$

By resolving this equation (Eq. (17)), we can directly obtain the thermal equation (EOS) $V(P, T)$ and therefore, we can also obtain the expression of heat capacity at constant volume $C_V$ and entropy by the following relations [67]:

$$
C_{V}=3 n k\left[4 D\left(\frac{\theta_{D}}{T}\right)-\frac{3 \theta_{D} / T}{e^{\theta_{D} / T}-1}\right] \tag{19}
$$

And

$$
S=n k\left[4 D\left(\frac{\theta_{D}}{T}\right)-3 \ln \left(1-e^{-\theta_{D} / T}\right)\right] \tag{20}
$$

Fig.11 and Fig.12 show respectively the unit cell volume variation of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ compounds calculated by PBE and PBEsol under the effect of a temperature ranging between 0 and $800^{\circ} K$ and under the effect of hydrostatic pressure between 0 and 14 GPa at room temperature.

We can clearly notice that unit cell volumes obtained by the used functional (PBE and PBEsol) for our materials ($KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$) are similar and the difference between them is almost constant during their variations.

According to our results, the unit cell volume increases with increasing temperature and this is due to the energy acquired by the material which increases the vibration frequency of atoms and consequently the volume increases. The curves obtained by used functional are

similar and their variation is almost linear with a very moderate increase between 0 and
100°K.

At room temperature, the pressure tends to reduce the volume which is evident and the
variation of the curve V = f (p) is almost linear. At zero pressure, the volumes of the unit cell
obtained by PBE and PBEsol are respectively 73.142, 68.826 for $KZnF_{3}$ and 69.113, 65.536
$\AA^{3}$ for $AgZnF_{3}$.

The variations of entropy of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ under the effect of temperature and
hydrostatic pressure at room temperature are shown in Fig.13 and Fig.14.

In Fig.13 the entropy increases with increasing temperature, and this is due to the
increase of the energy acquired as heat.

At room temperature (Fig.14) the entropy of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ decreases under the
effect of hydrostatic pressure and this is due to the decrease in volume which consequently
decreases the vibration of the atoms (decrease of their energies). The obtained curves for the
two materials by PBE and PBEsol are similar. At room temperature and zero pressure, the
entropy values obtained by PBE and PBEsol are respectively 130.882, 122.945 for $KZnF_{3}$ and
134.028, 134.255 J/ mol K for AgZnF3.

The effects of temperature and hydrostatic pressure at room temperature on the heat
capacity Cv of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ are shown in Fig.15 and Fig.16.

At low temperatures (< 800 K), the heat capacity for $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ increases
with increasing temperature (Fig.15) and it is almost constant at high temperature and its
value is close to Dulong-Petit limit [68] (~ 3nR= 124.717 J/mol K).

At room temperature (Fig.16), heat capacity $C_{v}$ decreases with the increasing
hydrostatic pressure and this is due to the decrease in volume reducing consequently the
capacity to acquire energy by materials. The curves obtained are similar and the variation of
heat capacity Cv under pressure is linear. The obtained results of heat capacity Cv at zero

pressure by PBE and PBEsol are respectively 113.423, 111.843 for $KZnF_3$ and 113.994,
114.0335 J/ mol K for $AgZnF_3$.

## Conclusion

In this study we have performed a theoretical prediction of the structural, mechanical, electronic and thermal properties of $AgZnF_3$ and $KZnF_3$ within FP-(L)APW+lo method with the use of several recent functional, because in this field the greatest impact on industry and everyday life is to be expected. Making this impact come true requires significant results on the one and organizational efforts on the other hand. According to the analysis of our results, we can deduce directly that the values of the lattice parameter obtained by PBE are similar to theoretical studies that are available; but those obtained by PBEsol are the closest to the experimental values compared to other author's results and those obtained by PBE. The same interpretation is approved for the predicted bulk modulus value of $KZnF_3$ by PBEsol, as we believe that the same applies on $AgZnF_3$. Mehl and Charpin models form the basis for predicting the elastic constants and many related parameters. The elastic constants Cij values and their related parameters obtained by the two models are similar to those available from theoretical studies and those of the experiment. From the criteria of the mechanical stability of cubic materials at P=0 GPa, Cij values show that $AgZnF_3$ and $KZnF_3$ are stable materials in this cubic Perovskite structure. According to found results and calculated band structures, we notice that $KZnF_3$ and $AgZnF_3$ have an indirect band gap with the minimum of the conduction band lying at the $\Gamma$-point and the maximum of the valence band lying at the R-point. One of the constraints is the lack of available experimental results, and the larger values of band gap are undoubtedly those calculated by TB-mBJ, which are far from the found theoretical values and those obtained by GGA-EV and GGA-PBEsol. However, we believe that the values found by TB-mBJ potential in predicting the band-gap value will be close to those of experiment once measured, due to its success. The charge density plots along [110] direction

for $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ and QTAIM model show that the ionic behavior is largely predominant for (K, Ag, Zn)-F bonds. According to the found thermal results, the unit cell volume increases with increasing temperature and the pressure tends to reduce the volume at ambient temperature of studied materials. The entropy of $KZnF_{3}$ and $AgZnF_{3}$ increases with increasing temperature and decreases under the effect of hydrostatic pressure at $300\ ^{\circ}K$. At room temperature, heat capacity Cv decreases with the increasing hydrostatic pressure and at high temperature, its value is close to Dulong-Petit limit.
