## ABSTRACT

The structural, elastic and phonon properties of $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ alloy are investigated by performing density functional theory and density functional perturbation theory calculations. The calculated lattice parameter increases with the increase of Sn content obeying Vegard's Law that is in good agreement with available experimental data. Shear modulus, Young's modulus and sound velocities are determined from the obtained elastic constants. Phonon dispersion curves show a pronounced softening with increasing of Sn content. The softening mechanism has been discussed based upon the element mass and bond strength. Besides, phonon contribution to the Helmholtz free energy, the entropy and the constant-volume heat capacity are calculated within the harmonic approximation based on the calculated phonon density of states. Results show $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ is thermodynamically more stable with higher Sn content.

### 1. Introduction

Magnesium silicide ($Mg_2Si$) is an intermediate thermoelectric material with maximum operating temperature of around 600 K and has potential applications in high temperature thermoelectric devices [1]. Also attraction of $Mg_2Si$ is due to its non-toxicity and high abundance of the constituent elements. However, the thermal conductivity of pure $Mg_2Si$ compounds is about 6–8 $Wm^{-1}K^{-1}$ [2], that is too large to result in a good thermoelectric performance. To reduce its thermal conductivity, $Mg_2B^{IV}$ ($B^{IV}=Si, Ge, Sn$) solid solutions are generally applied. A continuous series of solid solutions in $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Ge_x$ has been reported [1]. But no solid solutions are found in the $0.4 < x < 0.6$ range for $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ and $0.3 < x < 0.5$ for $Mg_2Ge_{1-x}Sn_x$ [1]. Because of a large difference of atomic mass between $Mg_2Si$ and $Mg_2Sn$, it bas been shown that their solid solutions in the $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ system have the lowest lattice thermal conductivity [2,3]. Therefore, this system is more promising for possessing good thermoelectric performance than the two end systems. Also experimental reports show that $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ solid solutions can achieve a $ZT$ value of 1.2 for bulk crystals [2] and up to 1.4 for bulk nano-structured materials [4].

Theoretical studies of electronic structure and lattice vibration properties of $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ have been carried out by several groups. Electronic band structures and band gaps of $Mg_2Si$, $Mg_2Ge$, $Mg_2Sn$ were calculated using the pseudopotential method [5,6]. Results showed that all three compounds exhibited indirect band gaps. The band structure character of $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ solid solutions were discussed by Fedorovet et al. [7], Zaitsev et al. [2]. They found that in the $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ solid solution, there existed a light and heavy band inversion, providing an avenue by which the electronic band structure can be optimized. Zhang et al. [8] showed that there existed continuous solid solutions in $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Ge_x$ and limited solid solutions in $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ and $Mg_2Ge_{1-x}Sn_x$ systems by the calculated capacity of forming $Mg_2B^{IV}$ solid solutions using the ab initio density functional theory (DFT) calculation. Elastic properties, lattice dynamics and thermodynamic properties of $Mg_2Si$ and $Mg_2Ge$ compounds were investigated using a first-principles pseudopotential method [9]. Calculation results were in good agreement with previous experimental data. As discussed above, $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ is a more economic and promising thermoelectric material. To be able to tune the thermoelectric properties, it is necessary to understand the mechanism of thermal conductivity, which in turn, is determined by thermodynamic functions.

In this work, the phonon and thermodynamic properties of $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ are investigated theoretically. Firstly, structural and elastic properties of $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ are studied using DFT calculation. Then the phonon properties of the $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ including phonon dispersion relations and phonon density of states (DOS) are calculated using the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) method [10]. Temperature dependence of thermal properties such as free energy, specific heat and entropy are predicted in the last part.

### 2. Computational methods

Mg₂Si is composed of one Si atom and two Mg atoms per primitive cell and four Si atoms and eight Mg atoms per conventional cell, belonging to the cubic system with space group Fm3m. Mg₂Sn keep the same structure but replacing Si with Sn. Our calculations carried out for Mg₂Si₁₋ₓSnₓ use three different supercell sizes: a primitive cell (3 atoms/cell), a conventional cell (12 atoms/cell) and a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ supercell from primitive cell (24 atoms/cell). The primitive cell is used to simulate the condition of $x = 0$ and $x = 1$, while the conventional cell to a doping level about 0.25, 0.75; and the 24-atom supercell to the concentration of 0.125, 0.875. DFT calculations are performed based on a plane-wave pseudo-potential approach using the CASTEP package [11]. The exchange correlation potential is described by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [12] with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) scheme. The interaction between the valence electrons and the ion core is substituted by an ultrasoft pseudopotential. Geometry optimization is performed before the single point energy calculation and the self-consistent convergence accuracy was set at $1 \times 10^{-5}$ eV atom⁻¹. The convergence criterion setting for the least force between atoms is $0.03$ eV Å⁻¹, for the maximum displacement is $1 \times 10^{-4}$ nm and for the stress is 0.05 GPa, respectively. For the primitive and the unit cells of Mg₂Si, we use $6 \times 6 \times 6$ and $4 \times 4 \times 4$ Monkhorst–Pack meshes [13], respectively, for the self-consistent run to calculate charge densities. For the $2 \times 2 \times 2$ supercell, we use $3 \times 3 \times 3$ **k**-point mesh. In all calculations, the kinetic energy cutoff for the wave function expanding is 300 eV.

The lattice dynamic properties are calculated on the corresponding optimized crystal geometries. The phonon frequencies are obtained as second-order derivatives of the total energy with respect to an external electric field or to atomic displacements within the framework of DFPT [10].

### 3. Results and discussions

The Sn concentration dependence of lattice constant after geometry relaxation is shown in Fig. 1, together with experimental values [1] for comparison. Generally the lattice constants of the ground states are smaller than that of the experimental results. When $x = 0$, the optimized lattice constant is 6.300 Å, which is in fair agreement with the experimental measurement 6.338 Å with the errors about 0.6% and other GGA pseudo-potential calculation result 6.295 Å. This demonstrates the reliability of our self-consistent calculations and the validity of the pseudopotentials. It can be easily seen from Fig. 1 that the ground state lattice constant increases almost linearly with the Sn substitution, i.e., following Vegard's law fairly well,

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{T}(x) & =6.70 * x+6.30 *(1-x) \\
a^{E}(x) & =6.75 * x+6.35 *(1-x)
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

where $a^{T}$ and $a^{E}$ are theoretical and experimental lattice parameter respectively. This result is in line with the expectation that stronger bonding characters result in smaller volume. Mg₂Si₁₋ₓSnₓ is not a continuous solid solution; solid solutions with Sn content of 0.4–0.6 not exist [1], so the calculation in the range of $0.4 < x < 0.6$ is not given here.

The calculated elastic constants of Mg₂Si₁₋ₓSnₓ are listed in Table 1. From these elastic constants, shear modulus $G$ and bulk modulus $B$ are calculated by the average of Reuss and Voigt values. The bulk modulus, which is defined as the second derivative of the total energy, are 56.15 GPa and 46.93 GPa for Mg₂Si and Mg₂Sn respectively. These values agree fairly well with experimental data of 59 GPa [14] and 41.2 GPa [6]. Bulk modulus $B$ is considered as the resistance of fracture, and shear modulus $G$ the resistance to plastic deformation. Young's modulus $(E)$, the evaluation of the stiffness of the materials, is related to bulk modulus and shear modulus as $E = (9BG)/(G+3B)$. From Table 1 we can see that the Young's modulus of Mg₂Si is larger than that of Mg₂Sn and the solid solution; or the stiffness of Mg₂Si is larger than that of the solid solution. $B/G$ is associated with ductility or brittleness, and the critical value which separates ductile and brittle is about 1.75 [16]. We can get that the brittleness of Mg₂Si₁₋ₓSnₓ is reduced by comparison with that of Mg₂Sn. From the calculated elastic constants, we can also derive the average sound velocities for Mg₂Si₁₋ₓSnₓ, which are expressed as

$$
v_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{v_{l}^{3}}+\frac{2}{v_{s}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1 / 3} \tag{2}
$$

where $v_{l}$ and $v_{s}$ are the compressional and shear wave velocity of the polycrystalline materials respectively, which are obtained from Navier's equation [17]

$$
v_{l}=\sqrt{\left(B+\frac{4}{3} G\right) / \rho}, \quad v_{s}=\sqrt{G / \rho}. \tag{3}
$$

However, experimental results [18] have shown that the solid solutions have smaller thermal conductivity than pure Mg₂Sn; this was attributed to the change in phonon relaxation time due to point defect scattering.

This is mainly caused by two reasons, weaker bond force (reason 1) and heavier atomic mass (reason 2) in $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$. The calculated bond population between Si-Mg for $Mg_2Si_{0.125}Sn_{0.875}$ is 0.48, and that between Sn-Mg for $Mg_2Si_{0.125}Sn_{0.875}$ is 0.36. The smaller bond population between Sn-Mg than that between Si-Mg is indicative of a weaker Sn-Mg bond than that of Si-Mg bond. Weaker interatomic bonding force means lower phonon frequencies. Hence the increase of Sn content intends to soften the phonon frequency. The second reason is the different atomic mass between Si and Sn elements. The heavier Sn reduces the vibration frequency. As a result, the phonon frequencies of $Mg_2Sn$ are lower than those of $Mg_2Si$. The main exception of the frequency reduction tendency is the longitudinal-acoustic (LA) phonon in $Mg_2Sn$, as can be see in the right-most panel in Fig. 2. The frequency of the LA phonon in pure $Mg_2Sn$ compound is more dispersive than that in the solid solutions, which would give a higher thermal transmission coefficient, since the low-frequency modes play a dominant role in thermal conductance at room temperature [19]. This is consistent with the issue that solid solutions can reduce lattice thermal conductivity.

The partial phonon densities of states are displayed in Fig. 3 for better understanding of contributions to phonon structure of each atom. Generally, the low frequency modes below $150\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ are mainly from Sn, and the high frequency modes (above $200\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) are dominated by the partial DOS of Si. The partial phonon DOS of $Mg_2Si_xSn_{1-x}$ clearly indicates that Sn atoms contribute more to the low frequency region than Si and Mg atoms, because of the heavier mass of Sn than the other two atoms. It is interesting to note that there exits overlaps between optical phonons and acoustic phonons in a broad frequency range up to $350\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ for $Mg_2Si_{0.8755}Sn_{0.125}$ and $Mg_2Si_{0.75}Sn_{0.25}$. This is different for the case in $Mg_2Si_{0.25}Sn_{0.75}$, $Mg_2Si_{0.125}Sn_{0.875}$ and $Mg_2Sn$, as there is a phonon band gap at frequency about $150\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. This can be understood from the atomic masses of Mg, Si and Sn. Similar atomic masses of Mg and Si lead to the overlapping phonon dispersions, while the significantly large atomic mass of Sn results in the phonon band gap.

Thermodynamic properties, such as the free energy, the entropy and heat capacity can be deduced from the phonon density of states. The heat capacity at constant volume $C_v$ is given by

$$
C_{v}(T)=k \int \frac{(\hbar \omega / k T)^{2} \exp (\hbar \omega / k T)}{[\exp (\hbar \omega / k T)-1]^{2}} g(\omega) d \omega \tag{4}
$$

where $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $\hbar$ is the Planck constant and $g\ (\omega)$ is the phonon DOS of the phonon frequency $\omega$. Here $g\ (\omega)$ is normalized so that $\int g(\omega) d \omega=3 N$, where $N$ is the number of atoms in the unit cell. The lattice vibrational contribution to the free energy $F(T)$ and entropy $S(T)$ can be evaluated as

$$
F_{\mathrm{vib}}(T)=E_{\mathrm{tot}}+E_{z p}+k T \int g(\omega) \ln \left[1-\exp \left(-\frac{\hbar \omega}{k T}\right)\right] d \omega \tag{5}
$$

$$
S_{\mathrm{vib}}=-\left(\frac{\partial F_{\mathrm{vib}}}{\partial T}\right) \tag{6}
$$

where $E_{zp}$ is the zero point energy.

## 4. Conclusion

In this work, a comprehensive analysis of the structural, phonon and thermodynamic properties of $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ solid solution has been performed using DFT and DFPT calculations. The calculated equilibrium lattice constant is in good agreement with experimental data. The obtained sound velocities become larger as Sn content increases, which is favorable for reducing the lattice thermal conductivity. Phonon dispersion relations show that phonon branches become soft with the increase of Sn content, which is due to the large atom mass and weak Mg-Sn bond. Solid solutions with more Sn content are expected having a lower thermal conductivity. Also a phonon band gap appears when content of Sn is at least larger than 0.75. Free energy calculations indicate that $Mg_2Si_{1-x}Sn_x$ is more stable with more Sn content.
