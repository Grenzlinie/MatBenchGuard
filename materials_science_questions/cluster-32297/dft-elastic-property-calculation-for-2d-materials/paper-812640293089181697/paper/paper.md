## Introduction

Two-dimensional (2D) materials show intriguing electronic, magnetic and chemical properties [1–6], and have been widely applied in different areas, such as nanodevices, optoelectronics, catalysis, and energy storage [7–9]. So far, a rich variety of 2D materials have been developed, such as graphene [10–12], silicene [13–15], germanene [16], black phosphorus [17], graphitic carbon nitride [18,19], transitional-metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) [20–22], MXenes [23–29] and Thallium(I) oxide [30]. For example, 2D TMDs have a chemical formula of $\text{MX}_2$ (M = transition metal atom; X = S, Se, or Te), where one layer of M atoms is sandwiched in between of two X atomic layers [31] and can be used in transistors [32,33], optics [34,35], or electrocatalysts for hydrogen production [36–47] due to their unique physical and chemical properties. MXenes (2D transition-metal nitrides/carbides) with a chemical formula of $\text{M}_n\text{X}_{n-1}$ (M = transition metal; X = C or N) [23–29] show great potentials for applications in energy storage (such as Li-ion batteries [48] and pseudo capacitors [49]) and hydrogen production [50]. In general, most of 2D materials are composed of hexagonal building blocks. Recently, Zhang et al. first reported that penta-graphene could be semiconductor [51], compared to the hexagonal graphene with metallic nature, which triggered increasing interest to tune the physical and chemical properties of 2D materials by structural engineering [52–56]. For example, pentagonal 2D $\text{PdSe}_2$ showed tunable band gap and high electron mobility, and could be used in the long-wavelength infrared photodetector with high sensitivity and stability [57]. Pentagonal building blocks have lower symmetry compared to hexagonal ones, leading to interesting electrical, optical, thermal and magnetic properties [58]. However, a systematic study on the physical and chemical properties of 2D pentagonal metal sulphides has not been available.

In this work, three new 2D pentagonal transition metal sulphides (MS, M = Fe, Mn, and V) are studied on the basis of DFT. The results show that the MS monolayers are metallic, and the magnetic properties of MS monolayers strongly depend on the types of metal atoms. We find that the FeS and VS monolayers have very high catalytic activity for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) due to their near-zero overpotentials, which may be useful as noble-metal-free electrocatalysts.

## Computational method

First-principles calculations based on DFT were carried out to design new pentagonal 2D materials and investigate their physical and chemical properties using Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [59,60]. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (PBE-GGA) [61] and the projector augmented wave (PAW) scheme were used [62]. Monkhorst-Pack scheme was applied for the integration of k-point sampling. For the geometry optimization and structure relaxation of unit cell, a k-point grid of $15 \times 15 \times 1$ and energy cut-off of 500 eV were set thorough the calculations. A vacuum layer with a thickness of $20\ \mathring{A}$ was used to avoid the artificial interaction between the monolayers. For the calculation of the density of states (DOS), Gaussian smearing of 0.05 eV and $41 \times 41 \times 1$ Monkhorst–Pack k-points were applied.

For phonon dispersion, a $3 \times 3 \times 1$ supercell with 72 atoms was used to simulate the dispersion curve by applying the finite displacement approach [63] as implemented in the PHONOPY code interfaced with VASP code [64].

## Results and discussion

### Crystal structures

The MS monolayers have sandwiched structures with S atoms at the top and bottom, while M atoms stay in the middle (Fig. 1). The 2D pentagonal MS monolayers are tetragonal systems, and there are four M atoms and four S atoms in the unit cell. The structural parameters, including the thickness of monolayer ($\text{h}_1$) (Fig. 1b), effective thickness of unit cell ($\text{h}_2$) (Fig. 1b), bond lengths ($\text{a}_1$ and $\text{a}_2$), and bond angles ($\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$) (Fig. 1a) are summarised in Table 1.

### Dynamic, thermal, and mechanical stability

After optimizing the structures and minimizing the total energies of MS monolayers, phonon dispersions were calculated to explore their structural stabilities. For a dynamically unstable system, significant imaginary fluctuating phonon frequencies will be shown. The results for the MS (M = Fe, Mn, V) monolayers show only negligible imaginary frequencies near the $\Gamma$ point of first Brillouin zones (Fig. 2). Therefore, the MS monolayers are predicted to be dynamically stable.

Although the MS structures are dynamically stable at 0 K, the stabilities at high temperature need to be further confirmed. Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) calculations were carried out in order to investigate the thermal stability. A $3 \times 3 \times 1$ supercell was used in AIMD. The MS monolayers were annealed under 673 K for 9 ps with a time step of 3 fs. The FeS and VS monolayers show no significant deformation of structures and negligible increase in magnitudes of potential energy (Fig. 3a&b). However, the MnS monolayer shows large

To further confirm the structural stability, the mechanical stability needs to be computed, which can be evaluated from the elastic moduli. For a tetragonal system, $c_{11}$, $c_{22}$, $c_{12}$, and $c_{66}$ are the four nonzero 2D elastic constants using the standard Voigt notation, 1-xx, 2-yy, and 6-xy [65]. The Born criteria [66] for stable 2D monolayer are $c_{11}c_{22}-c_{12}^{2}>0$ and $c_{66}>0$. Since $c_{11}=c_{22}$ due to the symmetries of structure, the necessary and sufficient conditions for stable MS monolayer are $c_{11}>|c_{12}|$ and $c_{66}>0$[65]. The calculated elastic moduli (Table 2) show that the values of $c_{66}$ are positive while $c_{11}>|c_{12}|$for all MS monolayers, indicating that the structures are mechanically stable.

Our calculations show that all three systems are dynamically, thermally and mechanically stable. Therefore, we conclude that the pentagonal MS (M = Fe, Mn, V) monolayers can theoretically exist.

### Electronic and magnetic properties

As the MS monolayers are stable and may be fabricated in experiments, their electronic and magnetic properties need to be explored for their potential applications. To confirm their ground magnetic states, the total energies of the MS monolayers with all possible alignments of moments, including nonmagnetic (NM), ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) states (Fig. 4), were calculated. There are two possible AFM arrangements for the unit cell of MS system, named as AFM1 and AFM2. For AFM1, the metal atoms on the same side have opposite spins with the other two metal atoms. For AFM2, the two diagonal metal atoms have opposite spins with the other two metal atoms. The energy differences, $\Delta E_{NM-FM}$ and $\Delta E_{AFM-FM}$, were calculated (where $E_{NM}$, $E_{FM}$ and $E_{AFM}$ denote the energies of nonmagnetic, ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic states, respectively) to find the most stable ground state (Table 3).

The calculated energy difference ($\Delta E_{AFM -FM}$) for FeS is positive, indicating that its ground magnetic state is ferromagnetic. For MnS, the negative energy difference shows that its ground state is antiferromagnetic (AFM1). Differently, VS is nonmagnetic because of the very small energy difference (2.5 meV/unit) (Table 3). The magnetic moments of the Fe and S atoms are about 1.4 and $-0.04\ \mu_B$/atom, respectively. For MnS, the Mn atom has a magnetic moment about $1.55\ \mu_B$, while the S atom shows no magnetic moment (Table 3).

To further find out the origin of the ground magnetic states, the projected density of states (PDOSs) were calculated. The d orbitals of M atoms are decomposed to in-plane component (d<sub>σ</sub>) and out-of-plane component (d<sub>π</sub>). For FeS, Fe_d<sub>σ</sub> dominates the spin polarization around the Fermi level, leading to a net magnetic moment of around 1.42 μ<sub>B</sub>/Fe (Fig. 5). The magnetic moment of S atom is about −0.04 μ<sub>B</sub> in FeS and antiparallel to that of metal atom, indicating that the dominant mechanism of ferromagnetism is the carrier-mediated double exchange [67,68]. Mn_d<sub>σ</sub> and Mn_d<sub>π</sub> of antiferromagnetic MnS make the main contribution to the spin-polarization near the Fermi level and the S atom is spin-unpolarized, demonstrating that the superexchange among Mn atoms leads to the ground AFM state [67,68]. Additionally, the calculated densities of states show that all these pentagonal 2D materials are metallic (Fig. 6).

### HER activity of pure MS
One of the important applications for 2D materials is in catalysis because of their large surface area and high active sites [9]. For example, 2D materials have been widely studied as electrocatalysts for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) [69]. As an important prerequisite, the electrocatalysts should be metallic, which is the case for the MS monolayers in this work. Therefore, the MS monolayers are investigated for their applications in HER. Firstly, the adsorption of hydrogen on pure MS monolayers is studied. There are three highly symmetrical sites to hold the hydrogen atom, including the top of S atom (TS) (Fig. 7a), hollow site (HC) (Fig. 7b), and top of M atom (TM) (Fig. 7c). After the geometry relaxation, we find that the hydrogen atom initially at TM or TS moves to HC, confirming that HC is the most stable site for hydrogen adsorption.

To evaluate the HER performance of MS monolayer, Gibbs free energy for hydrogen adsorption ($\Delta G_{\text{H}}$) is calculated as below [71]:

$\Delta G_{\text{H}} = \Delta E_{\text{H}} + \Delta E_{\text{ZPE}} - \text{T}\Delta S_{\text{H}}$, where $\Delta E_{\text{ZPE}}$ is the change in zero-point energies between the adsorbed hydrogen and that in gas phase, $\Delta S_{\text{H}}$ is the change in entropy after the hydrogen adsorption, and T is the temperature. $\Delta E_{\text{ZPE}}$ for FeS, MnS and VS are 0.04, 0.03 and 0.08 eV/H, respectively, for hydrogen atom adsorbed at hollow site. The entropy of $\text{H}_2$ in the gas phase at 300 K is 130.858 J/mol-K [72]. The average vibrational entropy in the adsorbed state of hydrogen atom is insignificant and has an average T*S as small as 0.01 eV at 300 K. Thus, the $\Delta E_{\text{ZPE}} - \text{T}\Delta S_{\text{H}}$ is about 0.24 eV for MS monolayer in average and able to be considered as a constant due to its negligible deviation. For a catalyst with high HER activity, $\Delta G_{\text{H}}$ is close to 0 eV.

In this case, a $2 \times 2 \times 1$ supercell was constructed with different hydrogen densities to investigate the effect of the H- coverage on the HER activity. There are eight possible hollow sites for holding the hydrogen atoms (Fig. 7b). Spin- polarization was considered in the calculations for the FeS and MnS monolayers.

The calculated $\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ indicates that the H-coverage and type of transition metals strongly affect the HERactivity. For the FeS monolayer with hydrogen atoms adsorbed on HC, d-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ is about −0.07 and −0.03 eV for n = 6 and 8, respectively, which are very close to zero, suggesting its excellent HER perfor- mance and high catalytic activity in the individual process at high hydrogen density. The calculated a-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ of FeS-HC is also close to zero from n = 3 to 5, where a-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ ranges from −0.07 to −0.09 eV in that interval (Fig. 8b). However, the total energy required in the average process needs to multiply the number of hydrogen atoms (n) on the surface, which makes the indi- vidual process more likely to take place on the FeS monolayer. The d-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ of VS-HC are −0.02, −0.06, 0.22 and 0.12 eV for n = 1, 2, 3 and 6, respectively. In the collective process, VS has low over potentials at n = 1, 2 and 3, where a-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ are −0.02, 0.03, and 0.11 eV, respectively (Fig. 8b). For the HER on VS mono- layer, both individual and average processes may take place at low hydrogen density, but average process is difficult to take place at high hydrogen density due to the large overpotential.

The weak chemical absorption leads to high catalytic HER performance for the FeS and VS monolayers. However, the absolute values of overpotentials for the MnS monolayer are larger than those of FeS and VS, leading to a relatively low catalytic activity for HER. The calculated d-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ of MnS monolayer is not far away from zero in the range of n = 1 to 5, where d-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ has the range from −0.25 to 0.15 eV (Figure S2a). However, the MnS monolayer shows great deformation at n = 7 and 8, due to the movement of hydrogen atoms from the top of monolayer to the middle in the individual process (Figure S3a&b). This characteristic may be useful for the sit- uation that requires transporting hydrogen in a more stable way, but it leads to an undesirable overpotential of −4.8 eV at n = 7 (Figure S2a). The a-$\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ of MnS monolayer is around 0.5 eV away from zero, indicating a poor collective process (Figure S2b). Therefore, we can conclude that the MnS monolayer has relatively low catalytic activity in HER, compared to the FeS and VS monolayers.

Nowadays, platinum is considered as the ideal HER catalyst with low $\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}$ (−0.09 eV) [36,74]. Based on our calculations, the theoretical values $|\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}^\ast|$ of FeS and VS are lower than that of platinum, which provides potential low-cost replacement to Pt (Fig. 9). It is worth mentioning that the $|\Delta \text{G}_\text{H}^\ast|$ of FeS and VS are lower than most of the common transition metal sul- phides, such as $\text{WS}_2$ [75] and $\text{MoS}_2$, [76] and MXenes, such as $\text{Ti}_3\text{C}_2$ [77] (Fig. 9), and its performance is also superior to that of sub-stoichiometric $\text{MoS}_x$ 2D film [78]. ## Conclusions

In this work, we present a family of three new pentagonal 2D metal sulphide monolayers on the basis of DFT calculations. The 2D MS monolayers (M = Fe, Mn and V) are thermally, structurally and mechanically stable by the analysis of their phonon dispersions, the molecular dynamic simulations, and the investigation of mechanical properties. All the MS monolayers are metallic, and show diverse magnetic properties strongly depending on the host transition metal. The FeS and VS monolayers have low overpotentials, which lead to great catalytic performance in the hydrogen evolution reactions in acidic solution. We expect that the MS monolayers can be noble-metal-free catalysts for practical applications, and our designs will provide guidance to explore cheap and abundant materials as catalysts for hydrogen evolution reactions.
