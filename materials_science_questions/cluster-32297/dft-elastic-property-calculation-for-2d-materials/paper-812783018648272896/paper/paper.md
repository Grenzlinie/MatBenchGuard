# Modulation of vertical strain and electric field on $C_{3}As$/arsenene heterostructure

Shivam Kansara $^{1}$ · Yogesh Sonvane $^{1}$ · Sanjeev K. Gupta $^{2}$

Received: 26 March 2019 / Accepted: 9 April 2019
© King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology 2019

## Abstract
Here, we have focused on electronic and optical properties of heterostructure $C_{3}As$/arsenene under vertical strain and electric field using density functional theory. The phonon dispersion calculation reveals the stability of the monolayer $C_{3}As$. In addition, the tunable electronic properties of heterolayer $C_{3}As$/arsenene have been discussed by addressing vertical strain and electric field. The bandgaps were tuned from 0.75 to 1.6 eV as indirect-to-direct transition by accounting vertical strain and for electric field, it is decreases upto 0.25 eV. The modulation of the strain and electric field in $C_{3}As$/arsenene heterolayer was investigated by the interlayer interaction strength of the layers. Moreover, the calculated optical absorption shows the strongly in UV, while weakly absorption in the visible spectrum. Therefore, the results show good structure tunablity by external strain and electric field for electronic photodetector device applications.

**Keywords** $C_{3}As$ monolayer · $C_{3}As$/arsenene heterostructure · Band modulation · Optical properties · Density functional theory

## Introduction
It is much interesting to understand the formation and geometry of the complex systems by controlling the parameters and properties of materials. Thus, the arrangement of various monolayer (2D) systems has numerous consideration due to their excellent properties (Li and Chen 2014; Guo et al. 2014; Zeng et al. 2015; She et al. 2016; Kansara et al. 2018c). Tunable electronic properties of 2D systems show a critical part in the optoelectronic and electronic devices for many potential applications. In general, heterostructure is broadly used to tune the electronic properties using external perturbation such as strain, electric field, and chemical modification (Xu et al. 2017; Kansara et al. 2018a). Actually, experimentally bandgap tuning with electric field is quite difficult because of requiring large surface area (Dai and Zeng 2014). Therefore, researches have the easy way to tune the bandgap as vertical strain and pressure, which is modulate the bandgap due to weaker vdW interaction (Kansara et al. 2016, 2018b; Zeng et al. 2018; Tamleh et al. 2018). Instead of that, the bandgap could be modulated by varying the stacking order of layers (Tran et al. 2014). In this era, various layered materials such as graphene-like boron nitride (g-BN) (Xia et al. 2014), transition-metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) (Ouyang et al. 2016; Kansara et al. 2018a), group IV (Mortazavi et al. 2017), and V (Shu et al. 2018) have been investigated under external mechanical field (Koda et al. 2017; Mortazavi et al. 2017; Dong et al. 2017; Shu et al. 2018; Kansara et al. 2018a).

Recently, first-time carbon-based monolayer $C_{3}N$ was successfully synthesized at room temperature with indirect bandgap 0.39 eV having higher carrier mobility (Yang et al. 2017). The bandgap has been tuned up to 2.6 eV for the quantum dot size (Hong et al. 2018). Furthermore, calculation for the same with good agreement of the bandgap and dynamic stability in the experiment has been reported (Hu et al. 2012; Wang et al. 2017). It is interesting that arsenene monolayer has a wide indirect bandgap and high stability (Zhang et al. 2015) instead of that bandgap is tuned by external electric field and strain (Shu et al. 2017); as a result, it is switched into a direct bandgap (Zhang et al. 2017). Therefore, we studied the dynamical stability of monolayer $C_3As$ to confirm the stability, and then, we have focused on the electronic and optical properties of each monolayer separately. Meanwhile, the methodology, electronic, and optical properties of $C_3As$/arsenene heterostructure in vertical strain and external electric field are noted. As well as also investigated the bandgap modulation and change in optical absorption under vertical strain and external electric field.

## Methodology

The electronic and optical properties have been calculated using computational method based on density functional theory (DFT). The non-relativistic generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) parametrization (Perdew et al. 1996) under the Quantum Espresso (QE) simulation code (Giannozzi et al. 2009) for the exchange–correlation effects. We have used the semi-empirical DFT-D3 formalism method (Grimme et al. 2010; Kansara et al. 2018c) for define vdW-energy expression, because the standard PBE functional is inefficient to define accurately weak correlation effects. The plane-wave kinetic energy cutoff has been taken 400 eV for the electron wave function and Monkhorst–Pack $k$-point mesh is set as $25×25×1$ for the primitive unit cell of monolayer, while $16×16×1$ has been taken for the bilayer system. We implemented the tetrahedron method and Blöch corrections (Blöchl et al. 1994) for all the presenting calculation expect structure optimization and phonon calculations by first-order Methfessel–Paxton (MP) method (Methfessel and Paxton 1989) using 0.005 eV smearing. The structure relaxation convergence criteria of energy, ionic force, and stress tensor reached up to $1.0×10^{-5}$ eV, $<10^{-4}$ eV/Å, and $10^{-3}$ GPa, respectively, and ensured that the structure configuration was globally stabilized. This convergence force and dynamical matrix of the system is used for the phonon calculation. We took 20 Å vacuum perpendicular to the surface to avoid the interaction from the replicated system. The calculated optical properties have been found out through the complex dielectric constant, and the methods and the formulas were described in recent previously reported works (Bhuyan et al. 2017; Shah et al. 2019; Kansara et al. 2017a, b; Singh et al. 2018).

The lattice mismatch of optimized heterostructure is 2.8%, which is negligible. ### Effect of vertical strain

The strain consequence to the electronic properties of $C_3$As/arsenene heterostructure, the band structures are plotted in Fig. 4a for different four cases. Earlier, we have discussed that the basic bandgap of heterolayer is 1.37 eV indirect gap. Here, we applied the vertical strain as reduced the interlayer distance in the range from $-1.0$ to $1.0$ Å. It is found that as applying vertical strain, the bandgaps are tuned from indirect to direct bands as well as increase in the bandgap.

As on increasing and decreasing the interlayer distance, the bandgap is going to change. From Fig. 2 of partial density of states plot, it is clear that the $p_z$ orbital of arsenic atom has predominated contributed near the Fermi level, while in range of $-0.7$ to $-1.0$ eV, $p_y$ orbital of arsenic (As) atom has partially predominated. After that, $p_z$ orbital of carbon (C) atom predominates up to $-2.0$ eV in the valence band. In the conduction band, $p_z$ orbital of C and As is mainly dominant after that partially $p_y$ orbital of C atom. By applying the vertical strain, the contribution of p orbital of carbon atom is going to be dominant when compared to the p orbital of arsenene. As well as, for the $0.8$ Å strain, $p_z$ orbital of arsenene is more dominate near the Fermi level of valence band and shows the direct bandgap 1.56 eV. The strain dependent PDOS has been show in ESI part, as shown in Fig. S1. From Fig. 4d of $0.8$ Å, the charge transfer between the layers is negligible. The conduction band, p orbital of C and As atoms, share similar states, so there might be possibility of the existence of orbital hybridization occurred between both layers for $-0.2$ to $-1.0$ Å. The different charge redistributions between the layers show the different bandgap variations, as shown in Fig. 4a, d. Due to the high electro-negativity of arsenene, there might be a possibility of charge transfer from $C_3$As to the As monolayer at the time they are close to each other. During the decrease of distance between layers, the weak ionic interaction has been presented along with the weak vdW interaction. To clear about the charge, transfer, and bonding mechanism between $C_3$As and As monolayers, the charge density difference plays a significant role to perform difference of layers, as shown in Fig. 4d. It is noted that as varying the interlayer distance of heterolayer, the distribution of charges is remarkably observed.

In Fig. 4b, the binding energies and bandgaps of heterostructure along with different interlayer distances have been reported. From Fig. 4b, it is clear that the behaviour of binding energies and bandgaps is exactly vice versa with each other. As there is decrease in the bandgap, the binding energy is increased. ## Effect of external electric field

Vertical external electric field is one of the additional unique approach to tune the electronic properties of the heterostructure (Song et al. 2016; Shu et al. 2017; Zeng et al. 2018).

Therefore, we have examined the effect of external electric field to the electronic properties of C₃As/arsenene heterostructure, as shown in Fig. 5. From Fig. 5a, it is clear that as applying positive and negative electric fields, the bandgap of the system is decreased for both the cases. Due to the charges in the charge transfer, the electronic properties have been changed during the application of electric field. When applying the positive external electric field, the bandgaps decreased from 1.32 to 0.45 eV for $0$–$1.0$ V/Å. In addition, when negative electric field is applied, it is decreased from 1.32 to 0.29 eV for $0$ to $-1.0$ V/Å. The external electric field to the heterostructure affects the symmetry of the geometry that responsible the bandgap structure is substantially changed (Balu et al. 2012). The above discussion of the bandgap tuning could be understood by the Stark effect (Huang and Li 2016). The PDOS under various electric fields have been shown in ESI part, as shown in Fig. S2. Due to the shifting of the CBM edge to the VBM during the applied electric field, the bandgap is reduced in a linear way. The applied external electric field induced more electron as charge density at the upper layer of arsenene, as shown in Fig. 5c. Therefore, the rising of the

## Conclusions

In summary, the vertical strain not only can tune the bandgaps, but also change the indirect-to-direct semiconducting transition. Here, the electronic and optical properties of heterostructure $C_3As$/arsenene under the vertical strain and external electric field have been discussed. We have reported the new dynamically stable monolayer $C_3As$ with indirect 2.31 eV bandgap and examine the dielectric properties, optical absorption in UV region, and refractive index as 1.8. The bandgaps are tuned from 0.75 to 1.6 eV (indirect-to-direct transition under vertical strain) and 1.32–0.25 eV (under external electric field). As well as, small effective mass is directly predicting the larger carrier mobility of the system. Furthermore, the optical absorption has the highly present in the UV region and weak absorption in the visible region. Thus, modulation suggests the great structure tunability for the potential application of heterostructure in UV photodetector devices.
