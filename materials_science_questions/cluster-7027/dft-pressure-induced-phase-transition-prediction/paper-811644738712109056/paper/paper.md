# Structural Behaviour of Uranium Sulfide under High Pressure

F.Shareef, S.Singh, A. Gour, P.Bhardwaj, M. Sarwan, R. Dubey and R.K.Singh*

High Pressure Research Lab, Department of Physics, Barkatullah University, Bhopal-462026, India.
*ITM University, Gurgaon, Haryana-122017, India.

**Abstract.** The study of pressure induced structural phase transition of uranium sulphide, which crystallizes in rock salt (B1) structure, has been performed using the well described three body interaction model (TBIPM). Our present TBIP model consists of long range coulombic interaction, three body interactions, Hafemeister and Flygare type short–range overlap repulsion extended upto the second neighbor ions and the van der Waals interaction. The present results are in good agreement with the available experimental data on the phase transition pressure (Pt= 80.2 GPa). So it can be considered as an adequate and suitable model to perform high pressure studies.

**Keywords:** Structural phase transition, Cohesive energy, Actinide compounds.
**PACS:** 61.50.Ks, 61.50.Lt, 61.66.f

## INTRODUCTION

The study of the high pressure structural behaviour of actinide compounds has been the object of considerable and keen interest in solid state physics. Most of the actinide monochalcogenides and pnictides crystallize in NaCl- type structure at ambient pressure and their f- electrons are localized. The majority of these compounds undergo a structural phase transition from the six fold-coordinated rock salt (B1) structure to the eight fold-coordinated cesium chloride structure at high pressure [1]. A phenomenon of interest in these compounds is the hybridization of 5f electrons with conduction electrons which is an important parameter and leads to complex properties such as Kondo -like, heavy fermions or intermediate valence behavior and also responsible for their unusual properties [1-3]. Among these compounds the uranium compounds are characterized by a more delocalized 5f state. The US shows a phase transition from B1 to B2 structure around 80 GPa in a silicone oil medium [3].

In recent years the induced phase transition properties of actinide compounds have been studied widely by using the different theoretical methods. The previous workers have analyzed the phase transition and elastic properties of actinide monochalcogenides and pnictides within the frame work of two body potential model. But our present three body potential model results show close agreement to experimental value available and is better than the result of others [4] who performed two body potential model.

## METHOD OF COMPUTATION

The application of pressure on crystals results in compression which leads to an increased charge transfer (many body interaction effects) [5] due to the deformation of the overlapping electron shells of the adjacent ions in solids. These effects have been incorporated in the Gibbs free energy ($G=U+PV-TS$) as a function of pressure and three body interactions. Here U is the internal energy which at 0K corresponds to the cohesive energy (lattice energy), S is the vibrational entropy at absolute T, pressure P and volume V. The Gibbs free energies $G_{B1}$ for the NaCl (B1) and $G_{B2}$ for the CsCl (B2) phases are given by,

$$
G_{B1} \left( \right) = U_{B1} \left( \right) + PV_{B1} \tag{1}
$$

$$
G_{B2} \left( \right) = U_{B2} \left( \right) + PV_{B2} \tag{2}
$$

With $V_{B1}$ (=2.00 $\text{r}^3$) and $V_{B2}$ (=1.54 $\text{r}^3$) as the unit cell volumes for B1 and B2 phases respectively. The condition for the pressure induced phase transformation is achieved at absolute zero of temperature because the energy description is not sufficiently sophisticated to take care of the vibrational contribution to entropy at other temperatures. At absolute zero, the Gibbs free energy (G) is the enthalpy (H) itself.
