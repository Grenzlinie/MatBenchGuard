# High-Pressure Phase Behavior of Alumina: Predictions of a Transferable Ionic Potential Model

Mark Wilson

Physical and Theoretical Chemistry Laboratory, Oxford University, Oxford OX1 3QZ, United Kingdom

A recently introduced potential model for alumina has been used to study a wider range of structures than previously attempted. The model contains both the spherical relaxation of, and induced dipoles and quadrupoles on, the oxide anions. The model is applied to the possible high-pressure phase transition from the ground-state corundum structure to the $\text{Rh}_2\text{O}_3$-II structure. A transition pressure of 194 GPa has been predicted, which is in excellent agreement with the most-recent experimental results. The spherical relaxation and induced quadrupoles are crucial in effectively modeling the transition. The shortcomings of the model are assessed by comparison with experiment and both *ab initio* and model calculations. Other structures common in this stoichiometry have also been considered.

## I. Introduction
$I^N$ A recent paper, $^1$ a new potential model was introduced to describe alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$). It was shown that a proper description of the spherical relaxation ("breathing") of, and induced quadrupoles on, the $\text{O}^{2-}$ anions was required to produce a more transferable model. This transferability was tested against both experimental observations and specific *ab initio* calculations, focusing on three polymorphs: corundum ($\alpha$-$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$, the experimentally observed ground state), bixbyite (C-type lanthanum oxide ($\text{Ln}_2\text{O}_3$), erroneously favored by previous shell models$^{2,3}$), and $\theta$-$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ (simply termed hereafter as $\theta$). The latter was included because the structure includes both three- and four-coordinated oxides (corundum and bixbyite have only four coordinate anions), which allows coordination-number-dependent properties to be assessed. Using these three relatively simple structures, the effects of the induced quadrupoles and the spherical relaxation were assessed. The induced quadrupoles stabilized the corundum structure rather than the bixbyite structure, because the oxide ions sit in larger field gradients (primarily as a result of the more-distorted local tetrahedron of cations that surround each anion), which leads to larger quadrupoles. For these three structures, the anion quadrupoles were identified as being crucial for the stabilization of the corundum structure instead of the bixbyite structure, whereas the spherical relaxation and quadrupoles both were responsible for reproducing the relative $\theta$/corundum energetics. The inclusion of anion compression is necessary, because recent models have demonstrated how a single effective pair potential (EPP) is fundamentally untransferable for oxide systems.$^{4-7}$ In this case, for example, a single EPP plus anion quadrupoles would predict the $\theta$ structure as being the ground state, which reflects an artificial stabilization of the lower coordinate structure by the EPP. The quadrupoles were included via a polarizable-ion model$^{7-9}$ and the anion spherical relaxation was included via a compressible ion model (CIM).$^4$ The full CIM, which includes both anion dipoles and quadrupoles, is termed the CIM-DQ.

In this paper, the scope of the potential model investigation is extended to examine other crystal structures found for this stoichiometry. The reasons for doing this are threefold. Firstly, a primary motivation for developing such models was to allow for the study of systems beyond the scope of current *ab initio* methods (for example, complex grain boundaries and surfaces$^{10}$). The study of a wider range of crystal structures, which involves a variety of different crystalline environments of differing symmetry, is another step along this path. Secondly, there is ongoing debate in the literature$^{11,12}$ as to whether the corundum structure undergoes a pressure-driven phase transition to a $\text{Rh}_2\text{O}_3$-II structure and, if so, at what pressure it actually occurs. Such a transition has important implications, in terms of the geophysics in the mantle and the use of alumina in high-pressure experiments. Thirdly, an understanding of the relative stabilities of the various polymorphs will be crucial in understanding how the potential-model parameters transfer in this stoichiometry. The transferability has already been demonstrated for the simpler MO stoichiometry (where M represents the metal cation and O represents the oxygen anion).$^{5,6,13}$ The generation of a model in which the parameters transfer in a physically transparent way is the main motivation behind our overall strategy. It is anticipated that the lower site symmetries that result in nonvanishing electric fields and gradients will make the transferability more complicated in the $\text{M}_2\text{O}_3$ stoichiometry. The additional structures considered here are the $\text{Rh}_2\text{O}_3$-II structure,$^{14}$ as well as the A- and B-type $\text{Ln}_2\text{O}_3$ structures (hereafter termed A and B structures, respectively) that are favored by the larger-cation systems with this stoichiometry.$^{15}$

The major shortcoming of the published shell models$^{2,3}$ is the inability to predict the corundum ground state without the use of nonphysical dispersion effects that act between $\text{Al}^{3+}$-$\text{Al}^{3+}$ pairs. This inability has led to somewhat of an impasse in the atomistic modeling in that, although much effort has been put into modeling $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$, it has been largely concentrated on this specific problem.$^3$

The potential-induced breathing model (PIB)$^{16}$ goes beyond the shell model by allowing for the spherical relaxation of the anion charge density calculated via a Watson sphere method. To date, two versions have been tested on the various possible $\text{M}_2\text{O}_3$ structures, based on the Thomas-Fermi (PIB-TF) and the Kohn-Sham (PIB-KS) kinetic-energy functionals.$^{11}$ The PIB model and the current CIM model (in the absence of induced dipoles and quadrupoles) are equivalent in the sense of including the spherical relaxation of the anions, although different in both the parameterization and implementation. For example, previous work that has focused on zirconia ($\text{ZrO}_2$)$^7$ has shown that the two models give similar results for coordination-number-dependent properties, when compared with results based on simple EPPs.

Previous work$^1$ has indicated that the anion *quadrupoles* (the response of the ion electron density to the field gradient) are largely responsible for the stabilization of the corundum structure rather than the bixbyite structure. Inter- estingly, the electric field at these sites is vanishingly small, as a result of the distortions of both the anion and cation sublat- tices. This observation means that the induced dipoles are themselves very small, with the result that the quadrupoles are "projected" into a greater level of significance than might be expected.

The local coordination around the bixbyite structure is much more similar to an ideal tetrahedral geometry, which leads to only small induced dipoles and quadrupoles. Because the co- rundum and bixbyite structures are both based exclusively on four-coordinated anions, the effect of allowing for the anion spherical relaxation (the compressible-ion effect) then is much less significant than the difference in induced quadrupoles that results from the local distortions of the first coordination shell. In the CIM model, this breathing is handled by dividing the short-range anion-cation energy into a rearrangement energy (the energy to place an $O^{-}$ ion plus an electron into the con densed environment) and an overlap term (the energy of over- lap with the nearest-neighbor cations). An EPP combines these two terms together into a single function that is dependent solely on anion-cation separation. However, as has been shown previously, $^{4,7}$ the two CIM energy terms vary differently with coordination number and, as a result, an EPP that is determined at one particular coordination number will be inapplicable at another-it is fundamentally untransferable. However, the co- rundum and bixbyite structures are based on the same coordi- nation number; therefore, this situation is not an issue for that pair, although it will be for the other systems under consider- ation. The implication of this analysis is that the bixbyite struc- ture is the true ground state in the PIB, although it was omitted in that study.

Recently, ab initio linear-augmented-plane-wave (LAPW) calculations that focus on the corundum $\to Rh_{2} O_{3}$ -II transition have been performed. These calculations highlight the differ- ences with the CIM and PIB models. $^{12}$ The LAPW calculations include all the significant anion distortions and, therefore, are expected to yield the correct ground state. Again, the bixbyite structure is not considered in this work. However, recent cal- culations using the local density approximation (LDA) indeed show the corundum to be the ground state, as long as a suffi-ciently expansive basis set is used. $^{1}$

### (1) Corundum $\to$ Rh₂O₃-II Pressure Transition
Table III lists the predicted transition pressures ($P_t$) from our models compared with the $ab$ initio (LAPW)¹² and the PIB models.¹¹ The PIB model, which includes the anion spherical relaxation, predicts a value of 6 or 62 GPa, depending upon whether the KS or TF kinetic-energy functional is used. This apparent large variation is simply a function of the almost-parallel free-energy-pressure ($G$-$P$) curves (see, for example, Fig. 2), which causes small model changes to result in large $P_t$ fluctuations.

In the absence of the induced quadrupoles, the CIM model predicts a relatively low pressure transition at 18 GPa, in the region of those predicted by the PIB model. This result is again consistent with the CIM and PIB models that contain the same basic aspects of the ionic physics already noted for ZrO₂.⁷ The addition of the induced quadrupoles greatly increases the value of $P_t$: the quadrupoles act to stabilize the corundum structure rather than the Rh₂O₃-II structure. The effect of the quadru- poles is also clearly shown in the volume reductions during the transition. In the absence of these effects, the volume is re- duced by 3.6%, compared with only 1.7% with quadrupoles.

Comparison with the experiment is difficult. The more- recent experiments indicate a transition at ~190–200 GPa.¹⁷,¹⁸ Mao *et al.*¹⁷ found the R₁ ruby peak to vanish at ≈190 GPa, whilst Yoo *et al.*¹⁸ observed an increase in the thermal emis- sion at ≈200 GPa. However, Xu *et al.*²³ found corundum to be stable up to 550 GPa, whilst older data suggests transitions at pressures as low as 15 GPa.¹⁹ Clearly, the extreme pressures that are involved make experimental observation of the pres- sure transition difficult. However, one might reasonably expect the more-recent results to be more reliable, thus indicating a value of 190–200 GPa. However, there may be significant kinetic barriers to overcome for this transformation that render its experimental observation difficult.

Table IV lists the bulk moduli calculated using the various models. The CIM-DQ bulk modulus is in less good agreement with the experiment and the LAPW model. This observation reflects the problems that are associated with the parameter- ization of the quadrupole portion of the potential model. As discussed in an earlier reference,¹ a ‘‘complete’’ model would use the full quadrupole polarizability ($C$), which would then be effectively dampened by the short-range overlap interactions with the nearest-neighbor cations in the manner used for the induced dipoles.⁶ The problem is that relatively little is known about such short-range interactions for the quadrupoles beyond the fact that they would act to dampen the coulombic induced moment. As a result, our initial approximation was to simply neglect the short-range effect but use a *reduced* value for $C$, which mimics the overall effect. A $C$ value of 6 au has been observed to give good relative energetics for the corundum structure when compared to the bixbyite and $\theta$ structures. The ‘‘full’’ $C$ value is ~26 au (the MgO $ab$ initio value²⁰). This approximation may be less valid away from the equilibrium volumes because the balance between the overlap and coulom- bic-induced quadrupoles will change and the overlap will be- come more significant as the ion becomes more compressed. This condition is unlikely to be correctly represented by using a small $C$ value. As a result, the current potential may show signs of losing transferability via significant errors in the bulk moduli in systems in which the quadrupole moments are sig- nificant. As a result, the errors associated with the corundum structure are larger than those of any of the other structures considered. The Rh₂O₃-II bulk modulus, for example, is in good agreement with the LAPW value.

The comparison with *both* PIB models (i.e., parameterized using different energy functionals) is also interesting. The best bulk moduli agreement is between the basic CIM model and the PIB-TF model. However, as noted earlier, the best agree- ment, in terms of the relative energetics, was between the CIM model and the PIB-KS model. This observation is consistent with the comments of Cynn *et al.*,¹¹ who noted that the use of the Kohn-Sham kinetic-energy functional leads to better agree- ment with the experiment, in terms of structural and dynamical properties, whilst the use of the Thomas-Fermi functional gives a better equation of state and better elastic properties. The basic CIM model is in agreement with the *best* PIB model in each case.

The corundum $\to Rh_2O_3$-II pressure-driven phase transition is relatively unusual in that there is no significant associated change in the anion–cation coordination number. A possible explanation for the high-pressure stability of the $Rh_2O_3$-II phase lies in the packing of the $Al^{3+}$ sublattice. The corundum structure is particularly favorable because it allows the more highly charged $Al^{3+}$ cations to occupy sites that maximize the Al–Al separations (the nearest-neighbor Al–Al separation is greater than the corresponding O–O separation; the system is charge-ordered). The apparent complexity of the structure (in comparison to typical MO or $MO_2$ structures) reflects the ‘‘awkwardness’’ of the $M_2O_3$ stoichiometry. In the corundum structure, the cations occupy two-thirds of the available octa- hedral holes in the anion sublattice. In the absence of any sublattice distortions, the nearest-neighbor Al–Al separation equals that of the O–O pair. As a result, the distortions in the two sublattices (in particular in the Al sublattice) are crucial in stabilizing this structure. The distortion of the Al sublattice is driven by the occupied/occupied/empty ordering of octahedral holes in the crystallographic $c$-direction. As a result, each cat- ion is pushed toward one side of the octahedral hole (toward a neighboring hole in the $c$-direction but away from an occupied site), resulting in two pairs of triply degenerate anion–cation nearest-neighbor separations. The application of pressure natu- rally results in a reduction in all bond lengths between the different ion pairs. However, the greater charge on the cations makes the Al–Al pairs more resistant to such compression. The $Rh_2O_3$-II structure represents a relaxation of the Al sublattice on a next-nearest-neighbor level. If we consider Fig. 5 in detail, we see that the Al–Al pair (numbered 1 and 2, straddling an oxide triangle) have more-distant neighbors (3 and 4) sand- wiched between adjacent oxide layers. In the $Rh_2O_3$-II struc- ture, the analogous pair (again 1 and 2) have cation 3 as the corundum but cation 4 is further away, as a result of the inter- vening $(10\overline{1}1)$ grain boundary. This relaxation of the Al sub- lattice favors the $Rh_2O_3$-II structure at high pressure and allows the nearest-neighbor cation separations to remain very similar in the two systems (2.90 Å in the corundum structure, com- pared to 2.80 Å in the $Rh_2O_3$-II structure).

Earlier work $^{1}$ highlighted the differences in the total disper sion energy between some different structures. Such differ- ences may be significant, because dispersion is not included in the PIB or LAPW calculations. Differences in the dispersion energy occur in $Al_2O_3$ in particular because it is dominated by the $O^{2-}-O^{2-}$ term, because the $Al^{3+}$ cation is relatively unpo larizable. Hence, the total dispersion energy is largely depen- dent on the packing of the oxide sublattice: the higher the anion–cation coordination number, the closer the packing. This condition has been shown to be significant for, for example, the corundum–$\theta$ energy difference, because these systems are based on different mean coordination numbers. The ‘‘correc- tion’’ of the $ab$ initio energy differences brings them into al- most-perfect agreement with the CIM-DQ values $^{1}$ (Table I). In the case of the pressure transition to the $Rh_2O_3$-II structure, the effect is only on the order of 5 kJ/mol, because this structure is only slightly more close-packed than the corundum structure.

### (2) Expansion to Other Structures

We now extend our discussion to include the other structures that have been considered. The basic CIM model again gives similar results to the PIB model, both in terms of the relative energetics and the equilibrium volumes (Tables I and II). An interesting conclusion is, as mentioned in the introduction, that the PIB model may well also predict the bixbyite ground state (this structure was not considered by Cynn $et\ al.^{11}$). Because the PIB model and the basic CIM model compare well in sev- eral other aspects, this seems to be a reasonable conclusion.

The inclusion of the induced quadrupoles widens the energy gap between the corundum and the A- and B-type structures, reflecting the large quadrupole stabilization energy that is as-

sociated with the corundum structure. The CIM-DQ equilib- rium volumes for the A and B structures are smaller than both PIB models, which indicates a slightly different transferability between the CIM and PIB models.

The quadrupole stabilization effects have a tendency to favor the lower-coordinated systems. The $\theta$ structure experiences the largest stabilization by virtue of the three-coordinated oxide sites $^{1}$ also known to be important in predicting the ground-state $ZrO_{2}.^{7}$ The corundum structure has significant anion quadru poles because the four-coordinated anion local symmetry is between tetrahedral and square planar. $^{1}$ The A and B structures have relatively small quadrupole effects, because of the higher anion-coordination numbers; both structures are based on $MO_{7}$  units. $^{15}$ Generally, the higher the coordination, the more the nearest-neighbor interactions behave as if they have been spherically averaged. In the A-type structure, these units are equivalent, leaving two-thirds of the oxides almost-tetrahe- drally coordinated and one-third almost-octahedrally coordi- nated. The high symmetry of these sites precludes both signifi- cant dipole and quadrupole polarization effects. The B-type structure is more complex, with one-ninth of the oxide sites being six-coordinated and the others being evenly divided be- tween five- and four-coordinated. the local anion environments are still relatively symmetric, which leads to in- significant polarization effects. A consequence of this is that the structural chemistry of the larger-cation $M_{2}O_{3}$ systems should be dominated by simpler packing arguments rather than the more-complex polarization effects that must be considered for the lower-coordinated (smaller-cation) systems. This result is analogous to the situation observed in the $MO_{2}$ stoichiometry in which the dipolar induction effects are critical in obtaining accurate models for silica $(SiO_{2})$ and germania $(GeO_{2})$ , which have small (and, therefore, low-coordinated) cations, whilst simple, charged, hard-sphere-packing arguments account for the static structures of the larger-cation systems such as ura-nium dioxide $(UO_{2})$ and ceria $(CeO_{2})^{6}$ 

In earlier work, $^{1}$ it was noted how the CIM model had a tendency to stabilize the higher-coordinated systems, with re- spect to using an effective pair potential; this is a general prop- erty of this type of representation. $^{4-7}$ A single EPP (appropriate for a single coordination number) systematically overestimates the short-range repulsion energy for higher-coordinated envi- ronments while similarly understanding the energy of the lower-coordinated systems. This phenomenon has been traced to the differing dependencies of the energy that is associatedwith the spherical relaxation (the rearrangement or self energy)and the overlap of the anion with its nearest-neighbor cations. $^{4}$  As a result, the A- and B-type structures both are destabilized, with respect to both the corundum and bixbyite structures, by virtue of the greater average anion-coordination number. The relatively high site symmetry in the A- and B-type structures, coupled with the high coordination numbers, means that this compressible-ion effect will be much more significant in de- termining the relative energetics of the lanthanide oxides than will polarization effects. This significance will be the subject of future investigation.

### III. Conclusions

In this paper, we have extended the potential model calcu- lations that were started in previous work. We have given particular attention to two aspects. Firstly, we have included the $A-Ln_{2}O_{3}, B-Ln_{2}O_{3}$ , and bixbyite $(C-Ln_{2}O_{3})$ structures that are adopted by larger-cation systems in this stoichiometry. This effort lays the groundwork for future studies that will identify how these potentials transfer in this stoichiometry, a procedure that is already well defined for the simpler MO stoichiometry in which polarization effects are much less prevalent. Sec- ondly, we have examined the possible pressure-driven phase transition to a $Rh_{2}O_{3}$ -II structure. Previous models have pre dicted a wide range of pressures. Our value of 194 GPa is in good agreement with the most-recent experimental data, which predict transitions between 190 and 200 GPa, although this data give no insight into the kinetics of the transformation. The current model is also in good agreement with previous ab initio and model calculations. The Al sublattice is thought to be critical in the stabilization of the $Rh_{2}O_{3}$ -II structure at high pressure.
