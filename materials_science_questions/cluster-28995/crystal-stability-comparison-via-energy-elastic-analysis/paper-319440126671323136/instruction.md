# Metallic Hydrogen Energy Structure Ordering via Many-Body Theory

## Problem background
Metallic hydrogen has attracted intense attention due to its predicted high-temperature superconductivity and the possibility of a metastable phase at ambient pressure. Determining the lowest-energy crystal structure at zero pressure is a fundamental step toward understanding whether such a phase can exist. This requires computing the total energy of the metallic state as a function of the ion arrangement using a reliable quantum-mechanical treatment of the interacting electron–ion system.

## Approach
The total energy is described within the many-body theory of simple metals developed by Brovman and Kagan (JETP 1967–1971). In this framework the energy per ion is expressed as a functional of the crystal structure (lattice vectors and ion positions) and the electron density parameter rₛ. The functional includes both electron–ion and ion–ion interaction contributions. By implementing this energy functional, one can evaluate the total energy for any candidate crystal configuration at a given rₛ. The workflow then computes the energy landscape for a set of high-symmetry lattices and for two families of structures that are generated by a continuous shear deformation: the triangular family (derived from simple hexagonal) and the square family (derived from simple tetragonal). For each family the shear parameter is sampled to locate the configuration that minimizes the total energy per ion.

## Reproduction target
Compute the total energy per ion for metallic hydrogen at an electron density rₛ ≈ 1.7 for the following candidate structures: simple cubic, bcc, fcc, hcp, simple hexagonal, diamond, and β‑Sn. For the triangular and square structure families, sample the shear parameter over an appropriate range and determine the configuration with the lowest energy. Output a single JSON file containing the minimum total energy for each family and each simple lattice. The goal is to verify the relative ordering of these energies.

## How you are scored
Your submission is evaluated by a hidden verifier that reads the file `/app/outputs/energy_ordering.json`. The verifier checks that the structural relationships among the reported energies satisfy strict inequalities: the minimum energy for the triangular family must be lower than the minimum energy for the square family, which in turn must be lower than the energy of every other structure listed (simple hexagonal, fcc, bcc, hcp, etc.). The verifier does not compare absolute energy values to any reference; it only enforces the required ordering within a small numerical tolerance to account for floating‑point differences. Each stage of the workflow contributes to the final reward, with the scoring of `energy_ordering.json` carrying the most weight. Providing the correct ordering — not matching any particular number — is what earns full credit.
