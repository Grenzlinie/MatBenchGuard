The role of $d$-electrons in the structural phase stability of bcc transition metals viz. V, Fe, Cr and Mn are investigated. The underlying theory expresses the relevant structural part of the free energy in terms of the repulsion of the $d$-electron muffin-tin orbitals assigned to atomic sites and the attractive contribution arising from the band broadening effects of the $d$-bands in the total energy. The magnetic contribution arising from the population of magnetic moments in the systems is also included in the theory. The $d$-electronic contribution to entropy is written in terms of the density-of-electronic states at the respective Fermi level. The phase stability of the bcc transition metals is explained in terms of the population of atoms on the local and extended sites. It is observed that the $d$-electron energetics can precisely and correctly predict the crystal structure of the bcc transition metals.

## 1. Introduction

Investigation of the structural properties of transition metals has been at the heart of metallurgy and materials science for the last two decades. $^{1}$ The presence of the incomplete $d$ electron orbitals in the atomic state of these metals is essentially playing the most important roles in their physical and chemical properties. $^{2}$ Because of these incomplete electron orbitals, a strong scattering situation persists in these systems and consequently the first principles calculations $^{3}$ based on non-perturbative treatment and self-consistency have been playing a dominant and logical role in predicting the crystal structures of these non-simple metals. $^{4}$ However, the involvement of too many fine details in the non-perturbative treatment very often fails to pin point the core cause responsible in controlling the cohesion and so the structural prediction of these metals. The recent investigation by Osman and Rahman $^{5}$ (also see references therein) reveals that a perturbative treatment with all its

simplicity can successfully calculate a good number of structural and thermody- namic properties of these metals.

In the present attempt we concentrate solely on the role of $d$ electrons in pre- dicting the crystal structures of the bcc transition metals viz. V, Fe, Cr and Mn.⁰ We employ the $d$-electron pair potential derived by Wills and Harrison⁶ based on the concept of band hybridization and bending of atomic bonds inherent to the systems concerned. It is relevant to mention here that while the repulsive part of the $s$-$p$ bonded systems is approximated merely by a coulombic interaction, the counter-part of the $d$-electron systems is approximated by the non-orthogonality of the $d$-electron muffin-tin orbitals assigned to the neighboring atomic sites. On the other hand while the indirect part of the $s$-$p$ bonded systems is assumed as an ion-ion interaction in the presence of an electron cloud, the counter part of a $d$-electron system is assumed to arise from the band broadening contribution⁶ that accounts for the cohesion in transition metals.

The main objective of the present investigation is to look for the phase stability of the bcc transition metals from the local and extended atomic occupations. The matching between the minima of the $d$-electron pair potential mentioned as above and the locations of the first few lattice vectors of the underlying lattice manifest the local effects, while consideration of the global atomic occupation in the entire crystal manifests the extended effects. It is also important to mention here that while the Wills-Harrison⁶ $d$-electron pair potential considers only the nearest neighbors, in the present investigation we have extended our calculations to go beyond the nearest neighbor approximation. Alongside we have also looked at the roles of the magnetic contribution in the structural energetics of the systems concerned.

The layout of the paper is as follows: in Sec. 2 we present the relevant formula- tion. Result are presented and discussed in Sec. 3. Finally some concluding remarks are presented in Sec. 4.

## 2. Formulation

We consider a crystalline transition metal at temperature $T$ with the effective $d$-electron valency $Z_d$ and the $d$-state radius $R_d$ having $N_c$ number of nearest neighbors. The $d$-electron contribution to the effective free energy per atom can be written as:⁵

$$
F_d=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{R \neq 0}^{N} U_{d}(R)-T S_{d} \tag{1}
$$

where $R$ is a lattice vector of the underlying lattice and $N$ denotes the number of atoms on a shell. In Eq. (1) $U_d$ is the effective $d$-electron pair potential given by (in Rydberg):

⁰Mn at room temperature corresponds to a complicated bct structure undergoing a martensitic phase transformation at low temperature range. In the limit of high temperatures, since the c/a ratio for Mn approaches a near-unity value, we have considered a virtual bcc structure as an approximation for it.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{d}= & -\left[Z_{d}\left(1-\frac{Z_{d}}{10}\right)\left(\frac{12}{N_{c}}\right)^{1 / 2}\left(\frac{56.12}{\pi}\right) \frac{R_{d}^{3}}{R^{5}}\right] \\
& +\left[\left(\frac{450}{\pi^{2}}\right) \frac{Z_{d} R_{d}^{6}}{R^{8}}\right]+\left[\frac{\left(\frac{1}{137}\right)^{1 / 2}}{R^{3}}\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

where in the second term the sum over $1/R^5$ must be replaced by the sum over $\{1/R^{10}\}^{1/2}$. The effective $d$-electron entropy $S_d$ is given by$^7$

$$
S_{d}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3} k_{\mathrm{B}}^{2} n_{d}\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right) T,
\tag{3}
$$

with $k_{\mathrm{B}}$, the Boltzmann constant and $n_d$ the density of $d$-electron states at the Fermi level.

The first term in Eq. (2) accounts for the attractive part of the pair potential arising from the Friedel band broadening contribution to the cohesion of transition metals.$^8$ The second term arises from the overlap of the $d$-electron muffin-tin orbitals assigned to the various atomic sites; this term precisely takes care of the repulsion due to the non-orthogonality of states concerned. The third term accounts for the dipole-dipole interaction$^9$ inherent to all transition metals at finite temperatures.

Now looking at the accumulated free energies on the various shell we note that the free energies for almost all of the systems start converging at around the 10th

shell; the prediction is, however, dominated by the first few shells only. This fea-
ture reveals that the phase stability of the transition metals concerned is by and
large controlled by the mode of local population of the atomic sites and not by the
extended population; the same conclusion applies for the cohesion as well. It is rel-
evant to mention here that the calculated transition metal band structures indicate
that the $d$-band not only lies high up in the conduction band but in general extends
through the Fermi enegy; this spread up of the $d$-derived levels lies at the heart
of the tight binding approximation appropriate for the transition metal cohesion.
The domination of the structural prediction by only a few lattice shells is possibly
a supplementary feature of this tight binding approximation.

The pair potentials used in the calculations are essentially based on the
nearest-neighbor approximation.⁶ We have extended our calculations to all distant
neighbors by replacing the coordination number $N_{\rm c}$ by the actual numbers of lattice
sites $N$ on all the distant shells. The results for free energies and their differences
between structures are shown in Fig. 2. A careful look shows that the structural
prediction for all the systems remain unaffected but the energies converge more
quickly than that by the nearest-neighbor approximation. This is expected because
the distant shells are more populated in most cases than the first shell.

The role of the magnetic term in the present form does not have any visible
effect on the structural prediction; this is primarily because the magnitude of the
magnetic term is negligibly small. It may be relevant to mention here that V is
paramagnetic, Fe is ferromagnetic while Cr and Mn are itinerant antiferromagnets.
This classification will naturally inherit different magnetic theory for the different
element but the direct dipolar interaction will remain the same for all cases.

## 4. Conclusions

The $d$-electron pair potentials have been employed to extract the roles of $d$ electrons
on the structural phase stability of the bcc transition metals. We would like to make
the following concluding remarks in the light of the present calculations:

(1) The calculations have correctly predicted the crystal structures of the bcc tran-
sition metals within the nearest-neighbor and extended shell approximations.
The energy differences are of the same order as was obtained by using the $s$-$p$-$d$
pair potentials;⁵ this reveals that these are the $d$-electrons which practically
decide the structural predictions while the $s$-$p$ electrons only supplement these.

(2) The display of energies and their differences between crystal structures ex-
hibit a transparent view of the mode of convergence of these quantities on
the surrounding atomic shells. Unlike the $s$-$p$ bonded systems¹¹ the structural
predictions are dominated primarily by atoms in the first few lattice shells.
This is expected because the $d$-electron pair potentials converge quickly and
monotonically.
