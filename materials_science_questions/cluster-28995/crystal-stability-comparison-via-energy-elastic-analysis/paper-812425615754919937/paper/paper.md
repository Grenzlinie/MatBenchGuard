## ABSTRACT
The ferromagnetic and nonmagnetic phases of 2-D Wigner electron crystal are investigated using a localized representation for the electrons. The ground-state energies of ferromagnetic and nonmagnetic phases of 2-D Wigner electron crystal are computed in the range of $r_{s}=10$–200. The low density favorable for Wigner crystallization is found to be $2.85\times10^{13}\mathrm{e\ cm^{-2}}$ for ferromagnetic phase and $5.07\times10^{13}\mathrm{e\ cm^{-2}}$ for the nonmagnetic phase of 2-D Wigner electron crystal. For the given structure, the ground-state energies of ferromagnetic and nonmagnetic phases are compared. It is found that the energy of the ferromagnetic phase is less than that of the nonmagnetic phase of the 2-D Wigner electron crystal. Also, the results are compared with various experimental and theoretical works and it is found that our results are in good agreement with the experimental and other theoretical results for the 2-D Wigner electron crystal. The structure-dependent Wannier functions, which give proper localized representation for Wigner electrons, are employed in the calculation. The role of correlation energy is suitably taken into account.

## Introduction

In a system of degenerate interacting electrons moving in a uniform background of positive charge, it is now in general accepted that the electronic assembly will crystallize out at sufficiently low densities. The basic quantity $r_{s}$ is the convenient measure of the electron density, which is a dimensionless parameter. It is defined as the radius of the unit sphere enclosing a volume equal to the volume per electron of the electron gas. Recently, interest in this and related problems has intensified and a series of experiments are carried out in 2-D electron systems to revisit the question of whether the electrons are localized in the low-density limit

The Wigner crystallization has been observed on the surface of the liquid helium, surface of the solid neon, and in various systems like Si inversion layers, Si MOSFETs, SiGe quantum wells, GaAs/AlGaAs hetero structures, and AlAs quantum wells [1–15]. In the low-density limit, the ground state of the 2-D electron system realized in semiconductors is expected to be a Wigner crystal when the carrier–carrier interaction becomes dominant over the kinetic energy of the carriers. Shashkin et al. [1, 2] studied the low-temperature parallel field magnetotransport in a 2-D electron system in silicon in a wide range of electron densities and the Wigner transition is observed at the density $8 \times 10^{12}$ e cm$^{-2}$ for the nonmagnetic phase and $7 \times 10^{12}$ e cm$^{-2}$ for the ferromagnetic phase of Wigner electron crystal. Abrahams et al. [3] reviewed the Wigner crystallization in the least disordered systems. Safonav et al. [4] observed the electron localization in Si MOSFET at the critical density above $3.91 \times 10^{12}$ e cm$^{-2}$. Yoon et al. [5] reported the transport properties of a 2-D electron system in the GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure and found the Wigner transition in the range $9.88$–$8.81 \times 10^{12}$ e cm$^{-2}$. Simmons et al. [6] observed the formation of Wigner crystal in a high-quality GaAs–AlGaAs heterostructure. The heterostructure was fabricated by MBE growth on the GaAs substrate, with Si as the acceptor. The structure consists of a P-type modulation doped GaAs quantum well with an $\text{Al}_{0.33}\text{Ga}_{0.67}\text{As}$ spacer. It has an external metal plate approximately $500\ \mu\text{m}$ away from the 2-D hole gas (2-DHG) acting as a back gate. The carrier density is reduced using the back gate. On reducing the hole density to $2.16 \times 10^{13}$ e cm$^{-2}$, it is observed that the hole gas sample becomes insulating. This metal to insulator transition (MIT) observed is consistent with the formation of a Wigner solid. Okamoto and Kawaji [7, 8] performed systematic measurements on the activation energy $E_A$ in the insulating phase of the 2-D electron system formed in a Si MOSFET as a function of magnetic field for various electron concentrations. The Wigner crystallization point is reached when the activation energy becomes zero [9]. It is observed experimentally that this happens for the electron concentrations between $1.33 \times 10^{13}$ and $1.42 \times 10^{13}$ e cm$^{-2}$. At these concentrations, quantum effects cause the electrons to fluctuate around their stationary locations. These fluctuations cause an exchange interaction among the neighboring electrons. The exchange interaction plays a dominant role in determining the magnetism of Wigner solid and leads to the ferromagnetic ground state of electron system [7, 8]. To justify this fact experimentally, the magnetic field is applied parallel to the 2-D plane and the activation energy is measured. It is found that the activation energy increases linearly as the magnetic field increases in the low magnetic field and attains the saturation in the high magnetic field. Thus the ground state of 2-D Wigner crystal is found to be ferromagnetic [7, 8]. In a series of experiments on metal–insulator transition in Si inversion layers, Kravchenko et al. [10–12] found the localization of electrons at a density of $1.0 \times 10^{11}$ e cm$^{-2}$.

On the theoretical side, Kwon et al. [16] and Iwamoto [17] found the ferromagnetic type of interactions in 2-D Wigner solid at the density above $1.1 \times 10^{14}$ e cm$^{-2}$. Tanatar and Caperley [18] analyzed the ground-state properties of the 2-D electron gas in the liquid and solid phases using the variational Monte Carlo (VMC) and fixed node Green's function Monte Carlo (FN-GFMC) calculations. They found the nonmagnetic phase of Wigner crystallization in the density range $6.47 \times 10^{12}$–$1.115 \times 10^{13}$ e cm$^{-2}$ and the ferromagnetic transition at $7.1 \times 10^{12}$ e cm$^{-2}$. Caperley [19] investigated the ground-state properties of 3- and 2-D Wigner crystal using Fermion Monte Carlo variational calculation and the Wigner crystallization is found to occur at $1.0 \times 10^{13}$ e cm$^{-2}$ for the 2-D case. In our earlier work [20], we computed the ground-state energy of 2-D electron crystal for $r_s$ values up to 40. We predicted, for the nonmagnetic phase of the Wigner electron crystal, the maximum attainable electron density as $1.0 \times 10^{13}$ e cm$^{-2}$.

In this article, we report the results of calculations of the ground-state properties of the ferromagnetic and nonmagnetic phases of the 2-D Wigner electron crystal, taking into account the effect of correlation, which has not been done earlier. We computed the energy values for the range of $r_s$ values from 10–200. The structure-dependent Wannier functions, which give the proper localized representation for the electrons, are constructed and employed in the calculation.

In the following section, we outline the theoretical formulation. The results and discussion are given in the final section.

### Theoretical Formulation

The theoretical model for the electron crystal is composed of point charges, namely, electrons on a Bravais lattice with uniform neutralizing positive

background. In the low-density limit, their Coulomb interaction becomes more and more important, that is, the Coulomb energy, which tends to localize electrons into individual lattice sites to keep them as far apart as possible from each other, dominates over the kinetic energy of the electrons in the low-density limit and the electron gas is condensed into an electron crystal. In our model, the electrons will be localized at the lattice sites of face-centered square lattice (corresponding to bcc in the 3-D case). The localized electrons are represented by Wannier functions, which are obtained by transforming Gaussians into orthogonalized atomic orbitals using the method given by Lowdin [21] and Calais and Appel [22] as

$$
W(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{m} \phi(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{r}) \Delta^{-1 / 2}(\mathbf{m}, 0), \quad(1)
$$

where $\phi(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{r})$ is the Gaussian function and $\Delta^{-1 / 2}(\mathbf{m}, 0)$ is the reciprocal square root of the overlap matrix [22]. $\mathbf{m}$ and $\mathbf{r}$ are the vectors in ordinary space. The 2-D lattice vector $\mathbf{m}$ represents the various lattice sites of the face-centered square lattice.

For the nonmagnetic crystal case, like Edwards and Hillel [23], the Fock-Dirac density matrix can be written as

$$
\rho\left(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right)=\rho\left(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)\left[\alpha \alpha^{\prime}+\beta \beta^{\prime}\right] \quad(2)
$$

and for the ferromagnetic case [23]

$$
\rho\left(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right)=\rho\left(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right) \alpha \alpha^{\prime}, \quad(3)
$$

where $\alpha \alpha^{\prime}$ and $\beta \beta^{\prime}$ represent the spin up and spin down states, respectively, of the electrons.

In terms of Wannier functions

$$
\rho\left(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{m, m^{\prime}} W(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{r}) R\left(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right) W^{*}\left(\mathbf{m}^{\prime}, \mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right), \quad(4)
$$

where $R\left(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right)$ is derived as [24]

$$
R\left(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right)=V_{o a} \int_{k_{F}} \exp \left[2 \pi i \mathbf{k}\left(\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right)\right] d \mathbf{k}, \quad(5)
$$

where $V_{o a}$ is the area per electron.

We further note that for the nonmagnetic case

$$
\int \rho\left(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right) d \mathbf{r}=\frac{N}{2} \quad(6)
$$

and for the ferromagnetic case

$$
\int \rho\left(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right) d \mathbf{r}=N. \quad(7)
$$

In the calculation of $R\left(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right)$, we can take the region of integration in $k$-space $\left(k=k_{F}\right)$ as a circle. Then, the expression for $R\left(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right)$ is obtained as [20]

$$
R\left(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right)=V_{o a} \frac{k_{F}}{\left|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right|} J_{1}\left(2 \pi k_{F}\left|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right|\right), \quad(8)
$$

where $k_{F}$ is the radius of the circle in $k$-space and $J_{1}$ is the Bessel function.

The Hartree-Fock effective operator for a normal system is

$$
\begin{array}{r}
H_{\mathrm{eff}}(1)=\frac{1}{2} \Delta_{1}-\sum_{g} \frac{Z_{g}}{\left|\mathbf{r}_{1}-R_{g}\right|}+\int \frac{\left(1-P_{12}\right) \rho\left(\mathbf{x}_{2}, \mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}\right)}{r_{12}} d \mathbf{x}_{2}, \\
(9)
\end{array}
$$

where $1 / 2 \Delta_{1}$ is the kinetic energy operator, $\sum_{g}\left(Z_{g} /\right.$ $\left.\left|\mathbf{r}_{1}-R_{g}\right|\right)$ is the interaction between the nucleus and electron, $\int\left(\rho\left(\mathbf{x}_{2}, \mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}\right) / r_{12}\right) d \mathbf{x}_{2}$ is the interaction between electrons, $\int\left(P_{12} \rho\left(\mathbf{x}_{2}, \mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}\right) / r_{12}\right) d \mathbf{x}_{2}$ is the exchange interaction term, and $P_{12}$ is the exchange operator.

For any extended system one has to evaluate carefully the matrix elements of the long-range electrostatic part

$$
V_{C}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}\right)=-\sum_{g} \frac{Z_{g}}{\left|\mathbf{r}_{1}-R_{g}\right|}+\int \frac{\rho\left(\mathbf{x}_{2}, \mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}\right)}{r_{12}} d \mathbf{x}_{2}. \quad(10)
$$

Uniform distribution and point-like nuclei are the two extreme cases of positive charge distribution and all the real situations lie between the above two cases. In the present work, we have taken both cases for investigation.

### NONUNIFORM POSITIVE BACKGROUND

For the Wigner electron crystal, it is reasonable to have a nonuniform distribution of positive charge that does not have to be uniform [26–28]. In our previous work, we found that the Yukawa-type positive background is more suitable and close to the real situation. Hence, in the present calculation the nonuniform positive background is represented by a periodic array of Yukawa distribution with variable ripple parameter $\lambda$. The Yukawa-type charge distribution is given by [26, 27]

$$
p(r)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4 \pi} \frac{\exp (-\lambda r)}{r}, \tag{12}
$$

where $\lambda$ is the variational parameter.

If we can define

$$
\bar{\rho}(\mathbf{r})=\rho(\mathbf{r})-P(\mathbf{r}), \tag{13}
$$

then the expression for $V_{c}(\mathbf{r}_{1})$ is [29]

$$
V_{c}(\mathbf{r}_{1})=\frac{1}{2} \int \frac{\bar{\rho}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)}{r_{12}} d \mathbf{r}_{2}. \tag{14}
$$

The expression for the electrostatic energy with Yukawa-type positive background is derived as [29]

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{c}= & \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{2 \alpha}{\pi}} \sum_{\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}} R(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}) M(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}) F_{o}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2} \mathbf{P Q}^{2}\right) \\
& \times \exp \left(-\frac{\alpha}{4} \overline{00}^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4} \overline{\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}}^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{4} \\
& -\sqrt{\frac{4 \alpha}{\pi}} \sum_{\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}} R(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}) M(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}) F_{o}\left(\alpha \mathbf{C P}^{2}\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \times \exp \left(-\frac{\alpha}{4} \overline{00}^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4} \overline{\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}}^{2}\right) \\
& +4 \pi\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{3 / 2} \sum_{\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}} R(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}) M(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime}) \\
& \times \int \exp \left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left|\mathbf{r}_{2}-\mathbf{m}\right|^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left|\mathbf{r}_{2}-\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\right|^{2}-\lambda \mathbf{r}_{2}\right) \\
& \times \mathbf{r}_{2} d \mathbf{r}_{2}, \tag{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $M(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}^{\prime})$ is the weight factor depending upon the number of nearest neighbors and $F_{o}(t)$ is defined as

$$
F_{o}(t)=\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}} \operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{t}). \tag{16}
$$

With this $H_{\text{eff}}$, we compute the Hartree–Fock ground-state energy by extremizing the functional

$$
E_{\mathrm{HF}}=\int W^{*}(\mathbf{r}) H_{\mathrm{eff}} W(\mathbf{r}) d r. \tag{17}
$$

Now, the ground-state energy of the 2-D electron crystal can be written as the sum of the Hartree–Fock ground-state energy $E_{\mathrm{HF}}$ and the correlation energy $E_{c}$. The correlation energy is defined as the difference between the exact energy of the system and the energy computed using Hartree–Fock approximation, that is,

$$
E_{c}=\varepsilon-E_{\mathrm{HF}}. \tag{18}
$$

For the 2-D electron system, Jonson and Srinivasan [30] interpolate the result of Singwi et al. [31] at $r_{s}=0.5$ and a low-density, Einstein solid result of $-1.103 / r_{s}+O(r_{s}^{3 / 2})$ Ryd. to get

$$
E_{c}=\frac{-1.103}{r_{s}+4.41} \text { Ryd. } \tag{19}
$$

Using Eq. (19), the correlation energy values are calculated as a function of $r_{s}$.

### Results and Discussion

We selected the single Gaussian of the form

$$
\phi(\mathbf{m}, \mathbf{r})=(2 \alpha / \pi)^{3 / 4} \exp \left(-\alpha|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{m}|^{2}\right), \tag{20}
$$


where $\alpha$ is the variational parameter. The Gaussians are transformed into Wannier functions as indicated in Eq. (1).

In the uniform neutralizing positive background case, the energy under Hartree-Fock approximation is computed using Eq. (17) for different values of the variational parameter $\alpha$ keeping $r_s$ constant. It is found that as the value of $\alpha$ increases the energy value decreases, attains minimum, and then increases. This minimum energy is the Hartree-Fock ground-state energy $E_{\text{HF}}$ and the correlation energy $E_c$ [Eq. (19)] is added to get the total energy. This procedure is repeated for different values of $r_s$, ranging from 10-200.

In our previous work [32], we found that the Yukawa-type positive background is close to the real situation for the variable ripple parameter value $\lambda = 0.01$. So, in the present work the ground-state energy is computed with $\lambda = 0.01$ for various values of $r_s$ using the above procedure. Thus, we determined the ground-state energy of nonmagnetic and ferromagnetic phases of 2-D Wigner electron crystal for the $r_s$ values, ranging from 10-200 with both neutralizing positive background and Yukawa-type positive background. We presented the ground-state energy values of 2-D nonmagnetic and ferromagnetic phases of Wigner crystal along with electron gas energy values for the $r_s$ values ranging from 10-200 for uniform neutralizing background and Yukawa-type distribution in Tables I and II. From these tables, it is found that the energies of the ferromagnetic and nonmagnetic phases of 2-D Wigner electron crystal are less than the gas-state energy for all the $r_s$ values. Also, it is seen that the ground-state energy of the ferromagnetic phase of Wigner crystal is less than the nonmagnetic phase of Wigner crystal. This trend is similar to the 3-D case of our earlier calculation [24] and the work of Edwards and Hillel [23], where the calculated ground-state energy of Wigner crystal corresponding to the ferromagnetic phase is the lowest of all the phases. Comparing the results in Tables I and II, it is found that, for both nonmagnetic and ferromagnetic cases, the Yukawa-type positive background leads to the lower ground-state energy.
