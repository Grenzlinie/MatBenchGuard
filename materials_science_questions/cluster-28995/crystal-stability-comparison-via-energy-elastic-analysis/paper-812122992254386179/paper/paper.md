Progress of Theoretical Physics, Vol. 56, No. 2, August 1976

# Stability of Metallic Hydrogens with Cubic Structure

Hitose NAGARA, Hiroshi MIYAGI and Tuto NAKAMURA

Department of Material Physics, Faculty of Engineering Science
Osaka University, Toyonaka 560

(Received December 18, 1975)

The structure-dependent ground-state energies of metallic hydrogens are figured out accurately up to $O(r_{s})$ for cubic crystals, in critical comparison with the previous work by Brovman et al. and by Hammerberg and Ashcroft. The elastic constants are also evaluated up to the corresponding order for the purpose of looking into the mechanical stability of cubic metallic hydrogens under high pressures. It is shown that no cubic form is stable at low- pressures below $P_{c}=59$ Mbar with fcc as the high pressure phase. By taking account of the mechanical instability of sc, an upper bound to the ground-state energy of low-pressure phase is looked for. It is concluded that the binding energy at zero pressure should be higher than 1.40 eV per H; a remarkable result in comparison with the highest figure 0.86 eV of the previous ones. This entirely new result comes out with due accounts of the third-order exchange energy, which was unproperly neglected by the previous authors.

## § 1. Introduction

Metallic hydrogen was first studied by Wigner and Huntington (1936), $^{1)}$ for the purpose of looking into possibility of preparing it under high pressure. They were also interested in examining the Wigner-Seitz theory in the case when the experiment gives an upper limit of the cohesive energy, and in getting some infor- mation concerning the condition for the metallic state. About ten years later Kronig, de Boer and Korringa $^{2)}$ studied the same problem with interest in the internal constitution of the Earth.

Recently, metallic H has revived with much more interests, being stimulated by increasing possibility of the terrestrial production. $^{3)}$ There have appeared many studies on the transition pressure from the molecular phase, $^{4) 7)}$ the superconduc tivity, $^{8), 9)}$ and the lifetimes at zero pressure. $^{10)}$ The internal structure of Jupitor and Saturn has also been another stimulating subject, $^{11) 18)}$ to which the equation of state of hydrogens is an indispensable key.

Very recently Brovman, Kagan and Kholas (BKK) $^{14)}$ pointed out the cubic form to be unstable under zero pressure, basing their theory on the so-called dielectric approach with the use of wave-vector-dependent dielectric constant, $\varepsilon(q)$ . They calculated the ground-state energy by the perturbation method up to third order. Along the similar line of thought Hammerberg and Ashcroft (HA) $^{15)}$ dis cussed the fourth order effect by the use of the Green's function method. We mention here Schneider $^{16)}$ who recognized the hexagonal close packed structure to

be stable with a considerable deviation of $c/a$ from its ideal value. According to HA and BKK, however, the structure dependence of the ground-state energy is extremely weak: Their estimates of the ground-state energy at zero pressure are not so different from Wigner and Huntington's one, being around $-1.06$ Ry.

In the dielectric formulation the accuracy of results depends seriously upon choice of the dielectric function. As was taken in HA, the use of the Lindhard formula for $\varepsilon(q)$ leads us to the complete neglect of the exchange energy in higher order effects. However the exchange energy works against the Coulomb repulsion one, both due to the inhomogeneity of electron charge distributions which is induced by the electron-ion interaction. The net contribution to the ground-state energy, resulting from a large cancellation, for metallic H is different in sign from that for Li. $^{1)}$ Since the cancellation is so delicate, it may be questionable whether the dielectric function of the Hubbard type as assumed by BKK has enough accu- racy to discuss the crystal stability.

It is noted here that for metallic H the electron-ion interaction is very strong in the vicinity of a nucleus while it is not the case for ordinary metals. It seems more important for metallic H to look into the higher order terms of the structure dependent energy in contradistinction to the current treatment done for ordinary metals. $^{16 a)}$

In a series of our papers we shall present completely the ground-state energy contributions up to fourth order, assuming both the electron-ion and electron-electron interactions to be the perturbed Hamiltonian. This perturbation approach seems most appropriate for metallic H, in which the relevant value of $r_{s}$, the radius of equivalent sphere to the volume per H in units of the Bohr radius, is less than 2. The result is merely a series in $r_{s}$ and was given by Carr $^{17)}$ up to third order. However Carr's evaluation of the third order terms was very partial.

The present paper has mainly three-fold purpose. First, the complete evalua- tion of the perturbation series up to third order will be given for the cubic forms of metallic H. Secondly, basing our treatment on the same series, we shall study the stability of them in connection with the results of BKK and HA. Thirdly, an upper bound to the ground-state free energy will be obtained with a great reduction in energy compared with the previous result, for low-pressure phase to be explained below.

According to HA, $^{15)}$ the simple cubic (sc) has lower energy than both the body-centered cubic (bcc) and the face-centered cubic (fcc) at low densities, while fcc is lower than the others at high densities. This is also true in the present result, though our density for the crossing of sc and fcc ground-state energies is much higher than the value of HA. It is, however, shown that sc is mechanically unstable at all densities or pressures. Therefore the cubic form of metallic H cannot be stable at lower pressures than that for the crossing of sc and fcc Gibbs free energies. This pressure is predicted to be $P_{c}=14.9$ Mbar, which is about five times as high as HA's one. Now fcc is mechanically unstable at low densities.

The mechanical stability is predicted to occur at $P_{c}{}'=15.3$ Mbar, which is slightly higher than $P_{c}$.

Utilizing the instability of sc against the tetragonal distortion, we look for an upper bound to the free energy of the low-pressure phase by calculating that of primitive tetragonal structures (pt). The upper bound is estimated to be $-1.103$ Ry at zero pressure, which is surprisingly lower than the lowest ground-state energy ever published. This extraordinary energy reduction comes from the third-order exchange energy, which proves to lower abruptly before and after the Fermi surface touches the Brillouin zone boundary.

The pressure for the crossing of the Gibbs free energies of fcc and pt is estimated to be 59 Mbar, which may be a lower limit to the true critical pressure $P_{c}$. It is quite high compared with the transition pressure of the molecular phase to the metallic one, which has been thought to be of order 1 Mbar.

In $\S 2$ we describe our Hamiltonians. In $\S 3$ the perturbation series of the ground-state energy is evaluated up to third order. The elastic constants are obtained in $\S 4$, where a new method is presented for the evaluation of strain energies under the volume-conserving distortions. In $\S 5$ we give our results, including our upper bound to the ground-state free energy of low pressure phase.

## § 2. Notations, Madelung term

The length will be measured in units of the Bohr radius $a_{\mathrm{H}}$ and the energy in Rydberg. The momentum of electrons will be reduced in units of the Fermi momentum $p_{\mathrm{F}}=\left(\alpha r_{s}\right)^{-1}$. Here $V / N=(4 \pi / 3) r_{s}{ }^{3}, \quad \alpha=(4 / 9 \pi)^{1 / 3}=0.5210618$, in which $V$ denotes the volume and $N$ the number of atoms.

The unperturbed Hamiltonian is taken to be the kinetic energy of electrons, $\left(\alpha r_{s}\right)^{-2} p^{2}$. The perturbation consists of two terms
$$
H^{\prime}=H_{1}+H_{2}, \quad(2 \cdot 1)
$$
where $H_{1}$ represents the electron-ion interactions and $H_{2}$ the electron-electron ones. In our units, the matrix element responsible for the momentum change $\boldsymbol{g}$ of an electron due to $H_{1}$ is given by
$$
v(\boldsymbol{g})=-(8 / 3 \pi)\left(\alpha r_{s}\right)^{-1} g^{-2} . \quad(2 \cdot 2)
$$

Here $\boldsymbol{g}$ denotes the momentum corresponding to the reciprocal lattice vector in units of $p_{\mathrm{F}}$, assuming a Bravais lattice. The matrix element responsible for the momentum transfer from an electron to another one by $\boldsymbol{q}$, being due to $H_{2}$, is written as
$$
-(2 N)^{-1} v(\boldsymbol{q}), \quad(2 \cdot 3)
$$
in which $v(\boldsymbol{q})$ is given by Eq. $(2 \cdot 2)$ with replacement of $\boldsymbol{g}$ by the quasi-continuous momentum $\boldsymbol{q}$.

Finally we shall present a simple expression for the Madelung energy, being

useful for cubic lattices. We start with¹⁸⁾

$$
E_{M}=\frac{4}{3 \pi}\left(\alpha r_{s}\right)^{-1} \lim _{q_{c} \rightarrow 0}\left\{\sum_{|\boldsymbol{g}|<q_{c}}^{\prime} g^{-2}-N^{-1} \sum_{|\boldsymbol{q}|<q_{c}}^{\prime} q^{-2}\right\}, \tag{2·4}
$$

referring to the considered energy per atom. (Throughout this paper we shall refer to the energy per atom, otherwise stated.) Here the second term in the brace stands for the electrostatic energy of an ionic charge with its self-potential, $e^{2}/r$ in the limit $r \to 0$, which is included in the first term and hence should be subtracted. Now the $\boldsymbol{q}$-points are distributed with density $(3/8 \pi)N$ which is $N$ times as large as the density of $\boldsymbol{g}$-points, both in the $\boldsymbol{q}$-space. Therefore, if we replace the two summation terms in Eq. $(2 \cdot 4)$ by the corresponding integrals, they are cancelled by each other. This cancellation must be the case in the region with sufficiently large $\boldsymbol{g}$. Since the distribution of $\boldsymbol{q}$-points is quasi-continuous, we have

$$
E_{M}=\alpha_{M} r_{s}^{-1}, \tag{2·5}
$$

$$
\alpha_{M}=\lim _{q_{c} \to \infty} \frac{2}{\pi \alpha}\left(\frac{2}{3} \sum_{|\boldsymbol{g}|<q_{c}}^{\prime} g^{-2}-q_{c}\right), \tag{2·6}
$$

where $q_{c}$ is the radius of the sphere circumscribing $\nu_{c}$ points of $\boldsymbol{g}$ inclusive of $\boldsymbol{g}=0$, as determined by $q_{c}^{3}=2 \nu_{c}$.

## § 3. Perturbation series

In the perturbation series of the ground-state energy we have a partial series in $H_{2}$, which is the ground-state energy of electron gas. This structure-independent contribution to our ground-state energy may be given by

$$
E_{\mathrm{eg}}=\frac{3}{5}\left(\alpha r_{s}\right)^{-2}-\frac{3}{2 \pi}\left(\alpha r_{s}\right)^{-1}+\left(0.031 \ln r_{s}-0.115\right), \tag{3·1}
$$

where the last term stands for the Nozières-Pines interpolation formula for the correlation energy.¹⁹⁾ We note here that the other formulas²⁰⁾ give us nearly the same value as the above one.

Thus we shall obtain the structure-dependent contributions to the ground-state energy in the perturbation series with the help of the Brueckner-Goldstone (BG) diagram. We note here that the anomalous diagram pointed out by Kohn and Luttinger,²¹⁾ which is not included in BG diagrams, does not bring us any contri- bution to the ground-state energy up to third order.¹⁵⁾ The detail will also be figured out in the subsequent paper.²²⁾

(a) Second order term The second order energy is simply given by

$$
E_{2}=-\frac{2}{3 \pi^{2}} \sum_{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\prime} \frac{1}{g^{2}} \chi(g), \tag{3·2}
$$

$$
\chi(q)=\frac{4}{q^{2}}\left(1+\frac{4-q^{2}}{4 q} \ln \left|\frac{q+2}{q-2}\right|\right).\qquad(3\cdot3)
$$

The above function is closely related to the dielectric function in the Lindhard formula
$$
\varepsilon(q)=1+\frac{1}{2 \pi}\left(\alpha r_{s}\right) \chi(q).\qquad(3\cdot4)
$$

(b) Third order terms The BG diagrams relevant to the third order energy are shown in Fig. 1, where $H_{1}$ is represented by black circles and $H_{2}$ by horizontal broken lines. The diagrams are classified into three groups, as shown in Fig. 1. The respective contributions to the ground-state energy will below be written down in accordance with the shown label of groups.

## §4. Evaluation of the elastic constants

In order to look into the stability of metallic H in cubic form, we shall evaluate the elastic constants, $c_{11}, c_{12}$ and $c_{44}$. The energy increase of the crystal due to strains is given by $^{23)}$

$$
U_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{1}{2} c_{11} \sum_{\mu} e_{\mu \mu}^{2}+c_{12} \sum_{\mu>\nu} e_{\mu \mu} e_{\nu \nu}+\frac{1}{2} c_{44} \sum_{\mu>\nu} e_{\mu \nu}^{2}, \quad(4 \cdot 1)
$$

referring to the unit volume. In the above,

$$
e_{\mu \nu}=\varepsilon_{\mu \nu}+\varepsilon_{\nu \mu} \quad(\mu \neq \nu), \quad e_{\mu \mu}=\varepsilon_{\mu \mu}, \quad(4 \cdot 2)
$$

where $\varepsilon_{\mu \nu}$ describes uniform displacement of ions $u_{\mu}$ as given by

$$
u_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu} \varepsilon_{\mu \nu} x_{\nu} \quad(4 \cdot 3)
$$

with $x_{\nu}$ the position coordinate of ions in the unstrained lattice.

The normal mode of uniform distortions may be described by

$$
\begin{aligned}
& e_{1}=1 / \sqrt{3} \cdot \delta=1 / \sqrt{3} \cdot\left(e_{x x}+e_{y y}+e_{z z}\right), \\
& e_{2}=\sqrt{3} / 2 \cdot\left(e_{x x}-e_{y y}\right), e_{3}=1 / 2 \cdot\left(2 e_{z z}-e_{x x}-e_{y y}\right), \\
& e_{4}=\sqrt{3} e_{y z}, e_{5}=\sqrt{3} e_{z x}, e_{6}=\sqrt{3} e_{x y}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting Eq. $(4 \cdot 4)$ into Eq. $(4 \cdot 1)$, we have

$$
U_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{11}+2 c_{12}\right) e_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(c_{11}-c_{12}\right)\left(e_{2}^{2}+e_{3}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{6} c_{44}\left(e_{4}^{2}+e_{5}^{2}+e_{6}^{2}\right). \quad(4 \cdot 5)
$$

Therefore the stability conditions for the cubic form prove to be $^{24)}$

$$c_{11}+2 c_{12}>0, \quad c_{11}-c_{12}>0, \quad c_{44}>0 \tag{4.6}$$

as far as the uniform distortions are concerned.

Now $(c_{11}+2 c_{12})$ is related to the compressibility $\kappa$ by $c_{11}+2 c_{12}=3 \kappa^{-1}$, where $\kappa$ is obtained from the expression for the pressure $P$ in our units

$$P=-\left(4 \pi r_{s}^{2}\right)^{-1} \partial E / \partial r_{s}, \tag{4.7}$$

as given by

$$\kappa=12 \pi r_{s} /\left[\frac{\partial^{2} E}{\partial r_{s}^{2}}-\frac{2}{r_{s}} \frac{\partial E}{\partial r_{s}}\right]. \tag{4.8}$$

In the above, $E$ is the total ground-state energy, including the energy of electron gas $E_{\text{eg}}$.

Let us now consider $(c_{11}-c_{12})$ and $c_{44}$, which will be evaluated under the volume-conserving distortion. Fuchs treated this by introducing nonlinear strains, $^{25)}$ where the nonvanishing strain components are assumed to be $\varepsilon_{x x}=\varepsilon, \varepsilon_{y y}=(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}$ -1 for the evaluation of $c_{11}-c_{12}$ and $\varepsilon_{x y}=\varepsilon, \varepsilon_{y x}=0$ for that of $c_{44}$. However our formalism does not necessitate such ingenious technique. In the rectangular coor- dinates system referring to the cube, we denote a component of reciprocal lattice vector by $g_{\mu}$ for the unstrained lattice and by $g_{\mu}^{\prime}$ for the strained one. Then we obtain the following:

$$g_{\mu}{ }^{\prime}=\eta\left(g_{\mu}+\sum_{\nu} \gamma_{\mu \nu} g_{\nu}\right), \tag{4.9}$$

$$\gamma_{\mu \nu}=-\varepsilon_{\mu \nu}+\sum_{\lambda} \varepsilon_{\mu \lambda} \varepsilon_{\lambda \nu}, \tag{4.10}$$

up to second order in $\varepsilon_{\mu \nu}$. In Eq. (4.9),

$$\eta=\left[1+\delta+\sum_{\mu>\nu} \varepsilon_{\mu \mu} \varepsilon_{\nu \nu}-\sum_{\mu>\nu} \varepsilon_{\mu \nu} \varepsilon_{\nu \mu}\right]^{1 / 3} \tag{4.11}$$

is a factor due to the change of $r_{s}$, which comes in because our reciprocal lattice vector is reduced in units of the Fermi momentum.

Now, contributions to the structure-dependent energy are of the form $(\alpha r_{s})^{n}$  $f(g)$ except $E_{3}^{(I)}$ given by Eq. $(3 \cdot 5)$, where $r_{s}$ remains invariable under the volume conserving distortions. Now

$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$

Considering the cubic symmetry in the unstrained lattice, we obtain

$$\sum_{\boldsymbol{g}}\left[\sum_{\mu}\left(g_{\mu}{ }^{\prime 2}-g_{\mu}{ }^{2}\right)\right] g^{-1} f^{\prime}(g)$$

## Appendix A
——Alternative expression for $I(g)$——

The expression for $I(g)$ which is relevant for numerical integrations in the case $g<2$ will be derived here. In the text, Eq. $(3\cdot11)$ is rewritten in the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
I(g)= & \frac{1}{2} \int\left(n_{\boldsymbol{p}_{1}}-n_{\boldsymbol{p}_{1}+\boldsymbol{g}}\right) d \boldsymbol{p}_{1} \int\left(n_{\boldsymbol{p}_{2}}-n_{\boldsymbol{p}_{2}+\boldsymbol{g}}\right) d \boldsymbol{p}_{2} \\
& \times \frac{1}{\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2}\right)^{2}} \frac{\left[2\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2}\right) \cdot \boldsymbol{g}\right]^{2}}{\left[\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{1}+\boldsymbol{g}\right)^{2}-\boldsymbol{p}_{1}{ }^{2}\right]^{2}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{2}+\boldsymbol{g}\right)^{2}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2}{ }^{2}\right]^{2}}
\end{aligned} \qquad(\mathrm{A} \cdot 1)
$$

with the aid of the symmetry property mentioned there. In the above integral, the non-vanishing contributions from each of $d \boldsymbol{p}_{1}$ and $d \boldsymbol{p}_{2}$ come out only in the shaded region illustrated in Fig. A-1, where the left sphere is obtained from the right one with displacement of the center by $-\boldsymbol{g}$. The respective contributions to the integral from the right- and left-halves have different signs in each of $d \boldsymbol{p}_{1}$ and $d \boldsymbol{p}_{2}$. We divide the integration region into three parts whose boundaries are the two planes: $z=1-g$ and $z=-1$ in the cylindrical coordinates $(\rho, \phi, z)$ for $\boldsymbol{p}$.

Performing the integrations in $\phi_{1}, \phi_{2}, \rho_{1}$ and $\rho_{2}$, after some rearrangements with change of the integration variable we obtain

$$
I(g)=\left(\frac{2 \pi}{g}\right)^{2}\left(J_{1}+2 J_{2}+J_{3}\right), \qquad(\mathrm{A} \cdot 2)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
J_{1}= & \int_{1-g}^{1} \frac{d z_{1}}{\left(g+2 z_{1}\right)^{2}} \int_{1-g}^{1} \frac{d z_{2}}{\left(g+2 z_{2}\right)^{2}}\left[H\left(1-z_{1}{ }^{2}, 1-z_{2}{ }^{2}, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-H\left(0,1-z_{2}{ }^{2}, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)-H\left(1-z_{1}{ }^{2}, 0, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)+H\left(0,0, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)\right],
\end{aligned} \qquad(\mathrm{A} \cdot 2 \mathrm{a})
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
J_{2}= & \int_{1-g}^{1} \frac{d z_{1}}{\left(g+2 z_{1}\right)^{2}} \int_{-g / 2}^{1-g} \frac{d z_{2}}{\left(g+2 z_{2}\right)^{2}}\left[H\left(1-z_{1}{ }^{2}, 1-z_{2}{ }^{2}, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)\right. \\

& \left.-H\left(0,1-\left(z_{2}+g\right)^{2}, z_{1},-z_{2}-g\right)\right],
\end{aligned} \qquad(\mathrm{A} \cdot 2 \mathrm{~b})
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
J_{3}= & \int_{-g / 2}^{1-g} \frac{d z_{1}}{\left(g+2 z_{1}\right)^{2}} \int_{-g / 2}^{1-g} \frac{d z_{2}}{\left(g+2 z_{2}\right)^{2}}\left[H\left(1-z_{1}{ }^{2}, 1-z_{2}{ }^{2}, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)\right. \\
& +H\left(1-\left(z_{1}+g\right)^{2}, 1-z_{2}{ }^{2},-z_{1}-g, z_{2}\right)+H\left(1-z_{1}{ }^{2}, 1-\left(z_{2}+g\right)^{2}, z_{1},-z_{2}-g\right) \\
& \left.+H\left(1-\left(z_{1}+g\right)^{2}, 1-\left(z_{2}+g\right)^{2},-z_{1}-g,-z_{2}-g\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{A·2c}
$$

Here $H(u, v, z_{1}, z_{2})$ is defined by
$$
H\left(u, v, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)=h\left(u, v, z_{1}-z_{2}\right)-h\left(u, v, g+z_{1}+z_{2}\right)
\tag{A·3}
$$
with
$$
h(n, v, \zeta)=\zeta^{2}\left[u \ln \left|\tilde{\Delta}+\zeta^{2}-u+v\right|+v \ln \left|\tilde{\Delta}+\zeta^{2}+u-v\right|+\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{2}\right],
\tag{A·3a}
$$

$$
\tilde{\Delta} \equiv \tilde{\Delta}(u, v, \zeta)=\left[(u-v)^{2}+2(u+v) \zeta^{2}+\zeta^{4}\right]^{1 / 2}.
\tag{A·3b}
$$

$\tilde{\Delta}(1-z_{1}^{2}, 1-z_{2}^{2}, g+z_{1}+z_{2})$ reduces to $\Delta$ as given by Eq. $(3 \cdot 14)$ of the text. We note here that $F(\xi, \eta)$ and $G(\xi, \eta)$ defined by Eqs. $(3 \cdot 13 a)$ and $(3 \cdot 13 b)$ in the text are related to $h(u, v, \zeta)$ respectively by
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(\xi, \eta) & =h\left(1-\xi^{2}, 1-\eta^{2}, \xi-\eta\right)-h\left(1-\xi^{2}, 0, \xi-\eta\right)-h\left(0,1-\eta^{2}, \xi-\eta\right) \\
& +h(0,0, \xi-\eta),
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
G(\xi, \eta) & =h\left(1-\xi^{2}, 1-\eta^{2}, g+\xi+\eta\right)-h\left(1-\xi^{2}, 0, g+\xi+\eta\right) \\
& -h\left(0,1-\eta^{2}, g+\xi+\eta\right)+h(0,0, g+\xi+\eta).
\end{aligned}
\tag{A·4}
$$

In the limit $g \to 0, I(g)$ remains finite. The limiting value is evaluated easily from Eq. $(A \cdot 1)$, where we put
$$
n_{\boldsymbol{p}}-n_{\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{g}} \cong-\boldsymbol{g} \cdot \boldsymbol{\nabla} n_{\boldsymbol{p}}=2(\boldsymbol{g} \cdot \boldsymbol{p}) \delta\left(p^{2}-1\right).
$$

Thus we obtain
$$
I(0)=-2 \pi^{2},
\tag{A·5}
$$
by taking the same integration procedure as before.
