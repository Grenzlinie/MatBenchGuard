# The Electronic Viewpoint on the Morphology of the Microclusters
## I. The Energy Spectrum of the Single Electron States

Tohru Ogawa\*
Max-Planck-Institut für Festkörperforschung, Stuttgart, Germany

Received June 13, 1977

Although the five-fold symmetry conflicts with the crystalline long range order, it can appear in some finite systems. In order to search for such possibility in the microclusters in the small size limit and to clarify the roles of the electrons in the morphology, the electronic states are studied in the tight binding scheme. The single electron spectra coming from the $s$- and $p$-atomic orbitals are derived for three types of the 13-atom clusters; with the fcc-like configuration, the hcp-like and the icosahedral. If the transfer integral between two surface atoms is put equal to that between the central and a surface atom in the icosahedral case, this configuration has the lowest total electronic energy in many cases studied, but not always. The fact that a surface atom has larger number of neighbours in the icosahedral configuration is surely of great advantage. It, however, is not almighty in the quantum case.

## § 1. Introduction

It had been already known by Plato [1] that there are only five regular polyhedra, which are sometimes called the Platonic solids [2]; tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron. Of course, they are closely connected with the fairest way of the division of the full solid angle $4\pi$. It is interesting that dodecahedron and icosahedron among them never relate essentially with any kind of crystalline order, as is well known, because of their five-fold symmetry [2,3]. Then two tendencies sometimes conflict with each other, one is that for short range symmetry and the other for long range symmetry**. For example, twelve neighbours in well known three dimensional close packed lattices, face-centred-cubic and hexagonal close packed lattice, are not arranged in the direction selected in the most symmetrical way, which are of course those to the centres of the faces of a dodecahedron from its body centre or, in other words, those to twelve vertices of an icosahedron.

In some finite system, however, the structures with five-fold symmetry arise [9]. Pentagonal packing of virus particles has been observed with as electron microscope [10]. Some boron hydrides molecules, and ions, for example $B_{12}H_{12}^{-}$, have the icosahedral structures [11]. In the crystals of tetragonal boron [12] and of some intermetallic compounds, for example $M_{0}Al_{12}$ [13], the icosahedral units arrange periodically. The external shape of the fine particles of various fcc metals have been observed to be the pentagonal decahedron [14]. Electron diffraction study of the microclusters of argon atoms has shown their non-crystalline structure [15]. The possible experimental realizations of five-fold symmetry in synthetic diamonds, copper dendrites, whiskers of Ni and Pt and cobalt crystals produced by hydrogen reduction of $CoBr_{2}$ has been suggested [16]. Among them, the structure of the metallic fine particles

In this case, the external form is de- termined semimacroscopically by the competition of three types of energy; the surface energy, the boundary energy and the deformation energy. Therefore, the symmetry of the external shape is not necessarily a direct reflection of the microscopic symmetry itself.
If, however, one considers the limitting case of small size, they should be no longer regarded as multiply twinning structure but as those of the microscopic one with five-fold symmetry. Let us call the aggregate of atoms with such small limit size as a microcluster. The standard macroscopic theory on the external shape [17] does not work in this size range.
Theoretical efforts have been devoted to clarify the physical properties of microclusters consisting of classi- cal atoms interacting through Lennard-Jones poten- tial [18]. It is well established now that the con- figuration of minimum potential energy is neither fcc nor hcp but icosahedral one in the size range of some 10~100 atoms and the above mentioned experiment by Farges et al. [15] has supported that.
As for the study on the electronic state in the fine par- ticles, the statistical effect connected with the discrete- ness of the energy levels with the number of atoms of $\sim 10^{4}$ has been pointed out by Fröhlich [19] and together with the neutrality condition by Kubo [20]. It, however, is only recently that the literatures can be found on the electronic states in the microclusters in the above mentioned size range [21]. The purpose of this series of works is to clarify the possible roles of the electrons in the morphology of the microclusters. This investigation also forms a part of our effort to understand systemati- cally the origin of structure [22].
It will be the best procedure of distinguishing between various effects of electrons to start with the study of the single particle spectrum without taking the many body effects into account. The aim of this paper is not to derive the morphological conclusion in a hurry but to establish the independent particle picture on which the further study will be based.

The geometrical structure of the 13-atom microclusters are introduced and some morphological discussion in the classical case is given in $\S 2$ . The electronic states in the microcluster are studied in $\S 3$ . Section 4 is devoted to concluding remarks.

If a pairwise interaction potential $\phi(r)$ is assumed, the total potential energy of an fcc-like microluster is given by

$$
E_{A}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \neq j} \sum \phi\left(\left|a_{i}-a_{j}\right|\right)=\sum_{k} l_{k} \phi\left(\alpha_{k}\right).
$$

In the case of the Lennard-Jones 6-12 potential

$$
\phi(r)=\varepsilon\left(r^{-12}-2 r^{-6}\right), \quad(2.16)
$$

it is easily derived that

$$
E_{A}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)=\varepsilon\left(36.221887 \alpha_{1}^{-12}-76.965278 \alpha_{1}^{-6}\right), \quad(2.17)
$$

$$
E_{A}^{(0)}=\operatorname{Min} E_{A}=E_{A}(0.989960)=-40.884494 \varepsilon . \quad(2.18)
$$

In the similar way,

$$
E_{B}\left(\beta_{1}\right)=\varepsilon\left(36.223736 \beta_{1}^{-12}-76.986916 \beta_{1}^{-6}\right) \quad(2.19)
$$

$$
E_{B}^{(0)}=\operatorname{Min} E_{B}=E_{B}(0.989922)=-40.905397 \varepsilon, \quad(2.20)
$$

$$
E_{C}\left(\gamma_{1}\right)=\varepsilon\left(28.480957 \gamma_{1}^{-12}-71.062500 \gamma_{1}^{-6}\right) \quad(2.21)
$$

$$
E_{C}^{(0)}=\operatorname{Min} E_{C}=E_{C}(0.963808)=-44.326801 \varepsilon, \quad(2.22)
$$

and the optimum value of $\gamma_{2}$ is 1.013408 in the last case.
Then, the most stable configuration of these three is apparently the icosahedral one. The energy difference is remarkable: $E_{B}^{(0)} / E_{C}^{(0)}=0.9228$, which should be compared with the ratio of the cohesive energy of bcc and fcc bulk crystals of Lennard-Jones system, being $E_{\text {bcc }} /$ $E_{\mathrm{fcc}}=0.9566$. The icosahedral configuration has a 'polytetrahedral' structure and is rather efficient in packing. On the contrary, fcc structure has octahedral parts as well as the regular tetrahedral parts. It is inevitable for the construction of a long range order to include rather inefficient octahedral parts. The local packing fraction can be defined in terms of the Voronoi polyhedron [23], that is the natural extension of the Wigner-Seitz cell for arbitrary configuration. Then the local packing fraction at the central part of the icosahedral packing is given by

$$
\pi \sqrt{90+145 \tau} / 75=0.754697
$$

besides $\sqrt{2} \pi / 6=0.740480$ in fcc and hcp packing. This packing point of view essentially explains the tendency for the icosahedral configuration in the classical system.

It should be noticed that the hcp-like configuration has lower energy than the fcc-like one as first pointed out for bulk case by Kihara and Koba [24]. This fact comes from the difference of the second neighbour distances in two configurations and valids rather generally for pair wise potentials.

Strictly speaking, the above mentioned hcp configuration with so-called $c / a$ ratio of unity is not stable because all the atoms except the central one do not locate at the minimum place of their potential. However, a perturbational calculation shows easily that the energy gain by small distortion is at most $10^{-4} \varepsilon$. In the other two microclusters with higher symmetry, all the atoms locate at the minimum points of the potential. But for the case of larger systems, it is rather complicated problem to find the stable configuration with surface displacements. The 13-atom clusters with fcc and icosahedral configuration which respectively requires only one parameter to specify the configuration is rather exceptional in this sense.

## § 3. Electronic States in Microclusters

Let us derive the single particle spectrum of electrons in three microclusters mentioned in the preceding section in the tight-binding scheme. We confine ourselves to the s- and p-orbitals in this paper and the d-orbitals will be studied in a forthcoming paper.
The basic assumptions are similar to the Hückel theory [25], being given below:
1) Single electron wavefunctions are given by linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO).
2) The orbitals on another atoms are orthogonal to each other.
3) The transfer integrals between non-neighbouring atoms are neglected.
4) The $\pi$-like transfer integrals are ignored.
5) The electron-electron interactions are discarded.
Then the transfer integrals appearing in the present theory are $V_{s}, V_{s p}$ and $V_{p}$. The energy levels of the electrons in a free atom are $\varepsilon_{s}$ and $\varepsilon_{p}$.

### I. Secular Equations in the Irreducible Representation

The consideration on the symmetry of the atomic configuration leads to the block diagonalization of the Hamiltonian in terms of the irreducible representation of the point group associated.

#### A. The fcc-like (Cubo-Octahedral) Microclusters.
The point group is $O_{h}$. The character table and the character system for s- and p-orbitals are shown in Table 1.
By the standard reduction procedure [26], the direct sum for $\Gamma_{s}^{(0)}, \Gamma_{p}^{(0)}, \Gamma_{s}$ and $\Gamma_{p}$ are derived as
$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
\Gamma_{s}^{(0)}=A_{1 g} \\
\Gamma_{p}^{(0)}=T_{1 u}
\end{array}\right\} \quad \text { for the central atom, }
$$

$$
\Gamma_{s}=A_{1 g}+E_{g}+T_{2 g}+T_{1 u}+T_{2 u},
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{p}= & A_{1 g}+A_{2 g}+2 E_{g}+2 T_{1 g}+2 T_{2 g} \\
& +A_{2 u}+E_{u}+3 T_{1 u}+2 T_{2 u}.
\end{aligned}
$$

In order to go further, it is required to make some assumption on the relation among the parameters $\varepsilon_{s}, \varepsilon_{p}, V_{s}, V_{s p}$ and $V_{p}$. Two cases are chosen; the first is the separation limit of two atomic levels, $\varepsilon_{s}$ and $\varepsilon_{p}$ and the second is the $s-p$ hybridization limit.

#### (i) The separation limit case
It is assumed that

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left|\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{s}\right| \gg \operatorname{Max}\left(\left|V_{s}\right|,\left|V_{s p}\right|,\left|V_{p}\right|\right) \\
& V_{s}>0, \quad V_{p}>0.
\end{aligned}\qquad(3.28)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \varepsilon_{p}=\varepsilon_{s}, \\

\end{aligned}\qquad(3.29)

The latter choice makes the value of the second moment of the spectrum the same as in another microcluster.

The obtained single particle spectrum in two cases are shown in Figure 4–6 and in Tables 4–6. The accidental degeneracy is generally removed if the far neighbour transfer is switched on. The total electron energy compared with the fcc case is depicted in Figures 7–9 and is shown in Table 7 together with the data on the multiplicity of the Fermi level for the case of the electron number per atom being an integer. In these the first neighbor distances are assumed to be common to these three configurations. The characteristic feature of the result is as follows. In the case (i), the fcc-structure is the most stable for \(s^1\) atom and another structures has lower electron energy only when the number of electron per atom is not an integer. In all the other cases examined the icosahedral structure is the most stable if the relation \(V' = V\) is employed. But if the other relation \(V'=\sqrt{0.8}\,V\) is adopted, various situations arise.

Therefore, the fact that a surface atom has larger number of neighbours in the icosahedral configuration is surely of great advantage. It, however, should be noted that this fact does not always bring the icosahedral structure the lowest energy.

In the case of the $s-p$ separate limit, the icosahedral microcluster has the lowest single electron level and the highest total energy if the number of the electron per atom is unity. It shows that the effect of the electrons can not be replaced by an effective pairwise classical potential even in the case where only $s$ orbital concerns.

The icosahedral configuration is the most stable for all the other cases studied if $V'=V$. But the various kind of results arise in the case of $V'=\sqrt{0,8}\,V$. This fact shows that a many-neighbour configuration is surely prefarable for electrons. The situation, however, is rather complicated and the general statement is difficult to make. Many things such as the directionality and the degeneracy of the $p$-levels, the distance dependence of the transfer integral and so on take part delicately in the result.

The problem of shape or pattern is very hard to be an object of physics and has been left behind yet. It is desirable to explore this field for more elavorate treatment of the short range order in liquids and amorphouses as mentioned in $\S 1$ and for many others, for example, concerning the problem of "Why solids?" [27]. The study on the microcluster will provide the excelent data in comparing the fruits of solid state physics, molecular physics and chemistry, and will be able to fill the gap which may possibly be among them.

In the calculation of the total electron energy in this paper, any special attention has not been paid on the degeneracy of the Fermi level. But a small deformation of the microcluster may lower the energy by removing the degeneracy and Jahn-Teller effect [28] may occur. Another possibility is that the large multiplicity of the degeneracy of the Fermi level is preferable for the exchange energy if the electron-electron interactions are taken into account. These two tendencies will conflict with each other. This phenomenon will be studied in the forthcoming paper.
