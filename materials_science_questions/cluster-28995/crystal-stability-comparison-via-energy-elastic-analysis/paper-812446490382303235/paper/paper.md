# BINDING ENERGY OF A PAIR OF HYDROGEN ATOMS IN $\alpha$-IRON

C. MINOT
Laboratoire de Chimie Thèorique, Bâtiment 490, Université Paris Sud, 91405 Orsay, France

and

C. DEMANGEAT
L.M.S.E.S. (U.A. au CNRS No. 306), Université Louis Pasteur,
4, rue Blaise Pascal, 67070 Strasbourg, France

Received 17 October 1984

The interaction energy between hydrogen atoms at tetrahedral positions in $\alpha$-iron is investigated using the tight-binding method of band structure calculation based on the extended Hückel formalism. Nearest and next-nearest pair interactions are shown to be considerably repulsive whereas the most probable stable situation appears to be the third nearest neighbouring position.

A large volume of data for the solubility and dif- fusivity of hydrogen in steel has appeared in the liter- ature. For example, Kiuchi and McLellan [1] have re- ported 150 references on this problem. Also, Pressouyre [2] has given a classification on hydrogen traps and repellers. In spite of the considerable interest on the influence of hydrogen on the mechanical behaviour of steel, a very few authors have started calculations [3,4] on this system. Moreover, these calculations [3,4] rely only on the electronic structure of a single hydrogen in $\alpha$-iron. The objective of this letter is to fill a gap between the considerable number of experimental results and the phenomenological models used to explain them. The present calculation will relate only hydrogen-hydrogen binding energies. Calculation of binding energy between hydrogen and other point defects (as discussed in the paper of Pressouyre [2]) is a little more complicated and will be reported in a later publication [5]. A pre- vious calculation of hydrogen binding energy in palla- dium has been reported recently. In this calculation the binding energy has been splitted in an electronic part [6] and an elastic part [7]. However, this method appears very complicated and needs the knowledge of the electronic structure of one hydrogen atom in the pure crystal and it is not a trivial task to extend it in a magnetic material.

In this letter we have computed directly, using the tight binding method [8] of band structure calculations based on the extended Hückel [9] formalism. It is generally admitted that hydrogen in bcc transition metals is located at tetrahedral positions [10,11]. However, in the case of H in $\alpha$-iron, due to the very low solubility, it is difficult to have a definite answer. Discussions [12,13] about the lattice location of H in Pd have shown that lattice relaxation around H atoms may have its importance for the determination of the stable position. This lattice relaxation appears of cen- tral importance in other calculations [14,15]. In the quantum mechanical calculation [14] the interacting po tential between H and the metal atom is approached by a phenomenological double Born-Mayer form, whereas i the most sophisticated calculation of Puska and Nieminen [15], the absence of short range repulsion between H and metal atoms does not give more con- fidence to the results obtained. To perform our cal- culation we then expect that the tetrahedral site in $\alpha$-iron is occupied [11].

The charge transfer from metal to hydrogen is found to be nearly equal to 0.1 electrons and it may have some influence on the final result. However, it has been shown [21] that a partial cancellation between the one-electron and the electron-electron term arises in the binding energy. At large distance, we may also question the validity of the Slater exponent and consequently we have to consider the binding energy in the $T_4$ position only as semi-quantitative. The present method is unable (because it is a band structure calculation) to take in to account the effect of the local lattice relaxation present around the hydrogen atom. Nevertheless, it has been shown recently [7] that in the case of $\alpha$-palladium hydrides the elastic binding energy is much less important than the electronic binding energy.

The geometry $T_3$ emerges from the results as the most probable (see table 2). The energy differences $\Delta E(T_3T_i)$ defined as
$$\Delta E(T_3T_i)=E(T,T_i)-E(T,T_3)$$
are reported in table 2 and show that in $\alpha$-iron hydrogen atoms cannot come closer than the third nearest neighbour. This may be compared to phenomenological models used for describing phase diagrams in VH, NbH and TaH systems and in particular the blocking model [22]. In the present calculation we have shown that the repulsion between screened protons extends out to second neighbours. This appears in agreement with the recent work of Boureau [23], in contradiction with Futran et al. [16] where the repulsion extends up to third nearest neighbours.

Further work seems to be necessary before at least a qualitative agreement can be reached in the complicated problem of metal hydride phase diagrams.
