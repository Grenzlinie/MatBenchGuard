# An RFA model calculation of the Compton profile of TiC

B K Panda†, D P Mahapatra‡ and H C Padhi†
† Institute of Physics, Sachivalaya Marg, Bhubaneswar 751005, India
‡ Institute for Plasma Research, Bhat, Gandhinagar 382424, India

Received 9 January 1987, in final form 5 May 1987

Abstract. A spherically averaged Compton profile of TiC has been calculated within the framework of the renormalised free-atom model. Calculations have been carried out using two different valence charge configurations, one with Ti as donor and the other with Ti as acceptor. The model in which Ti is a donor is found to provide a profile with very good agreement with experimental data. The reciprocal form factor also supports this model.

## 1. Introduction

Among transition-metal carbides, TiC represents a unique system for which various theoretical and experimental data support two different bonding mechanisms based on two different charge configurations.

The semi-empirical tight-binding band-structure calculations of Lye and Logothesis (1966) indicate a transfer of charge from the C 2p to the Ti 3d state consistent with a valence configuration of $2s^{2}2p^{0.75}$ for C and $3d^{4}4s^{0.75}4p^{0.5}$ for Ti. Later a study of C K and Ti $L_{II,III}$ x-ray emission bands (Holliday 1967) supported a similar configuration.

In contrast, all the APW band-structure calculations (Ern and Switendick 1965, Conklin and Silversmith 1968, Schwarz and Conklin 1971, Neckel *et al* 1976) support charge transfer from Ti to C. Schwarz and Conklin (1971) gave a charge configuration $2s^{1.92}2p^{3.81}$ for C and $3d^{2.27}4s^{0}$ for Ti. In comparison with this Neckel *et al* (1976) gave a charge configuration $2s^{1.99}2p^{3.66}$ for C and $3d^{2.35}4s^{0}$ for Ti. Both these configurations indicate a transfer of about 1.7 electrons from Ti to C. All the APW calculations also indicate a low density of states at the Fermi level with good agreement with the interpretation of ESCA, x-ray emission and absorption data by Ramqvist *et al* (1969a, b). On the basis of these findings, it has been suggested that the bonding in TiC is partly ionic because of the transfer of charge and partly covalent because of overlapping C 2p and Ti 3d states. However, there is another very interesting observation. The Compton profile (CP) of TiC has been calculated by Seth *et al* (1977) from wavefunctions determined from an LCAO scheme with an HFS-$X\alpha$ model potential. The CP calculated with the exchange parameter $\alpha = 1$ was found to agree very well with experimental data (Manninen and Paakkari 1976) but, interestingly enough, the calculations of Seth *et al* for $\alpha = 1$ indicate a metallic system. The APW calculation of Neckel *et al* (1976) is also in agreement with this.

Since the valence electrons in TiC are very much like those in a pure transition metal, it is quite tempting to carry out a CP calculation using the renormalised free-atom (RFA)

model based on an appropriate charge configuration. Such a calculation may even support a particular configuration out of two configurations, i.e. that of Lye and Logo- thesis (1966), and one corresponding to either of the other two as mentioned earlier. This is the motivation behind the present work in which we have calculated the CP using the RFA model (Berggren 1972) based on two different charge configurations. As our second configuration, we choose that due to Neckel et al (1976); we expect the other configuration to yield very similar results.

## 2. Theory
The RFA model has been described in detail elsewhere (Berggren 1972). However, for completeness, we give below some of the important formulae that we used.

To start with, we take a simple free-atom wavefunction truncated at the Wigner- Seitz (WS) sphere and renormalise it to unity within the WS sphere. This is the RFA wavefunction and is denoted by $\varphi_{0}$. When s electrons are considered the spherically averaged CP is given by
$$
J(q)=4 \pi \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\psi_{0}\left(K_{n}\right)\right|^{2} G_{n}(q) \tag{1}
$$
where $\psi_{0}(K_{n})$ is the Fourier transform (FT) of the RFA wavefunction, the sum extending over all reciprocal-lattice vectors $K_{n}$.

For a cubic lattice the function $G_{n}(q)$ is given by
$$
G_{0}(q)=\frac{1}{2}\left(p_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-q^{2}\right) \tag{2}
$$
while, for $n \neq 0$,
$$
G_{n}(q)= \begin{cases}0 & \\ \tilde{G}(q) & \text { for } \begin{cases}q>K_{n}+p_{\mathrm{F}} \\ K_{n}-p_{\mathrm{F}} \leqslant q \leqslant K_{n}+p_{\mathrm{F}} \\ q<K_{n}-p_{\mathrm{F}}\end{cases} \\ \tilde{G}\left(K_{n}-p_{\mathrm{F}}\right) & \end{cases} \tag{3}
$$
where the auxiliary function $\tilde{G}(x)$ is given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{G}(x)=N_{n}\{ & \left(p_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-K_{n}^{2}\right)\left(K_{n}+p_{\mathrm{F}}-x\right)-\frac{1}{3}\left[\left(K_{n}+p_{\mathrm{F}}\right)^{3}-x^{3}\right] \\
& \left.+K_{n}\left[\left(K_{n}+p_{\mathrm{F}}\right)^{2}-x^{2}\right]\right\} / 4 K_{n}. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $N_{n}$ denotes the number of reciprocal-lattice points in the $n$th cell. $p_{\mathrm{F}}$ corresponds to the calculated value of the Fermi momentum.

Compared with valence s electrons, the calculation of CP corresponding to p and d states is slightly tricky. The CP due to the valence d electrons can be calculated in the tight-binding limit assuming that the d states are symmetrically distributed spherically. Following Berggren (1972), we treat these electrons in the extreme tight-binding approximation. We construct the Bloch function for state $k$ as
$$
\psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})=N^{-1 / 2} \sum_{g} \exp \left(\mathrm{i} \boldsymbol{k} \cdot \boldsymbol{R}_{g}\right) \chi_{l}\left(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{R}_{g}\right) \tag{5}
$$
where $\chi_{l}$ represents an atomic orbital centred at site $\boldsymbol{R}_{g}$. The sum extends over all lattice sites.

The momentum density per atom can be written as
$$
\rho_{l}(\boldsymbol{p})=2 \frac{2 l+1}{4 \pi}\left|R_{l}(p)\right|^{2} \sum_{k} \delta_{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k}, \boldsymbol{K}_{n}} \tag{6}
$$

where $k$ runs over all occupied states. Here $R_l(p)$ is the FT of the normalised radial part
of the wavefunction $R_l(r)$:

$$
R_{l}(p)=\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{1 / 2} \int_{0}^{\infty} \mathrm{d} r r^{2} R_{l}(r) j_{l}(p r). \tag{7}
$$

The spherical average can be written as

$$
\left\langle\rho_{l}(p)\right\rangle=2[(2 l+1) / 4 \pi]\left|R_{l}(p)\right|^{2} Q(p) \tag{8}
$$

where

$$
Q(p)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} F_{n}(p). \tag{9}
$$

The auxiliary function $F_n(p)$ for $n=0$ is given as

$$
F_{0}(p)= \begin{cases}1 & p \leqslant p_{\mathrm{F}} \\ 0 & \text { otherwise }\end{cases} \tag{10}
$$

but for $n \neq 0$

$$
F_{n}(p)=N_{n}\left[p_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\left(K_{n}-p\right)^{2}\right] / 4 K_{n} p. \tag{11}
$$

Now the CP is evaluated using the formula

$$
J_{l}(q)=\int_{q}^{\infty}\left\langle\rho_{l}(p)\right\rangle p \mathrm{~d} p. \tag{12}
$$

More exactly, one can write

$$
J_{l}(q)=2 \frac{2 l+1}{4 \pi} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}\left|R_{l}(p)\right|^{2} F_{n}(p) p \mathrm{~d} p \tag{13}
$$

where

$$
p_{2}=K_{n}+p_{\mathrm{F}} \tag{14}
$$

and

$$
p_{1}= \begin{cases}q & \text { for } \begin{cases}K_{n}-p_{\mathrm{F}} \leqslant q \leqslant K_{n}+p_{\mathrm{F}} \\ q<K_{n}-p_{\mathrm{F}}.\end{cases} \end{cases} \tag{15}
$$

In order to apply the RFA model the approach of Mahapatra (1986) is followed.
The face-centred cubic (FCC) lattice of TiC is considered to be formed out of two
interpenetrating FCC sublattices of Ti ions and C ions, respectively. The CP of each ion
is calculated separately as a superposition of all the individual valence orbital CPS using
the appropriate formulae as given earlier. In this calculation the lattice constant was
taken to be 8.176 au corresponding to a WS radius of 3.195 au. For both configurations
the free-atom wavefunctions of Clementi and Roetti (1974) were used and the core
contributions were taken from the tables of Biggs et al (1975). In each case the CP
was normalised in the range 0.0-5.0 au to the free-atom Hartree-Fock value of 12.10
electrons. Further, to compare this with the experimental data of Manninen and Paakkari
(1976), the theoretical data were convoluted with the residual instrumental function of
the experiment (Manninen et al 1974).

## 3. Results and discussion

The spherically averaged CPs calculated using the two configurations are presented in table 1 together with the experimental data of Manninen and Paakkari (1976). In the same table, we have also included a CP calculated from the data of Seth *et al* (1977). One can clearly see that the CP obtained with the configuration of Neckel *et al* (1976) agrees remarkably well with the experimental data. It also agrees very well with the theoretical data of Seth *et al* (1977), but the CP corresponding to the configuration of Lye and Logothesis (1966) can be seen to provide only qualitative agreement with the experimental data as well as with the data of Seth *et al*. It has a sharper peak and is seen to deviate considerably from the experimental data, particularly around 1.5 au. This result clearly indicates that the valence electron configuration of Lye and Logothesis (1966) is incorrect in the sense that it cannot explain the CP data very well. This conclusion can also be derived in a slightly different manner.

The CP data when Fourier transformed yield $B(r)$, which is known as the reciprocal form factor (RFF) or the autocorrelation function. Unlike in the CP, in the RFF the contributions due to core and valence electrons separate out as short- and long-range contributions. For a solid with filled bands, the RFF corresponding to a CP measurement along a certain direction is known to vanish whenever $r$ equals a lattice translation along the same direction. However, for a system with unfilled bands the RFF essentially corresponds to the shape transform of the occupied volume in $k$ space. In the most simplified case, electrons corresponding to an unfilled band can be considered to be non-interacting free particles with a spherical Fermi surface. In such a case the RFF vanishes at a set of points given by $p_{\text{F}}r = 4.493, 7.725, \dots$ (Pattison and Williams 1976). In the present case, since we have used the RFA model, we expect the RFF to be similar to that corresponding to a simple metallic system, but there are bound to be some differences which are expected to arise because of the presence of highly localised d electrons. Localised d electrons in a metal are expected to produce finer oscillations in the RFF (Pattison *et al* 1982) which may not be noticeable in the RFF corresponding to a spherically averaged CP, but the CP in general will be broader than that due to a set of free electrons. Because of this the zero passages in $B(r)$ can no longer be used to determine the value of $p_{\text{F}}$. In order to check these facts the RFF due to the eight valence electrons of TiC was calculated for the four sets of data presented in table 1 and the results are shown in figure 1. One can see that, except for the RFF calculated from the data of Lye and Logothesis (1966), all the remaining curves are very similar. In fact the RFF due to the configuration of Lye and Logothesis does not show any negative autocorrelation which is quite contrary to the general expectation. On the basis of this as well as the poor agreement of the CP with the experimental data, such a configuration can clearly be ruled out.

It is very interesting to note that the remaining RFFs are very similar with negative dips around 4.5 au. The RFF due to Seth *et al* (1977) is closer to that due to the experimental CP

below 4.5 au above which the present data show better agreement with experiment. If one considers the free-electron product $p_{\mathrm{F}} r=4.493$, the first zero passage corresponding to the experimental data yields a value of 1.222 au for $p_{\mathrm{F}}$ which is quite close to the value of 1.203 au obtained considering eight valence electrons with a density of $4.938 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$. Compared with this, the RFF due to the remaining CPs yields values of 1.252 and 1.145 au, the lower one corresponding to the present data. All these values are very close to the $p_{\mathrm{F}}$ value of 1.203 au and hence indicate a metal-like system.

Compared with the experimental RFF the present RFF shows a reduced negative dip. At present, it is not possible to point out the exact reason behind this. We hope that the disagreement is mainly due to the following facts. First of all, the present calculation is based on non-overlapping Fermi spheres centred at all reciprocal-lattice points. This, in fact, is not true. The actual Fourier coefficients which weight each Fermi sphere contribution will be slightly different from the present values, where in addition the $k$ dependence is not considered at all. Further more, we have considered Ti and C ions on two different sublattices and calculated their contributions independently of each other. In the actual case, there will be a bonding interaction which will definitely affect the negative dip. However, in view of the simplicity of the model, such discrepancies can be ignored.

## 4. Conclusion

The aim of the present work was mainly to test the applicability of the RFA model in a complex system such as TiC and it is found to provide a CP in good agreement with experimental data. As shown earlier, the present calculations support the well accepted configuration where Ti is a donor and C is an acceptor. For this system, our results also indicate a metal-like system in agreement with the finding of some recent band-structure calculations.
