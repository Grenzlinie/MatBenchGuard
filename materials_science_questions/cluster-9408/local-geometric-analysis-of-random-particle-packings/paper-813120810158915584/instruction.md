# Random Particle Packing Characterization via Area Density Statistics

## Problem background
The structure of random particle packings serves as a basis for models of amorphous materials, powders, green compacts, and porous media. Characterising whether a computer-generated packing is truly random, homogeneous, and isotropic requires robust statistical tests. This work develops methods based on cutting the packing with equally spaced parallel planes and analysing the area densities of the particle cross‑sections on those planes. From these area‑density sequences one can quantify: convergence of the mean area density to the packing density; randomness via autocorrelation analysis; homogeneity by examining the spatial uniformity of particle counts; and isotropy both at the micro‑level (the distribution of relative projections of touching‑pair centre‑to‑centre lines) and at the macro‑level (comparison of area‑density variances along different axes). This task reproduces the computational pipeline that generates a random packing of equal spheres and evaluates it using all five tests, producing the complete set of quantitative metrics that constitute the paper‘s main experimental result.

## Approach
A packing of 10^4 equal spherical particles (mean radius normalised to 1.0) is generated by a collective rearrangement simulation with periodic boundary conditions. Particles are initially placed randomly; overlaps are reduced iteratively until the mean overlap falls below 10^{-4} of the mean radius. The final particle positions, radii, and pairs of touching particles are saved.

To evaluate the packing, it is cut by m=40 equally spaced planes perpendicular to each Cartesian axis (X, Y, Z). For each plane the area density is computed from the cross‑sections of the cut particles. The three sequences of 40 area‑density values form the basis for the statistical tests:

- **Randomness** – treat the area‑density sequence as a time series and compute autocorrelation coefficients C_k for lags k=1,…,8 per axis. For a completely random packing, C_k should lie within the random‑series bounds.
- **Homogeneity** – exclude a surface layer of particles, divide the remaining packing into 27 equal cubic subregions, count particles in each, and compute the chi‑square test statistic for a uniform distribution.
- **Isotropy (micro‑level)** – for every touching‑particle pair, compute the absolute relative projections |x'|, |y'|, |z'| of the centre‑to‑centre vector normalised by the sum of the two radii. For an isotropic packing these should follow a uniform distribution over [0,1]; evaluate via their means and variances per axis.
- **Isotropy (macro‑level)** – calculate the sample variances of the area densities for the X, Y, and Z cutting‑plane sequences, and perform F‑tests (F_xy and F_yz) to test whether the population variances differ significantly.

All numerical results are collected into a single JSON summary that characterises the packing.

The intermediate process files (`packing_data.json` and `area_densities.json`) must be generated as described because the statistical summary depends on them, but they are not directly scored; the whole reward is determined by the contents of `simulation_summary.json`.
