# Fe doping-stabilized $\gamma$-Ga₂O₃ thin films with a high room temperature saturation magnetic moment†

Yuanqi Huang, $^{\ddagger a}$ Ang Gao, $^{\ddagger b}$ Daoyou Guo, $^{c}$ Xia Lu, $^{de}$ Xiao Zhang, $^{af}$ Yalei Huang, $^{c}$ Jie Yu, $^{a}$ Shan Li, $^{a}$ Peigang Li*af and Weihua Tang*af

Saturation magnetic moment $(M_{sm})$ is a key evaluation parameter of diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMSs). The higher the $M_{sm}$, the more widespread the applications of electron spin for non-volatile information manipulation and storage. In this paper, first-principles simulations predicted that cation-deficient $\gamma$-$Ga_2O_3$, having an intrinsic magnetic moment per formula unit cell of about $1\ \mu_B$, is an inherent candidate to prepare DMS with high $M_{sm}$. To activate the potential of its magnetism, Fe was introduced as a dopant. A thermostable Fe-doped $\gamma$-$Ga_2O_3$ thin film with high room temperature (RT) $M_{sm}$ of $5.73\ \mu_B$/Fe was first obtained using the laser molecular beam epitaxy (L-MBE) technique. While combining theoretical calculations and experimental data, the main source of RT ferromagnetism could be ascribed to the strong ferromagnetic (FM) coupling between Fe ions, p-d orbital overlap of the Fe-O bond, and defects. Our results provide a feasible model for designing high-performance $\gamma$-$Ga_2O_3$-based DMSs.

## Introduction

Diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMSs) have motivated extensive research interest for the fabrication of fast nonvolatile semiconductor memories, integrated magneto-optic devices and microelectronics. They utilize charge as well as spin degree of freedom to guide the electrons and create currents. $^{1}$ Among them, a class of DMSs with a Curie temperature higher than RT has attracted special interest of both scientists and technologists because they can open a new approach for the applications of bulk, two-dimensional (2D) materials and other semi-magnetic semiconductors. There are a lot of evaluation parameters of DMSs, such as saturation magnetization $(M_{s})$, $M_{sm}$, magnetic remanence $(M_{r})$, coercivity and Curie temperature. Among them, $M_{sm}$ is the most vital property, which is directly related to the capacity of the use of electron spin for non-volatile information manipulation and storage devices. Hence, fabricating DMS with high RT $M_{sm}$ is an essential mission. Experimental and theoretical studies have indicated that high $M_{sm}$ can not only be induced by doping with magnetic elements such as transition metals or rare earth metals, $^{2-6}$ but also be enhanced by cation vacancies in $SiC,^{7-9}ZnO,^{10-12}TiO_2,^{13}GaN^{14,15}$ and other semiconductors.

$Ga_2O_3$, one of the ultra-wide bandgap (UWBG) semiconductors, having large intrinsic direct BG of 4.5-4.9 eV is promising for use in deep ultraviolet and high-power devices. $^{16-19}$ Among five polymorphs $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$ and $\varepsilon)$, $\gamma$-$Ga_2O_3$ is the only one that has a cation-deficient structure ($MgAl_2O_4$ cubic spinel type). $^{20}$ Since high photon energy as well as high transparency in the visible and ultraviolet regions (>280 nm) enables the easy manipulation of the spins by photons, possessing intrinsic cation vacancies and large BG make $\gamma$-$Ga_2O_3$ one of the best candidates for new-generation DMSs.

In the last decade, the epitaxial growth of the $\gamma$-$Ga_2O_3$ thin films has been studied on sapphire $^{21-23}$ and $MgAl_2O_4^{24-27}$ substrates. $Cu,^{21}Mn^{22-25}$ and $Si^{27}$ dopants have been used. To the best of our knowledge, Hayashi *et al.* were nearly the only and first team who studied the RT ferromagnetism behavior in $\gamma$-$Ga_2O_3$ thin films (RT $M_{sm}=0.42\ \mu_B$/Mn). $^{22}$ Experiments with first-principles calculations implied that the Mn atoms were located at tetrahedrally coordinated Ga sites with the valence of +2, and the cation vacancies were energetically favorable at the

In this paper, we provided convincing theoretical and experimental evidence that γ-Ga₂O₃ is a promising candidate host for DMSs with high RT Mₛₘ. First-principles calculations confirmed that the natural spinel structure of γ-Ga₂O₃ with cation vacancies itself has a slight magnetic moment. To activate the potential of its magnetism, Fe was used as a dopant. The obtained Fe-doped γ-Ga₂O₃ thin film exhibited high RT Mₛₘ of 5.73 μB/Fe. From the theoretical and experimental results, the origin of RT ferromagnetism might be ascribed to the strong FM coupling between Fe ions, p-d orbital overlap of the Fe–O bond, intrinsic cation vacancies and oxygen defects. This work provides an essential theory for designing γ-Ga₂O₃-based DMSs.

## Experimental section

### Theoretical calculation details

All first-principles calculations were implemented in the Vienna Ab Initio Simulation Package (VASP) based on Density Functional Theory (DFT).³⁷ The projected augmented wave (PAW)³⁸ potentials were used to deal with the exchange–correlation functional along with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) functional in the parameterization of the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) pseudopotential.³⁹ In this work, an effective $U$ value of $U_{\text{eff}} = 5$ eV was taken into account for the electronic structure prediction of the Fe 3d orbitals. The convergence criterion of energy and force are $10^{-5}$ eV and $0.01$ eV Å⁻¹ for the geometry optimizations. The $K$-point mesh with a spacing of $\sim 0.03$ Å⁻¹ was adopted for structural optimization and self-consistent calculations. The crystal structures were built using VESTA software.⁴⁰ The unit cell containing 56 atoms for Ga₃O₄ was adopted. A plane wave representation for the wave function with a cut off energy of 500 eV was applied.

### Experimental process details

Ga₂₋ₓFeₓO₃ thin films were grown on (0001) sapphire substrates by the L-MBE technique. Fe-doped Ga₂O₃ targets with different doping concentrations were used as precursors. The wavelength of the KrF excimer laser was 248 nm. The laser ablation was carried out at the laser energy of 400 mJ with a frequency of 2 Hz. The thin film deposition was in a vacuum environment of $10^{-5}$ Pa and at the growth temperature of 750 °C. Facing the substrate, the distance between the target and substrate was about 50 mm. After 2 hours and 46 minutes of deposition, all the samples went through an *in situ* annealing treatment for 1 hour, and the annealing temperature was 750 °C.

### Characterization details

An EDAX9100 energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) was used to detect the Fe dopant content in the thin films as 0, 5.38 and 9.62 at%. The crystalline structure and orientation of the films were analyzed *via* XRD (Bruker D8 Discover) $\theta$–2$\theta$ scans and pole figure measurements. The surface morphology was characterized by an Asylum Research atomic force microscope. The chemical compositions and valence of elements were tested by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). The optical absorption and BG were acquired by a UV/Visible/NIR spectrophotometer (Macy UV1900). The magnetic properties of the films were measured using a physical property measurement system (PPMS).

Generally, the site occupancies of dopants and vacancies are of considerable interest because they can be closely correlated with electronic, optical, and magnetic properties. While the actual molecular formula of the $\gamma$ phase should be Ga$_{2.67}$O$_4$ after matrixing [PDF#97-015-2085], three cation vacancies are introduced.$^{43}$ All coordinative modes have been considered (ESI,† Fig. S2) and the optimized $\gamma$-Ga$_{21}\square_3$O$_{32}$ unit cell is illustrated in Fig. 2a. Compared with standard PDF data, the $\gamma$-Ga$_{21}\square_3$O$_{32}$ unit cell showed a slight lattice expansion. In oxides, there is a strong link between the vacancy concentration and the molar volume of a compound, which is generally referred to as chemical expansivity.$^{44-46}$ The Ga/O ratios in the standard PDF data and $\gamma$-Ga$_{21}\square_3$O$_{32}$ unit cell are 0.6675 and the 0.6563, respectively. Hence, having more Ga vacancies results in the lattice parameter expansion of the $\gamma$-Ga$_{21}\square_3$O$_{32}$ unit cell. The most stable cation vacancy distribution is that the cation vacancies are long-range orderly distributed along the [111] crystal orientation and appear alternately in the form of T-O-T (ESI,† Fig. S3). While cation vacancies exist, there appear spin anisotropy and spin asymmetry in the TDOS pattern (Fig. 2b), indicating slight magnetism in $\gamma$-Ga$_{21}\square_3$O$_{32}$. General DFT calculations predict that the magnetic moment per formula unit cell is about $1\ \mu_B$. To further prove this, the net spin charge integral values of five distinct Ga positional configurations are calculated in the ESI,† Fig. S4. Herein, we can use the charge transfer (CT) mechanism to explain the source of magnetism. In order to obtain a stable non-magnetic state in $\gamma$-Ga$_{24}$O$_{32}$, each O atom needs to capture two extra electrons from the nearest-neighbor (NN) Ga atoms. When a vacancy substitutes a Ga atom, it also needs to transfer the original quantity of valence electrons to the neighboring O atoms. The asymmetry of electron occupation leads to the asymmetry of spin, due to which cation vacancies can induce magnetism.$^{47-50}$

According to the above calculations, $\gamma$-Ga$_{21}\square_3$O$_{32}$ is an inherent candidate host to prepare high-performance DMSs. However, it is well-known that the single crystalline $\gamma$ phase is hard to fabricate. Herein, a doping approach was used to obtain thermostable thin films (the reduced formation energy and impact on film morphology of Fe doping can be seen in the ESI,† Table S2$^{85,86}$ and Fig. S10) and the Fe dopant was adopted to enhance the magnetism.

It is well-known that localized magnetic moments and the coupling between them are two indispensable factors to induce long-range spin ordering, mainly exhibiting FM and antiferromagnetism (AFM). To explore the origin of the high RT $M_{\text{sm}}$ property in Fe-doped $\gamma$-Ga$_2$O$_3$ thin films, a theoretical calculation of the Fe doping process was applied (for the origin of the RT ferromagnetic property in Fe-doped $\beta$-Ga$_2$O$_3$ thin films, please see the ESI,† E1).$^{87}$ The most stable monoatomic doping model is shown in Fig. 5a, where the Fe atom is located at the octahedrally coordinated Ga sites (for the optimization details, please see the ESI,† Fig. S6). Fig. 5b gives the total and local DOS of $\gamma$-Ga$_{20}$Fe$_1$$\square_3$O$_{32}$. General DFT predicts that the total magnetic moment is $4\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ per formula unit cell. As the magnetic moment of high spin Fe$^{3+}$ ($3\text{d}^5$) is $5\ \mu_{\text{B}}$/Fe, the configuration herein should be an AFM ground state.

## Conclusions

In summary, theoretical and experimental studies suggested that the stable Fe-doped $\gamma$-Ga₂O₃ magnetic thin films can be promising candidates for applications in high RT $M_{\text{sm}}$ spintronic devices. The electronic structures of pure and Fe-doped $\gamma$-Ga₂O₃ were investigated using first-principles calculations. The calculated results on the defective spinel structured $\gamma$-Ga₂₁□₃O₃₂ unambiguously verified that cation vacancies can induce magnetism. The most stable system as well as highly asymmetric spin polarization and high RT $M_{\text{sm}}$ property could be obtained in the case of only two Fe atoms substituting the NN octahedrally coordinated Ga atoms. Experimentally, Fe-doped Ga₂O₃ (0, 5.38 and 9.62 at%) thin films were deposited on sapphire substrates by the L-MBE technique. With the increase in the Fe dopant concentration, the lattice strain induced a phase transition from $\beta$ to $\gamma$. Meanwhile, the XPS results indicated that the bonding mode of the Ga–O–Fe bond changed in the Fe-doped $\gamma$-Ga₂O₃ thin film. RT ferromagnetism appeared in both Fe-doped $\beta$-/$\gamma$-Ga₂O₃ thin films, and the magnetic properties were apparently enhanced compared with that reported in some works. The FM coupling between Fe ions, the strong p–d orbital overlap and lots of defects resulted in high RT $M_{\text{sm}}$.
