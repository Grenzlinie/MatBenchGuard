# Structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of gas molecules on Mo-, Si-, and Pt-doped $BC_3$ sheets

Yanan Tang $^{a,b,1*}$, Minghui Zhang $^{a}$, Weiguang Chen $^{a,1}$, Xiao Cui $^{a}$, Yi Li $^{b}$, and Xianqi Dai $^{a,b*}$

$^{a}$ College of Physics and Electronic Engineering, Quantum Materials Research Center, Zhengzhou Normal University, Zhengzhou Henan, 450044, China

$^{b}$ College of Physics and Electronic Engineering, Henan Normal University, Xinxiang Henan, 453007, China

## Abstract

In this study, based on first principles calculations, we compared the performance during the sensing of toxic gas molecules (NO, $NO_2$, HCN, and $NH_3$) by Mo, Si, and Pt dopants within $BC_3$ sheets (D-$BC_3$). Compared with the Pt-$BC_3$ sheet, the Si-$BC_3$ and Mo-$BC_3$ sheets exhibit a stronger affinity for the adsorption of gas molecules. The adsorbed NO and HCN had larger energy differences than those of the $NO_2$ and $NH_3$ molecules, thereby indicating that $NO_2$ and $NH_3$ could be detected readily as specific gas molecule on the D-$BC_3$ substrates. In addition, the adsorption of gas molecules induced great changes in the electronic structure and magnetic properties of the D-$BC_3$ systems. In particular, the adsorption of NO on the Pt-$BC_3$ and Si-$BC_3$ substrates yielded a larger magnetic moment (2.0 $u_B$) than those when the other gases were adsorbed on D-$BC_3$ sheets. These results may facilitate control over the adsorption properties of toxic gas molecules and the design of $BC_3$-based gas sensors or spintronic devices.

### 1. Introduction

It is well known that small gas molecules such as HCN, NO, NO₂, and NH₃ are ubiquitous in the air and they are considered major gaseous pollutants. These toxic gases are generated from reactive intermediate products due to incomplete combustion and they are harmful to human health [1-3]. Toxic gas molecules are monitored widely for purposes such as environmental protection, industrial production, and medical diagnostics [4-7]. The miniaturized sensors employed for monitoring should have a rapid response, high sensitivity, good reliability, and a low detection limit [8-10]. Many studies have explored high performance solid-state gas sensors. Since the discovery of graphene [11-13], thin two-dimensional (2D) nanomaterials have been prepared for various applications in monitoring, such as graphene derivatives [14, 15], hexagonal boron nitride (BN) [16], transition metal dichalcogenides [17-19], silicene [20, 21], and black phosphorus [22-24]. Furthermore, theoretical studies have predicted the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties as well as the thermal conductivity of BC alloys, such as BC₂N monolayers [25-27]. Graphene-like BC₃ is a compound semiconductor with an indirect band gap of about 0.50–0.66 eV [28-30], and uniform BC₃ sheets with a honeycomb lattice have been synthesized in experiments [31, 32]. After graphene, the BC₃ sheet may be a good candidate for potential applications in monitoring.

Recently, some experimental and theoretical studies have shown that metal decorated carbon nanotubes or BN nanotubes can be used to detect different types of gas molecules, such as CO, SO₂, NO, and H₂CO [33-42]. In particular, the 2D crystal structures of graphene-like nanomaterials have unique electronic, mechanism, optical, and thermal properties [12, 22, 43-47]. Their huge specific surface-to-volume ratio makes them efficient at adsorbing gas molecules, which is essential for applications in gas sensors, catalysis, and energy conversion [48-59]. Pristine graphene exhibits low sensitivity to some gas molecules such as NO₂, H₂S, and H₂O [60-62] because of the weak interaction and insufficient charge transfer between the adsorbates and substrates, which limits its potential for detecting individual gas molecules. However, it has been reported that functionalization, defects, and substitutional doping of metal or non-metal atoms can regulate the electronic and magnetic properties of 2D nanomaterials [63-68]. The gas sensitivity of graphene-based materials can be improved effectively by introducing metal

and non-metal dopants for detecting $NO_{2}, H_{2}S, SO_{2}, H_{2}O$, and $NH_{3}$ [69-74]. In addition, studies have demonstrated that pristine and Li-doped $BC_{3}$ sheets have high potential for hydrogen storage [75-77]. Investigations of the electronic and magnetic properties of pristine $BC_{3}$ sheets [78, 79] as well as those with defects [80-82] and dopants [83-85] have shown that they exhibit high sensitivity to $H_{2}O, H_{2}S$, and $H_{2}CO$. Previous theoretical studies of the $BC_{3}$ system have mainly focused on its electronic structure and surface reactivity [83, 86-88] in order to extend their applications in the fields of gas sensing and spintronic devices.

The chemical activity and sensitivity of nanomaterial-based gas sensors can be improved dramatically by introducing appropriate dopants [89]. In this study, we investigated doping with three different elements: Pt is used widely as a catalyst for the conversion of harmful gases and electrode reactions in fuel cells [90-92]; Mo is a $4d$ transition metal that can greatly modify the electronic and magnetic properties of the graphene system [93-95]; and the Si atom has a similar electronic configuration to the C atom and it is known to strongly prefer $sp^{3}$-like bonding [96, 97]. There have been no previous systematic analyses of the adsorption properties of different adatoms (metal and non-metal) on the $BC_{3}$ system in pristine and single vacancy states (pri-$BC_{3}$ and SV-$BC_{3}$, respectively), as well as their geometric, electronic, and gas sensing properties with toxic gas molecules containing nitrogen. Thus, we investigated the adsorption behavior and diffusion pathways of Mo, Pt, and Si adatoms on the pri-$BC_{3}$ sheet. The doped atoms within the SV-$BC_{3}$ configuration (D-$BC_{3}$) exhibited high stability. We then systematically determined the adsorptive stability of gas molecules and the changes they induced in the electronic and magnetic properties of D-$BC_{3}$ systems.

### 2. Computational model and methods
All of the spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [98, 99] with the projector augmented wave pseudo-potentials [100]. We used the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the form of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [101] for the exchange-correlation functions. The kinetic energy cutoff for the plane-wave basis set was selected as 450 eV. The Mo $4d^{5}5s^{1}$, Pt $5d^{9}6s^{1}$, C $2s^{2}2p^{2}$, N $2s^{2}2p^{3}$, O $2s^{2}2p^{4}$, Si $3s^{2}3p^{2}$, and H $1s^{1}$ electron configurations were treated as valence

electrons. The adsorption of a single atom (Mo, Pt, or Si) was considered on hexagonal 2
× 2 BC₃ unit cells (including 32 non-metal atoms). In addition, the vacuum layer was set
to 16 Å between BC₃ sheets along the z direction, which led to negligible interactions
between mirror image layers. In order to improve the convergence of states near the
Fermi level (E_F), the Brillouin zone integration was sampled using a 3 × 3 × 1 Γ-centered
Monkhorst–Pack grid, and a Γ-centered Monkhorst–Pack grid of 15 × 15 × 1 was used
for the final density of states (DOS) calculations. Bader charge analysis [102] was used to
evaluate the atomic charges and electron transfer in the reactions.

The adsorption energy ($E_{ads}$) was calculated using the formula:

$$
\mathrm{E_{ads}} = \mathrm{E_A} + \mathrm{E_B} - \mathrm{E_{AB}}, \tag{1}
$$

where E_A, E_B, and E_AB are the total energies of the optimized adsorbates in the molecules
or atoms (A: Mo, Pt, Si, HCN, NO, NO₂, and NH₃), the clean graphene substrates (B:
pri-BC₃ and D-BC₃), and the adsorbate–substrate systems, respectively. According to this
definition, a higher E_ads value indicates stronger adsorption.

### 3. Results and discussion

#### 3.1. Formation geometry and electronic properties

As shown in Fig. 1(a), the geometric structure of the BC₃ sheet was almost identical to
that of graphene, where some carbon atoms were replaced by boron atoms so six carbon
atoms formed a hexagon surrounded by six boron atoms. The calculated bond lengths of
the B-C bond and C-C bond were 1.56 Å and 1.42 Å, respectively, which agreed well
with previous calculations [30, 80]. First, we investigated the adsorption properties of
single Mo, Pt, and Si adatoms on the 2 × 2 pri-BC₃ unit cell, where the distance between
adatoms in the supercell was larger than 10 Å so the interactions between neighboring
adatoms could be ignored. In order to determine the most stable configuration for the
metal adatom on the BC₃ surface, we performed a scan of the energy of the adsorbed
atom at the possible adsorption sites, including the hollow sites of the C-B (H1) and C-C
rings (H2), the top sites of the B (T1) and C atoms (T2), and the bridge sites of the B-C
(B1) and C-C bonds (B2), as shown in Fig. 1(a). The adsorption energies, bond lengths,
and transferred electrons calculated for the adatoms on the pri-BC₃ systems are shown in
Table 1. We found that the adatoms were more stable at H1 or H2 than the other sites. In
particular, the Mo adatom was preferentially adsorbed on the H2 site, whereas the Si and


Pt adatoms could be stabilized at the H1 site, and the corresponding distance between the adatom and carbon atoms changed from 2.17 to 2.32 Å.

An example of Mo adsorbed on pri-BC₃ (Mo/pri-BC₃) is shown in Fig. 1(b) and the other adatom/pri-BC₃ systems followed this template. The adsorbed Mo atom preferred to be anchored at the H2 site with an $E_{\text{ads}}$ of 3.05 eV, which was larger than the values on H1 (2.40 eV), T1 (1.88 eV), and T2 (1.97 eV) (B1 and B2 sites are unstable), and the corresponding bond length for Mo-C was 2.17 Å, as shown in Table 1. Compared with the single Mo, Pt, or Si atom on a graphene sheet [97, 103], these adatoms had higher adsorption energies (> 2.0 eV) on the pri-BC₃ surface. In order to characterize the weak or strong interactions between the adsorbate and substrate, we investigated the diffusion pathway for single adatoms on the BC₃ sheet from the H1 site to the H2 (or H1) site. The energy barriers ($E_{\text{bar}}$) calculated for Mo (0.43 eV), Si (0.73 eV), and Pt (0.20 eV) adatoms on pri-BC₃ were larger than those on the graphene sheet (Pt = 0.14 eV), but the relatively small energy barriers indicate that these adatoms are mobile on the BC₃ surface and they may aggregate to form clustered structures. Hence, the pri-BC₃ sheet is not an ideal substrate for improving the dispersion of catalysts.

To address this problem, one B atom was substituted with a single Mo atom on the pri-BC₃ sheet (Mo-BC₃), as shown in Fig. 1(c). Table 1 lists the adsorption energies and structural parameters for the D-BC₃ systems. At the B vacancy on the BC₃ sheet, the $E_{\text{ads}}$ for a single Mo adatom was 6.59 eV and the Mo-C bond distance was 1.98 Å. Compared with the adatoms on the pri-BC₃ sheet (2.97-3.53 eV), the dopants at SV-BC₃ had much larger adsorption energies (from 6.59 to 7.95 eV). More electrons transferred from the dopants to the SV-BC₃ sheet (-0.53 to -2.87 $|e|$) and the formation of covalent bonds between the dopants and neighboring carbon atoms could enhance their interactions. In addition, the Mo and Pt dopants were displaced outside from the BC₃ surface because the atomic radii of the metal atoms were larger than that of the carbon atom, and the bond lengths between the dopants and C atoms changed from 1.73 to 1.95 Å. Moreover, we investigated the stabilities of dopants in the C vacancy on the BC₃ sheets. The results showed that the Mo, Si, and Pt atoms doped in the C vacancy on the BC₃ sheet (5.54, 6.28, and 3.51 eV, respectively) exhibited lower stability compared with those in the B vacancy (6.59, 7.81, and 7.95 eV, respectively), thereby demonstrating that the dopants in

the B vacancy were quite stable on the defective $BC_3$ nanosheets.

As shown in Fig. 1(d), the electron distribution value was larger at the Mo-C bonds, which demonstrated that more electrons transferred $(-2.54\ |e|)$ from the doped Mo atom to the SV-BC₃ sheet, so the Mo dopant in the $BC_3$ system (6.59 eV) was more stable than that on the pri-$BC_3$ sheet (3.05 eV), which was similar to the stability of the Mo dopant on the monovacancy graphene (7.25 eV). Furthermore, Bader charge analysis showed that more electrons transferred from Mo $(-2.54$ or $-1.49\ |e|)$ and Si $(-2.87$ or $-1.44\ |e|)$ adatoms to the SV-$BC_3$ (or pri-$BC_3$) sheet compared with those from the Pt adatom $(-2.53$ or $-0.26\ |e|)$ because the Si $(s2p2)$ and Mo $(4p5s4d)$ adatoms had more $s$ and $p$ valence electrons than the Pt $(s1d9)$ atom. As shown in Fig. 1(e), we investigated the spin charge redistribution between the Mo atom and SV-$BC_3$ sheet, where more electrons accumulated in the vicinity of the Mo-C interfaces whereas fewer electrons were located on the neighboring carbon atoms. The uneven distribution of the spin charge induced the magnetic property of the Mo-$BC_3$ system $(2.0\ u_B)$.

Figure 2(a) shows the total DOS (TDOS) and partial DOS (PDOS) plots for the $BC_3$ systems with and without Mo adsorption. Compared with the semiconducting property of the pri-$BC_3$ system, the band gap moved to a lower energy and the DOS peaks were located at the $E_F$, and thus the SV-$BC_3$ exhibited metallic properties. In addition, the spin-up and spin-down channels were symmetric for the pri-$BC_3$ and SV-$BC_3$ systems, thereby indicating that these systems had non-magnetic characteristics $(0.0\ u_B)$. For the Mo-$BC_3$ system, the SV-$BC_3$ system states changed greatly when doped with Mo and the PDOS peaks for the Mo $4d$ states overlapped with the TDOS for the systems around the $E_F$, which suggests that there was a strong interaction between the Mo atom and SV-$BC_3$. In addition, the continuous DOS peaks were distributed in the spin-up channels because the transferred electrons moved from the Mo dopant to the SV-$BC_3$ and then saturated the dangling bonds of the adjacent C atoms. According to the DOS plots, the asymmetry of the spin channels indicated that the Mo-$BC_3$ system exhibited magnetic characteristics $(2.0\ u_B)$, and thus doping with the Mo atom was responsible for the electronic and magnetic properties of the $BC_3$ system.

As shown in Fig. 2(b–c), the DOS peaks of the Si-$BC_3$ and Pt-$BC_3$ systems around $E_F$ were partially modified compared with the SV-$BC_3$ systems, and the symmetric spin

channels of the $Si-BC_3$ and $Pt-BC_3$ systems showed that these systems exhibited non-magnetic characteristics. The broadened TDOS peaks overlapped with the Si $3p$ states around the $E_F$ to indicate the strong hybridization between the Si atom and SV-BC$_3$ sheet. In addition, the DOS peaks were located at the $E_F$ and the $Si-BC_3$ system exhibited metallic properties, and thus the semimetal $BC_3$ system was converted into a metallic material when doped with the Si atom. Moreover, the unsaturated $d$-shells of the Mo and Pt dopants formed stronger covalent bonds with the carbon atoms at the vacancy site. The broadened PDOS of the Pt $5d$ states overlapped more strongly with the TDOS of the system than those of the Mo $4d$ states, and thus the Pt doped at the SV-BC was more stable than that of the Mo adatom. Overall, the results showed that the electronic structures of the pri-$BC_3$ and $SV-BC_3$ systems could be regulated by different types of atoms. The semiconducting properties of $BC_3$ were converted into metallic properties for the $D-BC_3$ systems. Compared with Mo on the pri-$BC_3$ system, doping with the Mo atom increased the magnetic moment of the system, whereas the $Si-BC_3$ and $Pt-BC_3$ systems exhibited non-magnetic properties.

### 3.2. Toxic gas molecules on D-BC3 sheets
The calculations presented above demonstrate that the $SV-BC_3$ sheets doped with Mo, Si, and Pt atoms exhibited high stability (> 6.0 eV), thereby indicating that the $D-BC_3$ systems would be sufficiently stable for use in chemical reactions. The dopant atoms transferred more electrons to the $BC_3$ substrates and they were more positively charged, and thus the surface active sites could regulate the stability of gas molecules. Each structure was fully relaxed and the possible configurations of several molecular orientations on the $D-BC_3$ substrates were tested. In the initial orientation, the gas molecule could be aligned in parallel or perpendicular with respect to the surface of the $BC_3$. For example, the H or C atom in HCN pointed toward the surface, the O or N atom within NO and $NO_2$ molecules could point up and down, and the N or H atom pointed down to the $BC_3$ surface. The most energetically favorable adsorption configurations were selected for further studies. The adsorption energies, bond length, adsorption distance, and charge transfer for gas molecules on $D-BC_3$ substrates are summarized in Table 2.

#### 3.2.1. $Mo-BC_3$ substrate

For the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ substrate, the most stable configurations of the toxic gas molecules are shown in Fig. 3. As shown in Fig. 3(a), the adsorbed HCN was parallel to the plane of the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ sheet. The N atom of HCN was closer to the Mo atom with $\text{E}_{\text{ads}} = 1.03$ eV, and the distance between HCN-Mo and the amount of electrons transferred from the Mo atom to HCN were about $2.13$ Å and $+0.64\ |e|$, respectively. The H-C and C-N bonds within HCN measured $1.09$ and $1.23$ Å, respectively. As shown in Fig. 3(b), the end-on configuration was favorable for the NO molecule on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ surface, and the adsorbed NO molecule had a larger $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$ (2.13 eV) than the HCN molecule. The adsorbed NO was nearly vertical to the surface and the corresponding NO-Mo distance and N-O bond were $1.86$ and $1.19$ Å, respectively. Compared with the electrons gained by HCN ($+0.64\ |e|$), the adsorbed NO gained more electrons ($+0.69\ |e|$) by transfer from the Mo atom and it exhibited greater stability on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ sheet.

As shown in Fig. 3(c), the adsorption of $\text{NO}_2$ on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ sheet had the largest $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$ value (2.38 eV) compared with the others and the N-O bond was $1.29$ Å. Two O atoms in $\text{NO}_2$ pointed toward the Mo atom and the O-Mo bond length changed from $2.15$ to $2.20$ Å. The most energetically favorable configuration of $\text{NH}_3$ on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ surface is shown in Fig. 3(d). The N atom was directly bonded to the Mo atom with a relatively larger $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$ value of $1.24$ eV compared with that for the HCN molecule, and the corresponding distance between $\text{NH}_3$ and the Mo atom was $2.34$ Å. We found that the adsorbed HCN, NO, and $\text{NO}_2$ could act as acceptors, whereas the $\text{NH}_3$ molecule acted as a donor on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ sheet, as shown in a previous study [3]. In addition, the adsorption energies and geometries of the gas molecules on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ sheets were investigated by using the DFT-D2 method and the results are shown in Table S1. We found that the $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$ values for HCN (1.16 eV), NO (2.23 eV), $\text{NO}_2$ (2.54 eV), and $\text{NH}_3$ (1.44 eV) on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ sheet were slightly larger than those obtained using the PBE functional, but the corresponding adsorption configurations were almost unchanged. Thus, the preferable adsorption energies of the gas molecules on the $Mo\text{-}BC_3$ sheets were increased slightly due to the introduction of the van der Waals (vdW) interaction (such as DFT+D2), but the vdW correction did not affect the stable structures and the trends in the stability of the adsorbates.

As shown in Fig. 4(a–d), we investigated the valence charge density distribution

between the gas molecules and $Mo-BC_3$ substrate. We found that more electrons accumulated in the vicinity of the HCN-Mo, NO-Mo, and $NO_2$-Mo interfaces, whereas fewer electrons were located on the $BC_3$ surface. Bader charge analysis showed that the doped Mo atoms lost about $-2.51$ to $-2.67\ |e|$ in the gas molecules on the $Mo-BC_3$ systems, which were partly transferred to the adsorbed gases ($+0.64$ to $+0.74\ |e|$) and the remainder were transferred to the $BC_3$ substrate, thereby indicating that the embedded atoms could facilitate the interaction between the adsorbates and substrates via the transferred electrons. In addition, the HCN, NO, and $NO_2$ molecules acted as acceptors to gain electrons from the $Mo-BC_3$ substrate (without $NH_3$), and the adsorption became more stable when the adsorbed molecules gained more electrons.

To obtain more insights into the stability of adsorbates on the $D-BC_3$ substrate, we investigated the electronic structures induced in the $Mo-BC_3$ systems due to the adsorbed gas molecules. The $Mo-BC_3$ sheet exhibited magnetic characteristics according to the asymmetry of the spin channels. The transfer of electrons between the adsorbate and substrate interfaces decreased the number of unpaired electrons in the system, thereby explaining the loss of the magnetic properties. For the adsorbed HCN molecule, the DOS plots for the $Mo-BC_3$ system were obviously modified compared with those obtained for the bare $Mo-BC_3$ system, as shown in Fig. 5(a). The broadened PDOS determined for the Mo-$4d$ states were strongly hybridized with the local DOS (LDOS) for HCN and the TDOS for the system. Compared with the Mo $4d$ orbital within $Mo-BC_3$, the large decrease in the Mo $4d$ orbital in the HCN adsorbed on the $Mo-BC_3$ system was due to the charge transfer ($+0.64\ |e|$) from the occupied Mo-$4d$ orbital to the LDOS of HCN, which was responsible for the strong HCN-Mo binding. According to the distributions of the DOS plots, the spin-up and spin-down channels became asymmetric, and thus the HCN adsorbed on the $Mo-BC_3$ system exhibited magnetic properties $(0.60\ \mathfrak{u}_B)$.

Figure 5(b) shows the DOS plots obtained for NO on the $Mo-BC_3$ substrate. Compared with the bare $Mo-BC_3$ system, the asymmetry of the spin channels around $E_F$ vanished and the PDOS of the Mo $4d$ orbital strongly hybridized with the LDOS of NO, thereby indicating the transfer of electrons from the Mo atom to NO ($+0.69\ |e|$). In addition, the spin-up and spin-down channels in the DOS plots became symmetric, and thus NO on the $Mo-BC_3$ system exhibited non-magnetic properties $(0.0\ \mathfrak{u}_B)$. As shown in

Fig. 5(c), the narrowed DOS peaks at the $E_F$ indicated that the $Mo-BC_3$ system provided the electrons transferred to the adsorbed $NO_2$. Compared with the adsorbed NO, more orbitals overlapped between the LDOS of $NO_2$, PDOS of the Mo atom, and TDOS of the system. The gain of more electrons by $NO_2$ increased the number of unpaired electrons in the system, so the spin-up and spin-down channels became asymmetric, and thus the whole system exhibited magnetic properties (1.0 $u_B$).

As an important reference, we also obtained the DOS plots for $NH_3$ on the $Mo-BC_3$ substrate, as shown in Fig. 5(d). The orbitals overlapped between the LDOS of $NH_3$, PDOS of the Mo-$4d$ orbitals, and TDOS of the system, where they were less than those with $NO_2$ adsorption. In addition, the DOS peaks still appeared at the $E_F$ and they were increased slightly for $NH_3$ on $Mo-BC_3$ compared with that for the bare $Mo-BC_3$ because electrons transferred from the adsorbed $NH_3$ to the substrate. The spin channels were asymmetric in $NH_3$ on $Mo-BC_3$ and it exhibited magnetic properties (1.0 $u_B$). According to the calculated results, we found that the DOS plots for the gases adsorbed on $Mo-BC_3$ changed dramatically due to their strong interactions. The adsorption of the gas molecules differed in terms of stability, and they could regulate the electronic structure and magnetic properties of the $Mo-BC_3$ system. The $NO_2$ and $NH_3$ adsorbed on the $Mo-BC_3$ system yielded a larger magnetic moment (1.0 $u_B$) than that with the HCN molecule (0.6 $u_B$), whereas the NO adsorbed on the $Mo-BC_3$ system resulted in non-magnetic properties.

#### 3.2.2. Si- and $Pt-BC_3$ substrates

In order to explore the effects of different $D-BC_3$ substrates on the stability of gas molecules, we investigated the adsorption properties of the HCN, NO, $NO_2$, and $NH_3$ molecules on the $Si-BC_3$ and $Pt-BC_3$ sheets, as shown in Figs. S1-S2. Figure S1(a-d) demonstrates that the stable configurations of individual gas molecules on the $Si-BC_3$ sheet were similar to those on the $Mo-BC_3$ sheet. It should be noted that one of the O atoms within $NO_2$ bonded to the Si atom with an Si-O bond length of 1.71 $\mathring{A}$, which differed from that in the $Mo-BC_3$ and $Pt-BC_3$ sheets, as shown in Fig. S1(c). The $E_{ads}$ values for HCN and NO molecules decreased greatly from 1.03 eV to 0.24 eV (for HCN) and from 2.13 to 1.18 eV (for NO), whereas the $E_{ads}$ values for $NO_2$ and $NH_3$ increased slightly on the $Si-BC_3$ sheet. Figure S2 shows the stable configurations of the individual

gas molecules adsorbed on the Pt-BC₃ substrate. The adsorption energies of the gas molecules were greatly reduced compared with those on the Mo-BC₃ sheet, as shown in Table 2. In addition, the strength of the interaction between the adsorbate and substrate was connected to the charge transfer, where the gain of electrons by the adsorbed gases determined the adsorption energy values. Compared with the HCN, NO, and NH₃ molecules, the adsorbed NO₂ gained more electrons by transfer from the D-BC₃ substrate and it exhibited greater stability.

The bonding between the active sites of catalysts and adsorbed species should not be excessively strong or weak [104]. We found that the different D-BC₃ substrates could effectively regulate the stability of the adsorption of toxic gas molecules. The energy values for the adsorbed gases clearly indicated that the HCN and NO molecules had larger energy differences (> 1.0 eV) on the D-BC₃ substrates than the adsorbed NO₂ and NH₃, and thus the adsorbed HCN and NO could be readily influenced by different reaction conditions, and we detected the higher stability of NO₂ and NH₃ as specific gas molecules. Therefore, the D-BC₃ substrates could be used as highly effective gas-sensitive nanomaterials for specific small molecules (NO₂ and NH₃).

To further understand the changes in the magnetic properties of the D-BC₃ systems according to the adsorption of different gases, we investigated the spin charge density plots for HCN, NO, and NO₂ adsorbed on different D-BC₃ sheets, as shown in Fig. 6, where the contour lines in the plots represent 0.003 |e|/Å³ intervals. In general, the transfer of electrons helped to explain the changes in the magnetic properties of the gases adsorbed on the substrates compared with the isolated substrate because the transfer of electrons at the adsorbate and substrate interfaces increased (or decreased) the number of unpaired electrons in the systems [72]. The adsorbed HCN and NO₂ molecules could induce the redistribution of the spin charge in the Mo-BC₃ system, as shown in Fig. 6(a-b). We found that fewer spin electrons were located in the Mo-BC₃ systems with the adsorbed HCN (or NO₂) molecule compared with the isolated system (2.0 u_B) because the transfer of electrons between gas molecules and the Mo-BC₃ sheets decreased the number of spin electrons in the system, and thus the magnetic moments for HCN (0.60 u_B) and NO₂ (1.00 u_B) were small on the Mo-BC₃ surfaces. For HCN on the Si-BC₃ system, we found that the spin charge distribution between the HCN-Si interfaces differed from that

on the $Mo-BC_3$ surface. The adsorbed HCN gained electrons transferred from the $Si-BC_3$ sheet and the induced spin electrons accumulated mainly on the HCN molecule, and thus this system exhibited a magnetic moment $(1.0\ u_B)$, as shown in Fig. 6(c). Figure 6(d-e) demonstrates that the spin charge distributions were mainly located at the adsorption interfaces with the NO molecules. Compared with the adsorption of HCN and $NO_2$, the more pronounced spin electrons between the NO and $Si-BC_3$ (or $Pt-BC_3$) demonstrated that these systems had larger magnetic moments $(2.0\ u_B)$. Therefore, the spin charge distribution and magnetic moments of the $D-BC_3$ systems can be regulated by different types of adsorbed gases, and this effect may have important applications in electronic and spintronic devices.

According to the results presented above, the gases adsorbed on the $D-BC_3$ substrates exhibited differences in stability. The $Mo-BC_3$ and $Si-BC_3$ sheets acted as reactive films with greater sensing performance for toxic gas molecules than the $Pt-BC_3$ sheet. As shown by the DOS plots, the high hybridization between the LDOS of the adsorbed gases, PDOS of the Mo $4d$, Pt $5d$ orbitals, and LDOS of the $D-BC_3$ systems indicated the strong interactions between the gas molecules and substrates. In addition, the gases adsorbed on the $D-BC_3$ systems exhibited various magnetic properties, where adsorbed NO on $Si-BC_3$ and $Pt-BC_3$ yielded larger magnetic moments than others, and thus we could distinguish the types of adsorbed species based on the changes in the magnetic properties of the systems. Therefore, the electronic structures and magnetic properties of the $BC_3$ substrates could be regulated by the adsorbed atoms and gas molecules. These results provide a reference to facilitate the design of $BC_3$-based nanomaterials for use as novel gas sensors and electronic devices.

### 4. Conclusions
Based on first principles calculations, we explored the stable configurations of $D-BC_3$ as a potential sensing device for detecting toxic gas molecules. First, we showed that the adsorption of Mo, Si, and Pt atom was more stable at H1 (or H2) than the other sites, and the relatively small diffusion barriers were unfavorable for the dispersion of catalysts. The dopants within the $SV-BC_3$ sheet exhibited greater stability and the more positively charged dopants could regulate the stability of the gases. Second, the $Mo-BC_3$ and $Si-BC_3$ sheets performed better at sensing the adsorbed gases than those on the $Pt-BC_3$ substrate.

We found that the adsorption of NO and $NO_2$ on D-BC₃ sheets was more stable than that of the HCN and $NH_3$ molecules. In addition, the adsorbed HCN, $NO_2$, and $NH_3$ could regulate the magnetic properties of the Mo-BC₃ system, and the NO adsorbed on the Pt-BC₃ and Si-BC₃ substrates yielded larger magnetic moments. Therefore, the electronic structures and magnetic properties of D-BC₃ systems can be tuned by the adsorption of gas molecules, which should facilitate the design of BC₃-based gas detection devices.
