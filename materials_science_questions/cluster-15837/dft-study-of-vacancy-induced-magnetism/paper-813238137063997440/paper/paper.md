## ABSTRACT

First-principle calculations were used to study the magnetic properties of Zn-doped $\text{SnO}_2$. We first investigated the energy of formation for oxygen vacancies ($V_O$), tin vacancies ($V_{Sn}$), interstitial oxygen ($O_i$), interstitial tin ($Sn_i$), Zn substitution of Sn ($Zn_{Sn}$), and Zn substitution of O ($Zn_O$). We then studied ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) coupling in Zn-doped $\text{SnO}_2$. The results show that the FM state has lower energy than the AFM state for all configurations, indicating that Zn-doped $\text{SnO}_2$ has FM stability. This stability can be explained by interaction of the O 2p level. We also analyzed the impact of $V_O$ and $V_{Sn}$ on FM coupling. $V_{Sn}$ can enhance FM coupling, but $V_O$ is unfavorable for FM coupling.

## 1. Introduction

In recent years, diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMS) have attracted great attention owing to their potential application in spintronic devices [1–5]. A key requirement in realizing these devices is that the DMS must be ferromagnetic above room temperature. In addition, it is necessary to have efficient spin-polarized carriers. Most previous studies have focused on magnetic transition metal (TM)-doped group III–V and II–VI semiconductors [6–10]. However, magnetic TMs easily form ferromagnetic clusters or secondary phases. These extrinsic magnetic behaviors are undesirable for practical applications and have led to controversy about the origin of ferromagnetism. Recently, unexpected ferromagnetism has been observed in a series of materials containing non-magnetic ions [11–18]. This discovery has provoked strong interest in further investigating the ferromagnetism of such materials.

$\text{SnO}_2$ usually has a rutile structure with a wide bandgap of 3.6 eV and has been used as a transparent conducting electrode in solar cells and flat panel displays. It has been found that magnetic TM-doped $\text{SnO}_2$ exhibits ferromagnetism [19–22]. To avoid magnetic metal clusters or secondary phases, $\text{SnO}_2$ doped with nonmagnetic elements has also been considered.

Ferromagnetism has been observed in Zn,Cu,N-doped $\text{SnO}_2$ [23–25], but the origin of ferromagnetism in $\text{SnO}_2$ doped with nonmagnetic elements is still not clear. In addition, $\text{SnO}_2$ usually exhibits interstitial tin ($Sn_i$) and oxygen ($O_i$) atoms, as well as tin vacancies ($V_{Sn}$) and oxygen vacancies ($V_O$) [26]. These defects can induce ferromagnetism in $\text{SnO}_2$. For example, Chang et al. found that $V_O$ can induce ferromagnetism in undoped $\text{SnO}_2$ thin films [27]. Calculations by Rahman et al. show that bulk $\text{SnO}_2$ is nonmagnetic, but exhibits magnetism with a magnetic moment of $\sim4.00\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ on introduction of $V_{Sn}$ [28].

We investigated the magnetic properties of Zn-doped $\text{SnO}_2$ using total energy plane-wave expansions based on density functional theory (DFT). The following key issues are addressed: (i) What are the point defect formation and ionization characteristics of $\text{SnO}_2$? (ii) What is the FM coupling mechanism in Zn-doped $\text{SnO}_2$? (iii) What is impact of point defects on ferromagnetism? Our results will be useful for application of new types of DMS.

## 2. Computation

First-principle calculations were performed in CASTEP code in a DFT theory framework within the local density approximation (LDA) using the Ceperley-Alder exchange correlation potential parameterized by Perdew and Zunger [29,30]. To investigate defect formation, we constructed a $2 \times 2 \times 3$ rutile $SnO_2$ supercell consisting of 24 Sn and 48 O atoms. Convergence tests of the total energy with respect to the plane-wave cutoff energy and $k$-point sampling were carefully assessed. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 400 eV and a Monkhorst-Pack $k$-point mesh of $2 \times 2 \times 2$ were used for geometry optimization and electronic property calculations. The internal coordinates and lattice parameters were fully relaxed for pure $SnO_2$. The convergence criteria for completion of optimization were as follows: maximum force between atoms, 0.03 eV/Å; maximum stress, 0.05 GPa; maximum displacement, $10^{-3}$ Å; and maximum energy change, $10^{-5}$ eV/atom. The calculated lattice constants of $a$=4.680 Å and $c$=3.154 Å are in agreement with the experimental values of $a$=4.738 Å and $c$=3.187 Å [23].

To investigate the magnetic properties, $4 \times 4 \times 1$ and $2 \times 2 \times 4$ rutile $SnO_2$ supercells consisting of 32 Sn and 64 O atoms were constructed. The plane-wave cutoff energy was set to 400 eV. Monkhorst-Pack $k$-point meshes of $1 \times 1 \times 6$ and $2 \times 2 \times 2$ were used for geometry optimization. In the $4 \times 4 \times 1$ supercell, two Sn atoms at (0, $n$) ($n$=1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) located in the $xy$ plane were replaced by Zn atoms. In the $2 \times 2 \times 4$ supercell, two Sn atoms located outside of the $xy$ plane were replaced by Zn atoms for six configurations. Fig. 1 shows the two supercells. The system is in the FM or AFM state when the electronic spin alignment is set to parallel or antiparallel, respectively. The total magnetic moment in Zn-doped $SnO_2$ is approximately $2$ $\mu_B$ [31]. Hence, the initial magnetic moment for $Sn_{30}Zn_2O_{64}$ was set to $2$ $\mu_B$ for FM configurations and was optimized during geometry optimization. For AFM configurations, the total magnetic moment was set to zero, and remained unchanged during geometry optimization.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Formation and ionization of point defects in Zn-doped $SnO_2$

We found that the total energy was 0.729 eV lower for the relaxed than for the unrelaxed $SnO_2$ supercell, corresponding to an energy gain of 0.061 eV/$SnO_2$ unit. In the relaxed $SnO_2$ supercell, each Sn atom is coordinated to six O atoms [17]. Four rectangular basal O atoms lie in the same plane, with an Sn-O bond length of 2.034 Å. Two apical O atoms lie in a vertical orientation, with an Sn-O bond length of 2.025 Å. When Zn replaces Sn, the Zn-O bond length in the rectangular base does not change, whereas the Zn-O bond length for the apical orientation decreases to 2.015 Å.

The energy for defect formation is calculated from the total energy for the supercell. For compound systems, the defect formation energy can be obtained from the function [32-34]

$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{f}}(\alpha, q)=\Delta E(\alpha, q)+\sum_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \mu_{\alpha}+q E_{\mathrm{F}} \tag{1}
$$

$$
\Delta E(\alpha, q)=E(\alpha, q)-E(SnO_2)+\sum_{\alpha} n_{\alpha} \mu_{\alpha}+q E_{v}, \tag{2}
$$

where $E(\alpha,q)$ is the total energy for the $SnO_2$ supercell containing relaxed defect $\alpha$ in charge state $q$ and $E(ZnO)$ is the total energy for the same $SnO_2$ supercell in the absence of the defect. $n_{\alpha}$ is the number of atoms for each defect: $n_{\alpha}=-1$ if an atom is added, while $n_{\alpha}=1$ if an atom is removed. $E_v$ is the energy of the valence band maximum for the defect-free system. $E_v$ is obtained from $E(0)-E(+1)$, where $E(n)$ denotes the total energy of a perfect lattice supercell with additional $n$ charge [35]. $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ is the Fermi level relative to $E_v$. $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ is limited in the range from the valence band maximum to the conduction band minimum. The calculated LDA bandgap for $SnO_2$ is 1.307 eV, which is much less than the experimental value of 3.60 eV [36]. It is well known that LDA calculation underestimates the bandgap of semiconductors. A method for correcting the LDA bandgap has been suggested in which the conduction band is shifted upwards to match the experimental bandgap [32,33]. For isolated defects, it is assumed that the energy levels for acceptor defects follow the valence-band maximum and are thus unchanged, while donor defect levels follow the conduction-band minimum and are thus shifted upwards by the same amount as the bandgap. Therefore, we do not add any correction to the formation energy for Snᵢ and Vₒ. μₓ is the chemical potential of the element and Δμ is the relative chemical potential. Thermodynamically stable SnO₂ requires that Δμ_Sn+2Δμ_O=ΔH_f (SnO₂), where ΔH_f is the enthalpy of formation for SnO₂, for which the experimental value is -6.02 eV [37]. The defect transition energy level ε(q/q') is defined as the value of the Fermi level at which the formation energy for q is equal to q' [39]:

$$

$$

The result also shows that the oxygen defects have lower neutral formation energies than tin defects. ### 3.2. Magnetic properties of Zn-doped $\mathrm{SnO_2}$

The total energy for the FM and AFM states with full geometry optimization was calculated to determine the preferred magnetic ground state. $\Delta E$ is defined as the energy difference between the FM and AFM states. In the $4\times4\times1$ supercell, two Zn atoms occupy the $xy$ plane. In the $2\times2\times4$ supercell, two Zn atoms are outside the $xy$ plane. Table 1 shows the results calculated for these configurations. The FM state has lower energy than the AFM state for these configurations, indicating that FM coupling is more stable than AFM coupling for Zn-doped $\mathrm{SnO_2}$, in agreement with the literature [17,31]. A similar cluster tendency has been observed in TM-doped ZnO [40-42]. The calculations show that the (0, 3) configuration in the $2 \times 2 \times 4$ supercell has the lowest FM state energy, and hence the (0, 3) configuration can be considered as the FM ground state. The local magnetic moment for Zn is small, with an approximate value of $0.10\ \mu_{B}$/Zn, because Zn hardly donates 3d electrons to O atoms. The magnetic moment of the second and third O neighbors is less than that of the first O neighbor. However, these atoms can produce long-range FM coupling. Shen et al. pointed out that TM valence electrons in p states are delocalized compared to d or f states, which may lead to long-range exchange coupling interactions in N-doped ZnO [15]. The mechanism of FM and AFM coupling between O atoms can be described by a concise physical picture [45]. The coupling can be achieved through interaction of O energy levels. We suppose that O atoms will obtain one electron from a zinc or tin atom. Fig. 5 shows FM and AFM coupling between O 2p levels. For the fully occupied spin-up p1 state, one level is pushed up due to FM coupling and the other is pushed down by the same amount; therefore, there is no energy decrease in the system. FM interactions between partially occupied spin-down p2 states can result in a decrease in energy because electrons are pushed down and occupy a lower energy level. AFM coupling between O 2p levels cannot result in a decrease in energy. Therefore, FM coupling is more energetically favorable than AFM coupling. This may explain why the FM state is more stable for the $Sn_{30}Zn_{2}O_{64}$ system, which is consistent with experimental results [23].

### 3.3. Impact of $V_O$ and $V_{Sn}$ on magnetic properties in Zn-doped $SnO_2$

Oxygen and tin vacancies are believed to be most common native defects in $SnO_2$ [46-48]. In this study, we only investigated the impact of $V_O$ and $V_{Sn}$ on the magnetic properties of the FM ground state. When $V_O$ and $V_{Sn}$ defects are introduced into the supercell, the Sn-O bond length near vacancies decreases by approximately 0.015 and $0.031\mathring{A}$, respectively, after full relaxation. For pure $SnO_2$, Sn donates four valence electrons to the $O_2$ complex, which thus gains four electrons. This results in pure $SnO_2$ as a nonmagnetic material. For Zn-doped $SnO_2$, Zn only donates two 4s electrons to the $O_2$ complex and hardly donates 3d electrons. Hence, the O 2p level will be partly occupied, which results in spin polarization of O 2p. For Zn-doped $SnO_2$ with $V_{Sn}$ defects, neighboring O atoms around Sn cannot obtain electrons from $V_{Sn}$. Fig. 6a shows the spin density of states for O 2p in Zn-doped $SnO_2$ with $V_{Sn}$ defects. The spin-up state of O 2p is fully occupied and the spin-down state is only partly occupied. The electronic states of O 2p at the Fermi energy are spin-polarized. It is clear that the appearance of $V_{Sn}$ gives rise to a local magnetic moment, which can mainly be attributed to neighboring O atoms around $V_{Sn}$. Rahman et al. studied the magnetic and electronic properties of defects in $SnO_2$ [28]. Their calculations showed that pure $SnO_2$ is nonmagnetic, but will show magnetism due to $V_{Sn}$. This result is in agreement with our calculation. The energy difference $\Delta E$ between FM and AFM coupling decreases to $-$304 meV if the system contains one $V_{Sn}$, which indicates that $V_{Sn}$ defects can enhance the FM coupling order. Fig. 6b shows the spin density of states for O 2p in Zn-doped $SnO_2$ with $V_O$. It is evident that the spin-up and spin-down states of O 2p are almost occupied. Hence, O 2p is hardly spin polarized. $\Delta E$ increases to $-$17 meV as $V_O$ is introduced into the system. This indicates that $V_O$ is unfavourable for FM coupling.

The spin polarization can also be visualized by plotting an isosurface of the spin charge density of $0.02\ e/\mathring{A}^3$, as shown in Fig. 7. It is clearly evident from Fig. 7a that the spin charge density in Zn-doped $SnO_2$ is localized mainly on the six neighboring O atoms around the Zn atoms. The total magnetic moment is $2.88\ \mu_B$ in the supercell. Fig. 7b shows the spin charge density for Zn-doped $SnO_2$ with $V_{Sn}$. The spin charge density of O atoms is increased. The total magnetic moment will increase to $7.59\ \mu_B$ in the presence of $V_{Sn}$. Fig. 7c shows the spin charge density in the presence of $V_O$. It is evident that the spin charge density of $Sn_{30}Zn_{2}O_{63}$ is lower than that of $Sn_{30}Zn_{2}O_{64}$ and the magnetic moment of the supercell decreases to $1.56\ \mu_B$.

### 4. Summary

Calculations showed that Zn prefers to replace Sn. The neutral formation energy is lower for oxygen defects ($V_O$ and $O_i$) than for tin defects ($V_{Sn}$ and $Sn_i$). The results also show that $V_{Sn}$, $Sn_i$, $V_O$ and $Zn_{Sn}$ are fully ionized, whereas $O_i$ is hardly ionized in the bandgap calculated. We found that Zn-doped $SnO_2$ has FM stability. The magnetic moment can mainly be attributed to the first neighboring O atoms around Zn. FM coupling can be explained by interactions of the O 2p level. We found that $V_{Sn}$ can enhance FM coupling, whereas $V_O$ is unfavourable for FM coupling.
