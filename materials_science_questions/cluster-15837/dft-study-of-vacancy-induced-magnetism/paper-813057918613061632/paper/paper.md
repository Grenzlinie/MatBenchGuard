# Electronic and magnetic properties of $SnS_2$ monolayer doped with non-magnetic elements

Wen-Zhi Xiao, $^{1,a)}$ Gang Xiao, $^{1}$ Qing-Yan Rong, $^{1}$ Ling-Ling Wang $^{2}$

$^{1}$ School of Science, Hunan Institute of Engineering, Xiangtan 411104, China
$^{2}$ School of Physics and Electronics, Hunan University, Changsha 410082, China

## Abstract:

We performed a systematic study of the electronic structures and magnetic properties of $SnS_2$ monolayer doped with non-magnetic elements in groups IA, IIA and IIIA based on the first-principles methods. The doped systems exhibit half-metallic and metallic natures depending on the doping elements. The formation of magnetic moment is attributable to the cooperative effect of the Hund's rule coupling and hole concentration. The spin polarization can be stabilized and enhanced through confining the delocalized impurity states by biaxial tensile strain in hole-doped $SnS_2$ monolayer. Both the double-exchange and $p$-$p$ exchange mechanisms are simultaneously responsible for the ferromagnetic ground state in those hole-doped materials. Our results demonstrate that spin polarization can be induced and controlled in $SnS_2$ monolayers by non-magnetic doping and tensile strain.

Keywords: $SnS_2$ monolayer; electronic structure; magnetic property; doping; first-principles

## I. INTRODUCTION

The discovery of graphene in 2004 has aroused tremendous research interest in the field of two-dimensional (2D) materials. $^{[1]}$ However, the lack of an intrinsic band-gap restricts its application in semiconductor devices. Nevertheless, this discovery inspires people's enthusiasm for searching for other analogous 2D graphene-like materials. Soon after, monolayer $MoS_2$ has been fabricated successfully by exfoliation technique. $^{[2,3]}$ Ataca *et al.* theoretically predicted 52 stable monolayer transition-metal (TM) dichalcogenides and -oxides in hexagonal phases using density functional theory (DFT). $^{[4]}$ Rasmussen *et al.* comprehensively study the detailed electronic structures of 51 monolayer TM dichalcogenides and TM-oxides using theoretical calculations. $^{[5]}$

To date, although research in monolayer TM dichalcogenides mainly focuses on the Mo- and W-based compounds, the monolayer tin-disulfide ($SnS_2$) also has attracted a huge research interest due to its exceptional optical and electronic properties. $^{[6,7]}$ Sun *et al.* firstly synthesized freestanding $SnS_2$ monolayer through a scalable liquid exfoliation strategy, and found a visible-light conversion efficiency of 38.7% that is suitable for visible-light water splitting. $^{[6]}$ Song *et al.* successfully synthesized high-performance top-gated field-effect transistors and related logic gates from monolayer $SnS_2$. $^{[7]}$ Recently, monolayer, and bilayer $SnS_2$ have been prepared successfully using mechanical exfoliation. $^{[8]}$ Stirred by these exciting findings, considerable experimental and theoretical efforts have been devoted to controlling the electronic structures and magnetic properties in such unusual material for a wide range of applications. The pursuit of controlled magnetism in 2D crystals, in particular, has remained a goal in the spintronics. As already demonstrated by several research groups, the electronic properties and magnetic properties can be modulated by external strain, intrinsic defects, and dopants. $^{[9-13]}$ The electronic and magnetic properties systematically were investigated in vacancy-doped and TM-atom-doped $SnS_2$ and $ZrS_2$ monolayers with the external lattice strain. $^{[9,10]}$ The structural, electronic, optical, and magnetic properties were studied in monolayer $SnS_2$ doped

with intrinsic defects and metal element $M$ (Li, Mg, Al and Zn).$^{[11-13]}$ Their results suggest that there are half-metallic state and long-ranged ferromagnetic ordering in the modified $SnS_{2}$ monolayer. Therefore, it is put forward that $SnS_{2}$ monolayer form a promising platform for 2D dilute magnetic semiconductors.

For better designs of such $SnS_{2}$-based magnetic nanodevices, it is highly desirable to further understand how to fully understand and control the magnetic properties of the monolayer $SnS_{2}$ doped with nonmagnetic elements. In this paper, we systematically studied the electronic structures and magnetic properties of $SnS_{2}$ monolayer doped with elements $M$ (=Li, Na, K, Mg, Ca, Sr, Al, Ga, In) based on the first-principles methods. We found that the nonmagnetic doping and applying biaxial tensile strain are effective routes to modulate its magnetic and electronic properties. Our results are important for further research on $SnS_{2}$ monolayers to reveal their properties for practical technological applications. The half-metallic nature makes the doped 2D $SnS_{2}$ monolayer a good candidate for future spintronics devices. Furthermore, due to its easily tunable electronic structures and magnetic properties by strain, layered $SnS_{2}$ carry potential applications in designing 2D electromechanical devices.

## II. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS

All calculations are performed in the framework of DFT $^{[14]}$ using the projector augmented-wave method as implemented in the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP)$^{[15]}$ at the level of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) format.$^{[16]}$ The crystal structures are optimized using the conjugate gradient method until the Hellman-Feynman force is smaller than 0.01 eV/Å. A vacuum space of at least 12 Å in the direction normal to the layers is used to avoid the unnecessary interactions between adjacent monolayers due to periodic boundary conditions. To model the vacancy and doping, we construct 4×4×1 and 6×6×1 supercells, as shown in Fig. 1. Convergency tests show that the plane waves cutoff energy of 480 eV is large enough for the total energy to be converged to $1×10^{-5}$ eV. The $\Gamma$-centered 9×9×1 $k$-mesh scheme and 70 $k$ points along the high symmetry

directions are used for electronic density of states (DOS) and band structures, respectively. The binding energy, $E_{\mathrm{b}}$, is calculated as $E_{\mathrm{b}}=E_{\mathrm{v}}+\mu-E_{\mathrm{d}} \cdot{ }^{[17]}$ Here, $E_{\mathrm{v}}$ is the ground-state energy of the $\mathrm{SnS}_{2}$ monolayer with one Sn vacnacy, $\mu$ is the energy of a doped metal atom in its bulk phase, and $E_{\mathrm{d}}$ is the total energy of the optimized system with a Sn vacancy $\left(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}}\right)$. In this work, we only consider the biaxial tensile strain. The biaxial strain is defined $\varepsilon=\left(a-a_{0}\right) / a_{0}$, where $a_{0}$ and $a$ are the unstrained and strained lattice constants, respectively. Here, the lattice constants are fixed, and internal coordinates of all atoms are been relaxed.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

### A. Stability and structural properties

To estimate the possibility of creation of intrinsic vacancy and substitutional doping with particular impurities, the calculated binding energies $E_{\mathrm{b}}$ are presented in Table 1. The value of $E_{\mathrm{b}}$ for different dopants varies strongly from 2.39 to $6.37 \mathrm{eV}$. For comparison, we firstly consider the binding energy of Sn self-substitution. The binding energies are $5.91 \mathrm{eV}$ for Sn self-substitution. As summarized in Table 1, the $\mathrm{K}$ and $\mathrm{Al}$ dopings have the lowest and highest binding energies, respectively. In particular, the binding energy of $\mathrm{Al}, \mathrm{Ca}$ and $\mathrm{Sr}$ is very close to that of $\mathrm{Sn}$, indicating that substitutional doping of $\mathrm{Al}, \mathrm{Ca}$ and $\mathrm{Sr}$ is favorable in $\mathrm{SnS}_{2}$ monolayer.

Initial $\mathrm{SnS}_{2}$ monolayer exhibits a hexagonal structure with space group $D_{3 \mathrm{~d}}$ $(P \overline{3} m 1)$ symmetry. When removing one $\mathrm{Sn} / \mathrm{S}$ atom from the $(4 \times 4 \times 1)$ supercell, a $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}} / \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{S}}$ will be created. This corresponds to a vacancy concentration of $6.25 \%$. Checked the local structure around the $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}}$, we find the neighboring atoms occur a very small rearrangement. The nearest $\mathrm{S}$ ions surrounding the $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}}$ are uniformly shortened by $\sim 0.062 \AA$, thus enhancing the binding between the $\mathrm{Sn}$ and $\mathrm{S}$ atoms adjacent to the $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}}$. As a result, the six $\mathrm{Sn}-\mathrm{S}$ bonds around the $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}}$ exhibit shorter bond lengths of $2.554 \AA$ as compared to the pristine bond lengths of $2.598 \AA$. Therefore, the $D_{3 \mathrm{~d}}$ symmetry is still preserved for the reconstructed local structure. However, for the case of $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{S}}$, the creation of $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{S}}$ repels the surrounding three $\mathrm{Sn}$ atoms

dramatically, leading to three elongated nearest Sn-S distance: one is 4.160 Å and the other two are 3.812 Å. Therefore, after the structure relaxation the symmetry of the local structure around the $V_S$ site is reduced from $C_{3v}$ to $C_1$. For all the M-doped cases, we observed that the local structures maintain the same $D_{3d}$ symmetry as the case of $V_{Sn}$, although they undergo extension or contraction (see Table 1). Along the line of IA→IIA→IIIA groups we find an increase in the inward relaxation of the S atoms, but as we go down the same group the trend of outward relaxation is observed, which may be mainly due to the ionic radius of the doped cations.

### B. Magnetic moment and its origin

Now we turn to the effect of the self-doping and nonmagnetic metal-doping on the electronic and magnetic properties of $SnS_2$ monolayer. Apart from $SnS_2$ monolayer with S vacancy, all the doped monolayers give rise to magnetic signature with observable magnetic moment, as shown in Table 1. The spin polarization energy, which denotes the energy gained from the spin polarization, is calculated as the total energy difference ($\varepsilon$=$E_{NSP}$-$E_{SP}$) between the non-spin-polarized and spin-polarized states for the doped $SnS_2$ monolayer. If the energy gain $\varepsilon$>0, the spin polarization will be stabilized. Among them, the $V_{Sn}$, IA and IIA group result in stable spin polarization. We also observe that the systems doped with IIIA elements should be nonmagnetic ground state, because the thermal fluctuation fairly predominates over the negligible values of $\varepsilon$. The results of Mg- and Al-doped case are consistent with the previous study. $^{[13]}$ Moreover, Wang *et al* observed experimentally that substitution doping of Mg may be the origin of the ferromagnetism in $SnS_2$ nanoflowers $^{[18]}$.

It is interesting that the induced magnetic moments are basically equal to the holes introduced by doping. $^{[9-13]}$ The $V_{Sn}$ and $V_S$ give rise to a magnetic moment of 4.0 and zero $\mu_B$ per vacancy, respectively, consistent with the previous studies. $^{[9-11]}$ In $SnS_2$ monolayer, each S atom puts a lone pair into the vacuum region and leaves two electrons to bond with Sn atom, and each Sn atom then needs to supply four electrons for the bonding states to be completely full. As a result, the $3p$ electrons of six neighboring S atoms hybridize with the $5s$ and $5p$ electrons of Sn atom, forming both

$\sigma$ and $\pi$ bonds. As mentioned above, the local structure around the doping site conserves the initial $D_{3d}$ symmetry. According to group theory, the two Sn:$5s$ electrons take the $a_{1g}$ orbital, and $p_{x,y}$ electrons of Sn and S atoms occupy the $e_{u(x)}$ and $e_{u(y)}$ orbitals. They form $\sigma$ bonds. Two $p_{z}$ electrons of S atom lie in $a_{2u}$ orbitals, forming $\pi$ bonds. Under $D_{3d}$ crystal field, both $a_{1g}$ and $a_{2u}$ levels are two non-degenerate states and $e_{u}$ levels is a doubly degenerate state. Upon formation of a neutral $V_{Sn}$, four electrons will be extracted from the neighboring anion S, resulting in two different spin configurations: $a_{1g}^{\uparrow}e_{u}^{\uparrow\uparrow}a_{2u}^{\uparrow}$ and $a_{1g}^{\uparrow\downarrow}e_{u}^{\uparrow\uparrow}a_{2u}^{0}$, as shown in Fig. 1. Chan
$^{[19]}$ suggested that the correlation effects not only limited to $3d$ and $4f$ states, but exist also in anion-$p$ orbitals like the $3p$ shell of S. The relatively localized nature of the $2p$ and $3p$ states favors spin-polarized electron configurations and leads to local moments due to the first Hund's rule.$^{[20,21]}$ If the spin splitting of S-$3p$ orbitals overpowers the crystal field effect, the system with $V_{Sn}$ will be stabilized in a high spin state. As shown in Fig. 1 (the energy level diagram and spin configuration), the four empty states introduced by $V_{Sn}$ account for the calculated local magnetic moment of $4.0\ \mu_{B}$ in the high spin configuration:$a_{1g}^{\uparrow}e_{u}^{\uparrow\uparrow}a_{2u}^{\uparrow}$. Following the physical picture, we can understand the magnetic moments induced by IA, IIA and IIIA elements quite naturally. The substitution of low-valent cations for Sn introduces different holes in the $SnS_{2}$ system, which results in unpaired electron in S-$3p$ orbitals. As a consequence, spin polarization occurs in the nonmagnetic host $SnS_{2}$.

As shown in Table 1 and Fig. 1, we observe the local magnetic moments distribute on the near neighboring S atoms surrounding the $V_{Sn}$ or the defects sites. Specifically, the net magnetic moment induced by $V_{Sn}$ is mainly distributed on the closest S atoms, as shown in Fig. 1(b). For cases doped with IA and IIA elements, the local magnetic moments gradually spread out from the closest S atoms with the increasing atomic number. This indicates that magnetic orbital coupling extends to the rather far S atoms from the dopant centers. The relatively delocalized $p$ spins of S atoms is conducive to a long-ranged magnetic coupling in doped $SnS_{2}$ systems. To understand this phenomenon, we examine the charge transfer based on Bader charge

analysis. $^{[22]}$ We observe what is interesting is the electrons transfer from dopant to the neighboring S atoms become gradually less (see Table 1) along the same periodic element group, although their electronegativity progressively decrease. Therefore, the more holes will be captured by the more and farther S atoms, leading to an extended nature of the spin density.

### C. Strain-dependent spin-polarization

For systems doped with IIIA elements at the doping concentration of 6.25%, the magnetic moments cannot stably exist in these doped systems. So an outstanding key puzzle would emerge: what are the essential factors that can lead to a stable local moment? According to the band picture model, the spontaneous magnetization would occur when the "Stoner Criterion": $D(E_{\mathrm{f}}) J>1$ is satisfied. Here $D(E_{\mathrm{f}})$ is the DOS near the Fermi energy $E_{\mathrm{f}}$, and $J$ denotes the strength of the exchange interaction $^{[23]}$. In other words, the relative gain in exchange interaction energy is larger than the loss in kinetic energy $^{[21]}$. There are two key conditions to switch on and enhance the spontaneous spin polarization in hole-doped oxides, nitrides and sulfides: One of them is the sufficient hole density that guarantee a large enough DOS at the Fermi level; the other on is the relatively localized orbtials, such as $3d$ and $2p$ orbitals, where large spin-exchange splitting happens due to the strong correlation effects. Following this scenario, it is easy to explain results that the substitution of Ca for Sn cannot induce spin polarization in bulk $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ at the doping concentration below $6.25\%^{[24,25]}$. As another example, it was found that carbon does not induce magnetism in bulk $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ when located at the oxygen site, but shows a large magnetic moment at the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ surface $^{[26,27]}$. This can be attributable to the quantum confinement effect which enhances the localization of electrons in $2p$ orbitals. However, the picture seems not applicable to the doped cases with IIIA elements. To further confirm it, we consider the Al-doped systems with the Al concentration at 12.5%. We actually find that the Al-doped systems still favor nonmagnetic state at the concentration of 12.5%. Although hole concentration here plays a role in stabilizing the spin-polarized state in some systems, it usually difficult to get high density of dopant in some matrices.

Therefore, it is necessary to pursue a better alternative route to enhance the spin polarization energy $\varepsilon$ and stabilize the ferromagnetism.

Following solid energy band theory which is one of the most successful and important theory in condensed physics, if we maintain the crystal structure unchanged and increase the lattice constants, the bandwidth of impurity band will become increasingly narrower. This will result in the enhanced intensity DOS near the Fermi energy. Therefore, the spin polarization energy $\varepsilon$ will increase. Here, we investigate the strain dependence of spin polarization energy $\varepsilon$ in Mg- and Al-doped $SnS_{2}$ monolayers by varying the isotropic strain from 0% to 15%, in which all crystal symmetries and overall honeycomb-like structure are maintained. The calculated $\varepsilon$ values and TDOS are shown in Fig. 2, from which one can see the stability of spin polarization increases monotonically with increasing isotropic strain from 0% to 15%. With an increase of tensile strain, the band gap and impurity bands near Fermi level in down-spin channel become narrower, and hence lead to an elevated intensity of DOS near the Fermi level. Therefore, the Stoner criterion is satisfied by enhancing $D(E_{f})$. This suggests that strain can be potentially used as an effective pathway to induce and engineer the band-gap and magnetic properties of nanomaterials.

### D. Electronic structure and magnetic coupling

Having interpreted the origin of magnetic moment in 2D $SnS_{2}$ and explored the potential way to tune and enhance magnetism in such materials by strain, we check the structure and magnetic coupling of the doped $SnS_{2}$ systems. The spin-resolved total DOS (TDOS) and band structures are presented in the Figs. 3, 4 and 5 for 2D $SnS_{2}$ with single defect. It can be seen from Fig. 3(a) that two acceptor states are observed in the gap. The half-metallic feature is formed because the shallow acceptor states pin the Fermi energy in the down-spin channel. It is also noted that S (3p) orbitals give dominant contributions to the two impurity states between -0.2 and 0.5 eV (see Fig. 3(b)). As motioned above, the acceptor states are mainly composed of S-3p orbitals that split into a single $a_{2u}$ (S-$p_{z}$) state and two twofold degenerate $e_{u}$ (S-$p_{x}$ and S-$p_{y}$) states [see Fig. 3(c)]. For 2D $SnS_{2}$ with S vacancy, the TDOS shows a

perfect symmetry with impurity states just below the Fermi level [see inset of Fig.
2(b)], suggesting that the presence of $V_S$ in 2D $SnS_2$ cannot produce any magnetism.
This agrees well with the known findings. $^{[10,11]}$ Although the removal of S atom
releases electrons in $SnS_2$, the system still preserves semiconducting nature due to the
reduction of the $Sn^{4+}$ ions to $Sn^{2+}$.$^{[26,27]}$ Indeed, we find the three nearest neighboring
Sn atoms captures about 0.72 electrons based on Bader analysis and the impurity
states mainly arise from the Sn-$4p$ and S-$3p$ states, indicating that the released
electrons forms weak bonding states. Thus, the system is semiconducting because the
released electrons cannot delocalize in all crystal. In contrast, the n-type doping,
which can be realized by substitution of halogen atom for S atom in the $SnS_2$
monolayers, makes the doped system metallic $^{[28]}$.

For all the doped systems (see Figs. 4 and 5), the Fermi energy shifts to lower
energy with holes existing at the top of the valence band. The impurity bands saddle
the Fermi level, leading to narrower band-gaps as compared with the perfect host
$SnS_2$. Particularly, these doped systems with IA/IIA elements exhibit the half-metallic
nature, since the up-spin channels carry the semiconducting behavior and the
down-spin channels are metallic character. To determine the composition of the
impurity states, we use Na-doped system as a representative case to discuss the results
since the electronic and magnetic properties of all six doped systems containing
IA/IIA elements are similar. As shown in Fig. 6(a), the impurity states above the
Fermi level predominantly are constituted of the degenerate states of $p_x$ and $p_y$ of S
atoms with slight contribution from S-$3p_z$ and Sn-$5p$ states. The spin-polarized
electronic structures clearly indicate the magnetic moment comes mainly from S
atoms. The PDOS analysis is qualitatively consistent with the spin density plot and
the calculated magnetic moment of each atom.

To investigate the magnetic interaction between the induced magnetic moments,
two dopants are introduced into a $6×6×1$ supercell with all the atoms fully relaxed.
Three different configurations ($C_{12}$, $C_{13}$, $C_{45}$) are considered, where two dopants take
the sites of (1, 2), (1, 3) and (4, 5), respectively, as marked in Fig. 1(a). We simulate
FM and AFM ordering for each configuration. The estimated the exchange coupling

parameter $J'$ can obtain from the difference between energies of the FM and AFM states ($\Delta E_{\text{ex}} = E_{\text{FM}}$-$E_{\text{AFM}}$). The sign and size of the exchange coupling parameter $J'$ reflect the magnetic ground state and the Curie temperature in the 2D doped systems. Here, we map the $\Delta E_{\text{ex}}$ onto the Heisenberg model Hamiltonian:$^{[29]}$
$$
H = \sum_{i,j} J_0 \vec{S}_i \cdot \vec{S}_j
$$
where $\vec{S}_i$ represents spin on lattice sites, $J_0$ is the exchange coupling parameter that gives the nearest neighbor interaction. Here, for $\text{C}_{12}$, $\text{C}_{13}$ and $\text{C}_{45}$ configurations, the $\Delta E_{\text{ex}}$ is proportional to $2J_0S^2$, $4J_0S^2$ and $6J_0S^2$, respectively. As listed in Table 2, the FM states are more favorable than the AFM states for all the considered configurations, since the total energy of the FM states are lower than those of the AFM ones.

To expound the magnetic coupling mechanism, taking Sr-doped system as an example we present the total DOS and spin density in Fig. 6(b). Unusually, the $p$-$p$ interaction proposed by Pan $^{[30]}$ and the double-exchange mechanism are responsible for the FM ground state in hole-doped various nonmagnetic semiconductor compounds $^{[31]}$. The parallel spin alignment (see Fig. 6(b)) allows for the virtual hopping between the electrons in the partially filled defect states in down-spin channel, lowering the kinetic energy. As a consequence, the FM coupling saves the energy of the system. In some sense, both the double-exchange and $p$-$p$ exchange mechanisms occur simultaneously in those hole-doped materials. If the Fermi level lies within the impurity band, double exchange usually dominates other exchange mechanisms.$^{[31]}$ For the $\text{C}_{13}$ configuration, the large spatial extension of spin density facilitate the longer-ranged interaction with relatively small $\Delta E_{\text{ex}}$ with respect to the case of the $\text{C}_{45}$ configuration due to a larger separation between two centers of impurities. For $\text{C}_{45}$ configuration, the shorter separation improve the FM stability, and the over-delocalized hole states occurring in the up-spin channel reduce the total magnetic moment to about $3.31\mu_{\text{B}}$.

### IV. SUMMARY

In conclusion, we predict the electronic structures and properties of $\text{SnS}_2$

monolayer doped with non-magnetic elements in groups IA, IIA and IIIA of the periodic table based on DFT. The systems show half-metallic ferromagnetism upon doping with IA, IIA elements. The doped monolayers with IIIA elements change from semiconductor into metlal. The formation of magnetic moment depends on the cooperative effect of the Hund's rule coupling and hole concentration introduced by doping. The spin polarization state in hole-doped $SnS_2$ monolayer can be stabilized and enhanced by applying biaxial tensile strain. Both the double-exchange and $p$-$p$ exchange mechanisms are simultaneously responsible for the FM ground state in those hole-doped materials. Our results suggest that non-magnetic doping and tensile strain can be efficient strategies for exploring two-dimensional diluted magnetic semiconductors.

### Highlights

-   Magnetism is induced in doped $SnS_2$ monolayer with non-magnetic elements.
-   Tensile strain is a strategy of tuning magnetism in $SnS_2$-based materials.
-   Magnetism originates from the collaboration of Hund's rule and hole concentration.
