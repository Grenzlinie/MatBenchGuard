# First-Principles Study on Magnetic Properties of V-Doped ZnO Nanotubes *

Electronic and magnetic properties of V-doped ZnO nanotubes in which one of $Zn^{2+}$ ions is substituted by $V^{2+}$ ions are studied by the first-principles calculations of plane wave ultra-soft pseudo-potential technology based on the spin-density function theory. The computational results reveal that spontaneous magnetization in V- doped (9,0) ZnO nanotubes can be induced without p-type or n-type doping treatment, and the ferromagnetism is isotropic and independent of the chirality and diameter of the nanotubes. It is found that V-doped ZnO nanotubes have large magnetic moments and are ferromagnetic half-metal materials. Moreover, the ferromagnetic coupling among V atoms is generated by O 2p electron spins and V 3d electron spins localized at the exchanging interactions between magnetic transitional metal (TM) impurities. The appearance of ferromagnetism in V-doped ZnO nanotubes gives some reference to fabrication of a transparent ferromagnet which may have a great impact on industrial applications in magneto-optical devices.

Dilute magnetic semiconductors (DMS) have at- tracted a great deal of interest in the past two decades due to the additional spin freedom of electron besides the charge and their promising materials for spintron- ics. Many experimental and theoretical investigations have been reported regarding the transitional metal- doped ZnO dilute magnetic semiconductor materi- als, which have many unique magneto-optical, mag-netoelectrical, and magnetotransport properties. $^{[1-6]}$  Some groups have studied the magnetic and electronic properties $^{[7-13]}$ of the $Zn_{x} V_{1-x} O$ systems. However, their research findings are considerably controversial. Compared with the bulk ZnO, ZnO nanostructures such as nanowires, nanotubes (NTs), nanobelts and nanorods are promising building blocks for optical, electronic, and chemical sensing devices and have great potential applications in the field of optoelec- tronic devices such as blue-light emitting, short wave- length laser diodes with low thresholds in the UV light-emitting diodes, solar cells, transparent and con- ducting electrodes in solar cells, surface acoustic wavedevices, and chemical and biological sensors. $^{[14-19]}$  Moreover, ZnO NTs have just two surfaces, which in- terduce interstices and make the substituting of impu- rities be easily implemented. In addition, ZnO NTs are wide-band-gap semiconductors and their proper- ties can be changed according to the chiralities, and have potential applications for the host materials of DMS. To our knowledge, systematic studies on tran- sitional metal-doped ZnO NTs are little made theoret- ically until now. In particular, the effects of electronic states due to the appearance of the sidewall in ZnO NTs on their electronic and magnetic properties are unclear. Thus it is necessary for us to use the first- principles methods to study these issues for developing future applications of ZnO NTs.

Our NTs are generated from ZnO wutzite crys- tal structure, which has the lattice parameters as $a=0.3253 nm, c=0.521 nm, \alpha=\beta=90^{\circ}, \gamma=120^{\circ}$ . ZnO nanotubes are obtained by removing the atoms outside the big circle area and inside the small cir- cle area via cutting along the (0001) axis with period c in Fig.1. The ZnO NT is a zigzag type nanotube with (9,0). The diameter of the ZnO NTs is 0.958 nm, corresponding to 72 atoms per unit cell. To study the properties of V-doped in the (9,0) ZnO NT, we replace one Zn atom with one V atom. This corresponds to a2.8 wt.% V doping concentration.

In our study, all the structural optimizations, mag- netic and electronic properties are performed using the density function theory (DFT) approach based onpseudo-potential technology in the code CASTEP $^{[19]}$  programme. The spin-polarized parameter is used in our calculations. To minimize the tube-tube interac- tion effect due to the finite supercell, the single-walled ZnO NTs are placed within a square supercell with a vacuum in thickness 1.2 nm. The PW91 parametriza- tion is taken as the exchange-correlation potential in the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). The cutoff energy of plane-wave is set to be 380eV. The

The fully relaxed results of V-doped ZnO NTs are shown in Fig. 1(c). As can be seen from Fig. 1(c), the initial polygon structure of the ZnO NT in Fig. 1(a) transformed into a perfect cylindrical single wall ZnO NT. It is found that the relaxed ZnO NTs have the same structural properties as carbon$^{[20,21]}$ and BN (9,0) SWNT. In order to reach a better understanding of the V-doped effect on the geometric structure of ZnO NT. Figure 2 shows the changes of bond length and bond angle for the V atom surrounding the ZnO NT. The V atom moves outward into the ZnO NT. The optimized bond length is found to be 0.1711 nm and 0.1720 nm, which induce a contraction of $-6.5\%$ and $-6.4\%$, respectively. One corresponding angle is increased from $115.01^\circ$ to $116.61^\circ$ and the other is decreased from $127.44^\circ$ to $125.92^\circ$. Moreover, it is found that the magnetic moment on V atom is $2.87\mu_B$ in V-doped ZnO nanotube system, which is larger than V-doped in the bulk and surface.$^{[7-11]}$ The calculated results exhibit some signature of ferrimagnetic properties either, because the value of integrated spin density (this value is 1.002) and the absolute value of integrated spin density (this value is 2.072) are finite, and the difference between them is also finite.

The calculated band structure of the pristine (9,0) ZnO NT is shown in Fig. 3(a). Clearly, it is found that the ZnO NT has a direct band gap at the relatively high dispersion along the $\Gamma(0,0,0) \to Z(0,0,0.5)$ direction. The direct gap of 1.97 eV is in good agreement with that of the ab initio calculation by Yang et al.$^{[22]}$ On the other hand, as can be seen from Fig. 3(a), the (9,0) ZnO NT is a semiconductor due to the existence of a large energy gap of 1.97 eV, which is larger than the gap of bulk ZnO ($E_g = 0.99\ \text{eV}$) for our calcu-

lation. The band gap widening effect in ZnO NT is quite important for building nanoscale optoelectronic devices. We also perform spin-polarized calculations on the pure (9,0) ZnO NT and the spontaneous magnetization is not found in the ZnO nanotube.

In order to investigate the magnetic properties of V-doped ZnO NT, we first calculate the band structures of spin-up and spin-down electrons, as shown in Figs.3(b)-3(c), respectively. It is obvious that a spontaneous magnetization is produced in the V-doped ZnO NT and the $d$ states of V atoms are appeared near the Fermi level. The Fermi level moves from the spin-up to the spin-down band. The system shows half-metallic behaviour in which the spin-up and spin-down possesses a Fermi surface. Moreover, $3d$-bands of the spin-up and spin-down states in the V-doped system are not fully occupied because $V^{3+}$ ion has $d^3$ electronic configuration, which induce a strong spontaneous magnetization. The splitting energy gap between the occupied and unfilled states is 1.25 eV. In addition, the whole band structures are shifted by 1.2-1.3 eV towards lower energies.

![](./images/811873913402294273_4.jpg)

Fig.4. Calculated density of states: (a) the total DOS of pure (9,0)NT, (b) the total DOS of V-doped NT, (c) the partial spin DOS of V atom, (d) the partial spin DOS of V $3d$ and O $2p$.

In order to further study on the spin polarization, Fig.4 shows the total density of states (DOS) and the partial spin density of states of V-doped ZnO NT. It is shown in Fig.4(a) that the total density of states of undoped ZnO NT, the total DOS curves for spin-up and spin-down are completely identical. Hence the pure ZnO NT is nonmagnetic. For the vanadium substitution for zinc in (9,0) ZnO NT, it is clear from our calculated results that the spin-up and spin-down peaks appear at the energy of $-1.1\,\text{eV}$, $0.0\,\text{eV}$ and $1.0$-$2.5\,\text{eV}$, respectively. It also can been seen from Fig.4 that the spin-up DOS exceeds the spin-down near the Fermi level, and in the lower and high energy, the spin-up and spin-down DOSs are almost symmetrical. The partial spin DOS of the V atom, the partial spin DOSs of the V $3d$ and the O $2p$ states are shown in Figs.4(c) and 4(d). The V $3d$ states contribute to the several peaks and dominate the DOS at the Fermi level. The spontaneous magnetization originates mostly from the V $3d$ states, and ferromagnetism is induced by the V $3d$ orbital hybridization with the O $2p$ orbit due to double exchange mechanism. In addition, these impurity states of the V atom clearly show large exchange splitting, which induce the high spin configuration of $d$ electrons in V atoms. Figure 4(d) shows the bonding states $t_a$ and anti-bonging states $t_b$ appear clearly at approximately $-5.2\,\text{eV}$ and $1.8\,\text{eV}$ due to the $d-p$ orbital hybridization causing the bonding and anti-bonding splitting. While the non-bonding states $e$ appear as a sharp peak just at the Fermi level. These behaviours indicate that the origin of the ferromagnetism in V-doped ZnO NT is mediated by double exchange mechanism.$^{[23]}$ The V atom has five valence electrons, $3d^34s^2$, and O has six valence electrons, $2s^22p^4$. Three of the valence electrons for the V atom are transferred to the O to form $d-p$ bonding states and two electrons left at the V site, and the spins of the four electrons are aligned according to the first Hund's rule. On the other hand, the five degenerated $d$-manifold are split into a threefold ($t_{2g}$ bonding) and a two-fold($e_g$ non-bonding) states due to the crystal field effect of the bond charges. The

relative energies of the spin-up and spin-down states are formed due to the exchange interaction (molecular field). These magnetic properties will be beneficial for exploiting more attractive electron's spin devices with new functionality in ZnO-based DMS.

In conclusion, we have investigated the electronic and magnetic properties of V-doped ZnO NTs using the first-principles calculation of spin polarized density function theory (DFT). Our results show that V-doped ZnO nanotubes have large magnetic moment $(2.87\mu_B)$ and the ferromagnetic coupling is resulted from the hybridization between the O $2p$ and V $3d$ states. It is found that V-doped ZnO nanotubes are ferromagnetic half-metal materials. The spontaneous spin polarization is independent of the chirality, diameter of the nanotubes and the site of substitution. Thus the vanadium-induced magnetization in ZnO nanotubes may be a good candidate for new DMS materials and these can be potential building blocks for spintronic devices.
