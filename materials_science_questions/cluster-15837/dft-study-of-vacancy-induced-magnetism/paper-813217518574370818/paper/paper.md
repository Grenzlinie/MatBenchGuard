### Enhance ferromagnetism by stabilizing the cation vacancies in GaN

Zhen-kun Tang$^{1,2}$, Deng-Yu Zhang$^{1}$, Li-Ming Tang$^{2,a}$, Ling-Ling Wang$^{2,b}$, and Ke-Qiu Chen$^{2,c}$

$^{1}$ Departments of Physics and Electronics, Hengyang Normal University, Hengyang 421008, P.R. China
$^{2}$ Department of Applied Physics, Hunan University, Changsha 410082, P.R. China

Received 14 June 2012 / Received in final form 20 December 2012
Published online 24 June 2013 – © EDP Sciences, Società Italiana di Fisica, Springer-Verlag 2013

**Abstract.** The magnetic properties related to cation vacancies in GaN are investigated by first-principles calculations. The results show that a neutral Ga-vacancy induces $3\mu_B$ magnetic moment in GaN, but is difficult to form due to the high formation energy. It is found that the Ga-vacancy formation energy can be reduced by adding electrons with uniform compensating positive background charge, by nano-structure engineering, or by co-doping donor-like defects. The Ga-vacancy induced colossal magnetic moment in Gd-doped GaN can be modulated by co-doping the donor like defects. It is suggested that ferromagnetism enhanced by stabilizing the cation vacancies may be applied to other wide band-gap semiconductors as well.

## 1 Introduction

In the past couple of decades, a great deal of effort has been put into the enhancement of ferromagnetism in diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMS) [1,2]. III-V-based DMSs, such as AlN and GaN, have particularly played a critical role on exploring new physics, ideas and concepts in spintronics [3–5]. Gd-doped GaN has aroused great interest in view of the fact that the magnetic moment in this system can reach to $4000\mu_B$ per Gd atom [6,7]. The colossal magnetic moment in GaN:Gd is much higher than the atomic Gd magnetic moment, indicating that the main origin of the magnetism does not arise from Gd itself. By using rare-earth elements as magnetic dopants, the symmetry allowed $s$-$f$ coupling can lead to ferromagnetism in semiconductors are possible [8]. The experiments strongly suggest that defects are responsible for the colossal magnetic moment in this system [9–14]. Recently, Liu et al. [15] confirm the existence of defect-induced magnetism and the possibility of turning the magnetism of wide band-gap semiconductors by defect engineering. First-principles calculations also show that the intrinsic ferromagnetism results from the defect of Ga vacancies ($V_{Ga}$) in GaN:Gd [16,17]. In fact, even without Gd dopants, the $V_{Ga}$ can also lead to ferromagnetism and exhibit rather strong long-range couplings in GaN [18–20]. Recent experiments demonstrate that the saturation magnetization in Ga deficient GaN film is increased with the increase of $V_{Ga}$ [21]. It is believed that such manifestation originates from the polarization of unpaired $2p$ electrons of N surrounding the $V_{Ga}$. This is similar to the case that spontaneous magnetization is an intrinsic property in wide band-gap semiconductors with sufficient holes and can be attributed to the localized nature of the $2p$ states of an ion [22]. Though the local magnetic moment is associated with $V_{Ga}$, the $V_{Ga}$ has high formation energy indicating its concentration is low in the neutral GaN bulk material [23]. Thus, how to reduce the vacancy formation energy, namely to stabilize the $V_{Ga}$, is the key issue to enhance the magnetic moment in GaN-based material.

In the present work, we focus on stabilizing $V_{Ga}$ in GaN. It is find that in both zinc blende and wurtzite structures, a charged $V_{Ga}$ has the lower formation energy than that of a neutral $V_{Ga}$, indicating that the $V_{Ga}$ dependent magnetic moment can be tuned by injecting negative carrier. The formation energy of $V_{Ga}$ is reduced by co-doping with donor-like defects or via nanostructure engineering. Thus, the magnetic moment related with $V_{Ga}$ can be modulated by the two ways as well.

## 2 Model and method

First-principles spin polarized DFT calculations have been performed based on the projector augmented wave (PAW) method [24,25] within the GGA of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) potentials [26] as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [27]. For Gd $4f$ electrons, the DFT + U method [28] is adopted to incorporate on-site Coulomb repulsion among localized $f$ electrons sufficiently, where the Hubbard $U$ (6.7 eV) and the exchange $J$ (0.7 eV) [8,16]. are expanded using the plane-wave basis set with a cutoff energy of 400 eV. The electronic-state sampling is made using $4 \times 4 \times 4$ grids of $k$ points over the first Brillouin zone in both the ZB and WZ supercells. For relaxed structures, the force was set as $0.01$ eV/Å.

The $2 \times 2 \times 2$ and $3 \times 3 \times 2$ supercells are built for zinc blende (ZB) and wurtzite (WZ) GaN structures, respectively. The defect system is modeled by putting a defect or defect complex in a supercell, and for charged defect, a uniform background charge is added to the periodic supercell. To determine the defect formation energy, we calculate the total energy for a supercell containing the relaxed defect in charge state $q$. The formation energy of point defects of a $V_{Ga}$ and a $N_i$ are calculated by using the following equations [29–33]:

$$
E_{V_{Ga}}^{f q}=E_{t o t}\left[V_{G a}^{q}\right]-E_{t o t}[\mathrm{GaN}]+\mu_{G a}+q E_{F}, \quad(1)
$$

and

$$
E_{N_{i}}^{f q}=E_{t o t}\left[N_{i}^{q}\right]-E_{t o t}[\mathrm{GaN}]-\mu_{N}+q E_{F}. \quad(2)
$$

Here, $E_{t o t}[V_{G a}^{q}](E_{t o t}[N_{i}^{q}])$ is the total energy derived from a supercell calculation with one $V_{Ga}$ ($N_i$) in the cell, and $E_{t o t}[\mathrm{GaN}]$ is the total energy for the equivalent supercell GaN. $E_F$ is referenced with respect to the valence-band maximum in the bulk, i.e., $E_F=0$ at the top of the valence band in bulk GaN. The chemical potentials $\mu_{G a}$ and $\mu_{N}$ depend on the experimental growth conditions and satisfy boundary conditions. Under equilibrium conditions, for GaN, the chemical potentials of N and Ga must satisfy the boundary conditions

$$
\mu_{G a}+\mu_{N}=\triangle H(\mathrm{GaN}) ; \quad(3)
$$

here, $\triangle H(\mathrm{GaN})$ is the formation enthalpy of GaN. For purposes of plotting the formation energies, we choose N-rich conditions, corresponding to $\mu_{N}=\triangle H\left(\mathrm{~N}_{2}\right) / 2$, and the $\mu_{G a}=\triangle H(\mathrm{GaN})-\mu_{N}$.

To see if the defect complex can form, we calculate the defect complex binding energy defined as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{b}=E_{t o t}\left(V_{G a}+D_{i}\right)+E_{t o t}(\mathrm{GaN})-E_{t o t}\left(V_{G a}\right)-E_{t o t}\left(D_{i}\right), \\
(4)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $E_{t o t}$ is the total energy, calculated using the same supercell at the neutral-charge state; $D_i$ is the other defect or defect complex considered in this paper.

## 3 Results and discussion

### 3.1 Formation energy of the intrinsic defect and binding energy of the defect complex

Mitra and Lambrecht [34] have suggested that $N_i$ is the source of the defect which can induce magnetism in GaN:Gd. Other groups have claimed that the magnetic signal significantly enhanced in GaN:Gd by co-doped with Si atoms [35–37]. Thus, the $N_i$ and Si dopants are considered as an intrinsic defect representative and an extrinsic impurity representative, respectively. The calculated formation energy of individual intrinsic defects of $V_{Ga}$ and $N_i$ in both ZB and WZ GaN structures are shown in Table 1. The results show that the neutral $E_{V_{Ga}^{0}}^{f}$ is very high in both ZB and WZ structures. However, the charged $E_{V_{Ga}^{q}}^{f}$ is reduced a lot by adding negative charge [38], and the $E_{V_{Ga}^{3-}}^{f}$ is the lowest of 3.385 eV in ZB structure and of 2.813 eV in WZ structure. For interstitial nitrogen, $E_{N_{i}^{q}}^{f}$ is lower than $E_{V_{Ga}^{q}}^{f}$ at the corresponding charged states in both ZB and WZ structures. For single Si dopant, each Si atom has four valence electrons, indicating that Si dopant may act as a donor when it substitutes for a Ga atom ($\mathrm{Si}_{Ga}$) or acts as an acceptor when it substitutes for a N atom ($\mathrm{Si}_{N}$). We only present the results of WZ structure in Si and defect complex doped GaN due to the calculated result of ZB is qualitatively similar to that of WZ. The calculated formation energy of $\mathrm{Si}_{Ga}$ is lower by 6.969 eV than that of $\mathrm{Si}_{N}$ under N-rich condition, and lower by 4.944 eV under Ga rich condition. This showed that Si prefers to occupy the cation site and act as a donor to provide free electrons.

### 3.2 Magnetic properties of $N_i$ and Si dopant

To understand the impact on magnetism of $N_i$ in GaN, we consider two different ways of relaxation in both ZB and WZ structures. First, fixed Ga and N atoms except the $N_i$ atom, and then, all the Ga and N atoms are allowed to relax without any constraint. In the first case, the DOSs of ZB and WZ structure are displayed in Figures 1a and 1b, respectively. As can be seen from Figure 1a, the spin-up and spin-down DOSs are not symmetric, which indicate that this system is magnetic semiconductor. All the spin up DOSs of $N_i$-$2p$ are under the Fermi level, while all the spin down partial DOSs of $N_i$-$2p$ are above the Fermi level. This means that the spin up $N_i$-$2p$ orbits are completely occupied by electrons, and the spin down $N_i$-$2p$ orbits are empty. The different occupation of spin up and spin down states results in $3\mu_B$ magnetic moment. While from Figure 1b, we can see that the spin down $N_i$-$2p$ orbits are fractional occupied, and the calculated magnetic moments are about $1\mu_B$. Compared with the first case, the second scenario has the equal magnetic moment of $1\mu_B$, and the smaller orbits split value of $N_i$-$2p$ as illustrated in Figures 1c and 1d.

The spin density and spin orientation of $N_i$ are also changed due to the change of symmetry, which led to a reduction of the magnetic moment from $3\mu_B$ to $1\mu_B$. After full relaxation, no matter in ZB or WB, it is seemed that the $N_i$ bonds with a neighbouring N atom of the distance within 1.35 Å, and the spin density locates primarily on the two bonding N atoms as can be seen from Figures 2c and 2d. It is noted that the total magnetic moment induced by a $N_i$ is just $1\mu_B$ after full relaxation, which is not consistent with the previous report without relaxation [34]. Especially in charged cases after full relaxation, the total magnetic moments all become zero as can be seen from Table 1. Compared with magnetic moments of $N_i$, the neutral $V_{Ga}$ has $3\mu_B$, and is reduced $1\mu_B$ by adding a negative charge in both ZB and WZ structures. Only when the negative charge per $V_{Ga}$ reaches three, the magnetic moment of $V_{Ga}$ become zero. We consider that the magnetic moment from the full relaxation structure may be more tally with the actual situation. Thus, we conclude that $V_{Ga}$ may be the origin of the colossal magnetic moment in GaN:Gd rather than $N_i$.

In addition, some states associate with Si dopant are around the Fermi level, which further confirms that Si is a donor impurity. The energy calculation points out co-doped with the donor-like impurity is a good way to increase the $V_{Ga}$ concentration, lead to the enhancement of the magnetic moment. However, free electrons provided by a donor dopant such as Si dopants can result in electron-hole recombination, and lead to the decrease of the magnetic moment. Therefore, the enhancement of the magnetic mo- ment comes from the competition of the two effects. This is similar to the case when dopant’s concentration increases. The doping properties of the system can change [39]. Ob- viously, the effect of the former is larger than that of the latter in the Si-doped GaN:Gd experiments [35–37]. We think that the magnetism may be further enhanced by passivated co-doping in the Si-doped GaN:Gd system.

The results shows that the ferromagnetism cou- pling occurrs between the two $V_{Ga}$ and results in a mag netic moment of $5.0\mu_{B}$, which is in accord with the report from Gohda and Oshiyama [17] and Dev et al. ### 3.3 Stabilize Ga vacancies by nanostructure engineering

Considering experimental samples are universally thin films [6,7], we model a $(2\times 2)$ ten-layer slab along (0001) orientation, which contains 40 Ga atoms and 40 N atoms. Each slab was separated from the other by a vacuum re- gion of $10$ Å. The atoms of the three layers at the bottom are fixed, while the remaining atoms of the slab allowed to full relax. The electronic-state sampling is made using $7\times 7\times 1$ of $k$ points over the first Brillouin zone.

## 4 Conclusion

In summary, the unconventional magnetism related to $V_{Ga}$
in GaN is investigated by using density functional theory.
It is found that $V_{Ga}$ formation-energy could be reduced
by adding electrons with uniform compensating positive
background charge, by nanostructure engineering, or by
co-doping donor-like defects. The $V_{Ga}$ induced colossal
magnetic moment in GaN:Gd can be modulated by co-
doping the donor-like defects. It should be noted that the
free electrons provided by a donor dopant can result in
electron-hole recombination, and then cause the decrease
of the magnetic moment. It is obvious that the effect on
the enhancement of the magnetic moment is larger than
the effect on the decrease in the Si-doped GaN:Gd system.
The magnetism may be further enhanced by passivated
co-doping in the Si-doped GaN:Gd system. It is suggested
that the method to decrease the cation vacancy formation
energy and increase the magnetic moment may be applied
also to other wide band-gap semiconductors.
