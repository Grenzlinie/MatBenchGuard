# Structural stability and electronic properties of an octagonal allotrope of two dimensional boron nitride $^{\dagger}$

Lauren Takahashi$^{a}$,Keisuke Takahashi,$^{*bc}$

An octagonal allotrope of two dimensional boron nitride is explored through first principle calculations. Calculations show that two dimensional octagonal boron nitride can be formed with a binding energy comparable to two dimensional hexagonal boron nitride. In addition, two dimensional octagonal boron nitride is found to have a band gap smaller than two dimensional hexagonal boron nitride, suggesting the possibility of semiconductive attributes. Two dimensional octagonal boron nitride also has the ability to layer through physisorption. Defects present within two dimensional octagonal boron nitride also lead toward the introduction of a magnetic moment through the absence of boron atoms. The presence of defects is also found to render both hexagonal and octagonal boron nitride reactive against hydrogen, where greater reactivity is seen in the presence of nitrogen. Thus, two dimensional octagonal boron nitride is confirmed with the potential to tailor properties and reactivity through lattice shape and purposeful introduction of defects.

## Introduction
The discovery of graphene has opened up the field of two dimensional (2D) materials in material science. $^{1,2}$ Since the discovery of graphene, various 2D materials have been designed and synthesized in both computation and experiment where such materials have unique physical and chemical properties. $^{3-6}$ In such 2D materials, structures generally exhibit hexagonal atomic configurations as seen in graphene. However,2D materials whose structures deviate from the traditional hexagonal structure draw interest due to the impact of structure on various properties of the material. In general, the structure of materials is strongly coupled with the properties of materials as different electronic structures are formed depending on how atoms are placed.

Several non-hexagonal shaped 2D materials have been reported for understanding how the structure of 2D materials affects its properties. One such case is pentagonal graphene which is energetically stable and predicted to be an allotrope of graphene, which has a honeycomb configuration. $^{7}$ Another case involves cluster-assembled 2D materials where square shaped 2D Au and Pt are designed by placing square shaped Au and Pt clusters into periodic boundary conditions. $^{8,9}$ In those cases, the properties of these 2D materials have unique physical and chemical properties. Thus, designing non-hexagonal shaped 2D materials would give rise to new opportunities in exploring alternative approaches to 2D material design.

Two dimensional boron nitride, known as white graphene, is reported to be an energetically stable 2D material. $^{10,11}$ Allotropes of graphene have been shown to posses properties different from graphene that are uniquely tied to their geometric shape. $^{12}$ Given that the bond lengths of B-N in BN and C-C in graphene are very similar, one can consider that the high stability of the boron and nitrogen interaction could allow the formation of non-hexagonal 2D boron nitride. First principle calculations are therefore implemented to design a non-hexagonal allotrope of 2D boron nitride.

## Computational method
Grid based projector augmented wave (GPAW) algorithm within density functional theory calculations is implemented. $^{13}$ The exchange correlation of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) is implemented with spin polarization. $^{14}$ The exchange correlation of vdW-DF is used for layer calculations in order to consider the effect of van der Waals forces between the layers. $^{15}$ Periodic boundary condition is set in x and y axis with $15\ \mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ of vacuum in z-axis. 4x4x1 special k points of the Brillouin zone sampling is applied. $^{16}$ Bader charge analysis is implemented for charge transfer analysis

To evaluate layerability further, a third layer of octagonal boron nitride was added. The third layer is again calculated using PBE and vdW-DF exchange-correlations. When using the PBE exchange-correlations, the distance between layers is calculated to be 4.54 Å and 4.51 Å , averaging to 4.53 Å . Additionally, the binding energy between the three layers is calculated to be -0.03 eV using the PBE exchange-correlations and -1.12 eV using the vdW-DF exchange-correlations. These results show that the average distance between layers slightly increases when a third layer is calculated via the vdW-DF exchange-correlations. Furthermore, the binding energy increases when a third layer of octagonal boron nitride is added.

## Defects

![](./images/811057204491714560_5.jpg)

Fig. 4 Locations of defect sites on octagonal boron nitride. a)Defect where a nitrogen atom is missing. b) Defect where a boron atom is missing. Atomic color code: blue- nitrogen, pink- boron. Grey lines represent the unit cell.

The stability of octagonal boron nitride was examined further by exploring the effects of the presence of defects. Figure 4 displays octagonal boron nitride where a) a nitrogen atom is missing, and b) a boron atom is missing. The B–N bonds found within the octagonal boron nitride with a boron defect is calculated to have an average binding energy of -2.32 eV. In the case of octagonal boron nitride with a nitrogen defect, the average binding energy of the B–N bonds is calculated to be -2.67 eV. Bader analysis reveals that when a boron atom is missing (as seen in Figure 4b), the nitrogen atom nearest to the defect site has 8.50 electrons. Meanwhile, when a nitrogen atom is missing (as seen in Figure 4a), the boron atom nearest to the defect site has 3.53 electrons. The nitrogen atom near the boron defect site thus has 0.5 electrons less than its octagonal boron nitride counterpart while the boron atom near the nitrogen defect site has 0.63 electrons more than its octagonal boron nitride counterpart. These results show that the defects result in weaker binding and that the boron and nitrogens atom nearest to the corresponding defect sites undergo less charge transfer than the defect-free octagonal boron nitride.

The presence of a defect, however, unveils interesting results relating to magnetic moment. Defect-free octagonal boron nitride is calculated to have a magnetic moment of 0 for both boron and nitrogen atoms. The boron atom nearest the nitrogen defect on a defected octagonal boron nitride also is calculated to have a magnetic moment of 0. However, when a boron defect is present in octagonal boron nitride, the nitrogen atom closest to the defect

site is found to have a magnetic moment of 0.78. These results show that it is possible to tailor the magnetic moment of the oc- tagonal boron nitride through defects.

In both of the hexagonal and octago- nal cases, the H adsorption energy is calculated to be positive, indicating an endothermic reaction. Additionally, the octagonal formation has a smaller adsorption energy and a shorter bond distance than the hexagonal formation, indicating that the octag- onal formation is more reactive against hydrogen. This can be attributed to the stability of hexagonal BN, as it has an average binding energy of -3.32 eV per atom whereas octagonal BN has an average binding energy of -2.92 eV.

Defected hexagonal and octagonal boron nitride are then ex- plored. The boron-defect and nitrogen-defect sites of both hexagonal and octagonal boron are chosen as the sites for hydro- gen adsorption. In these cases, the hydrogen is adsorped onto the atom closest to the defect site which is of the opposite element. The defects are created for both hexagonal and octagonal boron nitride by removing a nitrogen atom or removing a boron atom. In all cases where defects are present, the hydrogen adsorption energies are calculated to be negative, indicating an exothermic reaction.

In the cases of hexagonal boron nitride, the hydrogen atom is adsorbed on the edge of a nitrogen atom (in the case of the boron defect) and on the edge of the boron atom (in the case of the ni- trogen defect) with adsorption energies of -1.63 eV and -2.39 eV, respectively. Similarly, the hydrogen atom is adsorbed onto the edges of the defect sites in octagonal boron nitride, which can be seen in Figure 5. Figure 5a shows a N-defected unit cell of oc- tagonal boron nitride where the H atom adsorbs onto the boron atom closest to the missing nitrogen atom, which is found to have an adsorption energy of -2.14 eV. Meanwhile, Figure 5b shows a B-defected unit cell of octagonal boron nitride where the H atom adsorbs onto the nitrogen atom closest to the missing boron atom with an adsorption energy of -2.49 eV. It can thus be understood that not only is the octagonal formation more reactive than the hexagonal formation, but the nitrogen atoms are more reactive to hydrogen for both hexagonal and octagonal boron nitride. This is further seen when analyzing the bond distances listed in Ta- ble 1, where the bond distances of nitrogen and hydrogen are consistently shorter than the distances between boron and hydro- gen. Thus, the results show that creating defect sites for both hexagonal and octagonal boron nitride provides reactive sites for hydrogen atoms.

Similar results could be expected when considering oxidation of octagonal BN. Previous studies have reported that hexagonal BN is highly-resistant against oxidation when it is defect-free. $^{26}$ Table 1 shows that defect-free hexagonal BN and octagonal BN are resistant to hydrogen atom. Coupled with previous reports of the oxidation resistance of hexagonal BN, one can consider that octagonal BN is also highly resistant against oxidation when defect-free.

## Conclusion
Density functional theory calculations determine the feasibility of 2D boron nitride with an octagonal lattice. It is found that 2D boron nitride can be designed based on an octagonal lattice with a binding energy of -2.92 eV, indicating that it can occur exothermi- cally. In comparison to 2D hexagonal boron nitride, less electron transfer occurs between the boron and nitrogen atoms, resulting in longer average B-N bond distances. When analyzing the den- sity of states, it can be seen that the octagonal lattice produces a smaller band gap than the hexagonal lattice, suggesting the 2D octagonal boron nitride to have semiconductive properties. This indicate that a tunable semiconductor may be able to be designed by tailoring the shape of the 2D material. Layerability is also ex- amined where PBE and vdW-DF calculations show that layers of 2D octagonal boron nitride are possible via physisorption. The effects of defects present in octagonal boron nitride were also in- vestigated and discovered that while defects cause weaker bind- ing and result in less charge transfer, the presence of a boron atom defect introduces a magnetic moment to nitrogen within

octagonal boron nitride, where boron and nitrogen atoms have no magnetic moment. Additionally, the presence of defects renders both hexagonal and octagonal boron nitride reactive against hydrogen, where particular reactivity is seen when a hydrogen atom adsorbs onto a nitrogen atom. Overall, it is shown that 2D octagonal boron nitride is a possible allotrope of 2D boron nitride where its properties can be tailored via purposeful defects and lattice shape.
