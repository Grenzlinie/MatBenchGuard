## ABSTRACT

First-principles calculations were performed to study the electronic structure and magnetic properties of Li(ZnV) As, as well as the effect of Li vacancy defects. The results showed that the ground state of Li(ZnV)As system was short-range ferromagnetic stable. The Li vacancy defects provided hole carriers but weakened the stability of the system. With the increase of Li vacancy concentration, the amount of electrons occupied in V-3d orbitals decreased gradually. As a result, the atomic magnetic moment of V reduced, and the ferromagnetic stability of Li(ZnV)As system was impaired. Electron occupation in $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals had a remarkable impact on magnetic properties of Li(ZnV)As.

## 1. Introduction

Diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMSs) were important components of functional materials, which were believed to be the basis of spin-based electronics (Spintronics) [1-4]. As a new type of DMSs, transition metal (TM) doped LiZnAs has attracted much attention due to its spin and carrier injection respectively [5-7]. Mašek et al. [8] first predicted the existence of LiZnAs based DMSs in theory, and found that the solubility of the isovalent substitutional Mn impurity was unlimited and the types and concentrations of carriers could be controlled by adjusting the stoichiometric number of Li. The magnetic ordering transition from antiferromagnetic (AFM) state to ferromagnetic (FM) state in Li(ZnMn)As occurred benefiting from carriers introduced by Li defects. Sato et al. [9] studied the effect of the concentrations of magnetic impurities and Li vacancy defects on the ferromagnetic stability of Li(ZnMn)As and Li(ZnCr)As systems. The results showed that Li vacancies suppressed the ferromagnetism, for lower magnetic impurity concentration. On the contrary, the ferromagnetic stability of Li(ZnMn) As and Li(ZnCr)As was enhanced by Li vacancy defects at high spin doping concentration. Wang et al. [10] found that the ground state of Li(ZnCr)As was FM state, and the nearest Cr doping configuration held the strongest FM stability. Li vacancy defects could further stabilize FM state in Li(ZnCr)As system.

Vacancy defect had an important effect on the electron distribution in $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals, which had a significance influence on magnetic properties of DMSs. Sato et al. [11,12] considered that due to the electronic configuration of $\text{Mn}^{2+}(\text{d}^{5})$ in ZnO etc. II-VI compounds, the up-spin $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals were occupied completely and Mn impurities exhibited AFM coupling. However, if the dopant was $\text{V}^{2+}(\text{d}^{3})$ or $\text{Cr}^{2+}(\text{d}^{4})$ whose $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals were occupied partially, FM stability appeared in the doped systems. Katayama-Yoshida et al. [13] studied II-VI and III-V magnetic semiconductors and the results showed that if the anti-bonding states were completely empty or fully filled, the doping systems behaved as spin glass ground states. Mounkachi et al. [14] found that the hetero-valent substitution of $\text{N}^{3-}(2\text{p}^{3})$ and $\text{O}^{2-}(2\text{p}^{4})$ introduced hole carrier for (ZnMn)O system. As a result, the up-spin Mn-3d orbitals became partially occupied, and the magnetic ordering of the system turned from AFM state to FM state [14].

The previous studies on LiZnAs based DMSs have focused on the selection of magnetic atom and the regulation of Li stoichiometric number, neglecting to discuss the influence of crystal field. In this paper, the effect of V separation on the electronic structure and magnetic properties of Li(ZnV)As was investigated. The evolution of ferromagnetic stability of Li(ZnV)As system with different Li vacancy concentrations was discussed from the perspective of crystal field theory.

### 2. Computational methods

LiZnAs has a cubic structure with the lattice constant $a = 5.94$ Å, belonging to the space group $F$-$43m$ [15]. A $2 \times 2 \times 1$ LiZnAs supercell accommodating 48 atoms was constructed, as shown in Fig. 1. A Zn atom inside the supercell was selected as the 0 site. According to the symmetry of the crystal structure, four Zn atoms with increasing separation relative to the 0 site were selected and labeled as 1, 2, 3 and 4, respectively. By means of double doping treatment, Zn atoms were substituted by V atoms. Four different doping configurations with increasing V-V seperation were formed, denoted as V@(0,1), V@(0,2), V@(0,3) and V@(0,4), and the doping concentration of V was about 12.50 *at.%. Li vacancy defects were introduced by removing Li near the two V impurities. The defective configurations were named $\text{Li}_{16+n}(\text{Zn}_{14}\text{V}_2)\text{As}_{16}$ ($n$ = -1, -2, -3, -4) and the corresponding Li vacancy concentrations were about 6.25 *at.%, 12.50 *at.%, 18.75 *at.% and 25.00 *at.%, respectively. The electronic structure and magnetic properties were calculated in both FM state (V atoms with the same spin directions) and AFM state (V atoms with the opposite spin directions) for all configurations after geometry optimization. To evaluate the energy stability, the formation energy ($E_f$) was calculated by the formula: $E_f = E_D - E_H - 2\mu_V + 2\mu_{Zn} - n\mu_{Li}$, where $E_D$ and $E_H$ were the total energies of LiZnAs with and without doping treatments; the $\mu_V$, $\mu_{Zn}$ and $\mu_{Li}$ were the chemical potentials of V, Zn and Li, respectively [16]. To determine the coupling between V dopants, the energy difference ($\triangle E$) was employed: $\triangle E = E_{AFM} - E_{FM}$, where $E_{AFM}$ and $E_{FM}$ were the total energies of configurations in AFM and FM states, respectively.

The first-principles calculations within Cambridge Sequential Total Energy Package (CASTEP) code were performed [17,18]. The generalized gradient approximations (GGA) of Perdew-Burke-Emzerh (PBE) was adopted to process the exchange correlation potential [19]. The plane wave super-soft pseudopotential method was used to describe the interaction between atomic core and valence electrons. The Monkhorst Pack grid with $1 \times 2 \times 3$ $k$-points for Brillouin zone integration was used, and the plane wave cut-off energy was 500 eV to ensure convergence of energy [20].

### 3. Results and discussions

The optimized lattice constants, distance between the two V dopants, formation energy and energy difference of Li(ZnV)As are shown in Table 1. Due to the similar ionic radius of V and Zn, the substitution of V for Zn has no obvious effect on the lattice constants. The magnetic moment is mainly provided by V atom and the maximum atomic magnetic moment emerging from V@(0,1) configuration is $3.20$ $\mu_B$/V. All the formation energies are negative, indicating that these structures are stable and our doping designs are reasonable. The values of $\triangle E$ are all positive manifesting that Li(ZnV)As system prefers ferromagnetic ground state. V@(0,1) configuration holds the maximum value of $\triangle E$ (0.31 eV), which is significantly larger than the configuration of second nearest-neighbor V. However, the values of $\triangle E$ in V@(0,2), V@(0,3) and V@(0,4) configurations have tiny discrepancy. Therefore, the ferromagnetic stability of Li(ZnV)As system shows short-rang characteristics.

The Mulliken atomic population of V-3d and V-As bond length are considered [21,22]. The results are shown in Table 2. The amount of Mulliken atomic population of V-3d is similar for the electron configuration of $\text{V}^{2+}(3\text{d}^3)$ despite of V different doping distances. The value in V@(0,1) configuration is 3.83e, which is slightly larger than other doping configurations. The V-As bond length is a little affected by the V-V separation. The shortest V-As bond length is $2.510$ Å, falling on the V@(0,1) configuration.

After introducing different concentrations of Li vacancies, the optimized lattice constants, Mulliken atomic population of V-3d, V-As bond length and formation energy are shown in Table 3. With the increase of Li vacancy concentration, the lattice constants of $\text{Li}_{16+n}(\text{Zn}_{14}\text{V}_2)\text{As}_{16}$ ($n$ = -1, -2, -3, -4) decrease gradually. For the small ionic radius of Li, the reduction of lattice constants is not obvious, and the introduction of Li vacancies does not cause lattice distortion. In the configurations with the same V-doping seperation but different Li vacancy concentrations, due to the existence of Li vacancy, the distance between V and its nearest neighbor As decreases. With the increase of Li vacancy concentration, the length of V-As bond is shortened and the strength is enhanced. Li vacancy defect belongs to $p$-type doping, which introduces hole carrier for each configuration. The results of Mulliken atomic population show that the number of electrons in the V-3d orbitals is reduced evidently compared with the corresponding configurations before the introduction of Li vacancy as shown in Table 2, which indicates that hole carriers enter the V-3d orbitals. The formation energy increases with the increase of Li vacancy concentration, which means that Li vacancy weakens the stability of the doping system. When the Li vacancy concentration is up to 25.00 *at.%, the formation energies of V@(0,1) and V@(0,2) arrive at 0.10 eV, indicating that the doping configurations can not exist stably. The formation energies of V@(0,3) and V@(0,4) are close to zero, showing the instability of the doping designs. As a consequence, the concentration of Li vacancy defect is limited in Li(ZnV)As system. Therefore, in the following analysis, we will just take the $\text{Li}_{16+n}(\text{Zn}_{14}\text{V}_2)\text{As}_{16}$ ($n$ = -1, -2, -3) into consideration.

The functional relationship between atomic magnetic moment of V and Li vacancy concentration is shown in Fig. 2. For comparison, the atomic magnetic moments of V in configurations without Li vacancies are also plotted. At the same V-V seperation, the atomic magnetic moment of V decreases linearly with the increase of Li vacancy concentration. This phenomenon arises from the reduction of unpaired electron number in V-3d orbitals for the introduction of Li vacancy defects, and is also related to the decrease of V-As bond length, which enhances the hybridization between V-3d and As-4p. Previous studies have shown that the enhancement of $p$-$d$ hybridization impairs the magnetic moment of magnetic impurity [23,24]. However, at the same Li vacancy concentration, the distance between V impurities has little effect on the atomic magnetic moment.

The variation trend of $\triangle E$ with Li vacancy concentration is present in Fig. 3. Under the condition of the same V separation, the value of $\triangle E$ is the largest in the configuration without Li vacancy, and decreases with the increase of Li vacancy concentration. It indicates that Li vacancy weakens the ferromagnetic stability of the doping system. It is worth noting that when the Li vacancy concentration increases from 0 to 6.25 *at.%, the values of $\triangle E$ decrease sharply. However, when the Li vacancy concentration increases from 6.25 *at.% to 18.75 *at.%, the values of $\triangle E$ decrease gently. Under the condition of the same Li vacancy concentration, the values of $\triangle E$ decrease with the increase of V separation. When the Li vacancy concentration is up to 18.75 *at.%, the values of $\triangle E$ tend to zero in the V@(0,4) configuration.

Therefore, $t_{2g}$ and As-4p hybridize strongly forming delocalized $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals, which generate bonding states ($t^b$) in the valence band and anti-bonding states ($t^a$) in the band gap. However, for the asymmetry of $e_g$ and As-4p wave functions, the hybridization between $e_g$ and As-4p barely happens. The wave functions of the $e_g$ extend to the interstitial region of the bonding states and anti-bonding states. In consequence, $e_g$ states remain as non-bonding states ($e$) [25–27]. Bonding states and anti-bonding states cover a wide energy range, in which electrons preserve very strong itinerant characteristics. On the contrary, non-bonding states are featured by a relative sharp and narrow peak and electrons in non-bonding states are rather localized. Therefore, $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals play a crucial role in the coupling between magnetic impurities.

To study the mechanism of ferromagnetic stability of $Li_{16+n}(Zn_{14}V_2)As_{16}$ ($n=0,-1,-2,-3$), V@(0,1) with and without Li vacancies are taken as a typical example to analyze the electronic structure. The total and partial density of states (DOS) are displayed in Fig. 4. The compounds show half-metallic characteristics, with the majority spin metallic and minority spin semiconducting [28]. Fermi level passes through V-3d orbitals and locates in the region between anti-bonding and non-bonding peaks, indicating that the V-3d orbitals are partially occupied with enough hole carriers. In consequence, ferromagnetic double-exchange is proved to be effective in promoting ferromagnetic coupling between V atoms [29]. In Li(ZnV)As without defect treatment, Fermi level falls in the anti-bonding states, as seen in Fig. 4 (a), which means that there are still a few itinerant electrons in the anti-bonding states of $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals. When the Li vacancy concentration increases from 0 to 6.25 at.%, as seen in Fig. 4 (b), Fermi level moves towards the valence band and the anti-bonding states change from partially occupied to completely empty. Due to the significant influence of the electron occupation in $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals on the magnetic stability, the ferromagnetic stability declines sharply. When Li vacancy concentration increases from 6.25 at.% to 18.75 at.% as the comparition of Fig. 4 (b), (c) and (d), Fermi level is gradually pushed to the valence band, approaching the peak of non-bonding states. It manifests that the number of electrons in the non-bonding states decreases gradually. Owing to the weak effect of non-bonding states on the magnetic properties, the ferromagnetic stability is impaired gently. This is consistent with the variation trend of energy difference with Li vacancy concentration. Therefore, in Li(ZnV)As system, when the $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals are partially occupied by electrons, the ground state is ferromagnetic stable. In particular, the ferromagnetic stability of the system is the strongest when there are both electron and hole carriers in the anti-bonding orbitals. The ferromagnetic stability of the system is weakened when the number of electrons occupied by $p$-$d$ hybrid orbitals decreases.

### 4. Conclusions

In this paper, electronic structure and magnetic properties of Li(ZnV)As with various V doping distance and different concentrations of Li vacancy defects were investigated based on the first-principles calculations within the density functional theory. The results showed that the ground state of Li(ZnV)As exhibited short-range ferromagnetic stability in the regime of double-exchange mechanism. Li vacancy defects were limited in Li(ZnV)As system. If the defect concentration reached 25.00 at.%, the configuration became unstable. Li vacancy defects were acceptor impurities, which introduced hole carriers entering V-3d orbitals.
