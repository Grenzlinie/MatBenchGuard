# Origin of ferromagnetism in Sm-doped $In_{2}S_{3}$ nanoparticles: experimental and theoretical insights

Yi Liu, Chengbo Zhai, Kewei Zhang, Liyong Du, Mingming Zhu and Mingzhe Zhang*

State Key Laboratory of Superhard Materials,College of Physics, Jilin University, Changchun 130012, People's Republic of China.

*E-mail address: zhangmz@jlu.edu.cn

**Abstract**: Diluted magnetic semiconductors with room-temperature ferromagnetism (RTFM) opens new prospects for the development of next-generation spintronic devices. For now, the rare earth doping is an effective method to tune the ferromagnetism of semiconductor materials. In this work, we fabricated undoped $In_{2}S_{3}$ and $In_{2}S_{3}: Sm^{3+}$ nanoparticles using gas-liquid phase chemical deposition method. UV-visible and photoluminescence spectra demonstrate that the introduction of $Sm^{3+}$ ions lead to bandgap narrowing and enhanced luminescence intensity. Magnetic characterizations show that all synthetic samples display a ferromagnetic behaviour, and the maximum saturation magnetization of the samples can be tuned by regulating the concentration of doped $Sm^{3+}$ ions. The RTFM in Sm doped $In_{2}S_{3}$ nanoparticles derives from the coupling interaction between the localized defect states and $Sm^{3+}$ ions and is described using bound magnetic polarons (BMPs) mechanism. First-principle calculations reveal that In vacancies and Sm dopants are responsible for the generation of magnetic moments which come mainly from S 3p orbitals around the In vacancy and Sm 4f orbitals. Our results suggest that $In_{2}S_{3}: Sm^{3+}$ nanoparticles constitute promising semiconductor materials for use in future electronic and spintronic devices.

**Keywords**: $Sm^{3+}$ doped $In_{2}S_{3}$ nanoparticles; Room temperature ferromagnetism; First principles calculations; Spin-polarized

### 1. Introduction

Diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMSs) have drawn intensive attention in recent years because of their promising applications in multi-functional spintronic devices [1-3]. Spintronics combines the merits of magnetoelectronics and semiconductor electronics to manipulate both the spin and the charge of electron for data storage and information processing. DMSs, having both magnetic and semiconducting properties, render a possibility for in-depth study of magnetic phenomenon in crystals with good transport and magneto-optical properties and uncomplicated band structure. Therefore, the fabrication of materials with high Curie temperature and stable magnetism is one of the crucial issues to realize the applications of DMSs. DMSs can be synthesized by substituting a fraction of the constituent ions with transition metal (TM) or rare earth (RE) elements. Coey *et al.* put forward that the BMP model could explain the long-range ferromagnetic ordering in magnetic materials [4]. In the BMP model, the exchange interaction between bound electrons associated with defects and $3d$, $4f$ electrons of the doped ions tends to align magnetic spins within the polaron's strain field, which results in the ferromagnetic regions to overlap and producing long range ferromagnetic ording [5, 6]. Since, $4f$ shells of RE atoms are unfilled and have highly localized nature, $4f$ RE elements have larger magnetic moments than $3d$ TMs [7]. The doping of rare-earth elements in semiconductor materials is to be considered promising to manipulate magnetic coupling.

A great deal of work has been done on the magnetic study of III-V, II-VI, and IV semiconductors [8-10]. Among many DMS candidates, indium sulfide (In₂S₃) has attracted considerable interest, due to its low toxicity, long-term stability, high photoconductivity and good photosensitivity. In₂S₃ was found to have three different crystalline polymorphs: α-In₂S₃ (defective

cubic structure), $\beta$-In$_2$S$_3$ (defective spinel structure), and $\gamma$-In$_2$S$_3$ (layered hexagonal structure) [11].

Among them, $\beta$-In$_2$S$_3$, the most stable state at room temperature with a cubic and tetragonal form,
is an n-type semiconductor with a wide bandgap of 1.9-2.4 eV that can be used in visible light region
[12]. $\text{In}^{3+}$ ions occupy 2/3 of tetrahedral sites and all octahedral positions of the crystal structure,
the remaining 1/3 of tetrahedral sites are unoccupied, resulting in many cation vacancies. This
structural uniqueness has made In$_2$S$_3$ a host for a number of magnetic dopants to form DMSs. All
kinds of In$_2$S$_3$ microstructures, such as nanosheets, nanoflakes, nanorods, nanobelts, hollow
microspheres, hierarchical structures, etc, have been synthesized using different preparation
methods [13-18]. Foreign ions doping can regulate the physical properties of the host In$_2$S$_3$.

Meng et al. reported that Ni/Fe codoped In$_2$S$_3$ nanosheet arrrays were prepared by a facile
hydrothermal method. This codoping led to decreased surface transfer impedance, prolonged
carrier lifetime, increased charge carrier concentration and reduced overpotential of hydrogen
evolution reaction [16]. Chen et al. synthesized Cu doped $\beta$-In$_2$S$_3$ bulks by solid-state reaction,
and experimental results indicated that Cu doping is an effective way to optimize the thermoelectric
performance of $\beta$-In$_2$S$_3$ [19]. Tiss et al. investigated the effect of Ag dopant on the structural and
optical properties of In$_2$S$_3$ thin films deposited by spray pyrolysis technique [20]. Recently, several
theoretical and experimental reports showed that cation and anion vacancies can induce room
temperature ferromagnetism in nanoparticles of nonmagnetic semiconductor materials such as ZnS,
In$_2$O$_3$, EuO, GaN [21-24]. However, the magnetic origin of In$_2$S$_3$ without dopants needs to be studies
deeply in experiment and theory. The impact of doped elements on the magnetic properties and
electronic structure of pristine In$_2$S$_3$ is still unclear. Thus the current work focuses on this issue to
research the manifestation of vacancy and RE (Sm) induced ferromagnetism in In$_2$S$_3$ nanoparticles.

In this study, we report the room temperature ferromagnetism of Sm-doped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ nanoparticles with 0.34, 1.13, 1.68 and 1.87 at.% Sm fabricated by a facile gas-liquid phase chemical deposition method. The structural and chemical compositions of all samples were investigated by XRD, EDS, TEM and XPS. The optical bandgap and photoluminescent properties of the samples were detected by using UV-visible spectra and PL spectra. Magnetic characterizations showed that the values of saturation magnetization in our samples can be tuned by regulating $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ ions doping concentrations. First-principles DFT computation was carried out to investigate the origin of the ferromagnetism in the undoped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ and $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles. The results revealed that the contribution to the magnetic moment may primarily derive from In vacancies and Sm substitution.

## 2. Experimental details

### 2.1 Preparation of samples

The undoped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ and $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles were fabricated by using a gas-liquid phase chemical method. Firstly, $\text{In(COOCH}_3\text{)}_3\text{·3H}_2\text{O}$ (10 mmol L⁻¹) and $\text{Sm(COOCH}_3\text{)}_3\text{·6H}_2\text{O}$ (2 mmol L⁻¹) were mixed with deionized water as a reactive solution. The molar ratio of $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ and $\text{In}^{3+}$ was 0.01, 0.04, 0.08 and 0.12 in the reaction solution, separately. $\beta\text{-HOCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{SH}$ (60 mmol L⁻¹) was slowly dropped into the mixed solution under constant magnetic stirring. Acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) in the well-mixed solution (1000 mL) was added to reach pH≈3. Then, the reaction solution is maintained at 30 °C by using a circulating water bath. Secondly, the $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ gas was synthesized by reaction of $\text{HCl}$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{S}$ solution with molar ratio of 2:1. Thirdly, the as-obtained $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ gas was directly introduced into the reaction solution by using a gas pump. After the solution completely turned yellow and these was no obvious change in colour, the $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ was stopped. Finally, the obtained yellow precipitate was collected by centrifugation and washed thrice with deionized water and

ethanol, respectively, and dried at 60 °C for 12 h in an oven. The $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles with different doping concentrations were formed from the following equations:

$$3\text{HOCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{SH}+ \text{In}(\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_3\rightarrow(\text{HOCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{S})_3\text{In}+3\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \tag{1}$$

$$3\text{HOCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{SH}+ \text{Sm}(\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_3\rightarrow(\text{HOCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{S})_3\text{Sm}+3\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \tag{2}$$

$$(\text{HOCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{S})_3\text{In}+(\text{HOCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{S})_3\text{Sm}+\text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow\rightarrow\text{In}_2\text{S}_3:\text{Sm}\downarrow \tag{3}$$

### 2.2 Characterization

The as-synthetized samples were examined by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) (Rigaku D/max-2550). The doping concentration and chemical compositions of the samples were evaluated using energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) in a Magellan-400 scanning electron microscopy. The transmission electron microscopy (TEM), high resolution TEM (HRTEM) image and selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern were recorded on a JEOL JEM-2200FS microscope. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB MK II) measurements were performed to obtain the information of the valance states of the elements. Room-temperature UV-visible spectra were taken by using a UV-2802S spectrophotometer. Photoluminescence (PL) spectra were carried out on a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Horiba QuantaMaster 400). Magnetic characterizations were performed using a Lakeshore 7410 vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM).

### 2.3 Calculation details

The theoretical calculations based on the density functional theory (DFT) were performed using the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) [25]. The electronic exchange-correlation potential was computed by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) within the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) formula, and the electron-ion interactions were described by the projector-

augmented wave (PAW) pseudo potentials [26]. The irreducible Brillouin zone was sampled using a grid of 3×3×1 $k$-points. A cut-off energy of 350 eV was used for expanding the plane-wave functions. In the structural optimization process, the convergence criterion for the total energy was $10^{-4}$ eV/atom and for the force was $0.05$ eV/Å$^{-1}$. The GGA+$U$ method was adopted to calculate the on-site Coulomb interaction, and $U_{eff} (U_{eff}=U-J)$ were set to 5 eV and 7.35 eV for In d orbitals and Sm f orbitals respectively.

## 3. Results and discussion

X-ray diffraction (XRD) is executed with Cu K$\alpha$ radiation ($\lambda$=1.5406 Å) to determine the crystal structures' phase. Fig. 1a shows the XRD patterns of the undoped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ and $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles with different $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ concentrations. The XRD peaks can be indexed by the tetragonal phase of $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ (JCPDS, No. 73-1366). No other secondary phases or impurity phases due to $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ ions doping are detected, which indicates that $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ ions substituted $\text{In}^{3+}$ ions of $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ without changing its crystal structure. The diffraction peaks of all samples correspond to (109), (00 12) and (22 12) crystal planes of the tetragonal phase $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$. The average crystallite sizes of the samples calculated by applying Scherrer's formula on the (22 12) peaks are roughly 3-5 nm. $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ stoichiometry and Sm doping concentrations are investigated by EDS spectra. The doping concentrations of Sm ions in these samples are determined to be 0.34, 1.13, 1.68 and 1.87 at.%. Fig. 1b shows the EDS results of the 1.13 at.% $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: Sm nanoparticles. The corresponding atomic ratio of In: S is approximately 1: 1.5, which accords with the stoichiometry of $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$. Fig. 1c and 1d presents TEM and HRTEM images of 1.13 at.% $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: Tm nanoparticles separately. The results reveal that the as-synthesized samples are highly populated and almost spherical in shape with an average diameter of 4.41 nm, which is consistent with the XRD evaluation. The lattice fringes with

a $d$-spacing of 0.271 nm correspond to the (00 12) planes of tetragonal phase $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$. The corresponding SEAD pattern is shown in the inset of Fig. 1d. The SAED pattern exhibits concentric rings indicating the polycrystalline nature of the samples, and the diffraction rings are indexed to the (109), (00 12) and (22 12) planes of the tetragonal phase $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$.

XPS measurements are carried out to futher analyze the chemical elemental composition and valence states in the materials. The survey and high-resolution XPS spectra of $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ (1.68 at.%) nanoparticles are shown in Fig. 2. The elements such as In, S, Sm, C, O are observed in the survey scan spectrum (Fig. 2a) and other impurity elements are not detected. The bingding energy of C 1s at 284.8 eV is as the reference. The observed O 1s peak at 532.9 eV is due to absorbed oxygen on the surface of the sample. The In 3d spectrum (Fig. 2b) shown at 445.16 eV and 452.68 eV assign to the binding energies of In $3\text{d}_{5/2}$ and In $3\text{d}_{3/2}$ for $\text{In}^{3+}$ ions, respectively [27]. The S 2p peak (Fig. 2c) splits into double peaks at 161.52 eV and 162.67 eV, corresponding to S $2\text{p}_{3/2}$ and S $2\text{p}_{1/2}$, which is characteristic of $\text{S}^{2-}$ in $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ [28]. The observed binding energy of the Sm 4d at 123.67 eV (Fig. 2d) correspond to the energy of $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ ions [29]. The results illustrate that the $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ ions have been incorporated into the $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ host material.

UV-vis diffuse reflectance spectra are used to investigate the optical bandgap energy of the obtained samples. Fig. 3 displays UV-vis diffuse reflectance spectra of the undoped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ and $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles. It can be clearly seen from Fig. 3 that the prepared samples have a strong edge absorption in the visible region, which is attributed to valence to conduction band transition. The bandgap energy can be estimated using the Tauc relation. According to the Tauc relation, the relation between the absorption coefficient $\alpha$ and the photo energy $h\nu$ is given by [30]

$$(\alpha h\nu)^{\text{n}} = \text{A}(h\nu - \text{E}_\text{g}) \tag{4}$$

Where $E_g$ is the optical bandgap, A is the absorption constant, n is an index which assumes the values 1/2 and 2 for indirect and direct bandgap transitions separately. The inset of Fig. 3 depicts the $(\alpha h\nu)^{1/2}$ versus $h\nu$ plot for an indirect transition. The intercept of the extrapolated linear part of the plot at $(\alpha h\nu)^{1/2}=0$ will represent the $E_g$ values. Thus, the bandgap values of $In_2S_3$: $Sm^{3+}$ nanoparticles are estimated to be 2.13 eV, 2.06 eV, 1.95 eV, 1.92 eV and 1.98 eV corresponding to $Sm^{3+}$ dopant as 0 at.%, 0.34 at.%, 1.13 at.%, 1.68 at.% and 1.87 at.%, respectively. Doping $Sm^{3+}$ ions can lead to the bandgap narrowing. This results can be attributed to the fact that strong sp-f exchange interactions between the band electrons and the localized Sm 4f electrons substituting In ions, which procures the maximum of the valance band moves up, thus leading to the bandgap narrowing [31, 32]. The absorption edges of the samples are shifted to the low energy region indicating that the $Sm^{3+}$ ions have been doped into the lattice of $In_2S_3$.

The room temperature PL spectra of the undoped $In_2S_3$ and $In_2S_3$: $Sm^{3+}$ nanoparticles with an excitation wavelength of 279 nm are shown in Fig. 4. The PL spectra of all samples can be resolved into six emission peaks centered at 419.6 nm, 467.1 nm, 481.3 nm, 491.5 nm, 530.1 nm and 583.3 nm, respectively. The emission peak at 419.6 nm can be attributed to higher-energy state transitions formed by the surface oxidation states [33]. The emission peak at 467.1 nm is ascribed to the near band edge emission associated with an exciton recombination [34]. The two shoulders at 481.3 nm and 491.5 nm are a characteristic of defect-to-band recombination emission, which may be caused by sulfur vacancies ($V_S$) and second order sulfur vacancies ($V_S'$) [35]. The observed emission peak at 530.1 nm is related to the indium interstitial sites [36]. The emission peak at 583.3 nm may be resulted from the transitions between sulfur vacancies (donors) and indium vacancies (acceptors) [37]. The emission peaks are significantly blue shifted relative to the reported data of 620 nm for

bulk $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$, which is attributed to quantum-confined effects of $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ nanoparticles [38]. It is obvious from Fig. 4 that the intensity of PL spectra increases as $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ dopant concentration increases to 1.33 at.%. This indicates that $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ as activation ions can enhance the radiative recombination process due to the increasing defect states [39]. Moreover, the distortion of the local symmetry around $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ increases the transition probabilities, which also leads to the enhancement of PL intensity [40]. It is reported that the defect concentration has a positively correlated with the intensity of the defect related emission peaks [41]. From the normalized PL spectra (see inset of Fig. 4), the weaker PL emission intensity of $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles at 419.6 nm as compared to that of undoped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ implies that doped $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ ions cause the reduction of surface defect states, which may be due to more covalent nature of the Sm-S bond. The PL emission intensity of $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles is stronger at 481.3 nm, 491.5 nm, 530.1 nm and 583.3 nm, indicating an increased concentration of native point defects ($\text{V}_{\text{In}}$, $\text{I}_{\text{In}}$ and $\text{V}_{\text{S}}$). With the doping of $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ ions above 1.33 at.%, the intensity of PL emission spectra significantly decreases due to concentration quenching effect. Increased ion-ion interactions between rare-earth ions lead to an increase of non-radiative processes, which is the main cause of the concentration quenching [42].

The magnetization versus magnetic field ($M$-$H$) curves at room temperature for the undoped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ and $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ nanoparticles with applied magnetic field ranging from -6000 Oe to 6000 Oe are shown in Fig. 5a. Fig. 5b shows the amplified details of $M$-$H$ curves within low fields. The $M$-$H$ curves with non-ignorable coercivity of the samples exhibit room temperature ferromagnetic (RTFM) behavior. Table 1 displays the magnetic parameters extracted from $M$-$H$ curves of the samples. The saturation magnetization ($M_s$) are 0.0067 emu/g, 0.0089 emu/g, 0.0127 emu/g, 0.0149 emu/g and 0.0111 emu/g, of the as-obtained with $\text{Sm}^{3+}$ doping concentration of 0 at.%, 0.34 at.%,

1.13 at.%, 1.68 at.% and 1.87 at.%, respectively. As the increase of the $Sm^{3+}$ doping concentration, the value of $M_s$ increases first and then decreases gradually after reaching the maximum observed value at 1.68 at.%, which can be observed in the inset of Fig. 5a. As illustrated in Table 1, the doping leads to increase in the coercive fields, which is related to the enhancement of the magnetocrystalline anisotropy and ion interactions [43, 44]. For sample with a doping concentration of 1.87 at.%, the coercive field are decreased which may be due to the reduction of vacancy defects and domain pinning [45]. The weak RTFM in undoped $In_2S_3$ nanoparticles is due to vacancy defects during growth process, the similar phenomenon is also found in $MoS_2$, $ZnS$, $SnS_2$ [21, 46, 47]. The ferromagnetism of Sm-doped $In_2S_3$ nanoparticles can be explained based on bound magnetic polaron (BMP) theory, considering that the BMPs in the mechanism are formed by coupling interaction between the localized defect states and the doped $Sm^{3+}$ ions [48]. According to the theory, The neighboring BMPs effectively overlap with increasing doping concentration to induce long-range ferromagnetic ordering. However, higher doping concentrations will reduce the average distance between adjacent $Sm^{3+}$ ions, and antiferromagnetic alignment appear due to short-range exchange interactions. The competition between the antiferromagnetism and ferromagnetism results in the decrease of the $M_s$ in the $In_2S_3$: $Sm^{3+}$ nanoparticles. Fig. S1 shows the temperature dependent magnetization ($M$-$T$) curves of 1.68 at.% $In_2S_3$: Sm nanoparticles under zero magnetic field cooling (ZFC) and field cooling (FC) at applied field of 500 Oe. The divergence between the FC and ZFC curves demonstrates that the Sm doped $In_2S_3$ nanoparticles are ferromagnetic and have a Curie temperature ($T_c$) above 300 K. Below 100 K, the downturn in the ZFC curve of the sample can be attributed to the presence of competing for anti-ferromagnetic interaction between doped Sm atoms or between cationic indium vacancies [49]. Above 100 K, the ZFC magnetization increases with the

increases in temperature, demonstrating that there are blocked moments which begin to contribute to the magnetization. The FC magnetization decreases as the temperature increases, because the frozen moments state to randomize under the action of thermal energy [50].

To further explore the electronic structure and the origin of the ferromagnetism in Sm-doped
$\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ nanoparticles, first-principles calculations are carried out using the VASP code. The following models are considered to investigate the magnetic properties and formation energies of different defects in $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$: (a) the undoped $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ ($\text{In}_{32}\text{S}_{48}$), (b) one S vacancy ($\text{V}_\text{s}$), (c) one In vacancy ($\text{V}_\text{In}$), (d) one In atom substituted by one Sm atom ($\text{Sm}_\text{In}$), (e) a defect complex with one Sm atom substituted In site and one In vacancy ($\text{Sm}_\text{In}$+$\text{V}_\text{In}$). The geometry of five models is depicted in Fig. S2. The total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) of the systems are calculated and plotted in Fig. 6. Part I and II in Fig. 6a shown perfectly symmetric TDOSs with respect to spin direction, which demonstrates that ideal model ($\text{In}_{32}\text{S}_{48}$) is non-magnetic and $\text{V}_\text{S}$ does not introduce magnetism into $\text{In}_2\text{S}_3$ and cause spin splitting. However, the TDOSs of the $\text{V}_\text{In}$, $\text{Sm}_\text{In}$ and $\text{Sm}_\text{In}$+$\text{V}_\text{In}$ systems display significant spin polarization near the Fermi level (part III-V in Fig. 6a). From the PDOSs of $\text{V}_\text{In}$ (Fig. 6b), we can observe that the presence of In vacancy induces an impurity band near Fermi energy, which is spin polarized and composed mainly of S-3p orbitals. Moreover, the calculated total magnetic moment ($\text{M}_\text{total}$=1.830 $\mu_\text{B}$) is mainly contributed by the localized S-3p orbitals around the In vacancy ($\text{M}_\text{S-3p}$=1.354 $\mu_\text{B}$). To further analyze the origin and distribution of the magnetism, the spin-density distribution calculations are performed and shown in Fig. 7a. The results indicate that a large portion of spin densities originate from the nearest neighboring six S atoms surrounding the In vacancy, which is consistent with the PDOS results.

From the PDOSs of $\text{Sm}_\text{In}$ (Fig. 6c), it can be clearly seen that the introduction of Sm polarizes the

host band and induces impurity band at the top of the valence band. This impurity band is mainly formed by Sm-4f orbitals, which is also spin polarized and hybridized with S-3p orbitals. The total magnetic moment of $Sm_{In}$ system is 5.143 $\mu_B$. The introduction of Sm brings a local magnetic moment of 5.528$\mu_B$, which comes mainly from Sm-4f orbitals ($M_{Sm-4f}$=5.523$\mu_B$). Furthermore, it can be observed from the spin-density distribution (Fig. 7b) that the spin density is mostly localized on Sm atoms. The PDOSs of the defect complex ($Sm_{In}$+$V_{In}$) system are displayed in Fig. 6d. We can see that the interaction of doped Sm with native defects can influence the DOS of the S atoms and induce impurity band close to Fermi level in the spin-up part of the TDOS [51]. Sm-S hybridizations can be observed near the Fermi level. The contribution to magnetism in $Sm_{In}$+$V_{In}$ system is credited to S-3p orbitals around the In vacancy and Sm-4f orbitals, which is agreement with the spin-density distribution calculation results (Fig. 7c). This defect complex carries a total magnetic moment of 4.658 $\mu_B$ in which Sm contributes 5.032 $\mu_B$ and S around the In vacancy contributes 0.098 $\mu_B$. Therefore, these results suggest that the Sm substitution and the In vacancy give contribution to the ferromagnetism of the samples.

The calculated band structures of the systems along the high symmetry directions of the first Brillouin zone are exhibited in Fig. 8. It can be clearly seen that the valence band maximum (VBM) is located at X point, whereas the conduction band minimum (CBM) is located at the G point, which means that the bandgaps of the samples are indirect. The calculated bandgap value is smaller than the experimentally measured values due to the fact that the standardized GGA calculations generally underestimate bandgap values [52]. We can see that bands splitting and impurity bands occur around the Fermi level in $V_{In}$, $Sm_{In}$ and $Sm_{In}$+$V_{In}$ system, which is in consistent with the TDOS results. The bandgap values of four systems are 1.079 eV, 0.755 eV, 0.341 eV and 0.133 eV for $In_{32}S_{48}$, $V_{In}$, $Sm_{In}$

and $Sm_{In}+V_{In}$ separately. The introduction of Sm leads to the decrease of bandgap value, which is in good agreement with the UV-vis analysis. Furthermore, the formation energies of the defect structures can be calculated using the following equation:

$$
\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{f}}=\mathrm{E}_{\text {defect }}-\mathrm{E}_{\text {perfect }}+\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{In}} \mu_{\mathrm{In}}-\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{Sm}} \mu_{\mathrm{Sm}} \tag{5}
$$

where $E_{defect}$ is the total energy of the defect structure and $E_{perfect}$ is the total energy of the perfect $In_{2}S_{3}$ structure, $n_{In}$ is the number of In atom substituted by defect, $n_{Sm}$ is the number of the introduced Sm atom, $\mu_{In}$ is the chemical potential of In and $\mu_{Sm}$ is the chemical potential of Sm. In this work, the growth environment of the samples preparation is under In-rich conditions, thus the chemical potential of the In is obtained by metal In. For $V_{In}$, $Sm_{In}$, $Sm_{In}+V_{In}$ structures, the calculated formation energies 5.01 eV, -6.36 eV, -1.75 eV, respectively. The above results indicate that the introduction of Sm not only reduces the formation energies of the system and stabilizes In vacancy, but also generates a larger magnetic moment.

## 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, the undoped $In_{2}S_{3}$ and $In_{2}S_{3}: Sm^{3+}$ nanoparticles were successfully prepared using a gas-liquid phase chemical deposition method. The purity and composition of the as-obtained samples were characterized using XRD, EDS, XPS measurements. The UV-visible spectra indicate that the presence of $Sm^{3+}$ in the samples leads to the bandgap narrowing due to strong sp-f exchange interactions between the band electrons and the localized Sm 4f electrons. The luminescence intensity is significantly enhanced when $Sm^{3+}$ ions are doped into the host $In_{2}S_{3}$, and the decrease in PL intensity with increasing of $Sm^{3+}$ dopant concentrations is due to concentration quenching. Room temperature ferromagnetism is observed in the undoped $In_{2}S_{3}$ and $In_{2}S_{3}: Sm^{3+}$ nanoparticles

and the values of $M_s$ can be manipulated by tuning the $Sm^{3+}$ dopant concentrations. The theoretical calculated results show that the observed intrinsic ferromagnetism in the samples originates from In vacancies and $Sm^{3+}$ ions. Furthermore, the calculation results of bandgap value also confirm that the doped $Sm^{3+}$ ions result in the decrease of bandgap. The results of this work suggest that as-synthesized $In_2S_3$ samples are desirable candidates for potential applications in spintronic and memory storage devices.
