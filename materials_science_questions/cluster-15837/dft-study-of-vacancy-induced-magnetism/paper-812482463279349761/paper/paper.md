## Abstract
Two-dimensional (2D) MXene materials have attracted much attention in recent years because of their excellent properties. In this paper, we theoretically investigated the quantum capacitance, electronic and optical properties of pristine $Sc_{2}CF_{2}$ and the vacancy-doping $Sc_{2}CF_{2}$ monolayers by first principles calculations. Pristine $Sc_{2}CF_{2}$ is the most stable structure among the four investigated systems according to the analysis of binding energy, and the introduction of vacancy does not improve the stability of the system. The introduction of vacancy produced the defect energy levels, which pass through the Fermi energy level and result in the semiconductor-metal transition. For Pristine $Sc_{2}CF_{2}$, the introduction of $V_{Sc}$ results in the strong magnetism with $1.0\ \mu_{B}$, which is mainly from the contribution of C-pz, Sc-dz₂, Sc-dxz states. The analysis of optical properties indicates that the introduction of the vacancy not only makes the system more sensitive to the infrared light, but also improves the reflectivity, especially in the infrared region. The introduction of vacancy improves the $C_{diff}$ of the systems at 0 V, and drastically increases the maximum $C_{int}$ of the vacancy-doping systems. Charge transfer is further explored.

## 1 | INTRODUCTION

With the rapid development of science and technology, the discovery of new functional materials plays an increasingly prominent role in scientific research [1]. Two-dimensional materials have attracted more and more attention because of their different characteristics from other materials [2]. Compared with other materials, 2D materials have superior electronic structure and physical properties [3]. 2D materials are proposed with the successful separation of graphene in 2004 [4, 5]. Some other 2D materials were later discovered, such as transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) [6] and layered double hydroxides (LDHs) [7]. The excellent properties of these materials make them show potential applications in energy storage [8], adsorption [9], catalysis [10, 11], photoelectricity [12] and so on. However, there are some shortcomings in the practical application of these materials. Monolayer graphene has high carrier mobility, large specific surface area, excellent mechanical strength [13], but the characteristics of zero bandgap of graphene hinders its applications in nanoelectronics and optoelectronic devices [14]. Although $MoS_{2}$ (TMDs) has good semiconductor properties, the low carrier mobility cannot meet the requirements of high-speed electronic devices [15]. Therefore, it is necessary to design or find other materials with excellent properties to satisfy the requirement of electronic devices.

MXene, a new large family of 2D materials, was exfoliated from ternary layered transition metal carbides and nitrides MAX $(M_{n+1}AX_{n},n=1-3)$ phases by etching the intercalated "A" layers, where M represents a transition metal, X is a carbon or nitrogen, and A is an element in groups IIIA or IVA [16]. The etching solution is usually hydrofluoric acid (HF) or a mixture of hydrochloric acid (HCl) and lithium fluoride (LiF) [10, 17, 18]. During etching, some functional groups such as $-O,-F,-OH$ are terminated on the surface of MXene, so the general formula of functionalized MXene is

$M_{n+1}X_nT_x$ (M = early transition metal, X = C or N, T = OH, O, F, n = 1-3). Over twenty types of MXene have been synthesized, while many more are theoretically predicted to exist [19]. MXenes have stimulated great enthusiasm in energy storage performances owing to the unique physical structures and high electronic conductivity [20, 21]. Zhang et al. [22] investigated the structural and electronic properties of MXene monolayer. They thought that the electronic structure of the planar forms of MXene depends on the arrangement of the functional groups.

Compared with graphene, MXenes are ideal 2D semiconductor materials with medium bandgap and high thermal stability [23]. It combines the metal conduction of transition metal carbides and the hydrophilicity of the —OH or —O functional groups, which makes it have great potentials in energy storage, adsorption, sensors and other fields [24]. Because of the existence of functional groups, MXenes are easy to introduce defects, such as atom vacancy [25]. The introduction of defects can affect the physical and chemical properties of materials [26-28]. Point defects [29], such as atomic vacancies, are unavoidable in MXenes because of their harsh preparation conditions [30]. Many materials have been investigated for defects, such as silicene, $MoS_2$ and $TiO_2$, all of which are examples of defects improving the practical applications of materials in the optoelectronic device field [31-33].

Similar to other MXenes, $Sc_2CF_2$ is a semiconductor with strong anisotropic carrier mobility and thermal conductivity. The electron mobility of $Sc_2CF_2$ in the zigzag direction is nearly fourfold of that of phosphorene in the armchair direction, and its thermal conductivity are higher than most low-dimensional metal and semiconductor materials [34-36]. Because of its excellent characteristics in electronic devices, $Sc_2CF_2$ has attracted wide attention in recent years.

At present, there are few reports on the effect of vacancy defects on the electronic structure and optical properties of $Sc_2CF_2$ MXene. Considering the importance of vacancy, we theoretically investigated the quantum capacitance (QC), electronic and optical properties of $Sc_2CF_2$ MXene with different atomic vacancies. We hope our results can provide the useful theoretical basis for experimental works.

## 2 | COMPUTATIONAL DETAIL

All calculations were performed using the density functional theory based on Vienna ab initio simulation package [37]. A $3 \times 3 \times 1$ of pristine $Sc_2CF_2$ MXene is constructed with 18 Sc atoms, 18 F atoms and 9 C atoms. $Sc_2CF_2$ MXene with vacancy defect is formed by the removal of one oxygen, fluorine, or scandium atom from the pristine $Sc_2CF_2$ MXene to highlight the influence of C-vacancy ($V_C$), F-vacancy ($V_F$), and Sc-vacancy ($V_{Sc}$), respectively. The three systems with vacancy are $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_C$, $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_F$, $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_{Sc}$, and presented in Figure 1. A plane wave base with an energy cutoff of 500 eV is used to extend the wave function [38]. The exchange-correlation functional adopts the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerh functional in the generalized gradient approximation GGA [39]. The pseudopotentials utilized the valence states of $3p4s3d$, $2s^22p^5$, and $2s^22p^2$ for Sc, F, and C, respectively [40]. The projector-augmented wave method was used to describe the interactions between the valence electrons and ionic cores [41, 42]. In order to simulate the isolated monolayers, a large vacuum space over $20\,\mathring{A}$ was used to avoid the interaction between the MXene layers. The van de Waals (vdW) interaction is considered with dispersion correction via the DFT-D3 method [43]. The magnetism is also considered in the calculation. The convergence threshold is $10^{-5}$ eV in energy and $0.01\,\text{eV}/\mathring{A}$ in force. The $5 \times 5 \times 1$ K point grid is adopted in structural optimization according to the Monkhorst Pack method. The density of states and optical properties are calculated with a denser K point grid of $13 \times 13 \times 1$ and $15 \times 15 \times 1$, respectively.

## 3 | RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### 3.1 | Structural stability

Vacancies are the most basic and common defects in solid materials. Vacancy formation energy (VFE, $E_{form}$) is to examine what defects are likely to form, and defined as the energy required to form a stable vacancy in the material. The VFEs can be obtained by the following equation:

$$
E_{\text{form}} = E_{\text{vac}} + E_{\text{atom}} - E_{\text{pristine}} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\text{pristine}}$ is the total energy of pristine $Sc_2CF_2$ monolayer, $E_{\text{atom}}$ is the total energy of an isolated atom, $E_{\text{vac}}$ is the total energy of defect-$Sc_2CF_2$ monolayer. The VFE of $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_C$, $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_F$, and $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_{Sc}$ monolayers are 9.97, 7.52, 11.64 eV, respectively. The smaller $E_{\text{form}}$ for $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_F$ system indicates that $V_F$ is energetically more favorable than $V_C$ and $V_{Sc}$. It is reported that the formation energies for C-vacancy, O-vacancy, and Ti-vacancy on $Ti_2CO_2$ monolayer are 5.31, 6.59, and 9.67 eV, respectively [44]. The VFE of $Sc_2CF_2$-$V_F$ system is slightly larger than that of $Ti_2CO_2$-$V_O$ system, which indicates that O-vacancy on $Ti_2CO_2$ monolayer is energetically more favorable than $V_F$ on $Sc_2CF_2$ monolayer when compared with the terminated groups on MXene.

The binding energy $E_b$ can be used to examine the energetic stability of pristine $Sc_2CF_2$ and defect-$Sc_2CF_2$ monolayers, and defined by the following equation:

![](./images/812482463279349761_1.jpg)

FIGURE 1 The structures of pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ (A), $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_\text{C}$ (B), $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_\text{F}$ (C), $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_\text{Sc}$ (D) from side and top view

$$
E_{\text{b}} = (E_{\text{vac}} - n_{\text{Sc}}E_{\text{Sc}} - n_{\text{C}}E_{\text{C}} - n_{\text{F}}E_{\text{F}})/(n_{\text{Sc}} + n_{\text{C}} + n_{\text{F}}) \tag{2}
$$

where $E_{\text{Sc}}$ ($E_{\text{C}}$, $E_{\text{F}}$) are the energies of an isolated atom Sc (C, F), respectively, while $n_{\text{Sc}}$ ($n_{\text{C}}$, $n_{\text{F}}$) are the number of the corresponding atom in unit cell. The more negative $E_{\text{b}}$, the more stable the structure. The binding energies of pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$, $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_\text{C}$, $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_\text{F}$, $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_\text{Sc}$ monolayers are $-6.39$, $-6.31$, $-6.36$, and $-6.28$ eV per atom, respectively. The lowest binding energy of pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ indicates pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ has the most stable structure. The introduction of vacancy does not improve the structural stability of $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ MXene. The differences of $E_{\text{b}}$ between the studied systems are very small. So the four systems are investigated systematically in the following.

Table 1 lists the bond lengths between two atoms near the $V_\text{C}$, $V_\text{F}$, and $V_\text{Sc}$ in these four systems. The introduction of Sc vacancy makes the F atom near Sc vacancy away from the vacancy, which results in the increase of F–F bond and the decrease of Sc–F bond when compared with the parameters of pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ MXene. The reason may be that the creation of Sc vacancy makes the charge redistribution, and results in the formation of stronger Sc–F near Sc vacancy. The creation of C vacancy makes more electron accumulate around C and Sc atoms near C vacancy, which results in the decrease of C–C bond and the increase of Sc–C bond. The similar analysis can be applied for the other systems.

## 3.5 | QC of the vacancy-doping $Sc_2CF_2$ monolayer

QC is an important character of nanomaterials in supercapacitor, which is a novel kind of electrochemical energy conversion and storage device. For the low-dimensional materials as electrodes, the differential QC can be obtained by the following equation:

$$
C_{\mathrm{diff}}=\frac{d \sigma}{d \phi_{\mathrm{G}}}=e^{2} \operatorname{DOS}(-V e) \tag{4}
$$

where $d\sigma$ and $d\phi_{\mathrm{G}}$ are the differentials of local charge density and local potential, respectively. The electrochemical potential shifts with $e\phi_{\mathrm{G}}$, and the excess charge density can be deduced by [48].

$$
\Delta Q=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} D(E)\left[f(E)-f\left(E-e \phi_{\mathrm{G}}\right)\right] d E \tag{5}
$$

where $D(E)$ represents the DOS of the studied system, $f(E)$ is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function, $E$ is the relative energy with reference to the Fermi level, and $e$ is the elementary electric charge. For 2D materials, $C_{\mathrm{diff}}$ can be obtained by the following equation [49]:

$$
C_{\mathrm{diff}}=e^{2} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} D(E) F_{\mathrm{T}}\left(E-e \phi_{\mathrm{G}}\right) d E \tag{6}
$$

where $F_{\mathrm{T}}(E)$ is the thermal broadening function and is obtained by [50]

$$
F_{\mathrm{T}}(E)=\left(4 \kappa_{\mathrm{B}} T\right)^{-1} \operatorname{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{E}{2 \kappa_{\mathrm{B}} T}\right) \tag{7}
$$

Here, $\kappa_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the Boltzmann's constant, and $T$ is 300 K. The storage charge on the MXene-based electrode's surface is obtained by

From Figure 8A, the maximum $C_{\text{diff}}$ of pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ at negative bias is $655\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$ at −0.43 V, while the introduction of $V_{\text{Sc}}$ improves the maximum $C_{\text{diff}}$ of $731\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$ at −0.37 V. At −0.03 V, $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{C}}$ has the $C_{\text{diff}}$ of $397\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$. At positive bias, pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ has little $C_{\text{diff}}$, while the introduction of vacancy improves the maximum $C_{\text{diff}}$ of $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ monolayer. The maximum $C_{\text{diff}}$ at positive bias is $192\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$ at 0.43 V for $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{Sc}}$, $493\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$ at 0.40 V for $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$, and $313\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$ at 0.57 V for $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{C}}$. It is noted that the introduction of vacancy also improves the $C_{\text{diff}}$ of the vacancy-doping systems at 0 V. The QC of pristine graphene is $9.9\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$ [52], which is much smaller than those of the four studied systems. From Figure 8A, $C_{\text{diff}}$ of $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$ monolayer in the positive bias is much larger than that in the negative range, which indicates that $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$ monolayer is a promising anode material.

From Figure 8B, the introduction of vacancy has significant effect on the Q value. The storage charge Q of $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$ monolayer is mainly stored at positive electrode potentials, indicating that $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$ is suitable for anode material, which is accordance with the analysis of $C_{\text{diff}}$. The Q values of the four studied systems increase with the increasing potential. Compared with pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ monolayer, the introduction of $V_{\text{Sc}}$ increases the Q value, with the maximum value of $44.29\ \mu\text{C/cm}^2$ for $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{Sc}}$, $128.53\ \mu\text{C/cm}^2$ for $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$, and $82.21\ \mu\text{C/cm}^2$ for $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{C}}$ at +0.6 V.

Integrated QC $C_{\text{int}}$ is also an important parameter. From Figure 8C, $C_{\text{int}}$ of $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$ monolayer in the positive range is generally larger than that in the negative range. This indicates that the system is more potential for anode materials, which is in good agreement with the analysis of $C_{\text{diff}}$ and Q as discussed above. $C_{\text{int}}$ of pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ and $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{Sc}}$ monolayers in negative bias is larger than that in positive bias, indicating that $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ and $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{Sc}}$ monolayers are suitable for cathode materials. $C_{\text{int}}$ at zero-voltage region are 0.13, 139.47, 338.85, and $290.85\ \mu\text{F/cm}^2$ for pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$, $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{Sc}}$, $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$, and $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{C}}$ monolayers, respectively. The introduction of vacancy drastically increases the $C_{\text{int}}$ of the vacancy-doping systems.

## 4 | CONCLUSION

In this paper, first-principles calculations were employed to investigate the QC, electronic structure and optical properties of pristine and vacancy-doped $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ monolayers. It was shown that pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ has the most stable structure because of the lowest binding energy among the studied systems. Pristine $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ is an indirect semiconductor, the introduction of vacancy results in the transition from semiconductor to metal. Defect energy level appears due to the doping vacancy. $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{C}}$ and $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$ were of nonmagnetic nature, while Sc-vacancy can induce high magnetic moment of $1.0\ \mu_{\text{B}}$, so $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{Sc}}$ is a magnetic semiconductor, and the magnetism is mainly from C-p, F-p and Sc-d states. The analysis of the optical property indicates that $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{C}}$, $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{F}}$, and $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$-$V_{\text{Sc}}$ monolayers have the strongest absorption and the highest reflectivity in the infrared region. The introduction of vacancy can make the system more sensitive to the infrared light. The analysis of charge transfer is in consistent with the analysis of bond length of the vacancy-doped systems in Section 3.1. QC of the vacancy-doping $\text{Sc}_2\text{CF}_2$ monolayer is further explored.
