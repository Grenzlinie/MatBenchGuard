# Tuning the Electronic Properties of $N@C_{60}$ Molecule: A Theoretical Study

Jing Huang$^{1,2}$, Qunxiang Li$^{2,*}$, and Jinlong Yang$^{2,*}$

$^{1}$ School of Materials and Chemical Engineering, Anhui University of Architecture, Hefei, Anhui 230022, China
$^{2}$ Hefei National Laboratory for Physical Sciences at Microscale, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei, Anhui 230026, China

We used first-principle calculations to explore the electronic and transport properties of $N@C_{60}$ molecule. The calculated results indicate that the N atom locates at either slight or significant off-center in $C_{60}$ cage under various different chemical environments. The localized N atomic magnetic moment in $N@C_{60}$ molecule is about $3.0\ \mu_{B}$, which is not sensitive to the chemical environment, such as the carrier doping, the external electric field, and the molecule-substrate interaction. On the other hand, the magnetism of $N@C_{60}$ can be effectively tuned by carrier doping and the cage chemical modification. The $C_{60}$ cage with delocalized $\pi$-electrons character demonstrates a significant shielding (about 80%) of the encapsulated N atom from the applied external electric field. We do not observe obvious transmission spin polarization in $N@C_{60}$ molecular junctions and the transmission spectra of two spin channels are similar near the Fermi level.

**Keywords:** Electronic Structure, Magnetic, Transport, Coupling, First-Principles.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

The N atom encapsulated fullerene ($N@C_{60}$) has attracted great experimental and theoretical research attention due to its unique electronic and magnetic properties. For example, several theoretical investigations have been performed to confirm the position for N $2p$ energy level in $N@C_{60}$ molecule,$^{1}$ determinate the N atomic positions in the derived $N@C_{60}$F and $N@C_{60}$F$_{2}$,$^{2}$ and explore the electronic transport properties of $N@C_{60}$ molecule inside carbon nanotubes.$^{3}$ It is well established that the encapsulated N atom at the center of $C_{60}$ cage has $S=3/2$ spin quartet quasi-atomic states. The $C_{60}$ cage shielding the N atom from the environment results in some excellent magnetic resonance properties,$^{4,5}$ such as long spin relaxation time. Thus, $N@C_{60}$ molecule has been proposed as a potential candidate for applications in biomedical sciences, quantum information processing,$^{6,7}$ and spin labeling. To determinate the charge and spin states of $N@C_{60}$ molecule, the spin excitations of $N@C_{60}$ connected to Pt electrodes$^{8}$ and the magnetic signature in a single $N@C_{60}$ connected to two Au electrods$^{9}$ were investigated with tunneling spectroscopy. It is clear that the $N@C_{60}$ molecular magnetism is maintained when fabricating the devices in these experimental investigations. Also, the spin properties of the encapsulated N atom can be tuned with chemical functionalization of $N@C_{60}$ molecule.$^{10,11}$

Recently, Liu et al.$^{11}$ synthesized and characterized pyrrolidine derivatives of $N@C_{60}$ including $N$-methyl-$N@C_{60}$ and $N$-methyl-2-pyrenyl-$N@C_{60}$. They concluded that the attachment of a pyrrolidine group to $C_{60}$ cage significantly lowers the photolytic stability of $N@C_{60}$ and observed the molecular spin loss. However, the mechanism to this molecular spin loss is not clear till now. In this paper, we perform a comprehensive investigation on the electronic and transport properties of $N@C_{60}$ molecule by using first-principles calculations. We find that the localized N atomic magnetic moment (MM is about $3.0\ \mu_{B}$) is not sensitive to the carrier doping, external transverse electric field, and the $N@C_{60}$-substrate interaction. However, the molecular magnetism of $N@C_{60}$ can be effective tuned by the carrier doping and the cage chemical modification. The $C_{60}$ cage with delocalized $\pi$-electrons character demonstrates a significant shielding of the encapsulated N atom from the applied external electric field. As expected, the calculated transmission spectra through $N@C_{60}$ molecule coupled to Au and Al electrodes show that two spin channels are similar near the Fermi level and the encapsulated magnetic N atom does not give obvious contribution to the spin-polarized transport.

## 2. COMPUTATIONAL METHOD

The geometrical optimization and electronic structures of the N@C₆₀ molecule are carried out based on the Spanish initiative for electronic simulations with thousands of atoms (SIESTA) code by spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT).¹² In our simulations, the Troullier-Martins pseudopotentials are used to describe the interactions between ionic cores and valence electrons. Double-zeta plus polarization basis sets are used for all atoms and the grid integration is defined by an energy cutoff of 150 Ry. The exchange and correlation functional was treated by the parametrization proposed by Ceperley-Alder local-density approximation.¹³ Actually, test calculations at generalized gradient approximation level with larger basis set and cutoff energy yield the very similar results. All molecule geometrical structures are fully self-consistently optimized until the forces are smaller than 0.02 eV/Å.

Electronic transport calculations are performed using the non-equilibrium Green's function (NEGF) combined with density functional theory (DFT) methods, implemented in ATK package,¹⁴,¹⁵ which has been successfully used to explain many experimental results.¹⁶ In ATK package, the transmission coefficients is calculated by

$$
T_{\sigma}(E)=Tr\left[\Gamma_{L}(E)G_{\sigma}(E)\Gamma_{R}(E)G_{\sigma}^{+}(E)\right]
$$

here, $\Gamma_{L,R}(E)$ presents the coupling matrix between the left (right) electrode with the scattering region, and $G_{\sigma}(E)$ stands for the spin-dependent spin-dependent retarded Green's function for the central scattering region at the energy $E$.

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### 3.1. Electronic Structures of N@C₆₀

To start the calculations, we examine the effects of encapsulated N atom on electronic structures of C₆₀. Figure 1(a) shows the optimized structure of N@C₆₀ molecule. The molecule has $I_{h}$ symmetry, and N atom locates at the center of the C₆₀ cage. The C–C bond lengths almost do not change (the 6-6 and 5-6 bond lengths is 1.40 and 1.45 Å, respectively) due to the present of N atom. The N@C₆₀ molecule has a spin-polarized ground state with the $3\ \mu_{B}$ MM, while C₆₀ possess spin-restricted ground states with the energy gap is about 1.64 eV. We find that the encapsulated N atom causes the lowering of the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO, $t_{1u}$) of C₆₀ and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO, $h_{u}$) by about ten meV. The energy separation between the HOMO and LUMO is slightly reduced from 1.64 eV (C₆₀) to 1.63 eV (N@C₆₀), which agrees well with the previous report.¹ The charge transfer from N atom to C₆₀ cage is almost zero according to the atomic Mulliken populations. The small charge transfer and the small change of the band gap indicate that the interaction between the encapsulated N atom and C₆₀ cage is weak and the encapsulated N atom has the quasi-atomic states in N@C₆₀. Figures 1(b) and 2(a) presents the spin density distribution and partial density of states of N@C₆₀ molecule, respectively. It is clear that the spin density localizes around the N atom, implying that N atom contribute to the $3\ \mu_{B}$ MM through the one-electron energy level coming from the $2p$ orbital which lies below the HOMO of N@C₆₀ molecule.

### 3.2. Carrier Doping

It is well established that the molecular magnetism of N@C₆₀ molecule can be effectively tuned by the carrier doping. Now we turn to investigate the electron and hole doped N@C₆₀ molecule. The adiabatic electron affinity (EA) and ionization energy (IE) is predicted to be 1.65 and 6.96 eV. As for the N@C₆₀ cation and anion, the molecular magnetic moment is predicted to be 4 and $2\ \mu_{B}$, the corresponding spin density distribution is shown in Figures 1(c)-(d), respectively. The most stable isomer of the N@C₆₀ anion is a triplet structure with the anti-ferromagnetic couple between N atom and C₆₀ cage. In contrary, N atom interacts ferromagnetically with C₆₀ cage in the N@C₆₀ cation. Note that the local magnetic moment of N atom does not change and fixes to be $3\ \mu_{B}$ regardless of either electron or hole doping. The calculated PDOS of N@C₆₀ anion is shown in Figure 2(b). Clearly, the PDOS feature is almost the same and the energy levels shift very slightly, compared to the neutral N@C₆₀ molecule.

### 3.3. Electric Field Shielding

In general, the neutral molecule will be polarized under the external electric field. The polarized ability can be quan- tified by the polarizability ($\alpha_{\perp}$), defined as $\alpha_{\perp}=p_{\perp}/E_{\text{out}}$. Here, $p_{\perp}$ and $E_{\text{out}}$ stands for the total induced dipole moment and the applied transverse electric field, respec- tively. In our calculations, we study the electric field shielding of N@C₆₀ molecule under the external electric field of 0.05 and 0.5 V/Å along $x$-axis direction. The calculated polarizabilities $\alpha_{\perp}$ of N@C₆₀ molecule for the different electric field are all about 10.55. It means that the polarizability is not sensitive to the applied transverse electric field. To describe the effective shielding from the $C_{60}$ cage to N atom under electric field, we define the shielding ($S$) as

$$
S=\frac{\alpha_{\perp}^{C_{60}}+\alpha_{\perp}^{N}-\alpha_{\perp}^{N @ C_{60}}}{\alpha_{\perp}^{N}}
$$

here, $\alpha_{\perp}^{C_{60}}$, $\alpha_{\perp}^{N}$, $\alpha_{\perp}^{N @ C_{60}}$ are the polarizability of the free $C_{60}$ cage, isolated N atom, and N@C₆₀ molecule, respec- tively. The calculated results indicate that the $C_{60}$ cage with delocalized $\pi$-electrons character demonstrates an effec- tive shielding with the order of 80% of the encapsulated N atom from the transverse external electric field. This nearly complete shielding was also observed in the coaxial hybrid double-walled nanotubes.¹⁷

The calculated PDOS of N@C₆₀ molecule with applying external electric field 0.5 V/Å are shown in Figure 2(c).

Theoretical results indicate that the shape of PDOS does not obviously change and the energy gap between the HOMO and LUMO decreases with the increase of trans- verse electric field. For example, the energy gap does not change obviously (change within 1.0 meV) under the elec- tric field of 0.05 V/Å, while the energy gap changed value is up to 110 meV when the electric field increases to be 0.5 V/Å. The correspond- ing molecular MM is $3\ \mu_{B}$, mainly contributed by the $2p$ orbital of N atom.

### 3.4. Cage Chemical Modification

As mentioned above, the encapsulated N atom does not obviously affect the electronic properties of the $C_{60}$ cage in N@C₆₀ molecule, it is generally accepted that the N@C₆₀ molecule has similar chemical reactivity to $C_{60}$ molecule. All functionalization reactions known for $C_{60}$ could in principle be transferred to N@C₆₀. However, only a few investigations on the cage chemical modification in N@C₆₀ molecule have been reported to date.¹⁰¹¹ For example, Liu et al.¹¹ investigated the photochemical stabil- ity of pyrrolidine derived N@C₆₀ molecule. They demon- strated that the attachment of a pyrrolidine group to $C_{60}$ cage reduces the molecular MM and significantly lowers the photolytic stability of N@C₆₀. To study the effect of functional groups on geometric and electronic properties of N@C₆₀, we examine two N@C₆₀ derivatives: N@C₆₀- C₂NH₅ and N@C₆₀-C₃NH₇, as shown in Figures 4(a) and (b), respectively. Theoretical results show that N atom obviously shifts toward to $C_{2}NH_{5}$ functional group and locates off-center in $C_{60}$ cage (away from the center about 0.9 Å), similar to other functional groups such as $CS_{2}$. ### 3.5. Substrate Effect
Understanding the adsorption and substrate-adsorbate interaction are the important prerequisite for the development of N@C60-based devices. There are several experimental and theoretical investigations about the adsorption of N@C₆₀ molecule on metallic and semiconducting surface. For instance, *ab initio* total energy DFT calculations and photoelectron spectra simulations could be used for diagnostics of the adsorption position of N@C₆₀ molecule on Si(100) surface.¹⁸ Here, we explore the adsorption behavior of N@C₆₀ on Au(100) and Al(100) surfaces modeled with a $(4\times4)$ supercell (the corresponding optimized lattice constant is about $11.54\ \mathring{A}$ and $11.45\ \mathring{A}$, respectively). In our calculations, five adsorption orientations including the 6-member ring, 5-member ring, 6-6 double bond, 5-6 single bond, and edge carbon atom of N@C₆₀ molecule adsorbs on three possible adsorption sites (the atop, bridge and hollow sites) are all examined. The relaxed results indicate that the most energetic favorable configuration is N@C₆₀ molecule lying on Au(100) and Al(100) surfaces with its 6-member ring. The vertical distance between the bottom 6-member ring and the top surface layer is about 1.95 and $2.05\ \mathring{A}$ for Al(100) and Au(100) surface, respectively. Note that the exact adsorption site is slightly different for two examined metallic surfaces. N@C₆₀ molecule adsorbs exactly on the hollow site of Al(100) surface, while it slightly shifts from the hollow site towards to the bridge site for Au(100) surface. The charge transferring from Au(100) and Al(100) surfaces to N@C₆₀ molecule is about 0.09 and 0.11 $e$, respectively. These values are close to the previous theoretical predictions of $C_{60}$ adsorbed on Au(111) surface.¹⁹ Due to the present of the substrate and the molecule-surface interaction, the encapsulated N atom does not locate at the center of $C_{60}$ cage. The N atom moves towards to the metallic surface and the off-center distance is about $0.8\ \mathring{A}$ for two adsorption systems. Figure 2(f) shows the calculated PDOS of N@C₆₀ adsorption on Au(100) surface. The PDOS peaks become broad due to the interaction between N@C₆₀ cage and the metallic surface. On the other hand, the N atom still retains the quasi-atom states with $3\ \mu_B$ atomic MM.

### 3.6. Transport Property
Very recently, magnetic single-molecule transistor based on N@C₆₀ connected to Pt and Au electrodes have been investigated. Grose et al.⁸ observed that the N@C₆₀ device exhibits magnetic character, indicating that the molecular magnetism is not destroyed during device fabrication. Roch et al.⁹ studied the cotunneling regime and found that the magnetic signatures in single N@C₆₀ devices originate from an antiferromagnetic exchange interaction between the $C_{60}$ spin and the N spin. Here, we present the transport properties of N@C₆₀ sandwiched between the

Au(100) or Al(100) electrodes. Figure 5(a) displays the schematic illustration of the molecular junction including three parts: left electrode, scattering region, and right elec- trode. The scattering region includes the $N@C_{60}$ molecule and several surface layers of left and right electrodes. To model the STM tip in experiments, we choose one Au/Al atom adsorbed on the right electrode surface. Based on the fully relaxed molecular junctions, the transmis- sion spectra as a function of the electron injecting energy $(E-E_{F})$ are calculated, and shown in the left panel of Figure 5(b). Clearly, the transmission spectra are similar for two spin channels (the spin-up and spin-down states) near the Fermi level. This observation implies that the localized N magnetism does not give its contribution to the conductivity at the small bias voltage due to the effec- tive shielding of $C_{60}$ cage with delocalized $\pi$ -electrons character from the applied external electric field. The right panel of Figure 5(b) shows the spatial distribution of three molecular projected self-consistent Hamiltonian (MPSH) molecular orbitals including the effect of left and right electrodes. It is clear that the significant transmission peaks, labeled with 1 and 2 in Figure 5(b), contributed by the delocalized molecular orbitals. The transmission valley (labeled with 3) originates from the localized molecular orbital, as shown in Figure 5(b). Note that total MM of $N @ C_{60}$ molecule in two examined junctions is about $3 \mu_{B}$ . It means that the molecular magnetism is not quenched in the present of metallic Au/Al electrodes. This findingagrees well with the recently experimental results. $^{8,9}$

## 4. CONCLUSION
In summary, the electronic and transport properties of $N @ C_{60}$ molecule are investigated by using first-principles calculations. The calculated results indicate that the posi- tion of N atom in $N @ C_{60}$ depends on the external environ ment, while the localized N atomic MM (about $3 \mu_{B}$ ) is not sensitive to the carrier doping, the applied transverse electric field, and the $N @ C_{60}$ -substrate interaction. The molecular magnetism of $N @ C_{60}$ can be effectively con trolled via various methods, especially by the cage chem- ical modification. Based on elastic transport calculations of the $N @ C_{60}$ molecule sandwiched between Au and Al electrodes, we do not observe obvious transmission spinpolarization effect due to the effective shielding of $C_{60}$  cage from the applied external electric field.
