## ABSTRACT

The magnetism of GaN (100) and (101) surfaces containing neutral intrinsic defects has been investigated using *ab initio* calculations. Ideal Ga-ended GaN (100) surfaces and (101) surfaces are nonmagnetic. After surface relaxation, an N-ended GaN (100) surface transforms to a Ga-end, which presents local magnetic moments being ferromagnetically coupled. Neutral gallium vacancies at the (100) surface bring about large magnetic moments, which are ferromagnetically coupled. The spin-polarization of 2p electrons of nitrogen atoms is responsible for the induced magnetic moments. Neutral nitrogen vacancies at the (101) surface induce a zero magnetic moment. Neutral gallium vacancies at the (101) surface might lead to an antiferromagnetic state.

## 1. Introduction

Since the theoretical prediction of ferromagnetism (FM) in Mn-doped GaN by Dietl et al. in 2000 [1], many groups have reported magnetism in GaN doped with transition metals (TM) [2–11]. While, many experimental works have indicated that the unexpected room-temperature FM in thin films or nanograins of undoped oxides such as $HfO_2$, $TiO_2$ and ZnO comes from oxygen vacancies [12–17]. In contrast, first-principles calculations demonstrate that FM originates from the cation vacancies in undoped $SnO_2$, $HfO_2$, $ZrO_2$, ZnO, and $TiO_2$ [18–22]. GaN is a widely-used semiconductor material. Over the past years, it has attracted considerable attention because of its interesting physical properties and application as a basic material for optoelectronic devices working in the blue and ultraviolet spectral region. GaN has two different crystal structures: the wurtzite structure (space group P63mc) and the cubic structure (space group F4-3m). According to Manhadevan et al. and Dev et al.'s calculations on cubic structure GaN [23,24], and Larson et al.'s study on wurtzite GaN [25], Ga vacancies could introduce magnetic moments which lie on the neighbouring N atoms that are spin-polarized due to Hund's rule. Their GGA calculations were based on the bulk GaN model. In 2008, Madhu et al. reported room-temperature FM observed in undoped wurtzite GaN semiconductor nanoparticles [26]. They suggest that the observed ferromagnetism is due to the intrinsic defects confined to the surface of the nanoparticles, while the core of the particles remains diamagnetic. They also suggest that Ga vacancies in GaN give rise to a ferromagnetic ground state. GaN with cubic structure is usually in metastable state, which has a very important application in laser pumps. In the present paper, we perform *ab initio* calculations on the magnetic properties of cubic structure GaN (100) and (101) surfaces containing $V_{Ga}^0$ and $V_{N}^0$ vacancies. Our results indicate that neutral gallium vacancies at a Ga-ended GaN (100) surface lead to a ferromagnetic stabilized state of the system. The existence of an N-ended GaN (100) surface is impossible, as it switches to a Ga-end (100) surface after surface relaxation, which exhibits ferromagnetism. Neutral gallium vacancies in the (101) surface could result in an antiferromagnetic state. The observed room-temperature FM of GaN with cubic structure originates from the neutral gallium vacancies at the surface of nanograins and/or some new surface states due to the surface reconstruction.

The PAW potentials were employed, and the GGA PW91 approximation was used to describe the exchange correlation energy. ### 3. Results

Fig. 2 displays the total DOS of an ideal Ga-ended GaN (100) surface. No spin-polarization emerges around the Fermi energy level. The spin-up and spin-down DOS are symmetrical, indicating the nonmagnetic state of the system. It is well known that GaN is a wide band-gap semiconductor, with a band-gap of about 3.4 eV [31,32]. The present calculations show that the cubic structure GaN (100) surface with a Ga-end has a band-gap of about 0.6 eV, which is smaller than the value (3.4 eV) of ideal bulk GaN.

For the (100) surface with $11.11\% V_{\text{Ga}}^0$ vacancies, we investigated four different distributions of two isolated neutral gallium vacancies at the surface using the GGA calculation. The four distributions are removing Ga1 and Ga2, Ga1 and Ga3, Ga1 and G4, Ga1 and Ga5 from the surface, respectively, (as shown in Fig. 1(a)). Comparison of the total energies indicates that when Ga1 and Ga5 are removed from the surface, the system is energetically favourable. Fig. 3(a) shows us the corresponding spin-resolved total DOS and partial DOS projected onto the s, p orbitals of N atoms and s, p, d orbitals of Ga atoms of the GaN (100) surface with Ga1 and Ga5 removed. We can see that with $11.11\% V_{\text{Ga}}^0$ vacancies, the system exhibits obvious spin-split at the Fermi level, which indicates the existence of magnetic moments. The presence of the neutral Ga vacancies results in a loss of donor charge, leading to 2p holes and a redistribution of the electronic energy bands of the system. Although the s orbitals of N atoms and the s, p, d orbitals of Ga atoms also exhibit spin-polarization, their contribution to the spin-split of the whole system is very weak. From the total DOS of a GaN (100) surface with $11.11\% V_{\text{Ga}}^0$ vacancies in Fig. 3(a), it seems that the system has no obvious band-gap. We also performed LDA calculations on the above four structures of a GaN (100) surface with two isolated neutral Ga vacancies, and the calculated results also indicate that when Ga1 and Ga5 were removed from the surface (see Fig. 1(a)), the system was energetically favourable. It is clear that the system has an obviously narrow band-gap from the LDA calculation, which is different from the result obtained by the GGA calculation. It is well known that GaN is a typical band-gap semiconductor, with a band-gap of about 3.4 eV. We ascribe the reason for the disappearance and narrowing of band-gap (by GGA and LDA calculations) to the coupling of the defect states at the GaN (100) surface. The introduction of $V_{Ga}^{0}$ vacancies at the surface results in impurity bands (IB), which inevitably locate at the wide band-gap between the conduction band and valence band. For the GGA calculation, these impurity states are strongly coupled, and cram the band-gap, leading to its disappearance (see Fig. 3(a)). When calculated by LDA, the impurity states are also coupled, which results in the narrowing of the band-gap seen from the DOS (as shown in Fig. 3(b)). Similarly, according to Larson et al.'s [25] GGA calculation on Mn doped wurtzite GaN, the band-gap of GaN disappears with the co-existence of N vacancy and Mn doping. In principle, LDA+$U$ calculation is needed as GGA/LDA may underestimate the Coulomb repulsion and tend to over localize the charge density. To our knowledge, there are neither LDA + $U$ calculations on the magnetic properties of pure or doped GaN nor reports on appropriate $U$ values for the GaN system. Fortunately, the accuracy of our results by GGA/LDA will not affect the coupling type (ferromagnetic or antiferromagnetic) between the vacancies, although it may affect the magnitude of magnetic moments in the system. This has been proved in our previous work, in which we calculated the magnetic properties of MgO containing intrinsic defects, by GGA, LDA and LDA+$U$ together [33]. From Fig. 3 we can see that LDA and GGA calculations present the same results about the origination of local magnetic moments: the spin-polarization of N 2p electrons. We studied the magnetic coupling between the two isolated neutral Ga vacancies by GGA and LDA calculations. The relative energies between ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic states ($\Delta E = E_{AFM} - E_{FM}$) are calculated. The results show that the total energy of the ferromagnetic alignment state is 5.4 eV (by GGA) or 4.96 eV (by LDA), lower than the corresponding value of the antiferromagnetic alignment state. Accordingly, it can be concluded that the ferromagnetic state might be energetically favourable for GaN (100) surfaces with 11.11% $V_{Ga}^{0}$ vacancies. Furthermore, our GGA and LDA calculations show that the GaN (100) surface with 11.11% $V_{Ga}^{0}$ vacancies (removing Ga1 and Ga5, see Fig. 1(a)) exhibits a magnetic moment of $2.77\mu_{B}$ (by GGA) or $1.17\mu_{B}$ (by LDA).

As the cubic structure GaN (100) surface consists of a stacking of alternating pure Ga and N planes, it is also necessary to investigate the magnetic properties of a GaN (100) surface which is completely N-ended. However, the constructed N-ended GaN (100) surface transforms into a Ga-ended GaN (100) surface after the surface relaxation (see Fig. 1(b)). It implies that the N-ended GaN (100) surface is not stable enough to exist, and the Ga-ended GaN (100) is the stable structure for a GaN (100) surface. This is similar to Neugebauer et al.'s report that the most stable GaN surface is Ga terminated [34]. We performed ferromagnetic calculations on the Ga-ended GaN (100) surface which is relaxed from the constructed N-ended GaN (100) surface. The spin-resolved total DOS and partial DOS of N atoms by GGA calculation are displayed in Fig. 4. Meticulous investigation shows that the spin-up DOS and spin-down DOS are not completely symmetrical in the region around the Fermi level, indicating the existence of small local moments in the system. Our ferromagnetic calculation shows that the system presents a magnetic moment of $0.3\mu_{B}$, which is almost exclusively from the spin moments of 2p electrons of the N atoms. The process of the surface reconstruction changes the distribution of the energy bands and electronic state of the system, resulting in a new surface state which is in charge of the occurrence of local magnetic moments. Fig. 5(a) shows the total DOS of an ideal GaN (101) surface which is ended with both nitrogen and gallium atoms. The spin-up DOS and spin-down DOS are completely symmetrical, indicating that the system is non-magnetic. The obtained band-gap is 1.1 eV, which is smaller than that of bulk GaN with cubic structure (3.4 eV). The magnetism of a GaN (101) surface with neutral N vacancies or Ga vacancies is also investigated. We introduced a concentration of 12.5% $V_N^0$ vacancies (removing N1 and N2, as shown in Fig. 1(c)) in a GaN (101) surface. The results show that a GaN (100) surface with 12.5% $V_N^0$ vacancies exhibits zero magnetic moment. The obtained total DOS is displayed in Fig. 5(b). We can see that the spin-up DOS and the spin-down DOS are completely symmetrical, indicating the nonmagnetic state of the system.

We also performed spin-polarized GGA calculations on the magnetic properties of a GaN (101) surface containing two isolated neutral Ga vacancies, which lead to a concentration of 12.5% $V_{Ga}^0$ vacancies at the (101) surface. We considered two distributions: removing Ga1 and Ga2 or Ga1 and Ga3 (see in Fig. 1(c)). The total energies obtained by the GGA calculation show that when Ga1 and Ga2 are removed, the system is energetically favourable. We investigated the effect of the magnetic coupling between the two vacancies, and found that the antiferromagnetic alignment state is 1.8 eV lower than the ferromagnetic alignment state. Our spin-resolved GGA calculation also shows that, due to the presence of $V_{Ga}^0$ vacancies (removing Ga1 and Ga2, see Fig. 1(c)), the N atoms of the system exhibit spin-polarization, which brings about local magnetic moments. N1 and N2 (see Fig. 1(c)) have magnetic moments of $0.622\mu_B$ and $-0.622\mu_B$, which are much larger than the magnetic moments of the other N atoms. It is clear that N1 and N2 are antiferromagnetically coupled. The total magnetic moments of the system is zero. Accordingly, a GaN (101) surface with 12.5% $V_{Ga}^0$ vacancies (removing Ga1 and Ga2, as shown in Fig. 1(c)) is antiferromagnetic.

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, the magnetism of GaN (100) and (101) surfaces containing neutral point defects has been studied, using ab inito calculations within the framework of density functional theory. Ideal Ga-ended GaN (100) and (101) surfaces are found to be nonmagnetic. A Ga-ended GaN (100) surface relaxed from the constructed N-ended GaN (100) surface exhibits ferromagnetism. Neutral gallium vacancies at a Ga-ended GaN (100) surface could induce large magnetic moments, leading to a ferromagnetically coupled state of the system. The spin-polarization of 2p electrons of nitrogen atoms is responsible for the induced magnetic moments. Neutral nitrogen vacancies at the (101) surface bring about a zero magnetic moment. Neutral gallium vacancies in the (101) surfaces might lead to an antiferromagnetic state of the system. We conclude that the room-temperature ferromagnetism of GaN with cubic structure originates from the neutral gallium vacancies at the surface of nanograins and/or some new surface states due to the surface reconstruction.
