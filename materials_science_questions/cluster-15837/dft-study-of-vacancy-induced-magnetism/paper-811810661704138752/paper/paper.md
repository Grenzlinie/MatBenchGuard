# A Density Functional Theory Study of Ferromagnetism in GaN:Gd

Lei Liu$^{1,2}$, Peter Y. Yu$^{1,2}$, Zhixun Ma$^{1}$, and Samuel S. Mao$^{1}$

$^{1}$Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Berkeley, CA 94720
$^{2}$Department of Physics, University of California, Berkeley, CA 94720

**Abstract.** First principle calculations of the electronic structure and magnetic interaction of GaN:Gd have shown that the ferromagnetic $p$-$d$ coupling is over two orders of magnitude larger than the $s$-$d$ exchange coupling. The experimental room temperature ferromagnetism in GaN:Gd are explained by the interaction of Gd $4f$ spins via $p$-$d$ coupling involving localized holes introduced by intrinsic defects such as Ga vacancies.

**Keywords:** Density-functional theory, Ferromagnetism, GaN, Gd, Vacancies.
**PACS:** 75.50Pp, 71.20.Eh, 71.55.Eq, 71.70.-d

## INTRODUCTION

Group III–V diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMSs) have captured much attention since ferromagnetism was reported in (Ga,Mn)As in $1996^{1}$. Free holes introduced by Mn acceptors in GaAs are believed to mediate the FM exchange between the magnetic dopants$^{2,3}$. However, this does not apply to the recently discovered GaN:Gd system$^{4-10}$ where Gd is an isoelectronic dopant. Dhar *et al.*$^{6}$ found room-temperature ferromagnetism in Gd-doped GaN and the average magnetic moment of Gd in their GaN:Gd layers were extremely large, reaching values of 4000 $\mu_{\text{B}}$, which is 500 times higher than the atomic Gd moment.$^{6}$ The microscopic nature of ferromagnetism in the Gd doped GaN has not been elucidated in spite of much theoretical work. Dhar *et al.*$^{6}$ have ruled out direct, double, or super-exchange between Gd atoms since the average Gd-Gd distance is too large. Since their samples are highly resistive, they also eliminated free-carrier mediated RKKY-type of long range coupling.$^{6}$ Dalpian *et al.* $^{11}$ showed that FM can exist in n-type GaN:Gd samples due to coupling between the Gd $f$ and the host $s$ states. We have recently presented a new first-principle calculation of the electronic and magnetic structure of GaN:Gd including intrinsic defects such as vacancies. In the present paper we have explored the role of other defects, such as oxygen donors, on the FM phases of GaN:Gd.

The electronic structures of GaN:Gd systems are calculated using a spin-polarized DFT using the Full-potential Linearized Augmented Plane Wave (FLAPW) method as implemented by the software Wien2K.$^{12}$ The GGA within the PBE (Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof) scheme$^{13}$ is used to obtain the exchange-correlation potential. The LDA+U method$^{14,15}$ is employed to calculate the onsite Coulomb correlation for the Gd $4f$ electrons, where the Coulomb repulsion energy U and the exchange energy $J$ are chosen to be 6.7 and 0.7 eV respectively$^{13,14}$. All our model structures have been optimized with the projector-augmented-wave (PAW)$^{16}$ calculations which approach the energy minimum of the LAPW results quite well.

## RESULTS

The band structures of GdGa₇N₈ calculated in the FM configuration have been reported already elsewhere so they will not be repeated. The spin-up (majority spin) and spin-down (minority spin) branches of the G,d $4f$ orbitals are found to be separated from the valance band maximum (VBM) and conduction band minimum (CBM) by about 5 eV. As a result, the coupling between the $4f$ orbitals and free carriers, whether n- or p-type, should be weak. Unlike other DMS systems, Gd in GaN has both partly filled $5d$ shell and $4f$ shell both of which can contribute to the magnetic moment of Gd. In GaN matrix, the fivefold-degenerate $5d$ orbitals of Gd will be split into two $\text{e}_{\text{g}}$ orbitals and three $\text{t}_{2\text{g}}$ orbitals by the tetrahedral crystal field from the surrounding N anions. The “d-band” DOS are significant only at about 2.5 eV or more above the CBM. As the lowest conduction band of GaN is mainly s-like any $s$-$d$ exchange coupling should be weak as long as the Gd local environment has $T_{d}$ symmetry. In contrast to the CBM, the Gd $5d$ orbitals with $\text{t}_{2\text{g}}$ symmetry occur just around the p-like

VBM of GaN. The $p$-$d$ hybridization should be very strong just as in the case of the Mn-doped GaAs.
Surprisingly, we found the energy difference $\Delta$E<sub>FM-AFM</sub> between FM and antiferromagnetic (AFM) configurations of our GdGa₇N₈ model structure to be essentially zero. We, therefore, conclude that GaN:Gd would be paramagnetic without defects in agreement with the experimental results.⁸

In the present study, we have considered both N vacancies (V<sub>N</sub>) and Ga vacancies (V<sub>Ga</sub>) and oxygen doping on FM in GaN:Gd. For V<sub>N</sub>, the removal of one N atom introduces three conduction electrons and causes the Fermi level to shift well above the VBM.
Nevertheless, the energy difference $\Delta$E<sub>FM-AFM</sub> equals only 3.9 meV/Gd atom suggesting that $s$-$d$ exchange is still rather weak even when allowed. Our results, therefore, suggest that n-type carriers will not contribute to the FM observed in GaN:Gd. This result is confirmed by recent experimental observation that n-type cubic GaN films exhibit paramagnetic-like behaviors¹⁰ rather than FM one. The band structure of the supercell: GdGa₆N₈ containing a V<sub>Ga</sub> suggests that it is strongly FM. The calculated energy difference $\Delta$E<sub>FM-AFM</sub> is equal to -697.1 meV/Gd. In this case each missing Ga atom contributes three holes to the sample.
The DOS of the $d$ states lying in the valence band near the Fermi level shows noticeable difference between the spin-up and spin-down states indicating a strong FM $p$-$d$ exchange coupling. Compared with the magnitude of the $s$-$d$ coupling in GdGa₇N₇, the $p$-$d$ exchange in GdGa₆N₈ is almost 180 times stronger.
Moreover, the calculated total magnetic moment per Gd atom for GdGa₆N₈ is 10 $\mu_\text{B}$ which is equal to the sum of the magnetic moments of the Gd $f$ orbitals plus that of the holes introduced by the vacancies. Recent calculation performed by another group¹⁷ has shown that the holes are strongly spin-polarized as they are highly localized on the N atoms neighboring the V<sub>Ga</sub>.
It is interesting to note that while V<sub>Ga</sub> has been shown to be unstable in GaN due to self-compensating, they are stabilized by the Gd ions in GaN as a result of tensile strain introduced by Gd<sub>Ga</sub>. This "synergism" between V<sub>Ga</sub> and Gd<sub>Ga</sub> is the underpinning of the strong FM in GaN:Gd. We have found that this strong FM persists even for model structures with multiple defects. For examples, we have studied supercell containing one to two oxygen O<sub>N</sub> donors. In each case we found the material remain strongly FM although the total moment per Gd atom is reduced by the number of O<sub>N</sub>. In GdGa₆N₆O₂, where two nitrogen atoms are replaced by O<sub>N</sub> we found that the total moment per Gd atom is reduced to 8 $\mu_\text{B}$ but E<sub>FM-AFM</sub> remains at a rather large value of -159.5 meV. The reported magnitude of the effective colossal magnetic moment per Gd atom suggests that the density of holes in real samples can be orders of magnitude higher than that of the Gd atoms. We note that the strong localization of defect states leading to spontaneous spin polarization occurs not only in GaN but also in other nitrides such as BN¹⁷. That samples of GaN exhibiting strong FM contains a high concentration of holes and yet are found to be insulating can be explained by the fact that the high density of holes introduced by the vacancies are strongly localized.

In conclusion, we found that the FM $p$-$d$ exchange coupling in GaN:Gd is over two orders of magnitude stronger than the $s$-$d$ exchange. Hence, holes are more effective than electrons in contributing to the observed large magnetic moment of Gd ions.
Intrinsic defects, such as Ga vacancies, are effective sources of these holes.
