## ABSTRACT

Determining the atomic origin of magnetism in non-magnetic elements doped dilute magnetic semiconductors (DMS) is a key issue to understand the room temperature ferromagnetism. Here, using electron magnetic circular dichroism (EMCD), an element-selectivity and high spatial resolution technique, we provide unambiguous experimental evidence: spin polarization of carbon atoms contributes to the local magnetic moments of Al-doped 4H-SiC DMS. The result is further confirmed by first-principle calculations.

---

## 1. Introduction

Transition-metal (TM) doped dilute magnetic semiconductors (DMSs) have been extensively studied due to their potential applications in spintronics. However, despite considerable theoretical and experimental efforts, the origin of ferromagnetism observed in DMS is still under debate [1,2]. The ferromagnetic property may arise from the doped magnetic clusters or secondary phases [3,4], which are extrinsic magnetic behaviors and undesirable for practical applications. A possible way to avoid the problem related to magnetic precipitate is to dope semiconductors or oxides with nonmagnetic elements instead of magnetic TM. Following this idea, room temperature ferromagnetism has been demonstrated in C-doped ZnO and Cu-doped GaN [5,6], etc.

It is well known that localized magnetic moments and the coupling between them are two indispensable factors to induce long-range spin ordering in DMS. For transition metal doped DMS system, the local magnetic moment is provided mainly by transition metal element, and the debate of different ferromagnetic theories focus on the determination of the coupling media between local magnetic moments [7-9]. There is even no $d$ and $f$ orbitals in nonmagnetic elements doped DMS, the existing ferromagnetic theories cannot be applied [10,11]. Different from transition metal doped DMS, there is no clear evidence that which atom can produce localized magnetic moments in nonmagnetic elements doped DMS. Thus, an experimental technique that allows the selective observation on magnetic polarization of each element should be used. On the other hand, the "oldest" while the "most important" question still exists for nanostructure or specimen in powder form. Magnetization data obtained from conventional techniques may include signals originating from trivial sources (such as magnetic impurities). Thus an experimental technique with high spatial resolution is essential, which can establish a clear and direct connection between the microstructures and the magnetic behaviors. That is, element selectivity and high spatial resolution are both essential in the study of the origination of local magnetic moments for powder form nonmagnetic elements doped DMS system.

The first direct observation of ferromagnetic spin polarization in carbon atoms was presented by means of electron magnetic chiral dichroism (EMCD) technique. First-principle simulations are performed to analyze the electron energy loss (EEL) spectroscopic character. EMCD, performed in transmission electron microscopy (TEM), is the difference in core- level EEL spectra collected at two opposite chirality conditions [14]. It allows the detection of the magnetic moments of a particular element through sensitivity to the difference between the up- and down- spin densities around the Fermi level.

## 2. Experiment and calculation method

The Al-doped 4H-SiC specimen were used to perform the TEM experiments, the details of the specimen preparation and the magnetic properties of the sample were reported in Ref. [12]. The dopant concentration of aluminum is determined as ~0.75 at% by inductively coupled plasma-atomic emission spectrometry (ICP- AES). The microstructures of the samples were examined using various TEM (Tecnai F20 ST) -related techniques. The EMCD measurements were performed using a Gatan imaging filtering system attached to the same microscope with an energy resolution of ~1.0 eV. Spin polarized density functional theory calculations were carried out with the CASTEP plane wave pseudopotential code. The exchange and correlation effects were treated with the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) in Perdew-Burke- Ernzerhof scheme (PBE) [15,16]. The electron wave functions were expanded in plane waves up to a cutoff energy of 400 eV and the k-space integrations were done by the Monkhorst-Pack grid with $2 \times 2 \times 2$ k-points in the Brillouin zone. The geometry optimization used the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shannon (BFGS) algorithm with convergence to $2 \times 10^{-5}$ eV/atom, $0.05\ \text{eV}/\text{\AA}$ for the Hell- mann-Feynman force on each atom and a stress of 0.1 GPa. Both the volume and atomic position have been relaxed. A 192-atom $4 \times 3 \times 2$ supercell was used with one Si atom replaced by Al atom, corresponding to the dopant concentration of ~1.04%, which was consistent with the experimental concentration. Compared with the perfect Al doped 4H-SiC structure, when Si vacancy ($V_{\text{Si}}$) and C vacancy ($V_{\text{C}}$) were introduced, C-Si bonds were shrunk while C-Al bonds were lengthened around the vacancy after the optimization.

## 3. Results and discussions

The typical morphology of the Al-doped 4H-SiC is shown in Fig. 1(a), which is a typical single crystalline slice. Selected area electron diffraction pattern (Fig. 1(b)) reveals its hexagonal struc- ture, which is further confirmed by the corresponding high resolution images shown in Fig. 1(c). The local chemical composi- tions of the samples are characterized by energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) using a ~1 nm electron probe. Fig. 1(d) reveals the existence of Si, C, and Al in the Al-doped 4H-SiC specimen. The doping concentration of Al is estimated as 0.73 at% by the EDS results, which is consistent with the macroscopic ICP-AES results. EDS experiments have been performed on several different loca- tions in the same slice with similar chemical compositions identified, which suggests uniform distribution of the dopant ions. No clustering of the Al is observed in the samples.

EMCD experiments were performed at two chiral positions named as "+" and "−" position in the diffraction pattern [17]. They are equivalent to the result generated by the left and the right circular light in X-ray magnetic circular dichroism (XMCD). Within the sensitivity limit no EMCD magnetic signal could be recorded for $Si-L_{2,3}$ (not shown here), it is reasonable because there is no $d$ electrons for Si atoms. However, a clearly visible magnetic signal was observed at the C-K edge. The EMCD signal measured at C-K edge is shown in Fig. 2(b), which is the difference spectra calculated by subtracting the corresponding "−" EEL spectra from the "+" EEL spectra (Fig. 2(a)). For DMS material, the EMCD signal at the nonmagnetic element C-K edge is reported here for the first time. This implies that the carbon atoms in Al- doped 4H-SiC (Fig. 2(a)) are spin polarized. We performed EMCD experiments at the C-K edge on several locations and consistent results are obtained. EMCD is an element-selective technique and atoms other than C do not influence the experimental results, thus we conclude definitely that the carbon atoms have large contribu- tion to the local magnetic moment of Al doped 4H-SiC. As a comparison, the EMCD experiments on C-K edge of prestine SiC were also performed, the results were shown in Fig. 2(c) and (d). From which we can see the spectra difference collected at "+" and "−" chirality is within the range of the noise, not providing any information of magnetism. More interesting, when we collected the large quantity of EEL spectra during the experiment, there is a small pre-edge in some spectra taken from some specimen region, indicated as inset in Fig. 2(a) (the small pre-edge was indexed by a small red arrow). While no pre-edge presented in many of the spectra. The main difference between the fine structure of C-K edge for Al doped SiC and prestine SiC sample is the onset of the edge. Onset for Al doped SiC sample is a little higher than that for prestine SiC sample (Fig. 2(c)), implying some states may exists on the onset of C-K edge for Al doped SiC sample. The difference would be more obvious when compare the inset of Fig. 2(a) with that of Fig. 2(c). To shed more light on the observed EMCD and EELS results, the first-principle calculations have to be performed.

Fig. 3(a) shows the calculated density of states for Al doped 4H- SiC (the Al atom was substituted at Si site and the doping concentration is ~1%). The Al dopant, Si and C atoms do not induce any spin-polarization. When we reset the supercell con- taining one Al and one $V_{\text{Si}}$ or $V_{\text{C}}$ during the calculations, the ferromagnetism is observed for Al and $V_{\text{Si}}$ ($V_{\text{C}}$) codoped 4H-SiC (the defect concentration of ~1%, note that this structure is studied only for obtaining the relationship between magnetism and defects and shall not be considered to represent a realistic distribution of defects). The results are shown in Fig. 3(b) and (c), respectively. The spin polarization mainly comes from C atoms while not from the dopant Al atoms for Al and $V_{\text{Si}}$ codoped 4H-SiC. The results are consistent with literatures [18] and also consistent with our experimental results (the EMCD signals on C-K edge are observed). From Fig. 3(c), for Al and $V_{\text{C}}$ codoped 4H-SiC, Si-sp, C-p, Al-p states all have contributions to the spin imbalance and thus the magnetism. So theoretically, there should exist MCD signal for Si-K edge and Al-K edge. However, experimentally Si-K edge locates at ~1839 eV and Al-K edge locates at ~1560 eV. When the EELS spectra are collected at higher energy loss (near 1000 eV is commonly accepted as "very high loss"), the signal-to-noise problem will be more and more significant. XMCD would be more appropriate to perform such kinds of MCD measurements. The saturation magnetic moment observed in this sample was $\sim 0.05\ \text{emu/g}$ (that is $\sim 5.39 \times 10^{18}\ \mu \text{B/g}$) [12]. Theoretically, the calculated net atomic magnetic moment is $\sim 0.05\ \mu \text{B/supercell}$, from which we can calculated the magnetic moment of the sample is $\sim 0.07\ \text{emu/g}$ (that is $\sim 7.89 \times 10^{18}\ \mu \text{B/g}$) theoretically. The calculated atomic magnetic moment was comparable with that obtained by macroscopic magnetic measurement.

The pre-edge observed in our experiment (inset of Fig. 2(a)) originate from the density of states induced by $V_{\text{Si}}$ or $V_{\text{C}}$ defects in some locations of the specimen. To find a possible explanation for the formation of localized magnetic moment of carbon atoms in Al-doped 4H-SiC, let us consider the substitution of Al for Si. The valence shell of Al atom is $3s^{2}2p^{1}$ and Si atom is $3s^{2}2p^{2}$. It means that when Al replaces for Si, each Al atom releases one less electron to the 4H-SiC lattice site than each Si atom does. So that, the introduction of one substitu- tion Al for Si removes one electron from the lattice and the system has an unpaired electron, which is very similar to the case of magnetic carbon [19,20], where unpaired spins arise in a conver- sion from $sp^{3}$ to the mixture of $sp^{3}/sp^{2}$ hybridization. In this process, the unpaired electron is prompted to a higher orbital enabling the nonmagnetic elements to participate in the magnetic ordering. Thus, the carbon atoms have large contribution to the local magnetic moments of Al-doped 4H-SiC. When $V_{Si}$ or $V_{C}$ were introduced into the Al-doped 4H-SiC, additional holes (p-type semiconductor) or electrons (n-type semiconductor) were induced to the system. As shown in Fig. 3(b) and (c), the band gap had to be narrowed through the conduction band and the valence band shifting. This is equivalent to the general rule: the Fermi level should rise due to the electron doping and should down due to the hole doping.

## 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, EMCD technique, with advantages of element sensitivity and high spatial resolution over other magnetism measurements, was performed to pinpoint the atomic origin of the magnetism in Al-doped 4H-SiC. EMCD signals were observed in nonmagnetic C atoms, indicating the spin polarization of C atoms contributes to the magnetic moments of Al-doped 4H-SiC.

The spin polarization of C atoms was further confirmed by first principles calculations. Different from transition metal doped DMS, the localized magnetic moments in Al-doped 4H-SiC were provided by nonmagnetic elements in host semiconductor. The results provide new refers on the origination of ferromagnetism in nonmagnetic elements doped wide-gap semiconductor.
