# Lattice Statics of Orthorhombic Sulphur.

R. P. RINALDI and G. S. PAWLEY

Department of Physics, Edinburgh University - Edinburgh

(ricevuto il 12 Luglio 1972)

Summary. - The structure of orthorhombic sulphur has been fitted using a 6-exp potential function to describe the interactions between nonbonded atom pairs. This is done for a series of different potential function parameter values and a means of deciding upon the best set of parameters is considered.

## 1. - Introduction.

As a basis for research at present being undertaken into the lattice dynamics of orthorhombic sulphur $(^{1})$, some form must be found for the intermolecular potential function. Following the procedure adopted in the lattice dynamical calculations carried out on naphthalene and anthracene $(^{2})$, this function is described by a sum over all nonbonded atom-atom interactions, where these interactions are given by the 6-exp potential function

$$
\text { (1) } \quad V(r)=-\frac{A}{r^{6}}+B \exp [-\alpha r] \text {, }
$$

where $r$ is the interatomic distance.

Experimentally determined values of $A, B$ and $\alpha$ are not available for sulphur. In a previous paper $(^{3})$, GIGLIO et al. investigated the applicability of the Ar-Ar

and Cl-Cl potential functions, proposed by MASON and KREEVOY $(^{4})$ and HILL $(^{5})$ , to the case of orthorhombic sulphur. Surprisingly the Mason-Kreevoy poten- tial is not differentiable at $r=3 \AA$ and is therefore unphysical and the results of using Hill's potential are somewhat poorer. In an attempt to improve upon these results, the present article is concerned with determining optimum values for the parameters $A, B$ and $\alpha$ .

## 2. - Determination of the potential function.
In terms of the 6-exp potential function, the crystal potential is written

$$(2)\qquad \Phi=-A \sum_{i} \frac{1}{r_{i}^{6}}+B \sum_{i} \exp \left[-\alpha r_{i}\right]=-a A+b B, \quad \text { defining } a \text { and } b.$$

The summation is over all nonbonded atom-atom interactions denoted by the index $i$ , making sure that each bond is only counted once.

At equilibrium $\Phi$ must be a minimum for all possible distortions, and hence in particular a homogeneous deformation. In such a deformation
$$r_{i}\to r_{i}+\delta r_{i}$$
 and
$$\Phi \to \Phi _ { 0 } + \delta \Phi \,,$$
 where $\delta r_{i}=\gamma r_{i}, \gamma$ being a homogeneous structure parameter. As $\Phi_{0}$ is the potential at equilibrium
$$\lim _{\gamma \rightarrow 0} \frac{\delta \Phi}{\gamma}=0,$$

$$\delta \Phi=A \sum_{i} \frac{6}{r_{i}^{7}} \delta r_{i}-B \alpha \sum_{i} \exp \left[-\alpha r_{i}\right] \delta r_{i}.$$

Substituting for $\delta r_{i}$ yields

$$(3)\qquad 6 A \sum_{i} \frac{1}{r_{i}^{6}}=B \alpha \sum_{i} r_{i} \exp \left[-\alpha r_{\imath}\right],$$

which can be written
$$6 A a=B \beta, \quad \text { defining } \beta.$$

(4) E. A. MASON and M. M. KREEVOY: Journ. Amer. Chem. Soc., 77, 5808 (1955).
(5) T. HILL: Journ. Chem. Phys., 16, 399 (1948).

For a given $\alpha$, the values of $a$, $b$ and $\beta$ can be found by using the known structure $(^{6})$. For the unit cell parameters we have used values obtained from prelim- inary data of a neutron structure investigation of orthorhombic sulphur, which we are at present carrying out at the Risø Research Establishment, Denmark, in collaboration with Dr. F. KREBS LARSEN. These are within the limits of error of Abraham's values $(^{6})$. $\Phi_{0}$ is taken as $-25.2 \mathrm{kcal} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$, the heat of formation of $\mathrm{S}_{8}$ gas from orthorhombic sulphur $(^{7})$.

Equations (2) and (3) give two conditions on the three parameters $A, B$ and $\alpha$:

$$
\text { (4) } \quad B=\frac{\Phi_{0}}{b-\beta / 6}, \quad A=\frac{B b-\Phi_{0}}{a},
$$

and we are left in effect with a one-parameter model. Table I shows values of $A$ and $B$ for a given $\alpha$ in the range $2.8 \AA^{-1}$ to $4 \AA^{-1}$. These parameters give a minimum in the potential function with respect only to homogeneous defor- mation; there is no guarantee that these parameters are consistent with the true potential minimum. Consequently the true minimum for each parameter set must be found.

### 3. - Minimization of potential.

To find the true potential minimum, the static structure is subjected to all possible symmetric distortions. The unit cell parameters are allowed to vary and small translational and rotational changes permitted in the position and orientation of the $S_8$ molecules, until the minimum is achieved. For all values of $\alpha$ within the specified range, we were able to achieve a minimum with very small shifts in the five structure parameters, each shift being com- mensurate with the standard deviations of the measurements.

In their treatment, Giglio et al. $^{(3)}$ consider the $S_8$ molecules to be in fixed orientations and allow only variation of the cell parameters. It is of interest to note that for the two values of $\alpha$ (3.62 $\mathring{A}^{-1}$, 3.67 $\mathring{A}^{-1}$) used in their investiga- tion, the discrepancy in unit cell volume (75.0 $\mathring{A}^3$, 278.4 $\mathring{A}^3$ respectively) on obtaining a minimum in potential is greater than that found in our case for any value of $\alpha$ we have chosen, as can be seen from Table I, column 4. ## 4. - Discussion.

In the present approach the optimum value of $\alpha$ is not determined although such an optimum must exist. The fact that we can satisfactorily obtain from

the crystal structure a potential minimum of the correct depth emphasizes the weakness in lattice static calculations: the position and depth of the poten- tial minimum are reproduced but no measurement is used to give the correct curvature of the potential function with respect to any of the structure para- meters. Such information can only come from dynamical measurements. Modes belonging to some of these representations will be Raman active and hence comparison of our calculated frequencies with assigned Raman

frequencies in principle allows a choice for $\alpha$ to be made, ensuring a potential function more realistic than any obtainable from lattice statics calculations alone. Unfortunately, in the case of $S_{8}$ we have not been able to find in the literature optical measurements in which the modes are clearly assigned.

However, the inelastic coherent neutron scattering measurements carried out at Harwell and Risø in collaboration with Drs. C. G. WINDSOR and J. KJEMS supply further information on the zero-wave-vector modes and analysis of these results will hopefully permit group-theoretical assignments to be made. Fur- thermore, in addition to the neutron structure investigation we are carrying out with Dr. F, KREBS LARSEN, Prof. P. COPPENS (Buffalo) is performing an X-ray structure analysis of orthorhombic sulphur. As well as leading to more accurate values of the structure parameters and hence better values for $a$, $b$ and $\beta$, a comparison between the X-ray and neutron work should prove of interest. Some internal-mode frequencies of the sulphur molecule are very low, indicating that the molecule is easily deformed by crystal forces and is highly polarizable. The extent of this polarizability will be revealed by a com- parison of the X-ray and neutron results. Thus, even with improved struc- ture parameters we anticipate that the one-parameter rigid unpolarizable molecule model will be inadequate, and the problem may demand a more sophisticated approach, similar in many ways to the shell model for polar- izable ionic crystals.
