## Zeroth-order calculations of the energy of movement of vacancies in alkali halides

By K. THARMALINGAM

6 Deans Court, Deans Road, Brentwood, Essex, England

[Received 27 January 1977 and in present form 23 May 1977]

## ABSTRACT
The energy of movement, of cations and anions, via single vacancies and vacancy pairs in some alkali halides have been calculated. The results of these calculations, in the zeroth order approximation, are in reasonable agreement with the experimental findings.

Dienes (1948) used the Born-Mayer repulsive potential (B.M.) to calculate the activation energy of movement of $Cl^-$ in KCl via vacancy pairs and found that the vacancy pairs are highly mobile. Subsequently Guccione, Tosi and Asdente (1959) calculated the activation energy of movement of ions, via single vacancies in NaCl and KCl, and found that they had to use a harder Born-Mayer-Verwey repulsive potential (B.M.V.) to obtain agreement with experimental results. Laurence (1960) studied diffusion in doped NaCl and gave conclusive, experimental evidence that the mobility of vacancy pairs is as low as that of single vacancies. Tharmalingam and Lidiard (1961) re-calculated the energy of movement of vacancy pairs in NaCl and KCl, in the first order with both the B.M. and B.M.V. potentials, and found that the mobility of vacancy pairs is as low as that of single vacancies. More recently, it was noted by Tharmalingam (1971) that the first-order calculations with B.M. potentials gave a lower value for the energy of formation of defects in ionic crystals and also that the zeroth-order calculation gave more reliable results. Subsequently, Boswarva (1972) showed that relaxation calculations, with the B.M. potential do in fact give reliable estimates for the energy of formation of Schottky defects in ionic crystals, providing the numerical values of the parameters that enter into the B.M. potential are rederived. We remark here that the numerical values of the parameters derived by Boswarva (1972) are such that the strength of the B.M. repulsive potential, with the Van der Waals term included, is about the same as that of the B.M.V. potential previously used (for cation-anion distances of $0.75\ r_0$ to $r_0$, where $r_0$ is the cation-anion separation in perfect lattice). Furthermore, the Boswarva (1972) values for the B.M. potential are such that the anion-anion and cation-cation repulsive interaction (with Van der Waals term taken into account) are negative at an ionic separation of $\sqrt{2}\ r_0$. Because of the hardness of the

cation-anion interaction, the B.M. potential of Boswarva (1972) is likely to give reasonable estimates for energies of formation and energies of movement in the relaxation calculation.

The aim of the present work is to find a method of calculation of activation energies, without complicated relaxation calculations, in the spirit of zeroth-order. The energy of the ground state is calculated in the zeroth-order, as defined earlier, with all the ions of the lattice treated in the Mott and Littleton (1938) continuum approximation. In the saddle point state, for the cation jump, the two neighbouring anions are displaced in the (110) direction such that the anion-cation separation is $0.85\ r_0$. The rest of the ions of the lattice are kept at lattice sites and, the calculation of the energy of the saddle point state was performed in the zeroth-order (without any minimization). The values of $r_0$, ionic radii, and T.K.S. polarizabilities that we have used are given in Kittel (1968, pp. 98, 105 (see table 6) and 385). In the B.M. potential that we have used, the average $\rho$ is taken to be $0.345\ \text{\AA}$ and $b=0.1429$ eV. The results of calculation are shown in the table and we note that the activation energy for movement of cations via single vacancies are in reasonable agreement with the experimental values quoted in Kittel (1968, p. 570). We have also extended our calculations for the energy of movement via vacancy pairs and the results are given in the last two columns. We note that the mobility of vacancy pairs is higher than that of anion vacancies. The energy of formation of vacancy pairs in NaCl, in the zeroth-order with the same numerical value of parameters, is 1·55eV. Hence the activation energy for diffusion via vacancy pairs in NaCl is $1.55 + 1.02 = 2.57$ eV.

This is in reasonable agreement with the experimental value of 2.49 eV found by Laurence (1960). The energy of formation of Schottky pairs in the zeroth-order in NaCl, with these values of parameters, is 2.35 eV. Hence the activation energy for diffusion of anion vacancies in NaCl is $(2\cdot 35/2)+1\cdot 19=2\cdot 37$ eV. This too is in reasonable agreement with the experimental value of 2.12 eV found by Laurence (1960). We note that had we used the B.M.V. repulsive potential, in the zeroth-order calculation, the values for the activation energy of movement of single vacancies quoted in the table will be raised by about $\frac{1}{2}$ eV and those of vacancy pairs by about $\frac{1}{4}$ eV.
