# Inelastic neutron scattering and dynamics of ions in the super-ionic conductor $\boldsymbol{Ag_2S}$

I Ebbsjö†, P Vashishta†, R Dejus‡ and K Sköld‡

† Materials Science and Technology Division, Argonne National Laboratory, Argonne, IL 60439, USA
‡ The Studsvik Science Research Laboratory, S-611 82 Nyköping, Sweden

Received 1 May 1987

**Abstract.** The dynamics of ions in the super-ionic conductor $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ and its manifestations in the inelastic neutron scattering function are studied using the molecular dynamics technique. The frequency spectrum of density fluctuations is investigated in the vicinity of $\boldsymbol{Q}=(1.6,1,0)$. The results for the intensities, linewidths and the temperature dependence of the quasi-elastic peak agree with those from the experiments of Grier, Shapiro and Cava. The low-energy excitation ($\simeq 2$ meV) observed below the super-ionic transition is also explained.

Super-ionic conductors [1] such as $\text{AgI}$, $\text{Ag}_3\text{SI}$ and $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ can be thought of as anisotropic Coulomb liquids—the strongly interacting Coulomb liquid consisting of $\text{Ag}^+$ cations diffuses under the effective one-body potential of cubic symmetry due to the BCC anion lattice. In these three super-ionic conductors, the average charge of the anion in the BCC lattice varies from 1 to $\frac{3}{2}$ to 2 as one goes from $\text{AgI}$ to $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$. The number of Ag ions per BCC unit cell is correspondingly 2, 3 or 4. Since the point group symmetry of the anion lattice remains unchanged as the number of Ag ions increases, it is of interest to look experimentally as well as theoretically for systematic differences in the single-particle motion and in the collective behaviour of the cations in these three systems.

Experimental studies of super-ionic conductors have included many different types of measurements [2], including x-ray [3] and neutron scattering (see [4] and references therein, [5]). In their inelastic neutron scattering experiment on super-ionic $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ [6], Grier and co-workers observed anomalous intensities at $\boldsymbol{Q}=(1.6,1,0)$ similar to those observed in x-ray diffuse scattering by Cava and McWhan [7]. In addition, Grier and co-workers also observed an excitation at approximately 2 meV in the vicinity of this $\boldsymbol{Q}$ vector, but only at temperatures below the super-ionic transition. A low-energy excitation of this kind is common to many super-ionic conductors and was first observed by Shapiro and co-workers [8] in $\text{RbAg}_4\text{I}_5$. However, none of the super-ionic conductors studied so far displays the strong $\boldsymbol{Q}$ dependence on $\boldsymbol{Q}$ of the $\simeq 2$ meV excitation observed in $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$. The present molecular dynamics (MD) study, which is based on an effective-pair-potential model, attempts to explain the anomalous neutron scattering intensities and the nature of the low-energy excitation in super-ionic conductors.

The problem of constructing effective pairwise potentials is approached from a phenomenological point of view [9]. The three interaction potentials are

$$
\begin{align*}
V_{\text{AgAg}}(r) &= \frac{H_{\text{AgAg}}}{r^7} + \frac{0.2025}{r} \\
V_{\text{AgS}}(r) + \frac{H_{\text{AgS}}}{r^7} &= \frac{0.4050}{r} - \frac{0.66}{r^4} \\
V_{\text{SS}}(r) &= \frac{H_{\text{SS}}}{r^7} + \frac{0.8100}{r} - \frac{5.28}{r^4}
\end{align*}
\tag{1}
$$

where $H_{\text{AgAg}} = 0.06042$, $H_{\text{AgS}} = 16.12$ and $H_{\text{SS}} = 346.3$; the units of length and energy are $\mathring{\text{A}}$ and $e^2/\mathring{\text{A}} = 14.40$ eV, respectively.

MD calculations were performed for 384- and 750-particle systems at the fixed number density of $5.233 \times 10^{22} \text{cm}^{-3}$. The Coulomb interactions were summed by the Ewald method. Initially, the sulphur ions were placed in a BCC array, and the silver ions were distributed over a third of the available $t$ sites. Each system was 'thermalised' for several thousand time steps. MD calculations for 18000 steps were then made to calculate the correlation functions of interest.

The velocity auto-correlation functions (VAF), $\varphi_{\text{Ag}}(t)$ and $\varphi_{\text{S}}(t)$, and the correct auto-correlation function (CAF), $\psi(t)$, respectively, are defined by

$$
\varphi_{\alpha}(t) = \sum_{i(\alpha)} \langle \boldsymbol{v}_i(t) \cdot \boldsymbol{v}_i(0) \rangle \Bigg/ \sum_{i(\alpha)} \langle v_i^2(0) \rangle \quad \alpha = \text{Ag, S}
\tag{2a}
$$

$$
\psi(t) = \langle \boldsymbol{J}(t) \cdot \boldsymbol{J}(0) \rangle / \langle J^2(0) \rangle
\tag{2b}
$$

where

$$
\boldsymbol{J}(t) = \sum_i Z_i e \boldsymbol{v}_i(t)
$$

and $Z_{\text{S}} = -2 Z_{\text{Ag}}$ are the charges on sulphur and on silver ions, respectively. The frequency spectra of Ag and S VAFs and CAF (proportional to the frequency-dependent ionic conductivity) are shown in figures 1(a) and (b), respectively. In figure 1(a), $\varphi_{\text{Ag}}(\omega = 0) (= M_{\text{Ag}} D_{\text{Ag}} / k_{\text{B}} T)$ is finite, indicating that Ag ions are diffusing, and the calculated constant of self-diffusion $D_{\text{Ag}} = 0.7 \times 10^{-5} \text{cm}^2 \text{s}^{-1}$ is in good agreement with the experiment [2]; the zero value of $\varphi_{\text{S}}(\omega = 0)$ confirms that S ions are not diffusing. The frequency spectrum for sulphur is consistent with that of a high-temperature BCC lattice. The spectrum for silver lies mostly in the range 0 to 10 meV. This is owing to the fact that the diffusing silver sublattice is considerably softer than the non-diffusing sulphur lattice and the Ag ions are heavier, $M_{\text{Ag}}/M_{\text{s}} > 3$. The phonon density of states, as it would be determined from a neutron scattering experiment, is obtained by adding the two functions $\varphi_{\text{Ag}}(\omega)$ and $\varphi_{\text{S}}(\omega)$, properly weighted by the appropriate concentration factors etc and incoherent scattering cross sections. The frequency spectra of Ag and S are also reflected in the ionic conductivity $\sigma(\omega)$, as seen in figure 1(b), with relative weights that depend on the square of effective charges on the ions, $Z_{\text{S}} = -2 Z_{\text{Ag}}$.

The temperature dependence of the Haven's ratio, $H_R$, the ratio of the tracer diffusion constant, $D_{\text{Ag}}$, to the diffusion constant, $D_{\sigma}(\omega = 0)$, obtained from $\sigma(\omega = 0)$, is nearly constant. The values of $H_R = 0.4$ at 470 K and $H_R = 0.5$ at 833 K are in good agreement with experiments [2].

The most intriguing results of the neutron scattering experiments for the super-ionic $Ag_2S$ are the anomalous intensity of the quasi-elastic diffuse scattering around $\boldsymbol{Q}=(1.6, 1, 0)$ and the observation of the low-energy ($\simeq 2$ meV) excitation below the super-ionic transition.

Even though the anomalous intensities observed in the x-ray diffuse scattering and inelastic neutron scattering are qualitatively similar, there are subtle differences in their dependence on $\boldsymbol{Q}$. To elucidate this, we examine the intermediate scattering functions defined as
$$
F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t)=\left\langle\rho_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t) \rho_{\beta}^{*}(\boldsymbol{Q}, 0)\right\rangle \quad \alpha, \beta \in\{\mathrm{Ag}, \mathrm{S}\} \tag{3a}
$$
where
$$
\rho_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\alpha}}} \sum_{i(\alpha)} \exp \left(\mathrm{i} \boldsymbol{Q} \cdot \boldsymbol{r}_{i}(t)\right)
$$
and the dynamical structure factors are
$$
S_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega)=\frac{1}{2 \pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t) \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \omega t} \mathrm{~d} t. \tag{4}
$$

The coherent neutron scattering function is obtained from the weighted sum of the Fourier transforms of $F_{\alpha\beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t)$:

$$
S(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega)=\sum_{\alpha, \beta} b_{\alpha} b_{\beta}\left(c_{\alpha} c_{\beta}\right)^{1 / 2} S_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega) \tag{5}
$$

where $b_{\alpha}$ and $c_{\alpha}$ are the neutron scattering lengths and atomic concentrations, respectively.

From the MD $\{\boldsymbol{r}, t\}$ trajectories for super-ionic $\mathrm{Ag}_{2} \mathrm{~S}$, we have calculated $F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t)$ and $S(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega)$ for a number of $\boldsymbol{Q}$ vectors, with special emphasis on wave-vectors in the vicinity of $(1.6,1,0)$. Results for $F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t)$ for $\boldsymbol{Q}$ vectors $(1.8,1,0),(1.6,1,0),(1.4,1,0),(1.6$, $0.8,0)$ and $(1.6,1.2,0)$ are shown in figure 2.

From figure 2, it is clear that (S, S), (S, Ag) and (Ag, S) correlations are negligible in the vicinity of $(1.6,1,0)$ and that the (Ag, Ag) correlations are the only dominant ones in the system. The static structure factor which determines the x-ray scattering is given by $S_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q})=F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t=0)$, whereas the neutron scattering functions for zero energy transfer, $S_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega=0)$, are determined by the time integral of $F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t)$. Thus, the neutron intensity, $S(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega=0)$, should show a much more rapid decrease than the x-ray intensity, $S(\boldsymbol{Q})$, as one moves away from $(1.6,1,0)$. This is exactly what has been found experimentally by Grier and co-workers [6]. The weighted sums of the Fourier transforms of $F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t), S(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega)$, as given in (5), incorporating the experimental resolution of FWHM = 0.7 meV, are shown in figure 3. We find that the neutron scattering intensity is maximal at $\boldsymbol{Q}=(h, k, 0)=(1.6,1,0)$; it decreases rapidly along the $h$-direction, but varies much more slowly along the $k$-direction, as one moves away from $(1.6,1,0)$. Thus, the neutron intensity, $S(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega=0)$, like the diffuse x-ray intensity, forms anisotropic discs around $\boldsymbol{Q}=(1.6,1,0)$; the neutron intensity, however, shows a stronger decrease with $\boldsymbol{Q}$ than the x-ray diffuse intensity, in agreement with experiments [5-7]. As expected, the calculations also show that the width of the quasi-elastic neutron peak increases and the intensity decreases as the temperature increases.

The other intriguing feature for $\mathrm{Ag}_{2} \mathrm{~S}$ is the observation of the low-lying ($\simeq 2$ meV) excitation below the super-ionic transition temperature, 450 K. Unlike other super-ionic conductors, e.g. $\mathrm{AgI}$ and $\mathrm{RbAg}_{4} \mathrm{I}_{5}$, in $\mathrm{Ag}_{2} \mathrm{~S}$ this peak shows a strong dependence on $\boldsymbol{Q}$. To investigate this excitation, we 'cooled' the $\mathrm{Ag}_{2} \mathrm{~S}$ system below the super-ionic transition temperature. Unlike real $\mathrm{Ag}_{2} \mathrm{~S}$, where the sulphur ions transform into a monoclinic lattice (small distortions of the BCC lattice), in the present calculations the sulphurs cannot undergo a structural transition. This is due to the restriction of the fixed shape of the MD cell.

Intermediate scattering functions $F_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t)$ were calculated for times up to 40 ps at 268 and 339 K, which are both below the transition temperature, and at 470 K, which is just above the super-ionic transition temperature. The wave-vector dependence of the low-energy excitation and its temperature variation were investigated. The results for $S(\boldsymbol{Q}, \omega)$ for $\boldsymbol{Q}=(1.8,1,0)$ at 268, 339 and 470 K are shown in figure 4. A low-energy peak is evident in the 268 K results at an energy transfer of approximately 3 meV. At 339 K, the height of the low-energy peak decreases and the intensity at $\omega=0$ increases. As the temperature is increased further, the excitation broadens and then disappears in the super-ionic phase at 470 K; also the intensity at $\omega=0$ increases greatly. All these results are in agreement with the experiment [5, 6].
