SPHEROIDAL MULTIPOLAR EXPANSIONS FOR INTERMOLECULAR ENERGY
IN CRYSTAL THEORY.
II. LATTICE ENERGY OF THE ORTHORHOMBIC STRUCTURE OF ACETYLENE CRYSTAL

Monique AUBERT-FRÉCON

Laboratoire de Chimie-Physique, Institut National des Sciences Appliquées de Lyon,
69621 Villeurbanne, France

Received 31 July 1978

The spheroidal formalism for the interaction energy between two non-overlapping ellipsoidal charge distributions is applied to the determination of the lattice energy of the orthorhombic structure of the acetylene crystal. The molecules are assimilated to quadrupolar prolate ellipsoids and the pair potential is assumed to be a sum of two terms: a Kihara core-potential extended to prolate ellipsoids and the spheroidal quadrupolar interaction. The value so obtained for the lattice energy is $-25.01 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mole}^{-1}$ while an evaluation of the corresponding observed value is $-23.81 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mole}^{-1}$.

### 1. Introduction
The structure of a molecular crystal is governed by the shape of the molecule and the intermolecular forces. For non-spherical molecules, models including shape effects for the repulsive part of the intermolecular potential have been developed. Among them the Kihara core-model [1] and the ellipsoidal overlap model [2] are currently explored [3-10] and give reasonably accurate representations of selected molecules or molecular crystals.

The long-range part of the intermolecular potential is assumed to be shape-independent and is generally expanded in terms of interactions between the permanent multipoles of two non-overlapping spherical charge distributions [11]. However this assumption is too strong for non-spherical neighbouring molecules in crystal structure, then it is more realistic to represent such molecules by ellipsoids of revolution (prolate or oblate).

In the preceding paper referred to as I, multipolar expansions of the interaction energy between two non-overlapping ellipsoidal charge distributions have been investigated. The main purpose of this second paper is to illustrate the spheroidal formalism by calculating the lattice energy of the low temperature phase structure of the acetylene crystal assuming that molecules are quadrupolar prolate ellipsoids. The pair-potential model used is a sum of two terms: a Kihara core potential extended to the ellipsoidal case and the spheroidal quadrupolar interaction. In section 2, the extension of the Kihara's model to prolate ellipsoids is presented and results are shown for the interaction between two acetylene molecules in several geometrical configurations. The calculation of the lattice energy of the orthorhombic phase of the acetylene crystal is described in section 3.

### 2. Model potential
The linear acetylene molecule is an axial quadrupole and at large distances the intermolecular potential is dominated by the electrostatic terms between two such quadrupoles. In prolate spheroidal coordinates, the quadrupolar interaction is given by (for details see paper I)

$$
U_{\Theta \Theta}=\frac{1}{4 \pi \epsilon_{0}} \frac{\Theta}{3 d^{5}} \sum_{\alpha \beta} \Theta_{\alpha \beta, 0}^{(2)} \sum_{u v} T_{\alpha \beta}(u, v) V_{u v}^{2}\left(\Theta_{z z}\right),(1)
$$

where $\Theta$ is the value of the acetylene quadrupole.
$\Theta_{\alpha \beta, 0}^{(2)}$ are the quadrupole components of molecule

(2) expressed in a system parallel to molecule (1)-fixed axis system. The quantities $T_{\alpha \beta}(u, v)$ and $V_{u v}(\Theta_{z z})$ are functions of molecule (2)-center position $(\lambda, \mu, \phi)$, and

$$\alpha, \beta \in(x, y, z),$$

$$u \in(\lambda, \mu), v \in(0, \lambda, \mu),$$

$$\left[V_{u v}(\Theta_{z z})=0 \text { if } u \equiv \phi \text { or } v \equiv \phi\right].$$

The actual potential between two acetylene molecules is of course not purely quadrupolar, eq. (1) including no short-range contribution. Thus, we assume the intermolecular potential $U(R)$ to be a sum of two terms: a Kihara's core potential term $U_{K}$ and the electrostatic quadrupolar interaction $U_{\Theta \Theta}$:

$$U(R)=U_{\mathrm{K}}+U_{\Theta \Theta}, \tag{2}$$

where $R$ is the center-to-center distance.

### 2.1. Ellipsoidal cores of acetylene molecules

Let us recall briefly the main lines of the core-potential model of convex molecules used in this paper, following Kihara's notations [1].

The intermolecular potential function is assumed to be:

$$U_{\mathrm{K}}(\rho)=U_{0}\left[\left(\rho_{0} / \rho\right)^{12}-2\left(\rho_{0} / \rho\right)^{6}\right], \tag{3}$$

where $\rho$ represents the shortest distance between the molecular cores, $\rho_{0}$ and $U_{0}$ are empirical parameters having the dimensions of length and energy, respectively. They are determined from the experimental values of the second virial coefficients and from the knowledge of three characteristic values of the convex cores: volume $V$, area $S$ and a linear quantity $M$. Kihara gave the quantities $V, S$ and $M$ for a lot of molecular cores but to the best of our knowledge, the ellipsoidal case has not been investigated.

The known charge density maps for the acetylene molecule [12,5] suggest as molecular core, a prolate ellipsoid with hydrogen atoms as foci; $d$, the distance between the foci is 0.33 nm, $a$, the major semi-axis, is 0.17 nm. By use of the relation

$$d=2\left(a^{2}-b^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}$$

the minor semi-axes $b=c$ are equal to 0.041 nm. The fundamental measures are:

$$V=\frac{4}{3} \pi a b^{2},$$

$$S=2 \pi b\left[b+\left(2 a^{2} / d\right) \operatorname{arc} \sin (d / 2 a)\right],$$

$$M=2 \pi\left[b+\left(2 a^{2} / d\right) \operatorname{arc} \sin (d / 2 a)\right].$$

A mean-squares fit of calculated second virial coefficients in terms of $\rho_{0}, U_{0}, V, S$ and $M$ to the corresponding observed values [13] gives: $\rho_{0}=0.268 \mathrm{~nm}$ and $U_{0}=3.32 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mole}^{-1}$. A fairly good representation of the molecular shape is then provided by a "parallel body" of the previously defined molecular core with thickness $\rho_{0} / 2$.

However there is a large numerical discrepancy between the two sets of results due to the different models used. For the intermolecular potential, Kihara assumed a sum of three terms: (a) the repulsive part of (3), $\rho_{0}$ and $U_{0}$ being derived from spindle-shaped molecular cores; (b) the potential of the dispersion force and (c) the electrostatic (spherical) quadrupolar interaction calculated with an estimated value: $\Theta=1.33 \times 10^{-39}\,\text{C m}^{2}$. ## 3. Lattice energy of the low-temperature phase of the acetylene crystal

### 3.1. Crystal structure

In 1975, Koski and Sandor [15] published the crystal structure of the low-temperature phase of solid acetylene ($\text{C}_2\text{D}_2$) determined at 4.2 K by neutron powder diffraction. Crystal data are gathered in table 3. The matrix of the direction cosines relating the space-fixed crystal system $XYZ$ to the molecule-fixed axis system $xyz$ is displayed in table 4 while the molecular positions (fractions of unit-cell edges) and orientations are listed in table 5.

### 3.2. Lattice energy

The lattice energy $E_{\text{latt.}}$ is obtained as a sum of all pair-interactions between a reference molecule 1 and the molecules $i$ surrounding 1 in the crystal

$$
E_{\text{latt.}}=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=2}^{N} U_{1 i}\left(R_{1 i}\right), \tag{4}
$$

where $U_{1 i}(R_{1 i})$ is given by eq. (2) and $R_{1 i}$ is the center-to-center distance. The pair potential used in this calculation contained the following parameters: $\rho_0=$ $0.268\ \text{nm}, U_0=3.32\ \text{kJ mole}^{-1}, \Theta=2.4×10^{-39}\ \text{C}\text{m}^2$, $d=0.33\ \text{nm}$. Molecules such as $R_{1 i} \leqslant 0.7\ \text{nm}$ are taken into account in the evaluation of (4), thus $N=19$.

For each molecule $i$, the center-to-center distance $R_{1 i}$, the distance between ellipsoidal cores $\rho_{1 i}$, the prolate spheroidal coordinates $(\lambda_i, \mu_i, \phi_i)$ of the center of molecule $i$ are presented in table 6 as well as the core-potential $U_{\text{K}}(\rho_{1 i})$, the quadrupolar interaction $U_{\Theta\Theta}(\lambda_i, \mu_i, \phi_i)$ and the pair potential $U_{1 i}(R_{1 i})$. For the lattice energy we obtained: $E_{\text{latt.}}=-25.10\ \text{kJ mole}^{-1}$. We found that the electrostatic quadrupolar energy is largely dominant (75% of the cohesive energy). Our result is to be compared with the lattice energy evaluated by adding the estimated experimental heat of sublimation [16] to the zero-point energy derived from the Debye temperature [17]. The value so obtained is $-23.81$ $\text{kJ mole}^{-1}$. The agreement between the calculated and the observed values of the lattice energy is fairly good.
