# Comment on the Elastic Constants of $\text{CaF}_2$-$\text{SrF}_2$ Mixed Crystals

By
C. VAROTSOS and M. LAZARIDOU

Singh et al. /1/ have recently investigated in detail cohesive and anharmonic elastic properties of $\text{CaF}_2$-$\text{SrF}_2$ mixed crystals. They used a three-body interaction potential which consists of the long-range Coulomb and three-body interactions and short-range van der Waals attraction and overlap repulsion effective up to the second neighbour ions. The two parameters of the model were obtained by using as input data:
(i) the experimental values of the nn distance and
(ii) the values of the isothermal bulk modulus B.

The latter values have been estimated by treating B to be linearly dependent on the concentration of $\text{Sr}^{++}$ ion. They finally conclude that although the calculated cohesive energies show the correct trend versus the composition, however, they do not agree with the experimental values.

To the best of our knowledge the analysis of the above authors is the most complete presented up to date. It is the scope of the present paper to indicate that it can be improved if more realistic B values are used as input data.

A few years ago it was suggested /2/ that the bulk modulus B of a mixed system, e.g. $\text{KCl}_{1-\text{x}}\text{Br}_{\text{x}}$ can be calculated as a function of the bulk moduli $\text{B}_1$, $\text{B}_2$ of the two end members (1) and (2) (i. e. KCl and KBr, respectively) as follows:

$$
B = \frac{1 + x\left(\frac{v_2}{v_1} - 1\right)}{1 + x\left(\frac{B_1}{B_2}\frac{v_2}{v_1} - 1\right)} B_1 \,, \tag{1}
$$

where $v_1$ and $v_2$ denote the molar volumes of (1) and (2) (i. e. KCl and KBr), respectively.

The reliability of (1) has already been checked /2, 3/ in the $KCl_{1-x}Br_x$ system where experimental elastic data are available as a function of composition. The same agreement holds when one applies (1) to the $NaCl_xBr_{1-x}$ system and compares the resulting values with the experimental values reported recently /4/. It is therefore justified to apply (1) to the $CaF_2$-$SrF_2$ mixed system. In Table 1 we give the B values as they result from (1) along with those reported by Singh et al. /1/ for various concentrations. A comparison of these two sets of values indicates that for $Ca_{90}Sr_{10}F_2$ and $Ca_{10}Sr_{90}F_2$ the deviation is only around 1%, i. e. comparable to the usual experimental error. At all other concentrations, however, the difference is appreciably larger and it lies between 20 and 42 kbar.

Due to the significance of the latter differences and in view of the fact that (1) has already been found to give satisfactory results in mixed systems where direct data are available, we suggest that the calculation of /2/ should be repeated by considering as input data the B values given in the last column of Table 1.
