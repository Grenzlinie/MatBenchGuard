# A Theoretical Investigation of the Epitaxial Relationship of Al/AlAs(001)
Tomonori ITO
NTT LSI Laboratories, 3-1, Morinosato Wakamiya, Atsugi-shi, Kanagawa 243-01, Japan

(Received February 28, 1996; accepted for publication March 22, 1996)

The epitaxial relationship between Al thin films and AlAs(001) substrate is investigated theoretically, using cohesive energy calculations based on empirical interatomic potentials. The planar energy difference between Al(001) and Al(110) is very small (estimated at 26 meV/atom) at the interface between the thin films and a substrate. Modification with a single monolayer of In at the interface dramatically stabilizes Al(001) by an energy difference of 242 meV/atom.

KEYWORDS: Al, AlAs, heterostructure, epitaxial relationship, empirical interatomic potential, cohesive energy

The capability of growing metal/semiconductor het- erostructures epitaxially would facilitate the develop- ment of electronic devices such as metal-base transistors and internal reflection planes in optoelectronic devices. To this end, many attempts have been made to grow Al single crystal films with face-centered cubic structure on (001) zinc blende structured substrates such as GaAs and AlAs. $^{1)}$ The epitaxial growth of the metal/semiconductor heterostructures, however, is not as well understood as is that of semiconductor/semiconductor heterostructures, because of the complexity of the structural differences between semiconductors and metals.

Ludeke et al. reported the growth of Al(110) on GaAs by molecular beam epitaxy (MBE). $^{2)}$ Petroff et al. observed Al(110) and (001) films and entirely Al(001) films deposited on GaAs at room temperature. $^{3)}$ Ludeke and Landgren concluded that at low substrate temperatures, the overlayers were Al(001) on a Ga-stabilized and Al(110) on an As-stabilized surface, $^{4)}$ while Massies and Linh pointed out that the growth of Al(001) and Al(110) was independent of the surface stoichiometry, even under similar growth conditions. $^{5)}$ Additionally, Nakahara et al. examined the growth of Al on AlAs, instead of GaAs, as a means of preventing Al-Ga exchange and interdiffusion. $^{6)}$ They found a growth model of Al(110) with multiple Al layers (referred to as Al(110)R) at the initial stage of the growth. More recently, Maeda et al. systematically investigated Al/AlAs heterostructure with various interface atomic species. $^{7)}$ They pointed out that interface atomic species are crucial in controlling the epitaxial relationship between semiconductor and metal. In particular, the growth of Al(001) on AlAs(001) has been successfully achieved by predeposition of 1 ML of In on AlAs(001), while the growth of Al(110)R is commonly found on As- and Al-stabilized AlAs(001).

In spite of the importance of the epitaxial relationship, only a few theoretical studies have been performed. Using the pseudopotential perturbation theory and empirical interatomic potentials, the author has studied the structural stability in thin films on zinc blende structured (001) substrates. These include CdSe with zinc blende and wurtzite structures on ZnTe, Sn with diamond and $\beta$-Sn structures on CdTe, and Al(001), Al(110) and Al(110)R on AlAs. $^{8-11)}$ In the previous study for Al/AlAs, Al(110)/AlAs(001) was found to be favorable, in terms of energy, at the interface by 20.2 meV/atom, but unfavorable in the thin film region by 45.3 meV/atom comparing to Al(001)/AlAs(001) with interface structure proposed by Ludeke and Landgren (referred to as Al(001)L). In this paper, the stability of Al(001)/AlAs(001) is reconsidered by employing the interface structure proposed by Maeda et al. (referred to as Al(001)) and is discussed in terms of the interface modification by calculating planar cohesive energy under coherent growth conditions.

To investigate the relative stability between various interface structures, empirical interatomic potentials are used for the cohesive energy calculations near the Al/AlAs interfaces. The universal empirical interatomic potential $^{12-15)}$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{i j}= & A \exp \left[-\beta\left(r_{i j}-R_{i}\right)^{\gamma}\right]\left[\exp \left(-\theta r_{i j}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-B_{0} \exp \left(-\lambda r_{i j}\right) G(\eta) / Z_{i}^{\alpha}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $r_{i j}$ is the distance between the atoms, $Z_{i}$ gives the effective coordination number of atom $i$, $R_{i}$ is the minimum distance between the neighbors, and $G(\eta)$ is the bond bending term for tetrahedrally bonded atom pairs. The potential parameters $A, B_{0}, \theta, \lambda, \alpha, \beta, \gamma$ and $\eta$ were determined using cohesive energy, bulk modulus and the relative stability among various structures obtained by $a b$ initio calculations and experiments. $^{8-15)}$

Stacking sequences (after Ludeke and Landgren, $^{4)}$ and Maeda et al. $^{7)}$ ) near the interface of four types of Al/AlAs(001) considered in this study are schematically shown in Fig. 1. Under coherent growth conditions, the basal plane of the Al layers was constrained by the lattice parameter $a$ of AlAs(001). The atomic positions in the system and $c$-axis of the Al layers were relaxed in the calculation procedure. To clarify the effect of the predeposition on the stabilization of Al(001) on AlAs(001), similar energy calculations for two stable phases such as Al(001) and Al(110) were performed by replacing the interface Al layer in Fig. 1 by 1 ML of In. The calculated planar energies for Al(001)/In/AlAs(001) and Al(110)/In/AlAs(001) are listed in Table II. Table II indicates that addition of the In plane increases the energy at the interface, particularly for Al(110)/In/AlAs(001). In comparison, as shown in Table I, the energy difference between Al(001) and Al(110) dramatically increases from 26 meV/atom in the structure without In to 242 meV/atom in the structure with In. This is because some of the In-As bonds at the interface in Al(110)/In/AlAs(001) are strongly distorted away from the ideal bond angle $109.47^\circ$ to $99.88^\circ$, due to the large covalent radius of In and more complicated interface atomic configurations in Al(110)/In/AlAs(001). This suggests that interface modification by 1 ML In can stabilize Al(001) on AlAs(001). This is consistent with experimental results.⁷) However, our calculations imply that interface modification by 1 ML Ga, which also experimentally stabilizes Al(001) on As-stabilized AlAs(001),⁷) does not contribute to the stabilization of Al(001) because of the similarity between Ga and Al.

Consequently, Al(001) is the most stable phase among Al(001), Al(110) and Al(110)R on AlAs(001). Al(110)/AlAs(001) emerges metastably because of small energy difference between Al(001) and Al(110). Interface modification by 1 ML of In strongly stabilizes Al(001)/AlAs(001). These calculated results are qualitatively consistent with some of the experimental results. However, some discrepancies remain between experimental results and those theoretical results which are based on the simple stacking of atomic layers at the interface. Further calculations are necessary, incorporating the effect of kinetics on a reconstructed surface and various interface atomic configurations, for precise comparison with experimental results.
