# Binding energies, bulk moduli and heats of solution of Cs-K alloys in the pseudo alloy atom model

S Tanigawa and M Doyama
Department of Metallurgy and Materials Science, Faculty of Engineering,
University of Tokyo, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo 113, Japan

Received 15 August 1972 in final form 21 November 1972

Abstract. The pseudo alloy atom model was applied to the Cs-K alloy system. Total binding energies, bulk moduli and heats of solution were calculated as a function of alloy compositions over the full composition range from pure Cs to pure K. The calculated values of total binding energies showed a good agreement with experiments. The results suggest that the pseudo alloy atom model is suitable for the description of disordered alloys. It was concluded that the potential of an ion in alloys is appreciably different from that in the pure metal case.

## 1. Introduction

The recent progress in the theory of simple metals in a pseudopotential approach (Heine 1970, Cohen and Heine 1970, Heine and Weaire 1970) has been followed by several papers on the phase stability of alloys in terms of the second order perturbation theory using weak pseudopotentials (Hayes *et al* 1968, Inglesfield 1969a, b, c, Stroud and Ashcroft 1971). These approaches start with a uniform electron gas, which is then subjected to a perturbation, expressed in terms of the atomic matrix elements of constituent metals in pure states and the dielectric function, which depends only on $r_{s}$, the radius of the electron sphere. On the other hand, Stern (1966, 1968) pointed out important differences between disordered alloys and pure metals with long range order, and suggested that the selfconsistent potential in the alloy is appreciably different from that in the pure metal. A new model of concentrated disordered alloys, named as the pseudo alloy atom model, was proposed in the previous paper (Tanigawa and Doyama 1973). There the model was applied to the Cs-Rb alloy system and the numerical results of the total binding energies as a function of the fractional concentration of alloying elements showed a good agreement with experiments over the full composition range from pure Cs to pure Rb.

In the present paper, this model is applied to the Cs-K alloy system and the results of calculations are compared with those of the Cs-Rb alloy system. The Cs-K alloy system forms a continuous solid solution over the full composition range in a similar way to the Cs-Rb system, as shown in figure 1. Furthermore, the results on both systems are compared with the usual virtual crystal approximation (Stroud and Ashcroft 1971) and its variations (Hayes *et al* 1968, Inglesfield 1969a, b,c). A part of the present work was reported previously (Tanigawa and Doyama 1972a, b).

## 2. Pseudo alloy atom model and other previous approaches

Ashcroft (1966) proposed the simple empty core model for electron-ion interactions of various simple metals. The empty core radii $r_{\mathrm{c}}$ for Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Mg, Zn, Hg, Al, In, Sn, Pb, Bi, Cu, Ag, and Au were determined by fitting to certain experimental data of Fermi surfaces or electrical resistivities in liquid state (Ashcroft 1966, Ashcroft and Langreth 1967a, b). The values of $r_{\mathrm{c}}$ for some of the above metals are in reasonable accord with the usual ionic radii. The binding energies, bulk moduli, electrical resistivities, phonon spectra and phase shifts calculated on this model showed a good agreement with experiment (Ashcroft and Langreth 1967a, Ashcroft 1968). We apply this empty core model to disordered alloys by employing the pseudo alloy atom model.

In the pseudo alloy atom model proposed by the present authors, a disordered alloy is replaced by a hypothetical perfect monatomic crystal, composed of pseudo alloy atoms. Such a hypothetical perfect monatomic crystal is made to possess the same properties as an actual alloy crystal as a whole. We assign the hypothetical empty core radius $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ and the mean valency $Z_{\mathrm{av}}$ to a pseudo alloy atom. If the pseudo alloy atom model is acceptable, a disordered alloy can be also dealt with in the same way as a pure metal. We treat the empty core radius $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ of a pseudo alloy atom as an adjustable parameter, and determine its value so that the total binding energy satisifies the zero pressure condition at the observed lattice parameter for each alloy composition.

According to the second order perturbation theory, the total binding energy per atom can be represented by
$$
E = E_{\mathrm{M}} + E_{0} + E_{1} + E_{\mathrm{bs}} \tag{1}
$$
where $E_{\mathrm{M}}$ is the electrostatic energy of point ions in the uniform gas of valence electrons, $E_{0}$ is the sum of the kinetic, exchange and correlation energies of the uniform electron gas, $E_{1}$ is the first order perturbation energy and $E_{\mathrm{bs}}$ is the second order perturbation energy or the so called band structure energy. These terms are expressed by the following equations in Rydberg units:
$$
E_{\mathrm{M}} = -\frac{\alpha Z_{\mathrm{av}}^{5/3}}{r_{\mathrm{s}}} \tag{2}
$$

$$
E_{0} = Z_{\mathrm{av}}(2\cdot21/r_{\mathrm{s}}^{2} - 0\cdot916/r_{\mathrm{s}} - 0\cdot115 + 0\cdot031 \ln r_{\mathrm{s}}) \tag{3}
$$
where the Nozières-Pines interpolation formula (Pines and Nozières 1966) was used for correlation energy,
$$
E_{1} = \lim_{q \to 0} \left(v_{i}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(q) + \frac{8\pi Z_{\mathrm{av}}}{\Omega_{0}q^{2}}\right) \tag{4}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{bs}} = \sum_{\boldsymbol{G} \neq 0} \frac{\chi(G)}{\epsilon(G)} \left|v_{i}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(G)\right|^{2} \left|S(\boldsymbol{G})\right|^{2} \tag{5}
$$

$$
\epsilon(q) = 1 - (16\pi/\Omega_{0}q^{2})(1 - f(q))\chi(q) \tag{6}
$$

$$
\chi(q) = -\frac{1}{2}Z_{\mathrm{av}}(\frac{2}{3}E_{\mathrm{F}})^{-1} \left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{4k_{\mathrm{F}}^{2} - q^{2}}{8qk_{\mathrm{F}}} \ln \left|\frac{q + 2k_{\mathrm{F}}}{q - 2k_{\mathrm{F}}}\right|\right). \tag{7}
$$

In the random phase approximation,
$$
f(q) = 0. \tag{8}
$$

In the above equations $\alpha$ is the Ewald constant, 1·79186 for a body centred cubic structure, $Z_{\mathrm{av}}$ the valency of a pseudo alloy atom ion, that is, the mean valency, $r_{\mathrm{s}}$ the radius of the sphere containing one electron, $\Omega_{0}$ the atomic volume, $S(q)$ the structure factor, $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ the Fermi energy and $k_{\mathrm{F}}$ the Fermi wavenumber. $v_{i}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(q)$ is the Fourier transform of the bare interaction of an electron with a single pseudo alloy atom ion. The sum in equation (5) extends over all nonzero reciprocal lattice vectors $G$.

As mentioned above, we adopt the Ashcroft empty core model, that is,
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{i}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(r) &= 0 & & r \leqslant r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}} \\
&= -\frac{2Z_{\mathrm{av}}}{r} & & r > { }_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}.
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
$$

Therefore, equation (1) can be rewritten in the form containing only one unknown adjustable parameter $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
E = -\frac{\alpha Z_{\mathrm{av}}^{5/3}}{r_{\mathrm{s}}} &+ Z_{\mathrm{av}} \left(\frac{2\cdot21}{r_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}} - \frac{0\cdot916}{r_{\mathrm{s}}} - 0\cdot115 + 0\cdot031 \ln r_{\mathrm{s}}\right) + \frac{4\pi Z_{\mathrm{av}}(r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}})^{2}}{\Omega_{0}} \\
&+ \sum_{\boldsymbol{G} \neq 0} \frac{\chi(G)}{\epsilon(G)} \left|\frac{8\pi Z_{\mathrm{av}}}{\Omega_{0}G^{2}} \cos (G r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}})\right|^{2} \left|S(\boldsymbol{G})\right|^{2}.
\end{aligned} \tag{10}
$$

Then, $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ is determined by the zero pressure condition, $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}r_{\mathrm{s}} = 0$, at the observed $r_{\mathrm{s}}$

for each alloy composition. Once $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ is determined at each alloy composition, the total binding energy $E(x)$ as a function of the fractional concentration $x$ of solute element can be calculated. Thus, the heat of solution $\Delta H(x)$ for the binary alloy $\mathrm{A}_{1-x} \mathrm{~B}_{x}$ can be calculated by

$$
H(x)=E(x)-\{(1-x) E(\text { pure } \mathrm{A})+x E(\text { pure } \mathrm{B})\}. \tag{11}
$$

On the other hand, in the usual virtual crystal approximation (eg Stroud and Ashcroft 1971), a disordered alloy is approximated by a periodic lattice composed of mean pseudopotentials $(1-x) v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}+x v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}$, where $v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}$ and $v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}$ are the atomic matrix elements of component metals in the pure state. In this case, the total binding energy can be expressed by

$$
\begin{aligned}
E=E_{\mathrm{M}}+E_{0} & +\lim _{q \rightarrow 0}\left((1-x) v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(q)+x v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}(q)+\frac{8 \pi Z_{\mathrm{av}}}{\Omega_{0} q^{2}}\right) \\
& +\sum_{\boldsymbol{G} \neq 0} \frac{\chi(G)}{\epsilon(G)}\left|(1-x) v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(G)+x v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}(G)\right|^{2}|S(\boldsymbol{G})|^{2}.
\end{aligned} \tag{12}
$$

If we restrict ourselves to the case where both A and B atoms have the same valencies and approximately the same atomic sizes, the heat of solution $\Delta H(x)$ can be given by

$$
\Delta H(x)=-x(1-x) \sum_{\boldsymbol{G} \neq 0} \frac{\chi(G)}{\epsilon(G)}\left|v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(G)-v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}(G)\right|^{2}|S(\boldsymbol{G})|^{2}. \tag{13}
$$

Inglesfield (1969a, b, c) extended the work of Hayes *et al* (1968) and showed that the configuration dependent part of a binary alloy may be expressed in terms of a single alloying potential, the difference between $v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(q)$ and $v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}(q)$. According to this work, $\Delta H(x)$ of the disordered system $\mathrm{A}_{1-x} \mathrm{~B}_{x}$ of the elements, having the same valencies and approximately the same atomic volume, is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H(x)=-x(1-x) & \left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{G} \neq 0} \frac{\chi(G)}{\epsilon(G)}\left|v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(G)-v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}(G)\right|^{2}|S(\boldsymbol{G})|^{2}-\Omega_{0}\left(2 \pi^{2}\right)^{-1}\right. \\
& \left.\int \frac{\chi(q)}{\epsilon(q)}\left|v_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(q)-v_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}(q)\right|^{2} q^{2} \mathrm{~d} q\right)
\end{aligned} \tag{14}
$$

where the first term is the same as equation (13). These approximations are based on the atomic matrix elements of component metals in the pure metal state, except for the selfconsistent screening by correction of $\epsilon(q)$. Stern $(1966,1968)$ discussed important differences between disordered alloys and pure metals and pointed out the following two main fundamental differences between them. One is the charging effect, that is, an alloy wavefunction deposits different amounts of electronic charge in the vicinity of the different constituents, due to both different valencies and atomic volumes. The other is the fact that the many body effect is complicated in alloys. The latter effect is closely related to the former, that is, the effective potential felt by an electron depends on the charging effect, and in turn, the charging depends on the effective potential. Stern con- cluded that the potential of an ion in the alloy may be appreciably different from that in the pure metal state. This means that the potential of the alloy cannot be independently approximated from those of component metals. Therefore, we have sought an adequate alloy potential and proposed the pseudo alloy atom model as the first step. In the next section, the results of calculations of the total binding energy, bulk modulus and the heat of solution as a function of the alloy composition in the Cs-K alloy system are shown.

From the change of the determined values of $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ with the alloy composition, the form of the desired alloy potential is suggested, and is compared with the one expected from the approximation based on the pseudopotentials of component metals in the pure metal state.

## 3. Numerical results on the Cs-K system and discussions

As described in $\S 2, r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ is the only unknown parameter. These values were measured at room temperature and so they were extrapolated to $0 \mathrm{~K}$ by the use of the fractional sum of the linear thermal expansion coefficients of pure Cs and K. The calculated values of the heat of solution $\Delta H$ are very small compared with the experimental values of the other usual alloy systems. Furthermore, they are positive and $\Delta H/x(1-x)$ is not constant over the full composition ranges in both alloy systems. The quasichemical theory of alloys, using three independent A-A, B-B and A-B bonds, predicts that $\Delta H/x(1-x)$ is constant. Hayes and Young (1970) derived a formula for the heat of solution of a binary alloy, in terms of the pseudopotentials of the components, for the case where the size factors vary over a wide range. They did not obtain $\Delta H$ directly, but showed that an indirect argument plausibly explains the widely varying solubilities in binary alkali systems. The present work gives the direct calculation of $\Delta H$, but, in the Cs-K and Cs-Rb systems, the calculated values may contain a considerable inaccuracy, because the absolute values of $\Delta H$ are very small in these systems and they are obtained as the difference of relatively large numbers. However, the change of $\Delta H$ with the alloy compositions is reasonable and it can be seen that $\Delta H/x(1-x)$ is not constant. r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(x)=(1-x) r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{Cs}}+x r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{K} \text { or } \mathrm{Rb}}
\tag{18}
$$
where $x$ is the fractional concentration of $\mathrm{K}$ or $\mathrm{Rb}$ in Cs. (iii) The values of $r_{\mathrm{c}}$ calculated for pure $\mathrm{Li}$ and $\mathrm{Na}$ in the same way were 1.3733 and 1.8041, as shown in figure 5. The extrapolation of equation (17) gives 1.3920 and 1.8092, respectively. (iv) If we assume that the values of two coefficients in equation (17) coincide, the empty core radius $r_{\mathrm{c}}$ is zero when $r_{\mathrm{s}}=1$ au. Furthermore, if we neglect the difference between the Bohr radius of the ground state of a hydrogen atom and $r_{\mathrm{s}}$ of the metallic hydrogen, this result is reasonable, because the core electrons are absent in hydrogen and so no cancellation occurs.

![](./images/812595305521348612_5.jpg)

Figure 5. The plot of $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}$ against $r_{\mathrm{s}}$ including $\mathrm{Li}$ and $\mathrm{Na}$.

The present results show that the alloy potential in the binary alloy $\mathrm{A}_{1-x} \mathrm{~B}_{x}$ is reasonably given by
$$
v_{i}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(q)=-\frac{8 \pi Z_{\mathrm{av}}}{\Omega_{0} q^{2}} \cos \left\{\left((1-x) r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{A}}+x r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{B}}\right) q\right\}.
\tag{19}
$$

According to the suggestion by Stern (1966, 1968), the selfconsistent potential in an alloy may be appreciably different from that in the pure metal. The usual virtual crystal approxi- mation and its variations are based on the electron-ion interactions in a pure metal, except for the selfconsistent screening by correction of $\epsilon(q)$. In the virtual crystal approxi mation, if we employ the empty core model as well, the average potential $v_{i}^{\mathrm{VCA}}(q)$ is given by
$$
v_{i}^{\mathrm{VCA}}(q)=-\left\{(1-x) \frac{8 \pi Z_{\mathrm{A}}}{\Omega_{0}^{\mathrm{A}} q^{2}} \cos \left(q r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{A}}\right)+x \frac{8 \pi Z_{\mathrm{B}}}{\Omega_{0}^{\mathrm{A}} q^{2}} \cos \left(q r_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{B}}\right)\right\}
\tag{20}
$$
where $Z_{i}, \Omega_{0}^{i}$, and $r_{\mathrm{c}}^{i}$ are the valency, atomic volume, and empty core radius of $i$ metal ($i = \mathrm{A}$ or $\mathrm{B}$). The difference between equation (19) and equation (20) is left as a problem for the future and it is desirable to seek an adequate form for the alloy potential. We have presented here the results of the pseudo alloy atom model, merely as the first step.

$$
v_{\mathrm{p}}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(q)=\frac{v_{i}^{\mathrm{PAA}}(q)}{\epsilon(q)}. \tag{21}
$$

These values should predict other extensive properties of concentrated disordered alloys.

## 4. Conclusion
The pseudo alloy atom model was applied to the disordered Cs-K alloys. Total binding energies and the bulk moduli were calculated as a function of alloy compositions over the full composition range from pure Cs to pure K. The calculated values of total binding energies showed a good agreement with experiments. Calculated heats of solution were positive in both Cs-K and Cs-Rb alloy systems in an accord with experiments. Further- more, it was found that the determined empty core radii vary linearly with the alloy composition. These results suggest that the pseudo alloy atom model is suitable for the description of disordered alloys and that the metallic cohesion of alkali metals and their alloys can be systematically treated in the present model.
