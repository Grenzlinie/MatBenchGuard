The expression for the Raman frequency has been modified taking into account many-body interactions and thermal phonon pressure. Modified expressions of the second-order elastic constants have then been used to evaluate the parameters appearing in the expression of the Raman frequency. The results are found to be in agreement with the experiment.

THE LUNDQVIST three-body potential has exten- sively been used to explain elastic and dielectric behaviour of ionic solids [1-5]. The Cauchy discrep- ancy in centro-symmetric ionic solids [6] is well explained if many body interactions are incorporated in the Born model. The application of this model to ZnS semiconducting and antifluorite type covalent crystals [7] does not yield equally good results primarily because the authors have not taken into account angular forces arising due to covalency.

Anastassakis and Hawranek [8] have successfully applied an angular force model to $Mg_{2} X$ type antifluorite solids. However, the model does not yield equally good results for $CaF_{2}, SrF_{2}$ and $BaF_{2}$ solids which lie high on the Phillip's ionicity scale. Covalency appears to be an important factor for the applicability of the model. It has already been reported by Bedi and Verma [10] that sublattice displacement and many body interactions contribute significantly to the Cauchy discrepancy in these type of solids. The temperature dependence of $CaF_{2}$ and $BaF_{2}$ solids have been studied by Rao [9] taking into account the central two body potential which even fails to explain the Cauchy deviations in the second- and third-order elastic constants, ignoring the many body interactions. The Cauchy deviations in the fluorite solids results from the thermal phonon pressure (TPP), three body interactions (TBI) and sublattice displacement. The experimental data on the elastic constant as well as the Raman frequency [10] is not well explainable if TPP is not taken into account. This is primarily due to the reason that the authors have derived the expressions at 0K while the input data used for calculations of the elastic constants and the Raman frequency refers to the room temperature.

In the present work we have improved upon our own reported results of second order elastic constants and principal Raman frequency $(W_{R})$ using the Lundqvist potential incorporating thermal phonon pressure and three body interactions.

The lattice energy per unit cell for $CaF_{2}$ type solids using Lundqvist potential is given by
$$\phi=\frac{e^{2} \alpha_{m}}{2 r} Z_{2}\left[Z_{1}+8 f(r)\right]+8 V_{1}(r)+6 V_{2}(r), \quad(1)$$
where $V_{1}(r)$ and $V_{2}(r)$ are the repulsive energy terms due to first and second neighbours respectively, $f(r)$ is the exchange charge parameter which determines the amount of charge transferred from the negative to the positive ion and $\alpha_{m}$ is the Madelung constant.

The repulsive potentials are defined as [11]
$$V_{1}(r)=b\left(1+\frac{Z_{1}}{n_{1}}+\frac{Z_{2}}{n_{2}}\right) \exp \left(\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}-r}{\rho}\right), \quad(2)$$

$$\begin{aligned}
V_{2}(r)= & b\left[\left(1+\frac{2 Z_{1}}{n_{1}}\right) \exp \left(\frac{2 r_{1}}{\rho}\right)\right. \\
& \left.+\left(1+\frac{2 Z_{2}}{n_{2}}\right) \exp \left(\frac{2 r_{2}}{\rho}\right)\right] \exp \left(-\frac{r}{\rho}\right), \quad(3)
\end{aligned}$$

where $b$ and $\rho$ are the strength and the hardness parameters; $r_1$, $Z_1$ and $n_1$ are the ionic radius, valency and number of valence electrons in the outermost shell of the positive ion, respectively. Similarly, $r_2$, $Z_2$ and $n_2$ refer to the negative ion.

The expressions for the second-order elastic constants derived using the homogeneous deformation technique [12] taking into account the thermal contributions and TBI in the framework of the Hildebrand approximation [18] are

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{11}= & 1.5731 T \beta B_{T}+\alpha^{-1}\left[-0.9065 B_{1}-1.5731 B_{2}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{A_{1}}{3}+A_{2}+20.6874 f^{\prime}\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{12}= & -1.7865 T \beta B_{T}+\alpha^{-1}\left[1.4532 B_{1}+1.7865 B_{2}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{A_{1}}{3}+20.6874 f^{\prime}\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{44}= & (-0.7865+2.5928 \Omega) T \beta B_{T} \\
& +\alpha^{-1}\left[1.4532 B_{1}+1.7865 B_{2}+\frac{A_{1}}{3}\right. \\
& \left.-\frac{\Omega}{3}\left(A_{1}+6.7783 B_{1}+7.7783 B_{2}\right)\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\Omega=\frac{-7.7783 \alpha T \beta B_{T}+A_{1}+6.7783 B_{1}+7.7783 B_{2}}{\left(A_{1}+2 B_{1}\right)+3\left(A_{2}+2 B_{2}\right)},
$$

$$
\alpha=\frac{4 a^{4}}{e^{2}},
$$

$$
\left.\begin{array}{rl}
A_{1} & =\frac{4 a^{3}}{e^{2}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} V_{1}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right]_{r=a \sqrt{3} / 2}, \\
B_{1} & =\frac{4 a^{3}}{e^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} V_{1}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}\right]_{r=a \sqrt{3} / 2}, \\
\text { and } & \\
A_{2} & =\frac{4 a^{3}}{e^{2}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} V_{2}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right]_{r=a}, \\
B_{2} & =\frac{4 a^{3}}{e^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} V_{2}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}\right]_{r=a},
\end{array}\right\}
$$

where $\beta$ is the coefficient of thermal expansion and $B_{T}=\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right) / 3$ is the bulk modulus.

The principal Raman frequency in $\mathrm{CaF}_{2}$ solids arises due to oscillations of one fluorine atom against another and is given by

$$
W_{R}^{2}=-\lim _{K \rightarrow 0} \frac{D_{11}(22, K)-D_{11}(24, K)}{m_{F}},
$$

where $D_{11}(22, K)$ and $D_{11}(24, K)$ are the elements of dynamical matrix designated by the suffixes and $m_{F}$ is the mass of the fluorine atom. The coupling coefficients are the same as defined by Kellerman [13]. It is found that the coupling coefficients, corresponding to the repulsive and three body interactions will contribute to the Raman frequency. The repulsive coupling coefficients are

$$
R\left[\begin{array}{ll}
1 & 2 \\
\alpha & \alpha
\end{array}\right]_{K=0}=\frac{e^{2}}{2 a^{3}}\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(A_{1}+2 B_{1}\right)\right],
$$

and

$$
R\left[\begin{array}{ll}
2 & 4 \\
\alpha & \alpha
\end{array}\right]_{K=0}=\frac{e^{2}}{2 a^{3}}\left[\left(A_{2}+2 B_{2}\right)\right].
$$

Adding equations (9) and (10), we get

$$
R\left[\begin{array}{ll}
2 & 2 \\
\alpha & \alpha
\end{array}\right]_{K=0}=-\frac{e^{2}}{2 a^{3}}\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(A_{1}+2 B_{1}\right)+\left(A_{2}+2 B_{2}\right)\right].
$$

Adding the coupling coefficients for the TBI, the expression for the Raman frequency which contains a separate three body term in addition to the modified short range repulsive parameters can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{R}^{2}= & \frac{e^{2}}{3 a^{3}}\left[A_{1}+2 B_{1}+3\left(A_{2}+2 B_{2}\right)+16 \pi Z_{2}\left(r \frac{\mathrm{d} f}{\mathrm{~d} r}\right)\right] \\
& \times \frac{1}{m_{F}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The values of the short range parameters $A_{i}, B_{i}$ are determined using equations (2), (3) and (7) which gives

$$
A_{1}=-\frac{a \sqrt{3}}{2 \rho} B_{1} ; \quad A_{2}=-\frac{a}{\rho} B_{2}
$$

and

$$
\frac{B_{2}}{B_{1}}=\sqrt{3} \frac{\exp \left[\left(2 r_{2}-a\right) / \rho\right]}{\exp \left[\left(r_{1}+r_{2}-r\right) / \rho\right]}.
$$

It is evident that the present results for the Raman frequency calculated taking thermal phonon pressure and three body interactions into account are in much better agreement with the experiment. Dasgupta *et al.* [14] have obtained the expressions for the third order elastic constants using Srinivasan's method for the evaluation of the Coulomb part and the homogeneous deformation technique for three body interactions.

In the present work the polarizability of the ions has been neglected. Agarwal and Verma [19] have observed that the polarizability does not play any significant role in the evaluation of the second-, third- and higher-order elastic constants in the case of alkali halides. The authors [15] have made lattice dynamical calculations assuming the negative ion to be polarisable.We have [16] also recently reported the expressions for the second- and third-order elastic constants using the TBI and TPP, assuming the ions to be polarisable in the case of the fluorite solids and have observed that TBI contribute more significantly to the elastic constants than the polarisability. The present study also highlights that the TBI and TPP contribute significantly to the Raman frequency in the fluorite solids.
