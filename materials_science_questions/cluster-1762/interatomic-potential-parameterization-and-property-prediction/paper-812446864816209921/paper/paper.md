# EFFECT OF THE PARTIAL COVALENCY ON THE SOLUBILITY OF AgBr IN $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$-AgI

H. TAKAHASHI and S. TAMAKAKI
Department of Physics, Faculty of Science, Niigata University, Niigata 950-21, Japan

Received 28 August 1984
Revised manuscript received 2 November 1984

The internal energy and the free energy of $\alpha$-AgI-type solid solution have been calculated. On the basis of this calculation, the solubility limit of AgBr in $\alpha$-AgI has been discussed. The difference of the solubility of AgBr in $\beta$-AgI and of $\beta$-AgI in AgBr has been interpreted due to the variation of the vibrational entropy depending on the mean value of anion mass.
The partial ionicity in these solid solutions has also been discussed.

## 1. Introduction

It is well known that $\alpha$-AgI is a typical superionic conductor of silver ions which distribute statistically over the tetrahedral sites around the body centered arrangement of iodine ions, whereas AgBr has a NaCl structure and does not exhibit superionic conduction. It is of interest to investigate the limiting composition for the superionic conductor in connection with the discussion of the stability of superionic conducting phase by adding AgBr into $\alpha$-AgI.

In a previous paper [1] the phase diagram and the heat of transformation from $\beta$ (normal ionic)- to $\alpha$(superionic)-phase of the AgI-AgBr system have been determined. And furthermore, from the thermodynamic point of view, we have discussed the phase equilibria between $\beta$- and $\alpha$-phase and have concluded that the correlation among the silver ions in the silver conducting phase varies with the substituted bromine ion concentration. For convenience, the phase diagram of AgI-AgBr system obtained is shown in fig. 1.

![](./images/812446864816209921_1.jpg)
Fig. 1. Phase diagram of AgI-AgBr system. Broken curve; virtual curve of two phase region in $\beta$-phase.

In this paper, we will calculate the internal energy and the free energy of $\alpha$-AgI-type solid solution and will discuss about the solubility limit (limiting composition for superionic conductor) of AgBr in $\alpha$-AgI and also discuss the change of the partial ionicity as a function of temperature and composition.

In the following section we will estimate the internal energy $U$ and the vibrational free energy $F_{\text{vib}}$ as a function of temperature $T$ and of the mole fraction $x$ of AgBr.

The constituent atoms of AgI and AgBr are regarded as the polarizable ions.

In addition, we assume that the hardness parameters and the polarizabilities of individual ions are not affected by the change of the component ratio of surrounding anions. Therefore all the terms in eq. (1) that do not contain the effective valence number $z_{i}$ are simply expressed as the weighted average of the pure components. We take the first and the second nearest neighbouring ions into account as the short range repulsive interaction and the point charge-dipole interaction (the second and the third term in eq. (1)). The dipole-dipole and the dipole-quadrupole interaction (the fourth and the fifth term in eq. (1)) of the unlike charged ions locating at the first nearest neighbouring sites and of the negatively charged ions locating at the second neighbouring sites are also included.

In their treatment, however, the electronic polarizabilities of cation and anion could not be assigned uniquely. So the value obtained by Tessman et al. [5] is employed as the electronic polarizability of silver ion. The Van der Waals coefficients of AgI and AgBr have been already defined by Mayer [6]. The values we obtained are somewhat larger than those of Mayer. Since AgBr has the NaCl structure, it seems that the covalent bonding nature is very small and therefore we take $A=0$ for simplicity. The number of remaining parameters to be determined are three $b$, $n$ and $z_{i}$. The remaining parameters are, therefore, determined by using three thermodynamic quantities as the cohesive energy [8], the thermal expansion coefficient [9] and the isothermal compressibility [10]. The thermal expansion coefficient and the isothermal compressibility are combined with the derivatives of the cohesive energy by the following equations, which are called as Hildebrand equation of state [11],

$$
\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right)_{T}=T \frac{\lambda}{\gamma}-P, \tag{2}
$$

$$
\left(\frac{\partial^{2} U}{\partial V^{2}}\right)_{T}=\frac{1}{V \gamma}\left[1+T \frac{\lambda}{\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial P}\right)_{T}+\frac{T}{\gamma}\left(\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial T}\right)_{P}\right], \tag{3}
$$

where $\lambda$ is the thermal expansion coefficient and $\gamma$ is the isothermal compressibility. For simplicity, the second term and the third term in the brackets of the right-hand side of eq. (3) is ignored as usual [11]. Thus the determined values of three parameters are as follows, $z_{i}=z_{j}=0.92$, $b=2.63 × 10^{-20}$ and $n=6.9$. The obtained pair potentials of AgBr are shown in fig. 2.

### 2.2. Pair potential of AgI
The parameters in the potential in the low temperature phase of AgI (=$\beta$-phase) are also assigned by utilising the similar procedure described above.

The Madelung constant $M$ of $\alpha$-AgI is not known and it can be decided as follows: Assuming that the parameters in the potential do not change through the $\beta-\alpha$ phase transition, only the structural change was considered. From the studies of X-ray diffractions [12], the most probable distance between the nearest neighbouring silver ions is about $4.0$ Å. Using this value and taking into account the heat of transformation [1] from $\beta$- to $\alpha$-phase, the Madelung constant was adjusted to coincide with the cohesive energy of $\alpha$-phase The obtained values are $z=0.75$, $M=1.574$, $n=10.9$, $b=7.14 × 10^{-21}$ and $A=5.44 × 10^{-29}$, respectively. The value of $z$ obtained in the present calculation is a little bit bigger than that of Vashishta and Rahman [13]. This discrepancy may be caused by taking a different form in the expression of the pair potential of AgI.

Making use of these potential parameters and the Hildebrand equation of state, it is possible to estimate the thermal expansion and the isothermal compressibility in $\alpha$-phase. The estimated value of the thermal expansion coefficient is $4.9 × 10^{-5}$/K, which is about one third as much as that of the experimental value of $\alpha$-AgI [14]. The evaluated isothermal compressibility has the value of $1.4 × 10^{-12}$ cm²/dyn. The calculation indicates that the isothermal compressibility in $\alpha$-phase is greater than that in $\beta$-phase, although the experimental value of isothermal compressibility in $\alpha$-phase is not known.

It is interesting that the Madelung constant calculated in such a way is smaller than that of the wurzite structure (= 1.64132).

The estimated pair potentials of $\alpha$-AgI are shown in fig. 3. It is interesting that the pair potential of $\mathrm{I}^{-}-\mathrm{I}^{-}$ ions has a steep increase at a distance of about $3$ Å, which is caused by the relatively large value of the electronic polarizability of the iodine ions.

The effective valence number $z$ of silver ion in the solid solution may increase with increase of the component of AgBr and its value is written as follows:

$$
z=p x+0.75, \tag{4}
$$

![](./images/812446864816209921_3.jpg)

Fig. 3. Interionic potential of like ions and of unlike ions of AgI.

where $p$ is an adjustable parameter dependent on temperature. Thus the internal energy of $\alpha$-AgI-type solid solution can be evaluated.

## 3. Free energy calculation

The free energy of a solid solution of $\operatorname{Ag}\left(\mathrm{Br}_{x} \mathrm{I}_{1-x}\right)$ can be written as
$$
F=U+F_{\text {vib }}-T S_{\text {conf }}, \quad(5)
$$
where $U$ is the energy of the static lattice, $F_{\text {vib }}$ is the vibrational free energy of the system and $S_{\text {conf }}$ is the configurational entropy of the substituting anion species, which is expressed as follows if the substitution occurs at random:
$$
S_{\text {conf }}=-R\{x \ln x+(1-x) \ln (1-x)\}. \quad(6)
$$

### 3.1. Vibrational free energy

The vibrational free energy of a crystal may be written,
$$
F_{\mathrm{vib}}=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{s} \hbar \omega_{s}+k_{\mathrm{B}} T \sum_{s} \ln \left[1-\exp \left(-\hbar \omega_{s} / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\right)\right],
$$
where $\omega_{s}$ is the angular frequency of mode $s$. In the high-temperature limit, above formula is expanded into [15]
$$
F_{\mathrm{vib}}=k_{\mathrm{B}} T \sum_{s}\left\{\ln \left(\frac{\hbar \omega_{s}}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}\right)+\frac{1}{24}\left(\frac{\hbar \omega_{s}}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}\right)^{2} \cdot \cdot \cdot \quad \cdot \quad(8)\right.
$$

Since the real density of state of $\alpha$-AgI is very complicated [16], it is inappropriate to apply the Debye approximation for the above calculation. However we are not interested in the absolute value of the free energy but interested in the composition dependence of the free energy. So the Debye approximation is applicable for the calculation of the vibrational free energy. In the Debye approximation, the density of state can be represented by [17]:
$$
g(\omega)=\frac{3 V}{2 \pi^{2} u^{3}} \omega^{2}. \quad(9)
$$
where $V$ is the volume of the crystal and $u$ is the sound velocity. From the normalizing condition, the Debye frequency $\omega_{\mathrm{D}}$ can be written
$$
\omega_{\mathrm{D}}=\left(\frac{12 \pi^{2} N}{V}\right)^{1 / 3} u. \quad(10)
$$

The sound velocity $u$ is represented in the linear diatomic chain,
$$
u=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d} \omega_{1}}{\mathrm{~d} q}\right|_{q=0}. \quad(11)
$$

In the case of $q \ll 1$, we have
$$
\omega_{1} \simeq\left[\frac{f(x)}{2\left(m_{\mathrm{Ag}}+x m_{\mathrm{Br}}+(1-x) m_{\mathrm{I}}\right)}\right]^{1 / 2} a q. \quad(12)
$$
where $f(x)$ is the harmonic force constant, $a$ is the lattice constant and $m_{\mathrm{Ag}}, m_{\mathrm{I}}$ and $m_{\mathrm{Br}}$ are the mass of a mole of silver ions, iodine ions and bromine ions, respectively. Thus the sound velocity $u$, or the Debye frequency $\omega_{\mathrm{D}}$ is formulated as a function of the mass of the constituent ions and the harmonic force constant. Accordingly the composition dependence of the vibrational free energy can be allowed. The resulting form of the vibrational free energy of the solid solution is then, expressed as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{vib}}= & \frac{K}{T} \frac{f(x)}{2\left[m_{\mathrm{Ag}}+x m_{\mathrm{Br}}+(1-x) m_{\mathrm{I}}\right]} \\
& +6 N k_{\mathrm{B}} T\left\{\ln \left[\left(\frac{12 \pi^{2} \hbar^{3} N}{k_{\mathrm{B}}^{3} T^{3} V}\right)^{1 / 3} u(x)\right]-\frac{1}{3}\right\},(13)
\end{aligned}
$$

![](./images/812446864816209921_4.jpg)

Fig. 4. Schematic diagram of the relation between the Gibbs free energy of the solid solution and the phase separation.

where
$$
K=\frac{3}{40}\left(\frac{6 \pi^{2}}{V}\right)^{2 / 3}(2 N)^{5 / 3} \frac{\hbar^{2} a^{2}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}} \tag{14}
$$

and
$$
u(x)=\left\{\frac{f(x)}{2\left[m_{\mathrm{Ag}}+x m_{\mathrm{Br}}+(1-x) m_{\mathrm{I}}\right]}\right\}^{1 / 2} a. \quad(15)
$$

As shown in fig. 4, the solubility limit of AgBr in $\alpha$-AgI at a given temperature corresponds to the minimum of the free energy of $\alpha$-AgI-type solid solution. Strictly speaking, in the phase separation the two co-existing phases must have the compositions given by the point of contact of common tangent to the free energy of $\alpha$-AgI and AgBr.

At a certain temperature, the solubility limit occurs at the composition that the differentiation of the free energy of $\alpha$-AgI($\partial F / \partial x$) is equal to zero in respect to the fraction $x$. The term $\partial F / \partial x$ can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}= & \frac{\partial U}{\partial x}+\frac{6 N k_{\mathrm{B}} T\left(m_{\mathrm{I}}-m_{\mathrm{Br}}\right)}{\left\{2\left[m_{\mathrm{Ag}}+(1-x) m_{\mathrm{I}}+x m_{\mathrm{Br}}\right]\right\}^{2}} \\
& +N k_{\mathrm{B}} T \ln \frac{x}{1-x} \\
& +\frac{K}{T}\left[\frac{2\left(m_{\mathrm{I}}-m_{\mathrm{Br}}\right) f(x)}{\left[2\left\{m_{\mathrm{Ag}}+(1-x) m_{\mathrm{I}}+x m_{\mathrm{Br}}\right\}\right]^{2}}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{\mathrm{d} f(x) / \mathrm{d} x}{2\left\{m_{\mathrm{Ag}}+(1-x) m_{\mathrm{I}}+x m_{\mathrm{Br}}\right\}}\right] \\
& +3 N k_{\mathrm{B}} T \frac{\mathrm{d} f(x) / \mathrm{d} x}{f(x)}. \tag{16}
\end{aligned}
$$

## 4. Result and discussion

In the preceding section, the expression for the free energy of $\alpha$-AgI-type solid solution was determined. The fourth and the fifth terms in eq. (16) tend to be cancelled out and the resulting order of magnitude of its sum is small compared with the other terms. So these two terms in eq. (16) are not taken into account in the subsequent calculation.

![](./images/812446864816209921_5.jpg)

Fig. 5. Composition dependence of the differentiation of the vibrational and configurational entropy.

![](./images/812446864816209921_6.jpg)

Fig. 6. Composition and the temperature dependences of the effective valence number.

The second term in eq. (16) comes from the vibra- tional entropy. Fig. 5 shows $-T(\partial / \partial x)(S_{vib}+S_{conf})$ . The curve intersects the abscissa at about $x=0.35$ . This means that the compositions of the phase separa- tion are close to 0.35 in the temperature range in which the entropy term is predominant. This trend agrees with the virtual curve of the two phase region in $\beta$ -phase as shown in fig. 1.

Furthermore the parameter $p$ in eq. (4) has been determined so as to hold $\partial F / \partial x$ equal to zero at each set of temperature and composition in $\alpha$ -phase. The calculated effective valence number $z$ is shown in fig. 6. It is likely that $z$ slightly increases with increas ing temperature on the same compositions as seen in fig. 6. Although the temperature dependence of the effective valence number seems to be small, it should be regarded as significant, because the effective va- lence number contributes to the predominant term in the cohesive energy.

The effective valence number $z$ or the effective charge $(z e)$ is regarded as a measure of the tendency of ionic character. Then the ionicity may increase with increasing temperature in $\alpha$ -AgI. The experim ental result of magnetic susceptibility indicates [18] that the paramagnetic contribution decreases and approaches to the diamagnetic susceptibility of free ions with increasing temperature in $\alpha$ -AgI. This fact supports the above inference.

![](./images/812446864816209921_7.jpg)

Fig. 7. The temperature dependence of the ionic conductivity of AgI.

The ratio of the bond bending force to the bond stretching force decreases with increasing ionicity [19]. Therefore it is supposed that the change of ion- icity affects the conduction mechanism of silver ions. Funke has suggested [20] that the ionic conductivity of $\alpha$ -AgI can be described by the itinerant oscillation model [21] above about $250^{\circ} C$ . Fig. 7 shows the curve of ionic conductivity $\sigma$ against $1 / T$ , which is character istic of the itinerant oscillation model. On the basis of the small polaron model Hinkelman and Huberman[24] have concluded that $\alpha$ -AgI has different conduc tion mechanisms in the high and low temperature re- gions. The structure of $\alpha$ -AgI may also be affected to a minor extent in this situation.

It is considered that the variation of binding energy with temperature is not so large, because the specific heat of $\alpha$ -AgI [25] does not exhibit the pronounced thermal anomaly.

It is expected that the physical properties of the superionic conductor, $\alpha$ -AgI, will be more comprehen sible, if the temperature dependence of the interionic interaction can be taken into account quantitatively.
