# Conductivity enhancement mechanism of interstitial-type $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$conductor, $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{~B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}(0 \leq x \leq 0.7)$

Yuria Saito, Kazuaki Ado, Takashi Asai, Hiroyuki Kageyama and Osamu Nakamura

Government Industrial Research Institute, Osaka, Midorigaoka 1-8, Ikeda, Osaka 563, Japan

Received 20 March 1991; accepted for publication 1 April 1991

An electrical conductivity of $\sigma_{100^{\circ} \mathrm{C}}=4.5 \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ in $\mathrm{Li}_{4.5} \mathrm{~B}_{0.5} \mathrm{Si}_{0.5} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ was observed, which is more than three orders of magnitude higher than that of $\mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}$. This enhancement is due to an increase of both the ionic hopping rate and the mobile-ion concentration. An expansion of the crystallographic unit-cell volume of $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{~B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}(0 \leq x \leq 0.5)$ was observed with increasing $x$. A simple point-charge model calculation shows that the nearest-neighbor $\mathrm{Li}^{+}-\mathrm{Li}^{+}$repulsive force mainly contributes to the expansion of each axis in the unit cell. The conductivity and only the $b$-axis length among the crystal lattice parameter decreases with $x$ beyond $x=0.5$. This result may be interpreted by the application of an $\mathrm{BO}_{4}{ }^{5-}+\mathrm{Li}^{+} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{BO}_{3}{ }^{3-}+\mathrm{LiO}^{-}$equilibrium model.

## 1. Introduction

Many solid-state $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$-ion conductors have been extensively studied as solid electrolytes in batteries and electrochemical devices. However, low conductivity or instability makes the developed $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$-ion electrolytes difficult to apply for high-rate batteries.

We have investigated $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$-ion conductors with high conductivity for use as the solid electrolyte in practical lithium batteries [1]. In the system of $\mathrm{Li}_{4.2} \mathrm{M}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$, where $\mathrm{M}$ is $\mathrm{B}^{3+}, \mathrm{Al}^{3+}, \mathrm{Ga}^{3+}, \mathrm{Cr}^{3+}$, $\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}, \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}, \mathrm{Co}^{2+}$, we have discussed the relationship between ionic radius of the substituted ions and either the conductivity or the lattice constants. Maximum conductivity has been observed in the $\mathrm{Al}^{3+}$ substituted compound.

Among these compounds, the $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$-substituted compound, $\mathrm{Li}_{4.2} \mathrm{~B}_{0.2} \mathrm{Si}_{0.8} \mathrm{O}_{4}$, showed the highest signal to noise (S/N) ratio and no extra peaks in the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern. This suggests a greater $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$ solubility limit with the occupation of a large amount of interstitial mobile $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$ions, indicating higher conductivity. In this paper, we discuss the changes of the conductivity and the crystal lattice parameters as a function of $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$ content in the $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{~B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ system.

## 2. Experimental

The compounds $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{~B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ were prepared with $x$ varying in steps $\Delta x$ of 0.1 by a conventional solid-state reaction with the starting materials $\mathrm{Li}_{2} \mathrm{CO}_{3}, \mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{BO}_{3}$. All mixtures with each stoichiometric ratio corresponding to a selected composition were sintered at $800^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ for $10 \mathrm{~h}$ and confirmed by XRD patterns to be solid solutions. S/ $\mathrm{N}$ ratios were evaluated by the peak height of the (200) peak against the noise level. Lattice constants were refined with the least-squares method from the observed peaks $(\sin \theta / \lambda<0.562)$ based on the structural results of Tranqui et al. [2].

Sample powder was pressed into a pellet with a pressure of $255 \mathrm{MPa}$ in a $10 \mathrm{~mm}$ diameter steel die and was sintered at $800^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ for two hours. Gold electrodes were formed from an Engelhard A-3360, which was decomposed and hardened by raising the temperature to $600^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ over a period of $20 \mathrm{~min}$.

The ac conductance was measured by inspection of the complex admittance plot obtained with a Hewlett-Packard 4192A in the frequency range of 5 $\mathrm{Hz}$ to $500 \mathrm{kHz}$.

## 3. Results and discussion

In a previous report, we have shown that trivalent and divalent substituted compounds $\mathrm{Li}_{4.2} \mathrm{M}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ have larger crystal lattice parameters than that of original $\mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}$ [1]. This arises from the extra $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$ ions and the $\mathrm{MO}_{4}$-tetrahedron expansions for $\mathrm{M}$ cations with a larger ionic radius than that of $\mathrm{Si}^{4+}$. On the other hand, the smaller $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$ ion substitution for $\mathrm{Si}^{4+}$ also increases the lattice volume; but this is only attributed to the increase of extra $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$ions.

### 3.1. Solubility limit of $B^{3+}$ in the system
$\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$

The XRD S/N ratio and melting point of the $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ compounds decreased gradually with the increase of $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$ content. Significant extra peaks assigned to $\mathrm{Li}_{2} \mathrm{CO}_{3}$ appeared for larger $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$ contents ($x>0.5$). The peak intensity grows on standing for a few months at room temperature, which suggests a reaction with $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ in air. This deduction suggests, in turn, that the $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ system may be a good $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$ionic conductor. The solubility limit of $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$ in $\mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}$ was found to be $x=0.7$; the mixture with $x=0.8$ gave a different phase. This value is quite a bit larger than the limiting composition of other substituents, e.g. $x=0.4$ for the $\mathrm{Al}^{3+}$-substituted system.

### 3.2. Conductivity of solid solution $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$

The ionic conductivities of several compositions are shown in fig. 1 in Arrhenius format. An apparent change in slope (activation energy) was not observed in any of the compounds up to $450^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ in contrast to the results of Jackowska and West [3], who reported a change in slope at $470^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ for $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{Al}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ ($x=0.1,0.2$), and of Shannon et al. [4], who reported a change at $180^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ for $\mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}$.

All the solid solutions have higher conductivity than $\mathrm{Li}_{4} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}$. The conductivity reaches a maximum for the composition with $x=0.5$. The activation energy, $E_{\mathrm{a}}$, extracted from the Arrhenius plot of conductivity also reaches a minimum at this composition.

It is helpful to understand the origin of the aboveconductivity enhancement with the decrease of activation energy. We, therefore, tried to estimate the carrier concentration and hopping rate of the $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$ions from the frequency dependence of the ac conductivity following this procedure of Almond et al. [5,6].

![](./images/812446625644412929_2.jpg)

Fig. 1. Temperature dependences of the electrical conductivity of $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x} \mathrm{B}_{x} \mathrm{Si}_{1-x} \mathrm{O}_{4}$. $x$ is marked on each.

The ac conductivity $\sigma(\omega)$ varies with angular frequency $\omega$ following the power law,
$$
\sigma(\omega)=\sigma(0)+A \omega^{n}, \quad(1)
$$
where $\sigma(0)=K \omega_{p}$ is the dc conductivity and $A=K \omega_{p}^{1-n}$ [7] is a temperature-dependent parameter; $n$ takes a value between 0 and $1, K$ is a factor proportional to the carrier concentration $c$, and $\omega_{p}$ is the hopping rate of ions. Conductivity data as a function of frequency at several temperatures for $\mathrm{Li}_{4.5} \mathrm{B}_{0.5} \mathrm{Si}_{0.5} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ are shown in fig. 2. The solid lines were obtained by least square fits. The values of $\sigma(0)$ and $A$ used for these fits are shown in fig. 3 with the conventional Arrhenius format. In each case, the activation energies of $\sigma(0)$ and $A$ were in the ratio of $1:(1-n)$. This result means that both the activation energies of $\sigma(0)$ and $A$ are consistent with that of the hopping rate,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_{p} & =\omega_{0} \exp \left(-\Delta G_{\mathrm{a}} / k T\right) \\
& =\omega_{\mathrm{c}} \exp \left(-E_{\mathrm{a}} / k T\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

Fig. 2. Conductivity data for $Li_{4.5}B_{0.5}Si_{0.5}O_4$ at a selection of temperatures. Solid curves are fits of eq. (1) to the experimental data.

![](./images/812446625644412929_4.jpg)

Fig. 3. Arrhenius plots of $\sigma(0)T$ and $AT$.

where $\omega_{\mathrm{e}}$ is an effective attempt frequency and $E_{\mathrm{a}}$ is the activation energy of the hopping process; it also means that the concentration $c$ of mobile ions involved in $K$ is independent of temperature. The hopping rates $\omega_{p}$ calculated from the same data are shown in fig. 4. The values of the activation energy were the same within experimental error as those found for $\sigma(0)$ in fig. 3. This also indicates that the thermal activations of these conductivities are caused by those of the hopping attempt alone. The effective attempt frequencies $\omega_{\mathrm{e}}$ obtained from the infinite temperature intercepts were $3\times 10^{14}$ and $2\times 10^{14}$ Hz for $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{SiO}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{4.5}\mathrm{B}_{0.5}\mathrm{Si}_{0.5}\mathrm{O}_{4}$, respectively. These frequencies reproduce the corresponding vibrational frequency, $\sim 10^{13}$ Hz, fairly well. Therefore, the treatment for ac conductivity proposed by Almond et al. is acceptable for this system. Moreover, the temperature independence of carrier concentration was proved for all of $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x}\mathrm{B}_{x}\mathrm{Si}_{1-x}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ compounds.

![](./images/812446625644412929_5.jpg)

Fig. 4. Arrhenius plots of ion hopping rates, $\omega_{p}$ for (a) $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{SiO}_{4}$ and (b) $\mathrm{Li}_{4.5}\mathrm{B}_{0.5}\mathrm{Si}_{0.5}\mathrm{O}_{4}$.

Fig. 5 shows the $c$ and $\omega_{p}$ at $100^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ as a function of $\mathrm{B}^{3+}$ content $x$. The maxima of $c$ and $\omega_{p}$ were observed at $x\sim 0.5$, similar to the case of ionic conductivity. The increase in $c$ is about one order of magnitude, whereas the increase in $\omega_{p}$ at $100^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is more than two orders of magnitude compared with that of $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{SiO}_{4}$. Therefore, the observed conductivity enhancement of $\mathrm{Li}_{4+x}\mathrm{B}_{x}\mathrm{Si}_{1-x}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ at $100^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is mainly due to the increase of hopping rate.

### 3.3. Changes of lattice parameters and the $\boldsymbol{Li}^{+}$-ion conduction mechanism in $\boldsymbol{Li}_{4+x}\boldsymbol{B}_{x}\boldsymbol{Si}_{1-x}\boldsymbol{O}_{4}$ solid solutions

Fig. 6 shows the observed lattice parameters as a function of $x$. The crystallographic $a$- and $c$-axes and the cell volume, $V$, increase with $x$, whereas the $b$-axis length shows a unique $x$ dependence, i.e., the

![](./images/812446625644412929_6.jpg)

Fig. 5. Change of (a) carrier concentration and (b) hopping rate with activation energy as a function of $x$ at $100^\circ$C in $\mathrm{Li_{4+x}B_xSi_{1-x}O_4}$.

rate of increase is twice as great as those of the $a$- and $c$-axis lengths until $x=0.5$, and gradually decreases with a further increase of $x$. This dependence, with a maximum value at $x=0.5$, is similar to that of the conductivity. We describe the $x$ dependence of crystal structure and conductivity to discuss the $\mathrm{Li^+}$-ion conduction mechanism.

$\mathrm{Li_4SiO_4}$ crystallizes in the monoclinic space group $\mathrm{P2_1/m}$ with $a=11.564$, $b=6.090$, $c=16.645$ Å, $\beta=99.5^\circ$ and $Z=14[2]$. The Li atoms are ordered and occupy 19 of 42 sites in the sevenfold supercell (fig. 7) as compared with the cell of Vollenkle et al. [8]. The $\mathrm{LiO_4}$, $\mathrm{LiO_5}$ and $\mathrm{LiO_6}$ polyhedra are linked by edge- and corner-sharing. There is good three-dimensional connectivity between Li sites with distances of less than 2.9 Å. The ordered nature of $\mathrm{Li_4SiO_4}$ is consistent with the low ionic conductivity, whereas the good conductivity of $\mathrm{B^{3+}}$-substituted $\mathrm{Li_{4+x}B_xSi_{1-x}O_4}$ is consistent with the good Li-site connectivity and the partial occupation of Li interstitials ($\mathrm{Li^+}$ ion disordered state), which results in the expansion of the cell volume with an increase of $x$ in the range $x<0.5$. Our experimental conductivity data show that the increase of $x$ is attributable to the increase of mobile ion content and the hopping rate, as shown in the previous section 3.2.

![](./images/812446625644412929_7.jpg)

Fig. 6. Lattice constants (monoclinic unit) for the $\mathrm{Li_{4+x}B_xSi_{1-x}O_4}$ systems. Broken lines are fits of the parameters in eq. (4) to the experimental data.

Once any Li interstitial sites, which were first introduced by Vollenkle et al. [8], are occupied by $\mathrm{Li^+}$ ions (model A), an extremely short $\mathrm{Li^+{-}Li^+}$ distances ($\sim1$ Å) are unavoidable. The summation was carried out over all the $\mathrm{Li^+{-}Li^+}$ repulsive forces as well as $\mathrm{Li^+{-}O^{2-}}$ ($\sim2$ Å) attractive and $\mathrm{Li^+{-}Si^{4+}}$($\sim3.5$ Å) repulsive forces. Hence, the total Coulomb force, $F$, can be described by

![](./images/812446625644412929_8.jpg)

Fig. 7. Projection along b axis of all Li atoms (O) and all Li available sites (□) for the interstitially occupied Li atoms. Open circles and squares are at y=1/4 or 3/4; shaded circles and squares are at y=0 and 1/2. Possible Li-Li distances are indicated, representatively.

$$
F=k \sum \frac{q_{i} q_{j}}{r^{2}}, \tag{3}
$$

where k is a constant, $q_{i}$ and $q_{j}$ are point charges of individual atoms, and r is the interatomic distance.
As a result of the above F calculation, the local at- mosphere at Li sites was repulsive. If we assume the repulsive force makes an expansion of cell volume, the relative expansion rates along individual direc- tions could be described as

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\Delta a \\
\Delta b \\
\Delta c
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0.62 \\
1 \\
0.49
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4}
$$

This is consistent with the anisotropy of experimen- tal results, i.e. a unique increasing rate of the b axis. Broken lines in fig. 6 show that the above calculated results fit the experimental ones in the region 0<x<0.5. The strong anisotropy of the total repul- sive force mainly arises from the $Li^{+}-Li^{+}$ repulsion rather than $Li^{+}-O^{2-}$ or $Li^{+}-Si^{4+}$ interactions. This structural anisotropy corresponds to an increase of the two-dimensionality in $Li^{+}$-ion conduction. This may give rise to two effects: (i) a total conductivity decrease because of the reduction of the $Li^{+}$-ion hopping probability along the b direction, and (ii) a hopping rate increase normal to the b-axis because of the elongation of the Li-O bond length along the b direction and because of the consequent reduction of the hindrance by $O^{2-}$ ions against $Li^{+}$-ion hop ping. The latter can interpret the experimental re- sults shown in fig. 5.

In the region x>0.5, only the b-axis length de- creases with increasing x. We can assume that the concentration of the planar $BO_{3}^{3-}$ triangles would increase compared with $BO_{4}^{5-}$ tetrahedra (model B) when Si atoms are substituted by B atoms. These changes of local structure have already shown to oc- cur in borosilicate glass [9]. Fig. 8 illustrates how $BO_{3}^{3-}$ arise from $BO_{4}^{5-}$ . Tranqui et al. [2] have also shown the unique long Si-O bond length $(\sim 1.68 \AA)$ compared with the other three $(\sim 1.62 \AA)$ in a $SiO_{4}^{4-}$ tetrahedron. This indication suggests the fol lowing equilibrium with increasing x:

$$
\mathrm{SiO}_{4}{ }^{4-} \rightarrow \mathrm{BO}_{4}{ }^{5-}+\mathrm{Li}^{+} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{BO}_{3}{ }^{3-}+\mathrm{LiO}^{-}. \tag{5}
$$

Eq. (5) indicates the same content of chemical spe- cies of $BO_{3}^{3-}$ and $LiO^{-}$ depending on x. On the other hand, the B-O bond length of $1.42 \AA$ in $BO_{3}^{3-}$ is shorter than that $(1.52 \AA)$ in $BO_{4}^{5-}[10]$ . This sug gests the reduction in cell volume with increasing x. The anisotropic reduction to each crystallographic axis may be interpreted by the equilibrium in eq. (5). Namely, the oxygen atom with a uniquely long B-O bond would recombine with the nearest $Li^{+}$ ion

Fig. 8. Structural relationship between the tetrahedral $BO_{4}$ and triangular $BO_{3}$.

rather than remain as one of the members of $BO_{4}^{5-}$, resulting in $LiO^{-}$ and $BO_{3}^{3-}$ formation. Under an assumption that a B atom is displaced from the center of a $BO_{4}^{5-}$ tetrahedron to the center of a $BO_{3}^{3-}$ triangle, the summation was carried out over all the individual crystallographic axis components of reduced B-O bond length; only the $b$-axis length was reduced whereas the $a$ and $c$ lengths were not affected. This is consistent with the experimental results in the range $x>0.5$. Moreover, the model B allows interpretation of the reduction of the conductivity. Namely, $Li^{+}$-ion trapping due to $LiO^{-}$ formation might result in the reduction of the mobile $Li^{+}$-ion concentration, which would be consistent with the results shown in fig. 5.

An ion coupling model might explain a conductivity decrease with increasing $x$ at higher $Li^{+}$-ion concentration as has been frequently suggested for some superionic crystals, e.g., $ZrO_{2}$ [11,12]. But we have no indication that this model applies to the $Li_{4}SiO_{4}$ system.

In conclusion, we have analyzed the conductivity so as to separate the hopping rate and the mobile-ion concentration. Both components have maxima at $x=0.5$. Also, a simple point-charge model can interpret the reason why the $x$ dependence of the $b$-axis length is unique and corresponds to that of the conductivity.
