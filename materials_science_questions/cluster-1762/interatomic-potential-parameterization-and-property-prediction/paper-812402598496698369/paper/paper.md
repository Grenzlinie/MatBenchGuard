# A lattice dynamical study of the role of pressure on Raman modes in high-$T_c$ ${\rm HgBa_2CuO_4}$

Prafulla K. Jha *, Sankar P. Sanyal

Materials Research Laboratory, Department of Physics, University of Bhopal, Bhopal 462 026, India

Received 1 September 1995

## Abstract
A lattice dynamical study has been carried out to investigate the Raman frequencies and the effect of pressure on the apical-oxygen $(O_A)$ vibration (Raman Active) in the $Hg-O_A-Cu$ bond in ${\rm HgBa_2CuO_4}$ high-$T_C$ superconductor by using an unscreened rigid ion model. The calculated Raman frequencies are, in general, in fair agreement with experimental values. The calculated values of the $O_A$ $A_{1g}$ Raman mode agree reasonably well with the experimental Raman data up to 7.5 GPa and show that the $O_A$ $A_{1g}$ vibrational mode increases linearly as the pressure is increased, suggesting phonons to play a dominant role in this system.

The newly discovered series of superconducting cuprates containing mercury [1,2] has generated considerable excitement in the superconductivity community because of the high transition temperatures that have been achieved in this system, 94 K in the single-layer ${\rm HgBa_2CuO_{4+\delta}}$ (Hg-1201) material [1] and already up to 140 K in multiple-layered materials [2,3]. More recently, it has been found that the superconducting transition temperature $(T_c)$ of the mercury-based superconductors increases by 30 K under high pressure, the largest amongst the oxide high-temperature superconductors. The highest $T_c$ recorded so far is known to be 164 K for ${\rm HgBa_2Ca_2Cu_3O_{8+\delta}}$ (Hg-1223) at 30 GPa [4]. Hence it is important to investigate the mechanism of the pressure-induced increase in $T_c$ in understanding the nature of high-temperature superconductivity and in achieving even higher $T_c$ values. Structural investigations in these superconductors under pressure have been carried out by neutron powder diffraction by Hunter et al. [5] and energy-dispersive X-ray diffraction by Eggert et al. [6]. Hunter et al. [5] have suggested a possible pressure-induced charge transfer between ${\rm CuO_2}$ planes, but could not find any conclusive evidence for it. There has been a theoretical study of the effects of pressure on the electronic structure and $T_c$ by using local density approximation (LDA) total-energy calculations [7]. However, there is no experimental result available so far for directly probing the changes in the electronic structure of the mercury-based superconductors under pressure. Very recently Yang et al. [8] have studied the effect of pressure on Raman modes of Hg-1201 and Hg-1223 up to 10 GPa. Their results show that

In the present paper we report for the first time the calculated Raman frequencies and the effect of pressure on frequency of the Raman $O_A$ $A_{1g}$ mode of Hg-1201 by using a lattice dynamical model. The study of lattice vibrations of the high-$T_c$ superconductors is of importance not only for the understanding of the physical nature and interatomic forces in these compounds, but also for the assessment of the

We calculate the Raman frequencies by using an unscreened rigid ion model [10] in which the ions are considered as rigid (unpolarizable) dur- ing vibration. This model has been used successfully earlier for several superconductors [11] and gives a very good qualitative account of the observed struc- tural and dynamical properties. According to this model the crystal potential energy is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{k k^{\prime}}\left(r_{i j}\right)= & \frac{e^{2} Z(k) Z\left(k^{\prime}\right)}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0} r_{i j}} \\
& +a \exp \left[\frac{-b r_{i j}}{R(k)+R\left(k^{\prime}\right)}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $e^{2} / 4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}=144 \mathrm{eV} / \mathrm{nm}, a=1822 \mathrm{eV}, b=$ 12.364 and $r_{i j}$ is the distance between the two atoms $i$ and $j$ of species $k$ and $k^{\prime} . Z(k), Z(k^{\prime})$ are the effective charges situated at the ionic positions. In the unscreened rigid ion model, the charge neutrality of the unit cell is maintained. The $R(k), R(k^{\prime})$ are referred to as "effective radii" and are treated as adjustable parameters along with the effective charges. The potential parameters are obtained by their systematic variation to provide the static and dynamic stability of the structure. In particular, it is ensured that the total potential is at a minimum with respect to the lattice constants and the atomic coordi- nates, and the eigenvalues $[\omega^{2}(q_{j})]$ are positive for all wave vectors in the Brillouin zone. The potential parameters as obtained by the above procedure are given in Table 1.

Our calculated results for the Raman active modes at ambient pressure for $\mathrm{HgBa}_{2} \mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ are given in Table 2 and compared with the available experimen- tal data from recent Raman scattering experiment [12]. There is generally a fair agreement between the calculated results with the observed modes. We have obtained four Raman active modes at frequencies $105,130,535$ and $583 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ which are assigned to $\mathrm{Ba}$ and $\mathrm{O}(2)$ atoms by using a factor group analysis which is presented in Table 3. The two lower fre- quencies at 105 and $130 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ are due to $\mathrm{Ba}$ atoms and two higher frequencies at 535 and $583 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ are due to the $\mathrm{O}(2)$ atom. For our calculations, we choose the structure measured by Hunter et al. [5].

Such an extrapolation for qualitative understanding of the phonon properties at high pressure can be justified from the linear variation of the lattice constants of the Hg-1223 compound up to 9 GPa, reported by Hunter et al. We have also calculated for the first time the effect of pressure on the Raman frequency of the $O_A$ $A_{1g}$ mode of $HgBa_2CuO_4$ up to 7.5 GPa. The calculated results plotted in Fig. 2 show that the Raman frequency $(\omega)$ of the $O_A$ $A_{1g}$ mode increases as the pressure is increased. In Fig. 2 we also compare our results with the experimental results of Yang et al. [8]. It can be seen from the figure that our results are in good agreement with the experimental values. Moreover, our calculated frequency at 0.6 GPa is $587\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ which is also in reasonable agreement with experiment compared to that of Yang et al. [8] who have reported $631\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ from a LDA calculation.

Finally, in the present paper we have presented the Raman frequencies by using a unscreened rigid ion model for $HgBa_2CuO_4$. The calculated results are in general in good agreement with experimental Raman data and qualitatively explain the trend of variation with pressure. We have also reported, for the first time, the effect of pressure on the $O_A$ $A_{1g}$ Raman frequency up to 7.5 GPa and found that this mode increases as the pressure is increased. A detailed study of the other lattice dynamical properties such as density of states, specific heat and complete phonon-dispersion curves etc. are in progress and will be published subsequently.
