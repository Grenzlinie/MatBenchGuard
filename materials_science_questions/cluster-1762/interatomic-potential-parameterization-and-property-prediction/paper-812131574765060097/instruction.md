# Compute Spontaneous Tetragonal Deformation and Ti Ion Displacement in BaTiO3 Crystal

## Problem background
Barium titanate (BaTiO₃) undergoes a spontaneous ferroelectric phase transition from a cubic paraelectric phase to a tetragonal phase at the Curie temperature. At 0 K, the lattice distorts: the unit cell elongates along the direction of spontaneous polarization and contracts perpendicular to it. This deformation is accompanied by a displacement of the Ti ions from the centrosymmetric positions. Theoretical modeling of this spontaneous deformation requires balancing short-range repulsion (overlap), van der Waals attraction, electrostatic Coulomb energy, and the dipole–dipole interaction arising from the shifted ions. The model predicts that the lattice deformation ratios and the Ti-ion shift depend sensitively on the effective charge of the Ti ion. By choosing an effective charge that matches experimental observations, the model yields specific values for the deformation parallel (Δa) and perpendicular (Δb) to the polarization, and for the Ti-ion displacement. The task is to implement this energy model and compute those quantities for a given effective Ti charge.

## Approach
The approach consists of several conceptual parts. First, the short-range energy is described by an overlap repulsion (λ r⁻⁹) and van der Waals attraction (−μ r⁻⁶) between ion pairs, using the nearest-neighbor approximation for the tetragonal lattice. The Coulomb energy of the tetragonal lattice is approximated by averaging the Madelung energies of three cubic lattices corresponding to the three cell edges. The dipole interaction energy arises from the local electric fields generated by the shifted Ti ions; by solving a set of Slater-type local-field equations for the induced dipoles on all ions, one obtains the effective dipole interaction coefficient as a function of the parallel deformation Δa. The total energy is then expressed in terms of the deformation ratios Δa and Δb, and the equilibrium values are found by solving the coupled energy-minimization equations. The Ti ion displacement is computed from the resulting on-site potential coefficients. All required physical constants (ion-pair overlap and van der Waals parameters, ionic polarizabilities, dipolar field lattice sums) are provided in the workflow, allowing a fully numerical implementation.
