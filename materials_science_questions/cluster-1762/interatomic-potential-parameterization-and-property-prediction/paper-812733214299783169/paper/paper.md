This paper discusses the high pressure-dependent crystal structure phase transformation and elastic behavior of $ZnSe_xTe_{1-x}$ ($x = 0.0, 0.2, 0.55, 0.81, 0.93$ and $1.0$) by speculating effective interionic interaction potential (EIoIP), that contains long-range type Coulomb force, short-range overlap repulsion of the Hafemeister and Flygare types and the van der Waals (vdW) interaction. The assessed values of structural phase transition pressure and the considerable volume discontinuity associated with the pressure-volume phase diagram support the structural phase transformation from zinc blende ($B3$) type to rock-salt ($B1$) type crystal structure. The equation of state curves (relationship between $V(p)/V(0)$ and pressure in the pressure-volume phase diagram) for both the zinc blende ($B3$) and rock-salt ($B1$) structures is in reasonably good agreement with that of experimental observations. Moreover, systematic variations of the second-order elastic constants (SOEC) with pressure have been found to follow systematic trends showed by the other systems with $B3$ type to $B1$ type pressure-dependent structural phase transition.

Keywords: Structural phase transition; high pressure; elastic constants; interionic interaction potential.

## 2. Theoretical Background and Method of Computation

For getting the full idea of thermodynamical properties for $\text{ZnSe}_x\text{Te}_{1-x}$ semiconducting alloys, it requires the establishment of an effective interionic potential for proper description of structural phase transition and elastic properties of $\text{ZnSe}_x\text{Te}_{1-x}$ compounds. The methodology adopted is as follows: when the variation in force constants is very small in magnitude, then the short-range interactions potential are extended effectively only upto the second-neighbor ions in the crystal lattice, and it is supposed that the atoms are vibrating within their elastic limit with harmonic elastic forces without any other internal strain or force within the crystal lattice.

The developed effective interionic potential between a pair of ions ($i$ and $j$th) is represented as
$$
U(r)=\sum_{i j} \frac{Z_{m} e^{2}}{r_{i j}}+\sum_{i j} b \beta_{i j} \exp \left(\frac{r_{i}+r_{j}-r_{i j}}{\rho}\right)+\sum_{i j} c_{i j} r_{i j}^{-6}+\sum_{i j} d_{i j} r_{i j}^{-8}. \tag{1}
$$

Here, the long-range type Coulomb interaction is represented by the first term, the second term corresponds to Hafemeister and Flygare types of short-range overlap repulsive energies³⁵ and the long-range vdW multipole were represented by the third and fourth terms, respectively. $\beta_{i j}$ is known as the Pauling coefficient, which is expressed as $\beta_{i j}=[1+(z_{i}/n_{i})/(z_{j}/n_{j})]$, with $z_{i}(z_{j})$ and $n_{i}(n_{j})$ as the valency and outermost shell electrons in the anions (cations), respectively. The other symbols $c_{i j}$ and $d_{i j}$ represent the $d$–$d$ and $d$–$q$ vdW coefficients, $Z_{m}e$ represents the modified ionic charge in the crystal lattice and $b(\rho)$ represent the hardness (range) of short-range material-dependent parameters. Therefore, it is convenient to state that the effective interionic potential basically includes only three material-dependent free short-range parameters ($Z_{m}$, $b$ and $\rho$). These parameters could be evaluated from the knowledge of the crystal-dependent properties.³⁸

### 2.1. Phase transition

It is well-established that any enhancement of pressure from ambient condition on a crystal eventually reduces its volume. For a given thermodynamic circumstance, it can be stated that an isolated structural phase is supposed to be steady only when it follows the minimum free energy criteria. The compounds under consideration transform from their ambient phase $B3$ to high pressure phase $B1$ on the application of high pressure.

In such a case, the structural stability of a given structure phase can be evaluated by the minima of its Gibbs free energy and this can be expressed as

$$
G = U + PV - TS. \tag{2}
$$

Here, $U$ represents the internal energy of the system and this is equivalent to cohesive energy at 0 K, pressure $P$, volume $V$ and $S$ being the vibrational entropy at a given temperature. The Gibbs free energies for the zinc blende ($B3$) [real] type phase and for rock-salt ($B1$) [hypothetical] type phase are represented by the following relations at a temperature $T=0$ K and pressure $P$:

$$
G_{B3}(r) = U_{B3}(r) + PV_{B3}, \tag{3}
$$

$$
G_{B1}(r') = U_{B1}(r') + PV_{B1}. \tag{4}
$$

Here, the volume of the unit cell is represented by $V_{B3}\ (=3.08r^3)$ and $V_{B1}\ (=2r'^3)$ for $B3$ and $B1$ types structural phases, respectively. $U_{B3}$ and $U_{B1}$ represent cohesive energies for ZnS ($B3$) type and NaCl ($B1$) type structural phases. These cohesive energies can be expressed as

$$
U_{B3}(r) = -1.6381\frac{e^2Z_m^2}{r} + 4V_{ij}(r) + 6V_{ii}(r) + 6V_{jj}(r) \tag{5}
$$

and

$$
U_{B1}(r') = -1.7475\frac{e^2Z_m^2}{r'} + 6V_{ij}(r') + 6V_{ii}(r') + 6V_{jj}(r') \tag{6}
$$

in $B3$ and $B1$ type structural phases, respectively. Here, $r$ and $r'$ are representing the nearest-neighbor ($nn$) separation related to ZnS and NaCl structural phases.

The short-range interaction potentials ($V_{ij}$) (as appeared in Eqs. (5) and (6)) related to both of the structural phases among the ions should follow:

$$
V_{ij}(r) = b\beta_{ij}\exp\left(\frac{r_i + r_j - r_{ij}}{\rho}\right) - c_{ij}r_{ij}^{-6} - d_{ij}r_{ij}^{-8};\quad i,j=1,2. \tag{7}
$$

The symbol $\rho$ and $b$ represent the short-range interaction potential material-dependent parameters. Therefore, the EIoIP has three material-dependent free parameters, namely: modified ionic charge ($Z_m$), range ($b$) and hardness parameters ($\rho$). These parameters were evaluated by the information of crystal properties, such as bulk modulus, equilibrium distance, etc. In this investigation, we have predicted the structural phase transition pressures and associated volume collapses in vicinity of structural phase transition. The calculated and estimated results following the EIoIP are discussed in Sec. 2.2.

### 2.2. Elastic constant

The study of the pressure-dependent SOEC ($C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$) at 0 K is very essential to comprehend the behavior of the interatomic forces in the crystal lattice. Perhaps, the estimation of these SOEC will prove an additional check on the accountability of short-range forces in the test materials as these elastic constants are functions of the first- and second-order derivatives of the short-range interaction potentials. Therefore, we have employed the following SOECs for $B3$ phase:

$$

$$

$$
C_{12}=\frac{e^{2}}{4 a^{4}}\left[-2.6458 Z^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(A_{1}-4 B_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\left(A_{2}-5 B_{2}\right)\right], \tag{9}

C_{44}=\frac{e^{2}}{4 a^{4}}\left[\begin{array}{l}

## 3. Results and Discussion
On the application of high pressure, other than the ambient crystal structure, different crystal phases emerged in systems and their relative stability of various crystal phases needed to be extremely exact predictions under the effect of applied pressure. The different theoretical computations for the theoretical estimation of cohesive energies, structural phases and vibrational properties of zinc-based alloys under high pressure are now briefly executed. The developed model was employed to examine the structural high pressure phase transformation and relative volume reduction in $\text{ZnSe}_x\text{Te}_{1-x}$ ($x=0.0,0.2,0.55,0.81,0.93$ and $1.0$) compounds. As mentioned above, the structural phase transition pressure was then estimated by evaluating the Gibbs free energy following $G=U+PV-TS$ relation for the two respective phases. As all the calculations were performed at $T=0$ K, therefore, at $T=0$ K, the Gibbs free energy eventually turned out to be the enthalpy $H=U+PV$.

The volume of the material can be considerably tuned as indicated by high pressure-dependent experiments. On the other hand, the temperature variations is believed to have less impact on the reduction of volume and less changes in the relative stabilities of phases under consideration of a material as compared to pressure. It is, therefore, meaningful if the estimation of Gibbs free energy is taken at zero temperature, i.e., enthalpy $H$. Furthermore, at $T=0$ K the thermodynamically stable phase at pressure $P$ is that which has the lowest enthalpy. Therefore, the zero-temperature limited theoretical results are in agreement with those measured experimentally. But in practice, the effects of finite temperature cannot be ignored and could have some considerable effect. The values of thermodynamical potential $G$ or $H$ have been estimated by utilizing the values of the three material-dependent model parameters, namely, modified ionic charge $(Z_m)$, range $(b)$ and hardness parameters $(\rho)$, and these can be estimated from the following equilibrium relation:

$$
\left.\frac{dU(r)}{dr}\right|_{r=r_0}=0 \tag{18}
$$

and the bulk modulus $(B_T)$:

$$
\left.\frac{d^2U(r)}{dr^2}\right|_{r=r_0}=(9kr_0)^{-1}B_T. \tag{19}
$$

We have also checked the stability of lattice phase. By the condition of Born criterion for a lattice phase, its mechanical stability is such that its elastic energy density (or the elastic constant) must be a positive and explicitly quadratic depen- dency of lattice strain. The above result can be interpreted as follows: the principal minors (alternatively the eigenvalues) of the second-order elastic constant matrix must be positive. The above condition for a stable cubic crystal structure system can be written in the form of SOEC as$^{47}$:

$$
B_T=(C_{11}+2C_{12})/3>0, \tag{24}
$$

$$
C_{44}>0 \tag{25}
$$

and

$$
C_s=(C_{11}-C_{12})/2>0, \tag{26}
$$

where $C_{ij}$ are the standard cubic elastic constants and $B_T$ is the bulk modulus of the compound, terms $C_{44}$ and $C_s$ are known as the shear and tetragonal moduli of a cubic crystal structure, respectively. The calculated values of these quantities are listed in Table 4 and perfectly assure the above elastic crystal stability conditions for $\text{ZnSe}_x\text{Te}_{1-x}$ ($x=0.0, 0.2, 0.55, 0.81, 0.93$ and $1$) compounds. Furthermore, Vukcevich$^{48}$ has formulated pressure-dependent structural stability conditions for ionic solids by merging mechanical stability of a crystal with its minimum energy. As per this criterion, it is essential for a stable phase for which the cubic shear elastic constant $C_{44}$ should be positive and nonzero (for mechanical stability of the crystal) and which also has the lowest potential energy among all the mechanically stable crystal lattices. Therefore, it is concluded that our calculations provide and follow all the criteria for stable phases.

### 4. Conclusion

Initially, an effective interionic interaction potential was developed to explore the pressure-dependent structural stability and allied volume reduction in $\text{ZnSe}_x\text{Te}_{1-x}$ compounds. The estimated values of model parameters, that are material- dependent, permit us to identify the structural phase transition pressures and asso- ciated volume collapses at the vicinity of structural phase transition in these alloys.

High pressure structural phase transition

It was identified that the huge volume discontinuity in pressure–volume structural phase diagram indicates the pressure-dependent structural phase transition from B3 to B1 phase. These observations are in excellent agreement with the reported theoretical and experimental data. The proper utilization of the material-dependent free parameters, that are evaluated from the experimental and theoretical observa- tions, were allowed to predict pressure-dependent structural phase transition and elastic properties of these alloys. The SOECs also support the applicability of Born criterion in these compounds. Further, $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ elastic constants are found to increase with the increase in applied pressure upto phase transition pressure, which provides pressure-dependent structural stability of $ZnSe_xTe_{1-x}$ compounds. More- over, $C_{44}$ is also found to be increasing linearly with the increase of applied pressure, indicating the first-order character of the transition as $C_{44}$ does not approach zero upto the transition pressure.
