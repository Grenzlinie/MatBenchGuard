# An overlap repulsive interaction in ionic molecules and crystals

Md. Sadique Ali and Md. Mazahir Hasan
Department of Physics, Bhagalpur University, Bhagalpur-812007, India

Received 27 July 1990

An overlap repulsive interaction in an interaction potential is used to describe the nature of ion-ion interaction in alkali halides in the molecular as well as crystalline states. The repulsive interaction comes from the knowledge of the effective electron density function $F(r)$ which satisfies the Poisson equation $\nabla^{2} U(r)=F(r)$. A constant based on the valence electrons of two combining atoms is introduced and assumed to be characteristic for both the states. The different contributions related to polarization forces and Van der Waals interactions are investigated for these molecules. In the analysis of crystal binding, the contributions arising from the Van der Waals dispersion forces and the repulsive interactions operative between nearest neighbours as well as next nearest neighbours have also been taken into account. The results of various spectroscopic constants, cohesive energy and bulk modulus for 20 alkali halides compare well with the corresponding experimental data.

## 1. Introduction

The interaction potential energy function is generally used to study the crystalline state properties of diatomic ionic crystals like alkali halide alkali hydrides, etc. The recent study shows that it is also applicable to describe the binding in the molecular or gaseous state. For this purpose, a suitable form of the short-range repulsive interaction, one of the central parts of the interaction potential energy function, is essential.

In the present communication, we apply a short-range repulsive interaction in the expression for the interaction potential and investigate its suitability for gaseous as well as crystalline states by keeping the hardness parameter for unlike ion pair interactions associated with the repulsive part the same for the gaseous as well as crystalline states. All types of ion pair interactions are taken into account for the crystal binding in alkali halides. The results obtained are compared with the available experimental data. The agreement in almost all cases is impressive.

## 2. Gaseous state

The potential energy, $U(r)$, for alkali halide molecules using the modified Rittner model [1, 2] is given by

$$
U(r)=-\frac{e^{2}}{r}-\frac{e^{2}\left(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\right)}{2 r^{4}}-\frac{c}{r^{6}}+\psi(r), \quad(1)
$$

where the first term is the electrostatic interaction energy, the second term the polarization energy, the third term is the Van der Waals dipole-dipole energy and $\psi(r)$ is the short-range repulsive interaction. $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{2}$ are the electronic polarizabilities of cation and anion, respectively, which are calculated by using the relations [3]

$$
\alpha_{1}=\frac{e^{2} h^{2} n}{4 \pi^{2} m\left(E_{\mathrm{f}}-e \phi\right)^{2}}, \quad(2)
$$

$$
\alpha_{2}=\frac{e^{2} h^{2} n}{4 \pi^{2} m\left(E_{\mathrm{f}}+e \phi\right)^{2}}, \quad(3)
$$

where $\phi=e^{2} / r$.

The values of $E_{\mathfrak{f}}$ appearing in expressions (2) and (3) are calculated from free-ion polarizabilities $\alpha_{\mathfrak{f}}$ as reported by Tessmann et al. [4] using Ruffa's theory of electronic polarizabili- ty [5] expressed as:

$$
E_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}=\frac{e^{2} h^{2} n}{4 \pi^{2} m \alpha_{\mathfrak{f}}}, \quad(4)
$$

where $e$ and $m$ are the electronic charge and mass, respectively. $n$ is the total number of electrons in the ion and $h$ is Planck's constant. Once the values of $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{2}$ are known, it is possible to estimate the Van der Waals dipole- dipole $(W_{\text{d-d}})$ energy from the Slater-Kirkwood variational approach [6] which is written as:

$$
W_{\text{d-d}}=-\frac{3 e \hbar}{2 m^{1 / 2} r^{6}} \frac{\alpha_{1} \alpha_{2}}{\left[\left(\alpha_{1} / N_{1}\right)^{1 / 2}+\left(\alpha_{2} / N_{2}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]}. \quad(5)
$$

Here, $N_{1}$ and $N_{2}$ are the effective numbers of electrons in the ions which are taken from the work of Shanker et al. [7]. The earlier workers have suggested that the Slater-Kirkwood varia- tional method is superior to other methods when evaluating the $W_{\text{d-d}}$ energy in ionic diatomic molecules. In the present study, we apply a short-range repulsive interaction recently sugges- ted by us [9, 10] which is written as;

$$
\psi(r)=\left(P^{\prime} / r^{m}\right) \exp \left(-b^{\prime} r^{N}\right), \quad(6)
$$

where $m=2$ and $P ; b^{\prime}$ and $N$ are the potential parameters.

### 2.1. Theoretical justification of our model

All contributions except $\psi(r)$ in eq. (1) can be calculated from electronic polarizabilities and the internuclear distance. It is difficult to calculate $\psi(r)$ from fundamental considerations based on quantum mechanics. However, Anderson and Parr [11] observed that a diatomic Born-Oppenheimer potential energy function $\nabla^{2} U(r)$ satisfies a Poisson equation of the form

$$
\nabla^{2} U(r)=F(r) \approx 4 \pi z_{\alpha} \rho_{\beta}(\alpha) \approx 4 \pi z_{\beta} \rho_{\alpha}(\beta), \quad(7)
$$

where $z_{\alpha}$ is the atomic number of nucleus $\alpha$ due to that part of the total electron density which perfectly follows nucleus $\beta$ during the vibration. Solving eq. (7) exactly Noor Mohammad [12] arrived at the result,

$$
U(r)=U\left(r_{\mathrm{e}}\right)+\int_{r_{\mathrm{e}}}^{r} s(1-s / r) F(s) \mathrm{d} s. \quad(8)
$$

Here each density function $F(r)$ determines a potential $U(r)$ and conversely. The quantum mechanical results [13-15] reveal that the repul- sive potential can be approximated by a potential of the exponential form dependent on interionic distance. On the assumption that $U(r)$ decays exponentially, we also suggest that

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r)= & s \exp \left(-\eta r^{N}\right) \\
& \times\left[\eta^{2} N^{2} r^{2 N}-\eta N(N-3) r^{N}+2\right] / r^{4}, \quad(9)
\end{aligned}
$$

and finally we get the following analytical form

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(r)= & U\left(r_{\mathrm{e}}\right)+s \exp \left(-\eta r_{\mathrm{e}}^{N}\right)\left(N \eta r_{\mathrm{e}}^{N}+1\right) / r_{\mathrm{e}}^{2} \\
& -s \exp \left(-\eta r_{\mathrm{e}}^{N}\right)\left(N \eta r_{\mathrm{e}}^{N-1}+2 / r_{\mathrm{e}}\right) / r \\
& +s \exp \left(-\eta r^{N}\right) / r^{2}. \quad(10)
\end{aligned}
$$

Equation (10) is a form of our model equation (6).

### 2.2. Correlation

According to valence bond theory and the quantum mechanical calculations of the repul- sion parameter [15] it is found that the major contribution to the bond formation in diatomic molecules comes from the valence electrons. We, therefore, correlate $m$ and $N$ to the valence electrons of the two combining atoms. For this we define [9] $m=2$ and

$$
N=\frac{m\left(V_{1}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}}{V_{1}+V_{2}}+0.085\left(\frac{V_{2}}{V_{1}}\right),
$$

when both atoms (like or unlike) have s or p

valence state, and $m=2$ and
$$
N=\frac{m\left(V_{1}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}}{V_{1}+V_{2}}-0.035\left(\frac{V_{2}}{V_{1}}\right), \tag{12}
$$
when one atom has the s-valence state and another has ns-np valence state. Here $V_{1}$ is the number of valence electrons associated with the outermost s or p orbitals in the first atom and $V_{2}$ is the number of valence electrons of other atoms having the valence state configurations like $ns^{1}$ or $ns^{2}$ or $ns^{2}np^{x}$ where $x$ varies from 1 to 5. It is remarkable to note that for all diatomic molecules (ionic or partial covalent) $N \simeq 1.5$.

### 2.3. Method of evaluation of parameters and molecular state properties
Having discussed the various contributions to the potential energy in eq. (1), we are now in a position to evaluate two adjustable parameters associated with the repulsive interaction. For this we apply molecular equilibrium criteria and molecular force constant conditions:
$$
\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} U(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}\right)_{r=r_{\mathrm{e}}}=0, \tag{13}
$$
$$
\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} U(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right)_{r=r_{\mathrm{e}}}=k_{\mathrm{e}}, \tag{14}
$$
where $k_{\mathrm{e}}$ is the molecular force constant related to vibrational frequencies. Potential parameters $P'$ and $b'$ have been determined from eqs. (1), (6), (13) and (14) using experimental data [16] on $r_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{e}}$. Once the potential parameters are known we are able to evaluate the molecular state properties like the binding energy $(D_{\mathrm{i}})$, the rotation-vibration coupling constant $(\alpha_{\mathrm{e}})$ and vibrational anharmonicity constant $(\omega_{\mathrm{e}}x_{\mathrm{e}})$ to test the suitability of the potential form adopted and to investigate the effect of newly calculated polarizabilities and Van der Waals energies. The suitability of our model is judged by comparing the calculated values of $D_{\mathrm{i}}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{e}}x_{\mathrm{e}}$ for twenty alkali halide molecules with the corresponding observed values. The relations expressing these quantities are [17]:
$$
D_{\mathrm{i}}=-U\left(r_{\mathrm{e}}\right), \tag{15}
$$
$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{e}}=-\left(\frac{X_{3} r_{\mathrm{e}}}{3}+1\right) \frac{6 B_{\mathrm{e}}^{2}}{\omega_{\mathrm{e}}}, \tag{16}
$$
$$
\omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}=\left(\frac{5}{3} X_{3}^{2}-X_{4}\right) \frac{h}{64 \pi^{2} c \mu}, \tag{17}
$$
where
$$
X_{p}=\frac{\left(\mathrm{d}^{p} U(r) / \mathrm{d} r^{p}\right)_{r=r_{\mathrm{e}}}}{\left(\mathrm{d}^{2} U(r) / \mathrm{d} r^{2}\right)_{r=r_{\mathrm{e}}}}
$$

Here $p$ denotes the order of differentiation, $c$ is the velocity of light and $\mu$ is the reduced mass of the molecule. $h$ is Planck's constant. The results of $D_{\mathrm{i}}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{e}}x_{\mathrm{e}}$ thus obtained are compared with the exerimental data [1] in table 1.

### 3. Crystalline state
The crystal lattice energy of an ionic crystal is normally expressed as;
$$
W=-A e^{2} / r-C / r^{6}-D / r^{8}+\psi(r), \tag{18}
$$
where the first term is the Coulomb energy (with Madelung constant $A$) arising from the electrostatic interaction between unlike point ions. The second and third terms represent the Van der Waals dipole-dipole and dipole-quadrupole interactions. Values of Van der Waals coefficients $C$ and $D$ are taken from Shanker et al. [7]. $\psi(r)$ is the short-range repulsive interaction arising from the overlap of two combining ions. Here, our short-range repulsive potential eq. (6) is investigated by taking into account (i) the effect of nearest neighbour (NN), and (ii) the effect of next nearest neighbour (NNN) interactions. The repulsive potential extended up to the second neighbour interactions is written as:
$$
\psi(r)=M \psi_{+-}+\frac{1}{2} M^{\prime}\left(\psi_{++}+\psi_{--}\right), \tag{19}
$$

Our repulsive potential for all types of ion pair interactions can be expressed in the generalized form as follows

$$
\psi_{i j}(r)=\frac{\beta_{i j} P}{r_{i j}^{m}} \exp \left(-b_{i j} r_{i j}^{N}\right).\qquad(20)
$$

$P$ is the repulsive strength parameter. For the NaCl structure $r_{i i}=r_{j j}=1.4142 r$ and for CsCl structure $r_{i i}=r_{j j}=1.1547 r$. $\beta_{i j}$ are the Pauling coefficients introduced in order to provide the appropriate weight for various ion pair interac- tions expressed as [18]

$$
\beta_{i j}=1+\frac{Z_{i}}{n_{i}}+\frac{Z_{j}}{n_{j}},\qquad(21)
$$

where $Z_{i}$ and $Z_{j}$ are the valencies and $n_{i}$ and $n_{j}$ are the numbers of the outermost electrons of ions $i$ and $j$.

### 3.1. Method of evaluation of parameters

The repulsive hardness parameters for all types of ion pair interactions, i.e. $b_{+-}, b_{++}$ and $b_{--}$ are determined empirically. For this purpose we assume that

$$
b_{+-}=b^{\prime},\qquad(22)
$$

$$
b_{++}=\left(\frac{1}{K}\right) b_{+-},\qquad(23)
$$

$$
b_{--}=\left(\frac{3}{K+m}\right) b_{+-}.\qquad(24)
$$

$K$ is the crystal structure constant. For the NaCl structure $K=2$ and for the CsCl structure $K=$ 1.54. $b^{\prime}$ is the repulsive hardness parameter de-

termined from eqs. (1), (6), (13) and (14) in the gaseous state.

The choice of relation (22) is based on the work of previous workers [19, 20]. Further we assume that the like ion pair interactions represent a small part of the total repulsion and these effects are taken into account by fitting relations (23) and (24). It is very difficult to give a satisfactory theoretical justification of the fitting schemes (22)-(24). However, the merit of these relations is tested by the success of the theoretical results. The repulsive strength parameter $P$ is evaluated from the crystal equilibrium conditions

$$
(\mathrm{d} W / \mathrm{d} r)_{r=r_{0}}=0. \tag{25}
$$

Now to investigate the suitability of the fitting schemes for our model we calculate the values of cohesive energy $(W)$ from eq. (18) and the results are compared with experimental data [21]. Bulk modulus $(B_{\mathrm{T}})$ is also calculated from the relation

$$
B_{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{r^{2} W^{\prime \prime}}{9 V}, \tag{26}
$$

where $W^{\prime \prime}$ is the second order derivative of the potential energy with respect to the interionic distance and $V$ is the unit cell volume. The results of $B_{\mathrm{T}}$ are also compared with the experimental data [22, 23]. The calculated values of $B_{\mathrm{T}}$, $W$ and repulsive hardness parameter $(b_{+-})$ for 20 alkali halide crystals are reported in table 2.

The newly calculated values of molecular state polarizability are employed to estimate the Van der Waals dipole-dipole energies. These values are found to yield $D_\text{i}$, $\alpha_\text{e}$ and $\omega_\text{e}x_\text{e}$ for 20 alkali halide molecules in good agreement with their corresponding experimental data [1]. It is evident from table 3 that the calculated values of dissociation energy ($D_\text{e}$), $\alpha_\text{e}$ and $\omega_\text{e}x_\text{e}$ for LiCl, NaF, NaCl and KF molecules compare well with the available ab initio quantum mechanical results [24].

Having taken an interaction potential model consisting of the various interionic forces, e.g. Coulomb interactions, Van der Waals dispersion forces and a new short-range repulsive interaction extended between nearest neighbours as well as next nearest neighbours (NNN), we have calculated the values of cohesive energy and bulk modulus for 20 alkali halide crystals. The calculated values of cohesive energy with and without NNN interactions are in close agreement with the experimental values [21].

With the inclusion of NNN interactions which arise from the contributions of cation-anion, cation-cation, and anion-anion ion pair interactions in the short-range repulsive potential, values of the bulk modulus have been improved significantly and the average percentage deviation reduces from 12.1 to 3.2 (table 2).

In the crystalline state, the repulsive hardness parameters to represent cation-anion, cation-cation and anion-anion ion pair interactions are derived from three empirical fitting schemes which reduce the number of potential parameters. In the present communication, the merit of these fitting schemes is judged by the success of the critical results which are found to be reasonably satisfactory in alkali halide crystals. The overall agreement between theoretical and experimental values obtained from our interaction potential model is good for the entire family of alkali halides in the molecular as well as crystalline states.
