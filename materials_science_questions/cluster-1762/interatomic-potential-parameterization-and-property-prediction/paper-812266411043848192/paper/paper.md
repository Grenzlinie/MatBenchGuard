# Role of spatial valence charge density on the metastability of an immiscible binary metal system at equilibrium
Y. Kong, J. H. Li, L. T. Kong, and B. X. Liu*
Advanced Materials Laboratory, Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Tsinghua University,
Beijing 100084, People's Republic of China
and State Key Laboratory of Solid-State Microstructure, Nanjing University, Nanjing 200039, People's Republic of China

(Received 29 April 2005; published 22 July 2005)

In the equilibrium immiscible Ag-Ru system, the elastic constants and phonon spectra of the possible $Ag_{3}Ru$ and $AgRu_{3}$ in $D0_{3}, L1_{2}$, and $D0_{19}$ structures, respectively, are first obtained by the lattice dynamics calculation, which shows that only $L1_{2}$ and $D0_{19} AgRu_{3}$ phases can relatively be stable. The spatial valence charge density (SVCD) is then acquired by first principles calculation for all the studied structures. It turns out that the high SVCDs distribute mostly between the similar atoms in the relatively stable phases, while located mainly between the dissimilar atoms in all the unstable structures, indicating that the SVCD plays a decisive role in determining the metastability, i.e., the structural stability of the nonequilibrium phase, in an equilibrium immiscible binary metal system.

## I. INTRODUCTION

In recent decades, some nonequilibrium processing techniques, such as ion beam mixing, $^{1}$ mechanical alloying, $^{2}$ and solid state reaction, $^{3}$ have been developed to produce various nonequilibrium alloys in many binary metal systems, which are essentially equilibrium immiscible. In an equilibrium immiscible system, since there is no thermodynamic driving force for intermixing between the constituent metals, the key issue in initiating an alloying process is to overcome the repulsive interaction originated from the positive heat of formation $(\Delta H)$ of the system, $^{4}$ thus artificially creating some nonequilibrium alloys. To achieve a thorough understanding of the physical origin of the metastability, namely the structural stability of the nonequilibrium phases/alloys, it has been a great challenge to the researchers in the fields ofphysics, chemistry, and materials science. $^{5}$

Besides the experimental studies, much attention has also been paid to the theoretical modeling concerning the formation and structural characteristics of the nonequilibrium solid phases in the equilibrium immiscible systems. $^{6-10}$ For example, based on Miedema's model, Liu et al. $^{8}$ have clarified that it is the interfacial free energies stored in the multilayered films that help to overcome the positive $\Delta H$ between the immiscible elements, leading to an alloying and/or amorphization process. Recently, some researchers $^{9,10}$ have employed first principles calculation to predict the energetically favored structures at a few specific chemical compositions in the equilibrium immiscible systems by comparing the calculated energetic sequence.

Up to now, however, convincing evidence is still lacking for establishing the exact extent of the alloying theory for the equilibrium immiscible binary metal systems with positive $\Delta H.^{5,11}$ In comparison, for the negative $\Delta H$ systems, it has been established that the short-range chemical and topological orders are of vital importance in determining their metastability. $^{12}$ Adopting those established methods, we investigate, in the present study, some hypothetical crystalline structures in an equilibrium immiscible model system, i.e.,the Ag-Ru system with a large positive $\Delta H=+35 kJ / mol,^{4}$ to explore the short range bonding or other types of local chemical or topological packing structures. The Ag-Ru system is selected, because there are some experimental data available for comparing with the present theoretical studies. For instance, there is a deep eutectic at $AgRu_{3}$ and nothing at $Ag_{3}Ru$ in the equilibrium phase diagram. $^{4}$ Moreover, despite the immiscibility between the Ag and Ru metals, an effective mutual solid solubility has been obtained at the Ag-Ru interface upon ion irradiation. $^{13}$ Besides these, the Ag-Ru bimetallic catalyst has been used in hydrogen adsorption, $^{14}$ andsome researchers have tried to fabricate unique compositesbased on the Ru-Ag mixture as coordination networks. $^{15}$

## II. COMPUTATIONAL METHODS

We use two calculation methods to study the structural stability of the metastable phases in the Ag-Ru system.

The first method is the standard lattice dynamics calculation. $^{16}$ To obtain the phonon spectra, there are basically three approaches in determining the force constants: analytic calculation, supercell calculation, and linear-response calculation. $^{17}$ In the present study, we perform analytic calculation with a newly derived semiempirical Ag-Ru potential, $^{18}$ which was constructed under the second moment approximation of the tight-binding scheme (TB-SMA), to obtain the elastic constants and lattice phonon spectra for some hypothetical crystalline structures. The elastic constants are calculated following Johnson's method that the second order elastic constants are the second derivatives of the single atom energy. $^{19}$ Based on the Born-von Kármán model, $^{16}$ using the TB-SMA potential, the interatomic force constants $\Phi_{\alpha \beta}(r_{i j})$ between the atom $i$ and atom $j$ can be obtained under the harmonic approximation. Consequently, the phonon spectra can be calculated through the dynamical matrix $D_{\alpha \beta}(q, k k')$, which is the Fourier transformation of the interatomic force constant.

$$
\left|D_{\alpha \beta}\left(\boldsymbol{q}, k k^{\prime}\right)-m_{k} m_{k^{\prime}} \omega^{2}(\boldsymbol{q}) \delta_{\alpha \beta} \delta_{k k^{\prime}}\right|=0,
$$

where $m_{k}$ is the mass of the $k$ atom, $\omega$ is the phonon frequency, $\boldsymbol{q}$ is the wave vector, and $\delta$ is the Kronecker function.

The second method is first principles calculation by using the well-established $ab$ initio simulation software named CASTEP, $^{20}$ from which the lattice constants and elastic constants are also obtained and used to check the relevance of those obtained from the semiempirical potential. More importantly, the spatial valence charge density (SVCD) $^{21}$ is determined by first principles calculation with CASTEP to reveal the bonding nature in the studied structures. In CASTEP calculation, we first perform the geometry optimization to find the ground state of the studied structures, and the elastic constants are then calculated with the same setting for the geometry optimization. The calculations are conducted in a plane-wave basis with a maximum plane wave cutoff energy of 330 eV, using fully nonlocal Vanderbilt-type ultrasoft pseudopotentials $^{22}$ to describe the electron-ion interaction, and the pseudoatomic calculation performed for Ag is $4 d 10\ 5 s 1$, and for Ru, is $4 s 2\ 4 p 6\ 4 d 7\ 5 s 1$, respectively. The exchange and correlation items are described by the generalized-gradient approximation, $^{23}$ and the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) method $^{24}$ is used in the geometry optimization. The integration in the Brillouin zone (BZ) is done on the special k points determined from the Monkhorst-Pack scheme. $^{25}$

### B. Phonon spectra

We now present the calculated phonon spectra of the hypothetical crystalline structures along the high symmetry directions of the BZ in Fig. 2 and we use the negative $y$ axis to plot the imaginary frequencies.

First, for the case of Ag₃Ru, one sees that for the D0₃ [Fig. 2(a)] and D0₁₉ [Fig. 2(c)] structures, some optical phonon branches are totally unstable in the entire BZ, and for the L1₂ [Fig. 2(b)] structure, the whole phonons are positive at central of the BZ, while only some phonon branches become imaginary at the large $q$ values. Second, for the case of AgRu₃, one sees that for the D0₃ [Fig. 2(d)] structure, only some transverse acoustic branches are unstable, while for the L1₂ [Fig. 2(e)] and D0₁₉ [Fig. 2(e)] structures, all the phonon frequencies are positive. In short, the lattice dynamics calculations show that the vibrational behaviors at chemical stoichiometries of 3:1 and 1:3 in the Ag-Ru system are quite different and that the L1₂, D0₁₉, and D0₃ Ag₃Ru phases as well as the D0₃ AgRu₃ phase are unstable, whereas the L1₂ and D0₁₉ AgRu₃ phases are relatively stable, i.e., they can be metastable. This conclusion based on the lattice dynamics calculation is consistent with the Ag-Ru equilibrium phase diagram, which shows that there is a deep eutectic at AgRu₃, yet nothing at Ag₃Ru.⁴

A scientific problem is then raised why the Ag-Ru system shows such different dynamical behaviors, i.e., what is the physical origin of the metastability of the above Ag-Ru phases/alloys?

### C. Spatial valence charge density (SVCD)

To achieve an answer, we calculate the SVCD by CASTEP to reveal the bonding nature in the studied structures. We use slices of some specific planes (shown in Fig. 1 for corresponding crystalline structures, respectively) in the electronic density field to show the details of the SVCD in a quantitative way. The slice is plotted with color filled contour between the lowest charge density (about 0.14 electrons per $\text{A}^3$) and maximum charge density (about 8.7 electrons per $\text{A}^3$). The maxima in the electronic density field correspond to the positions of the atoms that have semicore states, which are explicitly treated as valence.

As clarified by the calculated phonon spectra, the L1₂ structured Ag₃Ru phase is unstable, yet the L1₂ structured AgRu₃ phase can be metastable. We show the contour plots of the calculated SVCD for the two L1₂ structures in Fig. 3. For the L1₂ Ag₃Ru, Fig. 3(a) shows that in the (110) plane, the electrons only distribute between Ag-Ru (A areas in Fig. 3(a), and similar for the followings) and Fig. 3(b) shows that in the (100) plane, the SVCDs between Ag-Ag (B) are low (0.22–0.35 electrons per $\text{A}^3$). While for the L1₂ AgRu₃, Fig. 3(c) is the SVCD along the [110] direction and it shows that, besides the electronic interactions between Ag-Ru (C), the interactions between Ru-Ru (D) are significantly increased, as evidenced by two high SVCD areas (D) appearing in the central portion. Figure 3(d) shows that in the (100) plane, there are also higher electronic interactions between Ru-Ru (E), as the calculated SVCDs are significantly increased (0.27–0.41 electrons per $\text{A}^3$). These different electronic interactions are attributed to the partial filling of the $d$ bands, i.e., one has covalent bonding of partially filled $d$ shells in Ru, however, which is absent in Ag.²⁷ The structural stability of the L1₂ AgRu₃ is therefore attributed to the increasing in the electronic interactions between Ru-Ru.

It is of interest to see whether the unstable L1₂ Ag₃Ru structure can be stabilized through artificially raising the external pressure.²⁸ In this regard, we calculate the phonon density of states to see the characteristic behavior of the phonons under various high pressures. It turns out that the imaginary phonons of the L1₂ AgRu₃ structure all disappear at a critical pressure of 21.6 GPa, which equals to lessen 6.25% of the lattice constant. We then recalculate the SVCD for the L1₂ Ag₃Ru structure at the critical pressure of 21.6 GPa and shown them in Figs. 3(e) and 3(f). For the D0₁₉ structured Ag₃Ru and AgRu₃ phases, from the lattice dynamics calculation we know that the D0₁₉ Ag₃Ru phase is unstable, while the D0₁₉ AgRu₃ phase is relatively stable. Figure 4 shows the contour plots of the SVCD of these two phases. From Fig. 4(a), one can note that there is only net valence charge (A) distributing between the atoms, and the net valence charge density is below 0.26 electrons per A³. From Fig. 4(b), however, besides the net valence charge, one can see that there is additive Ru-Ru (B) covalent bonding, which stabilizes the D0₁₉ AgRu₃ phase, and the valence charge density is 0.30-0.41 electrons per A³.

Moreover, according to the lattice dynamics calculations, both D0₃ Ag₃Ru and D0₃ AgRu₃ phases are unstable, and comparing their calculated phonon spectra, the D0₃ AgRu₃ phase could relatively be more stable than D0₃ Ag₃Ru. Figure 5 is the calculated SVCDs for the D0₃ structured Ag₃Ru and AgRu₃. From Fig. 5(a), one sees that in the less stable D0₃ Ag₃Ru, besides the net valence charge (below 0.27 electrons per A³), there is little SVCD between Ag-Ag (A), while there is additive Ag-Ru (B) covalent bonding (0.27-0.41 electrons per A³). From Fig. 5(b), one sees that for the relatively more stable D0₃ AgRu₃, the SVCDs between Ru-Ru (C) are significantly increased (0.33-0.47 electrons per A³). This additive covalent bonding between the similar atoms makes the D0₃ AgRu₃ more stable than D0₃ Ag₃Ru. Nonetheless, since the D0₃ AgRu₃ is an open-packed structure, in which the similar atoms are not close enough, it cannot be stabilized.

In short, in the unstable Ag-Ru phases, the high SVCDs only distribute between the dissimilar atoms, resulting in strong repulsive interactions, whereas in the relatively stable Ag-Ru phases, there are large enough SVCDs locating between the similar atoms, leading to strong attractive interactions. It follows that the SVCD is a decisive factor in determining the metastability of an equilibrium immiscible binary metal system. With this insight, we can understand why some kinds of phases/alloys can be created between the immiscible metallic elements. For example, from the reported Refs. 6 and 7, we can see that amorphous structure or homogeneity of a supersaturated solid solution is obtained between the immiscible metallic elements, and that the showed atomic-level topology is phase separated. ## IV. CONCLUSION
In summary, we achieve a new insight that the SVCD, which reflects quantitatively the amount of electronic interactions between the similar and/or dissimilar atoms, is a decisive factor determining the metastability, i.e., the structural stability of the nonequilibrium phases, of an equilibrium immiscible binary metal system. We find that a specific phase/ structure can only be stabilized, when there are high enough spatial valence charge density, corresponding to large enough electronic interactions, between the similar atoms to form the relevant bonding of the phase/structure.
