# Phonon dispersion in alkali metals and their equiatomic sodium-based binary alloys

Aditya M. VORA (⊗)

Parmeshwari 165, Vijaynagar Area, Hospital Road, Bhuj-Kutch, 370 001, Gujarat, India

© Higher Education Press and Springer-Verlag 2008

**Abstract** In the present article, the theoretical calculations of the phonon dispersion curves (PDCs) of five alkali metals viz. Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs and their four equiatomic sodium-based binary alloys viz. ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Li}}_{0.5}$, ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{K}}_{0.5}$, ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Rb}}_{0.5}$ and ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Cs}}_{0.5}$ to second order in a local model potential is discussed in terms of the real-space sum of the Born von Karman central force constants. Instead of the concentration average of the force constants of pure alkali metals, the pseudo-alloy-atom (PAA) is adopted to directly compute the force constants of the four equiatomic sodium based binary alloys and was successfully applied. The exchange and correlation functions due to the Hartree (H) and Ichimaru-Utsumi (IU) are used to investigate the influence of the screening effects. The phonon frequencies of alkali metals and their four equiatomic sodium-based binary alloys in the longitudinal branch are more sensitive to the exchange and correlation effects in comparison with the transverse branches. The PDCs of pure alkali metals are found in qualitative agreement with the available experimental data. The frequencies in the longitudinal branch are suppressed rather due to IU-screening function than those due to static H-screening function.

**Keywords** alkali metals, sodium based equiatomic alloys, phonon dispersion curves (PDCs), exchange and correlation functions

## 1 Introduction

In the study of various properties of solids, one frequently requires the knowledge of the interaction energy between the ions or atoms. The studies of a pair effective interionic interaction in simple metals has a long history and originally, they were not systematized and were concerned with individual metals groups of metals. In the recent years, considerable attention has been devoted to the theoretical study of the nature of effective interaction between constituent atoms or ions in simple metals. The analysis of such studies allows us to conclude: (1) this interaction in metals arises due to interatomic spacing in metals; (2) the potential energy of an effective pair interaction has sufficiently deep minima in a negative energy region; (3) the depth of a potential well increases with increase of a valence and; (4) the interatomic distance, corresponding to the potential energy minimum, increases with the increase of an atomic number. The pseudopotential approach has been proven to be one of the best methods in computing the pair potentials. But the studies on the pair potential of binary solid solutions and related properties have not yet been taken up systematically. There are some attempts on the investigation of static and vibrational properties of binaries based on the pseudopotential theory. But studies on simple systems of alkali-alkali solid solutions are not rigorous enough, especially, the computations of elastic constants, bulk modulus and phonon dispersion relations. Also, the ${\text{A}}_{1-X}{\text{B}}_{X}$ ($X=0.5$; A = Na; B = Li, K, Rb, Cs) solid alloy systems forms substitutional solid solution overall region of concentration ($X$) of the second metallic component and the crystal binding of the solid solution is unchanged compared with that of the pure alkali metals. Theoretical studies about the lattice dynamics of the alloy systems have been devoted to ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Li}}_{0.5}$, ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{K}}_{0.5}$, ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Rb}}_{0.5}$ and ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Rb}}_{0.5}$ systems since the lattice dynamics of the pure alkalis have been investigated in detail. However, the work on the comprehensive study of phonon dispersion curves (PDCs) of their binary alloys is almost negligible in the literature [1–6].

Therefore, we have decided to work on the solid alkali metals and their four equiatomic sodium based binary alloys viz. ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Li}}_{0.5}$, ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{K}}_{0.5}$, ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Rb}}_{0.5}$ and ${\text{Na}}_{0.5}{\text{Cs}}_{0.5}$ using model potential formalism. By treating the sodium based solid binary alloys as a pseudo-alloy-atom (PAA), a real space sum analysis is used to compute phonon dispersion relation along three major symmetry directions [1–3]. ## 2 Theoretical methodology

The phonon frequencies can be obtained by solving the standard secular determinental equation [1,2]
$$
\det\left|D_{\alpha \beta}(q)-4\pi^{2}v^{2}M\delta_{\alpha \beta}\right|=0 \tag{1}
$$
where, $M$ is the ionic mass, $v$ is the phonon frequency and $D_{\alpha \beta}(q)$ is the dynamical matrix in which the force between two ions depends only upon the distance between them is given by
$$
D_{\alpha \beta}(q)=\left.\sum_{n}\left(1-e^{iqr}\right)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\Phi(r)}{\mathrm{d}r_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}r_{\beta}}\right|_{r=r_{n}} \tag{2}
$$
where $\Phi(r)$ is the interionic pair potential, $r_{\alpha}$ and $r_{\beta}$ are the $\alpha^{\text{th}}$ and $\beta^{\text{th}}$ Cartesian components of the position vector of $n^{\text{th}}$ ion, respectively.

This dynamical matrix element used in the present calculation finally takes the form as follows:
$$
D_{\alpha \beta}(q)=\sum_{n}\left(1-e^{iqr}\right)\left[K_{t}+\frac{r_{\alpha}r_{\beta}}{r^{2}}\cdot\left(K_{r}-K_{t}\right)\right] \tag{3}
$$
where $K_{t}$ and $K_{r}$ are the force constants between a pair of ions interacting through a central interaction and $n$ specifies shell index. $K_{t}$ and $K_{r}$ can be expressed as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{t}&=\frac{1}{r}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\Phi(r)}{\mathrm{d}r} \\
&=-\frac{Z^{2}e^{2}}{r^{3}}+\frac{\Omega_{0}}{\pi^{2}r^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(q)q^{2}\left[\cos(qr)-\frac{\sin(qr)}{qr}\right]\mathrm{d}q \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{r}&=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\Phi}{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}=\frac{2Ze^{2}}{r^{3}} \\
&\quad+\frac{\Omega_{0}}{\pi^{2}r^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(q)q^{2}\left[\frac{2\sin(qr)}{qr}-2\cos(qr)-qr\sin(qr)\mathrm{d}q\right] \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
$$
where $F(q)$ is the energy wave number characteristic given by
$$
F(q)=\frac{\Omega_{0}q^{2}}{8\pi e^{2}}\left|W_{\text{B}}(q)\right|^{2}\frac{\left[\varepsilon_{\text{H}}(q)-1\right]}{1+\left[\varepsilon_{\text{H}}(q)-1\right][1-f(q)]} \tag{6}
$$
with $\Omega_{0}$, $W_{\text{B}}(q)$, $\varepsilon_{\text{H}}(q)$ and $f(q)$ are the atomic volume, bare-ion pseudopotential, static Hartree dielectric function and local field correlation function, respectively.

The bare-ion pseudopotential due to Gajjar et al. [1,2] is given by
$$
V(q)=\frac{-8\pi Z}{\Omega_{0}q^{2}}\left(\cos(qr_{\text{C}})-\frac{\left(qr_{\text{C}}\right)^{2}}{1+\left(qr_{\text{C}}\right)^{2}}\right) \tag{7}
$$
where $Z$, $\Omega_{0}$ and $r_{\text{C}}$ are the valence, atomic volume and parameter of the model potential, respectively. The details of the model potential are narrated in Refs. [1,2].

## 3 Results and discussion

Moreover, instead of taking the concentration average of pure metallic alkali elements, we have treated here $\mathrm{A}_{1-X}\mathrm{B}_{X}$ ($X=0.5$; $\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{Na}$; $\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{Li}$, $\mathrm{K}$, $\mathrm{Rb}$, $\mathrm{Cs}$) as a PAA, which is a more meaningful approach [1-3]. Following definitions for the parameters of alloy $\mathrm{A}_{1-X}\mathrm{B}_{X}$ is adopted for making the computations of the PDCs of $\mathrm{Na}_{0.5}\mathrm{Li}_{0.5}$, $\mathrm{Na}_{0.5}\mathrm{K}_{0.5}$, $\mathrm{Na}_{0.5}\mathrm{Rb}_{0.5}$ and $\mathrm{Na}_{0.5}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.5}$ alloys [1-3]:
$$
r_{\text{C}}=(1-X)r_{\text{C(A)}}+Xr_{\text{C(B)}} \tag{8}
$$

$$
Z=(1-X)Z_{(\text{A})}+XZ_{(\text{B})} \tag{9}
$$

$$
\Omega_{0}=(1-X)\Omega_{0(\text{A})}+X\Omega_{0(\text{B})} \tag{10}
$$

$$
M=(1-X)M_{(\text{A})}+XM_{(\text{B})} \tag{11}
$$

$$
k_{F}=(1-X)k_{F(\text{A})}+Xk_{F(\text{B})} \tag{12}
$$

In evaluating the integration in Eqs. (4) and (5), the upper limit of the integral is taken as $40k_{F}$ so that a complete convergence of the model potential is achieved at higher momentum transfer and it covers all the oscillations of the form factor. Therefore, any artificial/fictitious cut-off in the present computations is avoided. We have performed the real space sum analysis up to 33 sets of nearest neighbours in $r$-space, which are sufficient for computing the elastic constants and bulk modulus using interatomic force constants, to consider a long-range character for proper convergence of the calculation and to achieve desired accuracy.

The input parameters viz. $Z$, $\Omega_{0}$ and $k_{F}$ used in the present computations of the PDCs of alkali metals and their equiatomic sodium based binary alloys are taken from [9]. The model potential parameter $r_{\text{C}}$ (in atomic units) for alkali metals viz. Li, Na, K, Rb and Cs metals are taken as 0.7738, 1.2182, 1.4031, 1.7880 and 1.9108, respectively. For equiatomic sodium-based binary alloys, the input parameters of the alloys are computed from the PAA model using Eqs. (8)-(12).

The PDCs of five alkali metals viz. Li, Na, K, Rb and Cs along [100], [110] and [111] directions of high symmetry, which are shown in Figs. 1–5 with available experimental findings [10–13]. The present results of pure alkali elements are found in qualitative agreement with the experimental data [10–13] available in the literature. From Figs. 1–5, we observed that, for pure metals like Na and K, the agreement of the present findings with the experimental data [10–13] is excellent. For Rb in [110] direction the comparison is found to be in good agreement, while in [100] and [111] directions, the better agreement with experimental data [10–13] was found for lower values of reduced wave vectors. For Li and Cs, present results deviate more from the experimental points [10–13]. Also, the PDC of four equiatomic sodium based binary alloys viz. $Na_{0.5}Li_{0.5}$, $Na_{0.5}K_{0.5}$, $Na_{0.5}Rb_{0.5}$ and $Na_{0.5}Cs_{0.5}$ along [100], [110] and [111] directions of high symmetry are displayed in Figs. 6–9.

We have found that the phonon frequencies are more sensitive to the exchange and correlation effects in the longitudinal branch in comparison with the transverse branches. The phonon frequencies in the longitudinal branch are suppressed due to IU-screening function than the phonon frequencies due to static H-screening function. While in the transverse branch, the effect of exchange and correlation slightly enhances the phonon modes. It is found that at the zone boundaries of [100] and [111] directions, i.e., for the larger momentum transfer the effect of local field correlation is almost negligible. These dispersion curves do not show any abnormality in the three regions of high symmetry directions and exhibit qualitative behavior like metallic elements.

## 4 Conclusions

In the end, we concluded that the presently computed results of the phonon dispersion relation of pure alkali elements are found in qualitative agreement with the experimental data. The present form of the PAA model is successful in explaining the phonon dispersion relation of equiatomic sodium based binary alloys and hence, it could be explored for predicting the behavior of other such solid solutions. The comparison of present theor- etical findings with the estimated experimental data helps us to note that the binding of solid $\mathrm{A}_{1-X} \mathrm{~B}_{X}(X=0.5$; $\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{Na} ; \mathrm{B}=\mathrm{Li}, \mathrm{K}, \mathrm{Rb}, \mathrm{Cs})$ binary alloys is comparable to the pure metals and therefore, it behaves like a metallic alloy system. This can be confirmed by investigating its total crystal energy and heat of solution. The present com- putation also confirms the applicability of the model potential and different forms of the local field correction functions.
