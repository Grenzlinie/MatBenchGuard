# Pressure derivatives of bulk and shear modulus
of rare gas solids

S. Gupta*, S.C. Goyal

Department of Physics, Agra College, Agra 282002, India

Received 2 September 2002; revised 20 December 2002; accepted 14 January 2003

## Abstract
Expressions for the isothermal bulk and shear modulus and also their first and second order pressure derivatives for rare gas solids are derived by a simple method considering the different interactions i.e. modified variable induce dipoles, short range overlap repulsion and the vibrational contribution. The derived relations for $dK_T/dP$, $dC_S/dP$, $dC_{44}'/dP$, $d^2K_T/dP^2$, $d^2C_S/dP^2$ and $d^2C_{44}'/dP^2$ are used to compute the numerical values of these constants at $P=0$ for Ne, Ar, Kr and Xe.

## 1. Introduction
The study of the isothermal bulk modulus and its pressure derivatives for the solids is an important tool for the study of the various physical properties [1-3] and the equation of state [4-8] which give us valuable informa- tion's about the relationship between the changes in pressure, volume and temperature and are also needed for the estimation of thermal properties. Various phenomen- ological theories [9-12] have been proposed for ionic solids. However, very few attempts [13-17] have been made to study the above properties in case of low transition temperature rare gas solids (RGS) due to their typical physical behavior.

Recently Hama-Suito [18] have reported the universal equation of state and has applied in case of RGS (Ne & Ar), metals and diatomic solids. In this study they have reported the fitted values of bulk modulus asnd its first and second order pressure derivatives at zero pressure. It is quite evident from their study that the theoretical values of the bulk modulus $(K_0)$, in case of diatomic solids and metals are very well in agreement with the experimental values. However, the theoretical values of $K_0$ in case of RGS (Ne & Ar) are much different from the experimental values [14] of these RGS. Thus it is also necessary to analysis the bulk modulus and its pressure derivatives in case of RGS.

Lehri and Verma [15,16] have developed a three-body interaction model considering variable induced dipoles for studying the lattice dynamics of RGS. On the basis of this model using the theory of finite deformation [1] the relation for the pressure derivatives of second-order elastic constants (SOEC) for RGS have also been derived.An analysis of short range interaction in RGS has been performed by Shanker and Rajauria [19] by considering two-body and three-body interaction terms and using essentially the same expressions of SOEC and TOEC as reported by Lehri and Verma [15,16]. They concluded that the results obtained from induce dipoles compare well with the triple interaction model due to Zucker and Paik [20].

The above studies of RGS by Lahri, Verma and Shanker [15,16,19] are limited to third order elastic constants (TOEC) hence as up to the first order pressure derivatives of the bulk and share modulus of the solids. But in order to investigate the higher order equation of state and other geophysical properties [5,6] one requires the values of higher order pressure derivatives of the second order elastic constant. ## 2. Theory

The potential energy $\phi$ of RGS can be expanded in terms of various $A$ coefficients which are related to potential energy coefficients as
$$
\phi=\phi_{0}+V A_{\alpha \beta} U_{\alpha \beta}+\frac{1}{2} V A_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta} U_{\alpha \beta} U_{\gamma \delta}
\tag{1}
$$
where $V$ is the volume of the unit cell $A$ coefficient are expressed as,
$$
A_{\alpha \beta}=V^{-1} \sum_{l^{\prime}}\left[\phi_{\alpha}\left(l^{\prime}\right) x_{\beta}\left(l^{\prime}\right)\right]
\tag{2}
$$
$$
A_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}=V^{-1} \sum_{l^{\prime}} \sum_{l^{\prime \prime}}\left[\phi_{\alpha v}\left(l^{\prime} l^{\prime \prime}\right) x_{\beta}\left(l^{\prime \prime}\right) x_{\delta}(l l)\right]
\tag{3}
$$

According to Brugger's definition [1] of various order elastic constants, the potential energy $\phi$ of the RGS can also be expanded in a series of power of the symmetric finite strain parameters $\eta_{\alpha \beta}$ as
$$
\phi=\phi_{0} V C_{\alpha \beta} \eta_{\alpha \beta}+\frac{1}{2} V C_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta} \eta_{\alpha \beta} \eta_{\gamma \delta}
\tag{4}
$$

Comparison of above Eqs. (1) and (4) leads to the following relations between the $A$ coefficients and the elastic constants:
$$
A_{\alpha \beta}=C_{\alpha \beta},
$$
$$
A_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}=C_{\beta \delta} \delta_{\alpha \gamma}+C_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}
\tag{5}
$$

Actually, $C_{\alpha \beta}$ and $C_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}$ are the mechanical analogue of the initial applied stress, second order elastic constants, respectively, and the $A_{\alpha \beta}$ and $A_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}$ are the mechanical analogous of the wave propagations coefficients. Mechanical approximation [1] to the stress strain coefficients is
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}= & C_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}+\frac{1}{2}\left(C_{\alpha \delta} \delta_{\beta \gamma}+C_{\beta \delta} \delta_{\alpha \gamma}+C_{\alpha \gamma} \delta_{\beta \delta}+C_{\beta \gamma} \delta_{\alpha \delta}\right. \\
& \left.-2 C_{\alpha \beta} \delta_{\gamma \delta}\right)
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

The initial stress must be isotropic pressure
$$
C_{\alpha \beta}=-P \delta_{\alpha \beta}
\tag{7}
$$

Hence, from Eq. (6), we get
$$
B_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}=C_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}-P\left(\delta_{\beta \delta} \delta_{\alpha \gamma}+\delta_{\alpha \delta} \delta_{\beta \gamma}-\delta_{\alpha \beta} \delta_{\gamma \delta}\right)
\tag{8}
$$

In view of Eq. (7), Eq. (5) reduces to
$$
C_{\alpha \beta}=A_{\alpha \beta}=-P \delta \alpha \beta
$$

$$
C_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}=A_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}+P \delta_{\beta \delta} \delta_{\alpha \gamma}
\tag{9}
$$
using the Voigt notation the above relations (7)-(9) reduce to
$$
B_{11}=C_{11}-P=A_{1111}
\tag{10a}
$$
$$
B_{12}=C_{12}+P=A_{1122}+P
\tag{10b}
$$
$$
B_{44}=C_{44}-P=A_{2323}
\tag{10c}
$$

From these conversions Eqs. (10a)-(10c), it is obvious that the expressions for the second order elastic constants can be derived from various order $A$ coefficients which are obtained by simply differentiating the known potential with respect to various position co-ordinates of the ions. The only condition is that the known potential must be in an analytical form i.e. it must be a function of relative positions of the ions in the solid.

In view of the above discussion the following relations for the potential energy $\phi$ for the RGS [15] is considered.
$$
\phi_{(r)}=\frac{A^{\prime} \lambda[\lambda+12 f(r)]}{r^{6}}+N w(r)+\frac{9}{8} K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}}
\tag{11}
$$
where $K_{\mathrm{B}}$ is Boltzman constant, $\theta_{\mathrm{D}}$ is the Debye temperature, $r$ is the nearest neighbour interatomic distance, $\lambda^{2}$ corresponds to the two body van der-walls coefficients. The first term denotes the van der-walls attractive potential energy modified by the three body interaction term arising from the variable induce dipoles due to overlapping of $(N)$ nearest neighbour atoms. The second term is the short range overlap repulsive potential energy. The least term represents the zero point energy. In case of RGS the volume of the unit cell $V=2 a^{3}$ ( $2 a$ is the lattice parameter). Using Eqs. (2), (5), (7), (10a)-(10c) and (11) the following expression for the pressure $(P)$ and the second order elastic constant using the various lattice sums [15] are derived and are given below:
$$
P=\frac{2}{a}\left[0.4516 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}-B+\frac{9}{32} \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}\right]
\tag{12}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{11}= & \frac{1}{2 a}\left[6.7862 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+2 A+1.6875 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}-20.439 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}\right. \\
& \left.+1.125 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{2}}{a^{2}}+a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{12}= & \frac{1}{2 a}\left[2.9302 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+A-20.439 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-0.2812 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}\right. \\
& \left.+1.125 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{2}}{a^{2}}-2.5 a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$

$$
C_{44}=\frac{1}{2 a}\left[2.9292 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+A-0.10546 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}+0.1875 a P\right]
\tag{15}
$$

where

$$
A=\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} w^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right]_{r=r_{0}}, \quad B=\left[\frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} w^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}\right]_{r=r_{0}}
\tag{16a}
$$

$$
\alpha^{\prime}=\lambda[\lambda+12 f(r)]
\tag{16b}
$$

$$
w^{\prime}(r)=w(r)+14.5 \frac{\lambda f(r)}{r_{0}^{6}}
\tag{16c}
$$

$$
w(r)=b \exp (-r / p)
\tag{16d}
$$

$$
\gamma=-\frac{\mathrm{d} \log \theta_{\mathrm{D}}}{\mathrm{d} \log V}
\tag{16e}
$$

Using the definition [1] of bulk modulus and share modulus, the above Eqs. (13)-(15) lead the expressions for the isothermal bulk modulus $(K_{\mathrm{T}})$ and share modulus as,

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm{T}}= & \frac{1}{6 a}\left[12.6448 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}-61.2992 \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}+4 A+1.1248 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}\right. \\
& \left.+3.374 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}-4 a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{17a}
$$

$$
C_{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{1}{4 a}\left[1.928 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+0.5 \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}+0.984375 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}+1.75 a P\right]
\tag{17b}
$$

$$
C_{44}=\frac{1}{2 a}\left[2.9292 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+A-0.10546 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}+0.1875 a P\right]
\tag{17c}
$$

Infact first and second order pressure derivatives of bulk and share modulus can be expressed as linear combinations of the SOEC and the third order elastic constants (TOEC), and the fourth order elastic constant (FOEC) which are generally derived [21] by well known methods invariably involving heavy algebra and exercise. In the following section we are presenting a simple producer to obtained the expressions of these constants for RGS.

### 3. First order pressure derivative of isothermal bulk-modulie $(K_{\mathrm{T}}^{\prime})$ and share modulie

The pressure derivatives of the constants require the relation for the variation of short range interaction parameter with pressure i.e. $\mathrm{d} A / \mathrm{d} P$ or $\mathrm{d} B / \mathrm{d} P$ which can be obtained from the definition of $A$ and $B$ Eq. (16a) and form the following conversion relation

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} p}=\frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} a} \frac{\mathrm{d} a}{\mathrm{~d} p} \text { etc. }
$$

The expression for $\mathrm{d} A / \mathrm{d} a$ and $\mathrm{d} B / \mathrm{d} a$ are derived with the help of relation (16c) for $w^{\prime}(r)$ and are given below:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} a}=\left[\frac{C}{a}-\frac{86.70 \times \sqrt{2}}{r^{7}} \lambda \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right]
\tag{18}
$$

and

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} B}{\mathrm{~d} a}=\left[\frac{A}{a}-\frac{B}{a}-\frac{86.70 \times 1.414 \lambda}{r^{8}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}\right]
\tag{19}
$$

here the parameter $C$ is defined as,

$$
C=\left[\frac{r \mathrm{~d}^{3} w^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{3}}\right]
\tag{20}
$$

Further the values of $\mathrm{d} a / \mathrm{d} P$ is obtained by differentiating Eq. (12) and using the derived expression for $\mathrm{d} B / \mathrm{d} a$ Eq. (19) as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d} P}{\mathrm{~d} a}= & \frac{1}{a^{2}}\left[-6.324 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+30.6496 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-2 A\right. \\
& \left.-0.5624 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}-1.6874 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{2}}{a^{2}}-a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$

where $\gamma$ is the Gruneisen parameter and assumed to be constant as it is related to the temperature and volume dependence of the entropy [14].

Now differentiating Eqs. (17a)-(17c) with pressure and using the definitions of $\mathrm{d} A / \mathrm{d} a$ and $\mathrm{d} B / \mathrm{d} a$ given by Eqs. (18) and (19), we have derived the following relations for $\mathrm{d} K_{T} / \mathrm{d} P, \mathrm{~d} C_{S} / \mathrm{d} P$ and $\mathrm{d} C_{44}^{\prime} / \mathrm{d} P$ as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d} K_{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrm{d} P}= & -\frac{1}{18 a K_{\mathrm{T}}}\left[-139.1084 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}-12 A+4 C\right. \\
& +827.721 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-130.052 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}} \\
& \left.+7.875 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{0} \gamma}{a^{2}}+3.375 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{2}}{a^{2}}-8 a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d} C_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{d} P}= & -\frac{1}{12 a K_{\mathrm{T}}}\left[-12.5748 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+6 A+C-41.832 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}}\right. \\
& \left.\times \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-10.838 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}-5.625 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}+9 a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d} C_{44}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d} P}= & -\frac{1}{6 a K_{\mathrm{T}}}\left[-27.5532 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}-1.375 A+C\right. \\
& +55.4582 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-10.8394 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}} \\
& \left.+0.2109 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}-0.375 a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$

It is interesting and supporting to note that the above derived Eq. (22) in view of Eq. (12) is in agreement with the relation derived by Shanker and Rajauria [19] using the TOEC constants. Thus the above derived expressions using simple method for the pressure derivatives of the share modulus $\mathrm{d} C_{\mathrm{S}} / \mathrm{d} P$ and $\mathrm{d} C_{44}^{\prime} / \mathrm{d} P$ are also important.

## 4. Second order pressure derivative of isothermal bulk modulus ($K''_{\text{T}}$) and share modulus

The variation of short range interaction parameter $C$ with pressure is derived as

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} C}{\mathrm{~d} a}=\left[\frac{4 C}{a}+\frac{D}{a}-\frac{14.45 \times 6}{r^{5}} \lambda \frac{\mathrm{d}^{3} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{3}}\right] \tag{25}
$$

where

$$
D=\left[r^{2} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{4} w^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{4}}\right]_{r=r_{0}} \tag{26}
$$

The above derived relations for $\mathrm{d} K_{\mathrm{T}} / \mathrm{d} P$, $\mathrm{d} C_{\mathrm{S}} / \mathrm{d} P$ and $\mathrm{d} C_{44}^{\prime} /$
$\mathrm{d} P$ Eqs. (22)-(24) are further used to derive the relations for $\mathrm{d}^{2} K_{\mathrm{T}} / \mathrm{d} P^{2}$, $\mathrm{d}^{2} C_{\mathrm{S}} / \mathrm{d} P^{2}$ and $\mathrm{d}^{2} C_{44}^{\prime} / \mathrm{d} P^{2}$ following the same procedure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} K_{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrm{d} P^{2}}= & \frac{1}{18 a K_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d} K_{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrm{d} P}\left(-139.108 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}-12 A+4 C\right.\right. \\
& \left.-827.721 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-130.059 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right. \\
& \left.+7.875 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D} \gamma}}{a^{2}}+3.375 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{2}}{a^{2}}-8 a P\right) \\
& +442.612 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}-3116.646 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r} \\
& +12 A-4 C+\frac{4}{3} D+736.957 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}} \\
& -81.759 \frac{\lambda}{a^{5}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{3} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{3}}-7.5937 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}} \\
& \left.-6.4687 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{2}}{a^{2}}-3.375 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{3}}{a^{2}}+8 a P\right] \quad(27)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} C_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{d} P^{2}}= & \frac{1}{12 a K_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d} K_{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrm{d} P}\left(-12.5748 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}+6 A+C\right.\right. \\
& -41.832 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-10.838 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}} \\
& \left.-5.0625 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}+9 a P\right)+18.7572 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}} \\
& -8 A-2 C+\frac{D}{3}+132.378 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r} \\
& -16.106 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}-10.223 \frac{\lambda}{a^{5}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{3} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{3}} \\
& \left.+3.375 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma^{3}}{a^{2}}+6 a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} C_{44}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d} P^{2}}= & \frac{1}{6 a K_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d} K_{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrm{d} P}\left(-27.5533 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}}-1.375 A+C\right.\right. \\
& +55.4582 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r}-10.8394 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}} \\
& \left.+0.21093 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}-0.375 a P\right)+83.499 \frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{a^{8}} \\
& -0.715 A+0.625 C+\frac{D}{3}+307.559 \frac{\lambda}{a^{7}} \frac{\mathrm{d} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r} \\
& +41.9464 \frac{\lambda}{a^{6}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}-5.1089 \frac{\lambda}{a^{5}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{3} f(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{3}} \\
& \left.-0.140625 \frac{K_{\mathrm{B}} \theta_{\mathrm{D}} \gamma}{a^{2}}+0.375 a P\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
