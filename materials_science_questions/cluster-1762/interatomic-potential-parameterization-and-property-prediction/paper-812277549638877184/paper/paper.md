The generalized form of exponential potential has been suitably extended to represent the correct nature of repulsive and attractive components of the forces for all the separations in general and that of small separations in particular. An additional parameter n is introduced to account for the multibody interactions in an indirect manner to some extent. The parameter m accounts effectively for the exchange and correlation effects due to electrons. The efficacy of the potential so developed has been tested by computing the second- and third-order elastic constants of Rh, Yb, Ir and Th for which Cauchy discrepancies are insignificant. Further, the pressure derivatives of the second-order elastic constants of the mentioned metals have also been computed. Finally, the potential is used to predict the cohesive energies of the mentioned metals which show remarkable agreement with the experimental values and thus establishing the empirical nature of the potential.

## 1 Introduction

The macroscopic studies (Thakur and Singh [1]) employed the force constants for paired and unpaired interactions which implicate the attractive and repulsive characters. Exponential form of the Born-Mayer potential has widely been used by Onwuaga [2], Upadhyaya and Prakash [3] to incorporate the unpaired forces into the interaction system of the metals. Dominant screened Coulombian interaction finds its implication in paired interactions when expressed by pseudo/model potentials in microscopic studies. Earlier forms of these potentials (Heine and Abarenkov [4], Ashcroft [5], Ho [6], Kumar [7], Sharma and Kachhava [8]), however, ignore the repulsive component of paired interactions. Modified form of model potentials (Idrees et al. [9], Khwaja and Razmi [10]) incorporates the repulsive character and adds to the efficacy of the paired interactions. The representation of unpaired interactions in the form of the Born-Mayer potential accounts only for the repulsive character. Some of the authors (Vrati et al. [11], Prakash and Upadhyaya [12]) have added the repulsive unpaired interactions superficially to the paired interactions. Moriarty [13] has shown that the multiparticle unpaired interactions assume the attractive as well as the repulsive characters. The core and the shell charge densities, responsible for the paired and unpaired interactions, have been represented by the exponential forms (Wang and Overhauser [14]). Analytical form of the paired potential (Pettifor and Ward [15]) adopts the damped exponential character. Generalized

The extended generalized exponential potential (EGEP) so developed is employed to explain the elastic behaviour of fcc metals.

## 2 Theory

### 2.1 Extended generalized exponential potential (EGEP)

The attractive as well repulsive components of the generalized exponential po- tential (Milstein [23]) have been extended for representing their true and realistic nature. Extended generalized form of exponential potential (EGEP) so developed assumes the form

$$
\Phi(r)=\frac{D}{m-1}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-m \alpha\left(r-r_{0}\right)} /(\alpha r)^{n}-m(\alpha r)^{n} \mathrm{e}^{-\alpha\left(r-r_{0}\right)}\right]. \tag{1}
$$

The average interaction (cohesive energy) energy per atom within the framework of EGEP is

$$
\Phi_{m}\left(r_{j}\right)=\frac{D}{2(m-1)} \sum_{j}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-m \alpha\left(r_{j}-r_{0}\right)} /\left(\alpha r_{j}\right)^{n}-m\left(\alpha r_{j}\right)^{n} \mathrm{e}^{-\alpha\left(r_{j}-r_{\alpha}\right)}\right], \tag{2}
$$

where $D$ stands for the dissociation energy, $\alpha$ for the hardness parameter and $r_{0}$ for the equilibrium separation parameter and $r_{j}$ for the distance of the $j$-th atom from the origin given by

$$
r_{j}=\sqrt{l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2}+l_{3}^{2}} a=L_{j} a. \tag{3}
$$

The parameter $n$ appearing in Eq. (1) does not account explicitly for the three- body interactions but its presence in the potential function introduces implicitly the three-body character. The parameter $m$ mainly delivers the electronic exchange and correlation corrections in the screening function. It may be noted that the exchange

For a given value of the parameter $n$, the parameter $m$ is adjusted to the most suitable value, which besides accounting for the appropriate depth, implies to some extent the incorporation of many-body forces in fcc metals under study. The parameter $n$ therefore, accounts for and also stresses the role of many-body forces indirectly for describing our interaction potential, as the mentioned parameter affects the depth [24] as well as the position [25] of the potential minima.

Mathematically, the condition (Milstein [23])

$$
\sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}}\left[l_{1}^{2} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \Phi(r)}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right]=0
\tag{4}
$$

for the equilibrium of the crystal in the absence of external forces is employed to compute the value of $\beta$ such that

$$
\beta=\exp \left(\alpha r_{0}\right).
\tag{5}
$$

The bulk modulus can be expressed as

$$
B=\frac{r^{2}}{9 V}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \Phi}{\mathrm{d} r^{2}}\right)_{r=r_{0}}.
\tag{6}
$$

The parameter $D$ can be evaluated through the expression of the bulk modulus following the condition given by Eq. (4) for stress-free lattice. The following expression for $D$ is obtained

$$
D=\frac{18 V B(m-1)}{P-Q},
\tag{7}
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
P= & \left\{\beta^{m}\left(\alpha a_{0}\right)^{-n}\left[\left(m \alpha a_{0}\right)^{2} \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} L_{j}^{-(n-2)} \exp \left(-m \alpha a_{0} L_{j}\right)+2 n\left(m \alpha a_{0}\right)\right.\right. \\
& \left.\left.\times \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} L_{j}^{-(n-1)} \exp \left(-m \alpha a_{0} L_{j}\right)+n(n+1) \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} L_{j}^{-n} \exp \left(-m \alpha a_{0} L_{j}\right)\right]\right\},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q= & \left\{m \beta\left(\alpha a_{0}\right)^{n}\left[\left(\alpha a_{0}\right)^{2} \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} L_{j}^{(n+2)} \exp \left(-\alpha a_{0} L_{j}\right)\right.\right. \\
& \left.\left.-2 n\left(\alpha a_{0}\right) \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} L_{j}^{(n+1)} \exp \left(-\alpha a_{0} L_{j}\right)+n(n-1) \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} L_{j}^{n} \exp \left(-\alpha a_{0} L_{j}\right)\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2.2 Analytical form of the EGEP
The computed potential parameters are used to arrive at a graphical representation of fcc metals under study (i.e. Rh, Yb, Ir and Th) using Eq. (1). Figure 1 shows the variation of the present potential function versus the interatomic distance $r$ for Rh ($n = 0.5$ and 2.0, $m = 1.5$), Yb ($n = 0.5$ and 2.0, $m = 1.5$), Ir ($n = 1$ and 2, $m = 15$) and Th ($n = 0.5$ and 2.0, $m = 1.5$). The potential confirms the observed cohesion in metals and therefore, the empiricial nature of the potential is confirmed.

### 2.3 The second-order and third order elastic constants
The following expressions for the SOEC and TOEC with the present interatomic interactions are used (Born [26])

$$
C_{11}=\frac{n^{\prime} a^{4}}{2 V} \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} l_{1}^{4} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \Phi(r)}{\left(\mathrm{d} r^{2}\right)^{2}}, \tag{8}
$$

$$
C_{111}=\frac{n^{\prime} a^{6}}{V} \sum_{l_{1} l_{2} l_{3}} l_{1}^{6} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{3} \Phi(r)}{\left(\mathrm{d} r^{2}\right)^{3}}, \tag{9}
$$

where $n'$ is the number of atoms per unit cell (4 for fcc and 2 for bcc) and $V$ represents the atomic volume.

Equation (8) transforms to represent $C_{12}$ when $l_{1}^{4}$ in the said equation is replaced by $l_{1}^{2} l_{2}^{2}$. Similarly, equation (9) transforms to represent $C_{112}(=C_{166})$ and $C_{123}(=C_{456}=C_{144})$ when $l_{1}^{6}$ in the said equation is replaced by $l_{1}^{4} l_{2}^{2}$ and $l_{1}^{2} l_{2}^{2} l_{3}^{2}$, respectively.

### 2.4 The pressure derivatives of SOEC
Birch [27] derived a set of effective elastic constants which determines the response of an initially stressed crystal to an additional infinitesimal strains. From these values, the pressure derivatives of the second-order elastic constants have been calculated.

## 4 Conclusions

The motivation for taking up this study was to examine the applicability of the extended generalized exponential potential (EGEP) in explaining the statical properties of fcc metals. We have, therefore, used the mentioned potential to calculate the second- and third-order elastic constants of Rh, Yb, Ir, and Th considering interactions of up to 248 atoms. It may be noted that the calculated second- and third-order elastic constants (Table 3) compare reasonably well with the available experimental values for the metals under study for which Cauchy discrepancies are insignificant. Our results also confirm that in general $C_{11}/C_{12}>1$ for all values of $m$, which is an essential condition (Milstein [28]) for mechanical stability of

M.L. Verma, R.P.S. Rathore, and A. Verma

the solids. The computed values of pressure derivatives of the second-order elastic constants are given in Table 4.

Besides predicting satisfactorily the elastic behaviour of the metals, the extended generalized exponential potential (EGEP) has produced remarkably good results particularly in the case of cohesive energy, which confirm the empirical nature of the potential. It may be concluded, therefore, that the present model is a good representation of average and effective potential for metals on account of the following grounds:

(a) The parameter $n$ appearing in Eq. (1) properly takes care of the multibody interactions in an alternative and simpler manner. For $n=0$, our potential transforms to represent *the generalized exponential potential* [23], while for $n=0$ and $m=2$, our potential transforms to represent *Morse potential* and the three-body forces cease to exist. As soon as $n$ is given any positive value, the three-body forces implicitly appear in our interaction system and represent *the extended generalized exponential potential* (EGEP).

(b) Perusal of Table 5 shows that EGEP successfully explains the cohesion in metals under study. The consistency of the results of cohesive energy points out the merit of our potential in the sense that repulsive and attractive part of cohesion change as the value of $m$ changes from 1.5 to 6 for a given value of $n$, but the values of cohesive energy for the metals study remain unaltered.

(c) Our potential does not resort to a fitting procedure either to measured elastic constants or to the measured phonon frequencies. The potential is, therefore, free from those inconsistencies which are associated with these fitting procedures.

(d) The study of the lattice dynamics and the strength and stability of these metals in the framework of the mentioned potential is nearing completion with encouraging results and will be reported shortly.
