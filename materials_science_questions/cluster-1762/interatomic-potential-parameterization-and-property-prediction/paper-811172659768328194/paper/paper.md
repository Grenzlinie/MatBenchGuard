# Theoretical calculation of superconducting transition temperature in vanadium under pressure

G. Vaitheeswaran$^{\text{a}}$, I.B. Shameen Banu$^{\text{b}}$, M. Rajagopalan$^{\text{a},*}$

$^{\text{a}}$Department of Physics, Anna University, Chennai 600 025, India
$^{\text{b}}$Department of Physics, Crescent Engineering College, Chennai 600 048, India

Received 16 June 2000; accepted 27 July 2000 by C.N.R. Rao

## Abstract

We report a calculation of band structure and superconductivity of vanadium under pressure, performed with the Tight Binding Linear Muffin Tin Orbital method. The effect of pressure on the band structure has been obtained. The superconducting transition temperature ($T_{\text{c}}$) has been calculated using the Allen-Dynes formula. The effect of pressure on the phonon frequency has been taken into account. It is found that $T_{\text{c}}$ increases continuously with pressure up to 130 GPa which is in agreement with experimental observations. The increase in $T_{\text{c}}$ is attributed to the continuous s $\rightarrow$ d electron transfer under pressure. © 2000 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

Keywords: D. Electronic band structure; D. Electron-phonon interactions

## 1. Introduction

The recent development for the measurement of electric and magnetic properties using a diamond anvil cell enables one to increase the pressure range to very high value where the electronic states of substances change drastically from those at ambient pressure. The subject in this pressure range focuses mainly on superconductivity to show pressure induced superconductivity at megabar pressures, very recently the pressure dependence of superconducting transition temperature $T_{\text{c}}$ of vanadium has been reported [1] up to 120 GPa. It was found that $T_{\text{c}}$ increased with pressure with a coefficient of $\text{d}T_{\text{c}}/\text{d}p = 0.1$ K/GPa, and the maximum $T_{\text{c}}$ reached 17.2 K, the highest $T_{\text{c}}$ among the elemental metals reported so far.

Superconductivity of the vanadium group (V, Nb, Ta) has been well studied. Unlike Nb and Ta, V has a large positive pressure coefficient of $T_{\text{c}}$ and $T_{\text{c}}$ increases with pressure. It is found that there is a linear increase in the $T_{\text{c}}$ of vanadium as a function of pressure up to 2.4 GPa [2]. Subsequently a monotonic increase in $T_{\text{c}}$ is observed up to 18 GPa [3].

The electrical resistance measurement was carried out up to 49 GPa [4] to investigate to what extent the monotonic increase in $T_{\text{c}}$ would hold. The authors interpreted such behaviour of $T_{\text{c}}$ under pressure as a result of the suppression of the fluctuations of the electron spins through the broadening of the d-band width.

In this paper we report the theoretical calculation of $T_{\text{c}}$ as a function of pressure using the outputs from the self-consistent band structure calculation performed by tight binding Linear Muffin Tin Orbital method (TB-LMTO). The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, the methodology of band structure calculation are outlined. Section 3 deals with the calculation of $T_{\text{c}}$. The final section presents the results and discussion.

### 3. Calculation of $T_{\mathrm{c}}$

The superconducting transition temperature is calculated using the Allen-Dynes formula [12]

$$
T_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{\langle\omega\rangle}{1.2} \exp \left\{\frac{-1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^{*}(1+0.62 \lambda)}\right\}
\tag{1}
$$

where $\langle\omega\rangle$ is the average phonon frequency, $\lambda$ the electron-phonon coupling constant, and $\mu^{*}$ the electron-electron interaction constant. McMillan [13] had shown that $\lambda$ can be written as

$$
\lambda=\frac{N\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)\left\langle I^{2}\right\rangle}{M\left\langle\omega^{2}\right\rangle}
\tag{2}
$$

where $M$ is the atomic mass, $\left\langle\omega^{2}\right\rangle$ an average squared phonon frequency, and $\left\langle I^{2}\right\rangle$ the square of the electron-phonon matrix element averaged over the Fermi surface. In the atomic sphere approximation $\left\langle I^{2}\right\rangle$ can be written as (in atomic Rydberg units) [14]

$$
\left\langle I^{2}\right\rangle=2 \sum_{l} \frac{l+1}{(2 l+1)(2 l+3)} M_{l, l+1}^{2} \frac{N_{l}\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right) N_{l+1}\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)}{N\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right) N\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)}
\tag{3}
$$

where $M_{l, l+1}$ are the electron-phonon matrix elements which can be expressed in terms of the logarithmic derivatives

$$
D_{l}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d} \ln \phi_{l}}{\mathrm{~d} \ln r}\right|_{r=s}
\tag{4}
$$

evaluated at the sphere boundary.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{l, l+1}= & -\phi_{l} \phi_{l+1}\left[\left(D_{l}\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)-l\right)\left(D_{l+1}\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)+l+2\right)\right. \\
& \left.+\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}-V(S) S^{2}\right]\right.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

where $V(S)$ is the one-electron potential and $\phi_{l}$, the amplitude of the $l$ partial wave function evaluated at $E_{\mathrm{F}}$. $N_{l}$ is the partial density of states for the angular momentum quantum number $l$.

Using the density of states (DOS) obtained from band structure results the value of $\mu^{*}$ is calculated by the above equation. The value is found to be 0.248, which is in agreement with the reported value [17].

It is well known that the phonon frequency or Debye temperature changes with pressure. This change in frequency with pressure is taken into account by a simple but straightforward relation given in Ref. [16] which is

$$
\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{D}}(P)}{\Theta_{\mathrm{D}}(0)}=\frac{a(0)}{a(P)} \sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathrm{F}}(P)}{E_{\mathrm{F}}(0)}}
$$

where $\Theta_{\mathrm{D}}$ is the Debye temperature related to the phonon frequency and the symbols 0 and $P$ refer to the corresponding quantities under normal and pressurised conditions. The Fermi energy $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ and the lattice parameter ‘$a$’ are taken from the self-consistent band structure calculations.

The above expression is used to calculate the phonon frequency as a function of pressure and the superconducting transition temperature is calculated as a function of pressure in a manner similar to our earlier works [18–22] Values of $T_{\rm c}$ along with other parameters that enter into the expression to calculate $T_{\rm c}$ are given in Table 2. The variation of $T_{\rm c}$ with pressure is presented in Fig. 1 along with the experimental results. It is seen from the table that the value of $T_{\rm c}$ increases with increase in pressure up to 130 GPa. This increase in $T_{\rm c}$ with pressure is attributed to a continuous s to d electron transfer under pressure.

### 4. Conclusions

The band structure of vanadium in the bcc phase as a function of pressure has been calculated by means of the TB-LMTO method. The total energies are computed as a function of volume from which the equilibrium lattice parameter, pressure and bulk modulus are obtained. From the band structure calculations we find that the s-like states move toward higher energies and the d-like states drop down, i.e., there is a continuous s to d electron transfer under pressure. The d-electron number, which is 3.64 electrons at ambient pressure, increases to 3.86 electrons at 130 GPa. The superconducting transition temperature is calculated as a function of pressure using the Allen–Dynes formula. The value of $T_{\rm c}$ increases with pressure. The increase of $T_{\rm c}$ is attributed to the continuous s to d electron transfer under pressure. The theoretically calculated values of $\lambda$, $\mu^{*}$ and $T_{\rm c}$ are in good agreement with experimental and other theoretically calculated values. The effect of pressure on the phonon frequency is taken into account by a simple but straightforward method. From this study we find that the phonon frequency increases with pressure. Theoretical or experimental phonon dispersion studies are necessary to observe the behaviour of phonon under pressure.
