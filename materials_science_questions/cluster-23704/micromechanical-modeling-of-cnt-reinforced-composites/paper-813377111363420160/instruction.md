# Stress-Strain Response of Randomly Oriented SWCNT Aggregates with Density Study

## Problem background
Carbon nanotube (CNT) aggregates, composed of many randomly dispersed nanotubes, are promising reinforcing components in nanocomposites. Understanding their mechanical behaviour—particularly the emergence of isotropic response and the dependence of Young's modulus on density—is challenging because direct molecular dynamics simulations become prohibitively expensive as the number of nanotubes grows. This work studies the mechanics of short single-walled CNT (SWCNT) aggregates using a molecular mechanics approach. The goal is to determine the stress-strain curves, to evaluate whether a sufficiently large number of randomly distributed CNTs yields an isotropic mechanical response, and to quantify how the Young's modulus varies with the aggregate density.

## Approach
The core idea is to replace costly molecular dynamics with an incremental constrained minimization procedure. The AIREBO potential models both covalent carbon–carbon bonds and the weaker inter-tube van der Waals interactions. Initial configurations are generated by randomly dispersing (5,5) armchair SWCNTs (length 2 nm, 160 atoms each) in a cubic simulation cell. The orientation of each tube follows a spherical uniform distribution constructed via the trig method, and the tubes are placed at random positions while avoiding covalent overlaps. Loading is applied by incrementally stretching the cell in one direction at a time, conformally rescaling atom coordinates, fixing the positions of CNT end atoms, and minimising the potential energy at each strain increment. The stress is obtained from the virial expression. By repeating this procedure for multiple CNT counts at constant density, the directional convergence of the stress-strain response can be assessed. By varying the density while keeping the CNT count fixed, the relationship between density and Young's modulus can be investigated.

## Reproduction target
Produce the following results using LAMMPS with the AIREBO potential and your own implementation of the random aggregate generator and the incremental constrained minimization loop:

1. For 25 CNTs at a constant density of 0.08 g/cm³, compute the stress-strain curve up to 5% strain (averaged over the x, y, and z loading directions).
2. Also compute the orientational order parameter S = ⟨(3 cos²θ − 1)/2⟩ for each of these configurations.
3. For aggregates of 800 CNTs, repeat the loading at densities of 0.10, 0.12, 0.14, and 0.16 g/cm³ and report the corresponding Young's moduli.

All output must be written to the specified CSV files in /app/outputs as detailed in the workflow steps.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Generate random CNT aggregate configurations
- Role: process
- Action: Generate random initial atomic configurations for (5,5) armchair SWCNTs (length 2 nm, 160 atoms each) randomly dispersed in a cubic cell using the spherical uniform orientation method (trig method) and uniform spatial placement, avoiding covalent overlaps. Generate configurations for CNT counts N = 25, 50, 200, 800, 1000 at a constant density of 0.08 g/cm³, and for N = 800 at densities 0.10, 0.12, 0.14, 0.16 g/cm³.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/configurations.log`

### Step 2: Run incremental constrained minimization simulations
- Role: process
- Action: For each configuration from step_config_gen, perform the incremental constrained minimization loading procedure using LAMMPS with the AIREBO potential. For each direction (x, y, z), repeatedly expand the cell incrementally (up to 5% total strain), conformally rescale atom coordinates, fix the positions of CNT end atoms, minimize the energy, and record the virial stress after each increment. Obtain stress-strain data for every configuration and also save the final relaxed configurations for order parameter calculation.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/simulation.log`

### Step 3: Extract stress-strain curve for 25 CNTs
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the simulation data for the 25-CNT configuration, compute the averaged stress-strain curve (average of the three loading directions) and write it to step_01_stress_strain_25_CNTs.csv containing at least 500 points spanning 0 to 5% strain.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_01_stress_strain_25_CNTs.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Comma-separated values with columns: strain (dimensionless), stress (GPa). At least 500 rows covering strain from 0 to 0.05.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 4: Compute Young's moduli for increasing CNT counts
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: For each CNT count (50, 200, 800, 1000) at constant density 0.08 g/cm³, compute the Young's modulus from the stress-strain slope in each direction, then calculate the mean Young's modulus (GPa) and the coefficient of variance across directions. Write the results to step_02_young_modulus_aggregates.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_02_young_modulus_aggregates.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Comma-separated values with columns: num_cnts (integer), young_modulus_gpa (float), coefficient_of_variance (float, fraction). One row per CNT count.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 5: Compute order parameters
- Role: scored
- Action: For each CNT count (50, 200, 800, 1000), calculate the orientational order parameter S = ⟨(3 cos²θ − 1)/2⟩ using the CNT axis directions from the initial configurations (or after relaxation). Write the results to step_03_order_parameter.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_03_order_parameter.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Comma-separated values with columns: num_cnts (integer), order_parameter (float between -0.5 and 1). One row per CNT count.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 6: Perform density study with 800 CNTs
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: For the 800-CNT configurations at densities 0.10, 0.12, 0.14, 0.16 g/cm³, compute the Young's modulus (average over the three loading directions). Write the density–modulus pairs to step_04_density_study.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_04_density_study.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Comma-separated values with columns: density_g_cm3 (float), young_modulus_gpa (float). One row per density tested.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

## How you are scored
Your submission will be scored by an automated checker that evaluates each of the four scored output files independently, then combines the per-step scores into a single reward (0–1). For step_01, the checker will compute the root-mean-square deviation between your stress-strain curve and a reference curve; a lower RMSE yields a higher score. For steps_02–04, the checker will compare your reported Young's moduli and order parameters to reference values; the closer your entries, the higher the score. The overall reward is a weighted sum of these per-artifact scores. Simply reporting literature values without running the full simulation pipeline will produce results that are inconsistent and will be penalised by the checker. The verifier is deterministic and self-contained; it does not rely on network access.
