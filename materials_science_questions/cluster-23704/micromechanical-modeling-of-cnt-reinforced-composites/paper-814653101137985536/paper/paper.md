# Effects of interphase on tensile strength of polymer/CNT nanocomposites by Kelly-Tyson theory

### Abstract

The micromechanics models for composites usually underpredict the tensile strength of polymer nanocomposites. This paper establishes a simple model based on Kelly-Tyson theory for tensile strength of polymer/CNT nanocomposites assuming the effect of interphase between polymer and CNT. In addition, Pukanszky model is joined with the suggested model to calculate the interfacial shear strength ($\tau$), interphase strength ($\sigma_i$) and critical length of CNT ($L_c$).

The proposed approach is applied to calculate $\tau$, $\sigma_i$ and $L_c$ for various samples from recent literature. It is revealed that the experimental data are well fitted to calculations by new model which confirm the important effect of interphase on the properties of nanocomposites. Moreover, the derived equations demonstrate that dissimilar correlations are found between $\tau$ and B (from Pukanszky model) as well as $L_c$ and B. It is shown that a large B value obtained by strong interfacial adhesion between polymer and CNT is adequate to reduce $L_c$ in polymer/CNT nanocomposites.

Keywords: Polymer/CNT nanocomposites; interface/interphase; tensile strength; micromechanics modeling

### 1. Introduction

In the recent years, carbon nanotubes (CNT) have shown many advantages in polymer industries such as largely mechanical, thermal, chemical and electrical properties (Johnson et al., 2009; Kundalwal and Ray, 2012; Seidel and Lagoudas, 2006; Zare, 2013; Zare and Garmabi, 2014a). The polymer/CNT nanocomposites are currently applied in many fields such as conducting plastics, aerospace structures, automotive components, sporting goods, optical barriers, biomaterial devices, nano-sensors, etc. (Zare, 2015). Therefore, the different aspects of polymer/CNT nanocomposites are valuable to study to find the best processing and material conditions which give the best properties.

It is known that the increased properties of polymer/CNT nanocomposites compared to unfilled polymers significantly depend on the interfacial bonding of CNT particles with polymer matrix (Moaseri et al., 2014; Pan et al., 2013; Sharma et al., 2010). Pan et al. (Pan et al., 2013) found that the viscoelastic behavior of polymer/CNT nanocomposites is very sensitive to the interface condition, and thus, the surface functionalization of CNT is necessary to take the full potential of CNT in nanocomposites. Moreover, it was reported that the interfacial interactions can significantly change the polymer composition, structure and properties in a thin layer near the interface (Dorigato et al., 2013). In the recent years, much research has been conducted to characterization of interface/interphase between polymer

and nanofiller in polymer nanocomposites (Zare, 2014a, b; Zare and Garmabi, 2014b, 2015a). Therefore, assuming a third phase between polymer matrix and CNT particles with different properties from both primary constituents is much necessary to understand the unexpected behavior of nanocomposites.

However, the experimental work on the interfacial characteristics is much limited due to the manipulation of components at nano-scale (Zare et al., 2014; Zare and Garmabi, 2012b; Zare et al., 2011). Unfortunately, a precise and practical technique for approximation of interfacial interactions has not been given yet. Accordingly, many researchers have applied modeling methods for characterization of interphase in the nanocomposites (Leterrier et al., 2010; Seidel and Puydupin-Jamin, 2011; Zare and Garmabi, 2015b). In other words, a simple and valuable estimation of interphase characteristics can be achieved by modeling procedure.

In the present article, we focus on the interphase properties in polymer/CNT nanocomposites. The Kelly-Tyson theory is developed for tensile strength of short fiber reinforced composites and a simple equation is suggested which considers the effects of interphase. In addition, Pukanszky model is combined with suggested model to calculate the interfacial shear strength ($\tau$), interphase strength ($\sigma_i$) and critical length of CNT ($L_c$). Subsequently, we use the suggested method for

estimation and evaluation of interphase properties in several samples from valid literature.

## 2. Theoretical modeling

The applied load to polymer/CNT samples should be transferred to CNT by means of shear forces at the polymer-CNT interface (Figure 1) (Kundalwal et al., 2014). A high shear stress is developed at the fiber ends. According to Kelly-Tyson theory, when the maximum stress in CNT is higher than its failure stress, it breaks (Vlasveld et al., 2005). With decreasing the CNT length (L), a critical length ($L_c$) is obtained as the minimum CNT length in which the CNT can be fractured by stress transfer via polymer- CNT interface. It means that at $L > L_c$, the shear stress which can be transferred through the interface is not large enough to reach the CNT failure stress; but, at $L < L_c$, the CNT is pulled out of matrix before its failure. As a result, the strength of CNT nanocomposites significantly depends to $L_c$, which is expressed as:

$$
L_{c}=\frac{\sigma_{f} R}{\tau}=\frac{\sigma_{f} L}{2 \tau \alpha} \tag{1}
$$

where $\sigma_f$ is the tensile strength of CNT. R is CNT radius and $\tau$ is interfacial shear strength. Also, $\alpha$ is aspect ratio of CNT as the ratio of L to diameter ($\alpha$ = L/d). The tensile strength of CNT ($\sigma_f$) varies from 11 to 63 GPa with no obvious dependence on outer shell diameter (Salvetat et al., 1999). As a result, a mean value of 37 GPa

can be assumed for $\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}$. Furthermore, it was reported that $\tau$ hardly reaches to about 10 MPa in polymer/CNT nanocomposites (Barber et al., 2003). Assuming a high approximation for CNT radius as R=10 nm, $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$ reaches to $37 \mu \mathrm{m}$ in the best condition, while the CNT length usually is in order of 1-20 $\mu \mathrm{m}$. Therefore, CNT is considered as short fiber in polymer/CNT nanocomposites $(\mathrm{L}<\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}})$. This idea will also be confirmed in the next section.

When $\mathrm{L}<\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$, the volume fraction of CNT $(\phi_{f})$ is always less than a certain value ( $\phi_{\text {min }}$ ), so the failure of the nanocomposite occurs by plastic flow of the matrix (Lazzeri and Phuong, 2014). Thus, the strength of polymer/CNT nanocomposites can be calculated by a model based on Kelly-Tyson theory for short fibers reinforced composites (Kelly and Davies, 1965) as:

$$
\sigma_{R}=\frac{\eta_{o} \alpha \tau \phi_{f}}{\sigma_{m}}+1-\phi_{f} \tag{2}
$$

where $\sigma_{\mathrm{R}}$ is relative tensile strength as $\sigma_{\mathrm{R}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{c}} / \sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$; $\sigma_{\mathrm{c}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ are the tensile strength of nanocomposite and polymer matrix, respectively. $\eta_{\mathrm{o}}$ is an orientation factor, which is equally to 1 for fully alignment, 3/8 for randomly in-plane two-dimensional (2D) orientation and 1/5 for randomly 3D arrangement of fibers.

When the development of an interphase in the polymer/CNT nanocomposites is assumed, the tensile strength of nanocomposites can be obtained by:

$$
\sigma_{c} A_{c}=\sigma_{m} A_{m}+\tau A_{i} \tag{3}
$$

where A is the surface area and c, m and i subscripts indicate the nanocomposite, matrix and interphase, respectively. The number of nanotubes in a nanocomposite is calculated by assuming the aligned CNT in an intersection as:

$$
n=\frac{A_{c} \phi_{f}}{\pi R^{2}} \tag{4}
$$

In this regards, $A_m$ and $A_i$ are expressed as:

$$
A_{m}=A_{c}-n \pi(R+t)^{2} \tag{5}
$$

$$
A_{i}=2 \eta_{o} n \pi(R+t) L_{p} \tag{6}
$$

where t is the thickness of interphase. Also, $L_p$ is pullout length of CNT. When fracture occurs, the CNT is pulled out of broken side, where the length is shorter. In other words, $L_p$ varies from zero up to half CNT length and the average $L_p$ is a quarter of L as $L_p$ = L/4 (Coleman et al., 2004). It shows that the CNTs do not break on the fracture of nanocomposite, i.e. the CNTs are generally much shorter than $L_c$ which justify the use of suggested equation for short fibers ($\text{L} < \text{L}_\text{c}$).

Replacing Eqs. 4-6 into Eq. 3, we get:

$$
\sigma_{c}=\eta_{o} \alpha \tau\left(1+\frac{t}{R}\right) \phi_{f}+\sigma_{m}\left[1-\left(1+\frac{t}{R}\right)^{2} \phi_{f}\right] \tag{7}
$$

This model can measure the thickness of interphase (t) by tensile strength results. If the effects of interphase are neglected (t=0), Eq. 7 reduces to Eq. 2. However, the value of $\tau$ is needed to characterize t.

Pukanszky model is applied to calculate $\tau$ in polymer/CNT nanocomposites.

Pukanszky (Pukanszky, 1990) developed a simple model based on the spontaneous formation of interphase in composites which assumes the dependence of tensile strength on the composition as:

$$
\sigma_{c}=\sigma_{m} \frac{1-\phi_{f}}{1+2.5 \phi_{f}} \exp \left(B \phi_{f}\right) \tag{8}
$$

where B parameter shows a quantitative measurement for filler-matrix adhesion as:

$$
B=\left(1+S \rho_{f} t\right) \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{9}
$$

where S is the specific surface area of filler at the interface and $\rho_{f}$ is density of filler. Also, $\sigma_{i}$ is interphase strength. The Pukanszky model can be rearranged as:

$$
\ln \left(\sigma_{R} \frac{1+2.5 \phi_{f}}{1-\phi_{f}}\right)=B \phi_{f} \tag{10}
$$

From the latter equation, B can be calculated from:

$$
B=\frac{\ln \left(\sigma_{R} \frac{1+2.5 \phi_{f}}{1-\phi_{f}}\right)}{\phi_{f}} \tag{11}
$$

Substituting $\sigma_{\mathrm{R}}$ from Eq. 2 into Eq. 11, we get:

$$
B=\frac{\ln \left(\frac{1+2.5 \phi_{f}}{1-\phi_{f}}\right)+\ln \left(\frac{\eta_{o} \alpha \tau}{\sigma_{m}} \phi_{f}+1-\phi_{f}\right)}{\phi_{f}} \tag{12}
$$

When $\phi_{f}$ is much little $(\phi_{f} \rightarrow 0)$, we can approximate:

$$
\ln \left(\frac{1+2.5 \phi_{f}}{1-\phi_{f}}\right) \cong 3.04 \phi_{f} \tag{13}
$$

$$
\ln \left(\frac{\eta_{o} \alpha \tau}{\sigma_{m}} \phi_{f}+1-\phi_{f}\right)=\ln \left[1-\phi_{f}\left(1-\frac{\eta_{o} \alpha \tau}{\sigma_{m}}\right)\right] \cong-\phi_{f}\left(1-\frac{\eta_{o} \alpha \tau}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{14}
$$

Replacing Eqs. 13-14 into Eq. 12, B can be simply calculated by:

$$
B=2.04+\frac{\eta_{o} \alpha \tau}{\sigma_{m}} \tag{15}
$$

Therefore, $\tau$ is correlated to B by:

$$
\tau=\frac{\sigma_{m}(B-2.04)}{\eta_{o} \alpha} \tag{16}
$$

Substituting $\tau$ from Eq. 16 into Eq. 7, the tensile strength of polymer/CNT nanocomposites can be calculated by:

$$
\sigma_{R}=(B-2.04)\left(1+\frac{t}{R}\right) \phi_{f}+1-\left(1+\frac{t}{R}\right)^{2} \phi_{f} \tag{17}
$$

which can directly give the interphase thickness (t) by experimental data of tensile strength.

The tensile strength of interphase $(\sigma_{i})$ can also be calculated by B parameter. The specific surface area of CNT at interface (S) can be given by:

$$
S=\frac{A_{i}}{m}=\frac{2 \pi R L}{\rho_{f} V}=\frac{2 \pi R L}{\rho_{f} \pi R^{2} L}=\frac{2}{\rho_{f} R} \tag{18}
$$

By substituting S in Eq. 9, we get:

$$
B=\left(1+\frac{2 t}{R}\right) \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{19}
$$

Now, $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ can be calculated by rearranging of Eq. 19 as:

$$
\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{m} \exp \left(\frac{B}{1+\frac{2 t}{R}}\right) \tag{20}
$$

Also, the critical length of CNT (Lc) can be connected to B parameter using $\tau$ from Eq. 16 in Eq. 1 as:

$$
L_{c}=\frac{\sigma_{f} L}{2 \tau \alpha}=\frac{\eta_{o} \sigma_{f} L}{2 \sigma_{m}(B-2.04)} \tag{21}
$$

which expresses the dependence of $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$ to $\mathrm{B}, \sigma_{\mathrm{f}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $\mathrm{L}$.

### 3. Results and discussion

In this work, $\eta_{0}=1 / 5$ is selected for polymer/CNT nanocomposites. However, this model usually underpredicts the tensile strength of polymer/CNT nanocomposites. Figure 2 illustrates the experimental tensile strength and the calculations by Eq. 2 for two nanocomposites from literature. It is clearly observed that the calculations are below the experimental data at whole CNT contents.

This discrepancy is much commonly in modeling of nanocomposites properties by conventional micromechanics models (Zare and Garmabi, 2012a, 2014a). As discussed before, the main reason for this occurrence may be the formation of a strong interphase between polymer and CNT in the CNT reinforced polymer nanocomposites which affects the overall properties of nanocomposites.

Figure 3 shows the calculations of Eq. 17 and the experimental strength for poly (ether ether ketone) (PEEK)/multi wall CNT (MWCNT) (No. 4 in Table 1) and epoxy/MWCNT (No. 5 in Table 1) samples. A good agreement is observed between the experimental and theoretical results by assuming a suitable value for thickness of interphase (t). Table 1 presents the reported samples from literature and the characteristics of CNT and polymer matrix. In addition, the t and B values calculated from fitting the experimental tensile strength to Eq. 17 and Eq. 11 are given in Table 1. The values of t show the existence of different interphase in the polymer/CNT nanocomposites. It is found that t values are less than diameter of CNT in all reported samples, which indicates the formation of a thin interphase in CNT reinforced polymer nanocomposites.

Also, the interfacial shear strength values ($\tau$) calculated from Eq. 16 are observed in Table 1. Salvetat et al. (Salvetat et al., 1999) claimed that $\tau$ hardly reaches to 10 MPa in polymer/CNT nanocomposites, but, $\tau$ is computed in a range of 1.13 to 51.94 MPa for the present samples. This evidence verifies the significance of interphase in the nanocomposite samples.

Eq. 16 demonstrates that $\tau$ directly connects to $\sigma_m$ and B. Furthermore, an inverse relation between $\tau$ and $\alpha$ is expressed in this equation. Figure 4a illustrates the dependency of $\tau$ to B, at different $\alpha$ (100, 500 and 1000) and $\sigma_m$=40 MPa. As shown, $\tau$ increases by growing B and decreasing $\alpha$, which demonstrate the positive

effect of interfacial interactions (B) on $\tau$. It is interesting that the largest $\tau$ is obtained at $\alpha$=100, which express the influence of $\alpha$ on $\tau$ value. Moreover, Figure 4b shows the dependency of $\tau$ on B at different $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $\alpha$=500. A direct link between $\tau$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ is observed in this illustration. Additionally, it is concluded that the effect of $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ on $\tau$ is more important at higher B levels.

The calculated values of $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ for reported samples are given in Table 1. They are much larger than $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ values which confirm the high strength of interphase in CNT reinforced polymer nanocomposites. Additionally, Eq. 20 shows a direct relation between $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ and B as verified by the calculations in Table 1. It is clear that $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ increases, when B enhances. However, the effect of interphase thickness (t) on $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ is some ambiguous, because t and B are not independent parameters. In other words, larger B and t are obtained by high strength of nanocomposites at the same time, while Eq. 20 shows different correlations between these parameters. It is believed that the grow rate and level of B is higher than t. Therefore, a higher B can often improve the $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$, as shown in Table 1. The variation of t/R is in order of 0.5 to 1.4, which cannot significantly affect the results of $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$, but the large variation of B in the range of 5 to 24 significantly improves $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$. Additionally, the effect of $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ on $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ is negligible. For example, smallest $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ is calculated as 699.4 MPa for PEEK/MWCNT sample (No. 4 in Table 1), while this sample has the largest $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ of 99.5 MPa among the reported samples.

The calculated values of $L_c$ assuming $\eta_0$=1/5 and $\sigma_f$ = 37 GPa are represented in Table 1. The values of $L_c$ vary from 7.12 $\mu$m for polydicyclopentadiene (PDCPD)/MWCNT (No. 3 in Table 1) to 559.4 $\mu$m for polystyrene (PS)/MWCNT (No. 2 in Table 1). Clearly, $L_c$ values are larger than L in all samples which validate the use of $L < L_c$ assumption for prediction of tensile strength in polymer/CNT nanocomposites. Figure 5a illustrates the dependence of $L_c$ to B at different L ($\sigma_m$ = 40 MPa).

$L_c$ reduces by enhancing of B and a significant rate of decrement is observed at larger L. Additionally, it is realized that a large L significantly affects $L_c$ only at small B values. Figure 5b also displays the relation between $L_c$ and B at different $\sigma_m$ (L=5 $\mu$m). In addition to the inverse connection between $L_c$ and $\sigma_m$, the most effect of $\sigma_m$ on $L_c$ is occurred at small B values. These evidences indicate that a good interfacial adhesion (high B) is more advantageous factor compared to L and $\sigma_m$ to decrease $L_c$.

### 4. Conclusions
The model suggested for tensile strength of conventional reinforced polymer composites with short fibers ($L < L_c$) calculates smaller results compared to experimental data. Therefore, a micromechanics model which assumes the thickness of interphase (t) is suggested for polymer/CNT nanocomposites. The calculations by new model show a good agreement with the experimental data.

Furthermore, Pukanszky model is coupled with the suggested model to estimate $\tau$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ and $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$.

In the next step, the proposed approach is used to calculate $\tau$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ and $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$ in various samples from literature. The considerable levels of t, $\tau$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}$ demonstrate the significant effects of interphase on the properties of polymers reinforced by CNT. Also, the high levels of $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$ confirm that the CNT can be taken as short fibers in the polymer nanocomposites. The proposed equations show a direct correlation between $\tau$ and B as well as an opposite connection between $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$ and B. Also, it is shown that B is dominant factor which affects $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$ at higher B values. As a result, a large interfacial adhesion at interface (larger B) is adequate to obtain a low $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{c}}$ in polymer/CNT nanocomposites.
