### ABSTRACT
In this article, the percolation threshold for tensile strength of polymer/CNT nanocomposites (PCNT) is studied by known Pukanszky model. Since the network of nanoparticles forms at very low concentration of CNT, the filler network shows strengthening efficiency in PCNT beside the interphase regions surrounding nanoparticles. The Pukanszky model is developed by the properties of network such as density (N) and strength ($\sigma_N$). The suggested equations are applied in some samples and the results are explained. Also, the effects of several parameters such as "N", "$\sigma_N$" and percolation volume fraction ($\phi_p$) on the strength of PCNT are plotted based on the developed model. The direct influences of "N" and "$\sigma_N$" as well as the inverse effect of "$\phi_p$" on the tensile strength of PCNT are displayed. The "$\sigma_N$" parameter only changes the strength of PCNT at low "$\phi_p$", which shows the important role of percolation level.

## 1. Introduction
Among the nanoparticles, carbon nanotubes (CNT) are good candidate and excellent reinforcement for polymer nanocomposites. The CNT consist of thin and long graphite layers with diameters of 1–100 nm and lengths of 1–10 $\mu$m. Moreover, CNT are conducive filler with the high Young's modulus of about 1 TPa (1000 GPa) and the good tensile strength of 10–50 GPa [1–5]. As a result, CNT show remarkable physical properties such as high aspect ratios and large surface area, exceptional mechanical behavior and noble conductivity, which cause the CNT to be a promising reinforcement in advanced nanocomposites [6–8].

During the synthesis of nanocomposites, the van der Waals attraction between CNT forms the aggregates and agglomerates. So, it is vital to break the aggregates/agglomerates and afford the good dispersion of nanotubes in the polymer matrix to improve the mechanical properties. In fact, good dispersion of CNT in polymer matrix is necessary to achieve the desirable mechanical performance in nanocomposites [9,10]. Also, the high interfacial area and the strong interfacial interaction/adhesion between polymer matrix and nanoparticles forms a third phase as interphase which significantly affects the mechanical properties of polymer nanocomposites [11–13]. The interphase properties mainly depend on several parameters such as polymer characteristics, dispersion and surface treatment of CNT, interfacial interaction, etc. [11,12]. The molecular interaction at the interphase determines the efficiency of stress transfer from polymer to nanofiller. The properties of interphase such as thickness, strength and modulus have been well studied in polymer nanocomposites by different models in the literature [14–16]. The significant reinforcement of nanoparticles in the nanocomposites was shown by formation of a thick and strong interphase between polymer matrix and nanoparticles.

The percolation threshold is the minimum content of nanofiller in the nanocomposite, which forms the filler network and produces an acceptable conductivity. So, the polymer/CNT nanocomposites (PCNT) display the electrical conductivity when the volume fraction of CNT is greater than the percolation threshold [17,18]. Beside the conductivity, a similar percolation was reported for mechanical properties of polymer nanocomposites by adding the filler concentration [19–21]. Researchers studied the mechanical percolation experimentally and theoretically. The electrical and mechanical percolations are consistent in PCNT, while the abrupt increase of mechanical behavior can be hardly explained by the mechanism of electrical percolation threshold [22]. It should be noted that the tensile strength of PCNT weakens at high filler concentrations, due to the aggregation/agglomeration of nanoparticles and stress concentration [23], while the electrical conductivity and tensile modulus improve upon increasing of CNT content. This issue is the main difference between tensile modulus and strength of nanocomposites and thus the theoretical bases for these properties are quite different.

Many authors in the literature have applied the models to study the electrical conductivity of PCNT by percolation effect [24–26]. Also, the role of percolation threshold in the tensile modulus of polymer nanocomposites was also reported. The conventional models cannot predict the percolation threshold in the polymer nanocomposites, because they cannot consider the unusual properties of nanofillers such as high aspect ratio, big surface area and networking above a certain concentration. Ouali et al. [27] developed a conventional model for tensile modulus of conventional composites by the percolation threshold. Researchers estimated the tensile modulus of polymer nanocomposites above percolation threshold by Ouali model [28,29]. However, the researches in this area should continue to suggest the models, which can take into account the properties of nanoparticles and its network above percolation threshold.

The percolation threshold in which the filler network forms was inversely related to the aspect ratio of nanoparticles [22,30]. Thus, the high aspect ratio of CNT produces the nanoparticles network in PCNT at very low filler concentrations. In this paper, the known and valid Pukanszky model is applied to investigate the percolation threshold for tensile strength of PCNT. The significant strength of polymer nanocomposites was attributed to the formation of interphase between polymer and nanoparticles. However, the tensile strength of some PCNT is higher than the predictions of the Pukanszky model in the highest levels of interphase thickness and strength. As a result, the high level of strength of PCNT is due to the percolation effect and the formation of CNT network above percolation threshold. The properties of network such as density and strength are assumed in a parameter as "B_N" which is determined in some samples by Pukanszky model. Also, the effects of network properties on the "B_N" parameter and tensile strength of PCNT are plotted and explained. By the suggested equations and the experimental results of tensile strength, it is possible to approximate the properties of interphase and filler network, when their influences on the strength of PCNT are certain. In fact, we try to present a simple approach for modeling of tensile strength in nanocomposites by considering the interfacial status between nanofiller and polymer matrix as well as the CNT networks above percolation threshold.

## 2. Model analysis

The Pukanszky model [31] for tensile strength of polymer nanocomposites containing well-dispersed nanoparticles (good dispersion of nanoparticles in polymer matrix is considered in this study) is expressed as:

$$
\sigma_{R}=\frac{1-\phi_{f}}{1+2.5 \phi_{f}} \exp \left(B \phi_{f}\right) \tag{1}
$$

where "σ_R" is relative strength as the strength of nanocomposite per the matrix strength. Also, "$\phi_f$" is filler volume fraction and "B" considers the capacity of stress transfer between the phases as an interfacial parameter. The Pukanszky model has been successfully applied for different polymer nanocomposites in the previous studies [31,32]. The "B" parameter depends on the thickness and strength of interphase as:

$$
B=\left(1+A_{c} d_{f} t\right) \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{2}
$$

where "σ_m" is the tensile strength of polymer matrix and "A_c" and "d_f" show the specific surface area and density of nanofiller, respectively. Also, "t" and "σ_i" are the thickness and strength of interphase. Therefore, the "B" parameter can be applied to characterize the properties of interphase in polymer nanocomposites. Both "t" and "σ_i" depend on the interfacial interaction/adhesion between polymer matrix and nanoparticles [33,34]. The strong interfacial interaction such as chemical bond causes thick and strong interphase, which can accelerate the percolation of CNT and improve the mechanical properties of PCNT. However, the low levels of "t" and "σ_i" negatively affect the networking of CNT and the mechanical behavior of nanocomposites.

The Pukanszky model can be restructured to:

$$
\ln \left(\sigma_{R e d u c e d}\right)=\ln \left(\sigma_{R} \frac{1+2.5 \phi_{f}}{1-\phi_{f}}\right)=B \phi_{f} \tag{3}
$$

where the plot of ln (σ_Reduced) against "$\phi_f$" results in a linear correlation whose slope determines the "B" parameter.

The "A_c" parameter can be expressed for nanocomposites containing well-dispersed CNT as:

$$
A_{c}=\frac{A}{m}=\frac{2 \pi R l}{d_{f} \pi R^{2} l}=\frac{2}{d_{f} R} \tag{4}
$$

where "A" and "m" are the surface area and mass of CNT, respectively. Also, "R" and "l" are the radius and length of nanoparticles. By replacing of "A_c" from above equation into Eq. (2), the "B" parameter can be expressed as:

$$
B=\left(1+\frac{2 t}{R}\right) \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{5}
$$

The "B" parameter was calculated and evaluated for all types of polymer nanocomposites to estimate the interfacial interaction. According to Eq. (5), the highest level of "B" is obtained by the thickest and the strongest interphase. However, the "t" level cannot be larger than the gyration radius of polymer macromolecules as about 40 nm [16]. As a result, the thickest interphase has a size of about 40 nm in polymer nanocomposites. Similarly, "$\sigma_{i}$" differs between the strengths of polymer matrix and nanofiller demonstrating that the best interphase strength may be near the filler strength. As mentioned, CNT show the tensile strength of 10-50 GPa with an average of 30 GPa. Accordingly, the maximum level of "$\sigma_{i}$" can be obtained as about 25 GPa in PCNT. According to these expressions, the "$B_{\text {max }}$" is given based on Eq. (5) by:

$$
B_{\max }=\left(1+\frac{80}{R}\right) \ln \left(\frac{25000}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{6}
$$

In this equation, "R" and "$\sigma_{m}$" have nm and MPa units, respectively. For example, the "$B_{\text {max }}$" at average $\sigma_{\mathrm{m}}=40 \mathrm{MPa}$ and $\mathrm{R}=15 \mathrm{~nm}$ is obtained as about 41 . The highest level of "B" obtained by fitting the experimental strength to the Pukanszky model should be lesser than "$B_{\text {max }}$". Many previous reports in the literature have reported the "B" level below the "$B_{\text {max }}$" [31,35]. However, the "B" range is higher than "$B_{\text {max }}$" in some PCNT. Here, some samples are shown from literature to clarify this evidence.

The properties of filler network in PCNT can be represented in "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" parameter by a similar expression for "B" in Pukanszky model as:

$$
B_{N}=\frac{N l}{10^{5} d} \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{N}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{7}
$$

where "$\sigma_{\mathrm{N}}$" is the network strength, "N" is the number of nanotubes in a unit area and "d" is the diameter of nanotubes. Assuming the aspect ratio of nanofiller $(\alpha=1 / d)$, the "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" parameter is expressed by:

$$
B_{N}=\frac{\alpha N}{10^{5}} \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{N}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{8}
$$

This definition for "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" shows the fraction of "B" obtained which determines the network properties, i.e. the "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" is calculated by:

$$
B_{N}=B-B_{I} \tag{9}
$$

where "$B_{\mathrm{I}}$" shows the value of "B" corresponding to the interphase characteristics. Assuming the new definition for "B" parameter in Eq. (9), the Pukanszky model is developed by the network characteristics to:

$$
\sigma_{R}=\frac{1-\phi_{f}}{1+2.5 \phi_{f}} \exp \left[\left(B_{I}+B_{N}\right) \phi_{f}\right] \tag{10}
$$

The "$B_{\mathrm{I}}$" is considered as "$B_{\text {max }}$" in the above samples, because it is not possible to determine the levels of interphase thickness and strength in the reported samples. The "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" is calculated as 17.3 and 57.9 for reported chitosan/MWCNT and polysilsesquioxane/ MWCNT samples. These levels can be applied to approximate the values of " $\alpha$ ", "N" and "$\sigma_{\mathrm{N}}$" in these samples. However, one may consider the equal portions of "$B_{\mathrm{I}}$" and "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" in "B" to simplify the calculations, which results in $B_{I}=B_{N}=B / 2$. This assumption is used in two examples of PCNT in the last paragraph of this paper and the average properties of interphase and filler network are guessed and evaluated. Generally, if the values of "$B_{\mathrm{I}}$" or "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" were determinate, it is possible to calculate the properties of interphase or network in PCNT.

In addition, the percolation thresholds for electrical conductivity and stiffness of PCNT were inversely related to " $\alpha$ " in many previous studies [22,30]. Chen et al. [22] reported the percolation threshold for stiffness of 3D CNT networks as:

$$
\phi_{p}=\frac{2.2}{\alpha}=\frac{2.2 d}{l} \tag{11}
$$

representing that CNT dimensions play an important role in the percolation threshold for mechanical behavior of nanocomposites. Actually, large and thin CNT mainly decrease the percolation threshold, which positively affects the mechanical and electrical properties of PCNT. This equation was originally suggested for stiffness percolation, but it is logical to use it for tensile strength, because both stiffness and strength consistently show the resistance of nanocomposite against deformation in response to an applied force. In fact, both tensile modulus and strength of nanocomposites grow by the formation of network, although very high filler concentration deteriorates the tensile strength due to defective dispersion of nanoparticles and stress concentration.

Using this equation into Eq. (8), the "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" parameter can be stated by percolation threshold as:

$$
B_{N}=\frac{2.2 N}{10^{5} \phi_{p}} \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{N}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \tag{12}
$$

By this expression, it is possible to show the effect of " $\phi_{p}$ " on the strength of PCNT by replacing of "$B_{\mathrm{N}}$" from above equation into Eq. (10) as:

$$
\sigma_{R}=\frac{1-\phi_{f}}{1+2.5 \phi_{f}} \exp \left[B_{I} \phi_{f}+\frac{2.2 N}{10^{5} \phi_{p}} \ln \left(\frac{\sigma_{N}}{\sigma_{m}}\right) \phi_{f}\right] \tag{13}
$$

Moreover, it is possible to calculate one parameter if the values of others were assured by morphological images, etc.

A high level of "N" reveals the large number of CNT in a unit area, which produce a dense network and a good strengthening effect in the nanocomposite. However, a low strength is observed in the samples, which contain a low-density network. Accordingly, the suggested equations show an acceptable dependency to "$\alpha$" and "N" parameters.

The "$\phi_p$" determines the minimum concentration of nanofiller necessary to form the network in the PCNT. Undoubtedly, a high level of "$\phi_p$" postpones the formation of filler network to the high concentrations of nanofiller, but a slight "$\phi_p$" forms the network of nanoparticles at low filler concentrations. Since the formation of filler network above percolation threshold expressively improves the tensile strength of PCNT, the observed relation between "$\phi_p$" parameter and nanocomposite strength is right.

The best "$\text{B}_\text{N}$" and strength of PCNT are observed at the smallest "$\phi_p$" and the highest "$\sigma_\text{N}$". ## 4. Conclusions
The significant strength of PCNT was attributed to the formation of interphase between polymer and nanoparticles as well as the percolation effect and formation of CNT network above percolation threshold. The Pukanszky model was developed to investigate the roles of interphase and percolation threshold in the tensile strength of PCNT. The developed "B" parameter is a representative of the levels of interface/interphase (Bᵢ) and network properties (Bₙ) as B = Bᵢ + Bₙ. The high values of "B" undoubtedly increase the tensile strength of PCNT. Also, the direct effects of "$\alpha$" and "N" parameters on the "Bₙ" parameter and the strength of PCNT are shown and explained. Moreover, a small "Bₙ" below 15 as well as poor strength ($\sigma_{\text{R}} < 1.5$) is calculated at $\phi_{p} > 0.003$ which reveals the important effects of "$\phi_{p}$" parameter on "Bₙ" and strength of PCNT. The results show that the effect of network strength is highlighted when "$\phi_{p}$" shows very low levels. When the effects of interphase and filler network on the strength of PCNT are certain, the properties of interphase and network can be approximated by the suggested equations and the experimental results of tensile strength. The dimension and strength of both interphase and filler network were approximated in two examples from literature assuming Bᵢ = Bₙ = B/2.
