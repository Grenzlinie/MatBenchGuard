# Ab-initio Study of Structural, Mechanical and Electronic Properties of Functionalized Carbon Nanotubes

Karolina Z. Milowska, Magdalena Birowska, and Jacek A. Majewski

Faculty of Physics, University of Warsaw, ul. Hoza 69, 00-681 Warszawa, Poland

**Abstract.** We present exemplary results of extensive studies of structural, mechanical and electronic properties of covalent functionalization of carbon nanotubes (CNTs). We report new results for metallic (9,0), and semiconducting (10,0) single-wall carbon nanotubes (CNT) functionalized with –COOH, –OH, and both groups with concentration up to 12.5%. Our studies are performed in the framework of the density functional theory (DFT). We discuss here the stability, local and global changes in structure, elastic moduli (Young's, Shear, and Bulk), electronic structure and resulting band gaps, as a function of the density of the adsorbed molecules.

**Keywords:** carbon nanotubes, covalent functionalization, elastic moduli, electronic structure.
**PACS:** 31.15.A-, 31.15.ae, 31.15.E-, 61.46.Fg, 73.20.At, 81.05.Zx, 81.07.De, 87.85.fk

## INTRODUCTION

Covalent functionalization of carbon nanotubes (CNTs) plays extremely important role in the potential applications of these systems in new materials [1,2] and functional devices, especially in nano-bioelectronics [3]. The functionalization is a prerequisite for effective dispersion and binding of CNTs in polymer or epoxy matrices. This type of functionalization is used also as experimental method [4] to separate semiconducting from metallic and semi-metallic tubes with high selectivity and scalability. In our studies, based on the first principles calculations in the framework of density functional theory (DFT), we have focused on the problem how the properties of CNTs change after the functionalization. We have employed the generalized gradient approximation for exchange and correlation, whereas the electron-ion interaction has been accounted for by using the norm conserving pseudopotentials, as implemented in the numerical package SIESTA [5,6].

We have performed *ab initio* studies of covalent functionalization of metallic (9,0), as well as semiconducting (10,0) and (11,0) single-wall carbon nanotubes (CNT) with simple organic molecules - $\text{NH}_n$, $\text{-CH}_n$, -OH, and -COOH at various concentrations (up to 12.5% ). In this communication, we focus on (9,0) and (10,0) CNT functionalized with -OH, -COOH and both groups simultaneously.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

We discuss here the stability and changes in the electronic structure of functionalized CNTs.

### Stability

The functionalization causes local (Fig 1(a)) and global (see Fig. 1(d)) structural changes to the CNTs. We observe elongation of C-C bond length and characteristic $\text{sp}^2$->$\text{sp}^3$ rehybridization [7,8,9] around the dopants. Functionalization with those groups slightly changes the cross-section of CNT - from circle to ellipse (see Fig. 1(a)).

The adsorption energy per molecule ($\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$=1/N ($\text{E}_{\text{tot}}$ (CNT+Ngroups) - $\text{E}_{\text{tot}}$(CNT)- N$\cdot\text{E}_{\text{tot}}$(group))) is used here as a measure of the stability of functionalized CNTs. It exhibits slightly decreasing trend with increasing number of attachments. The most stable structures, with the most negative $\text{E}_{\text{ads}}$, are CNT functionalized with –OH (Fig. 1(c)). For more than 16.7% concentration of –OH groups, we observe forming of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules and binding of O atoms to two $\pi$ bonds each.

Radius of CNTs, also varies with the strength of binding. The functionalized CNTs exhibit larger radius than the pure ones (Fig. 1(d)) and it increases with number of attached molecules.

## Elastic Properties

The pure CNTs exhibit extremely high Young's modulus of the order of 1 TPa. For design of composites, it is of great importance to know how the functionalization changes the elastic moduli. Changes in structure caused by functionalization, are also very well noticeable in Young's modulus (Fig. 2(a)). Increasing number of covalently bound groups to the surface of CNT decreases their elastic moduli (Fig. 2).

## Electronic Properties

Functionalization of CNTs changes their electronic properties. All of the considered functionalizing molecules introduce impurity bands (see Fig. 3) around Fermi level which originate from (i) stress induced by attaching groups to lateral surface, (ii) interaction between atoms from the adsorbents and tube [7], and (iii) $sp^2$$\rightarrow$$sp^3$ rehybridization [7,8]. Semiconducting (10,0) CNT can be changed into metallic one by functionalization with -OH, -COOH, and both groups simultaneously. Whereas there are none relevant changes in the electronic structure of functionalized semi-metallic (9,0) CNT.

## CONCLUSSIONS

In summary, the have performed *ab initio* studies of the structural, mechanical and electronic properties of the functionalized CNTs which clearly demonstrate that covalent functionalization changes local and global morphology, reduces the elastic moduli, and modifies the electronic structure.
