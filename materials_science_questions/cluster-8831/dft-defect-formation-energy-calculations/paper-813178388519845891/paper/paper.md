## ABSTRACT

Usually, experimental bulk modulus is close to the average value of bulk modulus obtained by GGA and LDA. However, it does not happen in OsN₂. For OsN₂, experimental bulk modulus is even lower than that with GGA. Such case suggests that N vacancy or O substitution may exist in synthesized OsN₂. We clarify this issue by calculating various properties of OsN₂ with N vacancy and O substitution. Calculated formation energy shows that O substitution can stabilize marcasite OsN₂. Moreover, the calculated bulk modulus of OsN₂ with O substitution is close to experimental results. The N vacancy or O substitution can decrease covalent interaction of OsN₂ and consequently its bulk modulus becomes lower. For OsN₂, O substitution only decrease the shear modulus by 9%. The calculated density of states indicates that OsN₂ with N vacancy or O substitution keep its metallic behavior.

## 1. Introduction

Ultra-incompressible and superhard materials are of considerable fundamental interest and practical importance because of their excellent mechanical properties, such as high elastic modulus and hardness, scratch resistance, and surface durability. They are widely used in many applications, such as abrasives, cutting tools, or scratch resistant coating. Hence, transition metal nitrides have received great attention for being the powerful candidates of this kind of materials [1–5]. Recently, several dinitrides of Os, Ir, and Pt have been synthesized at extreme conditions and possess ultrahigh bulk moduli, which are comparable to those superhard materials exhibiting interesting mechanical properties [6,7]. High-pressure X-ray diffraction experiments on OsN₂, synthesized under conditions above 50 GPa and 2000 K, give bulk modulus of 358 GPa [$K_0$) = 358(6) GPa with $K_{0}' = 4.67$] [6]. X-ray diffraction measurements were able to clarify for all compounds the nature of the sublattice of the transition metal atoms, but due to the large mass difference between nitrogen and the parent metals, nitrogen positions could not be conclusively determined by Rietveld refinement for orthorhombic OsN₂. Therefore, great efforts have been devoted to investigate the synthesized orthorhombic OsN₂ with high bulk modulus by the first-principles method. The calculated results showed that the crystal structure of OsN₂ is isostructural to that of marcasite, a known polymorph of FeS₂ [8–13]. A recent first-principles study proposed that the marcasite phase of OsN₂ might be a superconductor with $T_c \approx 1$ K, which originates from the stretching of covalently bonded $N_2$ units embedded in the transition metal matrix [11].

Theoretically, the calculated bulk moduli of orthorhombic OsN₂ in the marcasite phase are 359 GPa [8] and 368 GPa [9] within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), and 413 GPa [8] and 420 GPa [9] within the local density approximation (LDA). As is well known, LDA usually underestimates the lattice constants and overestimates elastic constants, while GGA overestimates the lattice constants and underestimates elastic constants. Previous study have shown that the GGA-PBE underestimates energetic properties of nitrides systems [14]. Besides, it is well known that in the synthesis of transition-metal nitrides under high pressure and at high temperature condition, nitrogen vacancy or oxygen substitution is easily generated. For example, previous theoretical works have proved that nitrogen vacancy or oxygen substitution might stabilized the experimentally synthesized $Ta_2N_3$, and pure $Ta_2N_3$ is not stable under zero pressure [15,16].

Obviously, the theoretically calculated bulk modulus of marcasite OsN₂ with GGA is larger than the experiment value, which suggests that N vacancy or O substitution may exists in synthesized OsN₂. However, there are no detailed investigations on the effect of N-vacancy or O-substitution of OsN₂, which may explain about the discrepancy in bulk modulus value between experiment and theory. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate the structure, elastic modulus, and the physical properties of OsN₂ with N-vacancy and O-substitution theoretically. Here, we investigate the structural, mechanical stability, and physical properties of the marcasite OsN₂ with N-vacancy and O-substitution, and compare them with the conventional super-structure using the first-principles calculations method based on the density functional theory.

### 2. Computational detail

The calculations presented in this study were performed within the density functional theory, using the projector-augmented wave (PAW) method [17,18] as implemented in vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [19–21]. Both the GGA [22] and LDA [23] with the PAW potential were used for comparison. The structure was optimized with the conjugate gradient algorithm method and a plane wave cutoff energy of 500 eV was employed. Structural optimization was performed until the energy change of each atom was less than 0.005 eV, the forces on atoms were less than 0.02 eV/Å. The $k$-point samplings $(9 \times 7 \times 13)$ in the Brillouin zone were performed using the Monkhorst–Pack scheme [24] for OsN₂. The strain-stress method was used to obtain elastic constants. From the calculated elastic constants $c_{ij}$, the polycrystalline bulk modulus $B$ and shear modulus $G$ were further estimated using the Voigt–Reuss–Hill (VRH) approximation [25].

### 3. Results and discussions

Osmium dinitride belongs to a family of nitrides synthesized at high pressure and high temperature from their parent elements. The previous calculated results show that the crystal structure of osmium nitride is isostructural to marcasite [8,9]. In the marcasite structure, two osmium atoms occupy the Wyckoff sites 2a [(0,0,0) and (1/2,1/2,1/2)], while four nitrogen atoms are located at 4g(0.127,0.404,0) site. The optimized nitrogen internal parameters at 0 GPa are $x = 0.12751$ and $y = 0.40386$ [13]. As is expected, for marcasite OsN₂, the optimized lattice constants generated by LDA ($a = 4.100$ Å, $b = 4.889$ Å, and $c = 2.653$ Å) and GGA ($a = 4.146$ Å, $b = 4.956$ Å, and $c = 2.699$ Å) are in good agreement with the experimental values [6] and previous theoretical results [13], suggesting the reliability of our calculation method. Besides, the calculated large bulk modulus and shear modulus of this compound also agree well with the previous theoretical data [9,8]. The following super-structure models are all based on the optimized geometry structure of this orthorhombic OsN₂ unit cell.

#### 3.1. Crystal structure and formation energy

In the experimental work, the materials synthesized at high temperature will form vacancy or draw in impurity when they are cool off to low temperature. Zerr et al. have pointed out that the experimentally synthesized Ta₂N₃ under high pressure and high temperature is slightly contaminated by oxygen (about 3%) [15]. In this work, the studied OsN₂ displayed high bulk moduli also has been synthesized under high pressure and high temperature, so it is necessary to investigate the effect of nitrogen vacancy and oxygen substitution. Three different super-structure models $(2 \times 2 \times 2$ supercell) of OsN₂ were compared: the conventional supercell structure of marcasite OsN₂ (labeled as c-OsN₂); one with nitrogen vacancy (labeled as N-vacancy); one with substitution of oxygen into nitrogen site (labeled as O-substitution). To study the influence of N-vacancy on marcasite OsN₂, calculations were carried out on the orthorhombic $(2 \times 2 \times 2)$ supercell, in which one nitrogen atom is removed, corresponding to about 3.1% vacancy concentration. At the same time, to simulate the effect of oxygen substitution, we constructed a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ supercell containing 48 atoms based on the orthorhombic OsN₂ unit cell, in which one of the nitrogen atoms were substituted by oxygen, corresponding to about 3.1% oxygen concentration. The optimized crystal structures of these three different super-structures of OsN₂ are displayed in Fig. 1. As seen from Fig. 1a, the calculated fractional coordinates show that the osmium atom is octahedrally coordinated by six nitrogen atoms, and each nitrogen atom is tetrahedrally coordinated by one nitrogen and three osmium atoms. These octahedra share edges in chains along the z axis and are interconnected with each other by nitrogen dimers in the xy plane, forming a three dimensional network in the c-OsN₂. The single-bonded N₂ units occupy the interstitial sites of the Os close-packed lattice, giving rise to a metallic compound.

All nitrogen atoms in marcasite OsN₂ are equivalent because the structure has the same total energy when any nitrogen is removed or when the oxygen atom occupies any nitrogen site in c-OsN₂. Besides, the optimized N-vacancy and O-substitution super-structures are both monoclinic.

Table 1 lists the calculated cell parameters, formation energy, and inter-atomic distances for the c-OsN₂, N-vacancy, and O-substitution structures. Besides, the corresponding experimental values and previous theoretical results are shown in the same table for comparison. We found that all the three structures of OsN₂ contain covalent N–N dimers and the distances are about 1.4 Å, which are close to the experimental values of OsN₂ [6] listed in Table 1. The calculated value of N–N bonding is also almost exactly the same as for single-bonded monatomic nitrogen (triply coordinated) that has been predicted by previous theoretical study [26] and also reported in a recent experimental study [27]. The appearance of N–N dimers in these compounds may contribute to their high incompressibility. Previous studies have shown that the N–N bond length is a determinative factor for the ultrahigh bulk moduli of the heavy-transition-metal dinitrides [28]. So decreasing the nitrogen content in OsN₂ can weaken the degree of directionality of the N–N covalent bonding, and then decrease the bulk modulus. Our calculated results show that the N–N covalent bonds in N-vacancy and O-substitution have been decreased because of the decreased nitrogen content. For O-substitution, the oxygen atoms into nitrogen site form covalent bonds with three Os atoms (as seen in Fig. 1c), with the Os–O bond distance 2.02–2.03 Å. The short Os–O covalent bonds could contribute to its elastic properties and strength.

The formation energy, $E_{form}$, shows the thermodynamic stability of the crystal structure. The lower of formation energy, the more stable crystal structure. The formation energy of the studied stoichiometric and un-stoichiometric OsN₂ is defined as,

$$
E_{form}=\frac{E_{total}-m \times E_{Os}-\frac{n}{2}E_{N_2}-\frac{l}{2}E_{O_2}}{m + n + l} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{total}$ is the total energy of the studied super-structure models, $m$, $n$, and $l$ are numbers of the metal and nonmetal (N and O) in the crystal, respectively. We obtained, $E_{Os}$, $E_{N_2}$, and $E_{O_2}$ from the total energies per atom of bulk Os ($P6_3/mmc$), and per unit cell of N₂ and O₂. The $P6_3/mmc$ phase of Os [29] and $\alpha$-phase N₂ [30] were chosen as the reference phases. As seen from Table 1, the O-substitution has a lower formation energy then other types structure, indicating that it is most stable. Besides, c-OsN₂ is more stable than the N-vacancy structure.

From Table 2, we can see that the values of $C_{11}$, $C_{22}$, and $C_{33}$ of these compounds are very large, indicating that they are extremely difficult to be compressed along the $a$-axis, $b$-axis, and $c$-axis, respectively. As is well known, the elastic constant $c_{44}$ is an important parameter indirectly governing the indentation hardness. As shown in Table 2, the value of $c_{44}$ for OsN₂ with the marcasite structure is small compared with the other elastic constants. This is consistent well with previous study [10] that OsN₂ are the potential ultra-incompressible not superhard material. The large $c_{22}$ may induced by the strong covalent Os–N and N–N bonds along b axis. Since there exists only one-dimensional bonds, the elastic constant components along other directions are relative small, leading to an overall small bulk moduli of OsN₂. Generally speaking, superhard materials possess high bulk modulus to support the volume decrease caused by an applied load and high shear modulus to restrict deformation in a direction different from the applied load. From Table 2, we can see that c-OsN₂ among the three models has the largest shear modulus and N vacancy has the smallest value, indicating that c-OsN₂ can withstand the shear strain to a large extent.

For marcasite OsN₂, the high elastic modulus can be attributed to a stacking of edge-shared OsN₆ octahedra connected by strong N–N covalent bonds. Apparently, when decreased the concentration of nitrogen, elastic modulus would decrease. OsN₂ with nitrogen vacancy and oxygen substitution have the same bulk moduli by GGA method (340 GPa), smaller than that of the conventional super-structure of OsN₂ and the experiment value, which agrees with the calculated results that GGA method underestimated the experiment value and LDA method overestimated the value. The lower bulk modulus of these two structure than c-OsN₂ are possibly due to their weaker bonding interaction between the N and Os atoms. For N-vacancy structure, it exhibits same bulk moduli with that of the oxygen substitution. But its shear modulus is relatively small due to the decreases of N–N covalent bonds. The comparable shear modulus of O-substitution structure to that of c-OsN₂, which is only 23 GPa lower by GGA method, originates from the

appearance of Os–O covalent bonds in O-substitution, which increase the shear modulus.

The value of Poisson's ratio ($v$) is indicative of the degree of directionality of the covalent bonding and the quantity is calculated using the following relationship:

$$
v=\frac{3 B-2 G}{2(3 B+G)}. \tag{2}
$$

The relative directionality of the bonding in the material also has an important effect on its hardness and can be determined by the value of $G/B$ [32]. The value of poisson's ratio and $G/B$ of these three different phases were calculated and are listed in Table 2. It is clearly seen that c-OsN₂ and the O-substitution exhibit larger $G/B$ and smaller $v$ values than that of N-vacancy. Their similar value of $G/B$ and $v$ indicates the similar physical properties of these two structures. A high (low) $B/G$ value [33] is associated with ductility (brittleness), and the critical value is about 1.75. Our calculated results show that all the $B/G$ value of these three structures are smaller than 1.75, indicating that they are all brittle.

It is interesting to investigate the concentration effect of N vacancy (or O substitution) on stability and elastic properties of OsN₂. By employing a supercell model $(2 \times 1 \times 2)$, we calculated the structure and elastic properties of OsN₂ with N vacancy and O substitution (6.2% density). The calculated formation energy (0.592 eV) increases with the increasing of nitrogen vacancy, and the formation energy (0.441 eV) decreases with the increasing of oxygen content. Thus, O substitution is helpful to enhance the stability of OsN₂, and the nitrogen vacancy will decrease its stability. The calculated elastic moduli of OsN₂ with 6.2% density of N vacancy and O substitution are listed in Table 2. As shown in this table, the increase of nitrogen vacancy slightly decrease the bulk modulus of OsN₂, and largely decrease its shear modulus. The increasing of oxygen content affects weakly the bulk and shear moduli of OsN₂.

### 3.3. Electronic structure and chemical bonding

It is interesting to explore the effect of the N vacancy and O substitution on electronic properties of OsN₂. Both the total and partial density of states (labeled as TDOS and PDOS, respectively) are calculated for the studied OsN₂ super-structure models at zero pressure, as shown in Fig. 2. From Fig. 2a, it can be seen that the calculated DOS of these three phases in OsN₂ reveal metallic characters because of the finite $N(E_{f})$ at the Fermi level. This is in agreement with the experimental observation that the lack Raman activity for OsN₂ is on account of its metallicity [6]. Moreover, there is a deep valley called pseudogap around the Fermi level, which leads to a separation between the bonding and antibonding states. The presence of pseudogap will surely increase the stability of compound. From Fig. 2, we can see that both the TDOS and PDOS for the three compounds of OsN₂ are very similar in the energy range of $- 10$ eV to 5 eV, the small difference near the Fermi level results from the decreased N content. This indicates that the N vacancy and O substitution have small influence on the DOS of OsN₂.

The effect of N vacancy and O substitution on Os-N chemical bonding can be analyzed from the change of Os d orbital. Fig. 3 shows calculated Os d orbital in pure OsN₂, N vacancy, and O substitution. As seen in Fig. 3, the partially filled $d_{yz}$ results in the metallic feature of OsN₂. Moreover, the unoccupied $d_{z^{2}}$ and $d_{xz}$ orbital are repelled by N p orbital and shift to higher energy. $d_{xy}$ orbital of Os atom are localized below the Fermi energy. The $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ states are distributed in both valence and conduction bands region. Compared with pure OsN₂, the $d_{xy}$, $d_{z^{2}}$, and $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ in N vacancy and O substitution are little changed. However, in OsN₂ with N vacancy and O substitution, $d_{xz}$ orbital shifts to lower energy and becomes broaden, indicating strong covalent interaction between Os d and N p. $d_{yz}$ orbital shifts to the higher unoccupied energy range.

ELF = 0.5 for a homogeneous electron gas, with values of this order indicating regions with bonding of a metallic character. It should be noted the ELF is useful in distin- guishing metallic, covalent, and ionic bonding. Fig. 4 shows calcu- lated ELF of OsN₂. As shown in Fig. 4a, a high electron localization appears between adjacent nitrogen atoms, indicating strong cova- lent N-N bonding. Meanwhile, the ELF is negligible at the Os sites, while ELF between Os and N atoms attains local maximum values but very close to the N sites, suggesting partially covalent and ionic interaction between adjacent Os and N atoms. In addition, ELF near the center of Os-N bond is close to 0.5, which represents a partially metallic bonding feature of Os-N bond. For OsN₂ with N vacancy (shown in Fig. 4b), ELF near the N vacancy is close to 0.5, which indicates that a increasing of metallic feature near N vacancy. The electron gas near the N vacancy mainly come from releasing of repelled Os d electron cloud by the corresponding N atom in pure OsN₂. In addition, removing one nitrogen atom leads to an increasing of electron density in its adjacent nitrogen atom. As a nitrogen atom is substituted by one oxygen atom (shown in Fig. 4c), the oxygen atom obtains more electron than nitrogen atom, and no apparent strong covalent bonding is formed between adjacent oxygen and nitrogen atoms. Moreover, the calculated ELF between Os and O atoms indicates that the Os-O bonding also have partially covalent and ionic feature.

## 4. Conclusion
In summary, osmium dinitrides with N vacancy and O substitu- tion are studied by the first-principles calculations. The calculated formation energy shows that O substitution can stabilize marcasite OsN₂. The positive eigenvalues of the elastic constant matrix of these studied structures confirm that they are all elastically stable. Osmium dinitrides with nitrogen vacancy and oxygen substitution structures have smaller bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, and elastic constants than the conventional structure of OsN₂, which is reduced by the decreasing of N-N covalent bonds. Besides, OsN₂ with oxygen substitution exhibits larger shear modulus than that of nitrogen vacancy because of the appearance of Os-O cova- lent bonds. The conventional structure of OsN₂ exhibits larger $G/B$ and smaller $v$ values than that of N-vacancy and O-substitution. The calculated $B/G$ of these three different phases for OsN₂ are all smaller than 1.75, indicating that they are brittle materials. The analysis of DOS indicates that the N vacancy and O substitution have small influence on the DOS of OsN₂. We hope our result can further stimulate theory calculations and experimental research on OsN₂.
