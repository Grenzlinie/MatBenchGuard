## Abstract

Using the first-principles calculations based on the spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT), we investigated the structural, electronic and magnetic properties of Cu-doped single walled ZnO nanotubes (SWZnONTs). Our results show that for a unit cell with 40 Zn and 40 O atoms, substitution of a single Zn atom by a Cu leads from a semiconductor to a ferromagnetic (FM) half-metallic phase transition with 100% spin polarization. In this case the total magnetic moment of super cell is $1.0\ \mu_B$. To investigate the effects of Cu-codpoed SWZnONTs two different configurations are considered, first we assumed the two Zn atoms replaced by two Cu atoms are close and second they are far from each other. When Cu atoms are at the nearest-neighboring positions, the antiferromagnetism (AFM) phase is stable, while increasing the distance between the two Cu atoms, the ferromagnetism stability increases. In the AFM phase the structures are nonmagnetic semiconductors, but in the FM phase all these systems are half-metallic systems with 100% spin polarization, so it can be used as magnetic nanostructure and future applications in permanent magnetism, magnetic recording, and spintronics.

**Keywords:** DFT, ZnO Nanotube, Electronic properties, Spin polarization.

### Introduction

Diluted Magnetic Semiconductor (DMS) in which cations are partially replaced by transition metals could incorporate the ferromagnetism into the semiconductor. Zinc oxide (ZnO) has wide applications in the optoelectronic devices such as gas sensors, transparent electrodes, solar cells, ferroelectric memories and acoustic wave devices [1-7] due to their wide band gap (Eg ~3.3 eV at 300 K) [8]. Also it could be base of a typical kind of DMSs; Dietl *et al.* [9] have predicted that *p*-type ZnO-based DMS could exhibit room temperature ferromagnetism by doping transition elements such as Mn. The wide band gap semiconductors such as ZnO have attracted considerable attention in searching for high $T_C$ ferromagnetic DMS materials. Studies on electronic structure and magnetism of wurtzite ZnO-doped by transition metals (TM) have also been widely performed [10–14]. Since Cu and its oxides (CuO and Cu₂O) are nonmagnetic, Cu dopant is the best candidate. Recently, room temperature ferromagnetism of Cu-doped ZnO has been reported in some experiments [15–18], and the first-principles calculations have reported that Cu-doped ZnO is FM [19–23]. Comparing with bulk ZnO; ZnO nanostructures usually exhibit some unique properties due to their special geometry and the quantum confinement effect. Consequently, it is useful to study Cu-doped ZnO nanostructures, which may bring new insight into the mechanism of FM coupling in DMSs. Cu-doped ZnO both in bulk and nanocluster are investigated [24]. It is found that the magnetic phase has a transition from FM phase to AFM phase as the size for the doping system decreases from bulk to nanocluster. Multi-walled 1D ZnO nanotubes (ZnONTs) have already been synthesized [25-27]. It was found that all SWZnONT are direct band gap semiconductors with approximately constant energy gap 1.66 eV at the center of first Brillouin zone $\Gamma$-point [28-30]. The Cu doped ZnO nanowire and nanotubes have been synthesized by electro-deposition for catalyzing higher alcohols from syngas [31-34]. Recently, Cu doping concentration effects on SWZnONT band gap have been investigated by first principles study [35]. They found that by adjusting the doping Cu concentration and the concentration gradient, the band gap can vary from 4.5 eV of the perfect ZnO SWNT to 1.95 eV. When the Cu concentration is fixed, the band gap decreases as concentration gradient decreases. Similarly, with the fixed concentration gradient, the band gap decreases as the concentration decreases. Thus, the band gap can be modulated through constructing appropriate impurity concentration and concentration gradient. Up to now, magnetic properties of Cu co-doped ZnO nanotubes (ZnONTs) have not been investigated. In this work, we performed first-principles density functional calculations to investigate Cu single-doped and Cu co-doped SWZnONTs with different doping positions. Our main purpose defers from the above mentioned ref [35], we

investigate the position dependence of co-doped Cu atoms in ZnONT. Comparing to ref [24] in ZnO nanoclusters the stable phase for Cu co-doped is AFM when two Cu atoms are nearest neighbors while in ZnO bulk the FM is the stable phase. It is clear that AFM coupling vanishes when moving from nanoscale to bulk. We also investigate AFM coupling effects in Cu co-doped SWZnONTs on structural, electronic and magnetic properties. But when the two Cu atoms are far from each other FM is the stable phase in both ZnO nanoscale and bulk. The stable geometries, electronic, and magnetic properties of the Cu-doped tubular systems were analyzed. The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 is introduction. The computational details are given in Section 2. In Section 3, we present our calculated structural, electronic and magnetic properties of doped SWZnONTs. The last section is conclusions.

## Computational details

The total energy and electronic band structure calculations are performed via first principles Full potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW) method in the framework of the density functional theory (DFT) [36], as implemented in the WIEN2K code [37]. For the exchange correlation energy functional, we used the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the form of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [38]. The radii of the muffin-tin spheres were set to $R_{Zn} = 1.80$ a.u., $R_O =1.58$ a.u., $R_{Cu} =1.78$ a.u. The maximum angular momentum of the atomic orbital basis functions was set to $l_{max} = 10$. In order to achieve energy eigenvalues convergence, the wave functional in the interstitial region were expanded in terms of plane waves with a cut-off parameter of $RMT \times K_{max} = 7.5$, where RMT denotes the smallest atomic sphere radius and $K_{max}$ largest k vector in the plane wave expansion. The Fourier expansion charge density is truncated at $G_{max} = 14Ry^{1/2}$. The Brillouin zone integration is performed within the Gamma centered Monkhorst-Pack scheme [39] using $1 \times 1 \times 14$ k-points.

## Results and discussions

In this paper we investigate the effects of Cu-codoped SWZnONT. For this purpose we should compare effects of Cu-codoped on physical properties of a SWZnONT and its pristine SWZnONT. Zigzag structures of SWZnONTs are chosen to investigate the effects of Cu codoped SWZnONTs because the

ZnONT's zigzag structures are more stable than armchair and chiral [40]. Two zigzag structure (5, 0) and (10, 0) of ZnONTs are considered. The equilibrium positions of Zn and O atoms in (10, 0) and (5, 0) pristine SWZnONTs are calculated. In relaxation process, all atomic positions are relaxed accurately down to the forces of about 1 mRy/a.u. Table 1 shows all Zn-O bond length in (10, 0) and (5, 0) pristine SWZnONTs. Also we found that the value of the radial buckling [29, 30] for (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs are 0.29 Å and 0.35Å, respectively. To investigation of electronic properties of pristine SWZnONTs the band structures of (10, 0) and (5, 0) pristine SWZnONTs are calculated. Fig. 1 shows the electronic band structure of the pristine (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs. Our results show that the (10, 0) and (5, 0) pristine SWZnONTs are nonmagnetic direct-gap semiconductor at the $\Gamma$ point. The calculated energy gaps are 1.5 eV, and 1.7 eV for (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs, respectively. For Cu-codoped (10, 0) SWZnONTs, we use a supercell which is two units long in the direction of the tube axis and contains 40 formula units $Zn_{40}O_{40}$ and for Cu-codoped (5, 0) SWZnONTs, we use a supercell contains 20 formula units $Zn_{20}O_{20}$. Before considering effects of Cu-codoped SWZnONTs let's see the effect of single Cu atom doped in the super cell. Usually in the doping process, substitution occurs when atomic radius of doping atom is close to one of the host atoms. Since the atomic radius of Cu atom is close to Zn atom, we expect that Cu substitute the zinc atoms. To confirm this we calculated formation energy for the Cu substitution on both oxygen and zinc sites. The formation energies $(E_f)$ of the Cu-doped on SWZnONT are calculated to evaluate their stability according to ref [35]. It is found that the formation energy of the Cu substitution on zinc site and oxygen site is -1.12 eV and +0.25 eV, respectively, so Cu substitution on zinc site is more stable. Moreover for the Cu substitution on oxygen site, the formation energy is positive and it is unstable, so the Cu substitution on zinc site is our favorite system. Interestingly, these results are the same as previous work of Cu-doped bulk ZnO [19-23]. For investigation of the structural properties of the one Cu atom doped SWZnONTs systems, calculations are done for both ferromagnetic (FM) and nonmagnetic (NM) cases. We found that total energy of all systems in the FM phase is lower than the NM case; hence the FM phase of these systems is more stable than the NM phase. Fig. 2 (a) and (b) show a relaxed super cell structure doped by a single Cu atom for (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs, respectively. The Cu-O bond length after relaxation turned out to be about 1.85 Å which is shorter than Zn-O bond length. Since the electronegativity of Cu is higher than that of Zn, the O atom shifted closer to its nearest Cu neighbor with respect to its nearest Zn neighbor. The relevant parameters of structural properties of these systems are presented in table 1. It can be seen that the single Cu atom doped SWZnONT's structure and its parameters, comparing to the

pristine SWZnONTs, doesn't change significantly. To compare the electronic properties of Cu-doped SWZnONT with undoped SWZnONT, the total density of states (DOS) of these systems are calculated for both majority (spin up) and minority (spin down) spin channels. Fig. 3 (a) and (b) show total DOS, partial DOS of Cu-3d orbital and partial DOS of O-2p orbital for Cu doped (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs, respectively. For these systems spin splitting between spin up and down of DOS around Fermi energy is observed due to a strong hybridization of Cu-3d orbital with O-2p orbital. The strong p–d exchange interaction mediates the stable FM ordering. The spin splitting leads to spin polarization, $P$, which is defined as:

$$
P = \frac{N_{EF}^{\uparrow} - N_{EF}^{\downarrow}}{N_{EF}^{\uparrow} + N_{EF}^{\downarrow}}
$$

whereas $N_{EF}^{\uparrow}$ and $N_{EF}^{\downarrow}$ represent the density of states of majority spin (spin up) and minority spin (spin down) at the Fermi level, respectively. Our results show that both Cu doped (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs systems are half-metallic system with 100% spin polarization. Also the spin splitting leads to magnetic moment, $M$, which is defined as follows:

$$
M = (N^{\uparrow} - N^{\downarrow})\mu_{B}
$$

where $N^{\uparrow}$ and $N^{\downarrow}$ represent the total number of electrons of majority spin and minority spin, respectively. We found that the magnetic moment is mainly contributed by the 3d orbital component of Cu atom and the 2p orbital components of the Cu surrounding O atoms. These results are very similar to Cu-monodoped bulk ZnO phase [19, 21, 41] and cu-doped $Zn_nO_n$ clusters [42]. The value of the total magnetic moment in each unit cell due to Cu impurity doped (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs is 1 $\mu_B$. The value of the partial magnetic moment for Cu atom doped (10, 0) ZnONT is 0.57 $\mu_B$ and it is 0.58 $\mu_B$ for Cu atom doped (5, 0) ZnONT. To investigate the effects of Cu codoping on ZnO nanotubes we consider two different configurations, the substitued two Cu atoms are close or far from each other. First we consider two Cu atoms close to each other. To examine the energetic form between the FM and AFM spin-polarized electronic configurations of the Cu ions, we have performed the energy calculations for these systems in both FM and AFM phases. The total energy difference between AFM and FM states of each configuration can be calculated as follows:

$$
\Delta E = E_{AFM} - E_{FM} .
$$


It is found that the values of $\Delta E$ for Cu-codoped (10, 0) and (5, 0) ZnONT are -0.4147 and -0.6127 eV, respectively. According to the calculated results, it is found that both doped configurations favor the AFM ground state, this means that the AFM interactions exist between the Cu doped atoms. Indeed unfilled states of Cu-3d orbital leads to completed singlet states which tend to bond to similar state in another impure atom and so AFM coupling happens. Figs. 4 (a) and (b) show a relaxed super cell structure of the Cu-codoped (10, 0) and (5, 0) SWZnONTs in AFM phase, respectively. It can be seen that AFM interaction between the Cu atoms leads to the significant changes in the geometrical structure. The relevant parameters of structural properties of these systems are presented in table 2. From table 2 and Fig. 4, it is observed that in AFM state the distance between the two impure atoms (d₇ in Fig. 4) is smaller than FM state, which indicates that impurity atoms get close to each other and these distances are very smaller than that of pristine nanotubes. To show its validity we present total and partial Cu-3d orbital DOS for Cu-codoped (10, 0) and (5, 0) ZnO nanotubes in Figs. 5(a) and 5(b) respectively. As it is observed from these figures that there is no spin splitting between the spin up and down and the spin up and down DOS are quite resemble hence these structures are nonmagnetic semiconductors. This result is in contrary to the Cu-codoped bulk ZnO while it is consistent with the Cu-codoped nanocluster [24]. For the Cu-codoped (10, 0) nanotube the semiconducting band gap is 0.66 eV while for the Cu-codoped (5, 0) nanotube it is 0.78 eV. In both cases it is found that semiconductor gap is smaller than that of pristine ZnO nanotubes.

In next step effects of Cu-codoped ZnO nanotube is investigated when these two impurity atoms are far from each other. Since (10, 0) nanotube is more stable than (5, 0) nanotube, we just consider (10, 0) ZnO nanotube. Calculating $\Delta E$ for these structures we imply that the stable structure for Cu-codoped ZnO nanotube is FM state while these two impurities atoms are far from each other. Figs. 6 (a), (b) and (c) show a relaxed super cell structure of the Cu-codoped (10, 0) SWZnONT in three different states in which impurity atoms stay far from each other, respectively. The values of $\Delta E$ energy for the states (a), (b) and (c) are 0.178, 0.203 and 0.25 eV, respectively. Positive sign indicates that FM energy state is higher; consequently all of these structures are more stable at FM phase. As it can be observed from Fig. 6 the nanotubes structure under impurity doping doesn't change much and their parameters are similar to pristine nanotube and Cu-O bond length almost equals to single Cu-doping Cu-O bond length. Due to far apart impurity atoms, interaction does not affect other impurity atoms which act like separate atom in the ZnO nanotube. Also it is found that $\Delta E$ is increased by increasing distance

between the Cu impurities. To found electrical features of these systems we calculated DOS. Figs. 7 (a), (b) and (c) show total DOS for (a), (b) and (c) configurations which shown in Fig. 6. Similar to single Cu doped ZnONTs spin splitting between spin up and down of DOS around Fermi energy is observed. Our results show that all these systems are half-metallic system with 100% spin polarization because the spin-up bands are fully occupied while the spin-down bands are partially filled. The value of the total magnetic moment in each unit cell due to Cu impurity doped (10, 0) SWZnONTs are 2 $\mu_B$. The value of the partial magnetic moment for Cu atom doped (10, 0) ZnONT is 0.57 $\mu_B$. The high magnetic moment and spin polarization of these systems imply that it can be used as magnetic nanostructure and possible future applications in permanent magnetism, magnetic recording, and spintronics.

## Conclusions

We have investigated first principles calculations details of structural, electronic and magnetic properties of the Cu-codoped SWZnONTs. For this purpose two zigzag structures (5, 0) and (10, 0) of ZnONTs are selected. The results show that Zn substitution by Cu atom is the most energetically favorite structure. First we investigate the effect of single Cu atom doped SWZnONTs. It is found that the single Cu atom doped SWZnONT's structure comparing to the pristine SWZnONT doesn't change significantly. These systems are half-metallic system with 100% spin polarization. The total magnetic moment of supercell including single Cu atom is 1.0 $\mu_B$. To investigate the effects of Cu-codpoed ZnO nanotubes we consider two different configurations, first the two substituted Cu atoms are close or far from each other. When Cu atoms are at the nearest-neighbor positions, the AFM phase are stable, this means that the AFM interactions exist between the Cu doped atoms. In these systems after relaxation AFM interaction between the Cu atoms leads to the significant changes in the geometrical structure. Total density of states of Cu-codoped ZnONTs at the nearest-neighbor positions has nonmagnetic semiconducting character. The semiconducting band gap for the Cu-codoped (10, 0) nanotube is 0.66 eV while for the Cu-codoped (5, 0) nanotube it is 0.78 eV, which in both cases the semiconductor gap is smaller than that of pristine ZnO nanotubes. On the other hand the stable structure for Cu-codoped SWZnONT is FM state while these two impurities atoms are far from each other. Like single Cu atom doped SWZnONTs, all these systems are half-metallic system with 100% spin polarization because the spin-up bands are fully occupied while the spin-down bands are partially filled. The value of the total magnetic moment of each unit cell due to Cu impurity doped (10, 0) SWZnONTs are 2 $\mu_B$. In

comparison with previous work [24] it is found that when Cu-codoped ZnONTs are at the nearest-neighbor positions, the magnetic phase has a transition from FM phase to AFM phase as the size for the doping system decreases from bulk to nanostructur.
