# DENSITY FUNCTIONAL THEORY CALCULATIONS OF PROTON-CONTAINING DEFECTS IN FORSTERITE

N. H. DE LEEUW$^{\text{a}}$ and S. C. PARKER$^{\text{b},*}$

$^{\text{a}}$Department of Chemistry, University of Reading, Whiteknights Park, Reading RG6 6AD, UK; $^{\text{b}}$Department of Chemistry, University of Bath, Claverton Down, Bath BA2 7AY, UK

(Received 7 April 2000; In final form 24 November 2000)

Electronic structure calculations using the density functional theory (DFT) within the generalized-gradient approximation and ultra-soft pseudopotentials have been used to investigate the absorption of water in forsterite ($\text{Mg}_2\text{SiO}_4$), periclase (MgO) and $\alpha$-quartz ($\text{SiO}_2$). The calculated structural parameters of forsterite are found to be in good agreement with experiment. Absorption of water in the perfect lattice is calculated to be an endothermic process in all three materials. Replacing MgO units with water molecules in MgO and forsterite is also energetically unfavourable, although at the M1 site it is only just endothermic. However, replacing $\text{SiO}_2$ units with gaseous water molecules in both $\alpha$-$\text{SiO}_2$ and forsterite is calculated to be exothermic and we therefore expect water to be present in these materials mainly as OH clusters at silicon vacancy sites.

Keywords: Forsterite; Computer simulation; Cation defects; Water absorption

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Forsterite ($\text{Mg}_2\text{SiO}_4$) is the magnesium end-member of the common rock-forming mineral group of olivines, which are generally found to be richer in magnesium than in iron [1]. Its crystal structure comprises of $\text{SiO}_4$ tetrahedra linked by magnesium cations in two distinct octahedral sites, M1 or M2, where M2 is the larger, more symmetric site. Forsterite can be synthesized by solid-state reactions between MgO and $\text{SiO}_2$ at elevated tempera-

tures $(1100-1400^{\circ} C)$ [2] and from stoichiometric mixtures in the presence of water at low temperature $(500^{\circ} C)$.

Of particular interest is the influence of water which has a profound effect on the melting-point $(1890^{\circ} C)$ , which increases with pressure under anhydrous conditions but decreases with pressure under water-saturated conditions [3]. Water is present in significant quantity both in the bulk crystal and at grain boundaries, often incorporated as OH clusters at cation defect sites [4]. In this paper we present results of quantum mechanical calculations of the incorporation of water in the forsterite crystal, both in the perfect lattice and at Mg and Si defect sites.

The total energy and structure of the systems were determined using the Vienna Ab Initio Simulation Program (VASP) [5-8], employing ultra-soft "Vanderbilt" pseudo-potentials [9, 10]. In our calculations the cores consisted of orbitals up to and including the 1s orbital for oxygen, the 2s orbital for Mg and the $2p$ orbital for Si (H of course has no core). The valence orbitals are represented by a plane-wave basis set, in which the energy of the plane-waves is less than a given cutoff $(E_{cut }=600 eV)$. The calculations were performed within the generalized-gradient approximation (GGA), using the exchange-correlation potential developed by Perdew and Wang [11] with a Monkhorst-Pack [12] $k$-point mesh of $3 \times 3 \times 3$.

## 2. RESULTS
Forsterite has an orthorhombic structure with spacegroup Pbnm, $a=4.7560 \AA, b=10.2070 \AA$ and $c=5.9800 \AA, \alpha=\beta=\gamma=90^{\circ}$ [13]. The calculated lattice vectors of $a=4.7810, b=10.2970$ and $c=6.0110$, being expanded by about $0.5 \%$, are in adequate agreement with the experimental values.

We first introduced a gaseous water molecule in the perfect forsterite lattice and calculated the energy of the following reaction:

$$\left(\mathrm{Mg}_{2} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}\right)_{n}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{(g)} \rightarrow\left(\mathrm{Mg}_{2} \mathrm{SiO}_{4}\right)_{n-1} \mathrm{Mg}_{2} \mathrm{SiO}_{4} \cdot \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} \tag{1}$$

### 3. CONCLUSION

We employed electronic structure calculations to investigate the absorption of water in forsterite, MgO and $\alpha$-quartz. The calculated structural parameters of forsterite are in good agreement with the experimental structure.

DEFECTS IN FORSTERITE

Absorbing water molecules in the perfect lattices of forsterite, MgO and $\alpha$-SiO$_2$ leads to distortion of the lattice and is calculated to be an endothermic process in all three materials.

Replacement of MgO units with water molecules in MgO and forsterite is also energetically unfavourable, although at the M1 site in forsterite the process is only just endothermic. However, replacing SiO$_2$ units with water molecules in both $\alpha$-SiO$_2$ and forsterite is an exothermic process and we would therefore expect water to be present in these materials mainly at silicon vacancy sites.
