## ABSTRACT

The group VA elements (P, As, Bi) doped VO₂ were thoroughly investigated using the DFT calculations to evaluate the electronic properties, formation Helmholtz free energy, formation energy and the phase transition temperature ($T_c$). The calculated results showed that these atoms (M = P, As, Bi) could be easily doped at the octahedral interstitial site or in replacing of V or O atoms to reduce the $T_c$ of VO₂. All M-doped VO₂ could narrow the band gap of $E_{g2}$ where the $E_{g2}$ value of Bi-doped VO₂ was the smallest (about 0.30 eV). The $T_c$ for Bi@i, Bi@V, and Bi@O could be reduced to 85, 159, 23 K for the model of one M atom doped in the octahedral interstitial site or in replacing of one V or O atom of VO₂ supercells containing 32 V atoms and 64 O atoms, respectively. It means that the Bi-doped VO₂ could be a better promising candidate to obtain good thermochromic energy-saving foils. These results will give the experimental researchers a new possibility to regulate the phase transition of VO₂.

## 1. Introduction

Vanadium dioxide (VO₂) is an important thermochromic material which is applied in the "smart window" due to its specific structural and optic properties [1]. It has a phase transition from the infrared-reflective metallic state with the high-temperature rutile structure (R phase) to the infrared-transparent insulator state with the low-temperature monoclinic structure (M1 phase) at approximately 340 K ($T_c$) [2]. Therefore, VO₂ can regulate the solar transmission in response to changes in ambient temperature.

However, the application of VO₂ as the "smart window" material is limited by its high $T_c$ [3]. It is necessary to lower $T_c$ from 340 K to the comfortable room temperature at 298 K. A lot of methods can be used to reduce $T_c$ [4]. Among them, the elemental doping method is an efficient strategy [5]. When the doping atom is introduced into the VO₂ (M1), the interval of V-V pairs shrinks to decrease the structural difference between VO₂ (M1) and VO₂ (R) and consequently to lower the $T_c$ [6].

Many elemental doped VO₂ experiments are successfully performed. For example, the $T_c$ of W-doped VO₂ can be easily reduced to the room temperature [7]. The presence of Mo donors can make the Fermi level shift toward the conduction band, resulting in the decrease of activation energy $Ea$ and then $T_c$ [8]. Mg-doped VO₂ can enhance the visible transmittance and decrease $T_c$ [9]. Lanthanides like La and Tb can also be doped into VO₂ to reduce the $T_c$ due to their large ionic radii and rich valence electrons [10].

The theoretical calculations about the elemental doped VO₂ have also been reported. The results of Zhang's group using the first-principles calculations showed that $T_c$ of Be-doped VO₂ [11] or B-doped VO₂ [12] can be reduced by 58 K at.% Be or 83 K at.% B. Our previous studies used the DFT electronic structure calculations to investigate the effect of elemental (X = F, Cl, Br, I) doping on the band edges of VO₂ [13]. The group VA elements N-doped VO₂ [14] and Sb-doped VO₂ [15] have also been studied. But, until now, the group VA elements (M = P, As, Bi)-doped VO₂ have not been investigated.

In the paper, we will theoretically calculate the $T_c$ and electronic properties of M-doped VO₂ (M = P, As, Bi doped at the substitutional O or V site or at the octahedral interstitial site), so that we could find out a better dopant to reduce the $T_c$ of VO₂ and improve the visible transmittance of VO₂. These calculations will be helpful for the experimental search of dopants used in the thermochromic VO₂ coating material.

The projected-augmented wave (PAW) method [17] is employed to describe the core valence interactions and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional under the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) is used [18]. The nonmagnetic calculations are performed. The calculations are performed in the restricted open shell Hamiltonian and the spin orbit coupling effect is not considered. The rotationally invariant LDA + U method [19] is used and the adopted value of U in the LDA + U is set as 3.5 eV for the vanadium d electrons. The calculations use a $4 \times 4 \times 4$ uniform Monkhorst-Pack k grid. The energy cutoff is set at 600 eV and the convergent criterion for geometrical optimization is set as < 0.01 eV/Å.

For M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) $VO_2$, we select $2 \times 2 \times 2$ super cell with 96 atoms for monoclinic phase (M1) and $2 \times 2 \times 4$ primitive unit cells with 96 atoms for the rutile phase of $VO_2$, which include 32 V atoms and 64 O atoms. In these supercells, one foreign atom M = P, As, Bi is added at the octahedral interstitial site as the dopant atom, which is named as M@i. When one foreign atom M = P, As, Bi is in replacing of one V atom or O atom at the substitutional site, it is named as M@V or M@O, respectively. The calculated model for M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) $VO_2$ at the octahedral interstitial site or at the substitutional V or O site is illustrated in Fig. 1. The red balls represent the oxygen atoms, the grey balls represent the vanadium atoms and the purple ball represents the foreign atom (M = P, As, Bi) at the octahedral interstitial site. The M@V atom or M@O atom is denoted as the blue or green color, respectively.

For M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) $VO_2$, the formation energies are calculated using:

$$E_{form}(M@i)=E_{doped}-E_{pure}-n\mu_M \tag{1}$$

$$E_{form}(M@V)=E_{doped}-E_{pure}-n\mu_M + n\mu_V \tag{2}$$

$$E_{form}(M@O)=E_{doped}-E_{pure}-n\mu_M + n\mu_O \tag{3}$$

where $E_{doped}$ or $E_{pure}$ is the total energy of the supercell with (M@i, M@V, M@O) or without dopants, respectively. n represents the number of M (M = P, As, Bi) doped atom, here, which equals to 1. $\mu_M$ is the chemical potential of M (M = P, As, Bi) which is calculated from the bulk material using $\mu_M = E(M_{bulk})/x$ (here, x is the number of M atoms contained in the bulk). $\mu_O$ and $\mu_V$ are the chemical potentials of O and V species, respectively. The calculations of $\mu_O$ and $\mu_V$ depend on the experimental growth conditions whether in O-rich or in V-rich [20]. We will consider the two conditions where the method is similar as that in the work of Chen et al. [21].

For the O-rich condition, the chemical potential $\mu_O$ is calculated using the half value of total energy of $O_2$ molecule (i.e., $\mu_O = E(O_2)/2$). Then, the chemical potential $\mu_V$ is calculated using the below equation:

$$\mu_V + 2\mu_O = \mu(VO_2) \tag{4}$$

Here, $\mu(VO_2)$ is estimated by the value of the total energy of $VO_2$.

For the V-rich condition, the chemical potential $\mu_V$ is determined by the total energy of V bulk using $\mu_V = E(V_{bulk})/x$ (here, x is the number of V atoms contained in the bulk.). Then, the chemical potential $\mu_O$ is calculated using Eq. (4).

To evaluate the relative stabilities for M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) $VO_2$, the formation Helmholtz free energies of $MV_{32}O_{64}$, $MV_{31}O_{64}$, and $V_{32}O_{63}M$ at 0 K are calculated as,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_f U(MV_{32}O_{64}) &= U(MV_{32}O_{64})-32 \times U(V)-64 \times U(O) \\
&-1 \times U(M) \quad \text{for M@i} \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_f U(MV_{31}O_{64}) &= U(MV_{31}O_{64})-31 \times U(V)-64 \times U(O) \\
&-1 \times U(M) \quad \text{for M@V} \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_f U(V_{32}O_{63}M) &= U(V_{32}O_{63}M)-32 \times U(V)-63 \times U(O) \\
&-1 \times U(M) \quad \text{for M@O} \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

where the corresponding U's in right side of Eqs. (5)-(7) are free energies taken from DFT calculations.

The phase transition temperature $T_c$ is estimated according to the thermodynamic model of Netsianda et al. [22] as:

$$T_c = T_{c,0}(\Delta H/\Delta H_0) \tag{8}$$

where $T_{c,0}$ represents the phase transition temperature of pure $VO_2$ system, which is 340 K. $\Delta H_0$ and $\Delta H$ are the enthalpies changed in the process of phase transition for pure $VO_2$ and M-doped $VO_2$, respectively. The enthalpy could be approximated as the Helmholtz free energy obtained from the DFT calculations by neglecting the pV term for condensed matter and omitting the contribution from entropy at 0 K [23]. This method was broadly used [11,12,21].

### 3.2. Formation Helmholtz free energy

In order to check the stabilities among M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) VO₂, the calculated formation Helmholtz free energies of M-doped VO₂ (for R and M1 phase) with the M doped atom at M@O, M@V, and M@i sites are listed in Table 2. It can be seen that all values of formation Helmholtz free energies are negative, which indicates that these atoms (M = P, As, Bi) are easy to be doped into the two phases of VO₂. It can also be seen that the formation Helmholtz free energies for the M1 phase of all M-doped VO₂ are all smaller than that of the R phase, which means the M1 phase is more stable at low temperature. The formation Helmholtz free energies of M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) VO₂ (M1) at three sites (i, V, O) are all in an order of P < As < Bi. The formation Helmholtz free energies of P-doped VO₂ (M1 phase) are the least (−235.770 eV for P@i and −235.308 eV for P@V, respectively). The experimental work of Lu [24] showed that P-doped VO₂ (V₀.₉₉P₀.₀₁O₂) had high transmittance at 25 °C and low transmittance at 100 °C, so that V₀.₉₉P₀.₀₁O₂ thin films exhibited the greatest transmittance difference for the entire wavelength region, which could be good for the smart windows application. These results about the formation energies and formation Helmholtz free energies show that the M doping site can be regulated by controlling the preparation conditions of VO₂ [25].

### 3.3. Phase transition temperature

The calculated phase transition temperatures of M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) VO₂ are listed in Table 3. It can be seen that all doped atoms can reduce $T_c$ for P, As, Bi-doped VO₂ at the octahedral interstitial site or at the substitutional V or O site. The Tc for P@O, As@O, Bi@O are 173, 178, 23 K, respectively. And the Tc for P@V, As@V, Bi@V are 223, 245, 159 K, respectively. Similarly, the Tc for P@i, As@i, Bi@i are 176, 213, 85 K, respectively. They are all smaller than the Tc (340 K) of pure VO₂. Totally, the $T_c$ for Bi-doped VO₂ reduces the most compared to the pure VO₂, which means Bi doping could be easily used to lower the Tc of VO₂. Until now, the experimental work about Bi-doped VO₂ has not been reported. But for other group VA elements, the experimental and theoretical works have shown that Sb doping can reduced Tc of VO₂ [15]. Our previous calculated results also showed that N-doped VO₂ could lower the Tc when N atom is at the substitutional site in replacing of O or V atom [14]. The experimental work of Zhang et al. also showed that Tc were observed to decrease with a value of ~18 °C for VO₁.₉N₀.₁ and VO₁.₈₇N₀.₁₃ samples for N-doped VO₂ at the substitutional sites [26].

The calculated unit cell structures of pure VO₂ for M1 phase and R phase have been displayed in our previous works [13–14]. All the above calculations were implemented using the non-spin polarization method. According to the results of Sun et al. In order to clarify whether the spin-polarization can have a significant effect on our calculated system, we calculated the electronic properties of M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) $\text{VO}_2$ (M1 phase) at V, O and octahedral interstitial sites using the spin-polarization method. The band structures, the TDOS and pDOS for V(3d) and O(2p) of the unit cell for the M1 phase of M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) $\text{VO}_2$ at V and octahedral interstitial sites (that at O site are not shown for the limited space) are illustrated in Fig. 5.

The values of $E_{\text{g2}}$ are smaller than that of pure $\text{VO}_2$ (0.734 eV for the spin-polarization calculation), which means the $T_c$ can be reduced for the M-doped $\text{VO}_2$ at the V site. Similarly, the band gaps of $E_{\text{g2}}$ for M-doped $\text{VO}_2$ (M1) are 0.511, 0.498, 0.544 eV at P@i, As@i, Bi@i, respectively. They are also smaller than that of pure $\text{VO}_2$. In all, the band gap of $E_{\text{g2}}$ for Bi-doped $\text{VO}_2$ at Bi@V is the least, which means that the $T_c$ of Bi-doped $\text{VO}_2$ can be most easily reduced. Compared to the results of non-spin polarization calculations shown in Table 3, the values of $E_{\text{g2}}$ using the spin polarization calculations have not significantly changed and do not affect the conclusion.

### 4. Conclusion

The M-doped (M = P, As, Bi atoms at O, V or octahedral interstitial sites) $\text{VO}_2$ have been investigated using DFT calculations. It can be seen that these atoms (M = P, As, Bi) are easy to be doped into the two phases of the $\text{VO}_2$ compound at three sites (O, V, i) from the results of the formation Helmholtz free energies and formation energies. The calculated results also showed that all doped atoms can reduce the $T_c$ of $\text{VO}_2$. The band gaps of $E_{\text{g2}}$ for all M-doped (M = P, As, Bi) $\text{VO}_2$ are smaller than that of pure $\text{VO}_2$ (M1 phase). Totally, the band gap of $E_{\text{g2}}$ for Bi-doped $\text{VO}_2$ is the least (0.297, 0.299 and 0.300 eV at Bi@O, Bi@V, Bi@i, respectively) compared to 0.631 eV of pure $\text{VO}_2$. The corresponding $T_c$ for Bi-doped $\text{VO}_2$ can reduce least to 23, 159, 85 K for Bi@O, Bi@V, Bi@i, respectively, compared to 340 K of pure $\text{VO}_2$. So it is concluded that the Bi dopant could be a better effective doping element in M-doped $\text{VO}_2$ to obtain good $\text{VO}_2$ thermochromic materials. The experimental work about the As, Bi-doped $\text{VO}_2$ have not been studied until now. These results will be helpful for the experimental search of dopants to regulate the $T_c$ of $\text{VO}_2$.
