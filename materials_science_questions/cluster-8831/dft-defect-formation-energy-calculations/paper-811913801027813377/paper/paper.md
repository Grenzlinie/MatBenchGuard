## ABSTRACT

The effects of hydrostatic pressure on the formation enthalpy and electronic level positions of $C_N$ and $C_B$ in c-BN are investigated by means of *ab initio* plane-wave pseudopotential method using a supercell approach in the range of 0–60 GPa. The formation enthalpy decreases with pressure for $C_B$, suggesting that $C_B$ becomes much more stable and has a larger concentration with pressure increase. In contrast to $C_B$, the $C_N$ impurity exhibits positive dependence of the formation enthalpy on pressure, so the concentration of $C_N$ reduces with pressure. With pressure increase, the defect energy levels of $C_B^{+1}$ and $C_N^{-1}$ move into the conduction band and the valence band, respectively, while the defect energy levels of $C_B$ and $C_N$ in other charge states all move toward the center of band gap. These results suggest that pressure may make electron transfer between the impurity band and the conduction band or the valence band difficult and weaken its conductivity when a C atom substitutes at a B site or at a N site in c-BN.

## 1. Introduction

As one of the widest band-gap III–V compound, cubic BN crystals offer many potentially useful characteristics [1] which include all desirable properties of diamond. Since it was first synthesized in 1957 by Wentfort [2], c-BN has attracted widely attentions both in theoretical and experimental fields for practical applications. Scientifically, it is one of the wide band-gap semiconductors which can be easily doped to obtain both *n*-type and *p*-type conductivities [3,4].

In fact, none of the nitride semiconductors form stoichiometric melts, and large deviations from stoichiometry are often observed. S. Matar et al. [5] and W. Orellana et al. [6] have used first-principles to study the doped c-BN. Carbon is an unintentional impurity appearing during the growth of group-III nitrides. Early, a significant amount of carbon and oxygen impurities has been detected in c-BN thin films by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy [7], showing that the two impurities are common contaminants.

Carbon lies on the middle position between boron and nitrogen in the periodic table and is an amphoteric impurity in BN [8]. The carbon impurity in c-BN is not a new problem, and has been treated by several different theoretical techniques. Early calculations by I. Gorczyca et al. [9], using GF LMTO method and supercell approach, predicted a shallow carbon-induced level occurring at 0.5 eV and 5.1 eV above valence-band (VB) edge for $C_N$ and $C_B$ respectively. This theoretical work offers the formation energies of $C_N$ and $C_B$ in a neutral charge state only. Walter Orellana [10] has performed detailed studies on the substitutional carbon in c-BN ($C_N$, $C_B$), using first-principles pseudo-potential approach. They investigated the formation energies and electronic and structural properties of defects in their various charge states. Gubanov et al. [11] have studied the pressure dependence of neutral charge state carbon impurity states in cubic boron nitride using the tight binding-linear combination of muffin-tin orbitals-atomic sphere approximation (TB-LMTO-ASA) method with the local density approximation (LDA). Nonetheless, a complete quantitative description of the formation enthalpy and energy-level positions of defects in different charge states under pressure is still lacking.

In this letter, we report *ab initio* calculations on both $C_N$ and $C_B$ in all possible charge states using the strict convergence criteria and a large simulation cell, for examining the influence of pressure on the formation enthalpies, level position and structural properties of substitutional carbon in their various charge states.

## 2. Method of calculation

Our calculations are carried out by using projector augmented-wave (PAW) method [12,13] as implemented in the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) [14–17]. To check the stability of defects created under pressure, we calculate the formation enthalpies as a function of hydrostatic pressure. Formation enthalpies are calculated using [23]

$$
H_{\mathrm{f}}(q)=H_{\mathrm{tot}}(q)-n_{\mathrm{B}} \mu_{\mathrm{B}}-n_{\mathrm{N}} \mu_{\mathrm{N}}-\mu_{\mathrm{C}}-q\left(\mu_{\mathrm{e}}+E_{\mathrm{VBM}}\right)+\xi(q). \tag{1}
$$

Here, $\mu_{\mathrm{e}}$ is the electron chemical potential that determines the Fermi level and varies in the range of the band gap; $n_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{A}}$ (A= B, N, C) are the number of atoms in the defective supercell and atomic potential, respectively. The energy of the valence-band maximum, $E_{\mathrm{VBM}}$, is adjusted so as to cancel the background charge neutralizing the defective supercell at the charge state $q$ [24-26], $E_{\mathrm{VBM}}=E_{\mathrm{VBM}}^{\text{perfect}}+V_{\mathrm{av}}^{\text{defect}}-V_{\mathrm{av}}^{\text{perfect}}$. The first term of the right-hand side of the equation can be obtained by $E_{\mathrm{VBM}}^{\text{perfect}}=E_{T}(\text{perfect};0)-E_{T}(\text{perfect};+1)$, where $E_{T}$ (perfect; 0) is the total energy of the neutral perfect supercell. $E_{T}$ (perfect;+1) is that of the +1 charged state perfect supercell, which corresponds to the situation that one electron is removed from the valence-band maximum (VBM) of the neutral perfect supercell. The last term $\xi(q)$ in Eq. (1) is proportional to $q^{2}$ and accounts for the electrostatic energy of point charges in an oppositely charged homogeneous medium (jellium background) [27,28]. We approximate $\xi(q)$ by a Madelung term divided by the dielectric constant of BN [29] to take account of the screening effect of the charge $q$ in the host crystal environment. That is $\xi(q)=e^{2} q^{2} \alpha / L / \varepsilon$, L is the size of supercell, $q$ is the net charge of the system, $\alpha$ is the dielectric constant of the system, and $\varepsilon$ takes the value $\varepsilon=3.86$. The Madelung term $\alpha$ for the zinc-blende crystal is 1.63806. The term $\xi(q)$ is negligible in our calculation due to its relatively small value. Our calculations take advantage of error cancellations to obtain energy differences that are more precise than the total energies themselves.

## 3. Results and discussion

The calculated formation enthalpies of point vacancies $(V_{B}, V_{N})$, antisites $(N_{B}, B_{N})$ and substitutional defects $C(C_{B}, C_{N})$ in c-BN for neutral charged state are shown in Fig. 1. In this figure, values in the left side represent the formation energy under B-rich, with ones in the right side, the formation energy under N-rich. It is found that the substitutional impurities $C_{B}$ and $C_{N}$ have formation enthalpies comparable to or lower than those of vacancies, which are the lowest-energy intrinsic defects in c-BN. In the case of $C_{B}$, there is $\mu_{B}=$ $\mu_{B}^{\text{bulk}}$ under B-rich condition, and $\mu_{B}=\mu_{BN}^{\text{bulk}}-\mu_{N}^{\text{bulk}}$ under N-rich condition. In the equation $E_{\mathrm{f}}(C_{\mathrm{B}}, \mathrm{N}$-rich $)-E_{\mathrm{f}}(C_{\mathrm{B}}, \mathrm{B}$-rich $)=\mu_{\mathrm{BN}}^{\text{bulk}}-\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{\text{bulk}}-\mu_{\mathrm{N}}^{\text{bulk}}$, the calculated value $(\mu_{\mathrm{BN}}^{\text{bulk}}-\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{\text{bulk}}-\mu_{\mathrm{N}}^{\text{bulk}})$ is the key source of the different slopes. These results are identical with another theoretical work [9], showing a strong support on our current theoretical model.

The calculated defect formation enthalpies are given in Fig. 2. The equilibrium constraint for the atomic chemical potentials is given by $\mu_{\mathrm{BN}}=\mu_{\mathrm{B}}+\mu_{\mathrm{N}}$, where $\mu_{\mathrm{BN}}$ is the chemical potential of BN obtained as the total energy of the primitive unit cell. The elemental atomic chemical potentials, $\mu_{\mathrm{A}}^{0}(\mathrm{~A}=\mathrm{B}, \mathrm{N}, \mathrm{C})$ are given as the total energies per atom of metallic boron $(\alpha-B)$, solid nitrogen $(\alpha-N_{2})$, and diamond, respectively. The heat of formation $(\Delta H)$ of bulk BN is the energy gain in forming the crystal and given by $\Delta H=\mu_{\mathrm{BN}}-(\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{0}+\mu_{\mathrm{N}}^{0})$, which also specifies the range of the atomic chemical potentials with the constraint equation: the B-rich (N-rich) growth condition is achieved by putting $\mu_{\mathrm{B}} \rightarrow \mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{0}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{N}} \rightarrow \mu_{\mathrm{N}}^{0}+\Delta H$ ( $\mu_{\mathrm{N}} \rightarrow \mu_{\mathrm{N}}^{0}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{B}} \rightarrow \mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{0}+\Delta H$ ), respectively. The calculated result of heat of formation is 2.966 eV, which is in agreement with the experiment value (2.77 eV) [30].

Fig. 3 shows the density of states (DOS) of ideal c-BN as well as the DOS of the samples with either the $C_N$ or $C_B$ impurities in different charge states. The calculated energy gap for the ideal c-BN is only 4.4 eV, smaller than the measured value of 6.4 eV. Due to the band-gap underestimation in the LDA calculations, the experimental band-gap values are used in the analysis of ionization levels and formation enthalpy (by extending the range of the chemical potential). From Fig. 3, it is known that the substitutional impurity $C_B$ is a single donor, while $C_N$ is a single acceptor. We have observed that the energy level of neutral $C_B$ impurity strongly overlaps with the conduction-band (CB) of the host crystal, and the neutral $C_N$ forms a sharp, shallow and partially occupied impurity band near the valence-band (VB) edge. The impurity band of $C_B^{-1}$, which is fully occupied, is close to the CB of the host crystal, and the impurity band of $C_N^{+1}$, which is partially occupied, is near the VB edge. These results can account for the observed n-type and p-type conductivity, respectively. In addition, the impurity band of $C_B^{+1}$ which is unoccupied, strongly overlaps with the CB of the host crystal, and the impurity band of $C_N^{-1}$ overlaps with the VB of the host crystal which is fully occupied.

All levels are given with respect to the valence-band maximum. ## 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have studied the influence of hydrostatic pressure on the formation enthalpies and energy levels of $C_B$ and $C_N$ in c-BN in the pressure range of 0-60 GPa. We have observed that the dependence of the formation enthalpy of $C_B$ on pressure is much stronger than that of $C_N$ on pressure. The formation enthalpy decreases with pressure for $C_B$, which suggests that $C_B$ becomes much more stable and has a larger concentration with pressure increase. In contrast to $C_B$, the formation enthalpy of $C_N$ impurity exhibits positive dependence on pressure, so the concentration of $C_N$ reduces with pressure. We think that the opposite trend for the formation enthalpies of $C_B$ and $C_N$ with pressure mainly originate in the enthalpies of the defect-contained supercell under different pressures. It is also observed that pressure may weaken its conductivity in the case of $C_N$ and $C_B$ in c-BN. These results are useful for making a boron nitride $pn$ junction diode and an ultraviolet light-emitting diode under high pressure.
