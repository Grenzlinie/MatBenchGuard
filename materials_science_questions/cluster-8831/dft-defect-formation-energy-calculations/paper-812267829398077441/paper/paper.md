# N interstitial and its interaction with substitutional Mg in $p$-type GaN

R. R. Wixom$^{\text{a)}}$ and A. F. Wright

Sandia National Laboratories, Albuquerque, New Mexico 87185

(Received 19 April 2005; accepted 31 May 2005; published online 4 August 2005)

Density-functional theory and the generalized gradient approximation were utilized to investigate the local-energy-minimum configurations and formation energies of N interstitials and their interaction with substitutional Mg in $p$-type GaN. Along with previously proposed configurations of the N interstitial, a new variant of the split interstitial is discussed. Split interstitials are more stable than the other configurations of the interstitial. The formation energies are such that N interstitials are not expected to form under equilibrium conditions, however, they may form during nonequilibrium processes and become mobile during annealing. The N interstitial is found to bind with substitutional Mg, with the binding energy of the complex being 0.75, 0.53, and 0.35 eV for the +2, +1, and neutral charge states of the complex. © 2005 American Institute of Physics.
[DOI: 10.1063/1.1984084]

## I. INTRODUCTION

Understanding the formation and behavior of native point defects (self-interstitials and vacancies) in GaN has both scientific and technological merits. From a scientific perspective, GaN is a rather poorly understood material and additional knowledge about these fundamental defects has obvious appeal. From a technological perspective, native point defects can limit the performance of optical and electronic devices by producing nonradiative recombination, trapping carriers, and passivating or compensating dopants. Improved understanding of these defects is expected to aid device engineers in lowering defect concentrations and thereby reducing their undesirable effects. Point defects can be formed during growth or as a result of postgrowth treatments such as annealing, ion implantation, or electron irradiation. Direct observation of self-interstitials and vacancies is generally difficult and only limited experimental data are currently available regarding these defects in GaN. Positron annihilation measurements have detected Ga vacancies ($V_{\text{Ga}}$) in $n$-type GaN(Si),$^{1}$ and open volume defects in $p$-type GaN(Mg), which are proposed to be neutral complexes of Mg with the N vacancy ($V_{\text{N}}$).$^{2}$ In addition, effects attributed to $V_{\text{N}}$ and the N interstitial ($N_{I}$) have been detected by optical measurements following irradiation of GaN with 0.42-MeV electrons,$^{3}$ and $N_{I}$ have been detected by near-edge x-ray-absorption fine structure after dopant implantation.$^{4}$ The Ga interstitial ($\text{Ga}_{I}$) has been identified by optical detection of electron paramagnetic resonance (ODEPR) following a 2.5-MeV electron irradiation.$^{5}$ Interpretation of the experimental data has been aided by density-functional theory (DFT) results for the atomic configurations, formation energies, and diffusion activation energies of $V_{\text{Ga}}$, $V_{\text{N}}$, and $N_{I}$ as a function of the defect charge state.$^{6-10}$ DFT has also been employed to study the interactions of these defects with H (Refs. 10–12) and the interaction of $V_{\text{N}}$ with Mg acceptors in $p$-type GaN(Mg).$^{13}$

This paper reports DFT results that bear on the formation of $N_{I}$ and $\text{Mg}_{\text{Ga}}N_{I}$ complexes in GaN. We report the local-energy-minimum (LEM) configurations, formation energies, and $\text{Mg}_{\text{Ga}}N_{I}$ binding energies as a function of charge state. Our study was motivated by recent experimental results which suggest that $N_{I}$ produced during proton irradiation of GaN(Mg,H) becomes mobile at moderate temperatures and forms complexes with Mg acceptors.$^{14}$ The $\text{Mg}_{\text{Ga}}N_{I}$ complex has not previously been investigated by DFT, however, the LEM configurations, formation energies, and diffusion activation energies for isolated $N_{I}$ have been reported in earlier DFT studies.$^{6,7,10}$ Our calculations improve upon the previous DFT calculations in two respects. First, we have used the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) for exchange and correlation (XC), whereas the earlier studies used the local-density approximation (LDA). The GGA adds to the LDA by utilizing information about the local gradients of the electron density, and thus is expected to yield improved results for interstitial defects. Second, we have included the Ga $3d$ electrons among the set of valence electrons, whereas two of the previous studies$^{6,7}$ approximated the effects of the Ga $3d$ electrons with the nonlinear core correction (nlcc).$^{32}$ An earlier study by one of us showed that treating the Ga $3d$ electrons as valence produced better agreement between theoretical and measured lattice constants for GaN.$^{15}$ In Sec. II we describe the theoretical techniques used in this study and elaborate on the differences between the GGA and LDA. In Sec. III we report our results for $N_{I}$ and $\text{Mg}_{\text{Ga}}N_{I}$ and in Sec. IV we discuss our findings in the context of experimental results and previous DFT results in the literature.

## II. THEORETICAL TECHNIQUES

Our calculations were performed using the Kohn-Sham formulation$^{16}$ of DFT and the PW91 formulation$^{17}$ of the GGA. As noted in the Introduction, the GGA differs from the LDA by including terms that depend on the gradient of the local electron density. As such, the GGA is expected to provide an improved description of XC for ionic materials, interstitial regions, and crystalline defects where the electron density exhibits pronounced spatial variation. In fact, several

authors have reported improved results for a wide variety of materials when using the GGA to calculate total energies, reaction energies, activation barriers, and structural stability. $^{18-22}$ In the case of GaN, the DFT-GGA activation energies for $H^{+}$ diffusion were found to be approximately50% larger than DFT-LDA values and were in better agree- ment with experiment. $^{23}$ Moreover, the improvement was de termined to result from the particular treatments of XC as opposed to the small difference between the LDA and GGA equilibrium lattice constants.
The DFT-GGA calculations were performed using theVienna ab initio Simulation Package $^{24-26}$ (VASP) with a 72 atom supercell and {2,2,2} Monkhorst-Pack parameters $^{27}$ to generate sampling points in the Brillouin zone. Ultrasoft pseudopotentials $^{28}$ from the VASP database were used with the Ga 3d and the Mg 2p electrons included among the set of valence electrons. An energy cutoff of 366 eV (821 eV) was used for the plane-wave basis (augmentation charges). The supercells were generated using DFT-GGA lattice constants, which are $0.5 \%(a=3.207 \AA)$ and $0.7 \%(c=5.223 \AA)$ largerthan the measured values. $^{29}$
Local-energy-minimum (LEM) configurations of $N_{I}$ and MgGaN, were obtained by specifying an initial configuration, computing the forces on all atoms in the supercell, moving the atoms in the direction of their forces, and iterating until the magnitudes of the forces were less than $0.01 eV / \AA$ . Us ing the cohesive energies of the resulting local minimum configurations, formation energies were calculated according to the equation,
$$E^{f}\left(D^{q}, E_{F}\right)=-E_{\mathrm{coh}}\left(D^{q}\right)-\sum n_{i} \mu_{i}+q E_{V}+q E_{F}, \quad(1)$$
 where $E_{coh}(D^{q})$ is the 0-K cohesive energy of a supercell containing defect D in charge state $q, n_{i}$ is the number of type i atoms in the supercell, $\mu_{i}$ is the corresponding refer ence chemical potential, $E_{V}$ is the energy of the valence-band edge (VBE) in GaN including a supercell-dependent shift, and $E_{F}$ is the Fermi level defined to be zero at the VBE with a maximum value equal to the measured GaN energy gap atlow temperature, $3.48 eV^{30}$
The supercell-dependent shift in the value of $E_{V}$ is im portant only for charged defects, and in the absence of an absolute reference, is utilized to align the defect band struc- ture with that of the bulk. Also, applying periodic boundary conditions to supercells containing charged defects creates the possibility of electrostatic interactions among the re- peated images. Since at this time there is not a clearly estab- lished method for removing them, the results of this study are presented without including corrections for spurious elec- trostatic interactions.
The formation energies were computed assuming that
$$n \mathrm{GaN} \rightarrow(n-1) \mathrm{GaN}+\mathrm{N}_{I}^{q}+\mathrm{Ga}+q e^{-}\qquad(2)$$
 is the dominant reaction producing $N_{I}$ . In this reaction, n formula units of GaN become (n-1) units of GaN containing one $N_{I}$ in charge state q, q electrons are deposited into the electron reservoir at the Fermi level, and one Ga atom re-turns to a reservoir of orthorhombic Ga. Consistent with this reaction, the Ga chemical potential $\mu_{Ga}$ was set to $-E_{coh}(Ga)$ and $\mu_{N}$ was set to $-[E_{coh}(GaN)-E_{coh}(Ga)]$ , where $E_{coh}(Ga)$  is the cohesive energy per atom of orthorhombic Ga and Ecoh(GaN) is the cohesive energy per formula unit of wurz- ite GaN. In the literature, these values chosen for $\mu_{Ga}$ and $\mu_{N}$ . are often referred to as Ga-rich conditions.
The reaction producing $Mg_{Ga}$ was taken to be
$$\begin{aligned}
n \mathrm{GaN}+\mathrm{Mg}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{2} \rightarrow(n+2) \mathrm{GaN}+3 \mathrm{Mg}_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{q}+\mathrm{Ga}+3 q e^{-}, \\
(3)
\end{aligned}$$
 where n formula units of GaN react with one $Mg_{3} N_{2}$ formulaunit to yield n+2 GaN formula units containing three $Mg_{Ga}$  in charge state g, one additional Ga atom in the Ga reservoir, and 3g electrons in the electron reservoir. Consistent with the method of choosing the Ga reference state, $\mu_{Mg}$ was setto $-{E_{coh}(Mg_{3} N_{2})-2[E_{coh}(GaN)-E_{coh}(Ga)]} / 3$ where $E_{coh}$ (Mg3N2) is the cohesive energy per formula unit of bulk $Mg_{3} N_{2}$ . The Ga, GaN, and $Mg_{3} N_{2}$ cohesive energies were obtained from DFT-GGA calculations using the same pseudopotentials and cutoff energies as in the defect calcula- tions, and the same or equivalent supercells and Brillouin- zone sampling points.
The energy to separate $(Mg_{Ga} N_{I})^{q}$ into isolated $Mg_{Ga}^{-}$ and N/41 was calculated according to the expression
$$\begin{array}{r}
E_{B}\left[\left(\mathrm{Mg}_{\mathrm{Ga}} \mathrm{N}_{I}\right)^{q}\right]=E^{f}\left(\mathrm{Mg}_{\mathrm{Ga}}^{-}\right)+E^{f}\left(\mathrm{~N}_{I}^{q+1}\right)-E^{f}\left[\left(\mathrm{Mg}_{\mathrm{Ga}} \mathrm{N}_{I}\right)^{q}\right], \\
(4)
\end{array}$$
 where a positive value indicates that $(Mg_{Ga} N_{I})^{q}$ is bound and dissociation is taken to be charge conserving. The value for $E^{f}(Mg_{Ga}^{-})(1.36 eV)$ was taken from Ref. 13, where the DFT-GGA calculations for $Mg_{Ga}$ were performed with the same technical parameters as the calculations here.
III. RESULTS
Four LEM configurations of the $N_{I}$ were investigated: type-I split interstitial, type-II split interstitial, channel cen- tered, and channel wall. The type-II split-interstitial configu- ration is unique to this study and has not been previously discussed. However, the other three configurations were pre- sented in previous theoretical treatments of the $N_{I}^{6,7,10}$ The type-I and type-II split interstitials, shown in Figs. 1(d) and1(h) and Figs. 1(c) and 1(g), differ by a $60^{\circ}$ rotation of the N-N bond about the c axis (vertical), with the N-N bonds lying in a plane of symmetry. Consistent with a hexagonal crystal, there are six orientations where the N-N pair is aligned on a symmetry plane-three of which result in the type-I structure and the other three result in the type-II struc- ture. Both of these structures were relaxed without symmetry constraints and additionally verified by explicitly breakingthe symmetry. The channel-centered structure, with the $N_{I}$  located in the center of the hexagonal channel, is shown in Figs. 1(b) and 1(f). The channel-wall configuration was de- termined to be unstable with respect to the other configura- tions and will not be discussed further in this report. A cor-responding section of bulk GaN was included in Figs. 1(a) and 1(e) for reference.

In this reaction, $n$ formula units of GaN and one $\mathrm{N}_{2}$ molecule become $n$ units of GaN, two $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ in charge state $q$, and $2 q$ additional electrons in the electron reservoir. The chemical potentials derived from this reaction are $\mu_{\mathrm{N}}=-E_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathrm{N}_{2}) / 2$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{Ga}}=-[E_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathrm{GaN})-E_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathrm{N}_{2}) / 2]$, where $E_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathrm{N}_{2})$ is the cohesive energy per molecule of $\mathrm{N}_{2}$. Using these chemical potentials would shift the $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ formation energies given in Table I by the heat of formation of GaN (-1.09 eV) [this is our computed value that is at the upper end of the range of measured values, -1.6 through -1.1 (Refs. 33-37)]. Even with this shift, the resulting $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ formation energies are sufficiently high that significant equilibrium populations are not expected. However, this does not imply that $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ are irrelevant. Many important processing and experimental techniques require conditions that are far from equilibrium. $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ may be produced by irradiation, as was the case in the experiments of Myers and Seager, $^{14}$ and $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ will certainly be created during ion implantation. $^{4}$ The results of this study indicate that $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ created in $p$-type material can bind to $\mathrm{Mg}_{\mathrm{Ga}}$ acceptors if they have sufficient mobility.

DFT calculations predict diffusion barriers of 1.4 eV for $\mathrm{N}_{I}^{3+}.^{6}$ We can estimate the diffusion length of $\mathrm{N}_{I}$ using the equations,

$$
D=D_{0} e^{-E_{A} / k T} \tag{6}
$$

and

$$
L=(D t)^{1 / 2}, \tag{7}
$$

where $D$ is the diffusivity, $D_{0}$ is set at $1 \mathrm{~cm}^{2} / \mathrm{s}$, $E_{A}$ is the diffusion barrier, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ is the temperature, $L$ is the diffusion length, and $t$ represents time. At $300\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, the lowest annealing temperature in the experiments

of Myers and Seager, $^{14}$ the diffusion length could be as great as $0.4\ \mu$m in 1 h.

## V. SUMMARY
Local-energy-minimum configurations of the $N_I$ and the $Mg_{Ga}N_I$ complexes were identified using DFT-GGA total-energy calculations. The formation energies of these structures were reported for Ga-rich conditions and a conversion to N-rich conditions was provided. The type-I split interstitial was identified as having the lowest formation energy in $p$-type GaN. Due to the prohibitively high calculated formation energies, few $N_I$ are predicted to form under equilibrium conditions. However, any $N_I$ that are created during nonequilibrium growth, ion implantation, or irradiation are predicted to bind with $Mg_{Ga}$ acceptors in $p$-type GaN at temperatures where the interstitials become mobile.
