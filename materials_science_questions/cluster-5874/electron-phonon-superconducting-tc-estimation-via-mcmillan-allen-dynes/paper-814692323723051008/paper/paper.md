# A possible explanation of pressure dependence of critical temperature $T_c$ of alkali doped $C_{60}$ superconductors

Rajiv Kumar Pandey

Department of Physics, Ideal Institute of Technology,
Govindpuram, Ghaziabad - 201003, U.P. India
drrkp70@rediffmail.com

Received 3 July 2014
Revised 23 August 2014
Accepted 10 September 2014
Published 9 October 2014

The pressure dependent changes in transition temperature $(T_c)$ of alkali doped $C_{60}$ superconductor has been studied theoretically considering the combination of phonon mediated mechanism and high energy electronic interaction mechanism. The latter mechanism involves bond polarization in interaction with conduction electrons. The expression for $T_c$, isotope effect coefficient $(\alpha)$ and $(dT_c/dP)_P$ has been obtained to explain the observed experimental results of alkali doped $C_{60}$. A good agreement has been found in experimental $(T_c)$ and $(dT_c/dP)_{P=0}$ values and calculated values.

Keywords: Fullerene; superconductors.

PACS numbers: 72.82.Rj, 74.20.Mn, 74.62.Fj

## 1. Introduction

Since the discovery of high $T_c$ superconductor, the different class of material systems e.g., oxides, metal, organic and inorganic compounds have been extensively studied. The carbon cluster balls, i.e., Buckminster fullerenes are one of them. The undoped films of carbon of molecular structure $C_{60}$ is insulator. It has to exhibit the conducting properties at room temperature by doping of alkali metals, e.g., $M = K, Rb$, Cs, I etc. But on the other hand, the astonishing properties of the alkali doped $C_{60}$ becomes superconductor at reasonably high critical temperature $(T_c)$ i.e., 19.3 K for $K_xC_{60}$ and 28 K for $Rb_xC_{60}$ $(X=3).^{1-4}$

After discovery of superconductivity in alkali doped fullerene, a new dimension has been added to field of high $T_c$ superconductivity. The value of critical temperature $K_3$ $C_{60}$ and $Rb_3$ $C_{60}$ systems have been reported to decrease with application of pressure. $^{5-8}$ The values of carbon isotope effect coefficient of alkali doped $C_{60}$ superconductors with 100% substation of $C_{13}$ for $C_{12}$ was shown to be $0.30 \pm 0.06$ for $K_3$ $C_{60}$ and $0.37 \pm 0.05$ for $Rb_3$ $C_{60}.^{9,10}$

The pairing mechanism in alkali doped carbon cluster ($\text{M}_x\text{C}_{60}$, $\text{M} = \text{K}$, $\text{Rb}$ and $\text{I}$) superconducting systems is still not clear and an extensive effort has been made to understand the pairing mechanism in high $T_c$ superconductors involving different kinds of excitations.$^{11-16}$

In the present communication, we have studied the pressure dependence ($T_c$) and isotope effect coefficient within the frame work of phonon and high energy excited electrons interactions to explain the reported experimental parameters. The central theme to consider the combined pairing mechanism is due to the variation of isotope effect value from BCS's value.

### 2. Model Hamiltonian
The experimental value of isotope effects coefficient of alkali doped $\text{C}_{60}$ superconductor$^{9,10}$ suggests that a pure phonon mediated mechanism may not be the only mechanism in these systems. The predicted value of isotope effect coefficient by pure phonon mediated mechanism is higher than observed value for alkali doped $\text{C}_{60}$ system.$^{5-10}$ So, we have considered a combined pairing mechanism which consists of phonon mediated and higher energy excitation, i.e., electronic in nature based mechanism, for $\text{M}_x\text{C}_{60}$ system. The latter mechanism is based on the bond polarization through charge transformation. It consists the conduction electrons of solids derived from the anti-bonding $t_{1\mu}$ type orbital of $\text{C}_{60}$ and two level system of polarizable bonds, e.g., two carbon atom involved in $\pi$-type bonding contains the valance bond ($\text{C=C}$) and ionic configuration ($\text{C}^+-\text{C}^--\text{C}^--\text{C}^+$). The ionic configuration has reasonable weightage, although it may be at higher energy level. The distortion and nonplanarity around each carbon atom in $\text{C}_{60}$ is reducing the repulsion between two electrons on the same atoms. An excess electron available at anti-bonding orbital of carbon in alkali doped $\text{C}_{60}$ are donated by alkali metals i.e., $\text{K}$, $\text{Rb}$ and $\text{I}$ etc. These electrons populate the energy bond arising from lowest unoccupied anti-bonding orbital $\text{C}_{60}$, i.e., $t_{l\mu}^*$, $t_{lg}^*$.$^1$ It is doubly occupied bonds in $\text{C}_{60}$ that are prone to considerable polarization which can be described in terms of charge transfer in bonds form $\text{C=C}$ singlet local pair to on site local pair ($\text{C}^+-\text{C}^--\text{C}^--\text{C}^+$). In such process spins of pair remains in singlet state configuration in both states.

The Hamiltonian for above discussed process can be expressed as
$$
H = H_0 + H' \tag{1a}
$$
where
$$
H_0 = \sum_{K,\sigma} \in_K a_{K\sigma}^+ a_{K\sigma} + \sum_j \hbar\omega_{bp}b_j^+b_j \tag{1b}
$$
and
$$
H' = \sum_{jK_1K_2\sigma} iP_j(a_{K_1\sigma}^+a_{K_2\sigma}b_j + \text{h.c.}) . \tag{1c}
$$

The term $H'$ is representing the interaction of pairing caused by bond polarization.
It is basically electronic in nature and very similar to electron–phonon interaction.
In the term $H_0$, $\in_k$ is single particle energy of electrons in conduction band derived
from the antibonding orbital of $C_{60}$ i.e., $t_{1\mu}^*$. The $a_{K\sigma}^+$ ($a_{K\sigma}$) is creation (annihila-
tion) operator, $K$ is wave vector, $\sigma$ is spin index and $\hbar\omega_{bp}=\{E(C^+-C^--C^--C^+$,
$Q_j^*)-E(C{=}C, Q_j)\}$, is the energy related to bond polarization. The $E(C^+-C^--$
$C^--C^+, Q_j^*)$ is energy for ionic configuration and the $E(C{=}C, Q_j)$ is the energy
corresponding to the HL — covalence bond resulting between two carbon atom.
The $Q{*_j}$ and $Q_j$ collectively denotes lattice configuration for these tow state. Here,
we are concerned with two levels Bose system. The creation (annihilation operator)
for bond $j$ is $b_j(b_j^+)$. The term $H'$ is the interaction between conduction electrons
and excitation of level Boson system, $P_j$ is coupling constant. The rough estimation
of magnitude of $|P_j|$ is done by using expression, $(e^2r/R)$ where $r = 1.4$ Å, $R$ ($C_{60}$
sphere) $= 5$–7 Å. The range of order of $|P_j|$ is 0.4–0.8 eV.¹⁷

As we are dealing with a two level system a pseudo spin formulation with spin
1/2 may be more suitable than boson method. So the Hamiltonian can be rewritten
as

$$
H = \sum_{K,\sigma} \in_K\ a_{K\sigma}^+a_{K\sigma} + \sum_j \hbar\omega_{bp}S_j^Z + i \sum_{j,K_1,K_2,\sigma} P_j(a_{K_1\sigma}^+a_{K_2\sigma}S_j^+ + \text{h.c.}). \quad (2)
$$

The system Hamiltonian can be canonically transformed as

$$
\bar{H} = e^{-\tau}He^{\tau}, \tag{3}
$$

where $\tau$ is given as

$$
\tau = \sum_{j,K,K',\sigma} iP_j \left[ \frac{a_{K\sigma}^+a_{K'\sigma}S_j^+}{(\in_{K'} - \in_K) + \hbar\omega_{bp}} + \frac{a_{K\sigma}^+a_{K\sigma}S_j^-}{(\in_{K'} - \in_K) - \hbar\omega_{bp}} \right]. \tag{4}
$$

So,

$$
H_{\text{int}} = \frac{1}{2}[H',\tau], \tag{5a}
$$

$$
H_{\text{int}} = - \sum_{j,j',K_1,K_1',K_2K_2',\sigma,\sigma'} \left( \frac{\hbar\omega_{bp}|P_j|^2\delta_{j,j'}\langle S_j^Z \rangle}{(\in_K - \in_{K'})^2 - (\hbar\omega_{bp})^2} \right) a_{K_1\sigma}^+a_{K_2\sigma'}^+a_{K_1\sigma'}a_{K_2'\sigma}.
\tag{5b}
$$

Now considering $\langle S_j^z \rangle = -(1/2)$ and $|\in_K - \in_{K'}| < \hbar\omega_{bp}$.

We have $H_{\text{int}}$ as

$$
H_{\text{int}} = - \sum_{K,K'} V_{bp}^*a_{K'\uparrow}^+a_{-K'\downarrow}^+a_{-K\downarrow}a_{K\uparrow}, \tag{6a}
$$

$$
V_{bp}^* = \frac{|P_j|^2}{\hbar\omega_{bp}}. \tag{6b}
$$

Above discussed pairing mechanism is considered as addition pairing mechanism alongwith electron-phonon mechanism to explain value of isotope effect coefficient in alkali doped C₆₀ superconductor. To explain experimental facts, the model Hamiltonian contains the phonon mediated and electronic (due to bond polarization) pairing interaction relevant to alkali doped C₆₀ superconductors, so the Hamiltonian ($H_{\text{Red}}$) will be.

$$
H_{\text{Red}} = \sum_{K,\sigma} \in_{K} a_{K \sigma}^{+} a_{K \sigma} + \sum_{K K'} V_{K K'} a_{K' \uparrow}^{+} a_{-K' \downarrow}^{+} a_{-K \downarrow} a_{K \uparrow}. \tag{7}
$$

The pairing matrix element $V_{K K'}$ appearing in $H_{\text{Red}}$ can be defended for different range of energies.

$$
V_{K K'} =
\begin{cases}
-(V_{ph} + V_{bp}) & | \in_{K} | < \hbar \omega_D \\
-V_{bp} & \hbar \omega_D < | \in_{K} | < \hbar \omega_{bp}
\end{cases} \tag{8}
$$

The $V_{ph}$ is coupling for phonon mediated and $V_{bp}$ is coupling strength of involving exchange of polarization modes from the excitation and de-excitation of two level systems. We have also considered a two square well potential and the $\Delta(\varepsilon_{K},T)$ for different region of energies is considered as:

$$
\Delta(\in_{K,T}) =
\begin{cases}
A \Delta_0(T) & | \in_{K} | < \hbar \omega_D \\
B \Delta_0(T) & \hbar \omega_D < | \in_{K} | < \hbar \omega_{bp} \\
0 & \hbar \omega_{bp} < | \in_{K} |
\end{cases} \tag{9}
$$

Now using values of $\Delta(\in_{K,} T)$ and $V(K, K')$, the energy gap equation will become as

$$
A = A(\lambda_{ph} + \lambda_{bp}) I_1(T) + B \lambda_{bp} I_2(T), \tag{10a}
$$

$$
B = A \lambda_{ph} I_1(T) + B \lambda_{bp} I_2(T), \tag{10b}
$$

where $\lambda_{ph} = N_0 V_{ph}$ and $\lambda_{bp} = N_0 V_{bp}$. $N_0$ is density of state (DOS).

$$
I_1(T) = \int_{0}^{\hbar \omega_D} \frac{d \in_{K'}}{\sqrt{(\in_{K'}^2 + A^2 \Delta_0^2(T))}} \tanh \left( \frac{\sqrt{\in_{K'}^2 + A^2 \Delta_0^2(T)}}{2 K_B T} \right) \tag{11a}
$$

and

$$
I_2(T) = \int_{\hbar \omega_D}^{\hbar \omega_{bp}} \frac{d \in_{K'}}{\sqrt{(\in_{K'}^2 + B^2 \Delta_0^2(T))}} \tanh \left( \frac{\sqrt{\in_{K'}^2 + B^2 \Delta_0^2(T)}}{2 K_B T} \right). \tag{11b}
$$

At $T = T_c \Delta_0(T_c) = 0$, then Eqs. (10a) and (10b) are reduced as

$$
A = A(\lambda_{ph} + \lambda_{bp}) I_1(T_c) + B \lambda_{bp} I_2(T_c), \tag{12a}
$$

$$
B = A \lambda_{ph} I_1(T_c) + B \lambda_{bp} I_2(T_c), \tag{12b}
$$

where

$$
I_1(T_c) \cong l_n \left( \frac{1.14 \hbar \omega_D}{K_B T_C} \right) \tag{13a}
$$

and

$$
I_{2}(T_{c}) \cong \ell_{n}\left(\frac{\hbar \omega_{b p}}{\hbar \omega_{D}}\right) \tag{13b}
$$

For nontrivial solution of Eqs. (12a) and (12b), the solution for $I_{1}\ (T_{c})$, we have

$$
I_{1}(T_{c}) = (\lambda_{ph} + \lambda_{bp}^{*})^{-1}, \tag{14a}
$$

where

$$
\lambda_{bp}^{*} = \frac{\lambda_{bp}}{1 - \lambda_{bp}I_{2}(T_{c})}. \tag{14b}
$$

Therefore the expression of $T_{c}$ will be

$$
T_{c} = 1.14\theta_{D}\exp(-(\lambda_{bp}^{*} + \lambda_{ph})^{-1}), \tag{14c}
$$

where

$$
\theta_{D} = \frac{\hbar \omega_{D}}{K_{B}} \quad \text{and} \quad \theta_{bp} = \frac{\hbar \omega_{bp}}{K_{B}}.
$$

The expression of isotope effect coefficient $(\alpha)$ has been given as

$$
\alpha = -\frac{d\ell_{n}T_{c}}{d\ell_{n}M}. \tag{15a}
$$

Substitute the $T_{c}$ expression from Eq. (14c) into Eq. (15a) we get

$$
\alpha = 0.5\left[1 - \left[1 + \lambda_{ph}\ell_{n}\left(\frac{T_{c}}{1.14\theta_{D}}\right)\right]^{2}\right]. \tag{15b}
$$

Equations (14) and (15) can be used to calculate the values of $T_{c}$ and $\alpha$ at different pressure. We have considered the pressure effect through DOS which is a function of pressure. It reduces with application of pressure.⁴ We have introduced this fact by considering an adhoc form of DOS with function $(P)$. It is given as

$$
N(P) = N(0)\exp(-AP), \tag{16}
$$

where $N(0)$ is DOS at $P = 0$. The value of $T_{c}$ and $\alpha$ become function of pressure $(P)$ through adhoc approximation because, coupling constant are dependent on DOS as

$$
\frac{\lambda_{ph}(P)}{\lambda_{ph}(0)} = \frac{\lambda_{bp}(P)}{\lambda_{bp}(0)} = \frac{N(P)}{N(0)} = \exp(-AP). \tag{17}
$$

The expression of $(dT_{c}/dP)_{P}$ is derived by considering above fact and we differentiate the $(T_{c})$ expression [Eq. 14(c)] with respect to $P$.

$$
\left(\frac{dT_{c}}{dP}\right)_{p} = -AT_{c}(P)\left[\ell_{n}\left(\frac{1.14\theta_{D}}{T_{c}(P)}\right) + (1 - 2\alpha(P))\ell_{n}\left(\frac{\theta_{bp}}{\theta_{D}}\right)\right]. \tag{18}
$$

This expression on (18) has been derived under approximation that $\theta_{D}$ is independent of pressure. It can be used to calculate $(dT_{c}/dP)_{P}$ at different value of pressure.

The energy gap $\beta=(2\Delta/K_BT_c)$ is given elsewhere.$^{16}$ The expression is given as

$$
\beta=\left(\frac{2\Delta}{K_BT_c}\right)=\frac{4\left(\frac{\theta_D}{T_C}\right)}{\exp(\lambda_{ph}+\lambda_{bp}^{*})^{-1}-1}. \tag{19}
$$

Equation (19) can be rewritten as

$$
\beta=\left(\frac{4}{1.14-\left(\frac{T_C}{\theta_D}\right)}\right). \tag{20}
$$

Equation (20) is used to compute the value of $\beta$ at different pressure.

### 3. Results and Discussion

We have studied the problem within the framework of bond polarization electronic and phonon mediated pairing mechanisms. We have developed the expression for different parameters.

The expressions (14) and (15) are used to find value of $T_c$ and $\alpha$ at different pressure $(P)$. We have first calculated the value $T_c$ and $\alpha$ at $P=0$ by choosing appropriate values of $\theta_D=80$ K, $\theta_{bp}=3000$ K, $\lambda_{bp}(0)=0.1609$ and $\lambda_{ph}(0)=0.2575$ for $K_3$ $C_{60}$ and $\lambda_{bp}(0)=0.1715$ and $\lambda_{ph}(0)=0.4355$ for $Rb_3$ $C_{60}$ superconductor. For calculation the pressure dependent value of $T_c$, we need the value of parameter $A$. The value of unknown parameter $A$ is estimated by using experimental values of $T_c=6.8$ K at $P=2.33$ GPa for $K_3$ $C_{60}$ and $T_c=12.7$ K at $P=1.92$ GPa for $Rb_3$ $C_{60}.^{5-8}$ The computed value of unknown parameter $A$ for $K_3$ $C_{60}$ and $Rb_3$ $C_{60}$ are 0.14091 and 0.1936, respectively.
