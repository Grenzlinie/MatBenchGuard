# Possibility of $LiPdH_x$ as a new ionic superconductor
D. Singh, R. E. Cohen, and D. A. Papaconstantopoulos
Naval Research Laboratory, Washington, D.C. 20375-5000
(Received 9 August 1989; revised manuscript received 28 September 1989)

We report local-density-approximation-based electronic-structure calculations for the compound LiPdH. As in the case of the recently discovered family of $CuO_2$-based superconductors, $LiPdH_x$ is predicted to be a metal with substantial ionic character. We have used the band-structure results to calculate the McMillan-Hopfield parameters within the rigid muffin-tin approximation. These parameters have values close to those of PdH and lead to the prediction that $LiPdH_x$ may be a good superconductor.

## I. INTRODUCTION
The discovery of high-temperature superconductivity in the La-Sr-Cu-O system $^1$ has, for obvious reasons, stimulated a great deal of interest in related cuprate materials. Since then, layered cuprates with $T_c$'s above 120 K have been discovered. $^2$ While the obvious relationship among these materials is the presence of $CuO_2$ layers, there are other similarities. One of these is their ionic character. Superconductivity is now known to exist in several ionic metals outside the cuprate family, particularly in the Ba-Pb-Bi-O and Ba-K-Bi-O systems$^{3,4}$ and in $LiTi_2O_4.^5$ $Ba_{1-x}K_xBiO_3$ is of particular interest, since it is a high-$T_c$ material with a transition temperature above 30 K.

On the theoretical side there is still no consensus regarding the origin of the high $T_c$'s in the layered cuprates. While electron-phonon interactions have not yet been ruled out as a pairing mechanism, there has been considerable speculation that the pairing may be due to magnetic interactions. Such a view is supported by the fact that the superconductors are often associated with nearby antiferromagnetic insulating phases. On the other hand, the existence of superconducting oxides, lacking copper ions, suggests that other mechanisms deserve study. In any case, the existence of the Bi-O superconductors suggests that it may be very worthwhile to search for new ionic superconductors outside the cuprate family.

Among the metal hydrides, Overhauser has analyzed x-ray powder diffraction data for $LiBeH_3$ and $Li_2BeH_4$, and based on his analysis of the structure concluded that, if metallic, these materials may be high-temperature superconductors. $^6$ Unfortunately, total-energy studies indicate that at least for $LiBeH_3$ the actual structure is different from the proposed structure and that the material is actually an insulator. $^{7-9}$

PdH, on the other hand, is a superconductor$^{10}$ with $T_c$=10 K. The discovery of superconductivity in this material stimulated a great deal of experimental and theoretical work in part because Pd itself is not a superconductor while PdH has a relatively high transition temperature and in part because PdH(D) has a strong inverse isotope effect. $^{11}$

Here we report self-consistent band-structure calculations for LiPdH. It is found that, as in the case of the high-$T_c$ cuprates, $^{12}$ LiPdH is an ionic metal. We have performed calculations of the McMillan-Hopfield$^{13}$ parameters in order to determine whether or not it is a likely superconductor.

## II. METHOD
The synthesis and structure of tetragonal $(P4/mmm)$ $LiPdH_x$ have been reported for hydrogen stoichiometries $x$ between 0.70 and $0.82.^{14}$ The (as yet unsynthesized) stoichiometric compound has three atoms per unit cell with an interesting structure consisting of PdH planes separated by Li atoms, somewhat reminiscent of the high-$T_c$ cuprates. Since the stoichiometric material has not yet been synthesized and, as a result, no experimental lattice constants are available for it, it was necessary to calculate them by total-energy minimization. This was done by calculating the total energy for several values of the $a$ and $c$ lattice parameters using the general-potential linearized-augmented-plane-wave (LAPW) method. $^{15}$

Since the LAPW method has been discussed in detail elsewhere, only those aspects specific to the present calculation are discussed here. The self-consistent total-energy calculations were performed using muffin-tin sphere radii, $R_{Pd}$=2.3 a.u., $R_{Li}$=2.0 a.u., and $R_{H}$=1.25 a.u., and a basis set cutoff corresponding to a maximum interstitial wave vector $K_{max}$=4.8 yielding a well converged basis of about 370 LAPW's. The Brillouin-zone averages were performed using 40 special $k$ points$^{16}$ in the irreducible wedge. The convergence with respect to this parameter was checked by performing calculations using a set of 288 $k$ points. These later calculations yielded total energies within 1 mRy of the results using 40 $k$ points with only small changes in the energy differences, indicating that the former were adequately converged.

In order to determine whether superconductivity is feasible, we have evaluated the McMillan-Hopfield parameter $\eta=\langle I^2\rangle N(E_F)$, where $\langle I^2\rangle$ is an electron-ion matrix element and $N(E_F)$ is the electronic density of states (DOS) at the Fermi level. In the rigid muffin-tin approximation$^{17}$ (RMTA),
$$
\eta_{a}=\frac{E_F}{\pi^{2}N(E_F)} \sum_{l} 2(l+1)\sin^{2}(\delta_{l+1}^{F}-\delta_{l}^{F}) \frac{N_{l}^{F}N_{l+1}^{F}}{N_{l}^{(1)a}N_{l+1}^{(1)a}}.
\tag{1}
$$

In this expression the $\delta_l^F$ are the scattering phase shifts

at the Fermi energy $E_{F}$ for atom $\alpha$ and angular momentum $l$. $N_{l}^{(1) \alpha}$ are the single scatterer densities of states defined in Ref. 17 and $N_{l}^{F}$ are the site angular momentum densities of states at $E_{F}$. These parameters are obtained from the calculated band structure and self-consistent potential. The McMillan equation is then used to estimate the superconducting transition temperature,

$$
T_{c}=\frac{\langle\omega\rangle}{1.45} \exp \left[-\frac{1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^{*}-0.62 \lambda \mu^{*}}\right], \quad(2)
$$

where we set the Coulomb pseudopotential, $\mu^{*}=0.1$ and the electron-phonon coupling,

$$
\lambda=\sum_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\eta}{M\left\langle\omega^{2}\right\rangle}\right)_{\alpha}, \quad(3)
$$

where the sum on $\alpha$ is over the different atoms in the unit cell. Since $\langle\omega\rangle$ is not known for $\mathrm{LiPdH}_{x}$ the same values were used for the $\mathrm{Pd}$ and $\mathrm{H}$ as in $\mathrm{PdH} ;{ }^{18}$ i.e., $M\left\langle\omega^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Pd}}$ $=4.951$ and $M\left\langle\omega^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}=1.062$, with $M\left\langle\omega^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{Li}}=M\left\langle\omega^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{H}}$, in order to estimate $T_{c}$.

There are two important uncertainties in this method of estimating the transition temperature. First of all, the RMTA neglects potentially important contributions to the electron-phonon coupling in "ionic metals" like $\mathrm{LiPdH}_{x}$. This is a result of the fact that in metals with substantial ionic character, moving an ion may result in substantial changes in the potential (which can be relatively poorly screened in these materials) at other sites. Such off-site contributions to the electron-phonon coupling are neglected in the RMTA. It has been argued that, as a result, the RMTA may seriously underestimate $\eta$ in ionic metals, $^{19}$ resulting in calculated values of $T_{c}$ which are too low. The other main uncertainty has to do with the unknown mean phonon frequencies in $\mathrm{LiPdH}_{x}$. The effect of errors in the estimates of the mean phonon frequencies is discussed in more detail below.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As mentioned, stoichiometric $\mathrm{LiPdH}_{x}$ has yet to be synthesized, and thus no experimental lattice parameters are available. Thus, before calculating the electronic structure it was necessary to perform total-energy calculations in order to obtain the lattice parameters. As mentioned, total-energy calculations were performed over a range of $\underline{a}$ and $\underline{c}$ lattice parameters. The resulting total energies (given in Table I) were then fit to a quadratic form in order to obtain the equilibrium lattice parameters, which were then used for the electronic-structure calculations discussed below. The resulting lattice parameters are $\underline{a}=2.751 \AA$ and $\underline{c}=3.826 \AA$. The in-plane $\underline{a}$ lattice parameter is thus about $3 \%$ smaller than that reported for the substoichiometric $x=0.82$ material, with the $\underline{c}$ lattice parameter almost exactly equal to the experimental value. The self-consistent band structure of $LiPdH_{x}$ at the equilibrium lattice parameters is shown in Fig. 1, with the corresponding resolved DOS in Fig. 2. As may be noted from these figures, there is a hybridized $H s$ -Pd band about $8 eV$ below the Fermi level, characteristic of metal hydrides, with only a small $H$ contribution to the DOS at the Fermi level. The remaining occupied bands are predominantly $Pd s$ and $Pd d$ in character, with a small $Li$  component. There are two bands crossing the Fermi ener- gy, both of which have a significant $Li$ character and show significant dispersions in the out-of-plane direction. The first yields a more or less cylindrical Fermi surface around the $M$ point while the second gives rise to a spheroidal electron pocket around $A$ . Although statements regarding ionic character in metals are necessarily imprecise, it is important to note that, while there is some $Li$ component in the resolved DOS at and below the Fermi energy, it is small so $Li$ occurs as $Li^{+}$ in $LiPdH_{x}$ . Thus, like the lay ered cuprates, $LiPdH_{x}$ is an ionic metal.

The parameters entering into the Gaspair-Gyorffy ex- pression [Eq. (1)] are given in Table II and the resulting $\eta$  and $\lambda$ values are given in Table III. The corresponding values for PdH are included for comparison purposes. In PdH there is a large contribution to $\lambda$ from $H s p$ scatter ing with a smaller (but significant) contribution from the $Pd$ atoms. In $LiPdH$ the contributions to $\lambda$ from the $H$  and $Pd$ atoms are only about half as large as in PdH. There is, however, a large contribution from $Li s p$ scatter ing which can be traced to the free scatterer value on the s channel which is a factor of 2 smaller for the $Li$ than for the $H$ .

Interestingly, our total-energy calculations indicate that $LiPdH$ is much softer against changes in the $\underline{c}$ lattice pa rameter than changes in the $a$ lattice parameter. (The quadratic fit yields elastic constants, $c_{11}+c_{12}=390 GPa$ , $c_{33}=100 GPa$ , and $c_{13}=30 GPa$ .) This implies that while the PdH planes are relatively stiff, the $Li$ atoms are only weakly coupled to them. This may have important conse- quences for superconductivity. In particular, this result suggests that there may be relatively soft phonons associ- ated with the $Li$ atoms, and thus the assumption that M<ω²>_Li=M<ω²>_H is a reasonable one. In fact, it could be that $Li$ in this material has even softer phonon modes, which would result in higher $\lambda$ and $T_{c}$ . In any case, given the uncertainties in applying the RMTA to a system like $LiPdH_{x}$ and the unknown average phonon frequencies, we do not take the above as a precise calculation of $T_{c}$ , but rather as an indicator of whether superconductivity is like- ly in $LiPdH_{x}$ . The results strongly suggest that further work on this system is called for.

## IV. CONCLUSIONS

Ab initio general-potential LAPW calculations of the electronic structure of $LiPdH$ have been performed. Stoichiometric $LiPdH$ is predicted to be an ionic metal with significant contributions to the DOS at the Fermi level from both the $Li$ and $Pd$ atoms. RMTA calculations indicate that there is a large contribution to the electron- phonon coupling arising from the $Li$ atoms and that $LiPdH_{x}$ is a likely superconductor.
