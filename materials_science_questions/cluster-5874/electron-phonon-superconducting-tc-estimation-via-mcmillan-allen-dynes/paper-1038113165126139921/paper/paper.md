## ABSTRACT
The report on near-ambient superconductivity in nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride is still under controversy. Here, guided by x-ray diffraction data of nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride, we choose a possible cubic superconducting phase named rocksalt-type LuH (RS-LuH) and study the superconductivity of pristine RS-LuH, nitrogen-doped RS-LuH named $\text{Lu}_4\text{NH}_3$, and lanthanide elements substitution of RS-LuH at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa by performing density functional theory and isotropic Eliashberg equation. As pressure increases from 0 to 10 GPa, all phonon spectra notably harden, resulting in the suppression of electron-phonon coupling. Moreover, the decrease in superconducting critical temperature ($T_c$) of $\text{Lu}_4\text{NH}_3$ is due to the reduction of electron-phonon coupling and the density of states at the Fermi level compared with pristine RS-LuH. Finally, our investigation reveals a monotonic increase in $T_c$ with ascending atomic numbers via lanthanide element substitution. Notably, RS-LuH exhibits the highest $T_c$ ($T_c = 19.7$ K) among all compounds we studied. Therefore, our theoretical exploration enriches the understanding of the superconductivity in nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride under varying pressures.

## I. INTRODUCTION
Since the discovery of superconductivity in $1911,^{1}$ both theoretical and experimental physicists have strived for superconductors with higher $T_c$ at ambient conditions. Even now, it is still a tremendous challenge in condensed matter physics. Half a century ago, metallic hydrogen, which was predicted to be the candidate for room-temperature superconductivity by Ashcroft, $^{2}$ exhibited the $T_c$ of 300 K at high pressure near $325\ \text{GPa}.^{3,4}$ However, such extremely high pressure for inducing a superconducting transition is tough to acquire, and how to reduce the high pressure comes to be a challenge. One effective way called "chemical pre-compression" was proposed by Ashcroft, which elaborated that dense hydrides compared to metallic hydrogen require lower pressure to induce metallization of hydrogen. $^{5}$ Following this theory, a variety of binary hydride superconductors have been predicted by using structural search methods at high pressure, and some have been successfully synthesized and proved in experiment, such as $\text{H}_3\text{S}$ ($T_c = 203$ K at $155\ \text{GPa}$), $^{6,7}$ $\text{LaH}_{10}$ ($T_c = 250$ K at $170\ \text{GPa}$), $^{8-11}$ and $\text{YH}_9$ ($T_c = 262$ K at $182\ \text{GPa}$). $^{12,13}$

Unfortunately, the extremely high pressure limits their application. Therefore, high temperature superconductivity is expected to be achieved at lower pressures in ternary hydride compounds, which is a natural thought. One way is based on the former structures of binary hydrides, doping or substituting metal atoms. Recently, $\text{Li}_2\text{MgH}_{16}$ has a remarkably high estimated $T_c$ about 473 K at 250 GPa through Li atoms doping $\text{MgH}_{16}.^{14}$ Besides, (Y, $\text{Sr})\text{H}_{11}$ possesses an estimated $T_c$ of 240 K at 175 GPa by Y substituting Sr in $\text{SrH}_{22}.^{15}$ Additionally, the enhancement of superconducting properties in the La-Ce-H system at moderate pressures also validate the feasibility of this method. $^{16}$ Another effective way is doping or substituting hydrogen with other light elements such as B, C, and N, which is foreseen to reduce the required high pressure. By applying this method, a large number of structures have been studied, including $\text{CeBeH}_8$ ($T_c = 118$ K at $150\ \text{GPa}$), $^{17}$ $\text{Ca}(\text{BH}_4)_2$ ($T_c = 110$ K by hole doping of 0.03 holes per formula unit at ambient pressure), $^{18}$ $\text{CsBH}_5$ ($T_c = 98$ K at $1\ \text{GPa}$), $^{19}$ $\text{La(BN)}_5$ ($T_c = 69$ K at ambient pressure), and $\text{Y(BN)}_5$ ($T_c = 59$ K at ambient pressure). $^{20}$

By employing both chemical pre-compression and nitrogen-doping ways, the report of near-ambient superconductivity in a nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride has attracted great attention, $^{21}$ exhibiting a maximum $T_{c}$ of 294 K at 10 kbar. From then on, numerous theoretical and experimental studies have been carried out. Unfortunately, on the one hand, the vast majority of experi- menters do not observe the claimed room-temperature supercon- ductivity phenomenon in nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride and similar structures. $^{22-27}$ On the other hand, the results of large-scale structural search and high-throughput first-principles calculations also indicate the absence of near-ambient room-temperature superconductors. $^{28-34}$ However, the exact structure of the reported cubic phase has not been clarified yet, and the existence of its room-temperature superconductivity is still under controversy. Thus, determining the lattice structure of nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride is crucial. As one of the primary methods in detecting crystal structures, x-ray diffraction (XRD) plays an important role in the research of the structure of nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride. $^{35}$ However, the difficulty in identifying lighter hydrogen and nitrogen elements via XRD complicates the determination of the crystal structure, actual stoichiometry, and precise atomic posi- tions in nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride. Thus, understanding its parent structure becomes imperative. Among the parent structures of this nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride, both rocksalt-type LuH (RS-LuH) and fluorite-type $LuH_{2}(FL-LuH_{2})$ contribute to XRD peaks. $^{35}$ Unfortunately, FL-LuH $_{2}$ are confirmed to exhibit a rela tively low $T_{c}$ of about $0.1 ~K^{34}$ Therefore, our researches have pre dominantly focused on RS-LuH. Here, based on the first-principles calculations, we study the superconductivity of pristine RS-LuH, nitrogen-doped RS-LuH $Lu_{4} NH_{3}$ , and lanthanide elements substi tution of RS-LuH at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa.

## II. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS

The structure relaxation, electronic energy band, electronic density of states (DOS) calculations of RS-XH (X=lanthanide ele- ments) and $Lu_{4} NH_{3}$ were performed in the framework of the density functional theory as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP). $^{36}$ The Fermi surface (FS) calculations were performed in the framework of the density functional theory as implemented in QUANTUM ESPRESSO package (QE). $^{37}$ The projector augmented wave method $^{38}$ was used to model the elec tron-ion interactions, and the generalized gradient approximation(GGA) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) parametrization $^{39}$  was adopted for the exchange-correlation potentials. Both the lattice parameters and the atom positions were relaxed to obtain the optimized structure. For VASP, the convergence criteria for energy and force are set as $10^{-6}$ and -0.03 eV. A plane-wave basis was used with a kinetic energy cutoff of 500 eV. For QE, the cutoffs for wave functions and charge density were set as 80 and 800 Ry, respectively. The Bader charge, $^{40}$ electron localization function, and charge density difference were calculated using the VASP. The electronic integration was performed over a $14 \times 14 \times 14 k$ -point grid. For FS and DOS calculations, denser $56 \times 56 \times 56$ and $42 \times 42 \times 42 k$ -point grids were adopted for RS-XH ( $X=$ lanthanide elements), respectively. Correspondingly, denser $32 \times 32 \times 32$ and $24 \times 24 \times 24 k$ -point grids were adopted for $Lu_{4} NH_{3}$ , respectively.

The lattice dynamics was performed within the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) $^{41}$ as implemented in QE. For RS-XH( $X=$ lanthanide elements), the phonon and EPC constant werecalculated on a $7 \times 7 \times 7 q$ -point grid, and a denser $28 \times 28 \times 28$  k-point grid was used for evaluating an accurate electron-phonon interaction matrix. For nitrogen-doped RS-LuH $Lu_{4} NH_{3}$ , the phonon and EPC were calculated on a $4 \times 4 \times 4 q$ -point grid, and a denser $16 \times 16 \times 16 k$ -point grid was used for evaluating an accurate electron-phonon interaction matrix, which are all imple- mented in QE.

The total EPC constant $\lambda$ is obtained via isotropic Eliashbergfunction $^{42-44}$

$$
\alpha^{2} F(\omega)=\frac{1}{2 \pi N\left(E_{F}\right)} \sum_{\mathbf{q} v} \delta\left(\omega-\omega_{\mathbf{q} v}\right) \frac{\gamma_{\mathbf{q} v}}{\omega_{\mathbf{q} v}},\qquad(1)
$$

$$
\lambda=2 \int \frac{\alpha^{2} F(\omega)}{\omega} d \omega=\sum_{\mathbf{q} v} \lambda_{\mathbf{q} v},\qquad(2)
$$

where $\alpha^{2} F(\omega)$ is Eliashberg function, $N(E_{F})$ is the DOS at the Fermi level, $\omega_{q v}$ is the phonon frequency of the vth phonon modewith wave vector $q$ , and $\gamma_{q v}$ is the phonon linewidth. $^{42-44}$ The $\gamma_{q v}$  can be estimated by

$$
\gamma_{\mathbf{q} v}=\frac{2 \pi \omega_{\mathbf{q} v}}{\Omega_{B Z}} \sum_{\mathbf{k}, n, m}\left|g_{\mathbf{k} n, \mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q} m}^{v}\right|^{2} \delta\left(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k} n}-E_{F}\right) \delta\left(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q} m}-E_{F}\right),\qquad(3)
$$

where $\Omega_{B Z}$ is the volume of the BZ, $\varepsilon_{k n}$ and $\varepsilon_{k+q m}$ indicate the Kohn-Sham energy, and $g_{\mathbf{k} n, \mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q} m}^{v}$ represents the screened electron phonon matrix element. $\lambda_{q v}$ is the EPC constant for phonon mode qv, which is defined as

$$
\lambda_{\mathbf{q} v}=\frac{\gamma_{\mathbf{q} v}}{\pi \hbar N\left(E_{F}\right) \omega_{\mathbf{q} v}^{2}}.\qquad(4)
$$

$T_{c}$ is estimated by McMillan-Allen-Dynes formula $^{44}$ 

$$
T_{c}=\frac{\omega_{\log }}{1.2} \exp \left[\frac{-1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^{*}(1+0.62 \lambda)}\right].\qquad(5)
$$

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
### A. Crystal structures and electron-transfer properties of RS-LuH and $Lu_4NH_3$ at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa

The lattice structures of RS-LuH and $Lu_4NH_3$ are depicted in Figs. 1(a) and 1(b). In Fig. 1(a), the conventional cell of RS-LuH reveals its space group to be $Fm-3m$, where the lanthanide element atoms occupy the face-centered and vertex positions of the cube, while the hydrogen atoms occupy the edge-centered and body-centered positions. Table I presents the lattice constants of RS-LuH at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa, illustrating a decrease in lattice constants from 4.78 to 4.60 Å as the pressure increases. The primitive cell of $Lu_4NH_3$ displayed in Fig. 1(b) indicates its space group to be $Pm-3m$, a structure akin to RS-LuH. The distinction between RS-LuH and $Lu_4NH_3$ lies in the replacement of body-centered hydrogen with nitrogen. Table I also presents the lattice constants of $Lu_4NH_3$ under pressures of 0, 1, and 10 GPa, demonstrating a decrease from 4.77 to 4.63 Å with increasing pressure. In summary, as pressure increases, the lattice constants of both compounds consistently decrease.

### B. Electronic structures of RS-LuH and $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa

The electronic structures of RS-LuH and $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ are analyzed through band structure, electronic density of states (DOS), and Fermi surface (FS) calculations, as depicted in Figs. 4–7. For RS-LuH at 0 GPa, Figs. 4(a) and 4(b) show the orbital-resolved band structure along the high-symmetry line $\Gamma$-$X$-$U$-$K$-$\Gamma$-$L$-$W$-$X$ for Lu and H, respectively. The metallic nature of RS-LuH is evident, with two bands crossing the Fermi level, forming a hole pocket around $\Gamma$ point, originating from the $d$ orbital of Lu, which is consistent with the previous studies. $^{34}$ According to Fig. 4(c), the DOS at the Fermi level is dominated by Lu atoms. Furthermore, the point group of RS-LuH is $O_{h}$. Based on the crystal-field analysis, Lu $d$ orbitals can be splitted into a doublet $e_{g}$ ($d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $d_{z^{2}}$) and a triplet $t_{2g}$ ($d_{xy}$, $d_{xz}$, and $d_{yz}$). In addition, due to the cubic structure of RS-LuH with high symmetry, Lu $p$ orbitals are triple-degenerated ($p_{x}$, $p_{y}$, and $p_{z}$). Thus, from the orbital-resolved DOS shown in Fig. 4(d), it is seen that the DOS near the Fermi level mainly comes from the $s$ orbital, triple-degenerated $p$ orbitals ($p_{x}$, $p_{y}$, and $p_{z}$), triple-degenerated $d$ orbitals ($d_{xy}$, $d_{xz}$, and $d_{yz}$), and double-degenerated $d$ orbitals ($d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $d_{z^{2}}$) of Lu atoms. Moreover, Fig. 6(b) displays the FS of RS-LuH at 0 GPa. The color of FS indicates relative Fermi velocity $v_{F}$, reflecting the different slopes of the bands that make up the FS. Compared with the high-symmetry line in the first Brillouin zone shown in Fig. 6(a), it can be seen that the highest $v_{F}$ mainly originates from the band along the $\Gamma$-$K$ direction, while the low $v_{F}$ is also mainly from the one along the $\Gamma$-$K$ direction. For RS-LuH at 1 and 10 GPa, the shape of electronic structures remains generally consistent compared with RS-LuH at 0 GPa. The main difference is a slight elevation of the Fermi level with increasing pressure, leading to a decrease in the density of states at the Fermi level $N(E_{F})$ from 8.07 states/spin/Ry/unitcell to 6.96 states/spin/Ry/unitcell listed in Table III, which is expected to impact superconductivity. In addition, as shown in Fig. 6, the increase in pressure also leads to an increase in $v_{F}$ at the FS.

For $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ at 0 GPa, Figs. 5(a)–5(c) depict the orbital-resolved band structure along the high-symmetry line $\Gamma$-$X$-$M$-$\Gamma$-$R$-$X$-$R$-$M$ for Lu, N, and H, respectively. It shows that $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ is metallic with four bands crossing the Fermi level. Moreover, there exist two flatbands and three saddle points near the Fermi level along $X$-$M$, $\Gamma$-$R$, and $R$-$X$ directions, corresponding to two van Hove singularities (vHs) at about 0.18 and $-0.30$ eV in the total DOS shown in Fig. 5(d), which may enhance the superconductivity. Furthermore, the DOS at the Fermi level is dominated by Lu atoms. Due to $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ and RS-LuH having the same point group, resulting in similar DOS degeneracy. Thus, from the orbital-resolved DOS shown in Fig. 4(e), it is seen that the DOS near the Fermi level mainly comes from the $s$ orbital, triple-degenerated $p$ orbitals ($p_{x}$, $p_{y}$, and $p_{z}$), triple-degenerated $d$ orbitals ($d_{xy}$, $d_{xz}$, and $d_{yz}$), and double-degenerated $d$ orbitals ($d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $d_{z^{2}}$) of Lu atoms. The FS of $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ at 0 GPa, presented in Fig. 7(b), indicates that the highest $v_{F}$ predominantly arises from the band along the $X$-$M$ direction, while the lower $v_{F}$ is mainly from the one along the $\Gamma$-$M$ direction. Regarding $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ at 1 and 10 GPa, there are no significant change observed in the electronic structures with increasing pressure. But the difference between electronic structures of $Lu_{4}NH_{3}$ at different pressures is that the Fermi level is elevated with the increasing pressure, which is similar to RS-LuH. It leads to a decrease of the $N(E_{F})$ from 30.98 states/spin/Ry/unitcell to 28.80 states/spin/Ry/unitcell listed in Table III and an increase in $v_{F}$ at the FS, as shown in Fig. 4.

### E. Electron-phonon coupling and superconductivity of lanthanide elements substituted RS-LuH at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa
We now focus on the impact of the increase in atomic number among lanthanide elements on the EPC and superconductivity at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa. According to Eqs. (4) and (5), it becomes evident that the increase in $\omega_{log}$, $N(E_{F})$, and $\lambda$ all have the potential to enhance $T_{c}$. The subsequent discussion will delineate the influence of these three factors on $T_{c}$ individually.

First, from Figs. 11(a)-11(c), the lattice constant of RS-XH (X = lanthanide elements) decreases as the number of lanthanide elements increases. On the other hand, from Table IV, the Bader charge analysis of RS-XH reveals an increase in the number of charge transfers with the rise in atomic number overall at pressures of 0, 1, and 10 GPa. The combined effect of these two factors results in the strength of ionic bond in RS-XH, which reflects the magnitude of $\omega_{log}$. As shown in Fig. 11(g), the $\omega_{log}$ monotonically increases with the increase in atomic number on the whole at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa. Due to the shortest lattice constant and relatively high charge transfer among the RX-XH, RS-LuH achieves a relatively high $\omega_{log}$ at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa, respectively. Second, owing to the volume reduction caused by the decrease in the lattice constants and the increase in the valence electrons due to the increase in the atomic number, the $N(E_{F})$ also exhibits a monotonic increase except for the La element shown in Fig. 11(h). RS-LuH possesses the highest $N(E_{F})$ among all RS-XH at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa, respectively. In addition, for the same RS-XH at different pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa, the $N(E_{F})$ decreases with the increase in the pressure. Third, as shown in Fig. 11(i), the $\lambda$ shows a monotonic enhancement with the increase in atomic number at different except Yb element, which can be attributed to the softening of the phonon spectra shown in Figs. 11(d)-11(f). The positions where softening occurs are consistent with the RS-LuH analyzed above. With the increase in atomic number, the softening of phonon spectra continues to strengthen until Lu. Consequently, RS-LuH possesses the highest $\lambda$ among all RS-XH at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa, respectively, which phenomenon was figured out in $LuH_{6}.^{49}$ In addition, for the same RS-XH at different pressure 0, 1, and 10 GPa, with the increase in the pressure, the hardening of phonon spectra leads to the weakening of $\lambda$. Combining the influence of $\lambda$, $\omega_{log}$, and $N(E_{F})$ three factors, $T_{c}$ monotonically increases with the increase in atomic number at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa except Yb element as Fig. 11(j) shown. Due to the highest $\lambda$, $N(E_{F})$, and relatively high $\omega_{log}$, RS-LuH at 0 GPa possesses the highest $T_{c}$ among all RS-XH at pressures 0, 1, and 10 GPa.

Moreover, it is necessary to discuss the anomalous behaviors of RS-YbH in the above figures. Due to Yb having fully filled $4f$ electrons, it weakens the shielding effect on the atomic nucleus, which leads to an increase in atomic radius and lattice constant. Because of the abnormal increase in lattice constant, the dynamic instability of RS-YbH occurs at 0 and 1 GPa. With the increase in the pressure, it stabilizes the lattice structure of RS-YbH at 10 GPa.

## IV. CONCLUSION
In summary, DFT and isotropic Eliashberg equation have been performed to demonstrate the effect of pressure, nitrogen doping, and lanthanide elements substitution on the superconductivity of RS-LuH. Based on the results of our calculations, the following conclusions can be obtained: (i) Increasing pressure from 0 to 10 GPa results in significant hardening of all phonon spectra, thereby suppressing the EPC. (ii) The decrease of $T_c$ of Lu₄NH₃ can be attributed to the decrease of $\lambda$ and $N(E_F)$. (iii) A monotonic increase in $T_c$ of RS-XH ($X$ = lanthanide elements) by lanthanide elements substitution is revealed. Combining the influence of $\lambda$, $\omega_{log}$, and $N(E_F)$, RS-LuH emerges as the compound with the highest $T_c$ among all studied compounds, reaching approximately 19.7 K. These results provide valuable insights for future investigations aimed at elucidating the superconductivity of nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride.
