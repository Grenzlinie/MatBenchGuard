# Highlights

- Li-deposited $C_3N$ superconducts with $T_c$ of 13.03 K, which is higher than 8.1 K of Li-deposited graphene.

- The EPC constant for electron doped $C_3N$ is less that 0.1 and no superconductivity appear.

# Phonon-mediated superconductivity in charge doped and Li-deposited two dimensional $C_3N$

Xing Wang $^{1}$, Jianyong Chen $^{2*}$

## ABSTRACT

By using first-principles calculations, we investigate the electron-phonon interaction and phonon-mediated superconductivity of charge doped and Li-deposited monolayer $C_3N$. No superconductivity is found for 0.2 e/cell doped $C_3N$ due to a small electron-phonon coupling (EPC) strength which is less that 0.1. Surprisingly, when Li atom is deposited the monolayer, much more low lying optical phonon modes especially the out-of-plane vibration mode participate the EPC process. This results in a pronounced boost of EPC constant to 0.86 and the transition temperature $T_c$ obtained according to isotropical McMillan-Allen-Dynes formula reaches 13.03 K, which is higher than 8.1 K of Li-deposited graphene and 1.4 K of Ca-deposited graphene. Our findings not only widening the potential application of the intriguing two-dimensional material $C_3N$, but also enrich the world of two dimensional superconductors with a relatively high $T_c$.

## 1. Introduction

Most recently, two-dimensional (2D) polyaniline ($C_3N$) was synthesized experimentally with controllable scale by the direct pyrolysis of hexaaminobenzene trihydrochloride single crystals [1] and the polymerization of 2, 3-diaminophenazine [2]. It has a 2D honeycomb lattice with the homogeneous distribution of N and C atoms. Due to comparable atomic sizes of C and N and consistent covalent configurations, fascinating electronic, chemical, thermal and mechanical properties of 2D $C_3N$ materials have been theoretically predicted [3-5]. It has a promising application in the fields of gas sensor and capture [6,7], Na, K [8] and Li-ion batteries [9]. Specifically, $C_3N$ monolayer is predicted to be a potential electrode material for Na- and K-ion batteries by first principle calculations. The diffusion barriers are as small as 30 meV for Na and 70 meV for K, implying high mobility and cycle stability [8]. $C_3N$

nanosheet is an excellent sorbent for negatively charge-controlled switchable CO₂ capture
[10]. NO and NO₂ gases can be sensed with high sensitivity. The recovery time is short in NO
sensing and competitive to other materials in NO₂ sensing [11]. For the electronic aspect, a
high on/off ratio of $5.5×10^5$ is obtained in back-gated field-effect transistors (FET) made of
monolayer C₃N. What's more, its bandgap can be modified to cover the whole visible light
region by tuning the quantum-dot diameters, although the as-synthesized 2D C₃N is a
semiconductor with an indirect bandgap of 0.39 eV. In terms of phonon related properties,
C₃N exhibit massively reduced in-plane thermal conductivity (κ) as compared to graphene,
making C₃N a potential material for thermoelectric applications. These results may make C₃N
a very promising candidate for future electronics and optoelectronics devices at nanoscale.

In the last decade, much efforts has been made to explore 2D superconductors. Searching for
exotic 2D superconductors with higher superconducting transition temperature (T_c) is highly
desired, due to its potential application in nano-superconducting quantum interference [12]. It is
predicted theoretically that superconductivity can be introduced by the metallization of monolayer
2D materials, such as graphene [13], monolayer MoS₂ [14], phosphorene [15] and borophene [16].
Many of the superconducting states are observed experimentally, e. g. Li-decorated graphene [17],
ion gated MoS₂ [18] and NbSe₂ [19]. Given that C₃N is made of light atoms and the interatomic
strength is strong, which is beneficial for phonon-mediated superconductivity, it is interesting to
see if monolayer C₃N can be tuned to another 2D superconductor and how high the transition
temperature is.

In this paper, the phonon dispersion, electron-phonon coupling mechanism and
phonon-mediated superconductivity of electron doped and Lithium deposited monolayer C₃N are
investigated in detail by first-principles calculations. In Section 2, we present the computational
method and models; Main results and neccesary discussions are elaborated in Section 3; The paper
is compledted with a conclusion drawn in Section 4.

## 2. Computational details and models

Electronic and phonon related calculations within density functional theory (DFT) are
carried out by Quantum Espresso package [20]. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerh of parameterized
generalized-gradient approximation (PBE-GGA) [18] is used to describe the exchange
correlation. An normconserving pseudopotential is adopted to represent the interaction
between ionic cores and valence electrons. Plane-wave basis set for the electronic wave
functions and the charge density with energy cutoffs are 80 Ry and 320 Ry respectively. The
electron-phonon coupling constants are calculated with a 36×36×1 $\mathbf{k}$ points. The dynamical matrix
is computed on a 9×9×1 Monkhorst-Pack (MP) $\mathbf{q}$ grid. To simulate two dimensional systems, a 15

Å vacuum is added to avoid the spurious interaction. The ionic positions in the cell are fully relaxed to their minimum energy configuration using the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) algorithm. The convergence criteria for energy and force are set as $10^{-7}$ Ry and $10^{-6}$ Ry a.u.⁻¹ respectively. Biaxial tensile strain is realized by directly enlarging the lattice constant defined as $\varepsilon=(a-a_0)/a_0\times100\%$, where $a_0$ and $a$ is the lattice constant for unstrained and strained structures.

The total electron-phonon coupling (EPC) constant $\lambda$ is obtained via isotropical Eliashberg function [21-23]:

(1)
$$
\alpha^{2}\mathrm{F}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{N}\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}\right)}\sum_{\mathbf{q}v}\delta\left(\omega-\omega_{\mathbf{q}v}\right)\frac{\gamma_{\mathbf{q}v}}{\omega_{\mathbf{q}v}}
$$

$$
\lambda=2\int\mathrm{d}\omega\alpha^{2}\mathrm{F}(\omega)/\omega=\sum_{\mathbf{q}v}\lambda_{\mathbf{q}v} \tag{2}
$$

where $\alpha^{2}\mathrm{F}(\omega)$ is Eliashberg function and $\mathrm{N}(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}})$ is the DOS at the Fermi level, $\omega_{\mathbf{q}v}$ is the phonon frequency of the $v$th phonon mode with wave vector $\mathbf{q}$, $\gamma_{\mathbf{q}v}$ is the phonon linewidth [21-23]. $\lambda_{\mathbf{q}v}$ is the EPC constant for phonon mode $\mathbf{q}v$. $\mathrm{T_c}$ is estimated by McMillan-Allen-Dynes formula [23]:

$$
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{\omega_{\log}}{1.2}\exp\left[\frac{-1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^{*}(1+0.62\lambda)}\right] \tag{3}
$$

The retarded Coulomb pseudopotential $\mu^{*}$ in equation (3) is set to the same value of 0.112 with that of ref [13] and logarithmic average of the phonon frequencies $\omega_{\log}$ reads as:

(4)
$$
\omega_{\log}=\exp\left[\frac{2}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\omega_{\max}}\alpha^{2}\mathrm{F}(\omega)\frac{\ln(\omega)}{\omega}\mathrm{d}\omega\right]
$$

Monolayer $C_3N$ possesses a planar honeycomb lattice with P6/mmm space group, it can be considered as nitrogen doped graphene, in which N atoms substitute for C atoms in the pristine graphene as shown in Fig. 1(a). Each N atom has three nearest-neighbor C atoms and each C atom has two nearest-neighbor C atoms and one nearest-neighbor N atom. The atomic bond lengths (1.40 $\mathring{A}$) of $C_3N$ are slightly smaller than that of graphene (1.42 $\mathring{A}$). The optimized lattice constant of monolayer $C_3N$ in this work is 4.84356 $\mathring{A}$, which agrees well with the experimental value of 4.75 $\mathring{A}$ [1] and other theoretical results of 4.862 $\mathring{A}$ [3].

3. Results and discussions

3.1 Superconductivity in charge doped monolayer $C_3N$

![](./images/812813810107678720_3.jpg)

(c) doped with 0.4e/cell and (d) doped with 0.8 e/cell ($3.92×10^{14}$ /cm²). PDOS of (e) C atoms and (f) N atoms. All Fermi level are set to zero. Unit of DOS is states/eV/cell.

Upon electron doping, conduction bands are partially occupied and the system becomes metallic (Fig.2 (a)). When 5% biaxial tensile strain is applied on 0.2 e/cell doped $C_3N$, the energy of valence band maximum at M lowers and the splitting of bands around $\Gamma$ point increases as can be seen in Fig. 2(b). The evolution of electronic bands with increasing of doping concentration is shown in Fig.2 (c) and (d). Fermi level moves deeper into the conduction bands with the increasing of doping concentrations. As shown in Fig. 2(b-d), the $N(E_f)$ remains almost the same when doping concentration increases from 0.2e/cell to 0.4e/cell. However, when doping reaches 0.8e/cell, $N(E_f)$ increase significantly to 0.81 state/eV/cell, indicating a strengthen of the electron phonon interaction. The maximum doping concentration we adopt here is 0.8 e/cell ($\sim 3.92×10^{14}$/cm²), which is experimentally available. Considering that in a graphene-based field effect transistor, a doping level up to $4 × 10^{14}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ for both electrons and holes has been reached by electrical gating [24]. Besides, a charge density modulation of $\sim 10^{15}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ has been experimentally achieved in 2D systems by using ionic liquid as gate dielectric [25,26]. The orbital composition of the bands was obtained by calculating the PDOS of the C and N atoms. S, p-x, p-y orbitals of C and N atoms are fully

occupied according to Fig. 2(e) and (f). The highest valence band of monolayer $C_3N$ is composed by a mixture of C-p$_z$ and N-p$_z$ orbital, while the lowest conduction band originate from C-p$_z$ orbital.

The calculated phonon spectra of pristine monolayer agrees well with previous reports [3,27]. There is a strong overlap between acoustic modes and low lying optical modes. Therefore, the scattering probability through the channels acoustic + acoustic $\rightarrow$ optical and acoustic + optical $\rightarrow$ optical is high. This leads to a relatively low thermal conductivity, which is confirmed by solving the boltzmann transport equation of phonons [27]. By comparing the phonon spectra of 0.2 e/cell doped $C_3N$ with undoped one in Fig. 3(a), one find two remarkable changes: the redshift of the highest optical C-N stretching modes and the blue shift of the lowest acoustic modes. For the highest optical C-N stretching mode, it involves the in-plane vibration of C and N atoms. The C-C bond length of 0.2e/cell doped monolayer $C_3N$ increases by 0.29% compared with undoped one (while the C-N bond length remains unchanged). This leads to the weakening of the bond strength and thus a red-shift of the phonon frequencies. For the lowest frequency acoustic branch, it is mainly associated with out-of-plane vibrations, which interact strongly with p$_z$ states. Upon electron doping, more

C-$p_z$ and N-$p_z$ orbitals are occupied as can be seen from Fig. 2. (e, f). The phonon frequency is renormalized (increased) due to increased screening. Eliashberg function has a narrow sharp peak locating at the highest optical phonon frequency as shown in Fig. 3(b). In addition, two small peaks exist with lower frequencies. By applying 5% biaxial tensile strain, most phonons exhibit obvious softening, the three peaks of $\alpha^2$F($\omega$) are widened and intensified, this lead to a magnificent increase of the EPC constant by 180% ( from 0.05 to 0.09) as listed in Table 1. However, the EPC is too weak to trigger superconductivity. The extremely small EPC constant can be understood by using equation (1) in Gianni Profeta's work [13]. On the one hand, effective phonon frequency of monolayer $C_3N$ is high, and complete flat structure (symmetry) forbids the coupling between $\pi$-states and the lower frequency out-of-plane modes.

![](./images/812813810107678720_5.jpg)

Fig. 4. Band structure of monolayer $C_3N$ doped with (a) 0.4 h/cell and (b) 0.8 h/cell. The Fermi level is set at zero energy.

We also explore the possibility of hole-doping induced superconductivity. By inspecting bands shown in Fig. 3, even when the doping reaches the maximum doping level of 0.8 hole/cell ($3.92×10^{14}/cm^2$), there is only one Fermi pocket around M point and no significant increase of DOS at Fermi level. Therefore, no additional electron-phonon interaction path are introduced [14] . Importantly, the symmetry constraints still exist. Thus, hole doping is likely to behave similarly with electron doping strategy.

To promote coupling to C/N out-of-plane vibrations, it is necessary introduce interlayer electronic states at the Fermi level which has multiple benefits on $\lambda$ [28], like what happens in graphite intercalated compounds (GICs) [29]. As Li has the smallest atomic radius among commonly used metals, we consider here the Li deposition case. Three configurations of Li deposited monolayer are considered, $Li@\alpha_{in}$ and $Li@\alpha_{out}$ means Li sits over $\alpha$ site and lies in or out of the $C_3N$ plane, $Li@\beta_{out}$ means Li sits over $\beta$ site and lies out of the $C_3N$ plane. Their optimized structural parameters and total energies are listed in Table. 2, the energy of $Li@\alpha_{out}$ configuration is 7.62 eV and 1.22 eV lower than that of $Li@\alpha_{in}$ and $Li@\beta_{out}$ configuration respectively, indicating its potential stability. What's more, the vertical height between Li and carbon plane ($h_{Li-C}$ =1.866 $\mathring{A}$) is lower than out-of-plane $\beta$ configuration ($h_{Li-C}$ =2.075 $\mathring{A}$), and comparable with Li deposited graphene system ($h_{Li-C}$ =1.80 $\mathring{A}$). The larger $T_c$ is indeed obtained when the distance ($h$) between the intercalant atom and the graphite plane is smaller [30].

The absence of imaginary frequencies of phonon dispersion and phonon DOS confirms the dynamical stability of Li deposited $C_3N$. The lattice constant is enlarged by 1.65% when Lithium are deposited on $C_3N$ monolayer. This leads to a weakening of the interatomic strength and most of the vibration frequencies are lowered, especially the high frequency in-plane stretching mode as can be seen in Fig. 6(a). The Eliashberg function $\alpha^2F(\omega)$ plotted in Fig. 6(b), gives the contribution of each frequency to the total EPC constant $\lambda$. The previous high frequency peak around $1500\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ preserves, and the vibrational mode of this region is the in-plane vibration of C and N atoms, leaving the Li atoms stay static. The most significant difference of Eliashberg function $\alpha^2F(\omega)$ between electron doped monolayer and Li-deposited monolayer is a relatively broad high peak located between $250\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and 400 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. We present the vibration mode at zone center ($\Gamma$ point) in this region in Fig. 6 (c). The highest peak of $\alpha^2F(\omega)$ locates at 372 cm-1, it can be seen clearly that this mode is involved with Li atom vibration, confirming the necessity and significance of the out-of-plane Li

atoms. Other related modes are out-of-plane vibration of C and N atoms. The participation of lower energy phonons around $300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is beneficial for enhancing the EPC, which is understandable utilizing Equation (2) and (7). This lead to a increase of EPC constant to 0.86 and the logarithmic averaged phonon frequency $\omega_{\text{log}}$ is lowered to $258.268\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Finally, the $T_{\text{c}}$ is estimated to be as high as 13.03 K, which is higher than Li-deposited graphene (8.1 K) and Ca-deposited graphene (1.4 K). Based on above analysis, we expect that EPC constant and transition temperature can be further increased by tensile strain induced phonon softening [31,32]. Significant coupling of electrons to out-of-plane vibrations is necessary to achieve a prominent enhancement of the total electron–phonon coupling strength.

### 4. Conclusion
In conclusion, by calculating phonon related properties and Eliashberg function with first-principles method, we are able to asses the transition temperature of charge doped and Li-deposited monolayer $\text{C}_{3}\text{N}$. Due to the complete planar structure of $\text{C}_{3}\text{N}$, out-of-plane phonons are prohibited to participate EPC process, the EPC constant for electron doped $\text{C}_{3}\text{N}$ is less that 0.1 and no superconductivity will appear. However, things are different when one Li atom is deposited on each unit cell, an interlayer band between Li and $\text{C}_{3}\text{N}$ layer is formed and the planar symmetry is broken. Therefore, much more low lying optical phonon modes especially the out-of-plane vibration mode participate the EPC process. This result in a boost of EPC constant to 0.86 and the transition temperature is estimated to be 13.03 K according to the isotropical McMillan-Allen-Dynes formula, which is higher than 8.1 K of Li-deposited graphene. The presence of an adatom band at the Fermi level increases the number of carriers, promotes electron coupling to the low energy carbon atoms out-of-plane vibrations, and generates coupling to the adatom vibrations. The $T_{\text{c}}$ is expected to be further enhanced providing the systems keep stable under biaxial tensile strain.
