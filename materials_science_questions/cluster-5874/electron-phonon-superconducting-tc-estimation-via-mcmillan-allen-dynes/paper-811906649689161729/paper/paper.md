A theoretical study of the structural and electronic properties of the beryllium semiboride $\text{Be}_2\text{B}$ and its ternary phases (AlBeB, NaBeB and MgBeB) is presented using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method. Exchange and correlation effects are treated within the Perdew-Wang local density approximation and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzer-hof generalized gradient approximation. Results are given for lattice constant and bulk modulus and its pressure derivative. Band structure, density of states and charge density are also given. We also have examined the possibility of modifying the band structure of $\text{Be}_2\text{B}$ via partial substitution of Al, Mg and Na for Be, in order to predict possible superconductive properties for these compounds.

## 1 Introduction
The discovery of superconductivity in $\text{MgB}_2$ [1] with transition temperature $T_c$ near 39 K is of immense interest not only because of the great potential applications but also because this material seems to be a BCS-type superconductor with much higher $T_c$ than previously reported conventional superconductors. Numerous results for the properties of this material have been reported and reviewed [2]. The boron isotope effect indicates that $\text{MgB}_2$ is a phonon-mediated superconductor [3]. In this regard, several other compounds with the boron-layered crystal structure have been studied [4–8]. Therefore, $\text{MgB}_2$ is only one member of a rich family of diborides. So the other members of this family (Li, Be, Al, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Mo) have become the subject of intensive studies of different groups, and could be some of the best candidates to replace Mg-based materials in applications of superconductivity [9–14]. According to Shein and Ivanovskii [15], possible candidates for a new superconductor should (i) have a fairly high $N(E_\text{F})$, (ii) consist of light sp atoms and (iii) possess a high degree of bond ionicity. In addition, high crystal symmetry is a favourable factor for superconductivity. Among the familiar stable boron compounds with light sp elements is the beryllium semiboride.

$\text{Be}_2\text{B}$ with a cubic crystal lattice ($\text{CaF}_2$ type, space group Fm3m [16]) typical of high-ionic compounds meets the above three conditions. Shein and Ivanovskii [15, 27] revealed several important features of the electronic band structure common to $\text{Be}_2\text{B}$ (antifluorite structure) and superconducting $\text{MgB}_2$. The two borides contain hole states in point $\Gamma$ of the Brillouin zone and have sufficiently high densities of states (DOS) near the Fermi level ($N(E_\text{F})$). In order to understand the hypothetical superconductivity in $\text{Be}_2\text{B}$, it is essential to have an accurate electronic structure description of the normal state of this material. Thus an understanding of how the $\sigma$ band and the $\text{p}_{x,y}$ orbital respond to changes in the chemical environment, lattice constants, etc., could be quite useful in understanding hypothetical superconductivity in $\text{Be}_2\text{B}$ and for synthesizing new materials with $\text{Be}_2\text{B}$-like superconductivity. Fortunately, these changes around the B atom can be mimicked without destroying its two-dimensional character which is essential for superconductivity. For example, the effects of removing one electron or adding one electron around the B atom can be studied by considering NaBeB and AlBeB, respectively. In other words, we examine the possibility of modifying the band structure of $\text{Be}_2\text{B}$ via partial substitution of Al, Mg and Na for Be

Although the hypothetical superconductivity in $Be_2B$ and its ternary phases is fairly well described in the context of the electron–phonon interaction theory, the peculiarities of the electronic spectrum, primarily the composition and structure of the near-Fermi bands, constitute the major factor responsible for the occurrence of this effect. Recently, Shein and Ivanovskii [15, 27] used the scalar relativistic full-potential method of linear muffin-tin orbital within the generalized gradient approximation of exchange-correlation effects (FP LMTO-GGA) to study the structural and elastic characteristics of $Be_2B$ and ternary phases. Except this work, to the best of our knowledge there are no theoretical works exploring the structural and electronic properties of the semiboride $Be_2B$ and its ternary compounds, and no calculations based on the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method have been performed for the electronic properties of this compound to date.

Therefore, the purpose of the work reported in this paper was to study the structural properties of $Be_2B$ and its ternary phases at equilibrium volume as well as under high pressure using the FP-LAPW method. This is followed by a detailed presentation of the band structure, the DOS and the electron charge density. The calculated total energy of solids allowed us to investigate several properties and in particular the equilibrium lattice parameters and bulk modulus and its pressure derivative. The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we describe the calculation procedure. Results and discussion are presented in Section 3. The paper is concluded in Section 4.

2 Calculations The electronic configurations for Be, B, Na, Mg and Al are: $[He]2s^2$, $[He]2s^22p$, $[Ne]3s$, $[Ne]3s^2$ and $[Ne]3s^23p$, respectively. In the following calculation, the subshells of the noble gas cores have been distinguished from the subshells of valence electrons given explicitly. Relativistic effects are taken into account within the scalar-relativistic approximation.

In the present work, we performed first-principles calculations of structural and electronic properties of beryllium semiboride and its ternary alloys (AlBeB, NaBeB and MgBeB), using the Vienna package wien2k [17]. This is an implementation of a hybrid full-potential (linear) augmented plane wave plus local orbitals (L/APW + lo) method within the density functional theory [18, 19]. This new approach is shown to reproduce the accurate results of the LAPW method, but using a smaller basis set size. Due to the smaller basis set and faster matrix set up, APW + lo offers a shorter run time and uses less memory than LAPW. The APW + lo method expands the Kohn–Sham orbitals in atomic-like orbitals inside the atomic muffin-tin (MT) spheres and plane waves in the interstitial region. The details of the method have been described in the Refs. [20–22]. The exchange and correlation effects were treated using the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (PBE-GGA) [23] and the Perdew–Wang local density approximation (LDA) [24]. We expand the basis function up to $R_{MT} \times K_{max}$ equal to 8 ($R_{MT}$ is the plane wave radii, $K_{max}$ is the maximum modulus for the reciprocal lattice vectors). The maximum quantum number $l$ for the wave function expansion inside atomic spheres is confined to $l_{max} = 10$. The MT radii of Be, B, Mg, Na and Al were chosen to be 1.5 bohr, 1.55 bohr, 1.7 bohr, 1.75 bohr, and 1.8 bohr, respectively. Accurate Brillouin zone integrations are performed using the standard special $k$-points technique of Monkhorst and Pack (MP) [25]. For all compounds, we used $8 \times 8 \times 8$ MP meshes. The self-consistent calculations are considered to be converged when the total energy of the system is stable within $10^{-3}$ Ry.

3 Results and discussion

The ground-state value of this parameter as well as the bulk modulus $B$ and its pressure derivative $B'$ are obtained by calculating the total energy for several values of the unit cell volume and fitting them to the Murnaghan equation of state (EOS) [12]. The results obtained are listed in Table 1, in which the available experimental data and results of other calculations are also given. To the best of our knowledge, there are no experimental data in the literature concerning investigation of the structural properties of hypothetical compounds MgBeB and NaBeB.

This result confirms the validity of the empirical formula proposed by Cohen [28]: $B_0 = 17761d^{-3.5}$ (where $d$ is the lattice spacing). The calculated lattice parameters and bulk modulus are in good agreement with the theoretical results of Shein and Ivanovskii [27]. Their work and ours are different, of course, in the method used (FP LMTO-GGA); they had used the Birch equation, whereas we used the Murnaghan equation [12]. The calculated lattice parameter given by LDA is about 3.21% lower than the experimental value for Be₂B, whereas the lattice parameter given by PBE-GGA is in good agreement with the reported experimental value. The computed equilibrium lattice parameters using LDA (and PBE-GGA) functional are 4.51 Å (4.57 Å), 4.89 Å (4.96 Å), 5.14 Å (5.23 Å) and 5.39 Å (5.51 Å) for Be₂B, AlBeB, MgBeB and NaBeB, respectively. The use of GGA in our calculations is more appropriate than LDA and gives a good description of structural properties as lattice parameter and bulk modulus. As we can see in Fig. 2, the lattice parameter of the ternary phases decreases on going from NaBeB to AlBeB, in agreement with expectations based on the ionic radii of the atoms that replace beryllium: 1.89 Å (Na) > 1.60 Å (Mg) > 1.43 Å (Al). Thus replacing Be with X (X = Na, Mg and Al) increases the unit cell volume: our calculated volumes of AlBeB (122.024 Å³), MgBeB (143.05 Å³) and NaBeB (167.28 Å³) are 25.35%, 46.96% and 71.85% larger than that of Be₂B (97.35 Å³). However, the bulk modulus is practically identical for Be₂B and AlBeB, the difference is less 6 GPa, i.e. about 3.52%, while it undergoes an abrupt decrease of about 33.64% and 58.43% in MgBeB and NaBeB, respectively. It is obvious from this that the insertion of Al affects slightly the structural properties; in other words, it conserves the stiffening of the lattice. The reverse situation is observed for the substitution of Mg and Na, which is detrimental to the cohesion of the crystal and consequently causes softening of the lattice. Thus, by doping Be sublattice and changing the lattice parameter, the two-dimensional $\sigma$ bands $(2p_{x,y})$ can be diminished or enhanced, and this will have important consequences for the DOS near the Fermi level, as will be discussed in the following section.

### 3.2 Electronic properties
In Fig. 3 we present the total DOS for Be₂B, NaBeB, MgBeB and AlBeB as obtained by means of GGA. The gross features of the DOS of the four compounds are similar if one takes into account the differences in the total number of valence electrons. The bottom of the band is the deepest for AlBeB which has a total of eight valence electrons and shallowest for NaBeB with only six valence electrons. However, the isoelectronic structures Be₂B and MgBeB show substantial differences in their DOS near the bottom of the band. This is due to the fact that the increased lattice constant leads to reduced repulsion which pushes the s- and p-electrons at the Be site upwards and, at the same time, increases the DOS for B s- and p-electrons in the middle of the band. In the case of AlBeB, we find B p-band in the vicinity of the Fermi level $E_F$.

The dependence of the DOS on volume is almost entirely due to the change in the width and position of a considerable van Hove peak (~2 eV below the Fermi level), which originates from a saddle point in the two-dimensional $\sigma$ bands $(2p_{x,y})$ at the W point. As can be seen from Table 2, there is a substantial increase in the contributions of the B 2p states to $N(E_F)$: NaBeB (1.097 states/eV) > MgBeB (0.732) > Be₂B (0.605). The valence bands of $Be_2B$ and its related compounds consist of two subbands separated by a pseudogap. The energetically low-lying band is derived from the Be 2s, B 2s states for $Be_2B$, and the Be 2s, B 2s and X 3s states (X = Al, Mg, and Na) for AlBeB, MgBeB and NaBeB, respectively. The upper subbands are derived mainly from the B 2p states except in the case of AlBeB where there is in addition Al 3p states. Indeed, the valence bandwidth de- creases as we go from $Be_2B$ to NaBeB: it is about 13.99 eV, 13.06 eV, 11.28 eV and 9.31 eV for $Be_2B$, AlBeB, MgBeB and NaBeB, respectively. This decrease looks consistent with the variation of the lattice parameter, arising from the effect of X atom substitution (see Table 1).

The difference between the valence bandwidths is al- most constant if we go along the same row of the periodic table, it is about 1.97 eV and 178 eV, which is in good agreement with the increase in the number of valenc elec- trons $N_v$: $6e^-$ (NaBeB), $7e^-$ (MgBeB) and $8e^-$ (AlBeB). This difference is substantially greater than that for Be and Mg which are in the same column of the periodic table (isoelectronic), it is about 2.71 eV. From our point of view, this difference can be attributed to the enhanced repulsion of Be 2s and B 2p states owing to the fact that they have close energies. This is not the case for MgBeB where Mg 3s and B 2p states are more or less distant.

The charge densities shown in Fig. 6 indicate a covalent character of the chemical bond accompanied by a charge transfer from one atom to the other. The boron compounds are strongly covalent since the electronegativity [32] of the Na (0.93), Mg (1.31), Be (1.57) and Al (1.61) anions is smaller than that of the B (2.04) cation. However, the boron is the most electronegative element and will thus attract electrons towards it. To illustrate the degree of covalency in the boron compounds, Fig. 7 shows typical features of the covalently bonded compounds. The bond charge is large and located close to halfway between the two nearest atoms for which the isoelectronic curves are symmetric. Indeed, B$-$B, Be$-$B and Be$-$Al covalent bonds hold a considerable amount of charge. On the other hand, the net charge transfer from Mg to Be and Na to Be, which indicates the degree of ionicity of the bonding, is large as is suggested by Fig. 7c. This charge transfer increases as the difference in the electronegativity values of the two atoms increases. Thus, the most pronounced effect of the substitution of Na and Mg is the almost absence of the Mg$-$Be and Na$-$Be bonds.

The large bulk modulus of doped phases can be explained, as discussed above, by a weak covalent bonding in them. The variation of bulk modulus follows the strengthening of the covalent bonding between X atom and boron, which is weakened as we go from AlBeB to NaBeB.

Finally, as expected, the variation both in the number of electrons per cell and in the volume has a profound effect on the DOS near the Fermi level. We note that the larger volume gives the $\sigma$ bands more two-dimensional character, and the lower bond in the electronic charge density results in both a smaller bandwidth and a decrease in the bulk modulus.

## 4 Conclusions
In this work we have presented the results of the structural and electronic properties of $\mathrm{Be}_{2}\mathrm{B}$ and its ternary phases using first-principles FP-LAPW calculations. We have analysed our results in terms of the lattice parameters, bulk moduli with respect to volume, DOS around $E_{\mathrm{F}}$, band structures and electronic charge densities. The important conclusions arrived at are the following.

The decrease in bandwidth with increasing volume reduces the separation between the peak and the Fermi level, driving up the DOS from AlBeB to NaBeB. As a result of the apparent increase in $N(E_{\mathrm{F}})$ for lower densities (i.e. larger volumes), the superconducting transition temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ should increase.

A general trend is clear: at lower volumes ($\mathrm{Be}_{2}\mathrm{B}$) $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ decreases, while as volume increases, in the case of MgBeB and NaBeB, $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ increases, in accordance with the DOS at the Fermi level. Indeed, according to our prediction, MgBeB was found to be superconducting with moderate $T_{\mathrm{c}}=12$ K ($\mathrm{AlB}_{2}$ structure) [29]. So NaBeB is likely to show superconductivity with enhanced $T_{\mathrm{c}}$. From the DOS of AlBeB, it is evident that it will not be superconducting; the $\mathrm{p}_{x,y}$ bands are completely filled. This is in agreement with the theoretical study performed by Mehl et al. [29] and experimental observations [30], which asserts that Al substitution destroys superconductivity.

Finally, the peculiarities of the electronic spectrum, primarily the composition and structure of the near-Fermi

bands, constitute the major factor responsible for the formation of the effect of superconductivity, which is fairly well described in the context of the electron–phonon interaction theory. Therefore, such studies are currently underway.
