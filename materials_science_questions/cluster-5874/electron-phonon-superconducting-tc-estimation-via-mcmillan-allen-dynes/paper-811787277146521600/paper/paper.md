## ABSTRACT

In the isoelectronic compounds $V_3$Ni, $V_3$Pd and $V_3$Pt the chemical bonding is ruled by the $\sigma$-type interactions of the V d-orbitals. It is observed that there is a tendency to form degenerate bands at the X and M points in the irreducible part of the Brillouin zone. An estimation of the electron-phonon coupling constant $\lambda$ yields 0.48 for $V_3$Ni, 0.42 for $V_3$Pd and 0.76 for $V_3$Pt. From Ni to Pt the unsystematic behavior experimentally observed for the unit cell volume arises from a competition mechanism between strengthening of interactions of bonding states and increasing atomic volumes.

## 1. Introduction

The A15 intermetallic compounds ($Cr_3$O-type structure) are among the materials with the highest superconducting transition temperatures and have been intensively investigated in the past decades [1–5], and since the earliest studies on the electronic structure of $A_3$B materials the V-based compounds have been the focus of attention [6–11]. Superconductive behavior has been reported in $V_3$Ni, $V_3$Pd, $V_3$Pt [12]. Among these $V_3$Pd has an anomalously low $T_c$ (0.08 K), whereas electronic specific heat data show a rather significant electronic specific heat coefficient $\gamma$ [13]. The electronic structure of $V_3$Pt has been studied by photoemission (XPS and UPS) and susceptibility measurements as well as by NMR and specific heat studies [14]; the heat capacity of $V_3$Pt has been also experimentally studied by Junod et al. [15], and its electronic structure has been investigated by Jarlborg et al. [9]. It has been verified that, for isolated Pt atoms in a vanadium environment, the Pt d-states are predominantly located at the bottom of the valence band. The density of states (DOS) for the V atom in $V_3$Pt is similar to the one for the pure element, at least for the upper part of the valence band, whereas particularly for the Pt atom, the DOS is drastically modified. $V_3$Ni and $V_3$Pd seemingly have not attracted much attention and no report has been found concerning electronic structure studies on these compounds. In this contribution a comparative study is presented in first-principles calculations of the band structure and Fermi surfaces of $V_3$Ni, $V_3$Pd and $V_3$Pt.

## 2. Method

Scalar relativistic calculations were performed with the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW) method [16] with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (1996) parametrization for the exchange-correlation potential [17]. Basis functions are expanded in combinations of spherical harmonic functions inside non-overlapping Muffin-Tin (MT) spheres surrounding the atomic sites and in a Fourier series in the interstitial region. Within the MT-spheres the l-expansion of the non-spherical potential and charge density is carried out up to $l_{\text{max}} = 10$. The plane-wave cut-off is $R_{\text{MT}}K_{\text{max}} = 9.6000$ $k$-points were used in the integration mesh within the Brillouin zone. The energy criterion for convergence was chosen to be 0.01 mRy. Plane waves with reciprocal lattice vectors up to $G_{\text{max}} = 14$ were included in the interstitial region. In the $Cr_3$O-type structure (space group $Pm3n$) of $V_3$B compounds the unit cell consists of V atoms in pairs on the cube faces (6e sites) at positions $\frac{1}{4},0,\frac{1}{2};\frac{3}{4},0,\frac{1}{2};\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4},0;\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4},0;0,\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4};0,\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4}$, and two equivalent B sites (2a sites) with atomic positions $\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}$ and 0,0,0. Hence, the V atoms are arranged along lines or chains in three orthogonal sets parallel to the unit cell edges.

## 4. Discussion

In Fig. 4 the center of gravity for d-bands from the B atoms is seen well below $\epsilon_F$. The d-orbitals of V are more spatially localized in real space which means that nearest-neighbor interactions plays a more significant role in the chemical bonding, which involves primarily the V orbitals of $d(\pi)$ symmetry. Despite the strong admixture of the $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ symmetry orbitals for the B atoms the chemical bonding in these compounds thus is found to be determined mainly by the interaction between $\pi$-orbitals of the V atoms. In Fig. 4, a tendency to form a gap for the d-states of V is observed. At the Fermi energy there is a preponderance of $V-\delta_2$ orbitals, which becomes even more important particularly for $V_3Pd$. The bonding states of V atoms are located within an energy window of $\approx$0.4 Ry below $\epsilon_F$. The Ni d-states are found predominantly within 0.15-0.30 Ry below $\epsilon_F$; for both Pd and Pt these are found between 0.3 and 0.40 Ry from $\epsilon_F$. From $V_3Ni$ to $V_3Pt$ the d-states from B sites are seen moving down to lower energies, whereas the V d-states accumulate near $\epsilon_F$.

It can be seen in Fig. 1 that a strong admixture of V-Ni d-states occurs along various directions in the Brillouin zone, an effect which is stronger within about 0.15-0.30 Ry from $\epsilon_F$. In the band structure plots for $V_3Pd$ and $V_3Pt$ the overlap between metal and metal d-states is seen to occur mostly around $M$ points, within about the same energy range. In $V_3Ni$ a stronger hybridization between d-states from both metals accounts for the observed strong resonance in the DOS. The sharp peak in the DOS seen in Figs. 1-3 arises from flat d-bands of V atoms near the $\Gamma$ point, along the $\Gamma-R$, $\Gamma-M$ and $\Gamma-X$ directions. From the band structure plots it is also seen that the flatter character of the bands in these compounds becomes more and more accentuated from $V_3Ni$ to $V_3Pt$, being that, in the former, the flattest portion of the subbands is seen around $M$ points in the IBZ, where in the others this feature is observed also at the zone center.

In Table 1 are listed the integrated orbital charges within the (metals) atomic spheres. From $V_3Ni$ to $V_3Pd$ one sees that the occupation of s-, p- and d-orbitals are practically the same for the V atom in these compounds. From $V_3Pd$ to $V_3Pt$ there is an increase in number of valence electrons for the V atom, which arises from an increase in the occupation of s, p and d bonding orbitals. This leads to a slight strengthening of the chemical bonding between the d-d orbitals from both metals, which thus is responsible for the observed behavior of the unit cell volume in this series. The Pt atom has nearly the same number of valence electrons as Pd, although possessing a lesser occupation of d-states.

The electronic heat-capacity coefficient $\gamma$ is given by
$$
\gamma=\frac{1}{3} \pi^{2} k_{B}^{2} N\left(\epsilon_{F}\right)(1+\lambda), \tag{1}
$$
where $N(\epsilon_F)$ is the DOS at Fermi level and $\lambda$ is the effective electron-phonon coupling constant, which has contributions from the electron-phonon coupling and spin fluctuations, i.e.,
$$
\lambda=\lambda_{e-p h}+\lambda_{s f}.
$$

Calculated $\lambda$ values for the $A_3B$ compounds have been reported by Jarlborg et al. [9], which yields 0.68 for $V_3Pt$. However, to the best of our knowledge there is no report of this quantity for both $V_3Ni$ and $V_3Pd$. Experimental results for the critical temperatures $T_c$ of this series are: 0.57 K ($V_3Ni$), 0.08 K ($V_3Pd$), 2.7 K ($V_3Pt$) [2]. Hence, as an attempt to estimate $\lambda$ values for these compounds, we adopted the McMillan formula [21] for the superconducting transition temperature $T_c$ in this family, which is given by
$$
T_{c}=\frac{\omega_{\log }}{1.2} \exp \left(-\frac{1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^{*}(1+0.62 \lambda)}\right), \tag{2}
$$
where [9]
$$
\mu^{*}=\frac{0.26 N\left(\epsilon_{F}\right)}{\left(1+N\left(\epsilon_{F}\right)\right)},
$$
being $N(\epsilon_F)$ given in states/eV.atom, and $\omega_{\log}$ is the averaged phonon frequency. Thus, by using our calculated $N(\epsilon_F)$ and the known experimental $T_c$ for the studied compounds we made a plot $\omega_{\log}$ against $\lambda$. From this, if one takes the calculated $\omega_{\log}$ values as reported by Jarlborg et al. [9] for $V_3Pt$ ($\omega_{\log}=180$), one obtains $\lambda=0.66$, which is in very good agreement with the reported value [9]. It is noteworthy that for several $A_3B$ compounds the reported $\omega_{\log}$ values are within the range $150<\omega_{\log}<200$. The variation of $\lambda$ in this range of $\omega_{\log}$ is very small for the studied compounds. From this, our estimates for $\lambda$ are 0.42 and 0.48, for $V_3Pd$ and $V_3Ni$, respectively.

In Table 1 are listed the available experimental data for the electronic specific heat coefficient $\gamma$ and the calculated values, with and without the electron-phonon coupling term $\lambda$. For $V_3Pt$ the calculated value obtained by including the estimated $\lambda$ agrees well with the experimental. For $V_3Pd$ the calculated $\gamma$ is higher than the reported experimental value [13]. This discrepancy clearly comes from a rather high $N(\epsilon_F)$ value at $\epsilon_F$ observed for $V_3Pd$. Conversely to the $V_3Pt$, where $N(\epsilon_F)$ changes less than 10% for variations of $\approx$1 mRy in the localization of the Fermi Level, in $V_3Pd$ and $V_3Ni$, $N(\epsilon_F)$ changes by $\approx$25% if the Fermi level is shifted by only 1 mRy (a highly accurate calculation of the Fermi energy is not an easy task). For $V_3Pd$ a better agreement between experiment and theory is achieved if $N(\epsilon_F)$ is reduced by an amount of approximately 25%, since this reduction leaves the calculated $\lambda$ practically unchanged. For $V_3Ni$ there is no reported experimental value for $\gamma$.

From the 3D plots of the FS shown in Fig. 5 it can be seen that the topological changes in the FS for electrons are reflecting mainly changes in the contributions from the V atoms. The protuberances towards the zone center observed in the contribution of electrons from band 26 in $V_3Pd$ are formed at the expenses of higher energy electrons from band 27. This seems to be a common feature in these compounds, i.e., the emptying of higher energy bands leading to an increase in the population of lower energy bands close to $\epsilon_F$, which increases the connectivity of the FS, and which allows also the formation of open orbits.

In order to make an assessment of the effect on the structure of the FS by changing the B atom in these compounds we performed calculations for the hypothetical compound $V_3V$. After minimizing the total energy against the cell volume we draw 3D plots of the FS from the three bands crossing $\epsilon_F$; all the FS show disconnected structures at the cube corners around $R$ points of the IBZ. This reveals how antibonding bands from the B atoms are directly involved in the formation of hole structures along the cube edges, whereas the V atoms are the major responsible for the observed structure of the electrons sheets (bonding charge) around $X$ points. Furthermore, as a result of relativity, which causes the contraction and lowering of energy of the B(s, p) bands, the outer d-electrons

of the B atoms experience lesser binding and consequently larger spatial extent, which leads to the accumulation of antibonding charge, thus increasing the connectivity of the FS in the IBZ.

Finally, from the band structure plots it is seen that the features of the d-DOS of both V and B atoms in these compounds are very distinct and show particular trends from $V_3Ni$ to $V_3Pt$. The unsystematic behavior experimentally observed for the cell volume in these compounds reflects the changes occurring in the chemical bonding which arises from the decreasing occupation of energy bands near the Fermi level and the emptying of higher energy states. The shifting of $\pi$-type bonding states, going down in energy from Ni to Pt, is indicative of strengthening of interactions, which tends to shrink the lattice, but which in addition to a volume effect (increasing atomic volume) leads to the observed behavior for the unit cell volume. In $V_3Ni$ and $V_3Pd$ the low $T_c$ is due to weak electron-phonon coupling.
