# LaPt₂B₂C: A conventional borocarbide superconductor

David J. Singh
Complex Systems Theory Branch, Naval Research Laboratory, Washington, D.C. 20375
(Received 16 May 1994)

Local-density-approximation electronic-structure calculations for the recently discovered LaPt₂B₂C superconductor are reported. The calculations show, as in LuNi₂B₂C, that despite the quasi-two- dimensional crystal structure, the electronic structure in the vicinity of the Fermi energy $(E_{F})$ is quite three dimensional, with substantial contributions from all atoms. In this compound, $E_{F}$ falls on the low-energy side of a peak in the electronic density of states, suggesting that $T_{c}$ may be increased by dop ing to a higher electron count. The value of the density of states at $E_{F}$ is found to be substantially small er than that of LuNi₂B₂C in agreement with experiment.

## INTRODUCTION

The recent discovery of superconductivity with critical temperatures up to 23 K in Ni-, Pd-, and Pt-containing rare-earth borocarbides¹⁻⁴ has renewed interest in non- cuprate superconductivity. Further, these observations have raised questions about our understanding of the ori- gins of superconductivity, since it is rare to find super- conductors containing large amounts of Ni; PdHₓ is one of the few Pd-based superconductors. The crystal struc- tures of LuNi₂B₂C with a superconducting transition temperature, $T_{c}$ of 16.6 K and LaPt₂B₂C with $T_{c}$ of 11 K, have been determined to consist of two-dimensional transition-metal boride layers, stacked in the $c$ direction with intervening $C$ and rare-earth atoms.⁴,⁵ This layering is somewhat reminiscent of the layered high-$T_{c}$ cuprate superconductors.

Local-density-approximation (LDA) electronic- structure calculations for LuNi₂B₂C (Refs. 6 and 7) show that despite the layered crystal structure, the electronic structure near the Fermi energy $E_{F}$ is essentially three di mensional and nearly isotropic. Additionally, $E_{F}$ occurs near a substantial peak in the electronic density of states (DOS). Electron-phonon calculations⁶ indicate the pres- ence of strong electron-phonon coupling consistent with the observed value of $T_{c}$ in this material, leading to the conclusion that despite its layered structure and chemical composition, the superconductivity of LuNi₂B₂C may be understood in terms of conventional strong-coupling electron-phonon theory, similar to earlier families of ni- tride and carbide superconductors.⁸ However, in the ab- sence of information regarding the phonon spectrum it was not possible to directly calculate $T_{c}$, which if in agreement with experiment would be a clear demonstra- tion of the electron-phonon mechanism.

The crystal structures, physical properties, and ion valences of LaPt₂B₂C and LuNi₂B₂C are similar suggest- ing that the superconductivity of these materials has similar origins as well. There are, however, some differences between these materials. For example, the $a$- axis lattice parameter is significantly larger in the former material, presumably to accommodate the larger $5d$ transition-metal ion and providing room for the replace- ment of Lu by La. On the other hand, the $c$-axis lattice parameter is not increased correspondingly. This results in a less perfect tetrahedral arrangement of the B atoms around the transition-metal site in LaPt₂B₂C (note that the B height is constrained by short bond lengths in B-C- B $c$-direction chains).

Here electronic structure calculations for LaPt₂B₂C are reported and contrasted with results for LuNi₂B₂C. As expected, a qualitatively similar three-dimensional elec- tronic structure is found with substantial contributions from all sites near $E_{F}$. However, the positioning of $E_{F}$ is found to be different leading to a reduction in the DOS at $E_{F}, N(E_{F})$. This may be at least partially responsible for the reduced $T_{c}$ in LaPt₂B₂C.

## METHOD

The present calculations were performed using a local orbital extension⁹ of the general potential linearized aug- mented plane wave (LAPW) method.¹⁰,¹¹ Both the valence and core states were treated self-consistently, the core states relativistically in a spherical approximation, and the valence states scalar relativistically but without shape approximations. No shape approximations were imposed on the charge density or potential. These LDA calculations, performed at the experimental crystal struc- ture,⁴ used the Hedin-Lundqvist functional.¹² Self- consistency was obtained with a set of 84 special $k$ points¹³ in the irreducible wedge of the tetragonal Bril- louin zone (IBZ). A well-converged basis consisting of approximately 1190 functions was employed with LAPW sphere radii of 2.5, 2.4, 1.35, and 1.28 a.u. for La, Pt, B, and C, respectively. The electronic DOS and its projec- tions were determined from calculations at 126 uniformly distributed $\mathbf{k}$ points in the IBZ.

Despite the larger La atom relative to Lu, no significant hybridization involving La $f$ orbitals is seen. The band structure is qualitatively similar to that obtained for $LuNi_2B_2C,^{6,7}$ the main differences being a larger transition-metal bandwidth, as expected for replacement of a $3d$ by a $5d$ atom, and in the structure around $E_F$. As in $LuNi_2B_2C$ the electronic structure around $E_F$ is quite three dimensional, and by no means quasi two dimensional. The differences between the two materials around $E_F$ are expected to be important for superconductivity. They arise from a larger splitting in $LaPt_2B_2C$ between the strongly hybridized electronic states that give rise to a peak near $E_F$ in these two materials and the main part of the transition-metal $d$ bands. This increased covalent splitting is likely a result of the more extended $5d$ orbitals in Pt and La relative to the Ni and Lu $d$ orbitals. This increased splitting causes the Fermi energy to occur 0.4 eV below the DOS maximum in $LaPt_2B_2C$ rather than at the maximum as in isoelectronic $LuNi_2B_2C$. Corresponding to this upward shift in the split-off peak relative to $E_F$ there is greater C $p$ and rare-earth $d$ character below $E_F$. As a result $N(E_F)$ is reduced to 33.9 (Ry formula unit) $^{-1}$ as compared to 65.3 (Ry formula unit) $^{-1}$ in $LuNi_2B_2C,^6$ although the rare-earth $d$ contributions to $N(E_F)$ are similar in the two materials. The lower $N(E_F)$ is qualitatively in accord with experimental data, $^4$ which indicates a lower value in $LaPt_2B_2C$, although the reduction found here is smaller than that observed.

Since increased values of $N(E_F)$ generally lead to higher values of $T_c$ in conventional transition-metal carbide and nitride superconductors, it may be expected that substitutions that increase the electron count will increase $T_c$ in $LaPt_2B_2C$. Possibilities include partial substitution of N for C and the addition of an electron donor to the material. This latter possibility seems particularly likely, since the relatively open crystal structure is known to accommodate at least 10% additional Pt. (N.B. Addition of 10% Pt leads to a substantial reduction in $T_c.^4$) This suggests the possibility that the lattice may accommodate other atoms instead, which could serve as dopants. Within a rigid-band picture, the addition of 1.2 electrons would be required to shift $E_F$ to the peak in the DOS.

### CONCLUSIONS

Electronic-structure calculations have been performed for $LaPt_2B_2C$, and compared with earlier calculations for $LuNi_2B_2C$. The general features of the electronic structure are found to be similar, although the increased radii of the La and Pt $d$ orbitals relative to Lu and Ni lead to broader bands and increased covalent splittings. As a result $E_F$ falls below rather than at the split-off peak in the DOS, which is characteristic of these materials. I suggest that increasing the electron count in $LaPt_2B_2C$ may lead to higher values of $T_c$.
