# Fully relativistic band structure of SmTe

A Caldast, C A Taft† and H N Nazareno‡
† Centro Brasileiro de Pesquisas Físicas, Rua Dr Xavier Sigaud, 150, Urca 22290, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
‡ Departamento de Física, Universidade de Brasília, 70910, Brasilia DF, Brazil

Received 16 October 1985, in final form 24 December 1985

Abstract. We report the results of a fully relativistic band-structure calculation made using the KKR method for SmTe with the NaCl structure in the low-pressure phase. The exchange parameter was obtained using the free-electron approximation scaled by the exchange parameter $\alpha$. We have solved the full Dirac equation for the interior of the muffin-tin spheres and the energies were evaluated at several symmetry points and other general points in the $\frac{1}{48}$ wedge of the FCC Brillouin zone. The energy band indicates a gap throughout the whole BZ with excited f-type bands located below the d-type conduction band, and the indirect band gap determined by the band structure for the $\Gamma$ to X direction to be 0.67 eV in good agreement with the experimental value of 0.63 eV.

## 1. Introduction

SmTe belongs to the class of NaCl-structured monochalcogenides (S, Se, Te) of the rare-earth elements, which display an exceedingly rich diversity of transport and magnetic properties representing just about every range of electrical conductivity, from good metals to wide-gap semiconductors. The differences and similarities in the physical properties of this class of rare-earth compounds should be directly related to the compounds' electronic band structure.

The formulation of a definite theory of intermediate valence, which is exhibited by the samarium monochalcogenides, has still not been achieved, although a great deal of advance has been made in this field in recent years. With the objective of obtaining a better understanding of the electronic structure of such materials we have performed, as a starting point, a fully relativistic band-structure calculation.

It has been noted that relativistic effects such as spin-orbit coupling, and mass-velocity and Darwin corrections should not be neglected in the investigation of the band structure of the heavy rare-earth systems (Nazareno *et al* 1983).

We report in this paper results of an *ab initio* relativistic band-structure calculation (Korringa 1947, Kohn and Rostoker 1954, Onodera and Okasaki 1966, Nazareno 1980) performed by the KKR method for SmTe in the low-pressure phase. However, since the electronic structure is obtained from a one-particle theory we should be aware of the natural limitations of the results presented.

### 2. Calculation procedure and discussion of results

The lattice structure of ordered SmTe is of the NaCl type. The corresponding Brillouin zone is FCC with two atoms per unit cell. We have constructed the 'muffin-tin' potential scaled by the exchange parameter $\alpha = 0.71$ and superimposed atomic charge densities obtained from our self-consistent HFD Slater calculation from Sm and Te atoms. The lattice parameter was taken to be 12.46 au for SmTe. The $\alpha$-expansion was performed over 14 shells of neighbours. The muffin-tin radii $R_i$ were determined from the intersections of the potential curves $V_i(|R - R_i|)$ centred around the $i$th atom in the unit cell. In figure 1 we show the crystal potential along the (100) directions. The nearest-neighbour distance was $R_{\text{SmTe}} = 6.23$ au. The constant potential outside the spheres was taken to be $V = -0.39$ Ryd and the energies were referred to a zero at this potential. The muffin-tin potential is stronger in the interior of the Sm sphere, which allows a certain amount of charger transfer into the Sm positions. We have not taken this effect into account in the present calculations. The trial wavefunction in the interior of the spheres consists of an expansion in terms of the bi-spinors of the Dirac central-field problem (Rose 1961, Nazareno *et al* 1983).

In solving the energy band problem we have followed the formalism of Onodera and Okasaki (1966). We have solved the secular KKR problem for each irreducible representation of the double group of the wave-vectors. We have included terms up to $l = 3$ since our interest is to describe the s, p, d and f atomic levels while they are going into the low-lying excited band states. This cut-off of the wavefunction expansion did not alter the desired accuracy of $10^{-3}$ Ryd in most of the Brillouin zone.

The electronic band structure of SmTe was calculated at several points of high symmetry and at other general points of the $\frac{1}{48}$ wedge of the FCC Brillouin zone. The resulting bands are shown in figure 2.

Below the valence band we find mainly s-type states derived from the Sm and Te atoms. Next there are the p states coming from the Te as well as from the Sm atoms going into the low-lying valence bands, starting at $\Gamma^{7-}$ and $\Gamma^{8-}$ at the centre of the BZ. The conduction bands are primarily composed of the Sm 5d-6s hybridised states. The

lowest-lying conduction band is d-like, occurring at $X^{7+}$. We also find p-f admixtures and s-like states in the excited states of the conduction band.

The top of the f band occurs at the zone centre at $\Gamma^{8-}$ at 0.30 Ryd. The direct gap between the f state $(X^{7-})$ and the conduction band $(X^{7+})$ is 1.0 eV. The indirect band gap between the states $(X^{7+}-\Gamma^{8-})$ is 0.67 eV in good agreement with the experimental value of 0.63 eV.

As previously emphasised we suggest that relativistic effects (Nazareno et al 1983) such as spin-orbit coupling, and mass-velocity and Darwin corrections should be con- sidered in calculating the band structure of rare-earth systems although other effects, such as modification of the electronic structure and exchange potential, many-body corrections along the lines of the non-degenerate Hubbard method (Hubbard 1964) and multiplet structure of excited configurations, may also affect these results.

At this point of the discussion we should comment on self-consistency. We are aware of the importance of a self-consistent calculation when dealing with the electronic structure of f-electron systems. Such a calculation was done for SmS by Strange (1984) using the local-density-functional model which showed good agreement with exper- imental results on the dielectric function, for instance. The bands described by Strange show a general resemblance to those found in our previous work in SmS (Nazareno et al 1983).

The narrow bands arising from the atomic $J=\frac{5}{2}$ and $J=\frac{7}{2}$ levels are located near the Fermi energy. The self-consistency should not substantially alter our results, provided we start with a charge density close to the correct one.

Most of the models proposed for interpreting the peculiar characteristics of the intermediate-valence materials are based on an electronic structure similar to the one obtained in this paper, which essentially consists of a well localised narrow 'f' band just before the conduction band (Ramirez et al 1970, Jefferson and Stevens 1978, Allen et al 1978, Robinson 1979, Iglesias-Sicardi and Aveline 1980, Mors 1985).

In addition, calculations under way on the collapsed metallic phase give preliminary evidence that the new phase arises because the 'f' localised band merges into the conduction band near point X in the BZ thus supporting the idea that pressure brings

about a semiconductor-metallic transition in this material (Coqblin and Blandin 1968, Davis 1977).

It is thus not surprising that such models can interpret well the experimental transport and magnetic properties.
