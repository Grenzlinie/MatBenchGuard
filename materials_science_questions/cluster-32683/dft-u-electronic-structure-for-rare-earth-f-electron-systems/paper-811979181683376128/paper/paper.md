# On the Influence of Relativistic Effects on the Electronic Structure of Luthecium

By

V.V. NEMOSHKALENKO (a), VL.N. ANTONOV (a), V.N. ANTONOV (a),
P. ZIESCHE (b), and H. WONN (b)

The purpose of this note is to study the influence of relativistic effects on the partial densities of states of 5d-transition metals. We studied the lightest of these metals, luthecium, as a first example. While relativistic band structures have been calculated already, relativistic partial densities of states are considered for the first time.

The calculations were carried out by means of the relativistic augmented-plane-wave (RAPW) method /1/. The crystal potential was determined in the scheme proposed by Mattheiss /2/ by using the atomic densities calculated in the Dirac-Hartree-Fock-Slater approximation /3/ for the $5d^16s^2$ configuration. An exchange potential was determined in the Slater approximation /4/ with $\alpha=1$. For the crystal potential thus obtained the Dirac linear differential equation system was solved and the radial wave functions, $f_{\varkappa}(r, E)$ and $g_{\varkappa}(r, E)$ up to $\varkappa=10$ were determined. Luthecium has the h.c.p. crystal lattice structure. The order of the secular equation used here, has been allowed to obtain the eigenvalues in the filled energy valence bands with an accuracy of 1 mRyd. The total and partial densities of states were calculated by means of a linear interpolating scheme /5/ with $\approx 3.2x10^5 \vec{K}$-points in the Brillouin zone. The band structure and the total density of states obtained by us agree quite well with the results of /6/.

Three calculations of the band structure, the total and partial densities of states for the same crystal potential of luthecium were carried out: A non-relativistic calculation, a relativistic one without spin-orbit coupling, and a full relativistic calculation.
