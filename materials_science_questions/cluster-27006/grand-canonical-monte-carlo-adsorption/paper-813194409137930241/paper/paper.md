# A flexible porous Cu(II) bis-imidazolate framework with ultrahigh concentration of active sites for efficient and recyclable CO₂ capture

Dong-Dong Zhou, Chun-Ting He, Pei-Qin Liao, Wei Xue, Wei-Xiong Zhang, Hao-Long Zhou, Jie-Peng Zhang* and Xiao-Ming Chen

5 Received (in XXX, XXX) Xth XXXXXXXXX 200X, Accepted Xth XXXXXXXXX 200X
DOI: 10.1039/b000000x

By virture of planar $Cu_{2}(\mu -OH)_{2}^{2+}$ cluster units, a flexible porous metal azoalte framework not only adsorbs large amounts of CO₂ (19.6 wt% or 266 g L⁻¹ at 298 K and 1 atm)
10 with a high zero-coverage adsorption enthalpy (-47 kJ mol⁻¹), but also desorbs fastly at very low temperatures.

The CO₂ capture and separation are of great significance in industrial and environmental aspects.¹ Traditionally, CO₂ is chemically absorbed by aqueous alkanolamine, which is very
15 powerful but costs much energy during the regeneration processes and has corrosive problems for the equipments. Physical adsorption by porous materials are expected to be an alternative strategy, although their CO₂ affinities are much weaker than the chemical absorbents.² Nevertheless, some porous
20 materials have achieved very high CO₂ uptake at ambient conditions. It should be noted that, the volumetric uptakes should be more important than the gravimetric uptakes for evaluation of the CO₂ capture performances of adsorbents, since the fixed-bed equipments for CO₂ capture and separation do not need to move
25 during operation.²

Porous coordination polymers (PCPs) are extensively studied for CO₂ capture due to their advantages such as high porosity, modifiable framework and pore surface, and flexible structures.³ Various types of active sites⁴ can be introduced onto the pore
30 surface to improve the CO₂ affinities. For example, PCPs functionalized by alkylamine active sites can achieve strong CO₂ binding comparable to that of aqueous alkanolamine.⁵ Many types of open metal sites (OMSs)⁶ can also greatly improve the CO₂ capture ability, although very strong active sites are
35 concerned for giving desorption difficulty and high energy cost. On the other hand, the CO₂ adsorption capacity at ambient conditions is usually limited by the relatively low concentration of these strongly active sites (OMS < 5 mol L⁻¹; Lewis basic site < 10 mol L⁻¹).⁷
40 To fabricate active sites with suitable CO₂ affinity and higher concentration, Cu(II) ion should be a good candidate, because it has moderate activity (much weaker than alkylamines and common metal ions, and comparable to arylamines and azolates) and generally (square-planar coordination geometry) offers two
45 potential coordination sites for guest molecules. Here, we report a novel flexible PCP with ultrahigh concentration of open Cu(II) sites, which showed not only high adsorption affinity and uptake, but also easy desorption for CO₂ capture.

Room-temperature diffusion reaction between
50 benzodiimidazole (H₂bdim) and [Cu(NH₃)₂]OH with benzene as a buffering layer gave dark-green crystals of a porous metal azolate framework [Cu₂(μ-OH)₂(bdim)]·5.4H₂O (1, MAF-35) in low yield (ca. 20%) after 4 months. A few dark-green single crystals of [Cu₂(μ-OCH₃)₂(bdim)]·C₆H₆ (2) was also isolated as
55 an byproduct. Apparently, Cu(I) was oxidized slowly during the diffusion reaction to give these Cu(II) complexes. When [Cu(NH₃)₄](OH)₂ were used to conduct the diffusion experiment, we always got dark-blue powders with unknown structure (Fig. S1, ESI†). Fortunately, bulk microcrystalline powder of 1 can be
60 synthesized in high yield (85%) by heterogeneous reaction of H₂bdim and Cu(OH)₂ at room temperature for 5 days.

1 and 2 have very similar network structures (Fig. S2, ESI†). The former crystallizes in the monoclinic space group P2₁/m
70 while the latter has a higher symmetry (C2/m) and slightly expanded unit-cell volume (5%). Their host frameworks can be simplied as 3-dimentional (3D) uninodal 4-connected Ivt networks consisting of four-coordinated bdim²⁻ ligands and planar, four-coordinated [Cu₂(μ-OR)₂]²⁺ (R = H, CH₃) dinuclear
75 clusters (Fig. 1a). To the best of our knowledge, this type of cluster has been only found in one PCP.⁸ The Cu(II) ions in 1 form weak coordination with the guest H₂O molecules [Cu···O

2.4743(2)-2.9730(3) Å] (Fig. 1b). If all water and benzene molecules were removed, 1 and 2 could possess solvent accessible volumes of 48.7% and 33.8%, respectively. Compared with 1, the void volume of 2 is obviously smaller because of its larger OCH₃ group. If OCH₃ in 2 could be replaced by OH without changing any other part of the framework, the void volume of 2 would increase to 51.0%.

It's worth noting that 1 has a very high concentration of Cu(II) ions (8.6 mol L⁻¹ and 6.3 mol kg⁻¹). Moreover, since the square planar Cu(II) ions expose two potential coordination sites on the pore surface, the concentration of OMSs in 1 (17.2 mol L⁻¹ or 12.6 mol kg⁻¹) is significantly higher than those of literature-reported PCPs (Table S2, ESI†).⁷ In most PCPs, Cu(II) ions can only expose one of the two potential coordination sides. For instance, porous Cu(II) carboxylate frameworks are generally composed of paddlewheel-like Cu₂(RCOO)₄ clusters, in which two square-planar Cu(II) ions form face-to-face stacking, leading to the blocking of a potential coordination site per metal ion. Pyridyl and azolate ligands tend to form mononuclear Cu building units,⁹ but counter anions or steric hindrance from the ligands usually hinder the exposure of OMSs. Therefore, the planar [Cu₂(μ-OH)₂]²⁺ cluster is the key to increase the concentration of the open Cu(II) sites.

Thermogravimetry analysis of 1 showed a weight loss of 23.5% below 70 °C corresponding to 5.4 water molecules per formula unit (calcd 23.4%), and then a plateau from 70 to 180 °C until decomposition (Fig. S3, ESI†). Power X-ray powder diffraction (PXRD) pattern of the guest-free sample was similar with that of 2 rather than 1. When 1 was soaked in H₂O or exposed in saturated H₂O vapour, the resulting sample also showed a PXRD pattern similar with that of 2, but it easily lost partial guest molecules in air to transform back to 1 (Fig. S4, ESI†). These observations demonstrated that the framework structure of [Cu₂(μ-OH)₂(bdim)] expanded to a structure similar to 2 (hereafter denoted as 1') when it is guest-free or saturated by guest molecules, but adopted the narrow pore phase 1 with partial guest loading. Similar phenomenon have been observed in a few PCPs, such as MIL-53.¹⁰ Indeed, the unit-cell parameters of 1' derived from its PXRD pattern are very similar to those of 2 (Fig. S5, ESI†).

N₂ sorption isotherm of 1' measured at 77 K exhibits a type-I character with a saturated adsorption capacity of about 240 cm³ (STP) g⁻¹, corresponding to a pore volume of 0.375 cm³ g⁻¹ (Fig. S6, ESI†). For comparison, the empirical pore volumes calculated from the crystal structures of 1 and 1' are 0.359 cm³ g⁻¹ and 0.373 cm³ g⁻¹, respectively. The Brunauer-Emmett-Teller and Langmuir surface areas were calculated to be 974 and 1043 m² g⁻¹, respectively. H₂ sorption isotherm (Fig. S7, ESI†) of 1' measured at 77 K demonstrated an uptake of 1.97 wt% at 1 atm, which is higher than most PCPs with similar/lower surface areas,¹¹ indicating relatively strong H₂ binding affinity of 1'.

To examine the CO₂ capture performance of 1', CO₂ sorption isotherms were measured at 273, 283, 290 and 298 K. The CO₂ uptakes at 1 atm reach 129 cm³ g⁻¹ (5.76 mmol g⁻¹, 25.3 wt%, 0.91 CO₂ per Cu) at 273 K and 100 cm³ g⁻¹ (4.46 mmol g⁻¹, 19.6 wt%, 0.71 CO₂ per Cu) at 298 K (Fig. 2a). While the gravimetric CO₂ uptakes of 1' are relatively high among known adsorbents, its volumetric uptakes (343 g L⁻¹ and 266 g L⁻¹ at 273 K and 298 K, respectively, at 1 atm) are more remarkable (Table S2, ESI†).

For comparion, N₂ sorption isotherms were also measured at 273 and 298 K, which showed uptakes of 16.0 and 9.9 cm³ g⁻¹ at 1 atm, respectively (Fig. S8a, ESI†). The Henry's law CO₂/N₂ selectivities¹² were calculated (Fig. S8b and Table S3, ESI†) as 77 and 43 at 273 and 298 K, respectively. Using the ideal absorbed solution theory (IAST),¹³ the CO₂/N₂ selectivities for 15/85 CO₂/N₂ mixture at 1 atm were calculated as 72 and 37 at 273 K and 298 K, respectively. The highly selective adsorption of CO₂ over N₂ suggests that 1' may be useful for CO₂ capture.¹⁴

![](./images/813194409137930241_3.jpg)

Fig. 2 (a) CO₂ adsorption (solid) and desorption (open) isotherms measured at different temperatures and (b) coverage-dependent CO₂ adsorption enthalpy profiles (calculated by two methods) of 1'.

The coverage-dependent CO₂ adsorption enthalpy ($Q_{\text{st}}$) of 1' was calculated with the Clausius-Clapeyron equation using isotherms measured at 273, 283, 290 and 298 K (Fig. 2a), which could be well described by the dual-site Langmuir-Freundlich model¹⁵ (Fig. S9, Table S4 and Fig. 2b, ESI†). The $Q_{\text{st}}$ value was 46.7±1.6 kJ mol⁻¹ at zero-coverage, and then decreased rapidly to 29.0±0.4 kJ mol⁻¹ at 1 mmol g⁻¹ (0.16 CO₂ per Cu), after that it increased slowly to 34.1±0.5 kJ mol⁻¹ at 4.5 mmol g⁻¹ (0.71 CO₂ per Cu). In situ PXRD measurement at different CO₂ pressures (CO₂/N₂ mixed gas flow, 298 K) demonstrated that a transformation from 1' to 1 started at 4 kPa and completed above 9 kPa (Figs. S10 and S11, ESI†). According to the sorption isotherm at 298 K, the CO₂ uptake at 9 kPa is about 1 mmol g⁻¹, being coincident with the lowest $Q_{\text{st}}$ value. These results demonstrated that the transformation from 1' to 1 could be induced by low-pressure CO₂, which consume partial host-guset interaction energy to give relatively low apparent $Q_{\text{st}}$. Above 9 kPa or 1 mmol g⁻¹, the increase of $Q_{\text{st}}$ can be ascribed to the increasing contribuition of guest-guest interaction at high CO₂ loading.

Fig. 3 GCMC derived (a) primary adsorption site at zero coverage and (b) host-guest interactions at the high coverage for the CO₂ adsorption in 1.

Furthermore, grand canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) simulation was used to evaluate the host-guest interactions between 1' and CO₂ at 298 K. At zero coverage, a CO₂ molecule is chelated by two Cu(II) ions (Cu∙∙∙O 2.74 and 2.86 Å) (Fig. 3a) with a high interaction energy of 48.0 kJ mol⁻¹ being similar to the measured value (46.7±1.6 kJ mol⁻¹). At high coverages, each Cu(II) ion can bind two CO₂ molecules by its two exposed sides (Fig. 3b), and guest-guest interactions can be also observed (Fig. S12, ESI†). At 1 atm, the simulated CO₂ uptake and binding energy reached 95 cm³ g⁻¹ and 37.2 kJ mol⁻¹, respectively, which are close to the measured values.

The CO₂ capture performance of 1' under more practical conditions was evaluated by thermogravimetry analysis, during which the partial pressure of CO₂ and/or temperature were varied to simulate the common adsorption/desorption conditions. Firstly, 1' was blown repeatedly using a CO₂/N₂ (15:85, v/v) mixture and a pure N₂ flow at 30 °C (Fig. 4). A 5.1 wt% weight change was observed over repeated cycles, which was much higher than those of the reported PCPs (at the same condition), such as en-CuBTTri (0.75 wt%)⁵ᵃ and SNU-21 (0.72 wt%).¹⁶ Significantly, the adsorption and desorption amounts can reach 90% of the total capacity after only 3 and 5 minutes, respectively, and no apparent change in capacity could be observed over repeated cycles, which demonstrated very fast sorption kinetics and good reversibility. When the temperatures of adsorption and desorption changed to 25 °C and 50 °C, respectively (Fig. S13, ESI†), the mass change could increase to 6.5 wt% over each cycle.⁵ᵇ Meanwhile, the adsorption and desorption kinetics were still very fast (4 and 6 min for 90% adsorption and desorption of the total capacity, respectively). So 1 may be suitable for use as an adsorbent in a pressure swing absorption type process for CO₂ capture.⁵ᵃ

Fig.4 Repeated adsorption-desorption kinetics for 1' under a mixed CO₂/N₂ (15:85, v/v) flow and a pure N₂ flow at constant temperature of 30 °C.

In summary, a flexible porous MAF with unique planar dinuclear Cu₂(OH)₂²⁺ clusters has been synthesized, which demonstrated that combination of high concentration of active sites and framework flexibility could be a viable strategy for efficient and low-energy CO₂ capture and separation.
