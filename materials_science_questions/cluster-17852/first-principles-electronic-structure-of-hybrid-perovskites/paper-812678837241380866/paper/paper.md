# ABSTRACT

Motivated by the chirality research of hybrid halide perovskite, we report the controllable growth of single crystals of (R)-, (S)- and (R, S)- $C_6H_5CH(CH_3)NH_3$ (MBA) based lead (Pb) halide perovskites. The crystal structures were redetermined and further refined to clarify the previously ambiguous crystal structure problems. We further investigated the chiral optical properties of these single crystals including nonlinear optical (NLO) properties, and photoluminescence (PL) properties. The as-fabricated (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals not only show notable circular dichroism (CD) signals in the absorption spectra, but also exhibit obvious circularly polarized luminescence (CPL) characteristics. The available chiral hybrid perovskite single crystals open up the possibility to study these intrinsic chirality properties for optoelectronic applications.

Circular dichroism (CD) and circularly polarized luminescence (CPL) based on the chiral materials have received increasing interests in the fields of optoelectronics, in which the chiral materials absorbed differently oriented left/right circularly polarized light (LCP/RCP). $^{1-5}$ To obtain CD and CPL signals of chiral hybrid halide perovskite materials, we should consider the chiral organic molecules induced effects including (i) acentric perovskite structures incorporated with chiral organic molecules, $^{6-7}$ (ii) electronic interactions induced by large dipole moments between chiral organic molecules and perovskite materials or quantum dots (QDs), $^{8}$ (iii) non-centrosymmetric dislocations of perovskite materials or QDs fabricated with chiral organic molecules as ligands $^{9}$ and (iv) chiral distortion of the perovskite material or QDs surfaces induced by chiral organic molecules. $^{10-11}$ Based on the above strategies, there have been already many efforts on the fabrications of chiral perovskite materials. For example, chiral methylbenzylamine (MBA) molecules-based halide lead (Pb) perovskites have been previously investigated. $^{12-22}$ Billing et al. first reported the synthesis and crystal structure of (R)- and (S)-methylbenzylamine molecules [(R)- and (S)-MBA] based chiral hybrid perovskite materials. $^{12}$ Later, chiral hybrid perovskite thin films with circularly polarization absorption feature were obtained by Ahn et al. $^{13}$ In addition to the thin films, there are also many reports on chiral MBA based perovskite micro/nanostructures and the effects on the performance of optoelectronic devices. $^{14-18}$ Ma et al. have demonstrated the chiral 2D perovskite microplates with a high degree of circularly polarized photoluminescence (CPL) and their photodetector devices combined with $MoS_2$ materials. $^{19}$ Miyasaka et al. show that bulk photovoltaic effect in a pair of chiral-polar layered perovskite-type lead iodides were altered by chirality of organic cations. $^{20}$ Lu et al. reported that spin-dependent charge transport through 2D chiral hybrid lead-iodide perovskite thin films was systematically studied. $^{21}$ To our knowledge,

there are no reports on chiral MBA based perovskite bulk single crystals, although single crystals are the ideal platforms for assessing their intrinsic properties.

In this work, we reported the growth of different kinds of MBA based lead (Pb) perovskite single crystals by controlling the molar ratio of starting reactants (MBA and PbO) in HX (X=Br, I) solution using temperature-lowering method. Powder second-harmonic generation (PSHG) measurements demonstrate that acentric (R)- and (S)-MBAPbX₃ (X=Br, I) single crystals are NLO-active when exposed to 1064 nm laser irradiations, while (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄, (R, S)-MBA₃PbBr₅ · H₂O and (R, S)-MBA₂PbI₄ single crystals with centrosymmetric structures are NLO-inactive. Interestingly, (R)- and (S)-MBA based halide perovskite single crystals exhibit obvious CD responses with the differently chiral organic configurations, while (R, S)-MBA based hybrid perovskite single crystals both display no CD signals. Moreover, (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals show CPL signals with photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) of 1.5 % and 1.4 % and average PL decay lifetime of 9.50 ns and 9.23 ns respectively.

Figure 1 schematically show the growth of MBA-based hybrid perovskite single crystals with different strategies. Considering the difficulties in obtaining bulk MBA-based hybrid perovskite single crystals, it is of great importance to control the growth conditions, including the proper

molar ratios of reactants and different cooling-down temperature, yielding the aimed bulk MBA-based hybrid perovskite single crystals. Here, (R)- and (S)-MBA solutions, as well as (R, S)-MBA solution, were reacted with PbO in HX (X=Br, I) solutions to form the product powders at ice bath under ambient atmosphere. The as-formed powders in HX (X=Br, I) solutions were heated to 75 °C, ensuring the complete dissolve of starting materials. In MBA-Pb-Br systems, plate- and block-shaped colorless (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals can be grown in HBr solution (see **Figure 1b and 1i**), but it is difficult to obtain (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbBr₄ perovskites. As shown in **Figure 1e**, we can only obtain (R, S)-MBA₃PbBr₅ · H₂O single crystals. In MBA-Pb-I systems, we can obtain (R)- and (S)-MBA based lead iodide perovskites of MBAPbI₃ and MBA₂PbI₄ (see **Figure 1c, d, g and h**). More importantly, yellow (R)- and (S)-MBAPbI₃ single crystals can be obtained in HI solution at a temperature above 45 °C and 50 °C, respectively. In comparison, needle-like orange-red (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ single crystals were formed below 45 °C and 50 °C. It was also found that a high concentration precursor solution formed by MBA and PbO in HI solution yield yellow (R)- and (S)-MBAPbI₃ single crystals were formed, while a low concentration precursor solution formed by MBA and PbO in HI solution yield orange-red (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ single crystals. In (R, S)-MBA-Pb-I systems, only orange-red (R, S)-MBA₂PbI₄ single crystals can be fabricated (see **Figure 1f**). Besides, powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns of MBA-based hybrid perovskites were in good consistence with their simulated sing-crystal XRD patterns (see Supporting Information, **Figure S1**), confirming the pure phase of these single crystals.

Single crystal structure analyses show that (R)- and (S)-MBA based halide perovskite single crystals belong to orthorhombic phases, while (R, S)-MBA based halide perovskite single crystals can be attributed to monoclinic system (see Supporting Information, **Figure S2, Figure S3 and Table S1-S2**). (R)- and (S)-MBAPbX₃ (X=Br, I) single crystals exhibit

the chiral structures with space group $P2_12_12_1$ (no. 19), which agreed with the previous reports.$^{12}$ However, (R)- and (S)-MBA$_2$PbI$_4$ single crystals previously exhibit a distorted perovskite crystal structures with space group chiral $P2_12_12_1$ (no. 19).$^{12}$ In our results, (R)- and (S)-MBA$_2$PbI$_4$ single crystals both belong to nonpolar space group $Pnma$ (no. 62) with the symmetry. Besides, (R, S)-MBA$_3$PbBr$_5$ $\cdot$ H$_2$O and (R, S)-MBA$_2$PbI$_4$ single crystals exhibit the nonpolar space group $C2/c$ (no.15) and $P2_1/c$ (no.14) in Figure S3, which is similar to former reports about the space group $C2/c$ (no.15) and $P2_1/a$ (no.14) $^{12, 22}$ Moreover, we studied the nonlinear optical (NLO) properties of chiral (R)- or (S)-MBAPbX$_3$ (X=Br, I) to verify their correct crystal structures. PSHG measurements demonstrated that chiral (R)- and (S)-MBAPbX$_3$ (X=Br, I) single crystals were NLO-active when exposed to 1064 nm laser irradiations in Figure S4a, while (R)- and (S)-MBA$_2$PbI$_4$, (R, S)-MBA$_3$PbBr$_5$ $\cdot$ H$_2$O and (R, S)-MBA$_2$PbI$_4$ single crystals with centrosymmetric structures were NLO-inactive. Additional NLO measurements showed that the SHG intensity of chiral (R)- or (S)-MBAPbX$_3$ (X=Br, I) increased with the increase of particle size, exhibiting the type-I phase-match PSHG effect in Figure S4b, which was similar to the previous reports. $^{23-24}$ Formerly, (R-MBA)$_{1.5}$PbBr$_{3.5}$(DMSO)$_{0.5}$ and (S-MBA)$_{1.5}$PbBr$_{3.5}$(DMSO)$_{0.5}$ single-crystalline nanowires exhibit obvious nonlinear optical characteristics were reported by Yuan et al.$^{25}$ Also, Luo's group reported that C$_5$H$_{14}$N$_2$PbCl$_4$$\cdot$H$_2$O and (R-C$_5$H$_{14}$N$_2$)PbBr$_4$ and (S-C$_5$H$_{14}$N$_2$)PbBr$_4$ showed second harmonic generation (SHG) properties.$^{26-27}$ From Figure S2(a-d), chiral (R)- or (S)-MBAPbX$_3$ (X=Br, I) crystallized with the R or S isomer and hence their corresponding structures are enantiomorphs with each other. Furthermore, the hydrogen bonding formations between the ammonium hydrogens and the halides in (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr$_3$ and (R)- and (S)-MBA$_2$PbI$_4$ crystal structures can exist. Differently, there were non-centrosymmetric structure units in form of triangular pyramid monomers or copolymers in all the chiral (R)- or (S)-

$\text{MBAPbX}_3$ (X=Br, I) crystal structures. However, (R)- and (S)-$\text{MBA}_2\text{PbI}_4$, (R, S)-$\text{MBA}_3\text{PbBr}_5$ · $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and (R, S)-$\text{MBA}_2\text{PbI}_4$ single crystals exhibit the $\{\text{PbX}_6\}$ (X=Br, I) octahedral structure in Figure S2 (e, f) and Figure S3. Therefore, the clear chiral crystal structures urged us to investigate their chiral optical properties including circular dichroism (CD) and circularly polarized luminescence (CPL).

UV-vis absorption spectra of these grinding single-crystalline powders were measured and shown in Figure $2.^{28-37}$ From Figure 2a, the optical band gap of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbI₃ and MBA₂PbI₄ are calculated to be about 2.55 and 2.14 eV, respectively. As shown in Figure 2b, (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ have similar optical band gap of about 3.30 eV in Figure 2c and 2d. We also determined the electronic structures of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbX₃ (X=Br, I) and MBA₂PbI₄ (see Figure S5) by theoretical calculation methods. PBE functional calculations³² usually underestimated the band gap. Theoretical calculations demonstrated that band structure diagrams for (R)- and (S)-MBAPbX₃ (X=Br, I) show a class of indirect semiconductors. However, (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ are direct semiconductors. The calculated band gaps agreed with the experimental values in Table S3. Based on the chiral (R)- and (S)-MBAPbX₃ (X=Br, I) single crystals, their wide band gap may forebode a large laser damage threshold, which can withstand the radiation of high energy laser in the NLO applications.

To investigate CD signals of these single-crystalline materials, thin films were first fabricated to investigate the absorption properties, according to the previous literature.¹³⁻¹⁴ The CD spectra of only (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ thin films were reported by Ahn et al. ¹³ The circularly polarized light detections based on (R)-and (S)-MBAPbI₃ thin film¹⁴ and (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ microplates with high degree of circularly polarized light¹⁷ were investigated. However, CD signals of MBA-based iodide perovskite single crystals were not obtained because of self-absorption of their single crystals. Therefore, we prepared MBA-based iodide perovskite thin films

and single-crystalline powders in KBr pellets to conduct CD measurements. CD signals based on (R)- and (S)-MBAPbI₃ and (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ single-crystalline powders in KBr pellets in **Figure S6**. It is concluded that the CD signals are probably from chiral MBA rather than chiral perovskite crystal structures. It is noticed that the CD signals of powder pellets are weaker than those of thin films on quartz substrates. As shown in **Figure 3a and 3b**, CD spectra and normalized extinction spectra of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbI₃ thin films demonstrated that the CD peaks were located before the extinction band edge. $^{2,38}$ (R)- and (S)-MBAPbI₃ thin films exhibit distinct CD spectra with opposite signs at the wavelength of 370, 372 and 389 nm, as shown in **Figure 3a**. Also, (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ thin films showed the obvious CD spectra with opposite signs at the wavelength of 300, 335, 369, 404, 424, 454, 490 and 504 nm and the CD peaks were also located before the extinction band edge in **Figure 3c and 3d**. The origins of those multiple peaks in (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ perovskites mainly resulted from the chiral molecules. The results are consistent with Ahn's previous reports. $^{13}$ Therefore, it is reasonable that the CD signals from these single crystals originate from the chiral (R)- and (S)-MBA molecules in the hybrid perovskites. Moreover, it was also observed that the CD spectra measured based on the chiral MBA lead iodide perovskites occurred with the changes of the spectra, which was similar to cotton effect in the chiral optics³⁹ and attributed to the energy levels splitting from the related electronic states affected by the chiral (R)- and (S)-MBA. Besides, the obvious CD signals were observed in the certain-wavelength region and the absorption spectra determinations apart from the optical reflectance and scattering effects. The phenomena may be attributed to the refractive index of the chiral medium and differential scatterings of circular polarized light. $^{40-42}$ Recently, Long et al. reported the CD spectra of (R)- and (S)-MBA based lead bromide perovskite thin films by spin-control strategies.¹⁸ However, CD signal measurement methods of chiral single crystals were illustrated by previous

report.⁴³ We tried to understand the CD differences between (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals and single-crystalline powders. The CD measurements were carried out on the single-crystalline powder samples in KBr pellets. As shown in **Figure 4a** and **4b**, (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single-crystalline powders exhibit the obvious CD spectra with opposite signs at 324, 328, 335, 350 and 356 nm, and the CD peaks were also positioned before the extinction band edge. However, (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals exhibit the obvious red-shift CD signals, exhibiting the obvious CD spectra with opposite signs at 365 nm (see **Figure 4c** and **4d**). It is concluded that CD signals of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals are stronger than those of their powders. In all the chiral MBA-based hybrid lead perovskites, CPL signals of (R)- and (S)-MBA based hybrid perovskite single crystals have not yet been demonstrated until now. It is difficult to obtain CD signals of (R)- and (S)-MBA based lead iodide perovskite single crystals, which may be attributed to the influences of linear dichroism (LD) and intrinsic birefringence based on the previous reports.⁴⁴⁻⁴⁸ Due to the weak PL of (R)- and (S)-MBA based lead iodide perovskite samples in **Figure S7**, CPL signals of (R)- and (S)-MBA based lead iodide perovskite single crystals have not been obtained, which may originate from linearly polarized luminescence (LPL) effect and crystal defects.⁴⁹⁻⁵⁰ Therefore, we only focused on the CPL signals of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals.

As we know, it is a great challenge to clarify the CPL signals for single crystals, because the single crystals possibly produced CPL signals combined with linearly polarized luminescence (LPL) effect when exposed to circularly polarized light.⁴⁹ According to the previous descriptions, ⁵,⁵⁰⁻⁵¹ CPL is often defined and measured as the difference value (I_L-I_R), where I_R, I_L represent the intensity difference between right and left circularly polarized light on the samples, and the average emission intensity is (1/2)(I_L+I_R). Generally speaking, its **Equation (1)** is described in form of the dissymmetry factor g_lum,

$$\mathrm{g_{lum}} = (\mathrm{I_L{-}I_R})/[(1/2)(\mathrm{I_L{+}I_R})] \tag{1}$$

Thus, we measured the CPL signals of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals with different chirality. Both samples exhibit the CPL signals with the mirror-symmetry, which was similar to the previous reports.¹⁶,⁵²⁻⁵⁶ The observed CPL spectra were possibly attributed to the interactions between the chiral MBA and Pb-Br based non-centrosymmetric triangular pyramid copolymers. CPL spectra of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals exhibit mirror-like signals from 450 to 850 nm in **Figure 5a** and **5b**. The PL decay lifetime of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ was performed by the time-resolved PL measured at room temperature. A further progress is the PL decay lifetime firstly reported for (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ perovskite single crystals in **Figure 5c** and **5d**. We used a single exponential function to fit the tail of the normalized PL decay lifetimes. The decay lifetime values of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ were 9.50 ns and 9.23 ns, respectively. The PL emission peaks of newly-synthesized (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ perovskite single crystals located at about 720 nm position. In **Figure S7**, (R)- and (S)-MBA based iodide perovskites exhibited the similar emission positions. The similar behaviors possibly originated from the chiral MBA cation group. Surprisingly, we found that the emission peaks of (R)-and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals located at around 620 nm position and exhibit the blue-shift broad peaks when placed in air for a long time in **Figure S8**. This probability is that the surfaces of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ perovskite single crystals are oxidized and decomposed induced by oxygen and H₂O, which is similar to the previous reports.⁵⁷⁻⁵⁸ Moreover, fluorescence quantum yield is one of important factor for optoelectronic applications. Formerly, Long et al. demonstrated that (R)- and (S)-MBA based bromide perovskite thin films exhibit the low PLQY characteristics, which was possibly attributed to their fast nonradiative rates.⁵⁹⁻⁶⁰ In our results, due to the poor photoluminescence of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals, the PLQY of (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals is 1.5 % and 1.4

% in **Table S4**. These attractive features may provide guidance for the further chiral optical applications of these hybrid perovskite single crystals.

**Figure 5.** (a) Circular polarized luminescence of (R)-and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals; (b) Emission spectra of (R)-and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals when exposed to circularly polarized light; (c, d) PL decay lifetime of (R)-and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals.

In summary, we report the fabrication of different kinds of chiral MBA based hybrid perovskite single crystals by controlling the ratios of reactants (MBA and PbO) in HX (X=Br, I) solutions using temperature-lowering method. Their crystal structures were redetermined and the PSHG measurements showed that (R)- and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ exhibit the centrosymmetric crystal structure. Interestingly, two kinds of (R)- and (S)-MBA based lead iodide perovskite single crystals (chiral MBAPbI₃ and centrosymmetric MBA₂PbI₄) were obtained. Optical measurements demonstrated that acentric (R)- and (S)-MBAPbX₃ (X=Br, I) single crystals are indirect band gap

semiconductors, while (R)-and (S)-MBA₂PbI₄ single crystals are direct band gap semiconductors.

More importantly, (R)- and (S)-MBAPbBr₃ single crystals showed the obvious red-shift CD signals, compared with their single-crystalline powder samples, and exhibit the outstanding CPL characteristics with short PL lifetime. These results enable the insightful understandings into the optical properties of chiral hybrid perovskite single crystals, which may provide guideline to achieve chiral optical or optoelectronic devices.
