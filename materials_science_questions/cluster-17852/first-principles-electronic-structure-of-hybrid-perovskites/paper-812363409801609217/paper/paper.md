# Research Article

## Architecturing 1D-2D-3D Multidimensional Coupled
## CsPbI₂Br Perovskites toward Highly Effective and Stable
## Solar Cells

Kun Liu, Songyang Yuan, Yeming Xian, Yi Long, Qin Yao, Naveed Ur Rahman, Yang Guo, Mingyuan Sun, Qifan Xue, Hin-Lap Yip, Andreu Cabot, Wenzhe Li,* and Jiandong Fan*

Despite the rapid development of $CsPbI_xBr_{3-x} (0 \leq x \leq 3)$ inorganic perovskite solar cells, associated with their superior thermal stability, their low moisture stability limits their commercial deployment. In this study, 1D-2D-3D multidimensional coupled perovskites are prepared by means of an in situ self-integration approach. This pioneering method allows incorporating thus far unreported 1D-Tpy₂Pb₃I₆ and 2D-TpyPb₃I₆ (Tpy; terpyridine) perovskites. Heterojunction perovskites demonstrate superior stability against water in comparison with control 3D CsPbI₂Br, which is related to the hydrophobicity of low-dimension (LD) perovskites. Remarkably, the spontaneous involvement of LD perovskites can adjust/reconstruct the interfacial structure. This modification allows releasing the residual strain, establishing effective charge transfer channels that increase the carrier transport ability. Accordingly, 1D-2D-3D hybrid CsPbI₂Br perovskite solar cells demonstrate a stabilized power conversion efficiency as high as 16.1%, which represents a very significant improvement, by a factor of 43%, with respect to control 3D CsPbI₂Br perovskite solar cell. Equally importantly, the multidimensional coupled perovskite solar cells exhibit extraordinary stability, well above 1000 h in ambient atmosphere.

### 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSC) have developed rapidly in recent years, with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) soaring from 3.8% to the current 25.5% record in just a decade.[1–5] However, its industrial development and commercialization are severely slowed down due to its poor thermal stability. All-inorganic PSC, for example, $CsPbI_xBr_{3-x} (0 \leq x \leq 3)$, with an excellent thermal stability, provide a strategy to tackle out all stability issues.[6,7] Unfortunately, $CsPbI_xBr_{3-x}$ all-inorganic perovskites generally suffer from severe instability to moisture and/or unsuitable bandgap for photon absorbance.[8–10]

Low-dimensional (LD) perovskites, that is, 2D, 1D, and 0D perovskites, have recently attracted great interest owing to their superior hydrophobicity.[11–14] Structurally, the radius of the organic group at the A position is large, and relies on hydrogen bonds, $\pi$-$\pi$ conjugate interaction, and other interatomic forces to bond with each other, "stretching" the massive 3D perovskite material to form an LD skeleton structure.[15,16] The skeleton of LD materials is separated and/or wrapped by hydrophobic organic molecules, which effectively improve the water and oxygen stability of the material. Likewise, in LD structures, the organic polyamine has a strong bond with the inorganic framework, which limits the migration of negative ions, and greatly improves stability.[13,17–19] Following this line of thought, the incorporation of an LD perovskite inside widely-used 3D $CsPbI_xBr_{3-x}$ may contribute to structurally sort out the stability issue. In this scenario, results in two main research directions have been reported. i) Various 2D-3D perovskite materials, for example, $BA_2CsPb_2I_6Br/CsPbI_2Br$,[20] $GABr/Cs_{1-x}Rb_xPbI_2Br$,[21] $GA_2PbI_4/CsPbI_xBr_{3-x}$,[22] and $Cs_2PbI_2Cl_2/CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}$,[23] were developed and incorporated in 3D $CsPbI_xBr_{3-x}$ perovskite. The obtained devices demonstrated remarkably enhanced stability against water whilst maintaining acceptable PCEs. ii) Few reports have focused on 1D-3D organic-inorganic hybrid PSC. For instance, Mercouri G. Kanatzidis group developed organic cation thiazolium based

Aside from the stability issue, it was recently observed the non-conformal growth in hybrid perovskites indicating strain relaxation by dislocation formation. $^{[26]}$ In 3D $CsPbI_xBr_{3-x}$ perovskites, the phase is unstable and non-uniform and phase separation through segregation of ions is commonly observed, which causes local lattice mismatch and leads to residual deformation. $^{[27]}$ Likewise, the lattice mismatch in the heterojunction domain between LD and 3D perovskite would also give rise to a lattice strain that inevitably increases the defect concentration and non-radiative recombination, $^{[28-30]}$ and thereby quench the PCE of LD-3D $CsPbI_xBr_{3-x}$ PSC.

The in situ prepared lattice-matching perovskite thin films allowed to alleviate the residual lattice strain, and thereafter reduce the defect concentration and non-radiative recombination, which facilitated the carrier transport through constructed transfer channel. The corresponding LD-3D $CsPbI_2Br$ PSC displayed enhanced photovoltaic performances, whilst improved stability against water in an ambient environment.

Herein, we found three types of $PbI_2$-based complexes as discussed in the following. Particularly, the complex of the doped precursor single crystal was determined to be $(C_{15}H_{13}N_3)_4Pb_5I_{18}$, and it will be referred to as $PbI_2$-Tpy (intermediate). In this case, $PbI_2$ can complex with Tpy to form a 1D perovskite structure, in which the $[PbI_6]^{4-}$ octahedra display a linear zigzag arrangement along the [010] direction (Figure S1c, Supporting Information). Unfortunately, the additional diffraction peaks are not consistent with the ones calculated from the resolved structure data of the $PbI_2$-Tpy (intermediate). We further resolved and refined the crystal structure of additional $PbI_2$-Tpy-based perovskite single crystals. They showed 1D and 2D structures, which we refer to as $1D-Tpy_2Pb_3I_6$ and $2D-TpyPb_3I_6$, respectively. In the case of $1D-Tpy_2Pb_3I_6$ ($n = 1$), that is, $(C_{15}H_{11}N_3)_2Pb_3I_6$, it includes linear arrangement of $[PbN_3I_3]^{2-}$ and $[PbI_6]^{2-}$ octahedra along [001] direction at regular intervals (Figure 1c). Particularly, the Pb atom is observed to coordinate with three I atoms and three N atoms in the $[PbN_3I_3]^{2-}$ octahedra. It can be envisioned that the Pb-I bond was broken during the in situ crystallization process, followed by the Pb-N bond forming for the $[PbN_3I_3]^{2-}$ octahedra, alongside the symmetry increase from triclinic P-1 to monoclinic P21/c space group. With the n value increase, the structure of $1D-Tpy_2Pb_3I_6$ perovskite appears to extend to $2D-TpyPb_3I_6$ ($n = 1$), that is, $n = \propto$ in the 1D scenario. The corresponding molecular was resolved to be $(C_{15}H_{11}N_3)Pb_3I_6$. Again, the structure displays linear arrangement of $[PbN_3I_3]^{2-}$ and $[PbI_6]^{2-}$ octahedra along [001] direction at regular intervals. In this case, the bond length of Pb-I varies from $3.16$ Å to $3.28$ Å, the different bond length leads to the strong distorted $[PbI_6]^{2-}$ octahedra and thereafter shoulder-by-shoulder arrangement along the [100] and [010] directions via sharing the I-I face of octahedra. The Tpy-based organic groups separately interpolate the space among the layer-by-layer arranged $[PbI_6]^{2-}$ octahedra in the view of <010> direction (Figure 1c), and $\pi$-$\pi$ stacking of organic molecules at the A site would be formed. In this regard, the specific 2D perovskite is capable of preventing the water from encroaching the 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ perovskite. $^{[34]}$ It is worth noting that the linear and/or planar metal halide octahedra ($n = 1$) are spaced out by the Tpy organic cations to form a bulk assembly of 1D and/or 2D halide perovskites with Pb-I bonding, which would facilitate the charge carrier transport within a working solar cell. Based on the crystal structure data, we calculated the corresponding XRD peaks of the $1D-Tpy_2Pb_3I_6$ and $2D-TpyPb_3I_6$ perovskite. The simulated patterns perfectly match the experimental diffraction peaks once removed the 3D perovskite contribution (Figure 1a and Figure S2, Supporting Information). Again, the calculated and experimental results suggest the coexistence of 3D perovskite with LD $PbI_2$-Tpy perovskite, that is, $1D-Tpy_2Pb_3I_6$ and $2D-TpyPb_3I_6$ perovskites.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to determine the bonding environment and elemental composition of 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ and 1D-2D-3D perovskite thin films (Figure S3, Supporting Information). As expected, the high resolution Cs 3d, I 3d, and Br 3d XPS spectra remain unshifted with and without LD perovskite additives (Figure S3a-c, Supporting Information). The unchanged binding energies suggest that the above-mentioned inner electron orbitals are relatively insensitive to the incorporation of TPY as well as to the distorted replacement of $[PbI_6]^{2-}$ octahedra, which would give rise to more stable inner orbital environment. Besides, no clear shift was observed for the Pb 4f peaks associated with the 3D chemical environment, but additional peaks at slightly lower energies appeared. These additional peaks are associated with the formation of $[PbN_3I_3]^{2-}$ octahedron, in which the forming Pb-N bond owns relatively low binding energy. $^{[13]}$ In contrast with the control 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ perovskite, a weak N 1s signal at 399 eV was observed in the case of 1D-2D-3D perovskite thin films, which is ascribed to the successful incorporation of Tpy. $^{[35]}$ Similarly, the broaden C1s peak of 1D-2D-3D perovskite thin film points at the presence of an additional C 1s band at 285.9 eV, which is also associated with the Tpy additive. $^{[36]}$ These results further confirm the incorporation of LD $PbI_2$-Tpy perovskite.

During the crystallization process, the $[PbI_6]^{2-}$ octahedra in cubic 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ perovskite tend to distort, which inevitably give rise to a residual strain inside the perovskite lattice, especially in the domain of grain interface and surface. Considering the distortions in-plane and out-of-plane relative to the cubic phase $CsPbI_2Br$ perovskite, we evaluated the degree of biaxial anisotropy as follows: $^{[37]}$

$$
\Delta d_{\perp}=1-\frac{d_{(220)}\left(q_{z}\right)}{d_{(002)}\left(q_{x, y}\right)} \tag{1}
$$

Where $d$ corresponds to the interplane spacing in the noted direction, $q$ is the scattering vector ($q = 4\pi\sin(\theta)/\lambda$), and $q_{x,y}$ and $q_{z}$ represent the direction of in-plane and out-of-plane, respectively. According to the Bragg law, $q = 2\pi/d_{hkl}$, where $h$, $k$, and $l$ are Miller indices used to designate planes within a crystal. $^{[38]}$ The grazing incidence wide angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) image of a cubic phase perovskite thin film shortly after quenching suggests the occurrence of anisotropic peak splitting in-plane and out-of-plane. The residual strain is associated with the peak splitting in-plane and out-of-plane. Clearly, the residual strain is proportional to the interplanar space and inversely proportional to the diffraction angle shift on the position of the Bragg peaks.

### 2.2. Interfacial Structure and Carrier Transport in LD-3D Heterojunction Domain

To explore the lattice matching of LD-3D heterojunctions in hybrid 1D-2D-3D perovskite thin films after the in situ co-crystallization process, we performed high-resolution transmission-electron microscopy (HRTEM) characterization. Figure 3a shows a HRTEM image of a heterojunction region of a 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskite thin film, clearly displaying the lattice alignment. In the case of control 3D CsPbI₂Br perovskite, the interplane distance (d values) is 3.0 Å for the (200) plane and 6.0 Å for the (100) plane, respectively, which is consistent with values obtained from XRD analysis as discussed above. Additionally, two more types of lattice fringes, with interplane distances 8.9 Å for (013) plane and 13.0 Å for (001) plane, were also observed, consistently with XRD results of 1D-Tpy₂Pb₃I₆ and 2D-TpyPb₃I₆ perovskites, respectively (Figure 1a and Figure S2, Supporting Information). Experimentally, both the (013) and (001) planes of 1D-Tpy₂Pb₃I₆ and 2D-TpyPb₃I₆ perovskite were observed to be at a certain angle of tilt with the {h00} plane family of 3D perovskite by means of overlapping each other layer by layer (Figure 3b,c). Once again, we observe here that the strain release occurs partially by the creation of misfits and partially by plane bending. In the case of 1D-3D heterostructures, the mismatch strain is almost fully released by the formation of dislocations, whereas the planes are bent only $+1^{\circ}$ near the heterointerface (Figure 3b). In the case 2D-3D heterostructure, with a slight interplane distance mismatch, the two segments of $[PbX_{6}]^{4-}$ octahedra in 3D and 2D perovskites are tilted one with respect to the other by $\approx 10^{\circ}$ along the growth direction (Figure 3c). Thus, we structurally observed that both relaxation mechanisms may coexist at the same interface including misfit dislocations and plane bending. Clearly, both the 1D-Tpy₂Pb₃I₆ and 2D-TpyPb₃I₆ perovskites would structurally incorporate inside the 3D perovskite lattice along their preferred orientation, which allows to release the residual strain in a combination of both plastic and elastic deformations.

Thus the delocalized $\pi$ electrons on the Tpy aromatic ring are expected to transfer to the 1D perovskite chain through the shared $Pb^{2+}$ , which is stronger than the Van der Waals interaction.

To further understand the effect of LD perovskite incorpo- ration on the photoelectric performance of 3D perovskites, we measured and calculated the carrier mobility by characterizing the dark $I-V$ curves of control 3D and 1D-2D-3D perovskites, respectively (Figure 4a,b). In both cases, the current increases linearly with the voltage up to a clear kink-point. Further increasing the voltage over the kink-point, the current exhibits a more rapid exponential rise, which is due to filling of the trap states by the injected carriers. The applied voltage at the kink point is known as the trap-filled limit voltage $(V_{TFL})$ , which allows determining the charge mobility as following the Mott-Gurney Law: $^{[41]}$

$$
u=\frac{8 J d^{3}}{\left(9 \varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon\right) V^{2}} \quad(2)
$$

where $J$ is the current density and $V$ is the applied voltage. The charge mobility of 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskite film is estimated to be $2.95 ×10^{-2} ~cm^{2} ~V^{-1} ~s^{-1}$ for holes and $1.24 ×10^{-3} ~cm^{2} ~V^{-1} ~s^{-1}$ for electrons, respectively. By contrast, the charge carrier mobilities of the reference 3D perovskite film were lower: $3.87 ×10^{-3} ~cm^{2} ~V^{-1} ~s^{-1}$ and $5.46 ×10^{-4} ~cm^{2} ~V^{-1} ~s^{-1}$ for holes and electrons, respectively. These results indicate that 1D-2D-3D perovskite films are characterized by superior carrier transport ability than 3D films, which agrees with calculated results. Fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy was used to determine the photoluminescence (PL) lifetime of 3D and 1D-2D-3D perovskite thin films. As shown in Figure 4c,d, regions characterized by fewer non-radiative pathways and a slower decay were more abundant in the 1D-2D-3D perovskite films. The whole 3D perovskite domain had a relatively short lifetime (light-blueish, <40 ns), whereas the lifetime distribution was widely broadened in the case of 1D-2D-3D perovskite that was centered on 52 ns. It is well known that the high-quality film is generally associated with slower PL decays and longer lifetimes.[42] It is proved that the incorporation of LD perovskite would effectively alleviate the nonradiative recombination centers in polycrystalline semiconductor films, and thereafter facilities the charge carrier transport.

### 2.3. Device Performance of 1D-2D-3D PSC

The experimental results show that, the 1D-2D-3D hybrid PSC with the 7.5 mg mL⁻¹ doping concentration of $PbI_2$-Tpy intermediate additive display best photovoltaic performances (Figure S9, Supporting Information).

Figure 5c displays the $J$-$V$ curves of the champion solar cell based on control 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ and 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskites solar cells. The overall photovoltaic parameters were remarkably enhanced with the incorporation of the LD perovskite. Particularly, the $V_{OC}$ increased from 1.05 to 1.29 V, and the FF improved from 0.69 to 0.78, which are associated with the suitable bandgap alignment and decreased carrier trapping centers. Likewise, the short circuit photocurrent density ($J_{SC}$) was observed to increase from 15.56 to 16.03 mA cm⁻², which is benefited from the improved carrier transport ability of organic group at A-site within the conjugate structure. When these organic cations are assembled with the inorganic framework, the extent to which the positively charged ammonium groups are inserted into the octahedral cavity depends on the length of the bridging alkyl chain. The electron delocalization in the large conjugated structure reduces the separation of the frontal orbital of organic molecules and realizes the orbital overlap with the inorganic part as shown in the differential-charges maps between 1D-3D and 2D-3D perovskite heterojunctions. The $\pi$–$\pi$ stacking of organic molecules would improve the carrier transport ability and thereby enhances the $J_{SC}$ in this regard (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The similar observation was also reported in science very recently.[25] The inset in Figure 5c displays the PCE distribution range for both cases. Accordingly, the champion PCE of 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskites solar cells was significantly increased with respect to control 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ perovskites, from 11.27% to 16.13%. Figure 5d displays the corresponding external quantum efficiency (EQE) and the integrated photocurrent of control 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ and 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskites solar cells. The wavelength range of photo-electron conversion remains the same, which is consistent with the UV-vis spectra considering the best recipe doped concentration is 7.5 mg mL⁻¹. But the corresponding integrated photocurrents increased for the 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskites solar cells when compared with control 3D $CsPbI_2Br$. The integrated photocurrents from the measured EQE spectra were consistent with the measured short-circuit current $J_{sc}$ values, with a discrepancy of less than 5% (Figure 5d).

The stability of as-prepared 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ and 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskite thin films was tested in ambient environment. Figure S11, Supporting Information displays how the 3D perovskite thin film changed to yellow gradually, whereas the color of the 1D-2D-3D hybrid perovskite thin films remained exactly the same. Likewise, the long-term stability of un-encapsulated 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ and 1D-2D-3D hybrid PSC in a controlled environment were also explored and the results are shown in Figure 5e,f. In the controlled condition (aging in dark, 30% R.H., in air), the short circuit current of the control 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ PSC decreased to 0% over 24 h, whereas the 1D-2D-3D hybrid solar cells maintained over 72% of the initial current after 1066 h. Equally important, in the controlled condition (air with 30% R.H, LED white illumination), the control 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ PSC quenched to 0% PCE in 24 h, whereas the PCE of the 1D-2D-3D hybrid solar cells remain over 52% after 1066 h in the exact same condition. The remarkably enhanced long-term stability of 1D-2D-3D hybrid solar cells confirms that the construction of multidimension coupled 1D-2D-3D perovskites is a suitable strategy toward highly efficient and stable PSC. Thus, additional exploration toward further increasing both PCE and area of $CsPbI_2Br$ PSC using this strategy is underway.

### 3. Conclusion
In summary, we designed and prepared a new class of LD perovskites, that is, 1D-Tpy₂Pb₃I₆ and 2D-TpyPb₃I₆ perovskites. The incorporation of LD perovskite inside cubic 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ perovskite structure allows to form multidimension coupled 1D-2D-3D perovskites that were proved to effectively release the residual strain whilst improving the charge carrier transport ability of the 3D $CsPbI_2Br$ perovskite. Accordingly, 1D-2D-3D hybrid PSC yielded remarkably enhanced PCEs by a factor of 43% and displayed superior stability in ambient environment. This study provides a new strategy to release the residual strain, improve the stability against water and enhance the carrier transport ability through dimension engineering, which would be propitious to the ongoing development of inorganic PSC as well as the perovskite-related photoelectron device.
