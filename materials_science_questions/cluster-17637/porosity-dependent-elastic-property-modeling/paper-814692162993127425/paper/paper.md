# Features of the Piezoelectric Effect in a Novel PZT-Type Ceramic/Clay Composite

S. E. FILIPPOV, $^{1}$ A. A. VORONTsOV, $^{1}$ V. YU. TOPOLOV, $^{1,2, *}$ O. E. BRILL, $^{1}$ P. BISEGNA, $^{3}$ AND A. E. PANICH $^{1}$

$^{1}$ Scientific Design \& Technology Institute "Piezopribor", Southern Federal University, 10 Milchakov Street, 344090 Rostov-on-Don, Russia
$^{2}$ Department of Physics, Southern Federal University, 5 Zorge Street, 344090 Rostov-on-Don, Russia
$^{3}$ Department of Civil Engineering and Computer Science, University of Rome "Tor Vergata", 00133 Rome, Italy

Communicated by Dr. Deborah J. Taylor
(Received in final form April 21, 2014)

Piezoelectric properties, their anisotropy and sound velocity are first studied in the composite based on ferroelectric ZTS-19 ceramic of the PZT type, and the second component of the composite is piezo-passive clay. The composite as a whole is characterised by a specific porosity and elements of various connectivity patterns. The important role of microgeometric factors in the formation of piezoelectric coefficients $d_{3j}^{*}$ ($j=1$ and $3$) and their anisotropy $\zeta_{d3j}=d_{33}^{*}/d_{31}^{*}$ is discussed in terms of connectivity patterns at the volume fraction of clay $0.01\leq m_{cl}\leq0.35$. The role of the clay component and clay-containing thin layers in the formation of the piezoelectric activity and sensitivity is interpreted within the framework of models of the two- and three-component composites based on poled ferroelectric ceramics. Results of modelling are compared to experimental data on the studied composite.

Keywords Piezoelectric composite; piezoelectric coefficients; piezoelectric anisotropy; ferroelectric ceramic; clay

Electromechanical properties of modern composites based on poled ferroelectric ceramics (FCs) depend [1–3] on the properties of components, microgeometry, connectivity patterns, poling and technological conditions, etc. The effective properties of these composites can be varied in wide ranges, as is known, for instance, from studies [1–4] on the piezoelectric coefficients $d_{3j}^{*}$, $g_{3j}^{*}$, their hydrostatic analogues $d_{h}^{*}=d_{33}^{*}+2d_{31}^{*}$ and $g_{h}^{*}=g_{33}^{*}+2g_{31}^{*}$, dielectric permittivity $\varepsilon_{33}^{*\sigma}$, and elastic moduli $c_{ab}^{*E}$. An electromechanical interaction between components at the specific microgeometry plays the key role in the formation of the piezoelectric performance of the composite. In the last decade various combinations of the components (FC and polymer, FC and FC, FC and ferroelectric single crystal) were considered [1, 3, 4] to show the piezoelectric performance and related parameters. To the best of our knowl- edge, in earlier experimental papers on piezo-active composites, clay was not a component of specific interest, and therefore, some relations between microgeometry and effective properties were not yet discussed in earlier publications. The aim of the present paper is to analyse the piezoelectric performance of a novel clay-containing composite and to interpret original experimental data at various volume fractions of clay \(m_{cl}\).

Samples of the clay-containing composite based on ZTS-19 FC were manufactured at the Scientific Design & Technology Institute “Piezopribor” (Southern Federal University, Russia). ZTS-19 is a PZT-type FC composition near the morphotropic phase boundary, and full sets of electromechanical constants of this FC in the poled state are given in Table 1. The composite samples were manufactured at mixing the particles of ground clay and the particles of ground preliminary-baked ZTS-19. After mixing, the compression pressure was applied, and sintering at \(950^\circ\)C was carried out. Air polarisation of the composite samples was carried out under an electric field \(E = 3.5\)–4.0 MV/m. Electrophysical measurements were performed on the poled samples with diameter ca. 23 mm and height 6.0–7.5 mm. Micrographs of the poled samples (Fig. 1) were taken using JEOL JSM-6390LA Analitycal Electron Microscope. The micrographs shown in Fig. 1 suggest that an evolution of the microgeometry is observed, and at increasing \(m_{cl}\), FC regions distributed continuously along the poling direction become rare. Pores are distributed over the samples fairly evenly. An interface separating the FC and clay components becomes less distinct (cf. Figs. 1(a) and (e), or Figs. 1(b) and (f)) at increasing the volume fraction \(m_{cl}\). Like poled FCs, the studied composite is characterised by \(\infty mm\) symmetry.

Experimental data from Fig. 2(a,b) show that the longitudinal piezoelectric effect (along the poling axis \(OX_3\)) strongly depends on \(m_{cl}\), and we see an unusual combination of the monotonic \(d_{33}^*(m_{cl})\) and non-monotonic \(d_{31}^*(m_{cl})\) dependences. As a consequence, the monotonic \(g_{33}^*(m_{cl})\) and non-monotonic \(g_{31}^*(m_{cl})\) dependences are mainly caused by changes in \(d_{3j}^*(m_{cl})\), while dielectric permittivity \(\varepsilon_{33}^{*\sigma}(m_{cl})\) remains monotonic. The non-monotonic piezoelectric response is concerned with changes in the microgeometry at increasing \(m_{cl}\) (Fig. 1(a,c,e)), and these changes would influence the electromechanical interaction between the adjacent regions on the non-polar direction \(OX_1\). Comparing curves 1–3 in Fig. 2(a), we note that \(d_{33}^*(m_{cl})\) strongly influences the hydrostatic piezoelectric coefficient \(d_h^*(m_{cl})\), and the large piezoelectric anisotropy \(5.4 < d_{33}^*/|d_{31}^*| < 8.2\) is observed at \(0.01 \leq m_{cl} \leq 0.05\). This means that changes in the microgeometry at small volume fractions \(m_{cl}\) lead to drastic changes in the piezoelectric effect. This method is applied to a composite comprising a system of aligned spheroidal clay inclusions in a large FC matrix. It is assumed that these inclusions with the volume fraction $m_{i}$ are regularly distributed over the matrix, each inclusion is characterised by semi-axes $a_{1}, a_{2}=a_{1}$ and $a_{3}$, and $\rho_{i}=a_{1}/a_{3}$ is the aspect ratio of the inclusion. Such a composite is described by 3-0 connectivity in terms of Refs. 3 and 12, however in a limiting case of $\rho_{i}=0$,3-1 connectivity is achieved. Data from Fig. 3 suggest that values of $|d_{3j}^{*}(m_{cl})|$ are a few times larger than those shown in Fig. 2, a for the same $m_{cl}$ range. Values of $d_{33}^{*}(m_{cl})\approx d_{33}^{(1)}$ (see curves 1-4 in Fig. 3) are typical of the FC-based composite and porous FC ceramics [3,13] wherein a reliable electromechanical coupling due to a continuous distribution of the piezoelectric component along the poling axis $OX_{3}$ takes place.

Our further modelling of the effective electromechanical properties sheds light on features of the piezoelectric effect of the studied composite. A system of the clay/FC and air/clay layers (Fig. 4) is assumed to be elastically matched, and interfaces are perpendicular to $OX_{3}$. The presence of spheroidal inclusions in each layer enables us to vary the elastic and dielectric properties of the layers and composite as a whole. In this case the effective properties are consecutively evaluated by means of the finite element method [4] (3-0 structure, inset 1 in Fig. 4), dilute approach [3,4] (3-0 structure, inset 2 in Fig. 4) and matrix method [3] (2-2 structure of layers alternating along $OX_{3}$, Fig. 4) on assumption that the inclusions and layers are distributed regularly. The presence of the FC matrix in the clay/FC layer promotes a high piezoelectric activity at various volume fractions $m_{i}$ and aspect ratios $\rho_{i}$ (see Fig. 3), however the piezo-passive porous clay layer with relatively low elastic moduli gives rise to weakening the electromechanical interaction between the aforementioned clay/FC layers. The studied ZTS-19-based composite is of interest as a piezoelectric material with the high piezoelectric sensitivity $(g_{33}^{*} > 50$ mV·m/N), the large anisotropy of the piezoelectric coefficients $d_{3j}^{*}$ at $0.01 \leq m_{cl} \leq 0.05$ and with the considerable variation of the sound velocity $v_{s}$. The mutual influence of the porous structure and clay component on the piezoelectric performance leads to a set of effective parameters that have an advantage over the well-known highly anisotropic $PbTiO_{3}$-type FCs [3, 14], namely, the larger values of $d_{33}^{*}$ and $d_{h}^{*}$ at $g_{33}^{*}$ being comparable to that in the $PbTiO_{3}$-type FCs. The set of the above-discussed parameters can promote use of the studied ZTS-19-based composite in sensor, hydroacoustic and other piezotechnical applications.
