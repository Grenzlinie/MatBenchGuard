# Mechanism and energetics of dissociative adsorption of $\text{SiH}_3$ on the hydrogen-terminated $\text{Si}(001)$-(2 $\times$ 1) surface

Stephen P. Walch $^{\text{a}}$, Shyam Ramalingam $^{\text{b}}$, Eray S. Aydil $^{\text{b}}$, Dimitrios Maroudas $^{\text{b},*}$

$^{\text{a}}$ Thermosciences Institute, MS 230-3, NASA Ames Research Center, Moffett Field, CA 94035-1000, USA
$^{\text{b}}$ Department of Chemical Engineering, University of California, Santa Barbara, CA 93106-5080, USA

Received 6 July 2000

## Abstract
A comprehensive theoretical study is presented of the adsorption mechanism and energetics of the silyl ($\text{SiH}_3$) radical on the $\text{Si}(001)$-(2 $\times$ 1) surface terminated by one monolayer of hydrogen atoms. $\text{SiH}_3$ adsorbs on the surface dissociatively through an insertion reaction that involves breaking of the Si–Si surface dimer bond and subsequent transfer of an H atom from the radical to an atom of the broken dimer, thus leading to formation of two surface dihydride ($\text{SiH}_2$) species. The structure and energies of the corresponding equilibrium and transition-state configurations are presented, as well as detailed energetics along the reaction pathway. © 2000 Elsevier Science B.V.

## 1. Introduction
Growth of hydrogenated amorphous silicon (a-Si:H) films on silicon substrates through plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) from silane ($\text{SiH}_4$) containing discharges is used widely in electronic, optoelectronic, and photovoltaic device manufacturing technologies [1–3]. However, the surface chemistry that determines the structure and properties of the deposited Si films is not yet understood [3]. Fundamental understanding of such surface reactions between chemically reactive radicals originating in the plasma and well-characterized silicon surfaces is crucial in determining the elementary mechanisms of silicon film growth by PECVD. Toward this goal, significant contributions can be made by first-principles theoretical calculations and targeted molecular-dynamics (MD) simulations of radical–surface interactions [4].

Among various reactive species present in the gas discharge, the $\text{SiH}_3$ radical created by electron impact dissociation of $\text{SiH}_4$ is believed widely to be the dominant precursor for plasma deposition of a-Si:H films [3,5,6]. This is based largely on observations that under typical deposition conditions $\text{SiH}_3$ is present at high concentrations in silane plasmas; the resulting Si flux to the surface can account for the measured deposition rates. It has been speculated that $\text{SiH}_3$ adsorption occurs on dangling bonds created on the otherwise H-terminated growth surface through abstraction of surface hydrogen by H atoms or $\text{SiH}_3$ radicals, possibly after going through a physisorbed state where $\text{SiH}_3$ is weakly bonded to the surface [7–12]. Keudell and Abelson [13] proposed an alternative reaction pathway for $\text{SiH}_3$ adsorption on hydrogenated silicon surfaces through direct insertion of $\text{SiH}_3$ radicals into surface Si–Si bonds [13]; this proposal was based on IR spectroscopic studies of interactions with a-Si:H film surfaces of $\text{SiH}_3$ generated by a remote $\text{SiH}_4$ plasma source [13]. In addition to these experimental studies, we examined theoretically the abstraction by $\text{SiH}_3$ of surface hydrogen from the dimerized Si(001) surface terminated by one monolayer of H atoms [14]; this surface structure will be denoted henceforth by Si(001)-(2 × 1):H. We established that this reaction occurs through an Eley–Rideal mechanism and is characterized by a low thermal activation energy barrier of 0.09 eV [14]. Nevertheless, the question remains if other mechanisms of $\text{SiH}_3$ adsorption are operative on the Si(001)-(2 × 1):H surface.

The purpose of this Letter is to study quantitatively the mechanism and energetics of $\text{SiH}_3$ adsorption on the Si(001)-(2 × 1):H surface through insertion reactions that involve breaking of the Si–Si surface dimer bond. First-principles calculations of such radical–surface reactions are reported together with analysis based on classical MD simulations. Our theoretical calculations reveal that $\text{SiH}_3$ adsorbs on the surface dissociatively by breaking the surface Si–Si dimer bond and, subsequently, transferring one of its H atoms to the H-terminated surface. As a result, two surface dihydride ($\text{SiH}_2$) species are generated. In conjunction with other reaction mechanisms that can form $\text{SiH}_2$ species on the growth surface of a-Si:H films, this insertion reaction can explain the high concentrations of surface dihydride species measured on the surfaces of plasma-deposited films [15,16].

## 2. Computational methods

Quantitative study of the interactions of the $\text{SiH}_3$ radical with the Si(001)-(2 × 1):H surface and the corresponding configuration structure and energetics was based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations employing a cluster model. For the DFT calculations, the B3LYP functional was used with the CEP-31G(d) basis set on Si and the 6-31G basis set on H [17]. The calculations were carried out with GAUSSIAN94. The $\text{Si}1\text{s}^22\text{s}^22\text{p}^6$ core was represented by an effective core potential [17]. In the calculations reported here, the cluster that was used to model the Si(001) surface consisted of two, four, two, and one Si atoms in the top, second, third, and fourth layers from the surface, respectively. The Si atoms in the second through the fourth layers were kept at the bulk lattice spacing, while the positions of the two top-layer atoms were optimized to allow for the $(2 \times 1)$ dimer reconstruction. The Si–Si bonds between bulk Si atoms and Si atoms in the second, third, and fourth layers were replaced by Si–H bonds with bond lengths of $1.50\ \text{\AA}$ ($\text{Si}_9\text{H}_{12}$ cluster model). A convergence test indicated that increasing the cluster size up to 23 Si atoms ($\text{Si}_{23}\text{H}_{24}$ cluster model) does not have any significant effect on the calculated energetics: the corresponding changes in the computed energies of two structurally optimized configurations were 0.1 eV or lower (configurations B and C below).

In addition to the DFT calculations, the dynamics of the radical’s interactions with the surface was studied in detail through MD simulations based on numerical integration of the classical equations of motion [4,14,18–20]. In the MD simulations, the interatomic interactions were described by an extended Tersoff potential [21–25], which has been tested extensively for assessing its quantitative capabilities in comparison with ab initio DFT calculations [4,14,18–20]. The generated classical MD trajectories were analyzed in detail to obtain the energetics of the dissociative adsorption reaction path along an appropriately defined reaction coordinate. Specifically, a conjugate gradient minimization algorithm was employed to map the dynamical system periodically onto its local minimum energy configuration (quenching) during the course of the insertion reaction, thus generating the corresponding energy landscape. In general, the reaction analysis presented in this Letter follows the same combined DFT/MD computational methodology that was used in our study of the Eley–Rideal abstraction of surface H by $\text{SiH}_3$ from the Si(001)-(2 × 1):H surface [14].

### 3. Reaction mechanism and energetics

In the DFT calculations, the geometry of the atomic configuration was optimized as the radical was directed toward the surface and after adsorbing on the surface. The radical was directed at the center of the Si-Si dimer bond at normal incidence with the Si atom of the radical oriented toward the surface (Si-down orientation). The radical inserts between the dimer atoms by breaking the Si-Si dimer bond and forming partial bonds with both atoms of the dimer through a transition-state complex (A) that involves a weak dimer bond and weak bonds between the radical's Si atom and the Si atoms of the surface dimer. In the resulting configuration (B), there are three electrons coupled into a doublet state (allylic resonance). These three electrons occupy the radical orbital of the $\text{SiH}_3$ and the two orbitals corresponding to the electrons of the dimer bond pair; thus, the radical's Si atom forms two partial bonds with the dimer Si atoms and may be formally thought of as fivefold coordinated. The radical insertion is exothermic with a small energy release of 0.04 eV and an activation energy barrier of 0.69 eV. An additional 0.72 eV is released when the $\text{SiH}_3$ is allowed to bond to just one of the Si atoms of the original dimer (C), thus eliminating the coordination defect. At this state, an H atom can be transferred from the radical to the Si atom of the original dimer that is not bonded with the radical (E). This reaction step involves a transition state (D) where the H that is transferred is bonded weakly with both the radical and the Si atom of the broken dimer. H transfer is endothermic with an energy increase of 0.16 eV and an activation energy barrier of 0.37 eV. In the resulting configuration, the radical's Si is threefold coordinated, while both surface Si atoms that formed the original surface dimer are fourfold coordinated. The energies of the configurations shown in Fig. 1 are summarized in Table 1. The overall reaction can be represented as

$$
\mathrm{SiH}_{3(\mathrm{~g})}+\mathrm{HSi}-\mathrm{SiH}_{(\mathrm{s})} \rightarrow \mathrm{HSi}-\mathrm{SiH}_{2(\mathrm{~s})}+\mathrm{SiH}_{2(\mathrm{~s})},
\tag{1}
$$

where the subscripts (g) and (s) are used to denote gas-phase and surface species, respectively. The reaction product in Eq. (1) implies the generation of two surface dihydride species by radical insertion between the dimer atoms and H transfer to one of these atoms.

The above mechanism of dissociative adsorption, Fig. 1, has been observed directly in MD simulations of $\text{SiH}_3$ impingement on Si(001)-(2 × 1):H. In these targeted MD simulations, the radical was impinged on the surface at the center of the dimer bond and in the Si-down molecular orientation. The kinetic energy of the impinging radical corresponded to the temperature of the substrate, which was maintained at 300 K by rescaling of the atomic velocities. An additional reaction step that was observed in the MD simulations was the bonding of the radical's Si with the surface Si atom to which the H atom was transferred: these Si atoms become fourfold and fivefold coordinated, respectively; the corresponding reaction product can be denoted by $\mathrm{HSi-SiH_2-SiH_{2(s)}}$. According to the DFT calculations, however, such a Si-Si bond formation does not lead to further lowering of the energy, i.e., the formation of the overcoordination defect is energetically more costly than leaving the radical's Si undercoordinated. More importantly, in either case the reaction led to formation of two surface dihydride species. Another dynamical feature that was observed in the MD trajectories was the hopping of the adsorbed $\text{SiH}_3$ radical from one atom of the broken dimer to the other prior to the H transfer step. Specifically, the radical hops between two equivalent states resembling configuration C of Fig. 1, through a transition state resembling configuration B of Fig. 1. The corresponding structures for the equilibrium and saddle-point configurations obtained through MD are fully consistent with the ones obtained through the DFT calculations, which are shown in Fig. 1. ## 4. Conclusions
Adsorption of the silyl radical on the H-terminated dimerized $\text{Si(001)}$ surface by insertion reactions has been studied theoretically. A dissociative adsorption reaction has been identified combining first-principles DFT calculations with analysis of classical MD trajectories. Specifically, $\text{SiH}_3$ has been found to adsorb dissociatively on $\text{Si(001)-(2} \times 1\text{):H}$ when impinged on the surface at the center of the $\text{Si-Si}$ dimer bond with the Si atom of the radical oriented closest to the surface. The adsorption mechanism involves breaking of the surface $\text{Si-Si}$ dimer bond, transfer of an H atom from the radical to one of the Si atoms of the original surface dimer, and formation of a $\text{Si-Si}$ bond between the radical's Si atom and one of the Si atoms of the broken dimer. The hydrogen transfer and $\text{Si-Si}$ bond formation steps of this reaction result in generation of two surface dihydride species, which provides a possible explanation for the large proportions of surface dihydride species that have been measured on plasma-deposited a-Si:H films. The energy barrier associated with breaking the $\text{Si-Si}$ dimer bond is about $0.7\ \text{eV}$, which suggests that this radical insertion reaction occurs at lower rates than the surface H abstraction by the radical; the barrier for the latter reaction is only $0.09\ \text{eV}$. Additional theoretical quantitative analyses of adsorption mechanisms and energetics of silyl and silylene radicals on the pristine and hydrogen-terminated dimerized $\text{Si(001)}$ surfaces will be reported in forthcoming publications.
