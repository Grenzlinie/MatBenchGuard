# Validity of the broken-bond model for the $DX$ center in GaAs

Mineo Saito
NEC Scientific Information System Development, Limited, 34 Miyukigaoka, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305, Japan

Atsushi Oshiyama and Osamu Sugino
Microelectronics Research Laboratories, NEC Corporation, 34 Miyukigaoka, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305, Japan

(Received 26 March 1992)

The validity of the broken-bond model for the $DX$ center is examined by performing supercell calculations within the local-density approximation for column-IV donors in GaAs. We confirm that the broken-bond geometry is the most stable among the atomic structures accompanied with large lattice relaxation. The calculated bond length between the dopant and the As atoms and frequency of the local vibrational mode (LVM) in the broken-bond geometry agree with extended-x-ray absorption-fine-structure and Fourier-transform-infrared-absorption measurements, respectively, supporting the broken-bond model for the $DX$ center. Furthermore we predict from the present calculation that there exists another infrared light active LVM with $274\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in the broken-bond geometry. Detection of this mode would be clear evidence for the broken-bond model of the $DX$ center.

Since Lang and Logan $^{1}$ found a deep center ($DX$ center) with persistent photoconductance in Te-doped $\mathrm{Al}_{x}\mathrm{Ga}_{1-x}\mathrm{As}$ alloys, an accumulation of studies $^{2}$ has clarified interesting features of the bistability between shallow and deep levels induced by column-IV or -VI donors in $\mathrm{Al}_{x}\mathrm{Ga}_{1-x}\mathrm{As}$ and in GaAs. The deep level appears in GaAs under hydrostatic pressure or in $\mathrm{Al}_{x}\mathrm{Ga}_{1-x}\mathrm{As}$ for $x\geq0.22$, while the shallow level is energetically lower in other cases. It is confirmed $^{2}$ that the $DX$ center arises from the donor impurity by itself: Both shallow and deep levels originate from the donor impurity alone with different geometries. As for the geometry of the shallow level, it is accepted that the donor atom is located at the substitutional site. On the other hand, the clarification of the geometry for the $DX$ center is still insufficient. Among several geometries $^{2}$ proposed for the $DX$ center, the broken-bond model by Chadi and Chang $^{3}$ (CC) from a calculation based on the local-density approximation (LDA) is shown to be consistent with various experiments. In this model, the column-IV donor atom is displaced along the $[\overline{1}\overline{1}\overline{1}]$ axis from the substitutional site and thus the atom is bonded to the three As atoms. CC have shown that the $DX$ center is negatively charged (a negative-$U$ center): The broken-bond geometry is stable only when the center is negatively charged. After CC's work, larger sizes of LDA calculations were performed by two groups. Dabrowski, Scheffler, and Strehlow's results $^{4}$ supported the broken-bond model. On the contrary, Yamaguchi, Shiraishi, and Ohno $^{5}$ concluded from their calculational results that the $DX$ center is accompanied with small lattice relaxation: The donor atom is located at the substitutional site. Thus, the validity of the broken-bond model is still controversial from the theoretical viewpoint. Experimentally a recent Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) light absorption measurement $^{6}$ suggests the negative-$U$ character of the $DX$ center, but at the same time, it shows that the local vibrational frequency of the $DX$ center is close to that of the shallow donor: The absolute value of the difference in frequency is only 9 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. It is not clear whether the broken-bond model is consistent with this experimental result for the similar frequencies between the shallow and $DX$ levels. Furthermore extended-x-ray absorption-fine-structure (EXAFS) measurements for the Se donor $^{7}$ and for the Sn donor $^{8}$ indicate that the bond lengths from the donor atom to the nearest As atom in the shallow donor and the $DX$ center are identical to each other. These results may suggest that the $DX$ center is not accompanied with large lattice relaxation as in the broken-bond model.

In this Rapid Communication, we perform $ab$ initio total-energy calculations within the LDA for the Si-, Ge-, and Sn-doped GaAs, in order to investigate the validity of the broken-bond model for the $DX$ center in GaAs. We confirm that the broken-bond geometry is the most stable among the atomic structures accompanied with large lattice relaxation. Determination of the total-energy difference between the broken-bond and the substitutional geometries is not performed since that task seems to require the treatment beyond the present approximation (supercell approximation and LDA). $^{9}$ Instead, we find unexpected features of the broken-bond geometry. First, the bond lengths between the dopant and the nearest-neighbor As atoms in the broken-bond and substitutional geometries are almost identical to each other. Thus, the result of the EXAFS observations of the identical bond length in the shallow and deep levels is not inconsistent with the broken-bond model. Second, the vibrational frequencies of the Si-As bond-stretching modes are close to each other between the substitutional and the broken-bond geometries, indicating that the broken-bond model is consistent with the FTIR result. Furthermore, we find another low-frequency ($274\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$) infrared-active vibrational mode of the $a_{1}$ representation in the broken-bond geometry. The detection would be clear evidence for the broken-bond geometry of the $DX$ center.

Each cell in the supercell model contains 32 atoms and 4 *k* points are used as sample points for the Brillouin-zone integration. The shape of the cell is a rectangular parallelepiped and the ratio in length for the three sides is 1:1:√2. The cutoff energy for the plane-wave basis set is taken to be 8 Ry for most of the calculations and 12 Ry for calculating the frequencies of local vibrational modes. In optimizing geometries, we relax all the atoms in the cell under no restriction of symmetry, in order for the force on each atom to become close to zero: The deviation arising from the remaining nonzero forces after the optimization is less than 0.005 Å for each bond length. The accuracy of the present calculation has been examined by varying calculational parameters: e.g., as for the size of the supercell, we have compared the optimized negatively charged broken-bond geometries with use of the 32- and 54-atom supercell models, and found that the differences in determined bond lengths between the two models are less than 0.01 Å. Details of the examination of the accuracy in the present calculation is reported elsewhere.⁹

In this work, we optimize the atomic structures of the substitutional geometry inducing the shallow level and of geometries accompanied with large lattice relaxation. In optimizing geometry accompanied with large lattice relaxation, we assume that the impurity is negatively charged. On the other hand, in obtaining the optimized substitutional geometry and the local-vibrational-mode frequency, the positive charge state is taken instead of the neutral state, since the delocalized one-electron wave function of the shallow level is difficult to be taken into account because of the finite size of the supercell. Since the wave function for the shallow level is very delocalized in the real situation, the atomic structure around the donor atom is expected to be insensitive to the charge state and thus it is reasonable to take the positive charge state in the supercell calculation.

We start with calculated total energies of optimized atomic structures of Si impurity accompanied with large lattice relaxation. We have examined a variety of possible geometries with large lattice relaxation (Fig. 1) and have found that the broken-bond geometry (SiᵢV_Ga) is the most stable among the obtained atomic geometries. We have imposed no restriction of symmetry to obtain the optimized SiᵢV_Ga geometry. Yet the calculated bond lengths of the three Si-As bonds are identical to each other within the numerical accuracy. We thus conclude that this broken-bond geometry holds the C₃ᵥ symmetry as was originally assumed by CC. The details of the optimized structure are shown below. In the second most stable structure, Si and As atoms exchange their positions (Si_AsAs_Ga) and thus the As atom is bonded to the Si and three As atoms (Fig. 1). It is found that one of these As bond lengths is 2.55 Å and the other two are 2.69 Å, while the Si-As bond length is 2.46 Å which is close to the crystal Ga-As bond length (2.45 Å). The longer bond lengths suggest the weak As-As bonds which raise the total energy. Actually the calculated total energy of Si_AsAs_Ga is found to be 0.6 eV higher than that of SiᵢV_Ga. In the other metastable geometries, the Ga atom at the second-nearest site from the substitutional site is distorted and becomes threefold coordinated (GaᵢV_Ga). As Fig. 1 shows, this Ga atom is bonded to three kinds of As atoms: the first-, the third-, and the fifth-nearest-neighbor As atoms from the substitutional site (the Ga atom is bonded to two equivalent third-neighbor As atoms). Thus there exist three types of the breaking of the bond between the Ga and As atoms. We find that all the total energies for the three geometries are 0.7-0.9 eV higher than that of SiᵢV_Ga. Therefore we conclude that the Si broken-bond model (SiᵢV_Ga) is the most hopeful candidate for the DX center among the large lattice relaxation models.

A similar situation is found for the pair of the vacancy and the As substitutional impurity in Si: The threefold coordinated As is located near the substitutional site and the binding energy of the As-V pair is large. $^{12}$ We find from the fully relaxed geometry optimization, that the bond length (2.42 Å) in the broken-bond geometry is identical to that (2.43 Å) in the substitutional geometry within an accuracy of 0.01 Å. This interesting feature of the identical bond length is also found for the Ge and Sn donors. As is seen from Table I, the bond lengths are longer as the atomic number becomes large. The order of the bond lengths is consistent with that of the covalent-bond radius of the donor atoms: 1.17, 1.22, and 1.40 Å (Ref. 13) for Si, Ge, and Sn atoms, respectively. As for the Sn donor, the nearest-neighbor peak in the radial distribution function in the EXAFS exeriment indicates 2.58 Å of the Sn-As bond length for both the $D X$ and shallow levels. This experimental result is in a fairly good agreement with our calculated values (Table I). The broken-bond model is thus consistent with the observed nearest-neighbor peak in the EXAFS experiment. It is noted, however, that the broken-bond model is not consistent with the experiment as for the coordination number of Sn: Hayes et al. concluded from analysis of EXAFS data that the coordination number of the Sn atom is 4. In spite of this disagreement, we emphasize that the observed identical location of the nearest-neighbor peak for the shallow and $D X$ levels cannot exclude the broken-bond model from the candidates of the $D X$ center.

Now we calculate frequencies of local vibrational modes for Si impurity by considering displacements of the Si and the nearest As atoms. For the Si-As bond stretching $t_{2}$ mode in the substitutional geometry with $T_{d}$ symmetry, the calculated frequency $v(T_{d})$ is $370 ~cm^{-1}$ whichfairly agrees with the experimental value $(384 ~cm^{-1})$ (Ref. 6) for the shallow impurity. On the other hand, the calculated frequency $v(C_{3 v})$ of the $e$ mode (Fig. 2) in the broken-bond geometry is $392 ~cm^{-1}$ . The calculated ratio $R=v(T_{d}) / v(C_{3 v})$ is 0.94. On the other hand, the corresponding experimental ratio of the shallow level to the $D X$  level in $1.02.^{6}$ This agreement in the ratio $R$ between experiment and theory indicates that the experimentally detected peak due to the $D X$ center is the $e$ mode of the theoretically determined broken-bond structure. There is a slight difference in $R$ between the present calculation(0.94) and the FTIR experiment (1.02). This difference may become small when the displacement of the second- neighbor $Ga$ atoms is considered.

The finding that the two vibrational frequencies are close to each other is interpreted based on the following simplified model. Since the bond-bending force is much smaller than the bond-stretching force in tetrahedrally bonded materials, it is reasonable to consider only the Si- As bond-stretching force. In this case, the ratio for thetwo frequencies is given by a valence force field (VFF)model: $^{14}$
$$R(\mathrm{VFF})=\left[k\left(1+\frac{4}{3} M\right) / k^{\prime}\left(1+\frac{3}{2} M \sin ^{2} \beta\right)\right]^{1 / 2}, \quad(1)$$
where $k$ and $k^{\prime}$ are the force constants for the substitutional and broken-bond geometries, $M$ is the ratio in the atomic mass between As and $Si$ , and $\beta$ is the angle between the [111] and the Si-As bond axes in the broken- bond geometry ( $\beta=104^{\circ}$ in the present case). We assume that $k=k^{\prime}$ because the identical Si-As bond length between the two geometries suggests the similar bond- stretching forces for the geometries. Then we obtain the value of 0.98 for $R(VFF)$ . Therefore the surprisingly similar vibrational frequencies are attributed to the two features of the Si-As bonding in the broken-bond geometry: The Si-As bond lengths in the substitutional and broken-bond geometries are close to each other andthe hybridization in the $Si$ atom is something between $sp^{2}$  and $s p^{3}(\beta=104^{\circ})$ .

We also calculate the frequency of the $a_{1}$ mode (Fig. 2) of the broken-bond geometry and find that the frequency is $274 ~cm^{-1}$ . Since this value is below the LO phonon frequency at the $\Gamma$ point $(293 ~cm^{-1}),^{15}$ the calculated value might be accompanied with some deviation: The vibrational mode is expected to be delocalized. Yet our calculation surely indicates that there exists the vibrational mode in the low-frequency region around $274 ~cm^{-1}$ . If the atomic structure of the broken-bond geometry is planar (the hybridization in the $Si$ atom is $s p^{2}$ ), this mode is infrared inactive, since the local structure consisting of $Si$  and the nearest three As atoms has $D_{3 h}$ symmetry (in this case, the $a_{1}^{\prime}$ mode which corresponds to the $a_{1}$ mode in the C3, symmetry is infrared inactive). However, the optimized geometry in this work shows that the symmetry of the local structure is lowered to $C_{3 v}$ , indicating that the low-frequency mode is detectable through the infrared light absorption measurement ( $a_{1}$ modes in the $C_{3 v}$ symmetry are infrared active). In summary, we performed total-energy first-principles calculations in order to study the validity of the broken-bond model. The broken-bond geometry, $\mathrm{Si}_{I} \mathrm{~V}_{\mathrm{Ga}}$, is found to be the most stable among the geometries with large lattice relaxation. The experimentally detected vibrational frequency for the $DX$ center is well explained in terms of this broken-bond model. Thus we conclude that the broken-bond model, $\mathrm{Si}_{I} \mathrm{~V}_{\mathrm{Ga}}$, is a strong candidate for the $DX$ center. We predict from the present calculation that there exists a low-frequency mode $(274 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1})$ in the broken-bond geometry. Detection of this mode by the infrared light absorption measurement would be clear evidence for the broken-bond structure of the $DX$ center.

Note added. After submission of the present paper, we have noticed the publication by Jones and Öberg. $^{16}$ They used the cluster model and calculated the frequency of the local vibrational mode of the Si impurity in GaAs. They estimated the frequency of the $e$ mode in the broken-bond geometry to be very high $(432 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1})$ compared with the value of the present work. This high frequency presumably originated from the fact that the Si-As bond length optimized by them is considerably short $(2.33 \AA)$ compared with the present value $(2.42 \AA)$.
