# The $[Si(S_2O_7)_3]^{2-}$ Anion: A First Example of Octahedral Silicon Coordination by Three Chelating Inorganic Ligands

Christian Logemann, Thorsten Klüner, and Mathias S. Wickleder*[a]

Tetravalent silicon surrounded in an octahedral environment by oxygen atoms is not frequently observed, but several compounds have been reported nevertheless. The typical textbook example is the complex $[Si(cat.)_3]^{2-}$ in which cat. is 1,2-dihydroxybenzene (catechol). The complex forms when 1,2-dihydroxybenzene reacts with $SiO_2.^{[1]}$ The silicon atom is coordinated by three chelating diolate ligands; this motif has varied with several derivatives of 1,2-dihydroxybenzene. $^{[2]}$ Subsequently, octahedral oxygen coordination has also been achieved with carboxylic acids, for example, citric acid, malic acid, and salicylic acid. $^{[3]}$ Furthermore, mixedligand complexes and neutral species exhibiting the $[SiO_6]$ skeleton have been reported. $^{[4]}$ Compared with these numerous examples of organic ligands, the reports on purely inorganic compounds with octahedral silicon coordination are scarce. Most of the examples are complex silicates that originate from high-pressure, solid-state reactions. $^{[5]}$ A remarkable example is the sodium silicate $Na_2[Si(Si_2O_7)]$ with part of the silicon atoms surrounded by six monodentate disilicate groups. $^{[6]}$ According to the formulation $_{\infty}^{3}[Si(Si_2O_7)_{6/6}]^{2-}$, the $[SiO_6]$ octahedra and the disilicate anions are linked to a three-dimensional network. A similar structural feature is found for the hydrogenphosphate complex $Cs_2[Si(HP_2O_7)_2].^{[7]}$ However, this compound has been prepared under ambient conditions and exhibits silicon atoms surrounded by two chelating and two monodentate $HP_2O_7^{3-}$ ions leading to a one-dimensional heteropolyanion according to $_{\infty}^{1}[P(Si_2O_7)_{4/2}]^{2-}$.

Herein, we present the first example of a complex silicate anion with octahedral silicon coordination by three chelating inorganic ligands, namely, three disulfate groups (Figure 1). The tris(disulfato)silicate anion $[Si(S_2O_7)_3]^{2-}$ is observed in the crystal structures of $A_2[Si(S_2O_7)_3]$ (A=Na, Cs), which are formed in the reaction of $SiCl_4$ with oleum at elevated temperatures in the presence of $A_2SO_4$ (A=Na, Cs). A potassium compound of the same composition was reported by Thilo and Winkler in $1969.^{[8]}$ The compound was obtained by the reaction of $K_2SiO_3$ with $SO_3$, but unfortunately no structure determination was performed. However, the authors suggested that a complex anion with octahedrally coordinated silicon may have formed. It is very likely that this compound adopts one of the structures presented herein.

Whereas the complex silicate anions in both compounds show nearly identical structural features, both crystal structures exhibit different symmetry due to the different arrangements of the $[Si(S_2O_7)_3]^{2-}$ anions (Figure 2). This can be attributed to the coordination requirement of the alkaline metal ions. In the sodium silicate both $Na^+$ ions are in a distorted octahedral coordination with Na–O distances ranging from 228.9 to 268.9 pm. The cesium compound shows two crystallographically different $Cs^+$ ions in ten- and elevenfold coordination, respectively, with the Cs–O distances of 302.4–379.5 pm

Compared with the observed distances within the $[SiO_6]$ moiety of the $[Si(S_2O_7)_3]^{2-}$ anion, the calculated distances (on the PBE0/cc-pVDZ level of theory) are remarkably long and range from 180.2 to 181.1 pm. This deviation from the experimental values might be due to the absence of the cation in our calculations; crystal-packing effects, which are not taken into account; or an insufficient treatment of electron correlation on the PBE0 level of theory. Future studies on the MP-2/cc-pVTZ level of theory will shed light on this question. On the other hand, the observed O-Si-O-angles are well reproduced and show values between 88.4 and 91.3°. The analysis of the effective charges of the atoms reveals remarkable covalent contributions to the Si–O bond. Within a Mulliken population analysis, the silicon atom shows an effective charge of +0.95, whereas the surrounding oxygen atoms have charges between $-$0.45 and $-$0.48. The covalent character of the Si–O bond is further corroborated by inspection of the contributing molecular orbitals. It turns out that the HOMO, containing a significant contribution of silicon atomic orbitals (AOs), is composed of $\sigma$ bonds formed by corresponding linear combinations with oxygen p AOs.

Currently we are investigating the bonding situation in this unique silicate anion in more detail and in comparison with analogous anions of other Group 14 elements, namely, $[Ge(S_2O_7)_3]^{2-}$ and $[Sn(S_2O_7)_3]^{2-}$, which we were able to prepare recently.$^{[10]}$ Furthermore, complementary characterizations are in progress.

## Experimental Section

Synthesis: $SiCl_4$ (170 mg, 1 mmol), oleum (1 mL, 65% $SO_3$), $Na_2SO_4$ (142 mg, 1 mmol), and $Cs_2SO_4$ (362 mg, 1 mmol) were filled in glass tubes ($d=16$ mm; $l=300$ mm). The tubes were sealed, placed in an oven, and heated to $250^\circ C$. After slow cooling $(1.8^\circ Ch^{-1})$, colorless and moisture-sensitive single crystals are obtained. *Caution!* During the reaction and even after cooling down to room temperature the glass tubes are under pressure. The tubes have to be protected with an explosion shield during the reaction and should be cooled with liquid nitrogen before opening.

X-ray structure determination: Single crystals of both compounds were selected under protecting oil and transferred onto a single-crystal diffractometer (BRUKER APEX II and IPDS I). Collections of intensity data were performed at 153 K. Structure solutions and refinements were done with the SHELX program package.$^{[9]}$

Crystallographic data for $Na_2[Si(S_2O_7)_3]$: Orthorhombic; space group $P2_12_12_1$; $a=954.15(5)$, $b=1266.76(7)$, $c=1443.63(8)$ pm; $V=1744.9(2)$ $\AA^3$; $Z=4$; $\rho=2.293$ g cm$^{-3}$; $\mu=10.12$ cm$^{-1}$; $5.34^\circ <2\theta <80.48^\circ$; 69560 measured reflections; 10891 independent reflections; $R_{int}=0.0567$; $R_1=0.0245$; $wR_2=0.0540$ (all data); Flack-x parameter 0.00(2); max./min. residual electron density: 0.733/–0.393.
