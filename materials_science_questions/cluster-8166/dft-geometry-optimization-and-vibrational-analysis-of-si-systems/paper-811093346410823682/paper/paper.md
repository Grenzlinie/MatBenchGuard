# CIRCUMSTELLAR CARBON-CHAIN MOLECULES.
## PREDICTION OF THE INFRARED SPECTRUM OF SiC₄ ★

N. MOAZZEN-AHMADI $^{a}$ and F. ZERBETTO $^{b}$

a Herzberg Institute of Astrophysics, National Research Council of Canada, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada K1A 0R6
b Division of Chemistry, National Research Council of Canada, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada, K1A 0R6

Received 21 August 1989; in final form 3 October 1989

The infrared spectrum of SiC₄, recently discovered in the circumstellar shell of the evolved star IRC+10216, is predicted ab initio at the MP2/6-311G(d) level of theory. A very intense asymmetric stretch is calculated to occur in an astrophysical window.

Carbon clusters have recently been subject of extensive experimental and theoretical investigations because of their relevance in astrochemistry and combustion processes [1]. Despite numerous works carried out in the last few years, basic issues such as the structure of $C_{4}$ and $C_{n}$, with $n \geqslant 6$, are still matter of controversy [2-4].

Very recently, the linear chain molecule $C_{5}$ has been detected [5-8] by infrared spectroscopy both in the laboratory and in the circumstellar shell of carbon star IRC+10216. This represented the first spectroscopic detection of gas phase $C_{5}$, which is now the largest pure carbon molecule to have been studied at high resolution. In December 1988, during the molecular line survey observation toward IRC+10216, Ohishi et al. [9] detected the rotational spectrum of a new carbon chain molecule SiC₄. They suggested that a new series of carbon-chain molecules $SiC_{n}$ may be present in circumstellar and interstellar environments and the study of these molecules ought to elucidate the chemistry of silicon and carbon in such media. A step towards the understanding of the chemistry and dynamics of these atoms would be the detection of the infrared spectrum of the first known molecule containing both Si and C, and the calculation either from spectroscopic data or ab initio of its force field.

In this note, we report ab initio calculations of the structure, force field, the harmonic infrared frequencies and the isotopic shifts of SiC₄ with the purpose of facilitating its IR detection either in the laboratory or in space.

The calculations were performed on an IBM 3090/200VF mainframe, with the GAUSSIAN 86 program [10]. The basis sets used in this work were the 6-311G(d) [11] basis set for the carbon atoms and the (12s, 9p) basis, contracted to [621111, 52111], for the silicon atom [12]. An additional set of d functions with exponent 0.450 was added on the silicon atom. This basis set was chosen because it was originally proposed for Møller-Plesset second-order (MP2) calculations, which is the level of theory used in this paper. All the 98 orbitals were included in the correlation correction to the energy and gradient.

The molecular geometry was optimized constrained to be linear and only the $^{1}\Sigma$ state was considered. The force constants and the infrared intensities were obtained by numerical differentiation of MP2 energy gradient and MP2 dipole moments.

1530 MHz is much closer to the observed $B_0$. The overall good agreement between the calculated and observed rotational constants, and the analogy with $C_5$, seem to exclude different isomers or electronic states than the one reported here.

Upon diagonalization the calculated force field yields no imaginary frequencies, thus the linear structure is a minimum on the potential energy surface of $SiC_4$, and the constrain to linearity was only a mean to save CPU time. In analogy with $C_5$ the calculated IR frequencies and intensities (see table 2) show that only one mode is strongly IR active. The analysis of the normal mode description in Cartesian coordinates shows that it barely involves the silicon atom (coefficient of 0.026). This is hardly a surprise if one considers the force constant matrix (see table 3), since the $SiC_1$ force constant is about half the CC force constants.

Since the MP2 frequencies are usually overesti- mated, a better estimate of the asymmetric stretch frequency can be obtained multiplying the calculated values by 0.93 to yield $2071\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ [13,14]. This frequency occurs in a region where the atmosphere is relatively transparent, a fact that ought to enable its experimental observation in space. The second most intense mode, $\omega_3$, as is also the case for $C_5$ [14], is much weaker than $\omega_1$. It is described as a $SiC_1$ stretch vibrating out of phase with the $C_2C_3$ and $C_3C_4$ stretches.

Comparison of the calculated $SiC_4$ frequencies with those of $C_5$ shows that the former are consistently lower. In particular the lowest bending frequency is calculated to be about 60% of its $C_5$ counterpart. Since isotopic substitution of $^{28}Si$ barely affects it (see table 4), we ascribe its lower frequency to smaller bending constants; it is unfortunate that the force constants of $C_5$ have not been published, so that a comparison is not possible. It is expected that this motion is more anharmonic than the corresponding motion for $C_5$ [15].

The calculated isotopic shifts upon $^{13}C$ substitution of $^{12}C$ and $^{30}Si$ substitution of $^{28}Si$ are given in table 4. The resulting isotopic shifts for the two strongest IR active modes are almost all larger than the resolution attainable in a laboratory matrix isolation experiment and should therefore be of help in the identification of such spectra.

In summary, we have presented calculations, at electron correlation level, of the structural parameter and the harmonic frequencies of the newly discovered carbon chain molecule $SiC_4$. These calculations find $SiC_4$ to be, in the lowest $^1\Sigma$ state, linear and predict that a very intense CC asymmetric stretch occurs in an astrophysical window.
