# BOND ANGLE DISTORTIONS AND HYDROGENATION AT THE Si / a-SiN:H INTERFACE

Etienne BUSTARRET*

LEPES-CNRS (associated to Université Joseph Fourier), 166X, 38042 Grenoble, France.

The local elastic potential fluctuations associated with bond angle ($\theta_{SiSiSi},\theta_{SiSiN},\theta_{NSiN}$) distortions in the Silicon-centered tetrahedra of the interfaces between c-Si or a-Si:H and a-Si₃N₄:H materials are evaluated within the Keating formalism. The particularities of the results for hydrogenated sites are in excellent agreement with some general features of the physico-chemistry of both the a-Si:H / a-Si₃N₄:H interface and the Si-rich a-SiₓNᵧH_z alloys.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

The rapidly increasing technological importance of the heterojunction between low temperature deposited silicon nitrides and either crystalline or hydrogenated amorphous silicon has prompted a large number of experimental studies of the electronic and physico-chemical properties of this interface, either isolated or in multilayers. Eventhough the results are preparation-dependent, some features are now widely accepted : in particular, the interface is considered to be atomically abrupt¹,², although there is some evidence for a tail³ of a few at.% Nitrogen in a-Si:H and for a N-H-rich sublayer³,⁴ on the other side of a-Si:H on a-Si₃N₄:H (bottom nitride) systems. A graded N-rich (N-H-rich ?) region was also observed⁵ in the nitride layer of a-Si₃N₄:H on a-Si:H (top nitride) interfaces. Finally, the fast electronic states at the interface are thought to be locatedin the a-Si:H layer, with a higher density in the top nitride than in the bottom nitride configuration⁶. To understand better this marked dependence of the interface properties on the order in which the layers are grown, we focus here on the local interplay between the hydrogenation and the angular distortions of various Si-centered tetrahedra.

## 2. DEFORMATION ENERGIES OF Si TETRAHEDRA

In the classical picture derived from Keating's work⁷, the deformation energy of a bond between atoms i and j is composed of a stretching term $V_{ij}^r \approx \frac{3}{4} \alpha_K^{ij}(\Delta r_{ij})^2$ and of a sum of 6 bending terms $V_{jik}^\theta$ and $V_{ijl}^\theta$ (k and l refer to any of the other first neighbors of atoms i and j respectively). In silicon nitrides, the variations $\Delta r_{ij}$ of the bond lengths are of the order of 1% while those of bond angles reach 10%, so that $V_{ij}^r$ can be neglected, eventhough $\alpha_K^{ij}$ is greater than the bending parameter $\beta_K^{ij}$ (typical values are given in Table 1). The deformation potential $V_{ij}^\theta$ seen on site i by one electron of the i-j bond may thus be written as

$$
V_{ij}^\theta =\frac{3}{16} \sum_{k} \beta_K^{jik} r_{ij} r_{ik}\ (\cos\theta_{jik} - \cos\theta^o_{jik}\ )^2 \ (1)
$$

where $\theta_{jik}$ is the angle between the i-j and i-k bonds ($\cos\ \theta^o_{jik}= - \frac{1}{3}$ ). In order to reduce the number of independent angular variables, one may assume an average "cylindrical symmetry" of the sites which leads to the following geometrical relationships : for Si ABBB sites, $3\ \sin^2\theta_{ASiB} = 2\ (1 - \cos\theta_{BSiB})$, while for Si ABCC sites :

$$
\cos\theta_{ASiC}= \cos\theta_{BSiC}\cos\theta_{ASiB}\pm\sin\theta_{ASiB}\sin\theta_{BSiC}\sin\frac{\theta_{CSiC}}{2}
$$

For Si AABB sites, the expressions will depend on the angle between the SiAA and the SiBB planes. For the two limiting cases of perpendicular (+) and parallel (=) orientations, one gets $\cos\theta_{ASiB} = \cos\frac{\theta_{ASiA}}{2}\cos\frac{\theta_{BSiB}}{2}\ (+)$

or $2\ \theta_{ASiB}+ \theta_{ASiA} + \theta_{BSiB} = 360^°\ (=)^{14}$.

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Despite its crudeness, this approach enabled us to calculate $V^\theta$ via eq. (1) for all distorted Si-centered tetrahedra present at the interface. We compare for instance on fig. 1 these $V^\theta$ values in sites involving at least two Si-Si bonds, as a function of the mean bond angle $\theta_{SiSiSi}$. The variations of $V^\theta$ in the Si HSi site given elsewhere⁸ superimpose with those calculated for Si NSi₃ at < 109.5°, and with those of Si Si4 at greater angles. A plot quite similar to that of fig. 1 could of course be drawn for the Si N₄, Si N₃Si and Si HN₂Si configurations, with higher energy values because of the stronger rigidity of the $\theta_{NSiN}$ angle (see Table 1). To first order, the assymetry of the curves is partly due to the built-in anharmonicity of the Keating model and partly to shortcomings of our cylindrical symmetry hypothesis. The most unexpected result is that the elastic deformation energy of the Si HNSi₂ site is minimum for $\theta_{SiSiSi} = 101.5^\circ$), in contrast with the minimum at the regular tetrahedron value of 109.5° obtained for the unhydrogenated and Si HSi₃ and Si HN₃ units. This may explain the experimental evidence from infrared spectroscopy for such monohydrogenated sites to be dominant at the bottom nitride interface⁴ or in Si-rich a-SiN:H films⁹,¹⁰ where two out of three Si neighbors of any Nitrogen atom are monohydrogenated⁹.

At intermediate interplane angles, the deformation potential will have a minimum for $\theta_{ASiB} < \theta^{°}_{ASiB}$ , again in agreement with the presence of dihydrogenated units at the a-Si:H on a-$Si_3N_4$:H interfaces as well as at internal surfaces in a-Si:H.

The efficiency of these hydrogenated sites as stress relievers at the interface is further attested by the a-Si:H layer thickness-dependence of the shape of the TO-like Raman peak observed in a-Si:H / a-SiN:H multilayers. More quantitatively, the $\theta_{SiSiSi}$ bond angle distribution in very thin layers ($7\text{\AA}$) is about twice broader than that of the bulk. In order to interprete the effects of substrate nature¹³ and growth sequence order on the electrical properties of the Si / a-$Si_3N_4$:H interface, we suggest therefore that the ability of both the Si-H and Si-Si bonds in strongly distorted sites to trap positive charges be investigated in some detail.
