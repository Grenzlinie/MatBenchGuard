# METASTABLE DEFECTS IN AMORPHOUS SILICON ALLOYS*

D. ADLER, M. E. EBERHART, K. H. JOHNSON, AND S. A. ZYGMUNT

Center for Materials Science and Engineerring, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Cambridge, MA 02139, U.S.A.

The self-consistent-field Xα scattered-wave technique is used to analyze the electronic structure of strained Si-Si bonds in amorphous silicon alloys. A $Si_{2}$ (sat) $_{6}$ cluster, which has been shown to be an effective model for the Si-Si bond, was used to analyze a local region of moderate strain resulting in a bond length of 2.5 Å. The strain was relieved by allowing relaxation from this nearly tetrahedral configuration $(T_{4}')$ to a trigonal configuration $(T_{3})$ at one end of the cluster. Transition-state caculations show that the separation in energy, $\Delta E$, between the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals is a maximum at the $2.5 \AA$ Si-Si distance; as this distance is increased, $\Delta E$ decreases to zero near $3.0 \AA$ and charge leaves the relaxed Si aton, producing a $T_{3}^{+}$defect. These results lead us to suggest a new model for the Staebler-Wronski effect.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Self-consistent-field Xα scattered-wave calculations of $Si_{5}(sat)_{12}$ and $Si_{4}H(sat)_{9}$ clusters, where the symbol (sat) refers to a bond saturator represented by a hydrogen atom placed at the normal Si position $2.35 \AA$ away, has been shown to provide good representations of the band structure of α-Si and α-Si:H, respectively. The results are plotted in Fig. 1. In addition, we have shown that similar calculations of a simple $Si_{2}(sat)_{6}$ cluster $^{2}$ represents a good model for the Si-Si bond in these alloys. Fig. 2a indicates that a nonbonding state in the gap (solid line) appears when the Si-Si bond length is stretched to $3.2 \AA$, while Figs. 2b-2d demonstrate its removal from the gap to deep into the valence band when the two dangling bonds are saturated by a single $H_{2}$ molecule.

Recently, there has been a great interest in the origin of the Staebler-Wronski effect $^{3}$, photo-induced changes in the electronic structure of a-Si:H films. It is clear that this effect is induced by metastable defects in the films $^{4}$, but the nature and origin of these defects is still unknown.

In this paper, we report the results of new SCF-Xα-SW calculations in which we begin with a moderately strained Si-Si bond characterized by a $2.5 \AA$ bond length. We allow the strain to be relieved by an atomic relaxation away from the tetrahdral coordination, $T_{4}$, to a trigonal coordination $T_{3}$ at one end of the cluster. We find that the Si-Si bond length then spontaneously increases to more than $3.0 \AA$, essentially breaking the bond, and that the equivalent of about half of an electronic charge spreads out to the boundary of the cluster. This leaves a $T_{3}^{+}$ center and a neutral dangling bond, $T_{3}^{0}$, with the remaining negative charge delocalized. We discuss possible implications of these results for understanding the origin of the photo-induced changes in a-Si:H and propose a

new model for the nature of the metastable defects.

## 2. CALCULATIONS

We begin with the $Si_{2}$ (sat) $_{6}$ cluster previously investigated, $^{2}$ and allow one of the Si atoms to move away from the other, departing from its tetrahedral environment. Of primary concern is the energy $\Delta E$ between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO). In Fig. 3, we plot $\Delta E$ as a function of the Si-Si bond length. It is evident that $\Delta E$ is a maximum at a bond length near $2.55\AA$. The resulting transfer of charge away from the vicinity of the non-tetrahedrally coordinated Si center, $\Delta q$, as a function of the bond length is plotted in Fig. 4. Clearly, there is little charge transfer until the bond length exceeds $2.9\AA$, beyond which point a $T_{3}^{+}$ center begins to appear.

The physics of these results can be understood by observing the contour plots shown in Fig. 5. Fig. 5a shows the HOMO state, in which the bonding nature of the interaction between the two Si atoms is clear. When an election is promoted from the HOMO to the LUMO states, at an energy cost of $\Delta E$, strong antibonding interactions are induced, as is evident from Fig. 5b. These interactions are then stabilized by an atomic relaxation, in which the Si-Si distance increases to $3.0\AA$. The appropriate contour map is

shown in Fig. 5c, in which it can be noted that netative charge leaves the vicinty of the non-tetrahedrally coordinated Si atom, essentially converting it into a $T_3^+$ center. Thus, the 0.55 eV energy separation $\Delta E$ of Fig. 3 can be regarded as an activation barrier for formation of the $T_3^+$ defect.

## 3. DISCUSSION
We have shown that an excitation from the HOMO to the LUMO states in the vicinity of a strained Si-Si bond induces a bond breaking, driving the reaction:

$$2T_4^{0'} \rightarrow T_3^+ + T_3^0 + e^-, \tag{1}$$

where the elction delocalizes. In a real situation, this election must find a region in which it returns to below $E_F$. An obvious candidate for such a position would be another strained Si-Si bond, in which negative charge could be stabilized by a trigonal distortion, yielding:

$$e^- + 2T_4^{0'} \circ + T_3^- + T_3^0 \tag{2}$$

The net result of reactions (1) and (2) is:

$$4T_4^{0'} \rightarrow T_3^+ + T_3^- + 2T_3^0 \tag{3}$$

The reactions (1) and (2) could both be excited by light, since (1) could represent the trapping of a photo-induced hole with subsequent relaxation,

while (2) clearly is a process involving the trapping of an election. The subsequent local relaxations then metastably return the trapped election below $E_{F}$ and the trapped hole to above $E_{F}$ in such a way that $E_{F}$ would tend to be moved to a region between the relatively closely spaced $T_{3}^{+}$ and $T_{3}^{-}$ levels. Since these are expected to be near mid-gap, $^{8}$ not only would $E_{F}$ move in the manner actually observed $^{5}$ but the experimentally observed increase of $g(E)$ in this region can be understood in a natural way. In addition, the appearance of new, widely separated $T_{3}^{0}$ centers is predicted by (3), yielding a second type of mid-gap state. All of the photo-induced states evolve from the band tails rather than other deep states in this model. If the activation barrier for (2) is also approximately 0.5 eV, the total energy for recapturing state A would be about 1 eV, as observed. $^{7,9}$ Of course, the energetics of reaction (2) remain to be calculated, and we are in the process of quantitatively evaluating the feasibility of the model.

On the other hand, the delocalization of the election that emerged from our analysis of reaction (1) may well be fundamental, reflecting the difficulty in stabilizing charged centers in Si-based alloys. If this turns out to be the case, such centers might still exist in $\alpha$-Si:H, but a more complex structure would be required. However, note that $\alpha$-Si:H alloys are expected to contain significant concentration of 5-fold and 7-fold Si rings. $^{10}$ This fact leads us to suggest that the structures sketched in Fig. 6 may well be present in these alloys in small but significant concentrations. Such structures are known to stabilize charge in C-based molecules," Fig. 6a being the analogue of the carbonium ion and Fig. 6b of a carbanion. $^{12}$ If these structures exist in a-Si:H, they provide a straightforward mechanism for the Staebler-Wronski effect, since the positive center would have a large cross section for election capture and the negative center for hole capture. In both cases, new $T_{3}^{0}$ sites would appear at

## 4. CONCLUSIONS
We have investigated the energetics of strained Si-Si bonds in a-Si alloys, by allowing for atomic relaxation away from tetrahedral coordination after electronic excitations. SCF-Xa-SW calculations indicate that an excited bond with a Si-Si separation greater thatn about 2.5Å will be unstable towards the creation of a $T_{3}^{+}-T_{3}^{0}$ pair, but the excess negative charge will delocalize. These results have led us to suggest two new possibilities for the origin of the Staebler-Wronski effect in a-Si:H films.
