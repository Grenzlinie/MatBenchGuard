# Theoretical Investigation of the Structures and Relative Energles of the Isomer Pairs HSIS/SISH, $HSIS^{+}/SISH^{+}$, and $HSIS^{-}/SISH^{-}$

Pablo J. Bruna $^{\dagger}$ and Friedrich Grein*

Department of Chemistry, University of New Brunswick, Fredericton, New Brunswick, Canada E3B 6E2
(Received: December 11, 1991)

The properties of the title molecules have been investigated by ab initio methods. The geometries were optimized at the MP2 level, and the relevant relative energies were recalculated by using a MP4 expansion and f-polarization functions. $HSiS(X^{2}A')$ is more stable than $SiSH(X^{2}A')$ by 0.18 eV. The order of stabilities is reversed for the positive ions, with $SiSH^{+}(X^{1}A')$ lying 0.58 eV below $HSiS^{+}(X^{1}\Sigma^{+})$. The negative ions have the ground state $X^{1}A'$ for $HSiS^{-}$ and $X^{3}A''$ for $SiSH^{-}$. Rather striking is a high value of 2.16 eV for the electron affinity of HSiS when compared with that of SiS (0.42 eV) or HCO and HCS (~0.4 eV). In addition, $HSiS^{-}$ constitutes the first example in the literature of a $HAB^{-}$ species having three bound electronic states. A computed hydride affinity of 2.46 eV for the process $SiS + H^{-} \to HSiS^{-}(X^{1}A')$ corroborates a recent experimental lower-bound value of 2.24 eV for that quantity. Other properties reported here include vibrational frequencies, dipole moments, dissociation energies, ionization potentials, electron affinities, and heats of formation.

## 1. Introduction
The electronic structure of molecules HCO and $H_2CO$ has been well studied both experimentally and theoretically. The information available on HCS, however, results mainly from ab initio studies. $^{1}$ When carbon is replaced by silicon, the compounds $HSiO, SiOH,^{2}$ and $H_2SiO^3$ have also been investigated by theoretical methods.

Species containing the SiS bond, such as HSiS and $H_2SiS$, received much less attention. Of the SiS compounds, $^{4}$ the positive ions $HSiS^{+}$ and $SiSH^{+}$ were investigated theoretically $^{5,6}$ because of their potential importance in interstellar chemistry. Only recently an experimental paper on $SiSH^{+}$ (the most stable isomer) has appeared, $^{7}$ in which the heat of formation $\Delta H_f$ as well as the S-site SiS proton affinity (PA) and the $SiS^{+}$ hydrogen affinity were reported.

The HSiS isomer was mentioned in an ab initio work devoted to $H_2SiS$ and $HSiSH.^{8}$ Similar to $HSiO,^{2}$ the SCF approximation predicts the existence of two close-lying structures HSiS(A) and HSiS(B), a numerical artifact which should disappear at higher levels of theory. To the best of our knowledge, nothing has been reported on the electronic structure of the isomer SiSH or the negative ions $HSiS^{-}/SiSH^{-}$.

An ongoing project of this laboratory concerns itself with the electronic spectrum of simple SiS species. For instance, the potential curves of several low-lying states of $SiS^{+9}$ as well as the vertical spectra of $H_2SiS,^{10}$ including ionization potentials, have recently been studied. The main goal of this work is to investigate the ground-state properties of the isomer pairs HSiS/SiSH, $HSiS^{+}/SiSH^{+}$, and $HSiS^{-}/SiSH^{-}$.

After the properties of $SiS, SiS^{+}$, and $SiS^{-}$ (section 3) are discussed, the equilibrium geometries of the triatomic compounds will be reported in section 4, their vibrational frequencies in section 5, charge distributions and dipole moments in section 6, and thermochemical data, e.g. isomerization and H-AB dissociation energies, ionization potentials, electron affinities, and heats of formation, in section 7. The most relevant features of this investigation are highlighted in section 8.

## 2. Technical Details
All calculations have been carried out with the GAUSSIAN $86^{11}$ packet of programs. The equilibrium geometries are obtained at both the HF and MP2 levels of calculation. The HF geometries were used as input to calculate the corresponding harmonic vibrational frequencies. All computations use 72 contracted Gaussian functions (basis set I). For both Si and S, Huzinaga's primitive set $11s7p^{12}$ was contracted to 6s4p according to Dun-ning. $^{13}$ Additional functions added to the heavy atoms are semidiffuse p (0.027 for Si and 0.041 for $S^{14}$) and two uncontracted d functions (0.30 and 0.05 for $Si^2$ and 0.54 and 0.15 for $S^{15}$). For hydrogen, Huzinaga's 5s set was contracted to $2s^{12}$ and expanded by semidiffuse s (0.0202) and polarization p functions (0.736). $^{2}$ Relevant total and relative energies were recalculated at an improved level. First, the AO basis set was expanded by adding to basis I polarization functions of f type, with exponents of 0.34 for Si and 0.55 for S as recommended by Frisch et al. $^{16}$ This basis will be called basis II (86 contracted species). Second, correlation effects were studied up to fourth-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory, MP4(SDQT). In the MP computations, only valence electrons have been correlated (frozen-core approximation).

The different HAB radicals lie in the yz plane, the z-axis being collinear with the AB bond.

### 4. Equilibrium Geometries of HSiS(±) and SiSH(±)

Table II summarizes the ground-state equilibrium geometries (HF and MP2, basis I) of the triatomic species HSiS and SiSH, as well as their positive and negative ions. Because of differences in the electronic structures of HSiS⁻ and SiSH⁻ (section 4.3), two electronic states had to be considered for these anions. There are no experimental results for comparison.

#### 4.1. HSiS/SiSH.
Both neutral radicals have a bent X²A' (12a'²3a''²13a') ground state. For HSiS, 12a' is an in-plane π-type MO (mainly $p_y$) localized on S, whereas 13a' is of σ-type with major contributions from Si (3s, 3pz) and H (1s). The latter MO is antibonding in both SiH and SiS. The π* orbital 4a'' is localized on Si.

For SiSH, 12a' is a σ-type MO consisting of 3pz on sulfur and 3s, 3pz on silicon. The open shell orbital 13a' is of π-type corresponding to 3p_y of Si (nonbonding MO); the virtual 4a'' species is also localized on the terminal Si. Earlier results on 9a' and 10a' of HSiO/SiOH² indicated charge distributions analogous to those of HSiS/SiSH.

The equilibrium distance $R_e$(SiS) in both triatomic radicals is larger than the bond length in the diatomic SiS. The elongation of $R_e$(SiS) is small for HSiS (about $0.04a_0$) but quite large for SiSH (about $0.36a_0$). In the latter isomer, $R_e$(SiS) = $4.067a_0$ indicates a typical SiS single-bond distance experimentally ranging from 4.04 to $4.07a_0$.²⁴,²⁵

SCF calculations on the lowest ²A' surface of HSiO²,²⁶ and HSiS⁸ reported the existence of two minima. However, an extended MRD-CI study on HSiO² demonstrated the importance of correlation effects in obtaining one minimum only, as expected. By use of $C_{\infty v}$ notation, the two minima at the SCF level correspond to configurations A, π⁴π* (at intermediate SiO distance and HSiO angle), and B, π³π*² (larger SiO distance and smaller HSiO angle). Minimum B disappears at the CI level, as discussed in more detail in ref. 2.

The same features were reported for HSiS by Kudo and Nagase.⁸ They symbolized the A structure as H$\dot{\text{S}}$iS, the dot above Si indicating the localization of the open-shell MO 13a' on Si. Structure B (HSi$\dot{\text{S}}$) is obtained from A by the excitation 12a' → 13a' ($\pi \rightarrow \pi^*$), in line with the s character of the MO 12a'. Our optimized SCF structure for HSiS (Table II) corresponds to the HSiS isomer of Kudo and Nagase. Inclusion of correlation effects at the MP2 level stabilizes structure A (SiS double bond) over B (SiS single bond), as similarly observed by earlier CI calculations on HSiO.²

The HSiS and SiSH isomers are expected to give ESR spectra similar to those observed experimentally for HSiO and SiOH.²⁷ Since HSiO and HSiS are σ-type radicals, their ESR signals are more intense than those of the π radicals SiOH and SiSH.

#### 4.2. HSiS⁺/SiSH⁺.
As seen in Table II, the positive ions have different equilibrium structures. The most stable species SiSH⁺ exhibits a bent structure (at variance with Walsh's rules predicting a linear conformation²⁸), whereas HSiS⁺ prefers a linear geometry. This feature is well documented in the literature.⁵⁻⁷

Comparing HSiS⁺ with HSiS, the ionization 13a' → ∞ leads to an opening of the molecular angle $\alpha$ by 60°. At the same time, the SiS and SiH bonds are slightly shortened. These geometrical changes reflect the nodal properties of 13a' (HSiS) discussed in section 4.1. Most importantly, in going from HSiS⁺ (X¹Σ⁺) to HSiS (X²A') the large change of $\alpha$ resulting from single occupation of 13a' points out a strong angular dependence for this MO (in line with the significant H-admixture into 13a', which is the π* in-plane component).

This angular behavior explains why HSiS (B, 12a'→13a') is strongly bent⁸ (~95°). The importance of the a'(π*) MO for stabilizing HAB isomers has been investigated before.²⁹,³⁰

The transition SiSH(X²A') → SiSH⁺(X¹A') is accompanied by a small change in the SiSH angle (~12°). Because of the Si(3p_y) lone pair character of 13a', it is understandable why for SiSH the 13a' ionization shows little effect on the molecular angle.

#### 4.3. HSiS⁻/SiSH⁻.

Formal addition of an electron to this configuration generates π⁴π*² (³Σ⁻ < ¹Δ < ¹Σ⁺). HSiS⁻ and SiSH⁻ behave like HSiO⁻ and SiOH⁻, respectively. HSiS⁻ has \(X^1A'(13a'^2)\) as ground state because of the strong stabilization of 13a' upon bending (the out-of-plane \(4a''\) (Si \(p_\pi\)) remains almost unchanged). By contrast, SiSH⁻ exhibits \(X^3A''(13a'4a'')\) as ground state since the stabilities of both open-shell MO's (pure silicon \(3p_\pi\) species) are practically angle independent. That is, bent SiSH⁻(X³A'') retains the electronic structure of linear SiSH⁻(³Σ⁻).

Looking now for changes in geometry upon electron attachment, for HSiS⁻(X¹A'') double-occupation of 13a' leads to the largest bond distances and the smallest angle among all HSiS(±) isomers (Table II). The excited state \(^13A''(13a'4a'')\) of HSiS⁻, after occupation of \(4a''\) relative to HSiS(X²A'), has a larger SiS distance but the same angle. All these geometry changes are in line with the bonding properties of MO's 13a' and 4a''.

For the two lowest states of SiSH⁻, the extra electron occupies nonbonding Si orbitals. Since in neutral SiSH the terminal Si is positively charged, the additional negative charge on Si in SiSH⁻ apparently lowers the strength of the ionic SiS bond, as pointed out by a substantial elongation of \(R(\text{SiS})\) by \(0.37a_0\) for \(^3A''\) (Table II).

### 5. Vibrational Frequencies

The frequencies calculated at the HF level usually overestimate experimental results by about \(10\%.^{33}\) Therefore, the frequencies listed in Table III were scaled by a factor of 0.9. The changes \(\Delta R(\text{SiS})\), \(\Delta r(\text{XH})\), and \(\Delta\alpha\) with respect to the neutral isomer are also tabulated.

As expected, \(\omega(\text{SiS})\) and \(\omega(\text{XH})\) increase as long as \(R(\text{SiS})\) and \(r(\text{XH})\), respectively, decrease upon ionization or electron attachment, and vice versa. For instance, in HSiS⁺(X¹Σ⁺) both SiS and SiH bonds are somewhat shorter than in HSiS(X²A'), and therefore the frequencies are shifted to higher values, by about 17% for SiS and 12% for SiH.

Analysis of the data from Table III leads to the following observations:

(1) For a given type of HXY isomer, \(\omega(\text{SiS})\) increases in the order HXY⁻ < HXY < HXY⁺, a feature which can be correlated with the corresponding decreases of \(R_e(\text{SiS})\) along each series.

(2) The different structure of 13a' in each isomer is reflected in \(\Delta\omega(\text{XH})\). For HSiS the 13a' species is SiH antibonding, so that \(\omega(\text{SiH})\) depends on the occupation of 13a'. Therefore, \(\omega(\text{SiH})\) has the lowest value for HSiS⁻(X¹A', 13a'²) (\(\omega \approx 1677\ \text{cm}^{-1}\)), increases for both HSiS⁻(¹³A'', 13a'4a'') and HSiS(X²A', 13a')(\(\omega \approx 1920\ \text{cm}^{-1}\)), and reaches its highest value for HSiS⁺(X¹Σ⁺, 13a' empty) (\(\omega = 2164\ \text{cm}^{-1}\)).

On the other hand, because in all SiSH isomers 13a' is localized on Si and the SH bond is described by lower-lying a' MO's, it is understandable why \(\omega(\text{SH})\) remains fairly constant (2434-2516 cm⁻¹), regardless of the charge and/or electronic structure of the SiSH compound.

(3) The stabilization of MO 13a' of HSiS upon bending is reflected in \(\omega(\text{HSiS})\) lying at 451 cm⁻¹ for HSiS⁺(X¹Σ⁺, 13a' empty), increasing to 615 cm⁻¹ for HSiS (X²A', 13a') and HSiS⁻(¹³A'', 13a'4a'') and reaching a maximum (≈750 cm⁻¹) for HSiS⁻(X¹A¹, 13a'²). Overall, HSiS⁺(X¹Σ⁺) shows the highest \(\omega(\text{SiH})\) and smallest \(\omega(\text{HSiS})\) because of 13a' being empty. By contrast, HSiS⁻(X¹A') exhibits the smallest \(\omega(\text{SiH})\) and highest \(\omega(\text{HSiS})\) because of double occupation of 13a'. Intermediate frequencies correspond to species having 13a' singly occupied, that is HSiS(X²A') and HSiS⁻(¹³A'').

(4) For all HSiS species, \(\omega(\text{SiS})\) is larger than for the corresponding SiSH isomers (double bond vs single bond). Moreover, for HSiS(X²A'), \(\omega(\text{SiS}) = 671\ \text{cm}^{-1}\) compares with \(\omega = 682\ \text{cm}^{-1}\) predicted for \(H_2SiS^8\) and 626 cm⁻¹ for \((CH_3)_2SiS.^{24}\) On the other hand, the SiS single bond of SiSH(X²A') has \(\omega(\text{SiS}) \approx 460\ \text{cm}^{-1}\), an estimate which should be compared with an experimental \(\omega(\text{SiS})\) of about 500 cm⁻¹ assigned to \(H_3SiSH.^{25}\)

(5) Particularly striking is the rather low \(\omega(\text{SiS})\) for both states of SiSH⁻, a feature in line with their larger SiS equilibrium distances.

Despite the fact that the dipole moments may have been overestimated by about 30% in the HF approximation, relative changes \(\Delta\mu\) can be used as a guideline for rearrangements in the spatial electronic distribution upon ionization or electron attachment.

Formation of a SiH bond in HSiS(X²A') is accompanied by a transfer of electronic charge from H into the SiS bond, with the consequent increase in \(\mu\) relative to an experimental \(\mu(\text{SiS}, X^1\Sigma^+)\) of \(1.73\ D^{17}\) (it should be noted that the dipole moment of SiH(X²Π) is rather small, about \(0.2\ D^{33}\)). On the other hand, addition of a second H atom to form \(H_2SiS(X^1A_1)\) leads to a further increase in the dipole moment, as is evident from \(\mu(\text{H}_2\text{SiS}, X^1A_1) = 3.18\ D\) obtained by extensive MRD-CI calculations.¹⁰

According to the above discussion, a HF estimate for \(\mu(\text{HSiS}, X^2A')\) of 3.12 D is evidently too high. The true dipole moment probably lies close to 2.5 D.

Regardless of their total charge, all HSiS species have terminal sulfur atoms which are negatively charged. Due to the predominant Si character of 13a', the ionization process HSiS → HSiS⁺ (13a' → ∞) mainly affects the charge distribution around the Si atom (this center becomes positively charged), as denoted by a relatively high \(\mu(\text{HSiS}^+)\). Similarly, double occupation of 13a' as in HSiS⁻(X¹A') increases the electronic density near Si, with a corresponding decrease in the dipole moment. The same effect is observed for HSiS⁻(¹³A'', 13a'4a'') since 4a'' is also localized on silicon. The predominance of the SiS bond over SiH explains why for the HSiS family the dipole moment vector is essentially parallel to the SiS bond.

The dipole moment for HSSi(X²A') is relatively small (~0.7 D, estimated); it lies almost perpendicular to the SiS bond because of an equilibrium molecular angle of \(99.4^\circ\) (Table II) and the intrinsic higher dipole moment of the SH radical (~0.76 D, experimental¹⁷,³⁴). Ionization of 13a'(Si \(3p_y\)) leads to HSSi⁺ with the positive hole mainly localized on Si, thus increasing the charge asymmetry relative to HSSi. In both the neutral and cation species the central sulfur atom is negatively charged.

### 7. Thermochemical Properties
In this section several thermochemical properties are discussed, namely isomerization energies, dissociation energies, ionization potentials, electron affinities, and heats of formation. All relevant information is collected in Tables V-IX.

#### 7.1. Isomerization Energies.
HSiS(X²A¹) is found to be more stable than SiSH(X²A¹) by only 0.15 eV (MP4 results, Table V). Inclusion of zero-point energy (ZPE) corrections leads to $\Delta E$ = 0.18 eV (~4.1 kcal/mol). This result indicates that the SiS double bond is slightly stronger than the SiS single bond, as pointed out in the literature.⁴⁸ Another similar example is provided by an energy difference of 0.40 eV (~9 kcal/mol) between H₂Si═S (the most stable) and HSiSH.⁸ By contrast, SiOH(X²A¹) is more stable than HSiO(X²A¹) by about 0.64 ± 0.05 eV,² in line with the fact that SiO single bonds are of a similar or even higher stability than double bonds.⁴

For the positive ions, the present MP4 treatment indicates that the bent species SiSH⁺(X¹A¹) is about 0.58 eV (~13.4 kcal/mol) more stable than linear HSiS⁺(X¹Σ⁺). As discussed in earlier sections, the MO 13a' plays a decisive role in stabilizing HSiS, whereas this MO is almost inactive in SiSH.

The difference of about 0.76 eV between the isomerization energies of the neutral and positive ions indicates that HSiS⁺(X¹Σ⁺) + e⁻ is more effective in stabilizing the system than SiSH⁺(X¹A¹) + e⁻, both processes leading to single occupation of the 13a' MO. The same feature holds for the pairs HSiO⁺/SiOH⁺ and HSiO/SiOH (the SiOH isomer being the most stable one in both cases) after occupation of the 10a' MO²⁹ where a relative separation of about 2.75 eV in the positive ions⁵,³⁰ is reduced to about 0.65 eV in the neutral species, so that a relative stabilization of 2.1 eV in the SiO compound is about 3 times larger than in the SiS compounds.

Earlier estimates of the isomerization energy between SiSH⁺ and HSiS⁺ are (without ZPE) 0.71,⁵ 0.74,⁶ and 0.52 eV.⁷ The latter value compares with 0.56 eV from this work (MP4). Recently, for the reaction SiSH⁺ → HSiS⁺ a barrier height of 7.81 eV has been predicted.⁶ However, since the dissociation energy SiSH⁺(X²A¹) → SiS⁺(X²Π) + H is only 3.85 eV (ZPE included), energetically the most effective path for isomerization should be first dissociation into SiS⁺ + H, followed by recombination. (Here it is assumed that no barrier exists for dissociation.)

For the negative ions, the most stable isomer is HSiS⁻(X¹A', 13a²), which lies about 1.43 eV below SiSH⁻(X³A'', 13a'4a''). The difference in the ground-state characters of the two isomers should be stressed, a feature in line with the observed tendency for HSiS to have the 13a' MO occupied. The same behavior is found for the pair HSiO⁻/SiOH⁻ where HSiO⁻(X¹A¹) is more stable than SiOH⁻(X³A'').²,³⁵

On the ¹A' surface, HSiS⁻ lies below SiSH⁻ by about 1.84 eV; however, on the ³A'' surface, HSiS⁻ is only 0.15 eV more stable than SiSH⁻. These features can be correlated with the relative occupation of 13a'. In going from HSiS(X²A¹, 13a') to HSiS⁻(³A'', 13a'4a'') the additional electron goes into 4a''(π*), leading to a lengthening of the SiS bond but not of the XH distances or molecular angles (section 4 and Table II). Moreover, the 4a'' electron attachment stabilizes both the HSiS and SiSH radicals by the same degree, as pointed out for the negative ions by an isomerization energy on the triplet surface (0.15 eV) being practically the same as for the neutral species (0.18 eV). By contrast, on the ¹A' surface double occupation of 13a' in HSiS⁻ causes a net gain of about 1.66 eV relative to the neutral radical.

#### 7.2. Energies for Dissociation $HAB^{(\pm)} \to H^{(\pm)} + AB$.
The data for the dissociations HSiS or SiSH → SiS(X¹Σ⁺) + H, HSiS⁺ or SiSH⁺ → SiS⁺(X²Π) + H, HSiS⁻(X¹A¹) or SiSH⁻(¹1A¹) → SiS(X¹Σ⁺) + H⁻, and HSiS⁻(¹³A'') or SiSH⁻(X³A'') → SiS⁻(X²Π) + H are given in Table VI. The discussion below is based on MP4 results including ZPE corrections.

For the neutral isomers, the predicted dissociation energies are quite small, namely 1.02 eV for HSiS and 0.84 eV for SiSH. These values have to be compared with the corresponding experimental dissociation energies of SiH (≈3.00 eV)¹⁷ and SH (≈3.55 eV).¹⁷ On the other hand, for HSiS the equilibrium bond distance and SiH frequency differ by only 0.03a₀ and 100 cm⁻¹ from that of the isolated SiH radical. For SiSH, the frequency is only ≈200 cm⁻¹ smaller than in SH. Taken together, the features above strongly suggest the existence for both neutral radicals of a potential barrier along the dissociation path HXY → H + XY.³⁶ Such dissociation barriers may arise from a crossing in the linear conformations of the lower ²Σ⁺ and ²Π states, as observed for HSiO/SiOH.²

Because IP(SiS) is about 3.2 eV smaller than IP(H), the ground-state positive ions dissociate into SiS⁺(X²Π) + H. Since linear HSiS⁺(X¹Σ⁺) correlates with A²Σ⁺ of SiS⁺ and not with X²Π of SiS⁺, a lowering of the symmetry is required to correlate HSiS⁺ with the ground state of SiS⁺.

The dissociation energies of 3.27 eV for HSiS⁺ and 3.85 eV for SiSH⁺ can be considered as "normal" when compared with the corresponding diatomic hydrides (see above). Berthier et al.⁵ᵇ reported dissociation energies of 3.91 and 4.60 eV for HSiS⁺ and SiSH⁺, respectively (without ZPE). A recent experimental study⁷ inferred a hydrogen abstraction energy (at room temperature) for SiSH⁺ of about 4.05 ± 0.13 eV, somewhat higher than our estimate of 3.85 eV.

In the case of the negative ions, the MP4 data assign to the process HSiS⁻(X¹A¹) → SiS(X¹Σ⁺) + H⁻(¹S) a $D_0$ of 2.46 eV, to be compared with dissociation energies (also known as hydride affinities) of 0.35 eV for HCO⁻(X¹A¹)³⁷ (experimental) and 1.68 eV for HCS⁻(X¹A¹)³⁸ (ab initio). On the other hand, SiSH⁻(¹A¹) is weakly bound with $D_0$(SiS-H⁻) of 0.62 eV.

The ground state of SiSH⁻(X³A'') dissociates into SiS⁻(X²Π) + H(²S), a channel located 0.33 eV above SiS + H⁻, with $D_0$ = 1.32 eV. The excited state ¹³A'' of HSiS⁻ exhibits a comparable SiH bond strength of 1.47 eV.

Since the outermost MO $3\pi$ of SiS is strongly localized on the S atom, the IP for the transition ${SiS}\rightarrow{SiS^{+} + e^{-}}$ compares with an atomic value of 10.357 eV for the ionization ${S(^{3}P_{g})}\rightarrow{S^{+}(^{4}S_{u})} + e^{-}$.³⁹

The photoelectron spectrum (PES) of HSiS, not recorded to date, should exhibit a long vibrational progression of the bending mode as a result of the opening of the molecular angle by $60^{\circ}$. 7.4. Electron Affinities. As seen in Table VIII the adiabatic electron affinity (EA) predicted at the MP4 level including zero-point energy correction for the process ${HSiS(X^{2}A^{\prime}) + e^{-}}\rightarrow{HSiS^{-}(X^{1}A^{\prime})}$ is 2.16 eV. This result is rather striking when compared with an experimental EA of 0.31 eV for $\text{HCO}^{37}$ or a theoretical estimate of 0.41 eV for HCS,³⁸ but more in line with $\text{EA(HSiO)}$ of 1.64 eV.³⁵

According to the discussion in section 4.3, the ground-state $X^{1}A^{\prime}$ of both $\text{HSiO}^{-}$ and $\text{HSiS}^{-}$ correlates with the linear state $^{1}\Delta$($\pi^{4}\pi^{*2}$). In the case of neutral HSiO, an earlier study by us indicated the low-lying character ($\sim$2.0 eV) of the excitation $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*}$, leading to the configuration $\pi^{3}\pi^{*2}$. At strongly bent conformations ($\alpha \simeq 90^{\circ}$) a strong interaction exists between the $\pi^{4}\pi^{*}$ and $\pi^{3}\pi^{*2}$ configurations (for $^{2}A^{\prime}$ states the interaction involves the configurations $\text{a}^{2}\text{a}^{\prime\prime2}\text{a}^{\prime}(\text{A})$ and $\text{a}^{\prime}\text{a}^{\prime\prime2}\text{a}^{2}(\text{B})$). This mixing explains why the SCF approximation locates two minima on the lowest $^{2}A^{\prime}$ surface of $\text{HSiO}^{3,26}$ According to Kudo and Nagase,⁸ the HSiS radical behaves like HSiO (see section 4.1), the two minima in the former radical being energetically closer than in the latter. Although we did not calculate excited $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*}$ states of HSiS, the features above indicate that the configuration $\pi^{3}\pi^{*2}$ is rather stable also in HSiS. Because the $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*}$ states of HCO lie at much higher energies ($\sim$5.0 eV),⁴¹⁴² the ground-state surface exhibits a regular behavior.⁸ The ground state of HCS may behave like HCO since in the sulfur compound the $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*}$ excitation appears near 3.0 eV.¹

Summing up, we address the larger EA or HSiO and HSiS when compared with that of HCO and HCS (larger by a factor of 5) to the significantly more stable character in HSiO and HSiS of excited configurations of type $\pi^{3}\pi^{*2}$.

The adiabatic EA for electron attachment into the virtual MO 4a′′ of $HSiS(X^{2}A^{\prime},13\text{a}^{\prime})$, leading to $\text{HSiS}^{-}(^{13}A^{\prime\prime},13\text{a}^{\prime}4\text{a}^{\prime\prime})$, is estimated to be 1.02 eV. Therefore, the excitation energy for the spin-forbidden transition $^{13}A^{\prime\prime}\leftarrow{X^{1}A^{\prime}(13\text{a}^{\prime}\rightarrow4\text{a}^{\prime\prime})}$ of $\text{HSiS}^{-}$ can be determined experimentally by recording the photodetachment spectrum $\text{HSiS}^{-}\rightarrow\text{HSiS}$ (assuming that $^{13}A^{\prime\prime}$ had been populated). We estimate $T_{0}(^{3}A^{\prime\prime} - X^{1}A^{\prime})$ to be 1.14 eV.

The electron affinities for the isomer SiSH are smaller. The process ${SiSH(X^{2}A^{\prime}) + e^{-}}\rightarrow{SiSH^{-}(X^{3}A^{\prime\prime})}$ is exothermic by about 0.91 eV, whereas $\text{EA(X}^{2}\text{A}^{\prime}\rightarrow^{11}\text{A}^{\prime})$ of SiSH is only 0.50 eV. Thus the excitation $^{11}A^{\prime}\leftarrow{X^{3}A^{\prime\prime}(4\text{a}^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow13\text{a}^{\prime})}$ of $\text{SiSH}^{-}$ lies in the infrared region with $T_{0} = 0.41$ eV. As for its experimental determination, photodetachment may be the most appropriate technique (see above).

Finally, for the negative ions it is possible that a third electronic state is also bound. The configuration $13\text{a}^{\prime}4\text{a}^{\prime\prime}$ generating the lowest $^{3}A^{\prime\prime}$ state also gives rise to a $^{1}A^{\prime\prime}$ state. We have not studied the latter explicitly. However, we can roughly estimate the separation $^{3}A^{\prime\prime} - ^{1}A^{\prime\prime}$ by using literature data on isoelectronic systems.

For the $\text{PS}^{-}$ radical, for instance, the separation $X^{3}\Sigma^{-}(^{3}A^{\prime\prime}) - {^{1}\Delta(^{1}A^{\prime},^{1}A^{\prime\prime})}$ has been estimated to be about 0.55 eV.¹⁵ Since the $^{3}A^{\prime\prime}$ and $^{1}A^{\prime\prime}$ states of $\text{HXY}^{-}$ formally correlate with linear $^{3}\Sigma^{-}$ and $^{1}\Delta(\pi^{4}\pi^{*2})$, respectively, we can assume for $\text{HSiS}^{-}$ at equilibrium a splitting $^{3}A^{\prime\prime} - ^{1}A^{\prime\prime}$ of about 0.60 eV. This estimate would place $^{1}A^{\prime\prime}$ of $\text{HSiS}^{-}$ approximately 1.74 eV above $X^{1}A^{\prime}$ or, most importantly, about 0.40 eV below neutral HSiS.

There is experimental and theoretical evidence that $\text{CS}^{-}$, $\text{HCS}^{-}$, and $\text{H}_{2}\text{CS}^{-}$ are bound.³⁸ Since $\text{SiS}^{-}$ and $\text{HSiS}^{-}$ are both stable, it is highly probable that $\text{H}_{2}\text{SiS}^{-}$ is also bound with respect to electron detachment.¹⁰ The ground state is expected to be $X^{2}B_{1}$ (like $\text{H}_{2}\text{CS}^{-}$), the extra electron occupying the $\pi^{*}(\text{b}_{1})$ MO (empty in neutral $\text{H}_{2}\text{SiS}$). On the basis of the data available for $\text{H}_{2}\text{CS}^{-}$,³⁸ we estimate the electron affinity of $\text{H}_{2}\text{SiS}$ to be about 0.55 eV.

For instance, the heat of reaction $\Delta H_{r}$ for the reaction $XY^{+} + H_{2} \to HXY^{+} + H$ is given by $\Delta H_{r} = D_{0}(H_{2}) - D_{0}(H-XY^{+})$.

The dissociation energy $D_{0}(H-XY^{+})$ is also designated in this section as the hydrogen affinity of $XY^{+}$ at the $X$-site (i.e. HA-$(XY^{+})$. The process above is exothermic if the relation $D_{0}(H- XY^{+}) > D_{0}(H_{2})$ holds, the latter $D_{0}$ being experimentally measured at 105.8 kcal/mol (4.59 eV, ref 17). As seen in Table IX, the hydrogen affinities of $SiS^{+}$ lie below 100 kcal/mol, so that the reaction between $SiS^{+}$ and $H_{2}$ cannot be a source for generating the isomers $HSiS^{+}/SiSH^{+}$ in outer space. An earlier study, however, predicted $SiS^{+} + H_{2} \to H + HSiS^{+}$ to be exothermic by $\sim 5$ kcal/mol.$^{6}$ On the other hand, recent experiments$^{7b}$ indicate that $SiS^{+} + H_{2}$ do not react, in line with our theoretical results which predict the formation of $SiSH^{+}(X^{1}A')$ by that reaction to be endothermic by about 17 kcal/mol (compared with $12 \pm 8$ kcal/mol, experimental, ref 7b). However, it should be pointed out that $H_{2}SiS^{+}$ lies about 30 kcal/mol below $H_{2} + SiS^{+};^{10}$ this reaction probably has a significant activation barrier (as in $H_{2}SiS \to H_{2} + SiS$, ref 8), which would provide a possible explanation for the experimental observations by Wlodek and Bohme.$^{7b}$

It is of interest to compare HA of $SiS^{+}$ and $SiS^{-}$ with HA(SiS), relating to changes in the $Si-H$ or $S-H$ bond strength in going from the neutral to the respective ionic species. Using the more general nomenclature $XY$, $HXY$, etc., and defining the difference $HA(XY^{+}) - HA(XY)$ as $\Delta HA(XY^{+},XY)$, it can easily be shown that

$$\Delta \mathrm{HA}\left(X Y^{+}, X Y\right)=\mathrm{IP}(X Y)-\mathrm{IP}(H X Y)$$

On the other hand IP$(XY) >$ IP$(HXY)$, at least for HXY species with 11 valence electrons, so that $HA(XY^{+}) > HA(XY)$. As expected, H abstraction from $HXY^{+}$ requires more energy than from neutral HXY. Moreover, in the present case of SiS species, IP$(SiSH)$ is smaller than IP$(HSiS)$ by about 0.75 eV ($\sim 17$ kcal/mol). Accordingly, upon ionization the largest variation in $\Delta E$(H-abstraction) takes place for the $S-H$ bond (Table IX).

For the negative ion $HXY^{-}$, similar arguments as above lead to

$$\Delta \mathrm{HA}\left(X Y^{-}, X Y\right)=\mathrm{EA}(H X Y)-\mathrm{EA}(X Y)$$

Since EA(HSiS) and EA(SiSH) are larger than EA(XY) (Table VIII), hydrogen abstraction from $HSiS^{-}$ and $SiSH^{-}$ requires more energy than for the neutral isomers (Table IX). Because of the large difference between the EA's of HSiS and SiSH (Table VIII), upon attachment the SiH bond stabilizes more than the SH bond (by 41 versus 11.2 kcal/mol). It is worth noting, however, that for $HSiS^{-}$ and $SiSH^{-}$ the hydride abstraction reaction leading to $H^{-} + SiS$ is energetically favored over the hydrogen abstraction.

Other reactions of interest are those related to double hydrogenation of SiS, leading to $H_{2}SiS$ or HSiSH. (In the following, trans-HXYH isomers will be assumed). The isomerization energy between these tetratomic species, $\Delta E_{i}(H_{2}SiS,HSiSH)$, is given as the difference between the Si and S-site hydrogen affinities of HSiS, that is

$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SiS}, \mathrm{HSiSH}\right)=\mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{HSiS})-\mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{HSiS})$$

From $\mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{HSiS}) = \mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{SiSH}) - \Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{HSiS},\mathrm{SiSH})$ it follows that
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SiS}, \mathrm{HSiSH}\right)= \\
\mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{HSiS})-\mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{SiSH})+\Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{HSiS}, \mathrm{SiSH})
\end{aligned}$$

In the last expression, it should be noted that the hydrogen affinities refer to the same atomic center (Si) but to two different triatomic species.

The result above is rather interesting since it points out that the isomerization energy of the triatomic molecules plays a significant role in determining the isomerization energy of the corresponding tetratomic molecules. As seen in Table IX, the difference HA(HSiS) - HA(SiSH) is 4.7 kcal/mol; on the other hand, data from the literature for the couple $H_{2}CS/HCSH^{44}$ indicate that $\mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{HCS}) - \mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{CSH}) \approx 5$ kcal/mol. For $H_{2}SiO$/HSiOH, combination of results from refs 2 and 8 leads to $\mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{HSiO}) - \mathrm{HA}(\mathrm{SiOH})$ of roughly 12 kcal/mol. Thus, one can assume that for a variety of $H_{2}XY$/HXYH compounds (with $\text{X} = \text{C, Si, Ge}$ and $\text{Y} = \text{O, S, As, etc.)}$, the following relation is valid: $\Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{XY}, \mathrm{HXYH}) \approx \Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{HXY}, \mathrm{XYH}) + (10 \pm 5)$ kcal/mol.

Data from the literature assign to HCO/COH and HCS/CSH isomerization energies of the order of 40 kcal/mol,$^{44,45}$ to be compared with $\Delta E_{i}(HSiS,SiSH) = 4.2$ kcal/mol from this work and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{HSiO}, \mathrm{SiOH}) \sim -15$ kcal/mol from a MRD-CI study.$^{2}$ These thermochemical results clearly point out the differences between carbon and silicon compounds; $H_{2}CO$ and $H_{2}CS$ are about 30-40 kcal/mol more stable than HCOH and HCSH, respectively, whereas $H_{2}SiO$ and $H_{2}SiS$ are energetically close to the corresponding $t$-HSiYH isomers. As a matter of fact, $t$-HSiOH is about 2.5 kcal/mol more stable than $H_{2}SiO$, whereas $t$-HSiSH is less stable than $H_{2}SiS$ by ca. 9 kcal/mol.

For the pair $H_{2}GeO/t$-HGeOH an isomerization energy of about -18 kcal/mol (CI results, ref 46) and -12 kcal/mol (MP4, ref 47) have been reported. The greater stability of $t$-HGeOH relative to $H_{2}GeO$ suggests that the isomer GeOH should be more stable than HGeO (as similarly observed for HSiO/SiOH, ref 2). By use of the approximation above for connecting the isomerization energies between tetraatomic and triatomic compounds, it should approximately hold that

$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{HGeO}, \mathrm{GeOH}) \approx \Delta E_{\mathrm{i}}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{GeO}, t-\mathrm{HGeOH}\right)- \\
(10 \pm 5) \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}
\end{aligned}$$

This simple relation indicates that GeOH is probably more stable than HGeO by about $-25 \pm 5$ kcal/mol. We are unaware of any ab initio studies on these triatomic germanium species.

SiSH, and HSiS⁻/SiSH⁻ have been studied here for the first time. Relative energies are based on MP4 calculations using f-type polarization functions on Si and S.

A computed adiabatic IP for the process $\text{SiS(X}^1\Sigma^+\text{)} \rightarrow \text{SiS}^+(\text{X}^2\text{II})$ of 10.35 eV reproduces an experimental value of 10.38 ± 0.02 eV quite well. The adiabatic EA for the electron attachment $\text{SiS(X}^1\Sigma^+\text{)} \rightarrow \text{SiS}^-(\text{X}^2\text{II})$ lies near 0.40 eV.

Neutral $\text{HSiS(X}^2\text{A}')$ is more stable than $\text{SiSH(X}^2\text{A}')$ by only 0.18 eV (~4 kcal/mol). Like the pair HSiO/SiOH, both HSiS and SiSH should exhibit a characteristic ESR spectrum whereby a large signal for the σ-type HSiS radical should be easily distinguished from the less intense line due to π-type SiSH.

Ionization of 13a' leads to the ground states of the positive ions. The order of stabilities is reversed with respect to the neutral species. $\text{SiSH}^+(\text{X}^1\text{A}')$ lies 0.58 eV (~13.4 kcal/mol) below $\text{HSiS}^+(\text{X}^1\Sigma^+)$. $\text{SiSH}^+(\text{X}^1\text{A}')$ has a bent equilibrium geometry at variance with Walsh's rules for HAB systems with 10 VE's. The isoelectronic systems HSIP/SiPH show the same behavior, the most stable isomer SiPH having a bent $\text{X}^1\text{A}'$ ground state, whereas $\text{HSiP(X}^1\Sigma^+)$ is linear.⁴³ The adiabatic IP's are 7.34 eV for SiSH and 8.09 eV for HSiS. The PES for the transition SiSH → SiSH⁺ should be simple because of the minimal geometrical changes between the two states. By contrast, for the process HSiS → HSiS⁺ an opening of ~60° in the molecular angle causes a rather long vibrational progression in the bending mode.

An interesting result of this work concerns the high stability of HSiS⁻ when compared with the ions HCO⁻ and HCS⁻, leading to a surprisingly high $\text{EA(HSiS} \rightarrow \text{HSiS}^-)$ of 2.16 eV. The $^3\text{A}''$ and $^1\text{A}''$ states of HSiS⁻ are predicted to be bound with respect to electron attachment. To our knowledge, the pair HSiS/HSiS⁻ is the first documented example of HAB molecules having such a photodetachment spectrum. Also interesting is the possibility to study the transition $^1\text{A}'' \leftarrow \text{X}^1\text{A}'$ of HSiS⁻ by standard optical spectroscopy. Furthermore, SiSH⁻ has at least two bound states, the ground state of SiSH⁻ corresponding to $\text{X}^3\text{A}''$.

In the final stage of writing this paper, an experimental work on the gas-phase chemistry of HSiO⁻ came to our attention. Gronert et al.⁴⁸ reported that three anions were formed by reacting HSiO⁻ with COS, namely HSiS⁻ (65%), HCOS⁻ (20%), and HSiOS⁻ (15%). According to these authors, the hydride affinity of SiS should be higher than that of CO₂ which experimentally lies at 2.24 eV.⁴⁷ In fact, our predicted value of 2.46 eV (MP4, basis II) for the hydride affinity of SiS (on the Si site and into ground-state HSiS⁻) agrees quite well with the experimental observations. Moreover, since the dissociation energies for $\text{HSiS}^-(^1\text{A}'')$ or both SiSH⁻ states are smaller than 2.0 eV (cf. Table VI), the [Si,S,H]⁻ isomer detected experimentally has to be the ground state of HSiS⁻.

The vibrational frequencies, dipole moments, ionization potentials, electron affinities, heats of formation, and dissociation energies reported in this work are expected to be of assistance in future experimental investigations of the various HSiS and SiSH species.
