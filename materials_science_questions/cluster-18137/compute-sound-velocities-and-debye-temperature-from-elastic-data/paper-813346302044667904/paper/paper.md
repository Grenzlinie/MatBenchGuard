### ABSTRACT

Density functional-based method has been used to investigate the systematic trends for structural parameters, elastic stiffness, lattice dynamics and thermal properties of cubic perovskite-type $RBRh_3$ depending on the type of $R$ atoms ($R$ are Sc, Y, La and Lu). The structural parameters, single-crystal elastic constants, directional elastic wave velocities and their pressure dependence are calculated and analyzed in comparison with the available experimental and theoretical data. A set of isotropic elastic parameters and related properties, namely bulk and shear moduli, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, Lamé's coefficients, average sound velocity, Debye temperature and thermal conductivity are predicted in the frame work of the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation for the polycrystalline $RBRh_3$. The correlation between the mechanical properties and electronic structures has been discussed. Using the density-functional perturbation theory (DFPT), the phonon properties of $RBRh_3$ ($R$ = Sc, Y and La) are investigated for the first time.

## 1. Introduction

Ternary perovskite-type IIIB-rhodium carbides and borides $RXRh_3$ ($R$ are IIIB elements; $X$ are B or C) have received great attention for their fundamental physics and technological applications [1-10]. Their high stability and hardness make them very useful for applications in high temperature environment, cutting tools and hard coating [1]. Recently, Shishido and co-workers successfully synthesized a number of compounds of this family and investigated their hardness and oxidation resistance [3-6]. Theoretical studies have been reported by several groups. Sahara et al. [7] studied the structural and elastic properties of $RBRh_3$ with $R$ = Sc, Y and La, using the projected augmented wave method. Structural and electronic properties of $RB_xRh_3$ ($R$ = Sc, Y and Ce) have been investigated using first-principles method [1,8].

In the present study we focus on the $RBRh_3$ system with $R$ are Sc, Y, La and Lu. First, these chosen rare earth elements are trivalent and one can expect their compounds to have similar properties. Second, despite the large number of interesting results reported in the recent years on some physical properties of $RBRh_3$ ($R$ = Sc, Y and La) materials, their mechanical behavior under pressure effect, lattice dynamics and thermal properties have not been investigated. Moreover, the reported elastic behavior has dealt with single-crystals, meanwhile, the most part of synthesized and experimentally examined $RBRh_3$ compounds are polycrystalline [9], i.e., in the form of aggregated mixtures of monocrystallines with a random orientation. Therefore, it is desirable to investigate the systematic trends of the structural parameters, elastic stiffness and thermal conductivity of the cubic mono- and polycrystalline $RBRh_3$ depending on the type of $R$ atoms ($R$ are Sc, Y, La and Lu).

## 2. Computational method

The present density functional theory calculations were performed using the CASTEP code [11]. The ultra-soft pseudo-potential [12] was employed to describe the interaction between ions and electrons. The exchange-correlation potential was calculated within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [13]. The plane-wave basis set cut-off was chosen to be 320 eV for all cases. For the Brillouin zone (BZ) sampling, the Monkhorst-Pack scheme with a $6×6×6$ special $k$-point mesh was adopted [14]. These parameters were sufficient in leading to well converged total energy, geometrical configurations and elastic moduli.

The optimized geometries for $RBRh_3$ compounds were determined using the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shenno (BFGS) minimization technique [15]; which provides a fast way of finding the lowest energy structure. For the geometry optimization, the convergence was assumed when (i) the total energy difference between the last two cycles was less then $5 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/atom; (ii) the maximum ionic Hellmann-Feynman force was below $0.01$ eV $\mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$; and (iii) the maximum stress was below $0.02$ eV $\mathring{\text{A}}^{-3}$.

The elastic coefficients were determined by applying the so-called "stress-strain" method within the density functional based electronic structure [16]. The stress-strain relation can be described as:

$$
(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3, \sigma_4, \sigma_5, \sigma_6) = C(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \varepsilon_3, \varepsilon_4, \varepsilon_5, \varepsilon_6) \tag{1}
$$

For the cubic crystal, there are only three nonzero independent elastic constants, namely $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{44}$. Appling two kinds of cubic distortions ($\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_4$) along the crystallographic directions can give stresses relating to these three elastic coefficients. The value of each strain was varied from $-0.005$ to $+0.005$ with a step of $0.002$, and then each of three elastic constants took the arithmetic average value of the six steps.

The lattice dynamical calculations are performed in a plane-wave pseudo-potential representation through the QUANTUM-ESPRESSO program [17]. We adopt the GGA of Perdew-Burke-Ern-zerhof [13] for the exchange-correlation potentials with a cutoff of 30 Ry. for the wave functions and 300 Ry. for the charge densities. BZ integrations were performed using a $10 \times 10 \times 10$ $k$-points mesh. Integration up to the Fermi surface is done with the smearing technique [18] with smearing parameter $\sigma = 0.05$ Ry. The phonon dispersion curve is obtained within the framework of the density functional perturbation theory, as explained in Ref. [19,20]. Dynamical matrices are evaluated on a $4 \times 4 \times 4$ $\mathbf{q}$-points mesh within the irreducible part of the BZ. These are then Fourier-interpolated to obtain the phonon dispersion curves.

### 3.1. Optimized structural parameters

Computed equilibrium lattice constants of $RBRh_3$ ($R =$ Sc, Y, La, Lu) are summarized in Table 1. Results from earlier works are quoted for comparison. Present results agree well with previous theoretical and experimental reports for the compounds of interest [1,5-7]. The optimized lattice constants $a_0$ deviate from the measured ones [1,5,6] within only 1.5%. Bulk modulus $B$ at zero pressure and its pressure derivative $B'$ are calculated by fitting pressure-volume data to a third-order Birch-Murnaghan equation of state ($EoS$) [21] (Fig. 1). There is good agreement between obtained bulk modulus values and those previously reported. To date, no experimental data for the bulk moduli of $ScBRh_3$, $YBRh_3$, $LaBRh_3$ and $LuBRh_3$ compounds are available to be compared with our theoretical results. Based on the above-mentioned accordance, therefore, we believe that the plane-wave ultra-soft pseudo-potential (PW-PP) method is reliable in investing the structural, mechanic and thermal properties of $RBRh_3$. It is noted that the lattice constant $a_0$ increases when $R$ is changed in the order Sc, Lu, Y and La. While the bulk modulus decreases when $R$ is changed in the same order. These results can be understood by taking into account the bonding characteristics and properties of $R$ ($R =$ Sc, Y, La, Lu) atoms. The radius of the $R$ atom increases from Sc to Lu to Y to La. The larger size of $R$ atoms forces the system to have a larger lattice constant and hence a longer $R$-Rh and Rh-B bond lengths. Therefore, the $R$-Rh and Rh-B bond strength decreases when $R$ changes from Sc to Lu to Y to La, which explains the bulk modulus decrease. This trend is in agreement with the well-known relationship between $B$ and the lattice constants: $B \propto V_0^{-1}$, where $V_0$ is the unit cell volume. This simple trend – a larger lattice constant leads to a smaller bulk modulus – has been demonstrated also for various perovkite-like and other materials [22-24].

### 3.3. Mechanical properties of polycrystalline $RBRh_3$

Elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$ are obtained from fist-principles calculations for $ScBRh_3$, $YBRh_3$, $LaBRh_3$ and $LuBRh_3$ singlecrystals, meanwhile, the majority of these synthesized materials are polycrystalline [9]. It is not possible to measure the individual elastic constants $C_{ij}$ for polycrystalline samples, but one can obtain the polycrystalline elastic parameters, namely bulk modulus ($B$), shear modulus ($G$), Young's modulus ($E$), Poisson's ratio ($\sigma$) and Lamé's constants ($\lambda$ and $\mu$), which are frequently measured for polycrystalline materials when investigating their hardness. The polycrystalline bulk modulus $B$ and Shear modulus $G$ can be estimated using the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximations [27,28-30]. Furthermore, Young's modulus and Poisson's ration can be calculated from the shear modulus and bulk modulus [27].

The computed values of above mentioned isotropic elastic parameters are summarized in Table 3. It is found that $B$, $G$, $E$, $\lambda$ and $\mu$ parameters decrease in the sequence $ScBRh_3 \to LuBRh_3 \to YBRh_3 \to LaBRh_3$. This results is similar to the trend obtained for the bulk modulus $B$, calculated from the EOS fitting, and the elastic constant $C_{11}$, which can be understood by considering the bonding characteristic and properties of $R$ ($R$ = Sc, Y, La, Lu) atoms as discussed in Section 3.1. Physically, the first Lamé constant $\lambda$ represents the compressibility of the material while the second Lamé constant reflects its shear stiffness. Accordingly, from our estimations (Table 3) we can conclude that the compressibility and shear stiffness decrease in the sequence $ScBRh_3 \to LuBRh_3 \to YBRh_3 \to LaBRh_3$.

One can estimate the brittle and ductile behaviors of polycrystalline materials by considering $B$ as the resistance to fracture and $G$ as the resistance to plastic deformation. A low (high) $B/G$ ration becomes therefore associated to brittleness (ductility) of materials. The consequence of brittleness is the sensitivity for thermal shocks, as the material cannot efficiently dissipate thermal stress via plastic deformations. Thus, a brittle solid can only be subjected to limited thermal shocks before its strength drops dramatically. Pugh [31] has proposed a critical value which separates ductile and brittle materials. It was fixed at about 1.75; i.e., if $B/G > 1.75$, the material behaves in a ductile manner; otherwise the material behaves in a brittle manner. From the computed $B/G$ ratios of Table 3 and referred to Pugh's criterion, we can conclude that $RBRh_3$ ($R$ = Sc, Y, La, Lu) are ductile and resistant to thermal shocks; their mechanic properties decreases slowly with increasing temperature. So, these ternary compounds may be machinable.

Zener anisotropy ratio \(Z\), defined by \(Z = 2C_{44}/(C_{11}-C_{12})\) [34]. A material is isotropic if \(Z = 1\), which means that the elastic moduli are independent of crystal orientation. The magnitude of the deviation from 1 is a measure of the degree of elastic anisotropy possessed by the crystal. For a cubic crystal, the Zener anisotropy ratio also determines the direction showing the maximum Young's modulus. For \(Z < 1\), the maximum young's modulus will appear along [100] direction; while for \(Z > 1\), the maximum young's modulus will be in the [111] direction. The calculated anisotropic factors of \(RBRh_3\) (\(R =\) Sc, Y, La and Lu,) are given in Table 1. The \(RBRh_3\) phases are characterized by a profound anisotropy, which might lead to a higher probability to develop microcracks or structural defects during the growing process. This result suggests also that the maximum Young's modulus appears along [100] direction. The anisotropic Young's modulus can be attributed to the orientations of the Rh–B bond, which are oriented along [100] direction, and the Rh–\(R\) bond, which is close to the [100] direction than to the [111] direction.

### 3.4. Elastic stiffness at high pressure and structural stability

In order to show the elastic behavior of \(RBRh_3\) (\(R =\) Sc, Y, La, Lu) compounds under pressure, the elastic parameters were computed at applied hydrostatic pressures ranging from 0 to 40 GPa. Fig. 2 shows the changes in the elastic parameters (\(C_{11}\), \(C_{12}\), \(C_{44}\), \(B\), \(G\), \(E\)) versus pressure for \(RBRh_3\) (\(R =\) Sc, Y, La, Lu). Linear pressure dependence in all curves in the considered range of pressure is found. The calculated first-order pressure derivative of the elastic constants \(\partial C_{11}/\partial p\), \(\partial C_{12}/\partial p\), and \(\partial C_{44}/\partial p\) are summarized in Table 1. It is seen that all investigated elastic constants increase with pressure increasing. It is known that the \(C_{11}\), \(C_{12}\) and the bulk modulus \(B\) are directly determined by the features of the average bond strength. So, the \(C_{11}\), \(C_{12}\) and the bulk modulus \(B\) increase as a result of bond strength (\(R\)-Rh and B-Rh) enhancement at elevated pressures. This result also suggests that these compounds in the perovskite structure remain stable at high pressures up to 40 GPa. Furthermore the shear moduli \(C_{44}\) and \(G\), which provides a measure of rigidity against the shape deformation, are changed less when the applied pressure is increased. The results indicate that the enhancement of bond strength has little influence on the shear deformation resistance. This deformation can be attributed to the fact that the shear deformation mechanism is determined by the distortion of the B–Rh bond and the rotation of the \(R\)-Rh bond. The less shear deformation resistance is helpful for improving the damage tolerance of compounds owing to their low shear strain energy.

The key criterion for mechanical stability of a crystal is that the strain energy must be positive [35], which means in a cubic crystal that the elastic constant should satisfy the following inequalities [27]:

$$
\frac{1}{3}\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}+P\right)>0 ;\left(C_{44}-P\right)>0 ; \frac{1}{2}\left(C_{11}-C_{12}-2 P\right)>0 \tag{2}
$$

Our results reveal that the stability criteria (Eq. (2)) are verified over the studied pressure range (up to 40 GPa), implying the stability of \(RBRh_3\) (\(R =\) Sc, Y, La, Lu) materials. The stability of these four compounds can also be confirmed by providing the poison's ratio, whose value is usually between \( - 1\) and 0.5, corresponding to the lower and upper limit where the materials do not change their shapes.

Elastic wave velocities along [100], [110] and [111] directions for \(ScBRh_3\), \(LuBRh_3\), \(YBRh_3\) and \(LaBRh_3\) increase linearly with pressure increasing (Fig. 3).

### 3.5. Thermal properties of polycrystalline $RBRh_3$

Thermal conductivity, $k$, is the property of a material that indicates its ability to conduct heat. So, in order to know if material is a potential candidate for thermal barrier coating application, its thermal conductivity need to be investigated. Based on the Debye model, Clarke [36,37] suggested that the theoretical minimum thermal conductivity can be calculated after replacing different atoms by an equivalent atom with a mean atomic mass $M_m$:

$$
k_{\min }=k_{B} v_{m}\left(M_{m} / \rho\right)^{-2 / 3} \tag{3}
$$

$k_B$ is the Boltzmann's constant and $M_m$ the average atomic mass. The average sound velocity $v_m$ in the polycrystalline material is given by [38]:

$$
v_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{t}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{l}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{3}} \tag{4}
$$

$v_l$ and $v_t$ are the longitudinal and transverse sound velocity obtained using the shear modulus $G$ and the bulk modulus $B$ from Navier's equation [38]:

$$
v_{l}=\left(\frac{3 B+4 G}{3 \rho}\right)^{1 / 2} \text { and } v_{t}=\left(\frac{G}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{5}
$$

One of the most important parameters that determine the thermal characteristics of materials is the Debye temperature ($\theta_D$). The Debye temperature is closely related to many physical properties such as elastic constants, specific heat and melting temperature. A higher $\theta_D$ implies a higher thermal conductivity. It is used to distinguish between high- and low-temperature regions for a solid. All modes are expected to have energy $K_B T$ if $T>\theta_D$, and if $T<\theta_D$ one expects high-frequency modes to be frozen [39]. At low temperature the vibrational excitation arise solely from acoustic modes. Hence, at low temperatures the Debye temperature calculated from elastic constants is the same as that determined from specific heat measurements. The Debye temperature can be defined in terms of the mean sound velocity as follows [38]:

$$
\theta_{D}=\frac{h}{k_{B}}\left[\frac{3}{4 \pi V_{a}}\right]^{\frac{1}{3}} v_{m} \tag{6}
$$

### 3.6. Electronic properties

The total densities of states (TDOS) for all studied compounds are presents in Fig. 5. There are several general remarks that can be made. First, the Fermi level is positioned in the bands which are mainly composed of Rh-4d. Part of these bands is unoccupied and the TDOS show the presence of a pseudo gap near $E_F$. The densities of states at the Fermi level are 3.30, 3.34, 3.56 and 3.83 states per eV and per formula unit for ScBRh₃, LuBRh₃, YBRh₃ and LaBRh₃, respectively. As a result $RBRh_3$ will exhibit metallic properties, such as metallic electrical and thermal conductivity, which agree well with the already published results [1,3-8]. These systems show a decrease in the densities of states at the Fermi level with an increase in the atomic radius of $R$. The metallic conducting behavior of $RBRh_3$ is mainly from the Rh atom. Second, the details of the peak structures in the TDOS spectra of the four compounds are rather similar, indicating similarity in chemical bonding, so all further discussion on bonding will be done with a single phase. We chose ScBRh₃ as a prototype compound, and studied the chemical bonding in more details. In Fig. 6, the charge density contour in the (110) plane is shown. Our calculated charge density distribution for ScBRh₃ in the (110) plane is in agreement with that already reported by Sahara et al. [1]. It is evident that there are electrons shared between Rh and B, which is consistent with the notion of strong covalent-ionic interaction between these constituents. While there is little charge between Rh and Sc, the whole plane

can be characterized by a uniform charge distribution of the Fermi gas. This is characteristic of metallic bonding. Also, there may be some ionic interaction between these two constituents, but it is probably screened due to this Fermi gas. $RBRh_3$ are characterized by strongly coupled Rh–B layers in the (1 1 0) plane which explain that both the bulk and shear moduli of these compounds are larger on average: 197 and 93 GPa, respectively. It is likely that strong covalent-ionic Rh–B layers are interleaved with predominantly metallic Rh–$R$ layers and the coupling between them is weak. Thus, it is suggested [40] that the bulk modulus-to-shear modulus ratio is large due to this weak coupling, which in turn may enhance the ductility of these compounds. To further explore the chemical bonding in the demonstrator phase, ScBRh₃, the partial densities of states (PDOSs) are provided in Fig. 7. Our calculated PDOS spectra are in good agreement with those previously reported [1]. The PDOS above the Fermi level are antibonding states of Rh-4d, B-2p and Sc-3d. For the PDOS below the Fermi level, it is evident that the Rh and B states, as well as the Rh and Sc states, are hybridized. The Sc-3d orbitals slightly hybridize with those of Rh-4d where most of the occupied states are the Rh-4d states. Therefore the bonding between Sc and Rh is ionic with some covalent and metallic character. The B-2p orbitals hybridize strongly with the Rh-4d and there are bonding states (at around $\sim$-6.8 to -3.5 eV) near the bottom of the d band. The average ratio of the bulk and shear moduli for $RBRh_3$ compounds is about 2.2 and the average Poisson's ratio is about 0.3, indicating that the metallic character is not totally constrained by the strong coupling. The $RBRh_3$ compounds clearly behave plastically (or metallically), as predicted by the $B/G$ ratio and the relatively high Poisson's ratio.

### 3.7. Phonon properties

Phonon dispersion along high-symmetry directions in the Brillouin zone and phonon density of states (DOSs) including total DOS and partial DOS (PDOS) of the $RBRh_3$ ($R$ = Sc, Y and La), are shown in Figs. 8–10, respectively. These calculations were done by using calculated lattice constants (ScBRh₃ = 4.127 Å, YBRh₃ = 4.194 Å and LaBRh₃ = 4.281 Å). The experimental data of the phonon spectrum for these materials are not yet available. As there are no imaginary frequencies in these spectra, the materials can be considered structurally stable. Owing to the mass ratio of B and the other atoms there is a clear band gap for these materials. The band gap of ScBRh₃, YBRh₃ and LaBRh₃ are around 8.01 THz, 7.33 THz and 4.60 THz, respectively. For cubic perovskite $RBRh_3$ ($R$ = Sc, Y and La), the total DOS at high frequencies mainly comes from the B states. The phonon-PDOS shapes of the Rh-1, Rh-2 and Rh-3 states of ScBRh₃, YBRh₃ and LaBRh₃ are quite similar. In ScBRh₃, YBRh₃ and LaBRh₃ the sharp and highest peak is observed at 5.41 THz, 5.23 THz and 4.60 THz, respectively. The peaks are composed of states of $R$ atoms and a small fraction of states of Rh-1 atoms. Zone-centre optical phonon modes are calculated to be 4.07 THz, 4.12 THz, 5.20 THz, 17.27 THz for ScBRh₃, 3.57 THz, 4.36 THz, 5.22 THz, 15.51 THz for YBRh₃ and 3.04 THz, 4.17 THz, 4.57 THz, 13.92 THz for LaBRh₃.
