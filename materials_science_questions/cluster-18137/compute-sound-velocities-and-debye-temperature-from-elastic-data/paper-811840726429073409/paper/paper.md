## ABSTRACT

In this paper the thermal variation of volume for NaCl, KCl, MgO and CaO has been investigated on the basis of Anderson model. We have evaluated the values of elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{44}$ and $K_{T}$ at different temperature on the basis of Murnaghan and Tallon models. Tallon's model with second approximation presents slightly better agreement with experimental results which shows that the Anderson–Grüneisen parameter is directly proportional to the volume ratio. Tallon's model can be used to evaluate the values of elastic constants for solids at different temperatures.

## 1. Introduction

For proper design and selection of solids for their thermal properties it is very important to have the sufficient knowledge about the thermal variation of elastic properties of solids. A lot of investigations have been done to characterize the thermal variations of elastic properties for ionic solids [1–11]. A number of existing equations of states (EoSs) can be reviewed from Anderson's book on equation of states of solids [12]. To make a good understanding for the equation of state for an ionic solid, the analysis of temperature dependence behaviour of elasticity is more informative than the thermal variation of volume. Moreover information regarding volume change can be realized from the compressibility variation. Though theoretical background in the thermoelastic properties is already there [12], still a complete general theory is lacking. Anderson [13] has reported the experimental data for temperature dependence of thermodynamic and elastic properties for many solids. Here we report a theoretical fitting of thermal variations of elastic constants for some ionic rock salt based cubic structured solids of structure AB type. The elastic properties of these solids such as the bulk modulus and the shear moduli are described in terms of independent second-order elastic constants, viz. $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$.

The EoSs are of three categories namely, based on finite strain, assumed relationships between the variables, and interatomic potentials [12]. EoS based on finite strain is straightforward in a sense that the strain can be uniquely determined by stress. The models due to Murnaghan [13] and Tallon [14] are EoS based on finite strain. In this paper we have presented a theoretical calculation of the temperature dependence of elastic constants for NaCl, KCl, MgO and CaO based on Murnaghan and Tallon models. The results are compared with the experimental values of elastic constants at defferent temperatures. The Debye temperature ($\theta_{D}$) for NaCl and KCl are close to room temperature (304 and 230 K), whereas those for CaO and MgO are much higher (945 and 670 K). We have taken a temperature range from room temperature to 950 K for NaCl, KCl and upto 2800 K for CaO and MgO.

## 2. Method of analysis

For investigating the temperature dependence of isothermal bulk modulus ($K_{T}$), Anderson introduced a parameter which is known as the Anderson–Grüneisen parameter and is defined as follows [12,15]:

$$\delta_{T}=-\frac{1}{\alpha K_{T}}\left(\frac{\partial K_{T}}{\partial T}\right)$$

where $\alpha$ is the coefficient of volume thermal expansion, and $T$ is the temperature. $\delta_{T}$ is related to Grüneisen parameter ($\gamma$) as

$$\delta_{T}=\left(\frac{\partial \ln \gamma}{\partial \ln V}\right)_{T}+K_{T}^{f}-1 \tag{1}$$

$K_T'$ is pressure derivative of $K_T$. The Grüneisen parameter is related to the thermoelastic properties of the solid by the formula
$$
\gamma=\frac{\alpha K_{S} V}{C_{P}}=\frac{\alpha K_{T} V}{C_{V}} \tag{2}
$$
where $C_P$ and $C_V$ are the specific heats at constant pressure and constant volume, respectively. $K_S$ is adiabatic bulk modulus. $K_T$ and $K_S$ are related as
$$
K_{S}=K_{T}(1+\alpha \gamma T) \tag{3}
$$

Though Grüneisen parameter is an important thermodynamic quantity and experimentally can be measured, Anderson-Grüneisen parameter is straightforward and simple to correlate the thermoelastic properties. Now substituting $\alpha=(1 / V)(\partial V / \partial t)_{P}$, the expression for $\delta_T$ can be written as
$$
\delta_{T}=-\frac{V}{K_{T}}\left(\frac{\partial K_{T}}{\partial V}\right)_{P} \tag{4}
$$

According to Tallon [14] Eq. (4) is applicable for any of the elastic moduli. Hence we can replace $K_T$ by $C_{ij}$ to get a modified relation as
$$
\delta_{i j}=-\frac{V}{C_{i j}}\left(\frac{\partial C_{i j}}{\partial V}\right)_{P} \tag{5}
$$
where $\delta_{ij}$ is the Anderson-Grüneisen parameter associated with the elastic modulus $C_{ij}$. Now we will consider two models regarding the variation of $\delta_{ij}$. In Murnaghan's model [13], $\delta_{ij}$ would be taken as constant with the change in volume. Hence Eq. (5) becomes
$$
\frac{C_{i j}}{C_{i j 0}}=\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}\right)^{-\delta_{i j}} \tag{6}
$$
where $C_{ij0}$ is the value of elastic constant $C_{ij}$ and $V_0$ is the value of $V$ at initial temperature $T=300 \mathrm{~K}$. Values of $\delta_{ij}$ can be calculated from the room temperature experimental data using the relation
$$
\delta_{i j}=-\frac{1}{\alpha C_{i j}}\left(\frac{\partial C_{i j}}{\partial T}\right) \tag{7}
$$

In the model of Talllon [14] $\delta_{ij}$ would be considered as directly proportional to the volume ratio $V/V_0$ and Eq. (5) can be expressed as
$$
\frac{C_{i j}}{C_{i j 0}}=\exp \left\{-\delta_{i j}\left(\frac{V}{V_{0}}-1\right)\right\} \tag{8}
$$

Anderson et al. [16] showed that at higher temperatures $(T>\theta_D$, Debye's temperature) the variation of the coefficient of thermal expansion $(\alpha)$ with $T$ can be expressed as the following relation:
$$
\alpha=\alpha_{D}\left[1-\alpha_{D} \delta_{T}^{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)\right]^{-1} \tag{9}
$$

Here $\alpha_D$ is the values of $\alpha$ at initial temperature $T_D$, which is nearer to Debye's temperature. Anderson [13] modified Eq. (9) to first order approximation as
$$
\alpha=\alpha_{D}\left[1+\alpha_{D} \delta_{T}^{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)\right] \tag{10}
$$

For better fitting we approximated Eq. (9) to two order as
$$
\alpha=\alpha_{D}\left[1+\alpha_{D} \delta_{T}^{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)+\alpha_{D}^{2} \delta_{T}^{D 2}\left(T-T_{D}\right)^{2}\right] \tag{11}
$$

From the definition of $\alpha$ we get the following relations as first and second order approximations, respectively,
$$
\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}\right)_{P}=\alpha_{D}\left[1+\alpha_{D} \delta_{T}^{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)\right] \tag{12}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}\right)_{P}=\alpha_{D}\left[1+\alpha_{D} \delta_{T}^{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)+\alpha_{D}^{2} \delta_{T}^{D 2}\left(T-T_{D}\right)^{2}\right] \tag{13}
$$

After integrating over Eqs. (12) and (13) an explicit relation of volume as function of temperature can be obtained as follows:
$$
\frac{V(T)}{V\left(T_{D}\right)}=1+\alpha_{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)+\frac{1}{2} \alpha_{D}^{2} \delta_{T}^{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)^{2} \tag{14}
$$
and
$$
\frac{V(T)}{V\left(T_{D}\right)}=1+\alpha_{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)+\frac{1}{2} \alpha_{D}^{2} \delta_{T}^{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{3} \alpha_{D}^{3} \delta_{T}^{D 2}\left(T-T_{D}\right)^{3} \tag{15}
$$

With Murnaghan's model the thermal variation of $C_{ij}$ can be given as [Eqs. (6), (14), (15)]
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{i j}(T) & =\left[1+\alpha_{D}\left(T-T_{D}\right)\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{1}{2} \alpha_{D}^{2} \delta_{i j}\left(T-T_{D}\right)^{2}\right]^{-\delta_{i j}} C_{i j 0} \text { (first order approximation) } \quad(16)
\end{aligned}
$$

and

& \left.\left.+\frac{1}{3} \alpha_{D}^{3} \delta_{i j}^{2}\left(T-T_{D}\right)^{3}\right)\right\} C_{i j 0}
\tag{19}
\end{aligned}
$$

In first approximation, for all the solids investigated in this work, the temperature variation of volume follows parabolic relation with the characteristic values of $\alpha_{D}$ and $\delta_{D}$ which are summarized in Table 1. For curve fitting we have used both approximations. Obviously the second approximation is better fitted with the experimental data. The temperature variation of bulk modulus $(K_{T})$ NaCl, KCl, MgO and CaO solids (Figs. 5–8) has been calculated using Eq. (17) according to Murnaghan's model and using Eq. (19) according to Tallon's model. It is noteworthy that for the calculation of bulk modulus $C_{ij}$ is replaced by $K_{T}$ in Eqs. (17) and (19). Excluding in the case of NaCl, the difference in theoretical curve fitting due to two models in other solids in the work is not so pronounced.

Experimental data for thermal variation of $K_{T}$ were taken from literature [17].

We have calculated the theoretical values for $C_{11}$ and $C_{44}$ for NaCl, KCl, MgO and CaO using Eqs. (16)–(19). For the input parameter data in these equations we have calculated the values of $\delta_{ij}$ using Eq. (7) and taking the room temperature data reported by Anderson and Isaak [17]. Values of $\delta_{ij}$ thus obtained are presented in Table 1. The theoretical and experimental values of elastic constants $(C_{11}$ and $C_{44})$ for NaCl, KCl, MgO and CaO have been compared. The elastic constants of MgO which are of as much interest to the geophysicist are presented in Table 2. A good agreement has been found between the experimental data and the theoretical calculation based on the models of Murnaghan and Tallon.

### 4. Conclusion

The variation of volume with temperature for ionic solids has been investigated on the basis of Anderson equation with first and second order approximations. With the second order approximation the theoretical curve fitting is more close to experimental data. The values of elastic constants at different temperature have been calculated based on the models of Murnaghan and Tallon. Tallon's model with second approximation presents slightly better agreement with experimental results. This fact describes that the Anderson-Grüneisen parameter can be considered as directly proportional to the volume ratio. This theoretical method can be used to predict successfully the values of elastic constants for solids.
