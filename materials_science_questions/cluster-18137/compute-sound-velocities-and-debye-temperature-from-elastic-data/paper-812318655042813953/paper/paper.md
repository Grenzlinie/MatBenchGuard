# Elastic behaviour of mixed manganese-magnesium ferrites

S. RAMANA MURTHY, P. VENUGOPAL REDDY, T. SESHAGIRI RAO
Physics Department, College of Science, Osmania University, Hyderabad 500 007, India

Manganese-magnesium ferrites and ferrite compositions based on them gained wide application in radio and electronics [1]. Although the magnetic, dielectric and electrical properties of manganese-magnesium ferrites have been investigated in detail [2]. However, the elastic behaviour has not been systematically studied so far. A study of the elastic behaviour is of particular significance in the theory of solids in understanding the nature of the interatomic and interionic forces in them. Further, a detailed investigation of the dependence of elastic moduli on magnetic field or $\Delta E$ effect is interesting from the stand point of explaining the domain process occurring in the ferrite material. Thus, a study of the dependence of the elastic behaviour of Mn-Mg ferrites on composition and magnetic field at room temperature has been undertaken. The results are presented in this paper.

Young's modulus $(E)$ and the rigidity modulus $(\mu)$ of polycrystalline Mn-Mg ferrites were measured by using the composite oscillator [3]. Mixed Mn-Mg ferrites having the chemical formula $\mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Mg}_{x} \mathrm{Fe}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{4}$ ($x=0$ to 1) are prepared by the double sintering method. X-ray diffraction patterns for these mixed ferrites have been obtained using $\mathrm{Cu} K \alpha$ radiation. The lattice constants for ferrites were calculated and it is observed that the variation of lattice constants with composition is linear (Table I). Bulk densities of samples are determined from volume and weight measurements in air at room temperature $(30^{\circ} \mathrm{C})$ are found to be $95\%$ of the corresponding X-ray densities in all the cases.

The experimental values of the elastic moduli obtained on porous specimens at room temperature are corrected to theoretical density using the method of Mackenzie [4]. The corrected values of Young's modulus $(E)$ and rigidity modulus $(\mu)$, together with calculated values of the bulk modulus $(k)$, compressibility $(\beta)$ and Poisson's ratio $(\sigma)$ are presented in Table I.

Following Wooster's work [5] the experimental data are interpreted in terms of the binding forces between the atoms. It can be observed from Table I that an addition of 0.25 mol magnesium ferrite to manganese ferrite has increased the value of the $E$ by about $15\%$. Similarly an addition of 0.1 mol manganese ferrite to magnesium ferrite has resulted in an increase in $E$ of about $5\%$. The value of the rigidity modulus is increased by about $15\%$ and $9\%$, respectively. Further addition of magnesium ferrite to manganese ferrite has also resulted in an increase of the values of $E$ and $\mu$. This shows that an addition of a small quantity of magnesium ferrite to manganese ferrite or manganese ferrite to magnesium ferrite has resulted in an increase in the binding forces between the atoms. It can also be observed from the table that the values of $k$ and $\sigma$ are smaller for mixed magnesium-manganese ferrites of various compositions than for magnesium ferrite.

In a paper dealing with the magnetic and magnetostrictive properties of mixed ferrites, Hoffmann [6] reported the variation of the modulus of elasticity with composition for Mg- Ni ferrites. The density of magnesium ferrites employed by Hoffmann is $4.45 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$. The value of Young's modulus reported by him is $19.20 \times 10^{10}\ \text{N m}^{-2}$ which is in good agreement with the present value of $19.08 \times 10^{10}\ \text{N m}^{-2}$. Sukurai [7] reported the values of $E$ and $\mu$ for single crystals of $\text{MnFe}_2\text{O}_4$ to be $15.04 \times 10^{10}\ \text{N m}^{-2}$ and $6.26 \times 10^{10}\ \text{N m}^{-2}$ respectively. His values are in good agreement with the experimental values obtained for manganese ferrite of the present study (Table I). No other elastic data are available for Mg-Mn ferrites in the literature for comparison.

Longitudinal and shear velocities ($V_1$ and $V_\text{s}$) were calculated from the corresponding elastic moduli corrected to theoretical density for Mn- Mg ferrites. Anderson [8] demonstrated that $V_1/\rho$ and $V_\text{s}/\rho$ are constant for oxide minerals having nearly the same mean atomic weight. The mean atomic weight is given by $M/P$, where $M$ is the molecular weight of the substance and $P$ is the number of atoms in the molecule. From the values of $V_{1}$ and $V_{\mathrm{s}}$ the Debye temperature $(\theta)$ has been calculated using the simple method given by Anderson [10]. Fig. 1 shows a plot of $E$ against magnetic induction $(B)$ for all $\mathrm{Mn}-\mathrm{Mg}$ ferrites at room temperature. It can be seen from the figure that, in all the cases the values of $E$ continuously increases with increase of $B$ and finally become constant for saturation values of magnetic induc- tion $(B_{\mathrm{s}})$.

With a view to obtain an idea of the magnitude of the $\Delta E$ effect, the percentage values of $(E_{\mathrm{s}}-$$E)/E_{\mathrm{s}}=\Delta E/E_{\mathrm{s}}$ have been computed in each case and they are given in Table III. It may be seen from the table that the values of $\Delta E/E_{\mathrm{s}}$ manganese ferrite increases with increasing magnesium ferrite content.

Following the work of Lewis and Street [11] the $\Delta E$ effect in a polycrystalline magnetic material could possibly be explained on the basis of two processes (i) the domain rotation against uniaxial strain anisotropy and (ii) the movement of $90^{\circ}$ boundary walls. The contribution of the domain rotation against the uniaxial strain anisotropy $\Delta(1/E)_{\mathrm{U}}$ and the contribution of $90^{\circ}$ boundary wall $\Delta(1/E)_{90}$ are calculated using Equations 1 and 2, respectively.

$$
\Delta\left(\frac{1}{E}\right)_{\mathrm{U}}=\frac{9 \lambda_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\left(\mu_{0}-1\right)}{20 \pi I_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}} \tag{1}
$$

and

$$
\Delta\left(\frac{1}{E}\right)_{90}=\frac{9 \lambda_{100}^{2}\left(\mu_{0}-1\right)}{40 \pi I_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}} \tag{2}
$$

where $I_{\mathrm{s}}$ is the saturation magnetization, $\mu_{0}$ the initial permeability, $\lambda_{100}$ the magnetostriction in the direction $\langle 100\rangle$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{s}}$ saturation mag- netostriction. For the calculation of these quan- tities the values of $I_{\mathrm{s}}, \lambda_{\mathrm{s}}$ and $\lambda_{100}$ were determined in this laboratory and are given in the Table III.

The sum of $\Delta(1/E)_{\mathrm{U}}$ and $\Delta(1/E)_{90}$ gives $\Delta(1/E)_{\text{cal}}$ which is compared with the observed values $\Delta(1/E)_{\text{obs}}$. The values of $\Delta(1/E)_{\text{obs}}$ are calculated from the present experimental data using the relation: $\Delta(1/E)_{\text{obs}}=[(1/E)-(1/E_{\mathrm{s}})]$, where $E$ is the Young's modulus at zero magnetic field and $E_{\mathrm{s}}$ is the corresponding value at saturation magnetization. It can be seen from Table III that the values of $\Delta(1/E)_{\text{cal}}$ and $\Delta(1/E)_{90}$ are in fair agreement with each other in the cases of $\mathrm{MnFe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Mn}_{0.5}\mathrm{Mg}_{0.5}\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4}$. This shows that in these ferrites the contribution to the $\Delta E$ effect is due to both the domain processes, namely, domain rotation against uniaxial strain anisotropy and movement of $90^{\circ}$ boundary walls. But in the cases of $\mathrm{Mn}_{0.75}\mathrm{Mg}_{0.25}\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4}$, $\mathrm{Mn}_{0.5}\mathrm{Mg}_{0.5}\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Mn}_{0.10}\mathrm{Mg}_{0.90}\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ results show that the con- tribution is due to the domain rotation against uniaxial strain anisotropy can alone explain the observed $\Delta E$ effect. We could not make similar calculations in the case of $\mathrm{MgFe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4}$, because of the non-availability of the initial permeability data in the literature.
