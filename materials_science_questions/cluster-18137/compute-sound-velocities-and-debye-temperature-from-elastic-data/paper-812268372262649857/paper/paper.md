# Lattice Dynamics of Potassium Halides
(KCl, KBr and KI)

M. S. KUSHWAHA and S. S. KUSHWAHA

Department of Physics, Banaras Hindu University,
Varanasi-221005, India

(Received September 3, 1979)

To investigate the lattice dynamics of potassium halides a newly proposed 7-parameter bond-bending force model (BBFM) is used. The total potential energy of the system incorporates the short-range forces (radial +bond-bending) as well as the long range Coulombic interactions. The 7 parameters employed in the present treatment are determined by making use of the experimental values of three elastic constants, four critical point phonon frequencies and the lattice equilibrium condition. The model has been applied to calculate the phonon dispersion relations, phonon density of states and the Debye-characteristic tem- peratures $(\theta_{D})$ of KCl, KBr, and KI. Theoretical results have been compared with the available experimental data. It is observed that the agreement between theory and experiment is very good and thus gives a confidence in this approach. The remarkable features pertaining to the physical significance of the model have been discussed.

## §1. Introduction
Since the pioneer work of Kellerman $^{1)}$ the lattice dynamics of the alkali halides has been devoted a considerable attention theoreti- cally $^{2-6)}$ as well as experimentally. $^{7-14)}$ The review of all this work can be seen in the articles written by Cochran, $^{15)}$ Sinha $^{16)}$ and others. $^{17)}$ The reason for this interest lies in their simple crystal and electronic structure which facilitate the theoretical calculations and also the fact that a great deal of experimental work has been carried out on thermodynamical, optical, electrical and other properties of these solids. Theoreticians have so far developed a large number of lattice dynamical models, which can be basically typified in two categories: (i) Rigid-Ion-Model (RIM) where the atoms are supposed to be rigid-spheres and (ii) Shell-Model (SM) wherein the polarizability of atoms is also taken into account. Several ramifications of RIM and SM have reached the stage that they explain various dynamical properties satisfactorily, at least up to some extent. SM, originally due to Dick and Overhauser, $^{18)}$ is the most elaborate model so far employed for studying the lattice dynamics of various class of crystals. However, it (SM) has been many times questioned on the funda- mental grounds. This is evident from the fact that a simple SM utilizing three or four force constants almost invariably fails to give even an approximate phonon dispersion relation. In cases SM has given a good agreement with experiment, quite of the model has employed a large number of parameters (sometimes as large as 14) and the evaluation thereof requires an extensive fitting of neutron scattering data, if available. Further, any such attempt as forc- ing SM to fit the phonon dispersion curves renders some of the parameters of the model to take quite unphysical values. The SM is, therefore, unsuitable for the crystals where sufficient neutron data are not available. On the other hand RIM and its several modified versions have been used, with a reasonable success, to explain the dynamical properties of various crystals. $^{19)}$ A more fundamental theory, in which, (many-body) interatomic forces are treated quantum mechanically from first principles, $^{20,21)}$ was developed by Lundquist, $^{22)}$ though originally putforward by Lowdin, $^{23)}$ but the greater difficulty of this approach has so far prevented a satisfactory calculation of the dynamical properties. More- over, it is questionable whether inclusion of these many-body-force terms can be termed as quantum mechanical since the parameters selected are as much as any thing to give a best overall agreement with the measured phonon dispersion curves and thus these are still model calculations.

In view of the situation we have recently developed a 7-parameter bond-bending force model (BBFM)²⁴⁾ for NaCl-structure with its successful application on LiF and NaCl. Since the agreement between theory and experiment was found to be encouraging, we thought it worthwhile to study the lattice dynamical properties of other members of this family. The purpose of the present paper is thus to give details of the BBFM with its application on KCl, KBr and KI to calculate their phonon dispersion relations, phonon density of states and Debye-characteristic temperatures below room temperature. The available experimental data have been used as a check on the adequecy of the model. The comparison reveals a fairly good agreement between theory and experiments. The merits and demerits, with possibilities of the improvement, of the model have been discussed.

## §2. Model Building

The secular determinant used for determining the frequencies of the normal modes of vibrations is usually written as
$$|D(\boldsymbol{q})-\omega^{2}\tilde{I}|=0,\tag{1}$$
where $\omega$ is the angular phonon frequency, $\tilde{I}$ the 6×6 unit matrix and $D(\boldsymbol{q})$ the 6×6 dynamical matrix, mathematically expressed as
$$D(\boldsymbol{q})=\begin{bmatrix}
A & B \\
B^{*} & A'
\end{bmatrix},\tag{2}$$
where $A$ and $B$ are 3×3 submatrices whose matrix elements are such as
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\alpha \beta} & =A_{\alpha \beta}^{C}+A_{\alpha \beta}^{B}+A_{\alpha \beta}^{L}, \\
B_{\alpha \beta} & =B_{\alpha \beta}^{C}+B_{\alpha \beta}^{B}+B_{\alpha \beta}^{L},
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
$$
where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ refer to the cartesian indices ($x, y, z$) and the superscripts $C$, $B$ and $L$ to the matrix elements respectively due to central forces, bond bending forces and the long-range forces. $B^{*}$ is the complex conjugate of $B$ (since all the matrix elements (see appendix) are real so $B^{*}\equiv B$)). $A$ and $A'$ difer from each other in the sense that if the diagonal elements of $A$ are $A_{\alpha \alpha}-m_{1}\omega^{2}$, those of $A'$ will be $A_{\alpha \alpha}-m_{2}\omega^{2}$ and vice versa.

The total potential energy $\phi$ of the system is assumed to be made up of three parts and is expressed as
$$\phi=\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}+\phi_{3},\tag{4}$$
where $\phi_{1}$ is the potential energy giving rise to the central forces usually expressed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi_{1}= & \phi_{0}+\sum_{l k} \phi_{\alpha}(l k) u_{\alpha}(l k) \\
& +\frac{1}{2} \sum_{\substack{l k \alpha \\
l^{\prime} k^{\prime} \beta}} \phi_{\alpha \beta}\left(\begin{array}{c}
l k \\
l^{\prime} k^{\prime}
\end{array}\right) u_{\alpha}(l k) u_{\beta}\left(l^{\prime} k^{\prime}\right), \quad(5)
\end{aligned}
$$
in the harmonic approximation, where the symbols have their usual meanings.²⁵⁾ $\phi_{2}$ is the potential energy giving rise to the bond-bending forces, arising because of the deformations in certain interbond-angles and has been assumed to be of the form
$$\phi_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{2} / a^{3}\right) \gamma_{\theta}(\delta \theta)^{2},\tag{6}$$
where $(\delta \theta)$ is the change in the inter-bond-angles under consideration and $\gamma_{\theta}$ is the corresponding force constant. The term $(e^{2}/a^{3})$ is simply adjusted to keep the force constants in the units of $(e^{2}/V_{a})$; where $e$ is the electronic charge and a semilattice constant. $V_{a}(=2a^{3})$ is the volume of the unit cell. In order to facilitate the understanding of the physics of the bond-bending forces we have given a figure (Fig. 1) with the help of which the change in the angle $(\delta \theta)$ can be geometrically calculated as
$$(\delta \theta)=\left[\frac{\left(S_{A}-S_{O}\right) \cdot \hat{\varepsilon}_{n A}}{|O A|}+\frac{\left(S_{B}-S_{O}\right) \cdot \hat{\varepsilon}_{n B}}{|O B|}\right],\tag{7}$$
where $S_{A}$, $S_{O}$ and $S_{B}$ are the displacement vectors of the respective atoms $A$, $O$ and $B$. $\varepsilon_{n A}$ and $\varepsilon_{n B}$ are the unit vectors perpendicular to the vectors $OA$ and $OB$ respectively and are such that their direction Cosins always tend to reduce the angle $AOB$ (see Fig. 1).

equations of motion are set up by using $F=-\operatorname{grad} \phi_{2}$. It deserves mention here that choice of the angles is made such that no part of any angle should be reconsidered during the whole treatment. The details of all such angles under consideration have been given in the previous paper. $^{24)}$ The third potential energy $\phi_{3}$, giving rise to the volume dependent long-range Coulombic interactions, is usually of the form

$$
\phi_{3}=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{l k} \sum_{l^{\prime} k^{\prime} \neq l k} \frac{Z e_{k} \cdot Z e_{k^{\prime}}}{\left[r(l k)+u(l k)-r\left(l^{\prime} k^{\prime}\right)-u\left(l^{\prime} k^{\prime}\right)\right]},
$$

where the symbols have their usual meanings. $^{26)}$ The matrix-elements due to all the three types of interactions are given in Appendix.

Further the long wavelength limit method, adopted to calculate the elastic constants in terms of force constants, crystal stability (lattice equilibrium condition) and the method of parameter determination have been widely discussed in the previous paper. $^{24)}$ However, we would like to state briefly that the 7 disposable parameters involved in the present treatment have been evaluated by making use of the three elastic constants, four critical point phonon frequencies and the resulting lattice equilibrium condition $(\beta_{1}=-0.29126 Z^{2})$ . The use of lattice equilibrium condition is made to restrain the parameters within their physical significance. The experimental data, fed as input, for evaluating the parameters are given in the Table I alongwith the numerical values of the parameters in the units of $(2 e^{2} / V_{a})$ .

§3. Results and Discussion

The numerical values of the parameters (Listed in Table) have been utilized to solve the secular determinant, eq. (1), for wave vector $q$ at all the 48-irreducible points in the first Brillouin zone, divided into 1000 finer subdivisions. The resulting phonon frequencies versus reduced wave vector $(q / q_{max})$ are displayed in Fig. 2(a), (b) and (c) respectively for KCl, KBr, and KI along the principal symmetry directions [100], [110] and [111]. The available experimental points for KCl due to Copley et al., $^{14)}$ for KBr due to Woods et al. $^{7)}$ and for KI due to Dolling et al. $^{8)}$ have also been plotted in the respective figures for comparison. It is observed that there is overall a very good agreement between theory and experiment in all the three symmetry directions. The discrepancies between theory and experiment at few points are not significantly greater than normal in any particular region of the reciprocal space. We can, therefore, have confidence that model does give a good description of the frequencies of the normal modes of vibrations.

The phonon density of states $g(v)$ have been calculated and are plotted in the Fig. 3(a), (b) and (c) respectively for KCl, KBr and KI. The frequency spectrum for this purpose was divided into the frequency interval $\Delta v=v_{LO}(\Gamma) / 100$ . The calculated frequency distribution curves if compared with the experimental ones show a good similarity; e.g. the calculation confirms that there is indeed a gap in the frequency distribution of KI between 2.10 and 2.80 (in the units of THz) as is shown by Dolling et al. $^{8)}$ But KCl and KBr do not show such singularities except some negligible gaps appearing intermitantly over the whole range.

## Appendix

### (i) Matrix elements due to Short-Range central foces:

$$
A_{x x}^{C}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k
\end{array}\right)=\left(e^{2} / a^{3}\right)\left[2\left(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}\right)+2\left(\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}\right)\left(2-C_{x}\left(C_{y}+C_{z}\right)\right)+4 \beta_{2}\left(1-C_{y} C_{z}\right)\right],
$$

$$
A_{x y}^{C}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k
\end{array}\right)=\left(e^{2} / a^{3}\right)\left[2\left(\alpha_{2}-\beta_{2}\right) s_{x} s_{y}\right],
$$

$$
B_{x x}^{C}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k^{\prime}
\end{array}\right)=-\left(e^{2} / a^{3}\right)\left[2 \alpha_{1} C_{x}+2 \beta_{1}\left(C_{y}+C_{z}\right)\right],
$$

$$
B_{x y}^{C}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k^{\prime}
\end{array}\right)=0,
$$

where $\alpha_{i}$ and $\beta_{i}$ are the central force constants for first $(i=1)$ and second $(i=2)$ neighbours and are defined by

$$
\partial^{2} \phi_{1} / \partial r_{i}^{2}=\left(e^{2} / a^{3}\right) \alpha_{i} \quad \text { and } \quad \partial \phi_{1} / r_{i} \partial r_{i}=\left(e^{2} / a^{3}\right) \beta_{i}.
$$

Since first neighbour forces are assumed to be non-central they give rise to two force constants
$(\alpha_1$ and $\beta_1)$ and the second neighbour forces are assumed to be central they give rise to only one
force constant $(\alpha_2)$. We thus take $\beta_2$ (the second neighbour non-central force constant) to be zero
which lessened the number of parameters from eight to seven and facilitated the parameter deter-
mination as well.

(ii) Matrix elements due to the Bond-Bending forces:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{x x}^{B}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k
\end{array}\right)=&(e^{2}/a^{3})[\gamma_1'(6-2(C_x C_y+C_y C_z+C_z C_x)) \\
&+\gamma_2'(6-C_x(C_y+C_z))+\gamma_3'(10-C_x(C_y+C_z))],
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
A_{x y}^{B}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k
\end{array}\right)=(e^{2}/a^{3})[(\gamma_1'+\gamma_2')S_x S_y],
$$

$$
B_{x x}^{B}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k'
\end{array}\right)=-(e^{2}/a^{3})[2\gamma_2'(C_y+C_z)+2\gamma_3'(2C_x+C_y+C_z)],
$$

$$
B_{x y}^{B}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k'
\end{array}\right)=0.
$$

In all the above expressions $S_x=\sin aq_x$ and $C_x=\cos aq_x$ and so on, where $\boldsymbol{q}$ is the propagation
wave vector. $\gamma_i'$ is substituted for $(2\gamma_i/a^{2})$ for brevity. Other elements of $D(\boldsymbol{q})$ can be produced by
changing cartesian indices $(x, y, z)$ in the cyclic order.

(iii) Matrix elements due to volume dependent Long-Range coulombic forces:

For $\boldsymbol{q}=0$
$$
A_{x y}^{L}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k'
\end{array}\right)=(2\pi/3)(Z^{2}e^{2}/a^{3})\delta_{x y},
$$

For $\boldsymbol{q}\neq0$
$$
A_{x y}^{L}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k
\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{2}(Z^{2}e^{2}/a^{3})\left[-G_{x y}(k k)+H_{x y}^{l}+\frac{8\varepsilon^{3}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\delta_{x y}\right],
$$

$$
B_{x y}^{L}\left(\begin{array}{l}
\boldsymbol{q} \\
k k'
\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{2}(Z^{2}e^{2}/a^{3})[G_{x y}(k k')-H_{x y}^{m}],
$$

where
$$
G_{x y}(k k)=4\pi \sum_{\boldsymbol{h}} \frac{(\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h})_x(\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h})_y}{|\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h}|^{2}} \mathrm{e}^{-(\pi^{2}/4\varepsilon^{2})|\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h}|^{2}},
$$

$$
G_{x y}(k k')=4\pi \sum_{\boldsymbol{h}} \frac{(\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h})_x(\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h})_y}{|\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h}|^{2}} \mathrm{e}^{-(\pi^{2}/4\varepsilon^{2})|\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{h}|^{2}} \cos \pi(h_1+h_2+h_3),
$$

$$
H_{x y}^{l}=2 \sum_{\boldsymbol{l}} \left[-f(\boldsymbol{l})\delta_{x y}+g(\boldsymbol{l})\frac{l_x l_y}{\boldsymbol{l}^{2}}\right] \cos \pi(\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{l}),
$$

$$
H_{x y}^{m}=2 \sum_{\boldsymbol{m}} \left[-f(\boldsymbol{m})\delta_{x y}+g(\boldsymbol{m})\frac{m_x m_y}{\boldsymbol{m}^{2}}\right] \cos \pi(\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{m}),
$$

$$
g(\boldsymbol{l})=\frac{4\varepsilon^{3}}{\sqrt{\pi}} \mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon^{2}\boldsymbol{l}^{2}}+3f(\boldsymbol{l}),
$$

$$
f(\boldsymbol{l})=\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\boldsymbol{l}^{2}}+(1/\boldsymbol{l}^{3})\psi(\varepsilon l),
$$

The expressions for $g(\boldsymbol{m})$ and $f(\boldsymbol{m})$ can be written analogously, and
$$
\psi(\varepsilon l)=1-(2/\sqrt{\pi}) \int_{0}^{\varepsilon l} \mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}} \mathrm{~d}t.
$$

Here $\varepsilon$ is an arbitrary parameter, generally, taken to be unity. $\boldsymbol{h}(\equiv h_1, h_2, h_3)$ is the reciprocal lattice vector and $\boldsymbol{l}(\equiv l_1, l_2, l_3)$ and $\boldsymbol{m}(\equiv m_1, m_2, m_3)$ are the direct lattice vector. For the details of the physics of these volume dependent long range forces a reader is referred to the pioneer work of Kellerman. $^{1)}$
