# Mechanical and thermodynamic properties of two-dimensional Monoclinic $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}}$

Jie Su, $^{a,b\#}$ Junjing Zhang, $^{a\#}$ Rui Guo, $^{a}$ Zhenhua Lin, $^{a}$ Mengyu Liu, $^{a}$ Jincheng Zhang, $^{a,b}$ Jingjing Chang, $^{a,b*}$ Yue Hao$^{a,b}$

$^{a}$State Key Discipline Laboratory of Wide Band Gap Semiconductor Technology, Shaanxi Joint Key Laboratory of Graphene, School of Microelectronics, Xidian University, Xi'an, 710071, China.

$^{b}$Advanced Interdisciplinary Research Center for Flexible Electronics, Xidian University, Xi'an, 710071, China.

*E-mail: jjingchang@xidian.edu.cn

ABSTRACT: The discovery of two-dimensional (2D) $\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$ has provided an efficient way to design high performance $\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$ devices. Here, mechanical and thermodynamic properties of 2D $\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$ are important parameters in device design but very few studies are investigated by density functional theory. Results show that 2D $\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$ retains the monoclinic character with thirteen independent elastic constants which meet with the mechanical stability criteria, and irrespective of its thickness. Interestingly, although the elastic constant $\mathrm{C_{11}}$ is lower than $\mathrm{C_{22}}$ and $\mathrm{C_{33}}$, 2D $\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$ may be harder to be compressed along the $a$ direction. Meanwhile, the elastic constant $\mathrm{C_{44}}$ is larger than $\mathrm{C_{55}}$ and $\mathrm{C_{66}}$, in contrast to that of bulk $\beta$-$\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$. Upon decreasing the thickness of 2D $\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$, the elastic properties continue reduce and lower than those of 2D layered materials, but their compressive anisotropic properties are enhanced and larger than those of bulk $\beta$-$\mathrm{Ga_2O_3}$. The thermal conductivity and capacity of monolayer

$Ga_2O_3$ are enhanced to $0.49\ W{\cdot}cm^{-1}{\cdot}K^{-1}$, and $27.58\ J{\cdot}mol^{-1}{\cdot}K^{-1}$, respectively. Such variations of bulk-transition-2D $Ga_2O_3$ are opposite to those of bulk-transition-2D GaN due to the recombined orbitals of GaN. These results are crucial to the device design based on 2D $Ga_2O_3$.

KEYWORDS: Density functional theory; Two-dimensional; $Ga_2O_3$; Thermal conductivity; Elastic constant

### 1. Introduction

Monoclinic $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ is an ultra-wide band gap (~ 4.85 eV) transparent conducting oxide (TCO) with a large breakdown field of ~ 8 MV/cm, a high Baliga's figure of merit (over 3000) and suitable for mass production that is attracting increasing interest for high efficient power electronic devices capable with high temperature and high frequency operation. [1-6] Until now, several $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ based metal-semiconductor field-effect transistors (MESFETs) with ultra-low contact resistances,[7] metal oxide semiconductor field-effect transistors (MOSFETs) with 1.85 kV breakdown voltages,[8] and Schottky barrier diodes (SBDs) with 1600 V rectifier [9] have been designed and fabricated. Moreover, these excellent properties also make $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ promising for the other applications, such as solar-blind detector, transparent conductors, and gas sensors.[10-13] To further explore the potential performances and applications of $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$, many researchers have tried to slice $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ into two-dimensional (2D) flakes in the last five years, since the low-dimensional material usually possesses the large surface to volume ratio and strong quantum confinement effect.[14-16]

Moreover, theoretical investigations have predicted that 2D $Ga_2O_3$ has a larger band gap, a higher carrier mobility, and a stronger electron relaxation time and exciton binding energy than those of bulk $Ga_2O_3$.[17–20] In addition, Kim et al. have found that 2D $Ga_2O_3$ based photodetectors exhibit an excellent responsivity of $1.8×10^5$ A/W and a low response time (<0.3 s). [21,22] Zhou et al. have reported that the saturation current density, ON/OFF ratio, and breakdown field of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ based FETs have reached up to 600 mA/mm, $10^{10}$, and 5.2 MV/cm, respectively. [23–25] Meanwhile, forming 2D $Ga_2O_3$ could extend the application of $Ga_2O_3$ to the photocatalytic hydrogen evolution. [26]

Although 2D $Ga_2O_3$ based nanodevices have showed some more excellent performances than $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ based nanodevices, such variation mechanisms were still not understood comprehensively. According to previous studies, the performances of $Ga_2O_3$ based devices were significantly affected by the mechanical properties of $Ga_2O_3$, because they were not only indispensable to evaluate the stress field in the device caused by thermal process and lattice mismatch with other materials, but also closely related to the thermal stability and transport properties of materials. [17,27] Thus, to further understand the performances and applications of 2D $Ga_2O_3$, it is necessary to investigate the mechanical properties, including elastic constants, anisotropic properties and thermal capacity, of bulk and 2D $Ga_2O_3$. Nevertheless, just the elastic constants and thermal stabilities of bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ have been measured until now. [28–31] Hence, geometric structures, elastic constants, anisotropic and thermal properties of bulk and 2D $Ga_2O_3$ with different thickness were studied by density functional theory in this study.

## 2. Computational method

All first-principles calculations were performed by using density functional theory on the basis of projector augmented wave method as implemented in the VASP code. The local density approximation (LDA)[32] was used to describe the exchange and correlation interactions. The cut-off energy was set to 450 eV for geometry optimization and 500 eV for elastic constants calculations. The convergence criterions were $1{\times}10^{-6}$ eV for the self-consistent field energy and 0.001 eV/Å for the residual forces on each atom, respectively. Γ-centered k-meshes of 5×19×11, 3×19×11, 3×19×11, and 1×19×11were used for bulk, monolayer, bilayer, and trilayer $\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$, respectively. For 2D $\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$, they were obtained by slicing the bulk $\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$ along the (100) surfaces which exhibited the lowest surface energies and exfoliation energies[17,19]. In addition, vacuums of 15 Å were added to minimize the artificial interlayer interactions, as displayed in **Figure 1**. The detailed elastic constants calculation equations of monoclinic crystals were listed in supporting information file.

## 3. Results and discussion

Bulk β-Ga₂O₃ is a monoclinic phase with C2/m symmetry. It contains two inequivalent Ga atoms and three inequivalent O atoms, as shown in Figure 1a. Upon slicing bulk β-Ga₂O₃ into 2D Ga₂O₃, it remains the monoclinic character with two inequivalent Ga atoms and three inequivalent O atoms, and irrespective of the thickness of 2D Ga₂O₃, although it shows P2/m symmetry, as shown in Figure 1(b-c). The lattice parameters of bulk and 2D Ga₂O₃ with different thickness are listed in **Table 1**. Compared to the lattice constants obtained by GGA functional, the lattice constants of bulk Ga₂O₃ obtained by LDA functional (a = 12.21 Å, b = 3.04 Å, and c = 5.67 Å) are closer to the experimental values.[17,29,34,35] Thus, LDA

functional has been employed to calculate the elastic constants which are sensitive to the lattice constants. For bulk β-Ga₂O₃, all the Ga-O bonds show covalent characters with different bond strengths viz. the inconsistent orbital overlap in Figure 2 and lengths listed in Table 1. Moreover, the lengths of Ga-O bonds around octahedra, especially the Ga₁-O₁ bond, are larger than those around the tetrahedra in bulk β-Ga₂O₃. Thus, in the process of slicing β-Ga₂O₃, the Ga₁-O₁ bond in the bulk β-Ga₂O₃ is broken preferentially.[19] Upon forming the 2D Ga₂O₃, all the Ga-O bond lengths are slightly enlarged compared to those in bulk β-Ga₂O₃, leading to the weaker covalent characters for Ga-O bonds in 2D Ga₂O₃, which is further evidenced by the density of states in Figure 2 and Figure S1. Therefore, the lattice constants of 2D Ga₂O₃ are slightly larger than corresponding lattice constants of bulk β-Ga₂O₃. Moreover, as the thickness of 2D Ga₂O₃ increases, its Ga-O bond lengths decrease gradually and then approach those in the bulk β-Ga₂O₃. It results that the lattice constants of 2D Ga₂O₃ decrease and approximate those of bulk β-Ga₂O₃ with the increasing thickness of 2D Ga₂O₃. In addition, regardless of the thickness of 2D Ga₂O₃, the Ga₁-O bonds around octahedra in 2D Ga₂O₃ are still longer than those Ga₂-O bonds around the tetrahedra in monolayer Ga₂O₃, like to those in bulk β-Ga₂O₃.

![](./images/812736954905919488_3.jpg)

Figure 2. Density of states of (a) bulk and (b) monolayer $\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$ to show the covalent bond strengths of Ga-O bonds.

Geometric structures directly determine the mechanical properties. Bulk and 2D monoclinic $\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$ possess the same form for the elastic matrix with thirteen independent elastic constants:

$$
\left[C_{ij}\right] = \begin{bmatrix}
C_{11} & C_{12} & C_{13} & 0 & C_{15} & 0 \\
& C_{22} & C_{23} & 0 & C_{25} & 0 \\
& & C_{33} & 0 & C_{35} & 0 \\
& & & C_{44} & 0 & C_{46} \\
& sym. & & & C_{55} & 0 \\
& & & & & C_{66}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The calculated elastic constants of bulk $\beta$-$\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$ are close to available theoretical values obtained by similar and more accurate functionals and experimental data, as listed in Table 2. [30,36] Moreover, all the elastic constants of bulk $\beta$-$\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$ listed in Table 2 meet with the mechanical stability criteria,[37] indicating that bulk $\beta$-$\text{Ga}_2\text{O}_3$ is mechanic stability at the ambient condition. For the monoclinic crystal, the elastic constants $\text{C}_{11}$, $\text{C}_{22}$, and $\text{C}_{33}$ are in relation to the resistance along the $a$, $b$, $c$ directions under the uniaxial compression, respectively; while the elastic constants $\text{C}_{44}$, $\text{C}_{55}$, and $\text{C}_{66}$ are associated with the shear

behaviors along the directions vertical to $a$, $b$, and $c$ directions under the uniaxial stress, respectively. The elastic constant $C_{11}$ of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ is smaller than the elastic constants $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$, suggesting that bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ is softer along the $a$ direction than other directions since the Ga-O bond along the $a$ direction is longer and weaker than those located at the (100) surface, as listed in **Table 1**. Moreover, the elastic constant $C_{44}$ of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ is smaller than the elastic constants $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$, indicating that bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ is easier to be sheared along the direction vertical to the $a$ direction than other directions. That is why 2D Ga₂O₃ is commonly obtained by slicing the (100) surface of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃.[17,19] Noted that, the C15 is related to the elasticity along the (100) surface in shape, and a transverse strain causes a change in shape without a change in volume. Thus, the $C_{15}$ of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ is negative. In addition, the obvious difference among these elastic constants suggests an evident anisotropy for the mechanical properties of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃. The detailed anisotropic indexes $A_{\text{B}}$ and $A_{\text{G}}$, bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, Young's modulus $E$, and Poisson's ratio $v$ are measured by the Voigt-Reuss-Hill method [38-40] and demonstrated in Figure 3. The values of $A_{\text{B}}$ and $A_{\text{G}}$ of monolayer Ga₂O₃ are close to 1, and the elastic anisotropies in the compressive and shear behaviors are significant, respectively. The values of $A_{\text{B}}$ and $A_{\text{G}}$ for bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ are 0.02 and 0.1, respectively, suggesting a relatively strong elastic anisotropy in the shear behavior. The detailed compressive and shear anisotropy characters of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ are shown in **Figure 3b** and **3c**, respectively. In addition, the average $B$, $G$, and $E$ of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ are 189.83, 71.62, 190.86 GPa, respectively, which are consistent with previous reports.[30] According to previous study, B/G is associated with the ductility and brittleness of a material, and the critical value is about 1.75. Meanwhile, if the $v$ is larger than 0.26, the material is

ductile, otherwise, the material is brittle. The values of B/G and $v$ of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃ are larger than 1.75 and 0.26, respectively, suggesting a ductile character for bulk Ga₂O₃.

For the 2D Ga₂O₃, their elastic constants also meet with the mechanical stability criteria of monoclinic crystal, and irrespective of their thicknesses,[37] indicating the mechanic stability for monolayer, bilayer, and trilayer Ga₂O₃. Meanwhile, previous studies about binding energies and formation energies of hydrogen passivated 2D Ga₂O₃ revealed the thermodynamic stability for these 2D structures [17]. Thus, there is not any imaginary spectrum for the phonon dispersion of 2D Ga₂O₃ in ref. 17. Similar to those of bulk $\beta$-Ga₂O₃,

these elastic constants of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ are also different as listed in **Table 2**, which suggests an obvious anisotropy for 2D $Ga_2O_3$ with different thicknesses. Moreover, the anisotropic indexes $A_B$ and $A_G$ for monolayer, bilayer, and trilayer $Ga_2O_3$ are 0.89 and 0.75, 0.50 and 0.81, 0.47 and 0.95, respectively, which are larger than those of bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$, as demonstrated in **Figure 3a**. In other words, both the anisotropies in the compressive and shear behaviors are enhanced upon forming 2D $Ga_2O_3$. Moreover, with the increasing thickness of 2D $Ga_2O_3$, the anisotropies in the compressive and shear behaviors are reduced and increased, respectively. The detailed compressive and shear anisotropy characters of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ are shown in **Figure 3(e-d) and Figure S2-3**. Nevertheless, it should be noted that although the elastic constants $C_{11}$ of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ are also smaller than the elastic constants $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$ and like those in bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$, 2D $Ga_2O_3$ may be harder to be compressed along the $a$ direction than other directions, which is different to that of bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$. The low elastic constant $C_{11}$ of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ is attributed to the weak hydrogen bondings which stem from the passivated hydrogen atoms. At the same time, the Ga-O covalent bond along the $a$ direction is shorter and stronger than other Ga-O bonds of all the 2D $Ga_2O_3$, as listed in Table 1. Due to such bond characters, the elastic constants $C_{44}$ of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ are larger than the elastic constants $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$, which is in contrast to that of bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$. In addition, $C_{ii}$ (i=1,2, ….., 6) of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ are increased with its increasing thickness since the Ga-O bond lengths decrease with the increasing thickness of 2D $Ga_2O_3$, as listed in **Table 1**.

In addition, the $B$, $G$, $E$ of 2D $\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ enlarge with the increasing thickness of 2D $\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$. The modulus $B$, $G$, $E$ of monolayer, bilayer, and trilayer $\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ are 8.86, 5.02, 12.66 N/m, and 27.11, 15.20, 38.41 N/m, and 73.28, 38.67, 98.66 N/m, respectively, which are lower than those of 2D layered materials, such as graphene, BN, and TMDs.[41,42]. It suggests that 2D $\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ has a potential to be a flexible material. Moreover, the values of B/G and $v$ of all these 2D $\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ are larger than 1.75 and 0.26, respectively, but lower than those of bulk $\beta$-$\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$, which indicates that the ductile property of $\beta$-$\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ is weakened upon slicing into 2D $\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$. Moreover, such ductile property is further reduced with the decreasing thickness of 2D $\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ since the values of B/G and $v$ decrease with the decreasing thickness of 2D

$\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$, as displayed in Figure 3.

### 3.3 Thermal properties

Both thermal conductivity $k$ and Debye temperature $\Theta_{\mathrm{D}}$ are closely related to the above analyzed elastic properties and are important fundamental parameters for materials. The lowest thermal conductivity $k_{min}$ can be evaluated by equation (1). Debye temperature $\Theta_{\mathrm{D}}$ of 2D and bulk materials can be estimated from equations (2) and (3), respectively [29,46]

$$
k_{min}=0.87k_{B}N_{A}^{2/3}\frac{\mathrm{N}^{2/3}\rho^{1/6}E^{1/2}}{M^{2/3}} \tag{1}
$$

$$
\Theta_{D}=\frac{\mathrm{h}v_{m}}{2\pi k_{B}}\left(\frac{4\pi N}{S}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{2}
$$

$$
\Theta_{D}=\frac{h}{k_{B}}\left[\frac{3 N}{4 \pi}\left(\frac{N_{A} \rho}{M}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{3}} v_{m}
\tag{3}
$$

where $h$ and $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ are the Planck and Boltzmann constants, respectively, $N$ and $S$ correspond to the number of atoms and the atomic area in the unit cell, respectively, $M$ is the weight of unit cell, $N_{\mathrm{A}}$ is Avogadro's number, $\rho$ is the density, $E$ is the Young's modulus. The average sound velocity $v_{\mathrm{m}}$ is obtained from

$$
v_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{l}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{t}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{3}}, \quad v_{l}=\sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho\left(1-v^{2}\right)}}, \quad v_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{E}{2 \rho(1+v)}}
\tag{4}
$$

Where $v_{\mathrm{m}}, v_{\mathrm{l}}, v_{\mathrm{t}}$ correspond to average, compressional and shear wave velocities, respectively.

In general, sound phonon is the heat-carrying ion of semiconductor and the average waver velocity determine the ability of heat transport. **Table 3** lists the $k_{min}$, $\Theta_{\mathrm{D}}$ and sound velocities of bulk and 2D $\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$. It can be found from **Table 3** that the $v_{\mathrm{m}}$ is enlarged and further improved when $\beta-\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ is changed into $2 \mathrm{D} \mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ with thinner thickness. It indicates that forming $2 \mathrm{D} \mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$, especially monolayer $\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$, can significantly improve the thermal conductivity of $\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$. One main reason is that, upon forming $2 \mathrm{D} \mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ with the light hydrogen passivation, the weight of unit cell is reduced, which can significantly enhance the thermal conductivity according to the equation (1). The calculated $k_{min}$ of bulk $\beta-\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ is about $0.28 \mathrm{~W} \cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{K}^{-1}$ which is consistent with previous experimental value of 0.27 $\mathrm{W} \cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{K}^{-1}$. The $k_{min}$ can be enlarged by slicing bulk $\beta-\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ into $2 \mathrm{D} \mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$. Such character is opposite to that of GaN because the orbital hybridization is disturbed by Ga-d orbitals when bulk GaN is sliced into monolayer GaN, while the orbital distributions remain when bulk $\beta-\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ is cut into $2 \mathrm{D} \mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$.[17,43] In addition, the $k_{min}$ can be further improved as the thickness of $2 \mathrm{D} \mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ decreases. The $k_{min}$ of monolayer, bilayer, and trilayer $\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ are 0.49,

0.35, and 0.29 W·cm⁻¹·K⁻¹, respectively, which are larger than that of monolayer GaN (0.15 W·cm⁻¹·K⁻¹).[47] In other words, design 2D with thinner thickness is beneficial to improve the thermal damage of Ga₂O₃ device. In addition, the calculated Θ_D and sound velocities of bulk β-Ga₂O₃ are in good agreement with previous reports.[43,44] Note that, the Θ_D of bulk β-Ga₂O₃ of about 750.54 K is smaller than the value of 872 K obtained by GGA functional, since GGA functional usually overestimates the volume of crystal which significantly affects the Debye temperature.[31,48] When bulk β-Ga₂O₃ is sliced into 2D phase, both the sound velocities and Θ_D are reduced. Moreover, they significantly decrease with the decreasing thickness of 2D Ga₂O₃. The values of Θ_D of trilayer, bilayer and monolayer Ga₂O₃ are 717.98, 602.28, and 460.72 K, respectively. It suggests that 2D Ga₂O₃, especially monolayer Ga₂O₃, is easier to be deformed and used as flexible material compared to bulk β-Ga₂O₃.

Figure 4 exhibits the temperature dependence of specific heat capacity $C_V$ of bulk and 2D $Ga_2O_3$. The $C_V$ of bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ enlarges rapidly as the temperature increases, and then approaches a constant of $20.69\ J{\cdot}mol^{-1}{\cdot}K^{-1}$ when the temperature is larger than $\Theta_D$. This is owing to that significant quantum mechanical effect influences the heat thermodynamic and heat capacity properties when the atmosphere temperature is lower than $\Theta_D$, while the quantum mechanical effect could be neglected above $\Theta_D$.[43] Such variation tendency of bulk $Ga_2O_3$ is in agreement with previous experimental results, except for a small discrepancy between the theoretical and experimental saturated $C_V$, as demonstrated in Figure 4, since tiny impurities exist in experimental samples. [43,49–52] Compared to bulk $Ga_2O_3$, similar variations are also observed for the specific heat capacity of 2D $Ga_2O_3$. However, it should be noted that the rate of variation and the saturated specific heat capacity $C_V$ of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ rapidly enhances as the thickness of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ reduces, as displayed in Figure 4. This not only further suggests that the bulk modulus of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ is larger than that of bulk $Ga_2O_3$, but also indicates that forming 2D $Ga_2O_3$, especially monolayer$Ga_2O_3$, can obviously improve the thermal properties of $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$. The saturated specific heat capacity $C_V$ of trilayer, bilayer and monolayer $Ga_2O_3$ are up to $22.33, 24.06, 27.58\ J{\cdot}mol^{-1}{\cdot}K^{-1}$, respectively.

### 4. Conclusion

In summary, geometric, mechanical and thermodynamic properties of bulk and 2D $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$ with different thicknesses were comprehensively investigated by density functional theory. Similar to bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$, 2D $Ga_2O_3$ retains the monoclinic character but with the P2/m symmetry, and irrespective of its thickness. Its thirteen independent elastic constants are met

with the mechanical stability criteria. Interestingly, although the $C_{11}$ is lower than $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$,
2D $Ga_2O_3$ may be harder to be compressed along the $a$ direction due to the shorter and
stronger Ga-O covalent bond along the $a$ direction than other directions. Meanwhile, the $C_{44}$
is larger than $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$, which is in contrast to those of bulk $\beta$-$Ga_2O_3$. In addition, upon
forming 2D $Ga_2O_3$, both the anisotropies in the compressive and shear behaviors are
enhanced. Moreover, as the thickness of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ decreases, the modulus $B$, $G$, $E$, and $\Theta_D$ of
2D $Ga_2O_3$ decrease while the $k$ and $C_V$ of 2D $Ga_2O_3$ increase. For monolayer 2D $Ga_2O_3$, the $B$,
$G$, $E$, $\Theta_D$, $k$ and $C_V$ are improved to be 8.88 N/m, 4.55 N/m, 11.65 N/m, 460.72 K, 0.49
W$\cdot$cm$^{-1}$$\cdot$K$^{-1}$, and 27.58 J$\cdot$mol$^{-1}$$\cdot$K$^{-1}$, respectively. These works provide comprehensive
understanding on the mechanical and thermodynamic properties and provide a guideline for
the device design based on 2D $Ga_2O_3$.
