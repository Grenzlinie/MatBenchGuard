## ABSTRACT

By means of first-principles calculations, we have systematically studied the structural, elastic, electronic, and thermodynamic properties of two related layered 122-type intermetallics: tetragonal LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂. The optimized lattice constants, cell volumes, electronic bands, Fermi surface topology, and densities of states were obtained and analyzed. We found that the bonding picture in the examined phases is of a complex anisotropic character and can be described as a mixture of metallic, ionic, and covalent contributions. We predicted that LaNi₂P₂, and LaNi₂Ge₂ are mechanically stable and relatively soft materials with high compressibility and rather small hardness. Both phases will exhibit comparable elastic anisotropy and behave in a brittle manner. Sound velocities, Debye temperatures, and heat capacity $C_{\text{p}}(T)$ were evaluated for LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ in the low-temperature limit, and are in reasonable agreement with available experiments.

## 1. Introduction

The broad family of 122-type intermetallics (including more than 600 compounds [1,2]) with a tetragonal ThCr₂Si₂ structure demonstrates a remarkable range of physical and chemical properties.

Enormous interest in this group of materials was attracted recently by the discovery of high-temperature superconductivity in proximity to magnetism in a series of ternary iron pnictides $A\text{Fe}_{2}\text{Pn}_{2}$ [3,4] and related iron chalcogenides $B_{x}\text{Fe}_{2-y}\text{Ch}_{2}$ [5], where $A$ and $B$ are alkali-earth and alkali metals, respectively, see also reviews [6–13]. Motivated by this discovery, spirited research of series of Ni analogues of the aforementioned Fe-based 122-type intermetallic compounds was undertaken lately, and here the greatest attention was paid to $A\text{Ni}_{2}\text{Pn}_{2}$ phases (such as SrNi₂As₂, BaNi₂P₂, etc. see for example [14–16]) with a quasi-two-dimensional (2D) tetragonal crystal structure (space group $I4/mmm$, #139) where $[\text{Ni}_{2}\text{Pn}_{2}]$ blocks are separated by atomic sheets of alkali-earth metals.

Further tuning of the basic physical properties and control of the functionalities of these Ni-based intermetallics can be reached by two main ways: by atomic substitutions in $A$ or $Pn$ sublattices. In this context, two related phases, LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂, seem especially interesting. Indeed, as compared with the aforementioned well studied phases $A\text{Fe}_{2}\text{Pn}_{2}$, the adjacent blocks $[\text{Ni}_{2}\text{P}_{2}]$ for LaNi₂P₂ are separated by atomic sheets of trivalent La – instead of divalent alkali-earth metals $A$. Next, as compared with LaNi₂P₂, for LaNi₂Ge₂ the atomic substitutions ($\text{P} \to \text{Ge}$) lead to doping of two holes per formula unit.

Primary data about synthesis and some properties of LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ were reported in several early works [17–25]; very recently, detailed experimental studies of structural, transport, magnetic, and thermodynamic properties of these phases were performed [26]. At the same time, as far as we know, the electronic structure was discussed earlier only for LaNi₂Ge₂ [27] in relation to de Haas van Alphen effect [24], whereas the elastic properties of the both materials have not been investigated so far.

In view of these circumstances, we present here for the first time a comparative *ab initio* study of the structural, elastic, electronic, and thermodynamic properties for LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ based on two complementary approaches: the full-potential method with mixed basis APW + lo (LAPW) implemented in the WIEN2k suite of programs, and Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) in projector augmented waves (PAW) formalism, which allowed us to perform a comprehensive investigation of the declared properties of the aforementioned materials.

The structure of these phases can be schematically described as a sequence of La sheets and $[Ni_{2} P(Ge)_{2}]$ blocks stacked as $... La/[Ni_{2} P(Ge)_{2}] / La/[Ni_{2} P(Ge)_{2}] / La...$

Our calculations were carried out by means of the full-potential method with mixed basis APW + lo (LAPW) implemented in the WIEN2k suite of programs [28]. The generalized gradient approx- imation (GGA) to exchange-correlation potential in the PBE form[29] was used. The plane-wave expansion was taken to $R_{MT} \times K_{MAX}$  equal to 7, and the $k$ sampling with $10 \times 19 \times 10 k$ -points in theBrillouin zone was used. The MT sphere radii were chosen to be 2.3 a.u. for $Ni, 2.4$ a.u. for $La$ , and 1.9 a.u. for $P$ and $Ge$ . The calculations are performed with full optimization including lattice parameters and atomic positions. The hybridization effects were analyzed using the densities of states (DOSs), which were obtained by the modified tetrahedron method [30].

Furthermore, for the calculations of the elastic parameters we employed the Vienna $a b$ initio simulation package (VASP) in projector augmented waves (PAW) formalism [31,32]. Exchange and correlation were described by a nonlocal correction for LDA in the form of GGA [29]. The kinetic energy cutoff of $500 eV$ and $k$ -mesh of $16 \times 16 \times 7$ were used. The geometry optimization was performed with the force cutoff of $1 meV / \AA$ .

### 3.2. Electronic properties and chemical bonding

Fig. 1 shows the total and atomic-resolved $l$-projected densities of states (DOSs) for $LaNi_{2} P_{2}$ and $LaNi_{2} Ge_{2}$ as calculated for equi librium geometries. For these phases, their electronic spectra exhibit some common features, namely the corresponding valence DOSs include four main subbands A, B, C, and D, Fig. 1. The subbands $A$ and $B$ contain mainly $P(Ge) s$ and $P(Ge) p$ states, respectively, where $P(Ge) p$ states are hybridized with Ni $3 d$ states and are responsible for the covalent $Ni-P(Ge)$ bonding. The intense peak $C$  is due to the Ni $3 d$ -like bands, as well as to the topmost part of the valence band (subb and D). The contributions from the valence states of La to the occupied bands are quite small; these states form the intense unoccupied peak $E$ at $\sim 3 eV$ above the Fermi level $E_{F}$ .

To clarify the difference between $LaNi_{2} P_{2}$ and $LaNi_{2} Ge_{2}$ , in Fig. 2 the near-Fermi bands and the Fermi surfaces (FSs) for these phases are depicted. We see that for $LaNi_{2} P_{2}$ the FS adopts a 3D-like topology and includes two disconnected sheets. One of them, centered at $Z$ , is hole-like and has an ellipsoid-like form with four cylindrical "sprouts" along the $Z-N$ direction. The second sheet is electron-like, adopts a quasi-2D-like type, is directed along $k_{z}$ , and is placed in the corner of the Brillouin zone.

In the sequence $LaNi_{2} P_{2} \to LaNi_{2} Ge_{2}$ , the number of valence electrons decreases, this leads to some shifting down of the Fermi level. As a result, the Fermi level intersects three bands and the topology of the FS becomes more complicated, comprising three sheets. One closed hole-like sheet is centered at $Z$ and has an ellipsoid-like form. Two other sheets (electronic-like) are directed along $k_{z}$ and are placed in the corners of the Brillouin zone, Fig. 2.

The total and orbital decomposed partial DOSs at the Fermi level, $N(E_{F})$ , are listed in Table 2. For both of the examined phases, the main contributions to $N(E_{F})$ come from the Ni $3 d$ states, whereas the contributions from $P(Ge)$ states are much smaller. The value of $N(E_{F})$ increases in the sequence $LaNi_{2} P_{2} \to LaNi_{2} Ge_{2}$ owing to an increase in the contributions from $Ni 3 d$ states and (much less) from Ge $4 p$ states. Accordingly, the Sommerfeld constants $(\gamma)$  and the Pauli paramagnetic susceptibility $(\chi)$ (estimated under the assumption of the free electron model as $\gamma=(\pi^{2} / 3) N(E_{F}) k_{B}^{2}$ andχ=μB²N(EF)) increase in this sequence, in agreement with available experimental data, Table 2.

Let us note that the values of $N(E_{F})$ as estimated from experi ments [26] are higher than our calculated data. These deviationscan be connected with the well-known fact that the values of $N(E_{F})$  are underestimated within DFT based approaches. Another reason of this divergence is strong electron correlations. Really, using

Since many strongly correlated Fermi liquids have a Wilson ratio close to 2, this implies relatively strong electron correlations. This issue was probed (on the example of LaNi₂Ge₂) by means of the LDA + U formalism [36] as implemented in WIEN2k code. A set of calculations with varied parameters $U$ and Hund's exchange integrals $J$ were performed, using an "effective" parameter $U_{\text{eff}}=U-J$ in range $0\leq U_{\text{eff}}\leq6$ eV. Our results reveal that with increase of $U_{\text{eff}}$ the values of $N(E_{\text{F}})$ decrease (at about 7–18%), therefore the inclusion of correlation effects does not lead to minimization of the aforementioned divergence of calculated and experimentally evaluated values of $N(E_{\text{F}})$.

Using the experimental data for $\gamma^{\exp}$ from analysis of specific heat $C(T)$ measurements [26], it is possible to estimate the average electron-phonon coupling constant $\lambda$ for the examined phases as $\gamma^{\exp}=\gamma^{\text{theor}}(1+\lambda)$ [37]. Within this crude estimation, the calculations yield $\lambda\sim0.3-0.6$, i.e. LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ are within a quite moderate coupling limit. For comparison, the available estimations of $\lambda$ for other related 122 intermetallics are about 0.20 (for SrNi₂As₂ [33]), 0.76 (for BaNi₂As₂ [38]) or 0.62 (for SrPt₂As₂ [39]). Note that the data on superconductivity in LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ remain contradictory, see the discussion in Ref. [26]. On the other hand, for the related 122 intermetallic system SrPd₂Ge₂ with the electronic parameters very similar to those of LaNi₂Ge₂ [40], low-temperature superconductivity ($T_{\text{C}}\sim2.7$ K) was reported recently [41]. This issue also calls for additional studies.

As to inter-atomic interactions in LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂, the overall picture of chemical bonding in these phases can be described as an anisotropic combination of mixed ionic-covalent-metallic-like bonds. The ionic contribution can be easily explained in the framework of the ionic model, assuming the usual oxidation numbers of atoms, $\text{La}^{3+}$, $\text{Ni}^{2+}$, and $\text{P}^{3-}$ ($\text{Ge}^{4-}$) and the differences in their electonegativity. Thus, the ionic bonds Ni–P (Ge) inside blocks $[\text{Ni}_2\text{P(Ge)}_2]$ together with ionic bonding between blocks $[\text{Ni}_2\text{P(Ge)}_2]$ and atomic La sheets (owing to charge transfer $\text{La}^{\delta+\to}[\text{Ni}_2\text{P(Ge)}_2]^{\delta-}$) take place.

Next, the metallic-like interactions Ni–Ni arise inside blocks $[\text{Ni}_2\text{P(Ge)}_2]$ owing to delocalized near-Fermi Ni 3d states, peak D in Fig. 1. These interactions can be ascribed to so-called metallicity which, in turn, can be estimated numerically using the parameter $f_{\text{m}}=0.026N(E_{\text{F}})/n_{\text{e}}$, where $n_{\text{e}}=N/V$; here $N$ is the total number of valence electrons and $V$ is the cell volume [42]. The calculated values are 0.24 (for LaNi₂P₂) and 0.56 (for LaNi₂Ge₂), from which we see that $f_{\text{m}}$ for LaNi₂Ge₂ increases.

& {\left[2\left(C_{11}+C_{12}\right)+C_{33}+4 C_{13}\right]>0}
\end{aligned}
$$

(ii). The bulk moduli $B$ decrease in the sequence: $B(\text{LaNi}_2\text{P}_2)>$ $B(\text{LaNi}_2\text{Ge}_2)$. As the bulk modulus $B$ can be used as a measure of the average inter-atomic bond strength [48], this trend coincides with the aforementioned weakening of bonding strength in LaNi₂Ge₂. Another simple explanation follows from the known correlations between $B$ and the structural parameters $B\sim1/V$ or $B\sim1/d^{3.5}$, where $d$ is the length of intra-atomic bonds, see review [46]. On the other hand, the obtained bulk moduli for the both phases are quite small ($\sim$60 GPa), and therefore these phases should be classified as relatively soft materials with high compressibility ($\beta\sim0.016\ \text{GPa}^{-1}$).

(iii). The shear moduli $G$, which represent resistance to shear deformation against external forces, also decrease in the sequence $G(\text{LaNi}_2\text{P}_2)>G(\text{LaNi}_2\text{Ge}_2)$, and this trend may be related to weakening of inter-blocks Ge–Ge bonds versus P–P bonds, see Figs. 3 and 4. At the same time, for both phases $G>B$; this implies that the parameter limiting the mechanical stability of these materials is the modulus $G$.

(iv). A widely used semiempirical malleability measure of materials is the Pugh's criterion ($G/B$ ratio) [49]. Here, if $G/B<0.5$, a material will behave in a ductile manner, and vice versa, if $G/B>0.5$, a material demonstrates brittleness. In our case, according to this indicator (Table 3), LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ should be brittle materials. An additional argument follows from the calculated small Poisson's ratio for LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ (Table 3): according to the phenomenological criterion [50,51], such materials should be brittle.

(v). Elastic anisotropy of crystals reflects different character of bonding in different directions and has an important implication since it correlates with the possibility to induce microcracks in materials. We have estimated the elastic anisotropy for the examined materials using the so-called universal anisotropy index [52] defined as: $A^{\text{U}}=5G_{\text{V}}/G_{\text{R}}+B_{\text{V}}/B_{\text{R}}-6$. For isotropic crystals $A^{\text{U}}=0$; the deviations of $A^{\text{U}}$ from zero define the extent of crystal anisotropy. In our case, according this parameter, both LaNi₂P₂ and LaNi₂Ge₂ will exhibit comparable elastic anisotropy, Table 3. A similar conclusion comes also from estimations of so-called elastic anisotropy (in percents) for polycrystalline materials in shear [53] $A_{G}=(G_{\text{V}}-G_{\text{R}})/(G_{\text{V}}+G_{\text{R}})$, where $A_{G}=0\%$ corresponds to elastic isotropy. Here we found $A_{G}=4.1\%$ for LaNi₂P₂ and $A_{G}=4.9\%$ for LaNi₂Ge₂.

(vi). The Young's modulus is defined as a ratio between stress and strain and is used to provide a measure of stiffness, i.e. the larger is the value of $Y$, the stiffer is the material. In our case $Y(\text{LaNi}_2\text{P}_2)>Y(\text{LaNi}_2\text{Ga}_2)$, Table 3.

(vii). Intrinsic hardness together with compressibility belongs to the most important mechanical characteristics of materials. Finally, using the calculated elastic and electronic parameters, we can evaluate the Debye temperature $\theta_D$ and also the heat capacity $C_p(T)$ behavior in the low-temperature limit. Here $\theta_D$ was estimated from the average sound velocity $v_m$ [25,56–58] as:

$$
\theta_D = h/k_B \{3n/4\pi(N_A\rho/\mu)\}^{1/3}v_m
$$

where $N_A$ is the Avogadro's number, $n$ is the number of atoms per formula unit, $\mu$ is the molecular mass per formula unit, $\rho = \mu/V$ is density, and $v_m$ is the averaged wave velocity. In turn, the average wave velocity $v_m$ is obtained as:

$$
v_m = \left\{1/3\left(2/v_t^3 + 1/v_l^3\right)\right\}^{-1/3}
$$

where $v_l$ and $v_t$ are the transverse and longitudinal elastic wave velocities, respectively, which are obtained as:

$$
v_l = (G/\rho)^{1/2} \text{ and } v_t = \{(3B+4G)/3\rho\}^{1/2}
$$

The values of $v_l$, $v_t$, $v_m$, and $\theta_D$ (as calculated by using elastic moduli $B$ and $G$ within the Voigt approximation) are listed in Table 4.

The largest $\theta_D = 487.4$ K was obtained for $LaNi_2P_2$, whereas the Debye temperature is lower for $LaNi_2Ge_2$ by $\sim$20%: $\theta_D = 386.4$ K. These data agree with the available experiments [25,26]; besides, as $\theta_D$ can be related to the strength of covalent bonds in solids, we can conclude that the covalent bonds in $LaNi_2P_2$ will be stronger than in $LaNi_2Ge_2$ – in agreement with our previous analysis.

Since the examined phases exhibit a metallic-like behavior, heat capacity $C_p(T)$ in the low-temperature limit ($T < \theta_D/10$) can be estimated by combining the calculated elastic and electronic parameters as:

$$
C_p(T) = \gamma T + \beta T^3
$$

where the coefficients $\beta$ are:

$$
\beta = 12\pi Rn/5\theta_D^2
$$

and $n$ is the total number of atoms per formula unit and $R$ is the molar gas constant.

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, by means of the first principles calculations, we have systematically studied the structural, elastic, electronic, and thermodynamic properties for two related 122-type intermetallics: tetragonal $LaNi_2P_2$ and $LaNi_2Ge_2$. The results obtained allow us to draw the following conclusions:

(i). When going from $LaNi_2P_2$ to $LaNi_2Ge_2$, the lattice parameters and the cell volume increase while the $c/a$ ratio decreases. At the same time, the *anisotropic deformation* of the crystal takes place, when the parameter $a$ increases more appreciably than the parameter $c$.

(ii). The electronic spectra for metallic-like $LaNi_2P_2$ and $LaNi_2Ge_2$ exhibit some common features, namely, the near-Fermi bands are due to the Ni $3d$-like states. On the other hand, their Fermi surfaces are quite different and include two or three hole- and electron-like sheets. In the sequence $LaNi_2P_2 \rightarrow LaNi_2Ge_2$ the value of $N(E_F)$ increases owing to an increase in the contributions from Ni $3d$ states and (much less) from Ge $4p$ states.

(iii). The bonding picture in the examined phases is of a complex anisotropic character and can be described as a mixture of metallic, ionic, and covalent contributions. The directional Ni–P and P–P bonds in $LaNi_2P_2$ are stronger than the Ni–Ge and Ge–Ge bonds in $LaNi_2Ge_2$.

(iv). Our analysis shows that $LaNi_2P_2$ and $LaNi_2Ge_2$ are mechanically stable and relatively soft materials with high compressibility and rather small hardness. For both phases $G > B$; this implies that the parameter limiting the mechanical stability of these materials is the modulus $G$. Besides, both $LaNi_2P_2$ and $LaNi_2Ge_2$ will exhibit comparable elastic anisotropy and behave in a brittle manner.

(v). By combining the calculated elastic and electronic parameters, we have evaluated for $LaNi_2P_2$ and $LaNi_2Ge_2$ the sound velocities ($v_l$, $v_t$, $v_m$), Debye temperature, and heat capacity $C_p(T)$ in the low-temperature limit, which turn out to be in reasonable agreement with available experiments. We found that $C_p$ for $LaNi_2Ge_2$ increases with $T$ more rapidly than for $LaNi_2P_2$.
