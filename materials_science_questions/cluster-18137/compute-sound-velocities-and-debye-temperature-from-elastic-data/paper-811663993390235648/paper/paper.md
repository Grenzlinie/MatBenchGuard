## ABSTRACT

The structural, elastic, electronic and optical properties of the cubic $M_3N_4$ ($M = Zr$, and Hf) have been studied for different pressure. The computational method is based on the Pseudo-Potential Plane-Wave method (PP-PW). The exchange correlation has been treated using the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA). We have evaluated the ground-state quantities such as lattice parameter, bulk modulus and its pressure derivative as well as the elastic constants. The elastic constants and their pressure dependence are calculated using the static finite strain technique. We derived the bulk and shear moduli, Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio for ideal polycrystalline $Zr_3N_4$ and $Hf_3N_4$ aggregate. Also, we have presented the results of the band structure and densities of states. Furthermore, in order to understand the optical properties, the dielectric function, optical reflectivity, refractive index, extinction coefficient, and absorption coefficient are calculated for radiation up to 20 eV. We predicted the elastic, electronic and optical properties of $Zr_3N_4$ and $Hf_3N_4$ compounds.

## 1. Introduction

The nitrides of the transition metals are known as refractory metal materials. They combine various physical and chemical properties such as high melting points (around $3000\ ^\circ$C), ultra hardness (comparable to diamond) and superconductivity. The progress concerning the nitrides was summarized in the two recent review papers [1,2]. Tantalum mononitride (TaN), is widely used in many technological applications owing to its outstanding mechanical and chemical stability [3], it is used as hard coating materials. Niobium nitrides are very interesting materials because of their high hardness [4,5], high wear resistance [6], high melting point [3], good temperature stability, good chemical stability at high temperature [7] and superconducting properties [8]. Ma et al. synthesized nanocrystalline niobium nitride (NbN) via benzene-thermal reaction of $NbCl_5$ and $NaN_3$ in an autoclave at $380\ ^\circ$C [9]. Papaconstantopoulos et al. [10] studied group V and VI transition metal mononitrides, focusing on superconducting properties. C. Stampfl et al. [11] investigated the electronic structure and physical properties of early transition metal mononitrides. S. Nagao et al. [12] studied group IVB transition-metal mononitrides, focusing on anisotropic elasticity. Chen et al. [13] investigated the pressure effects on niobium nitride. P. Ojha et al. [14] studied the pressure-induced structural phase transformation and elastic properties of transition metal mononitrides. T. Joelsson and L. Hultman [15] studied the phase stability tunning in the $Nb_xZr_{1-x}N$ thin films system. Many of these nitrides can be described as a close-packed metal lattice (f.c.c. or h.c.p.), with the nitrogen in the center of the octahedral interstices. The hexagonal $Zr_3N_4$ and $Hf_3N_4$ have the $Th_3P_4$-type structure with the space group I-43d. This article is organized as follows: The computational method is described in Section 2. In Section 3, the results of the calculations are presented and compared with available experimental and theoretical data. Conclusion is given in Section 4.

The Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA), using the scheme of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof, known as PBE [19], is made for electronic exchange-correlation potential energy. Coulomb potential energy caused by electron-ion interaction is described using ultra-soft scheme, in which the orbitals of Zr ($4d^2$ $5s^2$), Hf ($4f^{14}$ $5d^2$ $6s^2$), and N ($2s^2$ $2p^3$) are treated as valence electrons. By the ultra-soft condition, the pseudo-wave function related to pseudo-potential matches the plane-wave function expanded with Kohn-Sham formation beyond cut-off energy. Using high cut-off (380 eV) energy at the price of spending long computational time can make accurate results. The cut-off energy for the plane-wave expansion is 380 eV and the Brillouin zone sampling was carried out using the 6 × 6 × 6 set of Monkhorst-Pack mesh [20]. Atomic positions are relaxed and optimized with a density mixing scheme using the conjugate gradient (CG) method for eigenvalues minimization. The equilibrium lattice parameter is computed from the structural optimization, using the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) minimization technique, which provides a fast way of finding the lowest energy structure, with the following thresholds for converged structures: energy change per atom less than $2 × 10^{-5}$ eV, residual force less than 0.05 eV/Å, the displacement of atoms during the geometry optimization less than 0.002 Å, and the maximum stress within 0.1 GPa. The optical properties may be attracted from the knowledge of the complex dielectric function:

$$
\epsilon(\omega) = \epsilon_1(\omega) + i\epsilon_2(\omega) \tag{1}
$$

The imaginary part $\epsilon_2(\omega)$ was calculated from the momentum matrix elements between the occupied and unoccupied wave functions within selection rules. The real part $\epsilon_1(\omega)$ of dielectric function $\epsilon(\omega)$ follows from the Kramers-Kronig relations.

$$
\epsilon_1(\omega) = 1 + \frac{2}{\pi} P \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega' \varepsilon_2(\omega')}{\omega'^2 - \omega^2} d\omega' \tag{2}
$$

where P implies the principal value of the integral. The knowledge of both the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function allows the calculation of important optical functions. We calculate the refractive index $n(\omega)$ using the following expressions:

$$
n(\omega) = \left[ \frac{\epsilon_1(\omega)}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{\epsilon_1^2(\omega) + \epsilon_2^2(\omega)}}{2} \right]^{1/2} \tag{3}
$$

The optical constants such as the refractive index $n(\omega)$, extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$, optical reflectivity $R(\omega)$, absorption coefficient $\epsilon(\omega)$, and energy-loss function $L(\omega)$ may be computed from the values of $\epsilon(\omega)$. At low frequency ($\omega = 0$), we get the following relation:

$$
n(0) = \epsilon^{1/2}(0) \tag{4}
$$

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Structural properties

Both $Zr_3N_4$ and $Hf_3N_4$ structures belong to the same space group I-43d (No. 220 in the table), prototype $Th_3P_4$, and the structures are shown in Fig. 1. The results for lattice parameters $a_0$ are reported in Table 1 and compared with experimental and previous theoretical calculations. Our calculated value for lattice parameter $a_0$ of both $Zr_3N_4$ and $Hf_3N_4$ structures are in good agreement with the experimental data [21] and [22] respectively. When comparing the results obtained within GGA, the computed lattice constant $a_0$ deviates from the experimental one within 1.4% for $Zr_3N_4$ and 1.8% for $Hf_3N_4$ respectively. We are interested to the pressure effect; we have calculated the unit cell volume at values of applied hydrostatic pressure in the range from 0 to 30 GPa (Fig. 4) to construct the equation of state (EOS), which was fitted to a third-order Birch-Murnaghan equation [23].

$$
P=\frac{3}{2} B_{0}\left[\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{7 / 8}-\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{5 / 8}\right]\left[1+\frac{3}{4}\left(4-B^{\prime}\right)\left\{\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{2 / 8}-1\right\}\right] \tag{5}
$$

With $V_0$ fixed at the value determined from the zero pressure data. We obtained, by least-squares fitting, the bulk $B_0$ and its pressure derivative $B'$. The obtained results are listed in Table 1. Our $B'$ value is located between 4.00 and 6.75 reported in refs. [21] and [24] for $Zr_3N_4$. However, $Hf_3N_4$ shows a pressure derivative situated between 5.36 and 6.56 cited in refs. [22] and [24]. We remark that after some calculations, $B'$ decreases and reaches the 4 value with increasing the K-points.

### 3.2. Elastic constants

In particular, they provide information on the stability and stiffness of materials. It is very well known that the first-order and second-order derivatives of the potential give forces and elastic constants. Therefore, it is an important issue to check the accuracy or the calculations of forces and elastic constants. The effect of pressure on the elastic constants is essential, i.e., for understanding interatomic interactions, mechanical stability, and phase transition mechanisms. $Zr_3N_4$ and $Hf_3N_4$ are cubic structures characterized by three independent elastic constants ($C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{44}$) and bulk modulus which are determined as a function of pressure up to 30 GPa as shown in Figs. 2 and 3 respectively. It is easy to observe that all elastic moduli increase when the pressure is enhanced. We can notice that $\text{C}_{11}$ and bulk modulus are more sensitive to the pressure than $\text{C}_{12}$ and $\text{C}_{44}$. For a cubic crystals under pressure P, the traditional mechanical stability conditions on the elastic constants are known as $\text{C}_{11} - \text{C}_{12} > 0$, $\text{C}_{11} > 0$, $\text{C}_{44} > 0$, $\text{C}_{11} + 2\text{C}_{12} > 0$, and $\text{C}_{12} < \text{B} < \text{C}_{11}$. The elastic constants reported in Table 2 satisfy these stability criteria, and the phases are elastically stable. Pugh [26] proposed that the ratio of B/G represents a measure of machinable behaviour. A high B/G value is associated with ductibility and the low value indicates brittleness. The critical value which separates ductile and brittle is at about 1.75. For instance, diamond has a B/G value of 0.8 [27], while aluminium, cobalt, rhodium and iridium have the B/G values 2.74, 2.43, 1.77 and 1.74, respectively [26]. From B/G calculated we can see that the B/G ratio of our two compounds $\text{Zr}_3\text{N}_4$ and $\text{Hf}_3\text{N}_4$ is the lower, being 1.274 and 1.020 respectively, based on Pugh's notion, the $\text{Zr}_3\text{N}_4$ and $\text{Hf}_3\text{N}_4$ compounds are brittleness. Besides B/G, it was found that $\text{C}_{11}-\text{C}_{12}$ and Young's modulus E are also very significant for the mechanical properties of materials [28]. From Fig. 5, we can see that $\text{Zr}_3\text{N}_4$ has lower values of $\text{C}_{11}-\text{C}_{12}$ and Young's modulus E implying better plasticity. On the contrary, $\text{Hf}_3\text{N}_4$ has the poorest plasticity. The bigger Poisson's ratio is the better plasticity. $\text{Zr}_3\text{N}_4$ has bigger Poisson's ratio (0.196) showing that is of good plasticity. $$

### 3.3. Electronic structures

Fig. 6 shows the partial s-, p-, and d-state densities for the $\mathrm{Zr}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Hf}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$. The DOS of $\mathrm{Zr}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}\left(\mathrm{Hf}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}\right)$ can be mainly divided into two parts in the valence bonding region. The first one extended between $-16.46 \mathrm{eV}$ and $-12.00 \mathrm{eV}(-17.70 \mathrm{eV}$ and $-12.29 \mathrm{eV})$ is the combination of $\mathrm{Zr}(\mathrm{Hf}) \mathrm{s}, \mathrm{Zr}(\mathrm{Hf}) \mathrm{d}$ and $\mathrm{N}$ s states, and it is mainly the contribution of $\mathrm{N}$ s states; the second part from $-6.79 \mathrm{eV}$ to $0 \mathrm{eV}$ $(-8.01 \mathrm{eV}-0 \mathrm{eV})$ is due to the contribution of $\mathrm{Zr}(\mathrm{Hf}) \mathrm{d}, \mathrm{Zr}(\mathrm{Hf}) \mathrm{s}$ and $\mathrm{N}$ p states. There is hybridization between $\mathrm{N}$ p and $\mathrm{Zr}$ (Hf) p. The first conduction band located between $0 \mathrm{eV}$ and $3.88 \mathrm{eV}(0 \mathrm{eV}$ and $4.91 \mathrm{eV})$ is mainly composed of $\mathrm{Zr}(\mathrm{Hf})$ d-states and $\mathrm{N}$ p-states. Our calculations show that the valence band maximum and the conduction band minimum are located at $\Gamma$ point for both $\mathrm{Zr}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Hf}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$. The DOS at the Fermi level is $4.35(3.69)$ for $\mathrm{Zr}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$ $\left(\mathrm{Hf}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}\right)$. The large value for $\mathrm{Zr}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$, implying the highest electronic conductivity. This is well correlated with its application as electronic conductors. The total electronic density of states (DOS) corresponding to the band structures is, also, depicted in Fig. 7, to our knowledge, there are no experimental data available to us to make a meaningful comparison.

### 3.4. Optical properties

It is common to evaluate the complex dielectric function $\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon_{1}(\omega)+\mathrm{i}\epsilon_{2}(\omega)$, when calculating the optical properties. The real part $\epsilon_{1}(\omega)$ of the dielectric function $\epsilon(\omega)$ is obtained by a Kramers-Kronig analysis. The imaginary part $\epsilon_{2}(\omega)$, where $\omega$ is the angular frequency, was calculated from the momentum matrix elements between the occupied and unoccupied wave functions within the selection rules. Fig. 8 shows the calculated dielectric function of the $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ compounds for the energy range up to 25 eV. It can be seen that the curves in the dielectric function of $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ are similar to those of $Hf_{3}N_{4}$. The imaginary (absorptive) part of the dielectric function has strong peaks at 2.99 and 3.81 eV for $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ compounds respectively. The origin of the strong peaks are due to the interband transition between N 2p electrons into Zr 4d conduction bands for $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ or into Hf 5d for $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ respectively. For $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ the dispersive part $\epsilon_{1}(\omega)$ spectra, the strong peak, has a magnitude of about 12.54. This peak is followed by a decrease which reaches a minimum around 5.98 eV. The Fig. 9 displays the refractive index as well as the extinction coefficient for the compounds studied. The static dielectric constants $\epsilon(0)$, calculated at the equilibrium lattice constant for the compounds are given in Table 4 along with the corresponding refractive indices $n(0)$. The refractive index of $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ reaches a maximum value of 3.91 at 2.10 and 3.67 at 2.91 respectively. In Fig. 10, the calculated optical reflectivities for the compounds are displayed. It is noted that the reflectivity increases in these compounds up to about 10.0 eV before decreasing. $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ on the average have a slightly higher reflectivity with a maximum value of about 100%. The maximum reflectivity of these compounds occurs between the energy range 5 and 10.0 eV which is in the ultraviolet. The compound can therefore serve as possible shields for ultraviolet radiation. The calculated linear absorption spectrum is displayed in Fig. 11. The absorption edge for $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ starts from about 0.60 to 1.05 eV respectively. This originates from a transition from N 2p electron states located at the top of the valence bands to the empty Zr 4d (Hf 5d) electron states dominating the bottom of the conduction bands for $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ respectively. The peak in the absorption spectrum occurs at around 6 eV.

## 4. Summary and conclusion

The paper reports a detailed study of the structural, mechanic, electronic and optical properties of the cubic $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$ using the pseudo-potential plane-wave (PP-PW) based on density functional theory, within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). The ground-state parameters, such as lattice parameter, bulk modulus and its pressure derivative were computed and compared with preceding theoretical works as well as with the available experimental, and show good agreement. We have also calculated the shear modulus, Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio for ideal polycrystalline $Zr_{3}N_{4}$ and $Hf_{3}N_{4}$. We have additionally predicted the elastic constants for the first time. The optical properties have been investigated. Values of the static dielectric constant $(\epsilon(0))$ and refractive index have been presented.
