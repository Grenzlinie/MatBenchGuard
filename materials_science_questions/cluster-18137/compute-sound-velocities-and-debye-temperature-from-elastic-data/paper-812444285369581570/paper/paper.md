# DEBYE FREQUENCY TENSOR AND ANISOTROPY OF THE MÖSSBAUER EFFECT IN TELLURIUM AND ANTIMONY SINGLE CRYSTALS

YA. A. IOSILEVSKII
Department of Physics, Technion-Israel Institute of Technology, Haifa, Israel

(Received 24 November 1975; accepted 11 February 1976)

Abstract-We suggest a semiphenomenological expression for the recoilless fraction in the Mössbauer effect in noncubic, and in particular, uniaxial crystals based on the idea of the Debye frequency tensor. Convenient formulae are derived expressing the principal values of the Debye tensor in terms of the elastic stiffness constants and density with the aid of integrals over an irreducible part of the solid angle. Tetragonal and trigonal system are both considered. The principal values of the Debye frequency tensor for tellurium and antimony single crystals belonging to the trigonal system are found by numerical integration through these formulae. The recoilless fractions for the isotopes $^{125} Te$ and $^{121} Sb$ are found in the direction of the principal crystallographic axis and in the directions normal to it over the temperature range $0-320^{\circ} K$ . The calculated values of the recoil-free fractions at $0^{\circ} K$ and their ratio at liquid nitrogen temperature agree with the available experimental data.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

The recoilless fraction in the Mössbauer effect in single crystals as a function of direction of the $\gamma$ -ray momentum with respect to the crystallographic axes has been studied experimentally in quite a number of works[1-8]. However, for an accurate theoretical calculation of the recoilless fraction we need the phonon polarization vectors in addition to their frequencies. Therefore, such a calculation in each particular case will entail great practical difficulties. For this reason the problem has so far been solved in the framework of a reasonably realistic model only for the white (tetragonal) tin[9]. In previous work[10] the anisotropy of the Mössbauer effect for tin was also considered in the framework of the nearest neighbor interaction model of the simple tetragonal lattice. The dynamical problem for the model is solvable in an analytical form. But in this model a number of components of the elasticity tensor, although in fact different from zero, vanish. Thus, a simple lattice with nearest neighbor interactions cannot be identified with any of the existing crystals from the point of view of either structure or elastic properties.

In the present paper a semiphenomenological expression for the recoilless fraction in the Mössbauer effect, based on the idea of the Debye frequency tensor[11], is suggested for noncubic (in particular, uniaxial) single crystals. This expression, establishing a direct connection between anisotropy of the $\gamma$ -ray phononless absorption and the elastic properties of the system which the $\gamma$-active nuclei belong to, is a natural generalization of the usual Debye approximation for the cubic crystals[12]. We find the principal Debye frequencies of tellurium and antimony single crystals belonging to the trigonal system using experimental[13-15] values for the components of the elasticity tensor and for the density. Using the Debye frequencies we calculate the Mössbauer effect probability as a function of the temperature and the direction and compare the results with the available experimental data[7,8,16].

## 2. THE RECOILLESS FRACTION IN NON-CUBIC SINGLE CRYSTALS

In the harmonic approximation, the probability of the phononless resonant absorption (emission) of the $\gamma$-quantum with the wave vector $\kappa$ by a nucleus of mass $M_{r}$ located at the site $\mathbf{r}$ may be written[17]

$$W_{r}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}, T)=\exp \left(-\kappa^{i} \kappa^{k}\left\langle\left\langle u_{r}^{i} u_{r}^{k}\right\rangle\right\rangle\right), \quad (1)$$

where

$$\left\langle\left\langle u_{r}^{i} u_{r}^{k}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\frac{\hbar}{2 M_{r}} \int_{0}^{\omega_{m}^{2}} \frac{1}{\omega} \operatorname{coth} \frac{\hbar \omega}{2 k T} g^{i k}\left(\mathbf{r} ; \omega^{2}\right) \mathrm{d} \omega^{2} \quad (2)$$

is the thermal fluctuation tensor of displacements of the given atom and

$$g^{i k}\left(\mathbf{r} ; \omega^{2}\right)=\pi^{-1} \lim _{\epsilon \to+0} \operatorname{Im} G^{i k}\left(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r} ; \omega^{2}-i \epsilon\right) \quad (3)$$

is the spectral density of the phonon Green function $G^{i k}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}_{0} ; w)$ at coinciding spatial points $(\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{0})$ normalized by the condition

$$\int_{0}^{\omega_{m}^{2}} g^{i k}\left(\mathbf{r} ; \omega^{2}\right) \mathrm{d} \omega^{2}=\delta^{i k} \quad (4)$$

for any $\mathbf{r} ; w$ is a squared complex frequency; $\omega_{m}$ is the upper limit of the phonon spectrum of the system; the Latin superscripts denote projections on the axes of an arbitrary Cartesian coordinate system; the double angle brackets denote the statistical and quantum-mechanical averaging[18].

If in the limit of long-wave lengths the system can be approximated by a homogeneous elastic continuum, then the function (3) has, according to Ref. [11], form

$$g^{i k}\left(\mathbf{r} ; \omega^{2}\right)=\frac{3 M_{r}}{2 M} I^{i k} \omega, \quad (5)$$

where $M$ is the average mass per atom and $I^{ik}$ is the tensor of reciprocal cubes of the Debye frequencies and can be expressed[11, 19] in terms of the density and the elastic constants. Let
$$I^{i k}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} e_{\alpha}{ }^{i} e_{\alpha}{ }^{k} I_{\alpha}, \quad \Omega_{\alpha}=I_{\alpha}^{-1 / 3}\qquad(6)$$
where $I_{\alpha}$ and $e_{\alpha}$ are eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the tensor $I^{i k}, \Omega_{\alpha}$ are the principal Debye frequencies. The vectors $e_{\alpha}$ coincide with the basis of the crystallographic coordinate system. We insert the asymptotic form of $g^{i k}(r ; \omega^{2})$ , given in eqn (5), into eqn (2) to obtain
$$\left\langle\left\langle u_{r}{ }^{i} u_{r}{ }^{k}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\frac{3 \hbar}{2 M_{r}} \sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} \frac{e_{\alpha}{ }^{i} e_{\alpha}{ }^{k}}{\Omega_{r \alpha}^{3}} \int_{0}^{\Omega_{\alpha}} \omega \operatorname{coth} \frac{\hbar \omega}{2 k T} \mathrm{~d} \omega. \quad(7)$$

The frequency
$$\Omega_{r \alpha}=\left(\frac{M}{M_{r}}\right)^{1 / 3} \Omega_{\alpha}\qquad(8)$$
has been fixed to preserve the normalization condition, eqn (4). The expression (7) is the natural generalization of the usual Debye approximation for cubic crystals[12]. One expects (7) to be justified mainly for monocomponent ideal crystals, especially for those which contain one atom per unit cell and thus have a simple phonon spectrum without optical branches. Evidently, in this case, it should be assumed that
$$M_{r}=M, \quad \Omega_{r \alpha}=\Omega_{\alpha}. \quad(9)$$

Consider a vector $\kappa$ directed along a principal axis $e_{\alpha}$ . For single crystals of hexagonal, trigonal and tetragonal sys- tems eqn (1) now takes the form
$$W_{\alpha}(T)=\exp \left\{-\frac{3 R}{2 k \Theta_{\alpha}}\left[1+4\left(\frac{T}{\Theta_{\alpha}}\right)^{2} \int_{0}^{\Theta_{\alpha} / T} \frac{\xi \mathrm{d} \xi}{e^{\xi}-1}\right]\right\}, \quad(10)$$
 where
$$R=\frac{E_{0}^{2}}{2 M c^{2}}, \quad \Theta_{\alpha}=\frac{\hbar \Omega_{\alpha}}{k} \quad \xi=\frac{\hbar \omega}{k T}, \quad \alpha=t, l. \quad(11)$$

$R$ is the recoil energy of the free nucleus, $E_{0}=\hbar c \kappa$ is the $\gamma$ -ray energy, $c$ is the velocity of light, $\Theta_{\alpha}$ are the principal "Debye temperatures", the suffix $l$ corresponds to the principal axis of symmetry, and $t$ corresponds to any direction normal to it.

In the case of trigonal crystals, the values $I_{l}$ and $I_{t}$ can be written according to Refs. [11, 19] in the form
$$I_{\alpha}=\frac{M \sqrt{ } \rho}{2 \pi^{3}} \sum_{\sigma=1}^{3} \int_{-1}^{1} \mathrm{~d} u \int_{0}^{\pi / 6} \mathrm{~d} \varphi \frac{A_{\alpha \sigma}(u, \varphi)}{S_{\sigma}^{3 / 2}(u, \varphi)}, \quad \alpha=t, l, \quad(12)$$
 where
$$\begin{aligned}
A_{\alpha \sigma}(u, \varphi)=\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}\left(u, \varphi, S_{\sigma}\right)}{\Delta^{\prime}\left(u, \varphi, S_{\sigma}\right)}, \quad \alpha=t, l ; \quad \sigma=1,2,3 ; \\
(13)
\end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned}

& -\frac{1}{2} c_{14}\left[2 c_{0} u\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{3 / 2} \sin 3 \varphi+c_{14}\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{2}\right],
\end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned}

& -2 c_{14}\left[c u(1-u)^{3 / 2} \sin 3 \varphi+2 c_{14} u^{2}\left(1-u^{2}\right)\right],
\end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta(u, \varphi, S)= & {\left[(S-U)(S-W)-c_{0}{ }^{2} u^{2}\left(1-u^{2}\right)\right](S-V) } \\

\end{aligned}$$

In the case of tetragonal crystals we have
$$I_{\alpha}=\frac{2 M \sqrt{ } \rho}{3 \pi^{3}} \sum_{\sigma=1}^{3} \int_{0}^{1} \mathrm{~d} u \int_{0}^{\pi / 4} \mathrm{~d} \varphi \frac{A_{\alpha \sigma}(u, \varphi)}{S_{\sigma}^{3 / 2}(u, \varphi)}, \quad \alpha=t, l, \quad(16)$$
 where $A_{\alpha \sigma}(u, \varphi)$ has the form (13) with
$$\Delta_{l}(u, \varphi, S)=\left[S-\frac{1}{2}(U+V)\right](S-W)-\frac{1}{2} c_{0}{ }^{2} u^{2}\left(1-u^{2}\right),$$

$$\Delta_{l}(u, \varphi, S)=(S-U)(S-V),$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta(u, \varphi, S)= & {\left[(S-U)(S-W)-c_{0}{ }^{2} u^{2}\left(1-u^{2}\right)\right](S-V) } \\

\end{aligned}$$

$$c_{q}=c_{11}-c_{12}-2 c_{66}, \quad c_{*}=c_{11}+c_{12}, \quad c_{0}=c_{13}+c_{44},$$

$$U=\left(c_{12}+2 c_{66}\right) n_{l}^{2}+c_{44} n_{l}^{2} \text { and } U_{*}=c_{11} n_{l}^{2}+c_{44} n_{l}^{2},$$

\Delta^{\prime}\left(u, \varphi, S_{\sigma 1}\right) & =\left(S_{\sigma 1}-S_{\sigma 2}\right)\left(S_{\sigma 1}-S_{\sigma 3}\right), \\
\left(\sigma_{1}, \sigma_{2}, \sigma_{3}\right) & =P(1,2,3),
\end{aligned}\qquad(18)$$
 where $P$ is a permutation. The values $A_{\alpha \sigma}(u, \varphi)$ for the given $u$ and $\varphi$ satisfy the relation
$$A_{l \sigma}(u, \varphi)+2 A_{t \sigma}(u, \varphi)=1\qquad(19)$$
 following from the formulae (2.22) and (3.20) of Ref. [11].

$$
\begin{gathered}
\Delta_{t}=\left(S-c_{33}\right)\left(S-c_{44}\right), \quad \Delta_{l}=\left(S-c_{44}\right)^{2}, \\
\Delta=\left(S-c_{33}\right)\left(S-c_{44}\right)^{2}, \quad S_{1}=c_{33}, \quad S_{2}=S_{3}=c_{44}, \\
\left(W=c_{33}, \quad U=V=c_{44}\right), \quad A_{t 1}=0, \quad A_{t 2}=A_{t 3}=\frac{1}{2}, \\
A_{l 1}=1, \quad A_{l 2}=A_{l 3}=0 \quad \text { for } \quad u= \pm 1.
\end{gathered}
$$

These relations should be taken into account to calculate the integrals (12) and (16).

In addition, for $u=0$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(0, \varphi, S)= & \left(S-c_{11}\right)\left(S-c_{44}\right)\left(S-c_{66}\right) \\
& -c_{14}^{2}\left(S-c_{66}-c \cos ^{2} 3 \varphi\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
in the case of trigonal crystals, and
$$
\Delta(0, \varphi, S)=\left(S-c_{44}\right)\left[\left(S-c_{11}\right)\left(S-c_{66}\right)+\frac{1}{4} c_{*} c_{q} \sin ^{2} 2 \varphi\right]
$$
in the case of tetragonal crystal. Thus, in the first case the cubic equation $\Delta(0, \varphi, S)=0$ factors into linear and quadratic ones when $\varphi=\pi n / 6, n=0,1, \ldots, 11$. In the second case this occurs for any $\varphi$. The above formulae allow us to calculate the values $I_{\alpha}$ and the principal Debye frequencies $Q_{\alpha}$ and temperatures $\theta_{\alpha}$ connected with them if the elastic constants and the density of the given crystal are known.

### 4. CONCLUSION

The method suggested above for calculating the recoilless fraction in tellurium and antimony can be used for single crystals of any symmetry. In the framework of this method we need only know the principal values of the Debye frequency tensor which can be calculated by means of rather simple formulae[11, 19] if the elastic constants and density are known. The method is an approximate one; however, it describes accurately the temperature dependence of the recoilless fraction at very low temperature $T$ as well as the temperature dependence of the anisotorpY.

In the case of a perfect crystal the tensor $g^{ik}\left(\mathbf{r} ; \omega^{2}\right)$ takes a diagonal form in the crystallographic coordinate system $\{\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}\}$. Therefore, expression (1) at $\boldsymbol{\kappa} = \kappa \mathbf{e}_{\alpha}$ reduces to
$$
W_{\alpha} \equiv W_{\mathbf{r}}(\kappa \mathbf{e}_{\alpha}, T) = \exp\left\{-\kappa^{2}\langle\langle(u_{r}^{\alpha})^{2}\rangle\rangle\right\} \tag{25}
$$
where, according to (2), (4) and (5)
$$
\langle\langle(u_{r}^{\alpha})^{2}\rangle\rangle =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\hbar}{2M_{r}}\left\langle\dfrac{1}{\omega}\right\rangle_{\alpha} + \dfrac{(\pi kT)^{2}}{2\hbar M\Omega_{\alpha}^{3}} + O(T^{3}), & \text{if} \quad T \ll \Theta_{\alpha}, \tag{26} \\[1em]
\dfrac{kT}{M_{r}}\left\langle\dfrac{1}{\omega^{2}}\right\rangle_{\alpha} + \dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{12kTM_{r}} + O\left(\dfrac{1}{T^{2}}\right), & \text{if} \quad T > \dfrac{\Theta_{\alpha}}{2\pi}. \tag{27}
\end{cases}
$$

Here we use the notation

$$
\left\langle\omega^{n}\right\rangle_{\alpha} \equiv\left\langle\omega^{n}\right\rangle_{\alpha \alpha}=\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}^{2}} \omega^{n} g^{\alpha \alpha}\left(\mathbf{r} ; \omega^{2}\right) \mathrm{d} \omega^{2}, \quad n=-1,-2
\tag{28}
$$

(no summation over $\alpha$).

Thus, if the temperature dependence of the value $W_{\alpha}(25)$ had been measured we could with the aid of eqn (26) have determined the values $\Omega_{\alpha}$ and compared them with those calculated through the elastic constants.

Let us characterize the Mössbauer effect anistropy by the ellipsoid

$$
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\left(\xi^{\alpha}\right)^{2} W_{\alpha}=1.
$$

If at not too low a temperature the inequalities

$$
W_{1}<W_{2}<W_{3}
\tag{29}
$$

are fulfilled, then, according to (25) and (27), the ratios of semiaxes of this ellipsoid, $W_{2} / W_{1}$ and $W_{3} / W_{2}$, increase without limit with $T$ and the reciprocal ratios tend to zero. Figures 3 and 4 serve as illustrations. If the inequalities

$$
\left\langle\omega^{n}\right\rangle_{1}>\left\langle\omega^{n}\right\rangle_{2}>\left\langle\omega^{n}\right\rangle_{3}, \quad n=-1,-2
\tag{30}
$$

are fulfilled for both values $n=-1,-2$ at a time then the inequalities (29) hold true in the whole temperature region. In particular, they take place in the Debye approximation. Actually, considering a crystal consisting of the atoms of one kind $(M_{r}=M)$ and having numbered the values $\Theta_{\alpha}$ in ascending order $\Omega_{1}<\Omega_{2}<\Omega_{3}$, we obtain

$$
\left\langle\omega^{n}\right\rangle_{\alpha}=\frac{3}{2 \Omega_{\alpha}{ }^{3}} \int_{0}^{\Omega_{\alpha}{ }^{2}} \omega^{n+1} \mathrm{~d} \omega^{2}=\frac{3}{n+3} \Omega_{\alpha}{ }^{n}
$$

from which (30) follows immediately.

The real vibrational spectrum of crystal is much more complicated than the Debye one. For this reason it may prove to be, for example, that $\left\langle\omega^{-1}\right\rangle_{1}>\left\langle\omega^{-1}\right\rangle_{2}$, and $\left\langle\omega^{-2}\right\rangle_{1}<\left\langle\omega^{-2}\right\rangle_{2}$.

Then the inequality $W_{1}<W_{2}$ which had been fulfilled at $T=0$ changes to the inverse one $W_{1}>W_{2}$ at high temperatures. In this case we have at some temperature inversion of the anisotropy of the Mössbauer effect as is known to take place for tin[5].
