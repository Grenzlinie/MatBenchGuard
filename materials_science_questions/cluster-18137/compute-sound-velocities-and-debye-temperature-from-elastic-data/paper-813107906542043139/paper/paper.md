## Abstract
The elastic parameters and ideal tensile strength in the [001] direction for the **body-centered cubic** solid solution phase of $FeCrCoMnAl_x$ ($0.6 \leq x \leq 1.5$) high-entropy alloys are determined using first-principle alloy theory. Based on the estimated theoretical Curie temperatures, all alloys considered here are predicted to order ferromagnetically at room temperature. The mechanical behaviors are analyzed through the single crystal and polycrystalline elastic moduli, Pugh ratio, and Debye temperature by making use of a series of phenomenological models. High ideal tensile strength is found for the equi-atomic FeCrCoMnAl system, and the intrinsic strength increases with decreasing Al content.

**Keywords:** High-entropy alloys; Mechanical performance; First-principle calculations.

### 1. Introduction

High-entropy alloys (HEAs), named by Yeh *et al.* in 2004 [1], have been considered as a promising new class of engineering materials in recent years [2-7]. Unlike traditional alloys with one or two major elements, HEAs are often composed of four or more elements in equal or nearly equal concentrations. Despite containing multiple components, HEAs tend to form simple yet chemically disordered solid solution phase rather than complex intermetallic structures. For example, HEAs consisting of late 3*d* transition elements usually crystalize in a face-centered cubic (fcc) phase, and the refractory HEAs in the body-centered cubic (bcc) phase [8, 9]. Besides the interesting structure, HEAs also exhibit a wide range of attractive properties, such as high hardness [10], excellent strength and ductility [11], outstanding thermal stability [12], as well as good resistances to wear, corrosion and oxidation [13-15]. Many important features, including high-entropy, sluggish diffusion, severe lattice distortion and the cocktail effect, have been revealed in HEAs, which are responsible for their unique microstructure and special properties [5-7].

Recently, Marshal *et al.* [16] introduced a non-refractory single bcc solid solution phase HEA, i.e., FeCrCoMnAl, which displays hardness (Vickers) value around 6.6 GPa. This value is ~22% higher than other Al containing bcc HEAs such as FeCrCoNiAl [17]. To further enhance or tailor the properties of the HEAs for special applications, systematic understanding of the mechanical performance remains critical. So far, very few theoretical investigations devoted to this question have been published, which may be attributed to the complexity of the problem related to the chemical and magnetic disorder in connection with the multicomponent nature of the HEAs. In this work, we focus on the effect of Al addition on the mechanical properties of the FeCrCoMnAlₓ HEAs. Our tool is based on first-principle alloy theory, which was found to have the necessary accuracy and predictive power to reveal various parameters and mechanisms in such complex multicomponent magnetic alloys. We study several physical parameters which today are directly accessible from *ab initio* calculations: equilibrium structure, elastic constants, elastic moduli, Debye temperature, and ideal tensile strength. These parameters in turn are used as input data in a series of phenomenological models commonly used in literature for discussing and predicting the mechanical properties of metals and alloys. Our results reveal the effect of Al addition to the base alloy on the above physical parameters and the derived mechanical properties.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we briefly present the theoretical methodology and in Section 3 we put forward the theoretical predictions and discuss the obtained trends. The paper ends with conclusions.

## 2. Theoretic methods
All calculations presented here were performed using the exact muffin-tin orbitals (EMTO) method [18, 19], in combination with the coherent potential approximation (CPA) [20-22]. The EMTO method is an improved screened Korringa-Kohn-Rostocker method [23], where the exact single-electron potential is represented by large overlapping potential spheres. Inside these spheres the potential is spherically symmetric and constant between the spheres. It was shown that within overlapping spheres, one describes more accurately the crystal potential compared to the traditional muffin-tin or atomic-sphere approximations [18, 24]. Further details about the EMTO method and its self-consistent implementation can be found in previous work [18, 19, 22, 23, 25]. The CPA represents one of the most efficient alloy theory for the electronic structure calculations in random solid solutions [19-22, 26, 27]. As a single-site approximation, the CPA neglects the short-range order and local lattice relaxation effects. Nevertheless, the EMTO-CPA approach can accurately trace energy change related to lattice distortion in complex alloys [28, 29]. Also it has been applied previously to study the phase stability, magnetic behavior and mechanical performance of other HEAs [30-35].

Here, we used the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation approximation to obtain the charge density and total energy [36, 37]. The Kohn-Shan equations were solved within the scalar-relativistic approximation and soft-core scheme. The Green's function was calculated for 16 complex energy point distributed around the valence states. The muffin-tin basis set included $s$, $p$, $d$ and $f$ orbitals. The Brillouin zone integrations were performed on 21×21×21 $k$-points mesh. The paramagnetic state was modeled with the disordered local moment (DLM) approximation [38]. All calculations were performed for completely disordered solid solutions, considering static lattices and neglecting electronic and magnetic thermal excitations.

## 3. Results and discussion
### 3.1. Structural and magnetic properties


Structural properties often hold the key to understand the materials properties from a microscopic point of view. Figure 1 (left panel) shows the total energy as a function of Wigner-Seitz radius for the equi-atomic FeCrCoMnAl HEA in three close-packed structures [i.e. bcc, face-centered cubic (fcc) and hexagonal close packed (hcp)] for both ferromagnetic (FM) and paramagnetic (PM) states. Here, all energies are plotted with respect to the equilibrium total energy of FM bcc. It is found that for all three structures and for the studied volumes, the FM state is always lower than that of the PM state. Extending the above study to other compositions (Fig. 1, right panel) demonstrate that within the considered composition range, the present HEAs prefer the bcc structure rather than the other two close-packed lattices.

The theoretical equilibrium lattice parameters for the FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs in the bcc phase at both FM and PM states are presented in Fig. 2. The experimental results measured for the as-cast and as-deposited thin film samples are also plotted for comparison. Notice that the calculated lattice parameter for the FM FeCrCoMnAl is 2.87 Å, which compares well with the experimental value of 2.88 Å [16] obtained for bulk alloy. The addition of Al is found to increase the volume of the solid solution, which is consistent with the fact that the atomic radius of elemental Al is larger than those of the other alloy components.

A key parameter to describe the magnetic state of a material is the Curie temperature ($T_C$). Here we estimated $T_C$ using the mean-field approximation [39] for system with a small concentration ($c$) of nonmagnetic elements

$$
k_B T_C=\frac{2}{3(1-c)}\left(E_{P M}-E_{F M}\right) \tag{2}
$$

where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, $E_{PM}$ and $E_{FM}$ are the equilibrium total energies for the PM and FM states, respectively. The calculated Curie temperatures for the present HEAs are plotted in Fig. 2 as a function of Al content. Accordingly, the Curie temperature for FeCrCoMnAl is 463 K, and decreases monotonously with increasing Al content. Considering that these transition temperatures are all above the room temperature, in the following we consider only the FM state for all FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs.

### 3.2. Elastic properties

Elastic constants describe the response of the crystal to relatively small external forces, and provide important information concerning the nature of the internal forces operating in the solids [40]. For a cubic system, the elastic properties are fully characterized by three elastic constants: $C_{11}$ , $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$. In Fig. 3 we present the calculated elastic constants for the FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs as a function of Al content. Results show that $C_{11}$ decreases from 254 GPa to 215 GPa when $x$ changes from 0.6 to 1.5. At the same time, $C_{12}$ decreases and $C_{44}$ increases weakly with Al addition. The mechanical stability requirement leads to the following restrictions [41]: $C_{11}>0$, $C_{44}>0$, $C_{11}-C_{12}>0$ and $C_{11}+2C_{12}>0$. The elastic constants of the studied HEAs all satisfy the above criteria, indicating the present alloys are mechanical stable. We should point out that the tetragonal elastic constant $C'=(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ slightly decreases with Al addition, suggesting that Al tends to reduce the dynamical stability of the bcc phase for the present alloys.

In a cubic lattice, the bulk modulus $B$ and the Voigt-Reuss-Hill averaged shear modulus $G$ can be obtained by means of the relations that $B=(C_{11}+2C_{12})/3$ and $G=(G_V+G_R)/2$, where the Voigt shear modulus $G_V$ and Reuss shear modulus $G_R$ are expressed as [19, 42]

$$
G_{V}=\frac{\left(C_{11}-C_{12}+3 C_{44}\right)}{5}, \quad G_{R}=\frac{5\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right) C_{44}}{4 C_{44}+3\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)} \tag{3}
$$

The Young's modulus $E$, Poisson's ratio $v$, and the Zener anisotropy parameter $A_Z$ can be calculated from the following equations [19]

$$
E=\frac{9 B G}{3 B+G}, \quad v=\frac{3 B-2 G}{2(3 B+G)}, \quad A_{Z}=\frac{2 C_{44}}{C_{11}-C_{12}} \tag{4}
$$

The effects of Al addition on the above-mentioned parameters for the FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs are summarized in Table 1. In general, the bulk modulus indicates the resistance to volume change by applied pressure, while the shear modulus represents the resistance to reversible deformations upon shear stress. It is clear that both parameters behave almost linearly with Al addition. The values of $B$ and $G$ at $x=0.6$ are 167.4 GPa and 100.6 GPa, respectively, and decrease to 146.1 GPa and 93.5 GPa, respectively, when $x$ reaches 1.5. Namely, Al addition has caused a larger influence on the bulk modulus than that of the shear modulus. The Young's modulus is defined as the ratio of the tensile stress to the corresponding tensile strain [43]. The larger value of the modulus mean the higher hardness of the materials. In this study, the calculated value of $E$ is 243.2 GPa for the

equi-atomic FeCrCoMnAl HEA, which is larger than the experimental values of 170 ~ 175 GPa [16]. The difference between theory and experiment may be attributed by the temperature effects, as the present calculations were performed at static conditions (0 K), while the experimental measurements were carried out at room temperature.

The ductile/brittle behavior is a crucial issue for the performance of structural materials. According to the Pugh criterion [44], a material behaves in a ductile manner if its $B/G$ ratio is larger than 1.75, otherwise it behaves in a brittle manner. As shown in Table 1, the $B/G$ ratios of the FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs are all below the critical value, which indicates the brittle nature of the alloys considered here. Negative Cauchy pressure $(C_{12}-C_{44})$ has been associated with the covalent nature of the metallic bond and is characteristic to brittle alloys [45, 46]. From Fig. 2, it is obvious that the values of $(C_{12}-C_{44})$ are negative, which further confirms that these alloys are brittle in their solid solution form. In addition, the Pugh criteria imposes the constraint $v>0.26$ for the Poisson's ratio of ductile materials, which has been confirmed for bulk metallic glasses [47]. The value of $v$ for the equi-atomic FeCrCoMnAl HEA is about 0.243, and it decreases slightly with increasing Al content. Namely, the addition of Al makes the ferrite FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs more brittle. The elastic anisotropy $A_Z$ is another important physical parameter, which highly correlates with the possibility to induce micro-cracks in materials [48]. For an isotropic crystal, $A_Z$ = 1, and values smaller or larger than unity measure the degree of elastic anisotropy. The calculated $A_Z$ for the FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs varies from 2.09 to 2.67 when $x$ changes from 0.6 to 1.5. These values suggest that the present alloys have a relatively low anisotropy and possess a low probability to develop the cross-slip pinning progress.

### 3.3. Debye temperature

Debye temperature is an important parameter that correlates with many thermal characteristics of materials such as specific heat and melting point. Notice that the vibration excitations arise solely form acoustic vibrations at low temperatures. Hence, the Debye temperature calculated form elastic modulus is close to that determined from specific heat measurements at cryogenic conditions. One of the standard methods to calculate the Debye temperature $\theta_D$ is from the average sound velocity $v_m$ by the following equation [49]

$$
\theta_{D}=\frac{h}{k_{B}}\left[\frac{3 n}{4 \pi}\left(\frac{N_{A} \rho}{M}\right)\right]^{1 / 3} v_{m}
\tag{5}
$$

where $h$ is the Planck's constant, $N_{A}$ is the Avogadro number, $\rho$ is the density, $M$ is the molecular weight, $n$ is the number of atoms in the unit cell, and $v_{m}$ is calculated from

$$
v_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{T}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{L}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1 / 3}
\tag{6}
$$

where $v_{T}$ and $v_{L}$ are the transverse and longitudinal sound velocities, respectively, which in turn can be obtained from the polycrystalline elastic moduli as

$$
\rho v_{T}^{2}=G, \quad \rho v_{L}^{2}=B+4 G / 3
\tag{7}
$$

The calculated sound velocities and Debye temperature for the FeCrCoMnAl$_x$ HEAs are presented in Fig. 4. It can be seen that the longitudinal sound velocities of these alloys are much larger than the transverse velocities. That is because the shear modulus is always below the bulk modulus, which is a result of the fact that $C'<C_{44}$ for all alloys considered here. The Debye temperature for the equi-atomic FeCrCoMnAl HEA is 542 K, and the Al addition results in a relatively small effect. To best of our knowledge, there is no other theoretical or experimental data available for the Debye temperature to make comparison with the present values. Hence, the present results can provide information for further experimental studies.

### 3.4. Tensile properties

The ideal tensile strength (ITS) is the maximum attainable stress of a perfect single crystal and an intrinsic property of a material. It can provide insight into the correlation between chemical bonding and crystal symmetry, and has been accepted as a mechanical parameter for the design of high performance materials [50-52]. In the theory of fracture [52], for example, the local stress for nucleation of a cleavage crack should overcome the ITS. This strength has also been used to determine whether a pristine material is intrinsically brittle/ductile depending on failure modes under tension [53, 54]. In addition, the ITS is relevant in situations where dislocation activity is suppressed, such as in Ti-Nb binary alloys [55], or for systems with very low defect density [56, 57] such as whiskers, nanowires and nanopillars. For a bcc crystal, the previous theoretical studies [58-60] and symmetry considerations [61] have identified the [001] direction as the weakest


direction in tension and {100} planes as the cleavage planes. The ITS in the [001] direction is typically much smaller than the ITS in the other ([111] or [110]) high symmetry directions [59, 60, 62]. As a consequence, we only consider the ITSs along the [001] direction (the lower bound) for the HEAs considered in this work. The present calculations followed the same procedure as described previously [60]. The tensile stress $\sigma(\varepsilon)$ as a function of strain $\varepsilon$ can be calculated using the following equation

$$
\sigma(\varepsilon)=\frac{1+\varepsilon \partial E}{V(\varepsilon) \partial \varepsilon} \tag{8}
$$

where $V(\varepsilon)$ is the volume at a given strain $\varepsilon$, and $E$ is the corresponding total energy. At each value of the strain, the two unit cell vectors perpendicular to the [001] direction were relaxed. The first maximum on the stress-strain curve defines the ITS $(\sigma_{m})$ with corresponding maximum strain ( $\varepsilon_{m}$).

The obtained tensile stress-strain curve for the equi-atomic FeCrCoMnAl HEA is presented in Fig. 5. As can be seen, the stress increases with increasing strain up to a maximum of $\sigma_{m}=7.7$ GPa at a maximum strain of $\varepsilon_{m}=9$ %. It is noteworthy that the value of ITS for the FeCrCoMnAl is larger than that of the refractory HEAs such as TiVZrNb, TiZrNbHf, and TiVZrNbHf [35]. Assuming that larger ITS improves the mechanical properties, the present finding suggests that FeCrCoMnAl can be considered as an alternative high-strength material.

Further analysis shows that the $FeCoCrMnAl_{0.6}$ HEA possesses approximately 1 GPa higher strength than FeCrCoMnAl with a similar maximum strain. Based on that, we conclude that Al decreases the strength of the present solid solutions, which is consistent with the observed trend of the Young's modulus as shown in Fig. 3. We notice that for both considered HEAs in Fig. 4 the bifurcation from tetragonal to orthorhombic symmetry occurs at strains above their maximum strains [60], which indicates that these alloys are fail by cleavage under [001] loading.

## 4. Conclusions

Using first-principle alloy theory, we put forward a comprehensive study of the mechanical performance for the $FeCrCoMnAl_{x}$ ($0.6 \leq x \leq 1.5$) HEAs. Within the considered composition range, the bcc structure is found to have lower energy than the fcc and hcp structures for both ferromagnetic and paramagnetic states. The calculated lattice parameters for the bcc phase agree

well with the available experimental data. The Curie temperature estimated via the mean field approach varies from 513 K to 413 K when $x$ changes from 0.6 to 1.5 in the bcc lattice, indicating that the present alloys adopt the ferromagnetic state at room temperature. Next the composition-dependent single crystal and polycrystalline elastic moduli calculated for the ferromagnetic state are used to analyze the mechanical performance including the brittle/ductile behavior. The ideal tensile strength in the [001] direction for the equi-atomic ferromagnetic FeCrCoMnAl alloy is predicted to be 7.7 GPa at a maximum strain around 9 %. The intrinsic strength can further be increased by decreasing the Al concentration. The present calculations serve as a guide for the design and optimization of alternative high strength materials based on HEAs.
