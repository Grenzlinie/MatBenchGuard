¹Department of Mechanical Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology(BHU)

## Abstract

Traditionally only symmetrical leg configurations were employed to evaluate the performance of the thermoelectric generator. With modern manufacturing technology, the complex shape of a thermoelectric leg can be made cost effectively. The current study proposes a novel design of a thermoelectric generator with asymmetric and segmented leg configurations with materials of modified lead telluride (material-1) and modified bismuth telluride (material-2). The p-type leg of traditional flat plate geometry and n-type leg of exponential variation area with geometric parameter 'a' is considered. The effect of efficiency, work output, work ratio and output current on the geometric parameter with temperature ratios and load ratios of the designed thermoelectric generator is investigated.

Theoretical results revealed that the geometric parameter has a strong influence on the temperature ratio and load ratio. Also, lower temperature ratio has the enhanced thermal performance within the Carnot cycle efficiency for the designed segmented thermoelectric generator. Nearly, 33.3% and 5% increase in output current and overall efficiency of the segmented thermoelectric generator is observed with a temperature ratio range within 0.45-0.55. Dimensionless work ratio compared the efficiency of a proposed segmented thermoelectric generator with a non-segmented device, which revealed that for effective segmentation, appropriate materials and geometry should be carefully matched. Thus, the present modified thermoelectric generator provides higher output power than those designs, which incorporate a single pin material for efficient energy conversion.

**Keywords:** Segmented TEG, thermal performance, geometric parameter, work ratio, temperature ratio.

## 1. Introduction

Challenges like environmental concerns and scarcity of natural resources have urged researchers to find efficient energy conversion methods. In traditional methods like in

internal combustion engines, a significant amount of energy is wasted as heat to the environment. Thermoelectric generators (TEGs) are often employed to recover the waste heat [1-2] thereby increasing the overall efficiency. TEGs utilize the Seebeck effect of producing electricity from a temperature gradient and their efficiency depends to a great extent on the leg geometry of the p-n junction. Researchers in the past have proposed a wide variety of leg geometries for the TEG but, almost all of them have been symmetric. Both the legs of the TEG were assumed to be identical with respect to shape and size for convenience of manufacturing and analysis. But with the help of modern manufacturing capability, it is possible to build intricate shapes of TEG leg which would improve the performance of the device.

TEGs are a promising source of electricity generation. They offer significant advantages compared to other power generation techniques like easy scalability, prolong service life, reliability, etc. Despite various advantages, a big hindrance to their practical use is their nominal efficiency (8-10%). Thus, considerable research needs to be done to improve this efficiency and make the device cost-effective. One of the most effective ways to improve the device efficiency is to adopt a TEG material with a high figure of merit (ZT). ZT is a measure of device performance and is directly proportional to conversion efficiency. Bismuth Telluride ($Bi_2Te_3$) is the most commonly used material for waste heat recovery. It has ZT around one within the temperature range 450 – 300 K. Other investigated TEG materials are, PbTe, PbSe, SnTe, etc. [3-4]. Each material of TEG provides a good ZT within a narrow temperature range. Segmented TEGs can work with higher temperature differences and thus are more efficient. However, the improper selection of material or geometry can also lead to deterioration in the performance of the TEG. Zhang et al. [5] showed that the power and efficiency figures are highly dependent on the length ratio. Optimization of leg ratio needs to be done based on the temperature ratio, geometry, and external load resistance. It was further established that the optimization with maximum efficiency was different from optimization for maximum power. A number of studies [6-8] have focussed on parametric optimization of the TEG system.Kim et al. [9] designed a segmented TEG system for low cost and lightweight applications and with BiTe and $Mg_2Si$ based alloys, maximum specific power density attained was 42.9 W/Kg at 498K. Thus, segmentation can be extremely effective, provided appropriate geometry, and TEG materials are chosen.

Another important factor that governs the TEG device performance is the pin geometry. Meng et al. [10] developed a three-dimensional model to the effect of the cross

section and leg length on the performance of the TEG system. A typical TEG consists of a pin of uniform cross-section. But lately, researchers[11-14] have been experimenting with other non-uniform pin geometries like trapezoidal, etc. Sahin et al. [11] formulated an expression for device efficiency as a function of the shape parameter (μ).The effect of temperature ratio and load resistance on performance was studied. It was observed that the efficiency improved for the shape parameter between $\pm1 \leq \mu \leq \pm2$. Moreover,the power and efficiency decreases with increasing the external load resistance. Also, the effect of shape parameters on the power becomes negligible at higher external load resistance values. Variation of shape parameter "a" along with load resistance and temperature ratio resulted in a change in conversion efficiency and power output. Ali et al. [12] did a thermodynamic analysis on a segmented trapezoidal pin. The conversion efficiency and power output whereas found as a function of the pin tapering parameter (s). The effect of load resistance and temperature ratio was also analyzed. It was established that the performance of the segmented pin was superior to the un-segmented pin. Also, the trapezoidal legs provided better conversion efficiency, especially at lower load resistance values. Moreover, a parameter called the dimensionless leg ratio was defined (ratio of segmented length to the total length)and optimized for maximum efficiency. Erzurum et al. [13] studied four different leg geometries namely rectangular prism, a trapezoidal prism, cylindrical and octagonal prism. Temperature distribution, conversion efficiency, power, and stress were evaluated in each case using finite element analysis in ANSYS. Maximum power was produced by a pin of cylindrical geometry under identical conditions. Later, a similar analysis was also done on segmented legs with $CoSb_{3}$ and BiTe-based alloys. At higher temperature gradients, segmentation is more effective due to higher Seebeck coefficients. Thermo-mechanical analysis revealed that highest von Mises stress occurs in a rectangular geometry, 49.9 MPa at $100^{0}C$ for unsegmented and 94.2 MPa at $300^{0}C$for segmented legs respectively. Ali et al. [14-15] performed a similar thermodynamic analysis for exponential variation in the leg.

Researchers also have analyzed different leg geometries in combination with segmentation. Fateh et al. [16] developed a numerical model to study the effect of leg length on the power output. It was shown that when contact resistances are negligible, and for the same module area, a large number of longer legs produce the same power as a small number of shorter length. Research [17-19] has also been done to optimize the geometric parameters using modern optimization techniques like genetic algorithm etc.

TEG needs to function under large temperature gradients constantly. Moreover, they are a composite material with layers of semiconductor sandwiched in between copper conductors which in turn are coated with ceramic plates. As a result of the difference in the coefficient of thermal expansion, the thermal stresses creep in the device and deteriorate the performance of the device. Thus, stress analysis of such structure is also necessary. Huang et al. [20] performed a stress analysis and found that the large shear stress is developed when the temperature difference is high or there is a large difference in the coefficient of thermal expansion of the composite materials. Merbati et al. [21] analyzed the stresses adhering to different cross-section area of the TEG legs. Thermal stresses were 1.12GPa, 0.969GPa, 0.912GPa when the upper area was half, equal and twice the lower area. To overcome this problem, Zhao et al. [22] used electrodes of W-Cu alloy of various compositions to connect it with $CoSb_3$ material to arrive at composition with minimum thermal stresses. The optimum composition found was $W_{80}Cu_{20}$.Meng et al. [23] recommended the use of thin ceramic plates for the module. Thin plates lead to lower thermal resistance and higher junction temperature within the TEG module improving the performance.

In order to realize the benefits of both asymmetrical legs and segmentation, researchers are finding new combinations where two (p and n) legs are of different geometry. Liu et al. [24] proposed design of solar TEG with asymmetrical and segmented p-type pin. The leg modified was the p-type leg with trapezoidal geometry was considered. A three- dimensional multi-physics model was built and analyzed. The focus of the study was to optimize the leg length ratio for maximum efficiency of TEG. The optimized leg resulted in an increase in 4.2 % in output power compared to conventional TEG design. Ouyung et al. [25] performed simulations on a wide range of materials and temperature range (under 1000 K). They inferred that for effective segmentation, the compatibility factor should see a gradual change from one material to the other.

In the current study, a TEG with asymmetrical and segmented legs of two different materials i.e. modified lead telluride (material-1) and modified bismuth telluride (material-2) is proposed. The p-type leg has traditional flat plate geometry with a uniform cross-section and n-type leg has non-uniform geometry having exponential variation in the area. The temperature distribution in the legs is found analytically. The effect of efficiency, work output, work ratio and output current on the geomrtric parameter with temperature ratios and load ratios of the designed thermoelectric generator are investigated for modified lead telluride and modified bismuth telluride materials. Analysis of such complex geometries

would help in improving the device performance and make TEG more feasible for practical applications to improve the overall performance of TEG system.

The paper is organized as follows. First, the brief motivation of study is discussed, and the literature survey is done. Secondly, mathematical modeling is performed, and the expressions are derived. The expressions are validated by the results obtained by the previous literature. A parametric study is performed with the existing model, and the results are obtained. The results are plotted and discussed in the subsequent section and lastly point by point conclusions are stated.

### 2.Mathematical modeling

For evaluating the performance comparison of the present modified TEG, three configurations are considered i.e. in the first configuration with both the p-n legs are made entirely of material-1, in the second configuration both the p-n leg materials are made of material-2 and lastly, the segmented configurations. The proposed model of the TEG is shown in Fig. 1. The p-type leg has traditional flat plate geometry with a uniform cross section and the n-type leg has with non-uniform geometry having exponential variation in the area with geometric parameter 'a'. Since the upper side of the device the region where high temperature is encountered, material-1 chosen is modified lead telluride due to its higher figure of merit at elevated temperature [26]. The lower side is exposed to cold junction. Thus, modified bismuth telluride is chosen as material-2 which provides the best fit of the figure of merit (ZT) for the given temperature range. The properties of both the material are given in Table-1. The parameters $A_0$ is $16\ \text{mm}^2$. Length (L) is taken as 4mm, and cold side temperature is fixed to 300K [15] for the present analysis.

![](./images/812778258826264576_2.jpg)

Fig. 1:Schematic of the asymmetrical and segmented TEG

The efficiency of the modified TEG system can be derived as [27]

$$
\eta=\frac{I^{2} R_{L}}{\alpha_{e f f, 1} T_{h i g h} I+\left(\Delta T_{n, 1} K_{n, 1}+\Delta T_{p, 1} K_{p, 1}\right)-0.5 I^{2}\left(R_{p, 1}+R_{n, 1}\right)} \tag{1}
$$

where, $\alpha_{e f f, 1}, \Delta T_{n, 1}, \Delta T_{p, 1}$, and I are defined in Eqn. (2)-(5) as follows

$$
\alpha_{e f f, 1}=\alpha_{p, 1}-\alpha_{n, 1} \tag{2}
$$

$\alpha_{e f f, 1}$ is the effective Seebeck coefficient of $1^{\text {st }}$ material, $\alpha_{p, 1}$ is the Seebeck coefficient of p type of material-1, $\alpha_{n, 1}$ is the Seebeck coefficient of n type of material-1.

$$
\Delta T_{n, 1}=T_{h i g h}-T_{i n t, n} \tag{3}
$$

$\Delta T_{n, 1}$ is the temperature gradient across in the interface of the p-type leg, $T_{high}$ is the temperature at hot side and $T_{int,n}$ is the interface temperature of the n-type leg.

$$
\Delta T_{p, 1}=T_{h i g h}-T_{i n t, p} \tag{4}
$$

Similarly, $\Delta T_{p, 1}$ is the temperature gradient across in the interface of the p-type leg, $T_{high}$ is the temperature at hot side and $T_{int,p}$ is the interface temperature of the p-type leg.

$$
I=\frac{V}{R_{T E G}+R_{L}} \tag{5}
$$

where I is the current. For segmented TEG, the expression for V is given as,

$$
V=\left(\alpha_{p, 1} \Delta T_{p, 1}+\alpha_{p, 2} \Delta T_{p, 2}\right)-\left(\alpha_{n, 1} \Delta T_{n, 1}+\alpha_{p, 2} \Delta T_{n, 2}\right) \tag{6}
$$

$$
\Delta T_{n, 2}=T_{i n t, n}-T_{l o w} \tag{7}
$$

$$
\Delta T_{p, 2}=T_{i n t, p}-T_{l o w} \tag{8}
$$

where $S_{\text {low }}$ refers to the cold side temperature of the TEG device.

As shown in Fig.1, the n-type leg in the device has exponential variation. The cross-sectional area varies as

$$
A(x)=A_{a} e^{-a \frac{x}{L}} \tag{9}
$$

where "a" is the geometric parameter. The variation in geometric parameter 'a' for leg geometry n-type with the exponential area is shown in Fig.2

![](./images/812778258826264576_3.jpg)

Fig. 2: Shape of leg at (A) a=-1 (B) a=-3 (C) a=1 (D) a=3

The geometric parameter is the central factor deciding the geometry of the exponential leg. In order to illustrate the effect of "a" and to visualize the geometry Fig. 2 provides the possible shape at a = ±1 and ±3. 'x' is the length at which cross-section is considered and L is the total leg length, and $A_{a}$ is the constant. Assuming the constant volume of the TEG leg, the value of $A_{a}$ can be found as follows

$$
\int A(x) d x=V_{o} \tag{10}
$$

$$
A_{a} \int e^{-a \frac{x}{L}} d x=V_{o} \tag{11}
$$

$$
A_{a}=\frac{a A_{o}}{\left(1-e^{-a}\right)} \tag{12}
$$

$A_{o}$ is the cross-sectional area of the rectangular geometry for comparison

$$
A(x)=\frac{a A_{o}}{\left(1-e^{-a}\right)} e^{-a \frac{x}{L}} \tag{13}
$$

Using Fourier law, the heat flow across n-type leg is given by,

$$
Q=-k_{n,1}A\frac{dT}{dx} \tag{14}
$$

$$
Q\int_{0}^{L_{n,1}}\frac{dx}{A(x)}=-k_{n,1}\int_{T_{high}}^{T_{int,n}}dT \tag{15}
$$

Defining dimensionless length ratio $\mu_n$ of the leg as

$$
\mu_n=\frac{L_{n,1}}{L}
$$

$$
Q\frac{(1-e^{-a})(e^{-a\mu_n}-1)L}{a^2A_o}=k_{n,1}(T_{high}-T_{int,n}) \tag{16}
$$

For overall leg as the whole, the heat flow is given as

$$
Q=k_{n,eff}A_o\frac{(T_{high}-T_{low})}{L} \tag{17}
$$

Comparing Eqn (18) and (19)

$$
\Delta T_{n,1}=\frac{k_{neff}}{k_{n,1}}\frac{(1-e^{-a})(e^{a\mu_n}-1)}{a^2}(T_{high}-T_{low}) \tag{18}
$$

Similarly $\Delta T_{n,2}$ can be found as

$$
\Delta T_{n,2}=\frac{k_{neff}}{k_{n,2}}\frac{(1-e^{-a})(e^{a}-e^{a\mu_n})}{a^2}(T_{high}-T_{low}) \tag{19}
$$

Where $k_{n,\text{eff}}$ is the overall effective thermal conductivity of the n-type leg. A similar analysis can be done for p-type leg using $k_{p,\text{eff}}$ which is the overall effective thermal conductivity of the n-type leg

$$
\Delta T_{p,1}=\frac{k_{p,eff}}{k_{p,1}}\frac{L_{p,1}}{L}\Delta T_p
$$

$$
\Delta T_{p,2}=\frac{k_{p,eff}}{k_{p,2}}\frac{L_{p,2}}{L}\Delta T_p
$$

Substituting

$$
V=\alpha_{p,1}\frac{k_{neff}}{k_{n,1}}\frac{(1-e^{-a})(e^{a\mu_n}-1)}{a^2}+\alpha_{p,2}\frac{k_{neff}}{k_{n,2}}\frac{(1-e^{-a})(e^{a}-e^{a\mu_n})}{a^2}(T_{high}-T_{low})
$$

Now, the effective Seebeck coefficient of p-type leg can be defined as

$$
\alpha_{p, e f f}=\alpha_{p, 1} \frac{k_{p, e f f}}{k_{p, 1}} \mu_{p}+\alpha_{p, 2} \frac{k_{p, e f f}}{k_{p, 2}}\left(1-\mu_{p}\right) \tag{20}
$$

On similar grounds,

$$
\alpha_{n, e f f}=\alpha_{n, 1} \frac{k_{n e f f}}{k_{n, 1}} \frac{\left(1-e^{-a}\right)\left(e^{a \mu_{p}}-1\right)}{a^{2}}+\alpha_{n, 2} \frac{k_{n e f f}}{k_{n, 2}} \frac{\left(1-e^{-a}\right)\left(e^{a}-e^{a \mu_{p}}\right)}{a^{2}}
$$

Now the voltage (V) and current (I) produced is given by

$$
V=\left(\alpha_{p, e f f}-\alpha_{n, e f f}\right)\left(T_{h i g h}-T_{l o w}\right) \tag{21}
$$

$$
I=\frac{\alpha_{e f f}\left(T_{h i g h}-T_{l o w}\right)}{R_{T E G}+R_{L}} \tag{22}
$$

where R<sub>TEG</sub> is the overall electrical resistance of the module and. R<sub>TEG</sub> is the sum of individual p- (R<sub>p</sub>) type and n-type leg electrical resistance (R<sub>n</sub>).

$$
R_{T E G}=R_{p}+R_{n} \tag{23}
$$

Since n-type has the exponential variation, the Rn is found by integrating as

$$
R_{n}=\int_{0}^{L_{n, 1}} \frac{d x}{\sigma_{n, 1} A(x)}+\int_{L_{n, 1}}^{L} \frac{d x}{\sigma_{n, 2} A(x)}
$$

$$
R_{n}=\frac{\left(\left(1-e^{-a}\right)\right) L}{a^{2} A_{o}}\left[\frac{\left(e^{a \mu_{n}}-1\right)}{\sigma_{n, 1}}+\frac{\left(e^{a}-e^{a \mu_{n}}\right)}{\sigma_{n, 2}}\right] \tag{24}
$$

The p-type leg has a traditional flat plate geometry whose electrical resistance is given by

$$
R_{p}=\frac{1}{A_{p}}\left(\frac{L_{p, 1}}{\sigma_{p, 1}}+\frac{L_{p, 2}}{\sigma_{p, 2}}\right) \tag{25}
$$

So,

$$
R_{T E G}=\frac{\left(\left(1-e^{-a}\right)\right) L}{a^{2} A_{o}}\left[\frac{\left(e^{a \mu_{n}}-1\right)}{\sigma_{n, 1}}+\frac{\left(e^{a}-e^{a \mu_{n}}\right)}{\sigma_{n, 2}}\right]+\frac{1}{A_{p}}\left(\frac{L_{p, 1}}{\sigma_{p, 1}}+\frac{L_{p, 2}}{\sigma_{p, 2}}\right) \tag{26}
$$

Now for comparison, reference thermal conductance (K₀) and electrical resistance (R₀)is expressed as

$$
K_{0}=\frac{k_{n, 1,273 K} A_{0}}{L}
$$

$$
R_{0}=\frac{L}{\sigma_{n, 1}{ }_{273 K} A_{0}}
$$

Overall effective thermal conductance is given by

$$
K_{e f f}=\frac{k_{n, e f f} A_{n}}{L_{n}}+\frac{k_{p, e f f} A_{p}}{L_{p}} \tag{27}
$$

The dimensionless figure of merit (ZT) can be defined as

$$
Z T_{a v g}=\frac{\alpha_{e f f}^{2} T_{h i g h}(1+\theta)}{2 R_{T E G} K_{e f f}} \tag{28}
$$

where $\theta$ is the ratio of lower temperature to higher temperature,

$$
\theta=\frac{T_{l o w}}{T_{h i g h}} \tag{29}
$$

Incorporating the above parameters into eq.(1), the efficiency is given as

$$
\eta=\frac{2 Z T_{a v g}(1-\theta)\left({ }^{R_{L}} / R_{0}\right)\left({ }^{R_{T E G}} / R_{0}\right)}{2\left(\alpha_{e f f, 1} / \alpha_{e f f}\right)\left({ }^{R_{T E G}} / R_{0}+{ }^{R_{L}} / R_{0}\right)\left({ }^{R_{T E G}} / R_{0}\right)+(1+\theta)\left({ }^{R_{T E G}} / R_{0}+{ }^{R_{L}} / R_{0}\right)^{2}-2 Z T_{a v g}(1-\theta)+\left({ }^{R_{T E G}} / R_{0}\right)\left(\frac{R_{n, 1}+R_{p, 1}}{R_{0}}\right)} \tag{30}
$$

The power output of the device can be defined as

$$
W=I^{2} R_{L} \tag{31}
$$

Putting the expression of current I, the dimensionless power output can be defined as

$$
\frac{W}{K_{0} T_{l o w}}=\frac{2 Z T_{a v g}(1-\theta)^{2} \frac{K_{e f f}}{K_{0}} \frac{R_{T E G}}{R_{0}} \frac{R_{L}}{R_{0}}}{\theta(1+\theta)\left(R_{T E G} / R_{0}+R_{L} / R_{0}\right)^{2}} \tag{32}
$$

For comparison of two materials, the dimensionless work is defined as follows

$$
\xi_{1}=\frac{W_{1}}{W_{\max}}
$$

$$
\xi_{2}=\frac{W_{2}}{W_{\max}}
$$

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Effect of efficiencies with temperature ratios

Variation of efficiency with a geometric parameter 'a' of the entire leg which is made up of material-1at temperature ratios of 0.45and 0.55 have been shown in Fig.4. The maximum value of geometric parameter 'a' is limited to 3 because of the further increase in a, leads to an increase in the volume of TEG material, which results in increasing the cost of TEG material. However, the lower value of geometric parameter 'a' is considered as -3 due to a decrease in the cross-section makes the device mechanically weaker. The geometric parameter, a = 0 corresponds to the traditional flat geometry and it is evident that with a>0, the efficiency of the proposed asymmetrical TEG is higher than that of conventional symmetrical flat plate TEG. It is noticed that minimum efficiency in both cases occurs at geometric parameter, a=-3, whereas the maximum efficiency at a = 3. This may be due to the reason that, for a = -3, the heat transfer area on hot side is less compared to cold side. Thus the device performance is poor but with an increase in the heat transfer area, cross-section area available for heat transfer on cold side decreases, thus improving the device performance. Also, it is noticed that a higher load resistance ratio leads to better efficiency of the device contrary to the flat plate TEG with asymmetrical TEG. Maximum efficiency occurs at a load ratio of 8 while the minimum occurs at a load ratio of 2. Although a similar trend is observed for a temperature ratio of 0.55 and the efficiency is comparatively lower. This is due to the higher temperature ratio applied across legs. Increase in temperature ratio increase the hot side temperature, and consequently, at a higher temperature, the higher Carnot efficiency is obtained for improving the conversion efficiency of the device.

The variation of efficiency for n-leg of the material-2 geometric parameter, 'a' with temperature ratios of 0.45 and 0.55 are shown in Fig. 5. It is clear that the device whose entire n-leg is made of material-1 is more efficient compared to a device made up of material-2. This attributes to the property of material-1, which offers a better range of ZT for the given temperature range. The fluctuation of the efficiency of the device with a geometric parameter for material-2 is lower as compared to material-1 for the same load resistance. This implies that the device with material-1 is more sensitive to the geometric parameter 'a'. ### Conclusions
The following conclusions are drawn from the theoretical performance analysis of a modified asymmetrical and segmented p-n leg configuration of a thermoelectric generator with modified lead telluride and modified bismuth telluride materials.

- Geometric parameter 'a' has a strong influence on the performance parameters of the present modified asymmetrical and segmented p-n leg configuration of TEG with various temperature ratio and load resistance ratio.
- The present TEG device efficiency, work output, dimensionless work output, and power ratios are influenced with the geometric parameter 'a' and load ratio ($\mathrm{R_L/R_O}$).
- Lower temperature ratio results the higher thermal performance within the Carnot efficiency limit. Also, the maximum efficiency increased by 5% within a temperature ratio range of 0.45-0.55 with geometric parameter, a=3 for the modified TEG.
- Thermoelectric generator with modified lead telluride (material-1) has higher performance compared to modified bismuth telluride material (material-2) due to its enhanced figure of merit. Also, TEG system with segmented materials is more efficient for energy conversion.

- Output power corresponding to modified asymmetrical and segmented TEG remains higher than the single material configurations.

Thus, modified asymmetrical and segmented thermoelectric generator with different materials p-n leg configuration provides higher output power than those designs, which incorporate a symmetrical pin material for efficient energy conversion.
