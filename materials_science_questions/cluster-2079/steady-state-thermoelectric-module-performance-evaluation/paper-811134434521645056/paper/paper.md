# Performance analysis of a hybrid solar thermoelectric generator

Pradeepkumar Sundarraj ${ }^{a}$, Susanta Sinha Roy ${ }^{b}$, Robert A. Taylor ${ }^{c}$, and Dipak Maity ${ }^{a}$

${ }^{a}$ Department of Mechanical Engineering, School of Engineering, Shiv Nadar University, Greater Noida, India;

## ABSTRACT
In this paper, a theoretical model is developed to investigate the performance of the hybrid solar thermoelectric generator (HSTEG) system, which is designed without (B-HSTEG) and with an evacuated glass tube (V-HSTEG). The heat loss, power output, thermal efficiency, and electrical efficiency of the B-HSTEG/V-HSTEG system are evaluated by analyzing the design parameters such as geometric solar concentration ratio, thermoelectric figure of merit, and cold-side inlet fluid temperature. The performance of the B-HSTEG is compared with the V-HSTEG system using two heat transfer fluids: water and Therminol VP-1. The maximum electrical efficiency of the B-HSTEG and V-HSTEG is estimated to be 12.2 and $15.6\%$ (ZT = 3) with a corresponding thermal efficiency of about 61.9 and $60.3\%$, respectively. Overall, this paper provides a systematic performance analysis of HSTEG systems.

## 1. Introduction

Solar thermoelectric generators (STEGs) have recently been gaining research attention due to improvements in thermoelectric material properties as well as system design (DiSalvo, 1999). Recently, hybrid solar thermoelectric generator (HSTEG) system has been extensively studied by several researchers to enhance both the thermal and electrical efficiency simultaneously. Miljkovic and Wang (2011) reported overall efficiency of about $52.6\%$ for the concentrated HSTEG system based on the thermosyphon cooling $(C = 100\times)$. However, the thermal and electrical efficiencies of the HSTEG system are not analyzed separately. He et al. (2011) have reported electrical efficiency value of about $3.4\%$ for a non-concentrated HSTEG system based on thermosyphon cooling, but there is a lack of complete thermal analysis (i.e., thermal loss and thermal efficiency). Miao et al. reported a HSTEG with thermal and electrical efficiencies of about 69 and $3.9\%$ (at a solar concentration ratio, $C = 50\times$), respectively, for a system based on the forced convection cooling (Miao et al., 2012; Li et al., 2014). However, the electrical efficiency value of this study is not in good agreement with the efficiency predicted by McEnaney et al. for a similar system.

From the above studies, it is clear that there is either a lack of a complete thermal analysis or consistency in the reported values reported to date. Moreover, there are only few studies on the HSTEG system based on forced convection cooling or on the effect of vacuum insulation in the HSTEG system. Thus, this paper represents a generalized theoretical model of a HSTEG system, which is designed without (B-HSTEG), and with an evacuated glass tube (V-HSTEG). The heat loss, power output, thermal efficiency, and electrical efficiency of the B-HSTEG/V-HSTEG system are evaluated by analyzing the design parameters such as geometric solar concentration ratios $(1 - 135\times)$, thermoelectric figure of merit (ZT) values (0.4–3.0), and cold-side inlet fluid temperature $(20 - 90^{\circ}C)$. The performance of the B-HSTEG is compared with the V-HSTEG system using two heat transfer fluids (HTFs): water and Therminol VP-1.

## 2. HSTEG: theoretical model

Figure 1a shows the vacuum enclosed HSTEG system (V-HSTEG), where a parabolic trough collector (PTC) is used to concentrate sunlight on a solar selective-coated TEG hot side and heat from the TEG cold side is removed using a HTF circulating inside a semicircular tube. Figure 1b shows the V-HSTEG where the TEG and cold-side tube are enclosed inside an evacuated glass tube. Figure 1c shows a bare HSTEG (B-HSTEG) without the evacuated glass tube. Figures 1d and 1e show the energy balance models of the B-HSTEG and V-HSTEG, respectively. Figure 1f and 1g depict the corresponding thermal resistance network for the B-HSTEG and V-HSTEG designs, respectively. In a B-HSTEG, the solar energy reflected by the PTC is absorbed by the TEG hot side ($Q_{\text{Solar,abs}}$) and then conducted through the TEG. A part of this energy is converted into electrical power ($P_{\text{TEG}}$) and the remaining energy is rejected to the cold-side tube ($Q_{\text{Waste-out}}$). The thermal loss from the TEG hot side to the surroundings is due to radiation ($Q_{\text{T-rad,loss}}$) and natural convection ($Q_{\text{T-conv,loss}}$). Thus, the energy balance equation for the TEG module of the B-HSTEG can be written as:

$$
Q_{\text{Solar,abs}} = P_{\text{TEG}} + Q_{\text{Waste-Out}} + Q_{\text{T-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{T-conv,loss}} \tag{1}
$$

The rejected thermal energy from the TEG ($Q_{\text{Waste-out}}$) together with the solar energy that falls directly on the cold-side tube ($Q_{\text{C-Solar-abs}}$) is conducted through the cold-side tube. A part of this energy is transferred to the HTF circulating inside the cold-side tube by convection ($Q_{\text{Cf-conv}}$) and the remaining energy is lost to the surroundings by radiation ($Q_{\text{C-rad,loss}}$) and convection ($Q_{\text{C-conv,loss}}$). Therefore, the energy balance equation for the cold side of the HSTEG can be written as:

$$
Q_{\text{Waste-Out}} + Q_{\text{C-Solar-abs}} = Q_{\text{Cf-conv}} + Q_{\text{C-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{C-conv,loss}} \tag{2}
$$

Total energy loss from the B-HSTEG system can be written as follows:

$$
Q_{\text{heat-Loss}} = Q_{\text{T-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{T-conv,loss}} + Q_{\text{C-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{C-conv,loss}} \tag{3}
$$

Energy balance of the V-HSTEG system is similar to that of the B-HSTEG system except that the energy loss from the system would be less due to the presence of the vacuum. The energy balance for the V-HSTEG system can be written as follows:

$$
Q_{\text{Solar,abs}} = P_{\text{TEG}} + Q_{\text{Waste-Out}} + Q_{\text{TE-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{TE-conv,loss}} \tag{4}
$$

$$
Q_{\text{Waste-Out}} + Q_{\text{C-Solar-abs}} = Q_{\text{Cf-conv}} + Q_{\text{CE-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{CE-conv,loss}} \tag{5}
$$

The radiation loss from the TEG and the cold-side tube ($Q_{\text{T-rad,loss}}$ and $Q_{\text{C-rad,loss}}$) to the evacuated tube contributes to the loss from the evacuated tubes to the surroundings (see Eqs. (6) and (7)). The thermal loss from the TEG hot side and cold side to the surroundings through the evacuated tube of the V-HSTEG is due to radiation ($Q_{\text{TE-rad,loss}}$ and $Q_{\text{CE-rad,loss}}$) and convection ($Q_{\text{TE-conv,loss}}$ and $Q_{\text{CE-conv,loss}}$). Hence, the total energy loss from the V-HSTEG system can be written as shown in Eq. (8).

$$
Q_{\text{T-rad,loss}} = Q_{\text{TE-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{TE-conv,loss}} \tag{6}
$$

$$
Q_{\text{C-rad,loss}} = Q_{\text{CE-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{CE-conv,loss}} \tag{7}
$$

$$
Q_{\text{heat-Loss}} = Q_{\text{TE-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{TE-conv,loss}} + Q_{\text{CE-rad,loss}} + Q_{\text{CE-conv,loss}} \tag{8}
$$

Equations for calculating the above parameters in Eqs. (1) to (8) can be found in Duffie and Beckman (2013).

### 3. Electrical efficiency and thermal efficiency of the HSTEG system

The efficiency of a TEG device depends on the properties of the thermoelectric materials. The most important material properties can be lumped into a dimensionless figure of merit (ZT) defined as $\text{ZT} = (S^2\sigma/k)T$, where $S$, $\sigma$, $k$, and $T$ are the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, and absolute temperature, respectively (Kraemer et al., 2011). The TEG device efficiency, $\eta_{\text{TEG}}$, can be determined with the following equation (Sundarraj et al., 2014):

$$
\eta_{\text{TEG}} = \frac{T_{\text{H}} - T_{\text{C}}}{T_{\text{H}}} \frac{\sqrt{1+(ZT)} - 1}{\sqrt{1+(ZT)} + T_{\text{C}}/T_{\text{H}}} \tag{9}
$$

where $T_{\text{C}}$ is the cold-side temperature, $T_{\text{H}}$ is the hot-side temperature, $\Delta T$ ($=T_{\text{H}} - T_{\text{C}}$) is the temperature difference across the TEG device, and ZT is the figure of merit of the TEG device. The electrical power, $P_{\text{TEG}}$, of the B-HSTEG and V-HSTEG can be determined using Eqs. (10) and (11), respectively (Miljkovic and Wang, 2011):

$$
P_{\text{TEG,(B-HSTEG)}} = \left(Q_{\text{Solar-abs}} - Q_{\text{T-rad,loss}} - Q_{\text{T-conv,loss}}\right) \times \eta_{\text{TEG}} \tag{10}
$$

$$
P_{\text{TEG,(V-HSTEG)}} = \left(Q_{\text{Solar-abs}} - Q_{\text{TE-rad,loss}} - Q_{\text{TE-conv,loss}}\right) \times \eta_{\text{TEG}} \tag{11}
$$

The electrical efficiency, $\eta_{\text{Elec-TEG}}$, of the HSTEG can be calculated by the following equation (Miljkovic and Wang, 2011):

## 4. Results and discussion

The corresponding ZT increased initially from 0.77 to 0.81 (for $C$ value 1–5) and then gradually decreased to 0.36 for B-HSTEG system. On the other hand, ZT gradually decreased from 0.81 to 0.27 for V-HSTEG system due to the higher $\Delta T$ values.

## 5. Conclusions
A theoretical model of the V-HSTEG/B-HSTEG system (with/without an evacuated glass tube) is developed. The governing equations for estimating the heat loss, power generated from the TEG, thermal efficiency, and electrical efficiency are formulated. A comprehensive performance analysis shows that the concentration ratio, ZT value, and cold-side tube inlet fluid temperature dictates the thermal and electrical efficiency of the system. Although all the parameters stated above control the system efficiency, the concentration ratio and ZT values have the largest influence on the HSTEG system efficiency. Increase in the cold-side tube inlet fluid temperature of the HSTEG system resulted in substantial reduction in the thermal efficiency and electrical efficiency of the HSTEG system. This study shows that the heat loss of the V-HSTEG system is less than that of the B-HSTEG system. The maximum electrical efficiency of the B-HSTEG and V-HSTEG is estimated to be 12.2 and 15.6% with a corresponding

thermal efficiency of about 61.9 and 60.3%, respectively (for $C = 25$, $T_{\text{Cfi}} = 20^\circ\text{C}$, and $\text{ZT} = 3$). Thus, the combined electrical and thermal efficiency of the V-HSTEG system is higher than that of the B-HSTEG system. This theoretical model also serves as a fundamental basis to understand and design for the complex energy transfer processes of HSTEG systems.
