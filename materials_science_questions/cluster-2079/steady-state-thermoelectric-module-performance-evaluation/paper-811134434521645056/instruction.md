# Hybrid solar thermoelectric generator (HSTEG) performance evaluation

## Problem background
Hybrid solar thermoelectric generators (HSTEG) combine photovoltaic and thermal energy conversion by placing a thermoelectric generator (TEG) at the focus of a concentrating solar collector. In these systems, sunlight is focused onto the TEG's hot side while a heat transfer fluid (HTF) removes waste heat from the cold side, allowing simultaneous electrical power generation and thermal energy collection. Understanding how design choices — such as the concentration ratio, the vacuum insulation of the hot side, the choice of HTF, the inlet temperature, and the thermoelectric figure of merit (ZT) — affect the temperature difference across the TEG, the heat losses, the electrical power output, and the overall electrical and thermal efficiencies is essential for optimizing HSTEG devices. This task investigates the performance of HSTEG systems in two configurations: a bare (B‑HSTEG) and a vacuum‑enclosed (V‑HSTEG) design, revealing how each parameter influences the system's energy flows and conversion efficiencies.

## Approach
A theoretical model based on a steady‑state energy balance is used to describe the coupled heat and electrical transport in an HSTEG. The absorber is a parabolic trough collector that concentrates sunlight onto a solar‑selective‑coated TEG. For the bare design (B‑HSTEG), heat losses from the hot side to the ambient include radiation and natural convection; the cold‑side tube also loses energy to the surroundings by radiation and convection. For the vacuum‑enclosed design (V‑HSTEG), an evacuated glass tube reduces these losses: radiation from the TEG and cold‑side tube is exchanged with the inner surface of the glass tube, and the outer surface of the glass tube loses heat to the ambient by convection and radiation. The model solves the coupled energy balance equations for the hot side (where the absorbed solar energy is partly converted to electrical power by the TEG, partly transferred to the cold side, and partly lost) and for the cold side (where the waste heat from the TEG plus direct solar absorption is either transferred to the HTF or lost to the surroundings). The TEG's efficiency is computed from the hot‑ and cold‑side temperatures using the temperature‑dependent figure of merit (ZT) of bismuth telluride, which varies with temperature. The model is solved for two heat transfer fluids — water and Therminol VP‑1 — whose temperature‑dependent properties come from polynomial correlations. Performance is evaluated over a range of geometric concentration ratios (1 to 135), constant ZT values (0.5 to 3.0), and cold‑side inlet temperatures (20 to 90 °C), with the system geometry and optical parameters fixed to published values.

## Reproduction target
Simulate the HSTEG system and produce the following performance metrics across the specified parameter sweeps: temperature difference ΔT across the TEG, the effective ZT, the total heat loss from the system, the electrical power generated by the TEG, the electrical efficiency (defined as the ratio of TEG electrical power to incident solar power), and the thermal efficiency (defined as the ratio of useful heat transferred to the HTF to incident solar power). For each configuration you must report the hot‑side and cold‑side temperatures (TH, TC) alongside the derived quantities, so that the ΔT and ZT can be independently recomputed. The sweeps are: (1) concentration ratio C from 1 to 135 with temperature‑dependent ZT; (2) C from 1 to 135 with water HTF at an inlet temperature of 20 °C and mass flow rate 0.8 kg/s; (3) C from 1 to 135 with Therminol VP‑1 as HTF under the same conditions; (4) five fixed ZT values (0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0) at C = 25, inlet 20 °C, water, 0.8 kg/s; (5) cold‑side inlet temperature from 20 to 90 °C at C = 25, ZT = 1, water, mass flow 0.4 kg/s. The results must be organized into the six CSV files specified in the workflow steps.

## How you are scored
A hidden verifier checks each scored CSV artifact independently. For the ΔT and ZT file, the verifier recomputes ΔT from the supplied hot‑ and cold‑side temperatures and recomputes ZT using a consistent temperature‑dependent property function; it then compares the values and trends to an undisclosed reference. For the heat loss, power, and efficiency files, the verifier reads the reported columns and compares them directly to hidden reference values at matching parameter points, using tolerances that account for legitimate differences between implementations. The verifier also checks that the reported efficiencies are consistent with the power, temperatures, and absorbed solar energy. Each scored artifact carries a weight that contributes to the final overall score, which ranges from 0 (no agreement) to 1 (full agreement). Reporting numbers that did not originate from an honest run of the required physical model will not earn a high score because the verifier cross‑validates internal consistency and checks against independently held targets.
