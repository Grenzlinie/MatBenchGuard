# Single-Leg Thermoelectric Efficiency Optimization for Silicide Material

## Problem background
In thermoelectric energy harvesting, material optimisation often focuses on maximising the dimensionless figure of merit ZT, but for a fixed application temperature range the device efficiency can correlate more strongly with the average ZT across that range. This task examines that relationship for an n‑type silicide (Mg₂Si₀.₄Sn₀.₆) leg operating between a hot‑side temperature of 400 °C and a cold‑side temperature of 30 °C. By systematically varying the doping concentration, the task quantifies the trade‑off between peak ZT and single‑leg efficiency, and determines whether the doping that maximises efficiency coincides with the doping that maximises peak ZT.

## Approach
The thermoelectric transport properties are modelled with a two‑parabolic‑band semiconductor model that includes acoustic‑phonon scattering (scattering factor -1/2, mobility ∝ T^{-3/2}) and alloy scattering (mobility ∝ T^{-1/2}); the full set of equations is provided in the Supplementary Information listed in the Assets section.  The model uses the following fixed material parameters for n‑type Mg₂Si₀.₄Sn₀.₆: band gap 0.42 eV, conduction‑band density‑of‑states effective mass 3.13 m_e, valence‑band effective mass 1.45 m_e, electron acoustic‑phonon mobility at low carrier concentration (300 K) 135 cm²·V⁻¹·s⁻¹, electron alloy‑scattering mobility 47.3 cm²·V⁻¹·s⁻¹, hole acoustic mobility 291 cm²·V⁻¹·s⁻¹, hole alloy mobility 102 cm²·V⁻¹·s⁻¹, lattice thermal conductivity at 300 K 2.1 W·m⁻¹·K⁻¹ with a linear temperature gradient of -0.0016 W·m⁻¹·K⁻².  The reference doping level is approximately 3.7 × 10²⁰ cm⁻³.  For each doping concentration in a suitable range, the charge neutrality condition is solved to obtain the Fermi level at every temperature, after which the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, electronic and lattice thermal conductivities, and ZT are evaluated from 30 °C to at least 450 °C at 5 °C intervals.  Using these temperature‑dependent material properties, the steady‑state efficiency of a single n‑type leg with ideal electrical and thermal contacts (hot side 400 °C, cold side 30 °C) is calculated with a 1D finite‑element, finite‑difference, or analytic integral solver that correctly accounts for the Thomson effect (an open‑source replacement for COMSOL).  For every doping, the peak ZT (maximum ZT over the temperature range) and the average ZT (arithmetic mean from 30 °C to 400 °C) are also recorded.

## Reproduction target
Produce a CSV file (step_01_efficiency_data.csv) containing, for each doping level in the chosen range, the peak ZT, average ZT, and single‑leg efficiency (as a fraction).  Then, from these data, generate a JSON summary (step_02_summary.json) that identifies the doping that maximises efficiency and the doping that maximises peak ZT, reports the efficiency and peak ZT at both optimal dopings, and computes the percentage change in peak ZT and in efficiency when moving from the efficiency‑optimal doping to the peak‑ZT‑optimal doping.  The computed percentages must be derived directly from the raw CSV data generated by the computational pipeline, not from assumed or hand‑picked values.

## How you are scored
Two artifacts are scored by a hidden verifier:

- **step_01_efficiency_data.csv**: the verifier performs a structural audit — it confirms the CSV contains the required columns with plausible shapes and that the doping giving the highest efficiency has a lower peak ZT and a higher average ZT than the doping giving the highest peak ZT.  This checks that the data are physically consistent.

- **step_02_summary.json**: the verifier compares the reported `peak_ZT_increase_pct` and `efficiency_decrease_pct` to the values published in the source study, accepting a tolerance that allows for differences in numerical implementation, solver choice, and discretisation.  The reward is a weighted combination of the scores from both artifacts, with the JSON trade‑off percentages carrying the bulk of the weight.

No reward is given for simply printing expected values; the numerical results must follow from a genuine execution of the described model and efficiency calculation.
