# Thermomechanical Stress Analysis with Temperature-Dependent Properties

This workflow family captures the core computational pattern for predicting the mechanical stress response of materials subjected to thermal loading, using constitutive models that incorporate temperature-dependent elastic and plastic properties. The family spans solid mechanics, materials science, thermodynamics, and computational mechanics, with a focus on quasi-static or dynamic scenarios where thermal and mechanical fields are coupled through material behavior.

## Common Computational Pattern

All tasks in this family share a **coupled thermal-mechanical analysis** in which temperature fields (generated by thermal shock, Joule heating, or prescribed histories) drive stress evolution via temperature‑dependent constitutive laws. The primary components are:

1. **Thermal Analysis** – solve for the transient or steady‑state temperature distribution, often using finite element, finite difference, or analytic integration.
2. **Mechanical Response** – incorporate the temperature into stress‑strain relations. Material properties (elastic modulus, yield strength, thermal expansion, viscosity, etc.) are explicit functions of temperature, and occasionally also pressure.
3. **Coupling** – the mechanical work (plastic/viscous dissipation) may feed back into the temperature field (adiabatic or quasi‑adiabatic assumptions), or the thermal and mechanical steps are iterated until consistency is reached.

The mathematical methods range from closed‑form integro‑differential equations for axisymmetric problems, to fully coupled multifield finite element models (e.g., thermal‑electric‑structural), to systems of ordinary differential equations for compressed disks. Plasticity models include von Mises with temperature‑dependent yield strength, bilinear plasticity, and viscoplasticity.

## Typical Verification

Verification is **numeric**: stress predictions are compared against experimental measurements, analytical solutions, or literature values using relative/absolute error. Because this is a “dry” lab, no physical experiments are conducted; instead, published experimental data or benchmark analytical results serve as ground truth.

## Required Resources (Typical)

- **Material property files** (temperature‑dependent curves for Young’s modulus, yield strength, thermal expansion, conductivity, resistivity, etc.)
- **Geometric parameters** (disk dimensions, hole radii, impactor masses)
- **Thermal loads** (prescribed temperature histories, heat sources, or electrical currents)
- **Numerical solvers** (custom integrators, ODE solvers, or finite element software like ANSYS® with APDL macros)

No datasets are bundled; the solving agent obtains needed files from the infrastructure or external references at runtime.
