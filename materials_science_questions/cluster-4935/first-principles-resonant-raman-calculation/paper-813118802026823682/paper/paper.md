# ON RAMAN SCATTERING IN MOLECULAR COMPLEXES INVOLVING CHARGE TRANSFER

Franz R. AUSSENEGG and Max E. LIPPITSCH

Institut für Experimentalphysik der Universität Graz, A-8010 Graz, Austria

Received 3 March 1978
Revised manuscript received 20 June 1978

A model is presented attributing the enhancement of Raman intensities in molecular complexes involving charge transfer to different displaceabilities of the electronic charge transferred between the donor and the acceptor. During certain vibra- tions the extent of charge transfer is changed due to alteration of the donor-acceptor distance, leading to an additional change in polarizability during the vibrational motion. An estimation of the order of magnitude of this effect is in good agreement with experimental values. Particularly, this model allows explanation of considerable Raman intensity enhance- ment found in molecules adsorbed on a metallic surface.

Formation of complexes involving charge transfer between different molecules (e.g. $CCl_{4}$-pyridine or $Br_{2}$-pyridine) may yield a considerable enhancement in Raman intensities [1,2]. In some cases, this enhance- ment can be accounted for resonance Raman scatter- ing involving absorption caused by charge transfer [3]. On the other hand, molecular complexes are known, which show no additional absorption near the exciting line when complexing. In this paper a model is pre- sented attributing the enhancement of Raman inten- sities in complexes involving charge transfer to differ- ent displaceabilities of the electronic charge transferred between the donor and acceptor molecule. For this model, the "charge transfer complex" formed is not necessarily a stable one for long durations. A complex life-time greater than the life-time of the vibrational excitation considered $(>10^{-12}$ to $10^{-11} ~s)$ is sufficient. The model therefore is valid even for collision com- plexes, if charge transfer occurs during the collision.

Polarizing a molecule means to displace its electronic charge by means of an electric field. Equal charges may cause different polarizabilities, according to different displaceabilities. Transferring a charge within a molec- ular system from a donor to an acceptor does not change the total charge, but may change the total po- larizability. The extent of charge transfer is known to depend on the intermolecular distance [4], which may in turn be dependent on internal movements of the molecules. This is true if the force between the com- plexing molecules is small compared to the molecular bonding forces. Regarding an internal vibration of one of the complexing molecules, the centres of mass of both molecules remain in nearly fixed position, while the distance between donor and acceptor is changed, the extent of change being dependent on the kind of vibration. In this way a molecular vibration may cause the total polarizability of the complex to oscillate with the vibration frequency, which gives rise to Raman scattering.

Let us assume two molecules (acceptor and donor) with polarizabilities $\alpha_{A}$ and $\alpha_{D}$ and charges $\rho_{A}$ and $\rho_{D}$ . The dependence of the total polarizability $\alpha_{tot }$ on the vibrational coordinate $q_{A}$ of the acceptor mole cule (it could have been a donor vibration as well) is given by
$$\mathrm{d} \alpha_{\text {tot }} / \mathrm{d} q_{\mathrm{A}}=\mathrm{d}\left[\alpha_{\mathrm{A}}\left(q_{\mathrm{A}}, \rho_{\mathrm{A}}\right)+\alpha_{\mathrm{D}}\left(q_{\mathrm{D}}, \rho_{\mathrm{D}}\right)\right] / \mathrm{d} q_{\mathrm{A}}.(1)$$

It is reasonable to assume, that $\alpha_{A}$ does not depend explicitly on $q_{D}$ and $\rho_{D}, \alpha_{D}$ on $q_{A}$ and $\rho_{A}$ , nor $q_{A}$ on qD. So partial differentiation yields
$$\frac{\mathrm{d} \alpha_{\text {tot }}}{\mathrm{d} q_{\mathrm{A}}}=\frac{\partial \alpha_{\mathrm{A}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{A}}}+\frac{\mathrm{d} \rho_{\mathrm{A}}}{\mathrm{d} q_{\mathrm{A}}} \frac{\partial \alpha_{\mathrm{A}}}{\partial \rho_{\mathrm{A}}}+\frac{\mathrm{d} \rho_{\mathrm{D}}}{\mathrm{d} q_{\mathrm{A}}} \frac{\partial \alpha_{\mathrm{D}}}{\partial \rho_{\mathrm{D}}}. \quad (2)$$

Since the charge decrease in the donor is equal to the charge increase in the acceptor, we have

$$d\rho_D = -d\rho_A \. \tag{3}$$

Taking into account the charge transfer being dependent on the donor-acceptor distance $x$ and hence — as shown above — on the vibrational coordinate $q_A$, we find (since $\rho_A$ is not an explicit function of $q_A$)

$$d\rho_A/dq_A = (\partial\rho_A/\partial x) dx/dq_A \. \tag{4}$$

For the dependence of the total polarizability of the system on the vibrational coordinate of the acceptor results

$$\frac{d\alpha_{\text{tot}}}{dq_A} = \frac{\partial\alpha_A}{\partial q_A} + \frac{\partial\rho_A}{\partial x} \frac{dx}{dq_A} \left( \frac{\partial\alpha_A}{\partial\rho_A} - \frac{\partial\alpha_D}{\partial\rho_D} \right) \. \tag{5}$$

The first term on the right hand side gives Raman scattering by the isolated acceptor, while the second term is the charge transfer contribution to the total Raman scattering.

In the following an estimation is given for the order of magnitude of the charge transfer contribution. In this estimation a number of effects is neglected (e.g. change in effective reduced mass by coupling of $\nu_{\text{Cl-Cl}}$ and $\nu_{\text{N...Cl}}$ vibrations, change in polarity of the Cl-Cl bond by complex formation) and therefore a knowledge of the accurate values of the molecular parameters used is not essential. Nevertheless we think that it will be apparent from the following estimation, that the charge transfer contribution to the Raman intensity can be comparable to the Raman intensity itself. For simplification we assume a diatomic molecule as an acceptor (as, for example, in the system $\text{R}_3-\text{N...Cl}_2$). Then we have

$$dx/dq = -\frac{1}{2} (m_r)^{1/2} \approx -2.5 \times 10^{-12} \ \text{g}^{1/2} \tag{6}$$

($m_r$ is the reduced mass). For $\partial\rho_A/\partial x$ we find from the curves calculated by Carreira and Person [4] a value of about $4 \times 10^7$ $e/\text{cm}$ ($e$ is the elementary charge). The quantity $\partial\alpha_A/\partial\rho_A$ (representing the displaceability) can be found in a rough approximation by linear interpolation from the polarizabilities of the neutral acceptor $\alpha_A$ and the acceptor ion $\alpha_A^-$

$$\partial\alpha_A/\partial\rho_A \approx (\alpha_A^- - \alpha_A)/e \, \tag{7}$$

and similarily for the donor from $\alpha_D$ and $\alpha_D^+$

$$\partial\alpha_D/\partial\rho_D \approx (\alpha_D - \alpha_D^+)/e \. \tag{8}$$

From the values given by Thorhallson et al. [5] for the polarizabilities of $\text{Cl}$, $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{N}$, and $\text{N}^+$ we find for $\partial\alpha_A/\partial\rho_A \approx 33 \times 10^{-25} \ \text{cm}^3/e$ and for $\partial\alpha_D/\partial\rho_D \approx 5 \times 10^{-25} \ \text{cm}^3/e$. So the charge transfer contribution to the Raman scattering of the acceptor stretching vibration is about $2.8 \times 10^{-28} \ \text{cm}^2 \ \text{g}^{1/2}$. This value is of the same order of magnitude as the value for the contribution from the isolated molecule, which is typically $10^{-28} \ \text{cm}^2 \ \text{g}^{1/2}$.

The model proposed here apparently can explain an enhancement in the Raman intensity without resonance effects involving a charge transfer absorption. There are charge transfer complexes showing Raman enhancement without any absorption to be found near the exciting line. We have investigated the Raman enhancement reported by Bahnick and Person [1] for the $459 \ \text{cm}^{-1}$ vibration in $\text{CCl}_4$ in a complex with pyridine. For a mixture of $90\%$ pyridine and $10\%$ $\text{CCl}_4$ we found, in agreement with Bahnick and Person [1], an enhancement of about 1.5 compared to pure $\text{CCl}_4$ (after correction for the concentration). For exciting wavelengths from 458 nm to 514 nm the frequency dependence of the Raman intensity was the same as for the standard cyclohexane ($1260 \ \text{cm}^{-1}$), for which the $\nu^4$-law was assumed to be valid. Furthermore in the region from 280 nm to 700 nm no considerable change in the absorption spectra of the separated and complexed molecules could be found . So one may conclude, that the Raman enhancement is not due to resonance Raman scattering.

It was pointed out by Bahnick and Person, that vibrations involving a stretch of the C-Cl bond show a Raman enhancement when complexing (459 and $314 \ \text{cm}^{-1}$), while a vibration with only negligible stretching ($218 \ \text{cm}^{-1}$) gives no enhancement. This observation is in agreement with our model, since only a stretching movement of the C-Cl bond can change the acceptor-donor distance appreciably.

A very detailed description of an experimental observation of Raman scattering by a monomolecular layer of adsorbed pyridine on a silver surface was given by van Duyne [6]. By electrochemical techniques the pyridine molecules are brought into close contact with the surface and are oriented in a certain position (presumably perpendicular to the surface). Under these conditions the Raman intensities of some pyridine bands (after allowance for the number of molecules) show an increase of about $10^5$ to $10^6$. Assuming a charge transfer between the pyridine molecule and the

metal surface, this increase could be explained by our model. Deduction of the charge transfer contribution for a donor vibration gives in analogy to eq. (5)

$$
\left(\frac{\mathrm{d} \alpha_{\mathrm{tot}}}{\mathrm{d} q_{\mathrm{D}}}\right)_{\mathrm{CT}}=\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathrm{A}}}{\partial x} \frac{\mathrm{d} x}{\mathrm{~d} q_{\mathrm{D}}}\left(\frac{\partial \alpha_{\mathrm{A}}}{\partial \rho_{\mathrm{A}}}-\frac{\partial \alpha_{\mathrm{D}}}{\partial \rho_{\mathrm{D}}}\right). \tag{9}
$$

Now the acceptor is a bulk metal. Due to metallic conductivity electrons can be displaced much more easily and hence the polarizability is many orders of magnitude higher than in molecules. Assuming the charge transferred from the donor to the metal to be much more displaceable as well, $\partial \alpha_{\mathrm{A}} / \partial \rho_{\mathrm{A}}$ is much higher than in ordinary charge transfer complexes. The charge transfer contribution now may be several orders of magnitude higher than the intramolecular contribu- tion to the Raman intensity. According to our model a strong enhancement should be found in vibrations causing strong changes in the acceptor-donor distance. Results given by van Duyne [6] show an exceptionally high increase in intensity of the band at $1006 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ (in pyridine in aqueous solution, corresponding to the $988 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ band in pure pyridine), while the increase for the $650 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ and $1572 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ bands is compara- tively very small. In fact, with the geometry postulated by van Duyne [6] (the pyridine molecule perpendicular to the surface, the $\mathrm{N}$ atom coupling to the metal), it is quite reasonable to assume, that the acceptor-donor distance is changed much more by the ring pulsation movement of the $1006 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ band than by the in- plane $(1572 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1})$ and out-of-plane $(650 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1})$ bend- ing vibrations [7], for which the $\mathrm{N}$ atom has practically no movement in the $x$ direction.
