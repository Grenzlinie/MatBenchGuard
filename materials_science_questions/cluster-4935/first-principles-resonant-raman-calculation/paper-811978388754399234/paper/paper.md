# Crystal-Field Analysis of Electronic Raman Scattering Data in YbAG and YbGG
By
V. NEKVASIL

The relative Raman intensities associated with various electronic transitions are used simultane- ously with the usually considered energy levels and $g$-values for determination of the phenomeno- logical crystal-field parameters in YbAG and YbGG. The best-fit parameters reproduce well all the available data but their values do not agree with the superposition model of the crystal field. It is concluded that the earlier crystal-field calculation for $Yb^{3+}: YAG$ is impaired by the misinter pretation of the optical data.

Для определения параметров кристаллического поля в YbAG и YbGG используются относительные рамановские интенсивности, связанные с различными электрон- ными переходами, однако, их значения не согласуются с моделью суперпозиции кристалли- ческого поля. Показано, что опубликованные раньше расчеты кристаллического поля для $Yb^{3+}: YAG$ были искажены не совсем правильной интерпретацией опти- ческих данных.

## 1. Introduction
The preparation of synthetic rare-earth-iron garnets in the late fifties initiated an intensive experimental and theoretical study of the interactions responsible for the anisotropy of their magnetic properties. The garnets with trivalent ytterbium were among the first thoroughly studied systems (Wickersheim [1], Wolf et al. [2], Hutch- ings and Wolf [3], Levy [4]). First detailed crystal-field calculation was done by Hutchings and Wolf [3] who determined the crystal-field parameters in $Yb^{3+}$ -sub stituted yttrium-aluminium garnet (YAG) and yttrium-gallium garnet (YGG). The work was hampered by the lack of reliable data on the splitting of the ground multiplet $^{2}F_{7/2}$. The overall scarcity of the experimental data was compensated by the applica- tion of constraints on the crystal-field parameters, emerging from point-charge calculations. Consequently, the number of independent parameters was reduced to four. It were only Pearson et al. [5] who were able to determine the full set of nine crystal-field parameters exploiting their more complete optical data [6]. Surprisingly, the sets of $YAG: Yb^{3+}$ and $YGG: Yb^{3+}$ parameters obtained in [5] significantly differ. Moreover, they also differ from the parameters reported in other rare-earth garnets, disturbing thus the otherwise gratifying picture of one-electron crystal-field param- eters gradually changing across the rare-earth series (see [7]). Furthermore, the parameters of Pearson et al. [5] do not fit in the superposition model.

Argyle et al. [10] used the Raman method to identify unambiguously the phonon and electronic lines in YbAG and YbGG and drew attention to misinterpretations which had occurred in [6]. Attempting to elucidate if the above-mentioned peculiarity is an intrinsic property of ytterbium garnets or just a consequence of errors in either experimental or numerical procedure we perform the crystal-field analysis for YbAG

## 2. Theoretical Background
### 2.1 Energy levels
The crystal-field splitting of the $\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}$ electronic spectra can be described by the commonly used Hamiltonian (Wybourne 1965)
$$
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{cf}}=\sum_{k q} B_{k q}\left(C_{q}^{k}+C_{-q}^{k}\right) ; \quad k=2,4,6 ; \quad q=0,2,4,6 ; \quad q \leqq k, \quad(1)
$$
where $B_{k q}$ are the crystal-field parameters and $C_{q}^{k}$ are the irreducible tensor operators defined by their reduced matrix elements
$$
\left(l\left\|C^{k}\right\| l^{\prime}\right)=(-1)^{l}(2 l+1)(2 l+1)^{1 / 2}\left(\begin{array}{lll}
l & k & l^{\prime} \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}\right).
$$

The $\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}$ ion is an odd-electron system and its crystal-field states in $\mathrm{D}_{2}$-symmetry sites of garnets remain twofold degenerate. For these Kramers doublets it is often advantageous to introduce the effective spin Hamiltonian with effective spin $s^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}$. The Zeeman splitting of the Kramers doublet is then expressed through $g$-values. Following the sign convention of Pearson et al. [5] we define the latter as
$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
g_{x}=-2\left(\alpha\left|L_{x}+2 S_{x}\right| \alpha^{\prime}\right), \\
g_{y}=-2 i\left(\alpha\left|L_{y}+2 S_{y}\right| \alpha^{\prime}\right), \\
g_{z}=-2\left(\alpha\left|L_{z}+2 S_{z}\right| \alpha\right).
\end{array}\right\}
$$

The $|\alpha\rangle,\left|\alpha^{\prime}\right\rangle$ are the Kramers conjugate eigenstates of (1).

### 2.2 Relative Raman intensity
The expressions for the relative Raman intensities of transitions in rare-earth ions in dodecahedral sites of garnets, which are appropriate to the scattering geometry chosen in Yale group experiments [11], are due to Lewis [13]. We shall write here the scattering tensor $\alpha_{q}^{k}[14,15]$ as
$$
\alpha_{q}^{k}=\alpha_{k} U_{q}^{k} ; \quad k=1,2. \quad(3)
$$

The $U_{q}^{k}$ are unit tensor operators defined by their reduced matrix elements $\left(l\left\|U^{k}\right\| l^{\prime}\right)=$ $=\delta\left(l, l^{\prime}\right)$. The $\alpha^{k}$ in the approximation of Axe [14] depend on the radial matrix elements $\left(4 \mathrm{f}\left|r^{2}\right| 4 \mathrm{f}\right)$ and $(4 \mathrm{f}|r| 5 \mathrm{~d})^{2}$, and the energies of the excited configurations, $\hbar \omega_{5 g}$ and $\hbar \omega_{5 d}$.

Substituting from (3) into the formulae given in [12] one gets
$$
\left.\begin{array}{rl}
I_{\mathrm{VV}} & =R^{2}\left\{2\left(U_{0}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{2}^{2}-U_{-2}^{2}\right)^{2}\right\}, \\
I_{\mathrm{HH}} & =\left(U_{1}^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{0}^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{-1}^{1}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{4} R^{2}\left\{2\left(U_{0}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{2}^{2}-U_{-2}^{2}\right)^{2}\right\}, \\
I_{\mathrm{VH}}=I_{\mathrm{HV}} & =\left(U_{1}^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{0}^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{-1}^{1}\right)^{2}+ \\
& +\frac{1}{2} R^{2}\left\{2\left(U_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}+2\left(U_{-1}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{2}^{2}+U_{-2}^{2}\right)^{2}\right\},
\end{array}\right\}
$$
where $R=\left|\alpha^{2} / \alpha^{1}\right|$. The left-hand and right-hand indices denote the scattered and incident radiation, respectively, indicating the polarization plane as being either horizontal (H) or vertical (V), with respect to the laboratory system of coordinates.

To calculate the relative Raman intensities according to (4) we only need the value of single parameter $R$. As there is a considerable uncertainty in the abovementioned quantities contained in $\alpha^{k}$, we treat $R$ as additional adjustable parameter in our calculations.

## 3. Crystal-Field Calculation

The least-squares method described elsewhere [16] has been used to get the unknown parameters, i.e. the nine crystal-field parameters $B_{k q}$, the difference between free-ion energies $\Delta E=E_{5 / 2}-E_{7 / 2}$, and the Raman intensity parameter $R=\left|\alpha^{2} / \alpha^{1}\right|$. The weights of individual experimental quantities considered in the least-squares sum were adjusted according to their accuracy. The numerical programs were checked by reproduction of the results of Pearson et al. [5].

### 3.1 YbAG

We have first fitted the unknown quantities to the following experimental data: the $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{7 / 2}$ energy levels reported by Argyle et al. [10], the $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{5 / 2}$ levels of Buchanan et al.[6], the $g$-values of the lowest Kramers doublet in the multiplet $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{5 / 2}$, due to Crosswhite quoted in [5], and the ground state $g$-values EPR data of Wolf et al. [2] in $\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}$ : LuAG. The LuAG was chosen because it is structurally close to YbAG.

The best-fit parameters obtained were essentially the same as those reported in Yb:YAG [5], i.e. they remain "anomalous". This result forced us, above all, to reconsider the reliability of the input experimental data.

We are going to demonstrate that it is, probably, the energy spectra of $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{5 / 2}$ multiplet which was also erroneously identified by Buchanan et al. [6]. Being in the position to choose three electronic lines among four candidates, these authors have judged that the proper energy levels are at $10328,10640$, and $10680 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, setting aside the fourth level at $10903 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. In order to test this assignment we have repeated the above numerical routine, this time omitting the $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{7 / 2}$ levels as input data. For different initial guesses we came to the same conclusion. The electronic levels are those at10328 and $10903 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, while the question which of the lines at 10640 and $10680 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ corresponds to the remaining electronic level continues to be open. Left with no other option we consider in the following calculation the $10680 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ level as the proper one, simply because it gives a better fit.

The line $10903 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ was identified in [6] as vibronic on the basis of the presumption that the temperature shifts of the crystal-field levels depend primarily on the changein the cubic parameters of the Hamiltonian (1). Derivating the eigenvalues of (1) with respect to the parameters $B_{k q}$ we have found, however, that this presumption is not necessarily fulfilled in the case of the highest-lying $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{5 / 2}$ level as the greatest are derivatives of its energy with respect to $B_{20}$ and $B_{22}$. At the same time, the latter are known to be the most sensitive to the variation of the crystal surrounding [3]. It is to be finally noted that admitting that the $10903 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ line is in fact electronic, we arrive at the expected picture of the $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{5 / 2}$ overall splitting in YbAG being larger than in YbGG.

In the final stage of the calculation we have added as input data the 10328, 10680, and $10903 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ levels of $^{2} \mathrm{~F}_{5 / 2}$ multiplet and four intensity data which we take as most reliable, the $I_{\mathrm{VH}}$ and $I_{\mathrm{HH}}$ for both 587 and $621 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ levels $[11,13]$. The calculated intensities are normalized to agree with $I^{\exp }\left(587 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\right)$ for polarization combination VH. Having no explanation for the third excited doublet "splitting" [10,11], we did not consider the relevant intensities. For the energy level position we took the average value of $758.5 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ in the calculation.

### 3.2 YbGG
The energy levels and $g$-values coming from the same sources as in the YbAG served as input data. For the ground state $g$-values we take now those in $\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}: \mathrm{LuGG}$. In contrast to YbAG, our attempt to include the Raman intensities as input data failed, as it led to unrealistic values for the other measurable quantities. We ascribe the failure primarily to the difficulties encountered in the deduction of electronic inten- sities from the Raman spectra, due to the presence of phonon lines. The proximity of the latter in YbGG is much closer [11].

It is to be mentioned that the parameters obtained account well for the experi- mental data not involved into the fitting procedure, too. Finally, we have calculated the parameter $\alpha$ in the susceptibility formula $\chi=C / T+$ $+\alpha$. In YbAG our theoretical value of 0.00336 compares favourably with 0.00359 , the experimental value given by Ball et al. [18]. Using their YAG: $\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}$ parameters, Pearson et al. [5] obtained $\alpha=0.00272$. In YbGG we got $\alpha=0.00356$, for YGG: $\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}$. Pearson et al. [5] obtained 0.00345 . These values are to be compared with the YbGG experimental data $\alpha=0.00358$ [18] and 0.00397 [19].

## 5. Discussion and Conclusions

In the present work we have succeeded to interpret diverse Raman, optical, EPR,
and susceptibility data in YbAG and YbGG by the set of phenomenological crystal-
field parameters $B_{kq}$ and Raman intensity parameter $R$. Considering the Raman
scattering data of Argyle et al. [10] and Wadsack [11], and reinterpreting the $^2\text{F}_{5/2}$
multiplet data of Buchanan et al. [6] in YbAG, we could remove one of the problems

Crystal-Field Analysis of Electronic Raman Scattering Data in YbAG and YbGG

underlying this work, the significant difference between the $Yb^{3+}$ crystal-field parameters in aluminium and gallium garnets obtained by Pearson et al. [5] (see Table 1). On the other hand, our YbAG and YbGG crystal-field parameters still do not agree with the superposition model, in the same way as reported by Newman and Stedman [8] in the case of YGG: $Yb^{3+}$. It means that especially the sixth-order parameters are very poorly reproduced by the model. As the validity of the super- position model has been verified in fourteen different $Nd^{3+}, Eu^{3+}, Tb^{3+}, Dy^{3+}$ , and $Er^{3+}$ garnets [9], we ascribe the above failure above all to the remaining uncertainty in the values of crystal-field parameters in Yb garnets. As we show in Table 1, the mean errors associated with the parameters, and those of the sixth order in particular, are rather important. For example, the latter are an order of magnitude greater than in $Nd^{3+}$ garnets [8].

The best-fit value of the relative Raman intensity parameter $|\alpha^{2} / \alpha^{1}|$ or $R$ equal to 2.21 is to be compared with the rough estimate $R \approx 9$ due to Lewis [13]. It is worth mentioning that the same difference by a factor four is estimated to appear in the case of $Dy^{3+}$ garnets [13].

The measurement of the electronic Raman scattering introduces new possibilities into the determination of the crystal field. Our results suggest that in the case that the phonon- and electronic lines are well separated, as in YbAG, then not only the energy shifts but also the relative Raman intensities may serve as the reliable reference data. It seems, however, that the necessary overdetermination of the numerical problem is in the particular case of ytterbium garnets not achieved before the $g$-values of the excited states will be determined.
