(Received 18 March 1998; revised manuscript received 28 May 1998)

The theory of a donor bound magnetic polaron (BMP) in Cr-based diluted magnetic semiconductors is presented. The Cr BMP generalizes the physical situation for Mn-based and Fe-based BMP due to particular energy structure of $Cr^{2+}$ ions, exhibiting features typical for both $Mn^{2+}$ and $Fe^{2+}$ ions. The ground state of the $Cr^{2+}$ ion is a semidoublet, yielding a permanent magnetic moment of the ion, similarly as for $Mn^{2+}$ or $Co^{2+}$ ions. On the other hand the ground state is followed by closely lying excited states, analogously to the situation for $Fe^{2+}$ ions. The developed Cr BMP model recovers characteristic behavior of both Mn BMP and Fe BMP: zero-field spin-flip energy (a fingerprint of Mn BMP) and anticrossings of BMP states (typical for Fe BMP). The model provides a perfect description of the BMP energy structure recently observed in a spin-flip Raman scattering experiment of $Cd_{1-x}Cr_{x}S$. Additionally, from this analysis the $Cd_{1-x}Cr_{x}S$ conduction band exchange integral is evaluated as $N_{0}\alpha=+0.22$ eV. [S0163-1829(98)02435-7]

## I. INTRODUCTION

The most characteristic feature of diluted magnetic semiconductors (DMS's, mixed semiconductor crystals, for which a fraction of nonmagnetic cations is substituted by magnetic ions $^{1-3}$) is strong $sp$-$d$ exchange interaction between spins of band or impurity electrons and spins of magnetic ions. This interaction leads to spectacular phenomena, such as giant conduction and valence bands splitting in magnetic field $^{1-3}$ or polaron effects. $^{5-12}$ The so-called bound magnetic polaron (BMP) is a system of an impurity bound carrier and magnetic ions within its orbit. The carrier and the magnetic ions are coupled by the exchange interaction, which lowers the total energy of the system and in this way a ''magnetic molecule'' is formed. $^{5,6}$ In the case of magnetic ions with permanent magnetic moments, such as $Mn^{2+}$ ($S=\frac{5}{2}$, $L=0$) or $Co^{2+}$ ($S=\frac{3}{2}$), in the absence of a magnetic field, there is always some magnetic moment $\mathbf{M}$ within the impurity orbit due to thermodynamic fluctuations. $^{7-9}$ The spin of the impurity electron can, in principle, be parallel or antiparallel to $\mathbf{M}$. Both configurations differ in energy due to $sp$-$d$ exchange, which gives rise to nonzero spin-flip energy considered to be a signature of BMP effect. $^{4-9,13}$ Additional lowering of the energy can be obtained if the spins of magnetic ions are aligned parallel, due to the same interaction with the impurity carrier spin (so-called saturation or collective regime). $^{4,7,9}$ The fluctuation regime is characteristic for donor BMP, for which $s$-$d$ exchange is relatively weak while the donor orbit is relatively large. The saturation regime may be reached either at very low temperatures, $^{9}$ or in the case of acceptor BMP$^{5}$ for which $p$-$d$ exchange is typically stronger, and the acceptor wave function is more localized. BMP based on DMS with ions, where the ground state is an isolated multiplet will be called Mn BMP along this paper.

The situation is different for Fe-based DMS's: the $Fe^{2+}$ ion is a Van Vleck-type ion, it carries no permanent magnetic moment, so there are no fluctuations giving rise to $\mathbf{M}$, as in the case of the $Mn^{2+}$ ion. $^{10,14-16}$ Although the exchange interaction between impurity carrier spin and $Fe^{2+}$ ions induces some magnetic moment of the ions, it is not possible to flip impurity electron spin with respect to $Fe^{2+}$ ion spins. Consequently, the spin-flip energy is strictly zero in the absence of a magnetic field. $^{16}$ However, the BMP effect manifests itself in another way. Unlike $Mn^{2+}$ or $Co^{2+}$ ions whose energy spectrum is dominated by an isolated multiplet, while the excited states are sufficiently remote to be neglected, the $Fe^{2+}$ ion energy structure consists of a series of five closely lying states. $^{17-19}$ In effect, the Fe-based BMP energy structure reveals characteristic anticrossings of the energy levels, in the presence of an applied magnetic field. $^{10,16}$ Further we call Fe BMP the BMP based on DMS with ions possessing singlet ground state.

Cr-based DMS's represent an intermediate case. The ground state of the $Cr^{2+}$ ion is a semidoublet, $^{20,21}$ yielding a permanent magnetic moment of the ion, similarly to $Mn^{2+}$. On the other hand the ground state is followed by another doublet and a singlet, i.e., there is a series of excited states. Therefore one could expect both types of polaronic effects: the zero-field splitting and the anticrossings of the excited states. The second expectation was recently confirmed in a spin-flip Raman scattering experiment of $Cd_{1-x}Cr_{x}S$, where the anticrossing of Raman lines was observed. $^{22}$ Only a simple analysis of these data was possible, since a proper BMP model was lacking. This situation motivated us to generalize the cases of Mn BMP and Fe BMP and to develop a model appropriate for Cr BMP.

Another aspect of Cr BMP concerns evaluation of $sp$-$d$ exchange parameters. Typically the conduction-band ($N_{0}\alpha$) and valence-band ($N_{0}\beta$) exchange parameters are obtained from the exciton splittings as functions of the macroscopic

magnetization. $^{1-3,23,24}$ In the case of Cr DMS's this method could be used only to a limited extent. Due to a low Cr content in available crystals, the exciton splittings were small and not all transitions could have been resolved. $^{25-28}$ In effect only the difference $N_{0} \alpha-N_{0} \beta$ could be evaluated. The difference obtained for Cr DMS's was negative, $N_{0} \alpha$ $-N_{0} \beta<0$, in contrast to the situation for all Mn-, Co-, and Fe-based DMS's studied so far. $^{28}$ Having in mind that $s$-$d$ exchange is dominated by a direct exchange mechanism, $N_{0} \alpha$ was expected to be positive, similarly to other DMS's. $^{1-3}$ This would be equivalent to positive $N_{0} \beta$, i.e., ferromagnetic, $p$-$d$ exchange. Basing ourselves on the assumption that $N_{0} \alpha \approx+0.2 \mathrm{eV}$, the positive values of $N_{0} \beta$ were obtained for $\mathrm{Zn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cr}_{x} \mathrm{~S}, \quad \mathrm{Zn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cr}_{x} \mathrm{Se}$, and $\mathrm{Zn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cr}_{x} \mathrm{Te} .{ }^{28}$ Since such a situation is unique among DMS's and points to a new exchange mechanism, it is crucial to obtain a reliable value of $N_{0} \alpha$. Recent results of spinflip Raman scattering $^{22}$ opened the possibility to evaluate $N_{0} \alpha$ separately and, then, $N_{0} \beta$ from the difference $N_{0} \alpha$ $-N_{0} \beta$. However, the spin-flip energy is affected by the BMP effect, so evaluation of $N_{0} \alpha$ requires an appropriate BMP model.

## II. THEORY OF CHROMIUM-BASED POLARON

Before the full model of the BMP in Cr DMS's is presented, we shall discuss two limiting cases of the polaron problem: a delocalized electron interacting with an infinite number of $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ions and one electron coupled by the exchange interaction to a single $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ion. The first one roughly gives a view of the energetic scheme of BMP, while the second one fully takes into account the exchange interaction and recovers basic BMP features.

### A. Noninteracting polaron

As the zeroth-order approach to the BMP problem, the energy spectrum of the set of $N$ noninteracting $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ions coupled with a completely delocalized electron will be found. This may be considered as a limiting case of the BMP with a large donor radius, where the distribution probability density of finding of an electron at a given site tends to zero. Therefore the interaction between the BMP levels is expected to be negligible, $^{16}$ as well as the zero-field splitting. In this case, where the actual polaron effects do not occur, the energy of the "BMP" is a simple sum of the $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ions' energy (exchange interaction) and the electron energy (direct interaction with magnetic field). The splitting, with respect to the ground state, of the lowest-energy levels of such a system is shown in Fig. 1 (the ground state means that all $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ions are in their ground states and the electron is in the spin-down state). The spin-flip energy of the electron is represented by the thick dashed line, intra-ion transitions of a single $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ion are represented by the solid lines (the $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ energy spectrum is calculated within the lines of the crystal-field model taking into account Jahn-Teller distortions $^{20,21,29,30}$ and the hexagonal crystal field), and combined intra- $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ and spinflip transitions are drawn by the dashed lines. This diagram does not include the possibility of the excitation of two or more $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ions (which, however, determines the higherenergy spectrum). In an example, the assumed molar fraction of the chromium ion was $x=0.0024$, and there are two possible crossings of the spin-flip line and the intrachromium transitions (at $B \approx 1.5$ and $7 \mathrm{~T}$, as marked by the arrows in Fig. 1).

### B. One-ion model

The simplest polaronlike system can be built of an electron coupled to a single magnetic ion. $^{16}$ The simple calculations below can provide only some insight into the problem. The set of the basis wave functions for such a problem,
$$
\Phi_{j \sigma}=\phi_{j} \chi_{\sigma}, \quad \text { (1) }
$$
where $\phi_{j}$ refers to the $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ion and $\chi_{\sigma}$ is the electron spindown and spin-up state, consists of 50 functions (25 ion functions and two spin states of the electron). In the absence of the magnetic field, the Hamiltonian takes the form
$$
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\mathcal{H}_{e-i}, \quad \text { (2) }
$$
where $\mathcal{H}_{0}$ represents the crystal field acting on the magnetic ion, and
$$
\mathcal{H}_{e-i}=-\alpha \hat{\mathbf{S}} \hat{\mathbf{s}} \quad \text { (3) }
$$
represents the isotropic, Heisenberg exchange interaction between the electron and the ion. Here $\hat{\mathbf{S}}=\left(\hat{S}_{x}, \hat{S}_{y}, \hat{S}_{z}\right)$ and $\hat{\mathbf{s}}$ denotes spin operators of the magnetic ion and the electron, respectively. Although the exchange interaction, in the case of the $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ion, may have a more complicated form, $^{31,33,32}$ it is believed that the isotropic, Heisenberg term dominates. $^{28}$ Moreover we will show below that anisotropic terms, if any, are irrelevant.

The calculations were performed assuming a simple basis of the $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ wave functions: $\phi_{j}=\left|l_{z}, s_{z}\right\rangle(L=2, S=2$, which is the case of both $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ion and $\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}$ ion). The eigenenergies of this problem [Eq. (2)] calculated as a function of the exchange constant, $\alpha$, are shown in Fig. 2 for ten lowest lying states. For specificity, the CdS crystal field was assumed in $\mathcal{H}_{0}$. Naturally, when $\alpha=0$ the energies of a $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ion in a CdS crystal field were recovered. $^{34}$

![](./images/812769332852424705_2.jpg)

FIG. 2. The eigenenergies of one-ion polaron model (the lowest energy spectrum) as a function of the exchange constant $\alpha$ (antiferromagnetic case on the left-hand side, and ferromagnetic on the right-hand side) for $B\parallel c$ and $B\rightarrow0$. All the lines are Kramers-(doubly) degenerate.

All the lines in Fig. 2 are doubly degenerate since the system consists of an odd number of electrons ($d^{4}$ of $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ion plus one coupled electron), so the Kramers theorem applies. This degeneracy is not lifted by the exchange, since the exchange does not remove the time-reversal symmetry (which the magnetic field does). A similar situation was noted in the case of the $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion coupled to an electron. $^{16}$ However, in the case of Fe BMP there is no first-order effect of the Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}_{e-i}$ on the ground state since it is an orbital and spin singlet. Despite this fact the lowering of the ground state does occur (proportionally to $\alpha^{2}$ for small $\alpha$), which is related to the Van Vleck nature of the Fe BMP. $^{16}$ In the case of Cr BMP the lowering of the ground state is linear in $\alpha$ when $\alpha>0$ (which is also the case for Mn BMP). In contrast, the first excited state reveals strong quadratic behavior for small $\alpha$ resulting from an admixture of higher states.

To close the discussion of the one-ion model, we would like to note some general remarks concerning more realistic Cr BMP models (i.e., involving more than just one magnetic ion). In the absence of the magnetic field, the mixing of the polaron states concerns the excited states only (above-mentioned quadratic dependence in $\alpha$). On the other hand, the presence of the exchange interaction distinguishes between the two states belonging to the ground semidoublet favoring energetically one of these states. As a consequence this provides the nonzero net magnetic moment of the BMP.

### C. Multi-ion model of Cr BMP

The one-ion model is conceptually important because it can be solved exactly for an arbitrary strength of the coupling constant $\alpha$ (at least, if one restricts the basis to the $^{5}\text{D}$ multiplet of the $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ion). Unfortunately, it cannot be used to describe a realistic Cr BMP containing $N>1$ ions. Indeed, as we will show below, the initial slope of the SF line versus $B$ is proportional to $N$, while the anticrossings between this line and the intra-ion excitation lines are proportional to the square root of $N$.

On the other hand, the multi-ion model for the Cr BMP is very difficult to solve, since the full basis of the BMP contains $[(2L+1)(2S+1)]^{N}=25^{N}$ states, which is about $10^{28}$ states for $N=20$. All these states will be mixed by the exchange interaction between the electron spin and the $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ions. Moreover, compared to the case of Fe BMP, which possesses a singlet ground state, many states of the Cr BMP are populated.

In the following we will solve the Cr BMP problem in the weak coupling limit when the exchange interaction can be treated perturbatively. Such an approximation is appropriate for the donor bound polaron (large Bohr radius and relatively small exchange interaction). In this case the calculation can be carried out using the property of the exchange Hamiltonian, namely, that it couples only multi-ion states that differ by one-ion excitation. Also we will focus only on the BMP states, which are accessible in the experiment under consideration, $^{22}$ namely, the quasidegenerate ground state (initial states of Raman transitions) and the one-ion excited states, which constitute the final states of the Raman process.

The Hamiltonian of the multi-ion BMP eigenproblem is of the following form
$$
\mathcal{H}_{\text{BMP}}=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\mathcal{H}_{\text{ex}}+\mathcal{H}_{Z}, \tag{4}
$$
where $\mathcal{H}_{0}$ represents the single $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ion crystal-field Hamiltonian (including cubic field with hexagonal crystal field of the wurtzite, static Jahn-Teller distortion of the $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ion, spin-orbit coupling, and the effect of the external magnetic field), $^{34}$ $\mathcal{H}_{\text{ex}}$ describes the exchange interaction between an electron and $N$ magnetic ions, $\mathcal{H}_{Z}$ is the Zeeman part representing direct interaction of an electron with the magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$:
$$
\mathcal{H}_{Z}=g_{e}\mu_{\text{B}}\mathbf{B}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{s}}. \tag{5}
$$

Let us now consider an electron of spin $\sigma$ (represented by the $\chi_{\sigma}$ spin function) and described by the spherically symmetric donor orbital wave function $\Phi(r)$. Let $N$ be the number of $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ions within the orbit of the electron, noninteracting with each other (we are dealing with a very dilute limit of chromium). The eigenstate of each $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ion in the crystal field and a given magnetic field will be denoted by $\varphi_{j}$, where subscript $j$ numbers the eigenenergies (for detailed calculations of $\varphi_{j}$ see Ref. 34). The BMP wave function in the Hartree-type approximation has the following form: $^{8}$
$$
\Psi_{\text{BMP}}=\Phi(r)\chi_{\sigma}\Psi(R_{1},\dots,R_{N}), \tag{6}
$$
where $\Psi(R_{1},\dots,R_{N})$ represents the wave function of the set of $N$ $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ions in the similar manner as in Ref. 16:
$$
\Psi(R_{1},\dots,R_{N})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\varphi_{j}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{i}). \tag{7}
$$
Here $j$ denotes the state of an ion, $i$ numbers the magnetic sites. The orbital part of the donor electron wave function is not affected by the exchange interaction since it has $S$ symmetry, which decouples it from the spin-orbit interaction. The exchange interaction described by the $s$-$d$ exchange constant $\alpha$ has, in this case, the following form:
$$
\mathcal{H}_{\text{ex}}=-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\hat{\mathbf{s}}\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{i}), \tag{8}
$$

where the operator $\mathcal{H}_{\text{ex}}$ acts only on the $\chi_{\sigma}$ and $\varphi$ at a given $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ site (denoted by $\mathbf{R}_{i}$). In this way the matrix element of the $\mathcal{H}_{\text{ex}}$ function reads

$$
\left\langle\Phi(r)\left|\mathcal{H}_{\text{ex}}\right| \Phi(r)\right\rangle=-\alpha \sum_{i=1}^{N} \hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i} \hat{\mathbf{s}}\left|\Phi\left(\mathbf{R}_{i}\right)\right|^{2}. \tag{9}
$$

Thus the orbital part of the electron wave function, $\Phi(r)$, enters the exchange Hamiltonian as a probability density distribution, describing, in effect, the strength of the interaction at the given site $i$. A realistic description assuming hydrogeniclike density distribution, $|\Phi(r)|^{2}$, was obtained in the case of Fe BMP. $^{16}$ We note that in the case of $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ions the ground state, $\varphi_{1}$, is a singlet and the first excited state (in the CdSe crystal field) is about $1.6 \text{ meV}$ ($18 \text{ K}$) above. $^{19}$ Therefore, the ground state of the set of $N \text{ Fe}^{2+}$ ions can be regarded in the form $\Psi_{1}\left(R_{1}, \ldots, R_{N}\right)=\Pi_{i=1}^{N} \varphi_{1}\left(r-R_{i}\right)$. Interchanging two ions in such a case reproduces the same ground state $\Psi_{1}$. The situation for the $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ is different—the ground state is a semidoublet located about $0.7 \text{ meV}$ ($8 \text{ K}$) below the first excited state $^{20-22}$—and therefore both $\varphi_{1}$ and $\varphi_{2}$ states are occupied at low magnetic fields. Having in mind that $\left|\Phi\left(R_{i}\right)\right|^{2}$ distinguishes, in general, magnetic ions' sites, there will be $2^{N}$ possible spatial configurations of $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ions, which complicates the form of the $\Psi_{1}$ ground-state wave function. In order to overcome this difficulty one has to assume $\left|\Phi\left(R_{i}\right)\right|^{2}$ is constant within the BMP range (muffin-tin approximation). Then, all the possible spatial distributions of the two types, $\varphi_{1}$ or $\varphi_{2}$, of $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ centers are equivalent. The normalized muffin-tin envelope has the form

$$
|\Phi(r)|^{2}=\frac{x N_{0}}{N} \theta\left(r_{0}-r\right), \tag{10}
$$

where $\theta\left(r_{0}-r\right)$ is the step (Heaviside) function, $N_{0}$ is the number of cations in the unit volume, $x$ is the molar fraction of magnetic ions, and $r_{0}$ is the muffin-tin radius.

The BMP basis will be built starting with the ground-state wave function:

$$
\Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}=\varphi_{1}^{1} \cdots \varphi_{1}^{P} \cdot \varphi_{2}^{P+1} \cdots \varphi_{2}^{N} \chi_{\sigma}, \tag{11}
$$

where the following convention is used for $\varphi_{j}^{i}$: subscript $j$ denotes the state of the $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ion located on the site $i$; $P$ and $N-P$ are the averaged ground and the first excited state occupation numbers of the set of $N \text{ Cr}^{2+}$ ions. The wave function (11) is not, in principle, the eigenstate of the Hamiltonian (4). However, we take advantage of the fact that the exchange interaction is only a small perturbation, so, in $B$ $=0$, this state is weakly perturbed (the larger the donor radius the weaker the perturbation).

We note that $P$ calculated for the set of $N$ ions within the ‘‘noninteracting’’ polaron (Sec. II A) in a thermodynamic equilibrium is

$$
P=\frac{N}{1+e^{-\Delta E / k_{\mathrm{B}} T}}, \tag{12}
$$

where $\Delta E$ is the splitting between the ground state, $\varphi_{1}$, and the first excited level of $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ion, $\varphi_{2}$. This result cannot apply to the case of the polaron formed from the ions with the multiplet ground state since it predicts an equal number of ions in spin-up and spin-down states in the absence of magnetic field (i.e., when $\Delta E=0$). Consequently there would be no zero-field splitting, which is not the case. Below, we will deal with this problem.

We recall that $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ions experience strong, static Jahn-Teller tetragonal distortion along one of the three $\langle 100\rangle$ axes. $^{20,21,28,34}$ In general, the external magnetic field applied along a certain direction will make these three types of Jahn-Teller centers inequivalent. As a consequence the interpretation of the BMP effects complicates. However, if $\mathbf{B} \|\langle 111\rangle$ the analysis simplifies, since for $\langle 111\rangle$ all Jahn-Teller centers are equivalent. Therefore, we restrict further discussion to this case, which is the experimental situation of Ref. 22. We will regard hexagonal (wurtzite) crystals (e.g., CdS) as cubic crystals stressed along the $\langle 111\rangle$ direction ($c$ axis).

The main consequence of the existence of three types of $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ centers is that the $\varphi$ wave functions are of three different forms. This is not explicitly expressed in Eq. (11) in order to simplify the notation (but we shall keep this fact in mind all along in this paper). Naturally for $\mathbf{B} \| c$ the average number of each type of the centers must be the same if $N$ is large enough. Then, although a single $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ center introduces a significant magnetic anisotropy, the resulting total mean spin $x\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$ is parallel to the $c$ axis. $^{34}$ This may not be true if the number of $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ ions is small—e.g., within the donor orbit for small $x$. Then the electron may interact with an unequal number of each type of Jahn-Teller centers. The region within the donor orbit becomes then magnetically anisotropic. This local effect may lead to an increase of the mean spin value $x\langle S\rangle$ (now $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$ is not parallel to the $c$ axis) affecting the donor electron in comparison with the mean-field approximation (MFA) for the extended states. Although for the finite $N$ there might be some fluctuations around an equal distribution of each center providing some local magnetic anisotropy, as mentioned above, they are of the order of $1 / 3 \sqrt{N}$, so we can neglect them.

Single magnetic ion excitation is one of the mechanisms responsible for the entire BMP excitation. $^{10,16}$ The excited state of the BMP depends on the initial state of the ion (which can be of two types, $\varphi_{1}$ or $\varphi_{2}$). Thus, the excited states, after normalization, have one of the following forms:

$$
\Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P}} \sum_{i=1}^{P}\left|\varphi_{1}^{1} \cdots \varphi_{j}^{i} \cdots \varphi_{1}^{P} \varphi_{2}^{P+1} \cdots \varphi_{2}^{N} \chi_{\sigma}\right\rangle, \quad(13 \mathrm{a})
$$

$$
\Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-P}} \sum_{i=1}^{N-P}\left|\varphi_{1}^{1} \cdots \varphi_{1}^{P} \varphi_{2}^{P+1} \cdots \varphi_{j}^{i} \cdots \varphi_{2}^{N} \chi_{\sigma}\right\rangle. \tag{13b}
$$

This set, obviously, is orthonormal: $\left\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{\mu P} | \Psi_{k \tilde{\sigma}}^{\lambda P}\right\rangle$ $=\delta_{j k} \delta_{\mu \lambda} \delta_{\sigma \tilde{\sigma}}$. In the calculations that follow, we will restrict ourselves to one-ion excitations, neglecting the possibility of multi-ion excitations (two ion excitations for Fe BMP were explored in Ref. 16). We claim that two or more ion excitations affect the low-energy BMP states determined by the $\text{Cr}^{2+}$ states: $\varphi_{1}$, $\varphi_{2}$, $\varphi_{3}$ less than one-ion excitations, due to energy denominators.

The BMP basis introduced above consists, in general, of $2(1+24 \cdot 2)=98$ functions (the factor 2 in the parentheses represents two possible one-ion excitations—Eqs. (13a) and

(13b); the multiplication factor 2 reflects two spin polariza- tions of the donor electron). However, the anticrossings of the polaron levels are not first-order effects, so we take ad- vantage of the fact that the pattern of the five lowest-lying Cr²⁺ energy levels is separated by about 100 meV from the still higher states. Therefore, we truncate the BMP basis to 2+8·2=18 functions [i is not larger than 5 in Eqs. (13a) and (13b)].

Before writing explicitly the matrix elements, we note that $\mathcal{H}_{0}$ and $\mathcal{H}_{Z}$ of the Hamiltonian (4) are diagonal in our basis. Diagonal matrix elements, responsible for the first- order effects, will be discussed first. The ground-level energy to the first-order approximation reads

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{1 \sigma}= & \left\langle\Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}\right\rangle \\
= & {\left[P E_{1}+(N-P) E_{2}\right]+\frac{-x N_{0} \alpha}{N}[P\langle 1 \sigma|\hat{h}| 1 \sigma\rangle} } \\
& +(N-P)\langle 2 \sigma|\hat{h}| 2 \sigma\rangle]+g_{e} \mu_{\mathrm{B}} B\left\langle\sigma\left|\hat{s}_{z}\right| \sigma\right\rangle, \quad(14)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $|i \sigma\rangle$ denotes $|\varphi_{i} \chi_{\sigma}\rangle$ and $\hat{h}=\hat{\mathbf{S}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{s}}, E_{i}$ represents the Cr²⁺ ion eigenenergies. Certainly $\hat{h}$ does not depend on the site number. Assuming $\alpha>0$ [which is the case of all known DMS's (Refs. 1–3 and 22)], the ground state of BMP to the first-order approximation has the form of Eq. (14), with $\sigma$ replaced by " $\downarrow$ " electron state. For a delocalized electron (i.e., when, formally, the donor radius $r_{0} \to \infty$ ), $P$ can be expressed by Eq. (12) yielding the mean field result for the spin-flip energy:

$$
\left\langle\Psi_{1 \uparrow}^{P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{1 \uparrow}^{P}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Psi_{1 \downarrow}^{P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{1 \downarrow}^{P}\right\rangle=x N_{0} \alpha\langle S\rangle+g_{e} \mu_{\mathrm{B}} B,
$$

where $\langle S\rangle=(P / N)\langle S_{1}\rangle+[(N-P) / N]\langle S_{2}\rangle$ represents the thermodynamical average of the $Cr^{2+}$ spin, assuming that only the ground and the first excited levels are occupied.

Other diagonal matrix elements are

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{j \sigma}^{1}= & \left\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}\right\rangle \\
= & \frac{-x N_{0} \alpha}{N}\left[\langle j \sigma|\hat{h}| j \sigma\rangle+(P-1)\langle 1 \sigma|\hat{h}| 1 \sigma\rangle\right. \\
& +(N-P)\langle 2 \sigma|\hat{h}| 2 \sigma\rangle] \\
& +\left[E_{j}+(P-1) E_{1}+(N-P) E_{2}+g_{e} \mu_{\mathrm{B}} B\left\langle\sigma\left|\hat{s}_{z}\right| \sigma\right\rangle\right], \\
& (16 \mathrm{a})
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{j \sigma}^{2}= & \left\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}\right\rangle \\
= & \frac{-x N_{0} \alpha}{N}\left[\langle j \sigma|\hat{h}| j \sigma\rangle+P\langle 1 \sigma|\hat{h}| 1 \sigma\rangle\right. \\
& +(N-P-1)\langle 2 \sigma|\hat{h}| 2 \sigma\rangle] \\
& +\left[E_{j}+P E_{1}+(N-P-1) E_{2}+g_{e} \mu_{\mathrm{B}} B\left\langle\sigma\left|\hat{s}_{z}\right| \sigma\right\rangle\right]. \\
& (16 \mathrm{~b})
\end{aligned}
$$

In the first-order approximation Eqs. (16) yield

$$
E_{j \uparrow}^{1}-E_{1 \downarrow} \approx E_{j}-E_{1}+x N_{0} \alpha\langle S\rangle+g_{\mathrm{e}} \mu_{\mathrm{B}} B, \quad(17 \mathrm{a})
$$

$$

Beginning the discussion concerning the off-diagonal matrix elements it is worth noting that all the elements of the form $\left\langle\Psi_{1 \tilde{\sigma}}^{P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}\right\rangle \quad\left(\left\langle\Psi_{j \tilde{\sigma}}^{1 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}\right\rangle\right.$, $\left\langle\Psi_{j \tilde{\sigma}}^{2 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}\right\rangle$ ), where $\tilde{\sigma} \neq \sigma$, are negligible. The reason is that there are three types of equivalent Jahn-Teller centers for $\mathbf{B} \| c$. By the equivalence of these centers, as already men tioned, we mean that the $c$ axis is the threefold symmetry axis for Jahn-Teller axes. For each of the $Cr^{2+}$ centers the spin of the ion is "pinned" to one of the three Jahn-Teller directions. $^{34}$ Therefore the spin component $\langle S_{\perp}\rangle$ perpendicu lar to the $c$ axis of a certain $Cr^{2+}$ is at the angle of $120^{\circ}$ or $-120^{\circ}$ to the other types of centers. In effect, for infinite $N$, the sum of the perpendicular spin components within BMP $(\langle\Sigma S_{\perp}\rangle)$ , vanishes-so the operator $\frac{1}{2} \hat{S}_{+} \hat{s}_{-}+\frac{1}{2} \hat{S}_{-} \hat{s}_{+}$ has no effect. Effectively, the nonzero off-diagonal matrix elements are the following:

$$
\left\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{1 \tilde{\sigma}}^{P}\right\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{P}}{N}\left(-x N_{0} \alpha\right)\langle j \sigma|\hat{h}| 1 \tilde{\sigma}\rangle, \quad(19 \mathrm{a})
$$

$$
\left\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{1 \tilde{\sigma}}^{P}\right\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{N-P}}{N}\left(-x N_{0} \alpha\right)\langle j \sigma|\hat{h}| 1 \tilde{\sigma}\rangle,
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{k \tilde{\sigma}}^{P}\right\rangle & =\frac{1}{N}\left(-x N_{0} \alpha\right)\langle j \sigma|\hat{h}| k \tilde{\sigma}\rangle \\
& =\left\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}\right| \Psi_{k \tilde{\sigma}}^{2 P}\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

It is worth noting that the matrix elements responsible for the mixing between the two populations of the BMP ground state differing by 1 are equal: $\langle\Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}|H_{BMP}| \Psi_{1 \tilde{\sigma}}^{P+1}\rangle$ $=\langle\Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}|H_{BMP}| \Psi_{2 \tilde{\sigma}}^{1 P}\rangle$ . However, mixing between the BMP's ground states, whose population difference exceeds 1, is zero: $\langle\Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}|H_{BMP}| \Psi_{1 \tilde{\sigma}}^{P+k}\rangle=0$ for $k>1$ .

The eigenproblem of the Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BMP}}$ simplifies be cause $\langle\Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}|H_{BMP}| \Psi_{k \tilde{\sigma}}^{2 P}\rangle=0$ , which means that the basis (13)defines two separate subspaces: $\Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P} \oplus \Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}$ and $\Psi_{1 \sigma}^{P}$ $\oplus \Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}$ , where $j$ runs over $2,..., 5$ . In effect the eigenproblem is further truncated, this time into two 10×10 matrices. However, while increasing the magnetic field the one-ion excitations described by the $\Psi_{j \sigma}^{2 P}$ functions become less probable due to the decreasing number of $Cr^{2+}$ ions involved in such processes $(N-P)$ . Therefore one can expect that in spin-flip Raman scattering experiments these excitations are much less important than $\Psi_{j \sigma}^{1 P}$ . Additionally, due to the in creasing splitting of the initial semidoublet in the applied magnetic field, $P$ increases (for the first crossing pointed by the arrow in Fig. 1 the splitting is about $\Delta E / k_{B}=3.5 ~K$ ).

The second anticrossing appearing at about 7 T should be less pronounced than the first one $(\sim 1.5$ T) due to the decreasing of $S_{+}^{1,3}$ magnitude with $B$. This decrease can be understood since, for high fields, magnetic ion spins become good quantum numbers. Consequently, the value of the operator $\hat{S}_{+}$between these states vanishes. On the other hand the similar argumentation applies to the increase of $S_{+}^{2,3}$.

In the present paragraph we shall deal with the problem of $P$ and $N-P$ values. As mentioned the difference between $P$ and $N-P$ in the absence of a magnetic field is responsible for the appearance of the splitting of the Cr BMP ground state. The values of $P$ and $N-P$ can be regarded as the mean populations of $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ ions in the ground and the first excited states, respectively. In order to find the appropriate partition function, we follow the semiclassical approach, with the spins treated as vectors of quantized length. ${ }^{4,8} \mathrm{We}$ also take advantage of the fact that only the two lowest $\mathrm{Cr}^{2+}$ levels are occupied below $T=4 \mathrm{~K}$. The energy of BMP in this "two-level" approximation at $B=0$ is defined by the equation
$$

$$

The problem of three or more occupied levels would be much more complicated, since then there are two unknown occupation numbers on the right-hand side of Eq. (20). In a particular case of two levels the mean occupation value $\langle P\rangle$ is determined simply by the mean polaron energy $\langle E\rangle$. ### III. COMPARISON WITH EXPERIMENTS

As mentioned above, the spin-flip Raman scattering (SFRS) experiment of $Cd_{1-x}Cr_xS$ was recently performed in the Voigt configuration with $\mathbf{B}\parallel c$ in the backscattering geometry at $T=1.9$ K. The incident and scattered light beams were linearly cross polarized. An argon laser line with the energy 2.539 eV (below the band gap) was used. $^{22}$ The Stokes part of Raman spectra is presented in Fig. 8 for three samples: CdS, $Cd_{1-x}Cr_xS$, and $Cd_{1-y}Cr_yS$, for which $x=y/10=0.0002$ (as obtained from magnetization measurements). The Raman shift for the sample with the lower chromium content has the same sign as the shift for the higher chromium concentration sample. The shift in the former sample is approximately ten times smaller compared to the shift in pure CdS. Having in mind that the electron $g$ factor is positive [$g_e=+1.76$ (Ref. 38)] this is consistent with a ferromagnetic sign of the $s$-$d$ exchange integral in $Cd_{1-x}Cr_xS$, as for other known DMS's.

The Stokes shift of the Raman lines as a function of the magnetic field for $Cd_{1-x}Cr_xS$ ($x=0.0024$, obtained from the magnetization) shows a pronounced anticrossing. Originally only one anticrossing was found in Ref. 22, but careful analysis of the experimental data suggests that there is an- other one, occurring between $B=6$ T and $B=7$ T (the energies of the transitions involved are marked by arrows in Fig. 9).

Finally, we shall discuss the observed changes in the intensity of the Raman line L1. The spin-flip (SF) Raman selection rules are well known for nonmagnetic semiconductors with wurtzite structure. $^{38}$ For $\mathbf{B}\parallel c$ the SF Raman transitions involve only $B$ excitons (i.e., light hole) or $C$ excitons (spin orbit). We recall that Raman processes are obtained in the second- or higher-order perturbation theory. Here, we focus on the lowest-order approximation, focusing on SF transitions (the single intra-Cr$^{2+}$ transitions or combined SF together with intrachromium transitions are described by the higher-order terms). The SF Raman transitions have been already investigated for Fe BMP, $^{15,16}$ however, the BMP wave functions entering the Raman tensors used there were not the eigenstates of BMP. R_{\mathrm{SF}}^{lk}=\sum_{\eta} \frac{\langle\tilde{\Psi}_{1}^{P}|S\uparrow\rangle\langle S\uparrow|\hat{P}^{k}|X_{\eta}\uparrow\rangle\langle X_{\eta}\downarrow|\hat{P}^{l}|S\downarrow\rangle\langle S\downarrow|\tilde{\Psi}_{1}^{P}\rangle}{h\nu-E_{X_{\eta}}+E_{\downarrow}},\qquad(31)
$$

where $\tilde{\Psi}$ are the eigenstates of the polaron: $\tilde{\Psi}_{1}^{P}$ corresponds to line L1, $\tilde{\Psi}_{0}^{P}$ is the ground state of the polaron (spin-down electron state) $\eta$ labels the exciton intermediate state—exciton bound to the neutral donor is represented by the hole eigenstate $|X\rangle$, $\hat{P}$ is the electric dipole operator of the valence-to-conduction-band transitions (indices $k,l$ run over the polarizations of the scattered or incident light, respectively). The result of the calculated Raman tensor $R_{\mathrm{SF}}^{lk}={\sum_{k,l}R_{\mathrm{SF}}^{lk}}$ for the L1 line in the Voigt configuration with crossed polarizations of the incident and scattered light is presented in Fig. 11. Two cases of the polaron muffin-tin radius are shown in this plot: $r_0=45$ Å, $N=20$ (solid line) and $r_0=100$ Å, $N=200$ (dashed line); other parameters are the same as for the calculations presented in Fig. 10. The incident light was assumed to have the energy $h\nu=2.539$ eV (argon laser line). The high intensity of a typical SF transition dominates in the smallest and the highest (10 T) fields in the range shown. In the anticrossing regions (polaron mixed modes) the intensity changes, since the character of the polaron mode changes (it is dominated by the spin-down electron states in the intermediate fields). It is clear that in the case of the larger donor radius the interaction between the polaron states is weaker. Therefore, the anticrossing regions are smaller. The electron spin polarizations are, then, less mixed—the intensity of L1 drops dramatically (dashed line in Fig. 11). For the same reason, the intensity of the line L2 should increase between 2 and 6 T. Finally it should be stressed that these calculations have rather qualitative value, since the higher-order Raman transitions are neglected (e.g., those responsible for intrachromium-type excitations).

## IV. CONCLUSIONS

A model of donor BMP in chromium-based DMS’s has been developed. The theory bridges the phenomena of Van Vleck–type BMP (Fe-based DMS’s) and the physics of Brillouin-type polarons (Mn-based DMS’s). The character of the first one is manifested by the anticrossings of the polaron levels, while the latter, coming from the multiplet ground state of the magnetic ion, is responsible for the zero-field splitting of the BMP spin-down/spin-up levels. The Cr BMP is, therefore, determined by both quantum-mechanical and thermodynamical features of the set of localized spins. The expectation value of the polaron free energy can be translated into the expectation values of the occupation numbers of the Cr$^{2+}$ ions within the donor orbit. The values of these occupation numbers are crucial for the construction of the BMP basis. They determine the zero-field splitting and the polaron energies at low magnetic fields. At higher fields the exchange induced interaction between the BMP states plays an important role, leading to a pronounced repulsion of the BMP levels, instead of the crossings. Finally, the existing

SFRS results for $\mathbf{B} \parallel c$ are well described by the present Cr-BMP model using only one adjustable parameter: $s$-$d$ exchange parameter $N_0\alpha$. The value of this parameter, $N_0\alpha$ $= +0.22$ eV extracted by us from the experiments, corroborates the previous estimate.
