# Vibrational polaritons in thin semiconducting films on metal surfaces

Evgeny An. Vinogradov

Institute of Spectroscopy, USSR Academy of Sciences,
142092, Troitsk, Moscow region, USSR

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Optical properties of thin films of polar dielectrics and semiconductors were the subject of a great number of scientific and technical publications. However, during the last twenty years new experimental results have been obtained which enriched our notions about the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with the condensed state of a substance. The most important of them are:
-detection of radiative surface polaritons in thin crystalline films of polar semiconductors $^{1,2}$;
-detection of strong interaction between elementary excitations in adjacent media (a film and a substrate) $^{3-5}$;
-detection of Raman scattering at the frequencies of wave-guide $^{1,6}$ and of interference $^{6}$ polaritons;
-observation of the spectra of thermostimulated radiation, absorption, luminescence and resonance Raman scattering (RRS) at the frequencies of interference modes (IMs) in films $^{5,7}$
-detection of strong resonance interaction between local vibrations of impurity atoms and IMs in films $^{5,8,9}$.

In the present paper an attempt is made to explain the totality of the experimental data the author is aware of on optical properties of the films of different thicknesses from super thin to bulk single crystals obtained by various optical spectroscopy methods within the framework of macroscopic linear crystal optics in the polariton language.

## 2. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND

Light absorption, reflection and radiation by vibrational states of crystals and films (by optical phonons and plasmons) can be explained successfully on the basis of the concept of macroscopic dielectric function of the medium, i.e. they can be well described by means of the Maxwell equations with ordinary boundary conditions and material equations of the medium. In fact, if the dielectric functions of a crystal (a film or a slab) and of surrounding media are known, the solution of the Maxwell macroscopic equations with ordinary boundary conditions yields a result which, as a rule, is in a very good agreement with experiments. However, such approach does not allow detailed clarification of what the absorbed energy turns into and what particular vibrational state of the sample is responsible for this or that peak in the spectrum. This problem became especially burning upon the detection of absorption, radiation and luminescence spectra at the frequencies of IMs in films of dielectrics and semiconductors on various substrates $^{1-12}$.

As shown in the works $^{13}$, the dispersion laws of bulk and surface polaritons for a three-layer "vacuum - dielectric slab - substrate" structure infinite in $x$ and $y$ directions and at the dielectric slab of the thickness $d$ in the direction of $z$ axis should be written, in particular, for $p$-polarization (for TM-polaritons) in the form $^{1,13}$:

$$
\cot(\beta_2 d) = i \frac{\beta_2^2 \epsilon_1 \epsilon_3 + \epsilon_2^2 \beta_1 \beta_3}{\beta_2 \epsilon_2(\beta_1 \epsilon_3 + \beta_3 \epsilon_1)}, \tag{1}
$$

where $\beta_j = \sqrt{\epsilon_j \frac{\omega^2}{c^2} - q^2 \epsilon_1}; i = (-1)^{1/2}$ and $q$ is the two-dimensional wave vector in the film plane,

$\epsilon_2 = \epsilon_2(\omega)$, $\epsilon_3 = \epsilon_3(\omega)$ and $\epsilon_1$ are the dielectric functions of the substances of the film, the substrate and vacuum, respectively. This equation describes three families of polariton branches of the structure - the dependencies $\omega(q)$: one at real $\beta_1$ and two at imaginary $\beta_1$. The first family which corresponds to interference modes is in the radiative region of the $\omega - q$ diagram and the other two which correspond to waveguide and surface (interface) modes of the structure are in the nonradiative region. The spectra of light absorption $(A)$ and thermal radiation $(E)$ by these modes are well described (including the case, when measurements are made in the ATR regime) by the formula $^{1,3}$:

$$
A = E = 1-\left| \frac{(\delta_1 - \delta_2)M + (\delta_1 + \delta_2)N}{(\delta_1 + \delta_2)M + (\delta_1 - \delta_2)N} \right|^2 \tag{2}
$$

where
$$
M = (\delta_2 + \delta_3)(\delta_3 + \delta_4) + (\delta_2 - \delta_3)(\delta_3 - \delta_4)exp(-2\kappa_3 l\omega/c);
$$
$$
N = [(\delta_2 - \delta_3)(\delta_3 + \delta_4) + (\delta_2 + \delta_3)(\delta_3 - \delta_4)exp(-2\kappa_3 l\omega/c)]exp(-2\kappa_2 d\omega/c);
$$

$\delta_j = \epsilon_j/\kappa_j$ for $p$-polarized light; $\delta_j = \kappa_j$ for $s$-polarized light; $\kappa_j = \sqrt{\epsilon_1 sin^2 \phi - \epsilon_j}$; $j$=1,2,3,4. Here $\epsilon_1$ is the dielectric function of the semi-cylinder material (ATR prism), $\epsilon_2$ is the dielectric function of the gap, $\epsilon_3(\omega)$ and $\epsilon_4(\omega)$ are the dielectric functions of the film and the substrate, respectively; $l$ and $d$ are the thicknesses of the gap and the dielectric (semiconductor) film, and $\phi$ is the radiation angle (inside the medium with $\epsilon_1$).

It was shown in the works $^{2,5}$ that in thin $\beta_2 d << 1$ films of polar semiconductors of the $A^2 B^6$ family on metallic substrates light absorption and thermostimulated radiation are due to only by surface polaritons of film - vacuum and film - substrate interfaces and vibrational states of the film bulk (longitudinal and transverse phonons) do not interact with light. Transverse optical phonons in the film are observed experimentally if the film is separated from the metallic substrate by a dielectric interlayer $^{2,3,5}$. These investigations revealed also the existence of radiative surface polaritons in films and the appearance of additional nonradiative surface polaritons in films on metallic substrates $^{1-5,12}$.

## 3. LIGHT ABSORPTION BY RADIATIVE POLARITONS

Radiative polaritons can be studied experimentally by light absorption (reflection) spectra as well as by the spectra of thermal radiation of samples. In the latter case polaritons are created inside the structure by thermal motion of its charged particles. Radiative decay of these polaritons, i.e. their transformation into light, is detected experimentally. Thus, in addition to conventional anharmonic decay of elementary excitations in a substance of finite size there exists an additional decay channel: a radiative channel. The radiative decay channel is absent in an infinite crystal. It is due to the radiative instability of these polaritons that their interaction with the external electromagnetic field, i.e. light, is possible. Interference polaritons can clearly manifest themselves in transmission, reflection, thermostimulated radiation and luminescence spectra of films $^{5-11}$. However, the role of IMs (radiative bulk polaritons) in the formation of absorption spectra of crystals still remains unclarified, the same as the role of a metallic substrate.

Consider the case of an ideal dielectric of finite thickness deposited on a metal. Only vacuum photons and surface plasmons of the metal are the initial excitations in such a structure. Their interaction in the "vacuum - film - metal" structure leads to the formation of polaritons whose dispersion branches are described by Eq.1. As seen from this Figure, in this structure interference polaritons, waveguide polaritons and a surface plasmon - polariton are present.

Figure 3 shows schematically the density of one-phonon states (for optical phonons) of a ZnSe single crystal $^{14}$ which is localized in the frequency region $\omega_{LO}<\omega<\omega_{TO}$. Figure 3b presents the spectra of thermostimulated radiation $E$ in ZnSe films of various thicknesses on aluminium substrates $^{1-3}$. The emissivity of the sandwiches shown in Fig. 3b is the intensity of the thermostim ulated radiation normalized by the radiation intensity of the black body at the same temperature. These experimental data show that in a bulk single crystal light absorption and radiation take place in the region of the frequencies $\omega_{e x}>\omega>\omega_{LO}$, and $\omega<\omega_{TO}$, where no one-phonon states of the crystal are present, rather than at the frequencies of one-photon states of the crystal in the fre- quency region $\omega_{LO}<\omega<\omega_{TO}$. From radiative polaritons into the frequency regions $\omega_{e x}>\omega>\omega_{LO}$ and $\omega<\omega_{TO}$ there are interference modes only.

The first and the only "interference mode" at $2 n d=\lambda$ appears in that spectral region where $n(\lambda)$ is maximum, i.e. in the frequency region which is adjacent to a transverse optical phonon of the crystal $\omega_{TO}$ from the low-frequency side, where the crystal has large values of $Re(\epsilon(\omega))$ and $Im(\epsilon(\omega))$. At $\beta_{2} d \simeq m \pi / 2, m=1,3,5,...$ Eq. 1 gives the frequencies of additional - compared to a thin film states of the film which at real $\epsilon(\omega)$ are well-known IMs of a mirror Fabry-Perot interferometer $^{14}$. Thus, upon a transition from $\beta_{2} d<<1$ to $\beta_{2} d \simeq \pi / 2$ the radiative surface polariton of the film substrate boundary smoothly transforms into the first IM. With further increase of the film thickness next IMs with $m=3,5,...$ will appear. Each arising new absorption (radiation) band at the frequency $\nu_{TO}=200 ~cm^{-1}$ grows rapidly in intensity proportionally to $d^{3}$, reaches its maximum and then slowly goes down becoming strongly broadened (see Fig. 3b and 4a). At the ZnSe film thickness in excess of $2 \mu m$ it becomes impossible already to refer these bands unambiguously to surface or bulk phonons. These bands are due to radiative states of the film which refer to appropriate polariton branches of the sandwich.

A generally accepted concept is that in the spectral region under consideration a photon incident from vacuum onto a crystal generates in this crystal a phonon near the centre of the Brillouin zone. Light absorption $A$ with no account for multibeam interference in a crystal slab is proportional tothe imaginary part of the dielectric function:

$$A \sim \operatorname{Im}(\epsilon(\omega))=\frac{\left(\epsilon_{O}-\epsilon_{\infty}\right) \omega_{T O}^{2} \gamma \omega}{\left(\omega_{T O}^{2}-\omega^{2}\right)^{2}+\gamma^{2} \omega^{2}}\qquad(3)$$

The spectrum $Im(\epsilon(\omega))$ and, consequently, the absorption spectrum in this case should be of the shape of the only Lorentz contour with the maximum at the frequency $\omega_{TO}$ and with a monotonously decreasing intensity in the contour wings. Experiments give a series of light absorption and radiation bands at the frequencies $\omega<\omega_{TO}$ (and $\omega>\omega_{LO}$ ), where there are no one-phonon states of the film substance. One can see from Eq.(3) that light absorption should occur in the case when the probability of anharmonic decay of a phonon differs from zero, i.e. when $\gamma>0$. The decay of a single optical phonon in a dielectric crystal is only possible into several other phonons in one act right into $2,3,4,...$ phonons, i.e. into multiphonon states, as both the energy and the impulseshould be conserved at the phonon decay. Light absorption in the region of frequencies $\omega>\omega_{LO}$  and $\omega<\omega_{TO}$ is, apparently, determined by the density of multiphonon states as the introduction of the decay probability (nonzero) of the one-phonon state $\gamma$ implies in a nonexplicit form the finite density of multiphonon states.

As follows from the above results, light absorption and thermostimulated radiation by a crys- talline film (a slab) in the region of the frequencies $\omega<\omega_{TO}$ and $\omega_{LO}<\omega<\omega_{e x}$ is primarily determined by interference polaritons. The notions that a photon incident onto a crystal generates a phonon into it contradict the whole set of the results being discussed, though optical properties of crystals and films are well described by means of the macroscopic dielectric function determined by phonons (excitons) of the bulk crystal.

### 4. RESONANCE BETWEEN INTERFERENCE MODES AND IMPURITY VIBRATIONS IN SLABS

Imperfections in the crystalline structure can change the density of phonon states in the region of the frequencies $\omega < \omega_{TO}$ and $\omega > \omega_{LO}$. At low concentrations of defects (impurity atoms) the density of phonon states and the dielectric function of a crystal change only slightly. However, the introduction into a crystalline film of low concentration of impurity atoms which form local vibrations, leads to a very great change in its optical properties. This variation manifests itself most vividly at the interaction of local vibrations of impurity atoms in a crystalline lattice with IMs; this interaction was observed experimentally in optical reflection-absorption spectra of films of solid solutions $Cd_{x}Zn_{1-x}Te$ $^{8}$ and $CdS_{1-x}Se_{x}$ $^{9}$. The maximum increase in absorption (up to $10^{5}$ times) takes place at the resonance of a local vibration with a IM with $m = 1$ and it decreases with increasing $m$. At very large $m$ in a thick (> 1mm) crystal slab this resonance was not observed. Figure 4a and 4b show the calculated p-polarized absorption spectra of pure $ZnTe$ films on aluminium substrates and of a $Cd_{0.05}Zn_{0.95}Te$ films of the same thicknesses, angles of light incidence and substrates. The calculated spectra are in a very good agreement with the experimental results. Spatial "quantization" by the slab boundaries of the lower branch of a bulk polariton in a bulk crystal with its splitting into IMs and wave-guide modes (WGMs) results in pumping of polaritons density from one region of $\omega - q$ states to another, i.e. from a nonradiative bulk polariton of the matrix or from interface polaritons of the sandwich to radiative polariton branches which coincide in frequency with local (gap) vibrations of impurity atoms in the film.

Thus, seemingly reasonable separation of optical phonons in a solid solution into matrix phonons and local vibrations of impurities for bulk crystals becomes unreasonable in films as because of the effects being discussed the gap vibrations are no more local but resonant.

In quantum electrodynamics a concept "cavity modes" $^{15,16}$ is widely used and their interaction with atoms in a cavity is discussed. IMs and WGMs considered here are good analogs of these cavity electromagnetic modes. Upon a transition from macroscopic ( multimode ) cavities to mi- croscopic ( one-mode ) cavities or microcavities there appears a set of new physical phenomena partially discussed in $^{15}$. Partial enhancement of stimulated radiation in microcavities and suppres- sion of spontaneous radiation in them $^{16,17}$ have, most probably, the same physical nature as the abovementioned resonance enhancement of light absorption by impurity vibrations in films at the frequencies of the first IMs. Such films with the only are macroscopic analogs of a microcavity.

### 5. LUMINESCENCE DUE TO INTERFERENCE MODES

Consider now how radiative polaritons (IMs) of a sandwich behave in luminescence and Raman scattering spectra. The spectra of secondary emission (luminescence and RS) of sandwiches were excited by different lines of a continuous argon laser (but at $\hbar\omega_{L} < E_{g}$) at different incidence angles of the laser beam onto the sample at $T = 300K$ and they were observed at different angles of light emission (or scattering). Figure 5 shows the spectra of secondary emission of one of ZnSe films $(d = 1.25\mu m)$ on an aluminium substrate at two exciting laser wavelengths 488 and 514.5 nm, and p-polarized absorption spectrum of this sandwich. Along with the bands usually observed in the spectra of spontaneous RS of ZnSe films at the frequencies of TO and LO phonons, bands were also observed in the spectral region of longer waves. Analysis of such spectra shows that we observe in our samples the spectra of luminescence by interference polaritons.

In addition, it turns out that the intensity of spontaneous RS by phonons increases when the wavelength of exciting or scattered light coincides with the maxima of light absorption at IM frequencies. This is an indication of the existence of Resonance RS by phonons due to IMs $^{7,18}$.

## 6. CONCLUSIONS

The analysis of a large number of experimental data shows that in crystals of finite sizes light absorption occurs not in one step (a photon creates a phonon) but a photon first transforms into an intermediate state of the electromagnetic field in a crystal, i.e. into a radiative polariton of the structure "vacuum - slab - metal substrate" and this polariton interacts with the whole totality of the crystal states and turns into a phonon or a multiphonon excitation. Light absorption in this case is determined by the probabilities of radiative and anharmonic decays of the polariton. A polariton in a multilayer thin film structure (sandwich) is a mixed state (existence) of different dipole-active excitations of each substance in the structure.

Absorption, thermostimulated radiation and luminescence at IM frequencies, a sharp increase in light absorption at the frequencies of local vibrations of impurities at their coincidence with the IM frequency, increasing intensity of spontaneous RS spectra at the coincidence of the exciting and (or) scattered light with the IM frequency - all these phenomena clearly observed in the spectra indicate that IMs are real states of the electromagnetic field in thin-film structures which arise as a result of size quantization of the electromagnetic field of the polaritons of the structure and they are good analogs of the cavity modes considered in quantum electrodynamics.
