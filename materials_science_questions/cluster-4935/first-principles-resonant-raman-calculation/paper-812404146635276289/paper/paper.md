# Physical properties of few electron mesoscopic rings: Persistent currents, optical absorption and Raman scattering

L. Wendler*, V.M. Fomin, A.V. Chaplik, A.O. Govorov

Fachbereich Physik, Martin-Luther-Universität Halle, Friedemann-Bach-Platz 6, D-06108 Halle, Germany

## Abstract
Two interacting electrons confined in a mesoscopic ring form a Wigner molecule due to the interplay between the Coulomb repulsion and the radial confinement. On the basis of the energy band structure, which results in the occurrence of a persistent current in the ring, the optical absorption and the differential cross section of resonant inelastic light scattering is calculated. The section rules are derived and discussed in dependence on the polarization of the incident light.

Keywords: Mesoscopic effects; Persistent currents; Optical absorption; Raman scattering

In this paper we calculate the persistent current, the optical absorption and the resonant inelastic light scat- tering of an ensemble of finite-width mesoscopic rings with two interacting electrons with spin in the presence of an Aharonov-Bohm magnetic flux. We consider two electrons $(\mu=1,2)$ with the effective conduction band-edge mass $m_{e}$ in the plane $z=0$, confined in a mesoscopic ring by the potential $V_{C}(x_{\mu})$ in the $x-y$ plane. This potential is taken as zero in the region $R-W / 2<r_{\mu}<R+W / 2$ and infinite otherwise. It is assumed that a static magnetic field $B_{0}=(0,0, B_{0})$, which is restricted to the area of a circle with radius $R_{AB}<R-W / 2$, threads the hole of the ring with the magnetic flux $\Phi=B_{0} \pi R_{AB}^{2}$ [Aharonov-Bohm (AB)] geometry. This system is considered in the presence of an external electromagnetic field, described by the vector potential $A_{1}(x, t)$ and the scalar potential $\phi_{1}(x, t)$. The unperturbed (equilibrium) Hamiltonian in the absence of impurities is given by

$$
H_{0}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}\left\{\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m_{\mathrm{e}}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r_{\mu}^{2}}+\frac{1}{r_{\mu}} \frac{\partial}{\partial r_{\mu}}\right)+V_{\mathrm{C}}\left(r_{\mu}\right)\right]\right\}
$$

$$
\begin{gathered}
-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 m_{\mathrm{c}}}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}^{2}}\right)\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial \gamma^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial \Theta}-2 \mathrm{i} \frac{\Phi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\right]\right. \\
\left.+\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{r_{2}^{2}}\right) \frac{\partial}{\partial \gamma}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial \Theta}-2 \mathrm{i} \frac{\Phi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)\right\} \\
+\frac{e^{2}}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{s}} \sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2 r_{1} r_{2} \cos \gamma}},
\end{gathered}
$$

where $\Phi_{0}=h / e$ is the flux quantum, $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the permit tivity in vacuum and $\varepsilon_{s}$ is the static dielectric constant of the host semiconductor.

For narrow-width rings, i.e. if $W \ll R$, the radial motion is much faster than the angular motions. Thus, the radial motion is adiabatically decoupled from the angular motions with the result [1-5]

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Psi\left(r_{1}, r_{2} ; \gamma, \Theta ; z_{1}, z_{2}\right) \\
& \quad=\sum_{P, K_{1}, K_{2}} \Xi_{K_{1}, K_{2}}^{P}\left(r_{1}, r_{2} ; \gamma, \Theta\right) \psi_{P, K_{1}, K_{2}}(\gamma, \Theta) \varphi\left(z_{1}, z_{2}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\left|\varphi\left(z_{1}, z_{2}\right)\right|^{2}=\delta\left(z_{1}\right) \delta\left(z_{2}\right),
$$

for the orbital part of the *two-electron wave function*.
In the framework of a second adiabatic approximation the relative angular motion is separated from the angular motion of the two-electron system in the mesoscopic ring. Then, we have

$$
\psi_{P, K_{1}, K_{2}}(\gamma, \Theta)=\sum_{p} \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \Phi_{j}^{\mathrm{p}}(\gamma) Q_{v_{\cdot}}(\Theta),\qquad(4)
$$

where the wave function of the relative angular motion is a solution of the equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left\{-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{R^{2} m_{\mathrm{e}}} \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial \gamma^{2}}+\frac{e^{2}}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{s}} R \sqrt{2(1-\cos \gamma)}}-\mathscr{E}_{j}^{\text {r.a.p }}\right\} \\
& \quad × \Phi_{j}^{\mathrm{p}}(\gamma)=0.
\end{aligned}\qquad(5)
$$

Here, p describes the possible symmetry types of $\Phi_{j}^{\mathrm{p}}(\gamma)$ and $\Upsilon$ stands for $\{P, K_{1}, K_{2}, p, j\}$. In view of the $2\pi$-periodicity of the mean Coulomb potential as a function of $\gamma$, the wave function of the relative angular motion has the form $\Phi_{j}^{\mathrm{p}}(\gamma)=\exp(\mathrm{i}p\gamma)u_{j}^{\mathrm{p}}(\gamma)$, where $u_{j}^{\mathrm{p}}(\gamma+2\pi)=u_{j}^{\mathrm{p}}(\gamma)$ and $-\frac{1}{2}<p\leqslant\frac{1}{2}$. If the typical Coulomb energy between the two electrons $E_{\text{Coul}}=e^{2}/(8\pi\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{s}}R)$ is much larger than the oscillation energy $\mathscr{E}_{j}^{\text{osc}}=\mathscr{E}_{j}^{\text{r.a.p}}-E_{\text{Coul}}$, the relative angular motion can be considered in the tight-binding like approximation.

The Schrödinger equation for the free rotation of the two-electron system as a whole is

$$
\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{~d} \Theta^{2}}+v_{Y}^{2}\right) Q_{v_{Y}}(\Theta)=0.\qquad(6)
$$

Thus, we finally obtain the eigenenergies $E_{\Upsilon,v_{\Upsilon}}\equiv E_{K_{1},K_{2},j,J}(\Phi,2)$ of the two-electron mesoscopic ring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{K_{1}, K_{2}, j, J}(\Phi, 2)= & \mathscr{E}_{K_{1}}+\mathscr{E}_{K_{2}}+\frac{e^{2}}{8 \pi \varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{s}} R} \\
& +\mathscr{E}_{j}^{\text {osc }}+\mathscr{E}_{J}^{\text {rot }}(\Phi),
\end{aligned}\qquad(7)
$$

where

$$
\mathscr{E}_{J}^{\mathrm{rot}}(\Phi)=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4 m_{\mathrm{e}} R^{2}}\left(J+2 \frac{\Phi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\qquad(8)
$$

is the rotational energy. These energy bands carry in the presence of a non-vanishing magnetic flux an equilibrium current, the *persistent current* [1,3,4], which follows at $T=0$ K by

$$
I_{A}^{\left(K_{1}, K_{2, j}, J\right)}(\Phi, 2)=-\mathrm{d} E_{K_{1}, K_{2, j}, J}(\Phi, 2) / \mathrm{d} \Phi.\qquad(9)
$$

If no vibrations are excited ($j=0$) the symmetry demands for the para state $|S=0,M_{S}=0\rangle$: $J=2m$ and for the three-fold degenerate ortho state $|S=1,M_{S}=0,\pm1\rangle$: $J=2m+1$, where $m=0,\pm1,\pm2,\dots$ [5].

The power absorption $\mathscr{P}_{\text{abs}}(\omega)$ by the electronic transition from $|i\rangle$ to $|f\rangle$ is $\mathscr{P}_{\text{abs}}(\omega)=\eta_{\text{QR}}\hbar\omega W_{if}$, where $\eta_{\text{QR}}$ is the number of mesoscopic rings per unit area in the $x-y$ plane and $W_{if}$ is the transition rate. Thus, we obtain for the power absorption of an ensemble of (uncoupled) mesoscopic rings per unit area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr{P}_{\mathrm{abs}}(\omega)= & \frac{2 \pi \omega}{\hbar} \eta_{\mathrm{QR}}\left|A_{1}^{(0)}\right|^{2} \sum_{f} \sum_{i} \omega_{f i}^{2} \\
& ×\left[w_{\mathrm{el}}(i)-w_{\mathrm{el}}(f)\right]|\langle f|\boldsymbol{e} \cdot \boldsymbol{d}| i\rangle|^{2} \delta\left(\omega_{f i}-\omega\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $A_{1}^{(0)}$ is the amplitude of the vector potential of the incident light with the unit polarization vector $\boldsymbol{e},w_{\text{el}}(v)$ is the probability that the two-electron system is in the state $|v\rangle$ and $\boldsymbol{d}=-e(\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{x}_{2})$.

Let us examine the *electric dipole selection rules* of a system of independent rings in the $x-y$ plane, i.e. no tunneling and Coulomb coupling between the ring is assumed. For this case, we identify the states $|i\rangle$ and $|f\rangle$ by those of the mesoscopic ring, given by $|i\rangle=|P,K_{1},K_{2}\rangle\otimes|p,j\rangle\otimes|J\rangle\otimes|S,M_{S}\rangle$ and $|f\rangle=|P',K_{1}',K_{2}'\rangle\otimes|p',j'\rangle\otimes|J'\rangle\otimes|S',M'S\rangle$. Calculating the matrix element $\langle f|\boldsymbol{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{d}|i\rangle$ we obtain the dipole selection rules $\Delta P=0,\Delta K_{1}=0,\Delta K_{2}=0,\Delta p=\pm\frac{1}{2},\Delta j=2l+1,\Delta J=\pm1,\Delta S=0$ and $\Delta M_{S}=0$, i.e. optical transitions are between the energy bands of the para state and that of the ortho state.

The *differential cross-section* for scattering of a photon (corresponding to a plane wave) from the state $\omega_{L},\boldsymbol{k}_{L},\boldsymbol{e}_{L}$ to the state $\omega_{S},\boldsymbol{k}_{S},\boldsymbol{e}_{S}$ and the concomitant transition of the two-electron system from the state $|i\rangle$ to the state $|f\rangle$ is given by [6]

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} \omega \mathrm{d} \Omega}= & \left(\frac{\varepsilon\left(\omega_{S}\right)}{\varepsilon\left(\omega_{L}\right)}\right)^{1 / 2} \frac{\omega_{S}}{\omega_{L}} r_{0}^{2} \sum_{f} \sum_{i} w_{\mathrm{el}}(i) \\
& ×|\langle f|\mathscr{M}(\boldsymbol{q})| i\rangle|^{2} \delta\left(\omega_{f i}-\omega\right),
\end{aligned}\qquad(11)
$$

where $\omega=\omega_{L}-\omega_{S}$ is the scattering frequency, $\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{k}_{L}-\boldsymbol{k}_{S}$ is the scattering wave vector, $r_{0}\equiv e^{2}/(4\pi\varepsilon_{0}m_{0}c^{2})$ is the *classical* electron radius and $\varepsilon(\omega)$ is the dielectric function of the embedding medium.

We will consider as an example the case of the resonance with the split-off valence band in the limit $\boldsymbol{q} \cdot \boldsymbol{x}_{\mu} \ll 1$. Then we write $\mathscr{M}(\boldsymbol{q})=\mathscr{M}_{0}(\boldsymbol{q})+\mathscr{M}_{1}(\boldsymbol{q})+\mathscr{M}_{2}(\boldsymbol{q})$, where

$$
\mathscr{M}_{0}(\boldsymbol{q})=D_{S O}\left[2 \boldsymbol{e}_{L} \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(\boldsymbol{e}_{L} \times \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*}\right) \cdot \boldsymbol{S}\right], \quad(12)
$$

$$
\mathscr{M}_{1}(\boldsymbol{q})=\mathrm{i} D_{S O}\left(\boldsymbol{e}_{L} \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*}\right)\left[\boldsymbol{q} \cdot\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\right)\right], \quad(13)
$$

$$
\mathscr{M}_{2}(\boldsymbol{q})=\mathrm{i} D_{S O} \sum_{\mu=1}^{2}\left(\boldsymbol{q} \cdot \boldsymbol{x}_{\mu}\right)\left[\left(\boldsymbol{e}_{L} \times \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*}\right) \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\mu}\right] \quad(14)
$$

and $\boldsymbol{S}=\hbar / 2\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2}\right)$ is the spin operator of the two electrons.

In the case of the polarized spectrum $\boldsymbol{e}_{L} \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*}=1$ and $\boldsymbol{e}_{L} \times \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*}=0$ is valid. From this we obtain that the selection rules for polarized Raman scattering spectra are identical with that for the absorption of light.

In the case of the depolarized spectrum we have $\boldsymbol{e}_{L} \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*}=1$ and $\boldsymbol{e}_{L} \times \boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*} \neq 0$. If the magnetic field does not penetrate the electron orbits, two different types of transitions are associated with the depolarized spectrum. The first one has selection rules $\Delta P=0, \Delta K_{1}=0, \Delta K_{2}=0, \Delta p= \pm \frac{1}{2}, \Delta j=$ $2 l+1, \Delta J= \pm 1, \Delta S=0$ and $\Delta M_{S}=0, \pm 1$, i.e. results in transitions within the energy bands of the ortho state. The second contribution gives the selection rules $\Delta P=0, \Delta K_{1}=0, \Delta K_{2}=0, \Delta p= \pm \frac{1}{2}, \Delta j=$ $2 l, \Delta J= \pm 1, \Delta S= \pm 1$ and $\Delta M_{S}=0, \pm 1$, i.e. transitions between the energy bands of the para and ortho state and vice versa result.

In summary, absorption and Raman scattering on the one hand and persistent currents, on the other hand, are manifestations of the same band structure of mesoscopic rings. The persistent current as a function of $\Phi$ gives a physical representation of the derivative of the energy bands with respect to $\Phi$ and is a continuous characteristic of the band structure. As distinct from that, the absorption and the Raman scattering select only some points in the $\Phi$-scale, at which the transitions are allowed according to the selection rules discussed in this paper and hence from discrete characteristics of the same band structure.
