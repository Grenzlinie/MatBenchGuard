## 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic, and 1,3-benzenedisulfonic acids

Hydrate clusters of 2-hydroxybenzenesulfonic and 1,3-benzenedisulfonic acids were calculated in terms of the density functional theory (DFT) by the B3LYP/6-31G** method. The process of water adsorption on the crystal surface of 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid dihydrate was simulated using the generalized gradient approximation (DFT/PBE) and periodic boundary conditions. For the model system $(OHC_{6}H_{4}SO_{3}^{-})\cdot H_{5}O_{2}^{+}$, the activation barriers for the proton transfer were calculated depending on the distance between the O atoms and the deviation of the proton from the O...O bond line. The presence of one $H_{2}O$ molecule per $SO_{3}H$ group is energetically most favorable for the formation of clusters of 1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid containing a stoichiometric amount of water. The simulation of the hydration of 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid dihydrate $(OHC_{6}H_{3}(SO_{3}H)_{2}\cdot 2H_{2}O + nH_{2}O$, $n=1-3)$ showed that the superstoichiometric $H_{2}O$ molecule is adsorbed on the crystal surface of this dihydrate with energy release of 0.75—0.95 eV. The position of this water molecule is less favorable in the bulk than on the surface.

**Key words**: 2-hydroxybenzenesulfonic acid, 1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid, 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid, hydrates, dihydrates, quantum chemical calculations, DFT/B3LYP/6-31G** method and DFT/PBE method with generalized gradient approximation, projector-augmented waves, proton transfer.

Investigations of the proton transfer in the solid state are of interest both from the fundamental and applied point of view. On the one hand, the proton transfer process (including its elementary events) is poorly known, and models for its description are often contradictory. On the other hand, many solid proton-conducting electrolytes, primarily, sulfonated polymers, $^{1-4}$ are widely used in the practice (fuel cells, sensors, electric double-layer capacitors, and so on).

Studies of the mechanism of the proton transfer in organic sulfonic acids are of great interest. Aromatic sulfonic acids are convenient models because they are readily crystallized, their composition can be rather easily varied by changing the number and the mutual arrangement of functional groups, and the composition of their proton hydration shell can be determined by conventional physicochemical methods (X-ray diffraction, IR and NMR spectroscopy). Therefore, a combination of experimental data with the molecular-level modeling of the properties of the proton hydration shell may allow one to describe in detail the mechanism of the proton transfer and predict characteristic features of electron-transport phenomenon in real objects.

In the crystalline state, substituted aromatic sulfonic acids most often exist as mono-, di-, tri-, and tetrahydrates (more rarely, in the form of other polyhydrates). The proton conductivity of these compounds greatly depends on atmospheric moisture. $^{5-10}$ In hydrate clusters of proton-conducting benzenesulfonic acids, protons exist in different forms. Thus, the $H_{3}O^{+}$ ion was found in the crystal structure of 4-methylbenzenesulfonic acid monohydrate, $^{11,12}$ in the monoclinic modification of 5-sulfosalicylic acid dihydrate $(C_{6}H_{3}(COOH)(OH)SO_{3}^{-}\cdot \cdot H_{3}O^{+}\cdot H_{2}O),^{13}$ and in the structure of 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid $(OHC_{6}H_{3}(SO_{3}^{-})_{2}\cdot (H_{3}O^{+})_{2})^{7}$ The $H_{5}O_{2}^{+}$ ion was found in the crystal structures of the triclinic modification of 5-sulfosalicylic acid dihydrate $(C_{6}H_{3}(COOH)(OH)SO_{3}^{-}\cdot H_{5}O_{2}^{+}),^{14,15}$ 2-sulfobenzoic acid trihydrate $(C_{6}H_{4}(COOH)SO_{3}^{-}\cdot H_{5}O_{2}^{+}\cdot H_{2}O),^{16,17}$ 2-hydroxy-4-methylbenzenesulfonic acid dihydrate $(MeC_{6}H_{3}(OH)SO_{3}^{-}\cdot H_{5}O_{2}^{+}),^{10}$ 2,4,6-trimethylbenzenesulfonic acid dihydrate $(Me_{3}C_{6}H_{2}SO_{3}^{-}\cdot H_{5}O_{2}^{+}),^{8}$ and 4-nitrobenzenesulfonic acid dihydrate $((NO_{2})C_{6}H_{4}SO_{3}^{-}\cdot \cdot H_{5}O_{2}^{+}).^{18}$ The $H_{7}O_{3}^{+}$ ion was found, for example, in the structure of 4-nitrobenzenesulfonic acid tetrahydrate $((NO_{2})C_{6}H_{4}SO_{3}^{-}\cdot H_{7}O_{3}^{+}\cdot H_{2}O).^{18}$

An analysis of the published data on the proton conductivity and the structures of different organic compounds containing the $SO_3^-$ group shows that the hydrogen bond distances $R(O...O)$ in most of crystalline proton conductors are in the range of $2.4-3.1$ $\mathring{A}$, $^{7-18}$ and the conductivity at room temperature can vary over a broad range from $1\cdot 10^{-11}$ to $1\cdot 10^{-3}$ S cm$^{-1}$ depending on atmospheric moisture or the structure of the hydrate cluster.$^{5-10,13,18}$

The aim of the present work is to model the crystal structures of hydrates of certain aromatic compounds containing sulfo groups by quantum chemical methods and to investigate changes in the cluster formation with an increase in the number of water molecules on the crystal surface. For 2-hydroxybenzenesulfonic acid $(HOC_6H_4SO_3^-)\cdot H_5O_2^+$ serving as a model system, the activation barriers for the proton transfer were calculated depending on the distance between the O atoms and the deviation of the proton from the O...O bond line. To understand the mechanism of the structure formation of sulfonic acid hydrates, we performed quantum chemical calculations of hydrate clusters of 1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid with different water content $(C_6H_4(SO_3H)_2\cdot n\ H_2O)$. The structure of proton transfer channels was studied by calculating the hydration of the crystal of 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid dihydrate $(OHC_6H_3(SO_3H)_2\cdot 2\ H_2O + n\ H_2O)$.

## Calculation procedure

The geometric parameters and thermodynamic stability of different clusters of sulfonic acids were calculated in terms of the density functional theory (DFT) with the B3LYP functional$^{19}$ and the 6-31G** basis set using the GAUSSIAN-03 program package.$^{20}$ All geometric parameters of the molecules under consideration were fully optimized. The computer simulation of the process on the crystal surface was carried out by the quantum chemical method with periodic boundary conditions. The calculations were performed at the generalized gradient approximation level (DFT/PBE), which provides high accuracy of the description of the structural and energy characteristics for systems composed of several hundreds of atoms.

The calculations were carried out with the use of the VASP program package$^{21-24}$ as the main tool and the hard projector-augmented wave (PAW) basis set$^{25}$ with the corresponding pseudopotential. The energy limit $(E_c)$ determining the completeness of the basis set was taken equal to 400 eV. The electronic state was optimized using the Methfessel-Paxton technique$^{26}$ with a smearing parameter $(\sigma)$ of 0.2 eV and the energy approximation to $\sigma = 0$. The wavefunction included components of differing multiplicity, whose contributions were evaluated from the total energy minimum.

The VASP program package is intended for calculations with allowance for translational symmetry. Hence, the surface processes were modeled using substrate slabs $14$ $\mathring{A}$ thick with water molecules deposited on the surface. The slabs consisted of two layers, and each layer was composed of four 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid molecules propagated in the plane (Fig. 1). The slabs were spaced by $17$ $\mathring{A}$ to diminish the effect of weak interactions between the particles in adjacent slabs on the results of the calculations. It should be noted that the calculation schemes implemented in the VASP and GAUSSIAN program packages gave similar relative energies and geometric parameters (within 0.01 eV and $0.005-0.01$ $\mathring{A}$, respectively) for the same clusters. The energy of the interaction $(E)$ between two objects was evaluated as the difference between the total energy of the whole system composed of the first and second objects that have interacted and the total energy of the isolated first and second objects. Therefore, the negative value of $E$ indicates that the reaction of the coupling of two objects is exothermic, i.e., is energetically favorable.

## Results and Discussion

According to the X-ray diffraction data, there is a continuous hydrogen bond system in the single crystal of 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid dihydrate (Fig. 2), i.e., the protons of the sulfo groups are transferred to water molecules to form hydroxonium ions at each sulfo group, and the $H_3O^+$ ions form hydrogen bonds with the O atoms of the sulfo groups $(R(O...O)$ ~$2.4-3.0$ $\mathring{A})$.$^{7}$ As can be seen from Fig. 2, the crystal structure is made up of corrugated layers.

The modeling of the crystal structure of 4-hydroxy-1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid dihydrate showed that the calculated lattice parameters and those determined by X-ray diffraction are equal within $0.1-0.2$ $\mathring{A}$; the equilibrium distances for the chemically bonded atoms are equal within $0.01-0.02$ $\mathring{A}$; the O...O distances are in the same range $(2.4-3.0$ $\mathring{A})$ (Fig. 3). Figure 3 shows the layered structure of the calculated fragment of the crystal structure, where the arrows indicate the possible directions of the adsorption of superstoichiometric water molecules on the crystal surface (see below).

When comparing the geometric parameters of the structures of benzenesulfonic acids, we hypothesized that the relatively short distance $R(\text{O}...\text{O})$ and the $\text{O}—\text{O}—\text{H}$ angle as close to the optimal value as possible are prerequisites for good conductivity. To validate this hypothesis, we performed model calculations of the barrier for the proton transfer along the proton-conducting channel for the $\text{H}_5\text{O}_2^+$ system by the B3LYP/6-31G** method. The dependences of the proton transfer barrier on the distance $R(\text{O}...\text{O})$ and the $\text{O}—\text{O}—\text{H}$ angle are shown in Figs 4 and 5, respectively. Without allowance for the zero-point energy $(E)$, the increase in the distance $R(\text{O}...\text{O})$ from 2.5 to 2.9 Å is accompanied by an increase in the proton transfer barrier from ~0 to ~0.5 eV (see Fig. 4). The calculations with the correction for the zero-point energy $(E_{\text{ZPE}})$ resulted in the shift of this range by ~0.1 Å, which is consistent with the experimental distances $R(\text{O}...\text{O})$ in crystal hydrates of most of substituted benzenesulfonic acids (2.6—3.0 Å).

A decrease in the O—O—H angle $(116^\circ)$ by $58^\circ$ leads to an increase in the barrier for proton migration by 1.6 eV (see Fig. 5, curve 1). The smaller this deviation, the smaller the change in the barrier height. Thus, the $\sim30^\circ$ deviation of the O—O—H angle from $\sim116^\circ$ leads to an increase in the barrier for proton migration by ~0.3 eV (see Fig. 5, curve 1). The smaller increase in the barrier height was obtained in the case of $\angle$(O—O—H) $\neq$ $\angle$(H—O—H) (see Fig. 5, curve 2). The larger the deviation of the O...O bond line from the optimal position, the higher the barrier for proton migration and the larger the deviation of the proton from the O...O bond line, thus partially compensating the bond strain. Hence, the deviation of the H atom from the O...O bond axis can serve as an indication that this direction is sterically unfavorable. Therefore, the barrier for proton migration should substantially depend on the mutual orientation of the O—H and O...O bonds, and the inclusion of additional water molecules can substantially enhance proton conductivity.

Consequently, a short O...O distance (2.4—2.9 Å) is a necessary but not sufficient condition for the existence of proton conductivity. In addition to the short O...O distance, the sterically favorable and nearly optimal O...O bond direction and the nearly zero angle between the O...O and O—H bonds with the participation of the migrating proton are necessary for good proton conductivity to occur.

2-Hydroxybenzenesulfonic acid. Figure 6 shows the structures corresponding to extreme points on the potential energy surface for the minimum energy paths for proton migration *via* the relay mechanism in the $\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ fragment through water molecules (**1a**—**c**) or $\text{OH}$ groups (**2a**—**c**). As opposed to the channel $\mathbf{1a} \rightarrow \mathbf{1b} \rightarrow \mathbf{1c}$, where (due to mobility of a water molecule) the migrating proton deviates from the O...O bond direction by no more than $5^\circ$, in the case of the path $\mathbf{2a} \rightarrow \mathbf{2b} \rightarrow \mathbf{2c}$ (due to lower mobility of $\text{OH}$ groups) the proton deviates from the O...O bond direction by $22^\circ$ ($\text{O(2)}—\text{O(1)}—\text{S}$, $84^\circ$ instead of $106^\circ$) in structure **2a** and by $17^\circ$ (the $\text{O(2)}—\text{O(1)}—\text{C}$ angle is $90^\circ$ instead of $107^\circ$) in structure **2c**. In structures **1a**—**c** (see Fig. 6), the bond angle strain is virtually absent in the regions of water molecules (due to their mobility), but there is a bond angle strain in the regions of $\text{OH}$ groups in structures **2a**—**c** (see Fig. 6). As a result, the corresponding transitions through the channel $\mathbf{1a} \rightarrow \mathbf{1b}$ take place more easily than through the path $\mathbf{2a} \rightarrow \mathbf{2b}$ (in the former case, the barrier is 0.24 eV; in the latter case, 0.44 eV). The barriers for these transitions are comparable with those reported for similar model systems (see Fig. 5).

1,3-Benzenedisulfonic acid. Let us consider the cluster formation and healing of water defects for 1,3-benzenedisulfonic acid hydrates $((1,3\text{-BDSA} \cdot 2\ \text{H}_2\text{O})_n \pm m\ \text{H}_2\text{O}$, $n=3—5$, $m=1—3$). Their calculated geometric characteristics are given in Fig. 7. As can be seen from Table 1, cluster $\mathbf{5}\ ((1,3\text{-BDSA} \cdot 2\ \text{H}_2\text{O})_4$, in which one of the $\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ groups is not bound to water molecules, is energetically less favorable than the analogous cluster **6**, in which each $\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ group is bound to at least one water molecule. Hereinafter, one water molecule per $\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ group involved in hydrogen bonding in crystal hydrates will be called stoichiometric.

The specific energies corrected for the zero-point vibrational energy are 0.10—0.15 eV lower than those calculated without this correction, the tendencies of changes in these energies being the same in both cases (see Table 1). The specific energy correlates with the number of water molecules per $\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ group. This confirms the fact that the cluster is formed due to the presence of water molecules. Thus, the smaller the number of water molecules, the lower the specific energy of the cluster formation (see Table 1). An increase in $L$ (the ratio of the number of water molecules to the number of $\text{SO}_3^-$ groups) from 0.7 to 1.125 leads to an increase in the specific stability of the cluster from 1.77 to 2.44 eV. For the stoichiometric clusters $(1,3\text{-BDSA} \cdot 2\ \text{H}_2\text{O})_n$ ($n=3$ (**3**) and 4 (**6**)), the specific energy is 2.08 and 2.26 eV, respectively (consequently, 1.04—1.13 eV per $\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ group).

0.94 eV (11, n = 1), 0.89 eV (15, n = 2), and 0.85 eV (17,
n = 3) (Figs 8 and 9, structures 10—17). The positions of
water molecules on the surface can differ in the energy by
~0.1—0.2 eV (see Fig. 8, structures 11—13), the energies
of the adsorption being higher than the energy given in Eq. 2
(see Table 2, -0.61 eV) for the adsorption of superstoi-
chiometric water on the surface of cluster 6. A water mol-
ecule in the bulk located 1.5 Å below the crystal surface is
0.5 eV higher on the energy scale compared to cluster 11,
i.e., it should tend to emerge to the surface. Consequently,
the position of a superstoichiometric water molecule on
the surface of polycrystals of 4-OH-1,3-BDSA·2 H₂O is
energetically more favorable than that in the bulk. How-
ever, once appearing in the bulk, the molecule cannot
emerge to the crystal surface due to the potential barrier.

Figures 8 and 9 show the calculated surface part of the
crystal of 4-OH-1,3-BDSA·2 H₂O. It can be seen that
there are hydrogen bonds between the SO₃H and OH
groups on the crystal surface, and along these bonds the
proton conductivity limited by the crystal stoichiometry
can occur. The addition from one to three superstoichio-
metric water molecules (per surface SO₃H group) onto
the surface leads to the formation of shorter O...O bonds
and straight O...H—O angles on the surface, which results
in a sharp decrease in the barrier for proton conductivity
(see Figs 8 and 9, structures 11, 15, and 17). A slight
elongation of hydrogen bonds or a change of surface SO₃H
groups involved in these bonds for other adjacent SO₃H
groups leads to small changes in the energy of the adsorp-
tion of water molecules (see Figs 8 and 9, structures 11—13
and 14—17). As can be seen from a comparison of struc-
tures 14 and 15, the formation of a uniform monolayer
coating on the crystal surface by superstoichiometric wa-
ter molecules and only then the formation of the second
layer is favorable from the energy standpoint.

Since hydrogen bridges on the surface formed by super-
stoichiometric water molecules are geometrically more
mobile and better satisfy the necessary and sufficient con-
ditions for proton conductivity (modeled above) compared
to stoichiometric water molecules, it can be hypothesized
that the conductivity in sulfonic acids occurs primarily on
the crystal surface. The results of the present work corre-
late with the theoretical and experimental data published
