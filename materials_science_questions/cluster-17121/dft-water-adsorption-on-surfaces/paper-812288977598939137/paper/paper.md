# Experimental and QM/MM investigation of the hydrated silica surface reactivity

Maurizio Casarin $^{a,*}$, Daniele Falcomer $^{a}$, Antonella Glisenti $^{a}$, Marta Maria Natile $^{a}$, Fabrizia Poli $^{a}$, Andrea Vittadini $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Dipartimento di Scienze Chimiche, Universitá di Padova, Padova, Italy
$^{b}$ Istituto di Scienze e Tecnologie Molecolari C.N.R. Padova, Italy

Received 28 January 2005; in final form 17 February 2005
Available online 16 March 2005

## Abstract
The interaction of a series of molecular probes $(O_{3}, H_{2}S, CO, NH_{3}, (CH_{3})_{2}CO, C_{5}H_{5}N$ and $CH_{3}CN)$ with the hydrated $SiO_{2}$ surface has been investigated by QM/MM calculations and diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform spectroscopy. Theoretical estimates of O-H stretchings are shown to be improved by a new treatment of the molecular mechanics part of the model. Such a result is encouraging for the application of the adopted approach to other systems.

## 1. Introduction
Bulk and surface properties of silica $(SiO_{2})$ represent a currently active field not only for applied but also for fundamental research [1-22]. One of the most interesting issues is represented by the interaction between surface OH groups and adsorbates. In this regard, Tsyganenko et al. [4,5] pointed out that hydrated $SiO_{2}$ surface might be used, in place of conventional chemical methods, to gauge the acid-base properties of adsorbed species. In fact, these are able to strongly influence the vibrational frequencies of surface O-H groups.

It is now unanimously accepted that a successful interpretation of surface phenomena is greatly helped by theoretical calculations [23]. To this end, three approaches can be in principle used: clusters, periodic models and quantum mechanical/molecular mechanics (QM/MM) hybrid models [24,25]. However, at variance to the case of $SiO_{2}$ bulk [26], only cluster and periodic models have been so far employed [8-20] to investigate $SiO_{2}$ surface properties. Here, we use for the first time a QM/MM approach to study the interaction of the hydrated $SiO_{2}$ surface with a series of molecular probes. The following molecules have been considered: $H_{2}S$, $O_{3}$, CO, $NH_{3}$, acetone - $(CH_{3})_{2}CO$, pyridine - $C_{5}H_{5}N$ - and acetonitrile - $CH_{3}CN$. Calculations were also complemented with new diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform (DRIFT) spectroscopy measurements when literature experimental data were controversial $((CH_{3})_{2}CO$ and $CH_{3}CN)$.

## 2. Computational details, models and experimental
$SiO_{2}$ can be modeled by referring to one of its crystalline structures. In fact, it has been shown that the local properties of $SiO_{2}$ bulk [21,22] are well described by $\alpha$-quartz. In contrast to that, there is no unanimously accepted model for the hydrated $SiO_{2}$ surface. Examples of employed models are: $SiH_{3}OH$ and other very small silanol clusters [7-12]; $\beta$-cristobalite [13-15]; zeolites such as Edingtonite [16-18]; $\alpha$-quartz [19,20]. Each of

these models provides a good description of the $SiO_2$ hydrated surface, but none of them is able to describe the properties of all the hydroxyl groups possibly present on the surface. As a matter of fact, three surface hydroxyl groups are present on untreated $SiO_2$: the isolated, the geminal and the vicinal ones, $\alpha$-quartz is a good model for geminal and vicinal OH groups, $\beta$-cristobalite for geminal and isolated OH groups, while the Edingtonite reproduces satisfactorily the properties of isolated OH groups. At RT, all these groups are present on the $SiO_2$ surface, while after high temperature treatment only isolated hydroxyl groups in concentration of $\sim 2$ $OH/nm^2$ are revealed [17].

In this contribution we have limited our interest to isolated OH surface groups. For our calculations, we have used as a model the neutral cluster $(OH)Si_{25}O_{66}H_{33}$ (hereafter A, see Fig. 1) derived from the (1 1 1) surface of $\beta$-cristobalite. Peripheral oxygen atoms have been saturated with hydrogen atoms placed at $0.98\mathring{A}$ [21,22] along the bond directions of the extended lattice. In the QM/MM treatment, A has been divided in two regions (see Fig. 1): a inner one treated by QM including the OH site and its neighbors $[(OH)Si_7O_6$, hereafter B], and an outer region, treated by MM. B was in turn terminated with hydrogen link atoms [24] placed at $1.497\mathring{A}$, as in the optimized $SiH_4$ molecule. In the QM/MM standard approach, atomic charges associated to the MM part are usually assigned on the basis of calculations on simpler prototype systems, and are kept fixed throughout the calculations. In this work, we adopted an alternative approach: specific charges for all the MM atoms have been obtained by running a single point QM calculation on the whole cluster A. This approach allows to have a better description of the MM system at a reasonable cost. In fact, the most demanding part of QM calculations is the gradient evaluation which is avoided in a single point run. Obviously, our approach implies that the MM size cannot exceed a few hundred of atoms, which is adequate in the present case.

All the calculations have been carried out by running ADF 2002 [27], a package based on the density functional theory (DFT). Optimized geometries, adsorbate-substrate interaction energies, NMR chemical shift and vibrational parameters have been evaluated by employing the PBE functional [28] because it is known that it provides a satisfactory description of hydrogen bonding [29]. A triple-$\zeta$ Slater-type basis set,

Because of the minor role played by the MM part in our calculations, the sybyl force field [30] was adopted. This defines a strain energy as:

$$
U_{\mathrm{tot}}=E_{\mathrm{bond}}+E_{\mathrm{bend}}+E_{\mathrm{tors}}+E_{\mathrm{oop}}+E_{\mathrm{vdW}} \tag{1}
$$

with

$$
E_{\mathrm{bond}}=\frac{1}{2} k\left(r-r_{0}\right)^{2}, \tag{2}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{bend}}=\frac{1}{2} k_{\delta}\left(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}\right)^{2}, \tag{3}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{tors}}=\frac{1}{2} k_{\tau}[1+A \cos (n \cdot \varphi)], \tag{4}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{oop}}=\frac{1}{2} k_{\omega} D^{2}, \tag{5}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{vdW}}=D_{0}\left[\left(\frac{R_{0}}{R}\right)^{12}-2\left(\frac{R_{0}}{R}\right)^{6}\right]+\frac{q_{i} q_{j}}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0} r_{i j}}. \tag{6}
$$

A large part of the force field parameters had to be determined because they were not available in the liter- ature. We have used clusters $\mathrm{Si}(\mathrm{OH})_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Si}_{5} \mathrm{O}_{16} \mathrm{H}_{12}$ to estimate bonding and bending parameters, while the tor- sional one has been evaluated by using the cluster $(\mathrm{OH})_{3} \mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{Si}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}$ (see Table 1). Force constants of the $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{O}$ and $\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{H}$ bonds have been calculated in the harmonic approximation [31]. Standard van der Waals parameters were adopted.

Three different sets of QM/MM calculations have been run to model the interaction of probe molecules (PM) with the hydrated $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ surface. In the first one, the electrostatic coupling between the QM and MM parts has been neglected; i.e., we have only accounted for the steric restrictions. The corresponding model clus- ter is hereafter labeled $\mathrm{PM}-\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{st}}$. In the second set of cal- culations, the electrostatic coupling between the QM and MM parts has been considered and the correspond- ing model cluster is hereafter labeled $\mathrm{PM}-\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{el}}$. In this set of numerical experiments, charges associated to different species have been chosen by performing a single point calculation on both the cluster $\mathrm{A}$ and the adsorbate free molecule. In the third set of QM/MM calculations the following steps have been considered: (i) the $\mathrm{PM}-\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{el}}$ optimized geometry is assumed; (ii) a single point QM calculation for the whole $\mathrm{PM}-\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{el}}$ cluster is run; (iii) Hirshfeld charges obtained from step (ii) are used in a new QM/MM optimization.

As already pointed out, new experimental measure- ments have been carried out for $(\mathrm{CH}_{3})_{2} \mathrm{CO}$ and $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{CN}$. Commercial fumed silica (Sigma-Aldrich $99.8\%$) with a surface area of $200\ \mathrm{m}^{2}/\mathrm{g}$ were preliminary treated at $1273\ \mathrm{K}$ for $5\ \mathrm{h}$ to remove adsorbed $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ and $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$. IR spectra have been collected in a Bruker TENSOR27 spectrometer (accumulating 128 scans at a resolution of $4\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$) and displayed in the Kubelka-Munk units [32,33]. Before each experiment, ca. $10\ \mathrm{mg}$ of the sample were loaded in the sample cup of a Low Temperature Reaction Chamber (CHC) installed in the Praying Matis $^{\mathrm{TM}}$ accessory for diffuse reflection spectroscopy (Harrick Scientific Corporation) and fitted with ZnSe windows. The powder sample was kept in nitrogen flow to eliminate water traces until a stable IR spectrum was obtained (ca. $1\ \mathrm{h}$). The background spectrum of the clean surface was measured for spectra correction. Silica samples were then exposed to the reactive species at a flow rate of $60\ \mathrm{cm}^{3}/\mathrm{min}$ by filling the CHC with the ace- tonitrile or the acetone vapors flowing nitrogen through a bubbler containing the liquid. $(\mathrm{CH}_{3})_{2} \mathrm{CO}$ and $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{CN}$ employed for chemisorption were taken from commer- cial source (Sigma-Aldrich $99.8\%$) and used without further purification.

### 3.1.1. Theoretical outcomes
The Clean Surface. Calculations pertaining to the clean hydrated surface have been carried out by optimiz- ing the coordinates of the OH group as well as those of its nearest neighbors (one Si and three O atoms). The computed OH stretching frequency ($v_{OH}$) is 3759.5 and 3760.5 cm⁻¹ for $A_{st}$ and $A_{el}$, respectively; while the computed surface hydrogen NMR chemical shift ($\delta$) amounts to 1.2 ppm for both systems. The ($v_{OH}$) value agrees very well with DRIFT data (see Figs. 2 and 3) as well as with those reported in literature [4-7]. At var- iance to that, the computed $\delta$ is in semiquantitative agreement with the experiment [34] and previous molec- ular cluster calculations [20].

The analysis of data reported in Table 2 points out that: (i) the pure MM coupling (PM–$\text{A}_{\text{st}}$) yields to $v_{\text{OH}}$ values in good agreement with experiment only for those cases characterized by an adsorbate–substrate interaction relatively strong ($\Delta H \sim 10$ kcal/mol), viz. $\text{NH}_3$, $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CO}$, $\text{C}_5\text{H}_5\text{N}$ and $\text{CH}_3\text{CN}$; (ii) the inclusion of not iterated atomic charges (PM–$\text{A}_{\text{el}}$) provides $v_{\text{OH}}$ values in good agreement with experiment only for those cases ($\text{O}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{CO}$) characterized by an adsorbate–substrate interaction relatively weak ($\Delta H \sim 5$–6 kcal/mol), i.e., when long-range effects play a significant role; (iii) the 'update' of atomic charges (PM–$\text{A}_{\text{ch}}$) significantly improves the agreement between $v_{\text{OH}}$ and experiment independently of the adsorbate–substrate interaction strength. Slight differences between theoretical and experimental $v_{\text{OH}}$ values for $\text{CO}$ and $\text{O}_3$ may have different origins and, besides the flatness of the corresponding potential energy surfaces which makes difficult the determination of the most stable configuration, both the computational parameters we used (PBE functional, adopted basis set, selected convergence optimization criteria) and the adopted crystalline model could play some role.

As a final consideration, few words have to be spent in relation to the adsorption of acetonitrile. It is well known [35] that the interaction of $\text{CH}_3\text{CN}$ with Lewis acid sites produces a blue shift of the C–N stretching frequency ($v_{\text{OH}}$) with respect to the free molecule ($v_{\text{CN}} = 2268$ $\text{cm}^{-1}$). According to that, the adsorption of acetonitrile on our fumed silica samples produces a $\Delta v_{\text{CN}} = +31$ $\text{cm}^{-1}$ (see the inset in Fig. 3). This value is in quantitative agreement with the one theoretically estimated ($+30$ $\text{cm}^{-1}$), thus providing further support to the adopted model. Incidentally, the computed $v_{\text{CN}}$ for the free and adsorbed $\text{CH}_3\text{CN}$ amounts to 2268 and 2298 $\text{cm}^{-1}$, respectively.

## 4. Conclusion

In this work, we have applied a new QM/MM approach to investigate the hydrated $\text{SiO}_2$ surface. Theoretical results indicate that this method reproduces quantitatively vibrational properties of the surface OH groups interacting with different chemical probes. The update of atomic charges of the MM part is determinant to obtain a good agreement between theory and experiment.
