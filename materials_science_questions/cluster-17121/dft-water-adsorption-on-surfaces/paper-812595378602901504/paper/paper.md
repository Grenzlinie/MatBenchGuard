## ABSTRACT
In the present study, the ZnO thin film was deposited on the surface acoustic wave (SAW) chip for sensitive humidity sensors by using radio frequency magnetron sputtering method. The microstructure of the ZnO thin film was characterized by using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), atomic force microscopy (AFM) and X-ray diffraction (XRD). The ZnO film exhibited rough morphology with the average thickness of about 0.3 µm. The humidity-sensing properties of the sensors were examined by changing the relative humidity (RH) within the range of 20%-60% RH at ambient temperature. The resonant frequency shifts increased gradually with the rising of RH levels. It was found that the ZnO-SAW sensors showed high sensitivity, mainly owing to the formation of the liquid water layer and superposition effect of water molecules (WMs). Moreover, the density functional theory (DFT) calculation revealed that the adsorption and desorption of WMs on the ZnO film were physical processes.

## 1. Introduction
Humidity measurement and control play an important role in many industries, like semiconductor manufacturing, biochemical factories, medical pharmaceutical and agricultural production [1-3]. The rapidly growing requirement for humidity detection has attracted many researchers to develop high-performance humidity sensors. A variety of sensing materials, such as silicon nanocrystals [4,5] metal oxides [6-8] and aerogels [9] have been investigated for enhancing the humidity-sensing properties. Among these materials, the metal oxide semiconductors (MOS) have been successfully applied in gas sensors with their characteristics of low cost, outstanding performance and simple operation [10-12].

As a kind of metal oxide material, ZnO has aroused many research interests due to its unique electronic, optical and piezoelectric properties. In recent years, ZnO has been deeply studied as a humidity sensing material. Duan et al. [13] reported that single-walled ZnO nanotubes used to detect water molecule (WM) concentrations could not be easily affected by other gas molecules existed in the environment. The work of Soyoun and Taeksoo [14] indicated that ZnO nanorod based humidity sensors developed with Wheatstone bride (WB) circuit had superior humidity sensing properties in the terms of response and recovery time and thermal stability. In the work of Zhang et al. [15], the sensitivity of ZnO humidity sensors was significantly improved by doping Au nanoparticles (AuNPs), because AuNPs enhanced the concentration of oxygen vacancy defects on the ZnO surface. Jacobs et al. [16] reported that at low temperature, the adsorption behavior of WMs on the ZnO surface by hydrogen bonding belongs to the physical adsorption type. At a higher temperature, WMs are adsorbed on the ZnO surface through chemical reaction. Previous studies mainly focused on improving the humidity-sensing properties of ZnO sensors, little attention was paid to investigating the relations of WM(s) adsorption mechanisms and the sensing properties of ZnO film sensors. Therefore, systematically investigating the response of ZnO sensors to different relative humidity levels by revealing its sensing mechanisms is important and worthwhile.

In the present study, the ZnO film was deposited on the surface acoustic wave (SAW) chip by radio frequency (RF) magnetron sputtering. The humidity-sensing properties of the ZnO-SAW sensors were studied with different relative humidity levels. Moreover, the adsorption mechanism of WMs on ZnO film surface was revealed by using the density functional theory (DFT) and related to the sensing properties. It appears that the ZnO-SAW sensor shows superior humidity monitoring properties with fast response and recovery.

## 2. Experimental procedures

ZnO film was deposited on the SAW chip by RF magnetron sputtering using a high-purity ZnO target material under the Ar gas atmosphere. The vacuum degree of 0.5 Pa and the sputtering power of 100 W were selected in this study. The microstructure of the as-deposited ZnO film was characterized by field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, JSM-7001F, JEOL, Japan) and atomic force microscopy (AFM, AFM5500M). Phase constitutions of the thin film were analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, X' pert pro, PANalytical B.V., Almelo, the Netherlands) using Cu K-alpha radiation.

The humidity-sensing properties of the ZnO-SAW sensor were

evaluated by the self-built gas detection platform (shown in Fig. 1(a)). The humidity generator (model HSDG-A from HuaXiang Star Environ- mental Technology Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China) was used to precisely control the humidity level by tuning the flow ratio of $N_2$ and $H_2O$ gases. Moreover, the mass flow meter and ball valve were added to the con- trolling system to adjust the flow of the mixed $N_2/H_2O$ gas. In the present study, the spectrum analysis method is used to test the sensing properties of the as-fabricated sensors. The resonant frequency shifts of the sensors were collected from the signal generator (model E4436B, Agilent Technologies Inc., California, America) and spectrum analyzer (model 4396B, Agilent Technologies Inc., California, America). Before the test, nitrogen gas was pumped through the testing box with the flow rate of 0.3 L/min to acquire a stable resonant frequency of the SAW chip. Then, the ZnO film sensor was exposed to 20%, 40% and 60% relative humidity levels to test its sensitivity. In the humidity-sensing property tests, the flow rate of the gas with various RH levels was set at 0.3 L/min. The response time of the sensors was set from 1 to 5 min and the frequency curve was recorded once a minute. Before the next circle of testing, 0.3 L/min nitrogen gas was used to recover the resonant frequency of the ZnO-SAW sensor to its initial state.

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. Microstructure and sensing performance of the ZnO-SAW sensor

As shown in Fig. 1(b), the ZnO film uniformly covered the entire surface of the substrate and it exhibited granular morphology. The thickness of the ZnO film prepared by 100 W magnetron sputtering is about 0.3 μm (see Fig. 1(c)). The AFM image (see Fig. 1(d)) shows that the ZnO film has an uneven surface. It is beneficial for the adsorption of the water molecules on the film surface and accelerates gas sensing response of the material. In addition, the root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of the ZnO film is about 1.35 nm. The XRD results of the as- deposited film are shown in Fig. 1(e). The sharp diffraction peaks of ZnO imply that the crystalline ZnO has been formed in the film. No diffraction peak of other impurity is observed, suggesting high-purity ZnO film was obtained.

The operating time is an important attribute to metal oxide sensors to obtain their optimal sensing performance. Therefore, the ZnO-SAW sensor was first exposed to 20-60% RH from 1 to 5 min to acquire the optimal operating time. The obtained resonant frequency shifts of the sensors were shown in Fig. 2. In the first 3 min, the frequency shifts increased gradually for all RH levels. However, the increasing rate for the 60% RH is obviously higher than that of 20% and 40% RH. When the response time exceeded about 3 min, the shifted frequency value of the ZnO-SAW chip increased very little with further increasing the testing time for all RH levels. It denotes that the adsorption of $H_2O$ molecules on the sensor surface is saturated. Therefore, in order to reveal the best sensing performance of the ZnO sensor, the response time of the humidity-sensing tests is set at 3 min in following part of this study.

In order to evaluate the frequency response characteristics of the ZnO-SAW sensor to different RH levels, the sensors were exposed to 20% RH, 40% RH and 60% RH for 3 min. The obtained resonant fre- quency response is shown in Fig. 3. The initial resonant frequency is 154.781129 MHz, and the resonant frequency response to 20% RH, 40% RH, and 60% RH are 154.781129 MHz, 154.777129 MHz, and 154.773004 MHz, respectively. The resonant frequency shift decreases gradually with the increasing of RH. Compared with the initial resonant frequency, 20% RH, 40% RH and 60% RH resulted in the decreased resonant frequency values of 2.125 kHz, 4 kHz and 8.125 kHz, re- spectively. It indicates that the ZnO sensor shows higher sensitivity to 60% RH, and the reason will be discussed later. By blowing 0.3 L/min nitrogen gas for 3 min, the resonant frequency of the ZnO-SAW sensor restored to the initial state. It manifests that the sensing process is re- versible and the ZnO-SAW sensor can be easily recovered.

### 3.2. The adsorption mechanisms of $H_2O$ molecules on ZnO surface

The adsorption mechanisms of 1-10 water molecules (WMs) on the ZnO film were investigated by using unrestricted spin-polarized DFT calculations, applying the Dmol3 package [17]. The PBE generalized gradient approximation was employed to the exchange-correlation functional, which has been widely adopted to investigate the molecule adsorption processes on ZnO [18-20]. DFT semi-core pseudopotential (DSSP) core treatment was employed with double numerical basis set plus polarization functions (DNP) [21]. The real-space global orbital cutoff radius was set to be 6.0 Å. The self-consistent field computationswere carried out based on a convergence criterion of $10^{-6}$ Hartree (Ha)on the total energy. Moreover, a convergence criterion of 0.002 Ha/Å for the maximum force and 0.005 Å for the maximum displacement of each atom were chosen in the structural optimization process. The ZnO surface was modeled by [5 × 3] super-cell geometry, and a 10 Å va- cuum region was added to the cell. The total volume of the super-cell was $9.75 × 16.88 × 13.21 Å^3$. In order to make the model used in the calculation agree well with the experiment and save the calculating time, a two-layer ZnO model with 42 Zn atoms and 42 O atoms was established. The bottom ZnO layer was fixed at the ideal location for simulating a bulk environment, while the other layer was arranged in relaxation. After the energy minimization calculation for the as-estab- lished model, the length of Zn-O bond in the same layer is obtained to be 1.913 Å, and the length of Zn-O bond between layers is 2.314 Å. The optimized structure of the ZnO can be found in our previous study [22].

Fig. 4 shows the model of 1, 4 and 10 WMs adsorbed on the ZnO surface. According to our calculating results, when one water molecule is adsorbed on the ZnO surface (see Fig. 4(a)), the water oxygen atom has a binding distance of 2.227 Å to the nearest Zn atom. And the bonding distance of water H atom to the O atom of ZnO is 2.993 Å. Based on the empirical bond-length and bond-strength relationship [23], the Zn-O bond is expected to be stronger than the H-O (of ZnO) bond. It indicates that the water molecule is prone to be adsorbed by Zn atoms forming the Zn-O covalent bond. The total binding energy of one water molecule on ZnO surface is calculated to be 0.147 eV, while the charge transfer is 0.007 e. The simulation results suggest that the binding energy is small, and there is almost no charge transfer. The calculating results of one water molecule model is basically consistent with the work of Meyer et al [24].

It is well-known that hydrogen bond plays an important role in the adsorption properties of WMs on ZnO [13,25,26]. Therefore, in order to investigate the effect of hydrogen bonding between WMs, we built the configuration of four WMs adsorbed on ZnO surface (see Fig. 4(b)). Our calculation results show that the bond length between water O atoms and Zn atoms increases from 2.2 Å to 2.4 Å, but 1.915 Å to the nearest H atom. Thus, there is a strong hydrogen bonding present between WMs. Further calculation shows that the average binding energy and charge transfer between two water molecules are 0.162 eV and 0.005 e, re- spectively. The binding energy between WMs (0.162 eV) is higher than that of WMs and ZnO (0.147 eV), which also indicates that the for- mation of hydrogen bonds between WMs is energetically preferred. When four water molecules are adsorbed on the surface of ZnO, the average binding energy (0.1475 eV) is consistent with that of one water molecule configuration.

Fig. 4(c) shows the model of 10 WMs on the surface of ZnO. When multiple water molecules are introduced into the system, the adsorption states of water molecules on ZnO surface can be divided into three types: i) WMs adsorbed on ZnO surface; ii) WMs adsorbed on the ZnO surface forming hydrogen bonds with neighboring WMs; and iii) WMs only form hydrogen bonds with adjacent WMs. For the type-1 WMs, the bonding distance between water O atom and Zn atom was calculated to be ~2 Å. Due to the effect of hydrogen bonds, the Zn-O bond length increases to ~2.5 Å for the type-2 WMs. When there are only hydrogen bonds being formed between WMs (type-3), their bonding distance is calculated to be 1.856 Å. They have reported that the dissociative adsorption is only energetically preferred when the water molecule coverage is larger than 1 monolayer. When the coverage is less than 0.5 monolayer, full molecule adsorption is energetically favorable. In our simulation results, less than 10 WMs adsorbed on ZnO surface obviously belongs to the low coverage (< 0.5 monolayer) condition. When 10 water molecules are adsorbed on ZnO surface, the overall binding energy is calculated to be 1.473 eV with the average binding energy of 0.1473 eV, which is nearly the same as that of the one water molecule model. It indicates that the adsorption of multiple WMs on ZnO do not change the average binding energy and distance. This is the reason the WM adsorption on ZnO belongs to physical process and WMs can be desorbed by pumping nitrogen gas in our experiments.

When there are numerous WMs in the system, hydrogen bonds are formed between water molecules, resulting in the superposition of WMs on the ZnO surface. When the concentration of water molecule increases, the superposition effect becomes greater. But the superposition of water molecules does not increase the average binding energy of the whole system, which means that the adsorption of water molecules on the ZnO surface is completely reversible. In the present work, the ratio of the relative humidity is 1:2:3 and the ratio of resonance frequency shifts is about 1:2:4. With the increasing of RH, the concentration of the water molecules increases and the superposition effect of water molecules becomes greater. Therefore, the resonance frequency of ZnO-SAW chip changes more greatly when exposed to 60% RH.

To gain further insight into the signal generation mechanisms of ZnO-SAW sensors, we explored the density of states (DOS) of ZnO before and after WM adsorption (as shown in Fig. 5). According to our simulation results, the WM adsorption did not cause significant influence on the total DOS of ZnO. After WM adsorption, the conduction band, upper valence band and the lower valence band remained in the almost same positions as pure ZnO. However, two island-like peaks appeared at the end of lower valence band and between lower and upper valence band, which was totally contributed by the adsorption of WMs. It indicates that the band gap tends to be narrower than that before the adsorption of WMs and the formation of impurity level. It is beneficial for electrons to be excited from the lower band to the conduction band. Therefore, the number of electrons existed in the conduction bond increased, which changes the conductivity properties of ZnO and generates the humidity sensing signals. Similar phenomenon was also found in the investigation of formaldehyde molecules adsorbed on the ZnO surface [22].

## 4. Conclusions

In this study, the radio frequency magnetron sputtering technology was used to prepare the ZnO-SAW sensor. The humidity-sensing properties were evaluated with different operating time and RH levels. The adsorption mechanisms of H₂O molecules on the ZnO surface were investigated by using the DFT theory. It was found that, when more than 1 WMs were absorbed on ZnO, some WMs forms hydrogen bonds with neighboring WMs, resulting in the increment of binding distance between O (of H₂O) and Zn atoms from 2.227 Å to 2.4 Å. However, the average binding energy and distance between WMs and ZnO are not obviously changed for 1, 4 and 10 WM(s) configurations. Furthermore, the adsorption of WMs belongs to physical processes and the ZnO-SAW sensor shows higher sensitivity to 60% RH due to the WM superposition effect.
