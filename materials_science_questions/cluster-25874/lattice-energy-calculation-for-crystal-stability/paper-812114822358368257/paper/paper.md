**Summary:** Two samples of syndiotactic 1,2-poly(1,3-
butadiene) with different constitutional and configurational
regularities were characterized by DSC analysis and X-ray
powder diffraction. Possible models of the crystal structure
were obtained by molecular mechanics calculations per-
formed with various sets of potential functions. X-ray powder
profiles calculated for these models were in good agreement
with experiments. The agreement between simulated and
experimental X-ray powder profiles was improved by taking
into account, in the calculated models, the presence of
configurational defects. This indicates that configurational
defects can be included in the crystal phase.

## Introduction

Syndiotactic 1,2-poly(1,3-butadiene) (sPBD12) is a ther-
moplastic elastomer of industrial interest due to the fact that
it shows properties of both plastics and rubbers. In fact, it
has a wide range of applications, and is used to provide
better resistance against wetting and slippage, and also as
films in ‘packaging breathing’ items for fruits, vegetables
and seafood, rubber goods, molded bottles, adhesives, oil
paints, photosensitive resins, plastic material, footwear
soles, tubes and hoses. $^{[1]}$

sPBD12 was obtained by Natta and Porri$^{[2]}$ in 1955 and
was the first prepared syndiotactic polymer. The crystal
structure of sPBD12 was determined by Natta and
Corradini$^{[3]}$ using X-ray investigations. The crystal struc-
ture is characterized by highly extended chains in the planar
zigzag conformation, packed in an orthorhombic unit cell
with $a=10.98\ \mathring{A},b=6.60\ \mathring{A}$ and $c=5.14\ \mathring{A}$. The proposed
space group is *Pcam*. The projections along two directions
of the content of the unit cell, obtained with the fractional
coordinates of the carbon atoms of the asymmetric unit and
the axes of the unit cell reported in ref.$^{[3]}$, are shown in
Figure 1.

In past years, some of the authors have performed
molecular mechanics calculations concerning both the
chain conformation$^{[4,5]}$ and the crystal structure$^{[6]}$ of
sPBD12, which gave results which were in very good
agreement with experimental data. In fact, conformational
analysis of the chains of sPBD12 showed that highly
extended chains with *tcm* symmetry were lower in energy

As a consequence of the availability of new catalytic systems suitable for the synthesis of samples with various degrees of constitutional and configurational regularity, $^{[7,8]}$ studies concerning the thermal behavior, crystallization kinetics and morphology of sPBD12, as well as studies on blends of syndiotactic and atactic sPBD12, have been performed and reported very recently. $^{[9-13]}$ The influence of the stereoregularity of the polymer samples on the intensities of the reflections in X-ray spectra has been also evidenced. $^{[12]}$

In light of both industrial interest and renewed scientific interest in sPBD12, and considering that its properties are closely related to the crystallinity of the samples and to the crystallization process, we have undertaken structural studies concerning the crystal phase and the interphase between the crystal and the amorphous phase of the polymer. The aims of these studies were to find: (i) relationships between the stereoregularity of the chains and the crystal structure; (ii) structural changes as a function of thermal and mechanical treatments; (iii) models of chain folding and possible mechanisms for crystallization and crystal growth.

In this paper we report experimental data on samples of sPBD12 with different degrees of stereoregularity. We also report the results of detailed molecular mechanics calcu- lations on the crystal structure and the comparison of calculated X-ray powder profiles of various structural models with experimental X-ray powder profiles. Finally, we give an evaluation of the compatibility of configura- tional defects in the crystal phase.

## Experimental Part

Two polymer samples, the first having $\approx 97 \%$ 1,2 content and $\approx 62 \%$ of [rrrr] pentads (sample A) and the second having $\approx 92 \%$ 1,2 content and $\approx 40 \%$ of [rrrr] pentads (sample B), were synthesized with catalyst systems based on a chromium complex and methylaluminoxane in the laboratory of Prof. Porri (Politecnico di Milano, Italy), as described in ref. $^{[8]}$. Differential scanning calorimetry measurements were per- formed using a Perkin-Elmer DSC7 calorimeter in a flowing nitrogen atmosphere. Powder diffraction patterns were col- lected with a Philips PW3710 automated diffractometer operating in the conventional $\theta / 2 \theta$ Bragg-Brentano geometry, using Ni-filtered $CuK_{\alpha}$ radiation $(\lambda=1.5418 \AA)$. An Anton Paar TTK 450 low temperature camera was used for the X-ray measurements at various temperatures. A $Pt_{100}$ thermocouple inserted into the sample holder was used for high precision measurements and for the control of the working temperature. All the calculations of internal energy and the diffraction patterns were performed using the program Cerius $^{2}$ (version4.2 MatSci) by Accelrys. The crystals were generated by the Crystal Builder module. The Analytical module was used to calculate the X-ray diffraction patterns. The isotropic thermal factor $B=12 \AA^{2}$ and the crystal size $a=80 \AA, b=80 \AA$ and $c=60 \AA$ were used in all the calculated powder diffraction patterns. The energy was minimized using the Open Force Field module by the smart minimizer method with standard convergence. The following force fields were used: Universal1.02; $^{[14]}$ Dreiding; $^{[15]}$ MM2_85; $^{[16]}$ Compass. $^{[17]}$ The electro static term was calculated for the Dreiding and Compass force fields. Gasteiger $^{[18]}$ estimates for charges were taken into account in the use of Dreiding, while the Compass force field has its own method of calculation of the electrostatic charges.

## Crystal Structure by Molecular Mechanics

Molecular mechanics analysis was performed by energy calculations with the aim of obtaining models of crystal structures of sPBD12 which give the best accordance with experimental data. We first performed energy minimiza- tions on models of perfect crystals, and subsequently on models of crystals containing configurational defects. In these calculations, we used various sets of potential functions.

## Models of Perfect Crystals

The calculations of models of perfect crystals were done by packing chains with highly extended conformations. In past years, we have performed energy minimizations on models of perfect crystals by packing chains with *tcm* symmetry in all the orthorhombic space groups in which the symmetry elements of the chains are also crystallographic symmetry elements.$^{[6]}$ Those minimizations, performed with fixed chain conformations and parameters of the unit cell, showed that the space group which corresponds to the lowest packing energy was $Pcam$,$^{[6]}$ that is, the space group experimentally found.$^{[3]}$ Now, maintaining this space group, we minimized the energy as a function of both the conformational parameters of the chains and the axes of the unit cell. We used previously reported values as starting conformational parameters of the chains,$^{[6]}$ and used experimental values as the starting parameters of the unit cell.$^{[3]}$ The fractional coordinates of the carbon atoms in the minimum energy points obtained for all the potential functions are shown in Table 1 in comparison with the experimental ones. The conformational parameters of the chains obtained with the various potential functions were very similar to the experimental ones, as deduced by the fractional coordinates. We have also compared the calculated spectrum of Figure 7 with the spectra of sample A and B reported in Figure 5. To this end, we evaluated for both samples the discrepancy factor between the calculated and observed intensities for the powder profiles using Equation (1):

$$
R = \left[\sum(I_0 - I_\mathrm{c})/\sum I_0\right] \cdot 100 \tag{1}

where $I_0$ and $I_\mathrm{c}$ are the intensities of the reflections of the profiles of Figure 5 and of Figure 7, respectively. # Models of Crystals Containing Defects

The higher value of $R$ calculated for sample B can be attributed to a higher percent of defects that can be included in the crystal phase. Therefore, we examined the compatibility of the defects with the crystal phase. We only considered the configurational defects which, leaving the constitution of the backbone unchanged, can affect the chain conformation to a lesser extent with respect to the constitutional defects. We considered two alternative kinds of configurational defects, i.e., the presence of [mm] triads or [m] diads in the completely syndiotactic chains of the ideal crystal structure of ref. $^{[3]}$. To introduce the [mm] triad in a completely syndiotactic chain, we changed the configuration of one chiral carbon atom of the backbone, exchanging the positions of the hydrogen atom bonded to it with that of the $-\text{CH=CH}_2$ lateral group, leaving the positions of all the other atoms unchanged. To introduce the [m] diad in a completely syndiotactic chain, we changed the configuration of a chiral carbon atom of the backbone and of all the chiral carbon atoms which follow it. The introduction of the defects causes intra- and intermolecular short distances. Figure 8 shows views along the $b$ axis of portions of the crystal structure in which we have introduced triads and diads (colored in red in Figure 8(a) and 8(b), respectively), with the indication of some short distances between carbon atoms which arise. Since in this step we did not consider conformational changes, the views along the $c$ directions of the crystal structures containing either defects are roughly the same as reported in Figure 1(a).

We underline that, while the introduction of the triad caused short distances localized around it, the introduction of the diad caused short distances between all the adjacent lateral groups that follow it, that is the short distances propagate along the $c$ direction. Therefore, while the short distances could be removed by slight conformational adjustments in the case of the triad defect, this should not be possible for the diad defect. We verified this affirmation by energy minimizations followed by calculations of the X-ray powder profiles of the optimized structures. To this end, we built up a portion of crystal containing chains having isolated defects. In order to simulate the number of [rrrr] pentads of sample A ($\approx$62%) and sample B ($\approx$40%) we introduced in each chain one configurational error every 12 and 8 monomeric units, respectively. The crystals were built up in such a way that the defects of adjacent chains were far apart, that is, each defect was surrounded by

The positions of the atoms of stems constituted by various monomeric units containing the triad or the diad in the core, and the positions of the atoms at the same heights of the adjacent chains were varied, while the positions of all the other atoms and the values of the cell parameters were fixed. In any case, when five or more monomeric units were considered, the same minimum energy points were obtained. In fact, the optimized conformational parameters obtained by the various force field were very similar, so that the corresponding models of chains were practically the same.

As far as the chains containing diads were concerned, the values of the minimum energy of the crystal were high and the chain conformations were very different from the regular chain conformation. Moreover, the calculated X-ray powder profiles were significantly different with respect to the experimental ones, mainly for the intensities of the reflections. In any case, the most significant changes were localized in the neighborhood of the defect and affected prevailingly the positions of the lateral groups of the triad. The short intermolecular distance was completely relaxed and the short intramolecular distances were enlarged as much as possible. These results indicate that the accordance with the experimental spectrum of sample A is slightly better for the crystal structure with chains containing one configurational error every 12 monomeric units ($R=6.7\%$) with respect to the accordance obtained by the regular crystal structure ($R=6.8\%$). Therefore we think that the defects contained in the sample A can be present as [mm] triads in the crystal phase. As far as the comparisons between the spectrum of sample B and those calculated are concerned, we found that the value of $R=18.1\%$ for the ideal crystal structure lowers if we consider both one triad defect every 12 monomeric units ($R=14.0\%$) and one triad defect every 8 monomeric units ($R=14.2\%$). These last comparisons give a clearer indication that the triad defects are included in the crystal phase, even if the amount cannot be evaluated in an exact way.

## Conclusion

Two samples of sPBD12 having different constitutional and configurational regularities exhibited some differences in thermal behavior and in X-ray powder profiles. In fact, the most regular sample had a higher melting temperature, a higher crystallinity and a larger crystal size. Over a wide range of temperatures, from room temperature to the melting point of the most regular sample, the only structural variations observed concerned the size of the unit cell. The X-ray powder profiles, calculated for models of crystal structure obtained with various sets of potential functions, were in good agreement with the experimental profiles of both samples. The discrepancy factor $R$ between calculated and observed intensities of the X-ray reflections was lower for the most regular sample. The value of $R$ was lowered, especially for the less regular sample, if the powder profiles were calculated for crystal structures containing configura- tional defects constituted by [mm] triads in syndiotactic chains. This indicates that the configurational defects present in the samples, evidenced by NMR analysis, were included in some amount in the crystal phase.
