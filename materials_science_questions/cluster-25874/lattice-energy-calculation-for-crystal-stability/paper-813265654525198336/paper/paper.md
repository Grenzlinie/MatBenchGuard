# Computational studies on the structures and energies of the tautomers of 1-amino-3-nitrotriazol-5-one-2-oxide

P. Ravi · Surya P. Tewari · Ram Ramaswamy

Received: 12 October 2012 / Accepted: 15 November 2012 / Published online: 30 November 2012
© Springer Science+Business Media New York 2012

Abstract Molecular orbital calculations at the DFT-B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ level were performed for the possible tautomers of 1-amino-3-nitro-1,2,4-triazol-5-one-2-oxide. We have examined the substitution effects of amino and nitro groups by comparing calculated geometries, relative energies, and electrostatic potentials of model molecules. The optimized structures, vibrational frequencies, and thermodynamic values for triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides were obtained in their ground state. The results show 1H, 4H tautomers to be most stable. Detonation velocity and detonation pressure were evaluated by the Kamlet and Jacob equations based on the predicted density and the calculated heat of explosion. Explosive properties appear to be promising compared with those of 1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazine ($D = 8.75$ km/s, $P = 34.7$ Gpa) and octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine ($D = 8.96$ km/s, $P = 35.96$ Gpa), 2,4,6,8,10,12-hexanitro-2,4,6,8,10,12-hexaazaisowurtzitane ($D = 9.20$ km/s, $P = 42.0$ Gpa) and octanitrocubane ($D = 9.90$ km/s, $P = 48.45$ GPa). The designed triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides satisfy the criteria of high energy density materials.

Keywords Triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides · Density · Heat of explosion · Detonation velocity · Detonation pressure

## Introduction

Nitrotriazoles have been used as biologically active compounds including insecticides, fungicides, bactericides, nematocides, and acaricides, fluorescent bleaching agents, whitening agents, and recently as energetic materials [1]. Synthesis and development of triazol-5-one based explosives due to their superior performance, high thermal stability, and remarkably low detonation sensitivity to impact, friction, and spark is of current interest to chemists [2]. 3-Nitro-1,2,4-triazol-5-one (NTO) is an insensitive thermally stable explosive [3, 4]. The unique insensitivity of NTO is attributed to the high degree of hydrogen bonding carbonyl and nitro groups along with the molecular symmetry which allows the molecule to form the layered crystal structure. It was first synthesized in 1905 by nitration of 1,2,4-triazol-5-one; however, the first report on the explosive nature of NTO was published by Lee and Coburn in 1985 [3]. In the past few decades, researchers have been evaluating NTO based formulations to replace 2,4,6-trinitrotoluene (TNT), 1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazine (RDX),and octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine (HMX). Several metal and amine salts of NTO were synthesized for use in gun propellants or as primary explosives [4].

Computational studies on the constitutional isomers/or tautomers of NTO have been reported elsewhere [5–13]. Türker et al. [14, 15] reported the structure and explosive properties of NTO isomers and their picryl derivatives. Among the properties sought in design and synthesis of potential energetic materials are high detonation velocity and detonation pressure. These refer to the stable velocity of the shock front that characterizes detonation and the stable pressure that is developed behind the front. Detonation velocity and pressure can be increased by increasing the density, oxygen balance, and/or heat of formation of the material. It is known that introduction of amino ($\text{NH}_2$) groups into nitroazoles decreases the sensitivity of the product relative to the unaminated starting materials [2]. ## Methods and computational detail

All the calculations were performed on the desktop Pentium computer using the Gaussian 03 package [16]. The geom- etry optimizations of all the structures of triazol-5-one-N- oxides leading to energy minima were achieved within the framework of density functional theory (DFT, B3LYP) [17,18] at the aug-cc-pVDZ level. The exchange term of B3LYP consists of hybrid Hartee-Fock and local spin density exchange with Beck's gradient correlation to local spin density exchange [19]. The correlation term of B3LYP consists of the Vosko, Wilk, Nusair (VWN3) local corre- lation functional [20] and Lee, Yang, Parr (LYP) correlation functional [21]. Vibrational analyses were carried out using the same basis set employed in the corresponding geometry optimizations. The normal mode analysis for each structure yielded no imaginary frequencies for the 3N-6 vibrational degrees of freedom, where N is the number of atoms in the system. This indicates that the structure of each molecule corresponds to at least a local minimum on the potential energy surface. All the bond lengths were thoroughly searched in order to find out whether any bond cleavage occurred or not during the geometry optimization process.

The heats of explosion $(Q)$ were calculated from the difference between sum of the energies for the formation of explosive components and sum of the energies for the formation explosive products.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q & =\Delta E_{298.15 K}+\Delta(P V) \\
& =\Delta E_{0}+\Delta \mathrm{ZPE}+\Delta_{T} H+\Delta n R T
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\Delta E_{0}$ is the change in total energy between the products and reactants at $0 ~K$; $\Delta ZPE$ is the difference between the zero-point energies of the products and reac- tants; $\Delta_{T} H$ is the difference between the thermal correction from 0 to $298.15 ~K$ of the products and reactants; $\Delta(P V)$ equals to $\Delta n R T. \Delta n=0$ for an ideal gases.

The optimized structures were used to determine the densities of triazol-5-one-N-oxides using Materials Studio4.1 with CVFF force field and Ewald summation method[22]. Kamlet and Jacob equations [23] were used to esti-mate the detonation velocity and detonation pressure:

$$D=1.01\left(N M^{1 / 2} Q^{1 / 2}\right)^{1 / 2}(1+1.30 \rho)\qquad(2)$$

$$P=1.558 N M^{1 / 2} Q^{1 / 2} \rho^{2}\qquad(3)$$

where $D$ is the detonation velocity in $km / s, P$ is the deto nation pressure in $GPa, N$ is the number moles of gaseous detonation products per gram of explosive, $M$ is the average molecular weight of gaseous products, $Q$ is the energy of explosion in cal/g of explosive, and $\rho$ is the density in $g / cm^{3}$.

Molecular weight is related to $Q, D$ , and $P$ and sensi tivity. Generally, $Q$ value reaches maximum for triazol-5- one- $N$ -oxides containing two $NO_{2}$ groups corresponding to

The highest occupied molecular orbitals and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals play an important role in governing the stability/or sensitivity of compounds. We have determined the stability of triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides from their frontier molecular orbital energies and their gaps [25]. The relationship between impact sensitivity $(h_{50\ \%})$ and electronic structure of triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides have been predicted by the nitro group charge analysis. The Mulliken charges were chosen to evaluate the stability/or sensitivity of designed molecules knowing that the Mulliken charges are qualitatively correct and reproducible for the basis set [26, 27].

## Results and discussion

### Optimized structures

We have optimized the structures at the DFT-B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ level, and the molecular frameworks of triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides are presented in Fig. 1. No imaginary vibration frequencies were found confirming that these structures correspond to true energy minima. All triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides belong to $C_1$ point group. The non-planarity or co-planarity of a molecule is due to the repulsion between the neighboring nitro and carbonyl or $N$-oxide group. Carbonyl and $N$-oxide groups form the molecular plane i.e., hydrogen atoms of amino group are either perpendicular or coplanar to the ring. The lowest frequency, total energy, zero-point energy, and thermal correction to enthalpy and frontier molecular orbital energies and their gaps of triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides are summarized in Table 1. The lowest frequencies varying from 30.3355 (ANTONO-15) to $75.8158\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (ANTONO-8) are for the torsions of $NO_2$ groups [28]. Ttriazol-5-one-$N$-oxide have $N-H\cdots O$ (2.233-2.872 Å) interactions that are shorter than sum of the vander Waals radii [29]. The discrepancies in the bond lengths and angles, total and zero-point energies, thermal correction to enthalpy, frontier molecular orbital energies, trigger (i.e., $C-NO_2$ and $N-NO_2$), and C=O and $N\rightarrow O$ lengths are presumably due to the relative positions of $NO_2$ and $NH_2$ groups in the triazol-5-one-$N$-oxide ring.

### Frontier molecular orbitals

The highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMO) play a prominent role in governing the chemical reactions of the compounds [25]. The 3D plots of the HOMO and LUMO of molecules are shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. In all the molecules the HOMO is found to be spread over the entire molecule, but the LUMO is predominantly localized over the oxygen atoms. The band gap $\Delta E_{(\text{LUMO-HOMO)}}$ between the LUMO and HOMO is an important stability index of the molecules. A large band gap implies high stability and small band gap implies low stability; in turn high stability indicates low chemical reactivity and low stability indicates high reactivity i.e., smaller the band gap between HOMO and LUMO, easier the electron transition and lesser the stability of the explosive will be. The compound having large differences in frontier orbital energies that is if $\varepsilon_{\text{HOMO}}-\varepsilon_{\text{LUMO}} \gg 0$, then very little electron transfer occurs. If the respective orbital energies are quite similar that is if $\varepsilon_{\text{HOMO}}-\varepsilon_{\text{LUMO}} \approx 0$, strong electron transfer occurs. The frontier molecular orbital energies and their differences are presented in Table 1. The band gap values of triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides vary from 0.10809 to 0.18143 a.u. ANTONO-13 (0.18143 a.u.), ANTONO-12 (0.16143 a.u.), ANTONO-11 (0.15618 a.u.), ANTONO-4 (0.14240 a.u.), ANTONO-6 (0.14161 a.u.), ANTONO-14 (0.14027 a.u.), and ANTONO-15 (0.13981 a.u.) are more stable while ANTONO-1 (0.13415 a.u.), ANTONO-2 (0.12974 a.u.), ANTONO-3 (0.12336 a.u.), ANTONO-5 (0.13478 a.u.), ANTONO-7 (0.13632 a.u.), ANTONO-8 (0.12229 a.u.), ANTONO-9 (0.12297 a.u.), and ANTONO-10 (0.10809 a.u.) are less stable. The high frontier orbital gap is also associated with a low chemical reactivity and high kinetic stability. Thus ANTONO-13 seems to be kinetically more stable and chemically less active than the other triazol-5-one-$N$-oxides (i.e., ANTONO-10 is the least stable with the band gap of 0.10809 a.u.). Larger the length of C=N, C-N, and N-N bonds of molecule is, easier is the dissociation or breakdown and hence the molecule becomes less stable. The $R-NO_2$ (where, $\mathrm{R}=\mathrm{C}$ or $\mathrm{N}$) bonds are the trigger sites in these molecules and resonance in the triazol-5-one moiety strengthens these bonds thereby the molecules gets stabilized. Also, higher the total energy of the molecule, lesser is the stability. The discrepancies in the band gap values among the isomers are caused by the relative positions of $NO_2$, $NH_2$, $N\rightarrow O$, and C=O groups in the triazole ring (Table 2).

Theoretical density

Density appears to a higher power in the Kamlet-Jacob expressions than does any of the other variables and is considered to be one of the top priorities in the develop- ment of new explosives. Several researchers have attemp- ted to predict the densities with satisfactory accuracy [31-33]. We have taken the optimized structures computed from the B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ level to predict the densities using Materials Studio 4.1 with CVFF force field and Ewald summation method. This approach is based on the gener- ation of possible packing arrangements in all reasonable space groups. Many organic compounds are known to pack in $C 2 / c, P 2_{1}, P 2_{1 / c}, P 2_{1} 2_{1} 2_{1}, P_{-1}, P b c a, P b c n, P n a 2_{1}, C_{C}$ , and/or $C_{2}$ space groups [34,35]. The crystal characteristics of triazol-5-one- $N$ -oxides are summarized in Table 4. The molecules were packed in three space groups $(P 21 / c$ , $P 2_{1} 2_{1} 2_{1}$ , and $P b c a)$ . ANTONO-1, ANTONO-2, ANTONO3, ANTONO-5, ANTONO-6, ANTONO-13, ANTONO-14, and ANTONO-15 were packed in $P 2_{1} 2_{1} 2_{1}$ , ANTONO-4, ANTONO-7, ANTONO-8, ANTONO-9, ANTONO-10, and ANTONO-12 in $P 2_{1 / C}$ and ANTONO-11 in Pbca groups. The predicted densities of triazol-5-one- $N$ -oxides varying from 2.113 to $2.492 ~g / cm^{3}$ seem to be highercompared with that of 1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazine (1.820 g/cm3) [2], octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine $(1.920 ~g / cm^{3})$ [2], octanitrocubane $(1.979 ~g / cm^{3})$ [36], and2,4,6,8,10,12-hexanitro-2,4,6,8,10,12-hexaazaisowurtzitane(2.040 g/cm3) [37]. Heat of explosion

The heat of explosion provides information about the work capacity of the explosive. The liberation of heat under adiabatic conditions is called the heat of explosion. It is an irreversible process and whatever the amount of heat released is ultimately lost to surroundings. It is known that higher the value of heat of explosion for an explosive, greater the heat generated when an explosion occurs. The determination of composition of the products of a detonation or explosion process has been known to be a key issue. Kim et al. [38] reported that a difference of 10 kcal/mol in heat of formation had little influence on the explosive performance. For accurate prediction of detonation performance the heat of formation should be provided within an error of 5 kcal/mol. If the explosive compound is composed of C, H, N, and O atoms, the products can conceivably be expected to be $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{CO}$, $\text{N}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{H}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{CH}_4$, and NO gases as well as soot or free carbon. For CHNO compounds, all of the nitrogen goes to form $\text{N}_2$, the hydrogen to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and the remaining oxygen to $\text{CO}_2$. Oxygens go to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ before $\text{CO}_2$. Unused carbon is present as the solid. However, the product composition may vary considerably depending upon the loading density of the explosive compound. For lower loading densities the amount of CO increases markedly and $\text{CO}_2$ decreases.

The modified Kistiakowsky–Wilson rules [24] give an approximate for the products of decomposition, which is independent of the temperature of explosion. Also, the composition of detonation products may vary considerably depending upon the loading density of the explosive compound. We have calculated the heats of explosion ($Q$) of triazol-5-one-*N*-oxide assuming that the explosive reactions go to total completion. Table 5 presents the calculated oxygen balance, heats of explosion, detonation velocity, and detonation pressure of triazol-5-one-*N*-oxide computed from B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ level. Usually a good oxygen balance results in a greater heat of explosion and, therefore, leads to a better performance of the explosive. ANTONO have $Q$ values varying from 1.058 to 1.317 kcal/g. ANTONO-1 (1.096 kcal/g), ANTONO-2 (1.136 kcal/g), ANTONO-3 (1.078 kcal/g), ANTONO-9 (1.095 kcal/g), ANTONO-10 (1.058 kcal/g), and ANTONO-15 (1.144 kcal/g) have lower $Q$ values than the other isomers. The $\text{C-NO}_2$ compounds have lower $Q$ values than those of $\text{N-NO}_2$ compounds. However, the calculated $Q$ values do not exactly agree with those obtained experimentally because the conditions of loading density, temperature, pressure, and so on are not taken into consideration. The total amount of energy liberated depends upon the relative proportions of the reactants to the products. The $Q$ values are related to the nature of triazol-5-one-*N*-oxide, the relative positions of $\text{NH}_2$ and $\text{NO}_2$ groups, and the strength of $\text{R-NO}_2$, $\text{C=O}$, and $\text{N} \rightarrow \text{O}$ bonds. However, the detonation energies obtained are for the gas phase compounds and in reality they should be for the solid phase which would diminish the magnitude of $Q$ values [2, 38, 39].

Detonation velocity and detonation pressure may be as high as is compatible avoiding excessive sensitivity of the compound to unintended stimuli (i.e., like impact, shock, friction, and electric spark). Kamlet and Jacob indicated the importance of the density as a determinant of detonation velocity and detonation pressure. It appears to a high power in each equation than do any of the other quantities [23]. However, one or more of the other factors do sometimes override the effects of the density. Detonation velocity ($D$) linearly increases with $\rho$ for most of explosives while detonation pressure ($P$) increases with the square of $\rho$, when $\rho$ is greater than one. Depluech and Cherville [40] related the shock and thermal sensitivities to the molecular electronic structure and the properties of trigger $C-NO_2$, $N-NO_2$, and $O-NO_2$ bonds such as electrostatic potential, length, and strength. Kamlet and Adolph [41] found the linear relationship between the impact sensitivity and the oxygen balance. Mullay [42] gave statistically significant relationship between the impact sensitivity and molecular electronegativity of nitro compounds. Politzer and coworkers [43, 44] related the bond energies, electrostatic potential maxima, and the impact sensitivity. They also have related the impact sensitivity to

We have predicted the impact sensitivity of triazol-5-one- N-oxides from the electronic structures by the Mulliken atomic charge analysis of $NO_{2}$ group. As for the $NO_{2}$ groups in nitro compounds, they are electron- withdrawing. Higher negative charge the nitro group pos- sesses, lower the electron-withdrawing ability and, there- fore, the more stable is the compound. In explosives, R-$NO_{2}$ bonds (R = C or N) are usually the weakest and their breaking is often the initial step in the decomposition or detonation of explosive molecule.

The nitro group charge $(-Q_{NO_{2}})$ is calculated by the sum of the net Mulliken atomic charges on the nitrogen and oxygen atoms and of the nitro group:

$$-Q_{\mathrm{NO}_{2}}=\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{N}}+\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{O} 1}+\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{O} 2}\tag{5}$$

$$V_{\text {mid }}=\frac{\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{C}}}{0.5 R}+\frac{\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{N}}}{0.5 R}\tag{6}$$

where R is trigger length, $Q_{C}, Q_{N}$ and $Q_{O1}, Q_{O2}$ and are the Mulliken charges on carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen atoms, respectively.

The higher the $-Q_{NO_{2}}$, higher is the impact insensitivity. ### Conclusion

Molecular orbital calculations at the DFT-B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ level have been carried out to explore the structure

![](./images/813265654525198336_24.jpg)

and explosive properties of aminonitrotriazol-5-one-*N*-oxides (ANTONO). Our computational results indicate that triotriazol-5-one-*N*-oxides do correspond to energy minima and accordingly should be able to exist. All triazol-5-one-*N*-oxides belong to $\text{C}_1$ point group. Detonation properties were evaluated by the Kamlet-Jacob equations based on the calculated density and the heat of detonation. Energetic $\text{NH}_2$, C=O, and $\text{N} \rightarrow \text{O}$ groups appear to be a particularly promising area for investigation since they may lead to the three desirable consequences of higher heat of explosion, higher density, and diminished sensitivities. All model compounds have higher $Q$ values varying from 1.058 to 1.317 kcal/g and the discrepancies are due to the position of $\text{NO}_2$ and $\text{NH}_2$ groups in the azole ring. Several triazol-5-one-*N*-oxides have densities varying from 2.011 to 2.266 g/$\text{cm}^3$ which are higher compared with CL-20 (2.040 g/$\text{cm}^3$) and ONC (1.979 g/$\text{cm}^3$). The detonation properties of ANTONO-2 ($D = 10.10$ km/s, $P = 51.03$ GPa), ANTONO-4 ($D = 10.10$ km/s, $P = 50.58$ GPa), ANTONO-5 ($D = 10.25$ km/s, $P = 52.23$ GPa), ANTONO-6 ($D = 10.15$ km/s, $P = 50.85$ GPa), ANTONO-7 ($D = 10.58$ km/s, $P = 56.40$ GPa), ANTONO-8 ($D = 10.33$ km/s, $P = 53.80$ GPa), ANTONO-11 ($D = 10.30$ km/s, $P = 52.74$ GPa), and ANTONO-14 ($D = 10.05$ km/s, $P = 50.30$ GPa) are higher compared with ONC. The substitution of hydrogen at 1-position of triazol-5-one-*N*-oxides by $\text{NH}_2$ group shows better impact sensitivity. The designed compounds satisfy the criteria of high energy density materials. Furthermore, these triazol-5-one-*N*-oxides may be used as biologically active compounds (insecticides, fungicides, bactericides, nematicides, and acaricides), fluorescent bleaching agents, and whitening agents in near future.
