## ABSTRACT
The heats of formation (HOFs), energetic properties, strain energies, thermal stability, and impact sensitivity for a series of trinitromethyl- or dinitromethyl-modified RDX and $\beta$-HMX derivatives were studied by using density functional theory. It is found that the introduction of trinitromethyl group is an effective structural unit for improving the HOFs and energetic properties of the derivatives. However, incorporating the dinitromethyl group into the parent compound is not favorable for increasing its HOF and detonation properties. The effects of trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl groups on the stability of the parent compound are discussed. An analysis of the bond dissociation energies for several relatively weak bonds suggests that the substitution of the trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl group decreases the thermal stability of the derivatives. On the whole, the C-NO₂ bond in the trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl group is the weakest one and the C-NO₂ cleavage may happen in thermal decomposition. The introduction of the trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl group increases the impact sensitivities of the derivatives. In addition, the introduction of trinitromethyl group intensifies the strain of the central ring for the title compounds, while the effects of the dinitromethyl groups on the strain of the central ring are coupled to those of the ring structures.

### 1. Introduction

With the development of national defense and economy, high energy density materials (HEDMs) have attracted increasing attention due to their excellent energetic properties and good thermal stability [1–5]. Several well-known explosives, such as HMX (1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetraazacyclooctane) and RDX (hexahydro-1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazine), have been used extensively in both civil and military fields as powerful solid explosives and propellants for a long time. So far, there has been a significant history of interest in their structures and properties since they were first reported [6–15]. To obtain novel HEDMs with improved energetic properties superior to HMX or RDX, it is desirable to tailor the molecular structure of HMX and RDX to develop desired energetic properties.

In the process of seeking more powerful and less sensitive energetic materials, high-nitrogen heterocyclic compounds in combination with energetic substituents such as trinitromethyl ($-C(NO_2)_3$) group and dinitromethyl ($-CH(NO_2)_2$) groups are of particular interest because these compounds have satisfactory oxygen content and promising detonation properties [16–18]. Although low thermal stability (e.g. low decomposition temperatures) draws attention away from further investigations on trinitromethyl-substituted compounds [18,19], recently, some energetic compounds with dinitromethyl or trinitromethyl functionalities were found to have acceptable thermal stability. Dinitromethyl- or trinitromethyl-substituted triazoles such as 5-nitro-3-trinitromethyl-1H-[1,2,4]triazole, 3-dinitromethyl-5-nitro-1H-[1,2,4]triazole, and 5,5'-bis(trinitromethyl)-3,3'-azo-1,2,4-triazole, were synthesized and exhibit high densities, high heats of formation, excellent detonation performances, and moderate thermal stabilities [20]. 4-trinitroethyl-2,6,8,10,12-pentanitrohexaazaisowurtzitane, which is synthesized by introducing the trinitroethyl group to the CL-20 (2,4,6,8,10,12-hexanitro-2,4,6,8,10,12-hexaazaisowurtzitane) cage skeleton, have been proved to have surprisingly high thermal stability (the decomposition temperature is 236.1 °C) and comparable excellent energetic properties with CL-20 [21,22]. Moreover, some energetic salts containing dinitromethyl or trinitromethyl groups were synthesized and exhibit moderate thermal stabilities and good energetic properties [23,24]. However, the design or synthesis of new energetic compounds with trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl functionality based on the HMX or RDX ring skeleton have not been reported. In addition, there lack comprehensive understanding on the structure–property relationships for trinitromethyl- or dinitromethyl-substituted derivatives.

Now, theoretical studies based on quantum chemical treatment have gained acceptance as a useful research tool to screen the candidates of high-energy density materials, thereby avoiding expensive and dangerous experimental tests. They can provide understanding in terms of the relationships between molecular structure and property, which in turn can help design better and more efficient laboratory tests [25]. Therefore, the optimization of the candidate compound with high energy and less sensitivity is the primary step for designing and synthesizing new HEDMs.

In this work, we systematically investigated molecular structure, HOFs, energetic properties, strain energy, thermal stability, and impact sensitivity for a series of trinitromethyl- or dinitromethyl-modified derivatives of RDX and $\beta$-HMX ($\beta$-HMX presents a chair structure with two $\text{NO}_2$ adjacent moieties on one side of the ring and the other two on the other side [26]) by introducing one or more trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl group(s) to replace the $-\text{NO}_2$ groups of the RDX and $\beta$-HMX by using density functional theory (DFT) method. The HOFs of the substituted derivatives were calculated by designing isodesmic reactions. Their detonation velocities and pressures were predicted using the calculated HOFs and densities. The strain energies were systematically investigated to provide a more insightful interpretation of their stabilities. The thermal stabilities were evaluated based on their bond dissociation energies. Finally, their impact sensitivities were investigated by calculating the free space per molecule in the unit cell. It is expected that our results can provide useful information for the molecular design of novel HEDMs.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. A description of our computational method is given in Section 2. The results and discussion are presented in Section 3, followed by a summary of our conclusions in Section 4.

## 2. Computational method

The object molecules, a series of trinitromethyl- or dinitromethyl-modified derivatives of RDX and $\beta$-HMX, are classed into four groups (molecular numbering as A11-A13, A21-A23, B11-B15, and B21-B25), as shown in Fig. 1. The hybrid DFT-B3LYP methods with the 6-31G(d,p) basis set were adopted for the optimizations of the molecular structures and the predictions of HOFs. Previous studies have shown that the basis set 6-31G(d,p) is able to precisely predict the molecular structures and energies of energetic organic compounds [27-32].

The method of isodesmic reactions was employed to calculate their HOFs using total energies obtained from the DFT-B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) calculations. Here we design isodesmic reactions in which the numbers of all kinds of bonds keep invariable to decrease the calculation errors of HOFs. Because the electronic circumstances of reactants and products are very similar in isodesmic reactions, the errors of electronic correction energies can be counteracted, and then the errors of the calculated HOFs can be greatly reduced. In our designed reactions, the basic structural unit of the ring skeleton, and the big molecules are changed into small ones too. This approach has been demonstrated to predict reliably the HOFs of many organic systems [31-37].

The isodesmic reactions used to obtain the HOFs of the title compounds at 298 K are as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{C}_{3} \mathrm{N}_{3} \mathrm{H}_{6}\left(\mathrm{NO}_{2}\right)_{n} \mathrm{R}_{3-n}+3 \mathrm{NH}_{3}+n \mathrm{CH}_{4} \\
& \quad \rightarrow \mathrm{C}_{3} \mathrm{N}_{3} \mathrm{H}_{9}+(3-n) \mathrm{NH}_{2} \mathrm{NO}_{2}+n \mathrm{H}-\mathrm{R}+n \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{NH}_{2} \quad(0 \leqslant n \leqslant 3)
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{C}_{4} \mathrm{N}_{4} \mathrm{H}_{8}\left(\mathrm{NO}_{2}\right)_{n} \mathrm{R}_{4-n}+4 \mathrm{NH}_{3}+n \mathrm{CH}_{4} \\
& \quad \rightarrow \mathrm{C}_{4} \mathrm{N}_{4} \mathrm{H}_{12}+(4-n) \mathrm{NH}_{2} \mathrm{NO}_{2}+n \mathrm{H}-\mathrm{R}+n \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{NH}_{2} \quad(0 \leqslant n \leqslant 4)
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
where $\mathrm{R}=-\mathrm{CH}\left(\mathrm{NO}_{2}\right)_{2}$, or $-\mathrm{C}\left(\mathrm{NO}_{2}\right)_{3}$.

For the isodesmic reaction, the heat of reaction $\Delta H_{298}$ at 298 K can be calculated from the following equation:
$$
\Delta H_{298 \mathrm{K}}=\sum \Delta H_{f, P}-\sum \Delta H_{f, R}
\tag{3}
$$
where $\Delta H_{f, R}$ and $\Delta H_{f, P}$ are the HOFs of reactants and products at 298 K, respectively.

As the experimental HOFs of chair 1,3,5-triazacyclohexane, chair 1,3,5,7-tetraazacyclooctane, and $\mathrm{NH}_{2} \mathrm{NO}_{2}$ are unavailable, additional calculations were carried out for the atomization reaction: $\mathrm{C}_{a} \mathrm{H}_{b} \mathrm{N}_{c} \rightarrow a \mathrm{C}(\mathrm{g})+b \mathrm{H}(\mathrm{g})+c \mathrm{N}(\mathrm{g})$ at the G2 level to get their HOFs. The G2 theory [38,39] has been verified to be able to predict the HOFs accurately. Now the most important task is to calculate $\Delta H_{298}$. The $\Delta H_{298}$ can be calculated using the following expression:
$$
\Delta H_{298 \mathrm{K}}=\Delta E_{298 \mathrm{K}}+\Delta(P V)=\Delta E_{0}+\Delta Z P E+\Delta H_{T}+\Delta n R T
\tag{4}
$$
where $\Delta E_{0}$ is the change in total energy between the products and the reactants at 0 K; $\Delta ZPE$ is the difference between the zero-point energies (ZPEs) of the products and the reactants at 0 K; $\Delta H_{T}$ is thermal correction from 0 to 298 K. The $\Delta(P V)$ value in Eq. (4) is the $PV$ work term and equals $\Delta nRT$ for the reactions of ideal gas. For the isodesmic reactions here, $\Delta n=0$, so $\Delta(P V)=0$.

Since the condensed phase for most energetic compounds is solid, the calculation of detonation properties requires solid-phase $HOF$ ($\Delta H_{f, solid}$). According to Hess's law of constant heat summation [40], the gas-phase $HOF$ ($\Delta H_{f, gas}$) and heat of sublimation ($\Delta H_{sub}$) can be used to evaluate their solid-phase $HOF$:
$$
\Delta H_{f, solid}=\Delta H_{f, gas}-\Delta H_{sub}
\tag{5}
$$

Politzer et al. [41,42] found that the heats of sublimation can correlate well with the molecular surface area and electrostatic interaction index $v \sigma_{tot}^2$ of energetic compounds. The empirical expression of the approach is as follows:
$$
\Delta H_{sub}=a A^{2}+b\left(v \sigma_{tot}^{2}\right)^{0.5}+c
\tag{6}
$$
where $A$ is the surface area of the 0.001 electrons/bohr$^3$ isosurface of electronic density of the molecule, $v$ describes the degree of balance between positive and negative potential on the isosurface, and $\sigma_{tot}^2$ is a measure of variability of the electrostatic potential on the molecular surface. The coefficients $a$, $b$, and $c$ were determined by Rice et al.: $a=2.670 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{kcal/mol}/\text{\AA}^4$, $b=1.650\ \text{kcal/mol}$, and $c=2.966\ \text{kcal/mol}$ [43]. The descriptors $A$, $v$, and $\sigma_{tot}^2$ were calculated using the computational procedures proposed by Bulat et al. [44]. This approach has been proved to credible for evaluating heats of sublimation of many compounds [43,45,46].

The detonation velocity and pressure were estimated by the Kamlet-Jacobs equations [47] as
$$
D=1.01\left(N \bar{M}^{1 / 2} Q^{1 / 2}\right)^{1 / 2}(1+1.30 \rho)
\tag{7}
$$

$$
P=1.558 \rho^{2} N \bar{M}^{1 / 2} Q^{1 / 2}
\tag{8}
$$
where each term is defined as follows: $D$, the detonation velocity (km/s); $P$, the detonation pressure (GPa); $N$, the moles of detonation gases per gram explosive; $\bar{M}$, the average molecular weight of these gases; $Q$, the heat of detonation (cal/g); and $\rho$, the loaded density of explosives (g/cm$^3$). For known explosives, their $Q$ and $\rho$ can be measured experimentally; thus their $D$ and $P$ can be calculated. However, as the $Q$ and $\rho$ of some compounds cannot be evaluated from experimental measures, their $Q$ and $\rho$ need be firstly calculated.

For the title compounds, the theoretical density was obtained from the molecular weight divided by the average molecular volume. The volume was defined as inside a contour of 0.001 electrons/bohr$^3$ density that was evaluated using a Monte Carlo integration. We performed 100 single-point calculations for each

optimized structure to get an average volume at the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) level [29]. Accordingly, the crystal density can be improved by introducing the interaction index $v\sigma_{tot}^2$ [48]:

$$
\rho=\beta_{1}\left(\frac{M}{V(0.001)}\right)+\beta_{2}\left(v \sigma_{t o t}^{2}\right)+\beta_{3} \tag{9}
$$

in which $M$ is the molecular mass (g/molecule), and $V(0.001)$ is the volume of the isolated gas molecule ($\text{cm}^3$/molecule). The coefficients $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$, and $\beta_3$ are 0.9183, 0.00278, and $\beta_3$ [48].

The heat of detonation $Q$ was evaluated by the $HOF$ difference between products and explosives according to the principle of exothermic reactions, i.e. all the N atoms turn into $\text{N}_2$, and oxygen atoms go to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ before $\text{CO}_2$. If the content of O is not enough to satisfy full oxidation of the H and C atoms, the remaining H atoms will convert into $\text{H}_2$, and C atoms will exist as solid-state C. The detonation products are supposed to be $\text{CO}_2$ (or C), $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (or $\text{H}_2$), and $\text{N}_2$, so released energy in the decomposition reaction reaches its maximum. On the basis of the $\rho$ and $Q$ values, the corresponding $D$ and $P$ values can be evaluated.

The concept of strain energy (SE) is defined as the difference between energies for a process that releases strain as determined by experiment and as obtained from a model that does not involve strain [49]. Strain energy can be regarded as an index to correlating the structure with the stability and reactivity of a molecule. The method of homodesmotic reaction has been employed successfully to calculate strain energy from total energies ($E_0$) with zero-point energies (ZPEs) based on ab initio calculations [49-52]. In this work, we investigate the strain energy of the triazinane or tetrazocane ring skeleton to understand the stability of the molecules with different substituents.

The corresponding homodesmotic reactions used to derive the strain energies of the title compounds are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{C}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{3} \mathrm{H}_{6}\left(\mathrm{NO}_{2}\right)_{n} \mathrm{R}_{3-n}+6 \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{NH}_{2} \rightarrow 3\left(\mathrm{NH}_{2}\right)_{2} \mathrm{CH}_{2}+n\left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\right)_{2} \mathrm{NNO}_{2} \\
& \quad+(3-n)\left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\right)_{2} \mathrm{~N}-\mathrm{R} \quad(0 \leqslant n \leqslant 3)
\end{aligned} \tag{10}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{C}_{4} \mathrm{~N}_{4} \mathrm{H}_{8}\left(\mathrm{NO}_{2}\right)_{n} \mathrm{R}_{4-n}+8 \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{NH}_{2} \rightarrow 4\left(\mathrm{NH}_{2}\right)_{2} \mathrm{CH}_{2}+n\left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\right)_{2} \mathrm{NNO}_{2} \\
& \quad+(4-n)\left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\right)_{2} \mathrm{~N}-\mathrm{R}(0 \leqslant n \leqslant 4)
\end{aligned} \tag{11}
$$

where $\text{R} = -\text{CH}(\text{NO}_2)_2$, or $-\text{C}(\text{NO}_2)_3$.

The change of energies with correction of the zero-point vibrational energy (ZPE) in the homodesmotic reactions can be obtained from the following equation:

$$
\Delta H_{298 \mathrm{K}}=\sum E_{\text {product }}-\sum E_{\text {reactant }}+\Delta ZPE \tag{12}
$$

The strength of bonding, which could be evaluated by bond dissociation energy, is fundamental to understand chemical processes [53]. The energy required for bond homolysis at 298 K and 1 atm corresponds to the enthalpy of reaction A-B(g) → A'(g)+ B'(g), which is the bond dissociation enthalpy of the molecule A-B by definition [54]. For many organic molecules, the terms "bond dissociation energy" (BDE) and "bond dissociation enthalpy" often appear interchangeably in the literature [55]. Therefore, at 0 K, the homolytic bond dissociation energy can be given in terms of the following equation:

$$
BDE_{0}(A-B) \to E_{0}(A')+E_{0}(B')-E_{0}(A-B) \tag{13}
$$

The bond dissociation energy with zero-point energy (ZPE) correction can be calculated by the following equation:

$$
BDE(A-B)_{ZPE}=BDE_{0}(A-B)+\Delta E_{ZPE} \tag{14}
$$

where $\Delta E_{ZPE}$ is the difference between the ZPEs of the products and the reactants.

The calculations were performed with the Gaussian 03 package [56]. The optimizations were performed without any symmetry restrictions using the default convergence criteria in the program. All of the optimized structures were characterized to be true local energy minima on the potential energy surfaces without imaginary frequencies.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Gas-phase and solid-phase heats of formation

The solid-phase $HOF$, $\Delta H_{f,solid}$, is an important property to predict the detonation properties of the energetic materials. Here we investigate the effects of different substituents on the gas-phase $HOF$ ($\Delta H_{f,gas}$) and solid-phase $HOFs$ ($\Delta H_{f,solid}$) of the title compounds. Table 1 lists the total energies, ZPEs, and thermal corrections for 8 reference compounds in the isodesmic reactions. The experimental gas-phase $HOFs$ of $\mathrm{CH}_{4}$, $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$, and $\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{2}$ was taken from Ref. [57], and the experimental gas-phase $HOFs$ of trinitromethane ($\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{3}}$) and dinitromethane ($\mathrm{CH_{2}(NO_{2})_{2}}$) was taken from Ref. [58]. The $HOF$ values of chair 1,3,5-triazacyclohexane, chair 1,3,5,7-tetraazacyclooctane, and $\mathrm{NH_{2}NO_{2}}$ were calculated from the atomization reaction at the G2 level. To validate the reliability of our results, the $HOFs$ of $\mathrm{CH}_{4}$, $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$, and $\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{2}$ were also calculated at the G2 level from the atomization reaction. The results show that their $HOF$ values are very close to corresponding experimental values with the relative errors of only 3.83%, 1.63%, and 0.94%, respectively. Therefore, the $HOF$ values from the G2 calculations are expected to be reliable in this work.

Table 2 presents the total energies, ZPEs, thermal corrections, $\Delta H_{f,gas}$, and $\Delta H_{f,solid}$ of the title compounds. It is found that the calculated $\Delta H_{f,gas}$ and $\Delta H_{f,solid}$ values of RDX and $\beta$-HMX are in agreement with their experimental ones [59-61]. The relative errors between our calculated and experimental $\Delta H_{f,gas}$ and $\Delta H_{f,solid}$ values of RDX ($\beta$-HMX) are 4.43% and 4.42% (0.79% and 13.38% for $\beta$-HMX), respectively, which indicates that the predicted $HOF$ method chosen for the title compounds are supposed to be credible. As shown in Table 2, when the $-\mathrm{NO}_{2}$ group of RDX or $\beta$-HMX is replaced by the $-\mathrm{C(NO_{2})_{3}}$ group, its $HOF$ value increases. All the trinitromethyl-substituted derivatives exhibit high positive $\Delta H_{f,gas}$ (220.06-412.17 kJ/mol) and $\Delta H_{f,solid}$ (90.97-179.14 kJ/ mol). However, the introduction of the $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ group decreases the $HOF$ value of the parent compound, and most of the dinitromethyl-substituted derivatives exhibit negative solid-phase $HOFs$. It is obvious from Table 2 that as the number (n) of the $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ groups increase, the $HOFs$ of these substituted derivatives decrease markedly. Hence, it can be concluded that the introduction of the $-\mathrm{C(NO_{2})_{3}}$ group plays a very important role in increasing the $HOFs$ of the title compounds, while the substitution of $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ group greatly reduces the $HOFs$.

To investigate the effects of the $-\mathrm{C(NO_{2})_{3}}$ and $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ groups on the $HOFs$ of the title compounds more clearly, Fig. 2 displays the relations between the $HOFs$ and the number of substituents (n), in which the average $HOFs$ of different isomers with the same n are used. Obviously, there exists a good linear relationship between $\Delta H_{f,gas}$ or $\Delta H_{f,solid}$ and the number (n) of the $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ group, e.g., for series A1, $\Delta H_{f,gas}=195.944-43.571n$ (R = 0.9964, SD = 5.8305), and for series B1, $\Delta H_{f,gas}=265.662-54.229n$ (R = 0.9981, SD = 6.0577). This indicates that the introduction of one or more $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ (n) decreases the gas-phase $HOFs$ by 43.571 kJ/mol for series A1 and by 54.229 kJ/mol for series B1 on average, respectively. These observations show that the $HOFs$ of the dinitromethyl-substituted derivatives are satisfactory with the relationship of simple group addition. In addition, there are binomial correlations between the $\Delta H_{f,gas}$ or $\Delta H_{f,solid}$ (Y) and the number (n) of $-\mathrm{C(NO_{2})_{3}}$ group, e.g., for series B2, $\Delta H_{f,gas}=269.661+16.255n+4.996n^{2}$ (R = 0.9951, SD = 5.7804), and $\Delta H_{f,solid}=121.521+0.584n+3.372n^{2}$ (R = 0.9901,SD = 3.2703). The established equations indicate that the $HOFs$ increases with the number (n) of the $-\mathrm{C(NO_{2})_{3}}$ group increasing.

Additionally, the relative positions of the substituents have some effect on the $HOFs$. For the isomers with the same number of the $-\mathrm{C(NO_{2})_{3}}$ or $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ groups, the shorter the distance between the substituents is, and the larger the $HOF$ of the compound is. B12 has a somewhat larger $HOF$ than B13, and the $HOF$ of B22 is also slightly higher than that of B23, which may be caused by larger static hindrance interactions between the $-\mathrm{C(NO_{2})_{3}}$ or $-\mathrm{CH(NO_{2})_{2}}$ groups.

### 3.2. Detonation properties

Detonation velocity and detonation pressure are two important performance parameters for an energetic material. Table 4 presents the calculated $\rho$, $Q$, $D$, $P$, and oxygen balance (OB) of the title compounds. For a comparison, the experimental detonation properties of two known explosives RDX (A0) and HMX (B0) are also listed in this table. The semi-empirical Kamlet-Jacobs formula has been proved to be reliable for predicting the explosive properties of energetic high-nitrogen compounds [29,33]. The calculated $\rho$, $D$, and $P$ values of RDX (1.802 g/cm³, 8.83 km/s, and 34.57 GPa) or $\beta$-HMX (1.909 g/cm³, 9.20 km/s, and 38.90 GPa) agree well with the available experimental data of RDX (1.816 g/cm³, 8.75 km/s, and 34.00 GPa) or $\beta$-HMX (1.90 g/cm³, 9.10 km/s, and 39.00 GPa) [4,62], respectively. This suggests that our predictions for the title compounds are reliable and further confirms the reliability of the calculation method used here.

As shown in Table 3, the substitution of $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ or $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ produces different effects on the heats of detonation ($Q$) of the title compounds. The dinitromethyl-substituted derivatives have lower $Q$ than RDX or $\beta$-HMX, and their $Q$ values decrease with the number of $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ increasing. However, the $Q$ values of the trinitromethyl-substituted derivatives firstly increase and then decrease with the number of $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ group increasing.

Oxygen balance ($OB$) indicates the degree to which an explosive can be oxidized, so it is another important criterion for selecting potential HEDMs. It is found from Table 4 that the substitution of the $-CH(NO_2)_2$ or $-C(NO_2)_3$ group is helpful for improving $OB$ for the title compounds. Compared with the $-CH(NO_2)_2$ group, the replacement of the $-C(NO_2)_3$ group extremely enhances $OB$ of the parent compounds. Some trinitromethyl-substituted derivatives have positive $OB$ values, which make these compounds be used as oxidizers in explosives. It is also seen in Table 4 that, in general, the higher the $OB$ value is, the larger the $D$ and $P$ values are. When the value of $OB$ is around zero, $D$ and $P$ reach the largest value for the substituted derivatives in the same series. However, the $D$ and $P$ of the substituted derivatives decrease as its $OB$ is too positive. The primary reason is that the additional oxygen will produce $O_2$ that takes away a great deal of energy during the explosion of high energy materials.

As shown in Table 3, all the trinitromethyl-substituted deriva- tives have higher $\rho$ values than their parent compound (RDX or $\beta$-HMX), indicating that the introduction of the $-C(NO_2)_3$ group can markedly increase the $\rho$ values of the parent compounds. In particular, the tetra-(trinitromethyl)-substituted derivative (B25) has the largest $\rho$ value ($2.003$ g/cm³) among the title compounds. However, the effect of the $-CH(NO_2)_2$ group on the densities of the derivatives may be coupled to those of the parent ring. For A1 series whose compounds contain a hexatomic heterocycle, the substitution of $-CH(NO_2)_2$ increases $\rho$ values of the parent compound (RDX), whereas the situation is quite the contrary for B1 series whose compounds contain a octatomic heterocycle.

It is observed from Table 3 that the $D$ and $P$ values of all the derivatives are over or close to these of RDX, which suggests that the derivatives can be regarded as potential candidates of HEDMs. Moreover, all the trinitromethyl-substituted derivatives (A2 series and B2 series) have higher $D$ and $P$ values than $\beta$-HMX. Especially, B22, B23 or B24 present excellent detonation properties: $D > 9.50$ km/s, and $P > 42.00$ GPa, which is even superior to those of the famous CL-20 (9.40 km/s, and 42.00 GPa). This suggests that the $-C(NO_2)_3$ group is an effective structural unit for increasing the detonation properties of the title compounds. However, the substi- tution of the $-CH(NO_2)_2$ group is not favorable for increasing their detonation properties for the title compounds. It can be seen from Table 3 that the derivatives of A1 series or B1 series have slightly lower $D$ and $P$ values than the parent compound. Additionally, the relative positions of the $-C(NO_2)_3$ or $-CH(NO_2)_2$ groups may influence the detonation properties of these isomers. For instance, B23 has slightly higher $\rho$, $D$, and $P$ values than B22. Similar trends can also be found between B13 and B12.

Fig. 3 displays the correlations between $Q$, $\rho$, $D$, and $P$ and the number ($n$) of $-C(NO_2)_3$ or $-CH(NO_2)_2$ groups, in which the average $Q$, $\rho$, $D$, and $P$ of isomers with the same $n$ are used. As is evident from Fig. 3, among each series, the trinitromethyl-substituted com- pound containing the octatomic heterocycle has the highest $\rho$, $D$, and $P$ values, while the dinitromethyl-substituted compound con- taining the hexatomic heterocycle has the least ones. In addition, the variation trends of $D$ and $P$ with the number ($n$) of substituents are basically the same for every series, and their variation trends are commonly determined by their $Q$ and $\rho$ when there is a great change in $Q$. Also, note that the $D$ or $P$ values of the trinitrometh- yl-substituted derivatives firstly increase and then decrease with the number of $-C(NO_2)_3$ group increasing. This is because the intro- duction of three or more $-C(NO_2)_3$ groups produces an too positive oxygen balance values and decreases $Q$ greatly, which further lead $\rho$ to have less influence on $D$ and $P$.

### 3.3. Strain energies

Table 4 lists the strain energies of the 1,3,5-triazinanane or 1,3,5,7-tetrazocane ring of the title compounds at the B3LYP/6-31G** level via homodesmotic reactions. It is found that when the $-NO_2$ group is replaced by $-C(NO_2)_3$ group, the strain energy of the 1,3,5-triazinanane or 1,3,5,7-tetrazocane ring increases for each series, and the strain energy of the central ring increases with the number ($n$) of $-C(NO_2)_3$ group increasing. This indicates that the introduction of $-C(NO_2)_3$ group intensifies the strain of the cen- tral ring for the title compounds. However, all the dinitromethyl- substituted derivatives have lower strain energy than the parent compound (RDX or $\beta$-HMX). It suggests that the strain of the 1,3,5-triazinanane ring or 1,3,5,7-tetrazocane ring is weakened as the $-NO_2$ group is replaced by the $-CH(NO_2)_2$ group.

In addition, for A1 series, the strain energy of the 1,3,5-triaz- inane ring increase with the number of $-CH(NO_2)_2$ group increas- ing. However, for B1 series, the case is quite the contrary. It may be inferred that the effects of the $-CH(NO_2)_2$ groups on the strain of the central ring are coupled to those of the ring structures.

Hence, compared with the $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ group, the pyrolysis of the $\text{C-NO}_2$ bonds in the $-\text{(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ group is easier to happen, which suggests that the $\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$-substituted derivatives is less stable.

### 3.5. Impact sensitivity

Impact sensitivity is often used to judge whether energetic compounds are sensitive or insensitive to external impact. The impact sensitivity is generally reported as the height in cm, designated $h_{50}$ ($h_{50}$ is the height from which the sample is impacted by a given mass and there is a 50% probability of causing an explosion) [63]. The higher $h_{50}$ is, the more sensitive is the explosive. Despite the impact sensitivity of an energetic compound depends upon a combination of factors, Pospíšil et al. [64,65] found that there does appear to be a general tendency for $h_{50}$ to decrease (increasing sensitivity) as the free space per molecule in the unit cell of energetic solids becomes large. The free space per molecule in the unit cell, designated $\Delta V$, can be represented as the difference between the effective volume per molecule that would be required to completely fill the unit cell, $V_{eff}$, and the intrinsic gas phase molecular volume, $V_{int}$:

$$
\Delta V = V_{eff} - V_{int} \tag{15}
$$

$V_{int}$ is defined as the volume enclosed by the 0.003 electrons/bohr$^{3}$ contour of the molecule's electronic density. $V_{eff}$ can be calculated exactly from the dimensions of the unit cell and the number of molecules that it encompasses, or alternatively by the formula,

$$
V_{eff} = M/\rho \tag{16}
$$

in which $M$ is the molecular mass and $\rho$ is the crystal density.

Table 6 lists the free space per molecule ($\Delta V$) in the unit cell of the title compounds. It is seen that all the derivatives have greater $\Delta V$ values than their parent compound (RDX or $\beta$-HMX). Moreover, for each series, the $\Delta V$ values of the derivatives increase as the number of the substituents increases. It is also found that the trinitromethyl-substituted derivatives have higher $\Delta V$ values than corresponding dinitromethyl-substituted ones with the same parent ring. According to the free space per molecule in the unit cell, it may be inferred that the introduction of the $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ or $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ group increases the sensitivity of the derivatives, and the trinitromethyl-substituted derivatives are more sensitive than the corresponding dinitromethyl-substituted derivatives with the same parent ring. In particular, the $\Delta V$ values of A23, B24, and B25 is over 100 $\text{\AA}^{3}$, which may mean that they are very sensitive to external impact. By and large, these conclusions are consistent with the thermal stability analysis as mentioned above.

## 4. Conclusions

In this work, we performed systematic studies on the HOFs, energetic properties, strain energy, thermal stability, and impact sensitivity for a series of trinitromethyl- or dinitromethyl-modified derivatives of RDX and $\beta$-HMX by using the DFT-B3LYP method. It is found that the introduction of the $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ group plays a very important role in increasing the HOFs of the title compounds, while the substitution of $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ group greatly reduces the HOFs. The calculated detonation properties reveal that the substitution of the $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ group greatly enhances the detonation performance of the derivatives. However, incorporating the $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ group into the parent compound is not favorable for increasing its detonation properties.

An analysis of the strain energy show that the substitution of $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ group intensifies the strain of the central ring, and the strain energy of the central ring increase with the number of $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ increasing. However, the effects of the $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ groups on the strain of the central ring are coupled to those of the ring structures. The bond dissociation energies for several relatively weak bonds suggest that the substitution of the trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl group decreases thermal stability of the derivatives. Our calculated free space per molecule in the unit cell suggests that the introduction of the $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ or $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ group increases the sensitivity of the derivatives. Moreover, their thermal stability or insensitivity to external impact decreases with the number of $-\text{C(NO}_2\text{)}_3$ or $-\text{CH(NO}_2\text{)}_2$ increasing. On the whole, the $\text{C-NO}_2$ bond in the trinitromethyl or dinitromethyl group is the weakest one and the $\text{C-NO}_2$ homolysis may be an initial step in thermal decomposition.
