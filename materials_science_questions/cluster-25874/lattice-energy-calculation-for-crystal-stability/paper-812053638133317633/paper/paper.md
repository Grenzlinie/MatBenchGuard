Summary: A comparison between the crystal structure of isotactic *cis*-1,4-poly(1,3-pentadiene) previously predicted by molecular mechanics calculations and that successively determined by other authors by experimental data is reported. The agreement between the two structures is very good as far as the space group, the unit cell parameters and the con- formation of the polymer chain are concerned. The mode of packing of the chains proposed in the experimental crystal structure is very similar to that found as relative minimum in the previous energy calculations. The coexistence, in different amounts, of these two modes of packing is suggested by the analysis of the simulated X-ray spectra and by the results of new energy calculations.

## Introduction

Isotactic *cis*-1,4-poly(1,3-pentadiene) (icPP) was synthe- sized by Natta et al. as early as 1963 starting from the *trans* isomer of 1,3-pentadiene, with the use of titanium-catalytic systems. $^{[1]}$ A partial characterization by IR and X-ray diffraction evidenced the *cis* configuration of the double bonds and the isotactic structure of the synthesized poly- mer. The $(cisS^{+}TS^{-})_{n}$ chain conformation, compatible with the determined value of the chain axis repeat $c=(8.15\pm0.05)$ Å, was proposed. A portion of the chain of icPP, constituted by two monomeric units, is shown in Figure 1. The X-ray powder profile exhibited an intense peak at $2\theta\sim17.2^{\circ}$ followed by a lower intensity peak at $2\theta\sim18.8^{\circ}$ above a rather large peak due to the amorphous phase. No crystal structure of icPP was pro- posed in the following 26 years, while crystal structures of other polydienes having a double bond in the main chain both in *cis* and in *trans* configurations were determined. $^{[2-5]}$

In 1990 a possible crystal structure of icPP was pro- posed $^{[6]}$ by the present author using molecular mechanics and non-commercial computer programs, based only on the experimental value of the chain axis repeat reported in the literature. In particular, conformational energy calculations performed both by maps and by minimizations allowed to find the best conformation of the isolated chain under rea- sonable symmetry constraints also related to the value of the chain axis repeat. The polymer chain was characterized by a two-fold helix [$\boldsymbol{s(2/1)}$ line repetition group] and the confor- mational parameters were in perfect agreement with the conformational sequence proposed by Natta et al. $^{[1]}$ Moreover, packing energy calculations were performed by imposing the screw two-fold axis as crystallographic symmetry element and analyzing the modes of packing in

all orthorhombic and monoclinic space groups containing the $2_1$ symmetry element. Those calculations were carried out as maps and minimizations of the energy as a function of the parameters of the unit cell, with the exception of $c$, of the translation [$z$] along the chain axis and of the rotation [$\omega$] around the chain axis. The lowest energy was found for the $P2_12_12_1$ space group. It is important to note that the map of the packing energy as a function of $z$ and $\omega$ for this space group, calculated with the best values of the $a$ and $b$ axes, exhibited two minima of different energies separated by a high barrier. On the basis of considerations concerning the ability of potential functions to reproduce the unit cell parameters, the final predicted values of the $a$ and $b$ axes were 9.47 and 5.97 $\mathring{A}$, respectively.

In the last few years, the use of catalytic systems based on various transition metals and the choice of suitable temperatures of polymerization have allowed the synthesis of polydienes having high degrees of constitutional and configurational regularity.$^{[7-9]}$ Recently, Purevsuren et al. reported on the synthesis, NMR solution characterization, and crystal structure of icPP prepared with neodymium-catalytic systems.$^{[10]}$ The polymer resulted to have 95% $cis$ content, clearly isotactic structure, and high molecular weight. By analyzing the X-ray fiber spectra of the synthesized polymer, independently of the data reported in ref.,$^{[6]}$ the authors proposed the same space group and unit cell and conformational parameters very similar to those predicted in ref.$^{[6]}$

In this paper, a comparison between the previously calculated crystal structure and the experimental crystal structure is reported for the first time. Moreover, new molecular mechanics calculations performed by present computer programs are reported.

### Simulations
The X-ray powder profiles and fiber spectra have been simulated by the *Analytical 1* module of the Cerius$^2$ program by Accelrys. Crystallite size $a=200$ $\mathring{A}$, $b=200$ $\mathring{A}$, and $c=100$ $\mathring{A}$ and $a=150$ $\mathring{A}$, $b=150$ $\mathring{A}$, and $c=150$ $\mathring{A}$ have been selected for the powder and fiber spectra, respectively. The global isotropic thermal factor $B=20$ $\mathring{A}^2$ has been used. Orientation half-width $=5^\circ$ has been chosen in the simulations of the fiber spectra. Molecular mechanics calculations have been carried out by the *off methods* module of the same program by *smart minimizer* method with *standard convergence*. The MM2_85 force field$^{[11]}$ has been adopted.

The crystal structure reported by Purevsuren et al. $^{[10]}$ substantially validates the previously calculated crystal structure. $^{[6]}$ The different arrangements of the chains in the two structures could not be discriminated on the basis of the X-ray powder profile available at the time, $^{[11]}$ while the recent X-ray data clarify that the best mode of packing is similar to model B. The previous attribution of the mode of packing to model A was due to the fact that the method of calculation did not take into account simultaneous varia- tions of conformational and packing parameters and consi- dered the methyl groups as single units, at variance with the current optimizations. However, the presence in the energy map of two minima, corresponding to model A and model B, could also indicate that the formation of either kind of packing is determined by kinetic factors and may suggest the presence of disorder, which agrees well with the high value of the isotropic temperature factor $(B=20 \AA^{2})$ in the experimentally proposed crystal structure. $^{[10]}$ This sugges tion is supported by the observations of the intensities of the reflections having indices (101), (111), and (210) at $2 \theta \sim 14.3^{\circ}, \sim 20.6^{\circ}$ , and $\sim 23.9^{\circ}$ , respectively, which dis play the main differences between the experimental crystal structure and model A. In fact, the calculated structure factor of the (111) reflection, which is more intense in model A than in the experimental crystal structure, is lower than the observed structure factor, $^{[10]}$ while the contrary happens for the (101) and (210) reflections, which are less intense in model A than in the experimental crystal structure.

## Conclusion

The following conclusions can be drawn: (i) the predictive power of molecular mechanics is confirmed by the agreement between the calculated and the experimentally determined crystal structure; (ii) the new calculations are in better accordance with the experimental data as far as the mode of packing of the chains is concerned; however, the energy difference between model A and model B is quite low (12.6 and $4.8 \mathrm{~kJ} \cdot \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$ of monomeric unit before and after the optimizations, respectively, calculated by the MM2_85 force field); (iii) the comparison between the intensities of the reflections in the simulated and in the experimental X-ray spectra and the new energy calculations suggest that the two best modes of packing previously found, i.e., model A and model B, could coexist in the actual crystal structure of icPP, the latter being present in a prevailing amount.
