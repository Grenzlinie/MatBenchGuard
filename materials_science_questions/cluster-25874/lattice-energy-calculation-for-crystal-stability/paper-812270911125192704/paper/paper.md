# Lattice Dynamics of Solid $\text{CS}_2$ Based on Kihara Potential

Yukio SHIBATA, Kimihiro ISHI and Toshiaki OHSAKA

Research Institute for Scientific Measurements,
Tohoku University, Aoba-ku, Sendai 980

(Received May 10, 1989)

The lattice dynamics of molecular crystal $\text{CS}_2$ is studied within the rigid molecule and harmonic approximations, on the basis of Kihara core potential. Frequencies of lattice vibrations, dispersion curves, density of states and heat capacity at low temperatures are calculated. The results are in good agreement with experimental data and also compared with previous calculations. A role of nonaxisymmetric short-range interactions is discussed.

lattice dynamics, molecular crystal, $\text{CS}_2$, Kihara potential, density of states, heat capacity

## §1. Introduction
Crystalline carbon disulphide is one of typical molecular crystals made up of linear triatomic molecules. The crystal structure is the one-face centered orthorhombic described by space group $Cmca$ ($\text{D}_{2\text{h}}^{18}$), with two molecules per primitive unit cell (Fig. 1). Solid $\text{CS}_2$ has no phase transition from melting point (161.7 K) to 5.3 K.¹⁾

There have been several spectroscopic studies on solid $\text{CS}_2$ and lattice vibrational frequencies are known to some extent, though the mode assignments are somewhat ambiguous. Far infrared absorption spectra,²⁾ Raman scattering³⁾ at low temperatures and inelastic neutron scattering spectra at 148 K⁴⁾ were measured on solid $\text{CS}_2$. Temperature dependence of the infrared active translational modes was also studied.⁵⁾

The lattice dynamics of solid $\text{CS}_2$ was studied by several researchers. Main effort has been concentrated on finding appropriate intermolecular potential to explain both the crystal structural data and lattice vibrational frequencies. In particular, Grout and Leech⁶⁾⁷⁾ investigated the properties of nonbonded atom-atom interactions: three pairs of C-C, C-S, and S-S atoms. They gave a variety of intermolecular potentials with variable weights of each atom pair, which showed fairly good agreement with spectroscopic data. However, none of the potential models could account for both the structural data and the lattice vibrational frequencies. Burgos and Righini⁸⁾ used a potential model including anisotropic atom-atom interactions and higher order multipole interactions. Though the model can account for both the structural data and observed frequencies of the lattice modes at the zone center, physical meaning of the potential seems to be vague. Recent results by Higgs and Anderson,⁹⁾ who used a simple force constant model, suggested that anisotropic S-S repulsive force played an essential role to explain the lattice vibrational frequencies.

Kobashi and Kihara¹⁰⁾¹¹⁾ studied theoretically molecular librations of solid $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$ under high pressures, on the basis of Kihara core potential. In the Kihara potential (see Fig. 1), a molecular shape is represented by the electron charge density contour¹²⁾ of 0.002 a.u. and the contour is approximated by a parallel body surrounding a hard convex core; the potential is given as a function of the minimum distance between the cores, each of which corresponds to the molecular axis. Their results showed that the potential used had a reasonable distance dependence particularly for the repulsive force. It suggests that the Kihara core potential is applicable to solid $\text{CS}_2$, since both $\text{CS}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$ are linear

In this paper, lattice dynamics of solid CS₂ is treated throughout the Brillouin zone (BZ) within the framework of classical harmonic and rigid molecule approximations, on the basis of Kihara core potential. The results are compared with experimental data of lattice vibrational frequencies, heat capacity and crystal structure.

## §2. Calculation

The intermolecular potential used consists of both Kihara core potential and quadrupole-quadrupole interaction.¹²)

$$
U=U_{0}\left[\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho}\right)^{12}-2\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho}\right)^{6}\right]+U_{Q Q}. \tag{1}
$$

Here, $\rho$ is the minimum distance between the rod cores, and is given as a function of the distance between molecular centers, rod length $(L)$ and orientations of molecular axis (see Appendix).

As an initial set of potential parameters $U_{0}$, $\rho_{0}$ and $L$, we used the values determined by Kihara¹³⁾ from the second virial coefficient: $U_{0}/k=680$ K, $\rho_{0}=3.60$ A and $L=3.078$ A. The three parameters were adjusted to fit the calculated frequencies of lattice vibrations to the observed ones. Value of the quadrupole moment used was $-3.1 \times 10^{-26}$ esu.¹⁴⁾ The lattice parameters at $5.3$ K¹⁾ were used in the calculation.

In the calculation, interactions between molecules within 6.2 A (16 molecules) were taken into account. §3. Results and Discussion

3.1 Lattice vibrations and dispersion curves

The vibrational frequencies calculated at the zone center are given in Table I with the observed ones at low temperatures; the mode assignments of observed frequencies are those of Higgs and Anderson⁹) for Raman spectrum and of Ishi and Takahashi²) for IR spectrum. In Table I are also shown calculations reported by other researchers. On the whole, the frequencies calculated here are in good agreement with the observed values except for the $A_u$ mode.

In solid CS₂, the configuration of the nearest neighbor molecules is nearly "T" shape in a layered plane, and that of the second nearest neighbors is slipped parallel between neighboring layers (see Fig. 1). Through the parameter adjustment, variation of the parameter $L$ has anisotropic effects: Variation of $L$ makes the minimum distance between the nearest neighbors change, but does not affect the minimum distance between the second nearest neighbors. On the other hand, variations in $\rho_0$ and $U_0$ affect the potential for all pairs irrespective to its geometric arrangement. A frequency ratio $(=(\nu(B_{1u})+\nu(B_{2u}))/2\nu(A_u))$ is, in our experience, slowly varying functions of the parameters; hence we were not able to explain both the frequencies of the $A_u$ mode and the other translational modes satisfactory, within a range of 3.6 A $\leqq \rho_0 \leqq$ 4.1 A. Since motion of the $A_u$ mode is vertical to the layered plane, this suggests that there should be nonaxisymmetric short-range interactions, i.e. repulsive force is biased to the pairs between neighboring layers relative to the pairs in a layered plane (or repulsive force is weakened to some extent in a layered plane). This nonaxisymmetric repulsive force does not conflict with character of the simple force constant model.⁹) In solid CS₂, the charge density contour of $\pi$ electrons should not be axisymmetry; hence it is natural that interactions between molecules in the neighboring layers are more or less different from interactions between molecules in a layered plane. Influence of these nonaxisymmetric interactions was not taken into account explicitly in both the potential used here and the nonbonded atom-atom interaction potentials.⁶,¹⁵) It is plausible that potentials, not including this nonaxisymmetry, cause calculated frequency of the $A_u$ mode lower. Since the potential parameters are adjusted to some extent for this weakened repulsive interactions in a layered plane, it makes the motion of the $A_u$ mode (out-of-plane) weak.

### 3.2 Heat capacity
The density of states of lattice vibrational frequencies was obtained by counting the number of lattice modes with a resolution of 1 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$; vibrational frequencies were calculated for 1000 wavevectors uniformly scattered in BZ (wavevector $q_{x}, q_{y}, q_{z} \geqq 0$ ), on the basis of computer-generated pseudo random number. The heat capacity at constant volume $C_{\mathrm{v}}$ was calculated in the usual manner from the density of states $G(v)$.

$$
C_{\mathrm{v}}=5 N_{0} k \int \frac{x^{2} \exp (x)}{(\exp (x)-1)^{2}} G(v) \mathrm{d} v, \quad(2)
$$

where $x=h v / k T$. The density of states and the heat capacity are given in Figs . 3 and 4, respectively. In Fig. 4 are also shown the experimental values. $^{16)}$ Since difference between the heat capacities $C_{\mathrm{v}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{p}}$ is small at low temperatures, the calculated values $\left(C_{\mathrm{v}}\right)$ are compared with the experimental ones $\left(C_{\mathrm{p}}\right)$ without any correction. The calculation is, on the whole, in accordance with the experiment. However, at low temperatures $(\lesssim 20 \mathrm{~K})$, the calculated heat capacity is larger than the measured values. The heat capacity for temperatures below $20 \mathrm{~K}$ is mainly determined by the lower part $(<40$ $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ ) of the density of states. The calculated density of states seems to be biased to lower frequencies, presumably caused from lower frequencies of the branch through the $A_{u}$ mode at the zone center.

### 3.3 Lattice parameters
By using the potential parameters determined, lattice constants and orientation angle ($\theta_{\mathrm{c}}$) of the molecular axis (see Fig. 1) were calculated to minimize the crystal energy, provided that the structure of solid CS₂ was orthorhombic. The results are given in Table II. The calculated lattice constants and molecular orientation show agreement with the structural data at high temperatures (150 K) rather than the ones at low temperatures (5.3 K), though the potential parameters were determined to fit the frequencies obtained at low temperatures.

Ignoring the $U_{QQ}$ term, the similar calculations were repeated on the lattice vibrational frequencies and crystal energy. The vibrational frequencies and the lattice parameters have suffered a minor change, confirming previous results that the quadrupole-quadrupole interaction does not play an essential role in solid CS₂.⁶)

### Appendix: Minimum Distance between Rod Cores
We consider the minimum distance between two rod cores $i$ and $j$ in an arbitrary arrangement (see Fig. A·1). Let $\boldsymbol{n}_{i}$ and $\boldsymbol{n}_{j}$ stand for unit vectors along rod cores $i$ and $j$, and $\boldsymbol{n}_{r}$ the unit vector connecting the core centers from $i$ to $j$. Suppose that points $Q_{i}$ and $Q_{j}$, specified by position vectors $p_{i}\boldsymbol{n}_{i}$ and $p_{j}\boldsymbol{n}_{j}$ from the each center of cores, give the minimum distance between the rod cores; points $Q_{i}'$ and $Q_{j}'$ specified by $p_{i}'\boldsymbol{n}_{i}$ and $p_{j}'\boldsymbol{n}_{j}$ give the minimum distance between the straight lines including the rod cores. Then, $p_{i}'$ and $p_{j}'$ are given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{i}^{\prime}= & \frac{r}{2}\left\{\frac{\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{i} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{r}\right)+\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{j} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{r}\right)}{1+\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{i} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{j}\right)}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{i} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{r}\right)-\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{j} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{r}\right)}{1-\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{i} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{j}\right)}\right\},
\end{aligned} \qquad (\text{A·1})
$$

$$

where $r$ is the distance between the centers of the cores. When each of $|p_{i}^{\prime}|$ and $|p_{j}^{\prime}|$ is less than $L/2$, the minimum distance between the cores is given by length $Q_{i}^{\prime}Q_{j}^{\prime}$. Otherwise, the larger value between $|p_{i}^{\prime}|$ and $|p_{j}^{\prime}|$ should be fixed to $L/2$. Then the minimum distance is obtained as follows, provided that $|p_{i}^{\prime}|>|p_{j}^{\prime}|$.

$$
p_{j}^{\prime \prime}=\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{i} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{j}\right) p_{i}-r\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{j} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{r}\right),
\tag{A·3}
$$

$$
p_{j}=\operatorname{sign}\left(\min \left(\left|p_{j}^{\prime \prime}\right|, L / 2\right), p_{j}^{\prime \prime}\right),
\tag{A·4}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho^{2}= & r^{2}+2 r\left[p_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{j} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{r}\right)-p_{i}\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{i} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{r}\right)\right] \\
& +p_{i}^{2}-2 p_{i} p_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{n}_{i} \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{j}\right)+p_{j}^{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A·5}
$$

In case that two molecular axes are parallel, some denominators in eqs. (A·1) and (A·2) become zero. Then, terms including the denominators should be neglected. Furthermore, it should be noticed that values of first derivatives with respect to some arguments are discontinuous at just parallel arrangement as seen in solid $\mathrm{CS}_{2}$. Then, appropriate interpolation is necessary in calculating the first derivatives to obtain the dynamical matrix elements.
