# Structural, thermochemical and detonation performance of derivatives of 1,2,4,5-tetrazine and 1,4 $N$-oxide 1,2,4,5-tetrazine as new high-performance and nitrogen-rich energetic materials

Akbar Gholami Roknabadi¹ · Mohammad Hossein Keshavarz¹ · Karim Esmailpour¹ · Mehdi Zamani²

Received: 6 July 2015 / Accepted: 5 August 2016
© Iranian Chemical Society 2016

**Abstract** In this study, density functional theory calculations are used to estimate enthalpy of sublimation, enthalpy of formation and crystal density of some important derivatives of 1,2,4,5-tetrazine and 1,4 $N$-oxide 1,2,4,5-tetrazine. These data were used for predicting their detonation properties including heat of detonation, detonation pressure, detonation velocity, detonation temperature, spark sensitivity, deflagration temperature and power of energetic using appropriate methods. The results show that the title compounds exhibit high positive solid-phase enthalpy of formation. It is found that detonation pressure and detonation velocity of these compounds are high because of the large values of crystal density and solid-phase enthalpy of formation. Detonation temperature and spark sensitivity of some derivatives are higher than octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine as one of the high-performance explosives.

**Keywords** Explosive · Enthalpy of formation · Density · DFT

## Introduction

The Study of the high-energy-density materials (HEDMs) has been a great amount of interest over the resent years [1–10]. In particular, energetic high-nitrogen organic compounds are promising candidates for HEDMs because of their superior energetic outputs over the currently used materials. Six-membered nitrogen-rich heterocyclic compounds such as 1,2,4,5-tetrazines have found widespread application in high-energy material chemistry [11]. It is believed that this class of compounds is an effective substituent for enhancing the detonation performance [12]. Recently, many researches deal with incorporation of the heterocyclic rings or energetic groups into 1,2,4,5-tetrazine to construct complex molecules in order to further improve the energetic properties (Fig. 1). A series of energetic derivatives of 1,2,4,5-tetrazine (1) and 1,4 $N$-oxide 1,2,4,5-tetrazine (2) are introduced and compared their properties with octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine (HMX) in this work. The new high-performance energetic compounds are based on 3,6-bis(1H-1,2,3,4-tetrazolyl-5-amino)-1,2,4,5-tetrazine (BTATz) (3), where the method of its synthesis is given elsewhere [13–15]. Compound BTATz can be oxidized with Caro's acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_5$) to form the 1,4-di-$N$-oxide derivative (4). Molecular structures of these compounds are given in Fig. 2.

If the condensed phase enthalpy of formation and crystal density of these compounds are measured or estimated, their detonation performance may be calculated using the computer codes, e.g., EXPLO5 [16], ICT [17], BKW [18], NASA-CEC-71 [19], CHEETAH [20] and EDPHT [21, 22], or using empirical methods. A variety of reliable empirical models have been recently introduced to investigate the performance of various important classes of energetic compounds [23–28].

The accurate calculation of the condensed phase enthalpy of formation and crystal density are two main factors for estimating the performance of explosives. The calculated enthalpies of sublimation can be combined with gas phase enthalpies of formation to predict enthalpies of formation at solid phase. ### Computational details

Geometry optimization and frequency calculations of title compounds were done at B3LYP/6-311G(d,p) level of theory using Gaussian-09 software package [49], which is one of the widely used density functionals for this pur- pose [50]. For all optimized structures, there are no imagi- nary frequencies so that the electrostatic potential analysis(ESP) was done at the $0.001 e / Bohr^{3}$ isosurface of electron density. The ESP maps were also plotted by B3LYP/6-311G(d,p) methods. The positive, negative and overall potentials on the surface $(\bar{V}_{S}^{+}, \bar{V}_{S}^{-}$ , and $\bar{V}_{S}$,$) were defined by Eqs. (1)-(3), where V_{S}(r)$ is an electrostatic potential on the surface:

$$
\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}=\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} V_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}(r)
\tag{1}
$$

$$
\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{-}=\frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^{n} V_{\mathrm{S}}^{-}(r)
\tag{2}
$$

$$
\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{1}{m+n}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n} V_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}(r)+\sum_{i=1}^{m} V_{\mathrm{S}}^{-}(r)\right]
\tag{3}
$$

The positive, negative and total variances on the molecu- lar surface $(\sigma_{+}^{2}, \sigma_{-}^{2}$ , and $\sigma_{tot }^{2})$ , which reveal the range and variability of $V_{S}(r)$ , were calculated by Eq. (4):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}=\sigma_{+}^{2}+\sigma_{-}^{2}= & \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[V_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}(r)-\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}\right]^{2} \\
& +\frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[V_{\mathrm{S}}^{-}(r)-\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{-}\right]^{2}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

The average deviation $(\Pi)$ of $V_{S}(r)$ and the electrostatic balance parameter $(v)$ were expressed by Eqs. (5) and (6):

$$
\Pi \frac{1}{m+n} \sum_{i=1}^{m+n}\left|V_{S}(r)-\overline{V}_{S}\right|
\tag{5}
$$

$$
v=\frac{\sigma_{+}^{2} \sigma_{-}^{2}}{\left[\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}\right]^{2}}
\tag{6}
$$

These molecular surface properties along with the molecular surface area $(A_{s})$ were used for estimating the enthalpy of sublimation $(\Delta H_{Sub}^{\circ})$ of title C, H, N, O compounds via Eq. (7) [36]:

$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{Sub}}^{\circ}=0.000267\left(A_{S}\right)^{2}+1.650087\left(v \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}\right)^{0.5}+2.966078
\tag{7}
$$

Equation (8) was used to calculate the crystal density $(\rho)$ of title compounds, where $M$ and $V_{m}$ are molecular mass in $g /$ molecule and molecular volume in $cm^{3} /$ molecule, respec tively [48].

## Results and discussion

The details of ESPs such as values of $\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+},\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{-},\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}},\sigma_{+}^{2},\sigma_{-}^{2},\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2},v$ and $\Pi$ as well as $V_{\mathrm{m}},A_{\mathrm{s}},\rho$ data are listed in Table 1. It is found that $\mathrm{NO}_{2},\mathrm{NH}_{2}\mathrm{NO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{N}_{3}$ groups improve the values of molecular surface area and volume. The overall average electrostatic potentials on the surface ($\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}$) for compounds **1–10** are in the range of 3.4 (compound **7**) to 7.5 kcal/mol (compound **1**) at B3LYP level of theory. The maximum difference between negative and positive potentials ($\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{-}$ and $\overline{V}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}$) among these compounds was calculated for compound **4** ($-12.1$ and 21.7 kcal/mol, respectively). The maximum value of the average deviation of electrostatic potential on the surface ($\Pi$) is also predicted for this compound (24.4 kcal/ mol). This parameter reflects the local polarity internal charge separation. The maximum value of electrostatic balance parameter among studied compounds is 0.246 which belongs to compounds **7** and **8**. The product of $v\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}$ describes properties that are related to the interactions of a molecule with others of its own kind. The maximum and minimum values of $v\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}$ were calculated for compounds **2** and **4**, respectively (80.3 and $44.8\ \mathrm{kcal}^{2}/\mathrm{mol}^{2}$).

Explosive performance of energetic compounds depends on the values of $\rho$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(s)$. The calculated values of molecular surface area, molecular volume and $v\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}$ presented in Table 1 can be employed for estimation of $\Delta H_{\mathrm{sub}}^{\circ}$ and $\rho$ of explosives **1–10** using Eqs. (7) and (8). Among explosives **1–10**, the order of $\boldsymbol{7>8>3>4}$ is found for $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(s)$. The values of $\rho$ for all of them are close to each other. The presence of nitro, azide and nitramide groups on these compounds improves $\rho$ and positive value of $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(s)$; therefore, the detonation performance for these explosives will be improved.

Table 1 lists $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(g),\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(s)$ of the new energetic compounds. Obviously, they have high positive values of $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(s)$ (164.4–377.4), which confirm that the derivatives have high energy extent. Table 2 lists the detonation performance and sensitivity of these compounds as compared to HMX (octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine) and TATB (triaminotrinitrobenzene). Detonation performance parameters include heat of detonation, detonation velocity, detonation pressure and detonation temperature. Sensitivity of the compounds with respect to electric spark ($E_{\mathrm{ES}}$) and heat (DT = deflagration temperature) is given in Table 2.

Existence of $N$-oxide group decreases $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(g),\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}$ (s) of the new energetic compounds. Compounds **7** and **3** have the maximum value of $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\circ}(s)$ among the studied compounds (377.4 and 309.6, respectively). Addition of $\mathrm{N}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{NO}_{2}$ groups in compound **7** increases the energy content of compound. Similarly compound **3** with the nitro tetrazole group has the high energy content.

The existence of $N$-oxide group increases the density of compounds. The maximum density is related to compound **6** ($1.96\ \mathrm{g/cm^{3}}$) and minimum density belongs to compound **1** ($1.79\ \mathrm{g/cm^{3}}$).

The higher values of detonation velocity and detonation pressure are denoted to compounds **4** and **6**. Detonation velocity of compounds **6** and **4** are 9.15 and 9.10 km/s, respectively. Detonation pressures of these compounds are also 404 and 398 kbar, respectively. The highest detonation temperature belongs to compound **8** (5826 K). Compound **2** has the lowest detonation temperature (3869 K). Compounds **6** and **4** have high powers (343 and 298 %TNT, respectively), which are consistent with the calculated their detonation velocity and pressure. Compound **1** has minimum value of power (13 %TNT). As seen in Table 2, compounds **6** and **4** have heats of detonation 5.42 and 5.88 kJ/ mol, respectively, which are close that of HMX (5.73 kJ/ mol).

The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMO) are very important parameters to govern the chemical reaction of the compounds [59]. The band gap energy ($\Delta E_{\mathrm{gap}}$) between LUMO and HOMO is important for stability concern of the explosives. These values for compounds **1–10** are listed in Table 1. High value of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{gap}}$ is associated with a low chemical reactivity and high kinetic stability [60, 61]. Therefore, compound **3** is more stable while compound **8** less stable. Thus, compound **3** may be kinetically more stable and chemically less active than the other compounds. ## Conclusion

Some new nitrogen-rich derivatives of 1,2,4,5-tetrazine and 1,4 $N$-oxide 1,2,4,5-tetrazine have been introduced in current study. Detonation performance, sensitivity and some of thermodynamic properties have been investigated through both quantum mechanical and empirical approaches. Among 10 different energetic compounds, the study of sensitivity and detonation performance of these compounds indicate that 6 and 4 have favorable characteristics: (1) They have high detonation performance, (2) their power are high and consistent with their detonation performance, (3) these compounds have higher performance and power than HMX as one of the well-known high-performance explosives, and (4) their sensitivities with respect to electric spark are moderate.
