# Lattice energy calculation and packing optimization of H3N–BF3 crystal

## Problem background
Predicting the packing structures and lattice energies of molecular crystals is important for materials science, yet remains a challenge due to the need for accurate, balanced descriptions of diverse intermolecular interactions. This is especially true for electron donor–acceptor (EDA) complexes, where classical force fields struggle with strong, anisotropic charge-transfer interactions. This work proposes a direct ab initio molecular orbital (MO) method that computes the lattice energy without adjustable parameters, incorporating many-body polarization effects and electron correlation via MP2 perturbation theory. The method is applied to the exemplary EDA complex H3N–BF3 crystal to compute its optimized crystal structure and energetic properties.

## Approach
The lattice energy is expressed as a sum of one-body energies (deformation + polarization) and pair interaction energies between molecules. At the Hartree–Fock (HF) level, the polarization energy is obtained by iteratively solving the HF equations for each unique molecule in the crystal under the electrostatic potential generated by the Mulliken charges of all surrounding molecules, updating charges until self-consistency. Pair interaction energies are computed for all symmetry-unique molecular pairs within a cutoff radius; the already-counted polarization contribution is subtracted from each pair energy to avoid double counting. The total HF lattice energy is then assembled from the one-body and corrected two-body terms. The crystal packing is optimized by numerically minimizing this lattice energy with respect to the lattice constants, fractional coordinates, the intermolecular B–N distance, and molecular orientations. After obtaining the optimized geometry at the HF/3-21+G level, the lattice energy and the major pair energies are recalculated at the MP2/6-311+G* level using the same polarization+pair-energy approach, treating the H3N–BF3 complex as the asymmetric unit. The crystal is modeled in the Pbca space group with 8 complexes per unit cell; NH3 and BF3 are treated as rigid bodies with C3v symmetry, their geometries taken from the experimental crystal structure.
