# Magnetisation depth profiles in trilayer systems with mixed-spin interfaces-a contribution to temperature dependent GMR-potentials

G. Wiatrowski $^{a, *}$ , D. Baldomir $^{b}$ , K. Warda $^{a}$ , M. Pereiro $^{b}$ , L. Wojtczak $^{a}$ , J.E. Arias $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Solid State Physics Department, University of Lodz, Pomorska 149/153, 90236 Lodz, Poland
$^{b}$ Departamento de Fisica Aplicada, Universidad de Santiago de Compostela, 15706 Santiago de Compostela, Spain

Received 22 May 2003; received in revised form 10 November 2003

## Abstract
We discuss the magnetic properties of trilayer $A(n)/A_{c}B_{1-c}/B(p)/A_{c}B_{1-c}/A'(m)$-structure with two mixed-spin interfaces $A_{c}B_{1-c}$ ($S^{A}=1$, $S^{B}=\frac{1}{2}$) for arbitrary concentration $c$ of A-spins and varied partial film thickness $(n,p,m)$. The description is obtained by means of an effective-field approach which is superior to the standard molecular-field treatment. Temperature dependent magnetisation depth profiles of trilayer structure vs. concentration $c$ of A-atoms are presented in the whole range of reduced temperature. The existence of enhanced interface magnetism is also discussed using the phase diagrams in $J^{AB}-c$ plane. Depth profiles are strongly inhomogeneous in the interface region and thus they lead to complex interface potentials contributing significantly to GMR-effect.

## 1. Introduction

Recently, there has been considerable interest in the magnetic properties of multilayer (ML) struc- tures owing to their particular technical applica- tions [1]. The studies of the last decade commercialised giant magnetoresistant layered materials (GMR) significantly. Now, it is well known that the magnetisation behaviour in the interface region is one of the most important features, but its knowledge still needs further investigations [2-7]. Already two decades ago, Wimmer, Freeman, Fu, Erskine and others showed us the existence of 2D-interface magnetism instead of surface (and interface) magnetically "dead" layers suggested earlier by theoreticians but not found by experimentalists [8-10]. Since then many experimental evidences have indicated a macro- scopic contribution of surface/interface properties. For example, the GMR-signal in NiFeCo/Cu ML-structure exhibits high thermal stability with thicker magnetic layers that have been assigned to

On the other hand, very recently, we have learned that the magnitude of GMR effect measured in trilayer systems is more or less affected (usually it fluctuates or even disappears) when the partial thickness of either the spacer or the magnetic layers is changed accordingly [11,12]. Moreover, it occurs that the contribution of additional scattering centres due to the alloy structure of magnetic overlayers or the spacer film can significantly increase the GMR- ratio [13,14]. In contrast to standard calculations by Hasegawa [15] and other experimental evidences of temperature destruction of GMR effect [16], in alloyed ML, like $Fe_{1-x} Cr_{x} / Cr(001)$ , the GMR effect can even increase with temperature assigned to spin-dependent electron-phonon scattering [14]. It is thus evident that the temperature dependent magnetisation distribution across the ML struc- ture, mostly in the interface region, can be one of the most relevant parameters for GMR effects. That characteristic is still not known enough from the experimental point of view (besides of non- linear MOKE [17] and spin-wave resonance possibilities [18]) as well as from the theoretical ab initio calculations (especially when the finite temperature properties are considered [19]). To fill this gap we propose to apply the approximate but analytical method to describe the magnetisation distribution profile (MDP) across the trilayer structure that will let us to estimate the interface state contribution to the problems in question.

Thus, we present our analytical results of one- atom cluster approximation (1-ACA) based on the exact Callen equations extended for the case of stochastic spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ and spin-1 alloy structure in the form of trilayer systems [4,20,21]. The method is superior to the standard molecular field approach and thus it is particularly well suited for the description of mixed-spins as well as layered systems [22-24]. Very recently, a similar effective field approach has been applied to the simplest spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ ML with double-plane interface considered as a stochastic binary alloy [25]. The phase diagrams were obtained and compared with the experimental evidences for Fe/Gd-ML system leading to the estimation of interface exchange interaction. Moreover, the 1-ACA method has also been successfully applied to spin-enhanced (i.e. spin-mixed) both surface and interface pro- blems in a single film and bilayer structures, respectively. It allowed us to describe the thermal reversal of magnetisation profiles in thin Fe-films in the case when the concentration of surface high- spin component did not exceed the critical value that has been well defined according to the discussed $T_{cr}-c$ phase diagram [20]. Further, we analysed also the variation of magnetisation depth profiles in heterostructure bilayer systems [4]. Below, the thermal variation of MDP across magnetic-nonmagnetic trilayer structure shows us the possibility of the existence of complex enhanced magnetic phases in the interface region. Moreover, the reversal of MDP from concave to the convex one is also found and assigned to the strong inter-compound exchange interaction as well as to the interface diffusion processes-the origin of interface spin-mixing considered. Finally, the discussion is completed with the phase diagrams in $J-c$ coordinates.

The obtained MDP contribution to electronic potentials should be understood as a background for our recent general analytical studies on the quantum electronic transport (extended Hood- Falicov theory [26]) in the case of GMR in layeredmagnetic-nonmagnetic structures (e.g. Fe/Cr/Fe) with current in-plane geometry considered [3].

## 2. Outline of the model

Below we consider a stochastic mixture of (A) spin-1 and (B) spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ components in the form ofmagnetic trilayer $A(n)/A_{c} B_{1-c}/B(p)/A_{c} B_{1-c}/A(m)$  with s.c. symmetry composed of a finite number, $n,p$, and $m$, of monatomic layers, respectively. The local $(v,j)$-site Ising Hamiltonian for such system is given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{v j}= & -\sum_{(\mu k) \equiv(v j)} \sum_{r=\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}} J_{v j ; \mu k}^{r r} \xi_{v j}^{r} \xi_{\mu k}^{r} S_{v j}^{r} S_{\mu k}^{r} \\
& -\sum_{(\mu k) \equiv(v j)} J_{v j ; \mu k}^{\mathrm{AB}} \xi_{v j}^{A} \xi_{\mu k}^{\mathrm{B}} S_{v j}^{\mathrm{A}} S_{\mu k}^{\mathrm{B}}+\delta_{p \mathrm{~A}} D\left(\xi_{v j}^{\mathrm{A}} S_{v j}^{\mathrm{A}}\right)^{2}, \\
& (1)
\end{aligned}
$$


where subsequent terms describe the exchange interactions in separated films, the interface regions and crystal-field contribution to the energy of the system in the form of uniaxial perpendicular anisotropy usually found in very thin magnetic films. The summations run over all pairs of nearest neighbour sites while each of them is labelled with the number of plane ($v$) and in-plane vector ($j$), respectively.

The method considered is based on Callen equations extended to take into account the stochastic spin-mixed magnetic structure introduced via stochastic operators $\xi_{vj}^r$ [4,20,22,24]. We impose only the obvious restriction condition: $\xi_{vj}^{\text{A}} + \xi_{vj}^{\text{B}} = 1$, where $\xi_{vj}^r$ takes the value 1 or 0, depending on whether or not the $(v,j)$-site is occupied by the spin $S_{vj}^r$ ($r = A$, B). Fig. 1 shows the position of two (1 0 0)-interfaces in s.c.-trilayer $\text{A}(n)/\text{A}_c\text{B}_{1-c}/\text{B}(p)/\text{A}_c\text{B}_{1-c}/\text{A}(m)$ with exchange parameters: $J^{\text{AA}}(\equiv J)$, $J^{\text{AB}}$ and $J^{\text{BB}}$. Three films of thickness, $n,p$ and $m$ (counted in a number of monatomic layers, respectively) are coupled via the mixed-spin single-(SLI) or double-layer interfaces (DLI) where the proximity effect is modelled assuming a disordered binary mixture $\text{A}_c\text{B}_{1-c}$ of (A) spin-1 and (B) spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ compounds with assumed concentration $c$. After thermodynamical $(\langle \dots T \rangle)$ and configurational averaging $(\langle \xi_{vj}^{\text{A}} \rangle_c \equiv c_v)$ using the integral operator technique [4,20,27,28] we obtain the system of $2(n + p + m + l_I)$ nonlinear algebraic equations for the layered magnetisation $m_v = \langle \xi_{vj}^{\text{A}} \langle S_{vj}^{\text{A}} \rangle_T \rangle_c + \langle \xi_{vj}^{\text{B}} \langle S_{vj}^{\text{B}} \rangle_T \rangle_c$ and the auto-correlation function $q_v = \langle \xi_{vj}^{\text{A}} \langle (S_{vj}^{\text{A}})^2 \rangle_T \rangle_c$, which should be solved numerically in a self-consistent way ($l_I = 2$ for SLI or $l_I = 4$ for DLI). Then, for any reduced temperature ($y = k_{\text{B}}T/J$) and a particular set of structure parameters we obtain the MDP across the layered system. Our approach is consistent with the first-order Matsudaira approximation [28] in the case in which we assume the decoupling of all the next nearest neighbours and further spin-spin correlation functions $\langle \xi_{vj}^p \xi_{v'j'}^r \langle S_{vj}^p S_{v'j'}^r \rangle_T \rangle_c \approx \langle \xi_{vj}^p \langle S_{vj}^p \rangle_T \rangle_c \langle \xi_{v'j'}^r \langle S_{v'j'}^r \rangle_T \rangle_c$ without lost of the generality.

Recently, taking into account the relation between the Fermi level and the effective constant potential $V_{\text{HF}}(\infty)$ introduced previously by Hood and Falicov, we came to the conclusion that the interface potential is no longer a homogeneous parameter in the GMR theory [3]. The distribution of local magnetisation with the largest variation in the neighbourhood of each interface influences the effective potential leading to space dependent potential $V_{\sigma}(v)$ instead of constant potential $V_{\text{HF}}(\infty)$ considered in the simplified calculations
$$
V_{\sigma}(v) = -V_{\text{HF}}(\infty) \left[ 1 + \sigma \frac{\mu_{\text{B}}H + Km_v}{2V_{\text{HF}}(\infty)} \right], \tag{2}
$$
where $H$ is an external magnetic field, $K$ denotes the coupling constant between s and d band elecdtrons of the system and $\sigma$ is a label of minority $(-1)$ and majority $(+1)$ spins, respectively. Below,

we discuss the spatial variation of potentials (2) along stacking direction obtained according to MPD in the ML-structure considered.

## 3. Results and conclusions

The purpose of this paper is to describe in sufficient detail thermodynamic properties of magnetic-nonmagnetic trilayer systems with dis- ordered interfaces. The distinct feature of the realistic interface regions is the fact that they are composed of both kinds of elements originally occupying respective film volumes. It turns out that the state of interfaces plays often a crucial role when the scattering phenomena become relevant for effects in question like the GMR effect [5]. Varying the occupation of B-spins $(S^{B}=\frac{1}{2})$ in each of the interface regions we change the occupation number of electrons there, which, being polarised by the magnetic atoms, contribute to interface magnetisation. This change of the magnetisation depth profile influences the magne- toresistance effect in such a way that the electronic potential (2) becomes changed, too. For the first time we consider more realistic depth- and temperature-dependent electronic potential in- stead of a sequence of constant potential steps in the growth direction. In turn, the geometric in-plane invariance is assumed for simplicity; however, it can also be ruled out by taking into account the specific interface positional- roughness [6,29].

In the system presented, the magnetic overlayers are practically exchange uncoupled as far as we do not consider magnetic interlayer coupling (see Figs. 2a and 3a, where all curves of MDP have a common point located in the central layer). Thus, finite temperature calculations give us the equiva- lent profiles of magnetisation for both, antiparallel and parallel, configurations (drawn as $|m_{v}|$ in Figs. 2a and 3a). Here, the former plays the role of the ground state mainly due to minimisation of the magnetic energy and stray fields action like usually in domain structures. The results of our quantitative model should be understood in the sense mentioned above when discussed and compared with GMR experimental data.

The spatial, thermal and stoichiometric char- acterisation of MDP resulting from the 1-ACA method for trilayer structures $A(6)/A_{c}B_{1-c}/B(3)/$ $A_{c}B_{1-c}/A'(6)$ with the values of exchange cou- plings and film thickness unchanged are presented in Figs. 2a and 3a. Then, in Figs. 2b and 3b, we show the related variation of potentials (2) across the samples with the MDP calculated just above. The present method and numerical results for the variation of local magnetisation in the inter- face region could be easily applied to extend our recent studies on the Boltzmann equation ap- proach to the GMR-layered structures according to Eq. (2) [3]. The method [2] which is the most convenient for this purpose, giving the results with similar accuracy when compared to those obtained from the original Hood-Falicov formulation, should show the importance of interface profiles in order to evaluate GMR. In this way, the conditions concerning the interface influence on GMR, which have been earlier predicted by Camley and Barnas [30] in their fundamental description of GMR, are now confirmed. More- over, the obtained profiles model the interface influence on GMR via the potential and, first of all, they determine its physical nature. Namely, the magnetisation inhomogeneity represented by inter- face profiles seems to be an original source of magnetic roughness. This interpretation makes the present paper an important contribution to the GMR background discussion. On the other hand, we would like to stress that the results of the present model should be understood qualitatively and, as such, they may be a complementary input to other approaches being in certain points more accurate.

We notice after Brey et al. that the latest trends in spintronic applications of GMR-structures are focused on magnetic semiconductors (mainly GaAs doped slightly with Mn) in respect to their room temperature limited ordered magnetic phase [31,32].

Using the above results we will be able to describe the GMR phenomena accounted for thermally dependent electronic transport in M/N/ M MLs where the magnetic film M is composed of antiferromagnetic or even ferrimagnetic (disor- dered or diluted) material instead of a standard ferromagnetic one (for experimental evidences of these effects we refer the reader to Refs. [13,14,33]). The work is in progress and the application of the present results to GMR will be published in a next paper.
