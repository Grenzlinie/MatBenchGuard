## Abstract
We study two generalized FK models. The phase diagrams vary with the period of the external potential $D$ and with the amplitude of the perturbation $\varepsilon$. Some novel and complex phase structures are found. There are two threshold values of $D$ for each model. Within different intervals of $D$, the phase diagrams exhibit different behavior. © 1997 Elsevier Science B.V.

PACS: 64.60.Fr; 05.70.Fh; 05.70.Jk
Keywords: FK model; CI transition; Phase diagram; Rotation number; Winding number

## 1. Introduction
Modulated phases and commensurate-incommen- surate (CI) transitions occur in a wide variety of phys- ical systems [1,2]. The earliest as well as the simplest model proposed to study such modulated structures and CI transitions is the Frenkel-Kontorova (FK) [3] model. The standard and the generalized FK models are described by the following Hamiltonian,
$$H=\sum\left[V\left(U_{n}\right)+W\left(U_{n+1}-U_{n}\right)\right], \tag{1}$$
where $W(x)$ is the interatomic interaction, $V(x)$ is a periodic external potential, and $U_{n}$ is the position of the $n$th atom in the chain. The models with convex $W(x)$ have been extensively studied.

In order to study the nonconvex interactions, we [4] studied two generalized FK models, where the $W(x)$ are double-well functions and $V(x)$ is a piece wise parabolic function. To describe more clearly the modulated phases, we redefined two parameters: the winding number $\omega$ and the rotation number $\Omega$. Some complex but regular phase structures are shown in Ref. [4]. Those structures result from the double-well interatomic interactions. In Ref. [5], some new struc- tures result from the perturbed sinusoidal external po- tential. In order to study the CI transition in more de- tail, it is interesting to study the generalized FK model with multi-well interatomic interactions subject to a perturbed sinusoidal external potential. In this paper, we propose to study two models with the following $V(x)$ and $W(x)$,

\begin{aligned}
V(x) & =\frac{K}{(2 \pi)^{2}}[1-\cos (2 \pi x / D) \\

$$

$$

$$
W(x)=-\frac{1}{2}(x-\gamma)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(x-\gamma)^{4} \quad(\text { model } 2) . \quad(4)
$$

Some surprising novel phase structures are found.

The most significant difference between the two models is that the region of nonconvexity of $W(x)$ is of finite width $(|x-\gamma|<3^{-1 / 2})$ in model 2 , whereas it is limited to the nonanalytic point $x=\gamma$ in model 1 . The distance between the two wells of $W$ for model 1 is $l_{1}=1$ and that for model 2 is $l_{2}=2$. (For the physical backgrounds of $W(x)$, see Ref. [4], Eqs. (3) and (4).)

### 2. Method to study the ground states
Since $V$ is periodic and $W$ is nonconvex, we use the effective potential method [5] to study the ground states.

In the calculation of the effective potential $R$, we use $\left\{u_{n}\right\}$, instead of $\left\{U_{n}\right\}$, to describe the possible positions of the atoms. Here
$$
u_{n}=U_{n}+m_{n} D, \quad(5)
$$
$m_{n}$ is a appropriate integer which makes $u_{n}$ satisfy $-D / 2 \leqslant u_{n} \leqslant D / 2$. In Ref. [4], the winding number $\omega$ is defined as
$$
\omega=P / Q, \quad(6)
$$
where $Q$ is the period of the ground state, $P$ is the period number of the external potential between the first atom and the $(Q+1)$ th atom; the rotation number $\Omega$ is defined as
$$
\Omega=\frac{1}{Q} \sum_{i=1}^{Q} \Theta\left(u_{i-1}-u_{i}\right), \quad(7)
$$
where $\Theta(x)=1$ if $x \geqslant 0, \Theta(x)=0$ if $x<0$, and $u_{Q}=u_{0}$. These two parameters can present more information about the ground states.

The ground states of (1) with (2) and (3) or with (2) and (4) can be studied using a numerical solution of the eigenvalue equation
$$
\lambda+R\left(u_{n}\right)=V\left(u_{n}\right)+\min _{u_{n-1}}\left\{W\left(u_{n}-u_{n-1}\right)+R\left(u_{n-1}\right)\right\}
$$
for the unknown eigenvalue $\lambda$ and periodic eigenvector
$$
R\left(u_{n}+D\right)=R\left(u_{n}\right). \quad(9)
$$

The real chains of atoms can be recovered from $\left\{u_{n}\right\}$. (See Ref. [4] for more details.)

### 3. Phase diagrams of model 1
For model $1, W(x)$ has two equal minimum values at $x-\gamma= \pm 0.5$, respectively. When $\gamma \geqslant 0.5, W(x)$ can have either of them whereas when $\gamma<0.5, W(x)$ can only have the right one. Fig. 1 show the phase diagrams of model 1 with $\varepsilon=0.1$, where only the phases with $Q \leqslant 5$ are shown. The fraction numbers labelled in the figure are values of $\Omega$ of the corresponding "tongues".

In Figs. 1a and 1b, $D=0.5$. The two phase diagrams are just the same in appearance. Similar to the phase diagram given in Ref. [5], the simple "tongues" corresponding to the rational values of $\omega$ and $\Omega$ are split by a series of horizontal bars. The numerical results show that at the bars there is a phase transition between phases with the same rotation number but different symmetry. In the phases marked "A" the ground states may have atoms at the bottom, but never an atom at the top of the external potential. By contrast, in the phases marked "B" there are always atoms at the top of external potential. The B-type phase can not be stable (assuming the ground state is periodic) when $K$ is large enough. In fact, the structures of the ground states in Fig. 1a are not the same as those in Fig. 1b. In Fig. 1a, $\omega=1 / 1$ in the left one of the regions where $\Omega=1 / 1$, and in the other regions $\omega=(P+Q) / Q$ when $\Omega=P / Q$. For a given "tongue" in Fig. 1b, the value of $\Omega$ is unique while the value of $\omega$ may be not unique. Table 1 shows some examples.

When $\gamma \geqslant 0.5$ and $0.5<D \leqslant 1$, the phase diagrams are similar to those given in Fig. 1b. The main difference is that the height of corresponding horizontal bars increases with the increasing of $D$.

## 4. Phase diagrams of model 2

For model 2, $W(x)$ has two equal minimum values at $x-\gamma= \pm 1$, respectively. When $\gamma \geqslant 1, W(x)$ can have either of them while when $\gamma<1, W(x)$ can only have its right one, so the phase diagrams in the two cases may be different. Fig. 2 shows examples of phase diagram of model 2 with $\varepsilon=0.1$. One can clearly find this property from Figs. 2a and 2b. $D=1$ and $0 \leqslant$
$\gamma \leqslant 1$ in Fig. 2a. $D=1$ and $1 \leqslant \gamma \leqslant 2$ in Fig. 2b. The phase diagram in Fig. 2b is symmetric about the line $\gamma=1.5$, while the phase diagram in Fig. 2a is not symmetric. In Fig. 2a, all the phases with $Q>1$ vanish when $K>K_{\mathrm{c}} \approx 7.8$. A important difference between the two figures and Figs. 1a, 1b is that the "bars" are not horizontal for most phases in most cases.

When $\gamma \geqslant 1$ and $D=2$, the phase diagram is

shown in Fig. 2c. It is symmetric about the line $\gamma=2$. All the phases with $Q>2$ vanish when $K>K_{c 1} \approx$ 9.0, and all the phases with $Q>1$ vanish when $K>$ $K_{c 2} \approx 11.5$. Within all the "tongues" with $Q>2$, the ground states exhibit B-type symmetry. The "tongue" with $\Omega=1 / 2$ is divided into two parts and within the two parts the ground states show different symmetries. When $K<8.65$ the ground states exhibit B-type symmetry while when $K>8.65$ the ground states exhibit A-type symmetry. The "bar" occurs at $K \approx 8.65$ instead of $K \approx 8.4$ where the width of the "tongue" is the smallest, surprisingly.

Fig. 2d, where $D=2.4$, shows an example of the phase diagrams when $\gamma \geqslant 1$ and $2<D<4$. In this case the parameter space is divided into two inequivalent parts, which cover the region $1 \leqslant \gamma \leqslant D-1$ and $D-1 \leqslant D+1$, respectively. The phase structures in the left part are similar to those of the standard FK model and no B-type phase occurs. $\omega=0 / 1$ in the most left one of the regions where $\Omega=1 / 1$. Within other "tongues" of the left part, $\omega=\Omega$. In the right part of Fig. 2d, the ground states within the "tongues" with $\Omega=1 / 4$ and $\Omega=3 / 4$ exhibit B-type symmetry for normal size $K$ and exhibit A-type symmetry for large enough $K$. Within the other "tongues" the ground states exhibit A-type symmetry. There are also two "irregular regions" in this figure which locate on the left side of the "tongue" with $\Omega=1 / 4$ and on the right side of the "tongue" $\Omega=3 / 4$, respectively. The phase structures in the case of $\gamma \geqslant 1$ and $D \geqslant 4$ are similar to those of model 1 in the case of $\gamma \geqslant 0.5$ and $D \geqslant 2$.

For model 2, the variation of the phase diagram with $\varepsilon$ is more complex than that for model 1 . When $\gamma \geqslant 1$ and $D \leqslant 2$, the "bars", at which the symmetry changes, move towards the line $K=0$ with the decreasing to zero of $\varepsilon$. When $\gamma \geqslant 1$ and $D>2$, the phase structures in the left part of the parameter space remains similar to those of the standard FK model and vary very slowly with $\varepsilon$, but the phase structures in the right part are more complex and B-type phase may occur. When $\gamma \geqslant 1$ and $D \geqslant 4$ the phase structures in the two parts are both similar to those of the standard FK model and vary very slowly with $\varepsilon$. In this case no B-type phase occurs. In the most left one of the regions, where $\Omega=1 / 1, \omega=0 / 1$. In the other regions of the left part, $\omega=\Omega$. In the right part, $\omega=Q / Q$ when $\Omega=P / Q$.

## 5. Summary and conclusion

In conclusion, the CI transitions in two generalized FK models are studied. We have presented the detailed phase diagram as a function of misfit $\gamma$ and the strength of the external potential $K$ for different choices of the remaining two parameters: the strength of perturbation $\varepsilon$ and the period of the external potential $D$. For the two models, in addition to the conventional phase structures, the double-well interactions and the presence of the perturbation result in more complex and interesting phase structures. The properties of the phase structures are closely related to the period of the external potential $D$. There are two threshold values of $D$ for each model, which equal the distance and double the distance between the two wells of the interaction, respectively. Thus the range of $D$ is divided into three parts. For model 1 (or model 2), when $0 \leqslant D \leqslant l_{1}$ (or $0 \leqslant D \leqslant l_{2}$ ), the original simple "tongues" are divided into two or more parts, and within the neighboring parts the ground states have the same rotation number but different symmetry (and may have different winding number). The B-type phase can not be stable when $K$ is large enough. The "bars" move towards the line $K=0$ with the decreasing of $\varepsilon$. When $\varepsilon=0$, no B-type phase occurs. When $\gamma \geqslant l_{1} / 2$ and $D>l_{1}$ (or when $\gamma \geqslant l_{2} / 2$ and $D>l_{2}$ ), the parameter space is divided into two parts, which cover the range of $l_{1} / 2 \leqslant \gamma \leqslant D-l_{1} / 2$ and $D-l_{1} / 2 \leqslant D+l_{1} / 2$, respectively (or $l_{2} / 2 \leqslant \gamma \leqslant D-l_{2} / 2$ and $D-l_{2} / 2 \leqslant D+l_{2} / 2$, respectively). When $\gamma \geqslant l_{1} / 2$ and $l_{1}<D<2 l_{1}$ (or when $\gamma \geqslant l_{2} / 2$ and $l_{2}<D<2 l_{2}$ ), in the left part the phase structure is similar to that of the standard FK model and the ground states exhibit A-type symmetry, while in the right part the phase structure is different from that of the standard FK model and the phase with B-type symmetry may occur. When $D \geqslant 2 l_{1}$ (or $D \geqslant 2 l_{2}$ ), the phase structures in the two parts of the parameter space are both similar to that of the standard FK model. In this case no B-type phase occurs whether $\varepsilon$ equals zero or not. In the left part, $\omega=\Omega$, except for the most left one of the regions, where $\Omega=1 / 1$ and $\omega=0 / 1$. In the right part, $\omega=Q / Q$ when $\Omega=P / Q$. When $D>l_{1} / 2$ (or $D>l_{2} / 2$ ) the phase diagram is periodic and the period is just the same as that of the external potential.

A model similar to our model 2 is given in Ref. [6],

where a first-order transition from a high-temperature "cubic" phase to a low-temperature "tetragonal" phase is studied. The authors present the phase diagram in rather great detail as a function of misfit $\gamma$ and temperature $A_{2}$ for four choices of the remaining two parameters. Among other unusual features, they find commensurate phases and soliton structures in which all competing phases are present simultaneously.

Our study contributes to a deeper understanding of the CI transitions and the FK-type models, especially the periodicity of the phase structures in the $(\gamma, K)$ parameter space and the effects of the perturbed sinusoidal external potential on the modulated structures.
