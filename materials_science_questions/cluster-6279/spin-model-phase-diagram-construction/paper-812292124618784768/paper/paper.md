## Synopsis
Néel's molecular field model is applied to an orthorhombic antiferromagnetic crystal and its magnetic and caloric behaviour is analysed. Special attention is given to the case that the external field is applied in the direction preferred for antiferromagnetic alignment. The transitions between the different antiferromagnetic states are considered as well as those to the paramagnetic state.

Fig. 1 gives a simple construction to obtain magnetizations in the antiferromagnetic $a$-solution. The figures 3, 4, 6 and 7 give examples of the magnetizations and the corresponding Gibbs free energies as functions of the field for different solutions. The figures 5, 8 and 9 represent phase diagrams that may occur in the $HT$-plane.

1. From various sides it has been pointed out that under the influence of a large external magnetic field antiferromagnetism will go over into paramagnetism $^{1)}$. This gives rise to a discontinuous transition, the nature of which depends on crystalline anisotropy $^{2)}$. In a previous paper $^{3)}$ the behaviour of the parallel and perpendicular susceptibilities has been analysed for orthorhombic and for cubic symmetry at the absolute zero of temperature.

In the present paper the whole range of temperatures between zero and the Néel temperature will be considered for the case of orthorhombic symmetry. The treatment will be based on Néel's molecular field model $^{4)}$, but it may be expected that the general features found are, in a certain measure, independent of it.

In section 2 expressions for the energy and the molecular fields, acting on the spins in the sublattices, will be proposed. They include Néel's original formulae, as well as the expressions used by Haantjes and one of us $^{5)}$, and by Yosida $^{6)}$, as special cases.

In section 3 those solutions of the equations are considered in which the magnetic moments of the sublattices are either in the direction of the field or opposite to it, this field being taken parallel to the direction preferred for antiferromagnetic alignment ($a$- and $p$-solutions). It is found that the transition from the antiferromagnetic to the paramagnetic state, which

is usually of the second order, may, under certain conditions, be of the first order. Sometimes a peculiar phase-transition may occur inside the antiferromagnetic $a$-state.

In section 4 those solutions are considered in which the magnetizations are not orientated in the common direction of preference for alignment and of the external field ($b$-solutions).

In section 5 the switch-over transitions of the first order between the antiferromagnetic $a$- and $b$-solutions enter the picture as well as the possibilities of hysteresis. In 6 the phase diagrams, in which the different possible transitions are displayed, are discussed.

In section 7, finally, a few remarks are made about other orientations of the external field.

2. For the internal energy $U^{*}$) per gram ion, the following expression is introduced:
$$
U=\frac{1}{2} N \Sigma_{x, y, z}\left\{A_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime} M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}^{2}}-D_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime 2}+M_{x}^{\prime \prime 2}}{2 \mu_{x}^{2}}\right\}. \quad \text { (1) }
$$

Here, $H$ is the external field, $M'$ and $M''$ are the average magnetizations per ion of the two equivalent sublattices, and $\mu$ is the maximum value of the magnetic moment of an ion.

For the effective fields $H'=H-(2 / N)(\partial U / \partial M')$ and $H''=H-(2 / N) \cdot$ $\cdot\left(\partial U / \partial M^{\prime \prime}\right)$, we obtain from (1):
$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}=\mu_{x} H_{x}-A_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}}+D_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}} \quad(e t c.) \\
\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}=\mu_{x} H_{x}-A_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}}+D_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}} \quad(e t c.)
\end{array}\right\} \quad \text { (2) }
$$

This formalism is that proposed by Néel, but extended to ortho- rhombic symmetry. $A_{x}, A_{y}$ and $A_{z}$ are the coefficients of antiferromag netic interaction between the sublattices, while $D_{x}, D_{y}$ and $D_{z}$ are the coefficients of ferro- $(>0)$ or antiferromagnetic $(<0)$ interaction inside one sublattice.

However, it is also possible to write $(1)^{*})$ and (2) in the form
$$
U=\frac{1}{2} N \Sigma_{x, y, z}\left\{\left(A_{x}+D_{x}\right) \frac{M_{x}^{\prime} M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}^{2}}-D_{x} \frac{\left(M_{x}^{\prime}+M_{x}^{\prime \prime}\right)^{2}}{2 \mu_{x}^{2}}\right\} \quad\left(1^{*}\right)
$$

$$
\left.\begin{array}{l}
\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}=\mu_{x} H_{x}-\left(A_{x}+D_{x}\right) \frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}}+D_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime}+M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}} \quad(e t c.) \\
\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}=\mu_{x} H_{x}-\left(A_{x}+D_{x}\right) \frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}}+D_{x} \frac{M_{x}^{\prime}+M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}} \quad(e t c.).
\end{array}\right\} \quad\left(2^{*}\right)
$$

*) Distinguished from $U_{I}$ in our earlier paper $^{3}$ ) $U$ does not include the potential energy - $(M . H)$.
$D$ is identical with $2 B$ in the earlier paper.

Now $D$ characterizes an overall interaction, while $A + D$ appears as the coefficient of sublattice interaction. We shall take the $x$-axis in the direction preferred for antiferromagnetic alignment and the $z$-axis in the least preferred direction. Thus:
$$A_{x}+D_{x}>A_{y}+D_{y}>A_{z}+D_{z}$$
and, since we deal in this paper with antiferromagnetism, $A_{x}+D_{x}>0$ and $A_{x}>0$.

The formulae used in this paper include those proposed by Haan- tjes and one of us $^{5)}(D=0)$ , and those used by $Yosida^{6)}$ in his discussion on $CuCl_{2} \cdot 2 H_{2} O(A_{x}=A_{y}=A_{z})$ , as special cases.

As regards the dependence of $M^{\prime}$ and $M^{\prime \prime}$ on $H^{\prime}$ and $H^{\prime \prime}$ , we shall assume that the effective ionic spin is $\frac{1}{2}$ , and that we may neglect its non diagonal elements.

This gives:
$$\left.\begin{array}{l}
\frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}}=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}}{W^{\prime}} \operatorname{tgh} \frac{W^{\prime}}{k T} \text { with } W^{\prime}=\left\{\Sigma_{x, y, z}\left(\mu_{x}^{2} H_{x}^{\prime 2}\right)\right\}^{1 / 2} \text { (etc.) } \\
\frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}}=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{W^{\prime \prime}} \operatorname{tgh} \frac{W^{\prime \prime}}{k T} \text { with } W^{\prime \prime}=\left\{\Sigma_{x, y, z}\left(\mu_{x}^{2} H_{x}^{\prime \prime 2}\right)\right\}^{1 / 2} \text { (etc.) }
\end{array}\right\}\qquad(3)$$
where $T$ is the temperature and $k$ is Boltzmann's constant.

This assumption, however, does not seem to be essential for the quali- tative conclusions drawn in the following.

3. We shall chiefly devote our attention to the most interesting case where $H$ is in the preferred direction, and so $H_{y}=H_{z}=0$ . With the help of symmetry considerations $^{*}$ ), one may show that for any temperature $T$ no other solutions are possible than those already found by $H$ a a $n t j$ e s and one of us $^{5)}$ for $T=0$ , namely:
the $a$-solution, magnetizations in the $x$-direction, $M_{x}^{\prime} \neq M_{x}^{\prime \prime}$;
the $p$-solution, magnetizations in the $x$-direction, $M_{x}^{\prime}=M_{x}^{\prime \prime}$;
the $b$-solution, magnetizations in the $x y$-plane, $M_{x}^{\prime}=M_{x}^{\prime \prime}, M_{y}^{\prime}=-M_{y}^{\prime \prime}$.
In the present section se shall only consider the antiferromagnetic $a$ -solution and the paramagnetic $p$ -solution, which occasionally may be considered as ferromagnetic. In considering these solutions only the $x$ -components of $A$ and $B$ are of importance $cf$ . however section 5.

The $p$-solution is given by
$$\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}=\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}=\mu_{x} H_{x}-\left(A_{x}-D_{x}\right) M_{x} / \mu_{x}$$
and thus:
$$\frac{M_{x}}{\mu_{x}}=\frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}}=\frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}}=\operatorname{tgh} \frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}-\left(A_{x}-D_{x}\right) M_{x} / \mu_{x}}{k T}\qquad(4)$$

*) It was supposed that the resulting magnetization $M^{\prime}+M^{\prime \prime}$ is orientated in the $x$ -direction. This condition is perhaps too stringent in certain cases and this matter shall be considered later in detail.

which latter expression is equivalent to

$$
\mu_{x} H_{x}=\left(A_{x}-D_{x}\right) \frac{M_{x}}{\mu_{x}}+\frac{1}{2} k T \ln \frac{1+M_{x} / \mu_{x}}{1-M_{x} / \mu_{x}} \tag{4*}
$$

giving for small fields the Curie-Weiss law

$$
M_{x}=\frac{\mu_{x}^{2} H}{k\left(T+\left(A_{x}-D_{x}\right) / k\right)} \tag{4**}
$$

(4) determines the average magnetization per ion $M_{x}$ uniquely as a function of $H$ and $T$.

For the Gibbs free energy $G$, one finds

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{\mathrm{p}} & =-N H_{x} M_{x}+\frac{1}{2} N\left(A_{x}-D_{x}\right) \frac{M_{x}^{2}}{\mu_{x}^{2}}-R T \ln 2+ \\
& +\frac{1}{2} R T\left\{\left(1+\frac{M_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right) \ln \left(1+\frac{M_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right)+\left(1-\frac{M_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right) \ln \left(1-\frac{M_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right)\right\}+G^{0}.(5)
\end{aligned}
$$

The $a$-solution satisfies the equations:

$$
\left.

\frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}}=\operatorname{tgh} \frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{k T},
\end{array}
\right\} \tag{7}

The four equations (6) and (7) may be handled more easily when the follow- ing dimensionless quantities are used:

$$
m^{\prime}=\frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}}, \quad m^{\prime \prime}=\frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}}, \quad m^{+}=\frac{M_{x}^{\prime}+M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{2 \mu_{x}}, \quad m^{-}=\frac{M_{x}^{\prime}-M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{2 \mu_{x}},
$$

$$
h^{\prime}=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}}{A_{x}+D_{x}}, \quad h^{\prime \prime}=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{A_{x}+D_{x}}, \quad h^{+}=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}+\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{2\left(A_{x}+D_{x}\right)}, \quad h^{-}=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}-\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{2\left(A_{x}+D_{x}\right)},

$$
h=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}}{A_{x}+D_{x}}, \quad t=\frac{k T}{A_{x}+D_{x}}.
$$

Adding and subtracting, then, the two equations (6), one obtains

$$
h=h^{+}+\frac{A_{x}-D_{x}}{A_{x}+D_{x}} m^{+}, \tag{8a}

$$
m^{-}=h^{-}, \tag{8b}

\left.
\begin{aligned}
m' &= \operatorname{tgh}\ (h'/t) \\
m'' &= \operatorname{tgh}\ (h''/t).
\end{aligned}
\right\} \tag{9}
$$

4. Next, we bring in the $b$-solution. For the ten variables $H_x', H_y', M_x', M_y'$, $H_x'', H_y'', M_x'', M_y'', W'$ and $W''$, we have the following 10 equations:

$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime} & =\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime \prime}=\mu_{x} H_{x}-\left(A_{x}-D_{x}\right) \frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}} \\
\mu_{y} H_{y}^{\prime} & =-\mu_{y} H_{y}^{\prime \prime}=\left(A_{y}+D_{y}\right) \frac{M_{y}^{\prime}}{\mu_{y}} \\
\frac{M_{x}^{\prime}}{\mu_{x}} & =\frac{M_{x}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{x}}=\frac{\mu_{x} H_{x}^{\prime}}{W^{\prime}} \operatorname{tgh} \frac{W^{\prime}}{k T} \\
\frac{M_{y}^{\prime}}{\mu_{y}} & =-\frac{M_{y}^{\prime \prime}}{\mu_{y}}=\frac{\mu_{y} H_{y}^{\prime}}{W^{\prime}} \operatorname{tgh} \frac{W^{\prime}}{k T} \\
W^{\prime} & =W^{\prime \prime}=\sqrt{\mu_{x}^{2} H_{x}^{\prime 2}+\mu_{y}^{2} H_{y}^{\prime 2}}.
\end{aligned}
\right\}
\tag{14}
$$

7. Only very little will be said about situations in which $H$ is not coincident with the direction preferred for antiferromagnetic alignment.

If $H$ is orientated in the $y$- or $z$-directions, the magnetizations of the sublattices remain in the $xy$- or $xz$-planes. Only one kind of transition occurs on a transition line having the character of the second order $bp$-line, which, however, ends at $T_{\text{N}} = (A_x + D_x)/k$.

If $H$ lies in the $xz$-plane, the diagrams may have a similar character to that found for the case where it has the $x$-direction. The situation, however, is more complicated, since the magnetizations do not stay in one single plane.

If $H$ lies in other directions, only one kind of transitions of the character of the $bp$-transitions occur; though in case it is near the $xz$-plane, rapid changes of the orientation and the magnitude of the magnetizations may remind of the other transitions described in this paper.
