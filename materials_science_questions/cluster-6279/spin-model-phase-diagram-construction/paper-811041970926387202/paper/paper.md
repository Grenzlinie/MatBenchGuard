MAGNETISM

# Dynamic and Static Properties of a Non-Heisenberg Magnet with Complex Interion Anisotropy

G. A. Gorelikov, O. A. Kosmachev, A. V. Krivtsova, and Yu. A. Fridman*

Crimean Federal University, Simferopol, 295007 Republic of Crimea, Russia

*e-mail: yuriifridman@gmail.com

Received July 11, 2016

**Abstract**—The influence of the anisotropic exchange interaction on the phase states of a non-Heisenberg ferromagnet with a magnetic-ion spin $S = 1$ is studied. It is shown that, depending on the relationships between the parameters of anisotropic exchange interaction and their signs, either a biaxial non-Heisenberg magnet or an Ising magnet are realized in the system. Dynamic properties of the system near orientational phase transitions and transitions in the absolute value of the magnetic moment are studied. Phase diagrams of the system for different relationships between constitutive parameters are plotted.

DOI: 10.1134/S1063783417030131

## 1. INTRODUCTION

The interest in the study of new phase states of spin systems of magnets has been growing for the last two decades [1–8]. For isotropic magnets with higher-order spin exchange terms of the form $(\mathbf{S}_1\mathbf{S}_2)^n$, $n > 1$, not only phases with the magnetic order, i.e., a non-zero mean spin (the dipole order parameter $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$), can be realized, but also phases with $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = 0$, in which the spontaneous distortion of the rotational symmetry is caused by the mean values of spin multipoles, e.g., quadrupole means $S^{ij}\langle S^iS^j + S^jS^i\rangle/2$. At first, such a state, called the spin nematic (SN) phase, was found for magnets with $S = 1$. For this system, the orthogonal nematic (ON) phase was also found, in which the principal axes of the quadrupole ellipsoid are perpendicular for different sublattices [1, 2, 5]. The geometrical image of the SN-phase is a quadrupole ellipsoid, which comprises a symmetric second-rank tensor $S^{ij}$ (the means with higher spins are trivial). In the ground state, this ellipsoid is an ellipsoid of revolution and the symmetry of the state at a node is $C_{\infty}$. Such states for higher spins $S > 1$ have been studied less, but for them qualitatively new effects, which are absent in the case of $S = 1$, have been found. For spin $S = 3/2$, phases with $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = 0$ have been found for which the time reflection symmetry is distorted due to nontrivial properties of three-spin means [9] and for which a planar pseudo-spin vector $\boxed{\sigma}$ can be formed [10]. Therefore, for spin $S = 3/2$, the existence of the antinematic (AN) phase in which the directions of $\boxed{\sigma}$ in two sublattices are antiparallel is possible [10]. For the purely isotropic model of magnets with spins $S =1$ and $S = 3/2$, in the mean-field approximation with the interaction between the nearest neighbors in a lattice admitting division into two sublattices, the full phase diagram was constructed (below, we perform the analysis only in the same approximations).

Along with the study of the static and dynamic properties of isotropic spin systems, it is of interest to study exchange-interaction models. One of the simplest models containing tensor interactions in the Hamiltonian is the generalization of the Ising model with allowance for the biquadratic interaction: the so-called Blume–Emery–Griffiths model [11]. This model was proposed for describing the thermodynamic properties of a He(III) and He(IV) mixture in the vicinity of a critical point. In [12, 13], the Blume–Emery–Griffiths model was extended to non-Heisenberg magnets with $S = 1$. It was shown that the allowance for the anisotropic exchange interaction (as in the Ising and $XY$ models) has a substantial effect on the dynamics of the system and formation of specific phase states. In [14, 15], the influence of the interion and single-ion anisotropies on the properties of non-Heisenberg magnets was studied. However, the aforementioned studies were performed for simplest models of interion anisotropy.

Without discussing the reason for the interion anisotropy of magnetic interactions, the aim of the present work is to study the influence of the anisotropic interion interaction—more complex than considered in [12–15]—on the phase states of a non-Heisenberg ferromagnet with the magnetic-ion spin $S = 1$. It is reasonable to expect that the study of more complex systems will reveal new, unusual properties of magnets. The consideration of additional possibilities of the symmetry of bilinear and biquadratic exchange interaction, apparently, will lead to a more diverse

structure of phase states and dynamic properties of the system.

## 2. THE MODEL

We consider a three-dimensional ferromagnet with a unit spin of the magnetic ion. This choice of the spin makes it possible to take into account—in addition to the bilinear (Heisenberg) exchange interaction—the biquadratic exchange interaction. We will regard these interactions as essentially anisotropic, i.e., the Hamiltonian of such a system can be represented in the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
H=-\frac{1}{2} \sum_{n \neq n^{\prime}} & \left\{J_{n n^{\prime}}\left(S_{n}^{z} S_{n^{\prime}}^{z}+\Delta\left(S_{n}^{x} S_{n^{\prime}}^{x}+S_{n}^{y} S_{n^{\prime}}^{y}\right)\right)\right. \\
+K_{n n^{\prime}}(& O_{2 n}^{0} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{0} / 3+\Delta_{1}\left(O_{2 n}^{z x} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{z x}+O_{2 n}^{y z} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{y z}\right) \\
& \left.\left.+\Delta_{2}\left(O_{2 n}^{2} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{2}+O_{2 n}^{x y} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{x y}\right)\right)\right\},
\end{aligned} \quad(1)
$$

where $J_{n n}$ and $K_{n n^{\prime}}$ are the bilinear and biquadratic interaction constants, respectively; $\Delta, \Delta_{1}$, and $\Delta_{2}$, are the parameters of interion anisotropy; and $O_{j}^{i}(j \leq 2 S$, $i \leq j)$ are the Stevens operators [16], related to the spin operators as follows:

$$
\begin{gathered}
O_{2}^{0}=3\left(S^{z}\right)^{2}-S(S+1), \quad O_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(S^{+}\right)^{2}+\left(S^{-}\right)^{2}\right], \\
O_{2}^{x y}=\frac{1}{2 i}\left[\left(S^{+}\right)^{2}-\left(S^{-}\right)^{2}\right], \\
O_{2}^{z x}=\frac{1}{2}\left[S^{z},\left(S^{+}+S^{-}\right)\right]_{+}, \quad O_{2}^{y x}=\frac{1}{2 i}\left[S^{z},\left(S^{+}-S^{-}\right)\right]_{+}.
\end{gathered}
$$

This representation of Hamiltonian (1) was chosen because the complete set of operators (2) with the operators $S^{x}, S^{y}$, and $S^{z}$ are the generators of the group $S U(3)$.

We will consider the behavior of the system in two limiting cases.

1. All parameters of interion anisotropy are positive $(\Delta, \Delta_{1}, \Delta_{2}>0)$.

2. All parameters of interion anisotropy are negative $(\Delta, \Delta_{1}, \Delta_{2}<0)$.

As will be shown below, the first case (with positive parameters of interion anisotropy) may be regarded as a biaxial non-Heisenberg magnet and the second case (with negative parameters of interion anisotropy) is an Ising-type non-Heisenberg magnet. Hamiltonian (1) describes an isotropic non-Heisenberg magnet if all parameters of interion anisotropy are equal to unity.

We will study the properties of a magnet in the lowtemperature region $(T \ll T_{\mathrm{C}}$, where $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ is the Curie temperature), because, in this case, the quantum properties of the system manifest themselves more pronouncedly.

At first, let us consider the case when all parameters of interion anisotropy are positive $(\Delta, \Delta_{1}, \Delta_{2}>0)$.

By choosing the reference frame along the crystallographic axes [17], we can make the order parameters, which characterize the system, positive, i.e., $\langle S^{y}\rangle=\langle O_{2}^{y z}\rangle=\langle O_{2}^{x y}\rangle=0$. Due to anisotropic exchange interaction, the magnetic moment in the general case is directed at an angle $\theta$ with the axis $O Z$. A unitary rotation $U(\theta)=\prod_{n} \exp (i \theta S_{n}^{y})$ makes it possible for each node to pass to its own reference frame in which the direction of the axis $O Z$ coincides with the direction of the mean spin. As a result, Hamiltonian (1) will depend on the angle $\theta$:

$$
\tilde{H}(\theta) \rightarrow U(\theta) H U^{+}(\theta).
$$

Separating in the Hamiltonian $\tilde{H}(\theta)$ the mean fields connected with both vector $(\langle S^{z}\rangle)$ and tensor $(q_{2}^{t}=$ $\langle O_{2}^{t}\rangle$ ) order parameters, we obtain the single-node Hamiltonian

$$
H_{0}(n)=-H_{z} S_{n}^{z}-H_{x} S_{n}^{x}-B_{2}^{0} O_{2 n}^{0}-B_{2}^{2} O_{2 n}^{2}-B_{2}^{z x} O_{2 n}^{z x},(3)
$$

where $H_{z}=J_{0}\langle S\rangle(\cos ^{2} \theta+\Delta \sin ^{2} \theta), \ H_{x}=$  $J_{0}\langle S\rangle \sin \theta \cos \theta(1-\Delta)$ , the coefficients $B_{2}^{0}, B_{2}^{2}$ , and $B_{2}^{z x}$ are proportional to $K_{0}$ and are functions of the quadrupole parameters $q_{2}^{t}=\langle O_{2}^{t}\rangle(t=0,2, z x) ; J_{0}=$  $\sum_{n^{\prime}} J_{n n^{\prime}}$ ; and $K_{0}=\sum_{n^{\prime}} K_{n n^{\prime}}$ .

Solving a one-node problem with Hamiltonian (3), we find the basis of eigenfunctions

$$
\begin{gathered}
|\psi(1)\rangle=\cos \beta(\cos \alpha|1\rangle+\sin \alpha|-1\rangle)+\sin \beta|0\rangle, \\
|\psi(0)\rangle=-\sin \beta(\cos \alpha|1\rangle+\sin \alpha|-1\rangle)+\cos \beta|0\rangle, \quad(4) \\
|\psi(-1)\rangle=-\sin \alpha|1\rangle+\cos \alpha|-1\rangle,
\end{gathered}
$$

in which Hamiltonian (3) becomes diagonal. In basis(4), we construct the Hubbard operators $X^{M^{\prime} M}=$ |ψ(M')⟩⟨ψ(M)| [18], whose relationship with the spin operators determines the form of the order parameters:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\left\langle S^{z}\right\rangle=\cos 2 \alpha \cos ^{2} \beta, \quad q_{2}^{0}=\cos ^{2} \beta-2 \sin ^{2} \beta, \\
q_{2}^{2}=\sin 2 \alpha \cos ^{2} \beta, \quad q_{2}^{z x}=(\cos \alpha-\sin \alpha) \sin 2 \beta / \sqrt{2}.
\end{gathered}
$$

In addition, as a result of unitary transformation, there appears one more parameter [19],

$$
\left\langle S^{x}\right\rangle=(\cos \alpha+\sin \alpha) \sin 2 \beta / \sqrt{2},
$$

which is equal to zero in its intrinsic reference frame. From the condition $\langle S^{x}\rangle=0$ , it follows that $\beta=0$ , because, if $\alpha=-\pi / 4$ or $\beta=\pi / 2$ , not only $\langle S^{x}\rangle=0$ but also $\langle S^{z}\rangle=0$ , which leads to the loss of solutions of ferromagnetic ordered states. Therefore, the parameters in the intrinsic coordinate frame can be represented in the form

$$
\langle S\rangle=\cos 2 \alpha, \quad q_{2}^{0}=1, \quad q_{2}^{2}=\sin 2 \alpha, \quad q_{2}^{z x}=0.(5)
$$

In the case of low temperatures ($T \to 0$), the energy of the ground state equals the free energy density. The analysis of the free energy density,

$$
\begin{aligned}
F = \frac{1}{2}[ & J_{0}(1-\Delta) \cos ^{2} 2 \alpha+K_{0}(1-\Delta_{1}+(\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}) \sin 2 \alpha) \\
\times(1-\sin 2 \alpha) ] & \sin ^{2} \theta+\frac{K_{0}}{8}(4 \Delta_{1}-3-\Delta_{2})(1-\sin 2 \alpha)^{2} \\
& \times \sin ^{4} \theta-\frac{1}{2}(J_{0}-K_{0} \Delta_{2}) \cos ^{2} 2 \alpha
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
$$

makes it possible to determine the spin states realized at different ratios of constitutive parameters.

## 3. "BIAXIAL" NON-HEISENBERG MAGNET

It is well known that, in a non-Heisenberg isotropic magnet, the ferromagnetic phases is stable for $J_{0}>K_{0}$ [1, 20]. The allowance for the anisotropy of exchange interaction corrects this condition.

It is evident that, if the bilinear exchange interaction prevails, the mean spin remains practically unchanged: $\langle S\rangle \simeq 1$ (parameter $\alpha=0$). Depending on the anisotropy parameter $\Delta$, the direction of the magnetization changes and ferromagnetic ordering is realized: the easy-axis ferromagnetic phase ($FM_{\parallel}$) with the magnetic moment vector oriented along the axis $OZ$, which is stable for $\Delta<1$, and the easy-plane ferromagnetic phase ($FM_{\perp}$) with the magnetic moment vector orthogonal to the axis $OZ$, which is stable for $\Delta>1$. In addition, an intermediate state appears, in which the vector order parameter (the magnetic moment $\langle S\rangle$) is unsaturated. This circumstance is related to the quantum reduction of spin [9], i.e., the absolute value of the magnetic moment $\langle S\rangle$ can vary from zero to unity, depending on the relationship between the constitutive constants:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\langle S\rangle=\sqrt{1-\sin ^{2} 2 \alpha_{\perp}}, \\
\sin 2 \alpha_{\perp}=K_{0}(\Delta_{2}-1) /(4 J_{0} \Delta-K_{0}(3+\Delta_{2})).
\end{gathered} \tag{7}
$$

Then, the easy plane ($FM_{\perp}$) phase at $K_{0} \neq 0$ transforms into the quadrupole-ferromagnetic ($QFM_{\perp}$) phase, in which the dipole and quadrupole order parameters can vary from the maximum to minimum values, depending on the relationship between the constitutive constants. In particular, for $\Delta_{2}=1$, the magnetization reaches saturation and $\langle S\rangle=1$.

In addition, as follows from (6), for $4 \Delta_{1}-3-\Delta_{2}>0$, the realization of the quadrupole-ferromagnetic ($QFM_{\angle}$) phase with $\langle S\rangle<1$ is possible, and the direction of the magnetic moment vector has no definite orientation and, depending on the relationship between the constitutive constants, the angle $\theta$ can vary from 0 to $\pi / 2$:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\sin 2 \alpha_{\angle}=\frac{K_{0} \sin ^{2} \theta(1-\Delta_{2}+(4 \Delta_{1}-3-\Delta_{2}) \cos ^{2} \theta)}{K_{0}(4 \Delta_{2}+3(1-\Delta_{2}) \sin ^{2} \theta+(4 \Delta_{1}-3-\Delta_{2}) \sin ^{2} \theta \cos ^{2} \theta)-4 J_{0}(\cos ^{2} \theta+\Delta \sin ^{2} \theta)}, \\
\cos 2 \theta=\frac{K_{0}(1-\Delta_{2})(1+3 \sin 2 \alpha_{\angle})+4 J_{0}(1-\Delta)(1+\sin 2 \alpha_{\angle})}{K_{0}(4 \Delta_{1}-3-\Delta_{2})(1-\sin 2 \alpha_{\angle})}.
\end{gathered} \tag{8}
$$

Additional information on the existence domains of the corresponding spin states and types of phase transitions between them can be obtained from the analysis of the dynamic properties of the system, i.e., the magnon spectrum. The spectra of elementary excitations are determined by the poles of the Green's function [21]

$$
G^{\lambda \lambda^{\prime}}(n, \tau, n^{\prime}, \tau^{\prime})=-\langle\hat{T} \tilde{X}_{n}^{\lambda}(\tau) \tilde{X}_{n^{\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle,
$$

where $\tilde{X}_{n}^{\lambda}(\tau)=\exp (\hat{H} \tau) X_{n}^{\lambda} \exp (-\hat{H} / \tau)$ is the Hubbard operator in the Heisenberg representation, $\hat{T}$ is the Wick's operator, and $\lambda$ are the root vectors determined by the algebra of Hubbard operators [18]. We will dwell here on deriving the dispersion relation, because this issue is thoroughly presented in [18, 22]. The dispersion relation is valid for an arbitrary relationship between the constitutive constants and in a wide temperature interval in which the magnetic ordering exists (except for the fluctuation region). The solution of the dispersion relation determines the spectra of elementary excitations in different phases.

The magnon spectra in the easy-axis, $FM_{\parallel}$, and easy-plane, $QFM_{\perp}$, phases have the following form, respectively:

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(k)=2(J_{0}-K_{k} \Delta_{2}) ; \tag{9.1}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(k)=J_{0}-J_{k} \Delta+K_{0}-K_{k} \Delta_{1}. \tag{9.2}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(k)=2 \sqrt{(J_{0} \Delta-K_{k} \Delta_{1})\{(J_{0}-J_{k}) \Delta+[J_{k} \Delta-K_{k}(3+\Delta_{2}) / 4](1-\sin ^{2} 2 \alpha_{\perp})\}} ; \tag{10.1}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(k)=\sqrt{\Omega_{1}\left(\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2}\right)}, \tag{10.2}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Omega_{1}=\left(J_{0}-J_{k}\right) \Delta\left(1-\sin 2 \alpha_{\perp}\right) \\
& +\left(K_{0}-K_{k}\right) \Delta_{2}\left(1+\sin 2 \alpha_{\perp}\right), \\
& \Omega_{2}=J_{k}(\Delta-1)-K_{k}\left(\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{1}\right) \\
+ & \sin 2 \alpha_{\perp}\left(K_{k}\left(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{1}\right)-J_{k}(\Delta+1)\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Let us analyze the behavior of the excitation spectra in the most interesting cases. Let $\Delta_{1}>(3+\Delta_{2}) / 4$. The easy-axis-angular and easy-plane-angular phases are orientational phase transitions of the second kind. With an approach to the angular-easy-axis phase transition line, the absolute value of the magnetic moment tends to the saturation value $\langle S\rangle=1$. On the phase transition line $J_{0}(1-\Delta)=K_{0}(\Delta_{1}-1)$, the gap in spectrum (9.2) vanishes. As follows from expressions (7) and (8), in the easy-plane, $QFM_{\perp}$, and angular, $QFM_{\angle}$, phases, $\langle S\rangle \neq 1$. On the angular-easy-plane phase transition line,

$$
\begin{gathered}
J_{0}[1-\Delta+(1+\Delta) \sin 2 \alpha] \\
=K_{0}\left[\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{1}+\sin 2 \alpha_{\perp}\left(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{1}\right)\right],
\end{gathered}
$$

the values of the magnetization become equal, i.e.,

$$
\sin 2 \alpha_{\perp}=\sin 2 \alpha_{\angle} \quad \text { for } \quad \theta=\pi / 2.
$$

On the same line, the gap in spectrum (10.2) vanishes. In the long-wavelength limit $k \to 0$, both spectra (9.2) and (10.2) on the lines of phase transition to the angular phase obey a quadratic law of dispersion. Such a behavior of the spectra corresponds to orientational phase transitions of the second kind.

If the parameters of interion anisotropy are related as $\Delta_{1}<(3+\Delta_{2}) / 4$, then the angular phase becomes unstable. The phase transition from the easy-axis phase proceeds directly to the easy-plane phase and is not purely orientational, because, on easy-axis-easy-plane phase transition line, in addition to the rotation of the magnetization vector by the angle $\pi / 2$, the value of the magnetization vector changes jumpwise from $\langle S\rangle=1$ in the easy-axis phase to $\langle S\rangle<1$ in the easy-plane phase (see expressions (7)). The phase transition line is determined from the equality of free energies in the easy-axis and easy-plane phases and has the form

$$
4 J_{0}(1-\Delta)=K_{0}\left(1-\Delta_{2}\right)\left(1+\sin 2 \alpha_{\perp}\right).
$$

In the vicinity of easy-axis-easy-plane phase transition line, branches (9.2) and (10.2) soften. The vanishing of the gaps in the spectra determines the lines on which the stability of the spectra is lost: $J_{0}(1-\Delta)=$ $K_{0}(\Delta_{1}-1)$ and $J_{0}[1-\Delta+(1+\Delta) \sin 2 \alpha]=K_{0}[\Delta_{2}-$ $\Delta_{1}+\sin 2 \alpha(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{1})]$. These lines determine the boundaries of the domain of coexistence of the phases considered here. This behavior of spectra is typical of phase transitions of the first kind.

Now let us consider the opposite situation, namely: the biquadratic exchange constant is substantially greater than the bilinear exchange constant $(K_{0}>J_{0})$.

As was shown in a number of works (see, e.g., [2, 20, 23, 24]), in an isotropic non-Heisenberg magnet with $S=1$, in the case of strong biquadratic exchange interaction, the ferromagnetic ordering becomes energetically unfavorable. The dipole order parameter becomes equal to zero, and the system is characterized by higher multipole order parameters in the components of the spin operator. For the model under study, as follows from (5), this state is characterized by quadrupole order parameters $q_{2}^{0}$ and $q_{2}^{2}$, which are diagonal components of the symmetric second-rank tensor $q_{2}^{ij}=\langle S^{i}S^{j}+S^{j}S^{i}\rangle / 2$.

The analysis of free energy density (5) if the exchange integrals relate as $K_{0}>J_{0}$ shows that, in this case, the following phase states with $\langle S\rangle=0$ can be realized:

—the nematic plane phase $N_{1}$ at $\theta=0$ and $\alpha=$ $-\pi / 4$;

—the nematic axial phase $N_{2}$ at $\theta=\pi / 2$ and $\alpha=$ $-\pi / 4$;

—the angular nematic phase $N_{\angle}$ at $\cos 2 \theta=(1-$ $\Delta_{2})(3+\Delta_{2}-4 \Delta_{1})$ and $\alpha=-\pi / 4$.

The nematic states are characterized by order parameters that are components of a symmetric second-rank tensor; the geometrical image of such a tensor (with positive components) in the three-dimensional space is a uniaxial ellipsoid. This orientation can be defined by a unit vector $\mathbf{n}$ orthogonal to the plane of a quadrupole ellipsoid (in the given case, a disk); in this respect, it is analogous to a molecular nematic, in which the orientation of molecules in space is characterized by a unit director vector [25]. As in a molecular nematic, the direction of the vectors $\mathbf{n}$ and $-\mathbf{n}$ are undistinguishable and the quantity $q_{2}^{ij}$ is a quantum analog of the de Gennes order parameter, which is introduced for ordinary nematic liquid crystals [25].

From the analysis of free energy (6), it follows that the ground state of nematic phases is connected with the direction of the director vector $\mathbf{n}$ and is determined by the function of the ground state $\psi=(|1\rangle-|-1\rangle) / \sqrt{2}$. In the intrinsic reference frame, the director vector $\mathbf{n}$ is parallel to the axis $OX$, because the "disk" of the quadrupole moment has nonzero components $\langle(S^{y})^{2}\rangle=\langle(S^{z})^{2}\rangle=1$. The angle $\theta$ determines the deviation of the vector $\mathbf{n}$ from the axis $OX$ of the laboratory frame of reference. In order to determine the form of the eigenfunction in the laboratory frame of reference, it is necessary to carry out a unitary transformation $U(-\theta)=\exp(-i \theta S^{y})$. Acting by the operator $U(-\theta)=$ $\exp(-i \theta S^{y})$ at $\theta=\pi / 2$ on the function $\psi=(|1\rangle-$ $|-1\rangle) / \sqrt{2}$, we determine the form of the function of the ground state $\psi=|0\rangle$ of the nematic phase $N_{2}$ in the laboratory frame of reference. The order parameters characterizing the $N_{2}$ phase are $q_{2}^{0}=-2$ and $q_{2}^{2}=0$. In

the nematic $N_1$ phase, the function of the ground state is $\psi=(|1\rangle-|-1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$ and the order parameters are $q_2^0=1$ and $q_2^2=-1$. It should be noted that, in the isotropic case, the $N_1$ and $N_2$ states are degenerate, i.e., their energies are equal and the corresponding wave functions pass to one another by a continuous rotation. However, for an exchange anisotropic magnet, the situation is different: as follows from (6), different states correspond to different free energies and, therefore, the exchange anisotropy removes the degeneracy of these spin states. In addition, it should be noted that the spin nematic $N_2$ resembles in its symmetry properties the quadrupole state, which arises in a magnet with a large easy-plane anisotropy [26, 27].

One of interesting points of this model is the appearance of the angular nematic phase [28]. The function of the ground state of this phase in the laboratory frame of reference,
$$\psi=\cos\theta(|1\rangle-|-1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}+\sin\theta|0\rangle$$
is the superposition of two vectors of the nematic states: $(|1\rangle-|-1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$ and $|0\rangle$. As is seen from the phase diagram in the plane $(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)$, at fixed parameters $\Delta$ and $J_0/K_0<1$ (Fig. 1), the nematic angular phase $N_\angle$ passes for $\Delta_2<1$ and $\Delta_1=1$ to the axial phase $N_2$ and, for $\Delta_2<1$ and $\Delta_1=(1+\Delta_2)/2$, to the plane nematic phase $N_1$.

It should be emphasized that the necessary condition for the stability of the angular nematic phase,

$$\varepsilon_1(k)=2\sqrt{(K_0\Delta_1-J_k\Delta)(K_0-K_k)\Delta_1+K_k\frac{4\Delta_1-3-\Delta_2}{4}(1-\cos^22\theta)};\tag{11.1}$$

$$\varepsilon_2(k)=\sqrt{\tilde{\Omega}_1(\tilde{\Omega}_1+\tilde{\Omega}_2)},\tag{11.2}$$

$$\tilde{\Omega}_1=(K_0-K_k)(\Delta_2-\Delta_1+\cos2\theta(\Delta_2-\Delta_1)),$$
$$\tilde{\Omega}_2=K_k(\Delta_2-\Delta_1)-J_k(\Delta+1)$$
$$+\cos2\theta(K_k(\Delta_2-\Delta_1)+J_k(\Delta-1)),$$

and, in the phase $N_1$, in the form
$$\varepsilon_1(k)=2\sqrt{(K_0\Delta_2-J_k)(K_0-K_k)\Delta_2};\tag{12.1}$$

$$=\frac{\varepsilon_2(k)}{\sqrt{(K_0(1+\Delta_2)-2J_k\Delta)(K_0(1+\Delta_2)-2K_k\Delta_1)}}.\tag{12.2}$$

In the phase $N_2$, due to the degeneracy of excited single-ion states, the magnon spectra coincide:
$$\varepsilon_1(k)=\varepsilon_2(k)=2\sqrt{(K_0-J_k\Delta)(K_0-K_k\Delta_1)}.\tag{13}$$

It should be noted that, in the isotropic case ($\Delta=\Delta_1=\Delta_2=1,\theta=0$), all three phases $N_1,N_2$, and $N_\angle$ become indistinguishable, their ground state is

To understand the specificities of formation of spin configurations, it is necessary to study the dynamics of spin excitations. Let us present the solutions of the dispersion relation in the nematic phases. The spectra of elementary excitations in the angular phase $N_\angle$ have the form
$$\psi=(|1\rangle+|-1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$$
and the spectra take the standard form [17, 23, 29]
$$\varepsilon_1(k)=\varepsilon_2(k)=2\sqrt{(K_0-J_k)(K_0-K_k)}.$$

Thus, the exchange anisotropy removes the degeneracy of the nematic states, which is observed in an isotropic non-Heisenberg magnet.

The phase transitions between nematic phases are orientational and proceed in the parameters of anisotropy of the biquadratic exchange interaction (Fig. 1). Since, in nematic phases, the magnetic moment (at a node) is equal to zero, the conception of "reorientation" is related to the rotation of the director vector $\mathbf{n}$. If $\Delta_1>(3+\Delta_2)/4$, the $N_1-N_\angle$ and $N_\angle-N_2$ phase transitions are orientational phase transitions of the second kind. On the line $\Delta_1=(1+\Delta_2)/2$ of the $N_1-N_\angle$ phase transition, the gaps in spectra (11.1) and (12.2) vanish and, on this line, the spectra become linear in the wave vector $\mathbf{k}$ in the long-wavelength limit. Similarly, on the line of the $N_2-N_\angle\Delta_1=1$ phase transition, $\Delta_1=1$ as $k\to0$, the gaps in branches (11.1) and

(13) vanish, and the spectra also become linear in the wave vector.

If $\Delta_1 < (3 + \Delta_2)/4$, the phase transition between the phases $N_1$ and $N_2$ proceeds jumpwise on the line $\Delta_2 =$ 1. This phase transition is an orientational phase transition of the first kind: the direction of the vector $\mathbf{n}$ changes jumpwise and is not accompanied with softening of the magnon spectra on the transition line $\Delta_2 = 1$.

Thus, there is an essential difference between the behavior of non-Heisenberg magnets in the vicinity of orientational phase transitions in the constitutive parameters for the isotropic and nonanisotropic exchange interactions. As was shown by the analysis of the model of a non-Heisenberg magnet with exchange interaction [30], orientational phase transitions are degenerate transitions of the first kind: on the phase boundary, the symmetry of the phases changes jumpwise simultaneously with the softening of elementary excitations. In the model under consideration, the orienational phase transitions between ferromagnetic and nematic phases occur on variation in the constants of anisotropic exchange interaction; these transitions can be of the first and second kind.

Let us consider the behavior of the system on the boundaries of the ferromagnetic and nematic phases. Phase transitions of this kind are connected with the vanishing of the magnetization at one node, i.e., with the effect of quantum spin reduction [17, 19]. In a number of works, e.g., [30, 31], it was shown that the single-ion magnetic anisotropy and the biquadratic exchange interaction amplify one another: the biquadratic exchange interaction and the single-ion anisotropy lead to nonequidistant repulsion of magnetic ion's energy levels. However, the biquadratic exchange tends to establish the nematic order as a whole with an abrupt change of the ferromagnetic order with $\langle S \rangle = 1$ to the nematic order $\langle S \rangle = 0$ [1, 5]. In this case, the increase in the constant of single-ion anisotropy leads to a smooth increase in the mean spin at a node and realization of the intermediate quadrupole-ferromagnetic order [31, 32]. In the given model, the quadrupole ferromagnetic phases are implemented due to the anisotropic exchange interaction.

Since the phase states of the system under consideration depend on a large number of parameters, it is impossible to present the graphical representation of the phase portrait of the system. However, we can plot the phase diagram of the system in different planes.

At first, let us consider the case of $\Delta_2 < 1$ and $\Delta_1 < 1$ (Fig. 2). For this relationship between the parameters of biquadratic anisotropy, the phases $FM_\parallel$, $QFM_\perp$, and $N_2$ are realized, depending on the relationship between $\Delta$ and $J_0/K_0$. The behavior of the magnet in the vicinity of the $FM_\parallel$-$QFM_\perp$ orientational phase transition has been studied above.

## 4. "ISING-LIKE" NON-HEISENBERG MAGNET

Now, let us consider an exchange anisotropic non-
Heisenberg magnet with the magnetic-ion spin $S=1$
and negative values of the parameters of interion
anisotropy. As will be shown below, such a system may
be regarded as a non-Heisenberg Ising-like magnet.
The Hamiltonian of this system is similar to (1) with

the replacement of the parameters of interion anisotropy $\Delta \to -\Delta$, $\Delta_1 \to -\Delta_1$, and $\Delta_2 \to -\Delta_2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\mathrm{int}} = & -\frac{1}{2} \sum_{n \neq n^{\prime}} \left\{ J_{n n^{\prime}} (S_{n}^{z} S_{n^{\prime}}^{z} - \Delta (S_{n}^{x} S_{n^{\prime}}^{x} + S_{n}^{y} S_{n^{\prime}}^{y})) \right. \\
& - K_{n n^{\prime}} (O_{2 n}^{0} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{0} / 3 - \Delta_1 (O_{2 n}^{z x} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{z x} + O_{2 n}^{y z} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{y z}) \\
& \left. \left. - \Delta_2 (O_{2 n}^{x y} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{x y} + O_{2 n}^{x y} O_{2 n^{\prime}}^{x y}) \right) \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

All notation in (15) is analogous to the above-introduced one. It should be noted that, in the limiting case of $\Delta, \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \to -1$, Hamiltonian (15) describes an isotropic non-Heisenberg magnet. It is obvious that the signs of the parameters of interion anisotropy $\Delta, \Delta_1$, and $\Delta_2$ are essential for the formation of the phase portrait of the magnet.

As in the previous case, let us analyze the behavior of the system in the low-temperature region $T \ll T_{\mathrm{C}}$ ($T_{\mathrm{C}}$ is the Curie temperature).

Passing in (15) to the intrinsic frame of reference, related to the quantization axis $OZ$, and to the mean fields, related to the ordering of the magnetic moments and quadrupole moments, we obtain a single-node Hamiltonian, analogous to (3):

$$
H_{0}(n) = -H_{z} S_{n}^{z} - H_{x} S_{n}^{x} - B_{2}^{0} O_{2 n}^{0} - B_{2}^{2} O_{2 n}^{2} - B_{2}^{z x} O_{2 n}^{z x}, (16)
$$

where
$$
H_{z} = J_{0} \langle S \rangle (\cos^{2} \theta - \Delta \sin^{2} \theta),
$$
$$
H_{x} = J_{0} \langle S \rangle \sin \theta \cos \theta (1 + \Delta),
$$
$$
B_{2}^{0} = K_{0} (A q_{2}^{2} + D q_{2}^{2} + H q_{2}^{z x}),
$$
$$
B_{2}^{2} = K_{0} (D q_{2}^{2} + B q_{2}^{2} + G q_{2}^{z x}),
$$
$$
B_{2}^{z x} = K_{0} (H q_{2}^{2} + G q_{2}^{2} + C q_{2}^{z x}).
$$

The amplitudes $A, B, C, D, G$, and $H$ are cumbersome functions of the angle $\theta$ and interion anisotropy parameters $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$.

Using the above results, we can obtain the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of Hamiltonian (16), which makes is possible to determine the free-energy density (as $T \to 0$) and the order parameters as functions of the parameters of the unitary transformations and constitutive parameters of the system:

$$
\begin{aligned}
F = & -\frac{1}{2} J_{0} \cos^{2} 2\alpha (\cos^{2} \theta - \Delta \sin^{2} \theta) \\
& + K_{0} \left\{ -\frac{1}{2} A - D \sin 2\alpha - \frac{1}{2} B \sin^{2} 2\alpha \right\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$

$$
\langle S \rangle = \cos 2\alpha, \quad q_{2}^{0} = 1, \quad q_{2}^{2} = \sin 2\alpha, \quad q_{2}^{z x} = 0.
$$

The analysis of the free-energy density for the extremum in the parameters $\alpha$ and $\theta$ makes it possible to assume that the realization of homogeneous phase states in the system is possible. The analysis of the energy levels of a magnetic ion shows that the ground state corresponds to $E_1$. This makes it possible to introduce an additional criterion for the realization of different phase states. From the condition that $E_1$ is the lowest level of the system, the difference $E_{-11} = E_{-1} - E_1$ must be positive as well as $E_{01} = E_0 - E_1$. Thus, we have formulated an additional condition for the stability of phases:

$$
E_{01} = E_{0} - E_{1} > 0, \quad E_{-11} = E_{-1} - E_{1} > 0. \tag{18}
$$

Let us dwell on the analysis of the system for $J_0 > K_0$. The analysis of free-energy density (17) shows that, for $\langle S \rangle \neq 0$, there can exist the same ferromagnetic phase states considered in the previous section: the easy-axis ferromagnetic phase $(FM_{\parallel})$, the easy-plane ferromagnetic phase $(FM_{\perp})$, and the quadrupole ferromagnetic phase $(QFM_{\angle})$. However, a more accurate analysis of the free-energy density for the minimum $\left( \frac{\partial^{2} F}{\partial \theta^{2}} > 0, \frac{\partial^{2} F}{\partial \alpha^{2}} > 0 \right)$ and criterion (18) shows that, beside the state $FM_{\parallel}$ for $J_0 > K_0$, the other states do not correspond to the minimum of the thermodynamic potential and become energy unfavorable.

Thus, for $J_0 > K_0$, only the $FM_{\parallel}$ phase ($\theta = 0, \alpha = 0$) with the magnetic moment vector parallel to the axis $OZ$ is realized. Additional information on the region of stability of homogeneous phases can be obtained from the analysis of spectra of elementary excitations. The obtainment of magnon spectra is thoroughly described in [12, 18, 22]. Therefore, we will only present here the explicit form of the magnon spectra in the $FM_{\parallel}$ phase:

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(k) = 2(J_{0} + K_{0} \Delta_2), \tag{19.1}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(k) = J_{0} + J_{0} \Delta + K_{0} + K_{0} \Delta_1. \tag{19.2}
$$

It is easy to see that spectra (19.1) and (19.2) are dispersionless and their energy gaps vanish at no values of the constitutive parameters. Therefore, the spectra of elementary excitations become dispersionless and resemble the spectrum of the Ising model [33]. The same circumstance follows from the form of Hamiltonian (15), in which the term $-\frac{1}{2} \sum_{n \neq n^{\prime}} (S_{n}^{z} S_{n^{\prime}}^{z} - \Delta(S_{n}^{x} S_{n^{\prime}}^{x} + S_{n}^{y} S_{n^{\prime}}^{y}))$ corresponds to the easy-axis interion anisotropy. All terms containing the parameters of interion anisotropy in Hamiltonian (15) have the same geometry as the Ising-like Hamiltonian. This means that the spectra of elementary excitations in the $FM_{\parallel}$ phase soften at no values of the interion anisotropy parameters and the phase transition from the $FM_{\parallel}$ phase will not be reorientational.

Now let us consider the opposite case, when the biquadratic exchange interaction exceeds the linear one ($J_0 < K_0$). In this case, the ferromagnetic order is energy unfavorable and the nematic order, which is determined by quadrupole order parameters, is realized in the system. It should be noted that the nematic order is not paramagnetic, because the diagonal com-

ponents of the quadrupole moments are not equal to one another [34].

The analysis of free-energy density (15) shows that the nematic state is the state realized at $\alpha = -\pi/4$ and the stable nematic state is $N_{2}$ (for $\theta = \pi/2$) with the wave function of the ground state $|\psi\rangle=|0\rangle$. In this case, the angle $\theta$ determines the orientation of the director vector rather than the direction of the magnetic moment, because $\langle S\rangle=0$. The nematic states $N_{1}$ with $\theta=0$ and $N_{z}$ become energy unfavorable.

In the $N_{2}$ phase, due to the degeneracy of excited single-ion energy states of the magnetic ion, the mag- non spectra coincide:

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(k)=\varepsilon_{2}(k)=2 \sqrt{\left(K_{0}+J_{0} \Delta\right) K_{0}\left(1+\Delta_{1}\right)}. \tag{20}
$$

As in the $FM_{\|}$ phase, spectrum (20) is dispersion less and its analysis shows that it vanishes at no values of the constitutive parameters. This circumstance gives evidence that this system undergoes a nonreori- entational phase transition to the $FM_{\|}$ phase.

To determine the phase transition lines, let us com- pare the free-energy densities in the $FM_{\|}$ and $N_{2}$ phases. From the equality of the free energies, it fol- lows that the phase transition line is $J_{0}=K_{0}$.

As a result of the analysis performed above, we have plotted the phase diagram of a non-Heisenberg mag- net with negative inter-ion anisotropy, which is shown schematically in Fig. 5.

The $N_{2}-FM_{\|}$ phase transition obtained in the given model is not related to the variation in the parameters of interion anisotropy, but is determined only by the relationship between the exchange integrals. The rea- son is that the given model describes an Ising-like magnet with a very large uniaxial anisotropy (whose axis of symmetry coincides with the quantization axis), which does not compete with other interactions.

The $N_{2}-FM_{\|}$ transition is caused by the competition between the bilinear (Ising) exchange interaction and the biquadratic (also Ising) exchange interaction.

## 5. CONCLUSIONS

The studies performed in this work make it possible to find out the influence of the exchange interaction anisotropy on the formation of the phase states and dynamic properties of a non-Heisenberg magnet with a magnetic-ion spin $S=1$. In particular, at positive values of interion anisotropy parameters, the system behaves as a magnet with a biaxial anisotropy. More- over, the behavior of such a non-Heisenberg magnet has a number of specificities. In the first place, the allowance for the anisotropic exchange interaction leads to the removal of the degeneracy in the director of the magnetic moment vector in the ferromagnetic phase and the director vector in the nematic state. The comparison with the results of [12, 13], where the anisotropic exchange interaction was considered too, shows that it is the allowance for the anisotropic biquadratic exchange interaction of a more complex form that leads to the realization of the angular and nematic phases.

A radically different behavior of the system under consideration is observed in the case of negative parameters of interion anisotropy. Such interion anisotropy leads to a very strong effective uniaxial anisotropy, parallel to the quantization axis. This sit- uation may be considered as an Ising system similar to the Blume-Emery-Griffiths model [11]. In this case, only two phase states are realized in the system: the ferromagnetic phase $FM_{\|}$ with the magnetic moment parallel to the quantization axis and the nematic $N_{2}$ phase, whose geometrical image is an infinitely thin disk oriented in the direction of the plane $XOY$. In this case, although the parameters of interion anisotropy determine the symmetry of the system, they do not determine the phase transition line, which coincides with the $FM-N$ phase transition line of a spin nematic [5].
