# Crossover from Frustrated XY Model to ANNNI Model
at Finite Temperature

Mamoru YAMASHITA* and Satoshi TANAKA¹,**

Department of Physics Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, Mie University,
Kamihama 1515, Tsu 514-8507
¹ Department of Physics, Kinki University, 3-4-1 Kowakae, Higashi-Osaka, Osaka 577-0818

(Received March 8, 1999)

To clarify the mechanism of successive phase transitions occurring in ferroelectric and antifer-
roelectric smectic liquid crystals, a crossover behaviour of the XY model with competing second
nearest neighbour interaction exposed to a twofold field to the Ising model (axial next nearest
neighbour Ising model) is studied at finite temperature. The phase transition line from an or-
dered phase with Ising symmetry to the one preserving characteristics of the XY model is shown
to lie at the low-temperature side of a critical curve between the former and a disordered phase,
which leads to the result that the freedom of the XY spin is suppressed more strongly at finite
temperature than at the absolute zero temperature. A phase with a structure of period 6 is
analysed in detail as a typical case of modulated phases with the freedom of XY spin.

KEYWORDS: successive phase transitions, antiferroelectric smectics, crossover from XY model to Ising model,
twofold field, ANNNI model

Successive phase transitions occurring in ferroelec-
tric and antiferroelectric smectic liquid crystals¹⁻⁴) have
attracted a great deal of attention.⁵) To clarify the
mechanism of phase transitions, two types of con-
crete models have been proposed.⁶⁻²²) One is essentially
an Ising model with competing interaction⁶⁻¹⁷) called
an axial next nearest neighbour Ising model (ANNNI
model),²³,²⁴) on the basis of which sequences of phases
appearing successively are explained in a natural man-
ner,⁶,⁷,¹⁰) an intermediate new phase is predicted⁶)
which is confirmed experimentally after that⁴) and the
structures of some phases are identified.³,¹⁴,¹⁵) The an-
other one is the XY models with competing interac-
tions,¹⁸⁻²⁰) aided by the boundary effect²¹) and accom-
panied by a chiral force.²²) On the basis of these models,
the optical observations at some phases are fitted suc-
cessfully by detailed analyses,¹⁸,²⁰) though the sequences
of many phases are not explained. Conoscopic observa-
tions support the Ising model.¹⁻³) Recently, an investi-
gation of structures of some mesophases appearing in
such successive phase transitions by mean of the reso-
nant X-ray diffraction has been reported, suggesting the
applicability of the XY model.²⁵) Although the experi-
mental results disagree to each other at present, these
models are considered to be mutually complementary,
rather than contradictory, because there are many an-
tiferroelectric materials showing various types of phase
sequences of transitions,¹⁻⁵,¹⁹,²⁰) and all the phenomena
are not necessarily explained by the ANNNI model. The
actual material is considered to be described by a certain
model which lies between those typical models. There-
fore, it is important to clarify the relation between these
models.

We have already introduced a model which connects
both models,²⁶,²⁷) whose Hamiltonian is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{H}= & -J \sum_{i, j} \cos (\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{j})-J_{1} \sum_{i}^{A} \cos (\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_{i}) \\
& -J_{2} \sum_{i}^{A} \cos (\varphi_{i+2}-\varphi_{i})+H \sum_{i}(1-\cos 2 \varphi_{i}), \quad (1)
\end{aligned}
$$

where the variable $\varphi_{i}$ denotes the state of the $i$-th XY
spin taking a value continuously in the interval $[0,2 \pi]$,
$J$ (>0) the interaction parameter between the nearest
neighbouring spin pair in the $xy$-plane, $J_{1}$ (>0) and
$J_{2}$ (<0) the ones between the nearest and next nearest
neighbouring pairs in the axial direction (say, $z$-axis), re-
spectively (the sites $i+1$ and $i+2$ denote the nearest
neighbours to $i$-th site in the adjacent and secondly adja-
cent layers, for simplicity), and $H$ ($\geq 0$) the twofold field.
Modulations of phases appear in the $z$-direction. In the
case of an infinitely large field magnitude, the system is
reduced to the ANNNI model. With the application of
the model to the phase transitions of smectic liquid crys-
tals, the variable $\varphi_{i}$ represents an azimuthal angle, that
is, the direction of tilt of the molecular long axis. For the
practical explanation of the successive phase transitions
of antiferroelectric smectics, the third nearest neighbour
interaction $J_{3}$ has also been taken into account.⁶,¹⁰⁻¹⁴)
However, in the present paper we neglect such a term for
simplicity, because a qualitative aspect of the crossover
between thsoe models is of primary concern.

state was already reported.²⁶,²⁷) In the present note, be-
haviours of the system at finite temperature are stud-

Ising model occurs by analysing the phase with period
6. At the ground state, a phase with wave number
$q=1/4$ has the following structure, $\varphi_1=\varphi_2=0$ and
$\varphi_3=\varphi_4=\pi$, which belongs to the Ising symmetry like a
uniform phase, $q=0$, and both phases dominate the $H$
versus $J_1$ plane, while other phases with $q$ in an interval
$(0,1/4)$ appear only in an area $H<0.75$ and $0<J_1<4$
(a unit, $|J_2|=1$, is used hereafter). In these phases the
freedom of the XY spin is still preserved even in the field
$H$. By the analysis based on soliton concepts, the phases
with $q=1/6,1/5$ and $1/7$ have finite ranges of stability
in the phase diagram, suggesting that the ground state
forms a kind of devil's staircase. $^{27)}$ However, the stable
range of each phase is quite narrow except for those with
$q=0,1/4$ and $1/6$. The freedom of the XY spin is sup-
pressed completely by $H$ in the region $H>0.75$.

In extreme cases, $H=0$ and $\infty$, the transition tem-
perature $T_\text{c}$ for the transition from a disordered phase to
a sinusoidal one is given in the mean field theory by

$$
T_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{a}{k_{\mathrm{B}}}\left(\frac{J z}{2}+\frac{J_{1}^{2}}{8\left|J_{2}\right|}+\left|J_{2}\right|\right), \quad\left(J_{1} \leq 4\left|J_{2}\right|\right), \quad(2)
$$

where $z$ is the coordination number in the $xy$-plane and
$k_\text{B}$ the Boltzmann constant. The wave number $q$ of the
modulated phase appearing just at $T_\text{c}$ is determined from

$$
\cos (2 \pi q)=\frac{J_{1}}{4\left|J_{2}\right|}. \quad(3)
$$

The parameter $a$ in eq. (2) takes the value 1 for the XY
model and 2 for the ANNNI model. So far the behaviours
of the present system are reviewed at the extreme situa-
tions for $T$ and $H$. In the following, we study the general
case.

In the mean field theory, the thermodynamical poten-
tial per spin for a structure with period $p$ is obtained
as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(\{c_{i}, s_{i}\}) &=\frac{1}{p} \sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\{\frac{J z}{2}\left(c_{i}^{2}+s_{i}^{2}\right)+J_{1}\left(c_{i+1} c_{i}+s_{i+1} s_{i}\right)\right. \\
&\left.+J_{2}\left(c_{i+2} c_{i}+s_{i+2} s_{i}\right)-k_{\mathrm{B}} T \ln \tau\left(\xi_{i}, \eta_{i}, h\right)\right\}, \quad(4)
\end{aligned}
$$

in which $\tau(\xi_i,\eta_i,h)$, one body partition function, is
given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau\left(\xi_{i}, \eta_{i}, h\right)\}= & \int_{0}^{2 \pi} \exp \left\{\xi_{i} \cos \varphi+\eta_{i} \sin \varphi\right. \\
&-h(1-\cos 2 \varphi)\} \mathrm{d} \varphi, \quad(5)
\end{aligned}
$$

with the inverse temperature $\beta=1/k_\text{B}T$ and $h=\beta H$.
The thermal averages of $\cos\varphi_i$ and $\sin\varphi_i$, $c_i$ and $s_i$, in
eq. (4) are determined by

$$
c_{i}=\frac{\partial \ln \tau\left(\xi_{i}, \eta_{i}, h\right)}{\partial \xi_{i}}, \quad(6)
$$

$$
s_{i}=\frac{\partial \ln \tau\left(\xi_{i}, \eta_{i}, h\right)}{\partial \eta_{i}}, \quad(7)
$$

respectively, and molecular fields $\xi_i$ and $\eta_i$ in eqs. (4)-(7)
are given by

$$
\xi_{i}=\beta\left\{J z c_{i}+J_{1}\left(c_{i+1}+c_{i-1}\right)+J_{2}\left(c_{i+2}+c_{i-2}\right)\right\}, \quad(8)
$$

$$
\eta_{i}=\beta\left\{J z s_{i}+J_{1}\left(s_{i+1}+s_{i-1}\right)+J_{2}\left(s_{i+2}+s_{i-2}\right)\right\}, \quad(9)
$$

The function $\tau(\xi_i,\eta_i,h)$ is rewritten in the form of a
series as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau(\xi, \eta, h)=2 \pi & I_{0}(\xi) I_{0}(\eta) I_{0}(h)\left[1+2 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} P_{2 k}(\xi) P_{k}(h)+2 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k} P_{2 k}(\eta) P_{k}(h)\right. \\
& \left.+2 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k} P_{2 k}(\xi) P_{2 m}(\eta)\left\{P_{k+m}(h)+P_{k-m}(h)\right\}\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

where $I_n(z)$ is the modified Bessel function of $n$-th order and $P_n(z)=I_n(z)/I_0(z)$. Equation (10) is useful in case
a table of the modified Bessel function is utilised.

Generally speaking, by expanding $\Phi(\{c_i,s_i\})$ with respect to $c_i$ and $s_i$, we obtain a matrix $\boldsymbol{A}$ which is expressed
in terms of the coefficients of quadratic terms of $c_i$ and $s_i$, and $T_\text{c}$ is determined by the condition that the minimum
eigenvalue of $\boldsymbol{A}$ vanishes. In the present case, $\Phi(\{c_i,s_i\})$ is expressed as

$$
\Phi(\{c_{i}, s_{i}\})=\Phi(\{0,0\})+\frac{1}{p} \sum_{i=1}^{p} \frac{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{2}\left[\left(\frac{2}{1+P_{1}(h)} c_{i}-\xi_{i}\right) c_{i}+\left(\frac{2}{1-P_{1}(h)} s_{i}-\eta_{i}\right) s_{i}+O(\{c_{i}^{4}, s_{i}^{4}, c_{i}^{2} s_{i}^{2}\})\right]. \quad(11)
$$

It is clear that $P_1(h)>0$ so long as $H>0$. Accordingly,
at the phase transition from the disordered phase, the
phase with Ising symmetry appears first. The transition
temperature $T_\text{c}$ is determined from

$$
T_{\mathrm{c}}=\left(1+P_{1}(h)\right) T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{XY}}, \quad(12)
$$

where $T_\text{c}^\text{XY}$ is given by eq. (2) with the value $a=1$. The
wave number of the ordered phase is given by eq. (3),
that is, the wave number is irrespective of value of $H$
at $T=T_\text{c}$. It is noted that in eq. (12), $T_\text{c}$ is not given
explicitly but it is contained in the factor $P_1(h)$. We can
however easily prove $\mathrm{d}T_\text{c}/\mathrm{d}H>0$, which means that $T_\text{c}$
increases monotonically with $H$ and in eq. (2) the factor
$a$ is considered as a monotonically increasing function of
$H$. By comparing the coefficients of $c_i^2$ and $s_i^2$ in eq. (11),
it is clearly observed that the temperature $T_\text{c}'$ at which
the order of $s_i$ appears, is located below $T_\text{c}$.

We study here the transition from the phase with the
Ising symmetry to the XY symmetry with finite $s_i$, using
$\Phi(\{c_i,s_i\})$ in the following expansion form with respect
to $s_i$,

$$
\Phi(\left\{c_{i}, s_{i}\right\})=\Phi(\left\{c_{i}, 0\right\})+\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{2 p} \sum_{i=1}^{p}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\left\langle\sin ^{2} \varphi_{i}\right\rangle_{\xi_{i}}} s_{i}-\eta_{i}\right) s_{i}+O\left(\left\{s_{i}^{4}\right\}\right)\right],
\tag{13}
$$

where the average $\langle\sin ^{2} \varphi_{i}\rangle_{\xi_{i}}$ . is given by

$$
\left\langle\sin ^{2} \varphi_{i}\right\rangle_{\xi_{i}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{1-\frac{P_{1}(h)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} P_{2 k}\left(\xi_{i}\right)\left(P_{k+1}(h)+P_{k-1}(h)\right)}{1+2 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(P_{2 k}\left(\xi_{i}\right) P_{k}(h)\right)}\right\}.
\tag{14}
$$

In order to obtain $T_{c}^{\prime}$, the set of values of $c_{i}$ are required. Here, we consider the phase with $q=1 / 6$, where six phase variables, $\varphi_{i}(i=1,2, \cdots, 6)$, are given by 0, $\varphi_{2}, \pi-\varphi_{2}, \pi, \pi+\varphi_{2}$ and $2 \pi-\varphi_{2}$, and $\varphi_{2}$ changes continuously. The independent order parameters required in this case are only three, $c_{1}, c_{2}$ and $s_{2}$. We obtain $T_{c}^{\prime}$ for this phase from eq. (13) in the form

$$
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\prime}=2\left\langle\sin ^{2} \varphi_{2}\right\rangle_{\xi_{2}} T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{XY}},
\tag{15}
$$

where the temperature $T$ in the factor $\langle\sin ^{2} \varphi_{2}\rangle_{\xi_{2}}$ should be replaced by $T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\prime}$.

The numerical result for $T_{c}^{\prime}$ is shown in Fig. 1, where $J_{1}=2, J_{z}=0$ (for simplicity) and the unit $k_{\mathrm{B}}=1$ is used. The critical temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ determined from eq. (12) is also drawn. In the region labelled as XY order, the order parameter $s_{i}$ is finite and the angle $\varphi_{2}$ is estimated by $\tan ^{-1}\left(s_{i} / c_{i}\right)$. Near the multiphase point $H=0.75$ and $J_{1}=2$, a reentrant phenomenon is observed. But in the present stage, it is not clear whether or not it is true, because of the accuracy of the numerical calculation at extremely low temperatures (a calculus software, Mathcard was used). Furthermore, we cannot neglect the possibility of the first order transition in the vicinity of this point.

Irrespective of the reentrant behaviour, we see that even in the region $H<0.75$, where the freedom of XY spin survives at the ground state, such freedom is suppressed at the temperature between $T_{c}^{\prime}<T<T_{c}$ and the ordered phase belongs to the one with Ising symmetry labelled as Ising order in Fig. 1. It is noticed that even in the region of Ising order, a fluctuation of the angle $\varphi_{i}$ occurs. The distribution of $\varphi_{i}$ in the region of XY order is quite interesting. Thus, even in the region of XY order, the characteristics of the XY spin are scarcely observed in the vicinity of the critical temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}'$.

The crossover behaviour from the XY model to the Ising model is studied for the model with both competing interaction and twofold field. The phase with Ising symmetry appears at the high-temperature side of the ordered phase, while the phase with finite value of XY order parameter appears at the low-temperature side bounded by $T_{\mathrm{c}}'$. This shows that the freedom of XY spin is suppressed more strongly than in the case of the ground state. In the phase diagram of the ground state, some types of phase changes, such as devil's staircase are shown. $^{27)}$ From the continuity of the phase diagram, successive phase transitions at finite temperature are expected to occur even in the region where the freedom of XY spin survives. In such a region, however, jumps at the transitions are considered to be small in comparison with those occurring for the ANNNI model.

As for the twofold field, it can be replaced by the cooperative type. $^{26)}$ The behaviour of such system is also quite interesting and the results will be reported elsewhere in the near future.
