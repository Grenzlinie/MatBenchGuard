# SURFACE SCIENCE LETTERS

## PHASE DIAGRAMS FOR A SQUARE LATTICE WITH TWO- AND THREE-BODY INTERACTIONS

Francisco CLARO

Instituto de Fisica, Universidad Católica de Chile, Casilla 114-D Santiago, Chile

and

Vijay KUMAR

Institut für Theoretische Physik, Freie Universität Berlin, Arnimallee 3, D-1000 Berlin 33, Fed. Rep. of Germany

Received 20 January 1982; accepted for publication 25 May 1982

The interactions of ad-atoms on a surface may be modeled with the Ising Hamiltonian conveniently extended to accomodate couplings beyond the simplest nearest neighbor. We use real space renormalization group methods to study the phase diagram for such a model for hydrogen on Pd(100). Confirming the previous Monte Carlo results we find qualitative agreement with experiment at high temperatures but with a too narrow ordered region. We report also the finding of a second order transition to a $(2 \times 2)$ superlattice structure at lower temperatures.

The observation of order-disorder transitions in layers of atoms physi- sorbed or chemisorbed over a planar substrate has made possible a direct comparison of experiment with results on two-dimensional models [1-10]. The substrate presents an ordered array of sites where the adatoms may sit and for partial coverage and a given temperature their choice of site is determined by the mutual interactions. Hydrogen atoms on a (100) surface of paladium show for example below room temperature a transition into an arrangement in which all nearest neighbor sites are empty and all next to nearest sites are occupied [4] at half the monolayer coverage. A model consistent with this preferred configuration is one in which the interaction between adatoms is repulsive and felt only when they are nearest neighbors. Actual calculations support this model as a first approximation [11]. The resulting Hamiltonian may be transformed easily into an Ising Hamiltonian with antiferromagnetic coupling for which the critical properties are known. The experimental phase diagram and that of the model do not agree in two most prominent features, however. First, the model gives an ordered region that is too narrow. Second, its results are symmetric about half coverage, while the experimental results are not. One may try to improve the model by adding interactions that lower the

energy of the ordered arrangement. This is achieved with a next to nearest neighbor attraction. Furthermore one may introduce an asymmetry on the phase diagram by including a three-body interaction. The Hamiltonian that results maps then into a modified Ising model represented by [9]

$$
H=-B \sum_{i} \sigma_{i}-J_{2} \sum_{\langle i, j\rangle} \sigma_{i} \sigma_{j}-J_{2}^{\prime} \sum_{\langle i, j\rangle} \sigma_{i} \sigma_{j}-J_{3} \sum_{\langle i, j, k\rangle} \sigma_{i} \sigma_{j} \sigma_{k}. \tag{1}
$$

Here $B$ is the external field, $J_{2}, J_{2}^{\prime}$ and $J_{3}$ are the nearest, next to nearest and three body coupling constants and the sums run over the corresponding pairs or triplets. $\sigma_{i}$ is the spin variable at the site $i$ and it may take on values $\pm 1$. Its average is related to the coverage $\theta$ through the equality

$$
\theta=\frac{1}{2}(1-\langle\sigma\rangle). \tag{2}
$$

The nearest neighbor Ising Hamiltonian is then recovered simply by setting $J_{2}'=J_{3}=0$. Further, the Hamiltonian (1) has the property that the phase diagram for $-J_{3}$ could be obtained from the one corresponding to $+J_{3}$ by taking the image about $\theta=1 / 2$.

The critical properties of (1) have been previously studied using Monte Carlo techniques [9]. This method predicts a phase diagram that for all reasonable values of the coupling constants disagrees with experiment. This leads to questioning of the method by which experimental points were ob- tained. With the aim of adding evidence about predictions of (1) we here present results for the square lattice obtained with real space renormalization group methods [12-16]. As we shall see, comparison of results from the two approaches shows good qualitative agreement.

It is well known that in order to treat the antiferromagnetic coupling properly by scaling the lattice one must choose cell for grouping the spins that preserves the symmetry after every transformation [14]. Here we must be especially careful because the interaction added to the original Ising Hamilto- nian brings in the possibility of ordered superlattice arrangements. We are particularly interested in not losing the $(2 \times 2)$ structure that corresponds to an ordered superlattice constant equal to twice the original and perfect coverage $1 / 4$ (or $3 / 4$ ). A choice having the desired property is shown in fig. 1. Each cell contains five spins and we have used a transformation that takes twenty spins of the original system into four cells, with an extension to the rest of the lattice through periodic boundary conditions. Phase boundaries are determined by watching the flows in the space of coupling constants as the renormalization proceeds, or by following maxima in the derivatives of the free energy.

We first tested the effect of the attractive coupling between next to nearest neighbors. Inspection of the figure shows that the added interaction has a very small effect on the width of the central peak. Critical fields were determined sharply by discontinuities in the flows in the coupling constant space. We also looked at the susceptibility and found it to be divergent below $T_{\mathrm{t}}$ and quite sharp at higher temperatures.

It is clear that the low temperature region is changed qualitatively by the added interaction, yet besides the shift in the position of the concentration for maximum transition temperature the high temperature region is changed little. In particular, the width of the peak is not changed much as can be seen in fig. 3 where we have included for comparison the data from fig. 4 (cross). Experimental points are shown in the latter as crosses and we see that the improvement over the results with $J_3 = 0$ leaves us still far away from experimental data. We studied larger values of $J_3$ and included a four-body interaction as well with similar results.

In conclusion, using renormalization group techniques we have verified that two- and three-body interactions treated by means of the equivalent extended Ising Hamiltonian (1) are capable of reproducing only qualitatively the experimental data for hydrogen on Pd(100) with an ordered region much too narrow. Our results are similar to the Monte Carlo data, except that we are able to locate a second order transition peculiar to the added three-body interaction that was searched for and not found in Monte Carlo studies [9]. We treated a 20 spin system which is about as high a number as real space renormalization group techniques can treat in reasonable computing time. This limited our search for the full critical curve for this transition. We hope our finding will stimulate a refining of the Monte Carlo analysis or a study by other methods of the region where the $(2 \times 2)$ transformation occurs.
