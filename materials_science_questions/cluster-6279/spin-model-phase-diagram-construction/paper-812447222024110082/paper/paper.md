# Phase structure of clock spin models with dipolar and quadrupolar interactions

Shyh-Tzer Chen
Department of Physics, National Kaohsiung Normal University, Kaohsiung, Taiwan, ROC

Received 27 January 1993; revised manuscript received 1 March 1993; accepted for publication 5 March 1993
Communicated by L.J. Sham

The Migdal-Kadanoff renormalization group method is used to study the phase diagrams of a two-dimensional $p$-state clock model with both dipolar and quadrupolar interactions. The nature of the phase transition is discussed.

Model Hamiltonians for spin systems with dipolar and quadrupolar interactions have been studied [1-9] because some physical systems are characterized by two order parameters. This model has been used to describe phase separation in $^3$He-$^4$He mixtures [1], phase transition in a simple fluid [2], and phase separation in binary alloys [7].

In a previous paper we used an infinite range method to study the phase structure of the $p$-state clock model with dipolar and quadrupolar interactions [8]. We found that the even clock models have dipolar and quadrupolar phase transitions, i.e., they exhibit a triple point, while the odd clock models have only a dipolar phase transition. However, it is well-known that the phase diagram of the clock models with purely dipolar interaction exhibits a dipolar transition for $p < p_{\rm c}$, while for $p \geqslant p_{\rm c}$ a massless phase separates the disorder and dipolar phases [10-15]. In this paper we will apply the Migdal-Kadanoff renormalization group (MKRG) method to study the phase structure in the two-dimensional $p$-state clock spin models with dipolar and quadrupolar interactions. It is interesting to see how the massless phase will exist in our model. Note that José et al. and Barber [16,17] used a simple Migdal-Kadanoff recursion relation to study the purely dipolar clock models in two dimensions, and found the existence of a massless phase in this model.

The Hamiltonian of the $p$-state clock models with dipolar and quadrupole interactions is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & J \sum_{\langle i j\rangle}\left[1-\cos \left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\right)\right] \\
& +K \sum_{\langle i j\rangle}\left[1-\cos ^{2}\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\right)\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

$$
\theta_{i}=\frac{2 \pi}{p} n_{i} \quad\left(n_{i}=0,1, \ldots, p-1\right),
\tag{2}
$$

where the sum is over all nearest-neighbour pairs, $J$ is the dipolar coupling constant and $K$ is the quadrupolar coupling constant.

For convenience we define the potential $V(\theta)$ as

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(\theta) & =V\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\right) \\
& =-\beta J\left[1-\cos \left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\right)\right] \\
& -\beta K\left[1-\cos ^{2}\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\right)\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

where $\beta=1 / k_{\rm B} T$ and the Boltzmann factor is

$$
f(\theta)=\exp [V(\theta)].
\tag{4}
$$

therefore, the Hamiltonian can be written as

$$
-\beta H=\sum_{\langle i j\rangle} V\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\right).
\tag{5}
$$

After a Migdal-Kadanoff transformation [18,19] by bond moving and point decimating, then, while the spatial rescaling factor $b=2$, the renormalized potential is given by

$$
\exp [\bar{V}(\theta)]=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}_{\phi} \exp [2 V(\phi)+2 V(\theta-\phi)]}{\operatorname{Tr}_{\phi} \exp [4 V(\phi)]}.
\tag{6}
$$


After the numerical calculation of the above renor-
malization group equation, we find that the clock
models with dipolar and quadrupolar interactions
have four phases, which are:

(a) the disorder phase: iterations drive the Boltz-
mann weight $f(\theta)$ to unity for all $\theta$ corresponding to
the infinite-temperature fixed point;

(b) the dipolar phase: the $f(\theta)$ is driven to zero
for all $\theta$ corresponding to the zero-temperature fixed
point;

(c) the massless phase: after iterations, the $f(\theta)$ is
irregular and has a value greater than 1;

(d) the quadrupolar phase: the $f(\theta)$ for $\theta=\pi$ is
driven to 1, and all the other $\theta$ are close to zero [8].

quadrupolar state, and then to a dipolar state.

there are two phase transitions called Kosterlitz-
Thouless (KT) [20] and dipolar transitions, where
a KT transition is followed by a dipolar transition.
However, for $\alpha_{\mathrm{A}}<\alpha<\alpha_{\mathrm{B}}$, these models have only
one dipolar phase transition. The phase structures of
the even clock models have a quadrupolar phase in-

rupolar phase separates the disorder and dipolar
phases.

phases in which there exists a massless phase, which
is between the disorder and quadrupolar phases.

In conclusion, we use the MKRG method to analyze the phase structure in the $p$-state clock spin models with dipolar and quadrupolar interactions. Our results show that the phase structures of the $p\leqslant5$ clock models are in agreement with those of the same models using the infinite range method [8]. However, for large $p$, the phase diagrams are quite different. In certain ranges of $\alpha$, a massless phase exists for the $5<p\leqslant12$ clock models. For all $\alpha$, the phase structures of the $p>12$ clock models indicate the existence of a massless phase.
