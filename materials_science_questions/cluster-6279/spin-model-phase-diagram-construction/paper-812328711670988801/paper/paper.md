# Tricritical behavior in the diluted transverse spin-1 Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field

K. Htoutou$^{\mathrm{a}}$, A. Oubelkacem$^{\mathrm{a}}$, A. Ainane$^{\mathrm{a}}$, M. Saber$^{\mathrm{a,b},*}$

$^{\mathrm{a}}$Physics Department, Faculty of Sciences, University of Moulay Ismail, B.P. 4010 Meknes, Morocco
$^{\mathrm{b}}$Max-Planck Institut für Physik Complexer Systeme, Nöthnitzer Str. 38, 01187 Dresden, Germany

Received 21 March 2004; received in revised form 13 September 2004
Available online 5 November 2004

## Abstract

The transverse spin-1 Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field exhibits a tricritical behavior. Within the effective field theory with a probability distribution technique that accounts for the self-spin correlations, we have studied the influence of site dilution on this behavior and have calculated the temperature-transverse field-longitudinal crystal field-concentration phase diagrams and determined, in particular, the influence of the concentration of magnetic atoms $c$ on the tricritical behavior. We have found that the tricritical point appears for large values of the concentration $c$ of magnetic atoms and disappears with the increase in dilution (small values of $c$). Results for square lattice are calculated numerically and some interesting results are obtained. In certain ranges of values of the strength of the longitudinal crystal field $D/J$ when it becomes sufficiently negative, we found re-entrant phenomenon, which disappears with increase in the value of the strength of the transverse field.

## 1. Introduction

Over recent years, there has been considerable interest in the theoretical study of the transverse spin-1 Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field. In the case of absence of the transverse field, the magnetic properties of the system have been studied by many approximate methods [1–11] and have successfully been used to describe cooperative physical systems, such as ternary alloys, multicomponent fluids as well as various magnetic problems, such as surface magnetism, thin films and multilayers, which are now current topics of magnetism [12–14]. Many authors have examined the critical properties of the system. These are of interest because a number of

Recently, it has been shown that the transverse spin-1 Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field shows a tricritical behavior, i.e., there exists a point at which the phase transition changes from the second order to the first order when the transverse field $\Omega$ and the single ion anisotropy $D$ (or the longitudinal crystal field) take the critical values $\Omega_{\mathrm{t}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{t}}$. Within the effective field theory based on the operator differential technique, Jiang et al. [5] have studied the pure transverse spin-1 Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field and have discussed the existence of a tricritical point when the value of $D$ becomes sufficiently negative in the transverse field for the honeycomb lattice. It is found that the tricritical point exists for $1.235<-D / J \leqslant 1.427$ and for $\Omega<0.934$. For the square lattice [11], we have found that the tricritical point exists for $1.568<-D / J \leqslant 1.8892$, when $\Omega / J<1.447$.

On the other hand, many authors have examined the critical phenomena of the diluted spin-S Ising model with spin of magnitude $S=\frac{1}{2}, 1$ and the mixed spin Ising model in the presence of a longitudinal crystal field or a transverse field by various methods, such as the effective field theory [15-22], the finite cluster approach based on a single-site cluster approximation and some interesting results were obtained. Generally speaking, the magnetic properties of the diluted magnetic systems may be obviously different from those of the corresponding pure systems. It has been known that a lot of new physical phenomena can appear in these diluted magnetic systems. Recently, the phase transition of the bond-diluted spin-1 transverse Ising model with longitudinal crystal field interaction has been examined for the honeycomb lattice [26], and some interesting results are obtained.

The purpose of this work is to investigate the phase diagrams of the diluted spin-1 transverse Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field using the numerical diagonalization method within the framework of the effective field theory based on a probability distribution technique that accounts for the single site kinematic relations [22,23] on a square lattice $(N=4)$. In this paper, we investigate the study of the dependence of the critical temperature versus concentration of magnetic atoms $c$, the strength of the transverse field and the strength of the longitudinal crystal field. We discuss also the tricritical behavior of the system. Besides, we have found that our system exhibits the reentrant phenomenon depending on the values of the strength of the crystal and transverse fields. Our results such as tricritical behavior and reentrant phase transition behavior are in agreement with those obtained in [26]. In Section 2, we outline the formalism and derive the equations that determine the critical and tricritical temperatures. The numerical results are discussed in Section 3. Section 4 is devoted to a brief conclusion.

## 2. Model and formalism

We consider a diluted transverse spin-1 Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field on a square lattice. The Hamiltonian of the system is given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & -\sum_{\langle i j\rangle} J_{i j} c_{i} c_{j} S_{i z} S_{j z}-\Omega \sum_{i} c_{i} S_{i x} \\
& -D \sum_{i} c_{i} S_{i z}^{2},
\end{aligned}\tag{1}
$$
where $S_{i z}$ and $S_{i x}$ denote, respectively, the $z$ and $x$ components of a quantum spin $\vec{S}_{i}$ of magnitude $S=1$ at site $i, J_{i j}=J$ is the strength of the constant coupling between the spins at nearest-neighbor sites $i$ and $j, \Omega$ is the applied transverse field, $D$ is the longitudinal crystal field and $c_{i}$ is the occupation number on the lattice site $i, c_{i}=1$ if the lattice site is occupied by a magnetic atom and zero otherwise.

In the single-site cluster theory, attention is focused on a cluster comprising just a single selected spin labelled 0, and the neighboring spins with which it directly interacts. To this end, the Hamiltonian is split into two parts, $H=H_{0}+H^{\prime}$, where $H_{0}$ is that part of the Hamiltonian containing the spin 0, namely
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta H_{0}= & -\beta c_{0} S_{0 z} \sum_{j=1}^{N} c_{j} S_{j z} \\
& -\beta \sqrt{2} c_{0} \Omega S_{0 x}-\beta c_{0} D S_{0 z}^{2}.
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
$$

We note that $H_0$ and $H'$ do not commute. Using the approximate relation introduced by Sà Barreto et al. [24].

$$
\langle c_{0} S_{0 z}^{p}\rangle=\left\langle c_{0} \frac{\operatorname{Tr}_{0}\left[S_{0 z}^{p} \exp \left(-\beta H_{0}\right)\right]}{\operatorname{Tr}_{0}\left[\exp \left(-\beta H_{0}\right)\right]}\right\rangle.
\tag{3}
$$

In Eq. (3) the single angular brackets denote the thermal average for a fixed spatial configuration of the occupied sites. If the exchange interactions are restricted to nearest-neighbor interactions only, evaluation of the inner traces over the selected spin $S_0$ with its occupation number $c_0$ in Eq. (3) yields the following equation:

$$
\langle c_{0} S_{0 z}^{p}\rangle=\left\langle c_{0} F_{p z}\left(\beta J \sum_{j=1}^{N} c_{j} S_{j z}, \beta \Omega, \beta D\right)\right\rangle,
\tag{4}
$$

where $N$ is the number of nearest neighbors and in particular,

$$
\begin{aligned}
& F_{1 z}\left(\beta \sum_{j}^{N} c_{j} S_{j z}, \beta \Omega, \beta D\right) \\
& \quad=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{3}\left\langle\Psi_{k}\left|S_{0 z}\right| \Psi_{k}\right\rangle \exp \left(-\beta E_{k}\right)}{\sum_{k=1}^{3} \exp \left(-\beta E_{k}\right)},
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& F_{2 z}\left(\beta \sum_{j}^{N} c_{j} S_{j z}, \beta \Omega, \beta D\right) \\
& \quad=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{3}\left\langle\Psi_{k}\left|S_{0 z}^{2}\right| \Psi_{k}\right\rangle \exp \left(-\beta E_{k}\right)}{\sum_{k=1}^{3} \exp \left(-\beta E_{k}\right)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

In deriving these results one can first find the eigenvalues $E_k$ and their corresponding eigenstates $|\Psi_k\rangle$ [11] of the Hamiltonian $H_0$ in a representation in $S_{0z}$ is diagonal:

$$
E_{1}=\frac{2}{3} p \sin \left(\frac{\theta}{3}\right)-\frac{2}{3},
\tag{7}
$$

$$
E_{2}=-\frac{p}{3}\left[\sin \left(\frac{\theta}{3}\right)+\sqrt{3} \cos \left(\frac{\theta}{3}\right)\right]-\frac{2}{3},
\tag{8}
$$

$$
E_{3}=\frac{p}{3}\left[\sqrt{3} \cos \left(\frac{\theta}{3}\right)+\sin \left(\frac{\theta}{3}\right)\right]-\frac{2}{3}
\tag{9}
$$

with
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \theta=\operatorname{Arc} \sin \left[\frac{a_{3}\left(9 a_{1}^{2}-9 a_{2}^{2}-a_{3}^{2}\right)}{p^{3}}\right], \\
& p=\left(3 a_{1}^{2}+6 a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

and
$$
\left|\Psi_{k}\right\rangle=\alpha_{k}|1\rangle+\beta_{k}|0\rangle+\gamma_{k}|-1\rangle
\tag{11}
$$

with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{k}= & \pm a_{2} \\
& \times \frac{\left[a_{1}-\left(a_{3}+E_{k}\right)\right]}{\sqrt{2 a_{2}^{2}\left[a_{1}^{2}+\left(a_{3}+E_{k}\right)^{2}\right]+\left[a_{1}^{2}-\left(a_{3}+E_{k}\right)^{2}\right]}}, \\
\beta_{k}= & -\frac{\left[a_{1}+\left(a_{3}+E_{k}\right)\right]}{a_{2}} \alpha_{k}, \\
\gamma_{k}= & -\frac{\left[a_{1}+\left(a_{3}+E_{k}\right)\right]}{\left[a_{1}-\left(a_{3}+E_{k}\right)\right]} \alpha_{k},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $k=1,2,3$ and $a_{1}=\beta \sum_{j=1}^{N} c_{j} S_{j z}, \quad a_{2}=$ $\beta \Omega / \sqrt{2}, a_{3}=\beta D$. The next step, for the quenched system, is to carry out the configurational averaging over the neighboring spins $S_{jz}$ with their occupation numbers $c_j$ that appear in the right-hand side of Eq. (4). For a spin-1 system the ordering parameters of interest are $m_z=m_{1z}$ and $q_z=m_{2z}$ defined by

$$
m_{p z}=\langle c_{0} S_{0 z}^{p}\rangle.
\tag{12}
$$

To make progress, the simplest approximation of neglecting the correlations between different sites will be made. This is achieved by introducing the probability distribution of the spin variable $S_{iz}$ with its occupation number $c_i$, $P(c_i,S_{iz})$, which is given by Tucker et al. [22,25]

$$
\begin{aligned}
P\left(c_{i}, S_{i z}\right)= & \frac{1}{3}(1-c) \delta\left(c_{i}\right)\left[\delta\left(S_{i z}+1\right)\right. \\
& \left.+\delta\left(S_{i z}\right)+\delta\left(S_{i z}-1\right)\right] \\
& +\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(q_{z}-m_{z}\right) \delta\left(S_{i z}+1\right)\right. \\
& +2\left(1-q_{z}\right) \delta\left(S_{i z}\right) \\
& \left.+\left(q_{z}+m_{z}\right) \delta\left(S_{i z}-1\right)\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

with
$$
c=\langle c_{i}\rangle.
\tag{14}
$$

Then, we get the following equation for long-
itudinal magnetization:
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{z}= & 2^{-N} \sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{N} \sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}} \sum_{\mu_{3}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{3}} C_{\mu_{1}}^{N} C_{\mu_{2}}^{N-\mu_{1}} \\
& \times C_{\mu_{3}}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}} 2^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{3}}(1-c)^{\mu_{1}}\left(q_{z}-m_{z}\right)^{\mu_{2}} \\
& \times\left(c-q_{z}\right)^{\mu_{3}}\left(q_{z}+m_{z}\right)^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}} F_{1 z} \\
& \times\left[\beta J\left(N-\mu_{1}-2 \mu_{2}-\mu_{3}\right), \beta \Omega, \beta D\right],
\end{aligned}\qquad(15)
$$
where $N=4$ in the case of a square lattice which is
considered here and $C_{k}^{l}$ are the binomial coeffi
cients, $C_{k}^{l}=l! / k!(l-k)!$.

The equation for the longitudinal quadrupolar
moment is obtained by substituting the function
$F_{1 z}$ by $F_{2 z}$ in the expression of the longitudinal
magnetization Eq. (15). This yields
$$
q_{z}=m_{z}\left[F_{1 z}(\gamma, \Omega, D) \rightarrow F_{2 z}(\gamma, \Omega, D)\right].\qquad(16)
$$

We have thus obtained the self-consistent Eqs.
(15), (16) for both the longitudinal magnetization
and quadrupolar moment which can be solved
directly by numerical iteration. No further alge-
braic manipulation is necessary.

In order to determine the transition temperature
$T_{\mathrm{c}}$, let us expand the right-hand sides of Eqs. (15),
(16), we obtain the following equation for $m_{z}$:
$$
m_{z}=a m_{z}+b m_{z}^{3}+\cdots\qquad(17)
$$
with $a$ and $b$ are given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
a= & 2^{-N} \sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{N} \sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}} \sum_{\mu_{3}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{3}} \sum_{i=0}^{\mu_{2}} \sum_{j=0}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}} \\
& \times 2^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{3}} C_{\mu_{1}}^{N} C_{\mu_{2}}^{N-\mu_{1}} C_{\mu_{3}}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}} \\
& \times C_{i}^{\mu_{2}} C_{j}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}}(-1)^{i} q_{0 z}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{3}-i-j} \\
& \times(1-c)^{\mu_{1}}\left(c-q_{0 z}\right)^{\mu_{2}} \delta_{1, i+j} \\
& \times F_{1 z}\left[\beta J\left(N-\mu_{1}-2 \mu_{2}-\mu_{3}\right), \beta \Omega, \beta D\right],
\end{aligned}\qquad(18)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b= & 2^{-N} \sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{N} \sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}} \sum_{\mu_{3}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{3}} \sum_{i=0}^{\mu_{2}} \sum_{j=0}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}} \\
& \times 2^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{3}} C_{\mu_{1}}^{N} C_{\mu_{2}}^{N-\mu_{1}} C_{\mu_{3}}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}} \\
& \times C_{i}^{\mu_{2}} C_{j}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}}(-1)^{i} q_{0 z}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{3}-i-j} \\
& \times(1-c)^{\mu_{1}}\left(c-q_{0 z}\right)^{\mu_{3}} \delta_{3, i+j} \\
& \times F_{1 z}\left[\beta J\left(N-\mu_{1}-2 \mu_{2}-\mu_{3}\right), \beta \Omega, \beta D\right]
\end{aligned}\qquad(19)
$$
with $q_{0 z}$ determined from
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{0 z}= & 2^{-N} \sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{N} \sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}} \sum_{\mu_{3}=0}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{3}} \\
& \times C_{\mu_{1}}^{N} C_{\mu_{2}}^{N-\mu_{1}} C_{\mu_{3}}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}} 2^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{3}}(1-c)^{\mu_{1}} \\
& \times\left(c-q_{0 z}\right)^{\mu_{3}} q_{0 z}^{N-\mu_{1}-\mu_{3}} F_{2 z} \\
& \times\left[\beta J\left(N-\mu_{1}-2 \mu_{2}-\mu_{3}\right), \beta \Omega, \beta D\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad(20)
$$

The second-order phase transition line is then
determined by the conditions
$$
a=1, \quad \text { and } \quad b\langle 0.\qquad(21)
$$

Near the second-order transition, the magneti-
zation $m_{z}$ is given by
$$
m_{z}^{2}=\frac{1-a}{b}.\qquad(22)
$$

The right-hand side of Eq. (22) must be positive.
If it is not the case, the transition is of the first
order, and hence the point at which
$$
a=1, \quad \text { and } \quad b=0\qquad(23)
$$
is the tricritical point.

### 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, using the effective field theory with a probability distribution technique that accounts for the self-spin correlation functions, we have studied the phase diagrams of the diluted transverse spin-1 Ising model with a longitudinal crystal field as function of the concentration of magnetic atoms $c$, the transverse field $\Omega/J$ and the longitudinal crystal field $D/J$. We have found that the critical temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}/J$ decreases with the increase of dilution at fixed values of $\Omega/J$ or $D/J$ and can decrease or increase with increasing or decreasing the values of the transverse field and the longitudinal crystal field. As discussed in this work, the system exhibits the tricritical behavior. This behavior depends on the value of the concentration of magnetic atoms $c$, the transverse field $\Omega/J$ and the longitudinal crystal fields $D/J$. We have found that the probability of the existence of tricritical behavior decreases with the increase of the transverse field $\Omega/J$ and the decrease of the concentration of magnetic atoms $c$. The tricritical behavior appears for great concentration $c>0.7$ and disappears otherwise. Furthermore, the tricritical concentration increases to approach of the corresponding value of the pure case $c_{\mathrm{t}} \rightarrow 1$ when the value of the longitudinal crystal field $D/J$ becomes sufficiently negative. Finally, we note that, our results show a re-entrant phenomenon in a certain range of negative values of the strength longitudinal crystal field $D/J$ when $1.0<-D/J \leqslant 1.88$. This phenomenon disappears

with increasing the values of the transverse field.
Our results are qualitatively similar to those
obtained in Ref. [26] for the honeycomb lattice case.
