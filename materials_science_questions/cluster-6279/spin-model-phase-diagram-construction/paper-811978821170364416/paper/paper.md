# The Spin-1 Anisotropic Heisenberg Ferromagnet with Biquadratic Exchange

By
H.A. BROWN

An anisotropic Heisenberg ferromagnet with biquadratic exchange is considered. The Hamiltonian is taken to be

$$
H=-2 J\left[\overrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}_{1} \cdot \overrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}_{2}+\alpha\left(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}_{1} \cdot \overrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}_{2}\right)^{2}\right]-\mu \mathrm{H}\left(\mathrm{S}_{1 \mathrm{z}}+\mathrm{S}_{2 \mathrm{z}}\right)-\mathrm{D}\left(\mathrm{S}_{1 \mathrm{z}}+\mathrm{S}_{2 \mathrm{z}}\right)^{2}
$$

and the constant-coupling approximation is used. Included here on the usual Heisenberg exchange integral, J, biquadratic exchange, $\alpha J$, the internal and/or applied field interaction, $\mu \mathrm{H}$, and the anisotropy term, $\mathrm{D}\left(\mathrm{S}_{1 \mathrm{z}}+\mathrm{S}_{2 \mathrm{z}}\right)^{2}$.

Because of the form of the anisotropy chosen here (it is usually assumed to be $\left.D\left(S_{1 z}^{2}+S_{2 z}^{2}\right) / 1 /\right)$, the Hamiltonian is diagonal in the $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}=\overrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}_{1}+\overrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}_{2}, \mathrm{M}=\mathrm{S}_{1 \mathrm{z}}+$ $+\mathrm{S}_{2 \mathrm{z}}$ representation and the partition function is obtained in closed form (which would not be the case with the other anisotropy term).

The critical temperatures, $T_{c}$, are evaluated for various values of $\alpha$ and D/J. As found for the isotropic case /2/, the transitions are second-order for small $\alpha$, but for large enough $\alpha$, they become first-order with a discontinuity

Fig. 1 shows that the second-order transition temperature decreases as $\alpha$ increases until a critical value, $\alpha_{c}$, is reached and the first-order transition occurs at a higher temperature. Fig. 2 shows the magnetization as a function of temperature for various values of $\alpha$, above and below $\alpha_{c}$. Fig. 2a refers to an isotropic system (D=0) for which $\alpha_{c} \approx 1.05$, and Fig. 2b to the case D=J for which $\alpha_{c} \approx 1.95$. There is little qualitative difference between the two, aside from the fact that the transition temperatures are higher in the anisotropic system. Both show the increasing sharpness, for larger $\alpha$, of the decrease in the magnetization as $T_{c}$ is approached from below, until for $\alpha>\alpha_{c}$, the curves show a discontinuous drop to zero.

The critical values of $\alpha$ as well as the transition temperature increase for larger values of the anisotropy parameter as shown in Fig. 3. The more anisotropic systems as well as those with a larger biquadratic exchange term are more likely to undergo a first-order transition, as seen from Fig. 1 and 3.

The advantage of using the anisotropy term as done here, instead of as in /1/, thereby obtaining a simple expression for the partition function and avoiding having to resort to numerical solutions, is that results are easily obtained for all values of $\alpha$ and D, and the first-order transitions uncovered.
