# Discrete unusual mixed-bridged trinuclear $Co^{III}_2Co^{II}$ and pentanuclear $Ni^{II}$ coordination complexes supported by a phenolate-based ligand: theoretical and experimental magneto-structural study†

Richa,‡$^{a}$ Muni Rathnam,‡$^{b}$ Akhilesh Kumar, $^{cd}$ Indresh Verma, $^{c}$ Julia Kłak, $^{*e}$ Joan Cano, $^{f}$ Antonio J. Mota, $^{*g}$ Amit Rajput*$^{a}$ and Himanshu Arora *$^{a}$

Two new complexes $[Co^{III}_2Co^{II}(\mu -OL)_2(\mu -OOCCH_3)_2(\mu -N_3)_2(N_3)_2]\cdot Et_2O$ ($1\cdot Et_2O$) and $[Ni^{II}_5(\mu -OL)_4(\mu -OOCCH_3)_2-(OOCCH_3)_2(\mu -N_3)_2]\cdot CH_3CN$ (2) (HOL = 2-((2-(pyridin-2-yl)ethylamino)methyl)phenol) have been synthesized and characterized by elemental analysis, IR, and UV/Vis spectroscopy. Structural analysis revealed that **1** is a discrete trinuclear and **2** is a discrete pentanuclear coordination complex. In complex **1**, terminal metal ($Co^{III}$) is in a distorted octahedral $MN_4O_2$ environment where coordination is satisfied by two nitrogen atoms and one oxygen atom of the ligand, and an oxygen atom from the acetate group and two nitrogen atoms from azide (one each from the bridging and terminal ones). The central metal ($Co^{II}$) is also in a distorted octahedral $MN_2O_4$ environment where coordination is satisfied by two oxygen atoms from the phenoxo bridge, two oxygen atoms from the acetate bridge and two nitrogen atoms from the azide bridge. In complex **2**, each Ni is in a distorted octahedral $MN_2O_4/MN_3O_3$ environment where coordination is satisfied by different donor atoms (amine, pyridine, acetate, phenolate and azide). Direct current (DC) variable-temperature magnetic susceptibility measurements on polycrystalline samples of $1\cdot Et_2O$ and **2** were carried out in the temperature range of 1.8–300 K. The magnetic behaviour of $1\cdot Et_2O$ indicates the presence of magnetic anisotropy of the cobalt(II) ion. The magnetic data of **2** reveal the coexistence of antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic interactions within the $Ni_5$ unit. Theoretical calculations made from the crystal structures reveal a very good agreement with the experimental findings.

## Introduction

In the modern era of coordination complexes, which consist of metal–ligand coordination, the metal centers are interconnected by organic linkers and metal–ligand units which generate a variety of supramolecular architectures. These systems have attracted great attention due to their diverse structural topologies and potential applications in areas of catalysis and materials science.¹ Controlling and manipulating the self-assembly process is a tough scholarly challenge that attracts interest from researchers.² Magneto-structural studies on polynuclear complexes, aimed at understanding the underlying structural factors that govern the magnetic-exchange interaction between paramagnetic centers mediated by ligand bridge(s), continue to be of interest.³⁻⁵

Creating new complexes with pre-dispensed magnetic properties is a contemporary challenge for synthetic inorganic chemists these days and, for this, the coherent synthetic design for the alteration of the solid-state structure is important. The versatility of the azide ion has been conveyed by researchers with their considerable contributions in the literature.⁷⁻¹³ The coordination modes of the azide ligands greatly affect the nature and magnitude of the magnetic exchange interaction in these polynuclear azide complexes. It is well proven in the literature that the complexes with double symmetric EE bridges produce antiferromagnetic coupling,⁹ whereas symmetric EO bridges usually exhibit ferromagnetic coupling.¹⁰

Polynuclear metal carboxylates¹⁴ are good candidates for the investigation of magnetic-exchange interactions between adjacent metal ions. It is well acknowledged that the carboxylate group can bridge metal ions to give rise to a variety of polynuclear transition metal complexes, ranging from discrete entities to three-dimensional systems.¹⁵⁻¹⁷ The carboxylate group can assume many types of bridging conformations, the most important being triatomic *syn-syn, syn-anti, anti-anti* and monoatomic ones.¹⁸,¹⁹ In works that further developed this approach, the research group of Rettig *et al.* reported that the generation of binuclear systems is favored by the *syn-syn* conformation whereas *syn-anti* favors the formation of extended structures with varying nuclearity. The former mediates the antiferromagnetic exchange pathway between the metal centers, while the latter favors the ferromagnetic exchange interaction with some exceptions where antiferromagnetic coupling is favored.¹⁶

Ribas *et al.*, Wang *et al.* and Zeng *et al.*, among others conferred that azide and carboxylate show great versatility and diversity in bridging metal ions and transmitting magnetic coupling, and they are among the most extensively used bridges in the construction of molecular magnetic systems.⁶ᵃ,²⁰ In past decades, many researchers reported magnetic systems with azide or carboxylate as bridges,²¹,²² but the versatility and diversity of the bridges cause great difficulties in the rational design of magnetically isolated chains. Using azide and carboxylate as co-bridges is an interesting approach to magnetic systems, but the co-bridging of the two anionic groups may be hindered by the competition in coordination and charge balance. In the past decade, Gao *et al.* have demonstrated that the use of zwitterionic pyridinium carboxylate ligands, in comparison with the usual anionic carboxylates, is an efficient synthetic approach toward 1D coordination motifs with simultaneous $\mu_{1,3}$-carboxylate and $\mu_{1,1}$-azide bridges.²³

In the present work, the coordination, and magnetic and electronic properties of cobalt and nickel complexes of an amine-phenol ligand, HOL, with N₂O donor atoms are described (Scheme 2). For both complexes **1** and **2**, we have performed density functional theory (DFT) at the B3LYP level to determine the correlation between the experimental and theoretical magnetic coupling constant ($J$) values. Thus, in our continuous search for magnetic systems, we have chosen a simpler ligand (HOL = 2-[(2-pyridin-2-yl)ethylamino)methyl]phenol) (Scheme 2) with carboxylate and azide as co-ligands to synthesize and characterize new $\mu_{1,3}$-carboxylate, phenoxo and $\mu_{1,1}$-azide-bridged complexes $[Co^{III}_{2}Co^{II}(\mu-OL)_{2}(\mu-OOCCH_{3})_{2}(\mu-N_{3})_{2}(N_{3})_{2}]\cdot Et_{2}O$ ($\textbf{1·Et}_{2}\textbf{O}$) and $[Ni^{II}_{5}(\mu-OL)_{4}(\mu-OOCCH_{3})_{2}(OOCCH_{3})_{2}(\mu-N_{3})_{2}]\cdot CH_{3}CN$ (**2**), with closely similar metal-ligand bonding characteristics. All complexes are structurally characterized. We present here also the magnetostructural behavior of **1-2**. Molecular structures of all the synthesized complexes were confirmed by a single crystal X-ray crystallographic technique (see Fig. 1-4; Tables 1 and 2).

## Results and discussion
### Syntheses and general characterization
$[Co^{III}_{2}Co^{II}(\mu-OL)_{2}(\mu-OOCCH_{3})_{2}(\mu-N_{3})_{2}(N_{3})_{2}]\cdot Et_{2}O$ ($\textbf{1·Et}_{2}\textbf{O}$) and $[Ni^{II}_{5}(\mu-OL)_{4}(\mu-OOCCH_{3})_{2}(OOCCH_{3})_{2}(\mu-N_{3})_{2}]\cdot CH_{3}CN$ (**2**) were synthesised in $CH_{3}OH$ by a straight forward reaction between $M^{II}(OAc)_{2}\cdot 6H_{2}O$ [M = Co and Ni] and appropriate ligand (HOL) in 1.5:1 metal ion-ligand stoichiometry. The syntheses of these complexes were based on the consideration that the phenolate-based ligand would help in the self-assembly process and eventually would form multinuclear complexes (see below). Elemental analysis, IR, and UV/Vis spectra are in excellent agreement with the above formulations of the two new complexes.

## Spectroscopic properties

IR spectra of the complexes show $\nu_{asym}(COO^-)$ stretching fre-
quency in the range 1599-1607 cm⁻¹ and $\nu_{sym}(COO^-)$ in the
range 1434-1448 cm⁻¹. The difference between $\nu_{asym}(COO^-)$ and
$\nu_{sym}(COO^-)$ is about 170 cm⁻¹, suggesting a bridging coordina-
tion mode for the carboxylate group. $^{14e}$ In addition to this,
complexes show $\nu_{asym}(N_3^-)$ stretching frequency in the range
of 2085-2090 cm⁻¹. 2085 cm⁻¹ corresponds to bridging and
2090 cm⁻¹ corresponds to terminal azide. The absorption spectrum
of $1\cdot Et_2O$ in $CH_3CN$ is rather complicated [Fig. S1, ESI†]. It does not
give any band which supports the presence of six-coordinate $Co^{II}$

centers in solution. All three spin-allowed d-d transitions expected for octahedral $Co^{II}$ are either found out of range or masked due to the presence of the phenolate-$Co^{III}$ charge transfer band {610 nm ($\varepsilon = 550\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}$)}. The absorption spectrum of 2 in $CH_3CN$ clearly supports the presence of six-coordinate $Ni^{II}$ centers in solution [Fig. S2, ESI†]. Three spin-allowed d-d transitions are expected for octahedral $Ni^{II}$, unless the field strength of the ligands is such that $^3T_{1g}(F)$ and $^3T_{1g}(P)$ terms have the same energy. $^{24}$ The assignment of $\nu_1$ (9804 $cm^{-1}$) as a $^3A_{2g}(F) \rightarrow ^3T_{2g}(F)$ transition is unequivocal, and the $\nu_2$ (15 873 $cm^{-1}$) is assigned as a $^3A_{2g}(F) \rightarrow ^3T_{1g}(F)$ transition.

Bond valence calculations

Theoretical bond valence calculations$^{25}$ give valences 2.94 and 2.12 in the trans configuration [2.93 and 2.14 in the cis configuration] for 1 which suggested a mixed valence of +2 and +3 [Table S2, ESI†].

Magnetic studies

The magnetic properties of $1{\cdot}Et_2O$ were determined over the temperature range of 1.8-300 K. Plots of magnetic susceptibility $\chi_{\text{m}}$ and $\chi_{\text{m}}T$ product vs. $T$ ($\chi_{\text{m}}$ is the molar magnetic susceptibility per $Co^{III}_2Co^{II}$ unit) are given in Fig. 5. At room temperature, the value of $\chi_{\text{m}}T$ is $3.47\ cm^{3}\ mol^{-1}\ K$, which is higher than the spin-only value of high-spin $d^7$ ion ($1.87\ cm^{3}\ mol^{-1}\ K$ with $g = 2.0$). This is due to the occurrence of an unquenched orbital contribution typical of the $^4T_{1g}$ ground-state in octahedral high-spin $Co^{II}$ complexes. $^{26}$ The two cobalt(III) ions, $d^6$ are diamagnetic. Upon cooling, the $\chi_{\text{m}}T$ decreases continuously and reaches a plateau at ca. 12 K. The decrease of $\chi_{\text{m}}T$ at low temperatures can be basically caused by depopulation of the higher-energy spin-orbit levels of the six-coordinated cobalt(II) centers. A further decrease of the $\chi_{\text{m}}T$ below 10 K is most likely due to weak intermolecular antiferromagnetic interactions. Moreover, there are no maximum of the molar susceptibility at low temperature for $1{\cdot}Et_2O$. Thus, significant antiferromagnetic interactions in the solid state can be excluded. Structurally, there are potentially several different magnetic coupling pathways in 2. For the interpretation of magnetic data in 2, a simple model accounting for nearest-neighbor interactions was used, while terms to account for the single ion axial zero-field splittings of the $\text{Ni}^{II}$ ions and the Zeeman splittings inside the magnetic field were also introduced. Taking into account the symmetry of the molecule, confirmed by X-ray study, it was assumed that $J_{23}=J_{2'3'}=J_{1}$ and $J_{12}=J_{1'2'}=J_{2}$. Additionally, a global single ion zero-field splitting (ZFS) parameter, $D$, was used for all nickel(II) ions to avoid overparameterization. The respective spin Hamiltonian suitable to describe the exchange interaction in 2 has the form (eqn (6)):²⁸

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat{H} &= -2J_{1}\left(\hat{S}_{2}\hat{S}_{3} + \hat{S}_{2'}\hat{S}_{3'}\right) + J_{2}\left(\hat{S}_{1}\hat{S}_{2} + \hat{S}_{1'}\hat{S}_{2'}\right) \\
&+ D\sum_{i=1}^{5} S_{i,z}^{2} + g\mu_{\text{B}}H\sum_{i=1}^{5} \hat{S}_{i}
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
$$

where $J_{1}$ is the exchange coupling constant between {Ni(2), Ni(3)} and {Ni(2'), Ni(3')} and $J_{2}$ is that between {Ni(1), Ni(2)} and {Ni(1'), Ni(2')}. The spin labels $S_{1}$, $S_{2}$, and $S_{3}$ correspond to the nickel(II) atoms Ni(1), Ni(2), and Ni(3) as shown in the crystal structure (Fig. 4). Fitting of the magnetic data of 2 was performed taking into account the two different exchange pathways, i.e., via acetate and phenolate bridges, $J_{1}$ (Ni2$\cdots$Ni3 $3.2174(6)\ \text{\AA}$ distances), and azide, acetate and phenolate bridges, $J_{2}$ (Ni1$\cdots$Ni2 $3.0714(6)\ \text{\AA}$). The intermolecular interactions between $\text{Ni}^{II}$ ions in neighboring molecules are omitted because of the long Ni$\cdots$Ni distances. A least-squares fitting of the experimental data leads to the following values: $J_{1} = -17.2(1)\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, $J_{2}=+18.5(1)\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, $|D|=3.7(2)\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and $g=2.21(2)$ ($R=8.37\times10^{-4}$), as indicated by the solid curve in Fig. 6. The criterion applied to determine the best fit was based on the minimization of the sum of squares of the deviation, $R=\Sigma(\chi_{\text{exp}}T - \chi_{\text{calc}}T)^{2}/\Sigma(\chi_{\text{exp}}T)^{2}$. The experimental data reproduce well in the whole temperature region. The calculations show that antiferromagnetic exchange interactions between nickel(II) ions and the zero-field splitting (ZFS) of $\text{Ni}^{II}$ ions are the main factors determining the decrease of magnetic moment in the low temperature range. The value of $D$ is in agreement with those reported for the octahedral nickel(II) complexes.²⁹

An antiferromagnetic behavior with a sharp decrease at lower temperatures in 2 is in contrast to the usual ferromagnetic spin interaction reported in most of the other polynuclear nickel(II) complexes.²⁸,³⁰ Such ferromagnetic coupling appears to be unexpected in 2 because of the syn-syn carboxylate and phenolate bridges, since the O-M-O angles larger than $97.5^{\circ}$ are known to cause antiferromagnetic coupling.³¹ The Ni-O-Ni angles of phenoxo ligands (above $100^{\circ}$) and longer Ni2$\cdots$Ni3 distances ($3.2174(6)\ \text{\AA}$) in 2 are large enough to promote antiferromagnetic couplings ($J_{1}=-17.2(1)\ \text{cm}^{-1}$). The structure of 2 reveals that central nickel(II) ions are additionally linked by azide bridges. For complexes with symmetric end-on azido bridges, Ruiz et al. found that the ferromagnetic coupling decreases upon increasing the M-N-M ($\theta$) bond angle, eventually reaching an antiferromagnetic behavior for $\theta\geq104^{\circ}$.⁶ᵇ The ferromagnetic interactions suggested by the fits ($J_{2}=+18.5(1)\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) are consistent with the presence of end-on bridging azides between the $\text{Ni}^{II}$ ions (Ni-N-Ni, $94^{\circ}$) and shorter Ni1$\cdots$Ni2 distances ($3.0714(6)\ \text{\AA}$), which are known to promote ferromagnetic couplings. The different kinds of these interactions in 2 can be attributed to the presence of three different types of bridging ligands (end-on azido, syn-syn acetato and phenoxo bridges) which may either add or counterbalance their effects. Additionally, some structural distortions may result in the reduction of orthogonality between the magnetic orbitals, decreasing the ferromagnetic coupling. Moreover, the sign and magnitude of $J$ values obtained from the magnetic calculations for 2 match the theoretical results (DFT).

From the calculations, it could be pointed out that an overall, slight ferromagnetic (F) behavior should be expected for an isolated $Ni_5$ cluster. Nevertheless, if intermolecular interactions are relevant in the crystal, it is possible that a slight antiferromagnetic (AF) behavior be exhibited instead as suggested by the experimental determinations (see above). Interestingly, although the overall magnetic behavior is expected to be small, the calculated values for the Ni1-Ni2 (=Ni4-Ni5) and Ni2-Ni3 (=Ni3-Ni4) interactions are relatively high (although of opposite sign) for a couple of interacting nickel(ii) centers, and similar to other related compounds. Focusing on the ferromagnetic part of this complex, namely the trimer formed by Ni2-Ni3-Ni4 moieties (see the inset in Table 4), we observe the simultaneous presence of three exchange pathways through $\mu_2$-phenoxido, syn-syn acetato, and the end-on azido bridges. Related to this part of our complex, there are similar structures, and among them, for example, a ferromagnetic, centrosymmetric pentanuclear Ni(ii) complex $(S=5)$, with a similar triply bridged scheme ($\mu_2$-acetato/syn-syn acetato/azido), which exhibits a mean value for the coupling constants of the two different 1-2 interactions of about $+15\ cm^{-1}.^{28}$ More similar (phenoxo/acetato/azido) is the bridging scheme of a centrosymmetric trinuclear Ni(ii) complex exhibiting a coupling constant of $+16.5\ cm^{-1},^{37}$ or a centrosymmetric tetranuclear Ni(ii) structure (also with a phenoxo/acetato/azido part) for which a $+28.8\ cm^{-1}$ coupling constant was experimentally determined. $^{38}$ With respect to the AF part of the complex, a phenoxo/$\mu_2:\eta_1$-acetato double bridging scheme is adopted, resulting in an AF coupling because the wide Ni-O-Ni $(\theta)$ angles: $104.5^{\circ}$ for the Ni-O(phenoxo)-Ni angle and 98.5 for the Ni-O(acetato)-Ni angle. ## Conclusions
In summary, new carboxylate, azide, phenolate-bridged trimer and pentamer complexes $[Co^{III}_2Co^{II}(\mu -OL)_2(\mu -OOCCH_3)_2(\mu -N_3)_2(N_3)_2]\cdot Et_2O$ (1·Et₂O) and $[Ni^{II}_5(\mu -OL)_4(\mu -OOCCH_3)_2(OOCCH_3)_2(\mu -N_3)_2]$ (2), with closely similar metal-ligand bonding characteristics assembled due to 'coordination-driven self-assembly' supported by a phenolate-based ligand, have been synthesised. In this work, we have shown that utilizing carboxylate and azide as co-ligands can lead to the synthesis of discrete multinuclear complexes. From the standpoint of magnetostructural investiga- tion by means of both experimental and theoretical methods, the outcome of the present work is noteworthy. Magnetic results for 1 suggest that the effect of zero-field splitting of the $Co^{II}$ ion and very weak antiferromagnetic exchange interactions between the cobalt(ii) ions in the crystal lattice are totally responsible for the decrease of the magnetic moment at low temperatures. CAS- NEVPT2 calculations carried out on both crystallographically different trimer units point to mean values of $-83.9\ cm^{-1}$ for the ZFS parameter $D$, and a mean value of 0.244 for the $E/D$ ratio revealing the presence of an easy axis of magnetization. The experimental (2.37) and theoretical (2.38) $g$ values are in perfect agreement, and experimental and theoretical magnetization curves against the applied magnetic field at 2 K are quite similar. On the other hand, the magnetic studies of 2 reveal both antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic coupling within the pen- tanuclear nickel(ii) unit. Magnetostructural correlations clearly support the obtained results and the spin density calcula- tion points to a direct exchange mechanism for the magnetic interaction. The generality of the formation of novel cluster architectures by utilizing a simple ligand is the subject of ongoing investigations.
