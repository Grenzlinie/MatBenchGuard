# CIRCULAR DICHROISM OF $Mn^{2+}$ IONS in $\alpha-Zn(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$ CRYSTAL

Toshiko KATÔ and Ikuji TSUJIKAWA

Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science, Kyoto University,
Sakyoku, Kyoto, Japan

Received 27 November 1973
Revised manuscript received 27 December 1973

The circular dichroism (CD) of $Mn^{2+}$ ions in $\alpha-Zn(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$ crystal has been measured at $77-300^{\circ} K$. CD lines associated with the spin forbidden transition $^{6} ~A_{1} \to\{^{4} ~A_{1},^{4} E\}$ are observed, and compared with uniaxial rotational strengths calculated on the basis of ligand field theory. The CD bands of $Mn^{2+}$ and $Ni^{2+}$ simultaneously doped into a $Zn(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$ crystal have the same sign, which accords with a theoretical prediction.

The CD of all the spin allowed $d-d$ transitions of enantiomorphic iron group selenate crystals, i.e., $\alpha-Ni(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$ crystal and $\alpha-Zn(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$ crystals doped with $Cu^{2+}, Ni^{2+}$ and $Co^{2+}$ ions, has been measured and analyzed on the basis of ligand field theory [1]. It was found that the CD spectra of these crystals can be understood in terms of the asymmetric field at the metal ions rather than from the excitation dispersion of $d-d$ transitions of helically arranged metal ions, a conclusion which has subsequently been derived for the CD of $\alpha-Ni(H_{2} O)_{6} SO_{4}$ by Strickland and Richardson [2]. This note concerns preliminary results of our studies on the CD lines ascribed to the spin forbidden transitions of $Mn^{2+}$.

Several $Zn(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$ crystals doped with 5-15 atomic \% $Mn^{2+}$ ions were examined by light propagated parallel to the tetragonal optic axis $(Z)$. The CD in the 200-700 nm region was measured at temperatures from $77^{\circ} K$ to $300^{\circ} K$ using a JASCO automatic spectro-polarimeter J-20. The minimum slit width employed corresponded to about $6 ~cm^{-1}$.

The CD spectrum shows a narrow band with structure at about $400 ~nm$ (fig. 1). We were not able to detect any CD bands in other regions, because of the limit of sensitivity of the instrument or of the difficulty of preparing sufficiently thick single crystals.

From X-ray [3] and neutron diffraction [4] studies of $\alpha-Ni(H_{2} O)_{6} SO_{4}$, which is isomorphous with $\alpha-Zn(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$, we can assume that nearly octahedral $Mn(H_{2} O)_{6}^{2+}$ clusters in $Zn(H_{2} O)_{6} SeO_{4}$ are affected by a perturbing field with $C_{2}$ symmetry. From the general assignments of the optical absorption spectra of octahedral manganous complexes

[5-7], the CD band in the 400 nm region observed in $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}: \mathrm{Zn}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)_{6} \mathrm{SeO}_{4}$ is ascribed to the spin forbidden $^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow\left\{{ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1},{ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}\right\}$ transition.

The rotational strength for the transition $a \rightarrow b$ in uniaxial systems has been derived by Buckingham and Dunn [8] as

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{a \rightarrow b}= & \operatorname{Im}\left\{\left(P_{X}\right)_{a b}\left(M_{X}-\frac{1}{2} c^{-1} \dot{Q}_{Y Z}\right)_{b a}\right. \\
& \left.+\left(P_{Y}\right)_{a b}\left(M_{Y}+\frac{1}{2} c^{-1} \dot{Q}_{Z X}\right)_{b a}\right\},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $X$ and $Y$ are crystalline axes perpendicular to $Z$. This has been successfully applied to spin allowed $\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{d}$ transitions of the iron group sulphate and sele- nate crystals [1,2] and of $\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{en})_{3}^{3+}$ [9].

In the case of spin forbidden transitions, one must consider the spin-orbit interaction in addition to the asymmetric field. The hamiltonian of our system is then given by

$$
H=H_{0}+V_{u}+V_{\text {so }},
$$

$$
V_{u}=\sum_{i} B_{1} r_{i}^{3} t_{2 \zeta}^{(3)}\left(\theta_{i}, \varphi_{i}\right),
$$

where the coordinate axes are those given in ref. [1]. In our calculation the angle between the ion fixed $z$-axis and the crystalline $Z$-axis is assumed to be $45^{\circ}$ and the local symmetry about the $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ ion can be approximated as $\mathrm{D}_{2}[1,2]$. The excited state ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}$ is considered split into orbital singlets, ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{u}$ and ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{v}$, by the second order perturbation of the axial field along the $z$-axis. A further splitting of the states, ${ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1}$, ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{u}$ and ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{v}$, into Kramers doublets is hidden in rela tively diffuse linewidths $\left(\sim 100 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\right)$.

The electric dipole matrix elements, e.g., for the ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1}$ transition, are calculated according to the formula:

$$
\sum_{j=a, b, c} \frac{\left\langle{ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} M_{s}\left|V_{\mathrm{so}}\right|{ }_{j}^{4} \mathrm{~T}_{1} M_{s}^{\prime} M^{\prime}\right\rangle\left\langle{ }_{j}^{4} \mathrm{~T}_{1} M_{s}^{\prime} M^{\prime}\left|\tilde{P}_{\lambda}\right|{ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1} M_{s}\right\rangle}{E\left({ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1}\right)-E\left({ }_{j}^{4} \mathrm{~T}_{1}\right)},(4)
$$

where $\lambda$ denotes a component of the effective electric dipole moment $\tilde{P}=-(2 / \Delta W) P V_{u}[1]$. Both magnetic dipole and electric quadrupole transition moments are allowed by the first order perturbation of the spin- orbit interaction, and can be calculated using a formu- la similar to (4). The contribution of the electric quadrupole moment and higher order effects due to the asymmetric field of even parity can be neglected as being small [1]. Wavefunctions used in the evalu- ation of the matrix elements are those given by Koide and Pryce [5], where the parameters $D q=800 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, $B=800 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ and $C=3200 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, are adopted.

Assuming the spin-orbit coupling parameter to be $\zeta=\zeta^{\prime}=270 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ [7], which is somewhat less than the $335 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ of the free $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ ion, we can give uniaxial rotational strengths for the transitions ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow$ ${ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1},{ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{u}$ and ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{v}$ (table 1). In table $1, P_{1} \beta$ is a constant proportional to the odd field parameter $B_{1}$ defined as

$$
P_{1} \beta=-(2 \Delta \mathrm{W})^{-1}(5 / 21 \pi)^{1 / 2} e B_{1}\left\langle r^{4}\right\rangle \beta,
$$

where $\beta$ is the Bohr magneton, and the orbital reduction factor has been assumed to be unity.

From table 1, when compared with tables I, III, and VI of ref. [1], we can predict that the transition ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow\left\{{ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1},{ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}\right\}$ of the $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ ion has the same CD sign as the spin allowed transitions of $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}, \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ and $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$ ions, if these ions are doped into the same host lattice. The study of a $\mathrm{Zn}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)_{6} \mathrm{SeO}_{4}$ crystal doped with both $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ and $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ ions showed that the two ions have the same CD sign, which is in agreement with the theoretical prediction given above.

Let us assume that the odd field acting on an $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ ion in $\mathrm{Zn}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)_{6} \mathrm{SeO}_{4}$ is close to those on the other transition metal ions and that

$$P_{1} \beta\left(\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}\right) \approx 2.0 \times 10^{-40} \text { c.g.s. } \quad(6)$$

The uniaxial rotational strengths calculated by using the assumption (6) are also listed in table 1. It can be predicted that the transitions to the ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1}$ and ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{v}$ states show strong CD of the same sign, while the transition to the ${ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{u}$ state shows small CD of the opposite sign.

Let us now compare the calculated uniaxial rotational strengths with the observed CD spectrum (fig. 1). At $77^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$, the CD band with its maximum at about $24893 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ observed at $300^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$ is resolved into two lines, I (at $24810 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ ) and II (at $24949 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ ), and a weak line, III (at $25214 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ ), appears. The rotational strengths estimated from the CD band areas are given in table 2. It is reasonable to suppose that the CD lines of the same sign, I and II, come from the pure electronic transitions ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1}$ and ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow{ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{v}$. The CD of the opposite sign corresponding to the ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow{ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{u}$ transition is hidden by the large CD of the ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow{ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}_{v}$ transition.

The weak CD line, III, lies about $265 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ beyond line II. This line cannot be assigned to one of the components of the pure electronic ${ }^{6} \mathrm{~A}_{1} \rightarrow\left\{{ }^{4} \mathrm{~A}_{1},{ }_{a}^{4} \mathrm{E}\right\}$ transition, but to one of its vibronic components. The vibronic structure associated with both even and odd vibrations has been observed by Gailey and Palmer [10] in the CD of $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$ ions in $\mathrm{Zn}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)_{6} \mathrm{SeO}_{4}$. By comparison of the spacings between the CD lines and the energies of the $\mathrm{M}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right)_{6}^{2+}$ vibrations observed in the vibrational spectra [11], line III can be assigned either to a progression in the $\mathrm{a}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}$ vibration based on line I as origin or to a progression in the $\mathrm{t}_{1 \mathrm{u}}$ vibration based on line II. In the former case the vibronic line is allowed by the nonorthogonality of the vibrational wavefunctions, while in the latter case vibronic rotational strength comes from the product of borrowed electric moment and zero order magnetic moment (Moffitt and Moscowitz' Case II $[12,13]$ ). Because line I is much broader than lines II and III, which have nearly equal linewidths (table 1), we assign line III as a progression in the $t_{1 u}$ vibration based on line II.

The total uniaxial rotational strength of the two zero-phonon lines was obtained as $4.02 \times 10^{-44}$ c.g.s. at $77^{\circ} K$ , which is close to the theoretically estimated value, $3.98 \times 10^{-44}$ c.g.s., using the assumption (6). Comparison between the relative intensity of the observed CD lines, $R(I) / R(II)=8 / 10$ , and the theoretically estimated one, $R(^{6} A_{1} \to ^{4} A_{1}) / R(^{6} A_{1} \to _{a}^{4} E)=$ 7.1/10, supports the assignment of line I to the $^{6} A_{1} \to ^{4} A_{1}$ transition and line II to the $^{6} A_{1} \to _{a}^{4} E$

transition. It is, however, to be noted that the reverse assignment (the sharper line II to $^6A_1 \rightarrow ^4A_1$ and the broader line I to $^6A_1 \rightarrow ^4E$) is favoured by an explana- tion involving their linewidths, because the $^4A_1$ state is less influenced by the crystalline field than $^4E$. We cannot at the present stage decide the better of these two assignments.

The CD curve of the zero-phonon lines I and II at $77^\circ$K is compared with gaussian and lorentzian shapes in fig. 2, and the observed CD agrees with a lorentzian shape within experimental error. This sug- gests that the CD linewidths of I and II at $77^\circ$K may be accounted for by the lifetime broadening mechan- ism due to phonon relaxation processes, while random microscopic crystal strains of the host crystal give a minor contribution [14, 15]. The CD study at tem- peratures from $300^\circ$K to $77^\circ$K shows that the uni- axial rotational strength is almost independent of temperature. The peak position shifts to the higher energy side and the CD linewidth decreases on low- ering the temperature.

High resolution measurements of the CD of $Mn^{2+}:Zn(H_2O)_6SeO_4$ at lower temperatures is in progress in our laboratories and will be reported later with more detailed analyses of the CD and of its temperature dependence.
