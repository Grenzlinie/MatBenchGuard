# Preparation and thermoelectric properties of $CeFe_4As_{12}$

A. Watcharapasorn$^{\text{a)}}$ and R. S. Feigelson
Geballe Laboratory for Advanced Materials, McCullough Building Room 237, Stanford University,
Stanford, California 94305

T. Caillat, A. Borshchevsky, G. J. Snyder, and J.-P. Fleurial
Jet Propulsion Laboratory, California Institute of Technology, MS 277/207, 4800 Oak Grove Drive,
Pasadena, California 91109

(Received 24 September 2001; accepted for publication 18 October 2001)

The thermoelectric properties of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ have been measured. The compound was synthesized by reacting a powder mixture of $CeAs_2$ and $FeAs_2$ with As metal. The sample was characterized by both x-ray diffractometry and electron microprobe analysis. The Seebeck coefficient, electrical resistivity, Hall effect, and the thermal conductivity were measured between room temperature and about 900 K. The results indicate that $CeFe_4As_{12}$ possesses semimetallic behavior similar to that of $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ with a room temperature electrical resistivity of $0.49\ \mathrm{m\Omega\ cm}$ and a Seebeck coefficient of about $40\ \mathrm{\mu V/K}$. The thermal conductivity of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ lies between that of $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ as may be expected from considerations of anionic mass and rattling effect of Ce atoms inside the skutterudite unit cell. A maximum thermoelectric figure of merit value of about 0.4 was obtained at 850 K for this compound. © 2002 American Institute of Physics.

## I. INTRODUCTION

To be useful for thermoelectric applications, a material must possess a large thermoelectric figure of merit ($ZT$). This figure of merit is defined by the relationship $ZT=\alpha^2T/\rho\kappa$, where $\alpha$ is the Seebeck coefficient, $\rho$ is the electrical resistivity, $\kappa$ is the thermal conductivity, and $T$ is the absolute temperature. Bismuth telluride alloys are currently the most commercially used thermoelectric materials with a maximum $ZT$ value of about 1.0 at room temperature. During the past several years, a vigorous research effort has been underway to find materials with higher $ZT$ values. A large number of compounds have been investigated both from a theoretical and experimental point of view. Among these is $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$, one of the skutterudite-structure compounds. It was found to have a maximum $ZT$ value of 1.4 at elevated temperatures. $^{1,2}$ As a result, a considerable amount of research has been de- voted to the antimonide skutterudites. Other skutterudite compounds, including phosphides, arsenides, and their solid solutions, have been much less explored and their transport properties have not been studied in detail. One of the reasons for this is the difficulty in preparing these materials in high- purity single phase form. In addition, high quality single crystals have been difficult to prepare and therefore transport measurements are usually made on dense polycrystalline samples.

A study of the phosphide and arsenide skutterudites was undertaken to further elucidate the mechanism responsible for the high $ZT$ value of $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$. Similar studies on III-V compounds such as InP, InAs, and InSb have yielded impor- tant information on the influence of anionic substitutions on electronic properties. A better understanding of the electronic structures and transport properties should provide better guidelines for improving the thermoelectric properties of these materials.

In an earlier article, we reported on the transport prop- erties of $CoP_3$ and $CeFe_4P_{12}$. $^3$ Both compounds exhibit semi- conducting behavior, in agreement with other experimental results $^{4-7}$ and theoretical calculations. $^{8-10}$ In comparing the properties of these compounds to their arsenide and anti- monide analogs, no precise trend can be established from the data of only a few available samples. For example, the very slight difference in carrier concentration (if any) can often be linked to the preparation method and stoichiometry of a com- pound. Hence, it is sometimes difficult to determine the in- trinsic value corresponding to its stoichiometric composition. Nevertheless, from the available data of $CoX_3$ (X=P, As, and Sb) compounds, $^{23,24}$ it was found that the carrier concen tration is highest in the phosphide and lowest in the anti- monide. A similar trend in carrier concentration was also observed in the far-infrared reflection study of these compounds. $^{11-14}$ In addition, the calculated energy gaps from the optical data were found to be 0.045 and 0.042 eV for $CoP_3$ (Refs. 12 and 13) and 0.21 eV for $CoAs_3$ (Refs. 11 and 14), which are similar to our values of 0.043 eV and 0.26 eV, respectively, obtained from high-temperature data of electri- cal resistivity. $^{23}$ It is to be noted that this is in contrast to the III-V compounds, where the carrier concentrations are found to be lowest for phosphides and highest in antimonides. $^{15,16}$

For the ternary compounds, it was found that while $CeFe_4P_{12}$ exhibits semiconducting behavior, $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ is a semimetal. Hence, it was expected that $CeFe_4As_{12}$ may be a semiconductor, similar to $CoAs_3$. An electrical resistivity measurement on a cold-pressed pellet of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ showed semiconducting behavior $^7$ that seems to agree with the ex- pectation. ## II. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

$CeFe_4As_{12}$ powder was synthesized by reacting a mixture of $CeAs_2$ and $FeAs_2$ in the presence of an additional amount of pure As (99.9998%). The binary compounds were prepared by reacting cerium powder (99.9% pure) or iron powder (99.9% pure) with arsenic powder (99.999+% pure) in a sealed fused quartz tube (30 mm in diameter and 10 cm long) evacuated to $10^{-5}$ Torr. The powders were then mixed and cold pressed into a dense pellet before being placed into another fused quartz ampoule (16 mm in diameter and 19 cm long) with approximately 0.012 mol of excess arsenic. $^{17}$ The tube was then heated to 1173 K, soaked for one week and then quenched in water. X-ray diffraction analysis showed that the reaction products contained not only the skutterudite phase but some unreacted materials ($CeAs_2$ and $FeAs_2$). It was, therefore, necessary to subject this material to an additional reaction treatment, in which the same amount of excess arsenic was used. The result was single phase $CeFe_4As_{12}$.

$CeFe_4As_{12}$ was hot pressed in a graphite die into a dense sample, about 2.5 mm long and 12.0 mm in diameter. The hot pressing was conducted at a pressure of about 18 000 psi and at temperature of 1073 K for 2 h under an argon atmosphere. Microprobe analysis was performed on the sample to determine its atomic composition. A JEOL JXA-733 electron superprobe operating with an accelerating potential of 20 $\times 10^3$ V and a probe current of $25\times 10^{-9}$ A was used. Pure elements and compounds were used as standards and x-ray intensity measurements of peak and background were conducted by wavelength dispersive spectrometry. The sample density was calculated from the measured weight and dimensions of the sample.

The electrical resistivity, Hall effect, Seebeck coefficient, thermal diffusivity, and heat capacity measurements were measured between room temperature and about 900 K. The electrical resistivity ($\rho$) was measured using the van der Pauw technique with a current of 100 mA in a special high-temperature apparatus. $^{18}$ The Hall coefficient ($R_H$) was measured in the same apparatus with a constant magnetic field value of $\sim$10 400 Gauss. The carrier density was calculated from the Hall coefficient by using the relationship $p=1/R_H e$, where $p$ is the density of holes, and $e$ is the electron charge, and assuming a scattering factor of 1.0 and a single carrier scheme. The Hall mobility ($\mu_H$) was calculated from the Hall coefficient and the resistivity using the formula $\mu_H=R_H/\rho$. Errors were estimated to be $\pm 0.5\%$ and $\pm 2\%$ for the resistivity and Hall coefficient data, respectively. The Seebeck coefficient ($\alpha$) was measured on the same sample used for the electrical resistivity and Hall coefficient measurements. A high-temperature light pulse technique was used. $^{19}$ The error in the Seebeck coefficient measurement was estimated to be less than $\pm 3\%$. The heat capacity and thermal diffusivity were measured using a flash diffusivity technique. $^{20}$ The thermal conductivity ($\kappa$) was calculated from the experimental density, heat capacity, and thermal diffusivity values. The overall error in the thermal conductivity measurements was estimated to be about $\pm 10\%$.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The room temperature properties of $CeFe_4As_{12}$, $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ are given in Table I. The measured lattice constant of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ is 8.2996 Å, which is slightly larger than the value of 8.2959 Å reported in literature $^{17}$ but is still in good agreement. Microprobe analyses showed that the sample was single phase with a composition $Ce_{6.1}Fe_{23.2}As_{70.8}$, which can be reduced to $CeFe_{3.8}As_{11.6}$, assuming the compound is fully filled with Ce atoms. From the formula, the deviation from $CeFe_4As_{12}$ is almost exactly by 0.2 mol of $FeAs_2$. Since the x-ray diffraction data and electron microprobe study did not indicate the presence of $FeAs_2$ or $CeAs_2$, the reaction was expected to go to completion. In addition, even after the accuracy of electron microprobe, typically 1 at. %, was taken into account, it seems that this compound is still nonstoichiometric and there should be iron and arsenic vacancies present in the lattice. The sample density was about 99% of theoretical density.

The Seebeck coefficient and carrier concentration were used to estimate the effective mass of holes in this compound, using a single parabolic band and acoustic phonon scattering approximation. The calculated value was quite large i.e. $m^*=1.336m_0$ where $m_0$=free electron mass. This value may be compared with $0.166m_0$ and $8.992m_0$ for $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$, respectively. Normally, the effective masses of different materials should be compared at the same carrier concentration and temperature since their dependence on these parameters may be appreciable. For example, it was found that the effective mass of both $n$- and $p$-typed $CoSb_3$ increased with carrier concentration at room temperature. $^{24}$ For the case of binary and ternary phosphide and arsenide skutterudites, the trend has not been studied experimentally but linear extrapolation to the same carrier concentration as that of $CeFe_4P_{12}$ gave the effective mass of $0.107m_0$ and $0.169m_0$ for $CeFe_4As_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$, respectively. This shows that at the same carrier concentration, the hole masses are similar for all three compounds.

The variation of electrical resistivity and Seebeck coefficient with temperature of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ is plotted along with the phosphide and antimonide data in Figs. 1 and 2, respectively. The electrical resistivity increases slightly from 0.49 m$\Omega$ cm at room temperature to a nearly constant value of about 0.9 m$\Omega$ cm above 500 K, indicating semimetallic conductivity. This observation, however, differs from a previous investigation that reported semiconducting behavior for this compound. $^{7}$ Two major differences can be pointed out regarding these discrepancies. First, these authors used a coldpressed pellet for their studies and, second, the temperature of measurement was made between 4.2 K and room temperature. This suggests that this material may be a narrow band gap semiconductor similar to $CoP_3$. $^{3}$ Nonstoichiometry can also play a role in determining the electrical properties of this compound. It is to be noted that the magnitude of resistivity of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ was nearly the same as that of $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$.

While the Seebeck coefficient of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ was lower than both $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ near room temperature, it becomes comparable to the phosphide above 500 K. It should be noted that the change in slope near 500 K corresponds to the region where the electrical resistivity started to become independent of temperature.

Table II lists the values of room temperature lattice thermal conductivity for the three Ce-based compounds along with their cage radii, rattling amplitude, and thermal parameters. Similar data for $LaFe_4P_{12}$, $LaFe_4As_{12}$, and $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$ are also given for comparison. The rattling amplitude and thermal parameters between the first two compounds are not much different. $^{17,21,22}$ However, there is a larger change in these properties when $LaFe_4As_{12}$ and $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$ are compared. This behavior can be related to the change in lattice constants of these compounds. From Table II, the change in lattice constant is calculated to be about 6% when going from $LaFe_4P_{12}$ to $LaFe_4As_{12}$, and the change is about 10% when going from $LaFe_4As_{12}$ to $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$. Similar changes are also found in the $CeFe_4X_{12}$ (X=P, As, and Sb) series. This behavior is apparently caused by the change in anionic size. Assuming that the radii of rare-earth atoms are not affected by this change, this also implies that the maximum rattling amplitude of the filling atom, hence the largest reduction in thermal conductivity, should be observed in $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ as confirmed by the results from thermal conductivity measurement.

Figure 3 shows the thermal conductivity as a function of temperature of $CeFe_4As_{12}$ as well as that of $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$. There is a slight temperature dependence of thermal conductivity for the arsenide and the values lie between those of $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ up to about 650 K. Above this temperature, its thermal conductivity lies below that of $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$. This is due to less contribution of electronic thermal conductivity in the arsenide compared to that in the antimonide. Room temperature lattice thermal conductivity values were plotted in Fig. 4 together with values reported in literature for other compounds.²,³,²³,²⁴ The upper curve shows the decrease in lattice thermal conductivity of the binary skutterudite compounds, CoX₃ (X=P, As, and Sb), with increasing anionic mass. This is mainly due to the more efficient scattering between the heat-carrying phonons and the vibrational modes of the heavier atoms in the lattice. When the voids in the skutterudite unit cell are filled with Ce atoms, a large reduction in thermal conductivity is observed. For example, the amount of reduction from CoP₃ to CeFe₄P₁₂ is approximately 47%. The largest amount of reduction is observed in the case of CoSb₃ and CeFe₄Sb₁₂, indicating that the rattling of Ce atoms produces the greatest effect in the skutterudite compound with the largest cage size.

From the high-temperature CeFe₄As₁₂ data, ZT values as a function of temperature were determined. A maximum ZT value of about 0.4 was obtained near 850 K. The ZT values of the skutterudite phosphides, arsenides, and antimonides were normally observed to increase with temperature. At 800 K, the values for CeFe₄P₁₂, CeFe₄As₁₂, and CeFe₄Sb₁₂ were found to be 0.10, 0.36 and 1.09, respectively. By referring to Figs. 1 to 3, the difference in ZT values of CeFe₄P₁₂ and CeFe₄As₁₂ was mainly due to the difference in their electrical resistivity and thermal conductivity. In the case of CeFe₄As₁₂ and CeFe₄Sb₁₂, the difference was due to the Seebeck coefficient and thermal conductivity.

From this study, an increase in the Seebeck coefficient with increasing temperature of CeFe₄X₁₂ (X=P, As, and Sb) favors the use of these materials at high temperatures, especially in the case of CeFe₄As₁₂ and CeFe₄Sb₁₂ whose electrical resistivities are also very low. It is possible that the improvement in their thermoelectric properties may be accomplished through a reduction of the lattice thermal conductivity by making solid solutions of these compounds. A study of the ternary solid solution, CoP₃₋ₓAsₓ, yielded an increase in the Seebeck coefficient and a decrease in thermal conductivity which led to an improved thermoelectric figure of merit.²³ Hence, quaternary solid solutions of the type CeFe₄As₁₂₋ₓPₓ and CeFe₄Sb₁₂₋ₓAsₓ may similarly yield improved properties.

## IV. CONCLUSIONS

The thermoelectric properties of CeFe₄As₁₂ have been measured over a wide range of temperatures. The compound exhibits p-type semimetallic conduction. The electrical resistivity of this compound is very low and its values are comparable to those of CeFe₄Sb₁₂. The Seebeck coefficient shows an increase in magnitude with increasing temperature, similar to that of CeFe₄P₁₂ and CeFe₄Sb₁₂, and its values are comparable to that of CeFe₄P₁₂ above 500 K. The thermal conductivity values lie between that of CeFe₄P₁₂ and CeFe₄Sb₁₂ from room temperature up to about 650 K. The effects of rattling atoms on lattice thermal conductivity are evidenced by the trends established in the variations of the room temperature lattice thermal conductivity as a function of composition. It can also be deduced from these data that making solid solutions of the ternary compounds may prove to be beneficial in improving the thermoelectric properties by further reducing the lattice thermal conductivity, assuming that the electrical properties are not significantly affected.
