# Thermoelectric properties of molten $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$, CuI, and AgI

Kazutaka Nishikawa, $^{\text{a)}}$ Yasuhiko Takeda, and Tomoyoshi Motohiro
Toyota Central Research and Development Laboratories, Inc., Nagakute, Aichi 480-1192, Japan

(Received 30 July 2012; accepted 4 January 2013; published online 23 January 2013)

To achieve large figure of merit ($ZT$) and superior thermal durability at high temperatures, we have investigated thermoelectric properties of molten $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$, CuI, and AgI up to 1173 K. Molten $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$ was found to have large electrical conductivity between 1800 and 2000 $(\Omega \cdot \text{cm})^{-1}$. Molten CuI and AgI, however, exhibited small electrical conductivity less than 1 $(\Omega \cdot \text{cm})^{-1}$, nevertheless they show very large Seebeck coefficients over $800\,\mu\text{V/K}$. We estimated thermal conductivity using Wiedemann Franz law and the model established by Regel *et al.* [Phys. Status Solidi A $\boldsymbol{5}$, 13 (1971)]. The evaluated $ZT$ for CuI is over 0.1. © 2013 American Institute of Physics.
[http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4788729]

In recent years, thermoelectric materials are increas- ingly expected for energy harvesting and exhaust heat re- covery to solve the global warming issue and the increasing concern over the exhaustion of fossil fuels. Especially, there is a real need for thermoelectric materials with large figure of merit $ZT$ as well as sufficient thermal durability. In these two decades, many attempts for large $ZT$ have been carried out using various materials, including Skutterudite structure, $^{1-4}$ half or full Heusler alloys, $^{5-7}$ polymer nanocomposite, $^{8,9}$ Co-oxides, $^{10-12}$ and supper lattice structures. $^{13-15}$ Many of them aimed to decrease the phonon thermal conductivity with maintaining high electrical conductivity to realize so called phonon glass and electron crystal (PGEC). As a result, the value of $ZT$ has been improved up to around $1.8.^{16}$ However, there remains a problem in using solid state thermoelectric materials under high temperature environment that those are likely to deteriorate due to the lattice defects. In contrast, molten materials, in other words liquid, do not suffer from lattice defects. Thus, we focus on molten materials to achieve good thermal durability at high temperatures. The report about the measured figure of merit of molten materials at high temperature is very limited. The evaluated $ZT$ values of molten $\text{Tl}_x\text{Te}_{1-x}$ alloys $^{17}$ range up to 0.8; however, such thallium compounds are environmentally unfriendly.

In this study, we investigated the thermal properties of molten $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$, CuI, and AgI in the temperature range of 880–1173 K. $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$, which $ZT$ is about 1 at room temperature, is one of the most popular thermoelectric materials, and the melting point is 858 K. Solid state CuI and AgI have attracted attention as super ionic conductive materials, and they have rather low melting temperature, 878 K for CuI and 825 K for AgI, and high boiling temperature, 1563 K for CuI and 1779 K for AgI. Therefore, operating temperature range in a molten state is very wide.

We used powder $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$, CuI, and AgI with over 99.9% purity purchased from Kojundo Chemical Laboratory. Our experimental set up is represented in Fig. 1. The powder sample was stuffed into a quartz tube with a bore diameter of 8 mm. The quartz tube has four Ta electrodes inserted from the side wall, each connected to a Ta wire, at intervals of 2 cm for measuring thermopower and electrical conductivity using a four probe method. Also, four K thermocouples were similarly inserted into the quartz tube through the same holes for the Ta electrodes to measure the temperature of the molten sample accurately. The four Ta wires and K thermocouples are brought out through the hermetic seal terminal connected with measurement instruments. For measuring the thermal properties of three molten samples, first, we put the quartz tube with the powder sample in an oven and hold it in vacuum $\sim 10^{-2}$ Pa. Next, we raised the temperature up to 1223 K over two hours in an $\text{N}_2$ gas flow, held the temperature for 30 min and then lowered the temperature down to be lower than the melting point over three hours. We measured resistivity, thermopower, and temperature of the Ta electrodes while the temperature fall. For measuring thermopower, we set the temperature of the upper part of the quartz tube higher than that of the lower part to prevent convection flow

We evaluated the form factor of the quartz tube by measuring the resistance of liquid mercury filled in the tube, being 0.44 cm. Figure 2 shows the result of current $I$--voltage $V$ measure- ment for $Bi_2Te_3$ by a four probe method at 1173 K. We con- firmed good linearity of the $I$-$V$ relationship that is needed for estimating the resistance $R$. Thermoelectric power due to a small temperature difference of around 5 K between the Ta electrodes causes a shift of the $I$-$V$ relationship from the point of origin as shown in Fig. 2. We evaluated electrical conductivity $\sigma$ of the samples as a function of the tempera- ture, using the form factor of the quartz tube by the formula $1/\sigma=0.44R$. As shown in Fig. 3, the molten $Bi_2Te_3$ indicates very large electrical conductivity $1700 \sim 2000$ $(\Omega\cdot cm)^{-1}$, in spite of $\sigma \sim 1000$ $(\Omega\cdot cm)^{-1}$ in a solid state at room temperature, and the $\sigma$ almost does not change with the temperature. Similarly, the $\sigma$ of molten CuI is almost constant through the temperature change, being $0.97 \sim 1.1$ $(\Omega\cdot cm)^{-1}$, however that of AgI is largely dependent on temperature, $4.9 \times 10^{-3} \sim 1.1 \times 10^{-1}$ $(\Omega\cdot cm)^{-1}$ and the value is much smaller than those of molten $Bi_2Te_3$ and CuI. Thus, only for molten AgI, the temperature dependence of $\sigma$ is similar to that of solid-state intrinsic semiconductors. The activation energies $E_A$ determined from $\sigma=\sigma_0 \exp(-E_A/k_BT)$ are 5.6 meV, 43 meV, and 1.2 eV for $Bi_2Te_3$, CuI, and AgI, respectively. For all the samples, the sign of $E_A$ is positive.

For evaluating the Seebeck coefficients $\alpha$ dependent on temperature in a molten state, we measured thermopower between the Ta electrodes. Unlike with electrical conductiv- ity measurements, we put the quartz tube stuffed with the sample in the oven with a temperature gradient to be over 10 K between the neighboring Ta electrodes. As shown in Fig. 4, we confirmed good linearity between the thermo- power and the temperature difference. We evaluated $\alpha$ of all the samples using the value of $\alpha$ of the Ta electrodes. We show the evaluated $\alpha$ for all the three samples dependent on the temperatures in Fig. 5. For the molten $Bi_2Te_3$, the value of $\alpha$ is very low, $2-20$ $\mu$V/K over the measured temperature range of $913 \sim 1123$K, which is remarkably smaller than a large $\alpha$ of a solid state at the room temperature being around $200 \mu$V/K, although the sign of $\alpha$ is the same as that of a solid-state showing p-type conductivity. The molten CuI exhibits a very large positive $\alpha$, $620-890$ $\mu$V/K in the meas- ured range, which is 3-4 times larger than that of solid state $Bi_2Te_3$. The molten AgI indicates a very large positive $\alpha$, $250-840$ $\mu$V/K, similarly to the molten CuI.

As well as solid state metals and semiconductors, $\kappa_{e}$ is calculated using Wiedemann Franz law $\kappa_{e} = L\sigma T$, where $L = 2.45 \times 10^{-8}\text{W} \cdot \Omega/\text{K}^{2}$. We evaluated $\kappa_{e}$ using the measured $\sigma$, $3.9 \sim 5.0$, $2.2 \times 10^{-3} \sim 3.1 \times 10^{-3}$, $2.0 \times 10^{-5} \sim 2.7 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{W/m·K}$ for $\text{Bi}_{2}\text{Te}_{3}$, CuI, and AgI, respectively. According to Regel model, $\kappa_{a}$ at the melting point is determined by the average atomic weight $\bar{A}$ of molten materials, $\kappa_{a} = 64\bar{A}^{-1.04}\text{W/m} \cdot \text{K}$, showing that $\kappa_{a}$ is a decreasing function of the average atomic weight. This model is based on the Lao's equation, $\kappa_{a}(T_{m}) \propto (\frac{d_{m}^{4}T_{m}^{3}}{M})^{\frac{1}{6}}$, where $T_{m}$ is the melting point, $d_{m}$ is the melt density, and $M$ is the molecular weight. $^{18}$ Generally, $\kappa_{a}$ is dependent on the temperature as the melt density changes with temperature. However, the amount of change in the melt density is negligibly small, less than $0.02\%/\text{K}$, so $\kappa_{a}$ is approximately constant in the present experimental range of $800 \sim 1200\text{K}$. We evaluated $\kappa_{a}$, being $0.31$, $0.57$, $0.46\text{W/m·K}$ for $\text{Bi}_{2}\text{Te}_{3}$, CuI, and AgI, respectively. The resultant thermal conductivity $\kappa$ is $4.2$-$5.3$, $0.57$, $0.46\text{W/m·K}$ for $\text{Bi}_{2}\text{Te}_{3}$, CuI, and AgI, respectively. For only $\text{Bi}_{2}\text{Te}_{3}$, electronic component determines $\kappa$ due to high electrical conductivity, whereas $\kappa$ of CuI and AgI is dominated by the atomic motion component, leading to much smaller $\kappa$ than that of $\text{Bi}_{2}\text{Te}_{3}$.

Last, we evaluated the figure of merit $ZT$ using the measured $\sigma$, $\alpha$, and calculated $\kappa$ for all the three samples as shown in Fig. 6. For the molten $\text{Bi}_{2}\text{Te}_{3}$, in spite of the large $\sigma$, $ZT$ is smaller than $0.01$, due to the small $\alpha$, less than $20\ \mu\text{V/K}$. Similarly, $ZT$ of the molten AgI is small, less than $0.01$, although the detrimental factor is the small $\sigma$. In contrast, CuI exhibited large $ZT$ being $0.07$-$0.16$, coming from the large $\alpha$ and small $\kappa$.

In conclusion, we have investigated the thermal properties of molten $\text{Bi}_{2}\text{Te}_{3}$, CuI, and AgI. Among them, molten CuI is found to exhibit large $ZT$ over $0.1$ due to the large Seebeck coefficient $\alpha$ $620$-$890\ \mu\text{V/K}$ and small thermal conductivity $\kappa$ $0.57\text{W/m} \cdot \text{K}$ under the high temperature $900$-$1150\text{K}$. The result presented here can open the door toward a new direction for promising thermoelectric materials under high temperature.
