JOURNAL OF THE PHYSICAL SOCIETY OF JAPAN, Vol. 25, No. 5, NOVEMBER, 1968

Debye-Waller Factors of Tetragonal Barium Titanate

Jimpei HARADA* and Turid PEDERSEN

School of Physics, University of Melbourne, Australia

(Received May 24, 1968)

The anisotropic temperature parameters of tetragonal $BaTiO_{3}$ are studied theoretically on the basis of thermal diffuse X-ray scattering, and the effect of piezo-electricity is particularly noted. The calculations of the average temperature parameters along the $a$- and the $b$-axis respectively are $B_{11}^{P}=0.69$ and $B_{33}^{P}=0.20$ (in $\mathring{A}^{2}$ units) in the unclamped case and $B_{11}^{E}=0.72$ and $B_{33}^{E}=0.29$ in the clamped case. These results are then compared with previous experimental results from X-ray (Evans) and neutron diffraction (Frazer).

## §1. Introduction

Of fundamental importance to the theoretical understanding of the ferroelectric behaviour of $BaTiO_{3}$ is a knowledge of the precise atomic positions in the three ferroelectric phases. The first detailed structure analysis of the tetragonal phase was made by Känzig¹⁾ in 1951 and later by Evans²⁾ (1953) whose careful analysis finally showed that a unique X-ray solution of the problem was not possible.³⁾ Because of the high scattering power of Ba and the small displacements of the Ti and O atoms from the cubic positions, the effects of the coordinate and temperature parameters along the tetragonal axis cannot be separated in the analysis of the diffraction data.⁴⁾

The difficulty was partly removed by the development of neutron techniques,⁵⁾⁶⁾ since in this case all the scattering factors are of the same order of magnitude. But the coupling of the parameters is still considerable enough to give incorrect results for the atomic positions unless the temperature parameters are known independently.³⁾ This paper describes an attempt to calculate the temperature parameters of the structure theoretically on the basis of thermal diffuse scattering.

The effect of temperature on the diffraction of X-rays as well as neutrons from crystals can be described, following Waller⁷⁾ (1925), by an exponential function $\exp\left\{-W_{k}\right\}$ where $W_{k}$ depends on the mean-square displacement $\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{k}{}^{2}\rangle$ of the $k$'th atom from its equilibrium position $\boldsymbol{R}_{k}$,

$$
W_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{Q} \cdot \boldsymbol{u}_{k}\right)^{2} ; \quad B_{k}=8 \pi^{2}\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{k}{}^{2}\rangle.
$$

In the occupation number representation⁸⁾ $W_{k}$ is given by

$$
W_{k}=\frac{\hbar}{2 N m_{k}} \sum_{s} \frac{\left(\boldsymbol{Q} \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_{s}{ }^{k}\right)^{2}}{\omega_{s}}\left(n_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\right), \quad(1)
$$

where $N$ is the number of unit cells in the crystal and $\boldsymbol{Q}(|\boldsymbol{Q}|=4 \pi \sin \theta / \lambda)$ the scattering vector. $M_{k}$ is the mass of the $k$'th atom, $\boldsymbol{e}_{s}{ }^{k}$ the polarization vector and $n_{s}$ is the number of phonons in state $s$.

The summation over $s$ is a summation over all possible modes of vibration $j$ for all wave vectors $\boldsymbol{q}$, and may thus be separated into a contribution from the acoustic vibrations $j=1,2,3$, plus a contribution from the optic vibrations $j=4,\cdots$, $3g$

$$
\text { i.e. } \sum_{s}=\sum_{q, j}=\sum_{q=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\text { acoustic })+\sum_{j=4}^{3 g}(\text { optic })\right),
$$

in the general case of $g$ particles in the unit cell.

## § 2. The Acoustic Branches

To obtain individual temperature parameters $B_{k}$ of a crystal, the dispersion relation $\omega_{s}=\omega_{j}(\boldsymbol{q})$ and the polarization vectors $\boldsymbol{e}_{s}{ }^{k}$ must be known. However, until the lattice dynamics of this substance is known, the true dispersion relation may be replaced, as a first approximation, by Debye model⁹⁾ for which the motion of the atoms in the unit cell resembles that of an elastic continum. The polarization vectors can then be written in the form

$$
\boldsymbol{e}_{s}{ }^{k}=\boldsymbol{e}_{j} \sqrt{\frac{m_{k}}{m}} ; \quad m=\sum_{q} m_{k}.
$$

If it is further assumed that the average energy of each mode* $E_{s}=\hbar \omega_{s}(n_{s}+\frac{1}{2}) \simeq k T$, the acoustic temperature factors $W_{k}$ are given by

$$
W_{k}=\frac{k T}{2 N m} Q^{2} \sum_{q} \frac{1}{q^{2}} \sum_{j=1}^{3} \frac{\left(\boldsymbol{e}_{(j)} \cdot \boldsymbol{h}\right)^{2}}{V_{(j)}^{2}(f)}, \quad(2)
$$

which are independent of the mass $m_{k}$. The unit vectors $\boldsymbol{e}_{(j)}\left(e_{(j) 1}, e_{(j) 2}, e_{(j) 3}\right)$ and $\boldsymbol{h}\left(h_{1}, h_{2}, h_{3}\right)$ are in the directions of $\boldsymbol{e}_{s}{ }^{k}$ and $\boldsymbol{Q}$ respectively, and the velocity $V_{(j)}(f)$ of a particular wave $j$ is given by the relation $\omega_{(j)}=\boldsymbol{q} \cdot \boldsymbol{V}_{(j)}(f)$ where $f$ corresponds to the direction cosines $\left(f_{1}, f_{2}, f_{3}\right)$ of the wave normal to the elastic wave.

From eq. (2)

$$
W \infty \sum_{q} \frac{1}{q^{2}} \sum_{j=1}^{3} \frac{\left(\boldsymbol{e}_{(j)} \cdot \boldsymbol{h}\right)^{2}}{V_{(j)}^{2}(f)},
$$

which may be given in terms of a second rank tensor $B_{p q}$ where

$$
B_{p q} \propto \sum_{q} \frac{1}{q^{2}} \sum_{j=1}^{3} \frac{e_{(j) p} \cdot e_{(j) q}}{V_{(j)}^{2}}. \quad(3)
$$

In the case of cubic, tetragonal and orthorhombic symmetry, $B_{p q}$ can be related to the surface integration of the $K$-surface of thermal diffuse scattering¹⁰⁾ around the $\left(h_{1}, h_{2}, h_{3}\right)$ reciprocal lattice point*.

$$
K(f)_{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{3} \frac{\left(\boldsymbol{e}_{(j)} \cdot \boldsymbol{h}\right)^{2}}{\rho V_{(j)}^{2}(f)}=h_{p} h_{q}\left(A^{-1}\right)_{p q}, \quad(4)
$$

where $\left(A^{-1}\right)_{p q}$ is the inverse matrix of $A_{p q}=C_{p q} f_{m} f_{L}$ which is obtained from the elastic constants $C_{p q}$ and the direction cosines of the wave normal. The explicit formulation of $A_{p q}$ in the case of tetragonal symmetry is shown in the Appendix which also gives the values of the elastic constants $C_{p q}$ at constant field $E$ and constant polarization $P$ for tetragonal $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$. The two different cases are due to the piezo electricity of $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ in this phase.

Comparison of eqs. (3) and (4) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{p q}= & \frac{16 \pi^{2} k T}{2 N m} \sum_{q} \frac{\rho}{q^{2}}\left(A^{-1}\right)_{p q}, \quad(5) \\
= & 0 \text { unless } p=q,
\end{aligned}
$$

in the case of tetragonal symmetry, in which case the temperature parameter tensor reduces to

$$
\left\|\begin{array}{ccc}
B_{11} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & B_{11} & 0 \\
0 & 0 & B_{33}
\end{array}\right\|,
$$

where $\overline{B_{11}^{a c}}=4 k T q_{m} \cdot \overline{K(f)_{x}} ; \quad \frac{4 \pi}{3} q_{m}{ }^{3}=N$

$$
\overline{B_{33}^{a c}}=4 k T q_{m} \cdot \overline{K(f)_{z}},
$$

and $K(f)_{x}=\left(A^{-1}\right)_{11}$ and $K(f)_{z}=\left(A^{-1}\right)_{33}$.

### §3. The Optic Vibrations
The contribution of the optic vibrations to the temperature parameters of tetragonal $\text{BaTiO}_3$ has been calculated in the harmonic approximation* on the basis of thermal diffuse scattering¹²⁾ which until recently¹³⁾ seemed to have established the existence of a very low frequency transverse optic (TO) branch at room temperature.

A neutron diffraction study of cubic $\text{BaTiO}_3$ at the two temperatures $230^\circ\text{C}$ and $430^\circ\text{C}$ by Shirane $et$ $al$.¹⁴⁾ indicates a low frequency TO branch with a fairly constant frequency in the region $q=0$ to $0.2\mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$ followed by a second constant frequency in the region $q=0.4\mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$ to $0.782\mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$ (Fig. 1). Measurements of X-ray diffuse scattering on tetragonal $\text{BaTiO}_3$¹²⁾ give two values for the frequencies $\nu=0.71\times10^{12}\sec^{-1}$ and $\nu=1.1\times10^{12}\sec^{-1}$ at $q=0.31\mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$ and $0.36\mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$ respectively. Using these two values together with Ballantyne's result from infrared spectroscopy,¹⁵⁾ $\nu=0.18\times10^{12}\sec^{-1}$ for the frequency at $q=0$, an approximate dispersion curve (Fig. 1) for the tetragonal phase can be constructed, if it is assumed that the general shape of the frequency distribution is the same in the tetragonal and cubic phases.¹⁶⁾

Infrared measurements¹⁷⁾ and the Raman spectrum¹⁸⁾ of $\text{BaTiO}_3$ indicate other optic branches, but because of the very much higher frequencies of these, (the next lowest frequency $\nu_3(\text{TO})=5.4\times10^{-12}\sec^{-1}$¹⁹⁾) their contribution to the temperature factor is very much less than that of the low frequency branch and may be ignored.

According to the X-ray study¹²⁾, the intensity distribution of the diffuse scattering due to the low frequency modes forms sheets parallel to the $a^*-c^*$ plane. This indicates that all the polarization vectors of the TO branch are parallel to the $a$-axis, $i.e$. either $e_k=e_{kx}\boldsymbol{i}$ or $e_k=e_{ky}\boldsymbol{j}$ with $e_{kz}=0$, and we obtain for the average $x$-component of the optic temperature parameter

$$
B_{11}^{op}=\sum_{k}\frac{m_kB_{k\ x}^{op}}{m}=\frac{2kT}{N}\sum_{q}\frac{1}{\nu^2},\qquad(6)
$$

making use of the orthogonality condition $\sum_{k}e_k\times e_k=1$. The summation over $q$ is a summation over the low frequency TO branch only. The absence of any vibration along the $c$-axis will give rise to a high degree of anisotropy such that

$$
\overline{B_{k\ x}^{op}}=\overline{B_{k\ y}^{op}}\ \text{and}\ \overline{B_{k\ z}^{op}}=0\ .\qquad(7)
$$

Referring to Fig. 1 and replacing the summation over $q$ by an integration over $q$-space

$$
\sum_{k}m_kB_{k\ x}^{op}=\frac{2kT}{N}\ \Delta\iint\frac{1}{\nu^2}dq_xdq_y,\qquad(8)
$$

Because of the very low frequencies of the TO lattice vibrations even fairly small changes in $\nu$ will have a relatively large effect on the exact values of the temperature parameters. The uncertainty of the TO frequency distribution for the tetragonal phase thus makes it very difficult to specify the accuracy of $B_{11}^{op}$, but estimated upper and lower limits, based on the equation for the temperature dependence of $\nu(TO)^{21)}$ and infrared spectroscopy measurements of $\nu$ at $q=0$, are 0.67 $\mathring{A}^{2}$ and $0.23 \mathring{A}^{2}$ respectively.

### §4. The Temperature Behaviour

The temperature behaviour of the acoustic temperature parameters may be investigated from a knowledge of the temperature dependence of the elastic constants. Experimental results $^{22)}$ indicate that these are almost constant with temperature except near the transition to the cubic phase. Now $\overline{B^{ac}} \propto T \cdot K(f)_{h}$ where $\overline{K(f)_{h}} \propto 1/C_{ij}$. Thus in the low temperature region $\overline{B^{ac}}$ varies linearly with $T$. As the temperature is increased there will be a gradual levelling off and then an increase corresponding to the decrease in the $C_{ij}^{E}$'s near the transition point (see Fig. 2). A similar behaviour is observed in the cubic phase where $\overline{B_{11}^{ac}}(T)=\overline{B_{33}^{ac}}(T)$ have been calculated from a set of elastic constants published by Huibregste et al. $^{22)}$

The variation of $\overline{B_{11}^{op}}$ with temperature was investigated assuming Cochran's relation for the temperature dependence of the TO modes. $^{14,21,23\rangle}$ The behaviour near the critical temperature, however, is rather uncertain because of the possible disappearance of these modes as $T_{c}$ is approached.

### §5. Conclusion and Discussion

Several general conclusions can be reached from the above theoretical consideration. In the case of the acoustic lattice vibrations, a first approximation to the polarization vectors is to let $e_{k}$ may be proportional to $\sqrt{m_{k}}$, in which case the acoustic temperature parameters are approximately the same for all the atoms in the unit cell. Although $\overline{B_{11}^{ac}}$ is smaller than $\overline{B_{33}^{ac}}$, the difference $\overline{B_{33}^{ac}}-\overline{B_{11}^{ac}}$ is less than $\overline{B_{11}^{op}}$ so that in general $B_{11}(k)>B_{33}(k)$ except in the case of a very heavy atom, e.g. Ba for which $B_{11}^{op}(k)$ may be very small, as $B_{11}^{op}(k)$ is not independent of $m_{k}$.

The values agree quite well both in magnitude and anisotropy, and except for $Ba, B_{11}$  $(k)>B_{33}(k)$ , which is in agreement with the above considerations. Evans' first set of results $(E_{1})$  obtained from a least squares analysis of his X- ray data with fixed $z$ -parameters is in particularly good agreement with our calculation, although these results were initially rejected by him becauseof the unusually small temperature parameters. $^{25)}$  However, the exact values of the individual tem- perature parameters are not very reliable, as we have reasons to believe Evans' intensity data to be inadequately corrected for extinction $^{26)}$ and scaling factor, $^{4)}$ both of which have a significanteffect on the temperature parameters. $^{27)}$

Another important conclusion is that the tem- perature parameters at constant field are greater than those at constant polarization, a tendency also observed when comparing Frazer's and Evans' results. The single domain crystal requir- ed for a neutron diffraction analysis can only be maintained with the application of an electric field. Under these conditions the specimen is said to be soft, much softer than that used by Evans. The values of the temperature parameters will there- fore be different; the softer crystal having larger temperature parameters, which is a result of the piezo-electricity of the substance. The very much greater difference between $B_{33}$ at constant field and constant polarization than the corresponding difference in $B_{11}$ can easily be explained in terms of the piezo-electric effect, confirming the fact that the direction of piezo-electricity is along the c-axis. The temperature parameters of piezo- electric substances will thus, as shown in the case of $BaTiO_{3}$ , depend on the experimental conditions and will be different for an unclamped and a clamped crystal.

So far the temperaturs parameters have been discussed on the basis of the existence of a low frequency TO branch. However, observations of neutron critical scattering in $BaTiO_{3}$ near and above the Curie temperature $^{14)}$ did not indicate optical phonons with $0<q<0.15 \AA^{-1}$ in the tem perature region $137^{\circ} C$ to $270^{\circ} C$ . Instead strong elastic or quasi-elastic scattering was observed around $q=0$ , whose temperature and $q$ -dependence is similar in nature to an order-disorder type critical scattering. If the tetragonal orthorhombic transition is similar to the cubic-tetragonal transi- tion it may be possible that the tetragonal orthor- hombic transition also shows such a strong quasi- elastic scattering, in which case the diffuse scat- tering observed in the tetragonal phase is due to critical scattering rather than to the low frequency optic vibrations. (Calculation of diffuse intensity modulation does not exclude this possibility). If this is so, there will be no contribution from the optic vibrations to the temperature parameters and $B_{11}=\bar{B}_{11}^{a c}$ since $\bar{B}_{11}^{o p}=0$ . Critical scattering, however, does modulate the scattering intensity in a manner similar to that of the temperature parameters and can be considered to add pseudo- temperature factors $^{28)}$ to the structure factor of a crystal.
