# Origin of Ferroelectric Phase Transitions of $\mathbf{Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI}$ Mixed Crystals

A. AUDZIJONIS, L. ŽIGAS,* A. KVEDARAVIČIUS, AND R. ŽALTAUSKAS

Department of Physics, Vilnius Pedagogical University, Studentu 39, LT-08106, Vilnius, Lithuania

The nature of normal modes' anharmonism of SbSI—type crystals is investigated in this work. In order to explain the nature of anharmonism we apply the normal vibration mode's average potential energy's $\bar{V}_p(z)$ dependence on amplitudes of normal coordinates along c(z)—axis. It is proved that the variation of potential barrier $\Delta V_C$ between two wells of double-well potential energy $V_p(z)$ is caused by temperature $T$, quasihydrostatic pressure $p$, and mixture $x$.

The temperature dependence of ferroelectric phase transition on quasihidrostatic pressure $p$ and mixture $x$ is determined for SbSI and $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ crystals. Applying the equality $\Delta V_C \approx k_B T_C$ is theoretically proved that there is no ferroelectric phase transition in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ crystals where $x > 0.7$. The ferroelectric phase transition in SbSI—type crystals and $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ crystals is caused by the phonon interaction's dependence on temperature, pressure and mixture.

Keywords SbSI; molecular cluster; total density; electronic band structure

**PACS NUMBERS:** 21.60.Gx; 36.40.–c; 61.50. Ah; 71.20.–b; 73.22.–f

## 1. Introduction
$Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ is isostructural to the well—known quasi—one—dimensional ferroelectric SbSI, which exhibits a number of strongly coupled semiconductive and ferroelectric properties [1]. Very little is known about the origin of phase transition in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ (x = 0.8 ÷ 1). A ferroelectric phase transition at 115 K in BiSI was reported [2, 3], but the existence of such transitions has not been confirmed by dielectric constant measurements in pure BiSI crystal [4–6].

The authors [7] found that Bi atoms are too large and the anharmonicity is too small to induce a ferroelectric phase transition as in SbSI crystal [1]. In view of fundamental difference between SbSI and BiSI, it seemed interesting to undertake a detailed study and the $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ mixed crystals. In addition to the interest on soft mode in mixed crystals, this study brings information on origin of phase transition in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$. Is is important to explain the experimental results of [4–6] that the mixed crystal maintains a soft—mode behavior for the Bi concentration lower than 0.70.

On the contrary, for x > 0.70 no appreciable temperature dependence of soft mode frequency is observed within a temperature range extending from 4.2 to 300 K. It is seemed also interesting to explain the influence of the Bi concentration and hydrostatic pressure effect as reported by Peercy [8] on the Curie temperature $T_C$.

The purpose of this paper was to study the influence of the Bi concentration on anharmoninity of soft mode and on the Curie temperature $T_C$ of $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ mixed crystals.

The organization of this paper is as follows:

In second section we investigate the nature of normal vibration modes' anharmonism in SbSI—type crystals. In order to explain the nature of anharmonism we apply the normal vibration mode's average potential energy's $\bar{V}_P(z)$ dependence on amplitudes of normal coordinates along $c(z)$—axis.

The ferroelectric phase transition temperature's $T_C$ dependence on quasihidrostatic pressure is being explained in third part of our work. The phase transition temperature $T_C$ is estimated applying the equality $\Delta V_C \approx k_B T_C$, where $\Delta V_C$ is the barrier height between two wells in double-well potential energy $\bar{V}_P(z)$.

In fourth section the dependence of ferroelectric phase transition's temperature $T_C$ in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ crystals is determined using the same equality $\Delta V_C \approx k_B T_C$.

It is being theoretically investigated the reason of absence of ferroelectric phase transition in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ crystals where x > 0.7.

## 2. The Nature Normal Modes' Anharmonism of SbSI—Type Crystals

In [7,9] we proposed the method for calculating the potential at point $\boldsymbol{r}$ of the unit cell. This method allows determination of the potential energy at any point $\boldsymbol{r}$ in the unit cell on the basis of standard structural data and requires no additional adjustable parameters for fitting the results to the experimental data.

All 12 atoms of the unit cell induce the following electronic potential at the point $\boldsymbol{r}$:

$$
V_{P}(\boldsymbol{r})=\sum_{\alpha} V_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{r})=\sum_{\alpha, s} e \frac{4 \pi}{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{s}|^{-2} f_{\alpha}(s) \exp \left[-i\left(r+\boldsymbol{R}_{\alpha}\right) s\right], \tag{1}
$$

were $f_\alpha(s)$—atomic form factors, $|s|$—reciprocal lattice vectors.

Equation (1) has been described in [10] and applied in [11] for studying the band is structure of crystals. For evaluating the dependence of $V_P(\boldsymbol{r})$ upon the normal mode coordinates $\boldsymbol{Q}_\alpha$ one should substitute $(\boldsymbol{R}_{0\alpha}+\boldsymbol{Q}_\alpha)$ for $\boldsymbol{R}_\alpha$.

Then the total electronic potential induced by the normal mode atoms is described at point $\boldsymbol{r}$ by the following formula:

$$
V_{P}(\boldsymbol{r})=\sum_{\alpha, s} e \frac{4 \pi}{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{s}|^{-2} f_{\alpha}(s) \exp \left[-i\left(\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{R}_{0 \alpha}+\boldsymbol{Q}_{\alpha}\right) s\right]. \tag{2}
$$

The electron density depends upon the amplitude of the thermal motion of atoms. Therefore, the temperature dependence of $V_P(\boldsymbol{r})$ is obtained by introducing the Debye–Waller factor determined by the mean-square amplitude of the thermal vibration of atoms [12]:

$$
\exp [-M(s)]=\exp \left[-\left(b_{11} h^2+b_{22} k^2+b_{33} l^2+2 b_{12} h k+2 b_{13} h l+2 b_{23} k l\right)\right]. \tag{3}
$$

Here $h, k, l$ are Miller indices, and $b_{11}, b_{22}, b_{33}$ are temperature factors in the direction of $x, y, z$—axes, respectively.

For convenience, we make the following substitutions:

$$
b_{11} h^{2}=\frac{1}{16 \pi^{2}}\left(s_{x}^{2} B_{x}\right), \quad \text { where } \quad s_{x}=\frac{2 \pi \cdot h}{a}, \quad B_{x}=4 a^{2} b_{11} ;
$$

$$
b_{22} k^{2}=\frac{1}{16 \pi^{2}}\left(s_{y}^{2} B_{y}\right), \quad \text { where } \quad s_{y}=\frac{2 \pi \cdot h}{b}, \quad B_{y}=4 b^{2} b_{22} ;
$$

$$
b_{33} l^{2}=\frac{1}{16 \pi^{2}}\left(s_{z}^{2} B_{z}\right), \quad \text { where } \quad s_{z}=\frac{2 \pi \cdot h}{c}, \quad B_{z}=4 c^{2} b_{33} ;
$$

were $a, b, c$—unit cell parameters.

For SbSI and BiSI chain crystals, the values of constants $a, b, c, b_{11}, b_{22}, b_{33}$ are provided in [12-13], and $s_{x}, s_{y}, s_{z}, s$ were calculated using a special computer routine.

With account of Eq. (3), Eq. (2) written as follows:

$$
V_{P}(\boldsymbol{r})=\sum_{\alpha, s} e \frac{4 \pi}{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{s}|^{-2} f_{\alpha}(s) \exp \left[-i\left(\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{R}_{0 \alpha}+\boldsymbol{Q}_{\alpha}\right) s\right] \exp [-M(s)]. \tag{4}
$$

The potentials energy of atoms in the normal mode is evaluated by substituting $\boldsymbol{r}$ with radius vectors $\boldsymbol{R}_{S b}, \boldsymbol{R}_{S}$, and $\boldsymbol{R}_{I}$ corresponding to the positions of the atoms Sb (or Bi), S, and I in the unit cell. Because of the equivalence of similar atoms in the unit cell, the following equalities are valid:

$$
V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S b 1}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S b 2}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S b 3}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S b 4}\right), \quad \text { where } \quad \boldsymbol{R}_{s b}=\boldsymbol{R}_{0 S b}+\boldsymbol{Q}_{S b},
$$

$$
V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S 1}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S 2}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S 3}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S 4}\right), \quad \text { where } \quad \boldsymbol{R}_{S}=\boldsymbol{R}_{0 S}+\boldsymbol{Q}_{S},
$$

$$
V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{I 1}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{I 2}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{I 3}\right)=V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{I 4}\right), \quad \text { where } \quad \boldsymbol{R}_{I}=\boldsymbol{R}_{0 I}+\boldsymbol{Q}_{I}.
$$

The average value of the potential energy per one atom of the unit cell $\bar{V}_{P}$ in the normal mode may be written as follows:

$$
\bar{V}_{P}=\frac{V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S b 3}\right)+V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S b 4}\right)+V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S 3}\right)+V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S 4}\right)+V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{I 1}\right)+V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{I 2}\right)}{6}. \tag{5}
$$

where $V_{P}(\boldsymbol{R}_{\mathrm{Sb} 3}), V_{P}(\boldsymbol{R}_{\mathrm{Sb} 4}), V_{P}(\boldsymbol{R}_{\mathrm{S} 3}), V_{P}(\boldsymbol{R}_{\mathrm{S} 4}), V_{P}(\boldsymbol{R}_{\mathrm{I} 1})$ and $V_{P}(\boldsymbol{R}_{\mathrm{I} 2})$ are electronic potentials of Sb, S, I atoms in the same chain (Fig. 1).

The atomic form factor is

$$
f_{a}(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{n l m}\langle n l m|\exp [-i(r \cdot s)]| n l m\rangle, \tag{6}
$$

where $n l m$ is a set of quantum numbers for the atom $\alpha$.

It should be noted that for calculating the form factors $f_{\alpha}(s)$ by Eq. (6) we used the functions $<n l m|$ of all electronic states of an atom. For the sum in Eq. (4) we used about 5000 vectors $s$.

The average value of the potential energy $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ per one atom of the unit cell in the normal mode of $\mathrm{Bi}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{SI}$ may be written as follows:

$$
\bar{V}_{P}=\frac{2 V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{A}\right)+2 V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{S}\right)+2 V_{P}\left(\boldsymbol{R}_{I}\right)}{6}
$$

Where $\bar{V}_{P}(R_{A})$, $\bar{V}_{P}(R_{S})$ and $\bar{V}_{P}(R_{I})$ are potential energy of atoms (S3 or S4), (I1 or I2) and A atom respectively. In this case the atomic form factor for A atom is
$$
f_{A}(\boldsymbol{s})=f_{B i}(\boldsymbol{s}) \cdot x+f_{S b}(\boldsymbol{s}) \cdot(1-x)
$$
where f$_{\text{Bi}}$(s) and f$_{\text{I}}$(s) are atomic form factors of atoms Bi and I respectively.

The dependence of the atomic form factors $f_{\alpha}(\mathbf{s})$ for Bi, Sb, S, Se, I, Br atoms on the modulus of the reciprocal lattice vector $|\mathbf{s}|$ in Fig. 2 has been published in [14]. Since during the phase transition the Sb and S atoms shift along the $\boldsymbol{c}(z)$—axis, we shall study the potential energy $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ of the B$_{1\text{u}}$ (A$_{\text{u}}$) symmetry soft mode vibrations upon the normal coordinates or symmetry coordinates (relative displacement of atoms).

Since in the B$_{1\text{u}}$ normal mode the atoms oscillate along the $\boldsymbol{c}(z)$ - axis, then in Eqs. (4) and (5) we put $Q_{\alpha}=z_{\alpha}$, where $Q_{\alpha}$ and $z_{\alpha}$ denote atomic displacements from their equilibrium position $z_{0,\alpha}$. For numerical evaluation of $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ (Figs. 6 and 8) by Eqs. (4) and (5), we need to vary all $Q_{\alpha}=z_{\alpha}$ by small steps from $-Q_{\alpha}$ (max) to $+Q_{\alpha}$ (max).

In Fig. 3 the average potential energy $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ per one atom of the B$_{1\text{u}}$ (A$_{\text{u}}$) soft mode vibrations in SbSI is shown as a function of the amplitude of B$_{1\text{u}}$ symmetry coordinate ones [1111 1111 -2-2-2-2] (relative displacement of the Sb atom from its equilibrium position) in the direction of $\boldsymbol{c}(z)$—axis in temperature region $T>T_{C}$. We have found that the curves $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ may be described by the equation:
$$
\bar{V}_{p}(z)=V_{0}+a z+b z^{2}+d z^{3}+c z^{4}, \tag{7}
$$

Where $V_{0}$ is the value of $V(z)$ at $z=0$. The coefficients $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ are temperature dependent. At high temperatures $(T \gg T_{C})$, the potential energy (per one atom) $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ is a symmetrical single-well ($a=d=0$). Closer to $T_{C}$, the potential energy $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ acquires a symmetrical double-well shape with the potential energy barrier height of $\Delta V_{c}=b^{2}/4c$ ($a$ $=d=0, b<0, c>0$). During the ferroelectric phase transition $(T \approx T_{\text{C}})$, the potential energy $\bar{V}_{P}(z)$ transforms from symmetrical single-well to asymmetrical double-well shape ($a \neq 0$ and $d \neq 0, b<0, c>0$).

### 4. $Bi_xSb_{1-x}$SI Crystals' PE Dependence on Mixture x

The $T_C$ dependence on mixture x is theoretically and practically important. Calculating PE $V(z)$ dependence on normal coordinates of $Bi_xSb_{1-x}$SI we used average form factors from work [16]: $\bar{f}_V=F_{Bi}x+f_{Sb}(1-x)$, where $f_{Bi}$ and $f_{Sb}$ are Bi and Sb atomic form factors (Fig. 2).

The $\Delta V$ decreases along with the increase of x (Fig. 6). Consequently, the increase of x (for the variation of $\Delta V$) is equivalent to pressure increase. Decreasing the crystals' temperature, the barrier $\Delta V$ height of double-well potential energy $V(z)$ increases. When $\Delta V$ reaches the value of $\Delta V_C=0.007$ a.u. (corresponds $\Delta V$ when $T_C=293$ K) the phase transition occurs. Therefore decreasing temperature and taking into account that $\Delta V=\Delta V_C$ one can see the $T_C$ dependence on mixture x for mixed $Bi_xSb_{1-x}$SI crystals (Fig. 7).

It is seen from Fig. 7 and Table 2 that when x > 0.7 there is no phase transition in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}$SI crystals. Crystals where x > 0.7 persist in paraelectric phase in temperature range from 0 to 400 K. In given temperature range $\Delta V<\Delta V_C$. This theoretical conclusion verifies experimental results published in articles [5, 6].

### Conclusions

At the time of phase transition, when unit cell's parameters a, b and c vary more than in paraelectric phase and Sb atoms change its equilibrium position and $B_{1u}$ soft mode potential energy $\bar{V}_p(z)$ transforms from symmetrical double-well to asymmetrical single-well shape.

The phase transition temperature $T_C$ depends on various factors:

1.  Mixed $Bi_xSb_{1-x}$SI crystals' phase transition temperature $T_C$ depends on mixture x and pressure p. Increasing x or p, $T_C$ decreases and vice versa.
2.  Mixed crystals' $T_C$ depends on the magnitude of form factor $\bar{f}_V$. Increasing $\bar{f}_V$, $T_C$ decreases and vice versa.

3. Mixed and stecheometric SbSI crystals $B_{1u}$ soft mode's potential energy $\bar{V}_p(z)$ depends on a temperature. $\Delta V$ depends on a phonon interaction. Increasing tem- perature phonon interaction increases. Decreasing temperature phonon interaction decreases while $\Delta V$ increases. Phase transition occurs when double-well potential energy's $\bar{V}_p(z)$ barrier height reaches value of $\Delta V_C$ (which is $\Delta V_C \approx k_B T_C$).

Theoretical and experimental $T_C$ dependence on p and x coincides quite well. This proves that ferroelectric phase transition in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ crystals is caused by phonon inter- action's dependence on p and x.

When x > 0.7 there is no ferroelectric phase transition in $Bi_xSb_{1-x}SI$ crystals.
