# The double level calculation of oxygen related donor states in Si and SiO₂

M.Z. Xu*, C.H. Tan, L.F. Mao

Institute of Microelectronics, Peking University, Beijing 100871, People's Republic of China

Received 10 August 2000; accepted 27 October 2000 by H. Eschrig

## Abstract
A calculation of the levels related to oxygen donors in Si and SiO₂ has been performed based on a helium-like model. These calculations agree well with the experimental data while the energies of these levels are in the ratio of about 2:1 and the effective radii of these levels are in the ratio of about 2:1. © 2001 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

Keywords: A. Insulator; A. Semiconductor; D. Electronic states (localized)

PACS: 71.55; 72.20; 73.40.Q

It is well known that in germanium and silicon-type semiconductors the substitutional impurities differing by two valences from the host crystal atoms generate two localized energy levels in the forbidden band. The upper level corresponds to the presence of two electrons; the lower one to the case in which one of the electrons has been removed (at a distance at which its presence has no more influence). The impurities like Se and Te in germanium and S in silicon can generate double donor states. Breitenecker et al [1] and Glodeanu [2] concluded that the double level can be determined by a Helium-like model. Though it is a simple method, it displays the rapidness in calculation especially for double level calculation because the effective nuclear charge is very close to 2 (where it ranges between 2 and 3).

The question of the energy levels related to oxygen in silicon is beyond our full understanding. Although the mechanism of formation of donor states in heat-treated silicon has been studied in detail by Kaiser [3], the oxygen double donor is not observed in his experiments. Maybe this is why Glodeanu did not consider the oxygen double donor in a series of his work. Twenty years later, Kimerling et al reported that oxygen donors are produced by heat treatment of silicon and their energy levels are $E_{\rm c} - 0.07$ eV and $E_{\rm c} - 0.15$ eV [4]. Recently, Lamp et al observed again that two energy levels of the thermal donor complex in silicon are $E_{\rm c} - 0.15$ eV (TD1) and $E_{\rm c} - 0.07$ eV (TD2) by a deep level transient spectroscopy [5]. But the calculation related to this phenomenon has not been done yet. Recently, the stress-induced defect-state activation energies of SiO₂ are observed that usually has two energy levels which are 2.0 eV [6], and 0.93 eV in average [7,8], respectively. We believe that the defects induced in SiO₂ by electrical stress also are oxygen-related defects that are similar to the oxygen donors in silicon. Under electrical stress, some Si-O weak bonds have been broken so that oxygen vacancies are produced and oxygen complexes are generated. The generated oxygen complex may be SiO₄, named as double donor states in Refs. [4,5]. There are some analogies between heat treatment of Si and high field treatment of SiO₂. The heat energy can be treated as a far infrared electromagnetic wave and the high field as an ultraviolet electromagnetic wave. Both treatments can cause the oxygen and silicon atoms to be rearranged and relocated. It is suggested that a helium-like model can describe the phenomena of oxygen-related donors for Si and SiO₂.

The first level is the ionization energy, which is the minimum energy, required to remove one of the electrons in the ground state. $$
E_{\mathrm{g}}=-\left(Z_{\mathrm{eff}}-\frac{5}{16}\right)^{2} \text{ (a.u.)} \tag{1}
$$

To calculate the ionization energy required to remove one electron we must substrate this from the energy of the single electron in the ion to give [9]

$$
I=\left(Z_{\mathrm{eff}}-\frac{5}{16}\right)^{2}-\frac{Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{2}=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{eff}}}{2}\left(Z_{\mathrm{eff}}-\frac{5}{4}\right)+\frac{25}{256} \text{ (a.u.)} \tag{2}
$$

where $Z_{\text{eff}}$ is the effective charge of the nucleus, the term of $-Z_{\text{eff}}^{2}/2$ is the energy of the single electron in the ion. It can be obtained from the following supposition: we view one of the electrons as existing in the Coulomb field of the nucleus shielded by the other. For the helium atom, when one electron is removed from the atom, the left electron is still in the orbit of 1s, according to the formula of the energy of hydrogen atom we can obtain its value

$$
E=-\frac{Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{2} \text{ (a.u.)} \tag{3}
$$

This is an energy required to remove the second electron. It is obvious that Eqs. (2) and (3) correspond to the double levels of donor states related to oxygen, respectively: the ionization energy of the first electron corresponds to the shallow level which is determined by Eq. (2) ($E_1=I$, i.e. TD2 [5]), the ionization energy of the second electron corresponds to the deep level which is determined by Eq. (3) ($E_2=-E$, i.e. TD1 [5]). If Eqs. (2) and (3) are written in terms of the effective Rydberg-unit, we have

$$
E_{1}=\left[Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}-\frac{5}{4} Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+\frac{25}{128}\right] R_{\mathrm{eff}} \tag{4}
$$

and

$$
E_{2}=Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2} R_{\mathrm{eff}} \tag{5}
$$

where

$$
R_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\left(m^{*} / m\right)}{(\epsilon / \epsilon_{0})^{2}} \frac{R_{0}}{2} \tag{6}
$$

where $R_0=2×13.6$ eV, $m^*$ and $m$ are the effective and free electron masses, $\epsilon$ and $\epsilon_0$ are the material and free space permittivities.

The effective electron orbit radii at the energies $E_1$ and $E_2$ are given by

$$
a_{1}=\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{0}} \frac{m}{m^{*}} 0.291 \mathring{\mathrm{A}}, \text{ and } a_{2}=\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{0}} \frac{m}{m^{*}} 0.529 \mathring{\mathrm{A}}, \text{ respectively} \tag{7}
$$

From the above discussion, it is clear that the second level (the lower level or deep level) which corresponds to Eq. (3) occurs only after the first electron is ionized. Eqs. (4) and (5) are the basic relations of calculating levels related double donor in Si and SiO₂. They are similar to those in Ref. [2]. The depth of the donor levels is counted from the conduction band. The conductivity effective mass for Si is given by [10]

$$
m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}=\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{3}}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{2}{m_{2}}\right) \tag{8}
$$

where $m_2=m_3=m_\tau=0.19m$, $m_1=m_\text{L}^*=0.98m$ [10], then $m_{\text{eff}}=0.26m$. The calculated and measured values of the energy levels related to oxygen donor states in Si and SiO₂ are shown in Table 1. Where the parameters used in the calculation: $m^*/m=0.26$ [10], $\epsilon/\epsilon_0=12$ for Si, and $m^*/m=0.50$ [11,12], $\epsilon/\epsilon_0=3.9$ for SiO₂. We treat $Z_{\text{eff}}$ as a parameter in Eqs. (4) and (5) which are fitted to the experimental results in a manner that the sum of the squares of the relative errors becomes minimum. For impurity of O in Si and SiO₂, there appear experimental double levels, one at 0.07 eV, and the other at 0.15 eV under the silicon conduction band; one at 0.925 eV (average) and the other at 2.00 eV under the oxide conduction band. The calculated values for the double levels are very well consistent with the experimental one for both Si and SiO₂. So the four valence electrons of O atom will bonds with the neighboring atoms forming four covalent bonds, and the excess two electrons will bond with the two positive charge centers forming a helium-like structure while the element O of group VI substitutes the silicon atom in silicon. It means that the ionization can occur twice and that will cause the double donor states related to oxygen.

A theoretical estimate of the double level energy for oxygen related donor states in Si and SiO₂ is made and based on the helium atom model. It is an inductive method based on the experimental data and the fundamental principles of quantum mechanics, whose advantage is clear in physical understanding and simple in mathematical expression. It is useful and important for understanding the basic properties of oxygen atom in Si and SiO₂.
