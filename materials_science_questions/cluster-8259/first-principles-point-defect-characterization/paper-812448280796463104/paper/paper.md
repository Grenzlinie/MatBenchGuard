## 3. Results and discussions

PL spectra in the photon energy region 1.85–3.60 eV were investigated from 4.8 K to room temperature. The spectra at 4.8 K exhibited a sharp intense emission line (denoted as FE) at the highest energy, a complicated band near the band edge, and a broad yellow luminescence band, as shown in Fig. 1 for a $2-\mu\text{m}$-thick epilayer.

The sharp intense emission FE appears at energy of 3.489 eV. This emission can be attributed to free exciton [12]. Two sharp emission peaks at energy lower than FE are present with intensities about two orders of magnitude weaker than that of exciton emission. The energy intervals between the sharp peaks are equal and close to the values of the longitudinal optical (LO) phonon in GaN of 93 meV. The sharp peaks on the low-energy side of the FE peak were thus identified as phonon replicas of the FE peak, labeled FE-LO and FE-2LO.

At energy lower than FE-2LO, a smaller peak appears at 3.275 eV, accompanied by several weaker peaks with similar lineshapes. They are broader than the FE peak and its phonon replicas. The energies of these peaks increased with increasing excitation intensity. This is a characteristic of DAP recombination because the radiative lifetime increases with increasing DAP separation, and saturation occurs more readily for distant pairs than for near pairs. The energy intervals of these peaks are about 93 meV. Obviously, these peaks originate from DAP recombination with its LO phonon replicas, denoted as DA, DA-1LO, DA-2LO and so on.

Several weak emission peaks are present at energies below DA-3LO. Their energy intervals are much smaller than that of the DA recombination and its phonon replicas. This indicates that they are different from the phonon replicas of the DA recombination, and are, therefore, denoted as a BB.

In order to understand the nature of this recombination, the BB spectra were investigated at various temperatures. Fig. 2(a) shows the BB spectra from liquid-helium to room temperature. As temperature increases, the BB structure appears more distinctly while the intensities of phonon replicas of DA recombination decrease. Finally, the BB lineshape is free from influence of the DA recombination at temperatures

higher than 150 K. The BB structure is characterized by several peaks with a constant energy interval of about 68 meV and centered at 2.9 eV. The wavy structure was previously suggested to be an intensity modulation due to microcavity formed by the semiconductor-air and semiconductor-substrate interface [13]. This seems not to be the case for our samples because the energy intervals in sample with thickness of $1\ \mu$m are not twice as large as those in sample with thickness of $2\ \mu$m as they should actually be if they were caused by Fabry–Perot interferences. Furthermore, the energy intervals of our data are close to those in Ref. [13], where the intensity modulation should be much smaller according to theoretical estimation. In addition, the wavy structures with lower energies in BB appear more distinctly as temperature increases, which is likely to be caused by stronger phonon coupling at higher temperature. For these reasons, the wavy structure is believed to be due to phonon replicas of the BB rather than to Fabry–Perot interferences. The fact that the energy interval is much smaller than the transverse optical (TO) phonon energy of GaN suggests that BB is related to defects with local vibrational modes (LVMs).

On the lower energy side of BB in Fig. 1, YB is characterized by several peaks with a constant energy interval of about 81 meV and centered at 2.27 eV. Their energy intervals are larger than that of BB recombination and its phonon replicas. The wavy structure was also suggested to be Fabry–Perot interferences [13]. However, as mentioned above, since their energy intervals are independent of epilayer thickness, we believe that they are related to phonon replicas of the YB.

Since YB overlaps with BB, we try to separate YB with BB by changing the measurement temperature. However, it was not possible to remove the influence of BB even when temperature was increased up to 293 K where YB was hardly detectable with ultimate system sensitivity, as shown in Fig. 2(b). We only can conclude that the zero phonon line (ZPL) should appear at energy larger than 2.6 eV. The positions of the peaks of YB were investigated at different temperatures. No appreciable shift of peak positions was found. This shows that the YB is associated to localized defect levels rather than hydrogen-like shallow levels following the energy band gap that decreases with increasing temperature.

The peak positions of BB were investigated for different excitation intensities. The energy corresponding to the BB maximum was observed to decrease by 6 meV when the excitation intensity was halved. This exhibits the DAP characteristic of BB. Broad PL bands in the same spectral region are common in most p-type GaN, irrespective of dopant type. It has been speculated that this BB is of intrinsic origin, i.e. $V_N$ related [14,15]. Recently, some authors reported that it is possibly related to other residual impurities such as H, C, and O [16]. On the other hand, no distinct shift of peaks in YB was observed. This shows that YB is associated with transitions between inner levels of defects or deep levels and energy band.

To understand the origin of the PL spectra, the residual impurities were assessed by EDS in GaN epilayer. Except for the strong characteristic X-ray peaks of Ga, and N elements, the intensity of C and O impurities is recognizable. Furthermore, the C impurity concentration varies from sample to sample. In the sample with lower concentration of the C impurity, the BB intensity increases distinctly whereas the YB intensity is nearly the same. This suggests that the residual C and O impurities are associated with some PL lines mentioned above.

The electronic structures of native defects, C and O impurities, and their complexes in wurtzite GaN, were calculated using ab initio LDF methods with LMTO. The band gap of wurtzite GaN is first calculated by 72-atomic supercell and atomic sphere approaches (ASA). Then, the electronic structures of some monodefects are calculated by substituting a defect onto a host atom site. Typical densities of states (DOS) derived from the defects are shown in Fig. 3(a). The Ga vacancy ($V_{Ga}$) appears as acceptor states above the top of valence band $E_V$. The N vacancy ($V_N$) behaves somewhat complicated. The $a_1$ (s-like) state lies above $E_V$ whereas the $t_2$ (p-like) state appears as resonance in the conduction band, which agrees with the results in Ref. [17]. Substitutional C onto N site ($C_N$) has acceptor states close to $E_V$. Substitutional C onto Ga site ($C_{Ga}$) has donor states below the bottom of conduction band $E_C$. Since the formation energy of $O_N$ is much lower than that of $O_{Ga}$ [18], only substitutional O onto the N site ($O_N$) which gives donor states slightly below $E_C$ has been considered.

On the basis of the calculated results, the electron transitions from $V_N$, $C_{Ga}$, or $O_N$ donor states to $V_{Ga}$ or $C_N$ acceptor states are possibly responsible for the DAP recombinations. However, C and O are the principal residual impurities in our samples, which may occupy N sites and lead to a lower $V_N$ concentration. For this reason, we exclude the possibility of the transition from $V_N$ donor states in our samples. On the other hand, the energies of the electron transitions from $O_N$ states to $C_N$ or $V_{Ga}$ states are much larger than that from $C_{Ga}$ states to $C_N$ or $V_{Ga}$ states and closer to the energies of ZPL of DA and BB, respectively. Furthermore, according to the model of the diatomic linear chain [19], the vibrational frequencies of $O_N$ and $C_N$ must be close to that of optical phonon, and the vibrational frequency of $V_{Ga}$ should be much different. This agrees with the above measurements that the phonon energy of the DA is close to that of LO and the phonon energy of the BB is associated with LVM. These suggest that the electron transition from $O_N$ to $C_N$ is responsible for the DA line,

It was observed in our previous work that YB is enhanced in the areas with higher C and O concentrations [11]. Thus, the complexes of $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ are further calculated with substitutional C and O at different positions of the supercell. The calculated total DOS (TDOS) of GaN, $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ and GaN, $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ are shown in Fig. 3(b). The TDOS of GaN, $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ exhibit distinct states below $E_{\mathrm{C}}$, which indicates that there are donor levels in the band gap. The peak of these states appears at 0.71 eV below $E_{\mathrm{C}}$ when the $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ lies in the third neighbor shell of the $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}$ ($d_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}}=3.24$ Å). The peak shifts 0.55 eV away from $E_{\mathrm{C}}$ as the $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ is placed in the first neighbor shell of the $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}$ ($d_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}}=1.97$ Å). The TDOS of GaN, $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ exhibit distinct states in both edges of the band gap. The states near $E_{\mathrm{C}}$ shift about 0.17 eV into the band gap while the states near $E_{\mathrm{V}}$ show no distinct change as the $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ moved from the fourth ($d_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}}=5.18$ Å) to the second ($d_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}}=3.17$ Å) neighbor shell of the $C_{\mathrm{N}}$. The $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ states in the band gap are mainly derived from the $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}$ states with $d_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}}=3.24$ Å. As the $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ is placed in the first neighbor shell of the $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}$, the states in the band gap are derived not only from the $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}$ states but also from the $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ states. This is attributed to the hybridization increase when the distance between $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}$ and $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ decreases. The $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ states near $E_{\mathrm{V}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{C}}$ in the band gap are mainly derived from the $C_{\mathrm{N}}$ and $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ states, respectively. The approach of the states in the band gap are attributed to the $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ states moving away from $E_{\mathrm{C}}$ as the $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ moved from the fourth to the second neighbor shell of the $C_{\mathrm{N}}$. If the electrons transit from the states near $E_{\mathrm{C}}$ to the states near $E_{\mathrm{V}}$, their luminescence energy must decrease with decreasing distance between $C_{\mathrm{N}}$ and $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$. This indicates that the recombination energies of the C and O complexes depend on the distances between the C and O impurities that have different average values in samples with different C and O concentrations, which agrees with our observation that the peak energies of YB are different in samples with different C concentration. Furthermore, the total energy of the $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ complex with the distance of $3.17$ Å is much lower than that of the $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ complex with the distance of $1.97$ Å. This shows that the C and O codopants are likely to form $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ rather than $C_{\mathrm{Ga}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$. In addition, since the average mass of O and C impurities is close to the mass of N atom, the $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ vibration frequency must be close to that of the optical phonon energy of GaN. This is supported by the above observation that the phonon replicas of about 81 meV in YB are close to the energy of TO phonon in GaN. For these reasons, it is suggested that the transition between the inner levels of the $C_{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{N}}$ complex may be responsible for the YB.
