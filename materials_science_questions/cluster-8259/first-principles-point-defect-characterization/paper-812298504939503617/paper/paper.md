# Electrical Properties of Hydrous Forsterite Derived from First-Principles Calculations *

WANG Duo-Jun(王多君) $^{1 * *}$ , LIU Zai-Yang(刘在洋) $^{1}$ , YI Li(易丽) $^{2}$ , SHI Bao-Ping(史保平) $^{1}$

$^{1}$ Key Laboratory of Computational Geodynamics, Graduate University, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049
$^{2}$ Institute of Earthquake Science, China Earthquake Administration, Beijing 100039

(Received 6 December 2010)

We investigate electrical properties of anhydrous and hydrous forsterite crystal with $3.2 wt \%$ water by using firstprinciples calculations. The calculation results indicate that the pressure weakly affects the electrical properties of anhydrous forsterite. Two types of defect configurations involving the two hydrogen atoms in different positions are considered. Type 1 involves the entrapment of two hydrogen atoms in a Mg vacancy, which demonstrates little effect on the electronic density of states (DoS) of the forsterite crystal. Type 2 corresponds to the replacement of one hydrogen atom into the Mg vacancy with the other one located in different orientations (free proton model). It is this configuration that can significantly change the DoS of the forsterite crystal. The gap energy of the free proton model derived at different orientations is in the range of 0.693-1.007 eV.

Olivine is the most abundant mineral in the upper mantle. In order to understand the upper mantle, physical and chemical properties of olivine need to be constrained. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy(FTIR) studies $^{[1,2]}$ indicate that a significant amount of water can be dissolved into the crystal lattice of olivine in the form of OH. In addition, the electrical conductivity of olivine is sensitive to hydrogen point defects. Therefore, the presence of water remarkably affects the electrical conductivity of minerals. $^{[3,4]}$ However, how the hydrogen point defect affects the electrical properties at the atomic level remains unclear. Computer simulation approach potentially provides a real sight into this issue, which is not easily accessible in the laboratory. Moreover, such studies from first-principles calculations used to determine the hydrogen effect on the electrical conductivity are extremely limited.

Forsterite $(Mg_{2}SiO_{4})$ is the Mg end-member of olivine, which consists of isolated $SiO_{4}$ tetrahedral and $MgO_{6}$ octahedral structures. Hydrogen atoms are incorporated into forsterite by forming either Mg (M1or M2) or Si vacancies. FTIR studies $^{[1]}$ show that the hydrogen atoms mainly occupy the Mg vacancies because the strongest absorption occurs in the Mg site.In addition, Wright $^{[5]}$ indicated that hydrogen bonds to two $O_{2}$ around the vacant Mg1 site.

Our first-principles calculations are performed based on density functional theory and the norm-conserving pseudopotential technique utilizing CASTEP 5.0 codes. $^{[6]}$ Generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was used to describe the exchangecorrelation interaction, which was considered as the best exchange-correlation function to predict hydrogen bonding. $^{[7]}$ The Brillouin zones were sampled with $3 ×3 ×3$ Monkhorst-Pack k-points for forsterite. The cutoff energy for the planewave basis was set to 800 eV.

We also test the increasing number of k-points up to $4 ×4 ×4$ and $5 ×5 ×5$ , respectively; which changes the total energy less than $10^{-5} eV$ per atoms. In addition, the structure optimizations for anhydrous and hydrous forsterite are carried out. The calculations for anhydrous forsterite are performed for different pressures with a variable cell geometry and the pressure increases at the step of 2 GPa. For hydrous forsterite, two types of defect configurations involving the two hydrogen atoms in the different positions are addressed: (1) two hydrogen atoms both are trapped in an M1 vacancy; (2) one hydrogen atom is located in an M1 vacancy, the other hydrogen atom moves along different orientations, i.e. the so-called free proton mechanism. The calculations for hydrous forsterite are only carried out under an ambient condition.

We calculate the lattice parameters $a, b, c$ and cell volume $V$ for anhydrous at various pressures and for hydrous forsterite crystal under the ambient condition. The results are listed in Table 1. The present calculated results are consistent with the previous ones obtained by both experiments and theoretical calculations. $^{[8-12]}$ The discrepancies in cell volume compared with the previous experiments are only about $2 \%$ . Effects of pressures on the electrical structure appear in the range determined to be 0-16 GPa. The band gaps of anhydrous forsterite approximately increase linearly with the increasing pressure from about 3.72 eV at 0 GPa to about 4.24 eV at 16 GPa (Fig. 1), suggesting that anhydrous forsterite shows an insulator behavior at various pressures. The densities of $p$ and $s$ states at 0 GPa and 10 GPa are also shown in Figs. 2(a) and 2(b). It can be seen that the electronic states around the valence band and conduction band remain unchanged due to the increase of pressure. The most stable configuration for the free proton model is that one hydrogen atom is associated with M1 vacancies at (0.5, 0.5, 0.5) and the others are located at (0.75,0.5,0.5), (0.5,0.63,0.5) and (0.5,0.5,0.68) along different orientations, respectively. The band gap en- ergies for hydrous forsterite along [100], [010] and [001] are calculated to be 0.942 eV, 1.007 eV and 0.693 eV, respectively. These results are shown in Fig.4. The differences among gap energies imply that the electri- cal conductivity of olivine is anisotropic, which is sim- ilar to the results from experiments on a single crystal olivine.[¹³] The densities of p states and s states for hydrous forsterite involving one proton located along [010] are shown in Fig.5. It can be seen that the elec- tronic band structure of the free proton model can be changed remarkably. An additional state due to H atoms above the Fermi level is visible (Fig.5(a)), corresponding to the gap energy of ~ 1.0 eV, which indicates that forsterite becomes from an insulator to a semiconductor due to the presence of a free proton. This additional state above the Fermi level is the sum of the s states from H atoms (Fig.5(b)) and the p state from O₂ and O₃ (Fig.5(c)).

In summary, the band gap structures of the anhydrous and hydrous forsterite have been determined by using first-principles calculations. The band gaps of anhydrous forsterite approximately increase linearly with increasing pressure. The calculations predict the preferential stability of structures for the free proton model that one hydrogen atom is associated with M1 vacancies at (0.5,0.5,0.5) and the others are located at (0.75,0.5,0.5), (0.5,0.63,0.5) and (0.5,0.5,0.68) along different orientations, respectively. The presence of additional state due to H atoms remarkably changes the electronic band structure of forsterite, which confirms that hydrogen can enhance the electrical conductivity of forsterite.
