# ELECTRONIC PROPERTIES OF A (1 1 1) GaAs-Al$_{x}$Ga$_{1-x}$As HETEROJUNCTION
A.C. Marsh and J.C. Inkson

Cavendish Laboratory, Madingley Road, Cambridge, CB3 0HE, U.K.

(Received 26 August 1984 by C.W. McCombie)

The electronic properties of a single (1 1 1) GaAs-Al$_{x}$Ga$_{1-x}$As heterojunction are investigated using empirical pseudopotentials for the two constituent materials. It is shown that the effective mass approximation is invalid due to the strong excitation of two evanescent waves on the alloy side of the heterojunction. The implications for quantum wells and superlattices is also discussed.

ANY CALCULATION of the electronic properties of quantum wells or heterojunction superlattices must have as its basis a realistic calculation of the electron states. To date the vast majority of experimental and theoretical work has depended upon the validity of the effective mass approximation. This almost ubiquitous assumption is not a satisfactory situation, especially as the dimensions of these systems are now on the atomic level. However, there has also been a number of calculations which are independent of the above assumption but their relevance to experimental situations is not immediate. The calculation of Pickett and Cohen [1] gives the eigenstates for a particular superlattice parameter over a large energy range ($\sim 20$ eV) whereas the experimental range of interest is of the order of an electron volt on either side of the band gap. Changing the superlattice dimensions would also necessitate a completely new calculation. The problem was overcome to some extent by Osbourn and Smith [2] in their tight binding calculation of the reflection and transmission coefficients for a single heterojunction. Unfortunately, their calculations concentrated on the properties of the electrons in the wide bandgap semiconductor which are not the ones which dominate the electrical properties of quantum well and superlattice systems.

In previous work [3,4] we have used the layer method of semiconductor bandstructure to generate the set of electron states at a given energy and wavevector parallel to a plane. These states (both Bloch and evanescent) have then been used in matching routines to obtain a number of properties. In this communication we report on the application of the method to the transmission and reflection coefficients of an electron incident on a single GaAs-Ga$_{1-x}$Al$_{x}$As heterojunction. The ingredients of the calculation are the empirical pseudopotentials of the constituent materials, the interatomic distance (which we have taken to be equal to the GaAs value) and the interface plane involved. So in essence this is a very flexible programme. In this preliminary work we have used the (1 1 1) face, this will be extended to other faces in later work. Note that although for the GaAs-Ga$_{1-x}$Al$_{x}$As system the (1 0 0) face is normally used, because of the ease of MBE growth, there appears to be no reason why other faces need not be used [5].

Figure 1 shows the valence band discontinuity calculated from the difference in bulk bandstructures for a range of alloy concentrations compared to a number of other experimental [6,7] and theoretical [8,9] results [note that not all refer to the $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 1\end{array})$ face] . The major result of our calculation is given in Figs. 2 and 3, this is the reflection coefficient $(R)$ for an electron in the $\Gamma_{1}$ minimum of the GaAs scattering off the heterojunction. As long as the electron energy is below the higher minima this must be of the form

$$
R=\exp \left[i \phi\left(E, k_{\|}\right)\right], \tag{1}
$$

where $E$ is the energy of the electron above the $\Gamma_{1}$ minimum, $\phi$ is the phase shift of the electron upon reflection and $k_{\|}$is its wavevector parallel to the interface. Figure 2 shows the values for a number of alloy concentrations with $k_{\|}=0$ and also gives the effective mass result for comparison. As can be seen, the effective mass value is quite inadequate. The importance of these curves is that for quantum wells wider than the typical decay length of the evanescent interface states $(\sim 10 \mathring{A})$ they may be used to calculate the quantum well eigenstates by requiring that the total state (incident plus phase shifted reflected wave) satisfy the usual symmetric or antisymmetric condition at the centre of the well. Any difference of these curves from the effective mass values would thus be reflected in a change in the calculated

quantum well energy. The importance of the interface or evanescent states may be judged from Fig. 4 where the excitation amplitude of the two most important evanescent states in the $\mathrm{Al}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}_{1-x} \mathrm{As}$ are shown for two alloy concentrations. The effective mass approximation would only allow for the $\Gamma_{1}$ derived state to exist but, as can be seen, the $L_{1}$ derived state is always significant. This will have important consequences for resonant tunnelling between quantum wells. The evanescent states will affect a number of properties of the quantum well or superlattice state. Since their excitation is significant the scattering from defects at the interface will be enhanced. In addition to this phonon emission and absorption will be altered with large wavevector phonon processes being enhanced. In the present case this would primarily be the phonons at the $L$ point but different faces would bring in other phonons.

In summary then we have presented preliminary results on the scattering of an electron from a GaAs- $\mathrm{Al}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}_{1-x} \mathrm{As}\left(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 1\end{array}\right)$ heterojunction. This yields a large amount of information which can then be used to calculate quantum well and superlattice properties. For other faces the positions of the various minima will obviously change although in essence the same problem will again remain, resulting in a significant evanescent state excitation. This is exactly what was found in the case of GaAs-vacuum scattering.
