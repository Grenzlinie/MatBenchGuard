# Hole mobility in wurtzite InN
N. Ma, X. Q. Wang, $^{a)}$ S. T. Liu, G. Chen, J. H. Pan, L. Feng, F. J. Xu, N. Tang, and
B. Shen $^{b)}$
State Key Laboratory of Artificial Microstructure and Mesoscopic Physics, School of Physics,

(Received 22 March 2011; accepted 28 April 2011; published online 13 May 2011)

Hole mobility in wurtzite InN at low electric fields is studied by an ensemble Monte Carlo calculation. Scatterings of holes by polar optical phonons, nonpolar optical phonons, acoustic phonons, ionized and neutral impurities, and threading dislocations are taken into account. Mobility of holes is $\sim 220$ cm²/V s at 300 K in the InN, where holes are only scattered by the lattice. It decreases to $20-70$ cm²/V s when the present quality of InN with threading dislocation density of $\sim 10^{10}$ cm⁻² and residual donor concentration of over $10^{17}$ cm⁻³ is considered. The calculated mobility coincides well with the recent experimental observation. © 2011 American Institute of Physics. [doi:10.1063/1.3592257]

InN has rapidly emerged as a promising candidate for applications in high-speed electronic and optoelectronic devices. Recent progress in InN epitaxial growth has led to improved quality, low residual electron concentration, and high electron mobility. The demonstration of $p$-type doping also made a great step forward the device applications. $^{1-4}$ However, understanding on hole transport in InN is still very limited since existence of the surface electron accumulation layer makes it impossible to detect hole properties in the buried $p$-type layers by conventional Hall-effect measurement. At this moment, hole mobility in InN can only be estimated through indirect ways by a few groups. Wang $e t$ $a l.^{5}$ reported hole mobilities of $17-36$ cm²/V s for hole concentrations of $1.4-3.0×10^{18}$ cm⁻³ by combination of electrolyte capacitance-voltage (ECV) and conductivity measurements on a series of Mg-doped $p$-type InN layers with different thicknesses. Through ECV and thermopower measurements, hole mobilities of $15-66$ cm²/V s have been estimated very recently. $^{6}$ Analyses of ambipolar carrier diffusion in $n$-type InN by time resolved transient grating technique have shown hole mobilities of 39 and $26$ cm²/V s near the surface of InN and the interface between InN and GaN buffer layers, respectively. $^{7}$ A value of $50$ cm²/V s has been estimated in quantitative mobility spectrum analysis. $^{8}$ Despite above estimations by experimental works, there is no reports on theoretical prediction of hole mobility in InN up to now.

In this letter, hole mobility $\mu_{h}$ in $p$-type InN is studied by ensemble Monte Carlo (EMC) method. Effects of temperature, carrier concentration, residual donor, and threading dislocation on hole transport are studied. It is shown that $\mu_{h}$ reaches to $220$ cm²/V s for a very-high-quality InN in which the transport is determined by phonons only. This value is greatly suppressed to $20-70$ cm²/V s when large numbers of compensating donor and threading dislocation are taken into account.

In the EMC analyses, the following scattering mechanisms were involved; polar optical phonons (LO), nonpolar optical phonons (TO), acoustic phonons [including deformation potential (DA) and piezoelectric (PE) modes], ionized impurities (II), neutral impurities (NI), and threading dislocations. When degenerate conditions were reached, the Pauli exclusion principle was taken into account by means of the rejection technique of Lugli and Ferry. $^{9}$ The scattering of hole by dislocations was modeled as described by Look and Sizelove. $^{10}$ Carrier screening was accounted for following the Brooks-Herring method, with screening length of $L_{D}$ $=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon k_{B}T/e^{2}(n+p)},^{11}$ where $n$ and $p$ denote the concentration of electrons and holes in the $p$-type InN, respectively. $\varepsilon$ is the static dielectric constant, $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the vacuum dielectric constant, and $e$ is the electron charge. $\mu_{h}$ was taken from the slope of the linear region of the velocity versus electric field curve. As the electric fields were very low, a long simulation time of 20 ps and the motion of 50 000 holes were used. We focus on wurtzite InN in this letter since it is the most studied at present. Important parameters used in the analysis, taking into account recently reported data in InN, are listed in Table I. $^{6,12-14}$

First, we study the relevant importance of different lattice scatterings, i.e., the InN considered in the calculation is supposed to be very low doped, light-compensated with $N_{a}$ $=2 \times 10^{17}$ cm$^{-3}$, $N_{d}=5 \times 10^{16}$ cm$^{-3}$ and is free of dislocation. Figure 1 shows the mobilities of holes dominated by different scattering mechanisms in InN. The total hole mobility increases with decreasing temperatures, and reaches 860 cm$^{2}$/V s at 100 K. It can be observed that, at the present doping level, hole transport is dominated by the ionized impurities scattering only at very low temperatures ($<$120 K). After that, the scattering due to acoustic phonons becomes increasingly important with ascending temperatures. $\mu_{h}$ is dominated by LO phonon scattering at temperatures higher than 200 K. The scattering rate due to LO phonon used in the EMC process is expressed by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{\tau}= & \frac{e^{2} m_{h}^{* 1 / 2} \omega_{L}}{4 \sqrt{2} \pi \hbar \varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{p} E^{1 / 2}}\left(N_{q}+\frac{1}{2} \mp \frac{1}{2}\right) \\
& \times\left[\left(1+3 \frac{\left(E+E^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{4 E E^{\prime}}\right) \ln \left|\frac{E^{\prime 1 / 2}+E^{1 / 2}}{E^{\prime 1 / 2}-E^{1 / 2}}\right|-\frac{3\left(E^{\prime}+E\right)}{2\left(E E^{\prime}\right)^{1 / 2}}\right],
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
$$

where $\omega_{L}$ is the angular frequency of the longitudinal phonon, $E$ and $E'$ are the initial and final energy of the hole during the scattering process. $\varepsilon_{p}$ is defined by $\varepsilon_{p}=\varepsilon_{\infty}^{-1}-\varepsilon^{-1}$, where $\varepsilon_{\infty}$ is the high-frequency dielectric constant. By only taking the lattice scattering into account, a hole mobility of 220 cm$^{2}$/V s at 300 K is obtained, which predicts the potential maximum mobility of holes in InN at room temperature.

Next, effect of compensation ratio ($N_{d}$ to $N_{a}$ ratio) on $\mu_{h}$ in $p$-type InN is studied. Nowadays, the growth of very pure InN layers is very difficult yet and the reported lowest electron concentration of as-grown InN is at low $10^{17}$ cm$^{-3}$. It means that the $p$-type InN layers are highly compensated with residual donors. Figure 2 shows the dependence of $t \mu_{h}$ on hole concentrations for a large range of residual donor concentrations at room temperature. Different values of $N_{a}$ are used to produce different hole concentration through Eq. (1). Two features can be observed. First, $\mu_{h}$ is almost independent of the hole concentration before it reaches $1 \times 10^{17}$ cm$^{-3}$ especially when $N_{d}$ is very low. This is because that when the sample is lightly doped, $\mu_{h}$ is dominated by LO phonons. The screening of LO phonons by mobile carriers is also very weak so that $\mu_{h}$ is almost independent of the hole concentration. After that, $\mu_{h}$ decreases very rapidly until degenerate condition is reached. Second, $\mu_{h}$ is greatly influenced by compensation ratio that the higher $N_{d}$, the lower hole mobility. On the other hand, when the sample is heavily doped, the scattering of holes by neutral impurities becomes prominent since the large activation energy (61 meV) of acceptors leads to high neutral impurity concentration in InN layers.

Finally, effect of threading dislocations on hole mobility is investigated. Despite recent advances in growth technique, InN layers are still characterized by a high density of threading dislocations (mainly edge-type component) ranging from $10^{9}-10^{11} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2} .{ }^{17}$ It is believed that when dislocation density is higher than $10^{9} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2}$, the scattering from dislocations has a significant impact on the carrier mobility. $^{18}$ Figure 3 shows $\mu_{h}$ in $p$-type InN as a function of hole concentration with dislocations densities of $10^{9}, 10^{10}$, and $5 \times 10^{10} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2}$ and residual donor concentrations of $5 \times 10^{17}, 1 \times 10^{18}$, and $5 \times 10^{18} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$. It is shown that, scattering due to dislocations is the most important in the sample with the lowest hole concentration, compared with those with higher hole concentrations. For example, at $p=10^{16} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ and $N_{d}=5$ $\times 10^{17} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$, the dislocations with density of $10^{10} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2}$ can cause a decrease in $\mu_{h}$ from $\sim 160$ to $\sim 20 \mathrm{~cm}^{2} / \mathrm{V}$ s. With increasing hole density, dislocation scattering is reduced and becomes negligible in high-hole-density sample because of the carrier screening effect of the dislocations. It is also found in Fig. 3 that hole mobility curves with different $N_{d}$ start to merge with each other at light-doping areas when $N_{d i s} \geq 5 \times 10^{10} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2}$. It means that the scattering of hole by threading dislocations becomes dominate over other mechanisms when the hole concentration is relatively low. Considering the present quality of the $p$-type InN layers, which usually shows $N_{d}>5 \times 10^{17} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ and $N_{d i s}$ around 1 $\times 10^{10} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2}$, the maximum hole mobility is about $70 \mathrm{~cm}^{2} / \mathrm{V}$ s. This value can be improved to $120 \mathrm{~cm}^{2} / \mathrm{V}$ s if the $N_{d i s}$ is reduced to $1 \times 10^{9} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2}$. Therefore, it is very important to reduce the density of dislocations for optimizing $\mu_{h}$. In addition, the recent estimation of $\mu_{h}$ through experiment are shown by open symbols in Fig. $3 .^{5,6}$ It is shown that all these estimated data coincide well with our calculation results.

In conclusion, we have studied hole transport in InN using an EMC method. The hole mobility is as high as $220 \mathrm{~cm}^{2} / \mathrm{V}$ s when the hole transport is dominated by lattice scattering. However, this value is greatly suppressed to less than $70 \mathrm{~cm}^{2} / \mathrm{V}$ s if the present quality of InN is taken into account. Our calculation shows that it is important to reduce the densities of both residual donors and threading dislocations for improving the mobility of holes.
