# High-Field Transport Properties of CdTe
J.G. Ruch
Bell Telephone Laboratories, Murray Hill, New Jersey 07974
(Received 18 October 1971)

A Monte Carlo calculation has been used to calculate the high-field transport properties of CdTe where the full complexity of the band structure has been taken into account. Compari- son with experimental results yields basic transport parameters, e.g., deformation poten- tial for nonequivalent intervalley scattering.

It is well known that CdTe, similarly to GaAs, exhibits a negative differential mobility due to the electric-field- induced transfer of electrons from a high-mobility cen- tral (000) valley to satellite valleys higher in energy.
Recently, a theoretical calculation $^{1}$ of this effect using a Monte Carlo method has been reported, and by fitting the results with experimental data, $^{2}$ a set of basic phys ical parameters has been obtained. However, in the above calculation, the nonparabolicity of the central valley has been neglected.
We report here the study of the transport properties of CdTe using the Monte Carlo method $^{3}$ where the full complexity of the band structure has been incorporated. The nonparabolicity of the (000) minimum has been taken into account using Kane's theory where the spin-orbit splitting has been neglected. $^{4,5}$ We assume that the con duction band shape is determined only by its interaction with the valence band. The satellite valleys are as-sumed to be at the edge of the Brillouin zone in the (111) directions and $0.51 eV^{6}$ higher in energy. Polar and acoustic scattering are considered in the (000) valley. The admixture of p-type valence-band wave functions into the (000) conduction band states is included in cal- culating the scattering rates in this valley and also in calculating intervalley scattering between the (000) and(111) minima. The parameters characterizing intraval- ley scattering in the central valley are the same as the ones used by Rode $^{7}$ in the calculation of the temperature dependence of the low-field mobility. The effective mass and the optic and acoustic deformation potentials in the (111) valleys were assigned values close to those observed in germanium. Polar mode scattering was also included in the (111) valleys, but equivalent inter- valley scattering between the (111) valleys was ne- glected since it is known to be weak in germanium.
Transitions between the (000) and (111) valleys can take place by interaction with either the interaction with either the LO or LA phonons at the < 111> edge of the Brillouin zone. $^{8}$ These phonons may have quite different energies, but since the cou- pling constants are unknown, we have only considered the interaction with the LA phonon and adjusted the cou-pling constant to have the best fit with the experimenta)data of Candi et al. $^{2}$ 
The parameters used in the calculation are given in Ta- ble I. The values in parentheses are those used in Ref.1, where the nonparabolicity of the central valley was neglected.

In particular, the polar optical scattering rate is weaker in our calculation, yielding a low-field mobility of $1100\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V sec}$ which is in good agreement with both experimental and theoretical results. $^{7}$ A large discrepancy is observed in the value adopted for the energy separation $\Delta E$ between central and satellite valleys. Because of the large avalanche breakdown field (many times the threshold field), $\Delta E$ is expected to be significantly smaller than the energy gap. Moreover, high-temperature $^{7}$ studies of the low-field mobility indicate that $\Delta E$ is less than 1 eV. Finally, the deformation potential for nonequivalent intervalley scattering has been found to be $6\times10^8\ \text{eV/cm}$ for the best fit of the experimental data. This value seems quite in line with similar results obtained in other polar semiconductors. $^{9}$ However, the accuracy of this result is hampered by the lack of knowledge about the density-of-states mass in the satellite valleys.
