**Abstract.** Electron transport in InN $n^+nn^+$ diodes is investigated by the Monte Carlo Particle technique at temperatures below about 80 K, when optical phonon emission is the dominating scattering mechanism. It is shown that at constant bias, high enough to force strong electron optical phonon interaction but below the threshold value for intervalley transfer processes, the profiles of the relevant physical quantities exhibit a periodic spatial behaviour in the $n$-region, thus leading to the formation of a superlattice like structure. Such a structure is found to be responsible for microwave power generation in the THz frequency range.

**Keywords:** Monte Carlo simulation, THz generation, nitrides

## Introduction

Semiconductor sources of tunable terahertz (THz) ra- diation are of strategic importance for their application in broadband communication, high-resolution spec- troscopy, radars, etc. In recent years, the considerable progress in the growth of good quality nitrides makes GaN, InN and AlN very promising materials for THz sources based on the optical-phonon transit-time reso- nance [1,2]. Indeed, when compared with other $A^{III}B^{V}$ compounds, the main advantages of nitrides are found to be the higher optical phonon energy and the asso- ciated higher rate for optical phonon emissions. Both features are favourable for the realization of THz gen- eration in bulk materials [1]. Alternatively, the possibil- ity of THz generation is predicted in $n^+nn^+$ submicron diodes where the generation mechanism is based on the excitation of coherent electron plasma oscillations by optical phonon emission under near ballistic dynamics in the $n$-region [2].

In this report we consider long (few $\mu$m) InN $n^+nn^+$ diodes, where electron transport is close to that of bulk semiconductors. The only difference from the bulk case is that in diodes the dependence of the electric field on coordinate is accounted for.

## Method and Model

The calculations of electron transport in 0.02 – 3.0 – 0.02 $\mu$m $n^+nn^+$ InN diode with electron concentra- tions $n=2.2\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ and $n^+=10^{18}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ are performed by the simultaneous solution of cou- pled Boltzmann, Poisson and, when necessary, external resonant circuit equations by the Monte Carlo particle

(MCP) technique [3]. The wurtzite InN band and material parameters are taken from [4]. Because of the small difference between parallel and transverse effective mass a spherically symmetric nonparabolic conduction band is used. Electron scatterings by polar optical phonons, acoustic deformation and piezoelectric phonons, and ionized impurities are accounted for. The piezoelectric constant values are taken from [5]. Due to electron heating up to energies of 50 ~ 100 meV the acoustic scattering is assumed to be elastic. The electron-electron scattering is neglected since it does not change the momentum and energy of the whole electron system and, hence, its influence on spatial velocity and, hence, superlattices is negligible. The microwave power generation is simulated for a diode operating in an external parallel resonant circuit. A standard $R = 50\ \Omega$ load resistor and a $L = 0.005$ pH inductor are connected in parallel with a variable capacitor $C$ and then in series with the diode. The generation frequency is tuned by the capacitor $C$. The cross-sectional area of the diode is taken to be $4000\ \mu\text{m}^2$. The number of simulated particles, depending on the case, varies in the range $2 \div 5\ 10^5$. The time step in all cases is taken of $1\ fs$.

## Steady State Transport

The spatial profiles of the physical quantities of interest calculated by the MCP technique in the $n$-region of the InN diode at 80 K and bias $U = 1.2$ V are reported in Figs. 1 and 2. The results evidence that inside the $n$-region the electron concentration $n(x)$, velocity $v(x)$, energy $\varepsilon(x)$, electric field $E(x)$ and effective optical phonon emission rate $v_o(x)$ exhibit the spatially periodic behaviour with a period $l_0 \approx 0.22\ \mu\text{m}$. This behaviour is a consequence of the space periodic character of optical phonon emission events performed by electrons injected from the cathode contact with energy $\varepsilon \ll \hbar\omega_0$. Indeed, injected low energy electrons are accelerated by the field up to the optical phonon energy $\hbar\omega_0 = 89$ meV and then loose energy and velocity after the first optical phonon emission. Then the process is repeated until electron leave the $n$-region through the anode contact. Thus, electron trajectories consist of flights and stops, where electrons emit an optical phonon. It should be emphasized that due to the elastic character of other scattering mechanisms at $\varepsilon < \hbar\omega_0$, they have no effect on the real space place where electrons emit optical phonons. Therefore, the number of stops, where electrons loose their velocity and energy is the integer number of the ratio $eU/\hbar\omega_0$. In Figs. 1 and 2 there are clearly seen the 13 stop regions characterised by minima of energy $\varepsilon(x)$ and velocity $v(x)$, maxima of concentration $n(x)$ and maxima of effective optical phonon emission rate $v_0(x)$, which is proportional to the number of optical phonon emission events. Therefore, these results confirm the conjecture that a superlattice like structure in the $n$-region arises due to the periodic electron motion associated with a strong optical phonon emission process.

### Small-Signal Analysis

Here we shall show, that the formation of the super- lattice like structure in the $n$-region favours the fulfill- ment of the main conditions necessary for the onset of negative differential conductivity caused by the transit- time resonance [1], namely: (i) low intensity of scatter- ings for carrier energies below $\hbar\omega_0$ (passive region in momentum space), and (ii) weak penetration of elec- trons into the energy region above $\hbar\omega_0$ (active region in momentum space). As follows from Fig. 2, in the space regions of electron stop the local electric field increases and quickly reaches the maximum values, thus providing the maximum acceleration for electrons to leave quickly the low-energy region where impurity and piezoelectric scatterings have the maximum inten- sity. This reduces the effective intensity of scattering in the passive region. By contrast, when electrons ap- proach the optical phonon energy, the local electric field is near the minimum values, thus preventing electrons from deep penetration into the active region. Such an improvement of the transit-time resonance conditions is illustrated by Fig. 5 which presents the frequency behaviour of the real and imaginary parts of the small- signal impedance, ReZ and ImZ, respectively. For the considered diode $Z(f)$ is calculated by simulating with the MCP technique the current response to a periodic voltage $U(t)=U_0+U_1\cos(2\pi ft)$, where $f$ is the fre- quency, $U_0=1.2$ V, and $U_1=0.1$ V. For comparison, Fig. 5 also shows $Z(f)=[en\mu(f)/d+i2\pi fC_d]^{-1}$ obtained for the considered diode of length d and geo- metrical capacitance $C_d$ from the small-signal mobility $\mu(f)$ calculated by MC simulations of the bulk mate- rial [1] subject to an electric field equal to $\bar{E}$ in the $n$-region. In the case of bulk material, ${\rm Re}\mu$ and, hence, the corresponding ReZ are positive everywhere since the conditions for a negative differential conductance are not satisfied, mainly due to the high intensity of elas- tic scatterings in the passive region. ## Microwave Power Generation

The frequency dependence of the microwave power generated by the simulated InN diode connected with a parallel resonant circuit is reported in Fig. 6. The average voltage drop between the diode terminals is taken to be the same of the static case. The microwave generation is simulated for two lattice temperatures of 10 K and 80 K, respectively. As expected, the microwave power is generated in the frequency region, where $\text{Re}Z$ is negative (compare $\text{Re}Z$ of Fig. 5 with curve at 80 K of Fig. 6). Furthermore, Fig. 6 shows that at 10 K the generated microwave power considerably exceeds that generated at 80 K, mostly because the scattering by deformation and piezoelectric acoustic phonons grows and deteriorates the time-periodicity of optical phonon emission with the temperature increase. In addition, the increased scattering by the above mentioned mechanisms leads to the decrease of the electron velocity and the transit-time frequency $f_0$, which is proportional to the velocity. As a consequence the microwave power generation maximum at 80 K shifts at lower frequency as compared with the maximum at 10 K.

The MCP analysis of the space charge profile evolution during the microwave power generation in the resonant circuit does not show the creation of any travelling structure like domains or accumulation layers inside the $n$-region of the InN diode. Therefore, the generation frequency is independent of the length $L$ of the $n$-region as in Gunn or IMPATT diodes. In this case the generation frequency is close to $f_0$, which depends on parameters $\bar{v}$ and $\bar{E}$ inside the $n$-region, as in the case of bulk material.

In conclusion, we have proposed a new microwave generation mechanism in $n^+nn^+$ InN diodes, which can be used for the development of radiation sources in the THz frequency range.
