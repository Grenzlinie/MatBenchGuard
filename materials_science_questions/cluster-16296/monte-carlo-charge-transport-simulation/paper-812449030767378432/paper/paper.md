The low-energy approximation leads to a re-
duction of the field-dependent electron transfer as
compared with the high-energy one. Hence result
larger threshold fields and peak drift-velocities.
The position of the threshold is brought closer to
the experimental values (for a review see ref. 2
or 3) which were found in most cases in excess
of 3 kV/cm. The agreement with respect to the
peak drift-velocity (in experiments mostly
$\gtrsim 1.5 \times 10^{7}$ cm/sec) is not very satisfactory;
this may be due to the overestimate of the number
of hot central valley electrons and, as a conse-
quence, of the intervalley transfer, caused by the
quasi-elastic approximation. However, the cal-
culated population ratio at 2 kV/cm is now, at
least for $D_{12}=5 \times 10^{8}$ eV/cm, no longer too far
from the value of $<2\%$ measured by Zylberstejn
and Gunn [5].

Preliminary incorporation of the central valley
nonparabolicity gives a threshold field of 3.5kV/cm
for the larger $D_{12}$ value (to compare with
2.9 kV/cm [2]) which is now in good agreement
with most experimental data.

# INFLUENCE OF NONPARABOLICITY ON THE GUNN-EFFECT CHARACTERISTIC IN GaAs IN THE DISPLACED MAXWELLIAN APPROXIMATION *
W.HEINLE
AEG-Telefunken Research Institute. 79 Ulm/Donau, Germany

Received 22 July 1968

The calculation of the static drift-velocity versus field characteristic in GaAs by Butcher and Fawcett has been extended to include the nonparabolicity of the central valley. and the result is related to the ex- periment of Ruch and Kino. As compared with the parabolic case. the peak and valley fields are increased from 3.3 to 4.0 kV/cm and from 25 to 39 kV/cm. respectively.

In order that the electron transfer to the sat-
ellite valleys, typical of the Gunn-effect in GaAs,
can take place the electrons in the central valley
must be heated up by the electric field to energies
that are comparable with the energy separation of
both types of valleys. For these energies the con-
duction band structure of the central valley de-
viates noticeably from the parabolic shape indi-
cating an influence on the electron transfer/field
relationship to be expected. This effect was
pointed out by Stenflo [1], and its influence on the
static drift-velocity/field relation, $v(E)$, has been
calculated in refs. 2 and 3 in the quasi-elastic
approximation for the distribution function of the
light mass electrons. A different approach to this
Gunn-effect characteristic makes use of displaced
Maxwellians whose parameters: normalization
constant, electron temperature and drift momen-
tum are determined from the equations of conser-
vation of particle number, energy and momentum
[4-6]. This method has been generalized to in-
clude the nonparabolicity of the central valley,
and some results will be reported in this letter.
