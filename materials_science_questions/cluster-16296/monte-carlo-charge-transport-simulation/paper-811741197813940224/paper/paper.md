# Terahertz emission from InAs induced by photo-Dember effect: Hydrodynamic analysis and Monte Carlo simulations

Antanas Reklaitis $^{a)}$
Semiconductor Physics Institute, Center for Physical Sciences and Technology, A. Goshtauto 11, Vilnius 01108, Lithuania

(Received 3 February 2010; accepted 28 June 2010; published online 2 September 2010)

Terahertz emission from InAs excited by the femtosecond optical pulse is investigated. The hydrodynamic model is developed for the simplified analysis of the terahertz emission induced by the photo-Dember effect. The hydrodynamic model predicts that the amplitude of the transient photocurrent is proportional to the difference between the squared velocities of photoexcited electrons and holes. The predictions of the hydrodynamic model are compared to the results of the rigorous ensemble Monte Carlo simulations. The dependences of the intensity of terahertz emission on the optical fluence, on the photon energy, and on the optical pulse duration are studied. At low intensities of the optical pulse, the emitted terahertz energy is proportional to the power ranging between 3/2 and 2 of the optical pulse intensity. The emitted terahertz energy saturates at high intensities of the optical pulse. The spectral dependence of the terahertz emission from InAs is investigated. It is found that the intensity of terahertz emission profoundly depends on the optical pulse duration. © 2010 American Institute of Physics. [doi:10.1063/1.3467526]

## I. INTRODUCTION

The excitation of semiconductors with femtosecond laser pulses is widely used to generate the broadband pulsed terahertz (THz) radiation. The two main methods $^{1}$ used to generate the broadband THz radiation are based on the THz emission from the biased photoconductive switches and on the THz emission from the surfaces of freestanding semiconductors. The multiple mechanisms are responsible for the THz emission from the freestanding semiconductor surface including nonlinear optical rectification, $^{2,3}$ photocurrent induced by the surface-field, $^{4-9}$ photocurrent induced by the built-in field in $p-i-n$ structures, $^{8,10-12}$ and by photocurrent induced by the photo-Dember effect. $^{13-16}$

Of the various freestanding THz emitters, InAs surface emitters $^{17-25}$ are considered as the most efficient THz emitters. The THz emission from the surface of InAs is complex due to two competing mechanisms leading to the radiation. The first mechanism is based on the photo-Dember effect which originates from the difference in the diffusion rates of photoexcited electrons and holes. The second mechanism of THz emission is the nonlinear optical rectification. The relative magnitudes of THz emission resulting from the two processes are dependent on the surface orientation and on the optical fluence. The THz emission from the (100) oriented InAs surface arises due to the transient photocurrent induced by the photo-Dember effect. $^{14,20,21,25}$ In the (111) oriented InAs, the THz emission induced by the transient photocurrent is superimposed by the nonlinear optical rectification, $^{13,22,23}$ which is significant at high optical fluence above $10 \ \mu J/cm^{2}$ . The experimental results $^{14,19}$ show that the THz emission from $p$-type InAs is more efficient as compared to $n$-InAs.

In the present work, the THz emission from InAs due to the photo-Dember effect is studied. The hydrodynamic model for the transient current induced by the photo-Dember effect is developed. The hydrodynamic model is applied for the qualitative evaluation of the THz emission from InAs. The predictions of the hydrodynamic model are compared to the results of the ensemble Monte Carlo (EMC) simulations of the dynamics of electron-hole plasma excited at the InAs surface. The results of EMC simulations are compared to the reported experimental data on THz emission from InAs. The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, the model of InAs and the details of Monte Carlo approach are presented. The hydrodynamic model of the photo-Dember effect is developed in Sec. III. The results of EMC simulations are presented and discussed in Sec. IV. Finally, the summary is given in Sec. V.

## II. MONTE CARLO TREATMENT OF PHOTO-DEMBER EFFECT IN INAS

The semiclassical motion of extrinsic and photoexcited carriers in the freestanding InAs is simulated by the EMC method. The time- and position-dependent electric field is obtained from the solution of Poisson equation which is self-consistently coupled with the instant space charge distribution of electrons, holes, and ionized impurities. The simulations are performed in the one-dimensional real space and in the three-dimensional momentum space. The one-dimensional real space approach is a good approximation as long as the size of the laser beam is significantly larger than the absorption depth. The details of the employed EMC method as well as the simulation of the photoexcitation are presented in Refs. 11 and 12.

The model of the conduction band structure of InAs consisting of nonparabolic $\Gamma$, $L$, and $X$ valleys is considered in the simulations. For the valence band, the heavy, light, and split-off nonparabolic subbands are taken into account. The

The exclusion principle is also essential at high excitation levels above $10^{17} \, \text{cm}^{-3}$. The degeneracy of holes is disregarded due to a high density of states in the valence band.

The thickness of the simulated freestanding InAs layer is taken to be 2 $\mu$m, which significantly exceeds the absorption depth. It is assumed that the reflection of electrons and holes from the InAs surface is specular. To check the possible effect of the surface roughness on the transient dynamics of photoexcited carriers, the simulations are carried out using the elastic isotropic reflection from the surface. No difference in the results obtained using specular and elastic isotropic reflection has been detected. The boundary conditions for carriers on the opposite side, i.e., at the interface of the simulated InAs layer and the InAs substrate are considered as the Ohmic contact. The injection of equilibrium carriers from the substrate is simulated using Fermi statistics. $^{36}$

The simulations are started from the equilibrium electron-hole ensemble before photoexcitation. The equilibrium potential profile is obtained from the Poisson equation using the Dirichlet boundary conditions. The potential at the surface is determined by the surface Fermi level. The surface Fermi level pinned at 0.2 eV above the conduction band edge $^{37,38}$ is taken in the simulations. The potential boundary condition on the opposite side is found from the Fermi level in the substrate. In the simulations of the temporal evolution of the electron-hole ensemble, the Dirichlet boundary condition for the surface potential is replaced by the Neumann boundary condition, i.e., by the fixed surface electric field which is conserved throughout the transient process.

The temporal shape of the optical pulse is represented by the squared hyperbolic secant. The Gaussian broadening of the photon energy is taken into account. $^{11}$ The simulations are carried out for different values of pulse duration $\tau_{F}$ (full width at half maximum) ranging from 5 to 300 fs. The peak of the optical pulse intensity is set at $t$=0.5 ps. The exponential profile of the density of created electron-hole pairs is considered. The dependence of the absorption coefficient on the photon energy is taken into account. For the range of photon energies $0.5\leq h\nu\leq1.0$ eV, the absorption coefficient of InAs is taken from the experimental data of Ref. 39. For the range $1.2\leq h\nu\leq1.4$ eV, the absorption coefficient is taken from Ref. 40. For the photon energies above 1.5 eV, the absorption coefficient is taken from Ref. 41. For the photon energy $h\nu$=1.55 eV, the absorption coefficient $\alpha$=7.0 $\mu$m$^{-1}$ is estimated from the linear interpolation between the values for 1.50 and 1.60 eV given in Ref. 41. The values of the InAs absorption coefficient are summarized in Table III.

During the illumination, electrons are excited from the heavy-hole, light-hole, and split-off-hole subbands. The dependences of relative intensities of excitation from each subband on the photon energy are considered. The intensities of excitation from each subband are evaluated from the joint densities of states of the $\Gamma$ valley and the corresponding valence subband. For the photon energy of 1.55 eV, the relative excitation intensities from heavy-hole, light-hole, and split-off-hole subbands are 49:21:30, respectively. The total kinetic energy of the created electron-hole pair is found from the difference between the photon energy and the band gap energy of the corresponding subband. The total kinetic energy is partitioned between electron and hole in accordance with the momentum conservation and taking into account the nonparabolicity of the conduction and valence bands. The initial momentum distributions of the photoexcited electrons and holes are assumed to be isotropic.

The energy of THz emission is obtained from the simulated transient current. The time-dependent power $P(t)$ of the dipole radiation in all directions is given by $^{42}$
$$
P(t)=\frac{1}{6\pi\kappa c^{3}}\left(\frac{dJ(t)}{dt}\right)^{2}, \tag{1}
$$
where $\kappa$ is the dielectric permittivity, $c$ is the speed of light, and $J(t)$ is the conductivity current density integrated over the thickness of the simulated structure. $^{11,12}$ In the EMC simulations, the emitted THz energy is calculated by the numerical integration of Eq. (1) over the duration of the transient process. The spectrum of THz emission is found from Fourier analysis of $P(t)$.

### III. HYDRODINAMIC MODEL OF PHOTO-DEMBER EFFECT

The hydrodynamic model of the photo-Dember effect is based on the following simplifying assumptions. The downward band bending (see Fig. 2) is neglected, and it is as-

![](./images/811741197813940224_4.jpg)

FIG. 2. Equilibrium potential profile (conduction and valence band edges) in InAs before photoexcitation. The solid, dashed, and dotted curves present the potential profiles in $i$-InAs, $n$-InAs ($N_{d}$=10$^{17}$ cm$^{-3}$), and $p$-InAs ($N_{a}$=10$^{17}$ cm$^{-3}$), respectively.

sumed that the potential is constant throughout the structure prior to photoexcitation. This assumption is justified when the kinetic energy of the excited carriers exceeds the energy of the electron confinement due to downward band bending. It is assumed that the carriers are generated instantly, i.e., the intensity of the time-dependent optical excitation fluence $I(t)$ can be approximated as $I(t)=I_{0} \delta(t)$. This assumption is valid when the condition $\tau_{F} f \ll 1$ is satisfied, where $\tau_{F}$ is the full width at half maximum of the optical pulse intensity, and $f$ is the frequency of oscillations of the excited carriers. In the model, the three types of carriers are considered: equilibrium electrons (for $n$-type semiconductor) or equilibrium holes (for $p$-type semiconductor), photoexcited electrons, and photoexcited holes. The equilibrium carriers and photoexcited holes are considered in the parabolic energy bands due to low energy of these types of carriers. The upper valleys of the conduction band as well as the light and split-off sub-bands of the valence band are disregarded. The nonparabolic energy-band dispersion relation is considered for photoexcited electrons. This relation is given by

$$
\frac{\hbar^{2} \mathbf{k}^{2}}{2 m_{e}}=\varepsilon\left(1+\alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon\right), \quad (2)
$$

where $\mathbf{k}$ is the electron wave vector, $m_{e}$ is the low-energy electron effective mass in $\Gamma$ valley, $\varepsilon$ is the electron energy, and $\alpha_{\Gamma}$ is the nonparabolicity coefficient of the $\Gamma$ valley.

In normal to the surface direction $z$, the momentum distribution function $f_{i}(\mathbf{k}, z, t)$ of the $i$-type carriers obeys the semiclassical Boltzmann transport equation,

$$
\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}+v_{z}(\mathbf{k}) \frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z}+\frac{e_{i} E}{\hbar} \frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial k_{z}}+\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}\right)_{c}=0, \quad (3)
$$

where $v_{z}$ and $k_{z}$ are the velocity and the wave vector component in the direction $z$, respectively, $E$ is the electric field strength, and $e_{i}$ is the carrier charge. The charge $e_{i}=e$ and $e_{i}=-e$ is taken for electron and hole, respectively. The carrier distribution function $f_{i}(\mathbf{k}, z, t)$ is normalized to the local carrier density $n_{i}(z, t)$ as

$$
n_{i}(z, t)=\int f_{i}(\mathbf{k}, z, t) d \mathbf{k}. \quad (4)
$$

In the hydrodynamic model, the collision term of Eq. (3) is approximated by the momentum relaxation rate. After multiplication of Eq. (3) by $e_{i} v_{z}(\mathbf{k})$ and integration over $\mathbf{k}$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\partial j_{i}(z, t)}{\partial t}+e_{i} \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \int v_{z}^{2} f_{i}(\mathbf{k}, z, t) d \mathbf{k}-\frac{e_{i}^{2} E(z, t) n_{i}(z, t)}{\tilde{m}_{i}}+\gamma j_{i} \\
& \quad=0, \quad (5)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $j_{i}$ is the conductivity current of $i$-type carriers, $\gamma$ is the momentum relaxation rate, and

$$
\tilde{m}_{i}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\hbar^{2} n_{i}(z, t)} \int \frac{\partial^{2} \varepsilon_{i}}{\partial k_{z}^{2}} f_{i}(\mathbf{k}, z, t) d \mathbf{k}, \quad (6)
$$

is the inverse effective mass of the carrier which depends on the distribution function in the case of nonparabolic band. $^{43}$ Equals momentum relaxation rates for electrons and holes are taken in Eq. (5) for simplicity. The carrier densities can be expressed as $n_{i}(z, t)=n_{i o}(z)+n_{i 1}(z, t)$, where $n_{i o}(z)$ is the density of $i$-type carriers immediately after photoexcitation, and $n_{i 1}(z, t)$ is the variation in the carrier density during the transient process. Let us further assume that $n_{i 1}(z, t) \ll n_{i 0}(z)$ throughout the transient process. This assumption is justified if the conditions $\alpha v_{i} \ll f$ are satisfied, where $\alpha$ is the absorption coefficient, and $v_{i}$ is the $i$-type carrier velocity. These conditions are fulfilled for the most semiconductors in the frequency range above 1 THz. Under this assumption, Eq. (5) can be rewritten as

$$
\frac{\partial j_{i}(z, t)}{\partial t}+e_{i} v_{i i}^{2} \frac{\partial n_{i o}(z)}{\partial z}-\frac{e_{i}^{2} E(z, t) n_{i o}(z)}{\tilde{m}_{i o}}+\gamma j_{i}(z, t)=0, \quad (7)
$$

where $v_{i i}^{2}$ and $\tilde{m}_{i o}$ are, respectively, the average squared velocity and the effective mass of the $i$-type carriers immediately after photoexcitation.

The net current of the conductivity and displacement currents is equal to zero in the freestanding structure. Therefore, the spatio-temporal variations in the electric field $E(z, t)$ and the sum of conductivity currents $j_{i}(z, t)$ obey the equation

$$
\kappa \kappa_{0} \frac{d E(z, t)}{d t}+\sum_{i} j_{i}(z, t)=0, \quad (8)
$$

where $\kappa_{0}$ is the static dielectric constant, and the summation is taken over contribution of all three types of carriers. After the differentiation of Eq. (7) by $t$, the summation over $i$, and the substitution of Eq. (8), we obtain the equation for the damped harmonic oscillator,

$$
\frac{d^{2} j}{d t^{2}}+\gamma \frac{d j}{d t}+\frac{j}{\kappa \kappa_{0}} \sum_{i} \frac{e_{i}^{2} n_{i 0}(z)}{\tilde{m}_{i 0}}=0, \quad (9)
$$

where $j$ is the total conductivity current. The initial conditions for Eq. (9) are $j(z, 0)=0$ and

$$
\left.\frac{d j}{d t}\right|_{t=0}=-\sum_{i} e_{i} v_{i i}^{2} \frac{\partial n_{i 0}}{\partial z}. \quad (10)
$$

The densities of photoexcited electrons and holes immediately after optical pulse can be expressed as $n_{0}(z)=p_{0}(z)$ $=n_{\text {exc }} \exp (-\alpha z)$, where $n_{\text {exc }}$ is the density of the excited electron-hole pairs at the surface. As the downward band bending is neglected, the density of equilibrium carriers $n_{e q}$ is uniform. Therefore, Eq. (10) can be rewritten as

$$
\left.\frac{d j}{d t}\right|_{t=0}=e v_{t}^{2} \alpha n_{e x c} \exp (-\alpha z), \quad (11)
$$

where

$$
v_{t}^{2}=v_{t e}^{2}-v_{t h}^{2}, \quad (12)
$$

is the difference between the average squared velocities of the excited electrons, $v_{t e}^{2}$, and holes, $v_{t h}^{2}$, immediately after photoexcitation. The transient current density $j(z, t)$ is obtained from the solution of Eq. (9) as

$$j(z, t)=\frac{e \alpha v_{t}^{2} n_{e x c} \exp (-\alpha z)}{\omega(z)} e^{-\gamma t / 2} \sin [\omega(z) t],\qquad(13)$$

where the position-dependent frequency $\omega(z)$ is given by
$$\omega^{2}(z)=\frac{e^{2}}{\kappa \kappa_{0}}\left[n_{e x c} e^{-\alpha z}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{m}_{e 0}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{m}_{h 0}}\right)+\frac{n_{e q}}{\tilde{m}_{e q}}\right]-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}.\qquad(14)$$

Here, $\tilde{m}_{e 0}, \tilde{m}_{h 0}$, and $\tilde{m}_{e q}$ are the effective masses of photoexcited electron, photoexcited hole, and equilibrium carrier, respectively, which are the defined by Eq. (6). As the nonparabolicities of holes and equilibrium carriers are disregarded, the effective masses $\tilde{m}_{h 0}$ and $\tilde{m}_{e q}$ are replaced in Eq. (14) by the heavy hole effective mass $m_{h}$ and the low-energy effective mass of equilibrium carrier $m_{e q}$ (electron or heavy hole), respectively. The integrated transient current density $J(t)$ (i.e., the first derivative of the dipole moment) is obtained by integrating Eq. (13) over $z$ from 0 to $\infty$. The integrated current density is given by
$$J(t)=\frac{4 \kappa \kappa_{0} m^{*} v_{t}^{2}}{e} \frac{e^{-\gamma t / 2}}{t} \sin \left(\frac{\omega_{\max }+\omega_{\min }}{2} t\right) \sin \left(\frac{\omega_{\max }-\omega_{\min }}{2} t\right),\qquad(15)$$
where $\omega_{\max }=(\omega_{e x c}^{2}+\omega_{e q}^{2}-\gamma^{2} / 4)^{1 / 2}, \omega_{ min }=(\omega_{e q}^{2}-\gamma^{2} / 4)^{1 / 2}, m^{*}$ $=(\tilde{m}_{e 0}^{-1}+m_{h 0}^{-1})^{-1}$ is the reduced effective mass of the photoexcited carriers,
$$\omega_{e x c}=\left(e^{2} n_{e x c} / \kappa \kappa_{0} m^{*}\right)^{1 / 2}\qquad(16)$$
is the plasma frequency of the photoexcited carriers at the surface, and
$$\omega_{e q}=\left(e^{2} n_{e q} / \kappa \kappa_{0} m_{e q}\right)^{1 / 2}\qquad(17)$$
is the plasma frequency of the equilibrium carriers.

The momentum relaxation rate $\gamma$ of photoelectrons in InAs is found from EMC simulations using a method described in Ref. 44. Using this method, the initial distribution function of photoelectrons in bulk InAs is instantly shifted in momentum space by the $\delta(t)$-shaped pulse of the electric field. The relaxation rate $\gamma$ is evaluated from the resulting transient response of the average electron velocity. It is found that $\gamma$ is close to $1.7 \times 10^{12} ~s^{-1}$ over the range of photon energies between 0.5 and 1.2 eV. In the range of photon energies above 1.2 eV, the momentum relaxation rate linearly increases and reaches $3.3 \times 10^{12} ~s^{-1}$ at $h \nu=1.55 eV$ due to raised intensity of electron transfer into $L$ valleys.

The Eq. (15) is valid when the condition $\omega_{e q}^{2} \geq \gamma^{2} / 4$ is satisfied. This condition results from the requirement $\omega^{2}(z)$  $\geq 0$ in Eq. (14) when $z$ tends to infinity. The condition $\omega_{e q}^{2}$ ≥ γ²/4 does not introduce any limitations on the validity of Eq. (15) for InAs. For photon energy below 1.2 eV, the condition $\omega_{e q}^{2} \geq \gamma^{2} / 4$ is satisfied for $n$ - and $p$-InAs when $n_{e q}$  $\geq 8 \times 10^{13} ~cm^{-3}$ and $n_{e q} \geq 2 \times 10^{15} ~cm^{-3}$ , respectively. We note, that the intrinsic carrier density in $i$-InAs is 1.1 × 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ at room temperature.

Due to low energy of photoexcited holes, the major contribution into $v_{t}^{2}$ is made by photoelectrons. Taking the initial distribution function of photoelectrons in momentum space $k$ proportional to $\delta(k^{2}-k_{e}^{2})$ , where $k_{e}$ is the wave vector of the photoexcited electron, we obtain
$$v_{t e}^{2}=\frac{2 \varepsilon_{e}}{3 m_{e}} \frac{1+\alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon_{e}}{1+4 \alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon_{e}\left(1+\alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon_{e}\right)},\qquad(18)$$
where $\varepsilon_{e}$ is the energy of photoexcited electron which is defined by the energy and momentum conservation in the photon absorption process as
$$\varepsilon_{e}=\frac{2\left(h \nu-\varepsilon_{g}\right) m_{h}}{m_{e}+m_{h}+\left[\left(m_{e}+m_{h}\right)^{2}+4 \alpha_{\Gamma}\left(h \nu-\varepsilon_{g}\right) m_{e} m_{h}\right]^{1 / 2}}. \quad(19)$$

Here, $\varepsilon_{g}$ is the band gap energy. Another important quantity in Eq. (15) is the photoelectron effective mass $\tilde{m}_{e 0}$ which differs significantly from the low-energy electron effective mass $m_{e}$ due to strong nonparabolicity of the $\Gamma$ valley. Afterthe substitution of the initial distribution function $\delta(k^{2}-k_{e}^{2})$  into Eq. (6), we obtain
$$\tilde{m}_{e 0}=m_{e} \frac{3\left[1+4 \alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon_{e}\left(1+\alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon_{e}\right)\right]^{3 / 2}}{3+8 \alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon_{e}\left(1+\alpha_{\Gamma} \varepsilon_{e}\right)}.\qquad(20)$$

In the limits of low and high excitation densities, Eq.(15) can be simplified. In the limit of low excitation densityn_ exc≪n_ eq, the condition ω_exc≪ω_eq is satisfied, and Eq. (15) is reduced to
$$J(t)=\frac{e n_{e x c} v_{t}^{2}}{\left(\omega_{e q}^{2}-\gamma^{2} / 4\right)^{1 / 2}} e^{-\gamma t / 2} \sin \left[\left(\omega_{e q}^{2}-\gamma^{2} / 4\right)^{1 / 2} t\right].\qquad(21)$$

In this limit, the frequency of plasma oscillations is position- independent, and it is controlled by the density $n_{e q}$ of the uniformly distributed equilibrium carriers. The substitution of Eq. (21) into Eq. (1) and the integration over $t$ from $t=0$ to t=∞ gives the THz energy emitted in all directions as
$$W_{\mathrm{THz}}=\frac{e^{2}}{12 \pi \kappa c^{3} \gamma} n_{e x c}^{2} v_{t}^{4}.\qquad(22)$$

In the opposite limit of high excitation density $n_{e x c} \gg n_{e q}$ , the condition $\omega_{e x c} \gg \omega_{e q}$ is satisfied, and Eq. (15) reduces to
$$J(t)=\frac{4 e n_{e x c} v_{t}^{2}}{\omega_{e x c}^{2}} \frac{e^{-\gamma t / 2}}{t} \sin ^{2}\left[\left(\omega_{e x c}^{2}-\gamma^{2} / 4\right)^{1 / 2} t\right].\qquad(23)$$

According to Eq. (23), the oscillations of the transient cur- rent are damped as $t^{-1}$ even under purely ballistic transport regime when $\gamma=0$ . In this case, the transient current is damped by the interference of different frequency plasma oscillations which are excited at different distances from the surface. The frequency of plasma oscillations is position- dependent due to exponentially decaying density of photoexcited carriers. The THz energy obtained from Eqs. (1) and (23) is given by

## IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The investigation of the main futures of THz emission induced by the photo-Dember effect is carried out by comparing the results given by the hydrodynamic model to the corresponding EMC simulations. The transient current obtained from EMC simulations and the transient current calculated from Eq. (15) are shown in Fig. 3(a). The comparison of the presented results shows the satisfactory agreement. We note that the agreement is improved when the excitation from the light-hole and split-off subbands is disregarded in EMC simulations. In such case, the hole current is reduced, and the resulting net electron and hole current is enhanced. The presented in Fig. 3(a) results of EMC simulations confirm the assumption made in Sec. III that the transient current in InAs is not sensitive to the position of the surface Fermi level. The obtained transient current is little affected when the surface Fermi level is reduced from 0.2 eV above the conduction band edge to the Fermi energy in the bulk of $i$-InAs (0.116 eV below the conduction band edge). The weak influence of the Fermi level position on the transient current can be explained as follows. At low photon energies, the absorption depth significantly exceeds the screening length of the surface field. Therefore, the majority of photocarriers are created in the region where the surface field is screened. At high photon energies, the kinetic energy of photocarriers exceeds the downward band bending. In this limit, the dynamics of photocarriers is determined mostly by their initial velocities.

The contribution of electron and hole currents to the resulting transient photocurrent is shown in Fig. 3(b). The major contribution to the transient current is made by electrons. After the fast transient process during a period $t\leq 1.5$ ps, the quasistationary diffusion of electrons and holes toward the bulk of semiconductor is established. The electron diffusion current is reduced by the self-consistent electric field, while the hole current is enhanced by this field. Therefore, the resulting electron and hole current densities are of the same order and opposite in the direction.

The experimental investigations $^{14,19}$ of the THz emission from the surface of $n$- and $p$-InAs show that the emission from $p$-InAs is essentially more intense. The results of EMC simulations agree with the experimental data. In Fig. 4, the comparison of the calculated transient current in $n$-type and $p$-type InAs is presented. At low excitation density $n_{exc}<n_{eq}$, the amplitude of transient current in $p$-InAs essentially exceeds the amplitude in $n$-InAs [see Fig. 4(a)]. The results of EMC simulations confirm the mechanism suggested in Ref. 14 to explain the superiority of the $p$-type InAs over the $n$-type InAs. The simulations show that in $n$-type InAs, the transient current of photoelectrons is reduced by the equilibrium electrons. The equilibrium electrons are accelerated by the self-consistent electric field in the direction opposite to the diffusion of the photoexcited electrons, i.e., toward the surface. In other words, the transient current is screened by the equilibrium electrons. In $p$-type InAs, the screening effect of equilibrium holes is much less pronounced due to low hole mobility. ### V. CONCLUSION
The THz emission from InAs induced by photo-Dember effect is investigated analytically and by EMC simulations. The hydrodynamic model for the analysis of transient dy- namics of electron-hole plasma is developed. The transient photocurrent obtained from the hydrodynamic model is in good agreement with the results of EMC simulations in the range of photon energy where the electron transfer into upper valleys can be disregarded. The downward band bending at the surface of InAs has a minor effect on the transient pho- tocurrent. It is obtained that the energy of THz emission from $p$-InAs exceeds the energy of emission form $n$-InAs. The hydrodynamic model and EMC simulations show that in the limit of a very short optical pulse of 10 fs THz energy is proportional to the three-half's power of the optical fluence. For the optical pulse duration of 100 fs, the emitted THz energy saturates at high optical fluence. The results of EMC simulations show that the intervalley electron transfer re- duces the emitted THz energy. The peak on the spectrum of the emitted energy is obtained if the intervalley coupling between $\Gamma$ and $L$ valleys is sufficiently strong.
