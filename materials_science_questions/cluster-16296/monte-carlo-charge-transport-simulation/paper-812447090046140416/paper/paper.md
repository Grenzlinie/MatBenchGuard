# ANISOTROPIC AND POLARIZED INTRABAND LIGHT EMISSION OF HOT ELECTRONS

O. M. BULASHENKO, A. G. KOLLYUKH and V. A. MOROZHENKO

Institute of Semiconductor Physics, Ukrainian Academy of Sciences, Pr. Nauki, 45,
Kiev-28, 252650, Ukraine

( Received 9 February 1994)

Abstract-Far infrared luminescence of hot electrons in InSb have been studied. The emission probabilities derived in the second order perturbation are obtained for different scattering mechanisms: acoustic phonons, optical phonons and impurities. The origin of anisotropy and polarization of the light emission due to anisotropy of the electron distribution function under the electric field is described theoretically and calculated using the Monte Carlo method. The theoretical conclusions are confirmed by the experiment.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, the hot carriers in semiconductors have attracted considerable interest because of its capability to generate and amplify radiation in the spectroscopic range covering far infrared to submillimeter wave frequencies. In such a consideration, special attention has generally been paid to the holes in Ge and related to the possibility of receiving the population inversion and laser action in crossed electric and magnetic fields. $^{(1)}$ In Ref. (2) the polarization of intersubband light emission of p-Ge has been additionally obtained, and this phenomenon has been studied theoretically in Ref. (3). In the high electric field the polarization of light emission in far infrared has also been studied in Ref. (4) and it was connected with the intraband hold transitions and explained by the anisotropy of the distribution function of current carriers. No attention has been paid, however, to the quantitative description of this phenomenon.

In this paper the origin of the anisotropy and polarization of the intraband light emission of hot electrons due to the electric field action is firstly described theoretically and then confirmed experimentally.

The investigation was carried out in a far infrared range $(\lambda \cong 100 \mu m)$ that corresponds to the electron transitions in the passive region $\epsilon \leqslant \hbar \omega_{0}$, where $\omega_{0}$ is the optical phonon energy. It is noted, that most electrons responsible for the electrical and optical properties of the semiconductor materials are in the passive region. Hence, a study of emission from the passive region gives information about the main part of the electron distribution function and the processes of energy and momentum relaxation.

## 2. LIGHT EMISSION IN THE SECOND ORDER PERTURBATION

The intraband transition probability of the electron of wave-vector $\mathbf{k}$ to any state with the emission of a photon of energy $\hbar \omega$ with integration over vector $\mathbf{q}$ can be expressed by

$$
W_{ \pm}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{e^{2}}{2 \pi n^{2} \omega^{3}\left(m^{*}\right)^{2}} \int\left|\left\langle\mathbf{k} \pm \mathbf{q}\left|H_{\mathrm{s}}\right| \mathbf{k}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left(\mathbf{e}_{\omega} \cdot \mathbf{q}\right)^{2} \cdot \delta\left(E_{\mathrm{f}}-E_{\mathrm{i}}\right) \mathrm{d} \mathbf{q}. \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{i}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{f}}$ are the energies for the total system in the initial and final states, $H_{\mathrm{s}}$ is the Hamiltonian of scattering, $e$ is the electron charge, $m^{*}$ is the effective mass, $n$ is the refractive index.

$\mathbf{e}_\omega$ is the vector of photon polarization. The subscript "+" refers to the phonon emission. In the case of the impurity scattering these subscripts may be omitted. We only consider the above mentioned scattering mechanisms within the simple parabolic band model. The spontaneous emission of a photon is only assumed since the photon density is small and the stimulated emission may be neglected.

It should be noted that emission probability (1) depends on the orientation of the polarization vector $\mathbf{e}_\omega$.

The exact inclusion of this parameter allows one to determine the characteristics of the radiation investigated. Using the energy and momentum conservation and integrating over all possible $\mathbf{q}$ vectors one can obtain the emission probabilities as the functions of the electron energy $\epsilon$, the photon energy $\Omega=\hbar\omega$, and the parameter $\beta_0$, characterizing the orientation of $\mathbf{e}_\omega$, for different scattering matrix element $\langle\mathbf{k}\pm\mathbf{q}|H_{\mathrm{s}}|\mathbf{k}\rangle$.

### Acoustic phonon
$$
W_{1}(\epsilon, \beta_{0}, \Omega)=C_{\mathrm{A}} \Omega^{-3}(\epsilon-\Omega)^{1 / 2}\left[\left(2+\beta_{0}\right) \epsilon-\Omega\right]. \tag{2}
$$

### Ionized impurity
$$
W_{2}(\epsilon, \beta_{0}, \Omega)=C_{1} \Omega^{-3} \epsilon^{-1 / 2}\left[\beta_{0}\left(1-\frac{\Omega}{\epsilon}\right)^{1 / 2}+\frac{2}{3}\left(2-\beta_{0}+\frac{3}{2} \beta_{0} \frac{\Omega}{\epsilon}\right) \operatorname{arc} \tanh \left(1-\frac{\Omega}{\epsilon}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]. \tag{3}
$$

### Optical phonon absorption
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{3}(\epsilon, \beta_{0}, \Omega)=C_{0} \Omega^{-3} n_{0}\left(\omega_{0}\right) \epsilon^{1 / 2}\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(4+\beta_{0}-\frac{3}{2} \beta_{0} \frac{\Omega_{-}}{\epsilon}\right)\right. & \cdot\left(1+\frac{\Omega_{-}}{\epsilon}\right)^{1 / 2} \\
& \left.+\frac{1}{2} \beta_{0}\left(\frac{\Omega_{-}}{\epsilon}\right)^{2} \operatorname{arc} \tanh \left(1+\frac{\Omega}{\epsilon}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Optical phonon emission
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{4}(\epsilon, \beta_{0}, \Omega)=C_{0} \Omega^{-3}\left(n_{0}\left(\omega_{0}\right)+1\right) \epsilon^{1 / 2}\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(4+\beta_{0}+\frac{3}{2} \beta_{0} \frac{\Omega_{+}}{\epsilon}\right)\right. & \cdot\left(1-\frac{\Omega_{+}}{\epsilon}\right)^{1 / 2} \\
& \left.+\frac{1}{2} \beta_{0}\left(\frac{\Omega_{+}}{\epsilon}\right)^{2} \operatorname{arc} \tanh \left(1-\frac{\Omega_{+}}{\epsilon}\right)^{1 / 2}\right]. \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $\beta_{0}=3 \cos ^{2} \beta-1$, $\beta$ is the angle between the electron wave-vector $\mathbf{k}$ and the photon polarization $\mathbf{e}_\omega$, $\Omega_{\pm}=\omega_{0} \pm \Omega$, $n_{0}(\omega_{0})$ is the optical phonon occupation number,
$$
C_{A}=\frac{4 e^{2} \Xi^{2}\left(k_{B} T\right)\left(2 m^{*}\right)^{1 / 2}}{3 V n^{2} \rho v_{s}^{2} \hbar^{2}}, \quad C_{I}=\frac{e^{6} N_{i} \hbar^{2}}{V n^{2} \epsilon_{0}^{2}\left(2 m^{*}\right)^{3 / 2}}, \quad C_{0}=\frac{e^{4} \hbar \omega_{0}}{2 V n^{2} \epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}\left(2 m^{*}\right)^{1 / 2}}.
$$

$T$ is the lattice temperature, $\Xi$ is the acoustic deformation potential, $\rho$ is the crystal density, $v_s$ is the velocity of sound, $V$ is the crystal volume, $N_i$ is the impurity concentration, $1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}=1/\epsilon_{0}-1/\epsilon_{\infty}$, $\epsilon_{0}$ and $\epsilon_{\infty}$ are the static and high-frequency dielectric constants. It is assumed that the acoustic phonon scattering is elastic, and the lattice vibrations are in thermal equilibrium. The case of $\hbar\omega<\hbar\omega_{0}$ is considered. The opposite case for consideration of the problem does not change the principal situation, but can result in an essential quantitative change. The requirement that the

square rooted expressions must be positive is in accordance with the condition $\epsilon > \epsilon_{\text{th}}$, where $\epsilon_{\text{th}}$ is the threshold energy of the electron to emit a photon. $\epsilon_{\text{th}} = \Omega$ for acoustic phonon and impurity scattering, and $\epsilon_{\text{th}} = \Omega_{+}$ for optical phonon emission.

The formulae obtained contains the angular dependence of the photon polarization, being characterized by the parameter $\beta_{0}$. To get the total probability of light emission in the given direction one has to take into account the contribution of all electrons of all energies and $\mathbf{k}$-directions, and to consider all possible vectors of polarization $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$. In the case of isotropic electron distribution in $\mathbf{k}$-space (e.g. at the thermal equilibrium), after considering all possible directions of $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$, the light emission will be also isotropic and non-polarized. In which case after averaging the angle of $\beta$, we can get in equations (2)-(5) $\langle\cos^{2}\beta\rangle=1/3$ or $\beta_{0}=0$ and the expressions are simplified significantly.

Well known in polar semiconductors due to the anisotropic nature of optical phonon scattering, for which small angle scattering is dominant, the electric field has a tendency to focus the electron distribution in $\mathbf{k}$-space along the direction of the field (streaming). $^{(1)}$ In such a case the intraband light emission becomes anisotropic. In the simple isotropic band model the distribution function has an axial symmetry in $k$-space with the axis along the direction of the field, and it can be described in terms of the electron energy $\epsilon$ and the angle $\theta$ between the axis and the electron wave vector $\mathbf{k}$.

Consider the light emission in the longitudinal direction with respect to the direction of the electric field $E$, which is caused by all electrons having the energy $\epsilon$ and the angle $\theta$. Averaging all possible directions of $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$ in the plane perpendicular to the direction considered we get $\langle\cos^{2}\beta\rangle=1/2\sin^{2}\theta$. It is understood, that the contributions of each $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$ are equal, and the emission in this direction will be non-polarized.

Consider now the light emission transverse to the electric field. In this case the situation essentially changes. Using the same procedure of averaging over $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$ we have $\langle\cos^{2}\beta\rangle=\frac{1}{4}(1+\cos^{2}\theta)$. The contributions of photons with different polarization $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$ are, however, not equal in this case. For $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}\parallel\mathbf{E}$ the emission probability is greater than that for $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}\perp\mathbf{E}$. This results in the linear polarization of the light emission. This phenomenon is shown in Fig. 1, where the light intensities in the transverse and longitudinal directions are demonstrated.

The circle in the $y-z$ plane illustrates that the intensities of light with $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$ laying in this plane are equal for all polarizations. The ellipsoid in the $x-z$ plane illustrates that the light intensity with $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}\parallel\mathbf{x}$ is greater than that with $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}\perp\mathbf{z}$. Hence, in this direction we have the polarized emission, with the polarization axis being parallel to the field direction $\mathbf{E}$. Averaging over the electrons with the

same $\epsilon$ and $\theta$ we have for transverse emission and for $\mathbf{e}_{\omega} \| \mathbf{E}:\langle\cos ^{2} \beta\rangle=\cos ^{2} \theta$ and for $\mathbf{e}_{\omega} \perp \mathbf{E}:\langle\cos ^{2} \beta\rangle=1 / 2 \sin ^{2} \theta$.

Thus, considering the fixed directions and making preliminary averages due to symmetry of the task one can get the emission probabilities $W(\epsilon, \theta, \Omega)$. Introducing the symbol $\theta_{0}=3 \cos ^{2} \theta-1$, these probabilities can be determined from equations (2)-(5) substituting for $\beta_{0}$ the expressions:
(a) $-\frac{1}{2} \theta_{0}$ (longitudinal emission, $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$ averaged),
(b) $\frac{1}{4} \theta_{0}$ (transverse emission, $\mathbf{e}_{\omega}$ averaged),
(c) $\theta_{0}$ (transverse emission, $\mathbf{e}_{\omega} \| \mathbf{E}$),
(d) $-\frac{1}{2} \theta_{0}$ (transverse emission, $\mathbf{e}_{\omega} \perp \mathbf{E}$).

The total transition rate for a given photon energy $\Omega$ can be determined for different scattering mechanisms $s$ by integrating the emission probability $W(\epsilon, \theta, \Omega)$ over the electron distribution function $F(\epsilon, \theta)$:

$$
R_{\mathrm{s}}(\Omega)=\int_{0}^{\pi} \sin \theta \mathrm{d} \theta \int_{\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}^{\mathrm{s})}}^{\infty} \mathrm{d} \epsilon W_{\mathrm{s}}(\epsilon, \theta, \Omega) F(\epsilon, \theta) g(\epsilon),
\tag{6}
$$

where $g(\epsilon)$ is the density of electron states.

We can get, finally, the number of photons emitted per unit time in given spectral range between $\omega_{1}$ and $\omega_{2}$ by the expression

$$
P=\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \int_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}} R_{\mathrm{s}}(\hbar \omega) \rho(\omega) \mathrm{d} \omega,
\tag{7}
$$

where $\rho(\omega)$ is the spectral density of photons.

## 3. MONTE CARLO CALCULATION

Using the one-particle Monte Carlo technique, $^{(6)}$ the numerical simulation of the electron motion in $\mathbf{k}$-space was carried out for $n$-type of InSb. The probabilities of electron scattering on acoustic phonons, polar optical phonons and ionized impurities and the parameters of the material were taken from Ref. (7). During the simulation the values of the electron energy $\epsilon_{\mathrm{k}}$ and the angle $\theta_{\mathrm{k}}$ at the end of each free flight before the scattering were stored in the computer. After a sufficiently large enough number of scattering events, $N_{\mathrm{sc}}$ having been realized, the total transition rates with the photon emission were calculated for different scattering processes $s$ by

$$
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{s}}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{sc}}} \sum_{\mathrm{k}=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}} W_{\mathrm{s}}\left(\epsilon_{\mathrm{k}}, \theta_{\mathrm{k}}, \Omega\right).
\tag{8}
$$

This formula is equivalent to equation (6), where the integral is replaced by the summation, and the random values $\epsilon_{\mathrm{k}}$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{k}}$ have the distribution correspondent to the distribution function $F\left(\epsilon_{\mathrm{k}}, \theta_{\mathrm{k}}\right)$. In addition to the light emission characteristics one can calculate such kinetic characteristics as the mean electron energy, the drift velocity and the electron distribution itself by the analogous procedure. $^{(7)}$

The number of photons emitted per unit time within the spectral range between $\lambda_{1}=80 \mu \mathrm{m}$ and $\lambda_{2}=110 \mu \mathrm{m}$ are calculated by using equations (7) and (8) for the transverse and longitudinal emission as the functions of the lattice temperature in Fig. 2.

Considering the contribution into the light emission from different scattering mechanisms we can conclude that the acoustic phonon scattering gives the main contribution. The matrix element of the acoustic-phonon scattering rises with increasing the temperature due to increasing the acoustic-phonon density. Hence this results in increasing the light emission with temperature. From Fig. 2 we can also see that the light emission in the transverse to the field direction is greater than that in the longitudinal direction, illustrating that the light emission is essentially anisotropic.

## 4. EXPERIMENT

Far infrared luminescence of hot electrons due to electric field action in the spectral range $80+110 \mu \mathrm{m}$ of n-InSb (concentration of donors $N_{\mathrm{d}}$ is $2 \cdot 10^{13} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ ) was observed experimentally.

![](./images/812447090046140416_4.jpg)

Fig. 3. Experimental temperature dependencies of emission intensities for two directions (according to Fig. 1). $E=100$ V/cm.

![](./images/812447090046140416_5.jpg)

Fig. 4. The degree of the light emission $K$ and degree of polarization of transverse emission $D$: calculated (solid line) and experimental (dashed line).

A sample with dimensions $4 \times 4 \times 1$ mm was placed in the waveguide on the holder which is temperature regulated. Non-equilibrium light emission was detected by the Ge:Ga photodetector ($T = 4.2$ K) using the phase detecting technique.

In order to check the theoretical model the dependencies of longitudinal and transverse to the electric field direction luminescence $P$ (as illustrated in Fig. 1) were measured. For this purpose the strip contacts were made along edges of the sample. The areas of emitting surfaces were made to be equal.

The results obtained for the electric field value of $E = 100$ V/cm are illustrated in Fig. 3. As was expected, we found the emission anisotropy displayed by the difference between the transverse and longitudinal luminescence, and the former is greater than the latter. As mentioned above, this situation appears under deformation of the electron function distribution only. Analysis of equation (8) shows that the intensity of radiation in the transverse direction increases because of the principal reason. Under the streaming condition the main part of electrons with all $\mathbf{k}$ redistribute in the electric field $\mathbf{E}$ direction where the probability of photon emitting is greater for $\mathbf{e}_{\parallel} \parallel \mathbf{E}$ than for other directions.

Simultaneously we measured the temperature dependencies of luminescence and their value for different directions are presented in the same figure. Comparing the obtained dependencies with these calculated in Fig. 2, we can conclude that the best contribution to the light emission gives the acoustic phonon scattering. The increase of the acoustic photon density with temperature is responsible for the temperature dependence of the luminescence.

Consider the polarization characteristics. Unfortunately, not having the possibility of direct measurement of polarization characteristics, we tried to obtain the polarization degree following the arguments cited in Section 2. The degree of polarization was determined as $D = (b - a)/(b + a)$, where $a$ and $b$ are the intensities with the correspondent polarization (see Fig. 1). Taking into consideration that the total intensity of radiation is proportional to the corresponding figure area it is easy to show that the degree of polarization can be expressed as $D = (I_{\perp} - I_{\parallel})/(I_{\perp} + I_{\parallel})$.

Comparing the emission intensities for two directions one can calculate the polarization of the transverse emission intensity $I_{\perp}$ (ellipse in $x-z$ plane). The results are presented in Fig. 4. The qualitative behavior of these dependencies is in accordance with those theoretically predicted. As mentioned above, the high degree of polarization at low temperatures is due to the anisotropy of streaming, but by increasing the lattice temperature the randomization of scattering process rises resulting in the destruction of the streaming and depolarization of the emission. The quantitative discrepancy between the theory and the experiment may be related by disregarding the aperture of the output luminescence, the model will consider the normal radiation only.
