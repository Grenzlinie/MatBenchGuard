## Abstract
Monte Carlo simulations of electron transport in $\text{Al}_x\text{Ga}_{1-x}\text{As/GaAs/}$ $\text{In}_y\text{Ga}_{1-y}\text{As}$ double quantum well heterostructures in high lateral electric fields are carried out. It is shown that, under the conditions of intervalley $\Gamma$-L electron transfer, there exists a population inversion between the first and the second quantum-confinement subbands in the $\Gamma$ valley. The population inversion appears in fields exceeding 4 and $5.5\ \text{kV}\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ at 77 and 300 K, respectively. The gain in a superlattice composed of such quantum wells is estimated to be on the order of $100\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ for radiation with a wavelength of $12.6\ \mu\text{m}$.

## 1. Introduction
A number of mechanisms leading to population inversion between subbands are related to electron heating in a high lateral electric field (see, e.g., [1, 2]). In particular, it was suggested that population inversion may result from the intervalley $\Gamma$-X transfer in GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructures. However, Monte Carlo simulations indicate that, in this case, population inversion can be attained only for electric fields well above the threshold for the Gunn effect ($\sim8\ \text{kV}\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) [3]; this hinders the implementation of such a scheme.

In this work, we consider another possible scheme for the attainment of population inversion between subbands; this scheme relies on the intervalley $\Gamma$-L transfer of hot electrons in double quantum wells (QWs). The fields required for attaining the population inversion in the $\Gamma$-L scheme are much lower than those in the $\Gamma$-X scheme, which is an advantage. The Monte Carlo simulations of electron transport carried out in this work indicate that the population inversion between the first and the second quantum-confinement subbands in the $\Gamma$ valley takes place in fields above $4\ \text{kV}\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ at 77 K and above $5.5\ \text{kV}\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ at 300 K.

A significant feature of this structure should be noted: the wavefunction of the lowest L subband ($L_1$) is localized in the same QWs as the wavefunction of the $\Gamma_2$ subband (see figure 1). Because of this circumstance, the overlap between the wavefunctions of the subbands $L_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ is substantially larger than the overlap between the wavefunctions of the first L and $\Gamma$ subbands. Thus, in high lateral fields, when the filling of the states in subband $L_1$ sets in, the electrons from these states are scattered mainly to the $\Gamma_2$ subband, which leads to population inversion between the second and the first $\Gamma$ subbands.

In high electric fields, the scattering of electrons in the structure under study mainly occurs due to their interaction with optical and intervalley phonons. Thus, we ignored the acoustic-phonon scattering, as well as the electron–electron and charged impurity scattering, assuming that the electron density is sufficiently low. In our consideration of the electron scattering by polar optical phonons, we assumed that the phonon dispersion relation is the same as in bulk GaAs. In addition, we assumed that the phonon gas is in equilibrium. We found that in fields exceeding $4$ kV cm$^{-1}$, a population inversion between the first and the second subbands sets in (see figure 2). If the system is periodic along the $z$-axis, then we find that the possible gain in such a structure is $\sim$100 cm$^{-1}$ for a wavelength of $12.6$ $\mu$m.
