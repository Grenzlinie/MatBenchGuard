# NONPARABOLICITY EFFECTS ON TRANSITION RATES DUE TO CONFINED PHONONS IN GaAs-AlGaAs QUANTUM WELLS

Augusto M. Alcalde and Gerald Weber
Instituto de Física, Universidade Estadual de Campinas,
Caixa Postal 6165, 13083-970 Campinas SP, Brazil

(Received 19 April 1995; accepted in revised form 6 July 1995 by E. Molinari)

We calculate electron-LO-confined-phonon scattering rates in GaAs-AlGaAs quantum wells considering the influence of nonparabolicity on the energy subbands. We find that the subband nonparabolicity increases the scattering rates significantly for all transitions and that this effect is more pronounced as transitions from higher subbands are involved.

Keywords: A. quantum wells, A. semiconductors, D. electron-phonon interactions, D. electronic band structure

Electron-phonon interaction in polar semiconductor quantum wells attracted a great amount of interest over the last years due to its importance for electronic properties. For instance the cooling of photoexcited carriers, carrier tunneling and the mobility of high-speed heterostructure devices are primarily governed by the scattering of electrons by polar-optical phonons. Particular interest was directed towards LO-phonon confinement which affects significantly the scattering rates in quantum wells.

The electron-confined LO phonon interaction was studied using either dielectric continuum models¹·² or microscopic lattice dynamical models,³·⁴ and much emphasis was given on the influence of the specific phonon model employed. In general, the use of dielectric continuum models is well established³ and scattering rates calculated with such models compare successfully with experimental results.⁵·⁶ From the calculation of capture times⁵ it became evident that capture processes with large kinetic energy influence the overall capture time. For those large kinetic energies, implying in large momenta, the parabolic-band approximation becomes less justified even for GaAs-AlGaAs structures where in general nonparabolicity effects can be safely neglected. A question which has not yet been properly addressed is how strongly the subband nonparabolicity affects the intra- and intersubband scattering rates in quantum wells.

The purpose of the present work is to study the influence of subband nonparabolicity on intra- and intersubband scattering rates by comparing the results to those obtained using the parabolic-band approximation.

In this way we are able to determine for which situations the subband nonparabolicity can be neglected or not. For GaAs-Al₀.₃Ga₀.₇As quantum wells we find that the subband nonparabolicity is important for transitions involving higher energy states and for structures with well thicknesses smaller that ≈150 Å. Also, we obtained transitions rates which have a stronger dependence on the subband nonparabolicity with increasing barrier height.

Several theoretical models were proposed, in various degrees of complexity, to take into account the effect of nonparabolicity in the electronic band structure.⁷⁻¹⁰ The main difference between those models is the form of the energy dispersion relation describing the subband nonparabolicity. We chose an energy dispersion relation which is thought to adequately represent the subband nonparabolicity of GaAs-GaAlAs quantum wells,¹⁰

$$
\mathcal{E}-V_{i}=\frac{\hbar^{2} k^{2}}{2 m_{i}^{*}}\left(1-\gamma_{i} k^{2}\right), \tag{1}
$$

where $\mathcal{E}$ is the total electron energy, $m_{i}^{*}$ is the band edge mass and $\gamma_{i}$ the nonparabolicity parameter. With $k^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}$ we have at the bottom of the subband ($k_{\parallel}^{2}=0$),

$$
E-V_{i}=\frac{\hbar^{2} k_{z}^{2}}{2 m_{i}^{*}}\left(1-\gamma_{i} k_{z}^{2}\right). \tag{2}
$$

The subscript $i$ indicates the well layer ($i$=W) and the barrier layer ($i$=B), and $V_{i}$ is the bulk conduction band offset taken as $V_{W}=0$ and $V_{B}=V_{0}$.

The electron-LO-confined phonon scattering rates are obtained from the Fermi golden rule and with the well known electron-confined-phonon interaction Hamiltonian (Fröhlich interaction).$^{11,12}$ The scattering rates for an electron initially in the well ($i$=$W$) or barrier ($i$=$B$) subband take the final form

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm{np}}^{(i)}= & \frac{m_{i}^{*} \lambda^{2} L}{\hbar^{3}} \sum_{n}\left|G_{n}\right|^{2}\left(N_{L O}+1\right) \\
& \times \frac{1}{\alpha_{i}}\left\{\frac{a_{n} L^{2}}{2 \gamma}\left[\left(1-2 \gamma k_{z}^{2}\right)-\alpha_{i}\right]+b_{n}\right\}^{-1}, \quad(3)
\end{aligned}
$$

with

$$
\alpha_{i}=\left[\left(2 \gamma k_{z}^{2}-1\right)^{2}+4 \gamma Q_{i}^{2}\right]^{1 / 2}, \quad(4)
$$

where $k_{z}$ is the $z$-component of the initial (final) electron wave vector for intrasubband (intersubband) transitions, and $G_{n}$ is the electron-phonon overlap integral. For phonon emission we define

$$
Q_{i}^{2}= \pm \frac{2 m_{i}^{*}}{\hbar^{2}}\left(E_{\text {initial }}-E_{\text {final }}-\hbar \omega_{L O}\right), \quad(5)
$$

where the upper sign corresponds to intrasubband transitions, while the lower sign corresponds to intersubband transitions. Further parameters in Eq. (3) are defined in previous works.$^{2,13}$ For intrasubband transitions we assume that the electron has just enough energy to emit one LO confined-phonon and for intersubband transitions that the electron is initially at the bottom of the subband. We express our results as an average scattering rate $W=p_{W} W^{(W)}+p_{B} W^{(B)}$, where $p_{W}$ ($p_{B}$) is the probability of finding the electron initially in the well (barrier) subband. This procedure is necessary because the effective masses as well as the nonparabolicity parameters in the well are different from those of the barrier. We believe that using an average in-plane effective mass$^{14}$ as used in some calculations$^{15}$ in addition to an average band nonparabolicity parameter is not appropriate for the calculation of scattering rates.

Note that the nonparabolic-band scattering rate, Eq. (3), has a non trivial limit for $\gamma \to 0$. Nonetheless, an elementary application of L'Hopital rule shows that in this limit we retrieve the well known parabolic-band scattering rates$^{1,2}$

$$
W_{\mathrm{p}}^{(i)}=\frac{m_{i}^{*} \lambda^{2} L}{\hbar^{3}} \sum_{n}\left|G_{n}\right|^{2}\left(N_{L O}+1\right)\left[b_{n}-a_{n} L^{2} Q_{i}^{2}\right]^{-1} \quad(6)
$$

For the description of the confined phonon modes we used the corrected slab model$^{16}$ which gives scattering rates very close to the phenomenological Huang and Zhu model$^{17}$ and compares successfully with experimental results.$^{5,6}$ However, any confined phonon model can be used for the scattering rates given in Eq. (3), by using the appropriate expressions for the coefficients $a_{n}$ and $b_{n}$, and the electron-phonon overlap integral $G_{n}$ (see Refs. 2 and 13).

We shall point out that the expression for the scattering rates, given by Eq. (3), follows exclusively from the energy dispersion relation of Eq. (1). No assumption about the model which calculates the energy eigenvalues and eigenstates, including the subband nonparabolicity, was made so far. This model will affect the values of $k_{z}$ and $Q_{i}^{2}$ and the overlap integral $G_{n}$ via the electron envelope wave functions. Evidently, the model should allow to decouple the in-plane and $z$ motion of the electron as otherwise the in-plane wave vector conservation would be affected. In order to obtain the electron energy levels and wave functions we use the model proposed by Nag and Mukhopadhyay.$^{10}$ This model has the advantage that the overlap functions have the same analytic functional form of the parabolic-band case,$^{2}$ thus it allows a straightforward inclusion of the nonparabolicty effects into existing parabolic-band calculations.$^{1,2}$ Unfortunately this and similar models suffer from a number of drawbacks: conflicting definitions of energy dependent effective masses, boundary conditions and slight non-orthogonality of the wave functions.$^{18}$ These questions are still under some debate and are beyond the scope of this work.

For our calculations of scattering rates we assume a GaAs-Al$_{0.3}$Ga$_{0.7}$As quantum well with finite barriers of 224 meV. The material parameters used in our calculations are: for GaAs, the effective mass $m^{*}$=0.0665$m_{0}$, the dielectric constants $\epsilon_{0}$=12.35 and $\epsilon_{\infty}$=10.48, the bulk phonon energy $\hbar \omega_{LO}$=36.8 meV, and the nonparabolicity parameter is taken as$^{7,10}$ $\gamma=4.9 \times 10^{-19} \mathrm{~m}^{2}$; for Ga$_{0.3}$Al$_{0.7}$As, the effective mass $m^{*}$=0.0901$m_{0}$, and the nonparabolicity parameter$^{10}$ $\gamma=2.67 \times 10^{-19} \mathrm{~m}^{2}$.

The phonon occupation number is assumed $N_{LO} \sim 0$, which is valid for low temperatures.

In Fig. 1 we show the calculated scattering rates for intrasubband transitions due to the emission of confined phonon modes as a function of the quantum well width. The solid lines represent the scattering rates with the inclusion of subband nonparabolicity and the dashed lines are for the parabolic-band approximation. We observe that the effect of the subband nonparabolicty on the scattering rates increases as transitions involve higher energy states, but the scattering rates are otherwise not qualitatively different from those in the parabolic-band approximation. For intersubband transitions we find a similar behaviour as shown in Fig. 2, but the effects of the subband nonparabolicity become smaller more rapidly as the well thickness increases. In particular, for large quantum wells where the subband separation becomes close to the LO phonon energy, the phonon in-plane wave vector becomes zero and thus the effect of nonparabolicity should be small. The intersubband $2\to1$ transitions of Fig. 2 shows this behaviour. For narrow quantum wells, the nonparabolicity effects are not visible as the scattering rates are much smaller when compared to those for large quantum wells. For these narrow quantum wells, the effect of the subband nonparabolicity is better illustrated in Fig. 3 where we display the ratio of the nonparabolic and parabolic scattering rates [$W_{\text{np}}/W_{\text{p}}$, Eqs. (3) and (6)].¹⁹ Except for $1\to1$ transitions for narrow wells (less than $15$ Å) all nonparabolic scattering rates are higher. On the other hand, for quantum wells larger than $150$ Å the transition rates with subband nonparabolicity are very close to those in the parabolic-band approximation. In general, for transitions from higher energy states the subband nonparabolicity affects the scattering rates more strongly. For instance, for the $3\to1$ transitions the nonparabolic transition rate is up to 6 times higher than the parabolic one. Strictly speaking, due to the model used for the calculation of the nonparabolic electron energy levels,¹⁰ the wave functions of the first and third state are not completely orthogonal. This non-orthogonality, which affects only the $3\to1$ transitions, is associated to the problematic definitions of energy dependent effective masses and boundary conditions, and does otherwise not affect the electron-phonon transition selection rules. We have numerically estimated that this non-orthogonality may cause a variation of the order of 1% in the overlap integrals of the $3\to1$ transitions and should have a negligible effect on the scattering rates.

The enhancement of the scattering rates with the inclusion of subband nonparabolicity results mainly from a larger electron-phonon overlap as well as from a larger density of final electron states. This enhancement occurs despite of the fact that the emitted confined phonons have a larger wave vector, due to the nonparabolicity of the electron subband, which implies

The electron-phonon overlap integrals of the $1 \to 1$ non-parabolic transition rates, for narrow wells, are the only ones which were found to be smaller than the overlaps of the parabolic case. This explains why, for this case, the scattering rates are reduced with the inclusion of the subband nonparabolicity.

For large quantum wells $(> 150$ Å) the nonparabolicity effects are practically negligible. In this case as the electronic confinement is greatly reduced the subband nonparabolicity has a smaller effect on the overlap integral. The electronic confinement can also be reduced by a smaller barrier height thus reducing the nonparabolicity effects as shown in Fig. 4. In this figure we show some scattering rates as a function of the Al concentration and note that the intrasubband rates have a marked dependence on the subband nonparabolicity as the Al concentration rises. The material parameters were taken from Ref. 20 and the nonparabolicity parameter $\gamma$ with Al concentration dependence was taken from Ref. 21.

Although we considered only confined phonons in this work, the above considerations can be extended to other phonon modes. As the effect of the nonparabolicity manifests itself mainly through the electron-phonon overlap and the density of final electron states (which does not depend on the phonon modes), it is reasonable to assume that scattering rates due to optic phonons, in general, will increase if the overlap increases. Also, we considered subband transitions where the nonparabolicity effect is indeed smallest: intrasubband transition to the bottom of the subband and intersubband transitions from the bottom of the initial subband. Away from these limits, i. e. at larger initial kinetic energies, we expect that the effect of the subband nonparabolicity becomes even more important.

In conclusion, we have calculated the scattering rates for intrasubband and intersubband transitions due to electron-confined-phonon interaction in quantum wells where we included the subband nonparabolicity. We find that for intra and intersubband transitions the scattering rates are significantly increased. In particular for higher subbands and larger electron confinement the nonparabolicity effects becomes more important.
