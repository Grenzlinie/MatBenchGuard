# The Surface Atomic and Magnetic Order in Crystals

By
Z.A. MATYSINA (a), M.I. MILYAN (a), D.R. RIZDVYANETSKII (b),
and S.YU. ZAGINAICHENKO (c)

**Introduction** As is known, the parameters of atomic pair interaction in the surface layer can considerably differ from similar parameters of interaction in the volume part of alloys. As a rule in the surface layer of substitute alloys, the segregation of one of the components takes place /1, 2/. As was found in the experimental works /3, 4/, if the alloy components are the atoms of transition elements, then the formation of ferromagnetism or antiferromagnetism on the surface of these alloys is possible.

A theoretical investigation of the mutual effect of atomic ordering (with the superstructure $AB_2$ formation) and the spontaneous magnetization in a hexagonal two-dimensional lattice was carried out.

The mutual effect of surface segregation and spontaneous magnetization on the (110) surface of binary alloys with b.c.c. structure was studied.

The calculations were carried out in the self-consistent field approximation using the Bragg-Williams-Gorsky method and the Heisenberg magnetism model.

**Atomic ordering in magnetic film** Let us discuss the two-dimensional hexagonal lattice where $AB_2$-type superstructure formation is possible. Let us discuss two flat magnetic sublattices which are characterized by $\mu_1$, $\mu_2$ magnetizations and coincide with the atomic sublattices.

The system of equilibrium equations has the form

$$
kT \ln \frac{\left(c_A+\frac{2}{3}\eta\right)\left(c_B+\frac{1}{3}\eta\right)}{\left(c_A-\frac{1}{3}\eta\right)\left(c_B-\frac{2}{3}\eta\right)} = 3\left[(W-2w)\eta + 2\alpha\eta(4\mu_1\mu_2 - \mu_2^2) + 3\beta(\mu_1\mu_2 - \mu_2^2)\right], \tag{1}
$$

$$
kT \ln \frac{1+\mu_1}{1-\mu_1} = \left(6A_o + \frac{4}{3}\alpha\eta^2 + \beta\eta\right)\mu_2 \ , \tag{2}
$$

$$
kT \ln \frac{1+\mu_2}{1-\mu_2} = 3A_o(\mu_1+\mu_2) + \frac{1}{3}\alpha\eta^2(2\mu_1-\mu_2) + \frac{1}{2}\beta\eta(\mu_1-2\mu_2) \ , \tag{3}
$$

where k is the Boltzmann constant, T is the absolute temperature, η is the atomic order parameter, $c_A$, $c_B$ are the atomic concentration of A and B components, W = $2V_{AB}-V_{AA}-V_{BB}$, w = $2v_{AB}-v_{AA}-v_{BB}$; $V_{ij},v_{ij}$ (i, j = A, B) are the atomic interaction energies with the opposite signs for the first and the second coordination spheres, respectively, $A_O = c_A A_{AA}+c_B A_{BB}+c_A c_B \alpha$, $\alpha = 2A_{AB}-A_{AA}-A_{BB}$, $\beta = 2\left[c_A A_{AA}-c_B A_{BB}+(c_B - c_A)A_{AB}\right]$; $A_{ij}$(i, j = A, B) are the exchange interaction parameters.

The high-temperature points $T_O^O$, $T_C^O$, $T_N^O$ and the low-temperature points $T_O$, $T_C$, $T_N$ of phase transitions are determined by the formulae

$$
\mathrm{kT}_{0}^{\mathrm{O}}=3 \mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{A}} \mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{W}-2 \mathrm{w}), \quad \mathrm{kT}_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{O}}=3 \mathrm{~A}_{0}, \quad \mathrm{kT}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{O}}=-\frac{3}{2} \mathrm{~A}_{0},
\tag{4}
$$

$$
\mathrm{kT}_{0}=3 \mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{A}} \mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{B}}\left(\mathrm{W}-2 \mathrm{w}+2 \alpha \mu_{*}^{2}\right),
\tag{5}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{kT}_{\mathrm{K}}= & \frac{1}{4}\left(3 \mathrm{~A}_{0}-\frac{1}{3} \alpha \eta_{*}^{2}-\beta \eta_{*}\right) \pm \frac{1}{4}\left[\left(3 \mathrm{~A}_{0}-\frac{1}{3} \alpha \eta_{*}^{2}-\beta \eta_{*}\right)^{2}\right. \\
& \left.+8\left(3 \mathrm{~A}_{0}+\frac{2}{3} \alpha \eta_{*}^{2}+\frac{1}{2} \beta \eta_{*}\right)^{2}\right]^{1 / 2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

where $\mu_{*}=\mu(T_{0})$, $\eta_{*}=\eta(T_{K})$. Signs "+" and "-" in formula (6) correspond to ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism. In the first case $\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}$ and $T_{K}=T_{C}$ is the Curie temperature. In the second case $\mu_{1}=-\mu_{2}$ and $T_{K}=T_{N}$ is the Néel temperature. The temperature $T_{0}^{O}$ defines the temperature of atomic ordering for a nonmagnetic alloy. The values of $T_{C}^{O}$ at $A_{0}>0$ and $T_{N}^{O}$ at $A_{0}<0$ define the Curie and the Néel points, respectively, for an atomically disordered alloy.

At high temperatures the alloy is disordered and nonmagnetic. When the temperature decreases, depending on the relation between the energetic parameters W, w, and $A_{0}$, at one of the temperatures (4) a transition of the alloy to an atomically ordered or magnetic state takes place.

The values of temperatures $T_{0}$ and $T_{K}$ can considerably differ from the temperatures $T_{0}^{O}$, $T_{C}^{O}$, $T_{N}^{O}$ due to the values of $\mu_{*}$, $\eta_{*}$. In this case positive values of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ increase the temperature of ordering and the Curie temperature and decrease the Néel temperature.

As follows from (5), (6) the atomic order can appear at negative ordering energy due to the magnetic order and the type of magnetization can be defined by the atomic order. An abrupt change in the magnetic properties of adatom films is possible due to the directed change in the degree of long-range atomic order.

Mutual effect of atomic ordering and magnetization in binary alloy with free surface. Surface segregation Let us consider the atomic ordering and atomic magnetic moments at the sites of ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic alloys with b.c.c. structure /5, 6/. Let us unite ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism into one system both in the alloy volume and in the surface monolayer.

The equilibrium condition of such an alloy is described by the following equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln \frac{\left(c_{s A}+\eta_{s}\right)\left(c_{s B}+\eta_{s}\right)}{\left(c_{s A}-\eta_{s}\right)\left(c_{s B}-\eta_{s}\right)} &-\frac{1}{2} \mathfrak{t}\left[2 W_{1} \eta_{s}+W^{1} \eta+J_{s} \eta_{s} \mu_{s 1} \mu_{s 2}\right. \\
&\left.+\frac{1}{4} J^{1} \eta\left(\mu_{s 1} \mu_{2}+\mu_{s 2} \mu_{s 1}\right)+\frac{1}{4} \varepsilon^{1}\left(\mu_{s 1} \mu_{2}-\mu_{s 2} \mu_{1}\right)\right]=0,(7)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\ln \frac{\left(c_{A}+\eta\right)\left(c_{B}+\eta\right)}{\left(c_{A}-\eta\right)\left(c_{B}-\eta\right)}-t\left(2 W+J \mu_{1} \mu_{2}\right) \eta=0, \tag{8}
$$

$$
\ln \frac{1+\mu_{s 1}}{1-\mu_{s 1}}+\frac{1}{4} \mathfrak{t}\left[2\left(\sigma_{s}+J_{s} \eta_{s}^{2}\right) \mu_{s 2}+\left(\sigma^{1}-\varepsilon^{1} \eta_{s}+\varepsilon_{s}^{1} \eta+J^{1} \eta_{s} \eta\right) \mu_{2}\right]=0, \tag{9}
$$

$$
\ln \frac{1+\mu_{s 2}}{1-\mu_{s 2}}-\frac{1}{4} t\left[2\left(\sigma_{s}+J_{s} \eta_{s}^{2}\right) \mu_{s 1}+\left(\sigma^{1}-\varepsilon^{1} \eta_{s}+\varepsilon_{s}^{1} \eta+J^{1} \eta_{s} \eta\right) \mu_{1}\right]=0, \tag{10}
$$

$$
\ln \frac{1+\mu_{1}}{1-\mu_{1}}-t\left(\sigma+J \eta^{2}\right) \mu_{2}=0, \tag{11}
$$

$$
\ln \frac{1+\mu_{2}}{1-\mu_{2}}-t\left(\sigma+J \eta^{2}\right) \mu_{1}=0, \tag{12}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \ln \frac{\left(c_{B}+\eta\right)\left(c_{B}-\eta\right)}{\left(c_{A}+\eta\right)\left(c_{A}-\eta\right)}+\ln \frac{\left(c_{s A}+\eta_{s}\right)\left(c_{s A}-\eta_{s}\right)}{\left(c_{s B}+\eta_{s}\right)\left(c_{s B}-\eta_{s}\right)}-\frac{1}{2} t\left[-4 \omega+2 \omega_{s}+\omega^{1}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{1}{4} \varepsilon^{1}\left(\mu_{s 1} \mu_{2}+\mu_{s 2} \mu_{1}\right)-\varepsilon_{s} \mu_{s 1} \mu_{s 2}-2 \varepsilon \mu_{1} \mu_{2}+\frac{1}{4} J^{1}\left(\mu_{s 1} \mu_{2}-\mu_{s 2} \mu_{1}\right)\right]=0,(13)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\eta_{s}$, $\eta$ are the atomic long-range order parameters for the surface monolayer (with index s) and the alloy volume of crystal; $\mu_{s 1}$, $\mu_{s 2}$, $\mu_{1}$, $\mu_{2}$ are the magnetic order parameters of surface and volume sublattices, $c_{s A}$, $c_{s B}$, $c_{A}$, $c_{B}$ are the relative A and B atom concentrations in the surface monolayer and in the alloy volume.

$$
\sigma=c_{A} J_{A A}+c_{B} J_{B B}+c_{A} c_{B} J \quad, \quad \sigma_{s}=c_{s A} J_{A A}^{(s)}+c_{s B} J_{B B}^{(s)}+c_{s A} c_{s B} J_{s},
$$

$$
\sigma^{1}=c_{s A} c_{A} J_{A A}^{1}+c_{s B} c_{B} J_{B B}^{1}+\left(c_{s A} c_{B}+c_{s B} c_{A}\right) J_{A B}^{1},
$$

$$
2 \omega=V_{A A}-V_{B B}+\left(c_{B}-c_{A}\right) W, \quad 2 \varepsilon=J_{A A}-J_{B B}+\left(c_{B}-c_{A}\right) J,
$$

$$
2 \omega_{s}=V_{A A}^{(s)}-V_{B B}^{(s)}+\left(c_{s B}-c_{s A}\right) W_{s}, 2 \varepsilon=J_{A A}^{(s)}+J_{B B}^{(s)}+\left(c_{s B}-c_{s A}\right) J_{s},
$$

$$
2 \omega^{1}=V_{A A}^{1}-V_{B B}^{1}+\left(c_{B}-c_{A}\right) W^{1}, \quad 2 \varepsilon_{s}^{1}=J_{A A}^{1}-J_{B B}^{1}+\left(c_{B}-c_{A}\right) J^{1},
$$

$$
\mathfrak{t}=z / \mathrm{kT}, \quad 2 \varepsilon_{s}^{1}=J_{A A}^{1}-J_{B B}^{1}+\left(c_{s B}-c_{s A}\right) J^{1},
$$

z is the number of the nearest atoms, $V_{\alpha \alpha'}$, $V_{\alpha \alpha'}^l$, $V_{\alpha \alpha'}^{(s)}$ are the atom interaction energies with opposite signs, and $J_{\alpha \alpha'}$, $J_{\alpha \alpha'}^l$, $J_{\alpha \alpha'}^{(s)} (\alpha,\alpha' = A,B)$ are the atomic exchange interaction parameters in the volume, between the surface monolayer and volume, and in the surface monolayer, respectively,

$$
W = 2V_{AB} - V_{AA} - V_{BB} \ , \ \ W^l = 2V_{AB}^l - V_{AA}^l - V_{BB}^l \ ,
$$

$$
W_s = 2V_{AB}^{(s)} - V_{AA}^{(s)} - V_{BB}^{(s)} \ ,
$$

$$
J = 2J_{AB} - J_{AA} - J_{BB} \ , \ \ J^l = 2J_{AB}^l - J_{AA}^l - J_{BB}^l \ ,
$$

$$
J_s = 2J_{AB}^{(s)} - J_{AA}^{(s)} - J_{BB}^{(s)} \ .
$$

The system of equations (7) to (13) permits to make some conclusions about the mutual connection of atomic and magnetic orders and surface concentration parameters $c_{sA}$.

If the energetic parameters of atomic and magnetic interaction are known, then (7) to (13) permit to calculate the temperature dependence of long-range order and the surface segregation parameters. Phase diagrams for the surface monolayer of ordered ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic alloys may also be calculated.

Let us carry out the calculation for a particular case. The nonmagnetic metal B has a CsCl-type lattice with free smooth (110) face surface and contains a small addition of the transition A component, the atoms of which have a magnetic moment equal to unity. Due to the low concentration of the A component, the atomic ordering in the alloy volume is absent /7/. The spontaneous magnetization is also absent /6/. As the temperature goes up, the Gibbs enrichment effect of the surface layer with A atoms may develop. This leads to an increase in the concentration $c_{sA}$ of A atoms in the monolayer. At some temperature T the concentration $c_{sA}$ may be sufficient for the atomic ordering effect and for spontaneous magnetization in this layer. The situation where the surface monolayer is positioned in the ferromagnetic atomically disordered condition is also possible. The equi-state of such an alloy is described by the following equations:

$$
kT \ln \frac{1 + \mu_s}{1 - \mu_s} = 4J_s c_{sA}^z \mu_s \ ,
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
kT \ln \frac{c_{sA}(1 - c_A)}{(1 - c_{sA})c_A} &+ 4\left[V_{AA}-V_{BB}+(1-2c_A)W\right]-2\left[V_{AA}^{(s)}-V_{BB}^{(s)}+(1-2c_{sA})W_s\right] \\
&- \left[V_{AA}^l-V_{BB}^l+(1-2c_A)W^l\right] = 0 \ ,
\end{aligned}
$$

The system (14), (15) permits to determine the equilibrium concentration of surface A atoms and to investigate its dependence on the A component concentration $c$ in volume of the alloy, the temperature, and the spontaneous magnetization values.

From (14) follows that the Curie temperature in the surface monolayer is determined by the equation $kT_C^{(s)} = 2J_s c_{sA}^2$. In the presence of surface segregation, i.e. when the value of $c_{sA}$ is high, this temperature may be significant. In Fig. 1 can be seen that the surface concentration c of A atoms is higher than the concentration c in the volume. In addition, the value of $c_{sA}$ increases with c for all values of the surface layer spontaneous magnetization. The relationship $c_{sA}=c_{sA}(\mu_{s})$ is plotted in Fig. 2. It is seen from Fig. 1 and 2 that an increase in the degree of surface layer spontaneous magnetization increases the values of $c_{sA}$. Hence, with increasing surface segregation of the A component in the investigated system it is necessary to increase the degree of surface monolayer spontaneous magnetization. Relation $\mu_{s}=\mu_{s}(T/T')$(Fig.3) indicates that spontaneous magnetization in the investigated alloy appears as a phase transition of the first kind.

Thus, the above examined simplified model for alloys with a free surface replicates the main features, manifest in experiments, of spontaneous magnetization and atomic ordering influence on the transition component A segregation, as, for instance in the $Ni_{3}Fe$ system /8/. The theoretical investigations which were carried out may be applied for a qualitative interpretation of the physical properties of films.
