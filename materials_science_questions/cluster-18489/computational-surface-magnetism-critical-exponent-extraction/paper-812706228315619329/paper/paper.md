# Edge and surface antiferromagnetism in $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle within the effective field theory

Serkan Güldalⁿ and Yasin Polᵃᵗ

ⁿDepartment of Physics, Faculty of Arts and Sciences, Adiyaman University, Adiyaman, Turkey; ᵇScience Technology Application and Research Center, Nevsehir Haci Bektas Veli University, Nevsehir, Turkey

## ABSTRACT
The edge and surface antiferromagnetic properties of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle and their components (A, B and O) are investigated through the Effective Field Theory developed by Kaneyoshi. It is found that the Edge AntiFerroMagnetism (EAFM) causes a decrease in the Curie temperature and separation in the magnetizations. However, the Surface AntiFerroMagnetism (SAFM) causes a compensation behaviour ($T_{comp}=1.23$) in the total magnetisation of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle. The magnetisation of the central B component has been affected by the edge antiferromagnetism more strongly than by the surface antiferromagnetism. Moreover, coercivity (Hc) and remanence magnetizations (Mr) decrease for the EAFM, but they increase for the SAFM. Triple hysteresis behaviour is obtained for the SAFM. Therefore, it is suggested that there is a strong relationship between the compensation behaviour and the triple hysteresis behaviour.

The new-found mineral, once identified, was named as *Perovskite*, the original name of which belonged to the Napoleonic War veteran and mineralogist called Count Lev A. For instance, they have appli- cations such as proton conductivity properties in Solid Oxide Fuel Cells (SOFC), hydrogen sensor and hydrogen production and removal [4–6], ionic conductivity properties in solid electrolytes [7], mixed conductivity properties in SOFC electrodes [8–10], ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties in thermis- tors and actuators [11,12], optical properties in electro-optic modulators and lasers [13–16], superconductivity properties in superconductivity applications [17–19] and magnetic properties in magnetic memory applications [20–22].

Thanks to these extensive numbers of applications, magnetic perovskites are attractive for scientific, technological and commercial purposes. For all these reasons, modelling and investigating the magnetic properties of the $ABO_3$ per- ovskite-type nanoparticle are focused in this paper.

The ferromagnetic properties of the perovskites were discovered by Jonker and Santen [23] in 1950. This discovery triggered many experimental studies and new magnetic $ABO_3$-type and $A_2BBO_6$-type perovskites. The magnetic properties of $A_2BReO_6$ [24], $La_xMnO3_{-\delta}$ ($0.67 < x < 1$) [25], $LaNiO_3$–$LaMnO_3$ [26], $Sr_{1-x}Ca_xRuO_3$, $Sr_{1-y}Ba_yRuO_3$ and $Sr_{1-z}Ba_{0.49}Ca_{0.51z}RuO_3$ [27], $CaRuO_3$ and $SrRuO_3$ [28], $GdFeO_3$ [29], $Ca_2CoWO_6$, $Sr_2CoWO_6$ and $Ba_2CoWO_6$ [30], $Sr_2CrReO_6$ [31], $Ba_{2-x}Sr_xYIrO_6$ [32], $LaAlO_3$ and $SrTiO_3$ perovskites [33] were investigated experimentally. Additionally, many theoretical studies were reported on the modelling and the investigation of the magnetic properties of the $ABO_3$-type and$A_2BBO_6$-type perovskites. For instance, the magnetic proper- ties of $GdFeO_3$, $Sr_2FeMoO_6$, $Sr_2OsMoO_6$, $LaPbMnSbO_6$, $Sr_2CrOsO_6$, $Sr_{2-}$ $CrReO_6$, $Sr_2CrMoO_6$, $La_2FeCoO_6$, $PrMnO_3$, $NdMnO_3$, $Sr_2CrMoO_6$, $Sr_2VMoO_6$, $Sr_{2-x}La_xFeMoO_6$, $Ba_2NiUO_6$, $Sr_2CrReO_6$, $CH_3NH_3PbI_3$, $Sr_{2-}$ $FeMoO_6$, and $La_{1-x}Ca_xMnO_3$ were investigated by using the Density Functional Theory and the Dynamic Mean Field Approximation [34–37], theMonte Carlo Method [38–45], the Mean Field Approximation [46–48], the Effective Mass Model [49], the Effective Spin Model [50], the Green Function Techniques [51] and the Local Spin Density Method [52].

The Effective Field Theory (EFT), developed by Kaneyoshi [53], is a successful method to model magnetic systems and their compounds such as graphene [54–58], diamonds [59], superconductors [60,61], fullerenes [62–64], thin films [65], nanoparticles [66–68], nanotubes [69], nanowires [70], alloys [71–73], core/shell systems [74], 1-dimensional systems [75], nanolattices [76,77], 2D-Austenite and Martensitic structural transformation [78] and nanostrips [79]. All of these magnetic systems have a specific crystal structure or could be modelled

by a specific crystal lattice. Likewise, as far as it is known, in only one study is the magnetic perovskite modelled, and its phase diagrams are examined by using the EFT, [80] in which $Sr_2CrReO_6$ double perovskites fcc-CrRe sublattice with mixed spin (3/2, 1) are modelled by the Ising spin system, and only the phase diagrams are investigated. However, the temperature and the external magnetic field dependences of the magnetizations of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle are not studied.

In this study, by considering the crystal structure of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle system [81-86], the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle system will be modelled by the EFT and its magnetic properties (M-T; magnetisation-temperature dependences, M-H; magnetisation-external magnetic field dependences) will be investigated for ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic (edge and surface antiferromagnetism) cases. Thus, the antiferromagnetic effects of the edge (A) and the surface atom (O) on the magnetic properties of the ABO3 perovskite-type nanoparticle will be obtained, and it is expected that this study will give a light into the theoretical and experimental research for the investigation of the ferromagnetic and the antiferromagnetic properties of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle systems.

## 2. Theoretical method

The $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle is modelled in this study, and their ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic properties (edge and surface antiferromagnetism) are investigated by using the Effective Field Theory, developed by Kaneyoshi [53], within the Ising Model. the Refs. [81-87] as shown in Figure 1. The $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle structure has three different magnetizations ($m_\text{A}$, $m_\text{B}$, and $m_\text{O}$). According to the nearest-neighbors, the $ABO_3$ has five different exchange interactions as $j_\text{AA}$, $j_\text{AO}$, $j_\text{AB}$, $j_\text{OO}$, and $j_\text{OB}$. $j_\text{AA}$ is the exchange interaction between A and A atoms on the edge of the $ABO_3$ lattice, and similarly, the others denote the interactions among the other atoms. In this paper, the magnetic properties versus the temperature and the external magnetic field for the ferromagnetic exchange interactions ($j_\text{AA}>0$, $j_\text{AO}>0$, $j_\text{AB}>0$, $j_\text{OO}>0$ and $j_\text{OB}>0$), the edge antiferromagnetic exchange interactions ($j_\text{AA}<0$, $j_\text{AO}>0$, $j_\text{AB}>0$, $j_\text{OO}>0$ and $j_\text{OB}>0$) and the surface antiferromagnetic exchange interactions ($j_\text{AA}>0$, $j_\text{AO}<0$, $j_\text{AB}>0$, $j_\text{OO}$ $>0$ and $j_\text{OB}>0$) were obtained.

The Hamiltonian and the magnetizations of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle are as follows;

Hamiltonian;

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{A}= & -j_{A A} \sum_{\langle A, A\rangle} S_{A}^{Z} S_{A}^{Z}-j_{A O} \sum_{\langle A, O\rangle} S_{A}^{Z} S_{O}^{Z}-j_{A B} \sum_{\langle A, B\rangle} S_{A}^{Z} S_{B}^{Z}-j_{O O} \sum_{\langle O, O\rangle} S_{O}^{Z} S_{O}^{Z} \\
& -j_{O B} \sum_{\langle O, B\rangle} S_{O}^{Z} S_{B}^{Z}-h\left(\sum_{A} S_{A}^{Z}+\sum_{B} S_{B}^{Z}+\sum_{O} S_{O}^{Z}\right)
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

Magnetizations;

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{A}= & {\left[\cosh \left(j_{A A} \nabla\right)+m_{A} \sinh \left(j_{A A} \nabla\right)\right]^{3}\left[\cosh \left(j_{A O} \nabla\right)+m_{O} \sinh \left(j_{A O} \nabla\right)\right]^{3} } \\
& {\left.\left[\cosh \left(j_{A B} \nabla\right)+m_{B} \sinh \left(j_{A B} \nabla\right)\right]^{1} F_{S-1 / 2}(x)\right|_{x=0^{\prime}} }
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\left.m_{B}=\left[\cosh \left(j_{A B} \nabla\right)+m_{A} \sinh \left(j_{A B} \nabla\right)\right]^{8}\left[\cosh \left(j_{O B} \nabla\right)+m_{O} \sinh \left(j_{O B} \nabla\right)\right]^{6} F_{S-1 / 2}(x)\right|_{x=0^{\prime}}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{O}= & {\left[\cosh \left(j_{O O} \nabla\right)+m_{O} \sinh \left(j_{O O} \nabla\right)\right]^{4}\left[\cosh \left(j_{O A} \nabla\right)+m_{A} \sinh \left(j_{O A} \nabla\right)\right]^{4} } \\
& {\left.\left[\cosh \left(j_{O B} \nabla\right)+m_{B} \sinh \left(j_{O B} \nabla\right)\right]^{1} F_{S-1 / 2}(x)\right|_{x=0^{\prime}} }
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

In Equation (2), $\nabla=(\partial / \partial x)$ is the differential operator, and the function of $\text{F}_{1/2}(\text{x})$ is defined in the EFT for the spin-1/2 Ising particles as,

$$
F_{1 / 2}(x)=\frac{1}{2} \tanh \left[\frac{1}{2} \beta(x+h)\right]
\tag{3}
$$

In Equation (3), $\beta=1/k_\text{B}T_\text{A}$, where $k_\text{B}$ is the Boltzmann's constant, and $T_\text{A}$ denotes the absolute temperature. The reduced temperature, i.e. $T=k_\text{B}T_\text{A}/J$, and the reduced applied field, i.e. $\text{H}=\text{h/J}$, in all the calculations were used. The total magnetisation of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle is in dicated below;

$$
M T_{A B O_{3}}=\frac{1}{15}\left(8 m_{A}+m_{B}+6 m_{O}\right)
\tag{4}
$$

### 3. Calculations

By using Equations (2) and (3), we obtain the temperature and external magnetic field dependences of each component (A, B and O) of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle. We insert the numerical result obtained from Equations (2) and (3) in Equation (4), and we obtain the magnetisation results of the total $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle system. However, by using the exchange interactions ($j_{AA}$, $j_{AO}$, $j_{AB}$, $j_{OO}$, and $j_{OB}$) between components, we obtain the ferromagnetic (if $j > 0$) and antiferromagnetic (if $j < 0$) properties of the total $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle and its every component. The numerical value of all exchange interactions is taken as $j = 1$ for the ferromagnetic case and $j = -1$ for the antiferromagnetic case. In this work, we focus on the edge and surface antiferromagnetism. Therefore, for the ferromagnetic properties, all exchange interactions are taken as $j_{AA}=j_{AO}=j_{AB}=j_{OO}=j_{OB}=1$, for the edge antiferromagnetism only $j_{AA}=-1$, and the surface antiferromagnetism only $j_{AO}=-1$. So, any changes in the value of the temperature, external field, and exchange interactions directly give us these effects on the magnetisation of the total $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle and its each component (Equations (2)-(4)). The edge and surface antiferromagnetism effects on the total $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle and its each component are given in Section 4.

### 4. Results and discussion

Firstly, the magnetizations ($m_A$, $m_B$ and $m_O$) versus the temperature (M-T curves) of the $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle and its components (A, B and O) were investigated. In Figure 2(a), M-T curves are obtained for the ferromagnetic (FM) case (all of the exchange interactions are positive, $j > 0$). The critical temperature is obtained at $\text{Tc}=2.59$. The $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle and its components exhibit ferromagnetic behaviours at $T < \text{Tc}$ and paramagnetic behaviours at $T > \text{Tc}$. Because of the Lattice Location Effect (LLE), discovered by Duran [72,73], The magnetisation of the A atoms is lower than that of the B and O. The central B atoms have the highest magnetisation of all the others.

In Figure 2(b), M-T curves are obtained for the Edge AntiFerroMagnetism (EAFM) case (only the exchange interactions of the edge atoms are negative, $j_{AA}<0$). The critical temperature is obtained at $\text{Tc}=2.19$. The magnetisation of the edge atoms (A) and the total $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle distinctly decreases. On the other hand, the magnetisation of the surface atoms (O) and the central B atom has no fluctuations due to the EAFM.

In Figure 2(c), M-T curves are obtained for the Surface AntiFerroMagnetism (SAFM) case (only the exchange interactions of the surface atoms are negative, $j_{AO}<0$). ## 5. Conclusion

The $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle and its components (A, B and O) are modelled and their magnetizations versus the temperature and external field are investigated by using the Effective Field Theory (EFT) developed by Kaneyoshi. It is found that;

i The central B atoms have a higher magnetisation than those of A and O. This result implicates the lattice location effects discovered by Duran [72,73].

ii The critical temperature is obtained at $Tc=2.59$, $2.19$ and $2.05$ for the ferromagnetic case, the edge antiferromagnetic case and the surface antiferromagnetic case, respectively.

iii The total $ABO_3$ perovskite-type nanoparticle exhibits compensation behaviour that is obtained at $T_{Comp}=1.23$ for the surface antiferromagnetism.

iv Surface antiferromagnetism causes multiple coercive field points and triple magnetic hysteresis behaviour.

v It is suggested that the compensations, the multiple coercive field points, and the triple magnetic hysteresis behaviour strongly depend on the surface antiferromagnetism.
