# Reaction-field approximation for the semi-infinite Ising ferromagnet with surface amorphization: phase diagrams

V. Ilkovič *

Department of Theoretical Physics and Geophysics, Faculty of Natural Sciences, P.J. Šafarik University, 040 01 Košice Moyzesova 16,
Slovak Republic

Received 13 October 1995; accepted for publication 21 March 1996

## Abstract

Using a reaction-field approximation that includes non-trivial spin fluctuation effects, we have studied the magnetic properties of the semi-infinite simple cubic spin-1/2 Ising ferromagnet with a free surface and with three different exchange parameters ($J_S$ couples surface spins, $J_1$ is the exchange interaction between spins in the surface and in the first (next) layer, and $J_B$ is the interaction in the bulk); $J_S$ and $J_1$ are assumed to be randomly distributed. We find a number of characteristic behaviours for the surface magnetic properties such as the possibility of the surface re-entrant phenomenon.

Keywords: Ising models; Phase transitions; Amorphous surfaces

## 1. Introduction

Magnetic behaviour near a surface (or interface) in a magnetically ordered solid may differ in many respects from that inside. The effects of surfaces on phase transitions have attracted much attention and have been studied by using a variety of approx- imations and mathematical techniques [1-10]. In the early stage of the investigations Mills [1] assumed a model in which the spins in the free surface interact with the exchange parameter $J_S$ which is different from the bulk exchange $J_B$. For this simple model with modified exchange only on the surface, it was pointed out on the basis of the mean-field approximation (MFA) that for $J_S$ greater than a critical value $J_S^c$ the system would order on the surface before it orders in the bulk.

When Mills [1] first calculated $J_S^c$, he expressed reservation about the validity of MFA since it completely neglects fluctuations. With the advent of the richer phase diagrams afforded by this system, it is perhaps not inappropriate to use methods in which spin fluctuations are taken into account and to ask the question: how will phase diagram be affected by fluctuations?

At the same time, it is also interesting to cover a large surface with an amorphous layer, since the formation of an amorphous layer on a surface may be effective in improving the mechanical, magnetic and corrosive resistance of a material with a free crystalline surface. Some recent works [11,12] indicate that surface (or interface) amorphization actually happens in some magnetic materials and gives rise to interesting magnetic phenomena.

## 2. Method of calculation

The reduced Hamiltonian (reduced means divided by $k_{\mathrm{B}} T=\beta^{-1}$) of the system is given by
$$-\beta H=\sum_{\langle i j\rangle} K_{i j} S_{i}^{z} S_{j}^{z},$$
where $S_{i(j)}^{z}$ takes the values $\pm 1$ and summation is carried out only over nearest-neighbor pairs of spins. The interatomic exchange interaction $K_{i j}=\beta J_{i j}$ has the value $\tilde{K}_{S}=\beta \tilde{J}_{S}>0$ if both spins lie on the surface, the value $\tilde{K}_{1}=\beta \tilde{J}_{1}>0$ if one is on the surface and its nearest-neighbour is on the next (first) layer and the bulk value $K_{\mathrm{B}}=\beta J_{\mathrm{B}}>0$ otherwise. Here, $\tilde{K}_{S}$ and $\tilde{K}_{1}$ are randomly distributed according to independent probability distribution functions $P(\tilde{K}_{S})$ and $P(\tilde{K}_{1})$. Following Ref. [15] a self-consistent RFA for the surface of the semi-infinite system can be obtained as follows. As Fig. 1a illustrates (a spin $S^{z}$ on the surface is surrounded by reaction fields $m_{S}(S^{z})=a_{S}+S^{z} b_{S}$ and $m_{1}^{-}(S^{z})=a_{1}^{-}+S^{z} b_{1}^{-}$ ),, the surface order parameter is obtained as

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{S} & =\left\langle S^{z}\right\rangle_{r} \\
& =\left\langle\frac{\sum_{S^{z}} S^{z} \exp \left[4 \tilde{K}_{S} m_{S}\left(S^{z}\right) S^{z}+\tilde{K}_{1} m_{1}^{-}\left(S^{z}\right) S^{z}\right]}{\sum_{S^{z}} \exp \left[4 \tilde{K}_{S} m_{S}\left(S^{z}\right) S^{z}+\tilde{K}_{1} m_{1}^{-}\left(S^{z}\right) S^{z}\right]}\right\rangle_{r} \\
& =\left\langle\tanh \left[4 \tilde{K}_{S} a_{S}+\tilde{K}_{1} a_{1}^{-}\right]\right\rangle_{r}. \quad (2)
\end{aligned}
$$

The symbol $\langle\ldots\rangle_{r}$ denotes the random bond average. To get an explicit result out of this, we should to specify $a_{S}, a_{1}^{-}$. This is done self-consistently as follows: $m_{S}(S^{z})=\langle\sigma\rangle S^{z}, m_{1}^{-}(S^{z})=\langle\sigma\rangle S^{z}$, a constrained average calculated according to the illustration in Figs. $1 \mathrm{~b}$ and $1 \mathrm{c}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
& m_{S}\left(S^{z}\right)=\tanh \left[4 \tilde{K}_{S} a_{S}+\tilde{K}_{1} a_{1}^{-}+\epsilon \tilde{K}_{S}\left(S^{z}-a_{S}\right)\right], \\
& m_{1}^{-}\left(S^{z}\right)=\tanh \\
& \quad ×\left[4 K_{\mathrm{B}} a_{1}+K_{\mathrm{B}} a_{2}^{-}+\tilde{K}_{1} a_{S}^{+}+\epsilon \tilde{K}_{1}\left(S^{z}-a_{S}^{+}\right)\right].(4)
\end{aligned}
$$

We introduced a small parameter $\epsilon$ and used the reaction-field correction as perturbation. The above equations involve $m_{S}^{+}(S^{z}), m_{1}(S^{z})$ etc., which

etc.

Using Eqs. (3)-(6) to order $\epsilon$ (we replace the function and its derivative at $\epsilon=0$ in the Taylor expansion by their mean values), taking into account Eq. (2) and the equation for the site magnetization of the first (next) layer,

$$
m_{1}=\left\langle\tanh \left[4 K_{\mathrm{B}} a_{1}+\tilde{K}_{1} a_{S}^{+}+K_{\mathrm{B}} a_{2}^{-}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{r}}, \quad(7)
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
& m_{S}\left(S^{z}\right)=m_{S}+\epsilon \tilde{K}_{S}\left(1-m_{S}^{2}\right)\left(S^{z}-a_{S}\right)+O\left(\epsilon^{2}\right), \\

& m_{1}^{-}\left(S^{z}\right)=m_{1}+\epsilon \tilde{K}_{1}\left(1-m_{1}^{2}\right)\left(S^{z}-\mathrm{a}_{S}^{+}\right)+O\left(\epsilon^{2}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the relation $a_{i}=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{S z} m_{i}\left(S^{z}\right)$, we get in the first order in $\epsilon$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{S} & =m_{S} /\left[1+\epsilon \tilde{K}_{S}\left(1-m_{S}^{2}\right)\right], \\
a_{1} & =m_{1} /\left[1+\epsilon K_{\mathrm{B}}\left(1-m_{1}^{2}\right)\right], \\
a_{S}^{+} & =m_{S} /\left[1+\epsilon \tilde{K}_{1}\left(1-m_{S}^{2}\right)\right], a_{1}^{-} \\
& =m_{1} /\left[1+\epsilon \tilde{K}_{1}\left(1-m_{1}^{2}\right)\right], \\
a_{2}^{-} & =m_{2} /\left[1+\epsilon K_{\mathrm{B}}\left(1-m_{2}^{2}\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

From Eq. (2) and Eq. (7), at $\epsilon=1$, we obtain finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
& m_{S}= \\
& \left\langle\tanh \left[\frac{4 m_{S} \tilde{K}_{S}}{1+\tilde{K}_{S}\left(1-m_{S}^{2}\right)}+\frac{m_{1} \tilde{K}_{1}}{1+\tilde{K}_{1}\left(1-m_{1}^{2}\right)}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{r}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For the site magnetization $m_{1}$ of the first (next) layer we get,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{1}= & \left\langle\tanh \left[\frac{4 m_{1} K_{\mathrm{B}}}{1+K_{\mathrm{B}}\left(1-m_{1}^{2}\right)}\right.\right. \\
& \left.\left.+\frac{m_{S} \tilde{K}_{1}}{1+\tilde{K}_{1}\left(1-m_{S}^{2}\right)}+\frac{m_{2} K_{\mathrm{B}}}{1+K_{\mathrm{B}}\left(1-m_{2}^{2}\right)}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{r}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, the site magnetization $m_{n}$ of the $n$-th layer is given by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{n}= & \tanh \left[\frac{4 m_{n} K_{\mathrm{B}}}{1+K_{\mathrm{B}}\left(1-m_{n}^{2}\right)}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{m_{n-1} K_{\mathrm{B}}}{1+K_{\mathrm{B}}\left(1-m_{n-1}^{2}\right)}+\frac{m_{n+1} K_{\mathrm{B}}}{1+K_{\mathrm{B}}\left(1-m_{n+1}^{2}\right)}\right].(10)
\end{aligned}
$$

The bulk transition temperature $T^{b}{ }_{C}$ can be determined by putting $m_{n}=m_{n-1}=m_{n+1}=m_{b}$ (bulk magnetization) into Eq. (10) that we can linearize. This procedure yields the following value for a simple cubic lattice: $k_{\mathrm{B}} T^{\mathrm{b}}{ }_{\mathrm{C}} / J_{\mathrm{B}}=5.0$. To obtain the thermal behaviours of the magnetization $m_{i}$ in the $i$-th atomic layer, it is necessary to solve coupled Eqs. (8-10). Using the ten-layer approximation, in which we assume that the magnetization remains unaltered after the tenth layer, namely, $m_{9}=m_{10}=... m_{n}=m_{b}$, the surface magnetization can be obtained with enough precision.

To evaluate the random bond averages, it is necessary to provide appropriate forms of the probability distribution functions $P(\tilde{K}_{S})$ and $P(\tilde{K}_{1})$ describing the structural disorder in a simple way. The structural disorder is replaced by the random distribution $\Delta \tilde{K}_{\alpha}$ ( $\alpha=S$ or 1) of the exchange interaction, putting $\tilde{K}_{\alpha}=K_{\alpha}+\Delta K_{\alpha}$, which is called the lattice model of amorphous magnets [16], $(K_{\alpha}=\beta J_{\alpha}$ is the mean value of $\tilde{K}_{\alpha})$. In most of the cases discussed [16-22] a discrete Handrich-Kaneyoshi (H-K) distribution for the exchange integrals

$$
\begin{aligned}
& P\left(\tilde{K}_{\alpha}\right)= \\
& \quad \frac{1}{2}\left[\delta\left(\tilde{K}_{\alpha}-K_{\alpha}-\Delta K_{\alpha}\right)+\delta\left(\tilde{K}_{\alpha}-K_{\alpha}+\Delta K_{\alpha}\right)\right] \quad(11)
\end{aligned}
$$

has been assumed. Now, to solve Eqs. (8)-(10) numerically, we introduce the following parame-

ters:
$$t=\frac{K_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{c}}}{K_{\mathrm{B}}}=\frac{1}{5 K_{\mathrm{B}}}, \Delta_{S} \text { and } \Delta_{1} ;$$
where $\Delta_{S}$ is a surface coupling enhancement $(K_{S}=K_{B}(1+\Delta_{S}))$ and $\Delta_{1}$ is a coupling enhancement between the surface and first (next) layer $(K_{1}=K_{B}(1+\Delta_{1}))$ in the lattice model of amorphous magnets.

### 3. Phase diagrams

In the following we would like to present our results for the phase diagrams (or for the surface Curie temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{s}}$ ). Near the critical point we can linearize Eqs. (8)-(10) and we find,
$$m_{S}=4 m_{S} A_{S}+m_{1} A_{1},\qquad(13)$$

$$m_{1}=\frac{1}{5 t+1}\left(4 m_{1}+m_{2}\right)+m_{S} A_{1},\qquad(14)$$

$$m_{n}=\frac{1}{5 t+1}\left(4 m_{n}+m_{n-1}+m_{n+1}\right) \text { for } n \geq 2, \quad(15)$$
with
$$A_{S}=\left\langle\frac{\tilde{K}_{S}}{1+\tilde{K}_{S}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{r}},$$

$$A_{1}=\left\langle\frac{\tilde{K}_{1}}{1+\tilde{K}_{1}}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{r}}.$$
The coupled equations can be solved by defining the transfer function,
$$m_{n+1} / m_{n}=a \text { for } n \geq 1.$$

Substituting it into Eq. (13) and Eq. (14) we obtain the following secular equation,
$$\tilde{M}\left(\begin{array}{l}
m_{S} \\
m_{1}
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{cc}
1-4 A_{S} & A_{1} \\
A_{1} & 1-\frac{4+a}{5 t+1}
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{l}
m_{S} \\
m_{1}
\end{array}\right)=0.$$

The parameter $a$ is then given (upon using Eq. (15)) by:
$$a=-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{5}{2} t-\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{5(t-1)(t-1 / 5)}.$$

The critical ferromagnetic frontiers can be derived from the condition $\tilde{M}=0$, namely,
$$\left(4 A_{S}-1\right)\left[\frac{4+a}{5 t+1}-1\right]=A_{1}^{2}.$$

The random bond averages of $A_{S}$ and $A_{1}$ are then given by:
$$A_{S(1)}=$$

$$\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1+\Delta_{S(1)}+D_{S(1)}}{5 t+1+\Delta_{S(1)}+D_{S(1)}}+\frac{1+\Delta_{S(1)}-D_{S(1)}}{5 t+1+\Delta_{S(1)}-D_{S(1)}}\right],$$
where we defined the dimensionless disorder parameters,
$$D_{\alpha}=\frac{\Delta K_{\alpha}}{K_{\mathrm{B}}}=\frac{\Delta J_{\alpha}}{J_{\mathrm{B}}} \text { for } \alpha=s \text { or } 1,$$
which measure the amount of fluctuations of exchange interaction if both spins lie on the surface $(\alpha=S)$ and if one is on the surface and its nearest neighbour is on the first (next) layer $(\alpha=1)$.

Within the framework of the standard MFA the critical frontier becomes independent of $D_{\alpha} ;(\alpha=S$ or 1), when one takes the random bond average in terms of Eq. (11). As shown in the following, in present formalism the surface transition temperature is clearly dependent on surface/interface structural fluctuations $D_{\alpha}$.

For a fixed value of $K_S$ it is useful to introduce the dimensionless disorder parameter $\delta_S=$ $\Delta K_S/K_S$ which measure the amount of fluctuations of exchange interaction. The parameter $\delta_S$ is often called the structural fluctuation in amorphous magnets [16]. When $\delta_S>1$, the random exchange interaction $\tilde{K}_{\varkappa}$ can take positive and negative values with equal probability and hence frustration is set in the system. On the other hand, when we shall restrict $\delta_S$ in the region $0\leq\delta_S<1$, the effect of frustration due to random exchange interaction in sign does not appear in the system. The value $\delta_S=1.0$ corresponds to the dilution problem.

In an amorphous bulk ferromagnet when the structural fluctuations $\delta=\Delta K_B/K_B$ become larger, the re-entrant phenomenon may apear [21]. Therefore, it is interesting to investigate whether the surface re-entrant phenomenon is possible or not, when the surface structural fluctuation $\delta_S$ increases. We see that no surface re-entrant phenomenon is possible for small surface enhancement $\Delta_S$ (curve a, b and c). For larger $\Delta_S$ (curve d) when the surface struc- tural fluctuations $\delta_S>1$ re-entrant phenomenon is possible.

## 4. Conclusion

In this work we have studied the phase diagrams of the semi-infinite simple-cubic spin-1/2 Ising ferromagnet with a surface amorphization within the reaction field approximation. We have calculated the phase diagrams in $(\Delta_{S}, T)$ space by using the parameters of the surface/interface amorphization $D_{\alpha}=\Delta J_{\alpha}/J_{B}$, $(\alpha=S,1)$ in opposition to the work of Kaneyoshi [19] where were used the parameters $\delta_{\alpha}=\Delta J_{\alpha}/J_{\alpha}$. For large $\Delta_{S}$ the curves characterising the dependence of $T^{s}_{C}$ on $\Delta_{S}$ with increasing $D_{S}$ are convergent, whereas Kaneyoshi [19], by using the parameter of amorphization $\delta_{S}=\Delta J_{S}/J_{S}$, obtained for $\delta_{S}=0.0$ and $\delta_{S}=0.5$ divergent curves. We think that using the parameter $\delta_{S}$ for determi- nation of the phase diagram in $(\Delta_{S}, T)$ space is incorrect, because this parameter involves the inde- pendent variable $\Delta_{S}$. More correctly is to use the parameter $D_{S}=\Delta J_{S}/J_{B}$.

We have shown that the surface re-entrant phe- nomenon is possible and this phenomenon is strongly influenced by the amorphization of the first (next) layer, when $K_{1}$ and $K_{S}$ become larger than the bulk value $K_{B}$. The amorphization of the first (next) layer emphasizes the re-entrant phenom- enon. Experimentally, however, it may not be so easy to observe this phenomenon in the semi- infinite systems since $K_{S}$ must be taken as a value larger than $5K_{B}$ [10]. We can conclude that spin fluctuations included in the RFA do not signifi- cantly change the results concerning the re-entrant phenomenon of the improved mean-field theory respecting the identity $S^{2}=1$ [19] and of the finite- cluster renormalization method [10]; of course, we have obtained in part some results beyond the scope of these works and in part the phase dia- grams in $(\Delta_{S}, T)$ and $(\Delta_{1}, T)$ space using other (in our opinion more correct) parameters of amorphi- zation i.e. parameters $D_{\alpha}=\Delta J_{\alpha}/J_{B}$ and no $\delta_{\alpha}=\Delta J_{\alpha}/J_{\alpha}$.

We can obtain phase diagrams for different systems by simply varying local physical quantities like the coordination number, interface structure and surface magnetic moments. It seems that by increasing the surface magnetic moments the re-entrant phenomenon may be possible for smaller values of $K_{S}$.

The above formalism can be applied to investi- gate other related problems, for example, the prob- lem of the semi-infinite ferromagnet with a spin-

one free surface, ferromagnetic thin films and other inhomogeneous systems.
