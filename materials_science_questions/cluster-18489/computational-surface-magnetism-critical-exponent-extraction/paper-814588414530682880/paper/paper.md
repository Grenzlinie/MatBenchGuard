**Magnetic properties of a hexagonal transverse Ising nanoisland**

Lu Zhao-Xin*

College of Mechanical Engineering, Linyi University, Linyi, China

(Received 15 May 2015; accepted 27 July 2015)

The magnetic properties of a hexagonal transverse Ising nanoisland have been calculated by the effective-field theory with correlations. The effects of surface coupling, interlayer coupling, and the competition between transverse fields at the surface shell and in the inner core on phase diagrams and magnetizations are examined. Meanwhile, the results are also compared with two different nanoislands which consist of two layers with nine atoms or five atoms in each of the layers.

**Keywords:** nanoislands; phase diagrams; magnetizations; transverse Ising model

**PACS:** 75.60.Ej; 78.67.Ch; 78.67.Uh; 77.80.B-

### 1. Introduction
In recent decades, various magnetic nanomaterials have been widely studied experimentally and theoretically due to their potential applications in technology and biomedicine, such as nanoparticles,[1–5] nanoislands,[6–10] nanofilms,[11–15] nanowires,[16–23] and nanotubes.[24–28] The magnetic properties of nanomaterials are significantly influenced by the distinct contributions of inside, perimeter, and surface atoms. The perimeter and surface atoms govern the magnetism of nanomaterials. Experimentally, the nanoscaled magnetic materials with a few atoms, such as Fe nanoislands and FeRh nanoislands, have been successfully fabricated.[6,7] From the practical applications point of view, the hysteresis is usually examined in various nanoscaled magnetic materials, since the hysteresis loop is important for the manufacture of magnetic recording media. From the academic point of view, however, the transition temperature and magnetization are the focus of research. Theoretically, the effective-field theory (EFT) with correlations, based on Ising spin identities and the differential operator technique, has been thought to be a good candidate to examine the properties of various nanomaterials. The EFT with correlations has been successfully used to study the hexagonal type Ising nanowires.[22,29–32] The magnetic properties of nanomaterials are strongly influenced by surface effect and atomic number as well as degrees of closed pack in their structures. In order to inspect the inherent relation among the interaction parameters, the nanoislands with three different structures are proposed. Recently, the phase diagrams and magnetizations in a transverse Ising nanoisland with two types of different structures which consist of two layers with nine atoms or five atoms in each of the layers have been examined by using the EFT with correlations.[8,9] Very recently, the magnetic properties of a hexagonal transverse Ising nanoisland have been studied by using the EFT with correlations.[10] However, the dependence of temperature on surface coupling, interlayer coupling, as

## 2. Model and formulations
In this work, the hexagonal nanoisland is assumed to consist of a three-dimensional arrangement of pseudo-spins. As shown in Figure 1(A), the hexagonal nanoisland is con- stituted with the surface shell (black circles) and the inner core (white circles). It is obvi- ous that the inner core is surrounded by the surface shell. The Hamiltonian is [10]

$$
H=-J_{s} \sum_{(i j)} \sigma_{i}^{z} \sigma_{j}^{z}-J \sum_{(m n)} \sigma_{m}^{z} \sigma_{n}^{z}-J_{r} \sum_{(i m)} \sigma_{i}^{z} \sigma_{m}^{z}-\Omega_{s} \sum_{(i)} \sigma_{i}^{x}-\Omega_{s} \sum_{(m)} \sigma_{m}^{x},
\tag{1}
$$

where $\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}$ ($\alpha = x$ or $z$) is the Pauli spin operator with the value of $\sigma_{i}^{z}=\pm 1$. $\Omega_{s}$ (or $\Omega_{b}$) is the transverse field at the surface shell (or in the inner core). $J_{s}$ is the exchange interaction connecting the magnetic atom on the surface shell. $J$ is the exchange interaction between the magnetic atom on the surface shell and the center atom in the inner core. $J_{r}$ is the exchange interaction connecting each of magnetic atoms on the upper layer with the cor- responding magnetic atom in the lower layer. This choice corresponds to the experimental facts in [33,34] which has been discussed in [14]. The first $(ij)$ and second $(mn)$ terms in Hamiltonian (1) represent the contributions from the surface shell and the inner core, respectively. Thus, the magnetic properties of nanoislands are sensitively influenced by different lattice structures, namely hexagonal, square, and triangular structures. In order to differentiate the effect of the surface shell on the physical properties of hexagonal nanoisland from the inner core, the surface exchange interaction $J_{s}$ can be defined as

$$
J_{s}=J(1+\triangle s).
\tag{2}
$$

Within the framework of the EFT with correlations,[8–10] the longitudinal magnet- izations $m_{s}$ ($m_{s}=\langle\sigma_{i}^{z}\rangle$) on the surface shell and the longitudinal magnetizations $m_{c}$ ($m_{c}=\langle\sigma_{m}^{z}\rangle$) in the center core for the hexagonal nanoisland in Figure 1(A) can be obtained as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{s}= & {[\cosh (A)+m_{s} \sinh (A)]^{2}[\cosh (C)+m_{c} \sinh (C)] } \\
& \left.\times\left[\cosh (B)+m_{s} \sinh (B)\right] f_{s}(x)\right|_{x=0},
\tag{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
m_{c}=\left.[\cosh (C)+m_{s} \sinh (C)]^{6}[\cosh (B)+m_{c} \sinh (B)] f(x)\right|_{x=0},
\tag{4}
$$

where $A=J_{s} D$, $B=J_{r} D$, $C=J D$, and $D=\partial / \partial x$ which is the differential operator. The

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_s(x) &= \frac{x}{y_s}\tanh(\beta y_s) \\
f(x) &= \frac{x}{y}\tanh(\beta y)
\end{aligned}
,\tag{5}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}

y &= \sqrt{x^2 + {\Omega_b}^2}
\end{aligned}
,\tag{6}
$$

where $\beta=1/k_B T$ , $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the temperature.

## 3. Phase diagrams

When the temperature is close to the Curie temperature $T_c$, the right-hand sides of Equations (3) and (4) can be expanded.[8–10] At this moment, the values of $m_s$ and $m_c$ will tend to zero and the values of higher order terms of $m_s$ and $m_c$ is faster than the corresponding linear terms. Therefore, we can neglect the higher order terms and only consider the linear terms. The transition temperature $T_c$ is determined by

$$[1-(2k_1+k_2)](1-k_5)-6k_3k_4=0,\tag{7}$$

where the coefficients $k_n$ ($n=1-5$) are

$$
\begin{aligned}
k_1 &= \sinh(A)\cosh(A)\cosh(B)\cosh(C)f_s(x)\big|_{x=0} \\
k_2 &= \cosh^2(A)\sinh(B)\cosh(C)f_s(x)\big|_{x=0} \\
k_3 &= \cosh^2(A)\cosh(B)\sinh(C)f_s(x)\big|_{x=0} \\
k_4 &= \cosh(B)\sinh(C)\cosh^5(C)f(x)\big|_{x=0} \\
k_5 &= \sinh(B)\cosh^6(C)f(x)\big|_{x=0}
\end{aligned}
.\tag{8}
$$

These coefficients $k_n$ can be calculated by a mathematical relation $e^{\alpha D}f(x)=f(x+\alpha)$.
In order to compare with references,[8–10] we also define the following four parameters $t,r,h,q$:

$$t=\frac{k_B T}{J},\quad r=\frac{J_r}{J},\quad h=\frac{\Omega_b}{J},\quad q=\frac{\Omega_s}{\Omega_b}.\tag{9}$$

### 4. Magnetizations
The total longitudinal (or transverse) magnetization $m_T = m_T^z$ (or $m_T^x$) per site of the hexagonal nanoisland in Figure 1(A) is defined as [10]

$$
m_T^\alpha = \frac{6m_s^\alpha + m_c^\alpha}{7}, \tag{10}
$$

where $\alpha = z$ (or $x$).

![](./images/814588414530682880_14.jpg)

Figure 5. Variations of saturation magnetizations, namely $m_T$ at $t = 0.0$ in the three nanoislands with $\triangle s = 0.0, h = 1.0$, and $q = 1.0$ are plotted as a function of $r$.

Figure 5 shows the saturation values of $m_T$ in the hexagonal nanoisland (or Figure 1 (A)) with $t=0.0, \bigtriangleup s=0.0, h=1.0$ , and $q=1.0$ as a function of $r$ so as to clarify what value may cause the frustration from the results of Figures 3 and 4. As we can see from Figure 5, first the $m_T \sim r$ curve takes the saturation value of $m_T=0.934$, then it begins to increase from the saturation value at $r=0.0$ and shows a broad maximum, and finally it decreases from the maximum and reduces to zero at a critical value of $r$. Obviously, it is also noticed that the critical value $r_{c A}=6.335$ is the same as that in the corresponding curve in Figure 3(b). Meanwhile, the critical values $(r_{c B}=7.183, r_{c C}=8.305)$ for Figure 1(B) and 1(C) are also equal to those of Figure 3(b), respectively.

Figure 6 shows the temperature dependence of $m_T$ in the hexagonal nanoisland with $\bigtriangleup s=0.0, h=1.0, q=1.0$, when the four typical values of $r$ are selected from Figure 1 (A) by solving the coupled Equations (3) and (4) numerically. The $m_T$ curve labeled $r=1.0$ exhibits the normal behavior and it takes the saturation value $m_T=0.968$ at $T=0.0$ K, then it decreases with increase of $T$, and finally reduces to zero at the same $T_c$ as the corresponding value in Figure 3(b). But, the $m_T$ curve labeled $r=4.0$ and $r=7.0$ starts from the saturation values, $m_T=0.934$ and $m_T=0.584$, respectively, shows a broad maximum and then reduces to zero at the corresponding values of $T_c$. The $m_T$ curve labeled $r=10.0$ clearly shows the reentrant phenomenon, since it starts to take a finite value at the lower transition temperature $T_{C L}$ and reduces to zero at the higher transition temperature $T_{C H}$. The values of $T_{C L}$ and $T_{C H}$ are also equivalent to the corresponding values in Figure 3(b).

## 5. Conclusion
In this work, the phase diagram and magnetization in the hexagonal nanoisland have been investigated by using the EFT with correlations. The dependence of temperature on the surface coupling $\bigtriangleup s$, interlayer coupling $r$, as well as on the competition between transverse fields at the surface shell and in the inner core $q$ has been calculated. Meanwhile, the dependence of magnetizations on the interlayer coupling $r$ and the transition temperature $t$ has also been examined. The results are also compared with two different
