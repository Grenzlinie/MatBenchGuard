# Surface phase diagrams of a spin-one monolayer on a semi-infinite spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ Ising ferromagnet

T. Kaneyoshi

Department of Physics, Nagoya University, 464-01 Nagoya, Japan

Received 7 January 1991

Surface phase diagrams of a spin-one monolayer on a semi-infinite spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ ferromagnet are investigated by using a version of the effective-field theory. In particular, the effects of the surface biquadratic exchange interaction on the surface phase diagram are discussed. Some characteristic features of the surface magnetism are found, such as the surface tricritical behavior and the surface reentrant phenomena.

## 1. Introduction

The magnetic behavior of semi-infinite solids has been extensively investi- gated for many years. In particular, the semi-infinite simple cubic ferromag- netic Ising system with $S=\frac{1}{2}$ ($S$ is the spin magnitude) has received much attention [1]. The presence of the surface may modify the interactions in the surface layers. If the surface coupling is stronger than that in the bulk, it is possible to have an ordering of the surface in the presence of a disordered bulk. The phenomenon is confirmed experimentally in some semi-infinite magnetic systems [2].

On the other hand, some new magnetic materials have been very recently fabricated by growing a few atomic layers epitaxially on a magnetic substrate, such as the Gd or Tb monolayers on a Fe (or Ni) substrate [3]. In these systems, the magnitude of the spins on the surface is different from that in the bulk. For these systems we had proposed a semi-infinite Ising system with a $S=1$ free surface different from $S=\frac{1}{2}$ in the bulk and the influences of a surface single-ion anisotropy on the surface magnetic properties were examined by the use of the effective-field theory (FET) [4-6]. In fact, it is known that a very strong anisotropy field acting on spins in the surface exists. This anisot- ropy is due to various causes associated with the material and its structure as well as the presence of the surface. The role of surface anisotropy may be of paramount importance in determining the magnetic properties.

The purpose of this work is to study the surface phase diagrams of the spin-one free surface (or the Blume-Emery-Griffiths (BEG) monolayer) on a semi-infinite spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ Ising system within a new framework of the EFT in relation to the previous works [4-6]. The BEG model is a spin-one Ising system with both bilinear and biquadratic interactions in which a single-ion uniaxial anisotropy is included [7]. The model has received much attention and has been studied extensively by using various techniques because of the fundamental role in the multicritical phenomena associated with physical systems such as $^3$He-$^4$He mixtures, multicomponent fluids, ternary alloys, and metamagnets [8]. The semi-infinite BEG model has been also investigated in the mean-field approximation, in order to clarify surface superfluidity in the mixtures of $^3$He and $^4$He adsorbed on graphite [9]. As far as we know, however, the surface phase diagrams of the present system have not been examined, which may have some experimental relevance.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In section 2, we briefly review the basic points of our theoretical framework (EFT) and expressions for evaluating the surface phase diagram are derived. In section 3, the numerical results are studied paying especial attention to the effects of biquadratic interaction on the surface phase diagram. We find some characteristic features of the surface magnetism.

## 2. Formulation

We consider a semi-infinite simple cubic spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ system with a spin-one (100) free surface which is described by the following Hamiltonian:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & -J_{\mathrm{s}} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle} S_{i}^{z} S_{j}^{z}-J_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle}\left(S_{i}^{z} S_{j}^{z}\right)^{2}-D_{\mathrm{s}} \sum_{i}\left(S_{i}^{z}\right)^{2} \\
& -J_{1} \sum_{\langle i, m\rangle} S_{i}^{z} \mu_{m}^{z}-J \sum_{\langle m, n\rangle} \mu_{m}^{z} \mu_{n}^{z},
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

where the spin variable $S_{i}^{z}$ takes the values $\pm 1$ and $0$, $\mu_{m}^{z}$ can be $\pm 1$, and the summations are carried out only over nearest-neighbor pairs of spins. $J_{\mathrm{s}}$ and $J_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}$ are the bilinear and biquadratic interactions between spins at site $i$ and $j$ on the surface. $D_{\mathrm{s}}$ is the surface single-ion anisotropy parameter. $J_{1}$ is the exchange interaction between a spin on the surface and its nearest-neighbor spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ and $J$ is the bulk exchange interaction. Fig. 1 shows a two-dimensional cross section of the system.

The problem is now the evaluation of the mean values $\langle S_{i}^{z}\rangle$, $\langle(S_{i}^{z})^{2}\rangle$ and $\langle\mu_{m}^{z}\rangle$. As discussed in the previous works [4-6], when $J_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}=0$, we can use the

Fig. 1. Part of a two-dimensional cross section through a semi-infinite Ising lattice. Black points denote the lattice points that are occupied by spins $\mu_{m}^{z}= \pm 1$. On the (100) surface the lattice points are occupied by $S_{i}^{z}= \pm 1$ and 0.

formal identities for the spin correlation functions in the Ising models [10]. On the other hand, when $J_{s}' \neq 0$, other identities for the BEG model derived recently by Fittipaldi and Kaneyoshi [11] have to be applied for the spins on the surface. In the same way as in the previous works, within the framework of the EFT introducing the following decoupling:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\langle S_{i}^{z}\left(S_{j}^{z}\right)^{2} \cdots \mu_{m}^{z}\right\rangle & \cong\left\langle S_{i}^{z}\right\rangle\left\langle\left(S_{j}^{z}\right)^{2}\right\rangle \cdots\left\langle\mu_{m}^{z}\right\rangle, \\
\left\langle\mu_{m}^{z} \mu_{n}^{z} \cdots \mu_{l}^{z}\right\rangle & \cong\left\langle\mu_{m}^{z}\right\rangle\left\langle\mu_{n}^{z}\right\rangle \cdots\left\langle\mu_{l}^{z}\right\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

with $i \neq j \neq \cdots \neq m$ and $m \neq n \neq \cdots \neq l$, the surface magnetization for the spin-one (100) surface (or the BEG molayer) can be derived as

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathrm{s}}= & \left\langle S_{i=\mathrm{s}}^{z}\right\rangle \\
= & {\left[1+\alpha_{\mathrm{s}} \rho_{\mathrm{s}} m_{\mathrm{s}}+\left(\rho_{\mathrm{s}} \gamma_{\mathrm{s}}-1\right) q_{\mathrm{s}}\right]^{4} } \\
& \times\left.\left[\cosh \left(J_{1} \nabla_{x}\right)+\xi_{1} \sinh \left(J_{1} \nabla_{x}\right)\right] F_{\mathrm{s}}(x, y)\right|_{\substack{x=0 \\
y=0}},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

with

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}=\sinh \left(J_{\mathrm{s}} \nabla_{x}\right), \quad \rho_{\mathrm{s}}=\cosh \left(J_{\mathrm{s}} \nabla_{y}\right), \quad \gamma_{\mathrm{s}}=\exp \left(J_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime} \nabla_{x}\right),
\tag{4}
$$

where $\nabla_{\mu}=(\partial / \partial \mu)$ ($\mu=x, y$) are the two differential operators and $\langle\cdots\rangle$ denotes the standard thermal average. The function $F_{\mathrm{s}}(x, y)$ is defined by

$$
F_{\mathrm{s}}(x, y)=\frac{2 \mathrm{e}^{\beta y} \sinh (\beta x)}{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta D_{\mathrm{s}}}+2 \mathrm{e}^{\beta y} \cosh (\beta x)},
\tag{5}
$$

where $\beta=1 / k_{\mathrm{B}} T$. The parameter $q_{\mathrm{s}}$ defined by $q_{\mathrm{s}}=\left\langle\left(S_{i-\mathrm{s}}^{z}\right)^{2}\right\rangle$ is given by, upon using the same procedure as that for $m_{\mathrm{s}}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{\mathrm{s}}= & {\left[1+\alpha_{\mathrm{s}} \gamma_{\mathrm{s}} m_{\mathrm{s}}+\left(\rho_{\mathrm{s}} \gamma_{\mathrm{s}}-1\right) q_{\mathrm{s}}\right]^{4} } \\
& \times\left.\left[\cosh \left(J_{1} \nabla_{x}\right)+\sigma_{1} \sinh \left(J_{1} \nabla_{x}\right)\right] G_{\mathrm{s}}(x, y)\right|_{\substack{x=0 \\
y=0}},
\end{aligned}
$$

where the function $G_{\mathrm{s}}(x, y)$ is defined by

$$
G_{\mathrm{s}}(x, y)=\frac{2 \mathrm{e}^{\beta y} \cosh (\beta x)}{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta D_{\mathrm{s}}}+2 \mathrm{e}^{\beta y} \cosh (\beta x)}.
$$

Here, the Hamiltonian of the present system is the same as that in the previous works [4-6], if we neglect the biquadratic interaction term in (1). Therefore, the first layer and $n$th layer magnetizations are given by the same equations as those in the previous works [4-6]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{1}= & \left\langle\mu_{m=1}^{z}\right\rangle \\
= & {\left[\cosh (J \nabla)+\sigma_{1} \sinh (J \nabla)\right]^{4}\left[q_{\mathrm{s}} \cosh \left(J_{1} \nabla\right)+m_{\mathrm{s}} \sinh \left(J_{1} \nabla\right)+1-q_{\mathrm{s}}\right] } \\
& \times\left.\left[\cosh (J \nabla)+\sigma_{2} \sinh (J \nabla)\right] f(x)\right|_{x=0}
\end{aligned}
$$

and for the $n$th layer $(n \geqq 2)$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{n}= & {\left[\cosh (J \nabla)+\sigma_{n} \sinh (J \nabla)\right]^{4}\left[\cosh (J \nabla)+\sigma_{n-1} \sinh (J \nabla)\right] } \\
& \times\left.\left[\cosh (J \nabla)+\sigma_{n+1} \sinh (J \nabla)\right] f(x)\right|_{x=0},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\sigma_{n-1}$ and $\sigma_{n+1}$ are the magnetizations in the $(n-1)$ th and $(n+1)$ th layers, respectively. The function $f(x)$ is then defined by

$$
f(x)=\tanh (\beta x).
$$

For $n \rightarrow \infty$, the $n$th layer magnetization should approach the bulk magnetization $\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}$. The bulk magnetization is given by, upon substituting $\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{n-1}=$ $\sigma_{n+1}=\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}$ into (9),

$$
\left.\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}=\left[\cosh (J \nabla)+\sigma_{\mathrm{B}} \sinh (J \nabla)\right]^{6} f(x)\right|_{x=0}.
$$

By expanding the rhs of (11) and retaining only the linear terms, we can easily determine the bulk transition temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{b}}$ (see refs. [4-6]), which is given

by

$$
k_{\mathrm{B}} T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{b}} / J=5.073 \text {. } \tag{12}
$$

The results is nothing but that obtained by Zernike by using another method [12].

We are in a position to examine the surface phase diagrams of the present system by using the above coupled equations. At this place, it is worth mentioning the following facts. First, if one puts $J_{1}=0$ in eqs. (3) and (6), these equations reduce to those for the two-dimensional bulk BEG model. The phase diagram and the thermal variation of magnetization in the bulk two- dimensional system (square lattice) have been investigated by Tucker [13]. Next, if one puts $J_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}=0$ in (3) and (6), these equations are equivalent to the corresponding ones in the previous works [4,6]. In this way, the same procedure as that in the previous works can be applied, in order to discuss the surface phase diagram of the present system. In the vicinity of the second-order phase transition line, the surface magnetization can be written as

$$
m_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}=(1-a) / b, \tag{13}
$$

where the parameters $a$ and $b$ are given by

$$
a=4 A_{1}+\lambda A_{2} \tag{14}
$$

and

$$
b=2\left(6 A_{3} q_{\mathrm{s}}^{1}+2 \lambda A_{4} q_{\mathrm{s}}^{1}+3 \lambda A_{5}+2 A_{6}\right). \tag{15}
$$

The parameter $\lambda$ as well as the parameter $a$ are equivalent to those in the previous works [4,6]. The only differences are the coefficients $A_{i}(i=1-6)$ and the expressions for $q_{\mathrm{s}}^{0}$ and $q_{\mathrm{s}}^{1}$. They are given in the appendix.

Now, the rhs of (13) must be positive. If this is not the case, the transition is of first order, and hence the point at which $a=1$ and $b=0$ is the surface tricritical point, if it exists above the bulk transition temperature $T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{b}}$. The second-order surface transition line is then determined by $a=1$.

### 3. Surface phase diagrams

In the present system, therefore, the spin-one overlayer may exhibit some characteristic features found in the bulk two-dimensional (square lattice) BEG model [13], if we take a large value of $J_{s}$.

## 4. Conclusions

A study of the surface phase diagram for the spin-one (BEG) monolayer on the semi-infinite spin-$\frac{1}{2}$Ising ferromagnet has been made by using a version of the effective-field theory. Our discussions have revealed some characteristic features of the surface phase diagram, as shown in figs. 2-6. To our knowl- edge, the effect of a biquadratic interaction $J_{s}^{\prime}$ on the surface phase diagram has not been investigated previously.

Finally, the present system may have some experimental relevance especially for the new magnetic materials fabricated by growing a few atomic layers (including a monolayer) epitaxially on a magnetic substrate. In fact, the temperature dependence of the Auger polarizations for the rare earths Gd and Tb on the transition metal surfaces $\mathrm{Fe}(100)$ and $\mathrm{Ni}(110)$ have been obtained very recently [3]. In these systems the magnitude of the spins on the surface is clearly different from that in the bulk. The results of this work may assist in clarifying the magnetic properties of these systems.
