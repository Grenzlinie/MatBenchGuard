ABSTRACT: Remote carbon−hydrogen activation on titanium dinuclear complexes $[\{Ti(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)R_2\}_2(\mu\text{-}O)]$ [R = $CH_2SiMe_3$ 2, $CH_2CMe_3$ 3, and $CH_2Ph$ 5) have been examined both synthetically and theoretically. While the thermal treatment of the oxoderivative $[\{Ti(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)(CH_2SiMe_3)_2\}_2(\mu\text{-}O)]$ (2) led to a series of metallacycle complexes (2a−c) by sequential carbon−hydrogen activation processes, $[\{Ti(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)(CH_2CMe_3)_2\}_2(\mu\text{-}O)]$ (3) gave rise to the formation of the metallacycle tuck-over species $[Ti_2(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)(\mu\text{-}\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_4CH_2\text{-}\kappa C)(CH_2CMe_3)(\mu\text{-}CH_2CMe_2CH_2)(\mu\text{-}O)]$ (4), as result of hydrogen abstraction from a $\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5$ ligand. However, the thermolysis of the tetrabenzyl complex $[\{Ti(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)(CH_2Ph)_2\}_2(\mu\text{-}O)]$ (5) yielded the derivative $[Ti_2(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)(\mu\text{-}\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_4CH_2\text{-}\kappa C)(CH_2Ph)_3(\mu\text{-}O)]$ (6) that only exhibits tuck-over $\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5$ metalation. DFT calculations show that the mechanism involves a first $\alpha$-hydrogen abstraction to generate a transient titanium alkylidene, which enables it to activate $\beta$- and $\gamma\text{-}C(sp^3)\text{-}H$ bonds on the adjacent titanium center. The calculations also establish a reactivity order for the different type of $\gamma$-H abstractions, trimethylsilyl > neopentyl ≅ benzyl, allowing us to explain the observed selectivity.

## INTRODUCTION

One of the holy grails of chemical synthesis is the activation/functionalization of unreactive, nonacidic $C(sp^3)\text{-}H$ bonds with high efficiency and selectivity. Therefore, the development of synthetic protocols that provides effective access to nonactivated carbon−hydrogen bonds represents a major challenge for chemical community.¹ One approach to transition-metal-mediated C−H activation relies on the use of pre-existing functional groups (directing groups) in the substrate, in order to ensure the selectivity on the activation.¹h,² Directed carbon−hydrogen activation processes classically proceed via five- or six-membered metallacycle intermediates, which are thermodynamically stable and therefore less reactive in the subsequent functionalization step.

The emphasis in most synthetic and mechanistic studies about cyclometalation has been on mononuclear late transition metal systems; processes based on early transition metals are less known.¹d,³ Pioneering results in these latter came from Rothwell et al.⁴ and Bercaw et al.,⁵ who showed that transient alkylidene species ($M{=}CHR$, M = Ta and Ti) could assist in the aryloxide-mediated cyclometalation using high-valent tantalum and in the activation of the pentamethylcyclopentadienyl ligand in complex $[Ti(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)_2Me_2]$ to give the tuck-in compound $[Ti(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)(C_5Me_4CH_2)Me]$, respectively. Although there is now considerable precedent in the literature for cyclometalation processes via transient alkylidene intermediates in early transition metals,⁶ examples of such processes on dimetallic species have not yet been reported.⁷ A better knowledge of such compounds might provide valuable insights about cooperative effects between adjacent metal centers inaccessible in the mononuclear analogous.²b,⁸

In this context, we have recently reported⁹ that the dinuclear titanium complex $[\{Ti(\eta^5\text{-}C_5Me_5)(CH_2SiMe_3)_2\}_2(\mu\text{-}O)]$ (2), comprising sterically demanding (trimethylsilyl)methyl ligands,¹⁰ can undergo sequential $C(sp^3)$-hydrogen bonds activation to give a series of metallacycle complexes. Thus, we also proposed that transient alkylidene species might enable such transformations. The present work extends this study to other very common alkyl ligands in organometllic chemistry, namely, neopentyl ($CH_2CMe_3$) and benzyl ($CH_2Ph$),¹¹ and analyzes theoretically the involvement of alkylidene intermediates in directing the selective activation of $C(sp^3){-}H$ bonds on the adjacent titanium center.

Certainly, it has been established experimentally that the dinuclear oxocomplexes [{Ti($\eta^5$-C₅Me₅)R₂}₂($\mu$-O)] [R = CH₂SiMe₃ 2, CH₂CMe₃ 3, and CH₂Ph 5) can promote the selective intramolecular cleavage of different unactivated $\beta$- and $\gamma$-C(sp³)-H bonds. Due to the importance of this transformation, as we have previously pointed out, it is important to elucidate the detailed reaction mechanism, including the origins of the C–H bond activation pathways for complexes 2, 3, and 5, as well as the formation of “tuck-over” complexes 4 and 6. Therefore, we decided to investigate the details of the reaction mechanism using density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Initially, we focused on the successive intramolecular C–H bond activation reactions of the complex [{Ti($\eta^5$-C₅Me₅)-(CH₂SiMe₃)₂}₂($\mu$-O)] (2) and evaluated the mechanism step by step, as depicted in Scheme 3.

## CONCLUSION
The synthesis of a series of dinuclear titanium complexes $[\{Ti(\eta^5-C_5Me_5)R_2\}_2(\mu-O)]$ has made possible a study of their thermal behavior in solution. Sequential C−H activation is possible in these compounds to form metallacycle and/or "tuck-over" species. DFT calculations have proven that the mechanistic pathway of such transformations involves a two- step process via alkylidene intermediates, which are basic enough to activate the quite inert C−H bonds. Generation of these intermediates occur through $\alpha$-H abstraction on the ligands attached to the same titanium atom, which is the determining step, whereas $\beta$- and $\gamma$-H abstractions take place between ligands of different metallic centers. This cooperative behavior allows the selective activation of the distal C−H bonds. The calculations also allowed establishment of a reactivity order for the different type of $\gamma$-C−H bonds: $-CH_2SiMe > -CH_2CMe_3 \cong -CH_2Ph$. Since the energy barrier of the pentamethylcyclopentadienyl activation varies to a lesser extent along the series of compounds, the observed selectivity can be straightforwardly explained. The most reactive alkylsilyl fragments in compound 2 undergo only $\gamma$-H abstractions. In contrast, for the benzyl ligands in compound 5 we only observed the $\beta$-H abstraction on a pentamethylcy- clopentadienyl fragment, whereas complex 3 gives a mixture of $\beta$- and $\gamma$-H abstractions.

## EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
**General Procedures.** All operations were performed in an M. Braun double-drybox or using vacuum standard Schlenk techniques under argon atmospheres. **Crystallography.** Crystals of 4−6 were grown at $-20\ ^\circ$C from saturated toluene solutions. Then, crystals were removed from the Schlenks and covered with a layer of a viscous perfluoropolyether (FomblinY). A suitable crystal was selected with the aid of a microscope, mounted on a cryoloop, and immediately placed in the low-temperature nitrogen stream of the diffractometer. The intensity data sets were collected at 200 K on a Bruker-Nonius KappaCCD diffractometer equipped with an Oxford Cryostream 700 unit. Crystallographic data for all complexes are presented in Table S1. The structures were solved by using the WINGX package$^{19}$ by direct methods (SHELXS-2014)$^{20}$ and refined by least-squares against $F^2$ (SHELXL-2014).$^{20}$ Each molecule of compound 5 crystallized with one disordered molecule of toluene, and no sensible chemical model could be obtained. Thus, the Squeeze$^{21}$ procedure was used to remove its contribution to the structure factors. Molecules of complex 6 crystallized with one toluene solvent molecule. All non-hydrogen atoms were anisotropically refined for 4−6, while hydrogen atoms were placed at idealized positions and refined using a riding model, except those of the methylene groups in 4 that were located in the Fourier difference map and isotropically refined.

**Computational Details.** Calculations were performed with the Gaussian09 series of programs$^{22}$ within the framework of the density functional theory (DFT)$^{23}$ using the B3LYP functional.$^{24}$ The basis set for titanium and silicon atoms was associated with a pseudopotential,$^{25}$ with a standard double-$\xi$ LANL2DZ contraction, and the basis set was supplemented by f and d shells, respectively.$^{26}$ The rest of atoms were described with a standard 6-31G(d, p) basis set.$^{27}$ The stationary points were located without any restriction. Transition states were characterized by single imaginary frequency, whose normal mode corresponded to the expected motion. Such a computational level has been successfully employed in the study of the reactivity of titanium molecular oxides, including quantitative agreement with kinetic

experimental data. $^{28}$ We have also tested the use of larger basis sets. The geometries were optimized with the method described above excluding the polarization functions of Ti and Si atoms, and the energy was calculated using an triple-$\xi$ LANL2TZ basis set$^{29}$ supplemented with an f shell$^{26}$ for Ti and a 6-311+G(2d, p) basis set$^{27}$ for the rest of atoms. Figure S15 compares the two computational levels for the free energy profile of the intramolecular C$-$H bond activation in complex 2. The results show very small energy differences $(<1\ \text{kcal·mol}^{-1})$ for the key initial steps ($\alpha$- and $\gamma$-H abstraction), and qualitative, if not quantitative, fitting for the other reaction steps. Thus, the discussion is based on the more affordable, double-$\xi$ quality basis set, what allows a straightforward comparison with our previous studies on bond activation in related molecular titanium oxides.$^{30}$ The electronic structure of key species was analyzed using charges that are fit to the electrostatic potentials (ESPs).$^{31}$
