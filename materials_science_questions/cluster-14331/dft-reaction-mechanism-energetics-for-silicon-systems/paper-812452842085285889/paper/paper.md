# Mechanism for Desorption of SiF₄ from an SiO₂ Film Surface in HF Solutions

Tomoki OKU, Kazuhiko SATO and Mutsuyuki OTSUBO

Mitsubishi Electric Corporation, Optoelectronic and Microwave Devices Laboratory,
4-1 Mizuhara, Itami, Hyogo 664, Japan

(Received September 9, 1996; accepted for publication December 9, 1996)

An extended Hückel calculation was employed to calculate the change in the electron density on the Si-O back bonds and the total electronic energy during the reaction at the SiO₂ film surface. We demonstrate that the desorption of SiF₄ occurs as follows: (1) bifluoride (HF₂⁻) ions dissociate into HF monomers and F⁻ ions near the surface, (2) F⁻ ions attack the -SiF₃ surfaces and hydrogen ions attack the oxygen atoms of the back bond, (3) the O-Si-F bond angle decreases and the Si-O bond strength is weakened, (4) the hydrogen atoms passivate the oxygen atoms after tetrahedral SiF₄ molecules are generated and the Si-O back bonds are broken. The evaluated activation energy of the desorption of SiF₄ is 0.8 eV.

KEYWORDS: wet etching, HF solutions, silicon dioxide, quantum chemistry, extended Hückel calculation

## 1. Introduction

The wet etching of silicon dioxide (SiO₂) films by hydrogen fluoride (HF) solutions is widely used as a clean etching process which does not damage semiconductor substrates. Since a flat, clean silicon surface is required to fabricate silicon metal oxide semiconductor field effect transistors (MOSFETs) with thin insulating films, the hydrogen passivation of silicon dangling bonds on silicon surfaces and surface roughness after HF treatment are being extensively investigated at present.¹⁻³⁾ However, the problems in the etching kinetics of SiO₂ films in HF solutions have been investigated many times in the past 60 years in order to clarify the relationship between the etching rate and the HF concentration.⁴⁻⁹⁾ Most research concluded that SiO₂ films are etched through the nucleophilic HF₂⁻ attack on Siᵟ⁺ and the electrophilic H⁺ ion attack on Oᵟ⁻. In particular, although the desorption of SiF₄ from the SiO₂ film surface in HF solutions plays a major role in the etching reaction SiO₂ + 2HF₂⁻ + 2H⁺ → SiF₄ + 2H₂O, it has not yet been studied using quantum chemical calculations. Trucks et al.¹⁾ and Raghavachari et al.²⁾ applied an ab-initio molecular orbital calculation to the etching reaction on silicon surfaces:

$$
\mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{SiF}_{3}+\mathrm{HF} \rightarrow \text { transition state } \rightarrow \mathrm{SiH}_{4}+\mathrm{SiF}_{4}.
$$

They explained the removal of SiF₄ from silicon surfaces by the insertion of HF into the Si-Si back bonds. They calculated an activation barrier of 1.0 eV and found that these reactions are exothermic by 1.8-2.0 eV. However, on the SiO₂ surfaces, the reaction based on HF₂⁻ ions and H⁺ ions has not been discussed using quantum calculations. In this study, we propose, for the first time, a mechanism for the SiF₄ desorption involving the attack of HF₂⁻ ions and H⁺ ions. We employed an extended Hückel calculation to determine the change in the electronic total energy and the electron density on the Si-O back bonds during the reaction at the SiO₂ film surface. We carried out this calculation using a personal computer. Although the extended Hückel calculation is not sufficiently accurate to analyze the energy level and the electron density in comparison with ab-initio calculations, it is suitable for analyzing many structures composed of a few tens of atoms qualitatively, and with low computation costs.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Internuclear distance between the silicon and fluo- rine of $SiF_{4}$ molecules and of the $-SiF_{3}$ surface
In order to calculate the desorption energy of an $SiF_{4}$ molecules, we must determine the structure of the $SiF_{4}$ molecules and the $-SiF_{3}$ surface. If an $SiF_{4}$ molecule has a tetrahedral structure with bond length $R(Si-F)$ , we can calculate the total electronic energy of an $SiF_{4}$ molecule as a function of $R(Si-F)$ , as shown in Fig. 3. The binding energy curve has a minimum energy point at the $Si-F$ bond length of $1.635 \AA$ defined as $R_{m}(Si-F)$ . This bond length is in good agreement with the exper-imentally reported $Si-F$ distance of $1.55 \AA$ for the $SiF_{4}$ molecule. $^{12)}$ Moreover, since the binding energy curve indicates the repulsion and attraction forces inside and outside the radius $R_{m}(Si-F)$ , the extended Hückel cal culation can reproduce the appropriate atomic poten- tial. Next, if we calculate the total electronic energy of the $(HO)_{3} SiOSiF_{3}$ cluster as a function of $R(Si-F)$ , we can show that $R_{m}(Si-F)$ is equal to $1.71 \AA$ . The slightly larger $R_{m}(Si-F)$ is reasonable since the $-SiF_{3}$ surface ofthe $(HO)_{3} SiOSiF_{3}$ cluster is approximated by an $OSiF_{3}$  molecule in which the oxygen atom has a larger atomicradius than the fluorine atom. However, we use $R_{m}(Si-$  $F)=1.635 \AA$ both for the $SiF_{4}$ molecule and for the $-SiF_{3}$  surface in order to decrease the number of parameters. This approximation overestimates the total electronic en- ergy at the initial state by $0.03 eV$ .

### 3.2 Dissociation reaction of $HF_{2}^{-}$ ion
We can determine the reaction route by moving the atoms along the bottom line in the valley on the en- ergy potential surface. To study the dissociation reac- tion $HF_{2}^{-} \to F^{-}+HF$ , we calculated the total electronic energy for the $HF_{2}^{-}$ ion as functions of the internuclear distances $r(FH-F^{-})$ and $r(F^{-}-HF)$ , as shown in Fig. 4. Since the extended Hückel calculations do not consider the repulsive interaction between electrons, the valley does not appear on the energy potential surface. How- ever, we draw the valley at the experimentally derived $H-F$ distance $(0.92 \AA)^{12)}$ of the HF monomer on the po tential energy surface for the description of the disso- ciation reaction. Consequently, a saddle point where r(FH-F-)=r(F--HF)=1.20 A is found in the calcula- tions. This is in good agreement with the experimentally observed H-F distance $1.13 \AA.^{12)}$ The reaction

$$\mathrm{FH}-\mathrm{F}^{-} \to \mathrm{HF}_{2}^{-} \to \mathrm{F}^{-}-\mathrm{HF}\qquad(7)$$

proceeds from one valley $(FH-F^{-})$ to the other $(F^{--}$  $HF)$ over a transition state with an activation energy of $0.38 eV$ . In other words, the $HF_{2}^{-}$ ions spontaneously dissociate by emitting energy of $0.38 eV$ in the solution.

Our etching model is appropriate for explaining the $SiF_{4}$ desorption from the $SiO_{2}$ surface in comparison with the HF insertion model proposed by Trucks et al. $^{1)}$ If HF insertion into the Si-O back bond is performed, the reaction has to proceed through HF dissociation into $H^{+}$ ions and $F^{-}$ ions. Since we calculate that the dissociation is endothermic by 2.7 eV, it is difficult for the reaction to proceed. On the other hand, in our model, the dissociation to produce the $F^{-}$ ions is exothermic and the $H^{+}$ attack is independent of the $HF_{2}^{-}$ attack. The process of $SiF_{4}$ generation in our model is known as the $S_{N} 2$ process (nucleophilic substitution) in organic chemistry $^{13)}$ and it is also proposed as the model for the fluorinate etching of silicon. $^{14)}$ We suggest that the desorption of the $SiF_{4}$ molecule involves the dissociation of the $HF_{2}^{-}$ ion, the attack of the $H^{+}$ ion and hydrogen passivation as well as the $S_{N} 2$ process.

In order to clarify the mechanism of the $SiO_{2}$ etching reaction in HF solutions, we have to consider the desorption of $H_{2} O$ molecules as well as $SiF_{4}$ molecules. We can speculate about the mechanism of the desorption of $H_{2} O$ molecules based on the hydrogen attack and the fluorine passivation. First, the O-H surfaces, which are generated by the hydrogen passivation of the oxygen atoms, change the surface structure into $O-H_{2}$ surfaces by the attack of the hydrogen ions. Second, the Si-O back bonds of the $O-H_{2}$ surfaces are stretched by the absorption energy of the hydrogen ions. Third, the fluorine ions insert into the stretched Si-O bonds and break the Si-O back bonds. Finally, the fluorine ions terminate the dangling bonds of the silicon atoms after the $H_{2} O$ molecules are generated. Consequently, the hydrogen ions and the fluorine ions play an attacking and terminating role alternately in the etching reaction $SiO_{2}+2 HF_{2}^{-}+2 H^{+} \to SiF_{4}+2 H_{2} O$.

## 4. Conclusions

We conclude that the desorption of $SiF_{4}$ molecules from the $SiO_{2}$ surfaces is assisted by (1) the energy produced through the dissociation of $HF_{2}^{-}$ ions and through the exothermic reaction by the hydrogen ion attack on the oxygen atoms, (2) the surface reconstruction of optimizing the O-Si-F bond angle and the surface relaxation of the Si-O back bond, and (3) the hydrogen passivation of the oxygen atoms.

## Appendix

Below is the scheme for solving the equations by the extended Hückel calculation. Multiplying $\chi_{\nu}$ to the left of eq. (3), we obtain
$$\left(H_{\mu \nu}-\varepsilon_{(i)} S_{\mu \nu}\right) C_{(i) \mu}=0, \quad \text { (A·1) }$$
where
$$H_{\mu \nu}=\int \chi_{\mu} \boldsymbol{H} \chi_{\nu} \mathrm{d} v \quad \text { (A·2) }$$
and
$$S_{\mu \nu}=\int \chi_{\mu} \chi_{\nu} \mathrm{d} v. \quad \text { (A·3) }$$

Since the molecular orbital function $\psi_{(i)}(r)$ must satisfy the norm-conserving condition
$$\int \psi_{(i)}(\boldsymbol{r})^{2} \mathrm{~d} v=1, \quad \text { (A·4) }$$
we obtain the additional condition
$$\begin{aligned}
\int \psi_{(i)}(\boldsymbol{r})^{2} \mathrm{~d} v & =\Sigma \Sigma C_{(i) \mu} C_{(i) \nu} \int \chi_{\mu} \chi_{\nu} \mathrm{d} v \\
& =\Sigma \Sigma C_{(i) \mu} C_{(i) \nu} S_{\mu \nu} \\
& =\Sigma C_{(i) \mu}^{2}+2 \Sigma \Sigma C_{(i) \mu} C_{(i) \nu} S_{\mu \nu} \\
& =1, \quad \text { (A·5) }
\end{aligned}$$

where we use $S_{\nu\nu}=S_{\mu\mu}=1$. In the extended Hückel calculation, the diagonal elements $(H_{\mu\mu})$ for a one-electron Hamiltonian are approximated by

$$
H_{\mu \mu}=\int \chi_{\mu} \boldsymbol{H} \chi_{\mu} \mathrm{d} v \fallingdotseq-I_{\mu}, \tag{A·6}
$$

where $I_{\mu}$ is equal to the energy needed to remove an electron which occupies a $\mu$th atomic orbital. The diagonal elements $(H_{\mu\mu})$ are generally called the "Coulomb integrals". The Coulomb integrals $H_{\mu\mu}$ for hydrogen, oxygen, fluorine and silicon are listed in Table I. $^{15)}$ On the other hand, the off-diagonal elements are approximated by

$$
H_{\mu \nu}=\int \chi_{\mu} \boldsymbol{H} \chi_{\nu} \mathrm{d} v \fallingdotseq(K / 2)\left(H_{\mu \mu}+H_{\nu \nu}\right) S_{\mu \nu}, \tag{A·7}
$$

where $K$ is the Hückel constant which is equal to 1.75 and $S_{\mu\nu}$ is calculated by eq. (A·3). The off-diagonal elements $(H_{\mu\nu})$ are generally called the "resonance integrals". We can obtain the eigenvalue $\varepsilon_{(i)}$ by solving the following determinant:

$$
\operatorname{det}\left(H_{\mu \nu}-\varepsilon_{(i)} S_{\mu \nu}\right)=0 \quad(\mu=1, \cdots, m, \nu=1, \cdots, m). \tag{A·8}
$$

Substituting the eigenvalue $\varepsilon_{(i)}$ into eq. (A·1), we can obtain $C_{(i)\mu}$ and $\psi_{(i)}(\boldsymbol{r})$. The total electronic energy $E$ is expressed by

$$
E=\Sigma n_{(i)} \varepsilon_{(i)}, \tag{A·9}
$$

where $n_{(i)}$ is the number of the electrons which occupy the molecular orbital $\psi_{(i)}(\boldsymbol{r})$. The electron density distribution $\rho(\boldsymbol{r})$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\boldsymbol{r}) & =\Sigma n_{(i)} \psi_{(i)}^{2}(\boldsymbol{r}) \\
& =\Sigma n_{(i)} \Sigma \Sigma C_{(i) \mu} C_{(i) \nu} \chi_{\mu}\left(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{\mu}\right) \chi_{\nu}\left(\boldsymbol{r}-\boldsymbol{r}_{\nu}\right). \\
& \tag{A·10}
\end{aligned}
$$

To understand the change of the electron density distribution, we will use the electron density defined by Mulliken population analysis as follows:

$$
N_{\mu}=\Sigma n_{(i)}\left(C_{(i) \mu}^{2}+(1 / 2) \Sigma C_{(i) \mu} C_{(i) \nu} S_{\mu \nu}\right), \quad(\mathrm{A} \cdot 11)
$$

$$
M_{\mathrm{a}}=\Sigma N_{\mu}, \tag{A·12}
$$

$$
N_{\mu \nu}=\Sigma n_{(i)} C_{(i) \mu} C_{(i) \nu} S_{\mu \nu}, \tag{A·13}
$$

$$
M_{\mathrm{ab}}=\Sigma \Sigma N_{\mu \nu}. \tag{A·14}
$$

Here $N_{\mu}$ is the average number of electrons on the atomic orbital $\chi_{\mu}, M_{\mathrm{a}}$ is the average number of outermost electrons on atom "a", $N_{\mu\nu}$ is the overlap population between the atomic orbital $\chi_{\mu}$ and the atomic orbital $\chi_{\nu}$, and $M_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the "atomic bond population" which is the number of electrons on the bond between atom "a" and atom "b". The atomic bond population indicates the strength of covalent bonding. When we calculate $M_{\mathrm{a}}$ and $N_{\mu}$, we apply the Mulliken approximation to eq. (A·11) and set the coefficient of the second term of eq. (A·11) equal to 1/2. As a result, the bond between each atom can share an equal number of electrons.
