# Theoretical Study of the $\text{Si}_2\text{NO}$ Potential Energy Surface

GUANG-TAO YU, XU-RI HUANG, YI-HONG DING, WEI CHEN, CHIA-CHUNG SUN

State Key Laboratory of Theoretical and Computational Chemistry, Institute of Theoretical Chemistry, Jilin University, Changchun 130023, People's Republic of China

Received 27 August 2006; accepted 5 September 2006
Published online 26 October 2006 in Wiley InterScience (www.interscience.wiley.com).
DOI 10.1002/qua.21232

## ABSTRACT:
The structures, spectroscopies, and stabilities of the doublet $\text{Si}_2\text{NO}$ radical are explored at the density functional theory (DFT) and ab initio levels. Seventeen isomers are located, connected by 26 interconversion transition states. At the CCSD(T)/6-311+G(2df)//QCISD/6-311G(d)+ZPVE level, three low-lying isomers are predicted, that is, one bent species SiNSiO 3 (5.1 kcal/mol) containing the important $\text{Si}\equiv\text{N}$ triple bonding and two four-membered ring isomers including cyclic cSiNSiO 1 (0.0) with Si—Si cross-bonding with $C_{2v}$ symmetry and puckered cSiNSiO 1' (11.9) with divalent carbene character. Three low-lying isomers $\textbf{1}$, $\textbf{1}'$, and $\textbf{3}$ have reasonable kinetic stabilities and might be observable either experimentally or astrophysically. The possible formation strategies of $\textbf{1}$, $\textbf{1}'$, and $\textbf{3}$ in laboratory and in space are discussed in detail. The calculated vibrational frequencies and possible formation processes of $\textbf{3}$ are consistent with recent experimental observations. In light of the fact that no cyclic nitrogen-containing species have been detected in space, two cyclic isomers $\textbf{1}$ and $\textbf{1}'$ could be promising candidates. Furthermore, the bonding nature of three isomers $\textbf{1}$, $\textbf{1}'$, and $\textbf{3}$ is analyzed. The calculated results are also compared with those of the analogue $\text{C}_2\text{NO}$ radical.

## 1. Introduction

The silicon-, nitrogen-, and oxygen-related chemistries have received considerable attention from various aspects. One interesting field is interstellar chemistry. Up to now, several silicon-, nitrogen-, or oxygen-containing molecules, such as $\text{SiC}_n$ ($n=1$–$4$), SiN, SiO, SiS, NO, and NP, have been detected in interstellar space [1–4]. In contrast, the silicon-, nitrogen-, and oxygen-related chemistries have been thought to play important roles in microelectronic materials. The reaction of silicon with NO is of particular interest in semiconductor construction. Ultrathin silicon oxynitride films are used increasingly as gate dielectric materials [5, 6]. Studies in the gas phase showed that NO reacted readily with all sizes of silicon clusters [7]. Very recently, reactions of silicon atoms with NO were also studied, using matrix isolation infrared (IR) absorption spectroscopy [8]. ## 2. Computational Methods

All computations are carried out using the Gaussian 98 [16] and MOLCAS 5.2 [17] (for CASSCF and CASPT2) program packages. The optimized geometries and harmonic vibrational frequencies of the local minima and transition states are obtained at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) [18] level. Single-point calculations are performed at the CCSD(T)/6-311G(2d) [19] level, using the B3LYP/6-311G(d) optimized geometries. To confirm whether the obtained transition states connect the right isomers, the intrinsic reaction coordinate (IRC) [20, 21] calculations are performed at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) level. The structures, frequencies, and energetics of the relevant species are further calculated at the QCISD/6-311G(d) [22, 23] and CCSD(T)/6-311+G(2df) (energy only) levels. The zero-point vibrational energies (ZPVE) at the 6-311G(d)-B3LYP and QCISD levels are also included for energy correction. Finally, CASSCF(13,13)/cc-pVDZ

### 3.1. SI₂NO ISOMERS

After numerous searches, 17 Si₂NO isomers (m) and 26 interconversion transition states (TSm/n) are obtained at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) level. Among these, four isomers have four-membered ring structures: cSiNSiO 1 (0.0), cSiNSiO 1' (9.7, 11.9, 7.8), cSiSiNO 2 (73.8), and cSiSiNO 2' (73.8). Obviously, 1 and 1' are two low-lying isomers. Isomer 1 is a C₂ᵥ symmetry structure with Si—Si cross-bonding, while 1' is the puckered structure with a long Si—Si cross-bonding. Interestingly, the two remaining isomers 2 and 2' can be considered as enantiomer. Unless otherwise specified, the first, second, and third values in parentheses are relative energies with reference to isomer 1 (0.0, 0.0, 0.0) at the CCSD(T)//B3LYP, CCSD(T)//QCISD, and CASPT2//CASSCF levels.

There are seven chain-like species on the PES: SiNSiO 3 (2.3, 5.1, 1.7), SiNSiO 3' (38.6), SiOSiN 4 (54.8), SiOSiN 4' (65.2), SiNOSi 5 (72.9), SiSiON 6 (139.6), and SiSiNO 12 (112.1). Except for the linear isomers 3' and 4', the other species are bent. Note that isomers 3' and 4' each have one imaginary frequency (362i and 51i cm⁻¹, respectively) at the higher QCISD/6-311G(d) level and relaxation of symmetry leads to 3 and 4. Thus, both are artifacts of B3LYP. The isomer SiSiNO 12 is located at the MP2/6-311G(d) level, since it cannot be obtained at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) level. Notably, although all the higher-level calculations, including CCSD(T)//B3LYP, CCSD(T)//QCISD, and CASPT2//CASSCF, lay isomer 3 as the second low-lying species after 1 by 2.3, 5.1, and 1.7 kcal/mol, respectively, as shown in Table II, on the basis of such a small energy difference, it is difficult for us to conclude which of the isomers 1 and 3 is the lowest-lying one after considering the existence of the computational errors. Among the postulated three-membered ring species, N-cSiSiO 7 (56.5), Si-cNSiO 7' (76.1), O-cNSiSi 8 (82.4), Si-cSiNO 9 (123.2), and N-cOSiSi 10 (128.2) are located as minima. The remaining isomer 11 (60.0) may be seen as an interesting cage-like structure. The branched-chain structures (in type V) cannot be located as minima.

### 3.2. SI₂NO ISOMERIZATION AND DISSOCIATION STABILITY

To discuss kinetic stability, one needs to consider various isomerization and dissociation pathways. The lowest isomerization or dissociation barrier governs the kinetic stability of an isomer. The relative energies of all the possible dissociation products are rather high-lying relative to isomer 1 (>60.7 kcal/mol at the CCSD(T)//B3LYP level, as shown in Table III), so the isomerization process governs the kinetic stability of Si₂NO isomers, as shown in Figure 5. For simplicity, the details of the obtained 26 transition states are omitted. We can find that three isomers 1, 1', and 3 are of interest. Besides being the low-lying isomers, 1, 1', and 3 have reasonable kinetic stabilities and could be observable either in the laboratory or in interstellar space. As can be deduced from Figure 5, two cyclic isomers 1, 1', and bent species 3 have kinetic stability as 11.1 (12.5) (1→3), 50.4 (52.3) (1'→7), and 8.8 (7.4) (3→1) kcal/mol, respectively. The italic values in parentheses are for CCSD(T)//QCISD single-point calculations. Note that the relative energy of TS1'/4 with reference to 4 is -0.5 kcal/mol at the CCSD(T)//B3LYP level, which indicates that 4 may isomerize to 1' with no or very small barrier. Instead, we select TS4/7 as the lowest isomerization barrier governing the stability of the species 1'. Moreover, TS8/12 and TS12/14 are located at the MP2/6-311G(d) level, since they cannot be obtained at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) level. Optimization of TS12/14 always leads to the fragments 14 Si₂+NO at the B3LYP level, which suggests that the process of 14 to 12 may proceed with no or very small barrier. Surely, this is consistent with the result of the present MP2 level.

Existence of the other isomers is less likely due to their higher energy and much smaller kinetic stability. At the CCSD(T)//B3LYP level, the lowest isomerization barriers of the isomers 2, 2', 4, 4', 5, 6, 7, 7', 8, 9, 10, and 11 are 2.3 (2→7'), 2.5 (2'→11), -0.5 (4→1'), -0.1 (4'→7), 8.9 (5→7'), -1.1 (6→10), 3.6 (7→4), 0.0 (7'→2), 10.3 (8→3), 2.7 (9→2), 10.3 (10→6), and 3.2 (11→1') kcal/mol, respectively. For the remaining species 12, the lowest isomerization barrier is -0.6 (12→8) kcal/mol at the CCSD(T)/6-311G(2d)//MP2/6-311G(d)+ZPVE level.

### 3.4. INTERSTELLAR AND LABORATORY IMPLICATIONS

The bent SiNSiO **3** has been characterized by recent study [8]. As shown in Table I, the present calculated vibrational frequencies for **3** at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) and QCISD/6-311G(d) levels are consistent with the recent experimental and theoretical values. Also, our calculated bond distances for **3** (see Fig. 2) are in agreement with the recent B3LYP/6-311+G(d) results. We know that in the recent experiment [8], isomer **3** was produced from codeposition of laser-ablated silicon atoms and NO in argon. It was suggested that SiNSiO **3** might be formed either via Si-atom and NSiO addition reaction or via silicon dimer Si₂ and NO direct reaction [8]. As shown in Figure 5, the reaction between the Si-atom and NSiO may lead to the species **3**. Because of the high relative energy (138.9 kcal/mol) of Si(³P)+NSiO(²Π), the isomer **1** may also be obtained. Alternately, the N atom of the NO radical attacks the Si-atom of the triplet Si₂ dimer to form the bent SiSiNO **12** via TS12/14 with a very small

However, since TS3/8 is lower in energy than TS7'/8, the isomerization of 8 to 3 is kinetically more favorable than that of 8 to $7'$. As a result, the formation of 1 and 3 from $Si_2$+NO reaction is not only thermodynamically but also kinetically more favorable than formation of $1'$, as shown in Figure 5. Moreover, on the basis of the fact that NO reacted readily with all sizes of silicon clusters in the gas phase [7], production of three $Si_2NO$ isomers 1, $1'$, and 3 is also possible in $Si_n$ and NO convaporization processes. It should be mentioned that in laboratory, the species $Si_2C_2$ and $SiC_3$, including both the linear and cyclic forms have been characterized in the $Si_n$ and $C_n$ convaporization processes.

In contrast, the fragments SiN, SiO, and NO have been detected in space [1], thus the formation of three isomers 1, $1'$, and 3 is possible in interstellar medium. As shown in Figures 6(a) and 6(b), fragments $NSi(^2\Sigma)$ and $-SiO(^1\Sigma)$ can associate to form SiNSiO 3 with no barrier, especially in dark interstellar clouds in which the temperature is very low and much collisional stabilization occurs. The observation of bent SiNSiO 3 under interstellar condition then appears possible. In view of the high entrance energy [relative energy of $SiN(^2\Sigma)+SiO(^1\Sigma)$ is 60.7 kcal/mol], isomers 1 and 3 may coexist. For cSiNSiO $1'$, the direct addition of $-SiN$ radical to the O-site of SiO molecule initially generates the high-lying isomer SiOSiN 4, which possesses much longer internal SiO bond (1.9565 Å) than Si—O single bond (1.6618 Å) by 17.7% at the B3LYP level. And then 4 would easily isomerize to $1'$ with no barrier. Of course, formation of 1, $1'$, and 3 via effective ion–molecule reaction is also feasible.

It is worth mentioning that the analogues $C_2NO$ [12, 13, 24–29], [Si, C, N, O] [30–32], and $C_2NS$ [33–35] have been studied extensively by experiment and theory. Naturally, the present $Si_2NO$ system is expected to obtain more attention. To aid future experimental and interstellar characterization, the QCISD/6-311G(d) structures and spectroscopic properties for the three isomers 1, $1'$, and 3 are provided in Figure 2 and Table I. It is worth mentioning that for the three isomers 1, $1'$, and 3 as well as the two transition states TS1/3 and TS4/7 that govern their kinetic stability, the obtained geometrical parameters and spectroscopic properties at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) level are generally in good agreement with those at the QCISD/6-311G(d) level (in Figs. 2 and 3 and Table I). The respective relative energies at the CCSD(T)//B3LYP level are close to those at the CCSD(T)//QCISD level (in Table II). Moreover, the CASPT2//CASSCF relative energies and structures of 1, $1'$, and 3 are close to the CCSD(T)//B3LYP and CCSD(T)//QCISD values, and at the CASSCF level, the leading electronic configurations occupied by 1, $1'$, and 3 (90.41%, 91.09%, and 89.16%, respectively) are the same as the spanned Slater Determinant of B3LYP. This indicates that the $Si_2NO$ system has negligible multi-configurational character, and the CCSD(T)//B3LYP method is adequate for calculation of the structures, spectroscopes and energies.

### 4. Conclusions

A detailed PES of $Si_2NO$ is theoretically established, involving 17 isomers and 26 interconversion transition states. At the CCSD(T)/6-311+G(2df)//QCISD/6-311G(d)+ZPVE level, three low-lying isomers are located, that is, one bent SiNSiO 3 (5.1 kcal/mol) and two cyclic isomers cSiNSiO 1 (0.0) and cSiNSiO $1'$ (11.9). In addition to experimentally known species 3, two new low-lying isomers 1 and $1'$ have reasonable kinetic stabilities and could be observable in laboratory and in interstellar space. The formation processes of recent experimental indication for 3 are reasonably explained by the present study. The possible formation processes of two new isomers 1 and $1'$ are also discussed in detail. Being aware that, up to now, no cyclic nitrogen-containing species have been detected in space, two cyclic isomers 1 and $1'$ could be promising candidates. Moreover, the structural and bonding analyses indicate that species 3 possesses the important $Si\equiv N$ triple bonding, thus 3 should be a good target for exploring the stability of SiN multiple bonding. Also, the cyclic species $1'$ is characterized by the divalent carbene feature. The present results are also expected to be useful for understanding the initial growing step for the NO-doped $Si_n$ vaporization processes and for future investigation of the potentially important $Si_2$+NO reaction.
