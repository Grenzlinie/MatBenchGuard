Effective-potential $a b$ initio self-consistent-field molecular-orbital computations of electronic wave functions and total energy have been made on the molecule $H_{3} SiH \cdot SiH_{3}$ , which was chosen to model the paramagnetic three-center bonded system $T^{0}$ postulated by Fisch and Licciardello to exchange charge and produce the diamagnetic anion $T^{-}$ and cation $T^{+}$ exothermically (in a process with negative $U$ ) with states in the gap of hydrogenated amorphous silicon. Structural computations described here predict a three-center bonded system for $T^{+}$ , which is symmetrical. For $T^{0}$ and $T^{-}$ a much weaker three-center bond is found. It is more appropriate to describe $T^{0}$ and $T^{-}$ as $SiH_{4}$ weakly associated with $SiH_{3}$ and $SiH_{3}^{-}$ , respectively. We find that stability of the three-center bond of $T^{+}$ provides a driving force of 33 kcal / mole for charge transfer, while the 1-kcal "hydrogen bond" of $T^{-}$ is unimportant in this regard. We are unable to estimate reliably the sign of $U$ for charge transfer by $T^{0}$ embedded in hydrogenated amorphous silicon because of the large uncertainty in our estimates of the Born cavity energy: Our results do not exclude the possibility that $U$ is negative. We suggest that at low concentrations of hydrogen in hydrogenated amorphous silicon, a more probable process is for a dangling bond of silicon proximate to a $Si-H$ bond to form $T^{+}$ with the transfer of its electron to an isolated dangling bond. We model this process with the reaction $T^{0}+SiH_{3}=T^{+}+SiH_{3}^{-}$ .

## INTRODUCTION

Fisch and Licciardello $^{1}$ have proposed that in hydrogenated amorphous silicon three-center bonds of a hydrogen atom between two silicon atoms $(T^{0})$ are unstable with respect to charge exchange which produces the cation $T^{+}$ and the anion $T^{-}$ in an exothermic process, that is, with negative $U .^{2}$ The charge exchange converts two paramagnetic $T^{0}$ systems into the diamagnetic $T^{+}$ and $T^{-}$ . A consequence of this charge exchange would be a reduction in the number of unpaired electrons relative to the number of silicon dangling bonds in hydrogenated amorphous silicon. $^{3}$

Fisch and Licciardello took the three-center bonded system to be linear with the hydrogen atom between the two silicon atoms. They discussed the electronic structures of $T^{0}, T^{+}$ , and $T^{-}$ in terms of three basis functions consisting of hybrid atomic orbitals from each silicon and directed toward the hydrogen atom, which contributed a 1s orbital. From these three basis functions were constructed three molecular orbitals: bonding $\psi_{1}$ , nonbonding $\psi_{2}$ , and antibonding $\psi_{3}$ . The bonding orbital $\psi_{1}$ is doubly occupied by electrons in $T^{+}$ . The bonding orbital $\psi_{1}$ is doubly occupied and $\psi_{2}$ is singly occupied by electrons in $T^{0}$ . In $T^{0}$ the nonbonding orbital $\psi_{2}$ is postulated to fall in the band gap. $^{1}$ Both $\psi_{1}$ and $\psi_{2}$ are doubly occupied by electron pairs in $T^{-}$ .

The value of $U$ is the change of energy in the reaction
$$2 T^{0}=T^{+}+T^{-}, \quad \Delta E=U. \quad (1)$$

In the discussion of Fisch and Licciardello $^{1}$ the above reaction is taken to be exothermic, that is, with negative $U$ . The change in energy was estimated to be -0.6 to -1.0 eV and was attributed to a strong hydrogen bond in $T^{-}$ .

By analogy with the length of the three-center bond of diborane from boron to hydrogen, relative to the terminal boron-hydrogen bond length, Fisch and Licciardello¹ also estimated that the three-center SiH distance in $T^+$ would be 1.6 Å, about 0.14 Å longer than the normal silicon-hydrogen bond length. They proposed that in both $T^0$ and $T^-$ the proton would be displaced from the bond center toward the more normal Si-H bond length of 1.45 Å to one silicon atom. In order to test the three-center bond model for $T^0$, $T^+$, and $T^-$, and better estimate the sign and magnitude of $U$ in Eq. (1), we have undertaken quantum-chemical computations on the corresponding molecular model system depicted in Fig. 1.

# THEORETICAL MODELS AND METHODS

In our computations to model the systems $T^+$, $T^0$, and $T^-$, we have extended the model system of Fisch and Licciardello by bonding each terminal silicon atom to three additional hydrogen atoms, thus saturating the remaining valencies as would occur in amorphous or crystalline silicon. We have previously applied this device in studies of molecular clusters chosen to model silicon (111) surface relaxation.⁴

FIG. 1. Computed three-center bond structures for $T^+$, $T^0$, and $T^-$.

The wave functions for only the valence orbitals were computed in our model systems, in which case $T^+$, $T^0$, and $T^-$ are 14-, 15-, and 16-electron systems, respectively. The effect of the core orbitals is included through the use of an effective potential.⁵,⁶ The computation of determinantal wave functions for the closed-shell systems $T^+$ and $T^-$ was made in the spin-restricted self-consistent-field molecular-orbital (SCF-MO) approximation. The spin-unrestricted⁷ approximation was employed for the open-shell system $T^0$. All computations were made on the computer program GAUSSIAN 78.⁸ The double-zeta basis of contracted Gaussian functions for hydrogen,⁹ and the LP-31G basis in Table I for silicon,¹⁰ was used throughout. Equilibrium geometries were determined by minimizing the computed total energy with respect to the two Si-H bond lengths of the three-center Si-H-Si portion, and other lengths and angles as required.

# COMPUTED STRUCTURES AND TOTAL ENERGIES

The equilibrium structures for the silicon hydrides $SiH_3^+$, $SiH_3$, $SiH_3^-$, $SiH_4$, and $SiH_2$ were determined. The silyl species were assumed to have a threefold axis. Silane was assumed to be tetrahedral. The computed structures and corresponding total energies are listed in Table II.

The structures of $T^+$, $T^0$, and $T^-$ were computed in two ways. The first was to assume tetrahedral angles at both silicon atoms and the silane Si-H bond length for the terminal bonds to silicon which tie up residual valencies. A staggered "ethanelike" conformation was assumed throughout. The three-center Si-H-Si portion was assumed to be linear and two distances were varied: the distance between the silicon atoms and the distance of the central proton from one of the silicon atoms. Minimization of the computed total energy with respect to these two parameters led to the geometries and total energies listed in Table III for tetrahedral ends.

The second geometry optimization was made for all coordinates, subject to the assumption that the system has a threefold axis of rotation. The end silyl groups of $T^+$ were found to be identical. However, the equilibrium geometries for $T^0$ and $T^-$ were computed to have silyl groups which differ at the two ends of the molecule. The final computed geometries for $T^+$, $T^0$, and $T^-$ are displayed in Fig. 1 and listed in Table III.

However, a fairly reliable esti- mate can be made which combines information from theory and experiment. Note that if one uses computed total energies from Table II to estimate the heat of reaction (4), one obtains $\Delta H=+176.5 kcal / mole$ , which is 23 kcal / mole more endothermic than the "experimental" esti- mate. This is fully expected because reaction (4) creates a new electron pair. It is well known that a single determinant wave function is unable to prop- erly correlate the motion of a pair of electrons of opposite spin. This inadequacy is called the correla- tion energy. It is known to be about 1 eV or 23.1kcal per valence electron pair. $^{15}$ When the heat of a reaction which creates an electron pair is estimated by the change in self-consistent-field energy $\Delta E_{SCF}$ , a correction for the correlation energy change of -23.1 kcal/mole should be added for each electron pair formed in the reaction. Thus corrected our theoretical estimate of the heat of charge transfer between silyl radicals is +153.4 kcal/mole, in agreement with "experiment."

In a similar way we can estimate the heat of transfer of electrons between $T^{0}$ molecules from the change of SCF total energies for $T^{0}, T^{+}$ , and $T^{-}$  with completely optimized geometries, as given in Table III,
$$2 T_{0}=T^{+}+T^{-}, \quad \Delta E_{\mathrm{SCF}}=+143.0, \quad(5)$$
where $\Delta E_{SCF}$ is measured in kcal/mole. This is less than the $\Delta E_{SCF}$ for the charge transfer between silyl radicals by 33.2 kcal/mole, almost exactly the com-puted binding energy of $SiH_{3}^{+}$ to $SiH_{4}$ of -32.9 kcal / mole. The extra stability of $T^{+}$ is apparently responsible for the transfer of charge, not the bind- ing energy of $SiH_{3}^{-}$ to $SiH_{4}$ to form $T^{-}$ , which is computed to be 1.1 kcal/mole. In reaction (5) there is an increase of one electron pair. We must add $\Delta E_{corr }=-23.1 kcal$ (Ref. 5) to obtain our estimate of the heat of reaction (1) in the gas phase $\Delta E=+119.9 kcal / mole$ .

We have made an estimate of the correlation en- ergy change by applying unrestricted Moeller- Plesset theory to third order (UMP 3) (Refs. 16 and17) with our LP-31G basis in Table I to the three species in reaction (5), with the consequence that $\Delta E$ is computed to be more exothermic by 9.2 kcal. As expected, this computation underestimates the correlation energy change due to the relatively small basis set employed.

The fact that the extra stability of \(T^+\) is responsible for the enhanced exothermicity of the charge transfer between \(T^0\) relative to \(SiH_3\) suggests that another process be considered to promote the transfer of dangling-bond electrons. This is the reaction

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{0}+\mathrm{SiH}_{3}=T^{+}+\mathrm{SiH}_{3}{ }^{-}, \quad \Delta E_{\mathrm{SCF}}=+143.9, \\
(6)
\end{aligned}
$$

where \(\Delta E_{\mathrm{SCF}}\) is computed from the SCF total energies of Tables II and III, in the latter case for the fully optimized geometry. Here the ability of a \(Si-H\) bond to stabilize \(SiH_3{}^+\) by the formation of \(T^+\) is the source of the extra exothermicity over reaction (4). This reaction has the property that each \(Si-H\) bond of the hydrogenated amorphous silicon that is directed toward a \(SiH_3\)-type dangling bond promotes the transfer of an electron to a \(SiH_3\)-type dangling bond with the formation of \(SiH_3{}^{-}\). The \(T^+\) formed in this way can serve as a trap for electrons, and the \(SiH_3{}^{-}\) as a trap for holes.

The previous estimates of the energy change in the charge exchange between gas-phase silyl radicals, or when stabilized by a \(Si-H\) bond as for \(T^0\), are both endothermic by about 6 eV. Because they represent processes in the gas phase, they do not include the effect of polarization of a solid medium as would occur in hydrogenated amorphous silicon. A more realistic estimate of the heat of charge transfer between silyl radicals or between a pair of \(T^0\) systems embedded in amorphous silicon requires an estimate of the polarization of the medium by the resulting charged fragments and by their interaction with that polarization charge. If the medium is assumed to have a uniform dielectric constant \(\epsilon\) up to the surface of a spherical cavity of radius \(R\) which encloses the charged particle, the corresponding interaction energy is called the Born cavity energy, an expression for which was derived by Stillinger,¹⁸

$$
\Delta E_{d}=-\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right) \frac{e^{2}}{2 R}, \quad(7)
$$

where \(e\) is the electron charge. For amorphous silicon we adopt the dielectric constant (11.4) of crystalline silicon.¹⁹ The choice of a cavity sphere radius is subject to great uncertainty. For the small silicon ions \(SiH_3{}^+\) and \(SiH_3{}^{-}\) we have chosen the \(Si-Si\) bond length of 2.35 Å on the assumption that polarization of all electron pairs to that radius are represented in our ab initio computations on these ions.

Application of these assumptions for $SiH_3{}^+$ and $SiH_3{}^{-}$ gives a cavity polarization energy of $-2.8$ eV or $-64.5$ kcal/mole for each ion. Thus our estimate of the cavity polarization-stabilization energy contribution to reaction (4) is $-5.6$ eV or $-129$ kcal/mole. It is insufficient by 24.4 kcal/mole to make this reaction exothermic, as is summarized in Table V.

The mutual attraction of an ion pair in a dielectric can be estimated$^{20}$ in atomic units by
$$
\Delta E_{\text {Coul }}=-\frac{e^{2}}{\epsilon r}, \tag{8}
$$
where $r$ is the distance between the ions. This Coulomb interaction would be sufficient to make reaction (3) exothermic if the distance between ions is $1.2$ Å or less. However, this is clearly a distance at which the dielectric approximation breaks down. It is also shorter than the silicon-silicon bond length of $2.35$ Å.

The estimation of cavity polarization energy for $T^{+}$ and $T^{-}$ embedded in amorphous silicon is very difficult because of uncertainty with respect to the proper cavity radius in these two cases. If the sphere size chosen for $SiH_3{}^+$ and $SiH_3{}^{-}$ is appropriate, then it is expected to be too small for both $T^{+}$ and $T^{-}$ and to overestimate the Born cavity energy. If we apply the $SiH_3{}^+$ and $SiH_3{}^{-}$ radii ($2.35$ Å) to estimate the Born cavity energy for $T^{+}$ and $T^{-}$, we obtain the estimate given in Table V that for reaction (1), $\Delta E=-9.1$ kcal/mole, we find $U$ to be negative in this assumption.

We have made a second estimate of cavity radii for $T^{+}$ and $T^{-}$ in which we take $R$ to be the average distance from the center of the three-center bond to the position of hydrogen atoms of terminal bonds which have been extended to the $Si-Si$ bond length of $2.35$ Å. In this way we obtain a cavity radius of $3.084$ Å for $T^{+}$ and $4.167$ Å for $T^{-}$. The corresponding Born cavity energies for $T^{+}$ and $T^{-}$ are $-49.2$ and $-36.3$ kcal with a total energy of $-85.5$ kcal. This falls short by 34.4 kcal/mole of making the charge transfer exothermic. A Coulomb interaction corresponding to an ion separation of $0.84$ Å or less would be required to make $U$ negative.

For the reaction (6) in which the coupling of $SiH_3{}^+$ to a $Si-H$ bond to form $T^{+}$ gives the added exothermicity of the charge transfer, we estimate the Born cavity energy of the products by using the radius $2.35$ Å for $SiH_3{}^{-}$ and $3.084$ Å for $T^{+}$. This gives a Born cavity energy contribution of $-113.6$ kcal, which, combined with an estimate of $-23.1$ for the correlation energy change, gives $\Delta E=+7.2$ kcal for reaction (6). $\Delta E$ would become exothermic if the ion pair formed is separated by $4$ Å or less.

Because of the large uncertainties in the magnitude of the Born cavity energy, we are not able to conclude whether $U$ is negative for the transfer of charge between $T^{0}$ molecules to form $T^{+}$ and $T^{-}$. However, it does appear that formation of the three-center bond in $T^{+}$ does favor charge transfer by 33 kcal/mole relative to the corresponding charge transfer by silicon dangling bonds not coupled to $Si-H$ bonds.
