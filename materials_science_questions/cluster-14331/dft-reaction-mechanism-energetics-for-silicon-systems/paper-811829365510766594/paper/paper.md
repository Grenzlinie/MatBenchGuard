# Direct Time-Resolved Study of the Kinetics of the Reaction of Silylene with Phenylsilane in the Gas Phase. Does $\text{SiH}_2$ React with the Aromatic Ring?

Rosa Becerra*

Instituto de Quimica-Fisica 'Rocasolano', C.S.I.C., C/Serrano 119, 28006 Madrid, Spain

J. Pat Cannady

Dow Corning Corporation, P.O. Box 994, Mail CO1232, Midland, Michigan 48686-0994

Received August 12, 2009

Laser flash photolysis studies of silylene, $\text{SiH}_2$, generated by the 193 nm laser flash photolysis phenylsilane, $\text{PhSiH}_3$, have been carried out to obtain rate constants for its bimolecular reaction with $\text{PhSiH}_3$ itself, in the gas phase. The reaction was studied in $\text{SF}_6$ (mostly at 10 Torr total pressure) over the temperature range 298−595 K. The rate constants (also found to be pressure independent) gave the following Arrhenius equation:

$$
\log(k/\text{cm}^3 \text{ molecule}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1}) = (-9.92 \pm 0.04) + (3.31 \pm 0.27) \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}/RT \ln 10
$$

Similar investigations of the reaction of silylene with benzene, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$, (295−410 K) gave data suggestive of the fact that $\text{SiH}_2$ might be reacting with photochemical products of $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$ as well as with $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$ itself. However, in the latter system, apparent rate constants were sufficiently low to indicate that in the reaction of $\text{SiH}_2$ with $\text{PhSiH}_3$ addition to the aromatic ring was unlikely to be in excess of 3% of the total. Quantum chemical calculations of the energy surface for $\text{SiH}_2 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_6$ indicate that 7-silanorcaradiene and 7-silacycloheptatriene are possible products but that $\text{PhSiH}_3$ formation is unlikely. RRKM calculations suggest that 7-silanorcaradiene should be the initial product but that it cannot be collisionally stabilized under experimental conditions

## Introduction

Silylenes are of importance because they are implicated in the thermal and photochemical breakdown mechanisms of silanes and organosilanes¹ as well as being key intermediates in CVD.² Time-resolved studies, carried out in recent years,³⁻⁶ have helped establish a growing database of gas-phase rate constants for reactions of these species with a wide range of substrate molecules. In our laboratories, much of the work has centered on silylene itself, $\text{SiH}_2$, the prototype species. In the majority of these studies $\text{SiH}_2$ has been produced by the 193 nm ArF laser photolysis of phenylsilane, $\text{PhSiH}_3$.⁷ Although the reaction of $\text{SiH}_2$ with $\text{PhSiH}_3$ itself always contributes to some extent to the overall decays of $\text{SiH}_2$ absorptions in these studies, no investigation directed specifically at this reaction has yet been carried out. Such a study, as well as increasing knowledge of the reactivity of $\text{SiH}_2$, should help to throw light on the mechanism, particularly on the role of the aromatic ring. It is known that phenyldisilane, $\text{PhSiH}_2\text{SiH}_3$, the product of the $\text{Si}−\text{H}$ insertion process, is formed in the reaction.⁸ What is not known is how the phenyl substituent affects the rate constant or indeed whether $\text{SiH}_2$ can interact with the benzene ring by direct addition. Some indication that this should be possible has been obtained by Suzuki et al.⁹ who found the 7-silanorcaradiene-like products of cycloaddition

In this paper, as well as studying the kinetics of the reaction of $SiH_{2}$ with $PhSiH_{3}$, we have carried out a brief investigation of the reaction of $SiH_{2}$ with benzene itself. This has been supplemented by ab initio calculations of the potential energy surface for the latter reaction together with RRKM calculations, to assist with understanding the mechanism. There have been no previous studies, either experimental or theoretical, of the silylene/benzene reaction.

## Experimental Section

### Equipment, Chemicals, and Method.
The apparatus and equipment for these studies have been described in detail previously. $^{10,11}$ Only essential and brief details are therefore included here. Silylene, $SiH_{2}$, was produced by the 193 nm flash photolysis of phenylsilane, $PhSiH_{3}$, using a Coherent Compex 100 exciplex laser. Photolysis pulses (beam cross section $4\ \text{cm} \times 1\ \text{cm}$) were fired into a variable temperature quartz reaction vessel with demountable windows, at right angles to its main axis. $SiH_{2}$ concentrations were monitored in real time by means of a Coherent 699-21 single-mode dye laser pumped by an Innova 90-5 argon ion laser and operating with Rhodamine 6G. The monitoring laser beam was multipassed 36 times along the vessel axis, through the reaction zone, to give an effective path length of $1.5\ \text{m}$. A portion of the monitoring beam was split off before entering the vessel for reference purposes. $SiH_{2}$ was detected by the monitoring laser at $17259.50\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, corresponding to the known $^{R}Q_{0,J}$ (5) strong rotation transition $^{12}$ in the $\tilde{A}^{1}B_{1}(0,2,0) \leftarrow \tilde{X}^{1}A_{1}(0,0,0)$ vibronic absorption band. Light signals were measured by a dual photodiode/differential amplifier combination, and signal decays were stored in a transient recorder (Datalab DL910) interfaced to a BBC microcomputer. This was used to average the decays of between 3 and 9 photolysis laser shots (at a repetition rate of 0.5 or 1 Hz). The averaged decay traces were processed by fitting the data to an exponential form using a nonlinear least-squares package. This analysis provided the values for first-order rate coefficients, $k_{\text{obs}}$, for removal of $SiH_{2}$ in the presence of known partial pressures of substrate gas. Initial concentrations of $SiH_{2}$ have been estimated$^{4}$ at 0.1 to $1.0\ \text{mTorr}$ in the reaction zone. Precise values are not needed when substrate pressures are in excess and decays are pseudo-first-order.

Gas mixtures for photolysis were made up as follows. Between 2.0 and $80\ \text{mTorr}$ of $PhSiH_{3}$ were used, together with inert diluent $(SF_{6})$ added to a total pressure of 10 Torr (1 Torr = $133.3\ \text{N m}^{-2}$). For the investigation of the reaction of $SiH_{2}$ with $PhSiH_{3}$, the latter serves as both precursor and substrate. For the experiments to study the reaction of $SiH_{2}$ with benzene, smaller amounts of $PhSiH_{3}$ were used ($2-5\ \text{mTorr}$) and benzene partial pressures of up to $800\ \text{mTorr}$, before the addition of $SF_{6}$. Pressures were measured by capacitance manometers (Chell, Barocel). All gases used in this work were frozen and rigorously pumped to remove any residual air prior to use. $PhSiH_{3}\ (99.9\%)$ was obtained from Ventron-Alfa (Petrarch). Benzene (anhydrous, $99.8\%$) was obtained from Aldrich. Sulfur hexafluoride, $SF_{6}$ (no GC-detectable impurities), was from Cambrian Gases.

### Ab Initio Calculations.
The electronic structure calculations were performed with the Gaussian 98 software package. $^{13}$ All structures were determined by energy minimization at the $\text{MP2} = \text{Full}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$ level. Transition state structures were characterized as first-order saddle points by calculation of the Hessian matrix. Stable structures, corresponding to energy minima, were identified by possessing no negative eigenvalues of the Hessian, while transition states were identified by having one and only one negative eigenvalue. The standard Gaussian-3 (G3) compound method$^{14}$ was employed to determine final energies for all local minima. For transition states the elements of the G3 method were used (viz: optimization to TS at $\text{HF}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$, frequencies at $\text{HF}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$, optimization to TS at $\text{MP2} = \text{full}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$, followed by four single-point energy determinations at the $\text{MP2} = \text{full}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$ geometry, viz: $\text{QCISD(T)}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$, $\text{MP4/6-31+G(d)}$, $\text{MP4/6-31G(2df,p)}$, and $\text{MP2} = \text{full}/6\text{-}31\text{G(large)}$) and the values were combined according to the G3 procedure. $^{14}$ The identities of the transition state structures were verified by calculation of Intrinsic Reaction Coordinates$^{15}$ (IRC) at the $\text{MP2} = \text{Full}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$ level. Reaction barriers were calculated as differences in G3 enthalpies at $298.15\ \text{K}$ (quoted simply as $\Delta H$ in the tables and figures of results). Where required harmonic frequencies were obtained from the values calculated at the $\text{HF}/6\text{-}31\text{G(d)}$ level adjusted by the correction factor 0.893 appropriate to this level. $^{16}$

Kinetics of SiH<sub>2</sub> + C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>6</sub>. At 193 nm benzene is a strong absorber of UV radiation,<sup>18</sup> with an absorption cross section of $1.4 \times 10^{-17}$ cm<sup>2</sup> which was verified by us. Since we were initially uncertain as to how this would affect our investigation we decided to undertake kinetic measurements in the normal way. Exciplex laser energies varied around 50−70 mJ/pulse. The scatter in $k_{obs}$ values was slightly larger than normal, and the signal decays were generally more noisy. Tests with up to 9 shots were carried out. The $k_{obs}$ values (3 shot averages) showed some scatter but not any systematic variation such as might suggest depletion of C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>6</sub>. The high sensitivities of detection of SiH<sub>2</sub> meant that only small quantities of PhSiH<sub>3</sub> (as precursor) were required. To maintain signal size these were slightly increased with temperature. However, at any given temperature precursor pressures were kept fixed to ensure a constant (but always small) contribution to $k_{obs}$ values. The reaction was investigated at 295, 341, and 410 K. At each temperature and at 10 Torr total pressure (SF<sub>6</sub> bath gas) at least six runs at different partial pressures of C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>6</sub> were carried out. The results of these runs at 295 K are shown in Figure 3, which demonstrates the linear dependence of $k_{obs}$ on [C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>6</sub>] expected for second-order kinetics. The data at the other temperatures lie rather close to those at 295 K and are not shown to maintain clarity. However they also give the linear fits within the reasonable uncertainties of the data. The scatter of points here is, nevertheless, larger than that for the experiments with PhSiH<sub>3</sub> alone. Because of the increasing scatter at 410 K, these experiments were not pursued to higher temperatures. In addition to these experiments, another set of runs was carried out at 295 and 410 K. In these, the total pressure $(SF_{6})$ was varied in the range 3 to 100 Torr, to test the pressure dependence of the second-order rate constants. The data were obtained in the same way as those at 10 Torr, although since second-order behavior had been established at 10 Torr, only two or three benzene partial pressures were tried at each total pressure. The study was limited to verifying whether there were indications of pressure dependence at the highest and lowest temperatures. The pressure range was limited by practical considerations. Above ca. 100 Torr transient signals became too small to measure reliably, and below 3 Torr signal decays became too noisy for reliable measurement. The rate constants obtained from these measurements are also included in Table 2. The rate constants are clearly pressure dependent, but the effect is not very large. Although this is suggestive of a possible third-body assisted association reaction, for reasons given later, such an interpretation has to be treated with caution.

For substrates with strong UV absorptions at 193 nm, we have previously found mechanistic complications arising from reactions of $SiH_{2}$ with photochemical transients generated from the substrate. $^{19,20}$ This was usually evident from a dependence of decay constants on laser intensity. We therefore decided to investigate this phenomenon in the present system. A series of measurements was carried at room temperature with 82 mTorr of $C_{6}H_{6}$ (with $SF_{6}$ added to 10 Torr) over a wide range of laser pulse energies (which determine the photon density). Figure 4 shows the dependence of $k_{obs}$ on these energies. It is quite clear, despite some scatter, that there is a significant dependence. This strongly suggests the likelihood of mechanistic complications in this reaction system.

Quantum Chemical (ab Initio) Calculations. The calculations were limited to the species we feel are the most likely to be involved in the reaction of silylene with benzene. Apart from the reactant species, $SiH_{2}+C_{6}H_{6}$, six other minima and five transition states were found in these calculations. The minima correspond to two adducts (both of them weakly bound complexes but with different extents of $\pi$ character),7-silanorcarcadiene, 7-silacycloheptatriene, tricyclo[4.1.0.0 $^{2,7}$ ]1-sila-hept-3-ene, and phenylsilane. The minima and transition states, TS1-TS4, linking these species are shown on the PES in Figure 5. The two complexes lie so close in enthalpy that they are not distinguished in the diagram. The transition state, TSa, linking them is also not shown. No transition states could be found between the reactants and the complexes. Total enthalpies (at 298 K) and enthalpy values for all structures relative to the $SiH_{2}+C_{6}H_{6}$ pair are listed in Table 3. The structures of the minima involved in these relatively low energy pathways and the transition states are shown in Figure 6.

All the stable species found here lie below the reactants in enthalpy and are therefore potential products. The initial interaction of $SiH_{2}$ with benzene produces two adducts which differ from one another as follows. In $C_{6}H_{6}\cdot SiH_{2}$ complex 1, the $SiH_{2}$ moiety is positioned above one of the C-atoms in $C_{6}H_{6}$ with two H-atoms (of the $SiH_{2}$) oriented away from the ring (in an anti configuration). Although this might be thought of as a $\sigma$-bonded complex, the C-Si bond length (2.586 Å) is rather long compared with a normal C-Si bond (ca 1.87 Å). In $C_{6}H_{6}\cdot SiH_{2}$ complex 2, the $SiH_{2}$ moeity is positioned over one of the C-C bonds with one H-atom oriented over the ring and the other away from the ring. This is much more like the $\pi$-bonded complex found in the reaction of $SiH_{2}$ with $C_{2}H_{4}.^{21}$ The two complexes can interconvert with one another via TSa which actually lies slightly below them in enthalpy. The finding of transition states at energies slightly below those of linked intermediates is an artifact of the calculations and has been found previously in other reaction systems. $^{22,23}$ What this indicates is that complexes 1 and 2 interconvert readily and that either of them could be an intermediate for the remaining products. However for convenience, specific products are described as coming from one or the other complex, because they were linked by the IRC calculations. It is noteworthy that the only product linked to complex 1 is phenylsilane, the most stable molecule on the surface. RRKM Calculations. These were limited to calculations of the degree of collisional stabilization (under experimental conditions) of 7-silanorcaradiene or 7-silacycloheptatriene on the assumption that one or other of these molecules was the sole product. RRKM theory is well established, $^{28}$ and its implementation in gas-phase studies has been described in detail in previous papers. $^{3,4,6}$ Computational details for the present systems are included in the Supporting Information. The results of these calculations are shown in Table 4. The figures in this table indicate that if 7-silanorcaradiene were the product, very little of it would be formed (0.022−1.334%) over the pressure range of interest. By contrast if 7-silacycloheptatriene was the product, it would be substantially stabilized (39.5−91.6%). The reason for this striking difference can be largely attributed to the substantial difference in the energy well depths of these two possible products relative to $SiH_2 +$ benzene.

$SiH_{2}$ + Benzene: Kinetic Considerations. This reaction system has not been previously investigated. However the interpretation of the data obtained in the present study is not clear-cut. Despite the apparent consistency of the $SiH_{2}$ decay traces with second-order kinetics, and the finding of a negative activation energy, as commonly encountered for $SiH_{2}$ reactions, $^{3,4,6}$ the observation of intensity dependencies for the decay constants shows that the mechanism is more complicated than a straightforward reaction with the substrate molecule alone. Since benzene is a strong absorber at 193 nm and is known to produce photochemical products, $^{24}$ it seems likely that $SiH_{2}$ decay rates are enhanced by reaction with one (or more) of these. A direct photoproduct of benzene could easily increase in concentration with laser intensity and, if it were reactive with $SiH_{2}$, would increase the decay constants. Molecular beam techniques $^{25−27}$ have shown that a variety of radical fragmentation products, prominent being $C_{6}H_{5}$ + H, are formed from benzene at 193 nm. Ab initio (G2 M level) calculations $^{24}$ have shown a complex energy surface for $C_{6}H_{6}$ species. On the assumption of the “hot benzene” mechanism for photodissociation,

The phenyl $(C_6H_5)$ and cyclohexadienyl $(C_6H_7)$ radicals should be formed early enough and in high enough concentration to make reaction with $SiH_2$ feasible. Reaction rates between $SiH_2$ and free radicals are likely to be barrierless and fast. Although it is not possible to quantify this fully in the present study, if we assume that $SiH_2$ reacts with the radicals on every collision ($k=ca. 3 \times 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{cm^3\ molecule^{-1}s^{-1}}$ at 298 K) the measured rate constants of Table 2 (295 K) could be accounted for by between 3 and 6% of radical formation from benzene. While this seems plausible and could mean that the decays are dominated by these reactions, further analysis of these data would require a knowledge of quantum yields and precise pulse intensities (beyond the scope of the present study). The best that can be said for the data obtained in this work is that the measured values of Table 2 represent upper limits for the true rate constants for reaction of $SiH_2$ with benzene (but see discussion of RRKM results). Despite this inconclusive result, the numbers do throw some light on the question of the extent of the addition of $SiH_2$ to the aromatic ring of $PhSiH_3$. If we assume that rate constants for this process are the same as those for reaction with benzene, then the maximum contributions of this pathway to the overall rate constants for reaction of $SiH_2$ with $PhSiH_3$ are 2.4% (at ca. 298K), 2.6% (at ca. 342 K), and 2.5% (at ca. 396 K). This strongly supports the conclusion (above) that reaction of $SiH_2$ with $PhSiH_3$ occurs overwhelmingly via the $Si-H$ insertion process.

Quantum Chemical (ab Initio) Calculations of the $SiC_6H_8$ Energy Surface, RRKM Calculations, and the Mechanism of Reaction of $SiH_2$ with Benzene. Despite the uncertainties of the kinetics of the $SiH_2 +$ benzene reaction, we felt it was, nevertheless, interesting to consider what mechanistic processes might be likely in this reaction system and whether the rate constant limit found in this study is at least consistent with them. There have been no previous calculations of this surface. The calculations carried out here suggest the exothermic formation of either 7-silanorcaradiene or 7-silacycloheptatriene assuming the complexes themselves are not capable of stabilization (which seems likely, given the very small barrier, TS3, of ca. $10\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}$ for conversion to 7-silanorcaradiene). The mechanism is shown in Scheme 1 below.

Whether 7-silanorcaradiene or 7-silacycloheptatriene is the ultimate product depends on the relative rate constants in the scheme. There are several considerations. For either product to be formed, one of the initially produced vibrationally excited species needs to be stabilized. This means that either or both of steps (2) and (4) need to be efficient, i.e. faster than steps (-1) and (-3) respectively, and additionally, reactivation steps (-2) and (-4) need to be slow. For 7-silanorcaradiene to be the product, step (3) needs to be

slow compared to (2) as well. If this is not so, then 7-silacycloheptatriene is the more likely product. The RRKM calculations carried out here have thrown some light on this. They show that if formed 7-silanorcaradiene would hardly be stabilized at all, whereas 7-silacycloheptatriene would be largely stabilized. This provides a clue. In the absence of any small positive energy barrier to be overcome, the inefficiency of the reaction of $SiH_2$ with benzene when compared with the high efficiency of silylene addition processes (generally greater that $50\%^{3,4,6}$) suggests that redissociation of initially formed species is probably occurring. This tends to suggest that vibrationally excited 7-silacycloheptatriene is not being readily formed because, if it were, it should be largely stabilized. The implication of this is that step (3) is not fast enough to compete. Thus, although vibrationally excited 7-silanorcaradiene is initially formed, it neither is stabilized (because step (2) is too slow to compete) nor reacts further, but simply redissociates. $^{29}$ A mechanism where step (3) was fast would be much more efficient and faster overall. It should be pointed out that, although the barrier for step (3) lies below the reaction threshold, the transition state for this isomerization, TS4, is tight compared with the loose transition state for redissociation. We have not investigated this computationally here, but at only $17.2\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}$ below threshold, it is very plausible that TS4 could be rate limiting. Thus the findings of this study, although not definitive, offer some support for the mechanism of Scheme 1.

### Summary and Conclusions

The gas-phase reaction of $\mathrm{SiH_2}$ with phenylsilane has been shown to occur overwhelmingly by $\mathrm{Si-H}$ insertion, and its kinetic behavior is very similar to that of $\mathrm{SiH_2}$ with other substituted silanes. When $\mathrm{SiH_2}$ is generated at 193 nm in the presence of benzene in the gas phase, its kinetic behavior suggests that, as well as probable reaction with benzene, reaction with photodecomposition products of benzene is also occurring. This shows that to obtain true rate data for this reaction, it must be studied at wavelengths where benzene photochemistry is unimportant. A quantum chemical energy surface, calculated at the G3 level of theory, suggests that 7-silanorcaradiene and/or 7-silacycloheptatriene should be the reaction products of $\mathrm{SiH_2 + benzene}$. RRKM calculations suggest that, if formed, 7-silacycloheptatriene should be collisionally stabilized and the reaction fast. The observed slow rate of reaction implies that initially formed vibrationally excited 7-silanorcaradiene mainly redissociates. The RRKM calculations suggest that very high pressures would be needed to stabilize it by collisions.
