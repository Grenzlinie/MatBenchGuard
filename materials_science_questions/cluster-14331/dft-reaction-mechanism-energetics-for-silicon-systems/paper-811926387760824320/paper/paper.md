# Kinetic calculation for the reaction of H with $\text{Si}_2\text{H}_6$ using the variational transition state theory

ZHANG Qingzhu (张庆竹), RU Miaoyan (茹淼焱), WANG Minggang (王明刚), WANG Shaokun (王少坤) & GU Yueshu (顾月妹)

School of Chemistry and Chemical Engineering, Shandong University, Jinan 250100, China
Correspondence should be addressed to Gu Yueshu (email: guojz@icm.sdu.edu.cn)

Received May 31, 2001

**Abstract** The reaction of disilane with atomic hydrogen has been studied. This reaction involves both substitution and abstraction. Calculations show that the hydrogen abstraction is the strongest competing channel. The canonical variational transition state theory with a small curvature tunneling correction (SCT) has been used for the kinetic calculation. The theoretical results are in good agreement with the available experimental data. Comparing the reactions of atomic hydrogen with disilane and silane, it can be seen that the reactivity of the Si-H bond is higher in $\text{Si}_2\text{H}_6$ than that in $\text{SiH}_4$.

**Keywords:** *ab initio* calculation, reaction mechanism, variational transition state, tunneling effect, rate constants.

Exposing silicon-hydrogen film to atomic hydrogen can result in etching, with silane as product $^{[1,2]}$. The etching process has a significant application in plasma chemical vapor deposition (CVD) process and semiconductor device process. The proposed mechanism for etching can be given as $\equiv\text{Si—SiH}_3+\text{H}\rightarrow\equiv\text{Si}+\text{SiH}_4$, where "$\equiv$" indicates three bonds from silicon to the surface. If silicon bonds to the surface could be replaced by $\text{Si—H}$ bond, the etching mechanism would be identical with the reaction of disilane and atomic hydrogen. So the reaction of disilane with hydrogen can be used as the model for the etching process $^{[3]}$. Therefore, it is necessary to study the mechanism and the kinetic nature of this reaction. Because of the high reactivity of disilane, two primary processes have been identified in the reaction of disilane with hydrogen: substitution and abstraction:

$$\text{H}_3\text{Si—SiH}_3+\text{H}\rightarrow\text{H}_3\text{Si—SiH}_2+\text{H}_2 \tag{1}$$

$$\text{H}_3\text{Si—SiH}_3+\text{H}\rightarrow\text{H}_3\text{Si}+\text{SiH}_4 \tag{2}$$

Several experimental studies have been done for this reaction. Pollock et al.$^{[4]}$ found that abstraction was twice as fast as substitution using deuterium-labeling technique. Austin and his coworker$^{[5]}$ obtained the overall rate constant at 305 K, $(3.7\pm0.6)\times10^{-12}\ \text{L·molecule}^{-1}·\text{s}^{-1}$. Fabry et al.$^{[6]}$ measured the overall rate constant by using flash photolysis over the temperature range of 494—408 K, and the Arrhenius expression he obtained is $k=(7.0\pm0.6)\times10^{-11}\exp\ ((-1155\pm25)/T)$ (in units $\text{L·molecule}^{-1}·\text{s}^{-1}$); they also estimated that the abstraction reaction is four times faster than the substitution reaction. Unfortunately, there are several deficiencies in these experi-

mental results: (i) Agreement between these experimental results is less than satisfactory, and this conflict between results often appears to be associated with the different experimental methods employed. For example, at 305 K, the overall rate constant obtained by Austin is $(3.7\pm0.6)\times10^{-12}\ \text{L·molecule}^{-1}·\text{s}^{-1}$, while that obtained by Fabry is $1.59\times10^{-12}\ \text{L·molecule}^{-1}·\text{s}^{-1}$. (ii) The rate constants were measured at a narrow temperature range. (iii) Especially the branching ratio values were reported with relatively large uncertainties.

The theoretical work is very limited for the title reaction. Only one theoretical study was reported. In 1993, Kerwin and his coworkers$^{[2]}$ had been studied the mechanism of the substitution reaction. Since hydrogen has both nucleophilic and electrophilic character, they thought that the substitution reaction had two pathways: hydrogen may make a frontside attack on the Si—Si bond, and hydrogen may attack from the backside of the silyl group:

$$\ce{H3Si-SiH3 + H -> H3Si + SiH4} \quad \text{Front attack} \tag{2a}$$

$$\ce{H3Si-SiH3 + H -> H3Si + SiH4} \quad \text{Back attack} \tag{2b}$$

Kerwin and his coworkers optimized the geometries of transition states of the substitution reactions at MP2/6-31g(d,p) level, and calculated the activation energy of 12.55 and 24.27 kJ/mol for the substitution reactions for frontside and backside attack using G2 theory.

Experimental results show that the hydrogen abstraction is the strongest competing channel, but Keiwin did not study this channel in details. Other theoretical studies have not been reported. Therefore, it is necessary to make a theoretical study about this reaction of atomic hydrogen with disilane. In this paper, we have revealed the detailed reaction mechanism using high level *ab initio* molecular orbital theory. Based on the *ab initio* data, the kinetic calculation has been carried out using the canonical variational transition state theory with the small-curvature tunneling correction method. Several features of this work are as the follows: (i) Overall reaction mechanism has been revealed; (ii) the potential energy profile surface has been calculated at the G3MP2 level; (iii) the rate constants have been obtained over a wide temperature range of 200—1000 K for each channel; (iv) the branching ratio has been discussed; (v) the calculated overall rate constants have been compared with the available experimental values. Our theoretical results will help understand the etching process of silicon-hydrogen film by atomic hydrogen.

## 1 Computational methods

*Ab initio* calculations have been carried out using Gaussian 94 program. The geometries of the reactant, transition states and products have been optimized at the UMP2(full)/6-31G(d,p) level. Vibrational frequencies and zero point energy (scaled by a factor of 0.95) have been calculated at the same level of theory. Transition states are subject to two criteria: the number of imaginary frequencies (NIMG = 1) and the intrinsic reaction coordinate (IRC) calculation confirming that they connect the designated reactants and products. At the UMP2(full)/6-31G(d,p) level, the minimum energy path (MEP) has been obtained with a gradient step size of 0.05

$amu^{1/2} \cdot$ bohr in mass-weighted Cartesian coordinates, and the force constant matrices of the stationary and the selected non-stationary points near the transition state along the MEP have also been calculated for each channel. Because the shape of the MEP is important for the calculation of the rate constant, the energy of the stationary points and the non-stationary points were refined by the G3MP2//UMP2 method, which applies the G3MP2 theory at the UMP2 geometries. The G3MP2 energy is given as

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(\mathrm{G3MP2}) =& E\ [\mathrm{QCISD(T)/6-31G(d)}] + E\ (\mathrm{UMP2/G3MP2large}) \\
&-E\ [\mathrm{UMP2/6-31G(d)}]+\mathrm{HLC}+\mathrm{ZPE},
\end{aligned}
$$

where HLC is a high-level correction, $\mathrm{HLC} = -0.004471\ n_{\alpha}-0.004808\ n_{\beta}$, $n_{\alpha}$ and $n_{\beta}$ are the number of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ valence electrons, respectively. It need to be pointed that we have made two modifications in our G3MP2 calculations: (i) the geometries are obtained at the UMP2((full)/6-31G(d,p) level instead of MP2 (full)/6-31G(d) and (ii) the zero point energy (ZPE) was obtained at the UMP2(full)/6-31G(d,P) level and scaled by 0.95.

The initial information obtained from our *ab initio* calculations allowed us to calculate the variational rate constants including the tunneling effects. The canonical variational theory (CVT) rate constant for temperature $T$ is given as

$$
k^{\mathrm{CVT}}(T)=\min _{s} k^{\mathrm{GT}}(T, s), \tag{3}
$$

where

$$
k^{\mathrm{GT}}(T, s)=\frac{\sigma k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{h} \frac{Q^{\mathrm{GT}}(T, s)}{\Phi^{\mathrm{R}}(T)} \mathrm{e}^{-V_{\mathrm{MEP}}(s) / k_{\mathrm{B}} T}, \tag{4}
$$

where $k^{\mathrm{GT}}(T,s)$ is the generalized transition state theory rate constant at the dividing surface $s$, $\sigma$ the symmetry factor accounting for the possibility of more than one symmetry-related reaction path, $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ Boltzmann's constant, $h$ Planck's constant, $\Phi^{\mathrm{R}}(T)$ the reactant partition function per unit volume, excluding symmetry numbers for rotation, and $Q^{\mathrm{GT}}(T, s)$ the partition function of a generalized transition state at $s$ with a local zero of energy at $V_{\mathrm{MEP}}(s)$ and with all rotational symmetry numbers set to unity. The tunneling correction has been considered by using the small curvature tunneling effect (SCT) method. All the kinetical calculations have been carried out using the POLYRATE 7.8 Program.

As shown in fig. 1, the geometric parameters of $Si_{2}H_{6}$, $SiH_{4}$, $SiH_{3}$ and $H_{2}$ are in good agreement with the available experimental values. From this result, it might be inferred that the same accuracy could be expected for the calculated transition state geometries. As can be seen from table 1, the scaled vibrational frequencies for both the reactant and the products agree well with the experimentally observed fundamentals, and the maximum absolute error is $80\ cm^{-1}$. So geometric parameters and frequencies calculated at the UMP2(full)/6-31G(d,p) are reliable.

In order to choose a reliable theory level to calculate the energy, we have calculated the bond dissociation energy for the $Si_2H_5$-H and $SiH_3$-H. The values at G3MP2, UMP2(full)/6-31G(d,p), and UQCISD(T)/6-31G(d) levels, and the experimental data are listed in table 3. The experiment gives the spectrum dissociation energy, and so the calculated dissociation energy has been corrected for the ZPE. The calculated results at the G3MP2 level are in good agreement with the experimental values. Both the UMP2 and the UQCISD(T) level underestimate bond dissociation energy. So the G3MP2 theory is a good choice to calculate accurate energy for the title system.

### 2.1 Reaction mechanism
The reactant disilane has two geometries: the staggered and the eclipsed. The energy of the eclipsed is 3.89 kJ/mol higher than that of the staggered at the G3MP2 level. So in this paper the staggered form is thought to be the stationary geometry of disilane.

As mentioned above, the reaction of H with $Si_2H_6$ involves both substitution and abstraction. The abstraction proceeds via the transition state $TS_1$. In principle, $TS_1$ has four possible structural forms defined by two dihedral angles: $\tau(H^{(4)}Si^{(2)}Si^{(1)}H^{(6)})$ and $\tau(Si^{(2)}Si^{(1)}H^{(6)}H^{(9)})$. The four forms are (cis, trans), (cis, cis), (trans, trans) and (trans, cis). However, optimizations at the UMP2(full)/6-31G(d,p) level reveal that only the structure (cis, cis) is the true transition state, which is characterized by only one imaginary frequency. By comparing the geometries of the reactant, product and the transition state ($TS_1$), it can be seen that the H atom attacks to one of the H atoms of $Si_2H_6$ with a slightly bent orientation angle of $176.8^\circ$ . In the transition state $TS_1$ structure, the breaking Si-H bond is stretched by 8.7%, while the forming H-H bond is 49.8% longer than the equilibrium value of the $H_2$. Therefore, $TS_1$ is reactant like, and the abstraction reaction will proceed via an early transition state.

Since atomic hydrogen has both nucleophilic and electrophilic characters, the substitution reaction has two pathways: hydrogen makes a frontside attack on the Si-Si bond and hydrogen attacks from the backside of the silyl group. For the transition state $TS_{2a}$ of frontside attack, the attacking hydrogen $H^{(9)}$ bridges these two silicons in a near-symmetric manner, and is a little closer to $Si^{(1)}$ (0.184 nm) than to $Si^{(2)}$ (0.196 nm). The breaking $Si^{(1)}$-$Si^{(2)}$ bond elongates a little which is 0.005 nm longer than the corresponding value in disilane. For the transition state $TS_{2b}$ of backside attack, the breaking $Si^{(1)}$-$Si^{(2)}$ is 0.002 nm longer than the corresponding value in $TS_{2a}$, while the forming $Si^{(1)}$-$H^{(9)}$ is 17.32% longer than the equilibrium value of the $SiH_4$. The metric parameters for the local environment around $Si^{(2)}$ have not significantly changed from the corresponding values in disilane. $TS_{2b}$ has high symmetry ($C_{3v}$). The structure of the transition state $TS_{2b}$ is reactant like, and the substitution reaction for backside attack will proceed via an early transition state.

The energy barriers $E_a$ and the heats of reaction $\Delta H$ calculated at G3MP2 for each channel involved in the reaction of H with $Si_2H_6$ are listed in table 4. From our calculated results, it can be seen that both the abstraction and the substitution are exothermic. The abstraction reaction has the

smallest energy barrier of 9.25 kJ/mol. It means that the abstraction will have the dominant channel, which is in good agreement with the experimental estimation. Pollock⁽⁴⁾ assumed that the Arrhenius prefactors for the abstraction and the substitution are the same, and this difference in rates implies that the barrier of the abstraction is 1.7—3.3 kJ/mol lower than that of the substitution. Our calculated barrier of the abstraction is 2.93 and 12.51 kJ/mol lower than that of the substitution reactions for frontside and backside attack, respectively. The substitution reaction for backside attack has the highest barrier, indicating that the substitution reaction will occur mainly by a frontside attack. This conclusion is in agreement with the analysis of Fabry⁽⁶⁾. The barriers obtained by Kerwin⁽³⁾ are 12.55 and 24.27 kJ/mol for frontside and backside attack using G2 theory, and these values match our results very well. The activation energy of the reaction H with Si₂H₆ obtained by Fabry⁽⁶⁾ is 9.58 kJ/mol by fitting the experimental rate constants, and this value is in good agreement with our calculated barrier 9.25 kJ/mol of the abstraction reaction.

There are two reverse reactions of hydrogen with disilane: the reaction of H₂ with Si₂H₅ and the reaction of SiH₃ with SiH₄. Our calculation shows that both reactions have higher energy barriers and are endothermic. So they are expected to have smaller rate constants. Loh and Jasinski had measured the rate constant of the reaction of SiH₃ with SiH₄ directly, and found that it was so slow that they can only determine an upper bound.

It is worth comparing the reaction of atomic hydrogen with disilane and silane. The reaction of H with SiH₄ occurs via a direct hydrogen abstraction mechanism ⁽⁹⁾. Because of the high reactivity of disilane, the reaction of dislane with hydrogen involves not only abstraction but also substitution. The barrier of H with SiH₄ is 21.34 kJ/mol ⁽⁹⁾, while the barrier of the abstraction reaction of H with Si₂H₆ is 9.25 kJ/mol at G3MP2 theory level. The latter is 12.09 kJ/mol lower than the former, which is reasonable in view of $D_{298}(\text{Si}_2\text{H}_5\text{-H})$ lying 10.75 kJ/mol below $D_{298}(\text{SiH}_3\text{-H})$. It means that the hydrogen abstraction from Si₂H₆ will be easier than from SiH₄. The conclusion can be also drawn from the hydrogen abstraction by atomic oxygen from Si₂H₆ and SiH₄. Taylor- lor⁽¹²⁾ estimated that the barrier of O with Si₂H₆ is 6 kJ/mol below that of the reaction of O with SiH₄, and the reaction of O with Si₂H₆ is approximately 20 times faster than the reaction of O with SiH₄. Since C and Si belong to the same group, we also compared the reactivity of C-H bond in C₂H₆ and that of Si-H bond in Si₂H₆. The barrier of the abstraction reaction of H with C₂H₆ is 50.61 kJ/mol, while the barrier of the abstraction reaction of H with Si₂H₆ is 9.25 kJ/mol, indicating that the hydrogen abstraction from Si₂H₆ is much easier than that from C₂H₆.

### 2.2 Kinetic calculation
The canonical variational transition state theory (CVT) with a small curvature tunneling correction (SCT), which has been successfully performed for several reactions ⁽⁹⁾, is an effective method to calculate the rate constant. In this paper, we have used this method to calculate the rate constants of each channel involved in the reaction of hydrogen with disilane.

In order to calculate the rate constant, 26 points have been selected near the transition state region along MEP——13 points in the reactant zone and 13 points in the product zone, and their energy gradients and force constants have also been calculated. Table 5 gives the computational rate constants for each channel in the temperature range of 200—1000 K. The rate constants of the abstraction reaction are noted as $k_1$, while the rate constants of the substitution reaction for frontside and backside attack are noted as $k_{2\text{a}}$ and $k_{2\text{b}}$. The total rate constants of the substitution reaction are noted as $k_2$, and $k_2 = k_{2\text{a}} + k_{2\text{b}}$. The overall rate constants are noted as $k$, and $k = k_1 + k_2 = k_1 + k_{2\text{a}} + k_{2\text{b}}$. The branching ratios of the abstraction reaction and the substitution reaction are noted as $k_1/k$ and $k_2/k$.

As seen from table 5, the abstraction is the fastest reaction channel in the whole temperature range studied due to the lowest barrier. However, with the temperature increasing, the substitution reaction of frontside attack becomes the competitive reaction channel. For example, at 600 K, the abstraction reaction is only one time faster than the substitution reaction of frontside attack. Due to the highest barrier height, the contribution of the substitution reaction for backside attack is minor. In the lower temperature range, the rate constant $k_{2\text{b}}$ is small and can be negligible, $k_2 = k_{2\text{a}} + k_{2\text{b}} \approx k_{2\text{a}}$. For example, at 500 K the reaction of frontside attack is 21.9 times faster than the reaction of backside attack. However, the rates of backside attack rise more rapidly than that of frontside attack. When the temperature $T \geq 900$ K, the rate constant $k_{2\text{b}}$ cannot be negligible and will account for some branching ratio to the overall rate constant. For example, at $T$=1000 K, $k_{2\text{b}}/k$ = 9.1.

Fabry fitted the Arrhenius expression of the overall reaction rate constants over the temperature range of 294—408 K, and we calculated four values from this expression and listed them in table 5. It can be seen that our CVT/SCT rate constants are in good agreement with the values of Fabry. For example, at 305 K our CVT/SCT overall rate constant is $1.39 \times 10^{-12}$ $\text{L} \cdot \text{molecule}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$, while the experimental value of Fabry is $1.59 \times 10^{-12}$ $\text{L} \cdot \text{molecule}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$. However, the experimental value of Austin is $(3.7\pm0.6) \times 10^{-12}$ $\text{L} \cdot \text{molecule}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$ at 305 K. It is

obvious that the value of Austin is larger than that of fabry and ours.

Disilane can be seen as silane that one of H is substituted by silyl. The effect of the silyl substitution on the strength and reactivity of the Si-H bond is an interesting topic. The effect can be seen by evaluating $k/n$, the rate constant corrected for the reaction-path degeneracy $n$ (where $n$ is the number of the Si-H bond. $n=6$ for $Si_2H_6$ and $n=4$ for $SiH_4$). At 298 K, the $k/n$ is $1.87×10^{-13}\ \text{L} \cdot \text{molecule}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$ for the hydrogen abstraction from disilane, while that is $0.5×10^{-13}\ \text{L} \cdot \text{molecule}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$ for the hydrogen abstraction from silane. It confirms the suggestion of Walsh$^{[13]}$ that the silyl substitution weakens the Si-H bond strength and increases its reactivity. The conclusion can be drawn from the reactions of various atoms with disilane and silane. At 298 K, the $k/n$ of the H abstraction by O atom from disilane and from silane is $1.1×10^{-12}$ and $8.8×10^{-14}\ \text{L} \cdot \text{molecule}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$, respectively. At 298 K, the $k/n$ of the H abstraction by Br atom from disilane$^{[14]}$ and from silane$^{[15]}$ is $6.6×10^{-12}$ and $2.4×10^{-14}\ \text{L} \cdot \text{molecule}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$, respectively.

## 3 Conclusions

Based on the ab initio data, the kinetic calculation has been carried out using the canonical variational transition state theory with the small-curvature tunneling correction method. The calculated results are in good agreement with the experimental values, showing that the selected theories and methods are reliable. From the above studies the following conclusions can be drawn:

(i) The reaction of atomic hydrogen with disilane involves both substitution and abstraction. The abstraction is the dominant channel in the lower temperature range. However, with the temperature increasing, the substitution reaction of frontside attack becomes the competitive reaction channel.

(ii) The substitution reaction had two pathways: hydrogen may attack from the backside of the silyl group, and hydrogen may make a frontside attack on the Si-Si bond. The channel attack on the Si-Si bond is the dominant channel.

(iii) The reactivity of the Si-H bond is higher in $Si_2H_6$ than that in $SiH_4$, and the silyl substitution weakens the Si-H bond strength and increases its reactivity.
