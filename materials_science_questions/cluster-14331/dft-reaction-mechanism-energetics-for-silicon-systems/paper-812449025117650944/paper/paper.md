# Quantum chemical study of silicon oxide surface reaction with (poly)silane

Akitomo Tachibana *, Tasuku Yano

Department of Molecular Engineering, Kyoto University, Kyoto 606-01, Japan

## Abstract

We have performed a quantum chemical study of the deposition of $SiH_{4}$ and $Si_{2}H_{6}$ and found that these gases are more easily decomposed on the surface of $SiO_{2}$ than by themselves. Moreover, although the bond energy of Si-H (89.4 kcal/mol) is larger than that of Si-Si (72.7 kcal/mol), the Si-H bond is more easily broken with the activation energy $(E_{a})$ of 39.0 kcal/mol than the Si-Si bond with $E_{a}$ of 53.7 kcal/mol on the $SiO_{2}$ surface. These high reactivities of (poly)silane with $SiO_{2}$ surface agree qualitatively well with the experimental observation.

Keywords: Silicon oxide; Silane; CVD; Reaction; Quantum chemistry

## 1. Introduction

Toward the future development of ultra-large scale integration (ULSI) technology, the atomic scale fine control of various silicon (Si) interface structures is considered a fundamentally indispensable subject, where the thermal chemical vapor deposition (CVD) processes should play an important role for fabrica- tion of novel Si semiconductor devices (see, for example, Refs. [1-3]). However, the CVD reaction mechanisms of a variety of source gases on various reaction surfaces have not been clarified so far. In this connection, quite recently, Ohmi et al. have devised ultra clean gas supplying system and applied it to the very basic study of the elementary processes of CVD and found very high reactivity of silane $(SiH_{4})$ and disilane $(Si_{2}H_{6})$ on the silicon oxide $(SiO_{2})$ surface [4].

In this paper, we have devised model reaction species and reaction paths and studied the local electronic property on the surface of $SiO_{2}$ pertaining to this interesting reaction mechanism in terms of ab initio molecular orbital (MO) theory of quantum chemistry. ## 2. Methods of calculation

Molecular orbital calculations were performed with the GAUSSIAN 94 program [5]. The geometries were optimized by the analytical energy gradient method with the inclusion of correlation for all electrons at the Hartree-Fock level by using the standard 6-31G** basis set (denoted as HF/6-31G**) [6]. Theoretical harmonic vibrational frequencies were obtained from analytical second derivatives to verify each equilibrium structure as a true energy minimum or a saddle point. Zero-point vibrational energies (ZPE) were then scaled by a factor of 0.89 [6]. To obtain reliable energetics, a series of single-point energy calculations on the HF/6-31G** geometries were carried out using the Møller-Plesset perturbation theory (denoted as MP2/6-31G**//HF/6-31G**).

### 3.2. Reaction paths

For Eqs. (1), (2a) and (2b) in Fig. 2, we assume the following reaction paths:
$$
\begin{align}
\mathrm{SiH_3-O-SiH_3 + SiH_4} & \\
\quad \to \mathrm{SiH_3-O-SiH_3 + SiH_4} & \tag{1} \\
\mathrm{SiH_3-O-SiH_3 + SiH_3-SiH_3} & \\
\quad \to \mathrm{SiH_3-O-SiH_2-SiH_3 + SiH_4} & \tag{2a} \\
\mathrm{SiH_3-O-SiH_3 + SiH_3-SiH_3} & \\
\quad \to \mathrm{SiH_3-O-SiH_3 + SiH_3-SiH_3} & \tag{2b}
\end{align}
$$

Eq. (1) and Eqs. (2a) and (2b) are, respectively, the reactions of $SiH_4$ and $Si_2H_6$. The Si-H bond is broken in Eqs. (1) and (2a), while Si-Si bond is broken in Eq. (2b). Fig. 3 shows TS's of Eqs. (1), (2a) and (2b) (**TS1, TS2a** and **TS2b**). The transition vector that corresponds to the reaction coordinate at the TS shows that new Si-O and Si-H bonds are formed while old Si-O and Si-H or Si-Si bonds are broken. The activation energies $(E_{\mathrm{a}})$ of Eqs. (1), (2a) and (2b) are 36.6, 40.7 and 55.4 kcal/mol, respectively. Reaction enthalpies are almost zero.

For the reaction schemes Eqs. (3a), (3b), (4a) and (4b) in Fig. 2, we assume the following reaction paths:
$$
\begin{align}
\mathrm{SiH_4} & \to \mathrm{SiH_2 + H_2} \tag{3a} \\
\mathrm{SiH_4} & \to \mathrm{SiH_3 + H} \tag{3b} \\
\mathrm{SiH_3-SiH_3} & \to \mathrm{SiH_3-SiH + H_2} \tag{4a} \\
\mathrm{SiH_3-SiH_3} & \to \mathrm{2SiH_3} \tag{4b}
\end{align}
$$

Eqs. (3a) and (3b) and Eqs. (4a) and (4b) correspond, respectively, to the decomposition of $SiH_4$ and $Si_2H_6$. Reaction enthalpies of Eqs. (3a), (3b), (4a) and (4b) are 55.6, 83.4, 55.5 and 68.8 kcal/mol, respectively. Silylene is more easy to form than the silyl radical. Comparison of $E_a$ of Eqs. (3b) and (4b) shows Si-Si is more easily broken than Si-H.

Comparing these energy diagrams, decompositions of the source gases are found to be more facile on the $SiO_2$ surface than in the gas phase. Moreover, on the $SiO_2$ surface, the Si-H bond in $Si_2H_6$ is weaker than the Si-Si bond. The order of ease in decomposition of $Si_2H_6$ is different on the $SiO_2$ surface from that in the gas phase.

For Eq. (5) in Fig. 2, we assume the following reaction path:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{4} \\
& \quad \rightarrow \underset{\mathrm{CX1}}{\left[\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{4}\right]} \\
& \quad \rightarrow \underset{\mathrm{CX2}}{\left[\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{2}+\mathrm{H}_{2}\right]} \\
& \quad \rightarrow\left[\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{2}\right]+\mathrm{H}_{2}
\end{aligned} \tag{5}
$$

Eq. (5) corresponds to the formation of silylene on the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ surface. CX1 and CX2 denote physisorbed complexes. The $E_{\mathrm{a}}$ is 61.4 kcal/mol. This value is comparable to the silylene formation in the gas phase. The surface of $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ has no significant effect. This proves that the decomposition schemes (Eqs. (1), (2a) and (2b)) are primarily important for the reaction processes on the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ surface.

For Eqs. (6), (7a) and (7b) in Fig. 2, we assume the following reaction paths:

$$\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{4} \tag{6}$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3} & +\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3} \\
& \rightarrow \mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{2}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{4}
\end{aligned} \quad(7 \mathrm{a})
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3} & +\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3} \\
& \rightarrow \mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}+\mathrm{SiH}_{3}-\mathrm{SiH}_{3}
\end{aligned} \quad(7 \mathrm{~b})
$$

Eq. (6) and Eqs. (7a) and (7b) are, respectively, the reaction of $\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Si}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{6}$ on the surface of $\mathrm{Si}$ as deposited. $E_{\mathrm{a}}$ of Eqs. (6), (7a) and (7b) is 80.4, 82.5 and $90.4 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$, respectively. This highlights again Eqs. (1), (2a) and (2b) that provides lowest activation energies for decomposition of $\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Si}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{6}$.

## 4. Conclusion

We have performed quantum chemical study of the thermal decomposition of $\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Si}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{6}$ on the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ surface. We have found that these gases are more easily decomposed on the surface of $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ than by themselves. Moreover, although the bond energy of $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{H}(89.4 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol})$ is larger than that of $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{Si}$ $(72.7 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol})$, the $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{H}$ bond is more easily broken with $E_{\mathrm{a}}$ of $39.0 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$ than the $\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{Si}$ bond with $E_{\mathrm{a}}$ of $53.7 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol}$ on the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ surface. These high reactivities of $\mathrm{SiH}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{Si}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{6}$ on the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ surface agree qualitatively well with the experimental observation [4].
