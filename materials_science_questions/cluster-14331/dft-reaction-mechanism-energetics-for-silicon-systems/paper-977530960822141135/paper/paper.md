# Iron(I) and Iron(II) Amido-imidazolin-2-imine Complexes as Catalysts for H/D Exchange in Hydrosilanes

Cite This: ACS Catal. 2024, 14, 1759−1772

### ABSTRACT:
The unsymmetrical amino-imidazolin-2-imine ligand [HAmIm, 1,2-(DippNH)−C₆H₄−N=C(NiPrCMe)₂] is employed in the synthesis of the iron(I) arene complex [(AmIm)Fe(η⁶-C₆H₆)] and the iron(II) neosilyl complex [(AmIm)Fe(CH₂SiMe₃)]. These compounds are highly efficient precatalysts in H/D exchange reactions with deuterium (D₂) in hydrosilanes. The scope comprises primary to tertiary silanes at a catalyst loading of 1 mol % at ambient temperature. In-depth mechanistic studies including various control experiments and the syntheses of isolated iron-hydride and iron-silyl compounds are performed. These studies reveal that the activation of both Fe(I) and Fe(II) complexes generates Fe−H/D species as key catalytic intermediates. An alternative catalytic pathway involving an iron-silyl intermediate, although shown to be less feasible by DFT calculations, may also be operative.

**KEYWORDS:** iron catalysis, amino-imidazolin-2-imine, sigma-bond metathesis, H/D exchange in hydrosilanes, deuterium D₂

## INTRODUCTION

Hydrosilanes ($\text{R}_3\text{SiH}$) are ubiquitous reagents in academia and industry and undergo addition reactions to a variety of unsaturated bonds (hydrosilylation) and reductions of carbon−halogen bonds.¹⁻¹⁰ They offer several advantages, including air and moisture tolerance and low toxicity, compared to metallohydrides or tin hydrides. Hydrosilanes can be replaced by deuterosilanes ($\text{R}_3\text{SiD}$) containing Si−D bonds to synthesize a wide range of deuterium-labeled carbon compounds.¹¹⁻¹⁴ In addition, deuterated silanes are an important mechanistic probe for investigating the deuterium kinetic isotope effect in transformations including hydrosilylation,⁷ ortho-silylation,¹⁵,¹⁶ and reduction of carbon-halogen¹⁷,¹⁸ and carbon-carbon multiple bonds.¹¹⁻¹³ Traditionally, deuterosilanes have been accessed via the reduction of halosilanes with stoichiometric amounts of expensive metallo-deuteride reagents, for example, $\text{NaBD}_4$ or $\text{LiAlD}_4$, which produce metal salts as waste.¹⁹⁻²⁵ Direct deuteration of Si−H bonds of hydrosilanes with deuterium ($\text{D}_2$) is an attractive alternative strategy to prepare deuterosilanes, and many precious transition metal catalysts have been reported for this transformation.²⁶⁻³¹ These protocols are mostly limited to the H/D exchange in tertiary silanes, and only three examples are known to retain the activity for primary and secondary silanes.²⁹⁻³¹ A single rhodium(III) catalyst described by Carmona et al. was shown to be active for Si−H/D exchange of an aliphatic secondary hydrosilane ($\text{Et}_2\text{SiH}_2$).³⁰

In contrast, very few 3d metal-based catalysts are known for H/D exchange in hydrosilanes. Peters et al. used a nickel catalyst for the direct deuteration of a single silane ($\text{Ph}_2\text{SiH}_2$) with $\text{D}_2$,³² while Webster et al. demonstrated the H/D exchange of hydrosilanes in the presence of the iron(II) $\beta$-diketiminate (BDK) complex $[\text{(BDK}^{\text{Dipp}}\text{)Fe(CH}_2\text{SiMe}_3\text{)}]$ (1).³³ In addition, our group has recently used the Co(I) catalyst $[\text{(AmIm)Co}(\eta^6\text{-C}_6\text{H}_6\text{)}]$ (2), bearing an amido-imidazolin-2-imine (AmIm) ligand, as a precatalyst in H/D exchange reactions with $\text{D}_2$ in hydrosilanes (Chart 1).³⁴ Given this small number of examples, it is desirable to establish additional catalytic protocols that facilitate the H/D exchange of hydrosilanes with $\text{D}_2$ based on earth-abundant metals, such as iron. Obviously, the large natural abundance of iron renders it an inexpensive and environmentally friendly substitute for precious metals, while its reduced toxicity is favorable for applications in the pharmaceutical industry.³⁵⁻³⁷

In light of these findings, we sought to synthesize related iron complexes containing the AmIm ligand, $^{50-52}$ which has been shown to be a stronger and more electron-rich monoanionic $N,N'$-donor ligand compared to $\beta$-diketiminates, $^{34}$ due to the strong $\pi$-electron donating ability of both the amido and the imidazolin-2-imine moieties. $^{53,54}$ Hence, in this work, we present the iron(I) and iron(II) complexes 5 and 7 supported by the AmIm ligand, which allow a systematic comparison with Webster's iron(II) catalyst 1 and with our Co(I) catalyst 2 (Chart 1). $^{33,34}$ The compounds 5 and 7 were used as precatalysts in H/D exchange reactions with D$_2$ in hydrosilanes. The scope of compounds 5 and 7 was investigated in detail and includes primary to tertiary silanes at a low catalyst loading of 1 mol %. Furthermore, in-depth mechanistic studies support a $\sigma$-bond metathesis pathway for both Fe(I) and Fe(II) precatalysts, involving Fe$-$H and Fe$-$D species as key on-cycle intermediates. DFT calculations indicate that an alternative pathway involving Fe-silyl species is less viable.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### Synthesis and Characterization of $[(AmIm)Fe(\eta^6\text{-}C_6H_6)]$ (5).
The reaction of the amino-imidazolin-2-imine HAmIm with anhydrous iron(II) chloride afforded complex 3 as a purple-brown, crystalline solid; it forms as a binuclear iron(II) complex containing the neutral HAmIm ligand, one bridging, and one terminal chlorido ligand per iron center. Deprotonation of the N$-$H entity in 3 was achieved by the addition of potassium hexamethyldisilylamide to give the binuclear complex 4 as a red-brown, crystalline material with a loss of the terminal chlorido ligands (Scheme 1). Both compounds 3 and 4 were fully characterized, including X-ray crystallography (Figures 1 and 2). The molecular structures confirm the chelate binding of the AmIm ligand, and the substituents on the nitrogen atoms are oriented roughly orthogonal to the coordination plane. The Fe1$-$N4 bond length is highly sensitive to the deprotonation at N4, decreasing from 2.3362(5) Å in 3 to 1.999(3) Å in 4 in accordance with the amide character of N4 in 4. Similarly, the N3$-$Fe1$-$N4 angle increases from 76.66(2)$^\circ$ in 3 to 84.04(11)$^\circ$ in 4.

Reduction of 4 with potassium graphite in benzene afforded the arene complex $[(\text{AmIm})\text{Fe}(\eta^6\text{-}C_6H_6)]$ (5) as a dark red crystalline material, which was fully characterized, including single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis (Scheme 2). The solid-state molecular structure of 5 shows the expected $\eta^6$-binding mode of the benzene ligand, which is perpendicular $(88.4(1)^\circ)$ to the chelate plane. The Fe−C distances in 5 range from 2.089(3) to 2.130(3) Å ($\Delta \approx 0.041$ Å). The Fe1-centroid distance (the centroid is defined as the center of the six-membered aromatic ring formed by atoms C30 to C35) is 1.5674(15) Å, which is slightly shorter than the corresponding values for the Co(I) analogue 2 $(1.673\ \text{Å})^{34}$ and the $\beta$-diketiminate complex $[(\text{BDK}^{\text{Dmp}})\text{Fe}(\eta^6\text{-}C_6H_6)]$ (1.622 Å, Dmp = 2,6-dimethylphenyl).$^{66}$ Furthermore, the bond lengths Fe1−N3 (1.950(2) Å) and Fe1−N4 (1.929(2) Å) in 5 are unequal and slightly shorter than the corresponding distances in $[(\text{BDK}^{\text{Dmp}})\text{Fe}(\eta^6\text{-}C_6H_6)]$ for which nearly identical Fe−N distances of 1.944(2) and 1.9460(19) Å have been reported.$^{66}$ We obtained two other polymorphs of 5 in which similar geometric parameters were found (see Tables S4−S6 of the Supporting Information). A correlation of the Fe−N bond lengths with the iron spin state could be expected, that is, the observation of longer bond lengths for the high-spin state. However, a closer examination of crystallographically characterized Fe(I) complexes indicates that the Fe−N distances in 5 are on the borderline between the low-spin $(S = 1/2)^{57,67−76}$ and the high-spin $(S = 3/2)$ Fe(I) state.$^{67,77}$

A possible temperature dependence of the Fe(I) spin state and the resulting effect on the geometric parameters prompted us to perform an X-ray diffraction analysis at different temperatures. At elevated temperature (273 K), a different triclinic unit cell was observed for 5 with a 3% increase in volume as compared to the data set collected at 100 K (see Tables S6 and S7 of the Supporting Information). However, the change in temperature did not result in a significant difference in the geometric parameters. Furthermore, the room temperature effective magnetic moment of complex 5 in a $C_6D_6$ solution was found to be $3.8\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ (Evans method), which agrees with the spin-only value of $3.87\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ for Fe(I) with a 3d⁷ configuration in an S = 3/2 high-spin state. Slightly higher values for the effective magnetic moment of $\mu_{\text{eff}} = 4.10$ and $4.39\ \mu_{\text{B}}$ were determined at room temperature (300 K) in THF solution and in the solid state, respectively, using a SQUID magnetometer (Figure 3; details are provided in the Supporting Information), which is also consistent with an S = 3/2 high-spin state for complex 5. Deviations from the spin-only expectation are typically observed for (high-spin) 3d⁶ and 3d⁷ systems due to an incompletely quenched orbital angular momentum of the d electrons.$^{78}$ In addition, the temperature-dependent effective magnetic moment of complex 5 shows significantly decreasing $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ values below about 50 K (Figure 3), suggesting the presence of substantial zero-field splitting, which also rules out a possible S = 1/2 spin state in this material. Therefore, based on the results of SQUID magnetometry and the Evans method, we infer the S = 3/2 spin state for complex 5, which is in contrast to the analogous complex $[(\text{BDK}^{\text{Dmp}})\text{Fe}(\eta^6\text{-}C_6H_6)]$, which has been assigned a low-spin electronic configuration $(S = 1/2)$.$^{66}$ This difference in the spin state can be attributed to the different electron-donating properties of the AmIm vs BDK ligands, which can be further investigated by comparing the carbonyl stretching frequencies in related metal carbonyl complexes.$^{79,80}$

Mechanistic Studies. Several control experiments were performed to gain insight into the mechanism using $Ph_{2}SiH_{2}$ as a representative silane (see Table S15 of the Supporting Information). The "mercury drop test" to quench colloidal or heterogeneous iron particles showed unaffected catalytic deuteration activity, indicating a homogeneously catalyzed process and excluding colloidal and heterogeneous mechanisms. Furthermore, the reverse reaction of the deuteriosilane $Ph_{2}SiD_{2}$ with hydrogen gas $(H_{2})$ in the presence of 5 afforded the protiosilane $Ph_{2}SiH_{2}$ in 95% yield. This observation indicates full reversibility of the catalytic cycle and provides evidence of microscopic reversibility of the elementary steps. And finally, similar catalytic activities of the Fe(I) and Fe(II) complexes 5 and 7 suggest a similar mechanistic pathway for both precatalysts.

Based on computational studies, a $\sigma$-bond metathesis process has been proposed for the activation of iron $\beta$-diketiminate complexes of the type $[(BDK)Fe(CH_{2}SiMe_{3})]$, including complex 1, which can react with silanes such as $PhSiH_{3}$, to give a dimeric hydride complex $[(BDK)Fe(\mu-H)]_{2}$ as a catalytic intermediate. $^{33}$ In the presence of $D_{2}$, the corresponding deuterium complex $[(BDK)Fe(\mu-D)]_{2}$ can be formed by $\sigma$-bond metathesis; this process may involve monomeric or dimeric complexes depending on the size of the ancillary $\beta$- diketiminate ligand. $^{103}$ Dissociation to the corresponding monomeric deuteride would then provide the active species for H/D exchange with the silane $PhSiH_{3}$ via $\sigma$-bond metathesis with formation of $PhSiH_{2}D$ and regeneration of $[(BDK)Fe(\mu- H)]_{2}$ after dimerization. $^{33}$ A similar mechanism can be invoked for the activation of neosilyl complex 7 in the presence of silanes to form the binuclear hydride complex $[(AmIm)Fe(\mu-H)]_{2}(8)$ via $\sigma$-bond metathesis. Notably, the use of 8 in a standard catalytic reaction afforded $Ph_{2}SiD_{2}$ in 91% yield (see Table S15 of the Supporting Information), corroborating the role of iron hydride and deuteride species also in the case of our AmIm- supported iron catalysts.

Regarding pathway I, the extension of the linear transit associated with the formation of AC from FeD$\cdot$S further revealed the subsequent dissociation of the Fe$-$Si bonding interaction, leading to the release of the deuterated silane and the formation of FeH. DFT studies of pathway II revealed that the formation of the iron-silyl species FeSi and the simultaneous release of HD are an exergonic process ($\Delta G = -8.4$ kcal mol$^{-1}$). On the contrary, despite the favorable driving force for the formation of FeSi in pathway II, the generation of the deuterated silane requires the transition through two additional energy barriers, namely, $\mathbf{AC_{D2}}$ and the one associated with the formation of FeSi$\cdot$HD. Overall, these calculations indicate that the catalytic pathway I is more viable in agreement with previous calculations involving the related [(BDK)FeD] system as the catalytically active species.$^{33}$ However, pathway II and the involvement of catalytically active iron-silyl intermediates cannot be completely ruled out on the basis of our theoretical results. In fact, PES scans across pathway II may suggest that energy barriers are <17 kcal mol$^{-1}$ (Figure S67), which is an energy cost that can be easily overcome under laboratory conditions, as demonstrated by our experimental studies.

## CONCLUSIONS

Herein, we have introduced the amino-imidazolin-2-imine ligand (HAImIm) to iron chemistry and presented the iron(I)

complex $[(AmIm)Fe(\eta^{6}-C_{6}H_{6})]$ (5) and the iron(II) complex $[(AmIm)Fe(CH_{2}SiMe_{3})]$ (7). The AmIm ligand serves as a highly robust monoanionic scaffold that exhibits significantly greater $\pi$-donation than $\beta$-diketiminate ligands as demonstrated by the preparation of the tricarbonyl complex $[(AmIm)Fe(CO)_{3}]$ (6). Complexes 5 and 7 were shown to be precatalysts for the H/D exchange in hydrosilanes, which is an attractive protocol to produce deuterosilanes from protiosilanes. At a standard catalyst loading of $1\ \text{mol}\ \%$ and a $\text{D}_{2}$ pressure of 1 bar, the AmIm complexes were more efficient compared to previously reported $\beta$-diketiminate analogues. A detailed mechanistic investigation is also reported, suggesting the activation of both iron(I) and iron(II) precatalysts to yield catalytically active iron(II) hydride or deuteride species. The involvement of iron(II) hydride species is strongly supported by the preparation and isolation of the dimeric complex $[(AmIm)Fe(\mu-H)]_{2}$ (8), which was also found to be catalytically active in H/D exchange. The catalytic pathway involves a critical $\sigma$-bond metathesis step between the iron deuteride species and the substrate (protiosilane). Due to the isolation of complex 11, which can be considered as a phosphine-stabilized iron(II) silyl intermediate, an alternative catalytic cycle involving $\sigma$-bond metathesis between iron(II) silyl species and $\text{D}_{2}$ also seemed conceivable. However, DFT calculations make this alternative pathway via iron(II) silyl intermediates less viable. On the other hand, the oxidative addition of the protiosilane to the iron center appears to be less likely, in contrast to the previously reported cobalt(I) precatalysts. $^{34}$ Collectively, these results demonstrate that AmIm ligands can be an attractive alternative to the ubiquitous $\beta$-diketiminates as ancillary ligands in transition metal chemistry and homogeneous catalysis. $^{107-110}$
