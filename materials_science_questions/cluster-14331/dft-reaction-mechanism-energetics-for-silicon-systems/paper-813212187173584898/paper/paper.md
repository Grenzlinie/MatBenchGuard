# Theoretical characterizations of the mechanism for the dimerization of monosilicic acid in basic solution†

Hui Hu, $^{a}$ Hua Hou, $^{a}$ Zhen He $^{b}$ and Baoshan Wang*$^{a}$

The anionic mechanisms for the elementary dimerization reaction of monosilicic acid in basic aqueous solution have been characterized comprehensively using various *ab initio* methods. Many new insights into the silicate oligomerization reaction, which is fundamentally important in sol–gel chemistry, zeolite synthesis, and cement hydration, are presented in this work. Conformational dependence of the dimeri- zation reaction is proposed in view of hundreds of conformations with various inter- and intramolecular hydrogen bonding patterns along the reaction routes. An alternative water cleavage route from the five-coordinated silicon intermediate is revealed. The detour involves a six-center cyclic transition state, which is more preferable energetically than the well-known four-center water removal step. By including explicit water molecules, the activation barrier of the four-center water cleavage path can be reduced considerably to be even lower than the first barrier of the Si–O bond formation. In contrast, the six-center detour is less affected by the additional water molecules due to the unfavorable geometric distortion. The new understanding of the dimerization mechanism could have considerable impact on the initial stages of silica nucleation.

## Introduction

Oligomerization of orthosilicic acid $[Si(OH)_4]$ is a fundamentally important reaction in the sol–gel process¹ and zeolite synthesis.² The mechanisms of dimerization have attracted considerable interest in both experimental³⁴ and theoretical studies.⁵⁻¹⁷ The dimerization reaction strongly depends on the pH values in aqueous solution. The neutral, the base-catalyzed, and the acid-catalyzed mechanisms show different pictures. In this work we report our new findings on the anionic mechanisms of dimerization in basic medium in the consideration of its importance in cement chemistry.¹⁸ Tricalcium silicate is the main constituent of the modern Portland cement clinker. It reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide and monomeric silicate species. The formation of calcium silicate hydrate phase (C–S–H), which is key to the molecular structures of the hydrated cement, takes place *via* the dimerization reaction of monosilicic acid (e.g., $2Q^0 \to 2Q^1$).¹⁹

Previous theoretical calculations⁷⁻¹⁷ suggested the stepwise reaction routes for the silica condensation, namely, formation of the SiO–Si bond linkage and the subsequent cleavage of water from the pentacoordinated silicon intermediate to form the dimer species. The second step is the rate-determining path because of its higher barrier. Xiao and Lasaga reported the silica dimerization in the gas phase at the MP2//HF/6-31G* level of theory.⁷ Formation of the five-fold coordinated silicon anionic intermediate is endothermic by 12 kcal mol⁻¹ with respect to the prereactive complex and the activation barrier of the Si–O bond formation is 16.5 kcal mol⁻¹. The cleavage of water from the $[(HO)_4SiOSi(OH)_3]^-$ intermediate undergoes *via* a four-center barrier of 18.9 kcal mol⁻¹ to form the dimer silica. Since then this mechanism has been generally accepted and supported by recent computational studies using various theoretical methods, for instance, B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p), B3LYP/ MG3S, B3LYP/6-31G*, MP2//HF/6-31+G(d,) with or without solvent models (*e.g.*, COSMO or IEFPCM). However, the calculated activation barriers do show apparent discrepancies especially for the water removal step so that the microscopic mechanisms of the dimerization reaction of silicic acid is indeed very ambiguous.⁷¹⁰¹² In addition, no *ab initio* calculations with high-level electron correlations have been reported to date.

The role of water in a silicate oligomerization reaction was studied by Trinh *et al.* using Car–Parrinello molecular dynamics simulation with the BLYP functional and the model of 64 water molecules at 350 K.¹³ The free energy profile of each

The rates of linear or ring containing silicate oligomers become substantially enhanced due to the water-assisted proton trans- fer and the hydrogen network environment created by water molecules. However, the mechanisms of the dimerization reaction are the same as those in the gas phase, that is, the water molecules act as spectators in the reaction, even though the activation barriers are lowered by a few kcal mol⁻¹. Therefore, the existence of the water-catalyzed mechanism for the dimer- ization reaction remains an open question. Explicit inclusion of water molecules in the calculations of the corresponding transition states and intermediates is highly desired.

Three new insights into the mechanisms of the dimerization of monosilicic acid in basic medium are presented in this work using high-level ab initio methods. Firstly, there are hundreds of conformations involving various hydrogen bonding patterns along the reaction paths. Therefore, the dimerization reaction of silicic acid is proven to be conformational dependent. Secondly, a new route of water cleavage taking place via the six-center cyclic proton-transfer transition state is discovered. This novel detour involves two silicon atoms in the reaction whereas the four-center internal proton transfer occurs around only the five-coordinated silicon atom. Thirdly, the water-mediated mechanisms are clarified by including water molecules explicitly. It is demonstrated that water may act as either a catalyst for proton transfer or just a spectator beside the reacting moieties. The energetic routes for the dimerization reaction change dramatically upon the participation of water molecules in comparison with those in the implicit water solvent.

## Computational methodology

Geometries of the reactants, complexes, transition states, intermediates, and products were optimized using Handy and coworkers' long-range corrected version of B3LYP using the coulomb-attenuating method (CAM-B3LYP)²⁰ with the 6-311++G(2d,2p) basis set. Solvent (water) effect was considered using the polarizable continuum model with the integral equation formalism (IEFPCM).²¹ Vibrational frequencies were calculated at the same level of theory [CAM-B3LYP/6-311++G(2d,2p)-PCM] to characterize the nature of the stationary points and to obtain the zero-point energies (ZPE). All the optimized structures are illustrated in Fig. S1-S20 (ESI†) and the corresponding Cartesian coordinates together with the total energies and ZPEs are listed in Table S1 (ESI†). Dependence of the geometrical parameters and energies on the theoretical methods (e.g., M06-2X and MP2) and the basis sets [e.g., 6-311++G(3df,3pd) and aug-cc-pVT(+d)Z] has been examined for the key stationary points (see Tables S2 and S3, ESI†). The CAM-B3LYP data are in very good agreement with the MP2 data and the 6-311++G(2d,2p) basis set appears to be flexible enough for the sake of convergence. Gibbs free energies were also calculated using the rigid rotor harmonic oscillator approximations at 298.15 K as listed in Tables S4 and S5 (ESI†). The contributions of entropy could be substantial especially for the species involved in the water-catalyzed pathways. Note that the relative orders of the stationary points are little affected in terms of either Gibbs free energies or ZPE corrected electronic energies.

To ensure reliable conclusions, the energies of all the species were refined using the composite CBS-QB3 scheme²² on the basis of the CAM-B3LYP/6-311++G(2d,2p) optimized geometries. The CBS-QB3 calculated relative energies of the most stable conformations involved in the dimerization reaction are listed in Tables S4 and S5 (ESI†) (for the water-mediated mechanism) and are used in the following discussion unless stated otherwise. Reliability of the CBS-QB3 energetics has been confirmed by the explicitly correlated CCSD(T)-F12 method²³ (see Table S6, ESI†). Ab initio calculations were all performed with the Gaussian09 program²⁴ and the Molpro program.²⁵,²⁶

## Results

### A. Conformational dependence of the reaction routes

Fig. 1 exhibits the minimum energy paths for the dimerization reaction of Si(OH)₄ with Si(OH)₃O⁻. In fact, there are a total of ninety-six conformations (see Fig. S1-S10, ESI†) for the stationary points along the reaction paths. The energies of the most stable conformations and those with the same intramolecular hydrogen bonding patterns are depicted in Fig. 1 for comparison. Apparently, the energy difference between the most stable and the least stable conformations (the corresponding geometries are shown in Fig. 2) spreads over a wide range, for instance, 0-13.6 kcal mol⁻¹ for the prereactive complexes (RC). It implies that the formation of silica dimer could be conformation dependent. The dimerization reaction starts from the hydrogen-bonding complex RC. As shown in Fig. 2, the energetically lowest conformation of RC involves three hydrogen bonds in which the oxygen atoms of $Si(OH)_3O^-$ are proton acceptors. The binding energy of RC is 19.8 kcal mol⁻¹ with respect to the $Si(OH)_4 + Si(OH)_3O^-$ asymptote, which accounts for 6.6 kcal mol⁻¹ per hydrogen bonds. The bind energy of the previously reported prereactive complex is only about 7 kcal mol⁻¹ at the B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p) - COSMO level due to the different hydrogen bondings.¹⁰ While the $Si(OH)_3O^-$ anion acts as proton donors, the RC-h structure represents the conformation with the highest energy. The reaction proceeds from RC to form the intermediate IM1 *via* transition state TS1. The barrier height for the Si-O bond formation is 5.0 kcal mol⁻¹, which is smaller by 1.2-4.7 kcal mol⁻¹ than previous calculations⁷⁻¹² since we located the lowest confor- mation. In fact, the conformational dependence of TS1 could be very significant. The highest TS1-h structure appears to be more reactant-like because the forming Si-O bond is about 0.3 Å longer than that in TS1. The barrier height for TS1-h increases dramati- cally up to 11.8 kcal mol⁻¹.

Intermediate IM1 involves a pentacoordinated silicon and a tetrahedral silicon. The newly formed Si-O bond is nearly collinear with the opposite Si-O bond. The other three Si-OH bonds equatorially bound to the 5-fold silicon. The collinear Si-O bonds are obviously longer than the equatorial Si-O bonds. Population analysis reveals that the charge transfer from $Si(OH)_3O^-$ to $Si(OH)_4$ is significant during the formation of IM1. The Mulliken charge of the 5-fold silicon decreases from +2.21*e* to +1.72*e* and that of the bridge oxygen atom changes from $-1.21e$ to $-1.13e$. The Si-O bond formation is exothermic by 2.3 kcal mol⁻¹, in contrast with the previously predicted endothermicity of about 3 kcal mol⁻¹ at the B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p) level.⁷⁻¹² However, the formation of IM1-h with a slightly longer Si-O bond becomes endothermic by 3.7 kcal mol⁻¹.

The cleavage of $H_2O$ from IM1 takes place *via* transition state TS2. The breaking Si-OH bond is stretched by 0.8 Å whereas the breaking equatorial OH bond is elongated very slightly. The forming OH bond (1.76 Å) is far away from the equilibrium length of $H_2O$ molecule. The barrier height for this 4-center $H_2O$-elimination pathway is 14.4 kcal mol⁻¹ and the formation of PC1 is nearly thermo-neutral. The current CBS-QB3 calculated barrier height for TS2 is slightly lower than the previously reported results.⁷⁻¹² In the case of the high-energy TS2-h, the activation barrier increases to 23.5 kcal mol⁻¹, which is even higher than that of the initial reactants.

In the anionic mechanism of dimerization reaction obtained previously using the B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p) - COSMO level of theory, the two key transition states TS1 and TS2 lie well above the $Si(OH)_4 + Si(OH)_3O^-$ asymptote by about 7 and 12 kcal mol⁻¹, respectively.¹⁰ As could be seen in Fig. 1, the energies of TS1 and TS2 are 14.8 and 7.7 kcal mol⁻¹ lower than that of the $Si(OH)_4 + Si(OH)_3O^-$ asymptote, implying the importance of conformations and high-level electron correlations in the calculation of the anionic mechanism of dimerization reaction of monosilic acid.

## C. The water-catalyzed mechanisms

We calculated the energetic reaction paths for the silicate dimerization with explicit inclusion of one water molecule. All possible binding sites of the water molecule have been explored and a total of forty-nine stable conformations are depicted in Fig. S11-S20 ESI.† The energetic profile for the dimerization reaction is shown schematically in Fig. 3. It is noted that the most stable conformations, as illustrated in Fig. 4, are used to depict the reaction routes.

A water molecule prefers to form two hydrogen bonds with the ${\rm Si(OH)_3O^-}$ moiety in the prereactive complex RC-w because the anionic oxygen atom is a strong proton acceptor. However, in the subsequent Si-O bond association reaction, the water molecule just acts as a spectator around the reacting moiety since the energy difference between four conformations of TS1-w is only marginal. The forming Si-O bond in TS1-w is about 0.04 Å longer than that in TS1. The corresponding barrier height is 6.1 kcal mol⁻¹, which is even higher by 1.1 kcal mol⁻¹ than that for TS1. This might be caused by the extra stabilization of the prereactive complex RC-w in view of the more reactant-like structure of TS1-w. Note that the energy of TS1-w is still lower than the total of energy of RC and H₂O. The most stable conformation of the pentacoordinated silicon intermediate IM1-w involves a water bridge between the OH groups. The adoption of H₂O into the intramolecular hydrogen bonding network makes the Si-O-Si bond angle increase from 127.2 degree in IM1 to 140.4 degree in IM1-w. The binding energy of IM1-w is 6.3 kcal mol⁻¹, which is slightly smaller than that of RC-w.

We found that H₂O molecule exert considerable influence on the water cleavage mechanism from IM1-w. An additional H₂O molecule can act as either a reactive catalyst forming PC1-cw *via* TS2-cw or a nonreactive spectator forming PC1-sw *via* TS2-sw. Transition state TS2-cw shows a floppy six-membered-ring structure. The breaking Si-OH bond is stretched to 3.030 Å. Simultaneously, one of the equatorial OH bonds on the 5-fold silicon is elongated to 1.024 Å. Then the consecutive two-proton transfer reaction takes place to form the silica dimer and the water catalyst is recovered. The external water molecule changes the original 4-center transition state to the 6-center transition state. Consequently, the activation barrier of the water removal step is lowered significantly from 14.4 kcal mol⁻¹ to 6.6 kcal mol⁻¹. More interestingly, the energy of TS2-cw is even lower by 1.4 kcal mol⁻¹ than that of TS1-w. Therefore, the Si-O bond formation becomes the rate limiting step. The structure of TS2-sw exhibits an internal proton transfer mechanism. Although the hydrogen bond between the oxygen atom of the leaving OH group and the hydrogen atom of the water molecule is as short as 1.491 Å, the external proton transfer does not occur as in TS2-cw, that is, the water molecule acts just as a spectator. TS2-sw is 5.3 kcal mol⁻¹ higher than TS2-cw in energy. It is noted that the final products PC1-cw and PC1-sw are different in hydrogen bonding patterns where the anionic oxygen site of the former is stabilized by both inter- and intra-molecular hydrogen bonds and thus more energetically favorable.

The other intermediate IM2-w is stabilized by introducing the water molecule as a bridge between one of the equatorial OH groups on the 5-fold silicon and one of the OH groups on the tetrahedral silicon. Meanwhile, the intramolecular hydrogen bonding is enhanced, as indicated by the shortened O$\cdots$H distance. The energy difference between IM2-w and IM1-w decreases to 2.7 kcal mol⁻¹.

Two types of the H₂O removal paths of IM2-w have been revealed. The transition state TS3-cw demonstrates the water-catalyzed mechanism. The breaking Si-OH and H-OSi bonds are stretched to 2.334 Å and 1.038 Å, respectively. One of the OH bonds of water is elongated to 1.482 Å to form H₂O with an OH leaving group. Very surprisingly, the distance of the forming H$\cdots$OH bond to recover the water catalyst is as long as 1.471 Å, which is far from the equilibrium length. Consequently, the barrier height for TS3-cw is 16.5 kcal mol⁻¹, which is about 4 kcal mol⁻¹ higher than that for the non-catalyzed TS3. It implies that the adoption of the external water molecule into the reacting cyclic moiety is unfavorable even though the ring is expanded to a six-center structure. Therefore, the external water molecule prefers to be a spectator as solvent in the reaction. TS3-sw exhibits such a transition state, in which the water molecule forms a short hydrogen bond (1.455 Å) with the detaching OH group from the five-coordinated silicon. The barrier height for TS3-sw was calculated to be 12.0 kcal mol⁻¹, which is slightly lower than that for TS3 with the implicit water solvent. It is worth noting that the hydrogen bonding patterns of the final products PC2-sw and PC2-cw are different in which one of the newly formed water molecules of the latter acts as only proton donor.

The influence of the explicit water molecule on the six-center detour to water removal has been investigated carefully. TS4-cw corresponds to the water-mediated two proton transfer mechanism. It exhibits a floppy eight-member-ring structure. One of hydrogen atoms of the water molecule approaches to the leaving OH group from the 5-fold silicon. Simultaneously, the water molecule is recovered by accepting one hydrogen atom from the HO group of the tetrahedral silicon. It is unexpected that the barrier height for TS4-cw is nearly 12 kcal mol⁻¹, which is almost twice of that for the six-center TS4. The negative catalysis effect of the water molecule might be understood in view of the geometric characteristics of the corresponding penta-coordinated intermediates and transition states. We noticed that the Si-O-Si bond angles appear to increase in the water cleavage of IM2. In contrast, the angles decrease for IM1. For example, the Si-O-Si angles of IM1 and IM2 are both around 127.0 degrees. The Si-O-Si angle of TS2 decreases to 123.0 degree, while those of TS3 and TS4 increase to 138.3 and 142.9 degrees, respectively. The same phenomenon exists in the water-mediated reaction. The Si-O-Si angles of TS2-cw and TS2-sw decrease by 6.0 and 15.3 degrees, respectively, in

comparison with that of IM1-w. The Si-O-Si angles of TS3-cw, TS3-sw, and TS4-sw increase only marginally by about 1-3 degrees. However, the Si-O-Si angle of TS4-cw increases dramatically by 16.7 degrees in order to accommodate the external water molecule into the six-membered ring. Apparently, the cyclic structure of TS4-cw is overexpanded and thus the corresponding barrier increases significantly. While the water molecule acts as a spectator by forming two hydrogen bonds with the unreacting OH groups, the geometric change of the reacting six-member-ring moiety of TS4-sw is marginal with respect to TS4. Moreover, the Si-O-Si angle increases slightly by 2.4 degree. Therefore, the barrier height for TS4-sw is similar to that for TS4.

## Conclusions
The microscopic mechanism for the dimerization of monosilicic acid in basic medium has been detailed theoretically in this work. It is revealed that the dimerization mechanisms are more complicated than the generally accepted picture. Hundreds of conformations with various inter- and intramolecular hydrogen bonding patterns have been found along the reaction paths, indicating that the silicate dimerization reaction can be strongly conformation dependent. In view of the equatorial arrangements of the OH groups, two types of the reaction intermediates with the five-coordinated silicons exist. Starting from the prereactive complexes, the Si-O-Si bond formation involves a barrier of about 5 kcal mol⁻¹ to form the intermediate with three OH groups equatorially bound to the 5-fold coordinated Si. The subsequent reaction proceeds via either the 4-center H₂O removal path, in which only the 5-fold coordinated silicon is involved and the other tetrahedral silicate acts as a spectator. The 6-center H₂O removal detour is proposed to be indeed more preferable energetically where the leaving OH group from the 5-fold silicon abstracts one proton from the tetrahedral silicate group. The explicit inclusion of the water molecule can affect the energetic reaction routes significantly. The water-catalyzed H₂O removal step from the five-coordinated silicon is enhanced significantly because the barrier is even lower than that for the Si-O bond formation step. However, the 6-center water removal mechanism appears to be less affected by the external water molecules because of the unfavorable Si-O-Si geometry distortion. The application of the current work in studies of the formation of linear, branched, and ring-containing silicates is in progress and the results will be reported in due course.
