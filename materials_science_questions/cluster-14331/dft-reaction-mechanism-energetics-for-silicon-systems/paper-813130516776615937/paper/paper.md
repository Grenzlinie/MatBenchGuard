# Hydrogen-Bridge $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$ and Inserted $\text{H}_3\text{SiCeH}$ Molecules: Matrix Infrared Spectra and DFT Calculations for Reaction Products of Silane with Ce Atoms

Bing Xu, Peipei Shi, Tengfei Huang, Xuefeng Wang*

School of Chemical Science and Engineering, Shanghai Key Lab of Chemical Assessment and Sustainability, Tongji University, Shanghai, 200092, China

## Abstract:
Reactions of laser-ablated cerium atoms with silane were investigated by matrix isolation infrared spectroscopy and theoretical calculations. The reaction products, $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$, $\text{H}_3\text{SiCeH}$, $\text{H}_2\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})\text{CeH}$ and $\text{HSi}(\mu\text{-H})_2\text{CeH}$ were identified on the basis of the $\text{SiD}_4$ isotopic substitutions and DFT frequency calculations. In the solid argon or krypton matrix, the inserted $\text{H}_3\text{SiCeH}$ molecule was observed as initial product on deposition, which rearranged to hydrogen bridge species $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$ on follow-up annealing through $\text{H}_2\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})\text{CeH}$ and $\text{HSi}(\mu\text{-H})_2\text{CeH}$ species. The Si-H-Ce hydrogen bridge was investigated by NBO and ELF analysis. Calculation suggested that in $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$ molecule Ce atom donated one electron to Si atom, resulting in electron-rich $\text{SiH}_3$ subunit, which was confirmed by ESP and AIM analysis. The increased basicity of Si-H bond facilitates the formation of hydrogen bridge bond between Si and Ce. For comparison only insertion $\text{H}_3\text{CCeH}$ structure was obtained from the reaction of Ce atoms with $\text{CH}_4$.

Key word: hydrogen bridge bond, Matrix isolation, Transition metal, DFT calculation

### Introduction

Si–H bond activation has attracted considerable attention due to fundamental and industrial significance [1-7]. Hydrosilanes are used as versatile material in the generation of silyl-metal complexes, which are thought to be intermediates for oxidative addition of Si-H bonds [8-17]. The interaction of a Si–H bond with the transition metal center may range from a weak interaction (generally a three-centered bond), where hydrogen forms a bonding interaction with both the silicon and the metal center, to full oxidative addition, where there are two-electron-two centered bonds between the metal and both the hydrogen and the silicon. For example in the complex $\text{M(CO)}_5(\eta^2\text{-H-SiEt}_3)$ (M=Cr, Mo and W), Si–H bonds were elongated and interacted with transition metal center[18]. Matrix isolation Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy has proved to be a highly informative means of studying metal atom reactions with $\text{SiH}_4$ in excess noble gas matrixes [19-20]. Group 6 metal atom (Cr, Mo, W) reactions with $\text{SiH}_4$ in solid argon matrix gave $\text{HSi}\equiv\text{MH}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{Si}=\text{MH}_2$ and $\text{H}_3\text{Si–MH}$[21]. The inserted molecule ($\text{H}_3\text{SiMH}$) (M = Zn, Cd, Hg) as the primary product was obtained by metal atoms reactions with $\text{SiH}_4$ in solid argon matrix [22]. Interestingly, for Ti atom reaction with $\text{SiH}_4$, $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{TiH}$ molecule with Si–H–Ti bridge bond is the major product[23].

Cerium is the most abundant rare earth element, which often exhibits special characteristics. Many researchers reported that C–H bonds can be activated by cerium complexes [24, 25]. For example, an inverse $\text{CeO}_2/\text{Cu}_2\text{O/Cu(111)}$ catalyst is able to activate methane at room temperature to produce $\text{C}$, $\text{CH}_x$ fragments and $\text{CO}_x$ species

on the oxide surface[26]. The relatively acidic C–H bonds can be activated by cerium and yttrium complexes of N-heterocyclic carbenes (NHCs) [27]. However, the activation of Si–H bonds by cerium and its complex has not been reported based on our knowledge.

In this paper we present a matrix infrared spectroscopic study of the laser-ablated cerium atom reactions with $SiH_4$ in solid argon and krypton. In addition, we also performed a parallel investigation for comparison, namely, laser-ablated Ce atom reactions with $CH_4$ in krypton matrix. Quantum chemical calculations are used to predict energies, molecular structures and vibrational frequencies. The silicon-hydrogen-cerium hydrogen bridge bond was first observed experimentally and analyzed theoretically in detail.

## Experimental and theoretical methods

The method of matrix isolation FTIR investigation has been described in detail previously [28, 29]. The Nd:YAG laser fundamental (1064 nm, 10 Hz repetition rate with 10 ns pulse width) was focused onto the rotating metal target (Johnson Matthey), which spread uniformly to the CsI window cooled to 4 K by using a closed-cycle refrigerator (Sumitomo Heavy Industries Model RDK 205D). Laser-ablated Ce atoms were co-deposited with $SiH_4$ or $CH_4$ in excess argon or krypton onto the CsI window for 1 h at a rate of 2-4 mmol/h. Infrared spectra was recorded at a resolution of 0.5 $cm^{-1}$ by using a Bruker Vertex 80V spectrometer. After deposition sample, sample was annealed to allow reagent diffusion and further reaction, and irradiated by a 175

W mercury arc lamp with globe removed using a combination of optical filters.

Complementary density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out using the Gaussian 09 program [30]. The hybrid B3LYP [31] and BPW91 [32] density functional were employed for product molecules. The 6-311++G(3df, 3pd) basis sets for H, C and Si atoms[33] and the Stuttgart small core relativistic effective core potential (ECP) with its accompanying segmented basis set (ECP28MWB_SEG) for Ce atoms [34, 35] were employed to predict the geometries, electronic structure, and vibrational frequencies of reaction products. All vibrational frequencies were calculated analytically with total energy included for the determination of reaction energies. Natural bond order (NBO 3.0) [30, 36] and electron localization function (ELF) analysis [37] were also performed to explore bonding in new molecules.

## Results and Discussion

### Infrared spectra of silane reaction with cerium

The reaction products of cerium atoms with silane were subsequently characterized by matrix isolation FTIR and DFT calculations. Common species, such as $SiH$, $SiH_2$, $Si_2H_2$, $CeH$, $CeH_3$ and $CeO$ which were produced during the experiment owing to precursor fragment and reactive species, have been identified in a previous paper [38-40].

In argon matrix: Four groups of bands (A, B, C and D) which displayed different responses to various photolysis and annealing cycles are shown in Figure 1(a-e) and corresponding absorptions are listed in Table 1-4. Group A bands at 1340.4,

1054.5 and $1023.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ grew significantly on annealing to 25 K, which is nearly three-fold the intensity of its co-deposited counterpart. These bands decreased stepwise after >500 nm photolysis and broadband irradiation, but increased substantially and dominated the spectra on the final annealing to 30 K. On the contrary, the strong group B bands was observed at 1307.8 and $849.6\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which nearly disappeared on annealing to 25 K, and increased by >500 nm photolysis and broadband irradiation, but decreased again when the annealing temperature reaches 30 K. Notice $849.6\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is the left shoulder on bands due to $\text{Si}_x\text{H}_y$ aggregates. Another two weak bands appeared at 1332.6 and $1323.0\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which are marked as C and D, respectively. The $1332.6\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ band increased on annealing to 25 K and after >500 nm photolysis, but decreased by broadband irradiation and final annealing to 30 K. The $1323.0\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ band increased on annealing, but decreased by >500 nm photolysis.

Isotopic labeling experiments were performed using $\text{SiD}_4$ samples. Figure 2(a-e) displays the spectrum of $0.5\%\ \text{SiD}_4$ experiment, which was run under approximately the same concentration and laser power as that for the $\text{SiH}_4$ experiments. Here, a new group bands were observed at 959.1, 768.0 and $750.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ upon deposition. These bands increased dramatically on annealing, but decreased by $\lambda > 500$ nm photolysis and broad-band irradiation, which apparently correspond to deuterated group A. The second new group band appeared at 937.9 and $631.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which are the counterparts for B species. The 953.0 and $946.0\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ are the counterparts of C and D, respectively.

In krypton matrix: the infrared spectra of Ce and $0.5\%\ \text{SiH}_4$ reaction products in solid krypton matrix are shown in Figure 1(f-j). Compared with the argon matrix, the

bands of species in the solid krypton matrix exhibited a slight red shift duo to the different matrix. The group A bands at 1321.2, 1044.1 and $1012.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ were characterized by their significant increase with the introduction of annealing to 25K, and the growth of these bands was also observed on final annealing to 30K. When $\text{SiD}_4$ was used, these bands shift to 959.1, 768.0 and $750.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, having same response to annealing and photo irradiation. The group B bands were found at 1282.2 and $847.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which decreased on annealing at 25 K but increased by $\lambda > 500\text{nm}$ photo irradiation. The deuterium counterpart bands located at 919.5 and $629.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, responding to annealing and photolysis in the same manner. The group C band at $1311.9\text{cm}^{-1}$ increased on annealing and $\lambda > 500\text{nm}$ photo irradiation. The group D band at $1303.7\text{cm}^{-1}$ increased on annealing, but decreased by $\lambda > 500\text{nm}$ photo irradiation. The deuterium counterpart of C and D band located at 938.3 and $929.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, respectively.

Infrared spectra of product of methane with cerium

A weak new band was observed at $1236.9\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ on the deposition of laser-ablated Ce atoms with $1\%\ \text{CH}_4$, as shown in Figure 3(A). This band increased on annealing to 30 K and at $300 < \lambda < 740$ nm photolysis, but decreased on broad-band irradiation. Investigation with $\text{CD}_4$ shows another new band at $885.1\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, exhibiting similar variation tendency compared with $\text{CH}_4$, namely, increasing on annealing but decreased on broad-band irradiation, as displayed in Figure 3(B).

Calculations

All possible product isomers were calculated based on the number of hydrogen

bridging bonds [41] between Si and Ce atom at B3LYP and BPW91 levels of theory and two stable structures were found in energy surface. In figure 4, triplet state $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$ with triply hydrogen bridge is found to be global minimum. The inserted $\text{H}_3\text{SiCeH}$ molecule, single bridge $\text{H}_2\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})\text{CeH}$ and double bridge $\text{HSi}(\mu\text{-H})_2\text{CeH}$ are 3.23, 2.38 and 2.26 kcal/mol higher in energy than $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$. Density functional calculations were also conducted to obtain the most stable $\text{H}_3\text{CCeH}$ molecule ($^3\text{A}$ ground state) with the reactions of Ce atom with $\text{CH}_4$, which was shown in Figure 4.

$\text{Si}(\boldsymbol{\mu}\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$

In figure 1 (a–e), the group A bands at 1340.4, 1054.5 and $1023.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ increased markedly on annealing to 25 K, decreased by irradiation and restored on final annealing to 30 K. These bands are assigned to the triply hydrogen-bridged $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$. Firstly, the $1340.4\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ band is assigned to Ce-H stretching mode. This band is near to Ce-H stretching frequencies, and a little higher than at $1271.0\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ for diatomic CeH molecule [39] and at $1269.5\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ for Ce-H stretching mode in the $\text{CH}_3\text{OCeH}$ molecule [40]. Secondly, with the deuterium enriched sample $\text{SiD}_4$, Ce–H bands shifted to $959.1\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, giving 1.3976 H/D ratios, which is typical deuterium shift for Ce–H vibrations [39]. Thirdly, two other frequencies at 1054.5 and $1023.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ shifted to 768.0 and $750.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, defining the 1.3730 and 1.3639 H/D isotopic ratios respectively, which are close to the Si-H/Si-D ratio. Notice these two modes are located much lower than Si-H stretching mode but higher than Si-H bending vibration, which are appropriate for the Si-H stretching vibrations perturbed by Ce atom.

In the solid krypton matrix shown in Figure 1 (f-j), the Ce–H stretching mode for the $Si(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$ was found at $1321.2\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which increased on annealing, but decreased on photo irradiation. When $\text{SiD}_4$ was used as reactant, this peak shifted to $945.9\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, giving 1.3968 H/D isotopic frequency ratio. Two perturbed Si-H stretching mode were found at $1044.1$ and $1012.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and their deuterium counterpart were moved to $759.6$ and $741.9\ \text{cm}^{-1}$.

Our B3LYP functional calculations support the assignment. In table 1, with the B3LYP functional, the Ce–H stretching mode for the $Si(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$ molecule is predicted at $1282.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which is underestimated by 4.3% (Ar), 3.0%(Kr). In addition the bridge Si–H–Ce stretching modes are calculated at $1053.7$ and $1026.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which are very close to the experiment values of $1054.5$ and $1023.3\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (Ar) and $1044.1$ and $1012.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (Kr), respectively. When the BPW91 functional is used, these modes are calculated slightly lower at $1275.4$, $1014.9$ and $1000.7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Importantly, the values of calculated isotopic frequency ratios for each mode are in excellent agreement with the experimental ratios.

The calculated structure of $Si(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$ molecule is shown in Figure 4. A global minimum structure with $C_1$ symmetry was obtained with three hydrogen bridge bonds. The calculations with B3LYP functional indicate that the lengths of the three $\text{Si–H}_\text{b}$ bonds is little different. The $\text{Si–H}_\text{b}^1$ bond is lengthened to $1.56\ \text{Å}$ and the $\text{Si–H}_\text{b}^2$ and $\text{Si–H}_\text{b}^3$ bonds are lengthened to $1.58\ \text{Å}$, which are about 5% and 7% longer than the terminal Si–H bond length (Si–H length in $\text{SiH}_4$ is calculated $1.48\ \text{Å}$), Meanwhile $\text{Ce–H}_\text{b}^1$ and $\text{Ce–H}_\text{b}^2/\text{Ce–H}_\text{b}^3$ bond lengths are calculated to be $2.38$ and $2.36\ \text{Å}$, which

are 15% longer than the terminal Ce–H bond length in the same molecule. Notably, the terminal Ce–H bond length is shorter than that of same bond in CeH fragment (2.115 Å) $^{[39]}$ and in $CH_3OCeH$ molecular (2.110 Å)$^{[40]}$, in agreement with the blue shift of Ce–H stretching mode in $Si(\mu\text{-H})_3CeH$ molecule.

### $H_3SiCeH$
The strong bands observed at 1307.8 on the deposition of Ce atoms with $SiH_4$ in solid argon decreased on annealing, and increased by irradiation. This band shifted to 937.9 cm⁻¹ with $SiD_4$, defining the 1.3944 H/D isotopic ratio that was typical of Ce–H stretching vibration [39, 40]. In addition, there is weak band at 849.6 cm⁻¹ on the top of the broad band of $Si_xH_y$, which is appropriate for the Si–H deforming mode of $H_3SiCeH$. The 849.6 band shifted to 631.7 cm⁻¹ in $SiD_4$, which is also on the top of the broad band of $Si_xD_y$, giving 1.3450 of H/D isotopic frequency ratios. Since these bands are overlapped by broad bands, our assignments are tentatively. Furthermore, in the case of experiments in krypton matrix (Figure1 (f–j)), the Ce–H stretching mode (1282.2 cm⁻¹) decreased on annealing, but increased by photo irradiation. This peak shifted to 919.5 cm⁻¹ when $SiD_4$ was used as reactant, giving 1.4098 H/D isotopic frequency ratios. The $Si\text{–}H_3$ deforming mode was located at 847.3 cm⁻¹, which shift to 629.7 cm⁻¹, having 1.3455 of H/D isotopic frequency ratios.

DFT frequency calculations substantiate the assignment of $H_3SiCeH$ molecule and the product absorptions are listed in Table 2. With B3LYP the Ce–H stretching mode and $Si\text{–}H_3$ deforming mode are predicted at 1263.1 and 845.5 cm⁻¹, which are underestimated by 3% and 1%, respectively. As shown in Figure 4, the calculated

$H_3SiCeH$ molecule possesses three equal length Si–H bonds (1.50 Å), which is approximately 1% longer than the Si–H bonds of $SiH_4$ (1.48 Å). The Ce–Si bond and the Ce–H bond are 3.02 and 2.04 Å, respectively.

$HSi(\mu-H)_2CeH$

Absorption at $1332.6\ cm^{-1}$ in solid argon was assigned to $HSi(\mu-H)_2CeH$ which increased on annealing to 25K and after >500 nm photolysis, but decreased by broadband irradiation. The $1332.6\ cm^{-1}$ band shifted to $953.0\ cm^{-1}$ with $SiD_4$ as reagent and H/D isotopic ratio (1.3983) are typical for Ce–H stretching vibration. The assignment is supported by quantum chemical calculations, as list in table 3. The Ce–H stretching vibration of trans-$HSi(\mu-H)_2CeH$ was predicted at $1303.8cm^{-1}$ by B3LYP and $1299.3cm^{-1}$ by BPW91, which was underestimated by 2.2% and 2.5%, respectively. As shown in Figure 4, the calculated $HSi(\mu-H)_2CeH$ molecule possesses two unequal Si–H–Ce bonds and Si–H and Ce–H bond stretch to opposite direction. Furthermore, the calculated intensity for other stretching mode is very low either at B3LYP or at BPW91 level and we have not found the other peaks in spectrum for $HSi(\mu-H)_2CeH$ molecule.

$H_2Si(\mu-H)CeH$

In $Ce + SiH_4$ experiment, The band at $1323.0\ cm^{-1}$ band increased on annealing to 25 K and 30 K but decreased by >500 nm photolysis. This band shifted to 946.0 $cm^{-1}$ with $SiD_4$, giving 1.3985 H/D ratios and assigned to $H_2Si(\mu-H)CeH$. B3LYP/6-311++G(3df,3pd) calculation predicts the $H_2Si(\mu-H)CeH$ to have $^3A$ ground

electronic state. The CeH stretching vibration of $\ce{H2Si(\mu-H)CeH}$ are calculated as $1300.0\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, which is underestimated only by 1.7%, as listed in table 4.

$\boldsymbol{\ce{H3CCeH}}$

The $1236.9\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ band sharpened markedly during first annealing at 30 K and further at $300<\lambda<740$ nm irradiation, but decreased on broad-band photolysis. Then this band was recovered on second annealing to 30 K. Among all the possible isomers, the triplet $\ce{H3CCeH}$ molecule is calculated to be the global minimum structure which is 26.29 kcal/mol lower in energy than its singlet isomer. Besides, the carbene dihydroceriumate $\ce{H2CCeH2}$ (Figure S1) is 32.0 kcal/mol higher in energy than triplet $\ce{H3CCeH}$ molecule and no observation was found in experiment. Notice the triplet $\ce{C(\mu-H)3CeH}$ could not be optimized due to not having minimum. Even though only one peak was observed, the $1236.9\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ band is assigned to Ce–H stretching mode of $\ce{H3CCeH}$ molecule. As listed in Table 5, the Ce–H stretching vibration mode was calculated at 1227.4 and $1267.5\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ by B3LYP and BPW91, which was 9.5 and 30.6 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ shift, in agreement with the experimental value of $1236.9\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in krypton. In addition, the calculated intensity for other stretching mode is very low either at B3LYP or at BPW91 level and we also have not found the other peaks in spectrum for $\ce{H3CCeH}$ molecule.

The structure of inserted $\ce{H3CCeH}$ is similar to $\ce{H3SiCeH}$ (figure 4), three C-H bond are also identical (1.10 Å) which are about 2% longer than C-H bonds of $\ce{CH4}$ (1.08 Å). The Ce-C bond and the Ce-H bond are lengthened to 2.39 and 2.06 Å, respectively.

### Reaction mechanisms

As is well known, reaction products from reaction between silanes (SiH₄) and transition metal atoms are more complicated than the case for methane. The process of laser-ablated Ce atom reactions with SiH₄ is shown in Figure 5. The B3LYP/SDD calculations suggest that Ce atom interacts with SiH₄ to form H₃SiCeH initially in exothermic reactions by 18.0 kcal•mol⁻¹ (reaction 1). The triplet H₃SiCeH further transfers H atom successively to produce H₂Si(μ-H)CeH, HSi(μ-H)₂CeH and Si(μ-H)₃CeH finally which are all thermodynamically favorable (reaction 2-4). Our observation verified that H₃SiCeH molecule dominates the sample on initial co-deposition. When annealing was used, the intensity of H₃SiCeH decreases and Si(μ-H)₃CeH increases and became the last winner on the 30K annealing. Experiment also suggests that photo irradiation would favor the producing of H₃SiCeH and make H₃SiCeH recovery. The H₂Si(μ-H)CeH and HSi(μ-H)₂HfH responding to annealing and photo irradiation show rise and fall due to locating in the middle of reaction chain.

$$\mathrm{Ce}\ (^3\mathrm{L}) + \mathrm{SiH}_4\ (^1\mathrm{A}) \rightarrow \mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{SiCeH}\ (^3\mathrm{A}) \quad \triangle\mathrm{E}=-18.00\ \mathrm{kcal/mol} \quad (1)$$

$$\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{SiCeH}\ (^3\mathrm{A}) \rightarrow \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{Si}(\mu\text{-H})\mathrm{HfH} \quad \triangle\mathrm{E}=-0.85\ \mathrm{kcal/mol} \quad (2)$$

$$\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{Si}(\mu\text{-H})\mathrm{HfH} \rightarrow \mathrm{HSi}(\mu\text{-H})_2\mathrm{HfH} \quad \triangle\mathrm{E}=-0.12\ \mathrm{kcal/mol} \quad (3)$$

$$\mathrm{HSi}(\mu\text{-H})_2\mathrm{HfH} \rightarrow \mathrm{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\mathrm{HfH} \quad \triangle\mathrm{E}=-2.26\mathrm{kcal/mol} \quad (4)$$

Compared with the products of Ce atoms with SiH₄ in the argon matrix, the reaction between Ce atoms with 1% CH₄ in the krypton matrix is simple. The only reaction product is the inserted H₃CCeH by hydrogen shift from methane to cerium

atoms, calculated exothermically by 13.74 kcal/mol at the B3LYP level. (reaction
5).

$$\mathrm{Ce}\left({ }^{3} \mathrm{~A}\right)+\mathrm{CH}_{4}\left({ }^{1} \mathrm{~A}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{H}_{3} \mathrm{CCeH}\left({ }^{3} \mathrm{~A}\right) \quad \Delta \mathrm{E}=-13.74 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol} \tag{5}$$

## Bonding

The triply hydrogen-bridged $\mathrm{Si}(\mu-\mathrm{H})_{3} \mathrm{CeH}$ possesses the lowest energy with $^{3} \mathrm{~A}$ ground state. Natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis listed in Table 6 indicates that a lone electron pair is found for Si, which mainly originates from the Si 3s orbital. Ce possesses two unpaired electrons, one of which is formed by $4 f$ orbital and the other is mainly from $5 d$ and 6s orbital. For silicon atom the bigger energy gap between 3s and $3 p\left(38.6 \mathrm{kcal} \cdot \mathrm{mol}^{-1}\right)$ make hybridization difficult and thus Si atom prefers to maintain the valence shell $(n s)^{2}(n p)^{2}$ configuration by preserving the valence ns electron as core-like electrons and use two $p$ electrons for bonding in their compounds [42,43].

The basicity of the Si-H bond is greater than a H-H or to a C-H bond, which makes the Si-H unit a better donor. NBO data suggest that the electrons of three pairs of Si-H $\sigma$ bond donate to virtual hybrid orbital of Ce atom (43.0% 6s and 52.7% $5 d$ of Ce atom). Meanwhile, the Si-H $\sigma *-$ antibonding orbital is a better $\pi$-type acceptor than either C-H or H-H. NBO data also suggest that the Ce atom donates one electron to the Si atom. This Si-H bond nature of the donor/acceptor ability defies the interaction between C-H bond and transition metal center and is stabilized as hydrogen bridge bond that is beyond $\alpha$-agostic interaction region. Like $\mathrm{X}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{X}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{4}$ (X=Si, Ge, Sn, Pb) molecule [44]. Electron localization function (ELF) analysis

[37] shows the bonding nature clearly (Figure 6). Three trisynaptic basins labeled V(Ce,H,Si) with population of 1.93 e in the Si-Ce interaction region were identified, which corresponds to the three Si-H-Ce hydrogen bridge bonds with the character of three center and two electrons. Our result was the first time to introduce the hydrogen bridge bond between silicon and lanthanum metal atom. Interestingly, the natural charges of $SiH_3$ and CeH are -0.58 and +0.58, respectively, which shows that $Si(\mu-H)_3$ fragment is similar to a mono-anion, as was consistent with the CeH mono-cation with the Ce (II) oxidation state. Furthermore, Electrostatic potential (ESP) is critical for studying and predicting intermolecular interaction [45]. As we can see, in figure 7(a), the average ESP values on the local surface of silicon and cerium is -10.2 and 40.3 kcal/mol in $Si(\mu-H)_3CeH$ molecule surface, respectively. While in $H_3SiCeH$ molecule surface (Figure 7(b)), the ESP is 2.1 and 35.0 kcal/mol for Si and Ce. This result clearly explained that more electrons accumulate around silicon atom in $Si(\mu-H)_3CeH$ molecule than in $H_3SiCeH$. On our AIM picture (Figure 7(c) and 7(d)), the bond critical point between bridge hydrogen and cerium in $Si(\mu-H)_3CeH$ molecule locates in the region with positive Laplacian value (0.083), suggesting the electron depletion in the bonding region, corresponding to ionic interaction (closed shell), which verified the interaction between $SiH_3^-$ and $CeH^+$. Whereas the negative Laplacian value (-0.005) at the bond critical point linked Si and Ce with a bond path in $H_3SiCeH$ molecule suggests the concentration of electrons in the bonding region, corresponding to shared interaction (covalent).

Thus the basicity of Si-H bond is increased much due to electron-rich $SiH_3$

subunit, which is more conductive to formation of hydrogen bridge bond between Si and Ce. On the other hand, the CeH can be stabilized by the electron-rich SiH₃ subunit, which could be an interesting intermediate for synthetic chemistry. This phenomenon has been observed in Si(μ-H)UF₃ system as well, where the Si(μ-H) fragment is similar to a mono-anion with the U(IV) oxidation state[46].

For the insertion product H₃SiCeH that is 3.23 kcal/mol higher in energy than Si(μ-H)₃CeH, the NBO data in Table 6 suggested that Si atom maintains $sp^3$ hybrid configuration to bond with three hydrogen atoms and one cerium atom. In figure 6, for H₃SiCeH molecule neither trisynaptic basin between Si, H and Ce region nor monosynaptic bow-shaped basin on the Si atom could be found. Instead there are three disynaptic basin with population of 1.97 e (V(Si,H)), 1.78 (V(Ce, Si)) and 1.78 e (V(H, Ce)), corresponding to Si–H, Si–Ce and Ce–H bond respectively.

In our experiment the H₃SiCeH could transform to Si(μ-H)₃CeH on annealing, companying by the transfer of electron from Ce atom to SiH₃ fragment. During photolysis, electron goes back to the Ce atom, and the Si(μ-H)₃CeH molecule returned to H₃SiCeH. Electron transfer happened between Si atom and Ce atom during the annealing and photolysis circle.

For the reaction product of cerium with methane, the inserted H₃CCeH compound is the global minimum structure, which is the only product we obtained, where C atom keep $sp^3$ hybrid orbital for bonding. Carbon and silicon are in the same group and could thus be regarded as similar behavior before. However recent studies suggest that the great difference exists between Si and C atoms [41]. Compare with Si

atom, the smaller energy gap between $2s$ and $2p$ ($15.4\ \text{kcal}\bullet\text{mol}^{-1}$) [42, 43] make $sp^3$ hybrid easier for carbon atom.

In the reaction of cerium with silicon or methane, the $4f$ electron of Ce atom did not participated in bonding, unlike other reaction, for example, $\text{H}_2$ produced by water-splitting based on $\text{CeO}_2/\text{Ce}_2\text{O}_3$, where $4f$ electron participated in the reduction and oxidation [47].

## Conclusion
The reaction products of laser-ablated cerium atoms with silane and methane in solid matrices were investigated through matrix isolation FTIR and theoretical calculations. Four products of the reaction of cerium atoms with silane were obtained, namely, the triply hydrogen-bridged $\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})_3\text{CeH}$, the inserted $\text{H}_3\text{SiCeH}$, the single bridge $\text{H}_2\text{Si}(\mu\text{-H})\text{CeH}$ and double bridge $\text{HSi}(\mu\text{-H})_2\text{CeH}$ which were identified on the basis of the $\text{SiD}_4$ isotopic substitutions and density functional calculations (B3LYP, BPW91). The basicity of $\text{Si--H}$ bond is increased due to electron-rich $\text{SiH}_3$ subunit and thus facilitates the formation of hydrogen bridge bonds with cerium atom. However, only inserted $\text{H}_3\text{CCeH}$ product was obtained from the reaction of Ce with $\text{CH}_4$, which has similar structure to $\text{H}_3\text{SiCeH}$ molecule.
