# Silicon Lewis Acid Catalyzed [3+2] Cycloaddition Reactions of Hydrazones/
Cyclopentadiene: Mild Access to Pyrazolidine Derivatives

Alexandru Zamfir, $^{[a]}$ Sebastian Schenker, $^{[a,b]}$ Walter Bauer, $^{[a]}$ Timothy Clark, $^{[b]}$ and Svetlana B. Tsogoeva*[a]

Keywords: Cycloaddition / Lewis acids / Nitrogen heterocycles / Hydrazones / Silicon / Density functional calculations

A first and fairly mild metal-free catalytic route was developed for the [3+2] cycloadditions of different $N$-acylhydrazones to cyclopentadiene, providing the synthetically and biologically important five-membered cyclic compounds pyrazolidines. The reaction was successfully conducted in high yields (up to 99 %) with high diastereoselectivities (up to 98:2 $dr$) by using catalytic amounts of TMSOTf (trimethylsilyl triflate) as a readily available Lewis acidic catalyst. The experimental findings were supported by DFT calculations of the reaction mechanism. The theoretical and experimental diastereomeric excess values show good agreement, rendering the computations an efficient tool for predicting the selectivities in these and related reactions.

## Introduction

Pyrazolidine and pyrazoline heterocycles are attractive subunits in many complex natural products and bioactive compounds, which demonstrate high potential as antidepressants, $^{[1]}$ acyltransferase inhibitors, $^{[2]}$ analgesic agents, $^{[3]}$ and antimicrobial agents; $^{[4]}$ they show antitumor $^{[5]}$ and anticonvulsant $^{[6]}$ (Figure 1) activities or can be used in agriculture as arthropodicidal agents. $^{[7]}$

[3+2] Cycloaddition of hydrazones to olefins is one of the most versatile reactions providing direct access to pyrazolidines and pyrazolines. $^{[8,9]}$ First reports concerning this reaction came in 1970 from the group of Hesse, $^{[8a]}$ using strongly acidic conditions (acetic acid in the presence of a stoichiometric amount of sulfuric acid) to obtain pyrazolidines out of a three-component reaction between an aldehyde, a $N$-acetylhydrazine, and an olefin. Protonated hydrazones were suggested to be the active species. $^{[8a]}$ Hamelin and co-workers reported in 1979 the results of their studies on the addition reaction of acetaldehyde hydrazone with $(E)$- and $(Z)$-alkenes in acidic media that were compatible with concerted process of the polar [3+2] cycloaddition type. $^{[8b]}$ Grigg and co-workers $^{[9a,9b]}$ further reported that the reaction between a hydrazone and an activated olefin could be carried out under thermal conditions (at a temperature of $150\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$).

Tremendous effort has been made by Kobayashi and co-workers to drive the progress of the reaction development. They found that Lewis acids also promote this transformation. $^{[10]}$ In the presence of a stoichiometric amount of $\ce{BF3\cdot OEt2}$ (1.1 equiv.) or a catalytic amount of $\ce{Zr(OTf)4}$ and $\ce{Hf(OTf)4}$, the corresponding pyrazolidine derivatives were isolated. An asymmetric version of these [3+2] cycloadditions has also been developed by the group of Kobayashi using $\ce{Zr(OTf)4/BINOL}$ derivatives as chiral Lewis acid catalysts. $^{[11]}$ However, the known catalytic methods mostly are limited to an intramolecular mode and/or the substrates are restricted to only activated olefins. Therefore, the search for efficient catalytic systems for this versatile transformation still remains a challenging task.

## Results and Discussion
Among tetravalent silicon compounds $^{[15]}$ there are several active Lewis acids that can catalyze a broad variety of organic transformations. Motivated by the practical advantages of using silicon-based Lewis acids, $^{[15e]}$ we decided to find silicon derivatives that could be used in catalytic amounts for the [3+2] cycloaddition of hydrazones to the non-functionalized olefin cyclopentadiene.

We started our investigations with the model reaction between ethyl glyoxylate derived hydrazone **1** and freshly distilled cyclopentadiene $(2),^{[10]}$ probing different tetravalent silicon compounds as catalysts (Table 1). To our delight, cycloaddition proceeded smoothly in the presence of all investigated silane Lewis acids at 10-mol-% loading (Table 1, Entries 1–6). Cyclopentadiene was added in excess due to its tendency to form dimers. Adducts of cyclopentadiene were observed as side products, which showed no problem to separation from the pyrazolidines. The reaction was found to perform best in the presence of TMSOTf (trimethylsilyl triflate), providing the product in high yields and diastereoselectivities of up to 94:6 in favor of the *syn* product (Table 1, Entries 2 and 3). Dichloromethane (DCM) was found to be a suitable solvent for the reaction.

Under acidic conditions, hydrazone **1** isomerizes also to the (Z) form, which seems to be less reactive. Lowering the temperature to $-10\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ reduced the isomerization to (Z)-hydrazone, giving higher yields and slightly better selectivities (Table 1, Entry 3).

For the scope of the reaction, we tested several substrates and obtained generally excellent yields and selectivities (up to $99\%$ yield, 98:2 $dr$; Table 2). Derivatization of the benzoyl protecting group brought a slight improvement in selectivity and yield (Table 2, Entry 1 vs. 2).

A variety of aliphatic aldehyde-derived N-benzoylhydrazones performed well in this cycloaddition reaction (Table 2, Entries 3–8). In the case of 3-phenylpropionaldehyde-derived hydrazones, the presence of 4-bromobenzoyl- and benzoyl-protecting groups, respectively, gave better selectivities but still lower yields (Table 2, Entries 3 and 4) than the 4-nitrobenzoyl derivative (Table 2, Entry 5). $^{[16]}$ Increasing the steric demand of the substituent on the aldehyde unit of hydrazone $(\text{PhCH}_{2}\rightarrow\text{Ph})$ strongly influenced the reaction outcome. Whereas phenylacetaldehyde-derived hydrazone gave a product in $58\%$ yield (Table 2, Entry 9), benzaldehyde-derived hydrazone did not undergo the reaction (Table 2, Entry 10).

Oxidation of pyrazolidine **3** with $\text{K}_{2}\text{S}_{2}\text{O}_{8}$, $\text{AgNO}_{3}$, and $\text{NaOH}$ delivered pyrazoline **4** in good yield, demonstrating that in addition to pyrazolidines, the new catalytic method can also provide efficient access to versatile pyrazolines (Scheme 1).

In addition to the experimental findings, a DFT study on the reaction mechanism was carried out. Based on a DFT computation by Frank, Wölfling, and co-workers, $^{[17]}$ who studied a closely related intramolecular 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition reaction of olefin-substituted hydrazones promoted by the Lewis acid $\text{BF}_{3}\text{·OEt}_{2}$, two plausible reaction pathways were considered (Scheme 2). In agreement with Frank–Wölfling's model, we found hydrazone isomer **1a** to be more stable than **1**. The DFT results suggest that, once **1a** emerges, a complex with the Lewis acidic silicon center is formed. Since the triflate is an excellent leaving group, it is eliminated when the catalyst approaches. From this point, the calculations suggest plausible mechanisms; pathway A, which is derived from the proposed mechanism, $^{[17]}$ and pathway B, which is based on the formation of a stable Si–O-bond, which is not feasible for Frank–Wölfling's substrates. In the overall process, pathway B is clearly favored energetically over pathway A. Since the selectivity of the catalyst hardly depends on the solvent, we can calculate predicted product distributions from the calculated energies. For kinetically controlled reactions, we observe at room temperature $\Delta\Delta H^{\ddagger}_{syn-anti}=1.1\ \text{kcal}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ and $\Delta\Delta G^{\ddagger}_{syn-anti}=0.9\ \text{kcal}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$, resulting in predicted product distributions of 84:16 or 88:12, in good agreement with the experimental value (89:11).
