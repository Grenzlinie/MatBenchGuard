# Experimental and Theoretical Study of $[2\sigma + 2\sigma + 2\pi]$–Cycloaddition of Quadricyclane and Ethylenes Containing Three Silyl-Groups

Maxim Bermeshev · Pavel Chapala · Valentin Lakhtin · Alexander Genaev · Marina Filatova · Alexander Peregudov · Kamil Utegenov · Nikolai Ustynyuk · Eugene Finkelshtein

Received: 22 July 2014 / Accepted: 12 November 2014

## Abstract
The reaction of $[2\sigma + 2\sigma + 2\pi]$-cycloaddition of quadricyclane with olefins is an attractive way for regio- and sterospecific synthesis of norbornene derivatives (*exo*-tricyclononenes) with desired substituents. The presence of $Cl_3Si$-containing groups in such compounds permits different monomer modifications for the development of new materials. Herein, for the first time the theoretical and experimental study of the behavior of different ethylenes containing three silyl-groups in the reaction of $[2\sigma + 2\sigma + 2\pi]$-cycloaddition with quadricyclane was systematically performed. Based on the data obtained for this reaction the activity scale of the ethylene derivatives was established, and the explanations for an ethylene activity were suggested. As a result, a new family of Si-containing tricyclononenes was successfully synthesized. As an example of a possible modification, the methylation of the sterically hindered cycloadducts was investigated and the conditions for full methylation were found. Considering that gas permeability of polymers increases with the increase of a number of the side $Me_3Si$-groups, the compounds are interesting monomers for synthesis of membrane polymers possessing high gas permeability values.

## 1 Introduction
Norbornenes are unique monomers which could be polymerized via different routes to give polymers with various backbones [1–4]. It has been recently shown that among norbornene derevatives *exo*-tricyclononenes were more attractive due to their enhanced activities in polymerization [5–9]. We have demonstrated previously that quadricyclane (**Q**) entered into $[2\sigma + 2\sigma + 2\pi]$ stereo- and regiospecific cycloaddition reaction with silicon- and germanium-substituted ethylenes and acetylenes resulting in a formation of substituted tricyclo[4.2.1.0^{2,5}]nonenes and tricyclo[4.2.1.0^{2,5}]nonadienes [7, 10–13]. Methylation of these cycloadducts led to highly active monomers. Polymers based on *exo*-tricyclononenes containing one or two bulky $Me_3Si$-groups in a monomer unit were successfully obtained [7, 10]. Our data indicated that the introduction of three $Me_3Si$-groups in a monomer unit of metathesis or addition polymers would significantly improve polymer properties (i.e. gas-transport parameters). ## 2 Experimental

### 2.1 General
All work involving manipulations with air- and moisture sensitive compounds was carried out under dried and purified argon using standard Schlenk and vacuum-line techniques. All monomers were stored under inert atmosphere. **Q** was synthesized as described in the literature [18] and twice distilled over sodium metal under argon before use.
Methyl iodide (Aldrich) was distilled over $CaH_2$. Diethyl ether (Alfa Aesar), and di-*n*-butyl ether (Alfa Aesar) were distilled sequentially from potassium hydroxide, sodium metal and sodium with benzophenone. Ethylenes **1–8** were synthesized according to the published procedures [19, 20]. Cyclopentadiene-1,3 was used as freshly cracked dicyclopentadiene (Sigma Aldrich).

NMR-spectra were recorded on a Bruker Avance DRX 400 spectrometer at 400.1 MHz ($^1$H NMR), 100.6 MHz ($^{13}$C NMR) and 79.5 MHz ($^{29}$Si NMR) in $CDCl_3$ solution as an external standard or in absolute $C_6D_6$ as an internal standard. Chemical shifts $\delta$ are reported in parts per million (ppm) relative to an external reference (residual $CHCl_3$ signal) or internal reference (residual $C_6H_6$ signal). FTIR spectra for **18, 19, 20, 21** were recorded on a FTIR-Microscope HYPERION 2000 Bruker associated with IFS-66 v/s Fourier spectrometer as ATR. Mass spectra were recorded on a Thermo Focus DSQ II (ionization energy 70 eV, source temperature 230° C). Preparative HPLC was performed on a Shimadzu 20 preparative chromatograph with ReproSil Pur C18 Aq, 10 mkm 250×20 mm column and equipped with LC-8A pump. Gradient elution was done from 90 % B up to 100 % for 30 min. Eluent A - water, eluent B - methanol. Flow rate 10 mL/min. All manipulations were done at room temperature.

### 2.2 Synthesis of Products of [2 + 2 + 2] Cycloaddition (9–15)
General procedure An oven-dried ampoule equipped with a magnetic stirring bar was charged with **Q** (1.5 eq.) and substituted ethylene (1.0 eq.). The main portion was freeze-pump-thawed, a Shlenk tube was sealed and kept at appropriate conditions. The conversion was monitored by $^1$H-NMR. To follow the reaction course by NMR Shlenk type tube-inserts with a diameter smaller than the NMR tube diameter were dried in the oven and purged with argon. Samples for NMR analysis were taken from the prepared above reaction mixture and transferred into Shlenk tube-inserts, followed by degassing mixtures in the tube-inserts using the freeze-pump-thaw method. After that tubes-inserts were sealed and kept under appropriate conditions. To run NMR analysis a tube-insert was placed into the standard NMR tube filled with chloroform-D. Based on $^1$H NMR results at the end of reaction a tube with the main portion was cracked and products were isolated under reduced pressure (2 mm Hg, 60° C).

## 3 Results and Discussion

Earlier we have involved ethylenes containing simultaneously two SiCl₃ and one SiCl₃₋ₙMeₙ (n = 1 to 3) groups in the reaction of [2+2+2]-cycloaddition with Q [12] (Fig. 1, 2–4, Scheme 1) but could not carry out a full methylation of the corresponding cycloadducts. In the present work we studied for the first time activities of different ethylenes having three silyl-groups (Fig. 1, 1, 5–8) in the cycloaddition reaction with Q and successfully performed full methylation of the corresponding sterically hindered cycloadducts.

The row of the chosen ethylenes allowed us to estimate the relative activities of different ethylenes in the [2σ+2σ+ 2π]-cycloaddition reaction with Q depending on the nature of Si-containing substituents and to determine the scope of Si-substituted ethylenes which are capable of reacting with Q.

The activities of 1, 5–8 compounds were studied for the first time in this work. Alkenes 1, 5–7 reacted with Q, while the alkene 8 containing weak electron-withdrawing SiMe₂Cl-substituents was not active at all. Interestingly, ethylenes 1 and 5 as well as the earlier studied 2, 3 [12] reacted with Q under mild conditions (at room temperature) and ethylenes 4[12],6, 7 reacted only at the raised temperature (95° C).

Monitoring the cycloaddition reaction was easy with the help of NMR-spectroscopy as ¹H signals of reagents, the by-product (norbornadiene-2,5) and products are well resolved in the region of 5–8 ppm. The results of the study of ethylenes reactivities by means of ¹H NMR spectroscopy are presented in Fig. 2.

As can be seen from Fig. 2, the most reactive olefin is ethylene 1 containing three Cl₃Si-groups, the strongest electron withdrawing substituents among those considered. Consequently the increase of the number of methyl groups at Si-atoms in the olefins resulted in the lower activity of the corresponding ethylene. Even the substitution of one Cl-atom by a Me-group gave a noticeable decrease in the ethylene activity (Fig. 2a). The reaction time for olefin 2 increased almost 21 times in comparison with 1 (for 70 % conversion of ethylenes). Further increase of number of methyl groups at Si-atoms in ethylenes resulted in a gradual decrease of their activities. Moreover, it was found that the activities of Si-containing ethylenes with two and more Me-groups depended on the position of Me-groups at silicon atoms. Thus, the ethylene 5 containing two Me-groups at different Si-atoms was more active than ethylene 3 containing both Me-groups at the same Si-atom. The same phenomenon was observed for ethylenes containing three Me-groups: the ethylene 6 containing Me-groups at three Si-atoms reacted with Q at room temperature while

The possible reason for a difference in reactivities between 3 and 5 as well as 6 and 4 is clearly connected to the double bond polarization of a olefin. It seems that ethylene 5 possesses a more polarized double bond than 3; the same was true for 6 and 4. It was indirectly confirmed by $^{13}$C NMR data of ethylenes 3 and 5. So the chemical activities of the studied silylolefins dramatically depended not only on electron-withdrawing properties of substituents but also on double bond polarization which is governed by the nature of substituents and their positions (Fig. 3). It was rather interesting that the ethylene containing three SiMeCl$_2$-groups (6) was active in the cycloaddition with Q while vinylmethyldichlorosilane containing only one SiMeCl$_2$-substituent was inactive [12]. The ethylene 7 containing five Me-groups demonstrated a very low activity in the reaction with Q even on heating. And finally when instead of six Cl-atoms six Me-groups were incorporated at Si-atoms, the corresponding ethylene 8 was inactive, possibly due to the SiMe$_2$Cl-substituent not possessing an electron-withdrawing properties.

The position of a silyl-containing ethylene in the sequence of activities (Fig. 3) was determined by the number and strength of electron withdrawing substituents as well as the double bond polarization of the corresponding olefin. The activity of the studied ethylenes became lower with an increase of the number of methyl groups and with a decrease of polarization of the double bond. The obtained sequence of activities was in a good agreement with theoretically determined activation barriers for the reaction of the corresponding ethylenes with Q. The results of DFT calculations for some silyl alkenes are presented in Table 1. As can be seen from Table 1 the lowest activation barrier, as expected, contained ethylene 1 which was the most active. When the number of Me groups in ethylene at Si-atoms became higher the activation barrier rose and the highest activation barrier was found for ethylene 8 which was inactive in the reaction with Q. It is also interesting that theoretical study confirmed our observations concerning the activities of ethylene isomers containing the same number of Me-groups: 6 had a lower activation barrier than 4 (Table 1).

Note that the theoretical study predicted an asynchronous addition of a Si-containing ethylene to Q (Fig. 4). These observations were in agreement with earlier published results for the reaction of Q with some other alkenes [14–17] and the fact that double bond polarization showed a significant influence on ethylene activity. Another point which is noteworthy is that the more preferable addition of an Si-containing ethylene proceeded via the least sterically hindered side of the ethylene (Fig. 4). So, the lowest activation barriers are for transition states TS-1 and TS-3 in the case of vinyltrichlorosilane and for TS-123 in the case of alkene 1 (Fig. 4). The noticeable difference of activation energies for tris-substituted ethylenes for different transition states in the cycloaddition of Q and 1 (Fig. 4, TS-012, 013, 023, 123) indicated that preferable formation of a single isomer should take place as was observed experimentally. The significant dependence of the activation barrier on steric hindrance was also illustrated by the unexpected extremely high value of $E_a$ (33.4 kcal/mol) for tetrakis(trichlorosilyl)ethylene in the reaction with Q. This value was higher than it was for unsubstituted ethylene (29.8 kcal/mol).

The cycloadducts of $\boldsymbol{Q}$ with all active Si-containing olefins (Scheme 1) were obtained in good yields. They were successfully isolated as individual compounds. Note that despite the presence of a strained cyclobutane ring and several bulky groups these products were thermally stable in comparison with the corresponding substituted norbornenes. Thus, the condensation of tris (trichlorosilyl)ethylene $\boldsymbol{1}$ with cyclopentadiene-1,3 gave a mixture of exo- and endo-5,5,6-tris(trichlorosilyl)norbornene-2 (16, 17) almost in equal ratio (exo/endo is 45/55) (Scheme 2).

This mixture of isomers showed thermal instability: even at room temperature it exists as an equilibrium between reagents (minor) and products (major) and the rising of temperature led to a formation of the initial reagents.

The methylation of the cycloadducts was studied under different conditions in order to obtain a monomer with three $\ce{Me3Si}$-groups (i.e. 3,3,4-tris(trimethylsilyl)tricyclonononene-7). The reaction of cycloadduct $\boldsymbol{15}$, containing only 4 chlorine atoms, with an excess of methyl magnesium iodide (MeMgI) in diethyl ether did not lead to a full methylation. It gave only one product (18) (Scheme 3) containing two chlorine atoms in the structure with a good yield. Two Si-Cl bonds in $\boldsymbol{18}$ were extremely sterically hindered and they were stable to hydrolysis under mild conditions (wet solvents at room temperature). The alkaline hydrolysis of $\boldsymbol{18}$ at reflux resulted in the formation of silanol $\boldsymbol{19}$. The study of the structure $\boldsymbol{19}$ by X-Ray analysis and NMR spectroscopy allowed us to establish unambiguously the positions of all substituents (Fig. 5).

The attempt to use a stronger alkylation agent (MeLi) for the full methylation of $\boldsymbol{13}$ resulted in obtaining the desirable 3,3,4-tris(trimethylsilyl)tricyclonononene-7 (20) however a simultaneous formation of the cyclic by-products $\boldsymbol{21}$ and $\boldsymbol{22}$ was also observed (Scheme 4).

According to GS-MS-analysis and NMR-spectroscopy the products $\boldsymbol{20}$ and $\boldsymbol{21}$ were obtained in approximately equal amounts while $\boldsymbol{22}$ was formed with less than 5 % yield. The products $\boldsymbol{20}$ and $\boldsymbol{21}$ were isolated with preparative HPLC. Their structures were confirmed with NMR, IR spectroscopy and elemental analysis. The possible way of the cyclic by-products (21, 22) formation is presented in Scheme 5.

We decided to try a more mild and selective alkylation agent such as MeMgI in more hard conditions. Diethyl ether was replaced as a solvent for di-n-butyl ether. This allowed us to raise the reaction temperature up to 140° C (Scheme 6).

Methylation of product $\boldsymbol{14}$ under these conditions was indeed successful and resulted in a desirable tricyclononene containing three bulky $\ce{Me3Si}$-groups (20) in a good yield without by-products formation.

## 4 Conclusions

For the first time the behavior of a series of ethylenes containing three $\ce{MenCl3-nSi}$-substituents (n=0 to 3) was systematically studied in the cycloaddition reaction with quadricyclane. The scope of active substrates was established and the sequence of activities of silicon-substituted ethylenes in cycloaddition with quadricyclane was determined. A series of new $\ce{MenCl3-nSi}$-substituted tricyclononenes was synthesized and the methylation of the corresponding sterically hindered chlorosilanes was studied under different conditions. The appropriate conditions for the full and selective methylation reaction of the cycloadducts were also found.
