## Abstract
Two-dimensional van der Waals (vdW) heterostructures were constructed using MoSSe and XN ($X = \text{Ga}, \text{Al}$) via density functional calculations to serve as water splitting photocatalysts. Both the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN heterostructures are energetically, dynamically and thermally stable. Interestingly, they exhibited type-II band structures, indicating the ability to continuously separate photogenerated electrons and holes. They also have appropriate band edge positions for redox reaction potentials of water splitting at pH 0. Remarkably, the MoSSe/GaN vdW heterostructure possessed excellent carrier mobility for holes along both the transport directions. Besides, the charge transfer between the MoSSe and XN layers induced a strong built-in electric field, which further separated the photogenerated carriers. In addition, the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures exhibit good optical absorption ability toward solar irradiation. All these excellent properties render the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN heterostructures are high-efficiency photocatalysts for water splitting.

Keywords: Janus transition metal dichalcogenides, Nitrides, vdW heterostructure, photocatalysis, Water splitting

## 1. Introduction

In recent years, much attention has been focused on energy shortages. One alternative that has attracted significant interest is hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$), which is considered to be a promising green energy source as it produces virtually no environmental pollution [1]. To advance this option, researchers have investigated efficient catalysts that can decompose water into $\text{H}_2$ and oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) [2]. For example, $\text{NiO/NaTaO}_3$ doped with lanthanum can be used for water splitting at rates of 19.8 and 9.7 mmol/h for $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$, respectively [3]. When co-doped with rhodium and antimony, tantalum, or niobium, $\text{TiO}_2$ can also be considered as an efficient visible-light driven photocatalyst for the production of $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ [4]. However, the ability to

use bulk materials as photocatalysts for water splitting is lim- ited by the short lifetime of the excited-state charges (about 3–10 ps) [5].

In contrast, the larger specific surface area of two-dimen- sional (2D) materials provides a shorter route for pho- togenerated electron–hole pairs to flow to the surface of the photocatalyst for the redox reaction [6], which will prolong the lifetime of the photogenerated electron–hole pairs. For these reasons, 2D materials are more suitable for use as pho- tocatalysts than bulk materials. The outstanding performance of graphene has prompted researchers to investigate other 2D materials [7–14], such as transition metal dichalcoge- nides (TMDs) materials, which is a category of layered mat- erials that have novel thermoelectric [15], catalytic [16, 17], and optical [18–20] properties. TMDs can be prepared by exfoliation [21], protein induces [22], chemical vapor trans- port (CVT) [23], and chemical vapor deposition (CVD) [24] methods. Very recently, TMD materials with a Janus structure, such as MoSSe, have been prepared by replacing half of the S atoms on one side of the $MoS_2$ with Se atoms at a certain temperature [25, 26], and the symmetry in this material can be further modified to exhibit much more beneficial proper- ties [27–30]. The electronic properties of the Janus MoSSe can be efficiently tuned through the biaxial strain and stacking effect [31]. In addition, they hold promise as a photocatalyst for water splitting and, when compared to monolayered $MoS_2$ with an adsorption energy of about 0.04 eV for water decom- position, MoSSe is more suitable due to its stronger adsorp- tion energy (about 0.16 eV) of $H_2O$ molecule on the surface [32]. Furthermore, the ability of the hydrogen evolution reac- tion (HER) of MoSSe can be enhanced by intrinsic defects, such as vacancies, antisites, and grain boundaries [33]. These characteristics hold promise for Janus MoSSe monolayer as a photocatalyst for water splitting.

When a monolayered semiconductor is utilized for water decomposition, reduction and oxidation reactions are simul- taneously occured at the surface, which increases the recom- bination rate of the photogenerated electron–hole pairs. This can overcome by constructing a heterostructure consisting of two different 2D materials joined by van der Waals (vdW) interaction to continuously separate the photogenerated elec- tron and hole pairs [1], which is now being used to expand the scope of application for 2D materials [34]. For example, some researchers focused on the fabrication of heterostructures based on Janus MoSSe such as MoSSe/WSSSe [27], MoSSe/ $WSe_2$ [35] and MoSSe/graphene [36], etc.

In parallel, monolayered aluminium nitride (AlN) pos- sesses interesting electronic and magnetic properties [37]. It has been reported that theoretically, synthesising mono- layered AlN via the epitaxial growth technique is preferred over mechanical exfoliation [38]. At present, wurtzite AlN film has been papered on substrates of 6H–SiC at $300\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ [39]. Interestingly, different forms of AlN, such as nanorib- bons [40], nanowires [41], and nanosheets [42], also exhibit notable properties. More recently, the monolayered honey- comb structure of gallium nitride (GaN) has received con- siderable attention, which is a 2D material prepared via the migration-enhanced encapsulated growth technique using epitaxial graphene [43]. It has a tunable bandgap with a large range, which means it can potentially be used in optoelec- tronic devices [44, 45], whose property also can be tuned by adsorption [46]. Besides, some investigations focus on GaN or AlN based heterostructures such as $GaN/Mg(OH)_2$ [47], TMDs/GaN [48], GaN/BSe [49], and $Mg(OH)_2/AlN$ [50], etc. However, there is a lack of reports to date discussing theor- etical or experimental investigations into Janus TMDs/GaN and Janus TMDs/AlN heterostructures.

Here, we investigate the structural, electronic, and optical properties of 2D vdW heterostructures composed by MoSSe and XN (X = Ga, Al) monolayers to serve as photocatalysts for water splitting, using first-principle calculations. These include the most stable stacking pattern, thermal and dynamic stability, band alignment, carrier mobility, interface charge transfer, and optical absorption of the MoSSe/XN heterostruc- ture. Our results show that the developed MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN heterostructures are promising candidates for photocatalysts in water splitting applications.

## 2. Computational details

In this study, the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [51, 52] was utilized to perform first-principle calculations based on density functional theory (DFT). The projector augmented wave potentials (PAW) [53] were employed to describe the core electrons with an energy cut-off of 550 eV. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [54] was used to describe the exchange correlation functional. The weak dis- person forces were described by the DFT-D3 method proposed by Grimme [55]. In addition, the Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof hybrid functional [56] was used to attain more accurate elec- tronic and optical properties. A $17\times17\times1$ Monkhorst–Pack k-point grids were adopted in the first Brillouin zone (BZ). A vacuum space of at least $20\ \mathring{\text{A}}$ was used to avoid interaction between adjacent slabs. The convergence for force and energy are choosen as $0.01\ \text{eV}\ \mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$ and 0.01 meV, respectively. As for the calculations of the phonon spectra, the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) within the PHONOPY code was used [57, 58].

The type-II band structure in the two semiconductor vdW heterostructures make them ideally suitable as photocatalysts to decompose water. A schematic of the migration of photogenerated electron–holes at the interface of the heterostructures is shown in figure 5(a). The calculated conduction band offset (CBO) and valence band offset (VBO) of the MoSSe/GaN (MoSSe/AlN) vdW heterostructure are 1.480 (2.294) eV and 0.377 (0.352) eV, respectively. When the MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure is irradiated by sunlight, the photogenerated electrons obtain energy from photons and will migrate from the valence band (VB) to the conduction band (CB) of the MoSSe and XN layers, respectively, thereby producing holes in the VB. Meanwhile, driven by the VBO, the photogenerated holes will flow from the VB of the MoSSe layer to the VB of the XN layer. In contrast, the photogenerated electrons will transfer from the CB of the XN layer to the CB of the MoSSe layer due to the CBO, which can effectively prevent the recombination of the photogenerated charges on a continuous basis.

### 3.2. Carrier mobilities

Charge carrier mobility is a key factor in constructing a high-efficiency photocatalyst for water splitting as higher mobilities result in a high utilization of charge in redox reactions before recombination. For these reasons, the carrier mobility of the MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure was computed. As per the Bardeen-Shockley method [66], the carrier mobility ($\mu$) was computed as

$$
\mu_{2 \mathrm{D}}=\frac{e \hbar^{3} C_{2 \mathrm{D}}}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T m_{\mathrm{e}}^{*} m_{\mathrm{d}}\left(E_{1}^{\mathrm{i}}\right)^{2}},\qquad(2)
$$

where $e$, $\hbar$, $k_{\mathrm{B}}$, $T$, $m_{\mathrm{e}}^{*}$, and $m_{\mathrm{d}}$ represent the charge of an electron, the Plank's constant, the Boltzmann constant, the temperature, the effective mass along the transport direction, and the average effective mass, respectively. Here, $m_{\mathrm{d}}$ can be determined by $m_{\mathrm{d}}=\sqrt{m_{\mathrm{x}}^{*} m_{\mathrm{y}}^{*}}$. The deformation potential constant is denoted by $E_{1}$, which is calculated by $E_{1}^{\mathrm{i}}=\Delta V_{i} /\left(\Delta l / l_{0}\right)$, where $\Delta V_{\mathrm{i}}$ is the energy difference between the CBM and VBM under external strain. As for the elastic modulus of the layered material $(C_{2 \mathrm{D}})$, it was computed via $C_{2 \mathrm{D}}=\left[\partial^{2} \Delta E / \partial l^{2}\right] / S_{0}$, where $\Delta E$, $l$, and $S_{0}$ are the energy change relative to the unstrained system, the external strain, and the lattice area of the 2D materials. The transport directions of for the carriers in the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures are illustrated in figures 6, which are the armchair and zigzag directions, respectively. The elastic modulus was obtained by computing $\Delta E$ of the layered materials under external uniaxial strain, and the responses for the strain along the transport direction of the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures

### 3.3. Interfacial properties
To qualitatively investigate the charge transfer between the layers of MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure, the isosurfaces of charge-density difference $(\Delta \rho)$ are shown in figures 7(a) and (c). These are defined by

$$
\Delta \rho=\Delta \rho_{\mathrm{MoSSe} / \mathrm{XN}}-\rho_{\mathrm{MoSSe}}-\rho_{\mathrm{XN}}, \tag{3}
$$

where $\rho_{\mathrm{MoSSe} / \mathrm{XN}}, \rho_{\mathrm{MoSSe}}$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{XN}}$ represent the total charge density of MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure, MoSSe mono- layer, and XN monolayer, respectively. It can be seen that the XN layer acts as an electron donor to the MoSSe layer in the MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure. The total number of elec-trons transferred between the MoSSe and XN layers $(\Delta Q(z))$ is determined by

$$
\Delta Q(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{z} \Delta \rho\left(z^{\prime}\right) d z^{\prime}. \tag{4}
$$

As shown in figures 7(a) and (c), the transformation of the charge was about 0.107 and 0.158 |e| for the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructure per unit cell, respectively. Figures 7(b) and (d) show the plane-averaged electrostatic potential along the vacuum layer across the interface of the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures, respec- tively. The potential drop $(\Delta V)$ across the interface of the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructure MoSSe layer was 7.03 and 2.23 eV, respectively, which will induce a large built-in electric field, and also provide a boost to sepa- rate the photogenerated electron-holes [70].

### 3.4. Optical properties
It is important for a photocatalyst to have good sunlight har- vesting ability. Considering half of solar energy is contained in the visible light region, the ability to absorb a meaningful fraction of visible light is essential [71]. The optical absorp- tion of the MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure was calculated as follows [72]

$$
\alpha(\omega)=\frac{\sqrt{2} \omega}{c}\left\{\left[\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)\right]^{1 / 2}-\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)\right\}^{1 / 2}, \tag{5}
$$

where $\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)$ represent the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric constant, respectively, and $\omega, \alpha$ and $c$ repre sent the angular frequency, the absorption coefficient, and the speed of light in vacuum, respectively. The calculated optical absorption of MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure is shown in figure 8. It clearly shows that the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/ AlN vdW heterostructures can enhance the optical absorption property of the monolayered GaN and AlN. In detail, the peaks of light absorption for the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures were $15.80 \times 10^{5}$ and $14.24 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, respectively, at wavelengths of 127 and 125 nm in the ultra- violet (UV) regions. In the visible-light region, the peaks of the optical absorption for the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures were $2.74 \times 10^{5}$ and $3.95 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ at absorption waves of 425 and 412 nm, respectively. ## 4. Conculsion

In summary, first-principle calculations were conducted to investigate the structural, electronic, interfacial, and optical properties of 2D vdW heterostructures based on MoSSe and XN. The MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures were found to be dynamically and thermally stable at 300 K. In addition, they both possess a type-II band structure that separated the photogenerated electron-holes pairs effectively. The band edge positions of the heterostructures were calculated and found to be sufficient to guarantee the occurrence of a redox reaction for water splitting at pH 0. The carrier mobility of these layered materials was also evaluated, and the MoSSe/GaN vdW heterostructure shows high carrier mobility, especially for holes, which can reach $3651.83 \mathrm{cm}^2 \cdot \mathrm{V}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ along the zigzag direction. At the same time, the MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructure also have comparable carrier mobility than other 2D materials as promising photocatalyst for water splitting. Moreover, the GaN layer and AlN layer donates 0.107 and 0.158 |e| to the MoSSe layer in MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures, respectively. The potential drops across the interface of the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures were computed to be 7.03 and 2.23 eV, respectively, which induce a built-in electric field to further prevent the recombination of the photogenerated electron-hole pairs. More importantly, the MoSSe/GaN and MoSSe/AlN vdW heterostructures were shown to have excellent optical absorption abilities for sunlight. Based on these results, the MoSSe/XN vdW heterostructure is a promising efficient photocatalyst for water splitting applications.
