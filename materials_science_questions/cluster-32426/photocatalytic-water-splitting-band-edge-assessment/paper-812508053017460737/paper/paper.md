## ABSTRACT

Using two-dimensional (2D) materials as photocatalyst for water splitting become a considerable technology for green and clean energy, where the candidate photocatalysts hold promising development of this field. Here, the monolayered Arsenene (As) and the group-III monochalcogenide, GaX (X = S, Se) are presented to construct the heterostructure, and the detailed theoretical study of the structural and electronic properties of the heterostructure are addressed by first-principle calculations. The As/GaX heterostructure are proved to be thermodynamic stable with intrinsic type-II band structure to prevent the recombination of the photogenerated electron-hole pairs and suitable band edge energy potential to induce the redox reaction for water splitting at pH value of 0. The calculated charge density demonstrates that the As layer is positively charged while the GaX layer is negatively charged. The obtained potential drop across the interface of the As/GaX heterostructure can result a built-in electric field, which also is a significant role to separate the photogenerated charges. Furthermore, the As/GaX heterostructure possess novel sunlight capturing performance and high solar-to-hydrogen efficiency, and all that promise the usage in photocatalytic water splitting for As/GaX heterostructure.

### 1. Introduction

Two-dimensional (2D) materials have attracted considerable attentions owing their fascinating properties [1,2]. In particular, transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), which can be describe as AB₂ (A = Mo, W; B = S, Se, etc.), possess excellent electrocatalytic [3], electronic [4], optical [5], thermoelectric [6,7], mechanical and thermal properties [8,9], promising their potential application for heterostructure [10], photocatalyst [11], electronics and energy harvesting [12]. Interestingly, the above-mentioned properties of TMDs can be tuned by doping [13], external strain [14] and electric field [15]. Phosphorus is another popular 2D material, Cai [16] investigated that the black phosphorus (BlackP) has ultrahigh activity for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) with defective activation by the calculation of the Gibb's free energy. The point defects also can modulate the electronic and optical performance of the blue phosphorus (BlueP) [17]. The nanophononic BlackP heterostructure shows the greatly thermal rectification characteristic while the mass-mismatched BlackP heterostructure can effectively reduce the interfacial thermal conductance by non-equilibrium molecular dynamics calculations [18]. Moreover, tremendous strategies have been developed for the preparation of phosphorus, such as epitaxial growth [19], quasi-free-standing growth [20], electrochemical exfoliation [21]. All those explain the 2D materials are promising utilized for thermoelectrics and photocatalytic devices [22,23].

Inspired by the novel properties of 2D materials, applying 2D materials for the photocatalytic water splitting of the energy convert into hydrogen (H₂) and oxygen (O₂) is promoted because of high specific surface area with respect to bulk materials [24], which provides ample area for HER and oxygen evolution reactions (OER). However, when the monolayered photocatalyst takes in the energy from light, the HER and OER will occur at the same surface, meaning the high probability of the recombination between the electrons and holes generated by sunlight [25]. Many investigations have reported that 2D heterostructure can efficiently act as a photocatalyst for water splitting because of their staggered band structure [28], lower exciton binding energy [29], high carrier mobility [30], decent redox ability [31], considerable optical property [32] etc. TMDs based heterostructure hold great application prospect, for example, GeS/WSe₂ [33], TMDs/ZnO [34], TiO₂/TMDs [35], BlueP/TMDs [36-38], Janus TMDs/XN (X = Ga, Al) [39], etc., are proved to be excellent photocatalyst to decompose water. Combined with nonadiabatic molecular dynamics, Liang found that Janus MoSSe/WSe₂ possesses ultrafast charge separation of time-scale about 286-1.028 ps implementing the time-dependent density functional theory [40]. Recently, Arsenene (As) has obtained much studies because its classy electronic properties which can be tuned into a direct bandgap semiconductor under decent external strain [41] and number of layers [42]. The As monolayer also possesses excellent optical properties [43,44], showing the promising applications for nanoscale optoelectronic and electronic devices. Besides, using Arsenene (As) monolayer to construct the heterostructure is popular because its novel electronic and structural properties [42-44]. As/TMDs van der Waals (vdW) heterostructure possesses a direct Z-scheme photocatalytic mechanism for water splitting and excellent solar-to-hydrogen (STH) efficiency [45]. Furthermore, the As based vdW heterostructures also exhibit tunable electronic performance under external biaxial or uniaxial strain and electric field [46]. More recently, group-III monochalcogenide monolayers also have been extensively investigated [47], the family of group-III monochalcogenide show excellent stability and high experimental feasibility [48]. They also exhibit excellent photocatalytic properties when they used as a heterostructure, which significantly increase the power conversion efficiency (PCE) by N atom doping [49], showing great potential for photocatalyst to decompose water. Other group-III monochalcogenide based heterostructure, such as PtS₂/InSe, whose electronic properties can be tuned by external electric field and vertical strain [50]; CdS/InSe heterostructure has been reported as a photocatalyst possessing direct Z-scheme characteristic in water splitting [51]; by controlling the Schottky contact types under vertical strain, the graphene/InSe heterostructure can be used in electronic and optoelectronic devices [52]; the high carrier mobility, ranging about $10^3$-$10^4$ cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹, is proved for electron and hole in InSe/Zr₂CO₂ heterostructure [53]; the excellent charge separation ability in Sb/InSe heterostructure provides a promising application for photodetectors and electronics [54]. Although lots of researches have been implemented to construct the As or group-III monochalcogenide monolayers based heterostructure for water splitting, only rarely works focus on the As/GaS and As/GaSe heterostructures, and the monolayers we selected have been prepared experimentally. The 2D As has been prepared as nanosheets and nanodots by the method of ultrasonication of grey arsenic in appropriate solvents, it also has a bandgap similar to the calculated value (about 2.3 eV) [55,56], while 2D ultrathin layer GaS and GaSe can be synthesized by a micro-mechanical cleavage technique from their bulk crystals [47,57]. 2D nanosheets of ultrathin GaSe has been prepared by using mechanical cleavage and solvent exfoliation method [58], the GaSe usually is p-type semiconductors with an indirect bandgap of 2.11 eV, which is only 25 meV lower than the direct bandgap [59-61]. GaS nanosheet photodetectors has been made on SiO₂/Si substrates and flexible polyethylene terephthalate substrates, whose bandgap is about 3.05 eV [62]. Therefore, the As/GaX (X = S and Se) heterostructure is meaningful and valuable to be formed for photocatalyst to decompose the water.

In this work, we carry out the first-principle calculations to explore the atomic and electronic properties of the monolayered As, GaX (X = S and Se) and their heterostructures. After the thermodynamic stability of the As/GaX heterostructure are studied, the type-II band structure are addressed into the As/GaX heterostructure, which allow them to separate the photogenerated charges. Then, the calculated band alignment of the As/GaX heterostructure demonstrates the appropriate band edge potentials to promote the redox reaction for water splitting. Besides, the interfacial performance of the As/GaX heterostructure are addressed. Except the calculated optical adsorption of the monolayered As, GaX and the heterostructure, the STH of them are also studied, which explains both As/GaS and As/GaSe heterostructures are promising photocatalyst for water splitting.

## 2. Computational methods

All the calculations in this investigation were based on density functional theory (DFT) [63]. We performed the DFT calculations using Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [64,65]. We carried out projector augmented wave potentials (PAW) [66] to reveal the core electrons. The energy cut-off was set as 450 eV. Combined with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional, the exchange correlation functional was demonstrated by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [67]. And the DFT-D3 described the weak dispersion forces [68]. For the accurate electronic and optical properties of the system, the Heyd-Scuseria-Ernzerhof hybrid functional was also considered [69]. Besides, the Monkhorst-Pack k-point grids were using by 11 × 11 × 1 in the first Brillouin zone. A vacuum space of at least 25 Å was used to avoid interaction between adjacent slabs. Moreover, the Hellmann-Feynman force on every atom was used as 0.01 eV Å⁻¹ to relax the structures of the layered materials by the conjugated gradient method.

The binding energy ($E_\text{b}$) for the As/GaX system were calculated as follows:
$$
E_\text{b} = E_{\text{As/GaX}} - E_{\text{As}} - E_{\text{GaX}}, \tag{1}
$$
where $E_{\text{As/GaX}}$, $E_{\text{As}}$, and $E_{\text{GaX}}$ are the total energy of the As/GaX system, monolayered As and GaX, respectively. In the calculations of the charge density difference ($\Delta\rho$) for the As/GaX system, it is decided by:
$$
\Delta\rho = \rho_{\text{As/GaX}} - \rho_{\text{As}} - \rho_{\text{GaX}}, \tag{2}
$$
where the $\rho_{\text{As/GaX}}$, $\rho_{\text{As}}$ and $\rho_{\text{GaX}}$ are the charge density of the As/GaX system, monolayered As and GaX, respectively. The optical absorption of the layered materials in our investigation is calculated as:
$$
\alpha(\omega) = \frac{\sqrt{2} \omega}{c} \left\{ \left[ \varepsilon_1^2(\omega) + \varepsilon_2^2(\omega) \right]^{1/2} - \varepsilon_1(\omega) \right\}^{1/2}, \tag{3}
$$
where $\varepsilon_1^2(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_2^2(\omega)$ express the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric constant, respectively. Then, the $\omega$, $\alpha$ and $c$ are angular frequency, absorption coefficient and the speed of light in vacuum, respectively [70]. As for the study of the STH efficiency ($\eta_{\text{STH}}$), we adopt the methodology as follows [71]:
$$
\eta_{\text{STH}} = \eta_{\text{abs}} \times \eta_{\text{cu}}, \tag{4}
$$
where the $\eta_{\text{abs}}$ demonstrate the efficiency of light absorption and the $\eta_{\text{cu}}$ is carrier utilization, respectively, in which the $\eta_{\text{abs}}$ is obtained from:
$$
\eta_{\text{abs}} = \frac{\int_{E_\text{g}}^\infty P(h\omega)\text{d}(h\omega)}{\int_{0}^{\infty} P(h\omega)\text{d}(h\omega)} \tag{5}
$$
where we used $h\omega$ to express the photon energy and $P(h\omega)$ is the AM 1.5 G solar energy flux; $E_\text{g}$ explains the bandgap of materials. The $\eta_{\text{cu}}$ is calculated as:

$$
\eta_{\mathrm{cu}}=\frac{\Delta G \int_{E_{\mathrm{g}}}^{\infty} \frac{P(h \omega)}{h \omega} \mathrm{d}(h \omega)}{\int_{E_{\mathrm{g}}}^{\infty} P(h \omega) \mathrm{d}(h \omega)}
\tag{6}
$$

where the $\Delta G$ is 1.23 eV describing the potential difference for water splitting and $E$ is the photon energy actually used for water splitting, which can be obtained by:

$$
E=
\begin{cases}
E_{\mathrm{g}},\left(\chi\left(\mathrm{H}_{2}\right) \geq 0.2, \chi\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right) \geq 0.6\right) \\
E_{\mathrm{g}}+0.2-\chi\left(\mathrm{H}_{2}\right),\left(\chi\left(\mathrm{H}_{2}\right)<0.2, \chi\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right) \geq 0.6\right) \\
E_{\mathrm{g}}+0.6-\chi\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right),\left(\chi\left(\mathrm{H}_{2}\right) \geq 0.2, \chi\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right)<0.6\right) \\
E_{\mathrm{g}}+0.8-\chi\left(\mathrm{H}_{2}\right)-\chi\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right),\left(\chi\left(\mathrm{H}_{2}\right)<0.2, \chi\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right)<0.6\right)
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
$$

where $\chi(\mathrm{H}_{2})$ and $\chi(\mathrm{O}_{2})$ show the overpotential for the HER and OER, respectively. In general, the necessary overpotentials of the HER and OER are obtained by previous experimental investigations, which are proposed to be 0.2 and 0.6 eV [71], respectively, so the Eq. (7) is used for the value of $E$.

## 3. Results and discussion

We optimized the structures of the As, GaS, GaSe monolayers as Fig. 1 by the lattice constants of 3.607, 3.624 and 3.803 Å, respectively, agreeing well with the previous reports [47,55]. The bond length of the As-As, Ga-S and Ga-Se are optimized by 2.506, 2.360 and 2.690 Å, respectively. Then, the band structure of the As, GaS and GaSe monolayers were calculated in Fig. 1 with the bandgap of 2.234, 3.279 and 2.751 eV by HSE06 functional, respectively, which are good agreement with other reports [45,47] and comparable with the above-mentioned experimental bandgap value. One can find that the As monolayer has the indirect bandgap, while GaS and GaSe possess indirect bandgap in the first Brillouin zone.

To construct the heterostructure based on As and GaS (or GaSe) monolayers, six representative stacking configurations were formed in Fig. 2. We decided the most stable stacking style by calculating the binding energy $(E_{\mathrm{b}})$ of the heterostructure. And the most stable system of the As/Ga and As/GaSe heterostructures are G-II and G-V, respectively, with the $E_{\mathrm{b}}$ of $-28.5 \mathrm{meV} / \AA^{2}$ and $-59.2 \mathrm{meV} / \AA^{2}$, demonstrating both heterostructures are formed by vdW interactions [72]. The optimized distance of the interface for the As/GaS and As/ GaSe vdW heterostructures were 3.803 and 3.291 Å, respectively. Furthermore, the following investigations of the As/GaX vdW heterostructure were based on the most stable structures.

To further evaluate the thermodynamic stability of the As/GaS and As/GaSe vdW heterostructures, $ab$ initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations were also explored with a Nosé-Hoover heat bath scheme [73]. Considering the lattice translational constraints, a $5 \times 5 \times 1$ supercell for the As/GaX vdW heterostructure containing 150 atoms was used in the AIMD simulation [74]. After finishing the simulations, the structures of the As/GaS and As/GaSe vdW heterostructures were still intact at 300 K after 5 ps, as shown in Fig. 3(a) and (b), respectively, which explains the robust thermal stability at room temperature. The fluctuations in temperature and total energy of the As/GaX vdW heterostructures by the simulation step are demonstrated in Fig. 3(c), showing the convergence of results.

The projected band energy of the As/GaS and As/GaSe vdW heterostructures were explored in Fig. 4(a) and (c), respectively. The gray and red marks explain the contributions of the As and GaS (or GaSe) monolayers, respectively, demonstrating the type-II band structure is generated in both heterostructures. In detail, the As/GaS vdW heterostructure possesses an indirect bandgap by the conduction band minimum (CBM) existing at the $\Gamma$ point, and the valence band maximum (VBM) is found between the $\Gamma$ and M points. For As/GaSe vdW heterostructure, it also has an indirect bandgap while the CBM and VBM are located between the $\Gamma$ and M points. At the same time, the projected density of sates of the As/GaX vdW heterostructure were also calculated in the Fig. 4(a) and (c). The projected density of sates of the As/GaS (As/GaSe) vdW heterostructure clearly shows that the CBM of the heterostructure is mainly resulted from Ga atoms, while the CBM is induced by As atoms. To further prove the donations of the CBM and VBM of the As/ GaS and As/GaSe vdW heterostructures, the band-resolved charge densities were obtained as Fig. 4(b) and (d), respectively. All that show the CBM and VBM of the As/GaX vdW heterostructure are donated by GaX and As monolayers, respectively. The bandgap of the As/GaS (or As/GsSe) vdW heterostructure is calculated as 1.852 (or 2.120) eV, which is larger than the potential difference of 1.23 eV to decompose the water [75].

The intrinsic type-II band structure in the As/GaX heterostructure can built a band offset near the interface, conduction band offset (CBO) and the valence band offset (VBO) shown in the Fig. 5(a), which is a crucial potential difference for preventing the recombination between the photogenerated electrons and holes. As Fig. 5(a) shown, when the light is illuminating at As/GaX vdW heterostructure, the heterostructure absorb the photon energy, the electrons can be excited from valence band to conduction band, leaving the holes at valence band. While the photogenerated electrons arrive at the conduction band of the As layer, they will continue to migrate to the conduction band of the GaS (or GaSe) layer by the assistance of the CBO; the photogenerated holes at the valence band of the GaS (or GaSe) layer will transform to the valence band of the As layer under the force of the VBO. Therefore, the photogenerated electrons and holes are separated constantly, and the photogenerated electrons at the GaS (or GaSe) layer act on the reduction reaction, while the photogenerated holes at the valence band of the As layer induce the oxidation reaction, which means $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ are decomposed at GaS (or GaSe) and As layers, respectively. Besides, the calculated CBO and VBO of the As/GaS (or As/GaSe) vdW heterostructures are calculated as 0.341 (or 1.255) eV and 0.110 (or 1.654) eV, respectively.

## 4. Conclusions

In summary, we systematically investigated the atomic and electronic properties of the monolayered As, GaX and As/GaX heterostructure. Both As/GaS and As/GaSe heterostructures are formed by vdW forces have a bandgap of 1.852 and 2.120 eV, respectively. The intrinsic type-II band structure provides them the ability to separate the photogenerated electrons and holes, while they also have suitable band edge positions for the potential of the oxidation and reduction reactions of the water splitting at pH 0. Furthermore, after As and GaX monolayers contact, the GaS and GaSe layers obtained 0.0081 and 0.0101 |e| form the As layer, and the calculated potential drop across the interface of the As/GaS and As/GaSe vdW heterostructures were calculated as 4.215 and 1.317 eV, respectively, such large potential drop can induce a built-in electric field near the interface, which also can prevent the recombination of the photogenerated electron-hole pairs. Besides, the As/GaX vdW heterostructure possesses excellent sunlight absorption comparing the original materials, and high solar-to-hydrogen efficiency of 24-26%. Therefore, the As/GaX vdW heterostructure has promising application as a candidate of photocatalyst for water splitting.
