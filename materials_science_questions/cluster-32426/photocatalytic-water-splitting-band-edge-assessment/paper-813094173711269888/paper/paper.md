# Enhancement of the optical absorption of carbon group elements doped ZnS in the visible light range

Hai-Cai Huang, Chuan-Lu Yang*, Mei-Shan Wang, and Xiao-Guang Ma

School of Physics and Optoelectronics Engineering, Ludong University, Yantai 264025, the People's Republic of China

## ABSTRACT

Although the pristine ZnS can perform photocatalytic hydrogen production from water splitting, the reaction can not be driven by the visible light because of its wide energy band gap. Therefore, ZnS doped with carbon group elements (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb) has been investigated by using first-principles density functional theory calculations to understand the feasibility of the reaction driven by solar energy. All the geometrical structures are fully optimized. The electronic and optical properties of these structures have been calculated with the meta-GGA+MBJ method and with projector augmented wave potential. The results show that all the doped structures have low formation energies, implying that the synthesis of the doped structures is not difficult in energy. Moreover, the absorptions of the doped structures in the visible light range are significantly enhanced, but their energy levels are still suitable for water splitting to generate hydrogen, which means that carbon group elements doped ZnS structures are promising candidate materials for water splitting driven by visible light.

**Keywords**: photocatalytic water splitting, hydrogen production, optical absorption coefficient, doped ZnS

## 1. INTRODUCTION

In order to solve the serious environmental problems and the depletion of fossil fuels effectively, a new form of energy has been searching for representing a viable alternative to the fossil fuels [1-4]. Hydrogen ($\mathrm{H_2}$), as a clean, cheap, renewable energy has attracted more attention [5, 6]. Recently, photocatalytic water splitting under sun light by some catalysts is becoming a promising way for the generation of $\mathrm{H_2}$ [7-9]. In fact, the conversion of solar light into hydrogen through $\mathrm{TiO_2}$ photocatalytic from water splitting has been firstly reported by Fujishima and Hongda in 1972 [10]. More semiconductors, such as $\mathrm{NaTaO_3}$, $\mathrm{KTaO_3}$, $\mathrm{K_3Ta_3B_2O_{12}}$ [11-15], have been reported as photocatalytic materials for the splitting water to produce hydrogen. Nowadays, more works have been devoted to enhancing the efficiency of the photocatalytic activity of these materials. In 2009, Valle et al. [16] found that the Zn concentration influenced the activity of $\mathrm{Cd_{1-x}Zn_xS}$ for water splitting obviously. In the same year, Kudo and Miseki found that $\mathrm{NaTaO_3}$ shows a higher water splitting rates with the addition of NiO [17].

As one of the most important II-VI semiconductors [18], ZnS has a wide band gap of about 3.7 eV at room temperature [19]. Due to its high refractive index, high effective dielectric constant and wide wavelength band gap, ZnS has wide technological applications such as filters, reflectors and planar wave guide [20]. ZnS has been used to splitting water to produce hydrogen[21]. However, the wide band energy gap makes it cannot be effectively driven by solar light. Fortunately, the gap can be tuned by using various dopants. For example, Manzoor et al. [22] reported

that the band energy gap of ZnS can be significantly reduced by doped Cu. Tang et al. [23] found that the doped-Pd can also result in similar effect for ZnS.

The visible light is the main component of the solar light reaching the surface of the earth. Therefore, the photocatalytic water splitting driven by visible light is one of the most important ways to utilize the solar energy. The absorption of ZnS in visible light range can also be enhanced by doping with various dopants. In 1993, Bhargava et al. [24] found that the quantum efficiency of Mn-doped ZnS increased and the optical properties were improved. In 2013, ZnS quantum dots as green nanophotocatalysts were successfully synthesized without the use of high temperature or any organic solvent by Rajabi et al. [25]. They found that the absorption of ZnS quantum dots in visible light and UV light range increased by doping with $Fe^{3+}$. Moreover, the doped structure shows a higher photocatalytic activity in comparison with the pristine one. Recently, Prasad et al. [26] found that the photocatalytic activity of ZnS is significantly enhanced by doped Cu. However, the possibility to use them to split water remains unexplored. But in 2011, Bi-doped ZnS hollow spheres were successfully synthesized using a simple cation exchange method by Zhang et al [27]. They found that the positions of the valence band maximum (VBM) and the conduction band minimum (CBM) for Bi-doped ZnS satisfied the requirements for $H_2$-production, meanwhile, the UV and visible light photocatalytic activity for $H_2$-production of ZnS was enhanced. The requirements for photocatalytic $H_2$-production from water splitting will be stated in the following text.

Recently, (C, Sn, Pb)-doped ZnS has been successfully synthesized in experiment

[28-30]. However, to the best of our knowledge, neither theoretical nor experimental investigation had been performed for carbon group elements doped ZnS to perform photocatalytic water splitting for hydrogen production, except for Pb-doped ZnS [30].

In the present work, the electronic and optical properties for the pristine and doped ZnS have been investigated based on the first principles density functional theory (DFT). The mechanism of the enhancement for carbon group elements doping ZnS in the visible light range is explored and the feasibility of carbon group elements doped ZnS to perform photocatalytic hydrogen production from water splitting has also been investigated.

## 2. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS

The doped structures have been constructed based on the 2×2×2 supercell of ZnS (including 64 atoms) with one Zn atom substituted by the carbon group elements. The doped structures are represented with C@Zn, Si@Zn, Ge@Zn, Sn@Zn, and Pb@Zn, respectively. All the structures have been fully optimized with the exchange and correlation potentials of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) parameterization under the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). Due to the inherent error of the self-interaction, GGA always fails to predict the correct electronic properties [31]. Therefore, the meta-GGA+MBJ potential [32] has been used to get more accurate electronic properties and band gaps. All the calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [33], with projector augmented wave (PAW) potential [34]. For PAW potentials, only Zn ($3d^{10}4s^{2}$), S ($3s^{2}3p^{4}$), C ($2s^{2}2p^{2}$),

Si $(3s^23p^2)$, Ge $(4s^24p^2)$, Sn $(5s^25p^2)$, Pb $(6s^26p^2)$ are considered as the valence electron described with plane wave basis sets. A plane-wave cutoff energy has been used as 450 eV throughout the calculations. The tolerance of electronic minimization was set to $10^{-6}$ eV. Brillouin zone sampling was performed using the Monkhorst-Pack k-point meshes of 3×3×3 and 5×5×5 for the optimizations of the structures and the calculations of electronic properties.

The complex dielectric functions are calculated for deducing the optical properties of the pristine and the doped ZnS. The imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ of the dielectric function is calculated from the momentum matrix elements between the unoccupied states and the occupied based on the meta-GGA+MBJ calculations. The real part $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ is derived from the imaginary part by the Kramers-Kronig transformation [35]. The corresponding formulas are as following [36]:

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)=\frac{4 \pi^{2}}{m^{2} \omega^{2}} \sum_{C, V} \int_{B Z} \frac{2}{(2 \pi)^{3}}\left|M_{C, V}(k)\right|^{2} \delta\left(\mathrm{E}_{C}^{k}-\mathrm{E}_{V}^{k}-\hbar \omega\right) \mathrm{d}^{3} k \tag{1}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\pi} \rho_{0} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega^{\prime} \varepsilon_{2}(\omega)}{\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}} d \omega \tag{2}
$$

Where $\omega$ is angular frequency, subscripts $C$ and $V$ represent conduction band and valence band, respectively. BZ is the first Brillouin zone, $\left|M_{C, V}(k)\right|^{2}$ is momentum matrix element, $k$ is reciprocal lattice vector, $E_{C}^{k}$ and $E_{V}^{k}$ are intrinsic energy level of the conduction band and valence band, respectively.

The formation energy is used to evaluate the feasibility of doped process, which can be calculated by using the following equation [40]:

$$E_{\text{form}} = E(\text{doped})-E(\text{pristine})-\mu_{\text{X}}+\mu_{\text{Zn}} \tag{3}$$

Where $E(\text{doped})$ and $E(\text{pristine})$ are the total energies of the doped and undoped ZnS, respectively. $\mu_{\text{X}}$ represents the chemical potential of the dopants element, while $\mu_{\text{Zn}}$ stands for the chemical potential of Zn. A smaller formation energy implies more feasible conditions to realize the doped process in energy. The calculated formation energies for all the doped structures are presented in Table 1. The results indicate that the formation energies of the doped structures become higher from C to Sn although those of Pb@ZnS and Sn@ZnS are equal. Interestingly, the formation energies for all the doped structures are negative, which means that the reaction process is exothermic and the preparation of these doped structures are no difficulty in energy.

### 3.2 Hydrogen production

The photoelectrochemical (PEC) water splitting using a single-crystal semiconductor is well-known as the Honda-Fujishima effect [41]. When the energy of the irradiation is equivalent to or greater than the band energy gap of the semiconductor photocatalyst, electrons in the valence band (VB) are excited into the conduction band (CB), resulting holes in the VB. These photogenerated electrons and holes can result in reduction and oxidation reactions, respectively. To achieve overall water splitting, the bottom of the CBM must be more negative than the reduction

potential of $H^{+}$ to $H_{2}$ supposing 0 V vs. normal hydrogen electrode (NHE) at pH 0, while the top of the VB must be more positive than the oxidation potential of $H_{2}O$ to $O_{2}$ (1.23 V vs. NHE) [42]. Therefore, the minimum photon energy thermodynamically required to drive the reaction is 1.23 eV. The band energy gap of each doped structure was calculated. As shown in Table 1, the calculated energy band gaps of all these structures are larger than the necessary 1.23 eV although those of the doped structures are obviously decreased. Just as mentioned above, the positions of VBM and CBM relative to the normal hydrogen electrode (NHE) potential are also very important for photocatalytic water splitting. The oxidation potential of $O_{2}/H_{2}O$ and the reduction potential of $H^{+}/H_{2}$ are -5.67 eV and -4.44 eV, respectively [43].

The VBM and CBM of the present structures are calculated from the following formulas [44]:

$$
E_{\mathrm{VBM}}=\chi-\frac{1}{2}E_{\mathrm{g}} \tag{4}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{CBM}}=\chi+\frac{1}{2}E_{\mathrm{g}} \tag{5}
$$

Where $\chi$ is the Mulliken electronegativity of the compound, $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ is the energy band gap of the structure. The calculated VBM and CBM positions relative to the NHE potential based on equation (4) and equation (5) are plotted in Fig. 2. For the pristine ZnS, the calculated energy band gap is 3.57 eV, which is in great agreement with the experimental value [19, 45]. The results in Fig. 2 show that the VBM of the pristine ZnS is about -7.05 eV, which is 1.38 eV lower than the oxidation potential, while the CBM of the pristine ZnS is about -3.48 eV, which is 0.96 eV higher than the reduction potential. Obviously, both the levels of VBM and CBM satisfy the

requirement of the reaction for water splitting.

![](./images/813094173711269888_5.jpg)

Fig. 2. The VBM and CBM energy levels of the pristine and doped ZnS relative to the NHE potential.

Compared with the pristine ZnS, the VBM of the doped structures are raised about 0.66 eV, 0.93 eV, 0.68 eV, 0.79 eV and 0.5 eV, respectively, while the CBM of these structures are lowered about 0.72 eV, 0.94 eV, 0.67 eV, 0.79 eV and 0.48 eV, respectively. Fortunately, all the VBMs and CBMs remain the requirement of the water splitting reaction. Therefore, all of them can be used as the candidates to catalyze the water splitting. The electronic band edge positions indicate the oxidation ability and reduction ability of these structures. A higher value of potential is corresponding to stronger reduction ability, while a lower value of potential is corresponding to stronger oxidation ability. In other words, among these doped structures, Si@Zn has the lowest reducibility to produce $\ce{H_{2}}$ and the lowest oxidability to produce $\ce{O_{2}}$, while opposite to the Pb@Zn. Fortunately, the energy band gap of C@Zn, Si@Zn, Ge@Zn, Sn@Zn, and Pb@Zn is range from 1.70 to 2.59 eV, which means that all of the structures are a response to the visible light. Additionally, the calculated band gaps for C and Pb doped ZnS are about 0.30 eV bigger than the experimental one, which implies that the meta-GGA+MBJ potential can give

acceptable results of the band gap.

### 3.3 Dielectric function and optical properties

In order to calculate the optical absorption coefficient of ZnS, we calculated the complex dielectric function firstly. The results of the imaginary parts $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ of the dielectric function for the pristine and the doped ZnS are plotted in Fig. 3. One can find that the $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ of the pristine ZnS is very small in the low energy region. For the Pb@Zn, the value of its $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ is also close to zero in the low energy region, but a sharp peak has been found at about 3.6 eV. For the other structures, a distinct peak has also been observed although it is in the lower energy range.

![](./images/813094173711269888_6.jpg)

Fig. 3. The imaginary part of the dielectric function of the pristine and doped ZnS structures.

Based on the complex dielectric function, the optical absorption coefficient can be calculated by using the following expression [46]:

$$
\alpha(\omega)=2 \omega\left(\frac{\left[\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}-\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{6}
$$

where $\alpha$ is the optical absorption coefficient. $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ is the real part of the complex

dielectric function. The results are presented in Fig. 4.

![](./images/813094173711269888_7.jpg)

Fig. 4. The absorption coefficient of the pristine and doped ZnS in energy.

For the pristine ZnS, there is no significant absorption in the low energy region, which is in good agreement with other works [47, 48]. But for Pb@Zn, although the absorption is not changed in the low energy region, it is enhanced in the near ultraviolet region significantly. However, the absorption coefficient of the other four doped structures changes significantly in the visible light region. The mono-doped of Sn and Ge enhance the absorption significantly in the purple light region, while the mono-doped of C and Si result in a significant enhancement of absorption coefficient in the green light region. Especially, the enhancement of absorption for C@Zn and Si@Zn are more obvious.

### 3.4 Mechanism of the enhancement of the photocatalytic effect

The total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) of the pristine and the doped ZnS have been plotted in the Fig. 5 to understand the effects of the dopants on the optical properties of ZnS [49]. From Fig. 5(a), we can find that the composition of the valence band maximum (VBM) is dominated by S $3p$ and Zn $6s$

states, including a small number of the 4s, 3p states of Zn, while the conduction band minimum (CBM) is dominated by S 3s and Zn 4s states. For the doped structures, two significant changes can be observed from Fig 5(b)-(f). For all the doped structures, a new additional donor energy level comprising of $s$ state of the dopant has been found in the band gap for all the doped structures, which is accord with the case of the Pb-doped ZnS [30, 50]. The energy levels are obviously located over the original VBM of the pristine ZnS, which result in the new VBMs with higher energy. As a result, the band energy gap decreases. On the other hand, the $p$ state of the doped atom obviously contributes to the CB but over the original CBM of the pristine ZnS, which has not change the energy level of CBM. Therefore, the CBM lift resulting from the dopant is responsible for the decrease of the energy band gap. Moreover, the dopants also result in an obvious peak in the CBM, which lead to a significant absorption enhancement. In other words, the $s$ and $p$ states of the dopants induce the great changes of the CBM and VBM, although the dopants have not resulted in an obvious change in the geometrical structure of ZnS. In addition, from Table 1, one can find that the band gap of the doped structures shows no single tendency, which can be understood from the random tendency of the energy gaps between the electronic density of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMO). Among these five elements, the energy gap between HOMO and LUMO of Pb is the largest and that of Si is the smallest, which is in concord with the tendency of the energy band gaps for the doped structures.

## 4.CONCLUSION

In summary, we have investigated the effects of photocatalytic water splitting for carbon elements doping ZnS (C@Zn, Si@Zn, Ge@Zn, Sn@Zn, and Pb@Zn) using the first-principles DFT with the meta-GGA+MBJ potential. The results of the electronics properties demonstrate that the band energy gaps of all the doped structures decrease. However, the band energy gaps and levels of the VBM and CBM still satisfy the requirement of water splitting. Therefore, as the pristine ZnS, all the considered structures can be used for water splitting. Moreover, the calculational

absorption coefficients show a redshift for all the doped structures in comparison with that of pristine ZnS. Especially, the obvious absorption enhancement in the range of the visible light energy is observed for C@Zn, which implies that this structure is a promising candidate to solar energy driven water splitting material.
