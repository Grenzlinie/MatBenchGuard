# ARTICLE

## A Promising Photocatalyst of Water Splitting Reaction with Stable Sandwiched $P_4O_2$/Black Phosphorus Heterostructure and High Solar-to-Hydrogen Efficiency

Baichuan Lu, $^{a}$ Xiaoyan Zheng $^{*a}$ and Zesheng Li $^{*a}$

Black phosphorus (BP) is a promising two-dimensional (2D) semiconductor, because of its tunable band gap and high hole mobility, however, its easy degradation under atmospheric condition largely limits its application in photocatalytic water-splitting reaction. To overcome this disadvantage, we proposed a strategy for designing sandwiched $P_4O_2$-encapsulated BP ($P_4O_2$/BP) 2D materials, considering the automatically formation and high stability of $P_4O_2$ in air. We systematically considered five different packing models involving twenty sandwiched $P_4O_2$/BP systems by first-principle calculations. Through triple processes screening from 20 sandwiched $P_4O_2$/BP systems, we found that the O-1-P system bearing intrinsic electric field ingeniously combines all desired features for photocatalytic water-splitting reactions, including the small direct band gap (1.34 eV), low exciton binding energy, high hole mobility and ultrahigh solar-to-hydrogen efficiency as high as 22.77 %. Through Gibbs free energy calculations, the active sites and possible reaction pathways of full water splitting reactions were also confirmed. Our work offers useful guidance for design and fabricating stable 2D materials with high performance on photocatalytic water-splitting application.

Solar-driven photocatalytic water splitting to produce hydrogen resources is urgent due to the world-wide energy shortage and environment pollution. $^{1}$ Material for photocatalytic water splitting needs enough kinetic overpotentials to overcome the barriers of hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER), but the large band gap makes its absorbance of long-wavelength photons in sunlight uneffectively. $^{2}$ The solar-to-hydrogen (STH) efficiency could balance the effects of both kinetic overpotential and light absorption. It can be taken as an important indicator for judging the performance of water-splitting photocatalysts. However, the limitations of light absorption and quantum efficiency, as well as the energy loss during electron transport, make the theoretical limit of STH efficiency approximately 18\%. $^{3}$ For economical production of hydrogen from photocatalytic water splitting, the STH efficiency is required to be at least above 10\%. $^{4}$ In recent years, although great efforts are devoted to enhancing the STH efficiency by improving the typical three steps (light absorption, carrier separation and charge transport, as well as HER and OER) of photocatalytic water splitting process, the efficiency is still hard to exceed 10\%. $^{1,5}$

Since the novel electronic and structural properties, 2D materials have been considered as an ideal candidate for photocatalytic water splitting at the nanoscale, such as g-$C_3N_4$ and transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs) $^{6,7}$. However, the large band gap (2.0-3.0 eV) and low carrier mobility significantly limit their efficiency. $^{6,7}$ Black phosphorus (BP) is a fascinating 2D material, it exhibits thickness-tunable direct band gap from 0.3 eV (bulk) to 1.58 eV (monolayer) and bears high hole mobility with obvious anisotropy, $^{8,9}$ but it cannot be used as a photolytic water material, because of its easy degradation in aerobic and watery environment $^{10}$ and too high conduction band minimum (CBM) position for OER. $^{11}$ To avoid the degradation of BP under ambient conditions, few-layer BP are encapsulated by highly stable 2D material (such as the hex-agonal boron nitride) $^{12}$ and the encapsulation keeps the carrier mobility of BP in vacuum without degradation. $^{13}$ To adjust the band gap of BP, the mechanical strain, electrical bias, pH, and defect or dopant doping are used in experiments. $^{14-18}$ All these protocols successfully extend the application of BP in photocatalytic water splitting reaction, they may require artificial synthesis and complicated device fabrication.

As reported by Edmonds et al. a certain amount of single-layer phosphorus oxide ($P_2O_5$ and $P_4O_2$) can generate at the topmost layer of BP with high stability in the air. $^{19}$ Wang et al. proposed that $P_2O_5$ or $P_4O_2$ layer is stable in aerobic and watery environment, as a native cap of BP, it could be taken as a feasible protection layer for BP. $^{20}$ However, the band gap of $P_2O_5$ (~7 eV) is too large to be a photocatalyst in water-splitting reaction. $^{20}$ In our previous work, few-layer $P_4O_2$ is proved to be an potential photocatalyst in water-splitting reaction, because of its tunable band gap, extremely high carrier mobility and great stability in aerobic and watery environment. $^{21}$ In this work, we encapsulate BP by $P_4O_2$, which automatically generated at the top of BP, to design sandwiched $P_4O_2$/BP

Taking P₄O₂ as the protection layer, the sandwiched P₄O₂/BP materials with five different packing manners were presented systematically by first principle calculation to demonstrate the structure-property relationships and to explore novel potential 2D materials with superior photocatalytic water splitting performance and great stability in aerobic and watery environments. In packing models design, three key factors are considered. First, the P₄O₂ monolayer has two different surfaces: oxygen-rich and phosphorus-rich surfaces (Figure 1e). Second, the layer number of BP can change, because of its thickness-tunable band gap and charge carrier mobility. Third, in some case, the presence of the substrate assists in protecting BP from the air. We assume that the substrate does not affect the structure and properties of BP. We set up five different packing models of sandwiched P₄O₂/BP materials. (1) In the first and second packing models, BP are sandwiched by P₄O₂ monolayer and substrate simultaneously. The one with oxygen-rich surface of P₄O₂ exposed in the air is referred as **O-n** (Figure 1a and Figure S1a), while the other with phosphorus-rich surface outside termed as **P-n** (Figure 1b and Figure S1b). Here, **n** is the layer number of sandwiched BP, see Figure 1f (n = 1-4). (2) In the third and fourth packing models, few-layer BP is protected by monolayer P₄O₂ with oxygen-rich (phosphorus-rich) surface on both sides, termed as **O-n-O** (**P-n-P**), see Figure 1c-d and Figure S1c-d, respectively. The last packing model is few-layer BP sandwiched by monolayer P₄O₂ with different surfaces exposed in the air, named as **O-n-P** in Figure 1e and Figure S1e. In total, there are 20 combinations involved in the five packing models.

The structural optimization of sandwiched P₄O₂/BP systems were performed by using Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhoff (PBE) functional²² with van der Waals correction (D3)²², ²³, which can well reproduce the structural parameters of BP and P₄O₂ monolayers (Table S1). We construct 2 × 2 supercell of standard P₄O₂/BP to minimize the edge effect. The optimized structure of representative sandwiched P₄O₂/BP configuration of each packing model was depicted in Figure 1 and Figure S1, respectively. As shown in Table S1, as the layer number n of BP increases, the lattice constant $a$ of the optimized structures in all five packing models decreased, while the lattice constant $b$ are almost constant (3.32 Å). Thus, as the layer number of BP increases, the puckering along the armchair direction (X direction) shrinking. It should be noted that the lattice constant $a$ of all packing models is in range 4.59-5.11 Å, that is just inbetween the corresponding lattice constants $a$ of monolayer P₄O₂ (5.19 Å) and monolayer BP (4.58 Å).

According to the requirements of photocatalysts in water-splitting reaction, we resort to triple screening process by three factors: band gap (primary screening), exciton binding energy (secondary screening) and energy level match (final screening) successively, to identify the potentially excellent materials for photocatalysis. First, the band gap needs to sit in the range of 0.8-2.0 eV by considering the light absorption efficiency.²⁴ Because as reported by Yang's group,³ the existing intrinsic electric field (IEF) in 2D materials can break the limitation of light absorption efficiency and overpotential on the bandgap requirement of traditional water-splitting photocatalyst (1.23 eV) and improve the STH efficiency. Second, the exciton binding energy ($E_b$) should be in between 0.025 eV and 0.48 eV to facilitate carrier dissociation, because small $E_b$ could promote the carrier separation during fast photon radiation. To prevent a charge carriers free from the Coulomb attraction of an opposite charge, $E_b$ needs to be higher than 0.025eV.²⁵ And to discover more efficiency material than BP, $E_b$ should lower than that of monolayer BP (0.48 eV).¹¹ Finally, the band edge positions must straddle over the water redox potentials in the water-splitting reaction.

The band gaps of sandwiched P₄O₂/BP materials calculated by Heyd-Scuseria-Ernzerhof (HSE06) functional²⁶, ²⁷ based on the optimized structures are listed in Figure 2a and Table S1. It indicates that as the layer number n increases from 1 to 4, the band gap of each system in all five packing models decreases obviously. For example, the band gap of **O-n** decreases from 1.86 to 0.94 eV, when $n$ goes from 1 to 4. In order to ensure efficient light absorption, the band gap should be controlled within the range of 0.8 to 2.0 eV. It is clear that the band gaps of systems in **O-n** or **O-n-O** (n=1-4) packing model satisfy the standard requirement and can be kept for the secondary screen.

The **P-1-P** contain only phosphorus-rich surface, the CBM locate above the reduction potential of $H^+/H_2$ and VBM sit above the $O_2/H_2O$ oxidation potential. Thus, they are not proper to be used as candidates for photocatalytic water splitting. **O-1-P** has two different surfaces and exists IEF, thus it bears two different vacuum levels relative to oxygen-rich and phosphorus-rich surfaces, respectively (Figure 2d). As shown in Figure 2c, referring to the vacuum level of the oxygen-rich surface, the CBM of **O-1-P** is much lower than the reduction potential of $H^+/H_2$ (-4.44 eV), while relative to the vacuum level of the phosphorus-rich surface, the VBM of **O-1-P** is much higher than the $O_2/H_2O$ oxidation potential (-5.67 eV). That is, considering either oxygen-rich or phosphorus-rich surface separately, **O-1-P** could not satisfy the requirement of the energy level for water-splitting reaction. However, combining both surfaces, the band edge position of **O-1-P** can straddle over the water redox potentials. The IEF of **O-1-P** leads to large vacuum level difference ($\Delta\Phi = 2.55$ eV) on two respective surfaces (Figure 2d), accordingly resulting in the obvious offset of the redox potentials of $H^+/H_2$ and $H_2O/O_2$ between the two respective surfaces. Therefore, **O-1-P** meets the basic requirements of photocatalytic water splitting and may serve as an excellent candidate for photocatalytic water-splitting reaction. Similar to **O-1-P**, the band edge positions of **O-2-P** also satisfy the requirement of photocatalyst material of water-splitting (see Figure S4).

To check the performance of **O-1-P** system as photocatalyst of water-splitting reaction, we further investigate its electronic properties. As shown in Figure 3a, **O-1-P** has direct band gap (1.34 eV), both CBM and VBM locate at the same high symmetry $\Gamma$ point. Through composition analysis of band edges by computing the partial density of states (PDOS), we found that the phosphorus-rich $P_4O_2$ contributes to the CBM, while VBM is mainly contributed by both BP and oxygen-rich $P_4O_2$. For a more intuitive analysis, the spatial distributions of VBM and CBM for **O-1-P** are shown in Figure 3b. Apparently, the wavefunction of VBM and CBM are isolated on separated positions of **O-1-P**, consistent with the PDOS result. This indicates the efficient electron-hole separation in **O-1-P** system, because the photogenerated electrons and holes scattered in different domains of one material benefits to decreasing their recombination probability. In addition, **O-1-P** also exhibits excellent absorption in the whole visible spectrum range (Figure 3c), it exhibits perfect performance on absorption of sunlight, which is key for the efficiency of photocatalytic water-splitting reaction. The carrier mobility acts as a parameter for quantitative evaluation on the ability in transferring electron and hole along specific directions of the water-splitting photocatalyst. Based on the deformation potential theory (see details in ESI), the carrier mobilities ($\mu$) of both holes ($h$) and electrons ($e$) along X and Y directions for **O-1-P**, **O-n** and **O-n-O** (n=1-4) at room temperature were calculated and summarized in Figure 3d and Table S4 in ESI. The carrier mobilities of few-layer BP were also included in Figure 3d for comparison. It is clear that the carrier mobility $\mu_h$ and $\mu_e$ of **O-1-P** along Y direction are large (18320 and $5430$ cm$^2$ V$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$), and shows obvious anisotropic. The huge difference in $\mu_h$ and $\mu_e$ facilitates the effective separation of electron-hole pairs and reduces the probability of recombination of electron and hole in photocatalytic water splitting reaction. Excitingly, the carrier mobility of the **O-1-P** system is superior to these of few-layer BP, because of its significantly larger elastic modulus ($C_{2D}$) along X and Y directions in $P_4O_2$ than that of BP (see Table S4 in ESI).

According to the charge distribution profiles (Figure 3b), the VBM of **O-1-P** mainly comes from the oxygen-rich surface, while the CBM is completely contributed by the phosphorus-rich surface, therefore we identify the active sites ($\alpha$-site and $\beta$-site) for OER and HER on the oxygen-rich surface (see Figure S6) and phosphorus-rich surface (see Figure S7), respectively. In water

splitting reactions, OER is the most challenging process because the formation of single $O_2$ involves the transfer of four electrons and four protons simultaneously, it requires a large overpotential.³⁰,³¹ The OER process can be considered as a four-electron transfer process, corresponding to the deprotonation of water molecules with several intermediates, such as *OH, *O and *OOH.³² While HER could be regards as two one-electron transfer process driven by photo-generated electrons, involving the formation of *H intermediate at the phosphorus-rich surface of **O-1-P** (more details in Computational methods section of ESI). In order to accurately identify and confirm the active sites with possible reaction pathways for HER and OER, the Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta$G) of two series reactions involving above mentioned intermediates are calculated with and without considering the external potentials. It needs to be mentioned that under the external potentials (U = 1.07 eV and U = 2.82 eV for photogenerated electrons and holes, respectively), $\Delta$G of the intermediates at various active sites are applied to those charge-related steps to identify active site with possible reaction pathway. The calculated $\Delta$G for all cases are summarized in Figure 4a-4b and Table S6. The results indicate that: (1) the OER at $\beta$-site is energetically spontaneous process under the visible light irradiation with negative $\Delta$G for all steps (Figure 4a, light blue line); (2) the third step of OER at $\alpha$-site is endothermic (Figure 4a, orange line), this process may be feasible since the positive $\Delta$G3 (0.33 eV) between *O and *OOH is not so large, the free energy may be overcome by the thermal fluctuation at the room temperature; (3) in the case of the HER, $\beta$-site in **O-1-P** could possibly serve as the active sites because it exhibits the favorable $\Delta$G (Figure 4b, light blue line), while $\alpha$-site could potentially serve as the active site due to the small positive $\Delta$G5 (0.04 eV) of *H. These results unambiguously prove that **O-1-P** indeed able to achieve overall photocatalytic water splitting reactions at favorable active sites for both OER and HER.

To systematically consider the photocatalytic water splitting performance, we further calculate the STH efficiency of **O-1-P**. Excitingly, the calculated STH efficiency of **O-1-P** can reaches up to 22.77 % (Table S5, see details in ESI), which breaks the conventional theoretical efficiency limit of ~18%.³ In the traditional theory of photocatalytic water splitting, the band gap has an irreconcilable contradiction with the overpotential, but when the 2D material has IEF, the band edge position is obviously shifted. This not only satisfies the band edge position of the photocatalytic water splitting but also increases the overpotential, and the inherent small band gap is not affected to ensure the light absorption, so the STH efficiency will break the traditional theoretical limit. For **O-2-P** system, although its band gap (1.09 eV) is smaller than **O-1-P** (1.34 eV), its STH efficiency (21.42%) is lower than **O-1-P**, due to its large IEF ($\triangle \Phi$ = 3.49 eV) (Figure S4 and Table S5). Therefore, **O-1-P** ingeniously combining all desired optoelectronic features, including direct band gap (1.34 eV), excellent absorption in the whole visible spectrum range of sunlight, high hole mobility of 18320 cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹ and high STH efficiency (22.77%), it should be an ideal candidate for photocatalytic water splitting.

The stability of **O-1-P** is crucial to realize experimental fabrication of specific optoelectronics. We performed a series of ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations for **O-1-P** to demonstrate the stability of them at different environments, such as in vacuum, in aqueous solution and in aerobic and watery environment, respectively. First, 3 × 3 supercell was setup in vacuum and 10 ps AIMD simulation was run at 300 K for **O-1-P**. The evolution of potential energy as a function of time and the snapshot of last frame were shown in Figure S2. It is obvious that the total energy of each system oscillates in a narrow range (within 0.02 eV) and the corresponding atoms of **O-1-P** only slightly vibrate around their equilibrium positions (Figure S2a). Second, we simulated the stability of 3 × 3 supercell O-1-P in aqueous solution (Figure S2b) and in aerobic

and watery environment (Figure S2c), respectively. After 10 ps AIMD simulations at 300 K, we found O-1-P is structurally intact in aqueous solution and in aerobic and watery environments, and the energy oscillation amplitude are within 0.02 eV and 0.04 eV, respectively. Due to the high stability of O-1-P, $P_4O_2$ should be great candidate as the protective layer to prevent internal BP from being degraded by $H_2O$ and $O_2$.

## Conclusions
In summary, we designed and investigated the detailed structural and optoelectronic properties of $P_4O_2$/BP by first-principle calculations and explored promising 2D semiconductors with excellent photocatalytic water-splitting potential. Through triple processes screening from 20 $P_4O_2$/BP systems, we found that the O-1-P system bearing IEF ingeniously combines all desired features for photocatalytic water-splitting reactions, including the small direct band gap (1.34 eV), perfect band edge position, low exciton binding energy, small photogenerated carrier recombination possibilities, ultrahigh STH efficiency as high as 22.77 %. Through Gibbs free energy calculations, the active sites and possible reaction pathways of both OER and HER were confirmed under visible light irradiation. Finally, O-1-P can be automatically produced by three layers BP in the air, with excellent stability in aerobic and watery environment. Therefore, it should be a promising candidate for photocatalytic water-splitting reaction. We are quite optimistic about the experimental realization of such compound in the near future.
