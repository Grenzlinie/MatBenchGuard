# DFT study on the adsorption of NO on iron tape-porphyrin

Tien Quang Nguyen, Mary Clare Sison Escaño, Nobuaki Shimoji, Hiroshi Nakanishi and Hideaki Kasai*

We have investigated the electronic properties of iron tape-porphyrin and its complex with NO. We found that at the stable position, Fe-N-O angle is about $148^\circ$. As regards the electronic properties, there appears an energy gap between conduction band and valence band. We attribute these changes in electronic properties to the strong interaction involving the $d_{xz}$, $d_{yz}$ and $d_{zz}$ orbitals of the Fe atoms and the $\pi^*$ orbitals of NO molecule. ## Introduction
Since the proposal of molecular electronic devices, $^{[1,2]}$ many kinds of organic functional molecules have been extensively studied both theoretically and experimentally. Among the various types of functional molecules, the molecular wires play an important role. Most of the molecular wires are oligomers whose structural units are composed of a few building blocks, such as thiophene, porphyrin, and so on. $^{[3]}$ The porphyrin is one of the building blocks from which several types of differently conjugated porphyrin molecular wires have been prepared. $^{[4]}$ In particular, tape-shaped porphyrin molecules, where the adjacent building blocks (referred to as porphyrin macrocycles) are linked via three conjugating C-C bonds (see Fig. 1), have attracted much attention due to their extremely small HOMO-LUMO energy gaps. $^{[4]}$ Therefore, the tape porphyrin molecules are expected to be useful as good conducting molecular wires. In fact, the porphyrin wires are considered to be useful as parts in molecular devices, but there have been very few studies dealing with the detailed electronic properties of these molecules and on the interaction between wires and other complexes.

In this work, we present detailed theoretically study on the electronic properties of the iron tape-porphyrin and its complex with NO. Here, we discuss the optimized geometric structures and changes in electronic structures of FeTP before and after binding to diatomic molecules.

## Calculation Method
All calculations were performed by using the VASP program. $^{[5,6]}$ Nonlocal correction in the form of generalized gradient approxima tion (GGA) $^{[7]}$ was included for the exchange-correlation function. The calculations used Projector Augmented-Wave $^{[8]}$ potential to describe the electron-ion interaction. A grid of $30\times1\times1$ Monkhorst-Pack special k-points is used, $^{[9]}$ which was found to sufficiently account for the Brillouin-zone integration. In order to avoid interactions between the tape-porphyrin in the cell with those in the neighboring cells, we introduce a vacuum region of about $8\,\mathring{A}$ for the y-axis and z-axis directions. Here, the tape lies along x-axis direction as shown in Fig. 1. All the calculations are converged with the atomic force less than $0.05\,\text{eV}/\mathring{A}$.

## Results and Discussions
The electronic band structures of FeTP are shown in the Fig. 2(a) and (b), for majority-spin and minority-spin electrons, respectively. It is clear that, in the majority-spin electrons band, the Fermi-level crosses the band-line suggesting a metallic property of FeTP. Also the charge density distributions on the energy bands near the Fermi-level are shown. We note a high dispersion on these bands, which is caused by the high charge density distribution on $d_{yz}$ orbital of Fe atom and $p_{z}$ orbital of the meso-C and $\beta$-C atoms. The calculations on electronic properties of tape-porphyrin are in good agreement with that of previous study. $^{[10]}$

Next, the interaction between NO and the tape-porphyrin is presented. In this section, we set some initial adsorption sites on top of FeTP to obtain the most stable FeTP-NO geometric configuration. After optimizing structures, the NO has an end-on configuration with FeTP. The direction of Fe-N bond is perpendicular to porphyrin plane. The Fe-N-O angle is about $148^\circ$, which is in good agreement with experimental results $^{[11]}$ and with density functional theory (DFT) calculation for porphyrin. $^{[12]}$ Here, we emphasize that so far no previous studies on geometric structure for diatomic molecules adsorbed on iron tape-porphyrin were done. We also note that the NO molecule binds to FeTP with an end-on configuration because of the hybridization of $\pi^*$ of NO and $d$ orbitals of Fe atom. ## Conclusions

In conclusion, we have used DFT method to investigate structural and electronic properties of the iron tape-porphyrin and its interaction with NO. We found that the FeTP is metallic, and the adsorption of NO on the FeTP induces significant changes in the geometric structure and the electronic properties of FeTP-NO. We note a metal-insulator transition. The electronic phase transition are caused by the strong interaction between $d_{zz}$, $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ orbitals of the Fe atom and $\pi^{*}$ orbitals of NO. The results of this work have shown that the iron tape-porphyrin shows potential for molecular device applications - either as sensor for detecting NO.
