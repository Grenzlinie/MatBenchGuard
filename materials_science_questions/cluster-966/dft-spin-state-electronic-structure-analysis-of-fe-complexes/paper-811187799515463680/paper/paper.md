# THEORETICAL STUDY OF THE BONDING IN Fe-M DIMERS

ELISHEVA GOLDSTEIN*, CYNTHIA FLORES and Y. P. HSIA

Department of Chemistry, California Polytechnic State University, Pomona, CA 91768
(U.S.A.)

(Received 30 September 1984)

## ABSTRACT

The electronic structures of $\text{Fe}_2$, FeMn, FeCo, FeCu and FeNi have been calculated using Gaussian-76 SCF techniques. Gaussian basis sets contracted to [4s, 2p, 1d] functions are used on each atom resulting in sets of near double zeta accuracy in the valence region. The calculated UHF electronic ground state configurations for these Fe-M dimers are comparable with those experimentally determined. By examining the Mulliken atomic overlap population analysis, a correlation is found between the Mössbauer spectrum and calculated binding strengths in these dimers. Our calculations, fixed at $r_{\text{e}}$ of $2.3\ \text{\AA}$, show that the binding strengths decrease from FeCu, FeMn, $\text{Fe}_2$, FeCo, FeNi.

## INTRODUCTION

In recent years, a large number of experimental [1-6] as well as theoretical [7-16] studies of homonuclear and heteronuclear transition metal dimers have been undertaken. The detailed studies of the electronic structures of small metal particles are finally within the domain of quantum chemical techniques, which are able to elucidate problems encountered by experimentalists in the fundamental areas of: catalysis [17], solid state physics [18], laser [19, 20] and photochemical behavior in polynuclear systems [20-22].

Although a large number of experimental studies have been carried out in this area, the molecular properties of the third-row diatomic Fe-M dimers where M includes Cu, CO, Mn, Fe and Ni are uncertain. Spectroscopic constants such as $r_{\text{e}}$, $\omega_{\text{e}}$, as well as dissociation energies and ionization potentials remain a mystery. Additionally, the multiplicities and symmetry type of the electronic ground states of these heteronuclear dimers as well as their relative binding strengths remain unknown. It is our objective to clarify the latter only and tabulate the trends in metal-metal bonding.

Using solid noble-gas matrices, investigators have studied the optical spectra of some first row transition metal dimers [23] as well as the Mössbauer [4] spectrum for Fe-M dimers. It is obvious, however, especially in light of the

The most important recent experimental work carried out by Montano [4] involves a Mössbauer study of the Fe-M dimers in a solid matrix. He determines the ground electronic states of the isolated species from the spec- trum as well as the relative bond strength of each dimer. His most conclusive results show that the isomer shift for FeNi is closest to the iron monomer, thus denoting the weakest bond, while FeCo possesses the most positive isomer shift closer to the $3d^{7}4s^{1}$ configuration at the Fe atom. The experi- mental bond trend derived from the Mössbauer isomer shift, in decreasing strength is $\mathrm{FeCo} \simeq \mathrm{FeCu}>\mathrm{FeMn}>\mathrm{Fe}_{2}>\mathrm{FeNi}$. Another interesting result of the Mössbauer study is that for an odd total number of electrons the isomer shift is more positive (indicating a stronger bond) than in the species with an even number of electrons. Furthermore, by measuring the magnetic hyperfine field strength and the magnetic field gradient at the nucleus, Montano assigned multiplicities and symmetries to the ground states of these dimers. His assignment is based on the fact that the electric field gradient takes on three different values for the five $d$ orbitals ranging from sigma and pi to delta.

Previous theoretical studies [8, 10, 11] have shown that the determination of ground state electronic structure in third row transition metals requires extensive electron correlation techniques even at the equilibrium bond distance. However, most of the ab-initio techniques have concentrated their attention on $\mathrm{Ni}_{2}$, $\mathrm{Cu}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Ag}$ clusters, whereas there has been little investiga- tion into the mixed dimers. One of these theoretical investigations [7] of the Fe-M dimers attempts to correct for lack of electron correlation techniques by employing an $X\alpha$ potential to represent exchange interactions. Their results are summarized in Table 1. In another theoretical study, Wolf and Schmidtke [9] looked at homonuclear dimers using RHF techniques. Due to their restricted calculations they found the $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}$ ground state to be $^{1}\Sigma_{g}^{+}$with the configuration:

$7\sigma_{g}^{2}5\sigma_{u}3\pi_{u}^{4}1\delta_{g}^{4}1\delta_{u}^{4}.$

They also report a bond distance of $1.58\ \mathring{A}$ and an ionization potential of 5.88 eV. Anderson [12], using EHMO calculations found the $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}$ bond distance to be $2.40\ \mathring{A}$.

Shim [10, 11], in the most extensive theoretical work done thus far, has included both $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{FeNi}$ in her calculations. She found, at the CI level, that the density of states at the $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}$ ground state is as high as 112, reflecting the interaction between the Fe atoms. The value of $R_{\mathrm{e}}$ was found to be $2.40\ \mathring{A}$ which differs significantly from the experimental value $(1.87\ \mathring{A})$. Theoretically, from her work the nature of the iron-iron bond appears to be mostly of $4s\sigma$ character with each $d$-orbital localized on the Fe atoms without significant interaction. ## DESCRIPTION OF THE CALCULATIONS

The $(12s, 6p, 4d)$ primitive Gaussian functions of Tatewaki and Huzinaga [25] were contracted to form the basis $[4s, 2p, 1d]$ used in this work. All calculations utilized the UHF segment of the Gaussian 76 program [26] with an equilibrium bond distance fixed at $2.3\ \text{\AA}$ for all $\text{Fe--M}$ species. The longer $r_{\text{e}}$ value was chosen for two basic reasons: First, the preliminary calculations using the extensive basis set of Roos et al. [27], augmented with Hay's diffuse $d$ functions [28], showed an unusually small bond order for all $\text{Fe--M}$ species. This suggests that essentially unbound species exist at the smaller bond distance of $1.87\ \text{\AA}$ and therefore casts some doubt on the metal--metal bond distance in the solid inert-gas matrix [29]. Second, there is much evidence [30] that the $\text{Fe--M}$ distance in the gas phase as well as in polynuclear centers exceeds $2\ \text{\AA}$. Since it is hoped to make calculations of oxyhemerythrin [31], $\text{M}_{2}\text{O}_{2}$, $2.3\ \text{\AA}$ was chosen as a representative bond distance.

Some difficulty was experienced in matching Huzinaga's [25] reported orbital energies with the UHF Gaussian 76 calculations. However, since the objective was to compare relative $4s$--$4s$ and $3d$--$3d$ bonding trends it was felt that the results were quite meaningful in a relative rather than absolute sense.

All spin multiplicities were adopted from experimental parameters as shown in Table 1, and no attempt was made to elucidate the exact nature of the hundreds of possible states near the dimers' ground states.

The Mössbauer Isomer Shift ($IS$) can be related to the $s$-electron density at the nucleus by:
$$
IS=(2/3)\pi Ze^{2}\Delta\langle r^{2}\rangle\left[|\psi_{\text{s}}(0)|_{\text{A}}^{2}-|\psi_{\text{s}}|0|_{\text{s}}^{2}\right] \tag{1}
$$
where $\Delta\langle r^{2}\rangle$ is the difference in the expectation value of the square of the nuclear radius between the excited and ground state; $|\psi_{\text{s}}(0)|_{\text{A}}^{2}$ and $|\psi_{\text{s}}(0)|_{\text{s}}^{2}$ are the squares of the absolute values of the $s$-electronic densities at the nucleus for the absorber and source, respectively; $e$ is the elementary charge, and $Z$ is the atomic number. Since $\Delta\langle r^{2}\rangle$ is a negative quantity, a greater negative isomer shift means a higher electronic density at the absorbing nucleus.

The results were correlated to the Mössbauer data using the first order density matrix which is given by Pople [26] as:
$$
P_{\mu\nu}=2\sum_{i}^{occ}C_{\mu i}C_{\nu i} \tag{2}
$$
and the Mulliken gross population of the atomic orbital is given by:
$$
Q_{\mu\nu}=P_{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu} \tag{3}
$$

The quantity $|\psi_{s}(0)|_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}$ in eqn. (1), will be reduced by the $s$-electron bonding and reduced again when $d$-electrons retained by the Fe nucleus shield the $s$-electrons from the nucleus. Thus the Mössbauer isomer shift can be directly linked to the $s$-bonding and $d$-shielding of the $^{57}\mathrm{Fe}$ nucleus and these quantities can be evaluated from the Mulliken population analysis. To calculate the Mulliken Population of the overlap of orbitals $\phi_{\mu}$ on atom $\mathrm{A}$ and $\phi_{\nu}$ on atom $\mathrm{B}$ (where $\mathrm{A}$ could equal $\mathrm{B}$, and $\nu$ can equal $\mu$) the $s$-bonding is the overlap of A's $s$ atomic orbitals with B's, and is given by:

$$
\mathrm{A}-s \times \mathrm{B}-s=\sum_{\mu}^{\mathrm{A}} \sum_{\nu}^{\mathrm{B}} Q_{\mu \nu} \tag{4}
$$

where $\mathrm{A} \neq \mathrm{B}$ and $\phi_{\mu}$ and $\phi_{\nu}$ must be $s$-type atomic orbitals (AO). Similarly, the $d$-shielding is the overlap of A's $d$ AO with themselves, given by:

$$
\mathrm{A}-d \times \mathrm{A}-d=\sum_{\mu}^{\mathrm{A}} \sum_{\nu}^{\mathrm{A}} Q_{\mu \nu} \tag{5}
$$

Therefore, more credence has been given to the data derived from the Mulliken Population analysis. As mentioned previously, the Mössbauer Isomer Shift (IS) can be directly related to the s-bonding and d-shielding on the iron atom as it participates in bonding. The most significant observations from the data in Table 4 are that all Fe-M dimers have roughly the same s-s binding ability and, based on this fact alone, the calculated Isomer Shift has the sequence FeCu > FeMn > Fe₂ > FeCo > FeNi.

If the s-density at the Fe nucleus in Fe-M is qualitatively modified by the results obtained for d-d shielding (column 4, Table 4) then FeMn, which possesses optimum calculated s-density at the nucleus, will have a significantly reduced IS, followed by FeCu and Fe₂. The two dimers least affected by d-shielding are FeCo and FeNi. Indeed, the latter is found to have the smallest s-density and hence the most negative IS and it resembles the iron configuration of $4s^{2}3d^{6}$ more than the other dimers. Although FeCu has a significantly stronger $s-s$ binding coefficient that FeCo it is closer to $3d^64s^1$ due to the stronger $d$-shielding. Since $Fe_2$ has a smaller $s$-binding coefficient, but the same $d$-shielding $(d^6)$ as FeCu, it should be displaced by an equal but opposite (negative) amount from $Fe^{+1}(3d^64s^1)$ and indeed this is observed on the Mössbauer.

In spite of having one of the strongest $s-s$ binding coefficients, FeMn has a smaller $IS$ than FeCo. The results obtained explain this phenomena readily, as FeMn possesses significant $d-d$ shielding thus reducing the net $s$ contribu tions near the nucleus. Table 4 indicates that Mössbauer studies involving transition metals may, in general, benefit from SUHF calculations. Binding effects which lie close together as in these Fe-M dimers are readily perturbed by $d$-shielding and a deeper insight into the meaning of the $IS$ may be gained from theoretical studies.

Column 5 in Table 4 simply illustrates that, with the exception perhaps of $Fe_2$, $d-d$ bonding is extremely weak. The order of $d$-bonding follows the sequence $Fe_2 > FeMn > FeCu$, $FeNi > FeCo$. This order, as Montano suggested, shows no relationship between $d-d$ bonding and the number of odd/ even electrons on these molecules. The strongest $d-d$ bond shown is on $Fe_2$, the least polarizable and, indeed, a molecule with an even number of electrons. These observations are summarized in Table 5 where the change in $s$ and $d$ configurations relative to $Fe$ $(4s^23d^6)$ atom are shown. Again, one notes the enhanced $d$-shielding on Fe in FeMn which diminishes the $IS$ while the opposite effect is observed on FeCo.

As a final note on our comparison with the Mössbauer, the base configuration $Fe(4s^23d^6)$ was chosen and variations of this distribution were studied in bonding and their effects on the Isomer Shift. There is, therefore, little quantitative comparison between our results and those of I. Shim [11] who based her work on Fe in the $4s^13d^7$ configuration.

Some preliminary studies of the Fe—M molecules at the bond distance of 1.87 Å have been carried out. A dramatic decrease in the separation between $\sigma^b$ and $\sigma^*$ orbitals at 1.87 Å, as compared to 2.3 Å, was observed Clearly, the question of internuclear bond distance is quite interesting and it is hoped that a full geometry optimization will be carried out in the near future.

Table 6 lists the small charge transfer which occurs towards the atom with the higher atomic number. The charge transfer increases from FeMn, FeCo, FeCu to FeNi, a result which is only in slight disagreement with Guenzburger's work [7]. More insight into this charge transfer phenomena may be gleaned from the results obtained since all dimers except FeMn have antibonding atomic contributions on Fe, and binding, more energetically stable contri- butions, on Cu, Co, and Ni. ## CONCLUSION

This study is able to elucidate the origin of the Isomer Shift of these dimers. There is much work left to be done on these molecules and their chemical properties. Work is underway on incorporating and comparing the basis sets of Tatewaki and Huzinaga [25] to those of Roos et al. [27] at an internuclear distance of $2.3\ \mathring{A}$. Also, our basis sets will be improved in the Rydberg region with both diffuse $s$ and $d$ orbitals for future CI calculations and determination of spectroscopic constants. Work underway includes full geometry optimization of the dimers as well as their structure and binding ability to $O_{2}$.
