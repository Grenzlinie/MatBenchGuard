## Deciphering the Origin of Invariance in Magnetic Anisotropy in $\{Fe^{II}S_{4}\}$ Complexes: A Theoretical Perspective

Arup Sarkar, Gunasekaran Velmurugan, Thayalan Rajeshkumar and Gopalan Rajaraman*

Received 00th January 20xx,
Accepted 00th January 20xx
DOI: 10.1039/x0xx00000x
www.rsc.org/

For Q-bit application based on coordination complexes, invariance in zero-field splitting parameters upon structural distortions is desired. Here employing *ab initio* calculations, we have probed the origin of such resistance observed in a four coordinate $[Fe^{II}(C_{3}S_{5})_{2}]^{2-}$ complexes. While unaltered D parameters are noted for a short range of structural distortions such as dihedral angle, if a wider range is chosen, larger variations are prominent in both D and E/D values.

Coordination complexes have long standing interest in the area of molecular magnetism, magnetic refrigeration, magnetocaloric effect (MCE), molecular switch and also in the field of qubits. $^{1}$ Among the Single lon Magnets (SIMs), low coordinate complexes are of great interest as they offer attractive SIM characterstics. $^{2}$ On the other hand, spin-based q-bits have application on quantum information processing (QIP), quantum computing and other quantum simulation operations. $^{3}$ To generate quantum logic gate operations from electronic spin-based q-bits, at least two spin Eigen states are required in which spin transitions with superposition of states can be achieved for a long timescale. $^{4}$

By fine tuning the axial zero-field splitting parameter D and rhombic parameter E, one can get desirable spin Eigen states and for this reason zero-field splitting parameters have considerable importance in the area of spin based q-bits or multi q-bit systems. $^{4}$ These two parameters controls the energy of separation between the $M_{S}$ levels which has been proposed to behave as quantum bits in nuclear spin-free high spin paramagnetic species. A multi-level $M_{S}$ system could also serve as a multi-qubit or qudit system as has been proposed recently. $^{5}$ Particularly for the development of qudit systems proposed recently, the D parameter is relevant as this accounts for the splitting between the two Q-bits.

For non-Kramers ions D and E/D parameters are important as they dictate the quantum tunnelling of magnetization (QTM) between spin Eigen states $(\Delta M_{S})$ differed by $\pm 2$ which in turn increases the relaxation for observing forbidden transitions in EPR spectroscopy. $^{4b,4g}$

While a number of experimental and theoretical studies focus on ways to fine-tune the D parameter, there are only a few reports on viable ways to tune the E parameter. Moreover, both D and E parameters are generally sensitive to small structural changes and therefore retaining the properties on surfaces upon adsorption, a challenging task. $^{6}$ In this regard report of three pseudo-tetrahedral $\{Fe^{II}S_{4}\}$ complexes- [(18-Crown-6)K]₂[Fe(C₃S₅)₂]; 1),(Ph₄P)₂[Fe(C₃S₅)₂]; 2),(Bu₄N)₂[Fe(C₃S₅)₂]; 3) assumes importance. The variation in the counter ion in complexes **1-3** lead to alteration in the dihedral angle($\theta_{d}$) between the two ligand planes from 72-90°, however the magnitude of D and E are reported to be unaltered. The D and E/D parameters are estimated to be ~5.2 and ~0.1 cm⁻¹ respectively (see **Table 1**) from HF-EPR and magnetic studies. $^{7}$ The invariance of D, E despite such a large variation in $\theta_{d}$ is remarkable, however, electronic origin for such resistance has not been clearly understood. In this study, we aim to study complexes **1-3** and related models using high-level *ab initio* methods such as SA-CASSCF/NEVPT2 to underpin the reason behind the resistance observed.

(see ESI for elaborate discussion on computational details).⁹ In addition, we have also performed DFT calculations to evaluate the Mössbauer parameters (isomer shift $\delta$ and quadruple splitting $\Delta E_Q$) to compare our values with the available experimental data (See Table 1).¹⁰ Additionally quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM) analysis have been performed to probe the nature of bonding upon structural variation using B3LYP/TZV set up employing AIM2000 package.¹¹ We have performed the calculations on the X-ray crystal structures of complexes **1-3**. Calculations were performed in the presence as well as in the absence of the counter ion and this yield similar set of D values (see Table 1). This suggests that counter ions do not influence the D and E values.

The coordination environment around the Fe<sup>II</sup> centre in **1-3** (see Fig. 1) is an elongated tetrahedron with the SHAPE calculations revealing deviation of 2.065, 2.073 and 3.515 for **1-3**, respectively, from an ideal tetrahedral geometry. This suggest that as we move from complex **1** to **3**, the distortion increases and complex **3** has the largest deviation from tetrahedral geometry (see Table 1 and **S1**). The calculated D values in complexes **1-3** are very similar (6.12, 6.07 and 6.01 cm⁻¹for **1-3**, respectively) and all these values match well with the experiments. However, the calculations suggest that the E/D values are increasing from 0.02 to 0.11 and to 0.24 as we go from **1** to **3** (see Table 1). The close similarity among the computed D values in complexes **1-3**, can easily be understood if we analyse contributions to D utilizing symmetry arguments and also gaze at the AIM topological parameters (Table **S2-S3** for AIM details in ESI). Complex **1** has D<sub>2d</sub> symmetry and the major anisotropy axes i.e., D<sub>zz</sub> and g<sub>zz</sub> directions werealong the principle C<sub>2</sub> axis. Therefore, the ground state electronic configuration is (d<sub>z</sub>²)²(d<sub>x2-y2</sub>)¹(d<sub>xy</sub>)¹(d<sub>xz</sub>)¹(d<sub>yz</sub>)¹ where the d<sub>xz</sub> and d<sub>yz</sub> orbitals are very close in energy, with a computed gap of 35 cm⁻¹ (See Fig. 1). The major spin-allowed contribution to the positive D results from two transitions, which are d<sub>z</sub>²(a₁) → d<sub>yz</sub>(e) and d<sub>z</sub>²(a₁) → d<sub>xz</sub>(e). Besides, there is also a significant spin-flip transition takes place from d<sub>x2-y2</sub>(b₁) → d<sub>xy</sub>(b₂) orbital that contributes to the total D value (4.9 cm⁻¹, 4.3 cm⁻¹ and 3.5 cm⁻¹ in complexes **1-3** respectively). Note here that in terms of absolute magnitude, the contributions arise from spin-flip excitations are much larger than the spin-allowed excitations and hence these transitions found to dictate the overall sign of the D values. The transition mentioned here are correlated to the point group symmetry of the molecule and any alteration in the symmetry thus likely to alter the sign/magnitude of D (see ESI for details).

As we move from complexes **1-3**, the rhombicity clearly increases and this is attributed to the fact that the energy of the d<sub>xz</sub> orbital decreases and the energy of the d<sub>yz</sub> orbital increases. As a result, the d<sub>xz</sub> orbital contribution to D increases while the contribution arising from d<sub>yz</sub> orbital decreases. As these two factors competes, the overall magnitude of the D remains the same for all the three complexes, despite a drastic change in the dihedral angles. The energy difference ($\Delta E$) between the d<sub>xz</sub> and d<sub>yz</sub> orbitals can be used to rationalise the variation in E/D values. This $\Delta E$ values are found to increase from 35 cm⁻¹ to 1146 cm⁻¹ and to 3681 cm⁻¹, as we move from complexes **1-3** (see Figure **S3** in ESI). This gap is correlated to the increase in E/D value of 0.02, 0.11 and 0.24 for complexes **1-3**, respectively. This increase in E/D value indicates mixing of the ±1 states and consequently this induces spin tunnelling between the two states. Although the variation in the E/D value has not been experimentally observed, this may be due to the insensitive nature of the transitions to the variation in the E/D value as noted by the authors.⁷ To ascertain confidence on computed E/D values, we have simulated the available magnetic data using the computed parameters and this tend to reproduce the data very well (see Figure **S4** in ESI).

To further probe the nature of Fe-S bonding and its influence on the D and E parameter, we have performed QTAIM analysis. For complexes **1-3**, the electron density $\rho(r)$ at the BCP between Fe and the S atoms (0.0642 au < $\rho(r)$ < 0.0663 au) and $\nabla^2_{\rho_{(r)}}$ (0.0393 au < $\nabla^2_{\rho_{(r)}}$ < 0.0426 au) are found to be small and positive. This indicates a closed shell character of the coordination bonds.¹² The $|V(r)|/G(r)$ < 1.0 to 1.28 found for these complexes suggest mixed (largely ionic with a significant covalent component) character of these coordination bonds.¹²ᵇ The $\rho(r)$ values are found to increase, as we move from **1** to **3** and this corresponds to the increase in both the covalency and bond strength of Fe-S bonds.

It is important to note here that among complexes **1-3**, the largest deviation in the structural parameter is observed in the dihedral angle ($\theta_d$), though smaller variations are also seen in other structural parameters. To fully comprehend the influence of $\theta_d$ parameter on D and E, we have developed a correlation on the crystal structure of complex **1** by varying only the $\theta_d$ parameter. The computed results are shown in Figure 2. The D and E parameters computed when the $\theta_d$ parameters are 80° and 69.9° are similar to the values obtained with the X-ray structures of **2** and **3**.

Fig. 2. Variation of d-orbital energies with respect to the dihedral angle. The values written above the diagrams are computed D and E/D values.

This affirms that the parameter D is indeed does not alter upon variation in the $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ parameter. Although the variation in the magnitude of D is not seen for $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ in the range of 70-90°, D found to alter significantly for lower $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ values. Particularly, at 64.7° of $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$, the sign of $D$ is found to switch to negative value and here the $E/D$ value is also very high. Here the positive contributions arising from spin-flip excitations disappears and this lead to switch in the sign of D. At this point, the $D_{zz}$ and $g_{zz}$ orientations are also found to be switched and lies perpendicular to earlier axes. At lower dihedral, the geometry approach towards square-planar and hence the change in the direction of the anisotropy axis with the expected orbital ordering. At this point, the major contributions to the negative $D$ corresponds to spin allowed transition from $d x^{2}-y^{2}$$\rightarrow$$d_{xy}$ orbital. As we move towards lower $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ values, the sign of $\mathrm{D}$ found to be switched. This happens when we reach at $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ = 40°and at this point E/D is also found to be very large. Lowering the $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ further takes the structure closer to a square planar arrangement with a large positive D obtained at $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ value of 30.7° (D=+16 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$). Here, the beta electron resides in $d_{z}^{2}$ orbital leading to two positive spin-allowed transitions i.e., $d_{z}^{2}(a_{1})$ $\rightarrow$$d_{yz}(e)$ and $d_{z}^{2}(a_{1})$ $\rightarrow$$d_{xz}(e)$ and hence a positive D value. This trend continues till $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ value of ~0°, with the D reaching up to ~20 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for perfect square planar geometry (Figure 3). The soft and weaker donor abilities of $\mathrm{S}^{2-}$ligand ensures high-spin S=2 ground state even for square planar geometry.¹³

Next we turn to analyse the transverse anisotropy or E values, here up to $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ of 70°, the major contributions arise from the energy difference between the $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ orbitals as discussed above. Interestingly, below 70° of $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$, the spin allowed contribution drastically decreases and significant contribution from spin-forbidden transitions arises from $d_{z}^{2}(a_{1})$$\rightarrow$$d_{xz}$/$d_{yz}(e)$ transitions (see equations in ESI).

![](./images/813021878535847936_5.jpg)

Fig. 3. Three-dimensional magneto-structural correlation developed by varying simultaneously $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ and average Fe-S bond lengths. Here pink-stars represent complexes 1-3.

In all the models with the $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ variation, the Fe-S BCPs suggest that closed-shell interactions continues and the |V(r)|/|G(r)| < 1.0 also reveal partly covalent Fe-S interactions. As the $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ varies, variation in $\rho(\mathrm{r})$ is also seen with $\rho(\mathrm{r})$ in the range of 0.0662-0.0645 yielding negative D values. The $\rho(\mathrm{r})$ values beyond this range found to yield positive D values. Besides, the Laplacian $\nabla_{\rho(\mathrm{r})}^{2}$ value found to decrease with a decrease in $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ value suggesting greater charge concentration in the Fe-S region for the square planar arrangement and this also indicate the bonds are becoming covalent as we reach square planar geometry. Particularly relevant parameter in AIM analysis is the local density energy H(r) which tend to saturate for $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ values of 0-30° and 65-90°, both range have positive D. For $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ in the range of 30-65°, significant variation are seen and these points corresponds to negative D value. Moreover, the ellipticity values ($\varepsilon$) computed at Fe-S BCP for models possessing $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ values in the range of 0.15-0.05 are small and this correlate well with the observation of negative D values. For the same models, the valence shell charge concentration (VSCC) zone of the S atom is more diffused towards the Fe atom compared to other models and this indicates that there is a larger charge transfer from the S atom to the Fe atom.

Now that role of $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ parameter on D and E is established, we turn to investigate the effect of Fe-S bond length on the anisotropy parameters. To understand how both $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ and Fe-S bond lengths are correlated to each other, we have developed another magneto-structural correlations where both Fe-S bond lengths and $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ parameter are simultaneously varied. This three-dimensional correlation developed is shown in Figure 3. This graph show two plateaus, one at higher $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ values (blue plateau) which is also insensitive to variations in the Fe-S bond lengths and another at lower $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ values (green plateau) which is sensitive to Fe-S bond lengths shorter than 2.35Å. In general, we observed that alteration in bond length significantly influence the magnitude of D parameter. Particularly, an increase in Fe-S bond length found to increases the |D| values and same is also true for very short Fe-S bond length. For very

short Fe-S bond lengths of $2.25$ Å, the magnitude of D tend to increase phenomenally at lower $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ values reaching D=+100 cm⁻¹ at square planar geometry (See ESI for details). At these geometries, the triplet states are found to be the ground state and they contribute significantly to the D value. Earlier report suggest that presence of hard donor ligand atoms at this bond length stabilise triplet state leading to a spin-crossover phenomena.¹⁴

For geometries possessing longer Fe-S bond lengths, as the Fe-S interactions are weakened, the metal $\mathrm{d}_{xz}/\mathrm{d}_{yz}$ orbitals energies are lowered and consequently the D value increases. Here, dominant contributions found to arise only from the quintet states. This is also found to supported by the AIM topological parameters such as $\rho(r)$, $\nabla_{\rho(r)}^{2}$ and $|V(r)|/G(r)$ and $\varepsilon$ values at the BCP between Fe and the S atoms which are found to decrease linearly with increasing Fe-S bond length. Clearly a stronger dependence of D on Fe-S bond lengths and $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ are visible in Fig. 3 where variation of D from -18 to +103 cm⁻¹ has been observed. A similar correlation for the E value suggest that the switching of the sign of D always found to pass through a higher E (or E/D) value. Particularly, larger dihedral values are found to yield E/D~0.3 more often than lower dihedral structures.

To this end, here we have employed $ab$ initio calculations and bonding analysis, to probe the variation of D and E/D values in complexes 1-3 which are reported to resist changes in these parameters. While calculations reaffirm that the D values are unaltered for the reported structures, E values found to vary. Additional calculations performed on model complexes suggest that the D value significantly vary if a wider window of $\theta_{\mathrm{d}}$ parameters are considered; however, two clear platonic regions are noted. Particularly both the sign and magnitude of D are found to vary significantly if minor alterations in Fe-S bond length are noted. As the bond length of the molecule are likely to be altered upon adsorption of molecule on the surfaces⁶, ¹⁵ and this parameter found to significantly influence the anisotropy here, it is important to target structures which are possessing very strong metal-ligand bond strength for potential Q-bit applications.
