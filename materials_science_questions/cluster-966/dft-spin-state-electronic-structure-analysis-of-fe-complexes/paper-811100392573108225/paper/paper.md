# ABSORPTION SPECTROSCOPY OF $AFeS_{2}$ (A = K, Rb, Cs) COMPOUNDS
C.A. TAFT and M.A. DE PAOLI*
Centro Brasileiro de Pesquisas Fisicas, Rio de Janeiro, Brasil

Received 12 January 1979; in final form 5 September 1979

The electronic absorption spectra at 77 K of $KFeS_{2}, RbFeS_{2}$ and $CsFeS_{2}$ were measured and the experimental bands were assigned on the basis of ligand-field theory regarding $d^{5}$ high spin $Fe^{3+}$ under a field of $T_{d}$ symmetry yielding $\Delta \approx$  $-4500$ to $-5000 ~cm^{-1}$ .

## 1. Introduction
The iron sulphides with linked $FeS_{4}$ tetrahedral clusters have been the subject of much theoretical and experimental interest due to the importance of these structures in biochemical and mineral systems[1-7]. The alkali-dithioferrate series of compounds are an interesting example of this type of iron-sul- phur bond [1-7]. We have consequently embarked upon a study of the previously reported Mossbauer EPR and magnetic susceptibility measurements of the alkali-dithioferrates in single crystals and in poly- crystalline samples [1-7]. In this paper our work is extended to absorption spectroscopy of the alkali- dithioferrates.

## 2. Structure and experimental
The specimens were prepared by fusing iron pow- der with the corresponding alkali-carbonate and sul- phur and leaching the cold product in water [1]. Our samples were characterized by X-ray examina- tion. $KFeS_{2}$ grows in permanganate colored needle shaped monoclinic crystals with space-group symme- try $C_{2 h}^{6}$ with four molecules per unit cell having allFe atoms at equivalent sites. The structure of $RbFeS_{2}$ is analogous to that of $KFeS_{2}$ whereas $CsFeS_{2}$ forms orthorhombic crystals with space group $D_{2 h}^{25}$ with a tetramolecular unit cell. In all these compounds the iron atoms are principally covalently bonded by near- ly regular sulphur tetrahedra [1]. The $(FeS_{2})^{-}$ ions are arranged in chains of $FeS_{4}$ tetrahedra linked by their edges with short Fe-Fe distances.

The electronic absorption spectra of the com- pounds at 77 K were measured on a Cary-17 recording spectrophotometer using a Cary liquid helium dewar. The samples were measured in solid state as KBr pel- lets [8]. A pure KBr pellet with the same thickness of the sample was taken as a blank. These pellets were prepared as thin as possible in order to have reasonable slit aperture in the region of measurement. The spectra measured in the absorbance versus wave- length scale were transformed to the energy scale and analyzed in its log-normal components using a computer program in a Burroughs 6700 computer[8]. The absorption spectra of $KFeS_{2}, RbFeS_{2}$ and $CsFeS_{2}$ are given in fig. 1. Mössbauer spectroscopy indicates that iron in the alkali-dithioferrates has the $Fe^{3+} 3 d^{5}$ high spin configuration [1-3].

The $3 d^{5}$ complexes are rather special because the absorption lines correspond to spin forbidden transi- tions, i.e. the rule of non-combination of states of equal parity becomes less effective in tetrahedral $(Fe^{3+})$ complexes, since under a field of $T_{d}$ symme try (i.e. hemihedral, no inversion centre) the electron- ic states have no longer definite g or u character [9,10]. We have assigned, in accordance with the work of Furlani and Furlani [14] and other authors [11], the experimental bands to transitions from the ground state $^{6} ~A(^{6} ~S)$ , to the ten levels into which the quartet terms $^{4} G,^{4} P,^{4} D$ and $^{4} F$ of $Fe^{3+}$ are split under a field $T_{d}$ of symmetry (table 1) [15].

For a $d^{5}$ high spin ion in a tetrahedral ligand-field[16], the lowest energy sextet-quartet transition is $^{6} ~A \to ^{4} ~T(^{4} G)$ , and the energy of this state can be obtained from the solution of the weak-field secular determinant

$$
\begin{vmatrix}
{ }^{4} \mathrm{P} & { }^{4} \mathrm{~F} & { }^{4} \mathrm{G} \\
35100 \beta-\lambda & 0 & -4 \sqrt{5} D q \\
0 & 52100 \beta-\lambda & -2 \sqrt{5} D q \\
-4 \sqrt{5} D q & -2 \sqrt{5} D q & 32000 \beta-\lambda
\end{vmatrix}
$$

where the diagonal energies are the free ferric-ion term energies relative to the $^{6} ~S$ ground term, multiplied by a reduction factor $\beta$ to account for the decrease in the free-ion values on forming a complex. Because of the large nephelauxetic effect of sulfur ligands the differences between the free-ion term energies may be considerably reduced.

We use $\beta=0.5$ , as estimated in the work of Eaton et al. [16] for iron-sulfur tetrahedral complexes,and $D q=450-500 ~cm^{-1}$ and calculate the $^{4} ~T(^{4} G)$  state to be $12000-12500 ~cm^{-1}$ above the $^{6} ~A$ ground state which is in good agreement with the experimental values $(12140,12500,12250 ~cm^{-1}$ for $KFeS_{2}$ , $RbFeS_{2}$ and $CsFeS_{2}$ respectively). Eaton et al. [16] estimate $D q=-600 ~cm^{-1}$ . We would also like to emphasize however that these results are very sensitive to the choice of the parameters $\beta$ and $D q$ . For example, variation of the parameters $\beta$ and $D q$ overthe physically reasonable ranges $0.4-0.6$ and -500 $cm^{-1}$ to $700 ~cm^{-1}$ , respectively, results in a variation of the calculated energy of $^{4} ~T(^{4} G)$ from 6700 $cm^{-1}$ to $14400 ~cm^{-1}$ .

It is possible to interpret [10] the value of $\beta(0.5)$  in terms of an effective charge $(Z_{eff}=3.0 \beta=1.5)$ of the central $Fe^{3+}$ ion which is in good agreement with the average effective charge $Z_{eff }(1.46)$ obtained in the alkali-dithioferrates by fitting Mossbauer quadrupole interaction parameters with electric field gradient calculations $[1,2]$ .
