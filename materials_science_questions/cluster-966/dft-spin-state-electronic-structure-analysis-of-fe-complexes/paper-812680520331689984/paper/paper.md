# Elucidating the Electronic Structure of High-Spin [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] Using Magnetic Circular Dichroism Spectroscopy
Mary Grace I. Galinato,* Emily P. Brocious, Florian Paulat, Sherri Martin, Joshua Skodack, Jill B. Harland, and Nicolai Lehnert*

![](./images/812680520331689984_1.jpg)

![](./images/812680520331689984_2.jpg)

ABSTRACT: Manganese porphyrins are used as catalysts in the oxidation of olefins and nonactivated hydrocarbons. Key to these reactions are high-valent Mn−(di)oxo species, for which [Mn(Porph)(X)] serve as precursors. To elucidate their properties, it is crucial to understand the interaction of the Mn center with the porphyrin ligand. Our study focuses on simple high-spin [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)X] (X = F, Cl, I, Br) complexes with emphasis on the spectroscopic properties of [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl], using variable-temperature variable-field magnetic circular dichroism spectroscopy and time-dependent density functional theory to help with band assignments. The optical properties of [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] are complicated and unusual, with a Soret band showing a high-intensity feature at 21050 cm<sup>−1</sup> and a broad band that spans 23200−31700 cm<sup>−1</sup>. The 15000−18500 cm<sup>−1</sup> region shows the Cl(p<sub>x/y</sub>) → d<sub>π</sub> (CT<sup>(Cl,π)</sup>), Q band, and overlap-forbidden Cl(p<sub>x/y</sub>)_d<sub>π</sub> → d<sub>x<sup>2</sup>−y<sup>2</sup></sup> transitions that gain intensity from the strongly allowed π → π*<sup>(0)</sup> transition. The 20000−21000 cm<sup>−1</sup> region displays the prominent pseudo A-type signal of the Soret band. The strongly absorbing features at 22500−28000 cm<sup>−1</sup> exhibit A<sub>1u</sub>⟨79⟩/A<sub>2u</sub>⟨81⟩ → d<sub>π</sub> CT<sup>(Cl,π/σ)</sup>, and symmetry-forbidden CT character, mixed with the π → π*<sup>(0)</sup> transition. The strong d<sub>x<sup>2</sup>−y<sup>2</sup></sup>_B<sub>1g</sub>⟨80⟩ orbital interaction drives the ground-state MO mixing. Importantly, the splitting of the Soret band is explained by strong mixing of the porphyrin A<sub>2u</sub>(π)⟨81⟩ and the Cl(p<sub>z</sub>)_d<sub>z<sup>2</sup></sup> orbitals. Through this direct orbital pathway, the π → π*<sup>(0)</sup> transition acquires intrinsic metal-d → porphyrin CT character, where the π → π*<sup>(0)</sup> intensity is then transferred into the high-energy CT region of the optical spectrum. The heavier halide complexes support this conclusion and show enhanced orbital mixing and drastically increased Soret band splittings, where the 21050 cm<sup>−1</sup> band shifts to lower energy and the high-energy features in the 23200−31700 cm<sup>−1</sup> range increase further in intensity, compared to the chloro complex.

## INTRODUCTION
Heme-based metal complexes are used to elucidate spectroscopic features and understand complex reaction mechanisms of the more intricate enzymes that they model. This is achieved by mimicking key geometric and/or electronic structural elements in the respective enzymes that generate unique spectral characteristics or play a crucial role in catalysis. In this regard, simple metal complexes containing the tetraphenylporphyrin (TPP<sup>2−</sup>) ligand, such as high-spin (hs) [Fe<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl],<sup>1,2</sup> low-spin (ls) [Co<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl],<sup>3</sup> and hs-[Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl],<sup>4</sup> have been previously synthesized, and their basic electronic and spectroscopic properties have been studied to generate benchmarks for less stable reactive intermediates in small molecule model complexes and enzymes.<sup>2,3</sup> Collectively, these model complexes and their derivatives have provided answers to many questions revolving around the general biological functions of hemoproteins, such as electron transfer (e.g., cytochromes c and b5), transport and storage of dioxygen (e.g., hemoglobin and myoglobin), NO sensing (soluble guanylate cyclase), and catalysis involved in different types of metabolic reactions (e.g., cytochrome P450s and peroxidases).<sup>2,3,5−13</sup> Beyond providing some understanding of the above-mentioned biological functions, [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] (Figure 1) and manganese porphyrins have been used for several other purposes such as building blocks in the construction of molecule-based magnets,<sup>14,15</sup> catalysts in the oxidation of olefins and nonactivated hydrocarbons,<sup>16−20</sup> and the removal of superoxide in mammalian cells.<sup>21</sup> High-valent Mn(IV)−oxo and Mn(V)−

## MATERIALS AND METHODS
Synthesis and Purification of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)X]$ (X = Halide). $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ was synthesized using a known procedure $^{1}$ and was isolated as a microcrystalline solid. Purity was further tested by thin-layer chromatography. The fluoro, bromo, and iodo analogues were synthesized from a $[Mn^{III}(TPP)CH_{3}COO]$ precursor, and ligand exchange was accomplished through extraction, as previously described. $^{41}$ Due to the lability of the acetate and halide ligands, column chromatography could not be performed. Any attempt to purify the compounds throughout the synthetic procedure resulted in generation of the chloro complex, consistent with observations by Powell et al. $^{41}$ Extraction with a mixture of $NaX/CH_{2}Cl_{2}$ was therefore the best means of obtaining the desired compounds. The identity of the compounds was established using UV−vis absorption spectroscopy, $^{1}H$ NMR, and elemental analysis (Atlantic Microlab). Mass spectrometry on all four halide complexes consistently showed a $m/z+$ peak at 667, which corresponds to $[Mn^{III}(TPP)]$ and suggests that the halide dissociates during MS analysis. Analyses of the compounds are provided in the Supporting Information.

UV−Vis Spectroscopy. Room-temperature absorption spectra of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ dissolved in a 1:1 mixture of propionitrile/ butyronitrile were recorded on a Varian Cary 5 UV−vis NIR spectrophotometer. Another set of measurements was made for the complete series of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)X]$ compounds (e.g., $X=-F$, $-Cl$, $-Br$, and $-I$) by dissolving these compounds in dichloromethane. The energies of their Soret bands were compared to those from a previous study $^{41,42}$ to confirm the integrity of the compounds. For MCD spectroscopy, polystyrene (PS) films of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ and the bromo complex were prepared by dissolving the compound along with PS beads in $CH_{2}Cl_{2}$ and toluene, respectively, and letting the solvent evaporate in a beaker.

MCD Spectroscopy. The theoretical background of MCD is explained in detail in previous publications. $^{2,43−46}$ To conduct the experiments, $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ and $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Br]$ were embedded in PS films, which were then placed between two quartz plate windows in a custom-made MCD sample holder. The MCD setup employs an Oxford SM4000 cryostat and a JASCO J-815 CD spectrometer. The SM4000 cryostat consists of a liquid-helium-cooled superconducting magnet, providing horizontal magnetic fields of 0−7 T. The J-815 spectrometer uses a gaseous nitrogen-cooled xenon lamp and a detector system consisting of two interchangeable photomultiplier tubes in the UV−vis and NIR range. The samples were loaded into a 1.5−300 K variable-temperature insert (VTI), which offers optical access to the sample via four optical windows made from Suprasil B quartz. The MCD spectra were measured in $[\theta]=$ mdeg and converted into MCD extinction coefficients, $\Delta\varepsilon$ $[M^{-1}cm^{-1}T^{-1}]$, using the conversion factor $\Delta\varepsilon=\theta/(32980cdB)$, where $c$ is the concentration, $B$ is the magnetic field, and $d$ is the path length. $^{2}$ The product $cd$ can be substituted by $A_{MCD}/\varepsilon_{UV−vis}$, where $A$ is the absorbance of the sample measured by the CD spectrometer and $\varepsilon_{UV−vis}$ is the extinction coefficient from UV−vis. The conversion is based on the Soret band of the heme. The VTVH curves were generated by collecting data at different temperatures (2, 5, 10, 20, and 100 K) and magnetic fields (0−7 T). Each set of spectra was obtained by varying the field at a particular temperature.

Density Functional Calculations. The coordinates of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$, $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Br]$, and $[Mn^{III}(TPP)I]$ are based on the crystal structures of these compounds. $^{3,47}$ The structures of these complexes were fully optimized via DFT calculations using Becke's three-parameter hybrid functional with the correlation function of Lee, Yang, and Parr (B3LYP) $^{48,49}$ as available in Gaussian 03. $^{50}$ For these calculations, the LanL2DZ* basis set was used, which comprises LanL2DZ $^{51−53}$ plus polarization functions from TZVP on all heavy atoms. $^{54,55}$ The theoretical absorption spectrum of the chloro complex was obtained using TD-DFT calculations using Gaussian and ORCA v.4.2.0 as platforms with several functionals and basis sets in order to determine the method that most accurately represents the spectroscopic data. The different functionals incorporate various degrees of HF exchange. Here, we tested BP86, $^{48,56}$ B3LYP, $^{48,49}$ BHandH, $^{57}$ BHandBLYP, $^{57}$ TPSS, $^{58}$ TPSSh, $^{58}$ and PBE1PBE. $^{59}$ The two basis sets tested were LanL2DZ* and TZVP. The TD-DFT spectra of the fluoro and bromo complexes were also generated using the B3LYP/ LanL2DZ* method. Due to the size of the molecule and the complexity of the calculations, the number of excited states was limited to 70; therefore, the calculations cover the range up to ∼330 nm, whereas transitions at higher energy are not included.

Analysis and Fitting of Spectral Data. Gaussian fitting of the UV−vis and MCD spectra of the chloro complex was carried out using the program PeakFit (version 4.12). The smallest number of Gaussian functions necessary to fit the data was used for the analysis, and the quality of the fits was accessed by $\chi^{2}.^{40}$ The experimental VTVH curves of the different C-term bands were fitted using a method devised by Neese and Solomon. $^{46}$ Here, the experimentally obtained zero-field splitting $(D=-2.29cm^{-1})$, $g$ values (2.002), and the degree of rhombicity $(E/D=0)^{60}$ determined by other methods were used in the fit that includes the relative MCD intensities, temperatures, and magnetic fields. $^{44}$ Results from fitting the VTVH saturation curves for each observed electronic transition generates its polarization, as calculated from the effective transition dipole moments. $^{46}$

## RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
Electronic Structure of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$. A theoretical description of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ was obtained by utilizing the B3LYP functional and LanL2DZ* basis set. This method was chosen based on the excellent agreement between the crystal structure and the calculated geometry of $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl],^{2}$ in which the same method was used. The coordination sphere of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ comprises the $Mn^{III}$ center surrounded by a square-pyramidal arrangement of four pyrrole nitrogens on the base and an axial chloride ion. A recent crystal structure of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ shows that this complex crystallizes in the monoclinic space group $P2_{1}/n.^{3}$ The fully optimized structure of the $Mn^{III}$ complex generated $d(Mn-Cl)=2.365$ Å (Figure 1), which is in excellent agreement with the crystal structure (2.389 Å) (Table S1). $^{3}$ The calculated average $d(Mn-N(Porph))$ distance is 2.037 Å, which compares well with 2.019 Å found in the crystal structure. $^{3}$ Similarly, the calculated displacement of the Mn atom from the basal nitrogen plane of the porphyrin of 0.302 Å is close to that observed experimentally (0.258 Å). The porphyrin moiety itself is saddled. Interestingly, there are distinct differences in the structural features between $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ and the analogous complex $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl]$, despite both metal ions having comparable size (for high-spin six-coordinate structures). $^{61}$ The $Fe^{III}$ complex has been shown to crystallize in either a tetragonal $^{62}$ or monoclinic $^{63}$ form. Compared to the $Mn^{III}$ complex, $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ exhibits a distinctively shorter $M-Cl$ bond length of 2.192 Å, a longer average $M-N(Porph)$ distance of 2.049 Å, and an increased out-of-plane displacement of 0.383 Å. The porphyrin skeleton is essentially planar in $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl].^{62}$ Compared to the Fe analogue, the longer $Mn-Cl$ and shorter $Mn-N(Porph)$ distances are likely a reflection of the smaller out-of-plane displacement of the Mn center and a stronger interaction of the

Mn center with the porphyrin ligand. The saddling of the porphyrin skeleton may be required to maintain the short Mn−N(Porph) distances, while allowing the metal to move out of the basal plane of the porphyrin. These unique structural features influence the spectroscopic properties of the complex, making it distinct from analogous compounds such as [Feᴵᴵᴵ(TPP)Cl] that display absorption spectra more typical for porphyrin complexes.

Figure 1 shows the coordinate system applied to [Mnᴵᴵᴵ(TPP)Cl] as needed for the discussion of its molecular orbitals (MO) and electronic transitions. Here, the x- and y-axes are located along the Mn−N bonds of the porphyrin ligand, and the z-axis is aligned with the Mn−Cl bond. In the former, coordination is mostly mediated by the $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital of Mn(III) and the $\sigma$-donating orbitals of the pyrrole nitrogens in the porphyrin plane. In the case of the Mn−Cl $\sigma$ bond, the $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital of Mn and the $p_{z}$ orbital of the chloro ligand interact. Additional Mn−Cl $\pi$ bonding interaction between the $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ (labeled $d_{\pi}$) orbitals of Mn and the $p_{x}$ and $p_{y}$ orbitals of chloride are also present. Details of the MOs generated from such types of interactions, in addition to other orbitals that play a key role in the electronic properties of [Mnᴵᴵᴵ(TPP)Cl], are highlighted in the next section.

Of course, this representation of the electronic structure of the complex is an oversimplification, and for the actual analysis of the optical data of [Mnᴵᴵᴵ(TPP)Cl], many more MOs of this complex in an unrestricted scheme need to be considered. In the spin-unrestricted formalism, four $\alpha$-d orbitals of Mn(III) are occupied and the $\beta$-d orbitals are empty. This leads to porphyrin $\rightarrow$ Mn-d CT transitions that mainly originate from the $\beta$-d orbitals, creating a multitude of electronic transitions, which manifests itself in the very complicated electronic spectra of hs Mnᴵᴵᴵ porphyrins.³³,³⁷,³⁸ Figure 5 shows the MO energy level diagram for [Mnᴵᴵᴵ(TPP)Cl], and Tables 1 and 2 list the charge contributions of important $\alpha$- and $\beta$-spin MOs. Because both sets of orbitals are important for interpreting the electronic spectra of [Mnᴵᴵᴵ(TPP)Cl], charge contributions of key $\alpha$ and $\beta$ MOs are discussed in this section.

Starting with the $\alpha$-spin MOs, the $\sigma$-bonding interaction between Mnᴵᴵᴵ and the porphyrin is mediated by the empty $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital of Mnᴵᴵᴵ and the $\sigma$-donor orbital of the pyrrole nitrogens with $B_{1g}$ symmetry (vide supra). The antibonding combination, $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}B_{1g}\langle 80\rangle$ ($\alpha\langle 174\rangle$), has 44% $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and 53% porphyrin contribution, which corresponds to a very strong interaction. This MO is the LUMO of the complex. At slightly higher energy, two nearly degenerate MOs ($\alpha\langle 175\rangle$ and $\alpha\langle 176\rangle$) are located, which correspond to the $E_{g}\langle 82/83\rangle$ $\pi^{*}$ orbitals of the porphyrin (the LUMO of the free ligand). The HOMO of [Mnᴵᴵᴵ(TPP)Cl] is the porphyrin $\pi$ orbital $A_{2u}\langle 81\rangle$ ($\alpha\langle 173\rangle$). Notably, this MO also contains contributions from $Cl(p_{z})$ (10%) and $d_{z^{2}}$ (7%). The other important porphyrin $\pi$ orbital, $A_{1u}\langle 79\rangle$ ($\alpha\langle 172\rangle$), is nonbonding with respect to the d orbitals and represents the HOMO−1 of the complex. The Mnᴵᴵᴵ−Cl $\sigma$-bond originates from the interaction of the $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital of Mnᴵᴵᴵ and $Cl(p_{z})$. The bonding combination, $\alpha\langle 171\rangle$, has 19% Mn and 39% Cl character. Additional $\pi$-interactions between the $d_{\pi}$ orbitals of Mn, and the $Cl(p_{x})$ and $Cl(p_{y})$ orbitals are also present. The bonding combinations correspond to $\alpha\langle 170\rangle$ (4% Mn, 81% Cl, and 10% porphyrin character) and $\alpha\langle 169\rangle$ (5% Mn, 83% Cl, and 9% porphyrin character). However, please note that in these cases both the Mnᴵᴵᴵ d orbitals ($d_{z^{2}}$, $d_{\pi}$) and the chloride donor orbitals are occupied, and hence, no net bonding between Mnᴵᴵᴵ and the chloro ligand results in $\alpha$-spin. Other essential MOs to note include $B_{2u}\langle 74\rangle$ ($\alpha\langle 158\rangle$ and $\alpha\langle 166\rangle$) and $A_{2u}\langle 72\rangle$ ($\alpha\langle 157\rangle$), which are found at lower energy.

In the $\beta$-spin MO diagram, the Mnᴵᴵᴵ−Cl $\sigma$-bond is represented by the bonding MO ($\beta\langle 153\rangle$), which shows 4% Mn, 37% Cl, and 41% porphyrin character, and the antibonding combination $\beta\langle 175\rangle$, which has 58% Mn, 11% Cl, and 13% porphyrin contribution. The Mnᴵᴵᴵ−Cl $\pi$-bond arises from two nearly degenerate bonding combinations, ($\beta\langle 166\rangle$ and $\beta\langle 167\rangle$), which have 2% Mn, 72% Cl, and ~20% porphyrin contribution. The corresponding, antibonding MOs are $\beta\langle 173\rangle$ and $\beta\langle 174\rangle$, which have 62−65% Mn, 3% Cl, and 24−26% porphyrin admixture. Hence, the $\sigma$- and $\pi$-covalency of the Mnᴵᴵᴵ−Cl bond is lower than expected, but this is in agreement with the relatively long Mn−Cl bond observed in the crystal structure (see above). The HOMO of the free porphyrin ligand, $A_{2u}\langle 81\rangle$, is mixed with 2% $d_{z^{2}}$, giving rise to the MO $\beta\langle 169\rangle$, whereas the HOMO−1 is $A_{1u}\langle 79\rangle$ ($\beta\langle 168\rangle$), which is nonbonding with respect to the metal. The two nearly degenerate $E_{g}\langle 82/83\rangle$ $\beta$-LUMOs are mixed with the $d_{\pi}$ and $Cl(p_{x/y})$ orbitals, generating $\beta\langle 170\rangle$ and $\beta\langle 171\rangle$, which show 8−10% Mn, 1−2% Cl, and ~77% porphyrin contributions. The formally nonbonding $d_{xy}$ orbital, $\beta\langle 172\rangle$, has 12% porphyrin character and corresponds to the LUMO+2 of the complex. In theory, CT<sup>(1)</sup> is overlap-forbidden and should not show any intensity because of the poor overlap between the porphyrin orbital $\text{A}_{1\text{u}}\langle 79\rangle$ with the $\text{d}_{\pi}$ orbitals of Mn<sup>III</sup>. However, as the $\text{d}_{\pi}$ orbitals are mixed with the $\text{E}_{\text{g}}\langle 82/83\rangle$ orbitals of the porphyrin dianion (~25%; cf. Table 2), the CT<sup>(1)</sup> gains intensity from the strongly allowed $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ transition through this orbital mixing. With the exception of CT<sup>(S)</sup> and CT<sup>(6)</sup>, the in-plane-polarized transitions ($\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ and porphyrin $\rightarrow \text{d}_{\pi}$CT) have $\text{E}_{\text{u}}$ symmetry and may undergo configuration interaction (CI). This phenomenon results in a strongly mixed set of electronic transitions that is manifested in the unusually complicated absorption spectrum of [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] and is also reflected in the TD-DFT results. Tables 3 and 4 show an abbreviated list of the transitions and assignments of the experimentally observed absorption bands. The complete list is provided in Table S6.

Spectroscopic Results. In order to simulate the temperature- and field-dependent (VTVH) MCD C-term intensity of [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] and to derive the polarizations of the electronic transitions, the magnetic properties of the ground state of this compound need to be known (see Materials and Methods section). In previous studies, the axial zero-field splitting ($D = -2.29$ cm<sup>−1</sup>), $g$ values (2.002), and the degree of rhombicity ($E/D = 0$) were obtained for this hs $3\text{d}^{4}$ system using magnetic susceptibility and high-field electron paramagnetic resonance studies.<sup>60,64</sup> We used these Spin Hamiltonian parameters for our VTVH simulations. It should also be noted that the optical properties of Mn<sup>III</sup>(Porph) complexes have been analyzed before.<sup>3,33,37,62,63,65−67</sup> Thus far, electronic absorption spectroscopy remains one of the most useful techniques in studying hs-[Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] because of the inherently unique UV−visible spectral features the complex displays compared to other metalloporphyrins (Figures 2 and 3). Comparisons with these previous studies are made in the Discussion section of this paper.

UV−Vis Spectroscopy. The typical spectra of metalloporphyrins (such as [Zn<sup>II</sup>(TPP)] and [Co<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl]) exhibit an intense Soret band at ~420 nm and weaker Q bands at ~550 nm.<sup>68−73</sup> In contrast, in the case of [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl]), the Soret band seems split into two less intense features, one of which is a prominent peak at lower energy (477 nm), and the other one is a very broad band that occurs at higher energy (around 375 nm).<sup>33,38</sup> Figure 2 presents the absorption and low-temperature MCD spectra of [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl], which are resolved into 18 Gaussians by conducting a correlated fit of these data. In this section, the Roman numeral scheme is used to make reference to the main absorption bands (see Figure 2). In the UV−vis absorption spectrum, the split Soret band occurs at 477 nm (band V) with broader features at 378 nm (band VI) and 406 nm (band Va). The extinction coefficient of the 477 nm band is 113000 M<sup>−1</sup> cm<sup>−1</sup>, which is distinctly smaller compared to that of the Soret band of [Co<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] (250000 M<sup>−1</sup> cm<sup>−1</sup> at 410 nm) but comparable to that of [Fe<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] (108000 M<sup>−1</sup> cm<sup>−1</sup> at 417 nm). The intensity ratio, $R$ (ratio of V/VI as described by Boucher, Figure 2),<sup>37,38</sup> of the Soret band (band V) and the most intense band within the 375 nm broad feature (band VI) is 1.96. This is similar to the ratio obtained from previous UV−vis studies on [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] in dichloromethane, reported as 1.93 and 2.02.<sup>42,74</sup> Here, the value of $R$ serves as a measure for the extent of coupling of the Soret band to higher-energy CT transitions, leading to an intensity transfer from the Soret excited state to these other excited states and also a shift of the Soret band. Magnetic Circular Dichroism (MCD) Spectroscopy. The low-temperature MCD C-term spectrum of [Mn$^{III}$(TPP)Cl] shows complicated features in the $15000-30000$ cm$^{-1}$ region, requiring 18 Gaussian bands to fit the experimental data (Figure 2 and Table 3). These bands correspond to unique electronic transitions that would have been impossible to identify from the UV−vis spectrum alone, illustrating the power of low-temperature MCD spectroscopy to identify individual electronic transitions (Figure S2). The band numbering scheme used in this section originates from the combined fits of the UV−vis and MCD spectra, which is used here to clearly refer to a certain feature in the spectra. In order to generate the pure MCD C-term data shown in Figure 2, it is essential to subtract the temperature-independent A- and B-term contributions. Figure S3 presents the MCD spectrum of [Mn$^{III}$(TPP)Cl] at 290 K, which is used to quantify the A- and B-term signals and subsequently subtract them from the total MCD intensity. According to Gouterman's four-orbital model, the $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ excitation gives rise to the Soret and Q excited states, which both have E$_u$ symmetry in perfect tetragonal symmetry. MCD is able to resolve the two Soret components as a derivative-shaped band (a "pseudo" A-term signal)$^{44}$ with $-\Delta\varepsilon/+\Delta\varepsilon$ pattern at 20750 cm$^{-1}$ (band 10, $\varepsilon = -2544$ M$^{-1}$ cm$^{-1}$ T$^{-1}$) and 21000 cm$^{-1}$ (band 11, $\varepsilon = 2201$ M$^{-1}$ cm$^{-1}$ T$^{-1}$). Two additional features in this region are observed at 21240 and 21671 cm$^{-1}$ (bands 12 and 13). A similar configuration of the Soret band features was observed in the MCD spectra of other Mn$^{III}$ porphyrin complexes.$^{33,38}$ To higher energy of the Soret band, bands 14 and 15 constitute a very weak derivative-shaped signal at 22500 and 23264 cm$^{-1}$, respectively. Analysis of the UV−Vis and MCD Spectra. Variable-Temperature Variable-Field Measurements. VTVH saturation curves were determined for all clearly separated bands in the MCD C-term spectrum of $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$ in order to generate the relative polarizations of these electronic transitions and to distinguish in-plane ($xy$)- and out-of-plane ($z$)-polarized transitions. In-plane-polarized transitions comprise the inner porphyrin $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ and porphyrin $\rightarrow \text{d}_{\pi}$ CT transitions, whereas

![](./images/812680520331689984_11.jpg)

Figure 8. MCD C-term saturation magnetization curves of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ at different selected energies, along with fits of those data. The red lines pertain to the best fit of the experimental data (black dots). The polarizations of the electronic transitions derived from the fit are also shown.

out-of-plane-polarized transitions mostly include $Cl^- \rightarrow$ d CT transitions. The effective four-fold symmetry of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ results in an axial zero-field splitting ($E/D = 0$), which allows for a clear distinction between the $xy$- and $z$-polarized transitions from the VTVH data. To understand how the Spin Hamiltonian parameters of the $Mn^{III}$ complex impact the properties of the VTVH curves, calibration curves with different contributions of $x$-, $y$-, and $z$-polarization were created (Figure S4). Transitions with significant $z$-polarization (>50%) demonstrate a nesting ("N") behavior. As the contributions of $xy$-polarized transitions become substantial, the saturation isotherms start to overlay ("O" behavior).

Figures 8 and S5 show the VTVH curves for 13 of the 17 clearly identified bands in the MCD C-term spectrum of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$. Bands 8, 9, 12, and 13 show too much overlap with other features to generate reliable saturation isotherms and are left out from the analysis. Bands 2−4 display "N" behavior, with significant contributions from $z$-polarization ($\sim$30−50%). The VTVH curves for bands 3 and 4 were challenging to generate, particularly in the high-temperature range due to their weak intensity. The polarizations for these features are therefore not as accurate as those generated for the other bands. Bands 5−7 predominantly have $xy$-polarization, with some out-of-plane contributions (5−15%). In the Soret region where bands 10 and 11 are located, the VTVH curves show distinguishable "O" behavior mainly due to $xy$-polarization with major contributions from $\pi \rightarrow \pi^*$ transitions. Bands 14 and 15 are also in-plane-polarized with minimal out-of-plane contributions. In the higher-energy region, bands 16−18 are mostly $xy$-polarized with about 10% $z$-polarized contributions. A summary of the polarizations of all clearly identified transitions is presented in Tables 3 and S6.

Assignments Using TD-DFT (B3LYP/LanL2DZ*) Calculations. The absorbance spectrum generated from the B3LYP/ LanL2DZ* TD-DFT calculations is presented in Figures S6 and S7. Several DFT functionals were tested in order to identify the theoretical method that best reproduces the spectroscopic data (Figure S7). Whereas B3LYP/LanL2DZ* is not perfect as it displays an overestimated intensity in the higher-energy region (at $27171\ \text{cm}^{-1}$), it best describes the overall experimental spectrum. The functionals BHandHLYP and BHandH show improved agreement over the rest of the functionals tested (with the former being slightly better) but have too little intensity in the high-energy region, overestimate the intensity of the Soret feature, and undervalue the intensities of the transitions in the Q band region. In addition, the overall spectrum appears shifted by $\sim 5000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ to higher energy with these methods. The discussion below focuses on the assignment of the bands using B3LYP/LanL2DZ* as a guideline, and a comparison of the assignments in the Soret and high-energy region to those obtained with BHandHLYP/LanL2DZ* is presented in the following section.

The prominent bands in the theoretical spectrum at 27171, 25372, 21595, and $16623\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ correspond to the experimental bands at 26470, 24661, 20951, and $16186\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Whereas the features in the TD-DFT generated spectrum are shifted to higher energy by $\sim 400-600\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ in relation to the experimental spectrum, the relative intensities somewhat pattern the experimental data (with the exception of the band at 25366 $\text{cm}^{-1}$), thus providing a good theoretical framework for the assignments of the bands. The TD-DFT calculated polar-

izations, oscillator strengths, and relative energies were collectively employed to assign the bands in the MCD spectrum of $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$. Although some features in the C-term spectrum are too weak to generate reliable saturation curves, assignments were still attempted. In addition, features in the UV−vis spectrum that have no parallel in the MCD spectrum have been assigned with the aid of TD-DFT and previous studies. Finally, key electronic transitions that are predicted by TD-DFT with no discernible peaks in either experimental spectra are also discussed. To readily follow the discussion, the features in the optical spectra of $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$ will be presented from lower to higher energy. Figures 2, 8 and S5 and Tables 3 and S6 may be referenced in the discussion of the assignments of the optical features. It is important to note that the assignments of the transitions discussed in this section are based on the best TD-DFT model of several tested that had various degrees of Hartree−Fock exchange (Figure S7).

To start, a weak and rather broad feature just above the baseline of the UV−vis spectrum is observed at $14935\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ ($\varepsilon = 1163\ \text{M}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-1}$) that has no corresponding feature in the MCD spectrum (band 1). We consider this feature because previous studies on $\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$ porphyrin chloro complexes display near-IR bands within this energy region, at 14500 and $14472\ \text{cm}^{-1}$.^{33,39} Although the experimental polarization for this band is not available, relative energies and oscillator strengths may be used to assign this feature in comparison with theoretical data. This band is possibly identified with two calculated features at 15186 and $15569\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which are $xy$- and $z$-polarized, respectively. The feature at $15186\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ mainly originates from an $xy$-polarized $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ transition, whereas the other feature at $15569\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is $z$-polarized and constitutes a combination of symmetry-forbidden $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{z})\text{d}_{z^{2}} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ CT and weak $\text{d}_{z^{2}} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ (d−d) transitions. As band 1 is not MCD active, we conclude that this feature is most likely identified with the $xy$-polarized $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ transition. Boucher observed a band at $14500\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ in the absorption spectrum of $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})(\text{Cl})(\text{H}_{2}\text{O})]^{33}$ and assigned this feature to a combination of $\text{CT}^{(1,2,5)}$ (Table 4).^{33,38} It is noted that in Boucher's paper, generalized assignments for several $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{Porph})\text{L}]$ complexes are given (not just $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})(\text{Cl})(\text{H}_{2}\text{O})]$). In Table 4, these assignments are listed under the $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})(\text{Cl})(\text{H}_{2}\text{O})]$ column. An analogous feature at $14512\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is observed for $[\text{Fe}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$ and is assigned to $\text{CT}^{(5)}$;^{2} however, this feature is MCD active, which is different from band 1 in $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$.

Band 2 ($15881\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) is the most intense C-term signal in the low-energy region and shows strong $z$-polarization (about 50%). Band 2 is assigned to the calculated band at $16614\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which has a dominant contribution (68%) from the overlap-forbidden $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{y})\text{d}_{yz} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ ($z$-polarized) CT transition mixed with two $xy$-polarized transitions: $\text{d}_{yz} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$. The latter contribution (about 12% by TD-DFT) is responsible for the predicted oscillator strength ($f = 0.0043$) of this feature. In $[\text{Fe}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$, a comparable feature at $15000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is assigned to $\text{CT}^{(1)}$ and $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ transitions.^{2} Band 3 ($16282\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) is assigned to the analogous $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{x})\text{d}_{xz} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ overlap-forbidden CT transition that gains intensity from $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ contributions. The calculated value of $32\%\ \pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ would be in agreement with the increased UV−vis absorption but decreased MCD intensity of this feature. According to the TD-DFT, this band has minor contributions from a d−d transition ($\text{d}_{xz} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$), as well.

Bands 4 and 5 at 16879 and $17273\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ constitute a pseudo A-term signal, and these are assigned to the two components of the Q band. Like the Soret excited state, the Q excited state has $\text{E}_{(\text{u})}$ symmetry (in tetragonal symmetry), giving rise to two coupled transitions with a $-\Delta \varepsilon /+\Delta \varepsilon$ pattern (from lower to higher energy) as in the case of the Soret band. Note that due to the lower symmetry environment of $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$, the $\text{E}_{(\text{u})}$ states are split in energy, as directly observed in the spectra. Both features show dominant $xy$-polarization, in agreement with this assignment. From the TD-DFT calculations, these features are predicted to be very close in energy, at 17058 and $17057\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Here, we inversed the order from TD-DFT by matching up the relative UV−vis absorption intensities of these features with the calculated oscillator strengths (see Table S6). While band 4 is hard to detect in the UV−vis spectrum, it becomes apparent as a weak signal ($-17\ \text{M}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-1}\text{T}^{-1}$) in the MCD spectrum. An analogous feature at $17200\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ in $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})(\text{Cl})(\text{H}_{2}\text{O})]$ has also been assigned to a $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ transition, with the possibility of it also being $\text{CT}^{(3)}$.^{33,38,39} According to the TD-DFT calculations, bands 4 and 5 (just like bands 2 and 3) also show an admixture of a symmetry-forbidden CT transition, $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{x/y})\text{d}_{\pi} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$, with 25 and 8% contributions, respectively.

The assignments of bands 2−5 are relatively straightforward based on the TD-DFT calculations, but there is one caveat. In the calculations, all of those transitions are strongly $xy$-polarized, whereas experimentally, bands 2−4 show 50, 40, and about 30% $z$-polarization, respectively. We interpret this deviation with the presence of $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ character in the corresponding excited states in experiment, which is not reproduced by the TD-DFT calculations (see next).

To higher energy of the Q bands, the MCD spectrum shows two weak, negative C-term features, designated as bands 6 ($18290\ \text{cm}^{-1}, -23\ \text{M}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-1}\text{T}^{-1}$) and 7 ($18890\ \text{cm}^{-1}, -25\ \text{M}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-1}\text{T}^{-1}$), which are largely $xy$-polarized with some $z$-polarization (about 10−15%). Based on relative oscillator strengths and polarizations, band 6 is assigned to the calculated feature at $20798\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ that corresponds to $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ and is predicted to be fully $z$-polarized. We tested the accuracy of the fit of the VTVH data of band 6 to see if the $z$-polarized contribution could be increased. Indeed, setting the $z$-polarization to 40% slightly improves the fits of the high-temperature data but at the expense of the accuracy for the low-temperature data. Band 7 is assigned to a calculated transition at $21244\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which corresponds to two symmetry-forbidden CT transitions, $\text{E}_{\text{g}}(\pi) \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{y})\text{d}_{yz} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$. This feature gains intensity through the presence of a fraction (7%) of the allowed $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ transition. The experimental data and theoretical calculations both predict this feature to be largely in-plane-polarized, in agreement with this assignment. However, experimentally, this feature also contains an admixture of a $z$-polarized component, which is not reproduced by the TD-DFT calculations. So, whereas the TD-DFT calculations predict that $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ is mostly localized around $21000-22000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ in the form of distinct transitions, our experimental results do not support this finding and show that $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ is delocalized over the whole spectral range from 16000 to $19000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, with strong $z$-polarization found especially for bands 2−4 and 6, and that $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ is mixed with $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ (especially $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$) and symmetry-forbidden CT transitions. Similar to this, $[\text{Fe}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$ shows the presence of the $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ transition throughout the spectrum in the 16000−$22000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ range. Also, a selective admixture of $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ character into one of the Q excited states (band 4) but not the other (band 5) in $[\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}(\text{TPP})\text{Cl}]$ would explain why the experimentally observed energy splitting between these


components is so much larger than predicted by TD-DFT (see above). In analogy to these findings, $CT^{(Cys,\pi)}$ has also been observed to be delocalized over a number of low-energy transitions in ferric cytochrome P450cam. $^{40}$ Finally, Boucher did not report a feature close to the energy of band 6 nor make any mention of a possible $CT^{(Cl,\pi)}$ transition throughout the spectral region of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)(Cl)(H_{2}O)]$.

It should be noted that the vibronic $Q_{v}$ bands should be located about $1300\ cm^{-1}$ to higher energy of the Q bands. Just like the Q bands, $Q_{v}$ should give rise to a pseudo A-term signal. Experimentally, we do not observe such a feature in the 18000-$18600\ cm^{-1}$ range, indicating that this transition is only of weak intensity in the MCD data of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ (but it cannot be ruled out that they contribute some intensity in this spectral region that we cannot identify in the spectra).

Leading into the mid-energy region are two bands located at $19541\ cm^{-1}$ (band 8, $-26\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$) and $20750\ cm^{-1}$ (band 9, $-270\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$), both of which are weak and convoluted, and hence, accurate saturation curves cannot be generated for these features. Both bands can be assigned to a feature predicted at $21254\ cm^{-1}$ that primarily consists of two symmetry-forbidden CT transitions, $E_{g}(\pi)\rightarrow d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and $Cl(p_{x})_{-}d_{xz}\rightarrow d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$. Like band 7, bands 8 and 9 gain intensity from the presence of a small amount of $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0)}$ character, causing these features to be $xy$-polarized.

The Soret region of the MCD spectrum is fit to four features, which are bands 10 ($20750\ cm^{-1}$, $-2544\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$), 11 ($21000\ cm^{-1}$, $2201\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$), 12 ($21240\ cm^{-1}$, $1225\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$), and 13 ($21671\ cm^{-1}$, $450\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$). The main features, bands 10 and 11, correspond to a pseudo A-type signal and correspond to the two components of the $E_{(u)}$-symmetric (in four-fold symmetry) Soret excited state, split in the lower symmetry of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$. As expected, both bands demonstrate $xy$-polarization. Band 10, predicted at $21573\ cm^{-1}$, also has $Cl(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}}+A_{2u}\rightarrow E_{g}$ CT character, as well as $5\%$ $CT^{(2)}$ and $3\%$ $CT^{(1)}$. Band 11, calculated at $21595\ cm^{-1}$, also shows $Cl(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}}+A_{2u}\rightarrow E_{g}$ CT as well as $8\%$ $CT^{(2)}$ and $3\%$ $CT^{(1)}$ contributions. Band 12, which is characterized as a shoulder along band 11 (hence, we cannot generate VTVH data for this feature), is assigned to the calculated $z$-polarized transition at $21977\ cm^{-1}$, which has $CT^{(Cl,\pi)}$, $CT^{(Cl,\sigma)}$, and $CT^{(7)}$ contributions. We believe that the TD-DFT calculations overestimate $CT^{(Cl,\pi)}$ in energy, and band 12 should therefore be identified with $CT^{(Cl,\sigma)}$ and porphyrin $\rightarrow$ Mn(III) CT. Band 13 tails band 12 as a shoulder and is therefore difficult to assign. In general, the Soret region is remarkable because it comprises an admixture of $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*}$ and various in-plane and out-of-plane CT transitions. Previous studies support the presence of $CT^{(1,2,3,4)}$ transitions in the Soret region of $Mn^{III}$ porphyrin complexes. $^{33,38,39}$ A comparable assignment is made for $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ in the Soret region, where $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0,2)}$ and $CT^{(4)}$ transitions are observed. $^{2}$

At slightly higher energy is a weak, positive C-term signal located at $22500\ cm^{-1}$ ($122\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$) that is hardly detectable in the UV−vis spectrum and labeled as band 14. This feature is mainly $xy$-polarized as predicted by theory and experiment and is calculated to occur at $22955\ cm^{-1}$. Just like band 13, it has dominant contributions from $CT^{(2)}$ and $Cl(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}}+A_{2u}\rightarrow E_{g}$ CT and also has minor $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0,1,2)}$, $CT^{(4)}$, and $CT^{(1)}$ character.

The other half of the "split" Soret band constitutes a broad feature in the high-energy region of the UV−vis spectrum, indicating the presence of several overlapping transitions. MCD resolves this region into four transitions: bands 15 ($23264\ cm^{-1}$, $-222\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$), 16 ($24375\ cm^{-1}$, $-407\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$), 17 ($26350\ cm^{-1}$, $-526\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$), and 18 ($27851\ cm^{-1}$, $205\ M^{-1}\ cm^{-1}\ T^{-1}$). Among these features, band 15 is unique in that it has the weakest intensity and is assigned to a TD-DFT feature located at higher energy compared to bands 16−18, on the basis of oscillator strength and, in particular, polarization. Band 15 is assigned to two $xy$-polarized features at 27809 and $27820\ cm^{-1}$ that are close in energy and very similar in nature, showing $Ph/E_{g}\rightarrow d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ and symmetry-forbidden $Cl(p_{x/y})\rightarrow d_{z^{2}}$ CT character with minor contributions from $CT^{(1)}$. The lower energy feature ($27809\ cm^{-1}$) also contains $43\%$ $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(1)}$, $10\%$ $CT^{(1,2,4)}$, and $4\%$ $CT^{(Cl,\pi)}$ admixture.

Band 16 is assigned to a superposition of two in-plane- polarized transitions, predicted at 25276 and $25468\ cm^{-1}$. Both features have major $CT^{(1)}$ and $CT^{(2)}$ character, with some contributions from $Cl(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}}+A_{2u}\rightarrow E_{g}$ and $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0)}$. The fact that band 16 contains $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0)}$ character supports the idea that this feature steals intensity from the Soret band. Band 16 is therefore a $A_{1u}/A_{2u}(\pi)\rightarrow d_{\pi}$ transition with additional $A_{1u}/A_{2u}$ $\rightarrow E_{g}\ (\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0)})$ character. The strong mixing between these states could be mediated by the strong mixing between the $d_{\pi}$ orbitals of $Mn^{III}$ and the porphyrin $E_{g}(\pi^{*})$ orbitals. $^{33,38}$ Alternatively, the strong admixture of $Cl(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}}$ character into the porphyrin $A_{2u}(\pi)$ orbital might play a role. This is further evaluated in the Discussion section. In $[Mn^{III}(TPP)-(Cl)(H_{2}O)]$, a feature at $25000\ cm^{-1}$ is assigned to a $CT^{(5,7)}$ and $A_{1u}\rightarrow d_{z^{2}}$ CT transition, $^{33,38}$ whereas $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ displays a band at $26181\ cm^{-1}$ that comprises $CT^{(Cl,\pi,\sigma)}$ and $CT^{(5)}$. $^{2}$ The most intense feature in the UV−vis spectrum arises from band 17, which is again predominantly $xy$-polarized and is assigned to a superposition of two energetically close transitions, predicted at 27144 and $27198\ cm^{-1}$. Like band 16, band 17 has principal contributions from $CT^{(1)}$ and $CT^{(2)}$, with some admixture arising from $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0,1)}$ and the $Cl(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}}+A_{2u}\rightarrow E_{g}$ CT and, hence, is of similar principal nature as band 16. An analogous band at $26700\ cm^{-1}$ in $[Mn^{III}(TPP)(Cl)(H_{2}O)]$ is attributed to $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0)}$ and $CT^{(3,4)}$. $^{33,38}$ $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ demonstrates synonymous assignments for a band at $26472\ cm^{-1}$, which shows an admixture of $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(0,2)}$ and $CT^{(4)}$. $^{2}$

The last MCD peak that is resolved in the high-energy region is band 18, which is assigned to a combination of two in-plane- polarized transitions, predicted at 28598 and $28744\ cm^{-1}$. Both features are dominated by $Cl(p_{x}/p_{y})\rightarrow d_{z^{2}}$ CT. Intensity arises from the presence of the strongly allowed $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(1,2)}$ transition. In $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl]$, comparable peaks at 28098 and $29819\ cm^{-1}$ have been assigned to $\pi\rightarrow\pi^{*(2)}$.

Within the high-energy region, bands 16−18 particularly have some ($\sim$5−10%) contribution from $z$-polarized transitions. In the case of band 18, this out-of-plane nature might arise from an admixture of the $Cl(p_{x}/p_{y})\rightarrow d_{z^{2}}$ CT. TD-DFT calculations predict the more strongly allowed $z$-polarized $CT^{(Cl,\sigma)}$ at much higher energy, which, however, is likely an overestimate, considering that similar $Cl\rightarrow$ Fe(III) and thiolate(Cys) $\rightarrow$ Fe(III) $\sigma$ CT transitions are observed in the $26000-29000\ cm^{-1}$ energy region in [Fe(TPP)Cl] and cytochrome P450cam, respectively. $^{2,40}$ We therefore assign the $z$-polarization of bands 16 and 17 to arise from an admixture of $CT^{(Cl,\sigma)}$ (and potentially some $CT^{(Cl,\pi)}$) character.

Comparison of the B3LYP/LanL2DZ* and BHandHLYP/ LanL2DZ* Calculations. Unlike B3LYP that predicts two dominant $xy$-polarized states assigned to the Soret band, BHandHLYP has four corresponding features, calculated at

25319, 25321, 25970, and $26056\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ that account for the very intense band seen in the theoretical spectrum (Figure S7). This is not in agreement with the MCD data, indicating that B3LYP gives overall the best agreement with experiment. These four features comprise mixed $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ and CT transitions. The dominant (~30−40%) CT character present among these features is the forbidden $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{x/y/z})\_\text{d}_{xz/yz}/\text{d}_{z^2} + \text{A}_{2\text{u}} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^2-y^2}\_\text{B}_{1\text{g}}$ transition that gains intensity from the strongly allowed $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ transition. Minor contributions from CT(1) are also observed.

Figure S7C shows an overlay of the experimental and BHandHLYP-derived spectra normalized to their Soret bands, which requires a shift of the BHandHLYP spectrum to lower energy by $5000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. The higher-energy features predicted by BHandHLYP do not reproduce the experimental results well, as they appear too high in energy [$28000$−$32000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (spectral energies adjusted to the Soret band) and $33000$−$37000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ when unadjusted] with too little intensity compared to experiment. Four features with $xy$-polarization are present at 33592, 33611, 34888, and $35553\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. These features contain an admixture of a variety of generally forbidden inner porphyrin and CT transitions with some contributions from $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ transitions. For example, $\text{A}_{1\text{u}}/\text{A}_{2\text{u}} \rightarrow \text{B}_{1\text{u}}, \text{A}_{1\text{u}} \rightarrow \text{d}_{z^2}\_\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{z}), \text{E}_{\text{g}}/\text{B}_{1\text{u}} \rightarrow \text{d}_{x^2 y^2}, \text{Cl}(\text{p}_{y})\_\text{d}_{yz} \rightarrow \text{E}_{\text{g}}$, and Ph (phenyl) $\rightarrow \text{E}_{\text{g}}$ transitions. Minor contributions from allowed transitions such as $\text{CT}^{(5)}$, $\text{CT}^{(7)}$, and $\text{CT}^{(\text{Cl},\pi)}$ are also observed. Interestingly, whereas B3LYP predicts a substantial $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{z})\_\text{d}_{z^2} + \text{A}_{2\text{u}} \rightarrow \text{E}_{\text{g}}$ CT character (~40%) spread throughout the bands in the higher- energy region, this is underestimated at only 3% with BHandHLYP. The major difference between the BHandHLYP and B3LYP calculations therefore is that BHandHLYP predicts a more diverse set of forbidden CT transitions in the high-energy region that gain intensity through $10$−$25\%$ $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ contribution, whereas B3LYP predicts fewer types of more ($65$−$80\%$) allowed CT character with about $5$−$25\%$ $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ contribution (see Table 3). This result therefore supports our conclusion that the $\text{Cl}(\text{p}_{z})\_\text{d}_{z^2} + \text{A}_{2\text{u}} \rightarrow \text{E}_{\text{g}}$ CT is the key transition that is responsible for the split Soret band in [Mn(TPP)(Cl)] (see discussion below), and that the B3LYP result is reliable in this regard.

Beyond the Soret and high-energy bands, it is noteworthy that BHandHLYP shows no intensity in the Q band region. This further highlights how B3LYP provides a better theoretical model for the experimental UV−vis spectrum of [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)- Cl], compared to BHandHLYP and the many other functionals that we tested (Figure S7).

Spectroscopic Effects of the Halide Ligand. By changing the halide ligand in the [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)X] complex, the relative intensities (Boucher's $R$ factor) and energies of the split Soret band should shift and, in this way, provide some explanation on how the axial anion influences the electronic structure and optical properties of the compound. In previous work, a series of $\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$ protoporphyrin IX dimethyl ester (PPIX DME) halide complexes were investigated via IR and UV−vis spectroscopy. $^{37}$ Similarly, we also prepared the fluoro, bromo, and iodo analogues of [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)Cl] to systematically elucidate the effect of the axial halide ligand. The energies of the Soret and Q bands of the [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)X] complexes have been reported, $^{41,42}$ which are consistent with our results (Figure 9). In general, all four [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)X] complexes display a prominent Soret band and broader absorption features in the higher-energy region. In the case of [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)F], the Soret band is located at 458 nm, and a broad feature is observed in the 315−415 nm region with no conspicuous peaks. Compared to the chloro complex, the intensity ratio, $R$, is much higher at 4.50. [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)Br] displays a shift in the Soret band to lower energy (486 nm), and a prominent band is now observed at 381 nm, with two shoulders at 352 and 406 nm. Compared to the chloro complex ($R = 1.96$), the $R$ value is decreased to 1.26. Among the [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)X] compounds, the Soret band of [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)I] occurs at the lowest energy, 498 nm, and this complex displays the most intense high-energy features, located at 388 nm with a subtle shoulder at 412 nm. In the Q band region, a band at 637 nm is observed with a weak shoulder at 596 nm. The most extraordinary feature of [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)I] is the intensity inversion with the high-energy feature now being more intense than the Soret band, producing a low $R$ value of 0.62. Please note that these $R$ values are in close agreement with a previous report; see ref 42. These results imply that as the axial ligand becomes softer, the mixing of the Soret excited state with porphyrin($\pi$) $\rightarrow \text{d}_{\pi}$ and $\text{Cl}(\text{p})\_\text{d} \rightarrow \text{porphyrin}(\pi^{*})$ CT transitions increases, and this causes a shift of the Soret band to lower energy, accompanied by an intensity transfer to the higher-energy feature. This general trend has been observed for other $\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$ porphyrin complexes with variable axial anionic ligands as well. $^{37,38,75}$

Another feature of [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)X] complexes that demon- strates a pattern as a function of the axial halide ligand is the intensity ratio between bands 3 and 5 in the Q band region (or, using the Boucher scheme, the ratio between bands III and IV; see Figure 2). In the case of the Mn porphyrins, complexes that display a ratio of $>1$ have soft axial ligands. The fluoride in [$\text{Mn}^{\text{III}}$(TPP)F] is a hard (=strong field) ligand that tends to elevate the $\text{d}_{z^2}$ orbital, restricting the mixing of $\text{d}_{z^2}$ with the porphyrin $\pi$ orbitals and, in this way, causing increased intensity for band $5.^{42}$ This is an interesting suggestion, as we propose that the split Soret feature in Mn porphyrins is dependent on the mixing between the porphyrin $\text{A}_{2\text{u}}(\pi)$ and the $\text{X}(\text{p}_{z})\_\text{d}_{z^2}$ orbital.

The optimized structure of $[Mn^{III}(TPP) F]$ generates a $d(Mn-F)=2.365 \AA, d(Mn-$  $N($ por $))=2.037 \AA$ , and a displacement of the $Mn$ atom from the basal nitrogen plane of $0.30 \AA$ . For $[Mn^{III}(TPP) Br]$ , the fully optimized structure generates a $d(Mn-Br)=2.548 \AA$ , which isin close agreement with the crystal structure value of $2.490 \AA$ (Table S1). $^{76}$ The calculated average $d(Mn-N($ por $))$ distance is $2.034 \AA$ , which is comparable to $2.008 \AA$ in the crystal structure. Finally, the calculated and experimental displacement of the $Mn$ atom from the basal nitrogen plane is 0.27 and $0.24 \AA$ , respectively. Among the four $[Mn^{III}(TPP) X]$ complexes studied in this work via DFT, the iodo compound therefore demonstrates the weakest $Mn^{III}$ -halide bond and the strongest $Mn^{III}$ -porphyrin inter action, where the latter is also reflected by the fact that[Mn"(TPP)I] has the most porphyrin distortion.

Figure S9 presents an overlay of the TD-DFT calculated spectra of the $F^{-}, Cl^{-}$ , and $Br^{-}$ analogues, in comparison to their experimental data (see also Figure 9). The TD-DFT spectrum of[Mn"(TPP)I] is excluded in the analysis due to the strong relativistic effects expected for the heavy halide, which makes regular DFT calculations unreliable. Whereas all of the complexes display overestimated intensities in the higher-energy region, the calculations qualitatively reproduce the experimental trends. This is noted by a decrease in the $R$ ratio and a shift to lower energy of the Soret and $Q$ bands as the halide becomes softer. Interestingly, TD-DFT also accurately predicts the subtle increase in the intensity of the $Q$ bands in going from the fluoro to the bromo analogue. Taken altogether, the TD-DFT results on the series of $[Mn^{III}(TPP) X]$ complexes further support our interpretation of the optical spectra of $[Mn^{III}(TPP) Cl]$ , using the B3LYP/LanL2DZ* model.

## DISCUSSION
The electronic spectra of "normal" metalloporphyrins are dominated by the distinctive Soret, $Q_{y}$ and $Q_{x}$ bands that arise from $A_{1 u}\langle 79\rangle / A_{2 u}\langle 81\rangle \to E_{g}\langle 82 / 83\rangle(\pi \to \pi^{*(0)})$ excitations, respectively, which undergo configuration interaction (Soret and $Q$ band) and vibronic coupling $(Q_{x})$ to produce the intensity differences between these features. $^{36}$ Deviations from this paradigm are observed for $[Fe^{III}(TPP) Cl]$ and other[Fe"(TPP)(X)] complexes (Figure 3), due to the presence of halide $\to$ hs Fe(III) and other, porphyrin-based CT transitions, which leads to a distinct shoulder on the higher-energy side ofthe Soret band and additional features in the Q band region. $^{2,68}$  In some systems, this subtle shoulder becomes more recognizable as a broad band in their low-temperature MCD spectra, as observed in thiolate-coordinated hs-ferric hemes(cytochrome $P 450_{cam }$ and corresponding model systems). $^{40,77}$  The difference between these hs ferric porphyrin complexes andthe $Mn^{III}$ compounds studied here (and other hs $3 ~d^{4} Mn^{III}$ porphyrin complexes $)^{33,38}$ is that, in the latter case, the Soret state interacts incredibly strongly with CT excited states,leading, for example, to a shift of the Soret band by $\sim 1800$  $cm^{-1}$ between $[Mn^{III}(TPP) F]$ and $[Mn^{III}(TPP) I]$ . For[Mn"(TPP)Cl], the splitting between the resulting mixed states (bands 10/11 and 17) is $\sim 6000 ~cm^{-1}$ . In contrast, in the hs ferric complexes, mixing of the Soret excited state with other CT excited states is also observed, but the resulting energy splittings are much smaller. This indicates that the electronicorigin of these excited-state couplings in hs $Fe^{III}$ and hs $Mn^{III}$  porphyrin complexes is fundamentally different in nature. In this paper, we use MCD spectroscopy, coupled to DFT calculations, to investigate the underlying electronic origin for the unusual properties of $Mn^{III}$ porphyrins. It is important to note that the assignments of the optical bands rely heavily on TD-DFT calculations. As presented in the Results and Analysis section, the high-energy region $(22500-27500 ~cm^{-1})$ displays several CT transitions that are mixed with the $\pi \to \pi^{*}$ transitions, asopposed to the Soret and Q band regions that have dominant $\pi$ → π* character. Therefore, the detailed assignments made in the high-energy region are more speculative.

The mid-energy region shows a prominent pseudo A-type signal assigned to the Soret band (20000–21000 cm⁻¹). The intensely absorbing high-energy features at 22500–28000 cm⁻¹ exhibit CT<sup>(1,2)</sup> and symmetry-forbidden (e.g., Cl(p<sub>x/y</sub>) → d<sub>z</sub>²) CT character mixed with $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0,1,2)}$. In previous studies, Boucher tentatively assigned the optical bands of several Mn<sup>III</sup> porphyrins that he collectively designated to a Roman numeral labeling scheme composed of seven general features (bands I–V, Va, and VI).<sup>33,38</sup> A comparison of his labeling scheme and our band assignments is shown in Figure 2 and Table 4. Interestingly, in the 25000–27000 cm⁻¹ region, he assigned the spectral features to an admixture of CT and $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ transitions, which is generally similar to our findings. However, in contrast to our work, Boucher states that the Soret feature is located at ~26700 cm⁻¹ (band 17 in our assignment), where the lowering and broadening of its intensity may be due to CT admixture into this transition. He instead assigns the prominent derivative signal in the mid-energy region (bands 10 and 11) to CT transitions (instead of the Soret band), which he based on a previous study involving polarized absorption spectroscopy on ferriyoglob-in.<sup>78</sup> In addition, Boucher did not identify the CT<sup>(Cl,σ,π)</sup> features, which admittedly is very difficult in the absence of polarization data for the different optical transitions. Importantly, our data show that bands 10 and 11 are fully xy-polarized, which strongly supports our assignment of these features to the two components of the Soret band.

In the case of [Fe<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl], the 14000–20000 cm⁻¹ spectral region is dominated by allowed porphyrin → d<sub>π</sub> CT mixed with the $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*(0)}$ transition. Unlike its Mn<sup>III</sup> counterpart, the Q<sub>y</sub> band is observed within the same region. The mid-energy region where the Soret band occurs shows $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ transitions, with additional CT<sup>(4)</sup> character. At higher energy, transitions of $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ character with an admixture of the CT<sup>(4,5)</sup> states are observed. The CT<sup>(Cl,π)</sup> transition is spread out over the 16000–22500 cm⁻¹ region, whereas CT<sup>(Cl,σ)</sup> is identified as a single distinct feature located at higher energy relative to the Soret band (at ~26200 cm⁻¹).

The disparities in the absorption spectra of [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] and its Fe<sup>III</sup> counterpart can be understood based on variations in their molecular and electronic structure. Here, the longer Mn–Cl distance suggests a weaker axial ligand bond, which is compensated for by a stronger Mn–N(Porph) interaction. Since in hs Mn(III) the d<sub>x²−y²</sub> orbital is unoccupied [in contrast to hs Fe(III)], this automatically enhances σ-bonding in the xy-plane. Correspondingly, the Mn center is located closer to the mean porphyrin plane compared to Fe (by ~0.12 Å in the TPP<sup>2−</sup> complexes). These cumulative attributes result in a stronger σ-interaction between the d<sub>x²−y²</sub> orbital of Mn and the σ-donating B<sub>1g</sub>⟨80⟩ orbital of the porphyrin. The charge buildup on the metal due to the enhanced σ-interaction combined with the better overlap between the d<sub>π</sub> orbitals and the E<sub>g</sub>(π*) (LUMO) acceptor orbitals of the porphyrin should increase the π-back-donation from Mn into the porphyrin E<sub>g</sub>(π*) orbitals. However, this effect is counteracted by spin-polarization, leading to negligible π-back-bonding in the α-spin MOs. In the case of the β-spin MOs, the d orbitals are unoccupied, leading to strong mixing with the E<sub>g</sub>⟨82/83⟩ orbitals. The resulting d<sub>π</sub>_Cl(p<sub>x/y</sub>) + E<sub>g</sub>⟨82/83⟩ MOs contain more metal character in the Mn<sup>III</sup> (e.g., ~64% Mn and ~25% Porph) compared to the Fe<sup>III</sup> case (59% Fe and ~34% Porph; see Table 2 and ref 2). Importantly, Boucher attributes the anomalous spectrum of Mn<sup>III</sup> porphyrin complexes to this enhanced mixing between d<sub>π</sub> and the E<sub>g</sub> (LUMO) orbitals.<sup>33</sup> Having substantial porphyrin π character within the d<sub>π</sub> level increases the intensity of the CT bands but dampens the $\pi \rightarrow \pi^{*}$ band intensity because the E<sub>g</sub> level is no longer a pure porphyrin orbital. However, [Fe<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl], like other hs Fe<sup>III</sup> porphyrin complexes, does not exhibit such a strong coupling between the Soret and CT states as the hs Mn<sup>III</sup> complex (i.e., no split Soret band—just merely a shoulder to higher energy of the Soret band), yet this complex exhibits a similar orbital mixing between the β-d<sub>π</sub> and the E<sub>g</sub>(π*) orbitals,<sup>2</sup> which made us wonder if this is really the underlying reason for the optical anomalies of [Mn(TPP)(X)] complexes.

In addition to the mixing observed between the metal (d<sub>π</sub>) and porphyrin E<sub>g</sub> (π*) orbitals, [Mn(TPP)(Cl)] also shows mixing between the porphyrin A<sub>2u</sub>(π) and the Cl(p<sub>z</sub>)_d<sub>z</sub>² and/or d<sub>z</sub>² orbitals. This is significant, as many π → π* and porphyrin(π) → Fe-d CT transitions originate from the A<sub>2u</sub>⟨81⟩ orbital. Seven MOs in [Mn<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] show mixed character, with the most significant differences to [Fe<sup>III</sup>(TPP)Cl] being observed for the α-spin A<sub>2u</sub> orbitals, which show distinct Cl(p<sub>z</sub>)_d<sub>z</sub>² character for

Importantly, along our series of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)(X)]$ complexes with $F^{-}$, $Cl^{-}$, $Br^{-}$, and $I^{-}$, this mixing greatly increases as the halide becomes softer ($F^{-}$ to $I^{-}$), as shown in Table 5. In particular, whereas the $\alpha$-HOMO of the chloro complex shows dominant porphyrin character (71% Porph, 10% Cl, 7% Mn), the porphyrin contributions redistribute toward the halide in the bromo analogue (65% Porph, 16% Br, 8% Mn) and does so more drastically for the iodo complex (54% Porph, 28% I, 8% Mn). This trend in MO decompositions therefore follows the experimental trend exactly, where the splitting of the Soret band systematically increases in this series from $F^{-}$ to $I^{-}$, as the polarizability of the anion increases (Figures 9 and 10). In addition, the $R$ value decreases in the order of hard to soft, $F^{-}$ (4.50) $>$ $Cl^{-}$ (1.96) $>$ $Br^{-}$ (1.26) $>$ $I^{-}$ (0.62), which is in agreement with the literature. $^{42,74}$ Interestingly, this further indicates that a $R<1$ (for $TPP^{2-}$ complexes) leads to a switch in the order of the resulting mixed states, where the one at higher energy (330−400 nm range) now shows dominant Soret character, and the one at lower energy has more CT character. An analogous trend is also observed for $[Mn^{III}(PPIX DME)X]$ (halide) complexes, where the $R$ values are $0.74$ ($F^{-}$) $\simeq 0.74$ ($Cl^{-}$) $>0.50$ ($Br^{-}$) $>0.28$ ($I^{-}$). $^{37}$ Based on these findings, we propose that the admixture of $X(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}}$ and $d_{z^{2}}$ character into the $A_{2u}$ orbitals causes the Soret transition $(A_{1u}\langle 79\rangle/A_{2u}\langle 81\rangle \to E_{g}\langle 82/83\rangle;\ \pi \to \pi^{*(0)})$ to directly acquire $X(p_{z})_{-}d_{z^{2}} \to E_{g}\langle 82/83\rangle$ and related CT character, and vice versa, the CT transitions to directly gain Soret character. Therefore, as the mixing between the Soret and CT excited states increases, the intensity lost from the Soret band is redistributed to the CT bands located at higher energy, accounting for their drastic increase in intensity, and the low $R$ values of the complexes with the heavier halides. This mechanism provides a direct orbital pathway that facilitates the strong mixing of the Soret and CT excited states, which constitutes an electronic mechanism by which the strong, unusual coupling between these states is induced in $Mn^{III}$ porphyrins. Table S8 presents key electronic transitions that originate from the mixed MOs of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ described above and that occur in the 330−500 nm region. This region encompasses bands 10−18 (Figure 2). The magnitude of the electronic transitions originating from the ground state mixed MOs that span the 330−500 nm region is extraordinary and has not been observed in other metal porphyrin complexes.

In contrast, in the case of the $Fe^{III}$ analogue, we propose that the mixing of excited states is mainly caused by a simple CI mechanism, which is much weaker and only becomes noticeable when the corresponding excited states are very close in energy. A splitting of the Soret band is therefore only observed when the coupling states are close to degenerate with the Soret state, and even then the resulting splitting of the Soret band is small. This emphasizes again the special role that enhanced orbital interactions play for the spectroscopic properties of transition- metal complexes, for example $[Mn^{III}(TPP)X]$.

Importantly, the Soret splitting is a universal phenomenon among $Mn^{III}$ porphyrin complexes, regardless of the axial ligand. $^{38,75}$ However, the energy of the Soret band and the $R$ ratio are unique to each complex as previously demonstrated for a series of $[Mn^{III}(Etio)(X)H_{2}O]$ compounds where the axial ligand comprises halides and small anions (e.g., $NCO^{-}$, $NCS^{-}$, $N_{3}^{-}$, $C_{2}H_{3}O_{2}^{-}$). $^{38}$ Figure S10 shows a comparison of two $[Mn^{III}(TPP)X]$ complexes ($X = Cl^{-}$ and $C_{2}H_{3}O_{2}^{-}$), revealing again that this splitting effect is not just limited to halides. The Soret band (469 nm) and the $R$ value (2.38) of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)-C_{2}H_{3}O_{2}]$ are consistent with previous reports. Finally, it is interesting to note that, in addition to the axial ligand, the type of porphyrin also influences the $R$ value and the energy of the bands in the Soret region, as reported for a series of $Mn^{III}$ porphyrin aquochloride complexes. $^{33}$ For example, $[Mn^{III}(PPIX DME)-$$(Cl)(H_{2}O)]$ has an $R$ value of 0.75 in chloroform, $^{33}$ which is $\sim$2.5 times lower than that of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ ($R = 1.96$) but comparable to $[Mn^{III}(TPP)I]$ ($R = 0.62$) from our work. The variation of the $R$ values among the series of $[Mn^{III}(Porph)-$$(Cl)(H_{2}O)]$ has been explained based on the extent of Mn porphyrin $\pi$ mixing and/or $\beta$-porphyrin substituents that influence the electronic structure and, hence, the energies of the porphyrin $\pi$ and $\pi^{*}$ orbitals in these complexes. $^{33}$ This is in agreement with our findings.

## CONCLUSIONS

The hs $3d^{4}$ nature of $Mn^{III}$ porphyrins, specifically the strong $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}-}B_{1g}\langle 80\rangle$ orbital interaction, drives the stronger mixing of the d orbitals with the porphyrin compared to the $Cl^{-}$ orbitals, generating atypical spectral features. Here, the dramatic Soret splitting that is unique to hs $Mn^{III}$ porphyrins, in general, and $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$, in particular, arises from the ground-state mixed MOs [e.g., $A_{2u}$, $d_{z^{2}}$, $X(p_{z})$] that provide a strong interaction between the porphyrin $\pi \to \pi^{*}$ and CT excited states. This admixture "spreads" the intensity of the Soret band, causing the high-energy features at $22500-28000\ cm\ ^{-1}$ to be more strongly absorbing at the expense of the intensity of the Soret band. This unique phenomenon becomes more pronounced as the halide axial ligand becomes softer, as is the case in the bromo and iodo analogues of $[Mn^{III}(TPP)Cl]$. The TD-DFT calculated spectra of the fluoro, chloro, and bromo complexes qualitatively reproduce the band shifts in the Soret and Q band regions and intensity changes in the high-energy region of the optical spectra. DFT calculations on these systems reveal a higher degree of mixing between the key orbitals of the complex that cause the split Soret band with increasing mass of the halide. In contrast, in the case of $[Fe^{III}(TPP)Cl]$ and related complexes, the mixing between the porphyrin $\pi \to \pi^{*}$ and CT excited states is largely caused by configuration interaction, which is a weaker effect that provides a much smaller coupling between the relevant excited states. Hence, the resulting band splittings in the hs $Fe^{III}$ complexes are much smaller.
