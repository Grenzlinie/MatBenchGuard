# APPLICATIONS OF THE DV-X$\alpha$ AND MS-X$\alpha$ METHODS IN THE STUDY OF HARDENING MECHANISMS CAUSED BY ION IMPLANTATION INTO METALS

Yinnong LIN, Zhijie LIU, Yi LI and Wenjing WANG
Tianjin Normal University, PR China

Junqing LI
University of Science and Technology of China, PR China

Ion implantation can improve many physical and chemical properties of metal surfaces, such as the hardness, wear and corrosion. Implantation of $N^{+}$ or $C^{+}$ can directly increase hardness thus modifying wear character. Using the MS-X$\alpha$ and DV-X$\alpha$ methods to study the bonds between implanted ions and target atoms, we present a new approach to investigate the micro-mechanisms of hardening. Hardness and wear were tested on pure iron samples implanted with $N^{+}$. Binding energies and electronic states of several phases induced by implantation were calculated. The results show that covalent bonds are established between N and Fe atoms and the binding energies of nitride and carbide are obviously superior to that of the pure iron phase. Experimental results and theoretical considerations lead us to the conclusion that the binding energy is one of the main factors of hardening.

## 1. Introduction

Ion beam bombardment, as a new method of metal surface treatment, has been greatly developed through experimental research. It has been found that implantation of $N^{+}$ or $C^{+}$ can directly increase the hardness, thus modifying the wear behavior [1]. Recent activity on this approach has concentrated on the hardening mechanism. The present mechanism says that solid solution hardening, dislocation pinning and dispersion hardening of precipitates are the main parts of hardening [2]. However, there are some experimental results which cannot be explained by these mechanisms. For example, implantation of $Ne^{+}$ and $Ar^{+}$ should also be beneficial for hardening according to the mechanism, but their hardening effects are rather different from that of $N^{+}$ or $C^{+}$. $Ne^{+}$ and $Ar^{+}$ can even make hardening and wear behavior worse [3,4].

In this paper, hardness and wear of pure iron samples implanted with $N^{+}$ were measured. By using the MS-X$\alpha$ and DV-X$\alpha$ methods to study the bonds between implanted ions and target atoms, we present a new approach to investigate the micromechanism of hardening.

## 3. Calculation
Multiple scattering $X\alpha$(MS-$X\alpha$) method and discrete variational $X\alpha$ (DV-$X\alpha$) method belong to quantum chemistry methods. They were developed from Hartree-Fock theory with $X\alpha$ approximation. The exchange-correlation potential was approximated by statistical local expression and is written as [5]

$$
V_{\mathrm{xc}}(r)=-6 \alpha\left(\frac{3}{8 \pi} \rho(r)\right)^{1 / 3}, \tag{1}
$$

where $\alpha$ is Slater's scaling parameter and $\rho(r)$ is the electron charge density. In the spin unrestricted approaches, a one electron wave equation for spin orbital $\phi_{i}$, with energy $\epsilon_{i}$, is introduced and it has the form (in hartree atomic units)

$$
\left(-\frac{1}{2} \nabla^{2}+V_{\mathrm{c}}+V_{\mathrm{xc}}\right) \phi_{i}=\epsilon_{i} \phi_{i}. \tag{2}
$$

The multiple scattering theory generates approximate solutions to eq. (2), called the "muffin-tin approximation". This is done by approximating the potential as being spherically symmetric in cells surrounding each atom, and outside the "out sphere" that surrounding the entire molecule. The potential in the intersphere region is assumed to be a constant. A self-consistent field procedure is used for the determination of orbitals and one-electron energies [6].

An LCAO wave function is used for the expression of molecular orbitals in the DV-X$\alpha$ procedure. The variational principle leads one to the secular equation

$$HC=ESC,\tag{3}$$

where $E$ and $C$ are the eigenvalue (one-electron orbital energy) and the eigenvector (the set of coefficients for molecular orbits) respectively. The DV-X$\alpha$ method represents the elements of the Hamiltonian and overlap matrices as

$$H_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\omega(r_{k})X_{i}^{*}(r_{k})h(r_{k})X_{j}(k),\tag{4}$$

$$S_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\omega(r_{k})X_{i}^{*}(r_{k})X_{j}(r_{k}),\tag{5}$$

where $r_{k}$ is one of the total $N$ sample points given by the sampling function and $\omega(r_{k})$ is the reciprocal of the sample density. In this procedure eigenvalues and eigenvectors rapidly converge as $N$ is increased, if a reasonable sampling function is used [7].

The analysis of samples implanted with $\mathrm{N}^{+}$or $\mathrm{C}^{+}$showed that there is an $\alpha'$-FeN(C) phase at low dose (less than $10^{17}$ ions/cm²) and with increased dose, the $\gamma'$-Fe₄N(C) phase appears first, then the $\epsilon'$-Fe₃N(C) and $\epsilon'$-Fe₂N(C) phases [8,9]. Their lattice structures are shown in fig. 4. ### 4. Discussion
(1) According to the calculation with the DV-X$\alpha$ method, as shown in table 1, the total overlap population's value (0.7853e) between N and Fe atoms is quite large. In such a case, a strong covalent bond is expected between N and its neighboring Fe atoms.

(2) The binding to the results in table 2 and fig. 5, we find that:
(a) The binding energies of Fe-N and Fe-C phases are obviously superior to that of the pure iron phase.
(b) The binding energies of Fe-N phases are greater than that of Fe-C phases.
(c) The binding energies of $\alpha'$-FeN, $\gamma'$-Fe₄N, $\epsilon'$-Fe₃N and $\epsilon'$-Fe₂N phases gradually increase, similarly to the binding energies of Fe-C phases, as shown in fig. 5.

(3) The covalent bond between N and Fe atoms, and between C and Fe atoms produces strong resistance to the movements of dislocations, thus hardening the metal surface. Being inert elements, Ne and Ar cannot establish bonds with Fe atoms, so they have not such modifying effects as N or C atoms.

(4) Based on the results from the calculations and the experimental results, the variation of binding energies in different Fe-N and Fe-C phases is consistent with that of hardening effects at different implantation doses.

(5) Experimental results and theoretical consideration lead us to the conclusion that the binding energy is one of the main factors of hardening.
