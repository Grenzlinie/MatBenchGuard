# Buckingham potential analysis of intra- and intermolecular energies in carbonyl clusters

## Problem background
Transition metal carbonyl clusters in the solid state adopt specific geometries that arise from a balance between intramolecular ligand–ligand interactions and crystal packing forces. Understanding which factors dominate and whether intermolecular packing can override intramolecular preferences requires quantitative analysis of non‑bonded interaction energies. This task addresses that question by computing partitioned intramolecular (IAM) and total intermolecular (IEM) energies for a set of prototypical carbonyl clusters using an atom‑atom potential model.

## Approach
The approach models non‑bonded atom–atom interactions with a Buckingham‑type potential (exponential repulsion + attractive r⁻⁶ term) using parameters specific to atom pairs involving carbon, oxygen, and a noble‑gas proxy (Kr) for metal atoms. For each cluster of interest, the experimental crystal structure—obtained from the Cambridge Structural Database—is parsed to extract atomic coordinates and symmetry operations. Intramolecular energy (IAM) is computed within a single reference molecule by summing over all non‑bonded pairs, excluding 1‑3 interactions on the same metal, and partitioned into contributions: O–O, O–C, C–C repulsive, C–C attractive, and metal–O. Intermolecular energy (IEM) is computed as the sum of interactions between the reference molecule and all surrounding molecules within a 10 Å cutoff, generated by crystallographic symmetry. For crystals containing multiple independent molecules, each is treated separately. The results are compared across structural isomers to assess the competing roles of intra‑ and intermolecular forces.

## Reproduction target
For the following three clusters:
- Mn₂(CO)₁₀ at room temperature,
- the black isomer of Ir₆(CO)₁₆ (which contains two independent molecules in its crystal),
- the red isomer of Ir₆(CO)₁₆,
perform the energy calculations described in the workflow steps. Report the partitioned IAM components (O–O, O–C, C–C repulsive, C–C attractive, metal–O where available) and total IEM for each species in a structured JSON file. For the red isomer, report a single total C–C IAM instead of splitting into repulsive and attractive parts. Based on the computed values, determine which isomer is favoured by intermolecular packing (lower IEM) and which by intramolecular interactions (lower total IAM).
