## ABSTRACT

The structure and chemical shifts of the carbene complex $(CO)_4FeCF_2$ were investigated at DFT level. The $CF_2$ ligand will occupy an equatorial position in a trigonal bipyramidal iron complex. The carbenic C atom is calculated to be much more deshielded with respect to the CO ligand at both BP86 and B3LYP level. Anisotropies of $^{13}$C chemical shifts of carbenic C can show us the certain direction where the attack of nucleophilic regents may take place. The $^{19}$F NMR chemical shift is predicted to be in the range of 160–180 ppm respect to the standard $CCl_3F$ scale by GIAO calculations.

## 1. Introduction

Metal carbene complexes have remarkable importance as intermediates of many organometallic reactions [1,2]. The nature of the chemical bond between a transition metal (TM) and a carbene fragment $CR_2$ in two types of carbene complexes can be explained by using the singlet or triplet states of the $CR_2$ fragments and the residual as building blocks for the respective complexes. The TM–carbene bond in Fischer-type complex [3,4] is described in terms of donor–acceptor interactions between a $(^1A_1)$ singlet carbene and a singlet metal fragment with $R_2C \to$ TM $\sigma$-donation and $\pi$-back donation $R_2C \leftarrow$ TM. The TM–C bond in Schrock complexes [5–7] is described as a covalent bond between a $(^3B_1)$ triplet carbene and a triplet metal fragment. Although many TM carbene complexes can easily be identified to belong to one of the two classes, some species are difficult to classify [8–10]. For example, dihalocarbenes have singlet ground state and large singlet $\to$ triplet excitation energy [11,12] and thus donor–acceptor bonding should be exclusively formed in their respective TM complexes, i.e. they are typical Fischer complexes. However, they may in fact exhibit either nucleophilic or electrophilic behavior at the carbene center indicating no strict separation between two classes [13].

In the past decades, a lot of experimental techniques have been applied to characterize carbene complexes. NMR spectroscopy is the most important analytical method for monitoring and controlling the proceeding of the syntheses and providing the first indications to which class of compounds the molecule belongs. However, because of the high activity of carbene complexes, it is often difficult to isolate the monomer, determine the structure parameters and characterize the nature of the complex. Iron tetracarbonyl complex with fluorine substituted carbene ligand may be a good example for illustrating this case [14]. As all attempts to directly observe and designate NMR spectroscopy were unsuccessful, theoretical calculations may be helpful to predict the electronic structure.

## 2. Computational methods

Geometries of $(CO)_4FeCF_2$ and related complexes $Fe(CO)_5$ and $Fe(CO)_4$ were optimized at the BP86 [15,16] level with the help of Gaussian 98 program [17]. Vibrational analyses were done at the same level. CCSD(T) [18–20] calculations were carried out with MOLPRO2000 [21] using the BP86-optimized geometries. In CCSD(T) calculations, triple excitations were taken into consideration non-iteratively and core orbitals were excluded from the active space. Three kinds of basis set systems (denoted as BS-A, BS-B, and BS-C, **Table 1**) were used in the work. A small-core ECP with a (441/2111/41) valence basis set for Fe [22] and 6-31G(d) basis sets [23–25] for other atoms have been employed in the geometry optimizations and following frequency calculation. This is Frenking group's standard basis set II [26] and called BS-A. Both BS-B and BS-C use ECP of the Stuttgart group [27] with larger valence basis set for Fe (311111/2111/411). ## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Geometries and bond dissociation energy

The optimized geometries of the carbene complex $(CO)_4FeCF_2$ (1), the relevant parent complex $Fe(CO)_5$ (2) and the dissociation fragment $Fe(CO)_4$ (3) are shown in Fig. 1. All energy related calculations are carried out at the CCSD(T)/BS-A//BP86/BS-A level if not specialized in the text and results are collected in Table 2.

As we know, the $CF_2$ ligand may occupy either an axial or equatorial position in a trigonal bipyramidal iron complex corresponding to $Fe(CO)_5$. The only isomer of 1 at the BP86/BS-A level is the equatorial one in $C_{2v}$ symmetry (1b). The axial form where the ligand $CF_2$ is eclipsed to one equatorial CO is a transition state. Further optimization could not locate another stationary point where the ligand $CF_2$ accepts a staggered form respective to one of the equatorial CO group. Therefore, it is reasonable that 1a is 4.2 kcal/mol less stable than 1b, which means that the potential energy surface of 1 is very flat.

The calculated $Fe-C_{carbene}$ dissociation energy with respect to singlet $Fe(CO)_4$ (3a) is $D_0 = 63.8$ kcal/mol, which is about 19 kcal/mol stronger than that of Fe-C in its parent complex $Fe(CO)_5$ (2) (Table 2) for which the $D_0$ value is comparable with experimental data [35]. It is reasonable to deduce that $D_0$ is slightly overestimated ($\sim$3 kcal/mol) for the carbene complex 1b at the highest theoretical level of the work, CCSD(T). The energy difference between singlet (3a) and triplet iron tetracarbonyl (3b) is negligible as it is only 0.2 at the BP86/BS-A//BP86/BS-A level (not given in Table 2 for clarity) and 7.7 kcal/mol at the CCSD(T)/BS-A//BP86/BS-A level.

The geometry of the fragment $Fe(CO)_4$ in the complex 1b is more similar to singlet 3a than to triplet 3b. The free ligand $CF_2$ is calculated to be 1.330 Å for the C-F bond length and $104.0^\circ$ for the F-C-F angle. These values are in good agreement with experimental results [36]. The calculated geometry of the $CF_2$ group changes only little ($0.009$ Å and $0.9^\circ$) on going from the free ligand to complex 1b. Hence, $(CO)_4FeCF_2$ (1b) may be explained as a combination of singlets $Fe(CO)_4$ and $CF_2$ and has the character of Fischer carbene complex.

There are no experimental results available to make a direct comparison of the Fe-carbene distance with the theoretical value. One might compare the calculated value of 1b with the complex $(CO)_4Fe=C=C=C[C(tBu)_2OC(O)O]$ [37], where the ligand is equatorial. A rather short bond distance (1.80 Å) was observed in experiment. The calculated Fe-C bond length of 1b is 1.827 Å.

Surely, vibrational frequencies and IR intensities of 1b may help to characterize the possible monomer complex experimentally too. For simplicity, detailed calculations results are however referred to the work by one of us somewhere else [38].

### 3.2. The $^{13}$C and $^{19}$F NMR chemical shifts

The calculated $^{13}$C and $^{19}$F NMR chemical shifts of 1b are given in Table 3. The relevant data of 2 are also collected for comparison. Due to the dynamical rearrangement of the axial and equatorial carbonyl groups only one signal is recorded for 2 in $^{13}$C NMR experiment. The calculated GIAO values (average of axial and equatorial ones) at the DFT level with the biggest basis set BS-C are $\sim$20 (BP86//BP86/BS-A) and 35 (B3LYP//BP86/BS-A) ppm higher than the experimental value [39] (Table 3). Similar results were obtained in Simion and Sorensen's quantum mechanical study [40]. The computational error in this work is acceptable because of the moderate basis set. By enlarging the basis set on going from BS-A to BS-C, the calculated GIAO-NMR values converge well. With 2 as a reference (assigned to 210 ppm same as experimental value), the estimated $^{13}$C NMR values of 1b with BS-C are given in parentheses (Table 3), which should be in better agreement with experimental data.

Most iron complexes with a formal $\text{Fe=C}$ bond have low-field $^{13}\text{C}$ resonance [41]. Even lower field $^{13}\text{C}$ resonances (above 300 ppm) than the values given in Table 3 were reported [42] for an iron complex where two tri- methyl phosphite ligands were axial. Note that, the experimental values of carbene compounds cited in Table 3 (column 9) have dif- ferent ligands.

Further investigation was made by examining the calculated anisotropies of $^{13}\text{C}$ chemical shifts of carbenic C in **1b**. Table 4 shows that the component of the $^{13}\text{C}$ chemical shift tensor, which is parallel to the $\text{Fe}-\text{C}_\text{carbene}$ bond of the complex **1b** (ZZ), is shielded more than other components. In the direction of the $\text{p}(\pi)$ orbital of carbenic carbon atom (XX), the shield effect is not as significant as that in the ZZ direction, but it is still $\sim$300 (BP86/BS-C) and 350 (B3LYP/BS-C) ppm stronger than the YY com- ponent where the attack of nucleophilic regents may take place, again indicating the character of Fischer carbene complex.

The $^{19}\text{F}$ NMR of **1b** was calculated with the GIAO approach, the improved estimations are given in parentheses in Table 3. HF is chosen as secondary reference which was optimized at the same theoretical level as for **1b**. The calculated $^{19}\text{F}$ NMR value of **1b** respective to HF is converted to the standard $\text{CCl}_3\text{F}$ scale of 214 ppm [43-46].

$$
\delta(^{19}\text{F of 1b})=\sigma(^{19}\text{F of 1b})-\sigma(^{19}\text{F of HF})-214\text{ ppm} \tag{1}
$$

In this way, the calculated $^{19}\text{F}$ NMR chemical shift for **1b** with BS-C is about $\delta$180 ppm (BP86) and $\delta$163 ppm (B3LYP), respectively.

## 4. Conclusion

We have performed the calculations of structure and chemical shifts of the carbene complex $(\text{CO})_4\text{FeCF}_2$ at DFT level. The $\text{CF}_2$ li- gand was calculated to occupy an equatorial position in a trigonal bipyramidal iron complex. The carbenic C atom is calculated to be much more deshielded ($\sim$-40 ppm) with respect to the CO ligand at DFT level. Anisotropies of $^{13}\text{C}$ chemical shifts of carbenic C can show us the certain direction where the attack of nucleophilic re- gents may take place. The $^{19}\text{F}$ NMR chemical shift of the complex $(\text{CO})_4\text{FeCF}_2$ (**1b**) is predicted to be in the range of 160-180 ppm re- spect to the standard $\text{CCl}_3\text{F}$ scale by GIAO calculations
