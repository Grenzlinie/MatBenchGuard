# THE DETERMINATION OF THE CATION DISTRIBUTION IN OLIVINE FROM SINGLE CRYSTAL MÖSSBAUER STUDIES

By J. F. DUNCAN* and J. H. JOHNSTON*

[Manuscript received 17 July 1972]

## Abstract
Mössbauer absorption spectra of different orientations of single crystals of olivine $(MgFe)_2SiO_4$ have been determined. The variation of the ratio of peak areas of the quadrupole split lines has allowed the M(1) and M(2) site positions of the $Fe^{2+}$ ions to be identified, and has shown that the $Fe^{2+}$ ions are evenly distributed between these sites.

## INTRODUCTION
The Mössbauer spectrum of high-spin $Fe^{2+}$ is invariably a quadrupole split doublet. The lines are of equal intensity only for a randomly oriented polycrystalline sample, and provided the anisotropy in the recoilless fraction is not significant. $^{1}$ For a single crystal absorber, the peak area ratio of the two quadrupole arms is dependent upon the angle the incoming gamma ray makes with the electric field gradient (EFG) at the Mössbauer nucleus. The EFG may be represented as a traceless tensor of second rank which is completely specified by five independent parameters. $^{2}$ Three of these locate the principal EFG axes $x_i$, $y_i$, and $z_i$. $V_{xx}$, $V_{yy}$, and $V_{zz}$ are the corresponding components of the EFG. By convention $z_i$ is chosen as the major axis, making $V_{zz}$ the major axis component with $V_{xx}$ and $V_{yy}$ being the minor axes components.

The EFG arises from the effects of the electronic environment of the Mössbauer nucleus and from the charges in the surrounding lattice. The latter is of greater importance in ionic lattices and this feature allows site identification when the overall crystal structure is known.

In olivine there are two distinct octahedral metal ion sites M(1) and M(2) with slightly differing distortions. $^{3}$ A recent X-ray structural study of four natural olivines $^{3}$ did not show any cation ordering in these sites within experimental error. Also earlier Mössbauer studies $^{4}$ on powdered olivines showed a single quadrupole split spectrum. This indicated that the $Fe^{2+}$ ions were either all in one site, or that the

Recently Busch et al.⁵ showed that for powdered olivines at elevated temperatures the high energy peak could be partly resolved into two components. The lower energy peak could not be resolved. From the least-squares fits and calculated peak areas, they determined the distribution of $Fe^{2+}$ between the two sites to be almost identical. However, they also mention that their results should be interpreted with caution as the strong overlap of the high-velocity peaks and the complete overlap of the low velocity peaks, resulted in the least-squares fit being not quite statistically acceptable.

By the use of polarized optical spectra Burns⁶ has recently determined, to an accuracy of 5-10%, the site populations of various olivine minerals in the forsterite- fayalite and fayalite-tephroite series.

Since in both the M(1) and M(2) sites the cation is surrounded at similar distances by six oxygen atoms, the electronic environment for both sites is very similar. We therefore make the reasonable assumption that any differences in the orientation of the principal axes of the EFG can be attributed to the differences in spatial arrange- ments of the six oxygen atoms surrounding each site.

In this paper Mössbauer methods have been used on single crystals of olivine to determine the orientation of the principal axes of the EFG, the sign of the EFG for each octahedral site, and the distribution of $Fe^{2+}$ ions between the M(1) and M(2) sites.

## EXPERIMENTAL

Single crystals of olivine (approximately 1·0 by 0·8 by 0·8 cm) from Cape Bird, Antarctica, were used. The unit cell dimensions of these crystals determined from single crystal rotation and Weissenberg X-ray photographs are $a=4.78\mathring{A}$, $b=10.22\mathring{A}$, and $c=5.96\mathring{A}$ (all subject to an uncertainty of $\pm0.02\mathring{A}$). These are almost identical with those obtained by Birle et al.³ for an olivine of composition $Mg_{1.80}Fe_{0.20}SiO_{4}$. The space group was similarly determined to be $Pbnm$.

The olivine, analysed by X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy, was found to have the composition $Fe_{0.163}Mg_{1.844}Ca_{0.004}SiO_{4}$.

The crystals were mounted on a goniometer head and the crystal axes located by X-ray methods, using rotation and Weissenberg photographs. Each crystal was then glued in a known orientation to a block of wood using Araldite as an adhesive. Initial slices of approximately 2 mm thick were cut parallel to the 021 and 100 planes using a diamond saw. The overall error in the aligning process was estimated as $\pm0.5^{\circ}$. For the thickness against intensity studies these initially thick samples were ground on a corundum wheel to an accuracy of $\pm0.01$ mm. Two different orientations were chosen to check whether thickness effects were isotropic, and to determine the optimum absorber thickness for further study.⁷

Mössbauer spectra of each orientation were recorded on a constant acceleration Mössbauer spectrometer⁸ with a 10-mCi $^{57}$Co source in a copper matrix. Unless otherwise stated all measure- ments were made at room temperature. The velocities were calibrated using sodium nitroprusside assuming the quadrupole arms of this material occur at $-1\cdot363$ and $+0\cdot349$ mm/s respectively.

Lorentzian curves were fitted to the envelope of the experimental points by minimizing the $\chi^{2}$ function.⁹ By independently varying the intensity, position, and linewidth of each peak, together with the off-resonance count, the best values for these parameters were computed. The resulting

## THEORETICAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Calculation of EFG
If $A_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $A_{\mathrm{L}}$ are the areas of the high- and low-energy peaks of the quadrupole split Mössbauer spectrum, then for a monochromatic unpolarized $\gamma$-ray source and a single crystal absorber with $n$ crystallographically equivalent sites per unit cell (i.e. those sites in which the surroundings of the absorbing nuclei are the same, but differ in orientation) the area ratio $A_{\mathrm{H}} / A_{\mathrm{L}}$ is given by$^{11}$

$$
A_{\mathrm{H}} / A_{\mathrm{L}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\theta_{i} \phi_{i}\right) f^{\prime}\left(\theta_{i} \phi_{i}\right) \bigg/ \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_{\mathrm{L}}\left(\theta_{i} \phi_{i}\right) f^{\prime}\left(\theta_{i} \phi_{i}\right)
$$

where $p_{\mathrm{H}}(\theta_{i} \phi_{i})$ is the relative angular-dependent absorption probability for the high energy transition; similarly for $p_{\mathrm{L}}(\theta_{i} \phi_{i})$. $f'(\theta_{i} \phi_{i})$ is the Lamb-Mössbauer fraction for the absorbing nucleus in the $(\theta_{i} \phi_{i})$ direction. $\theta_{i}$ and $\phi_{i}$ are the polar and azimuthal angles of the incident unpolarized beam with respect to the principal axes $x_{i}$, $y_{i}$, and $z_{i}$ of the EFG (see Fig. 1).

![](./images/811747508714733569_1.jpg)

Fig. 1.—Orientation of the principal axes of the electric field gradient $(x_{i}, y_{i}, z_{i})$ with respect to the crystallographic axes $(a, b, c)$.

Using the fact that the $14 \cdot 4$ keV transition of $^{57}\mathrm{Fe}$ is a magnetic dipole, $^{12}$ Zory$^{11}$ showed that the relative absorption probabilities $p_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{L}}$ are given by

$$
p_{\mathrm{H}}(\theta_{i} \phi_{i})=4[(3+\eta^{2})/3]^{1/2}+(3 \cos^{2} \theta_{i}-1+\eta \sin^{2} \theta_{i} \cos 2 \phi_{i})
$$

$$
p_{\mathrm{L}}(\theta_{i} \phi_{i})=4[(3+\eta^{2})/3]^{1/2}-(3 \cos^{2} \theta_{i}-1+\eta \sin^{2} \theta_{i} \cos 2 \phi_{i})
$$

where the asymmetry parameter

$$
\eta=(V_{xx}-V_{yy})/V_{zz}
$$

such that $0 < \eta \leqslant 1$.

In order to relate the angles $\theta_{i}$ and $\phi_{i}$ to the angles $\Theta$ and $\Phi$ between the $\gamma$-ray direction and the $a$, $b$, and $c$ crystallographic axes (see Fig. 1) we use the relations:

$$\cos(\gamma.x_{i})=\sin\theta_{i}\cos\phi_{i}=\sin\Theta\cos\Phi(b.x_{i})+\sin\Theta\sin\Phi(c.x_{i})+\cos\Theta(a.x_{i})$$

$$\cos(\gamma.y_{i})=\sin\theta_{i}\sin\phi_{i}=\sin\Theta\cos\Phi(b.y_{i})+\sin\Theta\sin\Phi(c.y_{i})+\cos\Theta(a.y_{i})$$

$$\cos(\gamma.z_{i})=\cos\theta_{i}=\sin\Theta\cos\Phi(b.z_{i})+\sin\Theta\sin\Phi(c.z_{i})+\cos\Theta(a.z_{i})$$

where $(\gamma.x_{i})...$ is the angle between the incoming $\gamma$-ray direction and the $x$-axis of the EFG for the $i$th site, and $(a.x_{i})...$ is the direction cosine of the angle between the crystallographic axis and the corresponding axis of the EFG for the $i$th site. These expressions differ slightly from those of Danon and Innarella, $^{13}$ and Zory$^{11}$ as they did not use a conventional right-handed crystallographic axis system.

The assumption that $f'$ is isotropic appears to be valid in the case of olivine, as we observed no measurable change in the Mössbauer parameters between spectra recorded at room and liquid air temperatures. This is supported by the work of Busch et al.$^{5}$ from which they concluded for fayalite over a temperature range of $210^{\circ}$ that $f'M(1)/f'M(2)\simeq1\cdot0$. By using the symmetry relations of reflection and rotation (neglecting translational effects as they are unimportant here) and assuming that $f'$ is isotropic, we can calculate the area ratio $A_{\text{H}}/A_{\text{L}}$ for a particular orientation of the sample with respect to the incoming $\gamma$-ray. With an assumed orientation of the principal axes of the EFG with respect to the crystallographic axes, calculated values of $A_{\text{H}}/A_{\text{L}}$ are compared with the experimental values to determine if the initial estimates of the direction cosines were correct. By making a series of estimates until self-consistent values are obtained, the sign and orientation of the EFG may be determined.

From the crystal structure of olivine$^{3}$ (see Fig. 2) we see that the configuration of the six nearest neighbour oxygen atoms surrounding the M(1) cation is a nearly regular axially elongated octahedron, with the cation centrally located in the rhomboid median plane. The point symmetry of this site is $C_{i}$ but can reasonably be approximated to $D_{4h}$ with the O(3b)-M(1)-O(3) bond axis as the tetrad axis.

The oxygen coordination polyhedra surrounding the M(2) site is much more irregular with a point symmetry of $C_{s}$, which may however be regarded as $C_{3v}$ with the triad axis parallel to $b$ and the cation offset along this axis.

Within the unit cell there are four different orientations of each of the M(1) and M(2) octahedra.$^{3}$ Neglecting translational effects the four M(1) sites are symmetrically related by reflection planes parallel to the 001, 100, and 010 crystallographic planes respectively. Similarly the four M(2) sites are related by twofold rotation axes parallel to the $a$, $b$, and $c$ crystallographic axes respectively.

If a site has a rotation axis of threefold or higher symmetry then the EFG is axially symmetric and completely specified by its major axis component $V_{zz}$. The minor axes components $V_{xx}$ and $V_{yy}$ are equal, so that the asymmetry parameter is zero.$^{14}$

For the M(1) site, $V_{zz}$ is therefore expected to be close to the O(3b)-M(1)-O(3) axis. However, since this is not a true fourfold axis $V_{xx}$ and $V_{yy}$ will not be equal and

If so, they could *either* lie along the O(1)-M(1)-O(1) and O(2)-M(1)-O(2) axes *or* be evenly disposed between them (since all the O-M(1)-O bonds are linear and the M(1) site symmetry is $C_i$).

For the more irregular M(2) site the orientations of the principal axes of the EFG are difficult to predict. One would, at first sight, expect $V_{zz}$ to lie close to the approximate threefold rotation axis parallel to the $b$ crystallographic axis with $V_{xx}$ and $V_{yy}$ being located somewhere in a plane parallel to the 010 crystallographic plane. Alternatively, $V_{zz}$, $V_{xx}$, and $V_{yy}$ could each lie along one of the three O-M(2)-O axes. However, the O-M(2)-O bonds are not quite linear so that it is difficult to make any firmly based assumption about the precise direction of the EFG in this case.

Nevertheless, with each of these four assumptions, values of $A_{\text{H}}/A_{\text{L}}$ were calculated using the self-consistent procedure described above and the resulting values were then compared with experiment.

### Effect of Thickness
For quantitative measurements of Mössbauer peak absorption areas and ratios it is necessary to eliminate effects of absorber thickness. The relation between thickness and intensity of absorption was studied for two different sample orientations

and the results are shown in Figure 3. The position of maximum percentage absorption occurs at the same thickness within experimental error for the two orientations. Also for both cases $A_{\mathrm{H}} / A_{\mathrm{L}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}$ vary with thickness in the same way indicating anisotropy to be insignificant. The value of $A_{\mathrm{H}} / A_{\mathrm{L}}$ appears to reach a constant value at and below the position of maximum absorption. All experimental work was done with absorber thickness of $0 \cdot 63 \pm 0 \cdot 01$ mm so as to eliminate error from variable absorption thicknesses.

The variation of linewidth with thickness in Figure 3 is similar to that deduced theoretically by Lang, $^{15}$ and by Margulies and Erhman. $^{16}$ Lang also shows that spectral line areas saturate less rapidly than do peak heights with increasing thickness, since as the absorber thickness increases the line shape has a large Gaussian component. For thin absorbers the line shape becomes Lorentzian.

Thickness effects can have a profound influence on the experimental results. $^{17}$ Previous authors have frequently neglected this variable. Although Danon and Innarella $^{13}$ have shown that $A_{H}/A_{L}$ tends to the theoretical value for thin absorbers, Chandra and Puri $^{18}$ ignored thickness effects in studies of single crystals of $Fe(NH_{4}SO_{4})_{2},6H_{2}O$ and $FeSO_{4},7H_{2}O$. On the other hand, Garg and Puri $^{19}$ used absorbers of less than 0·2 mm as an approximation to zero thickness. Our results, however, demonstrate that for precise absorption area measurements an investigation of this variable is essential.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Typical spectra with computer-fitted Lorentzian curves are shown in Figure 4. From such data the values of $A_{H}/A_{L}$ in Table 1 were calculated. There is very little change in this peak area ratio over the 17 orientations studied.

Although the data in Table 1 are statistically acceptable $(\chi^{2}=260)$ the higher-energy peak always had a larger linewidth than the lower-energy peak, suggesting the presence of at least two closely overlapping $Fe^{2+}$ spectra. Attempts were made to resolve each experimental spectrum into two component overlapping spectra, but these were unsuccessful due to the extensive overlap. This conforms with the work of Bancroft et al. $^{20}$ who were unable to resolve the individual peaks when their positions differed by less than 0·1 mm/s.

For all spectra the quadrupole splitting was $\Delta E_{Q}=3\cdot07\pm0\cdot02$ mm/s, and the isomer shift $\delta=+0\cdot95\pm0\cdot02$ mm/s.

Theoretical values of $A_{H}/A_{L}$ were recalculated assuming the $Fe^{2+}$ to be either all in the M(1) site or all in the M(2) site. No correlation between theoretical and experimental values could be found in these cases for any feasible orientation of the principal axes of the EFG.

Good agreement between the theoretical and experimental values of $A_{\mathrm{H}} / A_{\mathrm{L}}$ could only be obtained under the following conditions and orientations of the principal axes of the EFG for the M(1) and M(2) sites, and provided there are equal proportions of $\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}$ in these sites.

(i) $M(1)$ site.-The major axis component $V_{z z}$ lies along the approximate fourfold rotation axis O(3b)-M(1)-O(3). Because of the effect of symmetry, the minor axes components $V_{x x}$ and $V_{y y}$ could lie along the $\mathrm{O}(1)-\mathrm{M}(1)-\mathrm{O}(1)$ axis and $\mathrm{O}(2)-\mathrm{M}(1)-\mathrm{O}(2)$ axis respectively, or vice versa, as these two are indistinguishable theoretically. The asymmetry parameter has a value of about $0 \cdot 2$.

Since the area ratio is $A_{\mathrm{H}} / A_{\mathrm{L}}$ and not $A_{\mathrm{L}} / A_{\mathrm{H}}$, the sign of the electric field gradient parameter $q$ is positive.

(ii) $M(2)$ site.-Because of the $C_{\mathrm{s}}$ point symmetry of this site it is not possible to distinguish between $V_{z z}$ and $V_{x x}$ thus giving rise to the two following circumstances which have identical values of $A_{\mathrm{H}} / A_{\mathrm{L}}$:

(A) The major axis component $V_{z z}$ lies along the $\mathrm{M}(2)-\mathrm{O}(3 \mathrm{c})$ bond axis with $V_{x x}$ along the $\mathrm{M}(2)-\mathrm{O}(3 \mathrm{~d})$ axis and $V_{y y}$ along the $\mathrm{M}(2)-\mathrm{O}(1)$ axis.

(B) $V_{z z}$ lies along $\mathrm{M}(2)-\mathrm{O}(3 \mathrm{~d})$ bond axis with $V_{x x}$ along the $\mathrm{M}(2)-\mathrm{O}(3 \mathrm{c})$ axis and again $V_{y y}$ lies along the $\mathrm{M}(2)-\mathrm{O}(1)$ axis.

In both cases $q$ is positive, and the asymmetry parameter is about $0 \cdot 4$ which is, as expected, greater for the more distorted site. The calculated data based on these assumptions are compared with experiment in Table 1 and Figure 5.

This overall conclusion is in agreement with that of Burns,⁶ obtained on the basis of the interpretation of the optical spectra of olivines by means of crystal field theory.
