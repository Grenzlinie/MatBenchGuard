# The Role of Histidine in the Ligand-Bonding Capacity of the Hemoglobin Gene

T. A. Romanova¹,²,*, P. O. Krasnov², A. A. Kuzubov², and P. V. Avramov²

¹ Institute of Computational Modeling, Siberian Division, Russian Academy of Sciences, Akademgorodok, Krasnoyarsk, 660036 Russia
² Kirenskii Institute of Physics, Siberian Division, Russian Academy of Sciences, Akademgorodok, Krasnoyarsk, 660036 Russia

Received September 11, 2002; in final form, February 19, 2003

**Abstract**—The atomic and electronic structures of heme complexes with His, Gly, and Cys residues (Heme–His, Heme–Gly, and Heme–Cys) in the fifth coordination position of the Fe atom and with oxygen and nitrogen oxide molecules in the sixth Fe position were studied by the semiempirical quantum-chemical method PM3. A comparative analysis of internuclear distances showed that the strength of chemical bonding between the ligand molecules (oxygen and nitrogen oxide) is greater for Heme–Cys than for Heme–His and Heme–Gly complexes. Consequently, the strengthening of the chemical bond of the oxygen (or nitrogen oxide) molecule with Heme–Cys substantially weakens the chemical bond in the ligand molecule. The Mulliken population analysis showed that the electronic density of ligand (oxygen or nitrogen oxide) *p*-orbitals is transferred to the *d*-orbitals of the Fe atom, whose charge, calculated according to the Mulliken analysis, formally becomes negative. In the Heme–His complex with oxygen, this charge is substantially greater than in the complex with NO, and the oxygen molecule becomes polarized. No oxygen polarization is observed in the Heme–Cys complex, and the electron density (judging from the change in the Fe charge) is transferred to the coordinated sulfur atom. This is also characteristic of Heme–Cys complexes with nitrogen oxide. An analysis of charges on the atoms indicates that the character of chemical bonding of the oxygen molecule in Heme–Cys and Heme–Gly complexes is similar and basically differs from that in the case of the Heme–His complex.

**Key words**: electronic structure, hemoglobin, heme, ligands, PM3 method, proximal amino acid residues

## INTRODUCTION

The functionally major structure of cellular hemoproteins (hemoglobin, myoglobin, cytochromes, catalase, and peroxidase) is heme, which is a chelate complex of protoporphyrin with Fe(II). Four nitrogen atoms are linked with Fe in this complex, and the remaining fifth and sixth coordination bonds are oriented perpendicularly to the plane of porphyrin ring [1]. In the molecules of hemoglobin and myoglobin, imidazole rings of two residues of His, a dibasic amino acid histidine, are located on the two sides of the heme plane. The nitrogen atom of proximal His residue forms a covalent bond with the heme iron in the fifth coordination position. The distal His residue is located at some distance from the heme iron and forms a pocket available for ligands (O₂, NO, and CO) (Fig. 1).

The His residue coordinated in the fifth coordination position of the heme Fe atom plays the leading role in the function of hemo(myo)globin, and its replacement by another amino acid residue exerts a substantial effect on the ligand-binding and catalytic activities of a hemoproteins molecule. For example, the substitution (by directed mutagenesis) of Cys for proximal His considererably enhances the ability of hemoproteins to heterolysis of O–O bond in cumene hydroperoxide [2]. The results of exchange of amino acid residues in the myoglobin structure on the kinetics of carbon(II) oxide molecule addition are described in [3, 4]. The exchange of amino acids may occur in each part of protein, including the proximal His residue. In this case, both amino acids (e.g., glycine [5, 6]) and heterocyclic molecules (pyridine, furan, thiophene [7], and methyl-substituted imidazoles [8]) may be the ligands. Substantial changes in absorption and NMR spectra of proteins result in this case.

The study of properties of mutant forms of hemoproteins is carried out by both experimental and theoretical methods [12–15]; the latters are based on quantum chemical calculations of model systems using various variants of Hartree–Fock approach and the theory of density potential, which seems to be quite warrantable for the prediction of properties of newly obtained compounds.

# THE CALCULATION TECHNIQUE

The nearest amino acid environment of heme was not considered at the calculations of electronic structure, since the goal of this work was the study of the role of the amino acid residue coordinated to Fe atom in the fifth position in the binding NO and $O_2$. The functional activity of biological molecules is mainly due to the structure of molecular orbitals of the current in valent zone. In hemoproteins, the nature of chemical bond is determined by the contributions of atoms of porphyrin ring and ligand in the fifth coordination position, whereas the amino acids nearest to it do not contribute to this energetic area [15]. In this connection, we used simplified model systems from a minimal number of atoms. Preliminary comparative calculations of the heme by semiempiric methods and a nonempiric method of Hartree–Fock with the use of standard atomic basis 6-31G showed a good consent and adequacy of use of semiempiric methods for the description of atomic electron structure in respect of geometry, configuration of densities of states of orbitals, and the energy levels of the higher occupied and lower vacant molecular orbitals [15, 16].

In this work, the atomic and electron structures of heme complexes with ligands were investigated by a semiempiric quantum-chemical method PM3 [17, 18]. The starting geometries of model systems were taken from PDB (Protein Databank, www.rcsb.org/pdb/). Within the frames of PM3, structures were optimized with the precision of 1 cal/mol using the method of conjugated gradients (Polak–Ribiere).

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

## 1. Geometry of Complexes

We calculated the heme complexes with His, Gly, or Cys amino acid residues in the fifth coordination position and with the molecules of oxygen or nitrogen oxide in the sixth position. Oxygen molecule was shown to be coordinated with the heme–His complex at an angle to the heme plane, whereas the heme–Cys complex coordinates it parallel to it. The distance between the Fe atom and the nearest O atom of the oxygen molecule in the heme–His is $1.815$ Å, whereas in the heme–Cys the actual distance to the oxygen molecule is substantially less ($1.706$ Å) due to the means of its coordination (Fig. 2). ### 2. The Mulliken Atomic Charges of the Heme Complexes with Ligands

The attachment of ligand (both $O_2$ and NO) to heme iron according to donor-acceptor mechanism results in a change in the effective charge of Fe atom calculated by Mulliken. In this case, the electronic density is transferred from the ligand $p$-orbitals to the vacant $d$-orbitals of Fe, whose charge becomes negative. This artefact is probably due to a substantial charge of oxygen (and NO too) on the nonbinding orbitals (the orbitals are filled), which begin to formally belong to the Fe $d$-orbitals.

Starting from the val- ues of the total charge of the ligand molecule, one can conclude that the oxygen molecule is polarized, because its total charge is close to zero, whereas, in the NO molecule, it is approximately equal to +0.3, which indicates a considerable redistribution of charge from NO to porphyrin plane. The NO molecule is covalently bound to the heme Fe, whereas only weak ion–dipole interactions are formed between oxygen and Fe [19]. In this case, the electron density on nitrogen of the imidazole ring substantially decreased indepen- dently of the ligand (O₂ or NO).

Oxygen molecule acquires almost no polarization in the heme–Cys complex, and the electron density is dis- placed to the coordinated sulfur atom judging from the change in the Fe charge. The displacement of electron density from Fe also bears the same character in the heme–Cys complexes with nitrogen oxide. An analysis of charges on atoms shows that the character of chemi- cal binding of oxygen molecule in the complexes with Cys and Gly is similar and basically differs from that in the case of His.

The p-orbitals of nitrogen or sulfur atom participate in the formation of coordination bond of amino acid with the heme Fe atom. In particular, the nature of the higher occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) determines the stability of this chemical bond. A comparison of partial electronic density of p-states in the HOMO of ligand atom (either one of nitrogen atoms or a sulfur atom) helped reveal that it is maximal for histidine in comparison with this value for all other amino acids participating in the formation of protein chain [20]. For example, histidine has a partial density of p-states of the imidazole ring nitrogen, which provide the coordi- nation, of 0.93 (relative units), whereas arginine (also a dibasic amino acid) has practically equivalent nitrogen atoms, with the density of each of them being 0.60 on the average. The corresponding value for glycine is 0.68, and the sulfur of cysteine has the value of 0.84. Histidine can form a more stable donor–acceptor bond with a complexing agent (Fe atom of porphyrin ring in this case [20]) due to higher electron density in p-state in HOMO.

Thus, a combined analysis of experimental results [2] and quantum-chemical calculations makes obvious that histidine plays a key and unique role in the ligand- binding properties of heme, which manifests itself in a minimal stability of the bonding with ligand in the sixth coordination position and a number of special features of the binding of simple ligands in the sixth coordina- tion position.
