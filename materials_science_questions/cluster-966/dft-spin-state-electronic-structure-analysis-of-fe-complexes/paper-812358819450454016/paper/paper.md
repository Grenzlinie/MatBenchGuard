# The Magnetic Behaviour of Cubic Field $^{5}T_{2g}$ Terms in Lower Symmetry

By B. N. Figgis, School of Chemistry, University of Western Australia, Perth, Australia
J. Lewis and F. E. Mabbs, Department of Inorganic Chemistry, University of Manchester, Manchester 13
G. A. Webb, Department of Chemistry, Battersea College of Advanced Technology, London S.W.11

The behaviour of the magnetic properties for the $^{5}T_{2g}$ term under simultaneous perturbation by spin-orbit coupling and an asymmetric (trigonal or tetragonal) ligand field is presented. The theory is applied to the variation of the magnetic properties of nine Fe(II) octahedral complexes over the temperature range $80-300^{\circ}K$. The absolute magnitudes of the magnetic moments of the complexes appear to be mainly related to the $t_{2g}$ electron delocalisation parameter, $k$. The variation with temperature depends upon the low symmetry ligand-field component and spin-orbit coupling. The magnitude and sign of the low symmetry ligand-field parameter, $\Delta$, cannot always be unambiguously determined from the present results, although it seems unlikely to exceed $\pm 400$ cm.$^{-1}$ in nearly all the cases studied. No correlation between $\Delta$ and the nature or position of the ligand atoms is obvious.

THE effect of asymmetric ligand fields, spin-orbit coupling and electron delocalisation on the magnetic behaviour of transition metal ions which have a $^{2}T_{2g}$ ground term in a cubic ligand field has been reported.¹
The results of a similar treatment using a tetragonal or trigonal distortion are given in this Communication. The splitting pattern for the $^{5}T_{2g}$ term under the influence

the absence of any spin-orbit coupling, and is taken to be positive when the orbital singlet is lowest. The reduction in the orbital part of the magnetic-moment operator, which has been associated with $t_{2 g}$ electron delocalisation, has been allowed for by introducing a constant, $k$ , into the matrix elements of the orbital angular-momentum operator. The magnetic-moment operator for the $z$ -direction is then $(k L_{z}+2 S_{z})$ . De localisation is also taken into account by permitting the spin-orbit coupling constant, $\lambda$ , to differ from the free ion value, $\lambda_{0}$ . However, $\lambda / \lambda_{0}$ is not required to be equal to $k$ . It must be emphasised that we are only dealing with the perturbations within the ground $^{5} T_{2}$ term, and do not consider any interaction with higher terms.

The matrix for the perturbation of the fifteen wave functions of the $^{5} T_{2 g}$ term by the potential $V_{axial }$ plus $\lambda L . S$ is given in the Appendix. The energy levels under this perturbation cannot be expressed in closed form, as they involve the solution of a cubic equation. The secular determinant was solved and the corre- sponding eigenfunctions deduced for a range of the parameters $k, v(=\Delta / \lambda)$ , and $k T / \lambda$ . The magnetic moment corresponding to the set of eigenfunctions was then calculated as described in the earlier Publication on the $^{2} T_{2 g}$ term. $^{1}$ These calculations were performed at the computing centre of the University of WesternAustralia.*

A representative range of nine ferrous complexes of octahedral co-ordination were synthesised. The magnetic susceptibilities of these compounds were measured over the temperature range $80-300^{\circ} K$ , and these measurements compared with the predictions of the above theory. The spin-orbit coupling constant forthe free $Fe^{2+}$ ion, $\lambda_{0}$ , was taken to be $-100 cm .^{-1,3}$

RESULTS
The calculated magnetic moments of the $^{5} T_{2 g}$ term for the various values of the parameters are given in Table 1.

The curves for $\mu_{eff }$ against $k T / \lambda$ , for some values of $k$ and $v$ are illustrated in Figure 2, for negative values of $\lambda$ , which apply to octahedrally co-ordinated iron(II). As observed in the corresponding calculations for the $^{2} T_{2 g}$ ground term, a decrease in the value of $k$ , for any particular value of $v$ , progressively reduces the magnetic moment, bringing the value at room temperature closer to the spin-only value of 4-90 B.M. However, for positive values of $v$ even as high as 10 the approach to the spin-only value is not very close. In general low- symmetry ligand-field components have comparatively little effect on the magnetic properties of the $^{5} T_{2 g}$ term unless they raise its orbital degeneracy by an amount greater than the spin-orbit coupling constant. This means that in the present case of iron(I)), the measure- ment of bulk susceptibilities over the limited range of temperature of $80-300^{\circ} K$ , are not likely to yield un ambiguous values of $\Delta$ unless it exceeds about 100-200 cm.-1. In the case of iron(II), for positive values of A, the overall variation of the magnetic moment with temperature tends to be reduced as the magnitude of the distortion increases. However, when $\Delta$ is negative there is still a very marked variation of the magnetic moment with temperature as the magnitude of the distortion is increased.

The variation with temperature of the magnetic moments of the complexes studied are given in Table 2. The values of the parameters $v, \Delta, \lambda$ , and $k$ obtained by curve fitting are summarised in Tables 3 and some of the fits are illustrated in Figures 3 and 4. In two instances curves in addition to the best fit are given in order to illustrate the sensitivity in the fitting of the parameters. The value of $k$ is very dependent on the absolute value of the magnetic moment and since this was determined to within $1-2 \%$ , the value of $k$ is probably reliable to about $\pm 0.05$ units. The variation of the magnetic moment with temperature to a large extent determines the value of $v$ which is derived and it is found that in the present instances, where the overall variation of the magnetic moment with temperature is small over the range studied, there may be uncertainty concerning the magnitude and sign of $v$ and hence $\Delta$ .

## DISCUSSION

The interpretation of the experimental results for the iron(II) complexes studied do not, in all cases, lead to an unambiguous choice of values for $k$, $\Delta$, or $\lambda$, due to the small variation of the magnetic moments over the temperature range studied. The parameter with the greatest degree of uncertainty is $\Delta$, which in one case can be assigned either a positive or negative value, although the magnitude is then quite small.

In the case of ferrous ammonium sulphate, the present results can be reasonably well interpreted using the following parameters; $k=1.0$, $\lambda=-90$ cm.$^{-1}$, and $\Delta$$\sim400$ cm.$^{-1}$. These parameters indicate that there is little $t_{2g}$ electron delocalisation and also that the octahedron of water molecules around the iron atom is not greatly distorted, although a tetragonal distortion has been inferred from $X$-ray work.$^{4,5}$ Single-crystal magnetic-anisotropy measurements $^{6}$ have also been interpreted on the basis of a tetragonal distortion, the magnitude of which was considered to vary with temperature due to the thermal expansion of the crystal lattice. The value of the parameter, $\Delta$, used to estimate the tetragonal component of the crystal field was $650$ cm.$^{-1}$ at $300^\circ$K falling progressively to $270$ cm.$^{-1}$ at $20^\circ$K. The spin-orbit coupling constant was also reduced from the free-ion value of $-100$ to $-80$ cm.$^{-1}$, no allowance being made for $t_{2g}$ electron delocalisation. Measurements between $1-4.2^\circ$K, show that, below $2^\circ$K, the susceptibility is constant, in agreement with the specific heat data.$^{7,8}$ These last results were interpreted on the basis of a tetragonal perturbation of the primary cubic field of about $100$ to $1000$ cm.$^{-1}$. The interpretation of our bulk susceptibility measurements are in general agreement with the single-crystal anisotropy measurements for the sign and order of magnitude of $\Delta$, and it seems that the neglect of $t_{2g}$ orbital delocalisation by these previous workers was justified. Also, we have found it unnecessary to vary $\Delta$ with temperature in order to fit our results.

The octahedron of water molecules in ferrous hexafluorosilicate hexahydrate has been shown by neutron diffraction studies to be elongated along the threefold axis of the octahedron.$^{9}$ Our results for the hexafluorosilicate salt give $k=0.7$, $\lambda=-100$ cm.$^{-1}$, $\Delta=750$ cm.$^{-1}$ A previous interpretation of low temperature magnetic anisotropy measurements on this substance $^{10}$ gave a value for $\Delta$ of $1200$ cm.$^{-1}$. However, allowance was only made for $\sigma$-bonding of the metal $e_g$ orbitals, $t_{2g}$ electron delocalisation being neglected. The value of the $t_{2g}$ electron delocalisation parameter, $k$, necessary to fit the present experimental data is only $0.7$, which indicates an appreciable degree of delocalisation. This low value is unexpected since the delocalisation must occur via a $\pi$-bonding mechanism, a mode of bonding in which water is not normally considered to participate to a great extent. Also the other hexa-aquo ferrous ions studied $[(NH_4)_2Fe(SO_4)_2$,$6H_2O$ and $FeSO_4$,$7H_2O]$ have higher values of $k$ and there seems to be no obvious reason why there should be a greater degree of delocalisation in the trigonally distorted complex compared to the tetragonally distorted ones.

For the other complexes studied, there are no crystal structure data available, although a tetragonal structure has been proposed for the $\alpha$-picolinic acid $N$-oxide complex, based on i.r. and electronic spectra.$^{12}$ The variation of the magnetic moments with temperature do not always yield unambiguous values for the distortion parameter $\Delta$. In two instances it is possible to fit the experimental results with sets of data which lead to distinctly different values for $\Delta$. Indeed, for $Fe(pyrazine)_2(NCS)_2$ it is not even possible to determine the sign of $\Delta$ with certainty. The difficulties arise largely because of the smallness of the spin-orbit coupling parameter in the ferrous ion; a low value for $\lambda$ means that $kT/\lambda$ in the range of temperatures which were available to us lies between about 0.5 and 3, where the magnetic moment varies but little. Lower temperatures are required to obtain a more critical fit of the data to the theory. Also, it seems that $\Delta$ is fairly small in nearly all the compounds, and the magnetic moment is rather insensitive to this quantity until it considerably exceeds $\lambda$ in magnitude.

One curious feature concerns the compound $Fe(py)_4(NCS)_2$; the data are interpreted to show no $t_{2g}$ electron delocalisation, but yet a considerable reduction in $\lambda$ below the free-ion value. The set of ligands present here might well be expected to take part in $\pi$-bonding, and consequently lead to an appreciable reduction in $k$. Also, while a one-to-one correlation between $k$ and $\lambda/\lambda_0$ is not expected, some concommittent reduction of $k$ with $\lambda/\lambda_0$ should be evident.

The general features which may be deduced from our results are that (i) $\Delta$ is fairly small and positive, mostly 400 cm.$^{-1}$ or less, and shows no obvious correlation with the nature or positions of the ligands about the ferrous ion, equivalent or not; (ii) $t_{2g}$ electron delocalisation is generally present to a moderate extent, as evidenced by the reduction of both $k$ and $\lambda/\lambda_0$ as far as about 0.7 in some compounds.
