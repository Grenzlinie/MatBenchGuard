# Photoionization spectra and electronic structure of small iron clusters

E. A. Rohlfing, $^{a)}$ D. M. Cox, and A. Kaldor
Exxon Research and Engineering Company, Corporate Research Science Laboratory,
Annandale, New Jersey 08801

K. H. Johnson
Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Center for Materials Science and Engineering,
Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Cambridge, Massachusetts 02139

(Received 22 June 1984; accepted 23 July 1984)

Laser photoionization spectra over the range 4.5-6.5 eV have been taken for iron clusters of from 2 to 25 atoms. From the observed ionization thresholds, the cluster ionization potentials are determined and trends in I.P. vs cluster size are examined. In the case of the iron dimer, a sharp, vertical threshold is seen which places the I.P. of $Fe_{2}$ at $6.30 \pm 0.01$ eV. Finally, SCF $K \alpha$ scattered wave molecular orbital calculations have been carried out on the iron dimer and iron trimer.
These results show the importance of ferromagnetic spin polarization in the electronic structure of $Fe_{2}$ and $Fe_{3}$.

## I. INTRODUCTION

The electronic and magnetic properties of small metal clusters is currently an area of intense research interest, both for fundamental and technological reasons. In this paper, we apply a combination of experimental and theoretical tech- niques to the study of iron clusters of from 2 to 25 atoms. Photoionization thresholds measurements on these species have yielded cluster ionization potentials which support our earlier findings on the variation of ionization potential with cluster size. $^{1}$ For the iron dimer, a sharp, vertical ionization threshold is observed, and, from the difference in atom and dimer ionization potentials, we speculate on the nature of the ground state potential curves of the neutral dimer and its ion. To better understand the bonding in $Fe_{2}$ and $Fe_{3}$ , self-consis tent-field $K \alpha$ scattered wave molecular orbital calculations $^{2}$ have been carried out on these molecules. By performing both spin-restricted and spin-unrestricted calculations, we find that the ferromagnetic state (spin polarized) is more sta- ble than the nonmagnetic state due to enhanced $d$ electron bonding.

## II. IONIZATION THRESHOLD MEASUREMENTS ON IRON CLUSTERS

The experimental technique for the generation and de- tection of a supersonic beam of metal clusters has been de- scribed in detail in our previous studies of iron $^{1(a)}$ and nickel $^{1(b)}$ clusters as well as elsewhere, $^{3}$ and will be only brief ly summarized here. The cluster beam source is a high pres- sure, pulsed nozzle to which is attached a faceplate holding the metal target rod, and an extension channel (2 $mm$ diame ter by $24 ~mm$ total length). During the peak carrier gas flow through the faceplate (typically helium with a backing pres- sure of $10 ~atm$ ), a frequency doubled Nd:YAG laser with 30- $40 ~mJ$ pulse energy is focused to approximately a $1 ~mm$ spot on the target rod. The resulting metal vapor is entrained in the carrier gas flow, and cluster formation occurs in the high pressure region of the extension channel. Free jet expansion into a vacuum subsequently quenches cluster growth and cools the metal clusters translationally, rotationally, and, to a lesser extent, vibrationally.

The He/iron cluster expansion is collimated by two conical skimmers located 20 to $70 ~cm$ downstream of the nozzle; this beam is then crossed at $96 ~cm$ downstream by a UV ionizing laser beam. The photoions are mass analyzed with a time-of-flight (TOF) mass spectrometer, with TOF signals being digitized and stored on a PDP 11/23 computer. For our photoionization spectra, stimulated Raman scatter- ing (SRS) in high pressure hydrogen gas is used to blue shift the frequency doubled output of a dye laser (Quanta Ray DCR-1A pumped PDL and WEX). By using the pump beam and each of up to four anti-Stokes (AS) orders, it is possible to generate continuously tunable laser radiation from $300 ~nm$  $(4.1 eV)$ down to $190 ~nm(6.5 eV)$ . For a pump energy of $15 ~mJ$  focused into 10-15 atm of hydrogen with a $15 ~cm$ focal length lens, the typical AS energies at the photoionization region are: $300 \mu J$ in AS1, $100 \mu J$ in AS2, $40 \mu J$ in AS3, and $10 \mu J$ in AS4.

Photoionization spectra for $Fe_{2}$ to $Fe_{25}$ are produced by moving the ionizing laser frequency in discrete increments and collecting an entire TOF mass spectrum each laser fre- quency. Subsequently, the ion signal for each cluster is nor- malized to the total number of laser pulses and the average ionizing laser energy as measured by a pyroelectric energy meter (Laser Precision). Since covering the entire 4.1 to 6.5 eV frequency range requires several dye changes and there- fore several days of experimentation, it was also necessary to compensate for day-to-day fluctuations in the cluster beam source. To do this, reference mass spectra were taken peri- odically with an ArF laser at $6.42 eV$ (Lambda Physik EMG101), and each cluster ion signal for a given frequency scan was normalized to the corresponding ion signal in the ArF reference spectrum. In general, this correction for source changes was small $(<20 \%)$ .

Below about 6.24 eV, no $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}^{+}$ signal is observed, and the data fluctuations represent background variation. We attribute the small step to a hot band ionization from $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}(v=1)$ to $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}^{+}$ $(v=0)$, and the sharp threshold to the 0-0 transition. This assignment places the ionization potential of the iron dimer at $6.30 \pm 0.01 \mathrm{eV}$, where the uncertainty has been conservatively estimated from the width of the threshold. We also note that the width of the hot band plateau, ~0.04 eV or $\sim 320 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, is in good agreement for the previously reported value of $299.6 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ for the $v=0$ to $v=1$ level spacing of the iron dimer. $^{4}$

From the difference in ionization potentials (I.P.s) of the iron atom and dimer, one can estimate the difference in dissociation energies $(D_{0})$ for $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}^{+}$ according to

$$\text { I.P. }(\mathrm{Fe})-\text { I.P. }\left(\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\right)=D_{0}\left(\mathrm{Fe}_{2}^{+}\right)-D_{0}\left(\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\right). \tag{1}$$

The I.P. of iron atom is $7.90 \mathrm{eV},{ }^{5}$ which means the dissociation energy of the dimer ions is larger than that of the neutral by 1.60 eV or 37 kcal/mol. From Knudsen cell mass spectrometric measurements, $D_{0}(\mathrm{Fe}_{2})$ has been determined to be $18 \pm 4 \mathrm{kcal} / \mathrm{mol},{ }^{6}$ and this implies a dissociation energy for $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}^{+}$ of about 55 kcal/mol. A factor of 3 increase in bond strength upon ionization of the iron dimer is difficult to rationalize. If we assume the "normal" picture, in which the difference in $D_{0}$ values is due entirely to a deeper potential well depth for the ground state of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}^{+}$, then we would not expect the essentially adiabatic ionization which seems to be observed experimentally.

The thermodynamic value of the dissociation energy of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}$ determined by Knudsen cell experiments is referenced to two ground state iron atoms, which gave $^{5} D$ terms arising from the $3 d^{6} 4 s^{2}$ configuration. Intuitively, one would expect two atoms in this configuration to interact only very weakly, if at all, at long range due to their filled s shells preventing $s \sigma$ bonding. However, the $^{5} F$ state of the iron atom arising from $3 d^{7} 4 s$ lies $\sim 0.9 \mathrm{eV}$ above the ground state, $^{7}$ and one would expect a strongly attractive long range interaction for two atoms of this configuration. (At shorter internuclear distances, $d$ bonding will play a role; see the discussion in the following section.) Assuming that there is an avoided crossing of the two potential curves arising from the $^{5} D+^{5} D$ and $^{5} F+^{5} F$ asymptotes, then there is a possibility that the ground state potential curve of the iron dimer has a maximum at an internuclear distance greater than the equilibrium internuclear distance. $^{8}$ Because of the large difference in the $^{5} D$ and $^{5} F$ atomic term energies, this maximum could be as much as 1.4 eV (40 kcal/mol) above the $^{5} D+^{5} D$ asymptote. Such an effect would not be expected for the dimer ions since the atomic ground state asymptote, $\mathrm{Fe}^{+}(3 d^{6} 4 s)+\mathrm{Fe}(3 d^{6} 4 s^{2})$, would interact at least somewhat attractively at long range. This situation, in which the ground state potential of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}$ has a maximum but that of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}^{+}$ does not, would account for the large difference in dissociation energies while allowing the two molecules to have similar potential well depths and equilibrium internuclear separations, and therefore show a sharp ionization threshold. In Fig. 2, examples of photoionization spectra for some larger iron clusters are shown. In each spectrum, there are four distinct sets of data points, one for each of the four AS orders, and these are not completely overlapped in frequency. The general observation is that the ionization threshold moves to lower photon energy and broadens as cluster size increases. Because the AS4 laser intensity is quite small and the cluster number density in the beam drops with increasing

From the thresholds observed in the photoionization spectra, we have determined the iron cluster ionization thresholds shown in Fig. 3. The observed thresholds, i.e., the appearance potentials for the individual cluster ions will be reasonable estimates of the true adiabatic ionization poten- tial if (a) the clusters are reasonably cold, i.e., the thermal energy content is not large; (b) the Franck-Condon factors are favorable; (c) dissociative ionization is not important; and (d) ionization competes favorably with predissociation. The general trends in I.P. vs cluster size seen in our earlier intensity dependent work $^{1}$ are confirmed by this data. On top of an overall decrease from the atomic I.P. toward the bulk work function as cluster size increases, there are clear oscillations in the 2-4 and 13-17 atom ranges. Due to the complicated nature of the electronic structure of iron clusters (species in which spin polarization is apparently im- portant; see the following section), it is difficult to interpret these features. We can, however, reiterate our previous con- clusion $^{1}$ that the observation of oscillations as a function of size is evidence in support of the unique, non-bulklike elec- tronic structure of small iron clusters.

The use of 10% overlapping atomic spheres⁹ and "cellular" averaging¹⁰ of the atomic potentials has permitted us to correct for the standard "muffin-tin" approximation² to potentials and charge distributions, and to optimize the molecular geometry. The relaxed orbital energy corresponds to an ionization potential of 6.4 eV, in reasonable agreement with the experimental value of $6.30 \pm 0.01$ eV given above. The transition state value for the ionization energy of Fe₂ at 1.9 Å in the non-spin polarized limit is 7.8 eV. ## IV. CONCLUSIONS

Using continuously tunable UV laser sources (anti-Stokes Raman shifting of double dye) we have measured the dependence of the laser photoionization threshold for iron clusters from 2 to 25 atoms in size. We find that the photoionization threshold varies nonmonotonically with cluster size in agreement with our preliminary findings reported earlier. $^{1}$ A sharp ionization threshold of $6.30\pm0.01$ eV is measured for the iron dimer. From the difference in atom and dimer ionization potentials, we infer properties about the ground state potential energy curves of the dimer and dimer ion. For larger clusters the ionization threshold is observed to become less sharp as cluster size increases. To obtain more insight into the ferromagnetic nature of $Fe_{2}$ and $Fe_{3}$, both spin polarized and non-spin polarized SCF-$X\alpha$-SW molecular orbital calculations have been carried out on these molecules. These calculations predict that the ferromagnetic state (spin polarized) is more stable than the nonmagnetic state (non-spin polarized) due to enhanced $d$ electron bonding.

In spin-polarized transition state calculations ioniza- tion potentials for $Fe_{2}$ and $Fe_{3}$ are found to be in much better agreement with experimental values than in the non-spin polarized calculation, indicating that agreement between theory and experiment is indeed sensitive to the magnetic state of iron. Further calculations are in progress on larger clusters.
