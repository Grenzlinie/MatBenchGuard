## ABSTRACT

The electronic and geometrical structures of the ground and excited states for the linear, fan and cyclic isomers of $FeC_n^+$ and $FeC_n^-$ ($n=1-8$) compounds are calculated using the B3LYP method. A comparison of these results with the previously obtained for $TMC_n^+/TMC_n^-$ (TM = Sc, Ti, V, Co and Zn) systems, is carried out. Quartet and sextet states are found the most stables for $FeC_n^+$ clusters. Linear $FeC_n^-$ clusters prefer doublet and quartet ground states, whereas for cyclic and fan isomers the most stables are quartet and sextet ones. $n$-odd linear, fan, and cyclic $FeC_n^+$ isomers are more stable than adjacent $n$-even ones. For $FeC_n^-$ clusters the opposite trend is found for linear and fan structures, whereas for cyclic isomers the relative stability decreases along the series. In general linear isomers are the most stable conformations for $FeC_n^+/FeC_n^-$ clusters. Systematic trends of different properties are analyzed for $TMC_n^+/TMC_n^-$ clusters.

## 1. Introduction

The study of pure and heteroatom-doped carbon clusters has been a subject of increasing interest in recent years. It is known that the inclusion of heteroatoms in pure carbon clusters provides a way to modify their properties and generate a wide variety of new materials depending on the different interactions between heteroatom and carbon atoms. Theoretical studies of small carbon clusters containing heteroatoms supply a medium to obtain a detailed description of the heteroatom carbon interactions and are very useful to understand the growth mechanism of the various materials formed. The determination of electronic structure, geometry, and stability in these systems is generally problematic. In the particular case of transition metal atoms, early transition metals form stable gas-phase metal-carbon clusters known as metallocarbohedrenes, *met-car* [1-6], with a $M_8C_{12}$ stoichiometry, the corresponding ions, such as $Ti_8C_{12}^+$, have also been studied by infrared spectroscopy [7]. Late transition metals have interesting properties as catalysts for carbon nanotubes formation [8], whereas some rare-earth elements can be trapped inside fullerene cages to form *endohedral metallofullerenes* [9]. On the other hand, networked metallofullerenes [10] can be originated when transition metals are incorporated into carbon cages. In addition to the interest in material science, the study of the structures and reactivity of metal carbides is also important for surface chemistry, combustion chemistry and astrochemistry.

In a recent work we have carried out a theoretical study on $FeC_n$ clusters [11] in the context of first-row transition metal-doped carbides, with a general formula $TMC_n$. Iron-doped carbon clusters are found to present an intermediate behavior between early and late first-row transition metal doped carbon clusters. In the case of anionic and cationic iron-doped carbon clusters only the small members of the series have been studied. Theoretical results are been carried out for $FeC^+$ [12-14], $FeC_2^+$ [15], $FeC^-$ [12,13], $FeC_2^-$ [16,17], $FeC_3^-$ [17,18]. In addition, the binding energy of $FeC^+$ [19,20] has been experimentally determined; $Fe_nC_m^-$ ($n=1,2,3$ and $m=2-8$) systems have been studied by von Helden et al. [21] using gas phase ion chromatography; photoelectron spectra of the $FeC_n^-$ ($n=3,4$) has been reported by Fan et al. [22] and more recently Li and Wang give photoelectron spectroscopy and DFT results for $FeC_2^-$ and $FeC_3^-$ clusters [23,24]. On the other hand, several systematic theoretical studies as a function of the size have been devoted to the study of charged first-row transition metals doped carbon clusters, such as $ScC_n^{+/-}$ ($n=1-10$) [25,26], $TiC_n^{+/-}$ ($n=1-8$) [27], $VC_n^{+/-}$ ($n=1-8$) [28,29], $CrC_n^-$ ($n=2-8$) [30], $CoC_n^{+/-}$ ($n=1-8$) [31], $CuC_n^+$ ($n=3-11$) [32] and $ZnC_n^{+/-}$ ($n=1-8$) [33]. Theoretical and experimental results on small first-row transition metal doped carbon clusters show that there are three different conformations competitive in stability: a linear isomer which has the transition metal atom sited at the end position; a fan-type structure with the transition metal atom interacting with the whole carbon chain; and a cyclic arrangement with the transition atom bonded to the two terminal carbon atoms of the chain. It is also found that in each system the most stable structure depends on the transition metal atom and the size of the clusters ($n$).

In order to complete our previous study on small iron-doped carbon clusters [11], in this work we report a systematic study for anionic and cationic clusters $\text{FeC}_n^+/\text{FeC}_n^-$ ($n=1$–8) taking into account the most stable structures for each multiplicity considered. The results obtained for the different electronic states and geometries could be helpful in future experimental studies (photo-electron spectroscopy or ion mass spectrometry) on these systems. In addition, the results obtained for charged iron-doped carbon clusters will be compared with our previous data for early (Sc, Ti and V) and late (Co, Zn) charged transition metal doped carbon clusters. The inclusion of iron in this comparison is interesting due to its position between first-row transition metals, as has been shown in our previous study on neutral clusters [11].

Finally, in the context of the interstellar chemistry, where neutral and charged heteroatom-doped carbon clusters, of general formula $\text{XC}_n$, have been observed [34–38], carbon clusters doped with iron have a potential interest. Furthermore, transition metals, such as iron, manganese, nickel, or zinc are relatively abundant in space [39]. In fact, the first compound containing iron, FeO, has been recently observed by radioastronomy observations [40]. This suggests that it is conceivable that other molecular species containing iron, and in particular iron carbides in their cationic and anionic form, could be reasonable candidates for interstellar detection. The reported equilibrium structures for the most stable isomers and some spectroscopic data could be helpful for their eventual interstellar detection.

## 2. Computational methods

The theoretical methods considered in this work are identical to those employed in the study of their neutral counterparts [11]. Geometrical optimization have been performed using hybrid density functional theory (HFDFT), in particular, we selected the B3LYP exchange-correlation functional [41,42] constructed from the Lee et al. [43] correlation functional in conjunction with a hybrid exchange functional first proposed by Becke [44]. The latter is a linear combination of local density approximation, Becke's gradient correction [45], and the Hartree–Fock exchange energy based on Kohn–Sham orbitals [46]. Geometries were optimized through analytical computation of analytical second derivatives at the initial point, and updating the hessian in the course of optimization. The 6-311 + G(d) basis set that include diffuse functions and is constructed employing the triple split-valence 6-311G [47] for carbon atoms, and the Wachters [48] and Hay [49] basis set with the scaling factor of Ragavachari and Trucks [50] for iron atom has been selected. Our recent study on first-row transition-metals dicarbide cations [15] has shown that B3LYP/6-311 + G(d) level predicts ground states and low-lying excited states in reasonable agreement with multiconfigurational approach and molecular orbital methods, such as QCISD or CCSD(T). In addition, recently the Fourier transform infrared spectra of $\text{ScC}_3$ [51] agrees with the ground state predicted in our study on $\text{ScC}_n$ clusters [25] at the B3LYP/6-311 + G(d) level.

The prediction of the ground state structure is very difficult in this type of systems because of the large number of local minima on their potential energy surfaces, related to the presence of $nd$ shells in the transition metal atom. Consequently, in order to establish the lowest-lying state, for each multiplicity, a stability calculation [52,53] has been carried out. Finally, harmonic vibrational frequencies were computed from analytical gradient techniques. This allows an estimate of the zero-point vibrational energy (ZPVE) correction for each structure, as well as to assess the nature of the stationary points and therefore to characterize if they are true minima on their respective potential surface.

All calculations reported in this work were carried out with the Gaussian-98 program package [54].

## 3. Results and discussion

Different conformations for $\text{FeC}_n^+/\text{FeC}_n^-$ ($n=1$–8) systems on the doublet, quartet, sextet and octet potential energy surface have been located. We will present the results for the three types of structures most stables on each potential energy surface: a linear structure, a fan isomer and a cycle type conformation. As in previous studies on first-row transition metal carbon clusters, other possible isomers, such as a linear isomer with the iron atom in an intermediate position or a cyclic isomer with an exocyclic iron atom, are not competitive in these systems. The cationic and anionic results for each type of structure are discussed separately in order to analyze systematic trends in their properties with the size of the clusters. The relative stability of the three structures along the series is also discussed. In the last section, a comparison of the results for cationic and anionic iron clusters with the previously obtained for other first-row transition-metal-doped carbides (Sc, Ti, V, Co, Zn) [25–29,31,33] will be carried out.

Harmonic vibrational frequencies and rotational constants calculated for all the isomers reported in this work which might be helpful in an experimental search for these species, are given as Supporting information. Tables S1–S3 report the information for linear, fan and cyclic $\text{FeC}_n^+$ systems, respectively, whereas, the corresponding information for $\text{FeC}_n^-$ clusters is given in Tables S4–S6. In general, it can be seen from Tables S1–S6 that all the isomers reported in this study are true minima in their respective potential energy surface, with the only exception of quartet state of cyclic $\text{FeC}_5^-$ and sextet state of cyclic $\text{FeC}_7^-$. In the case of $\text{FeC}_5^-$ when we tried to optimize the geometry following the normal mode associated to the imaginary frequencies, we reached a fan structure that is more stable. $\text{FeC}_7^-$ shows a small imaginary frequency of $\text{a}_2$ symmetry, all our attempts to found a true minimum in $\text{C}_2$ symmetry failed, however at the B3LYP/6-311G(d) we found that $^6\text{A}_2$ is a true minimum, this fact can be related to a large overlap between the diffuse functions [55]. It should be pointed out that in the case of linear isomers, for some structures reported in this work, for example, those with $^4\Phi$ or $^8\Phi$ electronic states, the B3LYP method, as well as single-reference based methods such as MP2, QCISD, etc., provides non degenerate $\pi$ frequencies. On the other hand, when degenerate frequencies are obtained (states of $\Sigma$ and $\Delta$ symmetry) they are denoted as (2).

In order to differentiate the carbon atoms into each structure we will denote carbon number one that bonded to the iron atom.

At the best of our knowledge, previous studies on FeCₙ⁺ systems have been only reported for FeC⁺ and FeC₂⁺. Tzeli and Mavridris [14] have studied 40 states of FeC⁺ by multireference methods in conjunction of relatively large basis sets. They predicted a ground state of ²Δ symmetry, with the first excited state (⁴Σ), lying 18 kcal/mol higher in energy. The ground state, ²Δ, is predicted to have a triplet-bond character with $r_{\mathrm{e}}=1.581\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ and an estimated dissociation energy including zero-point vibrational energies (ZPEs) corrections with respect to adiabatic fragments, $D_{\mathrm{o}}=87$ kcal/mol, which agree quite well with our B3LYP predicted results shown in Table 1 and Fig. 1. We predict $D_{\mathrm{o}}=79.33$ kcal/mol, similar to those obtained experimentally, 94.5 ± 7 [19] and 84.2 ± 4.1 [20] kcal/mol. In addition, a DFT calculation using the BPW91 functional [12,13], found the same ground and lowest-lying quartet and sextet states that we have obtained with the B3LYP functional. Comparative studies of the performance of different functionals showed that in general B3LYP and BPW91 methods give similar results for relative energies and geometrical parameters [56]. On the other hand, in our systematic study in first-row transition metal dicarbide cations, TMC₂⁺ [15], we have found that the B3LYP level gave the same results that QCISD, CCSD(T) and MRCI methods. For FeC₂⁺ a ⁶A₁ and ⁶Δ were found to be the lowest-lying states for $C_{2v}$ symmetry arrangement or linear geometries, respectively. All levels employed [15] found the ⁶A₁ state as the lowest one.

From Table 1 we can see that linear FeCₙ⁺ clusters have a ⁶Σ ground state for n-even clusters whereas for n-odd ones the most stable state corresponds to a ⁴Σ, with the only exception of the first members of the series (FeC⁺ and FeC₂⁺). Both states are obtained from the ground states of FeCₙ clusters, ⁵Δ (n-even) and ³Δ (n-odd) [11], by elimination of one δ-type electron mainly located at the iron atom, giving the following electronic configurations:

$$\{core\}1\sigma^{2}...1\pi^{4}....(n+2)\sigma^{1}\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)\pi^{2}1\delta^{2}\quad n\text{-even FeC}_{n}^{+}\text{clusters}$$

$$\{core\}1\sigma^{2}...1\pi^{4}....(n+2)\sigma^{1}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\pi^{4}1\delta^{2}\quad n\text{-odd FeC}_{n}^{+}\text{clusters}$$

For n-even linear FeCₙ⁺ systems the lowest-lying quartet state is very close in energy to the sextet one (energy difference less than 2.00 kcal/mol), and therefore, it is possible that quartet states could be also accessible to experimental observation. In the case of FeC₂⁺ we have found a ⁴Δ state located 4.08 kcal/mol above the ground state, but it has an imaginary π-type frequency and corresponds to the transition state for the $C_{2v}$ structure rearrangement. On the other hand, doublet and sextet states for n-odd clusters are relatively high in energy. Octet states are the least stable ones, and their relative stability increases with the size of the clusters. Finally it must be pointed out that the results obtained for doublet states, mainly those of n-even, must be taken with caution due to the high spin contamination of their wavefunction.

### 3.3. Stabilities of $FeC_n^+$ and $FeC_n^-$ clusters

Firstly, we will analyze the stability of the different $FeC_n^+/FeC_n^-$ structures considered, namely linear, fan and cyclic, separately as a function of the size of the clusters, n. As we have shown in our previous studies on doped carbon clusters [25–29,31,33], the incremental binding energy [58] is a suitable concept for analyze the relative stability of these systems with n. It can be defined as the change in energy accompanying to the process:

$$FeC_n^+/FeC_n^- \rightarrow FeC_{n-1}^+/FeC_{n-1}^- + C \tag{1}$$

and can be computed as the consecutive binding energy differences between adjacent $FeC_n^+/FeC_n^-$ and $FeC_{n-1}^+/FeC_{n-1}^-$ clusters of the same structure taking in each case the lowest-lying state.

In Fig. 4 the calculated incremental binding energies for linear, fan and cyclic $FeC_n^+$ and $FeC_n^-$ clusters as a function of the number of carbon atoms are collected. The incremental binding energies for linear, fan and cyclic $FeC_n^+$ isomers (Fig. 4a) show a clear even-odd alternation in stability, with their $n$-odd members being more stable than the corresponding $n$-even ones. For linear isomers these differences in stability between $n$-odd and $n$-even clusters are slightly attenuated for the last members of the series, whereas the differences are increased for fan structures, where the low value found for fan $FeC_8^+$ indicates its relative unstability. The parity effect found for linear $FeC_n^+$ structures indicates that the electronic configuration of $n$-odd clusters, $\sigma^1\pi^4\delta^2$ ($^4\Sigma$ state), is more stable than the corresponding of $n$-even ones, $\sigma^1\pi^2\delta^2$ ($^6\Sigma$ state), therefore, low spin multiplicity states are relatively more stable. In the case of cyclic structures we can see a large variation between $n=4$-$5$, showing that $FeC_4^+$ is relatively unstable respect to $FeC_3^+$ and $FeC_5^+$. Concerning incremental binding energies obtained for the anionic systems (Fig. 4b), it exists also a clear even-odd alternation in stability for linear and fan structures, however, the trend found is the opposite to that of cationic clusters, $n$-even members being more stable than the corresponding $n$-odd ones. In linear $FeC_n^-$ clusters a ground state corresponding to a $\sigma^2\pi^2\delta^3$ ($n$-even) electronic configuration is more stable than a $\sigma^2\pi^4\delta^3$ ($n$-odd) one, consequently, linear isomers with high spin multiplicity states are relatively more stable, similar behavior was found for neutral systems [11]. As in the case of cationic compounds the incremental binding energies for linear systems slightly decrease with $n$. The low incremental binding energy found for fan $FeC_8^-$ isomer, shows that the relative stability of fan structures decreases for the last members of the series. For cyclic $FeC_n^-$ structures, incremental binding energies can only be computed for the last members in the series and show a decrease along the series. The largest value is found for $n=6$, and could be related to a high stabilization between cyclic clusters with $n=5$ and 6.

Another interesting point related to the stabilities of $FeC_n^+$ and $FeC_n^-$ clusters is the analysis of the competition between the three structures as a function of the size of the clusters. ### 3.4. Ionization potentials and electron affinities of $\text{FeC}_n$ clusters

The results obtained for $\text{FeC}_n^+/\text{FeC}_n^-$ clusters and the previous reported for the $\text{FeC}_n$ system [11] allow an estimation of the ionization potentials (IPs) and electron affinities (EAs) of the three different $\text{FeC}_n$ isomers, namely linear, fan and cyclic. The calculated values correspond to adiabatic IPs and EAs, therefore they were computed as the energy difference between the charged and neutral species (including zero-point vibrational energies) at their respective optimized geometries. The IPs and EAs as functions of the number of carbon atoms are shown in Fig. 6.

The IPs found for linear $\text{FeC}_n$ isomers shows a clear even-odd parity effect, with higher IP values for $n$-even members than for $n$-odd one. This parity effect is related to the opposite parity effect reported for the stability of linear neutral and cationic systems, in both cases the parity effect is attenuated along the series (Fig. 4), and this attenuation effect is also reflected in IPs values. It seems that the ionization potential for large linear clusters tends to a limit value, in this case very close to the IP of the iron atom, 7.94 eV. This trend arises from the fact that ionization on $\text{FeC}_n$ linear clusters takes place from detachment of an electron mainly located at the iron atom and the location of the HOMO orbital at the iron atom increases with $n$. On the other hand, IPs for fan and cyclic $\text{FeC}_n$ clusters show a similar parity effect, with $n$-even clusters having larger IP, also related to the opposite variation in the incremental binding energy for $\text{FeC}_n$ and $\text{FeC}_n^+$ fan and cyclic structures. For fan clusters this parity effect slightly increases along the series. We can also see that the IPs calculated for cyclic and mainly fan structures are larger than that of the linear ones. This behavior could be related to the relative stability of the different structures for neutral and cationic $\text{FeC}_n$ clusters. As we have pointed out (Section 3.3), linear isomers are relatively more stable for cationic systems than for neutral, giving slow IPs for linear structures.

In the case of fan $\text{FeC}_n$ structures the EAs increase up to $n = 6$, showing a slightly parity effect, and decrease for the rest of the series. On the other hand, EAs tend to increase for cyclic $\text{FeC}_n$ system mainly for the first members. As for IPs, the behavior of the computed EAs is directly related to the relative stability of the different $\text{FeC}_n$ and $\text{FeC}_n^-$ isomers. Finally, it should be pointed out that our EAs computed for $\text{FeC}_2$ and $\text{FeC}_3$ at the B3LYP/6-311 + G(d) level, 1.86 and 1.65 eV, respectively, are in agreement with the experimental adiabatic electron affinities reported by Wang and Li [23,24] (1.96 and 1.68 eV).

Different properties which depend on the size of the clusters for each transition metal, as incremental binding energies, stability of the isomers, ionization potentials and electron affinities, are considered. With the only exception of the comparison of the stability of the three types of isomers, linear, fan and cyclic, we will consider the behavior of the different properties of the clusters, taking into account the results obtained for linear structures, because fan and cyclic structures are not located in some cases: cyclic structures for $\text{ScC}_n^+/\text{ScC}_n^-$ and fan structures for $\text{ZnC}_n^+/\text{ZnC}_n^-$ only have been located for $\text{ScC}_7^+$, $\text{ScC}_8^+$ and $\text{ZnC}_2^+/\text{ZnC}_2^-$, respectively.

In general we can see that for the ionic first-row transition metal doped carbon clusters included in this work ($TMC_n^+/TMC_n^-$, with $n = 1-8$) fan structures are in general the most stable ones for first members of early first-row transition metal whereas for larger clusters linear structures are the most stable ones. Late transition metals in general have linear ground states. Cyclic structures are the most stable ones only for isolated members. The preference of linear and fan-type structures, as in the case of neutral $TMC_n$ clusters [11], could be related to two concomitant effects: charge donation from the metal to the carbon unit and back-donation from the carbon unit. Charge donation from the metal to the carbon unit, is more favorable along $C_{2v}$ symmetry than for linear arrangements and should favor fan isomers. However, the energy of the metal 4s (and 3d) orbital lowers along the transition series, and consequently this interaction is less favorable for late transition metals. On the other hand, back-donation from the carbon unit that is also in principle more favorable in $C_{2v}$ symmetry than in $C_{\infty v}$ one, should be hindered to a large extent for late transition metals, due to the large occupancy of the metal $3d$ orbitals. In general, the stability trends of the three structures for ionic first-row transition metal doped carbon clusters are similar to that found for neutral one [11]. The only difference is a slightly stabilization of linear structures respect to both, cyclic and fan ones in charged clusters.

In general, we can see that EAs reported in Fig. 10b for $TMC_n$ clusters, increase from early to late transition metals.

## 4. Conclusions

Linear, fan and cyclic conformations of $FeC_n^+$ and $FeC_n^-$ ($n=1-8$) clusters on the doublet, quartet, sextet and octet potential energy surfaces have been studied at the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) level of theory. Predictions for their geometrical parameters, vibrational frequencies or dipole moments which could be useful for an even- tual experimental characterization have been reported.

The ground state for linear $FeC_n^+$ ($n=1-8$) clusters is found to be a $^6\Sigma$ state for $n$-even clusters whereas for $n$-odd ones the most stable state corresponds to a $^4\Sigma$, with the only exception of the first members of the series, $FeC^+$ and $FeC_2^+$ which have $^2\Delta$ and $^6\Delta$ lowest-lying states, respectively. The results obtained for fan and cyclic $FeC_n^+$ clusters show that the lowest-lying states on these conformations are quartet with the exceptions of $FeC_2^+$ and $FeC_4^+$, where the sextet state is slightly lower in energy than the corresponding quartet one. On the other hand, for anionic clusters we have found that the ground state for $n$-odd linear $FeC_n^-$ clusters has a $^2\Delta$ symmetry, whereas for $n$-even ones a $^4\Delta$ is located. In general, the lowest-lying states for fan and cyclic $FeC_n^-$ clusters present quartet or sextuplet multiplicity. It is also found that octet states are not competitive in energy for cationic and anionic $FeC_n$ clusters.

We have found for linear, fan and cyclic $FeC_n^+$ isomers the same pattern in stability, that is $n$-odd clusters are more stable than adjacent $n$-even ones. In the case of $FeC_n^-$ clusters the opposite trend is found for linear and fan structures, whereas for cyclic isomers the relative stability decreases along the series. Relating to the stability of the three types of structures along the series, we can conclude that linear isomers are the most stable conformations for $FeC_n^+$ clusters, with the only exception of $FeC_2^+$ system, where a $C_{2v}$ structure is the most stable. Linear structures are also the most stable ones for anionic clusters, with the exception of $FeC_2^-$ and $FeC_7^-$ where the fan and the cyclic structure are the most stable, respectively.

Linear $TMC_n^+$ clusters (TM = Sc, Ti, V, Fe, Co and Zn) present an even-odd parity effect with $n$-odd members more stable that the corresponding $n$-even ones. Linear $ScC_n^+$ clusters are an exception, in this case the opposite effect is found. On the other hand, the same behavior is found for the different $TMC_n^-$ clusters, a clear even-odd parity effect with $n$-even members more stable than the corresponding $n$-odd ones.

In general, the relative stabilities of linear, fan and cyclic structures for ionic first-row transition metal doped carbon clusters with $n$ are similar to that found for the neutral systems. The only difference is a slightly stabilization of linear structures respect to both, cyclic and fan ones in charged clusters.

Finally, IPs and EAs calculated for $TMC_n$ clusters are in general, large for $n$-even clusters, and increase from early to late transition metals.
