# The Orientational Disordering and Thermodynamic Properties of Simple Molecular Crystals

By
V. A. SLUSAREV, YU. A. FREIMAN, I. N. KRUPSKII, and I. A. BURAKHOVICH

The Bogolyubov variational method has been applied to the investigation of the ordered phase thermodynamics for $\mathrm{N}_{2}, \mathrm{CO}, \mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}$, and $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ crystals in the molecular field approximation. The libron heat capacity, the ordering parameter, and the libration excitation energy have been calculated for the whole range of the ordered phase. The orientational disordering temperature has also been estimated. The theoretical results have been compared with experimental data.

## 1. Introduction

Molecular crystals, comprising a vast and comparatively scarcely investigated class of solids, are characterized by a diversity of properties. Up to now only solidified noble gases have systematically been investigated, and this is due to the availability of the relevant theoretical models and to the ease of comparing theories with experimental results. Recently experimental data have been obtained for simple non-monoatomic molecular crystals as well, which in turn has stimulated the appearance of several theoretical papers on that subject.

This paper deals with the analysis of thermal properties of the group of non-monoatomic molecular crystals including $\mathrm{N}_{2}, \mathrm{CO}, \mathrm{CO}_{2}$, and $\mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ that have similar physical properties. These crystals are formed by linear molecules, and in their ordered phase the molecular centres of mass are situated at the sites of the f.c.c. pattern, the molecular axes being directed along the four spatial diagonals of a cube (space group $\mathrm{Pa} 3$). The characteristic feature of the intermolecular interaction in such crystals is that the non-central part of the potential results from quadrupole forces and from the part of valency and dispersion forces having the analogous angular dependence as quadrupole forces, and further, that dipole interaction either does not exist $(\mathrm{N}_{2}, \mathrm{CO}_{2})$ or is negligible $(\mathrm{CO}, \mathrm{N}_{2} \mathrm{O})$ to influence the majority of thermodynamical properties. In addition, all crystals considered have a common feature, namely their intrinsic rotational temperatures $\theta_{\text {rot }}=\hbar^{2} / 2 I$ ($I$ is the momentum of inertia of the corresponding molecule) are small compared to the energy of non-central interaction.

The expression for the intermolecular interaction potential has been derived in [1]. However, the complexity of the equations of motion hinders to solve them without further simplifying assumptions. In the low-temperature range it is reasonable to apply an assumption successfully used by several authors [2, 3] that the translational motions of the molecular system are independent. As shown [4, 5] there are two types of excitations in molecular crystals — phonons and librons; furthermore, the thermodynamic functions of the system can be written as a sum of two independent terms corresponding to each sub-system. In such a treatment, the translational-orientational interaction leads to a renormalization of the sound velocity and of the libron dispersion law only.

The investigation of the librational behaviour of molecules is usually carried out within the framework of the harmonic approximation [7 to 11]. Estima- tions of libration amplitudes as well as the analysis of experimental data on thermodynamical properties of the system show, however, that anharmonic effects are to be essential at temperatures substantially lower than the orien- tational disordering temperature. The effect of molecular rotations in $N_2$ and CO crystals not restricted by the assumption of harmonicity of oscillations has been calculated numerically in the molecular field approximation by Kohin [1]. The libron activation energy calculated in [1] is in good agreement with the data obtained in the analysis of the low-temperature heat capacity [2, 4]. This paper represents further investigations of anharmonic libration effects on the thermodynamic properties of simple molecular crystals.

## 2. Theory

The system of interacting rotators can be treated in the molecular field approximation by means of the following Hamiltonian:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H = & -\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 I} \sum_{f}\left[\frac{1}{\sin \vartheta_{f}} \frac{\partial}{\partial \vartheta_{f}} \sin \vartheta_{f} \frac{\partial}{\partial \vartheta_{f}}+\frac{1}{\sin ^{2} \vartheta_{f}} \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial \varphi_{f}^{2}}\right]- \\
& -U \eta \sum_{f} P_{2}\left(\cos \vartheta_{f}\right)+N \frac{U}{2} \eta^{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

Here $N$ is the total number of lattice sites; $\vartheta_{f}, \varphi_{f}$ are the angles determining molecular orientation in the site $f$, $\vartheta_{f}$ being the angle of deviation of the molecular axis from the equilibrium position, $P_{2}(\cos \vartheta_{f})=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2} \cos ^{2} \vartheta_{f}$. $\eta$ is the order parameter, determined by the self-consistency condition

$$
\begin{gathered}
\eta=\left\langle P_{2}(\cos \vartheta)\right\rangle \\
\left\langle P_{2}(\cos \vartheta)\right\rangle=\frac{\text { Sp } \mathrm{e}^{-\beta H} P_{2}(\cos \vartheta)}{\text { Sp } \mathrm{e}^{-\beta H}} ; \quad \beta=\frac{1}{T}
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
$$

$U$ is the molecular-field constant.

The Schrödinger equation of a rotator in the mean molecular field,

$$
E \psi=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 I}\left(\frac{1}{\sin \vartheta} \frac{\partial}{\partial \vartheta} \sin \vartheta \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \vartheta}+\frac{1}{\sin ^{2} \vartheta} \frac{\partial^{2} \psi}{\partial \varphi^{2}}\right)-U \eta P_{2}(\cos \vartheta) \psi,
\tag{3}
$$

can be solved analytically provided that the ratio $\theta_{\text {rot }} / U$ is small. There are sharp peaks of the wave function near the values $\vartheta=0$ and $\vartheta=\pi$. Since the Hamiltonian (3) is invariant under the operation $x=\cos \vartheta \rightarrow-x$, each state is either odd or even under inversion. Odd and even states are grouped into

pairs having exponentially close energy levels [12]. For that reasons, we are permitted to consider the wave function as being localized near the position $\vartheta=0$, each level being doubly degenerate.

Let us introduce the variables $u$ and $v$ related to the angular variables $\vartheta$ and $\varphi$ by the equations
$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}
u & =\varrho \cos \varphi, & \varrho & =2 \operatorname{tg} \frac{\vartheta}{2}, \\
v & =\varrho \sin \varphi, & & -\infty<u, v<+\infty.
\end{aligned}
\right\}
\tag{4}
$$

Using the substitution
$$
\Psi=\psi \frac{1}{1+\frac{\varrho^{2}}{4}},
\tag{5}
$$
one can express equation (3) in the new variables
$$
E \Psi=-\frac{h^{2}}{2 I}\left(1+\frac{\varrho^{2}}{4}\right)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\varrho^{2}}{4}\right) \Psi-U \eta\left(1-\frac{3}{2} \frac{\varrho^{2}}{1+\frac{\varrho^{2}}{4}}\right) \Psi. \quad(6)
$$

As mentioned above, in the case $U / \theta_{\text {rot }} \rightarrow \infty$ the important region is that of small values of $\varrho$ (small $\vartheta$ ). In this case equation (6) describes the system of two non-interacting oscillators. Therefore it seems natural to use the Bogolyubov variational principle [13] for evaluating the statistical sum of the system.
To this end let us split the Hamiltonian of the system into two parts
$$
H=H_{0}+H^{\prime},
\tag{7}
$$
$$
H_{0}=\frac{V_{1}}{2} \sum_{f}\left(P_{u f}^{2}+P_{v f}^{2}\right)+\frac{V_{2}}{2} \sum_{f}\left(u_{f}^{2}+v_{f}^{2}\right),
\tag{8}
$$
where
$$
P_{u}=-i \frac{\partial}{\partial u}, \quad P_{v}=-i \frac{\partial}{\partial v}.
$$

Since the statistical sum satisfies the inequality
$$
Z \geqq Z_{0} \exp \left(-\beta\left\langle H^{\prime}\right\rangle_{0}\right)
$$
$$
\left\langle H^{\prime}\right\rangle_{0}=\frac{1}{Z_{0}} \operatorname{Sp} \mathrm{e}^{-\beta H_{0}}\left(H^{\prime}\right) ; \quad Z_{0}=\operatorname{Sp} \mathrm{e}^{-\beta H_{0}}
\tag{9}
$$
the parameters $V_{1}$ and $V_{2}$ in (8) are found by minimizing the free energy of the system. Finally the free energy is written down in the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{F}{N}= & 2 T \ln 2 \sinh \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}+\frac{h^{2}}{2 I}\left\{\frac{1}{\gamma} \operatorname{coth} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}+\frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{\sinh ^{2} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}}+\frac{1}{8} \gamma \operatorname{coth}^{3} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}\right\}- \\
& -\frac{U}{2} \eta^{2}-\Delta \operatorname{coth} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
where $\gamma=\sqrt{V_{1} / V_{2}}, \Delta=\sqrt{V_{1} V_{2}}$. The minimum conditions of the free energy

48 physica (b) 54/2

$\partial F / \partial \gamma=0$, and $\partial F / \partial \Delta=0$ can be represented in the form

$$
\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 I}\left\{-\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}+\frac{1}{8} \operatorname{coth}^{2} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}\right\}-U \eta \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial\left\langle\varrho^{2}\right\rangle_{0}}=0,
\tag{11}
$$

$$
\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2 I}\left\{\frac{2}{\gamma}+\operatorname{coth} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{4} \operatorname{coth}^{2} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}\right\}=\Delta,
\tag{12}
$$

$$
\left\langle\varrho^{2}\right\rangle_{0}=\gamma \operatorname{coth} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}.
\tag{13}
$$

To obtain the closed system of equations it is necessary to express the order parameter $\eta$ in terms of $\left\langle\varrho^{2}\right\rangle_{0}$. The latter is easily done by means of (4), the result being

$$
\eta=1-6\left\langle\frac{1}{1+\frac{\varrho^{2}}{4}}-\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{\varrho^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}}\right\rangle_{0}=1-6 \int_{0}^{\infty} \mathrm{d} s(1-s)\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{-s\left(1+\frac{\varrho^{2}}{4}\right)}\right\rangle_{0}
$$

and finally

$$
\eta=1-6 \int_{0}^{\infty} \mathrm{d} s(1-s) \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-s}}{1+\frac{s}{4}\left\langle\varrho^{2}\right\rangle_{0}}.
\tag{14}
$$

The final expressions for the mean energy and heat capacity per molecule take the form

$$
\frac{E}{N}=\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}\left\{\frac{1}{\gamma} \operatorname{coth} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}+\frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{\sinh ^{2} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}}+\frac{1}{8} \gamma \operatorname{coth}^{3} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}\right\}-\frac{U}{2} \eta^{2},
\tag{15}
$$

$$
\frac{C_{v}}{R}=\frac{1}{2} \frac{(\beta \Delta)^{2}}{\sinh ^{2} \frac{\beta \Delta}{2}}\left(1+\frac{\beta}{\Delta} \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial \beta}\right).
\tag{16}
$$

Thus the anharmonicity of the system leading to a temperature dependence of the libron energy originates the additional term with regard to the Einstein term.

At sufficiently high temperatures (i.e. in the classical limit) the hyperbolic cotangent in equations (11) to (13) can be substituted by the reciprocal of its argument. In terms of the following reduced quantities:

$$
\Delta^{*}=\frac{\Delta}{2 \alpha \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}}, \quad T^{*}=\frac{T}{\alpha^{2} \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}}, \quad \gamma^{*}=\alpha \gamma, \quad\left(\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2} \frac{U}{\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}}}\right),
$$

(11) to (13) can be written as

$$
-\frac{1}{\gamma^{* 2}}+\frac{1}{8} \frac{T^{* 2}}{\Delta^{* 2}}-\frac{2}{3} \eta \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial\left\langle\varrho^{2}\right\rangle_{0}}=0,
\tag{11'}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{\gamma^{*}}+\frac{T^{*}}{2 \Delta^{*}}+\gamma^{*} \frac{T^{* 2}}{8 \Delta^{* 2}}=\Delta^{*},
\tag{12'}
$$

$$
\left\langle\varrho^{2}\right\rangle_{0}=\gamma^{*} \frac{T^{*}}{\Delta^{*}}.
\tag{13'}
$$

Since equations (11') to (13') do not contain intrinsic parameters of a substance, in the high-temperature limit a law of corresponding states must be valid for the crystals of the considered type, namely solutions of (11') to (13') must be universal if they are expressed in reduced coordinates. Temperature decrease would naturally break down this universality since in this temperature range the influence of zero-point orientational oscillations becomes important.

Substantially different values for the magnitudes of the repulsive constant $B$ given by different authors greatly complicate the consideration of the problem, since these repulsive forces comprise a large part of the anisotropic intermolecular interaction (see Table 2). It seems therefore reasonable to determine the constant $B$ for all the crystals under consideration starting from low temperature values of the characteristic Einstein temperature (libron activation energy) $\Delta_{0}$. For this purpose let us investigate equations (11) to (13) and (14) in the limit $T=0\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{K}$.

By means of the asymptotic representation of the integral exponent,
$$\mathrm{Ei}\ (-x)=\mathrm{e}^{-x}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{(k-1)!}{x^{k}},\tag{18}$$
we reduce $\eta$ to
$$\eta=1-\frac{3}{2}\gamma+\frac{3}{2}\gamma^{2}-\frac{27}{16}\gamma^{3}+\cdots.\tag{19}$$

Introducing a dimensionless parameter $\alpha=\sqrt{3/2\ U_{0}/\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}}$ where $U_{0}$ is the magnitude of the molecular-field parameter at $0\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{K}$, and omitting terms of the fourth and higher powers of $\gamma$ we obtain
$$\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}-\frac{1}{8}=\alpha^{2}\left(1-\frac{7}{2}\gamma+\frac{63}{8}\gamma^{2}-\frac{63}{4}\gamma^{3}\right).\tag{20}$$

At $T=0\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{K}$ equation (12) is reduced to a quadratic equation,
$$\Delta_{0}=\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}\left(1+\frac{2}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{4}\right),\tag{21}$$
relating $\gamma$ with the low-temperature limit of the characteristic Einstein temperature. Knowing $\Delta_{0}$, from (20) and (21) one easily finds the value $\alpha$. It permits to obtain sufficiently reliable values of the constant $B$, as soon as we know the parameters of the intermolecular potential and the nearest neighbour distance at $0\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{K}$. In fact, changes of temperature dependence of the lattice parameter do not influence the calculated values of the heat capacity, but the correction $C_p - C_v$ is changed substantially, and consequently the experimental and theoretical curves of Fig. 3 practically coincide.

The reliable experimental results for the librational part of heat capacity of $CO_2$ and $N_2O$ crystals can be obtained only for temperatures not exceeding approximately $100\ ^{\circ}\text{K}$ [3]. In this temperature range, because of the small value of the derivative $\partial\Delta/\partial\beta$, the librational heat capacity is satisfactory described by a single Einstein term. Therefore, the absence of sufficiently reliable experimental data on $C_v$, especially for temperatures in the vicinity of melting points, hinders an unambigous checking of applicability of equation (16) to the treatment of the librational part of heat capacity.
