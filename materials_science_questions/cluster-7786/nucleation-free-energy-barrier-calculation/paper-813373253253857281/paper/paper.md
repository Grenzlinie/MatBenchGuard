# NANOSTRUCTURES

## ON FORMATION OF CARBON CLUSTERS
## ON A SUBSTRATE IN PLASMA PYROLYSIS
## OF HYDROCARBON GASES

D. A. Takopulo and S. P. Fisenko
UDC 533.915:001.573

The influence of nanoparticles of soot precipitated onto a catalytic metal substrate from a flow on the forma- tion of carbon clusters in a supersaturated solid solution on the substrate boundary is considered. The free energy and the probability of the formation of critical clusters are considered.

Keywords: critical cluster, surface tension, supersaturation of a solution, diffusion.

Introduction. By now it has been established experimentally that to obtain carbon nanofibers on a silicon substrate it is necessary to have a metal catalytic nanodroplet with a characteristic dimension of tens of nanometers on a substrate. On the surface of the metal nanodroplet, the molecules of hydrocarbon gases split [1], with carbon diffus- ing inside. After a supersaturated solid solution is created inside the nanodroplet, in the first stage of obtaining nanofi- bers at the interface between the nanodroplet and substrate, carbon clusters are formed [2, 3]. In the process of diffusion interaction of these clusters a tubular nanofiber is formed [4]. Thus, the process of the formation of carbon clusters near the interface is the most important stage of obtaining nanofibers. It should be noted that precisely this regime, called the top regime, is mainly investigated in physical experiments [1] (Fig. 1).

In the process of obtaining carbon nanofibers with participation of plasma pyrolysis of hydrocarbon gases it was discovered that nanoparticles of soot with sizes of tens of nanometers are deposited on the metal wall of a reactor under the action of thermophoresis [5]. The negative role of soot nanoparticles that prevent the molecules of hydro- carbon gas from arriving at a catalytic surface is obvious. However, the situation is not so simple, since, as it was found out, the soot particles can favor the formation of nanofibers.

The aim of the present work is to investigate the probability of formation of carbon clusters in the case where in a supersaturated solid solution carbon clusters are formed at the interface between a metal substrate and the soot particle surface. The supersaturation of a solid solution $S$ is defined as the ratio of the number density of an admixture $n$ to the equilibrium solubility of the admixture $n_{\text{eq}}(T)$ in a metal at a temperature $T$: $S = n/n_{\text{eq}}(T)$.

Free Energy of Cluster Formation. In [2], the thermodynamics and kinetics of the vapor–liquid–solid (VLS) process that determines the formation of nanofibers for the case of their growth in the top regime are considered. In particular, a substantial influence of the surface free energy of the system on the process of formation of carbon clus- ters of size less than 1 nm was shown.

Next, we will consider the base regime of the growth of carbon nanofiber, viz., we consider that a soot nanoparticle that precipitated from the gas phase on the metal surface acts as a substrate (Fig. 2). We investigate the free energy of the formation of a semispherical one-component carbon cluster consisting of $g$ atoms for two cases of the formation of clusters: on the metal–gas phase interface and the metal–soot nanoparticle interface. In the former case, the free energy of the formation of a cluster $\Delta F_{1}$ from $g$ atoms in a supersaturated solid solution has the form

$$
\Delta F_{1}(g)=-g k T \ln (S)+2 \pi r^{2} \sigma_{\text{c,m}}+\pi r^{2}\left(\sigma_{\text{c,g}}-\sigma_{\text{m,g}}\right). \tag{1}
$$

When a cluster is formed on the metal–soot nanoparticle interface, the surface tension between the nanoparticle and the carbon cluster is obviously equal to zero, and the expression for the free energy of the formation of a cluster $\Delta F_{2}$ takes the form

$$
\Delta F_{2}(g)=-g k T \ln (S)+2 \pi r^{2} \sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{m}}-\pi r^{2} \sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{m}}. \tag{2}
$$

According to the inferences of the statistical physics [6], the probability of formation $f(g)$ of a carbon cluster is directly proportional to the exponential function of the dimensionless free energy:

$$
f(g)=A \exp \left(\frac{-\Delta F(g)}{k T}\right), \tag{3}
$$

where $A$ is a constant the precise value of which cannot be calculated at the present time. However, the ratio of the probabilities of the formation of clusters for the two considered cases is independent of $A$ and is equal to

$$
\frac{f_{1}}{f_{2}}=\exp \left(\frac{\Delta F_{2}-\Delta F_{1}}{k T}\right)=\exp \left(-\frac{\pi r^{2} \sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{m}}}{k T}\right). \tag{4}
$$

As follows from Eq. (4) for $r=1$ nm and $\sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{m}} \sim 1 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^{2}$, at a temperature of 1000 K the difference of the free energies of the formation of identical clusters at any supersaturation is smaller by about 100 $kT$ under a soot nanoparticle. Thus, the probability of the carbon cluster formation under the soot nanoparticle considerably exceeds the probability of the formation of a "free" cluster, which opens up good prospects for the formation of carbon nanofibers on a metal substrate [7, 8] in plasma pyrolysis of hydrocarbon gases. We will refine this effect for critical clusters which are important for calculating the nucleation rate [2] and which are determined by the extremum condition of the free energy of cluster formation:

$$
\frac{\partial \Delta F_{1}(g)}{\partial g}=\frac{\partial \Delta F_{2}(g)}{\partial g}=0. \tag{5}
$$

We will denote the volume per carbon atom in graphite by $v_{\mathrm{A}}$; then the radius of a semiempirical cluster consisting of $g$ atoms can be presented in the following form:

$$
r=\left(\frac{3 g v_{\mathrm{A}}}{2 \pi}\right)^{1 / 3}. \tag{6}
$$

The quantity of atoms $g^{*}$ that form the critical cluster for each of the cases of formation is defined by corresponding expressions:

$$
g_{1}^{*}=\frac{2 \pi v_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}}{3(k T \ln S)^{3}}\left[2 \sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{m}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{g}}-\sigma_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{g}}\right]^{3}, \tag{7}
$$

for the base regime

$$
g_{2}^{*}=\frac{2 \pi v_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}}{3(k T \ln S)^{3}}\left[\sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{m}}\right]^{3}. \tag{8}
$$

Let us present several numerical estimates. For $T \sim 1000 \mathrm{~K}, S \sim 10$, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{m}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{g}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{g}} \sim 1 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^{2}$ we obtain the following values: $g_{1}^{*} \sim 40$ atoms and $g_{2}^{*} \sim 5$. The free energy of the formation of the critical cluster $\Delta F^{*}$ can be presented in the form [2]

$$
\Delta F^{*}=\frac{g^{*} k T \ln S}{2}.
$$

Thus, the free energies of formation of the critical cluster for two considered cases by the order of magnitude are equal to $\Delta F_{1}^{*} \sim 4 \cdot 10^{4} \mathrm{~k}$ and $\Delta F_{2}^{*} \sim 5 \cdot 10^{3} \mathrm{~k}$.

Since the nucleation rate $I$ [2, 3] can be represented as

$$
I \sim D \exp \left[-\frac{\Delta F^{*}}{k T}\right],
$$

where $D$ is the coefficient of carbon diffusion in the material of the catalytic substrate, then the rate of nucleation of carbon clusters under a soot particle in a supersaturated solid solution is many orders of magnitude higher than the nucleation rate on a free interface.

After the stage of diffusion interaction between neighboring clusters [4], a tubular nanofiber is formed for which it is thermodynamically expedient to grow outside the substrate. Following [2, 3], the rate of change in the nanofiber length $L$ is defined by the expression

$$
\frac{d L}{d t} \sim \frac{D n_{\mathrm{eq}}(S-1)}{R}.
$$

Let us compare our theoretical results with indirect experimental data [9 and references therein]. At the Heat and Mass Transfer Institute the effective surface area of the SU-100 reactor onto which the deposition of soot particles is possible is approximately $0.8 \mathrm{~m}^{2}$. For soot particles with $R \sim 25 \mathrm{~nm}$, we will estimate the quantity of carbon nanofibers $N$ formed on the reactor surface by the technique considered. Then, in order of magnitude, $N \sim 10^{14}$. If we admit that $L=1 \mu \mathrm{m}$, the wall thickness is $1 \mathrm{~nm}$, and the fiber density is approximately equal to the graphite density $\left(2000 \mathrm{~kg} / \mathrm{m}^{3}\right)$, then the maximum total mass of all the fibers in one cycle of growth is about $1 \mathrm{~g}$; in order of magnitude it coincides with the experimental data obtained in plasma pyrolysis of hydrocarbon gases.

Conclusions. Plasma pyrolysis of hydrocarbon gases above a catalytic metal substrate leads to the formation of soot particles in the volume of the reactor and to their subsequent deposition on the substrate under the action of thermophoresis. Here, due to the diffusion of carbon atoms inside the catalytic substrate, a solid solution is formed.

We considered the free energy and the possibility of formation of carbon clusters on the interface between a soot nanoparticle and the catalytic metal substrate as a result of the formation of the supersaturated solid solution of carbon. As is known, the process of formation of carbon clusters is one of the most important constitutive processes of the formation of a nanofiber according to the VLS scheme [2].

It is shown that the presence, on the substrate, of a soot nanoparticle with base radius not smaller than several nanometers in many orders of magnitude increases the rate of nucleation of carbon clusters, which raises the probability of formation of carbon nanofibers in plasma pyrolysis as compared to conventional methods [7].

The problems of optimization of deposition of soot nanoparticles and of growth of carbon nanofibers will be considered in further publications.
