# LETTERS TO THE EDITORS
## ADSORPTION OF IMPURITIES AT GROWTH STEPS

### R. J. DAVEY
Technisch-Chemisches Laboratorium, ETH, CH-8006, Switzerland

Received 13 January 1975

The data of Davey and Mullin for the growth of the {100} faces of ammonium dihydrogen phosphate from aqueous solution in the presence of $CrCl_{3} \cdot 6 H_{2} O$ is reconsidered in relation to the available models describing the effect of impurity adsorption at growth steps on step velocity.

For the growth of the {100} faces of ammonium dihydrogen phosphate (ADP) from aqueous solution, Davey and Mullin¹) have recently reported the effect of added $CrCl_{3} \cdot 6 H_{2} O$ on the advance velocities of growth layers (step bunches) in the [001] direction. For concentration $c_{1}$ of $CrCl_{3} \cdot 6 H_{2} O$ in solution for which $c_{1}<1.0 ×10^{-2} kg / m^{3}$, the layer velocity (at a fixed relative supersaturation of 0.052) remained unchanged from its value in pure solution $(5.8 ×10^{-7} m / s)$ , while for $c_{1}>1.0 ×10^{-2} kg / m^{3}$ a rapid decrease in layer velocity occurred. These data are shown in fig. 1 by the full circles. It was suggested²) that this behaviour resulted from adsorption of the impurity species (possibly $Cr(H_{2} O)_{4}(OH)_{2}^{+}$ and $Cl^{-}$ ) at the growth steps.

In this note a further discussion of these data is presented, in the light of an extended analysis of the available models describing the effect of impurity adsorption at the step on step velocity.

### The model of Sears³)
Sears gives a qualitative description of a model in which the number of growth sites along the step decreases with increasing impurity adsorption. This results in a step velocity which is insensitive to impurity adsorption until monostep coverage is achieved, since providing some free step lengths exist the step can advance due to the spontaneous formation of kinks in these regions of the step.

### The model of Albon and Dunning⁴,⁵)
This represents a quantification of the Sears model in which the probability of a solute molecule finding a free step length is evaluated, and the concept of monostep coverage correctly interpreted as being the concentration of adsorbed impurities at which free step lengths are equal to, or less than, the diameter of the critical nucleus since it is at this point that the step can no longer progress. ### The model of Cabrera and Vermilyea $^{6)}$

In this model step motion is not impeded due to direct adsorption at the step but rather due to the step encountering impurity species which are adsorbed on the ledges ahead of the steps. Reduction in step velocity then results from increased curvature of the steps as they squeeze between the impurities. For a density of $x$ impurity species per (molecular spacing) $^{2}$ ahead of the step (assuming a lattice of adsorbed impurities $1 / x^{\frac{1}{2}}$ from each other), the relationship between $V, V_{0}$ and $d$ is*

$$V=V_{0}\left[\left(1-\mathrm{d} x^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}. \quad (2)$$

Fig. 2 shows the computed form of this equation with $V_{0}=5.8 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ and various values of $d$. The distribution of impurity species in this model is precisely specified, so that for $1 / x^{\frac{1}{2}} \leq d$ the step velocity falls rapidly to zero. However, there is no region for which the velocity is unaffected by impurity adsorption, as proposed in Sears' model.

Since Davey and Mullin $^{1)}$ have shown that adsorption on the ledge may decrease the step velocity if surface diffusion is rate controlling, it may be illustrative to combine the reduction in surface flux with the reduction due to the step squeezing between impurities. The reduction in step velocity due to adsorption on the ledge is given by:

$$V=V_{0}(1-x), \quad (3)$$

where $x$ replaces $A_{1}$ in Davey and Mullin's eq. $(5)^{1)}$.

### Data-model comparison

It is clear from the above considerations that the critical impurity concentration predicted by Sears $^{3)}$ and observed by Davey and Mullin $^{1)}$ does not appear in the quantitative models of Albon and Dunning or Cabrera and Vermilyea. However, a true confrontation between experiment and theory does require knowledge of the $c_{1}(c)$ and $c_{1}(x)$ isotherms which unfortunately is not available. Nevertheless, despite the absence of such information one can draw the following tentative conclusions:

(1) Cabrera-Vermilyea model. Adsorption on the ledges between steps is, according to Davey and Mullin $^{1)}$, well described by the isotherm:

$$x=K c_{1} /\left(1+K c_{1}\right), \quad (5)$$

$$d=2 a \gamma / k T \sigma,$$

## Conclusions
The above considerations indicate:
(a) The model of Sears for adsorption of impurities at the step is not consistent with the quantitative descriptions of Albon and Dunning or Cabrera and Vermilyea.
(b) The data of Davey and Mullin $^{1}$ ), while consistent with the model of Sears, can only be confronted with the Albon and Dunning, and Cabrera and Vermilyea models if assumptions regarding the adsorption of the impurity on the crystal surface are made. When this is done, the Albon and Dunning model gives a self-consistent description of the data.
