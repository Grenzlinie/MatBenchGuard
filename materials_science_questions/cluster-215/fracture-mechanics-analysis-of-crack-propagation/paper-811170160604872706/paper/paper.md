# DEPENDENCE OF FATIGUE CRACK CLOSURE BEHAVIOR ON VOLUME FRACTION OF MARTENSITE IN DUAL-PHASE STEELS

Lizhi Sun, Shouxin Li, Qishan Zang and Zhongguang Wang

State Key Laboratory for Fatigue and Fracture of Materials
Institute of Metal Research, Academia Sinica, Shenyang 110015, P.R. China

(Received June 9, 1994)
(Revised September 15, 1994)

## Introduction

In the threshold stage, the fatigue crack propagation process is very sensitive to the microstructure of materials. This sensitivity arises as a result of microstructure-induced crack deflection[1,2] and crack closure[3] which effectively shield the crack tip from the far field stress intensity range. Roughness-induced crack closure mechanism is the most prevalent one among the three major closure mechanisms[3] (i.e., plasticity-induced closure, oxide-induced closure and roughness-induced closure) in the near-threshold fatigue crack propagation stage.

The ferrite/martensite dual-phase steel is a high-strength low-alloy steel for potential applications in the automobile industry. Considerable attention has been paid toward the fatigue crack propagation of the material. With the stimulation of experimental investigations[4,5], the purpose of the present paper is to discuss theoretically the relationship between the volume fraction of martensite and the fatigue crack roughness factors as well as the closure stress intensity, and to investigate the micromechanism for the crack closure behavior in the dual-phase material.

## Theoretical Analysis

In the present study, the duplex microstructure of the dual-phase steel is a continuous ferrite matrix with islands of martensite encapsulated. It may be assumed that the islands of martensite are regarded as second phase particles of spherical shape for the theoretical study. This is suitable for the case where the volume fraction of martensite is less than $\pi / 6(\approx 52 \%)$. Otherwise, the martensite islands will touch each other.

When the average diameter of martensite particles is $d_{m}$, the sphere volume will be $\pi d_{m}^{3} / 6$. If there exist n martensite particles per unit volume in the dual-phase steel, their total volume $V_{m}=n \pi d_{m}^{3} / 6$. Thus, the average spacing between the center of martensite particles $\triangle$ (Fig.1) is

$$
\triangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{n}}=\left(\frac{6}{\pi} V_{m}\right)^{-1 / 3} d_{m} \tag{1}
$$

Fatigue crack roughness factors can be characterized by the angle of facets with respect to the average fracture surface and by the height of these deflections. The dependence between roughness factors and vol-

ume fraction of martensite in the dual-phase steel will be analyzed as follows.

As the main crack propagates, it may be deflected after propagating a certain distance because of the influence of the martensite particles. In addition to the plastic damage zone caused by the propagating crack, additional plastic deformation is achieved due to the mismatch between the deformations of ferrite and martensite (i.e., the large deformation for ferrite and small deformation for martensite). The magnitude of the deformation can be estimated by means of the Eshelby's equivalent inclusion theorem[6].

From Eshelby's theorem, the stresses, caused by the mismatch between one martensite particle and the matrix are written as

$$
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{rr} &= -\frac{Ed_m^3 \bar{\varepsilon}}{12(1-v)r^3} = -\frac{\eta}{r^3} \\
\sigma_{\theta\theta} &= \sigma_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{\eta}{2r^3}
\end{aligned}
\right.
$$

where $\eta = \frac{Ed_m^3 \bar{\varepsilon}}{12(1-v)}$ and $\bar{\varepsilon}$ is the misfit strain.

If only two nearest martensite particles are considered for the first approximation(Fig.2), the stresses, located at point M, can be expressed as

$$
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{rr} &= -\frac{\eta}{r^3} - \frac{\eta}{(\triangle - r)^3} \\
\sigma_{\theta\theta} &= \sigma_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{\eta}{2r^3} + \frac{\eta}{2(\triangle - r)^3}
\end{aligned}
\right.
$$

According to the Von Mises yield criterion, we can obtain the following formula when the effective stress at point M reaches the yield stress $\sigma_y$ in the ferrite matrix

$$
\frac{\eta}{r_p^3} + \frac{\eta}{(\triangle - r_p)^3} = \frac{2}{3} \sigma_y \tag{2}
$$

where $r_p$ is the size corresponding to plastic damage zone.

Whether the propagating crack is deflected or not is controlled by the damage field caused by the effect of the reinforcement phase[7]. Experimental observations[4,5] showed that slip bands formed first in the ferrite matrix ahead of the crack tip and then the plastic damage zone was created in the matrix. The hard martensite hindered crack propagation and forced the crack to deflect and deviate away from the martensite. Therefore, it is reasonable to assume that the crack surface roughness height h is equal to $r_p$, the size of the plastic damage zone caused by the mismatch between the deformations of ferrite matrix and martensite particle (Fig.2). Such a crack growth mode will result in a zig-zag crack path with periodic crack deflection around the martensite particles producing strong crack closure effects[7].

Hence, from eqs.(1) and (2) we find the following relationship between roughness height h and volume fraction of martensite $V_m$

$$
\frac{1}{h^3} + \frac{1}{\left[d_m / \left(\frac{6}{\pi} V_m\right)^{1/3} - h\right]^3} = \frac{C}{d_m^3} \tag{3}
$$

where
$$
C=\frac{8(1-v) \sigma_{y}}{E \bar{\varepsilon}} \quad, \quad h=r_{p}
$$

On the other hand, by using the idealized geometry of the crack deflection depicted in Fig.2, the de- flected angle $\theta$ can be expressed as
$$
\operatorname{tg} \theta=\frac{2 h}{\Delta}=\frac{2 h\left(\frac{6}{\pi} V_{m}\right)^{1 / 3}}{d_{m}}
$$

Suresh and Ritchie[3] proposed a roughness-induced fatigue crack closure model near the threshold
$$
\frac{K_{c l}}{K_{m a x}}=\sqrt{\frac{A t g \theta}{1+A t g \theta}}
$$

where $K_{cl}$ and $K_{max}$ are the closure stress intensity and the maximum stress intensity, respectively. Parameter $A=u_{2} / u_{1}$ represents the ratio of local sliding (Mode II) displacement $u_{2}$ vs tensile opening(Mode I) displacement $u_{1}$. Approximately, we may get $A=u_{2} / u_{1}=k_{2} / k_{1}$ with the knowledge of fracture mechanics, where $k_{1}$ and $k_{2}$ are the local Mode I and Mode II stress intensity factors for the deflected crack, respectively, which can be written following Cotterell and Rice[8] as
$$
\left\{\begin{array}{l}
k_{1}=\cos ^{3} \frac{\theta}{2} \cdot K_{I} \\
k_{2}=\sin \frac{\theta}{2} \cos ^{2} \frac{\theta}{2} \cdot K_{I}
\end{array}\right.
$$

In these equations, $K_{I}$ is the macroscopic (far-field) stress intensity factor.

Thus, parameter $A$ is rewritten as $A=\operatorname{tg}(\theta / 2)$ and finally, the relationship between $K_{cl}$ and $V_{m}$ can be calculated through the combination of eqs.(3), (5) and (6).

## Results and Discussion

For the dual-phase steel[4,5], $d_{m}=50 \mu m, E=200 GPa, v=0.3$ and $\sigma_{y}=350 MPa$ . Under a reversed load, there is a transition from monotonic loading to cyclic loading[9], i.e., $\sigma_{y} \to 2 \sigma_{y}$ . The misfit strain can be taken as the $0.2 \%$ engineering strain corresponding to the yield stress. Therefore, $C=9.8$ is derived from eq.(4). Thus, the curve between $V_{m}$ and $K_{cl} / K_{max}$ may be calculated from the combination of eqs.(3), (5) and (6) (Fig.3). It is shown that the extent of crack closure, $K_{cl} / K_{max}$ , is a strong function of the volume fraction of martensite $V_{m}$ and increases with increasing $V_{m}$ .

According to Ravichandran's formula[10] which roughly indicates the magnitude of crack closure

$$
K_{c l}=\triangle K_{t h}^{R=0.1}-\triangle K_{t h}^{R=0.9},
$$

the indicated experimental data for $\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{cl}} / \mathrm{K}_{\max }$ are obtained and presented in Table 2 and also plotted as a function of $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{m}}$ in Fig.3. A very good similarity between the theoretical and experimental values are abtained and the slopes of two curves closely match indicating good agreement.

In fact, the theoretical analysis of crack deflection processes is a complex problem. In the present model, it has been explicitly assumed that only martensite particles offer resistance to plastic damage and cause crack deflection. However, this may not be the case for all microstructures since the grain boundary of ferrite matrix may also influence the crack deflection processes[10], especially for lower martensite contents.

### Conclusions
The effects of fatigue crack roughness factors and the volume fraction of martensite on the closure behavior near the threshold in the dual-phase steel were theoretically discussed and quantitative relationships were established. It is shown that the extent of crack closure increases as the volume fraction of martensite increases. The experimental data are found to be consistent with the model predictions.
