# Strength prediction of tensile loaded graphite fiber-reinforced aluminum composites containing circular holes

Jun Su¹, Huasong Gou¹, Yan Li¹, Gaohui Wu¹,² and
Guoqin Chen¹

## Abstract
The residual strength of fiber-reinforced aluminum composite ($Gr_f$/Al) with circular holes was investigated and predicted by four prediction methods, including PSC, AVC, DZC, and the ECGM. The 'knee' points in stress–strain curve and fracture surfaces of $Gr_f$/Al with holes were analyzed. In order to apply the ECGM method, the apparent fracture energy of $Gr_f$/Al was determined through notched three-point bending test, which was 4.1 kJ/m². All four models provided prediction with good accuracy while the ratio of diameter/width of $Gr_f$/Al specimen ranged from 0.05 to 0.2. Furthermore, ECGM showed better accuracy than DZC while predicting the residual strength at the whole range (0.05–0.5).

## Keywords
composites, holes, residual strength, stress concentration

## Introduction
As a typical metal matrix composites (MMCs), contin-uous graphite fiber-reinforced aluminum composite ($Gr_f$/Al) has attracted the interest of composite manufacturers and researchers due to their high specific strength, high specific stiffness, low thermal expansion coefficient, and high thermal conductivity, etc.¹⁻⁸

The design of structures and components to withstand the initiation of failure is of prime concern to engineers. Fracture in composites is strongly dependent upon lamination order, ply-orientation,⁹ and constitutive relations. As basic building blocks of multidirectional composites, little work has been undertaken to establish the role of holes in strength of $Gr_f$/Al composites. Models¹⁰,¹¹ have been built for the residual strength prediction for graphite/epoxy composites (polymer matrix composites, PMCs), such as point stress criteria (PSC), average stress criteria (AVC),¹²,¹³ damage zone model,¹⁴,¹⁵ damage zone criterion (DZC),¹⁶ inherent flaw model,¹⁷,¹⁸ and effective crack growth model (ECGM).¹⁹ These models were applied to PMC laminates with good accuracy,¹⁸,²⁰ which were also applied to other advanced composites, such as newly developed ceramic matrix composites.²¹ However, it is not well known whether such failure can be reliably predicted for MMCs using an analysis similar to those commonly applied to PMCs.²² In addition, both DZM and ECGM models require basic parameters of laminates, such as unnotched strength, stiffness, and the apparent fracture energy ($G^*_c$) calculated from finite element analysis.²³ It is noted from their results that there is a large variation in $G^*_c$ values and hence taking average $G^*_c$ values as basic input in their analysis will be erroneous.¹⁸

## Materials and experimental methods
Aluminum alloy 6061 (Northern Light Alloy Company Ltd, Harbin, China), which contained (in wt%) 1.0 Mg, 0.8 Si, 0.7 Fe, and 0.2 Mn, was used as the matrix, while the reinforcement consisted of continuous M40J graphite fibers $(Gr_f)$ of $\sim 5\,\mu\text{m}$ in diameter (Toray Industries, Inc., Japan). $Gr_f/Al$ with $Gr_f$ content of $65\,\text{vol}\%$ was fabricated by pressure infiltration method. Heat treatment of the $Gr_f/Al$ composite involved annealing at $350^\circ\text{C}$ for half an hour and air cooling. The microstructures of transverse and longitudinal sections of $Gr_f/Al$ are shown in Figure 1. The graphite fibers distributed uniformly in aluminum matrix.

The apparent fracture energy $G_\text{c}^\ast$ was determined through notched three-point bending test for stable crack growth. $^{24}$ Six specimens and the notches were fabricated using wire cut electric discharge machine with a 0.1-mm diameter tungsten wire. The dimension and schematic of tested specimens are shown in Figure 2(a). Specimens were tested in three-point bending using a universal test machine (Instron 5569, Instron Corp., Canton, MA) with a span of 40 mm and a crosshead speed of 0.1 mm/min. The load and crosshead displacement were measured and recorded for each specimen. The trapezoidal rule, of which the crosshead displacement was applied instead of the midpoint displacement, was used to calculate the work.

The apparent fracture energy is then obtained by:$^{25}$

$$
G_\text{c}^\ast = W/A \tag{1}
$$

$$
W = \int_0^{u_l} P\mathrm{d}u \tag{2}
$$

where $W$ denotes the total work of fracture and measured by the area underneath the load–displacement curve, $A$ the area of the triangular section at the midpoint of the specimen, $P$ the load during the test, $u$ the load–point displacement, and $u_l$ the final displacement at which the load is reduced to zero.

Unidirectional $Gr_f/Al$ specimens embedded with holes in the center (Figure 2(b)), with diameters listed in Table 1, were used for tensile test. The holes were machined by electric spark machining. A universal test machine (Instron 5569, Instron Corp., Canton, MA) with a loading rate of 0.5 mm/min was used for the tensile test. The results of each tensile strength are the averages of five specimens. Morphologies of $Gr_f/Al$ and fractured surfaces of tensile specimens were observed by scanning electronic microscopy (Quanta 200, FEI Company, Holland).

Residual strength of Gr/Al with circular holes

Figure 5 presents stress-strain curves for the Gr/Al specimens with circular holes. The curves show two stages. For Gr/Al without hole, the initial stage was linear with a slope corresponding to 278.2 GPa. When the stress reached 121.5 MPa, there was a reduction in gradient to give the characteristic 'knee-point'²⁶ (marked as 'K' in Figure 4) in the curve. The next stage was also linear but with the slope equal to 240.1 GPa that was used as basic material parameter for the prediction of ECGM. The 'knee-point' is where the matrix began to yield.²⁶ However, because of holes, the global slope of the curves was lower than specimen without hole. For the high volume fraction (65 vol%) of fiber in Gr/Al, the yielding of matrix was not obvious compared with Chen et al.,²⁶ in which fiber fraction is 40 vol%.

The yielding of aluminum matrix accompanied with its plastic deformation, which resulted in the 'knee-point' in stress-strain curve. At last, plastic fracture surface of aluminum formed after final failure of $Gr_f$/Al composites.

Stress concentration factors (SCFs) for composite laminates with finite width can be determined using equation in Refs.: $^{19,27}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{K_{T}^{\infty}}{K_{T}}= & \frac{3(1-D / W)}{2+(1-D / W)^{3}} \\
& +\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{D}{W} M\right)^{6}\left(K_{T}^{\infty}-3\right)\left[1-\left(\frac{D}{W} M\right)^{2}\right]
\end{aligned} \quad (3)
$$

The effect of the hole-diameter on the residual strength of $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathrm{f}} / \mathrm{Al}$ is illustrated in Figure 9. The illustration in Figure 9 is the normalized applied stresses evaluated for two different $\Delta c=0.001$ and 0.0001 for the ECGM. Also, $\Delta c$ is the damage increment in the ECGM simulation. The two curves are almost overlapped. The max absolute error between the max values of the two curves was less than 0.02. Therefore, the convergence of the ECGM prediction was reached, and $\Delta c=0.001$ was chosen for all the simulations. The relative error of residual strength prediction by each model was listed in Table 1. All four models provided prediction with good accuracy while the ratio of diameter/width of $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathrm{f}} / \mathrm{Al}$ specimen ranged from 0.05 to 0.2. Furthermore, ECGM showed better accuracy than DZC while predicting the residual strength at the whole range (0.050.5). The apparent fracture energy obtained from three-point bending test is reliable for the ECGM prediction.

The apparent fracture energy used in AfaghiKhatibi et al. $^{19}$ was obtained through finite element analysis, and chosen from a series of $G_{\mathrm{c}}^{*}$, through which the prediction of DZM was most close to the residual strength of certain notched specimen. $^{23}$ In this study, the apparent fracture energy was determined from notched three-point bending test, and the accuracy was enough for the prediction. So, the notched three-point bending test may be used to replace the calculation of apparent fracture energy in the ECGM for that the test specimen and test method are simple.

## Conclusions
Apparent fracture energy of $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathrm{f}} / \mathrm{Al}$ is $4.1 \mathrm{~kJ} / \mathrm{m}^{2}$. Smoothing load-displacement curve was obtained through notched three-point bending test. There is ' $\mathrm{K}$ ' point in the stress-strain curve of $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathrm{f}} / \mathrm{Al}$ composite with and without holes, which is caused by yielding of aluminum matrix.

The apparent fracture energy determined through notched three-point bending test is suitable for the ECGM model, and this test method maybe used to replace the calculation method determining apparent fracture energy.

PSC and AVC methods provided prediction with good accuracy with circular holes $(D / W=0.05-0.2)$, while higher relative error occurred at larger holes $(D / W=0.5)$. Furthermore, ECGM with standard deviation of less than $4 \%$ showed better accuracy than DZC while predicting the residual strength at the whole range (0.05-0.5). DZC and ECGM can be utilized for the accurate prediction of residual strength for $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathrm{f}} / \mathrm{Al}$ composites with circular holes.
