## ABSTRACT

The delamination damage caused by the drilling thrust has been recognized as one of the major problems during drilling. Based on the earlier concentrated-load model proposed, the thrust force is the main cause to delaminate at the drill exit in workpiece. However, the central concentrated loading (twist drill) is considered a special case of the general condition also with circular loading (saw drill, candle stick drill and core drill). A comprehensive critical thrust force model considering the effects of the peripheral moment for saw drill, candle stick drill and core drill compared with twist drill is constructed in the present study. In this analysis, the critical thrust force associated with the effect of distributed peripheral moment causing the onset of delamination in use of special drills is predicted and discussed.

## 1. Introduction

Drilling is the most commonly employed operation of secondary machining for fiber-reinforced materials owing to the need for structure joining. The delamination damage caused by the tool thrust has been recognized as one of the major problems during drilling. The fact that a rapid increase in feed rate at the end of drilling will cause the cracking around the exit edge of the hole was first reported (Kobayashi, 1967). The drill geometry was also considered important for drilling results (Galloway, 1957). Twist drills are widely used in industry to produce holes rapidly and economically. The delamination in drilling has been correlated to the thrust force of the twist drill. Hocheng and Dharan firstly employed linear elastic fracture mechanics (LEFM) method and solved for the critical thrust force that relates the delamination of composite laminates to drilling parameters and composite material properties (Hocheng and Dharan, 1990). Chandrasekharan et al. proposed a mechanistic approach and found that the thrust force is primarily a function of feed rate and tool geometry (Chandrasekharan et al., 1995). Hocheng and Tsao developed a series of analytical models for saw drill, candle stick drill and core drill for correlating the thrust force at the onset of delamination (Hocheng and Tsao, 2003). Although significant efforts have been made to realize the drilling-induced delamination for saw drill, candle stick drill and core drill, there have been few papers reporting the effect of distributed peripheral moment on delamination in drilling composite materials based on the mechanical and energy analysis.

This paper presents a series of drilling model to explain the effect of the peripheral moment on delamination in drilling composite materials by saw drill, candle stick drill and core drill. The critical thrust force with the effect of distributed peripheral moment was compared with the saw drill, candle stick drill and core drill without the effect of peripheral moment.

## 2. Delamination analysis
### 2.1. Physical model

At the propagation of delamination, the drill movement of distance dX is associated with the work done by the thrust force F, which is used to deflect the plate as well as to propagate the interlaminar crack. The energy balance equation gives
$$
G_{\mathrm{IC}} \mathrm{d} A=F \mathrm{d} X-\mathrm{d} U \quad(1)
$$
where $\mathrm{d} U$ is the infinitesimal strain energy, $\mathrm{d} A$ is the increase in the area of the delamination crack, and $G_{\mathrm{IC}}$ is the critical crack propagation energy per unit area in Mode I. The value of $G_{\mathrm{IC}}$ is assumed a constant to be a mild function of strain rate (Saghizadeh and Dharan, 1986).

### 2.2. Mathematical analysis
#### 2.2.1. Twist drill without peripheral moment

In Fig. 1, the center of the circular plate is loaded by a twist drill of diameter $d . F_{\mathrm{A}}$ is the thrust force, $X$ is the displacement, $H$ is the workpiece thickness, $h$ is the uncut depth under tool, and $a$ is the radius of delamination.

For a circular plate subject to clamped ends and a concentrated load, the thrust force at the onset of crack propagation can be calculated (Hocheng and Dharan, 1990)
$$
F_{\mathrm{A}}=\pi\left[\frac{8 G_{\mathrm{IC}} E h^{3}}{3\left(1-v^{2}\right)}\right]^{1 / 2}=\pi \sqrt{32 G_{\mathrm{IC}} M} \quad(2)
$$
where $M$ is flexural rigidity of the plate, $E$ is Young's modulus, and $v$ is Poisson's ratio.

#### 2.2.2. Saw drill with peripheral moment

Fig. 2 depicts a saw drill with distributed peripheral moment and the induced delamination, where $c$ is the radius of saw drill. $F_{\mathrm{SD}}$ is the thrust force of saw drill with distributed peripheral moment.

![](./images/811951713178091521_4.jpg)

Fig. 2 - Circular plate model of delamination for saw drill with peripheral moment.

The deflection of the circular plate is found (Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Keiger, 1959):

(i) $0 \leq r \leq c$ (inner portion)
$$
X=\frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}}{8 \pi M}\left[\left(c^{2}+r^{2}\right) \ln \frac{c}{a}+\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}+r^{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{c^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)\right] \quad(3)
$$

(ii) $c \leq r \leq a$ (outer portion)
$$
X=\frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}}{8 \pi M}\left[\left(c^{2}+r^{2}\right) \ln \frac{r}{a}+\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}-r^{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{c^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)\right] \quad(4)
$$

The stored strain energy is given as follows (Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Keiger, 1959):

(i) $0 \leq r \leq c$ (inner portion)
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{1} & =\pi M \int_{0}^{c}\left[\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} X}{\mathrm{~d} r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} r}\right)^{2}-2(1-v)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} X}{\mathrm{~d} r^{2}} \times \frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} r}\right)\right] r \mathrm{~d} r \\
& =\frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}^{2} c^{2}(1+v)}{64 \pi M}\left(4 \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}+1+\frac{c^{4}}{a^{4}}+4 \ln \frac{c}{a}-\frac{2 c^{2}}{a^{2}}-\frac{4 c^{2}}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a}\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

(ii) $c \leq r \leq a$ (outer portion)
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{2} & =\pi M \int_{c}^{a}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} X}{\mathrm{~d} r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} r}\right)^{2} r \mathrm{~d} r \\
& =\frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}^{2}}{32 \pi M}\left(a^{2}-c^{2}+\frac{c^{4}}{a^{2}}-4 c^{2} \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}-\frac{c^{6}}{a^{4}}+\frac{4 c^{4}}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a}\right) \quad(6)
\end{aligned}
$$

The total strain energy is then
$$
\begin{aligned}
U=U_{1} & +U_{2}=\frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}^{2}}{64 \pi M}\left\{\left[2 a^{2}-c^{2}-\frac{c^{6}}{a^{4}}-4 c^{2} \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}+\frac{4 c^{4}}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a}\right]\right. \\
& \left.+v\left[c^{2}-\frac{2 c^{4}}{a^{2}}+\frac{c^{6}}{a^{4}}+4 c^{2} \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}+4 c^{2} \ln \frac{c}{a}-\frac{4 c^{4}}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a}\right]\right\}
\end{aligned}
$$

![](./images/811951713178091521_5.jpg)

Fig. 3 - Circular plate model of delamination for candle stick drill with peripheral moment.

Differentiation of Eq. (7) with respect to $a$ yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d} U}{\mathrm{~d} a}= & \frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}^{2}}{16 \pi M}\left\{\left[a-\frac{c^{2}}{a}-\frac{c^{4}}{a^{3}}+\frac{c^{6}}{a^{5}}+\frac{2 c^{2}}{a} \ln \frac{c}{a}-\frac{2 c^{4}}{a^{3}} \ln \frac{c}{a}\right]\right. \\
& \left.+v\left[-\frac{c^{2}}{a}+\frac{2 c^{4}}{a^{3}}-\frac{c^{6}}{a^{5}}-\frac{2 c^{2}}{a} \ln \frac{c}{a}+\frac{2 c^{4}}{a^{3}} \ln \frac{c}{a}\right]\right\}
\end{aligned}
$$

At $r=c$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} a}=\frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}}{8 \pi M}\left(a-\frac{2 c^{2}}{a}+\frac{c^{4}}{a^{3}}\right)
$$

The critical thrust force of saw drill with distributed peripheral moment $(F_{\mathrm{SD}})$ at the onset of crack propagation can be derived by substitution of Eqs. (8) and (9) into Eq. (1)

$$
F_{\mathrm{SD}}=\pi \sqrt{\frac{32 G_{\mathrm{IC}} M}{C_{1}+v C_{2}}}
$$

where $C_{1}=1-(3+2 \ln s) s^{2}+(3+2 \ln s) s^{4}-s^{6}, \quad C_{2}=(1+2$ $\ln s) s^{2}-(2+2 \ln s) s^{4}+s^{6}$ and $s=c / a$.

The comparison of $F_{\mathrm{SD}}$ and $F_{\mathrm{A}}$ in Eqs. (10) and (2) gives

$$
\frac{F_{\mathrm{SD}}}{F_{\mathrm{A}}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{C_{1}+v C_{2}}}
$$

### 2.2.3. Candle stick drill with peripheral moment
Fig. 3 depicts the schematics of a candle stick drill with distributed peripheral moment and the induced delamination. The thrust force of the candle stick drill with distributed peripheral moment $(F_{\mathrm{CD}})$ can be expressed as follows:

$$
F_{\mathrm{CD}}=p_{1}+p_{2}
$$

where $p_{1}$ and $p_{2}$ are the central concentrated force and the peripheral circular force, respectively, as shown in Fig. 3. Let

$$
p_{2}=\alpha p_{1}
$$

Using superposition theorem of twist drill and saw drill, the thrust force $p_{1}$ can be calculated as:

$$
p_{1}=\pi \sqrt{\frac{32 G_{\mathrm{IC}} M}{1+\alpha^{2}\left(C_{1}+v C_{2}\right)}}
$$

Substituting Eqs. (13) and (14) into Eq. (12), one obtains the thrust force of the candle stick drill with distributed peripheral moment at the onset of crack propagation.

$$
F_{\mathrm{CD}}=\pi(1+\alpha) \sqrt{\frac{32 G_{\mathrm{IC}} M}{1+\alpha^{2}\left(C_{1}+v C_{2}\right)}}
$$

The comparison of $F_{\mathrm{CD}}$ and $F_{\mathrm{A}}$ in Eqs. (15) and (2) gives

$$
\frac{F_{\mathrm{CD}}}{F_{\mathrm{A}}}=(1+\alpha) \sqrt{\frac{1}{1+\alpha^{2}\left(C_{1}+v C_{2}\right)}}
$$

### 2.2.4. Core drill with peripheral moment
Fig. 4 depicts the schematics of a core drill with distributed peripheral moment and the induced delamination.

The deflection of a circular plate of radius $a$, which is clamped and subjected to a distributed load over an annulus external and internal radii $c$ and $c^{*}$, respectively, is given by (Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Keiger, 1959).

(i) $0 \leq r \leq c$ (inner portion)

$$
\begin{aligned}
X= & \frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}}{64 \pi M}\left[4 a^{2}-3\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)-\frac{2 r^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}}\right. \\
& +4\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right) \ln \frac{c}{a}-\frac{4 c^{* 4}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)+8 r^{2} \ln \frac{c}{a} \\
& \left.-\frac{8 r^{2} c^{* 2}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

(ii) $c \leq r \leq a$ (outer portion)

$$
\begin{aligned}
X= & \frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}}{32 \pi M}\left[2 a^{2}-2 r^{2}+2\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right) \ln \frac{r}{a}+4 r^{2} \ln \frac{r}{a}\right. \\
& \left.+\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)-\frac{r^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}}\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

![](./images/811951713178091521_6.jpg)

Fig. 4 - Circular plate model of delamination for core drill with peripheral moment.

where $F_{\mathrm{RD}}$ is the thrust force of the core drill with distributed peripheral moment. $c^*$ and $c$ are the inner and outer radii of core drill, respectively. $t$ is the thickness of core drill, and $\beta$ is the ratio of thickness to radius of core drill (namely, $\beta = t/c$).

Differentiation of Eq. (18) with respect to $a$ yields

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} a}=\frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}}{64 \pi M}\left[8 a-\frac{8 r^{2}}{a}-\frac{4\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a}+\frac{4 r^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{3}}\right]
\tag{19}
$$

The stored strain energy is

(i) $0 \leq r \leq c$ (inner portion)

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{1}= & \pi M \int_{0}^{c}\left[\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} X}{\mathrm{~d} r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} r}\right)^{2}-2(1-v)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} X}{\mathrm{~d} r^{2}} \times \frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} r}\right)\right] \\
r \mathrm{~d} r= & \frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}^{2} c^{2}(1+v)}{256 \pi M}\left[16 \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}+\frac{16 c^{* 4}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)^{2}} \ln ^{2}(1-\beta)\right. \\
& -\frac{8\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a}+\frac{\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{a^{4}}-\frac{32 c^{* 2}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta) \ln \frac{c}{a} \\
& \left.+\frac{8 c^{* 2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)\right]
\tag{20}
\end{aligned}
$$

(ii) $c \leq r \leq a$ (outer portion)

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{2}= & \pi M \int_{c}^{a}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} X}{\mathrm{~d} r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r} \frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{~d} r}\right)^{2} r \mathrm{~d} r \\
= & \frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}^{2}}{64 \pi M}\left[2 a^{2}-2 c^{2}+\frac{\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{2 a^{2}}-8 c^{2} \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{4 c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a}-\frac{c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{2 a^{4}}\right]
\tag{21}
\end{aligned}
$$

The total strain energy is

$$
\begin{aligned}
U= & U_{1}+U_{2} \\
= & \frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}^{2}}{256 \pi M}\left\{\left[8 a^{2}-8 c^{2}+\frac{2\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{a^{2}}-16 c^{2} \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}\right.\right. \\
& +\frac{16 c^{2} c^{* 4}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)^{2}} \ln ^{2}(1-\beta)+\frac{8 c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a} \\
& -\frac{32 c^{2} c^{* 2}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta) \ln \frac{c}{a}+\frac{8 c^{2} c^{* 2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta) \\
& \left.-\frac{c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{a^{4}}\right]+v\left[16 c^{2} \ln ^{2} \frac{c}{a}+\frac{16 c^{2} c^{* 2}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)^{2}} \ln ^{2}(1-\beta)\right. \\
& -\frac{8 c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}} \ln \frac{c}{a}-\frac{32 c^{2} c^{* 2}}{\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta) \ln \frac{c}{a} \\
& \left.\left.+\frac{8 c^{2} c^{* 2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{2}\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)+\frac{c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{a^{4}}\right]\right\}
\tag{22}
\end{aligned}
$$

Differentiation of Eq. (22) with respect to $a$ yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d} U}{\mathrm{~d} a}= & \frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}^{2}}{64 \pi M}\left\{\left[4 a+\frac{8 c^{2}}{a} \ln \frac{c}{a}+\frac{8 c^{2} c^{* 2}}{a\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)\right.\right. \\
& -\frac{\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{a^{3}}-\frac{4 c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{3}} \ln \frac{c}{a}-\frac{2 c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{3}} \\
& \left.-\frac{4 c^{2} c^{* 2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{3}\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)+\frac{c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{a^{5}}\right] \\
& +v\left[-\frac{8 c^{2}}{a} \ln \frac{c}{a}+\frac{8 c^{2} c^{* 2}}{a\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)+\frac{2 c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{3}}\right. \\
& -\frac{4 c^{2} c^{* 2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{3}\left(c^{2}-c^{* 2}\right)} \ln (1-\beta)+\frac{4 c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)}{a^{3}} \ln \frac{c}{a} \\
& \left.\left.-\frac{c^{2}\left(c^{2}+c^{* 2}\right)^{2}}{a^{5}}\right]\right\}
\tag{23}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting Eqs. (19) and (23) into Eq. (1), one obtains the thrust force of core drill with distributed peripheral moment at the onset of crack propagation as

$$
F_{\mathrm{RD}}=\pi \sqrt{\frac{32 G_{\mathrm{IC}} M}{C_{3}+v C_{4}}}
\tag{24}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{3}= & 1-\left[\left(2-2 \beta+\frac{3}{2} \beta^{2}\right)+2 \ln s+\frac{2(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta(2-\beta)} \ln (1-\beta)\right] s^{2} \\
& +\left\{\left(2-2 \beta+\beta^{2}\right)\left[\frac{\left(2-\beta+\beta^{2}\right)}{2}+\ln s\right.\right. \\
& \left.\left.+\frac{(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta(2-\beta)} \ln (1-\beta)\right]\right\} s^{4}-\frac{\left(2-2 \beta+\beta^{2}\right)^{2}}{4} s^{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{4}= & {\left[2 \ln s-\frac{2(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta(2-\beta)} \ln (1-\beta)\right] s^{2} } \\
& +\left\{\left(2-2 \beta+\beta^{2}\right)\left[-\frac{1}{2}-\ln s+\frac{(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta(2-\beta)} \ln (1-\beta)\right]\right\} s^{4} \\
& +\frac{\left(2-2 \beta+\beta^{2}\right)^{2}}{4} s^{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

The comparison of $F_{\mathrm{RD}}$ and $F_{\mathrm{A}}$ in Eqs. (24) and (2) gives

$$
\frac{F_{\mathrm{RD}}}{F_{\mathrm{A}}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{C_{3}+v C_{4}}}
\tag{25}
$$

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Effect of saw drill on threshold thrust force

Fig. 5 depicts the theoretical critical thrust ratio for saw drill with and without peripheral moment. It is seen that the thrust ratio for saw drill without peripheral moment is slightly larger than the thrust ratio for saw drill with peripheral moment

![](./images/811951713178091521_7.jpg)

up to $s \approx 0.85$. It means that the peripheral moment can contribute to the delamination in addition to thrust force in drilling composite materials. As pointed out by DiPaolo et al., the spalling occurred via Mode I (opening) and Mode III (tearing) damage mechanism (DiPaolo et al., 1996). The Mode III was subjected to the drilling torque and a twisting due to the combination of the downward thrust force and the back rake angle along the cutting lips. Fig. 5 also shows that the increasing Poisson's ratio (large $\nu$) has a mild effect on the growth of the critical thrust force for the saw drill with peripheral moment.

The results of the theoretical critical thrust ratio for candle stick drill with and without peripheral moment are presented in Fig. 6(a). ### 3.3. Effect of core drill on threshold thrust force

Fig. 7(a) compares the theoretical critical thrust ratio of core drill with and without peripheral moment with varying $s$. In this figure, the critical thrust ratio of core drill with and without peripheral moment increases with the reduction of $\beta$. The critical thrust ratio of core drill without peripheral moment is much higher than core drill with peripheral moment. Fig. 7(b) shows that the increasing Poisson's ratio (large $\nu$) has little difference on the growth of the critical thrust force for the core drill with peripheral moment. However, the critical thrust ratio of core drill with peripheral moment is below 1 at certain $s$ value between 0 and 0.6.

## 4. Conclusions

A comprehensive analysis for critical thrust ratio associated with the peripheral drilling moment using special drills (saw drill, candle stick drill and core drill) in drilling of composite materials is developed in the present study. The analytical results are obtained based on classical elasticity, linear elastic fracture mechanics and energy conservation. The results reveal that, the critical thrust force of the special drills considering peripheral drilling moment is lower than those without peripheral moment. In other words, the peripheral moment can cause larger delamination at the drill exit in the workpiece.
