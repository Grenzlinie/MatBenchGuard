## ABSTRACT

The mixed mode I/II fracture initiation angle and the crack growth trajectory of a soft rock (Guiting limestone) were investigated experimentally and theoretically for two different shaped test specimens with various sizes. It was observed that for similar mode mixities in the two specimens, the fracture paths grew in two different trajectories. It is shown that the observed crack path and the fracture initiation angle can be predicted theoretically by using a generalized form of the maximum tangential stress criterion. The main difference in the fracture initiation angles was found to be related to the magnitude and sign of the T-stress.

## 1. Introduction

In many practical applications of rock engineering, such as rock cutting, rock fragmentation, excavation, and rock slope stability analysis, the investigation of the fracture path is an important task for optimizing the size of rock pieces or controlling the stability of cracked rock structures. Most fractures in rock structures occur under complex loading states and usually under a combination of opening and sliding deformation (mixed mode I/II). For example, hydraulic fractures initiated from the wall of inclined and horizontal wells propagate under mixed mode loading [1]. For another practical application, most of the observed cracks in gravity dams are prone to mixed mode fracture [2]. Under mixed mode loading conditions, fracture of cracked components and structures may grow along curvilinear paths and not necessarily along the direction of original crack. Furthermore, when an estimate of crack arrest is required, the direction of fracture initiation from existing cracks must be determined particularly under mixed mode loading. Therefore, the investigation of the fracture initiation angle and the fracture propagation path under mixed mode loading is an interesting subject for researchers working in the field of rock mechanics. There are a number of theoretical models and various experimental techniques to investigate mixed mode crack growth of rock materials. Some of the previous studies for mixed mode fracture of rocks are reviewed briefly here. Theoretical fracture criteria such as the maximum tangential stress (MTS) criterion [3], the minimum strain energy density criterion [4], the maximum energy release rate criterion [5], and the cohesive zone model [6] have been frequently used by rock or geo-material researchers to predict the resistance and the direction of mixed mode fracture growth. These fracture models are usually developed based on the state of stress, strain, energy etc. in front of the crack tip and often use the stress intensity factors ($K_{\text{I}}$ and $K_{\text{II}}$) for predicting the crack growth direction. Meanwhile, from the viewpoint of numerical and experimental studies, Xeidakis et al. [7] for example, have investigated the trajectory of crack growth for marble rock using anti-symmetric

Other rock fracture researchers have also employed edge cracked beams subjected to anti-symmetric four point bend loading to investigate the fracture path of rock or concrete materials either experimentally or numerically [9-12]. The fracture resistance and the fracture path of rocks and concrete materials under pure mode II (pure shear) loading have been also studied by researchers using different test configurations such as the four point bend shear specimen [13,14], the double edge notch (DEN) specimen [15] and the cen- tre cracked plate subjected to compressive loading [16]. However, a review of the literature reveals that the most commonly used test samples for conducting fracture tests and for studying the crack growth resistance in rocks and geo-materials are disc shape specimens e.g. [17-27].

Two frequently employed disc type specimens are the centre cracked circular disc (CCCD), subjected to diametral com- pression (often called the Brazilian disc) and the edge cracked semi circular bend (SCB) specimen subjected to three-point bend loading. The major advantages in using these two specimens are that specimens can be easily extracted from rock cores, they have a simple geometry and simple loading configuration. In addition, the test procedure is straightforward, there are few machining operations and different mode mixities may be introduced from pure mode I to pure mode II. Hence these test specimens have been used frequently to investigate mixed mode crack growth of rock materials. For example, Chen et al. [17] used the CCCD specimen for studying the fracture initiation path of anisotropic shale with different bedding orientations relative to the direction of applied load. They conducted a set of fracture experiments and also employed the numerical tech- nique of boundary element method to simulate the observed fracture paths. Liu et al. [19] have used a numerical method for modeling the mixed mode fracture process of heterogeneous rocks using different test samples including CCCD specimen. Jia et al. [20] conducted a series of mixed mode fracture experiments using the CCCD specimen to observe the fracture path of concrete under mixed mode I/II loading. More recently, Aliha [22] tested different rock types using CCCD and SCB configu- rations to study mixed mode fracture characteristics.

In this paper, the mixed mode I/II crack growth behavior of a sedimentary soft rock (Guiting limestone) is investigated experimentally and theoretically using both CCCD and SCB specimens. It is shown that the specimen geometry and the type of loading can influence strongly the fracture path under mixed mode loading. The effect of T-stress (which is a geometry dependent stress term) in addition to $K_{\text{I}}$ and $K_{\text{II}}$ is considered as an additional parameter for evaluating the observed fracture initiation angle and its path in these two samples.

### 3. Theoretical investigation of fracture trajectory

To predict the crack growth path under mixed mode conditions it is necessary to determine the fracture initiation angle at the crack tip. A number of mixed mode fracture theories have been proposed for determining the direction of brittle fracture e.g. [3-5]. The maximum tangential stress (MTS) criterion [3] is one of the well known and commonly used fracture criteria for predicting the direction of fracture angle. Based on the MTS criterion the direction of fracture initiation ($\theta_0$) for any mode mixity can be determined from:

$$
K_{\mathrm{I}} \sin \theta_{0}+K_{\mathrm{II}}\left(3 \cos \theta_{0}-1\right)=0 \tag{2}
$$

According to Eq. (2), for each mode mixity a unique value is obtained for the direction of fracture initiation in all cracked specimens. Other conventional mixed mode fracture theories e.g. [5] also predict a unique value for $\theta_0$ for each mode mixity. However, the observed fracture patterns for the CCCD and SCB specimens reveal that the fracture initiation angles and frac- ture paths were not the same for mixed mode I/II loading. For example, Fig. 3 shows the fracture paths observed in the CCCD and SCB specimens with radius of R = 50 mm but for similar values of $M^e$. It can be seen in this figure that except for pure mode I conditions ($M^e=1$) which has a fracture path along the initial crack line, the fracture paths were not the same for any other states of mixed mode I/II loading. In other words, for any two similar mode mixities (or $K_{\mathrm{I}}/K_{\mathrm{II}}$ ratio) in both sample shapes, the fracture paths differed. The deviation of crack path from the initial crack line was more pronounced for the SCB geometry and also for mode II dominant loading conditions in both sample shapes.

![](./images/811800632825479168_3.jpg)

Fig. 3. Mixed mode fracture path observed in the two limestone specimen shapes; dashed line for the CCCD specimen and the solid line for the SCB specimen (in both specimens R = 50 mm and B = 40 mm).

Since the conventional MTS criterion fails to provide good predictions for the direction of fracture initiation angle in the tested CCCD and SCB samples, a generalized form of the MTS (or GMTS) criterion is employed here to predict the observations. The generalized MTS criterion [31] suggests that fracture initiates along a direction where at a critical distance from the crack tip $r_c$ the tangential stress is a maximum. The tangential stress ($\sigma_{\theta\theta}$) component can be written as a series expansion [32,33]:

$$
\sigma_{\theta\theta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi r}}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\left[K_{\mathrm{I}}\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{3}{2}K_{\mathrm{II}}\sin\theta\right]+T\sin^{2}\theta+O(r^{1/2})
\tag{3}
$$

where $r$ and $\theta$ are the crack tip co-ordinates. $T$ is a non-singular and constant stress term which is independent of the distance from the crack tip and usually called the $T$-stress. The remaining terms of the series expansion represented by $O(r^{1/2})$ are negligible near the crack tip. Accordingly, the direction of fracture initiation ($\theta_0$) can be determined based on the GMTS criterion [31] from:

$$
\left.\frac{\partial\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta}\right|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}=0,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta^{2}}<0
\tag{4}
$$

Using Eq. (4) together with Eq. (3) gives [26,27,34,35]:

$$
[K_{\mathrm{I}}\sin\theta_{0}+K_{\mathrm{II}}(3\cos\theta_{0}-1)]-\frac{16T}{3}\sqrt{2\pi r_{c}}\cos\theta_{0}\sin\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}=0
\tag{5}
$$

$r_c$ is often taken as the size of fracture process zone in rock materials. By ignoring the effect of $T$-stress in Eq. (5), the generalized MTS criterion will be identical to the conventional MTS criterion. According to the GMTS criterion, the fracture initiation angle under mixed mode loading decreases for those specimens having a negative $T$-stress and conversely $\theta_0$ increases for the specimens with a positive $T$-stress. Therefore, in contrast with the conventional MTS criterion, the GMTS criterion is able to predict different values of $\theta_0$ for a given mode mixity. In the next section the generalized MTS criterion is used to predict the observed experimental results.

### 4. Results and discussions

In order to use the generalized MTS criterion (Eq. (5)), three parameters, $K_{\mathrm{I}}$, $K_{\mathrm{II}}$, and $T$, need to be known for any test specimen and for each mode mixity. These parameters can be written for the CCCD and SCB samples as [28]:

$$
K_{i}=
\begin{cases}
\frac{P}{RB}\sqrt{\frac{a}{\pi}}Y_{i}(a/R,\alpha) & \text{for CCCD specimen} \\
\frac{P\sqrt{\pi a}}{2RB}Y_{i}(a/R,S/R,\alpha) & \text{for SCB specimen}
\end{cases}
\quad i=\mathrm{I,\ II}
\tag{6}
$$

$$
T=
\begin{cases}
\frac{P}{\pi(R-a)B}T^{*}(a/R,\alpha) & \text{for CCCD specimen} \\
\frac{P}{2RB}T^{*}(a/R,S/R,\alpha) & \text{for SCB specimen}
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
$$

where $Y_{\mathrm{I}}$, $Y_{\mathrm{II}}$, and $T^{*}$ are the non-dimensional forms of $K_{\mathrm{I}}$, $K_{\mathrm{II}}$, and $T$, respectively. These parameters are functions of the crack length ratio ($a/R$), the crack angle $\alpha$ and also the location of loading supports ($S/R$) in the SCB specimen. The curves of $Y_{\mathrm{I}}$, $Y_{\mathrm{II}}$, and $T^{*}$ extracted from [28] for the tested CCCD and SCB specimens are given in Figs. 4 and 5 for various combinations of modes I and II. (5), a fixed value of $r_{\rm c}=2.3$ mm was used for all specimens since the critical distance in the GMTS criterion is taken as a constant material property. It can be seen that when $r_{\rm c}$ is assumed to be a fixed value for all sizes and all mode mixities, the GMTS criterion provides good predictions for the angle of fracture initiation in both CCCD and SCB specimens.

There are several methods for predicting the fracture trajectory after the initiation stage (e.g. [38,39]). The incremental crack growth method which was used in this research involves a large number of small crack extensions in appropriate directions. The direction of crack growth for each increment was determined by using the generalized MTS criterion. For each stage of incremental crack growth, the fracture parameters $K_{\rm I}$, $K_{\rm II}$, and $T$ were obtained from finite element analysis and then the direction for the next increment was determined from Eq. (5). After calculating the direction of maximum tangential stress, the crack was remodeled with a small extension along the calculated direction and the same procedure was repeated for the next increment till the whole fracture path was predicted. This technique was applied using the ABAQUS

## 5. Conclusions

- Guiting limestone was used to study mixed mode brittle fracture in the CCCD and SCB specimens with different sizes and various mixed mode loading conditions. The observed fracture initiation angle and the crack path trajectory were not the same in the CCCD and SCB specimens even under identical mode mixities. The observed trajectories depended not only on the mode mixity but also on the specimen shape.
- The generalized MTS criterion was successfully employed to predict the fracture initiation angles and the fracture trajectories in both specimen types.
- In general, the positive $T$-stress that exists in the SCB specimen can be the main reason for a higher deviation in the fracture initiation angle under mixed mode loading compared to the CCCD specimen which has a large negative $T$-stress.
- The mixed mode fracture initiation angles in both CCCD and SCB specimens were dependent on the size of test specimen. This dependency was more pronounced for CCCD specimens.
- By decreasing the size of specimen in both samples, a higher discrepancy was observed between the fracture initiation angles obtained from experiments and those predicted using the conventional MTS criterion. However, the generalized MTS criterion provided better estimates for the direction of fracture initiation for different mode mixities and various specimen sizes.
- For a given mode mixity, the fracture trajectories (in either of the CCCD and SCB limestone specimens) were in a similar manner when only the size of specimen was changed. In other words, while the size of specimen affected the fracture initiation angle, it had no significant effect on the general shape of fracture trajectory.
