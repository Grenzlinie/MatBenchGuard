## ABSTRACT

The spring-back phenomenon, an elastic recovery after sheet metalworking, will affect the accuracy of part geometry inevitably. The present paper is to explore the influence of coining force on the spring-back reduction in the V-die bending process.

The highlight of this paper is that a basic approach, incorporating material properties and geometrical parameters, is developed to estimate the amount of spring-back reduction caused by coining force upon an elementary analysis. The approach is based on the assumption that the unloading moment (as a pure-moment bending under the punch nose) will be neutralized by the applied bending moment induced by the coining force in bottoming stage.

A spring-back ratio SR is defined as a measure to predict the amount of spring-back reduction and derived as a function of normal anisotropy $R$, strain-hardening exponent $n$, thickness/punch radius ratio $t/2\rho$, die width/thickness ratio $w/t$, friction coefficient $\mu$ and half bent angle $\theta$. And a loading factor $\alpha$ will be defined to measure the amount of coining force for trying to reduce the spring-back after unloading. In the meantime, a critical loading factor $\alpha_{\mathrm{c}}$ for completely eliminating the spring-back will be also discussed herein. As above, it will reflect the reliability of the spring-back reduction mechanism in the present model.

It is thereby possible to better understand and control the spring-back reduction of V-die bending process in industry necessity.

## 1. Introduction

Sheet bending, one of the most important metalworking processes in variety of industry, is widely used in producing structural stamping parts. During sheet bending, there are several severe elastic distortion problems would be encountered such as spring-back and sidewall curl. Although much progress has been done, there are still needs for further research to solve these problems important for industrial utility. Trial-and-error methods are still being used to establish suitable procedures for solving these elastic distortion problems.

The most important die-bending process is the V-die bending which consists of two distinct parts: air bending and bottoming (as coining). During air bending stage, the elastic recovery after unloading causes the spring-back phenomenon, the bent part tends to return to the original, undeformed state. The shape of work-piece is changed with the spring-back but high precision is often an essential requirement for fine products. For this reason, precise prediction of spring-back seems a key to assess the accuracy of part geometry. Much work has been done for assessing the prediction of spring-back of sheet bending such as Refs. (Weinmann and Shippell, May 1978; Huang et al., 1989, 1992; Leu, 1997; Huang and

Leu, 1998). However the prediction of spring-back seems to be passive in improvement. To suppress or eliminate the spring-back phenomenon seems an active idea in improved modeling of bending. A simple method for eliminating spring-back is therefore very valuable and useful for the practical purpose. Several researchers had studied the spring-back reduction (Liu, 1984; Tan et al., 1992; Shu and Hung, 1996; Livatyali and Altan, 2001) in various bending processes with different ways except the present way of coining. During the bottoming stage of V-die bending, the punch force will increase rapidly and would distribute on the flat sheet (as a coining force) much like in forging. In the meantime, the coining force will produce a moment which sign is opposite to the unloading bending moment. As the above construction, coining force seems an effective and new idea to eliminate the spring-back in V-die bending.

In the present study, non-linear strain-hardening and normal anisotropic sheet metal under the plane strain condition of V-die bending are implemented in the current model. And the way of coining force induced in bottoming stage is employed to reduce the spring-back after unloading. As a result, spring-back in V-die bending is very difficult to predict. Thus the analysis of spring-back reduction becomes even more complicated as a result of coining force applied. The motivation is spring-back with coining effect appears to have received little attention in the past. Based on the elementary theory of plasticity, an attempt is made to investigate and develop an accurate model for predicting the amount of the spring-back reduction in V-die bending. On the other hand, the effects of various process parameters, including the material and geometrical characteristics, on the spring-back reduction are also confirmed. Such an approach can be used for serving as a guideline for component shape-accuracy control and spring-back reduction assessment.

## 2. Analysis

The following simplified assumptions are made for the formulation of mathematical model: (1) moderate punch radius is adopted to avoid the spring-forward occurring in so-called "coining" stage; (2) the elementary bending theory is applied to the elastic unloading; (3) Coulomb friction coefficient $\mu$ is considered; (4) Hill's anisotropic theory of plasticity (Hill, 1950) is adopted to describe the anisotropic characteristics of sheet metal.

Normal anisotropy $R$ of the sheet, a measure of the resistance to through thickness thinning, can be represented by the plastic strain ratio of width to thickness in a simple tensile test (Hill, 1950). The rigid-plastic, strain-hardening characteristic of the sheet is assumed to follow the power-law form, $\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}=K \varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^{n}$, where $\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}$ denote the effective stress and the effective strain respectively, $n$ is the strain-hardening exponent and $K$ is a material constant.

### 2.1. The spring-back reduction resulted from the coining force in bottoming

The presented model of V-die bending process is shown in Fig. 1. When bending proceeds, the deformation region OA under punch nose will invariably keep contact first, and then something separation will be produced by the compression of punch and die in bottoming stage. At this moment, the region OA seems to contact free, and the other region of bent part AB is restricted firm by the compression of punch and die (as a coining effect). The change of state for region OA from contact to separation can be seen as an unloading procedure of pure-moment bending. It would produce an unloading moment $-M_{1}$ (the reverse of $M_{1}$ at contact state). Meanwhile, other two bending moments $(M_{2}$ and $M_{3})$ induced by coining force (divided into normal and shear components in region AB) will neutralize the unloading moment $-M_{1}$. As above construction, a certain coining force is possible to eliminate the unloading moment completely (as spring-back free), and a precise bent part is possible obtained. As a reduced mechanism for trying to eliminate the spring-back defect, this model seems to be reasonable.

### 2.2. The bending moment induced by coining force

The applied bending moment $M_{1}$ of pure bending in bottoming stage is written as,

$$
M_{1}=K\left[\left(\frac{1+R}{\sqrt{1+2 R}}\right)\left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)\right]^{1+n} \frac{\rho b t}{2(1+n)},\qquad(1)
$$

where $\rho=R_{\mathrm{p}}+t / 2$, and detailed derivation can be found in Leu (1997).

Based on the assumption of uniform stress distribution and the force balance relationship, the bending moment $M_{2}$ induced by normal component of coining force can be obtained as

$$
M_{2}=(-1) \Delta P \cos \theta \cdot 2 \mu \rho \frac{1-\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta(t / 2 \rho)}{\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta},\qquad(2)
$$

where $\Delta P$ is the coining force, $\Delta \vec{P}=\Delta \vec{P}_{n}+\Delta \vec{P}_{\mathrm{t}}, \Delta P_{n}=\Delta P \cos \theta$ and $\Delta P_{\mathrm{t}}=\Delta P \sin \theta$.

Taking the same way as above, the bending moment $M_{3}$ induced by shear component of coining force can be obtained as

$$
M_{3}=\Delta P \cos \theta \tan \theta \cdot \rho\left[\frac{t}{2 \rho}+\frac{1-\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta(t / 2 \rho)}{\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta}\right].\qquad(3)
$$

From Section 2.1, the total bending moment $M$ in bottoming can be represented as $M=-M_{1}+M_{2}+M_{3}$. For mea-

suring the effect of coining force $\Delta P$ on spring-back reduction, the normal component of $\Delta P$ is assumed as $\Delta P_{n}=\Delta P \cos \theta=\alpha \sigma_{u} l_{0} b=\alpha K(n / e)^{n} l_{0} b$, where $\alpha$ denotes the loading factor defined as a measure of coining force, $l_{0}$ denotes the contact length and $\sigma_{u}=K(n / e)^{n}$ denotes the tensile strength of material.

### 2.3. The spring-back ratio SR and critical loading factor $\alpha_{c}$

For the unloading procedure is an elastic recovery which causes the spring-back phenomenon, it is assumed that the unloading moment $\Delta M$ has the same magnitude but opposite sign to the applied bending moment $M$ in bottoming,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta M= & -M=\frac{b E t^{3}}{12\left(1-v^{2}\right)}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{*}}\right) \\
= & K\left[\left(\frac{1+R}{\sqrt{1+2 R}}\right)\left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)\right]^{1+n} \frac{\rho b t}{2(1+n)}-\alpha K\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n} \\
& \times l_{0} b \rho\left[\tan \theta \frac{t}{2 \rho}+(\tan \theta+2 \mu) \frac{1-\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta(t / 2 \rho)}{\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta}\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

where the change in curvature $(1 / \rho-1 / \rho^{*})$ due to elastic unloading (spring-back), under the assumption of $\rho \theta=\rho^{*} \theta^{*}$, can be represented as $\Delta \theta / \theta=(1 / \rho-1 / \rho^{*}) /(1 / \rho)=\Delta M \rho / E I$.

Based on the above construction, the spring-back ratio SR can be defined as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SR}= & \frac{\Delta \theta}{\theta}\left(\frac{2}{3} \frac{E}{K\left(1-v^{2}\right)}\right) \\
= & \left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)^{-2}\left\{\frac{1}{1+n}\left[\left(\frac{1+R}{\sqrt{1+2 R}}\right)\left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)\right]^{1+n}\right. \\
& -\alpha\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}\left[\frac{w}{t} \frac{1}{\cos \theta}-\left(\left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)^{-1}+1\right) \tan \theta\right] \\
& \left.\times\left[\tan \theta \frac{t}{2 \rho}+(\tan \theta+2 \mu) \frac{1-\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta(t / 2 \rho)}{\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta}\right]\right\},
\end{aligned}
$$

where the SR is a function of various process parameters as $\mathrm{SR}=f(\alpha, R, n, \theta, \mu, t / 2 \rho, w / t)$. It is defined as a measure to estimate the amount of spring-back reduction in V-die bending.

As above, the condition of $\alpha=0$ denotes the case of simple pure bending which had been shown in Leu (1997). And the condition of $-M=\Delta M=0$ shows the critical value of loading factor $\alpha_{c}$ that it could possible eliminate the spring-back completely, i.e. $\mathrm{SR}=0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{\mathrm{c}}= & {\left[\left(\frac{1+R}{\sqrt{1+2 R}}\right)\left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)\right]^{1+n}(1+n)^{-1}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{-n} } \\
& \times\left[\frac{w}{t} \frac{1}{\cos \theta}-\left(\left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)^{-1}+1\right) \tan \theta\right]^{-1} \\
& \times\left[(\tan \theta+2 \mu) \frac{1-\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta(t / 2 \rho)}{\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta}+\tan \theta \frac{t}{2 \rho}\right]^{-1} .
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2.4. The lower limit of geometrical ratio $t / 2 \rho$ for V-die bending

On the geometrical relation in bottoming and the condition of contact length $l_{0}>0$, a minimum condition for geometrical constrain in V-die bending can be obtained as,

$$
\begin{gathered}
\frac{l_{0}}{t}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{w}{t \cos \theta}-\tan \theta\left[\left(\frac{t}{2 \rho}\right)^{-1}+1\right]\right\}>0 \text { or } \\
\frac{t}{2 \rho}>\frac{\sin \theta}{w / t-\sin \theta}.
\end{gathered}
$$

## 4. Conclusions

A new model, including material and geometrical characteristics, for predicting the amount of spring-back reduction resulted from the coining force in V-die bending is presented.

Based on this study, the following conclusion can be drawn:

1. The SR linearly decreases as R decreases, or $t/2\rho$, $w/t$, $\theta$ and $n$ increases. But it is noted that $\mu$ shows little effect on SR in such manner.

2. The $\alpha_{\mathrm{c}}$ decreases with respect to $\theta$ and $w/t$. But the effects of $\mu$, R and $n$ on $\alpha_{\mathrm{c}}$ are limited. A minimum value of $\alpha_{\mathrm{c}}$ for spring-back free in the case of $t/2\rho$ is found.

3. The lower limit of geometrical ratio increases as $\theta$ increases or $w/t$ decreases. The interaction of parameters $\theta$ and $w/t$ obviously shows a neutralized action in this matter.

The existing model can serve as a guideline for tool and product design, and improves understanding of the mechanism of spring-back reduction in V-die bending process.
