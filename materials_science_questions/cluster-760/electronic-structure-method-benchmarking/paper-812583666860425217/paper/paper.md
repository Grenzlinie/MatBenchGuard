# The structure and binding energy of $K^+$-ether complexes: A comparison of MP2, RI-MP2, and density functional methods

David Feller, Edoardo, Aprà, Jeff A. Nichols, and David E. Bernholdt⁽ᵃ⁾

Environmental Molecular Sciences Laboratory, Pacific Northwest Laboratory, Richland, Washington 99352

(Received 13 February 1996; accepted 23 April 1996)

The structures and binding energies of several cation:ether complexes ($K^+$:dimethyl ether, $K^+$:dimethoxyethane, $K^+$:12-crown-4 and $K^+$:18-crown-6) were determined with second and fourth order perturbation theory using correlation consistent basis sets. Several of these are the largest correlated calculations yet attempted on crown ethers. The observed systematic convergence to the complete basis set limit provides a standard by which the accuracy of previous studies can be measured and facilitates the calibration of density functional methods. Recent Fouier transform ion cyclotron resonance experiments predicted $K^+$:18-crown-6 binding energies which were significantly smaller than *ab initio* calculations. None of the potential sources of error examined in the present study were large enough to explain this difference. Although the 6-31+G* basis set used in an earlier theoretical study was smaller than the smallest of the correlation consistent basis sets, with suitable correction for basis set superposition error, it appears capable of yielding binding energies within several kcal/mol of the basis set limit. Perturbation theory calculations exploiting the "resolution of the identity'' approximation were found to faithfully reproduce binding energies and conformational differences. Although the cation–ether interaction is dominated by classical electrostatics, the accuracy of density functional techniques was found to be quite sensitive to the choice of functionals. The local density SVWN procedure performed well for binding energies and conformational differences, while underestimating $K^+$O distances by up to 0.08 Å. The gradient-corrected Becke–Lee–Yang–Parr functional underestimated the $K^+$:12c4 binding energy by 4–7 kcal/mol or 15%. © 1996 *American Institute of Physics*. [S0021-9606(96)00429-1]

## INTRODUCTION

The class of macrocyclic polyethers ($\cdots\mathrm{CH_2-O-CH_2-CH_2-O-CH_2}\cdots$) known as crown ethers displays an unusual ability to selectively bind metal cations in the presence of complex aqueous mixtures of chemically similar ions. Horwitz *et al.*¹ have proposed crown ethers for separating high-level nuclear wastes such as $^{90}\mathrm{Sr}$ and $^{137}\mathrm{Cs}$. The binding specificity of these compounds is a function of (i) the size of the cavity enclosed by the crown, (ii) the nature of the donor atoms in the ring, e.g., oxygen vs sulfur, (iii) the presence of substituents in the ligand backbone which can alter the molecule's electronic environment and (iv) a subtle balance between cation–ether and cation–water interactions. The importance of the latter point was under- scored in a recent *ab initio* study by Glendening *et al.*² Their study of the 18-crown-6 (18c6) ion exchange, cluster reactions

$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathrm{K^+:18c6+M^+(H_2O)_n{\to}M^+:18c6+K^+(H_2O)_n} \\
(\mathrm{M=Li,\ Na,\ Rb,\ and\ Cs}),
\end{gathered} \tag{1}
$$

demonstrated that a relatively small number of waters was sufficient to qualitatively reproduce the experimentally observed binding preferences in aqueous solution ($\mathrm{K^+}{>}\mathrm{Na^+}{\sim}\mathrm{Rb^+}{>}\mathrm{Cs^+}$). The notation "x-crown-y'' refers to a cyclic compound composed of $x$ nonhydrogen atoms, $y$ of which are ether oxygens. In sharp contrast to the aqueous findings, gas phase calculations showed that $\Delta H$ varied nearly linearly with the reciprocal of the cation radius, i.e., the strongest binding occurring for $\mathrm{Li^+}$ and the weakest for $\mathrm{Cs^+}$. Geometries were optimized with restricted Hartree–Fock (RHF) wave functions using the 6-31+G* polarized basis set.³⁻⁵ Binding energies were evaluated with second order Møller–Plesset perturbation theory (MP2) and corrected for the effects of basis set superposition error (BSSE). In addition to qualitatively reproducing the aqueous binding enthalpies, this level of theory provided excellent agreement with the available x-ray crystallographic structures for cation:ether complexes. $\mathrm{C-C}$ and $\mathrm{C-O}$ bonds were typically within $\pm0.01$ Å of experiment, while $\mathrm{M^+-O}$ bonds showed slightly larger deviations of $\pm0.02$ Å.

The basis set used by Glendening *et al.*² included a dif- fuse $(s,p)$ shell on oxygen to reduce the undesirable effects of BSSE. Carbon and hydrogen were treated with the some- what smaller 6-31G* basis set in order to reduce the cost of the calculations without adversely affecting the energetics. For the sake of brevity, we shall hereafter denote this hybrid basis set simply as "6-31+G*.''

Smaller basis sets are incapable of uniformly describing all of the chemical systems appearing in reaction (1). BSSE was especially troublesome for the larger $\mathrm{M^+(H_2O)_n}$ ($n{\geqslant}4$) clusters, where extensive hydrogen bonding networks increased the magnitude of the error to the point where it was a significant fraction of the total binding energy. BSSE was also a problem for $\mathrm{M^+:18c6}$ complexes with small valence

basis sets, such as 3-21G. They were found to produce errors as large as 23 kcal/mol at the RHF level of theory. In the work of Glendening et al. $^{2}$ BSSE was estimated by the full Boys-Bernardi counterpoise (CP) method. $^{6}$

Significant disagreement was reported between the only available experimental gas phase binding enthalpies and the best ab initio theoretical values. For example, MP2/6-31+G* predicted a value of $\Delta H^{298}(\mathrm{~K}^{+}: 18 \mathrm{c} 6)$ =-71.5 kcal/mol, more than 30 kcal/mol larger than the Fourier transform ion cyclotron resonance (FT-ICR) value. $^{7,8}$ For $\mathrm{Cs}^{+}: 18 \mathrm{c} 6$ the discrepancy fell to only 17 kcal/mol, but on a percentage basis the theoretical value was still 50% larger than experiment.

Attempts by Glendening et al. $^{2}$ to calibrate their ap proach led to a series of calculations on the prototypical $\mathrm{K}^{+}-\mathrm{O}$ bond in $\mathrm{K}^{+}:$dimethyl ether $(\mathrm{K}^{+}: \mathrm{DME})$. Based on the largest calculations which could be performed at the time, the authors argued that the MP2/6-31+G* bond energies were within 2 kcal/mol of the apparent complete basis set limit. They were, thus, unable to resolve the disagreement with experiment. More extensive correlation recovery, e.g., via fourth order perturbation theory, resulted in insignificant changes. The adequacy of low-order perturbation theory for metal cation-crown ether systems is attributable to the domi- nance of classical electrostatic interactions. A natural energy decomposition analysis (NEDA) $^{9,10}$ showed quantum mechanical effects, such as charge transfer, played only a minor role.

Crown ether complexes pose enormous computational challenges due to their size, as well as the relatively floppy nature of the ether backbone which results in unusually slow geometry optimizations. These factors impose a practical limit on the sophistication of the theoretical treatment which can be applied, but the unresolved disagreement with experi- ment still brings into question the accuracy of predictions made by Glendening et al. In the present work we will re- examine several aspects of the earlier study in an attempt to identify sources for error which were originally assumed to be small. In particular we will examine the use of larger basis sets and the consequences of reoptimizing geometries at the correlated level.

Because of the time consuming nature of correlated cal- culations on large ligands, we have also explored the reliabil- ity of two computationally faster alternatives to traditional perturbation theory, the mainstay of our previous crown ether studies. The first of these is the resolution of the iden- tity MP2 (RI-MP2) $^{11-13}$ method, which utilizes an auxiliary fitting basis to avoid treating the complete set of two-electronrepulsion integrals. RI-MP2 converges to the traditional MP2 result in the limit of a complete fitting basis. The second approach is density functional theory (DFT) $^{14,15}$ which has attracted intense interest in the past few years. DFT offers the promise of less drastic scaling with the size of the system than traditional ab initio methods.

## PROCEDURE

Three collections of orbital basis sets were used in the present work. For hydrogen, carbon, and oxygen these are(1) the 6-31+G* hybrid set used by Glendening et al. $^{2}$ (2) the correlation consistent basis sets defined by Dunning and co-workers, $^{16-18}$ and (3) the DZVP and DZVP2 DFT basis sets from DGauss. $^{19}$ The majority of calculations made use of either the cc-pVDZ basis, which consists of a(9s,4p,1d)→[3s,2p,1d] "double zeta'' contraction on car- bon and oxygen and a $(4 s, 1 p) \to [2 s, 1 p]$ contraction on hy drogen or an extension of this basis which contains an addi- tional shell of diffuse functions. Following the normal convention, the latter set is designated aug-cc-pVDZ. $^{17}$ Po tassium was described by either a [3s,2p,1d] basis set $^{2}$ coupled with the Hay-Wadt $^{20} n-1$ shell ECP or a series of DZ, TZ, and QZ all-electron basis sets $^{21}$ derived from theGaussian primitive set of Schääfer et al. $^{22}$

Although the cc-pVDZ basis set lacks diffuse functions such as those present in the 6-31+G* basis, it should provide a more balanced correlation treatment because of the phi- losophy underlying its construction. Nevertheless, the cc- pVDZ basis set's lack of diffuse functions unavoidably re- sults in larger BSSEs than 6-31+G*. For example, frozen core MP2 [MP2(FC)] calculations with the cc-pVDZ basisset overestimate the binding energy of the water dimer by 3.4 kcal/mol out of 7 kcal/mol, due to BSSE. The comparable error for the 6-31+G* basis is only 2.1 kcal/mol. With the aug-cc-pVDZ set, BSSE drops below 0.9 kcal/mol for thesame system. $^{23}$

As the smallest member of the correlation consistent ba- sis set family, the cc-pVDZ basis set offers the advantage of a clearly defined, systematic growth path to the complete basis set limit. Furthermore, since DFT methods are widely reported to converge more rapidly with increasing basis set size than more traditional wave function-based methods, it seemed possible that the accuracy of DFT/cc-pVDZ calcula- tions might be equivalent to much larger basis set MP2 re- sults. One of the goals of this work is to calibrate DFT meth- ods against the best available ab initio results for $M^{+}$ :ether systems.

Sporadic linear dependency problems were found for18c6 geometries in the vicinity of the $C_{i}$ minimum when using the full aug-cc-pVDZ basis set. Experimentation showed that these problems could be avoided by deleting the most diffuse hydrogen s function. Comparisons of binding energies and molecular structures obtained from both the original and the modified sets showed minimal differences. Similar conclusions regarding the removal of hydrogen dif- fuse functions were reached by Del Bene and Shavitt. $^{24}$ This basis set, which is the largest affordable basis for 18c6, will be denoted as aug-cc-pVDZ' in order to distinguish it from the original aug-cc-pVDZ set. For the $K^{+}: 18 c 6$ complex, the aug-cc-pVDZ' basis set resulted in a total of 637 basis func- tions using five-component d functions (or 675 functions with six-component d functions).

Local spin density DFT calculations were performed with the Slater $^{25} \rho^{1 / 3}$ exchange functional coupled to the Vosko-Wilk-Nusair $^{26}$ correlation function (SVWN). Gradient-corrected DFT calculations used the Becke $^{27}$ ex change functional and either the PERDEW86 $^{28}$ (BP86), or the Lee-Yang-Parr $^{29}$ (BLYP) correlation functional.

RHF and perturbation theory calculations were performed with the GAUSSIAN 92/DFT,⁵⁰ GAUSSIAN 94,³¹ or NWChem³² programs utilizing a frozen core definition which included the carbon and oxygen (1s) and potassium (1s,2s,2p) orbitals. Such core/valence calculations will be denoted as MP2(CV). Inclusion of core/valence functions on carbon and oxygen was reported to be unimportant in a study of the M⁺(H₂O)ₙ complexes.²¹ However, failure to account for core/valence effects in potassium produced undesirably large errors.

RI-MP2 calculations utilized the BV⁻¹B integral approximation described by Vahtras et al.¹² All RI-MP2 results were obtained with programs SUPERMOLECULE³³ and NWChem.³² In most cases the RI-MP2 fitting basis was taken to be the one-particle orbital basis set. Several RI-MP2 calculations on K⁺:12-crown-4 with the aug-cc-pVDZ′ basis used a fitting basis generated from the uncontracted aug-cc-pVDZ′ primitives, plus the additional Gaussians listed in Table I.³⁴ This fitting set was designed to accurately reproduce the MP2 binding energy in the absence of a counterpoise correction.

Specification of the fitting basis is essential when comparing RI-MP2 results. With large fitting sets the MP2 energy can be reproduced to within a microhartree. With smaller fitting sets the errors can be tens of millihartrees. Although adopting the orbital basis set as the fitting set was found to yield results in good agreement with MP2 for the present set of cation:ether complexes, other studies³⁴ suggest that care must be taken with small basis sets, such as cc-pVDZ or 6-31+G*. Fortunately, as will be shown, the difference between RI-MP2 and MP2 energies tends to be highly systematic, so that energy differences are reproduced quite accurately.

Preliminary DFT calculations were performed with program DeFT.³⁵ All results presented in the following tables were obtained with NWChem.³² DeFT utilizes Coulomb and exchange-correlation fitting basis sets taken from Godbout et al.¹⁹ In NWChem the same Coulomb fitting basis sets were used, but the exchange-correlation potential was evaluated on a numerical grid, without any fitting procedure. Calculations performed with GAUSSIAN and SUPERMOLECULE used the spherical (d,f,g) Gaussian components, while DeFT and NWChem calculations included the complete complement of Cartesian Gaussians due to current software limitations.

## K⁺:DIMETHYL ETHER

The K⁺:DME system was sufficiently small to permit extended basis set MP2 and RI-MP2 calculations to be run for purposes of probing the complete basis set (CBS) limit. Properties determined with the correlation consistent basis sets frequently converge with enough regularity to permit a meaningful estimate of the CBS limit to be obtained from a simple exponential expression. Empirical evidence²³,³⁶⁻⁴¹ has shown that the expression
$$
A(x)=A_{\infty}+B^{*} \exp (-C x), \tag{2}
$$
where $x=1-3$ for DZ through QZ basis sets and $A_{\infty}$ represents an estimate of property $A$ in the complete basis set limit, works reasonably well. Alternate forms, such as a combined Gaussian and exponential form, have also been suggested.⁴² The estimated CBS limit values show in Fig. 1 were obtained with expression (2).

At the estimated Hartree-Fock limit for K⁺:DME, $R_{\text{KO}}=2.624$ Å, $R_{\text{CO}}=1.408$ Å and $\Delta E=-17.4$ kcal/mol, where $\Delta E$ represents the binding energy. Correlation recovery from MP2(CV) calculations contracts $R_{\text{KO}}$ to 2.550 Å and lengthens $R_{\text{CO}}$ to 1.431 Å. In the MP2 CBS limit $\Delta E=-19.7$ kcal/mol, a 14% (2.3 kcal/mol) increase in magnitude over the HF value. As seen in Fig. 1, $\Delta E$ is relatively insensitive to basis set improvements, as the entire range in values

for a given method and basis set sequence spans less than 3 kcal/mol. Correlation recovery produces a small (10%) increase in $\Delta E$.

Two additional effects were explored. A limited number of aug-cc-pVDZ basis set MP2 calculations were performed without the frozen core approximation in order to provide a more direct comparison with DFT calculations. None of the DFT results reported here used a frozen core approximation. Figure 1 shows that MP2 without the frozen core approximation yields only slight variations in bond lengths and binding energies. We also examined the effects of fourth order perturbation theory (MP4). It also produced small additional changes in the binding energy, decreasing it by just 0.2 kcal/mol. MP4 increases the K–O bond length by 0.005 Å compared to the MP2 value.

Using identical orbital and fitting basis sets, RI-MP2 was found to produce results which closely paralleled those obtained from MP2 calculations. Even with the small cc-pVDZ basis, which poses the greatest challenge for the approach which uses equivalent orbital and fitting sets, RI-MP2 accurately reproduces the conventional MP2 geometric parameters and binding energies.

Local density calculations within the SVWN approximation did a poor job of predicting the K–O bond, underestimating the MP2 value by 0.08 to 0.11 Å. The SVWN C–O bond length is also 0.02 Å too short. On the other hand, the SVWN/aug-cc-pVDZ binding energy is just 0.6 kcal/mol larger in magnitude than the MP2 value. Nonlocal DFT using the BP86 functionals faired much better in terms of bond lengths, overestimating $R_{\text{KO}}$ by only 0.03 Å with the aug-cc-pVDZ basis. But the binding energy obtained with the same basis was ~1 kcal/mol smaller than the RHF value, whereas MP2 increases the value by somewhat more than 1 kcal/mol. In general, gradient-corrected density functional methods are known to predict longer bond lengths and weaker bonds than their local density counterparts. $^{43,44}$

Although the amount of data is rather limited, it does not appear that BP86-DFT is any less sensitive to basis set improvements than MP2. The addition of a shell of diffuse functions (cc-pVDZ→aug-cc-pVDZ) decreases the magnitude of $\Delta E$ by 2 kcal/mol at both the MP2 and BP86 levels of theory. Comparing DFT findings with MP2 values obtained without the frozen core approximation does not result in improved agreement.

As mentioned previously, the primary motivation for considering RI-MP2, and DFT is their attractiveness as less costly alternatives to traditional MP2. RI-MP2 typically requires a small fraction of the time needed for MP2 and nonlocal DFT should run 2–3 times faster than comparable Hartree–Fock calculations with the same integral code. However, in practice these expectations may be overshadowed by practical considerations of how well a particular application runs on a given hardware platform. On an IBM RS/6000 model 590 workstation an MP2 energy evaluation required 2775 s (GAUSSIAN 92/DFT) using a semidirect algorithm. Semidirect RI-MP2 needed 587 s (SUPERMOLECULE) compared to a surprising 2148 s (DeFT) for the DFT calculation. Some of this difference can be attributed to the fact that DeFT was forced to include the full Cartesian set of $d$ functions, resulting in 162 total basis functions vs 154 functions with Gaussian and SUPERMOLECULE. Another factor contributing to the longer-than-expected DFT run time was the larger number of iterations needed to reach convergence. While the Gaussian RHF calculation converged in 11 iterations, DeFT required 42 iterations. The latter number will show wide variations, depending upon which convergence accelerators are applied. For example, DGauss on a single Cray C90 processor, which is roughly 4–5 times faster than the 590 workstation, required only 38 s for this same calculation and converged in ten iterations. $^{45}$

### 12-crown-4
The x-ray crystal structure of free 12-crown-4 (12c4) displays $C_i$ symmetry, $^{46}$ as does 18-crown-6 (18c6). An analysis of the Cambridge Crystallographic Database by Hay et al., $^{47}$ including many free and hydrated forms of 12c4, identified a majority of structures in the $C_i$ conformation. A smaller number displayed $C_s$ or $C_4$ symmetry. An extensive conformational search by the same authors using a modified MM3 force field $^{48}$ predicted an $S_4$ global minimum. Compared to the $C_i$ structure, the carbon/oxygen ring in the $S_4$ form of 12c4 is significantly more buckled, thereby reducing the transannular 1,5 CH–O distance (see Fig. 2) by 0.2 Å and increasing this favorable interaction. In 12c4 ($S_4$) the 1,5 CH–O distance is nearly identical to the corresponding distance in 18c6.

Table II lists the six lowest energy structures in ascending order based on the modified MM3 force field calculations of Hay et al.⁴⁷ This ordering is in qualitative agreement with the earlier RHF/STO-3G and RHF/DZ+$d$ findings of Seidl and Schaefer.⁴⁹ Also listed in Table II are RHF, MP2, RI-MP2, and DFT relative energies obtained with the 6-31+G*, cc-pVDZ, and aug-cc-pVDZ basis sets. All structures were verified to represent minima on the RHF/6-31+G* potential energy surface.

Although there is agreement among most methods as to the symmetry of the highest ($C_4$) and lowest ($S_4$) energy conformations, the ordering of the intervening four structures, covering approximately 2 kcal/mol in energy, varied widely with the level of theory. As noted by Hay et al.,⁴⁷ different molecular mechanics force fields yield quite different orderings for even the lowest few 12c4 conformations. For example, the AMBER force field predicts the $C_s$ structure to be the global minimum,⁵⁰ whereas the modified MM3 force field places the $C_s$ structure 1.6 kcal/mol above the $S_4$ conformation. While MM3 is in better qualitative agreement with $ab$ initio energy ordering than AMBER, it still underestimates several of the MP2 energy differences by a factor of 4 or more. Semiempirical models have recently been used in hybrid molecular dynamics simulations which combine a quantum mechanical treatment of 18c6 with a molecular mechanics treatment of surrounding waters. Of the two most widely used semiempirical parameterizations, AM1 clearly does a better job at mimicking the $ab$ initio energies than PM3.⁵¹

Three general observations can be drawn from the $ab$ initio results in Table II. First, correlation recovery tends to increase the gap between the $S_4$ global minimum and the other structures. Second, RI-MP2 does a reasonable job of reproducing the MP2 energetics even with a fitting basis set as small as 6-31+G*. Errors are typically less than 10% of the value of $\Delta E$ and are systematically positive, i.e., RI-MP2 overestimates the energy difference. Finally, use of the somewhat larger cc-pVDZ basis scrambles the order of the four intermediate energy structures and even causes the second $C_1$ structure to vanish. This last finding suggests that moderately large basis sets and correlation recovery may be required to obtain relative energies for low-lying crown ether conformations accurate to $\pm 1$ kcal/mol.

Of the four DFT methods examined, all were in qualitative agreement with the 6-31+G* $ab$ initio energy ordering. The local density SVWN functional performed slightly better at reproducing the MP2 results, while BLYP and BP86 tended to underestimate the distortion energy. For some conformations the error was more than a factor of 2.

# K⁺:12-CROWN-4 AND K⁺:DIMETHOXYETHANE

The lowest energy conformation for $K^+$:12c4 obtained from RHF/6-31+G* calculations possesses $C_4$ symmetry (Fig. 4). Each oxygen in the ether backbone is directed upwards towards the cation. Correlation recovery shortens the $K^+$–O bonds by 0.02 to 0.04 Å relative to the RHF value. The BP86-DFT bond length is very close to the MP2(CV) value, but SVWN-DFT underestimates $R_{KO}$ by a sizable 0.07 Å. The latter observation is consistent with what was observed for the dimethyl ether complex. The metal–oxygen distance in this complex is nearly identical to the value in the $S_4$ conformation of $K^+(H_2O)_4$. However, compared to the larger $K^+$:18c6 complex, potassium sits 0.05 Å closer to the oxygens in 12c4. We are unaware of a crystallographic structure for $K^+$:12c4.

A lower symmetry $(C_s)$ conformation was found for $K^+$:12c4 (see Fig. 5) and was verified to represent a minimum on the RHF potential surface. This minimum lies approximately 4 kcal/mol higher in energy than the $C_4$ conformation and differs from the former primarily in a slight distortion of the ring. No attempt was made to identify a connecting transition state. A second $C_s$ conformation, 0.4 kcal/mol above the first, was also identified.

Our previous study of $M^+$:18c6 complexes relied on RHF optimized structures when evaluating MP2 binding energies due to the prohibitive expense of correlated geometry optimizations for such large molecules. The 6-31+G* entries in Table III clearly illustrate the lack of sensitivity to the choice of geometries. While MP2 geometry optimization lowers the individual reactant and product energies by as much as 5 kcal/mol, the effect on the binding energy is an order of magnitude smaller. ## CONCLUSION

Extended basis set *ab initio* calculations have been car-
ried out on a series of potassium–ether complexes in an at-
tempt to identify potential sources of error in a previous
theoretical study of alkali cation/18-crown-6 complexes
which predicted a binding enthalpy for potassium more than
30 kcal/mol larger than experiment. The present work, which
includes some of the largest correlated calculations yet at-
tempted on crown ethers, supports the reliability of
counterpoise-corrected MP2 results obtained with the
6-31+G* hybrid basis set in the earlier study. Based on the
present findings, it would appear that error bars, with respect
to the complete basis set limit, are on the order of ±3 kcal/
mol. The use of MP2 geometries was found to cause only
small changes in binding energies.

For the present set of complexes, the RI-MP2 approxi-
mation was found to faithfully reproduce traditional MP2
geometries and energies when used with either the orbital
basis or an uncontracted fitting basis augmented with addi-
tional Gaussian primitives. Typical errors in bond energies
and conformation energy differences, measured with respect
to the MP2 results, were on the order of several tenths of a
kcal/mol with the smaller fitting basis. When efficient
RI-MP2 gradients become available, this method should pro-
vide an excellent alternative to MP2 at a small fraction of the
computational cost.

The performance of the various density functional meth-
ods showed large variations, with the simple SVWN local
density approximation frequently providing the best overall
agreement with MP2 binding energies. However, it underes-
timated K–O distances by as much as 0.08 Å. The gradient-
corrected BP86-DFT method performed nearly as well for
binding energies and did a much better job at predicting
structures. BLYP-DFT underestimated the K⁺:12c4 binding
energy by 4–7 kcal/mol or 15%.
