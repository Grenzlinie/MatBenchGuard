# Spectral Assignments and Reference Data

## Synthesis and GIAO NMR calculations for some new 4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-one derivatives: comparison of theoretical and experimental $^1$H and $^{13}$C chemical shifts

Haydar Yüksek,¹* Ozlem Gürsoy,¹ Ismail Cakmak¹ and Muzaffer Alkan²

¹ Department of Chemistry, Kafkas University, 36100 Kars, Turkey
² Education Faculty, Kafkas University, 36100 Kars, Turkey

Received 11 November 2004; revised 22 February 2005; accepted 28 February 2005

Four novel 3-alkyl(aryl)-4-(4-methoxycarbonylbenzylidenamino)-4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-ones (2) were synthesized by the reactions of 3-alkyl(aryl)-4-amino-4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-ones (1) with methyl 4-formylbenzoate and characterized by elemental analyses and IR, $^1$H NMR, $^{13}$C NMR and UV spectral data. In addition, isotropic $^1$H and $^{13}$C nuclear magnetic shielding constants of 2 were obtained by the gauge-including atomic-orbital (GIAO) method at the B3LYP density functional level. The geometry of each compound was optimized using the 6-311G basis set. Copyright © 2005 John Wiley & Sons, Ltd.

**KEYWORDS:** NMR; $^1$H NMR; $^{13}$C NMR; GIAO; chemical shifts; 4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-ones; Schiff base

## INTRODUCTION
Compounds of the 1,2,4-triazole and 4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-one type are reported to show a broad spectrum of biological activities¹⁻⁷ and several papers have dealt with the synthesis of N-arylidenamino-1,2,4-triazole and N-arylidenamino-4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-one derivatives.⁸⁻¹⁰ These reports prompted us to prepare a number of new potential biologically active 4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-one derivatives.

In this paper, we report the synthesis and NMR spectral characterization of four novel 3-alkyl(aryl)-4-(4-methoxycarbonylbenzylidenamino)-4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-ones (2) (Scheme 1). They were synthesized by the reactions of 3-alkyl(aryl)-4-amino-4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-ones (1) with methyl 4-formylbenzoate and DFT methods¹¹⁻¹⁴ were applied to analyze the experimental $^1$H and $^{13}$C data.

## EXPERIMENTAL
### Computational methods
The compounds for which calculations were carried out are as indicated in Table 1. The numbering system is shown in Scheme 1. All the structures were fully optimized with the Gaussian 03 program at the B3LYP/6-311G theoretical level.¹⁵ After the optimization, $^1$H and $^{13}$C chemical shifts were calculated with the GIAO method,¹⁶⁻¹⁹ using corresponding TMS shielding calculated at the same theoretical level as the reference. All the computations were done using an IBM PC Pentium-IV computer with 1024 MB RAM. Linear correlation analyses were carried out using SigmaPlot program. The quality of each correlation was judged by examining R, the Pearson correlation coefficient.²⁰

### Spectra
IR spectra were recorded using potassium bromide disks on a Mattson 1000 FTIR spectrometer. $^1$H and $^{13}$C NMR spectra were recorded in deuterated dimethyl sulfoxide with tetramethylsilane as internal standard on a Varian Gemini spectrometer at 200 and 50 MHz, respectively. UV absorption spectra were measured for ethanol solutions in 10 mm quartz cells between 200 and 400 nm using a Shimadzu 3101 PC UV–VIS–NIR spectrophotometer.

### Compounds
Melting-points were taken on a Electrothermal 9100 digital melting-point apparatus and are uncorrected. The starting compounds 1a–d were prepared from the reactions of the corresponding ester ethoxycarbonylhydrazones with hydrazine hydrate according to the literature.²¹

General method for the preparation of 3-alkyl(aryl)-4-(4-methoxycarbonylbenzylidenamino)-4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-ones (2)
3-Alkyl(aryl)-4-amino-4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-one (1) (0.01 mol) was dissolved in acetic acid (15 ml) and treated with methyl 4-formylbenzoate (1.64 g, 0.01 mol). The mixture was refluxed for 1 h and then evaporated at 50–55 °C in vacuo. Several recrystallizations of the residue from an appropriate solvent gave pure compound 2.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The experimental and theoretical results, along with the error for each compound, are presented in Table 1. A least-squares fit of all data shows a strong linear relationship with an $R^2$ value of 0.9989. This relationship is also reflected in the results for the individual compound, in which $R^2$ values are 0.79, 0.99 (2a); 0.84, 0.99 (2b); 0.66, 0.98 (2c); 0.56, 0.96 (2d). The overall standard error of the estimate is 3.18 with the 6–311G basis set. The following correlations $\delta_{\text{calc}} = a\delta_{\text{exp}} + b$ were obtained: 2a (¹H), B3LYP/6–311G, $R = 0.889$; SE = 1.23, $a = 1.54$, $b = 0.68$; 2a (¹³C), B3LYP/6–311G, $R = 0.996$, SE = 4.26, $a = 1.00$, $b = 5.61$; 2b (¹H), B3LYP/6–311G, $R = 0.918$, SE = 1.19, $a = 0.75$, $b = 1.03$; 2b (¹³C), B3LYP/6–311G, $R = 0.997$; SE = 4.13, $a = 1.01$, $b = 3.48$; 2c (¹H), B3LYP/6–311G, $R = 0.814$, SE = 0.28, $a = 0.629$, $b = 2.29$; 2c (¹³C), B3LYP/6–311G, $R = 0.995$, SE = 3.40, $a = 0.99$, $b = 5.35$; 2d (¹H), B3LYP/6–311G $R = 0.752$, SE = 1.13, $a = 0.54$, $b = 2.98$; 2d (¹³C), B3LYP/6–311G, $R = 0.983$, SE = 4.71, $a = 1.04$, $b = -0.27$.

A linear correlation between the theoretical and experimental carbon and proton chemical shifts is clearly visible in Table 1. Good quantitative agreement within 1–8 ppm for 2a and 2d is observed for the aromatic carbons of these compounds, which appears to be very satisfactory, taking into consideration the fact that the experimental chemical shifts in solution are subject to solvent, concentration and temperature effects. Differences are particularly apparent in the aromatic carbons in which chemical shifts are sensitive to substitution. It can also be seen at that chemical shifts of the aromatic carbon atoms are not well produced by the 6–311G basis set. This is probably due to the sensitivity of the calculations to the geometry and rapid rotation of these groups, particularly on the NMR time-scale.

As can be seen from Table 1, the errors range from 14 to 17 ppm for C-1 and C-11 atoms, which belong to the carboxyl group, due to attached oxygen atoms. The carbonyl carbon atoms C-1 and C-11 are more deshielded as a consequence of binding to the oxygen atoms. All the statistical calculations were affected by these results.

## CONCLUSION

Correlations between experimental chemical shifts and GIAO-calculated isotropic shielding constants of protons and carbons as obtained from four new 4,5-dihydro-1H-1,2,4-triazol-5-one derivatives were established in order to assess the performance of NMR spectral calculations. The DFT-B3LYP level of the 6–311G basis set was considered for geometry optimization and spectral calculations. Depending on the computational level, linear regressions $\delta_{\text{calc}} = a\delta_{\text{exp}} + b$ yield standard deviations of 1.09–4.71 ppm for hydrogen and carbon atoms.
