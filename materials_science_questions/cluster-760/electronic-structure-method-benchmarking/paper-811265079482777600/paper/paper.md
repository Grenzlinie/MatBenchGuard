REGULAR ARTICLE

# Theoretical study on interactions of N-heterocyclic carbene with the bare first-row transition metals

Xiang-Fei Zhang¹ · Ming-Jun Sun¹ · Ze-Xing Cao¹

Received: 3 November 2015 / Accepted: 4 June 2016
© Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2016

## Abstract
The electronic and structural features of the free and substituted N-heterocyclic carbenes (NHCs) and their complexes with the bare first-row transition metals (TMs) have been explored theoretically. The present results indicate that the electron-donating behavior of NHC can be tuned by introduction of the substituent, and the NHC–TM σ-bonding interactions strongly depend on the d-electron configuration of TMs. The B3LYP- and CCSD(T)-predicted bond dissociation energies show a double-peak profile, and the maxima appear in [NHC–V] (26.2 kcal/mol) and [NHC–Ni] (29.7 kcal/mol) with the stable half- and full-filled $d$ configurations, respectively, after accepting the σ-lone pair of NHC. Theoretical calculations on the $\eta^{2}$-type structure of [NHC–TM] from $\pi$ interactions between TM and C=C double bond in NHC are also done by B3LYP-D3. For Zn with $d^{10}$, no σ-type structure was located, and all geometry optimization attempts converge to only metastable $\eta^{6}$-type configuration, arising from the weak aromatic $\pi$ interactions. For other TMs, the $\pi$ interactions between the double C=C bond and TM are responsible for their $\eta^{2}$-type structures. The σ- and π-type [NHC–TM] complexes can accommodate additional NHC ligand to form stable [NHC–TM–NHC], and the introduction of second NHC can enhance the bonding interactions between TM and NHCs for most of the bare TMs, showing cooperation effect of the multiple NHC ligands on the TM–NHC bonding.

**Keywords** N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) · Transition metal (TM) · Bond dissociation energy (BDE) · σ- and π-bonding interactions · Density functional theory (DFT)

## 1 Introduction

The N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) is a nitrogen-containing heterocycle with a carbene carbon, and its free form is thus less stable and difficult to isolate. Öfele [1] and Wanzlick and Schönherr [2] successfully synthesized the N-heterocyclic carbenes containing transition metals (TMs) for the first time, in which coordination of metal to the carbene carbon stabilizes the elusive free NHC. Until 1991, Arduengo et al. [3] reported the synthesis and structural characterization of the first stable crystalline NHC. Differing from the conventional carbene, the N-heterocyclic carbene has a ground state of $\sigma^{2}\pi$ and it basically serves as a σ ligand, as shown in Scheme 1.

It was well known that the phosphine ligand plays an important role in catalytic transformations in the chemical industry, and it not only donates σ electrons but also accepts π-feedback electrons from the $d$ orbitals of TMs in the metal–phosphine complexes [4]. In comparison with the phosphine, NHCs generally have high σ-donating ability and almost without π-accepting ability, and they can form stronger metal–NHC σ bond [5, 6]. As novel ligands for transition metals, NHCs have been widely used in important catalytic reactions, and many advances in the chemistry of NHCs have been made in past decades [7–17].

In metal–NHC complexes, the transition metals (TMs) accommodate the σ electrons of carbene carbon due to the comparable energy and available empty $nd(n + 1)s(n + 1)p$ orbitals, whereas the feedback donation interaction is almost negligible, since the empty $p$ orbital of carbene

However, the electronic and ligating properties of NHCs can be subverted by the structural modification such as substitution, annulation, and carbonylation, and plausible strategies to tune their electronic structures were proposed, based on extensive calculations [18, 19].

The bonding interactions of transition metals with carbon are quite important for a variety of catalytic reactions, and their bonding features and strengths are related to reactivity. In previous pioneering experimental studies by Armentrout et al. [20], bond dissociation energies (BDEs) for the first-row transition-metal methylidenes $\text{TM}^+\text{-CH}_2$ (TM = Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, and Cu) were determined, and the periodic trends for BDEs and the multiple bond features in the $\text{TM}^+\text{-CH}_2$ species were also explored theoretically [21].

Tonner et al. [22] performed DFT calculations on the transition-metal complexes with the NHC ligand, and their results indicate that the phosphine ligands are weaker $\sigma$ donors and better $\pi$ acceptors than the NHC ligand. Schwarz et al. [23] investigated polymer-supported NHC complexes of Pd, both experimentally and theoretically, and they found that the complexes with the NHC ligand could efficiently catalyze the C–C bond coupling. The substituent effect on the TM–NHC bond and the catalytic role of NHC ligand received considerable attention [24–28]. Here, we carried out extensive density functional theory (DFT) calculations on the complexes of NHCs with the bare first-row transition metals, and the TM–NHC bonding properties in [NHC–TM] and [NHC–TM–NHC] complexes have been discussed.

## 2 Computational details

The full geometry optimization on all $\sigma$-type species has been performed by the B3LYP functional based on the generalized gradient approximation [29, 30]. Here, the basis sets of the triplet-$\xi$ LanL2TZ with $f$ polarization functions for the TM atoms [31] and the 6-311 + G($d,p$) for other atoms have been used unless otherwise stated. Frequency calculations at the same level of theory have been used to assess the nature of optimized structures, and the stable configurations correspond to local minima without imaginary frequency on the potential energy surface. In consideration of the dispersion compensation for the $\pi$-type metastable complexes, the B3LYP-D3 approach [32] was considered in calculation. The natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis [33] was also carried out to unveil the electronic structures of [TM–NHC] complexes. In order to validate use of the B3LYP functional, test calculations on the copper–carbene complex (NHC)–CuCl have been carried out. As shown in Fig. 1, the optimized structure shows good agreement with previous theoretical and experimental values [17, 34, 35]. The B3LYP method has been widely and successfully used in previous computational studies on the NHC–TM systems [36–39].

![](./images/811265079482777600_3.jpg)

Fig. 1 B3LYP-optimized structure of NHC–CuCl (units in Å, experimental values in parentheses)

In consideration of the multi-reference character for the low-lying states of carbene, CASSCF calculations on the low-energy electronic states of both CH₂ and NHC have been implemented with the Molpro 2010 package [40]. Furthermore, the charge decomposition analysis (CDA) [41] was also carried out to explore the charge transfer in the TM–NHC bonding.

The bond dissociation energy (BDE) is defined as
$$\text{BDE} = E_{\text{NHC}} + E_{\text{TM}} - E_{\text{NHC-TM}},$$
where $E_{\text{NHC}}$ and $E_{\text{TM}}$ are the total energies of free NHC ($^1\text{A}_1$) and the transition metal (TM) in the ground state (GS) or the same spin state (SS) with its carbene complex, respectively, and $E_{\text{NHC-TM}}$ represents the energy of [NHC–TM] complex in either the ground state (GS) or the first excited state (ES). Corresponding dissociation channels are described as
$$[\text{NHC}-\text{TM}](\text{GS}) \to \text{NHC}\left(^1\text{A}_1\right) + \text{TM}(\text{GS or SS})$$
$$[\text{NHC}-\text{TM}](\text{ES}) \to \text{NHC}\left(^1\text{A}_1\right) + \text{TM}(\text{GS or SS})$$

## 3 Results and discussion

### 3.1 Electronic structures of NHC

For the simplest carbene CH₂, the carbene carbon adopts the sp² hybridization with three σ-sp² orbitals in the molecular plane and one perpendicular single-occupied p orbital, and it has a triplet ground state of X³B₁ (σ¹π¹ in Scheme 1). The low-lying states of ¹A₁(σ²π⁰) and ¹B₁(σ¹π¹) are 10.4 and 33.1 kcal/mol [45-49] above the ground state, respectively (Schemes 2, 3).

In contrast to CH₂, the five-membered N-heterocyclic carbene has a singlet ground state with C₂ᵥ symmetry. Such ground-state electronic structure of σ²π⁰ can be ascribed to the conjugation and inductive bonding interactions in NHC, in which the unoccupied p orbital of carbene carbon C1 is saturated by the aromatic six-electron π system (π conjugation effect), and the neighbor N atoms can withdraw σ electrons of carbene carbon (σ inductive effect), leading to stabilization of σ-lone pair [50]. We note that there is large HOMO-LUMO gap of 138.4 kcal/mol for NHC in the ground state. At the CASSCF level of theory, the low-lying ³B₁(σ¹π¹) and ¹B₁(σ¹π¹) states of NHC are higher than the ground state by 92.8 and 139.2 kcal/mol (see Table 1), respectively. Owing to lack of dynamical correlation effect in CASSCF calculations, here the predicted singlet-triplet energy splitting is larger than the experimental value of 82 kcal/mol [51] (Fig. 2).

In comparison with NHC, its isoelectronic species, imidazole, also has similar geometrical and electronic structures as shown in Fig. 3, and it is more stable than NHC by 25.7 kcal/mol at the B3LYP/6-311 + G(d, p) level. ### 3.2 Influence of substituent group on the electron-donating behavior of NHC

Generally, the electron-rich TM center has stronger catalytic activity after accepting the $\sigma$-donating electrons of NHCs [52]. To explore the possibility to regulate the $\sigma$-lone pair behavior, different substituents at the nitrogen site (NR; $\text{R} = \text{H}, \text{CH}_3, \text{CN}, \text{BH}_2$) were considered, and selected frontier orbitals are displayed in Fig. 5. Here, the $-\text{CH}_3$ group has stronger electron-donating ability than $-\text{H}$, resulting in more negative charges at the carbene carbon (C1) as given in Table 2, compared with the unsubstituted NHC. Similarly, the $-\text{BH}_2$ substituent with the strong $\sigma$-donating ability remarkably increases the negative charge population of C1. On the contrary, the electron-withdrawing group of $-\text{CN}$ decreases the electronic density at C1 and stabilizes the $\sigma$-lone pair, giving rise to the energy-level flip of $\sigma$-type and $\pi$-type frontier orbitals as shown in Fig. 5. The $-\text{BH}_2$ group is an electron-poor $\pi$-acceptor and lowers the LUMO level, decreasing the HOMO–LUMO gap (see Table 2).

We note that the $-\text{CH}_3$ substituent synchronously slightly elevates the energy levels of HOMO and LUMO by ~4 kcal/mol as found in previous studies [53], while the $-\text{CN}$ substituent synchronously remarkably lowers the energy levels of both frontier orbitals by ~44 kcal/mol. Accordingly, the HOMO–LUMO gap maintains almost unchanged for both substitutions. For the substitution of $-\text{BH}_2$, the HOMO–LUMO gaps decrease by ~37 kcal/mol, owing to its strong $\pi$-accepting ability [24]. Such stabilization of $\pi$-type LUMO by the electron-poor $-\text{BH}_2$ also decreases the singlet–triplet splitting from 82.7 kcal/mol in the unsubstituted NHC to 48.3 kcal/mol in the $\text{BH}_2$-substituted NHC, increasing the possibility to access the triplet state.

### 3.3 Interactions of NHC with the bare first-row transition metals

A schematic diagram of possible orbital interactions between NHC and TM is shown in Fig. 6, which are approximately divided into two classes, electron-donating

![](./images/811265079482777600_12.jpg)

![](./images/811265079482777600_13.jpg)

and electron-accepting bonding interactions arising from NHC. Among the electron donation of NHC to TM, the $\sigma$ dative bond between the carbene carbon and TM is very strong due to the high $\sigma$-donating ability of NHC [5, 54], while the $\pi$-type donation is much weaker since the $\pi$ electrons are involved in the aromatic bonding system. The $\pi^*$ and $\sigma^*$ orbitals with relatively large coefficients at the carbene carbon might accept electrons of TM, and they are involved in the back-donation bonding interactions between NHC and TM as shown in Fig. 6. The conventional out-of-plane $\pi^*$ interaction with the metal is quite weak as mentioned above. Here, the recombined orbital of $\sigma_{\text{C-N}}^* + \sigma_{\text{C-N}}^*$ has similar symmetry with the $d$ orbital of TM, which can give rise to an in-plane $\pi^*$ back-donation bonding. Presumably, such back-donation interactions are almost negligible owing to substantially high levels of both antibonding orbitals. These bonding interactions at the carbene carbon are responsible for the $\sigma$-type complex. In addition, the $\pi$-type compounds, arising from the conventional $\pi\cdots$TM interactions, are also probably formed, as shown in Scheme 4.

### 3.4 The $\sigma$-type structure and bonding

Selected predicted properties of $\sigma$-type [NHC-TM] complexes are given in Table 3. Clearly, all TM atoms in stable [NHC-TM] compounds have negative charge populations, whereas the carbene carbon is positively charged in most of complexes. Such charge populations show that the TM center behaves as an electron acceptor and NHC serves as an electron donor in the NHC-TM bonding. The optimized C-N bond lengths in the [NHC-TM] complexes basically maintain about $\sim$1.37 Å and are almost unchanged in comparison with those in the free NHC, suggesting that the back-donation of the occupied $d$ orbitals of TM into the $\pi^*$-type antibonding orbitals is quite inappreciable.

### 3.5 The $\pi$-type structure and bonding

In previous laser ablation studies on organometallic clusters $\text{TM}_n(\text{Bz})_m$ ($\text{Bz} = \text{benzene}$; $\text{TM} = \text{Sc–Cu}$) [57–63], the multiple sandwich for the early transition metals and rice-ball structures for the late transition metals were proposed and characterized. Such aromatic $\pi$–TM clusters show remarkable size dependences of their ionization energies and stabilities. Similarly, here the $\pi$ interactions between TM and NHC also give rise to the $\pi$-type structures of [NHC–TM] and [NHC–TM–NHC].

Considering importance of the dispersion interaction for the weakly bound $\pi$ complexes, we examined different functional approaches, including B3LYP, B3LYP-D3, M06-2X, M06L, and $\omega$B97XD, for the [NHC–Ni] complex in the triplet state. Clearly, the B3LYP-predicted BDE of 3.0 kcal/mol is smallest among those values in Table 5 owing to lack of dispersion effect. Furthermore, during all full geometry optimizations with different initial $\pi$-type structures, only a $\eta^2$-type structure (see Scheme 4) was located by B3LYP-D3, M06-2X, and $\omega$B97XD. ### 3.6 Structure and bonding of [NHC–TM–NHC]

The late transition-metal compounds bearing multiple NHC ligands have been successfully synthesized and discussed [33, 65], and they show high potential in the NHC-bearing transition-metal catalysis. In order to understand interactions of the first-row transition metals with multiple NHCs, the possible $\sigma$- and $\pi$-type structures of [NHC–TM–NHC] have been investigated. As Fig. 9 shows, the $\sigma$-type structures in $D_{2h}$ and $D_{2d}$ symmetries (D) and the $\pi$-type structures, arising from weak d–$\pi$ interactions between the TM atom with the C=C double bond (E) or the aromatic $\pi$ electrons (F) of NHC, are considered here. The present calculations indicate that for Zn only the aromatic $\pi$-type structure of [NHC–Zn–NHC] might be located successfully, in which the distance between Zn and the NHC ring center is 3.5 Å (refer to F in Fig. 9) and the predicted BDE for the binding of second NHC is almost zero. For other $\pi$-type systems, all geometry optimizations converge to the $\pi$-type structure from the d–$\pi$ interactions between TM and C=C bonds in two NHCs (refer to E in Fig. 9).

As Tables 6 and 7 show, the $\pi$ interactions between [NHC–TM] and NHC, yielding [NHC–TM–NHC], are stronger than those between NHC and TM for TM = Sc, Ti, V, Mn, Fe, and Co, while the BDE for [NHC–Cr] binding NHC is almost negligible. We note that there are notable charge transfers from Cu to NHC in the $\pi$-type [NHC–Cu–NHC], resulting in quite small spin population of 0.06 at Cu, different from [NHC–Cu]. Particularly, the initial $\pi$-type structure of [NHC–Fe] evolves to the $\sigma$-type structure during the full geometry optimization. ## 4 Conclusions

The interactions between the bare first-row transition metals and NHC ligands have been investigated by theoretical calculations. In [NHC–TM] complexes, NHC serves as a σ-electron donor, while TMs are generally σ-electron acceptors. The relatively large bond dissociation energies of 26.2 and 29.7 kcal/mol appear in the σ-type [NHC–V] $(X^{4}A_{2})$ and [NHC–Ni] $(X^{1}A_{1})$ complexes, respectively, whereas the dissociation of [NHC–Mn] $(X^{6}A_{1})$ just requires 0.6 kcal/mol. Such sharp difference can be ascribed to the half- and full-filled stable electronic configurations $(3d^{5}$ and $3d^{10})$ of V and Ni atoms after accepting the σ-lone pair electrons of NHC. The free Mn with $3d^{5}$ is unlikely to capture the lone pair of NHC, yielding a loosely associated [NHC–Mn]. These σ-type [NHC–TM] complexes may accommodate second NHC ligand to form the stable σ-type [NHC–TM–NHC] complexes in $D_{2d}$ and $D_{2h}$ symmetries. The π interactions between C=C double bond and TM give rise to metastable $\eta^{2}$-type [NHC–TM] π complexes, and the second NHC can ligate to the $\eta^{2}$-type [NHC–TM] to form more stable π-type complexes of [NHC–TN–NHC]. The predicted bond dissociation energies indicate that multiple NHC ligands have cooperation role in enhancing the interactions between TM and NHC.
