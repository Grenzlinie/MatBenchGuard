# The molecular structure and conformation of bicyclo[3.3.1]nonan-9-one: ab initio and DFT calculations

Jaebum Choo$^{\text{a}}$, Sunghwan Kim$^{\text{b}}$, Hyun Joo$^{\text{b}}$, Younghi Kwon$^{\text{b},*}$

$^{\text{a}}$Department of Chemistry, Hanyang University, Ansan 425-791, South Korea
$^{\text{b}}$Department of Chemistry, Hanyang University, Seoul 133-791, South Korea

Received 20 February 2002; accepted 5 August 2002

## Abstract

Ab initio and density functional theory methods have been applied to investigate the molecular structures and $^{13}$C chemical shifts for bicyclo[3.3.1]nonan-9-one (BNO), and energy differences and barrier potentials between its conformers. The calculated free energy difference ($\Delta G_{1}$) between twin-chair (CC) and boat-chair (BC) conformations is about 1 kcal/mol, while the inversion barrier ($\Delta G_{1}^{\ddagger}$) between them is about 6 kcal/mol. Because of this small energy difference, BNO might have orientationally disordered crystal structure with the CC and BC conformers.

## 1. Introduction

Molecules in approximately globular shape, such as adamantane and camphor, often undergo rapid overall molecular reorientation immediately below their melting points [1–4]. Such molecular solids are known as plastic crystals or orientationally disordered solids. The molecular motions in these disordered states are depicted as a rapid tumble from one orientation to another [5,6], with correlation times in the range of $10^{-10}$-$10^{-12}$ s [7]. In addition to the rapid reorientation, the molecules also execute slower translational motion and sometimes coupled molecular rotation and transition [8]. On cooling, these solids slowly lose their mobility, and eventually undergo one or several transitions until they reach a fully ordered arrangement.

Because of their potential conformational mobility, bicyclo[3.3.1]nonane (BN) and bicyclo[3.3.1]nonan-9-one (BNO) have been of great interest. These compounds have three possible conformational minima, corresponding to the twin-chair (CC), boat-chair (BC), and twisted twin-boat (BB) conformers. The electron diffraction study on gaseous BN revealed that the BN molecule exists predominantly in the CC form. However, as the temperature rises, the portion of the BC form increases: 5% at 65 °C and 25% at 400 °C [9]. According to this study, the free energy difference $\Delta G$ between CC and BC forms is 2.3–2.5 kcal/mol. As expected from these results, there are no X-ray studies on BN and its derivatives at room temperature providing the evidence of CC $\leftrightarrow$ BC conformational disorder. On the contrary,

a lanthanide-induced shift (LIS) investigation of BNO in $CCl_4$ solution showed that there is 22% of the BC conformer at room temperature [10]. This equilibrium proportion between CC and BC conformers of BNO causes the free energy difference $\Delta G$ to be ~1.16 kcal/mol, which is lower than that of BN. Therefore, it is quite likely that BNO and its derivatives might show the chair $\leftrightarrow$ boat interconversion even in the solid state and have disordered crystal structures with the CC and BC conformers [11,12].

Many previous studies of BNO have been reported, including LIS [10], NMR [11,13], microwave spectroscopy [14], X-ray diffraction [15], and molecular mechanics [13,16]. In this paper, ab initio and density functional theory (DFT) methods are applied to calculate the molecular structures of BNO. In addition, the molecular structures of BN were reinvestigated since a previous work for this molecule was done at the HF/STO-3G level [17]. We also investigated the energy differences and ring inversion barriers between three conformers for each compound. Finally, we discussed the $^{13}$C chemical shifts for these compounds.

## 2. Computational details

All calculations were performed by using the GAUSSIAN 98 program package [18]. The structures of three conformers of BNO and BN are depicted in Fig. 1, along with the numbering schemes of the atoms. The geometries of three conformers are fully optimized by using the Hartree–Fock (HF) method with the 6-31 + G(d) and 6-311 + G(d) basis sets. The effect of electron correlation on the geometry optimizations is taken into account by the second-order Møller–Plesset perturbation (MP2) theory as well as Becke’s three-parameter hybrid method (B3LYP) in the DFT with the 6-31 + G(d) and 6-311 + G(d) basis sets. Vibrational frequency analyses at the HF and B3LYP levels showed that three conformers are at local minima with no imaginary frequencies. In order to calculate the ring inversion barrier between these conformers, the geometries of transition states were also optimized. In addition, $^{13}$C NMR chemical shifts for BNO and BN are calculated with the gauge-independent atomic orbital (GIAO) method [19–23].

Generally, the computed geometrical parameters for the CC conformer for BNO are closer to those determined by the microwave study than to X-ray diffraction data. This is because the molecular geometries in the solid state are influenced by the medium effect and/or interatomic contact with neighbor molecules. On the other hand, the computed parameters of BN at the HF level deviate from its experimental values more than those of BNO do. When the electron correlation effect is taken into account by the B3LYP and MP2

methods, the calculated parameters are closer to the experimental values. According to our calculations, the calculated bond lengths of bicyclic rings are highly dependent upon a functional group at the C₉ position. The calculated bond length C₁–C₉ of BNO, which has a carbonyl group at its bridgehead position, is quite shorter than that of BN, which has a methylene group. This is because the C₉ atom of BNO is sp² hybridized, while that of BN is sp³ hybridized. The computed bond length C₁–C₂ of BNO is around 0.015 $\mathring{\text{A}}$ longer than the C₂–C₃ bond length, while the corresponding bond lengths of BN are similar to each other. This might be due to the repulsion between two methylene groups at $C_3$ and $C_7$ positions. For BNO, the interatomic distances $H_{31}\cdots H_{71}$ of 1.914 Å and $C_3\cdots C_7$ of 3.116 Å calculated at the MP2/6-311 + G(d) level are shorter than the corresponding sum of van der Waals radii [25]. For both compounds, this repulsion between two close methylene groups brings about some deformation of the bond angles of the CC conformation. The calculated bond angles $\angle C_2-C_1-C_8$ and $\angle C_1-C_2-C_3$ for both compounds are somewhat wider by 5−6° than the tetrahedral angle of 109.5°. When the CC conformer for both compounds changes into the BC conformer, the repulsion between two methylene groups vanishes, and the calculated bond angles change dramatically. Due to the loss of this repulsion, at all the calculation levels, the dihedral angles $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ for BNO increase by around 6°. In addition, the bond angles $\angle C_1-C_2-C_3$, $\angle C_2-C_1-C_8$, $\angle C_2-C_3-C_4$ decrease, while the bond angle $\angle C_2-C_1-C_9$ increases by around 2°. These angles are closer to the tetrahedral angle of 109.5° than the corresponding values in the CC conformer. This implies that the change in conformation from CC to BC reduces the angle strain to some extent. Like BNO, upon changing in conformation, BN also shows an increase in $\phi_1$ and

$\phi_2$ and these bond angles are finally approaching to the tetrahedral angle. However, the calculated dihedral angles $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ in BNO are almost equal, while $\phi_1$ in BN is smaller than $\phi_2$. In the BC conformation of BN, there is some repulsion between the hydrogen atom $H_{32}$ at the $C_3$ position and the $H_9$ atom at the $C_9$ position. At the MP2/6-311 + G(d) level, the calculated distance between them is only 2.256 Å. Because of this, the dihedral angle $\phi_1$ becomes smaller than $\phi_2$. In addition, the bond angles $\angle C_1-C_2-C_3$ and $\angle C_2-C_1-C_9$ in the boat ring of the BC conformer are larger than the angles $\angle C_1-C_8-C_7$ and $\angle C_8-C_1-C_9$ in the chair ring of the BC conformer. However, in the BNO, which has a carbonyl group instead of a methylene group, there are no repulsive interactions between the $H_{32}$ atom and the carbonyl group since the interatomic distance between them is longer than 3 Å. Thus, in the BC conformation of BNO, the dihedral angle $\phi_1$ does not differ significantly from $\phi_2$.

The BB conformer for both compounds has a twisted structure with the $C_2$ symmetry. Contrary to the CC and BC conformers, the $C_1$, $C_2$, $C_4$ and $C_5$ atoms in the BB conformer are not in the same plane. The calculated dihedral angle $\angle C_2-C_1\cdots C_5-C_4$ of the BB conformer is 23.5 and 23.6° for BNO and BN, respectively. Generally, the calculated geometrical parameters of the BB conformer for both compounds are nearly identical to the geometrical values of the boat ring of the BC conformer. For BNO, the interatomic distance $O\cdots H_{32}$ of the BB conformer is also much the same to that of the BC conformer. For the case of BN, however, the distance $H_{32}\cdots H_9$ of the BB conformer is somewhat 0.05–0.07 Å longer than that of the BC conformer. For both compounds, the bond angle $\angle C_1-C_9-C_5$ of the BB conformer is smaller than that of the CC and BC conformers.

The calculated energy differences and ring inversion barriers between conformers at 298.15 K are presented in Table 3, along with the available experimental data [9,13]. As shown in Table 5, these atoms are most electronegative in the BC conformer. Interestingly, it seems likely that conformational change of BNO from CC to BC does not affect significantly on the chemical shift of $C_3$ atom. Instead, the calculated chemical shift of $C_7$ atom decreases by about 5 ppm. A similar trend is also found in the case of BN. This may be because the peak of $C_3$ atom in the boat ring of the BC conformer is overlapped by the same peak of the CC conformer, and they cannot be distinguishable.

## 4. Conclusion

The geometries, energy differences between conformers and energy barriers of ring inversion of BNO and BN have been investigated by ab initio and DFT methods. $^{13}$C chemical shifts were also investigated. At all the levels of theory, the calculation shows good agreement with experimental geometrical parameters of the CC conformer. The B3LYP and MP2 methods well reproduce the energy differences and barriers between conformers. The calculated $^{13}$C chemical shifts are also in good agreement with the experiments. Because CC conformer of BNO is destabilized by the repulsion between endo-hydrogens at $C_3$ and $C_7$ positions, $\Delta G_1$ at 298 K is quite smaller than $\Delta G_2$. In addition, the BC conformer of BNO does not have repulsion between hydrogen atom at $C_3$ position and carbonyl groups, and $\Delta G_1$ of BNO is smaller than that of BN. Due to this small energy difference, BNO can have orientationally disordered crystal structure with CC and BC conformers [11] although the inversion barrier ($\Delta G_1^{\ddagger}$) between them is about 6 kcal/mol. On the other hand, $\Delta G_2$ is about 6 kcal/mol and $\Delta G_2^{\ddagger}$ is over 9 kcal/mol. Thus, the possibility of the existence of the BB conformer at room temperature is very low.
