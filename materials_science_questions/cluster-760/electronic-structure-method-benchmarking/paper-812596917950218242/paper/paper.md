# Density functional studies of the pseudo-$\pi$.a$\sigma$ charge-transfer complex between cyclopropane and chlorine monofluoride

A. Garcia,† J. M. Elorza and J. M. Ugalde*

Kimika Fakultatea, Euskal Herriko Unibertsitatea, P.K. 1072, 20080 Donostia, Euskadi, Spain

Received 8th February 1999, Accepted 12th March 1999

The pseudo-$\pi$.a$\sigma$ charge-transfer complex formed by cyclopropane and chlorine monofluoride was studied with various approximate pure and hybrid density functional methods and the second-order Møller–Plesset (MP2) theory. The calculations demonstrate that one hybrid method, namely the so-called B3LYP, leads to reasonably good estimates of the experimentally measured rotational constants. In addition, the predicted B3LYP intermolecular distance is found also to be close to the experimental value. This lends confidence to the prediction of the intermolecular interaction energy, which is found to be 1.42 kcal mol⁻¹. It was also possible to calculate the number and energies of the vibrational states supported by the intermolecular stretching mode. Only five such states have been found. The performances of the various approximate density functionals and MP2 theory are compared and discussed. Finally, the analysis of the natural bond orbitals, which has been found to be very valuable in understanding the nature of the weak intermolecular interaction is discussed.

## Introduction

The structure and bonding of charge-transfer complexes between unsaturated olefins and halogen $\text{X}_2$ or interhalogen XY molecules have attracted considerable interest in the last decade. These complexes have proved to be elusive and challenging for both experimentalists and theoreticians.

However, since the advent of modern microwave spectrometers incorporating the fast-mixing nozzle¹ utility, structural gas phase information on weakly bound complexes has been made accessible with unprecedented accuracy. Thus, charge-transfer complexes have become the subject of substantial rotational spectroscopy research² which is capable of giving not only highly accurate structural data, but also intermolecular force constants.³ These data can be used to assess the strength of the intermolecular bonding, along with nuclear quadrupole coupling constants, from which the electronic charge redistribution of the halogen/interhalogen molecule, upon complex formation, can be determined.⁴

These complexes, labeled b$\pi$.a$\sigma$ by Mulliken and Pearson,⁵ due to electronic charge transfer from an occupied $\pi$ orbital of the donor (base) moiety to an unoccupied $\sigma$ orbital of the acceptor (acid) moiety of the complex, have also attracted the attention of theoreticians. Thus, the origin of the forces responsible for the intermolecular binding has been studied⁶⁻⁸ very carefully. Indeed, the analysis of *ab initio* calculations indicates that both dispersion and polarization forces must be handled properly in order to achieve an understanding of the chemistry of these interesting supramolecular species;⁹ therefore, inclusion of electron correlation is necessary.

Density functional theory (DFT) offers an alternative treatment of electron correlation effects as compared with more traditional molecular orbital methods,¹⁰ and it has recently been suggested that it is a good choice for the study of charge-transfer complexes.¹¹⁻¹⁵

In this paper we present a detailed calculation using DFT based methods of the properties of a recently characterized pseudo-$\pi$.a$\sigma$ charge-transfer complex of cyclopropane and chlorine monofluoride.¹⁶ Cyclopropane may be regarded as the prototype pseudo-$\pi$ unsaturated hydrocarbon. Because the internal angles of the ring are $60^\circ$, the line of largest electron density lies necessarily outside the C–C internuclear line, provided that the C–C bond is formed by overlap of the sp³ hybrid orbitals on adjacent carbon atoms. The C–C bond can therefore be described as having a pseudo-$\pi$ character, an interpretation consistent with some properties of cyclopropane.¹⁷

## Computational method

We have recently shown that a slight modification¹⁸ of Becke's gradient correction term to the exchange functional yields a noticeably improved performance of the so-called B3LYP hybrid method¹⁹ for the calculated properties of both the G2 set of molecules²⁰ and some selected charge-transfer and hydrogen-bonded complexes.²¹ Also, it has been shown²¹ that the 6–311++G(d,p) basis set²² represents a reasonable compromise between computational cost and accuracy. Consequently, all the calculations presented in this paper were carried out with the 6–311++G(d,p) basis set, with a locally modified version of the Gaussian 94/DFT suite of programs,²³ which incorporates the above mentioned modification in Becke's exchange functional.

Becke optimized the $\beta$ parameter of his gradient correction, the LDA exchange energy, used in the BX and B3X functionals, by using Slater type functions. In our re-evaluation of the appropriate value to be used for $\beta$, we used instead various Gaussian basis sets. This new value of $\beta$, optimized¹⁸ and tested in earlier work by this group,¹⁵,²⁰,²¹ was used throughout this work in place of the standard value of $\beta$.

We used two sets of approximate functionals,²⁴ one set of pure functionals, denoted BX, X = P86, PW91, LYP, and one set of hybrid functionals, B3X, X = P86, PW91, LYP.

Owing the delicate balance between the dispersion and polarization contributions along with the charge-transfer term, existing in these complexes,⁶⁻⁸ their adequate description represents a very demanding test for density functionals methods.¹³ Indeed, inspection of Table 1 reveals that all three pure approximate functionals, BP86, BPW91 and BLYP, are far from satisfactory. They follow closely the trend found in our recent study of the $b\pi$.$a\sigma$ charge-transfer complex between ethyne and chlorine monofluoride.¹⁵ In summary, the rotational constant $A$ is underestimated and both rotational constants $B$ and $C$ are overestimated. This is a result of the underestimation of both intermolecular distances $r_{cm}$ (the distance between the mass centers of cyclopropane and ClF subunits) and $r(*\cdots \text{Cl})$ (the distance from the midpoint (*) of the C–C internuclear line to Cl) (see Fig. 1). Note that $r_{cm}$ is calculated as 0.137, 0.087 and 0.018 Å and $r(*\cdots \text{Cl})$ as 0.125 and 0.065 Å, shorter than the experimental value for X = P86, PW91 and LYP, respectively. Note the superior performance of the LYP correlation functional. This supports the evidence of the quality of the LYP correlation functional in describing weakly bound complexes.²⁹

Another interesting question concerning these weakly bound complexes is the number of vibrational states supported by the intermolecular stretching mode. This is not a trivial task, for there do not appear to be any general and exact methods for determining the number of discrete eigenvalues of operators that admit a finite number of such eigenvalues, apart from simply counting them after they are calculated. Therefore, we will calculate the vibrational energies supported by the intermolecular potential using the WKB method.$^{31}$ The WKB method gives energy levels comparable to those of the Schrödinger equation for a number of smoothly varying potentials that posses both an infinite and a finite number of bound states.$^{32}$ The WKB integral for the energy levels is

$$
\int_{x_{\text {in }}}^{x_{\text {out }}}\{2 \mu[E-V(x)]\}^{1 / 2} \mathrm{~d} x=\frac{h}{2}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right) \tag{1}
$$

where $h$ is Planck's constant, $x$ is the intermolecular distance, $x_{\text {in }}$ and $x_{\text {out }}$ are, respectively, the inner and outer classical turning points associated with the $n$th vibrational state of energy $E_{n}$, in the intermolecular potential $V(x)$ and $\mu=7.3938$ u is the reduced mass of the harmonic intermolecular stretching vibration. Fig. 2 shows the IR vectors of this vibrational normal mode calculated at the B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) level of theory.

We calculated the intermolecular potential $V$, at the B3LYP /6-311++G(d,p) level of theory, at 35 selected $r(* \cdots \mathrm{Cl})$ intermolecular distances, with the ClF and cyclopropane fragments kept rigid, and then fitted them to the Morse analytical potential:

$$
V(x)=V_{0}\left\{\left[1-\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\left(x-x_{\min }\right)}\right]^{2}-1\right\} \tag{2}
$$

where $V_{0}=0.00305$ au is the depth of the potential well with respect to the separated molecular fragments. The optimum $\gamma$ value is 0.86897 and the root mean square (rms) deviation between the calculated and fitted points is $5.34 \times 10^{-4}$. We used this Morse potential in eqn. (1), for which a simple analytical solution is not possible. Nevertheless, we implemented a reliable and efficient Newton-Rapson algorithm$^{33}$ for the automatic solution of eqn. (1) for arbitrary input potentials. To help establish the reliability of our approach we calculated the vibrational states of the hydrogen molecule and compared their energies with the experimental values.$^{34}$ The $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ potential was calculated at the QCISD/aug-cc-PVDZ level of theory at 77 selected distances. The optimized $\gamma$ was 1.0953 and the rms deviation was $2.8 \times 10^{-3}$. As can be seen from Table 2, our procedure predicts the existence of the experimentally observed 15 vibrational states, with energies differing slightly from their corresponding experimental values.

According to the data in Table 3, the energy for the $1 \leftarrow 0$ fundamental vibrational excitation is 115.9 cm⁻¹ and for the first overtone, $2 \leftarrow 0$ at 219.5 cm⁻¹. From the fundamental vibrational frequency $v$ and the reduced mass of the mode $\mu$, the stretching force constant $k_{\sigma}$ can be easily estimated as

$$
k_{\sigma} = \nu^{2}\mu \tag{3}
$$

which for the cyclopropane$\cdots$ClF complex gives $k_{\sigma} = 5.88$ N m⁻¹, a small value compared with the experimental estimate of 9.8(2) N m⁻¹ of Hinds et al.¹⁶ Note, however, that Hinds et al. measured the force constants of the $\text{C}_{2}\text{H}_{2}\cdots$ClF ³⁵ and $\text{C}_{2}\text{H}_{2}\cdots\text{Cl}_{2}$ ³⁶ complexes. For the former, with a dissociation energy of 3.43 kcal mol⁻¹, $k_{\sigma}$ is 10.01(3) N m⁻¹, and for the latter, whose dissociation energy is 1.19 kcal mol⁻¹, the force constant is 5.61(1) N m⁻¹. Based on the close relationship between force constants and dissociation energies, these data clearly indicate that our prediction of the force constant of the cyclopropane$\cdots$ClF complex, $k_{\sigma} = 5.88$ N m⁻¹, having a dissociation energy of 1.42 kcal mol⁻¹, is more logical that Hinds et al.’s estimate.

The harmonic force constant, obtained in the frequency calculation of the cyclopropane$\cdots$ClF complex at the B3LYP/6-311 ++ G(d,p) level of theory, is 2.69 N m⁻¹. The discrepancy with our WKB value is suggestive of the expected high anharmonicity of the potential energy of the intermolecular stretching mode.

As shown earlier,⁵ the charge redistribution on complex formation can be derived from the analysis of the nuclear quadrupole coupling constants. Thus, as a result of the careful study of the nuclear quadrupole coupling constants associated with the inner and outer halogen nuclei for several complexes, a simple proportionality rule, $\Delta Q_{\text{F}} = Ak_{\sigma}$, between the increment of the electronic charge of the outer halogen, $\Delta Q_{\text{F}}$, and the observed intermolecular stretching force constant, $k_{\sigma}$, has been established,³⁰ where $A = 4 \times 10^{- 3}$ N⁻¹ according to Legon.¹ Using this rule, with the experimental $k_{\sigma}$, the increment of electronic charge of the F atom is estimated to be 0.039 electron (e). However using our calculated value of $k_{\sigma}$, the resulting $\Delta Q_{\text{F}}$ is 0.024 e. This figure is consistent with the natural population analysis excess charges, shown in Table 4, which corresponds to the difference between the electronic charge in the complex with respect to the free molecule. Thus, the B3LYP and B3PW91 methods predicted amounts of electronic charge transferred over the outer halogen atom, $\Delta Q_{\text{F}} = -$0.020 e, compare well with the above estimate. However, the MP2 and BLYP values are smaller and larger, respectively, than our calculated value. Nevertheless, our analysis is suggestive of a net, though weak, transfer of electronic charge form the $\text{C}_{3}\text{H}_{6}$ moiety to the halogen dimer. Very small at the MP2/6-311 ++ G(d,p) level of theory, $\Delta Q_{\text{C}_{3}\text{H}_{6}} = 0.009$ e, and slightly larger at the B3LYP and B3PW91 methods, $\Delta Q_{\text{C}_{3}\text{H}_{6}} = 0.027$ e and 0.037 e, respectively. Recall that these numbers correlate with the intermolecular bond distance: the more electronic charge transferred, the shorter is the intermolecular distance.

Further inspection of the NBO second-order interaction energies reveals the $\pi_{\text{CC}} \rightarrow \sigma_{\text{ClF}}^{\ast}$ donor–acceptor interaction as the source of such intermolecular charge transfer. For the sake of brevity, Table 5, gives only the MP2, B3LYP, B3PW91 and BLYP second-order interaction energies, which are calculated by the equation²⁶

$$
\Delta E_{ij}^{(2)} = \frac{2F_{ij}^{2}}{E_{i} - E_{j}} \tag{4}
$$

with $F_{ij} = \langle f_{i}|F|f_{j}\rangle$, where $F$ is the Fock operator and $E_{i}$ and $E_{j}$ are the orbital energies of the donor $f_{i}$ and acceptor $f_{j}$ natural bond orbitals. Thus, from Table 5, it is observed that the energy term $F_{ij}$ is independent of the method, contrary to the energy resonance term ($E_{i} - E_{j}$), which depends markedly on the method. The best method, B3LYP, yields an energy difference of 0.88 kcal mol⁻¹, intermediate between the MP2 and BLYP values. However, it is not only this donor–acceptor NBO orbital energy resonance term what determines the intermolecular interaction. A correct description of the $\sigma_{\text{ClF}}^{\ast}$ orbital is also essential to describe properly the sharing within the halogen molecule of the electronic charge donated by the cyclopropane. Note that for the B3LYP method, of the 0.027 e withdrawn from the $\text{C}_{3}\text{H}_{6}$ moiety, 74% (0.020 e) goes to the fluorine and 26% (0.007 e) to the chlorine. However, for the BLYP method, 44% (0.030 e) of the withdrawn electronic charge goes to the fluorine atom and 56% (0.038 e) goes to the chlorine atom of the interhalogen acceptor moiety. This erratic behavior of the sharing of the withdrawn electronic charge should be associated with the correct description of the $\sigma_{\text{ClF}}^{\ast}$ natural bond orbital, a task apparently only achieved by the B3LYP method. A similar behavior in both ethylene and ammonia¹³ and ethyne¹⁵ complexes with halogen molecules has been reported earlier.

### Conclusions
The recently experimentally characterized¹⁶ psuedo-$\pi$.a$\sigma$ charge-transfer complex between cyclopropane and chlorine monofluoride ($\text{C}_{3}\text{H}_{6}\cdots$ClF) was studied with various density functional methods including gradient corrections for both the exchange and electron correlation functionals and the second-order Møller–Plesset (MP2) theory, with the 6-311 ++ G(d,p) basis set. In particular, two sets of approximate functionals were used, namely approximate pure functionals (BP86, BPW91, BLYP) and hybrid methods (B3P86, B3PW91, B3LYP). The B stands for Becke’s correction¹⁹ to the exchange density functional and P86, PW91 and LYP stand for three popular approximations to the electron correlation functional.²⁴ Then, all the density functional calculations were carried out with a slightly modified Becke’s correction term,¹⁸ which has been found to yield improved results for both strongly bound molecules²⁰ and weakly bound complexes.¹⁵,²¹ The latter was found to be the case also for the $\text{C}_{3}\text{H}_{6}\cdots$ClF charge-transfer complex.

In general, it is observed that the approximate pure functionals, BX, X = P86, PW91, LYP, describe poorly the experimental intermolecular distance and hence do not reproduce the rotational constant satisfactorily. Indeed, all three functionals underestimate severely the intermolecular distance. Therefore, it is expected that they will lead to an erroneous underestimation of the intermolecular energies. In particular, the B3LYP method agrees reasonably well with the experimental rotational constants, and hence the experimental intermolecu- lar distance is predicted to be only $0.005\ \mathring{A}$ shorter than that determined experimentally. Accordingly, the calculated inter- molecular energy of $1.42\ \text{kcal}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ is expected to be a trust- worthy value, although no experimental value has been determined yet. The MP2 predicted rotational constants have an error of $1.3\%$ with respect to the experimental values. Con- sequently, the predicted intermolecular distance and inter- action energy are expected to be satisfactory, although at a higher computational cost compared with B3LYP.

We calculated, using the WKB approximation for the inter- molecular motion, the number and energies of the vibrational states supported by intermolecular potential. It is found that five such states exist. This enabled us to estimate both the energy of the fundamental $1\leftarrow0$ and, the first overtone $2\leftarrow0$ vibrational excitations at 115.9 and $219.5\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, respectively.

Finally, the analysis of the natural bond orbitals sheds light on the nature of the weak intermolecular interactions. It is found that, on average, the $\text{C}_{3}\text{H}_{6}$ moiety transfers electronic charge to the ClF, and then this charge is redistributed among the two halogen atoms. This conclusion is reached by analys- ing the $\pi_{\text{CC}}\rightarrow\sigma_{\text{ClF}}^{*}$ donor-acceptor interaction between the $\pi_{\text{CC}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{ClF}}^{*}$ natural bond orbitals of the complex. The proper description of the delicate balance between the amount of electronic charge transferred and its subsequent redistribution among the two halogen atoms has proved to be a very diffi- cult task, apparently achieved only by the B3LYP method with a sufficiently flexible basis set.
