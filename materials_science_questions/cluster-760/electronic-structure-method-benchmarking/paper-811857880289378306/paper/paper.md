# Molecular structure, barriers to internal rotation, and the enthalpy of formation of cumene hydroperoxide $PhCMe_2OOH$: a DFT study

A. N. Egorova, $^{\star}$ M. V. Vener, I. S. Zhukov, G. D. Kozak, and V. G. Tsirelson

D. I. Mendeleev University of Chemical Technology of Russia,
9 Miusskaya pl., 125047 Moscow, Russian Federation.
Fax: +7 (499) 978 7903. E-mail: anegor@muctr.edu.ru

Conformational analysis of cumene hydroperoxide $PhCMe_2OOH$ (1) has been carried out using the density functional methods B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) and B3LYP/6-311+G(3df,2p). Ignoring rotation of methyl groups, molecule **1** has seven conformers differing in orientation of the $-CMe_2OOH$ fragment relative to the benzene ring and in mutual position of atoms in this fragment. The molecular structures, relative energies, and statistical distribution of the conformers were determined, and intramolecular rotational barriers were estimated. The enthalpies of formation of all conformers of molecule **1** were calculated using two approximations with inclusion of zero-point vibrational energy and temperature correction. Calculations using the isodesmic reaction (IDR) scheme made it possible to reduce the systematic error of the determination of the enthalpy of reactions. The total enthalpy of formation of compound **1** calculated with inclusion of statistical distribution of rotamers equals $-19.7\pm3.6$ kcal mol$^{-1}$. The combination of the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) approximation and the IDR scheme gives fairly accurate results (relative error is $\pm0.4$ kcal mol$^{-1}$) as compared to those obtained with the extended basis set 6-311+G(3df,2p).

**Key words:** molecular structure, rotational barrier, enthalpy of formation, cumene hydroperoxide, quantum chemical calculations, density functional method.

Peroxides are important intermediates of photochemical oxidation of hydrocarbons in atmosphere, $^{1,2}$ low-temperature combustion (especially, in the early stages), $^{3,4}$ and initiation of polymerization. A model was proposed $^{5-7}$ for ignition and low-temperature behavior of hydrocarbons (the case where the reactions involving peroxides dominate over other chemical transformations).

Peroxide molecules represent extended or closed chains bearing a large number of various substituents. This suggests the existence of certain conformations due to internal rotation of functional groups about ordinary bonds. Recently, peroxides of different composition $^{8-11}$ have been a subject of theoretical investigations including *ab initio* quantum chemical calculations to elucidate the molecular structure and to determine the statistical distribution of the most stable conformations based on analysis of the total energies of different conformers. This is necessary for calculations of thermodynamic characteristics of peroxides, which allow one to predict their behavior in reactions and to determine the kinetic and equilibrium characteristics. Experimental determination of thermodynamic characteristics of peroxides is often difficult owing to their instability and a large number of fast conformational transitions.

Cumene hydroperoxide $PhCMe_2OOH$ (**1**) is a weak explosive $^{12,13}$ widely used as an initiator of chain reactions in polymerization of unsaturated compounds and in joint production of acetone and phenol. $^{14}$ Molecule **1** contains an explosiphore peroxide group $-O-O-.^{15}$ An approach $^{16}$ to explosiveness studies of such substances includes computer simulation of explosion parameters. This requires not only the net formula and the density of the compound, but also the enthalpy of formation, $\Delta_fH(298$ K). The experimentally determined$^{17}$ $\Delta_fH(298$ K) value for liquid **1** is $-35.5$ kcal mol$^{-1}$. Gas-phase $\Delta_fH(298$ K) values obtained for compound **1** using indirect methods differ significantly (*cf.* $-18.81\pm1.60$ kcal mol$^{-1}$ according to Ref. 17 and $-26.49$ kcal mol$^{-1}$ according to Ref. 18).

The aim of this study is to carry out conformational analysis of molecule **1**, to estimate intramolecular rotational barriers, to reveal the most stable conformer, and to determine the statistical distribution of rotamers based on the results of density functional calculations. The results obtained allowed us to calculate the $\Delta_fH(298$ K) value for compound **1** in the gas phase. No similar calculations for this compound and its analogs have been reported so far.

The B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) method was used in this work be- cause it has a lower computational cost than the MP2 technique but gives quite similar results (geometric parameters and vibra- tional frequencies of compounds containing atoms of elements with $Z \leq 10).^{20-23}$ The B3LYP/6-311+G(3df,2p) method was used to elucidate the effect of basis set extension on the results of cal- culations.

The enthalpy of formation of **1** was determined using the total energies obtained from B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) and B3LYP/6-311+G(3df,2p) calculations for three isodesmic reactions (IDR). The total energies were calculated with inclusion of zero-pointvibrational energy correction using a scale factor of $0.9806.^{24}$  The temperature correction to the enthalpy of formation was calcu- lated as described below.

Partial compensation of a systematic error in the density func- tional and *ab initio* calculations of the enthalpy of reaction is attained $^{25}$ using the IDR scheme characterized by conservation of the number and type of chemical bonds in reactants and products.We used the following IDRs:

$$\mathbf{1}+\mathrm{CH}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{Me}_{2} \mathrm{CHOOH}+\mathrm{PhCH}_{3}, \tag{1}$$

$$\mathbf{1}+\mathrm{CH}_{4}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} \rightarrow \mathrm{Bu}^{\mathrm{t}} \mathrm{OH}+\mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{6}+\mathrm{HOOH}, \tag{2}$$

$$\mathbf{1}+\mathrm{MeOOH}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} \rightarrow \mathrm{Bu}^{\mathrm{t}} \mathrm{OOH}+\mathrm{PhOH}+\mathrm{HOOH}. \tag{3}$$

B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) and B3LYP/6-311+G(3df,2p) calculations of IDRs (1)-(3) with inclusion of the zero-point vibrational energy and temperature corrections made it possible to determine the enthalpies of reactions, $\Delta_{\mathrm{r}} H(298 \mathrm{~K})$. Since the enthalpies of forma- tion of all reactants except **1** are known (being determined experi- mentally or calculated theoretically). one can calculate the $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H(298 \mathrm{~K})$ value for compound **1**. It is in this way that the enthalpies of formation of all conformers of molecule **1** were calculated. The final value $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H(298 \mathrm{~K})$ of **1** was calculated using the statistical distribution of rotamers.

The enthalpy of formation of **1** was also calculated using the MOPAC program $^{26}$ by the semiempirical methods AM1 $^{27,28}$ and PM3 $^{29}$ with the IDR scheme and directly.

## Results and Discussion

Molecular geometry and harmonic vibrational frequen- cies. The equilibrium structures of conformers of mole- cule **1** calculated by the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) method are shown in Fig. 1. No imaginary vibrational frequen- cies were obtained, which indicates that all the gas-phase molecular structures located correspond to the ener- gy minima. The calculated O(2)-O(1) bond lengths in the stable (at room temperature) conformers **A-I, A- II, B-I, B-II, B-III, C**, and **D** (they are listed in or- der of decreasing stability) are equal to 1.452, 1.459,1.459, and 1.456 Å, respectively (Table 1). This is in reasonable agreement with the experimental value of 1.452 $\AA$ obtained $^{30}$ for $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{2}$ by IR spectroscopy. The O(1)- H(12) bond lengths in all the conformers studied are of about 0.97 Å. In the two lowest-lying, equal-energy con- formers of molecule **1 (A-I and A-II)**, the hydroperoxide fragment O(2)-O(1)-H(12) is directed toward the ben- zene ring, the group O(1)-H(12) being "above" the ring in **A-I** and "under" the ring in **A-II**. The dihedral angles C(6)-C(5)-C(4)-O(2) (see Fig. 1) characterizing the orientation of the fragment O(2)-O(1)-H(12) in these conformers differ by about $180^{\circ}$ (see Table 1). The bondangles with the central peroxide oxygen are close to $109^{\circ}$ (see Table 1) and imply a tetrahedral bond configuration at O(2). This is in agreement with the conclusion $^{31,32}$ about tetrahedral structure of the O atom in peroxides, in partic- ular, chloromethyl hydroperoxides.

Harmonic vibrational frequencies of **1** were also calcu- lated for the structure optimized by the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) method. For all conformers of molecule **1**, we determined five harmonic vibrations with frequencies lower than $250 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. Since many low-frequency vibra- tions have a rotational nature, the corresponding vibra- tional component of the temperature correction to the enthalpy of formation are often calculated using the free-rotator model. Following the study, $^{24}$ the contribu tion of each vibration to the temperature correction to the enthalpy of formation at 298 K was assumed to be equal to $(1 / 2) R T(0.296 \mathrm{kcal} \mathrm{mol}^{-1})$.

Rotational barriers. The barriers to internal rotation were calculated using the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) method. The dependences of the potential energy on the dihedral angles (Fig. 2) were determined by scanning the dihedral angle from 0 to $360^{\circ}$ with an increment of $10^{\circ}$ and ge ometry optimization in each step. Rotation was per- formed sequentially about the C(4)-C(5), C(4)-O(2), O(2)-O(1), C(4)-C(3) (C(4)-C(11)) bonds. At each energy minimum, the molecular geometry was fully opti- mized by the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) method.

As mentioned above, the hydroperoxide fragment O(2)-O(1)-H(12) in the most stable conformers **A-I** and **A-II** is directed toward the benzene ring. This signif- icantly differs these conformers from other conformers (see Fig. 1). From the data in Table 1 it follows that all conformational transitions of the cumene peroxide molecule are accompanied by rotation of methyl groups.

Analysis of the potential energy vs. dihedral angle plots (see Fig. 2) showed that the largest energy expenditures $(12.73\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1})$ are required for the $\mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{B}$ transformation to occur. The process is accompanied by simultaneous internal rotation about the bonds C(5)—C(4) and C(4)—O(2) and about the peroxide bond O(1)—O(2) with consideration of steric hindrance produced by hydrogen atoms of the peroxide group, Me group, and the benzene ring. The transformations $\mathbf{B} \leftrightarrow \mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{B} \leftrightarrow \mathbf{D}$ do not require large energy expenditures and can occur at room temperature. They involve rotation about the bonds C(4)—O(2) and C(5)—C(4), respectively. Taking the conformer **B-II** as an example (see Fig. 2), rotation of Me groups about the bonds C(3)—C(4) and C(11)—C(4) can be treated as hindered rotation with symmetrical barriers of $3.20\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$. Thus, ignoring rotation of Me groups, molecule **1** has seven conformers whose stabilities decrease as follows: **A-I, A-II > B-I, B-II, B-III > C > D**.

The relative distribution of conformers (see Table 2) was determined based on the analysis of the average differences between the total electronic energies of the conformers calculated by the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) and B3LYP/6-311+G(3df,2p) methods. Conformers **A-I** and **A-II** dominate at room temperature, the total proportion of the conformers **B-I, B-II**, and **B-III** being ~21%. The content of the conformers **C** and **D** is very low.

Enthalpy of formation, $\Delta_{f} H(298 ~K)$, of cumene hydroperoxide 1. The enthalpy of formation, $\Delta_{f} H(298 ~K)$, of compound $1$ was determined using the enthalpies $\Delta_{r} H(298 ~K)$ of the IDRs (1)-(3) calculated based on the total energies of reactants. The enthalpies $\Delta_{f} H(298 ~K)$ of the conformers of molecule $1$ were calculated from the enthalpies of IDRs (1)-(3) and from the thermodynamic characteristics of reactants and products of IDRs (1)-(3) estimated using published data (see Table 3).

The enthalpies of IDRs (1)-(3) and the $\Delta_{f} H(298 ~K)$ values for all conformers of molecule $1$ are given in Tables 4 and 5, respectively. The data used to determine the desired $\Delta_{f} H(298 ~K)$ values are listed in Table 3.

From the data in Table 4 it follows that the results of calculations using the numerical values obtained by the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) and B3LYP/6-311+G(3df,2p) methods are close. The average values $\Delta_{f} H(298 ~K)$ determined by the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) method using IDRs (1)-(3) are -20.01, -18.95, -18.250, and $-16.03 kcal mol^{-1}$ for the conformers $A, B, C$, and $D$, respectively. This is in good agreement (within the limits of $\pm 0.4 kcal mol^{-1}$ ) with the results of calculations in the extended basis set (B3LYP/6-311+G(3df,2p)). The results of the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) and B3LYP/6-311++G(2d,p) calculations of $\Delta_{f} H(298 ~K)$ for hydroperoxides are in excellent agreement (within the limits of1 kcal mol-1) with the results obtained by the CBS-QB3 method. $^{39}$ One can assume that combining "inexpensive" B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) calculations and the IDR approach will also make it possible to obtain reasonably accurate results for related compounds containing a larger number of heavy atoms.
