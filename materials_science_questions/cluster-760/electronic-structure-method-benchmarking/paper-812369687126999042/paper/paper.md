# A simple rule for determining ferromagnetism based on direct exchange energies

## Abstract
Direct exchange energy can be used as a measure of the ferromagnetic properties between two atoms, although we apply it to several diatomic species, and we propose that it is possible to obtain information about the magnetic properties of the lattice based on the correlation between the direct exchange energy and the exchange constant predicted by density functional theory methods.

## 1. Introduction
In previous works, we have examined direction exchange energies for different systems using ab initio and density functional theory methods (DFT) [1-3]. Although the RKKY interaction theory seems to be a relatively reasonable explana- tion for the origin of ferromagnetism in materials [4], the direct exchange energy contributions have only been limitedly studied in previous investiga- tions [5-7].

According to the basic principles of solid-state physics [8-10], the interaction energy between two moments may be written as the negative productof their two spins:
$$V_{\mathrm{ex}}=-J^{\prime} \mathbf{s}_{1} \mathbf{s}_{2}. \tag{1}$$

In the above expressions, $\mathbf{s}_{1} \mathbf{s}_{2}$ is the product ofthe two spins, $J'$ is the exchange constant and $V_{ex}$  is the exchange energy. In order for an interaction to lead to ferromagnetism, the exchange constant must usually be positive, because the parallel spin state leads to an exchange energy of $-J' s^{2}$ . Thus in an antiparallel orientation, $s_{1}=-s_{2}$ , and leads to an exchange energy of $J' s^{2}$ .
We can define the Weiss constant [10] as $\lambda$ , which is a measure of the strength of the molecular

$$
\begin{aligned}
J^{\prime}=\left(\mu_{0} N g^{2} \mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}\right) \lambda z^{-1} \approx & 0.1 \mathrm{eV} \text { (for most crystals). } \\

\end{aligned}
$$

Although there are other considerations that must be accounted for (distance between the dipoles, etc.), we propose a rule based on this simple principle. Our rule states that in a lattice of various spin concentrations, the average ferromagnetic properties of the crystal will be dependent on the direct exchange interaction between them. We apply this method in this work and apply it to the first row transition metals ( $\mathrm{Sc}_{2}$ through $\mathrm{Zn}_{2}$ ).

## 2. Methodology

All calculations in this paper were performed with the GAUSSIAN 98 software package [11]. Complete descriptions of methods and applications can be found elsewhere [12-13]. To compare the results of our DFT methods to the ideas (on related systems) proposed by Slater [8], we have performed Hartree-Fock (HF) and also local spin density approximation calculations, using the Slater exchange functional and the VWN correlation functional for the DFT calculation (SVWN). Although the B3PW91 method was shown to compute accurate exchange energy properties for the $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ molecule [1], we have improved these results by noting that (from the B3 functional):

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{XC}}= & (1-A) \mathrm{EXHF} \\
& +(A \text { EXSlater}+C D \text { Exnon-local }) \\
& + \text { Elocal }+B \Delta E \text { (Becke). }
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{XC}}= & P 2 \text { EXHF } \\
& +P 1(P 4 \text { EXSlater }+P 3 \text { DExnon-local }) \\
& +P 6 \text { EClocal }+P 5 D E,
\end{aligned}
$$

where the non-local correlation is provided by the LYP expression, and VWN is functional III (not functional V). The constants $A, B$, and $C$ are those determined by Becke by fitting to the G1 molecule set. In his work, Becke determined the values of the three parameters by fitting to the 56 atomization energies, 42 ionization potentials, 8 proton affinities, and 10 first-row atomic energies in the G1 molecule set [12-13], computing values of $A=$ $0.80, B=0.72$, and $C=0.81$. The LDA densities were used in addition to the Perdew/Wang 1991 correlation functional rather than VWN functional III and LYP.

Eq. (3) shows adjustable parameters in the GAUSSIAN 98 suite of codes. Since $P 2=1-A$, $B=P 3, C=P 5, P 4=A$, if Becke defined $A=$ $0.8, B=0.72$, and $C=0.81$ we can adjust them ( $P 1=P 6=1$ which is the default). From the recommendations previously published on small diatomic molecules [6-7], we have set the parameters of the B3 functional (in combination with the B3PW91 method) to $P 1=1.00, P 2=0.06$, $P 3=0.90, \quad P 4=0.85, \quad P 5=1.0, \quad P 6=1.0$. We have chosen the LanL2MB basis set due to its proven ability in calculations involving transition metals ([1,7] references therein).

## 4. Conclusions
It is very interesting to see that the application of such a simple principle can actually be used to determine the ferromagnetic behavior (at least qualitatively) in various species. Although we have applied these methods to very simple molecules, the potential to perform such calculations on large species, perhaps even performing simulations using the DFT potentials, is a definite possibility, and is currently being explored. Through an extensive search of the literature we are unable to find references dealing with this concept as applied to ferromagnetism, except for very old works [8], that do not take into account the advent of DFT methods. In our opinion, this area of research is timely, important, and potentially very useful in the design of novel ferromagnetic materials.
