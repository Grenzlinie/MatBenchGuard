# A Theoretical Survey on the Structures, Energetics, and Isomerization Pathways of the $B_5O$ Radical

CHANG-BIN SHAO, LIN JIN, YI-HONG DING

State Key Laboratory of Theoretical and Computational Chemistry, Institute of Theoretical Chemistry, Jilin University, Changchun 130023, People's Republic of China

Received 25 October 2009; Revised 10 July 2010; Accepted 21 July 2010
DOI 10.1002/jcc.21652
Published online 14 October 2010 in Wiley Online Library (wileyonlinelibrary.com).

**Abstract:** Boron-centered radicals have received growing interest. Recently, two groups reported density functional theory investigations (GGA-PW91 and B3LYP) on a hexa-atomic boron-oxide radical, $B_5O$, which has led to great discrepancies on the type of low-lying structures. In this article, we not only explore the energetics of doublet and quartet $B_5O$ isomers at high electron-correlated levels (CCSD(T)/6-311+G(2df), CCSD(T)/aug-cc-pVTZ, and G3B3) but also investigate the isomerization and fragmentation stability of the low-lying $B_5O$ isomers. All the high-level studies consistently show that the $B_5O$ radical possesses a belt-like ground structure $^201$ in doublet electronic state followed by isomer $^202$ with an exocyclic —BO moiety at around 3.0 kcal/mol. Kinetically, $^201$ and $^202$ are separated by a considerable barrier of about 20 kcal/mol. Thus, the two isomeric forms of $B_5O$ radical should be very promising for isolation in laboratory. However, the other four isomers reported recently are all kinetically unstable toward conversion to $^201$ and $^202$. The high thermodynamic and kinetic stability of $^201$ and $^202$ might make them as important building cores in the growth of boron-oxide clusters. This results would also help deeply understand the oxidation and doping mechanism of pure boron clusters.

## Introduction

The electron-deficiency of boron element has resulted in a rich chemistry of boron-related clusters, which has formed the focus of numerous experimental and theoretical studies. $^{1-10}$ Bulk or large boron-related clusters usually adopt three-dimensional (3D) structures, which are usually associated with the famous Wade-Mingos rules. $^{10-12}$ Yet, for smaller clusters, the effective delocalized $\pi$- and $\sigma$-bonding electrons along boron atoms might make the planar or quasi-planar structures more stable than the 3D structures. $^{13,14}$ Recently, with the aid of quantum chemical calculations, photoelectron studies have firmly demonstrated that the anionic boron clusters $B_n^-$ $(n=3$–15) all contain the planar ground structure. $^{3,4,15-21}$ In particular, paramagnetic compounds with boron-centered electron spins have been constructed using either the electron-accepting boron atoms bridged by planar $\pi$-conjugated organic systems, or by taking advantage of the 3D delocalized bonding in oligonuclear borane, haloborane, or carborane clusters. $^{22}$

Knowledge of boron-oxygen clusters is not only well related to the understanding of the eventual formation of bulk $B_2O_3$ but also important for understanding the doping and oxidation process of pure boron clusters. Up to now, besides the simple diatomic BO, small neutral B—O clusters $BO_2$, $B_2O_2$, and $B_2O_3$ have been characterized in gas phase in laboratory and have been theoretically shown to each have a linear ground structure. $^{23-25}$ Larger B—O clusters have been mainly limited to theoretical investigations. $^{26-29}$ In 2007, Drummond et al. made a very extensive theoretical investigation on various $B_xO_y$ $(1\leq x\leq9, 1\leq y\leq3)$ clusters at the GGA-PW91/6-311G(d) level and reported the lowest lying structures. $^{26}$ In 2008, Feng et al. theoretically studied $B_nO$ and $B_nO_2$ $(n\leq6)$ at the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) level. $^{27}$ In 2009, Nguyen et al. theoretically deter-

In this work, we will consider a hexa-atomic radical, $B_5O$. On
one hand, Drummond et al. reported two low-lying isomers with
almost isoenergetic energies (see the belt-like form $^2I$ and the $-BO$
bearing form $^2II$ in Fig. 1a). On the other hand, besides the belt-like
isomer $^2I$, Feng et al. reported four high-energy $B_5O$ isomers $^2III$-$VI$
(see Fig. 1b), but they did not report the very low-lying isomer $^2II$.
Both groups claimed to have considered various structural forms.
Because reliable energetic and isomerization information of the low-
lying isomers is vital for understanding the building processes from
small to larger clusters and even to bulk materials, it would be of
great interest to reinvestigate the structures and energetics of the $B_5O$
radical cluster. We attempt to resolve two basic issues. (1) Which is
the ground state isomer, $^2I$ or $^2II$? To discriminate between them,
higher level calculations such as CCSD(T)/aug-cc-pVTZ and G3B3
are very desirable. (2) What is the isomerism of $^2I$ and $^2II$ as well as
of other isomers? To answer both issues, a detailed potential energy
surface (PES) of $B_5O$ radical is constructed in this work, among
which the isomerism of $B_5O$ is considered for the first time.

## Computational Methods

The initial structural optimization and vibrational analysis are
performed at the B3LYP$^{30-33}$/6-311+G(d)$^{34-36}$ level. Twenty-
two minimum isomers (with all real frequencies) and 16 transi-
tion states (with only one imaginary frequency) are obtained.
For each transition state, the intrinsic reaction coordinate
(IRC)$^{37,38}$ calculations are carried out to ensure its right connec-
tion. To gain the detailed electronic information of $B_5O$, we per-
form the NBO$^{39-41}$ analysis at the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) level.
For further refinement of the energetics, the costly calculations
such as CCSD(T)$^{42}$/6-311+G(2df), CCSD(T)/aug-cc-pVTZ, and
G3B3$^{43,44}$ are applied. All calculations are performed with the
Gaussian03 program package. $^{45}$

## Results and Discussion

### $B_5O$ PES

Our isomeric search for the hexa-atomic system $B_5O$ undergoes
the following strategy. First, we structurally design various $B_6$
isomers according to one-dimensional (1D), two-dimensional
(2D), and 3D spatial atomic arrays covering the belt-like, chain-
like, cage-like isomers. Second, we sequentially replace one B-
atom of $B_6$ to obtain $B_5O$ isomers. At the B3LYP/6-311+G(d)
level, we finally locate 13 doublet and 9 quartet $B_5O$ isomers to-
gether with 16 doublet interconversion transition states (Fig. 2).
We can find that in general, the quartet states are energetically
much less stable than the corresponding doublet ones by at least
around 20 kcal/mol. The exception lies in the rather high-energy
isomer $^211$, for which the quartet is energetically close to the
doublet. The high-level single-point energy calculations at the
CCSD(T)/6-311+G(2df)//B3LYP/6-311+G(d)+ZPVE still favor
the doublet form for 11 by 2.2 kcal/mol. So, the doublet state is
generally more important for $B_5O$, and in the following parts,
we focus on the doublet species.

We first describe the general feature of the low-lying doublet
$B_5O$ PES, which is built at the CCSD(T)/6-311+G(2df)//
B3LYP/6-311+G(d)+ZPVE level. As shown in Figure 3, the
lowest energy isomer is $^201$ (0.0 kcal/mol) with a belt-like struc-
ture. The second low-lying isomer is $^202$ (2.1) with a structure
bearing an exocyclic $-BO$ moiety. Between $^201$ and $^202$, two
distinct low-lying isomerization pathways are identified, which
indirectly takes place via the intermediates $^203$ (9.7) and $^204$
(15.7), respectively, that is, $^201\leftrightarrow^203\leftrightarrow^202$ (Path I) and
$^201\leftrightarrow^204\leftrightarrow^202$ (Path II). The rate-determining (highest energy)
transition state is $^2$Ts02/03 (21.4) for Path I and $^2$Ts01/04
(32.5) for Path II. A lower lying rate-determining transition
state means a more competitive channel. Thus, Path I with in-
termediate $^203$ is kinetically much more competitive than Path
II with $^204$. We can say that in spite of the close energy, the
interconversion is difficult to occur between $^201$ and $^202$ at low
and normal temperature. Reasonably, no direct transition states
can be located between isomers $^201$ and $^202$.

As shown in Figure 3, besides Path I and Path II, there
are other conversion pathways for the lowest lying $^201$ and
$^202$, that is, $^201\leftrightarrow^206$ (30.9) via $^2$Ts01/06 (34.4), $^201\leftrightarrow^208$
(45.2) via $^2$Ts01/08 (51.9), $^202\leftrightarrow^207$ (43.4) via $^2$Ts02/07
(45.0), and $^202\leftrightarrow^211$ (65.4) via $^2$Ts02/11 (77.5). The involved
transition states are higher than those in Path I and Path II,
and more importantly, the product isomers have much higher
energy than $^203$ in Path I and $^204$ in Path II, respectively,
which indicates that once formed, these isomers can easily be
converted back to $^201$ and $^202$. Thus, these pathways cannot
compete with Path I and Path II. In addition, the fragmenta-

This can be ascribed to the fact that the rhombic $B_4$ has undergone significant structural changes during the dissociation (see the Supporting Information SI1 and SI4 ), that is, the peripheral B—B bonds that contributes much to the energetic stabilization are decreased from 1.550 and 1.597 Å (before the dissociation) to 1.522 and 1.522 Å (after the dissociation), respectively. For the remaining isomers $^2$03-$^2$13, their lowest conversion barriers are low and can be easily transformed to the lower-lying isomers. So, we will not discuss them further.

At the CCSD(T)/6-311+G(2df)//B3LYP/6-311+G(d)+ZPVE level, $^2$02 lies 2.1 kcal/mol above $^2$01. For further energetic refinement, we perform CCSD(T)/aug-cc-pVTZ and G3B3 calculations using the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) geometries. We find that $^2$02 is 3.0 and 2.2 kcal/mol, respectively, higher in energy than $^2$01 at the two levels. So, the belt-like $^2$01 is definitively the ground isomer of $B_5O$ radical.

### Bonding of Low-Lying $B_5O$ Isomers

Figure 4 depicts the characteristic orbitals of the isomers $^2$01 and $^2$02 at the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) level. For isomer $^2$01, HOMO-1 and HOMO-6 are mainly of the delocalized $\pi$-bonding over $B_4$-ring and BOB-ring, respectively. HOMO, HOMO-2, HOMO-3, and HOMO-5 are the delocalized $\sigma$-bonding over $B_5$. The bonding situation mimics the HOMO, HOMO-1, and HOMO-5 of the free $B_5$ cluster. So, in this sense, the belt-like isomer $^2$01 can be considered as the O-adducted $B_5$-ring. On the other hand, the HOMO-1, HOMO-2, and HOMO-3 of $B_5O$ $^2$01 behave similar to the HOMO-2, HOMO-1, and HOMO of $B_4$, respectively. In this respect, isomer $^2$01 can be structurally viewed as being formed when BO radical undergoes the (2+2) attack to the side of the rhombic $B_4$. For $^2$02, HOMO, HOMO-1, and HOMO-2 are mainly associated with the delocalized $\sigma$-bonding radial, delocalized $\sigma$-bonding tangent, and delocalized $\pi$-bonding over the $B_4$-ring. They nicely correspond to the HOMO, HOMO-1, and HOMO-2 of the naked $B_4$-ring. So, similar to the free $B_4$-ring, isomer $^2$02 can be viewed as containing the triple aromaticity$^{47}$ ($\pi$ + $\sigma$ + $\sigma$) within $B_4$-ring. In addition, HOMO-3 and HOMO-4 are two localized $\pi$-bonding between the exocyclic B—O bond. They well correspond to the free BO radical. Clearly, from both the structural and electronic viewpoints, $^2$02 can result from the (1 + 1) addition between a rhombic $B_4$-ring and a BO unit. As depicted in SI1 and SI4, the belt-like $B_5$ and the rhombic $B_4$ moieties are generally retained in isomers $^2$01 and $^2$02, respectively, despite somewhat quantitative changes in structural parameters.

Table 1 and Figure 5 depicts the natural charges and Wiberg bonding$^{48}$ and Mulliken spin distribution analysis$^{49}$ of the lowest-lying isomers $^2$01 and $^2$02. In both isomers, the nearest BO moiety bears very small charges, that is, −0.081e for $^2$01 and 0.089e for $^2$02. This is consistent with the above description that $^2$01 and $^2$02 can be structurally considered as the respective 2 + 2 and 1 + 1 combination between rhombic $B_4$ and BO. For the belt-like $^2$01, the unpaired spin is mainly divided to the closest (0.358) and farthest (0.697) B-atoms. For the isomer $^2$02, the spin is distributed mainly over the $B_3$-ring (0.369, 0.458, and 0.369) opposite to the —BO moiety. Again, the B-atom being farthest from the O-atom has the most spin (0.458). Concerning the bond order analysis, in $^2$01, the calculated Wiberg bond indexes for two peripheral B—O bonds (0.835 and 0.955) are smaller than yet close to 1, indicative of their typical single bonding. By contrast, the three internal B—B bonds (the bond indexes are 0.425, 0.667, and 0.849) are associated with single bonding. Interestingly, the closer the B—B bond is to O-atom, the weaker the single bond. The four peripheral B—B bonds

## Comparison with Previous Work and Implications
Both Drummond et al.$^{26}$ and Feng et al.$^{27}$ have revealed that isomer $^2$01 is the lowest energy one. The difference is that Drummond et al. also predicted an almost isoenergetic isomer, that is, isomer $^2$02, which lies only 0.7 kcal/mol higher at the GGA-PW91/6-311G(d)+ZPVE level. Our work is qualitatively closer to the work of Drummond et al. The difference between our and Drummond et al. results is that our sophisticated calcu- lations now separate $^2$02 from $^2$01 by about 3 kcal/mol. This energy difference can sufficiently lead us to conclude that the belt-like isomer $^2$01 is more stable than isomer $^2$02.

Also, we make comparative B3LYP calculations using the 6-31G(d), 6-31+G(d), 6-311+G(d), 6-311+G(2d), 6-311+G(3d), 6-311+G(2df), 6-311+G(3df), and aug-cc-pVTZ basis sets, each with frequency confirmation. At the eight ZPVE-corrected lev- els, $^2$02 lies at 1.15, 0.15, $-$1.85, $-$0.54, $-$0.46, 0.03, 0.30, and 0.48 kcal/mol with reference to $^2$01, as listed in Table 2. The lowest B3LYP/6-31G(d) level fortuitously provides the closest result to the CCSD(T) and G3B3 calculations. Yet, B3LYP/6-311+G(d) leads to the reverse (and also "incorrect") conclusion that $^2$02 is the ground isomer of B₅O. Our work should draw us to take caution that though being frequently used for geometrical optimization, $^{50,51}$ the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) method might lead to incorrect conclusion for B₅O, if without high-level energetic cor- rection.

The isomers $^2$04, $^2$07, $^2$09, and $^2$13 were also reported by Feng et al. Surely, as shown in Figure 1, six isomers ($^2$05, $^2$06, $^2$08, $^2$10, $^2$11, and $^2$12) calculated in this work are more stable than their reported ones. Most importantly, these high-energy isomers are kinetically unstable toward conversion to the low- lying $^2$01 and $^2$02, which is disclosed in our work for the first time.

That is to say, the O-doping results in an important structural pattern, that is, —BO, in boron-oxide clusters. It is of interest to point out that very recently, the first oxoboryl complexes with —B=O was isolated with X-ray structural characterization.$^{52}$

Bearing an unpaired electronic spin, the present studied B₅O belongs to the class of boron-rich radical. We are aware that with the development of synthetic technologies, more and more boron-centered radicals have been discovered by taking advant- age either of the $\pi$-conjugated organic systems or the effective electron delocalization in 3D skeletons.$^{22}$

The present simple planar radical, B₅O, also benefits from the effective $\pi$-aromaticity between the boron-atoms in two- dimensional skeleton. During the stepwise growing process of complex boron-oxide clusters, small BₙO radicals as well as closed-shell species should be experienced as the important intermediates. Therefore, the structures and stability of such rad- icals are vital for understanding the stepwise formation mecha- nism of larger boron-oxide clusters. This article reports the first description of the isomerization pathway between various iso- mers of the B₅O radical. Table 3 lists the detailed spectroscopic properties of $^2$01 and $^2$02. They could provide useful information for future laboratory characterization.

## Conclusions
Our detailed potential surface survey of the B₅O radical reveals that there are two thermodynamically and kinetically very stable isomers. One is the belt-like form $^2$01, the other is isomer $^2$02 that contains a rigid —BO moiety. The costly CCSD(T)/aug-cc- pVTZ and G3B3 calculations predict $^2$02 to lie higher in energy than $^2$01 by about 3 kcal/mol. Yet, B3LYP calculations with most basis sets predict $^2$01 and $^2$02 to be almost energetic, and the frequently used B3LYP/6-311+G(d) even leads to a reverse conclusion. The missing of the low-lying $^2$02 in the work of Feng et al. is reasonably interpreted by their structural optimiza- tion procedure. Further, we show that their calculated high- energy isomers are all kinetically unstable.

Altogether, the detailed isomerism of $B_5O$ radical reported in this work should be helpful for our understanding of the detailed doping and oxidation process of pure boron clusters, and can provide useful information for future characterization of the two low-lying and kinetically stable isomers $^201$ and $^202$.
