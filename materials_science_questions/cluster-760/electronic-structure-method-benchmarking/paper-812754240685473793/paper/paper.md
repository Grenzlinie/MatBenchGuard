# DFT Study on Dibenzothiophene Pyrolysis Mechanism in Petroleum

Tianshuang LI, Jie LI, Hongliang ZHANG*, Kena SUN, Jin XIAO

(School of Metallurgy and Environment, Central South University, Hunan Province,
Changsha 410083, China. *E-mail:csu13574831278@csu.edu.cn)

## Abstract

Dibenzothiophene sulfur (DBTs), the most stable sulfur species, are present in remarkable concentration in petroleum. Removal of DBTs is with profound significance in environmental protection. In this work, a density function theory method was adopted to investigate the pyrolysis mechanism of DBT. It was found that the pyrolysis of DBT is possibly started by H-migration or S-C bond rupture. Three main reaction pathways were found. Two dominating pyrolysis pathways are through thiols intermediate pyrolysis, another is through DBT carbene direct dissociation.The dominating products are sulfur free atom, 2-ethynyl-benzothiophene, 3-ethynyl-benzothiophene, ethyne, biphenylene, 1, 8-dihydrocyclopentaindene, with a certain amount of benzothiophene and SH radical.

For DBT, BT and thiophene, the most difficult step is the initial step. The highest energy barrier of DBT is about 17kcal/mol higher than BT and thiophene indicating pyrolysis of DBT needs more intense reaction condition.

Thiophenes can be stable in extreme conditions is possibly caused by the high energy barrier of initial reaction step. After the initial reaction steps, BT and DBT can be desulfurized more easily through thiols intermediates. Searching the catalyst that could lower the energy barrier of initial step and the reagent that could convert thiophenes directly into thiols may be a potential desulfurization approach for thiophenes.

# 1 Introduction

Petroleum is the most important energy source and accounts for more than 41% of the total energy¹. Due to the increasing utilization of high sulfur crude feedstocks, the average concentration of sulfur in fuel and petroleum products have been increasing¹⁻³.

In fuel, sulfur is an undesirable element that could cause severe problems, such as catastrophic equipment corrosion and catalyst poisoning⁴, acid rain, haze weather⁵,⁶, and even respiratory and cardiovascular diseases⁷. To minimize the negative effects, worldwide countries are adopting more severe regulations to achieve zero-sulfur emission⁸⁻¹⁴.

Sulfur compounds in crude oils can be classified into five different groups, mercaptans, sulfides, sulfoxides, sulfones, and thiophenes¹. To remove sulfur, several desulfurization approaches have been developed¹⁵⁻²³. The most widely used desulfurization method in refineries is catalytic hydrogenation desulfurization (HDS). Unfortunately, HDS is ineffective for the removal of thiophenes sulfur²⁴⁻³⁴ which are present in remarkable concentrations³⁵⁻⁴³ in petroleum. Studies on DBTs desulfurization shows that DBTs are stable to heating⁴⁴⁻⁴⁶, even in high temperature-pressure-active catalytic condition⁴⁷. Particularly, DBT is a representative sulfur aromatic hydrocarbons. Removal of DBTs is with challenge accompanied with significance in environmental protection.

The reason why DBTs can be stale in extreme condition is essential for promoting DBT desulfurization. Studies on DBT pyrolysis will contribute to understanding this

reason. However, the challenge is the complicated inter reactions that could take place between products. DBTs or its primary pyrolysis products could react with other products or secondary products to form a quite complicated products system. Only few primary products could remain to be detected. Jens⁴⁶ reported a flow pyrolysis experiment on DBT. Benzene, biphenylene and benzothiiren, with PAHs and PHAs were found in DBT pyrolysate. Dartiguelongue⁴⁸ performed an experiment on DBT pyrolysis and the results showed that the pyrolysis products are mainly consist of BT and biphenylene with large amount of PHAs. Xing⁴⁹ found that the amount of BT and DBT release behavior is related with temperature, the release rate increases during 600℃ ~800℃ then decreased till 1200℃~1300℃.

To study the pyrolysis behavior of DBT, it is necessary to identify pyrolysis primary products. Electron impact and mass spectra could provide more information. However, the fragments still can't be proved to be the primary products. In Roger's study⁵⁰, elemental sulfur, CS, $C_2H_2$, allylene, benzyne, $C_5H_3$, are the primary DBT fragments. Budgell⁵¹ implemented the major mass of DBT fragments are 184, 139, 92, 183 and 152. Meyerson⁵² illustrated the primary mass loss of DBT are $C_2H_2$, elemental sulfur, $H_2S$, $C_3H_3$ and CHS radical. Suhr⁵³ found that char, sulfur, toluene and phenylacetylene are formed.

No theoretical study on DBT pyrolysis has been reported yet. So far, the behavior of DBT is still unclear.

It is known that quantum chemical calculation plays a major role in chemical reaction mechanism study. In this work, we adopted a density function theory (DFT)

method to study the pyrolysis mechanism of DBT. This method has been successfully applied in our study of BT and thiophene pyrolysis⁵⁴. The aim of this study is to identify the primary pyrolysis products and the dominating pyrolysis reaction pathway of DBT. A further comparison of the pyrolysis difference between DBT, BT and thiophene is illustrated to give an overview of thiophenes sulfur pyrolysis behavior. We hope our research would shed light on DBT pyrolysis mechanism and provide guidance for new desulfurization technology development.

# 3 result and discussion

## 3.1 DBT pyrolysis initial reaction steps

Initial reaction step is the first reaction that will take place during pyrolysis. Since initial reaction step has great influence on following reaction path, taking all initial reaction steps into consideration to locate the step with lower energy barrier will improve the calculation reliability of pyrolysis mechanism. For DBT, the initial reaction steps can be classified into three classes, H migration steps, bond rupture steps and isomerization steps. In the following, all initial steps will be illustrated.

Eight H-migration initial steps could take place during pyrolysis. Eight DBT-carbenes could be formed directly by H-migration initial steps, as shown in Figure 1.

![](./images/812754240685473793_3.jpg)

Figure 1. Carbenes formed by DBT H-migration initial steps. C is short for DBT carbene.

All the eight H-migration initial steps are H proton migration processes. In previous, it has been proven that H proton migration is with lower energy barrier than H neutral atom migration⁵⁷. Hence, no single electron will be produced in H migration initial steps. However, some triplet state DBT-carbenes formed by H-migration initial steps are with lower energy than the singlet state. The singlet and triplet energies of DBT carbenes can be found in *Supporting Information Part 3C*. In calculation, all the DBT-Carbene are into pyrolysis processes under singlet state.

The second group of initial reaction steps is triggered by bond rupture which would result in the formation of bi-radical intermediate. For DBT, two bonds can be ruptured leading to two bi-radical intermediates formation. Details are given in Figure 2. None of the bond rupture on benzene ring was taken into consideration for the energy needed is truly high for initial steps.

![](./images/812754240685473793_4.jpg)

Figure 2. Bond rupture initial steps. *BIM* is short for bi-radical intermediate(triplet state).

In bond rupture initial steps, BIM3 and BIM4 are formed by two successive processes. Taking BIM3 as an instance, the formation process can be described below. First, S1-C2 bond is stretched to a certain length to form BIM1. Second, BIM1 is twisted around C7-C8 bond to generate BIM3. By scanning energy versus bond

distance or rotation angle, it was found the first step is a barrierless process and the energy ever increases to BIM1 energy. It is indicated that BIM1 and BIM2 are unstable and could convert back to DBT without energy barrier. The second step is with a twisting energy barrier. The energy firstly increases then decreases along with bond twisting, indicating biradical intermediates BIM3 is stable on potential energy surface. Hence, the energy barrier for BIM3 formation is constituted by two energies, bond rupture energy and rotation energy barrier.

The third group is isomerization initial steps. Six isomerization initial steps were found for DBT, as showed in Figure 3. For the high energy barrier needed to overwhelm, isomerization initial reactions could hardly take place. Hence, it has little influence on DBT dominating pyrolysis reactions. In this paper, is will not be introduced in detail.

![](./images/812754240685473793_5.jpg)

Figure 3. Intermediates formed by isomerization initial steps.

### 3.2 DBT-BIM3 pyrolysis reactions

BIM3 intermediate is formed by S1-C2 bond rupture and C7-C8 bond rotation.

BIM3 pyrolysis pathway is shown in Figure 4, Figure 5 and Figure 6. Details of IM and TS geometry appeared in this section can be found in *Supporting Information Part 2*.

![](./images/812754240685473793_6.jpg)

Figure 4. The three reaction pathways of BIM3. The t in bracket indicates the IM is in triplet state. The s in bracket and no bracket indicate the IM is in singlet state. A means intermediate and AS means TS. All energies are reported relative to DBT energy without ZPE correction.

From BIM3, three reaction pathways were found, BIM3-A1(t), BIM3-A2(t) and BIM3-C62(t). In this section, the first two reaction pathways will be explained. The third one could lead to the formation of C62 DBT carbene and will be illustrated in Section 3.3.

A1(t) is formed from BIM3 by C9-H migration to C2 atom. Two sulfur containing

compounds could be generated, CS and sulfur free atom. Details of A1(t) pyrolysis pathway are shown in Figure 5.

![](./images/812754240685473793_7.jpg)

Figure 5. BIM3-A1(t) reaction pathway. PRO is short for products. All energies are reported relative to DBT energy without ZPE correction.

A3(t) is formed from A1(t) by C8 atom bonded with C12. Energy barrier of this process is 61.48kcal/mol and transition state is AS4. CS is generated from A3(t) dissociation. A1(t) could also dissociate into sulfur free atom by S1-C13 bond rupture. The barrierless energy is 66.83kcal/mol.

Another pyrolysis pathway for BIM3 is through A2(t), which is evolved from BIM3 by C9-C2 bond formation. Primary pyrolysis products of A2(t) are sulfur free atom and biphenylene. The pathway is shown in Figure 6.

![](./images/812754240685473793_8.jpg)

Figure 6. BIM3-A2(t) reaction pathway. All energies are reported relative to DBT energy without ZPE correction.

In A2(t) pyrolysis process, a thiol IM (A5 in Figure 6) is formed. Sulfur atom and biphenylene are evolved from the thiol intermediate. Desulfurization reaction of thiol intermediate has been reported in our previous study⁵⁴. The thiols will first convert into the three member ring C-S-C IM (A6) with sulfur atom bonded to two adjacent carbon atoms and then dissociate into sulfur free atom to complete desulfurization. The highest energy barrier of A2(t)-PRO3 process is 55.29kcal/mol, located at A5-A6 process.

Among the three pathway of BIM3, the energy barriers of BIM-A2(t) is the lowest. It is obvious that sulfur free atom and biphenylene are the dominating primary products for BIM3 pyrolysis. The dominating pathway of BIM3 is DBT-BIM3-A2(t)-A5-PRO3.

### 3.3 DBT-Carbene pyrolysis reactions

In addition to BIM3, primary pyrolysis products could also be generated from DBT carbenes. More than 30 DBT carbenes have appeared in calculation. They are produced

directly or indirectly from the eight DBT carbenes formed by H-migration initial steps which have been illustrated in Section 3.1. In the following, the pyrolysis pathways of DBT carbenes will be illustrated in detail.

### 3.3.1 DBT-Carbenes inter-reactions

Upon illustration of DBT carbenes pyrolysis reactions, it is necessary to explain how so many DBT carbenes are produced. To present it clear, DBT carbenes are named based on the position of lone pair electrons and H proton. For instance, provided the lone pair electrons are located at C3 atom and the migrated H proton is bonded to C6 atom, the carbene is named by C36 carbene. If the lone pair electrons position of several DBT carbenes are the same, such as C5, they are in one series and share the name of C5 series carbene. Every series of DBT carbenes consist of 8 DBT carbenes. All DBT carbenes are presented in Figure 7.

![](./images/812754240685473793_9.jpg)

Figure 7. All DBT carbenes in appeared in DBT pyrolysis. Details of geometry can

be found in Supporting Information Part 3B.

All the DBT carbenes are formed from initial step carbenes. In one series, DBT carbenes could convert mutually by continuous H (proton) migration. Between different series, DBT carbenes could convert mutually by the migration of H(proton) adjacent to long pair electrons. Details of DBT carbenes inter and intra series reactions TS geometry can be found in Supporting information Part 3A and Part 3B.

By contrast, the average lowest energy barrier of DBT carbenes intra series reactions is 66.05kcal/mol. It can be concluded that DBT carbenes can easily transform into each other in one series by H migration steps. While the intra series reactions are with more resistance.

In every series, two DBT carbenes could be formed directly through H migration

initial step. Given the situation that H migration steps could take place, it is believed that every DBT carbene in this four series could be formed from initial step carbenes. It is necessary to take all DBT carbenes into calculation to explore possible pyrolysis reactions pathways.

Three main pyrolysis pathways have been found. The first is the pathway through dihydrocyclopenta[$c$]thiochromene (DTC), named by DTC pathway. The second is direct dissociation to ethynyl-benzothiophene (EBT, including 2-ethynyl-BT and 3-ethynyl-BT), named by EBT pathway. The third is to convert into 3-(but-1-en-3-yn-1-yl)-BT and 2-(but-1-en-3-yn-1-yl)-BT, named by BT pathway. Besides, several other pyrolysis pathway were also found. In the following, details of DBT carbene pyrolysis pathways will be illustrated.

### 3.3.2 Pyrolysis through DTC pathway

In DTC pathways, DTCs is formed from DBT carbene by the rearrangement reaction of C3 carbon atom and the thiophene ring, such as C32, C34, C36, C42, C46, C53, C54 and C62 DTC pathway. Most primary pyrolysis products in DTC pathways are sulfur atom and 1,8-dihydrocyclopentaindene. Details of IM and TS geometry appeared in DTC pathway can be found in *Supporting Information Part 4*.

In C32, C42 and C62 DTC pathway, DTC is formed. The highest energy barriers of this three reaction pathways are all 72.48kcal/mol, located in DTC pyrolysis process.

### 3.3.4 Pyrolysis through BT formation pathway.

## 4 Conclusion

Through detailed calculation on DBT pyrolysis, it is found that the dominating pyrolysis pathways of DBT are initialed by S-C bond rupture or H migration initial steps. S-C bond rupture will lead to a biradical intermediate formation and H migration

initial steps will generate carbenes. The dominating pyrolysis primary products are formed from the biradical intermediate and DBT carbenes. The biradical intermediate will transform into a thiol intermediate and then decompose into sulfur free atom and biphenylene. DBT carbenes could convert to thiol intermediate and subsequently decompose into sulfur free atom and 1,8-dihydrocyclopentaindene. DBT carbenes could also generate 2-ethynyl-BT and 3-ethynyl-BT by direct dissociation. The dominating primary sulfur containing products of DBT pyrolysis are sulfur free atom, 3-ethynyl-BT, 2-ethynyl-BT with a certain of BT and SH radical.

For DBT, BT and thiophene, the most difficult step is the initial step. From the aspect of energy barrier, BT and thiophene may have a similar initial pyrolysis temperature. Pyrolysis of DBT needs more intense reaction condition.

From our study on DBT, BT and thiophene pyrolysis mechanism, it is found that thiophenes can be stable in extreme sever conditions is possibly caused by the high energy barrier of initial reaction steps. After the initial reaction steps, BT and DBT can be desulfurized more easily through thiols intermediates. Hence, lowering the initial step energy barrier may be more efficient than enhancing temperature or pressure. Searching the catalyst that could lower the energy barrier H migration or S-C bond rupture and the reagent that convert thiophenes directly into thiols may be a potential desulfurization approach for thiophenes.
