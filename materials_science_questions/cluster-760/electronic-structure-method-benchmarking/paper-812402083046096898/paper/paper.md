# Structures and binding energies of $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$, $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{CO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{N}_{2}$

## Abstract

High-level ab initio and DFT calculations are performed on $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}, \mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{N}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{CO}_{2}$. We use large basis sets, both all-electron as well as ECP-based basis sets, for potassium. The calculated geometries and harmonic vibrational frequencies are shown to be reliable, with consistent results being obtained, and very good agreement with previous reliable calculations and experiment, where available. The basis set superposition error is shown to be small, and hence we believe that the binding energies, $D_{0}$, obtained (16.4, 4.4 and $9.0 \mathrm{kcal} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$, respectively) are reliable.

## 1. Introduction

For a long time, the study of isolated metal-cation/ligand complexes has been in the domain of the mass spectrometrist, gas-phase spectroscopist or theoretician, but with little practical application beyond 'testing methods of solvation' or 'testing theoretical methods'. Sometimes detailed spectroscopy has provided insight into chemical bonding, but again such discussions have been at a far remove 'from the real world'. Recently, however, atmospheric chemists with an interest in the upper reaches of the atmosphere have become interested in the link between the chemistry of metal ions in the ionosphere [1] and macroscopic phenomena, such as sporadic metal layers [2]. It has been hypothesized that the neutralization of a cloud of metal cations can lead to the sudden appearance of clouds of neutral species, which then rapidly vanish as neutral chemistry removes the neutral atoms. Critical to the chemistry is the clustering of isolated $\mathrm{M}^{+}$cations with atmospheric ligands, such as $\mathrm{O}_{2}, \mathrm{O}, \mathrm{N}_{2}, \mathrm{CO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ - this is because the neutralization reaction

$$\mathrm{M}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \stackrel{\text { third-body }}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm{M}\tag{1}$$

is inefficient, owing to the low number of exit channels. If, however, $\mathrm{M}^{+}$is complexed to a ligand, L, then the corresponding neutralization

Recently, we have been part of a collaboration involved in a detailed study of the structure and thermodynamics of $\mathrm{Na}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{X}$ species, and we have reported a range of results on $\mathrm{Na}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{O}$ (the cation of sodium monoxide, $\mathrm{NaO}$), $\mathrm{NaO}_{2}^{+}$, $\mathrm{Na}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{N}_{2}$, $\mathrm{Na}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{CO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Na}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$. These results were used to generate thermodynamic and kinetic data over a range of temperatures, and these were then used in an atmospheric model to simulate the behaviour of descending $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ clouds in the upper atmosphere under a range of conditions, and the results compared to actual sporadic sodium layer events [3,4]. The agreement has been very pleasing.

We have now started considering the $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{X}$ species, and have completed a detailed study of $\mathrm{KO}$ [5] and $\mathrm{KO}^{+}$ [6], and $\mathrm{KO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{KO}_{2}^{+}$ [7]. In the present work, we present the structures and binding energies of the remaining complexes: $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{N}_{2}$, $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{CO}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$. In a future study we shall employ the data in an atmospheric model for sporadic potassium layers, whose observation has been reported previously [8], and further observational studies are underway. Important for these studies is that the results are both accurate, and that they are performed at a consistent level of theory for all species involved.

There have been a number of studies of $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ species, both experimental [9,10] and theoretical [11-14]. The $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{N}_{2}$ complex has been less studied, with an experimental determination of the molecular potential from scattering studies [15,16] and two ab initio studies by Ikuta [17,18] and one by Pullman et al. [19]. The only work on $\mathrm{K}^{+} \cdot \mathrm{CO}_{2}$ appears to be as part of series of scattering studies [15,16]. We shall discuss the results of the previous studies and compare with our own below.

## 2. Theoretical details

We employ all-electron and effective core potential (ECP)-based basis sets, described below. Geometries are optimized at the B3LYP, MP2, QCISD and CCSD(T) levels of theory. Note that the correlated electrons are specified in the footnotes to the relevant Tables, but that the 3s3p electrons of potassium are always correlated. Single-point CCSD(T) calculations using a larger basis set were performed in order to obtain more accurate energetics; for these, the 1s orbitals were frozen on the non-hydrogen first row atoms, for potassium, the 3s3p electrons were correlated. For the latter, full counterpoise (CP) corrections were employed in order to correct for basis set superposition error (BSSE). The counterpoise corrections, and the calculated BSSEs, were at the relevant optimized geometries at the minimum of the complex. All optimizations and harmonic frequency calculations were carried out with **GAUSSIAN** [20], with the single-point CCSD and CCSD(T) calculations being performed with **MOLPRO** [21].

### 2.1. Basis A

For potassium, a 6-311G(2df)-type basis set was employed [22], which was obtained from the Gaussian Basis Order Form [23], and had a (62111111/33111/311/1) contraction. For all other atoms, the standard 6-311++G(2df) basis sets were employed.

### 2.2. Basis B

For potassium, the LANL2 [24] ECP was employed, which describes the 1s, 2s and 2p core electrons. To this were added (8s8p4d) functions, as used previously in our work for $\mathrm{KO}^{(+)}$ and $\mathrm{KO}_{2}^{(+)}$ [5-7], together with two f functions with $\zeta=1.0$ and 0.25. For all other atoms, the standard aug-cc-pVTZ basis sets were employed.

### 2.3. Basis C

For potassium, the ECP10MWB [25] ECP was employed, to which [10s9p6d4f3g] functions [26] were added, as used in out previous work on $\mathrm{KO}_{2}^{(+)}$. For all other atoms, the standard aug-cc-pV5Z basis sets were employed, omitting the h functions for the non-hydrogen atoms, and the g functions for hydrogen.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. $K^+ \cdot H_2O$

The optimized geometries and vibrational frequencies for $K^+ \cdot H_2O$ are shown in Table 1. As may be seen, there is excellent agreement between the all-electron basis set results using Basis A and the ECP-based Basis B results using the B3LYP method. Consequently, we conclude that the use of the ECP in Basis B is having little effect on the accuracy of the results. There are small changes in the calculated parameters when moving to the MP2 and QCISD methods, but the agreement between all of the results is very pleasing. ### 3.2. $\mathbf{K^+ \cdot N_2}$

The results of the geometry optimizations and harmonic frequency calculations for $\mathrm{K^+ \cdot N_2}$ are given in Table 3. As may be seen, we considered both the linear and the T-shaped structures. As with $\mathrm{Na^+ \cdot N_2}$ [28], we found that the T-shaped structure was a saddle point, obtained from internal rotation of the $\mathrm{N_2}$, and being the saddle point between the two equivalent linear structures. The agreement between the different levels of theory is again very good, and again the geometry of the $\mathrm{N_2}$ moiety agrees well with experiment (1.098 Å) [29], as does the frequency ($\omega_\mathrm{e}=2359$ cm$^{-1}$)

[29]. Ikuta [18] optimized the geometry of $\mathrm{K^+ \cdot N_2}$, but only at the HF/3-21G level of theory, where $r_{\mathrm{K-N}}$ was calculated to be 3.03 Å – reasonably close to our best value of 2.94 Å, perhaps showing that the geometry is not dependent on electron correlation to a large extent, or that a fortuitous cancellation of errors occurs; the bond length of 3.53 Å obtained in [19] seems a little long. No vibrational frequencies were calculated in [18], but Pullman et al. [19] calculated the intermolecular stretch and bend frequencies, obtaining $\omega_1=112$ cm$^{-1}$, and $\omega_2=13.5$ cm$^{-1}$. Although the stretch is in good agreement with our CCSD(T) value of 135 cm$^{-1}$, the bending frequency is very far removed from our value of 177 cm$^{-1}$.

As may be seen from the values in Table 4, as with $\mathrm{K^+ \cdot H_2O}$, the binding energy changes by a very small amount from CCSD to CCSD(T), and again with the large basis sets employed, the BSSE is small at 33 cm$^{-1}$. We can correct our $D_\mathrm{e}=5.1$ kcal mol$^{-1}$ to a $D_0$ value, giving a value of 4.4 kcal mol$^{-1}$. Ikuta [18] also reported binding energies, obtaining a value of 7.5 kcal mol$^{-1}$ at the MP3// HF/3-21G level of theory (where different basis sets were employed at the MP3 level) – a result that is a little higher than our best value of 5.1 kcal mol$^{-1}$ (see Table 4). ### 4. Concluding remarks

High-level ab initio and DFT calculations have been performed on $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{N}_2$ and $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{CO}_2$. We have demonstrated that the all-electron and ECP-based basis sets perform similarly, and that our results appear to be reliable, as judged by the consistency of the results. In addition, for the binding energy, the BSSE is very small, and demonstrates that the basis sets are large enough and flexible enough for these species.

For $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$ our results are in excellent agreement with previous theoretical and experimental data for the geometry, vibrational frequencies and binding energies. For $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{N}_2$, limited theoretical studies appear to have been performed, and the agreement is variable for the calculated parameters. The values obtained herein were in excellent agreement with a potential derived from scattering data. For $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{CO}_2$, the work presented herein seems to be the only reliable data.

The binding energies are indicative of the interactions that occur in these species, with the value for $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$ being the highest, as expected from the charge/dipole interaction, with the geometry following expectations also, with the $\text{K}^+$ being along the dipole direction, and interacting with the $\delta$-end of the molecule. $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{CO}_2$ is found to have a geometry very similar to that of $\text{Na}^+\cdot\text{CO}_2$ [30]: the binding energy reflects the fact that $\text{K}^+$ is interacting with a polar CO bond, and as noted above, there appears to be no bonding interaction of $\text{K}^+$ approaching the $\delta+$ carbon atom along a $\text{C}_2$ axis. The weakest interaction is that of $\text{K}^+$ with $\text{N}_2$, owing to the lack of a dipole, or polarity, in the ligand. The interaction energies of $\text{K}^+\cdot\text{X}$ follow the same trend as seen previously for $\text{Na}^+\cdot\text{X}$, where the $D_0$ values are $22.7\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$ [30], $12.2\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$ [30] and $6.6\ \text{kcal mol}^{-1}$ [28] for $\text{X}=\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{N}_2$, respectively. The higher values for $\text{Na}^+\cdot\text{X}$ (even though $\text{K}^+$ is expected to be more polarizable) arise from the higher charge density in the smaller sodium cation, and some contribution from electron–electron repulsion, which will be greater for $\text{K}^+$.
