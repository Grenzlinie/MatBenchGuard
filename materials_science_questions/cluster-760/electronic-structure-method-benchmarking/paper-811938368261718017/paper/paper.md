# Density functionals for calculating NMR $^1J_{CH}$ coupling constants in electron-rich systems

Alvaro Cunha Neto, Francisco P. dos Santos, Alex S. Paula, Cláudio F. Tormena, Roberto Rittner *

Physical Organic Chemistry Laboratory, Chemistry Institute, State University of Campinas, Caixa Postal 6154, 13084-971, Campinas, SP, Brazil

Received 6 December 2007; in final form 18 January 2008
Available online 30 January 2008

## Abstract
Several DFT exchange-correlation functionals were used to calculate one-bond carbon-hydrogen spin-spin coupling constant ($^1J_{CH}$) for halocyclohexanes and 2-substituted-tetrahydropyrans. The prediction of $^1J_{CH}$ for halocyclohexanes using the B3LYP and BLYP functionals gave better results than other functionals, while the PBE functional showed to be more appropriate to predict $^1J_{CH}$ for some electron-rich systems – the tetrahydropyran derivatives.

## 1. Introduction
Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy is an essential tool for the determination of molecular structure and conformations since nuclear shielding constants and scalar spin-spin coupling constants (SSCCs) provide sensitivity probes for electronic structure of a molecule, as it has been demonstrated in many review articles and specialized books [1–5]. The great interest in carbon–hydrogen coupling constant is clearly spurred on by the great applicability of these coupling constants. They are used extensively for assignment purposes, in structure elucidation, stereochemistry, substituent effects and studies to gauge steric strain [6]. The SSCC between two nuclei depends on the distribution of electrons in a bond or in a chain of bonds and, therefore, it represents an important source of information on the bonding situation of the molecule under investigation [6]. However an efficient quantum chemical method to reliably predict NMR SSCCs is a prerequisite for obtaining theoretical data in agreement with NMR experimental results, for the adequate interpretation of the latter and, consequently, the corresponding determination of molecular geometry [7].

The calculation of SSCC as a derivative of the total energy was originally performed, numerically, using the finite perturbation theory (FPT), which was implemented into existing quantum chemical methods with little additional programming efforts, because it required just the consideration of a magnetic field produced by a nuclear magnetic moment [8,9]. The first attempt to calculated SSCCs with density-functional theory (DFT) using FPT allowed the calculations of its Fermi contact (FC) term [10]. Several levels of theory as MP2 [8,9], MCSCF [11–13], CI [14], and CC [15] have been applied using the coupled perturbation theory (CP) for the calculation of SSCC values.

Recently, the development of various correlations corrected *ab initio* methods, including the second order polarization propagator approach (SOPPA) [16] and the equation-of-motion (EOM) coupled cluster (CC) method [17] followed where these methods are based on sum-over-states (SOS) rather than second derivative formulations of the SSCC. DFT represents an attractive way for the calculation of SSCCs. The DFT methods have received widespread recognition among chemists due to their relatively high accuracy and modest computational cost

In a recent study, Maximoff et al. [20] determined 96 one-bond $^1J_{CH}$ coupling constants using 20 functionals, at several levels of sophistication from the local density approximation (LDA) to generalized gradient approxima- tions (GGAs), hybrid GGAs, and meta-GGAs. Although, no correlation between SSCC quality and the class of DFT approximation was observed, it was possible to conclude that the PBE functional gives accurate $^1J_{CH}$ values involv- ing $sp^2$, $sp^3$, and aromatic carbon atoms, while the semi- empirical HCTH407 and VSXC allows to obtain $^1J_{CH}$ SSCCs involving a sp carbon atom. However, Keal et al. [21] demonstrated that PBE functional is less accurate than B3LYP for couplings involving N, O and F atoms, in opposition to Maximoff [20] findings. The accumulated computational experience with DFT exchange-correlation functional shows that the spin-spin coupling constant description is poor when electron-rich atoms are involved. In general, the Fermi contact term deteriorates with there is an increase in the number of lone pairs on the coupled atoms [22].

In present study, it was employed different exchange- correlation functional in the SSCC calculation for some 2-substituted tetrahydropyrans, which present a very elec- tron-rich O–C–X system. The 2-substituted tetrahydropy- rans were chosen because they are in a close relationship with carbohydrates and the application of NMR SSCC cal- culations in carbohydrate systems is scarce [23]. It is expected that the results showed here will lead to an increase of the application of NMR spin–spin coupling constant calculations to solve some problems such as deter- mination of conformation and of the three-dimensional structures for carbohydrate molecules.

The calculations were also performed for some halocy- clohexanes which were used as reference compounds to analyze the effect of O–C–X moiety in $^1J_{CH}$ SSCC calcula- tions with several functionals.

## 2. Computational details

The SSCCs were determined including the four terms described by Ramsey: Fermi contact (FC), spin–dipole (SD), diamagnetic spin–orbit (DSO) and paramagnetic spin–orbit (PSO) [24]. All calculations were performed using the Gaussian 03 program [25]. The geometries employed in the SSCCs calculation were optimized at MP2/cc-pVTZ. For compounds containing iodine atom, the LANL2DZ ECP basis set was employed for the iodine. In SSCCs calculation, the EPR-III basis set was employed for carbon and hydrogen atoms, while for oxygen, fluorine, chlorine and bromine the cc-pVTZ basis set was used. For iodine derivatives the LANL2DZ ECP was also used. Some exchange-correlations functionals were used in the present work. The polarizable continuum media (PCM) [26] was used as a solvation model (using $CH_2Cl_2$ as sol- vent) in SSCC calculation at the same theory level.

## 3. Experimental

### 3.1. Compounds

Chloro-, bromo- and iodocyclohexanes and 2-meth- oxypyran were obtained commercially. Fluorocyclohexane was prepared by the reaction of cyclohexene and hydrogen fluoride–pyridine [27]. The 2-chloropyran [28], 2-bromopy- ran [29], and 2-iodopyran [30] were prepared according to procedures described in the literature.

### 3.2. Spectra

The purity of studied compounds were checked by their $^1$H and $^{13}$C NMR spectra in $CDCl_3$ recorded on a Bruker DPX 250 spectrometer operating at 250.13 and 62.90 MHz, respectively. The $^1J_{CH}$ couplings were mea- sured from low temperature $(-90\ ^\circ\text{C})$ coupled $^{13}$C NMR spectra, with samples prepared from 30 mg of compounds and 0.7 mL of $CD_2Cl_2$ as solvent. Spectra were all refer- enced to $Me_4Si$ and the typical conditions for coupled $^{13}$C spectra were: spectral width 7400 Hz with 32K data points and zero-filled to 64K to give a spectral resolution of 0.2 Hz/point.

## 4. Results and discussion

### 4.1. Halocyclohexanes

Table 1 presents the calculated and experimental one- bond spin–spin coupling constant $(^1J_{CH})$ values for cyclo- hexane derivatives. In this case, the cyclohexanes present two conformations that contribute for experimental results. Thus, the SSCCs of the two conformers, obtained from the topoisomerization process (Fig. 1) [31], were observed at low temperature spectra. A comparison of data obtained with B3LYP functional and PBE functional shows that the former leads to a better agreement with the experimen- tal data. Actually, in this case, the PBE functional results present larger deviations $(\sim 14\ \text{Hz})$ than any other func- tionals used here. These findings are in agreement with the results reported in the literature [21]. SSCC calculations including solvent effects were performed for all functionals used in the present work and it was not observed any sig- nificant solvent effect in $^1J_{CH}$ SSCC values for the studied systems. These effects were around $\pm 2\ \text{Hz}$.

Table 2 shows the calculated and experimental SSCCs for this class of compounds. In this table, all DFT functionals overestimate the $^1J_{CH}$ values, since they are not accurate for systems involving electron-rich atoms [22]. Here, the PBE functional gives better results, as observed by Maximoff et al. [20], while B3LYP functional leads to the highest differences between calculated and experimental data. The B972 DFT calculation in isolated molecule gives similar results as those obtained using PBE functional (Table 2). It can also be observed from Table 2, that the difference between calculated and experimental values increases along to the series of halogens, which may be due to the increasing acceptor character of $\sigma^{*}_{C-X}$ orbital in the series Cl > Br > I. As it was observed for halocyclohexanes, in the 2-halo-tetrahydropyranes case, there are also no significant solvent effects in $^1J_{CH}$ SSCC calculations.
