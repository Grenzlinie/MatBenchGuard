## ABSTRACT:
The structures, energetics, and bonding properties of small $\text{Si}_n$ ($n =$ 1–6) clusters doped with an yttrium atom are studied by means of a hybrid density functional technique (B3LYP). Close similarity is found between $\text{YSi}_n$ and $\text{ScSi}_n$ clusters in their geometries, size dependences of binding energies and fragmentation energies, charge transfer, spin distribution, and bonding features. It is found important in density functional calculations as well as in Hartree–Fock calculations to examine several different electronic configurations for a given spin multiplicity to determine the most stable configuration because these systems are characterized by small energy gaps and numerous close-lying states.

## 1. Introduction

S ilicon plays the central role in microelectronic technology and its properties can be modified dramatically by the presence of metal impurities. Currently, there is a continuing trend toward the miniaturization of electronic devices, and the structures and properties of these reduced systems are known to be very different from those of their bulk counterparts. Thus, it is of fundamental and technological importance to explore how these well-described and extensively used systems will transform as they are scaled down to cluster dimension. A detailed study on the size dependence of the structures and properties of clusters will greatly deepen our understanding of the formation and bonding mechanisms of metal–silicon composite materials and metal–silicon contacts. Moreover, the properties characteristic for clusters could be exploited for new technological applications.

Using laser vaporization techniques, Beck [1] and Scherer et al. [2] generated small silicon clusters doped with transition metal (TM = Cr, Mo, and W) and noble metal atoms (Cu, Ag, Au). Recently, Hiura et al. [3] reported the formation of a series of $\text{TMSi}_n^+$ clusters (TM = Hf, Ta, W, Re, Ir, etc. with $n = 14, 13, 12, 11, 9$, respectively) using an ion trap. According to their combined experimental and theoretical analysis, the metal atoms were believed to be trapped at the center of polyhedral $\text{Si}_n$ cages and a hexagonal prism structure was proposed for $\text{W@Si}_{12}$. This is the first experimental evidence for the formation of stable metal-encapsulating Si cage clusters and has thus aroused renewed interest in the search for fullerene-like Si cage clusters stabilized by metal atoms.

Stimulated by these experimental results, considerable computational efforts focused on small silicon clusters doped with noble (Cu) [4–7] and TM (Cr, Mo, W) [8–11] atoms in the range of experimentally detected sizes to understand the size dependence in the geometric, energetic, and bonding properties of these clusters. In addition, there is an intense recent effort dealing with the structures and stability of cage-like $\text{TM@Si}_n$ clusters [12–19].

In a series of computations [4–9, 12–14, 20], we studied the geometric and electronic properties of silicon clusters doped with noble and TM atoms (Cu, Sc, Cr, Mo, W, and Ir). In a recent contribution [20], we studied the geometric, energetic, and bonding properties of $\text{ScSi}_n$ ($n = 1$–6) clusters at the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) level and compared the behaviors of these systems with those of $\text{CuSi}_n$ [4–7] and $\text{TMSi}_n$ (TM = Cr, Mo, W) clusters [8–10]. It was found that the electron transfer behavior is similar in $\text{ScSi}_n$ and $\text{CuSi}_n$ (from metal to $\text{Si}_n$) but opposite in $\text{TMSi}_n$ (from $\text{Si}_n$ to metal), while the adsorption and substitutional structures of Sc, Cu, and TM in $\text{Si}_n$ matrices as well as the metal–silicon bonding patterns for these species differ from each other.

In this article, the influence of a Y atom on the structures and properties of small $\text{Si}_n$ ($n = 1$–6) clusters is studied by means of a hybrid density functional technique (B3LYP). Sc, Y, and La have been extensively studied as impurities in fullerenes. Earlier molecular dynamics [21] and Hartree–Fock calculations [22] both predicted that the valence $3d$ electron of Sc remains localized and only its two valence $4s$ electrons are donated to the fullerenes while all of the three valence electrons of Y and La are transferred to the fullerenes, leading to a mixed covalent and ionic bonding between the metal and the cage in the former and a pure ionic interaction in the latter. However, resonant photoelectron spectroscopy experiments [23] and recent theoretical calculations [24, 25] both indicated that there is strong hybridization between the metal valence $d$ orbitals and the cage orbitals for all three elements (Sc, Y, and La). It is therefore interesting to examine how Sc and Y will behave in the environment of $\text{Si}_n$ matrices. We will focus on the influence exerted by the Y atom on the structures and stability of $\text{Si}_n$ matrices, the charge transfer and bonding between Y and the $\text{Si}_n$ framework, the ground-state electronic configuration of $\text{YSi}_n$ clusters, and the distribution of the spin polarization among the cluster constituents. To our knowledge, there are only a thermodynamic investigation of $\text{YSi}_n$ ($n = 1, 2$) [26] and an ab initio molecular dynamics simulation of amorphous alloy $\text{Y}_x\text{Si}_{1-x}$ [27].

The article is organized as follows: The computational details are described in Section 2; the results are presented and discussed in Section 3; and our final conclusions are given in Section 4.

## 2. Computational Details

All calculations for the $\text{YSi}_n$ clusters ($n = 1$–6) were carried out using a hybrid density functional method (B3LYP) implemented in the Gaussian 98 code [28]. This density functional consists of a combination of Becke's three-parameter exchange functional (B3) [29] with Lee, Yang, and Parr's (LYP) correlation functional [30].

In the calculation for $\text{ScSi}_n$ clusters [20], we used a 6-311+G(d) basis set. However, this basis set is not available for Y. Therefore, we have chosen two other basis sets for $\text{YSi}_n$ clusters. The first is an all-electron basis set (called DZVP [31]) that is constructed with the following contraction scheme: [4s, 3p, 1d]/(12s, 8p, 1d) for Si and [6s, 5p, 3d]/(18s, 12p, 9d) for Y. For the Sc atom, the DZVP basis set is [5s, 3p, 2d]/(15s, 9p, 5d). The quality of the DZVP basis set is tested under two aspects. First, we reoptimized the structures of the most stable isomers of $\text{ScSi}_n$ ($n = 1$–6) and calculated their binding energies with the DZVP basis set and found little difference from the corresponding 6-311+G(d) results in both the geometry and binding energy. Further, we calculated the properties of Y and Si atoms and their dimers using the DZVP basis set and compared these results with available experimental data and our previous results for Si and $\text{Si}_2$ [7] using the 6-311+G(d) basis set (see Table I). ### 3.1. YSi

The ground state for YSi is determined to be a spin quartet (⁴Σ⁻). The same spin multiplicity was obtained for the ground state of ScSi. At the B3LYP/DZVP level, the bond length of YSi is 2.69 Å (see Table I), which is slightly longer than the value of 2.52 Å for ScSi [20]. Both Y—Si and Sc—Si bond lengths are considerably longer than the Si—Si dis- tance (2.28 Å) in Si₂ and the Cu—Si distance (2.25 Å) in CuSi [7]. Therefore, a noticeably larger geo- metric distortion is expected when an Si atom in Siₙ₊₁ is replaced by Y or Sc than by Cu.

The binding energy calculated for YSi is 2.28 eV at the B3LYP/DZVP level, which is slightly larger than the value of 2.04 eV for ScSi at the same level of theory. Both values are considerably smaller than the binding energy of 3.01 eV found for Si₂ (see Table I). Therefore, the Y—Si and Sc—Si bonds are considerably weaker than the Si—Si bond.

The charge and spin distributions in YSi are sim- ilar to those found in ScSi [20]. Both Y and Sc atoms transfer about 0.4 electrons to the Si atom and the unpaired electron is shared by Y or Sc and Si.

### 3.2. YSi₂

Three isosceles-triangle structures with C₂ᵥ sym- metry were identified for YSi₂ (see Fig.1). They are in the doublet states of ²B₂, ²A₁, and ²A₂. The ²B₂ state is the ground state and the ²A₁ and ²A₂ states are higher by 0.23 and 0.39 eV, respectively, at the B3LYP/DZVP level.

For ScSi₂, only the ²A₂ state was identified and reported in our previous article [20]. By examining more low-lying electronic configurations, we do find the ²B₂ and ²A₁ states and they lie lower than the ²A₂ state by 0.31 and 0.01 eV, respectively, analogous to the pattern found for YSi₂. From these results, we conclude that it is important to examine several different electronic configurations to deter- mine the most stable one for systems containing transition elements. In general, not only the spin multiplicity of a state should be indicated but also its symmetry or electronic configuration. It is worth mentioning that this is ignored in many density functional calculations. In fact, in our previous cal- culation for ScSi₂ we examined different electronic configurations by exchanging the order of highest occupied and lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (HOMO and LUMO). This, however, is not suffi- cient, as more low-lying electronic configurations should be examined.

The three states for YSi₂ are similar with respect to charge transfer. In each case, about 0.7–0.8 elec- trons are donated from Y to the Si atoms. However, they are different in their spin distributions. As in the case of YSi, the unpaired electron of YSi₂ is

### 3.3. YSi₃

The two lowest isomers identified for YSi₃ ($^2$A$_1$ and $^2$A$_2$) both assume a planar rhombus structure with $C_{2v}$ symmetry (see Fig.1). They correspond to the substitutional structures of planar rhombus for Si₄ ($D_{2h}$, $^1$A$_g$) [35, 36]. The $^2$A$_1$ state for YSi₃ is lower than the $^2$A$_2$ one by 0.26 eV at the B3LYP/DZVP level. In our previous article [20], the $^2$A$_2$ state was reported to be the lowest structure for ScSi₃. By further examining more electronic configurations, we have now found the $^2$A$_1$ state, which lies lower than the $^2$A$_2$ one by 0.12 eV at the B3LYP/6-311+G($d$) level.

From the above results, one realizes that there exists a close correspondence between the ground-state and low-lying structures of YSi$_n$ and ScSi$_n$ ($n = 1$–3). By cross-checking the structures and electronic configurations of these two systems, we can find more possible low-lying structures of both systems. Such an analogy has proven useful as a heuristic principle in our search for possible low-lying structures of larger YSi$_n$ clusters.

The charge transfer in YSi₃ and ScSi₃ is similar. About 0.8–0.9 electrons are transferred from Y or Sc to the atoms of the Si₃ framework. The unpaired electron in both the $^2$A$_1$ and $^2$A$_2$ isomers of ScSi₃ is localized on the Sc atom. For YSi₃, the unpaired electron is localized on the Y atom in the ground-state $^2$A$_1$ isomer, but it has a considerable component on the atoms of the Si₃ framework in the $^2$A$_2$ state.

### 3.4. YSi₄

A total of five isomers were identified for YSi₄ (see Fig.1). These isomers can all be derived from the substitution of Y for an Si atom in the trigonal bipyramid of Si₅ ($D_{3h}$, $^1$A$_1$') [35, 36]. Among them, the YSi₄ ($C_s$, $^2$A') and YSi₄ ($C_{2v}$, $^2$B$_2$) isomers are the lowest in energy and nearly isoenergetic at the B3LYP/DZVP level. Both undergo considerable distortion from the starting structures. The latter isomer, YSi₄ ($C_{2v}$, $^2$B$_2$), can also be derived from the adsorption of a Y atom to the face of a planar Si₄ ($D_{2h}$, $^1$A$_g$) rhombus and, therefore, represents a distorted adsorption structure. The remaining three isomers represent typical substitutional structures, which leave the framework of Si₅ ($D_{3h}$, $^1$A$_1$') nearly unchanged with the Y atom occupying an apical ($C_{3v}$, $^2$A$_1$) or equatorial site ($C_{2v}$, $^2$B$_1$, and $^2$A$_1$).

Four ($^2$A', $^2$B$_2$, and two $^2$A$_1$) of these five states were found and reported for ScSi₄ in our previous article [20]. By examining more electronic configurations, the $^2$B$_1$ isomer of $C_{2v}$ symmetry is also identified for ScSi₄. The energy order of these states is different for YSi₄ and ScSi₄. For YSi₄, the energy order is $^2$A' ($C_s$, 0.0 eV) ≈ $^2$B$_2$ ($C_{2v}$, 0.002) < $^2$A$_1$ ($C_{3v}$, 0.16) < $^2$B$_1$ ($C_{2v}$, 0.19) < $^2$A$_1$($C_{2v}$, 0.36) at the B3LYP/DZVP level. For ScSi₄, the energy order becomes $^2$B$_2$ ($C_{2v}$, 0.0) < $^2$A' ($C_s$, 0.12) < $^2$B$_1$ ($C_{2v}$, 0.22) < $^2$A$_1$($C_{3v}$, 0.39) < $^2$A$_1$($C_{2v}$, 0.46) at the B3LYP/6-311+G($d$) level.

About one electron is transferred from Y to the atoms of the Si₄ framework in all of the five isomers of YSi₄. The spin is delocalized among the Y and Si atoms in all isomers of YSi₄, except the YSi₄ ($C_{2v}$, $^2$A$_1$) isomer, where the unpaired electron is localized on the Y atom. The charge transfer and spin distributions are similar in corresponding isomers of ScSi₄.

### 3.5. YSi₅

The ground state of Si₆⁻ is a face-capped trigonal bipyramid ($C_{2v}$, $^2$B$_2$) [36]. As seen in Figure 1, the candidate for the ground state of YSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A') assumes a substitutional structure of Si₆⁻ ($C_{2v}$, $^2$B$_2$). For ScSi₅ [20], three isomers were identified and the lowest isomer corresponds to the above structure of YSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A'). The other two isomers relaxed to this structure when Sc was replaced by Y. If we compare the structural parameters in YSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A') and ScSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A'), we find that the corresponding Si—Si bond lengths are close in the two clusters, although the Y—Si and Sc—Si bond lengths are different. For example, The 1Si—2Si, 1Si—3Si, 1Si—6Si, and 4Si—6Si distances in YSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A') are 2.57, 2.40, 2.48, and 2.46 Å, respectively, at the B3LYP/DZVP level, while the corresponding bond lengths in ScSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A') are 2.58, 2.39, 2.48, and 2.45 Å, respectively, at the same level of theory. In fact, this observation is true for all YSi$_n$ and ScSi$_n$ ($n = 2$–6) clusters in the corresponding structures. Therefore, Y and Sc atoms have a similar influence on the structure of the Si$_n$ framework.

About one electron (1.09 e) is donated to the Si₅ framework by the Y atom in YSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A'), which is slightly larger than the value (0.84 e) found for ScSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A'). Similar to the findings in ScSi₅ ($C_s$, $^2$A') and in the most stable isomers of YSi, YSi₂, and

### 3.6. $YSi_6$
The ground state of neutral $Si_7$ is a pentagonal bipyramid ($D_{5h}, ^1A_1'$) [36, 37]. The three isomers we identified for $YSi_6$ all correspond to the substitutional structures of $Si_7$ ($D_{5h}, ^1A_1'$). Two of them ($C_{2v}$, $^2A_1$ and $^2A_2$) are the substitutional structures with Y occupying the equatorial site and the third ($C_{5v}$, $^2A_1$) is a substitutional structure with Y at the apical site. At the B3LYP/DZVP level, the equatorial substitutions, $YSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_1$) and $YSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_2$), are more stable than the apical substitution, $YSi_6$ ($C_{5v}$, $^2A_1$), by 0.63 and 0.35 eV, respectively. For $ScSi_6$, two isomers ($C_{2v}, ^2A_1$ and $C_{5v}, ^2A_1$) were identified and reported in our previous article [20]. By exploring more electronic configurations, we now are able to determine the $ScSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_2$) isomer. Similar to the results of $YSi_6$ at the B3LYP/DZVP level, the most stable isomer is $ScSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_1$), while the $ScSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_2$) and $ScSi_6$ ($C_{5v}, ^2A_1$) isomers lie higher by 0.16 and 0.60 eV, respectively, at the B3LYP/6-311+G(d) level.

About one electron is transferred from Y to the atoms of the $Si_6$ framework in the two equatorial substitutional structures of $YSi_6$, while 1.26 electrons are donated to the Si atoms in the apical substitutional structure. The charge transfer in the corresponding structures of $ScSi_6$ adopts slightly smaller values. The unpaired electron in the most stable isomer of $YSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_1$) is localized on the Y atom, but it is delocalized among the Y and Si atoms in the other two isomers. Similar spin distributions were observed in corresponding structures of $ScSi_6$.

From the above description, a close similarity exists between $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ clusters in their geometric structures, charge transfer characteristics, and spin distributions. In the following we will show that these two systems are also similar in their energetic and bonding properties.

The stability for the most stable isomers of $YSi_n$ was understood by analyzing the size dependence of the binding energies per atom with respect to isolated atoms and of the fragmentation energies (FEs) with respect to the processes $YSi_n \rightarrow Y + Si_n$ or $Si + YSi_{n-1}$.

Figure 2 shows the size dependence of the binding energies per atom (BEs) for the most stable isomers of $YSi_n$. For comparison, the BE curve for $ScSi_n$ clusters at the B3LYP/DZVP level is also shown. A similar size dependence appears in the BEs of $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$, both of which show slightly enhanced stabilities at $n = 2$ and $n = 5$. For all cluster sizes, the BE for $YSi_n$ is slightly larger than that of $ScSi_n$ at the B3LYP/DZVP level. Because the $Si_n$ frameworks in $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ are close in the Si—Si distances, this implies that the Y—Si interaction is slightly stronger than the Sc—Si bond.

Figures 3 and 4 present the size dependence of the fragmentation energies of $YSi_n$ with respect to either $Y + Si_n$ (FE1) or $Si + YSi_{n-1}$ (FE2). For comparison, the FE curves for $YSi_n$ obtained at the B3LYP/DZVP level are also included. Again, a similar size dependence is observed between the FEs of $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ for both fragmentation channels. With respect to the fragmentation into both $Y + Si_n$ and $Si + YSi_{n-1}$, enhanced stability appears at $YSi_2$ and $YSi_5$, in agreement with the BE profile. In addition, $YSi_3$ shows noticeably enhanced stability in both the BE and FE1 curves. We thus predict that $YSi_2$, $YSi_5$, and perhaps $YSi_3$ could be generated with high abundance in the mass spectrum. Obviously, this conclusion is also true for $ScSi_n$.

Finally, we briefly analyze the bonding features in $YSi_n$ clusters based on the densities of states (DOS) of the clusters. The total DOS for a cluster is obtained by a Lorentzian broadening of the discrete energy levels of the cluster and a summation over them, while the partial DOS are obtained by decomposing the total DOS into contributions of various atomic orbitals, applying Mulliken population analysis. The broadening width is chosen to be 0.10 eV.

Figure 5 shows the total and partial DOS for the representative $YSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_1$) cluster. One finds that the Y $d$ orbital contributes significantly to the four highest occupied peaks of the total DOS, spreading over a range from $-0.8$ to $-3.3$ eV below the Fermi level, and that there is strong hybridization between Y $d$ and Si $s$ and $p$ orbitals. Therefore, a mixed ionic and covalent bonding picture between Y and the Si atoms emerges in $YSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_1$). Similar total and partial DOSs were reported for $ScSi_6$ ($C_{2v}, ^2A_1$) in our previous article [20]. Recently, evidence for the strong hybridization between the $d$ orbitals of doped TM atoms and the cage orbitals was reported experimentally [23] for $La@C_{82}$, and demonstrated by theoretical calculations [24, 25] for $TM@C_{60}$ and $TM@C_{82}$ with TM = La, Y, and Sc, which is in contrast to the pure charge transfer model for TM = La and Y and the $d$-electron localization model for TM = Sc that were proposed previously for these systems [21, 22].

### 4. Conclusions

In this contribution, the interaction of a Y atom with small $Si_n$ ($n = 1$-$6$) clusters was studied by means of a hybrid density functional technique (B3LYP) in combination with two basis sets: an all-electron basis set (DZVP) and an effective core potential basis set (SDDAll). Both basis sets generate results that agree qualitatively, but the effective core potential basis set tends to overestimate the bond lengths and underestimate the charge transfer, binding, and fragmentation energies as compared with the all-electron basis set. Close similarity is observed between the $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ clusters in their ground-state and low-lying structures, charge transfer, spin distribution, energetic properties, and bonding features.

The ground states for YSi and ScSi dimers are both spin quartets, while the ground states for all larger $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ ($n = 2$-$6$) clusters correspond to spin doublets. It is found important to examine several different electronic configurations of a given spin multiplicity to determine the most stable one for systems containing transition elements.

The structures for the most stable isomers of $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ ($n = 2, 3, 5, 6$) can be classified as substitutional structures, where the frameworks of $Si_{n + 1}$ or $Si_{n + 1}^-$ are maintained nearly unchanged with Y or Sc occupying various substitutional sites. For $YSi_4$ and $ScSi_4$, two isoenergetic structures are proposed as the ground-state candidates, one of which adopts an adsorption structure of $Si_4$. Therefore, $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ clusters reveal similar growth patterns and their most stable structures adopt various substitutional and adsorption structures of $Si_n$ and $Si_{n + 1}$.

The charge transfer in all $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ clusters was found to proceed from Y or Sc to the $Si_n$ framework and is slightly stronger in $YSi_n$ than in $ScSi_n$.

The bonding properties in the most stable isomers of $YSi_n$ were analyzed by examining the total and partial DOSs. The total and partial DOSs of $YSi_n$ are similar to those of the corresponding $ScSi_n$ clusters, indicating similar bonding features in the two systems. Strong hybridization is found between the $d$ orbital of Y or Sc and the $s$ and $p$ orbitals of Si atoms. Therefore, a mixed ionic and covalent bonding picture between Y or Sc and the Si atoms is established in $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$. This bonding picture is consistent with recent experimental and theoretical findings concerning the existence of strong hybridization between TM $d$ and cage orbitals in TM@$C_{60}$ and TM@$C_{82}$ (TM = La, Y, Sc) [23-25]. In a recent molecular dynamics simulation [27] on the amorphous alloy, $Y_xSi_{1 - x}$, the Y atoms are found to donate all of their valence electrons to the Si sublattice, which is in contrast to the mixed ionic and covalent bonding picture for $YSi_n$ clusters, as emerging from this work.

The unpaired electron in $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ was found to be either localized on the Y (Sc) atom or shared by Y (Sc) and the $Si_n$ framework in Y(Sc)$Si_n$ clusters. This is in agreement with the strong Y—Si and Sc—Si hybridization in $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$.

A similar size dependence is observed for $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ in their binding energies per atom and the fragmentation energies with respect to M + $Si_n$ or Si + $MSi_{n - 1}$ (M =Y, Sc). All of these curves indicate that there is enhanced stability at $MSi_2$, $MSi_5$, and possibly $MSi_3$. Therefore, we expect that these clusters could be observable with high abundance in mass spectroscopy experiments.

Future efforts will be directed at an extension of our studies to larger $YSi_n$ and $ScSi_n$ clusters. Also, it is planned to examine the critical size at which the growth patterns of these two cluster groups start to diverge, as well as the minimal cluster sizes at which cage-like $Si_n$ structures with encapsulated Y or Sc metal atoms emerge as ground states.
