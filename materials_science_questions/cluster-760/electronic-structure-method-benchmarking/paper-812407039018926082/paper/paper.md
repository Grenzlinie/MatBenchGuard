# Theoretical and experimental studies of the vibrational spectra of m-methylaniline

A. Altun*, K. Gölcük, M. Kumru

Department of Physics, Faculty of Arts and Sciences, Fatih University, 34900, B. Çekmece, İstanbul, Turkey

Received 25 July 2002; revised 3 October 2002; accepted 10 October 2002

## Abstract

The FT-IR and FT-Raman spectra of m-methylaniline (mMA) have been recorded. Optimized molecular structures and normal vibrations of mMA have been obtained from the ab initio-HF and the DFT-B3LYP levels. The 6-31G* basis set has been used for each level of calculations. Correction factors, which bring computational frequencies in closer agreement with the experimental data, have been calculated for predominant vibrational motions of the normal modes at each level of theory. All IR and Raman bands of mMA have been assigned in the frameworks of the calculations. The assignments have been compared each other and with the 30 benzene-like modes. The DFT-B3LYP/6-31G* calculations have been found more reliable than the ab initio-MP2/6-31G* calculations for the vibrational study of mMA.

## 1. Introduction

Aniline and its derivatives have been widely used as starting materials in a vast amount of pharmaceu- tical, electro-optical and many other industrial processes. Hence, understanding of their molecular properties as well as the natures of reaction mechan- isms they undergo has great importance. That is why investigations on the structures and the vibrations of aniline and substituted anilines are still being carried out, increasingly [1-15].

The inclusion of a substituent group in aniline leads to the variation of charge distribution in the molecule, and consequently this greatly affects the structural, electronic and vibrational parameters [14]. The methyl and amino groups are generally referred as electron donating substituents in aromatic ring systems [1,5]. The $CH_{3}$ interacts with nearby $\pi$ systems via hyperconjugation, while the $NH_{2}$ shares its lone pair electrons with the p electrons in a ring. Both mechanisms imply electronic delocalization and are taken into account by the molecular orbital approach [10,13].

Although ab initio molecular orbital calculations involving Hartree-Fock (HF), Møller-Plesset second order perturbation theory (MP2), the complete active space multiconfiguration self-consistent field (CASSCF) and the configuration interaction singles (CIS) methods tend to overestimate the vibrational frequencies with respect to the experimental ones,

This overestimation is due to several reasons, for instance, the use of finite basis set, the incomplete implemen- tation of the electronic correlation and the neglect of anharmonicity effects in the theoretical treatment [7].

Recently, it has been shown [14] that density functional theory (DFT) calculations with the hybrid exchange-correlation functionals B3LYP [16,17], B3P86 [17,18] and B3PW91 [17,19,20] show better agreement with the experimental values of structural characteristics of aniline and its derivatives than the calculations containing the gradient corrected exchange functional (B88) [21]. DFT calculations with the B3LYP functional have been also found very effective for vibrational studies on aniline [22] and 4- fluoraniline [7].

In spite of extensive recent studies on vibrational spectra of aniline and a number of substituted anilines [7-15,22], complete vibrational mode and frequency analyses of m-methylaniline (mMA, also known as 3-methylaniline, m-toluidine and 3-aminotoluene) have not been hold in contrast to its importance on the field of especially coordination- and photo-chemistry. We report our comparative ab initio and DFT calculation results in this study.

## 2. Experimental

mMA was used as received from Aldrich Chemical Co. with $99\%$ purity. The $4000-400\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ region IR spectrum of the mMA, being liquid at room temperature, was recorded between CsI windows with Perkin Elmer Paragon 1000 FT-IR spectrometer calibrated using polystyrene film.

Raman spectrum of the sample was recorded on a Bruker RFS 100/S FT-Raman Spectrometer in the range $3600-70\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. It uses a 1.5 W Nd-YaG air- cooled laser delivering excitation wavelength of 1064 nm. The detector is a liquid nitrogen cooled Ge detector.

FT-IR (between CsI windows) and FT-Raman spectra of mMA, which are recorded at room temperature, are given in Figs. 1 and 2, respectively. To the best of our knowledge, the Raman spectrum of mMA has not been reported so far. But, the IR spectrum of mMA with some of its metal-halide complexes was studied partially [23].

## 4. Results and discussion

The conformation of mMA is related to different rotamers with respect to torsion around $C-CH_{3}$ bond.

Since the energy barrier among such rotamers is usually small, different rotamers may exist in equilibrium and may be calculated as the lowest energy conformation with the change of compu- tational method or the basis set used [12].

The optimized structures for staggered, eclipsed and gauched conformers of mMA have been computed at ab initio-HF/6-31G* and DFT- B3LYP/6-31G* levels. Ab initio-HF/6-31G* level calculations show that the gauched form $(-324.7682387$ a.u.) has less energy than the eclipsed $(-324.7682331$ a.u.) and the staggered $(-324.7682381$ a.u.) forms while DFT-B3LYP/6-31G* level calculations are resulted that the staggered form $(-326.7060656$ a.u.) is lower in energy than the eclipsed $(-326.7060643$ a.u.) and the gauched $(-326.7060652$ a.u.) forms. All vibrational frequencies have been calculated positive for the lowest energy configuration of each type of theory. Each of the remaining conformers has an imaginary frequency for the $CH_{3}$ torsion mode. This means that the optimized structures of these rotamers are unstabilized with the torsional vibration around $C-CH_{3}$ bond in the frameworks of the methods.

It is known that most of the vibrational modes in monosubstituted and disubstituted benzenes ($\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{X}$ and $\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_4\mathrm{XY}$) are quite similar to those of benzene. This general rule also works very well in the normal mode analysis of mMA. In order to confirm the assignments, the 30 benzene-like modes of mMA among 45 normal vibrations are shown in Table 2 with the convention used by Varsanyi and Szoke for benzene derivatives [28,29].

We reassigned some ring vibrations compared to Varsanyi's compilation as a result of the detailed vector displacement analyses. The frequency of the in-plane ring $\mathrm{C-H}$ bending (mode 3) is found to be higher than that of the Kekulé-type ring distortion (mode 14). Similarly, out-of-plane ring deformation mode 16b is found at the higher energy side than mode 16a. Also, the frequency of the $\mathrm{C-H}$ stretching (mode 20b) is higher than that of mode 13. In addition to these, mode 12 (in-plane deformation) appears to be higher in energy than breathing (mode 1). All of these trends are also seen in the HF level ab initio studies of aniline [9,13], $p$-diaminobenzene [30], $p$-methylaniline [12] and $o$-methylaniline [10]. However, these trends are not obtained with the semiempirical calculations [9,22]. Hence, the reassignments made in this study are due mainly to the methods used but not to amino or methyl substitution.

In Ref. [28], it is expressed that the frequencies of the amino group appear around $3500-3300\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for the $\mathrm{NH}_2$ stretching, $1630-1610\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for the scissoring deformation, and $1090-1060\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for rocking deformation. The previous $^{15}\mathrm{N}$-labelling IR study [23] shows that the bands observed at 3435, 3354, 1622, and $1077\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ are N-sensitive. Hence, these N-sensitive bands are due to $\mathrm{NH}_2$ symmetric stretching, $\mathrm{NH}_2$ antisymmetric stretching, $\mathrm{NH}_2$ scissoring, and $\mathrm{NH}_2$ rocking, respectively. The band observed at $1293\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, being also N-sensitive [23], is due to $\mathrm{C-N}$ stretching. Mode 15 (in-plane $\mathrm{C-NH}_2$ deformation), which couples strongly with the in-plane $\mathrm{C-CH}_3$ deformation, downshifts from $390\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in aniline [13] to $294\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in mMA. The frequency of this mode in $p$-methylaniline [12] and $o$-methylaniline [10] is predicted to be $290\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $303\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, respectively. The frequencies of out-of-plane $\mathrm{C-NH}_2$ deformation (mode 10b) and amino group twisting vibrations in aniline [22] are nearly the same as those observed in mMA. Hence, these two modes are not affected by the methyl substitution.

Several ring modes are affected by the methyl substitution to the aromatic ring of aniline. For example, the ring modes labelled 18a, 12, 17b, 10a, 1 and 6b downshift, respectively, from 1028, 957, 874, 825, 812 and $619\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in aniline [13] to 996, 926, 855, 775, 738 and $538\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in mMA. The changes in frequencies of these modes compared to aniline are due to the changes in the (force constant/reduced mass) ratio resulting mainly from addition of methyl group to aniline and from different extents of mixing between the ring and substituent groups vibrations. Hence, the frequency reductions with methyl substitution are seen both in the pure ring modes (18a, 17b and 10a) and in the modes showing mixed motions of the ring and substituent vibrations (12, 1 and 6b).

The frequency of mode 9b (in-plane $\mathrm{C-CH}_3$ deformation) in mMA is expected around $420\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ while it is observed and calculated for toluene [31] around $350\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. The frequency of this mode in $p$-methylaniline [12] and $o$-methylaniline [10] is predicted at 399 and $412\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, respectively. These upward shifts in the frequency of mode 9b with the amino substitution can be assigned to the changes in force constant and reduced mass resulting mainly from the strong interaction with the in-plane $\mathrm{C-NH}_2$ deformation. The in-plane and out-of-plane methyl deformations are not affected by the amino substitution.

Ab initio calculations at the handled level have shown some weakness for predicting the correct ordering of some neighbouring modes. As a result of previous $^{15}\mathrm{N}$-labelling IR study, we have concluded above that the higher frequency band in $1080-1030\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ region is $\mathrm{NH}_2$ rocking vibration. Hence, the lower frequency one is out-of-plane methyl deformation. Our DFT results are in agreement with this experimental fact whereas the ab initio calculations could not predict this tendency.

The in-plane ring deformation band at $996\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (mode 18a) is the characteristic of disubstituted benzenes in *meta* position. Ab initio calculations

resulted for this band and the neighbouring out-of-
plane C-H deformation band at $965\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (mode
17a) in reverse order while DFT calculations resulted
in the correct estimation.

C-N stretching observed at $1293\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (mode
20a) and the neighbouring C-C stretching vibration
(mode 14) are also found in reverse order in the
frameworks of the calculations. Although mode 14 is
observed in neither IR nor Raman spectrum, we
suggest that DFT results are again the correct ones. Ab
initio studies on the vibrations of $o$-methylaniline [10]
and $p$-methylaniline [12] have shown the same
reversal.

In low frequency region, some second and/or
higher order contributions of the modes related to
methyl and amino group vibrations appear to be
different in the calculations (Table 2). Due to the
absence of symmetry, one cannot be sure about the
types of these contributions being small compared
with the main vibration of the mode in any level of
calculation.

All frequencies obtained by the ab initio calcu-
lations are overestimated whereas the DFT frequen-
cies are overestimated with respect to the
experimental ones in the region between 3500 and
$1000\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and are accurate enough in the range
$1300-100\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. The overestimations are due to
vibrational anharmonicity, basis set incompleteness
and electron correlation lack or incompleteness, etc.

Correction factors for the different types of
vibrations given in Table 3 have been calculated
for each level of calculations to bring the computed
and experimental frequencies into better agreement.
Each type of molecular motions (stretching and
deformation) is divided into two sets, one of which
is for C attached to C or to N, and the other is for
C or N attached to H, in this study. The exception
to this classification, as seen from Table 3, is the
out-of-plane C-H deformation coupled strongly
with the out-of-plane ring deformation.

The correction factors are obtained simply as the
average of the ratio between the experimental and
computed frequencies for each class. The rationale
and an explanation of this approach were discussed
previously [31-38]. Correction factors have been
developed for the predominant vibrational motion
in each normal mode. The factors computed in this
study are similar to the ones derived previously
[31-38] for each vibration types of HF and DFT-
B3LYP frequencies.

DFT-B3LYP correction factors are all much closer
to unity than the HF correction factors, which means
that the DFT-B3LYP frequencies are much closer to
the experimental values than the HF frequencies.

### 5. Conclusions

Optimized molecular structures and vibrational
characteristics of mMA have been obtained from
ab initio and DFT calculations. For the first time,
all observed IR and Raman bands of mMA are
assigned with the comparison of the theoretical and
previous $^{15}$N-labelling study results. The theoretical
results are also compared with the benzene modes.
The effects of substituents (amino and methyl
groups) on vibrational frequencies are discussed.

Although both types of calculations are useful to
explain vibrational spectra of mMA, ab initio
calculations at HF/6-31G* level is found poorer
than DFT-B3LYP/6-31G* level calculations. In the
light of this study, DFT-B3LYP/6-31G* level
calculation results can be used reliably for predict-
ing vibrational properties of medium sized systems
even they have lone pair electrons.

The results of this study can also be used for
identification of ligand (mMA) vibrations of transition
metal complexes with the exception of amino modes
shoving substantial shifts on coordination. We are
going to discuss structural and vibrational properties
of some transition metal complexes of mMA with the
aid of this study, later.
