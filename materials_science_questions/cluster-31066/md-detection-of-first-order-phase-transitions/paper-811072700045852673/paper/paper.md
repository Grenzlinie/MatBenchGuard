# PHASE TRANSITIONS IN TRIANGULAR LATTICE GASES WITH HARD CORES

J. ORBAN* and A. BELLEMANS
University of Brussels. Belgium

Received 3 July 1967

The existence of phase transitions in triangular lattice gases of hard molecules with second- and third-neighbour exclusions is investigated by the matrix method.

Solid-fluid transitions in lattice gases of hard molecules have been investigated by several authors. Most efforts were directed to first-neighbour exclusions in two- and three-dimensional lattices and there is now a general agreement on the fact that these systems exhibit continuous second-order phase transitions at relatively high densities (horizontal inflexion point in the pressure versus density curve) [1,2,6,7]. More distant exclusions on the square lattice were also studied: second-neighbour exclusions lead to a third-order transition [3,5], while good evidence for a first-order phase change has been reported for third-neighbour exclusions [3,4]. Preliminary calculations on the next two cases (fourth- and fifth-neighbour exclusions) carried out by the matrix method, indicate second- and third-order transitions respectively. These striking irregularities probably result from the residual degrees of freedom existing in the close-packed configurations corresponding to second-, fourth- and fifth-neighbour exclusions. The aim of this paper is to present some results obtained by the matrix method for the triangular lattice gas with second- and third-neighbour exclusions. The close-packed configurations of these systems are perfectly well defined so that the order of their transitions is not expected to vary in an obscure way; they should therefore approach the behaviour of a system of hard disks in a more straightforward manner.

As the application of the matrix method to the evaluation of the thermodynamic properties of semi-infinite periodic lattices bent into a torus of circumference $n$ has been fully described [4], we here limit ourselves to present the results.

Fig. 1 shows the exclusion cores and the close-packed structures of triangular lattice gases with first-, second- and third-neighbour exclusions (cases A, B and C below); for practical purpose the triangular lattice is twisted into a quadratic one. In order to approach the close-packed configuration at high density, $n$ ought to be a multiple of 3.2 and 7 for cases A, B and C respectively; their maximum densities $\rho_O$ are respectively $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{7}$.
