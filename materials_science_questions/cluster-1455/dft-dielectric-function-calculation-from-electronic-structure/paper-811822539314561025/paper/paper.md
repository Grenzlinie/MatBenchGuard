## A B S T R A C T

The high-pressure and high-temperature spinel phases $(\gamma)$ of the $Si_{3}N_{4}$ and $Ge_{3}N_{4}$ have attracted great attention due to the wide, direct electronic band gaps. Their derivatives, spinel $SiGe_{2}N_{4}$, have also raised notice because of its stability and interesting properties. However we noticed that the optical properties of this group are not satisfied comparing to those of well-known semiconductor. The main purpose of present study is to find the possibility of improving the optical properties of IVA group spinel nitrides by first-principles calculation combining the random phase approximation (RPA). The focus of present work is put on raising the peak value of the imaginary part of dielectric function($\varepsilon_{2}$) by site substitution as the simulation of doping. We proposed a method to select doping elements based on detailed analysis on $\varepsilon_{2}$ to identify the largest interband transitions. Various doping effects by different dopants at different doping sites in different doping concentrations are investigated, by taking $\gamma$-$SiGe_{2}N_{4}$ as the example, and the trend of re-distribution of energy band by different dopants is revealed. Calculations show that for $\gamma$-$SiGe_{2}N_{4}$, substituting Si in tetrahedrally coordinated cation sites in spinel structure with Ga and As significantly raises the peak value of $\varepsilon_{2}$, which proposed an effective approach to improve optical property of IVA group nitrides.

### 1. Introduction

The nitrides formed from nitrogen and elements in Group-IV and Group-V are an interesting and important class of materials. Silicon nitride ($Si_{3}N_{4}$) is a well-known material because of its high fracture toughness, hardness and wear resistance. [1,2] Another well-researched polymorph is $Ge_{3}N_{4}$. The existence of several solid phases and the phase transitions among them have been attracted interesting for years. $\alpha$ (P31c, hp28), $\beta$ (P6${_3}$/m, hp14) and $\gamma$ (spinel, Fd$\overline{3}$m, cF56) phases of $Si_{3}N_{4}$ and $Ge_{3}N_{4}$ are well studied by experiments [3–7] and theoretical calculations. [8] The forth phase, olivine phase, has also been reported and investigated by *ab initio* calculation. [8] It has been shown that the $\gamma$-$Si_{3}N_{4}$ and $\gamma$-$Ge_{3}N_{4}$ have wide, direct electronic band gaps that are comparable to those of the newly developed solid state promising optoelectronic materials such as GaN, InN, AlN. [9]$\gamma$-$Sn_{3}N_{4}$ was synthesized both by a rapid solid metathesim reaction [10] and from an annealed tin amidimide precursor at ambient pressure. [11]

Encouraged by the above-described information, the stabilities of the spinel nitrides of IVA and IVB group have been systematically investigated with the orthogonalized linear combination of atomic orbitals (OLCAO) method [12–15] and full potential lin-earized augmented plane wave. [16] Their results raise a particular attention on $\gamma$-$SiGe_{2}N_{4}$ due to its interesting properties and potential technological application. It has a negative formation energy with a direct band gap of 1.85 eV. [15] Based on these researches, we further investigated the optical property of $\gamma$-$SiGe_{2}N_{4}$. [16] It is well known that several optical properties such as the real part of the optical conductivity and the loss function depend closely on the imaginary part of the dielectric function, $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$, we specially pay attention on $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$. It is found that $\gamma$-$SiGe_{2}N_{4}$ has a rather low peak of $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ which can not compare with some typical semiconductors such as Si. In this research, the electronic properties and optical properties of $\gamma$-$SiGe_{2}N_{4}$ are investigated in detail as an example with aiming at raising the optical property of spinel nitrides. To improve the static dielectric constant, we propose a method in which the band transition with the maximum contribution to the peak of $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ is selected as a key factor. Then doping in both tetrahedrally coordinated cation sites and octahedrally coordinated cation sites of the spinel structure is performed and analyzed. Furthermore, the effects of different dopants in tetrahedrally coordinated sites are presented and discussed.

The calculation method and parameters are described in the next section, followed by results and discussion in Section 3. Conclusion is contained in Section 4.

## 2. Calculation method and parameters

In order to investigate the structural and electronic properties of spinel nitrides, we perform calculations based on the density functional theory (DFT) and local density approximation (LDA) with generalized gradient approximation (GGA). [17,18] The FLAPW method using a scalar relativistic effect as embodied in the WIEN2k code is employed. [19] The linearized augmented plane waves (LAPW) and augmented plane wave with local orbitals (APW+lo) are applied as basis because the highest efficiency is found for a mixed basis set. The PBE-GGA is employed for the exchange and correlation potential. [18] The plane wave cut-offs of calculations are all above 25 Ry. The random phase approximation (RPA) is used to calculate the optical properties. [20,21] Broadening is taken to be 0.2 eV without energy shift.

The dielectric function, $\varepsilon(\omega)$ is used to describe the linear response of the system to an electromagnetic radiation, which is related to the interaction of photons with electrons. There are two contributions to $\varepsilon(\omega)$, namely, intraband and interband transitions. The intraband transition is important only for metals. The interband transitions can further be split into direct and indirect transitions. Here we neglect intraband transition for possible semiconductor-metal transitions for the sake of comparison with the undoped semiconductor and ignore the indirect interband transitions which involve scattering of phonons that are expected to give only a small contribution to $\varepsilon(\omega)$. [22]

The imaginary part of the dielectric tensor component $\varepsilon_{2}$ can be estimated by

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)=\frac{V e^{2}}{2 \pi \hbar m^{2} \omega^{2}} \sum_{n, n^{\prime}} \int_{\mathbf{k}}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{k} n|\mathbf{p}| \mathbf{k} n^{\prime}\right\rangle\right|^{2} f(\mathbf{k} n)\left[1-f\left(\mathbf{k} n^{\prime}\right)\right] \delta\left(E_{\mathbf{k} n}-E_{\mathbf{k} n^{\prime}}-\hbar \omega\right) \quad(1)
$$

According to Kramers-Krönig relation, [23,24], the corresponding real part is obtained by

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\pi} \wp \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega^{\prime} \varepsilon_{2}\left(\omega^{\prime}\right)}{\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}} \mathrm{~d} \omega^{\prime} \quad(2)
$$

where $\wp$ is integration principle value

$$
\wp \int_{0}^{\infty} \equiv \lim _{\Delta \rightarrow 0}\left(\int_{0}^{\omega-\Delta}+\int_{\omega+\Delta}^{\infty}\right) \quad(3)
$$

The evaluation of the matrix elements of the momentum operator in Eq. (1) is performed over the muffin-tin and the interstitial regions separately.

From Eq. (2), we can see that $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ is dependent on $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ over an infinite frequency range. In addition, when $\omega=0, \varepsilon_{2}$ has a definite contribution to the static dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{1}(0)$. Since various optical properties can be estimated from $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$, the focus of present study is put on how to raise the peak value of $\varepsilon_{2}$ by doping.

According to Eq. (1), the peak value of $\varepsilon_2$, 13.056 is located at a photon energy of 6.653 eV to which many band transitions correspond. As discussed in Section 2, we identify the band transition which contributes maximally to the peak of $\varepsilon_2$. It is found that the contribution comes from the transition from band No. 58 to No. 66 at k-point (0.10209, 0,0) takes the responsibility, which is shown by a black arrow In Fig. 1. Due to the largest contribution to the $\varepsilon_2$ peak, the detailed components of atomic states in band No. 58 and No. 66 are further analyzed. It is obtained that the band No. 58 is mainly composed of N-p states, and the band No. 66 is hybrid with N-p, Si (tet)-p and Ge (oct)-p in a slightly decreasing order of their contribution. Since the transitions between the same N-p states are not permitted due to the Hund's Rule, the interband transitions which contribute maximally to the peak of $\varepsilon_2$ are N-p $\rightarrow$ Si (tet)-p and N-p $\rightarrow$ Ge (oct)-p. This fact leads to an assumption that adjusting the number of p-electrons of cations at Si (tet)-site or Ge (oct)-site induces the re-distribution of the energy band, especially the bottom of the conduction band and the top of the valence band, which in turn changes the energy difference and the number of states of the interband transitions, then results in an alteration in the value of the $\varepsilon_2$ peak. Along this line, we perform site substitution on Si (tet)-site or Ge (oct)-site with group elements from IIIA to VIA as a simulation of doping for the sake of increasing the peak value of the $\varepsilon_2$.

### 3.2. Model systems

An ideal spinel unit cell of $\gamma$-SiGe$_2$N$_4$ is built with 56 atoms, A$_8$B$_{16}$N$_{32}$. In this unit cell, there are 4 nonequivalent Si (tet) sites with a multiplicity of 2, and 4 Ge (oct) sites with a multiplicity of 4. Doping effect is investigated from the following three aspects:
1. dopant.
2. doping site.
3. doping concentration (substitution rate).

Doping on the ideal spinel structure is simulated by substitution of element surrounding Si and Ge in the periodic table, in detail As, P, Se as $n$-type impurity and Ga, B, Al as $p$-type impurity for both Si (tet) site and Ge (oct) site with various concentrations as shown in Table 1. The dopants considered to substitute for Si or Ge are typed in bold. Various supercells are built as following:
- Dopant: D = B, Al, P, Ga, As, Se.
- Substitution of Si (tet) site: Si(Ge$_{1-x}$D$_x$)Ge$_2$N$_4$ ($x = 0, 0.5$).
- Substitution of Ge (oct) site: Si(Ge$_{1-y}$D$_y$)$_2$N$_4$ ($y = 0, 0.25, 0.5$).

In order to investigate the site dependence of $\varepsilon_2$ peak, the same concentration should be kept. Therefore $y = 0.25$ in Ge (oct) sites substitution Si(Ge$_y$D$_{1-y}$)$_2$N$_4$ corresponds to $x = 0.5$ in Si (tet) sites substitution (Si$_{1-x}$D$_x$)Ge$_2$N$_4$ due to the double atoms of Ge relative to those of Si in a unit cell.

### 3.3. As and Ga substitution

Since the impurity atoms in Ge (oct) introduce anisotropy into the ideal cubic spinel cell, the dielectric function of Ge (oct) site substitution becomes anisotropic along $x$-, $y$- and $z$-directions. We focus on the direction with the highest value of peak of $\varepsilon_2$ in all cases. The concentration dependence of peak values of $\varepsilon_2$ with doping As and Ga are presented in Fig. 3. The letters in parenthesis below data points correspond to the sub-figure number of the $\varepsilon_2$ as the function of incident energy in Fig. 4 and the energy bands in Fig. 5 respectively. It is observed that substitutions of As and Ga for Si (tet) induce a higher $\varepsilon_2$ peaks, whereas substitutions of As or Ga for Ge (oct) only change the peaks slightly.

This indicates that the substitutions in Si (tet) site benefit raising $\varepsilon_2$ peaks value. We found that doping Ga into Si (tet) improves $\varepsilon_2$ peak values remarkably from undoped 13.065 to 35.898. Moreover, it is found that increasing concentration of $n$-type impurity element As in Ge (oct) sites raises the $\varepsilon_2$ peak, whereas increasing $p$-type impurity element Ga in Ge (oct) sites has an opposite effect.

All of these impacts on $\varepsilon_2$ peak by site substitution can be explained by the changes in band structures. Observing the bands of six cases of Ga and As substitution, we notice that $n$-type dopant As mainly induces a change in the bottom of conduction band, and the $p$-type dopant Ga varies the top of valence band.

However the energy positions of $\varepsilon_2$ peaks in both situations are shift to higher energies comparing to the one in undoped system because the energy span between the transition bands with the most contribution expands.

According to Table 2, the substitution of As for Si (tet) causes the average of $\varepsilon_1$ (0) to increase remarkably with As concentration increasing. Whereas it is contrary to the case of substitution of Ga for Ge (oct) that $\varepsilon_1$ (0) decreases slightly with Ga concentrating. It is expected that the trends of $\varepsilon_1$ (0) are similar to those of the $\varepsilon_2$ peak of As and Ga substitution in both sites respectively.

The dielectric constant is directly related to the polarizability of the crystal. The polarizability, which represents the deformability of the electronic distribution, is to be connected with the distribution of the valence charge density. The bond lengths in As and Ga substitutions for Ge (oct) and Si (tet) are summarized in Table 3. With longer bonds comparing with the undoped system, $\varepsilon_1$(0) of As substitution for Ge (oct) with 0.25 concentration, which has still a regular octahedron, increases comparing to the one of the undoped system. Whereas the supercell with 0.5 concentration is no longer a regular octahedron and deforms remarkably. Moreover the bond lengths in 0.5 concentration is longer than ones in 0.25 concentration. Those bond length changes result in varying in band structure near the bottom of the conduction band which is mostly composed of Ge-p states.

Ga substitution for Ge (oct) is similar to that of As substitution except that in the 0.5 concentration the bond lengths are shorter than one in the 0.25 concentration. Such difference of deformation in 0.5 substitution concentration can be taken as the reason that in As substitution for Si (tet), $\varepsilon_1$ (0) in 0.5 concentration is higher than that in 0.25 concentration, whereas in opposite in Ga substitution.

For the substitution in tetrahedral Si sites, although the less coordination number of Si (tet), the bond length changes in a larger percentage relative to the substitution in octahedral sites. Therefore the shape of the valence charge density changes obviously and results in a significant improvement in dielectric constants.

The more important effect of tetrahedral sites than octahedral sites in substitution can also be verified by the PDOS. According to analyzing element structure, band No. 58 is mostly composed of N-p states. However excluding N-p states which do not contribute to the band transition, band No. 66 is mostly dominated by octahedrally coordinated cations Ge-4p states in terms of total PDOS of a unit cell. Whereas tetrahedrally coordinated cations Si-3p states contribute mostly to the band if taking PDOS/atom into account. The PDOS and PDOS/atom of three elements in $\gamma$-SiGe₂N₄ are displayed in Fig. 6, in which the black solid line stands for Si, red dashed line for Ge, green dotted line for N respectively. The blue solid line is for guidance to the energy of band No. 66 in k-point (0.10209, 0,0), the inset shows the details of peaks of PDOS and PDOS/atom of three elements corresponding to the shaded region respectively. In Fig. 6(a), the peaks of three PDOS decrease in order of N, Ge and Si. Nevertheless in Fig. 6(b) they decrease in order of Si, N and Ge. Since the substitution of Si (tet) site produces the highest $\varepsilon_2$ peak, it can be concluded that the PDOS/atom might be a more sensitive factor of controlling the peak of $\varepsilon_2$.

### 3.4. Other dopants in Si (tet) sites

Since doping in Si(tet) site influences more efficiently the peak of $\varepsilon_{2}$, we further investigate the Al, B, P and Se substitution for Si (tet) with 0.5 concentration. The $\varepsilon_{2}$ and band structures of four supercells are presented in Figs. 7 and 8 respectively. $\varepsilon$ of Al, B, P and Se substitution for Si (tet) are summarized in Table 4. Similar trend as manifested in As and Ga doping is observed, that is, doping IIIA group elements mainly raise changes on the top of valence band, whereas doping VA and VIA group elements induce changes at the bottom of conduction band mainly.

The band structures of Al and B substitution show still a semiconductor character with a wide direct band gap. Impurity substitutions have not changed the 3-degenerated band structure at the bottom of conduction band. For $\varepsilon_{2}$ of them, no explicit difference appears due to little change of the band structures.

In case of P dopant, the added electrons occupy the bottom of conduction band. The $\varepsilon_{2}$ peaks near zero energy are apparently due to the band transition near the Fermi energy. The second highest peak is almost the same to the one of the undoped system. For Se substitution, conduction bands change remarkably, thus $\varepsilon_{2}$ has a different shape from the one in the undoped situation.

Based on above results, we can observe the tendency of doping effect by different elements from following aspects:

- substitute with elements in same period as Si or Ge,
- substitute with elements in same group,
- substitute with elements in next period to Si.

This is the same case for these elements in Ge (oct) site with 0.5 concentration. The substitutions of Al and P in Si (tet) site with 0.5 concentration show the same tendency. It can be concluded that for substitution with elements in the same period, when the valence electron number increases, the $\varepsilon_{2}$ peak increases.

We investigate dopants in IIIA group, it is found that peak of $\varepsilon_{2}$ increases with the valence electron number increasing. The substitution of Ga for Si (tet) obtains a significant improvement in the $\varepsilon_{2}$ peak than substitutions of B and Al. It is remarkable that the supercells with substitution of B and Al in Si(tet) site are still semiconductors whereas the supercell with Ga substitution turns to be metallic.

In case of substitution of next period elements, Ga and As in Si(tet) site with 0.5 concentration, it can be seen that both Ga and As substitution raise the $\varepsilon_{2}$ peak and Ga induces a higher value. This gives a hint that substitution of element with one less electron in the next period is more effective than that with one more electron during doping atoms in $\gamma$-SiGe₂N₄.

Connected with the discussion in Section C, we can obtain that among the investigated dopants, substitution of Ga for Si(tet) has a large effect on the $\varepsilon_{2}$ peak and thus on the $\varepsilon_{1}(0)$. We hope this improvement of $\varepsilon_{1}(0)$ by substitution provide a valuable guide to how to raise the $\varepsilon_{1}(0)$.

Although tetrahedron structure expand or shrink to some extent, the four bond lengths remain the same. For all the cases, it is noticed that substitutions in tetrahedral Si (tet) sites keep the symmetry of cubic, whereas substitutions in octahedral Ge (oct) sites lead a deformation.

Due to the same structure and same valence electrons numbers of constituent elements, the trend of doping effect which obtained through the calculations conducted on $\gamma$-SiGe₂N₄, that is, substitution of p-type impurity in the next period for the tetrahedral site atoms brings an important influence on the $\varepsilon_{2}$, can be extended to the spinel nitrides formed from IVA Group elements.

## 4. Conclusions

In order to improve the optical properties, we proposed an approach to raise the peak value of the imaginary part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_{2}$ by site substitution of neighboring elements to constituting elements in the spinel nitride based on the analysis of band structure. The substituting elements in IIIA, VA and VIA groups for Si (tet) and Ge (oct) in $\mathrm{SiGe}_{2} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$ with different concentrations are investigated. Substitution of As for both Si (tet) and Ge (oct) sites results in an improvement of $\varepsilon_{2}$ peak as concentration increases. Substitution of Ga for Si(tet) site improves $\varepsilon_{2}$ peak remarkably whereas substitution of Ga for Ge(oct) site induces an opposite effect on $\varepsilon_{2}$ peak with concentration increasing. It is obtained that doping Ga in Si(tet) site is the most effective within various elements substitution in $\mathrm{SiGe}_{2} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$. The reasons of changes in dielectric constants are explained from analyzing electronic band, bond length and valence charge density. Further calculation of stability shows substitutions for Ge (oct) sites have less expansion and lower formation energy than for Si (tet) sites. Substitutions of Ga for Ge (oct) and Al for Si (tet) have minus formation energies. It is reasonable to extend these conclusions observed on $\gamma-\mathrm{SiGe}_{2} \mathrm{~N}_{4}$ to other IVA spinel nitrides as some general regularities in site substitution.
