## ABSTRACT

Structural parameters, elastic, electronic, bonding and optical properties of delafossite $\text{CuAlO}_2$ have been investigated using the plane-wave ultrasoft pseudopotential technique based on the first-principles density-functional theory (DFT). The ground-state properties obtained by minimizing the total energy are in favorable agreement with the previous work. We have derived the bulk, shear and Young's modulus, Poisson coefficient for delafossite $\text{CuAlO}_2$. We estimated the Debye temperature of $\text{CuAlO}_2$ from the acoustic velocity. Electronic and chemical bonding properties have been studied throughout the calculation of band structure, density of states and charge densities. Furthermore, in order to clarify the mechanism of optical transitions for delafossite $\text{CuAlO}_2$, the dielectric function is calculated, which shows a significant optical anisotropy in the components of polarization directions (100) and (001).

## 1. Introduction

Transparent conductive oxides (TCOs) are widely used in industrial fields, such as transparent electrodes in flat panel displays, window layers in solar cells, room-temperature sensing of ozone, semiconducting materials in ultraviolet (UV) and blue lasers, thermoelectric material, thin film photovoltaics applications, etc [1-6]. Hence, they gained wide interest, especially $\text{CuAlO}_2$, which is a commercially important material as p-type TCO [7]. The $\text{CuAlO}_2$ delafossite structure belongs to space group of $R\overline{3}m$ and has the hexagonal structure, which can be viewed as a layer structure with the sequence Cu-O-Al-O-Cu along the c-axis [7]. In the delafossite structure, there are two cation sites, $\text{Cu}^{+1}$ [at position 3(a)(0,0,0)] and $\text{Al}^{+3}$ [at position 3(b)(0,0,1/2)], and one anion site, $\text{O}^{-2}$[at position 6(c)(0,0,u)] [8].

A considerable amount of work has been developed in the investigation of $\text{CuAlO}_2$ both experimentally [9-16] and theoretically [17-23]. Aston et al. [16] have used X-ray photoemission spectroscopy (XPS), X-ray emission spectroscopy (XES) and X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) to study the electronic structure of $\text{CuAlO}_2$. They found that there is a significant mixing between O 2p and Cu 3d states and provided a consistent picture of the electronic structure in broad agreement with ab-initio calculations. More recently, this compound is found to show very strong electron-hole correlation effects, with the lowest exciton binding energy of the order of 0.47 eV for perpendicular polarization [23]. Furthermore, these authors have used the LAPW-LDA [17], KKR-CPA-LSDA [18,21], FLAPW-LDA [19] and FPLAPW [22] calculations.

It can be seen that a few reported theoretical studies have been devoted to the $\text{CuAlO}_2$. However, a number of basis properties of $\text{CuAlO}_2$ are still unknown. To the best of our knowledge, there are no experimental or theoretical works exploring the elastic constants. The elastic properties of solids are important because they provide information on interatomic potentials and relate to various fundamental solid state phenomena such as interatomic bonding, equations of state, phonon spectra as well as specific heat, thermal expansion, Debye temperature and Grüneisen parameter [24,25]. Moreover, in order to fully take advantage of the properties of $\text{CuAlO}_2$ in the fabrication of optical devies, a theoretical investigation of the electronic structure and optical properties is necessary. There are some optical properties calculations exist in the case of $\text{CuAlO}_2$ [17,23], but there exist two points which should be clarified: (1) detailed charge densities were not presented and the bonding structure was not clearly understood, (2) the physical origins of the optical properties were not presented.

Hence, the aim of this paper is to give a study of mechanical, electronic, chemical bonding and optical properties of delafossite $\text{CuAlO}_2$ using the plane-wave ultrasoft pseudopotential technique based on the first-principles density-functional theory. The paper is organized as follows: in the next section, we will give the technical details of the employed methods. Section 3 is devoted to the results and discussion, including the geometry and structural properties, elastic and mechanical properties, electronic and chemical bonding, optical properties. We summarize our main finding in Section 4.

## 2. Computational methodology

Density-functional theory calculations are performed with plane-wave ultrasoft pseudopotential using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-Wang 1991 (PW91) functional [26] as implemented in the CASTEP code [27]. The ionic cores are represented by ultrasoft pseudopotentials for Cu, Al and O atoms. The Cu 3d¹⁰, 4s¹ electrons, Al 3s², 3p¹ electrons and O 2s², 2p⁴ electrons are explicitly treated as valence electrons. The plane-wave cutoff energy is 380 eV and the Brillouin-zone integration is performed over the 10 × 10 × 10 grid sizes using the Monkhorst-Pack method for delafossite structure optimization. This set of parameters assure the maximum force of 0.01 eV/Å, the maximum stress of 0.02 GPa and the maximum displacement of 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ Å.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Geometry and structure optimization

The crystal structure of delafossite CuAlO₂ belongs to the space group$R\overline{3}m$ and the local symmetry $D_{3d}^{5}$. The energy versus volume curve is fitted using the third-order Birch-Murnaghan equation [28]:

$$
P=\frac{3}{2} B_{0}\left[\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{\frac{7}{3}}-\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{\frac{5}{3}}\right]\left\{1+\frac{3}{4}\left(4-B^{\prime}\right)\left[\left(\frac{V_{0}}{V}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}-1\right]\right\}
\tag{1}
$$

to find the optimized parameters. The calculated lattice parameters $a$, $c$ (in Å), internal parameter $u$, bulk modulus ($B_0$) and the formation energy ($\Delta E_f$) compared with available theoretical [8,17,29] and experimental data [8,17,30] for delafossite CuAlO₂ are summarized in Table 1. Compared to the experiments [8,30], the computed lattice constant $a$, $c/a$, and internal parameter $u$ are overrated about 0.80%, 0.18% and 0.05%, respectively, which show the agreement with the experiments can be considered to be very good. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first theoretical prediction of the formation energy ($\Delta E_f$) with $-27.36$ eV, which is negative and indicates that the formation of delafossite phase is exothermic and awaits experimental confirmation.

### 3.2. Elastic and mechanical properties

Elastic constants are defined by means of a Taylor expansion of the total energy, the energy of a strained system is expressed as follows [31-33]:

$$
U(V, \varepsilon)=U\left(V_{0}, 0\right)+V_{0}\left[\sum_{i} \tau_{i} \varepsilon_{i} \xi_{i}+\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i j} C_{i j} \varepsilon_{i} \xi_{i} \varepsilon_{j} \xi_{j}\right]
\tag{2}
$$

where $U(V_0,0)$ is the energy of the unstrained system with equilibrium volume $V_0$, $\tau_i$ is an element in the stress tensor and $\xi_i$ is a factor to take care of Voigt index. Additionally, there are five independent components of elastic constants for delafossite structure $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, $C_{13}$, $C_{33}$, and $C_{44}$. Then, the elastic energy $U$ for delafossite structure takes the form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U= & \frac{1}{2}\left(C_{11} \varepsilon_{1}^{2}+C_{11} \varepsilon_{2}^{2}+C_{33} \varepsilon_{3}^{2}\right)+C_{12} \varepsilon_{1} \varepsilon_{2}+C_{13} \varepsilon_{1} \varepsilon_{3}+C_{13} \varepsilon_{2} \varepsilon_{3} \\
& +2\left[C_{44} \varepsilon_{4}^{2}+C_{44} \varepsilon_{5}^{2}+\left(\frac{C_{11}-C_{12}}{2}\right) \varepsilon_{6}^{2}\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

Then, the calculated elastic properties of the five independent elastic constants are $C_{11}$=277.73 GPa, $C_{12}$=68.90 GPa, $C_{13}$=82.65 GPa, $C_{33}$=533.73 GPa, and $C_{44}$=41.04 GPa. The mechanically stable phases are dependent on the positive definiteness of stiffness matrix and the Born stability criteria are [34,35]

$$
C_{44}>0, \quad C_{11}>\left|C_{12}\right|, \quad C_{33}\left(C_{11}+C_{12}\right)>2 C_{13}^{2}
\tag{4}
$$

It can be seen that these criteria are satisfied, which indicate delafossite CuAlO₂ compound is mechanically stable.

From the calculated elastic constants, we can obtain the macroscopic mechanical parameters of CuAlO₂ compound, namely the bulk, shear and Young's modulus, which are defined as [36-38]

$$
\begin{aligned}
& B_{\text {Hill }}=\frac{1}{2}\left(B_{\text {Reuss }}+B_{\text {Voigt }}\right) \\
& B_{\text {Reuss }}=\left[\left(2 S_{11}+S_{33}\right)+2\left(S_{12}+2 S_{13}\right)\right]^{-1} \\
& B_{\text {Voigt }}=\frac{1}{9}\left(2 C_{11}+C_{33}\right)+\frac{2}{9}\left(C_{12}+2 C_{13}\right) \\
& G_{\text {Hill }}=\frac{1}{2}\left(G_{\text {Reuss }}+G_{\text {Voigt }}\right) \\
& G_{\text {Reuss }}=15\left[4\left(2 S_{11}+S_{33}\right)-4\left(S_{12}+2 S_{13}\right)+6\left(S_{44}+S_{11}-S_{12}\right)\right]^{-1} \\
& G_{\text {Voigt }}=\frac{1}{15}\left(2 C_{11}+C_{33}-C_{12}-2 C_{13}\right)+\frac{1}{5}\left(2 C_{44}+\frac{C_{11}-C_{12}}{2}\right) \\
& E_{X}=\frac{9 B_{X} G_{X}}{G_{X}+3 B_{X}}
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

where $X$ is Voigt, Reuss or Hill, and $S_{ij}$ is the inverse matrix of the elastic constants matrix $C_{ij}$, which is given by [31]

$$
\begin{aligned}
& S_{11}=\left(C_{11} C_{33}-C_{13}^{2}\right) /\left[\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)\left(C_{11} C_{33}+C_{12} C_{33}-2 C_{13}^{2}\right)\right] \\
& S_{12}=\left(C_{13}^{2}-C_{12} C_{33}\right) /\left[\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)\left(C_{11} C_{33}+C_{12} C_{33}-2 C_{13}^{2}\right)\right] \\
& S_{13}=-C_{13} /\left(C_{11} C_{33}+C_{12} C_{33}-2 C_{13}^{2}\right)
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

From our values, we can see that $B_{X}>G_{X}$, which implies that the parameter limiting the stability of this compound is the shear modulus [40]. Additionally, the lower Poisson coefficients of $v_{R}$=0.313, $v_{V}$=0.269 and $v_{H}$=0.290 mean that an increasing in the volume is associated with the uniaxial tensile deformation. According to criterion of Pugh [41], a parameter $B_{X}/G_{X}$ defined: the shear modulus is relative with the resistance to plastic deformation and the bulk modulus is associated with the resistance to fracture and Pugh proposed that if the $B_{X}/G_{X}$ is more than the critical value (1.75), the material would be ductile. In our case, the values of $B_{X}/G_{X}$ are all more than 1.75, which mean this compound will behave in a ductile manner.

Moreover, the elastic anisotropy ratio is an important physical quantity about the structural phase stability of crystal structures and the anisotropies of the compressional (P) and two shear waves (S1 and S2) which are defined as [42]

$$
\Delta_{p}=\frac{C_{33}}{C_{11}} ; \quad \Delta_{S 1}=\frac{C_{11}-C_{13}}{2 C_{44}} ; \quad \Delta_{S 2}=\frac{2 C_{44}}{C_{11}-C_{12}} \tag{8}
$$

Estimations of the anisotropy ratio $\Delta_{p}$, $\Delta_{S1}$ and $\Delta_{S2}$ are 1.922, 2.377 and 0.393, indicating the anisotropy of this compound. Additionally, for anisotropic system the small $C_{13}/C_{12}$ (1.200) and large $C_{33}/C_{11}$. (1.922) values indicate that atomic bonding along the z-axis is stronger than along the x-axis.

### 3.3. Electronic and chemical bonding

The calculated energy band structure of delafossite $CuAlO_2$ along with the high-symmetry points of the Brillouin zone is shown in Fig. 1. The top of the valence band is taken as the zero of energy. The calculated band structure shows that delafossite $CuAlO_2$ has indirect band gap because the top valence and the bottom conduction are found at $F$ point and $\Gamma$ point, respectively. In addition, the indirect gap from $F$ to $\Gamma$ is calculated to be about 1.80 eV and the direct gap at $\Gamma$ is 2.86 eV, which are consistent with the prior LAPW-LDA results of 1.97 eV (indirect) and 2.93 eV (direct) [17], the FLAPW-LDA results of 1.95 eV (indirect) and 2.7 eV (direct) [19], the FLAPW-LDA results of 1.7 eV (indirect) and 2.8 eV (direct) [20], as well as the experimental values of 1.65 [43] and 1.8 eV [20] for the indirect gap, 3.5 eV [20] for the direct gap.

The total and partial density of states are shown in Fig. 2. It can be seen that the bandwidth of the lower valence bands is about 4.14 eV and the peak of the lower valence bands is located at – 18.65 eV, which are composed predominantly of O-2s character and show a hybridization with Al 3s and 3p electrons. The upper valence bands include mainly three structures labeled (1), (2) and (3) for delafossite $CuAlO_2$. Structure (1) originates mainly from the hybridization $\sigma$ bonding between Al (3s+3p) and O-2p, as well as $\sigma$ bonding between Cu 3(d$_{xz}$, d$_{yz}$) and O-2p. ### 3.4. Optical properties

The investigated compound delafossite $CuAlO_2$ have a hexagonal symmetry, so the experiments are performed with electric vector $\vec{E}$ perpendicular or parallel to the $c$ axis, namely (100) and (001) vectors. The optical properties can be gained from the complex dielectric function [44,45]:
$$
\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+i \varepsilon_{2}(\omega)
\tag{11}
$$
which is mainly connected with the electronic structures. The imaginary part $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ is calculated from the electronic structure through the joint density of states and the momentum matrix elements between the occupied and the unoccupied wave functions within the selection rules and given by
$$
\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{2 e^{2} \pi}{\Omega \varepsilon_{0}} \sum_{k, v, c}\left|\left\langle\psi_{k}^{c}|\hat{u} \times r| \psi_{k}^{v}\right\rangle\right|^{2} \delta\left(E_{k}^{c}-E_{k}^{v}-E\right),
\tag{12}
$$
where $e$ is the electronic charge, and $\psi_{k}^{c}$ and $\psi_{k}^{v}$ are the conduction band (CB) and valence band (VB) wavefunctions at $k$, respectively. The real part $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ can be attracted from the Kramer-Kronig relationship.

The imaginary part and the real part of the calculated dielectric function from (100) and (001) are presented in Figs. 4 and 5, respectively. For the sake of comparison, the imaginary part of dielectric function of Laskowski et al. [23] calculated results are also presented in Figs. 4 and 5. It is obvious that our calculated results are close to the calculated data. The dielectric functions show a significant anisotropy from (100) and (001). The absorptive part of the imaginary part exhibits some structures labeled A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H and J for delafossite $CuAlO_2$. Structure A originates mainly from the hybridization bonding between Cu-$3d_{z}^{2}$ and O-2p into antibonding from Cu $3(p_{x}+p_{y})$ and O-$2p_{z}$, as well as Cu-$3d_{z}^{2}$ and O-$2p_{z}$. Structures B, C, F, G originate mainly from the hybridization $\pi$ bonding between Cu $3(d_{xz}, d_{yz}, d_{xy}, d_{x^{2}-y^{2}})$ and O-2p into antibonding from Cu $3(p_{x}+p_{y})$ and O-$2p_{z}$, as well as Cu-$3d_{z}^{2}$ and O-$2p_{z}$. Structures D, H originate mainly from the hybridization $\sigma$ bonding between Cu $3(d_{xz}, d_{yz}, d_{xy}, d_{x^{2}-y^{2}})$ and O-2p into antibonding from Cu $3(p_{x}+p_{y})$ and O-$2p_{z}$, as well as Cu-$3d_{z}^{2}$ and O-$2p_{z}$. Structures E, J originate mainly from the hybridization $\sigma$ bonding between Al (3s+3p) and O-2p into antibonding from Al (3s+3p) and O-$2p_{z}$. The calculated static dielectric constants are 7.18 and 5.24 from (100) and (001).

### 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have performed first-principles computations on delafossite $CuAlO_2$, including structural parameters, elastic, electronic, bonding and optical properties. It has been shown that the structural parameters obtained after relaxation are in favorable agreement with the previous work. We have calculated the elastic constants and derived the bulk and shear moduli, Young's modulus, Poisson coefficients and Debye temperature. The results show delafossite $CuAlO_2$ is mechanically stable and behave in a ductile manner. The electronic structure presents that delafossite $CuAlO_2$ has indirect band gap with 1.80 eV and the direct gap with 2.86 eV. From the band structure, the charge densities are obtained and the chemical bonding is analyzed, which show that Cu and O is mainly covalent whereas Al and O is mainly ionic. The complex dielectric function have been shown and the peaks position distribution of imaginary parts of the complex dielectric function have been explained according to the band structure, which show a significant optical anisotropy in the components of polarization direction (100) and (001).
