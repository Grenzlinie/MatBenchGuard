# AB INITIO CALCULATION OF THE ELASTIC AND OPTICAL PROPERTIES OF Al₃Sc COMPOUND

M. Song* and D.H. Xiao

State Key Laboratory of Powder Metallurgy, Central South University,
Changsha 410083, China

Manuscript received 10 January 2007; in revised form 3 March 2007

The ab initio method has been performed to explore the elastic and optical properties of Al₃Sc compound, based on a plane wave pseudopotential method. It can be seen that the calculated equilibrium lattice parameter and elastic constants are in reasonable agreement with the previous experimental data. The elastic constants satisfy the requirement for mechanical stability in the cubic structure of the Al₃Sc compound. The optical property calculations show that a strong absorptive peak exists from 0-15eV and a relative small absorptive peak exists around 30eV. The form is caused by the optical transitions between high s, p, and d bands, and the latter results from the optical transitions from high s, p, and d bands to the low 2p band.

**KEY WORDS** intermetallic compound; Al₃Sc; elastic property; optical property; ab initio calculation

## 1. Introduction

Most age hardening aluminum alloys are limited to be used in the elevated temperature range, because of the rapid coarsening of the strengthening precipitates at high temperature⁽¹⁾. However, the addition of trace element Sc to aluminum alloys can substantially improve both their strength and ductility at elevated temperatures because of the formation of very fine coherent Al₃Sc compounds⁽²⁾. Thus, a lot of work has been carried out to study the effects of Sc on the microstructures and properties of the aluminum alloy⁽³⁻⁸⁾, as well as, the structure of the Al₃Sc compounds⁽²,⁹,¹⁰⁾. It has been shown that Al₃Sc is a stable $L1_2$ intermetallic compound. This compound has a very low density $(3.03×10^3 \mathrm{kg·m^{-3}})^{[9]}$, and distributes along the {100} planes⁽²⁾ with a melting temperature of $1320^\circ \mathrm{C}^{[10]}$ and very low coarsening rates⁽²⁾. The electronic structure of Al₃Sc has been calculated by using the first principle method, in the previous studies⁽¹¹⁻¹³⁾. In this article, the elastic and optical properties are explored based on the DFT (density-functional theory), aiming to further understand other properties of the Al₃Sc compound.

## 3. Results and Discussion

Al₃Sc is a binary stable $L1_2$ intermetallic compound. The equilibrium lattice parameter $a_0$ is 0.4053393nm after ultrasoft pseudopotentials calcualtion, similar to the previous published data (0.4101nm by calculation and 0.4106nm by experiment)¹⁶. The value of the equilibrium lattice parameter 0.4053393nm is very close to the lattice parameter of pure Al, with a value of 0.40496nm at 298K¹⁷. Thus the Al matrix can easily accommodate the elastic strain energy during the nucleation and growth of the Al₃Sc compound, which exhibits a stronger coherent effect on the strength, compared to other Al₃X compounds ($X = Zr, Cr, Er, etc.$).

To obtain the elastic constants of the Al₃Sc compound with a cubic structure, the first principle calculation has been used by computing the stress tensor $\delta$ for small strains (about 1/10 of the lattice parameter $a_0$). It is well known that a cubic crystal has only three independent elastic constants ($C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{44}$) or only three independent compliance constants ($S_{11}$, $S_{12}$, and $S_{44}$). The bulk modulus ($B_0$) and Poisson's ratio ($v$) can be determined through equations as follows

$$
B_{0}=(C_{11}+2 C_{12}) / 3 \tag{1}
$$

$$
v=-S_{12} / S_{11} \tag{2}
$$

The calculated elastic constants and previous experimental data¹⁸ have been shown in Table 1. It can be seen that the calculated values are in reasonable agreement with the experimental data, except for $C_{44}$, where the result is smaller than the experimental counterpart. The mechanical stability in this cubic structure leads to the following restrictions on the elastic constants, $C_{11}-C_{12}>0, C_{44}>0, C_{11}+C_{12}>0$. From Table 1, it can be seen that the calculated elastic constants obey these stability conditions, including the fact that $C_{12}$ must be smaller than $C_{11}$. The calculated elastic constants also obey the cubic stability condition that $C_{12}<B_{0}<C_{11}$.

As Al₃Sc is a cubic structure, the optical properties will not display anisotropy. Thus, polarization of the electromagnetic field will not be taken into account during the optical properties calculation. The optical properties of a material can be expressed by refraction ($N$)

$$
N=n+i k \tag{3}
$$

where $n$ is the refractive index and $k$ is the absorption coefficient (or extinction coefficient).

Fig.1 shows the calculated refraction as a function of energy (frequency) of $Al_3Sc$ compounds. It can be seen that the real part (refractive index) of the refraction has a strong peak at the position of low energy. This peak is accompanied by another strong peak at the imaginary part (absorption) of the refraction. As the intraband transitions in the far infrared (IR) region are not considered at all in this study, the strong peak is caused by the direct optical transitions between different bands. Detailed information about the optical transitions can be illustrated by checking the partial density of the states (PDOS). Fig.2 illustrates the calculated PDOS of the $Al_3Sc$ compound. It can be seen that the energy difference is about 20V between low s and p bands, whereas, it is more than 18eV between low p band and high s, p, and d bands. Thus, the strong peak is caused by the optical transitions between high s, p, and d bands. It can also be seen that a small absorption peak exists around 30eV. This small peak is caused by the optical transitions from high s, p, and d bands to the low 2p band. One should be careful of the calculated refraction, as the intraband transitions are not considered in this calculation. Normally, the intraband transitions are observed in metallic materials instead of insulators because electrons can only be excited into empty states because of the Pauli principle.

It is common to evaluate the complex dielectric constant when calculating the optical properties. The frequency dependent complex dielectric constant, $\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_1(\omega)+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_2(\omega)$, where $\varepsilon$ is dielectric constant, $\omega$ is the angular frequency, $\varepsilon_1$ is the real part and $\varepsilon_2$ is the imaginary part, is known to describe the optical response of the medium at all phonon energies, $E=\hbar\omega$, $E$ is energy and $\hbar$ is the Planck constant, using the formalism of Ehrenreich and Cohen $^{[19]}$. The real and imaginary parts can be described as follows

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=n^{2}-k^{2} \tag{4}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)=2 n k \tag{5}
$$

## 4. Conclusions

Elastic and optical properties of the $Al_3Sc$ compound have been studied based on DFT. The calculation makes use of the ultrasoft pseudopotentials, and an expansion of electronic wavefunctions in plane waves. It has been shown that the elastic constants of $Al_3Sc$ compound, obtained by the calculations, satisfy the requirement of the mechanical stability in a cubic structure. The equilibrium lattice parameter of the $Al_3Sc$ compound is very close to the lattice parameter of pure Al, thus the $Al_3Sc$ compound exhibits a stronger coherent effect on the strength. It has also been shown that two absorptive peaks exist for the $Al_3Sc$ compound. The strong peak is caused by the optical transitions between high s, p, and d bands, whereas, the relative small peak is caused by the optical transitions from high s, p, and d bands to the low 2p band.
