# Optical Study of Filled Tetrahedral Compounds $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$

M. DADSETANI, $^{1,2}$ S. NAMJOO, $^{1}$ and H. NEJATI $^{1}$

1.—Physics Department, Faculty of Science, University of Lorestan, Lorestan, Iran. 2.—e-mail: dadsetani.m@lu.ac.ir; dadsetani_m@yahoo.com

A detailed analysis of the optical properties of filled tetrahedral semiconductors $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ has been performed, using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave method within the density functional theory. The real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$, the optical absorption coefficient $I(\omega)$, the reflectivity $R(\omega)$, and the electron energy loss function are calculated within the random phase approximation. The interband transitions responsible for the structures in the spectra are specified. Looking at optical matrix element, we note that the major peaks are dominated by transition from metal $s$, N $2p$ states to N $2p$, Ga $3d$ states. The theoretical calculated optical properties and electron energy loss spectrum yield a static dielectric constant of 5.34 and a plasmon energy of 19.47 eV for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$. In the $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ compound, the static dielectric constant decreases to 4.75 and yields a plasmon energy of 18.5 eV. The effect of spin–orbit coupling on the optical properties is also investigated and found to be quite small, especially in the low-energy region. In order to check the reliability of our calculations, analogous results obtained for $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ in the same structure [space group $Ia3(206)$] are included in this work.

**Key words:** Filled tetrahedral semiconductors, optical properties, EELS, FP-LAPW, DFT

## INTRODUCTION

$\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ are Nowotny–Juza compounds, $^{1,2}$ which form very important wide-bandgap semiconductors with a body-centered cubic (bcc) structure and space group $Ia3(206)$ at normal conditions. These compounds have recently been considered as filled tetrahedral semiconductors after synthesis and photoacoustic spectroscopy studies. $^{3}$ A filled tetrahedral compound (such as $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$) can be viewed as a zincblende-like $(\text{Li}_{0.5}\text{Al}_{0.5}\text{N})$ lattice filled with He-like $\text{Li}^+$ ions at the empty tetrahedral sites. $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ are characterized by their wide bandgap, high thermal conductivity, and large bulk modulus. $^{4}$ These interesting properties make them potential materials for use in the development of solid-state illumination, optoelectronic, and electrochemical applications. $^{5–7}$ $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ is also an interesting material for GaN single-crystal growth. $^{8,9}$ They are technologically important materials, with a bandgap in the ultraviolet region, which can be used as alternatives to aluminum nitride and gallium nitride. $^{10,11}$

The available experimental studies on the optical properties of these materials are limited to the optical bandgap. $^{3,11,12}$ To our knowledge, there are not yet any experimental results on optical quantities (dielectric function, reflectivity, electron energy loss function) in the optical frequency range. The results of this paper could be used as a reference for future experimental work on these compounds.

On these materials, from a theoretical point of view, there are a number of first-principles calculations concerning their stability and electronic as well as structural properties. $^{4,10,13}$ To the best of our knowledge there is no theoretical research on optical properties (i.e., real and imaginary parts of dielectric functions, reflectivity, absorption, and electron energy loss function). Moreover, it seems

that there is a lack of both experimental and theoretical data on the optical properties of filled tetrahedral semiconductors $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$, and no systematic research on the optical properties of these compounds has been reported.

In this work, we have investigated a full range of optical properties of $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$, including real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function, the absorption coefficient, and the energy loss function, using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) for the exchange correlation potential, within the density functional theory.

## CALCULATION METHOD

The calculations presented in this work were performed using the FP-LAPW method. In this method, no shape approximation on the potential or the electronic charge density is made. The calculations of the electronic and optical properties have been done relativistically with and without spin-orbit coupling. We used the WIEN2K$^{14}$ implementation of the method, which allows inclusion of local orbitals in the basis, improving upon linearization and enabling consistent treatment of the semicore and valence states in an energy window, hence ensuring proper orthogonality. The exchange correlation potential within the GGA was calculated using the scheme of Wu and Cohen.$^{15}$ To calculate the bandgap, the Engel and Vosko$^{16}$ scheme was also applied. The convergence parameter $R_{\text{MT}}^{\text{min}}K_{\text{max}}$ (the product of the smallest of the atomic sphere radii $R_{\text{MT}}$ and the plane wave cutoff parameter $K_{\text{max}}$), which controls the size of the basis sets in these calculations, was set to 8. The maximum $l$ quantum number for the wavefunction expansion inside the atomic sphere was confined to $l_{\text{max}} = 10$. The $G_{\text{max}}$ parameter was taken to be 14.0 Bohr$^{-1}$. Brillouin-zone (BZ) integrations within self-consistency cycles were performed via a tetrahedron method,$^{17}$ using 35k points in the irreducible BZ. For calculation of the optical properties, however, a denser sampling of the BZ was needed, where we used 76k points. The muffin-tin radii of lithium (Li), metal (M), and nitrogen atoms (N) were chosen as $(R_{\text{MT}}^{\text{Li}}, R_{\text{MT}}^{\text{M}}, R_{\text{MT}}^{\text{N}}) = (2.03, 1.73, 1.84)$ and $(1.99, 1.65, 1.80)$ a.u. for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$, respectively. All of these values were chosen so as to ensure convergence of the results. The Wyckoff positions of Li, Al, N1, and N2 are 48e $(x, y, z)$, 16c $(u, u, u)$, 8a $(0, 0, 0)$, and 24d $(v, 0, 0.25)$, respectively. The bcc (48-atom) structure of cubic $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ is characterized by the single lattice constant $a$ and the internal parameters of atoms. The optimized $a$, $x$, $y$, $z$, $u$, and $v$ values were calculated, being in good agreement with the experimental results.$^{1,2}$ The calculation for the optical properties has been done with and without spin-orbit coupling to ascertain whether the latter has any dramatic influence on the optical properties. We found that spin-orbit interaction has a minor influence on the optical properties.

## ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE RESULTS

Since the optical spectra are calculated from the interband transitions, first we present our calculated electronic structure of these compounds. Our calculated total density of states (DOS) for $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ are presented in Fig. 1. Due to the close similarity between the results obtained for these compounds, the electronic band structure (Fig. 2) and the partial DOS (Fig. 1) are given only for $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$. The results are given both with and without spin-orbit coupling. Major contributions to the occupied part of the DOS come from the N $2p$ and $2s$ states.

The first structure in the low-lying energy side of the DOS consists of a peak around $-12.5$ eV for the two compounds. This structure originates from N $s$ states, corresponding to the lowest-lying bands in Fig. 2. These bands are lower in energy for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ than for $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ by 1.0 eV. Spin–orbit coupling has a very minor effect on the structure of these peaks for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$.

The next structure, which in the absence of spin–orbit coupling, is separated from the first by a gap of 5.64 eV for $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and 3.95 eV for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$, is a broad peak that is situated between $-7$ eV and the Fermi level, corresponding to the valence bands shown in the band structure in Fig. 2. From the partial DOS, it is seen that, just below the Fermi level, the bands are dominated by N $2p$, where metal $s$ and $p$ are hybridized. These bands are lower in energy for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ than for $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ by 2.0 eV. The structure of the spin–orbit splitting and its size are not considerable in this region.

There are two main features in the broad structure above the Fermi level up to around 15 eV, containing a broad peak around 7 eV for $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and 5 eV for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$. The distinctive structure in the DOS above 7.0 eV is a broad peak at around 10 eV for the two compounds. These structures are mainly from the $p$ states of both nitrogen and lithium atoms, and $3d$ states of gallium.

In $\text{Li}_3\text{AlN}_2$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ we found a direct band-gap between nitrogen $2p$ bands occurring at the BZ center, confirming the results found previously by Kushida et al. $^{3}$ and Kuriyama et al. $^{11,12}$ The calculated values of the bandgap are compared with the experimental and other calculated results in Table I. We included the spin–orbit coupling in the calculation of the bandgap, but the effect was negligible. Compared with the experimental and other calculated results, our results are in fair agreement with those of previous studies. The small difference in bandgap between our result and another FP-LAPW result $^{4}$ for $\text{Li}_3\text{GaN}_2$ is due to the difference in exchange correlation functional used in the two calculations.

The results presented in Table I confirm the well-known expectation that theoretical bandgaps are on the whole underestimated when using GGA, in comparison with experimental values. The major shortcoming of DFT for calculation of the energy gap lies in the exchange correlation term, which cannot be handled accurately using this method. Engel and Vosko¹⁶ constructed a new functional form of the GGA and proved that it improves quantities that depend on the energy eigenvalues, including the bandgap.¹⁸ Using Engel and Vosko’s GGA, we were able to improve our bandgap results, as shown in Table I. It can be seen that the calculated energy gap values decrease as the metal size increases.

## OPTICAL PROPERTIES

The optical properties of matter can be described by means of the transverse dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$. There are two contributions to $\varepsilon(\omega)$, namely intraband and interband transitions. The contribution from intraband transitions is important only for metals. Interband transitions can further be split into direct and indirect transitions. Here we neglect the indirect interband transitions, which involve scattering of phonons and are expected to make a small contribution to $\varepsilon(\omega)$.¹⁹ To calculate the direct interband contribution to the imaginary part of the dielectric function $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$, we should sum all possible transitions from occupied to unoccupied states. Taking the appropriate transition matrix elements into account, the imaginary part of the dielectric function $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ is given by the random phase approximation, neglecting local field effects, as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)= & \frac{V e^{2}}{2 \pi \hbar m^{2} \omega^{2}} \int d^{3} k \sum_{n n^{\prime}}\left|\left\langle k n|p| k n^{\prime}\right\rangle\right|^{2} f(k n) \\
& \times\left(1-f\left(k n^{\prime}\right)\right) \delta\left(E_{k n}-E_{k n^{\prime}}-\hbar \omega\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\hbar \omega$ is the energy of the incident photon, $\mathbf{p}$ is the momentum operator $\frac{\hbar \partial}{i \partial x},|k n\rangle$ is the eigenfunction with eigenvalue $E_{\mathbf{k} n}$, and $f(\mathbf{k} n)$ is the Fermi distribution function. The evaluation of the matrix elements of the momentum operator in Eq. 1 is performed over the muffin-tin and the interstitial regions separately. A full, detailed description of the calculation of these matrix elements is given by Ambrosch-Draxl and Sofo.²⁰

The real part of the dielectric function $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ derives from the Kramers-Kronig relation.²¹ In order to calculate $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$, we need to have a good representation of $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ up to high energies. In the present work we calculated $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ up to 75 eV and used this value as the truncation energy in Kramers-Kronig relations. This energy range was chosen to produce convergence in the Kramers-Kronig transformations. Other optical parameters, such as energy loss spectrum and absorption coefficient, can be immediately calculated in terms of components of the complex dielectric function.²¹

Our calculated imaginary parts of the dielectric function for the two compounds with and without spin-orbit coupling are shown in Fig. 3. The behavior of $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ is rather similar for the two compounds, with small differences in details. The calculated $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ has two major peaks, labeled $a$ and $b$ in the figure. There is also a shoulder around 7 eV for the two compounds. Note that all structures in the imaginary part of the dielectric function are shifted toward lower energies as we go from Li₃AlN₂ to Li₃GaN₂. This trend can be directly inferred from the band structure results, given the shift in the location of the first structure above the Fermi level in DOS curves. It is worthwhile to attempt to identify the transitions that are responsible for the structures in $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ using our calculated band structures. Looking at optical matrix elements, it is obvious that the metal $s$ states and N $p$ states play a major role in these optical transitions as initial and

The results for the dispersive part of the dielectric function, $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$, for the two compounds are shown in Fig. 4. The main features of these curves are as follows: a peak at around $4 \mathrm{eV}$; a rather steep decrease between $4 \mathrm{eV}$ and $7 \mathrm{eV}$, after which $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ becomes negative; a minimum at around $11.5 \mathrm{eV}$ for $\mathrm{Li}_{3} \mathrm{GaN}_{2}$ and $12.5 \mathrm{eV}$ for $\mathrm{Li}_{3} \mathrm{AlN}_{2}$; and then again a slow increase toward zero at higher energies.

The function representing characteristic energy losses (or plasmon oscillations) is one of the most important among those suitable for the description of microscopic and macroscopic properties of solids. This function is proportional to the probability that a fast electron moving across a medium loses an energy $E$ per unit length. The most prominent peak in the electron energy loss spectrum is identified as the plasmon peak, signaling the energy of collective excitations of the electronic charge density in the crystal. It is possible to have several plasmon peaks in a crystal. The EELS behavior is similar for the two compounds, with small differences. The calculated EELS has two major peaks, the bulk plasmon and the surface plasmon, for the two compounds. The main peaks in the EELS become sharper as we go from Ga to Al. This trend could be linked to the trends observed in the DOS and band structures. Compare the highest-lying valence bands for the two systems and also the lowest-lying conduction bands. In $Li_3AlN_2$, these bands have clearly less dispersion than in $Li_3GaN_2$. This is the reason why main peaks become sharper as we go from Ga to Al.

The local maxima of the extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$ correspond to the zeroes of $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$. However, the energy loss spectra do not show distinct maxima at the zeroes of $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ with energies smaller than 15 eV for the two compounds. The reason is that $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ is still large at these energy values. At 20 eV there are large peaks in the energy loss spectra. At such high energies $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ is small, and thus the amplitude of the energy loss function becomes large.

The static dielectric constants for $Li_3AlN_2$ and $Li_3GaN_2$ are summarized in Table II. Without spin-orbit coupling, we get $\varepsilon(0)=4.75$ and $\varepsilon(0)=5.34$ for $Li_3AlN_2$ and $Li_3GaN_2$, respectively. The spin-orbit coupling has a negligible effect on the dielectric constants. For $Li_3AlN_2$ and $Li_3GaN_2$, there is no experimental results on dielectric constants to compare with.

Figure 6 presents reflectivity spectra with and without spin-orbit coupling. There is a close resemblance between the spectra for the two compounds. The reflectivity spectra can be split into two regions, below and above 8 eV. The total amount of reflectivity in the second region is larger than in the first region. The calculated reflectivity starts around 16% for $Li_3GaN_2$ and 13% for $Li_3AlN_2$, irrespective of whether spin-orbit coupling is included or not. Our reflectivity reaches the maximum value of 28% for the two compounds around 5 eV. There is a dip in the reflectivity spectra around 8 eV for both $Li_3GaN_2$ and $Li_3AlN_2$, after which the reflectivity increases and reaches 38% around 13 eV.

## CONCLUSIONS

We have applied a FP-LAPW method to study the electronic and optical properties of the filled tetrahedral semiconductors $Li_3GaN_2$ and $Li_3AlN_2$ within density functional theory, with and without spin-orbit coupling. The frequency-dependent dielectric functions show two main structures arising primarily from band transitions. We have shown that the metal $s$, N $2p$ states and N $2p$, Ga $3d$ states play a major role in optical transitions as initial and final states, respectively. The calculations show a static dielectric constant of 5.34 and an EELS of 19.47 eV for $Li_3GaN_2$. In $Li_3AlN_2$, the static dielectric constant is reduced to 4.75. Spin-orbit coupling has a very small (almost negligible) effect on the optical properties. At higher energies, for $Li_3GaN_2$ it has a more pronounced effect on the details of the peak positions (overestimates/underestimates peak heights) and the average values of the calculated extinction coefficient, EELS, and imaginary part of the dielectric function. The nature of the fundamental gap in these materials was found to be direct. Using Engel and Vosko's GGA, we have performed band structure calculations and obtained better value for the bandgaps, with respect to the experimental results. To our knowledge, there are no earlier studies on these optical properties and dielectric constants. Thus, our calculations can be used to cover the lack of data for these compounds.
