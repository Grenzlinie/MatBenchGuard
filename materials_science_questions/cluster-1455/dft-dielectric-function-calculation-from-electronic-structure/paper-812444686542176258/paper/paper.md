# PHONON DISPERSION AND DIELECTRIC BEHAVIOUR
## OF MIXED ALKALI HALIDE CRYSTAL $NaCl_{0.5} Br_{0.5}$

SUNIL ASTHANA, $^{1}$ R. P. GOYAL $^{1}$ and S. C. GOYAL $^{2}$

$^{1}$ Department of Physics, St John's College, Agra and $^{2}$ Agra College, Agra, India

(Received 6 June 1992)

Abstract-In the present paper we have calculated the phonon dispersion curves and dielectric constants, their volume derivatives and the Gruneisen parameters of the mixed alkali halide $NaCl_{0.5} Br_{0.5}$, using the extended three body force shell model. The results of dielectric constants, their volume derivatives and Gruneisen parameters are found to agree reasonably well with the experimental values, which establishes the validity of the three body charge transfer parameter. With this concept the phonon dispersion curves are obtained in all the three symmetry directions $(q, o, o),(q, q, o)$ and $(q, q, q)$, which will be of interest for experimental workers, since no experimental values have so far been obtained for phonon frequencies.

---

## THEORETICAL BACKGROUND

The modified three body force shell model $^{(1-3)}$ has been used to calculate phonon frequencies in the three symmetry directions $(q, o, o),(q, q, o)$ and $(q, q, q)$ for the mixed alkali halide crystals $K_{0.5} Rb_{0.5} Cl, KCl_{0.5} Br_{0.5}$ and $Na_{0.5} K_{0.5} Cl$, and has successfully explained their dispersion curves. The same model has been used to describe the dispersion curves for the mixed alkali halide crystal, $NaCl_{0.5} Br_{0.5}$ and to calculate the dielectric constants, their volume derivatives and Gruneisen parameters.

The values within parentheses are experimental values.⁽⁴⁾

The input data are given in Table 1. They have been calculated by Vegard's law using the experimental values for pure crystals. The calculated values of the model parameters are listed in Table 2. The various phonon frequencies calculated in the three symmetry directions $(q,o,o)$, $(q,q,o)$ and $(q,q,q)$ have been plotted against the reduced wave vector $\vec q$ and the resulting phonon dispersion curve is shown in Fig. 1. The calculated values of the dielectric constants, their volume derivatives and Gruneisen parameters are reported in Table 3, and agree well with their experimental values.⁽⁴⁾ This establishes that the present model is quite adequate for explaining the dielectric behaviour of the mixed alkali halide crystal NaCl₀.₅Br₀.₅, and so the present study of phonon dispersion will stimulate experimentalists to determine phonon frequencies in all the symmetry directions.
