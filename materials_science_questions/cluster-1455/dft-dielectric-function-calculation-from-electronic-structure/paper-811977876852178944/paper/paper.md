# Interrelationship between Higher-Order Elastic Constants and Pressure Derivatives of Dielectric Constants for Ionic Crystals

By
G. M. S. SRIVASTAVA, R. K. SINHA, and J. SHANKER

An interrelationship between the pressure derivatives of dielectric constants and higher-order elastic constants is investigated. Some useful relations connecting the first- and second-order pressure derivatives of dielectric constants, third- and fourth-order elastic constants, and bulk modulus and its pressure derivatives are obtained. A numerical application of these relations in the case of alkali halide crystals yields results which are found to be in good agreement with available experimental data. The utility of the mathematical expressions reported in the present work is demonstrated by calculating the pressure derivatives of dielectric constants.

## 1. Introduction

In order to investigate the anharmonic or non-linear behaviour of ionic crystals it is necessary to evaluate the pressure or volume derivatives of dielectric constants [1 to 3]. Similarly an analysis of higher-order elastic constants is needed to evaluate the vibrational contributions containing coupling parameters of higher order which are directly related to the anharmonic behaviour [4]. Thus the pressure derivatives of dielectric constants as well as higher-order elastic constants are directly related to anharmonic effects in crystals.

Recently it has been possible [5] to derive relations between thermoelastic quanti- ties and higher-order elastic constants. These relations have been quite successful in predicting thermoelastic quantities such as the Grüneisen-Anderson parameters, thermal expansion coefficient, etc. In the present paper we derive some useful rela- tions for the first- and second-order pressure or volume derivatives of dielectric con- stants in terms of second-, third-, and fourth-order elastic constants. A numerical analysis based on these relations is then performed in case of alkali halide crystals. The method of analysis is presented in Section 2. Results are discussed and compar- ed with available experimental data in Section 3.

### 2. Method of Analysis

The low-frequency and high-frequency dielectric constants, $\varepsilon_0$ and $\varepsilon_\infty$, are related to the ion-displacement polarizability $\alpha_{\mathfrak{i}}$ by the Clausius-Mosotti and Lorentz-Lorenz expressions which yield the following relations [6]:

$$
\frac{\varepsilon_{0}-1}{\varepsilon_{0}+2}-\frac{\varepsilon_{\infty}-1}{\varepsilon_{\infty}+2}=\frac{4 \pi}{3 V} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}. \tag{1}
$$

The expressions for volume derivatives of dielectric constants are obtained from (1) as follows:

$$
V \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} V}=\left[\frac{4 \pi}{3} \frac{\mathrm{d} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{d} V}-\frac{4 \pi}{3 V} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}+\frac{3 V}{\left(\varepsilon_{\infty}+2\right)^{2}} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d} V}\right] \frac{\left(\varepsilon_{0}+2\right)^{2}}{3} \tag{2}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
V^{2} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}= & {\left[\frac{4 \pi}{3} V \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{d} V^{2}}-\frac{8 \pi}{3} \frac{\mathrm{d} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{d} V}+\frac{8 \pi}{3 V} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}+\right.} \\
& +\frac{6}{\left(\varepsilon_{0}+2\right)^{2}}\left(V \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} V}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{\left(\varepsilon_{\infty}+2\right)^{2}} V^{2} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \varepsilon_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d} V^{2}}- \\
& \left.-\frac{6}{\left(\varepsilon_{\infty}+2\right)^{3}}\left(V \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d} V}\right)^{2}\right] \frac{\left(\varepsilon_{0}+2\right)^{2}}{3}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
$$

The volume derivatives are related to the corresponding pressure derivatives of $\varepsilon_0$ by the following expressions:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} P}=-\frac{V}{B_{T}} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} V}, \tag{4}
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} P^{2}}=-\frac{1}{B_{T}} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} P}+\frac{V}{B_{T}^{2}} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} P} \frac{\mathrm{d} B_{T}}{\mathrm{~d} V}+\frac{V^{2}}{B_{T}^{2}} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}, \tag{5}
$$

where $B_{T}=-V(\mathrm{~d} P / \mathrm{d} V)_{T}$ is the isothermal bulk modulus. The ion-displacement polarizability is given by

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}=\frac{\left(Z^{\prime} e\right)^{2}}{A}, \tag{6}
$$

where $Z'e$ is the effective ionic charge [1] and $A$ the force constant related to the derivatives of the short-range potential energy $\varphi(r)$ is expressed as follows [7]:

$$
A=\frac{1}{3}\left[\varphi^{\mathrm{II}}(r)+\frac{2}{r} \varphi^{\mathrm{I}}(r)\right]. \tag{7}
$$

Here $\varphi^{\mathrm{I}}(r)$ and $\varphi^{\mathrm{II}}(r)$ are the first- and second-order derivatives of $\varphi(r)$ with respect to $r$. The total potential energy $W$ for an ionic crystal can be written as

$$
W=-\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{M}} e^{2}}{r}+\varphi(r), \tag{8}
$$

where $\alpha_{\mathrm{M}}$ is the Madelung constant. The volume derivatives of $\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$ can be obtained from (6) by assuming that $Z'$ does not change with volume. The validity of this assumption

for ionic crystals has been demonstrated recently [3, 6]. We thus obtain
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{d} V}=-\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}{V} \frac{V}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} V}
\tag{9}
$$
and
$$
V \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{d} V^{2}}=-\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}{V}\left[\frac{V^{2}}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} A}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}-2\left(\frac{V}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} V}\right)^{2}\right].
\tag{10}
$$

Thus the evaluation of the volume derivatives of $\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$ and hence those of $\varepsilon_{0}$ depend on the short-range force constant $A$ and its volume derivatives. The optic mode Grün-eisen parameters $\gamma_{\text {TO }}$ and $\gamma_{\text {LO }}$ which are also related to the dielectric behaviour can be expressed as follows [6]:
$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{TO}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{V}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} V}-\frac{V}{\varepsilon_{0}+2} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} V}+\frac{V}{\varepsilon_{\infty}+2} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d} V}\right)
\tag{11}
$$
and
$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{LO}}=\gamma_{\mathrm{TO}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{V}{\varepsilon_{0}} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{0}}{\mathrm{~d} V}-\frac{V}{\varepsilon_{\infty}} \frac{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d} V}\right).
\tag{12}
$$

In order to obtain direct interrelations between volume derivatives of $\varepsilon_{0}$ and higher-order elastic constants we make use of the correlation between short-range force constants and elastic constants. From the equation of state [8]
$$
P=-\frac{\mathrm{d} W}{\mathrm{~d} V},
\tag{13}
$$
where $P$ is the pressure, and (7) and (8) one can find
$$
B_{T}=\frac{A}{3 x r}+\frac{4}{3} P,
\tag{14}
$$
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} B_{T}}{\mathrm{~d} V}=\frac{A}{6 x r^{4}}\left(\frac{V}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} V}-\frac{5}{3}\right)-\frac{16}{9} \frac{P}{V},
\tag{15}
$$
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} B_{T}}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}=\frac{A}{12 x r^{7}}\left(\frac{V^{2}}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} A}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}-2 \frac{V}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} V}+4\right)+\frac{112}{27} \frac{P}{V^{2}},
\tag{16}
$$
where $x$ is a geometrical factor depending on the type of crystal structure. For NaCl-structure solids $x=2$. On solving (14) to (16) we get
$$
A=-3 x r\left(V P^{\mathrm{I}}+\frac{4}{3} P\right),
\tag{17}
$$
$$
\frac{V}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d} A}{\mathrm{~d} V}=\frac{3 V^{2} P^{\mathrm{II}}+8 V P^{\mathrm{I}}+\frac{4}{3} P}{3 V P^{\mathrm{I}}+4 P},
\tag{18}
$$
$$
\frac{V^{2}}{A} \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} A}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}=\frac{3 V^{3} P^{\mathrm{III}}+12 V^{2} P^{\mathrm{II}}+4 V P^{\mathrm{I}}-\frac{8}{9} P}{3 V P^{\mathrm{I}}+4 P},
\tag{19}
$$
where
$$
P^{\mathrm{I}}=\frac{\mathrm{d} P}{\mathrm{~d} V}, \quad P^{\mathrm{II}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} P}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}, \quad \text { and } \quad P^{\mathrm{III}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3} P}{\mathrm{~d} V^{3}},
$$

and these are directly related to the second-, third-, and fourth-order elastic constants
by the following expressions [5]:

$$
P^{\mathrm{I}}=\frac{1}{3 V}\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right),\qquad(20)
$$

$$
P^{\mathrm{II}}=\frac{1}{9 V^{2}}\left[3\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right)-\left(C_{111}+6 C_{112}+2 C_{123}\right)\right],\qquad(21)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P^{\mathrm{III}}= & \frac{1}{27 V^{3}}\left[\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right)+3\left(C_{111}+6 C_{112}+2 C_{123}\right)+C_{1111}+8 C_{1112}+\right. \\
& +6 C_{1122}+12 C_{1123}-\frac{\left(C_{111}+6 C_{112}+2 C_{123}\right)^{2}}{C_{11}+2 C_{12}}+ \\
& \left.+\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right)\left(3-\frac{C_{111}+6 C_{112}+2 C_{123}}{C_{11}+2 C_{12}}\right)\left(6-\frac{C_{111}+6 C_{112}+2 C_{123}}{C_{11}+2 C_{12}}\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
\qquad(22)
$$

Thus (17) to (22) provide direct interrelations between the volume derivatives of
dielectric constants and higher-order elastic constants.

sary to have reliable knowledge of higher-order pressure and volume derivatives
of dielectric constants and higher-order elastic constants. Experimental values of
these quantities are available only for a few crystals and therefore the relations ob-
tained in the present paper are useful to predict new results.

The thermodynamic properties were calculated [5] from the values of higher-order elastic constants determined from an interionic potential model taking account of three-body interactions [9]. Using the values of second-, third-, and fourth-order elastic constants [5, 9] and (17) to (22) we have obtained first the values of $A$, $(V/A)\mathrm{d}A/\mathrm{d}V$, and $(V^2/A)\mathrm{d}^2A/\mathrm{d}V^2$ for fifteen NaCl-structure alkali halide crystals. They are listed in Table 1.

Values of $\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$ calculated from (6) taking $Z'=1$ and $A$ from Table 1 turn out to be larger than those determined from (1) using experimental values of $\varepsilon_0$ and $\varepsilon_\infty$. Values of $Z'$ necessary for making an agreement between the two sets of $\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$ (see (1) and (6)) are calculated and reported in Table 1. We have thus used the values of $\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$ obtained from experimental data on dielectric constants. Values of $Z'$ (Table 1) are found to be consistent with earlier investigations [1, 12].

First- and second-order volume derivatives of $A$ are useful quantities as they are directly related to the anharmonic properties of solids [10]. Values reported in Table 1 are used to calculate the volume derivatives of dielectric constant $V(\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_0/\mathrm{d}V)$ and $V^2(\mathrm{d}^2\varepsilon_0/\mathrm{d}V^2)$, optical mode Grüneisen parameters $\gamma_{\mathrm{TO}}$ and $\gamma_{\mathrm{LO}}$, and pressure derivatives $\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_0/\mathrm{d}P$ and $\mathrm{d}^2\varepsilon_0/\mathrm{d}P^2$. They are compared with available experimental values [11 to 15] in Tables 2 to 4. The good agreement between calculated and experimental values supports the validity of the present analysis.
