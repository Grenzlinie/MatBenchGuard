# Insights on the opto-electronic structure of the inorganic mixed halide perovskites $\gamma$-CsPb(I$_{1-x}$Br$_x$)$_3$ with low symmetry black phase.

$^1$Laboratoire de Physique des Matériaux, Université Amar Telidji de Laghouat, BP 37G, Laghouat, 03000, Algeria

## Abstract

Inorganic halide perovskites especially $CsPbI_3$ have attracted great attention in optoelectronics and photovoltaic fields due to their better thermal stability and comparable efficiency with organometallic perovskites. $CsPbI_3$ has many phases, active black phase at high temperature and inactive yellow phase at low temperature. To obtain the perovskite phase, thin films should be heated above $320^\circ$ C, this high temperature can damage the flexible substrates. Recently, it has been suggested that partial or total substitution of iodide by bromide such as $CsPbI_{3-x}Br_x$, could stabilize the preferred perovskite black phase at lower temperature. Hence, it's very necessary to investigate the electronic and optical characteristics of these materials. The structural, electronic, some carrier transport and optical properties of the all-inorganic cesium lead mixed halide perovskite with a low symmetry orthorhombic structure were methodically investigated using density functional theory.

**Keywords:** inorganic mixed halide perovskites, optical properties, electronic structures, effective masses, DFT

**Corresponding author:** sosaid@alfaisal.edu

Recently, organic-inorganic halide perovskites of general formulas ABX3 (A methylammonium MA formamidinium FA Cs … , B Pb Sn Ge … , X = Cl,Br, I) have attracted great attention in optoelectronics and photovoltaic fields due to their excellent photoelectric performance, such as high carrier mobility, large absorption coefficients and tunable direct band gap [1–5]. Indeed, the first perovskite solar cells PSC with a 3.8% power conversion efficiency (PCE) reported in 2009 as light absorber material [1], in just a few years are today leading field of low-cost solar energy technology with PCE exceeding 24 % [6]. Approaching mono-crystalline silicon 26% [7]. Unfortunately, these materials showed very poor stability, are optically and thermally unstable especially under heat or humidity. Today, in order to improve material stability, organic molecule FA and MA have been replaced by smaller inorganic cations such Cs that stabilize perovskite structure, the PCE increased from 2.9% to 17.1% and better thermal stability [8–15]. Most of the researchers paid less attention to the all-inorganic perovskites compared to the organic perovskites, due to the difficulty in their synthesis methods, however, recently all-inorganic perovskites were fabricated and exhibited good thermal stability. This fully inorganic compound has 1.72 eV gaps which suitable for perovskite-Si tandem solar cells. We should be not that $CsPbI_3$ has many phases, active black phase at high temperature and inactive yellow phase at low temperature. To obtain the perovskite phase, thin films should be heated above $320\ ^{\circ}C$ and this high temperature can damage the flexible substrates. Recently, it has been suggested that partial or total substitution of iodide by bromide ($CsPbI_{3-x}Br_x$) could stabilize the preferred perovskite black phase at lower temperature [11, 15, and 16]. Hence, it's very necessary to investigate the electronic and optical characteristics of these compounds. In this work, the structural, electronic and optical properties including lattice parameter cohesive energy, formation enthalpy, band structures, optical absorption spectra and effective masses of the all-inorganic cesium lead mixed halide perovskite $CsPb(I_{1-x}Br_x)_3$ with a low symmetry and the most stable orthorhombic black phase were systemically investigated by using density functional theory.

In the present letter, we discussed the modeling and calculations of the inorganic mixed halide perovskites $\gamma$-$CsPb(I_{1-x}Br_x)_3$ for various bromine concentrations. We have examined the structural and energy stability in the low symmetry black phase of $\gamma$-$CsPb(I_{1-x}Br_x)_3$. Also, we

explored its potential use for solar cells by calculating the opto-electronic properties. Our obtained results were compared and discussed with the available data.

The calculations were carried out using the pseudo-potential plane wave (PP-PW) method as implemented in CAmbridge Serial Total Energy Package (CASTEP) [17, 18] code. The electron-ion potential described by ultra-soft pseudo potentials. Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (GGA-PBE)[19] exchange-correlation functional is used. The pseudo-atomic calculation performed for the following valence electronic configurations of atoms Cs-$5s^25p^66s^1$, Pb-$5s^25p^65d^{10}6s^26p^2$, Br-$4s^24p^5$ and I-$5s^25p^5$. For crystal structure optimization, the electronic and optical properties calculation we choose k-point mesh of 4-4-3. With plane-wave cutoff energy of 450 eV and ultra-soft pseudo potential. When we include spin orbit coupling (SOC), we used a cutoff energy 910 eV and norm-conserving pseudo potential. With maximum stress 0.02 GPa and all of the atomic positions were relaxed until the atomic forces were less than $0.02\ \text{eV}\text{\AA}^{-1}$. Furthermore, to generate different configurations, we break the symmetry and substitute Iodine by Bromine with an interval of 2 Br atoms, as a first step. Indeed, we choose the most energetically favorable position. In general, the best configuration and the fastest converged is the high symmetry ones.

We first determined the optimized lattice constants of both $CsPbI_3$ and $CsPbBr_3$ compounds and their solid solution $CsPbI_{3-x}Br_x$ by increasing the $x$ from 0 to 1 with an interval of 0.167(2 Br atoms). The unit cell of perovskite $CsPbI_3$ possesses twelve I atoms Fig.1.

The magnitude of cohesive energy increase, while the equilibrium volume decrease. In Fig. 2, we show the systematic change of lattice volume. Which is of almost quadratic functions $V=983-79x-65x^{2}$.

![](./images/812658106671562753_4.jpg)

Fig. 2: Lattice volume as a linear and quadratic function of Br content $x$

From (Table.1 and Fig.2), it's observed that, lattice volume values are decreased by iodine →Br substitution, this behavior is explained by the large ionic radius of iodine compared to bromine. Our results are in good agreement with experimental values reported in [8, 20] for $CsPbI_{3}(CsPbBr_{3})$ compounds with disagreement of the order of 3.82%(5.40%). Moreover, the calculated negative cohesive energy values indicate the structural stability of these perovskites.

We have already discussed the material stability using the cohesive energies, but cant give a direct estimation of material stability. Therefore, we calculate the formation enthalpies using the following chemical reaction : $CsX + PbX_2 = CsPbX_3$

The formation enthalpy can be calculated as follows

$$
\Delta H f \approx \Delta E f = E\left[CsPbX_{3}\right]-E\left[CsX\right]-E\left[PbX_{2}\right]
$$

The calculated formation enthalpies were presented in Fig. 3. As we can see, all compounds have negative formation enthalpies and show an increase of their magnitudes as a function of Br content. This is a proof of thermodynamic stability of the present perovskites by the Br content increase.

![](./images/812658106671562753_5.jpg)

Fig. 3: Structure and materials stability

Furthermore, we investigated the electronic band structures and deduced at $\Gamma$ point their effective masses as shown in Figure 4. In general, the band structure of the seven configurations are quite similar, with gradual increase of band gaps. Moreover, all the studied compounds are exhibiting a direct band gap semiconductor, where the conduction band minimum (CBM) and valence band maximum (VBM) are located at the $\Gamma$ point. These semiconductors, by absorption of the photons, can generate an electron-hole pair (exciton) without any energy loss by

interacting with phonons. In order to check the importance rule of the relativistic effect of the heavy metallic elements [21-25], we calculated the electronic band structures with and without the SOC, which split and downshifted of the degenerate unoccupied $Pb^{+} 2 p$ orbitals. In (Fig. 4). We present the calculated band gaps while increasing the Br content x. The energy gap observed in $CsPbI_{3}$ is 1.75 eV. It observed that when Br replaces two iodine atoms, the energy gap value increased to 1.81eV. When other iodine atoms are replaced, the band gaps are further increased. When all the 12, iodine atoms are replaced by bromine atom, the energy gap is 2.16 eV. This is due to the higher electronegativity of bromine compared to iodine. Indeed, the band gaps are likely to increase quadratically as $E_{g}=0.2 x^{2}+0.22 x+1.75$ ($E_{g}=0.11 x^{2}+0.38 x+0.68$) from 1.75 to 2.16(0.68 to 0.95) without (with) SOC respectively. The band edge almost has the same shape for all concentrations which indicate all compounds have the same effective mass. The calculated band gaps without including SOC are found to be in good agreement with the experimental data such 1.73 eV [13], 1.82 eV [1] and 2.32 eV [26] for $CsPbI_{3}$ and $CsPbBr_{3}$ respectively. This is due to the fortuitous error cancellation between overestimation from ignoring the SOC effect and the well-known DFT underestimation error.

In order to estimate the optical properties such as the photo-absorption coefficient, we first determined the frequency-dependent dielectric functions $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{2}$, the computed dielectric function are plotted and in the range of 0-20 eV in Fig. 6.

![](./images/812658106671562753_8.jpg)

Fig. 6: Calculated frequency dependent dielectric function for $CsPb(I_{1-x}Br_{x})_{3}$ perovskites.

In the imaginary part, several main peaks are observed which originate from the electronic transitions from I-p/Br-p, Pb-s in the valence band to the Pb-p state in the conduction band. The static dielectric constant gives information about the material electronic polarizability which decreases linearly with a function of Br content x as $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{x})=5.70-1.15 \mathrm{x}$ (see Fig.7).

![](./images/812658106671562753_9.jpg)

Fig 7: Static dielectric constants of mixed halide perovskites $CsPb(I_{1-x} Br_{x})_{3}$ when increasing the Br content x.

As a crucial factor for determining the light-harvesting ability of material as solar energy absorber, the photo-absorption coefficient was calculated based on the dielectric functions using the following relation. The absorption coefficients of CsPb(I1-xBrx)₃ as calculated from

$$
\alpha(\omega)=\frac{2 \omega}{c} \sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)}-\varepsilon_{1}}{2}}
$$

are presented in Fig.8.

![](./images/812658106671562753_10.jpg)

Fig 8 : Calculated photo absorption coefficients for $CsPb(I_{1-x}Br_x)_3$ perovskites

Obviously going from iodine→ bromine substitution decrease the absorption of mixed halide perovskite. In general, all compounds characterized by high absorption coefficient values compared with other conventional materials such as CdTe.

In summary, based on density functional pseudo-potential plane wave calculations, we have studied the material stability of black $CsPbI_{3-x}Br_x$ perovskites. The electronic properties and light absorption characteristics of black phase has been presented. The results show that, the iodine partial substitution is significantly enhancing the material stability. In the increase of Br content, lattice volume decrease linearly, the static dielectric constants decrease quadratically while the band gap increases quadratically with a function of Br content.
