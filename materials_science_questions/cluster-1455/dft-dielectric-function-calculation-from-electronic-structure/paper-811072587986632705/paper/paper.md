# Comprehensive SPHYB and B3LYP-DFT Studies of Two Types of Ferrocene

H. A. Rahnamaye Aliabad*[a] and Mohammad Chahkandi*[b]

**Abstract.** Structural and optoelectronic properties of ferrocene (FeC₁₀H₁₀) using various exchange correlation potentials including Spin Polarized Generalized Gradient Approximation (SPGGA), Hybrid Density Functional Theory (SPHYB-DFT), and hybrid density functional Becke3LYP are investigated. Obtained bandgap by the SPHYB-DFT and SPGGA methods show consistency with the experiment, that are indirect and direct, respectively. The cell size effects on physical properties of ferrocene studied about two types of its lattice parameters (I and II). The calculated results reveal that the cell size and the lattice parameters have a remarkable effect on optoelectronic and magnetic properties of ferrocene. However, there is no significant difference between I and II within molecular, structural and charge transitions in calculating UV/Vis spectrum. The calculated electronic absorption spectrum is in good agreement with experiment, in which two major electron-transition bands derived from d–d (n → n*) and n → π* metal to ligand. NBO analyses show that there are strong donor-acceptor interactions between central Fe atoms and cyclopentadienyl (Cp) rings that these results are in close agreement with contour plots of charge densities for prediction of the strong covalent bond between C and Fe. The optoelectronic properties of ferrocene predict that it can be efficiently used in the semiconductor devices.

## 1 Introduction

Organometallic compounds attracted attention due to the second order nonlinear optical properties with many applications in the charge transfer (CT) processes. Some of these compounds can be used in electronics devices. Ferrocene (FeC₁₀H₁₀) is an important organometallic compound with some applications in the molecular switches.[¹] This compound was discovered in 1951[²] and has revolutionized the views of chemists about how metals bind to organic π systems. It is a distinguished metallocene that shows an interesting aromaticity in conjunction with thermal stability. It is worthwhile mentioning that $M(\text{C}_{5}\text{H}_{5})_{2}$ ($M$ = V, Cr, Mn, Co, Ni) metalocens have different electronic properties.[³] Ferrocene can crystallize in three different crystal structures of monoclinic, triclinic, and orthorhombic as fully investigated by Dunitz.[⁴,⁵] Among these structures, the monoclinic structure[⁵] is the only stable form of ferrocene at room temperature. The two eclipsed ($D_{5h}$) and staggered ($D_{5d}$) conformers of ferrocene would be difficulty differentiated using the FTIR data collection in the region of 450–500 cm⁻¹.[⁶] The second symmetry ($D_{5d}$) is compatible with the molecular inversion center required by the space group ($C_{2d}^{5}=P2_{1}/a$).[⁷] The X-ray analysis of ferrocene crystal indicates that it has $D_{5d}$ symmetry and distance between the two rings is 3.32 Å.[⁸] Figure 1 shows two possible symmetries $D_{5h}$ and $D_{5d}$ for ferrocene.

## 2 Computational Details

The presented theoretical study was executed using DFT calculations by means of the Wien2k and Gaussian 09 packages.[11,12] Wien2k calculations: We have used the self-consistent scheme by solving the Kohn-Sham equations using the FPLAPW method in the framework of DFT. Spin Polarized Generalized Gradient Approximation (SP-GGA)[10,12] and Hybrid Density Functional Theory (SPHYB-DFT)[13] are used for calculation of exchange-correlation potentials. In hybrid functional calculations the exact Hartree-Fock (HF) exchange energy is mixed with an approximate DFT exchange energy functional for the calculation of accurate bandgap.

$$
E_{\text{xc }^{\text{HYB}}}=E_{\text{xc }^{\text{DFT}}}+\alpha\left(E_{\text{x }^{\text{HF}}}-E_{\text{x }^{\text{DFT}}}\right)
\tag{1}
$$

where $\alpha$ is the fraction of Hartree-Fock (HF) exchange energy. In this work, $\alpha$ is equal to 0.25. The SPHYB-DFT approximation is better than LDA and GGA. They severely underestimate the bandgaps of semiconductors.[14] However, for prediction of accurate bandgap, the expensive approximations, including the time-dependent DFT,[15] GW approximation[16,17] and modified Becke-Johnson (MBJ) potentials[18,19] are used. The muffin-tin radii for Fe, C, and H are selected in such way that no charge leakage from the core is taken place and the total energy is converged. For the wave function in the interstitial region, the plane wave cutoff value of $K_{\text{max}}$ is 3/RMT, while the charge density is Fourier expanded up to $G_{\text{max}}=20$ Ry$^{1/2}$. Meshes of 300 ($5×6×10$) k-points were used. The energy convergence criterion was set to be 0.0001 Ry.

Gaussian 09 calculations: As starting point to geometry optimization of **I** and **II**, we kept the non-hydrogen atoms fixed at the coordinates obtained from the crystal structure[9] and the positions of the hydrogen atoms were optimized. For full DFT geometry optimization calculations, the hybrid density functional Becke3LYP[20–22] was applied with the LANL2DZ basis set for the Iron atom and the triple-$\zeta$ 6-311++G (d,p) basis set for C and H atoms. Indeed, employing effective core potential (ECP) functions[23–26] such as LANL2DZ for transition metals, while using all-electron basis sets for all other non-transition-metal atoms; because of save time consuming; has become more and more popular in computations on transition-metal-containing systems.[27] The calculated vibrational frequencies indicated that the structure was stable (no imaginary frequencies). High-spin density for the Fe atom for the calculations of **I** and **II** were considered. The UV/Vis spectrum was calculated for the optimized geometry with the time-dependent DFT (TD-DFT) method. Singlet and triplet excited states were both considered; however, as expected, all excitations to triplet states where prohibited. Natural bond orbital (NBO) analyses were also performed based on the optimized geometries.

## 3 Structural Results

### 3.1 Molecular Geometry

The CIFs of **I** and **II** show that they crystallize in monoclinic system with $P2_1/a$ space group with $a = 10.530(8)$ Å, $b = 7.604(5)$ Å, $c = 5.921(4)$ Å, $\beta = 121.05(8)^\circ$ and $a = 10.443(5)$ Å, $b = 7.572(4)$ Å, $c = 5.824(4)$ and $\beta = 120.95(8)^\circ$. Selected bond lengths and angles experimentally obtained from the published structure[9] and the theoretically optimized values are collected in Table 1 and Table 2. The B3LYP/LANL2DZ/6-311++g (d, p) optimized structures of **I** and **II** show same C–C and Fe$\cdots\pi$(Cp) bond lengths as 1.426 and 1.690 Å, respectively. Since the structure in the solid state involves a huge network of intermolecular interactions (like $\pi\cdots\pi$ and C–H$\cdots\pi$), its structural parameters sometimes differ from the calculated ones since the latter are based on isolated units in the gas phase. However, there is a significant difference of 0.5 Å Fe$\cdots\pi$ (Cp) bond length between experimental and optimized structures which may related to electrostatic repulsion of the electron densities of Fe and Cp rings (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The obtained results are compared with the other theoretical results such as CCSD (T) method[28] in Table 1. It can be seen that there is a close agreement between our results and the CCSD(T) equilibrium structure of ferrocene.

## 4 Electronical Results

### 4.1 Electron Density

Electronic charge density is connected with X-ray diffraction and provides a detailed picture of the electronic distribution and bond nature in the complexes.[29,30] Magnetic anisotropy calculations on single crystals of ferrocene result dπ–pπ metal-to-ring single covalent bonding. Overlap between the d pπ orbitals of the rings and the d orbitals in iron construct two covalent bonds between the iron atom and the two nearly parallel rings. The theoretical calculations strongly support dπ–pπ covalent bonding in ferrocene.

Obtained results show that the forms of charge densities are the same for FeC₁₀H₁₀ (I) and (II), therefore we have shown just up state of FeC₁₀H₁₀ (I) in Figure 2 (in the units of e/a.u.³). Also, the values of thermo scale are presented in Table 3 with spin polarization. These values show that there are differences between two spin states (up and down). In the Morokuma scheme, $\Delta E_{\text{int}}$ consists of an electrostatic (ionic) component, a Pauli repulsion component (steric) and an orbital relaxation (covalent) in any molecular orbital (MO) model accounting for the CT ($\Delta E_{\text{CT}}$) and polarization ($\Delta E_{\text{pol}}$) interaction. $^{[37]}$ $\Delta E_{\text{CT}}$ could be estimated from the $D$-$A$ interaction in NBO analysis. The NBO describes $^{[32]}$ electron transfers between delocalized and localized MOs which closely related to the bond strength concept. The interaction amount of filling NBOs as lone pair electrons and antibonding orbitals indicates the deviation of molecule from the Lewis structure and can be used as a measure of delocalization due to $D$-$A$ interaction. The delocalization interactions can be further tracked by the second-perturbation energies $E(2)$:

$$
E(2)=-n_{\sigma} \frac{\left\langle\sigma|F| \sigma^{*}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon_{\sigma^{*}}-\varepsilon_{\sigma}}=-n_{\sigma} \frac{F_{i j}^{2}}{\Delta \varepsilon} \tag{3}
$$

where $F_{ij}$ is the Fock matrix element between the NBO i($\sigma$) and j($\sigma^{*}$), $\varepsilon_{\sigma}$ and $\varepsilon_{\sigma^{*}}$ are the energies of $\sigma$ and $\sigma^{*}$ NBOs, and $n_{\sigma}$ is the population (a lone pair in the bond). $^{[32]}$ In the other hands, decreasing energy gap between donor ($\sigma$) to acceptor ($\sigma^{*}$) orbitals is, enhancing CT from $\sigma$ to $\sigma^{*}$ is, increasing $E(2)$ is, the stronger interaction is, and vice versa. However, the summation of all influencing parameters should be considered.

The formally zero charge Fe(0) atoms have natural charges of around 0.21 in **I** and **II**. Moreover, more electronegative carbon atoms negatively charged as -0.247 which delocalized -0.1 charge density on the Cp rings (Figure S2, Supporting Information). These indications are due to CT from Fe to Cp (Figure S3, Supporting Information) resulting in the formation of the strong covalent bonds (1.690 Å) between Fe and unfilled MOs on the Cp rings (see Sections 5.2 and 5.3). No significant differences of atomic charge distributions between **I** and **II** can describe the same strength of Fe∙∙∙π (Cp) bond (see Figure S1, Supporting Information). The following $D$-$A$ interactions of Lewis model framework can operate between unit 1 and units 2 and 3 in **I** and **II**.

(i) $\sigma$ donation from the occupied d orbitals of Fe to anti-bonding orbitals of the C–C bonds.

(ii) back-donation from bonding orbitals of the C–C bonds to unfilled s and d valence orbitals of Fe.

Based on the obtained results about **I** and **II** (see Table 4), the expected major component of the charge transfer interactions is the $\sigma$ donation from the lone pairs of d orbitals of Fe to Cp rings. The results show that, indeed, this interaction is larger by an order of magnitude than the C–C → Fe donations. However, there is an intense intra-CT in Fe orbitals that results in electron density around central Fe atoms (see Table 4 and Figure S3, see Supporting Information).

### 4.3 Band Structure and Density of States
The quantities of band structure and density of states prepare significant information about the electronic properties (metal, insulator, or semiconductor), the location of non-degenerated orbitals, and gives insight into the nature of the chemical bonds. $^{[38]}$ This concept is very useful to classify compounds

into different categories with respect to different chemical and physical properties. $^{[39]}$ Energy bands are plotted along the lines with high symmetry points in the first Brillouin zone (i.e.: R$\rightarrow$ $\Gamma$$\rightarrow$ X$\rightarrow$ M$\rightarrow$$\Gamma$ directions). In the spectra of calculated band structures and density of states zero point energy, is selected to be Fermi level $E_F$ (Figure 3, Figure 4, and Figure 5). A direct correlation between the nature of bandgap for ferrocene and SPGGA results was found (see band structure plots in Figure 4 and bandgap values in Table 1). An important limitation for calculation of bandgap is that the LDA and GGA produce bandgaps significantly smaller than experiment. $^{[14,40]}$ The bottom of the conduction band and top of valence band are mainly composed of 3d-Fe and 4s-Fe orbitals, respectively. The bottom of the conduction band and the top of valence band are almost parabolic. Dangle bands at the bottom of the conduction band indicate the high conductivity describing the high mobility of electrons in these states. Obtained bandgap by SPHYB-DFT is indirect and it is close to the experiment. $^{[41]}$ W. W. Zhang et al. $^{[42]}$ have calculated the HOMO-LUMO bandgap of ferrocene by the DFT-TDDFT method. Their results indicate that the HOMO-LUMO bandgap for 3-(ferrocenylethynyl)-10-methylphenothiazine (1) and 3,7-bis(ferrocenylethynyl)-10-methylphenothiazine (2) are 4.3 and 4.1 eV, respectively. It is shown that the structural and conjugative effects play an important role in the electronic properties of the molecules.

### 4.4 Spin Magnetic Moments

Spin magnetic moments ($\mu_{\text{B}}$) for $\boldsymbol{I}$ and $\boldsymbol{II}$ are presented in Table 1. The results show that the net magnetic moments of ferrocene are due to the magnetic moments of Fe and interstitial sites. Decrease of magnetic moment with the reducing of lattice constants is due to the decrease in the volume of the unit cell. On the other hand, the cell magnetic moment of $\boldsymbol{II}$ is higher than $\boldsymbol{I}$ due to the increasing of bonding strengths in $\boldsymbol{II}$.

## 5 Optical Results

### 5.1 Dielectric Function

Dielectric function includes both the real and imaginary parts [$\varepsilon_1(\omega)$, $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$] and it is important to explain both parts together.[44] The imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ which is associated with the interband transitions is obtained from the momentum matrix elements between the occupied (HOMO) and unoccupied (LUMO) wave functions. In this code, we used the following equations for calculation of complex dielectric tensor:[45]

$$
\operatorname{Im} \varepsilon_{\alpha \beta}^{\{i n t e r\}}(\omega)=\frac{\hbar^{2} e^{2}}{\pi m^{2} \omega^{2}} \sum_{c, v} \int d k\left\langle c_{k}\left|p^{\alpha}\right| v_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle v_{k}\left|p^{\beta}\right| c_{k}\right\rangle \delta\left(\varepsilon_{c k}-\varepsilon_{v k}-\omega\right)
\tag{4}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Re} \varepsilon_{\alpha \beta}(\omega)=\delta_{\alpha \beta}+\frac{2}{\pi} P \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega^{\prime} \operatorname{Im} \varepsilon_{\alpha \beta}\left(\omega^{\prime}\right)}{\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}} d \omega^{\prime}
\tag{5}
$$

where $P$ is the principal value of the integral. The SPHYB-DFT calculations are very expensive; therefore SPGGA is used for optical calculations with high k mesh. The real parts of the dielectric function $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ for $\boldsymbol{I}$ and $\boldsymbol{II}$ are plotted in Figure 7. The figure clearly shows the anisotropic nature of the molecule in monoclinic structure. In monoclinic anisotropic molecules,

## 6 Conclusions

The structural, optoelectronic, and magnetic properties of ferrocene are investigated using the spin polarized generalized gradient approximation (SPGGA) and Hybrid Density Functional Theory SPHYB and Becke3LYP. It is shown that decreasing the lattice parameters has considerable effect on physical properties of ferrocene. However, there is no significant difference in molecular, structural and electronic properties of ferrocene molecule. Computed bandgaps for $\mathrm{FeC}_{10} \mathrm{H}_{10}$ I (II) are 4.87 (4.93) and 5.15 eV by SPHYB and B3LYP-DFT, respectively. These values are in close agreement with experiment. The electron density plots show that the most of the charge is populated around the Cp ring, and it increases with the spin polarization. Pulling of electronic cloud between Fe and C in spin down is larger than spin up state. The maximum spin magnetic moment is created in interstitial sites. The optical results show two main transition peaks between levels of $2 \mathrm{a}_{1 \mathrm{~g}} \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{e}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}{ }^{*}$ and $1 \mathrm{e}_{2 \mathrm{~g}} \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{e}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}{ }^{*}$. All optical spectra show that there is a difference between $\mathrm{FeC}_{10} \mathrm{H}_{10}$ (I) and (II) for 8-20 eV

in the z direction. The present optical spectra for ferrocene predict the usefulness in optical and optoelectronic devices.

NBO analyses suggest that the bands in the electronic spectrum are mainly assigned to $n \to n^*$ and $n \to \pi^*$ d-d (Fe) and MLCT transitions. The predicted electronic spectrum in ferrocene by the TD-DFT method has two major bands whose energies compare favorably to the experimental peaks but with some blue shifts.
