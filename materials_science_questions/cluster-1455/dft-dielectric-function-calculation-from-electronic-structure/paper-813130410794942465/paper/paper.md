## ABSTRACT

The electronic properties, such as the layer-dependent behavior of the band structure, band gap, work function alignment and dielectric properties of the few-layer GeSe are systematically investigated via gradient-corrected density functional theory computations, inspired by the experimentally observation of two-dimension materials such as graphene, phosphorene, MoS₂ and BN. The results indicate that the few-layer GeSe presents a robust direct band gap, which decreases with increasing the thickness from bilayer (1.15 eV) to six-layer (1.00 eV) around the X point. Furthermore, the work function increases rapidly from monolayer (4.44 eV) to trilayer (4.95 eV). The robust direct band gap characteristics and the layer-dependent band gap suggest that the few-layer GeSe is a promising material for efficient solar energy harvesting applications. The layer dependence of the GeSe work function offers a practical route to tune the Schottky barrier in GeSe based electronic devices. Our results provide new insights on utilizing the layer-controlled band gap of the atomic layers of GeSe.

## 1. Introduction

Recently, significant progresses of two-dimensional (2D) materials in various fascinating applications are witnessed [1–11] due to their unique electronic, optical and mechanical properties, which are different from their bulk counterparts [12]. Nevertheless, the famous pristine graphene is not suitable for high-performance field effect transistors due to its zero band gap characteristic which significantly limits the on/off current ratio of the devices. Therefore, it is an ongoing subject of intensive active research to seek other 2D materials that possess properties complementary to graphene. For instance, previous studies have presented that phosphorene [13], MoS₂or the other transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) family members are promising candidates due to their presence of an intrinsic band gap³ and other intriguing electronic properties distinctive from grapheme [14,15].

In recent years, the lead chalcogenide nanocrystals have been widely studied in a variety of photovoltaic devices, which brings tremendous attentions to this particular group of materials. Bulk group-IV monochalcogenides MX ( M = Ge, Sn; X = S, Se) are typically highly stable and abundant on earth [16]. Among them, the eco-friendly germanium monochalcogenides seem to be promising candidates for photovoltaic applications in order to replace the lead chalcogenide containing the toxic lead element. Bulk GeSe is a p-type semiconductor with closely placed direct and indirect band gaps ($E_g=1.1 - 1.2$ eV) that match well with the solar spectrum. In addition to photovoltaics, GeSe also has shown great potentials in other applications, such as photodetector, thermoelectricity, and resistive switching etc [17–20]. The GeSe nanostructures, e.g., nanorods, nanosheets and nanobelts, have been synthesized successfully in experiments by various methods and applied in fabricating a variety of nano-devices [18–29]. Despite of all the intriguing properties of bulk GeSe, there has been very limited amount of works on 2D few-layer GeSe.

In this work, density functional theory (DFT) computations are performed to investigate the electronic properties of monolayer and few-layer GeSe, focusing on their band structure, electronic density of states, work function and dielectric properties. We show that the few-layer GeSe exhibits a direct band gap with the number of layer from one to six by calculating the energy band structure. In addition, GeSe monolayer is found to possess a direct band gap of 1.15 eV. A tunable work function up to 0.5 eV can be achieved by increasing the thickness from monolayer (1 L) to six layers (6 L). The results suggest that the few-layer GeSe can be a promising candidate for various applications in optoelectronics, photovoltaics and thermoelectricity by engineering their thickness-dependent

## 2. Theoretical methods

The atomic structure and the stacking order of the few-layer GeSe are shown in Fig. 1. In the present study, the number of the few-layer GeSe is denoted by n L, where n is the number of the monolayer GeSe (from 1 L to 6 L). Structural relaxation and electronic calculations are carried out by first-principles calculations based on DFT [30] as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [31]. The DFT exchange-correlation contribution is evaluated using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [32]. Ultrasoft pseudopotentials are used for germanium and selenium atoms. In the direction perpendicular to the GeSe sheet, a vacuum layer of $30\ \mathring{A}$ in the z-direction is applied to avoid the interaction between layers caused by the periodic boundary condition (PBC), which is used to calculate the work function(WF) precisely. For the geometry optimization, the Brillouin zone integration is sampled by a $7\times7\times1$ k-grid mesh for the bulk GeSe and the few-layer GeSe, respectively. The charge density is calculated with a plane-wave cutoff of 500 eV and a $21\times21\times1$ Monkhorst Pack grid [33]. We adopted the DFT+D method with the Grimme vdW correction to include van der Waals interaction in few-layer GeSe structures. All the structures are relaxed until that the remaining force acting on each atom is no more than $0.005\ \text{eV}/\mathring{A}$. The self-consistent field (SCF) calculations are carried out with a convergence criterion of $10^{-5}$ a.u. on the total energy and electronic computations to ensure the accuracy of the results.

## 3. Result and discussion

The thermodynamically most favorable structures of GeSe are presented in Fig. 1(a). The coordination number of each Ge and Se atom are four, and Ge covalently bonded with Se forming a puckered two dimensional honeycomb structure, which is different from the flatness of graphene. In analogy to phosphorene, we define the armchair and zigzag edge of the monolayer GeSe in Fig. 1(b). Similar to the structure of phosphorene, the puckered layer is consisted of top and bottom ridges along the zigzag direction, and the two ridges are connected with Ge-Se bonds forming graphene-like armchair structure along the x direction. The few-layer GeSe have the AB stacking feature with a shift of b/2 between adjacent layers along the y direction, as shown in Fig. 1(b). The layer of GeSe is held together by van der Waals forces, which is similar to graphite.

Moreover, for the bulk and few-layer GeSe, our calculations begin with determining the optimized geometry. The optimized lattice parameters for the 1 L is $\text{a}=4.27\ \mathring{A}$ and $\text{b}=3.98\ \mathring{A}$, which are close to the previous work [34]. To further evaluate the stability of the GeSe monolayer with respect to their bulk forms, we calculate the formation energy $\Delta E$ of monolayer GeSe, which is determined by the energy difference between the monolayer and bulk compounds. The formation energy is defined as $\Delta E = E_{monolayer}/N_{monolayer} - E_{bulk}/N_{bulk}$, where $E_{monolayer}$ and $E_{bulk}$ are the total energies of a monolayer GeSe and a bulk GeSe, and $N_{monolayer}$ and $N_{bulk}$ are the corresponding total atom numbers of the two systems, respectively. Our calculated formation energy of 88 meV of the GeSe monolayer is consistent with the weak electrostatic and van der Waals (vdW) interactions, which is comparable to those of graphite and BN [36,37], suggesting that the few-layer GeSe is more stable than chalcogenides.

The band structures of bulk GeSe and few-layer GeSe are presented in the Fig. 2. To reveal the effects of layer interactions on the electronic properties of GeSe, the band structures of GeSe are computed with the layers from 1 L to 6 L. The band structure of bulk GeSe is firstly computed to determine that the energy change of GeSe with different layers. The results for band structure of bulk GeSe are shown in Fig. 2(a). The band gap of the few-layer GeSe is higher than that of bulk GeSe. For few-layer GeSe (Fig. 2c), the bottom of the conduction band and the top of valence band are both at X point, indicating a direct band gap structure. The bilayer GeSe has the exact same band gap of 1.15 eV as the monolayer GeSe. To obtain an accurate band gap value, we performed hybrid functional calculations (HSE06) for the GeSe monolayer, which holds a direct band gap of 1.75 eV. Due to the quantum confinement effect, the band gap decreases with the increasing thickness, from 2 L (1.15 eV) to 6 L (1.00 eV). The topology of the band structure for other few-layer GeSe is similar to that of the 1 L case. There is only a 0.15 eV decrease for band gap, which is quite different from the phosphorene [38]. There is a clear asymmetric shape of the band dispersion around the point along - X and - Y branches, suggesting a large anisotropic behavior of electronic properties of few-layer GeSe (similar to phosphorus), as compared to the isotropic electronic structures of graphene, BN and $MoS_2$ layers.

In order to investigate the contribution of different orbitals to the electronic states, we carried out the calculations of total density of states (DOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) densities of states for the few-layer GeSe, and the results are shown in Fig. 3. Fermi levels of all the few-layer GeSe are set at 0 eV and are indicated by the black vertical dashed lines. It can be observed from the PDOS of the few-layer GeSe that the states near the Fermi level are attributed to both S and P orbitals. However, the contributions of the P orbitals to the total DOS are much higher than that from S orbitals. Due to the structure of fewer layers GeSe don't changed and the stack order maintain uniformly, the band structures of few-layers GeSe remain unchanged except band gap(See in Fig. 2). Therefore, when the thickness of GeSe increases, the DOS remains almost unchanged, indicating that the electronic properties has a weak dependence on the number of layer.

We have calculated the work function by comparing the Fermi energy with the electrostatic potential of vacuum level [39,40]. Fig. 4(a) shows that the various work function with the number of layers. The work function of the few-layer GeSe increases as the number of layers increases from 1 L to 3 L. Fig. 4(b) presents the electrostatic potential of 6 L GeSe and its atomic structure. The band gap value of 1 L GeSe is 1.146 eV, which is less than that of 2 L GeSe (1.153 eV). From 2 L to 6 L, it is obvious that band gap decreases when the number of GeSe increases, which is consistent with the aforementioned results. This notable change in the work function with the number of layers suggests the possibility to tune the contact resistance by controlling the number of GeSe layers and selecting suitable contact metal.

The study of the optical properties is of vital importance for the optoelectronic related applications. Therefore, we also calculate the dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+\mathrm{i} \varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ to investigate whether the number of layer can affect the dielectric properties of few-layer GeSe. The imaginary part $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ can be obtained by calculating the momentum matrix elements between the occupied and unoccupied wave functions with selection rules. Then, The real part $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ of dielectric function for different layers of GeSe is calculated by integrating $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ in k space with the standard tetrahedron-method which is based on the Kramer-Kronig relationship. Fig. 5 illustrates that the real part $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ seems to have a rather different frequency dependence for GeSe with the odd number and even number of layers. The static dielectric constants (at 0 eV) are 3.28, 4.08 and 4.38 for the 1 L, 3 L and 5 L, respectively. Obviously, they increase with increasing the number of layer, which can be attributed to the interlayer van der Waals force. In the low energy region, the real parts of the dielectric functions increase with photon energy initially, reaching the maximum values of 6.02, 8.39 and 9.55 at 2.23 eV, 2.58 eV and 3.11 eV, and then decrease to the minimum values of -0.66, -1.51 and -2.21 at 3.65 eV, 6.76 eV, and 5.40 eV. In contrast to the odd number layer, the dielectric properties of even number layer GeSe have another tendency. In the Fig. 5(b), it was found that there is a drop near the 0 eV, which is contrary to those of odd layers. For the even number layer of GeSe, there are three major peaks contrast to the odd number layer of GeSe with just one major peak. In yz and zx directions, the curves of all kind of the few-layer GeSe have the same tendency, respectively.

## 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have investigated the electronic properties of few-layer GeSe via first-principles calculations. The results demonstrate that the band gap, work function and dielectric function are all dependent on the number of layers. The band structure of bulk GeSe possesses an indirect gap, whereas the ribbon shows a direct band gap for few-layer GeSe (1 L to 6 L). For the odd number of few-layer GeSe (at 0 eV), the static dielectric constants increase with increasing the number of layer. The layer number-dependent band alignment and work function offer a practical route to tune the Schottky barrier, which can lead to more efficient carrier injection across the contact and facilitate the subsequent carrier transport.
