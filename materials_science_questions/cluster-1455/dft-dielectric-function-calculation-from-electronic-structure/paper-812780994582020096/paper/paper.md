First principle characterization of structural, electronic, mechanical, thermodynamic, linear and nonlinear optical properties of zinc-blende InAs, InSb and their ${\text{InAs}}_{\text{x}}{\text{Sb}}_{1\text{-x}}$ ternary alloys

Y. Asadi, Z. Nourbakhsh*
*Department of Physics, Faculty of Sciences, University of Isfahan, Isfahan, Iran
The corresponding author: E-mail address: z.nourbakhsh@sci.ui.ac.ir
Tel: +983137934725; Fax: +983137932723

The structural and electronic properties of ${\text{InAs}}_{\text{x}}{\text{Sb}}_{1\text{-x}}$ ternary alloys are studied using plane-wave pseudopotential method based on the density functional theory. The Born effective charge, elastic constants, phonon dispersion curves, linear and nonlinear optical properties of these compounds are calculated using density functional perturbation theory. Based on the calculated phonon frequency, the specific heat at constant volume and entropy are obtained within the harmonic approximation. Moreover, due to the density functional theory band gap problem, the band gap correction is performed within the one-shot GW approximations. The distinguished role of In 4d states on the electronic energy band gap and nonlinear properties of these alloys are extensively explored by the comparison between the calculated results of Hartwigsen-Goedecker-Hutter and Trouiller-Martins-type pseudopotentials. The calculated results show that ${\text{InAs}}_{\text{x}}{\text{Sb}}_{1\text{-x}}$ alloys are mechanically stable and can be a good candidate for high-performance nonlinear optical material. The calculated results of InAs and InSb compounds are in acceptable agreement with available theoretical and experimental results, so the calculated results of ${\text{InAs}}_{\text{x}}{\text{Sb}}_{1\text{-x}}$ alloys will be helpful for future experimental and theoretical investigations.

Keywords: ${\text{InAs}}_{\text{x}}{\text{Sb}}_{1\text{-x}}$; Density functional theory; Phonon modes; Nonlinear properties; GW approximation

### 1. Introduction

The III–V semiconductors have attracted much attention because of their successful applications in electronic and optoelectronic devices technology. Among these semiconductors,

Indium compounds have received a lot of interest due to their special physical properties which make them to be particularly worth studying. InSb is used in mid-infrared optoelectronics [1] and InAs has an important role in the electron quantum well material for the long-wavelength optoelectronic devices [2].

Lockwood et. al. investigated the optical phonon frequencies and damping of InAs and InSb by means of the oblique incidence infrared spectroscopy [3]. They showed that InAs and InSb compounds have the transverse (longitudinal) optical phonon frequency of 218.0 and 179.1 $cm^{-1}$ (240.0 and 190.4 $cm^{-1}$) at room temperature respectively. Furthermore, Massidda. et al. studied the structural and electronic properties of InAs and InSb compounds using the full-potential linear augmented-plane-wave method and ab initio norm conserving pseudopotentials [4]. Although, they investigated the role of In 4d states on the structural and electronic properties of these compounds, they could not truly predict the semiconductor behavior of these compounds and their electronic energy band structures results show metallic character of InAs and InSb compounds. Corso et. al. investigated the static and dynamic nonlinear optical susceptibilities of InAs and InSb compounds based on the density functional theory [DFT] [5]. Huang and Ching studied the linear optical dielectric functions of InAs and InSb cubic semiconductors using the first-principles orthogonalized linear-combination-of-atomic-orbitals framework within the local density approximation (LDA) [6]. Their results showed that InAs and InSb compounds have the static dielectric function of 10.02 and 13.51 respectively. Nemiri et. al. calculated the heat capacity and entropy of InAs semiconductor over a wide range of pressures at T = 100 K and 300 K using the full potential-linearized augmented plane wave method based on the density functional theory [7]. At room temperature, they obtained 48.11 and 75.22 $\frac{J}{mol.K}$ for the specific heat at constant volume and entropy of InAs compound respectively.

Although some physical properties of InAs and InSb have already been reported, the present work is also improving the existing theoretical studies on these compounds and is focused on some physical properties of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$. The theoretical investigations including structural, electronic, elastic, mechanical, thermodynamic, linear and nonlinear optical properties of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys have not been studied by others. Therefore, it is interesting to study the physical properties of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys. In the present work, InAs and InSb cubic compounds with different physical properties are combined with the motivation of finding promising $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys with improved intermediate physical properties.

The phonon frequencies, high frequency dielectric and Born effective charge tensor components of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys are analyzed as the first goal of the present work. The mechanical and thermodynamic properties of materials are of particular interest for their industrial applications. These properties determine the specific behaviors of materials when they are subjected to serious constraints such as stress and temperature. The elastic properties describe the response of the material to an applied stress [8, 9]. So, the second aim of this work is to focus on the theoretical calculations of the compositional dependence of mechanical and thermodynamic properties of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys under stress and temperature. The elastic constants $\text{C}_{11}$, $\text{C}_{12}$ and $\text{C}_{44}$, Cauchy pressure $( \text{C}_{12} - \text{C}_{44})$, isotropic bulk modulus, shear modulus, Pugh criteria, specific heat at constant volume $C_v$ and the entropy $S$ of these alloys are calculated.

Finally, the last purpose of the present study is to investigate the linear and nonlinear optical properties of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys. In linear manner, it focuses on the effect of electron-hole interaction on the dielectric function within the Bethe-Salpeter approach (BS). The nonlinear optical properties play an important role in optoelectronics, laser science, optical signal

processing and optical computing applications [5]. The nonlinear optical susceptibilities can describe several physical phenomena such as optical rectification, wave mixing and the Kerr effect relies on the knowledge of the nonlinear optical susceptibilities [5]. Corso et. al. [5] showed that In 4d states have significant effect on nonlinear optical susceptibility of InSb compound. So that the inclusion of In 4d states in the calculations changes the nonlinear optical susceptibility of InSb compound from 407 to 957 pm/V. So, the influence of In 4d states on the nonlinear optical properties will be examined below. Also, considering In 4d states could improve the electronic energy band gap.

All calculations of the present work are based on the virtual crystal approximation (VCA) method [10, 11] and are performed using the ABINIT software [12] which employs the plane-wave pseudopotential method based on the density functional theory (DFT). This paper is organized as follows: The computational method is presented in Sec. 2. The structural and electronic calculations are summarized in Sec. 3. The results of linear response properties such as mechanical characteristics, vibrational and thermodynamic properties are discussed in Sec. 4. In Sec. 5, the nonlinear optical properties are analyzed, and finally the conclusions are given in Sec. 6.

## 2. computational method

In the present work the exchange-correlation potential is treated within the local density approximation (LDA) in the formulation of the Teter fit to Ceperly-Alder data [22]. The norm-conserving Hartwigsen-Goedecker-Hutter pseudopotential (HGH) [14] is used for replacing the core electrons in the atoms. So, the $4s^24p^3$ electrons of As, the $5s^25p^1$ electrons of In and the $5s^25p^3$ electrons of Sb are treated explicitly. For including In 4d states in the computation of nonlinear optical properties and improving the energy band gap, the Trouiller-Martins-type (FHI)

[13] pseudopotential is also used. The Trouiller-Martins-type pseudopotential considers In 4d states more appropriately [43]. So, it leads to highly accurate calculations. In all the present work except in phonon frequency and quasiparticle corrections for electronic energy band gap, the integrals over the Brillouin zone are performed with a $12 \times 12 \times 12$ Monkhorst-Pack grid [15]. For expanding the electronic wave functions in a basis set of plane waves, a kinetic energy cutoff of 45 (in HGH pseudopotential) and 55 Ha (in FHI pseudopotential) is used to achieve the desired level of convergence in all calculations.

The structural geometry optimization and the electronic band structure of $InAs_xSb_{1-x}$ ternary alloys are calculated within DFT. For optimizing the crystalline structure of these alloys to their lowest energy configuration using the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno minimization [16], the tolerance criteria of the force difference between two consecutive steps in the self-consistent field cycles and the maximum component of force acting on any atomic positions are chosen as $5 \times 10^{-6}$ and $5 \times 10^{-5}$ H/Bohr respectively. Based on the ground state results, the quasiparticle electronic energy band gap of these ternary alloys are calculated within the so-called one-shot GW or $G_0W_0$ approximation of the quasiparticle equation [17]. It is based on the Green's function method and quasiparticle concept to describe the electronic excited-state properties [12]. The cutoff energies of 20 Ha and 22 Ha are used for the screening and for the self-energy matrix components respectively. A mesh of $4 \times 4 \times 4$ Monkhorst-Pack sampling of the Brillouin zone is employed in this calculation.

The linear response properties (static $\varepsilon(0)$ and high-frequency $\varepsilon(\infty)$ dielectric constants, Born effective charge tensor, phonon frequencies and elastic constants) and nonlinear optical properties of these alloys are obtained within density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) [18-

21]. In the calculation of phonon frequencies and thermal properties, a mesh of $4 \times 4 \times 4$ Monkhorst-Pack grid is also used to sample the Brillouin zone (BZ).

Virtual crystal approximation (VCA) [25] is used for calculating the $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys pseudopotential. Using the following relation, the ionic pseudopotential of As and Sb are combined to construct the $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys pseudopotential:

$$
V_{\mathrm{VCA}}=x V_{\mathrm{As}}+(1-x) V_{\mathrm{Sb}} \tag{1}
$$

### 3. Structural and electronic properties

InAs and InSb semiconductors crystallize in the zinc-blende structure with space group F-43m. There are two atoms in the primitive unit cell of these compounds. The In and As (Sb) atoms occupy the (0, 0, 0) and (0.25, 0.25, 0.25) sites respectively. The calculated lattice parameter of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys is listed in Table 1. As can be seen from Table 1, the calculated results are also compared with the other theoretical and experimental data [27-28]. The calculated results within both methods, local density approximation with two Hartwigsen-Goedecker-Hutter and Trouiller-Martins-type pseudopotentials (hereafter, HGH.LDA and FHI.LDA respectively), are in acceptable agreement with the available experimental results.

The lattice parameters of alloys such as $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$, according to the vegard's law [30] is usually linear combination of two forming binary alloy's lattice parameters. It is expected that the lattice parameters of these alloys obey the following relation:

$$
\mathrm{a}(\mathrm{x})=\mathrm{x} \ \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{InAs}}+(1-\mathrm{x}) \ \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{InSb}} \tag{2}
$$

where x represents the concentration of As component, a(x) is the lattice parameter of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ alloys, $\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{InAs}}$ and $\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{InSb}}$ are the equilibrium lattice parameters of InAs and InSb binary compounds respectively. Fig. 1 displays the lattice parameters of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ as a function of As concentration x. It shows that the lattice parameter of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys decreases almost

linearly with increasing As concentration (x) and the deviation of the calculated lattice parameters from Vegard's law is less than 0.74 and 1.21% within HGH.LDA and FHI.LDA approaches respectively. Since the ionic radius of As is smaller than that of Sb, the lattice parameter of InAsₓSb₁₋ₓ ternary alloys decreases with increasing As concentration x.

The energy band gap of InAsₓSb₁₋ₓ ternary alloys is calculated using relaxed lattice parameters within local density approximation and the one-shot GW approximation (G₀W₀), and the results are also presented in Table 1. As can be seen, the calculated energy band gap of InSb and InAs compounds within local density approximation plus norm-conserving Hartwigsen-Goedecker-Hutter pseudopotential (HGH.LDA) is in agreement and in disagreement with the experimental results respectively. To improve the energy band gap of these alloys, the one-shot GW approximation using the norm-conserving Hartwigsen-Goedecker-Hutter pseudopotential (HGH.G₀W₀) [12] is also used. The HGH.G₀W₀ results are also shown in Table 1. The calculated results for both InAs and InSb compounds are in disagreement with the experimental results. So, the one-shot GW approximation using Troullier-Martins type pseudopotential is inappropriate for energy band gap calculation of these alloys. In the next step, to improve the energy band gap of InAs and InSb compounds, In 4d states are included in the computations (FHI.G₀W₀). The calculated energy band gap of these alloys within FHI.G₀W₀ is also shown in Table 1. This approach improves the energy band gap of InAs compound. The disagreement between FHI.G₀W₀ and experimental energy band gap of InSb compound may be related to the spin-orbit coupling effect that not included in the present work.

The calculated energy band gap of these alloys within HGH.LDA, HGH.G₀W₀ and FHI.G₀W₀ is depicted in Fig 1. It shows that the variation of the calculated energy band gap of InAsₓSb₁₋ₓ ternary alloys within both HGH.G₀W₀ and FHI.G₀W₀ is not linear with respect to As

## 4. Linear response properties

### 4.1. Born effective charge, phonon frequencies and optic constants

Born effective charge describes the behavior of material under lattice vibration and determines the influence of the macroscopic electric field on the phonon frequencies. So, it determines the polarization induced by the atom displacement at vanishing electric field [34].

Based on the acoustic sum rule [33], the components of Born effective charge tensor z* have the following relation:

$$
\sum_{k} z_{\alpha \beta}^{*}(k)=0 \tag{3}
$$

where $z_{\alpha \beta}^{*}(k)$ is the Born effective charge of atom k in the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ directions. $z^{*}$ tensor of InAsₓSb₁₋ₓ ternary alloys has only one independent component, so the effective charge of the cation and anion are equal with opposite sign. The Born effective charge ⟨z*⟩ of InSb, InAs and InAsₓSb₁₋ₓ alloys is calculated based on DFPT using HGH. LDA and the calculated results are presented in Table 2. As can be seen from Table 2, the effective charge of InAsₓSb₁₋ₓ ternary alloys is smaller than the corresponding value of In, As and Sb atoms and increases linearly with As concentration increasing. So, it does not show any strong hybridization interaction among atoms of these compounds.

The material's phonon density of states determines the distribution of lattice vibrational frequencies in the crystal structure. In three dimensions, the phonon density of states at low and high frequencies is determined by the acoustic and optic modes respectively. The vibration


modes of these atoms are experimentally studied by infrared and Raman spectroscopy [3]. In the present work, the dynamical matrices are calculated on a 4×4×4 grid of $q$ points. In order to calculate the phonon dispersion curves along the high symmetry lines of the Brillouin zone, the real-space force constants are then found using the Fourier transformation of the dynamical matrices. The acoustic

sum rule is also applied. It forces the three acoustic phonon frequencies at the $\Gamma$ symmetry point to become zero.

The phonon dispersion curves of InAs, InSb and their $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys are calculated based on DFPT using HGH. LDA and displayed in Fig. 3. It shows that all phonon branches in the whole Brillouin zone have positive frequency values. So, these compounds have dynamical stability at zero pressure. As can be seen from Fig. 3, there is a splitting between the highest transverse acoustic (TA) mode and the longitudinal acoustic (LA) mode of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys at W symmetry point. There is also a gap between the acoustical and optical phonon branches of InAs compound while, this gap disappears in $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys for x= 0.0, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75.

Fig. 3 shows a nonvanishing splitting between the transverse optical (TO) and longitudinal optical (LO) phonon dispersion curves at $\Gamma$ symmetry point. This splitting can be related to the partial ionic bonding of these alloys. It also shows these ternary alloys are polar and anisotropic to some extent.

The calculated longitudinal ($\omega_{\text{LO}}$) and transverse ($\omega_{\text{TO}}$) optical phonon frequencies at $\Gamma$ symmetry point are also presented in Table 3. The calculated results of InAs and InSb compounds are in acceptable agreement with available theoretical and experimental results [3]. The calculated optical phonon frequencies at $\Gamma$ symmetry point increase almost linearly by the

increase of As concentration. This trend is due to the decreasing the lattice parameter by the increase of As concentration. This is the cause of the fact that a longer bond length gives less force constants and this leads to smaller vibrational frequencies.

Macroscopic dielectric constant determines the response of matter to the electromagnetic radiation. The static $\varepsilon(0)$ and high-frequency $\varepsilon(\infty)$ dielectric constants are important physical quantity for semiconductors. Using the previous calculated results of longitudinal and transverse optical phonon frequencies at $\Gamma$ symmetry point, the high-frequency dielectric constant $\varepsilon(\infty)$ is obtained using Lydane-Sachs-Teller relation [3]:

$$
\varepsilon(\infty)=\varepsilon(0) \frac{\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}^{2}}{\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}^{2}} \tag{4}
$$

The present work focuses on the variation of these important physical quantity with respect to As concentration. Using the calculated static dielectric constant based on the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT), the high-frequency dielectric constant of $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}}$ ternary alloys are obtained and are also presented in Table 2. There is a good agreement between these calculated results and the available experimental and other theoretical results. The static and high-frequency dielectric constant of $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}}$ ternary alloys decrease going from x=0 to x=1.

The high frequency dielectric constant of InAs is smaller than that of InSb compound. It shows that the polarization of "electron cloud" around As anion is smaller than the corresponding value around the Sb anion [8]. According to Penn model [36], the static dielectric constant of InSb with smaller energy band gap is larger than the corresponding value of InAs.

### 4.2. Elastic properties
The elastic constants describe the mechanical behavior of a material and they can also predict the effect of strain on the electronic properties. The mechanical quantities such as the isotropic bulk modulus, shear modulus and Pugh criteria [38, 39] can determine the anisotropy, ductility,

stability, brittleness and stiffness behavior of a material. Materials with cubic symmetry such as $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys have three independent elastic constants and can be obtained using the following relation [42]:

$$
\mathrm{U}=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{E}}{\mathrm{V}_{0}}=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{\mathrm{i}}^{\mathrm{n}} \sum_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{m}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{ij}} \mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{i}} \mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{j}} \tag{6}
$$

where $\Delta \mathrm{E}$, $\mathrm{V}_{0}$ and $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{ij}}$ are the difference between the deformed and initial total energy, equilibrium unit cell volume and elastic constant respectively.

Using the calculated total energy of these alloys as a function of unit cell volume, three independent elastic constants of these alloys are calculated. The calculated results are presented and compared with the available theoretical and experimental results in Table 4. As can be seen from Table 4, the calculated results are in acceptable agreement with available theoretical and experimental results [40, 41]. The calculated elastic constants of these alloys are positive and satisfy the Born stability criteria, $\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)>0$ and $\left(C_{11}+2 C_{12}\right)>0$ [24]. So, $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ alloys with x=0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 are mechanically stable. All the elastic constants increase almost linearly with increasing As concentration. $\mathrm{C}_{11}$ describes the elastic stiffness respect to (1 0 0) uniaxial pressure. Increase of the $\mathrm{C}_{11}$ coefficient of these alloys with increasing As concentration exhibits more resistance under uniaxial compression. $\mathrm{C}_{12}$ and $\mathrm{C}_{44}$ show shear deformation in (1 0 0) plane along [1 0 0] direction and (0 1 0) plane along [0 0 1] direction respectively. The variation of $\mathrm{C}_{12}$ and $\mathrm{C}_{44}$ of these alloys versus As concentration shows the increasing of shear stress as the x increase. The shear stress of these alloys in (1 0 0) plane along [1 0 0] direction is larger than (0 1 0) plane along [0 0 1] direction.

The Cauchy pressure $\mathrm{C}_{12}$–$\mathrm{C}_{44}$ describes the atomic bonding of materials [29]. The calculated results of Table 4 show the positive value of Cauchy's pressure for $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys. It

shows that the alloys have the ionic bonding nature and it increases by increasing As concentration.

The Pugh's ratio (B/G) can be used to determine the brittle and ductile nature of a material [24]. B and G as isotropic bulk modulus and shear modulus are obtained through the following relations [24]:

$$
B=(C_{11}+2C_{12})/3 \tag{7}
$$

$$
G=\frac{G_R + G_V}{2} \tag{8}
$$

which $G_R$ and $G_V$ can be calculated by these relations [25]:

$$
G_R=5\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)C_{44}\bigg/\left[4\ C_{44}+3\left(C_{11}-C_{12}\right)\right] \tag{9}
$$

$$
G_V=(C_{11}-C_{12}+3\ C_{44})/5 \tag{10}
$$

The calculated isotropic bulk modulus of these alloys is also presented in Table 4. The calculated results show that the isotropic bulk modulus of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys increases with increase of As composition. The threshold value of Pugh's ratio separates brittle and ductile materials. Materials with B/G larger and smaller than 1.75 are ductile and brittle materials respectively. The Pugh's ratio of these alloys is calculated and shown in Table 4. Table 4 shows that Pugh's ratio of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys is larger than 1.75, so these alloys are ductile materials.

### 4.3. Thermal properties

Using the calculated phonon density of states, the thermodynamic properties of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys are calculated within the harmonic approximation [8]. In this approximation, the specific heat at constant volume $\boldsymbol{C_v}$ and entropy $\boldsymbol{S}$ can be calculated using the following relations [37]:

$$
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{v}}=3 \mathrm{nNk}_{\mathrm{B}} \int_{0}^{\omega_{\mathrm{L}}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{h} \omega}{4 \pi \mathrm{k}_{\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{T}}\right)^{2} \csch^{2}\left(\operatorname{coth}\left\{\frac{h \omega}{4 \pi k_{B} T}\right\}\right) \mathrm{g}(\omega) \mathrm{d} \omega,
\tag{11}
$$

$$
S=3 n N k_{B} \int_{0}^{\omega_{L}}\left[\frac{h \omega}{4 \pi k_{B} T} \operatorname{coth} \frac{h \omega}{4 \pi k_{B} T}-\ln \left\{2 \sinh \frac{h \omega}{4 \pi k_{B} T}\right\}\right] g(\omega) d \omega,
\tag{12}
$$

where $k_{B}$ and $\mathrm{g}(\omega)$ are the Boltzmann constant and phonon density of states respectively.

The specific heat at constant volume $\mathrm{C}_{v}$ and the entropy $\mathrm{S}$ of $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}}$ ternary alloys as a function of temperature are illustrated in Fig. 4. As can be seen from Fig. 4, the behavior of $C_{v}$ and $\mathrm{S}$ of these alloys is similar. $C_{v}$ at high temperature tends to the classical Dulong-Petit limit of $C_{v}=3 \mathrm{nkB}$ [8]. As Debye model [8], the $C_{v}$ at sufficiently low temperature is proportional to $\mathrm{T}^{3}$ arising from the acoustic modes. The entropy increases sharply with increasing temperature due to increase of the contribution of vibrational to the entropy. The specific heat at constant volume decreases by increasing As concentration. Similar to the specific heat at constant volume, over the whole range of temperature, the value of the entropy decreases with the increase of the As concentration. The calculated results of heat capacity and entropy of $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-}$ ${ }_{\mathrm{x}}$ ternary alloys at room temperature are compared with available others results [7] in Table 3.

### 5. The nonlinear optical properties

The nonlinear optical constants have important applications in optoelectronics, laser science, optical signal processing and optical computing [5]. It must be used to describe several physical phenomena such as optical rectification, wave mixing and the Kerr effect relies on the knowledge of the nonlinear optical susceptibilities [5].

Second order nonlinear effects such as the second harmonic generation (SHG), sum frequency generation (SFG), difference frequency generation (DFG), optic parametric generation (OPG) and linear electro-optic (LEO) effect are related to the second-order optical susceptibility.


Optical susceptibility appears when the polarization decomposes into the electric field $ \varepsilon_j $ using the Taylor expansion as follow [26]:

$$
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\mathrm{s}}+\sum_{\mathrm{j}} \chi_{\mathrm{ij}}^{(1)} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{j}}+\sum_{\mathrm{j}} \chi_{\mathrm{ijk}}^{(2)} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{j}} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{k}}+...,
\tag{13}
$$

where $ \mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\mathrm{s}} $, $ \chi_{\mathrm{ij}}^{(1)} $ and $ \chi_{\mathrm{ijk}}^{(2)} $ are the spontaneous polarization, linear dielectric susceptibility and second-order optical susceptibility tensor respectively [26]. The nonlinear optical susceptibility tensor of materials with cubic symmetry has one independent component $ \mathrm{d}_{36} $ [26]:

$$
d_{i j k}=d_{36}\left|\epsilon_{i j k}\right|,
\tag{14}
$$

where $ \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i j k} $ is the Levy-Civita tensor. So InAs$_{\text{x}}$Sb$_{1-\text{x}}$ ternary alloys with cubic symmetry have one independent nonlinear optical susceptibility component. The calculated $ d_{36} $ of InAs$_{\text{x}}$Sb$_{1-\text{x}}$ ternary alloys within density functional perturbation theory beyond the simple Sum-Over-State approximation using HGH.LDA and FHI.LDA approaches are presented and compared with the available experimental and theoretical results [5] in Table 5. The calculated results within FHI.LDA compare to HGH.LDA are in better agreement with the experimental results [5]. As it turns out from the calculated results, InAs$_{\text{x}}$Sb$_{1-\text{x}}$ ternary alloys have large nonlinear optical susceptibility component and so they can be important materials in nonlinear optics. Fig. 5 shows In 4d states have distinguished effect on the calculated $ d_{36} $ of these alloys. It also shows that the calculated $ d_{36} $ increases up to x=0.5, then decreased. InAs$_{0.5}$Sb$_{0.5}$ and InAs have largest and smallest nonlinear optical susceptibility tensor component respectively.

The Raman scattering efficiency $ \mathrm{dS}/\mathrm{d}\Omega $ of each phonon mode m with $ \omega_{\mathrm{m}} $ frequency is calculated from the following relation [26]:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{dS}}{\mathrm{d} \Omega}=\frac{\left(\omega_{0}-\omega_{\mathrm{m}}\right)^{4}}{\mathrm{c}^{4}}\left|\overrightarrow{\mathrm{e}}_{\mathrm{s}} \cdot \alpha_{\mathrm{m}} \cdot \overrightarrow{\mathrm{e}}_{0}\right|^{2} \frac{\mathrm{h}}{2 \pi \omega_{\mathrm{m}}}\left(\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{m}}+1\right),
\tag{15}
$$

where the temperature dependency of $\mathrm{d} S / \mathrm{d} \Omega$ is given by the Bose-Einstein distribution function
$\left(\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{m}}\right)$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{m}}$ is the Raman susceptibility. In the static limit, $\alpha_{\mathrm{m}}$ can be calculated as the
derivative of the electronic linear dielectric susceptibility component with respect to the atomic
displacement which is a nonlinear response [26]. By employing 2n+1 theory [26], the ground-
state and first-order changes in the wave functions [26] is only needed to calculate this nonlinear
response. The Raman susceptibilities for both transverse $\alpha(\omega_{T})$ and longitudinal $\alpha(\omega_{L})$
optical modes of $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}}$ ternary alloys at $\Gamma$ symmetry point within HGH.LDA and FHI.LDA
approaches are calculated and also presented in Table 5. The calculated transvers and
longitudinal Raman susceptibility of these alloys within FHI.LDA is much larger than the
corresponding result within HGH.LDA. In fact, In 4d states have considerable effect on the
Raman susceptibilities of transverse and longitudinal mode of these alloys. The calculated
$\alpha(\omega_{T})$ and $\alpha(\omega_{L})$ within HGH.LDA and FHI.LDA with respect to As concentration are shown
in Fig. 5. In FHI.LDA approach, $\alpha(\omega_{T})$ and $\alpha(\omega_{L})$ increase up to 0.5 and then decrease up to
$\mathrm{x}=1$, so $\mathrm{InAs}_{0.5} \mathrm{Sb}_{0.5}$ has maximum Raman susceptibility.

As can be seen from Table 5, the calculated $\alpha(\omega_{L})$ is larger than $\alpha(\omega_{T})$ within both
HGH.LDA and FHI.LDA approaches. This difference is due to the macroscopic electric field
which is generated by a longitudinal polar lattice vibration. The longitudinal optical phonons of
polar crystal for $q \to 0$ are modified by the $\frac{-8 \pi}{\Omega_{0}} \frac{\sum_{l} z_{k \lambda l}^{*} q_{l}}{\sum_{l, l^{\prime}} q_{l} \varepsilon_{l, l^{\prime}} q_{l^{\prime}}} \sum_{l} \chi_{i j l}^{(2)} q_{l}$ term (where $z^{*}$ is the
Born effective charge tensor, $\varepsilon$ and $\chi^{(2)}$ are the optical dielectric constant and the non-linear
optical dielectric susceptibility respectively). This term must be added to take into account the
effect of the macroscopic electric field generated by the lattice polar vibration [26].

According to the Pockels effect, the refractive index of materials is linearly proportional to the
external electric filed. The electro-optic (EO) coefficients $\left(\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{ij} \gamma}\right)$ of zinc-blende $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}}$ ternary

alloys are calculated within HGH.LDA and FHI.LDA approaches through the following relation [26]:

$$
\Delta(\varepsilon^{-1})_{\mathrm{ij}}=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{3} \mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{ij} \gamma} \varepsilon_{\gamma}, \qquad (16)
$$

where $\varepsilon_{\gamma}$ is a low-frequency or static electric field.

These coefficients have electronic, ionic and piezoelectric contributions. In the present work, the piezoelectric contribution is not considered, so the cells are clamped and do not change with applied electric field. The electro-optic coefficient of the cubic semiconductors has one independent component $\mathrm{r}_{63}^{\mathrm{s}}$. The calculated ionic and electronic contributions to the electro-optic coefficients have opposite sign and are also presented in Table 6.

The calculated results show that In 4d states have significant effect on the electronic, ionic and total EO coefficients. The InSb compound within HGH.LDA has the minimum value of total EO coefficient, while within FHI.LDA approach (by including In 4d states) the $\mathrm{InAs}_{0.25} \mathrm{Sb}_{0.75}$ has the minimum value of total EO coefficient (-6.64 $^{pm}/V$). In HGH.LDA approach, the total EO coefficient decrease up to 0.25 and then increase up to x=1.

### 6. Conclusions

The present work reports the first principle study of the linear and nonlinear response properties of $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}}$ alloys by using the plane-wave pseudopotential method within density functional theory, density functional perturbation theory and GW approximation.

The calculated lattice parameters of InAs and InSb compounds are in good agreement with the experimental data and other theoretical results. The lattice parameter of the $\mathrm{InAs}_{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{Sb}_{1-\mathrm{x}}$ ternary alloys increases almost linearly with increasing Al concentration and it follows closely Vegard's law. The calculated energy band gap of these alloys using the one-shot GW approximation

compare to calculated results obtained by DFT within simple form of LDA is in better agreement with the experimental results.

The calculated elastic constants show that $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ alloys are mechanically stable. These alloys have positive Cauchy pressure and metallic bond character. The $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ ternary alloys are ductile. The variation of $C_{44}$ shows that the increase in As concentration leads to weaker bonds. The nonlinear decreasing of $C_{11}$ elastic constant with As concentration decreasing shows that the crystal becomes stiffer. The Born effective charge of these alloys increases nonlinearly with As concentration increasing. The static dielectric function of these alloys and their abilities to store electrical potential energy under the influence of an electric field decrease by increasing the As concentration.

The phonon-dispersion curves of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ alloys along the high symmetry lines of the Brillouin zone have positive frequency values. So, these alloys have dynamical stability at zero pressure. are calculated. The longitudinal ‘$\omega_{\text{LO}}$‘ and transverse optical phonon frequencies ‘$\omega_{\text{TO}}$‘ of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ alloys at $\Gamma$ symmetry point increase with increasing As concentration. The phonon density of states within harmonic approximation is successfully applied to determine the thermal properties of $\text{InAs}_x\text{Sb}_{1-x}$ alloys between 0 to 1200 K temperatures. The heat capacity at constant volume tends to the classical Dulong-Petit limit at high temperature and is sufficiently proportional to $\text{T}^3$ at low temperatures. The increase of the specific heat indicates that phonon thermal softening occurs with the temperature increases. The entropy increases sharply with increasing temperature because it increases the vibrational contribution to the entropy.

The calculated results show that to obtain acceptable nonlinear optical properties, it is necessary to include In 4d states. $\text{InAs}_{0.25}\text{Sb}_{0.75}$ has the minimum value of total EO coefficient. $\alpha(\omega_T)$ and $\alpha(\omega_L)$ of these alloys increase up to 0.5 and then decrease up to x=1, so

$InAs_{0.5}Sb_{0.5}$ has maximum Raman susceptibility. $InAs_{0.5}Sb_{0.5}$ and InAs have the largest and smallest nonlinear optical susceptibility tensor component respectively. According to the calculated nonlinear properties, $InAs_xSb_{1-x}$ ternary alloys can be a candidate as a high-performance nonlinear optical material. The present study would be helpful for future experimental and theoretical investigations.
