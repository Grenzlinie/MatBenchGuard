## ABSTRACT

In the present work the electronic, optical, and dielectric properties of the orthorhombic crystal structure of OsP₂ semiconductor are investigated through first-principles calculations using the all-electron full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW). All properties were calculated and discussed within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and the local density approximation (LDA), with and without the modified Becke-Johnson exchange-correlation potential (mBJ). The optimized structure parameters obtained by minimizing the total energy and electronic charge as part of the LDA were found to be in good agreement with existing experimental parameters. The calculation performed by the LDA with mBJ exchange-correlation potential confirms the indirect electronic transition located in the Γ-Z direction and gives a bandgap energy closer to the experimental value compared with GGA and GGA-mBJ results. The SOC underestimates the bandgap, and the splitting of the Os-5d states causes a band shift toward lower energies. In addition, GGA-mBJ and LDA-mBJ calculations demonstrate that the OsP₂ semiconductor exhibits an insignificant optical anisotropy character in the infrared region, with a high refractive index of approximately 1000 cm⁻¹ (n = 3.95).

## 1. Introduction

Transition-metal dipnictides have attracted attention regarding both theoretical studies and technological applications because of their exceptional physical and chemical properties. Investigations carried out on the compounds NbSb₂ [1], TaAs₂ [2,3], and MoAs₂, which crystallize in a monoclinic structure, reveal their extraordinary properties, including high mobility and extremely large positive magnetoresistance (MR) [4]. In addition, these materials may display field-induced metal-semiconductor transitions at low temperatures. Transition-metal dipnictides that crystallize in the marcasite-type structure, are orthorhombic, and exhibit the properties of semiconductors also deserve particular attention from both theoretical and experimental aspects. Indeed, it has been demonstrated that some of these compounds, such as FeP₂ [5], which has a narrow bandgap (0.37 eV), have excellent thermoelectric properties [6] and potential applications in photoelectrochemical cells as photoelectrodes [7], as well as in electrocatalysis for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) [8].

Over the previous decade, particular attention has been given to OsP₂. This compound, in its marcasite structure, has previously been synthesized in evacuated and sealed silica tubes by heating a stoichiometric mixture of osmium and phosphorus elements with a 1:2 ratio [9]. Moreover, it has been demonstrated that OsP₂ is a diamagnetic n-type semiconductor with a bandgap energy of approximately 1.2 eV [10]. Recently, Bugaris et al. reported the synthesis and growth of single crystals of osmium dipnictides, OsPn₂ (Pn = P, As, Sb), in marcasite-type structures using metal flux techniques [11]. They confirmed that all of these compounds have a semiconductor character, with narrow bandgap energies of approximately 1.13, 0.92, and 0.21 eV, respectively. Furthermore, the electrical characterization of the resistivity and Seebeck coefficient at room temperature concluded that OsAs₂ is a p-type semiconductor with low electrical resistivity (0.62 Ωcm), whereas OsP₂ and OsSb₂ are n-type semiconductors with electrical resistivities of 6.408 and 0.90 Ωcm, respectively. Additional theoretical studies have been performed by Z-L Lv et al. to understand their elastic and vibrational properties [12]. The authors report that OsP₂ in a marcasite-type structure is dynamically stable and brittle, with covalent bonds between (P – P) and (Os – P). They also suggest that only the vibration of the phosphor ions is possible with Raman spectroscopy, contrasting with infrared spectroscopy, where the vibrations of both phosphor and osmium ions are observed.
