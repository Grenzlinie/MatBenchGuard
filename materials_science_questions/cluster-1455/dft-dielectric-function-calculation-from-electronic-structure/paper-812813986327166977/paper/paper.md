# Electronic and optical properties of double perovskite oxide $\boldsymbol{Pb_2ScMO_6}$ (M = Ta, Sb) using a first principles approach

## ABSTRACT

In this study, the electronic and optical properties of the double perovskite (DP) oxides $\text{Pb}_2\text{ScMO}_6$ (M = Sb, Ta) were systematically investigated using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave model. Calculations were performed using both the generalized gradient approximation and modified Becke–Johnson potentials. The results showed that the O-atom is responsible for the maxima in the density of states (DOS) in the valence band, whereas the Sc-atom contributes to the maximum DOS in the conduction band. The dielectric constants, absorption coefficients, and reflectivity were also calculated for these systems. The results indicated the presence of wide band gaps in both of these DPs, thereby indicating their insulating semiconducting nature.

Keywords: band gap, density of states, dielectric, double perovskite, energy, photon energy

## 1. Introduction

The double perovskites (DPs) are among the most widely studied structures in materials science because of their half-metallicity, ferromagnetic, thermoelectric, and tunneling magnetoresistance properties [1]. Moreover, since the discovery of colossal magnetoresistance in DPs, such as $\text{Sr}_2\text{FeMoO}_6$ [2] and $\text{Sr}_2\text{FeReO}_6$ [3], there have been many theoretical and experimental studies of DPs due to their varied crystal structures, chemical compositions, and physical characteristics. DPs have Curie temperatures above 300 K [4] with significant spin–orbit interactions and correlated electron behavior, which can be utilized in nonvolatile logic devices [5].

The DP oxide compounds have the general formula of $A_2BB'O_6$ or $AA'BB'O_6$. The A or AA' sites are occupied by alkaline-earth, rare earth, or transition metals (Ba, Sr, Mn, Pb, etc.) and the BB' sites are occupied by transition metals (Sb, Ta, Sc, Fe, Mo, Co, W, V, Mo, etc.) [6,7]. $A_2BB'O_6$ has the ordered rock salt (NaCl) crystal structure with alternate perovskite $ABO_3$- $AB'O_3$ units along the three crystallographic axes [2,8]. Each transition metal site B or B' is surrounded by an oxygen octahedron, and A atoms are located in the cavities formed by eight adjacent $MO_6$ and $B'O_6$ octahedra [9].

Several theoretical and experimental studies have investigated 3d-5d DP oxides. The Sc-based DPs $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ and $Pb_2ScNbO_6$ were studied by Kishi et al. [10] who found a paraelectric state at 400 K, which differed from the ferroelectric phase at 4.2 K and 200 K due to cooperative ion shift [10]. $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ is ferroelectric in both the ordered and disordered phase [11,12], which is an interesting behavior. The DP oxides have attracted much attention because of their potential applications in electronic and optical devices. Several DP oxides have been synthesized recently and studied experimentally over a wide temperature range in order to understand their electronic structure and phase transitions [13-14].

In this study, we conducted simple systematic investigations of the contributions of the Sc-3d orbitals to the density of states (DOS), as well as the electronic and optical behavior of Sc-based lead DP oxides $Pb_2ScMO_6$ (M = Ta, Sb). To the best of our knowledge, no previous theoretical and experimental studies have investigated these characteristics. The Wien2k code [15] was used to compute the DOS, energy, and optical properties.

## 2. Computational methods

The electronic and optical properties of DP oxides $Pb_2ScMO_6$ (M = Ta, Sb) were calculated by using the density functional theory. The ab initio band structure calculations were conducted using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method. In the FP-LAPW method, the core states are treated fully relativistically whereas the semi-core and valence states are treated semi-relativistically. Brillouin zone (BZ) integrations within the self-consistency cycles were performed via a tetrahedron method. The exchange and correlation potentials were treated using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [16] and modified Becke-Johnson (mBJ) potentials [17]. For a well conserved self-consistency cycle, a $20 \times 20 \times 20$ k-mesh was

used in the first Brillouin zone, and the energy cut-off was set to –6.00 Ry [18]. The convergence parameter $\text{R}_\text{mt}\text{K}_\text{max} = 7$ controlled the size of the basis sets, which determined the matrix size for convergence, where $\text{K}_\text{max}$ is the plane wave cut-off and $\text{R}_\text{MT}$ is the smallest of all the atomic sphere radii. The $\text{G}_\text{max}$ parameter was taken as 12 Bohr$^{-1}$. The energy convergence criterion was set to $10^{-5}$ Ry [19].

### 3.2. Electronic properties
#### 3.2.1. DOS for $Pb_2ScMO_6$

The DOS were calculated for (M = Sb, Ta) DPs by using the FP-LAPW method with the GGA and mBJ potentials. The optimized lattice constants shown in Table 1 were used to calculate the electronic structures of the $Pb_2ScMO_6$ perovskites.

![](./images/812813986327166977_4.jpg)

Fig. 2. Total DOS calculated for $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ and individual Pb, Sc, Sb, and O atoms using the GGA and mBJ with $E_F=0$ eV.

Figure 2 shows plots of the total DOS (TDOS) for $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ and individual Pb, Sc, Sb, and O atoms. High DOS peaks were visible in the valence region near the Fermi level ($E_F$) with both GGA and mBJ. In the valence region, sharp DOS peaks were present at around $-1$ eV, $-2.75$ eV, $-4.86$ eV, and $-6.9$ eV, were the highest peak was equal to 16 states/eV at $-1$ eV below $E_F$. After including mBJ, the TDOS peaks were slightly higher than those obtained by GGA, but the overall nature of the plots was very similar. Similarly, in the conduction region, the high DOS peaks at 5.96 eV shifted to 6.8 eV when using the mBJ potential. The sharp DOS curve at the Fermi level with GGA indicates the high mobility of the charge carriers and $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ is predicted to be a wide gap semiconductor. The DOS curve at the Fermi level shifted below $E_F$ and the DOS curves in the conduction band also shifted toward the higher energy range when using the mBJ potential. Thus, a possible phase change from a semiconductor to insulator was predicted, which was more evident when observing the band structures for this compound. In

$Pb_2ScSbO_6$, the total DOS was found to be higher with GGA than that using mBJ, thereby indicating the increase in degeneracy from GGA to mBJ. The bands obtained by mBJ were more degenerate than those using GGA. The DOS in the valence region was dominated by the contribution from the O-atoms. In the conduction region, the sharp DOS peak was mainly due to the Sc-atoms with both GGA and mBJ.

To determine the exact contributions of the atoms or electron states of $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ to the TDOS, we also plotted the projected DOS (PDOS) for each individual atom with their respective TDOS, as shown in Fig. 3.

![](./images/812813986327166977_5.jpg)

Fig. 3. TDOS and PDOS obtained for Pb, Sc, Sb, and O atoms in $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ perovskite using the GGA and mBJ methods with $E_F = 0$ eV.

Sharp DOS peaks were observed for the Pb, Sc, and Sb atoms but they were in the core regions away from the Fermi level, and thus they were not relevant. Sharp DOS peaks were observed at or around the Fermi level only for O-atoms. Thus, only O-atomic states are available for

occupation by electrons in the valence band near the Fermi level, which affects the electronic property of the system and this may explain the change in the phase to an insulator, as also shown by using the mBJ method. Therefore, the effective DOS in $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ is due to the $p$-states of O atoms in the valence band. However, in the conduction band, the $d_{t_{2g}}$-states of Sc atoms contribute to the maxima in the PDOS. This was demonstrated clearly by the TDOS plots obtained using the GGA and mBJ method, as shown in Fig. 2.

![](./images/812813986327166977_6.jpg)

Fig. 4. Total DOS obtained for $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ and individual Pb, Sc, Ta, and O atoms using the GGA and mBJ methods ($E_{\text{F}}=0$ eV).

Figure 4 shows the TDOS obtained for the DP compound $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ using the GGA and mBJ methods. In contrast to $Pb_2ScSbO_6$, the DOS peaks obtained with GGA where higher compared with those produced using the mBJ method. The sharp DOS peaks at around –1 eV in the valence region decreased from 23 states/eV to about 18 states/eV with both the GGA and mBJ methods. In addition, the peaks at around 7 eV in the conduction region decreased from 20 states/eV to about 18.5 states/eV. The decrease in the DOS peaks with the mBJ method was due to its dependency on the potential energy because this potential is not functionally dependent on the density in the same manner as the GGA. The DOS peaks in the valance region in the energy range –1.5 eV to –3 eV increased with the GGA compared with the mBJ method, thereby increasing the degeneracy of the bands in this region, as shown in the band structure plots. Similar to $Pb_2ScSbO_6$, a phase change in $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ can also be predicted from a wide band semiconductor to an insulator according to the DOS characteristics determined by the GGA and

mBJ methods. In the valence band, the O-atom made the maximum contribution to the TDOS, whereas in the conduction band, the Sc-atom made the main contribution according to both the GGA and mBJ methods. However, the band gap was wider than that determined by mBJ method.

![](./images/812813986327166977_7.jpg)

Fig. 5. TDOS and PDOS obtained for the Pb, Sb, Ta, and O atoms in Pb₂ScTaO₆ perovskite using the GGA and mBJ methods with E_F = 0 eV.

The PDOS plots in Fig. 5 also show that the p-states of the O-atom made effective contributions to the total DOS in the valence region. The other atoms made almost no contributions in this region. In the conduction region, the $dt_{2g}$-states of the Sc-atom made the maximum contribution to the DOS. However, the DOS peaks due to the Sc and Ta atoms indicate that this compound is a better electrical conductor than Pb₂ScTaO₆. Due to the gap and the sharp DOS peaks just above the E_F, we predict that this compound is semiconducting insulator according to the GGA and

mBJ methods. The nature of this compound was investigated further by studying its band structure characteristics.

### 3.2.2. Energy bands
Figure 6 shows the energy band structures obtained for $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ and $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ DP by considering the GGA (Fig. 6(a)) and mBJ potential (Fig. 6(b)).

![](./images/812813986327166977_8.jpg)

Fig. 6. Band structure plots obtained for (a) $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ and (b) $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ using the GGA and mBJ methods.

The energy bands plot for the $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ perovskite shown in Fig. 6(a) demonstrate that the maximum contribution to the valence bands was due to the O-$p$ state electrons according to the GGA. In the conduction bands, the maximum contribution to the energy bands came from the Sc-$d_{t2g}$ state electrons. This was clearly demonstrated by the DOS studies, but the occurrences of minima in the energy bands at the X-symmetry point were due to the Sc-$d_{eg}$ electrons lying at the X-symmetry point. In the conductions bands, the maximum contributions to the energy bands at the X-symmetry point were due to Sc-$d_{t2g}$ and Ta-$d_{t2g}$ state electrons. Similar results were obtained for the $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ perovskite using the mBJ method, as shown in Fig. 6(a), where the bands were all related to the X-symmetry point and the transition was of a direct type. The only difference was in terms of the width of the energy band gap, which was wider in this case than that using the GGA. The energy band gaps were calculated for $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ as 2.712 eV by GGA and 3.842 eV by the mBJ method.

Figure 6(b) shows the energy bands obtained for $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ perovskite using both the GGA and mBJ methods. The maxima in the valence bands were due to the O-$p$ state electrons lying at $-4$ eV below the Fermi level. In the conduction bands, the Sc-$d_{t2g}$ and Ta-$d_{t2g}$ electrons made the maximum contributions to the bands. Similar trends were also found using the mBJ potential, where the only difference was that a wider band gap was obtained with the GGA. The calculated energy band gaps were 2.657 eV with GGA and 3.889 eV using the mBJ method, and the transition type was direct at the X-symmetry point.

The band structure plots showed that the top of the valence band and the bottom of conduction band were at the X-symmetry point for both of the compounds, and thus direct band gaps were identified. Therefore, direct recombination occurs with the release of energy equal to the energy band gap. The efficiency factor for a direct band gap semiconductor is higher than that for an indirect band gap semiconductor. Therefore, a direct band gap semiconductor is the best candidate for applications that require the emission of photons via the radiative recombination of electrons and holes, thereby making them suitable for uses in light-emitting diodes, lasers, and optical sources.

### 3.3. Optical properties

Optical transitions are very important for understanding the optical properties of solids. Optical transitions can be associated with the DOS results, and thus with energy band transitions, thereby allowing us to understand the variations in optical parameters such as the dielectric constants, absorption coefficient, reflectivity, and refractive index. The dielectric response function for a medium is given by:

$$
\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+i \varepsilon_{2}(\omega), \tag{1}
$$

where $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ are the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric response function, respectively. The optical response of a medium to incident photon energy is given by

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)=\left(\frac{4 \pi^{2} e^{2}}{m^{2} \omega^{2}}\right) \sum_{i, j} \int\langle i|M| j\rangle^{2} f_{i}\left(1-f_{j}\right) \times \delta\left(E_{f}-E_{i}-\omega\right) d^{3} k, \tag{2}
$$

where $M$ is the dipole matrix, $i$ and $j$ are the initial and final states, respectively, $f_{i}$ is the Fermi distribution function for the $i$th state, and $E_{i}$ is the energy of electron in the $i$th state. The real part of the dielectric function can be extracted from the imaginary part using the Kramers-Kronig relationship in the form [24, 25]:

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\pi} P \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega^{\prime} \varepsilon_{2}\left(\omega^{\prime}\right) d \omega^{\prime}}{\left(\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}\right)}, \tag{3}
$$

where P indicates the principal value of the complex integral. The optical reflectivity spectra can be derived from the Fresnel's formula for normal incidence by assuming that the orientation of the crystal surface is parallel to the optical axis according to the following relationship [24, 26-28].

$$
R(\omega)=\left|\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon(\omega)}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon(\omega)}+1}\right|^{2} \tag{4}
$$

The absorption coefficient $\mathrm{I}(\omega)$ is calculated using the following expression [20, 24].

$$
\mathrm{I}(\omega)=2 \omega\left\{\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)}-\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)}{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}} \tag{5}
$$

Figure 7(a) shows the variations in the real $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ and imaginary $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ parts of the dielectric constants as a function of the incident photon energy for $\mathrm{Pb}_{2} \mathrm{ScSbO}_{6}$ using the GGA and mBJ methods. The real dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ increased with the photon energy and reached a maximum photon energy of 4 eV where it exhibited normal dispersion. As the photon energy increased above $4 \mathrm{eV}, \varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ decreased and became zero at $\sim 7 \mathrm{eV}$, thereby indicating an anomalous type of dispersion. Above a photon energy of $7 \mathrm{eV}, \varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ became negative and finally tended toward a value of zero. $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ was zero in the range from $0-3.5 \mathrm{eV}$, before increasing and reaching a maximum at $6.0 \mathrm{eV}$. Subsequently, $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ decreased with further increases in the photon energy above $6.0 \mathrm{eV}$. The behaviors of $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ determined using the mBJ method were similar to those obtained with the GGA method, as shown in Fig. 8(a). The only difference was that the peaks occurred at different photon energies. For example, the $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ peak value occurred at $6 \mathrm{eV}$ whereas it occurred at $7 \mathrm{eV}$ photon energies for $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$.

It is interesting to note that the first peak for $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ coincided with the transition at the X-symmetry point between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band. The major difference between the $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ spectra for the compounds occurred in the region where $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ was negative, which corresponded to direct interband transitions originating from the top of the valence band at the X-symmetry point to the lowest conduction band. The onset of the absorption edge in $\mathrm{Pb}_{2} \mathrm{ScSbO}_{6}$ occurred at $2.5 \mathrm{eV}$ when $\varepsilon_{2}$ increased from a value of zero, which corresponded to the direct optical band gap at the X-symmetry point, and there was a sharp

increase in the slope of $\varepsilon_{2}$ starting from the optical band gap and increasing to the main peak. The global peak in $\varepsilon_{2}$ occurred at 6 eV and 7 eV according to the GGA and mBJ methods, respectively. Another small peak in $\varepsilon_{2}$ occurred due to direct transitions from the top of the valence band at the L- symmetry point to the next higher conduction band.

The onset of absorption involved a sharp increase in the spectra of $\varepsilon_{2}$ at the energy corresponding to the energy band gap. For $Pb_{2}ScSbO_{6}$, this occurred at 2.5 eV with the GGA and 3.5 eV using the mBJ method, as shown in Fig. 7(b), and the values calculated from the energy bands gaps were 2.712 eV and 3.84 eV, respectively. Thus the values obtained using the band gaps agreed well with the calculated values.

Figure 7(c) show plots of the reflectivity obtained for $Pb_{2}ScSbO_{6}$ using both the GGA and mBJ methods. Using both methods, the reflectivity was small in the low photon energy up to 2 eV, which indicates that interband transition do not occur in the far infrared spectrum because this compound is a wide band insulator. However, the reflectivity was 20% to 50% in the photon range of 6–10 eV, which indicates that more reflection occurs in the ultraviolet region. However, above 10 eV, the reflectivity decreased in both cases, so this compound is suitable for applications in the visible and ultraviolet regions. The low reflectivity values also demonstrate that this compound has possible applications in anti-reflection coatings and in the deep ultraviolet region.

Fig. 8. Plots of (a) real and imaginary dielectric constants, (b) absorption coefficient, (c) and reflectivity obtained for $Pb_2ScTaO_6$ using the GGA and mBJ method.

Figure 8(a–c) shows the variations in the real and imaginary dielectric constants, absorption coefficient, and reflectivity as a function of the incident photon energy for $Pb_2ScTaO_6$. The variations in all of these parameters were similar for $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ when using both the GGA and mBJ methods. The energy band gaps for this system were calculated as 2.5 eV with the GGA and 3.8 eV using the mBJ method, whereas the band gaps obtained from the absorption coefficients were calculated as 2.4 eV with the GGA and 3.6 eV using the mBJ method.

Conclusion

In the present study, we investigated the electronic and optical properties of the cubic DPs $Pb_2ScMO_6$ ($M = Sb$, Ta) using the GGA and mBJ approximations based on the FP-LAPW

method implemented in the WIEN2K code. A direct band gap was determined by the GGA and mBJ methods along the X-symmetry direction around the Fermi level, as well as semiconducting behavior in both the DPs. However, $Pb_2ScSbO_6$ was determined as an insulating semiconductor with wide band gaps of 2.712 eV according to the GGA and 3.84 eV using the mBJ method. The energy band gaps were wider with the mBJ method than the GGA. The general trend found with the mBJ method was comparable to the experimental values. Further from the onset of the absorption edge, the band gaps were calculated as 2.5 eV according to the GGA and 3.5 eV using the mBJ method, which were comparable to the calculated results. Similarly, for $Pb_2ScTaO_6$, the energy band gaps were calculated as 2.5 eV according to the GGA and 3.8 eV using the mBJ method. The band gaps obtained from the absorption results were 2.4 eV according to the GGA and 3.6 eV using the mBJ method, and they were comparable to calculated values in a similar manner to those for $Pb_2ScSbO_6$.

However, our results could not be compared with previous theoretical and experimental values due to the lack of reported data for these DPs.

Finally, the results obtained for the dielectric function, absorption coefficient, and optical reflectivity confirmed the suitability of the cubic DPs $Pb_2ScMO_6$ (M = Sb, Ta) for applications in optoelectronic devices in the low and ultraviolet spectrum. However, the optical parameters could not be compared with previous theoretical and experimental values due to the lack of reported data for these DPs.

### HIGHLIGHTS

Theoretical investigation of electronic and optical properties of double perovskites (DP) oxide $\text{Pb}_2\text{ScMO}_6$ (M=Sb, Ta) are presented in this paper. Study have been performed by using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method of density functional theory. We have used both the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) and modified Becke-Johnson (mBJ) potentials which is included in Wien2k code. Physical structure, density of states and energy bands have been have been calculated by using the code Wien2k. In addition to this, using the same code, we have also studied the optical properties of these DPs by calculating the dielectric constants, absorption coefficient and reflectivity. From our study it is seen that O-atom is responsible for maxima in density of states (DOS) in valence band, whereas Sc-atom contributing to maximum DOS in the conduction band. The results showed wide band gaps in both of these DP indicating an insulating semiconducting nature. We have also evaluated the energy band gaps by using the plots in the absorption coefficients and found the values are almost equal to energy band gaps from energy bands of each DPs.
