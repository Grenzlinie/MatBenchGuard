## ABSTRACT

A detailed analysis of the optical properties of alkaline-earth metal nitrides ($\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$, $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$ and $\text{Ca}_3\text{N}_2$) has been performed, using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method within density functional theory. The exchange correlation potential is treated by the generalized gradient approximation within Perdew et al. scheme. The real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function $\epsilon(\omega)$, the optical absorption coefficient $I(\omega)$, the refractive index $n(\omega)$, the extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$ and the electron energy loss function are calculated within the random phase approximation (RPA). The calculated results show a qualitative agreement with the available experimental results in the sense that we can recognize some peaks qualitatively, that is, ones that are due to single particle transitions. Furthermore, the interband transitions responsible for the structures in the spectra are specified. The metal $s$ states and nitrogen $p$ states play the major role in these optical transitions as initial and final states, respectively, for $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$ and $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$. In the case of $\text{Ca}_3\text{N}_2$, where Ca has $d$ levels lying near the Fermi level, the Ca $d$ states are mostly final states. The effect of the spin-orbit coupling on the optical properties is also investigated, and it is found to be quite small, especially in the low energy region. The dielectric constants are calculated and compared with the available experimental results.

## 1. Introduction

The alkaline-earth metal nitrides ($\text{M}_3\text{N}_2$, M = Be, Mg and Ca) form a very important wide band gap semiconductor with a cubic bcc structure and the space group of Ia3(206) at normal conditions [1–3]. There are two crystallographic structures of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$: $\alpha\text{-Be}_3\text{N}_2$, cubic bcc with 40 atoms per cell, and $\beta\text{-Be}_3\text{N}_2$, hexagonal close packed with 10 atoms per cell [4]. The first structure is stable between 20 °C and 1200 °C, which changes to the hexagonal form over 1400 °C. The calcium nitride exists in various structures, viz., $\alpha\text{-Ca}_3\text{N}_2$ (reddish-brown) [5], $\beta\text{-Ca}_3\text{N}_2$ (black) [6], $\gamma\text{-Ca}_3\text{N}_2$ (yellow) [7], and the different high pressure phase (yellow) [8]. It is further known that $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$ and $\alpha\text{-Ca}_3\text{N}_2$ compounds are isostructure [9]. They are characterized by their wide band gap, high thermal conductivity and large bulk modulus [10,11]. These interesting properties make them potential materials for the use in the development of solid state illuminations, optoelectronic applications, and display and communication devices [12]. They are technologically important materials, with a band gap in the ultraviolet region, which can be used as an alternative to the aluminum nitride, the aluminum–gallium nitride [12], and the beryllium for some optical applications [13].

The available experimental studies on the optical properties of these materials are mostly limited to the optical band gap [14–16].

In the optical frequency range, Soto et al. [12,17,18] have measured the electron energy loss spectra of $\text{Be}_3\text{N}_2$ and $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$ to determine the dielectric constant, and de la Cruz et al. [13] have measured the absorption coefficient, the refractive index, and the extinction coefficient of beryllium nitride. But for $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$ and $\text{Ca}_3\text{N}_2$, to our knowledge, there is not yet any experimental results on these quantities in the optical frequency range. Our calculated results could serve as a reference for future experimental work on this compound.

On these materials, from theoretical point of view, there exist a number of first principles calculations in terms of their stability, and electronic as well as structural properties [4,10,11,19–25]. To the best of our knowledge, there is no theoretical research on optical properties (i.e., real and imaginary parts of dielectric functions, reflectivity, absorption and electron energy loss function). Moreover, it seems that there is a lack of both experimental and theoretical data on the optical properties of alkaline-earth metal nitrides, and no systematic research on the optical properties of these compounds have been reported.

In this work, we have investigated a full range of optical properties of alkaline-earth nitride, including real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function, the absorption coefficient, and the energy loss function, using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) for the exchange correlation potential, within the density functional theory.

## 2. Calculation method

The calculations presented in this work were performed using the FP-LAPW method. In this method no shape approximation on the potential or the electronic charge density is made. The calculations of the electronic and optical properties have been done relativistically with and without the spin-orbit coupling. We use the WIEN2K [26] implementation of the method which allows the inclusion of local orbitals in the basis, improving upon linearization and making possible a consistent treatment of the semicore and valance states in an energy window, hence ensuring proper orthogonality. The exchange correlation potential within the GGA is calculated using the scheme of Perdew et al. [27]. The convergence parameter $R_{\text{MT}}^{\text{min}}K_{\text{max}}$ (the product of the smallest of the atomic sphere radii $R_{\text{MT}}$ and the plane wave cutoff parameter $K_{\text{max}}$) which controls the size of the basis sets in these calculations, was set to 8. The maximum $l$ quantum number for the wavefunction expansion inside the atomic sphere is confined to $l_{\text{max}} = 10$. The $G_{\text{max}}$ parameter was taken to be 14.0 Bohr⁻¹. Brillouin-zone (BZ) integrations within self-consistency cycles were performed via a tetrahedron

We find that spin-orbit interaction has a minor influence on the optical properties.

## 3. Electronic structure results
Since the optical spectra are calculated from the interband tran- sitions, first we present our calculated electronic structure of these compounds. The primitive cell of body centered cubic phase of alkaline-earth metal nitrides has 40 atoms, 24 similar M atoms and two types of N atoms, 12 N(1) and 4 N(2). A point worth men- tioning is that the similar nitrogens in the cell are not equivalent. Our calculated total density of states (DOS) and electronic band structures for $Be_{3}N_{2},Mg_{3}N_{2}$ and $Ca_{3}N_{2}$ are given in Fig. 1. Due to the close similarity between the results obtained for these com- pounds, the partial DOS are given only for $Be_{3}N_{2}$ . The results are gi ven both with and without the spin-orbit coupling. Major contributions to the occupied part of the DOS come from the N2p and 2s states.

The first structure in the low lying energy side of the DOS con- sists of a narrow peak centered on -10.64 eV for $Ca_{3}N_{2}$ and -12.00 eV for $Mg_{3}N_{2}$ , and a broad peak about -15 eV for $Be_{3}N_{2}$ . This structure originates from Ns states, corresponding to the low- est lying bands in Fig. 1. These bands are lower in energy for $Be_{3}N_{2}$ and $Mg_{3}N_{2}$ than $Ca_{3}N_{2}$ by around 2.7 and 1.0 eV, respectively. The structure of the spin-orbit splitting and its amount are not consid- erable in this region.

The next structure which, in the absence of spin-orbit coupling, is separated from the first by a gap of 5.64 eV for $Be_{3}N_{2},7.22$ eV for $Mg_{3}N_{2}$ , and 8.55 eV for $Ca_{3}N_{2}$ , is a broad peak that is situated be tween -7.5 eV and the Fermi level, corresponding, to the valence bands shown in the band structure in Fig. 1. From the partial DOS, it is seen that just below the Fermi level, the bands are dom- inated by the N 2p, where metals s and p are hybridized. These bands are lower in energy for $Be_{3}N_{2}$ and $Mg_{3}N_{2}$ than $Ca_{3}N_{2}$ by5.63 and 2.28 eV, respectively. Spin-orbit coupling has a minor ef- fect on the structure of these peaks.

There are two main features in the broad structure above the Fermi level up to around 15 eV, containing, a broad peak around7 eV for $Be_{3}N_{2},5$ eV for $Mg_{3}N_{2}$ and 2.5 eV for $Ca_{3}N_{2}$ . The distinctive structure in the DOS above 7.0 eV is a broad peak at around 8 eV for $Ca_{3}N_{2}$ and 10 eV for $Be_{3}N_{2}$ and $Mg_{3}N_{2}$ . These structures are mainly from the p states of both nitrogen and metal atoms, and 3d states of calcium.

In $\mathrm{Ca_3N_2}$ we found an indirect band gap between the top of nitrogen valence $2p$ bands occurring at the $H$ point and the bottom of the Ca $3d$ bands occurring at the $\Gamma$ point, confirming the previously found results by Imai et al. [20]. It is clearly seen that $\mathrm{Be_3N_2}$ and $\mathrm{Mg_3N_2}$ are two semiconductors with a direct band gap at the Brillouin zone center, confirming the results obtained by Armenta et al. [10] and in contradiction to results obtained for $\mathrm{Mg_3N_2}$ by Fang et al. [21].

The calculated values of the band gaps for $\mathrm{Ca_3N_2}$, $\mathrm{Be_3N_2}$ and $\mathrm{Mg_3N_2}$ are 1.13, 3.26 and 1.50 eV, respectively. The results confirm the well known expectation that the theoretical band gaps are on the whole underestimated within GGA, in comparison with the experimental values [17,23,24]. We have included the spin-orbit coupling in the calculation of the band gap, but found the effect was negligible. Compared to the experimental and other calculated results, our results are in fair agreement with the previous studies. It can be seen that the calculated energy gap values decrease as the anion size increases. Similar behavior was observed in Sr and Ba chalcogenides.

### 4. Optical properties

The optical properties of matter can be described by means of the transverse dielectric function $\epsilon(\omega)$. There are two contributions to $\epsilon(\omega)$, namely, intraband and interband transitions. The contribution from intraband transitions is important only for metals. The interband transitions can further be split into direct and indirect transitions. Here we neglect the indirect interband transitions which involve scattering of phonons and are expected to give a small contribution to $\epsilon(\omega)$ [29]. To calculate the direct interband contribution to the imaginary part of the dielectric function $\epsilon_2(\omega)$, we should sum up all possible transitions from the occupied to the unoccupied states. Taking the appropriate transition matrix elements into account, the imaginary part of the dielectric function $\epsilon_2(\omega)$ is given by random phase approximation (RPA), neglecting local field effects:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_{2}(\omega) & =\frac{V e^{2}}{2 \pi \hbar m^{2} \omega^{2}} \int d^{3} k \sum_{n n^{\prime}}\left|\langle\mathbf{k} n|\mathbf{p}| \mathbf{k} n^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{2} f(\mathbf{k} n) \times(1 \\
& \left.-f\left(k n^{\prime}\right)\right) \delta\left(E_{k n}-E_{k n^{\prime}}-\hbar \omega\right)
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

where $\hbar \omega$ is the energy of the incident photon, $p$ is the momentum operator $\frac{\hbar}{i} \frac{\partial}{\partial \mathbf{k}}$, $|\mathbf{k}\rangle$ is the eigenfunction with eigenvalue $E_{k n}$, and $f(\mathbf{k} n)$ is the Fermi distribution function. The evaluation of the matrix elements of the momentum operator in Eq. (1) is performed over the muffin-tin and the interstitial regions separately. A fully detailed calculation of the matrix elements is given by Ambrosch-Draxl et al. [30].

The real part of the dielectric function $\epsilon_1 \omega$ follows from the Kramers-Kronig relation [31]. In order to calculate $\epsilon_1 \omega$, we need to have a good representation of $\epsilon_2 \omega$ up to high energies. In the present work we have calculated $\epsilon_2 \omega$ up to 75 eV and used this value as the truncation energy in Kramers-Kronig relations. This energy range was chosen to produce convergence in the Kramers-Kronig transformations. Other optical parameters, such as energy loss spectrum and absorption coefficient can be immediately calculated in terms of components of the complex dielectric function [31].

Our calculated real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function for the three compounds with and without spin-orbit coupling are shown in Fig. 2. We note that all the structures in the imaginary part of dielectric function are shifted towards lower energies as we go from Be to Ca. This trend may be directly inferred from the band structure results, given the shift in the location of the first structure above the Fermi level in DOS curves. It is worthwhile to attempt to identify the transitions that are responsible for the structures in $\epsilon_2 \omega$, using our calculated band structures. There are four prominent peaks and shoulders appearing in the $\epsilon_2 \omega$ spectra, denoted by a, b, c and d in Fig. 2. Looking at optical matrix elements, it is obvious that the metal $s$ states and N $p$ states play a major role in these optical transitions as initial and final states, respectively for $\mathrm{Mg_3N_2}$ and $\mathrm{Be_3N_2}$. In the case of $\mathrm{Ca_3N_2}$, where Ca has $d$ levels lying near the Fermi level, this contribution is reasonably big. The $2p$ electrons of nitrogen and $3d$ electrons of calcium are involved in the interband transitions responsible for the peaks b and c in Fig. 2. Spin-orbit coupling has a minor effect on the dielectric function of $\mathrm{Mg_3N_2}$, as can be seen from Fig. 2, shifting it down by a very small amount. The spin-orbit coupling does not have any significant effect on the results of $\mathrm{Be_3N_2}$ and Ca₃N2. This is expected, as the spin-orbit coupling changes the eigenvalues only by 0.1 eV, which is not significant in the calculation of optical properties. This has also been found in FP-LMTO calculations for $\mathrm{HgI_2}$ [32].

In the dispersive part of the dielectric function, $\epsilon_1 \omega$, the main features are as follows: a peak at around 2–9 eV; a rather steep decrease between 3 and 12 eV, after which $\epsilon_1 \omega$ becomes negative; a minimum at around 8 eV for $\mathrm{Ca_3N_2}$ and about 12 eV for others; and then again a slow increase towards zero at higher energies.

It is interesting to note that in the optical spectra for Ca₃N₂, a broad structure around 25 eV is clearly visible. For Be₃N₂ and Mg₃N₂, such a structure is not visible. We conclude that the origin of this structure is due to d states of calcium.

## 5. Conclusions

We have applied a FP-LAPW method to study the electronic and optical properties of the alkaline-earth nitrides Be₃N₂, Mg₃N₂ and Ca₃N₂ within the density functional theory with and without spin–orbit coupling.

We have shown that the metal s states and nitrogen p states play the major role in these optical transitions as initial and final states, respectively, for Mg₃N₂ and Be₃N₂. In the case of Ca₃N₂, where Ca has d levels lying near the Fermi level, the Ca d states are mostly final states. There is a good agreement between our calculated electron energy loss, refractive index, extinction coefficient and absorption coefficient with experiment especially in the low energy part of the spectra. Our scissors shifted results of the dielectric constants in the presence of spin–orbit coupling have an average deviation of about 7% from the experiment.

The spin–orbit coupling has a very small (almost negligible) effect on the optical properties. At high energies, for Mg₃N₂ it has a more pronounced effect on the details of the peak positions (overestimates/underestimates the peaks height) and the average values of the calculated extinction coefficient and imaginary part of dielectric function. The nature of the fundamental gap in these materials were found to be direct for Be₃N₂ and Mg₃N₂ and indirect for Ca₃N₂.
