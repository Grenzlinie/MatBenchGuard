# An estimation
of the quadratic electrostrictive coefficients
in LiCl, LiBr, NaI, KF and RbF crystals*

W. Kucharczyk

Institute of Physics, Technical University of Łódź, Wólczańska 219, 93-005 Łódź, Poland

Received: April 22, 1987

## Quadratic electrostriction | Alkali halides

Abstract. An empirical connection between the quadratic electrostrictive coefficients and the relative change in the bond length is reported. Employing the empirical rule an attempt is made to predict the electro- strictive coefficients in LiCl, LiBr, NaI, KF and RbF.

## 1. Introduction

The quadratic electrostrictive coefficients $d_{1111}, d_{1122}$ and $d_{1212}$ have been measured at room temperature in ten halite-type alkali halide crystals: LiF, NaF, NaCl, NaBr, KCl, KBr, KI, RbCl, RbBr and RbI (Bohatý and Haussühl, 1977). It is known that the electrostrictive coefficients are related to the electrostrictive-elastooptic contribution to the quadratic electrooptic effect. Because the relative quadratic electrooptic coefficients $g_{1111}-g_{1122}$ have been measured in fifteen alkali halides (Haussühl and Hesse, 1968) the electrostrictive coefficients in LiCl, LiBr, NaI, KF and RbF are also of interest. The determination of the elastooptic-electrostrictive term is necessary to discuss the nature of the quadratic electrooptic effect and interpret the theoretical results obtained, for an example, in the framework of the bond-charge model (Kucharczyk, 1987). There exist also some calculations of the electrostriction (Grindlay and Wong, 1969; Srinivasan and Srinivasan, 1972; Goyal et al., 1978). One can compare the agreement between theory and experiment by evaluating the quantity

$$
\sigma_{i j k l}=\left\{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{n}\left[\left(d_{i j k l}^{\exp }-d_{i j k l}^{\text {theor }}\right) / d_{i j k l}^{\exp }\right]^{2}\right\}^{1 / 2},
\tag{1}
$$

where the summation is extended over the $n$ species under investigation.
Calculations performed by Grindlay and Wong (1969) are based on the model of a finite non-piezoelectric ionic crystal composed of nonpolarizable point charges. Their approach leads to coefficients $f_{klmn}$ connected with $d_{ijmn}$ by

$$d_{ijmn} = -s_{ijkl}f_{klmn} \text{ (Preu and Haussühl, 1983),} \tag{2}$$

where $s_{ijkl}$ is the elastic compliance. For LiF and NaCl slight different experimental values of the electrostrictive coefficients are reported (Bohatý and Haussühl, 1977; Luymes, 1983; Preu and Haussühl, 1983). Therefore we employed their average values but neglected data for NaCl of Zheludev and Fotchenkov (1958) which seem to be to small (Preu and Haussühl, 1983). Signs of all coefficients were taken according to Preu and Haussühl (1983). Using theoretical values of Grindlay and Wong (1969) for ten alkali halides for which the electrostrictive coefficients have been measured one can obtain $\sigma_{1111}=20\%$, $\sigma_{1122}=41\%$ and $\sigma_{1212}=126\%$. Other theoretical coefficients were derived on the basis of the shell model and more refined interaction potentials. The deviations for values derived by Srinivasan and Srinivasan (1972) are as follows: for LiF, NaF, NaCl, NaBr, KCl, KBr and KI $\sigma_{1111}^{\text{I}}=37\%$, $\sigma_{1122}^{\text{I}}=58\%$ and $\sigma_{1212}^{\text{I}}=110\%$ and for NaF, NaCl, NaBr, KCl, KBr and KI $\sigma_{1111}^{\text{II}}=36\%$, $\sigma_{1122}^{\text{II}}=60\%$ and $\sigma_{1212}^{\text{II}}=113\%$ where superscripts I and II denote different models. The latest theoretical coefficients published by Goyal et al. (1978) for LiF, NaCl, KCl, KBr and KI lead to $\sigma_{1111}=20\%$, $\sigma_{1122}=54\%$ and $\sigma_{1212}=104\%$. In the theoretical treatments the temperature effects were neglected therefore their results are valid at 0 K. However, one can expect that changes in $d_{ijkl}$ between the low and room temperatures are not significant (Bohatý and Haussühl, 1977). Using Eq. (2) we employed components $s_{ijkl}$ determined at room temperature (Landolt-Börnstein, 1979). Taking into account the values of $\sigma_{ijkl}$ one can note that only for $d_{1111}$ the agreement with the experiments is satisfactory. In the present work we will discuss an empirical connection between the relative change induced in the bond length by the low-frequency electric field and the electrostrictive coefficients in the NaCl-type alkali halides.

## 2. Results
Recently, the simple model of the quadratic transverse electrostrictive effect in crystals belonging to the point symmetry group $m3m$ was considered (Kucharczyk, 1986). In this approach two contributions were taken into account. The first term $d_{ijkl}^{(s)}$ was related to the relative displacement of the positive and negative ions $Q$. The second one was related to the coupling of transverse and longitudinal deformation. Employing the known experimental data one can find a roughly linear dependence of $d_{1111}$, $d_{1122}$

It seems to be reasonable because within a given structure type when identical interaction potentials are assumed one can expect that the same deviations of ions from equilibrium position results in comparable changes in the lattice constants.

Following Shih and Yariv (1982) the relative change in the bond length induced in the NaCl-type crystals by the electric field $E$ can be given by

$$
\frac{Q}{d}=\frac{3 \varepsilon_{0}[\varepsilon(0)-\varepsilon(\infty)] \varepsilon(\infty) a^{3} E}{4 e_{\mathrm{s}}[\varepsilon(\infty)+2]}, \tag{3}
$$

where $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the permittivity of free space, $\varepsilon(0)$ and $\varepsilon(\infty)$ are the static and high-frequency dielectric constants, respectively, $e_{\mathrm{s}}$ is the Szigeti effective charge and $a$ is the lattice constant. The Szigeti charge can be derived from refractive data or calculated as the ratio (Lawaetz, 1971)

$$
e_{\mathrm{s}}=e C / \hbar \omega_{p}. \tag{4}
$$

Here $e$ is the electron charge, $C$ is the heteropolar part of the effective band gap (see, for example, Phillips, 1970) and $\omega_{p}$ is the plasma frequency given in the NaCl-type crystal by

$$
\omega_{p}^{2}=32 e^{2} / \varepsilon_{0} m a^{3}, \tag{5}
$$

where $m$ is the electron mass.

For the known experimental values of $d_{i j k l}$ the least-squares method leads to the following dependences

$$
d_{1111}=1.39\ (Q / d)^{2}+0.29 \cdot 10^{-20}\ \text{m}^2\text{V}^{-2}, \tag{6a}
$$

$$
d_{1122}=-0.559\ (Q / d)^{2}-0.08 \cdot 10^{-20}\ \text{m}^2\text{V}^{-2}, \tag{6b}
$$

$$
d_{1212}=0.801\ (Q / d)^{2}+0.21 \cdot 10^{-20}\ \text{m}^2\text{V}^{-2}, \tag{6c}
$$

where $Q/d$ is obtained from Eq. (3) and $e_{\mathrm{s}}$ from Eq. (4). Evaluating the deviations yields for Eqs. (6) $\sigma_{1111}=22\%$, $\sigma_{1122}=30\%$ and $\sigma_{1212}=64\%$. Using $e_{\mathrm{s}}$ derived from the refractive data (Lowndes and Martin, 1969) one

can obtain $1-3\%$ greater values of $\sigma_{ijkl}$. In our calculations we employed dielectric constants $\varepsilon(0)$ and $\varepsilon(\infty)$ of Lowndes and Martin (1969), values of the heteropolar term $C$ listed by Levine (1973) and the lattice constants from Landolt-Börnstein (1973).
