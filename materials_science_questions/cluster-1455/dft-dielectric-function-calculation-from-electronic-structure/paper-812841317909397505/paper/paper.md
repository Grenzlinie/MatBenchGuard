# Effect of oxygen vacancy on $\mathbf{Sb^{3+}, Nb^{3+}}$ and $\mathbf{V^{3+}}$ doped $\mathbf{KTaO_3}$ compounds

İsmail Yücel$^\text{a}$ ismaillyucell@gmail.com , Seyfettin Çakmak$^\text{b}$

$^\text{a}$Süleyman Demirel University, Graduate School of Natural And Applied Science, 32260 Isparta/Turkey

$^\text{b}$Süleyman Demirel University, Faculty of Arts and Sciences, 32260 Isparta/Turkey

## Abstract
In this study, the structural, electronic and optical properties of undoped and $\text{Sb}^{3+}$, $\text{Nb}^{3+}$ and $\text{V}^{3+}$ doped defective $\text{KTaO}_3$ compounds are investigated using the full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method within the density functional theory (DFT). We have calculated bond length, band gap energies and density of states for all compounds. For undoped $\text{KTaO}_3$ compound, the lattice parameter and bond length of Ta-O are found 4.0301 Å and 2.0150 Å, respectively. Besides, all optical parameters (real and imaginary parts of dielectric function, absorption coefficient, reflectivity and refraction index) are calculated. The refractive indices and percentage reflection increase with the doping and defect. This theoretical paper motivates further future studies.

**Keywords:** DFT, Perovskite material, Electronic properties, Optic properties, Density of state

## 1. Introduction
Perovskite materials have attracted the great attention of researchers due to their interesting properties [1-3] such that their optical properties [4], using as water splitting [5-9]. $\text{KTaO}_3$ is one of these perovskite materials that is used in many applications such as red luminescence [10] and production of $\text{H}_2$ with water splitting [11-12]. Besides the band gap energy of $\text{KTaO}_3$ is reported between 3.40 eV and 3.60 eV which exhibits wide band gap semiconductor properties [13]. This amount of energy corresponds to ultraviolet region in the

electromagnetic spectrum. It means that $KTaO_3$ compound can only use %4 of solar energy as a photocatalyst. The photocatalyst properties of $KTaO_3$ compound can be improved by doping atoms. The band gap energy can be regulatable to the visible region in the electromagnetic spectrum and solar energy efficiency can be increased by doping atoms. A significant improvement under the visible light of $KTaO_3$ has been observed by forming composite system with CdS and $MoS_2$ [14]. The aim of this work is to examine the effect of doping atoms ($Sb^{3+}$, $Nb^{3+}$ and $V^{3+}$) on $KTaO_3$ compound. The structural, electronic and optic properties of undoped and $Sb^{3+}$, $Nb^{3+}$ and $V^{3+}$ doped $KTaO_3$ compounds have been studied using the full linear augmented plane wave method (FP-LAPW) [15] within the density functional theory (DFT) [16].

## 2. Computational Details

In this paper, the calculations have been performed using WIEN2k code [17-19]. This code based on the FP-LAPW method within the DFT. The exchange correlation energy potential is treated using Perdew-Burke-Enzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [20]. In order to improve band gap and electronic structure calculations, Tran-Blaha modification of the Beckee-Johnson potential has been used in the self-consistent cycle (SCF) calculations [21-22]. The convergence parameter $R_{MT}*K_{MAX}$ is set to 7. ($R_{MT}$ is the smallest atomic sphere radius in the unit cell and $K_{MAX}$ is the magnitude of the largest K vector). $G_{MAX}$, the plane wave vector, magnitude of the largest vector, is equal to 12. The cutoff energy for separating core from valence state is set to -8.0 Ry.

## 3. Results and Discussions

### 3.1 Structural properties

The $KTaO_3$ compound has a cubic crystal structure (Fig. 1. (a)) which is space group ($Pm$-$3m$) [23]. The K, Ta and O atoms are at the corners, in the body center and the center of face in the cubic crystal structure, respectively. The undoped and doped $KTaO_3$ compounds are formed using 2x2x2 supercell in order to replace Ta atoms with Sb, V and Nb respectively. Ion charge of K, Ta and O atoms are (+1), (+5) and (-2), respectively. Because of this $KTaO_3$ compound has a zero charge. When $Sb^{3+}$, $Nb^{3+}$ and $V^{3+}$ atoms have been doped, one oxygen vacancy is formed to provide charge balance. This vacancy causes the formation of defective

structures. Defective structures have been created in two different ways using the chemical formula of $KTa_{1-x}Y_xO_{3-x}$ (Y= Sb, V and Nb and x=0.125). Firstly, the one oxygen atom has been removed between doping atom and Ta atom (defect-1). Secondly, the one oxygen atom has been removed between Ta and Ta atom (defect-2). The ground state lattice parameters are calculated by fitting experimental lattice parameter [24]. In the present study, we have calculated lattice parameters, bulk modulus, pressure derivatives of bulk modulus, bond length at the ground state by using Murnaghan equation of state (EOS) [25] to the total energy versus volume graph (Fig. 2). For undoped $KTaO_3$ compound, the lattice parameter and bond length of Ta-O are found 4.0301 Å and 2.0150 Å, respectively. These calculations are in a good agreement with other theoretical results [12, 23, 26]. Detailed structural properties have been reported in Table 1 for defective structures. The bulk modulus of defect-1 is bigger than the bulk modulus of defect-2. On the other hand, the oxygen vacancies at different points do not cause a huge change in lattice parameters and ground state energy.

### 3.2 Electronic properties

The total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) for undoped $KTaO_3$ have been shown in Fig. 3. The calculated band gap from TDOS is 3.52 eV for $KTaO_3$ compound which is in a good agreement with experimentally and theoretically reported band gap values such as 3.42 eV [27], 3.60 eV [11] and 3.57 eV [24]. The valence band consists of two sub-band. The low energy sub-band between -11.27 eV and -10.94 eV result from 2p-states of O atoms. The valence band lies between -5.45 eV and Fermi level result from hybridization of O 2p-states and Ta 5d-states. This hybridization shows the covalent bond type between the Ta and O atoms. This result is closely matched with other theoretical studies [12, 23]. Besides the first peaks in conduction band occur at about 3.51 eV for undoped $KTaO_3$ compound, these peaks mainly result from the 5d-states of Ta atoms.

The TDOS plots for doped and defective $KTaO_3$ compounds are presented in Fig. 4. The obtained TDOS indicates that $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compound exhibits metallic characteristics for defect-1 and defect-2. It means that the location of the oxygen vacancy does not affect the characteristic features of the $Nb^{3+}$-doped compound. $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds exhibits semiconductor and metallic characteristics for defect-1 and defect-2, respectively. The band gap energies of $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds exhibiting semiconducting properties are 1.23 eV and 0.79

eV, respectively. In addition, the formation of trapping bands have been found in $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compound. How these trapping bands occur will be explained in the next section with PDOS calculations.

The PDOS plots for doped and defective $KTaO_3$ compounds are given in Fig. 5. As can be seen clearly from Fig. 5 (a) and Fig. 5 (b), the $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compound exhibits same behaviour. The valence band consists of two sub-band and the first peak in the conduction band starts with negative energy value for defect-1 and defect2. The low energy sub-band in the valence band results form the p-states of O atoms. The p-states of O atoms, the d-states of Ta and Nb atoms are formed the high energy sub-band in the valence band. Moreover, the d-states of Nb and Ta atoms dominantly form the conduction band. The first peak in the conduction band starts -1.17 eV and -1.06 eV for defect-1 and defect-2, respectivelty. The d-states of Nb atom cause changing of the characteristic features of compound. The $KTaO_3$ compound turns into from the semiconductor to metallic with Nb doping. The location of oxygen vacancy does not affect the characteristic features. The Sb-doped $KTaO_3$ compounds exhibit different properties in place of the oxygen vacancy. The Sb-doped defective $KTaO_3$ compound shows the semiconductor features for defect-1 whose band gap energy is 1.23 eV, whereas The Sb-doped defective $KTaO_3$ compound shows the metallic features for defect-2. The new sub-band occurs around the Fermi level. Its energy range is between -0.78 eV and Fermi level. It can be seen from Fig. 5 (c), the s- and p-states of Sb atoms cause the formation of this band. Furthermore, the decrease of the band gap energy results from the s-states of Sb atom and d-states of Ta atom for defect-1. On the other hand, for defect-2, the s-states of Sb atom and d-states of Ta atom shift toward to Fermi level (Fig. 5 (d)). For this reason, the Sb-doped defective $KTaO_3$ compound has a metallic property for defect-2. The characteristic properties of V-doped compounds have changed by the location of oxygen vacancy (Fig 5 (c) and Fig. 5 (d)). The V-doped defective $KTaO_3$ compound exhibits the metallic features for defect-2 (Fig. 5 (f)). When the oxygen vacancy has occurred between Ta and Ta atoms, the V-doped defective $KTaO_3$ compound turns into the metallic character due to the d-states of V atom. In addition, the conduction band results from the p-states of O atoms, d-states of Ta and V atoms. The valence band consists of p-states of O and K atoms, d-states of Ta and V atoms. The dominant ones are the p-states of K, the p-states of O and the d-states of V, for defect-2. When the oxygen vacancy has occurred between V and Ta atoms (defect-1), the V-doped defective $KTaO_3$ compound exhibits semiconductor features (Fig. 5 (e)). The d-states of V atom has formed the trapping band in the forbidden band. The energy range of trapping band

is between 0.17 eV and 0.65 eV. Moreover, the new sub-band in the valence band results from the d-states of V atoms whose energy range is between -0.92 eV and Fermi level.

### 3.3 Optical properties

The optical properties of undoped and doped $KTaO_3$ compounds are determined by the dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$, which is defined as a function of photon energy based on frequency: $\varepsilon(\omega) = \varepsilon_1(\omega) + i\varepsilon_2(\omega)$. The $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ are defined the real and imaginary parts of dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$, respectively. The dielectric function has provided an insight into the relationship of a material to electromagnetic radiation. For this reason, the imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ of the dielectric function has been calculated from the momentum matrix elements between the occupied and unoccupied wave functions.

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)=\frac{V^{2} e^{2}}{2 \pi m^{2} \omega^{2}} x \int d^{3} k \sum_{n n^{\prime}}|k n| p\left|k n^{\prime}\right|^{2} f(k n) x\left[1-f\left(k n^{\prime}\right)\right] \partial\left(E_{k n}-E_{k n^{\prime}}-\omega\right) \quad (1)
$$

where $f(kn)$ is the Fermi distribution function, V is the volume of system, m is the mass of electron, e is the electron charge, $p$ is the momentum matrix element between the states of $n$ and $n'$ and $|kn|$ is the crystal wave function corresponding to the $\mathrm{n}^{\text {th }}$ eigenvalue $E_{kn}$ with crystal wave function $k$. After the calculation of $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$, the real part $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ of dielectric function has been derived from the imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ by using the Kramers-Kronig relationship [28]. It can be written as:

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\pi} \mathbf{P} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega^{\prime} \varepsilon_{2}\left(\omega^{\prime}\right) \mathrm{d} \omega^{\prime}}{\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}} \quad (2)
$$

The obtained real part $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ and imaginary part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ of dielectric function for undoped $KTaO_3$ compound are presented in Fig. 6 (a)-(b). The calculated static dielectric constant at zero energy is 3.76 for undoped $KTaO_3$. The maximum static dielectric constant is 7.02 at 4.75 eV. The static dielectric constant is negative between 6.93 eV and 7.25 eV. It means that $KTaO_3$ compound exhibits metallic character in this energy range. Fig. 6(b) shows the imaginary part of dielectric function. The peaks formed in the ultraviolet region represent optical transitions of electrons passing from the valence band to the conduction band. The first three peaks A, B and C are 4.90 at 4.99 eV, 4.83 at 5.92 eV and 5.09 at 6.73 eV, respectively.

The real and imaginary part of the dielectric function of the doped and defective $KTaO_3$ compounds are shown in Fig. (7). The static dielectric constants for defect-1 at the zero frequency limit are 5.55, 4.79 and 6.61 for $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$, $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$, respectively. The static dielectric constants reach the maximum values in infrared region for $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds. The maximum static dielectric constant values are 7.06 at 1.51 eV and 10.53 at 1.27 eV for $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds respectively. On the other hand, the static dielectric constant of $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compound is 7.76 at 1.86 eV. This energy corresponds to the visible region of the electromagnetic spectrum. The $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$, $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds have 5.00, 5.31 and 46.87 static dielectric constant values at zero energy for defect-2, respectively. The maximum static dielectric constant for $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compound is 49.21 at 0.12 eV, whose energy corresponds to the infrared region of the electromagnetic spectrum. The maximum static dielectric constants of $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds are 6.37 at 2.27 eV and 8.25 at 2.22 eV, respectively. These energy values respresent the visible region of electromagnetic spectrum. Fig. 7(b-d) show the imaginary part of dielectric function for defect-1 and defect-2. The formed peaks represent optical transitions of electrons passing from the valence band to the conduction band. The energy values of first three peaks are given in Table 2.

## 4. Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the structural, electronic and optical properties of undoped and $Sb^{3+}$, $Nb^{3+}$, $V^{3+}$ doped defective $KTaO_3$ compounds. Our calculations have been performed using WIEN2k code within the DFT. The defective structures have occurred with the doping of $Sb^{3+}$, $Nb^{3+}$ and $V^{3+}$ atoms. In order to provide charge balance, the oxygen vacancy is formed within the crystal structure in two different points. They have been called as defect-1 and defect-2. The bulk modulus of defect-1 is bigger than the bulk modulus of defect-2. On the other hand, the oxygen vacancies at different points do not cause a huge change in lattice parameters and ground state energy for doped and defective structures. TDOS and PDOS calculations have been performed to examine electronic properties for all structures. For undoped $KTaO_3$, the band gap energy is calculated as 3.52 eV, which is in a good agreement with literature. It has been found that, The $KTa_{0.875}Nb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compound exhibits metallic characteristics for defect-1 and defect-2. It means that the location of the oxygen vacancy does not affect the characteristic features of the $Nb^{3+}$-doped compound. $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds exhibits semiconductor characteristics for defect-1. The band gap energies of $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds are 1.23 eV and 0.79 eV, respectively. In addition, the formation of trapping bands have been found in $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compound. The d-states of V atom has formed the trapping band. The energy range of trapping band is between 0.17 eV and 0.65 eV. On the other hand, for defect-2, $KTa_{0.875}Sb_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ and $KTa_{0.875}V_{0.125}O_{2.875}$ compounds demonstrates metallic characteristics. It means that, the location of oxygen vacancy have changed characteristic features of the $Sb^{3+}$ and $V^{3+}$ doped $KTaO_3$ compounds. The real and imaginary part of dielectric function have been calculated to understand optical properties of all compounds. It has found that, the static dielectric constant at the zero frequency limit varies with the location of oxygen vacancy. In addition, the maximum value of the absorption coefficients corresponds to ultraviolet region of the electromagnetic spectrum for all compounds. The refractive indices and percentage reflection increase with the

doping and defect. This theoretical paper is of great interest for experimental and theoretical researchers. It motivates further future studies.
