## ABSTRACT

Digital epitaxial rare-earth oxide layers are grown on Si(1 1 1) substrates by molecular beam epitaxy at substrate temperatures as low as 200 °C. It is demonstrated by X-ray diffraction and transmission electron microscopy that coherent digital layers form with an abrupt interface to the substrate. Theoretical investigations employing density functional theory demonstrate the potential in designing physical properties by strain. The large lattice mismatch of 9% between $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$ and $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ allows for an intentional variation of the internal strain in the layers over a wide range.

### 1. Introduction

Designing material properties by growing at will single atomic layers is at the heart of molecular beam epitaxy (MBE). Challenges arise when materials need to be combined with different crystal structures and in cases where the lattice mismatch exceeds a few percent. The epitaxial growth then usually leads to defect formation due to lattice relaxation in the growing layer. This limits the application in devices. In addition, the growth proceeds three-dimensionally and part of the control for the complete structure is lost. On the other hand, if growth proceeds coherently, the layers can be strained and their physical properties altered compared to their bulk values. In order to utilize strain as a design tool in epitaxy, one needs suitable substrates with different lattice constants or, as demonstrated here, layered structures with tunable lattice parameters.

Rare-earth oxides (REO) including $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$ and $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ form a group of materials with a large variety of interesting material and physical properties. Besides their potential technological relevance for CMOS integration as gate dielectric layer with a high dielectric constant $\kappa$ [1–3] and their variable magnetic properties due to the localized f-electrons of the rare-earth metal atoms they also display a fascinating variation of complex crystal structures. The stable crystal structures are hexagonal for $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$, cubic (bixbyite) for $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$, but distorted perovskite for $\ce{LaLuO_{3}}$ [4]. This enables a unique variability in the structural properties of REO layers grown by molecular beam epitaxy.

### 2. Experimental details

Details of the growth procedure have been published recently [5]. $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$ and $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ serve as source materials and are evaporated from high-temperature effusion cells [6] assisted by an additional molecular oxygen gas flow from a source port location.

In our previous growth experiments we attempted periodic layering of 1 monolayer (ML) $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ and 1 ML $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$ on a Si(1 1 1) substrate at a substrate temperature of $T_S = 700$ °C. The epitaxial layer was grown coherently with a smooth interface to the Si substrate. The crystal structure determined by TEM and X-ray diffraction was bixbyite (cubic). However, further characterization by TEM did not show any expected layer contrast and no superlattice diffraction peaks were obtained in X-ray diffraction as would be present for a periodic arrangement of layers [5]. We explained this behavior by the high formation enthalpy deduced from DFT calculations of digital layers compared to random alloys. Therefore, the intermixing of $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$ and $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ is energetically favorable. This suggests to use a kinetically limited growth and to attempt a layering with a larger period. The first can be achieved by reducing the substrate temperature and therefore limiting the diffusion, the second is done by increasing the individual layer thickness to 2 ML or more. We found that $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$ changes its crystal structure from the metastable cubic to the thermodynamically stable hexagonal crystal structure within the first few monolayers during the deposition on Si(1 1 1). Therefore, the initial layer is chosen to be $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ with a stable cubic phase. Subsequently 12 layers with 2 ML $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ and 2 ML $\ce{La_{2}O_{3}}$ each are grown at variable temperatures on Si(1 1 1) substrates. As a final layer 2 ML of $\ce{Lu_{2}O_{3}}$ were added. The thermal transient within the effusion cells after opening and closing of the shutters makes it necessary to introduce growth interruptions of 150 s between the individual layers to stabilize the individual

## 3. Density functional calculations

Structural and electronic properties of the $(La_{1-x}Lu_x)_2O_3$ system are investigated by density functional calculations as implemented in the abinit computer code [7,8]. The local density approximation (LDA)+U [9] within the fully localized limit (FLL) is used to treat the exchange correlation and to add an onsite Coulomb correlation for the explicitly treated f-electrons. The values are identical with our previous calculations [10]: La: $U=7.47$ eV, $J=0.989$ eV, Gd: $U=9.2$ eV, $J=1.2$ eV, and Lu: $U=10.941$ eV, $J=1.449$ eV and are based on the evaluation for rare-earth pnictides [11]. Elastic properties, phonon band structure, and dielectric properties are calculated for $La_2O_3$ by employing response function theory. In this case, since La does not possess occupied f levels, a treatment within LDA is sufficient [10]. Hartwigsen-Goedecker-Hutter (HGH) pseudopotentials [12] are used for the LDA treatment, whereas the projector augmented wave (PAW) method [13,14] is used in the LDA+U case.

## 4. Results

By changing the distribution of the $La_2O_3$ and $Lu_2O_3$ in the layer the electronic properties are modified due to strain. As an important finding for strain dependent properties the dielectric properties of hexagonal $La_2O_3$ are calculated. The calculated static dielectric tensor component $\varepsilon_{33}$ (along the c-axis) for the stable hexagonal crystal of $La_2O_3$ is given in Fig. 1 as a function of the lattice constant for hydrostatic strain. With increasing lattice constant the dielectric constant increases. This is in qualitative agreement with experimental results of cubic ionic compounds which show positive volume-dependent contributions to the dielectric constant as discussed on the basis of the Claudius-Mosotti equation [15]. Due to the calculated volume dependence it might be of advantage to use the metastable bixbyite crystal structure of $La_2O_3$ which has a 13% larger volume than the corresponding hexagonal cell, in agreement between our calculations and reported experimental values [16,17]. This value corresponds to an approximate value of $\Delta a/a=4\%$. Furthermore, the layer spacing along the (1 1 1) direction, corresponding to the hexagonal c-axis direction, is even nearly 8% larger in the cubic structure of $La_2O_3$ compared to the hexagonal phase. The calculated increase of the dielectric constant with the unit cell volume and the larger volume of the cubic compared to the hexagonal phase of $La_2O_3$ suggests that cubic epitaxial $La_2O_3$ layers might have advantageous dielectric properties.

A quantitative conclusion is, however, difficult solely on the basis of the XRR measurement. An additional contribution to the peak can originate from the violation of Vegards's law, i.e. the violation of the linear dependence of the lattice constant on the concentration. DFT calculations of model structures in hexagonal geometry show that for perfect $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3/\text{Lu}_2\text{O}_3$ superlattices with a 2 ML thickness the c-axis length deviates from Vegard's law and results in a shorter layer spacing [18]. A similar effect can be expected also for the cubic phases which would result in a smaller out-of-plane lattice constant for layers with an increased digital character and a shift of the X-ray peak.

Further structural information is obtained from TEM investigations. Fig. 4(a) shows a cross-sectional high-resolution TEM image reflecting the superlattice periodicity as weak contrast modulation along the growth direction (marked by arrows). A coherent layer is formed with an abrupt interface to the Si substrate without silicate or silicide interlayer (Fig. 4(b)). This behavior is analogous to the alloyed layers [5]. The individual layers appear straight without visible stress-induced undulations. The surface is smooth and with low roughness, in agreement with the XRR results shown in Fig. 3. This confirms that the layer-by-layer growth indicated by the specular spot intensity oscillations in RHEED results in periodic layers in the final structure with the intended thicknesses.

## 5. Discussion

The growth of $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{Lu}_2\text{O}_3$ on $\text{Si}(1\ 1\ 1)$ from oxide sources proceeds by diffusion of the constituents on the surface. The hypothesis that the observed intermixing can be suppressed by lowering the substrate temperature has been proven successful and shows the advantages of MBE. Surprisingly enough, we find that the layers grow epitaxially and layer-by-layer even at $T_S=200\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$. Therefore, even at this temperature there has to be a sufficient surface mobility of the molecules. The high temperatures necessary to evaporate the oxides $(>2000\ ^{\circ}\text{C})$ together with the observed behavior indicates that the diffusion process is not mediated by forming bulk-like bonds at the surface with subsequent jumps from site to site. Instead, the diffusing molecules are only loosely bound during diffusion and only at the final step incorporated into the crystal. The last step might be influenced by an incorporation barrier. To suppress the intermixing of $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{Lu}_2\text{O}_3$ it is therefore necessary to minimize the amount of active molecules when switching the materials. The growth interruption between the layers has the side-effect that molecules have enough time to get incorporated into the crystal.

The layers are grown coherently on Si. Therefore, the individual $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{Lu}_2\text{O}_3$ experience biaxial strain. The cubic $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3$ layer, with a nominally 5% larger lattice constant than Si, is compressed laterally and therefore dilated out-of-plane. The opposite is true for the $\text{Lu}_2\text{O}_3$ layer, nominally 4% smaller. Our DFT calculation gives a formation energy difference between an alloyed layer and a digital layer with a layer stacking of 1 ML of approximate 15 meV per rare-earth metal atom.

## 6. Conclusions

Since the formation enthalpy of superlattice structures is high [5], their growth is only possible in a kinetically limited regime for which MBE is ideally suited. Supplying $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{Lu}_2\text{O}_3$ alternatingly leads to the formation of a superlattice structure at lower substrate temperatures, whereas at higher temperatures strong intermixing is observed. The high structural quality is confirmed by TEM and XRR. The oxide grows coherently on $\text{Si}(1\ 1\ 1)$ with a very smooth interface. The layer thickness is very homogeneous across the sample. The in-plane lattice constant of the $(\text{La}_{1-x}\text{Lu}_x)_2\text{O}_3$ layer is close to the one of Si for $x=0.5$. Therefore, the $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3$ layers are compressed in-plane and dilated out-of-plane, whereas for the $\text{Lu}_2\text{O}_3$ layers the opposite is true. The strain, which is induced in the REO layers, depends on the size of the layer period and the degree of intermixing which can be influenced by the variation of the substrate temperature.

The achieved coherent growth of REO layers on $\text{Si}(1\ 1\ 1)$ is a major step towards application. We demonstrate on the basis of DFT results that by using a kinetically limited growth mode designing and varying the layer sequence on the atomic scale and with this the physical properties of the layer is possible.
