# Lattice dynamics and inelastic neutron scattering experiments
on andalusite, $Al_2SiO_5$

Mala N. Rao$^{a,*}$, Prabhatasree Goel$^{a}$, Narayani Choudhury$^{a}$, S.L. Chaplot$^{a}$, Subrata Ghose$^{b}$

$^{a}$Solid State Physics Division, Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, Trombay, Mumbai 400 085, India
$^{b}$Mineral Physics Group, Department of Earth and Space Sciences, University of Washington, Box 351310, Seattle, WA 98195, USA

Received 13 November 2001; accepted 10 December 2001 by C.N.R. Rao

## Abstract
We report coherent inelastic neutron scattering measurements of the phonon dispersion relations and lattice dynamics shell model calculations of several microscopic and macroscopic properties of andalusite, $Al_2SiO_5$. Andalusite has an orthorhombic structure with 32 atoms/unit cell. The inelastic neutron scattering measurements (up to energy transfers of 45 meV) were carried out using the triple axis spectrometer at Dhruva reactor, India using a single crystal of andalusite and the phonon dispersion relations along the [100] direction have been measured. The shell model calculations have been used to compute the crystal structure, elastic constants, phonon frequencies, dispersion relations, density of states and the specific heat. The calculated results are in good agreement with available experimental data. The computed one-phonon neutron scattering structure factors based on the shell model have been very useful in the planning and analysis of the inelastic neutron scattering experiments. © 2002 Elsevier Science Ltd. ## 1. Introduction
The phase diagram of the aluminosilicate minerals, anda- lusite, sillimanite and kyanite, find important applications in geothermometry and geobarometry [1]. These minerals being highly refractory also find other useful applications. A thorough understanding of the thermodynamic properties and phase transitions of these minerals is therefore important. A basic requirement for the prediction of the thermodynamic properties of minerals at high temperatures and pressures is the knowledge of their phonon properties (in particular, phonon dispersion relations and density of states). Using a combination of inelastic neutron scattering experiments and theoretical shell model calculations, we have undertaken lattice dynamics studies of the alumino- silicate mineral, andalusite. The neutron scattering experiments using single crystals of andalusite provide measurements of its phonon dispersion relations. Using suitable, reliable and transferable interatomic potentials which correctly predicts a variety of microscopic and macroscopic properties of the aluminosilicate poly- morphs—sillimanite and kyanite [2], we have now calcu- lated the crystal structure, elastic constants, phonon frequencies, phonon dispersion relations, density of states, specific heat and mean square atomic displacements of andalusite. The results are found to be in good agreement with available experimental data of andalusite.

The inelastic neutron scattering measurements have been used for experimental determination of the phonon dis- persion relation along the [100] direction in andalusite. An experimental determination of the phonon dispersion relation requires large good quality single crystals, which are rarely available. These measurements provide detailed information about the relations of individual phonon energies with the wave vector and can be very useful in validating theoretical lattice dynamics models. The models in turn may be suitably employed for lattice dynamics studies of various microscopic and macroscopic properties,

The unit cell of andalusite [1] is orthorhombic (Fig. 1) and
it crystallizes in the space group Pnnm ($Z=4$). The
structure possesses edge-sharing aluminium octahedra in
chains that extend along $c$-axis, this accounts for half of
the aluminium atoms. The other half of the aluminium
atoms are five-coordinated; they alternate with silicon
tetrahedra in double chains that also run parallel to $c$-axis.
A variety of experimental measurements including X-ray
[3–6] and neutron diffraction [7], ultrasonic measurements
[8], Raman [9] and infrared [10] spectroscopy, inelastic
neutron scattering studies [11], calorimetric studies
[12–15], etc. have been used to understand the physical
and thermodynamic properties of andalusite. Theoretical
studies [16–20] of the structural, vibrational, thermo-
dynamic properties, electronic structure, etc. employing
methods with varying degrees of sophistication have also
been reported. A unified approach involving experiments
and calculations, explaining these properties in all the
three aluminosilicate polymorphs is, however, still desirable
and has been the goal of the present study.

In Section 2, we describe the lattice dynamical model
used while Section 3 gives the experimental details. The
results are discussed in Section 4 and Section 5 gives our
conclusions.

## 2. Lattice dynamics calculations

Our lattice dynamics calculations were carried out using a
model previously successfully used [2] for sillimanite and
kyanite. The interatomic potentials consisted of Coulomb
and short-range interaction terms. Oxygen ions were
modelled using the shell model, where a mass less shell is
linked to the core by harmonic interactions.

The harmonic lattice dynamics calculations were
performed using the current version of the program DISPR
[21]. The equilibrium crystal structure is computed by mini-
mizing the Gibbs free energy with respect to the structural
variables. The structure thus obtained was used in the
lattice dynamics calculations as a function of pressure.
The symmetry vectors obtained through detailed group
theoretical analysis along the three high-symmetry direc-
tions and the zone centre point were employed to classify
the phonon frequencies obtained into their irreducible
representations. This enabled direct comparisons with the
observed Raman and infrared data as well as the observed
phonon dispersion relations. The corresponding eigen-
vectors were used to calculate the one-phonon neutron
scattering structure factors for scattering from a single
crystal of andalusite for each mode in various planes of
reciprocal space, according to the expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S(\mathbf{Q}, \omega)= & A \sum_{\mathbf{q} j} \frac{\hbar}{2 \omega(\mathbf{q} j)}\left\{n(\mathbf{q} j, \omega)+\frac{1}{2} \pm \frac{1}{2}\right\}\left|F_{j}^{(1)}(\mathbf{Q})\right|^{2} \\
& \times \delta(\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{q}) \delta(\omega \mp \omega(\mathbf{q} j))
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
F_{j}^{(1)}(\mathbf{Q})=\sum_{k} b_{k}^{\mathrm{coh}} \frac{\mathbf{Q} \cdot \boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbf{q} j, k)}{\sqrt{m_{k}}} \exp \left\{-W_{k}(\mathbf{Q})\right\} \exp (i \mathbf{G} \cdot \mathbf{X}(k))
$$

where $b_{k}^{\text{coh}}$ and $m_{k}$ are coherent neutron scattering lengths
and mass of the atom $k$, respectively, $\mathbf{q}$ is the phonon wave
vector, $j$ is the phonon branch index, $\mathbf{G}$ is a reciprocal lattice
vector, $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ is the polarization vector, $\mathbf{X}(k)$ is the coordinate,
$\exp\{-W_{k}(\mathbf{Q})\}$ is the Debye–Waller factor and $A$ is a pro-
portionality constant. $\hbar \mathbf{Q}$ and $\hbar \omega(=E)$ are, respectively, the
momentum and energy transfer on scattering of the neutron,
while $n(\mathbf{q} j, \omega)$ is the phonon population factor given by,

$$
n(\mathbf{q} j, \omega)=\frac{1}{\exp \left(\hbar \omega_{j}(\mathbf{q}) / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\right)-1}
$$

The computed structure factors aid in the identification of
regions in reciprocal space with large cross-sections and
have been very useful in the planning of the experiments
and in the data analysis.

The phonon density of states was computed and was used
to evaluate the specific heat at constant volume $C_{\mathrm{V}}(T)$. To
compare with the experimental data, we have computed
the specific heat at constant pressure through a calculation
of the bulk modulus and the thermal expansion $C_{\mathrm{P}}=C_{\mathrm{V}}+$
$TVB\alpha^{2}$, with $\alpha$ being the volume coefficient of thermal
expansion, $B$, the bulk modulus, and $V$, the molar volume.
We used the computed values of $\alpha$, $B$ and $V$.

## 3. Neutron inelastic scattering experiments

The neutron scattering experiments were carried out on the triple axis spectrometer [22] at Dhruva reactor. This is a direct geometry spectrometer for inelastic neutron scattering located at beam T1007 at Dhruva. It has a copper (111) monochromator, and a pyrolytic graphite (0002) analyser. The experiments were performed at a constant final energy $E_{\text{f}} = 14.8$ meV in the neutron energy loss mode with a PG filter placed after the sample to prevent higher order contamination. The constant momentum-transfer mode was used for the measurements. The momentum transfer varied from 3 to $5$ Å$^{-1}$. The optically clear andalusite single crystal used in our experiments is a natural sample of volume $8$ cm$^3$. This crystal was aligned with its $z$-axis vertical and the measurements were carried out at room temperature and ambient pressure.

Previously, several phonon modes have been measured in the [001] direction up to energies of 30 meV [11]. In the work presented here, phonons along [100] up to energies of 45 meV have been determined. The calculated structure factors based on the model calculations guided the choice of wave vector transfers and resultant instrument settings in the actual experiment.

## 4. Results and discussion

The calculated crystal structure parameters of andalusite are compared with available experimental X-ray diffraction data in Table 1. The computed equilibrium structure is in good agreement with experiments. The elastic constants (Table 2) are within 6% of the respective experimental [8] values except for $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$.

The group theoretical classification at the $\Gamma$ point is given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma =&\, 14\text{A}_{\text{g}} + 14\text{B}_{1\text{g}} + 10\text{B}_{2\text{g}} + 10\text{B}_{3\text{g}} + 9\text{A}_{\text{u}} + 9\text{B}_{1\text{u}} \\
&\, + 15\text{B}_{2\text{u}} + 15\text{B}_{3\text{u}}
\end{aligned}
$$

The $\text{A}_{\text{g}}$, $\text{B}_{1\text{g}}$, $\text{B}_{2\text{g}}$ and $\text{B}_{3\text{g}}$ modes are Raman active, while the $\text{B}_{1\text{u}}$, $\text{B}_{2\text{u}}$ and $\text{B}_{3\text{u}}$ modes are infrared active. The $\text{A}_{\text{u}}$ modes are optically inactive and can be observed only from inelastic neutron scattering experiments. The calculated long wavelength vibrational frequencies are compared with available Raman and infrared spectroscopic data in Fig. 2. For the infrared active modes, the LO and TO modes are separately shown. The calculated phonon frequencies are in satisfactory agreement with the observed optical data, although the calculations gave frequencies that are slightly lower than experiments.

Hence, phonons belonging to representations $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_3$ alone could be measured in these experiments. The computed phonon density of states of andalusite (Fig. 4) spans the spectral range from 0 to 140 meV. The density of states shows a broad peak in the 0–100 meV range, and a sharp peak around 110 meV. Furthermore, a very distinct gap in energy is seen around 100 meV. The spectrum is similar to that obtained in sillimanite and kyanite, owing to their structural similarities. The computed partial density of states (Fig. 5) give the dynamical contributions to frequency distribution arising from the various species of crystallographically distinct atoms Al1, Al2, Si, O1, O2, O3, O4 along the x, y and z directions. The Al atom contributions from atoms occupying the Al1 (octahedral site) and Al2 (five-coordinated) sites are significantly different. The Al1 and all the oxygen atoms are dynamically more anisotropic as compared to Al2 and Si atoms.

The computed specific heat of andalusite was found to be in very good agreement with the experimental measurements (Fig. 6), particularly in the low-temperature region. The calculated correction $(C_{\rm P}-C_{\rm V})$ to the specific heat is around 2% at 1500 K, for andalusite.

## 5. Conclusions

In this paper, we have discussed the inelastic neutron scattering measurements of the phonon dispersion curves of andalusite, $\rm Al_2SiO_5$ and its interpretation based on theoretical calculations based on a transferable shell model. The results provide a strong experimental basis to validate the model, which in turn has been used to provide a microscopic understanding of the structural, vibrational and thermodynamic properties of the mineral andalusite. The calculated phonon structure factors were very useful in the planning, execution and analysis of the experimental data and emphasize the role of theoretical models in the interpretation of complex experimental data. Our motivation in the study of the $\rm Al_2SiO_5$ polymorphs—andalusite, sillimanite and kyanite has been to provide an atomic level understanding of the macroscopic thermodynamic properties through a combination of experiments and lattice dynamics calculations. We hope these studies will eventually lead to an interpretation of their phase diagram.
