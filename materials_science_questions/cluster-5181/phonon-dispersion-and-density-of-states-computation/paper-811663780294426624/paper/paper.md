# Vibrational Spectra and Quantum Chemical Calculations of the Pure and Mixed Cluster Anions $[Si_{x} Ge_{4-x}]^{4-}$ and $[Ge_{x} Sn_{4-x}]^{4-}(x=0-4)$ in Compounds with Potassium and Cesium

Alim Ormeci, $^{[a]}$ Umut Aydemir, $^{[a]}$ and Mehmet Somer*[b]

In Memory of Professor Hans Georg von Schnering

**Keywords:** Zintl anions; Pure and mixed hetero-tetrahedranide anions; Raman spectroscopy; Force constants; Quantum chemical calculations

Abstract. The Raman spectroscopic studies on samples with the starting compositions $Cs_{4}[Si_{2} Ge_{2}]$ and $M_{4}[Ge_{2} Sn_{2}](M=K, Cs)$ verify unambiguously the presence of phases composed of the mixed cluster moieties $[Si_{x} Ge_{4-x}]^{4-}$ and $[Ge_{x} Sn_{4-x}]^{4-}(x=1-3)$ including the pure anions $[E_{4}]^{4-}(E=Si, Ge, Sn)$ . The assignment of the vibrational frequencies is supported by normal coordinate analysis (NCA). The results for the mixed cluster anions are discussed in context with their isoelectronic analogs $P_{x} As_{4-x}(x=1-3)$ and $SbP_{3}$ . Using the characteristic breathing frequencies, one obtains for the ratio factor of the mixed moieties $\kappa=v([Si_{x} Ge_{4-x}]^{4-}) / v(P_{x} As_{4-x})=0.79$ , which is in good agreement with the reported value of 0.77 for the pure cluster series $[E_{4}]^{4-}$  $(E=Si, Ge, Sn)$ and $X_{4}(X=P, As, Sb)$ . The experimental average force constants $f(Si-Ge)=1.01 Ncm^{-1}$ and $f(Ge-Sn)=0.77 Ncm^{-1}$ correspond to arithmetic mean of those obtained for the pure anions in the binaries $M_{4} E_{4}(M=K, Rb, Cs ; E=Si, Ge, Sn)$ . The phonon frequencies of binary $M_{4} E_{4}(M=K, Cs ; E=Si, Ge, Sn)$ and ternary $K_{4} E_{x} E_{4-x}'(E, E'=Si, Ge$ or $Ge, Sn, E ≠E', x=1,2,3)$ were calculated by using pseudopotential electronic structure method SIESTA. Excellent agreement between the calculated and experimental frequencies was obtained for the pure binaries. The theoretical results compare also well with experimental values for the mixed series $[Si_{x} Ge_{4-x}]^{4-}$ , whereas for the corresponding mixed Ge-Sn species the differences between measured and calculated frequencies are greater than $10 \%$ .

## Introduction

The profound investigations of the hetero tetrahedranides M4E4 and their mixtures were initiated and carried out by Prof. Dr. H. G. von Schnering et al. for almost 25 years ago. The existence of the mixed anions $[E_{4-x} E'_{x}]^{4-}(E=Si, E'=Ge)$ was first reported by his PhD student Llanos from X-ray single crystal diffraction measurements of a sample with the nominal composition " $Na_{4}[Si_{2} Ge_{2}]$ ". $^{[1]}$ Careful analysis of the crystal structure data revealed that the selected single crystal did not represent a monophase product but comprised a mixture of the anion series $[E_{x} E'_{4-x}]^{4-}(E=Si, E'=Ge)$ with x=0-4. Similar distribution of the mixed $E_{9}$ cluster were also detected in the system $[E_{x} E'_{9-x}]^{4-}(E=Sn, E'=Ge, Pb ; x=0-9)$ in en andliquid ammonia solutions by NMR spectroscopy. $^{[2,3]}$

Recently, we have reported on the vibrational spectra andforce constants of the hetero-tetrahedranide anions $[E_{4}]^{4-}(E=$  Si, Ge, Sn) in the alkali and earth alkaline metal compounds $M_{4} E_{4}(M=Na, K, Rb, Cs)$ and $Ba_{2} E_{4}(E=Si, Ge)^{[4,5]}$ The investigations have revealed that Raman spectroscopy is a powerful tool to detect and to identify $[E_{4}]^{4-}$ moieties by using the characteristic breathing mode as a "sensor". Meanwhile the method - now combined and supported by quantum chemical calculations and normal coordinate analysis (NCA) - could successfully be applied also on mixed cluster species $[E_{x} E'_{4-x}]^{4-}(E=Ge, E'=Si, Sn)$ with x=1-3. Herein, we report on novel Raman spectroscopic studies on samples with the nominal compositions " $Cs_{4}[Si_{2} Ge_{2}]$ ", " $K_{4}[Ge_{2} Sn_{2}]$ ", and " $Cs_{4}[Ge_{2} Sn_{2}]$ ". The frequencies and force constants of the mixed cluster anions are discussed and compared in context with the isoelectronic series $P_{x} As_{4-x}(x=1,3)$ and $SbP_{3}$ in the gas phase. $^{[6]}$ In addition quantum chemical calculations were performed to compute the internal modes of the pure and mixed cluster anions. The theoretical results are compared with their experimental counterparts.

## Results and Discussion

The successive introduction of a foreign E' atom in an $[E_{4}]^{4-}$ cluster (E', E=Si, Ge, Sn ; E ≠E') with $T_{d}$ symmetry results in three different types of mixed tetrahedro-tetrahedranide ani- ons: $[E' E_{3}]^{4-},[E'_{2} E_{2}]^{4-}$ , and $[E'_{3} E]^{4-}$ . $$\Gamma_{\mathrm{vib}}(C_{3v}) = 2\mathrm{A}_{1}\ (\mathrm{R},\ \mathrm{IR}) + 2\mathrm{E}\ (\mathrm{R},\ \mathrm{IR})\ \mathrm{and}$$

$$\Gamma_{\mathrm{vib}}(C_{2v}) = 3\mathrm{A}_{1}\ (\mathrm{R},\ \mathrm{IR}) + \mathrm{A}_{2}\ (\mathrm{R}) + \mathrm{B}_{1}\ (\mathrm{R},\ \mathrm{IR}) + \mathrm{B}_{2}\ (\mathrm{R},\ \mathrm{IR}).$$

All modes are both Raman and IR active, except for $\mathrm{A}_{2}\ (C_{2v})$ appearing only in the Raman spectrum. Thus, a single-phase $M_4[E'_2E_2]$ with the cluster anion $[E'_2E_2]^{4-}$ and the symmetry $C_{2v}$ is expected to have a simple Raman spectrum consisting of six bands as shown in Figure 2, Figure 3, and Figure 4.

However, the measured spectra are much more complex and patterned suggesting the presence of all five possible mixed and pure tetrahedro-tetrahedranide units.

In the previous assignments of the vibrational spectra of the pure $M_4E_4$ phases $(M = \mathrm{Na,\ K,\ Rb,\ Cs}:\ E = \mathrm{Si,\ Ge,\ Sn})^{[4,5]}$ there were two major problems, which had to be solved: the correct interpretation of the extra bands due to site-symmetry splitting and the overlap of the internal and external modes in the low frequency region. The latter showed up particularly in the spectra of compounds with lighter alkali metals, such as

![](./images/811663780294426624_5.jpg)

Figure 4. Comparison of the Raman spectra of "Cs₄Ge₂Sn₂" and "K₄Ge₂Sn₂". The breathing modes of the [GeₓSn₄₋ₓ]⁴⁻ (x = 0–4) clusters are indicated. (*) = Laser lines at 62, 76, and 83 cm⁻¹; (+) = lattice modes.

Na₄Si₄ and Na₄Ge₄, in which E and F₂ modes strongly split and the superposition of the internal and external vibrations were much more pronounced. The consequence for the strongly patterned mixed "Na₄Si₂Ge₂" and "Na₄Ge₂Sn₂" would be, thus, an increased probability of an overlap between the bands of the pure and mixed cluster species hindering a clear distinction and an unequivocal assignment particularly in the frequency range $v < 200$ cm⁻¹. The reasonable solution of this problem is the substitution of the light sodium atoms by the heavier potassium, rubidium, or cesium atoms. Since alkali metal counterions are primarily involved in lattice translational motions and librations, an increase of their masses would lead to a frequency downshift according to $v \approx \sqrt{m}^{-1}$, whereas the frequencies of the fundamentals remain practically unaffected. In addition, the $M_4E_4$ compounds ($M$ = K, Rb, Cs; $E$ = Si, Ge) and $M_4Sn_4$ crystallize in the cubic K₄Ge₄ and tetragonal NaPb type of structures, respectively, which are known to give rise to quite simple Raman patterns.⁴ Hence, similar behavior is expected also for their mixed cluster phases and particularly for those with cesium as counterion.

As predicted, the Raman spectra of "Cs₄Si₂Ge₂" and "Cs₄Ge₂Sn₂" exhibit patterns of well-separated bands, which allow a profound vibrational analysis. The distinction between modes of the mixed species $[E'_xE_{4-x}]^{4-}$ ($x = 1-3$) and pure $[E_4]^{4-}$ is done by direct comparison of the respective spectra, as depicted in Figure 2 and Figure 3. The former shows the Raman spectrum of "Cs₄Si₂Ge₂" versus the pure binaries Cs₄Si₄ and Cs₄Ge₄. For purpose of a rough distinction it is sufficient to focus firstly on the well-known characteristic breathing modes of the pure cluster moieties at 476 cm⁻¹ (Cs₄Si₄)⁷ and 274 cm⁻¹ (Cs₄Ge₄).⁴ The remaining bands in-between are expected to represent the breathing modes of the three mixed cluster anions $[Si_xGe_{4-x}]^{4-}$ ($x = 1-3$), apart from those at 357 and 282 cm⁻¹ arising from the fundamentals of [Si₄]⁴⁻. They are localized at 446, 413, and 364 cm⁻¹. The simple comparison allows indeed a qualitative statement whether the observed mode is resulting from a pure or any of the three possible mixed clusters, but will fall far short of a reasonable vibrational assignment. For this purpose, NCA was carried out. The first force constant calculations on tetrahedrane-like species with $T_d$ symmetry were performed by Pistorius,⁸ McDowell,⁹ and Edwards¹⁰ on the P₄ molecule. The force field of the tetrahedrane molecule is quite different from that of other cage-like molecules, since all six internal coordinates are bonds, meaning that there is no bending force constant – and hence no bending mode – and all six fundamentals are valence vibrations. The force field used in this case is quite simple and can be described by three force constants: the primary $fr$, and $frr$ and $fr'$ for the adjacent and diametrically opposite bonds, respectively.

In more recent studies, the simple valence force field for P₄ could be successfully applied also to the hetero-tetrahedranides $[E_4]^{4-}$ ($E$ = Si, Ge, Sn) with different alkali metals and barium as counterions. In the present study, the force field calculations for the mixed cluster anions $[Si_xGe_{4-x}]^{4-}$ and $[Ge_xSn_{4-x}]^{4-}$ ($x = 1-3$) with $C_{3v}$ ($x = 1,3$) and $C_{2v}$ ($x = 2$) symmetry were performed by using the slightly extended simple valence force field. Due to the introduction of new bonds – such as (Si–Ge) and (Ge–Sn) – additional primary force constants ($fr1$, $fr2$, $fr1r2$ etc.) were necessary, while the two interaction force constants were maintained. The NCA was carried out based on Wilson FG-matrix method¹¹ using the program Prometheus.¹² The preliminary calculations revealed that a set of four ($C_{3v}$: $fr1$, $fr1r2$, $frr$, $frr'$) and five ($C_{2v}$: $fr1$, $fr2$, $fr1r2$, $frr$, $frr'$) force constants, respectively, were completely sufficient to simulate the experimental frequencies with differences less than 1 %. The initial data consisted of structure parameters from the compound series $M_4E_4$ ($M$ = K, Cs; $E$ = Si, Ge, Sn)¹⁷,¹³⁻¹⁵ and the force constants reported in references⁴,⁵. The bond lengths for the heteroatomic bonds [i.e. (Si–Ge) and (Ge–Sn)] and the corresponding force constants were obtained from the arithmetic mean of the values for the homonuclear $[E_4]^{4-}$ species (Table 1). In each case, the frequencies were calculated from the initial data and refined until the best fit to the observed frequencies was achieved.

Supported by the results of the NCA, the Raman spectra of the "Cs₄Si₂Ge₂" and "Cs₄Ge₂Sn₂" could be completely assigned. The results explain also that the absence of missing bands in the observed spectra is due to accidental coincidence and/or superimposition by the more intense bands (Table 2 and Table 3).

The vibrational behavior and the force constants for the homonuclear $[E_4]^{4-}$ ($E = \text{Si}, \text{Ge}, \text{Sn}, \text{Pb}$) species in context with the isoelectronic pnictogen molecules $X_4$ ($X = \text{P}, \text{As}, \text{Sb}, \text{Bi}$) were first analyzed by *Kliche et al.*$^{[16]}$ The comparison showed that the negative charge on the cluster anions leads to a remarkable decrease of both the vibrational frequencies and the force constants with respect to the neutral $X_4$ units. The ratio of the frequencies $v([E_4]^{4-}) / v(X_4)$ for the isoelectronic species is practically constant and corresponds to $\kappa = 0.77$. This empirical value can be used as a correlation key to check and confirm the assignments made for the mixed cluster anions $[E'_xE_{4-x}]^{4-}$ ($E' = \text{Ge}$; $E = \text{Si}, \text{Sn}$) and the respective isoelectronic analogs $[X'_xX_{4-x}]$

$(X' = \text{P}; X = \text{As}, \text{Sb})$. The only available vibrational spectroscopic data on gaseous pnictogen mixtures stem from $Ozin,^{[6]}$ who investigated the mixed phosphorus-arsenic and phosphorus-antimony vapors as a function of concentration and temperature by Raman spectroscopy in 1970. In the case of gaseous $\text{P}_4\text{-As}_4$ mixtures, the spectra clearly proved that there is no preferred species present in the gas phase but a series of molecules with the general composition $\text{P}_x\text{As}_{4-x}\ (x=0\text{--}4)$, whereas $\text{SbP}_3$ seemed to be the only mixed species detected in the gaseous $\text{P}_4\text{-Sb}_4$ system. The results for the $\text{P}_4\text{-As}_4$ vapor mixtures are in best agreement with those for the mixed isoelectronic anion series $[\text{Si}_x\text{Ge}_{4-x}]^{4-}$ $(x=0\text{--}4)$ in "$\text{Cs}_4\text{Si}_2\text{Ge}_2$". This can easily be seen from comparison of the Raman patterns of the two respective isoelectronic mixtures (molar ratio $\approx1:1$) revealing the identical sequence of five bands of strong to medium intensity, each representing the breathing modes of the $\text{P}_x\text{As}_{4-x}{^{[6]}}$ and $[\text{Si}_x\text{Ge}_{4-x}]^{4-}\ (x=0\text{--}4)$ species in "$\text{Cs}_4\text{Si}_2\text{Ge}_2$", respectively, in the order: $\text{v(P}_4\{600\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}/[\text{Si}_4]^{4-}\{476\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}),\ \ \text{v(P}_3\text{As}\{560\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}/[\text{Si}_3\text{Ge}]^{4-}\{446\ \text{cm}^{-1}\})$, $\text{v(P}_2\text{As}_2\{512\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}/[\text{Si}_2\text{Ge}_2]^{4-}\{413\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}),\ \ \text{v(PAs}_3\{450\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}/[\text{SiGe}_3]^{4-}\{364\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}),\ \ \text{v(As}_4\{340\ \text{cm}^{-1}\}/[\text{Ge}_4]^{4-}\{274\ \text{cm}^{-1}\})$. Based on these frequencies, one obtains for the ratio factor $\kappa=\text{v([Si}_x\text{Ge}_{4-x}]^{4-})/\text{ v(P}_x\text{As}_{4-x})\ (x=0\text{--}4)$ a value of 0.79, which agrees well with the aforementioned correlation key $\kappa=0.77,^{[16]}$ in the calculation, of which not only the breathing modes but entire sets of frequencies were used. The result clearly confirms that the vibrational assignments made for the breathing modes of both the mixed anionic and neutral clusters are reasonable.

No experimental Raman data are available on gaseous $\text{As}_4\text{-Sb}_4$ mixtures, yet, that would have allowed a similar spectral comparison between the spectra of the isoelectronic series $\text{As}_x\text{Sb}_{4-x}$ and $[\text{Ge}_x\text{Sn}_{4-x}]^{4-}\ (x=1\text{--}3)$. For the $\text{P}_4\text{-Sb}_4$ mixtures there is a Raman spectroscopic evidence for the existence of the mixed species $\text{SbP}_3$ in the gas phase. $^{[6]}$ This result is quite remarkable, particularly considering the significant difference in the covalent radii of the two elements $(d_1(\text{P}_4)=2.200\ \text{Å}$, $d_1(\text{Sb}_4)=2.872\ \text{Å}^{[17]})$. The isoelectronic analog to $\text{SbP}_3$ would be the anion $[\text{SnSi}_3]^{4-}$. Unfortunately, the vibrational spectra of the samples with the nominal compositions $\text{Cs}_4\text{Si}_2\text{Sn}_2$ and $\text{Cs}_4\text{Si}_3\text{Sn}$ give no hint for the formation of any mixed species instead showing only the pure binaries $\text{Cs}_4\text{Si}_4$ and $\text{Cs}_4\text{Sn}_4$ as constituents.

### Quantum Mechanical Calculations
For the full structure optimization of the binaries, experimental structure data were used. $^{[4,13\text{--}15,18]}$ In the $M_4\text{Sn}_4\ (M=\text{K},\ \text{Cs})$ compounds, there is only one Wyckoff position for the tin atoms meaning that all $\text{Sn}_4$ tetrahedra are identical. On the other hand, the cubic (space group $P\overline{4}3n$, no. 218) $M_4E_4\ (M=\text{K},\ \text{Cs};\ E=\text{Si},\ \text{Ge})$ phases contain two types of $E_4$ tetrahedra. Whereas type I tetrahedra are regular and formed by $E$ atoms occupying the $8e$ site $(x\ x\ x)$, type II tetrahedra have two long and four short bond lengths, and are formed by $E$ atoms at the $24i$ site $(x\ y\ z)$. As mentioned before, the six fundamentals of the former are related to $\text{A}_1+\text{E}+\text{F}_2$ symmetry types. The degenerate modes split in the type II tetrahedron as $\text{E}\rightarrow\text{A}_1+\text{B}_1$ and $\text{F}_2\rightarrow\text{B}_2+\text{E}$ yielding $2\text{A}_1+\text{B}_1+\text{B}_2+\text{E}$. Experimentally, it is almost impossible to distinguish between type I and type II frequencies. However, the finite displacement method allows calculating the vibrational frequencies of a chosen cluster of atoms by discarding the force constant matrix elements between the atoms of the chosen cluster and the rest of the atoms in the unit cell when constructing the dynamical matrix. Such an approach is particularly well suited to the problem at hand, since the internal modes of the $E_4$ tetrahedra are quasi-independent from their environment. $^{[4,7]}$ Thus, separate calculations on type I and type II tetrahedra in $\text{Cs}_4\text{Si}_4$ and $\text{Cs}_4\text{Ge}_4$ were carried out to obtain quantitative estimates of the splitting between the frequencies of the two types. Results are presented in Table 4. Another advantage of the finite displacement method is the flexibility in choosing the size of the cluster of interest. Each $E_{4}$ tetrahedron in both types of crystal structure is coordinated by 16 alkali metal atoms. Therefore, in addition to the four-atom clusters discussed above, 20-atom clusters can also be calculated in order to quantify the effect of immediate neighborhood on the internal modes of the $E_{4}$ tetrahedra. Results of such calculations are also listed in Table 4 for selected cases. One finds that in silicides there is practically no effect on internal modes (differences less than one wavenumber); in germanides and stannides breathing mode is not affected and other modes shift upwards by 2–4 wavenumbers. In $K_{4}Sn_{4}$ there is a problem in identifying the two lower-lying internal modes due to a strong overlap between the bands originating from the vibrations of tin and potassium atoms. Of the various modes with frequencies between 89 and $114\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, tin atoms contribute between $\approx 20$ to $30\ \%$ of the total weight for the following modes: $113\ (\times 2)$, 108, 107, 105, 103, 93, $89\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. These results provide a first-principles-based quantitative justification for the assumed quasi-independence of the $E_{4}$ tetrahedra vibrational modes.

A final remark about the results on binary compounds concerns the relatively larger differences in theory compared to experiment between the internal frequencies of the $E_{4}$ tetrahedra in $K_{4}E_{4}$ and $Cs_{4}E_{4}$ ($E = Si$, Ge). These differences are more pronounced in silicides with maximum difference of $15\ \%$ (compare with $9\ \%$ in germanides). The main reason for these differences is the inadequacy of the approximate treatment of the exchange-correlation functional of the density functional theory in current state-of-the-art first-principles calculations. It is well known that due to its overbinding nature the local density approximation (LDA) usually gives equilibrium volumes smaller than experiment (up to $10\ \%$), while the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) usually corrects the overbinding tendency of the LDA to some degree yielding equilibrium volumes larger than those found by the LDA. In some cases GGA over-corrects and one obtains equilibrium volumes even larger than the experimental values. In our GGA calculations, $K_{4}E_{4}$ volumes are overestimated by 6.8, 9.4, and $8.9\ \%$ for $E = Si$, Ge, Sn, respectively. $Cs_{4}E_{4}$ volumes, on the other hand, in the same order, are $-3.6$, $-0.9$ and $+0.9\ \%$ of the respective experimental values. These differences in equilibrium volumes mean relatively longer $E-E$ bonds in $K_{4}E_{4}$ than those in $Cs_{4}E_{4}$, and hence lower frequencies in $K_{4}E_{4}$ than in $Cs_{4}E_{4}$.

The very good agreement between the theory and the experiment obtained in the binary $M_{4}E_{4}$ compounds (largest difference in frequencies is less than $10\ \%$, most of the differences are $\approx 5\ \%$) shows that the pseudopotentials (see Calculation Methods) are reliable for use in calculating the internal mode frequencies of the mixed $E_{x}E'_{4-x}$ ($E,E' = Si$, Ge or Ge, Sn, $E \neq E'$, $x = 1$, 2, 3) tetrahedra. However, these mixed tetrahedra are found in the chemically disordered "$M_{4}Si_{2}Ge_{2}$" or "$M_{4}Ge_{2}Sn_{2}$" ($M = K$, Cs). Therefore, the main problem in a first-principles solid state calculation of the vibrational frequencies of these mixed tetrahedra is the modeling of this disorder.

Our approach consisted of constructing hypothetical crystal structures, $K_{4}E_{x}E'_{4-x}$ ($x = 1$, 2, 3), for each $x$, whose starting lattice parameters were obtained by assuming a linear variation with respect to $x$ between the binary end compounds. All tetrahedra in the resulting hypothetical crystal structure corresponding to a particular $x$ have identical composition $E_{x}E'_{4-x}$. The justification of this type of treatment of the mixed $E_{x}E'_{4-x}$ tetrahedra lies in the observation that the frequencies of a given tetrahedron, pure or mixed, depend very weakly on its environment.$^{[4,5]}$ This observation implies that the interactions between any two tetrahedra have negligible effect on their internal frequencies$^{[4,16]}$ so that knowing or modeling the exact distribution of the $E$ and $E'$ atoms in the mixed tetrahedra may not be necessary.

The theoretical results are in well agreement with the experimental values in the case of Si-Ge mixed tetrahedra (see Table 2). However, the differences between the theoretical and experimental values exceed $10\ \%$ in Ge-Sn mixed tetrahedra (see Table 3). The better agreement in the former group is probably due to the fact that both end members are cubic so that the hypothetical crystal structures (themselves cubic) used in the calculations provided a realistic modeling of the $Si_{x}Ge_{4-x}$ tetrahedra. In the latter case one had to interpolate between the cubic ($c/a = 1$) and the tetragonal end members to get starting structures. The structural optimizations seem to have yielded slightly distorted tetrahedra so that the doubly degenerate E modes expected in the $x = 1$ and 3 cases were all split. In order to obtain better agreement for the $Ge_{x}Sn_{4-x}$ tetrahedra a more complicated treatment of the inherent chemical disorder is required.

## Conclusions

The present Raman spectroscopic study on samples with the nominal compositions $Cs_{4}[Si_{2}Ge_{2}]$ and $M_{4}[Ge_{2}Sn_{2}]$ ($M = K$, Cs) has shown that the phases are composed of a series of pure and mixed cluster anions $[Si_{x}Ge_{4-x}]^{4-}$ and $[Ge_{x}Sn_{4-x}]^{4-}$ ($x = 0$–4). The Raman spectra of the $[Si_{x}Ge_{4-x}]^{4-}$ species reveals significant similarities with those of the isoelectronic analogs $P_{x}As_{4-x}$ ($x = 0$–4), both in the distribution of the band intensities, as well as in the order of the frequencies for the characteristic breathing modes. The assignment of the internal modes was supported by NCA. From the ratio factor $\kappa = v([Si_{x}Ge_{4-x}]^{4-}) / v(P_{x}As_{4-x})$ one obtains for the mixed moieties a value of 0.79, which agrees well with the $\kappa = 0.77$ reported for the pure cluster series $[E_{4}]^{4-}$ ($E = Si$, Ge, Sn) and $X_{4}$ ($X = P$, As, Sb). First-principles calculations of the vibrational frequencies provide an independent confirmation for the existence of both pure and mixed cluster anions in the nominal $M_{4}[E_{2}E'_{2}]$ compounds.

## Experimental Section

Synthesis and Characterization: The samples with nominal composi- tions $Cs_4[Si_2Ge_2]$, $Cs_4[Ge_2Sn_2]$, and $K_4[Ge_2Sn_2]$ were synthesized from the elements [molar ratio: Cs(K)/Ge/Si(Sn) = 6:2:2] in sealed Nb ampoules encapsulated in evacuated silica tubes. The specimens were heated to 1073 K in 4 h, annealed for further 4 h and cooled down to room temperature within 24 h. The excess alkali metal was removed by high vacuum distillation ($p < 10^{-5}$ mbar) at 623 K (K) and 573 K (Cs), respectively, after the reaction. The dark red ($Cs_4[Si_2Ge_2]$) and black ($Cs_4[Ge_2Sn_2]$, $K_4[Ge_2Sn_2]$) compounds are very sensitive to air and moisture and were stored and manipulated in an argon-filled glove box ($H_2O < 1$ ppm, $O_2 < 1$ ppm).

The phases of the reaction products were analyzed by X-ray powder diffraction (XRPD) measurements. No byproducts were observed in the powder patterns. The powder diagrams did not allow a clear con- clusion whether the samples are single phase or multiphase mixtures as no resolvable peak splitting or peak broadening was identified even for reflections at high angles. Therefore, Raman spectroscopy was cho- sen as an additional characterization method.

XRPD Measurements: For the X-ray powder diffraction experiments, powdered samples were spread in between two Polyimide (Kapton®, Chemplex) films, which were stretched and fixed by a ring on an alu- minum film holder. XRPD data were collected with X-ray Guinier diffraction technique in transmission mode (Huber G670 camera, graphite monochromator, $Cu-K_{\alpha 1}$ radiation, $5^{\circ} \leq 2\theta \leq 100^{\circ}$, $\Delta 2\theta = 0.005^{\circ}$). All X-ray powder diagrams were analyzed with Stoe WinXPOW. $^{[21]}$

Raman Spectra: The FT-Raman spectra of the samples were recorded from crystalline pieces sealed in pyrex tubes ($d = 4$ mm) with a Bruker RFS 100/S spectrometer with 10 mW input power (Nd: YAG-Laser, 1064 nm, 200 mW).

Calculation Methods: The phonon frequencies of binary $M_4E_4$ ($M = K$, Cs; $E = Si$, Ge, Sn) and ternary $K_4E_xE'_{4-x}$ ($E,E' = Si$, Ge or Ge, Sn, $E \neq E', x = 1,2,3$) at the Brillouin zone center, $\Gamma$ point, were calculated using the first-principles, pseudopotential electronic structure method SIESTA. $^{[22,23]}$ The exchange-correlation effects were taken into ac- count through the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) to the density functional theory. $^{[24]}$ The pseudopotentials were generated ac- cording to the Troullier-Martins scheme$^{[25]}$ using the atom code in- cluded in SIESTA. The so-called nonlinear core corrections$^{[26]}$ were included when generating the pseudopotentials for potassium and ce- sium. The Kleinman-Bylander$^{[27]}$ separable form was applied through one projector per angular momentum channel. Both lattice parameters and atomic positions were relaxed in order to minimize stresses on unit cell faces and forces on atoms. Frozen-phonon approach with the finite displacement method as implemented in the vibra package pro- vided by SIESTA was used to obtain the phonon frequencies of the relaxed structures. In this method each atom is displaced from its equi- librium position by a small amount (typically $\Delta = 0.04$ Å) along each Cartesian axis in both directions and the resulting forces on all atoms in the unit cell are calculated. The force constants in the Cartesian coordinate system are obtained by averaging the force/displacement ratio $F_{\alpha}/\Delta$ for parallel and anti-parallel directions along $\alpha = x,y,z$. The dynamical matrix is generated from these force constant values and diagonalizing it gives the eigen frequencies and eigenvectors.

Remark on the Raman Spectrum of $Cs_4Si_4$: It is important to note that the Raman spectrum of $Cs_4Si_4$ depicted in Figure 2 reveals an additional band at $333$ cm$^{-1}$, which is missing in the previously re- ported spectra.$^{[7,28]}$ This vibration results from the B component of the originally $F_2$ mode of the $[Si_4]^{4-}$ cluster of type II, which splits into E and B modes due to the lower site symmetry $D_{2d}$ (see Figure 2). Theoretical calculations also indicate the presence of this mode (see text and Table 4). The reason for the absence of this band is very likely due to thermal excitation induced by laser radiation during the Raman experiment. The previous measurements were performed with Kr-Ion laser$^{[7]}$ or Nd:YAG laser$^{[28]}$ with relatively high input power (100 mW). Under these conditions a local heating of the sample may take place leading to thermal decomposition or to a rapid dynamical disorder (rotor phase) as was discussed for $\alpha$-Na$_4$Sn$_4$. $^{[4]}$ For $Cs_4Si_4$, the thermal transition into the rotor phase would mean that the cluster of both types become equivalent – i.e. $T_d$ symmetry – so that the B mode disappears completely. Thus, the Raman band at $333$ cm$^{-1}$ in the present case could be observed due the use of Nd:YAG laser at rela- tively low power (< 10 mW).
