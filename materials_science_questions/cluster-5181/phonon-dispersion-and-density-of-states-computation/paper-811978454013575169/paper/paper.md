# Phonon Dispersion Relations in Copper and Silver
by a Modified Sharma-Joshi Model

By
C. M. GOEL, B. P. PANDEY, and B. DAYAL

The Sharma and Joshi model has been modified by substituting an exact expression for Bardeen's $G$-function in the electron-ion interaction term and to include angular forces, in addition to usual central forces, proposed by Clark, Gazis, and Wallis, up to the second neighbours. The modified model has been applied to study the phonon dispersion relations in copper and silver. The results obtained show an excellent agreement with experimental data.

Das Modell von Sharma und Joshi wurde durch Einfügung eines exakten Ausdrucks für die Bardeensche $G$-Funktion im Elektron-Ion-Wechselwirkungsterm und durch Einbezie- hung von Winkelkräften bis zu den zweiten Nachbarn zusätzlich zu den normalen Zentral- kräften von Clark, Gazis und Wallis modifiziert. Das modifizierte Modell wird zur Unter- suchung der Dispersionsbeziehungen der Phononen in Kupfer und Silber benutzt. Die erhaltenen Ergebnisse zeigen ausgezeichnete Übereinstimmung mit den experimentellen Werten.

## 1. Introduction

The main complexity in the rigorous treatment of lattice dynamics of metals is concerned with the interaction of conduction electrons with lattice vibrations. The first theory of electron-ion interaction in metals was proposed by Bardeen [1]. Later on Toya [2] extended Bardeen's theory to include effects of correla- tion and exchange energies of the valence electrons and employed it to calculate the phonon dispersion relations of several monovalent metals (Toya [3 to 5]). A general agreement between theoretical results and experimental data was observed. However, a calculation of specific heats on the basis of Toya's theory showed considerably higher values than the experimental ones. Dayal and Srivastava [6] pointed out that this discrepancy in results is present due to the erroneous Bardeen's $G$-function, which is based on the assumption that atomic polyhedra can be approximated by spheres of equal volumes, occurring in Toya's theory. So, they modified the Bardeen's $G$-function by multiplying its argument with an arbitrary constant whose value is slightly larger than unity. This yielded a considerable improvement in the degree of agreement between theoretical and the experimental results.

Recently, Bross and Bohn [7] have derived rigorous expressions for Bardeen's $G$-function by considering the actual shape of the Wigner-Seitz cell for f.c.c. as well as b.c.c. crystals. It is, therefore, of interest to make use of these correct expressions for the $G$-function in studying the lattice dynamics of metals. The purpose of the present paper is to study phonon dispersion relations in copper and silver by employing usual central forces along with the angular forces of Clark et al. [8], both up to second neighbours, and the electron-ion interaction on the lines of the Sharma and Joshi model [9] with an exact expression for

Bardeen's $G$-function. The calculated results are compared with the experimental data obtained by various workers (Svensson et al. [10], Kamitakahara and Brockhouse [11]) and with the theoretical results of Behari and Tripathi [12] who have also employed the present scheme but with an approximate expression for the $G$-function, originally given by Bardeen.

## 2. Dynamical Matrix
The phonon frequencies are given by the secular determinant
$$|D-Mw^{2}I|=0,\tag{1}$$
where $D$ is the dynamical matrix, $M$ the mass of the atom, and $I$ a unit matrix of order three.

The typical elements of the dynamical matrix are
$$\begin{aligned}
D_{11}= & {\left[2 A_{1}+8 a^{-2}\left(K_{1}+K_{2}\right)\right]\left[2-C_{1}\left(C_{2}+C_{3}\right)\right]+} \\
& +4 A_{2} S_{1}^{2}-8 a^{-2} K_{1}\left(2 C_{1}^{2}-C_{2}^{2}-C_{3}^{2}\right)+a \pi^{2} K_{\mathrm{e}} q_{1}^{2} G^{2}(\boldsymbol{q}),
\end{aligned}\tag{2}$$
$$D_{12}=\left(2 A_{1}-16 a^{-2} K_{1}\right) S_{1} S_{2}+a \pi^{2} K_{\mathrm{e}} q_{1} q_{2} G^{2}(\boldsymbol{q}),\tag{3}$$
where
$$C_{i}=\cos \pi q_{i}, \quad S_{i}=\sin \pi q_{i},$$
$q_{i}$ are the Cartesian components of the reduced wave vector $\boldsymbol{q}$, and $a$ is the lattice constant. $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ are the central force constants and $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$ are the angular force constants for nearest and the next-nearest neighbours, respectively.
The function $G(\boldsymbol{q})$ for f.c.c. metals, from the work of Bross and Bohn [7], may be expressed as follows:
$$\begin{aligned}
G(\boldsymbol{q})= & \frac{-2}{u^{2}+v^{2}+w^{2}}\left\{\frac{u+v}{(u-v)^{2}-w^{2}}\left[\sin u+\sin v-\sin \left(\frac{u+v+w}{2}\right)-\right.\right. \\
& \left.-\sin \left(\frac{u+v-w}{2}\right)\right]+ \\
& +\frac{u-v}{(u+v)^{2}-w^{2}}\left[\sin u-\sin v-\sin \left(\frac{u+v+w}{2}\right)-\sin \left(\frac{u-v-w}{2}\right)\right]+ \\
& + \text { four additional cyclic permuted terms }
\end{aligned}\tag{4}$$
with
$$u=\pi q_{1}, \quad v=\pi q_{2}, \quad \text { and } \quad w=\pi q_{3}.$$

The other elements of the dynamical matrix can be obtained by cyclic permutation of the indices.

## 4. Results and Discussion

The vibrational frequencies for copper and silver are obtained by solving (1) for selected values of the wave vectors in symmetry directions. The computed dispersion curves are shown in Fig. 1 and 2 along with the experimental data.

Fig. 2. Phonon dispersion curves of silver at $296\ ^{\circ}\text{K}$. The experimental points are due to Kamitakahara and Brockhouse [11]

In order to show the definite importance of the correct Bardeen's $G$-function we have compared the present results with the theoretical calculations of Behari and Tripathi, shown in the figures by dashed lines.

From Fig. 1 and 2 it is obvious that the use of the correct expression for Bardeen's $G$-function in the electron-ion interaction term leads to results which show remarkably good agreement with the experimental data and are much superior to those obtained by Behari and Tripathi employing the original $G$-function of Bardeen.
