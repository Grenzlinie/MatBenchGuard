# LATTICE DYNAMICS OF GREY TIN (α-Sn)

M. S. KUSHWAHA

Department of Physics, Banaras Hindu University, Varanasi-221005, India

Received 1 October 1979

The lattice dynamics of $\alpha$-Sn has been investigated using a five parameter model recently proposed for covalent crystals. The phonon dispersion relations, phonon density of states, Debye-characteristic temperatures $(\theta_{\mathrm{D}})$ and the compressibility $(\chi)$ have been calculated. The comparison of the theoretical and available experimental results reveals a fairly good agreement and thus lends support to our lattice dynamical calculations for $\alpha$-Sn.

## 1. Introduction

The lattice dynamics of covalent crystals has been devoted a considerable interest since the time of Born [1] only. Theoreticians have adopted several kinds of phenomenological models which can, however, be broadly classified into two categories: (i) Rigid-Ion-Model (RIM) and (ii) Shell-Model (SM). Various extended and modified versions of RIM as well as SM have been employed to calculate the lattice dynamical properties of covalent crystals [2-9]. Quite recently we have developed a five parameter model [9] which was found to be physically significant and adequate enough to study the lattice dynamics of diamond, silicon and germanium. Since a fairly good agreement was found between theory and experiments, we thought it worthwhile to study the phonons in the fourth member $(\alpha$-Sn) of this (covalent) family employing the same model.

The purpose of the present paper is thus to report the theoretical results for phonon dispersion curves, phonon density of states, Debye-characteristic temperatures $(\theta_{\mathrm{D}})$ and compressibility $(\chi)$. Available experimental results have been used as a check on the adequacy of the theoretical calculations. The noteworthy features pertaining to the reliability of the model have been mentioned in the last section.

## 2. Theory

The present model incorporates two types of potential energies: (i) $\phi_{1}$, giving rise to the short-range central (radial) forces and (ii) $\phi_{2}$, giving rise to the unpaired (bond-bending) forces. The model involves five disposable parameters which have been calculated by using the two elastic constants $(C_{11}$ and $C_{12})$ and three critical point phonon frequencies $(\nu_{\mathrm{LO/LA}}(\mathrm{X}), \nu_{\mathrm{TO}}(\mathrm{X})$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{TA}}(\mathrm{X}))$. The details of the theory and the physical significance of the model have been widely discussed in the previous paper of this series [9]. The experimental data fed as an input and the numerical values of the model parameters are listed in table I.

Further, the use of Brout sum rule, extended to check its validity to diamond and diamond like (zinc- blende) crystals [15], has been made to calculate the compressibility $(\chi)$. It is observed that the sum of the squares of the phonon frequencies of all the six branches at every value of $q$ in the whole zone is nearly constant. We thus used the Brout sum rule $(\sum_{i=1}^{6} \omega_{i}^{2}(q)$ $=16 \sqrt{3} r_{0} / \mu \chi)$ to calculate the compressibility $(\chi)$ which comes out to be $1.76 ×10^{-12} cm^{2} /$ dyne.
Though the experimental value of $\chi$ is not available, we believe the theoretical value to be correct.

We may, therefore, conclude that the present model involving five disposable parameters has well explained the lattice dynamics of diamond, silicon, germanium and $\alpha$-tin which are typical covalent crystals charac terized by high directional valencies. The agreement obtained between theory and available experiments in all these crystals is found to be good and of the quality better than the six-parameters valence-force-field model and ten, fourteen and even more parameter shell-models. The following criteria (among others) are helpful to justify the physical significance of the present model:

(a) that the model has worked not only for one or two specific crystals but for a whole family of similar crystals, such as covalent crystals;

(b) that the model parameters exhibit some qualitative resemblance, particularly in case of $Si, Ge$ and $\alpha$ Sn, to the microscopic theory of phonons;

(c) that it takes appropriate account of the deformations in the interbond angles;

(d) that the compressibility $(\chi)$, if calculated using a static deformation method [16], does not involve any contribution of the unpaired (angular or bond- bending) forces, which is, of course, a realistic result because if the crystal is subjected to a uniform compression (or dilation) the interbond angles do not change at all and hence $\chi$ should be independent of any contribution of the unpaired forces.

It deserves mention here that in the present treat- ment of covalent crystals we have not considered the Coulombic electrostatic interactions. This approxima- tion is reasonably acceptable for most of the covalent crystals, because the atomic charges thereof are zero or very small. Besides, the unpaired (bond-bending) forces employed in the present treatment [9] have been found to be physically sound enough in the lattice dynamical treatments of zinc-blend crystals[17] and even in the alkali halides [18] wherein the long range Coulombic interactions were also embodied along with the short-range forces.
