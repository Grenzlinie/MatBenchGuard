# The Raman spectra of the hexagonal and cubic (spinel) forms of $Ge_3N_4$: an experimental and theoretical study

S.K. Deb $^{1,a}$, J. Dong $^{b}$, H. Hubert $^{c}$, P.F. McMillan $^{a,c,*}$, O.F. Sankey $^{b}$

$^{a}$Center for Solid State Science, Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ 85287, USA
$^{b}$Department of Physics and Astronomy & Materials Research Center, Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ 85287, USA
$^{c}$Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry & Materials Research Center, Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ 85287-1604, USA

Received 11 October 1999; received in revised form 3 January 2000; accepted 3 January 2000 by F.J. DiSalvo

## Abstract
The Raman spectra of the hexagonal ($\beta$) and cubic spinel ($\gamma$) forms of $Ge_3N_4$, newly obtained by high-pressure synthesis, have been measured and are compared with zone-center phonon frequencies calculated using first principles methods in a plane wave pseudopotential local density approximation. The observed and calculated phonon frequencies are in excellent agreement and we use the calculated values to perform mode assignment for both the phases. Both phases show all the expected Raman active modes. © 2000 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

**Keywords**: B. Chemical synthesis; D. Phonons; E. Inelastic light scattering

## 1. Introduction
Polymorphism in the oxide compounds of Si and Ge has been studied extensively as a function of pressure and temperature, whereas relatively little is known about the corresponding nitrides. Silicon nitride ($\alpha$- and $\beta$-Si$_3$N$_4$) is a well-known refractory ceramic with applications in cutting tools, in high-temperature parts, and as a passivating material in electronic devices. The corresponding hexagonal form of germanium nitride ($\beta$-Ge$_3$N$_4$) has not been developed for practical application, but it is known as a refractory ceramic with a hexagonal structure analogous to $\beta$-Si$_3$N$_4$, with all germanium atoms in tetrahedral coordination to nitrogen. Recently, we reported results of an in situ study of compression of $\beta$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ to high pressure, using synchrotron X-ray diffraction in the diamond anvil cell (DAC) to follow the effects of structural change [1]. During that study, it was observed that the X-ray diffraction peaks weakened dramatically above 18–20 GPa, indicating substantial disorder and perhaps the onset of pressure-induced amorphization on compression under non-hydrostatic conditions. This observation prompted us to carry out heating experiments in the DAC on a sample that had been compressed to pressures in this range. The results showed growth of a new set of crystalline diffraction peaks that could be indexed to the new cubic spinel form, termed $\gamma$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ [2]. This compound contains Ge in both tetrahedral and octahedral sites. Subsequent heating experiments in a large volume multi-anvil apparatus permitted us to obtain large samples of the $\gamma$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ form which could be recovered to ambient conditions, for crystal structure analysis and physical properties measurement [2]. While our work was being published, a report of a high-pressure synthesis of the spinel form of $Ge_3N_4$ appeared in the literature, as the result of a YLF DAC laser heating experiment on a mixture of Ge and nitrogen in a diamond anvil cell [3]. The same group had published previously the result of a similar experiment on $Si + N_2$, which resulted in synthesis of a cubic spinel form of $Si_3N_4$ [4]. These reports represent the first synthesis of nitride compounds with the cubic spinel structure, and the first occurrence of Si and Ge in octahedral coordination to nitrogen [1–4]. In the present work, we have undertaken a careful experimental study of the Raman spectra of the $\beta$- and $\gamma$-forms of Ge$_3$N$_4$. These results reveal all lines expected by symmetry analysis. We have supplemented the experi- mental study with a first principles calculation of the vibrational mode frequencies and eigenvectors, which greatly aids in mode identification and analysis. Our results are compared with the spectra used for phase identification of the Ge$_3$N$_4$ spinel structure in the work of Serghiou et al. [3], and provide the basis for a detailed study of the high- pressure behavior of $\beta$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ and its transformation into the spinel phase.

The $\beta$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ phase is hexagonal with space group $P6_3/m$ ($C_{6h}^2$) with $Z=2$ and lattice constants $a=8.028$ Å and $c=3.052$ Å. The structure consists of corner linked, slightly distorted, Ge$_3$N$_4$ tetrahedra. Each nitrogen atom is in planar 3-coordination. One set of N atoms occupies a fully symmetric $C_{3h}$ site, while the other set has $C_s$ symmetry, as do the Ge atoms within their distorted tetrahedra. From symmetry "factor group" analysis, the zone-center optical phonons are distributed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{\text{optic}} =& 4\mathrm{A_g} + 2\mathrm{A_u} + 3\mathrm{B_g} + 4\mathrm{B_u} + 2\mathrm{E_{1g}} + 5\mathrm{E_{2g}} + 3\mathrm{A_u} \\
&+ 4\mathrm{B_u} + 5\mathrm{E_{1u}} + 2\mathrm{E_{2u}}
\end{aligned}
$$

where the $\mathrm{A_g}$, $\mathrm{E_{1g}}$ and $\mathrm{E_{2g}}$ modes are Raman active and $\mathrm{A_u}$ and $\mathrm{E_{2u}}$ are IR active. Raman and IR active modes are mutually exclusive because of the presence of the inversion symmetry.

The cubic spinel ($\gamma$-) phase of Ge$_3$N$_4$ is made up of Ge atoms which are both tetrahedrally and octahedrally coordi- nated to the nearest N atoms with $\mathrm{Ge^{vi}:Ge^{iv}}$ in a 2:1 ratio. The N atoms each have four Ge neighbors, one occupying a tetrahedral site and the remaining three occupying octa- hedral sites. The space group is $Fd\overline{3}m$ with $Z=2$ and lattice constant $a=8.2125$ Å [2]. The zone-center optical phonons are distributed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{\text{optic}} =& \mathrm{A_{1g}} + \mathrm{E_g} + \mathrm{T_{1g}} + 3\mathrm{T_{2g}} + 2\mathrm{A_{2u}} + 2\mathrm{E_u} + 4\mathrm{T_{1u}} \\
&+ 2\mathrm{T_{2u}}
\end{aligned}
$$

These include five Raman active modes distributed as $\mathrm{A_{1g}}+\mathrm{E_g}+3\ \mathrm{T_{2g}}$ and four IR active $\mathrm{T_{1u}}$ modes. The other modes are optically inactive.

## 2. Experimental

Commercially available Ge$_3$N$_4$ usually consists of an approximate 80:20 mixture of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ phases, as well as a small amount (a few per cent) of elemental Ge. This material was heat treated at 0.9 GPa and 700 K to convert the $\alpha$ component to the $\beta$ form (the resulting material still contained the Ge impurity). The cubic ($\gamma$-) phase of the material was synthesized from $\beta$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ in a Walker-style split cylinder multi-anvil apparatus. Graphite or Re heaters were used and samples were enclosed in Re capsules. The synthesis conditions were typically about 12 GPa and 1200–1400 K [2]. All samples for Raman spectroscopy were characterized by powder X-ray diffraction.

The Raman spectra were excited using the 514.5 or 488 nm line from an $\mathrm{Ar^+}$ laser. The beam was focused onto the sample surface using a Mitutoyo SL50 objective to a spot size of $\sim$3–4 $\mu$m. The scattered light was collected in backscattering geometry using the same objective lens. The laser power was always kept below 5 mW for the $\beta$-phase samples and below 1 mW for the cubic phase material to avoid laser-induced modification or excessive heating of the sample. The Raman spectra were recorded using an ISA S3000 Raman triple monochromator system with liquid nitrogen cooled CCD detection. Spectra were obtained using either 1800 or 600 gr/mm gratings in the spectrograph and 1800 gr/mm gratings in the premono- chromator stage. The spectra were also recorded by bypass- ing the filter stage and using the HR 640 spectrograph stage rejecting the laser line with a Kaiser 514.5 nm super-notch filter. The orientations of different grains were random (and in the case of the high-pressure synthesized $\gamma$-spinel phase of Ge$_3$N$_4$, the individual crystallite sizes were sub-micron, from TEM observations). All of the Raman active modes could generally be observed in both the "polarized" (HH) and "depolarized" (HV) laser excitation and polarization geometries for the $\beta$-phase and most of the spectra were recorded in the HV geometry for convenience (related to the orientation of grating grooves, and the spectrometer optics). Our calculations are based on the density functional theory with local density approximation (LDA). The imple- mentation we adopt uses plane-wave basis sets and pseudo- potentials [5]. We first optimize the structures to achieve minimal energy (zero force) configuration. Then the zone- center phonon modes are derived from a direct force constant matrix calculation, which displaces atoms, one at each time, and determines the resultant force on each atom in the unit cell. Hence, both frequencies and eigenmodes are calculated with first-principle methods.

## 3. Results
### 3.1. $\boldsymbol{\beta-Ge_3N_4}$

The factor group analysis predicts 11 Raman active modes for this phase and a typical Raman spectrum of the β-Ge₃N₄ obtained in this study is shown in Fig. 1. The spectrum shows eleven lines that can be assigned to this phase, along with an additional weak feature at $298\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. This coincides with the intense T₂g optic phonon of elemen- tal Ge, which we know is present in small quantities in the sample. The intensity of this feature varied from point to point within the sample, consistent with its assignment to Ge impurities present as sub-microscopic grains. The frequen- cies obtained from the planewave pseudopotential calcu- lation are given in Table 1. The calculated frequencies are in excellent agreement with the measured ones, particularly for the modes below $500\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. The result of the calculation permits us to make a detailed symmetry assignment of the observed modes, in the absence of polarized spectra on single crystals.

The eigenvectors for different vibrational modes have been calculated and we discuss the eigenvectors of the three A<sub>g</sub> modes only. This is mainly because they, being totally symmetric modes, are easier to visualize. These three modes are classified as low, medium and high- frequency bands depending on their frequencies in the Raman spectra of β-Ge₃N₄. Due to the totally symmetric nature of the A<sub>g</sub> modes, the nitrogen atoms at $C_{3h}$ sites are motionless and the motion of all the other atoms are perpen- dicular to the c-axis of the unit cell. The β-Ge₃N₄ structure can be viewed as stacking of GeN₄ tetrahedral units with each corner (N atom) shared by three such tetrahedra. The low-frequency A<sub>g</sub> mode near $108\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is best characterized as GeN₄ tetrahedra "rotating" around one connected corner (N at $C_{3h}$ site) without having severe bond length and bond angle changes in this libration, which is a common feature of modes in the low-frequency range. On the other hand, the vibration of the high-frequency A<sub>g</sub> mode near $798\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is a typical bond-stretching mode with the N atoms moving exactly along Ge-N bonds. The character of the medium- frequency modes is shown in Fig. 2. In this A<sub>g</sub> mode, the motion is mainly Ge-N(C<sub>s</sub>)-Ge bond-bending when viewed as $\text{N}(C_{s})\text{Ge}_{3}$ units, the vibration of Ge atoms however also causes the stretching of $\text{Ge-N}(C_{3h})$ bonds. In general, the modes in the medium-frequency bands can be described as combined bond-bending and bond- stretching motions.

## 4. Discussion

Serghiou et al. [3] have used Raman spectroscopy to identify “$\gamma$-Ge$_{3}$N$_{4}$” synthesized in the DAC by laser heating at high temperature and pressure. Their reported spectrum shows six Raman modes. However, the normal spinel phase is expected to have only five Raman active modes. As discussed by Serghiou et al. [3], it is well known that spinels can exhibit more Raman modes than expected, for various reasons. The spinel phase type phase, MgAl$_{2}$O$_{4}$, can contain substantial disorder of Mg$^{2+}$ and Al$^{3+}$ ions between tetrahedral ions, to produce a mixture of “normal” (Al$_{2}^{vi}$Mg$^{iv}$O$_{4}$) and “inverse” (Al$^{vi}$Mg$^{vi}$Al$^{iv}$O$_{4}$) spinel structures, depending upon the conditions of synthesis. The result is some broadening of Raman peaks, and also appearance of “extra” peaks, particularly in the high-frequency region assigned to AlO$_{4}$ stretching vibrations [6–9]. In particular, there is often appearance of an “unexpected” mode between the two high-frequency peaks of the “normal” spinel structure [9]. In the case of $\gamma$-Ge$_{3}$N$_{4}$, Serghiou et al. [3] observed such a “third mode” in their Raman spectrum of the compound they identified as “Ge$_{3}$N$_{4}$- ($\gamma$-) spinel”. This feature is not present in our spectrum of a well-crystallized material, with known stoichiometry (Fig. 3). For $\gamma$-Ge$_{3}$N$_{4}$, the cations at both the sites are the same, and so the origin of any additional modes cannot be due to cation disorder, as in MgAl$_{2}$O$_{4}$. In our early attempts to synthesize $\gamma$-Ge$_{3}$N$_{4}$ in the multi-anvil apparatus, we often observed these same additional high-frequency modes (Fig. 4), as well as other features including a series of sharp low-frequency Raman peaks (Fig. 5). Careful examination of the X-ray powder pattern showed only peaks for the spinel phase, along with very weak diffraction peaks due to traces of untransformed $\beta$-Ge$_{3}$N$_{4}$. In addition, the Raman spectrum often contained broad background features indicative of the presence of an amorphous Ge$_{3}$N$_{4}$ phase. Our interpretation of these observations is the following.

Fig. 4. Raman spectrum of one of our earlier synthesized samples of $\gamma$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ over the range 500–1000 cm$^{-1}$ showing the presence of two "additional" modes between the high-frequency modes at 730 and 858 cm$^{-1}$. Under high-pressure–high-temperature treatment, the metastably compressed $\beta$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ material begins to transform to the thermodynamically stable $\gamma$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ phase. However, this transformation is sluggish, and part of the sample remains as an "amorphous" form, as observed in our Raman spectra. On further heating or progress of the solid state reaction within certain regions, the "amorphous" material recrystallizes to material which does not correspond to ideal spinel, but to a "defective spinel", "spinelloid", or other structure. This intermediate crystalline material results in the Raman spectrum with additional peaks observed by Serghiou et al. [3]. We are currently pursuing theoretical calculations to identify this intermediate phase, which we believe might correspond to an olivine structure. We note that Serghiou et al. [3] did not observe the lowest frequency mode at 321 cm$^{-1}$ that is characteristic of the $\gamma$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ spinel structure. This mode is largely translational in character as opposed to other modes which originate from internal vibrations of the GeN$_4$ and GeN$_6$ groups.

## 5. Conclusion

We have obtained Raman spectra for the $\beta$- and $\gamma$-phases of Ge$_3$N$_4$, which are necessary for further studies and phase identification of these materials. Both compounds show all lines expected from theoretical (symmetry) analysis, and the observed frequencies are in excellent agreement with predictions from first principles theory. The ab initio calculations permit unique identification of the mode symmetries, and interpretation of the mode eigenvectors. Our Raman spectrum of the newly synthesized high-pressure phase $\gamma$-Ge$_3$N$_4$ with the cubic spinel structure shows the five expected Raman active peaks. The highest frequency peak is unambiguously assigned to the fully symmetric $A_{1g}$ mode from polarization studies. These results, along with previous investigations by us of materials obtained in a series of multi-anvil syntheses, indicate that the material obtained and recently described by Seghiou et al. [3] was not phase-pure $\gamma$-Ge$_3$N$_4$, or contained substantial defects in its structure.
