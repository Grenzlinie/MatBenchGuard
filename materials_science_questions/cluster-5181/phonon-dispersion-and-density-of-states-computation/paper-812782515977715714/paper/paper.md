# Inelastic-neutron-scattering line shapes in $PdD_{0.63}$
C. J. Glinka, J. M. Rowe, and J. J. Rush
Institute for Materials Research, National Bureau of Standards, Washington, D.C. 20234

A. Rahman, S. K. Sinha, and H. E. Flotow
Argonne National Laboratory, Argonne, Illinois 60439

(Received 18 July 1977)

Previous measurements of the phonon-dispersion curves in a single crystal of $PdD_{0.63}$ have been augmented by detailed inelastic-neutron-scattering measurements of the optical-phonon line shapes at 80 K. The line shapes observed at this temperature show substantial broadening and subsidiary structure. The data have been compared to line shapes calculated from a Born-von Kármán type model in which the nonstoichiometric structure of the crystal is explicitly taken into account. Although the model results cannot reproduce all details of the observed line shapes, the observed and calculated linewidths agree reasonably well, which demonstrates that the broadening is primarily an effect of nonstoichiometry and not, for example, due to anharmonicity. The Pd-D force constants derived from the nonstoichiometric model agree closely with those obtained from an earlier analysis of the dispersion curves based on a conventional Born-von Kármán approach in which the crystal was treated as stoichiometric PdD. The near-neighbor D-D force constants obtained in the present analysis, however, are some 50% larger than those deduced with a stoichiometric model.

## I. INTRODUCTION
The high superconducting transition tempera- tures and reverse isotope effect observed for the $PdH_{x}(PdD)_{x}$ systems $^{1,2}$ when x approaches unity have been the subject of several recent experi- mental and theoretical studies. $^{3-7}$ The results $^{8,9}$ of superconducting tunneling measurements indi- cate that the optic part of the phonon spectrum, which involves mainly the motion of the light atom, contributes strongly to the electron- phonon cou- pling. It is of interest therefore to study this region of the phonon spectrum in detail for both isotopes.

The most direct information on the phonon spec- trum and interatomic forces in the deuteride has been obtained from coherent-neutron-scattering measurements $^{10}$ of the phonon dispersion curves in a single crystal of $PdD_{0.63}$ . In spite of the large number of vacant sites on the deuterium sublat- tice at this concentration (ideal structure is the NaCl-type $^{11}$ ), sufficiently well-defined coherent scattering peaks were observed from the optic vibrations to permit complete dispersion curves to be mapped out along the major symmetry direc- tions. These are shown in Fig. 1. The solid curves in the figure represent the results of a least-squares fit of a fourth nearest-neighbor Born-von Kármán model to the data in which the crystal was treated as stoichiometric PdD.

Although the dispersion curves in Fig. 1 pro- vide a good fit to the observed phonon peak posi- tions, even in the optical-mode region, the use of a stoichiometric model for the structure is not entirely satisfactory for two reasons. First of all, it is not clear how closely the force constants derived from such a model approximate those for the real, highly defected, system. And secondly,the optical-phonon peaks observed for $PdD_{0.63}$  were in most cases far broader than the instru-

With regard to the hydride system, the large incoherent cross section of hydrogen prohibits the direct measurement of the phonon dispersion curves by coherent neutron scattering. As a re- sult, the dynamics of $PdH_{x}$ have been studied via the incoherent scattering from polycrystalline samples. $^{12-14}$ Primarily because the heavy Pd atoms remain nearly stationary in the optic vi- brations, the incoherent scattering from the hydro- gen provides essentially a direct measurement of the optical-phonon density of states. In a recent time-of-flight measurement of the frequency dis-tribution of the incoherent scattering from $PdH_{0.63}$  by Rahman et al., $^{12}$ the optical spectrum was ob served to be far broader than the density of states calculated from the stoichiometric model used to describe the dispersion curves in $PdD_{0.63}$ . Rahman was able to show that the breadth of the optical-mode spectrum is primarily a consequence of nonstoichiometry by performing a Born-von Kármán treatment on a model for the structure which incorporates defects and also handles ap- proximately the breakdown of translational sym- metry which is an essential feature of the real structure. The inelastic scattering cross section calculated on the basis of this model, using the force constants derived by Rowe et al. $^{10}$ for PdD。. 63, gave a good account of the width and most other features of the time-of-flight data. It was found, however, that the peak in the theo- retical spectrum was shifted to lower frequencies than in the measured spectrum. Accordingly, the Pd-H force constants, which essentially affect only the peak position in the theoretical spectrum, were uniformly increased by $20 \%$ over the corre sponding Pd-D force constants to obtain agree- ment with the data. The implication that the metal-hydrogen forces are larger in $PdH_{x}$ has important consequences for some currently pro- posed theories $^{3,4,7}$ for the reverse isotope effect in these systems.

In the present study, the frequency shapes of the coherent one-phonon scattering at selected values of the neutron momentum transfer $\vec{k}$ have been cal culated using Rahman's model and have been com- pared with new, more extensive, inelastic scat- tering line-shape measurements on the same crystal of $PdD_{0.63}$ used in the earlier work. Since the intensity observed in coherent scattering de- pends on the relative atomic displacements as well as the frequencies of individual vibrational modes, such a comparison provides a more criti- cal test of how closely the theoretical model de- scribes the dynamics of the real system than can be obtained from incoherent scattering data alone. In addition, analyzing the coherent scattering from PdD。. 63 in this way serves to check the consistency of the force constants derived from the earlier analysis in terms of a stoichiometric model.

## II. MODEL FOR THE NONSTOICHIOMETRIC STRUCTURE

The model used here for the lattice dynamics of $PdD_{x}$ has been described by Rahman et al. $^{12}$ Here we merely summarize the essential features of the model and include some comments pertaining to the calculation of coherent-scattering line shapes.

In the model, the nonstoichiometry is simulated by considering the structure of $PdD_{x}$ (or $PdH_{x}$ ) tobe made up of enlarged cubic cells of size $n a_{0} ×n a_{0}$  $\times n a_{0}(a_{0}$ is the lattice constant) with, therefore, $N=4 n^{3} Pd$ atoms occupying the face-centered cubic sites and xN D atoms randomly distributed over the octahedral interstitial sites. The configura- tion of N Pd's and xN D's is treated as the unit cell of a simple cubic lattice and standard Born- von Kármán methods are applied to calculate the frequencies and polarization vectors of the normal modes of vibration. The true aperiodicity of the real structure is then approximated by solving for the lattice dynamics for many such cells, each obtained by a random filling of the D sites, and then averaging the resulting one-phonon scatter- ing from each "sample" of $PdD_{x}$ over the ensemble of samples. To carry out such a procedure dy- namical matrices of size $3 N(1+x) ×3 N(1+x)$ must be diagonalized. For n=2 and $x=\frac{2}{3}$ , the size ofthe dynamical matrix will be 160 by 160, for n=3 it will be 540 by 540 while for n=4 it becomes 1280 by1280!

The utility of this approach depends on reaching the point where the results become insensitive to the choice of cell size before the computations in- volved become prohibitive on even a very largecomputer. We have applied the model to $PdD_{0.63}$  using unit cells of 32 Pd's and 20 D's (n=2), and108 Pd's and 68 D's (n=3) as a check on the con- sistency of the results. To solve even the next size cell (n=4) would require specialized tech- niques for manipulating large matrices and some considerable expense.

Our calculations were carried out using the same twelve parameter force constant model employed in Ref. 10 in which first and second like-neighbor(Pd-Pd or D-D) and first-neighbor Pd-D interac- tions are considered. Values for the Pd-D and D-D force constants, which are the ones which determine the optic vibrational modes, were

estimated by fitting the peak positions in the one-phonon scattering calculated from the above struc- tural model for $n=1$ and $x=\frac{3}{4}(Pd_{4} D_{3})$ to the optic phonon data of Fig. 1. The model for $Pd_{4} D_{3}$ approximates the vacancy concentration in $PdD_{0.63}$  but has a unit cell of only seven atoms so that the required computations are relatively simple and may therefore be iterated as needed in a non- linear least-squares procedure. The eighteen optical-mode frequencies for each value of $\kappa$ were weighted with the appropriate values of the neutron inelastic structure factor and then averaged for comparison with the data. The dispersion curves for the optical-modes obtained in this way are similar to those shown in Fig. 1. The fitted values of the nearest-neighbor D-D force constants are, however, $50 \%$ larger (in magnitude) than those derived from the model with no vacancies. This is roughly consistent with each $D$ in $Pd_{4} D_{3}$ having fewer like nearest neighbors than in PdD. All other force constants were nearly the same as those derived from the PdD model. Hence the force constants found in Ref. 10, with the first- neighbor D-D constants increased by $50 \%$ , were used in the full calculations for $PdD_{0.63}$ .
The normal-mode frequencies and polarization vectors computed for each sample of $PdD_{0.63}$ were used to obtain coherent scattering line shapes atselected values of neutron momentum transfer $\vec{\kappa}$  by constructing a histogram based on the one- phonon approximation for the scattering law
$$\begin{aligned}
S_{1}(\vec{\kappa}, \omega)= & \sum_{j}\left|\sum_{d} \frac{b_{d}}{\sqrt{M_{d}}}\left(\vec{\kappa} \cdot \vec{\sigma}_{d}^{j}\right) \exp \left(i \vec{\kappa} \cdot \vec{\mathrm{r}}_{d}\right) e^{-W_{d}}\right|^{2} \\
& \times \frac{1}{\omega_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{e^{\beta \omega_{j}}-1}+1\right) \delta\left(\omega-\omega_{j}\right), \quad(1)
\end{aligned}$$
 where we have assumed neutron energy loss and $\beta=\hbar / k T$ , with $T=80 ~K$ corresponding to the con ditions of our experiment. Subscript $d$ stands for all the atoms in the enlarged unit cell with basis positions $\overrightarrow{r}_{d}$ , coherent scattering length $b_{d}$ , mass $M_{d}$ , and Debye-Waller factor $W_{d}. \ W_{d}$ was obtained by integration over the frequency spectrum of the sample, with appropriate eigenvector weighting, and averaging over all atoms of that type in the unit cell. The index $j$ sums over all modes cal culated for the wave vector $\vec{q}$ in the Brillouin zone of the large cell to which $\vec{\kappa}$ was reduced, and the corresponding eigenvectors and eigenfrequencies are denoted by $\vec{\sigma}_{d}^{j}, \omega_{j}$ . In practice, $\vec{\kappa}$ was chosen so that $\vec{q}=0$ . Because deuterium is also an inco herent scatterer $(\sigma_{coh }=5.4, \sigma_{incoh }=2.2 barn)$ , the inelastic incoherent scattering for each sample was evaluated as well, as has been described by Rahman. $^{12}$ The frequency distributions of the co herent plus incoherent scattering computed for individual computer-generated samples were similar so that summing the results obtained from a number of samples quickly converged to a limit- ing form for the line shape. Typically, 25 sam- ples were used to arrive at a final line shape which was then convoluted with the instrumental energy resolution before being compared with the data.
It is instructive to compare the method outlined above with another, far simpler, approach which seemingly avoids the difficulties involved in mani- pulating very large matrices. In this approach the frequencies and polarization vectors calcu- lated for the simple two-atom/cell structure of stoichiometric PdD are used in evaluating Eq. (1). The sum over atoms in the equation can then be taken to extend as far as is feasible, using the fact that the displacement of an atom is related by the phase factor $e^{i \vec{q} \cdot \vec{l}}$ to that of the corresponding atom in the two-atom unit cell, where $\vec{l}$ is the appropriate lattice translation vector. The non-stoichiometry is handled simply by setting $b_{d}=0$  at random for $(1-x)$ of the deuterium atoms in the course of the summation. The result of such a procedure, however, is not to broaden the one- phonon line shapes. The only effect is to reduce the intensity of the coherent scattering (which is proportional to the squared average value, $\langle b_{d}\rangle^{2}$ , taken over all deuterium sites, whether occupied or vacant) while increasing the incoherent den- sity-of-states scattering (which is proportional to the variance of the scattering amplitudes,(b)-(b)2). Thus, the mere absence of atoms is not the essential feature of a nonstoichiometric structure that produces broadening. Rather it is the perturbed nature of the frequencies and char- acter of the normal modes caused by the vacancies which is responsible. To be successful, a model must not only incorporate vacancies but must also encompass the perturbing effects of the vacancies.
## III. RESULTS

The measurements consisted of constant- $\vec{\kappa}$ scans in which the scat tered energy was held fixed to assure that the ef- ficiency of the spectrometer remained constant over the scans, which typically covered 50 meV in energy transfer. Copper (220) and pyrolytic- graphite (002) crystals were used as monochroma- tor and analyzer, respectively. A 6-cm pyrolytic graphite filter was inserted in the scattered beam to remove high order contamination as well as elastic incoherent scattering. The measurements shown in Figs. 2 and 4 were made at 80 K. Additional measurements at 5 K gave virtually identical results, however, indicating that the observed widths are not due to phonon lifetime effects but reflect the character of the excitations in this highly defected structure. This is not to imply that anharmonic forces are unimportant in this system. Evidence for anharmonicity has been noted¹⁰ in the dramatic broadening of the optical-phonon line shapes with increasing temperature which occurs above about 150 K.

One further comparison with the theoretical model was made by measuring the intensity of the sharp profile observed at the gamma point at a number of equivalent reciprocal lattice points in order to extract the deuterium Debye-Waller factor. This intensity was found to vary as $\kappa^{2}e^{-\kappa^{2}\langle u_{\kappa}^{2}\rangle}$, independent of the direction of $\kappa$, whereby we obtained $\langle u_{\kappa}^{2}\rangle=0.026\pm0.003$ $\mathring{A}^{2}$ which agrees well with the value $\langle u_{\kappa}^{2}\rangle=0.029$ $\mathring{A}^{2}$ calculated from the model.

### IV. DISCUSSION
The model we have employed for $PdD_{0.63}$ satisfactorily accounts for the measured widths of the inelastic scattering in the optical-mode region. The model results demonstrate that the broadening and structure in the observed line shapes are intrinsic features of the one-phonon response in this highly defected system. The coherent-scattering contribution to the calculated line shapes is, however, dependent on the choice of unit cell used in the computations. To obtain more quantitative information regarding the details of the collective vibrations would require that the calculations be extended to much larger unit cells, which is probably not feasible. We find, however, that the frequency shape of the incoherent scattering calculated from the model is far less sensitive to the choice of cell size. Furthermore the calculated incoherent scattering has been shown by Rahman et al. to give a good account of not only the width but also the shape of the inelastic scattering from $PdH_{0.63}$. Since the incoherent scattering in the optical-mode region from a material like $PdH_{x}$ (or $PdD_{x}$) (in which the light atom motions dominate the optical modes) is essentially proportional to the density of states, we conclude that even with rather small unit cells the frequency spectrum calculated from the present model contains the characteristic features of that of the real system. What is now needed is to incorporate this essential information into a theoretical framework which would be more effective in eliminating the constraint of periodic boundary conditions than merely extending the present calculations to larger and larger unit cells.

A significant result of the present analysis is that it demonstrates that the Pd-D force constants previously estimated by treating the system as stoichiometric PdD are indeed valid for the nonstoichiometric crystal (by contrast, using a stoichiometric model underestimates the firstneighbor D-D force constants by more than 50%). These force constants, which determine the gap between the bands of acoustical and optical vibrations, are, however, about 20% smaller than the Pd-H force constants deduced by Rahman et al. for $PdH_{0.63}$. Thus the present analysis establishes that the effective metal-hydrogen forces are weaker in the deuteride than in the hydride, presumably as a result of the unusually large mean-square displacements of the light atom in these materials. The possibility of this result was suggested by Ganguly³ who proposed that the relative softening of the optic modes in PdD is primarily responsible for the reverse isotope effect observed in the superconductivity of these materials.³⁷ Miller and Satterthwaite⁴ have also argued in their analysis of the reverse isotope effect that the proton is more strongly bound than the deuteron. Quite apart from its relation to superconductivity, an increased force constant in PdH relative to PdD is evidence that anharmonic forces are important in these systems and must be considered in any thorough analysis of the dynamics.
