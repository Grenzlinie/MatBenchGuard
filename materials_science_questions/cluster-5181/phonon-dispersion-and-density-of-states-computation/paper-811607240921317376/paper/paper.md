# Elastic and phonon properties of FeSi and CoSi in the B2 structure

Alev Duygu Acun and Fethi Soyalp*

Yüzüncü Yıl Üniversitesi Eğitim Fakültesi Fizik Bölümü, Van, Turkey

(Received 13 June 2011; final version received 22 August 2011)

First principles calculations of structural, electronic, elastic, and phonon properties of the intermetallic compounds FeSi and CoSi in the B2 (CsCl) structure are presented, using the pseudopotential plane-wave approach based on density functional theory, within the local density approximation. The optimized lattice constants, independent elastic constants, bulk modulus, and first-order pressure derivative of the bulk modulus are reported for the B2 structure and compared with earlier experimental and theoretical calculations. A linear-response approach to density functional theory is used to derive the phonon dispersion curves, and the vibrational partial and total density of states. Atomic displacement patterns for FeSi at the $\Gamma$, X, and R symmetry points are presented. The calculated zone-center optical phonon mode for FeSi is in good agreement with experimental and theoretical data.

**Keywords:** FeSi; CoSi; electronic structure; elastic properties; phonons

## 1. Introduction

Due to their physical properties, silicides of iron-group metals (Fe, Co, and Ni) have attracted considerable attention in recent years [1–4]. FeSi and CoSi can crystallize with different crystal structures (such as the B20 and B2 structures). In a recent study, the elastic, thermodynamic, and electronic properties of MnSi, FeSi, and CoSi with the B20 crystal structure have been studied [5]. Moreover, FeSi in the B20 structure is currently being actively investigated in view of its semiconducting and magnetic properties. FeSi is a strongly correlated semiconductor in the B20 structure [6]. It has a small energy bandgap of about $0.05\mathrm{eV}$ at low temperature and this gap decreases above $100\,\mathrm{K}$ and vanishes at $300\,\mathrm{K}$ [7–9]. On the other hand, CoSi is a good candidate for thermoelectric applications [10]. The structural, elastic, electronic, and vibrational properties of these materials have been studied by several groups. On the experimental side, the elastic and vibrational properties of pseudomorphic FeSi in the CsCl structure have been investigated by Rutherford-backscattering spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, infrared (IR) transmittance/reflec-tance, and Brillouin-light-scattering measurements [11]. The electronic structures of these materials have been investigated using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), bremsstrahlung isochromat spectroscopy (BIS) [12], ultraviolet laser photo-emission spectroscopy (laser-PES) [13], and high-resolution angle-resolved

In addition to these experimental studies, a first- principles density functional calculation based on the pseudopotential method [16–19], a scalar relativistic version of the linear augmented-plane-wave (LAPW) method [20], an *ab initio* full-potential linearized augmented-plane-wave (FLAPW) method [21] and a semi-empirical tight binding (TB) scheme [22] have been used to investigate the structural, electronic, elastic, and dynamical properties of these materials.

Despite much work on the structural, elastic, and electronic properties of these compounds in the CsCl structure, the dynamical properties are relatively poorly known in the literature. It is well known that a wide variety of physical properties of solids depend on their phonon properties such as phase transition, specific heats, thermal expansion, and heat conduction; and the electron–phonon interaction plays an important role in the resistivity of metals and superconductivity. Up to now, infrared (IR) transmittance/reflectance, and Brillouin-light-scattering measurement techniques have been used to measure the zone-center phonon modes of FeSi [11]. In addition, the frozen phonon frequencies of the TO and TA modes at the $\Gamma$ and R points have been calculated by the TB method [22]. To the best of our knowledge, the full phonon spectra of FeSi and CoSi have not been investigated by using any theoretical or experimental methods. Thus, we have carried out a study of the structural, electronic, elastic, and phonon properties of FeSi and CoSi, in order to provide a sounder basis for further experimental and theoretical investigations.

### 2. Method
The calculations were performed in the framework of the density functional theory as implemented in the Quantum-ESPRESSO package [23], within the local density approximation (LDA) using the Ceperley–Alder [24] correlation as parametrized by Perdew and Zunger [25]. The functions were expanded in a plane-wave basis set with a kinetic energy cut-off of 50 Ry. The electronic charge density is evaluated up to the kinetic energy cut-off of 600 Ry. Brillouin-zone integrations were performed using a $10 \times 10 \times 10$ $\mathbf{k}$-point mesh. Integration up to the Fermi surface was done with the smearing technique [26] with smearing parameter $\sigma = 0.05$ Ry. Having obtained self- consistent solutions of the Kohn–Sham equations, the lattice dynamical properties were calculated within the framework of self-consistent density functional pertur- bation theory [27]. In order to obtain complete phonon dispersions and densities of states, ten dynamical matrices were calculated on a $4 \times 4 \times 4$ $\mathbf{q}$-point mesh. The dynamical matrices at arbitrary wavevectors could be evaluated by means of a Fourier deconvolution on this mesh.

Elastic constants are important parameters of a material and can provide valuable information about the structural stability. A cubic system has three independent elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{44}$. The bulk modulus $B$ of the system can be expressed as a linear combination of $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$.

For cubic crystals the mechanical stability requires the elastic constants to satisfy the well-known Born stability criteria [28]: $C_{44} < 0$, $C_{11} < 0$, $C_{11}-C_{12} < 0$, $C_{11}+2C_{12} < 0$ and $C_{11} < B < C_{12}$.

The elastic constants can be obtained by calculating the total energy as a function of the volume-conserving strains that break the cubic symmetry. The bulk modulus $B$, $C_{44}$, and the shear modulus $C'=(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ are calculated from the hydrostatic pressure $e=(\delta, \delta, \delta, 0, 0, 0)$, the triaxial shear strain $e=(0, 0, 0, \delta, \delta, \delta)$, and volume-conserving orthorhombic strain $e=(\delta, \delta, (1+\delta)^{-2}-1, 0, 0, 0)$, respectively [29]. Hence, $B$ can be obtained from

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{V}=\frac{9}{2}B\delta^{2} \tag{1}
$$

where $V$ is the volume of the unstrained lattice cell, and $\Delta E$ is the energy variation as a result of an applied strain with vector $e=(e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{3}, e_{4}, e_{5}, e_{6})$. $C'$ can be calculated from

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{V}=6C'\delta^{2}+O\delta^{3}. \tag{2}
$$

The two expressions above yield $C_{11}=(3B+4C')/3$ and $C_{12}=(3B-2C')/3$, and $C_{44}$ is given by

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{V}=\frac{3}{2}C_{44}\delta^{2}. \tag{3}
$$

In this study, we calculated 21 sets of $\Delta E/V$ by varying $\delta$ from $-0.02$ to $0.02$ in steps of 0.002. Then, we fitted these data to a parabola, and the elastic constants were obtained from the quadratic coefficients.

### 3. Results
The structural properties of FeSi and CoSi in the B2 (CsCl) structure are investigated by calculating the total energy for the different volumes of the unit cell. The calculated total energies were fitted to the Murnaghan equation of state [30] to obtain ground state properties such as the lattice constants $a$, the bulk modulus $B$, and its pressure derivative $B'$. Second-order elastic constants and bulk modulus are determined from the curvature of the energy curve as a function of strain for selected deformations of the unit cell. The lattice constants, bulk modulus, first-order pressure derivative of the bulk modulus, and second-order elastic constants are listed in Table 1, along with experimental and other theoretically calculated results. From Table 1, one can see that the calculated lattice constants for FeSi and CoSi are in good agreement with the other theoretical and experimental results. Our calculated lattice constant agrees within $1.87\%$ for FeSi [31] and $0.07\%$ for CoSi [14] with the corresponding experimental values. Also, it can be clearly seen that the equilibrium lattice parameter and the bulk modulus are in good agreement with the earlier theoretical results.

Though it is not our main purpose to give a detailed review of the electronic structure, the electronic band structures at the equilibrium lattice constants of FeSi and CoSi in B2 structures have been calculated along the high-symmetry directions in the Brillouin zone and are shown in Figure 1, in which $E_{F} = 0$ is assumed. The overall electronic band structure and the density of states spectrum profiles are in fairly good agreement with newly calculated results [33–35].

The calculated total density of states and partial density of states for FeSi and CoSi are presented in Figure 2. The bands in the energy range between $- 15$ and $- 7$ eV are from Si 3s and Si 3p states with less contribution from Fe (Co) 4s and 3d states. For the energy range from $- 7$ to $- 1$ eV the density of states mainly originates from the hybridization of Fe (Co) 3d and Si 3p states. In the B2 structure at the Fermi level, the electronic densities of states for FeSi and CoSi are seen to be 0.78 and 1.62 $\frac{\text{states}}{\text{eVcell}}$, respectively. The calculated value for FeSi can be compared with the previous theoretical value of 0.80 $\frac{\text{states}}{\text{eVcell}}$ [33]. For both materials the density of states at the Fermi level is mainly from the Fe (Co) 3d and Si 3p states.

two optical modes along the high-symmetry directions $\Gamma$-X and $\Gamma$-R due to the degeneracy of the transverse modes in both the acoustic and optical branches.

From Figure 3, one can see that in the phonon spectra of FeSi and CoSi in the B2-type structure, all phonon frequencies are safely positive. There is no imaginary frequency near the high-symmetry points. This indicates that the structure is dynamically stable. The optical phonon modes include atomic vibrations of Si atoms; on the other hand, acoustic phonon modes are pictured exclusively by the vibrations of Fe (Co) atoms. We consider more attentively the high-frequency optical modes with high participation of Si vibrations. This can be seen from the phonon partial density of states.

In metallic materials, an additional degeneracy is obtained between the LO and TO modes at the zone center, due to screening of the macroscopic electric field by the conduction electrons. Since FeSi and CoSi are metallic crystals in the B2 (CsCl) structure, longitudinal optical (LO) and transverse optical (TO) phonon modes are

The zone-center optical phonon modes of FeSi and CoSi are calculated to be 9.91 THz and 8.37 THz, respectively. A comparison with theoretical and experimental data for FeSi is given in Table 1. There is good agreement between our calculated TO phonon mode at the $\Gamma$ point and the experimental and theoretical modes. Figure 4 depicts the shapes of these phonon modes. The arrows represent the directions of the atomic vibrations for Fe (Co) and Si. Also the lengths of the arrows are proportional to the contributions of vibrations from each atom. There is no clear gap between the acoustic and optical branches for FeSi due to the mass difference between the Fe and Si atoms. In addition, when the $\mathbf{q}$-wavevector increases along the [110] direction, the LA phonon branch lies higher in frequency than the lowest TO branch. According to the projected density of states for FeSi, the peaks at the smaller frequency region are due to TA(X), TA(M), and LA(R), whereas those at the higher frequency region are due to optical phonons. Figure 5 shows the eigen-displacement patterns of all phonon modes at the X point for FeSi in the B2 structure. The four distinct phonon energies are found to be 5.22 THz (TA), 10.57 THz (LA), 9.37 THz (TO), and 14.80 THz (LO). All the acoustic phonon modes include atomic vibrations from the Fe atoms. On the other hand, the optical phonon modes are localized on the Si atoms.

In accordance with symmetry, at the R point both acoustic and optical modes are triply degenerate. The two distinct phonon modes are found to be 8.66 THz and 11.00 THz. Figure 6 shows the eigen-displacement patterns of these phonon modes at the R point. Similar to the X point, while acoustic phonon modes include atomic vibrations from the Fe atoms, the optical phonon mode is localized on the Si atom.

In fact, they also indicated that the accuracy of their value is smaller than the predictions of the TO phonon mode at the $\Gamma$ point, and explained this as being due to large fluctuations in the energy curve because they interpolated with a polynomial function.

For CoSi, there is an optical-acoustic energy bandgap in the phonon dispersion curves. The bandgap between the acoustic and optical branches of CoSi is around 0.8 THz. The mass ratio of Co and Si is responsible for the bandgap between the acoustic and optical branches for CoSi.

First, the distributed peaks between 3 THz and 4.5 THz are due to transverse acoustic (TA) phonon modes. ## 4. Summary
We have presented a thorough theoretical analysis of the structural, elastic, and electronic properties of FeSi and CoSi in the B2 (CsCl) structure by using the local density approximation of density functional theory and *ab initio* pseudopotentials. Our calculated results for the lattice constants, bulk modulus, and three independent elastic constants agree quite well with previous experimental and theoretical results. The electronic band structures were calculated for both materials, in the B2 (CsCl) structure. The electronic band structures of FeSi and CoSi showed a metallic-like character. Our electronic density of states results suggested that the main contributions to the density of states at the Fermi level come from the Fe 3d, Co 3d, and Si 3p states.

Using our calculated lattice constants and electronic structures, phonon properties of FeSi and CoSi have been studied by employing a linear-response approach based on density functional perturbation theory. The calculated zone-center phonon mode for FeSi is in good agreement with IR transmittance/reflectance measurements and TB calculations. We have observed that all phonon modes are positive and thus these compounds are dynamically stable. Schematic illustrations of the eigen-displacements of the phonon modes for FeSi in the B2 (CsCl) structure at the $\Gamma$, X, and R symmetry points are presented and discussed.
