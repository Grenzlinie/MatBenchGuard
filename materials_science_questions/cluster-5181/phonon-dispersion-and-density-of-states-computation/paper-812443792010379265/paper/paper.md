# Vibrational properties of isolated AlAs monolayers embedded in GaAs: a theoretical study of the effects of disorder

Elisa Molinari
Consiglio Nazionale delle Ricerche (CNR), Istituto "O.M. Corbino", Via Cassia 1216, I-00189 Roma, Italy

Stefano Baroni
Scuola Internazionale Superiore di Studi Avanzati (SISSA), Via Beirut 4, I-34014 Trieste, Italy

Paolo Giannozzi and Stefano de Gironcoli
Institut Romand de Recherche Numérique en Physique des Matériaux (IRRMA), PHB - Ecublens, CH-1015 Lausanne, Switzerland

Received 6 May 1991; accepted for publication 8 May 1991

We present ab-initio calculations of Raman spectra of a prototype superlattice (SL) formed by the periodic repetition of an AlAs single monolayer embedded between two thicker GaAs layers. By comparing results for the ideal structure and for disordered configurations characterized by cationic intermixing, we demonstrate that AlAs-like longitudinal optical modes are extremely sensitive to disorder and may allow a quantitative characterization of the actual composition profiles obtained in real samples. The general implications of this conclusion for the study of interface intermixing in GaAs/AlAs SL's are discussed.

## 1. Introduction

In spite of the enormous amount of theoretical and experimental work devoted to SL's in the last decade, several open issues still exist concerning the actual structure of interfaces even in the case of MBE-grown (001) GaAs/AlAs, which is by far the most studied system. In particular, following very recent high-resolution transmission electron microscopy experiments and the re-evaluation of excitonic splittings in photoluminescence spectra, the actual existence of large atomically flat inter- face portions is presently the subject of a re- newed discussion for both direct (AlAs deposited on GaAs) and inverse (GaAs on AlAs) interfaces [1]. A quantitative characterization of the amount and the extension of cationic intermixing is still an open question. The purpose of the present paper is to show that the study of phonon spectra may help to answer this question.

The use of vibrational spectroscopies - partic- ularly Raman scattering - to obtain information about the structure of (001) SL's has been suc- cessfully introduced since a few years. In the acoustical frequency range the SL phonon spec- trum can be described in terms of folding of a bulk branch, with appropriate effective velocity, into the superlattice Brillouin zone (BZ), with small gaps opening at the center and at the boundary of the BZ owing to the different acous- tic impedance of the two constituents. As these modes are *extended* to the whole SL, by measur- ing their frequency one obtains information on the SL period $d$ [2]. In the optical frequency range, on the other hand, SL vibrations are *con- fined* in either GaAs or AlAs because of the non-overlapping bulk branches of the two materi- als; they are non-dispersive, and their frequencies have been shown to correspond with a good accu- racy to the frequency of the bulk materials at the

## 2. Theoretical approach

A proper account of cationic intermixing requires the use of large supercells to simulate the effects of disorder. Though the use of density-functional linear-response techniques have allowed us to calculate entirely from first principles the vibrational properties of some AlGaAs superlattices [6], the application of these techniques is computationally demanding, and practically limited at present to systems not larger than $\sim 20$ atoms. Recently we have proposed a new scheme for calculating phonon spectra of GaAs/AlAs SL's, which is as simple to use as phenomenological models (thus allowing to treat systems as large as $\sim 10^{3}$ atoms), $\cdots$ t being as accurate and predictive as first-principles calculations. This scheme is described in more detail elsewhere [4,5]: here we just recall the main ideas.

As a consequence of the close chemical similarity between Ga and Al, the difference between the bulk phonon dispersion of GaAs and AlAs can be accurately accounted for by the different cationic masses [4,6]. The real-space interatomic force constants of the two materials are very close to each other thus suggesting that the force constants of any AlGaAs mixed system are independent of composition. We therefore calculate the interatomic force constants of an average periodic crystal whose cationic pseudopotential is the average of the Ga and Al pseudopotentials (virtual crystal). We then use these force constants to build up the SL dynamical matrix using the appropriate distribution of masses (mass approximation). Phonon frequencies and displacement patterns are obtained by direct diagonalization.

The Fourier transform cannot be done in a straightforward way owing to the non-analytic character of $D$ at long wavelengths, which is due to the long-range dipole-dipole interactions characteristic of polar materials. However, this non-analytic behavior can be easily subtracted, as it is described by a Coulomb contribution to the dynamical matrix which is exactly obtained from the effective charges and dielectric constants, also calculated ab-initio within our linear response scheme. Once the interatomic force constants are available, their ability to describe both bulk GaAs and AlAs and GaAs/AlAs mixed systems within the mass approximation has been proved by comparing the results with those of full ab-initio calculations [4,6]. It turns out that the agreement is excellent: the effect of the mass approximation is negligible for acoustic and transverse optical (TO) modes, while it is limited to a quasi-rigid shift of $\sim 5-6 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ towards higher (lower) energies of the GaAs- (AlAs-) like longitudinal optical (LO) modes; this shift originates from the slightly over- (under-) estimated LO-TO splitting produced for GaAs (AlAs) by the virtual-crystal effective charges. The effect of disorder is simulated by choosing a random distribution of cations in the appropriate concentration for the mixed planes, and then by averaging the calculated properties over $\sim10$ different random configurations.

Fig. 2a presents the calculated Raman spectrum for an ideal $(GaAs)_{5}(AlAs)_{1}$ SL (no intermixing) in the usual backscattering $z(xy)\bar{z}$ configuration. The three peaks in the GaAs-like frequency range represent the odd-order LO confined modes, $\omega_{1.01}$, $\omega_{1.03}$, $\omega_{1.05}$, the even ones being forbidden for this scattering geometry. Clearly, the single monolayer of AlAs introduces a perturbation which is strong enough to confine GaAs-like modes; the deviation of their frequencies from the corresponding frequencies in a SL with thicker AlAs barriers (ticks on the top scale of the figure) is a measure of the coupling between successive GaAs layers. In the AlAs-like range, a well defined peak is present, whose frequency obviously falls within the bulk AlAs band.

It is now interesting to focus on the behaviour of this peak in an intermixed configuration, such that the same Al content is spread over two monolayers rather than one. For example, the unit cell of fig. 2b, $(GaAs)_{4}(Al_{0.78}Ga_{0.22}As)_{1}$ $(Al_{0.22}Ga_{0.78}As)_{1}$ might model a structure grown by MBE with low substrate temperature; the effect of Ga segregation at the surface during growth [7] is thus assumed to be limited to a slight asymmetric broadening of the Al concentration profile. In the AlAs-like range, the corresponding Raman spectrum shows a very evident difference from the ideal case: besides the main peak, which is shifted to lower frequency by almost $7\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ with respect to fig. 2a, a secondary peak appears at lower frequencies. The origin of these peaks can be identified if one refers to the two lower panels, corresponding to a single $\text{Al}_{x}\text{Ga}_{1-x}\text{As}$ monolayer embedded in GaAs with $x=0.78$ or with $x=0.22$. It appears that each individual alloy monolayer gives rise to an individual Raman peak, whose frequency shift from the pure AlAs monolayer frequency depends on concentration. When two alloy monolayers are close to each other – as in fig. 2b – their AlAs-like spectrum is very similar to a superposition of the two individual peaks. This turns out to be true even when the difference in concentration $\Delta x$ between the two monolayers is as low as $\Delta x\approx0.1$.

To explain this behaviour, it is essential to recall two main specific features of the AlAs LO branch: the first is the extreme flatness of its (001) dispersion, as apparent from fig. 1 (see also refs. [4,8]); the second is that the AlAs-like "quasi-dispersion", which can be defined in the $\text{Al}_{x}\text{Ga}_{1-x}\text{As}$ alloy [9,5], is found to maintain the same flat shape and to shift almost rigidly and linearly with the concentration $x$ [5]. This last fact reflects in a quasilinear $x$ dependence of the AlAs-like peak associated to an isolated $\text{Al}_{x}\text{Ga}_{1-x}\text{As}$ monolayer in GaAs (fig. 3). Moreover, when the difference in concentration of between an $\text{Al}_{x}\text{Ga}_{1-x}\text{As}$ monolayer and the neighbouring layers is such that the AlAs-like LO bands of the corresponding bulk alloys do not overlap in energy, a confined AlAs-like mode of the $\text{Al}_{x}\text{Ga}_{1-x}\text{As}$ monolayer exists; its frequency is then very close to the frequency of an isolated $\text{Al}_{x}\text{Ga}_{1-x}\text{As}$ monolayer in GaAs and can be extracted from fig. 3.

By using this scheme to interpret experimental Raman spectra, it is therefore possible to discrim-

inate between the actual existence of a nominal AlAs monolayer in GaAs and the existence of several intermixed layers. Raman experiments [10] on samples grown at substrate temperatures in the range $T_s=400-600^\circ$C indicate that a substan- tial degree of intermixing takes place even at the lowest values of $T_s$. More detailed investigations and a quantitative determination of the composi- tion profiles will be the subject of a forthcoming publication [11].

In summary, we have shown that the AlAs-like optical phonons are very sensitive to the composi- tion of individual cationic planes in a superlattice, and therefore allow one to discriminate between a nominal (AlAs)$_1$(GaAs)$_5$ SL and a superlattice with the same period and average composition but with intermixed cationic planes. This results is of special relevance in view of the possibility of extending the use of Raman spectroscopy to de- termine cationic composition profiles in real GaAs/AlAs superlattices.
