# Mean-Square Displacements for $\mathrm{NH}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ and $\mathrm{ND}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ Crystals

By
J. KURITTU and M. MERISALO

The mean-square displacements for $\mathrm{NH}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ and $\mathrm{ND}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ crystals at various temperatures between 85 K and 165 K have been calculated from the eigenfrequencies and eigenvectors of the phonon states as obtained from a rigid ion-rigid molecule model of lattice dynamics, and from the internal vibrations of ammonium ions. The calculated results are discussed in relation to available experimental data.

Es wurden die mittleren Verrückungsquadrate für $\mathrm{NH}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ - und $\mathrm{ND}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$-Kristalle bei ver schiedenen Temperaturen zwischen 85 K und 165 K aus den Eigenfrequenzen und Eigen vektoren der Phononzustände - erhalten aus einem Modell starres Ion-starres Molekül der Gitterdynamik - und aus den inneren Schwingungen der Ammoniumionen berechnet. Diese Ergebnisse werden mit den zugänglichen experimentellen Angaben verglichen.

## 1. Introduction

Thermal parameters of atoms in charge density studies are usually obtained by the conventional least-squares refinement of X-ray data. However, it has been shown [1] that the good agreement obtained between observed and cal- culated X-ray densities in a least-squares refinement is often produced by a combination of systematic errors of thermal parameters with small but system- atic shifts in positional parameters. Consequently, quantitative studies of electronic structure can be significantly misleading, if one applies the X-ray values of these parameters (cf. e.g. [1, 2]).

This is a field where neutron diffraction studies lend themselves to proper use. The potential of these studies is based essentially on their superiority over X-ray diffraction in inferring thermal (and positional) parameters of atoms in crystals. However, the results obtained by neutron diffraction techniques can be interfered with by the thermal diffuse scattering, for which a reliable cor- rection can seldom be made. An alternative is the calculation of thermal param- eters from the eigenfrequencies and the corresponding eigenvectors, as obtained from a lattice-dynamical model based on inelastic neutron scattering data.

This paper reports the calculation of thermal parameters for $\mathrm{NH}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ and $\mathrm{ND}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ crystals. These crystals have been selected as objects of joint X-ray and neutron diffraction studies [3,4].

## 2. Lattice-Dynamical Model

An experimental study of the lattice vibrations in $\mathrm{ND}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ at 85 K by in elastic scattering of neutrons have been carried out recently by Teh and Brock- house [5]. The results were analyzed by Cowley [6] using a shell model in which the unit cell was regarded as containing six independent ions. Good agreement was found between the experimental and calculated dispersion curves.

The afore-mentioned experimental results were analyzed subsequently by Teh [7] using a modification of the rigid ion-rigid molecule model of Parlinski

12 physica (b) 74/1

[8, 9] whereby the ionic charge was allowed to vary, to make a total of eleven parameters. This model was shown to give a good overall description of the experimental dispersion curves.

It is evident from the large gap between the frequencies of the internal and external phonon modes that the intramolecular forces of the $\mathrm{NH}_{4}$ ion are larger than the intermolecular forces by an order of magnitude at least. Therefore, the $\mathrm{NH}_{4}$ ion can be considered as a rigid unit in the calculation of the contri bution of external vibrations to the mean-square displacements, and the contri bution of the internal modes can be calculated separately using a free ion model. Furthermore, by the translation-libration formalism of the molecular motion, the curvilinear features of the motion of hydrogen atoms can be accounted for in the harmonic approximation. The same formalism has become widely used in structural studies (for references, cf. [10], p. 203) and the comparison of experimental and calculated results is thus straightforward.

In the present work the rigid ion-rigid molecule model of Teh [7] has been adopted. Small discrepancies between Teh's model and the experimental results for some modes at large wave vectors are considered insignificant in the calculation of mean-square amplitudes, because these are weighted heavily by low acoustic frequencies.

### 3. Calculation of Thermal Parameters

The dynamical matrix for $\mathrm{NH}_{4} \mathrm{Cl}$ in the rigid ion-rigid molecule formalism can be written
$$
D=m^{-1 / 2}(R+Z C Z) m^{-1 / 2}, \tag{1}
$$
where $R$ is a matrix describing the short-range interactions, $C$ is a matrix specifying the long-range Coulomb interaction calculated using the Ewald transformation [11], and $Z$ is a diagonal matrix representing effective ionic charges. $m^{-1 / 2}$ is a diagonal matrix, whose first three diagonal elements are $m_{1}^{-1 / 2}$, the next three $m_{2}^{-1 / 2}$, and the last three $I^{-1 / 2}$, where $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ are the masses of chlorine and the ammonium group, respectively, and $I$ is the moment of inertia of the ammonium group.

The model parameters of Teh [7] were used to set up the dynamical matrix and its eigenvalues $\omega^{2}(\boldsymbol{q} j)$ and corresponding eigenvectors $\boldsymbol{e}(\boldsymbol{q} j)$ were computed for a mesh of 1000 regularly distributed wave vectors $\boldsymbol{q}$ in the first Brillouin zone.

The translational mean-square displacement $\left\langle u_{n}^{2}\right\rangle$ for chlorine and the am monium group were calculated from the expression
$$
\left\langle u_{n}^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{3 N m_{n}} \sum_{\boldsymbol{q} j} \frac{E(\boldsymbol{q} j)}{\omega^{2}(\boldsymbol{q} j)}|\boldsymbol{e}(n | \boldsymbol{q} j)|^{2}, \tag{2}
$$
where $N$ is the number of wave vectors, $m_{n}$ is the mass of the $n$-th ion (chlorine or ammonium group), $E(\boldsymbol{q} j)$ is the mean energy of the $(\boldsymbol{q} j)$ mode, and $\boldsymbol{e}(n | \boldsymbol{q} j)$ is the appropriate part of the eigenvector. Rotational mean-square displacement $\left\langle\theta^{2}\right\rangle$ for the rigid ammonium group was calculated from a similar expression by replacing $\boldsymbol{e}(n | \boldsymbol{q} j)$ by the rotational component of the eigenvector, and $m_{n}$ by the moment of inertia of the ammonium group.

The mean-square displacement matrix $B$ of the hydrogen atom is obtained from the equation
$$
B=T+R L R^{\mathrm{T}}, \tag{3}
$$

where $T$ and $L$ are the diagonal matrices (so-called translation and libration matrices), whose diagonal elements are $\langle u^{2}\rangle$ and $\langle\theta^{2}\rangle$, respectively, as given by (2), and where $R$ is defined by

$$
R=\begin{pmatrix}
0 & z & -y \\
-z & 0 & x \\
y & -x & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

$x,y,z$ represent the components of a vector defining the position of the hydrogen atom with respect to the centre of libration, which coincides with the nitrogen atom.

The terms corresponding to the $\boldsymbol{q}=0$ acoustic-mode phonons in the sum over the wave-vector mesh in (2) are divergent. The divergence was removed by converting this part of the summation to an integral over a sphere of volume equal to one thousandth of that of the Brillouin zone around the origin and by assuming that the frequency in this region is given by the average velocity times the wave vector.

Contributions to the mean-square displacements of atoms from the internal vibrations of the $\mathrm{NH}_{4}$ (and $\mathrm{ND}_{4}$) group were obtained according to the standard procedure described by Cyvin [12]. These contributions can be calculated once the internal symmetry coordinates and the corresponding force constants have been established. The force constants adopted in this work were those given by Ramaswamy and Ranganathan [13].

There may be in addition a lowering of frequencies because of the disordering of the $NH_4$ or $ND_4$ groups. As estimated from the observed frequency shifts of external modes the present values of $\langle u^2 \rangle$ for Cl and N at 165 K are in error on the low side by about $2\%$. The anharmonicity observed in librational modes will cause an extra error of about $2\%$ in the values of $\langle u_\perp^2 \rangle$ for H and D at 165 K.

On the other hand, contributions from the internal vibrations to the mean-square displacements can be considered to be independent of temperature.

The present results can be compared with the recent neutron diffraction study at 138 K [15], which yielded the values $9.7 \pm 0.8, 11.0 \pm 0.8, 31.2 \pm 1.7$, and $20.1 \pm 2.1$ (in units of $10^{-3} \mathring{A}^2$) for $\langle u^2 \rangle_{Cl}, \langle u^2 \rangle_{N}, \langle u_\perp^2 \rangle_{H}, \langle u_\parallel^2 \rangle_{H}$, respectively. The corresponding values calculated in this work are 9.4, 10.7, 34.5, and

15.7, respectively. Note the good agreement for Cl and N. The calculated results indicate more anisotropic motion of hydrogen than the results of the neutron diffraction study, and it is suggested that the model of independent vibrations of atoms used in analyzing the neutron data is insufficient.

The mean-square amplitudes of the radial vibrations of N-H and N-D bonds were also calculated by applying the method described by Cyvin [12]. The values obtained were $5.76 \times 10^{-3}$ and $4.16 \times 10^{-3} \mathring{A}^2$ for N-H and N-D bond vibrations, respectively. The value of $(6 \pm 1) \times 10^{-3} \mathring{A}^2$ obtained in a neutron diffraction study [4] agrees with the present calculated result.

The value of N-H bond length $1.041 \mathring{A}$ obtained in analyzing the neutron diffraction data in terms of a model of independent vibrations [15] can be corrected for libration (cf. [10], equation (6.16)) and gives the result $1.058 \mathring{A}$.
