# Polonium bulk and surface vibrational dynamics

Antoine Khater*,¹, Rachid Tigrine¹,², and Boualem Bourahla¹,²

¹ Laboratoire de Physique PEC UMR 6087, Université du Maine, 72085 Le Mans, France

Published online 25 May 2009

* Corresponding author: e-mail antoine.khater@univ-lemans.fr

Calculations are presented for the bulk phonons and for surface Rayleigh phonons and resonances for Polonium, the only element known to form in the simple cubic lattice. The static stability of this lattice has been confirmed recently by ab initio simulations which yield two bulk elastic constants, $c_{11}$ and $c_{12}$. Constitutive equations are derived for the isotropic cubic lattice based upon the Fuchs's method. This permits effectively a numerical evaluation of central potential force constants for Polonium from the ab initio results. Numerical calculations are then made for the material vibration dynamics in the force constant model with the use of the matching method. The numerical applications yield for Polonium the bulk phonon branches along [100], [110], and [111], and the Rayleigh phonons and surface resonances along the [010] direction in an unreconstructed (001) surface. The local vibrational densities of states are calculated for bulk and surface sites for this element.

## 1 Introduction
The metal Polonium is a unique material in so far that it is the only element known to form in the simple cubic lattice. Its crystal structure was first determined by Beamer and Maxwell in 1946 [1], but there has been very little experimental work reported since [2–4].

Polonium is found to have two structures, the $\alpha$-Po which is simple cubic with a lattice constant of $3.36$ Å, and the $\beta$-Po which is quasi-simple cubic with a lattice constant of $3.37$ Å and an angle of $89^\circ14'$. In a recent paper [5], the authors report an ab initio calculation for Polonium, and their calculation confirms its simple cubic structure; this work gives an analysis of why the simple cubic lattice is energetically favorable for Polonium rather than other structures, such as the fcc and the bcc lattices.

The stability of cubic crystals under constant hydrostatic pressure was first analyzed by Born [6]. A rich and lengthy analysis of this question has been made since [7–9]. Although simple cubic crystals are inherently unstable, recent molecular-dynamics simulations verify that a range of hydrostatic pressure exists for which the simple cubic crystals are indeed stable [10]. We have not attempted a comparison between Refs. [5] and [10], but both give credibility to the notion of the static stability of simple cubic crystals under certain hydrostatic conditions.

To our knowledge the experimental study of Polonium vibration dynamics has not been attempted yet, probably due to the toxicity of this material and to the difficulty of its preparation. The motivation of this investigation is hence to present a calculation for the bulk and surface vibrational properties of a statically stable simple cubic Polonium. It is hoped that future measurements of the lattice dynamics in this material would contribute, in comparison, to further information as regards the question of static and dynamic stability of this crystal.

We consider the lattice to be simple cubic corresponding to $\alpha$-Po. The matching method [11–13], is employed for a force constant model over nearest and next nearest neighbors, in the harmonic approximation, to calculate the dispersion phonon branches along the principal lattice symmetry directions in the bulk, and the dispersion of the Rayleigh surface phonons for the (001) surface. The spectral densities and the vibrational density of states (VDOS) for bulk and surface sites are also calculated.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 an analysis is presented to derive constitutive equations for the isotropic cubic lattice based upon the Fuchs's method [14–16]. This procedure yields the central force constants for Polonium from the elastic constants given in Ref. [5].

2 Model bulk force constants The primary difficulty for the present work is how to derive appropriate force constants from the limited available elastic information for Polonium. In Ref. [5], only two elastic constants, $c_{11}=113$ GPa and $c_{12}=28$ GPa, are given from the ab initio calculation. To be able to extract information for the force constants from these results, we have developed for the simple cubic lattice a method first proposed by Fuchs [14] and used by a number of authors to derive the ion–ion force constants for the face centered cubic and the body centered cubic lattices [14–18].

We have developed this same approach to derive for the simple cubic lattice a set of constitutive equations. These may be presented in the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{11}-c_{12} & =\frac{3 n}{4}\left[r^{2} \varphi_{1}^{\prime \prime}\right]_{1}+\frac{3 n}{2}\left[r \varphi_{1}^{\prime}\right]_{1}+6 n\left[r \varphi_{2}^{\prime}\right]_{2}, \\
c_{44} & =\frac{3 n}{2}\left[r \varphi_{1}^{\prime}\right]_{1}+\frac{3 n}{4}\left[r^{2} \varphi_{2}^{\prime \prime}\right]_{2}+\frac{3 n}{2}\left[r \varphi_{2}^{\prime}\right]_{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

The functions $\varphi_{1}$ and $\varphi_{2}$ denote the central force pair potentials between nearest and next nearest neighbor ions, respectively, and $n$ is the atomic density. The differential forms in Eq. (1) are evaluated for the equilibrium lattice positions at the nearest neighbor distance, $a=3.36$ Å, for $i=1$, and for the next nearest neighbor distance, $\sqrt{2} a$, for $i=2$. For isotropic cubic lattices the elastic constant $c_{44}$ may be written [6, 19] as $(c_{11}-c_{12})/2$. Furthermore, the approximation for small lattice vibrations about equilibrium implies that

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\left[r \varphi_{1}^{\prime \prime}\right]_{1} } & \ll\left[r^{2} \varphi_{1}^{\prime \prime}\right]_{1}, \\
{\left[r \varphi_{2}^{\prime}\right]_{2} } & \ll\left[r^{2} \varphi_{2}^{\prime \prime}\right]_{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

We are hence able to derive for bulk Polonium the numerical values for the nearest and next neighbor force constants, as $k_{1}=38.1$ Nm⁻¹ and $k_{2}=9.5$ Nm⁻¹, using the calculated data $c_{11}=113$ GPa and $c_{12}=28$ GPa with the assurance of the static stability of the cubic structure.

The phonon branches in the bulk are calculated next along the primary directions [100], [110], and [111]. The Rayleigh surface modes for the unreconstructed (001) surface along the direction [010], are also calculated using the matching method [11–13]. Cartesian directions $x$, $y$ and $z$ are along [100], [010], and [001], respectively.

The dynamics of the system are described by the equations of motion of atomic sites $l$, which in the harmonic approximation may be expressed as

$$
\omega^{2} M(l) u_{\alpha}(l)=-\sum_{l^{\prime} \neq l} \sum_{\beta} k(l, l^{\prime}) d_{\alpha} d_{\beta} / d^{2}\left[u_{\beta}(l)-u_{\beta}(l^{\prime})\right],
\tag{3}
$$

$u_{\alpha}(l)$ is the corresponding vibrational displacement vector for atom $l$. In the bulk the lattice sites are identified by $l=n_{1}, n_{2}, n_{3}$, along the $x$-, $y$-, and $z$-axes, respectively. The indices, $\alpha$ and $\beta$, denote Cartesian co-ordinates, and $M \equiv M(l)=3.49 \times 10^{-25}$ kg for all $l$, is the atomic mass of Polonium. The radius vector $\boldsymbol{d}$ between sites at $l$ and $l^{\prime}$ has Cartesian components $d_{\alpha}$, and $d=|\boldsymbol{d}|$. The force constants between any two given sites is denoted by $k(l, l^{\prime})$.

The system of linear equations for the bulk from Eq. (3), may be cast in the form

$$
\left[\Omega^{2} \boldsymbol{I}-D\left(\mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{x}}, \mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{y}}, \mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{z}}, r\right)\right]|\boldsymbol{u}\rangle=0,
\tag{4}
$$

$|\boldsymbol{u}\rangle$ is the vector of vibration displacements along the principal axes in a unit cell. $\left[\Omega^{2} \boldsymbol{I}-D\left(\mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{x}}, \mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{y}}, \mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{z}}, r\right)\right]$ is the bulk dynamics matrix, where $\mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{x}}$, $\mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{y}}$, and $\mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{z}}$, are the phase factors along the principal axes, and $\boldsymbol{I}$ is the corresponding unit matrix. $r$ is the ratio $k_{2}/k_{1}$, and is approximately 0.25 for bulk Polonium. $\phi_{\alpha}=q_{\alpha} a$ is a normalized wave vector in the first BZ over the interval $[-\pi,+\pi]$.

By diagonalizing the matrix of Eq. (4), the phonon dispersion branches are calculated along the directions [100], [110], and [111], and are presented respectively in Figs. 1, 2 and 3. Note that $\Omega=\omega/\omega_{0}$ is a dimensionless frequency, where $\omega_{0}=10.45 \times 10^{12}$ rad s⁻¹ is a characteristic frequency in bulk Polonium. For the phonons along the symmetry directions [100] and [111], modes 1 and 2 are degenerate throughout the BZ. For the phonons along [110] modes 1 and 2 are degenerate at the zone boundary.

3 Surface phonons The vibration dynamics in the unreconstructed (001) surface for Polonium is next considered. This surface breaks the translation symmetry along the $z$-axis normal to its plane. To analyze the surface vibration dynamics there is need to consider both the propagating and the evanescent modes along the $z$-axis [11, 20], where the more general phase $\eta_{z}$ replaces the Bloch phase factor $\mathrm{e}^{i \phi_{z}}$. The modes along the $z$-direction are described effectively in general by the phase doublets $\{\eta_{z}, \eta_{z}^{-1}\}$. The

The nontrivial doublet solutions $\{\eta_{z},\ \eta_{z}^{-1}\}$ are consequently calculated, as a function of the dimensionless frequency, $\Omega$, and for given force constants of the system from the solutions of the secular equation for the resultant matrix $[\Omega^{2}I-D(\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{x}},\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{y}},\eta_{z},r)]$, as in Eq. (4).

Since the dynamics of the surface generates an infinite system of coupled equations, the dynamics of an irreducible set of sites at the surface need to be decoupled appropriately from the rest of the system to render the problem tractable. In the matching domains, between the surface region and the bulk, the vibration displacements $u_{\alpha}$ for an atom may be expressed in a Hilbert space constructed over the basis vectors $|\boldsymbol{R}\rangle$ of the evanescence field [11, 12, 20]. Using Eq. (4) for the surface vibrations, and the transformations mapping the two vectors $|\boldsymbol{R}\rangle$ and $|\boldsymbol{u}\rangle$, we obtain a system of linear equations

$$
\left[D_{m}(\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{x}},\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{y}},\eta_{z},\Omega,r)\right]|\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{R}\rangle=|0\rangle\,. \tag{5}
$$

The matrix $[D_{m}(\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{x}},\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{y}},\eta_{z},\Omega,r)]$ is a characteristic square matrix, calculated in the matching formalism. The dimensions of this matrix are characteristic of an irreducible set of sites at the surface boundary and of the size of the constructed Hilbert space.

For the purpose of numerical applications, the force constants are assumed unchanged at the unreconstructed Polonium (001) surface. Diagonalizing the square matrix $[D_{m}(\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{x}},\mathrm{e}^{i\phi_{y}},\eta_{z},\Omega,r)]$, yields the Rayleigh phonons and the surface resonances. In Fig. 4 are presented the phonons and resonances along the [010] direction in this surface. We identify a high frequency non dispersive Einstein mode, clearly visible in Fig. 4, and another dispersive phonon branch in the window of the bulk band. To make this latter mode visible a zoom is made in Fig. 5 for the window inside the projected bulk band. There is evidence for a non dispersive Rayleigh mode at the surface.

The matching formalism provides a framework for the calculation of the localized modes and of the spectral densities at the surface [20, 21]. The most direct manner to calculate these spectral densities is via the Green's functions, which may be expressed formally, using Eq. (5) as

$$
G(\Omega^{2}+i\varepsilon)=\left[D_{m}(\mathrm{e}^{i\phi x},\mathrm{e}^{i\phi y},\eta_{z},\Omega,r)\right]^{-1}. \tag{6}
$$

The local vibration densities of states, VDOS for atomic sites $l$, denoted by $\Xi_{l}(\Omega)$, are obtained as a sum over the trace of the spectral density matrix

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Xi_{l}(\Omega) & =\sum_{\alpha} \rho_{(\alpha, \alpha)}^{(l, l)}(\Omega) \\
& =-\frac{2 \Omega}{\pi} \sum_{\alpha} \lim _{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0^{+}}\left\{\operatorname{Im}\left[G_{\alpha \alpha}^{l l}\left(\Omega^{2}+i \varepsilon\right)\right]\right\}. \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

The calculated localized VDOS for the bulk and for a site in the topmost atomic layer in an unreconstructed Polonium (001) surface are presented in Fig. 6. The dashed and dotted spectra correspond respectively to these VDOS for bulk and surface sites.

4 Conclusions Under the assumption of a statically stable simple cubic Polonium crystal, calculations are presented for the bulk and surface vibration dynamics of this material. Two force constants are derived for nearest and next nearest neighbor interactions with the use of the available ab initio data, thanks to the development of a Fuchs-like analysis for a simple cubic crystallographic lattice that relates the elastic constants $c_{11}$ and $c_{12}$ to the central force constants. These force constants are used in a dynamics model to calculate the bulk phonon branches along the high symmetry directions, [100], [110], and [111], in the bulk crystal. The model yields furthermore the Rayleigh phonons and the surface resonances along the direction [010] in an unreconstructed Polonium (001) surface. There are no available experimental measurements at present for the vibration dynamics of this material to compare our calculations with.

There are other methods to calculate the bulk phonon bands such as the ab initio methods, and it would indeed be useful to cross-check the results of the force field calculation presented in this paper with such results when these are available for the phonon bulk bands. One of these is the projector augmented plane wave method implemented in the VASP package [22]. The PAW method is somewhat more accurate in describing the electronic structure than others at the expense of being a slower method. It is useful to note further that there is a trade-off in computational efficiency and predictive capability between ab initio and force field methods; the latter can also be more accurate than the former in certain cases [23].
