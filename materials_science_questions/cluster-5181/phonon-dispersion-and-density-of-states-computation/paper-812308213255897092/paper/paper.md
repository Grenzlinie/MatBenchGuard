# Low-Frequency Lattice Vibrational Spectrum of Indium
L. J. SLUTSKY AND GEORGE JELINEK

Department of Chemistry, University of Washington Seattle 5, Washington

(Received August 6, 1962)

IX independent interatomic force constants are required to describe the interactions of an atom in a monatomic body-centered tetragonal lattice with its first- and second-nearest neighbors. $^{1}$ Thus, if interactions between more distant pairs of atoms are neglected, the force constants may be evaluated from the six independent elastic constants of a tetragonal crystal and, the force constants being known, the vibrational frequency distribution function $g(\nu)$ and the thermodynamic properties may be approximated. Force constants have been evaluated from the elastic constants of indium at $0^{\circ} K^{2}$ and the secular equation $^{1}$

which expresses $\nu$ as a function of the wave vector $\mathbf{y}=y_{1}\mathbf{b}_{1}+y_{2}\mathbf{b}_{2}+y_{3}\mathbf{b}_{3}$ (where the $\mathbf{b}$'s are the basis vectors of the reciprocal lattice) has been solved at each point of a body-centered mesh generated by letting $y_{1}$, $y_{2}$, and $y_{3}$ run from 0.0 to 0.2 in steps of 0.004.

Since solutions to the secular equation were obtained only in the immediate neighborhood of the origin of the reduced Brillouin zone, the resulting frequency distribution is valid only at the extreme low-frequency end of the spectrum between $\nu=0$ and $2.5\times10^{11}$ cps. In this range the spectrum, normalized to $3\times$Avogadro's number of frequencies, is given approximately by $g(\nu)=1.45\times10^{-13}\ \nu^{2}-2.75\times10^{-37}\nu^{4}$. The lattice heat capacity computed from this spectrum is compared in Fig. 1 with the values measured by Bryant and Keesom for superconducting indium³ and with those predicted by the $T^{3}$ law using the Debye temperature of $111.3^{\circ}\mathrm{K}$ computed by Chandrasekhar and Rayne² from the low-temperature elastic constants. As would be expected from the negative quartic term in the spectrum, the heat capacity predicted by our model lies between that given by the Debye theory and the experimental results suggesting that the anomalously low lattice heat capacity of superconducting indium is in part due to a significant effect of dispersion even on the rather long waves which determine the specific heat below $1^{\circ}\mathrm{K}$.

A discussion of the lattice vibrations of a metal in terms of a short-range force, or indeed in terms of any force derived from an effective potential between ion cores,⁴ must be regarded with suspicion. However, this model has been quite successful in predicing the lattice heat capacity of In at higher temperatures. In con- sidering possible explanations of the apparently anom- alous behavior of the specific heats of certain super- conductors, it is desirable to have some estimate of the effect of deviation from the Debye spectrum.
