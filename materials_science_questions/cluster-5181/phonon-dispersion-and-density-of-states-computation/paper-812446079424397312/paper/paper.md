# A MODEL PSEUDOPOTENTIAL FOR ELECTRON-ION INTERACTION IN NOBLE METALS

Mahendra KUMAR and M.P. HEMKAR

Physics Department, University of Allahabad, Allahabad 211002, India

Received 7 September 1977

A simple model pseudopotential has been proposed for electron-ion interaction in noble metals and used to calculate the form factor, liquid metal resistivity and phonon dispersion curves for gold. The results are in reasonable agreement with the experimental data.

In recent years, the pseudopotential theory has been used successfully to compute various metallic properties of simple metals. The difficulties arise when this method of calculation is used for noble and transition metals because of the hybridization of d-states with the free-electron-like conduction band and of sufficient sizes of ionic cores. Harrison [1] has generalized the pseudopotential theory to include the d-band metals. Moriarty [2] has also carried out numerical calculations of several properties of the noble metals. Animalu [3] has used the concept of quantum defects in setting up a model potential for transition metal ions. Recently Lal et al. [4] have modified Ashcroft's empty core model potential [5] for the study of noble metals and have applied it to calculate phonon dispersion in silver. In noble metals the bands are completely filled and lie below, in close proximity to, the Fermi energy. These d-states contribute substantially in determining the electronic properties of metals. For this category of d-band metals, we propose a simple model potential in $r$-space along the lines of Lal et al. [4], given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{b}}(r) & =\frac{z e^{2} R_{\mathrm{ps}}}{R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}\left(R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}-R_{\mathrm{ps}}\right)}, \quad r<R_{\mathrm{ps}} \text { [region (i)] } \\
& =-\frac{z e^{2}}{R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}}, \quad R_{\mathrm{ps}}<r<R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}} \text { [region (ii)] (1) } \\
& =-\frac{z e^{2}}{r}, \quad r>R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}} \quad \text { [region (iii)], }
\end{aligned}
$$

where region (i) is pseudocore region and $R_{\mathrm{ps}}<R_{\mathrm{c}}$ (the core radius), region (ii) is effective d-electron region, and region (iii) is Coulombic region. $R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}$ defines core radius including d-electrons and will be taken equal to the ionic radius. Eq. (1) says that the potential is repulsive in region (i) and is attractive in region (ii). It becomes purely Coulombic in region (iii). The form of the model potential in $r$-space is shown in fig. 1. The repulsive part of the proposed potential arises from the tightly bound inner core states and attractive part in region (ii) is due to s-d hybridization [6,p.388] We have abandoned the small-core approximation in accordance with the generalized pseudopotential method [1] and considered the overlapping repulsive potential in the form of a central pair potential, exactly as given by Moriarty [2] .

In $q$-space the bare electron ion model pseudopotential assumes the form given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
& V_{\mathrm{b}}(q)=-\frac{4 \pi z e^{2}}{\Omega_{0} q^{2}}\left\lfloor\frac{\sin q R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}}{q R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}}\right. \\
& \quad+\frac{R_{\mathrm{ps}}}{R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}-R_{\mathrm{ps}}}\left(\cos q R_{\mathrm{ps}}-\frac{\sin q R_{\mathrm{ps}}}{q R_{\mathrm{ps}}}\right)\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

where $z$ is the chemical valence, $e$ is the electronic charge and $\Omega_{0}$ the atomic volume.

There are two unknown parameters, $R_{\mathrm{ps}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}$. $R_{\mathrm{ps}}$ is determined by using the relation [6, p. 419]

$$
R_{\mathrm{ps}}=0.51 R_{\mathrm{a}} z^{-1 / 3}, \tag{3}
$$

where $R_{\mathrm{a}}$ is the atomic radius. The value of $R_{\mathrm{a}}$ is determined by using the relation,

$$
\left(R_{\mathrm{c}} / R_{\mathrm{a}}\right)^{3}=2 \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}, \tag{4}
$$

which measures, quantitatively, the size of an ion (for a given $z$ ). $R_{\mathrm{c}}$ and $\alpha_{\text {eff }}$ are the same as defined and given in table 1 of ref. [3]. As for $R_{\mathrm{c}+\mathrm{d}}$, we have taken it equal to $R_{\mathrm{m}}$ as given in ref. [3]. In the present short note we have used this model for the calculation of form factors, liquid metal resistivity and phonon dispersion curves of gold. The parameters used in the calculations are given in table 1.

The screened form factors $v(q)$ have been obtained using the modified dielectric function $\epsilon^{*}(q)$ of Vashishta and Singwi [7], which describes the conduction electron screening, and are given by

$$
v(\boldsymbol{q})=V_{\mathrm{b}}(q) / \epsilon^{*}(q). \tag{5}
$$

The computed form factors for gold together with those of Moriarty [2] are shown in fig. 2.

The liquid resistivity of gold has been calculated by using the well-known relation [8,9]

$$
\rho=\frac{3 \pi m \Omega_{0}}{2 \hbar e^{2} E_{\mathrm{F}}}\left\langle a(K)|v(K)|^{2}\right\rangle, \tag{6}
$$

where the average function $\langle f(K)\rangle$ is defined by

$$
\langle f(K)\rangle=\frac{1}{4 k_{\mathrm{F}}^{4}} \int_{0}^{2 k_{\mathrm{F}}} f(K) K^{3} \mathrm{~d} K, \tag{7}
$$

$m$ and $E_{\mathrm{F}}$ are the electronic mass and the Fermi energy, $k_{\mathrm{F}}$ is the Fermi momentum, $K$ is the modulus of the momentum transfer vector, and $a(K)$ is the expectation value of $|S(K)|^{2}, S(K)$ being the structure factor. The structure factor data for gold are not available, therefore, we have taken the values of the squared structure factors to be the same as those used by Moriarty [2]. The calculated value of the liquid metal resistivity is obtained to be equal to $31.256 \mu \Omega \mathrm{cm}$. This value is very close to the measured one, $32.0 \mu \Omega \mathrm{cm}$ [10], while that obtained by Moriarty [2] is equal to 52.1 $\mu \Omega \mathrm{cm}$, a value $62.8 \%$ higher than the experimental value.

In the harmonic approximation, the angular frequencies of lattice vibrations of a monatomic lattice are obtained from the solution of the usual determinantal equation
$$\operatorname{det}\left|D_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{q})-M \omega^{2} \delta_{\alpha \beta}\right|=0, \quad(8)$$
where $D_{\alpha \beta}(q)$ represents the elements of the dynamic matrix, $M$ is the ionic mass, and $\alpha, \beta$ are the Cartesian components $(x, y, z). D_{\alpha \beta}(q)$ is written as the sum of the three terms [11]:
$$D_{\alpha \beta}(\boldsymbol{q})=D_{\alpha \beta}^{(\mathrm{C})}(\boldsymbol{q})+D_{\alpha \beta}^{(\mathrm{R})}(\boldsymbol{q})+D_{\alpha \beta}^{(\mathrm{E})}(\boldsymbol{q}).\qquad(9)$$
$D_{\alpha \beta}^{(C)}(q)$ is the Coulomb part which arises due to the electrostatic interaction between the ions, $D_{\alpha \beta}^{(R)}(q)$ is the repulsive contribution due to exchange overlap between the ion cores, and $D_{\alpha \beta}^{(E)}(q)$ is the band structure part.

Along the principal symmetry directions (100),(110) and (111), eq. (8) factorizes and the solutions can be expressed as [12]
$$\omega^{2}(\boldsymbol{q})=\omega_{\mathrm{C}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{q})+\omega_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{q})-\omega_{\mathrm{E}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{q}),\qquad(10)$$
where $\omega_{E}^{2}(q)=(1 / M) D^{(E)}(q)$ etc.

For the calculations of phonon dispersion curves we have taken the Coulomb contribution directly from the paper of Animalu et al. [13]. The repulsive part has been calculated exactly following Moriarty[2]. The calculation of the band structure part depends on the pseudopotential and is given by
$$\begin{aligned}
{\left[\omega_{\mathrm{E}}^{2}\right]_{\alpha \beta} } & =\omega_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\left[\sum_{\boldsymbol{H}} \frac{(\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{H})_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{H})_{\beta}}{|\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{H}|^{2}} G(\boldsymbol{q}+\boldsymbol{H})\right. \\
& \left.-\sum_{\boldsymbol{H} \neq 0} \frac{H_{\alpha} H_{\beta}}{|\boldsymbol{H}|^{2}} G(\boldsymbol{H})\right],
\end{aligned}\qquad(11)$$
where $\omega_{p}$ is the plasma frequency, $H$ is a reciprocal lattice vector and the function $G(q)$ is related to the bare electron-ion pseudopotential $V_{b}(q)$ and the dielectric function $\epsilon^{*}(q)$ ,
$$G(q)=\left(\frac{4 \pi z e^{2}}{\Omega_{0} q^{2}}\right)^{-2}\left|V_{\mathrm{b}}(q)\right|^{2} \frac{\epsilon^{*}(q)-1}{\epsilon^{*}(q)}.\qquad(12)$$

Phonon dispersion relations were calculated for three principal symmetry directions $[00 \zeta],[0 \zeta \zeta]$ and[ζζζ] from eqs. (8) to (12), and are shown in fig. 3 together with the experimental points of Lynn et al.[15]. The results obtained show a very good agreement for the longitudinal branches in all directions,but along the transverse branches, except the $T_{2}$  branch in the $[0 \zeta \zeta]$ direction, in all directions the agreement is poor. It may be pointed out here that since we have neither derived any of the parameters from the neutron data nor varied them for fitting the experimental phonon frequencies, our calculated results compare well to those of Moriarty [2], using Harrison's generalized pseudopotential formulation for d-band metals. To conclude we would like to mention that the proposed simple model potential is expected to explain satisfactorily various atomic properties for noble metals. Further study along these lines is in progress and will be reported in due course.
