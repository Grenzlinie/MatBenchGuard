**Linear dependence of the interaction energy on intramolecular distance for adsorbed or clustered diatomic molecules**

A. V. LARIN¹², L. LEHERTE¹, D. P. VERCAUTEREN¹* and D. N. TRUBNIKOV²

¹ Institute for Studies in Interface Sciences, Laboratoire de Physico-Chimie Informatique, Facultés Universitaires Notre Dame de la Paix, Rue de Bruxelles 61 B-5000 Namur, Belgium
² Laboratory of Molecular Beams, Department of Chemistry, Moscow State University, Vorob'evu Gory, Moscow, B-234, 119899, Russia

(Received 8 November 1999; revised version accepted 12 April 2000)

The dependence of several molecular properties (polarizabilities, multipole moments) of adsorbed diatomic molecules on the intramolecular distance are discussed within time-independent perturbation theory. The nearly linear dependence of the intermolecular interaction energy and molecular properties rationalizes the small variation of the property functions compared with those for the gas state. This relative independence of the property functions was demonstrated numerically in a previous paper using an iterative procedure for molecular hydrogen adsorbed in a zeolite-A-type framework.

### 1. Introduction

Precise modelling of the behaviour of adsorbed (and clustered) molecules can be performed via the calculation of the energy of interaction between the molecule and the adsorbent (or with other molecules, in the cluster case) if the properties of both partners in the adsorbed (or cluster) state are known. As expected, these properties are usually affected reciprocally by the interaction, and the changes are more pronounced for the adsorbate than for the adsorbent. For simplicity, properties such as parallel and perpendicular polarizabilities, multipole moments, and the intramolecular distances related to the different vibrational states of the adsorbate are often supposed to be identical to those in the gas state. But clearly, this coincidence of property functions in both states needs to be tested.

The interpretation of the infrared spectra of adsorbed diatomic molecules requires an estimation of their molecular properties as functions of their intramolecular distance (see for example, [1], references 2–11). Such functions can be used for the calculation of the intermolecular interaction energy (IE), which itself can be considered as a vibrational perturbation. Initial functions for either the gas state or adsorbed state could indeed provide, through the solution of the vibrational problem, average values of the adsorbate properties and intramolecular distances in separate vibrational states. These 'perturbed' properties will affect the IE and other observables, for example, the infrared intensities by amounts proportional to the molecular polarizability of the diatomic molecule. IEs calculated using the perturbed property values will again affect the average molecular properties and distances. Thus, the interpretation of spectroscopic data (i.e. calculation of intensities and band shifts) can be considered as an iterative procedure. To the best of our knowledge, such a procedure has not been implemented so far, even for diatomic molecules, apart from the treatment in [1], where the convergence of the procedure was demonstrated for adsorbed hydrogen. If vibronic bands have already been recorded for transitions between states for clustered polyatomic molecules like benzene [2], then clearly a closer theoretical analysis of the dependence of the molecular properties on the normal vibrational coordinates of polatomic molecules is required to interpret the measured band shifts. Before this is attempted, a discussion of a simpler case like molecular hydrogen is surely helpful.

In a previous paper [1], we used an iterative numerical procedure in order to estimate the variation of some properties such as the polarizabilities, multipole moments, and the intramolecular distance on the basis of perturbation theory for the vibrational component of the total wavefunction upon adsorption. First, we formulate the question in terms of stationary perturbation theory. Then in section 2.1 we compare the derivatives (with respect to intramolecular distance) of the properties in the gas and adsorbed states, and in section 2.2 the relation between the free parameter in the linear dependences of the properties in both states is obtained. The similarity between the values related to the gas phase and adsorbed state is stressed in both sections 2.1 and 2.2.

## 2. Theoretical aspects and results

The perturbed or adsorbed vibrational state functions (denoted a) can be expressed via first-order wavefunctions of the gas state (denoted g) [4]:

$$
\left|v^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle=\left|v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle+\sum_{v^{\prime}} \frac{\left\langle v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}|U(R)| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}{E_{v}^{0}-E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\left|v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\right\rangle, \quad(1)
$$

wherein $E_{v}^{0}$ are the energy levels in zero-order (gas) state and $U(R)$ is the perturbation potential corresponding to the interaction energy (IE) between the adsorbed molecule and the adsorbent, $R$ being the intramolecular distance of the molecule.

The ratio between the IE and the vibrational quanta of a probe diatomic molecule may be used as an approximate criterion to justify the application of perturbation theory, i.e. of expression (1). The perturbation potential could then be modified via the approach proposed by Buckingham [9]. In such a case, the IE of the diatomic molecule at its equilibrium geometry can be introduced into a zero-approximation Hamiltonian. This modification corresponds to a uniform displacement of the electronic potential curve in the gas state along the energy axis by a constant value, i.e. the IE with the adsorbent estimated at the equilibrium intramolecular distance $R_{\text{e}}$ of the diatomic molecule.

If the IE between the probe and the adsorbent can be expressed satisfactorily through a linear approximation, such as $U(R)=K_{0}+K_{1}\Delta R$, with $\Delta R=R-R_{\text{e}}$, then the contributions to the $|v^{\text{a}}\rangle$ function come from $|(v+1)^{\text{g}}\rangle$ and $|(v-1)^{\text{g}}\rangle$ only. We have already discussed such an approximation for $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ interacting with the NaA zeolite [3]. The potential used here differs from that obtained from the numerical approximation of the calculated IE with $R$ [10]. The $K_{1}$ value used here corresponds to a slightly modified zeolite model with the zeolite ionicity value $q_{0}=q(\text{Na})+q(\text{Al})+q(\text{Si})=4|q(\text{O})|$ [10] (see [3], figure 4), the ionicity being the total charge of the cationic or anionic subpart for a NaA zeolite model possessing the unit formula $\text{NaAlSiO}_{4}$. Owing to the charge dependence of the ionic radius and polarizabilities used in [3] and [10], the ionicity is thus a very important characteristic of the zeolite system considered. $K_{1}=-6.1\times 10^{-3}\ E_{\text{h}}\,a_{0}^{-1}$, used herein (instead of $-4.4\times 10^{-3}\ E_{\text{h}}\,a_{0}^{-1}$ [3]), is related to $q_{0}=3.7e$ [10] instead of $5.7e$ [3].

The linear $R$ dependence of a molecular property $X$ in the gas and adsorbed states will coincide if and only if the two parameters $a$ and $b$ of the linear approximation $X(R)=a+b\Delta R$ in the gas and adsorbed states coincide. Thus let us first show that the change in the derivative ($b$ value) upon adsorption is very small in the case of molecular hydrogen.

### Linear dependence of interaction energy

#### 2.1. Closeness of the property derivatives

The derivatives of a property $X(R)$ can be expressed via finite differences related to the vibrational states. The condition for equivalence of the first derivative values in adsorbed (LHS) and gas phase (RHS) molecule is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{a}}|X|(v+1)^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle-\left\langle v^{\mathrm{a}}|X| v^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle}{\left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{a}}|R|(v+1)^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle-\left\langle v^{\mathrm{a}}|R| v^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle} \\
=\frac{\left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}|X|(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle-\left\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}{\left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}|R|(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle-\left\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|R| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}. \quad(2)
\end{aligned}
$$

The differences in the LHS can be expressed using perturbation equation (1), i.e. the change in the property value $X$ in a given vibrational state $v$ upon adsorption may be defined as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\langle v^{\mathrm{a}}|X| v^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle-\left\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle= & \sum_{v^{\prime}=v-1}^{v+1},\left(\frac{K_{1} R_{\mathrm{e}}\left\langle v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}{E_{v}^{0}-E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\right)^{2} \\
& \times\left\langle v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\right\rangle \\
& +\sum_{v^{\prime}=v-1}^{v+1}, \frac{K_{1} R_{\mathrm{e}}\left\langle v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}{E_{v}^{0}-E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\left\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\right\rangle, \\
& (3)
\end{aligned}
$$

with $\xi=(R-R_{\mathrm{e}})/R_{\mathrm{e}}$. The terms in the first sum on the RHS of equation (3) may be neglected compared with those in the second sum. To test this assertion, we use the linear gas state dependence $X(R)=a+b \Delta R$. The requirement for small values of the respective terms is then

$$
\left|\frac{\left\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}{E_{v}^{0}-E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\right| \ll\left|\frac{2 b}{K_{1}}\right|, \quad(4)
$$

which is justified for all hydrogen properties at low vibrational states. The derivatives can be estimated on the basis of the values given in the RHS of equation (2) and table 2. These estimates range from $1$ au to 7–8 au for all hydrogen properties considered. They are of nearly the same order of magnitude as $\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\mathrm{g}}\rangle$, while relation (4) is justified by the large vibrational quanta for the fundamental transition $E_{1}^{0}-E_{0}^{0} \approx h c \omega_{\mathrm{e}}=0.02 E_{\mathrm{h}}$ and $K_{1}=-6.1 \times 10^{-3} E_{\mathrm{h}}$ $a_{0}^{-1}$. As a result, we can express the differences in the LHS of equation (2) using only the second sum on the RHS of equation (3).

Combining the variation in the property upon adsorption (equation (3)) for both states of the vibrational transition $v+1 \leftarrow v$, the variation in the property with the transition in the adsorbed state is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{a}}|X|(v+1)^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle & -\left\langle v^{\mathrm{a}}|X| v^{\mathrm{a}}\right\rangle \\
= & \left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}|X|(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle-\left\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle \\
& +\sum_{v^{\prime}}, \frac{K_{1} R_{\mathrm{e}}\left\langle v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}|\xi|(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}{E_{v+1}^{0}-E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\right\rangle \\
& -\sum_{v^{\prime}}, \frac{K_{1} R_{\mathrm{e}}\left\langle v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle}{E_{v}^{0}-E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\left\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last two sums (denoted below as $S$) on the RHS of equation (5) correspond to the difference between the numerators of the left- and right-hand sides of equation (2) and the difference between the respective denominators when $X=R$, i.e. $a=R_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $b=1$. After introducing the known linear gas state dependence of $X(R)$ and expressing the denominators for an anharmonic potential, the sum $S$ becomes (in au):

$$
\begin{gathered}
S=\frac{b K_{1} R_{\mathrm{e}}^{2}}{2 \pi c}\left(\frac{2\left\langle(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle^{2}}{\omega_{\mathrm{e}}-2(v+1) \omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}}-\frac{\left\langle(v-1)^{\mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle^{2}}{\omega_{\mathrm{e}}-2 v \omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}}\right. \\
\left.-\frac{\left\langle(v+2)^{\mathrm{g}}|\xi|(v+1)^{\mathrm{g}}\right\rangle^{2}}{\omega_{\mathrm{e}}-2(v+2) \omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}}\right),
\end{gathered}
$$

where $\omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}$ is the anharmonicity coefficient of $\mathrm{H}_{2}$. If we neglect the terms proportional to $\omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}$ in the denominators of equation (6) and estimate the matrix elements $\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\rangle$ via harmonic oscillator functions [15], $S$ expressed via equation (6) vanishes for any vibrational state (harmonic approximation).

A closer estimate may be obtained using a ‘semi-harmonic’ approximation while considering the terms proportional to $\omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}$ and estimating $\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\rangle$ via harmonic oscillator functions (the latter can be justified for the lowest vibrational states):

$$
\begin{gathered}
S=\frac{b K_{1} R_{\mathrm{e}}^{2}}{4 \pi^{2} c^{2} \omega_{\mathrm{e}}}\left(\frac{2(v+1)}{\omega_{\mathrm{e}}-2(v+1) \omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}}-\frac{v}{\omega_{\mathrm{e}}-2 v \omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}}\right. \\
\left.-\frac{v+2}{\omega_{\mathrm{e}}-2(v+2) \omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}}\right).
\end{gathered}
$$

It is easy to show that the expression between parentheses has a value of the order of $-4 \omega_{\mathrm{e}} x_{\mathrm{e}}/\omega_{\mathrm{e}}^{2}$ for all vibrational states (although the denominator decreases steeply with $v$). The precision of $S$ using equation (7) is higher for the lower vibrational states when the harmonic approximation on the matrix elements $\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|\xi| v^{\prime \mathrm{g}}\rangle$ is less crude; it then provides a rather small value of $S$ within equation (5). In equation (2) the denominator coincides with the numerator when $X=R$, i.e. $a=R_{\mathrm{e}}$ and $b=1$. However, $b$ ranges between $1$ au and 7–8 au, as can be shown using the RHS of expression (2) and the $\langle v^{\mathrm{g}}|X| v^{\mathrm{g}}\rangle$ values from table 2. ### 2.2. Coincidence of the free parameter $a$ of the property dependence in the adsorbed and gas states

In the next step, we verify that the parameter $a$ of the linear approximation to the molecular property $X{(R)} = {a + {b\Delta R}}$ is the same in the gas and adsorbed states. Given the result of the previous section, the approximate form of $X^{\text{a}}{(R)}$ within the adsorbed state is ${a^{\prime} + {b\Delta R}}$, where $a^{\prime}$ may differ from $a$. Then the LHS of equation (3) may be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\langle v^{\text{a}}|{a^{\prime} + {b\Delta R}}|v^{\text{a}}\rangle} &- {\langle v^{\text{g}}|{a + {b\Delta R}}|v^{\text{g}}\rangle} \\
&= {a^{\prime} - a} + {b\left({\langle v^{\text{a}}|R|v^{\text{a}}\rangle} - {\langle v^{\text{g}}|R|v^{\text{g}}\rangle}\right)}, \quad (8)
\end{aligned}
$$

while the RHS of equation (3) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{v^{\prime} = {v - 1}}^{v + 1}\prime\left(\frac{K_{1}R_{\text{e}}{\langle v^{\prime\text{g}}|\xi|v^{\text{g}}\rangle}}{E_{v}^{0} - E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\right)^{2} &(a + b{\langle v^{\prime\text{g}}|R|v^{\prime\text{g}}\rangle}) \\
&+ \sum_{v^{\prime} = {v - 1}}^{v + 1}\prime\frac{bK_{1}R_{\text{e}}^{2}{\langle v^{\prime\text{g}}|\xi|v^{\text{g}}\rangle}^{2}}{E_{v}^{0} - E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}. \quad (9)
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing the term proportional to $b$ in equations (9) and (3) obtained for the case $X = R$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\langle v^{\text{a}}|R|v^{\text{a}}\rangle} &- {\langle v^{\text{g}}|R|v^{\text{g}}\rangle} \\
&= \sum_{v^{\prime} = {v - 1}}^{v + 1}\prime\left(\frac{K_{1}R_{\text{e}}{\langle v^{\prime\text{g}}|\xi|v^{\text{g}}\rangle}}{E_{v}^{0} - E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}\right)^{2}{\langle v^{\prime\text{g}}|R|v^{\prime\text{g}}\rangle} \\
&+ \sum_{v^{\prime} = {v - 1}}^{v + 1}\prime\frac{K_{1}R_{\text{e}}^{2}{\langle v^{\prime\text{g}}|\xi|v^{\text{g}}\rangle}^{2}}{E_{v}^{0} - E_{v^{\prime}}^{0}}, \quad (10)
\end{aligned}
$$

we find that the difference $(a^{\prime} - a)$ corresponds to the terms of the first sum in equaiton (10):

$$

## 3. Discussion

The intramolecular $R$ dependence of molecular properties, such as polarizabilities and multipole moments, of adsorbed (or clustered) molecules is important for the calculation of the intermolecular interaction energy (IE) between the adsorbed diatomic molecule and the adsorbent, the latter often being required as a function of $R$. In particular, knowledge of the dependence of the IE on $R$ allows the simulation of the vibrational spectra of probe molecules in the adsorbed state. The similarity of molecular property functions in the gas and adsorbed states simplifies the problem. To explain this similarity, we have estimated the shift on adsorption of the two parameters $a$ and $b$ of the functions represented as linear approximations, i.e. $X{(R)} = {a + {b\Delta R}}$.

The main reason for the small variation in the parameters comes from the ‘linear’ perturbation with respect to the intramolecular distance. This linearity is a consequence of the combination of the different IE components. They are proportional to various molecu-

Linear dependence of interaction energy

lar properties, which individually behave like nearly linear functions. The linear character of the molecular properties of molecular hydrogen were shown in [3], figure 2. Properties usually present no maximum or minimum (the derivative with respect to $R$ has a constant sign) in the range of average intramolecular distances in the lower vibrational states, $v=0$-$6$. Only the hexadecapole moment differs from the other properties in that it presents a slightly parabolic character [1]. However, its contribution to the total IE is too small to change the total linear IE behaviour with $R$. The IE considered as a perturbation $U$ in equation (1) generates (for any given vibrational state $v$) a shift of the properties $\langle v^{g}|X|v^{g}\rangle\rightarrow\langle v^{a}|X|v^{a}\rangle$ which is proportional to the shift of the intramolecular distance $\langle v^{g}|R|v^{g}\rangle\rightarrow\langle v^{a}|R|v^{a}\rangle$. As shown in [1], the proportionality between the shift of the property $X$ and distance holds within a sufficiently wide range of the intramolecular distance of adsorbed hydrogen, i.e. up to average distances corresponding to the vibrational states $v=4$-$6$. The linear behaviour of the IE seems moreover to be valid for other adsorbed molecules like nitrogen and oxygen. It is a consequence of the lower vibrational amplitude for the heavier molecules compared with hydrogen, due to a higher mass of the nuclei. These molecules have a narrower area of nonzero vibrational probability distribution over which the $R$ dependence of the molecular properties is important for band shift (BS) calculations [3]. As a result, linear approximations for both the molecular properties and the IE components may be more accurate for heavier molecules than for molecular hydrogen.

In the case of clustered molecules, the average IE is significantly lower than in the adsorbed state case. Then expression (1), in terms of perturbation theory, may be applied directly, as for adsorbed hydrogen without a modification [9] of the zero-approximation Hamiltonian as discussed above. We found that the linear approximation is sufficiently accurate enough for the total IE of $\mathrm{N}_{2}$, $\mathrm{Br}_{2}$, and $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ interacting with benzene [16]. For clustered molecules, the IE corresponds to the sum of the same electrostatic, dispersive, and repulsive IE components (apart from a small inductive component) expressed through the multipole moments and polarizabilities. These electric properties for $\mathrm{Br}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ also present a nearly linear behaviour with respect to the intramolecular distance [16].

Other forms of variation of a property with $R$ could be considered as for example in the strongly nonlinear hyperpolarizability of $\mathrm{CO}$ in the gas state [17]. Nonlinear behaviour could cause a shifted dependence of the property [1] so that the BS values could change significantly on inclusion of the perturbed quantities. This is one reason why it is important to confirm the convergence of the iterative procedure derived in [1]. If convergence were obtained also for another property function, then a more accurate evaluation of the adsorption (or clustering) effects could be achieved readily with the simple iterative procedure proposed in [1]. The latter allows computation of IE values using the $R$ dependence of the adsorbate properties, self-consistently corrected, taking into account the particular IE of this system. The procedure is valid also for a wide series of calculations of the IE, not only for the BS calculation, because the property dependence perturbed upon adsorption leads to another property value in the ground state too. Hence, the behaviour of the molecular property dependence of a diatomic molecule versus its intramolecular distance for the IE calculation could be studied for a wider series of related systems.

The absolute values of the potential $U(R)$ [10] used in [1] and its behaviour with $R$, i.e. $K_{0}$ and $K_{1}$ values (see section 2), can be verified by comparison with available $ab$ initio estimates at the MP2 level. This approach was, for example, applied to the $\mathrm{X}_{2}/\mathrm{Me}^{+}$ system, $\mathrm{X}_{2}=\mathrm{H}_{2}$, $\mathrm{N}_{2}$ [18, 19], or $\mathrm{D}_{2}$ [19], $\mathrm{Me}=\mathrm{Li}$ [19] or $\mathrm{Na}$ [18, 19], in order to interpret the experimental values of the frequency shift [18, 19] and IR intensity [19] for $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ adsorbed in the NaA zeolite. The resulting IE values obtained at the MP2/6-311** level [18] and with more advanced basis sets for all relevant atoms [19], $-4.594\times10^{-3}E_{\mathrm{h}}$ and $-5.307\times10^{-3}E_{\mathrm{h}}$, respectively, are in agreement with our empirical values, $-4.829\times10^{-3}E_{\mathrm{h}}$ and $-5.712\times10^{-3}E_{\mathrm{h}}$, for the zeolite ionicity $3.9\ e$ and different occupations of the degenerate $\mathrm{Na}_{III}^{+}$ positions in the neighbour cells ([10], table 2). The respective $ab$ initio band shifts, $-67\ \mathrm{cm}$ [18] and $-70.7\ \mathrm{cm}$ [19], respectively, also confirm the $K_{1}$ value of the $U(R)$ potential (fitted to $-79.2\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ [10] assigned to the para-$\mathrm{H}_{2}$ [20]). However, the interpretation with the simple $\mathrm{H}_{2}/\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ model does not correspond to the preferred position of a diatomic molecule adsorbed near two $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ cations as in NaA [3, 10] ([1], figure 1). This location has been confirmed by Anderson $et\ al.$ [21] using an empirical method with a different parametrization. The resulting strong electrostatic field leads to out-and in-plane librational motions of the molecular axis in agreement with the absence of a rotational structure of the fundamental vibrational transition band, as shown experimentally for $\mathrm{H}_{2}/\mathrm{NaA}$ [20], while both rotational P and R branches were observed for the respective fundamental transition band for $\mathrm{H}_{2}/\mathrm{NaCaA}$ [20], for which the favoured $\mathrm{H}_{2}/\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$ T shape geometry does not cause any problem. The large rotational barrier for $\mathrm{H}_{2}/\mathrm{NaA}$ allows us to avoid the development of a sophisticated anisotropic rovibrational Hamiltonian as fitted by Le Roy and Hutson [22] for the $\mathrm{H}_{2}/\mathrm{Ar}$ case. For this last system, numerous data, including IR spectra band posi-

tions, hyperfine transition frequencies, mixed virial coef- ficients and averaged anisotropy strength functions, indeed allowed resolution of the problem of the rela- tively flat 'van der Waals' surface compared with the essentially 'electrostatic' potential for the $H_{2} / NaA$ system. Our simplified $U(R)$ for the perturbation is jus tified because of the sharper potential variation with respect to the coordinates of the centre of mass and of the molecular axis. If only the local $H_{2}$ behaviour is discussed, then the contributions to the total IE from the coupling terms (between axis rotation, vibration of the centre of mass with respect to zeolite, and intra- molecular coordinates) can be omitted.

The small variations in table 2 are due partly to the presence of the electric field within the zeolite frame- work but they are of 'vibrational' origin, i.e. molecular distortion in the zeolite. A convenient way to express it could be shown for the case where the IE terms propor- tional to the field value strongly dominate in the total IE. Such an approach was developed by Bishop [23] for CO using a perturbation including only the terms pro-portional to the field vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(R)= & -\mu_{i}(R) F_{i}-(1 / 2) \alpha_{i j}(R) F_{i} F_{j}-(1 / 3) \Theta_{i j}(R) F_{i j} \\
& -(1 / 6) \beta_{i j k}(R) F_{i} F_{j} F_{k}-(1 / 3) A_{i, j k}(R) F_{i} F_{j k} \\
& -(1 / 24) \gamma_{i j k l}(R) F_{i} F_{j} F_{k} F_{1} \ldots
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$

where $\mu_{i}, \alpha_{i j}, \Theta_{i j}, \beta_{i j k}, A_{i, j k}$, and $\gamma_{i j k l}$ are the tensor components of the molecular dipole, polarizability, quadrupole, first hyperpolarizability, dipole-quadrupole polarizability, and second hyperpolarizability, respect- ively, $F_{i}$ is component $i$ of the electrostatic field vector, and $F_{i j}$ the derivative of component $i$ with respect to the j direction. Within this perturbation scheme (equation(12)), the variations in the properties as presented by formula (3) could be discussed only in terms of thefield influencing the molecule. Bishop's approach [23] can be applied provided that the molecular location with respect to the field can be determined from an independent source as, for example, an empirical scheme. But it has been shown, within the scope of an accurate empirical scheme [24], that all the terms (polar- ization, dispersive, repulsive) are important for the IE calculation for $CO$ adsorbed on $NaCl$ . The same con clusion has been obtained recently with an empirical pair-wise addition approach for $CO$ adsorbed inside NaA, NaY, and NaRbY zeolites [25], including the con- sideration of a wide range of estimates of the dispersive constants. Thus, the contributions of all terms should be included also in the total perturbation; terms in the IE are in a similar ratio for the $H_{2}$ molecule $[3,10]$ . This is why we have concentrated here on the general presenta- tion of the potential $U(R)$ as a function of the intra molecular distance of the probe. The property variation due to the imposed electrostatic field then includes part of the differences between the values shown in the 'gas" and 'adsorbed' situations of table2, respectively, together with other contributions from dispersive and repulsive interactions.

Only the second and third field-dependent polariza- tion and electrostatic terms (equation (12)) were included in the total interaction in our previous work[1] (first and fourth terms are equal to zero for $H_{2}$ ). Neglect of the last term in equation (12) can be justified by the small static value $\gamma_{0}=682.7 e^{4} a_{0}^{4} E_{h}^{-3}$ [26]. While a recent experimental estimation of the electric field for NaY zeolite was around $10^{-2} E_{h} e^{-1} a_{0}^{-1}$ [27], the resulting IE contribution using equation (12) is around $-2.8 ×10^{-7} E_{h}$ . This can be considered as small com pared with the total IE values $-4.829 ×10^{-3} E_{h}$ and $-5.712 ×10^{-3} E_{h}$ for the zeolite ionicity value $3.9 e$  and different occupations of the degenerate $Na_{III}^{+}$ posi tions in the neighbour cells ([10], table 2), and hence can be omitted. The respective contributions to the polariz- ability and quadrupole components due to the electro- static field were not estimated in [1], while corrections for the polarizability from the terms proportional to the hyperpolarizabilities could be more important than the small correction $(-2.8 ×10^{-7} E_{h})$ from the same terms in the IE. In order to evaluate these field-dependent variations of the polarizability and quadrupole compon- ents one should add terms proportional to the second hyperpolarizability $((1 / 12) \gamma_{i j k l}(R) F_{k} F_{1})$ and dipole quadrupole polarizability $(A_{k, i j}(R) F_{k})$ values to the initial values, $\alpha_{i j}$ and $\Theta_{i j}$ , respectively.

Having no information about the values of $A_{i, j k}(R)$ ,we estimated small polarizability changes $\Delta \alpha^{0}=$ (1/12)γ₀F², proportional to the field, as $0.0057 e^{2} a_{0}^{2} E_{h}^{-1}$ . The latter value may seem small but is essential for both the parallel and perpendicular components of $\Delta \alpha^{0}$ ,which increase by $0.079 e^{2} a_{0}^{2} E_{h}^{-1}$ and $0.037 e^{2} a_{0}^{2} E_{h}^{-1}$  from the gas to the adsorbed state at the ground vibra- tional level without this $\Delta \alpha^{0}$ value. Usually these vari ations are larger for the upper levels (table 2). In general,these additions should be slightly different for the $\alpha_{\|}$  and $\alpha_{\perp}$ polarizabilities, because different field compon ents $F_{i}$ should be applied. This component is larger for a because the molecule experiences a larger field value(see [1], figure 1) in the perpendicular direction. This $\Delta \alpha^{0}$ addition depends on the variable $R$ that could lead to a change of the slope of the polarizability depen- dence with $R$ . Thus the behaviour of $\Delta \alpha^{0}$ with $R$ has to be discussed.

## 4. Conclusion
The simulation of the vibrational spectra of probe molecules in a cluster or in an adsorbed state requires the calculation of the intermolecular interaction energy as a function of the probe intramolecular distance. The interaction energy, in its turn, requires the calculation of the intramolecular distance dependence of the properties of the adsorbed or clustered molecule. In this contribu- tion we have shown that, for molecular hydrogen, a satisfactory linear approximation of any gas state prop- erty with the intramolecular distance is affected only slightly when the molecule is subject to an adsorption potential. The latter satisfies the case of a nearly linear behaviour of the interaction energy with the intramol- ecular distance [3]. The main features of such behaviour are that the molecular property dependence on intra- molecular distance seem to be valid for other diatomic molecules. Further studies are required to verify whether the convergence of the step-by-step variation of the properties found in [1] is maintained when the inter- action energy and the properties are nonlinear in the intramolecular distance. As a possible source for shifts in the respective property dependence on the intramol-ecular distance, the field-dependent parts of the $H_{2}$  polarizabilities were estimated and found to be small.
