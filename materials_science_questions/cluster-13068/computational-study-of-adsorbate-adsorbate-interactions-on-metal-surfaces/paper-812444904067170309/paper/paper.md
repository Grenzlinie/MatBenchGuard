# Indirect interactions between adsorbates in contact with quasi-one-dimensional surface electronic chain states

B. Gumhalter $^{1}$, W. Brenig $^{*}$

Physik-Department, TU München, D-85747 Garching, Germany

Received 8 November 1994; accepted for publication 22 March 1995

## Abstract

We consider the screening properties of quasi-one-dimensional electronic states which may occur on low index surfaces (flat or stepped) of some fcc metals as localized chain states. Motivated by the analysis of experimental data on the H/Ni(110) system, we discuss the possible contributions to indirect interactions between two H adatoms which are mediated by such states. We first show in the linear model of screening that these interactions should exhibit long range oscillations which scale as $d^{-1}$ for large distances $d$ between the adatoms. We use experimental data for the indirect interactions in H/Ni(110) systems to obtain an order of magnitude estimate of parameters involved. It turns out that a relatively large phase shift $\eta_{a}$ is required for such oscillations. We therefore also use a nonlinear model for the screening of adatoms, based on the Anderson Hamiltonian. This model yields an indirect interaction between two adatoms which is regular for all $d$ and behaves asymptotically as $\sim \cos[2k_{\text{F}}d - 2\eta_{a}(\epsilon_{\text{F}})]/d$ where $k_{\text{F}}$ is the Fermi wavevector at the metal surface. The physical significance of the parameters of the model derived thereof is discussed in the context of incomplete screening of H adatoms by quasi-one-dimensional electronic chain states existing on the substrate surface.

## 1. Introduction

Since the very beginning of the development of theoretical models of chemisorption on metals at- tempts have been made to use these not only in the description of the electronic properties of chemisorp- tion systems in the single adsorbate limit (applicable to low coverages), but also in the studies of adsor- bate-adsorbate interactions which are important for understanding the properties of adlayers. Several types of interactions which are sufficiently long ranged may take place between adsorbates and thereby influence the structure of adsorbed layers. Such are, for instance, direct interactions arising from the repulsion of static dipoles induced in ad- sorption complexes, attractive van der Waals or dis- persive interactions among adatoms, etc., but in the context of chemisorption theory special attention has been paid to indirect interactions between adsorbates which are mediated by the valence electrons of the underlying substrate.

General features of indirect interactions between two atoms in contact with electron gas have been known from the studies of magnetic impurities in

metals (the so-called RKKY interactions [1]) where they exhibit oscillatory asymptotic behaviour of the type $\alpha \cos(2k_{\text{F}}d/d^{3})$ where $k_{\text{F}}$ is the Fermi wavevector of the metal and $d$ is the distance between the impurities. The existence of similar interactions between adsorbates which could significantly affect the chemisorption processes in overlayers has been anticipated rather early [2] and, hence, their studies proved to be of considerable interest.

The first model study of chemisorption induced indirect interactions between two adatoms was carried out by Grimley [3] within the Anderson model [4] adopted for this problem. This study revealed an oscillatory behaviour of the interaction, falling off asymptotically as $\alpha \cos(2k_{\text{F}}d)/d^{2}$. Although the power law dependence was later corrected to $1/d^{5}$, this work aroused considerable interest in such a type of interactions. It was soon followed by the work of Einstein and Schrieffer [5] who investigated indirect interactions between atoms chemisorbed on a tight-binding solid. The behaviour they found was also oscillatory but with an exponentially falling off amplitude in the range of distances they considered (preasymptotic behaviour). Schönhammer et al. studied within the nonorthogonal Anderson model the effects of correlation on indirect interactions between two hydrogen adatoms on a simple cubic solid and found that a $(2 \times 2)$ structure would be energetically preferable [6]. Later studies of indirect interactions between two impurity adatoms on a metal surface carried out by Flores et al. [7] and Lau and Kohn [8] within the linear response established the asymptotic behaviour $\alpha \cos(2k_{\text{F}}d)/d^{5}$ and thus corrected Grimley's result [3]. Lau and Kohn [8] (hereafter to be referred to as LK) also studied indirect interactions between two impurities embedded into a truly two-dimensional (2D) Fermi gas and found the asymptotic behaviour of the form $\alpha \cos(2k_{\text{F}}d)/d^{2}$ which emphasized the enhancement of the long range character of the indirect interactions upon the reduction of the dimensionality of the electronic states mediating the interaction. The works of Joyce et al. [9] and March [13] demonstrated that the nonlinear character of screening of adsorbates (in all these works described by the Anderson model of chemisorption) gives rise to a phase shift $\eta_{a}$ in the interaction which then acquires a form $\alpha \cos(2k_{\text{F}}d - 2\eta_{a})/d^{5}$ for large $d$.

Thus, all the continuum models for indirect interactions $W_{\text{ab}}(d)$ between two impurity atoms/adsorbates a and b, at distance $d$ from each other, predict an asymptotic behaviour of a general form:

$$
\lim _{d \rightarrow \infty} W_{\mathrm{ab}}(d)=W_{\mathrm{ab}}^{0} \frac{\cos \left(2 k_{\mathrm{F}} d-2 \eta_{a}\right)}{d^{n}}, \tag{1}
$$

where $n$ is an integer, depending on the (effective) dimensionality of the metal band states which mediate the interaction, and $\eta_{a}$ appears only in nonlinear theories. Using this form of the interaction with $n=5$ it was possible to explain some peculiar properties of adsorbed layers and to fit the experimental data for the adsorption energy of CO on transition metal surfaces with a remarkable accuracy [9].

The power law $1/d^{n}$ in Eq. (1) can be looked upon from a somewhat different point of view [10-12]. Namely, if the Fermi surface of the metal electrons is strongly anisotropic, then its flat (planar) parts will give rise to the $1/d$ and the cylindrical ones to the $1/d^{2}$ behavior of the indirect interaction (1), respectively, in the direction perpendicular to these sections of the Fermi surface. This indicates what role the effective dimension of the electronic states may play which participate in the indirect interactions: Along quasi-1D chains one will expect a $1/d$ contribution, perpendicular to them a $1/d^{2}$ behaviour and from the embedding electron gas a (standard) fall off $\alpha \ 1/d^{5}$ [8].

Note that, strictly speaking, the assumption of a one-dimensional Fermi surface is only an approximation for a strongly anisotropic three-dimensional one and that the 3D nature will dominate the chemistry and the asymptotic behaviour of the indirect interaction at the surface at very large distance.

One should also keep in mind that we neglect the effect of Coulomb correlations and their possible contributions to interchain couplings.

The discussion of the effective dimensionality of the surface electronic states of some metallic substrates has recently been revived in the studies of hydrogen adsorption on Ni(110) [14,15] and Cu(110) [16] surfaces. On both substrates the adsorption of hydrogen induces missing/added row re-construction of the surface within a certain temperature range. Specifically, on the Ni(110) surface the H atoms are found to be sitting in the troughs along the $[\overline{1}10]$ direction of the reconstructed surface and forming an

ordered $(2 \times 1)$ superstructure at the substrate temperature below 200 K [17-19]. EELS investigations of the vibrational spectra of adsorbed H atoms indicated strong anisotropy in coupling parameters between the first few neighbouring adatoms [14] responsible for the dispersion of optical phonons. The concomitant theoretical interpretation of the EELS data pointed towards the existence of an oscillatory long range H-H interaction [20]. There are not enough data to decide whether the interaction potential is anisotropic. In fact a central potential could be chosen to fit the data. However an isotropic RKKY type interaction (1) with $n=5$ would require values of the matrix elements for the adatom interaction with the substrate too large to be physically justified on the grounds of dominantly neutral H chemisorption on Ni (see also discussion in Section 2). This has lead to the conjecture that the propagation of the substrate valence electrons on the reconstructed Ni(110) surface might be channelled or confined to the troughs along the $[\overline{1} 10]$ direction, thereby giving rise to quasi-one-dimensional (Q-1D) surface electronic states delocalized in the direction of the troughs but localized perpendicular to them. Similar conclusions have been arrived at regarding the lateral interactions among the adatoms in the H/Cu(110) adsorption system [16].

Further and more direct evidence as to the existence of the Q-1D electronic surface states in the H/Ni(110) system came from the inverse photoemission (IPES) studies. Bischler and Bertel [21] have reported on the existence of unoccupied electronic states on the hydrogen induced reconstructed Ni(110) surface whose characteristics was to propagate unattenuated along the troughs but whose wavevector, and hence propagation, was completely attenuated in the direction perpendicular to the rows.

Studies of the unoccupied electronic states at stepped Cu(100) surfaces also revealed quasi-one-dimensional character of electron propagation along the steps [22]. Moreover recent studies of the growth of Cu layers on Pd(110) surfaces clearly indicated the existence of 1D Cu chains which also may sustain quasi-1D electronic propagation [23].

An immediate consequence of the existence of such states below the Fermi level would be the possibility of mediation of a one-dimensional (1D) RKKY interaction between adsorbates, of the form given by Eq. (1), but with an as yet unknown exponent $n$. However, following the argument that the effective dimensionality of the electronic states determines $n$ ($n=3$ for a true 3D electron gas and $n=2$ for a 2D case), it may be anticipated that for the indirect adsorbate-adsorbate interaction mediated by the 1D electronic states one would have $n=1$. Therefore, in such a case of strongly reduced dimensionality of surface electronic states the indirect adsorbate-adsorbate interaction would be extremely long ranged.

Hence, it is of practical as well as of theoretical interest to investigate this case, bearing in mind various consequences such an interaction can give rise to. One has to keep in mind, of course, that there will be additional contributions to the adatom-substrate and adatom-adatom interaction due to electrons residing in the "'standard'' surface projected bulk electronic states. These would be responsible for the indirect interactions considered in Refs. [8,9].

In Section 2 we present a derivation of the indirect interaction between two structureless adsorbates (impurities) in a 1D electron gas using a simple linear model of screening. By comparing the obtained expression with the available data for the H/Ni system we shall point out its limitations, as well as the limitations of similar models leading to the power laws $d^{-5}$ and $d^{-2}$ in the asymptotic form of the interactions. This will necessitate the development of a nonlinear model of screening of adatoms and of their mutual interactions, which we carry out in Section 3. The last section summarizes our results and compares them with the predictions of other models and the experimental data.

### 2. Linear model of indirect interactions between impurities in one-dimensional electron gas

In order to illustrate basic features of indirect interactions between two impurity atoms embedded in a Q-1D electron gas, we shall start from the simplest model which can mimic such a situation. We assume 1D electronic states subjected to quantization length $L$ so that their wavefunctions are given by
$$
\langle z \mid k\rangle=\psi_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} k z}, \tag{2}
$$

with $k=2\pi n/L$, where $n$ is the quantum number for 1D motion in the $z$ direction. These states are orthonormalized, i.e.
$$\langle k | k' \rangle = \delta_{k,k'}. \tag{3}$$

The Fermi surface in this system shrinks to the points $\pm k_{\text{F}}$. Let us further assume two impurity potentials $V_{\text{a}}$ and $V_{\text{b}}$ which can scatter the states $|k\rangle$:
$$V_{\text{a}} = V(z-z_{\text{a}}), \quad V_{\text{b}} = V(z-z_{\text{b}}), \tag{4}$$
so that
$$\langle k' | V_{\alpha} | k \rangle = \frac{a_0}{L} \text{e}^{-\text{i}qz_{\alpha}} V_q, \quad \alpha = \text{a, b}, \tag{5}$$
where
$$V_q = \frac{1}{a_0} \int \text{d}z\ V(z)\text{e}^{-\text{i}qz}, \tag{6}$$
and $a_0$ is the Bohr radius. It should be noted that owing to the normalization chosen the matrix elements (6) retain in the $k$-space the dimension of energy.

The leading contribution to the change of the ground state energy of the system due to the mutual interaction of the impurities is obtained most directly from second-order perturbation theory [8] which applied to the present problem yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\text{ab}}(d) =& 2\left( \frac{a_0}{L} \right)^2 \left( \frac{L}{2\pi} \right)^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \text{d}q \int_{|k|<k_{\text{F}}}^{|k+q|>k_{\text{F}}} \text{d}k \\
& \times \text{e}^{\text{i}qd} \frac{|V_q|^2}{\epsilon_k - \epsilon_{k+q}} + \text{c.c.},
\end{aligned} \tag{7}
$$
where the factor 2 accounts for the spin,
$$d = z_{\text{a}} - z_{\text{b}} \tag{8}$$
and
$$\epsilon_k = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m}, \tag{9}$$
where $m$ is the electronic mass. Carrying out the $k$-integral in (7) leads to
$$W_{\text{ab}}(d) = -\frac{4ma_0^2}{(\pi\hbar)^2} \int_0^{\infty} \text{d}q\ |V_q|^2 \frac{\cos(qd)}{q} \ln\left| \frac{2k_{\text{F}}+q}{2k_{\text{F}}-q} \right| + \text{c.c.} \tag{10}$$

For the problem defined through Eqs. (2)-(9) this expression is exact. It clearly demonstrates that indirect interactions between two impurities will be determined by the logarithmic singularity at $q=2k_{\text{F}}$ arising from the discontinuity of the occupation of the $k$-states at the Fermi level.

In order to proceed, we shall simplify (10) by assuming screened matrix elements $V_q$, i.e. nonsingular for $q=0$ and slowly varying over the whole band width. This allows us to replace all $V_q$ by their value at the Fermi energy $V_{2k\text{F}}=V_0$ which can then be taken outside the integral on the right-hand side (RHS) of (10). Then, by differentiating $W_{\text{ab}}(d)$ with respect to $d$, integrating by parts over $q$ the remaining singular logarithmic factor and integrating again over $d$ in the interval $(d, \infty)$, we find
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\text{ab}}(d) =& \frac{8\pi ma_0^2}{(\pi\hbar)^2} |V_0|^2 \left[ \text{Si}(2k_{\text{F}}d) - \text{Si}(\infty) \right] \\
& + \text{O}\left( \frac{1}{d^2} \right),
\end{aligned} \tag{11}
$$
where $\text{Si}(x)$ is the sine integral function. For $2k_{\text{F}}d$ $>3$ we may make use of its asymptotic expansion $\text{Si}(x) \to (\pi/2\text{-cos }x/x)$ for $x \to \infty$, to get
$$\lim_{2k_{\text{F}} \gg 3} W_{\text{ab}}(d) = -\frac{4}{\pi} |V_0|^2 \frac{\cos(2k_{\text{F}}d)}{2k_{\text{F}}d}\ \text{Ry}^{-1}, \tag{12}$$
where $1\ \text{Ry} = \hbar^2/2ma_0^2 = 13.6\ \text{eV}$ is here a convenient unit of energy which we shall use throughout in the expressions for $W_{\text{ab}}$.

Now, we may try to adjust the two parameters $V_0$ and $k_{\text{F}}$ of the expression (12) to obtain the best fit of the H-H interaction potential as deduced in Ref. [20] from the EELS data [14]. The fit should reproduce in particular the oscillatory behaviour in the vicinity of the first and third nearest neighbour H adatoms. These overlap with the electrons of the *same* chain on the reconstructed Ni(110) surface whereas the second nearest neighbours belong to different chains. Note that the data of Ref. [20] give the interaction potential only up to an additive constant irrelevant for the properties of the oscillatory component of the full potential.

Having done this, we first find that in order to get

a correct position of the maxima and minima of the interaction (12), it turns out necessary to introduce a relatively large phase shift $\eta_{a}=0.462=0.294 \pi / 2$ into Eqs. (1) and (12). With this additional requirement taken into account we find

$$
V_{0}=0.754 \mathrm{eV}, \quad k_{\mathrm{F}}=0.55 a_{0}^{-1}, \quad(13)
$$

which are reasonable values in the view of dominantly neutral character of H chemisorption on Ni(110). This value of $V_{0}$ should be correlated with the values obtained by fitting the same data to the $d^{-5}$ and $d^{-2}$ power laws. As in the latter two cases the values obtained for $V_{0}$ exceed 10 eV, this illustrates on physical grounds the inadequacy of assertion of the existence of the interactions exhibiting the asymptotic decay following these power laws.

We shall briefly summarize the results of this section on noticing that a physically acceptable fit of the interaction (1) to the data obtained for the H/Ni(110) system can be achieved by assuming:
(i) Existence of Q-1D electronic states in (along) the troughs of the reconstructed Ni(110) surface which may mediate indirect interactions between adatoms, and
(ii) nonlinear character of screening of the impurity adatom potentials which manifests itself through the occurrence of the phase shift $\eta_{a}$ in the oscillations of the interaction energy.

The point (ii) also necessitates setting up a nonlinear model of screening of the impurity potentials in which $\eta_{a}$ would not appear merely as a phenomenological parameter but, instead, be deducible from theory. Such a model is discussed in the next section.

## 3. Nonlinear model for indirect interactions between two impurities in Q-1D electron gas

To assess the basic features of the mechanism of nonlinear screening of impurity atoms, which gives rise to indirect adatom-adatom interactions mediated by Q-1D electronic states, we shall first demonstrate how one can deduce the basic properties and parameters of the model in the single impurity limit. Again we restrict our discussion to the system of H/Ni(110). With the understanding of this simpler situation, we shall extend the model to the case of two impurity atoms.

### 3.1. Nonlinear screening of a hydrogen atom embedded in Q-1D electron gas

Here we start from the Anderson model to describe the interaction of the substrate Q-1D electronic states $|k\rangle$ with the adatom valence orbital state $|a\rangle$. The electrons are assumed to be confined to a tube of quantization length $L$ and radius $l$. Thus

$$
\langle\boldsymbol{r} \mid k\rangle=\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} k z} \varphi_{0}(\rho), \quad(14)
$$

where $z$ and $\rho$ are the electron coordinates for motion in the direction along the tube and perpendicular to its axis, respectively. $\varphi_{0}(\rho)$ is a localized wave-function corresponding to the lowest electronic state obtained as a solution of the Schrödinger equation in polar coordinates for the boundary condition $\varphi_{0}(\rho=l)=0$. Hence, for the lowest state

$$
\varphi_{0}(\rho)=\frac{J_{0}(\kappa \rho)}{N}, \quad(15)
$$

where $\kappa=x_{0}^{1} / l$ and $x_{0}^{1}$ is the first zero of the cylindrical Bessel function $J_{0}(x)$ of zeroth order. $N$ is the appropriate normalization yielding

$$
\int_{\rho=0}^{l} \mathrm{~d}^{2} \rho\left|\varphi_{0}(\rho)\right|^{2}=1 . \quad(16)
$$

The adatom orbital in question is the hydrogen 1s orbital whose wavefunction reads

$$
\langle\boldsymbol{r} \mid a\rangle=\psi_{1 \mathrm{~s}}(\boldsymbol{r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a_{0}^{3}}} \mathrm{e}^{-r / a_{0}} . \quad(17)
$$

In terms of these states the corresponding Anderson Hamiltonian is written in the second quantization form as

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & \sum_{k, \sigma} \epsilon_{k} c_{k \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{k \sigma}+\sum_{\sigma} \epsilon_{a} c_{a \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{a \sigma} \\
& +\sum_{k, \sigma}\left(V_{a k} c_{a \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{k \sigma}+\text { h.c. }\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\epsilon_{k}$ and $\epsilon_{a}$ are the effective one-electron energies of the quasiparticle states $|k\rangle$ and $|a\rangle$, respectively (including also correlation effects), and $V_{a k}$ describes electron tunneling (hopping) from a metal state $|k\rangle$ into a localized state $|a\rangle$ induced by the (self-consistently screened) impurity atom potential $V$ [24]. $c^{\dagger}$ and $c$ are the electron creation and annihilation operators for the states as denoted by the subscripts.

In the following we shall employ the overlap approximation for the matrix elements $V_{ak}$ [24,25]. To this end, we first introduce a complete set of atomic quantum states $\{|m\rangle\}$ and write

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{a k} & =\langle a|V| k\rangle=\sum_{m}\langle a|V| m\rangle\langle m|k\rangle \\
& =\sum_{m} V_{a m} S_{m k},
\end{aligned}\qquad(19)
$$

where $S_{mk}=\langle m|k\rangle$ denotes the overlap integral. Then, we observe that due to a large separation of the 1s and higher levels of the hydrogen atom we shall approximately have

$$
\langle a|V| m\rangle \approx V_{a a} \delta_{m a},\qquad(20)
$$

which substituted in (19) yields for our model

$$
V_{a k} \approx V_{a a} S_{a k}.\qquad(21)
$$

The use of the overlap approximation for the matrix elements requires that we also consistently express the electron self-energies and related quantities in the form complying with the nonorthogonal Anderson model [4]. In particular, the localized level self-energy $\Sigma_{a}(\omega)$ calculated in the overlap approximation (21) acquires in the nonorthogonal Anderson model the form [24]

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_{a}(\omega) & =\Lambda_{a}(\omega)-\mathrm{i} \Delta_{a}(\omega) \\
& =\left(V_{a a}-\omega\right)^{2} \sum_{k}\left|S_{a k}\right|^{2} G_{k}^{0}(\omega),
\end{aligned}\qquad(22)
$$

where

$$
G_{k}^{0}(\omega)=\left(\omega-\epsilon_{k}+\mathrm{i} \delta\right)^{-1},\qquad(23)
$$

is the one-electron Green's function for the unperturbed $k$-states of energy $\epsilon_{k}$.

The change of the density of states upon introducing the impurity state $|a\rangle$ into the continuum of unperturbed itinerant $|k\rangle$-states is then given by [4]

$$
\Delta N(\omega)=-\frac{2}{\pi} \frac{\partial}{\partial \omega} \eta_{a}(\omega),\qquad(24)
$$

where the factor 2 stands for the spin and the phase shift $\eta_{a}$ is given by

$$
\eta_{a}(\omega)=\arctan \frac{\Delta(\omega)}{\omega-\epsilon_{a}-\Lambda_{a}(\omega)}.\qquad(25)
$$

The spatial distribution of the screening charge around the impurity is obtained by integrating the change of the local one-electron density of states $\Delta N(\boldsymbol{r}, \omega)$ up to the Fermi energy:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta N(\boldsymbol{r}) & =\int_{-\infty}^{\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}}} \mathrm{d} \omega \Delta N(\boldsymbol{r}, \omega) \\
& =-\frac{2}{\pi} \operatorname{Im} \int_{-\infty}^{\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}}} \mathrm{d} \omega\left\langle\boldsymbol{r}\left|\hat{G}(\omega)-\hat{G}^{0}(\omega)\right| \boldsymbol{r}\right\rangle,
\end{aligned}\qquad(26)
$$

where $\hat{G}(\omega)=(\omega-H+\mathrm{i} \delta)^{-1}$ and $\hat{G}^{0}(\omega)=(\omega-$ $H_{0}+\mathrm{i} \delta)^{-1}$ are the Greenians of the system in the presence and the absence of the impurity, respectively. As has been shown in Refs. [24] and [25], the matrix elements of these operators can be expressed in terms of the matrix elements $\left\langle\boldsymbol{r}\left|\hat{G}^{0}(\omega)\right| a\right\rangle$ and $\langle\boldsymbol{r}|\hat{G}(\omega)| a\rangle$ and, hence, calculated relatively easily once the overlap is known because $\hat{G}^{0}(\omega)$ is diagonal on the states $|k\rangle$.

The values of the effective localized level energy $\epsilon_{a}$ and the perturbing matrix element $\mathrm{V}_{a a}$ are not independent but connected through the Friedel sum rule:

$$
\Delta N=\frac{2}{\pi} \eta_{a}\left(\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}}\right)=1,\qquad(27)
$$

which expresses the requirement of the charge neutrality in the system, i.e. of total screening of the impurity charge (proton). Using this one can obtain the self-consistent pairs of values of $\epsilon_{a}$ and $V_{a a}$ once one of them is known. In the following we shall make use of the range of values of $V_{a a}$ discussed in Refs. [24] and [25]. However, it will turn out below that their magnitude $\left(<\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}}\right)$ and variation cannot significantly affect the difference $\left(V_{a a}-\omega\right)$ near $\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}}$ which are effectively responsible for the screening process.

Eqs. (19)-(27) provide the required framework for the calculation of the screening charge density around a hydrogen atom embedded in a Q-1D electron gas. Their basic ingredient is the overlap integral in the system of a highly reduced dimensionality of one of its components. Putting the centre of the hydrogen orbital at $\boldsymbol{r}=0$, we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{a k}=\langle 1 \mathrm{~s} \mid k\rangle= & \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi L a_{0}}} \int \mathrm{d}^{2} \rho \varphi_{0}(\rho) \frac{2\left(\rho / a_{0}\right)}{\sqrt{1+\left(k a_{0}\right)^{2}}} \\
& \times K_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{a_{0}} \sqrt{1+\left(k a_{0}\right)^{2}}\right).
\end{aligned}\qquad(28)
$$

Here, $K_{1}(x)$ is the Bessel function of the third kind and first order and the integration over $\rho$ is taken in the interval $(0 \leqslant \rho \leqslant l)$. Now, for the values $l<a_{0}$ (and we shall see below that this condition is justified on physical grounds), the dominant contribution to the integrand will come from large values of $K_{1}(x)$ for small argument. In this case we may use the asymptotic expression

$$
\lim _{x \rightarrow 0} K_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{x}, \quad(29)
$$

to obtain

$$
S_{a k} \approx \frac{\beta l}{\sqrt{L a_{0}}} \frac{2}{1+\left(k a_{0}\right)^{2}}, \quad(30)
$$

where $\beta \leqslant 1$ is a factor whose exact magnitude depends on the choice of the wavefunction $\varphi_{0}(\rho)$. Thus, $\beta=1$ for homogeneous lateral density in the tube, and in our case of an infinite barrier at the boundary $\rho=l$ (c.f. Eq. (15)) we have $\beta=0.8$.

Substitution of (30) into (22) yields for the localized level self-energy expressed in terms of the dimensionless energy $\Omega$ and matrix elements $V_{a}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_{a}(\omega)= & \frac{2}{\pi}\left(\frac{\beta l}{a_{0}}\right)^{2}\left(V_{a}-\Omega\right)^{2} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\mathrm{d} x}{(\Omega-x+\mathrm{i} \delta)\left(1+x^{2}\right)^{2}} \mathrm{Ry} \\
= & \left(\frac{\beta l}{a_{0}}\right)^{2}\left(V_{a}-\Omega\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Omega+3}{(1+\Omega)^{2}}-\mathrm{i} \frac{2}{\sqrt{\Omega}(1+\Omega)^{2}}\right) \mathrm{Ry}, \\
& (31)
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\Omega=\omega / 1 \mathrm{Ry}, \quad V_{a}=V_{a a} / 1 \mathrm{Ry}. \quad(32)
$$

From this we readily obtain the real and imaginary parts, describing the level shift and the effective level halfwidth, respectively, as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda(\omega)= & \left(\frac{\beta l}{a_{0}}\right)^{2} \frac{\left(V_{a}-\Omega\right)^{2}}{(1+\Omega)^{2}} \\
& \times\left((3+\Omega)-\frac{2}{\sqrt{|\Omega|}} \theta(-\Omega)\right) \mathrm{Ry}, \\
& (33)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(\Omega)= & \left(\frac{\beta l}{a_{0}}\right)^{2}\left(V_{a}-\Omega\right)^{2} \frac{2}{\sqrt{\Omega}(1+\Omega)^{2}} \\
& \times \theta(\Omega) \mathrm{Ry} . \quad(34)
\end{aligned}
$$

An interesting property of the quantities (33) and (34) is the $|\Omega|^{-1 / 2}$ singularity at the bottom $\Omega=0$ of the band of one-dimensional electronic states. This is simply a consequence of the same type of singularity appearing in the density of 1D free electron states.

Now we may substitute expressions (33) and (34) into Eq. (27) and require that the Friedel sum rule be obeyed. By doing this we tacitly assume that the hydrogen impurity is completely immersed into the Q-1D electronic density which can then give rise to a perfect screening of the impurity charge. Hence, we may set for convenience $\beta l / a_{0} \approx 1$ and find the values of $\epsilon_{a}$ and $V_{a}$ which satisfy Eq. (27). In fact, we find three possible consistent pairs which satisfy Eq. (27):

$$
V_{a a}=-0.019 \mathrm{Ry}, \quad \epsilon_{a}=0.102 \mathrm{Ry} ; \quad(35)
$$

$$
V_{a a}=0.1 \mathrm{Ry}, \quad \epsilon_{a}=0.222 \mathrm{Ry} ; \quad(36)
$$

$$
V_{a a}=-0.1 \mathrm{Ry}, \quad \epsilon_{a}=-0.0113 \mathrm{Ry} . \quad(37)
$$

In view of the later development of the model of screening of two impurities, it turns out that the values given by (35), for which $\epsilon_{a}>0$ and $V_{a}<0$ are the acceptable ones on the physical grounds, as it was also the case with a single impurity embedded into a 3D electron gas [24].

Fig. 1a gives the plots of $\omega-\epsilon_{a}, \Delta_{a}(\omega)$ and $\Lambda_{a}(\omega)$ in the interval around the band bottom at $\omega=0$ in which the latter two quantities exhibit a singular behaviour. Fig. 1b shows the behaviour of these functions in the interval $\left(0, \epsilon_{\mathrm{F}}\right)$. The intersection of $\omega-\epsilon_{a}$ with $\Lambda_{a}(\omega)$ below the band bottom gives the position of a sharp bound state $\epsilon_{\mathrm{B}}$ (since here $\Delta_{a}(\omega)=0$ ), whereas for $\omega>0$ such intersections give the positions (energies) of either resonant or antiresonant states.

The behaviour of the phase shift $\eta_{a}(\omega)$ in a small interval of energy around the band bottom is shown in Fig. 2a and in the whole interval $\left(0, \epsilon_{\mathrm{F}}\right)$ in Fig. 2b. The value of $-(2 / \pi) \eta_{a}(\omega)$ starts from zero at $\omega<\epsilon_{\mathrm{B}}$ and at $\epsilon_{\mathrm{B}}$ sharply rises up to the value of 2, i.e. the bound state accommodates exactly two electrons. Hence, the proton is overscreened by the charge residing in the orbital derived states. This value of 2 is retained up to the band bottom where the phase shift drops down to the value of 1 due to the existence of an antiresonance pinned to the lower

band edge and accommodating −1 electron. This antiresonance, which is a consequence of the singu- lar behaviour of $\Delta(\omega)$ at the band bottom, acts so as to compensate the excess screening charge around the proton. Therefore, little phase space is left for the variation of the phase shift between the band bottom and the Fermi level since at both ends of this interval $\eta_{a}=1$ . All these features are the consequence of a highly reduced dimensionality of the electron gas which, according to our requirement (27), should act as a perfect screening medium.

The spatial variation of the screening charge den- sity around the impurity is obtained from Eq. (26). To this end we have to calculate the function $\langler|G^{0}(\omega)| a\rangle$ which in our model reads
$$\begin{aligned}
\langle & \boldsymbol{r}\left|G^{0}(\omega)\right| a\rangle \\
= & \sum_{k}\langle\boldsymbol{r}|k\rangle G_{k}^{0}(\omega)\langle k| 1 s\rangle \\
= & \frac{\varphi_{0}(\rho)}{\pi \sqrt{a_{0}}}\left(\frac{\beta l}{a_{0}}\right) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \mathrm{d} x \\
& × \frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} x z / a_{0}}}{\left(\Omega-x^{2}+\mathrm{i} \delta\right)\left(1+x^{2}\right)} \mathrm{Ry}^{-1} \\
= & \frac{\varphi_{0}(\rho)}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}\left(\frac{\beta l}{a_{0}}\right) \\
& ×\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-|z / a_{0}|}}{(1+\Omega)}-\mathrm{i} \frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}|z / a_{0}| \sqrt{\Omega}}}{\sqrt{\Omega}(1+\Omega)}\right) \mathrm{Ry}^{-1}, \quad(38)
\end{aligned}$$
where z is the coordinate along the tube containing the Q-1D electron gas and the atom is placed at r=0. The presence of two terms in the square brackets on the RHS of (38), i.e. the one which decays exponentially with the distance from the cen- tre of the impurity, and the other which oscillates forΩ>0, will give rise to two types of charge densities involved in the screening of the proton charge. The former type is associated with the impurity orbital derived states (intra-atomic screening) and hence very localized. The latter is the screening charge density induced in the continuum of the one-dimen- sional band states (extra-atomic screening) and, hence, delocalized. Now, both $\langler|\hat{G}(\omega)| r\rangle$ andΔN(r, ω) are readily expressible in terms of $\langler|G^{0}(\omega)| a\rangle, \Lambda(\omega)$ and $\Delta(\omega)$ [24] which gives
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta N( & \boldsymbol{r}, \omega) \\
= & -\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right) \operatorname{Im}\left[\omega-\epsilon_{a}-\Sigma_{a}(\omega)\right]^{-1} \\
& ×\left[\left(V_{a a}-\omega\right)^{2}\left\langle\boldsymbol{r}\left|G^{0}(\omega)\right| a\right\rangle\left\langle a\left|G^{0}(\omega)\right| \boldsymbol{r}\right\rangle\right. \\
& +\left(V_{a a}-\omega\right)\left\langle\boldsymbol{r}\left|G^{0}(\omega)\right| a\right\rangle\langle a| \boldsymbol{r}\rangle \\
& +\left(V_{a a}-\omega\right)\left\langle\boldsymbol{r}\left|a\right\rangle\left\langle a\left|G^{0}(\omega)\right| \boldsymbol{r}\right\rangle\right. \\
& \left.+|\langle\boldsymbol{r}|a\rangle|^{2}\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad(39)$$

The plot of the spatial variation of the total screening charge density $\Delta N(z) ×a_{0} / \varphi_{0}^{2}(0)$ around the hydrogen atom is given as function of z in Fig.3 , for $\beta l / a_{0}=1$ . The screening charge density is intra-atomic like up to $z ≈1 a_{0}$ , which determines the effective radius of the hydrogen impurity. For z>1a, the screening charge density exhibits the Friedel oscillations typical of extra-atomic screening, whose amplitude in the present case falls off asymp- totically as $cos [2 k_{F} z-\eta_{a}(\epsilon_{F})] / z$ . However, our derivation of the screening charge also shows that it never becomes singular as there is a smooth transi- tion between the intra-orbital and extra-orbital char- acter of screening in the preasymptotic region, here taking place already around $z=1 a_{0}$ .

The long range character of the oscillations of the screening charge density around a hydrogen atom embedded into a Q-1D electron gas may strongly perturb any other atom in the system at distances at which all other direct interactions would be negligi- ble. Hence, we shall apply the same formalism to investigate the indirect interaction between two adatoms which is mediated by the Q-1D electronic states, thereby extending the model of nonlinear screening to the case of two impurities.

## 4. Conclusion

EELS studies of the system H/Ni(110) [14] and their theoretical interpretation [20], later IPES investigations of the electronic structure of the same system [21] as well as He scattering experiments carried out on the H/Cu(110) system [16], implied, in one way or another, the existence of relatively

strong lateral long range interactions between ad- sorbed H atoms. In particular, in Refs. [20] and [16] an oscillatory character of these interactions has been invoked explicitly in order to interpret the experi- mental data. Now, a question arises if these interac- tions can be of the RKKY type, i.e. indirect interac- tions between adsorbates which are mediated by the substrate electrons and, if so, which power law is going to describe correctly the fall off of their ampli- tude. Namely, as experiments clearly demonstrate, the (110) surfaces of Ni and Cu exhibit very peculiar electronic structure which may allow for deviations of the standard $\sim \cos(2k_{\text{F}}d)/d^{5}$-like behaviour of the indirect adsorbate-adsorbate interactions found under the assumption of essentially three-dimen- sional truncated substrate electron gas as being the mediator of the indirect interaction. Fitting the exist- ing data points of Ref. [20] to the $d^{-5}$ power law requires unreasonably large values of the matrix elements $V_{ak}$ of the order of 10 eV or even higher. Similar though less strong conclusions are arrived at if one assumes the $d^{-2}$ power law for scaling the amplitude of the indirect interactions, which was shown by LK to correspond to the inter-impurity interaction in a 2D electron gas. Hence, following the experimental evidence of the preferred interac- tion between H adatoms in the direction parallel to the troughs on the reconstructed Ni(110) surface, we have investigated the possibility of the occurrence of indirect interactions between two adsorbed H atoms mediated by Q-1D electronic states strongly local- ized to and propagating along the troughs.

Our first simple linear model of indirect interac- tions between two structureless impurities in 1D electron gas developed in Section 2 demonstrated that such an assumption was reasonable because it provided a framework for a reasonable description of the data. However, it also pointed out the limitations of the linear model of screening of impurity poten- tials for a complete description of the problem. To overcome these difficulties we have developed in Section 3.2 a nonlinear model of screening and indirect interactions between adsorbates which are mediated by the Q-1D electronic states propagating in the direction along the troughs on the (110) surfaces of fcc metals.

Within this model it was possible to fit both the amplitude, wavelength and phase of the interaction between two H atoms adsorbed on the Ni surface. The details of this fit should not be taken too seri- ously, since the adjustment of the parameters of the interaction from the data was not unambigous. What can be said with certainty, however, is that there is an oscillatory interaction decreasing much slower with distance than $d^{-5}$.

Our fit was achieved under the assumption that the diameter of the effective overlap area between the H1s orbitals and the region of the maximum density of the Q-1D electronic states was relatively small, here of the order of half a Bohr radius. In view of the adsorption geometry revealed for this system such an assumption is plausible because the H atoms in the unit cell of the superstructure on the Ni(110) surface are displaced away from the bottom of the troughs. However, this also implies that other electronic states of ‘‘standard’’ surface symmetry contribute to the H-substrate chemisorptive bonding and, in fact, can be responsible for the major part of the bond strength. This component of the chemisorp- tive bond gives also rise to the H–H indirect interac- tion, which scales either as $d^{-5}$ or $d^{-2}$. Their amplitudes at shorter distances could be of the same order as the one arising from the ‘‘chain’’ states, which falls off as $d^{-1}$, but become negligible at larger distances.

However, one should be aware of the possibility that at very large distances further effects such as e.g. disorder and strong correlations in a 1D elec- tronic system (Luttinger liquid) may change the os- cillatory $1/d$ behaviour of the indirect interaction as well as the related Kohn anomaly in the adsorbate phonon dispersion curve.

Nevertheless, we believe that this latter interac- tion, whose properties were derived and described in Section 3.2, could play an important role in the formation of ordered H adlayers on the Ni(110) surfaces (and most probably also on Cu(110)), as well as in the determination of their vibrational and other dynamical properties.
