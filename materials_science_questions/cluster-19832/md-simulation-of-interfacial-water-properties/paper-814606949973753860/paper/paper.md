# WATER DYNAMICS IN MODEL TRANSBILAYER PORES.

M.S.P. Sansom, J. Breed, R. Sankararamakrishnan and I.D. Kerr.

Laboratory of Molecular Biophysics, University of Oxford, The Rex Richards Building, South Parks Road, Oxford, OX1 3QU.

A number of membrane transport proteins (eg. porins, the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor, aquaporin) form water-filled pores which span a lipid bilayer. In order to understand the molecular properties of such transbilayer pores, it is essential to characterize the dynamic properties of the water molecules within them. Here we report the results of molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of several simple models of transbilayer pores. These models were solvated so as to include water molecules within and at either mouth of the pore. All simulations were of duration 100 ps (6ps heating + 9 ps equilibration + 85 ps production), and were carried out using *Charmm v23*, the *param19x* parameter set and modified *TIP3P* water molecules.

Two sets of models were explored:- (a) models in which water molecules were confined within pore-like cavities of differing geometry and dimensions; (b) pores formed from assemblies of $Ala_{M}$ polypeptide chains previously generated by restrained MD simulations. Two classes of $Ala_{M}$ pores were studied:- (a) α-helix bundles, composed of either 5 or 6 approximately parallel $Ala_{20}$ helices packed around a central pore (Kerr et al., 1994); (b) β-barrels, composed of 8 anti-parallel $Ala_{10}$ β-strands (Sansom and Kerr, 1995). In all of these models dynamic (self-diffusion coefficient, $D$; rotational reorientation rates $\tau_{1}^{-1}$ and $\tau_{2}^{-1}$) and orientational (z-axis projection of dipole moment) properties of the water molecules were analyzed as a function of their position within the pore.

In all simulations the dynamic properties of intra-pore water molecules are perturbed relative to those of bulk water. For example, values of $D$ for intra-pore waters are significantly lower than that for bulk water. For the $Ala_{M}$ pore models the intra-pore waters exhibit reduced translational and rotational mobility, whereas at the mouths of the pores their mobility is close to that of bulk water. In addition, for pores formed from $Ala_{20}$ α-helix bundles the water dipoles are aligned anti-parallel with the helix dipoles.

In Fig. 2, $D$ and $\mu_{z}$ for each water molecule in model *AN5* are plotted as functions of the mean z coordinate of the corresponding water oxygen atom. This illustrates quite clearly how the dynamics and orientation of the waters vary as one moves along the pore axis from the N-terminal mouth ($z < -15\mathring{A}$) into the pore and back out at the C-terminal mouth ($z > +15\mathring{A}$).

Overall, our results suggest that both the translational and rotational mobility of water molecules within narrow transbilayer pores are reduced relative to bulk water, independent of the exact nature of the pore. This has important implications for studies of ion transport through such pores. In particular, it suggests that continuum electrostatics calculations upon such systems should employ a value of $\epsilon << 78$ for the intra-pore solvent region. We are currently extending our simulations to:- ion channels formed by bundles of amphipathic α-helices; porins; and the pore domain of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor.
