# GROWTH OF AQUEOUS SULPHURIC ACID DROPLETS AS A FUNCTION OF RELATIVE HUMIDITY

P. V. N. NAIR and K. G. VOHRA

Air Monitoring Section, Division of Radiological Protection,
Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, Trombay, Bombay-400085, India

(First received 15 July 1974; and in revised form 9 December 1974)

Abstract-The paper presents computed values of equilibrium sizes and growth curves of aqueous solution droplets of sulphuric acid of initial dry size in the range of 10-5000 $\AA$ for relative humidities of 0,10,30,50,70,80,90,100,101 and 110 per cent and acid activities of $10^{-13}, 10^{-10}, 10^{-7}, 10^{-4}$ and $10^{-1}$. The computations are based on generalized Kelvin equations which incorporate the composition dependence of surface tension and density. The results indicate that the relative increases in size and mass depend on the quantity of solute per particle and that the equilibrium solute activity is very small for particles of radii above $50 \AA$ for r.h. above 50 per cent. The possibility of a hysteresis in the experimental growth curves, the threshold activity for nucleation, the growth of particles of mixed composition and the growth of Aitken nuclei to cloud nuclei are also briefly discussed.

## INTRODUCTION

Problems related to nucleation from the gas phase and growth of aqueous solution droplets have been studied by several investigators including Mason (1959), Doyle (1961), Fletcher (1962), Pilat and Charlson (1966), Low (1969), Hanel (1970), Vohra and Nair (1971), Cinkotai (1971), Winkler and Junge (1972) and Kiang and Stauffer (1973). Sul- phuric acid has been recognized as an important trace constituent of the atmosphere and it is known to give rise to heteromolecular nucleation of droplets in the presence of water vapour, besides condensing on preexisting nuclei (Stauffer et al., 1973; Cox, 1973). However, detailed estimates of growth parameters for aqueous solution droplets of sulphuric acid are not available. A detailed study on the growth of solution droplets of sulphuric acid as well as other important solutes as a function of relative humidity will be useful in the analysis of aerosol growth curves to identify the chemical composi- tion and understanding of the solute-solvent interaction in the process of growth of solution droplets. This paper presents calculated values of some parameters of growth of sulphuric acid droplets of dry sizes ranging from 10 to $5000 \AA$ for relative humidities of 0,10,30,50,70,80,90,100,101 and 110 per cent and acid activities of $10^{-1}$, $10^{-4}, 10^{-7}, 10^{-10}$ and $10^{-13}$.

## EQUILIBRIUM SIZES AND GROWTH PARAMETERS

The equilibrium sizes are computed for a range of acid concentrations (X, percentage by mass of acid) from 0.5 to 85 per cent for which the density $\rho$, surface tension $\sigma$ and water activity $a_{w}$ are available in standard references (International Critical Tables, 1928; Timmermans, 1960; Handbook of Chemistry and Physics, 1965; Robinson and Stokes, 1959), and are summarised in Table 1. The estimated values of $d \rho / d x$ and $d \sigma / d x$ are also given in the table. The acid activity $a_{o}$ was computed from activity coefficients (Giauque et al., 1960) assuming sulphuric acid to be a 1:2 electrolyte and normalizing to unit activity for 99 per cent acid. Doyle (1961) had adopted the same procedure for concentrations above 60 per cent. The ratios of mass of water to mass of acid $m_{w}/m_{o}$, equilibrium radius to dry radius $r/r_{o}$ and number of water molecules to acid molecules $n_{w}/n_{o}$ have been calculated from the molecular weights, concentrations and corresponding densities. The equilibrium radii r of particles may be computed using modified Kelvin equations developed for solution droplets (Mason, 1959; Vohra and Nair, 1971) which relate the saturation ratio of the vapour to the radius of the particle in equilibrium. ## DISCUSSION

The following points may be noted from this study:

(1) The growth curves for fixed r.h. and fixed acid activities (Fig. 1) have different
slopes and intersect at points corresponding to sizes at equilibrium with a specific r.h.
and an acid activity. Hence it is necessary to indicate both r.h. and the acid activity
to specify an equilibrium size. However under actual atmospheric conditions the equili-
brium with water vapour seems to be more important, except when factors leading to
the instability of aerosols in the atmosphere are to be evaluated.

(2) The equilibrium acid activities are very low for relative humidities above 50 per
cent and the sizes above $50\ Å$, as seen in columns $a_{w}$ and $a_{o}$ of Table 1. The humidities
and sizes observed in the atmosphere are frequently higher and will correspond to
lower values of acid activity. The vapour pressure of 99 per cent $H_{2}SO_{4}$ which is
taken as the standard is not accurately known but is expected to be close to $10^{-6}$
torr. Hence the actual acid vapour concentration in equilibrium with sizes above $50\ Å$
for humidities above 50 per cent are $\leqslant 1000$ molecules/$cm^{3}$. The rate of evaporation
of droplets depends on the particle $Kn$ number, diffusion coefficient of the constituent
molecules, their ambient concentration and the vapour pressure over the droplet and

(4) Figures 3 and 4 indicate that for dry sizes above $0.5 \mu \mathrm{m}$ the relative increase in mass and size tend to become independent of initial size for humidities below 100 per cent.

(5) When r.h. is increased particles will grow not only by condensation of water vapour but also by the condensation of solute molecules as the grown particle can accommodate more solute molecules due to the reduced equilibrium solute activity for the larger size. This in turn increases the condensation of water vapour which can lead to instability and continuous growth. (Figs. 1, 2 and 4). In the reverse process of decreasing r.h. solute will be lost by evaporation. Consequently changes in size per unit change in r.h. will depend on the direction of change of r.h. This can give rise to a hysteresis effect in the actual growth of solution droplets in closed systems but such an effect will be significant only for droplets containing solutes of high vapour pressure and will be enhanced in a sample of particles by the presence of particles in a wide range of size and solute molecules of low diffusion coefficient.

(6) Figures 1 and 5 indicate that the equilibrium vapour pressure of $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ for par ticles below $10 \AA$ is in the neighbourhood of $10^{-8} \mathrm{~mm}$ which is in agreement with the values of Doyle (1961) and Kiang and Stauffer (1973) for the nucleation threshold vapour pressure for $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ at 50 per cent humidity.

(7) The loops in the growth curves for 101 and 110 per cent r.h. show the activation of solution droplets in their transition from stable equilibrium to unstable equilibrium. Thus the curves give two values of $n_{w}$ for any value of $n_{0}$, the smaller $n_{w}$ corresponding to a free energy minimum and the larger $n_{w}$ to a free energy maximum. (Stauffer et al., 1973).

(8) Figures 1 and 2 show that sulphuric acid nuclei in the Aitken size range $(<0 \cdot 1 \mu)$ can grow to cloud nuclei as the relative humidity increases. Nuclei can grow con tinuously at relative humidities below 100 per cent due to the instability produced by the chain process of sequential condensation of water and solute molecules on the nuclei.

## APPENDIX I

The free energy of formation of an embryo consisting of $n_{o}$ moles of $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ and $n_{w}$ moles of $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ is represented by

$$
\Delta G=n_{o}\left(\mu_{o l}-\mu_{o g}\right)+n_{w}\left(\mu_{w l}-\mu_{w g}\right)+4 \pi r^{2} \sigma
$$

where $\mu_{o l}, \mu_{w l}$ are the chemical potentials taken for the liquid phase of the two components and $\mu_{o g}, \mu_{w g}$ are the existing chemical potentials in the gas phase in equilibrium with the droplet of radius $r$. At the equilibrium

$$
\left(\frac{\partial \Delta G}{\partial n_{w}}\right)_{n_{o}}=0 \quad \text { and } \quad\left(\frac{\partial \Delta G}{\partial n_{0}}\right)_{n_{w}}=0
$$

so that

$$
\mu_{w l}-\mu_{w g}+8 \pi r \sigma \frac{\partial r}{\partial n_{w}}+4 \pi r^{2} \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial n_{w}}=0
$$

and

$$
\mu_{o l}-\mu_{o g}+8 \pi r \sigma \frac{\partial r}{\partial n_{o}}+4 \pi r^{2} \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial n_{o}}=0
$$

$$
\frac{\partial r}{\partial n_{w}} \quad \text { and } \quad \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial n_{w}} \quad \text { as well as } \frac{\partial r}{\partial n_{o}} \quad \text { and } \quad \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial n_{o}}
$$

are evaluated from the following (iii, iv, v and vi):

$$
\frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3} \rho=n_{w} m_{w}+n_{o} M_{o}
$$

$(M_{o}, M_{w}$ are the molecular weights of acid and water respectively).

Per cent mass of acid:

$$
x=\frac{100 n_{o} M_{o}}{\left(n_{o} M_{o}+n_{w} M_{w}\right)}
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} \rho}{\mathrm{d} x}=\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial n_{w}} \frac{\partial n_{w}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial n_{o}} \frac{\partial n_{o}}{\partial x}
$$

and

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} x}=\frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial n_{w}} \frac{\partial n_{w}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial n_{o}} \frac{\partial n_{o}}{\partial x}
$$

Hence

$$
\frac{\partial n}{\partial n_{w}}=\left(M_{w}-\frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3} \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial n_{w}}\right) \frac{1}{4 \pi r^{2} \rho}
$$

and

$$
\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial n_{w}}=-\frac{3 M_{w} x}{4 \pi r^{3} \rho} \frac{\mathrm{d} \rho}{\mathrm{d} x}.
$$

Similarly

$$
\frac{\partial r}{\partial n_{o}}=\left(M_{o}-\frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3} \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial n_{o}}\right) \frac{1}{4 \pi r^{2} \rho}
$$

and

$$
\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial n_{o}}=\frac{3 M_{o}(100-x)}{4 \pi r^{3} \rho} \frac{\mathrm{d} \rho}{\mathrm{d} x}.
$$

The derivatives involving $\sigma$ are similar so that (i) and (ii) reduce to

$$
\mu_{w l}-\mu_{w g}+\frac{2 M_{w} \sigma}{r \rho}\left[1+\frac{x}{\rho} \frac{\mathrm{d} \rho}{\mathrm{d} x}-\frac{3}{2} \frac{x}{\sigma} \frac{\mathrm{d} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} x}\right]=0
$$

and

$$
\mu_{o l}-\mu_{o g}+\frac{2 M_{o} \sigma}{r \rho}\left[1-\frac{(100-x)}{\rho} \frac{\mathrm{d} \rho}{\mathrm{d} x}+\frac{3}{2} \frac{(100-x)}{\sigma} \frac{\mathrm{d} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} x}\right]=0.
$$

As $S_{w}, a_{w}$ and $S_{o}, a_{o}$ are defined with respect to the vapour pressure of the pure component it can be shown that

$$
\mu_{w l}-\mu_{w g}=-R T \ln \frac{S_{w}}{a_{w}}
$$

and

$$
\mu_{o l}-\mu_{o g}=-R T \ln \frac{S_{o}}{a_{o}} \tag{x}
$$

where $R$ is the gas constant $T$ the absolute temperature and $S_{w}, S_{o}$ are the saturation ratios of water and acid vapour respectively.

Hence

$$
\ln \frac{S_{w}}{a_{w}}=\frac{2}{R T} \frac{M_{w} \sigma}{\rho} \frac{1}{r}\left[1+\frac{x}{\rho} \frac{\mathrm{d} \rho}{\mathrm{d} x}-\frac{3}{2} \frac{x}{\sigma} \frac{\mathrm{d} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} x}\right] \tag{1}
$$

and

$$
\ln \frac{S_{o}}{a_{o}}=\frac{2}{R T} \frac{M_{o} \sigma}{\rho} \frac{1}{r}\left[1-\frac{(100-x)}{\rho} \frac{\mathrm{d} \rho}{\mathrm{d} x}+\frac{3}{2} \frac{(100-x)}{\sigma} \frac{\mathrm{d} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} x}\right] \tag{2}
$$
