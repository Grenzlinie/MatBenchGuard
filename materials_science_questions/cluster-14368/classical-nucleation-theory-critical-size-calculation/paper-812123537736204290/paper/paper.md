
Progress of Theoretical Physics, Vol. 49, No. 4, April 1973

# Grain Formation in the Expanding Envelopes of T Tauri Stars

Shuzo NISHIDA and Kiyoshi NAKAZAWA

Department of Physics, Kyoto University, Kyoto

(Received October 17, 1972)

The possibility of the nucleation in the expanding envelopes of T Tauri stars is investigated by using the nucleation theory by Becker and Döring. The nucleation time scale $\tau_n$ in the gas is investigated assuming that only graphite is condensable. The expansion time scale $\tau_{\text{exp}}$ of the gas ejected from a T Tauri star is calculated assuming that the gas flow is steady and spherically symmetric. The time scale $\tau_n$ is compared with $\tau_{\text{exp}}$ for $X_0$=$10^{-2}$ ($X_0$ is the concentration of carbon by mass) assuming that the gas expands adiabatically, and the mass-loss rate needed for nucleation is found to be larger than those observed in T Tauri stars. This suggests that nucleation around T Tauri stars is rather difficult.

## § 1. Introduction

It is generally believed that grains are contained in interstellar and proto-planetary gas clouds. The importance of the existence of grains has been pointed out in connection with the cooling mechanism during the contraction of the gas cloud and with the origin of protoplanets. $^{1)}$ In spite of the importance, however, the formation of grains has not yet been understood well. A few authors have pointed out the possibility that grains are formed in atmospheres of cool stars or in expanding shells of active stars such as T Tauri stars, planetary nebulae, novae and supernovae. $^{2)\sim4)}$ This is also suggested by the infrared excesses of active stars. $^{5)\sim18)}$

Hoyle and Wickramasinghe $^{3),8)}$ have investigated the conditions for the formation and the growth of solid particles in the static stellar atmospheres of $N$-type stars. Kamijo $^{14)}$ and Donn et al. $^{15)}$ have re-examined Hoyle and Wickramasinghe's mechanism in cool carbon stars. On the other hand, Fix $^{16)}$ has studied the formation of grains in the adiabatically pulsating atmosphere. He has shown that if the pulsation period is longer than a few months, the grains are condensed in expanding phases of pulsation.

In this paper we will investigate the possibility of the formation of grains in the expanding envelopes of T Tauri stars using the nucleation theory by Becker and Döring $^{17)}$ and others. $^{18)\sim20)}$ The reasons why we take notice of T Tauri stars are as follows: Firstly, some of them are known as infrared stars $^{11)}$ so that grains may exist in the circumstellar spaces around T Tauri stars. The mass-loss rates are known from the observations together with the initial velocities of ejected matter and stellar radii. $^{21)}$ Hence we can easily compare our estimation with

observations. Secondly, in connection with the origin of the planets, we are in- terested in the problem whether or not grains can be formed in the matter ejected from central stars which are in the premain-sequence stage. T Tauri stars are considered to be in this stage.

For simplicity, we have assumed that the expanding gas is composed of only H, He and C, as carbon may be the most abundant among the heavy elements, and that the concentration by mass of the available carbon $X_{\mathrm{C}}$ is $10^{-2}$ as the optimum case. So we are restricted to the discussion of the condensation of graphite. We have assumed the other optimum conditions for nucleation, such as the sticking probability, the initial temperature of ejected material and the neglect of radiation pressure.

In $\S 2$ the theory of homogeneous nucleation by Becker and Döring is briefly reviewed and applied to the super-saturated carbon gas. The time scale of nu- cleation is given as a function of temperature and number density of carbon.

In $\S 3$ the behavior of expanding gas is considered. We have assumed that the mass from a T Tauri star flows steadily, adiabatically and spherically. As to the expanding velocity $v$, we have considered two simple cases; one is that $v \propto R$, where $R$ is the distance, from the center of T Tauri star, and the other is that $v=$ constant.

In $\S 4$ the possibility of nucleation is examined by comparing the nucleation time scale and the expansion time scale obtained in $\S \S 2$ and 3 , respectively. In other words, the mass-loss rate needed for the appreciable nucleation is found for two alternative cases on the expansion velocity mentioned above and is com- pared with the observed one.

The assumptions, which we have taken in this article, are reconsidered in $\S 5$; especially, the assumptions that the gas is composed of only H, He and C, and that the mass flows spherically and adiabatically. From these considerations it is concluded that as long as we are concerned with the steady mass-loss, the nucleation of graphite is rather difficult in expanding envelopes of T Tauri stars.

## $\S 2$. The nucleation time scale of graphite

On the basis of molecular equilibrium, many authors $^{2), 22), 23)}$ have examined what kinds of species are mainly condensed in a cooling gas, for example, in a cool stellar atmosphere. According to them, the main condensed species are silicate, graphite, ice, iron oxide, magnesium oxide, silicon carbide and so on. The chemical compositions of these condensed matter are, of course, dependent upon the initial composition of gas and temperature. However, for the case of a gas with the solar abundance, graphite is one of the most condensable species, and hence we will consider, as an extreme case, a simple system which is com- posed of only H, He and C. In the case of an oxygen-rich gas, most of carbon atoms react rapidly with oxygen atoms to become CO molecules, so that the num- ber density of monatomic carbons which are available for nucleation becomes

quite small. Our choice of chemical composition gives an optimum condition for the nucleation of graphite.

Thus we consider a simple gas system composed of H, He and C with temperature $T$ and density $\rho$. In order for the nucleation to occur, the carbon vapor must be super-saturated; that is,
$$
S=P_{\mathrm{C}} / P_{v}>1, \quad (2 \cdot 1)
$$
where $S$ is called the super-saturation ratio. The partial pressure of carbon gas $P_{\mathrm{C}}$ and its vapor pressure $P_{v}$ are expressed as
$$
P_{\mathrm{C}}=n_{\mathrm{C}} k T=\frac{\rho X_{\mathrm{C}}}{m_{\mathrm{C}}} k T \quad (2 \cdot 2)
$$
and
$$
\log P_{v}=-\frac{\gamma}{T}+\delta, \quad (2 \cdot 3)
$$
respectively, where $n_{\mathrm{C}}$ is the number density of carbon atoms, $m_{\mathrm{C}}$ is the mass of a carbon atom, and $\gamma$ and $\delta$ are constants and taken to be $\gamma=3.71 \times 10^{4}$ and $\delta=14.1$ in c.g.s. units. $^{24)}$

The rate of the nucleation in such a super-saturated vapor has been studied by Becker and Döring $^{17)}$ and others. $^{18) \sim 20)}$ Their basic idea in deriving the nuclea tion rate is as follows: The Gibbs free energy of the formation of a spherical cluster of molecules with radius $r$ is given by
$$
\Delta G=\frac{4 \pi r^{3}}{3} \Delta G_{v}+4 \pi r^{2} \sigma, \quad (2 \cdot 4)
$$
where $\sigma$ is the surface energy and $\Delta G_{v}$ is the Gibbs free energy difference per unit volume, i.e.,
$$
\Delta G_{v}=-(k T / \Omega) \ln S, \quad (2 \cdot 5)
$$
where $\Omega$ is the volume occupied by a molecule. As shown in Fig. $1 \Delta G(r)$ has the maximum value $\Delta G^{*}$ at some critical value of $r$ which is hereafter denoted as $r_{*}$. From Eqs. $(2 \cdot 4)$ and $(2 \cdot 5)$ we have
$$
\Delta G^{*} / k T=(16 \pi / 3) \Omega^{2} \sigma^{3} /(k T)^{3} \ln ^{2} S \quad (2 \cdot 6)
$$
and
$$
r_{*}=2 \sigma \Omega / k T \ln S. \quad (2 \cdot 7)
$$

When the size of cluster is smaller than $r_{*}$, it evaporates easily, but, as soon as the cluster becomes larger than this size, it begins to grow rapidly (see Fig. 2).

Becker and Döring have obtained a solution for a set of bimolecular steady- state reactions
$$
B_{g-1}+B \rightleftarrows B_{g}, \quad (2 \cdot 8)
$$

Thus, the detailed expression for the rate of homogeneous nucleation of graphite
$J$ is given by

$$

where $\alpha$ is the sticking probability of carbon, $f$ is the flux of incident carbon
atoms, i.e.,

$$

\bar{V} \equiv \sqrt{k T / 2 \pi m_{\mathrm{C}}}. \tag{2.13}
$$

Now, we can define the nucleation time scale of graphite $\tau_{n}$ as

$$
\tau_{n} \equiv n_{\mathrm{C}} /\left|d n_{\mathrm{C}} / d t\right|=n_{\mathrm{C}} / g^{*} J, \tag{2.14}
$$

where $g^{*}$ is the number of atoms contained in a critical carbon cluster. Using

occur. As $J$ is approximately proportional to $\left(\rho X_{\mathrm{C}}\right)^{2}, \tau_{n}$ is nearly proportional to
the inverse of $\rho X_{\mathrm{C}}$ and is approximately expressed as

## § 3. The behavior of expanding envelope

Let us consider the behavior of material ejected from a T Tauri star. We assume that the mass is ejected isotropically and steadily. From the equation

![](./images/812123537736204290_4.jpg)

of continuity, we have
$$
4 \pi \rho v R^{2}=A, \quad(3 \cdot 1)
$$
where $v, R$ and $A$ are the velocity of ejected matter, the distance from the cen- ter of T Tauri star and the mass-loss rate, respectively.

Parker has investigated the steady solar wind. $^{25)}$ According to his solution, the velocity of solar wind is approximately proportional to the radial distance near the sun and is nearly constant far away from the sun. This is schematically shown in Fig. 4. Parker's precise solution lies between the two extreme approximations. We assume that the behavior of the expanding envelope of a T Tauri star is similar to that of the solar wind. We consider two alternative cases A and B for the $R$ dependence of velocity $v$, that is,
$$\text { Case A: } \quad v=\text { constant }, \quad(3 \cdot 2 a)$$

$$\text { Case B: } \quad v \propto R \text {. } \quad(3 \cdot 2 b)$$

The expansion time scale $\tau_{\exp }$ defined by
$$\tau_{\exp } \equiv|d t / d \ln \rho|\quad (3.3)$$
is written as
$$\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{A}}=R / 2 v=R / 2 v_{0}\quad (3.4)$$
for Case A using Eqs. $(3 \cdot 1)$ and $(3 \cdot 2 a)$, and
$$\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{B}}=R / 3 v=R_{0} / 3 v_{0}\quad (3.5)$$
for Case B using Eqs. $(3 \cdot 1)$ and $(3 \cdot 2 b)$, where the suffix 0 denotes the values at the surface of the star.

Next we consider the thermal behavior of expanding envelope. $^{26)}$ For sim plicity the ejected material is assumed to expand adiabatically. Then we have
$$T \frac{d s}{d t}=\frac{d u}{d t}+P \frac{d}{d t}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)=0, \quad(3 \cdot 6)$$
where $s, u$ and $P$ are the entropy, internal energy per unit mass and the pressure of ejected material, respectively. The temperature of expanding material changes with its density $^{27)}$ as
$$\frac{d \ln T}{d \ln \rho}=\frac{8-6 \beta}{24-21 \beta}, \quad(3 \cdot 7)$$

where $\beta$ is the ratio of gas pressure to total pressure. In the extreme case where $\beta=1$, we have $d \ln T / d \ln \rho=2 / 3$ and in general $d \ln T / d \ln \rho \leqq 2 / 3$. As will be mentioned later, the nucleation becomes easier as $d \ln T / d \ln \rho$ increases. In this article we discuss the problem under the best condition for nucleation, so that we will neglect the radiation pressure. Thus, we have

$$
\frac{d \ln T}{d \ln \rho}=\frac{2}{3}. \quad(3.8)
$$

If the mass-loss rate $A$, distance $R_{0}$ (the radius of T Tauri star), temperature $T_{0}$, and velocity $v_{0}$ at the surface are given, ${ }^{21)}$ the dynamical and thermal properties of the ejected matter are completely determined as the function of $R$ or $\rho$.

## §4. Nucleation

We will examine the possibility of nucleation of graphite in the matter ejected from $\mathrm{T}$ Tauri stars. We assume that the surface temperature $T_{0}$ of $\mathrm{T}$ Tauri star is $4000^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$ for all cases considered here. This will be justified by the observations. For example, Kuhi $^{21)}$ has reported that the surface temperatures of six T Tauri stars lie between $4090^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$ and $4960^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$.

When the temperature of the matter becomes lower than some critical value, the partial pressure of carbon exceeds the vapor pressure. In this situation the nucleation begins if the condition

$$
\tau_{n}<\tau_{\text {exp }} \quad(4 \cdot 1)
$$

is satisfied. In this section we will examine the physical conditions on the mass-loss rate with which the condition $(4 \cdot 1)$ is satisfied for each of the two cases mentioned in $\S 3$. For Case A we have from Eqs. $(3 \cdot 1)$ and $(3 \cdot 2 a)$

$$
A=4 \pi R_{0}^{2} \rho_{0} v_{0}=4 \pi R^{2} \rho v_{0}, \quad(4 \cdot 2)
$$

where $\rho_{0}$ is the density of expanding envelope at the surface of the star. Hence, the density varies with the distance from the center of the star as

The parameter $\eta$ is determined only by the values at the surface. In Fig. 5, the lines on which the condition $\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{A}}=\tau_{n}$ is satisfied are shown for various values of $\eta$. Above the curve with a fixed value of $\eta$, we have $\tau_{n}<\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{A}}$ and on the opposite side, $\tau_{n}>\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{A}}$. When $\rho_{0}, T_{0}, v_{0}$ and $R_{0}$ are given, we can draw the adiabat on the $\rho-T$ plane (see Eq. $(3 \cdot 8)$ ), and if the adiabat crosses over the locus of $\tau_{n}=\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{A}}$ with $\eta$ given by Eq. $(4 \cdot 6)$, the expanding matter experiences the situation of $\tau_{n}<\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{A}}$ in some time-interval. Let us consider the adiabat which touches the locus determined by $\eta$, and let $\rho_{c}$ be the density at the stellar surface on this adiabat. Then, the relation between $\eta$ and $\rho_{c}$ can be easily obtained graphically (see Fig. 6). This relation is approximately given by

$$
\log \rho_{c}=a \log \eta+b, \tag{4·7}
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are constants depending on $X_{\mathrm{C}}$ and $T_{0}$, and for $X_{\mathrm{C}}=10^{-2}$ and $T_{0}=4000^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$, we obtain $a=-1.88$ and $b=-22.1$. This shows that for a larger

$\eta$, the nucleation can occur in the matter of lower density. In Eq. (4.7) $\log \rho_{c}$ is linearly dependent on $\log \eta$. This relation can be easily understood, because the nucleation time scale $\tau_{n}$ has an approximately linear dependence on the inverse of density $\rho$. The requirement that the matter must experience the situation of $\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{A}}>\tau_{n}$ on the way of expansion, is equivalent to the condition

$$\rho_{0}>\rho_{c}. \quad (4 \cdot 8)$$

From Eqs. (4·2), (4·6), (4·7) and (4·8), we have an inequality

$$\log A \geq \frac{1}{2-a}\left\{4 \log R_{0}+(2-3 a) \log v_{0}+\left(a \log \frac{3}{2} G+2 b+2 \log 4 \pi\right)\right\}$$

or

$$\log \left(\frac{A}{10^{-7} M_{\odot} / y}\right) \geq 1.03 \log \left(\frac{R_{0}}{R_{\odot}}\right)+1.97 \log \left(\frac{v_{0}}{10^{7} \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{sec}}\right)-1.29, \quad (4 \cdot 9)$$

which must be satisfied for the nucleation of graphite to occur in the expanding envelope of T Tauri stars.

In Case B the time scale of density decrease $\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{B}}$ is constant during the expansion and is determined by $v_{0}$ and $R_{0}$, i.e.,

$$\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{B}}=R_{0} / 3 v_{0} \equiv \zeta. \quad (4 \cdot 10)$$

We can again find the relation between $\zeta$ and $\rho_{c}$, when the adiabat beginning at the point of $\rho=\rho_{c}$ and $T=T_{0}$ touches the locus of $\tau_{n}=\tau_{\exp }^{\mathrm{B}}$; that is (see Fig.7),

$$\log \rho_{c}=a^{\prime} \log 3 \zeta+b^{\prime}, \quad (4 \cdot 11)$$

where $a^{\prime}$ and $b^{\prime}$ are constants, and for $X_{\mathrm{C}}=10^{-2}$ and $T_{0}=4000^{\circ} \mathrm{K}, a^{\prime}=-0.935$ and $b^{\prime}=-8.42$. Demanding again the condition (4·8), the procedures similar to Case A give an inequality

$$\log A \geq\left(2+a^{\prime}\right) \log R_{0}+\left(1-a^{\prime}\right) \log v_{0}+\left(b^{\prime}+\log 4 \pi\right)$$

or

$$\log \left(\frac{A}{10^{-7} M_{\odot} / y}\right) \geq 1.07 \log \left(\frac{R_{0}}{R_{\odot}}\right)+1.94 \log \left(\frac{v_{0}}{10^{7} \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{sec}}\right)-1.03. \quad (4 \cdot 12)$$

Only when the observed mass-loss rate satisfies the inequality (4·9) or (4·12) for each case, the nucleation can occur in the expanding envelope of the T Tauri star. Thus, using the observational results on radii and expanding velocities of T Tauri stars, we can find the minimum mass-loss rates required for the nucleation. In Table I the mass-loss rates required by the condition (4·9) or (4·12) for various T Tauri stars are shown together with the observed mass-loss rates, radii and velocities. The results in Table I indicate that the nucleation of graphite is rather difficult in the expanding envelopes of T Tauri stars.

## § 5. Discussion

Table I indicates that the observed mass-loss rates of T Tauri stars are slightly smaller than, or nearly the same as those required for the appreciable nucleation of graphite. This means that the formation of graphite in the material ejected from T Tauri stars is not easy even under the optimum condition. Actual conditions will be much severer. Firstly, the carbon concentration has been assumed to be 0.01 in our estimation. This is the maximum value to be expected. Furthermore, we have not taken into account the decrease of the concentration of atomic carbon due to the reactions between C- and O-atoms. If O-atoms exist, they react rapidly with C-atoms to become CO, and the available C-atoms are reduced before the graphite is formed. Thus, the mass-loss rate required may be at least ten times as those estimated in $\S 4$, because oxygen is as abundant as carbon.

Secondly, as the optimum case, we have set $\alpha$ to be 1. However, the sticking probability is also an ambiguous factor. Thorn and Winslow$^{28)}$ have estimated that $\alpha=0.37$ for monatomic carbon. However, there is a report$^{29)}$ that the sticking probability has a value of about $10^{-3}$. If this is true, the mass-loss rates required must be a thousand times as those calculated in $\S 4$.

In the previous section, we have assumed that the initial temperature is $4000^\circ$K and the temperature of the expanding envelope changes with its density adiabatically. For the higher initial temperature the larger mass-loss rate is demanded for nucleation (see Figs. 6 and 7). According to Kuhi, $^{21)}$ the observed surface temperatures of T Tauri stars lie between $4090^\circ$K and $4960^\circ$K. In this sense, our estimation corresponds to the optimum case, though the choice of the initial temperature has no important influence. Furthermore, the nucleation occurs more easily for the larger value of $d\ln T/d\ln\rho$. Hence, the neglect of radiation pressure also gives a better condition for nucleation, and, on the other

hand, the assumption that the envelope expands adiabatically suppresses the nuclea- tion. Even in this case, the error in mass-loss rates obtained in $\S 4$ is within factor 2.

We have assumed the spherically expanding mass-loss. If the disk-like ex- pansion is assumed for the mass flow, the mass-loss rate $A$ is given by
$$A=2\pi Rh\rho v,$$
where $h$ is the thickness of the disk. Thus, with the same procedure mentioned in $\S 4$ , we have
$$\log A \geq \log v_{0}^{2} h+\log K(T) / X_{\mathrm{C}}+\log 2 \pi\qquad(5.2)$$
from the condition $\tau_{n}<\tau_{exp }$ for Case A, where $K(T)$ is given by Eq. $(2 \cdot 15)$ , and
$$10^{-11.5} \lesssim K(T) \lesssim 10^{-11.0}\qquad(5.3)$$
for the relevant range of temperature in c.g.s. units. This gives the mass-loss rate needed for the nucleation. The results shown in Table II indicate that the required mass-loss rate for nucleation in this case is smaller than that of the spherical case by factor 4 to 10.

In this paper we have restricted ourselves to the homogeneous nucleation. Other processes of nucleation have been proposed, such as ionic nucleation $^{15), 16), 19)}$ and heterogeneous nucleation. $^{19)}$ However, the ionic nucleation is unlikely to take place since the number density of ions is estimated not to be so large that they do not play a role of kernels for nucleation. We can neglect the heterogeneous nucleation because of the small concentration of elements other than carbon.

From these considerations we can conclude that the nucleation of graphite in the ejected envelopes of T Tauri stars is rather difficult as long as the steady mass-loss is assumed. The nucleation of other elements such as silicates and ironic oxides is also difficult because these elements are less abundant than car- bon. Nevertheless, the observations of T Tauri stars by means of infrared radia- tion $^{11)}$ support the existence of solid particles in expanding envelopes. Possible room for improvement is only to take into account the non-steady mass flow, if grains are formed in the matter ejected from central stars.

Our procedure can be applied to the other active stellar objects $^{80)}$ such as novae, supernovae and planetary nebulae. Let us consider a supernova explosion, for example, which has an initial density $\sim 10^{81}$ nucleons $/ cm^{3}$ , that is, $\sim 2 \times 10^{7} ~g / cm^{3}$ , an initial temperature of $\sim 10^{100} ~K$ , a radius at the moment of explosion $\sim 10^{9} ~cm$ and an expansion velocity $\sim 5000 ~km / sec^{4)}$ In the same manner we find that the initial density needed for nucleation must be larger than $10^{-4} ~g / cm^{3}$ in the case of the constant velocity of expansion. This is obtained for the optimum condi- tion, and the consideration of some unfavorable situations will increase the neces- sary density by several orders. However, the actual density is larger by eleven orders, so that there may be enough possibility of nucleation in a supernova ex- plosion.
