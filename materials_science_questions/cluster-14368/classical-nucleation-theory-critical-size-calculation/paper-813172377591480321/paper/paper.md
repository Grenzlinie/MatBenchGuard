# Comparison of Theories of Ion-Induced Nucleation
G. V. Anikin and V. A. Vlasov

Institute of the Earth Cryosphere, Siberian Branch, Russian Academy of Sciences, Tyumen, 625000 Russia
e-mail: anikin@ikz.ru, vlasov.ikz@gmail.com

Received March 5, 2013

Abstract—A theory of nucleation on ions and a theory of nucleation in the presence of ions are presented. The calculated data obtained within these theories are compared to the experimental data for the nucleation of vapors of different compounds. It is shown that the theory of nucleation in the presence of ions is in better agreement with the experimental data than the theory of nucleation on ions. It is assumed within the theory of nucleation in the presence of ions that each ion in the vapor phase forms a stable ion associate with vapor molecules.

Keywords: heterogeneous nucleation, ions, work of formation, rate nucleation.

DOI: 10.1134/S0036024414010026

## INTRODUCTION

Ion-induced nucleation is a phenomenon that is very widespread in nature. It is of special importance when considering processes that occur in Earth's atmosphere [1–3]. Controlled ion-induced nucle- ation is also applied in different technologies, e.g., the manufacture of nanosized particles [4].

There have been a number of theoretical works in which the effect of ions on the process of nucleation was considered within the classical thermodynamic approach. In most of these works [5–11], a situation was considered in which nucleation proceeds on ions, i.e., when an isolated ion is located in the cen- ter of a cluster of a new phase (the Thompson model). There are, however, other works [12–15] that consider a situation where nucleation proceeds in the presence of ions, i.e., an isolated ion can be located arbitrarily with respect to the center of a cluster of a new phase, either inside or outside it. The aim of this work was to compare the theory of nucleation on ions and the theory of nucleation in the presence of ions by comparing the predictions of these theories to the experimental data. We consider the vapor–liquid phase transition with vapor as an ideal gas.

## THEORY OF THE NUCLEATION ON IONS

If an ion is located in the center of a spherical clus- ter of the condensed phase (Fig. 1), the work of forma- tion of this cluster is written as [16]

$$
\begin{aligned}
W= & -\left(\mu_{\text {old }}-\mu_{\text {new }}\right) \frac{4 \pi}{3 v}\left(R^{3}-R_{\text {ion }}^{3}\right)+4 \pi \sigma\left(R^{2}-R_{\text {ion }}^{2}\right) \\
& -\frac{(Z e)^{2}}{8 \pi \varepsilon_{0}}\left(\varepsilon_{\text {old }}^{-1}-\varepsilon_{\text {new }}^{-1}\right)\left(R_{\text {ion }}^{-1}-R^{-1}\right), \quad R \geq R_{\text {ion }},
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

where $R$ is the radius of the cluster of the new phase; $R_{\text{ion}}$ is the radius of the ion; $\mu_{\text{old}}$ and $\mu_{\text{new}}$ are the chemical potentials of the old and new phases, respec- tively; $v$ is the volume per one molecule in the new phase; $\sigma$ is the coefficient of the surface tension between the old and new phases; $\varepsilon_{\text{old}}$ and $\varepsilon_{\text{new}}$ are the static dielectric permittivity of the old and new phases, respectively; $e$ is the elementary electric charge; $Z$ is the charge multiplicity of the ion; and $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the electric constant.

The function in form (1) at $\varepsilon_{\text{new}} > \varepsilon_{\text{old}}$ has two extrema: a minimum at $R = R_{\text{min}}$ and a maximum at $R = R_{\text{max}}$. The quantities $R_{\text{min}}$ and $R_{\text{max}}$ are the real roots of the equation $dW/dR = 0$:

$$
(\mu_{\text{old}} - \mu_{\text{new}}) R^{4} - 2\sigma V R^{3} + \frac{(Ze)^{2} V}{32\pi^{2} \varepsilon_{0}} \big(\varepsilon_{\text{old}}^{-1} - \varepsilon_{\text{new}}^{-1}\big) = 0. \tag{2}
$$

In the case of the phase vapor-liquid transition [17]
$$
\mu_{\text{old}} - \mu_{\text{new}} = kT \ln S, \quad S = P/P_{\text{eq}},
$$
where $k$ is the Boltzmann constant; $T$ is temperature; $S$ is the supersaturation of vapor; $P$ is the pressure of vapor; and $P_{\text{eq}}$ is the pressure of the equilibrium vapor-liquid.

The rate of nucleation on ions during the vapor-liquid phase transition is written in the form [18, 19]

$$
\begin{gathered}
J = \frac{njA}{3N_{\text{nuc}}} \left( \frac{4\pi\sigma R_{\text{max}}^{2} - \frac{(Ze)^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0} R_{\text{max}}} \big(\varepsilon_{\text{old}}^{-1} - \varepsilon_{\text{new}}^{-1}\big)}{\pi kT} \right)^{1/2} \quad (3) \\
\times \exp\left( -\frac{W^{*}}{kT} \right),
\end{gathered}
$$

where $n$ is the concentration of ions; $N_{\text{nuc}}$ is the number of molecules in a cluster of critical size; $j$ is the flux of molecules joining the cluster of the new phase; $A$ is the surface area of the sphere of action of a cluster of critical size; and $W^{*}$ is the work of formation for a cluster of critical size. The $N_{\text{nuc}}$, $j$, $A$, and $W^{*}$ are given by

$$
N_{\text{nuc}} = \frac{4\pi}{3V} \big(R_{\text{max}}^{3} - R_{\text{ion}}^{3}\big), \quad j = \frac{\alpha S P_{\text{eq}}}{(2\pi m k T)^{1/2}},
$$

$$
A = 4\pi \left( R_{\text{max}} + \left( \frac{3V}{4\pi} \right)^{1/3} \right)^{2}, \quad W^{*} = W_{\text{max}} - W_{\text{min}},
$$

where $\alpha$ is a condensation coefficient that differs slightly from unity; $m$ is the mass of a molecule of the compounds undergoing phase transition; $W_{\text{max}}$ and $W_{\text{min}}$ are the work of formation of a cluster with radii $R_{\text{max}}$ and $R_{\text{min}}$, respectively. We can show [16] that the work of formation for a cluster of critical size can be written in the form

$$
\begin{gathered}
W^{*} = \frac{4\pi\sigma}{3} \big(R_{\text{max}}^{2} - R_{\text{min}}^{2}\big) \\
- \frac{(Ze)^{2}}{6\pi\varepsilon_{0}} \big(\varepsilon_{\text{old}}^{-1} - \varepsilon_{\text{new}}^{-1}\big) \big(R_{\text{min}}^{-1} - R_{\text{max}}^{-1}\big).
\end{gathered}
$$

The last relation is valid only under the condition $\varepsilon_{\text{new}} > \varepsilon_{\text{old}}$, which holds at the phase transition vapor-liquid when $\varepsilon_{\text{new}} > 1$, and $\varepsilon_{\text{old}} \approx 1$.

# THEORY OF NUCLEATION IN THE PRESENCE OF IONS

It was shown in [12] that the work of formation of a spherical cluster of a new phase in the volume of a old phase can be written in the form

$$
\begin{gathered}
W_{\text{out}} = -\frac{4\pi}{3V} (\mu_{\text{old}} - \mu_{\text{new}}) R^{3} + 4\pi\sigma R^{2} \\
- \frac{(Ze)^{2}}{8\pi\varepsilon_{0} R} \big(\varepsilon_{\text{old}}^{-1} - \varepsilon_{\text{new}}^{-1}\big) \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon_{\text{new}} l}{\varepsilon_{\text{new}} l + \varepsilon_{\text{old}} (l + 1)} \left( \frac{R}{r} \right)^{2(l+1)}, \quad (4)
\end{gathered}
$$
$$
r > R + R_{\text{ion}}, \quad R > 0,
$$

when the ion is wholly outside the cluster (Fig. 2a), and in the form

$$
\begin{gathered}
W_{\text{in}} = -\frac{4\pi}{3V} (\mu_{\text{old}} - \mu_{\text{new}}) R^{3} + 4\pi\sigma R^{2} \\
+ \frac{(Ze)^{2}}{8\pi\varepsilon_{0} R} \big(\varepsilon_{\text{old}}^{-1} - \varepsilon_{\text{new}}^{-1}\big) \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon_{\text{old}} (l + 1)}{\varepsilon_{\text{new}} l + \varepsilon_{\text{old}} (l + 1)} \left( \frac{r}{R} \right)^{2l} + W_{\text{add}}, \quad (5)
\end{gathered}
$$
$$
r < R - R_{\text{ion}}, \quad R > R_{\text{ion}},
$$

when the ion is wholly inside the cluster (Fig. 2b). Here, $r$ is the distance between the center of the cluster and the center of the ion; $W_{\text{add}}$ is the contribution to the work of formation of the cluster of the new phase, allowing for the specific energy of the interaction between the ion and the molecules of the new phase surrounding it $E_{\text{i-m}}$, along with the geometric size of

the ion $W_{\text{geom}}$. The expression for the $W_{\text{add}}$ can thus be
written in the form
$$
W_{\text{add}} = E_{\text{i-m}} + W_{\text{geom}}.
$$

The practicability of considering energy $E_{\text{i-m}}$ was indirectly confirmed in [20–26].

At $r=0$ (the ion is in the center of a cluster of a new phase) and $W_{\text{add}} = W_{\text{geom}}$ ($E_{\text{i-m}}=0$), Eq. (5) should change to Eq. (1). We can easily establish that
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\text{geom}} =& \frac{4\pi}{3V} (\mu_{\text{old}} - \mu_{\text{new}}) R_{\text{ion}}^3 - 4\pi\sigma R_{\text{ion}}^2 \\
&- \frac{(Ze)^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R_{\text{ion}}} \left( \varepsilon_{\text{old}}^{-1} - \varepsilon_{\text{new}}^{-1} \right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Finding the energy $E_{\text{i-m}}$ is an separate research problem. It is obvious only that this energy depends on type of the ion, and on the type of the molecules of the compound undergoing the phase transition.

Equations (4) and (5) can be rewritten as
$$
\begin{gathered}
W_{\text{out}}=E_{\mathrm{s}}\left(-\frac{2}{3} y^{3}+y^{2}-\frac{p}{y} \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{new}} l}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{new}} l+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{old}}(l+1)}\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^{2(l+1)}\right), \\
z>y+u, \quad y>0,
\end{gathered}\tag{6}
$$
$$
\begin{gathered}
W_{\text{in}}=E_{\mathrm{s}}\left(-\frac{2}{3} y^{3}+y^{2}+\frac{p}{y} \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon_{\text {old }}(l+1)}{\varepsilon_{\text {new }} l+\varepsilon_{\text {old }}(l+1)}\left(\frac{z}{y}\right)^{2 l}+q_{0}\right), \\
z<y-u, \quad y>u.
\end{gathered}\tag{7}
$$

Here,
$$
\begin{gathered}
E_{\mathrm{s}}=4 \pi \sigma R_{0}^{2}, \quad p=\frac{(Z e)^{2}}{32 \pi^{2} \varepsilon_{0} \sigma R_{0}^{3}}\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{old}}^{-1}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{new}}^{-1}\right), \\
q_{0}=\frac{W_{\mathrm{add}}}{E_{\mathrm{s}}}, \quad y=\frac{R}{R_{0}}, \quad z=\frac{r}{R_{0}}, \quad u=\frac{R_{\mathrm{ion}}}{R_{0}},
\end{gathered}
$$
where $R_0$ is the critical radius of homogeneous nucleation (no ions). It was considered when writing
Eqs. (6) and (7) that $\frac{4\pi}{3V}(\mu_{\text{old}} - \mu_{\text{new}})R_0^3 = \frac{2}{3}4\pi\sigma R_0^2$.

If the ion is on the boundary of a spherical cluster with the old phase (Fig. 2c), it is difficult to find the work of formation of such a cluster explicitly. In [15], this value was approximated by the third-power polynomial
$$
\begin{gathered}
W_{\text {inter }}=a_{0}+a_{1} y+a_{2} y^{2}+a_{3} y^{3}, \\
y-u \leq z \leq y+u, \quad y>u,
\end{gathered}\tag{8}
$$
where the coefficients $a_0$, $a_1$, $a_2$, and $a_3$ are functions of coordinate $z$. These coefficients are found from the condition of continuity of the function for the work of formation of the cluster of the new phase and its first derivative over $y$ at $y=z+u$ and $y=z-u$:
$$
\left.W_{\text {in }}\right|_{y=z+u}=\left.W_{\text {inter }}\right|_{y=z+u},\tag{9}
$$
$$
\left.W_{\text {inter }}\right|_{y=z-u}=\left.W_{\text {out }}\right|_{y=z-u},\tag{10}
$$
$$
\left.\partial W_{\text {in }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z+u}=\left.\partial W_{\text {inter }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z+u},\tag{11}
$$
$$
\left.\partial W_{\text {inter }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z-u}=\left.\partial W_{\text {out }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z-u}.\tag{12}
$$

Solving system of Eqs. (9)–(12), we find that
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{3}=& \frac{1}{4 u^{2}}\left(\left.\partial W_{\text {in }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z+u}+\left.\partial W_{\text {out }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z-u}\right. \\
&\left.+\left.\frac{1}{u} W_{\text {out }}\right|_{y=z-u}-\left.\frac{1}{u} W_{\text {in }}\right|_{y=z+u}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
a_{2}=\frac{1}{4 u}\left(\left.\partial W_{\text {in }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z+u}-\left.\partial W_{\text {out }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z-u}\right)-3 z a_{3},
$$
$$
a_{1}=\left.\partial W_{\text {out }} / \partial y\right|_{y=z-u}-2(z-u) a_{2}-3(z-u)^{2} a_{3},
$$
$$
a_{0}=\left.W_{\text {out }}\right|_{y=z-u}-(z-u) a_{1}-(z-u)^{2} a_{2}-(z-u)^{3} a_{3}.
$$

Considering Eqs. (6) and (7), it is easy to obtain explicit expressions for coefficients $a_0$, $a_1$, $a_2$, and $a_3$.

It was shown in [14] that the expression for the rate of nucleation in the presence of ions takes the form
$$
J=N_{1} n_{V} R_{0}^{2} j \int_{0}^{z_{\max }}\left(\int_{\delta}^{\infty} \exp \left(\frac{W(y, z)}{k T}\right) d y\right)^{-1} 4 \pi z^{2} d z, \quad(13)
$$
where $N_1$ is the concentration of single molecules in the old phase; $W(y, z)$ is the work of formation of the cluster of the new phase, given by Eqs. (6)–(8) depending on the region of its determination. The $z_{\max}$ and $\delta$ are given by
$$
z_{\max }=\frac{1}{R_{0}}\left(\frac{3}{4 \pi n}\right)^{1 / 3}, \quad \delta=\frac{1}{R_{0}}\left(\frac{3 V}{4 \pi}\right)^{1 / 3}.
$$

For the vapor–liquid phase transition,
$$
N_{1}=\frac{S P_{\text {eq }}}{k T}, \quad R_{0}=\frac{2 \sigma v}{k T \ln S}.
$$

Integration in Eq. (13) can be performed only numerically.

## COMPARING THE THEORIES

Experimental data from [27], in which the ion-induced nucleation of vapors of six different compounds was studied, were used in comparing the predictions of the theory of nucleation on ions and the theory of nucleation in the presence of ions. In [27], the critical supersaturation of vapor at different temperatures when the average ion concentration was $5.5 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ was determined experimentally for each investigated compound. The critical supersaturation of vapor $S_{\text{cr}}$ is observed when $J=1\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$. In addition, for comparison, Fig. 3 shows the calculated curves of homogeneous nucleation.

It was considered that each ion forms a stable ion associate: an ion surrounded by a “jacket” of molecules of the compound undergoing the phase transition. The electric charge of such an ion associate is equal to the electric charge of the ion. The existence of similar stable ion associates is indicated by the local minimum of function (1) for the vapor–liquid phase transition. Within this assumption, energy $E_{\text{i-im}} = 0$, and the expression for the $W_{\text{add}}$ takes the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\text{add}} =& \frac{4\pi}{3v} (\mu_{\text{old}} - \mu_{\text{new}}) R_{\text{min}}^3 - 4\pi\sigma R_{\text{min}}^2 \\
&- \frac{(Ze)^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R_{\text{min}}} \left( \varepsilon_{\text{old}}^{-1} - \varepsilon_{\text{new}}^{-1} \right).
\end{aligned}
$$

In addition, $u = R_{\text{min}}/R_0$. Here, radius $R_{\text{min}}$ determined from Eq. (2) denotes the radius of the ion associate.

## CONCLUSIONS

In this work, it was assumed that within the theory of nucleation in the presence of ion there are stable ion associates in the metastable phase. This assumption generally allowed us to consider nucleation that occurs not in the presence of ions but in the presence of ion associates. The validity of this assumption is con- firmed by the theory of nucleation in the presence of ions yielding better agreement with the experimental data for the vapor-liquid phase transition than the theory of nucleation on ions. We should note, however, that complete agreement between the experimental and calculated data is not observed in most cases. It is possible that discarding our assumption about the existence of stable ion associates and allowing for the energy of the local interaction between the ion and the surrounding molecules will improve the agreement between the experimental and calculated data obtained within the theory of nucleation in the pres- ence of ions.
