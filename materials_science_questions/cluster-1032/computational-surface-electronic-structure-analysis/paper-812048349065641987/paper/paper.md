# (0 0 1) Ideal-surface band structure for the series
of Cu-based $\boldsymbol{A}^{\mathrm{I}} \boldsymbol{B}^{\mathrm{III}} \boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ chalcopyrites

H. Tototzintle-Huitle$^{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b},*}$, R. Baquero$^{\mathrm{a}}$

$^{\mathrm{a}}$Department of Physics, Cinvestav del IPN, Apdo. Postal 14-740, 07000 México D.F., Mexico

## Abstract

We use the surface Green's function matching method and a tight-binding Hamiltonian to calculate the (0 0 1)-surface electronic band structure and local density of states for the series of Cu-based $A^{\mathrm{I}}B^{\mathrm{III}}C_{2}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ chalcopyrites. We find four surface states in the optical gap energy region of s-p character and three surface states in the conduction band region of p-character. We show the trend of different characteristics within the series and analyze their quantitative behavior. We did not find frontier-induced semi-infinite medium states of non-dispersive character in the studied range of energy within the valence band as we found in the case of the (1 1 2) surface electronic band structure for CuInSe$_2$.

## 1. Introduction

Semiconducting chalcopyrites are interesting for their non-linear optical properties and for their possible technological applications [1,2]. Chalcopyrites are used in processes such as chemisorption, catalysis and bioleaching [3], solar cells [4] and other applications [5,6]. Surfaces, interfaces, quantum wells and superlattices with chalcopyrites as one of the elements are of interest in this context.

At the basis of a proper description of these systems is an accurate enough calculation of the electronic band structure. The first calculation of the electronic band structure for the series of ternary Cu-based chalcopyrites was presented by Jaffe and Zunger [7]. It was calculated self-consistently within the density functional formalism. They present a generic diagram of the bulk electronic band structure for the Cu-based chalcopyrites. The electronic band structure of bulk CuInSe$_2$ and of its (1 1 2) surface has been studied later on by Rodríguez et al. [8] using the tight-binding (TB) method. They implemented a set of TB parameters for the series of chalcopyrites CuB$^{\mathrm{III}}$C$^{\mathrm{VI}}$ with B = Al, Ga, In and C = S, Se, Te that describe quite accurately the bulk electronic band structure and have been used successfully [9,10] as input to calculate the surface electronic band structure using the surface Green's function matching (SGFM) method [11]. Interfaces [12] and superlattices [13] can also be described successfully by this method.

Ab initio methods have been used to calculate the bulk electronic band structure although sometimes the semiconductor optical gap is not reproduced accurately. Recently these methods have been used successfully to study ferromagnetism of Mn-substituted Cu-based chalcopyrites [14]. The main interest of these works is related to spintronics. Mn-doped II-IV-V$_2$ chalcopyrites have been studied in Ref. [15]. Comparison of predicted ferromagnetic tendencies of Mn substituting the Ga site in II-V's and I-III-VI$_2$ chalcopyrites semiconductors is done in [16] and the site preference for Mn substitution in spintronic chalcopyrite semiconductors is explored in [17]. Other recent studies on chalcopyrites are on magnetic defects in a photovoltaic material (Fe-doped CuInSe$_2$) [18] and the

effect of hydrogen (H) on Cu-based chalcopyrites. Actually, for $CuGaSe_{2}$ it is found that H is a deep donor while for $CuInSe_{2}$ it is a shallow one. The conductivity can be changed from p-type to n-type either by introducing hydrogen to the sample as a dopant or by passivating the defects. The final result depends on details of the minimum of the conduction band (CB) of the chalcopyrite and on stoichiometry [19]. Ab initio methods have also been used to study other bulk properties as intrinsic point defects in $CuInSe_{2}$ [20], anion vacancies as a source of persistent photoconductivity in $CuGaSe_{2}$ and $CuInSe_{2}$ [21], and to establish the existence of an energetic barrier for holes arriving from the grain interior to the grain boundaries in $CuInSe_{2}$ [22].

There are in the literature several other studies on chalcopyrites of the type $A^{I}B^{III}C_{2}^{VI}$. The stability of this crystal phase has been studied and its possible coexistence with a phase of CuAu-type has been predicted in some cases [23]. Structural and dynamic properties of the lattice of some chalcopyrites using the perturbation method to the density functional are presented in Ref. [24]. They calculate the static- and high-frequency dielectric tensor and get a good agreement with infrared and Raman experimental results. Order-disorder transitions can be induced by doping the sample with a magnetic ion as Mn to a dilute magnetic semiconductor with a slightly modified crystal structure [25].

Ref. [26] deals with a systematic treatment of the stability as a function of the concentration of the components in $ZnGeAs_{2}/GaAs$ in the $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ direction. The effect of anion and tetragonal distortions on the $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 2\end{array})$ surface has been recently studied for a series of Cu-based chalcopyrites by Tototzintle et al. [27]. For $CuInSe_{2}$, the electronic states of the ideal surfaces $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ and $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 2\end{array})$ have been calculated by Rodriguez et al. [28], the reconstruction of the polar $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 2\end{array})$ and $(\begin{array}{lll}\overline{1} & \overline{1} & \overline{2}\end{array})$ surfaces versus the non-polar $(\begin{array}{lll}2 & 2 & 0\end{array})$ one has been studied by Zhang and Wei [29]. Also the defect induced non-polar to polar transition at the $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 2\end{array})$ surface is considered in Ref. [30]. On the basis of atomic chemical potentials, they show that the $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 2\end{array})$ surface is the more stable. The mechanism of linear and non-linear optical effects of $AgGaX_{2}(X=S,Se,Te)$ has been recently studied by Bai et al. [31].

Recent experiments on Cu-based chalcopyrites include the study of the influence of hydrogen [32], Raman [33], atomic defects and non-stoichiometry [34] and muon charge states [35] all in $CuInSe_{2}$; quasi real-time Raman studies on the growth of Cu-In-S thin films [36] and temperature-dependent Hall measurements of intrinsic defects in $CuGaSe_{2}$ [37]. No extensive calculation on the $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ surface for the series we deal with in this work is known to us.

In this paper we use the TB Hamiltonian together with the parameters previously constructed [8-10] and the SGFM method [11] to study the electronic band structure of the ideal $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ surface for the series of the Cu-based chalcopyrites $CuB^{III}C_{2}^{VI}$ where $B=Al,Ga,In$; $C=S,Se,Te$. The method has been proved accurate enough to describe other systems as II-VI [38,39] and III-V zincblende [40] and wurzite [9] semiconductors. The SGFM method [11] starts with the bulk TB parameters as input and takes care by itself for the proper matching condition that describes the surface. We include in the next section a brief description of the SGFM method for completeness [11,41,42].

## 2. The formalism

### 2.1. The SGFM method

We have first calculated the bulk band structure of the compounds by the TB method in the Slater- Koster language using an orthogonal basis of orbitals [43]. For chalcopyrites it is calculated using one s- and three p-like atomic functions for each $B=Al,Ga,In$; $C=S,Se,Te$ and nine atomic functions (one s-, three p-, and five d-like) for each Cu. The unit cell contains eight atoms (two Cu, two B's and four C's). Since we have not included the effect of the spin-orbit (SO) interaction, the dimension of the Hamiltonian is $42 \times 42$. A very detailed construction of the TB Hamiltonian can be consulted in Ref. [10]. It reproduces the known bulk band structure calculations for all the series of chalcopyrites studied here. In particular, the experimental gap is reproduced for the whole series [8]. To calculate the Green's function for the free surface, we assume ideal truncation. The general equation for the Green's function can be written as:
$$(w I-H) G=I,\qquad(1)$$
where $w$ is the energy eigenvalue, $H$ the Hamiltonian and $I$ the unit matrix. To construct the TB Hamiltonian, we adopt the customary description in terms of principal layers. We label them with positive numbers and zero for the surface principal layer. Atomic layers are labelled with negative numbers and zero for the atomic surface layer. Let $|n\rangle$ be the wave function describing the $n$th principal layer. It is a LCAO wave function calculated on the surface Brillouin zone showed in Fig. 1a using the same basis of orbitals as for the bulk. If we take matrix elements of Eq. (1) in the Hilbert space generated by the complete set of the wave functions $|n\rangle$, we get
$$\langle n|(w I-H) G| m\rangle=\delta_{m n}.\qquad(2)$$

Since there are only nearest-neighbor interactions between principal layers, by definition, $H_{m,m+i}=0$ for $i \geqslant 2$ and the identity operator for the $n$th principal layer can be expressed as
$$I=|n-1\rangle\langle n-1|+| n\rangle\langle n|+| n+1\rangle\langle n+1|.\qquad(3)$$

By inserting Eq. (3) into Eq. (2) we get
$$\begin{aligned}
& \langle n|(w I-H)| n-1\rangle\langle n-1|G| m\rangle+\langle n|(w I-H)| n\rangle\langle n|G| m\rangle \\
& \quad+\langle n|(w I-H)| n+1\rangle\langle n+1|G| m\rangle=\delta_{m n},
\end{aligned}\qquad(4)$$

which we rewrite as

$$
H_{n,n-1}G_{n-1m}+(wI-H_{n,n})G_{n,m}-H_{n,n+1}G_{n+1,m}=\delta_{mn}. \tag{5}
$$

Using Eq. (5) for $n=m$, and $m=0$ for the surface, it is straightforward to get the surface Green's function

$$
G_{\mathrm{S}}^{-1}=(wI-H_{00})-H_{01}T, \tag{6}
$$

and the principal layer projected bulk Green's function

$$
G_{\mathrm{b}}^{-1}=G_{\mathrm{S}}^{-1}-H_{10}\tilde{T}. \tag{7}
$$

The transfer matrices, $T$ and $\tilde{T}$ are defined as

$$
G_{k+1p}=TG_{kp},\quad k\geqslant p\geqslant0, \tag{8}
$$

$$
G_{ij+1}=\tilde{T}G_{ij},\quad j\geqslant i\geqslant0. \tag{9}
$$

These matrices can be calculated using the algorithm discussed in [44].

In the case of the (0 0 1) surface of a chalcopyrite, we use a principal layer formed by eight atomic planes to take into account of all the interactions of the crystal. Then the matrix elements that appear in the Hamiltonian, $H_{mm}$, are $8\times8$ supermatrices with $42\times42$ matrices as their elements. For example,
$H_{00}$ and $H_{01}$ are

$$

where $h_{00}$, for example, is a matrix labelled by the atomic functions that form the basis of the eight atoms in the unit cell. We use 18 atomic orbitals for the Cu atoms, 8 and 16 for the B and C atoms, respectively. These $42\times42$ matrices are readily calculated in the TB approximation. To construct these matrices, we shall adopt the hypothesis of an ideal, non-reconstructed surface. From

the knowledge of the Green's function, the surface states and the surface-induced bulk states can be calculated from the poles of the real part of the corresponding Green's function.

### 2.2. Geometry of the surface

There are several theoretical and experimental works on the surface electronic structure of $\mathrm{CuB}^{\mathrm{III}} \mathrm{C}_{2}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ chalcopyrites. They are concerned mainly with the $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 2\end{array})$ surface [29,30]; less work is available on the $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ and the $(\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 0\end{array})$ surfaces [45]. These results show that the reconstruction and the relaxation of the surface atoms in chalcopyrites depend on the kind of atoms involved. Different geometries are obtained, e.g. $(\begin{array}{lll}4 & \times & 1\end{array})$ for $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array}) \mathrm{CuGaSe}_{2}$ and $(\begin{array}{lll}4 & \times & 2\end{array})$ for (001) $\mathrm{CuInSe}_{2}$. So, as a first approach to the general characteristics of the $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ chalcopyrite surfaces, we take the ideal case, i.e. $c=2 a$ and with $\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ atom at the center of the tetrahedron surrounded by two $\mathrm{Cu}$ and two $\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{III}}$ atoms [8]. The geometry of the $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ ideal chalcopyrite surface used in the calculation is shown in Fig. 1b. The bulk crystal structure and the surface Brillouin zone are illustrated in Fig. 1 for completeness.

## 3. The (001) bulk-projected bands for the series $\mathrm{CuB}^{\mathrm{III}} \mathrm{C}_{2}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ where $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{A l}, \mathbf{G a}, \mathbf{I n} ; \mathbf{C}=\mathbf{S}, \mathbf{S e}, \mathbf{T e}$

Before presenting the electronic density of states for the (001) ideal surface, we will describe the general trends in the $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$ bulk-projected electronic band structure for the whole series. A generic diagram emerges from the bulk electronic band structure similar to the one elaborated previously by Jaffe and Zunger [7]. Using the SGFM method [11] we calculate the (001)-surface Green's function to obtain the band structure shown in Figs. 2-4. The shaded areas are the $(\begin{array}{lll}0 & 0 & 1\end{array})$-projected bulk bands. The origin is at the top of the valence band. Notice that we have drawn the bands from -6 to $+10 \mathrm{eV}$. The bands are drawn through the high-symmetry points $X-\Gamma-M$ in the irreducible part of the two-dimensional first Brillouin zone (2D-FBZ). In the range of energy that we have considered for this calculation, the valence band is characterized by a lens at the top at $\Gamma$. It gets wider as the $\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{VI}}$-anion gets heavier. The width of the upper valence band (UVB) shrinks as the mass of the $\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{III}}$ cation enhances. For $\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{III}}=\mathrm{In}$ the small gap that separatesthe UVB from the intermediate valence band (IVB) appears in the region around $-5 \mathrm{eV}$. It is evident that the UVB-width shrinks also with a bigger $\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{VI}}$-anion mass as the optical gap does for the whole series. This behavior can be appropriately explained in terms of the p-d repulsion effect enhanced in $\mathrm{CuB}^{\mathrm{III}} \mathrm{C}_{2}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ chalcopyrites compared with its binary compounds counterpart. The Cu-d character present in the UVB, interacts with the p-like character of the lowest CB, contributed by the $\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{III}}$ cation and by the $\mathrm{C}^{\mathrm{VI}}$ anion, defining a direct gap at $\Gamma$. This interaction depends inversely on the energy separation between the $\mathrm{Cu} 3 \mathrm{~d}$ orbitals and the anion p orbitals. This repulsive interaction pushes the UVB to higher energies, reducing the band gap whenever the cation or the anion is changed by a heavier one [7]. This is the main effect that explains the reduction of the bandgaps found in the valence band region. In the CB energy region three sub-bands are apparent, separated by inner gaps. The gap around $7 \mathrm{eV}$ is of particular interest as surface states appear within this energy region. We will comment further on the behavior of these states below.

The $B^{III}-C^{VI}$ bond is mostly covalent, while the $Cu-C^{VI}$ one has a large ionic character.

Nevertheless these two states remain well within the gap for the whole series and almost at the middle of this energy interval. That is to say that the gap also shrinks as the mass of the anion gets heavier for the same cations in the chalcopyrite. Also as the mass of the $\text{B}^\text{III}$ cation is heavier, the gap decreases and the distance between the states $E_1$ and $E_2$ and the top of the valence band is shorter in energy. As the mass of the anion enhances, a reduction of the ionic character of the surface states (dipoles) manifests itself. This is due to the fact that the covalent bonds between the cation $\text{Cu}$ and anion $\text{C}^\text{VI}$ get stronger lowering the energy of the surface states, a trend that is reflected in the calculations presented here. These features are universal and can be appreciated quantitatively in Table 1 where we present the energy of the four states within the optical gap at three high-symmetry points in the 2D-FBZ, namely, $\Gamma$, $X$ and $M$.

Furthermore, the states $E_3$ and $E_4$ lie very close to the bottom of the CB and remain as truly surface states for the whole series except for $\text{CuInSe}_2$. There the two states become a resonance around $\Gamma$. The surface state $E_4$ enters into the CB at $X$ and $M$ for the three chalcopyrites $\text{CuAlC}_2^\text{VI}$ concerned with here.

The composition of the surface states $E_1$ and $E_2$ is mainly of s and p character. We also find a small contribution (less than 3%) from states of d-symmetry. This contribution decreases as the anion mass gets heavier while at the same time the p-character of the state increases and its s-character diminishes. The surface states $E_3$ and $E_4$ are more of s-character but its p-contribution enhances as the anion mass gets heavier. The d-contribution to this two states is very small or absent at all. We quote the composition of these states for some cases in Table 2.

Notice that as the mass of the cation ($\text{B}^\text{III}$) gets heavier a lens develops around $\Gamma$ at the bottom of the CB. Of special interest in this context is $\text{CuInSe}_2$ since the two states $E_3$ and $E_4$ lie in the lens space. For $\text{CuInTe}_2$ we find the $E_4$ state in the lens while the $E_3$ state is not.

### 4.2. States in the CB-gap energy region

Above the optical gap, within 10 eV from the top of the valence band (the origin), there are two gaps in the CB. They do not shrink very much with the enhancement of the anion mass conserving the cation ($\text{B}^\text{III}$) fixed but the whole bulk band structure moves downwards to the origin so that the net effect is that the optical gap diminishes as we already mentioned above. As a consequence a third small gap appears around 10 eV. Notice also the displacement of the structure towards lower energies as the anion gets heavier for fixed cations. Three new surface states develop in this range of energy, namely, $E_5, E_6$, and $E_7$. The energy at which these states occur is smaller as the anion mass or the cation $\text{B}^\text{III}$ gets heavier. We have presented Table 3 to report on the energy at which these states appear.

In the series $\text{CuAlC}_2^\text{VI}$ the state $E_5$ lies in the bulk CB for the whole interval $X-\Gamma-M$ for $\text{C}^\text{VI}=\text{S}$, emerges from the bulk band energies for $\text{C}^\text{VI}=\text{Se}$ and is neatly detached from the bulk CB for $\text{C}^\text{VI}=\text{Te}$. $E_6$ is a resonance around $\Gamma$ but emerges as a pure surface state around $X$ and $M$ for the first element of this sub-series but is a pure surface state for the other two. $E_7$ remains as a pure surface state in the whole branch of the 2D-FBZ reported here for the three elements.

In the series $\text{CuGaC}_2^\text{VI}$ the behavior of the three surface states $E_5, E_6$, and $E_7$ is approximately the same as for the $\text{CuAlC}_2^\text{VI}$ one.

The series $CuInC_{2}^{VI}$ presents a different behavior. The state $E_{5}$ lies in the upper border of a lens that develops in the second inner gap in the CB and it is therefore to be considered as a resonance for $C^{VI}=S$. Also the state $E_{6}$ behaves specially. In $X-\Gamma-M$, it is a resonance in almost the whole interval (see Fig. 4 for details) for $C^{VI}=S,Se$. But for $C^{VI}=Te$ it emerges as a surface state in the whole interval presented here. $E_{7}$ remains essentially as a pure surface state in $X-\Gamma-M$ for the whole series. Finally, two more surface states emerge in the energy region under study (less than 10 eV) as a consequence of the displacement towards lower energies of the bulk bands (shadowed areas in Figs. 2-4) as the mass of the $B^{III}$ cation and of the anion gets heavier, a new third gap appears in the CB below 10 eV. This is apparent in $CuAlTe_{2}$ in Fig. 2. Te is the heavier anion of the sub-series CuAl. In the second series CuGa in Fig. 3, the third gap in the CB is seen for the anion Se at around 10 eV and appears around 9 eV for the anion Te and moves towards 8 eV for $CuInTe_{2}$. The energy at $\Gamma$ for some of these states is presented in Table 4. The composition at $\Gamma$ for both the states that appear at this energy is almost exclusively of p-orbitals, with a very minor contribution from s-orbitals and a tiny one from d-orbitals.

### 5. The (0 0 1) ideal surface local density of states for the series of chalcopyrites $CuB^{III}C_{2}^{VI}$ ($B=Al,Ga,In$; $C=S,Se,Te$)

In Fig. 5, we present the (0 0 1) surface local density of states (LDOS) for the series of chalcopyrites studied here. The points indicate the optical gap. The peaks in the optical gap indicate the spectral weight of the surface states in the optical gap energy region, namely $E_{1}$, $E_{2}$, $E_{3}$, and $E_{4}$.

The CB presents two minima associated with the two inner gaps that we have presented above. Also its evolution with the anion and $B^{III}$ cation masses is apparent. The shrinking of the optical gap as the anion mass, on one hand, and the cation one, on the other, increases is clearly seen in this figure as well. Between $-6$ and $-8$ eV the LDOS has the highest spectral weight, a common feature to all the nine Cu-based chalcopyrites due to the contribution of d-Cu orbitals. Notice that this peak becomes thinner and higher as the mass of the $B^{III}$ cation enhances. The LDOS at this energy presents two peaks for $C^{VI}=S,Se$ and only one for $C^{VI}=Te$.

To the best of our knowledge, there is no experimental work on the electronic surface states in chalcopyrites to compare our calculations with. One of the most recent works is Ref. [45], where the method to obtain the reconstruction and relaxation of a (1 0 0) chalcopyrite surface is presented in detail, but no electronic properties are measured. In [46], a detailed analysis of the surface properties of the (1 1 1), (0 0 1), and (1 1 0) epitaxial $CuInS_{2}$ films grown on Si is presented and the DOS is given that is in agreement with the one presented in Fig. 5. Further a resonance state at $-3.3$ eV below the top of the valence band is presented which does not appear in our calculation. This could be a state that originates at the interface of the film [47]. Later work in this direction might help to clarify this situation.

### 6. FISIM states

Non-dispersive states have been experimentally found in (0 0 1) CdTe [48]. These states were described as bulk states induced by the presence of a surface [38]. It was found that these states are more general and they were predicted to exist at interfaces as well. The states were consequently suggested to be named frontier-induced semi-infinite medium (FISIM) states [49]. In these papers, the FISIM states are shown to be built from spectral weight that accumulates at a certain fixed energy from states with the same crystal momentum $k$ and therefore their occurrence does not violate the conservation of the number of states when integrating in the energy interval of the whole valence band at constant $k$. A detailed study of the valence-band electronic structure for ZnSe (0 0 1) is presented in Ref.

### 7. Conclusions

We presented in this work a first study of the general characteristics of the (100) surface for the whole series of Cu-based chalcopyritas. Since reconstruction is specific almost to each member of the series, a preliminary general trend can be easily studied by taking the surfaces as ideal for the whole series. A further calculation should deal with the specific reconstructed surfaces for each particular case. We have used the SGFM method and a TB Hamiltonian to calculate the ideal (001)-surface electronic band structure and the local projected density of states (LDOS) for the series of Cu-based chalcopyrites $\mathrm{CuB^{III}C_2^{VI}}$ (B = Al, Ga, In; C = S, Se, Te). A TB description allowed us to get the experimental gap for each member of the series [8]. The general characteristics are similar although important differences occur in the bands and in the LDOS as well. Within the optical gap energy region we find four surface states of mainly s-p character and in the conduction band (CB) region we find three surface states of predominantly p-character. The bulk-projected electronic bands evolve in the series as the mass of the anion, on one side, and of the $\mathrm{B^{III}}$ cation on the other, gets heavier, moving the CB as a whole towards lower energies. We found two consequences of this fact. First, the optical gap shrinks and second a third inner gap appears in the higher energy region of the

CB. Two new surface states of predominantly p-character exists in this new gap for the chalcopyrites that do show this third inner gap within 10eV from the origin. Presumably the gap is a common fact to the whole series but lies at higher energies in the rest of the members of the series. Finally, we did not find FISIM states for the (0 0 1) surface band structure as it was found for the (1 1 2) one previously for CuInSe₂ [49].
