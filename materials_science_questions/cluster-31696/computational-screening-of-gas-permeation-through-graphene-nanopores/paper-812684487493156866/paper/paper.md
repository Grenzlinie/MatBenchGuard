# A novel T-$C_3$N and seawater desalination

Jia-Qi Zhou$^{a}$, Lei Li$^{a}$, Cong Fu$^{b,c}$, Jian Wang$^{a}$, Peng Fu$^{a,d}$, Chui-Peng Kong$^{a}$, Fu-Quan Bai$^{a}$, Roberts I. Eglitis$^{d}$, Hong-Xing Zhang$^{a,*}$, Ran Jia$^{a,d,*}$

$^{b}$Key Laboratory of Organ Regeneration & Transplantation of the Ministry of Education, The First Hospotal of Jilin University, 130061 Changchun, PR China
$^{c}$National-Local Joint Engineering Laboratory of Animal Models for Human Diseases, 130061 Changchun, PR China

## Abstract
A structurally stable stacked multilayer carbonitride is predicted with the aid of ab-initio calculations. This carbonitride consists of $C_3$N tetrahedrons, which is similar to T-carbon and thus coined as T-$C_3$N. Its 2-dimensional (2D) monolayer is also carefully investigated in this work. The studies on electronic properties reveal that bulk and 2D T-$C_3$N are insulators with a 5.542 eV indirect band gap and a 5.741 eV direct band gap, respectively. However, the monolayer T-$C_3$N exhibits an excellently uniform porosity. Its 5.50 Å pore size is perfect for the water nanofiltration. The adsorption and permeation of water molecules on the monolayer T-$C_3$N are investigated. Its promising potential application in highly efficient nanofiltration membrane for seawater desalination is discussed.

Keywords: Carbonitride, T-$C_3$N, Monolayer, Water Nanofiltration.

### 1. Introduction

Due to the special electronic configuration, carbon atoms can be combined into abundant allotropes[1]. Graphene is the most remarkable allotrope at present[2, 3, 4, 5]. Its synthesis inspires a new wave of searching novel carbon allotropes. Some of theoretically predicted carbon allotropes have been successfully synthesized. One most recent success is the theoretical prediction[6] and thereafter experimental synthesis[7] of the so-called T-carbon. Su and co-workers first predicted a structurally stable carbon allotrope with the aid of first-principle calculations. This new allotrope has actually the same symmetry configuration as cubic diamond, but a carbon tetrahedron substitutes each original single carbon atom. The advantages of T-carbon are the extraordinary mechanical properties and ultra light weight due to its high porosity. Additionally, T-carbon is a semiconductor with direct band gap around 3.0 eV according to their theoretical prediction, which brings the promising application potentials in photo-catalysis and energy conversion field. Cui and co-workers predicted the mechanical and dynamical properties of T-carbon as well as other T-carbon like structures by using ab-initio calculations[8]. Recently, Zhang and co-works reported their synthesis results for T-carbon nanowire from pseudo-topotactic conversion of a multi-walled carbon nanotube suspension[7]. Most recently, Sun and co-workers suggested that T-carbon could be employed in perovskite solar cells as electron transport material owing to its direct band gap and high carrier mobility[9]. Su and co-worker also explored the application prospects in hydrogen storage and lithium ion batteries this year[10].

As we all known, doping generally is an effective way to modify the band

structure of a semiconductor[11]. However, a recent research revealed that the p- or n-doping with nitrogen (N) or boron (B) atom in T-carbon will completely destroy its lattice dynamic stability[12]. Surprisingly, a novel carbonitride T-$C_3$N can be obtained from a heavily N doped T-carbon system with energetic, lattice dynamic and thermal stabilities, which will be introduced in present work. This new carbonitride possesses a stacked multilayer structure composed of $C_3$N tetrahedrons. Its monolayer system aroused our great interest due to its promising application potential in water nanofiltration. Freshwater shortage is a global problem at present and even in the long future because of rapid population growth and fast growing industry demands. As formidable challenge, waste water treatments and seawater desalination are of vital importance for humanity survival and development. Graphene nanomaterials, as building blocks of ultrathin membranes, are the most popular research objects for water desalination and purification[13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 19, 20, 21]. The pristine graphene is of course not permeable, even for the smallest molecule helium. Thus, introducing defects on graphene becomes an essential processing step, e.g. $O_2$ plasma-etching. The average size, density, and distribution of the defects (or rather pores) are the key factors for the water nanofiltration. Yet, relative large defects and high defect density will significantly reduce the mechanical strength and durability of the membranes. Another very important point is that the biotoxicity of graphene nanosheet has already been confirmed[24, 25, 26, 27].

The monolayer T-$C_3$N exhibits extraordinary mechanical strength, although its surface pore density is extremely high. The inherent pore size and

uniform pore distribution, as well as the thinness of the monolayer imply that T-C₃N should possess an excellent water permeance. These properties just meet all the requirements for an excellent water treatment membrane[13]. We will explore its permeance in detail in the main text. The present work is organized as follows: The simulation methods and computation details are introduced in Section 2. Right after, the stability, geometric and electronic configurations of the bulk T-C₃N are discussed. Subsequently, the geometric and electronic properties of the monolayer T-C₃N are presented. As an exploratory study, the water permeance of the monolayer T-C₃N is reported in the last subsection. A summary of the conclusions can be found at the end of this work.

## 2. Computation details

In present work, the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional[28] is employed for the geometry optimization and electronic structure investigation. In order to gain a relatively accurate band gap, the global hybrid density functional B3LYP[29, 30] is also taken into account. The all-electron Gaussian basis sets Triple-Zeta Valence with Polarization (TZVP)[31] are adopted for all involved species in this research. The simulations are performed within the computer code CRYSTAL17[32, 33]. The sampling Monkhorst-Pack (M-P) k-points grids are set to be 16×16×16 and 16×16×1 for the integrations in the 3D and 2D first Brillouin zones, respectively. These MP meshes are dense enough for the calculations of a semiconductor or insulator. To fix the lack of the omnipresent attractive London dispersion interaction (i.e., the attractive part of the van der Waals force), especially in stacked multilayer systems and

molecule adsorption studies, the D3 London dispersion correction[34] is also adopted in our investigations. During a simulation of 2D system, a 500 Å vacuum space in the non-periodic direction is automatically imposed by the code CRYSTAL17 to avoid interactions with its own image in adjacent cells. A 3×3×3 or 3×3×1 supercell is established to evaluate the phonon disper- sion in the first Brillouin zones of the 3D or 2D system, respectively. The pristine T-carbon and cubic diamond are first studied to prove the reliability of the simulation method and settings. The results of the geometric con- figurations and the electronic structures are all in good agreement with the literature reports. Detailed results and comparisons with other theoretical and experimental works can be found in Supporting Information.

The crystal orbital overlap population (COOP) analysis can offer a better understanding of the interactions between two selected groups of atoms (e.g., group A: the N dopants, and group B: the other C atoms) in our research object. With the help of this method, the bonding and anti-bonding states, as well as bond orders can be intuitively exhibited in a diagram. The math- ematical form of the COOP method is similar to the electronic density of states (DOS):

$$
COOP(\epsilon)=\frac{2}{V} \sum_{j} \sum_{\mu \in A} \sum_{\nu \in B} \int_{V} S_{\mu \nu}(\vec{k}) a_{\mu j}^{*}(\vec{k}) a_{\nu j}(\vec{k}) \delta\left(\epsilon-\epsilon_{j}(\vec{k})\right) \mathrm{d} \vec{k}, \quad(1)
$$

where $a_{\mu j}$ is the Bloch coefficients used to define the crystalline wavefunctions, and $\epsilon$ is energy. Note that the integration is performed in the first Brillouin zone. Therefore, $V$ represents the volume of the first Brillouin zone, and $\vec{k}$ is the reciprocal space vector. In the integration, the symbol $\hat{S}$ represents the overlap matrix. More theoretical details can be found in Ref. [35].

In order to study the water molecule migration through a pore on the involved monolayer system, the so-called distinguished reaction coordinate (DRC) method is employed. DRC method defines the transition state (TS) in two steps: First, DRC calculation will find a structure close to the real TS; Then, this structure will be refined to locate the actual TS. The simulations for the water molecule and clusters adsorption, as well as searching TS are carried out in a 3×3×1 supercell.

Some ab-initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations are also performed to validate the thermal stabilities of the predicted novel material systems. The AIMD simulations are carried out in canonic ensembles at 1000 K for 20 ps with the aid of the computer code CASTEP[36]. The AIMD simulations are also at PBE level with the norm-conserving planewave pseudopotential. The energy cutoff is set to be 770 eV. The k-grids in AIMD simulations are 6×6×6 and 6×6×1 for different periodic conditions.

Adaptive steered molecular dynamics (ASMD) simulations[37, 38, 39] are carried out using AMBER (version 11) software package[40] with the TIP3P water model[41] and ff99SB force field[42] for the rest species in a cubic box with periodic boundary conditions. The ASMD simulations provide detailed mapping of the force profiles along the reaction coordinate for the permeation processes of water and ions. The overall reaction coordinate is divided into several smaller stages. The potentials mean of force (PMFs) for water and ions are acquired through ASMD trajectories utilizing Jarzynski's equality[43]. The pulling velocity $v$ and the spring constant $k$ are set to be 4 $\mathrm{\AA}/\mathrm{ns}$ and $20\ \mathrm{kcal{\cdot}mol^{-1}{\cdot}\AA^{-2}}$, respectively. The trajectories are divided into 8 stages. Three independent simulations are repeated in each stage. Totally,

for each system, 12 ns is needed for the entirely pulling process during an ASMD simulation.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. N-doping in T-carbon
As a starting point, the B/N doping in T-carbon are carefully tested. The substitution of one carbon atom in a T-carbon supercell by a B or N atom will lead to an unstable doping system according to the corresponding phonon dispersion map, if the doping concentration is controlled between 1.56% and 6.25%. The corresponding geometric configurations and phonon maps can be found in Support Information. This outcome is consistent with the literature report[12]. However, we have to admit that the system with even lower concentration is still untested. Therefore, we can't guarantee its stability with extremely low B/N doping concentration. The first interesting thing is that when the N doping concentration is further increased to 12.5% (N:C=1:7), the imaginary frequency in its phonon spectrum disappears. Yet this heavily N doped system cannot maintain the original geometric structure after full geometric relaxation. It is converted into a graphite-like stacked structure, as shown in Fig. 1. The contained monolayer is actually the N doped $\alpha$-graphyne (see Fig. 1(c)). This may be a way to synthesize the theoretical predicted $\alpha$-graphyne. Although this is not the keynote of this study, it drives us to further increase the N doping concentration in T-carbon.

If two carbon atoms in a primitive cell of T-carbon are substituted by N atoms, the doping concentration is increased to 25% (N:C=2:6). After full

Even in this stacking form, the coherence energy of T-$C_3$N is still negative (-8.248 eV/atom) according to its definition:

$$
E_{coh} = \frac{1}{a + b} \left(E_{tot} - aE_{C} - bE_{N}\right), \tag{2}
$$

where $E_{tot}$, $E_{C}$, and $E_{N}$ represent the total energy of a system, the energies of single isolated C and N atoms, respectively. The factors $a$ and $b$ are the numbers of C and N atoms containing in the system, respectively. Due to heavily N doping in T-carbon, the spatial symmetry is significantly changed.

The labels of the space groups are listed in Table 1 for different stacking forms. Four stacking forms for T-$C_3$N are taken into account as shown in Fig. 3. Continuous this translation will repeat the labels $A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow A$. The names of the stacking forms are according to this naming rules in this work. The energy differences point out that the ACB stacking form possesses the lowest total energy, so the T-$C_3$N system in this form has the advantage of the interlayer stability. The maximum energy difference is about 89 meV/atom between ABC and ACB stacking. The minimum energy difference appears between the AC and ACB stacked T-$C_3$N, which is still around 41 meV/atom. The energy differences are all much higher than that between different stacked graphite and h-NB[44, 45, 46], which means that the ACB stacking form will predominate the experimental observation of a 3D T-$C_3$N, and the interlayer sliding will not be so easy like in graphite.

The C−C and C−N bonds in the tetrahedron are 1.459 Å and 1.516 Å, respectively. The inter-tetrahedron C−C bond length is 1.411 Å which is slightly shorter than the former ones. According to our calculation at the same level, the intra- and inter-tetrahedron C−C bond lengths in pristine T-carbon are 1.492 Å and 1.408 Å, respectively. As one can see, the inter-tetrahedron C−C remains almost unchanged, while there are marked changes on the intra-tetrahedron bonds. The shortest distance between two N atoms in two separated layers is 3.117 Å. However, the interlayer distance is only 0.510 Å, because the layers interlace with each other, and almost stick together like LEGO blocks. The pore size (diameter) in the individual layer is 5.510 Å. This size is very suitable for seawater desalination[13], which also arouses our strong interest. The mechanical parameters of the stacked T-C₃N bulk are listed in Table 1. With the aid of the Voigt-Reuss-Hill averaging scheme,

the Young's, bulk, and shear moduli are computed from the elastic constants,
which are 73.93, 43.54, and 109.18 GPa, respectively. The Young's and shear
moduli are not significantly smaller than the pristine T-carbon, while the
bulk modulus is smaller than the half of the T-carbon owing to the lack of
interlayer bonding and the change of the orbital hybridization between the
intra-tetrahedron N and C atoms which will be discussed later.

The calculated electron energy band map at PBE level is illustrated in
Fig. 5(a). This heavily N doping results in an indirect energy gap, which
is 3.876 eV lying between the valence band maximum (VBM) at $H$ point
and the conduction band minimum (BCM) at $\Gamma$ point in its first Brillouin
zone. However, the direct band gap between the valence band (VB) and
conduction band (CB) at $\Gamma$ point is 3.894 eV, which is only 18 meV higher
than the indirect one. Note that the calculation of the electronic structure is
still at PBE-D3 level. Therefore, this band gap is apparently underestimated
according to common sense, which means that the 3D T-$C_3$N is likely to be an
insulator. A B3LYP-D3 calculation provides a 5.542 eV indirect band gap in
this situation. Of course, it is worth carrying out a more accurate and reliable
GW, even GW-BSE, investigation in the further study in order to get a
more elaborate electronic structure for T-$C_3$N. For newly designed materials,
some excellent electronic properties are always expected for its promising
applications as photocatalysts or photoelectric materials, etc. Even though
T-$C_3$N is a semiconductor with large band gap or just insulator as shown
above, as a new material, its electronic structure also deserves to be clearly
shown here.

In Fig. 5(b), the DOS map reveals the orbital hybridizations of N and

C atoms near VBM and BCM. In order to further discern the nature of the electronic interaction between the neighboring N and C atoms, the COOP method[35] is employed to gather the information about the bonding and anti-bonding components of a C−N bond. The solid state COOP diagrams for the bonds in T-C₃N are illustrated in Fig. 5(c). The positive and negative COOP values represent bonding and anti-bonding over the energy range, respectively. As shown in Fig. 5(c), the positive areas (bonded attractions in Valence-Bond (VB) theory[47]) are still dominant between C atoms, while the negative (non bonded repulsions in VB theory) and positive areas are nearly equivalent on C−N and N⋅⋅⋅N. The bond orders of the intra- and inter-tetrahedron C−C bonds obtained from the integrated COOP curve over the occupied energy range are 0.104 and 0.230 which are significantly smaller than 0.456 and 0.474 for C−C bonds in pristine T-carbon, respectively. Meanwhile, the calculated bond order for C−N bond in T-C₃N is extremely close to zero, which indicates that there is no covalence bond caused by orbital overlap between the neighboring C and N atoms, but a typical ionic bond. The Mulliken population analysis confirms this result, wherefrom the effective charges on N and C atoms are -0.741 e and 0.247 e, respectively. For reference, the COOP diagrams for the intra- and inter-tetrahedron C−C bonds in the pristine T-carbon are illustrated in Fig. 5(d). The strong changes in the chemical bonds are an important factor of the decline in mechanic properties of the T-C₃N system.

The total energies per atom as functions of the lattice constants per atom are plotted in Fig. 7 for monolayer T-$C_3$N, graphene, graphene like $C_3$N (g-$C_3$N), graphdiyne, $\alpha$- and $\gamma$-graphyne. There is a single minimum on the curve for monolayer T-$C_3$N, indicating that the structure would be stable. Its minimum total energy per atom is higher than the other typical 2D carbon materials, e.g. graphene and g-$C_3$N. Graphene, as shown in Fig. 7, possesses the lowest minimum total energy per atom. Note that graphdiyne also bears a relative higher minimum total energy. However, graphdiyne has already been successfully synthesised[48]. According to our calculation at PBE-D3 level, the minimum total energy of monolayer T-$C_3$N is -8.245 eV/atom, suggesting that monolayer T-$C_3$N could be a thermodynamically metastable phase against g-$C_3$N[49]. The recent predicted monolayer materials, *Pmma*-CO[50] and *Amm2*-BeC[51], all possess relatively high cohesive energies with -7.72 eV/atom and -6.67 eV/atom, respectively.

It is not very easy to define the thickness or, more accurately, the cross

section area, for a 2D monolayer system. Therefore, the elastic tensor of the T-C₃N monolayer is described using the surface area $A_0$ instead of volume as follows:

$$
C_{vu} = \left. \frac{1}{A_0} \frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial \eta_v \partial \eta_u} \right|_0, \tag{3}
$$

where $\eta$ is a two order symmetric matrix for the strain tensor. Note that the Voigt's notation is used to label the elastic tensor. According to the symmetry of the system, there are only two independent elements $C_{11} = C_{22} = 163.76$ eV and $C_{12} = 85.23$ eV, wherefrom the third element can be defined as $C_{66} = \frac{1}{2}(C_{11} - C_{12}) = 39.27$ eV. Its mechanical properties are, to a certain extent, better than graphene, whose independent elements of the elastic tensor are $C_{11} = 118.02$ eV and $C_{12} = 21.63$ eV.

Once the monolayer is exfoliated from the matrix, the charge configurations on the atoms are changed, but very slightly. According to the Mulliken population analysis, the effective charges on the N and C atoms in a T-C₃N monolayer are -0.668 e and 0.223 e, respectively. Except the strong ionic interaction in a C-N bond, there still exists a weak covalent bond reflected by a 180 me Mulliken bond population. The bond populations in the intra- and inter-tetrahedron C-C slightly increase from 920 me and 418 me (in 3D bulk) to 922 me and 428 me (in 2D monolayer), respectively. The strong covalent bonds between the neighboring C atoms can therefore be confirmed. The contour maps of the electron localization functions (ELF) for the C-C and C-N bonds are illustrated in Fig. 8(a). Therefrom, one can find the clear electron localizations around N atoms and between the neighboring C atoms. The electron localizations around N atoms confirm the ionization of N and C atoms. Conversely, the high electron localization

values between the neighboring C atoms imply the covalent bonds. More-
over, the ELF values on the inter-tetrahedron C-C bonds are higher than
that on the intra-tetrahedron ones indicating the order of the covalent bond
strengths: $C-C_{inter} > C-C_{intra}$ , which is consistent with previous Mulliken
population analysis for monolayer and COOP analysis for 3D system. The
negative Laplacian of the electron density $-\nabla^{2}\rho$ for the monolayer can also
confirm the above outcomes. Red and blue in Fig. 8(b) point out the bonding
and anti-bonding regions, respectively. The red color on a inter-tetrahedron
C-C is obviously deeper than that on the inter-tetrahedron one. Due to
a slight change of the electron population, the intra- and inter-tetrahedron
C-C bond lengths in 2D T-$C_{3}$N are slightly reduced to 1.456 Å and 1.409
Å, respectively. Conversely, the C-N bond length increases to 1.522 Å. The
shortened quasi in-plane C-C bonds results in a slight shrinkage of the pore.
Nevertheless, the pore size is still 5.50 Å which is bigger than the water
molecule, but smaller than the hydrated inorganic salts.

### 3.3. Water Nanofiltration

As mentioned above, the diameter of the pore on the monolayer T-$C_{3}$N is
around 5.50 Å which is estimated by measuring the center-to-center distance
between two opposite C atoms. Considering the electron distribution on
the edge of a pore, the width of the electron void area is still around 3.2 Å
according to the ELF map in Fig. 8(a). An experimental study pointed out
that the optimal pore size on graphene for allowing the transport of one water

The pore size determines a full retention for organic dyes and ion salts by physical sieving. Moreover, the negatively charged N atoms on the surface could provide a significant electrostatic interaction with the anions dissolved in water[52, 53]. In order to verify the water permeability of monolayer T-C₃N, the interaction between a water molecule and the surface of the monolayer T-C₃N is also carefully studied. After a full geometric relaxation, the most stable adsorption position of a water molecule on the monolayer T-C₃N is the center of the pore. The water molecule is not perpendicular to the surface. The H atoms of the water molecule approach their neighboring N atoms. The distance between the neighboring H and N atoms (2.250 Å) falls in the expected hydrogen bond range of 1.70~2.45 Å[54], indicating two relative weak hydrogen bonds between water molecule and the pore on 2D T-C₃N. The geometric configuration of this adsorption arrangement is illustrated in Fig. 9(a). The adsorption energy is defined as the difference between the total energy of the adsorption system and the energies of the isolated systems (i.e., the isolated water molecule and monolayer T-C₃N). The calculated adsorption energy is around 0.617 eV, which is much higher than the upper limit of a physisorbed state (~0.3 eV/molecule)[55]. Note that the adsorption energy here is an absolute value. The adsorption of a water molecule on the monolayer T-C₃N is an exothermic process. Such a relative high adsorption energy is caused by the aforementioned hydrogen

bonds between the neighboring H and N atoms. However, the interaction between the H and N atoms can weaken the hydrogen bonds in a water cluster, which will further help the penetration of the water molecule. The energy profile shown in Fig. 9(b) identifies the transition state of a water permeation along the minimum migration path with an energy barrier 26.58 kcal/mol. As we all known, the reactions with a barrier equal or less than 21 kcal/mol can proceed readily at room temperature[56]. The permeation barrier in present work is under 6 kcal/mol higher than this general rule. However, this barrier is obtained from a DFT calculation where only one water molecule is taken into account, without any environment effects as well as any pressure and temperature conditions. The osmotic pressure applied to one side of the T-$C_3$N monolayer and other environment factors can help to easily cross this barrier.

In order to reveal the kinetic crossing process of water molecules in present of $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions, the potentials mean force (PMF) free energies of a water molecule, $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions across T-$C_3$N are gathered by performing some adaptive steered molecular dynamics (ASMD) simulations[37, 38, 39]. To verify the reliability of this simulation method, the PMF free energy profiles for water, $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions across pristine $\alpha$-graphyne are plotted in Fig. ESI-5. The kinetic energy barriers for the permeations through a monolayer $\alpha$-graphyne obtained from our ASMD simulations are consistent very well with the literature report[57]. The corresponding PMF free energy profiles for T-$C_3$N are plotted in Fig. 9(c). The permeation energy barriers for water, $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions imply the desalination ability of T-$C_3$N. The extremely high permeation energy barriers for ions can be rationalized by

the Coulomb interactions between ions and the electron enriched N-atoms on T-$C_3N$. Especially for $Na^+$, the pore on T-$C_3N$ will distinctly shrink with the approach of $Na^+$ ion owing to the dragging effect of the N atoms around the pore. Yet, the negative charged N atoms will of course repulse the approaching $Cl^-$ anions. Therefore, the salts could be fully rejected by T-$C_3N$ monolayer.

The adsorption configurations of two simplest water clusters, namely $(H_2O)_2$ and $(H_2O)_3$, are also gained from full geometric relaxation calculations at PBE-D3 level. The smallest isolated water cluster $(H_2O)_2$ possesses only one hydrogen bond, while there are three hydrogen bonds in $(H_2O)_3$ cluster forming a three-membered ring as shown in Fig. 10(a). The calculated bond length of the hydrogen bond in $(H_2O)_2$ is $1.873$ Å which is about $0.08$ Å longer than those in $(H_2O)_3$. The calculated geometric configurations for such small water clusters agree very well with the literature reports[58, 59]. The adsorption energies on the monolayer T-$C_3N$ are $0.883$ eV and $0.907$ eV for $(H_2O)_2$ and $(H_2O)_3$ clusters, respectively. These absolute values are even higher than that for single water molecule adsorption as mentioned before, indicating a more complex hydrogen bond combination. The geometric configurations of the adsorption systems are illustrated in Fig. 10(b) and (c). The water molecules are labeled with numbers as shown in the sketches. For $(H_2O)_2$ cluster on a T-$C_3N$ monolayer, the hydrogen bond between molecules 1 and 2 is shorted. The distance between one of the hydrogen atom on molecule 2 and an N atom on T-$C_3N$ surface is $1.942$ Å indicating a new hydrogen bond. More important, the water molecule 1 stands perpendicularly in the middle of the pore, not like the single water molecule case mentioned

before. There is only one hydrogen bond left between the water molecule 1 and the N atoms on T-C₃N surface. This hydrogen bond is even enlarged to 2.327 Å, approaching the limit of the definition of a hydrogen bond. For the case of the (H₂O)₃ adsorption, the electrostatic interaction from the negatively charged N atoms break the three-membered ring in the water cluster. A new O−H⋯N hydrogen bond is formed between water molecule 3 and its neighboring N atom with a bond length 1.928 Å as shown in Fig. 10(c). In this situation, all hydrogen bonds between the water molecules are longer than them in the isolated water cluster. These calculated results affirm the aforementioned standpoint that the negative charge N atoms on T-C₃N surface can weaken the hydrogen bonds in a water cluster, and so as to promote the penetration of a water molecule.

The permeation flux of water $J$ in liter·m⁻²·s⁻¹ for a membrane can be described by the Hagen-Poiseuille equation[19, 60]:

$$
J=\frac{\varepsilon \pi r_{p}^{2}}{8 \mu \delta \tau} \Delta p, \tag{4}
$$

where $\mu$ is the viscosity of the liquid, $r_{p}$ and $\delta$ represent the radius of the pore and the thickness of the membrane, respectively. The tortuosity of the pore $\tau$ describes the geometry of the membrane pore, which is 1.0 in this case. The surface porosity $\varepsilon$ of the monolayer T-C₃N is above 97%, which is defined as the ratio of the total pore area to the total membrane area. Assuming that the water viscosity $\mu$ is a constant $1.002 \times 10^{-3}$ Pa·s at $20^{\circ} C$. The water permeation flux is a linear function of the pressure drop $\Delta p$ across the membrane. Significantly, the in-plane pore density of the monolayer T-C₃N is two orders of magnitude larger than that of the high efficient graphene composite membranes[13, 61], indicating that the water permeation flux of the mono-

layer T-C₃N could be hundred times higher than those graphene membranes.
As reported in Ref.[13], the graphene/SWCNT membrane obtained with 5 s of etching showed a permeance of 7.5 liter·m⁻²·hour⁻¹·bar⁻¹. According to their report, a 5 s O₂ plasma-etching time results in an average pore size of 0.55 nm, which is exactly the same size as the pores on T-C₃N monolayer. Therefore, a water permeance across T-C₃N monolayer can be expected to be more than 750 liter·m⁻²·hour⁻¹·bar⁻¹. Of course, increasing the etching time can significantly enlarge the pore size and density on graphene. However, this will cause great loss of mechanical properties and durability of the membrane. The inherence of the pore on T-C₃N monolayer will not arise the similar concerns.

### 4. Conclusion

A novel carbonitride, T-C₃N, is obtained during a theoretical study on heavily N doped T-carbon system. The stability of this new carbonitride crystal is confirmed from the energetic, lattice dynamic, and thermal view points. T-C₃N consists of C₃N tetrahedrons, and possesses a stacked multi-layer structure. Its most stable stacking form is established by comparing the energy differences. T-C₃N is an insulator with a 5.542 eV indirect band gap, according to our B3LYP-D3 calculation. After exfoliation from the matrix, the band gap of T-C₃N monolayer changes to be direct and expands to 5.741 eV at B3LYP-D3 level. The mechanic properties and bond compositions are investigated and discussed.

Due to the extremely suitable pore size (5.50 Å) on T-C₃N monolayer, water adsorption and permeation on this material are studied. The simulation results indicate that the pore on T-C₃N monolayer should be water permeable and be able to reject all inorganic salt ions as well as organic contaminants in water. The negatively charged N atoms on T-C₃N surface can help the rejection of the salt ions and weaken the hydrogen bonds in a water cluster. The suitable pore size, high in-plane pore density (4×10¹⁴ cm⁻²), uniform pore distribution, very thin thickness, negative charged surface, and excellent mechanic properties, such merits imply that monolayer T-C₃N could have a high water permeability and selectivity, which make this material has the promising application potential in seawater desalination and water purification.
