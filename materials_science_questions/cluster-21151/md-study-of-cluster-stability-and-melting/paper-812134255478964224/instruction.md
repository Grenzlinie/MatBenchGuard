# Liquid Drop Model Coefficients and Fragmentation Patterns of Multicharged Morse Clusters

## Problem background
Multicharged finite systems—clusters, nuclei, droplets—can undergo Coulomb instability that leads to fragmentation. This task studies the energetics, liquid-drop-model description, and fragmentation patterns of (A⁺)ₙ Morse clusters (n = 55, 135). The clusters are modeled by a pairwise potential that combines a short- or long-range Morse attraction with a Coulomb repulsion. The balance between repulsive Coulomb energy and cohesive surface energy is captured by the Rayleigh fissibility parameter X = E(Coulomb) / 2 E(surface). The objective is to compute the equilibrium potential energies, extract liquid-drop-model (LDM) coefficients, and simulate fragmentation dynamics to obtain fragment size distributions and energy balances, thereby quantifying how the fragmentation mode depends on the cluster potential parameters and the dissociation energy D.

## Approach
Classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are performed on (A⁺)ₙ clusters using the Morse-Coulomb pair potential. Two sets of Morse parameters are employed: a short-range (s) potential (α = 3 Å⁻¹, Rₑ = 3 Å) and a long-range (ℓ) potential (α = 1 Å⁻¹, Rₑ = 2 Å). For each parameter set, neutral icosahedral clusters are first generated by simulated annealing, then each atom is assigned a unit positive charge and the structure is relaxed to a metastable equilibrium configuration. The dissociation energy D is varied over several values in the range 4–16 eV, and for each combination of cluster size n and D the equilibrium per-ion potential energy ε and the Coulomb energy ε_C are computed. The resulting data are fitted to extract the LDM coefficients a_c, a_v, a_s and linear scaling parameters A, B, as well as the scaled coefficients a_v⁰, a_s⁰ that describe the universal dependence on D. To investigate fragmentation, two representative MD runs are carried out: one with long-range potential, n = 55, D = 6.5 eV, initial temperature T = 3000 K (fission-like); another with short-range potential, n = 55, D = 4.2 eV, T = 500 K (Coulomb-explosion-like). The final fragment coordinates and velocities are analyzed to obtain fragment size histograms and the total kinetic and inner energies of the products.

## Reproduction target
The goal is to produce two JSON artifacts that contain the key quantitative results.

1. Liquid-drop model fitting and linear scaling: From the computed equilibrium energies for n = 55 and 135, derive the parameters A, B (from ε = A + B D) and the LDM coefficients a_c, a_v, a_s, a_v⁰, a_s⁰ for both the short-range and long-range potential classes. The fitted values must be written to `step_01_linear_fit_and_LDM.json` according to the schema given below.

2. Fragmentation analysis: For the two fragmentation simulations described above (fission case and Coulomb explosion case), identify all fragment clusters, compute the fragment size histogram (list of [k, n_k] pairs), total kinetic energy ΣE_KIN, total inner energy ΣE_IN, and the initial cluster potential energy n·ε. Verify that energy is conserved to within 2%. Write these data to `step_02_fragmentation_data.json` with the exact structure required.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Compute metastable (A⁺)ₙ cluster energies
- Role: process
- Action: For cluster sizes n=55, 135, generate neutral icosahedral clusters using a short-range Morse potential (α=3 Å⁻¹, R_e=3 Å) and a long-range Morse potential (α=1 Å⁻¹, R_e=2 Å) via simulated annealing. Then for each (n, α, R_e) combination, assign a unit positive charge to every ion, add the Coulomb repulsion, and perform energy minimization to obtain the metastable equilibrium configuration. Repeat this for several values of the dissociation energy D spanning 4–16 eV (at least five equally spaced values). For each case, record the total potential energy per ion ε and the Coulomb energy per ion ε_C. Write all pairs (D, n, ε, ε_C) for both potential types to a CSV file metastable_energies.csv.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/metastable_energies.csv`

### Step 2: Perform fragmentation MD simulations
- Role: process
- Action: Using the metastable coordinates obtained in the previous step, set up and run constant-energy molecular dynamics simulations for two representative fragmentation cases: (i) fission mode: long-range potential (α=1 Å⁻¹, R_e=2 Å) with D=6.5 eV, n=55, initial temperature T=3000 K; (ii) Coulomb explosion mode: short-range potential (α=3 Å⁻¹, R_e=3 Å) with D=4.2 eV, n=55, T=500 K. Initialize atomic velocities from a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution at the target temperature and run the simulation until the cluster has fully fragmented (at least 10–20 ps). Save the final atomic coordinates and velocities for all fragments to a file final_fragments.csv so that fragment masses/charges can be identified.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/final_fragments.csv`

### Step 3: Liquid drop model fitting and linear scaling analysis
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the metastable_energies.csv produced in step_00, extract the per-ion potential energies ε(D,n) and Coulomb energies ε_C(D,n). For the equilibrium configurations, fit the LDM form ε = a_c n^{2/3} + a_v + a_s n^{-1/3} to obtain numerical values of a_c, a_v, a_s for both the short-range (s) and long-range (ℓ) potential parameter classes. Separately, for fixed n=55 and n=135, fit the linear dependence ε = A + B D to the data across the whole D range, and verify that A ≈ a_c n^{2/3}. Using the fitted B values at two sizes, solve for the scaled coefficients a_v⁰ and a_s⁰ from B = a_v⁰ + a_s⁰ n^{-1/3}. Store all fitted coefficients in the JSON output.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_01_linear_fit_and_LDM.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: A JSON object with two top-level keys 'potential_s' and 'potential_l', each containing: 'n55' and 'n135' objects with fields A (eV), B (eV/eV), epsilon_C (eV); 'a_c' (float), 'a_v' (float), 'a_s' (float); 'a_v0' (float), 'a_s0' (float). All numeric values in eV where applicable.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 4: Analyse fragmentation patterns and energy balance
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the final_fragments.csv produced in step_01, identify all fragment clusters (each fragment is a set of atoms that remain within a distance cutoff of 4–5 Å after fragmentation). For each fragment, compute its charge (number of ions) k, the number of fragments of that size n_k, its total kinetic energy E_KIN, and its internal potential energy E_IN. Sum the kinetic and internal energies over all fragments and compare the total (ΣE_KIN+ΣE_IN) with the product n·ε (where ε is the initial per-ion potential energy computed in step_00) to verify energy conservation within 2%. Report the fragment size histogram (list of [k, n_k] pairs) and the energy totals for both the fission and Coulomb explosion cases.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/step_02_fragmentation_data.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: A JSON object with two keys 'fission' and 'coulomb_explosion'. Each value is an object with: 'n_k' (array of [k, n_k] pairs, k integer, n_k integer), 'total_KE' (float, eV), 'total_IN' (float, eV), 'n_epsilon' (float, eV).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier
