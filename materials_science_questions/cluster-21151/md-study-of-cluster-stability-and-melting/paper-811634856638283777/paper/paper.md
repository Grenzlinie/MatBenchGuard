# Oxygen impact on quantum confinement effect for silicon clusters in different size regimes: ab initio investigations

S. Chakraborty¹,²,ᵃ, Ch. Rajesh³, S. Mahamuni³, and S.V. Ghaisas²

¹ Max-Planck-Institut für Eisenforschung, 40237 Düsseldorf, Germany

Published online 17 August 2011 – © EDP Sciences, Società Italiana di Fisica, Springer-Verlag 2011

**Abstract.** The phenomena where the parameter like optical gap, HOMO-LUMO gap etc of a cluster will behave like a monotonic function with respect to its size is called quantum confinement effect. Here we are dealing with clusters having number of silicon atoms as 10, 16, 19, 20, 35, 54 and 78 which are representatives of different size regime clusters. For each clusters we have experimented with a number of isomers and the results we are showing are only of the stable most isomers. Then for each clusters we are capping them with oxygen atoms and calculating their optical response using DFT based calculations. We also calculate their HOMO-LUMO gap with and without oxygen atoms. The main essence of this work is mainly revolving around the variation of optical gap as well as the HOMO-LUMO gap for all the clusters with respect to their size and also whether oxygen insertion can induce any changes in quantum confinement effect. Also for Si₂₀, Si₁₉, Si₁₆ and Si₁₀ we have calculating the optical spectra with a variation of inserted oxygen on the surface of those clusters. All the calculation are performed using Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) and Car Parrinello molecular dynamics (CPMD) for geometry optimization. The optical spectra of the clusters are performed by real space PARSEC code and time dependent density functional theory implemented RGWBS code which takes into account the many body effects using GW and BSE equations.

## 1 Introduction

Experimental studies of forming low dimensional silicon clusters started getting the attention of scientific community thirty years back [1,2]. This attention was mainly due to the possibility of fabricating new materials with desired optical and electronic properties as these are immensely depending upon the size [3,4] of the system. In this context, several theoretical works also have been done. Tomanek and Schluter were the first to analyze the small silicon clusters $Si_n$ ($n\leq14$). They found that the structural conversion similar to the bulk silicon was happened if the number of atoms in the cluster was about $10^2$–$10^3$. The system of nanocrystalline silicon in $SiO_2$ system represents the greatest research interests [5–7]. In some of the works related to $SiO_2$ systems it has been found that oxygen renders a considerable effect on both bulk and surface electronic properties of semiconductor [8,9]. Oxygen and $SiO_2$ take the main part during the vapor chemical growth of silicon nanostructures [10]. With this consequence, a large number of experimental and theoretical investigations have been done in recent past years explaining the interaction of oxygen and silicon clusters [11–22]. In reference [11], the $O_2$ molecule on neutral and charged silicon clusters $Si_n$ ($n=3$–$16$) was investigated where the change in geometry and electronic structure was found as a consequence of oxygen-molecule adsorption. The verification of fragmentation process of silicon nanoclusters in the interaction with oxygen has also been carried out with different experimental [12,13] and theoretical calculations [14,15]. For $Si_mO_n$ nanoclusters ($m=1$–$5$ and $n=1,2m+1$), it was shown that the clusters consisting of isolated silicon oxide fragments are energetically most favorable structures. In the same case a considerable increase in the energy gap was observed when the silicon clusters are saturated by oxygen. Similar results are found for $Si_6O_n(n=1$–$12)$ nanoclusters also [15]. Due to the high importance of these systems for microelectronics and nanoelectronics, it is indeed necessary to expand similar investigations for obtaining results on the electronic structure of silicon nanoclusters of an arbitrary shape and size for different oxidation pattern.

In the following work, we have studied the structural, electronic and optical properties of silicon nanoclusters

## 2 Computational methodology

Computations are performed on CPMD (Car Parrinello molecular dynamics) [23], VASP (Vienna ab initio simu- lation package) [24,25] and PARSEC (pseudopotential al- gorithm for real space electronic calculations) [26] to ob- tain various electronic and optical properties. CPMD and VASP use the plane wave basis. All these programs use the density functional theory (DFT) to compute the ground state as well as excited states within Kohn-Sham single particle effective potential interaction. VASP uses gener- alized gradient approximation (GGA) for the exchange correlation and ultra-soft pseudopotential of Vanderbilt. CPMD and PARSEC use the local density approximation (LDA) for the same purpose. The LDA is known to under- estimate the HOMO-LUMO gaps. We report the HOMO- LUMO gaps computed from GGA. Absorption spectra are obtained within TDLDA framework [27], provided as an extended PARSEC program. To incorporate the many body effect in the optical spectra we use RGWBS code [28] introduced by Tiago which is implemented using GW and Bathe saltpeter equation.

In the following, we will mainly discuss the structural and optical properties of $Si_{10}$, $Si_{16}$, $Si_{19}$ and $Si_{20}$ structures with and without oxygen atoms. The oxygen atoms are added at the surface of each cluster.

## 3 Results and discussions

### 3.1 Oxygen effect on $Si_{10}$ clusters

Out of different isomers of $Si_{10}$ clusters, we have found the stable most optimized $Si_{10}$ structure with binding energy 3.84 eV/atom. We have optimized the bare $Si_{10}$ structure as well as the structure with two and six oxygen atoms. In each case we have found the corresponding optical spectra from TDDFT implemented RGWBS code. Figures 1a, 1c and 1e show the optimized structures and Figures 1b, 1d and 1f show the corresponding optical spectra in the fol- lowing.

The binding energies of $Si_{10}O_{2}$ and $Si_{10}O_{6}$ structures are 4.30 eV/atom and 4.48 eV/atom and they show sta- ble geometries with oxygen atoms at the surface. In all the figures of silicon structures the number of connector between the atoms are shown for the more clarity, they do not bring any significance as per the bonds are concerned.

### 3.2 Oxygen effect on $Si_{16}$ clusters

Like $Si_{10}$, we have considered a number of configura- tions for $Si_{16}$ structures and after doing the geometry optimization we have found the stable most $Si_{16}$ structure with 3.75 eV/atom as the binding energy. Along with the optimization of the bare $Si_{16}$ structure, we have also deter- mined the structure with number of added oxygen atoms two, four and six. For all the cases we have found the corresponding optical spectra from TDDFT implemented RGWBS code. Figures 2a, 2c, 2e and 2g show the opti- mized structures and Figures 2b, 2d, 2f and 2h show the corresponding optical spectra in the following.

The binding energies of $Si_{16}O_{2}$, $Si_{16}O_{4}$ and $Si_{16}O_{6}$ are 4.23, 3.91 and 4.97 eV/atom correspondingly.

Here also the connectors are not the real bonds be- tween the atoms. They are shown to give a good clarity in the figures only.

### 3.3 Oxygen effect on Si₁₉ clusters

We have considered a number of isomeric configurations for Si₁₉ cluster and after doing the geometry optimization we have found the stable most Si₁₉ structure with 3.79 eV/atom as the binding energy.

After getting the optimized geometry, we calculate the corresponding optical spectra for each case. Also we are adding different number of oxygen atoms on the surface of the Si₁₉ cluster. As a consequence, we calculate the optical spectrum for various Si₁₉Oₙ clusters with n is equal to 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12.

Figures 3a, 3c, 3e, 3g, 3i, 3k and 3m represent the optimized structures and Figures 3b, 3d, 3f, 3h, 3j, 3l and 3n are the corresponding optical spectra for $n=0,2,4,6$, 8, 10, 12 respectively. The fragmented structures of the oxygen passivated Si clusters are because of the simulated annealing carried out by CPMD program. The annealing is carried out from $1500~^\circ\text{C}$ up to room temperature with $100~^\circ\text{C}$ temperature steps. The fragmentation path taken during annealing is one of the minimum kinetic barrier path. But if we optimize the clusters without annealing we get non-fragmented clusters. The optical spectra obtained for fragmented as well as non-fragmented clusters are apparently same in the range from 0 to 6 eV. Hence, we have showed only the optical spectra for the fragmented clusters.

### 3.4 Oxygen effect on Si₂₀ clusters

We have considered a number of configurations for Si₂₀ structures and after doing the geometry optimization we have found the stable most Si₂₀ structure with 3.66 eV/atom as the binding energy. Along with the optimization of the bare Si₁₆ structure, we have also determined the structure with number of added oxygen atoms two, four and six. For all the cases we have found the corresponding optical spectra from TDDFT implemented RGWBS code. Figures 4a, 4c, 4e, 4g, 4i, 4k and 4m show the optimized structures and Figures 4b, 4d, 4f, 4h, 4j, 4l and 4n show the corresponding optical spectra in the following.

We have plotted the HOMO-LUMO gap for Si₂₀ cluster with different number of oxygen atoms and also the optical gap variation, both of which are shown in Figures 5a and 5b.

The optical gap is dependent on number of oxygen atoms though the HOMO-LUMO gap does not depend on the number of atoms. Here the size of the cluster does not depend on the inclusion of the oxygen atoms. The change in the binding energy w.r.t. the number of oxygen atoms can be displayed in Figure 6 and also it has been displayed in Table 1.

The binding energy is increasing with the increase in the number of oxygen for Si₂₀, Si₃₅, Si₅₄ and Si₇₈ clusters. It concludes that these clusters can get easily oxidized. But for Si₁₆ and Si₁₉, the binding energy is not monotonically increasing or decreasing with respect to the number of oxygen atoms attached to it.

Inclusion of oxygen atoms does not change the size of the cluster like it is changed while replacing oxygen.
