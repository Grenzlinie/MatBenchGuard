CONTRIBUTION TO THE LATTICE THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY DUE TO THE CORRECTION TERM IN THE FRAME OF THE GENERALIZED CALLAWAY INTEGRAL: APPLICATION TO Ge

K.S. Dubey

Department of Physics, College of Science, University of Basrah, Basrah, Iraq

(Received 25 January 1977; in revised form 25 May 1977 by A. Zawadowski)

The contribution to the lattice thermal conductivity due to the correction term (due to the three phonon normal processes) has been studied at very low temperatures in the frame of the generalized Callaway integral by deriving analytical expressions for it. The contribution of the correction term towards total phonon conductivity of Ge has been calculated in the temperature range 1-5 K and negligible contribution is found due to it.

### 1. INTRODUCTION
THE GENERALIZED form [1-5] of the Callaway [6] integral of the lattice thermal conductivity as based on two mode conduction of phonons can be expressed as a sum of two parts. The first part gives the phonon conductivity of an insulator due to the combined scattering relaxation rate while the second part has a complicated form and it is known as the correction term due to the three phonon normal processes. Some studies [7-15] have been made to calculate the contribution of the correction term ($\Delta K$) towards total phonon conductivity of an insulator in the frame of the Callaway integral as well as in the frame of the generalized Callaway integral. But these studies are confined to temperatures at which the three phonon scattering relaxation rate dominates over other scattering processes, i.e. at temperatures for which $c_{3ph}^{-1} > (c_{B}^{-1} + c_{pt}^{-1})$; terms are defined later on in the text. Recently, Dubey [16] studied the contribution of $\Delta K$ towards total phonon conductivity of Ge at very low temperatures where the boundary scattering relaxation rate dominates over other scattering processes. But his entire study is based on the Callaway integral of the lattice thermal conductivity. The previous report of Dubey of $\Delta K$ in the frame of the Callaway integral allows one to make a similar study of $\Delta K$ in the frame of the generalized Callaway integral also. Thus, the aim of the present note is to derive an analytical expression for $\Delta K$ in the frame of the generalized Callaway integral at very low temperatures where boundary scattering relaxation rate dominates over other scattering relaxation rates, i.e. temperatures for which $c_{B}^{-1} > (c_{pt}^{-1} + c_{3ph}^{-1})$; say 1-5 K for Ge. The contribution of the correction term $\Delta K$ towards total phonon conductivity of Ge has also been calculated in the frame of the generalized Callaway integral in the temperature range 1-5 K as an example and a negligibly small contribution is found due to it.

### 2. THEORY
The generalized form of the Callaway integral of the lattice thermal conductivity of an insulator can be expressed as [5]

$$
\begin{aligned}
K= & C\left[\sum_{s}\left(1 / v_{s}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{n} / T}\right. \\
& \times \frac{\left(1+R_{s} x^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+3 R_{s} x^{2}\right)^{-1} x^{4} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} d x}{\left(c_{N, s}^{-1}+c_{R, s}^{-1}\right)} \\
& +\Delta K]
\end{aligned}
$$

and $\Delta K$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta K= & C\left[2\left(\Delta T_{1}+\Delta T_{2}\right)+\left(\Delta L_{1}+\Delta L_{2}\right)\right]^{2} \\
& \times\left[2\left(\Delta T_{1}^{\prime}+\Delta T_{2}^{\prime}\right)+\left(\Delta L_{1}^{\prime}+\Delta L_{2}^{\prime}\right)\right]^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$C=\left(k_{B} / 6 \pi^{2}\right)\left(k_{B} T / \hbar\right)^{3}\tag{3}$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T_{1}= & \left(1 / v_{T 1}^{3}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{1} / T} c_{N, T}^{-1}\left(c_{N, T}^{-1}+c_{R, T}^{-1}\right)^{-1} \\
& \times\left(1+R_{1} x^{2}\right)^{3} x^{4} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} d x
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T_{2}= & \left(1 / v_{T 2}^{3}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{1} / T} c_{N, T}^{-1}\left(c_{R, T}^{-1}+c_{N, T}^{-1}\right)^{-1} \\
& \times\left(1+R_{2} x^{2}\right)^{3} x^{4} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} d x
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L_{1}= & \left(1 / v_{L 1}^{3}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{2} / T} c_{N, L}^{-1}\left(c_{N, L}^{-1}+c_{R, L}^{-1}\right)^{-1} \\
& \times\left(1+R_{3} x^{2}\right)^{3} x^{4} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} d x
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L_{2}= & \left(1 / v_{L 2}^{3}\right) \int_{\theta_{3} / T}^{\theta_{4} / T} c_{N, L}^{-1}\left(c_{N, L}^{-1}+c_{R, L}^{-1}\right)^{-1} \\
& \times\left(1+R_{4} x^{2}\right)^{3} x^{4} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} d x
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T_{1}^{\prime} & =\left(1 / v_{T 1}^{5}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{1} / T} c_{N, T}^{-1} c_{R, T}^{-1}\left(c_{N, T}^{-1}+c_{R, T}^{-1}\right)^{-1} \\
& \times\left(1+R_{1} x^{2}\right)^{4}\left(1+3 R_{1} x^{2}\right) x^{4} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{~d} x
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L_{1}^{\prime} & =\left(1 / v_{L 1}^{5}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{3} / T} c_{N, L}^{-1} c_{R, L}^{-1}\left(c_{N, L}^{-1}+c_{R, L}^{-1}\right)^{-1} \\
& \times\left(1+R_{3} x^{2}\right)^{4}\left(1+3 R_{3} x^{2}\right) x^{4} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{~d} x
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

Similar types of expression can also be written for $\Delta T_{2}^{\prime}$ and $\Delta L_{2}^{\prime}$ with the help of equations (5), (7), (8) and (9). Where $k_{B}$ is the Boltzmann constant, $\hbar$ is the Plank constant divided by $2 \pi, v$ is the velocity of phonons, $\theta$ is the temperature corresponding to the zone boundary of the crystal, $R$ is a constant and its value can be calculated $[17,18]$ with the help of the dispersion curve of the sample, suffix $s$ is taken to differentiate between transverse and longitudinal phonons ( $s$ - represents mode of phonons). $T$ and $L$ represent transverse and longitudinal phonons respectively. $\Delta K$ is the correction term due to the three phonon normal processes. $c_{N}^{-1}$ is the three phonon normal processes scattering relaxation rate and $c_{R}^{-1}$ is the combined scattering relaxation rate due to all Umklapp processes and it is given by $c_{R}^{-1}=c_{B}^{-1}+c_{P t}^{-1}+$ $c_{U}^{-1}$. the term $c_{B}^{-1}$ is the boundary scattering relaxation rate [19], $c_{P t}^{-1}$ is the point defect scattering rate [20] and $c_{U}^{-1}$ is the three phonon Umklapp processes scattering relaxation rate [20].

Since our interests lie to very low temperatures, the following expression for the respective scattering relaxation rates have been used in the present study.
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{N, T}^{-1} & =B_{1} w T^{4}=b_{1} x, \quad c_{R, T}^{-1}=c_{B}^{-1}+A w^{4} \\
& +B_{2} w T^{4} e^{-\theta_{D} / \alpha T}=c_{B}^{-1}+D x^{4}+b_{2} x \\
c_{N, L}^{-1} & =B_{3} w^{2} T^{3}=b_{3} x^{2}, \quad c_{R, L}^{-1}=c_{B}^{-1}+A w^{4} \\
& +B_{4} w^{2} T^{3} e^{-\theta_{D} / \alpha T}=c_{B}^{-1}+D x^{4}+b_{4} x^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
where $A$ is the point defect scattering strength, $\alpha$ is a constant [20], $\theta_{D}$ is the Debye temperature of the sample, $B$'s are the scattering strength of the respective three phonon processes and $x=\left(\mathrm{hw} / k_{B} T\right)$. It is found that the contributions of integrals $\Delta T_{2}, \Delta T_{2}^{\prime}, \Delta L_{2}$ and $\Delta L_{2}^{\prime}$ are very small at low temperatures due to their limits of integration and their contributions can be ignored in comparison to the contributions of integrals $\Delta T_{1}, \Delta T_{1}^{\prime}, \Delta L_{1}$ and $\Delta L_{1}^{\prime}$. Using above stated expressions for the different scattering relaxation rates, $\Delta T_{1}$ can be expressed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T_{1} & =b_{1} c_{B} v_{T 1}^{-3} \int_{0}^{\theta_{1} / T}\left[1+D c_{B} x^{4}+\left(b_{1}+b_{2}\right) c_{B} x\right]^{-1} \\
& \times\left(1+R_{1} x^{2}\right)^{3} x^{5} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{~d} x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

Since our study is limited to temperatures at which $c_{B}^{-1}>\left(c_{P t}^{-1}+c_{3 p h}^{-1}\right)$, therefore, using the above stated approximation and neglecting lower value terms, equation (10) reduces to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T_{1} & =b_{1} c_{B} v_{T 1}^{-3}\left[\left(I_{5}+3 R_{1} I_{7}+3 R_{1}^{2} I_{9}+R_{1}^{3} I_{11}\right)\right. \\
& -D c_{B}\left(I_{9}+3 R_{1} I_{11}+3 R_{1}^{2} I_{13}+R_{1}^{3} I_{15}\right) \\
& \left.-\left(b_{1}+b_{2}\right) c_{B}\left(I_{6}+3 R_{1} I_{8}+3 R_{1}^{2} I_{10}+R_{1}^{3} I_{12}\right)\right] \quad(11)
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\Gamma$'s are integrals and can be expressed as
$$
I_{n}=\int_{0}^{\theta_{s} / T} x^{n} e^{x}\left(e^{x}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{~d} x,
$$
where $n$ is any positive integer. By introducing a new term $X(R)$ as a function of dispersion constant $R$, equation (11) can be simplified as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T_{1} & =b_{1} c_{B} v_{T 1}^{-3} I_{5}\left[X_{5}^{7}\left(R_{1}\right)-D c_{B} F_{5}^{9} X_{9}^{11}\left(R_{1}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-\left(b_{1}+b_{2}\right) c_{B} F_{5}^{6} X_{6}^{8}\left(R_{1}\right)\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
where $X_{n}^{m}(R)=1+3 R_{1} F_{n}^{m}+3 R_{1}^{2} F_{n}^{m+2}+R_{1}^{3} F_{n}^{m+4}$ and $F_{n}^{m}=I_{m} / I_{n}$ for any positive value of $m$ and $n$ and for any value of $R$, i.e. $R_{1}$ and $R_{3}$.

In a similar way, one can evaluate other integrals $\Delta T_{1}^{\prime}, \Delta L_{1}$ and $\Delta L_{1}^{\prime}$ also. Thus the expression for $\Delta K$ reduces to
$$
\Delta K=C\left(2 N_{1} / v_{T 1}^{3}+N_{2} / v_{L 1}^{3}\right)^{2}\left(2 N_{3} / v_{T 1}^{5}+N_{4} / v_{L 1}^{5}\right)^{-1}
\tag{13}
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{1} & =b_{1}^{\prime} I_{5}\left[X_{5}^{7}\left(R_{1}\right)-D_{1} F_{5}^{9} X_{9}^{11}\left(R_{1}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-\left(b_{1}^{\prime}+b_{2}^{\prime}\right) F_{5}^{6} X_{6}^{8}\left(R_{1}\right)\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{2} & =b_{3}^{\prime} I_{6}\left[X_{6}^{8}\left(R_{3}\right)-D_{1} F_{6}^{10} X_{10}^{12}\left(R_{3}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-\left(b_{3}^{\prime}+b_{4}^{\prime}\right) F_{6}^{8} X_{8}^{10}\left(R_{3}\right)\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{3} & =b_{1} I_{5}\left[Y_{5}^{7}\left(R_{1}\right)-b_{1}^{\prime} F_{5}^{6} Y_{6}^{8}\left(R_{1}\right)\right. \\
& -\left(b_{1}^{\prime}+2 b_{2}^{\prime}\right) D_{1} F_{5}^{10} Y_{10}^{12}\left(R_{1}\right)-b_{2}^{\prime}\left(b_{1}^{\prime}+b_{2}^{\prime}\right) F_{5}^{7} Y_{7}^{9}\left(R_{1}\right) \\
& \left.-D_{1}^{2} F_{5}^{13} Y_{13}^{15}\left(R_{1}\right)\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{4} & =b_{3} I_{6}\left[Y_{6}^{8}\left(R_{3}\right)-b_{3}^{\prime} F_{6}^{8} Y_{8}^{10}\left(R_{3}\right)\right. \\
& -\left(b_{3}^{\prime}+2 b_{4}^{\prime}\right) D_{1} F_{6}^{12} Y_{12}^{14}\left(R_{3}\right) \\
& \left.-b_{4}^{\prime}\left(b_{3}^{\prime}+b_{4}^{\prime}\right) F_{6}^{10} Y_{10}^{12}\left(R_{3}\right)-D_{1}^{2} F_{6}^{14} Y_{14}^{16}\left(R_{3}\right)\right]
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
where $Y_{n}^{m}(R)=1+7 R F_{n}^{m}+18 R^{2} F_{n}^{m+2}+22 R^{3} F_{n}^{m+4}$ $+7 R^{4} F_{n}^{m+6}+3 R^{5} F_{n}^{m+8}$:
for $R=R_{1}$ and $R_{3} . b_{r}^{\prime}=b_{r} / c_{B}^{-1}: r=1,2,3$ and 4 .
$D_{1}=D / c_{B}^{-1}$.

It is found that the contribution of the three phonon Umklapp processes scattering relaxation rate $c_{U}^{-1}$ towards the combined scattering relaxation rate $c_{R}^{-1}$ is very small at low temperatures and one can ignore its contribution in the calculation of the phonon conductivity of an insulator at low temperatures. ## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

The contribution of $\Delta K$ towards total phonon conductivity of Ge has been calculated in the frame of the generalized Callaway integral in the temperature range 1-5 K and obtained results are reported in Table 1. The constants used in the calculations are taken from the earlier reports of Holland [21] and Sharma et al. [17, 18]. Therefore, these constants are not reported here. From equations (18) and (22), it can be concluded that $\Delta K$ is proportional to the product of the three phonon normal processes scattering strength and square of the boundary scattering relaxation time, i.e.
$\Delta K \propto b_{1}c_{B}^{2}$, which is similar to the earlier findings of Dubey based on the Callaway integral of the lattice thermal conductivity. From Table 1, it is very clear that the contribution of the correction term $\Delta K$ towards total phonon conductivity of Ge is very small and one can neglect its contribution in comparison to the total phonon conductivity. Thus, one can say that the lattice thermal conductivity of an insulator can be calculated excluding the contribution of the correction term $\Delta K$ due to its very small contribution.
