# Lattice Thermal Conductivity of UO2+x via Direct-Method Molecular Dynamics

## Problem background
Uranium dioxide (UO2) is the standard nuclear reactor fuel; its thermal conductivity is a key performance metric that governs heat transfer from the fuel pellet to the coolant. The fuel operates off-stoichiometric, with oxygen interstitials and vacancies that significantly affect thermal transport. This task addresses the computation of the lattice thermal conductivity of UO2+x as a function of defect concentration x (covering both hypo- and hyperstoichiometric regimes) at two relevant temperatures (800 K and 1600 K) using direct-method molecular dynamics simulations. The aim is to quantify how the bulk thermal conductivity changes with defect concentration and temperature, providing data critical for fuel performance analysis.

## Approach
The approach uses molecular dynamics (MD) simulations with an empirical interatomic potential (Busker potential, Buckingham form) that describes U–O and O–O short-range interactions plus Coulombic interactions. To model off-stoichiometric UO2+x (x from −0.02 to +0.25), appropriate oxygen vacancies or interstitials are introduced along with charge-compensating uranium ions (U3+ for hypostoichiometric, U5+ for hyperstoichiometric). Atomic configurations are generated by annealing at high temperature, slow cooling, and quenching to obtain minimum-energy structures. Thermal conductivity is computed via the direct method: a non-equilibrium MD setup imposes a steady heat current and measures the resulting temperature gradient. From Fourier's law, the size-dependent conductivity κ(Lz) is obtained for several simulation cell lengths Lz. The infinite-size (bulk) limit is then extracted by extrapolating 1/κ vs 1/Lz to infinite length using a finite-size scaling theory that involves the lattice parameter and sound velocity. Finally, the raw bulk conductivity is renormalized by a fixed factor (3.0) to correct for the difference between the potential energy anharmonicity and experiment. The simulations are carried out for a set of composition values x at two temperatures (800 K and 1600 K). The final output is the bulk thermal conductivity for each (x, T) condition.

## Reproduction target
Compute the bulk lattice thermal conductivity κ (in W/mK) of UO2+x for x in the set [−0.02, 0.00, 0.02, 0.04, 0.08, 0.125, 0.25] at temperatures 800 K and 1600 K, using direct-method MD with the Busker potential, extrapolate to infinite length, and apply the renormalization factor 3.0. Report the results as a CSV with columns: x (float), temperature_K (int, 800 or 1600), kappa_WmK (float).

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Generate off-stoichiometric UO2+x equilibrium structures

- Evidence: `/app/outputs/structure_generation.log`

### Step 2: Run direct-method MD to compute size-dependent thermal conductivity
- Role: process
- Action: For each composition x and for temperatures T = 800 K and 1600 K, run non-equilibrium MD (direct method) with simulation cell lengths Lz corresponding to 48, 64, 80, 96 unit cells (cross-section 4×4 unit cells). Impose a constant heat current, measure the steady-state temperature gradient, and compute κ(Lz) from Fourier's law. Record all raw κ(Lz) values.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/raw_kappa.csv`

### Step 3: Extrapolate to infinite size and renormalize thermal conductivity
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: From the raw κ(Lz) data, for each (x, T) perform linear extrapolation of 1/κ vs 1/Lz to 1/Lz → 0 using the scaling relation that involves the lattice parameter a (taken from the 0-K structures), number of atoms per unit cell N_C = 12, Boltzmann constant k_B, and the mean sound velocity v_S (use literature value or calculate from elastic constants). The intercept gives the raw infinite-size thermal conductivity. Multiply each raw infinite-size value by the anharmonic renormalization factor 3.0 to obtain the final bulk thermal conductivity κ. Write the results for all (x, T) to the output file.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/kappa_values.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: CSV with columns: x (float, -0.02 to 0.25), temperature_K (int, 800 or 1600), kappa_WmK (float)
