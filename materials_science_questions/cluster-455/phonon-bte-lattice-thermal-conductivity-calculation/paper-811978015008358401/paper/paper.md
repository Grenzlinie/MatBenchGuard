# Three-Phonon Scattering Processes in p-Type Mercury Telluride

By
K.P. ROY, V. RADHAKRISHNAN, and P.C. SHARMA $^{2)}$

The lattice thermal conductivity of p-HgTe /1/ is explained up to 300 K for a hole concentration of $5x10^{16} cm^{-3}$ using the SDV model /2/ for three-phonon scattering processes by taking the exponent m of the temperature $(T^{m})$ as a continuous function of temperature, m(T).

According to the SDV model, the three-phonon scattering processes can be classified into longitudinal and transverse categories and subsequently into class I and class II events. The transverse phonons contribute to class I events alone, whereas longitudinal phonons contribute to both class I and class II events. The three-phonon relaxation rates are given as

$$
\left(\tau_{3 \mathrm{ph}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{T}}=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{T}} \omega \mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{T} 1}(\mathrm{~T})} \exp (-\Theta / \alpha \mathrm{T}), \tag{1}
$$

$$
\left(\tau_{3 \mathrm{ph}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{L}}=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{L} 1} \omega^{2} \mathrm{~T}^{\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{L} 1}(\mathrm{~T})} \exp (-\Theta / \alpha \mathrm{T})+\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{L} 2} \omega^{2} \mathrm{~T}^{\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{L} 2}(\mathrm{~T})} \exp (-\Theta / \alpha \mathrm{T}), \tag{2}
$$

where 1 and 2 represent class I and class II events. The true values of m(T) for transverse and longitudinal phonons are given as

$$
\mathrm{m}_{\text {true }}(\mathrm{T})=\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{av}}(\mathrm{T})+\frac{\ln (1+\Theta / \alpha \mathrm{T})}{\ln \mathrm{T}}, \tag{3}
$$

where the $m_{av}(T)$ values are calculated as the average of $m_{min}$ and $m_{max}$ values given elsewhere /1/. Hence the values of $m_{true}(T)$ for different polarizations are given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{L} 2}(\mathrm{~T}) & =0.5 \mathrm{x}_{\max, \mathrm{L}}\left(\exp \left(\mathrm{x}_{\max, \mathrm{L}}\right)-1\right)^{-1}\left(\exp \left(0.5 \mathrm{x}_{\max, \mathrm{L}}\right)\right)+ \\
& +0.5+\frac{\ln (1+\Theta / \alpha \mathrm{T})}{\ln \mathrm{T}},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mathrm{x}_{\max }=\hbar \omega_{\max } / \mathrm{k}_{\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{T}$, which can be calculated from the phonon dispersion curves for longitudinal and transverse phonon branches. The lattice conductivity integrals can be obtained for longitudinal and transverse phonons as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{T}}= & \frac{2}{3} \frac{\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{B}}}{2 \pi^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{T}}{\hbar}\right)^{3}\left[\frac{1}{\left(\mathrm{~V}_{\mathrm{T} 1}\right)_{0<\omega<\omega_{1}}} \int_{0}^{\Theta_{1} / \mathrm{T}} \frac{\mathrm{x}^{4} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{dx}}{\tau_{\mathrm{B}}^{-1}+\left(\tau_{\mathrm{pt}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{T}}+\left(\tau_{3 \mathrm{ph}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{T}}}+\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{1}{\left(\mathrm{~V}_{\mathrm{T} 2}\right)_{\omega<\omega<\omega_{2}}} \int_{\Theta_{1} / \mathrm{T}}^{\Theta_{2} / \mathrm{T}} \frac{\mathrm{x}^{4} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{dx}}{\tau_{\mathrm{B}}^{-1}+\left(\tau_{\mathrm{pt}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{T}}+\left(\tau_{3 \mathrm{ph}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{T}}}\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}= & \frac{1}{3} \frac{\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{B}}}{2 \pi^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{T}}{\hbar}\right)^{3}\left[\frac{1}{\left(\mathrm{~V}_{\mathrm{L} 1}\right)_{0<\omega<\omega_{4}}} \int_{0}^{\Theta_{4} / \mathrm{T}} \frac{\mathrm{x}^{4} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{dx}}{\tau_{\mathrm{B}}^{-1}+\left(\tau_{\mathrm{pt}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{L}}+\left(\tau_{3 \mathrm{ph}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{L}}}+\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{1}{\left(\mathrm{~V}_{\mathrm{L} 2}\right)_{\omega_{4}<\omega<\omega_{3}}} \int_{\Theta_{4} / \mathrm{T}}^{\Theta_{3} / \mathrm{T}} \frac{\mathrm{x}^{4} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{x}}-1\right)^{-2} \mathrm{dx}}{\tau_{\mathrm{B}}^{-1}+\left(\tau_{\mathrm{pt}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{L}}+\left(\tau_{3 \mathrm{ph}}^{-1}\right)_{\mathrm{L}}}\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the dispersion curves of HgTe /3/, the phonon frequencies $\omega_{max}$ corresponding to longitudinal and transverse branches are calculated and using expressions (4) to (6), the

values of m(T) for HgTe are obtained for class I and class II events at different temperatures between 10 and 300 K. The values of $B_T$, $B_{L1}$ and $B_{L2}$, $\Theta_1$, $\Theta_2$, $\Theta_3$, and $\Theta_4$ and other quantities are given in Table 2.

The calculated and experimental thermal conductivity curves in the entire temperature range from 2 to 300 K are shown in Fig. 1, indicating the contributions of transverse and longitudinal phonons. It is found from the figure that above 80 K the contribution of transverse phonons is greater than the contribution of longitudinal phonons and the same is the case below 10 K. However, between 10 and 80 K the contribution of longitudinal phonons is greater. It is concluded that the SDV model explains the thermal conductivity curves of p-HgTe of low and high temperatures in a more realistic manner.
