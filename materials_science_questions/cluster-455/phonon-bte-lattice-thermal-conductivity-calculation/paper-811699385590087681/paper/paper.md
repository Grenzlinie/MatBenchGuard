# Thermal conductivity and its anisotropy research of germanium thin film

Z. H. Wang · M. J. Ni

Received: 23 April 2008 / Accepted: 9 November 2010 / Published online: 8 December 2010
© Springer-Verlag 2010

## Abstract
Germanium is an important element in semi-conductor and microelectronics industries. Its thermal conductivity is an invariable and isotropic parameter in germanium crystal in the same temperature and its thermal conductivity is difficult to be measured in thin film. In the present paper, using the non-equilibrium molecular dynamics simulation method and Stillinger–Weber potential model, the normal and tangential thermal conductivity of germanium thin film are studied. There are remarkable anisotropy and size effect of thermal conductivity in normal and tangential direction of germanium thin film. In the theoretical analysis, Boltzmann transport theory is used to analyze the phonon transport in germanium thin film. Theoretical results further demonstrate the anisotropy and size effect of thermal conductivity in thin film, which indicate the significant decrease of phonon mean free path and predominant boundary phonon scattering in germanium thin film.

## 1 Introduction

The dielectric thin film with thickness at micrometer and nanometer scale act important roles in high-speed and large-scale integration circuits, and the heat generated by electric power must be removed efficiently from the system. Germanium is an important element in semiconductor and microelectronics industries. However, heat removal from the dielectric thin film is limited by the thermal conductivity of the germanium, its thermal conductivity is invariableness and isotropy in germanium crystal in the same temperature. But the thermal conductivity is difficult to be measured in thin film. So many researchers have paid their attention to the physical characteristic theoretical and numerical investigation of thin film system. In this case, molecular dynamics simulation method and Boltzmann transport theory are valuable tools for the study of nanometer scale heat conduction in solids.

In recent years, thermal resistance, thermal conductivity and other physical properties have been investigated in insulator and semiconductor using molecular dynamics simulation. Zhong [1] and Robert [2] studied the interfacial thermal resistance between carbon nanotubes and solid–solid interfaces using the molecular dynamics simulations. Tang [3] studied the thermal conductivity of silicon nanotubes using the molecular dynamics simulations. These studies showed that the obvious size effect and phonon boundary scattering existing in nanometer film. Wang [4, 5] studied thermal conductivity of silicon nanoscale film and found obvious size effect in nanoscale film. The Stillinger–Weber potential model is proved to be an effective potential model in molecular dynamics simulation. The results showed the relationship between normal thermal conductivity and film thickness was non-monotonic relation. The prediction thermal conductivity results were consistent with the experiment data of silicon. Han [6] studied the thermal properties of liquid cobalt using molecular dynamics simulation. Sun [7] studied the domain size effects in silicon phonon transport. These researches showed that there were two reasons for the decrease of film thermal conductivity: the restriction of phonon and the influence of interface resistance. The study showed equilibrium molecular dynamics simulation was strongly dependent on the initial conditions and had a slowly-converging autocorrelation function [8]. And non-equilibrium thermodynamics molecular dynamics (thermostat and zero-limited thermal force) yields best results [9]. So far, the thermal conductivity and its anisotropy of thin film have not been clearly analyzed in germanium thin film. In the paper, the molecular dynamics simulation method and Boltzmann transport theory are used to simulate and analyze the anisotropy of thermal conductivity of the normal and tangential thermal conductivity in germanium thin film with the film thickness ranging from 5.67 to 50.92 nm in 10–90 germanium crystal lattices.

## 2 Molecular dynamics simulation

In order to using non-equilibrium thermodynamics molecular dynamics simulation method, it is required to set up a particle system in which the film is heated by the power to produce the temperature difference. Figure 1 shows the different boundary condition of normal and tangential heat system.

Through solving dynamics equation, the movement of every particle can be acquired. In molecular dynamics simulation, a finite and limitless system with periodic boundary condition is built up to study. There are $N(N-1)$ pairs of acting force between $N$ interacting atoms. In the paper, $N=720$–6,480 atoms. Newton equations of motion of atomic system is

$$
m \frac{d^{2} r_{i}}{d t^{2}}=\sum_{j=1, j \neq i}^{N} F_{i j} \tag{1}
$$

where $F_{ij}$ is acting force of atom $j$ on atom $i$.

In non-equilibrium molecular dynamics simulation method, two constant temperature reservoirs are placed in normal and tangential direction. The high temperature reservoir is 330 K, the low temperature reservoir is 270 K and the average temperature of thin film is 300 K. The thickness of the germanium thin film ranges from 5.67 to 50.92 nm. The heat wall boundary is adopted in the normal film direction. When any atom moves near the heat wall boundary and collides with heat wall, the heat wall boundary will reflect it into the system. The periodic boundary is adopted in the plane of film. When any atom enters the periodic boundary, the atom will become a mirror atom in another side of the system.

The Stillinger–Weber potential function [10, 11] is employed to describe the interaction among different germanium atoms in the simulation film that is reliable to describe the germanium potential properties. The potential used in the study has two body and three body part. If there

Temperature can be computed since the Boltzmann
distribution function allows the straightforward derivation
of the kinetic energy in the following way.

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\sum_{i}^{N_{L}} m v_{i}^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{3}{2} N_{L} k_{\mathrm{B}} T_{L, \mathrm{MD}} \quad (5)
$$

where $N_{L}$ is the atom number in $L$ layer, $T_{L, \mathrm{MD}}$ is the
average temperature in $L$ layer. The reservoir temperature
decides the velocities of reflecting atoms, which is
a random sampling function of Maxwell velocity
distribution.

$$
\phi(v)=4 \pi N\left(\frac{m}{2 \pi k_{B} T_{\mathrm{w}}}\right)^{3 / 2} v^{2} \exp \left[-\frac{m v^{2}}{2 k_{\mathrm{B}} T_{\mathrm{w}}}\right] \quad (6)
$$

where $T_{w}$ is the reservoir temperature, $v$ is the atom
velocity, $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the Boltzmann constant number.

The thermodynamic parameter can be calculated
according to statistic physics method. When the system is
in local thermodynamic equilibrium, the thermodynamic
parameters can be got according to statistic physics theory.
The thermal conductivity can be computed according to
Fourier law.

$$
k=\frac{\Delta E_{k, \text { in }}-\Delta E_{k, \text { out }}}{2 t A|\nabla T|} \quad (7)
$$

where $\Delta E_{k, \text { in }}$ and $\Delta E_{k, \text { out }}$ are the heat transfer with high
temperature wall and low temperature wall; $\nabla T$ is the
temperature gradient along heat conduction direction; $t$ is a
time step; $A$ is the heat transfer area.

with the decrease of thin film thickness and show notable
dimensional effect in germanium thin film.

Germanium thermal conductivity mainly depends on the lattice vibration. In thin film, the lattice vibration and heat conduction are greatly influenced by the thin film boundary scattering. The surface's structure is remarkably different from it in body, and the atomic bond disconnects to be relaxation and reconfiguration so that the mean free path of phonon remarkably decreases. The influence of normal boundary phonon scattering more prominent than tangen- tial boundary phonon scattering resulting that the thermal conductivity decreases with the thin film thickness decreasing and tangential thermal conductivities are bigger than normal thermal conductivities. Figure 3 showed the ratio of tangential thermal conductivity $k_{T}$ to normal ther mal conductivity $k_{N}$ with the thin film thickness. The fol lowing equation can be gotten as a good power function relation between the ratio of tangential to normal conduc- tivity and the film thickness d using regression analysis method.

$$
\frac{k_{\mathrm{T}}}{k_{\mathrm{N}}}=1+28.27 d^{-1.4}
\tag{8}
$$

From the equation, we can find the ratio to tend to the constant value 1.0 which indicates the anisotropy of ther- mal conductivity in thin film and it would become isotropy in large scale with the film thickness increasing.

According to phonon gas kinetics theory, the thermal conductivity expression is

$$
k=\frac{1}{3} c v l_{\text {eff }}
\tag{9}
$$

where $c$ is the specific heat per unit volume, $v$ is the phonon average velocity, $l_{\text {eff }}$ is the valid phonon mean free path. When the film thickness is greater than phonon mean free path $l_{\infty}, l_{\text {eff }}=l_{\infty}$. In thin film, valid mean free path will be [12]

$$
\frac{1}{l_{\text {eff }}}=\frac{1}{l_{\infty}}+\frac{a}{d}
\tag{10}
$$

which $a$ is a variable decided by the valid phonon mean free path.

So we can get the equation between thin film thermal conductivity and film thickness.

$$
\frac{1}{k}=\frac{1}{k_{\infty}}+\frac{S}{d}
\tag{11}
$$

which $S$ is a variable decided by the valid phonon mean free path.

The parameter $S$ is decided by the boundary scattering in thin film which is not a constant value. Figure 4 and 5 showed the parameter $S$ variation with the film thickness increasing in normal and tangential direction thin film. From the figure, we can find a good power function relation between the parameter $S$ and the film thickness that shows the normal and tangential thermal conductivity would tend to the bulk crystal thermal conductivity in large scale. The thin film thickness restricts the mean free path so that the motion of phonon mainly represents the limitation of thin film. In germanium thin film, the anisotropy of the thermal conductivity rests with the diversification of thermal transport's direction and tangential direction's optimization choice because of the impact of the normal boundary scattering. According to the results of the molecular dynamics in Figs. 4 and 5, using regression analysis method, we can get the normal and tangential thermal conductivity will be the following equations which can be referred for the microelectronics engineering in germanium thin film.

## 3 Theory model

In order to verify the molecular dynamics simulation results, the Dubey model [13, 14] is used to analyze phonon relaxation time based on the Boltzmann transport theory. Real phonon dispersion in Dubey model has been taken into account to be transversal wave phonon and longitudinal wave phonon according to relaxation time. Dubey had taken the better dispersion relation to express the thermal conductivity with the help of the dispersion curve. It is also found that the velocity of phonons in the entire Brillouin Zone does not remain constant. Thus, one can express the phonon conductivity integral as

$$
k = k_{\mathrm{T}} + k_{\mathrm{L}} \tag{13}
$$

where $k_{\mathrm{T}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{L}}$ are the contributions of transverse and longitudinal phonons, respectively, and are given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
k_{T}=& \left(\frac{C}{v_{T 1}}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{1} / T} \tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{T}} x^{4} \mathrm{e}^{x}\left(\mathrm{e}^{x}-1\right)^{-2}\left(1+R_{1} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{2} \\
& \times\left(1+3 R_{1} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{-1} d x \\
&+\left(\frac{C}{v_{T 2}}\right) \int_{\theta_{1} / T}^{\theta_{2} / T} \tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{T}} x^{4} \mathrm{e}^{x}\left(\mathrm{e}^{x}-1\right)^{-2}\left(1+R_{2} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{2} \\
& \times\left(1+3 R_{2} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{-1} d x
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
k_{L}=& \left(\frac{C}{v_{L 1}}\right) \int_{0}^{\theta_{3} / T} \tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{L}} x^{4} \mathrm{e}^{x}\left(\mathrm{e}^{x}-1\right)^{-2}\left(1+R_{3} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{2} \\
& \times\left(1+3 R_{3} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{-1} d x \\
&+\left(\frac{C}{v_{L 2}}\right) \int_{\theta_{3} / T}^{\theta_{4} / T} \tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{L}} x^{4} \mathrm{e}^{x}\left(\mathrm{e}^{x}-1\right)^{-2}\left(1+R_{4} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{2} \\
& \times\left(1+3 R_{4} x^{2} T^{2}\right)^{-1} d x
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

where
$C=(k_{\mathrm{B}} / 3 \pi^{2})(k_{\mathrm{B}} T / \hbar)^{3}, R_{i}=r_{i}(k_{\mathrm{B}} / \hbar)^{2}, i=1,2,3$ and 4;
$v_{\mathrm{T} 1}, v_{\mathrm{T} 2}, v_{\mathrm{L} 1}$ and $v_{\mathrm{L} 2}$ are the transverse and longitudinal phonons velocities;
$\theta_{i}=(\hbar \omega_{i} / k_{\mathrm{B}}), i=1,2,3,4$, integrals limit parameter;
$\omega_{1}, \omega_{2}, \omega_{3}$ and $\omega_{4}$ are the frequencies of transverse and longitudinal phonons, respectively;
$\tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{T}}^{-1}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{L}}^{-1}$ are the combined relaxation rates for transverse and longitudinal phonons.

Supposing every scattering process to be independent, total average relaxation time can be computed according to Matthiessen law [15]

$$
\tau^{-1}=\tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{T}}^{-1}+\tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{L}}^{-1} \tag{16}
$$

The relaxation time of transversal wave and longitudinal wave models are [5]

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{T}}^{-1}=v_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{T}}\left(\frac{1}{L_{b}}+\frac{1}{d F}\right) \tag{17}
$$

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{L}}^{-1}=v_{\mathrm{C}, \mathrm{L}}\left(\frac{1}{L_{b}}+\frac{1}{d F}\right) \tag{18}
$$

where $d$ is the thickness of thin film, $v_{C,T}$ and $v_{C,L}$ are the transverse and longitudinal phonons velocities, $L_{b}$ is the Casimir length of the crystal, $F$ is decrement gene, which is

## 4 Conclusions

The present paper studied the normal and tangential direction thermal conductivity in germanium thin film using the molecular dynamics method. It is found that obvious size effect and anisotropy of thermal conductivity to be existed in germanium thin film because of the impact of the boundary scattering and significant decrease of phonon mean free path in germanium thin film.

According to the normal and tangential thermal conductivity simulation results’ regression analysis, the tangential thermal conductivities are bigger than normal thermal conductivities. The ratio of tangential and normal thermal conductivity is a good power function regression relation with the film thickness.

Theoretical analysis has been fulfilled based on the Boltzmann transport theory. Theoretical results further demonstrate the anisotropy and size effect of thermal conductivity in thin film, which indicate the significant decrease of phonon mean free path and predominant boundary phonon scattering in germanium thin film. The simulation results are valuable for the application of germanium thin film in microelectronics, optoelectronics, MEMS and the burgeoning nanotechnology.
