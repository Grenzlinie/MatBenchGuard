# CONDENSATE FRACTION OF LIQUID $^{4}$He AT LOW TEMPERATURES *

P.M. LAM
Institut für Theoretische Physik, Universität zu Köln, 5 Cologne 41, Germany

and

M.L. RISTIG $^{1}$
Max-Planck-Institut für Kernphysik, 69 Heidelberg, Germany

Received 29 November 1977

The condensate fraction of liquid $^{4}$He has been evaluated at low temperatures and density $\rho=0.0218 \AA^{-3}$ employing the diagrammatic cluster approach of Ristig and Clark within Feenberg's paired-phonon model. The calculations are based on the optimal Jastrow ground state which corresponds to the interatomic Lennard-Jones (6-12) potential.

One of the fundamental ideas underlying the modern theory of liquid $^{4}$He is that of macroscopic occupation of the zero-momentum single-particle state [1,2]. Woods and Sears have recently performed a new analysis of neutron-scattering results for liquid helium which suggests a value $n(T)=(6.9 \pm 0.8)\%$ for the condensate fraction at $T=1.1$ K [3]. Theoretical calculations of the quantity $n(T)$ have been performed at zero temperature. They are based on correlated wave functions of Jastrow type and give values of $n(T=0)$ ~ 9-13% at saturation density [4-6]. The condensate fraction decreases with increasing density $\rho$ and is sensitive to the interatomic potential assumed [7].

The present note describes a generalization of the diagrammatic approach of Ristig and Clark [8] for the condensate fraction to finite temperatures sufficiently below the transition temperature, $T<T_{\lambda}$.

The macroscopic occupation of the zero-momentum single-particle state is a consequence of the off-diagonal long-range order exhibited by the one-body density matrix [9]:
$$n(T)=\lim _{\left|\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime \prime}\right| \to \infty} \frac{1}{\rho}\left\langle\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}|\Gamma(T)| \boldsymbol{r}^{\prime \prime}\right\rangle.\tag{1}$$

Penrose [10] presented an explicit formula for the density matrix $\langle\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}|\Gamma(T)| \boldsymbol{r}^{\prime \prime}\rangle$ of liquid $^{4}$He under equilibrium conditions at temperatures $T<0.5$ K.

Feenberg [11] developed this concept systematically further within the framework of the method of correlated basis functions employing paired-phonon analysis [12]. The model rests on the existence of elementary excitations in $^{4}$He propagating freely through the liquid with neglect of all processes in which excitations scatter, coalesce or split. In Feenberg's approach the condensate fraction (1) is given by [11]:
$$\begin{aligned}
n(T) & =A(\rho N)^{-1} \exp \left[-\frac{1}{8}\left(T / T_{0}\right)^{2}\right] \\
& × \lim _{\left|\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime \prime}\right| \to \infty} \int \Psi\left(\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}, \boldsymbol{r}_{2},..., \boldsymbol{r}_{A} ; T\right) \\
& × \Psi\left(\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime \prime}, \boldsymbol{r}_{2},..., \boldsymbol{r}_{A} ; T\right) \mathrm{d} \boldsymbol{r}_{23 \ldots A}.
\end{aligned}\tag{2}$$

The temperature $T_{0}$ is defined as $T_{0}^{2}=\rho(\hbar s)^{3}$ $\times\left(m s^{2} k_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}\right)^{-1}$ with $m$ being the mass of a $^{4}$He atom, the velocity of ordinary sound $s$, the Boltzmann constant $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ and the saturation density $\rho$ taken to be $\rho$ $=0.0218 \AA^{-3}$. Inserting the actual values of the quantities involved we find $T_{0}=2.195$ K. $A$ is the total

number of $^4$He atoms and $N$ is the normalization integral of the temperature dependent function
$$
\Psi\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{1},..., \boldsymbol{r}_{A} ; T\right)=\prod_{i<j}^{A} f_{\mathrm{th}}\left(r_{i j}, T\right) \Psi_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{1},..., \boldsymbol{r}_{A}\right). \quad (3)
$$

$\Psi_{0}$ represents the optimal ground-state function of Jastrow type [12]:
$$
\Psi_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{1},..., \boldsymbol{r}_{A}\right)=\prod_{i<j}^{A} f\left(r_{i j}\right). \quad (4)
$$

The thermal correlations which develop in the fluid at finite temperatures are described by the function $f_{\mathrm{th}}(r, T)$. The explicit form of these correlations is [11]:
$$
2 \ln f_{\mathrm{th}}(r, T) \equiv w(r, T)=\int w(k, T) \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \boldsymbol{k} \cdot \boldsymbol{r}} \mathrm{d} \boldsymbol{k}, \quad (5)
$$
$$
w(k, T)=\frac{2}{\rho}\left\{S(k)\left[\exp \frac{\epsilon(k)}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}+1\right]\right\}^{-1}. \quad (6)
$$

Expression (6) vanishes as $T \to 0$. The function $S(k)$ is the liquid structure function corresponding to the ground state (4), $\epsilon(k)$ represents the dispersion relation of the elementary excitations.

In the paired-phonon model the excitation energy is described by the Bijl-Feynman formula [12]:
$$
\epsilon(k) \to \epsilon_{0}(k)=\left(\hbar^{2} / 2 m\right) k^{2} S^{-1}(k). \quad (7)
$$

The substitution of $S(k)$ by $\hbar k(2 m s)^{-1}$ in eq. (6) together with
$$
\epsilon(k) \to \epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}(k)=\hbar s k, \quad (8)
$$
defines the hydrodynamic limit which gives an adequate description of phonon effects. In this specialization the correlations (5) are described by the expression
$$
w(r, T) \to w_{\mathrm{p}}(r, T)=\left(T / T_{0}\right)^{2} x^{-2}\left(1-x \sinh ^{-1} x\right), (9)
$$
with $x=r \pi(\hbar s)^{-1} k_{\mathrm{B}} T$. The limit (8) provides a good approximation for the condensate fraction (1) of liquid $^4$He in the temperature range $T<0.5$ K [11].

To include initial signs of strong roton effects Feenberg introduced the "empirical" approximation
$$
\epsilon(k) \to \epsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(k), \quad (10)
$$
or, more generally, $\epsilon(k) \to \epsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(k, T)$, which represents the experimental energy functions at given temperature. It is expected that replacement (10) takes account of a substantial part of the effects generated by the interactions between and among the elementary excitations. The range of validity of the formalism extends now above 1 K [11].

We have evaluated numerically the thermal correlations at various temperatures and $\rho=0.0218 \AA^{-1}$ for the paired-phonon model (7), the approximation (9) and the "empirical" approximation (10) employing the experimental results of ref. [13]. The optimal Jastrow function $f(r)$ and the structure function $S(k)$ have been obtained by carrying out a selfconsistent paired-phonon analysis in conjunction with the hypernetted-chain approximation, the interatomic potential being described by the Lennard-Jones (6-12) form [14].

The results at $T=1.4$ K are shown in fig. 1. The empirical approximation exhibits a structure which reflects the presence of roton effects. For temperatures above 1.4 K the function $w_{\mathrm{e}}(r, T)$ begins to develop pronounced peaks at $r=0$. With decreasing temperature the structure disappears and the three curves merge together.

Combining eqs. (4) and (5) we find
$$
\Psi\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{1},..., \boldsymbol{r}_{A} ; T\right)=\prod_{i<j}^{A} f_{\mathrm{th}}\left(r_{i j}, T\right) f\left(r_{i j}\right) \equiv \prod_{i<j}^{A} f\left(r_{i j}, T\right).
$$

$$
n(T)=\exp \left\{-\frac{1}{8}\left(T / T_{0}\right)^{2}+Q(T)\right\}, \quad(12)

for the condensate fraction (2). The function $Q(T)$ is
defined by $Q(T)=2 D[\xi]-D[\eta], D$ being a functional of the correlations $\xi(r, T)=f(r, T)-1$ or $\eta(r, T)$
$=f^{2}(r, T)-1$ given explicitly in ref. [8] .

At the present stage of development we approximate the function $Q(T)$ by

$$
Q(T)=Q_{1}(T)+Q_{2}(T)+Q_{3}(T). \quad(13)
$$

The first term is $Q_{1}(T)=-\rho \int \xi^{2}(r, T) \mathrm{d} r$ and the
second term is a functional of the liquid structure function at finite temperatures

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{2}(T) & =\frac{\rho}{(2 \pi)^{3}} \int \mathrm{d} k[S(k, T)-1] \\
& \times\left[\xi^{2}(k, T)-\frac{1}{2} \eta^{2}(k, T)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The third term, $Q_{3}(T)$, involves the three-body structure function which we approximate by its convolution form [7,8] .

Exploiting separability in the paired-phonon analysis [11,12] the structure function at finite temperatures is simply

$$
S(k, T)=S(k) \operatorname{coth}\left(\frac{1}{2} \epsilon(k) / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\right). \quad(15)
$$

The separability condition is essentially the statement
that the total number of excitations must be small
compared to the number of particles [11] :

$$
\nu(T)=\frac{1}{2 \pi^{2} \rho} \int_{0}^{\infty} k^{2} \mathrm{~d} k\left[\exp \frac{\epsilon(k)}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}-1\right]^{-1} \ll 1. \quad(16)
$$

In the temperature range $T<1.6 \mathrm{~K}$ condition (16)
is very well fulfilled (table 1), $\nu(T)$ being less than $2 \%$
in the various approximations (7), (8) and (10). For
an independent check of eq. (15) we have evaluated
$S(k, T)$ by performing a hypernetted-chain calculation
based on function (11). We found excellent agreement
of the result with the expression (15), even up to $T$
$=3 \mathrm{~K}$.

The results for the condensate fraction $n(T)$ based
on eqs. (12), (13) are collected in table 2. We note
that the value of $\left|Q_{3}\right|$ at any temperature considered
is small compared with the value of $\left|Q_{1}+Q_{2}\right|$; for example, at $T=1.4 \mathrm{~K}$ we get $\left|Q_{3}\right|=0.119$ and $\left|Q_{1}+Q_{2}\right|$
$=1.872$. The fraction $n(T)$ depends weakly on temperature and is insensitive to the various approximations
(7), (8), and (10). The model adopted limits the validity of our calculation for liquid ${ }^{4} \mathrm{He}$ to temperatures
below $T \lesssim 1.4 \mathrm{~K}$.
