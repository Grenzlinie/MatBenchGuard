# Angular and mode distribution of acoustic phonon emission by hot 2D electrons in GaAs: the effect of acoustic anisotropy and screening

Cz. Jasiukiewicz$^\text{a}$, D. Lehmann$^\text{b,*}$, A.J. Kent$^\text{c}$, A.J. Cross$^\text{c}$, P. Hawker$^\text{c}$

$^\text{a}$Institut für Theoretische Physik, TU Dresden, D-01062 Dresden, Germany

## Abstract

We report a detailed theoretical study of the angular and polarization dependence of the acoustic phonon emission by a hot two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) in GaAs/AlGaAs heterojunctions and quantum wells (QW) and compare the results with some recent heat pulse measurements. We use a model which not only includes the dynamical screening of electron–phonon interaction but also takes proper account of the finite “thickness” of the 2D gas and above all, considers the strong acoustic anisotropy of the electron–phonon matrix elements and the propagation process. For QW with different widths we demonstrate the strong dependence of the angular distribution on the well width. For heterostructures in the (0 0 1)-plane, we can explain, in contrast to earlier theoretical studies, the phenomena of missing longitudinal modes for phonon emission close to the two-dimenional electron gas (2DEG) normal. © 1999 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

**Keywords:** Phonon emission; Electron–phonon interaction; Two-dimensional electron gas

## 1. Introduction

Phonon emission by hot 2D electrons has been observed in a number of heat pulse experiments (for a review see Ref. [1]). Using this technique angular and temporal resolution of the emitted phonons is possible. Common to all the heat pulse experiments was that for 2D electrons in the (001) plane of GaAs heterostructures, no signal due to longitudinal acoustic (LA) phonons propagating in a direction close to the 2DEG normal could be detected. Only transverse acoustic (TA) phonons were picked up on a bolometer directly opposite the 2DEG. This result was in strong contradiction to earlier theoretical studies which suggested that the emission of deformation potential (DP) coupled LA modes should be dominant at angles close to the [100] direction in the electron temperature range $10\ \text{K} < T_{\text{e}} < 40\ \text{K}$. The inclusion of phonon focusing effects alone does not explain this discrepancy. To explain this phenomenon and to understand fully the process of electron–phonon coupling

### 2. Phonon emission rate

To describe the emission of acoustic phonons we consider a quasi-2DEG, containing $N$ carriers. The electrons have an effective temperature $T_{\rm e}$ and the lattice temperature is $T < T_{\rm e}$.

The general expression of the acoustic phonon emission rate per electron is within linear response theory [3]
$$
\begin{aligned}
P= & \frac{2}{N V} \sum_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda} \omega_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}\left(N_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}^{T}-N_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}^{T_{\mathrm{e}}}\right)\left|h_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}\right|^{2}\left|G\left(q_{\perp}\right)\right|^{2} \\
& \times \operatorname{Im}\left\{\frac{\chi_{T_{\mathrm{e}}}\left(\omega_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}, \boldsymbol{q}_{||}\right)}{1-v\left(q_{||}\right) g\left(q_{||}\right) \chi_{T_{\mathrm{e}}}\left(\omega_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}, \boldsymbol{q}_{||}\right)}\right\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\omega_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}$ is the frequency of a phonon in a substra- te of volume $V$ and density $\rho$ with (3D) wave vector $\boldsymbol{q}$, polarization $\lambda$ ($\lambda=$ LA, fast TA, slow TA) and polarization vector $\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}, \quad N_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}^{T}=\left(\mathrm{e}^{\hbar \omega_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda} / k_{\mathrm{B}} T}-1\right)^{-1}$. $h_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}$ is the familiar electron–phonon matrix element
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}= & \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2 \rho \omega_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}}}\left\{\mathrm{i} \Xi_{\mathrm{D}} \boldsymbol{q} \boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}\right. \\
& \left.+2 e h_{14} \frac{e_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda_{x}} q_{y} q_{z}+e_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda_{z}} q_{x} q_{y}+e_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda_{y}} q_{z} q_{x}}{q^{2}}\right\}, \quad (2)
\end{aligned}
$$
which includes both DP and piezoelectric (PE) coupling. $\Xi_{\mathrm{D}}, h_{14}$ are the relevant coupling con- stants. The functions $g(q_{||})$ and $G(q_{\perp})$ arise from the finite extension of the electron wave functions along the axis normal to the 2DEG plane and depend strongly on the chosen confinement po- tential. $v(q_{||})$ is the 2D Fourier transform of the Coulomb potential. Electron–electron interaction is considered in RPA, thus $\chi_{T_{\mathrm{e}}}(\omega_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}, \boldsymbol{q}_{||})$ is the polar izability function for a non-interacting 2DEG at temperature $T_{\mathrm{e}}$.

Taking into account the focusing of the phonons in the GaAs substrate from formula (1) the emitted power per electron in all directions of real space is derived. For a given direction $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}$ the emitted power of mode $\lambda$ in the unit solid angle around $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}$ is in the long-wave limit
$$
P_{\hat{r} \lambda}=\frac{2}{N(2 \pi)^{3}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\boldsymbol{q}_{\lambda}}} \mathscr{A}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{i} \lambda} P_{\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{i} \lambda},
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{P}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{q}} \lambda}= & \frac{1}{c_{\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}, \lambda}^{3}} \int \mathrm{d} \omega \omega^{3}\left|h_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}\right|^{2}\left|G\left(q_{\perp}\right)\right|^{2}\left(N_{\omega}^{T}-N_{\omega}^{T_{\mathrm{c}}}\right) \\
& \times \operatorname{Im}\left\{\frac{\chi_{T_{\mathrm{c}}}\left(\omega, \boldsymbol{q}_{||}\right)}{1-v\left(q_{||}\right) g\left(q_{||}\right) \chi_{T_{\mathrm{c}}}\left(\omega, \boldsymbol{q}_{||}\right)}\right\},

$$
where $\mathscr{A}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{q}} \lambda}$ is the focusing factor and the sum is over all directions of phonon wave vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{i}(i=1, \ldots$, $N_{\hat{\boldsymbol{q}} \lambda}$ ) for which the corresponding direction of the group velocity $\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{q}}_{i} \lambda}$ is equal to $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}$. As input the group velocity $\boldsymbol{v}_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}$, the phase velocity $c_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}$ and the polarization vector $\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{q} \lambda}$ have to be calculated numerically for all phonons $(\boldsymbol{q} \lambda)$.

## 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1 shows the calculated patterns of the phonon energy flux on the surface of the (100) wafer directly opposite the 2DEG for two QWs with width 3 nm (Fig. 1a) and 15 nm (Fig. 1b). The patterns were calculated using for the DP coupling constant $\Xi_{\mathrm{D}}=9 \mathrm{eV}$ and the PE constant $h_{14}=$ $1.4 \times 10^{9} \mathrm{eV} / \mathrm{m}$. They clearly demonstrate the fact that the emission is highly anisotropic, and depends strongly on the well width.

To compare with the heat pulse experiments, we integrated the flux in a window corresponding to the detector size. Fig. 2 shows the dependence of the phonon signal for the different modes and coupling mechanism as a function of QW width. The detector $(100 \times 10 \mu \mathrm{m})$ is centrally opposite the 2DEG $(120 \times 50 \mu \mathrm{m})$, the calculations were done for a $0.4 \mathrm{~mm}$ (001) substrate. For the form factor we used a confining potential with finite depth of the well $(350 \mathrm{meV})$. It is interesting, that the contribution from the PE coupled LA phonons is negligible (not seen in Fig. 2), but a significant contribution arises from the DP coupled TA phonons (this term is zero in the isotropic approximation for the electron-phonon matrix elements). For the heterostructure we used a confining potential with a conduction band offset of $225 \mathrm{meV}$ allowing a penetration of the wave function into the barrier. In all cases our new theory is in reasonable agreement with the experimental results, validating this new approach and very much better than the "conventional" theory. The results show clearly the importance of including all of the four factors: acoustic anisotropy of the electron–phonon interaction, screening, finite "thickness" of the 2DEG, and focusing in the substrate. Particularly, we have demonstrated that the isotropic approximation of the electron–phonon matrix elements is insufficient for the theoretical description of heat pulse experiments.

No free parameters were used in our theory, a further improvement could be reached by fitting the ratio of the coupling constants. Other reasons for the small discrepancies could be the uncertainty in the temperature of the hot 2DEG and/or an additional contribution of TA phonons from the decay of renormalized (plasmon-like) optical modes.
