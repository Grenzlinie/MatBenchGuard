# Calculation of Electrical Resistivity
of Some Liquid Metals by the Mode-Coupling Method

By
D. N. JHA $^{1)}$

The resistivities and temperature coefficients of resistivity (TCR) of some liquid metals (Ag, Au, Fe, Ni, Eu) are calculated by the mode-coupling approximation method of Götze and compared with respective experimental values. Good agreement is found for some of them. The sign of TCR is closely related to the variations in the values of the electron-phonon coupling parameter.

Die Widerstände und die Temperaturkoeffizienten des Widerstandes (TCR) einiger flüssiger Metalle (Ag, Au, Fe, Ni, Eu) werden mittels der Moden-Kopplungs-Näherungsmethode von Götze berechnet und mit entsprechenden experimentellen Werten verglichen. Für einige von ihnen wird gute Übereinstimmung gefunden. Das Vorzeichen der TCR ist eng verknüpft mit den Änderungen der Werte der Elektron-Phonon-Kopplungsparameter.

## 1. Introduction
Different liquid metals have been considered in the present paper, in a quantitative calculation of their resistivity and TCR (temperature coefficient of resistivity), based on the mode-coupling approximation [1 to 5].

In Section 2, the mathematical formalism has been presented, followed by the results and discussions in Section 3.

## 2. Formulation
The expressions for different correlation functions and conductivity, as used in the practical calculations, have been quoted in the Appendix.

The terms $U_{1}(q)$ and $U_{2}(q)$ are the local and non-local potentials describing random scattering and random tunnelling processes, respectively [3]. It was analysed [3] that the origin of $U_{2}(q)$ should be attributed to the phonon disorder to explain Mooij's observation [6], i.e. the occurrence of negative TCR for high resistivity (above $150 \mu \Omega cm$ ), disordered systems, in general. To account for the temperature dependence of resistivity, the electron-phonon coupling term was introduced by Belitz and Schir- macher [4]. To characterise different individual liquid metals for quantitative cal- culations of their resistivity and TCR, it is necessary to substitute suitable expres- sions for the potentials and analyse the dependence of resistivity on electron-phonon coupling strength parameter at high temperatures.

For the sake of proper selection of the potential form factors $u_{1}(q)$ and $u_{2}(q)$, the energy-dependent $\Gamma_{q}^{KKR}$ matrix elements may be interpreted as an effective potential

experienced by the valence electrons (Matsubara [7]),

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}= & \frac{4 \pi R^{2}}{\Omega} \sum_{l=0}^{2}(2 l+1) \mathrm{P}_{l}(\cos \theta) \mathrm{j}_{l}(\boldsymbol{K} \cdot \boldsymbol{R}) \mathrm{j}_{l}(|\boldsymbol{K}+\boldsymbol{q}| \cdot \boldsymbol{R}) \times \\
& \times\left[\frac{1}{R_{l}} \frac{\mathrm{d} R_{l}}{\mathrm{~d} r}-\frac{\mathrm{j}_{l}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{K} \cdot \boldsymbol{R})}{\mathrm{j}_{l}(\boldsymbol{K} \cdot \boldsymbol{R})}\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

where $R$ is the muffin-tin radius, $\Omega$ the atomic volume, and $\cos \theta=1-q^{2} / 2 K_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}$. The other notations are as usual.

The variation of phase shifts $\delta_{l}(l=0,1,2)$ with energy introduces the energy dependence of matrix elements $\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}$ through the logarithmic derivative,

$$
\frac{1}{R_{l}} \frac{\mathrm{d} R_{l}}{\mathrm{~d} r}=\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{r}}}\left[\frac{\cos \delta_{l} \mathrm{j}_{l}^{\prime}-\sin \delta_{l} \mathrm{n}_{l}^{\prime}}{\cos \delta_{l} \mathrm{j}_{l}-\sin \delta_{l} \mathrm{n}_{l}}\right],
\tag{2}
$$

where $\mathrm{n}_{l}$ is the Neuman function.

The strong dependence of the phase shifts $\delta_{2}$ for different liquid metals on energy (Faber [8]), compared with $\delta_{0}$ and $\delta_{1}$ implicitly imparts a non-local character to $\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}(l=2)$. The comparatively slow dependence of $\delta_{0}$ on energy reflects the local character of $\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}(l=0)$ and it can be chosen as the local potential form factor $u_{1}(q)$ with $\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}(l=1)$ added to serve the purpose of continuity of the correlation between the local potential $U_{1}(q)$ and non-local one $U_{2}(q)$, in accordance with the original parametric model of these potentials [3]. A change in $u_{2}(q)$ brings forth a simultaneous variation in $u_{1}(q)$ and the potential correlation $\left\langle U_{1}^{*}(q) U_{2}(q)\right\rangle$ naturally comes out to be non-local in character,

$$
\begin{aligned}
& u_{1}(q)=\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}(l=0)+\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}(l=1), \\
& u_{2}(q)=\Gamma_{q}^{\mathrm{KKR}}(l=2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

Due to the ambiguous nature of the potential fluctuation range $q_{0}$ and the electron-phonon coupling potential parameter $v(q)$, no definite values can be attributed to them. They are, therefore, considered to be adjustable parameters, which is in accordance with the paper of Belitz and Schirmacher [4].

### 3. Results and Discussion

The equations were solved self-consistently by direct iteration for the non-negative solutions of $L_{0}^{\prime \prime}, M_{0}^{\prime \prime}$, and $\Phi^{\prime \prime}, \Phi_{1}^{\prime \prime}$, and $\Phi_{\mathrm{t}}^{\prime \prime}$ and subsequently the resistivity was calculated in $\mu \Omega \mathrm{cm}$ by adjusting the electron-phonon coupling potential parameter $|v(q)|^{2}=|V|^{2}$ and the potential fluctuation range $q_{0}$. Convergence up to any desired accuracy was available.

The input data is listed in Table 1. The estimated values of the Fermi energy $E_{\mathrm{F}}$, the Fermi wave vector $K_{\mathrm{F}}$, and phase shifts were taken from earlier works [9 to 12]. The hard-sphere and muffin-tin radii were estimated as usual and the static structure factor $S(q)$ was chosen as the well-known Percus-Yevick hard-sphere model and $T^{*}$ as the melting point of the metal. To calculate the electron density $n$, only those electronic configurations were considered, which exist in the largest abundance and it can be seen that the resistivity is inversely proportional to $n$.

The adjustable parameters $|V|$ and $q_{0}$ showed the interesting feature that for every liquid metal numerically there exists a critical value of $|V|$ for different values of $q_{0}$, above which TCR is always negative and below which it is positive. In Table 3, the characteristic length $l^*$, as given in [4] and the potential extension $L_0=2\pi/q_0$ have been listed. It can be observed that $L_0\geqq l^*$. A deeper theoretical interpretation of the potential extension is needed for disordered systems.

The calculated and experimental values of resistivity for some liquid metals (like Au, Fe) differ appreciably, which is quite sensitive to the values of $q_0$ or potential extension range $L_0$. The value of $q_0$ or $L_0$ for different liquid metals cannot be approximated a priori for disordered systems. Therefore, the result provides estimated values of $q_0$ up to an obtainable accuracy.

The comparitively large value of $|V|$ for liquid Eu with its negative TCR is in conformity with the findings of Jäckle and Fröbos [15] that for disordered systems a large value of electron-phonon coupling parameter does not necessarily mean a greater value of TCR. Unfortunately, experimental values of electron-phonon coupling constants for disordered systems are not available. The adjusted values of $|V|$ may be compared with the values obtained by some other methods.

### Appendix

In the appendix expressions for different correlation functions have been quoted, as used in calculations [1 to 4].

The static parts of the relaxation kernels for density and longitudinal momentum are $(\hbar=1)$

$$
L_{0}(z)=\frac{4}{q_{0}^{7}} \int_{0}^{q_{0}} \mathrm{~d} q q^{2}\left\langle\left|U_{2}(q)\right|^{2}\right\rangle\left[\Phi_{1}(q, z)+2 \Phi_{t}(q, z)\right], \tag{A1}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{0}(z)= & \frac{1}{m n q_{0}^{3}} \int_{0}^{q_{0}} \mathrm{~d} q q^{4}\left[\left\langle\left|U_{1}(q)\right|^{2}\right\rangle+\right. \\
& \left.+2 q_{0}^{-2}\left\langle U_{1}^{*}(q) U_{2}(q)\right\rangle E(q)+q_{0}^{-4}\left\langle\left|U_{2}(q)\right|^{2}\right\rangle E^{2}(q)\right] \Phi(q, z). \tag{A2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The temperature-dependent parts of these relaxation kernels are given, in the dc limit $z=0+i 0$, by

$$
L_{\mathrm{T}}^{\prime \prime}=\frac{4}{q_{0}^{7}} \int_{0}^{q_{0}} \mathrm{~d} q[q v(q)]^{2}\left[\Phi_{1}^{\prime \prime}(q, 0)+2 \Phi_{\mathrm{t}}^{\prime \prime}(q, 0)\right] S_{\mathrm{T}}(q), \tag{A3}
$$

$$
M_{\mathrm{T}}^{\prime \prime}=\frac{1}{m n q_{0}^{7}} \int_{0}^{q_{0}} \mathrm{~d} q\left[q^{2} E(q) v(q)\right]^{2} \Phi^{\prime \prime}(q, 0) S_{\mathrm{T}}(q). \tag{A4}
$$

The density correlation function is given by

$$
\Phi(q, z) \equiv \frac{\Phi^{0}\left[q, z+M_{0}(z)\right]}{1+\left[M_{0}(z)-\frac{1}{3}(q \xi)^{2} L_{0}(z) / g(q)\right] \Phi^{0}\left[q, z+M_{0}(z)\right] / g(q)}, \tag{A5}
$$

where $\xi=2 K_{\mathrm{F}} / q_{0}$ and

$$
\Phi^{0}\left[q, z+M_{0}(z)\right]=\frac{\varrho_{\mathrm{F}}}{z+M_{0}(z)}\left[K^{0}\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}, \frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}}\right]-K^{0}\left(\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)\right]. \tag{A6}
$$

The generalised compressibility $g(q \rightarrow 0)=\varrho_{\mathrm{F}}$ (density of states) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{g(q)}{\varrho_{\mathrm{F}}}=K^{0} & \left(\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4\left(q / 2 K_{\mathrm{F}}\right)}\left[1-\left(\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)^{2}\right] \times \\
& \times \ln \left|\frac{\left(q / 2 K_{\mathrm{F}}\right)+1}{\left(q / 2 K_{\mathrm{F}}\right)-1}\right|. \tag{A7}
\end{aligned}
$$

The Lindhard function is

$$
\begin{aligned}
& K^{0}\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}, \frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}}\right]=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{8\left(q / 2 K_{\mathrm{F}}\right)}\left\{\left(1-\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}-\frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}} \frac{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}{q}\right]^{2}\right) \times\right. \\
& \times \ln \left[\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}+1-\frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}} \frac{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}{q}\right]\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}-1-\frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}} \frac{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}{q}\right]^{-1}\right]+ \\
& +\left(1-\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}+1+\frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}} \frac{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}{q}\right]^{2}\right) \ln \left\{\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}+1+\frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}} \frac{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}{q}\right] \times\right. \\
& \left.\left.\times\left[\frac{q}{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}-1+\frac{z+M_{0}(z)}{4 E_{\mathrm{F}}} \frac{2 K_{\mathrm{F}}}{q}\right]^{-1}\right\}\right\}. \tag{A8}
\end{aligned}
$$

The longitudinal momentum correlation function is
$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{l}}(q, z)=\frac{3}{(q \xi)^{2}}\left\{\left[z+\frac{1}{3}(q \xi)^{2} \frac{L_{0}(z)}{g(q)}\right]\left[z+\frac{1}{3}(q \xi)^{2} \frac{L_{0}(z)}{g(q)}\right] \Phi(q, z)+g(q)\right\}. \quad(\mathrm{A} 9)
$$

The transverse momentum correlation function reads
$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{t}}(q, z) \equiv \Phi_{\mathrm{t}}^{0}\left[q, z+M_{0}(z)\right]=\frac{n m}{z+M_{0}(z)}\left\{X_{\mathrm{t}}^{0}\left(\left[q, z+M_{0}(z)\right]-g_{\mathrm{t}}(q)\right\}, \quad(\mathrm{A} 10)\right.
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_{\mathrm{t}}^{0}\left[q, z+M_{0}(z)\right]= & \frac{1}{8}\left[\left(5-3 Q^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{3 Z_{1}}{Q}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{4 Q^{5}}\left(Z_{1}-\Omega_{+}\right)^{2}\left(Z_{1}-\Omega_{-}\right)^{2} \times\right. \\
& \times \ln \left(\frac{Z_{1}-\Omega_{+}}{Z_{1}-\Omega_{-}}\right)+\left(Z_{1}+\Omega_{+}\right)^{2}\left(Z_{1}+\Omega_{-}\right)^{2} \times \\
& \left.\times \ln \left(\frac{Z_{1}+\Omega_{+}}{Z_{1}-\Omega_{-}}\right)\right]
\end{aligned}
$$
and transverse momentum compressibility is
$$
g_{\mathrm{t}}(q)=\frac{1}{8}\left[\left(5-3 Q^{2}\right)+\frac{3}{2 Q}\left(1-Q^{2}\right)^{2} \ln \left|\frac{1+Q}{1-Q}\right|\right], \quad \text { (A12) }
$$
where $Z_{1}=(z+M_{0}(z)) / 4 E_{\mathrm{F}}, Q=q / 2 K_{\mathrm{F}}, \Omega_{ \pm}=Q^{2} \pm Q$.

The dc conductivity is
$$
\sigma=e^{2}\left\{\frac{n / m}{M_{0}^{\prime \prime}(0)+M_{\mathrm{T}}^{\prime \prime}(0)}+\left[L_{0}^{\prime \prime}(0)+L_{\mathrm{T}}^{\prime \prime}(0)\right]\right\}. \quad \text { (A13) }
$$

The expressions for the vertex function $E(q)$ and the dynamical structure factor $S_{\mathrm{T}}(q)$ are the same as in [4]. The average potentials are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\langle\left|U_{1}(q)\right|^{2}\right\rangle & =\left|u_{1}(q)\right|^{2} X_{i} S(q), \\
\left\langle\left|U_{2}(q)\right|^{2}\right\rangle & =\left|u_{2}(q)\right|^{2} X_{i} S(q), \\
\left\langle U_{1}^{*}(q) U_{2}(q)\right\rangle & =\left|u_{1}(q)\right|\left|u_{2}(q)\right| X_{i} S(q),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $X_{i}$ is the atomic concentration, $S(q)$ the static structure factor, and $u_{1}(q)$ and $u_{2}(q)$ are suitable potential form factors.
