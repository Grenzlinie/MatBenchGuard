# Estimating Thermodynamic Stability Relationship of Polymorphs of Sofosbuvir

Ming-Hui Qi, $^\dagger$ Ming-Huang Hong, $^\dagger$ Yan Liu, En-Fu Wang, Fu-Zheng Ren and Guo-Bin Ren*

Laboratory of Pharmaceutical Crystal Engineering & Technology, School of Pharmacy, East China University of Science and Technology, Shanghai 200237, P. R. China

ABSTRACT: The polymorphism of sofosbuvir has been investigated. Three polymorphs have been identified and characterized by X-ray powder diffraction (XRPD) and thermal analysis. Form A and Form B was determined as enantiotropic system according to the Heat of Fusion Rule. The melting data and isobaric heat capacities of both polymorph have been determined calorimetrically, and the solubility data of each polymorph in water at different temperatures have been determined. With these data, the transition temperature ($T_{tr}$) was then estimated using solubility extrapolation method, heat of transition ($\Delta H_{tr}$) method, melting data method and configurational free energy ($G_c$) phase diagram method. The values of $T_{tr}$ was found to be 93.29 °C, 90.59 °C and 108.37 °C for the first three method, respectively. In the $G_c$ phase diagram method case, $T_{tr}$ was found to be 109.25 °C when eq 19 was used, compared to 109.28 °C when eq 20 was used.

INTRODUCTION

Polymorphism is defined as the ability of a compound to exist in several solid crystalline phases, which have different arrangements and/or conformations of molecules in the solid state.¹

Increasing numbers of polymorphs have been recorded over the past decades proving the growing interest in polymorphism in pharmaceutical industry, because of different polymorphs have different properties and solubility. Process parameters such as solvents, temperature, ultrasound, supersaturation, and even storage conditions, can greatly affect the appearance of polymorphs or induce polymorphism.²·³ Polymorphs fall into two types, i.e., enantiotropic system or monotropic system. Polymorphs that are enantiotropic have $T_{tr}$, at which, their Gibbs free energy are equal, below the melting temperature ($T_{m}$) of the lowest melting polymorph, and the relative stability between two polymorphs changes before and after $T_{tr}$. In contrast, polymorphs that are monotropic have their $T_{t}$ beyond $T_{m}$ of the polymorphs and one form is always more stable than another across the temperature range.⁴·⁵ Therefore, evaluating the thermodynamic stability relationships of polymorphs and estimating in the case of enantiotropic polymorphic pairs is very important for drug development, as a more stable form should be chosen on the basis of the thermodynamic stability.

Sofosbuvir⁶·⁷ (Sovaldi®; formerly GS-7977; Gilead Sciences, USA) is a novel anti-HCV agent that has demonstrated impressively high SVR rates that have never been seen before in HCV-infected patients. It is a nucleotide analogue inhibitor of the HCV NS5B polymerase that was approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration on 6 December 2013 for the treatment of chronic HCV infection in patients with genotypes 1, 2, 3 or 4, including those with hepatocellular carcinoma meeting Milan criteria (awaiting liver transplantation) and those with HCV/HIV-1 coinfection. There are a number of reports available in the literature on the synthesis⁸, pharmacology and pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics⁹ or clinical studies¹⁰ of

sofosbuvir. However, the thermodynamic relationship of polymorphs of sofosbuvir has not been reported until now. In this work, we determine the thermodynamic relationship of polymorphs of sofosbuvir, as well as estimate the $T_{tr}$ of a polymorphic pair which are defined as enantiotropic system on the basis of four methods: solubility extrapolation method, $\Delta H_{tr}$ method, melting data method and $G_{c}$ phase diagram method.

It was found that sofosbuvir exists in six polymorphic forms (Forms 1-6), out of which Forms 4 and 5 are solvates, Forms 2 and 3 are very unstable that can easily transform to Form 1 on isolation, while Form 1 will be transformed into Form 6 (refer to as Form A in the context for the purpose of convenient description) as an anhydrous solid when suspends in water 5-50 mg/mL at ambient temperature over a few hours.¹¹ Recently, we found a new crystal form of sofosbuvir named Form B via High-throughput screening technique, Form B will also be transformed into Form A via solution-mediated phase transformation (SMPT).

THEORETICAL SECTION

**Solubility Extrapolation Method.** The solubility of a crystal, as a function of the temperature follows van't Hoff equation¹²:

$$
\ln X = \frac{\Delta H_f}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_m} - \frac{1}{T}\right) \tag{1}
$$

where $X$ is the ideal molar solubility, $R$ is the ideal gas constant, $\Delta H_f$ is the heat of fusion, $T_m$ is the onset melting temperature, and $T$ is any temperature. Provided $\Delta H_f$ is assumed to be independent of $T$ in a narrow temperature range, therefore, the $\ln X$ versus $1/T$ plot is linear. At the transition temperature, the Gibbs free energies or solubility of two polymorphs are equal. $T_{tr}$ is then estimated by extrapolating the solubility curves to the intersection point.

$\Delta H_{tr}$ Method.$^{13}$ According to eq 1, at a temperature of $T_0$, the solubility of an enantiotropic polymorphic pair Form A and Form B can be written as below:

$$
\ln X_{A,T_0} = \frac{\Delta H_{f,A,T_0}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_{m,A}} - \frac{1}{T_0}\right) \tag{2}
$$

$$
\ln X_{B,T_0} = \frac{\Delta H_{f,B,T_0}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_{m,B}} - \frac{1}{T_0}\right) \tag{3}
$$

Subtracting eq 3 from eq 2, obtains:

$$
\ln X_{A,T_0} - \ln X_{B,T_0} = -\frac{\Delta H_{tr,T_0}}{RT_0} + \frac{\Delta H_{f,A,T_0}}{RT_{m,A}} - \frac{\Delta H_{f,B,T_0}}{RT_{m,B}} \tag{4}
$$

where $\Delta H_{tr,T_0}$ is the heat of transition at $T_0$ corresponding to the difference in the heat of fusion ($\Delta H_{f,A,T_0} - \Delta H_{f,B,T_0}$). In the same manner, at $T_{tr}$:

$$
\ln X_{A,T_{tr}} - \ln X_{B,T_{tr}} = -\frac{\Delta H_{tr,T_{tr}}}{RT_{tr}} + \frac{\Delta H_{f,A,T_{tr}}}{RT_{m,A}} - \frac{\Delta H_{f,B,T_{tr}}}{RT_{m,B}} = 0 \tag{5}
$$

Provided $\Delta H_f$ is assumed to be independent of $T$ in a narrow temperature range, the values of $\Delta H_{tr,T_0}$ and $\Delta H_{tr,T_{tr}}$ can be regarded as equal, therefore, subtraction of eq 5 from eq 4 yields:

$$
\ln X_{A,T_0} - \ln X_{B,T_0} = -\frac{\Delta H_{tr}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_0} - \frac{1}{T_{tr}}\right) \tag{6}
$$

Thus, $T_{tr}$ can be calculated from $\Delta H_{tr}$ and solubility at a given temperature $T_0$:

$$
T_{tr} = \left[\frac{R(\ln X_{A,T_0} - \ln X_{B,T_0})}{\Delta H_{tr}} + \frac{1}{T_0}\right]^{-1} \tag{7}
$$

Melting Data Method. $Yu^{14}$ proposed a method to determine the $T_{tr}$ of a pair of enantiotropic polymorphs. The transition enthalpy ($\Delta H_{tr,T_{m,A}}$) and transition entropy ($\Delta S_{tr,T_{m,A}}$) from the lower melting polymorph (Form A) to the higher melting polymorph (Form B) at $T_{m,A}$ can be computed by the following equations:

$$
\Delta H_{tr,T_{m,A}} = \Delta H_{f,A} - \Delta H_{f,B} + \int_{T_{m,A}}^{T_{m,B}} \left(C_{p,L} - C_{p,B}\right)dt \tag{8}
$$

$$
\Delta S_{tr,T_{m,A}} = \frac{\Delta H_{f,A}}{T_{m,A}} - \frac{\Delta H_{f,B}}{T_{m,B}} + \int_{T_{m,A}}^{T_{m,B}} \frac{\left(C_{p,L} - C_{p,B}\right)}{t} dt \tag{9}
$$

where $C_{p,B}$ and $C_{p,L}$ are the heat capacities of Form B and the supercooled liquid, respectively. $\Delta H_{f,A}$ and $\Delta H_{f,B}$, $T_{m,A}$ and $T_{m,B}$ are the heat of fusion and melting temperature of Form A and the Form B, respectively. $(C_{p,L} - C_{p,B})$ is assumed constant as the range of the integration ($T_{m,A}$ to $T_{m,B}$) is normally not too large (typically < 20 K). From eqs 8, 9 and $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$, obtains:

$$
\Delta G_{tr,T_{m,A}} = \Delta H_{f,B} \left(\frac{T_{m,A}}{T_{m,B}} - 1\right) + \left(C_{p,L} - C_{p,B}\right)\left[T_{m,B} - T_{m,A} - T_{m,A} \ln\left(\frac{T_{m,B}}{T_{m,A}}\right)\right] \tag{10}
$$

where $\Delta G_{tr,T_{m,A}}$ is the transition free energy from Form A to Form B at $T_{m,A}$. Since the value of $\Delta G_{tr,A}$ is determined mainly by the first term in eq 10, it can be obtained that $d\Delta G/dT = -\Delta S$. If $\Delta G$ is approximately liner in a temperature range, its value at an arbitrary temperature $T$ below $T_{m,A}$ can be obtained by:

$$
\Delta G = \Delta G_{tr,T_{m,A}} - \Delta S_{tr,T_{m,A}}\left(T - T_{m,A}\right) \tag{11}
$$

At $T_{tr}$, $\Delta G$ equals zero, which gives:

$$
T_{tr}=\frac{\Delta H_{tr,T_{m,A}}}{\Delta S_{tr,T_{m,A}}}=\frac{\Delta H_{f,A}-\Delta H_{f,B}+\left(C_{p,L}-C_{p,B}\right)\left(T_{m,B}-T_{m,A}\right)}{\frac{\Delta H_{f,A}}{T_{m,A}}-\frac{\Delta H_{f,B}}{T_{m,B}}+\left(C_{p,L}-C_{p,B}\right)\ln\left(\frac{T_{m,B}}{T_{m,A}}\right)} \tag{12}
$$

$G_c$ Phase Diagram Method. Configurational terms of the amorphous form and crystalline form, namely, enthalpy ($H_c$), entropy ($S_c$), and free energy ($G_c$) were calculated using the following equations$^{15}$:

$$
H_{c}=\Delta H_{f}-\int_{T}^{T_{m}} C_{p C o n f} d t \tag{13}
$$

$$
S_{c}=\Delta S_{f}-\int_{T}^{T_{m}} \frac{C_{p C o n f}}{t} d t=\frac{\Delta H_{f}}{T_{m}}-\int_{T}^{T_{m}} \frac{C_{p C o n f}}{t} d t \tag{14}
$$

$$
G_{c}=H_{c}-T S_{c} \tag{15}
$$

where $C_{pConf}$ is the heat capacity difference of amorphous and crystalline form.

$$
C_{p C o n f}=C_{p A m o r p h o u s}-C_{p C r y s t a l l i n e} \tag{16}
$$

The obtained $G_c$ is then plotted against $T$ to get $G_c$ phase diagram of the respective polymorphs.

The point of intersection of two curves of an enantiotropic polymorphic pair can be considered as $T_{tr}$.

## EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Materials. Sofosbuvir were obtained from Zhejiang Ausun Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China as Form 1 which was confirmed by XRPD with purities > 99%. Deionized water and high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) grade acetonitrile were used for HPLC analysis.

Other solvents used in this study were of analytical reagent grade.

### Preparation of Sofosbuvir Polymorphs.

Form A. Sofosbuvir (5g) was stirred in 120 mL of methyl tert-butyl ether at 40 °C, the solid was dissolved at first, and the solution was maintained over a period of time until new crystals precipitating out. The resulting crystals were collected by filtration and dried at 40 °C in vacuum.

Form B. Sofosbuvir (5g) was stirred in 20 mL of ethyl acetate at 40 °C, the solid was dissolved, then 100 mL of isopropyl ether was added slowly into this solution and crystals gradually precipitated out, the suspension was maintained for a few hours. The resulting crystals were collected by filtration and dried at 40 °C in vacuum.

Amorphous. Amorphous sofosbuvir was prepared in situ using differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). Accurately weighed (ca. 5 mg) Form B was heated to 423.15 K at a heating rate of 10 K/min and holding isothermally for 5 min. The melt was then rapidly cooled to 313.15 K. Amorphous sofosbuvir was confirmed in subsequent DSC run and by XRPD analysis.

XRPD. The XRPD patterns were recorded using a Rigaku Ultima IV diffractometer with Cu K$\alpha$ radiation (40 kV, 40 mA) scanned at 20°/min over an angular range of 5° to 45° 2$\theta$.

Thermal Analysis. The melting temperatures and corresponding melting enthalpies of the polymorphs of sofosbuvir were obtained using a TA DSC Q2000 with hermetically sealed aluminum pans at a heating rate of 10 K/min under a nitrogen gas flow. The instrument was calibrated against the melting properties of indium.

The isobaric, specific heat capacity ($C_p$) of the pure crystal form was determined with modulated DSC, using the same instrument. Non-hermetic sample pans were used in order to improve the accuracy. A modulation period of 40 s and an amplitude of 0.53 °C were employed, together

with a constant heating rate of 5 °C/min preceded by an initial isothermal period of 5 min at 35 °C. Pans were selected so as to limit the difference in weight between sample pan and reference pan to 0.20 mg to avoid any background heat capacity contribution. The instrument was calibrated according to standard procedure against the melting properties of indium, and the heat capacity signal was calibrated with a sapphire sample in the relevant temperature interval.

Thermogravimetric (TG) properties of the polymorphs of sofosbuvir were obtained using a TA TGA Q500 with platinum pans at a heating rate of 10 K/min under a nitrogen gas flow. The instrument was calibrated with mass and temperature according to standard procedure.

Each sample was analyzed in triplicate with RSD < 2%.

HPLC. A Waters 1260 HPLC system was used along with an Acchrom Unitary C18 5 µm 100A 4.6 mm × 250 mm column. The mobile phase was a mixture of 0.1% of trifluoroacetic acid in water and 0.085% of trifluoroacetic acid in acetonitrile (1:1), and the flow rate was 1.0 mL/min. The detection wavelength was 260 nm. Sample volumes of 10 µL were injected with an automatic injector.

Measurement of Solubility. An excess amount of each polymorph was stirred in 20 mL water at different temperatures. The experiment was performed using a Mettler Toledo EasyMax 102 system, the temperature was controlled within 0.01 °C. Samples of solution were collected using preheated syringes with filters (0.22 µm), the filtrates were then suitably diluted with the mobile phase, and the concentrations of each sample were determined by HPLC as described above. For Form A, the solution was allowed to equilibrate by dissolution. On the other hand, in case of Form B, the solution was sampled after 1, 3, 5, 10 and 15 min since it is unstable at the temperature range (see estimating $T_{tr}$ using melting data method section). Interestingly, the

measured solubility of Form B decreased with time, and patterns of Form A was detected in the sampled solids which was confirmed by XRPD. One can conclude that Form B will be rapidly transformed into Form A *via* solution-mediated phase transformation (SMPT). Additionally, when the temperature beyond 50 °C, the solubility measured for Form B is lower than that at 50 °C, which may be caused by the very fast SMPT of Form B to Form A at high temperatures. In consequence, the temperature range of the measurement was limited to 20 to 50 °C in increments of 5 °C. Each sample was performed in triplicate with RSD < 2%.

Using Melting Data Method. Assumes that $(C_{p,L}-C_{p,B})$ is constant as the range of the integration ($T_{m,A}$ to $T_{m,B}$) is normally not too large (typically < 20 K), the value of which can be calculated by the following equation:

$$
C_{p,L}-C_{p,B}=k\Delta H_{f,B} \tag{17}
$$

where $k$ is the heat capacity correction constant. From representative data collected, the value of $k$ is generally between 0.001 and 0.007/K, Yu gave the value of $k$ as 0.003/K with an average value$^{14}$, therefore, from eq 12 obtains:

$$
T_{tr}=\frac{\Delta H_{f,A}-\Delta H_{f,B}+0.003\Delta H_{f,B}\left(T_{m,B}-T_{m,A}\right)}{\frac{\Delta H_{f,A}}{T_{m,A}}-\frac{\Delta H_{f,B}}{T_{m,B}}+0.003\Delta H_{f,B}\ln\left(\frac{T_{m,B}}{T_{m,A}}\right)} \tag{18}
$$

$T_{tr}$ calculated using eq 18 with melting data of Form A and Form B of sofosbuvir in Table 1 was found to be 108.37 $^\circ$C.

$C_p$ data were selected from 85 to 105 $^\circ$C since amorphous sofosbuvir has a glass transition temperature at about 60 $^\circ$C. The value of $C_{pConf}$ generally decreases with increasing temperature, it can be approximated by one of the following assumptions$^{16}$.

(1) $C_{pConf}$ linearly decreases with $T$:

$$
C_{p C o n f}=k_{1}-k_{2} T \tag{19}
$$

(2) $C_{pConf}$ is a hyperbolic function of $T$:

$$
C_{p C o n f}=\frac{k_{3}}{T} \tag{20}
$$

where $k_{1}$, $k_{2}$ and $k_{3}$ are empirical constants. The values of $k_{1}$ and $k_{2}$ can be obtained by plotting $C_{pConf}$ versus $T$ and check the intercept an slope, on the other hand, the value of $k_{3}$ can be obtained by plotting $C_{pConf}$ versus $1/T$ and check the slope to be essentially zero. On the basis of eqs 13, 14, 15, 19 and 20, $G_{c}$ can be described by the following equations:

$$
G_{c}=\Delta H_{f}\left(1-\frac{T}{T_{m}}\right)+k_{1}\left[T-T_{m}+T \ln \left(\frac{T_{m}}{T}\right)\right]+\frac{k_{2}}{2}\left(T-T_{m}\right)^{2} \tag{21}
$$

or

$$
G_{c}=\Delta H_{f}\left(1-\frac{T}{T_{m}}\right)+k_{3}\left[1-\frac{T}{T_{m}}-\ln \left(\frac{T_{m}}{T}\right)\right] \tag{22}
$$

approximation shown in Table 4, respectively. It is then estimated from the intersection point of
$G_c$ curves that the $T_{tr}$ is 109.25 °C if eq 19 is used, compared to 109.28 °C if eq 20 is used.

Comparison of the $T_{tr}$. The $T_{tr}$ of Form A and Form B of sofosbuvir obtained by four methods is summarized in Table 5. In all cases, the $T_{tr}$ lies below the $T_{m}$ of the lower melting polymorph (Form A), which suggesting an enantiotropic relationship. However, they are quite different with each other. The solubility extrapolation method and $\Delta H_{tr}$ method are based on solubility data, hence from which the $T_{tr}$ estimated are similarly. Their difference is due to the assumption that values of $\Delta H_{tr,T0}$ equals to $H_{f,A} - H_{f,B}$, and the value of $T_{tr}$ is obtained as an average value at a range of temperature in $\Delta H_{tr}$ method. Likewise, the $T_{tr}$ calculated from the melting data method is approach to that from $G_c$ phase diagram method, for the reason that these two method are on the basis of thermal analysis data obtained from DSC. The difference is mainly because of the different assumption of $C_{pConf}$. In general, the variation of $T_{tr}$ might due to the following possible reasons. Firstly, the assumptions (e.g. $\Delta H_f$ or $C_{pConf}$ is independent of $T$) may not apply in some systems. Secondly, such factors as chemical purity, polymorphic purity and degree of crystallinity can affect the experiment results. Thirdly, the observer $T_{tr}$ depends on the experimental technique. For example, $T_{tr}$ determined by DSC is often influenced by parameters such as particle size, thermal conductivity of the sample, the heating rate, and so on. On the other hand, determined solubility of the unstable polymorph is usually reduced by SMPT, which results in a lower $T_{tr}$ than the real $T_{tr}$.

The choice of a suitable method depends on the applicability of each method to the best system.

For instance, the methods base on thermal analysis data is unsuitable for thermally unstable samples and solvates, whereas those based on solubility data is unsuitable for polymorphs that undergo a rapid transition in solution, or for samples that are scarce or expensive, which is very common in pharmaceutical industry. In this work, methods based on thermal analysis data is quite suitable since SMPT of polymorphs of sofosbuvir was observed. Furthermore, the melting data method is preferred among others as the technically simplest, the least time consuming and labor intensive method.

CONCLUSIONS

The solubility data of Form A and Form B of sofosbuvir in water were experimentally measured, and SMPT was observed during the experiment. Meanwhile, thermal analysis data of these two polymorphs were also determined by DSC, from which it is concluded that Form A and Form B are enantiotropic relative polymorphs according to the Heat of Fusion Rule. The $T_{tr}$ of Form A and Form B was then estimated using solubility extrapolation method, $\Delta H_{tr}$ method, melting data method and $G_c$ phase diagram method with experimental data. The values of $T_{tr}$ was found to be 93.29 °C, 90.59 °C and 108.37 °C for the first three method, respectively. In the $G_c$ phase diagram method case, $T_{tr}$ was found to be 109.25 °C when eq 19 was used, compared to 109.28 °C when eq 20 was used. In addition, the value of $T_{tr}$ may be verified more accurately by

suspending a mixture of polymorphs in a solution at several temperatures around the estimated $T_{tr}$. The $T_{tr}$ can be determined within a narrow temperature rage with monitoring the change of polymorphic composition periodically. However, in the case of sofosbuvir, the estimated $T_{tr}$ is too high to perform the verification experiment.

## Synopsis

Three polymorphs have been identified and characterized by X-ray powder diffraction (XRPD) and thermal analysis. The melting data and isobaric heat capacities of an enantiotropic

polymorphic have been determined calorimetrically, and the solubility data in water at different temperatures have been determined. With these data, the transition temperature ($T_{\text{tr}}$) was then estimated using solubility extrapolation method, heat of transition ($\Delta H_{tr}$) method, melting data method and configurational free energy ($G_c$) phase diagram method.
