**PHONON COUPLED COOPERATIVE LOW-SPIN**
$^1A_1 \rightleftharpoons$ HIGH-SPIN $^5T_2$ TRANSITION IN $[Fe(phen)_2(NCS)_2]$
AND $[Fe(phen)_2(NCSe)_2]$ CRYSTALS

M. SORAI and S. SEKI

Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science, Osaka University, Toyonaka, Osaka 560, Japan

(Received 5 July 1973)

Abstract--Heat capacities of $[Fe(phen)_2(NCS)_2]$ and $[Fe(phen)_2(NCSe)_2]$ were measured between $13^5$ and 375 K. A heat capacity anomaly due to the spin-transition from low-spin $^1A_1$ to high-spin $\pi_2$ electronic ground state was found at 176.29 K for the SCN-compound and at 231.26 K for the SeCN-compound, respectively. Enthalpy and entropy of transition were determined to be $\Delta H = 8.60 \pm 0.14$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ and $\Delta S = 48.78 \pm 0.71$ J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$ for the SCN-compound and $\Delta H = 11.60 \pm 0.44$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ and $\Delta S = 51.22 \pm 2.33$ J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$ for the SeCN-compound. To account for much larger value of $\Delta S$ compared with the magnetic contribution, we suggest that there is significant coupling between electronic state and phonon system. We also present a phenomenological theory based on heterophase fluctuation. Gross aspects of magnetic, spectroscopic, and thermal behaviors were satisfactorily accounted for by this model. To examine closely the transition process, infrared spectra were recorded as a function of temperature in the range 4000-30 cm$^{-1}$. The spectra revealed clearly the coexistence of the $^1A_1$ and the $^5T_2$ ground states around $T_c$.

### 1. INTRODUCTION
Octahedral transition metal complexes having $d^4$, $d^5$, $d^6$ and $d^7$ configurations can exist in two different electronic ground states with different spin-multiplicities depending on the strength of ligand field. If the field strength is close to the so-called 'spin-pairing energy' corresponding to the cross-over of ground-state terms in the Tanabe-Sugano diagrams[1], unusual magnetic behaviors may be expected. Extensive experimental evidences and theoretical treatments have been summarized in a few review articles[2-5].

The compounds *cis*-dithiocyanatobis(1,10-phenanthroline)iron(II), $[Fe(phen)_2(NCS)_2]$, and *cis*-diselenocyanatobis(1,10-phenanthroline)iron(II), $[Fe(phen)_2(NCSe)_2]$, seem to be of particular interest because they are the rare examples in which reversible conversion between high-spin and low-spin electronic ground states occurs cooperatively at a definite critical temperature $T_c$ in a solid state without any indication of a structural phase transition[6-8]. These two compounds have a molecular structure shown in Fig. 1. The Fe(II) ion is octahedrally coordinated by six N atoms. At low temperatures the iron ion possesses the $t_2^6$ electronic configuration showing the low-spin $^1A_{1g}$ ground state under a strong ligand field arising from rather large back-donation of metal electrons into the phenanthroline orbitals[8,9]. With a rise in temperature the $^1A_{1g}$ ground state is converted into the high-spin $^5T_{2g}(t_2^4e^2)$ ground state at $T_c$, which is evidenced by an abrupt increase in the effective magnetic moments[6-8] and a characteristic change in Mössbauer spectra[7,8]. Strictly speaking, the magnetic properties above $T_c$ follow closely the theory of paramagnetism for axially distorted cubic $^5T_{2g}$ terms including the spin-orbit coupling, name-

This distortion could arise due to the stereochemical arrangement of the cis-configuration and be due to a Jahn-Teller effect in the high-spin state. In what follows, however, octahedral notation is used for electronic terms unless otherwise noted.

Abundant experimental studies by use of magnetic and other kinds of spectroscopies have revealed the atomistic aspect concerning the present spin-transition. From these, however, one cannot draw knowledge about the long-range correlation inherent in the cooperative transition. One of the most fundamental unsolved problems may be a comprehensive explanation of why these compounds undergo a spin-transition cooperatively at a certain critical temperature. It is strange that thermodynamic investigations have not been made at all. Energetic aspects relevant to the problem can be obtained precisely mainly from thermodynamic measurements. A natural phenomenon should be understood complementally and consistently from both the atomistic and the thermodynamic aspects.

From this point of view we have measured the heat capacities of $[Fe(phen)_{2}(NCS)_{2}]$ and $[Fe(phen)_{2}(NCSe)_{2}]$ in the temperature region from 13 to 375 K. In this paper, we have presented the results which clearly demonstrate that the coupling between the electronic state and the phonon system plays a predominant role for the cooperative transition observed here. Preliminary accounts of the results have been communicated elsewhere[12].

Also described in this paper is a phenomenological description of the phase transition based on the analogy to the theory of heterophase fluctuations in a liquid[13]. By assuming a temperature-independent energy separation between the $^{1} A_{1 g}$ and the $^{5} T_{2 g}$ states and the coexistence of the low-spin and the high-spin states, we were able to account for the gross aspects of phase transition.

To confirm the validity of these assumptions, temperature variations of the C-N stretching frequency and the Fe-N bond vibrations have been closely examined in the vicinity of transition point by using infrared spectra.

### 2.3 Infrared spectra
Spectra in the range $4000-400\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ were recorded for nujol mulls with an Infrared Spectrophotometer Model DS-402G (Japan Spectroscopic Co., Ltd.) and in the range $400-30\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ with a Far Infrared Spectrophotometer Model FIS-3 (Hitachi, Ltd.) at various temperatures. To examine closely the temperature dependence of a characteristic band due to the $\nu_1$(SCN) mode around $T_c$, spectra in the range $2200-2000\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ were recorded with a wavenumber expansion method $(\times 10)$. Wavenumbers in this region were calibrated against the spectrum of indene.

## 3. THERMODYNAMIC QUANTITIES ASSOCIATED WITH THE PHASE TRANSITION

Prior to estimating thermodynamic quantities associated with the phase transition, it is appropriate to review the spin and electronic structures so far revealed, so that one can easily follow the present procedures for the estimation. Figure 4 represents the low-lying energy schemes for the low-temperature (LT-phase) and the high-temperature phase (HT-phase) established by König *et al.* [8,10,11,17]. At the HT-phase, the magnetic moments can be fitted well with the calculated values when the axial field splitting parameter $\Delta_a$ is $-880\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, the spin-orbit coupling parameter $\lambda=-80\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, and the orbital reduction factor $\kappa=0.8$ for [Fe(phen)$_2$(NCS)$_2$], and $\Delta_a=-1400\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, $\lambda=-80\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, and $\kappa=0.8$ for [Fe(phen)$_2$(NCSe)$_2$][8,17]. At the LT-phase, on the other hand, the $^1A_{1g}$ ground level is separated by $\epsilon$ from a higher-lying thermally accessible $^5T_{2g}$ state[8]. However, as far as one adheres to this energy scheme, the magnetic moments cannot be interpreted in terms of a constant energy-separation $\epsilon$. König and Kremer[5,17] have assumed a temperature dependence of $\epsilon$ as well as the existence of 'permanent paramagnetic impurities' in the LT-phase. In connection with these problems, we will present in the following sections an alternative interpretation with a constant $\epsilon$ and discuss possible origins of the permanent paramagnetic impurities which might be existing in the LT-phase. For the time being, we shall assume that $\epsilon$ may be regarded as being independent of temperature and the numerical values[17] will be assigned to $\epsilon=1000\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $\lambda=-80\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ for both [Fe(phen)$_2$(NCS)$_2$] and [Fe(phen)$_2$(NCSe)$_2$].

In order to determine the thermodynamic quantities related to the phase transition, it is necessary to separate the relevant contributions from a total heat capacity by estimating a plausible 'normal' heat capacity. In the present case the normal heat capacity may be divided into following three parts:
$$
C_{p}(\text{normal})=C(g(\tilde{\nu}))+\sum_{i} g_{i} C_{\mathrm{E}}(\tilde{\nu}_{i})+C_{\mathrm{mag}}, \quad (1)
$$
where $C(g(\tilde{\nu}))$ corresponds to the contribution from lattice vibrations with a frequency distribution function $g(\nu)$, $C_{\mathrm{E}}(\tilde{\nu}_{i})$ is the Einstein heat capacity due to an internal vibration with a wavenumber $\nu_i$, $g_i$ is its degeneracy, and $C_{\text{mag}}$ is the magnetic or electronic heat capacity arising from thermal excitation of electrons. The contributions from $\sum_{i} g_{i} C_{\mathrm{E}}(\tilde{\nu}_{i})$ and $C_{\text{mag}}$ can be calculated using i.r. absorption and/or Raman scattering frequencies and with the aid of the partition function based on the energy diagram shown in Fig. 4. We first determine the heat capacities due to lattice vibration by subtracting the above two terms from the experimental $C_p$ values in the temperature region far from the transition point, where the anomalous effect of transition may be neglected. ## 5. MODEL CALCULATION OF THE PHASE TRANSITION
Recently, theoretical treatments have been made by several authors to interpret the mechanism of transition between low-spin and high-spin states. Wajnflasz and Pick[24,25] attempted to apply the Ising model to this problem by assuming fictitious spins in a four-level scheme. Similar consideration was made by Bari and Sivardière[26], who extended the Chesnut exciton model[27,28] in a molecular-field approximation. These two models could explain the magnetic behavior of octahedral $\mathrm{Fe}$ (II) complexes and delimit an area of existence of a first-order transition between low-spin and high-spin states. However, a shortcoming in common with these models will appear with respect to a transition entropy which depends only on the degeneracy of each energy-level set up at the beginning. Transition entropy is obviously diagnostic value to know how much degrees of freedom may be concerned with the relevant transition.

More comprehensive treatments in this regard have been reported by Slichter and Drickamer[29], who generalized the Guggenheim regular-solution theory and applied it to pressure-induced electronic changes in $\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{II})$ and $\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{III})$ complexes. Major anomalies in the combined temperature and pressure variation of the spin conversion have been accounted for by including the temperature dependence of the free energy of interaction. We should not also overlook the suggestive paper by Ewald, Martin, Ross and White[30], who predicted that vibrational partition functions should be taken into account.

We propose here an alternative model which is virtually the same as the Frenkel theory of heterophase fluctuations in liquid[13]. This model is based on an assumption that a phase transition is of essentially first-order. We shall assume that one phase contains 'embryos' of a second phase in the transition region and that each embryo consists of an uniform size. The assumption of such embryos with a critical size does not conflict with the experimental results obtained from Mössbauer spectra $[3,31,32]$ and the present infrared spectra which give evidence for coexistence of the high-spin and the low-spin states in the vicinity of $T_{C}$.

Let us consider a system consisting of $N$ cells each of which contains $n$ molecules. The product of $N$ and $n$ is equated to Avogadro's constant $N_{A}$. We assume that there is a mole fraction $x$ which had been converted from the LT-phase to the HTphase. By assuming a simple linear interpolation with mole fraction between the Gibbs energy $G_{\mathrm{L}}$ of the LT-phase and the corresponding quantity $G_{\mathrm{H}}$ of the HT-phase, we obtain the Gibbs energy at $T$;

$$
\begin{aligned}
G=x G_{\mathrm{H}}+(1-x) G_{\mathrm{L}}+N k T\{ & x \ln x+(1-x) \\
& \times \ln (1-x)\}, \quad(2)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $k$ is Boltzmann's constant. The equilibrium mole fraction is determined by the requirement that $\partial G /\left.\partial x\right|_{T}=0$;

$$
x=1 /\{1+\exp (\Delta G / N k T)\}, \quad(3)
$$

where $\Delta G \equiv G_{\mathrm{H}}-G_{\mathrm{L}}$. The third term on r.h.s. of equation (2) corresponds to the mixing entropy term, namely,

$$
S_{\text {mix }}=-N k\{x \ln x+(1-x) \ln (1-x)\}. \quad(4)
$$

The molar heat capacity at constant pressure, $C_{p}$, is obtained by the following relations;

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{p}= & \frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left\{x H_{\mathrm{H}}+(1-x) H_{\mathrm{L}}\right\} \\
= & x C_{p, \mathrm{H}}+(1-x) C_{p, \mathrm{~L}}+ \\
& +\frac{\left(H_{\mathrm{H}}-H_{\mathrm{L}}\right)^{2} \exp (\Delta G / N k T)}{N k T^{2}\{1+\exp (\Delta G / N k T)\}^{2}},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $H$ means molar enthalpy and $C_{p, \mathrm{H}}$ and $C_{p, \mathrm{~L}}$ the 'normal' heat capacities of the HT and the LTphases, respectively.

At the transition temperature $T_{C}$, the free energies of both phases must be equal. By substituting the condition $\Delta G=0$ at $T=T_{C}$ into equation (5), we obtain a maximum value of $C_{p}$;

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{p}\left(T_{C}\right)= & \frac{1}{2}\left\{C_{p, \mathrm{H}}\left(T_{C}\right)+C_{p, \mathrm{~L}}\left(T_{C}\right)\right\} \\
& +\left\{H_{H}\left(T_{C}\right)-H_{L}\left(T_{C}\right)\right\}^{2} / 4 N k T_{C}{ }^{2} .
\end{aligned}
$$

### 7. DISCUSSION
In the present investigation it was found, for thefirst time, that the phase transition due to the $^{5} T_{2}-$  'A, crossover is accompanied with a large amount of phonon entropy other than the magnetic entropy and that the transition is characterized by a co- quencies would bring about an entropy difference as large as $17.4 ~J ~K^{-1} ~mol^{-1}$ and a heat capacity jump $\Delta C_{p}$ of $17.1 ~J ~K^{-1} ~mol^{-1}$ at $T_{c}$ provided that we accept the assignments by Takemoto and Hutchin- son[20,21], who have reported the strikingly large shifts of the $Fe-N$ frequencies. As the total entropy change $\Delta S$ is $48.78 ~J ~K^{-1} ~mol^{-1}$ and the magnetic contribution may be the order of $R \ln 5$ (=13.4J K- mol-'), the remaining entropy amounts to35 J K-' mol-'. About 50 per cent of this could be accounted for by the changes in the $Fe-N$ stretching frequencies. Although we have not succeed- ed in assigning the $N-Fe-N$ deformation vibrations, it seems very likely that these modes would also contribute largely to $\Delta S$ since the frequencies of these modes are generally very low. Therefore, it is not unreasonable to conclude that the predominant phonon modes concerned with the spin-transition

Phonon coupled cooperative low-spin $^{1} A_{1} \rightleftharpoons$ high-spin $^{5} T_{2}$ transition

may be the metal-ligand stretching and deforma- tion vibrations and, of course, intermolecular lattice vibrations as a minor contribution. Similar results were also found for $[Fe(phen)_{2}(NCSe)_{2}]$ , in which the entropy change due to the Fe-N stretching was $22.5 J K^{-1} mol^{-1}$ and their contribution to $\Delta C_{p}$ was $13.4 J K^{-1} mol^{-1}$ at $T_{c}$ .

According to the ligand-field theory, a stronger field $(D q)$ brings about a larger 'crystal-field-stabili zation-energy (c.f.s.e.)'. As shown in Fig. 4, the potential energy of the $^{5} T_{2 g}$ ground state in the HT-phase would be considerably higher than that of the $^{1} A_{1 g}$ ground state in the LT-phase. However, it is not this potential energy but a free energy in- cluding entropy term that controls a thermal stability of a system at a non-zero temperature. At absolute zero where the entropy term can be ne- glected the stability is, of course, determined only by the enthalpy term. In fact, the potential energy difference between the two ground states (P.E.D.in Fig. 4) is reflected in the energy difference $\Delta E$ (see Fig. 10) at 0 K through equation (8) because P.E.D. just corresponds to ${H_{mag, H}(0)-H_{mag, L}(0)}$ . With a rise in temperature the energy differenceΔE is compensated gradually with the entropy term coming from both the electronic and the phonon excitations. At a critical temperature $T_{c}$ a complete compensation is attained, and a first order phase transition occurs.

In Sections 5 and 6, we have analysed the trans- ition behaviors based on the idea of heterophase fluctuation. In this treatment we have assumed thatthe energy separation $\epsilon$ between the original $^{1} A_{1}$ and $^{5} T_{2}$ states in the energy scheme shown in Fig. 4 is independent of temperature. Contrary to this, König and Kremer[17] have assumed the strong temperature-dependence of $\epsilon$ . In view of the fact that the value of $\epsilon$ is varied primarily with the ligand field strength, such strong T-dependence of $\epsilon$ as they predicted would give rise to a conspicuous T- dependence of the Fe-N and the $\nu_{1}(C-N)$ stretch ing frequencies in the identical spin state. However, as was described in Section 4, the experimental evidence obtained from i.r. spectroscopy was favorable to the assumption of the constant $\epsilon$ . By the way, it should be noted here that compounds in which $\epsilon$ varies with temperature are known to exist. For example, Morassi and Sacconi[33] have re- ported the i.r. spectrum of a five-coordinated Co(II) complex which shows a considerable tem- perature dependence of $\epsilon$ in the spin-equilibrium between $^{4} T_{1 g}$ and $^{2} E_{g}$ .

As far as one attempts to interpret the spin- transition based on a single energy diagram, e.g. an energy scheme drawn in Fig. 4 for the LT-phase, apparent T-dependence of $\epsilon$ seems to be inevitable in order to obtain a good fit with experiment. In the present model this conflict was overcome by as- suming the coexistence of two distinct spin-ground- states.

Characteristic parameters in the present model are the number of cells, N, and the number of molecules in a cell, n. When N=1, the phase transition is of pure first-order nature. The effect of heterophase fluctuation and hence a reduction of first-order nature are reflected in a larger N. Physi- cally, a cell has some analogy to a domain of ferro- magnet, namely a correlation region. We suppose that at the time of spin-transition one electronic state of molecules would be simultaneously con- verted into the other in a group of n molecules and that the conversion-process like this would play an important role for the significant coupling between electronic state and phonon system. If this is the case, an extremely small group of molecules would not be able to undergo the spin-transition because of a lack of such correlation. Although there is no experimental evidence, one can easily imagine that a free molecule, say, in a gas phase or in a solution would not show a sharp spin-transition such as the present cases. It is interesting to note here that octahedral iron(II)poly(1-pyrazolyl)-borate exhibits a sharp spin-transition around 200 K due to the $^{5} T_{2}-^{1} A_{1}$ crossover in a solid state[34] but not in a chloroform solution[35]. This fact seems to give a favorable evidence for the present prediction.

Finally discussed is a possible origin of the 'per- manent paramagnetic impurity' which might existin the LT-phase. Magnetic studies[5,8,15,17] have revealed that the low-temperature values of the magnetic moments depend to some extent on the preparative method employed, though the quali-tative behavior is identical. Köning et al.[8,36] have attributed the origin of 'impurity' to incomple- tion of the spin-conversion from $^{5} T_{2}$ to $^{1} A_{1}$ . Taking into account the long thermal relaxation time, this explanation seems to be reasonable. However, if the incomplete transition is due to only the slow relaxational effect, the 'impurity' content would be considerably reduced by a sufficient annealing at the temperature just below $T_{c}$ .

There still remains a question why the transition does not proceed to 100 per cent of the $^{1} A_{1}$ state. A plausible interpretation derived from the present model is that a complete transition would not take place in an extremely small crystallite indifferently to the magnitude of the relaxation time, since such a small group of molecules is not large enough to

constitute a cooperative region. As given in Table
