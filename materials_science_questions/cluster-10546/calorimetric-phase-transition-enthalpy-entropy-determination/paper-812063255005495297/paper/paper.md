# THEORY OF ORDER-DISORDER TRANSFORMATION IN BINARY SYSTEMS OF THE Cu-Au TYPE-I

N. S. GOLOSOV, L. E. POPOV and L. YA. PUDAN

Siberian V. D. Kuznetsov Physico-Technical Institute, Tomsk., U.S.S.R.

(Received 2 November 1971)

Abstract - The phase diagram of f.c.c. alloys which order with formation of $L1_{2}$ and $L1_{0}$ superstructures is calculated by using the Kikuchi approximation. For both superstructures the order-disorder transformation is a first-order transition. There are three regions of ordered phases. The phase region boundaries are significantly different from those found by Shockley and Li. The maximum of the order-disorder transition temperature in the case of the $L1_{2}$ superstructure is displaced from the stoichiometric composition $AB_{3}$ to a composition having a greater concentration of $A$. The following correlation was found between the ordering energy $v=2v_{AB}-v_{AA}-v_{BB}$ and the critical order-disorder transition temperature $T_{c}$ : for the $L1_{2}$ superstructure, $v=2.059kT_{c}$; for the $L1_{0}$ superstructure, $v=2.113kT_{c}$ ($k=$ the Boltzmann constant).

## 1. INTRODUCTION

THE PHASE diagram of the binary system with the f.c.c. lattice was first calculated by Shockley[1] in the Gorsky-Bragg-Williams approximation[2,3]. In this diagram the only maximum of the order-disorder transition temperature is at the equiatomic composition. The absence of maxima at the compositions $AB_{3}$ and $A_{3}B$, which are stoichiometric for the superstructure $L1_{2}$, is explained by Nix and Shockley[4] by the low degree of long-range order at temperatures near the transition temperature in the Bragg-Williams approximation. As Peierls showed[5], this approximation results in under-estimation of the ordering energy. If the short-range order is taken into account, there is a considerable increase in $v$ and in the degree of long-range order near $T_{c}$.

The theory of phase equilibrium in f.c.c. binary alloys was given by Li[6], using the Yang-Li quasi-chemical approximation[7,8]. In this approximation a tetrahedron of four sublattice points was chosen as a cluster. It follows from the theory[6] that the order-disorder transition resulting in the appearance of the $L1_{2}$ and $L1_{0}$ superstructures is a first-order transition, in accordance with experiment. Because of difficulties in calculation only a qualitative picture of the phase equilibrium diagram was obtained. According to Li's estimates[6] this phase diagram has three ordered phase regions, with transition temperature maxima corresponding to the stoichiometric compositions $AB_{3}$, $AB$ and $A_{3}B$.

An analysis by Kurata *et al.*[9] showed that in the Yang-Li approximation the tetrahedrons are distributed arbitrarily on lattice and, therefore, a lattice site is frequently occupied by atoms in conflicting ways in two or more adjoining tetrahedrons (two different atoms belong simultaneously the same lattice site). By including such impossible configurations, the number of distinct configurations of the crystal which can be built up from the cluster and, hence, entropy are calculated erroneously. The method of the exclusion of the conflicting configurations was worked out by Kikuchi[10] and Kurata *et al.*[9]. They proposed an improved mathematical treatment of f.c.c. lattice case with tetrahedron taken as a cluster.

The purpose of this paper is to consider

the order-disorder transformation in binary systems with the f.c.c. lattice on the basis of their method[9, 10].

## 2. FREE ENERGY OF THE f.c.c. SOLID SOLUTION

As in other papers[8, 11], a face centered cubic lattice is represented in the form of four elementary superlattices (designated as shown in Fig. 1).

![](./images/812063255005495297_1.jpg)

Fig. 1. Designation of sublattice points of a f.c.c. lattice. Tetrahedrons of different sublattice neighbour points are shown.

The probabilities of appearance of the lattice points, the bonds and tetrahedrons having some configurations of occupation by $A$ or $B$ atoms are denoted as $x_{l}, y_{k}$ and $z_{j}$ respectively (Fig. 2). In Fig. 2 the sublattice points are designated by figures. For example, $z_{2}$ is the probability of appearance of a tetrahedron configuration in which the 0 sublattice point is occupied by $A$-, and the 1, 2 and 3 sublattice points are occupied by $B$-atoms. The probabilities are normalized by

$$
\sum_{\substack{j=1 \\(i=0,1,2,3)}}^{2} x_{j+2 i}=1, \quad \sum_{\substack{j=1 \\(i=0,1, \ldots, 5)}}^{4} y_{j+4 i}=1, \sum_{j=1}^{16} z_{j}=1.
$$

The configuration free energy of the solid solution can be represented as $F=E-$ $k T \ln G$, where $E$ is the configuration energy, $G$ is the combinatory factor, and $k$ is the Boltzmann's constant.

The configuration energy in the nearest- neighbour approximation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
E=-N \sum_{i=0}^{5}\left[y_{1+4 i} v_{B B}+\left(y_{2+4 i}+y_{3+4 i}\right) v_{A B}\right. & \\
& \left.+y_{4+4 i} v_{A A}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, for instance, $-v_{A B}$ is the interaction energy of the nearest adjacent atoms $A$ and $B$, and $N$ is the total number of atoms in an alloy.

Following Kikuchi[10] we take $G$ in the form

$$
G=\frac{Y_{N^{8}} N!}{X_{N^{5}} Z_{N^{2}}}.
$$

In the case considered

$$
\begin{gathered}
X_{N}=\prod_{l=1}^{8}\left(\frac{1}{4} x_{l} N\right)!, Y_{N}=\prod_{k=1}^{24}\left(\frac{1}{6} y_{k} N\right)!, \\
Z_{N}=\prod_{j=1}^{16}\left(z_{j} N\right)!.
\end{gathered}
$$

Use of the Stirling formula and ordinary manipulations gives a configuration part of the free energy per atom in the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
f= & \frac{F}{N v}=-\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=0}^{5}\left(y_{2+4 i}+y_{3+4 i}\right) \\
& -t\left(\sum_{k=1}^{24} y_{k} \ln y_{k}-\frac{5}{4} \sum_{l=1}^{8} x_{l} \ln x_{l}-2 \sum_{j=1}^{16} z_{j} \ln z_{j}\right) .
\end{aligned}
$$

Here

$$
t=\frac{k T}{v}, \quad v=2 v_{A B}-v_{A A}-v_{B B}.
$$

The configuration free energy is a function of the probabilities $x_{l}, y_{k}, z_{j}$. But not all of them are independent. As it follows from the cluster variation method the number of independent variables is equal to the number of cluster configurations minus unity (due to the normalization equation (2.1)). We can use for the independent variables some of $x_{l}, y_{k}, z_{j}$ or their combinations as it was done by Kikuchi

### 3. DISORDERED STATE

For the disordered state the probabilities of lattice points being occupied by $A$ or $B$ atoms are equal to the concentrations of these atoms in the alloy:

$$x_{1}=x_{3}=x_{5}=x_{7}=c_{A}.$$

These conditions, together with those of the normalization (2.1), allow the elimination of five probabilities $z_{j}$. The probabilities $z_{j}$ with $j$ from 1 to 5 can be represented as functions of

the rest, $z_i$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_{1}= & 1-4 c_{A}+z_{6}+z_{7}+z_{8}+z_{9}+z_{10}+z_{11} \\
& +2\left(z_{12}+z_{13}+z_{14}+z_{15}\right)+3 z_{16}, \\
z_{2}= & c_{A}-z_{6}-z_{7}-z_{8}-z_{12}-z_{13}-z_{14}-z_{16}, \\
z_{3}= & c_{A}-z_{6}-z_{9}-z_{11}-z_{12}-z_{14}-z_{15}-z_{16}, \\
z_{4}= & c_{A}-z_{7}-z_{9}-z_{10}-z_{12}-z_{13}-z_{15}-z_{16}, \\
z_{5}= & c_{A}-z_{8}-z_{10}-z_{11}-z_{13}-z_{14}-z_{15}-z_{16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, the free energy for the disordered state is seen to be dependent on the $c_{A}, t$ and $z_{i}$ $(i=6,7,\dots,16)$.

### 4. ORDERED STATE

For the ordered state, besides the normal- ization conditions (2.1) there is the additional condition of the alloy composition being constant:

$$
x_{1}+x_{3}+x_{5}+x_{7}=4 c_{A}.
$$

Therefore, two probabilities may be eliminated as dependent variables. The probabilities $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ can be expressed by the rest of the probabilities of appearance of the tetrahedron configurations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_{1}= & 1-4 c_{A}+z_{6}+z_{7}+z_{8}+z_{9}+z_{10}+z_{11} \\
& +2\left(z_{12}+z_{13}+z_{14}+z_{15}\right)+3 z_{16}, \\
z_{2}= & 4 c_{A}-z_{3}-z_{4}-z_{5}-2\left(z_{6}+z_{7}+z_{8}+z_{9}+z_{10}\right. \\
& \left.+z_{11}\right)-3\left(z_{12}+z_{13}+z_{14}+z_{15}\right)-4 z_{16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, the solid solution free energy is seen to be dependent only on the $c_{A}, t$ and $z_{i}$ $(i=3,4,\dots,16)$.

### 5. EQUILIBRIUM CONDITIONS OF ORDERED AND DISORDERED PHASES

The equality of the chemical potentials of the components of both phases,

$$
\mu_{A}{ }^{(1)}=\mu_{A}{ }^{(2)}, \quad \mu_{B}{ }^{(1)}=\mu_{B}{ }^{(2)}, \quad(5.1)
$$

is the equilibrium condition of the phases. In (5.1) and from here on values referring to the disordered and ordered states are denoted by indexes (1) and (2) respectively.

In the case of binary alloy:

$$
\mu_{A}=f-c_{A} \frac{\partial f}{\partial c_{A}}, \quad \mu_{B}=f+\left(1-c_{A}\right) \frac{\partial f}{\partial c_{A}}.
$$

Making use of these equations, it is possible to transform equations (5.1) to the following form

$$
\begin{gathered}
\frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(1)}}-\frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(2)}}=0, \\
f^{(1)}-f^{(2)}-\left(c_{A}{ }^{(1)}-c_{A}{ }^{(2)}\right) \frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(2)}}=0 ;
\end{gathered}\tag{5.2}
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(1)}}=\sum_{l=1}^{8} \frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial x_{l}{ }^{(1)}} \frac{\partial x_{l}{ }^{(1)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(1)}}+ & \sum_{k=1}^{24} \frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(1)}} \frac{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(1)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(1)}} \\
& +\sum_{m=1}^{5} \frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(1)}} \frac{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(1)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(1)}},
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(2)}}=\sum_{l=1}^{8} \frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial x_{l}{ }^{(2)}} \frac{\partial x_{l}{ }^{(2)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(2)}}+ & \sum_{k=1}^{24} \frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(2)}} \frac{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(2)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(2)}} \\
& +\sum_{m=1}^{2} \frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(2)}} \frac{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(2)}}{\partial c_{A}{ }^{(2)}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

In addition to the conditions of the equili- brium between phases the equilibrium conditions of each phase must hold:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial z_{i}{ }^{(1)}}+\sum_{k=1}^{24} \frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(1)}} \frac{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(1)}}{\partial z_{i}{ }^{(1)}}+\sum_{m=1}^{5} \frac{\partial f^{(1)}}{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(1)}} \frac{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(1)}}{\partial z_{i}{ }^{(1)}}=0 \\
(i=6,7,\dots,16) ; \quad(5.3)
\end{gathered}
$$

$$
\begin{gathered}
\frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial z_{i}{ }^{(2)}}+\sum_{l=1}^{8} \frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial x_{l}{ }^{(2)}} \frac{\partial x_{l}{ }^{(2)}}{\partial z_{i}{ }^{(2)}}+\sum_{k=1}^{24} \frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(2)}} \frac{\partial y_{k}{ }^{(2)}}{\partial z_{i}{ }^{(2)}} \\
+\sum_{m=1}^{2} \frac{\partial f^{(2)}}{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(2)}} \frac{\partial z_{m}{ }^{(2)}}{\partial z_{i}{ }^{(2)}}=0 \quad(5.4)
\end{gathered}
$$

$(i=3,4,\dots,16)$.

Equations (5.2)-(5.4) make up a system

which contains one extra variable compared with the number of equations. Therefore, the concentration $c_{A}^{(2)}$ should be considered as given. The values $c_{A}^{(1)}, t_{e}, z_{i}^{(1)}$ and $z_{i}^{(2)}$ corresponding to the equilibrium of the two phases can be found from the system of equations (5.2) to (5.4). Knowing $t_{e}$ and $c_{A}^{(1)}$ one can solve the system (5.4) separately for the ordered phase at $t<t_{e}$, and the system (5.3) for the disordered phase at $t>t_{e}$, and thus the temperature dependence of the probabilities for the corresponding homogeneous phases can be found.

The systems (5.2) to (5.4), (5.4) and (5.3) were solved with the M-20 computer by numerical methods for a wide range of concentrations $c_{A}^{(2)}$. The precision of the solution was such that the modulus of the values of the expressions in the left part of the equations was less than $10^{-4}$, which corresponds to a relative error in the determination of the variables less than $10^{-5}$ at high temperatures; as the temperature decreases, the error also decreases.

## 6. PHASE EQUILIBRIUM DIAGRAM
The phase diagram drawn according to the results of the solution of the system (5.2) to (5.4) is given in Fig. 3. The order-disorder transition temperature passes through 3 maxima. In the case of the $L1_{0}$ superstructure the maximum corresponds to the stoichiometric composition $AB$. But in the case of the $L1_{2}$ superstructure the maximum is a little displaced from the stoichiometric composition $AB_{3}$ or $A_{3}B$ in the direction of an increase in the $A$ or $B$ component. The deviation value is 0.015 (1.5 per cent). The regions of the $L1_{2}$ phase are sharply non-symmetrical relative to the concentrations $c_{A}=0.265$ and $c_{B}=0.265$, which correspond to the maximum transition temperature.

The region of two-phase equilibrium between the ordered phase with the $L1_{0}$ superstructure and the disordered phase [the upper temperature boundary of the two-phase region will be denoted below as $T_{c}^{(1)}(c_{A})$ or as a dimensionless number $t_{c}^{(1)}(c_{A})$ and the low temperature boundary as $T_{c}^{(2)}(c_{A})$ or $t_{c}^{(2)}(c_{A})$] becomes wider as the alloy composition deviates from the stoichiometric one and then contracts again; at $c_{A}\simeq0.388$ its width approaches zero.

The two-phase equilibrium region width in the case of the $L1_{2}$ superstructure changes in the same way when $c_{A}$ is higher than 0.265 (the concentration corresponding to the maximum transition temperature).

The maximum value of the order-disorder transition temperature for the $L1_{2}$ superstructure is $T_{c}^{(1)}(0.265)=T_{c}^{(2)}(0.265)=0,4857v/k$.

## 7. DISCUSSION

The phase diagram that results from use of the Kikuchi combinatory factor $G$ is different from that calculated by Shockley[1] in the Bragg-Williams approximation[2, 3] and from the one schematically given by Li[6] on the basis of the Yang-Li method[7,8]. First, the phase diagram differs from Shockley's in that it has three transition temperature maxima corresponding to three long-range phase regions. Second, the existing regions of the stable ordered phases are considerably wider than was supposed by Li. Third, the phase region of the $L 1_{2}$ superstructure is sharply asymmetrical, whereas in Li's schematic diagram it is symmetrical with respect to the stoichiometric composition. Finally, the maximum transition temperature for the $L 1_{2}$ superstructure is displaced from the stoichiometric composition $A B_{3}$ to compositions with more $A$ component.

The ordering energy value reported in this paper is $v=2 \cdot 059 k T_{c}(0 \cdot 265)$ for the $L 1_{2}$

superstructure and $v=2 \cdot 113 kT_{c}(0 \cdot 5)$ for the $L1_{0}$ superstructure, somewhat lower than that found by the Yang-Li method[6-8].

It should be noted that the phase diagram calculated in this work agrees better with the experimental one[11-13] in the Au concentra- tion region between 0 and 0.5 than those presented by Shockley and Li. The shift of the transition temperature maximum to an Au concentration of about 26 atom per cent was suggested by Grube *et al.*[11] on the basis of data on the dependence of electrical resistivity on temperature.
