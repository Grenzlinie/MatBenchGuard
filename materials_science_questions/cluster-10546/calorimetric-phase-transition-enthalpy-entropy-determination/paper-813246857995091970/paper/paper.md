# Structural, magnetic and calorimetric studies of a crystalline phase of the spin crossover compound [Fe(tzpy)₂(NCSe)₂]†

Zulema Arcís-Castillo,ª Lucía Piñeiro-López,ª M. Carmen Muñoz,ᵇ Rafael Ballesteros,ᶜ Belén Abarcaᶜ and José Antonio Real*ª

The compound [Fe(tzpy)₂(NCSe)₂] (tzpy = 3-(2-pyridyl)-[1,2,3]triazolo[1,5-a]pyridine) has been synthesized and its crystal structure, magnetic behavior and calorimetric properties investigated. Samples constituted of single crystals of [Fe(tzpy)₂(NCSe)₂] display a relatively cooperative spin-state change centered at $T_{1/2} \approx$ 251.7 K with a hysteresis loop 3.5 K wide. The average enthalpy ($\Delta H$) and entropy ($\Delta S$) changes upon the spin crossover behavior (SCO) obtained from DSC measurements are $11.1 \pm 0.4$ kJ mol⁻¹ and $44.5 \pm 3$ J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, respectively. The magnetic and calorimetric data have been satisfactorily simulated using the mean-field regular solution model (Slichter-Drickamer) and the domain (Sorai-Seki) models, respectively. The crystal structure of [Fe(tzpy)₂(NCSe)₂] has been investigated at 120 K and 325 K where the compound is in the low-spin state and essentially in the high-spin state, respectively. The intramolecular and intermolecular structural changes associated with the spin conversion are discussed and correlated with the magnetic and calorimetric data.

## Introduction

Iron(ii) spin crossover (SCO) compounds represent an important class of switchable molecular materials intensively investigated not only for fundamental reasons but also for their potential applicability in the design of new sensory and memory molecular materials. They have the ability to change between two thermodynamically stable electronic states, the diamagnetic low-spin (LS, $S = 0$) state and the paramagnetic high-spin (HS, $S = 2$) state in a reversible, detectable and controllable way by the action of external stimuli (typically T, P, light).¹

Most iron(ii) SCO complexes display the $[FeN_6]$ coordination sphere,¹ⁱ however, there have also been reports of pseudooctahedral compounds with $[FeN_4O_2]$,² $[FeP_4X_2]$ (X = $Cl^-$, $Br^-$),³ $[FeN_4S_2]^4$ or even in tetracoordinate $[FeN_4]^5$ environments. Among $[FeN_6]$ complexes those based on triazole ligands represent a type of compounds singularly relevant in SCO.⁶ In particular, bidentate $\alpha$-diimine ligands combining triazole and pyridine moieties have attracted considerable interest (Chart 1). For example, the use of the symmetrically substituted ligand 4-amino-3,5-bis(pyridin-2-yl)-1,2,4-triazole (abpt) has afforded a number of SCO compounds with formula $trans$-$[Fe(abpt)_2(XCN)_2]$ being $XCN = TCNQ^{-\cdot}$,⁷ $NCS^-$,⁸ $NCSe^-$,⁸ and $N(CN)_2^-$.⁹ The $TCNQ^{-\cdot}$ derivative represents the unique example reported of a SCO complex containing this radical anion; it displays a gradual SCO centered at $T_{1/2} = 270$ K ($T_{1/2}$ is the temperature at which $\gamma_{HS} = \gamma_{LS} = 0.5$, and $\gamma_i$ is the molar fraction). The $NCS^-$ derivative has been isolated in four different polymorphs, so called A,⁸ᵃ,ᵇ B,⁸ᵃ,ᵇ C,⁸ᶜ and D⁸ᶜ. Polymorph A undergoes gradual SCO with $T_{1/2} = 180$ K and light induced spin state trapping (LIESST) effect, polymorph B remains HS whatever the temperature at atmospheric pressure, however, displays SCO ($T_{1/2} = 179$ K) at 10.5 kbar, while, polymorphs C and D undergo half SCO with $T_{1/2} = 86$ K and 162 K, respectively. The $NCSe^-$ derivative was also synthesized in a SCO form A ($T_{1/2} = 224$ K) and a HS form B, which is isostructural to those found for the $NCS^-$ derivative.⁸ᵇ The $N(CN)_2^-$ derivative represents the first example of magnetic and photomagnetic SCO behavior reported for a compound containing this anion. The characteristic SCO temperature for this compound is $T_{1/2} \approx 86$ K. The related ligands 3,5-bis(pyridin-2-yl)-1,2,4-triazolate (bpytz) and 3-(2-pyridyl)-5-(4-pyridyl)-1,2,4-triazolate (2,4-bpt) favor the formation of the binuclear complex $trans$-$\{[Fe(pyridine)(NCBH_3)]_2(bpytz)\}^{10}$ and the two-dimensional coordination polymer $[Fe(2,4-bpt)_2]_n$,¹¹ respectively. The former shows a gradual two step spin transition with $T_{1/2}^1 = 194$ K

In contrast to previous examples, the use of asymmetric 2-pyridyl $^{12}$ and 3-pyrazinyl $^{13}$ -1,2,4-triazole ligands (L) have afforded an important number of SCO salts constituted of tris-coordinated iron(II) complex cations $[FeL_{3}]^{2+}$.

Less common bidentate $\alpha$-diimine ligands combining triazole and pyridine moieties are those derived from the triazolopyridines. $^{14}$ They are heterobicyclic ligands made up of 1,2,3- or 1,2,4-triazole rings fused to a pyridine ring. In this respect, the 1,2,4-triazolopyridine ligand 3-(2-pyridyl)-[1,2,4]triazolo[4,3-a]pyridine (tzpy*) has recently been employed to synthesize the SCO compound trans-$[Fe(tzpy^{*})_{2}(NCS)_{2}](T_{1/2}=269 \mathrm{~K}).^{15}$ Some years before we reported, for the first time, the synthesis, X-ray single crystal studies, magnetic and photomagnetic properties of the compounds based on the ligand 3-(2-pyridyl)-[1,2,3]triazolo[1,5-a]pyridine (tzpy) (Chart 1) trans-$[Fe(tzpy)_{2}(NCS)_{2}] \cdot S$ (S $=2 \cdot CHCl_{3}$ (2), $H_{2}O$ (3)) and trans-$[Fe(tzpy)_{2}(NCSe)_{2}]$ (4). $^{16}$ Furthermore, the syntheses of two unsolvated polymorphs of the $NCS^{-}$ derivative have also been reported together with their SCO photomagnetic and photocrystallographic studies. $^{17}$

In a previous paper $^{16}$ the properties of precipitated powders of the SCO compound trans-$[Fe(tzpy)_{2}(NCSe)_{2}]$ (4) were investigated. In the present paper we describe the crystal structures of the LS and HS states and reinvestigate the magnetic and calorimetric behavior of samples of compound 4 constituted of single crystals. The SCO properties of 4 are compared with those of relevant related SCO compounds.

## Crystallogenesis
The compound was synthesized by slow diffusion between two layers of solution in a test tube under an argon atmosphere. The bottom layer contained $FeSO_{4} \cdot 7H_{2}O$ (0.21 mmol) in 3 mL of $H_{2}O$, the tube was filled with 30 mL of a $H_{2}O-MeOH$ mixture (1 : 1). The top layer contained the ligand (tzpy) and KNCSe, which was prepared by mixing stoichiometric amounts of tzpy (0.42 mmol) and KNCSe (0.42 mmol) in 3 mL of MeOH. After several weeks, orange crystals were obtained in the central part of the test tube. Yield 35%. Anal. calcd for $C_{24}H_{16}N_{10}Se_{2}Fe$ (4): C, 43.79; H, 2.45; N, 21.28. Found: C, 43.54; H, 2.61; N, 21.08.

## Physical characterization
Variable-temperature magnetic susceptibility measurements of samples (20-30 mg) were recorded at the rate of $2 \mathrm{~K} \mathrm{~min}^{-1}$ with a Quantum Design MPMS2 SQUID susceptometer equipped with a 5.5 T magnet, operating at 1 T and at temperatures from 1.8-400 K. The susceptometer was calibrated with $(NH_{4})_{2}Mn(SO_{4})_{2} \cdot 12H_{2}O$. Experimental susceptibilities were corrected for diamagnetism of the constituent atoms by the use of Pascal's constants.

Calorimetric measurements have been performed using a differential scanning calorimeter (Mettler Toledo DSC 821e). Low temperatures were obtained with an aluminium block which was attached to the sample holder, refrigerated with a flow of liquid nitrogen, and stabilized at a temperature of 110 K. The sample holder was kept in a drybox under a flow of dry nitrogen gas to avoid water condensation. The measurements were carried out using around 20 mg of a powdered sample sealed in aluminium pans with a mechanical crimp. Temperature and heat flow calibrations were made with standard samples of indium by using its melting transition (429.6 K, $28.45 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~g}^{-1}$ ). An overall accuracy of 0.2 K in the temperature and 2% in the heat flow is estimated.

### Single crystal X-ray diffraction
Single-crystal X-ray data of 4 were collected at 120 K and 325 K on a Nonius Kappa-CCD single crystal diffractometer using graphite mono-chromated Mo K $\alpha$ radiation $(\lambda=0.71073 \AA)$. A multi-scan absorption correction was performed. The structures were solved by direct methods using SHELXS-97 and refined by full-matrix least squares on $F^{2}$ using SHELXL-97. $^{19}$ Non-hydrogen atoms were refined anisotropically and hydrogen atoms were placed in calculated positions refined using idealized geometries (riding model) and assigned fixed isotropic displacement parameters. At 325 K the ratio of observed/unique reflection data is low probably due to deterioration of the crystal. CCDC 915013 and 915014 files contain the supplementary crystallographic data for this paper. $^{\dagger}$

$$\ln[(1-\gamma_{\text{HS}})/(\gamma_{\text{HS}}-f_{\text{HS}})]=[\Delta H+\Gamma(f_{\text{HS}}+1-2\gamma_{\text{HS}})]/RT-\Delta S/R\ (1)$$

where $\Delta H$, $\Delta S$ and $\Gamma$ are the enthalpy and the entropy variations and the parameter accounting for cooperativity associated with the spin conversion, respectively. The HS

### Calorimetric data (DSC)
The calorimetric measurements were carried out in the 120–340 K temperature range. A smooth line has been interpolated from the values in the normal regions, below 220 K and above 290 K. The heat capacity due to the transition has been deduced by subtraction of this baseline. The temperature dependence of the resultant excess of heat capacity, $\Delta C_{\text{p}}$, in the cooling and heating modes for **4** is displayed in Fig. 5. The enthalpy variation, $\Delta H$, associated with the spin-transition determined from the area under the DSC curves and the critical temperatures, $T_{1/2}$, obtained from the maximum of $\Delta C_{\text{p}}$ vs. $T$ curve, are $\Delta H = 11.1 \pm 0.4$ kJ mol⁻¹ ($T_{1/2} = 250.7$ K) and $\Delta H = 11.3 \pm 0.4$ kJ mol⁻¹ ($T_{1/2} = 253.5$ K) for the cooling and warming modes, respectively. From these values entropy variations ($\Delta S = \Delta H/T_{1/2}$) of $44.3 \pm 3$ J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ and 44.7 $\pm 3$ J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ were found for each mode respectively. These values are reasonably consistent with those obtained from the $\chi_{\text{M}}T$ vs. $T$ plot.

![](./images/813246857995091970_6.jpg)

Fig. 5 Excess of heat capacity $\Delta C_{\text{p}}$ versus $T$ in the cooling and heating modes for trans-[Fe(tzpy)₂(NCSe)₂] (4). Red solid lines correspond to the best simulation obtained from the domain model (see text).

The so-called domain model by Sorai and Seki was applied to simulate the $\Delta C_{\text{p}}$ vs. $T$ curve (eqn (3) and (4)).²³ In this model the SCO centers are not distributed in a random fashion but form domains of homogeneous size around $T_{1/2}$, each domain being constituted of n SCO centers. In the frame of this model the excess of heat capacity due to the SCO is expressed as follows (eqn (4) and (5)):

$$
\Delta C_{\mathrm{p}} \approx \frac{n \Delta H^{2} A}{R T^{2}(1+A)^{2}} \tag{4}
$$

$$
A=\exp [(n \Delta H / R)(1 / T-1 / T 1 / 2)] \tag{5}
$$

A good agreement between experimental and calculated values was obtained using eqn (4) and (5) (red line in Fig. 4). The obtained parameters are $\Delta H = 11.08$ kJ mol⁻¹ (11.56 kJ mol⁻¹), $T_{1/2} = 250.7$ K (253.5 K) and $n = 7$ (7) for cooling (heating) curves.

## Discussion
Previous unsuccessful attempts to get single crystals of **4** were carried out from methanolic solutions containing Fe(II)/NCSe⁻/tzpy in stoichiometric amounts (1 : 2 : 2). The solubility of **4** in these conditions is high and microcrystalline ocher-colored solids appear only after evaporating the methanolic solutions in an argon stream for several days. In contrast, the use of slow diffusion (layering method) of the reagents in water/methanol (1 : 1) solutions enables formation of good single crystals of **4**. Clearly, the use of water as a co-solvent makes **4** more insoluble thereby facilitating formation of single crystals by slow diffusion in several weeks.

The molecular structure of **4** is virtually the same as previously reported for compound trans-[Fe(tzpy)₂(NCS)₂]·2CHCl₃ (2)¹⁶ and the corresponding unsolvated polymorphs A and B.¹⁷ Two planar tzpy ligands occupy the equatorial positions of the iron(II) while two pseudohalide $\text{NCX}^-$ (X = S, Se) anions fill the axial positions. The average HS bond length $\langle d(\text{Fe-N})\rangle_{\text{HS}}$ found for compound 2, polymorph A, polymorph B and **4** is 2.173(3) Å (293 K), 2.165(3) Å (150 K), 2.158(2) Å (300 K) and 2.146(5) Å (325 K), respectively. The differences between these values can be considered marginal for the three $\text{NCS}^-$ derivatives. However, the similarity in $\langle d(\text{Fe-N})\rangle_{\text{HS}}$ strongly contrasts with the characteristic $T_{1/2}$ SCO temperatures: *ca.* 75 K (2), 118 K (3 and polymorph B), 150 K (polymorph A). The scatter of $T_{1/2}$ temperatures must be a consequence of the different crystal packing shown by these compounds.

Considering $T_{1/2}$ of polymorph A, the replacement of $\text{NCS}^-$ by $\text{NCSe}^-$ involves an upward shift $\Delta T_{1/2} = (T_{1/2}^{\text{NCSe}} - T_{1/2}^{\text{NCS}} \approx 100$

$K$ indicating a strong stabilization of the LS state. This is also reflected on the significantly shorter $\langle d(\text{Fe-N})\rangle_{\text{HS}}$ value observed for **4** at 325 K. Indeed, the magnetic properties of **4** clearly indicate that the SCO is not complete at that temperature. Taking into account that the total variation $\Delta\langle d(\text{Fe-N})\rangle=[\langle d(\text{Fe-N})\rangle_{\text{HS}}-\langle d(\text{Fe-N})\rangle_{\text{LS}}]$ for the complete SCO of polymorph A was found to be $0.189\ \text{\AA}$, and ascribing a similar $\Delta\langle d(\text{Fe-N})\rangle$ value for **4**, a population of *ca.* 10% LS centers can be estimated at 325 K. This result is consistent with an $\chi_{\text{M}}T$ value equal to $3.18\ \text{cm}^3\ \text{K}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ observed at that temperature and with the calculated one for 100% of HS centers $(\chi_{\text{M}}T)_{\text{HS}}=3.45\ \text{cm}^3\ \text{K}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$. In fact, the difference between both $\chi_{\text{M}}T$ values, $0.27\ \text{cm}^3\ \text{K}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$, represents the occurrence of *ca.* 9% of LS molecules at 325 K, in good agreement with the crystallographic data.

Stabilization of the LS state when replacing $\text{NCS}^-$ by $\text{NCSe}^-$ estimated from $\Delta T_{1/2}$ has typically been found to be in the interval 40–60 K for mononuclear $[\text{Fe(L)}_2(\text{NCX})_2]$ complexes where L is an $\alpha$-diimine ligand such as, for example, 1,10- phenanthroline, $^{23}$ 2,2'-bithiazoline$^{24}$ or 4-amino-3,5-bis(pyri- din-2-yl)-1,2,4-triazole.$^{25}$ A larger stabilization of the LS state was observed for the two-dimensional coordination polymer $[\text{Fe(btr)}_2(\text{NCX})_2]\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (btr = 4,4'-bis-1,2,4-triazole) for which a shift of 80 K was observed. In this regard, as far as we know the $[\text{Fe(tzpy)}_2(\text{NCX})_2]$ system shows the larger $\Delta T_{1/2}$ value so far reported. A plausible explanation of this could be tentatively ascribed to the different crystal packing and its possible influence on the coordination geometry of the iron(II) ion. Thus, looking at the $[\text{FeN}_6]$ octahedron of **4** and polymorphs A and B one finds quite similar $\Sigma$ values: $72.8^\circ$, $74.8^\circ$ and 72.7, respectively. However, the average trigonal distortion $\varPhi$ is significantly smaller for the $\text{NCSe}^-$ derivative **4**, both in the LS and HS states, $[5.4^\circ$ (HS), $3.7^\circ$ (LS)] than for the $\text{NCS}^-$ derivatives $[11.3^\circ$ (HS), $7.1^\circ$ (LS)]$^{17,26}$ (polymorph A) and $[11.3^\circ$ (HS)] (polymorph B).$^{17,26}$ It is well-known that the greater the $\varPhi$ value the more stable the HS state is, being $\varPhi=$ $0^\circ$ for a regular octahedron and $60^\circ$ for the trigonal prism.$^{20b}$

As far as the SCO properties of the precipitated and single crystal samples are concerned, an obvious increase of cooperativity and completeness of the SCO is observed for the latter sample. The small hysteresis, *ca.* 2–3 K wide, results from the large number of $\pi$–$\pi$ contacts found for the $\text{NCSe}^-$ derivative. The lack of hysteresis in the precipitated samples can be explained by the presence of *ca.* 8% HS species in the LS phase $(f_{\text{HS}}\approx0.08).^{27}$ These inactive iron(II) centers, randomly distributed in the solid, dilute the SCO centers weakening cooperativeness effects and, hence, narrowing the hysteresis. The possible existence of two distinct polymorphs has been ruled out since, as discussed above, subtle structural changes in SCO polymorphs usually involve drastic changes in $T_{1/2}$. However, in the present case the average $T_{1/2}$ value is virtually the same in both cases. Furthermore, in order to get more direct evidence about this conjecture we have taken advantage of the higher insolubility of **4** in water to induce rapid precipitation of microcrystalline samples by evaporating water/methanol (1 : 1) solutions under reduced pressure.

Interestingly, the resulting microcrystalline solid has the same XRPD pattern and similar magnetic properties as the samples constituted of single crystals (see ESI, Fig. S2$\dagger$).

The $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S$ values deduced from the DSC measurements and the estimated ones are in the range expected for a $^1\text{A}_1\ (S=0)\leftrightarrow{}^5\text{T}_2\ (S=2)$ SCO behavior.$^{28}$ The $\Delta S$ value is significantly larger than that resulting from the change in spin only values $(\Delta S=R\ln[(2S_{\text{HS}}+1)/(2S_{\text{LS}}+1)]=13.45\ \text{J}\ \text{mol}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ for $S_{\text{HS}}=2$ and $S_{\text{LS}}=0$). The excess of entropy, 44.5–13.45 = $40.55\ \text{J}\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ essentially corresponds to the changes associated with the intramolecular Fe–N bond lengths, about $0.2\ \text{\AA}$ shorter in the LS than in the HS state. The longer bond lengths of the HS state decrease the interatomic force constants, reducing the corresponding vibrational frequen- cies. As a result, the number of excited vibrational states experiences an abrupt jump with the lattice expansion. The smaller $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S$ values reported for the precipitated samples can also be explained in terms of the presence of HS species in the LS phase.

## Conclusion

In this work we have improved the synthetic protocol previously described for obtaining the SCO complex *trans*- $[\text{Fe(tzpy)}_2(\text{NCSe})_2]$ (**4**). The use of methanol-water (1 : 1) mixtures as solvent decreases conveniently the solubility of **4** enabling the synthesis of appropriate single crystals and microcrystalline precipitates, which display moderate coop- erative SCO showing a small thermal hysteresis *ca.* 3 K wide. The SCO has been characterized by means of magnetic and calorimetric measurements, which have successfully been analyzed in the context of the regular solution and domain thermodynamic models. The single crystal analysis at 120 K and 325 K is consistent with the magnetic and calorimetric studies and shows the presence of a number of significant intermolecular interactions that account for the observed cooperativity.
