# Thermodynamic Approach to Glass Transitions of Plastically Crystalline Cyanoadamantane and Isocyanocyclohexane

Osamu YAMAMURO*, Mariko ISHIKAWA, Isao KISHIMOTO**, Jean-Jacques PINVIDIC and Takasuke MATSUO

Department of Chemistry, Graduate School of Science, Osaka University, 1-1 Machikaneyama-cho, Toyonaka, Osaka 560-0043

(Received March 3, 1999)

The heat capacity of plastically crystalline cyanoadamantane was measured with an adiabatic calorimeter in the temperature range 5-310 K. A glass transition appeared at 163 K in the supercooled plastic phase. The ordered phase obtained by annealing underwent a first-order transition to the plastic phase at 273 K. Enthalpy relaxation was also measured around $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ to investigate the molecular reorientation with long relaxation times $(10^{3}<\tau<10^{8} \mathrm{~s})$. Dielectric permittivity of plastically crystalline isocyanocyclohexane was measured in the temperature range 120-230 K and frequency range $20-10^{6} \mathrm{~Hz}$ to determine the relaxation times of the molecular reorientation. The fragility of cyanoadamantane was small while those of isocyanocyclohexane and cyclohexanol were intermediate between the fragile and the strong systems. The configurational entropies of these plastic crystals were calculated as functions of temperature. Their relaxation times were reproduced well by an extended version of the Adam-Gibbs theory which is modified by considering the configurational entropy inside the cooperatively rearranging region.

KEYWORDS: glass transition, plastic crystal, orientational relaxation, heat capacity, dielectric permittivity, fragility, configurational entropy, Adam-Gibbs theory

## §1. Introduction

Many globular molecules crystallize into an intermediate phase in which the molecular center of mass is ordered as in the normal crystal while the molecular orientation is disordered as in the liquid. $^{1)}$ This phase is called "orientationally disordered crystal (ODIC)", or "plastic crystal (PC)" based on its plasticity under mechanical stress; we use the latter in this paper. Plastic crystals can sometimes be quenched by rapid cooling into a "glassy crystal" (GC) where the disordered molecular orientations are frozen-in; e.g., ethanol $(\mathrm{C}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{5} \mathrm{OH}, T_{\mathrm{g}}=97 \mathrm{~K}),^{2)}$ cyclohexanol $(\mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{11} \mathrm{OH}, T_{\mathrm{g}}=150 \mathrm{~K}),^{3-5)}$ cyanocyclohexane $(\mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{11} \mathrm{CN}, T_{\mathrm{g}}=135 \mathrm{~K}),^{6)}$ isocyanocyclohexane $(\mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{11} \mathrm{NC}, \quad T_{\mathrm{g}}=129 \mathrm{~K}),^{6)} \quad 1,2-$ difluorotetrachloroethane $(\mathrm{CFCl}_{2} \mathrm{CFCl}_{2}, \quad T_{\mathrm{g}}=90 \mathrm{~K}),^{7)}$ 2, 3-dimethylbutane $(\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}) \mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}) \mathrm{CH}_{3}, T_{\mathrm{g}}=$ 76 K),8) cyanoadamantane $(\mathrm{C}_{10} \mathrm{H}_{15} \mathrm{CN}, T_{\mathrm{g}}=170 \mathrm{~K}),^{9,10)}$ ortho-carborane $(\mathrm{B}_{10} \mathrm{C}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{12}, \quad T_{\mathrm{g}}=165 \mathrm{~K}),^{11)}$ and cyclooctanol $(\mathrm{C}_{8} \mathrm{H}_{15} \mathrm{OH}, T_{\mathrm{g}}=168 \mathrm{~K}).^{12,13)}$ The glass transitions and related molecular reorientations in the glass-forming plastic crystals have not been investigated systematically. For several plastic crystals, it has been reported that their glass transitions are similar in many respects to those of glass-forming molecular liquids; e.g., a large heat capacity jump appears at $T_{\mathrm{g}}^{2-8)}$ and the temperature dependence of the relaxation time is nonArrhenius. $^{14-20)}$ On the other hand, it is also reported that carborane exhibits Arrhenius type relaxation. $^{21)}$

The first aim of the present study is to investigate the temperature dependence of relaxation time related to the glass transitions of the plastic crystals and to determine their fragility. Fragility $^{22,23)}[m=\mathrm{d} \log (\tau / \mathrm{s}) / \mathrm{d}(T_{\mathrm{g}} / T)$ at $T_{\mathrm{g}}]$ is an important parameter reflecting the local structure of the liquid and its temperature dependence. It is of interest to compare the fragility of plastic crystals with those of molecular liquids and simple glassy crystals such as $\mathrm{RbCN}^{24)}$ and ice. $^{25)}$ The second purpose, which is the main one in this study, is to investigate the glass transitions and related molecular relaxations of the plastic phase from the thermodynamic point of view. We propose an extension of the Adam-Gibbs theory $^{26)}$ (abbreviated as AG theory hereafter) for the analysis of the plastic crystals.

The original AG theory is one of the successful theories explaining the non-Arrhenius behavior of glass-forming liquids. The theory assumes the existence of "cooperatively rearranging regions" (CRR) of molecules. This quantity is closely related to the configurational entropy $(S_{\mathrm{c}})$ which can be evaluated from heat capacity data. The asymptotic increase of the CRR size, which becomes infinite at the Kauzmann temperature $(S_{\mathrm{c}}=0)$, is consistent with the divergence of the $\alpha$ relaxation time experimentally observed. Most of the previous studies $^{27-43)}$ have provided evidences supporting the existence of the CRR and the validity of AG theory at least in a qualitative level.

For the analyses of the present study, the relaxation time and heat capacity data are required over the wide temperature and frequency ranges. However, such data were available only for cyclohexanol. We have therefore measured the heat capacity and enthalpy relaxation

Cyanoadamantane is a rigid globular molecule, which has $C_{3v}$ symmetry and bears a large dipole moment (3.8 debye) along its three-fold axis. Cyanoadamantane crys- tallizes into an fcc plastic phase (Fm3m, $Z=4$) at room temperature and transforms into a monoclinic ordered crystal (C2/m, $Z=4$) on cooling (at ca. $280 \mathrm{~K}).^{10)}$ In the plastic phase, the molecular center of mass is ordered while the orientation of the molecular three-fold axis is disordered among the six equivalent $\langle 100\rangle$ directions of the fcc lattice. $^{44-46)}$ The plastic crystal can be quenched by rapid cooling into a glassy crystal $(T_{g} \approx 170 \mathrm{~K}).^{9,10)}$ The short-range orientational order ing in the plastic phase, especially at lower tempera- tures, was demonstrated by X-ray diffuse scattering stud- ies. $^{44,47)}$ Because of these properties, cyanoadamantane has been regarded as a model system of the orienta- tionally disordered crystal and extensively studied by thermal, $^{9,10)}$ dielectric, $^{14-16)} NMR,^{48)}$ neutron scatter ing, $^{49,50)}$ and X-ray diffraction $^{10,44-47)}$ methods. The preliminary data of the heat capacity have already been reported. $^{51)}$

Isocyanocyclohexane has been studied less extensively than cyanoadamantane. Liquid isocyanocyclohexane crystallizes into a plastic phase at $280 \mathrm{~K}.^{6)}$ The plastic phase is easily supercooled and undergoes a glass tran- sition at $129 \mathrm{~K}$. Infrared and Raman spectroscopic stud ies $^{52)}$ revealed that the axial and equatorial conformers coexist in the glassy-crystalline, plastic crystalline and liquid phases. The calorimetric study $^{53)}$ showed that the conversion between the two conformers is frozen at an- other small glass transition at $160 \mathrm{~K}$. The ordered crys talline phase, whose structure is unknown, is obtained by annealing the sample at $175 \mathrm{~K}$ for three weeks. $^{53)}$ The temperature of the ordered-to-plastic phase transition is $192.6 \mathrm{~K}$.

## §2. Experimental
### 2.1 Cyanoadamantane
The commercial product from Aldrich was purified by the recrystallization from ethanol solution and then driedin vacuo. The result of the elemental analysis (wt %)was as follows. C: 81.21 (81.94), H: 9.40 (9.38), N: 8.28(8.69); the numbers in the parentheses are calculated from the molecular formula. The mass of the sampleloaded in the calorimeter cell was $1.30524 ~g(8.09467 \times$  $10^{-3} ~mol)$ . Helium gas $(2.06 \times 10^{-4} ~mol)$ was charged into the dead space $(4.62 ~cm^{3})$ of the sample cell to enhance the thermal equilibration at low temperature.

Heat capacities were measured by an automatic adi- abatic calorimeter $^{54)}$ in the temperature range $5-310 ~K$ . The measurement was carried out using a standard inter- mittent heating method, i.e., repetition of equilibration and energizing intervals. The temperature increment for each measurement was between 0.3 and $2.5 ~K$ . It took about $30 ~s$ for the sample to reach thermal equilibrium in the cell after each energy input at $10 ~K$ and about $3 ~min$ at $310 ~K$ . The accuracy of the heat capacity measurement was better than $1 \%$ at $T<10 ~K, 0.5 \%$ at $10<T<20 ~K$ , $0.2 \%$ at $20<T<30 ~K$ , and $0.1 \%$ at $T>30 ~K$ .

The temperature of the sample was measured with a precision of $100 \mu K$ using Rh-Fe resistance thermome ters $(27 \Omega$ at $273 ~K$ , purchased from Oxford InstrumentsCompany) calibrated on the temperature scale EPT76 $(T<30 ~K)$ and IPTS68 $(T>30 ~K)$ . The heat capacity difference caused by the conversion to the new temper- ature scale ITS $90^{55)}$ was estimated to be smaller than $0.05 \%$ over the $30 ~K$ to $310 ~K$ temperature range.

### 2.2 Isocyanocyclohexane
Commercial reagent from Fluka was fractionally dis- tilled under reduced pressure with a concentric-type rectifier (HC-5500-F, Shibata Kagakukikai Kogyo Co., Ltd.). The sample was then dried with molecular sieves(4A 1/16, Wako Pure Chemical Ind., Ltd.) and distilled again in vacuo in a home-made vacuum-line. The pu- rified sample showed no trace of organic impurity as detected by gas chromatography (F21, Perkin-Elmer). Karl-Fischer test was also carried out and the amount of water contained in the sample was found to be negligible(<0.04 mol%). The final purity was determined to be $99.965 \%$ by the fractional melting method. $^{53)}$

The dielectric permittivity was measured using a3-electrode cell described elsewhere. $^{56)}$ The cell was mounted in a double thermostated environment in a cryostat. The sample temperature was stabilized to $\pm 0.03 ~K$ with an accuracy of $\pm 0.1 ~K$ . The capaci tance and the loss were measured with a LCR meter(HP4284A-Hewlett Packard). The frequency range ob- served was $20 ~Hz$ to $1 MHz$ .

The glassy crystalline phase was obtained easily at a relatively slow cooling rate (ca. $0.2 Kmin^{-1}$ ). The di electric permittivity was measured on this phase in the temperature range $120-230 ~K$ in the heating direction.

### 3.4 Angell plot and fragility

Figure 6 gives the Angell plots $(\log (\tau / s)$ vs. $T_{\mathrm{g}} / T)^{22,23)}$ for the plastic crystals of cyanoadamantane, $^{14-16)}$ isocyanocyclohexane, $^{53)}$ cyclohexanol, $^{17,18)}$ and cyclooctanol. $^{20)}$ The relaxation times plotted are those from the dielectric measurement and enthalpy relaxation obtained in the present and previous studies. Therefore, we assumed the same situation in cyanoadamantane. The $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ of the present plot is determined by an interpolation or extrapolation method as the temperature where the relaxation time is 100 s. The data on molecular glasses (glycerol $^{61)}$ and orthoterphenyl $^{62,63)}$ ), a network glass $(SiO_{2}^{64)})$ , and a simple glassy crystal $(RbCN^{24)}$ and $C_{60}^{65-69)})$ are also plotted for comparison. RbCN is not a plastic crystal but rigid ionic crystal which exhibits the simple disorder involving head-and-tail orientations of a $CN^{-}$ ion. $^{70)}$ In the glassy state of $C_{60}$, orientational disorder over two orientations with slightly different energy is frozen. $^{71)}$ The fragilities $[m=d \log (\tau / s) / d(T_{g} / T)$ at $T_{g}]$ of the compounds examined here are as follows; cyanoadamantane:24, isocyanocyclohexane: 38, cyclohexanol: 33, cyclooctanol: 33, glycerol: 53, orthoterphenyl: $81, SiO_{2}: 20$ , RbCN: 14, C₆₀: 16. The fragilities of isocyanocyclohexane, cyclohexanol and cyclooctanol were intermediate between the fragile systems (molecular glasses) and the strong systems (network glasses and simple glassy crystals). However, cyanoadamantane belongs to the strong systems in terms of the fragility. This result is similar tothat of ortho-carborane $(m=20)^{21)}$

It is known that the AG theory explains relative magnitude of the fragility; the steeper the temperature dependence of the configurational entropy is, the larger the fragility becomes. $^{22,23)}$ This is based on the fact that the configurational entropy directly reflects the local intermolecular structure controlling the fragility. We therefore analyze the temperature dependence of the relaxation times of the plastic crystals in terms of the AG theory and its modification in §3-5 and 3-6.

### 3.5 Configurational entropy of plastic crystals

The temperature dependence of the configurational entropy $S_{\mathrm{c}}(T)$ was calculated by the equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{c}}(T)= & \Delta_{\mathrm{trs}} S-\int_{T}^{T_{\mathrm{trs}}}\left[C_{p}^{\mathrm{pc}}\left(T^{\prime}\right)-C_{p}^{\mathrm{gc}}\left(T^{\prime}\right)\right] / T^{\prime} \mathrm{d} T^{\prime} \\
& -\int_{0}^{T_{\mathrm{trs}}}\left[C_{p}^{\mathrm{gc}}\left(T^{\prime}\right)-C_{p}^{\mathrm{oc}}\left(T^{\prime}\right)\right] / T^{\prime} \mathrm{d} T^{\prime}, \quad(4)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $T_{\mathrm{trs}}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{trs}} S$ denote the temperature and entropy of the first-order transition, respectively, and $C_{p}^{\mathrm{gc}}$, $C_{p}^{\mathrm{pc}}$ and $C_{p}^{\mathrm{oc}}$ the heat capacities of the glassy crystal, plastic crystal and ordered crystal, respectively. This equation gives the absolute value of the configurational entropy by using entropy of the plastic crystal at the transition temperature as a reference point. The second term of eq. (4) corresponds to the configurational entropy decrease caused by the temperature decrease into the metastable regime of the plastic phase and the third term for the correction due to the difference in vibrational entropy between the glassy and crystalline states(usually smaller than 5%). Further details for the calculation is described elsewhere. $^{43)}$

### 3.6 Analysis based on the Adam-Gibbs theory

In the Adam-Gibbs theory, the relaxation time is given by
$$
\tau=\tau_{0} \exp (z \Delta \mu / R T)=\tau_{0} \exp \left(s_{\mathrm{c}}^{*} \Delta \mu / k_{\mathrm{B}} T S_{\mathrm{c}}\right), \quad(5)
$$
where $z$ is the number of the molecules in a cooperatively rearranging region (CRR), $\Delta \mu$ the molar activation free energy, $s_{\mathrm{c}}^{*}$ the configurational entropy of CRR, which is assumed to be independent of CRR size, and $S_{\mathrm{c}}$ the macroscopic configurational entropy. In eq. (5), $s_{\mathrm{c}}^{*} \Delta \mu / k_{\mathrm{B}}$ can be regarded as a constant for a given system and so the logarithm of $\tau$ is proportional to $1 / T S_{\mathrm{c}}$.

Figure 8 shows $\log (\tau / \mathrm{s})$ plotted against $T_{0} / T S_{\mathrm{c}}$ for the plastic crystals of cyanoadamantane, isocyanocyclohexane and cyclohexanol. $T_{0}$ is the Kauzmann temperature $^{72)}$ where the extrapolation of $S_{\mathrm{c}}$ in Fig. 7 becomes zero. It has been known that such plots lie on straight lines for most of molecular liquids over a wide time range $(10 \mathrm{ps}-1 \mathrm{ks}),{ }^{42,73)}$ indicating the validity of the AG theory. However, the present plots in the plastic crystals, especially for cyanoadamantane, cannot be reproduced by a straight line.

In the Adam-Gibbs theory, the configurational entropy inside the domain (CRR) is assumed to be zero. However, such a rigid domain may be overly restrictive in the plastic crystals since each of the molecules occupies its own lattice point unobstacled by its neighbors and so the restriction to molecular reorientation from the surrounding molecules may be less stringent. Therefore, we propose a modified version of the Adam-Gibbs theory taking the entropy inside the domain into consideration. We assume that the relaxation time is given by
$$
\tau=\tau_{0} \exp \left[s_{\mathrm{c}}^{*} \Delta \mu / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\left(S_{\mathrm{c}}+S_{\mathrm{id}}\right)\right], \quad(6)
$$
where $S_{\mathrm{id}}$ is intra-domain entropy. It should be noted that $S_{\mathrm{id}}$ is introduced as "entropy" but does not change equilibrium properties such as domain size and heat capacity. $S_{\mathrm{id}}$ changes only relaxational process of CRR. If $S_{\mathrm{id}}$ happens to be equal to $S_{\mathrm{c}}$, for example, the effective size of CRR is the half of the size determined from $S_{\mathrm{c}}$ and orientational relaxation breaking the CRR into two pieces occurs. Therefore, $S_{\mathrm{id}}$ may be regarded as a parameter representing the softness of the domain or the extent of non-cooperative molecular rearrangements. The temperature dependence of $S_{\mathrm{id}}$ is neglected as a first approximation.

It is important to find the dominant factor for the cooperativity of the molecular reorientation in the plastic crystal. The intermolecular interaction energy is clearly not dominant since cyanoadamantane has the smallest cooperativity in spite of the largest interaction energy originating from the large dipole moment. ## §4. Concluding Remarks

The fragility of cyanoadamantane was small while those of isocyanocyclohexane and cyclohexanol were intermediate between the fragile and the strong systems. The analysis based on the modified Adam-Gibbs theory demonstrated that the cooperativity of molecular reorientation in a plastic phase, especially for cyanoadamantane, is smaller than that in a liquid phase. It is also suggested that the cooperativity in the plastic phase is related to the flexibility of the molecule and the number of the molecular orientations. To make further discussion on the molecular cooperativity in the plastic crystals, it is desirable to investigate the plastic crystals of rigid molecules besides cyanoadamantane. We are planning to measure the heat capacity of ortho-carborane. It is also of interest to investigate the molecular reorientation of the glass-forming plastic crystals from the view point of non-exponentiality. Systematic study of the dielectric measurements over wide frequency range is desirable for the glass-forming plastic crystals.
