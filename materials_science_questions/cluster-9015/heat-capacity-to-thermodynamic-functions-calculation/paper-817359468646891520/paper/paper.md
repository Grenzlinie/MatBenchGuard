# Thermodynamic Characteristics of Cobaltite(Nickelite)-Cuprate-
Manganites $LaSrCoCuMnO_{6}$ and $LaSrNiCuMnO_{6}$

B. K. Kasenov$^{a,}$, Sh. B. Kasenova$^{a}$, Zh. I. Sagintaeva$^{a}$,
E. E. Kuanyshbekov$^{a}$, and G. Khabdolda$^{a}$

$^{a}$Abishev Chemical–Metallurgical Institute, Karaganda, 100009 Kazakhstan
*e-mail: kasenov1946@mail.ru

Received February 21, 2019; revised August 16, 2019; accepted December 24, 2019

Abstract—The temperature dependences of the specific heat of nanoscale (nanocluster) cobaltite(nickel-
lite)-cuprate-manganites $LaSrCoCuMnO_{6}$ and $LaSrNiCuMnO_{6}$ have been studied via experimental
dynamic calorimetry in the range of 298.15–673 K. The temperature dependences of the specific heat
$C_{p}^{\circ}\sim f(T)$ of $LaSrCoCuMnO_{6}$ (at 323 and 523 K) and $LaSrNiCuMnO_{6}$ (at 348 K) exhibit anomalous jumps
in the specific heat, which are likely due to the second-order phase transitions. Based on the experimental
data on $C_{p}^{\circ}(T)$ and the calculated value of entropy $S^{\circ}(298.15)$, the temperature dependences of the specific
heat and thermodynamic functions of the studied compounds are calculated in the temperature range of
298.15–673 K.

DOI: 10.1134/S0018151X20020091

## INTRODUCTION

Modern inorganic materials science has undergone
intense development due to the discovery of supercon-
ductivity in cuprates and high magnetic resistance in
manganites of rare-earth metals in addition to alka-
line-earth metal oxides [1]. In addition, samples with
a very high permittivity have recently been obtained
based on oxides of rare-earth and transition $3d$ ele-
ments [2]. Among the oxide materials, two of the most
promising classes can be singled out, and they have
been intensely studied in recent years: high-tempera-
ture superconductors (HTSCs) and materials with
colossal magnetoresistance (CMR) [3]. Despite the
uniqueness of some cuprates and manganites, new
compounds in which manganites, cuprates, cobaltites,
nickelites, etc., are in a unified phase (e.g., cobaltite-
cuprate-manganite and nickelite-cuprate-manganite)
were synthesized.

The purpose of this study was to carry out calori-
metric analysis of the specific heats of nanoscale
cobaltite-cuprate-manganite $LaSrCoCuMnO_{6}$ and
nickelite-cuprate-manganite $LaSrNiCuMnO_{6}$ and to
calculate their thermodynamic functions. Note that
the existing data in the literature on physicochemical
properties are mainly related to individual manganites,
cuprates, and cobaltites of rare-earth elements and
their derivatives doped with oxides of alkaline and
alkaline-earth metals [4–13]. The results of our previ-
ous studies in this field and relevant data in the litera-
ture were generalized in [14].

## EXPERIMENTAL

Cuprate-Manganites $LaSrCoCuMnO_{6}$ and $LaSr-$
$NiCuMnO_{6}$ were synthesized with ceramic technol-
ogy via the high-temperature interaction of stoichio-
metric amounts of $La_{2}O_{3}$ (special purity grade), $CoO$,
$NiO$, $CuO$, $Mn_{2}O_{3}$, and $SrCO_{3}$ (analytical grade).
Mixtures of these materials were previously dehy-
drated at $400^{\circ}C$, carefully mixed in stoichiometric
amounts, ground in an agate mortar, and then trans-
ferred to alundum crucibles and annealed in an SNOL
furnace at $800-1200^{\circ}C$ for 20 h. Before each increase
in the temperature (to 800, 1000, and $1200^{\circ}C$), the
mixtures were cooled and carefully ground. To make
the obtained phases stable at low temperatures, they
were annealed at $400^{\circ}C$ for 10 h. Nanoscale (nano-
cluster) cuprate-manganite particles were fabricated
via grinding on an MM301 vibrational mill (Retsch,
Germany). The particle sizes were determined on a
JSPM-5400 atomic force microscope (JEOL, Japan)
(Fig. 1).

Note that atomic force microscopy (AFM) is a
high-resolution (up to 1 nm) method that makes it
possible to obtain 3D surface images. AFM is widely
used for nanoscale structural analysis of different
materials [15].

The X-ray diffraction patterns were indexed analytically [16]. It was established that the synthesized compounds are crystallized into the cubic system with the following lattice parameters: $a = 14.15 \pm 0.02$ $\mathring{A}$, $V^0 = 2835.19 \pm 0.05$ $\mathring{A}^3$, $Z = 4$, $V^0_{\text{cell}} = 708.80 \pm 0.01$ $\mathring{A}^3$, $\rho_{\text{X-ray}} = 4.42$ g/cm$^3$, and $\rho_{\text{picn}} = 4.38 \pm 0.01$ g/cm$^3$ for LaSrCoCuMnO<sub>6</sub> and $a = 14.49 \pm 0.02$ $\mathring{A}$, $V^0 = 3039.43 \pm 0.06$ $\mathring{A}^3$, $Z = 4$, $V^0_{\text{cell}} = 759.86 \pm 0.02$ $\mathring{A}^3$,

$\rho_{\text{X-ray}} = 4.43$ g/cm³, and $\rho_{\text{picn}} = 4.38 \pm 0.002$ g/cm³ for $\text{LaSrNiCuMnO}_6$ [17, 18]. Based on the XRD data, we can suggest that the studied compounds (as the other binary and ternary manganites of rare-earth, alkaline, and alkaline-earth metals [14]) have the perovskite space group $Pm3m$.

The individuality of the compounds was confirmed via IR spectroscopy of nanoparticles on an FSM 1201 Fourier spectrometer. For $\text{LaSrCoCuMnO}_6$, the absorption band at $628.88$ cm⁻¹ may be related to the vibration $\nu_i(\text{MnO}_6)$ ($W_c(\text{CoO})$ harmonic frequency); the band at $1087.99$ cm⁻¹ may be related to a change in the symmetry of the $\text{CoO}_3$ group; and the bands at $1469.95$ and $1635.84$ cm⁻¹ may be related to changes in the symmetry of the $\text{MnO}_3$ group. For $\text{LaSrNiCuMnO}_6$, the band at $551.72$ cm⁻¹ can be assigned to the vibration $\nu_i(\text{MnO}_6)$ ($W_c(\text{NiO})$ harmonic frequency) and the bands at $1454.51$ and $1628.13$ cm⁻¹ can be considered changes in the symmetry of the $\text{MnO}_3$ group. The thermal analysis of the compounds was performed on a Paulik–Paulik–Erdey derivatograph in the range of $25-1000^\circ\text{C}$.

### $\text{LaSrCoCuMnO}_6$

The compound mass before analysis was 840 mg. During scanning from $25^\circ$ to $1000^\circ\text{C}$, the mass loss was as low as 10 mg (i.e., 1.2% of the total mass). The endothermic effect¹ in the temperature range of 300–$410^\circ\text{C}$ is likely related to ordering of the low-temperature phase. Apparently, the following clear endothermic effect at $850^\circ\text{C}$ corresponds to the enantiotropic polymorphic transformation with slight congruent evaporation.

### $\text{LaSrNiCuMnO}_6$

The compound mass before analysis was 550 mg. During scanning to $950^\circ\text{C}$, the mass loss was as low as 5 mg (i.e., the compound does not decompose below this temperature). The material exhibits a flat exothermic effect between 180 and $470^\circ\text{C}$ (with a maximum at $360^\circ\text{C}$), which is likely related to some kind of ordering of the low-temperature phase. The analysis was completed at $950^\circ\text{C}$.

Based on the these results, we can conclude that new nanoscale $\text{LaSrCoCuMnO}_6$ and $\text{LaSrNiCuMnO}_6$ compounds were obtained. According to [19], they can be assigned to solid nanoclusters, the sizes of which vary in an extremely wide range (from one to several hundred nanometers).

The isobaric specific heats of $\text{LaSrCoCuMnO}_6$ and $\text{LaSrNiCuMnO}_6$ were studied in the temperature range of 173–673 K on an IT-S-400 calorimeter. According to the instrument specification, the temperature range of specific-heat measurements is from $-100$ to $+400^\circ\text{C}$ (173–673 K). The principle of operation of this device is based on the comparative technique of a dynamic c-calorimeter with a heat meter. The measuring schematic of the device makes it possible to measure temperatures from $-100$ to $+400^\circ\text{C}$ at fixed points with a step of $25^\circ\text{C}$ with a built-in dc potentiometer and a switch. The bulk specific heat measurement range is no less than $10^6$ J/(K m³). The time spent for scanning of the entire temperature range and the processing of experimental data does not exceed 2.5 h. The measurement error of IT-S-400 is within $\pm10\%$. Liquid nitrogen was used as a cooling agent.

The device was calibrated based on the determination of the heat-meter thermal conductivity $K_T$ [20, 21]. To this end, several experiments with a copper sample and an empty ampoule were carried out. The heat-meter thermal conductivity was determined from the formula
$$
K_T = C_{\text{c.sample}} \big/ \left(\overline{\tau}_{\text{TM}} - \overline{\tau}_{\text{r}}^\circ\right),
$$
where $C_{\text{c.sample}}$ is the total specific heat of the copper sample (in J/(mol K)) and $\overline{\tau}_{\text{TM}}$, and $\overline{\tau}_{\text{r}}^\circ$ are the average delays in the heat meter in the experiments with the copper sample and the empty ampoule, respectively (in s).

The total specific heat of the copper sample was calculated from the formula
$$
C_{\text{c.sample}} = C_{\text{c}}m_{\text{s}},
$$
where $C_{\text{c}}$ is the tabular value of the copper specific heat (in J/(mol K)) and $m_{\text{s}}$ is the copper sample mass (kg).

The specific heat of the material was calculated by the formula
$$
C_{\text{sp}} = K_T \big/ \left(m_0(\tau_{\text{h}} - \tau_{\text{h}}^\circ)\right),
$$
where $m_0$ is the mass of the studied material, $\tau_{\text{h}}$ is the time delay of the heat-meter temperature, and $\tau_{\text{h}}^\circ$ is the time delay of the heat-meter temperature in the experiments with the empty ampoule (in s).

The molar specific heat was calculated with the specific-heat value and allowance for the molar mass. Five simultaneous experiments were carried out at each temperature step, the results of which were averaged and processed by the methods of mathematical statistics.

At each temperature step, the rms deviation $\overline{\delta}$ for the averaged specific-heat values was estimated according to [21]:
$$
\delta = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (C_i - \overline{C})^2}{n-1}}. \tag{1}
$$

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The results of calorimetric analysis presented in Fig. 2 and Table 3 show that there are anomalous jumps in the specific heat in the dependences $C_{p}^{\circ}(T)$ for LaSrCoCuMnO₆ (at 323 and 523 K) and LaSrNi-CuMnO₆ (at 348 K), which are likely related to a second-order phase transition. These transitions may be due to the Schottky effects; changes in the magnetic resistance, electrical conductivity, and permittivity; the presence of the Curie and Neel points; etc.

Note that the real errors in the experimental data on specific heat determined from (1) and (2) are much lower than the limiting device accuracy (i.e., below 10%).

The technical capabilities of the IT-S-400 calorimeter do not make it possible to increase the number of measurements near the phase-transition points because, according to the device technical characteristics, specific heat is measured only with a step of 25 K.

The standard entropy values of the compounds were estimated with the use of a system of ionic entropic increments [23], which were equal to 262 ± 8 and 253 ± 8 J/(mol K) for LaSrCoCuMnO₆ and LaSrNi-CuMnO₆, respectively.

The $C_{p}^{\circ}(T)$ dependences and thermodynamic functions (entropy $S^{\circ}(T)$, enthalpy $H^{\circ}(T)-H^{\circ}(298.15)$, and Gibbs potential $\Phi^{xx}(T)$) were calculated in the range of 298.15–675 K with the known relations (Table 4). This temperature range was chosen, and the fact that the function $\Phi^{xx}(T)$ is calculated beginning with 298.15 K was taken into account. ## CONCLUSIONS

(i) The temperature dependences of the specific heat of nanoscale cobaltite(nickelite)-cuprate-man- ganites were experimentally studied via dynamic calo- rimetry in the range of 298.15–673 K.

(ii) Anomalous jumps in the specific heat were observed in the dependences $C_{p}^{\circ}(T)$ of $\mathrm{LaSrCoCuMnO}_{6}$ (at 323 and 523 K) and $\mathrm{LaSrNiCuMnO}_{6}$ (at 348 K).

(iii) The temperature dependences of the specific heat, entropy, enthalpy, and Gibbs energy of the stud- ied compounds were calculated based on the experi- mental data on the dependence $C_{p}^{\circ}(T)$.
