## Abstract

In the present study, 1-Benzyl-4-(N-Boc-amino)piperidine was characterized by spectroscopic investigations employing FT-IR, FT-Raman, UV-Vis and NMR ($^{13}$C, $^{1}$H) techniques. The optimized geometrical parameters and complete vibrational assignments of frequencies based on Potential Energy Distribution (PED) were determined by using Density Functional Theory (DFT) through the B3LYP and dispersion correction method WB97XD with the level of 6-311++G(d,p) basis set and compared with the experimental values. The Potential Energy Scan (PES) studies have been employed to investigate the conformational preference of the molecule. Besides, to gain better insight on structural features of title composite, HOMO-LUMO bandgap energy and electron excitation analysis was obtained. Molecular Electrostatic Potential (MEP) energy surface and Fukui function descriptor using NPA (Natural Population Analysis) charges were employed to investigate the most reactive sites of the title compound. Natural bond orbital analysis (NBO) elucidates the delocalization of charge due to intramolecular interactions. The topological studies (AIM, ELF and LOL) and thermodynamic parameters at different temperatures were performed. The anticancer activity of header compound against VEGFR-2 Kinase inhibitor receptors for different protein targets (6GQO, 6GQP, 4AG8 and 1Y6B) was studied using molecular docking.

Key words: DFT; FT-IR; HOMO-LUMO; Fukui functions; Molecular docking

## 1. Introduction

The heterocyclic aromatic system has a promising role in the organic molecules. With its origins entrenched in the fields of medicinal chemistry, pharmaceutical and agrochemical industries, the compounds present themselves as an essential division for therapeutic agents, drug discovery and dyestuffs [1]. The piperidine and its derivatives have a chief scaffold in

the synthesis of pharmacological activity particularly in performing as an anticancer agent by acting on various receptors of extreme importance [2]. Further, it is a major importance in various chemical dehydration reactions such as DNA sequencing in the cleavage of modified nucleotides [3]. Kallstrom et al.,[4] reported that in recent years the piperidine derivatives are the platforms in preclinical and clinical studies. There are some complex human diseases with specific cancer types that had inadequate curative options, thus the investigation of new chemical entities with enhanced therapeutic responses and reduced unfavourable drug reactions was a crucial preclinical process for effective treatment of cancers. Angiogenesis inhibitors (formation of new blood vessels) play a crucial role to decrease or slow down the spread and development of cancer cell types by suppressing the tumor ability to form new blood cells [5, 6]. Thus, there is a requirement for an angiogenesis inhibitor to hinder the growth of tumor cells. Hence, the importance of research in the field of angiogenesis inhibitors, the role of VEGF's (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor) Kinase families (an ideal therapeutic target) has been extensively identified as the critical factor in regulating normal physiological and tumor angiogenesis [7]. The VEGFR inhibitors have become an important arsenal in the treatment of various cancer types including colorectal cancer (CRC), breast, lung, glioblastoma [8–11] respectively. Hence the Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Receptor-2 (VEGFR-2)- a receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) [12] is one of the prime regulators of angiogenic inhibitors from vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) family which are pivotal encouragement of both biological as well as pathological angiogenesis [13].

A complete literature survey reported that no detailed quantum computational methods were investigated for the title compound, 1-Benzyl-4-(N-Boc-amino)piperidine (1B4NAP).This inspires to provide a complete vibrational as well as spectroscopic characterization (FT-IR, FT-Raman, UV-Vis and NMR) on the 1B4NAP using DFT methods with B3LYP, WB97XD /6-311++G(d,p) level as the basis set. In this attempt, the electronic properties were investigated from HUMO-LUMO energies. The electron excitation and topology analysis (AIM, ELF and LOL) were obtained from a wave function analysis program Multiwfn 3.7. The reactive sites and the distribution of electrons were analysed using Molecular Electrostatic Potential (MEP) and Fukui function descriptors. The Natural Bond Orbitals (NBO) and the variation of thermodynamic potentials with temperature have also been reported. Molecular docking studies have been performed (ligand-protein interactions) to explore the binding interactions of the most active compound to the targeted proteins (VEGFR-2) inhibitors along with DFT calculations.

### 3.Computational details

In this research, the computation of 1B4NAP was achieved by adopting the DFT
method by using the Becke-3-Lee-Yang-Parr (B3LYP) level of theory with 6-311++G(d,p)
basis set that is implemented in the Gaussian 09W program package [14]. The structural
conformational behaviour by Potential Energy Scan (PES) studies and the molecular
geometric parameters for 1B4NAP were investigated. The Theoretically calculated FT-IR
and FT-Raman spectra at the same level of the theory. The calculated harmonic vibrational
frequencies were generally higher than the observed value. Hence the computed
wavenumbers were scaled by introducing the scaling factors as 0.961,0.957 respectively. The
complete theoretical vibrational distribution analysis of the 1B4NAP was procured based on
potential energy distribution (PED) analysis with the same level of theory that has been done
with the VEDA 4 program [15]. The Carbon $^{13}$C and Proton $^{1}$H NMR chemical shifts were
performed with the Gauge-Including Atomic Orbital (GIAO) approach by applying the
B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) method and compared with the experimental ($^{13}$C and $^{1}$H ) NMR
spectra. MEP (Molecular Electrostatic Potential), NBO (Natural Orbital Analysis), Fukui
functions, FMO's were also implemented using the aforesaid basis theory level. The electron
excitation analysis and the topology analysis (AIM, ELF and LOL) were performed using
MULTIWFN program for wavefunction analysis [16]. The temperature dependence on the
thermodynamical variables was obtained using THERMO.PL program [17]. Molecular
docking analysis (ligand-protein docking interaction) has been performed using Auto dock
suite program 4.2.1[18].

### 4.4. Electronic properties

#### 4.4.1. Frontier Molecular Orbital

The Frontier molecular orbital (HOMO and LUMO) affords the information about the electrical and optical properties, as well as in UV-Vis spectra and quantum chemistry, it is used to explain various types of reactions in conjugated systems [35]. Frontier Molecular Orbital (FMO) theory also associates molecular orbital energies and orbital coefficients with the reactivity of compounds. The HOMO-LUMO orbitals are referred to as Frontier Molecular Orbitals (FMO) as they lie at the outermost boundaries for the electrons in a system. The pictorial representations of HOMO and LUMO orbitals have been calculated using the B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) basis set as depicted in Fig.5. In 1B4NAP, the Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital (HOMO) signifies the ability to donate an electron (nucleophile) and the energy of HOMO is directly connected to the ionisation potential (I). The Lowest Unoccupied Molecular Orbital LUMO value implies the tendency to gain an electron (electrophile) and LUMO energy associated directly with electron affinity (A). As can be seen from Fig.5, the HOMO is mainly localized over piperidine ring with calculated energy as EHOMO = -6.222 eV, while the LUMO ($\pi$ nature) is confined mainly in a benzene ring structure (C-C bond) with ELUMO = -0.596 eV. Thus, the energy gap (eV) was observed to be 5.626 eV between HOMO and LUMO shown in Fig.5, revealed the chemical stability of the 1B4NAP and defined the charge transfer interactions within the compound which are required for biological activity descriptor. The HOMO and LUMO energies, Ionisation potential (I), Electron affinity (A), Energy gap, chemical hardness ($\eta$), chemical potential ($\mu$), Electrophilicity Index ($\omega$), Nucleophilicity Index (N), Global Softness (S), Additional Electronic Charges ($\Delta$N) and Optical Softness ($\sigma_.$) are calculated by using the relation $\eta$ = (I − A)/2 = 2.813 eV and $\mu$ = − (I + A)/2 = − 3.409 eV, Electrophilicity Index ($\omega$) = $\mu^2$ /2$\eta$ = 2.065 eV, Nucleophilicity Index (N) (eV⁻¹) =1/ $\omega$= 0.484, Global Softness (S) = 1/2$\eta$= 0.177, Additional Electronic Charges ($\Delta$N) (donor and acceptor charge behavior) = - $\mu$ / $\eta$ = -1.211, Optical Softness ($\sigma_.$)=1/(Energy gap) = 0.177 for 1B4NAP have been tabulated in Table 3. respectively [36]. The quantum chemical parameters significantly predict the biological activity of 1B4NAP. The electrophilicity index ($\omega$) scale allowable the organic compound as strong electrophiles with $\omega$ > 1.5 eV, moderate electrophiles with ranges 0.8 < $\omega$ < 1.5 eV, and very weaker electrophiles with $\omega$ < 0.8 eV. Herein, the header compound (1B4NAP) indicates that the higher value of $\omega$, so are good as an electrophile. Thus, the 1B4NAP may be predicted as a possible descriptor of biological activities [37].

### 4.5. Molecular electrostatic potential (MEP)

Molecular Electrostatic Potential (MEP) is also abbreviated as ESP, is an essential tool for interpreting electrostatic (electron and nuclei) distribution potential and envisaging the reactive site of wide ranges in both electrophilic and nucleophilic attacks in chemical reactions, hydrogen bonding interactions and for biological platforms [39]. The ESP iso-surface plot provides a significant visual method to comprehend about reactive sites and various polar regions of the compound. To identify suitable sites for nucleophilic and electrophilic attacks, MEP and contour diagram for the 1B4NAP was calculated at the basis set B3LYP/6-311++G (d,p) level and represented in Fig. 7a and 7b. The surface region arranged hierarchically as red < orange < yellow < green < blue. The red regions (electron enrichment regions) which are countered over around the regions of oxygen (O1 and O2) atoms in 1B4NAP exhibit nucleophilic region, whereas blue colour region (absence of electrons) prone to electrophilic region. From Fig. 7a, the maximum positive region is localized on the C-H bonds over the piperidine ring. The average of two extremes represents zero potential as green colour, whereas the slightly electron-rich areas represented as yellow in colour respectively. The colour code of the map in 1B4NAP is in the range between -6.031 a.u (dark red) and 6.031 a.u (dark blue region) respectively. These active regions are the rich validation of biological activity in the header composite.

### 4.6. Natural Bond Analysis

Natural Bond Analysis (NBO) analysis contributes a convenient basis for inspecting the high electron density in the orbital bonding of the molecular sysytem [40]. Besides, 1B4NAP with the aid of second order perturbation theory analysis using the Fock matrix in NBO basis was carried out to assess the electron-donors (Lewis type-occupied) and electron-acceptors (Non-Lewis type-vacant) i.e, the more donating propensity from donors to acceptors and the larger the extent of conjugation of whole system respectively. NBO analysis has been achieved for header composite at the DFT/B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) level and a summary of electron donor, acceptor orbitals, and stabilization energies E(2) for the substantial intramolecular charge transfer interactions construed from the NBO calculations were tabulated in Table S3.

The Energy E(2) associated with each donor (i) to an acceptor (j) delocalization is determined from the second-order perturbation method is estimated as:

$$\mathrm{E}(2)=\Delta \mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{ij}}=\mathrm{q}_{\mathrm{i}}\left[\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{i}, \mathrm{j})^{2} /\left(\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{i}}-\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{j}}\right)\right] \tag{1}$$

where $q_i$ is the population of donor orbital tenancy, are Ei and Ej are orbital energies (diagonal elements) of donor and acceptor orbitals and F(i,j) is the off-diagonal NBO Fock matrix elements [41] respectively. For 1B4NAP, the notable interactions from $\pi$ (C11-C15), $\pi$(C20-C21), $\pi$(C14-C19) conjugate to the antibonding orbitals of $\pi$ *(C14-C19), $\pi$ *(C20-C21) with stabilization energies of 20.47, 20.95 respectively. ### 4.7. Topology Analysis

#### 4.7.1. Atoms in Molecule (AIM)

One of the most powerful tools is preferred to exploit the topology analysis of electron density or charge density of molecules, in the real space boundaries of the atoms forming a system and aiding to evaluate their properties using the Bader theory of 'Atoms in Molecules' (AIM) [42]. There are four kinds of non-degenerate stable critical points (CP's) of rank 3 is, local minima (3, +3) (Cage Critical Point CCP), local maxima (3, -3) (Nuclear Critical Point NCP), first-order saddle point (3, +1) (Ring Critical Point RCP) and second-order saddle point (3,-1) (Bond Critical Point BCP) corresponding to their positions of nuclei, bonds, and rings. The topology analysis was performed using Multiwfn (3.7) program [16]. The chemical insights of atoms, molecules, structures, and bonds can be defined by the analysis of the topology of the total electronic density ($\rho_{BCP}$), Laplacian of electron densities ($\nabla^{2}\rho$) (measure of the local charge concentration) at the bond critical point (BCP), the potential energy density ($V_{BCP}$) that describes the system to concentrate electrons at BCP's,

and the local kinetic energy densities ($G_{BCP}$) that represents the electron tendency to spread out in space. The total electronic energy densities ($H_{BCP}$) ($H_{BCP} = V_{BCP}+ G_{BCP}$) will give evidence about the concentration of electron density and bonding types. The ellipticity ($\varepsilon$) can be calculated at their position in space of interacting atoms or fragments as listed in Table S4 and the molecular surface map for 1B4NAP is shown in Fig.S4. According to the theory, the Laplacian shows a significant role in trace the effects of chemical bonding in the total charge density and the interaction types classified as (i) if the values of ($\nabla^{2}\rho_{BCP}$) and $H_{BCP}<0$ it specifies the strong H-bond interaction, shared electron (covalent), and excess potential energy at BCP (ii) if the values of ($\nabla^{2}\rho_{BCP})>0$ and $H_{BCP}<0$ indicates that the H-bond is of medium-weak interaction and moderately covalent in nature, and the kinetic energy dominant iii) H-bond is weak and electrostatic (closed shell) nature if both the values are ($\nabla^{2}\rho_{BCP}$) and $H_{BCP}>0$ [43]. Further, the closed type of interaction occurs when the ratio of ($V_{BCP}/G_{BCP})<$ 1 and for the shared shell interaction the ratio values are greater than two. The intermediate interaction occurs when $1<(V_{BCP}/G_{BCP})<2$ [42]. Further, the closed shell interaction between O2-H42 (positive ($\nabla^{2}\rho_{BCP}$) value) and the shared shell interaction of C5-H22 (negative ($\nabla^{2}\rho_{BCP}$)) atom for header composite were shown in Fig. 8A. respectively. From the Table S4, the $\rho(r)$ values are positive, the negative Laplacian of the electron density value (C5-H22) is strong evidence that the electronic charge is locally concentrated in the internuclear region with potential energy dominant (shared-electron) whereas the interaction of O2-H42, O2-H38 atoms are electrostatic nature where both ($\nabla^{2}\rho_{BCP}$) and $H_{BCP}$ values are slightly greater than zero (closed-shell). The lower values of ellipticity infer that there is delocalization of electrons in aromatic rings [44].

### 4.8. Fukui function

Fukui function is among the most basic and key factor to describe chemical reactivity and site-selective indicators. The Fukui function analysis obtained from effective NPA (Natural Population Analysis) atomic calculation has been reported to be in good acceptance. Nevertheless, the Fukui function is a local reactive parameter that specifies the tendency of the electronic density to deform at a given position to donate or accept electrons that are more inclined to undergo an electrophilic or a nucleophilic attack respectively. The global reactivity parameters such as electronegativity ($\chi$), chemical hardness ($\eta$) and softness (S) describes the grades of the reactivity of an atomic site in a molecular system. The values of the condensed Fukui function ($\mathrm{f_j^+}$, $\mathrm{f_j^-}$, $\mathrm{f_j^0}$), local softness's ($\mathrm{S_j^+}$, $\mathrm{S^-_j}$, $\mathrm{S_j^0}$) and local electrophilicity indices ($\omega_j^+$, $\omega_j^-$, $\omega_j^0$) were calculated for electrophilic, nucleophilic and radical attacks have been performed using B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) level of computation for the 1B4NAP are given in Table S5. The condensed or atomic Fukui functions on the $\mathrm{j^{th}}$ atom site in the neutral (N), anionic (N+1) or cationic (N−1) chemical species were calculated using following equations,

$$\mathrm{f_j^- = [q_j(N)-q_j(N-1)] \text{ for electrophilic attack} \tag{2}}$$

$$\mathrm{f_j^+ = [q_j(N+1) -q_j(N)] \text{ for nucleophilic attack} \tag{3}}$$

$$\mathrm{f_j^0 = [q_j(N+1) -q_j(N-1)] / 2 \text{ for radical attack} \tag{4}}$$

Parr et.al [46], reported that the larger value of the Fukui function designates the higher reactivity of a particular atomic center in a molecule. Further, the dual descriptor ($\Delta$f(r)) can distinguish those sites of nucleophilic and electrophilic behavior at a particular site with a suitable sign. Thus, the general working equation to achieve dual descriptor is given by the difference between nucleophilic and electrophilic Fukui function such as $\Delta$f(r) = [$\mathrm{f^+}$(r) - $\mathrm{f^-}$(r)] and when, $\Delta$f(r) > 0, preferable sites for nucleophilic attack (or Lewis basic), whereas if $\Delta$f(r) < 0, then the site indicates electrophilic attack (or Lewis acid) into the system at point r. They afford positive value for sites prone to nucleophilic attack and a negative value for an electrophilic attack. In the present investigation, the Fukui function has been performed based on Natural Population Analysis (NPA). The higher values of electrophilic nature in C12 and C13 are due to the presence of high electronegativity of two oxygen atoms O1 and O2. The dual descriptor, nucleophile sites (i.e., $\Delta$f(r) > 0) for 1B4NAP are from O1 to C10, and C14 to C21. Similarly, for electrophilic sites are C12, C13, and C11 atoms. The local softness (Sr) and local electrophilicity ($\omega$r) are also estimated to describe the reactivity of atoms for the present header composite. These equations conclude that the most nucleophilic site in a title compound has the highest value e of s- and $\omega$- while the highest value of s+ and $\omega+$ reveals the most electrophilic site in the compound. The maximum values of s-, $\omega$-, s+, $\omega+$ envisage the most nucleophilic and electrophilic sites in a system.

### 4.10. Molecular Docking

Molecular docking analysis is an imperative strategy for assessing potential ligand (1B4NAP)-target (protein/enzyme) interactions, which play a significant role in the discovery of drugs. This technique can be effectively used to predict the bonded residues, hydrogen bond, estimated inhibition constant, binding energy, RMSD values of selected protein receptors. The most powerful rationale for targeting VEGF's families is its major role in tumor angiogenesis. Several other characteristics are also proved that the VEGF can be used as an attractive agent for anti-cancer therapy. The VEGFR's Kinase Inhibitor was selected for clinical development based on preclinical evidence which is showing high antiangiogenic and antitumor activity. Several small molecule VEGFR-2 kinase inhibitors including sorafenib, regorafenib, sunitinib, vandetanib, pazopanib, axitinib and cabozantinib targeting signal pathway [48-49] have developed as potential antiangiogenic drugs for treatment against a wide variety of cancer types. Narasimhamurthy et al [50] reported about recent advancement of anticancer profiles in the piperidine derivatives. In this connection, molecular docking simulations were performed to study the binding interactions of the 1B4NAP with VEGFR-2 Kinase inhibitor active site. The title compound was chosen to be docked into the active sites of the protein targets such as 6GQO, 6GQP, 4AG8, and 1Y6B which are downloaded from RCSB (Research Collaboratory for Structural Bioinformatics) protein data bank. To predict the active binding site (ligand-protein) interaction the Autodock software [25] tool was

executed. Initially, the suitable targeted protein in .pdb format was well prepared by removing co-crystalline ligands, water, and the co-factors. The atomic charges were computed and polar hydrogen was attached using the Kollman and Gasteiger method. The targeted protein was defined with a suitable grid size in the grid set map types and the Lamarckian Genetic Algorithm (LGA) method was used to implement the process. The molecular docking binding energies (kcal/mol), estimated inhibition constant (µm), reference RMSD (Å) values are enlisted in Table 5. The ligand formed a stable complex with selected targeted protein 6GQO with minimum binding energy value which is observed as -6.12 kcal/mol and confirms the formation of amino acid residues as ARG1027, ASP1046 with bond distances 2.6 Å, 2.2 Å. The molecular docking diagram and their binding interactions of 1B4NAP with VEGFR-2 receptor kinase proteins are depicted in Fig.9. The interaction of the 1B4NAP was found to show hydrogen bond interaction with active sites including H33 and O2 atoms respectively. Thus, the 1B4NAP validates that it may act as an ideal drug target for anticancer therapy.

### 5. Conclusion

In the present study, the experimental and theoretical spectroscopic investigations of 1B4NAP using FT-IR, FT-Raman, NMR ($^{13}$C, $^{1}$H) and UV-Vis analyses have been reported. The experimental and simulated interpretations were found to be in harmony. The Potential Energy Scan (PES) showed the conformational stability of the header composite. The optimized geometry structure of 1B4NAP has been compared with the X-ray crystallographic data of related molecule which has a reliable agreement. Frontier Molecular Orbital analysis reveals the energy gap E= 5.626 eV which indicates the chemical stability of 1B4NAP. The electronic excitation analyses (S0, S1 and S2) were performed using Multiwfn 3.7 program. The study of the Molecular Electrostatic Potential (MEP) map revealed the most reactive sites of electrophilic (electron-deficient species) region around the C-H bonds over the piperidine region, nucleophilic (electron-rich species) region over the oxygen atoms (O1 and O2) respectively. Further, the NBO analysis reveals that there is an effective charge transfer within the molecule. The Fukui function was performed to predict the chemical reactivity sites using Natural Population Analysis (NPA) atomic charges and topological studies were reported based on AIM, ELF, LOL using Multiwfn 3.7 analyser. Thermodynamic parameters were calculated at different temperatures ranging from 100-1000 K. Molecular docking investigation was carried out on 1B4NAP with VEGFR-2 kinase inhibitor with protein receptors such as 6GQO, 6GQP, 4AG8 and 1Y6B. The minimum binding energy (-6.12 kcal/mol) confirmation is seen in the interaction of protein 6GQO with bonded residues as ARG1027 and ASP1046. Thus, a novel title compound 1B4NAP which may be predicted as a potential therapeutic target for anti-cancer treatment.
