# Determination of the thermodynamic stability of europium boride (EuB₆)

Ashish Jain¹ · R. Kandan¹

Received: 15 June 2017 / Accepted: 3 December 2017 / Published online: 11 December 2017
© Akadémiai Kiadó, Budapest, Hungary 2017

## Abstract
The standard Gibbs energy of formation of europium boride ($\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}G^{\circ}\langle\mathrm{EuB}_{6}\rangle$) was experimentally measured by the electromotive force method by using yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) as the solid electrolyte. Two galvanic cells, viz., cell I: Pt, EuB₆ (s), Eu₂O₃ (s), B (s) | YSZ | NiO (s), Ni (s), Pt and cell II: Pt, EuB₆ (s), Eu₂O₃ (s), B (s) | YSZ | FeO (s), Fe (s), Pt, were constructed in order to determine the $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}G^{\circ}$ of EuB₆ (s). Enthalpy increments on EuB₆ (s) were measured by using inverse drop calorimeter over the temperature range 765–1549 K. The heat capacity, entropy and the Gibbs energy functions were derived from these experimental data in the temperature range 300–1600 K. The mean value of the standard enthalpy of formation of EuB₆ ($\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H_{298}^{\circ}\langle\mathrm{EuB}_{6}\rangle$) was obtained by $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}G^{\circ}$ values and the Gibbs energy functions (fefs) of EuB₆ (s) derived from drop calorimetric data. The mean values of $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H_{298}^{\circ}\langle EuB_{6}\rangle$ derived from the $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}G^{\circ}$ data obtained from cells I and II were $-173\pm3$ and $-180\pm3$ kJ mol⁻¹, respectively. These values were found to be in good agreement with the values calculated from the reported data on enthalpy increments.

**Keywords** Europium boride · Yttria-stabilized zirconia · EMF method · Standard Gibbs energy of formation · Enthalpy increment

## Introduction
Europium boride (EuB₆) has got interesting physical and chemical properties, viz., antiferromagnetism below 6 K, low thermal expansion coefficient, good thermal conductivity, high hardness, high wear resistance, excellent corrosion and oxidation resistance at high temperature and high melting point (2933 K) [1, 2]. EuB₆ (s) is one of the candidate materials for control rod application in nuclear reactors. High neutron absorption cross sections of boron (759 barns) and europium (4600 barns) and ability of EuB₆ (s) structure to accommodate helium atoms make this compound useful for control rod application in nuclear reactor. EuB₆ has special advantages with respect to nuclear worth [3, 4].

In the past, many authors have attempted synthesis and characterization of EuB₆ (s). The work on the synthesis of EuB₆ (s) by solid-state method (reduction in Eu₂O₃ (s) by boron or boron carbide) was reported in Ref. [1, 2]. Bao et al. [5] have reported the synthesis of nanocrystalline EuB₆ (s) by solid-state reaction of Eu₂O₃ with NaBH₄ over a temperature range of 1173–1473 K. Bukatova et al. [6, 7] have reported electrochemical synthesis of EuB₆ (s) using a molten salt over a temperature range 973–1023 K.

Fundamental thermodynamic quantities such as enthalpy of formation at 298 K, entropy and heat capacity are required in order to estimate the standard Gibbs energy of formation of EuB₆ (s). Literature on the thermodynamic investigation on EuB₆ (s) is scarce. Few investigators have examined the enthalpy of EuB₆ (s) and reported its thermodynamic characteristics.

Dunner et al. [3] reported temperature dependence of thermal conductivity for non-irradiated EuB₆ (s) having density, 90% of theoretical value. It was observed that B₄C (s) and EuB₆ (s) had similar heat conduction behavior.

### Experimental

Boron, iron and nickel powders with a purity of
99.99 mass% and powders of NiO (s), FeO (s) and $\mathrm{Eu}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$
(s) with a purity > 99 mass% were procured from M/s.
Aldrich, USA. YSZ solid electrolyte pellet (10 mm diam-
eter and 2 mm height) was procured from M/s. Advanced
Tech, Mumbai. Standard reference material $(\alpha-\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$,
SRM 720) was procured from NIST, USA. Europium
boride was synthesized in Materials Processing Chemistry
Section, IGCAR, Kalpakkam.

### Synthesis of $\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ (s)

$\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ (s) was synthesized by borothermic reduction method
[1, 2]. The starting materials $(\mathrm{Eu}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$, boron) were heat
treated at 423 K under vacuum (0.1 Pa) to remove
adsorbed moisture and oxygen impurities. Stoichiometric
quantities of vacuum-dried $\mathrm{Eu}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ (s) and boron (s) powder
were mixed and homogenized in a planetary ball mill. The
powder was cold compacted into pellets of 16 mm diam-
eter and 2–3 mm height at a pressure of 100 MPa. These
pellets were heat treated at 1473 K by gradually raising
temperature $(10 \mathrm{~K} \mathrm{~min}^{-1})$ from ambient to 1473 K under a
vacuum of the order of $\sim 0.1$ MPa. The sample was held
at this temperature and pressure for 72 h. The product
obtained was ground to fine powder (particle size
$<150 \mu \mathrm{m}$). The fine powder was leached with acidified
water (5% HCl solution) in order to remove boric oxide.
The typical chemical reaction involved in the synthesis of
$\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ could be represented as:

$$\mathrm{Eu}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}(\mathrm{~s})+14 \mathrm{~B}(\mathrm{~s}) \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{EuB}_{6}(\mathrm{~s})+\mathrm{B}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}(\mathrm{~g}).$$

In order to ascertain the chemical purity of synthesized
$\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ (s), a complete chemical assay of $\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ (s) was
carried out with the help of an inductively coupled plasma
mass spectrometer (ICPMS, Model: Elan 250, M/s. Sciex,
Toronto, Canada). X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis of
$\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ (s) was carried out by using an Explorer System,
procured from M/s. G N R, Italy.

The test electrode was prepared by homogenizing an
equimolar mixture of the powdered constituents [$\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ (s),
$\mathrm{Eu}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ (s) and B (s)]. The powder mixture was cold com-
pacted into pellets of 10 mm diameter and 1–2 mm height
at a pressure of 100 MPa. These pellets were heat treated
under helium at 900 K for 24 h. The phase purity of a heat
treated pellet was ascertained by XRD analysis, and it was
found to contain only $\mathrm{EuB}_{6}$ (s) $\mathrm{Eu}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ (s) and B (s). These
pellets were used as the test electrode in the EMF
experiment.

The reference electrode for cell I was a mixture of
Ni (s) and NiO (s) and for cell II it was a mixture of Fe
(s) and FeO (s). These reference electrodes were prepared
in the same way as test electrode.

## Enthalpy increment measurement on EuB₆ (s) by drop calorimetry

A differential calorimeter (Model HT 1500 from M/s. SETARAM, France) was used to measure enthalpy increment on EuB₆ (s). The detailed description of calorimeter, the procedure adopted to determine enthalpy increment and method to compute thermodynamic functions, has been described elsewhere [14]. The instrument functions as an isoperibol calorimeter and employs a thermopile detector for measuring the heat flow between sample and reference pans. The thermopile detector was made of 18 Pt–Rh thermocouples. Two types of thermopiles were used as detectors. One made out of Pt/Pt—10% Rh thermocouples to measure temperature below 1573 K, and the other made of Pt—30% Rh/Pt—6% Rh thermocouples to measure temperature above 1573 K. These thermocouples were arranged in such a way that the two sets of junctions form two rings each supporting a crucible. The crucibles were either half filled with reference material (alumina powder) or kept empty. The crucibles were positioned in a constant temperature zone of a furnace fitted with graphite heating elements. When a physicochemical change occurred in the sample crucible, the associated thermal effect produced a difference in temperature between the sample and reference crucibles. This resulted in a heat flow between them. The integrated output of the thermopile measured this thermal effect. A data acquisition system was provided to measure and record the thermo-EMF values at constant time intervals. Computer-based data acquisition was enabled by using HP-34812A BenchLink data acquisition software (Version 1.0) through a multimeter (6½—digit multimeter model HP-34810A). Subsequent data evaluation was carried out by using Microcal Origin (Version 5.0) to generate $\Delta T-t$ curves and to calculate the area under the peak of $\Delta T-t$ curves.

In a typical experiment, pellets of reference, $\alpha$-alumina [15] and pellets of sample, EuB₆ (s) (4 mm diameter and 1–2 mm thickness) were dropped alternatively from the specimen holder (maintained at the ambient temperature), into the sample crucible (maintained at experimental temperature ‘$T$’). From the relative heat flow signals corresponding to the reference ($\alpha$-Al₂O₃), sample (EuB₆) and the known enthalpy increment data of the standard, the enthalpy increments of EuB₆ (s) were derived. Enthalpy increment values of $\alpha$-alumina were taken from Ref. [16]. The mean of the five heat flow values for the reference and that of the sample was used to compute the enthalpy increment at one particular temperature. The enthalpy increment values thus obtained at different temperatures were fitted by the weighted least-squares method to obtain a nonlinear expression in temperature.

The calorimeter was standardized by carrying out enthalpy increment experiments on ThO₂ (s). The results were compared with the assessed data as reported by Godfrey et al. [17]. The overall error in these measurements was ascertained to be ± 2.5%. The temperature of the sample was measured with an accuracy of ± 0.5 K.

### Enthalpy increments by drop calorimeter

The enthalpy increment values measured in the temperature range of 765–1549 K were fitted into a polynomial expression in temperature by using the least-squares method and the resulting expression is given below.

$$
\begin{align}
(H_{\mathrm{T}}^{\circ}-H_{298}^{\circ})/\mathrm{J\ mol^{-1}} &= (124.236\ T + 29.713 \times 10^{-3}T^{2} \
&\quad + 40.201 \times 10^{5}T^{-1} - 53166) \
&(298 - 1600\ \mathrm{K})
\end{align}
\tag{12}
$$

The constraints used for fitting were: (a) $H_{\mathrm{T}}^{\circ}-H_{298\mathrm{K}}^{\circ}=0$ at 298 K and (b) the derivative of the function at 298 K is equal to value of $C_{\mathrm{p}}$ at 298 K ($\mathrm{EuB}_{6}=96.73\ \mathrm{J\ K^{-1}\ mol^{-1}}$) as reported in Ref. [22]. The standard error in the experimental data and the enthalpy values from the fit equation was computed using the following expression.

$$
\text{Standard error} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (\text{Residual})^{2}}{(\text{No. of observations} - \text{No. of coefficients})}}
\tag{13}
$$

The standard error in the fit was 0.3%, and the standard deviation of the fit was estimated to be $\pm\ 407\ \mathrm{J\ mol^{-1}}$. The $S_{298}^{\circ}$ value needed for the computation of the entropy and Gibbs energy functions was obtained from Ref. [22]. The experimentally measured enthalpy increment and the data obtained from the linear fit are given in Table 4.

### Third-law analysis of the EMF data

In order to assess the temperature dependency in the values of the thermodynamic quantities derived in this study, a 'third-law' analysis [20] was carried out. This method verifies if the values of $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H_{298}^{\circ}$ derived from the experimental data show any temperature dependence. This analysis was carried out by using the Gibbs energy functions (fefs) cited in the literature. These (fefs) are auxiliary functions derived from independent experimental determinations. In this study, the fefs pertaining to various elements involved in the equilibrium reactions were obtained from different standard compilations [23, 24].

In order to determine the value of $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H_{298}^{\circ}\left\langle\mathrm{EuB}_{6}\right\rangle$ from the data, the change in Gibbs energy functions ($\Delta$fefs) of EuB₆ (s) was computed by combining Gibbs energy functions of EuB₆ (s) (derived from drop calorimetry data) with the Gibbs energy functions of 'Eu' and 'B' obtained from Ref. [24]. The following reaction (15) was considered in the computation of change in Gibbs energy functions ($\Delta$fefs).

$$
\mathrm{Eu}(\mathrm{s})+6 \mathrm{~B}(\mathrm{~s}) \rightarrow \mathrm{EuB}_{6}(\mathrm{~s})
\tag{15}
$$

The values of $\Delta_{\mathrm{r}} G_{(4)}^{\circ}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{r}} G_{(9)}^{\circ}$ were computed for each experimental temperature using EMF data listed in Tables 2 and 3. These data were combined with the corresponding values of $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} G^{\circ}\langle\mathrm{NiO}\rangle$ ($\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} G^{\circ}(\mathrm{FeO})$) and $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} G^{\circ}\left\langle\mathrm{Eu}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}\right\rangle$ in order to derive $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} G^{\circ}\left\langle\mathrm{EuB}_{6}\right\rangle$ from cells I and II. \tag{16}
$$

## Conclusions
The standard Gibbs energy of formation of EuB₆ (s) was determined by using an EMF method that employed yttria-stabilized zirconia as the oxide solid electrolyte. Enthalpy increments of EuB₆ (s) were calculated by using inverse drop calorimetry over the temperature range 765–1549 K. By using the derived values of the enthalpy increments, other thermodynamic functions, viz., heat capacity, entropy and the Gibbs energy functions, have been derived in the temperature range 298–1800 K. Two independent EMF measurements (cell I and cell II) carried out in this study yielded $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_{298}^{\circ}$ values of $-173\pm3$ and $-180\pm3$ kJ mol⁻¹, respectively. These values agree with those cited in the literature as well.
