# THE METHANE EQUILIBRIUM IV

BY R. C. CANTELO

The data given in the third paper¹ of this series permits the calculation of the free energy decrease attending the synthesis of methane from amorphous carbon and hydrogen.

The free energy expression for the Second Law of Thermodynamics is given by

$$
\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{-\Delta \mathrm{F}}{\mathrm{T}}\right)=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{H}}{\mathrm{T}^{2}} \cdot \mathrm{dT} \text{ and } \Delta \mathrm{H},
$$

the increase in heat content may be expressed in terms of the heat capacities of the substances involved.

If $\Delta \mathrm{C}$ represents the difference in the heat capacities of the products and the reactants, the equation becomes

$$
-\frac{\Delta \mathrm{F}}{\mathrm{T}}=\int \frac{\int \Delta \mathrm{c} \cdot \mathrm{dT}+\Delta \mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{o}}}{\mathrm{T}^{2}} \cdot \mathrm{dT}+\mathrm{I}
$$

where $\Delta \mathrm{H}_{\circ}$ and I are constants of integration.

$\Delta \mathrm{H}_{\circ}$ may be calculated from the fact that for the reaction
$\mathrm{C \ (amorph) \ + \ 2H_2 \rightleftarrows CH_4; \ - \Delta H_{293} \ = \ 21,730 \ calories}$

If $\Delta \mathrm{C}$ be expressed in series form,
$\Delta \mathrm{C} \ = \ \Delta \mathrm{C}_{\circ} \ + \ \alpha \mathrm{T} \ + \ \beta \mathrm{T}^{2} \ + \ ---$
and the free energy expression simplifies to

$$
\frac{-\Delta \mathrm{F}}{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{-\Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}}{\mathrm{T}}+\Delta \mathrm{C}_{\circ} \ln \mathrm{T}+\frac{\alpha}{2} \mathrm{~T}+\frac{\beta}{6} \mathrm{~T}^{2}+\mathrm{I}
$$

or

$$
-\Delta \mathrm{F}=-\Delta \mathrm{H}_{\circ}+\Delta \mathrm{C}_{\circ} \mathrm{T} \ln \mathrm{T}+\frac{\alpha}{2} \mathrm{~T}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{6} \mathrm{~T}^{3}+\mathrm{I} \mathrm{T}
$$

Dixon, Campbell, and Parker² determined the $\mathrm{C_{p}^{T}/T}$ curve for methane and gave the linear equation

$$
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{p}}^{\mathrm{T}}=3.47+0.019 \mathrm{~T}
$$

to represent their results. This equation is used in the calculations given below.

For hydrogen³

$$
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{p}}^{\text {O.T }}=6.52+0.00044 \mathrm{~T} \text {. }
$$

For carbon⁴

$$
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{p}}^{\cdot \mathrm{O} \cdot \mathrm{T}}=\mathrm{I} . \mathrm{I}+0.0024 \mathrm{~T}+4.0 \cdot 10^{-7} \mathrm{~T}^{2}
$$

This latter equation gives the value 2.4 for the atomic heat of carbon at ordinary temperatures. The atomic heat of amorphous carbon at ordinary temperatures is 2.6.

These values give

$$
\begin{align*}
\Delta \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{o}} &= -10.67 \\
\alpha &= +0\ 01572 \\
\beta &= +4.0\ .\ 10^{-7}
\end{align*}
$$

The free energy equation thus becomes

$$-\Delta \mathrm{F}=19,280-10.67 \mathrm{~T} \ln \mathrm{T}+0.000786 \mathrm{~T}^{2}+0.6 .10^{-7} \mathrm{~T}^{3}+\mathrm{I} \mathrm{T}.$$

The value of I, the constant of integration, may be determined from the values of $-\Delta \mathrm{F}$ calculated from the determined equilibrium constants. For this calculation, the values obtained by the writer with ethylene as the initial system were used. This gave an average value of 42.2. The "constant" varied between $+41.3$ and $+43.3$.

$$-\Delta \mathrm{F}=19,280-10.67 \mathrm{~T} \ln \mathrm{T}+0.0786 \mathrm{~T}^{2}+0.0000006 \mathrm{~T}^{3}+42.2 \mathrm{~T}$$

which gives for $\mathrm{T}=298$ that

$$-\Delta \mathrm{F}_{298}=14,500 \text { calories for the reaction } \mathrm{C} \text { (amorph) }+2 \mathrm{H}_{2} \rightleftarrows \mathrm{CH}_{4}.$$

Lewis and Randall $^{1}$ have calculated $-\Delta \mathrm{F}_{298}$ for the reaction
$\mathrm{C}$ (graph) $+2 \mathrm{H}_{2} \rightleftarrows \mathrm{CH}_{4} ;-\Delta \mathrm{F}_{298}=12,800$ calories and $\mathrm{C}$ (amorph) +
$2 \mathrm{H}_{2} \rightleftarrows \mathrm{CH}_{4} ;-\Delta \mathrm{F}_{298}=14,500$ calories from which for the reaction
$\mathrm{C}$ (amorph) $\longrightarrow \mathrm{C}$ (graph); $-\Delta \mathrm{F}_{298}=1,700$ calories.

### Summary

The free energy decrease attending the synthesis of methane from amor- phous carbon and hydrogen has been calculated and found to equal 14,500 calories.
