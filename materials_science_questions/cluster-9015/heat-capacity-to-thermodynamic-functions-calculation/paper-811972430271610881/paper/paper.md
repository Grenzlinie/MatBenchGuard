**THERMODYNAMIC PROPERTIES OF GADOLINIUM GERMANIDES AT 298.15-2155 K**

N. P. Gorbachuk and A. S. Bolgar

UDC 546.661'289:536.63'722

Enthalpies of the germanides $Gd_5Ge_3$, GdGe, and $GdGe_{1.5}$ were measured in the range 298.15-2155 K for the first time by the method of mixing calorimetry. The temperatures, enthalpies, and entropies of melting of the compounds were determined, and also those of the polymorphic transformations in $GeGd_{1.5}$. Equations for the temperature dependence of the basic thermodynamic functions over a wide temperature range were determined and recommended for practical use.

Thanks to their physico-chemical properties the germanides of gadolinium, as well as those of other rare earth metals (REM), are considered as promising materials for semiconductor and optical applications [1]. In order to determine the optimal conditions for preparation and use of these materials it is necessary to know their thermodynamic properties over a wide temperature range. In addition to their practical value, thermodynamic properties are of theoretical interest inasmuch as they reflect the fundamental nature of atomic interactions in, and crystal structure of, a material. Data on the thermodynamic properties of the given materials is very limited. The enthalpies (kJ·mole⁻¹) and entropies (J·mole⁻¹·K⁻¹) of formation, determined in [2] by measuring the EMF of concentration galvanic cells at 760-910 K, are equal, respectively, to $-112.8 \pm 7.0$ and $19.0 \pm 12.9$ ($Gd_5Ge_3$), $-105.4 \pm 5.0$ and $17.2 \pm 8.5$ (GdGe), $-87.7 \pm 3.6$ and $10.5 \pm 5.2$ ($GdGe_{1.5}$). Calorimetric measurements of the standard enthalpies of formation (kJ·g-atom⁻¹) carried out in [3] yielded $-83.1 \pm 3.4$ ($Gd_5Ge_3$), $-79.6 \pm 3.5$ (GdGe), $-70.7 \pm 4.1$ ($GdGe_{1.5}$). According to [4] the entropy of $Gd_5Ge_3$ at 298.15 K is 53.61 J·g-atom⁻¹·K⁻¹.

The objective of the present work was to measure the enthalpies of $Gd_5Ge_3$, GdGe, and $GdGe_{1.5}$ in the range 300-2200 K, and determine the temperature dependence of the basic thermodynamic functions of these compounds.

The experimental specimens were prepared by arc-melting stoichiometric mixtures of grades GdM-1 and GÉS5G5, 99.80 and 99.98 mass % pure, respectively, in purified argon. The synthesized materials were annealed at 1200 K for 50 h in order to remove internal stresses and homogenize. According to the results of x-ray diffraction and chemical analyses* (Table 1) the specimens were single-phase with lattice constants in satisfactory agreement with those given in [5].

Enthalpies were measured in the range 300-1200 K by the method of mixtures in a Seteram high-temperature differential calorimeter, and above 1200 K in a high-temperature evacuated calorimeter using a tantalum ampules [6]. Calibration of the equipment using the standard specimens SOTS-1a ($\alpha$-$Al_2O_3$) and SOTS-3 (Mo) indicated that the enthalpy measurement error did not exceed 1.5%.

Experimental enthalpy values (Table 2) obtained in both units agreed within the measurement error in overlapping temperature ranges. Small jumps in the enthalpy of $GdGe_{1.5}$ were observed in the ranges 1110-1118 and 1436-1448 K, due to two polymorphic transformations according to [5]. The average temperatures in these intervals were $1114 \pm 10$ and $1442 \pm 15$ K which, allowing for experimental error, are in good agreement with the data in [5]. At higher temperatures jumps were observed in the enthalpies of all three germanides as a result of melting. As seen in Table 2, the enthalpies of the compounds vary monotonically below the temperatures designated by (*) and above those designated by (**). In our opinion the materials are in the solid and liquid states, respectively, in these temperature ranges.

A linear equation was taken to represent the data for the liquid state:

$$
H^{0}(T)-H^{0}(298.15 \mathrm{~K})=a T+b. \tag{2}
$$

The coefficients of equation (1) were calculated by the least squares method according to the procedure in [7] using two boundary conditions: enthalpy at 298.15 K equals to 0; standard heat capacities equal to $213.50 \pm 0.86; 48.87 \pm 0.20$, and $61.90 \pm 0.15 \mathrm{~J} \cdot \mathrm{K}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{mole}^{-1}$, respectively, for Gd₅Ge₃, GdGe, and GdGe₁.₅. These values were determined by us earlier at low temperatures using the adiabatic method. The entropies at 298.15 K were taken as $443.8 . \pm 3.6; 89.9 \pm 0.8$, and $98.3 \pm$ $\pm 0.8 \mathrm{~J} \cdot \mathrm{K}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{mole}^{-1}$, respectively. The parameters of equation (1) for the β- and γ-modifications of GdGe₁.₅, and those of equation (2) for all three germanides, were determined using only the first boundary condition.

At 0.95 probability the average relative confidence limits of the enthalpy values given by equations (1) and (2), respectively, do not exceed 0.83 and $1.5 \%$ (Gd₅Ge₃), 0.84 and $1.5 \%$ (GdGe), 0.95 (α), 1.30 (β), 2.0 (γ) and $1.73 \%$ (GdGe₁.₅).

The temperature dependencies of heat capacity, entropy, and reduced Gibbs energy from (1) have the forms:

$$
C_{\mathrm{p}}^{0}(T)=2 A T+B-C T^{-2}, \tag{3}
$$

$$
S^{0}(T)=2 A T+B \ln T+0.5 C T^{-2}+E, \tag{4}
$$

$$
\Phi^{1}(T)=A T+B \ln T-D T^{-1}-0.5 C T^{-2}+(E-B). \tag{5}
$$

The coefficients of equations (1)-(5) are given in Table 3. The melting points of the germanides (Table 4), taken as the averages of the temperatures indicated by (*) and those following in Table 2, are in good agreement with the data of [5], allowing for experimental error. The error in melting point is determined by the difference between it and the preceding temperature (*), and also by the temperature measurement error itself (0.8%). The enthalpy and entropy of polymorphic transformations in the sesquigermanide, and of melting in all germanides (Table 4), was obtained from equations (1) and (2) by extrapolation to the corresponding temperatures.

The monotonic variation with temperature of the heat capacities obtained from (3) is explained, in all probability, by the absence of noticeable contributions from the formation of thermal vacancies. Comparison of the heat capacities of gadolinium germanides with those of the corresponding gadolinium silicides [6] and lanthanum germanides [8] indicates that they differ by no more than 3-4% at average and high temperatures, which is comparable to the error in calculation of heat capacity itself.

At high temperatures, where the phonon component of heat capacity reaches its ultimate value, it is probable that the differences in isobaric heat capacity between corresponding compounds are due to electronic and anharmonic contributions. It should be noted that the heat capacities of $Gd_5Ge_3$ and $GdGe_{1.5}$ increase more than 50% upon melting, and that of GdGe more than 30%. This may indicate that short-range order in the crystal lattices of gadolinium germanides changes drastically when they are melted.
