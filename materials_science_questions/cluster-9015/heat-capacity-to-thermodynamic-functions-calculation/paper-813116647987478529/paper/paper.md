# ISOTHERMS AND THERMODYNAMICAL FUNCTIONS OF THREE HYDROGEN-NITROGEN- AMMONIA MIXTURES BETWEEN $0^{\circ}$ AND $150^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ AND PRESSURES UP TO 300 ATMOSPHERES

by A. MICHELS, T. WASSENAAR, G. J. WOLKERS, W. DE GRAAFF and P. LOUWERSE

109th publication of the Van der Waals Fund, Van der Waals Laboratory, Gemeente Universiteit, Amsterdam

## Summary
Compressibility isotherms of three hydrogen-nitrogen-ammonia mixtures have been measured between $0^{\circ}$ and $150^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ up to densities of 180 Amagat (pressures up to 300 atmospheres). From these data thermodynamical functions have been calculated; they are given as function of temperature and pressure.

$\S 1$. Introduction. As a continuation of earlier measurements $^{1}$ ) on the compressibilities of gas mixtures of technical importance the system hydrogen-nitrogen-ammonia has been investigated by a technique described previously $^{2}$ ). Three mixtures were prepared with 7,12 and 17 mole $\%$ ammonia respectively. The exact compositions were found by analysis, they are given in table I. The percentage of ammonia was determined from the pressure drop when the gas was brought in contact with concentrated sulfuric acid, the hydrogen percentage in the remaining gas from the pressure decrease after the gas was led over hot cupric oxide.

THERMODYNAMICS OF $H_2$-$N_2$-$NH_3$ MIXTURES

§ 2. Results. For each mixture two series of measurements were carried out with different piezometers; the normal volume of each filling is given in the first table. The normal volumes of the first series were determined by direct measurement, those of the second from the overlap with the first series.

No irregularities by adsorption greater than one part in $10^4$ were met with the $7\%$ mixture. However, at higher ammonia concen- tration difficulties were encountered. If the total amount of gas in the second piezometer was calculated from the isotherm measure- ments at $150^\circ$C and of those at $50^\circ$C, the first result gave a slightly greater quantity. This phenomenon can be explained by the desorp- tion of the ammonia from the glass walls at higher temperatures. How serious the discrepancy can be, depends on the ratio of the total quantity of the gas and the glass surface exposed. For the $12\%$ mixture it was of the order of $0.02\%$ and for the $17\%$ mixture $0.06\%$. Assuming a degree of adsorption it was possible to make a fair estimate of the correction to be applied to the experimental data.

The experimental $PV$-values corrected in this way are given in table II, where pressures are given in international atmospheres and densities and volumes in Amagat units. The conversion factors into other units are given in table I.

A power series with four coefficients $PV = A + Bd + Cd^2 + Zd^3$ was fitted to the experimental points over the whole density region. The coefficients of this expansion are given in table III.

§ 3. Thermodynamical functions. Thermodynamical functions were calculated from the $P$-$V$-$T$-data, following the same procedure as described in a previous publication $^3$). The specific heat data at zero pressure for each of the components were taken from the litera- ture $^4$).

Values are given for $PV$, the entropy $S$, the enthalpy $H$, the energy $U = H - PV$ and the specific heat at constant pressure $-C_P$ as a function of temperature and pressure. The accuracy of these figures is estimated to be better than $1\%$.
