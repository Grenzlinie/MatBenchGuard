# Heat capacity of aluminum silicon carbide $(Al_{4}SiC_{4})$ from 5.26 to 1047 K

R. P. BEYER and E. A. JOHNSON

Thermodynamics Laboratory, Albany Research Center,
Bureau of Mines, U.S. Department of the Interior,
P.O. Box 70, Albany, Oregon 97321, U.S.A.

(Received 10 April 1984)

The molar heat capacity of aluminum silicon carbide $(Al_{4}SiC_{4})$ was measured at the Bureau of Mines from 5.26 to 301.5 K by adiabatic calorimetry and from 347 to 1047 K by differential scanning calorimetry. The values at $T=298.15$ K for $C_{p,m}^{\circ}$, $\{S_{m}^{\circ}(T)-S_{m}^{\circ}(0)\}$, $-\{G_{m}^{\circ}(T)-H_{m}^{\circ}(0)\}/T$, and $\{H_{m}^{\circ}(T)-H_{m}^{\circ}(0)\}$ are 145.07, 107.62, $40.66\ \text{J·K}^{-1}·\text{mol}^{-1}$, and $19.96\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$, respectively. These properties are also reported from 5 to 2000 K at convenient temperature intervals. The standard molar enthalpy of formation $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_{\text{m}}^{\circ}(\text{Al}_{4}\text{SiC}_{4})$, calculated from Behrens and Rinehart's Knudsen-effusion mass-spectrometric results, is $-(203.3\pm4.9)\ \text{kJ·mol}^{-1}$ at 298.15 K.

## 1. Introduction
One of the goals of the Bureau of Mines is to provide thermodynamic quantites for minerals and related inorganic compounds. As part of this effort, the molar heat capacity of aluminum silicon carbide, $Al_{4}SiC_{4}$, was measured from 5.26 to 301.5 K by adiabatic calorimetry and from 347 to 1047 K by differential scanning calorimetry. No reports of measurements on this compound over this temperature range were found in the literature. These heat-capacity measurements were also done to support another effort by Bureau scientists to determine the (aluminum + silicon + carbon) phase diagram.

## 2. Experimental
$Al_{4}SiC_{4}$ was prepared by a method modified from Schreider *et al.*$^{(1)}$ Stoichiometric amounts of the powdered carbides, $Al_{4}C_{3}$ and SiC, were ball-milled in an argon atmosphere for 20 h. The powders were handled in the inert atmosphere at all times to avoid hydrolysis and oxidation. The powder mixture was placed in a graphite die and loaded into a vacuum hot press induction furnace. The furnace chamber was evacuated and backfilled to 50 kPa with argon. The sample was then hot-pressed for 2 h at 2130 K and 6.89 MPa. The sample was then ground to a fine powder with a boron-carbide mortar and pestle. X-ray powder-diffraction analysis showed only

single-phase $Al_4SiC_4$ and was the same as that reported by Barczak$^{(2)}$ and Inoue et al.$^{(3)}$ Optical-emission spectrographic analysis showed the following mass percentages of impurities: B, $<0.3$; Co, $<0.03$; Cr, $<0.01$; Cu, $<0.01$; Fe, $<0.03$; Ni, $0.03$; Ti, $<0.001$; V, $<0.003$.

The automated adiabatic calorimeter used to measure the heat capacities has been described previously by Beyer et al.$^{(4)}$ A sample of 99.8413 g of $Al_4SiC_4$ was loaded into the calorimeter along with $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ mol of He. A molar mass of $184.056\ \text{g}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ was used for $Al_4SiC_4$.

A model TA2000C simultaneous differential scanning calorimeter (d.s.c.) and thermogravimetric analyzer manufactured by Mettler Instrument Corporation$\dagger$ was used. This instrument is a heat-flow type d.s.c. and has a sensitivity of approximately $1.0\ \mu\text{V}\cdot\text{mW}^{-1}$ and a nominal accuracy of $\pm 5$ per cent. A sample of 0.08800 g of $Al_4SiC_4$ was loaded into a 0.47041 g platinum capsule, and both the full capsule and an empty reference capsule were placed in the d.s.c. The d.s.c. chamber was evacuated, filled with argon, and operated at a scan rate of $0.167\ \text{K}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ in 50 K steps from 322 to 1072 K. Three separate determinations were made with the single capsule empty, filled with $Al_4SiC_4$, and filled with sapphire. The results were analyzed according to the "enthalpy method", as described by Mraw and Naas.$^{(5)}$

### 3. Results

The experimental molar heat capacities are listed chronologically in table 1. The temperature increment for each measurement can be calculated from the temperatures tabulated for adjacent measurements. The overall uncertainty of the measurements with the adiabatic calorimeter was estimated to be ±2 per cent from 5 to 15 K; ±1 per cent from 15 to 50 K; ±0.2 per cent from 50 to 200 K; and ±0.1 per cent from 200 to 300 K. The uncertainties of the d.s.c. measurements were determined by measuring the heat capacity of MgO and comparing the measured values with accepted values from Pankratz.⁽⁶⁾ The uncertainties were found to be ±3 per cent from 298 to 700 K, and ±1 per cent from 700 to 1473 K.

The molar heat capacities from 5.26 to 301.5 K and from 347 to 1047 K were smoothed using a curve-fitting routine developed by Justice⁽⁷⁾ to give the values of $C_{p,\,\text{m}}^{\circ}$, $\{S_{\text{m}}^{\circ}(T)-S_{\text{m}}^{\circ}(0)\}$, $-\{G_{\text{m}}^{\circ}(T)-H_{\text{m}}^{\circ}(0)\}/T$, and $\{H_{\text{m}}^{\circ}(T)-H_{\text{m}}^{\circ}(0)\}$ listed in table 2. ### 4. Discussion

The thermodynamic properties given in table 2 can be combined with the Knudsen-effusion mass-spectrometric results of Behrens and Rinehart(¹²) to deduce the

standard molar enthalpy of formation $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H_{\mathrm{m}}^{\circ}(\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{SiC}_{4})$ at 298.15 K. Behrens and Rinehart report two sets of measurements for the reaction:

$$\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{SiC}_{4}(\mathrm{cr})=4 \mathrm{Al}(\mathrm{g})+\mathrm{SiC}(\mathrm{cr}, \text { hexagonal })+3 \mathrm{C}(\mathrm{cr}, \text { graphite). } \tag{1}$$

One set covered the range 1427 to 1784 K and the other set covered the range 1430 to 1772 K. Both sets were critically evaluated using second- and third-law methods. The second-law method involved calculating a least-squares fit of the measured vapor pressures to give $\Delta_{\mathrm{r}} H_{\mathrm{m}}^{\circ}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{r}} S_{\mathrm{m}}^{\circ}$ at the mean temperature of the measurements. These values were then combined with auxiliary data to give $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H_{\mathrm{m}}^{\circ}$ values at 298.15 K of $-(194.2 \pm 6.7)$ and $-(211.3 \pm 6.7) \mathrm{kJ} \cdot \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$. There was no evident reason for the difference in these two values.

The third-law method involved calculating $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H_{\mathrm{m}}^{\circ}$ at 298.15 K for each point and averaging the results. These calculations gave third-law values for $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H_{\mathrm{m}}^{\circ}$ at 298.15 K of $-(203.2 \pm 4.1)$ and $-(203.4 \pm 2.7) \mathrm{kJ} \cdot \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$. Therefore, the recommended value for the molar enthalpy of formation at 298.15 K is

$$\Delta_{\mathrm{f}} H_{\mathrm{m}}^{\circ}(\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{SiC}_{4})=-(203.3 \pm 4.9) \mathrm{kJ} \cdot \mathrm{mol}^{-1}. \tag{2}$$

Values of the molar enthalpies of formation of $\mathrm{Al}_{4} \mathrm{C}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{SiC}$ are from Wagman et al., $^{(13)}$ and for $\mathrm{Al}(\mathrm{g})$ from Pankratz. $^{(6)}$ Auxiliary results for $\mathrm{C}($ graphite) and $\mathrm{Al}(\mathrm{cr})$ are also from Pankratz. $^{(6)}$
