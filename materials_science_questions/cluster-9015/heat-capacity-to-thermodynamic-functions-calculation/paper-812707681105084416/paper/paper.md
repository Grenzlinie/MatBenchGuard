# On thermodynamic equilibria of solid BN and gas phases in the B-N-H-Cl-He system

A.N. Golubenko $^{a}$, M.L. Kosinova $^{b, *}$, V.A. Titov $^{b}$, A.A. Titov $^{b}$, F.A. Kuznetsov $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Novosibirsk State University, Novosibirsk 630090, Russia
$^{b}$ Institute of Inorganic Chemistry, Russian Academy of Sciences, Siberian Branch, Epitaxial Layers Laboratory, Lavrent'ev Avenue 3, Novosibirsk 630090, Russia

Received 21 August 1995; accepted 25 June 1996

## Abstract

Thermodynamic analysis of the chemical vapour deposition (CVD) of boron nitride was performed for the B-N-H-Cl-He system, with new thermodynamic data for different modifications of boron nitride (hexagonal h-BN, cubic c-BN and wurtzite w-BN). The phase equilibria were calculated for the temperature region 673-2273 K, at total pressures of $1.013×10^{3}$ and $1.013×10^{5}$ Pa and for a wide range of atomic ratios of elements in the system. The results are presented in the form of CVD phase diagrams which are a good way of illustrating the influence of the system parameters on the formation of stable phases. c-BN is formed in a quasi-equilibrium process at temperatures below 1804 K, the h-BN modification is most stable above this temperature, and w-BN is a metastable phase for all possible variations of the process conditions.

Keywords: Boron nitride; Chemical vapour deposition (CVD); Computer simulation

---

## 1. Introduction

Chemical vapour deposition (CVD) of boron nitride is a promising process for different applications [1]. In the literature there are a number of publications dealing with thermodynamic simulations of deposition in boron-nitrogen containing systems: B-N-Cl-H [2], B-N-F-H, B-N-H-F-Si-C-O [3], B-N-O-C-Cl-H [4], B-N-Si-H-Cl [5]. It should be noted that in these studies the graphite-like hexagonal BN (h-BN) is considered as the only thermodynamically stable condensed phase at $T=298.15$ K and $P=1.013×10^{5}$ Pa. All other modifications of boron nitride such as cubic BN (c-BN) and wurtzite type BN (w-BN) are assumed to be stable only at high temperatures and pressures. Recently, new thermodynamic data [6-16] [17-20] have been obtained experimentally. It follows from these data that the relative stabilities of various modifications of BN differ from what was previously accepted. The main conclusion is that the cubic modification of BN is the most thermodynamically stable phase at low temperatures (below 1273 K [21], 1570 K [22]). This conclusion is supported by the fact that c-BN films were prepared by low pressure CVD processes in the B-N-H-Ar system at 673 K [23], in the B-N-H-F system at 948 K [24], and in the B-N-H-Cl-Ar system [25].

The aim of the present study is to perform thermodynamic modelling of the chemical vapour deposition of boron nitride in the B-N-H-Cl-He system, taking into account the new thermodynamic data for different modifications of BN.

## 2. The approach to simulation and thermodynamic data of compounds

The main aim of the simulation is to outline regions of experimental conditions corresponding to the deposition of different combinations of condensed phases in quasi-equilibrium processes in the B-N-H-Cl-He system. To elucidate the phase equilibria in the B-N-H-Cl-He system, the molecular compositions of gas phases in equilibrium with possible condensed phases have been calculated for a wide range of system parameters ($T$, $P$ and the input atomic ratio of components). In the calculations all condensed phases in the system were assumed to be phases of fixed composition.

The calculation of the equilibrium is based on minimization of the ratio of the total Gibbs free energy of the open system to the amount of the chosen non-depositing elements (for example, H, Cl or inert carrier gas) [26]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi\left[\frac{n_{k}}{n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}}, p_{i}\right] & =\frac{\Delta G}{n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}} \\
& =\sum_{k=1}^{L_{k}} \frac{n_{k}}{n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}} \Delta G_{k}^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{L_{i}} \frac{n_{i}}{n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}} \Delta G_{i}^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{L_{i}} \frac{p_{i}}{p_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}} R T \ln p_{i} \quad(1)
\end{aligned}
$$

The equations of conservation conditions are:
$P^{0}=\sum p_{i}$ Dalton's Law

and known relationships connecting the independent components of the system
$$
\frac{n_{j}^{*}}{n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}}=\sum_{K}^{L_{k}} \alpha_{k j} \frac{n_{k}}{n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}}+\sum_{i}^{L_{l}} \alpha_{i j} \frac{p_{i}}{p_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}}
$$

where $n_{k}$ and $n_{i}$ are the numbers of moles of the condensed phase and the gaseous molecular species, $n_{j}^{*}$ are the analytical numbers of moles of independent components in the system, $n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}$ is the analytical number of moles of one of the non-depositing elements
$$
p_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}=n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*} \frac{R T}{V}=\sum_{i}^{L_{i}} \alpha_{i \mathrm{H}} p_{i}
$$

$p_{i}$ are the partial pressures of gaseous species, $P^{0}$ is the total pressure, $V$ is the volume of the gas phase, $T$ is the temperature, $L_{k}$ is the amount of condensed phase in equilibrium, $L_{l}$ is the amount of gaseous species, $\alpha_{k j}$ and $\alpha_{i j}$ are stoichiometric coefficients with which each $j$-component is included in the composition of $k$-condensed or $i$-gaseous compounds, and $\Delta G_{k}^{0}$ and $\Delta G_{i}^{0}$ are the molar standard free energies of condensed and gaseous compounds respectively.

When boron nitride is deposited, one of Eq. (2) disappears to suit the Gibbs phase rule requirements. In this case, the relation $(n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*}-n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*})/n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}$ is an invariant of the system. This value does not depend on the quantity of deposited BN.

The equilibrium calculations were performed for total pressures of $1.013 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~Pa}$ and $1.013 \times 10^{3} \mathrm{~Pa}$ and for the temperature interval 673-2273 K. The input atomic ratios of elements were varied over a wide range. The following were taken into account: condensed phases h-BN, c-BN, w-BN, B; gaseous species $\mathrm{H}, \mathrm{H}_{2}, \mathrm{NH}, \mathrm{NH}_{2}, \mathrm{NH}_{3}, \mathrm{~N}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{2}, \mathrm{~N}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{4}, \mathrm{~N}, \mathrm{~N}_{2}$, $\mathrm{BH}, \mathrm{BH}_{2}, \mathrm{BH}_{3}, \mathrm{~B}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{6}, \mathrm{~B}_{3} \mathrm{~N}_{3} \mathrm{H}_{6}, \mathrm{BH}_{3}^{*} \mathrm{NH}_{3}, \mathrm{~B}, \mathrm{~B}_{2}, \mathrm{BN}, \mathrm{BCl}$, $\mathrm{BCl}_{2}, \mathrm{BCl}_{3}, \mathrm{~B}_{2} \mathrm{Cl}_{4}, \mathrm{BHCI}, \mathrm{BHCI}_{2}, \mathrm{BH}_{2} \mathrm{Cl}, \mathrm{Cl}_{2}, \mathrm{Cl}, \mathrm{HCl}, \mathrm{He}$.

Standard thermodynamic characteristics of individual substances $\Delta_{f} H_{298}{ }^{\circ}, S_{298}^{0}, C_{p}^{0}=f(T)$ were used as the initial thermodynamic information. Data for all compounds except modifications of boron nitride were taken from [27]. Estimation of data for the modifications of boron nitride is based on experimental results of fluorine calorimetry experiments. The values of $\Delta_{f} H_{298}{ }^{\circ}$ for w-BN and c-BN were reported as $-263.2$ [6] and $-266.8 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$ [7] respectively. The value of standard enthalpy of h-BN formation obtained by the same authors [8] is $-250.6 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$ and agrees with the value recommended in [27].

### 3. Results of the equilibria calculations

In the present study of the CVD of BN in the B-N-H-Cl- He system the calculations were performed for total pressures $P$ of $1.013×10^{5}$ Pa and $1.013×10^{3}$ Pa, and temperature interval 673-2273 K. The relative initial concentrations of elements were varied in the ranges 0.1-10 for $n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$, 0.1-1.0 for $n_{\mathrm{Cl}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}$ and 0.1-60 for $n_{\mathrm{He}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$. The concentration of inert carrier gas He was determined from the Dalton low condition.

Description of the initial concentrations of elements in the form of atomic ratios permits the use of results obtained for various types of deposition process with different B- or N-containing precursors. For example, the initial atomic ratio $n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*}: n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}: n_{\mathrm{Cl}}^{*}: n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}: n_{\mathrm{He}}^{*}=1:1:3:3:10$ describes two different systems with molecular ratios of precursors used equal to $\mathrm{BCl}_{3}:\mathrm{NH}_{3}:\mathrm{He}=1:1:10$ and $\mathrm{B}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{3}\mathrm{H}_{3}\mathrm{Cl}_{3}:\mathrm{HCl}:\mathrm{He}=1:3:10$ in respective technological schemes.

The variety of conditions used in our calculations covers all possible combinations of experimental conditions in real CVD experiments.

The results of calculations are presented as sections of CVD phase diagrams (Figs. 1-6) for some fixed ratios of atomic concentrations of depositing $(n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*})$ and non-depositing $(n_{\mathrm{Cl}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}$ or $n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*})$ elements.

Figs. 1 and 3 and Fig. 5 present the CVD phase diagrams as a function of deposition temperature and the $n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$ ratio. Here values of $n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$ and $n_{\mathrm{Cl}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}$ are fixed.

Figs. 2 and 4 and Fig. 6 show the CVD diagrams for $n_{\mathrm{Cl}}^{*} /$ $n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}$ and temperature as two independent variables. Values of $n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$ and $n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$ are fixed in these cases.

The appearance of the phase diagram changes moderately with variation of the input $n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$ atomic ratio. The general feature of all CVD diagrams is that no wurtzite BN phase exists in the B-N-H-Cl-He system in the considered interval of conditions.

The effect of deviation from stoichiometry of the gas mix- ture with respect to concentrations of depositing elements $(n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}=1: 1)$ is asymmetrical. For $n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n^{*}=1: 1$ and excess nitrogen $(n_{\mathrm{B}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}=1: 10)$, the CVD phase diagrams are rela- tively simple and analogous. They consist of two single-phase fields, h-BN and c-BN, in equilibrium with the gas phase. Cubic BN should be formed for temperatures up to 1804 K. Hexagonal BN exists above this temperature. The h-BN field appears to be quite restricted. The BN deposition fields become narrower as the temperature and the initial atomic ratios $n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}$ and $n_{\mathrm{Cl}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*}$ increase. This effect is more pronounced at the lower total pressure, as can be seen in Fig. 1(b), Fig. 2(b), Fig. 3(b) and Fig. 4(b). As shown in Fig. 1(a) and (b) for $n_{\mathrm{H}}^{*} / n_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}=3$, h-BN forms in the deposition temperature regions $1804-2220$ K $(P=1.013 \times 10^{5}$ Pa) and $1804-1870$ K $(P=1.013 \times 10^{3}$ Pa).

## 4. Conclusion

The deposition of boron nitride in the B-N-H-Cl-He sys- tem was considered with the aim of outlining the conditions of formation of h-BN and c-BN films at $P \leq 1.0183×10^5$ Pa.

As can be concluded from the above described calculated results, the factors essentially affecting the phase composition of deposits in CVD processes in the B-N-H-Cl-He system are the ratios of concentrations of depositing elements in the input mixture, the deposition temperature and the value of total pressure. Excess boron can result in the appearance of multi-phase deposits. Thus, if the aim of the process is to obtain single-phase BN, the most favourable conditions cor- respond to a stoichiometric initial gas mixture or mixtures with excess nitrogen.

All the above considerations are valid, of course, for close to equilibrium state CVD processes. Using a thermodynamic approach to modelling the CVD processes, we would like to note that in some practical cases, for example at high super- saturations or for activated processes (plasma enhanced CVD), metastable or unstable phases can appear. The appear- ance of metastable phases can, possibly, be assisted by the presence of some impurities. We believe that this consider- ation can explain the contradiction of our conclusion with some experimental observations.
