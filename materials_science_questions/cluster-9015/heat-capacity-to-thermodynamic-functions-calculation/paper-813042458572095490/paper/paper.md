## Thermodynamic Functions of Yttrium Trifluoride and Its Dimer in the Gas Phase

E. L. Osina$^{a,*}$ and D. M. Kovtun$^{a,b}$

$^{a}$ Joint Institute for High Temperatures, Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, 125412 Russia
$^{b}$ Department of Chemistry, Moscow State University, Moscow, 119991 Russia
*e-mail: j-osina@yandex.ru
Received September 8, 2017

Abstract—New calculations of the functions for $YF_3$ and $Y_2F_6$ in the gas phase using quantum-chemical calculations by MP2 and CCSD(T) methods are performed in connection with the ongoing work on obtaining reliable thermodynamic data of yttrium halides. The obtained values are entered in the database of the IVTANTERMO software complex. Equations approximating the temperature dependence of the reduced Gibbs energy in the $T = 298.15–6000$ K range of temperatures are presented.

Keywords: yttrium trifluoride, yttrium trifluoride dimer, thermodynamic properties, MP2, CCSD(T), relativistic pseudopotentials for $d$-elements $YF_3$, $Y_2F_6$

DOI: 10.1134/S0036024418050242

## INTRODUCTION

For the thermodynamic modeling of high-temperature processes involving halogenides of rare-earth elements, we need reliable thermodynamic functions of the main constituent vapors: $MX_3$ molecules and their dimers $M_2X_6$ ($M =$ Sc, Y, La–Lu, $X =$ F–I) in the gas phase, which, according to mass spectrometric studies, are present in vapor over trihalides of rare-earth elements but in smaller amounts. The thermodynamic functions of $ScX_3$ and $LnX_3$ in the gas phase were recently calculated in [1, 2]. Data on the thermodynamic properties of yttrium trihalides were obtained long ago [3] using estimated values of their molecular constants, since there were no experimental values (except for $YCl_3$ [4]). The thermodynamic functions of the dimers of lanthanide and scandium trihalides in the gas phase were calculated in [2, 5]. There are no data in the literature on the thermodynamic functions of the dimeric molecules of yttrium trihalides.

We therefore need to calculate the thermodynamic functions of monomers and dimers of yttrium trihalides in the gas phase in light of all available experimental and theoretical results.

The aim of this work was to obtain a set of reliable data on the structural and energy properties of $YF_3$ and $Y_2F_6$ molecules in the gas phase, and to calculate thermodynamic functions based on these data.

## YTTRIUM TRIFLUORIDE $YF_3$

Yttrium trifluoride was studied via high-temperature gas electron diffraction [6], infrared spectroscopy using matrix isolation [7–10], and the deflection of molecular beams in an inhomogeneous electric field [11].

As with $ScF_3$ [12], the results from experimental studies of the structure of the $YF_3$ molecule are contradictory. The data on the molecular beam deflection of $YF_3$ in an inhomogeneous electric field [11] testify to the polarity of this molecule. Electron diffraction data on $YF_3$ [6] do not contradict the planar structure of this molecule. However, one band attributed to the fully symmetric valence vibration of $YF_3$ and indicating its nonplanar structure has been found in the IR spectra of $YF_3$ molecules [7, 9]. In [10], only one band ($561$ cm$^{-1}$) attributed to asymmetric valence vibrations was observed in the region of valence vibrational frequencies, and the planar structure of the molecule was assumed. The results from experimental studies thus do not allow us to draw a definitive conclusion about the structure of $YF_3$. The contradictions between experimental studies on the structure of $YF_3$ molecules were resolved by theoretical calculations [10, 13–15] and calculations performed in this work using the GAUSSIAN03 software package [16], which confirmed the results from electron diffraction and spectral studies [6, 10] and confirmed the planar structure of $YF_3$. The vibrational spectra of the $YF_{3}$ molecule have been studied only in inert gas matrices [7-10]. The authors of $[7,9]$ observed two bands at 561 and $567 ~cm^{-1}$ (Ar matrix) in the high-frequency region of the IR spectrum and assigned them to the frequencies of the asymmetric $v_{3}$ and symmetric $v_{1}$ vibrations of the $YF_{3}$ molecule. The authors of [9] noted that the band at $567 ~cm^{-1}$ could be the result of the band at $561 ~cm^{-1}$ splitting under the action of the matrix. Data from theoretical calculations in $[10,13-15]$ and this work indicate that the band at $567 ~cm^{-1}$ was erroneously assigned to symmetric vibrations. In all calculations, the value of the vibrational frequency $v_{1}$ was less than the $v_{3}$ value. The authors of $[10,13,14]$ considered the band at $567 ~cm^{-1}$ to be a result of matrix splitting. It should be also noted that the band at $567 ~cm^{-1}$ was observed in [9] only in the Ar matrix. In the Ne matrix [9], there was only one band in the region of the valence vibrational frequencies $(580 ~cm^{-1})$ .

The assignment of the bands in [8] by the authors of $[9,13]$ was recognized as erroneous. The band at $663 ~cm^{-1}$ assigned in [8] to valence frequency $v_{3}$ is the known frequency of the deformation vibration $v_{2}$ of the carbon dioxide molecule $CO_{2}$ formed upon interaction between oxygen-containing impurities in samples with graphite cells. The frequency of the nonplanar deformation at $119 ~cm^{-1}$ [8] coincided with the corresponding value for the $ScF_{3}$ molecule, which seems unlikely. A more reliable value of the frequency of nonplanar deformation $v_{2}(95 ~cm^{-1})$ was estimated in [9] on the basis of the corresponding experimental values for $ScF_{3}(119 ~cm^{-1})$ and $LaF_{3}(82 ~cm^{-1})$ molecules. ## YTTRIUM TRIFLUORIDE DIMER $Y_{2} ~F_{6}$ 
In the literature, there are no experimental data on the structure of the yttrium trifluoride dimer. The structure of $Y_{2} ~F_{6}$ was calculated in $[10,15]$ (DFT) and in this work (MP2(FC)/cc-pVTZ-PP). It was shown in all calculations that the $Y_{2} ~F_{6}$ molecule has the configuration of symmetry $D_{2 h}$ with a plane four-member F_b-Y-F_b-Y cycle. The product of the moments of inertia in Table 1 is calculated with the structural parameters we calculated: $r(Y-X_{t})=2.024(20) \AA$ , $r(Y-X_{b})=2.238(20) \AA, \angle X_{b}-Y-X_{b}=71.7^{\circ}(5.0)$ , $\angle X_{t}-Y-X_{t}=121.0^{\circ}(2.0)$ (where $X_{t}$ is the terminal halogen atom and $X_{b}$ is a halogen atom in the plane four member cycle). Their values with allowance for errors coincide with the data in $[10,15]$ .

Products of the interaction of laser-ablated yttrium atoms, with fluorine molecules in an Ar matrix, were studied in [10]. Bands at 579.8 and 554.7, and a weak band at $425 ~cm^{-1}$ assigned to the stretching $(v_{2}, v_{4})$ and bending $(v_{5})$ frequencies of $Y_{2} ~F_{6}$ were observed in the IR spectrum. The vibrational frequencies of $Y_{2} ~F_{6}$ were calculated in this work and in $[10,15]$ , and their values are in agreement. From the example of the $YF_{3}$ molecule, we were convinced that in contrast to the DFTcalculations in $[10,15]$ , calculations in the MP2(FC)/cc-pVTZ-PP approximation reproduce the experimental vibrational frequencies well, especially the low frequencies of nonplanar vibrations. In Table 1, the experimental values in [10] were thus taken for vibrational frequencies $v_{2}, v_{4}$ , and $v_{5}$ , while those obtained in our calculations were used for others. Theoretical frequency values $v_{2}, v_{4}$ , and $v_{5}$ in our calculations were 604,588, and $437 ~cm^{-1}$ .

The thermodynamic functions of $\text{YF}_3$ and $\text{Y}_2\text{F}_6$ molecules in the gas phase are calculated in the rigid rotator-harmonic oscillator approximation in the temperature interval of 298.15–6000 K according to the same algorithm [1, 20, 21]. The excited electron states were ignored in calculating the thermodynamic functions, since the $\text{Y}^{3+}$ ion has the filled electron configuration $...4s^24p^6$. As in [20], the results from calculations of the thermodynamic functions are presented as polynomials

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Phi^{\circ}(T)=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2} \ln X+\varphi_{3} X^{-2}+\varphi_{4} X^{-1} \\
& +\varphi_{5} X+\varphi_{6} X^{2}+\varphi_{7} X^{3} \quad\left(\mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}\right),
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

approximating the values of reduced Gibbs energy $\Phi^\circ(T)=S^\circ(T)-[H^\circ(T)-H^\circ(0)]/T$ with a maximum deviation of $0.03\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$. The coefficients of the polynomials are given in Table 2. Table 3 presents the values of the thermodynamic functions of $\text{YF}_3$ and $\text{Y}_2\text{F}_6$ molecules at several temperatures.

The errors in the thermodynamic functions are due to errors in both the accepted values of the molecular constants and the means of calculation. They are 2, 4, 6, and $8\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ for $\text{YF}_3$, and 9, 13, 18, and $21\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ for $\text{Y}_2\text{F}_6$ in reduced Gibbs energy $\Phi^\circ(T)$ at $T=298.15$, 1000, 3000, and 6000 K, respectively.

The thermodynamic functions of $\text{YF}_3$ were calculated in [22] up to a temperature of 2200 K while assuming the symmetry $C_{3v}$. The deviations between the data in [22] and Table 3 do not exceed 6 and $5\ \text{J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$ in $\Phi^\circ(T)$ and $S^\circ(T)$ values, respectively, and are explained by the difference in the accepted structure of the molecule. The thermodynamic functions of $\text{Y}_2\text{F}_6$ were calculated for the first time.

## CONCLUSIONS

The literature data for $\text{YF}_3$ and $\text{Y}_2\text{F}_6$ molecules in the gas phase were analyzed critically. Ab initio calculations of the structure and frequencies of these molecules in a nonrelativistic approximation with relativistic pseudopotentials were performed. Values of molecular constants were selected and the thermodynamic functions of $\text{YF}_3$ and $\text{Y}_2\text{F}_6$ were calculated. The composition of the vapor above $\text{YF}_3(\text{cr})$ and its temperature dependence, determination of the enthalpy of sublimation of $\text{YF}_3(\text{cr})$, and the enthalpies of formation of $\text{YF}_3$ and $\text{Y}_2\text{F}_6$ using the results obtained in this work will be the subject of a future work.
