# Heat Capacity of $\text{Tm}_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ in the Temperature Range 431–1004 K

V. M. Denisov, L. T. Denisova*, L. G. Chumilina, and S. D. Kirik

Institute of Non-Ferrous Metals and Materials Science, Siberian Federal University,
pr. im. Gazety "Krasnoyarskii rabochii" 95, Krasnoyarsk, 660025 Russia

* e-mail: antluba@mail.ru

Received June 7, 2013

**Abstract**—The molar heat capacity of $\text{Tm}_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ has been studied using differential scanning calorimetry in the temperature range 431–1004 K. The thermodynamic functions of the solid oxide compound have been calculated from the experimental data $C_p = f(T)$.

DOI: 10.1134/S1063783414020073

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Although high-temperature superconductor cuprates have been attracting interest in the field of research already for a long time, many problems associated with these materials still remain unsolved [1]. A suggestion was made that in order to understand the nature of high-temperature superconductors, one should perform studies not only of such materials but also of compounds approaching them in crystal structure and chemical composition while not actually being superconductors as such [2]. $R_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ cuprates ($R = \text{Tb–Lu}$ and Y) are assigned to such compounds [2, 3]. One of the least studied of them is $\text{Tm}_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ [3–7]. Preparation of $\text{Tm}_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ meets with difficulties because this compound melts incongruently and can be formed either by solid-phase synthesis or from a melt-solution. At the same time, optimization of the conditions under which it can be obtained requires thermodynamic investigation of its phases and this, in its turn, cannot be done without prior accumulation of information related to its thermodynamic properties. Data on the heat capacity of $\text{Tm}_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ are available for the region of very low temperatures only (2–30 K) [5].

The purpose of this work was to investigate the high-temperature heat capacity of $\text{Tm}_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ and to determine its thermodynamic characteristics from these data.

## 2. EXPERIMENT

The $\text{Tm}_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ compound was synthesized by the solid-phase reaction method from the initial oxides $\text{Tm}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{CuO}$. After preliminary annealing, these oxides were converted to a stoichiometric mixture, which after pressing was annealed in air at 1273 K for 25 h, intermittently with grinding and pressing every 5 h. X-ray diffraction patterns were recorded on an X'Pert Pro diffractometer (Panalytical, The Netherlands) with a semiconductor detector and a graphite monochromator. The data obtained are shown graphically in Fig. 1. At room temperature, the samples have orthorhombic crystal structure (space group $Pna2_1$) and belong to the so-called "blue phases" $R_2\text{Cu}_2\text{O}_5$ ($R$ stands for a rare-earth element) [8]. Our data give for the lattice parameters $a = 10.7418(7)$ Å, $b = 3.4556(2)$ Å, and $c = 12.3811(8)$ Å. These results are close enough to the data of [8]: $a = 10.7353(1)$ Å, $b = 3.4575(1)$ Å, and $c = 12.3704(2)$ Å.

The heat capacity $C_p$ was measured in platinum crucibles by differential scanning calorimetry with an STA 449 C Jupiter (NETZSCH). The measurement technique employed was described by us earlier [9, 10].

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As follows from our calorimetric measurements (Fig. 2), within the temperature interval 431–1004 K the molar heat capacity grows regularly, with no extrema in the dependence $C_p = f(T)$. The graph obtained can be fitted with the Maier–Kelley equation (in units J/(mol K))

$$
\begin{align*}
C_p &= a + bT + cT^{-2} \
&= 210.32 + 33.7 \times 10^{-3}T - 5.45 \times 10^5 T^{-2}.
\end{align*} \tag{1}
$$

We used Eq. (1) to derive with known thermodynamic relations the variations of enthalpy $H_T^0 - H_{431}^0$ and entropy $S_T^0 - S_{431}^0$. These data are listed in the table.

It is pertinent to note that at temperatures above 450 K the heat capacity exceeds the classical Dulong–Petit limit $3Rs$, where $R$ is the universal gas constant, and $s$ is the number of atoms in the formula unit of Tm₂Cu₂O₅ ($s = 9$).

Using the value of the characteristic Debye temperature $\Theta_\mathrm{D} = 250$ K obtained by us and the tables of Debye functions $D(\Theta_\mathrm{D}/T)$ [11], we calculated $C_p$ assumed in the first approximation to be close to $C_V$. The data obtained reveal a significant difference between the experimental and calculated values of $C_p$. This difference grows with increasing temperature. At the same time the temperature dependence of the heat capacity can be described in terms of combinations of the Debye and Einstein functions $E(\Theta_\mathrm{E}/T)$ [11]. In our case, by selecting appropriately combinations of these functions we come to the following equation

$$
\begin{align*}
C_p &= D\left(\frac{\Theta_\mathrm{D}}{T}\right) + mE\left(\frac{\Theta_\mathrm{E}}{T}\right) \
&= D\left(\frac{250}{T}\right) + 0.12E\left(\frac{2521}{T}\right),
\end{align*} \tag{2}
$$

which fits satisfactorily the experimental data on the heat capacity (Fig. 2). Note the closeness of the coefficient by the Einstein function to $1/s = 0.11$.

The compositions of the oxides of the systems GeO₂–PbO [9] and Bi₂O₃–Fe₂O₃ [12] had been found to correlate with their specific heat $C_p^0$. Assuming the same correlation to hold for the Tm₂O₃–CuO system as well (Fig. 3), we can, despite the small number of experimental points available, estimate $C_p^0$ for Tm₂Cu₂O₅. As seen from Fig. 3, in this particular case the estimated value of $C_p^0$ is smaller than the value obtained with the Neumann–Kopp method [13]. The values of $C_p^0$ for CuO and Tm₂O₃ were taken from [14] and [14, 15], respectively. The graphs of Fig. 3 demonstrate that the magnitude of $C_p^0$ grows regularly in the Tm₂O₃–Tm₂Cu₂O₅–CuO sequence.

### 4. CONCLUSIONS

The effect of temperature on the heat capacity of Tm₂Cu₂O₅ has been studied. It has been demonstrated that a combination of the Debye and Einstein functions fits satisfactorily the experimental $C_p = f(T)$ data.
