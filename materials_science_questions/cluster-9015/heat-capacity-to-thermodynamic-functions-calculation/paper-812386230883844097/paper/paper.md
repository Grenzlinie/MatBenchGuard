# Thermochemical stability of the $\mathrm{HO}_{2}-\mathrm{HClO}_{4}$ complex

Abraham F. Jalbout ${ }^{\mathrm{a}, \mathrm{b}, *}$, M. Solimannaejad ${ }^{\mathrm{a}, \mathrm{b}}$, J.K. Labanowski ${ }^{\mathrm{c}}$

## Abstract

The thermochemical stability of the $\mathrm{HO}_{2}-\mathrm{HClO}_{4}$ complex is studied using both second-order Møller-Plesset perturbation theory (MP2) and the B3LYP density functional theory (DFT) method. Our calculations reveal stabilization energies of 11.1 and $13.2 \mathrm{kcal} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$ at the MP2/6-311++G(3df,3pd) and B3LYP/6-311++G(3df,3pd) levels, respectively.

## 1. Introduction

In a previous investigation the thermochemical stability of the $\mathrm{HO}_{2}-\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ complex was reported [1]. Due to the fact that for many bimolecular complexes formed between closed-shell species generally tend to have binding energies of around $10 \mathrm{kcal} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$, there was an increased interest in those systems. This work focuses on the thermochemical properties and stability of the complex formed as a result of the bonding between an open-shell peroxy radicals $\left(\mathrm{HO}_{2}\right)[2,3]$ and the perchloric acid $\left(\mathrm{HClO}_{4}\right)$ molecule [4-6]. For the $\mathrm{HO}_{2}-\mathrm{HClO}_{4}$ complex the binding energy of around -13.3 (MP2/ $6-311++\mathrm{G}(3 \mathrm{df}, 3 \mathrm{pd})$ level of theory) $\mathrm{kcal} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$ is computed, versus the value of around $-18.9 \mathrm{kcal}$ $\mathrm{mol}^{-1}$ for the $\mathrm{HO}_{2}-\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ complex, and around -15 to $16 \mathrm{kcal} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$ for $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{2}-\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ or $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}-\mathrm{H}_{2}-$ $\mathrm{SO}_{4}$ complexes [1]. $\mathrm{HO}_{2}$ uptake by sulfuric acid aerosols in the atmosphere [1,7-14] can be at least partially accounted for by the complexation in the form of $\mathrm{HO}_{2}-\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$, given the strength of the bonds involved. This has motivated the search for a stable $\mathrm{HO}_{2}-\mathrm{HClO}_{4}$ complex.

## 2. Results and discussion

Many studies have previously focused on the kinetics and the behavior of chlorofluorocarbons in the stratosphere, which of course are involved in ozone catalytic cycles [15-17]. However, the possibility that perchloric acid can uptake certain peroxy radicals (i.e., $\mathrm{HO}_{2}$ ) is an interesting possibility. We fully optimized all structures using tight convergence criteria with the unrestricted second-order Møller–Plesset perturbation theory (MP2) method [18] and with the unrestricted density functional Becke's three parameter and Lee–Yang–Parr functional (B3LYP) method [19]. Although, our first set of calculations centered upon the optimization of the structures with small basis sets (6-31G(d), 6-31G(2df,2p), 6-311G(d), 6-311++G (2d,2p), etc.), we later performed optimizations with the 6-311++G(2df,2p) and 6-311++G (3df,3pd) basis sets to reduce the basis set superposition error (BSSE) [20]. The larger basis sets produced a counter poise correction that is rather small (0.40 kcal mol⁻¹), and not likely to make a difference in the final computed binding energies. Zero-point energies (ZPE) corrections were calculated using the harmonic frequencies at the B3LYP/6-311++G(3df,3pd) level of theory, due to the enormous computational resources needed for MP2/6-311++G(3df,3pd) frequency calculations [21]. The GAUSSIAN 98 program was used in all calculations [22]. In fact for complexes such as HO₂–H₂SO₄ and H₂O–H₂SO₄ complexes the stabilities calculated using the MP2 and B3LYP method shows small differences and good correlations to one another. The levels of theory employed are satisfactory for assessing relative energies in this study.

The stabilities calculated for the HO₂–H₂SO₄ and H₂O–H₂SO₄ complexes calculated using the B3LYP method also show small differences [1,23,24] to that for HO₂–H₂SO₄, which are in agreement with the MP2 results. Thus, the DFT methods are quite reliable for these systems (at least qualitatively).

Previously, the reliability in the geometrical parameters for HO₂ [1], were discussed, by which the MP2 methods provide a slightly better geometry than at the DFT-B3LYP method. However, in general for peroxides DFT methods generally have this well-known flaw [25]. As was mentioned, the HClO₄ MP2 and B3LYP geometry was previously investigated [4–6]. This species has Cₛ symmetry, with MP2 (B3LYP) bond lengths of: R(Cl═O) = 1.6582(1.6360)Å, R(Cl–O′)=1.4107 (1.4108) Å, R(O–H)=0.9713(0.9722) Å, compared to the experimentally known values of 1.636 and 1.408 Å.

As in the HO₂–H₂SO₄ structure, the optimized HO₂–HClO₄ structure forms a nearly planar seven-membered ring due to the formation of two strong hydrogen bonds. The bond strengths calculated at the MP2/6-311++G(3df,3pd) level of theory are 1.7812 Å for the OOH···O═Cl and 1.6474 Å for the HOO···H═O′Cl, respectively. This suggests that the HOO···H–O′Cl bond is the greater stabilization force, due to the contraction of the bond. Our results suggest that stronger bonding occurs in the HOO···H–O′Cl linkage, consistent with its shorter bond length. The extra unpaired electron of HO₂ resides on the terminal oxygen atom in an orbital perpendicular to the HOO···H–O′Cl bond, and does not participate in the bond formation.

Our computed value for $D_e$ ($D_0$) in kcal mol$^{-1}$ at the MP2/6-311++G(3df,3pd) and B3LYP/6-311++G(3df,3pd) level of theory are 13.3(12.2) and 11.1(9.09). Although, the B3LYP/6-31G(d) tends to overestimate the dissociation energies probably due to the increased BSSE effect using a small basis set, the MP2/6-31G(d) predicts rather good binding energies. These results dictate a decreased stability, relative to $\text{HO}_2\text{–H}_2\text{SO}_4$ of around 5 kcal mol$^{-1}$ (around 3 kcal mol$^{-1}$ with zero-point corrections). It is interesting to note that the $D_0$ value calculated at the MP2/6-311++G(2d,2p) level for $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{–H}_2\text{–SO}_4$ is around 11.2 kcal mol$^{-1}$ [26], in relatively good agreement with our results. While the optimized B3LYP structures show similar trends the MP2 structures generally predict stronger complex bonding, in qualitative agreement with $\text{HO}_2\text{–H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

Table 2 shows the thermodynamic properties for the $\text{HO}_2\text{–HClO}_4$ (g) $\rightarrow \text{HO}_2$ (g) $+\text{HClO}_4$ (g), where $T$ (temperature in K), $S$ (entropy in J mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$), $C_\text{p}$ (heat capacity at constant pressure in kJ mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$), and $H^0 - H_{298.15}^0$ (enthalpy content, in kJ mol$^{-1}$), $T_1 = 100$ K, $T_2 = 298.15$ K, and $T_3 = 1000$ K calculated using B3LYP/6-311++G(3df,3pd) frequencies. The fits were performed according to the equations implemented by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) [27]. To further analyze the accuracy of our thermodynamic data, we have compared the $\text{HO}_2$ entropy values (at 298.15 K) to the experimental value of 229.1 J mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ [28], and the following theoretical data, 228.5 mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ (MP2) and 228.8 mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ (B3LYP), leading to relative differences of only 0.26% and 0.13%, respectively. Within the entire range of temperatures for $\text{HO}_2$, the experimental agreement of our theoretical thermodynamic functions is within very small error, and shall be used as test for which our data on $\text{HO}_2\text{–HClO}_4$ can be verified. However, no experimental data on the thermochemical properties (as for $\text{HO}_2$) are available for the $\text{HClO}_4$ monomer. At the same level of theory as the data in Table 2, we have computed that values for the $\text{HO}_2$ and $\text{HClO}_4$ monomer (using the same units at 298.15 K) as 34.59 ($C_\text{p}$), 228.82 ($S$), 9.98 ($H^0 - H_{298.15}^0$), 74.08 ($C_\text{p}$), 297.4 ($S$), 14.93 ($H^0 - H_{298.15}^0$), respectively. Thus, upon complexation substantial increases in these properties can indicate increased stabilization.

### 3. Conclusions
The ability of peroxy radicals to donate and accept hydrogen bonds, partially accounts for the partitioning between gas phase and aerosol droplets [1,29], which makes these results more timely, from an atmospheric perspective. We have explored the structures, dissociation energies, and thermodynamic properties of the $\text{HO}_2\text{–HClO}_4$ complex. We believe that this complex appears to behave similarly to $\text{HO}_2\text{–H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{–H}_2\text{–SO}_4$; however it is not as tightly bound (see the $\Delta D$ values of Table 1). In addition to this, we have briefly examined the possibility of other dissociation pathways (such as formation of $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{H}$, etc.) but barriers for this process are too high to be considered probable mechanisms. This shall be the focus of future investigations. In any case, the thermochemical properties of this complex can be potentially important, and deserves further experimental verification of the presented theoretical results.
