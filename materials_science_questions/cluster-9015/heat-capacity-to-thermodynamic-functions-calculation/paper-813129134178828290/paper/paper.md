# Heat capacity on data of DSC calorimetry and thermodynamic functions of barium cerate doped by holmium and indium oxides in the temperature range of 200-700 K

## Abstract
For the first time the heat capacity of $BaCe_{0.7}Ho_{0.2}In_{0.1}O_{2.85}$ was measured in the temperature range of 200-700 K. Calorimetric experiments were performed using differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). We identified three areas where heat capacity was smoothly varying: 200-500, 500-573, and 573-700 K. The heat capacity in all these areas has been described by polynomials. Heat capacity of $BaCe_{0.7}In_{0.1}Ho_{0.2}O_{2.85}$ in the temperature range 200-500 K is well described by a polynomial: $C_{p,m}^{\circ}=55.606 + 0.23556\ T - 1.9546\times10^{-4}\ T^{2}-1.2072\times10^{-8}\ T^{3}$. The heat capacity in the range of 500-573 K is well described by a polynomial of the form: $C_{p,m}^{\circ}=429.55 - 0.66985\ T + 4.0801\times10^{-4}\ T^{2}-1.8387\times10^{7}/T^{2}$. The heat capacity in the temperature range 573-700 K is well described by the equation: $C_{p,m}^{\circ}=135.72 - 0.060195\ T + 6.7994\times10^{-5}\ T^{2}$. The enthalpy increments $(H_{m}^{\circ}(T)-H_{m}^{\circ}(298.15))$ and entropy $(S_{m}^{\circ}(T))$ were evaluated from heat capacity data ($T=200-700$ K).

Keywords DSC calorimetry · Heat capacity · Doped barium cerate

## Introduction
Mixed oxides which can conduct electricity either totally, or partially, by ion migration are important in a wide range of applications, many of which are of importance to environmental and energy problems [1-7]. At present, cerates of alkaline earth elements with perovskite structure are studied extensively because of their fuel cell applications. Trivalent elements such as indium or rare earth metals may substitute tetravalent cerium resulting in $MCe_{1-x}A_{x}^{III}O_{3-x/2}$ compounds. At high temperature, these perovskites are ionic or proton conductors.

The increase in proton conductivity with doping is mainly related to the formation of protonic defects in the $ABO_{3}$ perovskites due to dissociative adsorption of water in the presence of oxygen vacancies.

With the increase in substitution of barium cerates by rare earth elements, the number of oxygen vacancies is increased that leads to conductivity increasing. However, as is known the homogeneity region in cerates of alkali earth metals is limited 20%. Therefore, there is a problem to increase the homogeneity region. In paper [8, 9], the new barium cerates where homogeneity region was increased up to 30% by introducing of indium and rare earth oxides were synthesized. It was shown that obtained cerates have more high thermodynamic stability in comparison with barium cerates without indium. It is necessary to perform full physical and chemical investigations for successful application of synthesized cerates.

In this paper, we for the first time performed measurements of heat capacity of barium cerate doped by holmium and indium oxides in the temperature range of 200-700 K. The heat capacities were measured by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). DSC calorimetry and thermal analysis

## Experimental
$\mathrm{BaCe_{0.7}Ho_{0.2}In_{0.1}O_{2.85}}$ was prepared by reaction of oxides, $\mathrm{CeO_2}$ (99.9%, Vetron GmbH, Karlsruhe), $\mathrm{Ho_2O_3}$ (99.99%, Ventron), $\mathrm{In_2O_3}$ (99.99%, Reacton, A Johnson Matthey Company), and barium carbonate, $\mathrm{BaCO_3}$ (CERAC, TM incorporated, 99.999% pure, USA). All starting materials were annealed at high temperature before undergoing the solid reaction.

Stoichiometric amounts of $\mathrm{BaCO_3}$, $\mathrm{CeO_2}$, $\mathrm{Ho_2O_3}$, and $\mathrm{In_2O_3}$ were mixed by ball milling in an agate container with agate balls using a planetary mill (Fritsch pulverisette) for several hours. The ground materials were palletized and fired at 1100–1700 K using a Carbolite furnace.

The phase purity was analyzed with X-ray diffractometer (STADI-P, Stoe diffractometer, Germany, Mo radiation). XRD pattern is presented in Fig. 1. X-ray diffraction indicated that the sample was single phase with orthorhombic structure. Cell parameters were: $a=0.877038$ nm, $b=0.618453$ nm, $c=0.619023$ nm.

$\mathrm{BaCe_{0.7}Ho_{0.2}In_{0.1}O_{2.85}}$ compound was characterized by chemical analysis [13, 14]. Analytical results are presented in Table 1. For the analysis of Ba, a flame photometric method was used. Ce, Ho, In were analyzed by a spectrophotometric method. The oxygen content was determined by reducing melting method. According to results of analysis, the investigated compound ($\mathrm{BaCe_{0.7}Ho_{0.2}In_{0.1}O_{2.85}}$) correspond to above-mentioned formula with accuracy $<1\%$.

Heat capacities of phase $\mathrm{BaCe_{0.7}Ho_{0.2}In_{0.1}O_{2.85}}$ were measured over the range from $T=(200$ to $700)$ K in the differential scanning calorimeter. Measurements were performed using calorimeter Netzsch DSC 404 F1 by continuous heating of $6$ K min$^{-1}$. The sample mass for $\mathrm{BaCe_{0.7}Ho_{0.2}In_{0.1}O_{2.85}}$ was 108.03 mg (molar mass is 325.48037 g mol$^{-1}$). Mass was weighted using electronic balance AND GH-252 with an accuracy 0.3 mg.

## Conclusions

For the first time, we measured the heat capacities of BaCe₀.₇Ho₀.₂In₀.₁O₂.₈₅ in the temperature range of 200–700 K. Differential scanning calorimetry was used for measurements. On the basis of obtained heat capacities, we calculated enthalpy increments and entropy (200–700 K).
