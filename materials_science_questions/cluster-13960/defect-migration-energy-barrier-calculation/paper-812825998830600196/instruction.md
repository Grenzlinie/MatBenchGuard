# Helium cluster formation and migration at a W⟨110⟩/W⟨112⟩ grain boundary: a density functional theory reproduction

## Problem background
Tungsten (W) is a leading candidate for plasma-facing components in future fusion reactors because of its high melting point and low sputtering yield. However, helium (He) atoms generated by plasma exposure can penetrate the material, migrate, and accumulate at internal defects such as grain boundaries. The formation energies, clustering behavior, and mobility of small He clusters at a W(110)/W(112) grain boundary are key quantities that determine whether such boundaries act as benign traps that promote helium outgassing or as nucleation sites for detrimental bubbles. This task uses density functional theory to compute these quantities from first principles.

## Approach
The interface is modeled with a periodic slab containing six layers of W(110) and six layers of W(112), separated by a vacuum gap. The geometry is relaxed using plane-wave DFT with the PBE exchange-correlation functional. A single W vacancy is created at the most stable site on the W(112) side, and the system is re-relaxed. To study He clusters, He atoms are placed (i) at the most stable groove that runs along the defect-free interface, and (ii) inside the monovacancy. For each cluster size (N_He = 1 to 9) and each location, several initial placements are tried, and the lowest-energy relaxed structure is retained.

Formation energies are computed from the total energies of these structures together with reference energies for bulk W and an isolated He atom. Binding energies are derived from the sequence of formation energies using the one-by-one formula. The migration barriers for 1, 2, and 3 He atoms moving together along the groove are obtained with the nudged elastic band (NEB) method. Finally, the relaxed geometries for the vacancy case are inspected to determine how many He atoms can fit inside the vacancy before additional atoms occupy neighboring interfacial sites.

## Reproduction target
Produce four output files using an open-source DFT code (the recommended code is Quantum ESPRESSO, but any plane-wave DFT code supporting PBE calculations and NEB may be used).

1. `formation_energies.csv`: for N_He = 1..9, give the formation energy (eV) at the most stable groove and inside the monovacancy.
2. `binding_energies.csv`: for N_He = 2..9, give the atom-by-atom binding energies (eV) derived from the formation energies.
3. `migration_barriers.csv`: for N_He = 1, 2, 3, give the NEB migration barrier (eV) along the most stable groove.
4. `max_occupancy.json`: report the integer maximum number of He atoms that remain inside the monovacancy, and a boolean indicating whether spillover to nearby interfacial sites is observed when more atoms are added.

All files must follow the schemas and paths specified in the Output files and Output contract sections.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Construct and relax the clean W⟨110⟩/W⟨112⟩ interface supercell
- Role: process
- Action: Build a periodic supercell containing 456 W atoms (288 W⟨110⟩, 168 W⟨112⟩), six layers per side, with a 12 Å vacuum gap. Fix the bottom layer of each surface and use DFT-PBE to relax all other atoms until forces drop below 0.025 eV/Å. Obtain the total energy of the relaxed clean interface.
- Evidence: none

### Step 2: DFT relaxations of He clusters at interface groove and inside monovacancy
- Role: process
- Action: Starting from the clean relaxed interface, introduce a single W vacancy at the most stable W⟨112⟩-side site (as identified in the literature) and relax. Then, for He cluster sizes N_He = 1–9, place He atoms (a) at the most stable interface groove (He1 equivalent position) and (b) inside the monovacancy. For each size perform DFT relaxations of several initial geometries, identify the lowest-energy configuration, and record its total energy.
- Evidence: none

### Step 3: Compute formation energies
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: Using the total energies from step 2, the clean interface energy, the energy of an isolated W atom in bulk, and the energy of an isolated He atom, calculate the formation energy for each cluster size at the groove and inside the vacancy using the standard formation energy formula. Write the results to formation_energies.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/formation_energies.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: CSV with columns: N_He (integer), E_f_groove_eV (float, 2 decimals), E_f_vacancy_eV (float, 2 decimals)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 4: Compute binding energies
- Role: scored
- Action: From the formation energies computed in step 3, calculate the atom-by-atom binding energy for each N_He using the one-by-one formula for both groove and vacancy systems. Write the results to binding_energies.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/binding_energies.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: CSV with columns: N_He (integer), E_b_groove_eV (float, 2 decimals), E_b_vacancy_eV (float, 2 decimals)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 5: Perform NEB calculations for He migration along the groove
- Role: process
- Action: Using the lowest-energy configurations from step 2 as initial and final states, compute minimum energy paths for 1, 2, and 3 He atoms moving together along the most stable interfacial groove via the nudged elastic band (NEB) method. Record the energy profile along each path.
- Evidence: none

### Step 6: Report migration barriers
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: Extract the energy barrier (difference between the highest-energy point along the NEB path and the initial-state energy) for each cluster size (1,2,3). Write the values to migration_barriers.csv.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/migration_barriers.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: CSV with columns: N_He (integer), barrier_eV (float, 2 decimals)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 7: Determine maximum He occupancy in the monovacancy
- Role: scored
- Action: From the vacancy relaxation results of step 2, determine the maximum number of He atoms that remain inside the monovacancy (not occupying adjacent interfacial sites) before additional atoms spillover to nearby interface positions. Report the result in max_occupancy.json.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/max_occupancy.json`
- Format: json
- Contract: JSON object with keys: max_He_in_vacancy (integer), spillover_observed (boolean)
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

## How you are scored
The hidden verifier reads the files you write to `/app/outputs` and assigns a score in [0,1] as follows: it checks that each file is present and correctly formatted, then compares the numeric values against reference data for the same system and against expected physical trends. The four artifacts are weighted:
- `formation_energies.csv`: 0.4
- `binding_energies.csv`: 0.2
- `migration_barriers.csv`: 0.2
- `max_occupancy.json`: 0.2

The total reward is the weighted sum. Because the verifier tests both specific values and monotonic trends (e.g., formation energies must increase with cluster size, barriers must increase with the number of He atoms, occupancy must show the correct spillover threshold), fabricating numbers without performing the DFT and NEB simulations is very unlikely to succeed.
