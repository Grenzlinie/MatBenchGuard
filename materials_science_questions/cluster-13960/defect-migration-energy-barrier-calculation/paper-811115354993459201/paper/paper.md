# MOLECULAR ORBITAL CALCULATIONS FOR SURFACE-BOUND METAL SPECIES: RHODIUM(I) DICARBONYLS ON ALUMINA OR SILICA

MICHAEL P. KEYES

Laboratory for Surface Studies, University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee, Milwaukee, WI 53201 (U.S.A.)

and KENNETH L. WATTERS*

Department of Chemistry, University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee, Milwaukee, WI 53201 (U.S.A.)

(Received February 16, 1988; accepted May 3, 1988)

## Summary

The oxide-supported $\text{Rh}^\text{I}(\text{CO})_2$ species, frequently generated on heterogeneous rhodium catalysts, has been modeled for extended Hückel molecular orbital calculations using, as oxide supports, simple oxyanion structures composed of Si or Al atoms in tetrahedral or octahedral oxygen environments. Calculations for different orientations of $\text{Rh}^\text{I}(\text{CO})_2$ with respect to the oxyanion structures showed the lowest energy configuration to be that in which the metal carbonyl fragment binds to two oxygen atoms, forming a square planar geometry about the $\text{Rh}^\text{I}$. Rearrangement to a five-coordinate species with $\text{Rh}^\text{I}(\text{CO})_2$ bound to three oxygens requires between 0.51 and 1.94 eV of energy, depending upon the oxyanion structure and whether the central atom is Si or Al. The lowest energy for conversion from four- to five-coordination about $\text{Rh}^\text{I}$ occurs upon octahedral anions. The square planar $\rightarrow$ five-coordinate conversion is discussed as a surface migration pathway, with relative activation barriers to the migration depending upon the energy of this conversion.

## Introduction

Much attention has been given to the identification and structural characterization of rhodium carbonyl species supported on oxide surfaces. It is widely observed for samples of $\text{SiO}_2$- and $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$-supported rhodium that linear and bridging CO adsorbs on $\text{Rh}^0$ particles, while a dicarbonyl species forms on atomically dispersed $\text{Rh}^\text{I}$ centers under appropriate experimental conditions [1]. For samples of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$-supported rhodium, recent EXAFS work has indicated that $\text{Rh}^\text{I}(\text{CO})_2$ is bound by three surface oxides, giving

Recently, the importance of $Rh^{I}$ sites as opposed to $Rh^{0}$ particles for $Rh/SiO_{2}$ catalysts was reported for hydrogenation of CO [5]. The $Rh^{I}$ spe cies, thought to be the principal catalytically active surface species, appar- ently was stabilized by promoters that prevented complete reduction to $Rh^{0}$. The question arises as to the similarity of the oxide-supported $Rh^{I}$ catalytic species to molecular Rh(I) complexes in solution.

Owing to the apparent importance of $Rh^{I}$ surface species and the unclear understanding of their role in heterogeneous reaction schemes, mol- ecular orbital calculations of supported $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$ were undertaken and are described in this paper. The extended Hückel method, which can be applied to molecular orbital calculations of large molecules with relatively modest investments of computer time, is used to obtain molecular orbital energy levels for the $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$ fragment bound in several different configurations to four molecular models of silica and alumina. Using the total energies calcul-ated for the various configurations, the preferred orientation of the $Rh^{I}$  $(CO)_{2}$ fragment is predicted, and a pathway for the surface migration of $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$ on $SiO_{2}$ and $Al_{2} O_{3}$ is proposed in which the metal species moves from two- to three-fold oxide coordination sites.

## Molecular models and calculation method

Figure 1 shows the four structures used to model silica and alumina. The tetrahedral or octahedral oxygen coordination about the central Si or Al includes, in each case, three oxygens serving as surface oxides and forming the trigonal face depicted above the central atom in the figure. The remain- ing oxygens, one for the tetrahedral and three for the octahedral models, represent bulk oxides to which are attached pseudoatoms, L, with 1s orbitals only and ionization energies initially set at -13.6 eV. Idealized tetrahedral and octahedral bond angles and Si-O, Al-O, and O-L bond distances of1.60, 1.82 and 0.96, respectively, were used in the calculations. Methodical adjustment of the pseudoatom 1s energy for each oxyanion structure had no effect on the charge of the three surface oxides, implying minimal effect ontheir electronic properties. Accordingly, the 1s energy of L was set at -13.6 eV in all calculations reported here.

Figure 2A-D illustrates the two configurations used in these calculations for the $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$ species relative to the Al or Si and to the three surface oxides for the four different oxyanion models. The Rh atom was either directly above the center of the trigonal face (Fig. 2A and C) with $C_{s}$ sym metry about the Rh, or the Rh was bonded to two of the three oxides (Fig.2B and D) with $C_{2 v}$ symmetry. Rh-O bond lengths were $2.12 \AA$ in eachmodel [2]; the OC-Rh-CO angle was $90^{\circ}$ ; and bond distances in $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$  were those reported for $Rh_{2}(CO)_{4} Cl_{2}$ [6]. Energy diagrams in Fig. 4 show the effect on total energies as the dicarbonyl fragment converts between the 2-O and 3-O coordinations for each of the oxyanion model structures. Calculations reveal the 2-O coordina- tion to have lower total energy than the 3-O coordination in each case. In other words, the square planar, four-coordinate surface configuration for the $Rh^I$ is predicted to be thermodynamically favored over configurations with $Rh^I$ in a five-coordinate environment. The energy difference between the 2-O and 3-O coordinations for tetrahedral oxyanion structures I and II (1.44 and 1.94 eV, respectively) exceed those for octahedral structures III and IV (0.50 and 0.73 eV, respectively). It also should be noted that the activation energy needed to convert from the 2-O to the 3-O coordination consists of just the energy difference between these two configurations for each model.

The total energy for each different configuration was approximately separated into the energies for the $Rh^I(CO)_2$ and oxyanion fragments by dividing the molecular orbitals into those of primarily $Rh^I(CO)_2$ and primarily oxyanion character, and summing the electron energies of each set. The diagrams in Fig. 4, illustrating the effect on the model fragment energies as the $Rh^I$ coordination changes, show two competing trends. Energies of the oxyanion fragments reach a minimum for the 3-O coordina- tion in each case, while the energies calculated for the $Rh^I(CO)_2$ fragment are at a minimum for the 2-O coordination. Keeping the Rh-O distance constant necessarily places the Rh nearer the center of the trigonal plane (and the central Al or Si) for structures I/II than for III/IV. Accordingly, the obtital overlap of each Rh d orbital with the surface oxide p orbitals varies between structures I/II versus III/IV. Qualitatively, $d_{z^2}$, $d_{xz}$, $d_{yz}$ should have lower overlap with the surface oxide p orbitals for structures I/II than for III/IV, while $d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $d_{xy}$ should have greater overlap. Because the metal d orbitals overlap in anti-bonding fashion with the surface oxide p orbitals, greater overlap would raise the total energy, while lower overlap has the opposite effect. The low symmetry and the considerable mixing of the d orbitals for $Rh^I(CO)_2$ in the 3-O coordination makes it difficult, though, to relate the change in overlap with a change in individual molecular orbitals. Regardless, the net difference between the 3-O coordination energy of $Rh^I(CO)_2$ with structures I/II versus III/IV is reflected in the calculated energy of the $Rh^I(CO)_2$ fragment in Fig. 5.

## Surface migration of $Rh^I(CO)_2$

The interconversion of $Rh^I(CO)_2$ between the 2-O and 3-O coordinations can be related to a mechanism for the surface migration of the metal species on oxide supports. The (111) surface planes of bulk silica and alumina are arrays of equilateral triangles of surface oxides [9]. If the trigonal face formed by the surface oxides in the oxyanion structures in the present study represents one triangle on a (111) surface plane, then the $Rh^I(CO)_2$ fragment bound to the oxyanion structures would model $Rh^I(CO)_2$ supported on (111) surface planes of silica and alumina. The forms of high surface area silica normally used to support transition metals for heterogeneous catalysis have silicon atoms in a tetrahedral oxide environment. The activation energy for $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$ migration on $SiO_{2}$ is expected, therefore, to be that calculated using structure I (1.44 eV). Alumina, on the other hand, has the aluminum atoms in both tetrahedral and octahedral oxide environments, and the activation energy for the surface migration of $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$ is predicted from structures II and IV (1.94 and 0.73 eV, respectively). The migration of $Rh^{I}(CO)_{2}$ on $Al_{2}O_{3}$ over octahedral aluminum sites is predicted to be much more facile than over tetrahedral sites. Reported studies with supported $Rh_6(CO)_{16}$ are consistent with this generalization [10]. Supported $Rh_6(CO)_{16}$ has been proposed to reversibly react on oxide supports to give $Rh^{I}(CO)_2$ surface species according to the reaction:

$$\mathrm{Rh}_{6}(\mathrm{CO})_{16}+6[*-\mathrm{OH}] \longrightarrow 6\left[*-\mathrm{O})-\mathrm{Rh}^{\mathrm{I}}(\mathrm{CO})_{2}\right]+3 \mathrm{H}_{2}+4 \mathrm{CO} \tag{1}$$

The decomposition is complete in minutes on pretreated $Al_2O_3$, but quite sluggish under identical conditions on $SiO_2$. Regeneration of the physisorbed cluster, accomplished by treating the samples with $H_2$ and CO, also is observed to be more rapid using $Al_2O_3$ as the support. Moreover, with $SiO_2$ as the support, the formation of an intermediate cluster, $Rh_4(CO)_{12}$, is reported. Both the forward and reverse of reaction (1) on the surface should be accel- erated by more rapid migration of $Rh^{I}(CO)_2$ across the $Al_2O_3$ surface.

## Conclusions

The results of the calculations on $Rh^{I}(CO)_2$ bound to the four oxyanion structures indicate that the 2-O coordination is the most energetically fav- ored of the surface configurations modeled in the present work. The metal center in the 2-O coordination has essentially square-planar geometry, with two surface oxides and two carbon atoms of CO groups in the coordination sphere. (It should be noted that the actual high area surfaces commonly studied may differ substantially from the idealized 111 surface planes mod- eled here. The identification of 3-O coordination for $Rh^{I}(CO)_2$ on $Al_2O_3$ in an EXAFS study under a particular set of conditions is, therefore, not neces- sarily contradictory to the results of this study, which predict 2-O coordina- tion to be energetically favored on the 111 surface. Finally, this study makes no attempt to assess the effect of hydroxyl groups, present on most com- monly studied high surface area oxides, on the process of the migration of the $Rh^{I}(CO)_2$ (fragment across an oxide surface.) Rearrangement to the 3-O coordination is found to be energetically reasonable, particularly with the oxyanion model structures with octahedral oxygen environments. Indeed, the rearrangement suggests a mechanistic pathway for the surface migration of $Rh^{I}(CO)_2$ across a (111) plane of silica and alumina, with the activation barrier for the surface migration being the difference in energy between the 2-O and 3-O coordinations. This barrier is lowest over the sites when the silicon or aluminum atoms are in octahedral oxygen environments.

Traditionally, differences in reactivities between oxide supports are discussed in terms of thermodynamic acid/base properties using Brönsted or Lewis definitions. The results of the present study suggest that the structure of the oxide also may influence the surface processes of supported metal species by affecting migration kinetics.
