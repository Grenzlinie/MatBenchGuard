# ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE

## Theoretical Study of Aluminide Clusters $\boldsymbol{\ce{Al_{13}X}}$, $\boldsymbol{\ce{Al_{13}X^-}}$, and $\boldsymbol{\ce{Al_{13}X^-2}}$ ($\boldsymbol{\ce{X= H}}$, $\boldsymbol{\ce{Hal}}$, $\boldsymbol{\ce{OH}}$, $\boldsymbol{\ce{NH_2}}$, $\boldsymbol{\ce{CH_3}}$, and $\boldsymbol{\ce{C_6H_5}}$)

### O. P. Charkin$^{a}$, V. K. Kochnev$^{b}$, and N. M. Klimenko$^{b}$
$^{a}$ Institute of Problems of Chemical Physics, Russian Academy of Sciences, Chernogolovka, Moscow oblast, 142432 Russia  
E-mail: charkin@icp.ac.ru  

$^{b}$ Lomonosov State Academy of Fine Chemical Technology, pr. Vernadskogo 86, Moscow, 117571 Russia  
E-mail: klimenko@aha.ru  

Received May 20, 2006

**Abstract**—The structural, electronic, energy, and vibrational characteristics of the $\ce{Al_{13}X^-}$ and $\ce{Al_{13}X^-2}$ clusters, with an aluminum-centered ($\ce{Al_{c}}$) icosahedral cage $\ce{Al_{13}}$ and with one or two outer-sphere ligands $\ce{X = H, F, Cl, Br, OH, NH_2, CH_3, C_6H_5}$, have been calculated within the B3LYP approximation of the density functional theory using the 6-31G* and 6-311+G* basis sets. In all $\ce{Al_{13}X^-}$ radicals, the unpaired electron is localized at the cage atom $\ce{Al^*}$ located opposite the $\ce{Al-X}$ bond. This $\ce{Al^*}$ atom is the most favorable site for attaching the second $\ce{X}$ ligand of any nature (trans-addition rule). According to the previously suggested molecular model of the valence state of the $[\ce{Al^-_{13}}]$ “superatom,” the calculated energies $D_1(\ce{Al^-_{13}-X})$ of addition of the first ligand to the $\ce{Al^-_{13}}$ anion are about 1 eV lower than the corresponding energies of addition of the second ligand $D_2(\ce{XAl^-_{13}-X})$. The structure of the $\ce{Al_{13}}$ cage depends on the nature of the nature of the substituent $\ce{X}$ and can radically change in going from anions to their neutral congeners. In the lowest-lying $\ce{Al_{13}X}$ isomer with electronegative substituents $\ce{X}$ (Hal, $\ce{OH, NH_2, CH_3}$, etc.), the aluminum cage has a marquee structure (**1**, symmetry $C_s$) with a hexagonal base and a pentagonal “roof.” For $\ce{Al_{13}X}$ analogues with electropositive ligands $\ce{X}$ (Al, Li, Na), a tridentate isomer ($\boldsymbol{T}$, $C_{3v}$) with the $\ce{X}$ substituent coordinated to a face of the $\ce{Al_{13}}$ icosahedron is preferable. In the case of moderately electronegative $\ce{X}$ ligands (of the $\ce{H}$ type), the marquee (**1**) and icosahedral ($\boldsymbol{T}$) isomers are close in energy. The stretching vibration frequencies of isomers **1** and $\boldsymbol{T}$ differ significantly in magnitude and intensity so that vibrational spectroscopy methods can be especially applicable to their experimental identification.

DOI: 10.1134/S003602360612014X

Numerous experimental and theoretical studies have been focused on the search for stable aluminum clusters that can be used as building blocks (“superatoms”) for obtaining aluminum nanoparticles by cluster assembling methods (see, e.g., [1–4] and references therein). Most attention has been given to bare icosahedral aluminides (in the gas phase) of the $\ce{M@Al^-_{12}^0}$ type with dopant atoms $\ce{M = B, Al, C, Si}$, and others, as well as to “completely coated” icosahedral alanes (in condensed phases) of the $\ce{Al_{12}R^{2-}_{12}}$ type with bulky ligands $\ce{R}$, which prevent metal cages from sticking together. An ordered two-dimensional lattice made up of aluminum clusters (identical superatoms) on the $\ce{Si(001)}$ surface was fabricated for the first time and experimentally studied in [5].

Intermediate (“partially coated”) *closo*-alanes, in which only some of the cage $\ce{Al}$ atoms are protected by outer-sphere ligands, have been less studied and a few experimental and theoretical works dealing with this class of compounds have been published only recently.
Ab initio calculations for the family of stepwise hydrogenatated alane anions $\ce{Al_{13}H^-_{n}}$ have been performed [6–8]. Mass spectrometric studies and ab initio calculations have been carried out for the related series of isolated stepwise iodinated anions $\ce{Al_{13}I^-_{n}}$ [4, 9, 10]. The general tendencies predicted by computations and derived from measurements [6–10] turned out to be common for both families: spin density in radicals is mainly localized at the $\ce{Al^*}$ atom that is opposite the $\ce{Al-X(X=H, I)}$ bond; every even $\ce{X}$ atom is attached to the corresponding $\ce{Al^*}$ atom (in [6, 8], we called this tendency the trans-addition rule); the plot of the energy of successive addition of the $n$th $\ce{H}$ atom $D_n(\ce{Al^-_{13}-H})$ versus $n$ has a zigzag shape since the compounds with even $n$’s are considerably more stable than those with odd $n$’s; etc. It has been suggested [6, 7] that these tendencies are mainly determined by the specific features of the aluminum cage $\ce{Al_{13}}$ (whereas the specific features of the outer-sphere ligand are of secondary importance),

and the zigzag plot of $D_n(Al_{13}^{-}-H)$ versus $n$ has been interpreted in the framework of the molecular model of metal cage valence states, in which $[Al_{13}^{-}]$ is treated as a superatom with valence that varies from 1 to 12 due to the successive unpairing of six cage electron pairs [7].

The previous results dealt with hydrides and iodides. The present work is a continuation of the investigations in [6–8] and is aimed at studying the effect of the specific features of the outer-sphere ligand on the geometric, electronic, energy, and spectroscopic characteristics of the aluminum cage $Al_{13}$ by means of ab initio calculations of the inner structures of the $Al_{13}X^-$ and $Al_{13}X_2^-$ clusters for a wider range of substituents X = H, F, Cl, Br, OH, $NH_2$, $CH_2$, and $C_6H_5$. Here, we compare the properties of the Al–X bonds in the aluminides under consideration and in simple molecules of the $H_2AlX$ type; reveal the influence of the "cluster effect" on the Al–X moiety in the metal cluster $Al_{13}$; calculate the geometric, energy, and vibrational characteristics of the neutral analogues $Al_{13}X$; and consider radical changes in the structure of the $Al_{13}$ cage as it loses an electron.

As previously [6–8], geometry optimization and calculations of normal modes of all systems under consideration were performed in the B3LYP approximation [11, 12] with the 6-31G* basis set. The energies at stationary points were refined with the more extended 6-311+G* basis set for the geometries optimized at the B3LYP/6-31G* level.

$R(\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}-\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{t}})$ from $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}$ to the opposite atom $\mathrm{Al}_{t}$ of the cage change within the ranges 2.65-2.69 and 2.64-2.70 Å, respectively. The former is shortest for the hydride analogue and longest for the hydroxyl derivative, whereas the latter is shortest for the fluoride analogue and longest for the methyl derivative. The aluminum cage in $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{X}^{-}$is slightly contracted along the $C_{5}$ axis, and the $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}$ atom is shifted by only a few hundredths of an angstrom from the center along the $C_{5}$ axis in the opposite direction from the $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}-\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{X}}$ bond. The distances from the central atom to the other atoms of the cage remain virtually unaltered ($\sim$2.68Å for all substituents X). The distances between the neighboring atoms of the cage are within $\sim$2.79-2.84 Å and also depend slightly on the ligand type. In methyl- and phenylaluminides, the $R(\mathrm{Al}-\mathrm{C})$ distance is roughly the same ($\sim$2.00 Å).

The bidentate structure of symmetry $C_{2 v}$ with the outer ligand X sitting atop the midpoint of a cage edge has an imaginary frequency and corresponds to the top of the potential barrier to migration $(h_{\mathrm{migr}}(\mathrm{X}))$ of the substituent X from one vertex to another over an edge. The calculated barrier $h_{\mathrm{migr}}(\mathrm{X})$ is 0.7-0.9 eV for the electronegative substituents $\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{Hal}, \mathrm{OH}$, and $\mathrm{NH}_{2}$ and decreases to $\sim$0.4 eV for $\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{H}$. The latter barrier is low; thus, the $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{H}^{-}$anion turns out to be the most nonrigid among the aluminides under consideration with respect to migration of the H atom. For $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{H}^{-}$, an anomalous temperature dependence of the effective structure, NMR spectra, and other structure-related properties is expected, analogous to that observed for protonated closo-borane anions $\mathrm{B}_{n} \mathrm{H}_{n+1}^{-}$($n=6-12$, see [13] and references therein). Migration of electronegative substituents X of the Hal, $\mathrm{OH}, \mathrm{NH}_{2}$, etc., type is likely strongly hindered or prevented completely.

For the $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{X}^{-}$complexes with polyatomic groups X, the PES minima corresponding to the most favorable conformations were found and the possibility of large-amplitude nonrigid motions (internal rotation, inversion, etc.) of these groups was studied. According to our calculations, in the $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{OH}^{-}$complex (symmetry $C_{1}$ close to $C_{s}$), the hydroxyl group with the bond angle $\varphi(\mathrm{AlOH}) \sim 114.2^{\circ}$ slightly deviates from the plane passing through the Al-O bond and the midpoint of the $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{r}}-\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{r}}$ edge normal to this bond. In the $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{NH}_{2}^{-}$complex (symmetry $C_{1}$, also close to $C_{s}$), the nitrogen atom of the amino group retains its pyramidal configuration with the angle $\varphi(\mathrm{AlOH}) \sim 116.6^{\circ}$; however, the barrier to inversion of the amino group through the flat conformation is imperceptible ($\sim$0.6 kcal/mol). In the $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{5}^{-}$anion, the angle between the phenyl ring plane and the plane passing through the Al-C bond and the midpoint of the perpendicular $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{r}}-\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{r}}$ edge is $\sim 28^{\circ}$. All of the above substituents, including the methyl group, can rotate quasi-freely about the Al-O, Al-N, and Al-C axes with low rotation barriers (no more the $\sim$1 kcal/mol).

Analysis of $\mathrm{Al}_{13}$ cage vibrations and force constants in $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{X}^{-}$clusters shows that they depend only slightly on the nature of X and are roughly the same as in the bare $\mathrm{Al}_{13}^{-}$anion (frequency shifts never exceed $\sim$10-12 $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$). In going from $\mathrm{Al}_{13}^{-}$to $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{X}^{-}$, these frequencies tend to increase slightly (by a few percent) for the substituents $\mathrm{X}=$ $\mathrm{H}, \mathrm{Hal}, \mathrm{OH}$, and $\mathrm{NH}_{2}$ and to decrease to the same degree for $\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{CH}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{C}_{6} \mathrm{H}_{5}$. We may assume that the $\mathrm{Al}_{13}$ cage becomes slightly stronger when the former ligands are attached, and weaker when the latter substituents are attached.

The calculated force constants of Al-X stretching vibrations in $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{X}^{-}$aluminides rapidly increases along the series $\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{H}(\sim 2.08$ mdyn/Å)-Br (3.28)-Cl ($\sim$3.95)-F (8.09) as the electronegativity of X increases. In the same sequence, the Al-X bond dissociation energy increases: 2.14, 2.87, 3.37, and 4.88 eV, respectively (Table 2).

In the free icosahedral anion $\mathrm{Al}_{13}^{-}$, the central atom $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}$ has a positive Mulliken charge ($\sim$+0.30 e), while the peripheral atoms are negatively charged ($\sim$ -0.10 e). When X is added, electron density in the metal cage is polarized mainly on three "axial" atoms ($\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{X}}, \mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}$, and $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{t}}$) that lie on the $C_{5}$ axis. In particular, for the hydride $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{H}^{-}$, the effective charge $Z(\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}})$ increases to $\sim$0.40e, the axial atoms $\mathrm{H}$ and $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{H}}$ and their opposite $\mathrm{Al}_{t}$ atom become nearly electroneutral ($Z \sim -0.06 \mathrm{e}$), while the charges on the other atoms remain virtually unaltered. The dominant axial polarization in the centered icosahedral cage persists for all X ligands considered and presumably has a general character. It is natural that the polarization of the $\mathrm{Al}_{r}$ and $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}$ atoms increases with an increase in the electronegativity of X, while the charge on the opposite atom $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{t}}$ changes only by a few hundredths of an electron charge as compared to $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{H}^{-}$.

Analysis of the spin density $\rho(\mathrm{Al})$ distribution in the $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{X}^{-}$radical anions (Table 1a) shows that the unpaired electron is mainly localized at the $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{t}}$ atom, which is opposite the $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{X}}-\mathrm{X}$ bond (hereinafter, this "radical" atom is marked with an asterisk). The spin density value, $\rho(\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{t}}^{*}) \sim 0.64$, is virtually independent of the substituent nature for all X's under consideration, which points to the validity of the assumption [7] that the character of spin localization at the trans atom $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{t}}^{*}$ is determined by the specifics of the aluminum cage $\mathrm{Al}_{13}$ and should persist in $\mathrm{Al}_{13} \mathrm{X}^{-}$radical anions with different X's. This radical atom $\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{t}}^{*}$ is the most favorable site for attaching the second ligand X to form a linear axial moiety $\mathrm{X}-\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{X}}-\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{c}}-\mathrm{Al}_{\mathrm{X}}-\mathrm{X}$.

### Diamagnetic Anions $Al_{13}X_2^-$ with Two trans X Ligands

The calculations of the $Al_{13}X_2^-$ anions with two outer-sphere ligands support this conclusion. Indeed, for hydrogenated and halogenated alanes with X = H, F, Cl, or Br, the symmetric $trans$ isomer $D_{5\text{d}}$ is obviously preferable. For this structure, all vibrational frequencies are real, whereas the other coordination modes ($ortho$ and $meta$) are considerably less favorable, have imaginary frequencies, and do not correspond to local minima of the PES. This result is consistent with the trans-addition rule for even ligands (they are coordinated to the site where the unpaired electron is localized, i.e., to the $\text{Al}_\text{t}^*$ atom opposing the "odd" $\text{Al}_\text{X}–\text{X}$ bond) suggested in [4, 6, 7] for hydrides and iodides. Judging by the weak dependence of $\rho(\text{Al}_\text{t}^*)$ on X, the trans-addition rule has a general character and should be valid for a wide range of monatomic and polyatomic (not too large) ligands X.

Below, we restrict our consideration to the symmetric isomer $Al_{13}X_2^-$ with a linear axial moiety $\text{X}–\text{Al}_\text{X}–\text{Al}_\text{c}–\text{Al}_\text{X}–\text{X}$. The optimized structures of this isomer for different X’s are shown in Fig. 2, and its equilibrium geometric parameters are given in Table 1b. As can be seen, the addition of the second X ligand has little effect on the structure of the $\text{Al}_{13}$ cage (a small additional contraction along the $C_5$ axis as compared to $Al_{13}\text{X}^-$). ### Neutral Diamagnetic Clusters $Al_{13}X$ ($X = H, F, Cl, Br$)

Up to this point, we have considered aluminide anions, which have received more experimental attention than other ions. The data on the structure and properties of their neutral congeners are much scantier. The monohydride molecule $Al_{13}H$ has been studied best of all by using mass spectrometry [14] and ab initio calculations performed by several teams (see [15, 16] and references therein for details). Monohalides $Al_{13}X$ ($X = Cl, Br, I$) have been studied recently [10]. As far as we are aware, this is a complete list of the studied neutral aluminides $Al_{13}X_n$.

Most authors of the previous calculations of monohydride $Al_{13}H$ by different density functional theory methods arrived at the conclusion that energetically almost degenerate tridentate $(T)$ and bidentate $(B)$ structures, with the outer H atom sitting atop a face or an edge of a slightly flattened centered icosahedron $Al_{13}$ (Fig. 3), are the most favorable. The monodentate on-top coordination $(M)$ is considerably less favorable, and the structure with the H atom inside the icosahedron is unstable. In [10], tridentate, monodentate, and bidentate structures were suggested to be the most favorable for $Al_{13}H$, $Al_{13}Cl$, and $Al_{13}Br$, respectively.

In most of the previous works, the range of starting $Al_{13}X$ structures used as candidates for subsequent optimization presumably comprised only those configurations in which the distortion of the $Al_{13}$ cage was not too severe and it retained the shape of a centered icosahedron. It is clear that, for moderate distortions, the icosahedral model is appealing for its simplicity, elegance, suitability for modeling, and consistency with the physical jellium model [17]. At the same time, the calculations in [3, 6, 7] demonstrated that, in addition to such structures, the PESs for the $Al_{13}X_n^-$ anions can have even deeper local minima corresponding to isomers in which the shape of the $Al_{13}$ cage differs significantly from a centered icosahedron. In some of these isomers, the thirteenth Al atom is located outside of an empty $Al_{12}$ icosahedron, whereas in other isomers, some Al–Al bonds are broken so that large “windows” appear in the metal cage or the cage loses its closo form, etc. Therefore, here, in addition to “classical” coordination modes $M$, $B$, and $T$, a wider set of starting structures with a non-icosahedral $Al_{13}$ cage were optimized. As a result, we localized several new low-symmetry isomers energetically more favorable than the classical bi- and tridentate structures recommended in [10, 14–16]. The most interesting new $Al_{13}X$ isomers (together with $B$ and $T$) are shown in Fig. 3, and their geometric and energetic parameters are listed in Tables 3 and 4.

According to B3LYP/6-311+G*//B3LYP/6-31G*+ ZPE(B3LYP/6-31G*) calculations, the most favorable isomers among the $Al_{13}X$ molecules under consideration are two marquee isomers (1 and 2) in which X is coordinated to the top of the marquee, the $Al_c$ atom is in its center, and the “base” and the “roof” of the marquee are formed by five- and six-membered rings ($Al_5$ and $Al_6$), which are almost flat in 1 and corrugated in 2. A marquee isomer of type 1 was described in our previous work for doped aluminide $L@Al_{12}$ with heteroatoms L (see, e.g., structures 8 in Fig. 1 in [2] and 5a in Fig. 1 in [3]). In 1, the base is larger than the roof (the ratio of the atoms in the layers is $1:5:1:6$); conversely, in 2, the roof is larger than the base ($1:6:1:5$). Isomer 1 is the most favorable. Isomer 2 is ~0.2–0.3 eV less favorable than 1, but it remains energetically more preferable than icosahedral structures $B$ and $T$. For all analogues of $Al_{13}X$ with $X = Hal, OH, NH_2, CH_3$, and $C_6H_5$, which are more electronegative than hydrogen, the tridentate $(T)$ and bidentate $(B)$ structures are unfavorable, since they lie ~1.0–1.3 eV higher than marquee 1 and have several imaginary frequencies (as a rule, $N_{im}=3$ for $B$ and 2 for $T$). For the intermediate hydride $Al_{13}H$, both types of structures are close-lying isomers. Near $Al_{13}H$, an inversion of the relative stability of the marquee and icosahedral structures takes place with a change of the sign of $E_{rel}$.

A specific feature of marquee isomers 1 and 2 is that they have a "coordination vacancy" under the base of the marquee. Another specific feature of 1 and2 is the extremely low frequencies and force constants(on the order of a few $cm^{-1}$ and a few thousandths of mdyn/Å, respectively) of their torsional vibrations cor- responding to the counter-rotations of the five- and six- membered rings about the $Al_{c}-Al_{X}-X$ axis, which point to the quasi-free rotation of the marquee base and roof about this axis. For other substituents (X = Hal, OH, NH₂), the analogous decrease, while not so dramatic, constitutes hundreds of reciprocal centime-ters. Such a significant difference in $v_{str}$(AlX) between the clusters with terminal and bridging substituents can be used for IR spectroscopic identification of isomers of type 1 and T, more reliable than other experimental methods of investigation of aluminum clusters in the gas phase. In particular, vibrational spectroscopy turned out to be efficient for identification of isomers with terminal and bridging hydroxy groups in amino acid associates [18]. Vibrational spectroscopy can also be efficient for identification of icosahedral, inverted, outer, marquee, and other isomers of doped bare aluminides MAl₁₂ with M = B, C, Si, Ge, and others (see [2, 3] for details): for example, the vibration frequencies of the dopant M located inside the Al₁₂ icosahedron are considerably lower than the analogous frequencies for the inverted isomer in which the dopant is built in the MAl₁₁ cage and the twelfth Al atom is located inside the cage.

Thus, our calculations show that two structural motifs—icosahedral and marquee—compete in neutral clusters Al₁₃X. The former is important for condensed phases, in particular, for cluster assembling of aluminum nanoparticles and synthesis of the $\beta$ modification of crystalline aluminum [1]; however, for isolated Al₁₃X molecules, marquee isomers are often (at least, for electronegative X) more preferable. Therefore, in our opinion, the interpretation of mass spectrometric measurements and other experimental data for these molecules in the gas phase in the framework of less favorable (and, sometimes, unstable) icosahedral structures needs to be extended and revised.

The $E_{VST}$ energies for Al₁₃X (1) molecules with all X’s under consideration are approximately in the same range 1.3–1.6 eV as for anions Al₁₃⁻, Al₁₃X₂⁻, etc.; therefore, in the framework of the molecular model of valence states, the dependence of the energy of stepwise hydrogenation or halogenation $D_{n}$(Al–X) of the neutral cluster Al₁₃ on $n$ is expected to be zigzag but shifted by unity with respect to the Al₁₃⁻ anion: for the neutral systems, odd bonds should be stronger than even bonds.

It is also worth noting that polyatomic groups OH, NH₂, CH₃, etc., in marquee isomers 1 for the neutral Al₁₃X molecules, as well as for their anions, can rotate about Alₓ–X bonds with low barriers.

### Bare Clusters $Al_{13}$ and $Al_{13}^{+}$

In [10], a slightly distorted centered icosahedron (Fig. 5) was suggested to be the most favorable structure of the neutral cluster Al₁₃. However, the optimization and calculation of vibrational modes that we performed for several structures with different shapes of the metal cage demonstrate the existence of at least two Al₁₃ isomers nearly degenerate in energy: distorted icosahedron 3 ($C_{1}$) and marquee $1^{0}$ ($C_{1}$ ~ $C_{s}$, Fig. 5). The former (3) has four broken peripheral bonds and three “windows” (one pentagonal and two tetragonal). The latter ($1^{0}$) has the same numbers of atoms in the layers (1 : 5 : 1 : 6) and nearly the same geometric parameters as the monosubstituted neutral molecules Al₁₃X (1) (Fig. 3). When Al₁₃X (1) loses the on-top atom X, the Alₓ atom becomes a few tenths of an

angstrom closer to the slightly widened roof of the mar- quee, the other distortions of the aluminum cage being considerably smaller.

Analogous calculations (in the same B3LYP/6-311+G*+ ZPE(B3LYP/6-31G* approximation) show that, for the $Al_{13}^{+}$ cation, marquee isomer $1^{+}$ (Fig. 4) is the most favorable, whereas icosahedral structures (or structures resembling distorted icosahedra with different distor- tion modes) have imaginary frequencies, do not corre- spond to local minima of the PES, and, when optimized without symmetry constraints, transform to the less favorable (6 kcal/mol higher than $1^{+}$ ) marquee structure4 with the same six-membered base as in $1^{+}$ but with another roof configuration. The distortions of the mar- quee structure in going from the $Al_{13}$ radical to its ion $Al_{13}^{+}(1^{0} \to 1^{+})$ are rather moderate: the top atom $Al_{8}$  comes almost $0.35 \AA$ closer to the pentagonal roof and the horizontal edges of the roof and base of the marquee are slightly elongated by $0.01-0.03 \AA$ .
