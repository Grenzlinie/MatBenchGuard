# Deep Proton Insertion Assisted by Oxygen Vacancies for Long-Term Memory in $VO_2$ Synaptic Transistor

Chadol Oh, Inseo Kim, Jaeseoung Park, Yunkyu Park, Minseok Choi,* and Junwoo Son*

Reversible phase transformation of correlated oxides by field-driven ionic process present opportunity to efficiently transduce between ionic transfer and electrical currents in insertion-based reconfigurable transistors. However, the switching rate of insertion transistors is fundamentally limited by the slow rate of ionic insertion into the lattices of correlated oxides. Here, it is demonstrated that preformed oxygen vacancies in $VO_{2-\delta}$ lattices strongly accelerate proton insertion by low gate voltage in synaptic transistors. As the degree of oxygen deficiency $\delta$ increases in $VO_{2-\delta}$ transistors, the steepness of phase transformation and transconductance increase during the voltage sweep at the expense of the channel current modulation. Theoretical and experimental analyses reveal that the accelerated of $H^+$ kinetics in the $VO_{2-\delta}$ lattice occurs because immobile oxygen vacancies reduce the energy barrier to $H^+$ migration. In an electronic synapse, this facile $H^+$ migration in $VO_{2-\delta}$ lattices renders "inscribed" memory by positioning the $H^+$ neurotransmitter far from the electrolyte/$VO_{2-\delta}$ interface. This discovery suggests a strategy to improve the learning and memory processes of artificial synaptic devices by controlling the density of intrinsic defects in the lattice framework to achieve efficient ion exchange.

Reversible insertion and extraction of chemical ions into solid-state materials by an electric field offers a versatile method to dynamically control the chemical composition and subsequently a wide range of properties under an external electrochemical stimulus.$^{[1-10]}$ During the operation of external electric field, the reversible ions are inserted into a host materials, thereby changing the host's chemical stoichiometry$^{[3,4,7-9]}$ and even transforming one phase to another$^{[1,2,5,6,10]}$; this phase transformation can yield novel electronic and magnetic properties.$^{[2,3,5,6]}$ When the electric field is reversed, the ions are removed from the lattice, so the host returns to its original stoichiometry and physical properties.$^{[1]}$

Recent efforts have focused on the demonstration of insertion-based reconfigurable three-terminal switching devices for the application of sensor,$^{[9-11]}$ artificial muscle,$^{[12]}$ neuromorphic computer,$^{[4,7,8]}$ spintronics.$^{[5]}$ In particular, amplified conductance response of correlated oxides (e.g., $VO_2$) with charged ions leads to high efficiency of ion-electron coupling due to extreme sensitivity of the electronic phase near the metal-insulator transition$^{[3,6,11,13]}$; small gate voltage causes ionic transfer across the electrolytes/oxides interfaces to sensitively modulate the conductivity of the underlying channel layers with correlated oxides (e.g., $VO_2$).$^{[3,6,10,13,14]}$ Due to an analogy with synaptic weight update by release of charged neurotransmitters ($Ca^{2+}$ or $K^+$ ions) in biological synapse, insertion-based $VO_2$ transistors emulate synaptic plasticity by undergoing tunable conductance update by low presynaptic gate voltage in the host materials.$^{[3,6,8]}$

Oxygen ions and hydrogen ions (i.e., protons $H^+$) provide the most efficient means to induce reversible phase transformation by ionic insertion to modulate physical properties of $VO_2$. For example, either oxygen ions or $H^+$ can be driven selectively into transition metal oxide lattices by controlling the type of electrolyte$^{[3,6,10]}$ or the polarity of applied voltage.$^{[2]}$ Unlike the devices using transport of oxygen ion (via oxygen vacancies),$^{[2,3]}$ $H^+$ as an extrinsic defect can be externally inserted into a target $VO_2$ channels to alter conductivity and induce phase transition between insulating and metallic phase without disrupting the crystal lattice framework$^{[6,10,13]}$; this smallest and lightest ions can increase the switching speed at low voltage in insertion transistors.$^{[4,6,10,15]}$

However, the switching speed of an insertion transistor is fundamentally limited by the slow rates at which ions are inserted into and extracted from the $VO_2$ lattice.$^{[1,3,6]}$ To overcome the slow switching by $H^+$ insertion, in the previous study, the rate of proton-induced phase transformation was controlled by inducing planar defects that determine alignment$^{[16]}$ and density,$^{[17]}$ but these strategies could only be achieved within a narrow range of a control parameter (e.g., use of a specific substrate). Here, we demonstrate that oxygen vacancies in $VO_{2-\delta}$ channel layers increase the rate of $H^+$ insertion by an external electric field in proton transistors. In $VO_{2-\delta}$ transistors, an increase in the degree of oxygen deficiency drives increase in the abruptness of switching between insulating and metal phase, and higher transconductance during the voltage sweep at the expense of the lower channel-current modulation. Interestingly, oxygen vacancies that form during film growth lead to acceleration of $H^+$ injection and "deep" location of $H^+$ in the $VO_{2-\delta}$ channel layers. Theoretical and experimental analysis reveal that the accelerated $H^+$ kinetics in the $VO_{2-\delta}$ lattice with oxygen vacancies is attributed to the reduction in $H^+$ migration barrier. This facile $H^+$ kinetics in $VO_{2-\delta}$ lattices favors long-term memory in three-terminal electronic synapse by "deeply" inserting $H^+$ neurotransmitter from the electrolyte/$VO_{2-\delta}$ interfaces. Our finding suggests that learning and memory process of artificial synaptic devices could be enhanced by efficient ion-electron coupling by ionic exchange in a lattice framework that has a controlled concentration of intrinsic defects.

All-solid-state three-terminal transistors with different degrees $\delta$ of oxygen deficiency in $VO_{2-\delta}$ channels were designed to modulate the conductance controlled by electrochemically induced $H^+$ transport (Figure 1a,b). Prior to the device fabrication, high-quality 10 nm thick $(101)_R$-oriented epitaxial $VO_{2-\delta}$ films were grown on (101) $TiO_2$ substrates or on (1$\overline{1}$02) $Al_2O_3$ substrates by pulsed laser deposition (PLD).$^{[6,13]}$ All $VO_{2-\delta}$ films showed identical $(101)_R$ peaks, which represents $(101)_R$-faceted epitaxial films regardless of underlying substrates (Figure 1c). The degree of oxygen deficiency $(0<\delta1<\delta2)$ was controlled by varying the oxygen pressure ($p(O_2)=10,0.45$, and 0.25 mTorr) in PLD chamber during $VO_2$, $VO_{2-\delta1}$, and $VO_{2-\delta2}$ growth, respectively.$^{[3,19]}$ In symmetrical X-ray diffraction (XRD) results, the $(101)_R$ peaks of $VO_{2-\delta}$ films shifted to progressively lower scattering angles as $pO_2$ decreased; this trend occurs because the formation of oxygen vacancies causes expansion of out-of-plane lattice parameters (Figure 1c).$^{[3,19-21]}$ These results are consistent with vanadium valence state switching ($V^{4+}-V^{3+}$) by the formation of oxygen vacancies in $VO_{2-\delta}$.$^{[3,19-21]}$ In contrast, the full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) of the rocking curves $(=0.025^\circ)$ of the $(101)_R$ peaks and root-mean-square roughness of epitaxial $VO_{2-\delta}$ films did not change with $pO_2$ during growth (Figure 1d; and Figure S1, Supporting Information); these results confirm that the concentration of oxygen vacancies in $VO_2$ films was tuned during the growth without damaging the crystallinity of $VO_{2-\delta}$ films or increasing their surface roughness.$^{[13]}$

Systematic tuning of oxygen vacancy formation in $VO_{2-\delta}$ films was confirmed in electrical transport by monitoring change in resistivity (Figure 1e). $(101)_R$-oriented $VO_2$ films grown under $pO_2=10$ mTorr underwent typical temperature-dependent metal-insulator transition with four orders of magnitude decrease in resistivity above $T_{MI}\approx320$ K;$^{[13,14]}$ this result confirms the optimized growth of stoichiometric $VO_2$ films with minimizing the formation of oxygen vacancies. As oxygen vacancies are introduced to $VO_{2-\delta1}$ or $VO_{2-\delta2}$ films grown under lower $pO_2$, the insulating phase destabilized at $T<T_{MI}$, so resistivity modulation near $T_{MI}$ was progressively suppressed by two orders of magnitude in $VO_{2-\delta2}$ films.$^{[3,19-21]}$ Destabilization of the insulating phase in $VO_{2-\delta}$ can be explained by the weakening of $V-V$ dimerization by formation of oxygen vacancies at $T<T_{MI}$.$^{[20]}$

Then, three-terminal transistors with $(101)_R$-$VO_{2-\delta}$ channel layers were patterned using conventional photolithography (Figure 1a), as described previously.$^{[6,15]}$ A 300 nm thick porous silica $H^+$ electrolyte was interfaced with the $VO_{2-\delta}$ channel, and was used as both the $H^+$ reservoir and the electrolyte for voltage-triggered protonation. The $(101)_R$-oriented $VO_2$ channel layer in the transistor is chosen due to the fact that the facile pathway for $H^+$ diffusion (i.e., $[001]_R$ in $VO_2$) was aligned at $32.45^\circ$ to the facet direction (Figure 1b)$^{[13,22,23]}$; this inclined diffusion pathway facilitates $H^+$ injection along the direction of gate voltage: $H^+$ transport along the "easy" $[001]_R$ direction is likely to occur by jumps of $H^+$ in a helical path along the oxygen atoms.$^{[13,22]}$ Moreover, insulating channel layer could be stabilized in $(101)_R$-oriented $VO_2$ films at room temperature before $V_G$ application due to $T_{MI}\approx320$ K, which enable $H^+$-induced insulator-to-metal transition.

The transfer curves (i.e., source-drain current $I_{SD}$ under gate voltage sweep of $2.0\leq V_G\leq +2.0$ at $10$ mV s$^{-1}$ at room temperature) showed reversible counterclockwise hysteresis loops in all transistors with $VO_{2-\delta}$ channel; this result indicates that the conductance was increased by positive $V_G$ and decreased by negative $V_G$ (Figure 1f).$^{[2,6,10]}$ Interestingly, small $I_G$ peaks near $|V_G|=1.0$-$1.5$ V appear in $V_G$-$I_G$ curves (Figure S2b, Supporting Information); they are related to the transition from electrostatic doping by an electric double layer to electrochemical doping by a redox reaction of $H^+$ transport across the interface between electrolyte and $VO_{2-\delta}$.$^{[2,24]}$ $V_G$ at the $I_G$ peaks coincides with $V_G$ of the transconductance ($g_m=\partial I_{SD}/\partial V_G$) peaks (i.e., threshold voltage $V_{th}$ for the insulating-to-metal transition, and vice versa) (Figure 1g); this result confirms that reversible $H^+$ migration is coupled to the redox process (i.e., $VO_{2-\delta}\leftrightarrow H_xVO_{2-\delta}$) that result in reversible modulation of channel conductance.$^{[6,7,10,24]}$ Positive $V_G$ drives directional $H^+$ movement toward the $VO_{2-\delta}$ channels, and negative $V_G$ drives the movement away from the channels, so this $H^+$-induced switching in an $VO_{2-\delta}$ transistor is attributed to reversible transition between insulating $VO_{2-\delta}$ and metallic $H_xVO_{2-\delta}$ at room temperature.$^{[6,9,10]}$

More importantly, device characteristics strongly depend on $\delta$ in $VO_{2-\delta}$ channels. First, the $I_{SD}$ modulation declined as $\delta$ increased in the $VO_{2-\delta}$ films because of partial metallization (i.e., increased $I_{SD}$ due to electron doping by oxygen vacancies) of oxygen-deficient $VO_{2-\delta}$ films at room temperature$^{[3,19-21]}$ (Figure 1e; and Figure S2, Supporting Information). Despite the lowest degree of $I_{SD}$ modulation in the transistor with $VO_{2-\delta2}$ during gate sweep, the steepest change of $I_{SD}$ was observed at $V_{th}$ in both positive and negative-polarity voltage sweeps of the $VO_{2-\delta2}$ transistor (inset of Figure 1f); because of this phenomenon $g_m$ is highest at $V_{th}$ during voltage sweep in the $VO_{2-\delta2}$ transistor (i.e., $g_m=0.93$ mA V$^{-1}$ at $-0.87$ V and $0.34$ mA V$^{-1}$ at $1.18$ V in $VO_{2-\delta2}$-transistor, $g_m=0.13$ mA V$^{-1}$ at $-0.87$ V and $0.25$ mA V$^{-1}$ at $-1.81$ V in $VO_2$-transistor) (Figure 1g). Thus, oxygen vacancies facilitated the charge transfer and redox kinetics near the $V_{th}$ that are induced by $H^+$ injection in $VO_{2-\delta}$ channels.$^{[18]}$

In contrast, the EPSC in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistors showed larger EPSC peaks (0.48 $\mu$A for $\text{VO}_{2-\delta1}$ and 1.23 $\mu$A for $\text{VO}_{2-\delta2}$) than the $\text{VO}_{2}$-transistors, and the peaks did not decay to resting $I_{\text{post},0}$; this nonvolatility is equivalent to long-term plasticity (LTP). By changing the amplitude (Figure 2c) of $V_{\text{pre}}$ (0.5 V $\leq |V_{\text{pre}}| \leq$ 3.0 V), and its duration (20 ms $\leq t(V_{\text{pre}}) \leq$ 100 ms) (Figure S3, Supporting Information), the EPSC peaks and residual EPSCs were consistently higher in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistors than in $\text{VO}_{2}$- transistors. The increased peak and residual current of EPSC in oxygen-deficient $\text{VO}_{2-\delta2}$-transistors are consequences of the facilitated kinetics of $\text{H}^{+}$ insertion in the $\text{VO}_{2-\delta2}$ lattice with its oxygen vacancies.$^{[18,22]}$

To elucidate the increased $\text{H}^{+}$ kinetics in the $\text{VO}_{2-\delta2}$ channel layers by $V_{\text{G}}$, the chemical composition in localized area ($25 \times$ 25 $\mu\text{m}^2$, below the gate electrode) was compared in oxygen-deficient $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ (Figure 2d,e) and stoichiometric $\text{VO}_{2}$ (Figure 2f) channel layers before and after positive $V_{\text{G}}$ of + 2 V for 10 min, by using depth profiling of time-of-flight secondary ionic mass spectroscopy (ToF-SIMS). At first glance, the concentration of H ($^1$H) was greater in silica electrolyte than the background $^1$H concentration in $\text{Al}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ substrates (Figure S5a,b, Supporting Information); this observation confirms that porous silica contains sufficient $\text{H}^{+}$ as a reservoir.$^{[6,15]}$ When positive $V_{\text{G}}$ was applied along the gate, the oxygen/vanadium did not change (Figure 2d), which indicates that the application of $V_{\text{G}}$ does not influence oxygen stoichiometry in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channels$^{[2,6,9,15]}$. In contrast, application of positive $V_{\text{G}}$ caused increase in the intensity of the $^1$H signal along the thickness direction of $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channels (Figure 2e).

The acceleration of $\text{H}^{+}$ kinetics by oxygen vacancies in the $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ lattice could be readily seen by comparing ToF-SIMS intensity of $^1$H in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channels with that in stoichiometric $\text{VO}_{2}$ channels. After application of positive bias, the $^1$H signal in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channels increased along the thickness direction of the channels, but in stoichiometric $\text{VO}_{2}$ channels, it only increased near the interface between electrolyte and $\text{VO}_{2}$ channels (Figure 2f); this result confirms that $\text{H}^{+}$ insertion is strongly suppressed along the thickness direction, and that the $\text{H}^{+}$ is located in the "shallow" region from the interfaces in stoichiometric $\text{VO}_{2}$ channels; the inserted $\text{H}^{+}$ returns to the electrolytes after removal of electric pulse, thereby leading to the transient decay of the channel conductance (i.e., volatile STP in EPSC). As $\delta$ increases in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channels, the $\text{H}^{+}$ is incorporated into a much "deeper" region from the electrolyte/$\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ interfaces (Figure 2e); these results clearly demonstrate that the deep penetration of $\text{H}^{+}$ during application of $V_{\text{G}}$ is facilitated by preformed oxygen vacancies in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ layers; $\text{H}^{+}$ are inserted more deeply into the $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ lattice than into the $\text{VO}_{2}$ lattice, so the $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ lattice retains more residual (or trapped) $\text{H}^{+}$ than the $\text{VO}_{2}$ lattice does.$^{[6,9,15]}$

First-principles calculations were performed to identify the microscopic mechanism of the deep $\text{H}^{+}$ insertion into oxygen-deficient $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channels in the transistors. For this purpose, energy barriers for $\text{H}^{+}$ migration were evaluated by considering various paths through $\text{VO}_{2}$ and $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ (Figure 2f; and Figure S6, Supporting Information). The calculated energy barrier in stoichiometric $\text{VO}_{2}$ is 0.43 eV,$^{[22]}$ while the energy barrier in the oxygen deficient $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ was estimated to be 0.30 eV along tunnel 1 (H3 $\rightarrow$ H2, Figure 2h), 0.27 eV along tunnel 2 (H7 $\rightarrow$ H6, Figure 2i), 0.37 eV along tunnel 3 (H9 $\rightarrow$ H12, Figure 2j); i.e., the oxygen vacancies significantly reduces energy barriers for $\text{H}^{+}$ migration by as much as $\approx$37 %. Therefore, both theoretical and experimental characterization strongly support the experimental observation that the increase of oxygen vacancies accelerates the kinetics of electrochemical reduction by protonation and subsequent diffusion; this hypothesis is supported by the increase of $g_{\text{m}}$ and steeper change of $I_{\text{SD}}$ in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistors. Moreover, the transport of ionic defects (i.e., $\text{H}^{+}$ in our case) requires electron supply from source to maintain charge neutrality in ionic crystals; in terms of electron supply, the lower electrical resistivity (or higher carrier density) in $\text{VO}_{2-x}$ is more advantageous for $\text{H}^{+}$ transport in $\text{VO}_{2-x}$ that that in $\text{VO}_{2}$.

The effect of oxygen vacancies in the $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channel presents a resource that can be used to modulate the degree of nonvolatility to emulate paired-pulse facilitation (PPF), which is a dynamic increase in synaptic weight after two consecutive pulses (Figure 3a,b).$^{[7,24,27]}$ PPF is a neural facilitation phenomenon in which a second postsynaptic signal ($\text{A}_{2}$) is increased when the second pulse closely follows the first pulse. Two consecutive pulses ($|V_{\text{pre}}| =$ 1.5 V, $t(V_{\text{pre}}) =$ 20 ms) were applied to a presynaptic gate at a range of certain pulse intervals (0 s $< \Delta t <$ 3 s) to emulate PPF (Figure 3a). The paired-pulse facilitation index ($\text{PPF} = 100\% \times \text{A}_{2}/\text{A}_{1}$) increased with decrease in $\Delta t$ in all $\text{VO}_{2}$ transistors (Figure 3b). Interestingly, the largest PPF was achieved in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistors (Figure 3b); this result is attributed to increased retention of $\text{H}^{+}$ in deep regions from the electrolyte/$\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ interfaces.

The dependence of EPSC peak (i.e., $\Delta I_{\text{post}}$ ($=\Delta I_{\text{SD}}$)) on $\Delta t$ in PPF emulated post-tetanic potentiation (PTP), which can be exploited as a high-pass temporal filtering device in neuron circuits by using EPSCs recorded in response to stimulus trains that have different frequencies.$^{[7,24,27,28]}$ The stimulus train at each frequency consists of 20 pulses, each with amplitude 1.5 V and duration 20 ms. The EPSC amplitude gain (i.e., the ratio $\text{A}_{20}/\text{A}_{1}$ of the 20th EPSC peak value to the 1st EPSC peak value) increased remarkably with increasing frequency of the pulse train (5 $\rightarrow$ 40 Hz) in all $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistors (Figure 3c). The amplitude gain was amplified by increasing $\delta$ in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ channels (1.09 $\rightarrow$ 1.80 for $\text{VO}_{2}$-transistor, 1.36 $\rightarrow$ 4.47 for $\text{VO}_{2-\delta2}$-transistor) (Figure 3d); the increased amplitude gain in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistor is also attributed to accelerated $\text{H}^{+}$, so increased stability of $\text{H}^{+}$ binding causes increased retention of $\text{H}^{+}$ in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ lattice, which is the same origin of increased PPF in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistors. The different EPSC amplitude gains also modulated the cut-off frequency $f_{\text{cut}}$ at a certain threshold value (Figure 3d). By the change of $\text{H}^{+}$ kinetics upon oxygen deficiency, $f_{\text{cut}}$ could be decreased ($f_{\text{cut, VO}_{2}} >$ 40 Hz $\rightarrow f_{\text{cut, VO}_{2-\delta2}} =$ 18 Hz) at threshold value = 2.0.

Finally, seven-row $\times$ seven-column synaptic transistor arrays were used to visualize how the transition from STP to LTP influences the dynamic learning and forgetting process, $^{[24,25]}$ which is modulated by $H^{+}$ kinetics in the $VO_{2-\delta}$ lattices with different $\delta$ (Figure 4). Characters "V," "O," and "2" in the synapse array were input by applying 20, 60, and 20 consecutive pulses, respectively, with a pulse amplitude of 1.5 V, a pulse duration of 20 ms, and pulse frequency of 30 Hz. In $VO_{2}$-transistors with negligible number of oxygen vacancies, these consecutive pulses induced a temporary EPSC peak right at the last pulses (Figure 4a, green box), but all decayed within 4.5 s (Figure 4a, orange box). Consequently, stoichiometric $VO_{2}$ lattices in our electronic synapse achieved only short-term memory, and all characters were soon forgotten due to escape of neurotransmitters (i.e., $H^{+}$) that were captured shallowly by $V_{pre}$.

In contrast, $VO_{2-\delta2}$-transistors after training the letter "O" with 60 consecutive pulses yielded long-term EPSC that persisted for several tens of seconds (Figure 4c):, i.e., higher excitatory currents occurs in the pixels of the character "O" at row 4 and column 6, and at row 6 and column 4, than in the pixel at column 2 and row 7 (Figure 4d); Facile $H^{+}$ kinetics in $VO_{2-\delta2}$ lattices favors long-term memory by embedding $H^{+}$ neurotransmitter "deeply" from the electrolyte/$VO_{2-\delta2}$ interfaces. Thus, the symbols "V" and "2" quickly faded due to STP, but the "O" remained observable for 5 s after last pulse. Our array of synaptic transistors demonstrates that long-term memory in artificial synaptic devices could be controlled by influencing the diffusion kinetics and retention of externally-injected $H^{+}$ by controlling the intrinsic defect concentration in the $VO_{2-\delta}$ lattice.

The increased $H^{+}$ transport in $VO_{2-\delta}$-transistors leads to more efficient ion-electronic coupling that increases the abruptness of $H^{+}$-driven reversible metal-insulator transition and increases the transconductance near the threshold voltage of $H^{+}$ transistors. ## Experimental Section
The Epitaxial Growth and Characterization of (101) $_{R}$-Faceted $VO_{2-\delta}$
Thin Films: The 10 nm thick epitaxial $VO_{2-\delta}$ films were grown at $300\ ^{\circ}C$ on (101)-oriented $TiO_{2}$ substrates using pulsed laser deposition. A stoichiometric $V_{2}O_{5}$ (99.95 %, Sigma-Aldrich) pellet was sintered in air at $600\ ^{\circ}C$ for 6 h to prepare a ceramic target for laser ablation. The substrates were loaded into the PLD chamber, then it was evacuated to a base pressure of $\approx 10^{-6}$ Torr. Then, the rotating targets were ablated by focusing a KrF excimer laser $(\lambda=248\ nm)$ with a fluence of $1\ J\ cm^{-2}$

and a laser pulse frequency of 1 Hz. The degree of oxygen deficiency ($0 < \delta < \delta 2$) of $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ films was controlled by varying the oxygen pressure ($p(\text{O}_2)$ = 10, 0.45, and 0.25 mTorr) in PLD chamber during $\text{VO}_2$, $\text{VO}_{2-\delta 1}$, $\text{VO}_{2-\delta 2}$ growth, respectively. After growth, the samples were cooled to room temperature for 15 min. For structural characterization of $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ epitaxial films, high-resolution X-ray scattering measurement (symmetric $2\theta$-$\omega$ scans and $\omega$ scans) and X-ray reflectivity (XRR) measurement were conducted using HRXRD on an X-ray diffractometer (Bruker D8 discover). The surface morphology of the films was observed using an atomic force microscope (AFM, VEECO Dimension 3100). Sheet resistance as a function of temperature was measured in the van der Pauw geometry during heating from 300 to 350 K with square samples $(5 \times 5\ \text{mm}^2)$ and indium Ohmic contacts $(<1 \times 1\ \text{mm}^2)$ in the sample corners. The four-terminal resistances were measured using a 10 μA current.

Device Fabrication: To fabricate three-terminal synaptic transistors that use the 10 nm thick $\text{VO}_2$, $\text{VO}_{2-\delta 1}$, and $\text{VO}_{2-\delta 2}$ films as channel layers, the channels were first patterned with a channel dimension of $100\ \mu\text{m} \times 40\ \mu\text{m}$ (width × length) by photolithography (SUSS MicroTec.) and chemical etching with nitric acid. Then 30 nm thick Pt electrodes for source and drain contacts were deposited by radio-frequency (RF) magnetron sputtering with an RF power of 40 W, Ar pressure of 10 mTorr, and base pressure of $\approx 10^{-7}$ Torr. The thickness of Pt electrodes was determined using XRR measurement. Then, 300 nm thick porous sol-gel silica films were coated using a synthesized silica solution (tetraethyl orthosilicate, ethanol, water, and phosphoric acid (85 wt%) in molar ratio 1:18:5.55:0.02). The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 1 h, then placed in an oven at $60\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 2 h to accelerate polymerization of Si—O—Si chains; the solution was spin-coated on the sample at 3000 rpm for 30 s, then baked at $120\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 30 min. The coating process was repeated twice to ensure that the layer was continuous enough to minimize gate leakage current. Then 30 nm thick Pt electrodes for gate contact were deposited using RF magnetron sputtering and patterned using a lift-off process. Finally, to expose Ohmic contact for the source and drain, porous silica layers right above the Ohmic contact were patterned using photolithography, then etched for 45 s using a buffered oxide etchant.

Electrical Measurement of Three-Terminal Transistors: The electrical characteristics of three-terminal $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ transistors with different $\delta$ were measured using a semiconductor parameter analyzer (B1500A, Keysight) with source monitor units (SMU) and a waveform generator/fast measurement unit (WGFMU) in a probe station under ambient conditions at room temperature. Gate voltage $V_{\text{G}}$ (i.e., presynaptic bias $V_{\text{pre}}$ in the electronic synapse) was applied to the gate electrode, and source–drain current $I_{\text{SD}}$ (i.e., postsynaptic weight $I_{\text{post}}$ in electronic synapse) was measured by applying $V_{\text{SD}} = V_{\text{post}} = 0.1$ V. The transfer curves were obtained by applying a $V_{\text{G}}$ (i.e., $V_{\text{pre}}$) sweep with a range of $|V_{\text{G}}| < 2$ V at a sweep rate of $10\ \text{mV}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ at a constant $V_{\text{SD}} = V_{\text{post}} = 0.1$ V. To emulate the synaptic functions (i.e., short-term plasticity (STP), long-term plasticity (LTP), paired-pulse facilitation (PPF), and post-tetanic potentiation (PTP)) using $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ synaptic transistors, the WGFMU module was used to apply $V_{\text{G}}$ pulses with various pulse amplitude, pulse duration and pulse interval. The amplitude and duration of each voltage pulse were selected considering the purpose of each characterization. The corresponding channel current was monitored with small $V_{\text{SD}} = V_{\text{post}} = 0.1$ V.

ToF-SIMS Analysis: For time-of-flight secondary ionic mass spectrometer analysis to elucidate the chemical origin of the enhanced ionic-electronic coupling by $V_{\text{G}}$ in $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$-transistors with oxygen deficiency, three-terminal transistors were fabricated using an $\approx 80$ nm thick (101)$_R$-oriented $\text{VO}_{2}$ and $\text{VO}_{2-\delta}$ films grown on r-$\text{Al}_{2}\text{O}_{3}$ substrates. The chemical composition of the transistors were analyzed before and after positive $V_{\text{G}}$ of +2 V for 10 min by using ToF-SIMS (TOF.SIMS 5, IONTOF) at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST, Seoul, South Korea) The samples were bombarded with a beam of 3 keV $\text{Cs}^{+}$ ions at 3 kV, so a 30 nA ion beam was focused on an area of $200 \times 200\ \mu\text{m}^2$. The compositional analysis was taken by $\text{Bi}^{+}$ ion beam accelerated at 30 keV, so a 1-pA ion beam was focused on an area of $50 \times 50\ \mu\text{m}^2$; ToF-SIMS data were taken in a region of $25 \times 25\ \mu\text{m}^2$ (below the gate electrode) within this bombarded area. The depth profiles of H and O were obtained by analyzing $^1\text{H}$ and $^{18}\text{O}$, respectively.

DFT Calculation: First-principles calculations were conducted using the projector augmented-wave method as implemented in the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package code. The exchange-correlation functional constructed by the GGA-PBE scheme was used. A rotationally invariant +$U$ method was applied for the V 3$d$ states. The effective $U$ value was set to be 3.25 eV. To describe the electronic wave functions with a plane-wave basis set, an energy cutoff of 450 eV was used. The integrations over the Brillouin zone were conducted using a $\Gamma$-centered $4 \times 4 \times 3$ k-point grid. All the lattice parameters and atomic coordinates are fully optimized until the Hellmann–Feynman force was <0.01 eV $\text{\AA}^{-1}$.
