## ABSTRACT

The demand for lead-free dielectric capacitors with rapid charge-discharge ability and high energy storage density is increasing owing to the rapid development of electronic equipment. Lead-free $\text{Sr}_{1-x}(\text{Bi}_{0.5}\text{Li}_{0.5})_{x}\text{Ti}_{0.99}\text{Mn}_{0.01}\text{O}_{3}$ ($x = 0.02, 0.025, 0.03, 0.035$) thin films grown on $\text{Pt/Ti/SiO}_{2}/\text{Si}$ substrates were prepared by sol-gel method. A huge enhancement in polarization was found in $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-$\text{Li}^{+}$ co-doped $\text{SrTiO}_{3}$ thin films. The large lattice distortion and local broken-symmetry due to formation of $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-$\text{Li}^{+}$ ionic pairs are responsible for ferroelectricity and high polarization. The maximum polarization ($42.1\ \mu\text{C/cm}^{2}$) and largest energy storage density of $47.7\ \text{J/cm}^{3}$ at $3307\ \text{kV/cm}$ were both achieved in $\text{Sr}_{0.975}(\text{Bi}_{0.5}\text{Li}_{0.5})_{0.025}\text{Ti}_{0.99}\text{Mn}_{0.01}\text{O}_{3}$ thin film. Moreover, an excellent temperature-dependent stability was also obtained in $\text{Sr}_{0.975}(\text{Bi}_{0.5}\text{Li}_{0.5})_{0.025}\text{Ti}_{0.99}\text{Mn}_{0.01}\text{O}_{3}$ thin film from 30 to $110\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$.

## 1. Introduction

Recently, there has been an urgent need for higher power density and better charging-discharging rate devices to meet the exponential growth of power requirement in modern society [1–7]. Dielectric thin film capacitors possess some unique advantages such as better thermal stability, high energy storage density and faster charge-discharge speed [8,9]. Furthermore, the energy storage density and efficiency are the two key factors when evaluating the performance of energy storage dielectric capacitors. The recoverable energy storage density of dielectric materials can be theoretically estimated by using the formula [10–12]:

$$
W = \int_{0}^{P_{max}} EdP = W_{rec} + W_{loss} \tag{1}
$$

$$
W_{rec} = \int_{P_{r}}^{P_{max}} EdP \tag{2}
$$

$$
\eta = \frac{W_{rec}}{W} \times 100\% \tag{3}
$$

where $E$ is the applied electric field, $P$ is the induced dielectric polarization, $P_{max}$ is the maximum polarization, $P_{r}$ is the remnant polarization, $W_{rec}$ is the recoverable energy storage density, $W_{loss}$ is the energy dissipated and $\eta$ is the energy efficiency, respectively.
Considering the formulas above, in order to design film capacitors with high energy storage density and energy efficiency, the demands of low $P_{r}$, high breakdown strength and large $P_{max}$ should be satisfied synchronously. In recent years, the majority of studies have been focused on anti-ferroelectrics, such as $(\text{Pb}_{0.97}\text{La}_{0.02})(\text{Zr, Sn, Ti)O}_{3}$ anti-ferroelectric thick films [13], $(\text{Pb, La})(\text{Zr, Ti)O}_{3}$ thick films [14] and Pr-doped $\text{PbZrO}_{3}$ anti-ferroelectric thin films [15] due to their high saturation polarization, small $P_{r}$ and moderate breakdown strength. However, toxicity of Pb may cause environmental deterioration and human health hazards, thus there is an urgent need to develop lead-free film capacitors with high energy storage density [16]. Nowadays, $\text{SrTiO}_{3}$-based lead-free materials come to our sight, owing to their unique properties. Firstly, $\text{SrTiO}_{3}$ is a typical paraelectric material and has non-ferroelectric properties at room temperature [17]. Therefore, $\text{SrTiO}_{3}$ has a larger dielectric breakdown strength than other $\text{ABO}_{3}$ ferroelectrics. Moreover, $\text{SrTiO}_{3}$ hardly volatilizes during the preparation process which leads to fewer Sr vacancies. Thus, there is no obvious decrease in dielectric breakdown strength [18]. However, compared with ferroelectric dielectric materials, $\text{SrTiO}_{3}$ possess relatively low $P_{max}$ due to its non-ferroelectric characteristic at room temperature. It is well known that even rather low concentration of ionic dopants in $\text{SrTiO}_{3}$ can modify its properties [19,20]. In addition, previous

Moreover, a donor-acceptor ion co-doping route can lead to room-temperature ferroelectricity in rutile $TiO_2$ based on the previous report [24]. Accordingly, room-temperature ferroelectricity in thin films via A-site donor-acceptor co-doping may be achieved.

In this paper, $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ as donor-acceptor co-doped $SrTiO_3$ thin films was investigated. Our previous work has demonstrated that 1 mol. % Mn doping can decrease the leakage current and increase electrical breakdown strength (BDS) of $SrTiO_3$ thin film [10,25–28]. Herein, $Sr_{1-x}(Bi_{0.5}Li_{0.5})_xTi_{0.99}Mn_{0.01}O_3$ ($x=0.02, 0.025, 0.03, 0.035$) thin films were prepared by sol-gel method. The microstructure and ferroelectric properties of the thin films were systematically analyzed. Furthermore, the effect of $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ ionic pairs on ferroelectric properties was discussed by the first-principles calculation. The co-doped thin films show ferroelectricity, enhanced polarization and energy storage density. This work demonstrates that $Sr_{0.75}(Bi_{0.5}Li_{0.5})_{0.25}Ti_{0.99}Mn_{0.01}O_3$ thin film is a promising candidate for electrostatic energy storage application.

## 2. Experimental section

### 2.1. Material preparation

$Sr_{1-x}(Bi_{0.5}Li_{0.5})_xTi_{0.99}Mn_{0.01}O_3$ (SBLTM) ($x=0.02, 0.025, 0.03, 0.035$) thin films were prepared on the $Pt/Ti/SiO_2/Si$ substrate via sol-gel method using bismuth nitrate pentahydrate, strontium acetate, tetrabutyl titanate and manganese acetate as the raw materials. 2-Methoxyethanol and acetic acid were used as the solvents. The concentration of the precursor solutions was adjusted to 0.2 M. After 24 h aging treatment, SBLTM thin films with different $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ content were deposited on a $Pt/Ti/SiO_2/Si$ substrate through a multiple-step spin-coating procedure. They were spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 5 s and then 4000 rpm for 20 s. In order to avoid the formation of cracks, each wet film was pyrolyzed on a hotplate at $350\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 5 min repeatedly until the desired film thickness was achieved. Finally, the films were annealed at $650\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 5 min by rapid thermal annealing (RTA, the heating rate is $60\ ^{\circ}\text{C}/s$) in the air atmosphere.

The accurate diameter of circular Pt top electrodes ($250\ \mu\text{m}$ in diameter) was observed by metallographic microscope. ### 2.3. Modelling procedures

The first principle calculations based on the density functional theory (DFT) implemented in CASTEP code were performed on $Bi^{3+}$-$Li^{+}$ co-doped $SrTiO_{3}$ $3\times3\times3$ supercells. The ion-electron interaction is modeled by ultrasoft pseudopotentials of the Vanderbilt type [29]. Exchange-correlation functions are described by spin-polarized Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (GGA-PBE) [30]. The cutoff energy on the wave functions is taken as 500 eV and the wave functions were sampled on a $3\times3\times3$ k-points mesh constructed using the Monkhorst and Pack scheme [31].

$\text{SrTi}_{0.99}\text{Mn}_{0.01}\text{O}_3$, SBLTM thin films exhibit ferroelectric nature with significantly enhanced polarization and saturated hysteresis loops (Fig. S3 in Supporting Information). The enhanced polarization results from local polarization related to $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-$\text{Li}^+$ defect dipole (ionic pairs). In order to show the variation of the hysteresis loops intuitively, the relevant $P_{\text{max}}$ and $E_{\text{c}}$ values are also depicted in Fig. 2. The $P_{\text{max}}$ value increases as the doping content increases initially and decreases afterwards. It reaches the maximum value of $42.1\,\mu\text{C/cm}^2$ with 2.5 mol. % $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-$\text{Li}^+$ doping, which may due to the largest number of $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-$\text{Li}^+$ ionic pairs. However, no more ionic pairs form in the films with increasing doping content, which are limited to solid solubility. On the contrary, the $E_{\text{c}}$ value shows an opposite trend, which may due to the formation of built-in electric field along the [001] direction induced by $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-$\text{Li}^+$ ionic pairs. When applying an external electric field, the local self-polarization induced by built-in electric field will easily lead to reverse for domains and the value of $E_{\text{c}}$ then decreases. Due to the largest number of $\text{Bi}^{3+}$-$\text{Li}^+$ ionic pairs in SBLTM thin film with $x=0.25$, the effect of self-polarization is the most significant, which causes to the smallest $E_{\text{c}}$.

The distance $(Z_{2})$ between $Bi^{3+}$ and $Li^{+}$ is much smaller than $Z_{1}$ and $Z_{3}$ , which means that $Bi^{3+}$ and $Li^{+}$ are closer. The electron density contour map of $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ co-doped $SrTiO_{3}$ on the (100) plane is shown in Fig. 3(b). When $Sr^{2+}$ is occupied by $Li^{+}$ , the $Li^{+}$ site can be regarded as a center of negative charge due to valence difference. Ac- cordingly, the substituted $Bi^{3+}$ for $Sr^{2+}$ is a center of positive charge. The Coulomb force between the centers of negative and positive charge strengthens the chemical bond between $Bi^{3+}$ and $Li^{+}$ [32] and resultsin smaller distance of $Z_{2}$ . Based on the above discussion, the $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$  ionic pair produces an electric dipole moment $(P_{D})$ from $Li^{+}$ to $Bi^{3+}$ , accompanied with the local electrical field $(E_{D})$ . The $E_{D}$ leads to the displacements of cations and anions surrounding the ionic pair, re- sulting in large lattice distortion and local broken-symmetry as shown in Fig. 3(a). As a result, the displacements around the ionic pair induce the local polarization and ferroelectricity. In addition, a schematic diagram of $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ ionic pair distribution parallel to the [001] di rection is shown in Fig. 3(c). On one hand, large radius ion $(Sr^{2+})$ re placed by small radius ions $(Bi^{3+}$ and $Li^{+})$ will make the interplanar spacing smaller. One the other hand, the formation of ion pair between $Bi^{3+}$ and $Li^{+}$ also reduces the interplanar spacing based on the above discussion. The simulation analysis agrees well with the XRD results.

BDS is an essential performance parameter for the practical appli- cation as it determines the maximum power density. The Weibull dis- tribution of BDS for SBLTM thin films is shown in Fig. 4(a). According to the Weibull analysis (in Supporting Information), the BDS are 2682,2440,2344 and $2208 kV / cm$ , for SBLTM thin films doped with 2, 2.5, 3 and $3.5 ~mol. \% Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ , respectively. The results are shown in theinsert image of Fig. 4(a). BDS values decrease with the increase of $Bi^{3+}$  Li+ content, which may due to oxygen vacancies caused by volatili- zation of $Bi$ and $Li$ during film processing [25]. The leakage current density for SBLTM thin films is plotted against the applied electric field shown in Fig. 4(b). The leakage current density increases with doping content. As is known to all, the increase of the leakage current density leads to the BDS reduction of thin films, which is suitable for the results of present work.

For further intuitive comparison, Fig. 4(c) shows the $P$ - $E$ hysteresis loops measured at their critical electric field of SBLTM thin films. Ob- viously, 2.5 mol. \% $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ co-doped $SrTiO_{3}$ thin film exhibits the slimmest and tilted $P$ - $E$ hysteresis loops with the highest polarization.The $P_{ max }-P_{r}$ value of SBLTM $(x=0.025)$ thin film reaches $38.6 \mu C / cm^{2}$  shown in Fig. 4(d), which is much higher than that of other thin films. Therefore, the SBLTM thin film with $x=0.025$ has the largest energy density. Obviously, the recoverable energy-storage density $(W_{rec })$ in creases initially and declines afterwards as $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ content increases, which shows that excessive doping content causes degradation of en- ergy storage performance. The fluctuation of the energy storage effi- ciency shows the same tendency. As a result, an ultrahigh $W_{rec }$ value of $47.7 ~J / cm^{3}$ at $3307 kV / cm$ and high efficiency of $66.5 \%$ are achieved in Sro.97s(Bio.sLio.5)0.025Ti0.99Mno.0103 thin film simultaneously. The in- creased $W_{rec }$ of $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ co-doped $SrTiO_{3}$ thin films is attributed to the enhancement of $P_{ max }-P_{r}$ value induced by the $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ ionic pairs. In addition, appropriate $Mn$ doping can result in large BDS, which further enhances the $W_{rec }$ value.

Fig. 5 shows a comparison of the energy storage performance and efficiency obtained in this work with the ones reported previously[4,5,8,10,11,25,33-40]. Notably, it is hard to obtain the thin films with both high energy storage density and efficiency. In addition, relatively low $W_{rec }$ value is still a road-block among most lead-free films. How ever, a high $W_{rec }$ value of $47.7 ~J / cm^{3}$ and high efficiency (66.5\%) of Sro.975(Bio.sLio.5)0.025Ti0.99Mno.0103 thin film are obtained in present work. It is evident that the $Sr_{0.975}(Bi_{0.5} Li_{0.5})_{0.025} Ti_{0.99} Mn_{0.01} O_{3}$ thin film is superior to most of other systems, which makes it an excellent environmental friendly material for energy storage.

## 4. Conclusions
In conclusion, the significantly enhanced polarization and ferro-electricity in SBLTM thin films are attributed to the formation of $Bi^{3+}$  Lit ionic pairs. Our work shows that the ferroelectric behaviors can be designed through doping ions. Meanwhile, SBLTM thin films with dif- ferent $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ contents exhibit saturated $P$ - $E$ hysteresis loops. Furthermore, owing to $Bi^{3+}$ and $Li^{+}$ co-doping in $SrTiO_{3}$ , the value of $P_{ max }$ and $P_{ max }-P_{r}$ sharply increases and reaches to the maximum value of $42.1 \mu C / cm^{2}$ and $38.6 \mu C / cm^{2}$ in $2.5 ~mol$ \% $Bi^{3+}-Li^{+}$ co-doped SrTiO, thin film, respectively. Finally, a high energy storage density of $47.7 ~J / cm^{3}$ and high efficiency $(66.5 \%)$ are obtained simultaneously in SBLTM $(x=0.025)$ thin film under the electric field of $3307 kV / cm$ at room temperature. In addition, SBLTM $(x=0.025)$ thin film displays excellent thermal stability in the range of $30-110^{\circ} C$ . This study de monstrates that the SBLTM $(x=0.025)$ thin film is a promising lead free candidate for high-power energy storage devices.
