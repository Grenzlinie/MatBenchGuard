# Halopentacenes: Promising Candidates for Organic Semiconductors *

DU Gong-He(杜恭贺)¹, REN Zhao-Yu(任兆玉)¹**, GUO Ping(郭平)², ZHENG Ji-Ming(郑继明)¹

¹Institute of Photonics and Photon-Technology, Northwest University, Xi'an 710069
²Department of Physics, Northwest University, Xi'an 710069

(Received 4 November 2008)

We introduce polar substituents such as F, Cl, Br into pentacene to enhance the dissolubility in common organic solvents while retaining the high charge-carrier mobilities of pentacene. Geometric structures, dipole moments, frontier molecule orbits, ionization potentials and electron affinities, as well as reorganization energies of those molecules, and of pentacene for comparison, are successively calculated by density functional theory. The results indicate that halopentacenes have rather small reorganization energies (< 0.2 eV), and when the substituents are in position 2 or positions 2 and 9, they are polarity molecules. Thus we conjecture that they can easily be dissolved in common organic solvents, and are promising candidates for organic semiconductors.

PACS: 71.15.Mb, 61.82.Fk, 78.66.Li, 74.25.Jb, 34.70.+e

In recent decades, there has been significant progress in the development and design of organic-based electronic devices.[¹⁻³] Among commonly used organic semiconductors, pentacene, pentacene ($\text{C}_{22}\text{H}_{14}$) is one of the most promising materials due to its high charge-carrier mobility.[⁴⁻⁸] Some ramifications of pentacene are semiconductors with p-type characteristics,[⁹] while N and CN substitutions of pentacene are candidates for n-channel semiconductors.[¹⁰] However, there is still a problem, i.e. poor reproducibility of functions in pentacene-based electronic devices. The main reason for this is the polycrystalline nature of pentacene. These crystalline types have different crystalline phase arrays or structure orders, which are essential to the function of the devices. For example, the film phase of pentacene is more effective in delivering charges than its bulk phase.[¹¹] We can form the film phase by depositing a vapor phase on the base material, but it is costly, and takes a long-winded pumping time during vacuum sublimation from bulk to vapor. The cost of production can be reduced greatly by solvent treatment. However, bulk pentacene is insoluble in common solvents.

Therefore, a stable, reproducible organic semiconductor is needed, which has equal or even higher charge-carrier mobility than pentacene. Gratifyingly, if we introduce polar substituents such as F, Cl, and Br to pentacene, we find that halopentacenes, which have high charge carrier mobility, are more adaptable organic semiconductors because of their good dissolubility in common organic solvents. This enables them to be potential organic semiconducting candidates.

In this Letter, we have theoretically study a series of halopentacenes with one or two substituents as well as pentacene by density functional theory (DFT). The DFT calculation provides various properties of halopentacenes, including frontier molecule orbits, ionization potentials and electron affinities, as well as reorganization energies. Furthermore, we can gain some deep insights into the electronic properties of halopentacenes. It is found that narrow HOMO-LUMO gaps, delocalized $\pi$ bonds between C-C atoms and small reorganization energies enable them to be promising candidates for organic semiconductors. We find that halopentacenes are more adaptable organic semiconductors because of their higher dissolubility in common organic solvents.

Geometric optimization and vibrational frequencies of all molecules as well as their cations and anions were calculated at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level of DFT.[¹²⁻¹⁶] The geometric structures of neutral molecules were optimized under no constraints. Nearly planar structures were used as the initial states because pentacene has a planar configuration.[¹⁷,¹⁸] For HOMO-LUMO gaps, ionization potentials, electron affinities and reorganization energies, the more flexible valence-triple-$\zeta$ $6-311+G(d,p)$ basis set was employed to calculate single-point energies. The use of diffuse functions is known to be essential for accurate determination of the energetics, particularly for anions.[¹⁹] All the calculations were carried out with Gaussian03.[²⁰]

The polarity of a molecule is related to its equilibrium geometry, and can be easily scaled by its dipole moment. When the configuration is asymmetric, the center of molecular positive and negative charge is misaligned; the molecular electronic cloud is heterogeneous, so the dipole moment is non-zero. The greater the dipole moment of the molecule, the stronger the molecule polarity, and the more easily the molecule can be dissolved in organic solvents. The dipole moments of halopentacenes are displayed in Table 1. The dipole moments decrease with the substituents from F and Cl to Br, and one substituent is higher than the other two. However, if the substituents are in the positions 2 and 10, the dipole moments are very small, and the dipole moment of pentacene is zero, because of their high symmetry. When the substituents are in the position 2 or positions 2 and 9, the halopentacenes have the low symmetry and have measurable dipole moment. Thus we can conclude that when the substituents in halopentacenes at position 2 or positions 2 and 9 are polarity molecules, they can easily be dissolved in common organic solvents.

The electronic properties of the materials are determined by their electronic structure and, in particular, with their frontier molecule orbitals. The conducting property of the material directly is related to the HOMO-LUMO gaps.[21] As seen from Table 1, pentacene and halogen-substituted pentacenes have narrow HOMO-LUMO gaps, which are in good agreement with the experiments,[22,23] and the gaps from F and Cl to Br substitution become slightly smaller, which indicates that the electroconductibility of halogen-substituted pentacenes increases. This possibly depends in turn on the increasing metallicity of the elements. Figure 2 gives the frontier molecule orbitals the frontier molecule orbitals and the corresponding energies of pentacene and 2-floutin-pentacene. Their corresponding frontier molecule orbitals are very similar. This implies that their conducting properties should be alike. From the frontier molecule orbitals of halogen-substituted pentacenes, it can be seen that delocalized $\pi$ bonds between C-C atoms are formed, which are different from the $\sigma$ bonds of ordinary organic compounds. The delocalized $\pi$ bonds enhance their conducting properties and make it possible for them to be semiconductors.

Ionization potential (IP) is the energy difference between a neutral molecule and the corresponding cation, while the electron affinity (EA) is the energy difference between a neutral molecule and the corresponding anion. A low IP (or high EA) is good for hole (electron) injection/transport as a p(n)-type semiconductor in electronic devices. Table 1 lists the calculated vertical ionization potentials (VIP), adiabatic ionization potentials (AIP), as well as vertical electron affinities (VEA) and adiabatic electron affinites (AEA). The VIP of pentacene is smaller than the experimental data by about 0.3 eV, while its VEA is in good agreement. $^{[24,25]}$ As seen from Table 1, the differences of IP and EA between pentacene and its halogen substitutes are small. This indicates that the substituents from F to Cl and Br retain the good electric properties of pentacene. All the results imply that halopentacenes are possible organic semiconductors.

The charge-carrier mobility $k_{et}$ can be modeled by the classical Marcus electron transfer theory:$^{[26–28]}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
k_{et}=&(4\pi^{2}\Delta H_{ab}^{2}/h)(4\pi\lambda_{+/-}T)^{-1/2}\\
&\cdot\exp(-\lambda_{+/-}k_{B}T/4)\\
=&A\exp(-\lambda_{+/-}k_{B}T/4),
\end{aligned}\tag{1}
$$

where $\lambda_{+/-}$ is the reorganization energy, $\Delta H_{ab}$ is the electronic coupling matrix element, $k_{B}$ is Boltzmann's constant, and $h$ is Planck's constant. In this electron-transfer model, the parameters $\lambda_{+/-}$ and $\Delta H_{ab}$ are important for determining relative hole/electron transfer rate. $\Delta H_{ab}$ shows a rather narrow range of values.$^{[29,30]}$ Therefore, the reorganization energies $\lambda_{+}$ and $\lambda_{-}$ dominate the mobility of electrons and holes, which need to be small for efficient transport.

In Eq.(1), the reorganization energy $\lambda$ can be defined as shown in Fig. 3. The parameter $\lambda_{+}$ ($\lambda_{-}$) for hole (electron) transport is calculated as the sum of the energy required for reorganization of the vertically ionized neutral to the cation (anion) geometry, $\lambda_{2}$ ($\lambda_{4}$), plus the energy to reorganize the cation (anion) geometry back to the neutral equilibrium structure on the ground state potential energy surface, $\lambda_{1}$ ($\lambda_{3}$).$^{[18]}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{+}&=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\\
=&E_{0}(Q_{+})-E_{0}(Q_{0})+E_{+}(Q_{0})-E_{+}(Q_{+}),\\
\lambda_{-}&=\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}\\
=&E_{0}(Q_{-})-E_{0}(Q_{0})+E_{-}(Q_{0})-E_{-}(Q_{-}).
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
$$

It can be easily concluded that high charge-carrier mobilities are attributed largely to small reorganization energies $\lambda$ of materials. Small $\lambda$'s are a consequence of the completely delocalized nature of the frontier molecular orbitals that are spread out over the entire molecule so that ionization or capture for an electron does not lead to significant geometrical distortions.

The calculated reorganization energies of halogen substituted pentacenes are summarized in Table 2. The ratio $\lambda_{+}/\lambda_{-}$ has almost no difference between the substituents in the positions 2, 9 and positions 2, 10, so we only list the results of $P_{2\text{F}}(P_{2\text{Cl}}/P_{2\text{Br}})$. The $\lambda_{+}$ value of pentacene is in good agreements with previous calculations,$^{[18,31]}$ as well as with experimental estimates.$^{[32]}$ Remarkably, all the halogen substitutes have very small reorganization energies ($<0.2$ eV), which mean high charge-carrier mobilities. The high mobilities can be attributed to the delocalized $\pi$ bonds between C–C atoms. The delocalized $\pi$ bonds enlarge the scope of $\pi$-electron, thereby hencing the conductive properties of pentacene and their halogen substitutes. Further, it is noticed that $\lambda_+$ is smaller than $\lambda_-$, implying that the halogen substitutes have better hole transport abilities and they are promising candidates for p-channel semiconductors.

In summary, a series of halopentacenes containing one or two substituents have been investigated computationally to elucidate their potential as organic semiconducting materials. The corresponding frontier molecule orbitals of pentacene and its halogen substitutes are very similar, which implies that their electrical properties should be basically similar. We also find that a delocalized $\pi$ bond between C-C atoms is formed, which enhances the molecular conducting properties. Most remarkablyand the small reorganization energies point out that halopentacenes have as high charge-carrier mobilities as that of pentacene. By comparing dipole moments we find that when the substituents are in position 2 or positions 2 and 9, they are polarity molecules and can easily dissolve in common organic solvents, and are more promising candidates for organic semiconductors than pentacene.
