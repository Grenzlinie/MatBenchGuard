# THEORY OF ELECTRO-OPTIC EFFECT IN THE TETRAGONAL PHASE OF BaTiO3

D. KHATIB $^{(*)}$, B. JANNOT $(^{* *})$ and P. JULLIEN $(^{* *})$

(*) Departement de Physique, Faculté des Sciences, B.P. 28/S, Université Ibnou Zohr, Agadir, MAROC

(**) Laboratoire de Physique du Solide, Université de Bourgogne, BP 138, 21004 DIJON, FRANCE

(Received for Publication July 25, 1992)

**ABSTRACT:** The microscopic mechanism of electro-optic effect in tetragonal phase of $BaTiO_{3}$ is discussed taking account of the piezoelectric effect due to the lattice deformation and the spontaneous Kerr effect as to a cause of the to the strong local field acting in the constituant ions. It is found that the birefringence $\delta n$ and the linear electro-optic coefficients (Pockels constants) $r_{33}$ and $r_{13}$ obtained are in good agreement with the experimental data.

## INTRODUCTION.

Promissing applications in electro-optic and non linear optic have given rise to a steadily increasing interest in the electro-optic properties of $ABO_{3}$ perovskites as $BaTiO_{3}$. A lot experimental results $^{[1-8]}$ (see also included references) indicate that $BaTiO_{3}$ shows a remarquable optical anisotropy. In this work, we discuss quantitatively the electro-optic effect in $BaTiO_{3}$ from a microscopic point of view. The origin of this phenomenon is considered to arise from the spontaneous Kerr effect and the piezoelectric effect. In this paper we consider, in section 2, the microscopic origin of the electro-optic effect and in section 3, the results of piezoelectric contribution due to the lattice deformation. The Kerr effect contribution based upon a quantum approch is calculated in section 4. Finally, section 5 is devoted to some discussions about the mechanism of the electro-optic effect.

## ORIGIN OF ELECTRO-OPTIC EFFECT.

The mechanism of the electro-optic effect in $BaTiO_{3}$ is generally considered as the results of two main mechanisms $^{[9,10]}$:
(1) The change of the dipole-dipole interaction due to the lattice deformation caused by the piezoelectric effect when an external field is applied.
(2) The change of the electronic polarisabilities by the Kerr effect due to the

perturbation of the local field acting on the constituant ions when the sample is biased by an external electric field.

The variation of the induced optical polarization $P_i^{opt}$ induced by the electric field of the incident light in the i-direction due to the application of a biasing field $E_k^{bias}$ in the k-direction is given by the relation:

$$
\frac{dP_i^{opt}}{dE_k^{bias}} = \sum_j \frac{\partial\Delta_j}{\partial E_k^{bias}} \frac{\partial P_i^{opt}}{\partial\Delta_j} + \sum_\ell \frac{\partial\alpha(\ell)}{\partial E_k^{bias}} \frac{\partial P_i^{opt}}{\partial\alpha(\ell)} \tag{1}
$$

where $\Delta_j$ is the lattice deformation in the j-direction, $\alpha(\ell)$ represents the electronic polarizability of the $\ell$-type ion ($\ell = Ba, Ti, O_3, O_2$ and $O_1$), and the summation is extended on the three cartesian components of the lattice deformation labelled 1,2 and 3.

The two terms of the right-hand part of eq. (1) correspond respectively to the two contributions (1) and (2) above mentioned. In the following, we have considered only the case of biasing field polarized in the 3-direction (k = 3) in order to calculate the $r_{33}$ and $r_{13}$ coefficients. In this case, eq. (1) can be written as:

$$
\delta P_i^{opt} = \sum_j d_{3j} \frac{\partial P_i^{opt}}{\partial\Delta_j} E_3^{bias} + \sum_\ell \frac{\partial\alpha(\ell)}{\partial E_3^{bias}} \frac{\partial P_i^{opt}}{\partial\alpha(\ell)} E_3^{bias} \tag{2}
$$

where $\delta P_i^{opt}$ is the variation of the polarization $P_i^{opt}$ induced by the incident light caused by biasing field $E_3^{bias}$, and $d_{3j} = \frac{\partial\Delta_j}{\partial E_3^{bias}}$ is the piezoelectic coefficient.

The change of the refractive index $\delta n_i$ caused by the biasing field is given by:

$$
\delta n_i = \frac{\delta P_i^{opt}}{2\varepsilon_0 n_i E_i^{opt}} \tag{3}
$$

where $\varepsilon_0$ is the permittivity of the free space. The linear electro-optic coefficients $r_{ij}$ are defined as:

$$
\delta\left(\frac{1}{n_i^2}\right) = \sum_j r_{ij} E_j^{bias} \tag{4}
$$

From eq. (3) and eq. (4) we obtain the following expression for the coefficient $r_{i3}$:

$$
r_{i3} = - \frac{\delta P_i^{opt}}{\varepsilon_0 n_i^4 E_3^{bias} E_i^{opt}} \tag{5}
$$

RESULTS OF THE PIEZOELECTRIC EFFECT CALCULATIONS.

Considering the relation $P(\ell)=\alpha(\ell) E(\ell)$ where $P(\ell)$ is the electric dipole moment, $\alpha(\ell)$ the electronic polarizability and $E(\ell)$ the local field seen by the $\ell$-ion, and taking account of the Lorentz correction and further correction terms due to the lattice deformation $^{[11]}$, we obtain the following expression of the dipole moment $P(\ell)$ induced by the incident light in the 3 - direction:

$$
\begin{align*}
P(\mathrm{Ba}) &= 4.6821 \mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{opt}}\left(1-1.2861 \Delta_{1}-1.2861 \Delta_{2}-1.7360 \Delta_{3}\right) \\
P(\mathrm{Ti}) &= 0.6027 \mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{opt}}\left(1+0.1781 \Delta_{1}+0.1781 \Delta_{2}-6.4758 \Delta_{3}\right) \\
P(\mathrm{O} 3) &= 7.2778 \mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{opt}}\left(1-1.2317 \Delta_{1}-1.2317 \Delta_{2}-2.9943 \Delta_{3}\right) \quad (6) \\
P(\mathrm{O} 2) &= 5.8964 \mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{opt}}\left(1-1.1066 \Delta_{1}-1.1066 \Delta_{2}-2.0998 \Delta_{3}\right) \\
P(\mathrm{O} 1) &= 5.8964 \mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{opt}}\left(1-1.1066 \Delta_{1}-1.1066 \Delta_{2}-2.0998 \Delta_{3}\right)
\end{align*}
$$

In these relations, $\Delta_{1}, \Delta_{2}$ and $\Delta_{3}$ are the lattice deformations along the 1, 2 and 3 directions respectively . Their expressions for a biasing field along the 3-direction are:

$$
\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{2}=\frac{a}{a^{\prime}}-1 \text { and } \Delta_{3}=\frac{c}{a^{\prime}}-1
$$

In the same manner, when the biasing field is along the 2-direction:

$$
\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{3}=\frac{a}{a^{\prime}}-1 \text { and } \Delta_{2}=\frac{c}{a^{\prime}}-1
$$

For the numerical calculations, we have used the data reported in table 1. $a^{\prime}$ is equal to $(c a^{2})^{1 / 3}$.

The total electronic polarization induced by the incident light is therefore obtained by summation of the $P(\ell)$ divided by the volume of the distorded unit cell which is written: $v = V_0\ (1+\Delta_1)\ (1+\Delta_2)\ (1+\Delta_3)$. This gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(P_{3}^{\mathrm{opt}}\right)_{\text{piezo}} &= \frac{1}{v} \sum_{\ell} P(\ell) \\
&= 0.3802\ E_{3}^{\mathrm{opt}}\ (1-1.1466\Delta_1 -1.1466\Delta_2 -2.4051\Delta_3 )
\end{aligned} \tag{7}
$$

Then we obtain the first term of eq. (2) from eq (7) and the data of table 1 as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\delta P_{3}^{\mathrm{opt}}\right)_{\text{piezo}} &= \left(d_{31} \frac{\partial P_{3}^{\mathrm{opt}}}{\partial \Delta_{1}}+d_{32} \frac{\partial P_{3}^{\mathrm{opt}}}{\partial \Delta_{2}}+d_{33} \frac{\partial P_{3}^{\mathrm{opt}}}{\partial \Delta_{3}}\right) E_{3}^{\text{bias}} \\
&=-4.8194\ 10^{-12}\ E_{3}^{\mathrm{opt}} E_{3}^{\text{bias}} \quad \mathrm{C.m}^{-2}
\end{aligned} \tag{8}
$$

Substituting this value of $\delta P_{3}^{\text{opt}}$ into eq. (5), we get the contribution to the Pockels coefficients $r_{33}$ due to the change of the dipole interaction caused by the piezoelectric effect as:

$$
\left(r_{33}\right)_{\text{piezo}}=19.261\ 10^{-12} \quad \mathrm{mV}^{-1} \tag{9}
$$

The birefringence $(\delta n)_{\text{piezo}}$ corresponding to the piezoelectric effect is given by:

$$
(\delta n)_{\text{piezo}}=n_{3}-n_{1}=-0.0339 \tag{10}
$$

Since $(\delta P_{1}^{\text{opt}})_{\text{piezo}}=0$, the Pockels coefficients $r_{13}$ is equal to zero.

## RESULTS OF THE KERR EFFECT CALCULATIONS.

The spontaneous Kerr effect due the strong local field in $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ acting on the respective ions was discussed by Kinase $^{[17,18]}$ in the scope of a quantum microscopic model. Using the variational method, the modification of the electronic polarizabilities of the constituant ions due to the local field was calculated by determining the change of the Slater-Type Orbitals (S.T.O.) $^{[19]}$.
The electronic polarizabilities of the ions in the unit cell are expressed as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^{\|}(\mathrm{Ba}) &=1.9460\left(1-18.1785\ 10^{-15} E_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathrm{Ba})\right) \quad \mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{3} \\
\alpha^{\|}(\mathrm{Ti}) &=0.1859\left(1-17.1039\ 10^{-16} E_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathrm{Ti})\right) \quad \mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{3} \\
\alpha^{\|}\left(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{p}}\right) &=2.3940\left(1-8.1059\ 10^{-14} E_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}\left(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{p}}\right)\right) \quad \mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{3}
\end{aligned} \tag{11}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^{\perp}(\mathrm{Ba}) &=1.9460\left(1-6.0595\ 10^{-15} E_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathrm{Ba})\right) \quad \mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{3} \\
\alpha^{\perp}(\mathrm{Ti}) &=0.1859\left(1-5.6798\ 10^{-16} E_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathrm{Ti})\right) \quad \mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{3} \\
\alpha^{\perp}\left(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{p}}\right) &=2.3940\left(1-2.6865\ 10^{-14} E_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}\left(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{p}}\right)\right) \quad \mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{3}
\end{aligned} \tag{12}
$$

$(p=1,2,3)$
where the superscripts $\parallel$ and $\perp$ refer to the polarizabilities respectively parallel and perpendicular to the 3-axis, that is the direction of the spontaneous polarization. Eq. (11) and eq. (12) represent the non-linear and anisotropic effect of the electronic polarizability due to the local field, so called the spontaneous Kerr effect. The induced polarization resulting from incident light in 1- and 3- directions are:

$$
\left(\delta P_{1}^{\text {opt }}\right)_{\text {Kerr }}=-3.799710^{-12} E_{1}^{\text {opt }} E_{3}^{\text {bias }} \quad \text { C.m }^{-2} \tag{13}
$$

$$
\left(\delta P_{3}^{\text {opt }}\right)_{\text {Kerr }}=-11.39910^{-12} E_{3}^{\text {opt }} E_{3}^{\text {bias }} \quad \text { C.m }^{-2} \tag{14}
$$

By substituting eq (13) and eq (14) into eq. (5), the linear electrooptic coefficients $r_{13}$ and $r_{33}$, in the clamped state, are then respectively given by:

$$
\left(r_{13}\right)_{\text {Kerr }}^{\text {clamp }}=15.1810^{-12} \mathrm{mV}^{-1} \tag{15}
$$

$$
\left(r_{33}\right)_{\text {Kerr }}^{\text {clamp }}=41.90310^{-12} \mathrm{mV}^{-1} \tag{16}
$$

These two coefficients correspond to the clamped Pockels constants due to the spontaneous Kerr effect.

In the previous calculations we have neglected the effect of the lattice deformation by piezoelectric effect on the electronic polarizability of the ions. In other words, we have only considered the clamped state. We will now consider the free state.

The relation between the electro-optic coefficients and the corresponding dielectric constants is given by $^{[20]}$
$$
r_{m k}=f_{m k} \varepsilon_{0}\left(\varepsilon_{k}-1\right) \tag{17}
$$
where the $f_{m k}$ are the polarization-optic coefficients and $\varepsilon_{k}$ the principal dielectric constants. As for $BaTiO_{3} \varepsilon_{k}$ is very large, this relation shows that $r_{m k}$ is proportional to $\varepsilon_{k}$ and consequently, the ratio between $(r_{mk})\backslash A(free;Kerr)$ and $(r_{mk})_{Kerr}^{clamp}$ is equal to the ratio between $(\varepsilon_{k})^{free}$ and $(\varepsilon_{k})^{clamp}$. The $(r_{mk})_{Kerr}^{free}$ coefficients represent the true ferroelectric contribution to the Kerr effect. With the data of table 1, we obtain then:

$$
\left(r_{33}\right)_{\text {Kerr }}^{\text {free }}=64.58310^{-12} \mathrm{~m} . \mathrm{V}^{-1} \tag{18}
$$

The birefringence $(\delta n)_{Kerr}$ due to the Kerr effect is then:
$$
(\delta n)_{\text {Kerr }}=-0.0566 \tag{19}
$$

## SUMMARY AND DISCUSSION.

The total birefringence corresponding to the two effects is thus:

$$\delta n=(\delta n)_{\text{piezo}}+(\delta n)_{\text{Kerr}}=-0.0905 \tag{20}$$

We have measured the birefringence at room temperature versus the wavelength $^{[21]}$; the experimental value of $\delta n$ at $\lambda=6328 \mathring{A}$ is equal to $-0.1$, very close to the above calculated value.

By summing the two contributions $(r_{33})_{\text{piezo}}$ and $(r_{33})_{\text{Kerr}}^{\text{free}}$, we obtain the value of the free electro-optic coefficient:

$$\left(r_{33}\right)^{\text{free}}=\left(r_{33}\right)_{\text{piezo}}+\left(r_{33}\right)_{\text{Kerr}}^{\text{free}}=83.843\ 10^{-12}\ \text{m.V}^{-1} \tag{21}$$

The experimental data of the linear electro-optic coefficients at room temperature reported in table 1are in fairly good agreement with our calculated values. These theoretical calculations show that the experimental data can be mainly explained by considering the contribution of the spontaneous Kerr effect which contributes for about 80% to the electro-optic effect and 60 % to the birefringence.
