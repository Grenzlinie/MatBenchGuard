# Ferroelectric Phase Transition in $(NH_{4})_{2}BeF_{4}$
## Possibility of Molecular Distortion Type of Transition

By
P. K. BAJPAI

The possibility of molecular distortion type (MD-type) microscopic mechanism (recently proposed for $(NH_{4})_{2}SO_{4})$ and the role of $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion in the phase transition of amonium fluoberryllate are examined by analysing crystal structural data in both, the paraelectric and ferroelectric phases. The results show that $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion attains a dipole moment in the FE phase and contribute significantly to the spontaneous polarisation $(P_{s})$ . The computed value of $P_{s}$ due to $NH_{4}^{+}$ and $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ions $(0.21 \mu C/cm^{2})$ matches satisfactorily with the experimental results. The nature of the phase transition is found to be mainly of MD-Type. The soft mode of the transition is proposed as a mixed mode having the internal mode of $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion and translatory modes of $NH_{4}^{+}$ ions.

Die Möglichkeit von mikroskopischen Mechanismen vom molekularen Verzerrungstyp (MD-Typ)(die kürzlich für $(NH_{4})_{2}SO_{4}$ vorgeschlagen wurden) und die Rolle des $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ -Ions im Phasenübergang von Ammoniumfluoberylat werden durch die Analyse der Kristallstrukturdaten sowohl in der paraelektrischen als auch in der ferroelektrischen Phase untersucht. Die Ergebnisse zeigen, daß das $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ -Ion ein Dipolmoment in der FE-Phase besitzt und beträchtlich zur spontanen Polarisation $(P_{s})$ beiträgt. Der berechnete Wert von $P_{s}$ infolge der $NH_{4}^{+}$ - und $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ -Ionen $(0,21 \mu C/cm^{2})$ stimmt befriedigend mit den experimentellen Ergebnissen überein. Die Art des Phasenübergangs ist hauptsächlich vom MD-Typ. Es wird angenommen, daß die weiche Mode des Übergangs eine gemischte Mode ist, die aus der inneren Mode des $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ -Ions und den translatorischen Moden der $NH_{4}^{+}$ -Ionen besteht.

## 1. Introduction

Ammonium fluoberyllate (AFB) undergoes two successive phase transitions at 182.9 K $(T_{i})$ and $177.2 K(T_{c})$ , respectively [1]. While the intermediate phase is found to be incommensurate along the $a$-axis with a period very close to $2a$, the lowest temperature phase is ferroelectric with the spontaneous polarization $(P_{s})$ along the $b$-axis [2]. The ferroelectric phase transition exhibits several interesting properties. For example, its Curie-Weiss constant is rather low (19 K) [3] while the spontaneous strain in the ferroelectric phase and the change in birefringence at the transition temperature $(T_{c})$ are remarkably large [4]. The dielectric behaviour of AFB is also peculiar in comparison to usual proper ferroelectrics such as $BaTiO_{3}$. The dielectric anomaly is very small at $T_{c}$; the dielectric constant shows very weak temperature dependence and the Curie-Weiss law holds only in a narrow temperature region near $T_{c}$ [5]. Moreover, the spontaneous polarization is also very small $(0.22 \mu C/cm^{2})$ [6]. The system has therefore been widely studied using spectroscopic (infrared [8], Raman [9], NMR [10, 11]), diffraction [12], dielectric [13, 14], and electromechanical [1]) measurements as well as

some other techniques [15, 16]. However, the exact nature of the phase transition is still in doubt, several proposals have been made regarding the microscopic mechanism of the transition. On the basis of NMR studies O'Reilly and Tsang [10] suggested an order-disorder model involving the distorted $\text{NH}_4^+$ ions in the lattice.

This model suggests that one of the two non-equivalent $\text{NH}_4^+$ ions in the unit cell orders in the ferroelectric phase and that the crystal undergoes a order-disorder type phase transition. However, in deuterated AFB almost no change in the activation energies of the deuterated $\text{NH}_4^+$ ions is observed [15] and the $\text{NH}_4^+$ ions do not undergo cooperative reorientation [17] which is necessary for the order-disorder mechanism. Further, no change in the proton linewidth is observed at $T_{\text{c}}$ [18]. In another model, the strong H-bonding in the ferroelectric phase is attributed to the ferroelectricity. The two $\text{NH}_4^+$ ions, i.e. $\text{NH}_4^+(\text{I})$ and $\text{NH}_4^+(\text{II})$, take antiparallel up and down states along the polar $b$-axis and, assuming that they have different magnitudes of the dipole moment, result in ferroelectricity. Therefore a ferrielectric structure is suggested. However, no double hysteresis loop has been observed indicative of ferroelectric structure. The third suggestion was due to Jain [20]; on the basis of the analysis of IR intensity of the $v_1$ forbidden mode of $\text{BeF}_4^{2-}$ ion, they have suggested that $\text{BeF}_4^{2-}$ ion gets distorted and this distortion in the molecular unit should trigger the transition. However, no further work has been done in this direction and the phase transition mechanism is still not well understood.

It is interesting to note that ammonium sulphate (AS) also undergoes a struc- tural phase transition at 223 K and is isomorphic to AFB. Moreover both these com- pounds show several similar anomalies such as weak dielectric anomaly, low Curie- Weiss constant, large spontaneous strain, and both are classified as improper ferro- electrics [21].

For the last few years we have been working on the AS phase transition [22 to 24]. Our detailed and systematic studies have revealed that the transition in AS is of molecular distortion type. Accordingly, the distortion in $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ ion serves as the order parameter and the transition is triggered due to the change in symmetry and struc- ture of the molecular unit ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ ion), rather than in their position or orientation. Note that AFB is not only isomorphous to AS and exhibits a dielectric anomaly simi- lar to ammonium sulphate but also, like the $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ ion in AS [12], the $\text{BeF}_4^{2-}$ ion in AFB undergoes a rapid structural change in the neighbourhood of $T_{\text{c}}$ [20], the line- width and second moment in the NMR spectra of $\text{F}^{19}$ also exhibit significant change at $T_{\text{c}}$ [25]. The possibility of a molecular distortion type microscopic mechanism of the transition in AFB therefore seems plausible. In order to examine this possibility and the role of $\text{BeF}_4^{2-}$ ion in the phase transition, we have analyzed crystal structural data available in the literature both in paraelectric (PE) and ferroelectric (FE) phases [19]. In this paper, we report the computed values of the permanent dipole mo- ments associated with $\text{NH}_4^+(\text{I})$, $\text{NH}_4^+(\text{II})$, and $\text{FBe}_4^{2-}$ ions in $\text{PF}\ (+80\ ^\circ\text{C})$ and in $\text{FE}\ (-140\ ^\circ\text{C})$ and spontaneous polarization $(P_{\text{s}})$ in the FE phase $(-140\ ^\circ\text{C})$. Results show that the $\text{BeF}_4^{2-}$ ion attains a dipole moment in the FE phase and contributes significantly to the spontaneous polarization. On the basis of this analysis and other experimental and theoretical results available, a critical discussion on the possible mechanism of ferroelectricity in AFB is presented.

### 2. Analysis of Crystal Structural Data

Crystal structural data of AFB reveal four formula units in the unit cell with two types of $\text{NH}_4^+$ ions, viz. $\text{NH}_4^+(\text{I})$ and $\text{NH}_4^+(\text{II})$. Recently crystal structure data in both PE and FE phases are reported by Onodera and Shiozaki [19]. The lattice param-

eters are $a = 1.51048$, $b = 1.04821$, and $c = 0.59191$ nm in the FE phase. In order to get useful information from these data we calculate the polar distortion ($\delta \boldsymbol{r}$) in individual ions using

$$
\delta \boldsymbol{r} = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \delta \boldsymbol{r}_{i} = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{r=1}^{4} \left( \boldsymbol{B}_{i}^{r} - \boldsymbol{A}_{i} \right), \tag{1}
$$

where $\boldsymbol{B}_{i}^{r}$ and $\boldsymbol{A}_{i}$ are the components of position vectors, respectively, of $r$-th B atom and A atom of the $\text{AB}_{4}^{\pm n}$ ion, along the crystallographic axis. In calculating the distortion $\delta \boldsymbol{r}$ we have used the point charge model [20], which describes the electronic charge distribution for any molecular ion to a good approximation. In such a model both $\text{NH}_{4}^{+}$ and $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$ ions are represented as $\text{AB}_{4}^{\pm n}$ tetrahedron, with $n = 1$ for $\text{NH}_{4}^{+}$ and 2 for $\text{SO}_{4}^{2-}$ ion. Since these ions are not much distorted from their regular tetrahedron, the assumption should be valid for our case.

If $\delta \boldsymbol{r}$ is measured in $10^{-1}$ nm, the corresponding $\boldsymbol{p}$ (in Debye units) can be expressed by

$$
\boldsymbol{p} = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \boldsymbol{p}_{i} = \pm 1.2n \sum_{i=1}^{3} \delta \boldsymbol{r}_{i}, \tag{2}
$$

the spontaneous polarization $P_{\text{s}}$ ($\mu\text{C/cm}^{2}$) in ferroelectric phase can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\text{s}} &= \frac{1000}{3V} \times \boldsymbol{p}\ \mu\text{C/cm}^{2} \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^{3} \sum_{\alpha=1}^{3} p_{i}(\alpha),
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
$$

where the numbers 1, 2, and 3 (for$\alpha$) refer to $\text{NH}_{4}^{+}$(I), $\text{NH}_{4}^{+}$(II), and $\text{BeF}_{4}^{2-}$, respectively, and $V$ is the volume of unit cell ($-140\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$) in FE phase.

The dipole moment $\boldsymbol{p}(\alpha)$ and their components $\boldsymbol{p}_{a}(\alpha)$, $\boldsymbol{p}_{b}(\alpha)$, and $\boldsymbol{p}_{c}(\alpha)$ along the crystallographic axes $\boldsymbol{a}$, $\boldsymbol{b}$, and $\boldsymbol{c}$, respectively, are computed using (2) and (3) and

### 3. Discussion

The estimated results of spontaneous polarization $0.21 \mu \mathrm{C} / \mathrm{cm}^{2}$ matches quite satisfactorily with the experimentally observed value of $P_{\mathrm{s}}\left(0.22 \mu \mathrm{C} / \mathrm{cm}^{2}\right)$. However, it may be mentioned that the assumed point charge model represents only approximately the real charge distribution in the $\mathrm{AB}_{4}^{n \pm}$ type ions; so that our computed value may have a small error. The important point to be noted is that the $\mathrm{BeF}_{4}^{2-}$ ion which was not considered to contribute to ferroelectricity earlier also contribute to the total spontaneous polarization. It may however be mentioned that unlike AS, where the $\mathrm{SO}_{4}^{2-}$ ion contribution is maximum [20], in AFB the $\mathrm{BeF}_{4}^{2-}$ ion contribution is small. This may be correlated with the fact that the IR intensity of the $v_{1}$ mode of $\mathrm{BeF}_{4}^{2-}$ at $T_{c}$ does not change as drastically as that of the $v_{1}$ mode of $\mathrm{SO}_{4}^{2-}$ in AS [22]. This fact also indicates that the microscopic origin in the two transitions, though seeming to be similar, has some basic difference.

In AFB, the changes in the $\mathrm{NH}_{4}^{+}$and $\mathrm{BeF}_{4}^{2-}$ ions at $T_{c}$ are the main microscopic phenomenon responsible for the transition. AFB has three types of directed bonds; $\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{H}, \mathrm{N}-\mathrm{H} \ldots \mathrm{O}$, and $\mathrm{Be}-\mathrm{F}$, which could be equally responsible for the origin of driving interaction. However, the fact that the NMR spectrum of protons is not affected by the transition [17] and the shift in deuteration is not as large as observed for other hydrogen type systems [20] reveals that the main driving force should lie along the $\mathrm{Be}-\mathrm{F}$ bond. We note, however, that (i) the transition is of first order but very close to second order [6], (ii) the deuteration shift in $T_{c}$ has almost the same ratio as that of $\mathrm{NH}_{4}^{+}$and $\mathrm{ND}_{4}^{+}$ion masses [20], and (iii) $\mathrm{NH}_{4}^{+}$ion translations contribute significantly to the spontaneous polarization [4]. These facts indicate that translational motion of $\mathrm{NH}_{4}^{+}$ions should also be considered as contributing towards

ferroelectric behaviour. It can be therefore argued that a mixed mode having $NH_{4}^{+}$ ion translational motion and the $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion internal mode should be responsible for the transition. Such a mixing is possible through long-range interaction between $NH_{4}^{+}$ ion and $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion modes.

On the basis of the above discussion we suggest the following microscopic mechanism for the phase transition in AFB. Note that $NH_{4}^{+}$ and $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ions in the crystal are bound by intermolecular forces, while individual ions are guided mainly by intra- molecular forces. Since inter-molecular forces are more anharmonic than intra-mole- cular forces, with decreasing temperature the decrease in the volume of the crystal should be more than the decrease in the size of the individual ion. In the process ions will experience a force that tries to squeeze and deform them. Consequently, strain will develop on the ions. At $T_{c}$ , this strain may be sufficient to deform the $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion, which at $T_{c}$ will get distorted and attain permanent dipole moment. This dipole will couple with the already existing dipoles of $NH_{4}^{+}$ ions in order to attain the configura tion of minimum free energy. Since, Be-F bonds have $72 \%$ ionic character [9], the dynamics of the $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ions is governed mainly by the ionic forces. As these forcesare stronger than covalent forces, the developed strain will not only distort the $BeF_{4}^{2-}$  ion but may have sufficient energy to displace the $NH_{4}^{+}$ ions. Thus a subtle, rather than drastic, change in $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion should be observed in the vicinity of $T_{c}$ , which in fact is observed in IR intensity of the $v_{1}$ mode.

It may be also mentioned that the stored energy in $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion will be mainly of electrostatic nature, that means a electrostatic coupling even through non-polar internal modes of $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion and translational modes of $NH_{4}^{+}$ ion should be energetically more favourable than a dipole-dipole type coupling between $NH_{4}^{+}$ and $BeF_{4}^{-2}$  dipoles.

The effect of electric field biasing on $T_{c}$ can also be understood if we consider the stored energy in $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion as electrostatic in nature, since the electric field can induce some strain in the $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion and may trigger the transition at a higher tem perature if applied as biasing field. This seems to be the basic difference between the transition in AS and AFB.

From the above discussion the microscopic mechanism in AFB also seems mainly of molecular distortion type as in the case of AS. However, the main difference be- tween the two transitions lies in the fact that the Be-F bond in AFB is mainly ionic in nature while S-O bond in AS is covalent up to a large extent. This results in pheno- menological dissimilarities in two transitions. The presence of an intermediate in- commensurate phase in AFB may also be responsible for some dissimilarities. The soft mode of the transition should be a mixed mode of internal and translatory modes. However, in order to understand the transition clearly a detailed and systematic study of vibrational spectra and crystal structure data at different temperature is essential. Work in this direction is in progress.

### 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, the estimated spontaneous polarization in FE phase from all the ions present in the unit cell matches satisfactorily with the experimental results. The analy- sis of crystal structure data reveals that $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion gets distorted in the FE phase and is mainly responsible for the transition. Discussion on the microscopic mechanism of phase transition is presented by critically analysing the various results reported in the literature. Our analysis shows that the main driving force for the phase transition lies in the $BeF_{4}^{2-}$ ion. The internal modes of this ion may couple with the $NH_{4}^{+}$ ion translatory mode, resulting into a ferroelectric behaviour. Such a mixed mode should

be the soft mode of the transition. A comparison of AFB and AS transitions reveal that transition in AFB is also of molecular distortion type. The phenomenological difference between the two transitions is attributed to the different nature of Be-F and S-O bonds and the presence of an incommensurate phase in AFB.
