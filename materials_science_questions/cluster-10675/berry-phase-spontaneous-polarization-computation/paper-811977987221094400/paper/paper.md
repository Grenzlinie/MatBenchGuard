# Isomer Shift and Quadrupolar Splitting of the Mössbauer Spectrum of Ferroelectrics

By
Sh. Sh. BASHKIROV and V. N. LEBEDEV

The parameters of the Mössbauer spectrum of $Fe^{3+}$ ions in ferroelectrics are considered. It is shown that the internal electric fields in ferroelectrics cause the strong polarization of the covalency occupied $4 s$ shell of $Fe^{3+}$. This gives the significant contribution to the isomer shift and quadru polar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum. For $BaTiO_{3}$ , this contribution determines both sign and magnitude of the observed discontinuous change of the shift at $T_{c}$ . It is also shown that the structure reconstruction of $BaTiO_{3}$ decreases the isomer shift at the transition in the ferroelectric phase. The contribution of the electrically polarized $4 s$ shell of $Fe^{3+}$ ion to the quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum for $BaTiO_{3}$ gives more than a half of the observed value of the quadru polar splitting.

Рассмотрены параметры мессбауэровского спектра ионов $Fe^{3+}$ в сегнетоэлектриках. Показано, что внутренние электрические поля в сегнетоэлектриках вызывают сильную поляризацию ковалентно-заселенной $4 s$ оболочки иона $Fe^{3+}$. Это дает существенный вклад в изомерный сдвиг и квадрупольное расщепление мессбауэровского спектра. Для $BaTiO_{3}$ этот вклад определяет как знак, так и величину наблюдаемого при $T_{c}$ скачка сдвига. Также показано, что структурная перестройка титаната бария уменьшает изомерный сдвиг при переходе в сегнетоэлектрическую фазу. Вклад электрически поляризованной $4 s$ оболочки иона $Fe^{3+}$ в квадрупольное расщепление мессбауэровского спектра для $BaTiO_{3}$ составляет больше половины наблюдаемого значения квадрупольного расщепления.

## 1. Introduction

Mössbauer effect has been used as an effective tool for studying the microscopic prop- erties of ferroelectric crystals [1 to 6]. The Mössbauer spectroscopy has been applied to investigate the crystal structure $[1,4$ to 6 $]$ , the character of the chemical bond $[1,3$ to 5 $]$ , and the phonon spectrum $[1,2,4,5]$ of ferroelectrics. However, so far the influence of the strong internal field on the Mössbauer parameters of ferroelectrics has been not investigated.

In this paper we consider the influence of the internal electric field on the isomer shift (Section 2) and the quadrupolar splitting (Section 3) of the Mössbauer spectrum of $Fe^{3+}$ ions in ferroelectrics. The theory is applied to the classical ferroelectric $BaTiO_{3}$ .

## 2. Isomer Shift in Ferroelectrics

Bhide and Multani [1] found that the shift of the Mössbauer spectrum of $Fe^{3+}$-doped $BaTiO_{3}$ undergoes in the region of the ferroelectric phase transition point a discontinu ous change. They supposed that this discontinuous change is caused by the structure reconstruction of $BaTiO_{3}$ at $T_{c}$ . Below we shall show the impossibility of such a sup position since the change of the isomer shift which is caused by the structure recon- struction of $BaTiO_{3}$ has the sign contrary to the observed one.

Barium titanate in the paraelectric phase possesses a perovskite structure with cubic unit cell. It changes to the ferroelectric phase at $T_{c}=107^{\circ} C$ [7]; the transition

is accompanied by a displacement of the ions along the ferroelectric $c$-axis. From $5\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ to $T_{\mathrm{c}}$, $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ has a tetrahedral structure.

The structure transition of $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ from the tetrahedral phase to the cubic phase changes the electronic density at the $\mathrm{Fe}^{57}$ nucleus because the chemical bond between the $\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ ion and the ligands is changed. The main contribution to the electronic density at the iron nucleus is caused by the covalency transfer of the electronic charge from the ligands to the empty $4s$ shell and the overlap of the internal $ns$ shells with ligand orbits [8]. For the octahedral environment of the iron ion, this contribution has the form [9]

$$
\Delta \varrho(0)=12 N^{2}\left\{-\sum_{n=1}^{3} S_{n s} \varphi_{n s}(0)+C_{4 s} \varphi_{4 s}(0)\right\}^{2},
\tag{1}
$$

where $\varphi_{n s}(0)$ are the values of the $ns$ electronic wave functions of the iron ion at the nucleus, $S_{n s}=\left\langle\varphi_{n s} \mid 2 p_{z}\right\rangle$ are the overlap integrals between the $\varphi_{n s}$ function and the $2 p_{z}$ ligand wave function, $N^{2}=\left(1+12 C_{4 s} S_{4 s}+6 C_{4 s}^{2}-6 \sum_{n=1}^{3} S_{n s}^{2}\right)^{-1}$ is the normali zation constant, $C_{4 \mathrm{~s}}$ is the transfer $2 p_{z} \rightarrow \varphi_{4 \mathrm{~s}}$ parameter,

$$
C_{4 \mathrm{~s}}=\frac{\varrho_{4 \mathrm{~s}}}{12 N^{2}},
\tag{2}
$$

where $\varrho_{4 \mathrm{~s}}$ is the occupation of the $4s$ shell.

The calculations of the covalency contribution to the electronic density at the $\mathrm{Fe}^{57}$ nucleus, $\Delta \varrho(0)$, in both phases of $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ near $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ were performed for all possible occu pations of the $4s$ shell (from 0 to 2). The structure data near $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ were adopted from $[10,11]: \Delta Z_{\mathrm{Ba}}=0.000, \Delta Z_{\mathrm{Ti}}=0.011, \Delta Z_{\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{I}}}=-0.013, \Delta Z_{\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{II}}}=-0.012, a=4.003 \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$, $c=4.026 \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ in the ferroelectric phase at $105\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$; $a=c=4.011 \mathring{\mathrm{A}}$ in the paraelectric phase. The overlap integrals, $S_{n s}$, were computed by using the $\alpha$-function technique [12] and the radial parts of the wave functions of $\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ and $\mathrm{O}^{2-}$ ions from [13, 14]. The radial dependence of the transfer parameter, $C_{4 \mathrm{~s}}$, is characterized by the corresponding overlap integrals, $S_{4 s}$ [9].

Calculations show that for all values of $\varrho_{4 \mathrm{~s}}$ the structure reconstruction of $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ increases the electronic density at the iron nucleus at the transition to the ferroelectric phase. For the usually observed value $\varrho_{4 \mathrm{~s}}=0.2$ [9], this increase is $0.029 a_{0}^{3}$. The change of the isomer shift of the Mössbauer spectrum, $\Delta \delta_{i}\ \ (\Delta \delta_{i}=\alpha\ \Delta \varrho(0)$, where $\alpha=$ $=-0.23 a_{0}^{-3}\ \mathrm{~mm} / \mathrm{s}$ [15] is the isomer shift calibrating constant for the $\mathrm{Fe}^{57}$ nucleus), corresponding to the above change of the electronic density at the $\mathrm{Fe}^{57}$ nucleus is $-0.007\ \mathrm{~mm} / \mathrm{s}$. The observed discontinuous change of the shift at the transition of $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ to the ferroelectric phase is about $+0.06\ \mathrm{~mm} / \mathrm{s}$ and, therefore, cannot be explained by the structure reconstruction.

In order to explain the observed behaviour of the isomer shift, let us consider the $\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ ion in a constant electric field, $E$, applied parallel to the $Z$-axis. The perturbation operator is

$$
\hat{V}=-e Z E.
\tag{3}
$$

In a first approximation the operator (3) does not mix $s$-states with the valence $3d$ shell of $\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$. Hence, the value of the electronic density at the nucleus of the free iron ion does not change under the influence of the electric field. However, the covalency effects lead to an essential transfer of electronic charge from ligands to empty states of $\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ [16]. In particular, a certain occupation of the $4s$ shell will take place. The elec tric polarization of the $4s$ wave function will change the electronic density at the iron nucleus and, hence, the isomer shift of the Mössbauer spectrum.

Since the 4p level is nearest to the 4s state, the 4s wave function polarized in the electric field, $\varPsi_{4s}(E)$, may be written as

$$
\varPsi_{4s}(E) = a(E) \varphi_{4s} + b(E) \varphi_{4p}, \tag{4}
$$

where $\varphi_{4s}$ and $\varphi_{4p}$ are the wave functions of the free iron ion. Using the perturbation theory, one obtains the following expressions for the coefficients $a(E)$ and $b(E)$:

$$
a(E) = \left[ \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2 \left(1 + (2e B/\Delta \varepsilon)^2 E^2\right)^{1/2}} \right]^{1/2}, \tag{5}
$$

$$
b(E) = [1 - a^2(E)]^{1/2}, \tag{6}
$$

where

$$
B = \langle \varphi_{4s}| Z |\varphi_{4p} \rangle, \tag{7}
$$

$\Delta \varepsilon = \varepsilon_{4s}^0 - \varepsilon_{4p}^0$, where $\varepsilon_{4s}^0$ and $\varepsilon_{4p}^0$ are the energies of the 4s and 4p free ion states, respectively. Substituting (4) in (1) yields

$$
\Delta \varrho(E) = 12N^2 \left\{ a^2(E) C_{4s}^2 \varphi_{4s}^2(0) - 2a(E) C_{4s}\varphi_{4s}(0) \sum_{n=1}^{3} S_{ns}\varphi_{ns}(0) \right\}. \tag{8}
$$

In equation (8), we retained the terms which depend on the electric field. It is seen from this equation and expression (5) for $a(E)$ that the increase of the electric field intensity decreases the electronic density at the iron nucleus and, hence, increases the isomer shift of the Mössbauer spectrum.

Using the wave functions and energies of the free iron ion states from [14], we obtain $(2eB/\Delta \varepsilon)^2 = 6.6 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V}^2$. Thus, an appreciable polarization of the $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ 4s state will occur in an electric field of the order of $10^8$ to $10^9\ \text{V/cm}$. It is substantial that such intensities are typical for the internal electric field in ferroelectrics [17]. Let us consider again $\text{BaTiO}_3$.

The spontaneous polarization and internal electric field in $\text{BaTiO}_3$ undergo at $T_c$ a discontinuous change [7]. The discontinuous change of the electric field on the $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ion is $4.5 \times 10^8\ \text{V/cm}$ [11]. The calculations of $\Delta \varrho(E)$ at $E = 0$ and $4.5 \times 10^8\ \text{V/cm}$ give a decrease of the electronic density at the $\text{Fe}^{57}$ nucleus at the transition of $\text{BaTiO}_3$ to the ferroelectric phase of $0.4493a_0^3$. This value corresponds to a discontinuous change of the isomer shift at $T_c$ of $+0.103\ \text{mm/s}$ at the transition to the ferroelectric phase. Thus, the polarization of the $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ 4s shell in the internal electric field determines in $\text{BaTiO}_3$ both sign and magnitude of the observed discontinuous change of the shift of the Mössbauer spectrum at $T_c$.

The above results allow us to understand also why the authors of [18] have obtained the wrong sign of the isomer shift calibrating constant for the $\text{Sn}^{119}$ nucleus. In [18], the discontinuous change of the shift of the Mössbauer spectrum observed at $T_c$ for the $\text{Sn}^{4+}$ ions in $\text{BaTiO}_3$ has, as well as in [1], been explained by the assumption that the electronic density at the $\text{Sn}^{119}$ nucleus is decreased at the transition to the ferroelectric phase. However, as we have shown above, the observed sign of the discontinuous change of the electronic density at the iron ion nucleus is caused by the electric polarization of the Fe 4s shell. For $\text{Sn}^{119}$ ions, the influence of the internal electric field on the electronic density at the nucleus is expected to be less than that for $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ions. Thus, the main contribution to the change of the electronic density at the $\text{Sn}^{119}$ nucleus in $\text{BaTiO}_3$ will be caused by the structure reconstruction at $T_c$. Taking into consideration the above obtained result that the structure reconstruction of barium titanate at $T_c$ increases the electronic density at the nucleus at the transition to the ferroelectric phase, we obtain the correct sign of the isomer shift calibrating constant for the $\text{Sn}^{119}$ nucleus: $\alpha_{\text{Sn}^{119}} > 0$.

### 3. Quadrupolar Splitting in Ferroelectrics

The polarization of the 4s shell in the electric field gives also a contribution to the electric field gradient (EFG) at the Fe⁵⁷ nucleus. Indeed, the average of the EFG operator, $\hat{q} = -(3 \cos^2 Q - 1)/r^3$, over the wave function $\varPsi_{4\text{s}}(E)$ (4) differs from zero and is

$$
\langle \varPsi_{4\text{s}}(E) | \hat{q} | \varPsi_{4\text{s}}(E) \rangle = -\frac{4}{5} b^2(E) \langle r^{-3} \rangle_{4\text{p}} . \tag{9}
$$

Then, for the contribution of the electrically polarized 4s shell to the quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum, $\Delta W_Q(E)$, we have

$$
\Delta W_Q(E) = -\frac{2}{5} e^2 Q \varrho_{4\text{s}} b^2(E) \left(1 - R_{4\text{p}}\right) \langle r^{-3} \rangle_{4\text{p}} , \tag{10}
$$

where $Q = +0.21b$ [19] is the quadrupolar moment of the Fe⁵⁷ nucleus, $R_{4\text{p}}$ is the screening factor for the 4p shell.

Using the free iron ion wave functions from [14], we obtain $\langle r^{-3} \rangle_{4\text{p}} = 6.2$ at. units. So, though the 4p shell is removed considerable from the iron nucleus, the $\langle r^{-3} \rangle_{4\text{p}}$ value is even larger than that for the 3d shell ($\langle r^{-3} \rangle_{3\text{d}} = 5.73$ at. units [20]). This is not surprising, since the radial wave functions change near a nucleus as $r^{l+1}$, where $l$ is the orbital quantum number.

Since there are no data in literature about the $R_{4\text{p}}$ value, for the evaluation of $\Delta W_Q(E)$ we use the screening factor for the 3d shell of Fe³⁺: $R_{3\text{d}} = 0.25$ [21]. Then, at $\varrho_{4\text{s}} = 0.2$ and $E \approx 5 \times 10^8$ V/cm (a value which is typical for the internal electric field in ferroelectrics [17]), one obtains from equation (10) $\Delta W_Q(E) \approx 0.3$ mm/s. Hence, the influence of the internal electric field in ferroelectrics on the quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum may be significant. Let us consider in detail BaTiO₃.

The quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum of Fe³⁺-doped BaTiO₃ has been observed in the ferroelectric phase [1]. Above the Curie temperature, the EFG at the Fe⁵⁷ nucleus vanishes. This corresponds to the transition of BaTiO₃ into the cubic phase. Calculations of the electric field gradients in BaTiO₃ have been carried out in [11]. These calculations were based on the static ion model with the consideration of the monopoles and dipoles of a lattice. However, the authors did not take into consideration the overlap effects which give a significant contribution to the EFG at the Fe³⁺ ion nucleus [20 to 23].

The calculation of the electric field gradient at the Fe³⁺ ion nucleus in the octahedral positions of BaTiO₃ at $T = 20$ °C with consideration of overlap effects was performed by using the Löwdin orthogonalization procedure [20] and structure data from [24]. The screening effects were calculated using the following values of the Sternheimer factors for the Fe³⁺ shells [21]: $R_{3\text{d}} = 0.25$, $R_{3\text{p}} = 0.03$, $R_{2\text{p}} = 0.00$.

As a result of this calculation, for the effective value of EFG which is produced at the Fe⁵⁷ nucleus by the electronic density and the nuclear charges of the octahedral complex, we obtained $q_{\text{compl}} = -0.03633$ at. units. For the evaluation of EFG from the remaining lattice we used the results obtained in [11]. Having computed the contribution of the monopoles and dipoles of the nearest oxygen ions to the EFG and subtracted this contribution from the value of the electric field gradient obtained in [11], we obtained $q_{\text{latt}} = -0.02911$ at. units. The quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum corresponding to the above values of the electric field gradients at the Fe⁵⁷ nucleus is $-0.196$ mm/s. This value of the quadrupolar splitting is significantly smaller than the observed one, $-0.484$ mm/s [1]. Comparing the calculated and observed value of the quadrupolar splitting, one obtains the contribution of the 4s shell polarized in the internal electric field to the quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum at $20$ °C, $\Delta W_Q(E) = -0.288$ mm/s.

The above result allows us to obtain the screening factor for the Fe³⁺ 4p shell. Using $E = 8 \times 10^8$ V/cm at $20$ °C [11] and $\varrho_{4\text{s}} = 0.2$, we obtain from (10) $R_{4\text{p}} = 0.51$. As expected, the screening factor for the 4p shell is larger than that for the 3d shell.

Having found $R_{4p}$, we can now calculate the quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum in the tetrahedral phase of $Fe^{3+}$-doped $BaTiO_{3}$ versus temperature. Since the exact structure of $BaTiO_{3}$ has been determined only at $T=20\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $105\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$, we assumed that the displacement of $Fe^{3+}$ from the centre of the oxygen octahedron is proportional to the spontaneous polarization, $P_{s}$. This kind of proportionality has been observed for a number of ferroelectrics [25]. We also considered that the local electric field on the $Fe^{3+}$ ion is proportional to $P_{s}$. This supposition follows from the fact that the electric fields and polarizations of the sublattices in $BaTiO_{3}$ are connected by a linear system of equations [17]. The temperature values of the spontaneous polar- ization of $BaTiO_{3}$ were adopted from [7].

Curve 1 in Fig. 1 is the quadrupolar splitting which is caused by the electronic density and nuclear charges of the octahedral complex and by the multipoles of the remaining lattice. Curve 2 was obtained by ad-ding the contribution of the 4s shell polarized in the internal electric field on the $Fe^{3+}$ ion to curve 1. One can see agreement between curve 2 and the experimental points.

## 4. Conclusions
1. The polarization of the covalency occupied 4s shell of the $Fe^{3+}$ ion in the electric field of $E\approx 10^{8}$ to $10^{9}\ \text{V/sm}$ gives an essential contribution to the isomer shift of the Mössbauer spectrum. For the ferroelectric $BaTiO_{3}$, this contribution determines both sign and magnitude of the discontinuous change of the shift observed at $T_{c}$. On the whole, the discontinuous changes of the shift for $Fe^{3+}$ ions in ferroelectrics in the region of the phase transition may occur without structure reconstruction at this point be- cause of discontinuous changes of the internal electric field.
2. The contribution of the electrically polarized 4s shell of $Fe^{3+}$ to the quadrupolar splitting of the Mössbauer spectrum is also significant. For $BaTiO_{3}$, it gives more than half the observed value of the quadrupolar splitting. It is interesting that the quadru- polar splitting may be observed even if the local symmetry of the environment of the $Fe^{3+}$ ion is cubic, because of the electric polarization of the 4s shell.
3. The weak binding between the 4s shell and the core of the iron ion causes also the observed temperature dependence of the isomer shift [26]. It would be interesting, therefore, to investigate the influence of the electric field and the thermal excitation on the valency $ns$ shells of other ions.
