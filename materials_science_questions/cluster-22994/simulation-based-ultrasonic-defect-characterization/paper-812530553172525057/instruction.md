# Pulse-Echo Nonlinear Parameter Simulation for Annular Phased Array Transducers

## Problem background
Nonlinear ultrasonic testing using second harmonic generation is a promising method for detecting micro-damage in materials. However, in the pulse–echo (PE) mode, the stress-free boundary of a specimen nearly cancels the reflected second harmonic when plane waves are used, making it difficult to measure the material nonlinearity parameter β. Focused beams from annular phased array transducers (APATs) can enhance the received second harmonic amplitude and enable reliable β measurement in pulse-echo mode. This task involves simulating the nonlinear wave fields generated by optimized APAT designs to compute their performance in terms of received fundamental and second harmonic displacements and the derived nonlinear parameters.

## Approach
The task uses a frequency-domain analytical model for nonlinear wave propagation in an elastic solid with quadratic nonlinearity. The model computes the incident and reflected fundamental and second harmonic pressure fields generated by a phased array of ring transmitters and received by a central disk receiver. Beam focusing is achieved by applying time delays to each transmitter element. The model accounts for diffraction, reflection from the stress-free boundary, and superposition of contributions from multiple elements. The material is aluminum with known longitudinal wave speed, density, and nonlinear parameter β. A through-transmission (TT) case with a single-element transducer serves as a reference. The average received displacements at the receiver are computed by numerically integrating the pressure fields over the receiver area. From these displacements, the uncorrected nonlinear parameter β' and the total correction C_T are calculated using the plane-wave relations with necessary corrections. The simulation is performed for three optimized equal-width (EW) APAT designs with focal lengths of 10, 15, and 20 mm, and for the TT reference.

## Reproduction target
Compute the average received fundamental displacement u₁ and second harmonic displacement u₂ for the following four configurations:
1. Through-transmission (TT) reference: single-element transmitter and receiver, both with diameter 12.7 mm, propagation distance 10 mm.
2. EW-type APAT with focal length F = 10 mm (case A13).
3. EW-type APAT with focal length F = 15 mm (case B13).
4. EW-type APAT with focal length F = 20 mm (case C13).
For each configuration, calculate the uncorrected nonlinear parameter β' and the total correction C_T using the relationship β' = (8/(k²·z))·(u₂/u₁²) and C_T = β/β' with β=5.5. Output a JSON array where each object contains the case identifier (string), u₁ (float, meters), u₂ (float, meters), beta_prime (float, dimensionless), and C_T (float, dimensionless). The simulation must use the EW-type geometry: four ring transmitters with inner/outer radii as specified, 0.5 mm kerf, 1 mm element width, central receiver diameter 3.2 mm, source displacement 1e-9 m, fundamental frequency 5 MHz, and total propagation distance 20 mm for the pulse-echo cases (10 mm specimen thickness, round-trip).
