# NONDESTRUCTIVE TESTING OF THE INNER NEAR-SURFACE ZONES OF NOZZLES, VESSELS AND PIPES FROM THE OUTSIDE

P. HÖLLER, G. HÜBSCHEN and H.J. SALZBURGER

Fraunhofer-Institut für zerstörungsfreie Prüfverfahren (IzfP), Universität, Bau 37, D-6600 Saarbrücken, Fed. Rep. Germany

Received January 1985

The most critical defects in pressure conducting components of nuclear power plants are cracklike defects at the inner surface of vessels, nozzles and pipes. This paper presents methods of testing the inner surfaces and the inner near-surface zones from the outside. Beside radiographic and radiometric methods the main point of this contribution treats ultrasonic testing methods. Especially results for testing the inner near-surface zones with electromagnetically excited SH-waves from the outside are presented. Using SH-waves the following phenomena contribute to an improvement of the testing quality:
- nearly total transmission of the sound pressure at the interfaces base metal-weld metal in a wide angle range;
- complete corner reflection;
- small disturbing signals from roots and crowns of butt welds in thin-walled pipes when using the first symmetrical mode of guided SH-plate waves;
- access of the inner radius of nozzles unaccessible with conventional ultrasonic methods.

## 1. Introduction

The objective nondestructive testing of the surfaces and the near-surface zones at the probe side has been treated in ref. [1]. New developments in this field to be mentioned are the current perturbation technique with eddy currents of low frequencies and a corresponding depth of penetration. This eddy current testing can be used as an imaging technique using magnetic tapes [2,3].

This contribution treats the more important and difficult problem of nondestructive testing of the inner surface from the outside. In pressure conducting plants flaws are expected only at the inner surface or in the inner near-surface zone, respectively. In most cases testing from the inside is not possible, or if it is possible, much more expense is necessary than for testing from the outside. Beside wall-thickness and materials for which magnetic or electromagnetic testing over the whole cross-section is possible including the backward near-surface zone, for the inspection of the inner near-surface zones from the outside only ultrasonic and radiographic or radiometric methods come into question; but the last one only then when vessels or pipes are completely radiated through; i.e. the source and the film or the detector, respectively, are arranged at opposite outside surfaces.

## 2. Radiography and radiometry

Essential developments of the radiography using films are not known. The fact that the classical radiography is used more and more for testing austenitic pipes does not mean any progress for this method. On the other hand, the tomography - well known from medical applications - has to be mentioned as a radiometric technique. Each volume element of the object is radiated through from many directions. Already this multi-angle passing through significantly enhances the flaw detectability for cracklike defects. Additionally, the tomographic reconstruction leads to sectional views with a true indication of the flaw positions. ### 3. Ultrasonic testing

For the ultrasonic testing of the inner near-surface zone (INZ) the angle incidence of shear waves is established for a long time using the so-called angle mirror reflect (corner reflect) for cracklike defects. Fig. 5 shows this method schematically. Concerning the defect detectability, it is comparable with the pulse-echo technique with normal incidence, respectively the tandem technique for crack orientations perpendicular to the surface. The sound beams are back reflected on geometrical ray paths into the probe whereby the different wave types are restricted with regard to the angle of incidence. According to fig. 6 this corner reflect is independent of the angle of incidence only for SH (Shear Horizontal) waves (fig. 10). This wave type until now is still rarely used. Incident longitudinal waves are reflected nearly in the whole angle range as SV (Shear Vertical) wave which only can be detected with another probe in a different position. When testing with SV-waves in a similar manner the incident energy of a SV-wave is mode converted at steep angles $\leq 30^{\circ}$ and flat angles $\geq 60^{\circ}$ into a longitudinal wave, which cannot be detected by the transmitter probe. For the use of the corner reflection with shear verti- cal waves (SV-waves) many practical experiences exist, for example, by this technique the INZ of primary circuit components in nuclear power plants is tested. This method fails when testing austenitic welds, espe- cially then, when the backward surface is not grinded. The upper oscillogram in fig. 8 shows the echo signals from the area of the INZ of an austenitic weld at $45^{\circ}$ incidence of SV-waves $(f=1 MHz)$ . A 2 mm deep notch cannot be detected because of echo signals com- parable in amplitude and time of flight coming from flawless areas of the weldment. In comparison, the lower part shows the clear and short echo of a corner. At angle incidence of longitudinal waves the dis- turbances by the interface weld metal - base metal respectively the scattering by the weld metal are smaller, but in practice there is no corner reflection as it is shown in fig. 6.

Two procedures of ultrasonic testing have shown advantages mainly for the inspection of austenitic welds:
- the mode conversion method using longitudinal waves under flat angles of incidence;
- testing with SH-waves at angles between $45^{\circ}$ and $90^{\circ}$ (grazing incidence).

The first method is already in use. Here the testing of longitudinal seams of austenitic pipes should be mentioned [11]. The principle is shown in fig. 9. It is based on the use of the oblique incident shear wave (SV) excited together with the longitudinal wave. The shear wave produces at the opposite surface also a longitudinal wave of grazing incidence. In most applica- tions the echoes of the shear wave from the sound paths2 and 3 are used. The method requires, because of the complex A-scan, a strict observance of the probe posi-

By using the second mentioned method, shown in fig. 10, mode conversions from the shear into the longitudinal wave are not possi- ble at reflectors orientated perpendicular to the plane of incidence - as for the testing of longitudinal flaws in welds. The electromagnetic acoustic transduction process directly permits the generation of shear horizontal waves besides the other wave types known from the ultrasonic testing with piezoelectric transducers. Hereby the ultra- sound is not produced in a probe and is not transferred into the medium by a coupling gap. The energy is transferred electromagnetically.

The generation of the mechanical stresses or the displacement fields, respectively, takes place in the test- ing object by superimposing the eddy currents by a magnetic bias field. Today this kind of ultrasonic trans- duction is to be attributed to the state of the art.

### 3.1. Testing with SH-waves

The ultrasonic inspection of the INZ of the reactor pressure vessel (RPV), pipes and nozzles from the out-side is disturbed by:
- the dendritic structure of austenitic welds, dissimilar metal welds and claddings;
- the differences in the characteristic acoustical imped- ances between weld metal and base metal;
- weld crowns and roots;
- the geometry in the range of RPV-nozzles.

The elastic anisotropy of austenitic welds, dissimilar metal welds and claddings leads to reflection and refrac- tion of the incident ultrasonic wave at the interfaces. In the weld metal, the dendritic structure results in an enhanced ultrasonic scattering. The geometry of nozzles permits only the access of the crotch corner of the nozzle. Additionally, the manual cladding leads to strong acoustical background interference.

### 3.1.2. Cladding
As known, when using SV-waves the cladding dis- turbs more the testing of the INZ from the outside than from the inside. In the case of the cladding the col- umnar grains are orientated nearly perpendicular to the surface. There is a jump of the acoustical impedance at the interface austenitic cladding - ferritic base metal.

The computation (fig. 19) shows that this jump only occurs at angles above $60^{\circ}$. ## 4. Conclusion

For the NDT of the INZ of vessels, pipes and nozzles
- the X-ray tomography,
- the mode conversion method with longitudinal waves near the critical angle,

- the SH-waves with electromagnetic transmitter and receiver probe,

are new and in comparison to radiography with film, angle incidence of bulk longitudinal and SV-waves es- sentially improved approaches. These improvements are related to the detection of cracks for the radiography and the detection of all kinds of flaws in austenitic welds, dissimilar metal welds, for testing the cladding respectively the near-surface zone of the ferritic base metal respectively at the clad side as for the testing of the inner radius of nozzles. When testing with SH-waves the following phenomena contribute to an improvement of the testing quality:

(1) the use of angles of incidence with nearly total transmission through the interfaces at the weld flanks or at the transition cladding-base metal, re- spectively;

(2) the complete corner reflectiion;

(3) the small disturbing signals from roots and crowns;

(4) the incidence into the pipe area near the crotch corner from the outside of the RPV;

(5) the use of SH plate waves in thin-walled compo- nents and in austenitic claddings, respectively.

In practice, only the second method is used up to now. For SH-waves prototype equipments are available. For the industrial X-ray tomography the basic knowl- edge and technology is present: however, the practical application is still far away. This method will be re- stricted to special applications because of the time con- sumption of the technique and the large expense of the apparatus.
