# Mechanical properties of bilayer graphene with twist and grain boundaries
Junfeng Zhang and Jijun Zhao $^{a)}$

Key Laboratory of Materials Modification by Laser, Ion and Electron Beams,
Dalian University of Technology, Ministry of Education, Dalian 116024, China

(Received 3 November 2012; accepted 4 January 2013; published online 30 January 2013)

We report the mechanical properties (including intrinsic strength, critical failure strain, and Young's modulus) of bilayer graphene with twist or grain boundaries from first-principles calculations. We find that the intrinsic strength and critical failure strain of a bilayer graphene are governed by the twist angle. If a bilayer graphene is formed by one perfect graphene sheet and another graphene monolayer with grain boundaries, its overall mechanical properties are dominated by the layer with grain boundaries. The larger grain boundary angle in bilayer graphene, the higher intrinsic strength it would possess because of the lower energy and shorter C-C bond lengths on the boundary. Owing to the interlayer van de Waals interaction, bilayer graphene with small grain boundary angle presents different behavior from monolayer graphene grain boundaries. © 2013 *American Institute of Physics*. [http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4789594]

## I. INTRODUCTION

Graphene, a two-dimension (2D) honeycomb monolayer of carbon atoms, has attracted great interest since its experimental isolation in $2004.^{1}$ As the thinnest and strongest material ever measured, graphene possesses extraordinary physical properties and holds promise for many potential applications in microelectronics and optoelectronics. $^{2}$ However, the pristine single layer graphene (SLG) suffers from a major drawback of zero energy gaps, which hinders its applications as electronic material. When graphene layers are stacked together, an electrical field perpendicular to the layers can create a tunable gap via breaking down of the inversion symmetry. $^{3,4}$ Thus, the unique ability of controlling the band gap in bilayer graphene (BLG) may lead to new opportunities of electronic devices. $^{3,5-8}$

Recent experiments have shown that a significant proportion of BLG samples obtained by mechanical exfoliating, $^{9}$ chemical vapor deposition (CVD), $^{10,11}$ or growing on insulating substrates $^{12,13}$ are indeed twisted bilayer graphene (tBLG), in which the two layers are stacked with a twist angle $\beta$. It was found that the interlayer interaction and twist between the layers play significant roles in many aspects of tBLG, e.g., Landau levels (LLs), $^{14}$ Raman spectra, $^{15}$ Van Hove singularities, $^{16}$ optical absorption, $^{17}$ carrier velocity, $^{6,18,19}$ and electronic properties. $^{14,20,21}$ The interlayer coupling in twisted bilayer graphene with a specific range of rotational angles would result in an unique electronic structure and a fractal energy Landau levels spectrum under laboratory accessible magnetic field strengths. $^{14}$ Because of the singularity in the joint density of states of tBLG, the Raman intensity closely correlates with the twist angle in the BLG with small twist angles. $^{15}$ In particular, the low energy electronic structure and Fermi velocity of tBLG are quite different from those in the AB-stacked BLG or SLG presented in scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) with LL spectroscopy $^{18}$ and theoretical predictions. $^{19}$ Moreover, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images presented the existence of the bilayer graphene grain boundaries (BLG-GBs). $^{10}$ The stacking configuration or the twist angle can thus be altered due to the existence of GBs.

In various fields of applications, the stiffness and strength of the materials are the key factors in determining the stability and lifetime of the devices. The SLG was found to possess extraordinary mechanical properties, $^{22-27}$ such as an ultrahigh Young's modulus of $\sim 1$ TPa and an intrinsic fracture strength of $\sim 130$ GPa. $^{26}$ However, little is known about the mechanical properties of BLG from either experimental or theoretical sides. Recently, Zhang *et al*. $^{28}$ investigated the mechanical properties of BLG sheets coupled by $\text{sp}^3$ bonding by molecular dynamics simulations. They found that the presence of $\text{sp}^3$ bonds can enhance the mechanic strength because of the boosted interlayer interaction and smaller interlayer distance. Compared with the comprehensive studies in monolayer pristine graphene $^{26,27}$ or graphene grain boundaries, $^{29-33}$ there are still many unsolved issues about the mechanical properties of BLG and BLG-GBs so far. For example, how does the interlayer van der Waals (vdW) interaction affect the mechanical properties of BLG? What role does the twist angle between the upper and lower layers play? Would the defect line (i.e., GB) in one layer of BLG-GBs reduce the intrinsic strength and Young's modulus of the entire system? What is the difference between the boundary effects in SLG and BLG?

In this paper, we addressed these important issues by carrying out systematic first-principles investigations on the mechanical properties of tBLG and BLG-GBs, including the Young's modulus, intrinsic strength, and critical failure strain. We found that the intrinsic strength of tBLG relies on its twist angle and the mechanical properties of BLG-GBs are dominated by the layer with defect line. Moreover, the interlayer interaction can affect the intrinsic strength of the BLG-GBs with small misorientation angles.

## II. STRUCTURAL MODELS AND THEORETICAL METHODS

The structures and mechanical properties of BLG and BLG-GBs were computed using density functional theory (DFT) and projector-augmented wave (PAW) method³⁴ implemented in the Vienna Ab initio simulation package (VASP).³⁵,³⁶ The PBE functional³⁷ was used to describe the exchange-correction interaction, and the plane-wave basis was expanded up to a cutoff energy of 400 eV. To describe the interlayer vdW interactions, we adopted a dispersion-corrected DFT (DFT-D) scheme³⁸ proposed by Grimme.³⁹ The supercell z dimension perpendicular to the graphene sheet (xy plane) was fixed to be 2 nm to avoid the interaction between the graphene layer and its periodic images. 2D periodic boundary condition (PBC) was applied in the other directions, as shown in Fig. 1. The distance between the boundaries and its periodic images (equal to half of supercell size perpendicular to the boundary direction) of the BLG-GBs was set to be more than 1.5 nm, which has been tested to achieve well converged results.³³

For every BLG and BLG-GBs configurations, the supercell parameters in xy dimensions and the atomic coordinates were fully relaxed without any constraint to allow possible out-of-plane inflection. Starting from the equilibrium geometries, a series of uniaxial tensile strains were gradually applied with 1% increment along the zigzag direction for BLG or perpendicular to the boundary line for BLG-GBs, while the atomic coordinates were fully relaxed at each strain steps. At each strain, the stress component along the elongation orientation was directly computed via the stress theorem⁴⁰,⁴¹ and then rescaled by a factor of Z/d to obtain the equivalent stress on the graphene sheet, where Z=2 nm is the thickness of vacuum space, d is the thickness of the sheet (which is double value of the interlayer distance).²⁷ From the theoretical stress-strain curves, the intrinsic tensile strengths were determined by the stress values at the critical strains at failure, where the stress-strain curves dramatically drop. The Young's modulus was obtained from the slope of stress-strain curve within the elastic range of which the Hook's law holds.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

A twisted BLG with twist angle $\beta$ was obtained from $\beta/2$ clockwise and anticlockwise rotations along the zigzag direction of an originally AA-stacked BLG for upper layer and lower layer, respectively. The twist angle ranges from 0° to 60° owing to the six-fold symmetry of honeycomb lattice. As a schematic representative for the interlayer twist and supercell used in this work, a 21.8° tBLG is plotted in Fig. 1. We chose quadrangled supercell in which the x and y directions are set along the zigzag and armchair dominated orientation, respectively. Very small and large twist angle would result in too large supercell beyond the capability of DFT calculations. Hence, we considered eight tBLG configurations with twist angle $\beta$ ranging from 5° to 42.1°. The calculated interlayer distances are 3.41 and 3.74 Å (see Table I) for the AB and AA stacked BLG systems, respectively, which compare well with the values of 3.35 Å for highly oriented pyrolitic graphite (HOPG)⁴² and 3.55 Å for AA stacked graphite measured by high-resolution TEM (HRTEM),⁴³ respectively. Moreover, the computed distances between the layers of tBLG fall in the range of $3.388\pm0.042$ Å, consistent with the experimental results of $3.27\pm0.18$ Å for the CVD synthesized BLG samples.¹⁰ These agreements demonstrate that the present theoretical scheme is appropriate for describing the interlayer vdW interaction in the BLG systems.

The relative energy per carbon atoms of tBLG can be determined by the equation: $\Delta\mathrm{E}=(\mathrm{E}(\beta)-\mathrm{N}\times\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{BLG}})/\mathrm{N}$, where $\mathrm{E}(\beta)$ is the energy of tBLG with a twist angle $\beta$, N is the total number of carbon atoms in the tBLG, and $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{BLG}}$ is the energy per carbon atom of AA stacked BLG, respectively. From Table I, one can see that tBLG are thermodynamically less stable than the AB or AA stacked BLG by about 0.4 eV/atom. However, tBLG possesses very close relative energies for different $\beta$, especially for those between 7.3° and 38.2°. Very small difference in $\Delta\mathrm{E}$ (e.g., 0.03 eV/atom) implies nearly equal possibility of existence. The present results indicate that the twisted layers are easy to move or rotate with respect to each other, suggesting superlubricity between the graphene layers.¹⁶,⁴⁴

From our DFT calculations, the Young's modulus and intrinsic strength of tBLG systems have been obtained. Moderate correlations between the intrinsic strength and twist angle $\beta$ for the tBLG can be found, as shown in Fig. 2. Generally, with increasing $\beta$ from 5° to 42.1°, both the intrinsic strength and critical failure stain decrease linearly from 111.23 GPa to 96.97 GPa and from 24% to 19%, respectively. Compared with the AB ($\tau=122.35$ GPa, $\varepsilon=27\%$) or AA ($\tau=111.67$ GPa, $\varepsilon=27\%$) stacked BLG, both intrinsic strength and failure stain are reduced by the interlayer twist. Intuitively, this effect must be associated with the vdW interaction between two graphene layers. However, according to the above results of relative energies (see Table I), the interlayer vdW interaction is indeed insensitive to the twist angle $\beta$. Alternatively, the reduction of intrinsic strength for a tBLG with $\beta$ can be explained by the following two factors. First,

For the tBLG with small twist angle considered in this work, tBLG with 7.3° and 5° showed little difference in both intrinsic strength and fracture strain, i.e., 110.1 GPa and 24% of 7.3° tBLG versus 111.2 GPa and 24% of 5° tBLG. The mechanical properties of BLGs with GB on one graphene layer have also been investigated using the DFT calculations. The atomic structures of GBs were chosen as same as our previous study, $^{32}$ in which the 7-5 or 7-5-7-5 defects distribute evenly on the boundaries. As shown in Fig. 3, the emergence of GBs can change the orientated BLG to twisted BLG with $\beta$. Here, $\beta$ is equal to the misorientation angle $\theta$ of the grain boundary. From Table II, we can see that the interlayer distance of BLG-GBs are smaller than that in AA BLG but bigger than that in the corresponding tBLG. The twisted BLG-GBs possess relative energies per carbon atom of $0.416\pm0.12$ eV/atom, which are only slightly larger than that of tBLG ($0.398\pm0.12$ eV/atom from Table I) due to the existence of defect rings.

Previously, it was found that the mechanical properties of monolayer graphene GBs rely on the misorientation angle$^{32}$ or the inflection angle. $^{33}$ In the BLG-GBs, the mechanical properties can be also affected by the GBs of the upper layer. The stress-strain curves of zigzag- and armchair-orientated BLG-GBs are plotted in Fig. 4. Similar to the cases of monolayer graphene GBs, as the misorientation angle in GB increases, intrinsic strength and failure strain increase for both zigzag- and armchair-oriented BLG-GBs.

For both zigzag- and armchair-oriented BLG-GBs, the larger misorientation angle means better accommodation of the defect rings. $^{32}$ The degree of accommodation can be measured by both the relative energy per carbon atoms and the bond length in the boundary. On the one hand, the relative energy per atom generally decreases with the increase of misorientation angle for both zigzag- and armchair-orientated BLG-GBs from Table II. The intrinsic strength shows linear dependence on the stability, as shown in Fig. 5(a). Similar results were found in the carbon nanotubes$^{45}$ and monolayer graphene GBs, $^{33}$ in which the intrinsic strength of the flat GBs drops with the decrease of formation energy. Moreover, the intrinsic strengths of BLG-GBs are very close to those of monolayer graphene GBs as shown in the inset of Fig. 4. We thus suggest that the mechanical properties of BLG-GBs are mainly controlled by the layer with defects. The significance of GB in the mechanical properties of both SLG and BLG further implies that GBs may also play an important role in the few-layer graphene. The large-angle BLG-GBs showed very similar intrinsic strength with that of monolayer graphene GBs. However, the intrinsic strengths of zigzag- and armchair-orientated small-angle BLG-GBs exhibit different behavior with regard to that of monolayer graphene GBs. For the zigzag-oriented BLG-GBs, the intrinsic strength (55.7 GPa) of smaller-angle system ($7.3^\circ$) is

lower than that in monolayer (66 GPa), whereas the intrinsic strength (62.3 GPa) of smaller angle (17.5°) is higher than that in monolayer (44.9 GPa) for the armchair-oriented BLG- GBs. Previous studies $^{16,18,19}$ have shown that the interlayer vdW interaction would be more effective for the BLGs with smaller twist angles. As for the BLG-GBs with small misor- ientation angle, the intrinsic strength may be affected by vdW interaction from the twisted part.

Different from the intrinsic strength and failure stain which rely on the misorientation angle, Young's modulus, however, is hardly affected by the defect line. For all BLG- GBs considered in this work, the computed Young's modu- lus $(963 \pm 13 GPa)$ are close to 975 GPa of AB BLG and larger than 866 GPa of AA BLG. As displayed in Fig. 4, in the strain range where the Hooke's Law holds, the stress- to-strain ratios are almost identical for different systems. Indeed, the overall texture of BLG-GBs is not affected by the grain boundaries because the $sp^{2}$ hybridization dominatesthe boundaries even for GBs with highest defect density. $^{33}$  On the contrary, the intrinsic strength is usually governed by the critical bonds, e.g., 7-6 bonds of both GBs and BLG- GBs. Then, Young's modulus of BLG can be retained even for small-angle BLG-GBs, in which the intrinsic strength has already been reduced to half of the pristine BLG.

## IV. CONCLUSION
Using first-principles calculations, we investigated the effects of the twist and GBs on the mechanical properties of BLG under tensile strains. Very small difference in relative energy per carbon atoms $(\Delta E)$ has been found for tBLGs with different twist angles, which implies nearly equal possi- bility of existence and superlubricity between the graphene layers. The intrinsic strength and critical failure strain show linear dependence on the twist angle. In addition, the me- chanical properties of BLG are mainly determined by the layer with defects. The intrinsic strength increases with misorientation angle for both zigzag- and armchair-oriented BLG-GBs since the grain boundary with larger angles can accommodate defect rings better. Meanwhile, the Young's modulus is almost independent on either relative rotation between the layers or GBs in one layer. The present theoreti- cal results provide valuable information for the future design of nanoscale materials and devices based on bilayer or few- layer graphene.
