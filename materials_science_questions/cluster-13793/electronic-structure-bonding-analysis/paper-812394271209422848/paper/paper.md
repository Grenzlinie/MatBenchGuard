# Geometry optimization and electronic structure of $BaCoO_{3}$

## Abstract
Geometry optimization and electronic band structure calculations were performed on pseudo-perovskite $BaCoO_{3}$. A drastic change in electronic structure is obtained by varying the geometry (changing the superexchange angle Co-O-Co and Co-O distances). Calculations show the key importance of the conditions of anion-cation interactions in electrical conduction properties of the material.

## 1. Introduction
Recently, perovskite-like transition metal oxides have drawn much attention because of their interesting physical properties, such as colossal magnetoresistance [1] or superconductivity [2].

In this paper, we present a full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method (LAPW) calculation for the electronic structure of hexagonal $BaCoO_{3}$. The importance of this material lies in the finding in the doped compound $La_{0.5}Ba_{0.5}CoO_{3}$ of a high CMR ratio [3], which is smaller than in manganites and in layered cobaltates, but it is still comparable to the highest ratio observed in Co-based oxides. Some interesting physical properties appear as well in the parent compound. The study of those properties may lead to understand the low-doping regions of $La_{x}Ba_{1-x}$.$CoO_{3}$.

The structure of $BaCoO_{3}$ is basically chains of Co ions separated by Ba spacers. The Co ions within the chains are separated by triangles of O atoms. The material was first proposed to be an antiferromagnetic insulator with $T_{\text{N}}=8$ K [4], but more recent studies showed the existence of a more complex magnetic behaviour [5]. Even though its ground state still appears to be that of an antiferromagnetic insulator, competition between in-chain and interchain magnetic couplings yields a complicated phase diagram, but this was not completely confirmed by experiments and, as far as we know, there is no complete phase diagram of the material available.

The experiments [5] on its electrical properties conclude that the system is a semiconductor with conduction occuring through electrons as seen by measuring a negative thermoelectric coefficient.

In a previous work [6], we focused on the computation of the minimum energy spin state, which was found to be, in agreement with experiment, the low spin state. In the present paper, we carry out, for the LS state, a geometry optimization and an analysis of the electronic structure for the optimized geometry. The calculations are restricted to a ferromagnetic phase.

## 2. Computational details
The calculations were carried out using the full-potential LAPW method [7] as implemented in the WIEN97 package [8]. This is a well-tested method that has been applied to a great variety of materials, like minerals [9], high $T_{c}$ superconductors [10] or transition metal oxides [11]. This

The values for the muffin-tin radii were chosen as: 1.70 a.u. for Co, 1.73 a.u. for Ba and 1.25 a.u. for O. The calculations of the geometry optimization used values of $R_{\text{MT}}K_{\text{max}}=6$ and 32 k-points in the irreducible wedge of the Brillouin zone (450 points in the whole zone). Calculations on the converged structure reached $R_{\text{MT}}K_{\text{max}}=7.5$ and 65 k-points in the IBZ (750 points in the whole zone). The modellation of the exchange-correlation potential utilized the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) within the scheme due to PBE [12]. The system was modelled in a ferromagnetic configuration of the Co spins. For that purpose, fixed spin moment method (FSM) was used [13]. A total moment of $2\mu_{\text{B}}$ was fixed within the cell (that contains two Co atoms). Hence, the Co ions were simulated to possess a spin moment $\simeq0.60\mu_{\text{B}}$, which, as showed in Ref. [6], happens to be the minimum-energy state (consistent with $S=1/2$ measurements on the system) [5].

## 3. Structure
$BaCoO_3$ crystallizes in an hexagonal pseudo-perovskite structure characterized by chains of face-sharing distorted $\text{CoO}_6$ octahedra [14]. Because of that way of stacking atoms along the $c$ axis, the material is basically a one-dimensional structure of Co chains. $\text{Ba}^{2+}$ ions play the role of spacers between the chains. Oxygen atoms are placed in triangles perpendicular to the Co chains. Each triangle is shared by two $\text{CoO}_6$ octahedra. In-chain Co–Co distance is fairly short $(\simeq2.38\ \mathring{\text{A}})$, even shorter than in Co metal $(\simeq2.50\ \mathring{\text{A}})$. Interchain Co–Co distances are much longer $(\simeq5.64\ \mathring{\text{A}})$, hence, Co–Co interactions will mostly occur into the chains. Even though several space groups are possible for explaining the X-ray diffraction pattern of the material, we choose the group $P6_3mc$ (no. 186) with a unit cell formed by two formula units and cell parameters $(a=5.645\ \mathring{\text{A}}$ and $c=4.752\ \mathring{\text{A}})$.

The position of the oxygens cannot be determined experimentally, one can only conclude that they are placed in the Wyckoff position $6(c):(x,-x,1/4)$. The value of $x$ is usually taken as $x=1/6$, which comes from close-packing reasons [15], but calculations using that geometry show big forces on the oxygen atoms. Hence, a partial geometry optimization of the structure was carried out keeping the compound in the same spatial group but varying the position of the oxygens (the parameter $x$). In the structure, changing the $x$ parameter means changing the position of the oxygens and by doing so vary both the so-called superexchange angle (Co–O–Co) and Co–O distances but does not modify in any way the symmetry of the crystal nor the Co–Co distances. The metal–nonmetal interaction will be highly modified. The procedure followed to optimize the geometry was minimizing the forces calculated on the oxygen atoms as well as the total energy of the system so as to yield the appropriate value of the $x$ parameter. The value we obtained for the optimized geometry is $x=0.1284$, with forces as low as 1 mRy/a.u.

## 4. DOS
In Figs. 1 and 2 we plot the DOS curves for two values of the parameter $x$. We observe very different physical properties by changing the position of the oxygen atoms. For $x>0.1450$, the material is a metal, whereas for $x<0.1450$, the behaviour is half-metallic. Hence, changing the conditions for metal–nonmetal interactions, electrical conduction properties change dramatically. This can be reasoned as follows.

The Co ions are embedded in a distorted and tilted $\text{O}_6$ octahedron. This noncubic symmetry produces full nondegeneracy of the Co d-levels; the valence band is formed mainly by mostly occupied Co $\text{d}_{x^2-y^2}$, $\text{d}_{3z^2-r^2}$ and $\text{d}_{yz}$ (here $x$,

The splitting between them, for $x=0.1284$, is about 1.5 eV, even though this should not be taken as a precise value because DFT tends to underestimate band gaps (for a review of the gap problem in DFT see Ref. [17] and references therein).

The splitting of the d-levels is caused by the crystal field produced by the O ions surrounding the Co cation. Hence, the positions of the anions play a crucial role in the electronic structure of ferromagnetic $BaCoO_3$. The band splitting will drastically change by changing the superexchange angle because Coulomb repulsion between the different Co d and O p orbitals will be modified leading to a different distribution of levels, even within the same spatial group.

Covalency effects are found in the DOS plots. We will develop these observations a bit further when dealing with bandstructure plots in Section 5, but from the DOS curves we can see that conduction comes mainly from Co $d_{x^2-y^2}$, $d_{3z^2-r^2}$ and $d_{yz}$ hybridized with the O $p_x$ and $p_y$. Fig. 1 shows the d-symmetry of the conduction electrons, but the conduction band has some p character due to some mixture of orbitals: Co $d_{xz}$ and $d_{xy}$ hybridized with O $p_z$.

Because the valency of the $Co^{4+}$ ion is so high, we expect a big influence of the crystal field. For the relaxed structure ($x=0.1284$), the band splitting is appreciable, such that the compound presents half-metallicity, but for nonequilibrium structures (the $x=0.1450$ configuration requires approximately 2.4 eV per Co ion), the system is a metal and the splitting between bands becomes smaller, all the Co d orbitals lie in the same energy interval. Changes in the superexchange angle have previously been reported as a reason for changing the conduction properties in transition metal oxides [18], the reason for that is a variation in the Coulomb repulsion between cation and anion orbitals, which produces a different bandstructure around the Fermi energy.

Within the GGA approximation, we are unable to obtain the experimentally found semiconducting behaviour. This is inherent to the GGA approximation, which cannot model the electron-electron interactions that yield to semiconducting behaviour in most transition metal oxides.

## 5. Bandstructure

Bandstructure plots (Figs. 3 and 4) further confirm the d symmetry of the states near the Fermi energy. The states lying closer to the Fermi level are: $d_{x^2-y^2}$, $d_{3z^2-r^2}$ and a smaller contribution from $d_{yz}$, all of them overlapping the O $p_x$ and $p_y$ bands. From that overlapping of bands near the Fermi level comes the hybridization observed in the DOS curves. Effects of covalency should then be seen in the system.

For characterizing the Brillouin zone, we used the standard notation of Ref. [20]. The first path chosen for the bandstructure, $\Gamma-M-K-\Gamma$, is that formed by directions perpendicular to the Co chains in the real crystal. In the plane formed by these directions, no Co-Co interaction should be observed, since interchain distance is quite large ($\simeq5.6$ Å), hence overlapping between interchain Co orbitals seems to be ruled out (as the plots confirm by showing no bands crossing the Fermi energy). We can observe in the plots (see Figs. 3 and 4, O p$_z$ bands at 2-3 eV above $E_F$ overlapping with Co d$_{xy}$ and d$_{xz}$. These are unoccupied bands because of a higher Coulomb repulsion. Below the Fermi energy, there exist two dispersionless d-bands. This occurs because there is weak in-plane wave-function overlap, neither Co-Co nor Co-O interactions take place in the plane perpendicular to the Co chains. As we will later see, more dispersion is observed in the bands for $k$-paths including Co-Co and Co-O interactions.

Secondly, the arrangement of the atoms around Co chains implies that the direction of the chains will play a crucial role in the electronic structure of the system. That direction in $k$-space joins the following points: $\Gamma-$A. A much higher dispersion is present in the bands, an indication of stronger interactions. Conduction takes place in these directions, it occurs through electrons in d$_{3z^2-r^2}$ and d$_{yz}$ orbitals, showing the d-character of the conduction electrons as we saw in the DOS plots. It is interesting to study the Co-Co interaction, which is mediated by the Co d$_{x^2-y^2}$ electrons. The Co orbitals are oriented along the internal coordinate system of the atom, marked by the environment the ion is in, so, this orbital has a lobe along the $c$ axis. Contrary to the observations in Ref. [19], from our plots we obtain a nondispersive d$_{x^2-y^2}$-band, what seems to indicate that $\sigma$ interactions between cobalts are not as important as intuitively thought by the way the Co ions are stuck in chains with a very short distance between them. From our data we interpret a much more important metal-nonmetal interaction. In fact, it is the mere change in the conditions for that interaction that completely changes the electrical properties of the system. That is an indication of the key role played by Co-O interactions. The role played by the Ba atoms is basically that of an electron donor.

Thirdly, to observe more deeply this Co-O interaction, we plotted the path containing Co-O directions: A-L-H-K-M-L. The most remarkable feature for this $k$-path is the band overlapping between O p$_x$ and p$_y$ orbitals with Co d. This indicates that Co-O interactions dominate for this $k$-directions (as expected). The bands lying closer to the Fermi energy are Co d-O p hybrid bands, as observed in the DOS plots, which is one more reason to think that Co-O interactions play a key role, rather than metal-metal interactions.

## 6. Charge density

### 6.1. Charge distribution

We can analyze the partial charges found in the valence states. These will be given by a decomposition of the electronic density in different $lm$ values. The value of the partial charges is highly dependent on the size of the muffin-tin spheres, but nevertheless their study may shed some light on the electronic structure of the system. In our case, the valence states of the different atoms are Co(4s,4p,3d), Ba(5p,5d,6s) and O(2s,2p).

From the values of the partial charges, we further confirm that the strong O p-Co d interactions lead to partial unoccupation of the Co d$_{xy}$ and d$_{xz}$ orbitals, whose partial charges are much lower than those of the other Co d orbitals (see Tables 1 and 2).

### 6.2. Electron density plots
The electron density distribution is shown in the electron density plots (Figs. 5-7) as a difference of electron densities ($\Delta\rho$) : (density for the crystal calculated from the LAPW selfconsistent cycle) $-$ (superposition of the densities of the isolated ions). The ions considered were $\text{Co}^{4+}$, $\text{O}^{2-}$ and $\text{Ba}^{2+}$. A negative $\Delta\rho$ implies a depletion of electron charge with respect to the ionic density, and hence a positive $\Delta\rho$ means a higher density than in the ion. The plots just include the valence orbitals, as in the study of the partial charges above.

The first plane we can consider is that containing the Co-O interaction (Figs. 5 and 6). We observe that the strong Co-O interactions lead to a depletion of charge for the Co orbitals directed towards the O ions, and a positive $\Delta\rho$ for the lobes along the directions between the oxygens. We can appreciate as well a directionality in the charge distribution of the oxygens. Observing the same plane for the metallic structure, smaller peaks are observed in the lobes directed between the oxygens. For the half-metallic, $x=0.1284$-structure, the maximum density for those lobes reaches $2.2\ \text{e}/\mathring{\text{A}}^{3}$; whereas for the metallic, $x=0.1450$-structure, the peak value reaches just $1.3\ \text{e}/\mathring{\text{A}}^{3}$.

When dealing with bandstructure plots, we argued that the most important interactions were Co-O interactions, that Co-Co intrachain interactions were secondary and Co-Co interchain interactions were negligible. We have already seen the key importance of Co-O interactions in the electronic structure. By observing Fig. 7 showing the differential electron density in the plane of the Co chains, we see high occupation of the orbitals along the chain reflected by positive $\Delta\rho$, hence the interaction those orbitals are suffering is not as important as cation-anion. This conclusion would not be intuitive at first because of the very short Co-Co distance, even shorter than in Co metal.

In the electron density figures, nonsphericity of the charge distributions indicates that pure ionicity of the bonds is not happening, the directionality observed in the bonds is a clear indication of some degree of covalency existing in the system, in agreement with the DOS plots.

The earlier assumptions on that matter were based on close-packing considerations, but by analyzing the forces on the anions and by minimizing the energy of the system we found that the assumed value was not appropriate and optimized it. That optimization leads to a change in the bandstructure close to the Fermi energy, and hence to a variation in the electrical conduction properties, by changing the conditions for cation-anion interactions. This can be analyzed by plotting the DOS curves. From the DOS curves, we observe mainly three features.

- A transition from metallicity to half-metallicity induced by changing the conditions for cation-anion interactions: Co-O distances and superexchange (Co-O-Co) angles.
- The Co-O interaction in the different structures is marked by the crystal field effect due to the anions, which will split the Co d levels in a particular way depending on the symmetry the Co ion is embedded in.
- Covalency effects are observed due to Co d-O p hybridization as a consequence of a strong Co-O interaction.

We analyzed as well the bandstructure of the system, observing that hybridization between Co d and O p orbitals occurs near the Fermi energy. Different $k$-paths were studied, focusing especially on Co-Co interactions within Co chains and Co-O interactions, which lead us to the conclusion that the latter have a bigger importance in the electronic structure because they control the conduction properties. The residual size of cation-cation interactions was further confirmed by analyzing the electron density plots, showing occupation of orbitals along the Co chains but reduction in the electronic charge for orbitals pointing along Co-O directions.
