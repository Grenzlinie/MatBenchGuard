The systematic trends of electrionic structure and optical properties of rutile ($P4_2/mnm$) $RuO_2$ have been calculated by using the plane-wave norm-conserving pseudopotential density functional theory (DFT) method within the generalised gradient approximation (GGA) for the exchange-correlation potential. The obtained equilibrium structure parameters are in excellent agreement with the experimental data. The calculated bulk modulus and elastic constants are also in good agreement with the experimental data and available theoretical calculations. Analysis based on electronic structure and pseudogap reveals that the bonding nature in $RuO_2$ is a combination of covalent, ionic and metallic bonds. Based on a Kramers-Kronig analysis of the reflectivity, we have obtained the spectral dependence of the real and imaginary parts of the complex dielectric constant ($\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_2$, respectively) and the refractive index ($n$); and comparisons have shown that the theoretical results agree well with the experimental data as well. Meanwhile, we have also calculated the absorption coefficient, reflectivity index, electron energy loss function of $RuO_2$ for radiation up to 30 eV. As a result, the predicted reflectivity index is in good agreement with the experimental data at low energies.

**Keywords:** first principle, rutile $RuO_2$, electronic structure, optical property

## 1. Introduction

Attractions to investigate $RuO_2$ are due to its interesting fundamental properties$^{[1-7]}$ and its potential superhard characteristics.$^{[2,3]}$ The search for new superhard materials has been undertaken extensively during recent years.$^{[8]}$ A possible new class of hard materials has been suggested: the transition-metal dioxides containing heavy elements,$^{[9]}$ typically, the bulk modulus of pyrite $RuO_2$ was found to be 399 GPa,$^{[10]}$ which is the highest value reported in a material, except diamond that has a bulk modulus of 442 GPa.$^{[11]}$ Under ambient pressure, many $MX_2$ compounds take either the rutile ($P4_2/mnm$) or the $CaCl_2$ ($Pnnm$) structure,$^{[12]}$ between which the primary difference involves a rotation about the $c$ axis of the $MX_6$ octahedra. Under usual conditions, $RuO_2$ has rutile structure,$^{[10]}$ and it transforms into a $CaCl_2$ structure at about 6 GPa$^{[13]}$ or 11.8 GPa,$^{[14]}$ and experiment shows that it transforms into a pyrite structure at about 12 GPa, while the theory reveals that $Pa\bar{3}$ structure can transform into an $fm\bar{3}m$ structure at about 89 GPa or 97 GPa.$^{[3]}$

As far as the electronic structures are concerned, many efforts have been made to understand the internal atomic and electronic properties, e.g. a detailed study of electronic structure of $RuO_2$ has been reported by Glassford and Chelikowsky.$^{[15]}$ A full-potential linear muffin-tin orbital (FP-LMTO) calculation on the electronic structure and bulk modulus of fluorite $RuO_2$ has also been performed by Lundin *et al.*$^{[16]}$ Elastic properties of $Pa\bar{3}$ phase of $RuO_2$ have been investigated systematically by Tse *et al.*$^{[3]}$ The band structure, density of states and charge density of $RuO_2$ in rutile and orthorhombic ($CaCl_2$-type) structures have been researched by Benyahia *et al.*$^{[17]}$ Moreover, the Fermi surface of $RuO_2$ in rutile structure has been calculated on the basis of the self-consistent energy band structure in the local density approximation method.$^{[18]}$ In order to determine

## 2. Theoretical methods

In the present study, we have elaborated the electronic structure and optical properties of rutile $RuO_2$ via first principles calculation. Our calculations have been performed using the plane-wave pseudopotential density functional theory method given by the Cambridge Serial Total Energy Package (CASTEP) program. $^{[23,24]}$ In the electronic structure calculations, we have used the norm-conserving pseudopotential introduced by Hamann, $^{[25,26]}$ together with the revised Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (RPBE) of the generalised gradient approximation (GGA) exchange-correlation function. $^{[27,28]}$ Considering the computational cost, a plane-wave basis set with an energy cut-off 600.0 eV$^{[3]}$ has been applied, and the 12×12×12 Monkhorst-Pack mesh$^{[29]}$ has been used for the Brillouin-zone (BZ) k-point sampling. Pseudo atomic calculations have been performed for $Ru$ ($4s^24p^64d^75s^1$) and $O$ ($2s^22p^4$), where the self-consistent convergence of the total energy is at $5.0×10^{-7}$ eV/atom. It is found that these model parameters are sufficient in leading to well converged total energy and geometrical configurations.

The linear optical properties in solids can be described with the complex frequency-dependent dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_1(\omega)+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_2(\omega)$. Since rutile $RuO_2$ compounds have tetragonal crystal structures, we need only to calculate one dielectric tensor component to characterise the linear optical properties completely. The imaginary or absorptive part $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ of the frequency-dependent dielectric function is given by

$$
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)=\frac{e^{2} \hbar}{\pi m^{2} \omega^{2}} \sum_{\mathrm{v}, \mathrm{c}} \int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\left|M_{\mathrm{vk}}(k)\right|^{2} \delta\left[\omega_{\mathrm{cv}}(k)-\omega\right] \mathrm{d}^{3} k.
\tag{1}
$$

The integral is carried out over the first Brillouin zone, and the momentum dipole elements $M_{\mathrm{vk}}(k)=\langle U_{\mathrm{cv}}|\boldsymbol{e} \cdot \nabla| U_{\mathrm{ck}}\rangle$, where $\boldsymbol{e}$ is the potential vector defining the electric field, are matrix elements for direct transitions between valence-band $u_{\mathrm{vk}}(r)$ and conduction band $u_{\mathrm{ck}}(r)$ states, and $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{cv}}(r)=E_{\mathrm{ck}}-E_{\mathrm{vk}}$ is the corresponding transition energy.

The real part $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ of the frequency-dependent dielectric function can be derived from the imaginary part using the Kramers-Kronig relation

$$
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\pi} p \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\omega^{\prime} \varepsilon_{2}\left(\omega^{\prime}\right)}{\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}} \mathrm{d} \omega^{\prime},
\tag{2}
$$

where $p$ denotes the Cauchy principal value of the integral. Based on the knowledge of both real and imaginary parts of the frequency-dependent dielectric function, we can calculate the important optical functions such as the refractive index $n(\omega)$

$$
n(\omega)=\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)}}{2}\right]^{1 / 2}.
\tag{3}
$$

In this work, we estimate the absorption coefficient $\alpha(\omega)$, the reflectivity coefficient $R(\omega)$, and the extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$ in the following forms:

$$
N(\omega)=n(\omega)+\mathrm{i} k(\omega),
\tag{4}
$$

$$
N(\omega)=n(\omega)+\mathrm{i} k(\omega),
\tag{5}
$$

$$
N^{2}(\omega)=\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+\mathrm{i} \varepsilon_{2}(\omega),
\tag{6}
$$

$$
R(\omega)=|(1-N) /(1+N)|^{2},
\tag{7}
$$

where $N$ is the complex refractive index, and the imaginary part $k(\omega)$ is related to the absorption coefficient by $\alpha(\omega)=2 k \omega / c$.

It is obvious that our calculations are in excel- lent agreement with experimental data. According to the generalised elastic stability criteria, $^{[36]}$ it is cer tain that rutile $\text{RuO}_2$ structure is elastically stable under ambient conditions. Furthermore, both the large $c_{33}/c_{11}$ and small $c_{13}/c_{12}$ values indicate that atomic bonding along the $c$-axis is stronger than the one along the $a$-axis, which is consistent with the fact that the bulk modulus $B$ is larger than shear modu- lus $G$ (109.86 GPa) under ambient pressure. At the same time, the fact that the mechanical anisotropy bulk modulus in $c$-axis (793.85 GPa) is larger than in $a$-axis (715.48 GPa) also indicates that the inter- atomic bonds along the $c$-axis are stronger than the bonds along the $a$-axis, which is consistent with the fact of $c_{33} > c_{11}$.

It is interesting to note that there is a substantial difference between $c_{44}$ and $c_{66}$ values, indicating the anisotropic character of atomic bonding between adja- cent planes in different directions. In rutile $\text{RuO}_2$, the shear anisotropy factor for the [101] shear direction, $A_1 = 4c_{44}/(c_{11} + c_{33} - 2c_{13}) = 0.9143$, is smaller than that for the [110] direction, $A_2 = 2c_{66}/(c_{11} - c_{12}) = 4.4354$. The small $A_1$ value is indicative of the weak cohesion between Ru and O layers. Based on the lin- ear elasticity approximation, the compressibilities $\chi_a$ and $\chi_c$ along the $a$ and $c$ directions have been cal- culated from the elastic constants respectively. Ob- tained values $\chi_a = 1.6268 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{GPa}^{-1}$ and $\chi_c = 0.8703 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{GPa}^{-1}$ show a big anisotropy between $a$ and $c$ directions.

It is well known that the Cauchy relation $c_{12} = c_{66}$ and $c_{13} = c_{44}$ should be satisfied when interatomic potentials are purely central. The deviations of the Cauchy relations, $\delta = c_{12} - c_{66}$ and $\delta = c_{13} - c_{44}$, are a measure of the contribution from the noncentral many-body force. Thus, the small $\delta = c_{12} - c_{66}$ (6.27) and large $\delta = c_{13} - c_{44}$ (62.42) values indicate that the noncentral many-body force is different in differ- ent directions.

According to the high symmetry lines in the Bril- louin zone, the valence bands and the conduction bands are presented at 0 K and 0 GPa. The valence band maximum occurs at the axis of $G$-$X$ which is higher than the Fermi level, and the conduction band minimum occurs at $X$ point which is lower than the Fermi level; this means that there exists a pseudo- gap. The pseudogap can still be found in pyrite $\text{RuO}_2$, however, it cannot be found in fluorite $\text{RuO}_2$.$^{[3]}$ At the same time, the band structure of rutile $\text{RuO}_2$ also shows strong metallic properties.

In Fig. 1 we present the density of states (DOS) calculated by GGA, and the Fermi energy is taken to be zero. The O 2s band is located approximately at $-$17.65 eV which is slightly higher than that in pyrite and fluorite $\text{RuO}_2$.$^{[17,18]}$ The valence band width is about 3 eV, which is larger than that of rutile $\text{RuO}_2$.$^{[17]}$ with a width of 2.5 eV, and is also larger than that in $\text{CaCl}_2$ $\text{RuO}_2$.$^{[17]}$ with a width of 1.4 eV, whereas it is smaller than that in pyrite $\text{RuO}_2$ with a width of 4 eV. The valence band width below the Fermi level of O 2p is 7.7 eV which is larger than in rutile $\text{RuO}_2$ with a width of 5.9 eV or 6.8 eV,$^{[18]}$ whereas it is lower than that in fluorite $\text{RuO}_2$.$^{[17]}$ with a width of 8.0 eV, and is also lower than that in pyrite $\text{RuO}_2$ with a width of 10.12 eV. The obtained O 2p band edge is

located at around -8.5 eV. Both the band width and the band edge are in agreement with the photoelectron spectroscopy measurements (XPS), which show a bandwidth of nearly 7 eV and the edge located at around -9 eV.[37,38] This pseudopotential results agree well with theoretical results,[20] having a bandwidth of 6.7 eV and band edge of -8.5 eV which have been obtained by using full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method. The conduction band width is about 6.29 eV, which is slightly lower than that of pyrite RuO₂ with a value of 6.74 eV, and is larger than that of fluorite RuO₂ with a value of 3 eV. The total DOS contributed by the O 2p density at the Fermi level is 0.28, which is far greater than that of fluorite RuO₂ (below 0.05)[18] and far smaller than that of pyrite RuO₂ (1.86), and the contributions of the Ru 4d electron are 0.98 which is smaller than that of fluorite RuO₂ (below 1.3) and pyrite RuO₂ (about 4.6). The bottom of the Ru 4d manifold is located approximately at -1.9 eV which is consistent with that in pyrite RuO₂ (-1.95 eV) and fluorite RuO₂ (-1.8 eV), and the intensity of rutile RuO₂ is 0.11, which is smaller than that of pyrite RuO₂ (0.65) but larger than that of fluorite RuO₂ (0.04). The main peak at -0.8 eV is the same as in fluorite RuO₂ but lower than that of pyrite RuO₂ (-0.6 eV). The rutile peak value is 7.2 which is lower than pyrite peak, the corresponding value being 11.55 but larger than fluorite RuO₂ peak the corresponding value being 3.05, and our results give a small shift compared with rutile values,[18] corresponding to -1.5 eV and -0.7 eV, respectively. The conduction band bottom of the Ru 4d manifold is located at 1.55 eV with the intensity of 0.21 which is consistent with that in pyrite RuO₂, corresponding to 1.5 eV with 0.27. The conduction band bottom in fluorite RuO₂, however, is located at 0.36 eV with an intensity of 0.18. The Fermi energy level shows sharp peaks, characters of which are contributed predominantly by the O 2p and Ru 4d electron densities, as is different from the rutile DOS[18] and fluorite RuO₂.[3] It is found that Ru 4d and O 2p states overlap completely from -10 eV to 5 eV, which is evidently indicative of a covalent interaction between them, resulting from strong hybridization.

The DOS of rutile RuO₂ possesses greater value (3.2) than that of fluorite RuO₂ (1.8), but is lower than that of pyrite RuO₂ (6.4) at the Fermi level, which indicates a moderate metallic feature of rutile RuO₂ in its crystalline state. The nearly free-electron states of the Ru 5s are the principal cause for metallicity of RuO₂.

In the rutile structure each metal atom Ru is surrounded by a nearly octahedral array of six oxygen atoms, and therefore the fivefold-degenerate $d$ states split into partially occupied triply degenerate $t_{2g}$ and unoccupied doubly degenerate $e_{g}$ states. As a result, the Fermi energy is near the minimum of the $t_{2g}$ manifold, showing a metallic feature. The low DOS at the Fermi level is responsible for the stabilisation of the structure, where the rutile level is higher than the counterpart in fluorite phase, and the Fermi level falls in a sharp peak, leading to a structural phase transformation induced by pressure.

Although there is a large hybridization between Ru 4d and O 2p states, the charge transfer from Ru to O is nonnegligible in the present case. From the Mulliken population results, it is also found that the charge transfer is as large as about 1.16 electrons, which are slightly larger than that in pyrite (1.02) or in fluorite (1.01), and the transfer shows weak pressure dependence. Therefore, the bonding behaviour between Ru-O has ionic nature owing to charge transfer. In a word, the results show that the bonding nature in RuO₂ is due to a combination of covalent, ionic and metallic characteristics.

The theoretical DOS function is compared to the XPS measurements[38] in Fig. 1. With the location of the experimental peak coincident with the theoretical one, the spectra are in a reasonable agreement. Because of the experimental broadening it is not possible to determine precisely the band width suggested by the XPS measurement. However, judging by the spectrum in Fig. 1, the valence band width consisting

of O 2p and Ru 4d agrees well with the XPS measurements, and the conduction band width is lower than the present theoretical results by 4.0 eV. In spite of the fact that the width of the O 2p and Ru 4d in the present calculation is in agreement with the XPS measurement, the comparison between the measured and the calculated results suggests that our total DOS is slightly underestimated at the Fermi level and over-estimated in the valence band, as is similar with the case of the ab initio calculations.[39,40] The slight deviation in values of the theoretical DOS at the Fermi level from experimental measurement is because of the complicated bonding nature of the transition metal Ru 4d and O 2p electrons.

The optical properties are studied through the absorption coefficient, reflectivity, energy loss function, refractivity index, dielectric function and conductivity index at the equilibrium lattice constant and the results are compared to the experimental data.[41] The calculated optical properties at the equilibrium lattice constant have been presented in Figs. 2 and 3, for the energy range up to 30 eV. In order to account for the structures observed in the optical spectra, it is customary to consider transitions from occupied to unoccupied bands in the electronic energy band structure, especially at high symmetry points in the Brillouin zone.

The calculated linear absorption spectrum is displayed in Fig. 2(a). The absorption edge starts from about 0 eV, corresponding to the energy pseudogap. The rutile RuO₂ has an absorption band from 0 to 23 eV, which contains eight main peaks for $E \perp c$ and seven main peaks for $E//c$. In the high energy region (above 23 eV), the crystal is transparent because it progressively becomes more difficult for the electrons to respond. The shapes and the intensities of the two absorption spectra are similar and comparable at these photon energies of 0-23 eV.

The theoretical spectra of the electron energy loss function are shown in Fig. 2(b). The electron energy loss function $L(\omega)$ is an important factor for describing the energy loss of a fast electron traversing in a material, which is the imaginary part of the reciprocal of the complex dielectric function. The prominent peaks in $L(\omega)$ spectra represent the characteristic associated with the plasma resonance, and the corresponding frequency is the so-called plasma frequency $\omega_{\rm p}$, above which the material is a dielectric $[\varepsilon_1(\omega) > 0]$ and below which the material behaves like a metallic compound in some sense $[\varepsilon_1(\omega) < 0]$. The peaks of $L(\omega)$ correspond to the trailing edges in the reflection spectra, and the prominent peaks of $L(\omega)$ are situated at energies corresponding to the abrupt reductions of $R(\omega)$. The $E \perp c$ peak of $L(\omega)$ is at 22.85 eV corresponding to the intensity 11.85, whereas the $E//c$ peak is at 22.27 eV corresponding to the intensity 15.85. The peak intensity shows the direction dependence and the anisotropy of the material. The wide range of energy loss is in agreement with the feature of $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ shown in Fig. 2(c), because wide range $[\varepsilon_1(\omega) < 0]$ regions exist for the two polarisations.

The reflectivity for the light polarisations $E \perp c$ and $E//c$ are compared to the experimental data in Fig. 2(c). Our reflectivity curves are in good quantitative agreement with the measured spectra up to $\hbar\omega = 2.5$ eV. For photon energies above 2.5 eV the experimental structures are too weak to establish a reliable correspondence with the theory. The reflectivity spectrum of the rutile RuO₂ shows some peaks which can be interpreted as the interband transitions from the valence to conduction band.

The refractive index and the extinction coefficient are displayed in Figs. 2(d) and 2(e), respectively. The static refractive index $n(0)$ is found to have the value 13.93 for $E\perp c$ and 8.13 for $E//c$. They decrease with energy in the transparency region, reaching a local minimum at 1.8 eV for $E\perp c$ and 2.4 eV for $E//c$. The extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$ increases with energy in the transparency region, reaching the peaks in the ultraviolet at about 0.78 eV for $E\perp c$ and 1.33 eV for $E\perp c$. It then decreases to a local minimum level at 2.36 eV $E\perp c$ and 2.85 eV $E//c$. The local minimum of the extinction coefficient $k(\omega)$ corresponds to the local minimum of refractive index $n(\omega)$ at around 2.5 eV.

The calculated real part and imaginary part of the conductivity index are shown in Fig. 3(a), from which we can see that it corresponds well to the variation of imaginary part of the dielectric function. Figures 3(b) and 3(c) show the imaginary and real parts of the dielectric function for rutile ${\rm RuO_2}$ respectively. As seen in Fig. 2 (b), the $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ spectra have a high absorption below 2 eV, which is due to transitions within Ru $t_{2{\rm g}}$ bands while the other optical structures above 2 eV arise from p-d electronic transitions. In the energy range of 0.83-2.04 eV of Fig. 2(c), the intraband transitions play an important role and the large negative values $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ for the $E\perp c$ components indicate that the crystal has a Drude-like behaviour, whereas it is not the case for the $E//c$ component. At higher energies, the optical features are due to electronic transitions from oxygen 2p to metal $d$ bands. For $E\perp c$, the absorptive part of the dielectric function $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ exhibits a main peak, with a magnitude of 55.23, which is located at about 0.01 eV. For $E//c$, the absorptive part of the dielectric function $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ exhibits a main weak peak, with a magnitude of 25.33, which is located at about 0.82 eV. Comparison between the measured and calculated spectra shows that the theory tends to overestimate the dielectric function in the low energy regime and underestimate it for high photon energies. The static dielectric constant $\varepsilon_2(0)$, calculated at the equilibrium lattice constant is about 55.23. Nevertheless, compared with the experimental dielectric function, the structures in the energy range of 1-3 eV in $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$, the refractive index and reflectivity curves seem to have their counterparts in the experimental spectra.

## 4. Conclusions

We have performed the comprehensive elastic, electronic structure and optical investigation of the rutile ${\rm RuO_2}$. The obtained equilibrium structure, elastic constants and bulk modulus of rutile ${\rm RuO_2}$ are in good agreement with the experimental data and available theoretical calculations. Our calculations reveal that the rutile phase shows elastic anisotropy and elastic stability under ambient conditions. The calculated band structure and DOS show that the bonding nature in ${\rm RuO_2}$ is a combination of covalent, ionic and metallic bonds, and that the theoretical DOS agrees well with the experimental data. The dielectric function and optical properties are presented in a wide energy range between 0 and 30 eV, and they agree well with the experimental data.
