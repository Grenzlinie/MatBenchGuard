# X-RAY PHOTOELECTRON AND QUANTUM-CHEMICAL INVESTIGATION OF THE ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE OF PALLADIUM CHLORIDE

S. G. Gagarin, Yu. A. Teterin,
and Yu. V. Plekhanov

UDC 539.192:541.128:546.98

The results of an x-ray photoelectron investigation of the core levels and va- lence band of $PdCl_{2}$ have been presented. A theoretical interpretation has been formulated on the basis of calculations of the expanded $Pd_{6} Cl_{8}^{4+}$ and $Pd_{6} Cl_{12}$ clusters by the Xα-scattered wave method and a band calculation of a one-dimen- sional $[PdCl_{2}]_{n}$ crystal. The proposed scheme for the electronic structure of $PdCl_{2}$ satisfactorily accounts for the features of the x-ray photoelectron spec tra, including the presence of satellite lines due to many-electron excitation. The inadequacy of the single-sphere approximation in the framework of the model of $PdCl_{4}^{2-}$ for the theoretical interpretation of the electronic structure has been noted, and the significant role of the covalent component of the Pd-Cl bonds in the formation of palladium chloride has been demonstrated. The re- sults of the XPS investigation in conjunction with the quantum-chemical calcu- lations makes it possible to examine the optical spectra and the x-ray absorp- tion and emission spectra of $PdCl_{2}$ on a single basis.

Palladium chloride $(PdCl_{2})$ has found extensive application both in metal-complex catal- ysis [1, 2] and in heterogeneous catalysis [3]. Various catalysts, for example an aluminum sulfide-palladium catalyst for the selective hydrogenation of unsaturated compounds, have been prepared on the basis of $PdCl_{2}$ [4]. However, the electronic structure has not been adequately studied. In particular, the investigation of the electronic structure by x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) [5] has been restricted to the study of the position and shape of the lines of the Pd 3d core electrons [3]. The $Cl K \beta_{1,3}$ and $Pd L \beta_{2,15}$ x-ray spec tra of $PdCl_{2}$ were obtained in [6]; however, an unreliable model of cross transitions was used to compare them on a single energy scale. Therefore, it would be useful to supplement the existing data on the electronic structure of $PdCl_{2}$ by investigating the valence band and

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As we know (see, for example, [6, 9, 10]), PdCl₂ has a polymeric structure with bridg- ing Cl atoms. The local symmetry of Pd is close to Dₕ; therefore, the theoretical model of palladium chloride is usually represented in the single-sphere approximation by the PdCl₄²⁻ cluster [9, 10]. According to this model, the valence band of PdCl₂ is composed of three subbands: 1) Cl 3s; 2) Cl 3p; 3) Pd 4d. In the D₄ₕ symmetry group, subband 3 includes the e_g(dₓz, dyz), a₁g(d_z²), and b₂g(dₓy) molecular orbitals (MOs), in which the eight d electrons of the Pd²⁺ ion are located. The lowest unoccupied b₁g MO represents the 4dₓ²_y² orbital of palladium, and above it along the energy scale there are two MOs, which are formed, respec- tively, from the Pd 5s and Pd 5p states. This scheme accounts for the optical spectra of PdCl₂ [9] on the basis of the Pd 4d → Pd 4d (peaks at 2-3 eV), Pd 4d → Pd 5p (3.5-4.5 eV), and Cl 3p → Pd 4d (5-6 eV) transitions.

Such a description of the electronic structure of PdCl₂ does not formally contradict the x-ray photoelectron spectrum of the valence states which we obtained (Fig. 1). As is seen from Fig. 1, three regions with gradually decreasing intensities of the photoemission can be identified in the spectrum.* Maxima a, b, and c for these regions are characterized by values of Eᵦ equal to 3.9, 7.2, and 17.4 eV, respectively. The most intense maximum (a) is naturally attributed to the photoemission of the Pd 4d electrons. In the framework of the PdCl₄²⁻ model, peak a reflects the density of states in subband 3, and peak c reflects the density of states in subband 1. The origin of maximum b is not so obvious, although it may formally be attributed to the photoemission of the Cl 3p electrons of subband 2, as is recommended by the single-sphere PdCl₄²⁻ model [9]. However, such an interpretation is not consistent with the ratio between the intensities of maxima a and b of the valence band. In fact, according to the data in [11], the ratio between the photoionization cross sections of the Cl 3p and Pd 4d subshells under AlKα radiation is (as calculated per electron) 0.0239/0.1235 = 0.1935. In the case of the ionic model of PdCl₂ (8 d electrons of palladium and 12 p electrons of chlorine), this value should be increased by a factor of 1.5; then the evaluation of the intensity ratio for subbands 2 and 3 gives a value of 0.29. Since the ratio between the heights of the maxima observed experimentally at Eᵦ = 7.2 and 3.9 eV (Fig. 1) is equal to 0.43 (i.e., is significantly greater), it should be concluded that the 4d electrons of the metal take an appreciable part in the formation of peak b. We note that the Pd Lβ₂,₁₅ emission spectrum [6], which is caused by Pd 4d → Pd 2p transitions, displays only one asymmetric maximum. As is seen from Fig. 2, it can be divided theoretically into two peaks, the maximum of the less intense of these peaks being at a distance of 3 eV from the main maximum in the direction of higher binding energies. This is consistent with the energy distance between maxima a and b in the x-ray photoelectron spectrum of PdCl₂ at 3.3 eV (see Fig. 1).

A comparison of Figs. 1 and 2 shows that the intensity ratio Iₐ/Iᵦ for the x-ray spec- trum is appreciably smaller than the value for the x-ray photoelectron spectrum. The natural interpretation of this fact suggests the significant participation of both Pd 4d and Cl 3p states in the region of peak b. In fact, the Pd Lβ₂,₁₅ spectrum displays only states of the metal, while the x-ray photoelectron spectrum displays states of both the metal and the ligands, since the electrons of the Cl 3p subshell make a contribution to the total photo- emission. The main results of the calculations are presented in Tables 1 and 2 (the data for the Cl 3s subband are not presented in them). The photoionization cross sections for the occupied MOs were calculated with the use of the data in [11] for the partial contributions of different states. The cross sections were normalized to unity for the $1t_{1g}$ MO of the $Pd_6Cl_8^{4+}$ cluster and the $3t_{1u}$ MO of the $Pd_6Cl_{12}$ cluster.

The data presented in Tables 1 and 2 show that the main special feature of the electronic structure of $PdCl_2$ is the significant interaction of the orbitals of the metal and the ligands, as a result of which the Pd 4d states are observed over the entire course of the valence band. The dispersion plots for the energy bands calculated according to [8] for $[PdCl_{2}]_{n}$ are presented in Fig. 3, from which it is seen that the parametrization of the EH method has a significant influence on the simulation of the electronic band structure of PdCl₂. The parameters in [16] produce separation of the two subbands of the valence region considered above by an energy gap of 2.5 eV (Fig. 3a), while this gap vanishes when the Xα-SW parameters are used, and the upper and lower subbands partially (~0.5 eV) overlap (Fig. 3b). An analysis of the eigenvectors for the Block functions [8] shows that the conclusions regarding the significant interaction of the Pd and Cl AOs are also maintained in the band treatment. The band approach also provides a better quantitative description of the features of the experimental x-ray photoelectron spectrum than do the cluster models. As follows from the data in Table 3, the possibility for an effective interaction between the states of the metal and the ligands is due to the resonance correspondence between the energies of the Pd 4d and Cl 3p AOs. The approach of the energies of these AOs toward one another is stimulated by the transfer of electron density from Pd to Cl, owing to which the Cl 3p level is destabilized in comparison to the value of its energy in the case of the free atom [16].

Besides the qualitatively correct description of the XPS data, the expanded cluster data provide a consistent interpretation for the Cl K and Pd LIII x-ray absorption spectra. According to the dipolar selection rules, the Cl K spectrum should be caused by transitions of the electrons of the Cl 1s subshell to free states including 3p orbitals of the halogen [17]. The Pd LIII spectrum is formed as a result of the excitation of the Pd $2p_{3/2}$ electrons to free states containing 4d or 5s orbitals of the metal atoms. It is customary to juxtapose the positions of the most intense maximum (A) in both spectra on a single energy scale [6], as was done in Fig. 4, where a comparison of the x-ray spectra from [6], the optical spectrum from [9], and the x-ray photoelectron spectrum of PdCl₂ which we obtained is given. In the framework of the simple $PdCl_{4}^{2-}$ model, the nature of maximum A in the Cl K spectrum is attributed to Cl 1s-Pd 4d cross transitions [6], since it is assumed in this case that the LUMOs are the palladium $4d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbitals. As follows from the results of our calculations, the $2e_{u}$ MO (the LUMO) and the $5t_{2g}$ and $3b_{2u}$ MOs of the $Pd_{6} Cl_{8}^{4+}$ cluster (Table 1) and the $2b_{2g}$ MO (the LUMO) and the $4e_{g}$ and $4t_{2u}$ MOs of the $Pd_{6} Cl_{12}$ cluster (Table 2) consist to approximately equal extents of the Pd 4d and Cl 3p orbitals. In the band calculation there is similar mixing of these AOs in the construction of the lowest unoccupied energy band. Therefore, transitions to the states at the bottom of the conduction band of PdCl₂ are allowed by the dipolar selection rules both from the Cl 1s level (maximum A in the Cl K spectrum) and from the Pd $2p_{3/2}$ level (maximum A in the Pd LIII spectrum). In addition, in this case, there is also a rational interpretation for the x-ray absorp- tion near edge structure (XANES) of PdCl₂ caused by the excitation of electrons from the palladium $2p_{1/2}$ and $2p_{2/3}$ orbitals by synchrotron bombardment [10]. The first, most intense maximum in the $L_{2}$ and $L_{3}$ XANES spectra should be attributed to transitions to MOs of the $2e_{u}, 5t_{2g}$, and $3b_{2u}$ types. This is consistent both with the participation of the 4d AO of palladium in their formation and with the half-width of the line (1.4 eV for $L_{2}$ and 1.5 eV for $L_{3}$ [10]). The width of the unoccupied $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ level for the $PdCl_{4}^{2-}$ model, which is de termined by the vibronic coupling, is, according to the estimate in [10], equal to 0.3 eV, which is more than two times smaller than the energy intervals of the unoccupied $2e_{u}, 5t_{2g}$, and $3b_{2u}$ levels in the $Pd_{6}Cl_{8}^{4+}$ model. Therefore, when the vibronic "erosion" of the levels is taken into account, this model is in better agreement with the experiment. From Fig. 3b it is seen that the band calculation is also in good agreement with the experiment, since the total dispersion of the lowest unoccupied band amounts to 2 eV. An additional source of broadening of the $L_{2}$ and $L_{3}$ XANES lines is the spin-orbit splitting of the Pd 4d states, which, according to our relativistic calculation of the atom, amounts to 0.52 eV. The selec- tion rules allow the $2p_{1/2} \to 5s_{1/2}$, $2p_{1/2} \to 4d_{3/2}$, $2p_{3/2} \to 4d_{3/2}$, $2p_{3/2} \to 4d_{5/2}$, and $2p_{3/2} \to$ $5s_{1/2}$ XANES transitions. Therefore, the $L_{3}$ XANES line, which is the principal line, has a greater width than does the $L_{2}$ line, as follows from [10]. The x-ray transitions with the participation of $ns_{1/2}$ final states are usually less intense; therefore, the excitation of the electrons of the 2p subshells to the orbitals of the $4a_{1g}, 4e_{g}$, and $6t_{1u}$ types of the $Pd_{6}Cl_{8}^{4+}$ cluster and to the orbitals of the $4a_{1g}, 6t_{1u}$, and $5e_{g}$ types of the $Pd_{6}Cl_{12}$ cluster, which contain significant contributions from the Pd 5s states (see Tables 1 and 2), produces a maximum of weak intensity in the XANES spectrum [10].

The results of the calculations are also in satisfactory agreement with the optical spectrum of PdCl₂ [9], as follows from a comparison of the calculated and experimental data presented in Table 4. In view of the large number of possible transitions and the simplified nature of the geometric model of the lattice which we adopted, the excitation energies were estimated from the differences between the energies of the MOs of the electrons participating in the optical processes. The utilization of the concept of a transition state in the cal- culation of energies by the Xα-SW method should not cause significant changes in the theo- retical spectrum due to the similarity between the composition of the occupied MOs and the composition of the unoccupied MOs. The data in Table 4 (in the example of the $Pd_{6}Cl_{8}^{4+}$ cluster) attest to the adequate conveyance of the features of the optical spectrum of PdCl₂.

In conclusion, we shall dwell on the discovery of many-electron effects in the x-ray photoelectron spectrum of PdCl₂, which are manifested as satellite lines accompanying both the main peaks of the valence band (Fig. 1) and the lines of the core levels (Fig. 5). In contrast to the x-ray photoelectron spectra of paramagnetic compounds, in the case of diamag- netic substances, the intensity of the satellites is usually lower [5], as is characteristic of PdCl₂. As is seen from Fig. 5, a satellite structure represented by peaks a and b is clearly observed on the side of the Pd $3d_{3/2}$ core line with higher energies. In this case, the intensity ratio $I_{a}/I_{b}$ practically coincides with the intensity ratio of the principal lines $I_{3d_{3/2}}/I_{3d_{5/2}}$, and the energy gaps between a and the Pd $3d_{3/2}$ line, on the one hand, and between peak b and the Pd $3d_{5/2}$ line, on the other hand, are identical and amount to 8.1 eV. The satellite line at $E_{b}=12$ eV in the spectrum of the valence band is also at a distance of 8.1 eV from the main maximum of the Pd 4d states (see Fig. 1).

An analysis of the results of the calculation of the clusters shows that the appearance of the satellite lines in the x-ray photoelectron spectra may be attributed to the excitation of the valence electrons to unoccupied orbitals, which takes place simultaneously with the photoemission of the core electrons, as well as valence electrons upon the x irradiation of samples of PdCl₂. The necessary conditions for the appearance of these so-called shakeup satellites include vacancies in the d subshell of the atom of the transition metal M in the original state and the participation of both states of M and states of the ligands L in the formation of the MOs whose electrons undergo "shakeup" (see, for example, [5, p. 302]). The electronic structure of PdCl₂ fully satisfies these conditions, creating the preconditions for the appearance of shakeup satellites in the x-ray photoelectron spectra. A list of the possible satellite excitations (Table 5) can be compiled with consideration of the "monopolar" selection rules (preservation of the symmetry of the initial and final MOs) and the data in Table 1 for the Pd₆Cl₈⁴⁺ cluster.

The data in Table 5 show that the $3e_g \to 4e_g$, $5t_{1u} \to 6t_{1u}$, and $4t_{1u} \to 6t_{1u}$ transitions, whose energies are close to the value of 8.1 eV (the energy gap between the satellite peaks and the main lines), apparently participate in the formation of peaks $a$ and b (see Fig. 5). As is seen from Fig. 5, a satellite structure which, however, has an unresolved character, is also observed at higher values of $E_b$. It may be assumed that it is formed as a result of high-energy excitations in the range from 9.1 eV and up (Table 5). The unresolved character of this structure and its significant expanse (Fig. 5) are due to the presence of several closely arranged transitions in this energy range and the appreciable magnitude of the spin-orbit splitting (5.2 eV) of the Pd 3d core levels, which result in the superposition of a considerable number of individual shakeup excitations in the x-ray photoelectron spectrum. From Table 5 it also follows that definite contributions to the intensity of the spectrum may also be made by transitions with small energies (2.55 and 5.55-5.64 eV). These contributions modify the lower part of the Pd $3d_{3/2}$ line, as well as the form of satellite lines $a$ and b. The main features of the satellite structure of the Pd 3d core levels are repeated in the spectrum of the valence band. For example, along with the previously mentioned maxi-mum at $E_b$ = 12 eV, the x-ray photoelectron spectrum (Fig. 1) also shows satellites beyond the Cl 3s band, which are similar to the high-energy shakeup excitations accompanying the core lines.

Thus, the x-ray photoelectron spectra obtained in the present work and the results of the quantum-chemical calculations with the use of x-ray emission and absorption spectra lead to a conclusion that the conceptions of the electronic structure of palladium chloride in the literature are insufficiently correct. This applies both to the overestimation of the role of the ionic character of the Pd-Cl bond* and the structural features of the valence band and the conduction band of PdCl₂. The chemical bonding of the lattice atoms of this compound has an essentially covalent character due to the effective interaction of the M and L orbitals, as is manifested by the considerable mixing of the states of the metal and the ligands accompanying the formation of both the valence band and the low-energy states of the conduction band.
