# Perovskite-type hydrides–synthesis, structures and properties

Dedicated to Professor Dr. Reiner Kirchheim on the occasion of his $65^{th}$ birthday

The present study gives an overview of the fundamental material properties of perovskite-type hydrides. Formation ability of the perovskite-type hydrides has been reasonably explained on the basis of the geometric restrictions on the composed ions using the Goldschmidt tolerance factor. Characteristic crystal structures and charge density maps of the perovskite-type hydrides without/with transition metals, such as $NaMgH_3/CaNiH_3$, were also examined in detail to clarify the relationship between the structures (atomic and electronic) and the hydriding properties.

**Keywords:** Hydrogen; Hydrides; Perovskites; Chemical bonding; Transformation

## 1. Introduction

Compounds with perovskite structure are typically expressed as $ABX_3$, where $A$ and $B$ are cations and $X$ is an anion. The idealised structure exhibits a primitive cubic unit cell with ions $A$ at the corners, $B$ at the centres, and $X$ at the faced-centred positions of a unit cell composed of $[BX_6]$-octahedra and $[AX_{12}]$-cubo-octahedra. Typically, these structures depend on the values of the Goldschmidt tolerance factor that can be defined as follows [1]:

$$
t = \frac{R_A + R_X}{\sqrt{2}(R_B + R_X)} \tag{1}
$$

where $R_A$, $R_B$ and $R_X$ are the ionic radii of the cations $A$, $B$ and the anion $X$, respectively. This factor provides necessary, although insufficient, conditions for the formation ability of perovskite-type structures. If $t$ is close to 1, the structure is presumed to be closer to the ideal cubic unit cell. When the value of $R_A$ is smaller than ideal and $t$ is less than or equal to 0.81, the $[BX_6]$-octahedra tilts in order to fill space and the $B$-$X$-$B$ bond angle changes from linear to bent. For example, few perovskite-type oxides exhibit the ideal cubic structure, whereas many oxides have slightly distorted variants with lower symmetry, such as tetragonal, orthorhombic and trigonal lattices [1, 2]. Perovskite structures have specific $t$ values–approximately $0.78 < t < 1.05$ for oxides.

So far, more than 500 perovskite-type oxides ($ABO_3$) have been extensively investigated [1, 2]; these are also known to provide various material functions such as electronic/ionic conductivity [3, 4], superconductivity [5, 6], ferroelectricity [7, 8], catalytic effects [9, 10], etc. Furthermore, perovskite structures have been prepared for carbides, nitrides, fluorides, chlorides, bromides and iodides [1, 2].

However, to the best of the authors' knowledge, only about 20 perovskite-type (and related) hydrides have been reported; these are listed in Table 1: $SrLiH_3$ [11], $BaLiH_3$ [11], $EuLiH_3$ [12, 13], $NaMgH_3$ [14–18], $KMgH_3$ [19–21], $RbMgH_3$ [22], $HT$-$CsMgH_3$ [23], $RbCaH_3$ [24], $CsCaH_3$ [22, 25], $Na(Li_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3$ [26–28], $\beta$-$Na(Na_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3$ [29], $K(Li_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3$ [28], $K(Na_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3$ [28, 30], $CaCoH_3$ [31], $CaNiH_3$ [31–33], $YbNiH_{2.7}$ [34], $CaPdH_2$ [35], $SrPdH_{2.7}$ [36], $EuPdH_3$ [37] and $YbPdH_{2.7}$ [34]. The number of perovskite-type hydrides reported thus far is considerably less than oxides. The first possible reason is inferred from the fact that the anion state of hydrogen is less stable than that of oxygen in ionic compounds because of the difference in their electronegativities ($\chi_P$); $\chi_P = 2.1$ and 3.5 for hydrogen and oxygen, respectively [38]. Secondly, it is suggested that the number of combinations of cations $A$ and $B$ for which charge neutrality is maintained in perovskite-type hydrides is limited as compared to that in perovskite oxides. This is because the total valences counterbalanced by the anions are ideally three in the former and less than six in the latter.

Presently, the most important property of perovskite-type hydrides is their high density of hydrogen, as in the cases of interstitial-type hydrides like $TiFeH_{1.94}$ [39] and $LaNi_5H_{6.4}$ [39], and complex-type hydrides [40] like $LiBH_4$ [41], $LiNH_2$ [42], $LiAlH_4$ [43] and $Mg_2NiH_4$ [39], also listed in Table 1. The hydrides with a high density of hydrogen are believed to be essential for developing safe, compact and lightweight hydrogen storage materials for fuel cells as well as hydrogen combustion applications [44].

The first objective of this study is to systematically clarify the formation ability of perovskite-type hydrides based on the Goldschmidt tolerance factor. This study enables us to predict and synthesise new perovskite-type hydrides that have not yet been reported. Second, we focus on the relationship between the atomic/electronic structures and the hydriding properties of the perovskite-type hydrides. Reversible hydriding properties with decomposition and recombination reactions were confirmed for the perovskite-type hydrides with typical ionic bonding, such as $NaMgH_3$. Transformation from the perovskite-type hydrides into complex-type hydrides was suggested for the perovskite-type hydrides containing transition metals, such as $CaNiH_3$.

The as-milled samples were then heat-treated for 3–120 h at 5 MPa of hydrogen at 673 K, to obtain samples with larger crystallite sizes and lower lattice strains.

The structural characteristics of the as-milled samples were determined by X-ray diffraction (Cu-K$_\alpha$ radiation) and neutron powder diffraction (HERMES, belonging to the Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, installed at the JRR-3M reactor at the Japan Atomic Energy Agency, Tokai [45]). The atomic/electronic structures were analysed by Rietveld and maximum entropy method (MEM) [46], using computer programs RIETAN-2000 [47] and PRIMA [48]. In the sample preparation and characterisation, we paid attention to avoid impurity effects on the dehydriding and rehydriding properties of the samples as far as possible. Hence the material and shape for the vial were carefully selected, so as to lower the amount of elemental Fe contamination during the milling process; less than 0.3 mol.%. In addition, the samples were always handled in a glove box filled with purified helium or argon (dew point below 183 K), so as to minimise any oxidation effect on the samples.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Formation ability based on Goldschmidt tolerance factor

The estimation of the formation ability of the perovskite-type hydrides based on the Goldschmidt tolerance factor (Eq. (1)), leads to the data given in Fig. 1. The $t$ values range from 0.90 to 1.10, assuming a radius of 0.140 nm for H$^-$ [49,50] and using the Shannon effective ionic radii [51]. The detailed procedure for estimation of the formation ability of the perovskite-type structures in Fig. 1 is as follows [18,52]:

i) Equation (1) is rewritten as $R_B = (R_A + R_X)/(\sqrt{2t}) - R_X$, and the equation is shown as the dotted inclined lines in Fig. 1. Slopes of the two lines are $1/(\sqrt{2t})$ with $t = 0.90$ and 1.10, respectively. Thus the area between the lines corresponds to the formation ability of the perovskite-type structures.

ii) The perovskite-type structure is composed of $[BX_6]$-octahedra and $[AX_{12}]$-cubo-octahedra for the composition of $ABX_3$. On $[BX_6]$-octahedra, Pauling's first rule about the stabilisation of the polyhedron suggests that the ratio of ionic radii $R_B$ and $R_X$ needs to match the equation; $0.414 < R_B/R_X < 0.732$. Therefore, the ionic radii for octahedral sites are calculated to range from 0.058 to 0.102 nm (between the dashed horizontal lines in the Fig. 1), using an ionic radius of hydrogen, $R_{\mathrm{H}}$, of 0.140 nm.

In fact, (inverse, ordered) perovskite structures [1,2] have been reported for $\mathrm{SrLiH_3}$ [11], $\mathrm{BaLiH_3}$ [11], $\mathrm{EuLiH_3}$ [12,13], $\mathrm{NaMgH_3}$ [14–18], $\mathrm{KMgH_3}$ [19–21], $\mathrm{RbMgH_3}$ [22], $\mathrm{HT-CsMgH_3}$ [23], $\mathrm{RbCaH_3}$ [24], $\mathrm{CsCaH_3}$ [22,25], $\mathrm{Na(Li_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3}$ [26–28], $\beta$-$\mathrm{Na(Na_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3}$ [29], $\mathrm{K(Li_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3}$ [28], $\mathrm{K(Na_{0.5}Al_{0.5})H_3}$ [28,30], $\mathrm{CaCoH_3}$ [31], $\mathrm{CaNiH_3}$ [31–33] and $\mathrm{EuPdH_3}$ [37], as represented by the solid circles in the quadrilateral regions indicated by thick lines in Fig. 1. The range of $t$ for the perovskite-type hydrides with stoichiometric compositions ($ABH_3$) is narrower ($0.90 < t < 1.10$) than that for the perovskite-type oxides ($0.78 < t < 1.05$); one of reasons for this result is the instability in the anion state of hydrogen compared to that of oxygen due to the smaller value of its electronegativity ($\chi_{\mathrm{P}}$); $\chi_{\mathrm{P}}=2.1$ and 3.5 for hydrogen and oxygen, respectively [38]. If hydrogen is stoichiometrically less than the perovskite composition $ABH_3$, such as in the cases of $\mathrm{CaPdH_2}$ [35], $\mathrm{SrPdH_{2.7}}$ [36], $\mathrm{YbNiH_{2.7}}$ [34] and $\mathrm{YbPdH_{2.7}}$ [34], the $t$ values may tend to be smaller than 0.90. These results support the suggestion that the formation ability of perovskite-type hydrides can be reasonably explained on the basis of the geometric restrictions on ions using the Goldschmidt tolerance factor. ### 3.2. Atomic and electronic structures
The precise atomic structures of perovskite-type deuterides and hydrides were confirmed by conducting diffraction measurements using neutron and synchrotron X-rays, respectively. Furthermore, the electronic structures were experimentally examined by conducting MEM/Rietveld analysis of the synchrotron X-ray diffraction profiles. In this study, the hydrogen occupations site and the chemical bondings of the perovskite-type hydrides without/with transition metals, such as NaMgH₃/CaNiH₃, have been investigated.

#### 3.2.1. NaMgH₃ – the perovskite-type hydride without transition metal
The unit cell of NaMgH₃ was identified as an orthorhombically distorted perovskite structure in space group Pnma (no. 62), which is similar to the reported structure [14, 15] as shown in Table 1. The precise atomic structure of NaMgH₃, schematically shown in Fig. 2, was confirmed by the synchrotron X-ray diffraction measurement [53]. We refer to hydrogen at tetrahedral 4c-site composed of 2Na2Mg and octahedral 8d-site composed of 4Na2Mg as H₄c and H₈d, respectively. H₄c and H₈d are the nearest neighbour hydrogen from Na and Mg, and they correspond to one third and two thirds of the total hydrogen in NaMgH₃, respectively.

The MEM charge density maps of the (040) and (020) planes of NaMgH₃ were examined using synchrotron X-ray diffraction measurement and MEM/Rietveld analysis [53], as shown in Fig. 2. The charge density distributions of Na and Mg are localised within the sphere of the averaged radii (0.105 nm and 0.090 nm), and a majority of the charge density distributions of H₄c and H₈d are located within the radii of 0.120 nm and 0.110 nm, respectively. By estimating the number of electrons within the Na, Mg, H₄c and H₈d regions, the ionicities are expressed as Na$^{1.00+}$, Mg$^{1.97+}$, H₄c$^{0.23-}$ and H₈d$^{0.13-}$, respectively. Since these values are similar to Na$^{0.99+}$ in NaH [54] and Mg$^{1.91+}$ in MgH₂ [55], Na and Mg in NaMgH₃ are almost ionised as Na$^+$ and Mg$^{2+}$, respectively. Moreover, H₄c and H₈d at the different occupation sites are suggested to be anions. This electronic structure is consistent with the results of the DV-X$\alpha$ molecular orbital method [56].

### 3.3. Hydriding properties

To clarify the hydriding properties of the perovskite-type hydrides, both the dehydriding and rehydriding reactions and the thermal stabilities were investigated by mass spectroscopy/thermogravimetry profiles and hydrogen pressure-composition ($p-c$) isotherms. In this study, we focus on two kinds of perovskite-type hydrides-$NaMgH_3$ indicating the ionic bonding characteristic between the constituent metals and H, and $CaNiH_3$ implying the covalent bonding characteristic between Ni and H.

#### 3.3.1. $NaMgH_3$ - dehydriding and rehydriding reactions

Figure 4 indicates the results of the (a) mass spectroscopy and (b) thermogravimetry of the as-milled $NaMgH_3$ at 0.1 MPa of helium. Two overlapping weight-loss reactions were detected. The total hydrogen released from the sample reached up to 5.8(2) wt.%; this value is in good agreement with the total calculated hydrogen content in the composition of $NaMgH_3$.

The $p-c$ isotherms of the as-milled $NaMgH_3$ were measured at 653 K, 673 K and 693 K [53], as shown in Fig. 5. The higher plateau pressure $P_{HP}$ is one order lower than that of the single phase $MgH_2$ at 673 K (typically about 2 MPa) [60]. On the other hand, the lower plateau pressure $P_{LP}$ corresponds well with that of NaH at the same temperature (about 0.030 MPa) [61]. The total hydrogen released was 6.0(3) wt.%; this value is in reasonable agreement with both the experimental result of the thermogravimetry (shown in Fig. 4b) and the composition of $NaMgH_3$.

On the other hand, only elemental Na and Mg can be observed at point $(f)$. Therefore, by using the enthalpy changes of each reaction ($\Delta H_{HP}$ and $\Delta H_{LP}$), the progression of the dehydriding reactions of $NaMgH_3$ can be expressed as follows:

$$
\mathrm{NaMgH}_{3} \rightarrow \mathrm{NaH}+\mathrm{Mg}+\mathrm{H}_{2}+\Delta H_{\mathrm{HP}} \tag{2}
$$

$$
\mathrm{NaH}+\mathrm{Mg}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{Na}+\mathrm{Mg}+\frac{3}{2} \mathrm{H}_{2}+\Delta H_{\mathrm{LP}} \tag{3}
$$

Furthermore, we believe that the sequential reactions (2) and (3) correspond to the higher and the lower plateau pressures in Fig. 5, respectively.

The enthalpy change $\Delta H$ and the entropy change $\Delta S$ in reactions (2) and (3) were calculated using the van't Hoff plot shown in Fig. 5. The values of $\Delta H_{HP}$ and $\Delta H_{LP}$ are $-93.9(6)\ \mathrm{kJ/molH_{2}}$ and $-102.2(4)\ \mathrm{kJ/molH_{2}}$ for reactions (2) and (3), respectively. The former value is between those reported $(-114.1\ \mathrm{kJ/molH_{2}}$ for NaH [61] and $-74.5\ \mathrm{kJ/molH_{2}}$ for $\mathrm{MgH_{2}}$ [60]) and the latter value is comparable to the one reported for NaH. The values of $\Delta S_{HP}$ and $\Delta S_{LP}$ are $-116.2(9)\ \mathrm{J/molH_{2}\cdot K}$ and $-125.9(6)\ \mathrm{J/molH_{2}\cdot K}$ for reactions (2) and (3), respectively. These are comparable to the standard entropy of hydrogen $(-130\ \mathrm{J/molH_{2}\cdot K})$, corresponding primarily to the change from molecular hydrogen gas to dissolved solid hydrogen in metal-hydrogen systems.

Furthermore, the decomposed phase of elemental Na and Mg observed at point $(f)$ was recombined with hydrogen at 1 MPa at 673 K for 3 h [17, 18]. As is also shown in Fig. 6 (indicated as $(r)$), the single phase $NaMgH_3$ with the perovskite structure is clearly detected, indicating that $NaMgH_3$ exhibits dehydriding and rehydriding reactions, which is important from the viewpoint of hydrogen storage property.


### 3.3.2. $CaNiH_3$ – continuous transformation from perovskite-type into complex-type hydrides

Figure 7 shows the results of the (a) mass spectroscopy and (b) thermogravimetry of the milled $CaT-H$ for $T=$ Ni, Co and Fe at 0.1 MPa of helium [31].

The mass spectroscopy profiles indicate that the peaks of the dehydriding reactions appear at approximately 580 K, 600 K and 720 K for $T=$ Ni, Co and Fe, respectively, as shown in Fig. 7a. It should be noted that the starting temperatures of the dehydriding reactions for $T=$ Ni and Co are lower than 400 K. The dehydriding reactions proceed even above 400 K and the amount of hydrogen released gradually increases, which is consistent with the thermogravimetric results, as shown in Fig. 7b. The amount released from $CaNiH_3$ below 650 K reaches up to 1.0(2) wt.%. This value corresponds to approximately one-third of the total hydrogen content (2.9(2) wt.%) determined by the hydrogen analysis of $CaNiH_3$. The dehydriding profiles of $CaCoH_3$ are similar to those of $CaNiH_3$, and the amount of hydrogen released from $CaCoH_3$ below 600 K is almost half of that from $CaNiH_3$, as shown in Fig. 7b. On the other hand, for $T=$ Fe ($Ca_2FeH_6$), the starting temperature of the dehydriding reactions is higher than 600 K.

The $p-c$ isotherms of the as-milled $CaNiH_3$ were measured at 573 K, 623 K and 673 K, as shown in Fig. 8. The total hydrogen released was 1.0(3) wt.% at 623 K and 673 K; this value is in reasonable agreement with the experimental result of thermogravimetry shown in Fig. 7b. A plateau pressure of approximately 1 MPa was observed in the dehydriding reaction at 673 K. This pressure is seven orders of magnitude greater (that is significantly less stable) than that of $CaH_2$ at 673 K (typically about $1 \times 10^{-7}$ MPa) [62]. Since the plateau pressure is extremely steep at 573 K, two different dehydriding reactions are expected at temperatures above and below 623 K.

Figure 9 shows the results of the X-ray diffraction measurements that were carried out at an ambient temperature as well as the dehydriding profiles of $CaT-H$ for $T=$ Ni, Co or Fe [31]. The results suggest that the dehydriding reactions of these transition metals proceed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CaNiH}_{3} \rightarrow \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Ca}_{2} \mathrm{NiH}_{4}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Ni}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{H}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{CaH}_{2}+\mathrm{Ni}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{H}_{2}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CaCoH}_{3} \rightarrow \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Ca}_{2} \mathrm{CoH}_{5}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Co}+\frac{1}{4} \mathrm{H}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{CaH}_{2}+\mathrm{Co}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{H}_{2}
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Ca}_{2} \mathrm{FeH}_{6}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Fe} \rightarrow \mathrm{CaH}_{2}+\mathrm{Fe}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{H}_{2}
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

For $T=$ Ni, the phase before heating is $CaNiH_3$ and no obvious peak-shift can be observed in this phase up to 550 K. This suggests that $CaNiH_3$ is continuously transformed to $Ca_2NiH_4$ ($CaNi_{0.5}H_2$) accompanied by partial hydrogen release and Ni precipitation during the dehydriding reactions, as shown in the first step of reaction (4).

Fig. 9. X-ray diffraction profiles at ambient temperature of the as-milled and heat-treated $CaT-H$ for $T = Fe$ and $Ni$. For $T = Ni$, $CaNiH_3$ is continuously transformed to $Ca_2NiH_4$ ($CaNi_{0.5}H_2$) accompanied by partial hydrogen release and Ni precipitation at 550 K, and then decomposes to $CaH_2$ and Ni at 650 K. For $T = Fe$, $Ca_2FeH_6$ decomposes to $CaH_2$ and Fe at 780 K.

For $T = Co$, the dehydriding profiles of $CaCoH_3$ are similar to those of $CaNiH_3$, and the hydrogen amount released from $CaCoH_3$ below 600 K is almost half of that from $CaNiH_3$, as shown in Fig. 7. This implies that $CaCoH_3$ is continuously transformed into $Ca_2CoH_5$ ($CaCo_{0.5}H_{2.5}$), similar to the case of $T = Ni$.

For $T = Fe$, only $CaH_2$ and elemental Fe are observed after the dehydriding reactions of $Ca_2FeH_6$, and there is no hydrogen release below 600 K.

These reactions suggest that the phases stabilised by geometric factors for perovskite-type hydrides continuously transform into those stabilised by electronic factors for complex-type hydrides during the dehydriding reactions. Each first step of reactions (4) and (5) proceeds below 550 K, indicating that the stabilities governed by the geometric factors and electronic factors are comparable to each other in $CaT-H$ for $T = Ni$ or Co. The dehydriding and rehydriding reactions accompanied by transformation of chemical bonding are useful for advanced hydrogen storage materials combined with the high hydrogen density of complex-type hydrides and reversible hydriding properties of interstitial-type hydrides.

### 3.4. Prospect – material functions of perovskite-type hydrides

Perovskite-type hydrides are expected to behave as functional materials owing to their high density of hydrogen and large variety of chemical bonding mentioned above.

Judging from the formation ability shown in Fig. 1, we predict new perovskite-type hydrides including complex ions, such as $[NH_4]^+$ and $[BH_4]^-$, will presumably achieve extremely high density of hydrogen for $(NH_4)_3AlH_6$ (more than 20 wt.%), $NaMg(BH_4)_3$ (more than 13 wt.%), etc. Other perovskite-type hydrides with appreciable amounts of lithium and hydrogen ion vacancy are expected to exhibit ion conduction, such as the complex hydride, $LiBH_4$ [63], and perovskite-type oxides, $La_{0.51}Li_{0.34}TiO_{2.94}$ [64]. Furthermore, we focus on new perovskite-type compounds including not only hydrogen but also oxygen or nitrogen as an anion. Actually, a mixed oxide-hydride perovskite compound, such as $LaSrCoO_3H_{0.7}$, is expected to have specific magnetic and electronic characteristics [65], such as possible superconductivity of the perovskite-type hydrides [66–68].

## 4. Conclusions

The fundamental material properties of perovskite-type hydrides as a new series of compounds with a high density of hydrogen were studied experimentally.

1) The formation ability of perovskite-type hydrides was reasonably explained on the basis of the geometric restrictions on the composed ions using the Goldschmidt tolerance factor $t$. The range of $t$ for the perovskite-type hydrides with a stoichiometric composition $(ABH_3)$ is narrower than that for the perovskite-type oxides, originating from the instability in the anionic state of hydrogen compared to that of oxygen.

2) The electronic structures of perovskite-type hydrides were obtained from MEM/Rietveld analysis of the synchrotron X-ray diffraction profiles. The electrons of Na, Mg and H were localised in $NaMgH_3$. Although Ca was almost completely ionised, the charge density distribution was partially overlapped among Ni and H in $CaNiH_3$.

3) The specific hydriding properties of perovskite-type hydrides were investigated in detail for the hydrides without/with transition metals, such as $NaMgH_3/CaNiH_3$. The two-step dehydriding and rehydriding reactions of 6 wt.% hydrogen were experimentally confirmed in the perovskite-type hydride $NaMgH_3$. The dehydriding reactions implied that the perovskite-type hydride $CaNiH_3$ is continuously transformed into the complex-type hydride $Ca_2NiH_4$ accompanied by partial hydrogen release and Ni precipitation.
