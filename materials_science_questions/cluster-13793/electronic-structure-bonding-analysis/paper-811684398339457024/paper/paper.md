## ABSTRACT

Electronic structure, Born effective charges, and spontaneous polarization of multiferroic single crystal $BiMn_2O_5$ have been investigated in the framework of density functional theory. The relative stability of the ground state and the origin of multiferroicity for magnetism and ferroelectricity are addressed. The results reveal that the stability of antiferromagnetic (AFM) state is better than the ferromagnetic (FM) and ferrimagnetic configurations. The Born effective charge tensors ($Z^*$) have been calculated for this compound using a Berry-phase approach, compared to their nominal ionic values, the $Z^*$ of Mn atoms show anomalous difference. By investigating the electric structure of $BiMn_2O_5$, there exists obviously hybridization between Bi 6s and O 2p states, our calculations indicate that the $6s^2$ lone pair on the formally trivalent Bi ion plays an important role in inducing the ferroelectric distortion.

## 1. Introduction

Multiferroics are an interesting group of compounds that have possessed electric, magnetic, and structural order parameters that result in simultaneous ferroelectrics, ferromagnetism, and ferroelasticity in the same phase [1–6]. These materials provide opportunities for potential applications in the information storage, the electric field controlled ferromagnetic response devices, and the transducers with magnetically modulated piezoelectricity [7–9]. However, earlier work on perovskite ($ABO_3$) structure oxides [10] revealed that the existence of ferroelectricity by the off centering of B cation which requires empty d orbitals is inconsistent with the magnetism, which requires filled d orbitals, so very few multiferroic materials exist in nature or have been synthesized experimentally. On the other hand, for ferroelectromagnets which display simultaneous electric and magnetic order, the cross-correlation between them may cause the magnetoelectric effect (the induction of magnetization by means of an electric field and induction of polarization by means of magnetic field). More recently the related phenomenon has been observed in $TbMnO_3$ [11], $YMnO_3$ [12], and $HoMnO_3$ [13]. However, multiferroic materials are few till now, and thus their puzzling and fascinating physical phenomenon and widely application prospects incentive us to search for new candidates, and carry out further research on them both in theory and experiment.

In multiferroics, an impressive example is the $RMn_2O_5$ (R=La, rare earth, Y and Bi) family of oxides, which are isostructural and contain infinite chains of $Mn^{4+}O_6$ and octahedral sharing edges, linked together by $Mn^{3+}O_5$ and $RO_8$ units [14–16]. These compounds undergo a ferroelectric transition just slightly below the antiferromagnetic (AFM) transition temperature, $T_N$=39–45 K [17–20]. For $BiMn_2O_5$, the high-resolution neutron diffraction data (NPD) [15] gave the crystal structure in details. The magnetic structure of this compound has been investigated in Refs. [15,21], the results revealed that the lattice geometry causes an inherent magnetic frustration, and there also exists strong magnetoelectric coupling in this system. However, at present, there are no reports about electric structure for this compound, the relation between them and the origin of the simultaneous coexistence of magnetism and ferroelectricity are not entirely clear. In this paper, we present the first-principles density functional calculations on $BiMn_2O_5$, the magnetic order, orbital hybridization, and the origin of the simultaneous occurrence of magnetism and ferroelectricity are discussed.

The spontaneous polarization of the ferroelectric state can be computed directly from the Berry-phase approach, given by the Born effective charge $Z^*$, which play a key role in understanding both the polar ground state and lattice dynamics. The Born effective charges are tensors, which are defined as

$$
Z_{i,\alpha,\beta}^{*}=\frac{\Omega}{e}\frac{\partial P_{\beta}}{\partial u_{i,\alpha}}=\frac{\Omega}{e}\frac{\partial F_{i,\alpha}}{\partial u_{i,\beta}} \tag{1}
$$

where $Z^*$ is a polarization per unit cell induced by displacement of atomic $i$ in direction $\alpha$ or force exerted on atom by macroscopic electric field in the direction $\beta$.

## 2. Computational method

In this paper, the geometric optimization, electronic, and magnetic properties are carried out using the full potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW) method as implemented in WIEN2K package [22]. We adopt generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [23,24] for the exchange correlation potential. The radii of atomic spheres are 2.13, 1.86, and 1.65 a.u. for Bi, Mn, and O atoms, respectively. The cutoff parameter $R_{MT}K_{MAX}$ is taken as 7.0, where $R_{MT}$ is the smallest radius of atoms and $K_{MAX}$ is the maximum value of the reciprocal lattice vectors used in the plane wave expansion. We have used $5 \times 4 \times 7$ meshes in the first Brillouin zone, and the self-consistent calculations are considered to be converge only when the integrate charge difference per formula unit, between input charge density $[\rho_{n-1}]$ and output $[\rho_{n}]$ is less than 0.00001.

In order to obtain Born effective charge tensors and the spontaneous polarization, the pseudopotential calculations of total energy and response properties are performed using the ABINIT [25] and Troullier-Martins plane wave potentials codes. We use the plane wave cut off of 35 hartrees, the $6 \times 6 \times 6$ Monkhorst-Pack $k$-point mesh [26], and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof functional (PBE) of GGA for the exchange correlation potential.

## 3. Results and discussion

First, we carry out both geometric optimization and atomic relaxation from the experimental crystal structure to find the equilibrium configuration. In Table 1, we list the calculated and experimental equilibrium lattice parameters of this compound. Our calculated lattice parameters are in good agreement with the experimental data. From Fig. 1, we can see the crystallographic structure of $BiMn_{2}O_{5}$ along the $c$ axis. There are two different oxygen environments for Mn: Mn1 ($Mn^{4+}$) cations are located with distorted octahedral environments, the $Mn^{4+}O_{6}$ octahedra form infinite linear chains along the $c$ axis, sharing edges via O2 and O3 atoms; Mn2 ($Mn^{3+}$) cations are surrounded by five oxygens, forming $Mn_{3}O_{5}$ distorted tetragonal pyramids. The Bi cations are in the eightfold-oxygen-coordinated holes of the network. As $BiMn_{2}O_{5}$ present a magnetic structure with propagation vector $k=(1/2\ 0\ 1/2)$ at low temperatures [15,16], we use a $2 \times 1 \times 1$ supercell (containing 64 atoms) along the $k_{x}$ direction. In order to explore the electronic structure and magnetic order in the ground state, we have performed the total energy calculations of $BiMn_{2}O_{5}$ in ferromagnetic (FM), ferrimagnetic, and two different antiferromagnetic (AFM) states with GGA, respectively. According to the charge density figures and Mulliken charge population, we conclude that the bonding character between Mn-O and Bi-O is a combination of covalent and ionic behavior. And that, the Mn-O bond show stronger covalent character than Bi-O bond. The Bi-O bond mainly presents ionic nature, and there only exists a weak covalent bonding. These results are in good agreement with our DOS computation. Electronic structure calculations reveal the covalent bonding between Mn and O in $BiMn_2O_5$ arises from the hybridization between Mn 3d and O 2p states. The hybridization between Mn1, Mn2 3d and surrounding O 2p leads to the bonding states, and the covalent Mn-O character will weaken the short-range repulsions and lower the energy, and these bonds act to stabilize the distortions for the ferroelectric phase. On the other hand, the coexistence of covalent and weak ionic Bi-O bond play a dominate role to balance the long-range Coulomb forces and the short-range repulsions to form the stabilized distorted ferroelectric phase. The hybridizations between Mn 3d and O 2p, Bi 6s and O 2p states lead to the charge transfer from Bi and Mn to O, which reduces the total energy and then enhance the stability of the ferroelectric structure of $BiMn_2O_5$. Due to the hybridizations of Bi-O and Mn-O, the spin magnetic moments of Mn1 and Mn2 are 2.5377 and $3.3137~\mu$B within GGA+SO, which are very close the experimental results.

The following discussion mainly focuses on the partial DOS of $Mn^{4+}$ (Mn1:$3d^3$) and $Mn^{3+}$ (Mn2:$3d^4$), which are presented in Fig. 7(a) and (b). From the experimental crystallographic structure (in Fig. 1), $Mn^{4+}$ (Mn1 atoms) are located in the distortional octahedral environments. Generally, the 3d orbitals within the central octahedral environments are split into $e_g$ ($d_{3z2-r2}$ and $d_{x2-y2}$) and $t_{2g}$ ($d_{xy}$, $d_{yz}$, and $d_{xz}$) states, so we present these states in Fig. 7(a). From Fig. 7(a) we find that the magnetism of $Mn^{4+}$ mainly come from $t_{2g}$ orbitals, because the distribution of spin electron cloud in the vicinity of the Fermi level is mainly from $t_{2g}$ states. However, it is found that $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ states do not obviously split into two subbands, the degeneration of $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ states mainly attributes to the crystal distortion. From Fig. 7(b), we can obviously see that in the vicinity of the Fermi level for $Mn^{3+}$ (Mn2) the four unpaired electrons mainly occupy $d_{z2}$, $d_{xy}$, $d_{xz}$, and $d_{yz}$ orbitals. From the crystal structure in Fig. 1, the $Mn^{4+}$ cations are linked by O2 and O3 oxygens along the c direction, and the two $Mn^{4+}$ neighbors are also linked by $Mn^{3+}$ cations via O3 and O4 atoms. The magnetic indirect ($Mn^{4+}-O-Mn^{3+}-O-Mn^{4+}$) coupling mechanism should be super-exchange or double-exchange [32,33], because the lattice distortion in this compound leads to the degeneration within Mn1 3d ($t_{2g}$ and $e_g$) and Mn2 3d ($d_{3z2-r2}$, $d_{xy}$, $d_{xz}$, and $d_{yz}$) orbitals. The double-exchange magnetic mechanism requires $Mn^{3+}$ ($t_{2g}^3$, $e_g^1$) and $Mn^{4+}$ ($t_{2g}^3$, $e_g^0$) as magnetically equivalent sites for $e_g$ electrons hopping and for maintaining the $e_g$ hole carrier concentration, however, the different spin arrangement and orbital occupation prevent the double-exchange magnetic interaction and in favor of the super-exchange magnetic interaction.

Born effective charges ($Z^*$) defined by the change in polarization created by atomic displacement, so the atomic charge can provide a fundamental understanding for the underlying basis of the spontaneous polarization. In order to investigate the origin of the ferroelectric behavior, we compute the Born effective charge tensors (in Table 4) and macroscopic spontaneous polarization $P$ (ionic polarization $P_{ion}$ and electronic one $P_{ele}$) [34], we use the Berry-phase method developed by King-Smith and Vanderbilt [35,36]. Compared with the nominal ionic value, the $Z^*$ could reflect the covalence of the bonding environment of each atom. The nominal ionic values for Bi, O, Mn1, and Mn2 are +3, −2, +4, and +3, comparing these values with $Z^*$ (Table 2), we find the $Z^*$ are anomalous, this anomalisim commonly as an important feather was also found in other ferroelectric materials [37–40]. Furthermore, the Born effective charge ($xy$ component) of Mn2 and O3 atoms is clearly far more anomalous than those of the rest, which reveals their special role in ferroelectricity of BiMn₂O₅. Due to the $Z_{xz}^*, Z_{yz}^*, Z_{zx}^*, Z_{zy}^*$ of these atoms are zero or nearly zero, so the direction of the spontaneously polarization should along the ab plane. Based on Table 2, we find that for the same atom some components of $Z^*$ for Bi, Mn1, Mn2, and O1 have opposite direction, which reveals the strong anisotropy in $Z^*$ for this compound. In order to botain the spontaneous polarization, we carry out the calculation from density functional linear response to finite field. The calculated the spontaneous polarization $P$ is 6.0 μC/cm². In our finite electric field calculations, we get the stress tensors, $\sigma_{11}, \sigma_{22}$, and $\sigma_{33}$ are −12.58, −31.97, and −39.94 GPa with the pressure=28.16 GPa.

Next, we give the static charge and spin distribution between Mn–O and Bi–O bonds in order to further investigate the charge transfer and magnetic coupling, it can also help us to explore the origin of magnetism and ferroelectricity. The partial charge and spin distribution are presented as below: the charge population of the bonds of Mn1–O2, Mn1–O3, Mn1–O4 is 0.31, 0.35, and 0.40 electrons, the spin of which is 0.02, 0.03, 0.03; the charge population of Mn2–O1, Mn2–O3, Mn2–O4 is 0.35, 0.36, and 0.39 electrons, the spin of which is −0.01, −0.03, −0.02; the charge population of Bi–O1, Bi–O2, Bi–O4 is 0.12, 0.13, 0.19 electrons, the spin of which is 0.00, 0.00, 0.00, respectively. The charge distribution reveals that there is more charge transfer between Mn–O than Bi–O, which is in agreement with the covalent Mn–O bond and coexistence of covalent and ionic Bi–O bond in the above discussion; the spin distribution indicates that the super-exchange interaction between Mn1 and Mn2 are mainly transmitted by Mn–O bond, while Bi–O bond has no contribution to transmit magnetic coupling, it mainly induces the breaking of the structural symmetry and leads to the ferroelectricity.

As discussed by Khomskii [41], there are several different microscopic sources of ferroelectricity in multiferroics, and hence they can be divided into two categories: type-I multiferroics, ferroelectricity, and magnetism are independent, at the same time the coupling between them is very weak; type-II multiferroics, magnetism causes ferroelectricity and a strong coupling between the two. For BiMn₂O₅, the ferroelectricity of BiMn₂O₅ arises from two factors: the role of Bi lone pairs (type-I multiferroics) and noncollinear magnetic ordering [15] (type-II multiferroics). As we can not calculate separately the direct contribution to spontaneous polarization of one of the factors, it is difficult to evaluate which factor play a major role. However, Kim et al.'s reports (“observation of a multiferroic critical end point” in PNAS (2009) 106, 15573–15576 and “magnetic-field-induced quantum phase transition in multiferroic BiMn₂O₅” in APS March Meeting, March 5–9, 2007) reveal that a magnetic-field-induced metaelectric critical end point is realized in BiMn₂O₅ near zero temperature, therefore BiMn₂O₅ should mainly belong to the type-II multiferroics with strong magnetoelectric coupling.

## 4. Conclusions
In this paper, the electronic structure, Born effective charges and spontaneous polarization of multiferroic single crystal BiMn₂O₅ have been investigated in the framework of density functional theory. Total energy calculations indicate that AFM state is the ground state. The charge density distribution shows that Mn–O bond shows obviously covalent character and Bi–O bond is mixture of ionic and infirm covalent due to the Bi 6s lone pair electrons, and the strong hybridization between Mn–O is in favor of forming the distorted ferroelectric structure. The magnetic correlation between the nearest Mn³⁺ and Mn⁴⁺ is superexchange interaction, which results in AFM semiconductor ground state with a band gap of 0.78 eV. Compared to their nominal ionic values, the Born effective charge tensors show anomalous difference, and this is similar to what is found in other ferroelectric materials. The ferroelectricity of BiMn₂O₅ arises from two factors: the role of Bi lone pair electrons and the magnetic ordering.
