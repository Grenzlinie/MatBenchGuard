## ABSTRACT

Electronic structure and bonding analysis of three isostructural intermetallic compounds $\{A_3Pd_5$ (A = Mg, Al, Ga)} are presented using first principles density functional theory. In a fully ordered metallic framework, covalent, localized and directional heteroatomic bonds, which are formed by s-d/sp-d type hybridization, drives the atomic ordering and provides exceptional stability.

---

### 1. Introduction

Unlike conventional metallic alloy or solid solutions, intermetallic compounds have a chemical formula which may have a narrow or wide range of chemical composition or it may be fixed. Mostly, the bonding characteristics may differ from relatively weak metallic bonding, i.e. it may be party ionic or covalent in nature in intermetallic compounds, sometimes providing a driving force to form a fully ordered lattice which may be exceptionally stable up to the melting point [1-5]. The difference in bonding behaviour often leads to a diverse class of materials in terms of crystal structure, electronic structure and physical properties, making it difficult to comprehend within a small number of groups.

Fully or partly ordered intermetallics with interesting bonding situation,caused by the presence of conducting electrons as well as the unique combination of covalent or partly ionic interactions between the heteroatoms, often results in useful combinations of unique crystal structure and electronic structure in the field of heterogeneous catalysis and surface chemistry [6,7]. Pd-based binary and ternary ordered intermetallic compounds are familiar due to their selective catalytic activity in acetylene semi-hydrogenation reactions. For instance, Armbruster et al. has found a correlation between the electronic structure and catalytic selectivity in Pd-Ga binary system. Pd-Ga short contacts have strong directional and covalent character, the reduction of density of states (DOS) at the Fermi level compared to pure Pd was described as a strong modification of electronic structure compared to pure metal, leading to selective catalytic properties of the material [8]. Also, Matselko et al. has developed ternary compounds in Ga-Sn-Pd system, and their catalytic activity was compared with the binary Pd-Ga counterparts [9,10]. A detailed correlation between electronic structure and catalytic selectivity towards $CO_2$ for steam reforming of methanol (SRM) of Ni, Pd, and Pt based intermetallics was reviewed by Tsai et al. Their observation concludes that larger the interval between the Fermi level and the top of the d-band in the electronic density of states, higher the selectivity of $CO_2$. The concept of treating intermetallics as pseudo-element was introduced after establishing clear correlations between electronic structures (bulk/surface) and the catalytic selectivity obtained in binary PdZn, PtZn, NiZn and PdCd [11].

$A_3Pd_5$ (A = Mg, Al, Ga, In) is a group of Pd-rich ordered intermetallic compounds reported in the literature, crystallize in orthorhombic space group *Pbam*, (No. 55; $Rh_5Ge_3$-type) with 5 independent crystallographic positions [12-15a]. Among these compounds, the electronic structure and bonding of $In_3Pd_5$ was detailed in a previous report [16]. Herein, electronic structures of $A_3Pd_5$ (A = Mg, Al, Ga) were calculated. The experimentally found specific occupancy pattern of these compounds were examined by first-principles total energy calculations. Moreover, the crystallographic ordering was rationalised and correlated with number of heteroatomic bonds.

## 2. Structural description

The crystal structure of $In_3Pd_5$ was reported by Kohlmann and Ritter [12] based on X-ray and neutron powder diffraction data. It crystallizes with the orthorhombic space group <i>Pbam</i>, ((No. 55 $a = 5.61346$ (8), $b = 11.0420$ (2), $c = 4.24263$ (6) Å $Rh_5Ge_3$-type). Like $In_3Pd_5$, $Ga_3Pd_5$ and $Al_3Pd_5$ also adopt the same structure type (Table S1, S2), which was reported by Schubert et al. by means of X-ray diffraction experiments [12–14]. Later on, their structures were reinvestigated by Ellner et al. [15]. $Mg_3Pd_5$ (Table S3) that was structurally characterised by Wannek and Harbrecht, adopts $Rh_5Ge_3$ [15a] structure type.

The structure of $\{A_3Pd_5$ (A = Mg, Al, Ga)$\}$ is described by two polyhedral units: a $APd_8$ (cube) and $APd_{10}$ coordination polyhedra (Fig. 1). A1 (2a) forms an endohedral cube and A2 (4g) forms an endohedral hybrid polyhedron ($APd_{10}$) (a combination of a cuboctahedron and a cube). The cubes ($APd_8$) form a chain along a direction, connected by sharing opposite edges (A = Mg/Al/Ga/In). $APd_{10}$ hybrid polyhedral forms face sharing two dimensional slabs which are infinite in the ab-plane but limited to only one-unit cell along the c-direction coordination environments of three independent Pd positions in $A_3Pd_5$ are compared with pure Pd in Fig. S3. A similar description for $In_3Pd_5$ was discussed in ref. 16.

## 4. Results and discussion

### 4.1. Site preference and atomic ordering

All the independent crystallographic positions were split according to Table S4 (supporting information) in $\text{A}_3\text{Pd}_5$ structure. To understand the site preference pattern and atomic ordering, four model configurations in each of the structures were prepared according to Table S5 (Fig. S1), their total energies, number of heteroatomic contacts ($\leq 3.1$ Å) and space group symmetries are compared (Table 1, S6-S8). The choice of these four particular configurations is the result of a previous work on the $\text{In}_3\text{Pd}_5$ intermetallic compound that belongs to the same crystal structure of the titled compounds. We have pursued extensive analysis of the crystal structure and its coloring (ref. 16). These configurations were chosen as the representatives among many possible structures to establish the rationale of the stability of the observed coloring in this material in the light of electronic structure theory.

For the current group of compounds, we observe a similar trend of stability as ref. 16. Conf. 1 the structure predicted from diffraction experiments demonstrate the lowest energy, therefore rendering them as the most stable configuration. It has been observed that when the number of heteroatomic bonds are preserved, total energy change is minimum. But when number of heteroatomic bond decreases, total energy increases with respect to parent configuration to a significant extent. Also, for all the three cases, conf. 4 has slightly lower energy over conf. 3; it is noteworthy that conf. 4 has higher number of heteroatomic contacts. So, probably heteroatomic bond energy plays the most important role towards the stability of the compound and the reason behind the specific site occupancy pattern of the titled compounds is to maintain the maximum possible heteroatomic bonds.

### 4.2. Density of states and bonding characteristics

The electronic structure of $\text{A}_3\text{Pd}_5$ (A = Mg, Al, Ga) was explored by density of states (DOS) and orbital-wise projected density of states (pDOS). Sufficient electronic density of states at the Fermi level reveals overall metallic character of the titled compounds (Fig. 2, S2). Also, three well-separated regions in the DOS are noteworthy; core states are composed of valence s states of Mg/Al/Ga, predominating Pd- 4d states just below the Fermi level and valence s and p states of Mg/Al/Ga dominates across Fermi level. According to orbital-wise projected density of states, it can be said that the hetero-atomic A-Pd (A = Mg/Al/Ga) bonds are formed by sp-d hybridization, which are expected to be stronger, covalent, and directional in nature compared to metallic bonds. Now, to understand the role of these bonds in the overall stability of the compounds, one needs to calculate Crystal Orbital Hamilton Population (COHP) for each bond. Band structures of these compounds are shown in Fig. S5 and S6.

Formation enthalpy of the titled compounds was calculated on the optimized structures. A very high negative formation energy (Table 2) of each of the compounds indicates strong chemical interaction between the heteroatoms and high thermodynamic stability.

Although a few antibonding states are populated near the Fermi level for Mg-Pd, Al-Pd and Ga-Pd bonds, these bonds are nearly optimized as the bonding-antibonding crossover exists just below the Fermi level for all the cases. In the core energy levels, bonding states are strongly popu- lated for the hetero-atomic contacts, and these get directional character by sp-d hybridization as already explained by orbital-wise projected density of states (Fig. 2).

Only Mg-Mg, Al-Al, Ga-Ga, bonds have positive population at the Fermi level, which implies that the metallic character of these phases is mainly contributed by s-s and s-p electron exchange between these bonds, i.e., the electrical conductivity of these materials are attributed to A-A homo-atomic interactions. But these bonds don't have much contribution towards the overall stability because the number of bonds is a notable factor, which is only 8 per unit cell. Pd-Pd interactions don't play any crucial role in the overall stability of the materials (Table S9- S10).

Comparing the total COHP (Fig. 3) with the orbital-wise COHP we can conclude that the heteroatomic interactions just below the Fermi level are primarily the result of the bonding between valence s and p orbitals of Mg/Al/Ga and Pd 4d orbitals.

Combining the bonding analysis by COHP calculations with Bader effective charge allows a clear understanding of crystal structure, sta- bility, and phase formation. ICOHP values indicate a stronger bonding interaction between the hetero atoms in the titled compounds. Calcu- lated effective Bader charges for $Ga_3Pd_5$ are +0.633 for Ga on an average and -0.38 for Pd on an average. Calculated effective Bader charges for $Al_3Pd_5$ are: on Al is +1.69 on average and -1.01 on Pd on average, Electronegativity of Pd (2.2) is higher over Al (1.61), Ga (1.8). Average Bader effective charges on Mg is +1.55, and on Pd is -0.93 for $Mg_3Pd_5$. This also implies partial charge separation between the heter- oatomic nearest neighbouring contacts in the titled phases which is responsible for the directional s/p-d hybridized bonds. So, combining the partial DOS, COHP, and average Bader effective charges on the atoms of titled phases confirms that directional, covalent heteroatomic bonds are responsible for their stability, and they significantly differ from conventional understanding of metallic bonding despite the overall metallic character of the materials.

This targeted group of compounds might be interesting in the field of heterogeneous catalysis as the Pd-coordination is altered by Pd-A (A = Mg, Al, Ga, In) bonds which have directional and partial covalent character, that we have proved by first principles calculations. Maxi- mizing the number of heteroatomic contacts is the driving force behind the specific atomic ordering pattern in this compounds, these bonds increases the scope of modification of electronic structure compared to pure Pd by partial charge separation inside an overall metallic frame- work. High negative formation energy of these compounds further proves their thermodynamic stability which majorly comes from the strong chemical interaction between the heteroatoms. Knowledge of crystal structure, electronic structure and chemical bonding helps to improve fundamental and intriguing aspects of intermetallic compounds which further leads to knowledge-based development of new materials.

## 5. Conclusion

The detailed electronic structure analysis of the titled phases reveals that the Fermi level is above the Pd states, and the Fermi-surface is mostly formed by the Mg/Al/Ga s/p band, of sp-sp bonding orbitals. They represent a group of compounds where the strongest hetero-atomic bonds that stabilize the structure have directional components, but the Fermi-surface is primarily a s/p band of the Mg/Al/Ga with overall bonding character. This implies conduction of the compounds similar to standard s-block metals, despite the more complex interactions required to stabilize the site ordering.
