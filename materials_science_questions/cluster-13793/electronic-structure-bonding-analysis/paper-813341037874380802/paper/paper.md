## Abstract
Special quasi-random structures (SQSs) with 32 atoms have been generated to model appropriate supercell structure of pseudo-binary random $L1_2$- $Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ (TM = Y, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb and Ta) alloys. The optimized lattice parameters were in good agreement with the experimental data, and the obtained formation energies showed that all $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ alloys were stable from energetic point of view. As the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased, the values of $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$ for $L1_2$- $Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ alloys exhibited an overall tendency of increase, implying an enhanced Poisson effect and larger resistance to {100} $\langle 001 \rangle$ shear. The elastic isotropy of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ alloys was overall lowered and the ductility could be improved. The calculated electronic structure demonstrated that below the Fermi level the hybridization of transition-metal $d$ states with Al $p$ states was reduced with decreasing of atomic radius of substitu- tion elements TM in the same Period, which uncovered underlying mechanism for stability and elastic properties of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ alloys.

## Introduction
The binary intermetallics compound $Al_3Sc$ is of interest owing to its low density, relatively high melting point, good oxidation resistance, and potentially useful high- temperature strength for aerospace and automotive appli- cations [1-5]. Although $L1_2$-$Al_3Sc$ is brittle at room temperature with fracture occurring in a transgranular manner by cleavage primarily on {110} planes [6], it shows potential ductility due to the high crystallographic symmetry and multiple-slip systems [7, 8]. Therefore, it is very beneficial to study partial substitution of Sc by other elements, which would not only improve ductility of $L1_2$- $Al_3Sc$ but also decrease the cost of the alloys.

Many experimental studies have been carried out to investigate the substitution of Sc with transition metal (TM) and rare earth (RE) elements [9-13]. It was found that Ti additions could further reduce the precipitation coarsening and provide creep resistance of Al-Sc alloys at high temperatures [14, 15], while Zr additions could lead to increase of the strength and recrystallization resistance of Al-Sc alloys [16-20]. The creep properties of $Al_3Sc$ were significantly improved by additions of Y, Ti, Zr and Hf in solid solution [21], and the increase of the lattice-parameter mismatch also leaded to the linear enhancement of Vickers micro-hardness of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{1-x}RE_x)$ (RE = Sm, Yb and Lu) with increasing RE concentration [10, 12].

In spite of numerous experimental studies on $Al_3$ $(Sc_{1-x}TM_x)$ alloys, the structural stabilities and elastic properties of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{1-x}TM_x)$ were rarely investigated theoretically since it was difficult to model the appropriate structure. Only Xu et al. [22] modeled 2-fcc supercell with $1 \times 1 \times 2$ to calculate the total energy and density of states (DOS) of $Al_3(Sc_{0.5}Zr_{0.5})$, which confirmed the Zr atoms occupied on the Sc sublattices. Recently, Simonovic et al.

![](./images/813341037874380802_1.jpg)

[23] modeled the 4-fcc supercell with $1 \times 1 \times 4$ to study the stability of quasi-binary Al–Sc–X structures from the enthalpy of mixing by replacement of Sc with TM or RE. Since both of the above supercell do not keep cubic L1₂ structure, it is not adequate to simulate and study the structure and properties of cubic L1₂–Al₃(Sc₁₋ₓTMₓ) alloys, so it is necessary to model appropriate structure to study the elastic properties of cubic L1₂–Al₃(Sc₁₋ₓTMₓ) alloys.

This article is focused on the pseudo-binary random L1₂–Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) (TM = Y, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb and Ta) alloys where the TM exclusively occupied the Sc sublattices. The special quasi-random structure (SQSs) approach, proposed by Zunger et al. [24–27], was used to model appropriate supercell structure of pseudo-binary L1₂–Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys, then the stability, elastic and electronic properties of pseudo-binary L1₂–Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys were studied by first-principle calculations.

## Calculation details

To study random alloys, cluster expansion (CE) technique, SQSs and coherent potential approximation (CPA) [28] are the most widely employed approaches. The CPA is a mean-field approach to treat random alloys with little lattice mismatch. However, the real random alloys are not-mean-field, there exists a distribution of local environment atoms [25, 26]. In comparison with CPA, SQSs are especially designed small-unit-cell periodic structures to overcome the limitation of mean-field theories, and the distribution of local environment is maintained. Furthermore, rapid convergence of alloy properties with respect to SQSs size has been observed [26, 29, 30], which provides further evidence that those properties are dominated by interactions between near neighbors. In the present investigation, SQSs were employed to model appropriate supercell structures of random L1₂–Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys. Using the gensqs code in the Alloy-Theoretic Automated Toolkit (ATAT) package [31], all possible SQSs based on symmetry of underlying L1₂ lattices have been enumerated. The optimum cubic SQS with 32 atoms per unit cell for the pseudo-binary L1₂–Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys has been found based on the correlation-error function; the detailed descriptions were described in [32].

Then first-principle calculations were performed using the projector augmented wave (PAW) approach within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA-PBE) [33], as implemented in the highly-efficient Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [34]. The semi-core electrons $3p$ for Sc, Ti and V, $4p$ for Y, Zr and Nb, and $5p$ for Hf and Ta were explicitly treated as valence, respectively. The electronic wave functions were expanded using a plane-wave basis set with a cutoff energy of 320 eV, the Brillouin zone was sampled employing Monkhorst and Pack [35] k-point meshes with $8 \times 8 \times 8$ for optimizing geometry and calculating elastic constants, and $10 \times 10 \times 10$ for the DOS calculation. The atomic geometry was fully relaxed with the conjugate gradient method. The convergence test showed that with the above calculation parameters the error for the total energy is less than 1 meV/atom.

## Results and discussion

### Crystal structures and stability

Keeping cubic structures, and using pair and multisite correlation functions with two-body figures spanning distance up to the ninth atomic shells, and three- and four-body figures up to third and second shells, all possible cubic SQSs with 32 atoms per unit cell with L1₂ symmetry have been enumerated with the gensqs code in the ATAT package.

By comparison of the pair and multisite correlation functions $\overline{\Pi}_{k,m}$, the cubic SQS with 32 atoms per unit cell with the smallest total correlation-error is the optimum cubic SQS. The stability of SQS supercell for L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys was studied by the calculation of formation energies $\Delta H$. The formation energies of L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) crystal cells were calculated by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H =& \left(E_{\text{tot}} - N_{\text{Al}}E_{\text{solid}}^{\text{Al}} - N_{\text{Sc}}E_{\text{solid}}^{\text{Sc}} - N_{\text{TM}}E_{\text{solid}}^{\text{TM}}\right). \\
& /\left(N_{\text{Al}} + N_{\text{Sc}} + N_{\text{TM}}\right),
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\text{solid}}^{\text{Al}}$, $E_{\text{solid}}^{\text{Sc}}$ and $E_{\text{solid}}^{\text{TM}}$ were, respectively, the first- principle calculated total energies per atom of fcc Al, hcp Sc, and pure elemental solid TM (hcp Y, Ti, Zr, Hf, and bcc V, Nb, Ta), at their equilibrium geometries. And $E_{\text{tot}}$ was the total energy of unit cell for corresponding random L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys. $N_{\text{Al}}$, $N_{\text{sc}}$ and $N_{\text{TM}}$ referred to the numbers of Al, Sc and TM atoms in SQS super- cell. First-principle calculated formation energies of L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) are shown in Table 2. Clearly, all the L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) exhibited negative formation ener- gies, indicating that these alloys can be stable from ener- getic point of view. It should be noted that all the formation energies of L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) were larger than that of L1₂-Al₃Sc, indicating that the structural stability was reduced with the substitution of TM atoms for Sc atoms. Furthermore, as the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased, the thermodynamic stability was lowered due to the larger formation energies $\Delta H$.

The obtained $C_{ij}$ of L1₂-Al₃Sc and L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) is summarized in Table 3. Obviously, all L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys were mechanically stable since the elastic constants obeyed the Born elastic stability criteria for a cubic structure [41]: $C_{11} > |C_{12}|$, $C_{44} > 0$, and $C_{11} + 2C_{12} > 0$. The present calculations demonstrated that for all L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys, $C_{12}$ was smaller than $C_{44}$, which was, in turn, smaller than $C_{11}$, so the principal axes were the strongest direction in L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys. For the substitution elements in the Group IVA and VA, as the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased, the values of $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$ of L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) (except for TM = Ta) were larger, implying an enhanced Poisson effect [42] and larger resistance to {100} ⟨001⟩ shear [43]. Among these L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys, the L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅Y₀.₅) has the lowest value of $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$.

The polycrystalline bulk modulus $B$, shear modulus $G$, Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $v$ are important for technology and engineering applications. From the calculated $C_{ij}$, the elastic modulus was also estimated by the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation [44], and the results are listed in Table 3 too. The bulk modulus was obtained from

$$
B=(C_{11}+2C_{12})/3. \tag{2}
$$

The results were in good agreement with the Birch- Murnaghan EOS fitted bulk modulus $B_{EOS}$ in Table 2, hence gave a consistent estimation of the incompressibility for materials, and further demonstrated the reliability of the present calculation. The results in Table 3 show that as the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased eventually, the incompressibility was enhanced due to increase of bulk modulus $B$. While the shear modulus $G$ of L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) (except for TM = Y) was reduced, the resistance to reversible deformations would be lowered when loading shear stress to the material because the shear modulus $G$ was a measure of resistance to reversible deformations upon shear stress [45]. Young's modulus $E$ was defined as the ratio of stress to strain, and can be used to provide a measure of the stiffness of the solid. The larger $E$ corresponds to the stiffer material. From Table 3, it was found that L1₂-Al₃Sc was stiffer than L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅), and as the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period reduced, L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) (except for TM = Y) were less stiffer [38].

According to Pugh's empirical criterion [46], $B/G$ ratio can predict the ductile and brittle behavior of materials. The higher $B/G$ ratio corresponds to the better ductility of the materials. Due to higher $B/G$ ratio, the L1₂- Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys were more ductile than L1₂-Al₃Sc, and the ductility of L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) was further improved as the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased. Furthermore, this result was supported by the obtained Cauchy pressure $C_{12}-C_{44}$, which can also describe the brittle and ductile properties of materials [47]. A more negative Cauchy pressure implies that it is covalent bonding in the brittle materials, while a positive Cauchy pressure implies more metallic bonding in the ductile materials. It was noted that all L1₂- Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys exhibited a negative Cauchy pressure, as shown in Table 3, indicating the formation of covalent bonding. The covalent bonding was weakened in view of the less negative Cauchy pressure as the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased. The weaker is the covalent bonding, the better ductility is the material. The variation tendency of $B/G$ was in accordance with the Cauchy pressure.

On the other hand, the Poisson's ratio $v$ was used to quantify the stability of the crystal against shear. The calculated Poisson's ratios of all L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅TM₀.₅) alloys were close to 0.25, meaning that those were materials with predominantly central interatomic force, and L1₂-Al₃(Sc₀.₅Nb₀.₅) has the largest Poisson's ratio, implying the obvious strongest central interatomic force [38].

Elastic anisotropy in structural intermetallics is impor- tant since it usually induces microcracks in materials, hence its investigation is necessary. The shear anisotropic factors ratio of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ alloys were firstly calculated as

$$
A = 2C_{44}/(C_{11}-C_{12}). \tag{3}
$$

The results are given in Table 3. For a completely isotropic material the $A$ factor takes the value of 1, while values smaller or greater than unity measure the degree of elastic anisotropy. Clearly, with the substitution elements TM in the Group IIIA and IVA, the $A$ values of the $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ alloys were very close to 1, suggesting a characteristic of higher isotropy. For the substitution elements TM in the Group VA, the $A$ values were less close to 1, indicating a characteristic of lower isotropy. Obviously, the $A$ values have nearly equal values for the TM with isovalent groups. The results in Table 3 still show that with decreasing atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period, the isotropy of the $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ alloys was lowered.

To further reveal the elastic anisotropy, the three-dimen- sional directional dependence of the Young's modulus of the $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ was calculated as described in Refs. [48, 49]. Due to the similar feature with isovalent groups, the three- dimensional directional dependence of the Young's modulus only for $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ (TM = Ti and V) as represen- tatives is shown in Fig. 2a, b. For the elastic isotropy system, the three-dimensional directional dependence of the Young's modulus would exhibit a spherical shape, while the elastically anisotropic system would display a nonspherical shape. Obviously, Fig. 2a, b indicates that the $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}Ti_{0.5})$ showed a little more isotropy than $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}V_{0.5})$. To demonstrate better the anisotropy of Young's modulus in different directions, the projections on plane {001} and {110} of three-dimensional directional dependence are also given in Fig. 2c and d, respectively. Clearly, $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}Ti_{0.5})$ showed more isotropy on both plane {001} and {110} than $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}V_{0.5})$. As the atomic radius of substitution ele- ments TM in the same Period decreased, the anisotropy of Young's modulus was slightly stronger.

### Electronic properties

In order to reveal the electronic mechanism for the structure stability and elastic properties, the total and partial electronic DOS of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ have been calculated and shown in Fig. 3. In the total electronic DOS of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$, there were three feature peaks below $E_{\rm F}$ near $-8.0$, $-5.0$ and $-2.5$ eV, respectively. Combining with the partial electronic DOS in the right column, it was found that the first two peaks in total DOS were predominantly derived from the Al $s$ states, and the third peak originated mainly from the Al $p$ states and transition-metal (Sc and TM) $d$ states. An important feature of the total DOS was the presence of a deep valley near the Fermi level called a pseudo-gap [50] which reflects a separation between bonding and anti-bonding states. The pseudo-gap was associated with hybridization between the Al $p$ states and transition-metal $d$ states, and indicated the existence of directional covalent bonding in $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$. The graphic features exhibit strong similarities with isovalent groups. As atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased, the $d$ electrons increased, and the Fermi level moved from the left of pseudo-gap to right, and the pseudo-gap moved to left, implying the increased occupation of anti-bonding states with more $d$ electrons. Therefore, the covalent bonding was weakened, which was in good agreement with the variation tendency of Cauchy pressure described above.

For further analysis of the bonding characteristics, the charge density of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ was calculated. As representatives, the charge density on the (001) plane for $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ ($\text{TM} = \text{Y}$, $\text{Zr}$, $\text{Nb}$) together with $L1_2$-$Al_3$Sc is shown in Fig. 4, in which the contour lines were plotted from 0.08 to $0.26\ \text{e}/\text{\AA}^3$ with $0.012\ \text{e}/\text{\AA}^3$ intervals, and the higher charge density distributions were omitted due to little contribution to the bonding. In Fig. 4a, the evident overlap of electron densities between Al and Sc atoms was observed, implying that covalent bonding was formed due to the hybridization between the Al $p$ states and Sc $d$ states, and the covalent bonding would lead to the brittleness of $L1_2$-$Al_3$Sc. In comparison with the Fig. 4b-d, as the atomic radius of substitution elements TM in the same Period decreased, the overlap between Al atoms and TM atoms was reduced, the covalent bonding between Al and TM atoms was weaker. Furthermore, the covalent bonding between Al and Sc atoms was also weakened owing to the reduction of the overlap between them. The weaker covalent bonding implied that the movement of the dislocations was easier [6], and the ductility of $L1_2$-$Al_3(Sc_{0.5}TM_{0.5})$ was improved, which was in excel- lent agreement with the above analysis of the elastic properties and DOS described above.
