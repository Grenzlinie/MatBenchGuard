Metal doping is considered as an effective method to stabilize the structures and optimize the properties of boron clusters. The structures and electronic properties of the TMB₃₆ clusters have been calculated at the Perdew–Burkle–Ernzerhof (PBE) level. The results reveal that the Cu atoms for the CuB₃₆ clusters unexpectedly enter the B₃₆ clusters. Ti, V, Co, Ni, Zr, Hf, Ta and W can obviously increase the structural stability of pristine B₃₆ clusters. The Ti, Cr, Fe, Ni and Zn; Y, Ru and Ag; Lu, Ta, Ir and Au-adsorbed B₃₆ clusters display higher kinetic activity than other B₃₆ clusters. The $d$ orbital electrons of the TM atoms will significantly affect the distributions of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) states of pristine B₃₆ clusters. All the TM–B bonds of the TMB₃₆ clusters display covalent characters.

Keywords: TMB₃₆ clusters; first-principles; structures; electronic properties.

PACS Number(s): 68.35.bg, 73.61.Ey

## 1. Introduction

Boron has been extensively applied to science and industry such as novel compounds (hard materials, semiconductors, glasses and antitumor medicines, etc.).¹ Over the past decade, boron clusters and nanostructures have been paid considerable attention due to their lightweight and porous characters.² The structures, electronic properties and bonding characteristics of the intriguing molecular and/or nanoscale systems have been revealed by experimental techniques and computational methods.¹,³ *Piazza et al.*⁴ prepared typical quasi-planar B₃₆ clusters which exhibit sixfold symmetry and possess a perfect central hexagonal hole. ## 2. Computational Details

Structure $(D_{2h}$ symmetry) of the $B_{36}$ clusters was selected from the previous reports. $^{15,16}$ Only a TM atom approaching the hexagonal of the $B_{36}$ clusters was considered. It arises from $\eta^{6}$ - and $\eta^{7}$ - complex formation with a TM fragment in the boron clusters, which should smooth the surface and make the molecule 'closo'. $^{17}$ Geometric optimization was performed by the DFT within the $DMol^{3}$ package implemented in the Materials studio software. $^{18}$ Perdew-Burkle-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional $^{19-21}$ of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) $^{22}$ was selected for the exchange-correction interaction. Symmetry unconstraint was adopted to avoid omitting ground-state structures. $^{19,23,24}$ Due to the $d$ orbitals existing in the TM atoms, spin unrestricted methods were considered $^{25,26}$ and the double-numerical polarized (DNP) basis set was adopted. $^{2,19,27}$ The electronic configurations of the TM atoms are affected by the electron relativity effects, so all electron relativis- tic was selected. $^{28}$ Mülliken population analysis was selected to reveal the on-site charges of the $TMB_{36}$ clusters. $^{29,30}$ Convergence thresholds for the self-consistent

The average binding energies $E_b$ of the B₃₆ and TMB₃₆ clusters are calculated to discuss the structural stabilities of them³⁶

$$
E_b = [36E(\text{B}) - E(\text{B}_{36})]/36, \tag{1}
$$

$$
E_b = [E(\text{TM}) + 36E(\text{B}) - E(\text{TMB}_{36})]/37, \tag{2}
$$

where $E(\text{TM})$ and $E(\text{B})$ are the isolate atomic energies of the TM and B atoms, respectively. $E(\text{B}_{36})$ and $E(\text{TMB}_{36})$ are the total energies of the B₃₆ and TMB₃₆ clusters.

The energy gap between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) states and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) states, which characterize the kinetic stabilities of clusters, is calculated as follows:³⁷

$$
E_g = E_{\text{LUMO}} - E_{\text{HOMO}}, \tag{3}
$$

where $\text{E}_{\text{LUMO}}$ and $\text{E}_{\text{HOMO}}$ present the energies of the LUMO states and the HOMO states of the corresponding clusters.

The temperature dependence of the free energies of the TMB₃₆ clusters is calculated as follows:³⁸

$$
F(T) = E_{\text{tot}} + \frac{1}{2} \int F(\omega) h\omega d\omega + kT \int F(\omega) \ln\left[1 - \exp\left(-\frac{h\omega}{kT}\right)\right] d\omega, \tag{4}
$$

where the second item is the zero point vibrational energy, $k$ is Boltzmann constant, $h$ is Planck constant and $F(\omega)$ is the phonon density of states. Due to the change of $P\Delta V$ being rather small under constant pressure, $F$ is approximately equal to the Gibbs free energy.

To verify the accuracy of the PBE functional, the calculated bond length ($r = 1.658$ Å) of B–B bond of the B₃₆ cages was compared with the corresponding calculated values ($r = 1.659$ Å¹⁹ and $r = 1.67$ Å⁴) by the PBE functional. As for the calculations on the TM atom doping by the PBE functional at the same level, it has been discussed in our previous work.²⁸ It indicates that the PBE functional adopted is accurate enough to describe the TMB₃₆ clusters.

It suggests that these clusters are preferred to metallic characters.$^{31}$ This is probably because the HOMO states, especially the LUMO states of the $B_{36}$ cages, have been transferred significantly to lower energies.$^{40}$

The stability of the bulk phase can be analyzed by the thermodynamic phase diagram.$^{42}$ However, clusters are not periodic like the bulk phases. The thermodynamic stabilities of the $TMB_{36}$ clusters can be analyzed by Gibbs free energy ($G$).

### 3.3. Electronic Properties

To investigate thoroughly the influence of the electronic properties of TM atoms on the B₃₆ clusters, the HOMO and LUMO states of the typical TMB₃₆ clusters have been plotted in Table 1. The trapping electron regions are depicted by blue and the releasing electron regions are depicted by yellow color.⁴¹ The HOMO states of the TMB₃₆ clusters are mainly formed by the $\sigma$-type bonds while the their LUMO states are partly localized $\sigma$-type bonds and partly delocalized $\pi$-type bonds.⁴⁴ Concretely, the $p$ orbital of the B atoms mainly contributes to the electron density of the HOMO and LUMO states of pristine B₃₆ shells.¹² Once the TM atoms are doped, the $d$ orbital electrons of the TM atoms will enable a special distribution to the HOMO and LUMO states of these TMB₃₆ clusters. It suggests the back-donation from the B₃₆ clusters to the unoccupied metal orbitals.³⁴

To explore the bonding behaviors and electronic configurations of the TMB₃₆ clusters, the net-charges of the TM atoms for the TMB₃₆ clusters have been displayed in Fig. 5. As for the $3d$ TM atoms, the TM atoms obtain less electrons except for Sc. While for the $4d$ and $5d$ TM atoms, the 7–10 subgroup atoms (Tc, Ru, Rh and Pd) and the 7–11 subgroup atoms (Re, Os, Ir, Pt and Au) obtain less electrons. As the electronegative differences in them are less than the threshold 1.7,²⁸ all the TM–B bonds of the TMB₃₆ clusters display covalent characters.
