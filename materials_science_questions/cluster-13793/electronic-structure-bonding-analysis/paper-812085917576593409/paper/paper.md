# First-principles study of alloying effect of Re on properties of $\mathbf{Ni/Ni_3Al}$ interface

P. Peng $^{a,b}$, A.K. Soh $^{b,*}$, R. Yang $^{c}$, Z.Q. Hu $^{c}$

$^{a}$ School of Materials Science and Engineering, Hunan University, Changsha 410082, China
$^{b}$ Department of Mechanical Engineering, The University of Hong Kong, Pokfulam Road, Hong Kong, China
$^{c}$ Institute of Metal Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shenyang 110016, China

Received 31 March 2005; accepted 3 October 2005

## Abstract
A first principles investigation of alloying effects of Re on the properties of $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface was conducted using a plane-wave pseudopotential method. Re atoms were found to occupy Ni sites of the interface region in the following order of preference: $\gamma$-Ni block, coherent (002) interface, $\gamma'-Ni_3Al$ block. A series of interface models were constructed and the influence of the presence and location of Re atoms on the local interatomic interactions at the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface was evaluated in terms of Griffith rupture work and Mulliken's population analysis. Consistent with experimental fact, the substitution of Re for Ni at the coherent interfacial layer was found beneficial for improving the rupture strength and toughness of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface.

## 1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal (SC) superalloys, which are used for turbine blades and vanes in the most advanced gas turbine engines, mainly consist of $\gamma'$ precipitates coherently dispersed on a $\gamma$ matrix. The $\gamma'$ precipitates (an intermetallic phase of stoichiometry based upon $Ni_3Al$) possess an $L1_2$ type ordered face-centered cubic structure. The $\gamma$ matrix has a disordered fcc (A1) structure. The first generation SC superalloys contain no Re, but the second and third generation alloys contain $3\ \text{wt}\%$ and $6\ \text{wt}\%$ Re, respectively, which significantly improves the overall performance, especially the high-temperature creep rupture strength, thermal fatigue resistance and oxidation resistance [1]. Re has been proven to be a potent strengthener and a key element for the increase in temperature capabilities even though a high content of Re in SC superalloys usually leads to a propensity to form deleterious topologically close-packed (TCP) phases during exposure at high temperatures [2]. Atom probe and field ion microscope (AP/FIM) experiments revealed that Re mainly partitions to $\gamma$ matrix and piles up ahead of the $\gamma/\gamma'$ interface, retarding the coarsening rate of the strengthening $\gamma'$ phase [3,4]. However, to-date no direct experimental study has been performed to examine the strengthening mechanism due to the addition of Re in SC superalloys. In view of the limitation of experimental techniques, first-principles approach has been extensively adopted to investigate the strengthening and toughening mechanism of Ni-based SC superalloys [5-11]. In-service examination and failure analyses have shown that the structure and properties of the $\gamma/\gamma'$ interface have a great influence on the shape, size and coarsening rate of $\gamma'$ precipitates [12] which in return strongly affect the creep strength or creep rupture life of SC superalloys subjected to stress at elevated temperature [13]. To some extent, the strengthening and toughening effect of Re on the $\gamma/\gamma'$ interface contributes to the increase in temperature capabilities of the Re-containing SC superalloys. Recently, some effort has been made to understand the strengthening and toughening effect of Re on the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni₃Al interface in SC superalloys [6–11]. Chen et al. [7–10] made use of the DMol molecular orbital package, which employs the first-principles based on the density functional theory, to calculate the electronic structures and bonding characteristics of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface using a cluster model. Their calculation showed that, with the addition of Re, the bonds between the Re atom and its first nearest neighbor (FNN) Ni atoms formed at the Ni/Ni₃Al interface are stronger than the FNN Ni–Ni bonds. And there is a significant increase of cohesive and shear strengths, which are characterized by the sum of the vertical fractions (across the Ni/Ni₃Al interface) and that of the horizontal fractions (along the Ni/Ni₃Al interface), respectively, of the bond overlap populations (BOP) (in the Mayer form) between FNN atoms in the Ni/Ni₃Al interfacial region. However, the ratio of shear to cohesive strength, which is a measure of interfacial embrittlement as proposed by Chen et al., increased from 0.526 in the case of Re-free interface model to 0.653 in that of Re-addition interface model (note that these values are extracted from Table 1 in Ref. [8]). In their opinion, a significant increase of this ratio indicates that the addition of Re at the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni₃Al interface does not relieve but promote the embrittlement of the Ni/Ni₃Al $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni₃Al interface. This seems inconsistent with the actual behavior of Ni-based SC superalloys with addition of Re because none of the service experiments and failure analyses showed that these superalloys exhibited very brittle behavior. We notice that in the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni₃Al interface cluster model proposed by Chen et al., the substitution of Re was deliberately placed at the Al site adjacent to the coherent interfacial layer. This specific way of substitution might be the cause of the controversial conclusion because very few experiments have detected the existence of Re in the $\gamma'$ phase [3,4]. In addition, the sums of the vertical and horizontal fractions of BOP between FNN atoms in the Ni/Ni₃Al interfacial region are not the real shear and cohesive strengths, respectively, of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface. This is because the BOP represents only a relative measure of covalent bonding between FNN atoms, and the influence of local environment, i.e., the influence of atoms other than those in the specified Ni/Ni₃Al interfacial region [11], was not considered in their approach. In this paper, we use a more reasonable supercell than the cluster model of Chen et al. to investigate the electronic and energetic structures of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface. Particular attention will be paid to the consideration of site preference of Re in the Ni/Ni₃Al interface region before attempting to study the strengthening and toughening effect of Re.

### 2. Procedure of calculation

Cambridge serial total energy package (CASTEP), which employs the first-principles plane-wave pseudopotential method [14,15] based on the density functional theory in which the exchange-correlation potential adopts a local density approximation (LDA), is used in the present study. This program is employed to investigate the energetics and electronic structures as well as the geometric characteristics of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface with addition of Re. Ultrasoft pseudopotentials [16] represented in reciprocal space with the Cepeley–Alder exchange-correlation potential were used for all elements in our models. In our calculations, the cutoff energy of atomic wave functions (PWs), $E_{\text{cut}}$, is set at 330 eV. Sampling of the irreducible wedge of the Brillouin zone is done by adopting $4\times4\times2$ with a regular Monkhorst–Pack grid of special $k$-points. A finite basis set correction [17] is applied for evaluation of energy and stress. In the calculation of self-consistent field (SCF), the Pulay scheme of density mixing [18] is adopted.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Site preference of Re in Ni/Ni$_3$Al interface region

In order to determine the site preference of Re at the Ni/
Ni$_3$Al interface, the total energies of the Ni/Ni$_3$Al interface
supercell and the correlated crystal model are calculated. It
is well known that the site preference can be deduced from
the magnitude of heat of formation of the supercell or
cluster [21,22]. In this work, the following expression is
adopted to estimate the heat of formation per atom $H$ at
the Ni/Ni$_3$Al interface [22]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H =& \left[E_{\text{i}}(n,m,l) - n\cdot E_{\text{c}}(\text{Ni}) - m\cdot E_{\text{c}}(\text{Al})\right.\\
& \left. - l\cdot E_{\text{c}}(\text{Re})\right]/(n+m+l)
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\text{i}}(n,m,l)$ is the total energy of the $\gamma/\gamma'$ supercell with
$n$, $m$ and $l$ atoms of Ni, Al and Re, respectively. $E_{\text{c}}(\text{Ni})$, $E_{\text{c}}$
(Al) and $E_{\text{c}}(\text{Re})$, are the energies per atom in the fcc-Ni,
fcc-Al and hcp-Re unit cell, respectively. As a comparison,
Fig. 2 presents the magnitude of $H$ of the Ni/Ni$_3$Al inter-
faces. Note that the negative heat of formation in Fig. 2
indicates that the addition of Re at the Ni/Ni$_3$Al interface
is an exothermic reaction; in other words, alloying of Ni/
Ni$_3$Al interface with Re exhibits energetic stability. The
larger the negative heat of formation, the more stable the
structure of the alloyed $\gamma/\gamma'$ interface. Hence the site
preference of Re atoms at the Ni/Ni$_3$Al interface can be
deduced from the data presented in Fig. 2, and the follow-
ing order of preference can be given: $\text{Ni(1)}>\text{Ni(2)}>$
$\text{no-add}>\text{Ni(3)}>\text{Al(4)}$, in which the no-add model repre-
sents Ni/Ni$_3$Al interfaces free of Re atoms. In other words,
the Re atoms would prefer the Ni sites in the $\gamma$-Ni region,
followed by the Ni sites of the coherent (002) interfacial
layer, then at the Ni sites in the $\gamma'$-Ni$_3$Al region, and finally
at Al site in the $\gamma'$-Ni$_3$Al region adjacent to the interfacial
layer. This theoretical result is consistent with the existing
experimental data, which showed an obvious Re enrich-
ment ahead of the $\gamma/\gamma'$ interface. And there is a so-called
'bow wave' of solutes rejected into the $\gamma$ phase ahead of
the growing $\gamma'$ phase rather than a simple segregated layer
at the $\gamma/\gamma'$ interface [4]. It is worth noting that the heat of
formation of the Ni(1) model is much more negative than
that of the no-add model. This means that the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-
Ni$_3$Al interface with Ni substituted by Re in the $\gamma$-Ni block
is more stable than the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni$_3$Al interface free of Re.
This may be the reason for the formation of TCP phase in
the $\gamma$ matrix induced by the Re cluster [2]. However, the
magnitude of the heat of formation of the Al(4) model is
much smaller than that of the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni$_3$Al interface free
of Re, which indicates that the Al(4) model is an energeti-
cally unstable structure compared with the Re-free $\gamma$-Ni/
$\gamma'$-Ni$_3$Al interface. This explains why few Re atoms can
be detected in the $\gamma'$-phase with the AP/FIM technique
[4]. In view of above discussion, only the case of Ni substi-
tution by Re at the interface will be considered in the

### 3.2. Griffith rupture work

In this section, the alloying effect of Re on the strength of $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface is considered. In the present study, Griffith rupture work is employed to evaluate the binding strength of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface. By definition, the work to cleave a crystal along the interface is Griffith rupture work, which can be calculated as the difference between the interfacial energy and surface energy [23], i.e.,

$$
\begin{aligned}
W=\left(-1 / 2 S_{\mathrm{i}}\right) \cdot\left[E_{\mathrm{i}}(n, m, l)-E_{\mathrm{s}}^{\gamma}\left(n_{\gamma}, m_{\gamma}, l_{\gamma}\right)-E_{\mathrm{s}}^{\gamma \prime}\left(n_{\gamma \prime}, m_{\gamma \prime}, l_{\gamma \prime}\right)\right] \\
(2)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $S_{\mathrm{i}}=a \times b$ is the area of the coherent atomic layer; $a$ and $b$ are the supercell lattice constants along the $x$- and $y$-directions, respectively, in the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface model; $E_{\mathrm{s}}^{\gamma}(n_{\gamma}, m_{\gamma}, l_{\gamma})$ and $E_{\mathrm{s}}^{\gamma \prime}(n_{\gamma \prime}, m_{\gamma \prime}, l_{\gamma \prime})$ are the total energies of the surface models of the $\gamma$-Ni and $\gamma^{\prime}-Ni_3Al$ blocks, respectively, corresponding to the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface model; $n, m, l$ denote the numbers of Ni, Al, Re atoms, respectively, where $n=n_{\gamma}+n_{\gamma^{\prime}}, m=m_{\gamma}+m_{\gamma^{\prime}}, l=l_{\gamma}+l_{\gamma^{\prime}}$.

Table 2 presents the total energies of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface and correlated surface models as well as the Griffith rupture work calculated using Eq. (2). Considering the two orientation relationships of the present $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interfacial model, i.e., $(002)\gamma\|(001)\gamma^{\prime}$ and $(001)\gamma\|(002)\gamma^{\prime}$, there exist two potential inter-phase fracture modes. Adjacent to the coherent (002) atomic layer, in the first mode cleavage occurs along the (001) atomic layer in the $\gamma^{\prime}-Ni_3Al$ block (in region-1, as shown in Fig. 1), and in the second mode split occurs along the (001) atomic layer in the $\gamma$-Ni block (in region-2). It can be seen from Table 2 that the Griffith rupture work $W$ of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface free of Re in region-1 is smaller than that in region-2. This indicates that the binding strength in region-1 is weaker than that in region-2. Usually fracture occurs at the weakest part of materials; therefore, the Griffith work in Region-1, i.e., $4.359\ \text{J/m}^2$, represents the rupture strength of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface free of Re.

Similar evaluation can be carried out for the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface with addition of Re. In the case where Ni atoms at $(001)\gamma$ and $(002)\gamma/\gamma^{\prime}$ are being substituted by Re, the weaker regions in the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface are still region-1, but the rupture strength of their $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interfaces does change. In comparison with the rupture strength of $4.359\ \text{J/m}^2$ for the Re-free $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface, a slight decrease of $\sim0.017\ \text{J/m}^2$ is observed in the Ni(1) model and an obvious increase of $0.131\ \text{J/m}^2$ in the Ni(2) model. This indicates that addition of Re to the $\gamma$-Ni block cannot increase the rupture strength of $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface. However, addition of Re to the coherent interfacial layer is favorable for increasing the binding strength of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface. It is a well-known fact that the $\gamma/\gamma^{\prime}$ interface is the weakest region in the Ni-based SC superalloys. To a certain extent the binding strength of the $\gamma/\gamma^{\prime}$ interface is the rupture strength of the SC superalloys. Hence, addition of Re to the coherent interfacial layer is beneficial to the improvement of the rupture strength of Ni-based single crystal superalloys [1,24].

In the case where Ni is replaced by Re at the $(001)\gamma^{\prime}$ layer, i.e., Ni(3) model, an inverse inter-phase fracture tendency can be observed from Table 2. In this model, the Griffith rupture work of $4.465\ \text{J/m}^2$ in region-2 is smaller than that of $4.807\ \text{J/m}^2$ in region-1. This means that the inter-phase fracture site in the Ni(3) model changes from region-1 at the Re-free $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface to region-2 and the rupture strength of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface is evidently increased by $0.106\ \text{J/m}^2$. This indicates that the use of Re to replace Ni in the $\gamma^{\prime}-Ni_3Al$ block is also favorable for improving the rupture strength of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface, even though the site preference presented in Fig. 2 and the experimental investigation by means of the AP/FIM technique [4] have shown that only a few Ni atoms at (001) atomic layer in the $\gamma^{\prime}-Ni_3Al$ block are substituted by Re atoms. ### 3.3. Toughness of $Ni/Ni_3$ $Al$ interface alloyed with $Re$

In order to qualitatively evaluate the effect of Re-addition on the brittleness or toughness of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface, the ionic and covalent bonding between FNN atoms at the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface is calculated using Mulliken's population analysis method [25]. Mulliken's charge $Q$(A) of A atom and the bond overlap population $Q_{\text{A-B}}$ between A and B atoms are defined as follows:

$$
Q(\mathrm{A})=\sum_{k} w_{k} \sum_{\mu}^{\mathrm{A}} \sum_{v}^{\mathrm{A}} P_{\mu v}(k) S_{v \mu}(k)
\tag{3}
$$

$$
Q_{\mathrm{A-B}}=\sum_{k} w_{k} \sum_{\mu}^{\mathrm{A}} \sum_{v}^{\mathrm{B}} 2 P_{\mu v}(k) S_{\mu v}(k)
\tag{4}
$$

where $P_{\mu v}(k)$ and $S_{v \mu}(k)$ are the density matrix and the overlap matrix, respectively; $w_k$ is the weight associated with the calculated $k$-points in the Brillouin zone. Usually, the magnitude and sign of $Q$(A) characterize the ionicity of the A atom in the supercell, and $Q_{\text{A-B}}$ can be used to approximately measure the covalent bonding strength. Tables 3 and 4 list the $Q$(A) at special sites and a part of $Q_{\text{A-B}}$ between FNN atoms in the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interfacial supercell, respectively.

With reference to Table 3, the small magnitude of $Q$(Ni) at Ni(1) and Ni(2) sites indicates that the ionic bonding between FNN Ni-Ni in the $\gamma$-Ni block is weak. In addition, a significant charge transfer from Al at Al(4) site to Ni at Ni(3) site of the $(001)\gamma'$ layer and to Ni at Ni(2) site of the $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ layer means that there is a strong ionic bonding between FNN Ni-Al atoms at the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface, which is in agreement with those reported in Refs. [8,26]. In this section we will focus on the analysis of the bond overlap populations between FNN atoms in the Ni/ $Ni_3Al$ interfacial region because the brittleness or toughness of materials is mainly correlated to the directionality of covalent bonding.

Table 4 shows that on the whole $Q_{\text{Ni-Al}}$ is larger than $Q_{\text{Ni-Ni}}$ at the Re-free $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface. In addition, the $Q_{\text{A-B}}$ between Ni at $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ layer and its FNN Ni illustrates an obvious directionality, in which the $Q_{\text{Ni-Ni}}$ within $(001)\gamma$ and $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ layers is far larger than that in the $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ layer and that within the $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ and $(001)\gamma'$ layers. However, for the $Q_{\text{A-B}}$ between Ni at $(001)\gamma$ layer and its FNN Ni, the $Q_{\text{Ni-Ni}}$ in the interlayer and intra-layer is of a larger magnitude with an excellent isotropic build-up. Obviously, the decrease in magnitude and the increase in directionality of $Q_{\text{Ni-Ni}}$ at Ni(2) site are caused by its closeness to Al at the Al(4) site which transfers part of its p electrons to the d shell of Ni at Ni(2) site [26]. With addition of Re, the magnitude and directionality of $Q_{\text{A-B}}$ at the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface change distinguishingly, especially for the $Q_{\text{A-B}}$ near Re in the interfacial region, and the following variation tendencies can be observed: $Q_{\text{Re-Al}} > Q_{\text{Ni-Al}}$ and $Q_{\text{Re-Ni}} \approx Q_{\text{Ni-Ni}}$ in intra-layers; $Q_{\text{Re-Al}} \approx Q_{\text{Ni-Al}}$ and $Q_{\text{Re-Ni}} \approx Q_{\text{Ni-Ni}}$ in interlayers. These tendencies seem to imply that addition of Re is not beneficial to the enhancement of the toughness of $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface due to the increasing directionality of $Q_{\text{A-B}}$. In order to accurately characterize the influence of Re-addition on the toughness and the fracture mode of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface, the local bond overlap populations (LBOP) [10,11] are adopted to evaluate the bonding strengths in intra- and interlayers. Although the LBOP in intra- and interlayer are not the real shear and cohesive strengths of the $Ni/Ni_3Al$ interface [7], the ratio of the LBOP in an intra-layer to that in an interlayer $(R_{\text{LBOP}} = \text{LBOP}_{\text{intra-layer}}/\text{LBOP}_{\text{interlayer}})$ has been confirmed to be an efficient parameter for judging the competition of the ductile and brittle fracture modes of the Ni/ $Ni_3Al$ interface [7-10]. Usually a material would fail in a brittle manner if the ideal cohesive strength were reached along the extension of the crack before the ideal shear strength is reached [7]. The closer the value of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ to 1, the weaker is the embrittlement tendency of the material, and the better is its toughness.

Table 5 presents the $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ values at the Ni/Ni₃Al interface with and without addition of Re. For the region between $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ and $(001)\gamma'$ layers, i.e., region-1, the $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ values are close to 1 but larger than 1 in all the Ni/Ni₃Al interfacial models, which means that region-1 has good toughness but the failure mode would be brittle fracture, while for the region between $(001)\gamma$ and $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ layers, i.e., region-2, the $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ values are significantly smaller than 1, which indicates that the toughness of Region-2 is low but the failure mode would be cleavage via shear, i.e., ductile fracture. In other words, addition of Re does not cause the change in local toughness or embrittlement and the inter-phase fracture mode. However, addition of Re does cause a significant variation in the magnitude of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$, as shown in Table 5. For example, in the case of replacing Ni atoms by Re atoms at the $(001)\gamma$ layer, i.e., Ni(1) model, the significant increments of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$, i.e., 0.12 in region-1 and 0.06 in region-2, with respect to the Re-free Ni/Ni₃Al interface, indicate that the addition of Re in $\gamma$-Ni block causes the toughness of the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni₃Al interface to decline in region-1 but enhance in region-2. For the Ni(2) model, the $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ value is close to that of the no-add model. In region-1 a very small increment of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$, about 0.01, means that the addition of Re at $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ layer does not change the toughness of the Re-free Ni/Ni₃Al interface. Meanwhile, in region-2 the significant increase of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ by 0.04 indicates that addition of Re causes the degree of embrittlement in this interfacial region to decline further. With reference to Table 2, it is worth noting that for the Ni(2) model, region-1 is the weaker region at the Ni/Ni₃Al interface and its rupture strength is $0.131\ \text{J/m}^2$ higher than that of the Re-free Ni/Ni₃Al interface. Hence, on the whole it is very beneficial to replace Ni by Re at the $(002)\gamma/\gamma'$ layer, which simultaneously elevates the rupture strength and toughness of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface. In the case of Ni(3) model, a significant increase of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ in region-1 (about 0.11 as shown in Table 5) means replacement of Ni by Re in the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block will decrease the toughness of region-1. However, as elucidated in Section 3.2, the Ni/Ni₃Al interface in the Ni(3) model would be cleaved along the $(001)\gamma$ layer in region-2, rather than along the $(001)\gamma'$ layer in region-1. Hence the small decrease of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ in region-2 (about 0.02) is beneficial to promoting shear fracture mode at the Ni/Ni₃Al interface even though it does not reduce the degree of embrittlement in this region. As for the Al(4) model, in region-1 the value of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ decreases slightly by 0.02 and it is closer to 1 compared with that of the no-add model, and a more significant change of $R_{\text{LBOP}}$ (an increase of about 0.04) in region-2 seems to indicate the decrease of local embrittlement in the Ni/Ni₃Al interfacial region. However, it is not helpful to reinforce the toughness of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface because the inter-phase fracture can only occur in region-2 of this model, in particular, this manner of site occupation has been proven to be non-existent [3,4].

### 3.4. Electronic structures of Ni/Ni₃Al interface with addition of Re

Fig. 3 shows the total valence charge density contour plots on the cross section of the two coherent interfacial layers of the Ni/Ni₃Al interfacial supercell with and without addition of Re. For the no-add, Ni(1), Ni(2) and Al(4) models, the above-mentioned cross section is the (100) plane, whereas for the Ni(3) model it is the (010) plane. For the Re-free Ni/Ni₃Al interface, the contour plots show similar bonding characteristics to that presented in Table 4, i.e., the electronic interaction between the FNN Ni–Al atoms (including ionic and covalent bonding) in the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block is stronger than that between the FNN Ni–Ni atoms in the $\gamma$-Ni block [26]. In addition, a significant anisotropic build-up of the directional d bonding charge at the Ni(2) sites, which is caused mainly by the polarization of p electrons at the Al sites as a result of the p–d hybridization effect, along the FNN Ni–Al and FNN Ni–Ni directions [8], can be also observed in Fig. 3. However, it is worth noting that for Ni atoms in the $\gamma$-Ni block, which is farther from Al in the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block, the electronic interaction between FNN Ni–Ni atoms (mostly in a covalent bonding mode, refer to Tables 3 and 4) displays obvious isotropy. On the whole Fig. 3 clearly illustrates that the valence charge density in region-2 is rich but poor in region-1. In other words, the local electronic interaction in region-2 is stronger than that in region-1. ## 4. Conclusion
A systematic investigation of the strengthening and toughening effects of Re-addition on the $\gamma$-Ni/$\gamma'$-Ni₃Al interface has been performed using the first principles plane-wave pseudopotential method. The Re atoms have preference in occupying the Ni site: the first choice being in the $\gamma$-Ni block, the second being at the coherent (002) interface, followed by the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block. The replacement of Al by Re in the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block is not likely to occur in view of energetics. As for replacing Ni by Re in the $\gamma$-Ni block, both the rupture strength and toughness of the Ni/ Ni₃Al interface decrease compared with those of the Re-free Ni/Ni₃Al interface. In the case where the Ni atoms are replaced by Re atoms at the coherent (002) interface and at the (001) atomic layer in the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block, an obvious strengthening effect of Re-addition on the Ni/ Ni₃Al interface can be seen. As for addition of Re at the coherent (002) interface, the inter-phase fracture occurs in region-1 along the (001) atomic layer in the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block and occurs in a brittle manner with a little decrease in toughness at the Ni/Ni₃Al interface. The strengthening effect of Re and the similar fracture site and fracture mode to that of the Re-free Ni/Ni₃Al interface can be attributed to stronger electronic interaction (involving ionic and cova- lent bonding) between FNN Ni-Re than between FNN Ni-Ni at the Ni(1) and Ni(2) sites and a little influence of Re-addition on the local electronic interaction in region-1 and region-2. For the Ni/Ni₃Al interface with Ni substi- tuted by Re in the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al block, the strong bonding between Re at Ni(3) site and its FNN Ni at Ni(2) site results in change of fracture site at the Ni/Ni₃Al interface from region-1 when no Re is added to region-2 along the (001) atomic layer in the $\gamma$-Ni block, with a little increase in the degree of local embrittlement in the Ni/Ni₃Al inter- facial region. The cleavage of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface is in a ductile manner, which originates from the less directional- ity of covalent bonding between FNN Ni-Ni in the $\gamma$-Ni block. In short, the substitution of Re for Ni at the coher- ent interfacial layer is most beneficial for improving the rupture strength and toughness of the Ni/Ni₃Al interface.
