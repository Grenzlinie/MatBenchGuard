# Chemical bonding of ion-exchange type sites in spinel-type manganese oxides $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$

Y.-S. Kim$^{a,*}$, H. Kanoh$^{b}$, T. Hirotsu$^{a}$, K. Ooi$^{a}$

$^{a}$National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology, Takamatsu 761-0395, Japan
$^{b}$Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science, Chiba University, 1-33 Yayoi, Inage, Chiba 263-8522, Japan

(Refereed)
Received 8 August 2001; accepted 21 September 2001

## Abstract
The electronic structure and chemical bonding nature of spinel-type $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$ system was calculated by a discrete-variational (DV)-X$\alpha$ clusters method. In order to elucidate the reason for the selective exchange of $Li^{+}$ ions, the bonding natures between tetrahedral and octahedral Li sites of the $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$ were compared. Li in the manganese oxides is highly ionized in both sites, but the net charge of Li was greater for tetrahedral sites than octahedral. These calculations suggest that the tetrahedral sites have higher $Li^{+}/H^{+}$ exchangeability than the octahedral sites, and are preferable for the selective adsorption for $Li^{+}$ ions.

## 1. Introduction
Spinel-type lithium manganese oxides are characterized by excellent selectivity for $Li^{+}$ ions after topotactic extraction of $Li^{+}$ with an acid [1–4]. This property makes the materials practical as a $Li^{+}$ adsorbent [1–4], a cathode material for lithium secondary batteries [5,6] and an electrode for selective electroinsertion of $Li^{+}$ [7]. Furthermore, the $Li^{+}$ insertion/extraction reaction occurs reversibly, maintaining the host structure. These characteristics strongly indicate that lithium exists in the spinel-type lithium manganese oxides in a highly ionized state.

We have studied the mechanism of lithium ion extraction/insertion reactions with spinel-type lithium manganese oxides in aqueous phases [8]. There are two types of extraction/insertion reaction: a redox-type and an ion-exchange type. A series of the spinel-type lithium manganese oxides which can be expressed by the general formula $Li_nMn_{2-x}O_4(1\leq n\leq 1.33, 0\leq x\leq 0.33, n\leq 1+x)$ can be formed [8,9]. $(Li)[Mn_2]O_4$ and $(Li)[Li_{0.33}Mn_{1.67}]O_4$ are two typical spinel-type lithium manganese oxides, where ( ) and [ ] are 8a tetrahedral sites and 16d octahedral sites, respectively, of the cubic closed-packed oxygen framework of the spinel structure. In $(Li)[Mn_2]O_4$ spinel, $Li^+$ occupy the 8a tetrahedral sites and Mn occupies the 16d octahedral sites. In $(Li)[Li_{0.33}Mn_{1.67}]O_4$ spinel, $Li^+$ occupy the 8a tetrahedral sites and up to a sixth of manganese ions in $LiMn_2O_4$ can be substituted by lithium as vacant 16d octahedral sites of manganese shown as Fig. 1 [10]. Extraction of lithium from this material progresses mainly by the ion-exchange of Li for protons to give a spinel-type manganese oxide with lattice protons. The ion-exchange type extraction/insertion reaction can be expressed by the following equation:

$$
(\mathrm{Li})[\mathrm{Li}_{0.33}\mathrm{Mn}_{1.67}^{\mathrm{IV}}]\mathrm{O}_{4}+1.33\mathrm{H}^{+}\rightarrow(\mathrm{H})[\mathrm{H}_{0.33}\mathrm{Mn}_{1.67}^{\mathrm{IV}}]\mathrm{O}_{4}+1.33\mathrm{Li}^{+}. \tag{1}
$$

Neutron diffraction studies by Ammundsen et al. have shown that reinsertion of $Li^+$ progresses for tetrahedral sites but not for octahedral ones [11].

In order to understand the guiding principle for the improvement of adsorption sites, fundamental knowledge on chemical bonding is essential. In a preceding letter, we have reported the results of preliminary calculation on the electronic structure and chemical bonding of $LiMn_2O_4$ spinel [12]. We are especially interested in the difference in magnitude of covalency around a Li atom between tetrahedral and octahedral sites of an ion-exchange type $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$ host since its protonated sample is a promising adsorbent for lithium in seawater. In the present paper, cluster models for tetrahedral and octahedral coordination of lithium are proposed for two kinds of spinel-type adsorbent, $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$ and chemical bonding characteristics

were calculated by the discrete-variational (DV)-X$\alpha$ molecular orbital method. The bonding natures between tetrahedral and octahedral Li sites of the $\text{Li}_{1.33}\text{Mn}_{1.67}\text{O}_4$ were compared. The exchange properties of $\text{Li}_{1.33}\text{Mn}_{1.67}\text{O}_4$ are discussed in terms of the bond overlap populations through Mulliken's population analysis [13].

## 2. Computational procedures

First-principles molecular orbital (MO) calculations by the DV-X$\alpha$ method [14,15] using the program code SCAT [15] were performed in order to obtain the electronic structure and chemical bonding nature of $\text{Li}_{1.33}\text{Mn}_{1.67}\text{O}_4$. Numerical atomic orbitals (NAO) were employed as the basis functions. They were generated and optimized by solving the radial part of the Schrödinger equation for a given environment at each iteration of the self-consistent calculation. All the elements of Hamiltonian and overlap matrices were calculated numerically on the basis of the discrete variational integration scheme [14,15]. The detailed computational procedure has been described in the literature [15]. The lattice constant used in the present study was $a=8.19$ Å for $\text{Li}_{1.33}\text{Mn}_{1.67}\text{O}_4$ [16] as shown in Table 1. Calculations were made for $\text{Li}_{1.33}\text{Mn}_{1.67}\text{O}_4$ using model clusters of $(\text{Li}_5\text{Mn}_{12}\text{O}_{40})^{27-}$ for tetrahedral sites in $\text{Li}_{1.33}\text{Mn}_{1.67}\text{O}_4$ and $(\text{Li}_7\text{Mn}_{18}\text{O}_{38})^3$ for octahedral site in $\text{Li}_{1.33}\text{Mn}_{1.67}\text{O}_4$, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2. ## 3. Results and discussion

The electronic structure and chemical bonding characteristics of $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_{4}$ were calculated using two cluster models, tetrahedral and octahedral coordination of lithium and oxygen.

Figs. 3 and 4 show the density of states (DOSs) obtained for tetrahedral and octahedral coordination models, respectively. Figs. 3(a) and 4(a) show the calculated DOS of $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_{4}$. The molecular orbital calculation yields discrete MO levels. The discrete MO levels are convoluted by Gaussian functions with a full-width at the half-maximum (FWHM) of 1.0 eV for easy visualization of DOS. The energy scale is shifted to make the Fermi level $(E_{\text{F}})$ zero. In Figs. 3 and 4, the partial density of states (PDOSs) is also plotted for each atomic orbital. Both the DOS and PDOS of these models are quite similar to those of each model. The filled band located in the energy range from $-3$ to $-8$ eV remains composed mainly of O 2p orbitals. The partially filled band located around the Fermi energy is also composed mainly of Mn 3d orbitals. An unoccupied band above 10 eV is made up of Li 2sp and Mn 4sp orbitals. The Li 2sp states made very slight contributions to the valence band, suggesting that Li is highly ionized in $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_{4}$. On the other hand, the Mn 3d, 4s and 4p bands significantly overlap the O 2p band. The mixing of O 2p states with the Mn 3d band is also notable. Accordingly, there are strong covalent interactions between Mn and O ions.

The interactions between Mn and O, and Li and O have been carefully investigated. In order to discuss the covalent interactions in more detail, overlap population diagrams for Mn-O and Li-O bonds were plotted, as shown in Figs. 3(b and c) and 4(b and c). The strength of the covalent bonding between atoms A and B can be estimated from the overlap population which is evaluated as having an energy distribution of overlap population , called the overlap population diagram or COOP (crystal orbital overlap population) diagram. Thus, the bond overlap population $Q_{AB}$ between A and B is obtained by summing the for all the MO levels below $E_F$. Figs. 3(b and c) and 4(b and c) show the bonding and antibonding contributions to the overlap population between Mn and O ions, Li and O ions. The sum of these two components is shown in the upper-right side of each figure.

Regarding the Mn-O bonding (Figs. 3(b) and 4(b)), the bonding and antibonding interactions coexist. However, the bonding contribution of Mn-O is greater than the antibonding; therefore, their contribution to the bonding states is very large. This shows that the dominant chemical bonding in $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$ is due to Mn-O. The bonding contribution of Li-O (Figs. 3(c) and 4(c)) is much smaller than that of the Mn-O bonding. This is probably due to the fact that Li atoms in $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$ are virtually in the $Li^+$ state and make slight covalent interactions with the surrounding atoms. In order to quantitatively evaluate the net charge of Li in the two adsorption sites, Mulliken's population has been analyzed. The calculated net charge of Li (tet) at 0.76 is greater than that of Li (oct) at 0.65, reflecting the shorter Li-O (oct) distance (1.93 Å) than that of Li-O (tet) (1.98 Å). This means that Li-O (tet) is more ionic than Li-O (oct). Such an interaction may play a role in adsorption when such lithium ions are inserted into $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$. In the case of $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_4$ stronger covalent bonding was shown for octahedral Li than tetrahedral Li, it is natural that the ion exchangeability of atoms in solid is correlated

with its ionicity. The present results suggest that the tetrahedral site has a higher ion exchangeability than the octahedral site.

These results are consistent with the widely accepted picture of Li-inserted compounds, and are likely to be a very important factor in determining the excellent selectivity for $Li^{+}$ ions of spinel-type lithium manganese oxides.

## 4. Conclusions

The electronic structure and chemical bonding nature of $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_{4}$ were studied by the DV-X$\alpha$ molecular orbital method using cluster models of $(Li_{5}Mn_{12}O_{40})^{27-}$ for tetrahedral sites in $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_{4}$ and $(Li_{7}Mn_{18}O_{38})^{3}$ for octahedral sites in $Li_{1.33}Mn_{1.67}O_{4}$, respectively. The Mulliken's population analysis was thoroughly conducted to examine the net charges of atoms as well as the magnitudes of covalent bondings between atoms. We found that strong covalent bonding formed between Mn and O ions, while however, Li-O was highly of the two adsorption sites, the tetrahedral sites have a higher Li ionic state than the octahedral sites, while the octahedral sites are more strongly covalent than the tetrahedral sites. These results are likely to be a very important factor in determining the optimum selectivity for $Li^{+}$ ions of spinel-type lithium manganese oxides through the exchange of Li and protons in aqueous solutions.
