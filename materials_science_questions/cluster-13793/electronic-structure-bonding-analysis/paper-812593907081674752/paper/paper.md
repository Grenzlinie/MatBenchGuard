# Spin Wave Theory and Ab Initio Calculations in Co/C Multilayers: Interfacial Effects

## Abstract
Co/C multilayers were prepared using DC triode sputtering with different Co thicknesses ($t_{Co}$), and their magnetic properties were investigated using a vibrating sample magnetometer. It is found that the magnetization of Co layers decreases with decreasing $t_{Co}$, at thicknesses below 6 nm, which can be understood by the presence of a magnetically dead layer, due to intermixing between Co and C layers, i.e., the alloy formation around the former interface. A model based on the spin-wave theory was utilized to explain the change of the magnetization as function of temperature, and estimate the coupling exchange interaction. Ab initio calculations were utilized to explain the decrease of the magnetic moment within the interfacial layer ($Co_{1- x}C_x$). In addition, the influence of pressure on the magnetic properties of the $Co_{1-x}C_x$ layer was studied as well.

**Keywords** Co/C multilayers · Ferromagnetic · Interface · Spin wave · Ab-initio calculations

## 1 Introduction
Investigation of multilayers has been one of the greatest research areas with important technological applications. The electronic and magnetic properties of these multilayers significantly depend on the layer thicknesses. Consequently, the study of the thickness effect in a multilayer system is a fundamental importance in order to exploit them in applications. The magnetic properties of multilayers are interesting since they could be different from the bulk or single thin films count parts [1, 2]. The multilayer structure composed of magnetic layers separated by nonmagnetic spacer layers present a high potential for applications in magnetic storage [3] and magnetic recording heads [4]. Especially, the multilayers based on Co found applications in various fields such as magneto elastic sensors [5] and in magnetooptical devices [6]. In this paper, we focus on the Co/C multilayers which could be used effectively in the areas where extremely high wear resistance is required [7]; they can exhibit magnetorestriction and uniaxial anisotropy [8]. In addition, this system is known to present soft magnetic properties, which makes it interesting in high-frequency magnetic devices with a good frequency response of the permeability [9]. We investigate the magnetic changes occurring in Co/C multilayers depending on cobalt thickness ($t_{Co}$). Magnetization measurement analyses allow determining the interfacial effect on the magnetization of the Co/C multilayers. In addition, we have used a model based on the spin-wave theory to depict the thermal change of the magnetization versus $t_{Co}$ and, thus, to determine the coupling exchange interactions.

The growth rates were $10$ Å min$^{-1}$ and $1.5$ Å min$^{-1}$ for Co and C layers, respectively. $t_{Co}$ was varied from 30 to 120 Å, while the C thickness ($t_C$) was fixed at 20 Å. Magnetic measurements were carried out using vibrating sample magnetometer. We have modeled the Co/C multilayers by a system consisting of an alternation of a ferromagnetic layer separated by a nonmagnetic layer. We represent the Heisenberg Hamiltonian in the ladder operators' base using the Holstein-Primakoff formalism. After that, the dispersion modes of the spin waves through a special numerical code were calculated. Ab initio calculations were carried out using first principles in the framework of density functional theory (DFT), as implemented in WIEN2k code [10-13]. The GGA-PBE (generalized gradient approximation energy functional formulated by Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof) parameterization of exchange-correlation energy was utilized. We have included the spin polarization to study the magnetic properties of the most stable phases of $Co_{1-x}C_x$ [14-17], such as $Co_3C$ and $Co_2C$. As seen from Fig. 1 and Table 1, $Co_3C$ and $Co_2C$ crystalize in an orthorhombic structure with the spatial groups Pnma and Pmnn, respectively. We have taken into account the self-consistent calculations to converge once the convergence tolerance of energy and charge were smaller than $10^{-4}$ Ry and $10^{-4}$ electron charges. The $k$-points number was 1000, in the irreducible Brillouin zone (BZ). We have used wave number cutoff $K_{\text{max}}$ such as $R_{\text{mt}} \times K_{\text{max}}=7.0$, where $R_{\text{mt}}$ is the minimum radius of the muffin-tin spheres and $K_{\text{max}}$ provides the amount of the largest $k$ vector in the plane wave expansion.

## 3 Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Magnetics Properties

The magnetization of Co/C multilayers decrease with the decrease of $t_{Co}$, which can be explained by the presence of a magnetic dead layer at each interface. The existence of a dead layer can be explained by the intermixing between Co and C layers at the interface, to form interfacial $Co_{1-x}C_x$ alloys. Using dead layer model, the magnetization of the multilayer, $M$ can be expressed as:

$$
M_{s}=M_{0} ×\left(1-2 \delta / t_{C 0}\right) \tag{1}
$$

where $M_0$ is the bulk magnetization of Co and $\delta$ is the dead layer thickness at each interface. Using the magnetization M data as a function of $t_{Co}$, the dead layer thickness was determined from Fig.2, where it is plotted the product $M × t_{Co}$ vs $t_{Co}$ both at 300 and 5 K. The corresponding Co dead layer thicknesses were 11.5 and 10.5 Å at 300 and 5 K, respectively.

The curve slope $M_0$ was found to be $(1.45±0.05)×10^3$ emu/cm$^3$ at 300 and 5 K, respectively, which is in line with the Co bulk value [19, 20].

The room temperature M-H hysteresis loops of $\mathrm{Co}_{80 \AA} / \mathrm{C}_{20 \AA}$ were displayed in Fig.3. The magnetic field was applied in-plane parallel and perpendicular to easy axis direction. As can be observed, the hysteresis shape depends on the orientation of the applied field direction indicating the existence of a uniaxial anisotropy $H_{\text{K}}$ in the plane of the multilayer. $H_{\text{K}}$ remains small ($H_{\text{K}} < 40$ Oe) and could arise from magnetoelastic interaction.

Figure 4 displays the variation of magnetization as a function of temperature for various Co thicknesses ranging from 30 to 120 Å. The magnetization decreases with the decrease of $t_{Co}$.

The calculations were carried out using a model based on spin waves in ferromagnetic/nonmagnetic multilayers [21, 22]. We have considered that the multilayer $(X_{n}/Y_{m})_{p}$ was made by the use of an alternate of a magnetic layer (X) and nonmagnetic layer (Y). The bilayers number was denoted $p$. Every bilayer $X_{n}/Y_{m}$ was indexed by a number $\beta(\beta=1,2,...,p)$. The ferromagnetic film is composed by $n$ atomic planes and the nonmagnetic one by $m$ atomic planes. The vector spin operator, of the atom i in the plane $\alpha(\alpha=1,2,...,n)$ of the magnetic film $\beta$, was denoted $\overrightarrow{e}_{z}$. The lattice unit vectors $(\overrightarrow{e}_{x},\overrightarrow{e}_{y},\overrightarrow{e}_{z})$ were taking so that $\overrightarrow{e}_{z}$ was vertical to atomic planes. It is also assumed that the lattice is rigid and that the interfaces are ideal with no defects.

The Heisenberg Hamiltonian can be expressed as:
$$
H=H_{e}+H_{a}+H_{d} \tag{2}
$$

$H_e$ describes the exchange interactions in the same magnetic layers and between adjacent magnetic layers:

$$
H_{e}=-J_{b}\left[\sum_{\langle i \alpha \mu, j \alpha \mu\rangle}^{b} \overrightarrow{S}_{i \alpha \mu} \overrightarrow{S}_{j \alpha \mu}+\sum_{\langle i \alpha \beta, j \alpha^{\prime} \beta\rangle}^{b} \overrightarrow{S}_{i \alpha \beta} \overrightarrow{S}_{j \alpha^{\prime} \beta}\right]-J_{S} \sum_{\langle i \alpha \beta, j \alpha \beta\rangle}^{S} \overrightarrow{S}_{i \alpha \beta} \overrightarrow{S}_{j \alpha \beta}-J_{I} \sum_{\langle i \alpha \beta, j \alpha^{\prime} \beta^{\prime}\rangle}^{I} \overrightarrow{S}_{i \alpha \beta} \overrightarrow{S}_{j \alpha^{\prime} \beta^{\prime}} \tag{3}
$$

$J_b, J_S$, and $J_I$ are the bulk exchange, interface exchange, and the interlayer coupling constants. The interface anisotropy contribution $H_a$ can be expressed as:
$$
H_{a}=D^{\perp} \sum_{i \alpha \beta}^{s}\left(S_{i \alpha \beta}^{Z}\right)^{2}+D^{/ /} \sum_{i \alpha \beta}^{s}\left(\left(S_{i \alpha \beta}^{X}\right)^{2}-\left(S_{i \alpha \beta}^{Y}\right)^{2}\right) \tag{4}
$$

$D^{\perp}$ and $D^{/ /}$are the interfacial magnetic anisotropy constants for uniaxial components perpendicular and parallel directions to the layers plane.

The contribution of dipolar interactions can be described by $H_d$ which is given by:

The Holstein-Primakoff linear approximation [23, 24] leads to the equation of the Heisenberg-type system Hamiltonian:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & H_{0}+\sum_{k \alpha \mu}^{s} A_{k}\left(b_{k \alpha \mu} b_{-k \alpha \mu}+b_{k \alpha \mu}^{+} b_{-k \alpha \mu}^{+}\right) \\
& +\sum_{k \alpha \mu}^{s} B_{k} b_{k \alpha \mu}^{+} b_{k \alpha \mu}+\sum_{k \alpha \mu}^{b} C_{k} b_{k \alpha \mu}^{+} b_{k \alpha \mu} \\
& +\sum_{k\left\langle\alpha \mu, \alpha^{\prime} \mu\right\rangle} D_{k} b_{k \alpha \mu}^{+} b_{k \alpha^{\prime} \mu}+\sum_{k\left\langle\alpha \mu, \alpha^{\prime} \mu^{\prime}\right\rangle} E_{k} b_{k \alpha \mu}^{+} b_{k \alpha^{\prime} \mu^{\prime}},(6)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $H_{0}$ is a constant

$$
A_{k}=2 S . D^{/ /}-\omega_{M}\left(N_{z}-\frac{1}{2} \sin ^{2} \theta_{k}\right),
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{k} & =2 S\left(J_{s}\left(n^{/ /}-\lambda_{k}\right)+J_{b} n^{\perp}+J_{I} n^{\uparrow}+D^{\perp}\right) \\
& +\frac{1}{4} \omega_{M} \sin ^{2} \theta_{k} \cdot e^{-i 2 \varphi_{k}},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C_{k}=2 J_{b} S\left(\left(n^{/ /}-\lambda_{k}\right)+2 n^{\perp}\right)+\frac{1}{4} \omega_{M} \sin ^{2} \theta_{k} \cdot e^{i 2 \varphi_{k}},
$$

$$
D_{k}=-J_{b} . S . \lambda_{k}^{\prime},
$$

$$
E_{k}=-J_{I} . S . \lambda_{k}^{\prime},
$$

$$
\omega_{M}=\gamma \sqrt{B_{o}\left(B_{o}+4 \pi M_{s}\right)},
$$

where $N_{z}$ is the demagnetization factor in the z-axis, $\theta_{k}$ is the angle between the z-axis and the magnon wave vector $\vec{k}$ and $\varphi_{k}$ is the angle between the projection of the vector $\vec{k}$ in film plane (x–y plane) and the x-axis, $(\gamma=\mathrm{g} \mu_{\mathrm{B}} / \hbar)$ is the gyromagnetic ratio and $B_{0}$ is the external dc field. The $\lambda_{k}$ and $\lambda_{k}^{\prime}$ coefficients are associated with the crystallography of the magnetic film. $n^{/ /}$is the nearest neighbor site number within the same atomic plan, and $n^{\perp}\left(n^{\uparrow}\right)$ is the nearest–neighbors number in the adjacent plan in the same (adjacent) magnetic layer, for a hexagonal structure, with (001) direction, $n^{/ /}=6, n^{\perp}=3$, and $n^{\uparrow}=3$.

### 3.2 Electronic Properties

According to literature, $Co_{3}C$ or $Co_{2}C$ are the most stable phases for the $Co_{1-x}C_{x}$ alloys and thus the most probable to be formed at the Co/C interface [5, 28–31], as a consequence we focus on these stable phases. We have studied their projected density of states (PDOS) (Fig.6). The partial density of states can be separated into 3parts. The first proportion of the lower valence band in the range of $-7.88$ to $-4.62$ eV, was composed mainly by Co-d states and C-p DOS. The second proportion consists of the upper valence band in the range of $-4.62$ to $0$ eV, the Co-d states overcome, and the influence of C states to the DOS are very negligible. The third part of the conduction band up to the Fermi level, the principle contributions were due to Co(1)-d, Co(2)-d, and C-p states. It can be seen that there is a hybridization between C-p and Co-d states in the first and third regions [32], highlighting a covalent bonding between Co and C atoms [33, 34].

For $Co_{2}C$ (Fig.6b), where the C concentration is greater, there is an overlap of Co-d and C-p states, above and below the Fermi level at the energy ranges from $-7.40$ to $-3.56$ eV and $1.40$ to $3.6$ eV, showing a covalent interaction between Co and C atoms. ## 4 Conclusion
In summary, the magnetic and electronic properties of Co/C multilayers were investigated. The magnetization was found to decrease with decreasing Co layer thickness, probably due to the presence of $Co_{1-x}C_x$ carbides at the Co/C interface. A model based on the spin-wave theory was utilized to extract the magnitudes of the exchange interactions for various Co thicknesses. The experimental temperature dependence of the magnetization was fitted. Ab initio calculations were per- formed to shed light on the magnetic and electronic structure of the formed interfacial layer. It is shown that both the incor- poration of C and the interfacial strain decrease the magnetic moment of Co atoms at the interface. From DOS calculations, the reduction of magnetism at the interface can be understood by taking into account p-d/or d-d interactions between C and Co or between Co and Co atoms.
