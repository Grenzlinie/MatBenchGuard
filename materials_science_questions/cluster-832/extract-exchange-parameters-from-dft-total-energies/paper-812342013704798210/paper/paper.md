# Electronic and magnetic properties of bulk and (100) and (111) surfaces of $MnPt_3$: An *ab initio* study

M. P. J. Punkkinen, $^{1,}$* L. Vitos, $^{2,3}$ K. Kokko, $^{1}$ K. Laaksonen, $^{1}$ and I. J. Väyrynen $^{1}$

$^{1}$Department of Physics, University of Turku, FIN-20014 Turku, Finland
$^{2}$Applied Materials Physics, Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Royal Institute of Technology, SE-10044 Stockholm, Sweden
$^{3}$Research Institute for Solid State Physics and Optics, P.O. Box 49, H-1525 Budapest, Hungary

(Received 12 August 2003; revised manuscript received 11 February 2004; published 19 July 2004)

Electronic and magnetic properties of bulk and close-packed surfaces of the intermetallic compound $MnPt_3$ have been investigated by means of the exact muffin-tin orbital method. The present *ab initio* bulk parameters compare well with the available experimental data. Theoretical ground state of the bulk $MnPt_3$ is ferromagnetic, in perfect agreement with the observation. Ferromagnetic configuration is found to be stable on the (111) free surface, while on the (100) surface an antiferromagnetic state is obtained. Top layer relaxation of the (100) surface is shown to have no influence on the antiferromagnetic ground state. Total energy differences between ferro- and antiferromagnetic structures on the surfaces are significantly smaller than in the bulk, which may have implications concerning the Kerr effect of $MnPt_3$. Using the linear muffin-tin orbital method we identify prominent structures in the density of states of the surface layers.

## I. INTRODUCTION

One important reason, which makes systems like $MnPt_3$ interesting, is that they present a large Kerr rotation $^{1}$ at particular wavelengths, thus exhibiting magneto-optical properties relevant for recording devices. Kato *et al.*$^{2}$ found a large Kerr rotation in ordered $MnPt_3$ alloy films which was discovered also theoretically.$^{3,4}$ The microscopic origin of the Kerr effect was connected to the spin-orbit coupling strength on Pt atoms by Oppeneer *et al.*$^{3,5}$ However, exchange splitting on Mn atoms is also necessary and, in fact, the magnitude of the magnetic moment on $X(=V,Cr,Mn,Fe,Co)$ atoms in $XPt_3$ compounds was found to affect the magnitude of the Kerr effect.$^{5}$ Bulk $MnPt_3$ has the ferromagnetic $L1_2$ structure.$^{6}$ In contrast with this experimental observation, former semiempirical investigation $^{7}$ predicted an antiferromagnetic ground state, with ferromagnetic (110) layers. On the other hand, antiferromagnetic materials do not show Kerr effect,$^{5}$ and thus, ferromagnetism of the $MnPt_3$ should be a prerequisite for the observed large Kerr effect $^{1}$ in this system. To the authors' knowledge, however, the correct ferromagnetic ordering of the bulk $MnPt_3$ has not been confirmed theoretically. Here we address this question by using an *ab initio* local density functional approach.

Bulk Mn presents a unique structural complexity among the transition metals, which is associated by rich magnetic ordering properties.$^{8}$ In low-dimensional systems, e.g., impurities or thin films, Mn exhibits an even more complex magnetic behavior. For instance, it has been predicted that Mn thin films on Co(001) surfaces are magnetically ordered and this magnetic ordering depends on the thickness of the film.$^{9}$ These exceptional properties of Mn are usually related to the half-filling of the $3d$ band.$^{8}$ Mn atoms preserve much of their atomic nature in $MnPt_3$, which can be realized, e.g., from the large spin magnetic moments of the Mn atoms in $MnPt_3$. Therefore, understanding the magnetic properties of bulk $MnPt_3$ represents an important task also if the atomic properties of Mn are considered. Detailed knowledge of the electronic and magnetic properties of $MnPt_3$ surfaces is needed for the correct interpretation of the surface-sensitive magneto-optical Kerr effect.$^{10}$ Since the magnetic ground state of the bulk $MnPt_3$ is almost degenerate (which is shown in Sec. III A), its surfaces are interesting from a theoretical point of view as well. Due to the reduced coordination number and to a different surface exchange constant compared to that of bulk,$^{11}$ it is plausible to assume that the surface magnetic order is changed, as it has been observed or predicted for surfaces of some nonmagnetic materials.$^{12-14}$

In the near past the electronic structure of bulk $MnPt_3$ has been thoroughly investigated both theoretically and experimentally.$^{3-5,7,15-19}$ However, the (111) free surface of $MnPt_3$ has been studied only by a semiempirical tight-binding scheme,$^{17,18}$ and no theoretical investigations for the (100) surface have been published so far. In this paper, we present results of an *ab initio* density functional$^{20}$ calculation of the $MnPt_3$ bulk, (100), and (111) surfaces, and compare them with the available experimental data. We investigate stability of different bulk and surface magnetic structures, and show that in the bulk and on the close-packed (111) surface the ferromagnetic structure is stable, while the (100) surface prefers an antiferromagnetic configuration.

## II. METHOD OF CALCULATION

The present *ab initio* calculations are based on the density functional theory$^{20}$ (DFT) using the local spin density approximation (LDA),$^{21}$ the generalized gradient approximation (GGA),$^{22}$ and the local Airy gas approximation (LAG) (Ref. 23) for the exchange-correlation energy functional. The Kohn-Sham$^{24}$ equations have been solved by using the exact muffin-tin orbitals (EMTO) method,$^{25-27}$ and the total energy

has been calculated by the full charge density technique. $^{28}$ In order to determine the site and layer projected density of states (DOS) the linear muffin-tin orbitals (LMTO) method within the atomic sphere approximation (ASA) has been employed. $^{29-31}$

In our *ab initio* simulations, in addition to the observed ferromagnetic (FM) phase, we have also included three antiferromagnetic configurations. These antiferromagnetic structures can be described as antiferromagnetically coupled ferromagnetic layers in the $L1_2$ crystal lattice: $AFM_1$ is ferromagnetic in (100) plane, $AFM_2$ ferromagnetic in (110) plane, and $AFM_3$ ferromagnetic in (111) plane. We note that former semiempirical tight-binding calculations $^{7}$ predicted the $AFM_2$ to be the theoretical ground state structure of bulk $MnPt_3$. The above magnetic structures, i.e., the FM, $AFM_1$, $AFM_2$, and $AFM_3$ phases, can be modeled by crystal lattices with unit cells of 4, 16, 8, and 16 atoms, respectively. Recently, using very effective LMTO-ASA method, total energy calculations were performed $^{32}$ for the 16-atoms supercells, and it was demonstrated that the FM and $AFM_2$ are the two most stable structures. The obtained energy differences between the FM and $AFM_2$ structures and the $AFM_1$ and $AFM_3$ structures were significantly larger than the characteristic LMTO-ASA errors, excluding the possibility that these latter structures would be competing low temperature phases. Consequently, the present EMTO total energy study of the bulk $MnPt_3$ will be focused mainly on the FM and $AFM_2$ magnetic structures. In many former applications the EMTO method, in contrast to the LMTO-ASA approach, has proved to be highly accurate, $^{27}$ and therefore, it is perfectly suitable to resolve the small energy differences between the competing FM and $AFM_2$ phases.

The (100) and (111) surfaces have been simulated by supercells of atomic layers, separated by layers of empty spheres (Em) representing the vacuum region. In the EMTO calculations the (100) and (111) supercells consisted of 24 and 36 lattice sites, formed by 9 atomic + 3Em(100) layers, and by 6 atomic + 3Em(111) layers, respectively. In the LMTO-ASA calculations somewhat larger unit cells have been used: in the case of the (100) surface we considered 13 atomic + 3Em layers, while in the case of the (111) surface we included 10 atomic + 5Em layers. The (111) surface is a nonpolar surface, each layer having the stoichiometry of the $MnPt_3$ formula unit (f.u.). In the case of the (100) surface there are two kinds of supercells: MnPt(100) with Mn-Pt termination, and Pt(100) with Pt-Pt termination.

The calculations have been performed within the scalarrelativistic and soft-core approximations. Mn $3d$, $4s$, and $4p$, and Pt $5d$, $6s$, and $6p$ orbitals were treated as valence states. In the following, we give values for some parameters used in the EMTO calculations. The same atomic and potential sphere radii $^{27}$ were used for the Mn, Pt, and Em sites. The Green's function was calculated for 16 complex energy points distributed exponentially on a semicircular contour. The full charge density (FCD) was represented in one-center expansion in terms of spherical harmonics including terms up to $l_{\text{max}}^{\text{FCD}}$=8. The total energy functional was evaluated using the shape function techniques. $^{28}$ To test the effect of the EMTO basis set some parts of the calculations were carried out using both $spd$ ($l_{\text{max}}$=2), and $spdf$ ($l_{\text{max}}$=3) basis sets. In order to compare the EMTO total energies of the FM and $AFM_2$ magnetic structures of bulk $MnPt_3$ we set up supercells of 8-atoms, and use 396 uniformly distributed $k$-points in the irreducible wedge of the Brillouin zone (IBZ). The bulk self-consistent calculations were performed for 5 different volumes, and the equilibrium volumes, bulk moduli and total energies have been extracted from Morse fit functions. $^{33}$ The surface calculations were performed for 55 and 31 $k$-points in the IBZ of the tetragonal and hexagonal unit cells corresponding to the (100) and (111) surfaces, respectively. All the self-consistent calculations were carried out within the LDA, and using the so generated full charge density we computed the total energies within the GGA and LAG approximations as well. The self-consistency was assumed, when the total energy and the Fermi level differed by less than $10^{-7}$ Ry in successive iterations. Our calculations showed that these values yield well converged results for the properties of the investigated systems. Especially, the amount of $k$-points required for accurate results was thoroughly tested.

### III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

#### A. Bulk MnPt₃

In order to attest the accuracy of the EMTO method in the case of $MnPt_3$ system we present theoretical bulk parameters and compare them with the experimental data. In Table I we list equilibrium lattice parameters ($a$), bulk moduli ($B$), and total energy differences between the $AFM_2$ and FM phases ($\Delta E$), for both the $spd$ and $spdf$ EMTO basis sets, obtained using the LDA, GGA, and LAG exchange-correlation functionals.

The theoretical equilibrium lattice parameters and bulk moduli of the FM and $AFM_2$ structures have very similar values. Inclusion of the $f$-orbitals has the usual effect: it decreases the lattice parameter by $\sim$1%, and increases the bulk modulus by $\sim$7%. The GGA is expected to be particularly suitable for calculating total energies of transition metals and transition metal compounds with localized moments, where

variations in the electronic spin and charge densities are large. However, in the present application the best agreement with the experimental lattice parameter¹⁰ ($a$=3.910 Å) is obtained within the LAG approximation, using the well-converged $spdf$ basis set. This is in accordance with the recent finding that for the $4d$ and $5d$ transition metals the LAG yields better agreement with the experimental data than the GGA.²³ It is remarkable that the theoretical ground state is ferromagnetic irrespective of the approximations used. The total energy difference is only slightly influenced by the choice of the exchange-correlation energy functional and the basis set, except that the LDA energy difference is reduced when the $f$-orbitals are included. This is partly due to the strong underestimation of the equilibrium volume. Unfortunately, we are not aware of any published experimental data for the bulk modulus of MnPt₃.

According to Antonini *et al.*⁶ the experimental values for the magnetic moments in bulk MnPt₃ are 3.64(±0.08) $\mu_B$ for Mn and 0.26(±0.03)$\mu_B$ for Pt. However, a larger experimental value for the Mn magnetic moment (3.9 $\mu_B$) has been deduced from the measurements of Auwärter and Kussmann¹⁰,³⁴ The present theoretical spin magnetic moments calculated within the LDA and LAG approximation, using the $spdf$ basis set, are: 3.72 $\mu_B$ and 3.84 $\mu_B$ for Mn and 0.15 $\mu_B$ and 0.13 $\mu_B$ for Pt, respectively. These values are close to the recently published LDA results,³⁵ which were, however, obtained at the experimental lattice parameter.

In order to investigate the nature of the magnetic interactions within the bulk MnPt₃ system we make use of the Ising model, described by the spin Hamiltonian

$$
H=-\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i j} J_{i j} S^{2} \sigma_{i} \sigma_{j}, \tag{1}
$$

where $J_{ij}$=0, $S$ denotes the total spin of the Mn ion, and $\sigma$ has two possible values ($-1$ or 1). In Eq. (1) we have included the nearest Mn neighbors ($J_1$), next-nearest Mn neighbors ($J_2$) and next-next-nearest Mn neighbors ($J_3$). The exchange coupling constants $J_1$, $J_2$, and $J_3$ are determined from the total energies calculated for the FM, AFM₁, AFM₂, and AFM₃ structures. Since our results for the FM and the AFM₂ structures suggest that the value of the equilibrium lattice parameter depends very little on the magnetic structure, total energy calculations for the antiferromagnetic structures have been carried out at the theoretical LAG lattice constant of the FM structure, using the $spdf$ basis set, and 140 $k$-points in the IBZ of the 16-atoms supercells. The calculated total energies, expressed relative to $E(\text{FM})$, are: $E(\text{AFM}_1)$=5.37 mRy, $E(\text{AFM}_2)$=2.38 mRy, and $E(\text{AFM}_3)$=14.60 mRy (per f.u.), and the resulting exchange constants have the following values: $J_1$=0.37 mRy, $J_2$=$-0.11$ mRy, and $J_3$=0.18 mRy. We find that the $J$ values depend only weakly on the chosen exchange-correlation approximation. Except the next-nearest neighbor term ($J_2$), the calculated exchange interactions are ferromagnetic. The next-nearest Mn neighbors are connected through the Pt atoms, which are ferromagnetically spin-polarized, but show antiferromagnetic contribution to the net spin magnetic moment from $s$- and $p$-bands, similar to the systems, which are antiferromagnetically ordered via the superexchange mechanism.

### B. Magnetic structures of surfaces

Next we present total energy results for the (100) and (111) surfaces of MnPt₃. In order to decrease computational load, these surface calculations have been performed using the $spd$ basis set. The accuracy of the local and semilocal exchange-correlation functionals was studied within the jellium surface model,³⁶ and it was shown that the accuracy of the LDA is superior compared to those of the gradient corrected functionals, e.g., GGA or LAG. Accordingly, in the present surface calculations we employed the LDA for the exchange-correlation functional.

The estimated surface energy of the pure Pt is about 60% larger than that of pure Mn.³⁷ On these grounds we expect the Mn-Pt terminated (100) surface to be more stable than the pure Pt terminated surface, and thus, model the (100) surface of MnPt₃ by the MnPt(100) supercells. Furthermore, our 24-site slab cell includes 5 Mn atoms and 13 Pt atoms, which corresponds to a system consisting about 28% Mn. We assume that the energy difference between the considered magnetic structures on (100) facet is not markedly influenced by this small change in the composition.

There appear to be different total energy local minima corresponding to different surface magnetic structures. On the (111) surface the ferromagnetic structure FM(111) is found to be the most stable one. This is followed by the antiferromagnetic state AFM(111), in which the two outermost layers have opposite Mn and Pt spin magnetic moments compared to the inner part of the slab. The energy difference between AFM(111) and FM(111) is 6.48 mRy per surface unit cell (1.08 mRy per f.u.). The (100) surface reveals an antiferromagnetic structure AFM(100), where the localized spin moments of the Mn and Pt atoms in the first layer are antiparallel compared to the Mn spin magnetic moments of the inner layers. The ferromagnetic phase of the (100) surface (FM(100)) is laying with 3.09 mRy per surface unit cell (about 0.69 mRy per f.u.) above the AFM(100) phase. We also calculated the surface energy for the nonpolar (111) surfaces. The resulting values are 1.897 J/m² for the FM(111) and 1.924 J/m² for the AFM(111) surfaces. These values are comparable with the surface energy of 2.3 J/m², calculated from first principles for the face-centered cubic (111) surface of Pt.³⁸

Next we address the question of surface relaxation. We investigate how the top layer relaxation of the (100) surface changes its magnetic structure. The total energies of the (100) supercells, calculated for five different FM(100) and AFM(100) surfaces having different interlayer distances, are plotted in Fig. 1. The calculated energies have been fitted to a polynomial, from which the relaxation of the first layer is estimated. We find that for both magnetic configurations the top layer of MnPt₃ move inward with about 0.03 Å ($\sim 1\%$). Although the two curves are very close to each other, the relaxation does not change the relative stability of the considered magnetic structures. We note that the GGA and LAG

The difference between the (100) surface and (111) sur- faces concerning the ground state magnetic structures can be partly explained by the fact that the interlayer distance of the Mn-Pt layers is smaller on the (111) surface $(\sqrt{3}/3a)$ than on the (100) surface $(a)$. This increases the strength of the mag- netic interlayer coupling on the (111) surface relative to the (100) surface. On the other hand, it seems that on the (111) surface it is easier to find antiferromagnetic systems, in which many layers (including subsurface layers) on the sur- face have opposite spin moments compared to the inner lay- ers. From Eq. (1) the AFM(100) structure will be predicted to be the ground state, if we suppose that the magnitude of the exchange constant $J_{2}$ is increased on the surface, while the exchange constants $J_{1}$ and specially $J_{3}$ are decreased. This is plausible, because the exchange constant $J_{2}$ on the surface can be related to the superexchange mechanism and covalent bonding, rather than to the itinerant exchange and metallic bonding, which are weakened on surfaces. Further studies are needed to determine surface exchange coupling constants $J_{i,\parallel}$ ($J$ parallel to surface) and $J_{i,\perp}$ ($J$ perpendicular to surface).

The obtained energy differences between the FM and AFM structures can be compared to the corresponding bulk values (see Table I). Both surfaces seem to destabilize the ferromagnetic order within the few top layers. We note that if the magnetic structure of the real surface of the ${\text{MnPt}}_{3}$ differs considerably from the ferromagnetic structure (if many lay- ers on the surface have opposite magnetic moments com- pared to the inner layers), e.g., because of the effects of temperature or surface imperfections, the magneto-optical Kerr effect could be suppressed to some extent.

Spin magnetic moments and charge transfers of the Mn and Pt atoms on the (100) and the (111) surfaces for the considered magnetic structures are given layer by layer in Tables II and III. Bulk spin magnetic moments, calculated within the LDA using the $spd$ basis, are $3.81\mu_{B}$ for Mn and $0.13 \mu_{B}$ for Pt, and charge transfers are 0.12 electrons for Mn and $-0.04$ electrons for Pt. Corresponding values on the (100) surface are very similar to the bulk values in layer 3 and below showing that changes in the electronic structures are mainly limited to the two outermost layers.

The current results for the spin magnetic moments of the atoms in different surface layers indicate similar trends as those of Gallego, Chico, and Munoz$^{17}$ using a tight-binding scheme. The amount of neighbors of the atoms in the first layer is decreased and therefore electronic states become more localized and bands narrower. Therefore, spin magnetic moments of the atoms are increased.$^{39}$ The coordination number of the atoms on the (100) surface is lower than on the (111) surface, which results in larger spin magnetic mo- ments on the (100) facet. Pt atoms have less Mn neighbors on the (111) surface than on the (100) surface, which may explain that the spin magnetic moments of the Pt atoms are decreased in the first layer on the (111) surface, because the magnetic coupling is indirect. Atoms in the first layer lose some amount of their electronic charge, whereas the atoms in the second layer receive a portion of this amount. Compared to the (111) surface the lower coordination number on the (100) surface results in the enhanced charge transfer in the first layer.

### C. Electronic structure of the (111) surface

Surface introduces a drastic change in the potential of the electrons modifying the electronic structure near the surface. Because the (111) layers have the same stoichiometry as in the bulk, we can compare the DOS of the (111) surface directly with the DOS of the bulk. Figure 2 shows total and spin-projected atomic DOS of layers 1 (surface) and 5 (core of the slab). As expected, the core of the slab yields an electronic structure similar to the bulk. The DOS of the layers 3, 4 (not shown) and 5 were found to be quite similar to the DOS of the bulk and even the DOS of the layer 2 (not shown) differs only slightly from the bulk DOS. This supports our earlier observation that changes in the electronic structure are mainly limited to the two outermost layers. The DOS consists mostly of Mn and Pt $d$-states with an exception at the bottom of the valence band where a considerable amount of Pt $s$-states were found. Compared to the bulk, the electronic states in the first layer are more localized, which induces decreased bandwidth, combined with an enhanced DOS at the center of the band, and reduced hybridization.

In the DOS of the layer 1 enhanced structures at energies $-3.0$, $-1.1$, and 1.1 eV were discovered of which the last two structures (near the Fermi level) have significantly weaker intensities in layer 3 and below. The occupied peaks consist mainly of Pt states, whereas the unoccupied peak consists mainly of Mn states. Unfortunately, we can compare our results with experiments only in the case of occupied states. Recently, Borgschulte *et al.*$^{19}$ characterized a structure at $-1.1$ eV, found in angle-resolved photoemission spectra, as a surface state by comparison with the results of Oppeneer *et al.*$^{3}$ As Fig. 2 shows the Mn DOS decreases at energy $-1.1$ eV indicating that this structure is not formed via Mn-Pt hybridization. The Mn-Pt hybridization is significantly weakened on the surface, which can be realized e.g., considering majority spin Mn DOS of the layer 1 (solid circles), which is almost structureless. However, the unoccupied DOS peak at 1.1 eV is very strongly hybridized.

### IV. CONCLUSIONS

The electronic and magnetic structures of the bulk, (100), and (111) surfaces of MnPt$_3$ have been investigated by the EMTO total energy method. Good agreement has been found for the bulk ground state properties: volume and magnetic structure. In particular, an excellent agreement with experimental data has been obtained within the LAG approximation for the exchange-correlation energy functional. Total energy calculations for the bulk show that the ferromagnetic structure is the ground state of bulk MnPt$_3$, in accordance with experiments. The calculated exchange coupling constants $J_1$, $J_2$, and $J_3$ predict an antiferromagnetic exchange interaction between next-nearest Mn neighbors.

Total energy calculations for different magnetic structures on (100) and (111) surfaces of MnPt$_3$ show that the ferromagnetic phase is destabilized at the surfaces. It has been found that the ground state on the (100) surface is antiferromagnetic, which is not destroyed by the layer relaxation. Spin magnetic moments of the Mn atoms are enhanced in the

top surface layers. Because of the lower coordination number on the (100) surface, compared to that on the (111) surface, the Mn atoms on the (100) surface show larger spin magnetic moments relative to those on the (111) surface.

Prominent structures in the DOS have been detected on the (111) surface at energies -3.0, -1.1, and 1.1 eV. We have found that changes in the electronic DOS are limited to the two outermost surface layers, and the inner layers show DOS similar to that of bulk MnPt₃.
