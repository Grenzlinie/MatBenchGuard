# Determination of the long-range magnetic interactions in $NdCo_2Si_2$ under high pressure

S. Hane$^{a,*}$, T. Goto$^{a}$, T. Shigeoka$^{b}$

$^{a}$Institute for Solid State Physics, University of Tokyo, Kashiwanoha, Kashiwa-shi, Chiba-ken 277-8581, Tokyo, Japan
$^{b}$Faculty of Science, Yamaguchi University, Yamaguchi 753-8512, Japan

## Abstract

The magnetization of antiferromagnetic $NdCo_2Si_2$ was measured in high magnetic fields up to 15 T along the $c$-axis under high pressure up to 1.0 GPa. $NdCo_2Si_2$ exhibits three metamagnetic transitions at low temperatures. From the critical fields and the Neel temperature determined, the nearest-neighbor, second-nearest-neighbor and third-nearest-neighbor interactions between the $c$-planes were estimated under high pressure. © 2001 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

**Keywords**: $NdCo_2Si_2$; Magnetization; High pressure; Magnetization, High magnetic field

## 1. Introduction

The rare-earth intermetallic compound $NdCo_2Si_2$ crystallizes in the tetragonal $ThCr_2Si_2$-type crystal structure [1]. In this compound, only the $Nd^{3+}$ ion carries a magnetic moment. According to neutron diffraction and magnetic studies [2,3], the magnetic susceptibility and magnetization are highly anisotropic and the Nd moment is aligned along the $c$-axis due to strong uniaxial anisotropy. $NdCo_2Si_2$ is considered as an Ising system and has three antiferromagnetic phases at low temperatures. The transition temperatures were determined to be $T_{\text{N}1}=15$ K, $T_{\text{N}2}=28$ K and $T_{\text{N}3}=35$ K. In each antiferromagnetic state, the magnetic moments in the $c$-plane are coupled ferromagnetically. The propagation vector is written as $(0,0,k)$, where $k=1$ for $T<T_{\text{N}1}$, $k=\frac{13}{14}$ for $T_{\text{N}1}<T<T_{\text{N}2}$ and $k=\frac{11}{14}$ for $T_{\text{N}2}<T<T_{\text{N}3}$. The magnetic structure is commensurate below $T_{\text{N}1}$ and incommensurate for $T_{\text{N}3}>T>T_{\text{N}1}$. Shigeoka et al. [4] measured the high-field magnetization curve of $NdCo_2Si_2$ along the $c$-axis up to 15 T at various temperatures and determined the magnetic $B$-$T$ phase diagram. With increasing magnetic field, successive metamagnetic transitions were observed at low temperatures below $T_{\text{N}1}$ from the commensurate state with $k=1$ to the incommensurate state with $k=\frac{13}{14}$, to the state with $k=\frac{11}{14}$ and then to the forced ferromagnetic (FF) state. The saturation magnetization is consistent with the theoretical value of the free $Nd^{3+}$ ion [4]. Here, we call the phases with $k=1$, $\frac{13}{14}$ and $\frac{11}{14}$, phases I, II and III, respectively.

From the data of the critical temperatures and fields, we determined the $B$-$T$ phase diagrams of $\mathrm{NdCo}_{2}\mathrm{Si}_{2}$ for $B\|[001]$ at 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 GPa. We try to estimate the magnetic long-range interactions in the Ising system $\mathrm{NdCo}_{2}\mathrm{Si}_{2}$. The appearance of the incommensurate states suggests that these interactions compete with each other. In such an Ising spin system, the axial next-nearest-neighbor Ising (ANNNI) model is useful to examine the phase diagram and the magnetization process [8,9]. The ANNNI model includes the nearest-neighbor interaction between the spins in the $c$-plane and the nearest-neighbor and second-nearest-neighbor interactions between the $c$-planes. We extend the model for $\mathrm{NdCo}_{2}\mathrm{Si}_{2}$ to evaluate the third-nearest-neighbor interaction between the $c$-planes. We use the following effective Hamiltonian:

$$
H=-\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}^{N}2J_{ij}S_{i}S_{j}-g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}B\sum_{i}^{N}S_{i}, \tag{1}
$$

where $J_{ij}$ denotes the interaction between two spins at sites $i$ and $j$. We assume that each spin is Ising-like along the $c$-axis; $S=\pm\frac{1}{2}$. The $g$-factor is 6.1 for $\mathrm{NdCo}_{2}\mathrm{Si}_{2}$ at ambient pressure. Nearest-neighbor spins $i$ and $j$ in the $c$-plane are coupled by a ferromagnetic nearest-neighbor interaction, $J_{ij}$ ($\equiv J_{0}/4$) $>0$. Here the effective interaction between a spin and its nearest-neighbor, second-nearest-neighbor or third-nearest-neighbor $c$-plane are defined as $J_{1}$, $J_{2}$ or $J_{3}$, respectively. Since the ground state has the type-I spin structure, $J_{1}$ is negative, $J_{1}<0$. For the frustrated spin system, $J_{2}$ is also negative, $J_{2}<0$. We adapt the mean field approximation to the Hamiltonian (1). $\mathrm{NdCo}_{2}\mathrm{Si}_{2}$ exhibits three metamagnetic transitions from phase I to phase II, to phase III and then to the FF phase in the ground state. Considering the magnetic structure in each phase, the magnetic energy per Nd ion is written as follows:

$$
E_{\mathrm{I}}=2J_{1}S^{2}-2J_{2}S^{2}+2J_{3}S^{2}, \tag{2}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{II}}=\frac{12}{14}2J_{1}S^{2}-\frac{10}{14}2J_{2}S^{2}+\frac{10}{14}2J_{3}S^{2}-\frac{1}{14}g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}BS, \tag{3}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{III}}=\frac{8}{14}2J_{1}S^{2}-\frac{2}{14}2J_{2}S^{2}+\frac{4}{14}2J_{3}S^{2}-\frac{3}{14}g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}BS, \tag{4}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{FF}}=-2J_{1}S^{2}-2J_{2}S^{2}-2J_{3}S^{2}-g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}BS. \tag{5}
$$

At the critical fields $B_{\mathrm{C}1}$, $B_{\mathrm{C}2}$ and $B_{\mathrm{C}3}$, we have the relations $E_{\mathrm{I}}=E_{\mathrm{II}}$, $E_{\mathrm{II}}=E_{\mathrm{III}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{III}}=E_{\mathrm{FF}}$, which give the following equations:

$$
J_{1}=\frac{1}{168}(-31B_{\mathrm{C}1}+22B_{\mathrm{C}2}-33B_{\mathrm{C}3})\frac{g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{S}, \tag{6}
$$

$$
J_{2}=\frac{1}{336}(67B_{\mathrm{C}1}-34B_{\mathrm{C}2}-33B_{\mathrm{C}3})\frac{g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{S}, \tag{7}
$$

$$
J_{3}=\frac{1}{6}(B_{\mathrm{C}1}-B_{\mathrm{C}2})\frac{g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{S}. \tag{8}
$$

On the other hand, the Fourier transform of the exchange interaction is written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
J(q)=&J_{0}+2J_{1}\cos(\pi q)+2J_{2}\cos(2\pi q) \\
&+2J_{3}\cos(3\pi q). \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the wave vector $q=Q$ for phase III, the Neel temperature is given by

$$
T_{\mathrm{N}}=\frac{2}{3} J(Q) S(S+1). \tag{10}
$$

On substitution of $B_{\mathrm{C} 1}=3.27 \mathrm{~T}, B_{\mathrm{C} 2}=5.35 \mathrm{~T}, B_{\mathrm{C} 3}$ $=13.44 \mathrm{~T}, T_{\mathrm{N}}=31.9 \mathrm{~K}$ and $Q=\frac{11}{14} \mathrm{c}^{*}$ in Eqs. (6)-(10) we have $J_{0} / k_{\mathrm{B}}=36.25 \mathrm{~K}, \quad J_{1} / k_{\mathrm{B}}=-22.23 \mathrm{~K}$, $J_{2} / k_{\mathrm{B}}=-10.58 \mathrm{~K}$ and $J_{3} / k_{\mathrm{B}}=-3.033 \mathrm{~K}$ for ambient pressure. This result indicates that the interaction between the Nd ions is of the RKKY-type long range. The negative $J_{2}$ plays an important role for the appearance of the incommensurate phases. Next we try to estimate the pressure dependence of the interaction. We have already determined the pressure dependence of $B_{\mathrm{C} 1}, B_{\mathrm{C} 2}, B_{\mathrm{C} 3}$ and $T_{\mathrm{N}}$ (see Figs. 3 and 5). From the liner pressure dependence of these values, we obtain $\mathrm{d} \ln J_{0} / \mathrm{d} P=-0.8 \% /$ $\mathrm{GPa}, \quad \mathrm{d} \ln J_{1} / \mathrm{d} P=6.3 \% / \mathrm{GPa}, \quad \mathrm{d} \ln J_{2} / \mathrm{d} P=-$ $1.8 \% / \mathrm{GPa}$ and $\mathrm{d} \ln J_{3} / \mathrm{d} P=4.6 \% / \mathrm{GPa}$. The relative changes of $J_{0}$ and $J_{2}$ are smaller than those of $J_{1}$ and $J_{3}$. According to the simple RKKY model with localized moments and free electrons, the relative change of the interaction does not depend on the distance between localized moments and is given by $\delta J / J=2 \delta V / 3 V$, where $V$ is the volume of the system. This simple model cannot explain the experimental result that the interaction depends on the distance. Now the data of the compressibility of $\mathrm{NdCo}_{2} \mathrm{Si}_{2}$ are not available, but the value of $\delta V / V$ may be about $-1 \%$ at $1 \mathrm{GPa}$. Using the value, we get $\delta J / J \approx-0.7 \%$ at $1 \mathrm{GPa}$. This value is nearly equal to the magnitude of the change of $J_{0}$ and $J_{2}$.
