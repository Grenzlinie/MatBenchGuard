We report on the magnetization measurement of amorphous $\mathrm{Gd}_{X}\mathrm{Y}_{68-X}\mathrm{Cu}_{32}$ ($17\leqq X\leqq68$) alloys between 4.2 K and 300 K in fields up to 7 T. This system varies from ferromagnetic to cluster glass states with decreasing $X$. The high field magnetization in a cluster glass region as well as in a ferromagnetic phase is described well in terms of the spin wave $T^{3/2}$ law corrected for the applied field. The spin wave stiffness constant $D$ thus deduced is independent of field and temperature. We have found the following features in some Gd-based alloys: an amorphous material shows a smaller $D$ value than a crystalline counterpart; in both states, $D$ is directly proportional to the paramagnetic Curie temperature. These facts are quantitatively understood by the nearest neighbor Heisenberg model with fluctuations in the exchange interaction.

## §1. Introduction

It has been widely believed that there exist sharp long-wavelength spin waves in an amorphous system as well as in a crystalline material.$^{1-3)}$ Hilscher *et al.* and Krishnan *et al.* reported that the spin wave stiffness constant $D$ determined from the magnetization measurement increased with increasing field in amorphous Fe-based ferromagnets.$^{4-6)}$ Kaneyoshi explained their results qualitatively assuming an inhomogeneous distribution of magnetic moments in amorphous materials.$^{7)}$ He also insisted that this behavior of $D$ was a general phenomenon in amorphous ferromagnets. There is, however, an ambiguity in their analysis of the observed results, because the 3d itinerant electrons in their specimens may bring another term than that of spin waves to temperature dependence of the magnetization as in crystalline Fe-Ni and amorphous Fe-B alloys.$^{8-11)}$ In order to exclude the ambiguity, we need to investigate an amorphous system without itinerant electrons.

Gadolinium has an isotropic S-state configuration and consists of highly localized 4f electrons which cause most of the magnetic properties.$^{12)}$ The Gd-based alloy system with nonmagnetic Cu shows a eutectic point near the composition $\mathrm{Gd}_{2}\mathrm{Cu}$ and the atomic volume of Gd is nearly as large as that of nonmagnetic Y. Therefore, the amorphous system $\mathrm{Gd}_{X}\mathrm{Y}_{68-X}\mathrm{Cu}_{32}$ can be easily prepared over the entire range of the Gd concentration $X$ and is regarded as one of the most suitable materials for solving the problem of spin waves in the amorphous state.

As shown in Fig. 1, Cornelius *et al.*$^{**}$ have found from AC susceptibility measurements that the amorphous Gd-Y-Cu system shows a variety of magnetic properties with changing $X$: the ferromagnetism, the transition from a ferromagnetic to cluster glass phase and the cluster glass (hereafter, we call the low temperature phase the cluster glass rather than the spin glass because of a high Gd content). In the present work we have made the magnetization measurement of this system to understand the magnetic properties, and the characteristics of spin waves particularly. ## §2. Sample Preparation and Measurement

The alloy ingots were prepared from 3N Gd, 3N Y, 5N Cu and 5N Ag by argon arc melting. The ribbons of $Gd_{X}Y_{68-X}Cu_{32}$ $(17\leqq X\leqq68)$ and $Gd_{40}Ag_{60}$ alloys were pro- duced by the melt spinning technique. We checked them for an amorphous state by X- ray diffraction and bending tests. The alloy composition of the specimen was determined by the energy dispersive analysis of X-rays within the precision of 1 atomic percent.

Magnetization measurements were carried out with a microcomputer-controlled mag- netometer by the Faraday method to a relative accuracy better than $5\times10^{-4}$. A superconduct- ing magnet produced magnetic fields up to 7 T which were applied parallel to the plane of the ribbons. The temperature of the sample was detected with a $Au+0.03$ at%Fe-Chromel thermocouple and stabilized whithin 0.2 K.

A procedure of analysis is as follows: $^{8,11)}$ fixing certain values for the parameters, we estimate a standard deviation which indicates the degree of difference between the observed magnetization and the calculated one from eq. (3); then, we search the values of parameters which make the standard deviation the least. This routine is applied to the temperature dependence of the magnetization for the ferromagnetic alloys ($42 \leqq X \leqq 68$) shown in Fig. 7.

The analysis has shown such contributions as the renormalization of $D$, the higher-order parts of spin waves and the Stoner-type term to be negligibly small for all the ferromagnets (i.e., $D_1=0$, $D_2=0$, $\beta=0$ and $S_B=0$). This means that the temperature dependence of the magnetization can be expressed in terms of a simple $T^{3/2}$ law

$$
M(B, T)=M(B, 0)-\frac{g \mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{\rho} F\left(3 / 2, B_{T}\right)\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{4 \pi D}\right)^{3 / 2}. \tag{4}
$$

We can clearly show this result by plotting $M$ against $F(3 / 2, B_{T}) T^{3 / 2}$ as in Figs. 8(a), 8(b) and 8(c). The fit to eq. (4) shown by the straight lines describes the data well up to above $T_{\mathrm{C}} / 2$. As seen in Fig. 8(c), on the other hand, the data for the specimens with $X=42$ and 47 below 1 T cannot be expressed by eq. (4) because of the appearance of the cluster glass phase at low temperatures (also see Fig. 3).

Attention should be paid to the fact that the solid lines of Fig. 8(a) are parallel to each other. We have observed such behavior for all the ferromagnets but the alloys with $X=42$ and 47 below 1 T. This means that $D$ is independent of $B$ in marked contrast to the results by Hilscher et al. and Krishnan et al. $^{4-6)}$ If we re-draw $M$ against $T^{3 / 2}$ rather than $F(3 / 2, B_{T}) T^{3 / 2}$, we get the following results: roughly speaking, the data points lie on straight lines, although they are not parallel to each other and the standard deviations are larger than those obtained previously; the $D$ values thus estimated increases considerably with increasing $B$ (e.g., $14 \mathrm{meV} \mathring{A}^2$ at 1 T and $17 \mathrm{meV} \mathring{A}^2$ at 7 T for the alloy with $X=68$). It is quite obvious that this field dependence of $D$ comes from neglect of $F(3 / 2, B_{T})$. We, therefore, conclude that the $D$ value in the amorphous Gd-Y-Cu ferromagnet is independent of $B$ in contrast to the theory of Kaneyoshi. $^{7)}$

## §5. Relation between $D$ and $T_{\mathrm{p}}$

The relation between $D$ and $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ has been discussed theoretically and experimentally in a number of crystalline and amorphous fer- romagnets, $^{21-23)}$ because it can be expected that both quantities are connected by the exchange interaction. Although it is very difficult to derive an exact solution of $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ in most theoretical models, it may be rather easier to obtain an exact expression of $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ which is closely related to the exchange interaction as well. In this section we propose relation between $D$ and $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ rather than $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ and discuss the magnetic properties of Gd-based alloys in amorphous states, comparing with those of crystalline phases.

In Fig. 9 the $D$ values for the present alloys are plotted as a function of $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ together with those of the amorphous $\mathrm{Gd}_{80} \mathrm{Au}_{20}$ and $\mathrm{GdAl}_{2}$ alloys and the crystalline $\mathrm{GdAl}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Gd}$ fer romagnets. $^{12,17-20)}$ This figure contains four features. The first is that the results are separated definitely into two branches: one is for amorphous substances and the other is for crystalline materials. The second is that the data which belong to one branch lie on a straight line. The third is that the straight line passes through the origin. However, this is not the case in the plot of $D$ against $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ for the present alloys as is usual with ferromagnetic substances in the literature. $^{22,23)}$ The last feature is concerned with the amorphous cluster glasses ( $\mathrm{GdAl}_{2}$ and Gd-Y-Cu alloys with $X=32$ and 37), shown as the closed marks, which are on the same straight line as the amorphous ferromagnets; this suggests that the ferromagnetic spin waves are excited in all alloys with positive $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ caused by the ferromagnetic interaction. This problem has been investigated extensively from the theoretical point of view. $^{24-26)}$

The ratio of the effective spin deduced from $\mu_{\text {eff }}$ to the spin $S$ derived from $M(\infty \mathrm{T}, 4.2 \mathrm{~K})$

has been estimated to be between 1.2 and 1.6 for the amorphous Gd-Y-Cu and $Gd_{40}Ag_{60}$ ferromagnets. This suggests that they belong to the localized system rather than the itinerant one according to Rhodes and Wohlfarth. $^{27)}$ Moreover, they have no Stoner-type $T^{2}$ contribution resulting from itinerant electrons as stated in the previous section. It follows from these facts that most of their magnetic behavior is ascribed to the localized 4f electrons in the Gd components. Therefore, we discuss the present amorphous system within the framework of the Heisenberg model

$$
H=-\sum_{i, j} J_{i j} S_{i} \cdot S_{j}, \tag{5}
$$

where $J_{i j}=J(r)$ is the positive exchange integral which depends on the distance $r=|r_{i}-r_{j}|$ between the two spin vectors $S_{i}$ and $S_{j}$. In the quasicrystalline approximation for a perfectly isotropic case with small $q$, the Hamiltonian gives the expression of $D$ as $^{25)}$

$$
D=\frac{1}{3} S \int r^{2} J(r) G(r) \mathrm{d} r, \tag{6}
$$

where $G(r)$ is the radial distribution function. We assume $J(r)$ in the form

$$
J(r)=J(r_{0})+\Delta J, \tag{7}
$$

where $r_{0}$ means the average nearest neighbor distance of the magnetic pairs and $\Delta J$ represents fluctuations in the exchange integral due to the distribution of the nearest neighbor pair distance $\Delta r$. Approximating a peak in $G(r)$ for the magnetic nearest neighbor pair to a Gaussian probability density function, we can rewrite eq. (6) as $^{28)}$

$$
D=\frac{1}{3} z S J(r_{0}) r_{0}^{2}\left[1-2 z \frac{\Delta r}{r_{0}} \frac{\Delta J}{J(r_{0})}\right], \tag{8}
$$

where $z$ is the mean number of magnetic nearest neighbors. Here we note that the first term of eq. (8) corresponds to the expression of $D$ for the crystalline counterpart. The correct expression of $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ for Heisenberg-type amorphous materials is given by

$$
T_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{2}{3 k_{\mathrm{B}}} S(S+1) \int J(r) G(r) \mathrm{d} r, \tag{9}
$$

which is equivalent to $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ in the mean field model. In the same approximation of $J(r)$ and $G(r)$ as used in eq. (8), eq. (9) reduces to the simple form for the magnetic nearest neighbor pairs

$$
T_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{2}{3 k_{\mathrm{B}}} S(S+1) z J(r_{0}). \tag{10}
$$

From eqs. (8) and (10) we obtain the relation between $D$ and $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ as

$$
D / T_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}} r_{0}^{2}}{2(S+1)}\left[1-2 z \frac{\Delta r}{r_{0}} \frac{\Delta J}{J(r_{0})}\right]. \tag{11}
$$

In the present system it is reasonable to assume that Y, Cu and Ag are nonmagnetic and thus that only Gd-Gd pairs contribute to the magnetic properties. Unfortunately, the values of $z$, $r_{0}$ and $\Delta r$ for the present system are not currently available. To calculate the value of $D / T_{\mathrm{p}}$, then, we take the values $z=6$, $r_{0}=3.47$ Å and $\Delta r=0.37$ Å of amorphous $GdFe_{2}^{29)}$ and assume $S=7 / 2$ and $\Delta J / J(r_{0})$ $=0.25$. Combining these values with eq. (11), we have $D / T_{\mathrm{p}}=0.078$ meVÅK $^{-1}$, which represents the observed results quite well as shown by the solid line in Fig. 9.

It should be noted that the value of $D / T_{\mathrm{p}}$ for the crystalline system can be evaluated from the first term of eq. (11). Crystalline $GdAl_{2}$ has a C15 Laves phase structure with $z=4$ and $r_{0}=3.422$ Å (which is calculated from the lattice constant $a=7.902$ Å$^{30)}$), from which we obtain $D / T_{\mathrm{p}}=0.112$ meVÅ$^{2}$K $^{-1}$ assuming $S=7 / 2$. For metallic Gd $D / T_{\mathrm{p}}=$ $0.122$ meVÅ$^{2}$K $^{-1}$ is calculated from $z=12$, $S=$ $7 / 2$ and $r_{0}=3.573$ Å (which is deduced from $a=3.6360$ Å and $c=5.7826$ Å$^{31)}$). These theoretical values are in fairly good agreement with the observed ones (see Table I).

We next concentrate on the value of $J$ which can be estimated in two ways: one is from $D$ using eq. (8) and the other is from $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ using eq. (10). Both $J$ values thus determined are listed in Table I. They agree quite well with each other in the amorphous system. For the crystalline ferromagnets, on the other hand, they are slightly different from each other. Such a difference has been already pointed out in crystalline Gd. $^{32)}$ It has been also observed that $J$ of crystalline $GdAl_{2}$ is fairly larger than that of the other alloys.

As seen in Table I, the $J$ value of the present amorphous alloy shows a linear increase with

increasing $X$. Extrapolation of this linear relation to $X=100$ gives the value $J=0.299$ meV, from which we estimate $D=17.1$ meV$\mathring{\text{A}}^2$ and $T_{\text{p}}=219$ K for amorphous Gd using eqs. (8) and (10) with the same values of $S$, $z$, $r_0$, $\Delta r$ and $\Delta J/J(r_0)$ that mentioned above. These values of $D$ and $T_{\text{p}}$ are also plotted in Fig. 9.

## §6. Onset of Ferromagnetism

In this section two approaches are attempted to the problem of the onset of ferromagnetism in the amorphous Gd-Y-Cu system: one is by the percolation theory and the other is by the representation of critical behavior.

In the constant coupling approximation, $T_{\text{C}}$ of the nearest neighbor Heisenberg ferromagnet with isotropic coupling is derived from the expression$^{33)}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^{2S} (2k+1)&[(z-1)k(k+1)-2zS(S+1)] \\
&\times \exp\left[k(k+1)J/k_{\text{B}}T_{\text{C}}\right]=0. \quad (12)
\end{aligned}
$$

If we regard the present system as a randomly diluted ferromagnet with nonmagnetic Y and Cu, $z$ in eq. (12) should be replaced by $(z'-1)X[1-(1-X)^{z'}]+1$ according to Oguchi and Obokata.$^{34)}$ Here $z'$ is the number of nearest neighbors which consist of magnetic Gd and nonmagnetic Y and Cu atoms. The following estimates are made from eq. (12) with $S=7/2$ (Heiman and Kazama have discussed $T_{\text{C}}$ of amorphous Gd-based alloys in the percolation theory with $S=1/2^{35)}$). The first estimate gives $X_{\text{c}}=13.9$ and $J=0.16$ meV by assuming $z'=12$ like FCC lattices, which is shown by the broken line in Fig. 10. Fixing $z'=8$ as BCC lattices, we obtain the second one with $X_{\text{c}}=21.4$ and $J=0.25$ meV as denoted by the dotted line. Both lines do not correspond to the observed values of $T_{\text{C}}$. Since these estimates show that $X_{\text{c}}$ increases with decreasing $z'$, we need to reduce the value of $z'$ in order to fit a calculated line to the observed result better than that evaluated above. Then we carry out the least-squares fitting with three disposable parameters $z'$, $X$ and $J$. This fit produces $z'=5$, $X_{\text{c}}=36.0$ and $J=0.50$ meV as shown by the solid line, which is in rather good agreement with the observed result. However, we should not accept this result as true for the following reasons: $z'$ thus obtained is smaller than the number of nearest neighbor Gd atoms $z=6^{29)}$ and the value of $J$ is too large to explain the observed $D$ and $T_{\text{p}}$ from eqs. (8) and (10) (see Table I). Therefore, the concentration dependence of $T_{\text{C}}$ cannot be interpreted in terms of the percolation theory.

The other approach to the problem is to apply the expression of critical behavior as

$$
T_{\text{C}}=A(X-X_{\text{c}})^b, \tag{13}
$$

where $A$ is a constant and $b$ is a critical exponent. The solid line in Fig. 1 represents the best fit to eq. (13) which gives $A=24.7$, $b=0.506$ and $X_{\text{c}}=36.5$. Excellent agreement with the observed result is a reliable evidence to support eq. (13). As shown in Fig. 1, we have found that ferromagnetism occurs at $X=38$ and that the cluster glass is a real phase at low temperatures, so that we should regard

the value of $X_{\mathrm{c}}=36.5$ as an apparent critical concentration of ferromagnetism assuming that ferromagnetism were realized in the low temperature region. It is noted that $b=0.506$ is very nearly equal to the value of $1 / 2$ obtained in the mean field approximation. Another important result is that $T_{\mathrm{C}}=202 \mathrm{~K}$ is estimated for amorphous Gd from eq. (13) if it is valid in all region of $X$. Hauser $^{36)}$ and Poon and Durand $^{17)}$ have estimated the $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ value of about $250 \mathrm{~K}$ for amorphous Gd assuming a linear dependence of $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ on concentration. ## §7. Conclusion

We have carried out magnetization measurements for the amorphous $\mathrm{Gd}-\mathrm{Y}-\mathrm{Cu}$ system as well as for the amorphous $\mathrm{Gd}_{40} \mathrm{Ag}_{60}$ alloy. The former shows a variety of magnetism: ferromagnetism $(X \simeq 68)$, the ferromagnetism to cluster glass transition $(30<X<65)$ and the cluster glass $(X<38)$. On the other hand, the latter is ferromagnetic as reported previously. $^{14)}$ All the present alloys obey the CurieWeiss law with positive $T_{\mathrm{p}}$.

Temperature variation of the magnetization of the present ferromagnet is interpreted well in terms of the simple $T^{3 / 2}$ law with correction of the applied field. Such behavior has been observed in the cluster glass phase as well. The value of $D$ thus obtained is independent of the applied field in contrast to the theory of Kaneyoshi. $^{7)}$

We have found that $D$ correlates linearly with $T_{\mathrm{p}}$ in the Gd-based alloys and that $D$ of the amorphous alloy is smaller than that of the crystalline counterpart. These results can be explained quantitatively in terms of the nearest neighbor Heisenberg model with fluctuations in the exchange interactions.

Concentration dependence of $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ for the amorphous $\mathrm{Gd}-\mathrm{Y}-\mathrm{Cu}$ system is represented well by the expression of critical behavior with $X_{\mathrm{c}}=36.5$ and $b=0.506$ rather than by the percolation theory.

We have estimated the magnetic characteristics of amorphous Gd: $g S=7.57$, $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=10.1, D=17.1 \mathrm{meV} \AA^{2}, T_{\mathrm{C}}=202 \mathrm{~K}, T_{\mathrm{p}}=$ $219 \mathrm{~K}$ and $J=0.299 \mathrm{meV}$.
