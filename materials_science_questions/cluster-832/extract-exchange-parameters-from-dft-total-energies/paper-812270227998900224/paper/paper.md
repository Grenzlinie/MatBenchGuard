# The Curie Temperature of the Two-Dimensional Quadratic Ising Ferromagnet with Mixed Spins of $S=1/2$ and $S=1$

Takashi IWASHITA and Norikiyo URYÛ†

College of General Education, Kyushu Tokai University, Kumamoto 862
†Department of Applied Science, Faculty of Engineering,
Kyushu University, Fukuoka 812

(Received March 11, 1983)

For the two-dimensional quadratic Ising ferromagnet with mixed spins of $S=1/2$ and $S=1$, the Curie temperature $T_{c}$ has been calculated with the use of Bethe-Peierls theory. Assuming the bilinear exchange interactions $J_{1}$ between nearest neighbors (n.n.) of the different spins, $J_{2}$ and $J_{3}$ between two kinds of next nearest neighbors (n.n.n.) of the same spins, and the biquadratic exchange $J_{1}'$ between n.n. and $J_{2}'$ between n.n.n. of spins $S=1$, the behaviors of $T_{c}$ as a function of these exchange parameters are investigated. The ferromagnetic order is shown to be unstable when the ratio $J_{1}'/J_{1}$ amounts to $-1.85$. It is also pointed out that $J_{1}'$ and $J_{2}'$ give a qualitatively different effect on $T_{c}$. Furthermore, it turns out that the exchange interaction $J_{2}$ between n.n.n. of spins $S=1$ gives more significant effect on $T_{c}$ than $J_{3}$ between n.n.n. of spins $S=1/2$.

## §1. Introduction
The biquadratic exchange interaction has been pointed out to give significant effects on the Curie temperature and other magnetic properties of ferromagnets and antiferromagnets. The need for a small negative biquadratic exchange term was first discussed by Harris and Owen $^{1)}$ and Rodbell et al. $^{2,3)}$ to explain their data on paramagnetic resonance of Mn spin pairs in MgO. Afterwards, Birgeneau et al. $^{4)}$ have made clear that the biquadratic exchange interaction is important for certain rare-earth compounds with incompletely quenched orbital angular momentum. Furthermore, Allan and Betts, $^{5)}$ Chen and Levy, $^{6,7)}$ and Brown $^{8,9)}$ have investigated the effects of the biquadratic exchange interaction on the Curie temperature, magnetic susceptibility, specific heat and magnetization of the Heisenberg ferromagnet by means of a high temperature expansion and a constant coupling approximation. The theoretical explanation for the origin of this interaction was given by Anderson, $^{10)}$ Huang and Orbach, $^{11)}$ Kittel $^{12)}$ and Iwashita and Uryû. $^{13)}$ Recently, we have also revealed that in either a ferromagnet or an antiferromagnet the biquadratic exchange interaction gives significant effects on the temperature dependence of the specific heat and the magnetization reflecting an abrupt change of the spin structure at the critical ratio of the biquadratic to the conventional bilinear exchange interaction. $^{14,15)}$ Furthermore, we have shown that the biquadratic exchange interaction affects largely the Curie temperature of the anisotropic Heisenberg ferromagnet. $^{16)}$

In all of these previous investigations, the spins have been confined to be of the same value in the spin system. Therefore, it may be interesting to extend the calculation to the Ising ferromagnetic system with mixed spins to investigate the effects of the biquadratic exchange interaction between spins of different values on the Curie temperature.

In the present paper, the effects of the biquadratic exchange interactions between nearest neighbors (n.n.) and between next nearest neighbors (n.n.n.) on the Curie temperature of two-dimensional quadratic Ising ferromagnet with mixed spins of $S=1/2$ and $S=1$ as shown by Fig. 1(A) are studied with the use of the Bethe-Peierls method. $^{17)}$ At the same time, the effects of two kinds of the bilinear exchange interactions between n.n.n. spins on the Curie temperature are investigated

## §2. Theory
Let us consider a mixed ferromagnetic Ising spin system with $S=1/2$ and $S=1$ on the two-dimensional quadratic lattice ($z=4$) as shown by Fig. 1(A). In the spin system, we assume three kinds of the bilinear exchange interactions $J_{i}$ and two kinds of the biquadratic exchange interactions $J_{i}^{\prime}$. Then the spin Hamiltonian for the present spin system can be written as follows:

$$
\mathscr{H}=-2 J_{1} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle} S_{i z} \sigma_{j z}-2 J_{2} \sum_{\langle i k\rangle} S_{i z} S_{k z}-2 J_{3} \sum_{\langle j l\rangle} \sigma_{j z} \sigma_{l z}-2 J_{1}^{\prime} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle} S_{i z}^{2} \sigma_{j z}^{2}-2 J_{2}^{\prime} \sum_{\langle i k\rangle} S_{i z}^{2} S_{k z}^{2}, \quad (1)
$$

where $S=1$ and $\sigma=1/2$, and $J_{1}$ represents the bilinear exchange interaction between n.n. spins and $J_{2}$ represents the one between n.n.n. spins $S$ and $J_{3}$ represents the one between n.n.n. spins $\sigma$. Furthermore, $J_{1}^{\prime}$ and $J_{2}^{\prime}$ represent the biquadratic exchange interactions between n.n. spins and between n.n.n. spins $S$ as shown in Fig. 2. The biquadratic exchange interaction $J_{3}^{\prime} \sigma_{j z}^{2} \sigma_{l z}^{2}$ between $\sigma$ spins was not introduced into the Hamiltonian (1) as such interaction can be reduced to a bilinear exchange interaction $J_{3} \sigma_{j z} \sigma_{l z}$.

Following the Bethe-Peierls method, $^{17)}$ the Hamiltonian for the cluster of the spin $S_{0}$ and its nearest neighboring spins $\sigma_{i}$ as shown by Fig. 2(A) is approximated as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr{H}_{i}= & -2\left(J_{1} S_{0 z}+3 J_{1} \lambda_{1}+2 J_{3} \mu_{1}\right) \sum_{i=1}^{4} \sigma_{i z}-2\left(J_{1}^{\prime} S_{0 z}^{2}+3 J_{1}^{\prime} \lambda_{2}\right) \sum_{i=1}^{4} \sigma_{i z}^{2} \\
& -2 J_{3} \sum_{\left\langle i i^{\prime}\right\rangle}^{4} \sigma_{i z} \sigma_{i^{\prime} z}-8\left(J_{2} \lambda_{1} S_{0 z}+J_{2}^{\prime} \lambda_{2} S_{0 z}^{2}\right),
\end{aligned} \quad (2)
$$

where $\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}$ and $\mu_{1}$ are parameters of the molecular field due to a neighboring spin. The parameters $\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}$ and $\mu_{1}$ are proportional to the values of $\langle S_{z}\rangle,\langle S_{z}^{2}\rangle$ and $\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle$, respectively, and are determined from the self-consistent condition.

On the other hand, the Hamiltonian for the cluster of the spin $\sigma_{0}$ and its nearest neighboring spins $S_{j}$ as shown by Fig. 2(B) can be approximated as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr{H}_{j}= & -2\left(J_{1} \sigma_{0 z}+3 J_{1} \mu_{1}+2 J_{2} \lambda_{1}\right) \sum_{j=1}^{4} S_{j z}-2\left(J_{1}^{\prime} \sigma_{0 z}^{2}+3 J_{1}^{\prime} \mu_{2}+2 J_{2}^{\prime} \lambda_{2}\right) \sum_{j=1}^{4} S_{j z}^{2} \\
& -2 J_{2} \sum_{\left\langle j j^{\prime}\right\rangle}^{4} S_{j z} S_{j^{\prime} z}-2 J_{2}^{\prime} \sum_{\left\langle j j^{\prime}\right\rangle}^{4} S_{j z}^{2} S_{j^{\prime} z}^{2}-8 J_{3} \mu_{1} \sigma_{0 z},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mu_{2}$ is a parameter and is proportional to the value of $\left\langle\sigma_{z}^{2}\right\rangle$.

For convenience's sake, $2 \sigma_{i z}$ in the cluster Hamiltonians is replaced with $\rho_{i z}$. Then, the partition functions for the Hamiltonians given by eqs. (2) and (3) can be written as

$$
Z_{i}=\sum_{S_{0}= \pm 1,0} \sum_{\rho_{1}= \pm 1} \cdots \sum_{\rho_{4}= \pm 1} \exp \left[-\mathscr{H}_{i} / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\right],
$$

$$
Z_{j}=\sum_{\rho 0= \pm 1} \sum_{S_{1}= \pm 1,0} \cdots \sum_{S_{4}= \pm 1,0} \exp \left[-\mathscr{H}_{j} / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\right].
$$

The thermal averages of the spins $S_{0}$ and $\rho_{i}$ are calculated from the partition function (4) as

$$
\left\langle S_{0 z}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{i}} \sum_{i=1}^{12} f_{i} \mathrm{e}^{x(i)},
$$

$$
\left\langle\rho_{i z}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{i}} \sum_{i=1}^{12} g_{i} \mathrm{e}^{y(i)}.
$$

Also the thermal averages of the spins $\rho_{0}$ and $S_{j}$ are obtained from the partition function (5) as follows:

$$
\left\langle\rho_{0 z}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{j}} \sum_{j=1}^{42} l_{j} \mathrm{e}^{z(j)},
$$

$$
\left\langle S_{j z}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{j}} \sum_{j=1}^{32} m_{j} \mathrm{e}^{w(j)}.
$$

In these formulae, $f_{i}, g_{i}, l_{j}$ and $m_{j}$ denote appropriate coefficients, and $x(i), y(i), z(j)$ and $w(j)$ are given as functions of $K_{1}, K_{2}, K_{3}, K_{1}', K_{2}', \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \mu_{1}, \mu_{2}$. Here the parameters $K_{1}, K_{2}, K_{3}, K_{1}'$ and $K_{2}'$ are defined as

$$
K_{1}=J_{1} / k_{\mathrm{B}} T, \quad K_{2}=J_{2} / k_{\mathrm{B}} T, \quad K_{3}=J_{3} / k_{\mathrm{B}} T, \quad K_{1}'=J_{1}' / k_{\mathrm{B}} T, \quad K_{2}'=J_{2}' / k_{\mathrm{B}} T. \tag{10}
$$

The conditions

$$
\left\langle S_{0 z}\right\rangle=\left\langle S_{j z}\right\rangle=\left\langle S_{z}\right\rangle, \tag{11}
$$

$$
\left\langle\rho_{i z}\right\rangle=\left\langle\rho_{0 z}\right\rangle=\left\langle\rho_{z}\right\rangle, \tag{12}
$$

are the self-consistent relations to determine the values of $\left\langle S_{z}\right\rangle$ and $\left\langle\rho_{z}\right\rangle$, respectively. The Curie temperature is obtained from eqs. (11) and (12) for vanishing values of $\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \mu_{1}$ and $\mu_{2}$ and is given as a solution of the following simultaneous equations:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{22}\left\{\left(a_{i} K_{1}+b_{i} K_{2}\right)+\left(c_{i} K_{1}+d_{i} K_{3}\right) \mu_{1} / \lambda_{1}\right\} \exp \left(p_{i} K_{1}+q_{i} K_{2}+r_{i} K_{3}+u_{i} K_{1}'+v_{i} K_{2}'\right)=0, \tag{13}
$$

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{27}\left\{\left(a_{j} K_{1}+b_{j} K_{2}\right)+\left(c_{j} K_{1}+d_{j} K_{3}\right) \mu_{1} / \lambda_{1}\right\} \exp \left(p_{j} K_{1}+q_{j} K_{2}+r_{j} K_{3}+u_{j} K_{1}'+v_{j} K_{2}'\right)=0, \tag{14}
$$

where $a_{i}, b_{i}, \cdots, v_{i} ; a_{j}, b_{j}, \cdots, v_{j}$ are appropriate coefficients and are given in Tables I and II, respectively. At the same time, the value of $\mu_{1} / \lambda_{1}$ is also determined from these equations.

The Curie temperature has been calculated numerically from eqs. (13) and (14) for both cases of positive and negative values of $J_{1}', J_{2}'$,

$J_2$ and $J_3$. Firstly, the $J_1'$-dependence of the reduced Curie temperature $k_{\rm B}T_c/J_1$ has been calculated in the range of $J_1'=-2.0J_1\sim 2.0J_1$. In this case, the values of $J_2'$, $J_2$ and $J_3$ were taken to be zero. The $J_1'$-dependence of $k_{\rm B}T_c/J_1$ is shown by the curve (a) in Fig. 3. Now, let us denote the Curie temperature with only one parameter $J_1$ as $T_c^0$. The $J_1'$-dependence of the ratio $T_c/T_c^0$ has also been calculated and is shown by the curve (a) in Fig. 4. It turns out from these figures that the $J_1'$-dependence of $T_c$ is much larger in the range of $J_1'<0$ than in the range of $J_1'>0$. As can be seen from the figures, the inclination of the $J_1'$-dependence of $T_c$ is positive.
