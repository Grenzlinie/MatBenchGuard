# $[Cu(HF_2)_2(pyrazine)]_n$: A Rectangular Antiferromagnetic Lattice with a Spin Exchange Path Made Up of Two Different $FHF^-$ Bridges**

Jamie L. Manson,* Michelle L. Warter, John A. Schlueter, Tom Lancaster, Andrew J. Steele, Stephen J. Blundell, Francis L. Pratt, John Singleton, Ross D. McDonald, Changhoon Lee, Myung-Hwan Whangbo, and Alex Plonczak

Very little is known about the ligand properties of the bifluoride ion $HF_2^-$ (i.e., $FHF^-$) in various chemical media including aqueous solution and the solid state. The $H\cdots F$ bonds of $HF_2^-$ are the strongest known hydrogen bonds (bond enthalpy of ca. $177\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$),$^{[1,2]}$ but the ability of $FHF^-$ to mediate magnetic interactions is not well understood. Various diamagnetic salts of $HF_2^-$ ions have been structurally characterized.$^{[3-8]}$ The only known paramagnetic compounds containing $HF_2^-$ ions are the $[pipzH_2][MnF_4(HF_2)]$ (pipz = piperazine)$^{[9]}$ and $[Cu(HF_2)(pyz)_2]X$ ($X=\text{BF}_4^-$,$^{[10]}$ $\text{ClO}_4^-$, $\text{PF}_6^-$,$^{[11]}$ $\text{AsF}_6^-$, $\text{SbF}_6^-$,$^{[12]}$ pyz = pyrazine) coordination polymers.$^{[10-12]}$ $[pipzH_2][MnF_4(HF_2)]$, with angular Mn-FHF-Mn bridges (Scheme 1a), exhibits negligible magnetic coupling,

while $[Cu(HF_2)(pyz)_2]X$ with linear bridging $\mu_{1,3}$-$HF_2^-$ ligands (Scheme 1b) shows long-range magnetic order (LRO) between 1.54 and $4.3\ \text{K}$ for $X=\text{BF}_4^-$ and $\text{SbF}_6^-$, respectively.$^{[10,12]}$ Our efforts to utilize $HF_2^-$ as a synthon much like $\text{CN}^-$, $\text{N}_3^-$, and NCS-$^{[13-15]}$ have now led to the synthesis of a two-dimensional (2D) rectangular coordination polymer, namely, $[Cu(HF_2)_2(pyz)]_n$, in which the adjacent $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions are linked by a pyz ligand in one direction, and by two $HF_2^-$ ions with two different bridging modes ($\mu_{1,1}$ and $\mu_{1,3}$ modes, Scheme 1c) in the other direction (Figure 1a). We establish that the $\mu_{1,1}$-mode $HF_2^-$ ion is close in nature to $\text{F}^-\cdots\text{HF}$, and that the magnetic properties of $[Cu(HF_2)_2(pyz)]_n$ are described by a 2D rectangular antiferromagnetic (AFM) lattice with a weaker spin exchange through the pyz bridge.

The structure of $[Cu(HF_2)_2(pyz)]_n$ was determined by single-crystal X-ray diffraction at $150\ \text{K}$. Each $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ion lies at the inversion center of an axially compressed $\text{CuF}_4\text{N}_2$ octahedron with Cu-F1 2.119(3), Cu-F2 2.137(1), and Cu-N 2.003(3) Å (Figure 1a). Very little distortion from ideal octahedral bond angles was observed. Like donor atoms that occupy trans positions around the Cu ion (i.e., N-Cu-N, F1-Cu-F1, and F2-Cu-F2) form $180^\circ$ bond angles. Mirror planes that contain F2, F3, H1, and H2 lie halfway between successive $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions along the $c$ axis and are parallel to the Cu-pyz-Cu chain axis.

Two unique bifluoride bridging modes (Scheme 1c and Figure 1) exist in $[Cu(HF_2)_2(pyz)]_n$, one of which forms a $\mu_{1,3}$-bridge with symmetric $H\cdots F$ bonds (H1-F1 $1.156(4)\ \text{Å}$), where F1$\cdots$H1$\cdots$F1A may be slightly bent $(177.4^\circ)$. The other $HF_2^-$ ion features a unique $\mu_{1,1}$-bridging mode and has asymmetric $H\cdots F$ bonds (H2-F3 0.91(3), H2-F2 $1.48(3)\ \text{Å}$). Furthermore, IR spectroscopy also revealed no O--H bend (near $1600\ cm^{-1}$).$^{[16]}$ Hence, the $\mu_{1,1}$-$HF_{2}^{-}$ ligand may be more accurately described as an F---HF moiety.

Each $CuF_{4}N_{2}$ octahedron in $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}(pyz)]_{n}$ is linked to two others through bridging $HF_{2}^{-}$ ions along the $c$ direction to yield one-dimensional (1D) linear chains (Cu---Cu 3.933(1) Å). These chains are connected by pyz ligands along the $a$ axis (Cu---Cu 6.7774(9) Å) to form 2D rectangular sheets (Figure 1b). The sheets pack in registry along the $b$ axis, with an interlayer $C-H_{pyz}\cdots F1$ contact of 2.61 Å. Along the $a$ axis, however, the sheets are staggered by one-half of a unit-cell translation to minimize steric interactions between pyrazine H atoms. The neutral $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}]_{n}$ chains in $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}(pyz)]_{n}$ resemble the motifs found in the 1D polymers $NMe_{4}[Cu(N_{3})_{3}]^{[14a]}$ and $[Cu(N_{3})_{2}(3-Clpy)_{2}]^{[14b]}$ in that they each have $\mu_{1,1}$ and $\mu_{1,3}$ bridging modes of the $N_{3}^{-}$ ligands. The more flexible H---F bonds and variable disposition of the $F^{-}$ lone pairs in $HF_{2}^{-}$ apparently facilitate the formation of planar pentagonal rings in $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}(pyz)]_{n}$.

The magnetic properties of $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}(pyz)]_{n}$ were explored by SQUID magnetometry, pulsed-field magnetization, and muon-spin relaxation ($\mu^{+}$SR). A broad maximum in $\chi$ versus $T$ (Figure 2a) was observed at 5.2 K, which is indicative of low-dimensional spin correlations, likely within the 2D layers. Below $T_{max}$, $\chi(T)$ decreases continually as the temperature is lowered and there are no artefacts suggestive of a transition to long-range magnetic order (LRO) above 2 K. The spin correlations between $Cu^{2+}$ ions are antiferromagnetic (AFM), as demonstrated by the temperature dependence of $\chi T$ versus $T$. At 300 K, $\chi(T)T$ has a value of $0.418\ emu\ Kmol^{-1}$, which exceeds the spin-only value of $0.375\ emu\ Kmol^{-1}$ expected for an isotropic spin-1/2 ion. This discrepancy is attributed to the higher $g$ value typically observed for $Cu^{2+}$ ions. Upon cooling, $\chi(T)T$ remains largely invariant until about 50 K, where a rapid decrease ensues to reach a minimum value of $0.057\ emu\ Kmol^{-1}$ at 2 K. The AFM nature of the spin exchange interactions were confirmed by a Curie-Weiss fit of $1/\chi$ versus $T$ ($30\leq T\leq 300$ K) leading to $g=2.147(1)$ and $\theta=-2.0(1)$ K.

In each 2D layer of $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}(pyz)]_{n}$, the adjacent $Cu^{2+}$ ions are linked by pyz in one direction and by $\mu_{1,1}$- and $\mu_{1,3^{-}}$ FHF$^{-}$ bridges in the orthogonal direction. Thus, the magnetic properties of $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}(pyz)]_{n}$ may be described by a spin Hamiltonian $H=-J_{ij}\sum S_{i}S_{j}$, where $J_{ij}$ refers to the $J_{FHF}$ and $J_{pyz}$ exchanges that define the spin lattice. A least-squares fit of the $\chi$ versus $T$ data ($3\leq T\leq 300$ K) leads to the green curve in Figure 2a and the parameters $C=0.4269(1)\ emu\ Kmol^{-1}$, $J_{FHF}/k_{B}=-10.099(6)$ K, and $\alpha=J_{pyz}/J_{FHF}=0.121(1)$ assuming a rectangular lattice model.$^{[17]}$ An equally good fit (red curve)

could be achieved for the parameters $C=0.4185(1)\mathrm{emuK}$ $\mathrm{mol}^{-1}$, $J_{\mathrm{FHF}}/k_{\mathrm{B}}=-8.13(1)\ \mathrm{K}$, and $zJ_{\mathrm{pyz}}/k_{\mathrm{B}}=-1.80(2)\ \mathrm{K}$ [for $z=2$, $J_{\mathrm{pyz}}/k_{\mathrm{B}}=-0.90(2)\ \mathrm{K}$, and $\alpha=0.11(1)$] by using a 1D Heisenberg linear-chain model that included a molecular- field contribution. $^{[18]}$ By comparison, assuming only isolated magnetic chain behavior (i.e., neglecting all possible inter- chain couplings) the theoretical fit underestimates the experimental curve, as demonstrated by the blue line in Figure 2a. Thus, an additional exchange parameter is needed to fit the data in the vicinity of the maximum.

The inset plot of Figure 2a shows the isothermal magnet- ization of $[Cu(HF_{2})_{2}(pyz)]_{n}$ obtained by using a pulsed-field magnet at $T=0.5\ \mathrm{K}$. As the field increases, the magnetization rises smoothly but exhibits a concave behavior typical of $S=$ 1/2 low-dimensional antiferromagnets. $^{[19]}$ An elbow occurs at a critical field $B_{\mathrm{c}}$ of $12.9\mathrm{T}$, and this feature can be used to estimate $J_{\mathrm{pyz}}$ on the basis of the principle that $B_{\mathrm{c}}=$ $2k_{\mathrm{B}}(J_{\mathrm{FHF}}+J_{\mathrm{pyz}})/g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$. This expression affords $|J_{\mathrm{pyz}}/k_{\mathrm{B}}|=$ $1.15\ \mathrm{K}$ when $|J_{\mathrm{FHF}}/k_{\mathrm{B}}|=8.13\ \mathrm{K}$, which is similar to the value obtained from the susceptibility fit. Hence, a 1D model that incorporates substantial interchain coupling seems most appropriate based on the experimental data.

A zero-field (ZF) muon-spin relaxation asymmetry spec- trum, measured at $0.02\ \mathrm{K}$ is shown in Figure 2b. Across the entire temperature range $(0.02\leq T\leq0.9\ \mathrm{K})$, the spectra display a fast- and a slow-relaxing component described by relaxation rates $\lambda_{1}$ and $\lambda_{2}$, respectively. No oscillation (characteristic of long-range magnetic order) is observed in the muon asymmetry down to as low as $0.02\ \mathrm{K}$. The data are best fitted with a function of the form [Eq. (1)]

$$
A(t)=A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{1}\mathfrak{t})+A_{2}\exp(-\lambda_{2}\mathfrak{t})+A_{\mathrm{bg}} \tag{1}
$$

where $A_{\mathrm{bg}}$ is a constant background offset term, accounting for those muons which stop in the silver sample holder or cryostat tail. At $0.02\ \mathrm{K}$ the fitted values in Equation (1) take the values $A_{1}=13\%,\ \lambda_{1}=35\ \mathrm{MHz},\ A_{2}=5.1\%$, and $\lambda_{2}=$ $0.6\ \mathrm{MHz}$. There is very little variation in the fitted parameters across the measured temperature range. We note that exponential relaxation functions often arise as a result of relaxation due to dynamic fluctuations in the local magnetic field at the muon site. $^{[20]}$ The fact that there is no evidence for long-range magnetic order above $0.02\ \mathrm{K}$ implies that the interlayer exchange interactions are extremely weak or there is more disorder in the local field distribution than in related systems that act to suppress the oscillatory response of $A(t)$.

To identify which exchange path-Cu-pyz-Cu or Cu- $(FHF)_{2}$-Cu-provides the stronger spin exchange and find the relative importance of the $\mu_{1,1^{-}}$ and $\mu_{1,3^{-}}\mathrm{FHF}^{-}$ bridges in the latter exchange path, we evaluated the Cu-pyz-Cu and Cu- $(FHF)_{2}$-Cu spin exchanges (designated $J_{\mathrm{pyz}}$ and $J_{\mathrm{FHF}}$, respec- tively, as before) by performing a mapping analysis based on first-principles density functional calculations. $^{[21]}$ Our calcu lations employed the frozen-core projector augmented wave method encoded in the Vienna ab initio simulation pack- ages, $^{[22]}$ and the generalized-gradient approximation $(\mathrm{GGA})^{[23]}$ with a plane-wave cutoff energy of $400\ \mathrm{eV}$ and a set of 32 k-points for the irreducible Brillouin zone. To describe the strong electron correlation in the Cu 3d states, we used the GGA plus on-site repulsion method (GGA + U) $^{[24]}$ was used with $U_{\text{eff}}$ values of 5, 6, and 7 eV.

## Experimental Section
Synthesis: In a typical preparation of $[Cu(HF_2)_2(pyz)]_n$, 10 mL of aqueous HF solution (48 wt %) containing $Cu(CH_3CO_2)_2\cdot2H_2O$ (0.3000 g, 1.50 mmol) was mixed with a 10 mL of aqueous HF solution containing $NH_4HF_2$ (0.0900 g, 1.50 mmol) and pyrazine (0.2410 g, 3.00 mmol) to afford a pale blue solution. Upon standing at room temperature for about 1 month, medium blue rods of [Cu- $(HF_2)_2(pyz)]_n$ were obtained in low yield (<20 % based on Cu). The material is stable in air for a short period of time but was typically stored in the mother liquor in plastic vials.
