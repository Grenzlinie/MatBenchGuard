# Theoretical Studies of Organometallic Conjugated Systems: Prussian Blue Analogs

M. Nishino⁸, S. Takeda⁸ᵇ, W. Moriᶜ, A. Nakamuraᵈ, K. Yamaguchi⁸

⁸Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science, Osaka University, Toyonaka, Osaka 560, Japan

## Abstract
The symmetry rules were applied for the d-d orbital interactions between transition metal ions in Prussian blue analogs. The sign of the effective exchange integrals was consistent with the experiments available. The ab initio UHF and DFT calculations were carried out for the Cr(III)-CN-V(II) and Cr(III)-CN-Ni(II) units to elucidate the nature of the magnetic orbitals. The magnitude of the calculated effective exchange integrals was compatible with the experimental values.

## 1. Introduction
Molecule-based magnets exhibit several spin-related phenomena such as spin alignment, spin gap, spin cross over, spin frustration and spin dynamics. Active controls of these spin properties by chemical and physical techniques have been investigated extensively, and a new field, "molecular spinics", is now developing rapidly. From the view point of the molecular spinics, Prussian blue type magnetic materials, $A_mM_n[M'(CN)_6]_l \cdot (H_2O)_y$, are particularly interesting because of several reasons; high transition temperature ($T_c$), varieties of transition metal combinations (M, M'), tunable magnetism, etc. As a first step of our theoretical approaches to these materials, the symmetry rules between magnetic d-orbitals are considered for simple prediction of the sign of the effective exchange integrals ($J_{ab}$) between the transition metal ions. The ab initio calculations of the simplest model systems, $M-C\equiv N-M'$, for several Prussian blue compounds are also carried out to elucidate the through-bond interactions between M and M'.

## 2. The symmetry rules
Table 1 summarizes the electronic configurations of the component transition metal ions and their combinations. According to the orbital symmetry rules for the superexchange interactions, the non zero orbital-overlap (OO) integral between the $t_{2g}$ (or $e_g$) orbitals provides the antiferromagnetic effective exchange interaction ($J_{ab}<0$). While the zero-overlap integral between the orthogonal $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ orbitals gives rise to the ferromagnetic exchange interaction ($J_{ab}>0$). In fact, the antiferro-, ferri-, or ferro-magnetic behaviors observed for the Prussian blue compounds are well rationalized with these simple rules as shown in Table 1. However, the so-called double exchange mechanism leading to the ferromagnetic exchange interaction plays an important role if the occupied d-orbital level of M becomes very close to that of a vacant d-orbital of M'. Therefore, active controls of the effective exchange interactions should be feasible by changing the d-electron configurations and d-orbital energy levels with chemical and physical techniques.

## 3. Ab initio calculations and discussions
The ab initio unrestricted Hartree-Fock (UHF) and density functional (DFT) calculations by the use of the triple zeta (TZ) basis sets were carried out for the simplest model systems, $M-C\equiv N-M'$ (M=Cr(III), M'=V(II), V(III), Cr(III), Mn (II), Ni(II)). As shown previously[1-4], the effective exchange integrals ($J_{ab}$) were calculated from the difference between the total energies obtained for the lowest spin (LS) and the highest spin (HS) states by the UHF and DFT/TZ methods. Table 2 summarizes the calculated $J_{ab}$ values. From Table 2, it is clear that their signs are consistent with the prediction by the symmetry rule (see Table 1). For examples, the Cr(III)-V(II) (or V(III)) interaction is antiferromagnetic because of the $t_{2g}$ orbital interaction but the Cr(III)-Ni(II) is ferromagnetic because of the orthogonality between the $t_{2g}$-and $e_g$-orbitals. However, the sign of $J_{ab}$ for the Cr(III)-Mn(II) pair is negative (antiferromagnetic) by UHF, whereas it becomes positive (ferromagnetic) by DFT. Then the superexchange and double exchange mechanisms are, respectively, predominant for the UHF and DFT calculations. This in turn indicates that the signs of $J_{ab}$ should be sensitive to environmental effects in the real Cr(III)-Mn(II) systems. Experimentally, the $J_{ab}$ value for these species is about $-16\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, supporting the UHF calculation.

Figure 1 shows (A) the magnetic $t_{2g}$-orbital pair for the Cr(III)-$C\equiv N$-V(II) system and (B) the $t_{2g}$-$e_g$ orbital pair for the Cr(III)-$C\equiv N$-Ni(II) system. The d$\pi$-orbital of V(II) interacts with the $\pi$-orbital of the CN group. On the other hand, the d$\pi$-orbital of Cr(III) or Ni(II) is localized essentially on the atomic site. The calculated results are consistent with the experiments[5-9].
