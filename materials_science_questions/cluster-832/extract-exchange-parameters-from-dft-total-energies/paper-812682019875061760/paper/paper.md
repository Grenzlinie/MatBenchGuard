The nature of the spin exchange interaction within the half-filled band of one-dimensional stacks of 1-D mixed molecular radical crystals (MMRC) with the general formula: $\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$PAH$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$R$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$PAH$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$R$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$ PAH$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$R$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$PAH$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$R$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$(R•, radical; PAH, diamagnetic polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon) was investigated theoretically. The various contributions to the Heisenberg effective exchange integral, the direct (Coulomb), kinetic, and indirect spin exchange for the MMRCs, are evaluated quantitatively, using band theory, and are compared with those of the corresponding parent molecular radical crystals (MRC, without hydrocarbons). The nature and the magnitude of the exchange interaction in MMRCs are significantly different in comparison with the parent MRCs: the ferromagnetic interaction in MRC changes into antiferromagnetic in MMRCs and vice versa.

## 1. Introduction
The relation between the geometry, the topology, and the nature of the *intramolecular* exchange interaction of the electrons within the half-filled band (HFB) in pure organic one-dimensional (1-D) oligomeric and polymeric $\pi$-electron systems is well investigated experimentally and theoretically.$^{1-4}$ There is a large group of quasi 1-D $\pi$-systems, namely, molecular radical crystals (MRC), whose elementary units (EU) consist of weakly interacting monoradicals with delocalized $\pi$-electrons.$^{4-7}$ The nature of the spin exchange interaction in such $\pi$-systems has been studied theoretically (in the framework of MO theory) in the papers of Yamaguchi et al.,$^{8-11}$ Kollmar and Kahn,$^{12}$ and Hoffmann and Yoshizawa.$^{7,13}$ In all these papers, the intermolecular magnetic interaction was studied for dimers and clusters of simple model radicals but not for infinite MRCs.

In our earlier papers,$^{14-16}$ band theory is applied to study the nature of the *intermolecular* spin exchange interaction in 1-D MRCs consisting of alternant *homonuclear* model radicals$^{14}$ and *heteronuclear* radicals, namely, the 1-D staple polymers of Wurster's red bromide ($N,N$-dimethyl-$p$-phenylenediamine bromide)$^{15}$ and galvinoxyl$^{16}$ were studied. In all the above cited papers$^{14-16}$ the intermolecular magnetic interaction was studied for pure MRCs, that is, for 1-D systems that consist of only one type of monoradical $\mathbf{R^\bullet}$.

The aim of the present paper is to extend the investigation of the nature (ferromagnetic (FM) or antiferromagnetic (AFM)) and magnitude of magnetic interactions to 1-D (1:1) mixed MRCs (MMRC). The general structure of MMRCs is:
$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$PAH$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$R$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$PAH$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$R$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$PAH$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$R$\boldsymbol{\cdots\cdots}$

where $\mathbf{R^\bullet}$ is a $\pi$-radical, and **PAH** is a diamagnetic alternant polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH).

In only the paper of Awaga et al.$^{17}$ was the magnetic interaction between galvinoxyl radicals in mixed crystals of galvinoxyl and hydrogalvinoxyl investigated experimentally; however, the crystal structure of the systems is unknown.

In this paper, we consider MMCRs that consist of homonuclear alternant $\pi$-radicals and alternant PAHs. Briefly, one example of an MMRC with a heteronuclear radical of Wurster's type is studied. Here, only the basic principle is presented, using model systems of MRCs and MMRCs independent of their actual structure. ### 4. Methods of Investigation

#### 4.1. Energy Spectra of the 1-D Stacks.
If the MOs of a one-dimensional (1-D) system have the form of Bloch running waves

$$
|\mathbf{k}>=N^{-1/2}\sum_{\mu}\sum_{r}C_{r}(k)\exp(-ik\mu)|r,\mu> \tag{1}
$$

where $k \in [-\pi,\pi]$ is the wave vector, $\mu$ denotes the number of the elementary units (EU), and $|r,\mu>$ is the $r$-th AO within the $\mu$-th EU), in the Hückel-Hubbard$^{28}$ version of the Bloch method the MO energies $e(k)$ are eigenvalues of the energy matrix (see ref 29):

$$
\mathbf{E}(k)=\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{V}\exp(ik)+\mathbf{V}^{+}\exp(-ik) \tag{2}
$$

In eq 2, $\mathbf{E}$ is the energy matrix of the EU, $\mathbf{V}$ is the interaction matrix between neighboring EUs ($\mu$-th and $(\mu + 1)$-th), and $\mathbf{V}^{+}$ is the transposed matrix.

#### 4.2. Spin Exchange of the Electrons in the Half-Filled Band.
Based on Anderson's theory,$^{30}$ it has been shown (see ref 29 and references given therein) that the effective exchange integral $J_{\text{eff}}$ in the Heisenberg Hamiltonian

$$
\mathbf{H}=-\sum_{\nu,\rho}J_{\text{eff}}(\nu,\rho)\mathbf{S}_{\nu}\mathbf{S}_{\rho}=J_{\text{eff}}(|\nu-\rho|)=J_{\text{eff}}(\tau) \tag{3}
$$

($\nu$ and $\rho$ denote elementary units) can be expressed as a sum of three contributions (for the sake of simplicity, the dimensionless distance parameter $\tau$ is omitted):

$$
J_{\text{eff}}=J+J_{\text{kin}}+J_{\text{ind}} \tag{4}
$$

where $J$ is the Coulomb exchange integral between the localized Wannier states within the $i\nu$-th and $j\rho$-th sites, $J_{\text{kin}}$ is the kinetic exchange parameter

$$
J_{\text{kin}}=-2t^{2}/(U_{0}-U_{1})=-2t^{2}/U \tag{5}
$$

representing the antiferromagnetic contribution to the spin exchange. $U_{0}$ and $U_{1}$ are the Coulomb repulsion integrals of two electrons residing in the same Wannier state and occupying adjacent Wannier states, respectively. $U = U_{0} - U_{1}$ is the renormalized Hubbard integral.$^{28}$

$t$ is the transfer (hopping) parameter between adjacent Wannier functions, and is given by $t=<\mu|\boldsymbol{h}(1)|\mu+1>$, where

4.3. Parametrization. The calculations were carried out using a standard set of parameters. $^{14,15,29}$ The intermolecular resonance integrals between two 2p AOs were calculated with Mulliken's formula: $^{32}$ $\beta(R)=\beta_{0}\ (S_{\sigma-\sigma},S_{\pi-\pi})/S(R_{0})$, taking into account the angular dependence of overlap integrals $S$ (calculated with $z_{\text{C}}=3.25$, and $z_{\text{N}}=3.90$). A standard value $\beta_{0}\ (R_{0}=1.40\ \mathring{\text{A}})$ = −2.4 eV was used for the resonance integrals $\beta_{\text{CC}}=\beta_{\text{CN}}$ between the $2\text{p}\pi-2\text{p}\pi$ AOs of the carbon and nitrogen atoms, respectively.

The two-center atomic Coulomb integrals $\gamma_{\text{pq}}$ for the calcula- tion of the various contributions to the effective spin exchange according to eq 4 were evaluated using the Mataga-Nishimoto approximation $^{33}$

$$
\gamma_{\text{pq}}=\mathrm{e}^{2}/(a+R_{\text{pq}})\tag{6}
$$

with $a=2\mathrm{e}^{2}/(\gamma_{\text{pp}}+\gamma_{\text{qq}})$. The following standard values of the one-center Coulomb integrals were used: $^{14,15}$ $\gamma_{\text{CC}}=10.84$ eV, and $\gamma_{\text{NN}}=12.27$ eV.

## 5. Results and Discussion

5.1. MMRCs with Homonuclear Alternant Radicals. All the components of the spin exchange $(J_{\text{kin}},J,J_{\text{ind}})$ decrease rapidly with the distance parameter $\tau$ (see eq 3). With $\tau>1$, the magnetic components are vanishingly small. For this reason, the results for these terms in Tables 1−4 are given only for adjacent EUs, that is, for the value $\tau=1$ of the distance parameter.

The width of the HFB arising from the HOMOs of the radicals depends on the topology and the geometry of the 1-D stacks and is an essential factor for the magnetic properties of the MRCs and MMRC. In the case of full face-to-face structures of the 1-D stacks of MRCs, the CRLH theorem is not valid, because in the stacks a doubling of the EU occurs, that is, $S^{*}-R\neq0$. The NBMOs split into a HFB of a width$^{14}$ (see Figure 4a with MRC with Pe-Pe-A):

$$
\Delta\epsilon=4\beta_{\sigma\sigma}=1.152\ \text{eV}\tag{7}
$$

where $\beta_{\sigma\sigma}=0.288$ eV is the resonance integral between $2\text{p}_{\sigma}-$ $2\text{p}_{\sigma}$ AOs at $R=3.35\ \mathring{\text{A}}$. The wide width of the HFB mainly determines the negative values of the effective exchange integral (of a magnitude of $\sim$ 50−90 meV) and the antiferromagnetic character of the spin exchange, respectively (see Figures 1−3 and Tables 1−3).

In all 1-D alternant stacks of MRCs (see Tables 1−3), the ground state is characterized by weakly ferromagnetically coupled electrons within the HFB of an order of magnitude of $\sim$10−20 meV.

### 5.2. MMRCs with Wurster’s radicals.
The experimental results of the antiferromagnetic interaction of Wurster’s radicals within the infinite 1-D stacks (see ref 8 and references given therein) are in qualitative agreement with the theoretical results (band theory)$^{15}$ and also with the results for dimers estimated by Yamaguchi et al.$^8$ The unusually short interplanar spacing in the 1-D stacks of Wurster’s radicals$^{26}$ affects significantly the splitting of the MO energies. The MO energies $e(k)$ of the HFB are situated in the interval between $-1.014$ eV $\leq e(k) \leq 0.278$ eV, that is, the HFB width is $\Delta\epsilon = 1.292$ eV. The width of the HFB mainly determines the negative value of the effective exchange integral and the antiferromagnetic character of the spin exchange, respectively.

In Figure 5, the considered 1-D stack with Wurster’s radicals separated by coronene hydrocarbons is shown. The numerical results show (Table 4) that the effective exchange integral is positive with a main contribution from the Coulomb exchange. The relatively small HFB width and transfer integral determine the small value of the antiferromagnetic contribution in comparison with the Coulomb exchange. Hence, the electrons within the HFB are ferromagnetically coupled in contrast to the antiferromagnetic spin exchange in the 1-D stacks of Wurster’s radicals.

### Conclusions
In this paper, we studied the basic principle of changing the nature of the magnetic interaction between organic radicals in pure MRCs and in 1:1 MMRCs of radicals and PAHs using model systems. The numerical results obtained should be regarded as a quantitative illustration of qualitative conclusions concerning the nature of the magnetic interaction (FM or AFM) in purely MRCs in comparison with those in MMRCs. In the case of alternant stacks, the topology is the main requirement (condition) that determines the nature of the magnetic interaction of the electrons within the HFB. A qualitative change in the nature and degree of the interaction between the radicals from FM into AFM (and vice versa) takes place upon transition from 1-D MRCs to 1:1 MMRCs in which a given radical has one and the same relative arrangement. The FM interaction between homonuclear radicals in MMRCs increases with the increase in the number of $\pi$-centers of the PAHs that separate the radicals. MMRCs with heteroatomic radicals (e.g., Wurster’s radicals) also show a different nature of the magnetic interaction in MRCs and MMRCs, respectively. The concept and the structural principle of MMRCs is very promising in terms of the possibility for the realization of 1-D high spin systems with intermolecular ferromagnetically coupled electrons and high critical temperatures. Further systematic experimental and theoretical studies are necessary concerning (1) the formation of 1:1 MMRCs formed from stable radicals of Weitz’ and Wurster’s type and extended PAHs, (2) the arrangement and geometry of radicals and PAHs within the MMRCs, (3) the nature of the magnetic interaction within the stacks.

Investigations in this direction are in progress.
