# Classical Molecular Dynamics Simulation of Radon Atom in a Carbon Nanotube

## Problem background
This task studies a classical molecular dynamics system consisting of a single radon atom confined inside a rigid zigzag carbon nanotube in vacuum. The central question is how the kinetic energy, potential energy, instantaneous temperature, and derived thermodynamic properties (molar specific heat, molar entropy variation, efficiency, and the EKIN/TEMP ratio) evolve over time. Such a system could serve as a molecular clock or motor, and understanding its temporal energetic and thermal behaviour is of interest for nanodevice design.

## Approach
The simulation uses classical molecular dynamics with a standard Lennard‑Jones force field for carbon–carbon and radon–carbon interactions. The carbon nanotube consists of 192 carbon atoms, has a length of 23.96 Å and a diameter of 6.04 Å; a single radon atom (radius 1.34 Å) is placed inside and allowed to move. The nanotube is held rigid throughout the simulation. The system is evolved in the microcanonical (NVE) ensemble at an initial temperature of 300 K with a 1 fs timestep for a total of 8 000 ps. No initial cooling is applied. Instantaneous kinetic, potential, and total energies and the instantaneous temperature are recorded every 1 ps. From these raw observables, the molar specific heat, molar entropy variation, instantaneous efficiency (kinetic energy divided by total energy), and the ratio EKIN/TEMP are then computed as a function of time. The entire workflow is implemented using the open‑source LAMMPS molecular dynamics simulator.

## Reproduction target
Your objective is to produce a single CSV file, `simulation_results.csv`, containing the complete time series of raw energies and temperature together with the derived thermodynamic properties for every picosecond of the 8 000 ps simulation. The file must have a header row and 8 000 data rows (one per picosecond), with columns as detailed in the output contract: time_ps, EKIN_kcal_per_mol, EPOT_kcal_per_mol, ETOT_kcal_per_mol, TEMP_K, molar_specific_heat_kcal_per_mol_K, molar_entropy_variation_kcal_per_mol_K, efficiency, and EKIN_over_TEMP_kcal_per_mol_K. The values must reflect the physical system described in the approach and be generated by executing the workflow steps in order.
