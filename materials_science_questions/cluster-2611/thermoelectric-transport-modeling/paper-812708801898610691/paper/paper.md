# Density-of-States Effective Mass of Current Carriers in Compensated $n$-Ge
K. C. Sood and G. S. Verma
Physics Department, Banaras Hindu University, Varanasi-5, India
(Received 20 July 1972)

The density-of-states effective mass of electrons in heavily doped and compensated $n$-type Ge has been calculated from the low-temperature phonon-conductivity data. The obtained results show that at liquid-helium temperatures the screening effect due to current carriers plays an important role in the phonon-electron interaction.

It has been well established${}^{1,2}$ until now that the drastic decrease in the low-temperature lattice thermal conductivity of heavily doped semiconductors (in which the impurity levels merge into the conduction band) comes from the scattering of phonons by free carriers of current. Recently, in the calculation for the lattice thermal conductivity of heavily doped uncompensated $n$-Ge, the authors${}^{3}$ have shown that the electron density-of-states effective mass, appearing in the electron-phonon relaxation rate, ${}^{4}$ changes with the carrier concentration as well as with temperature. It is desirable to investigate this effect also for compensated samples of $n$-Ge. The experimental measurements on the lattice thermal conductivity of compensated $n$-Ge have been taken from Mathur and Pearlman. ${}^{5}$

The present calculations of lattice thermal conductivity are based on the well-known Callaway model. ${}^{6}$ Since we are interested in the temperature range $1.5{-}4$ $^\circ$K, three-phonon scattering processes have been neglected. The relaxation rate for the electron-phonon scattering is given by

$$
\tau_{e p}^{-1}=D T \ln \frac{1+\exp \left(\eta^{*}-N / T-P T x^{2}+\frac{1}{2} x\right)}{1+\exp \left(\eta^{*}-N / T-P T x^{2}-\frac{1}{2} x\right)}. \tag{1}
$$

Here
$$
D=E_{l}^{2} m^{* 2} k_{B} \delta^{3} / 2 \pi \hbar^{4} M v,
$$
$$
N=m^{*} v^{2} / 2 k_{B},
$$
$$
P=k_{B} / 8 m^{*} v^{2},
$$
$$
x=\hbar \omega / k_{B} T,
$$
$\omega$ being the phonon frequency, and $M$ and $\delta^{3}$ the atomic mass and atomic volume, respectively. $v$ is the average phonon velocity and $\eta^{*}=E_{f} / k_{B} T$, where $E_{F}$ is the Fermi energy. $E_{l}$ is the longitudinal deformation potential, depending upon impurity concentration. Therefore we have varied $E_{l}$ with the impurity concentration and assumed it to be an adjustable parameter. $m^{*}$ is the density-of-states effective mass and has been treated as a parameter varying both with temperature and impurity concentration. It may be noticed that the experimental results of the lattice thermal conductivity cannot be explained in the whole temperature range, even if one takes larger variations in $E_{l}$, keeping $m^{*}$ constant. For a theoretical explanation of the experimental results using Ziman's expression, one has to take the variation in $m^{*}$ with temperature, as it is the parameter which decides the cutoff value of $x$ in the calculation of $\tau_{e p}^{-1}$ (see Figs. 3 and 4 in Ref. 3).

In Ge, it is well known that the conduction-band minima are fourfold degenerate. According to Herring, $^{8}$ the density-of-states effective mass $m^{*}$ $=g^{2 / 3} m_{n}$. Here $g$ is the number of valleys and $m_{n}$ is the usual electron effective mass, which can be written $^{9}$ as $1 / m_{n}=1 / 3 m_{\|}+2 / 3 m_{\perp}$, where $m_{\|}$and $m_{\perp}$ are the longitudinal and transverse masses. In Ge, $g=4$, $m_{\parallel}=1.58 m$, and $m_{\perp}=0.084 m$, where $m$ is the free-electron mass. Thus the density-of- states effective mass $m^{*} \approx 0.55 m_{n}$.

In the present work it has been found that $m^{*} / m$ varies from 0.21 to 0.52 in the temperature range $1.5-4^{\circ} \mathrm{K}$. Such a sharp increase in $m^{*} / m$ with temperature cannot be related to any temperature effect on the band structure of Ge. In fact, Ziman's theory for electron-phonon scattering does not in- clude the effect of screening due to collective vibra- tions of electrons. In fact, the electron system re- acts as a plasma to the long-wavelength phonons, and the resulting screening decreases the electron- phonon interaction. If one follows Crosby and Gre- nier $^{10}$ in calculating the screening effect, the fol lowing expression for the electron-phonon relaxa-tion rate will be obtained:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{e p}^{-1}=D T\left[1+3\left(\frac{q_{\mathrm{TF}}}{q}\right)^{2}\right] \\
× \ln \frac{1+\exp \left(\eta^{*}-P T x^{2}-N / T+\frac{1}{2} x\right)}{1+\exp \left(\eta^{*}-P T x^{2}-N / T-\frac{1}{2} x\right)}, \quad(2)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $q_{\mathrm{TF}}$ is the Thomas-Fermi wave vector given by
$$q_{\mathrm{TF}}=\omega_{p} / v_{F} \quad.$$

$\omega_{p}$ is the plasma frequency and $v_{F}$ is the Fermi velocity. One can see that when $q>q_{\mathrm{TF}}$, this ex- pression for $\tau_{e p}^{-1}$ will approach the unscreened value given by Eq. (1). For $q<q_{\mathrm{TF}}$, the screening term becomes important and gives a rapid decrease in the relaxation rate. Since in the present work we have used Ziman's expression for $\tau_{e p}^{-1}$, which ne- glects screening effects, the adjusted values of $m^{*} / m$ at low temperatures are lower than the actual values. As the temperature is raised, the average phonon wavelength decreases and the effect of screening becomes less important. Hence, at high- er temperatures, more accurate values of $m^{*} / m$ are obtained (at $T=4^{\circ} \mathrm{K}, m^{*} / m \approx 0.5$ ). It is also to be noted that Conwell, $^{11}$ while analyzing the elec trical properties of degenerate $n$-Ge, obtained the

values of $m^*/m \approx 0.5$ to 0.6. These values are in excellent agreement with the present values of $m^*/$ $m$ at $T=4^\circ$K.
