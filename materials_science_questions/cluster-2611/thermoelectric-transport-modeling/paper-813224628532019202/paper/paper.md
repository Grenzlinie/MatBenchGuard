# Crystal structure, properties and nanostructuring of a new layered chalcogenide semiconductor, $Bi_2MnTe_4$$\dagger$

Dong Sun Lee, $^{ab}$ Tae-Hoon Kim, $^{c}$ Cheol-Hee Park, $^{*c}$ Chan-Yeup Chung, $^{c}$ Young Soo Lim, $^{*a}$ Won-Seon Seo $^{a}$ and Hyung-Ho Park $^{b}$

A new layered chalcogenide semiconductor, $Bi_2MnTe_4$, was discovered. It was prepared by melting and annealing methods and its crystal structure was determined by powder X-ray diffraction and Rietveld refinement. It showed p-type conducting behavior at room temperature and it was quite consistent with our density functional theory calculations. Furthermore, based on the metastability of $Bi_2MnTe_4$, a nanostructuring strategy was attempted to fabricate nanostructured bulk composites. Nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3/MnTe_2$ composite was successfully achieved in this work, and its thermoelectric properties were discussed.

## Introduction

Anomalous physical properties, such as topological insulating/superconducting properties, and excellent thermoelectric transport properties, have often been discovered in layered chalcogenide semiconductors. $^{1-6}$ The layered chalcogenide semiconductors typically have two-dimensional (2D) layered structures with van der Waals gaps between the layers. Among numerous kinds of the layered chalcogenide semiconductors, $Bi_2Te_3$ and its alloyed compounds with Sb and Se have been mostly investigated for thermoelectric applications, such as thermoelectric power generation and thermoelectric cooling/heating at near-room temperature. $^{6-10}$

$Bi_2Te_3$ has three 2D layers, and each 2D layer consists of five monoatomic layers with the stacking sequence of Te1-Bi-Te2-Bi-Te1 along the $c$-axis. $^{11,12}$ The 2D quintuple layers are weakly bound to each other by van der Waals forces, and the weak interaction across the gaps has provided us with the possibilities to explore new properties of $Bi_2Te_3$ via exfoliation and intercalation routes. $^{13-16}$ Furthermore, many new materials have been discovered in the homologous compounds of $Bi_2Te_3$ with intervening $A^{IV}Te$ ($A^{IV}$ = Pb, Ge, and Sn), and the intergrowth of $\{111\}$ planes of rock-salt $A^{IV}Te$ with the monoatomic layers in the quintuple layers of $Bi_2Te_3$ has been proposed as a formation model. $^{18-22}$ The simplest form of the homologous compounds can be represented as $Bi_2A^{IV}Te_4$ with a tetradymite-type structure ($R\overline{3}m$), and the anomalous physical properties of the layered chalcogenide semiconductors have also been found in the layered ternary chalcogenide semiconductors. $^{17,23}$

In this work, we report a new ternary chalcogenide semiconductor, $Bi_2MnTe_4$. It was fabricated by using melting and annealing methods, and its crystallographic information was characterized by using X-ray diffraction (XRD) for the first time. Although Mn is a typical transition metal rather than $A^{IV}$ elements, $Bi_2MnTe_4$ was found to be a new member of the $Bi_2A^{IV}Te_4$ family. As well as its crystal structure, spin-polarized partial density of state (PDOS) of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ was also obtained based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations. $Bi_2MnTe_4$ was a p-type semiconductor, and some physical properties, such as electrical conductivity, Seebeck coefficient, and band gap energy, were obtained at room temperature. At high temperature, it was decomposed into non-stoichiometric $Bi_2Te_3$ and $MnTe_2$ phases. By using the decomposition behavior, we could successfully achieve nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3$(matrix)/$MnTe_2$(inclusion) composite. Microstructural and thermoelectric properties of the nanostructured composite were also characterized.

### Determination of crystal structure
Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) data was collected at room temperature on a Bragg-Brentano diffractometer (Bruker-AXS, Endeavor D4) with a Cu X-ray tube (K$\alpha$) over an angular range of $10^\circ \leqslant 2\theta \leqslant 70^\circ$ with a step of $0.02^\circ$. The structure determination and refinement were performed by using TOPAS software. $^{24}$ *In situ* high-temperature X-ray diffraction data were obtained at various temperatures between 303 and 773 K using an Anton Paar HTK 1200 attached to a Bruker-AXS D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer (Bragg-Brentano geometry, Cu X-ray tube, focusing primary Ge(111) monochromator ($\lambda = 1.5405$ Å), Vantec position sensitive detector, detector slit of $6^\circ$). To prevent oxidation of $Bi_2MnTe_4$, every experiment was performed in a He(g) atmosphere.

### DFT calculations
To compute structural and electronic properties of $Bi_2MnTe_4$, spin-polarized first principles calculations were performed using DFT with the exchange-correlation energy function treated by Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) from the generalized gradient approximation (GGA).$^{25}$ Using the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) code, the self-consistent electronic density function and total energy was calculated with the pseudopotential, and the plane wave basis set expanded to an energy cutoff of 500 eV (36.749 Ry).$^{26}$ Furthermore, every calculation was performed with $\Gamma$-centered $9 \times 9 \times 1$ grid of $k$-point meshes. The structural optimization was performed with a conjugated-gradient scheme until the maximum Hellmann-Feynman force reached the range of $\pm 5.0 \times 10^{-3}$ eV Å$^{-1}$.$^{27}$

For the transport calculations, BoltzTrap code with constant relaxation time was utilized to predict the Seebeck coefficient and electrical conductivity after one self-consistent DFT calculation with 2596 $k$-points in irreducible Brillouin zone which were generated from $36 \times 36 \times 4$ $k$-points mesh.$^{28}$ To obtain temperature-dependent transport properties, Fermi integration was performed with 0.001 eV energy grid from the energy 0.5 eV below the $E_{\text{F}}$ (Fermi level obtained from preceding DFT calculation) to the energy 0.5 eV above the $E_{\text{F}}$.

### Nanostructuring of $Bi_2MnTe_4$
Mechanical decomposition of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ was carried out by using high-energy ball-milling (8000 M, Spex Sample Prep) for 20 min. Prior to the ball-milling, extra Te was added for the compensation of the non-stoichiometry in the decomposed phases of $Bi_2Te_3$ and $MnTe_2$. Resulting high-energy ball-milled powder was consolidated by using spark plasma sintering (WT-4000A, Well Tech) at 673 K under 50 MPa of uniaxial pressure for 5 min and a nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3$/MnTe$_2$ composite was obtained.

### Characterization of microstructural and physical properties
Electrical conductivities and Seebeck coefficients of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ and nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3$/MnTe$_2$ composite were characterized by using a thermoelectric properties measurement system (RZ-2001i, Ozawa Science) based on four-point probe method, and their thermal conductivities were measured by a laser flash method (LFA-457, NETZSCH). Band gap energy of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ was determined by using diffuse reflectance (Shimadzu, UV-3600) with MgO as a reference. Microstructural characterizations in the nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3$/MnTe$_2$ composite were carried out by using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, JSM-6700, JEOL) and elemental distributions were characterized by using electron probe micro analysis (EPMA, JXA-8100, JEOL).

## Results and discussion
### Crystal structure of $Bi_2MnTe_4$
In order to determine the unit cell, peak positions of the XRD pattern were firstly obtained by single peak profile fitting run in TOPAS and a rhombohedral unit cell with $a = 4.334$ Å and $c = 40.91$ Å was readily obtained. Rietveld refinement of the powder X-ray diffraction data was carried out using the structure as a starting model, resulting in good profile fitting (Fig. S1, ESI$\dagger$). Final crystallographic parameters for the refinement are given in Table 1, and the positional parameters are listed in Table 2. The refined crystal structure of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ is represented in Fig. 1(a). It can be described as rhombohedrally centered slabs composed of septuple monoatomic layers with a stacking sequence of Te1-Bi-Te2-Mn-Te2-Bi-Te1 along the $c$-axis. The structure may also be described by using the $Bi_2Te_3$ structure in Fig. 1(b), where the Te2 layer in Te1-Bi-Te2-Bi-Te1 slabs in the $Bi_2Te_3$ structure is substituted with Te2-Mn-Te2 layers. As summarized in Table 3, the lattice parameters of $a$ and $c$ in the $Bi_2MnTe_4$ were 4.3338(2) Å and 40.910(2) Å, respectively.

The maximum calculated Seebeck coefficient of $\sim 1186$ $\mu\text{V K}^{-1}$ was found at 0.07 eV above $E_{\text{F}}$, and it can be achieved by proper electron doping.

## Stability and physical properties of $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$

Stability of $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$ was tested by using HT-XRD, and it was found to be a metastable compound. Fig. 4 shows temperature-dependent powder XRD patterns of $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$, and the results revealed that $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$ starts to be decomposed into $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$ and $\text{MnTe}_2$ phases above 423 K. The diffraction peaks of $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$ completely disappeared at 773 K. When we consider the elemental ratio in $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$, both phases which were produced by the thermal decomposition should be highly non-stoichiometric compounds of $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_{3-x}$ and $\text{MnTe}_{2-y}$, where $x + y = 1$.

Properties of $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$ were not stable at relatively high temperature due to its metastable nature (Fig. S2, ESI†), so that we could obtain the properties only at room temperature as summarized in Table 4. Positive Seebeck coefficient (p-type) was observed in $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$ as expected from the BoltzTrap calculations in Fig. 3. The Seebeck coefficient of $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$ was significantly higher than those of other $\text{Bi}_2\text{A}^\text{IV}\text{Te}_4$ compounds, while its electrical conductivity was quite lower than those of other compounds. Because Seebeck coefficient is proportional to $p^{-2/3}$, where $p$ is hole concentration, these results are quite consistent to each other. Band gap energy of $\text{Bi}_2\text{MnTe}_4$ was determined by using diffuse reflectance spectrum *via* the Kubelka-Munk method at room temperature.$^{29,30}$ The band

In many solid materials, smaller difference in the electro-negativities of M and X gives more covalent M-X bonding and higher mobility.$^{31}$ The Pauling's electronegativity value for Mn (1.55) is much smaller than those for Bi (2.02), Ge (2.01), Sn (1.96) and Te (2.10),$^{32}$ and this larger difference in electro-negativity values in $Bi_2MnTe_4$ also agrees with its lower electrical conductivity (39 S cm$^{-1}$) compared to isostructural $Bi_2GeTe_4$ ($E_g$ = 0.19 eV, $\sigma$ = 500 S cm$^{-1}$). Therefore, the low electrical conductivity in $Bi_2MnTe_4$ can be attributed to its low carrier concentration due to the relatively large band gap energy and also to its lower mobility due to the weakened covalent bonding character.

### Nanostructuring of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ based on its metastability

As described in the previous section, $Bi_2MnTe_4$ can be spontaneously decomposed into $Bi_2Te_3$ and $MnTe_2$ phases at high temperature. By using the phenomenon, we tried to fabricate nanostructured $MnTe_2$ inclusions in $Bi_2Te_3$ matrix. Although thermal annealing could be applicable for the decomposition, the resulting structure was not precisely controlled for the nanostructuring (Fig. S3, ESI$\dagger$). Therefore, we attempted to decompose the $Bi_2MnTe_4$ phase by applying mechanical energy. Prior to the high-energy ball-milling, a mixture of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ powder with excess Te (1 mole per $Bi_2MnTe_4$) was prepared to compensate Te deficiency in the decomposed phases of $Bi_2Te_3$ and $MnTe_2$. High-energy ball-milling of the mixture (Bi : Mn : Te = 2 : 1 : 5) was carried out, and resulting powder was consolidated by using spark plasma sintering. Phase analysis of the sintered body was performed by using XRD, and it was confirmed that the original $Bi_2MnTe_4$ with extra Te was completely transformed into $Bi_2Te_3$ and $MnTe_2$ phases (Fig. S4, ESI$\dagger$).

Microstructures of the sintered body are shown in Fig. 6. As shown in Fig. 6(a), micron-scale $MnTe_2$ inclusions with relatively dark contrast were uniformly produced especially at the grain boundaries in $Bi_2Te_3$ matrix. The $MnTe_2$ phase was not only observed at the grain boundaries, but also observed inside the $Bi_2Te_3$ grains. Finely distributed in-grain $MnTe_2$ nanoinclusions were clearly observed in Fig. 6(b). Furthermore, EPMA results in Fig. 6(c)-(e) manifested that the multi-scale inclusions of $MnTe_2$ were uniformly distributed in the $Bi_2Te_3$ matrix. Therefore, we could successfully fabricate the nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3/MnTe_2$ composite by consolidating the high-energy ball-milled powder, and this result suggests that the mechanical decomposition of meta-

### Thermoelectric properties of nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3/MnTe_2$ composite

Thermoelectric performance of a material is determined by dimensionless figure of merit, $ZT = S^2\sigma T/\kappa$, where $S$, $T$ and $\kappa$ are the Seebeck coefficient, absolute temperature and thermal conductivity, respectively. Therefore, nanostructuring has been extensively applied to reduce the thermal conductivity by phonon scattering at the interface between the matrix and nanoinclusions. $^{33-39}$ However, in this nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3$/ $MnTe_2$ composite, the thermal conductivity was surprisingly higher than our expectation. Thermal conductivity of the nanocomposite was $1.345$ W mK$^{-1}$ at room temperature as shown in Fig. 7(a) and it is a comparable value to conventional $Bi_2Te_3$ composites. Considering its nanostructure observed in Fig. 5, it seems to be a quite unusual phenomenon and the origin of its high thermal conductivity is under investigation.

Electrical conductivity and Seebeck coefficient of the nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3/MnTe_2$ composite were simultaneously characterized by using four-point probe method. Electrical conductivity of the nanocomposite was $536$ S cm$^{-1}$ at room temperature and it was a quite higher value than that of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ as shown in Fig. 7(b). The electrical conductivity monotonically decreased with temperature, so that its conducting behavior is metallic. The Seebeck coefficient of the nanocomposite was a negative value in the entire temperature range in Fig. 7(c) and the result represented that the $Bi_2Te_3$ matrix has n-type property in this experimental condition. Because Te-deficiency in $Bi_2Te_3$ leads to p-type property due to the formation of anti-site defect ($Bi_{Te}$), the composition in the $Bi_2Te_3$ matrix, which was prepared by nanostructuring of $Bi_2MnTe_4$ and extra Te, seems to be very close to its stoichiometric composition. $^{40,41}$ The Seebeck coefficient of the nanocomposite ($-113.4$ $\mu$V K$^{-1}$ at room temperature) was significantly lower than reported values in the literature, and it can be attributed to the p-type property of $MnTe_2$ (Fig. S5, ESI$\dagger$) in the n-type matrix. With those results, we could obtain the maximum $ZT$ of $\sim$0.24 at 400-440 K in the nanostructured $Bi_2Te_3/MnTe_2$ composite.

### Conclusions

A new layered compound $Bi_2MnTe_4$ was prepared for the first time, and its crystal structure was determined via powder X-ray diffraction and Rietveld refinement method. Although Mn is a typical transition metal, this observation adds another member to the $Bi_2A^{IV}Te_4$ family. $Bi_2MnTe_4$ was p-type semiconductor with relatively large Seebeck coefficient at room temperature. These experimental results were quite consistent to our DFT calculations. Furthermore, in situ XRD
