# Electronic structure and thermoelectricity of filled skutterudite $\mathbf{CeRu_4Sb_{12}}$

A. Shankar$^{1,*}$, D. P. Rai$^{2}$, Sandeep$^{3}$, R. Khenata$^{4}$, R. K. Thapa$^{3}$ and P. K. Mandal$^{1}$

$^{1}$Department of Physics, University of North Bengal, Darjeeling -734013, India

$^{2}$Department of Physics, Pachhunga University College, Aizawl 796001, India

$^{3}$Condensed Matter Theory Research Group, Department of Physics, Mizoram University, Aizawl-796004, India.

$^{4}$Laboratoire de Physique Quantique et de Modélisation Mathématique (LPQ3M), Département de Technologie, Université de Mascara, 29000, Algeria

Email: amitshan2009@gmail.com

First-principles calculations of the energy band structure and density of states of filled skutterudite $\mathrm{CeRu_4Sb_{12}}$ have been performed to understand the origin of thermoelectricity. The calculations are carried out using the full potential linear augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method within a framework of LDA approach. $\mathrm{CeRu_4Sb_{12}}$ is a metal with bands crossing Fermi energy level more than twice with indirect energy band gap of ~0.09 eV above the Fermi energy level. The study of the elastic properties suggests the ductile nature of the material with covalent contribution in the atomic bonding. Our calculations performed for the density of electronic states near the Fermi energy level show that the large thermo-power at room temperature originates from the hybridized $\mathrm{Ru\textit{-}d}$ and $\mathrm{Sb\textit{-}p}$ orbitals. The study of the thermal transport properties suggests the high value of Seebeck coefficient with figure of merit $(ZT)=0.12$, which is consistent to the values obtained for the analogous compounds.

**Keywords:** FP-LAPW, filled skutterudites, elastic constants, DOS, figure of merit.

### 1. Introduction

The filled skutterudites are among the class of intermetallic compounds with increasing interest to understand the underlying physics and materials for technologies. The presence of strong electron-electron correlation in the skutterudite compounds gives low temperature properties [1] and other interesting phenomena such as, heavy fermion behavior [2], ferromagnetism [3-6], semi-conductivity with small hybridization gap [7], non-Fermi liquid behavior [10], which make them highlighted for the material research. Several theoretical and experimental works have been performed on Ce based candidate compounds of lanthanide filled skutterudite. The experimental report of Dordevic *et al*. [8] suggested an indirect band gap semiconducting nature of CeFe₄P₁₂, which later on confirmed by DFT based theoretical investigation [9, 10]. Similarly, the semiconducting nature was also observed in CeRu₄P₁₂ by experimental [11] and FP-LMTO based study [12]. The magnetization measurement of CeRu₄As₁₂ [13] shows a transition from weakly insulating to semi-metallic non Fermi liquid for polycrystalline to single crystal sample. The analysis of the thermal transport measurement of CeRu₄As₁₂ [14] also reflects a transition from a hybridization gap insulator to Kondo semiconductor with increase in temperature from 50K. The experimental analysis of Ultrahigh resolution photoemission (UHRPE) spectroscopic [15,16] measurement suggested the semi-metallic nature of CeRu₄Sb₁₂ with hybridized $4f$-valence band just below the Fermi energy level at low temperature. The consistent result of heavy-fermion semi-mettalic behaviour of CeRu₄Sb₁₂ has been reported by Kobayashi *et al*. [17]. In contradict to the previous reports on CeRu₄Sb₁₂, the ultrasonic measurement revealed the metallic nature of this compound [18], where the elastic parameters decreases sharply at low temperature. The strong hybridization between the rare earth and pnictogen also makes these compounds suitable for thermoelectric applications [19]. The thermoelectric efficiency of a material is determined by its figure of merit, which depends upon

power factor, electrical conductivity and thermal conductivity [20]. The narrow band gap semiconductors have large mobility of charge carriers, which leads to high power factor and moderate electric resistivity [21-23], giving the possibility of the thermoelectric applications. The loosely bounded Ce ions within the cages formed by the Ru and Sb, lowers the thermal conductivity of $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ material which is inversely related with the figure of merit. The studies of the energy bands suggest the presence of large thermopower in metallic $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$ and semiconducting Ce-filled skutterudites [24-26]. Similar results were obtained for narrow band gap semiconductors like $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $ThFe_4P_{12}$ [10] making them suitable for thermoelectric applications.

The studies of the relative position of the Fermi energy level $(E_F)$ in the energy band and the distribution of upper valence density of states (DOS) help us to understand the origin of the thermoelectric properties of a material. In the present work, we have performed the first principles investigations of energy bands and DOS of $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ in the region close to Fermi energy level using the FP-LAPW method. The study of the energy bands near the Fermi energy level suggests the possibility of the material for thermoelectric applications. Hence, we have performed the thermal transport measurement using Boltzmann semi-classical theory [27]. The numerical values of elastic constants are also estimated to understand the physical and mechanical properties of $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ at ambient condition.

## 2. Computational details

Density functional theory (DFT) based full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP- LAPW) method implemented in the Wien2k code [28] has been used to perform the work presented here. The exchange correlation potential was calculated by using the local density approximation (LDA). In order to achieve energy eigenvalues convergence, the wave functions in the interstitial region were expanded in plane waves with a cutoff, $K_{max}=7/R_{MT}$, where $R_{MT}$

denotes the smallest atomic sphere radius and $K_{max}$ is the maximum value of the wave vector in the plane wave expansion. The different MT sphere radii ($R_{MT}$) used were 3.1 a.u., 2.4 a.u. and 1.9 a.u. for Ce, Ru and Sb, respectively. Nonspherical contributions to the charge density and the potential within the MT spheres were considered up to $l_{max}=6$, while the charge density and the potential were expanded as a Fourier series with the wave vectors up to $G_{max}=12$ (a.u.)$^{-1}$. The sets of valence orbital in the calculations were selected as 4f,5d, 6s for Ce; 4d, 5s for Ru and 5s, 5p for Sb. All lower states were treated as core states. The energy cut-off for separation of valence and core regions is taken at -6.0 Ry. The multiple expansion of wave function as well as electron density and the potential inside the muffin-tin (MT) spheres were cut at $l$=10. The self-consistent potentials were calculated on a $17 \times 17 \times 17$ $k$-mesh in the Brillouin zone (BZ) and the self-consistent calculations are considered to be converged when the total energy of the system is stable within $10^{-4}$ Ry.

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1 Structural optimization and elastic constants

Ce-filled skutterudite crystallizes in unique bcc structure with $Im\overline{3}$ space group. In the unit cell of $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ crystal, one Ce atom takes the atomic position of (0, 0, 0) with the transitional metal atom (Ru) located at (0.25, 0.25, 0.25) and the antimony located at the position of (0, 0.35, 0.16). The symmetry operations associated with the space group determines the positions of the remaining atoms in the unity cell. The unit cell of a crystal is composed of eight similar cubes with Ru atom at the centre surrounded by the Sb rings. The structural optimization has been performed within the frame work of PBE-GGA as exchange correlation potential. The equilibrium lattice constant corresponding to the minimum total energy of the crystal is calculated by fitting the crystal volume and the total energy to the Murnaghan's equation of

states. The estimated value of the lattice constant from the equation of states is 9.373 Å, which is in close agreement with the experimental value of 9.266 Å [19]. It is known that PBE-GGA often overcorrects the approximation by predicting the values 1% - 2% higher than the experimental ones. The elastic constants of a material define the response of the material to the applied external hydrostatic pressure. The elastic constants for the title material are calculated by volume deformation technique as implemented in Wien2k, by choosing the strain in such a way that the volume of the unit cell remains constant. The three independent elastic parameters calculated for stable structure are presented in Table 1. We find that the calculated independent elastic parameters are in qualitative agreement with the previously available theoretical results on $CeFe_4P_{12}$ [9], $CeRu_4P_{12}$ [12], $CeFe_4As_{12}$ [24], $LaFe_4As_{12}$ [25] and experimental results on $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ [18]. We find that the unidirectional elastic constants $C_{11}$ is higher than $C_{44}$ by more than 60%, which makes this compound relatively weaker resistance to the pure shear deformation. The elastic constants derived parameters are given in Table 1, and compared with the available results. Nakanishi *et al.* [18] have analysed the elastic constant of the sample material using ultrasonic measurement. They have mentioned the temperature dependence of the elastic constants, which decreases abruptly at low temperature due to the coupling of the elastic strain and the quasiparticle energy band with small energy band gap.

Bulk modulus ($B$), which represents the resistance to volume change, has a strong correlation with the binding energy of a crystal. Isotropic shear modulus ($G$), measures the resistance to reversible deformations upon shear stress. The calculated $B$ and $G$ values are qualitatively similar to that of isostructural $La/CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ [24,25] which indicates that the hardness of the studied material is comparable with that of $La/CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ [24,25]. The analysis of Cauchy's pressure (CP = $C_{12}$-$C_{44}$) and Pugh's $B/G$ ratio [30] suggest the ductile nature of the material. The stiffness of a solid can be predicted from Young's modulus ($Y$), which gives the relation of linear stress to

strain. Our computed $Y$ indicates the stiffness of the material is comparable to the isostructural $\mathrm{CeOs}_{4} \mathrm{Sb}_{12}$ [31] and $\mathrm{ThFe}_{4} \mathrm{P}_{12}$ [10]. The sound velocity, which is related to the lattice vibration of a solid, can be used to understand the Debye temperature $(\theta_{D})$, and hence the thermal conductivity of the solid. $\theta_{D}$ is also related to thermal properties such as specific heat and melting temperature. The knowledge of such properties is essential for manufacturing electronic devices. Higher the sound velocity larger will be the Debye temperature and hence the thermal conductivity. The computed $\theta_{D}$ is comparable to the previous values of $\mathrm{CeOs}_{4} \mathrm{Sb}_{12}$ [31], $\mathrm{CeFe}_{4} \mathrm{P}_{12}$ [9] and $\mathrm{CeRu}_{4} \mathrm{P}_{12}$ [12]. Poisson's ratio $(v)$ gives the idea of the stability of a material against shear. The obtained value of $v$ indicates very less volume deformation with covalent contribution in the intra-atomic bonding of the material. The anisotropy factor $(A)$ value smaller or greater than the unity defines the degree of elastic anisotropy and the stiffness along cubic axis and body diagonal respectively. Hence our compound is elastically anisotropic with stiffness along cubic axis in the crystal, which might lead to a probability to develop microcracks during the growing process of the material.

### 3.2 Density of states and energy bands

The DFT based energy band calculation [7,32] have suggested the efficient thermoelectric properties of Ce filled skutterudites and $\mathrm{La}(\mathrm{Fe}, \mathrm{Co})_{4} \mathrm{Sb}_{12}$, within the framework of local density

approximation. Later on, Fornari *et al.* [33] used the electronic structure calculation to show the opening of the energy band gap on filling of $CoP_3$ by La. Similarly, Takegahara and Harima [34] used FP-LAPW method to investigate the enhanced DOS at $E_F$ by replacing pnictogen element from $P$ to $Sb$ in $LaFe_4X_{12}$ ($X = P, As, Sb$). The TB-LMTO and FP-LAPW method [26] based comparative study of the electronic structure revealed the metallic and semiconducting nature of $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$, respectively. Furthermore, the DFT based calculation also suggested the narrow band gap semiconducting nature of $CeOs_4Sb_{12}$ [31]. From the above, it is clear that there is a considerable electronic structure calculation on various combinations of rare-earth and transition metal based filled skutterudites, whereas the electronic structure calculations for the studied material is limited. We therefore calculated the energy band structures, total and partial density of states (DOS) of $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ and presented in Fig. 1. The overall profile of the obtained energy bands are in fairly good agreement with the previous FP-LAPW calculated results of the La and Ce filled skutterudites [7,26,35] and binary skutterudite [36,37] for the valence and conduction region, except the region close to the Fermi energy level. The replacement of transitional metal Co/Ir by Ru and the insertion of Ce in the voids of binary skutterudite vary the nature of the obtained band structure in the region close to the Fermi level. The band energy dispersions are different for the conduction and the valence region due to the different mobilities of the localized holes, delocalized and non-occupied bands. From the DOS plot, one can note that there are three distinct regions separated from each other by more or less distinct energy gaps. The analysis of the partial DOS plot (Figs. 1b, 1c) suggests that the core region is predominated by the Sb. The lower valence region also has contribution from $p$- orbital of Sb hybridized with Ru. The region below the Fermi energy level ($E_F$) has major contribution from the transition metal, which is weakly hybridized with pnictogen. The consistent result was also observed for homologous $LaFe_3CoSb_{12}$ [38]. Harima [38] have suggested the indirect band gap

semiconducting nature of $LaFe_3CoSb_{12}$, where the valence region is mainly contributed by pnictogen. The conduction region is consists of $d$ orbitals of Ce and Ru, weekly hybridized with antimony. The analysis of energy band structure reveals the densely populated bands just above the Fermi energy level, which is contributed by the Ce-$4f$ electrons as seen from the partial DOS plots.

The electronic structure calculations of Ce filled skutterudites suggest the semiconducting ground states [7,39,40], where the energy gap is transitional metal dependent. The hybridization

between the Ru-Sb opens the gap in Ce filled skutterudites, in such a way that the compounds become Kondo semiconductor or Kondo semimetal. Contrary to the previous results of Ce filled skutterudites, the electronic structure calculation of $CeRu_{4}Sb_{12}$, predicts the metallic ground state [15]. The analysis of the energy band structure near $E_{F}$ suggests that the hybridization between Ru-Sb is not able to open up the gap. The increase in the unit cell volume with addition of Sb, reduces the strength of hybridization and corresponding hybridization gap in $CeRu_{4}Sb_{12}$. The Ultrahigh-resolution photoemission spectra measurement of $CeRu_{4}Sb_{12}$ [15] also suggested the vanishing of hybridization gap due to the presence of residual charge carrier at low temperature. They have classified the compound as 'Kondo semimetal'. However, the $CeFe_{4}Sb_{12}$ and $CeRu_{4}Sb_{12}$ are metallic and have some character of heavy fermions [7]. The LDA calculated energy band structure (Fig. 1a) reflects an indirect energy gap of ~0.09 eV from the $\Gamma$ - point (top of valence band VB) to the N- point (bottom of the conduction band CB) along the $\Sigma$ direction of the Brillouin zone (BZ). The substitution of M (Co, Ir, Rh) in the presence of Sb and filling of void by Ce, increases the interaction between the cerium and ruthenium in $CeRu_{4}Sb_{12}$, which results the displacement of energy bands as well as the crossing the Fermi energy level by valence band and corresponding decrease in energy gap values as compared to binary skutterudites. The more important feature of the energy band structure is that $E_{F}$ is not in between the conduction and valence band and the top of the VB crosses the Fermi energy level at least two times in the directions $\Gamma-H$ and $N-\Gamma-P$, which increases the concentration of bands along the $E_{F}$ and the corresponding Seebeck coefficient. The above feature, two bands originating from the conduction region crossing the Fermi energy level twice, has also been observed for analogous $LaFe_{4}Sb_{12}$ which posses direct band gap ($\Gamma$ point of BZ) of 0.81 eV [34,35]. The computed energy band structure and DOS plots suggest the metallic character of $CeRu_{4}Sb_{12}$, with the presence of highly concentrated bands in the region close to $E_{F}$. Similar result was predicted

for metallic analogous $CeOs_4Sb_{12}$ [35,38]. Thus $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ is a metal in agreement with the experiment results of analogous compounds $UFe_4P_{12}$, $CeFe_4P_{12}$ and $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$ [39,40]. The result is in contradict to the experimental results of Kanai *et al.* [15], where they have concluded the semi-metallic nature of $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ using UHPRE spectroscopic study at low temperature. From the DOS and energy band structure plots, one can mention that, in the region above the $E_F$ and bottom of conduction band, the concentration of bands is very low corresponding to low DOS region in DOS plot. Here, the density of state is very low but not zero and the presence of such region with a low density of states is typical characteristic of the skutterudites [26,33,34]. The bottom of the valence region has been shifted to higher energy, compared to previous band structure of $CeRu_4P_{12}$ [7], which suggest the more covalent nature of the title compound as compared to $CeRu_4P_{12}$, similar to the result obtained from Poisson's ratios of this compound given in section 3.1. The density of states at $E_F$ is also computed from the energy band structure, which has been found as $N(E_F)=26.65$ states/eV and the electronic specific heat ($\gamma$), estimated from $N(E_F)=63.31$ mJ/mol-K$^2$. Singh and co-workers [32] have also used such approach of comparison of electronic specific heat calculated from energy band structure. The computed values of $\gamma$ is in similar range of that obtained by Harima [38], Harima and Takegahara [27] and Nouneh *et al.* [26] for isostructural $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$ and $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$. The experimental analysis of electrical resistivity have suggested $\gamma=73$ mJ/mol-K$^2$ for $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ [29], which is in close agreement with our theoretically calculated value.

### 3.3 Thermoelectric properties

The study of the energy band structure suggests the metallic nature of the material with presence of narrow band gap above $E_F$. The Fermi energy level is crossed by a band originating from the bottom of the conduction region, which increases the number of DOS at $E_F$ ($N(E_F)$). The

crossing of Fermi energy level increases the charge carrier concentration, which makes the material suitable for thermoelectric applications. We, therefore think it is worthwhile to study the thermal transport properties of the sample material by employing Boltzmann semi-classical theory [27] as implemented in Boltztrap [41]. The thermo power is a measure of the ability of a material to generate electromotive force from the applied temperature gradient across the material. The variation of Seebeck coefficient $S(T)$, which is also called coefficient of thermo power, versus temperature in the temperature range 0-300K is presented in Fig. 2. The Seebeck coefficient obtained from our band energy calculation are in qualitative agreement with the experimental data [42] in the wide temperature range. The compounds under study exhibit positive $S$ value, which shows the majority of the charge carriers, are holes. The transitional metal used (Ru) has one electron less compared to Co atom, which gives four electron deficiencies in $Ru_{4}Sb_{12}$ as compared to binary skutterudites. The Ce atom donates its valence electrons to fill the voids created, but the trivalent Ce is not able to compensate all the voids in $CeRu_{4}Sb_{12}$. Hence, they contains hole as majority charge carrier and gives positive value of $S$.

From the figure, the Seebeck coefficient shows values close to horizontal axis until temperature reaches 20 K, thereafter showing the abrupt rise with a maxima ($S = 129\ \mu\text{V/K}$) at 80 K. The experimental report [42] gives minima in $S$ value at 20 K, with the maximum amplitude of ($\sim$135 $\mu\text{V/K}$) at 90 K. This low temperature peak is due to the electron scattering by the ground state doublet of the $Ce^{3+}$ ion. Our result of $S$ suggests a sharp decrease in amplitude from 80 K till 200 K and there after the plot shows slow reduction in $S$ value with increasing temperature. The experimental result also shows the similar sharp negative slope till 250 K and thereafter slow increase. The slight deviation in our calculated results may be due to the presence of strongly correlated electrons of Ce and the choice of correlation function. This problem may arise in other p-type materials also. At room temperature the value of $S$ is found to be $53\ \mu\text{V/K}$, which is lower

than the experimental value of $60\ \mu$V/K [42]. The $S$ value obtained at room temperature is much larger than that of same carrier concentration p-type binary skutterudite $CoSb_3$ [43], which is attributed by the presence of the bands crossing $E_F$ giving large effective mass and the presence of hybridization of transitional metal and pnictogen present in the material. The band energy and thermal transport calculations unambiguously show that the hybridization of Ru and Sb gives the large thermopower at room temperature and crossing of $E_F$ by the bands, which enhance the density of states $N(E_F)$. The result also predicts the high effective mass, which gives substantial rise to the thermoelectric Seebeck coefficient [26], similar to analogous $LaFe_4Sb_{12}$ [29]. At room temperature the thermoelectric efficiency of the material is found to be 0.12. Here, we can mention that the obtained value of figure of merit is close to the previous values obtained for analogous compounds 0.13 for $EuFe_4Sb_{12}$ [6], 0.13 for $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$ [44], which has been enhanced to 1.0 to 1.5 with recent development of nano-structuring and nanolayers of composite material $CeFe_4Sb_{12}$. The various theoretical and experimental results prove that the heavy guest atom with small radius is more effective in reducing the thermal conductivity. It has also been found that the partial filling of the void with filler atoms helps to increase the efficiency value close to unity. Filling of binary skutterudite with 0.19% of Y at 600 K yields the efficiency up to 1 [45]. Similar result was obtained for $Eu_{0.42}Co_4Sb_{11.37}Ge_{0.50}$ [46]. The $ZT$ value of 1.17 was obtained at 750 K for $Ca_{0.18}Ce_{0.12}Fe_{1.45}Co_{2.55}Sb_{12.21}$ [47], $ZT$ value more than 1 for $Yb_{0.19}Co_4Sb_{12}$ and $Yb_{0.21}Co_4Sb_{12}$ beyond 600 K, 1.3 for the $Yb_{0.25}Co_4Sb_{12}/Yb_2O_3$ and 1.2 for the $Yb_{0.21}Co_4Sb_{12}/Yb_2O_3$ at 850 K [48]. The addition of Ca in Yb filled skutterudite gives $ZT=1.03$ for $Ca_{0.12}Yb_{0.12}Co_4Sb_{12.17}$ at 750 K [49]. $ZT=1.7$ was obtained for $CoSb_3$ with multiple co-fillers Ba, La, and Yb at 850 K [50]. The sample material also can be excellent candidate for thermoelectric application provided the efficiency of the material can be enhanced by some chemical doping by adding various elements in different concentrations.

## 4. Conclusions

We have studied the elastic, electronic and thermal transport properties of the filled skutterudite $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ using the FP-LAPW method. Our computed results show consistency with the available experimental data. The analysis of elastic constants suggested the ductile nature of the material and covalent contribution in the bonding with the stiffness comparable to analogous $CeOs_4Sb_{12}$ and $ThFe_4P_{12}$. The study of energy bands and density of states reflects the presence of heavily concentrated bands above the Fermi energy level with an indirect band gap of 0.09 eV. The Fermi energy level is situated within the valence region with the bands originating from bottom of conduction region crossing $E_F$ more than twice. This crossing of $E_F$ by the bands due to Ce-$f$ and Ru-$d$ orbital increases the number of DOS at $E_F$ and suggest the metallic nature of the material, which gives large charge carriers and predicts enhanced Seebeck coefficient for thermoelectric application. The thermal transport analysis confirms the results of electronic structure calculation with high thermopower. The thermal efficiency of the materials has been estimated as 0.12 at room temperature. This $ZT$ value of $CeRu_4Sb_{12}$ may be enhanced by doping, which attributes further reduction of lattice thermal conductivity. Several approaches can be employed for this purpose including partial substitution of Ru by Co, Ni etc. which have more

valence electrons than Ru. This could reduce the high hole concentration in the material and increase the Seebeck coefficient to enhance ZT.

- $\text{CeRu}_4\text{Sb}_{12}$ is ductile material with covalent contribution in bonding.
