## Abstract
The electronic structure and thermoelectric properties of $CaMnO_3$ doped with 8% and 17% f block element Sm using first principles calculations and semi-classic Boltzmann theory were presented in this paper. The G-type AFM phase is most stable among five phases for $CaMnO_3$, however, with 8% and 17% Sm doping, these compounds became nonmagnetic phases. $CaMnO_3$ calculated electronic band structure shows an indirect band gap of 0.523 eV, which is underestimated by the density functional theory (DFT) calculations but the band gap explains the semiconducting behavior. However, with 8% and 17% Sm doping, the electronic bandstructure of these compounds exhibit metallic behavior, with Sm 4f and Mn 3d electrons contributing to conduction band, increasing the magnitude of conductivity for doped compounds. All temperature dependence Seebeck coefficient plots show n-typed conduction for all compound with reduced magnitude of Seebeck coefficient for doped compounds. The temperature dependence thermal conductivity plot shows overall thermal conductivity is reduced in Sm doped compound. $CaMnO_3$ with 17% Sm doping exhibit much higher ZT of 0.32 at 800 K showing enhanced thermoelectric properties at high temperature and suitability or high temperature energy conversion devices.

## Introduction
Thermoelectric devices allow for the direct conversion of heat to electrical energy. The increase in global energy consumption as well as the negative impacts on the environment of many current energy conversion technologies, such as the combustion of fossil fuels, has led to increased activity in developing alternative energy conversion technologies. One of the alternative energy source routes is the application of thermoelectric devices that convert waste heat into electrical energy[1,2].

However, the major challenge in the implementation of thermoelectric power generation is that of low energy conversion efficiency. A promising n-type oxide for thermoelectric applications is $CaMnO_3$ which has $ABO_3$ perovskite structure that can be doped on site A or site B [3]. A study of several lanthanide dopants demonstrated that ytterbium additions were most effective for increasing the figure of merit [5]. Ytterbium is particularly beneficial in reducing the thermal conductivity [4,6] which is attributed to a decrease in phonon contributions to the thermal conduction [7]. Niobium additions have been shown to lead to an increase in the electrical conductivity and decrease in the thermal conductivity, but also a decrease in the Seebeck coefficient [8]. Similar trends were observed with tantalum additions, but niobium additions resulted in a higher electrical conductivity [8].

In this work, the electronic structures are calculated using the first principles method for the CaMnO₃ doped by 8.3% and 16.6% samarium at Ca-site. ## Materials and Methods

Our electronic structure and total energy calculation are based on the density-functional theory (DFT) [10] with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [11] version of generalized gradient approximation. The self-consistent total energy calculations are performed by using the plane wave pseudopotential method implemented in the Quantum-Espresso package [12] which is used to solve the Kohn-Sham equations through the plane wave expansion for the valence electron density and wavefunctions. The interactions between ions and electrons are described by means of the nonconserving pseudopotentials with generalized gradient approximations (GGA) [13]. The plane wave cutoff is taken to be 100 Ry, which provides a good total energy convergence within 0.0005 Ry/atom. The Brillouin zone is sampled according to the Monkhorst Pack method [14]. The special size of the k-point mesh will be given below for each system studied. Structure relaxations are performed by using the quasi-Newton algorithm and the structures are optimized until all components of forces are smaller than 0.0004 Ry/atom. The Seebeck coefficient $S$, thermal conductivity $\kappa$ and electronic conductivity, $\sigma$, were obtained using the semi-classical Boltzmann theory in conjunction with rigid band and constant relaxation time approximations using the BoltzTraP package [16]. The necessary crystal structures and eigen-energies for BoltzTraP calculation were obtained from ab initio results from Quantum Espresso package. The constant relaxation time used in the calculations is 0.8 x 10⁻¹⁴ s as described in the Aschroft and Mermin, Solid States Physics textbook [17].

## Results and Discussion

In this work, the CaMnO₃, Ca₀.₉₂Sm₀.₀₈MnO₃ and Ca₀.₈₃Sm₀.₁₇MnO₃ supercells were modeled with 60 atoms, a 3 x 1 x 1 supercell derived from a crystal structure of CaMnO₃ with Pbnm unit cell of 20 atoms from experiment [15]. Table 1 shows the lattice constants of supercell structures of CaMnO₃, Ca₀.₉₂Sm₀.₀₈MnO₃ and Ca₀.₈₃Sm₀.₁₇MnO₃ after variable cell relaxation procedures. With 8% and 17% Sm doping, all lattice constants increase to a slightly bigger volume. However with 17% Sm doping the lattice constant $a$ and $c$ slightly decrease while lattice constant $b$ increases slightly to a smaller volume compare to 8% Sm doping. The decrease of lattice $a$, $c$ and the increase of $b$ indicates that there are higher degree of connection between Mn and O along $b$ directions than that between Mn and O along $a$ and $c$, which indicates that more charge carriers transport along Mn–O in $b$ directions for conduction process [18].

The relaxed structures were used in series of magnetic calculations with the structures were set in ferromagnetic phase (FM), nonmagnetic phase (NM), antiferromagnetic A phase (AFMA), antiferromagnetic C phase (AFMC) and antiferromagnetic G phase (AFMG). The total energy of the five phases of CaMnO₃ were calculated to find the most stable phase among the five phases, and it is noted that the AFMG is the most stable phase among the five phases. With magnetic properties, the bandstructure split into spin up and spin down band structures. Fig. 1 (a) and (b) show the electronic band structure of CaMnO₃ spin up and spin down respectively. Both band structures

show indirect band gap about 0.523 eV wide  with Fermi level close to the conduction band indicating the n type semiconductor [19]. With single Sm doping and double Sm doping, the magnetic calculations indicates that both materials are of non magnetic phase. Fig. 1 (c) and (d) shows the band structure of $Ca_{0.92}Sm_{0.08}MnO_3$ and $Ca_{0.83}Sm_{0.17}MnO_3$ respectively. Both bandstructures show the Fermi level overlaps many band lines and there are no energy gap indicated. The Fermi levels of the doped systems shift upwards into the conduction indicates doped systems become metallic.

Fig. 1: The electronic band structure of (a) $CaMnO_3$ spin up, (b) $CaMnO_3$ spin down, (c) $Ca_{0.92}Sm_{0.08}MnO_3$, (d) $Ca_{0.83}Sm_{0.17}MnO_3$.

Fig. 2 shows the transport properties for $CaMnO_3$, $Ca_{0.92}Sm_{0.08}MnO_3$ and $Ca_{0.83}Sm_{0.17}MnO_3$. Fig. 2 (a) shows the conductivity vs temperature for the three materials. The increasing conductivity with increasing temperature for 300 K to 800 K confirmed the semiconducting nature of $CaMnO_3$, while for 8% and 17% Sm doping, the conductivity decreased with increasing temperature indicate the metallic nature of the materials. The magnitude of conductivity for 8% and 17% Sm doping is also several times higher than the magnitude of the conductivity for $CaMnO_3$ also confirming the metallic nature of Sm doping compared to the undoped $CaMnO_3$.

Fig. 2 (b) shows the Seebeck coefficient vs temperature for $CaMnO_3$, $Ca_{0.92}Sm_{0.08}MnO_3$ and $Ca_{0.83}Sm_{0.17}MnO_3$. The negative Seebeck coefficients for $CaMnO_3$ for the range of temperature of 300 K to 800 K indicate n type conductivity, as reported by Flahaut et. al. [9]. From Fig. 2 (b), it is noted that the intrinsic compound $CaMnO_3$ exhibits a large absolute value of S, which decreases with the increasing temperature. This implies the semiconductor electron transport behavior and in good agreement with experimental data for pure $CaMnO_3$ [9]. Doping with 8% and 17% Sm reduced the absolute magnitude of Seebeck coefficient but they are still in negative value from 300 K to 800 K implies n type conductivity. For 8% Sm doped $CaMnO_3$, the absolute value of Seebeck coefficient increased with increasing temperature from 300 K to 800 K implies metallic

behavior as confirmed from the band structure shown in Fig. 2 (c) and this behavior also seen in for 17% Sm doped $CaMnO_3$ initially for temperature range of 300 K to about 400 K.

Fig. 2 (c) shows the thermal conductivity vs temperature for $CaMnO_3$, $Ca_{0.92}Sm_{0.08}MnO_3$ and $Ca_{0.83}Sm_{0.17}MnO_3$. For intrinsic compound, the thermal conductivity decreases with increasing temperature. With 8% and 17% Sm doping, the magnitude of thermal conductivity increases for 8% Sm doping, showing more contribution from electronic thermal conductivity, as expressed from electrical conductivity using Wiedemann Franz's law but decreases with temperature for 17% Sm doping, suggesting 17% Sm doping is helpful in reducing thermal conductivity.

Fig. 2: The transport properties of $CaMnO_3$, $Ca_{0.92}Sm_{0.08}MnO_3$and $Ca_{0.83}Sm_{0.17}MnO_3$ (a) conductivity vs temperature, (b) Seebeck coefficient vs temperature, (c) thermal conductivity vs temperature and (d) the thermoelectric coefficient vs temperature.

Fig. 2 (d) shows the thermoelectric dimensionless figure of merit of $CaMnO_3$, $Ca_{0.92}Sm_{0.08}MnO_3$ and $Ca_{0.83}Sm_{0.17}MnO_3$ for temperature range of 300 K to 800 K. The $ZT$ values increase with increasing temperature for all compounds indicating that these compounds are suitable for high temperature energy conversion fields. However the $ZT$ value of 8% Sm doped compound show reduced $ZT$ magnitude compared to the intrinsic compound, due to reduced magnitude of Seebeck coefficient and higher thermal conductivity compare to the other compounds. The $ZT$ value of 17% Sm doped compound are remarkably higher than that of the intrinsic compound, and this confirm that the TE transport property could be really improved by rare earth elements doping at a 17% doping content. It is worth noting that the 17% Sm doped has $ZT$ value of 0.32 at 800 K which is much higher value of intrinsic compound ($ZT=0.07$) and 8% Sm doped compound ($ZT=0.004$).

## Summary

The first principles calculations and semi-classic Boltzmann theory are employed to investigate the Sm doping effect of electronic structure and thermoelectric properties of $CaMnO_3$. The AFMG phase is most stable among five phases for $CaMnO_3$, however, with 8% and 17% Sm doping,

nonmagnetic phases are most stable for these compounds. With 8% and 17% Sm doping, the bandstructure of these compound exhibit metallic behavior. All Seebeck coefficient vs temperature plots show electrons conduction for all compound and thermal conductivity is reduced in Sm doped compound. $CaMnO_3$ with 17% Sm doping exhibit much higher ZT of 0.32 at 800 K showing enhanced thermoelectric properties at high temperature.
