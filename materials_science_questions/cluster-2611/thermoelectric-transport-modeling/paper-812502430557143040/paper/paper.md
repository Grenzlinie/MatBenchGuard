### ABSTRACT

We report results of a detailed first-principles study of physical parameters associated with the structural, electronic, optical and elastic properties of the ternary gallium-arsenides $Sr_3GaAs_3$ and $Ba_3GaAs_3$. Calculated equilibrium structural parameters are in excellent agreement with the available experimental counterparts, providing evidence of the reliability of the reported results. Monocrystalline elastic constants are numerically estimated and analyzed. From the monocrystalline elastic constants, a set of related properties, viz. mechanical stability, anisotropic sound velocities, polycrystalline elastic properties, including bulk modulus, shear modulus, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, average sound velocity and Debye temperature, are deduced. Crystal direction dependences of the linear compressibility and Young's modulus are analyzed and visualized by plotting their spatial distributions. From analysis of the energy band dispersions, it is found that the title compounds are semiconductors with direct band gaps positioned in the visible sunlight spectrum in the energy window 1.271-1.285 eV. Origins of the electronic states composing the energy bands are determined using the PDOS diagrams. Effective masses of holes and electrons are numerically evaluated at the valence band and conduction band extremes towards the three major crystalline directions. Anisotropies of the hole and electron effective masses are visualized by plotting their dependencies on the crystalline direction. Frequency-dependent linear optical parameters are predicted in an energy window from 0 eV to 14 eV for incident electromagnetic radiation polarized parallel to the three principal crystalline directions.

### 1. Introduction

Many experimental and theoretical studies have recently been devoted to the crystal chemistry and physical properties of ternary pnictides in the systems $AE-Tr-Pn$, especially in the "3-1-3" composition, where $AE$ = alkaline-earth/rare-earth, viz. Ca, Sr, Ba, Eu, Yb; $Tr$ = triel elements, viz. Al, Ga, In; $Pn$ = pnictogen, viz. P, As, Sb. The many possibilities for modifying the chemical composition of the $AE-Tr-Pn$ systems by changing their constituents pave the way for the development of a large number of isomorphic materials providing possibilities to tune their properties to meet the requirements of technological applications. Interest to these systems is because of their outstanding wide range of technologically useful chemical and physical properties [1-23]. Almost exclusively, the $AE-Tr-Pn$ systems can be classified according to the concept of Zintl. Named after the German chemist Eduard Zintl [22], classic Zintl phases are a subset of salt-like intermetallic compounds resulting from the reaction of alkali/alkaline-earth metals with transition/post-transition/rare-earth

The first Zintl phases, which were synthetized and structurally characterized by Zintl and co-workers, are binary intermetallic compounds, where the constituent elements are alkali or alkaline-earth elements as cations and group 13–15 elements as anions [24,25]. The compositions of these compounds are simple ratios of their oxidation states, indicating the oxidation states of constituent elements obey the octet rule. Besides the ‘classical’ binary Zintl phases, numerous ternary and quaternary compounds, amongst them transition metal containing ones, obeying the Zintl rules, have been synthetized [26], such as Ca₁₁Sb₁₀, K₄Pb₉, Na₈Si₄₆, Ca₁₄AlSb₁, KBa₂InAs₃ Yb₁₄MnSb₁₁, Pr₄MnSb₉, Eu₁₀Mn₆Sb₁₃ and Cs₁₃Nb₂In₆As₁₀ [22]. It is conventionally assumed that the electropositive alkali/alkaline-earth metals; the cations, donate their valence electrons to the more electro-negative elements (anions) to have stable electronic configurations; similar to ionic salt-like compounds. In cases where the total number of electrons transferred from the cations is insufficient to satisfy the octet rule for anions, the anions complete their closed-shell configuration by forming covalent bonds within polyanionic substructure [27,28]. Thus, in Zintl compounds both ionic and covalent bonding are observed. In the Zintl concept, the transfer of electrons between the cations and the electronegative elements (anions) is typically considered to be “complete’’; all constituent atoms achieve a filled valence configurations.

In the last decade, Zintl phases have attracted more and more attention from both fundamental scientific and technological applica-tion viewpoints and consequently a large number of new Zintl com-pounds of various complex crystal structures and compositions have been synthesized. These new synthetized Zintl phases have a wide va-riety of interesting physical properties, such as metallic/semi-metallic and semiconductor natures, thermoelectricity, superconductivity, mag-netic order, colossal magnetoresistance, photovoltaics, energy storage, energy conversion, energy-related applications and mixed-valence, among others [29–49]. In addition, some Zintl phases have been identified as topological insulators and Dirac semimetals; materials of importance for their quantum properties [22]. Recently, Stoyko et al.[10] reported on the synthesis and crystal structures of the ternary gallium-arsenide Zintl phases Sr₃GaAs₃ and Ba₃GaAs₃. A preliminary ab initio calculation of the band structure of Ba₃GaAs₃ using the tight-binding linear muffin-tin orbital (TB-LMTO) method with the local density approximation reveals the semiconducting nature of this com-pound [10]. As far as we are aware of, expect [10], there are no prior reports on in-depth theoretical or experimental studies of the basic physical properties of the $AE_3GaAs_3$ ($AE$ = Sr, Ba) compounds, such as elastic constants, charge carrier effective masses, optical properties and anisotropy of physical properties. Thus, the present study is devoted to a compressive study of the structural, electronic, optical and elastic properties of the Zintl phases Sr₃GaAs₃ and Ba₃GaAs₃ via DFT-based first-principles calculations, for both fundamental physics and applica-tion perspectives. Note that one of the main roles of computational physics is to determine materials worthy of experimental research and to assess perspectives of potential applications.

This paper is divided into four sections. The state-of-the-art and aims of the present investigation has been given in Section 1. Calculation methods and settings are provided in Section 2. Detailed analyses and discussions of the results regarding the structural, elastic, electronic and optical properties of the studied compounds are the subject of Section 3. A summary of the main results of the present work is presented in the last section; Section 4.

## 2. Calculation methods and settings

DFT-based pseudopotential plane-wave (PP-PW) method with the GGA-PBEsol functional to treat the exchange-correlation effects [50], as implemented in the CASTEP package [51], is used to perform the required quantum mechanics calculations to determine the equilibrium crystalline structure parameters and elastic constants. Vanderbilt's ul-trasoft pseudopotentials [52] are used to describe Coulomb's interactions between the valence electrons; As: $4s^24p^1$; Ga: $3d^{10}4s^24p^1$; Sr: $4s^23p^2$; Ba: $5s^25p^66s^2$, and the pseudo-ionic cores (nuclei plus frozen core electrons). An energy cut-off of 350 eV for the plane-wave basis set and a $5 \times 3 \times 10$ special Monkhorst-Pack [53] $k$-points mesh for the Brillouin zone (BZ) sampling are adopted to ensure a sufficiently accu-rate total energy convergence.

As the first step in this work, the BFGS (Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shannon) algorithm [54] is used to determine the minimum energy crystal structure with the following convergence tolerance parameters: i. Self-consistent total energy within $5.0 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/atom, ii. Maximum force acting on each atom within 0.01 eV/Å, iii. Maximum stress within 0.02 GPa and iv. Maximum atomic displacement within $5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ Å.

The stress-strain method, which is based on the generalized Hooker's law [51], is used to calculate the monocrystalline elastic constants ($C_{ij}$s). Three different strain tensors; the first with non-zero $\varepsilon_{11}$ and $\varepsilon_{32}$ components, the second with non-zero $\varepsilon_{22}$ and $\varepsilon_{31}$ components and the third with non-zero $\varepsilon_{33}$ and $\varepsilon_{12}$ components, are applied to the equilibrium crystal structure for calculating the nine independent $C_{ij}$s required to describe the elastic properties of the studied orthorhombic systems. The $C_{ij}$s are computed with the following settings: i. Total energy convergence within $1.0 \times 10^{-6}$eV/atom, Hellman-Feynman force within 0.002 eV/Å and ionic displacement within $1.0 \times 10^{-4}$Å. Some physical properties related to the $C_{ij}$s, such as mechanical stability, elastic anisotropy, anisotropic sound wave velocities, and polycrystalline elastic moduli and related properties, such as bulk modulus, shear modulus, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, brittleness/ductility, average sound velocity and Debye temperature, are deduced from the $C_{ij}$s via the well-known empirical and theoretical relationships [55].

DFT-based full-potential linearized/augmented plane wave plus local orbitals (FP-L/APW + lo) with both the GGA-PBEsol and Tran-Blaha modified Becke-Johnson (TB-mBJ) potential [56,57], as imple-mented in the WIEN2k package [58], is used to solve the one-electron Kohn-Sham equations for computing the electronic structure and related properties. Note that the TB-mBJ functional was developed to improve the electronic structure of solids, especially for better quanti-tative estimates of semiconductors' and isolators' bandgaps. A cut-off parameter $l_{\text{max}} = 10$ for the spherical harmonics basis set and a recip-rocal lattice vector cut-off $K_{\text{max}} = 10/R_{MT}^{\text{min}}$ for the plane-wave basis set, where $R_{MT}^{\text{min}}$ is the smallest muffin-tin sphere, are selected to ensure a sufficiently precise total energy calculation. The muffin-tin sphere radii are Sr: 2.50 Bohr, Ba: 2.50 Bohr, Ga: 2.34 Bohr and As: 2.34 Bohr. The self-consistent calculations are considered convergent when the total energy is stable within $10^{-5}$ Ry and the force acting on each atom of the system is less than $5 \times 10^{-4}$ Ry/Bohr.

Linear optical responses of matter to incident electromagnetic radi-ation are described by the complex dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega)$; $\varepsilon(\omega) = \varepsilon_1(\omega) + i\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ [59], where the real part ($\varepsilon_1(\omega)$) and the imaginary one ($\varepsilon_2(\omega)$) describe, respectively, the dispersion and the absorption of the electromagnetic radiation by the medium which it crosses. $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ spectrum is calculated by summing the electric dipole operator matrix ele-ments between the occupied and unoccupied wave functions over the Brillouin zone while respecting the selection rules [60]. $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$ spectrum is calculated from that of $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$ using t Kramers-Kronig relationship [59]. Spectra of all the other linear optical parameters, including refractive index, extinction coefficient, reflectivity and absorption coefficient, are deduced from $\varepsilon_1(\omega)$and $\varepsilon_2(\omega)$spectra, using the well-known relationships [60].

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Ground-state crystal structure parameters

According to Ref. [10], the isotypic ternary gallium-arsenide Zintl--phases $AE_3GaAs_3$ ($AE$ = Sr, Ba) crystalize stably in the orthorhombic space group Pnma; no. 62, (Ba₃GaSb₃-type structure), with eight formula

units per unit-cell; $Z=8$. A schematic representation of the $\mathrm{Sr}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ unit-cell is given in Fig. 1(a). The prominent structural feature in the $A E_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ structure is that the $\mathrm{Ga}$ and $\mathrm{As}$ atoms form $\mathrm{GaAs}_{4}$ tetrahedra (Fig. 1(a)), which by connecting via two common edges form isolated $\left[\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{As}_{6}\right]$ units with covalent bonds (Fig. 1(b)). These isolated $\left[\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{As}_{6}\right]$ polyanions are separated from each other by the alkaline-earth $A E$ cations. The $\left[\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{As}_{6}\right]$ units are oriented along the $b$-axis, and are arranged in slabs shifted by $\frac{a}{4}$ [10]. The $A E$ cations both separate and counterbalance the charge of the polyanions $\left[\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{As}_{6}\right]$. Therefore, this structure can be rationalized as valence-precise $\left[A E^{2+}\right]_{3}\left[\mathrm{Ga}^{3+}\right]\left[\mathrm{As}^{3-}\right]_{3}$ or rather $\left[A E^{2+}\right]_{6}\left[\mathrm{Ga}_{2} \mathrm{As}_{6}\right]^{12-}$, i.e., as Zintl phases. It should be noted that the Zintl formalism oversimplifies the interactions between cations and anions, and the actual bonding pictures in these compounds could be far more complicated [13]. The $A E(\mathrm{Sr}, \mathrm{Ba})$ atoms occupy four inequivalent crystallographic positions referred to as $A E 1, A E 2, A E 3$ and $A E 4$, and there are also four kinds of As atoms labeled As1, As2, As3 and As4. Wyckoff positions and coordinates of atoms in the $A E_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ unit-cell are $A E 1: 8 d\left(x_{A 1}, y_{A 1}, z_{A 1}\right), A E 2: 8 d\left(x_{A 2}, y_{A 2}, z_{A 2}\right), A E 3: 4 c\left(x_{A 3}, 1 / 4\right.$, $\left.z_{A 3}\right), A E 4: 4 c\left(x_{A 4}, 1 / 4, z_{A 4}\right), \mathrm{Ga}: 8 d\left(x_{\mathrm{Ga}}, y_{\mathrm{Ga}}, z_{\mathrm{Ga}}\right)$, As1: $4 c\left(x_{\mathrm{As} 1}, 1 / 4\right.$, $\left.z_{\text {As1 }}\right)$, As2: $4 c\left(x_{\text {As2 }}, 1 / 4, z_{\text {As2 }}\right)$, As3: $8 d\left(x_{\text {As3 }}, y_{\text {As3 }}, z_{\text {As3 }}\right)$ and As4: $8 d\left(x_{\text {As4 }}\right.$, $\left.y_{\text {As4 }}, z_{\text {As4 }}\right)$. Thus, there are 26 free structural parameters, viz. three lattice parameters ( $a, b$ and $c$ ) and 23 internal coordinate parameters, not fixed by symmetry and consequently we should optimize them, i.e., determining their values that correspond to the minimal total energy, before exploring other physical properties. Using the experimental crystal structure data [10] as input, the equilibrium structural parameters are determined by relaxing the atomic positions and lattice parameters of the unit-cell by minimizing force and stress. The optimized crystal structure parameters obtained through the aforementioned technique and settings are collected in Table 1 (unit-cell volume and lattice parameters $a, b$ and $c$ ) and S1 (coordinates of atomic positions; see complementary information) along with the corresponding experimental counterparts from Ref. [10]. From Table 1 and S1, one appreciates that our results are in excellent agreement with the respective experimentally measured values, e.g., analysis of Table 1 data shows that the relative discrepancy between the computed value and the corresponding measured one in $\mathrm{Sr}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}\left(\mathrm{Ba}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}\right)$ is less than $-0.4 \%$ $(-0.3 \%)$ for the lattice parameter $a,-0.5 \%(-0.4 \%)$ for $b,-0.3 \%$ $(-0.3 \%)$ for $c$ and $-1.2 \%(-0.9 \%)$ for the unit-cell volume. In addition, relative change of the averaged unit-cell deviation $\left(d_{r}\right)$ defined as follows: $d_{r}=\left(V_{u c}^{(o p t) 1 / 3}-V_{u c}^{(\exp ) 1 / 3}\right) / V_{u c}^{(\exp ) 1 / 3}$ [61], where $V_{u c}^{(o p t)}$ and $V_{u c}^{(\exp )}$ are the optimized and experimental unit-cell volumes, can be used to give an average measure of the relative deviation of the optimized structure from the experimental counterpart. The very small value of $d_{r}$; $d_{r}=-0.39 \%$ for $\mathrm{Sr}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ and $d_{r}=-0.31 \%$ for $\mathrm{Ba}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$, provides a solid proof of the reliability of the theoretical method used for predicting the structural parameters and gives confidence in the reported results on the electronic, optical and elastic properties of the title compounds that will be presented in the following sections. One notes that the unit-cell parameters ( $a, b$ and $c$ ) are related to the sizes of the constituent elements. The unit-cell volume $(V)$ and lattice parameters $(a, b$ and $c)$ of $\mathrm{Ba}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ are larger than those of $\mathrm{Sr}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$. This is attributed to the fact that the atomic radium of $\mathrm{Ba}(2.53 \AA)$ is larger than that of $\mathrm{Sr}(2.19 \AA)$.

### 3.2. Electronic properties

#### 3.2.1. Energy band dispersions

The electronic structures of both studied compounds are calculated using the FP-LAPW method with the GGA-PBEsol and TB-mBJ functionals. Note that the TB-mBJ potential was developed to better describe the energy band dispersions and related properties, especially to overcome the failure of the common functionals; LDA and GGA, in determining the bandgap widths. The TB-mBJ potential dramatically improves bandgap values and even gives bandgap values in agreement with experimental counterparts for various classes of semiconductors [57,62]. Calculated energy band dispersions of both explored systems along a selected high symmetry path that connects points of high symmetry in the BZ; red line in Fig. 2, using the TB-mBJ potential at the optimized structural parameters are depicted in Fig. 3. One notes that the overall band profiles of the studied compounds are somewhat similar. Both the valence band maximum (VBM) and the conduction band minimum (CBM) occur at the BZ center; $\Gamma$-point, indicating that both investigated compounds are direct bandgap materials. The TB-mBJ (GGA-PBEsol) fundamental bandgap value is equal to $1.271 \mathrm{eV}(0.655$ $\mathrm{eV})$ for $\mathrm{Sr}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ and $1.285 \mathrm{eV}(0.572 \mathrm{eV})$ for $\mathrm{Ba}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$. Unfortunately, no experimental data regarding the bandgaps of the studied compounds have been reported so far up to date to be compared with our findings. Nevertheless, our GGA-PBEsol bandgap for $\mathrm{Ba}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ is in acceptable agreement with the unique reported theoretical result for this compound; $E_{\mathrm{g}}=0.78 \mathrm{eV}$, calculated using the tight-binding linear muffin-tin orbital method with the LDA functional [10] and our TB-mBJ bandgap for $\mathrm{Sr}_{3} \mathrm{GaAs}_{3}$ is in acceptable agreement with the unique reported value

### 3.2.2. Charge-carrier effective masses
In this work, the numerical estimates of the effective masses normalized to the free electron mass $m_0$ for holes and electrons; $m_h^*$ and $m_e^*$, respectively, are performed by fitting the energy band dispersion E (k) closely around the VBM and the CBM, respectively, to a parabola; E $(k)=Ak^2$, then the effective mass $m^*(m^*=m_h^*$ or $m_e^*)$ is deduced using the following relationship: $\frac{1}{m^*}=\frac{m_0}{\hbar^2}\left.\frac{\partial^2 E(k)}{\partial k^2}\right|_{k=k_0}$ for a direction k about an extreme point $k_0$ in the Brillouin zone. The calculated hole and electron effective masses ($m_h^*$ and $m_e^*$; in unit of free electron mass $m_0$) at the VBM and the CBM, which are located at the $\Gamma$-point, towards Y, X and Z points in BZ are gathered in Table 2. Note that the directions $\Gamma \to Y$, $\Gamma \to X$ and $\Gamma \to Z$ in the Brillouin zone correspond to the [100], [010] and [001] crystalline directions, respectively. One notes that the smallest hole effective mass is along the [010] direction, while the smallest electron one is along the [100] direction, which means that contribution of holes to the electrical current will be much larger than that of the same concentration of electrons along the [010] direction, while contribution of electrons will be larger than that of the same concentration of holes along the [100] direction. The heaviest effective mass of both the electrons and holes is along the [001] direction. It is known that the charge-carrier effective masses are inversely (directly) proportional with the electrical conductivity (the Seebeck coefficient). Thus, $Sr_3GaAs_3$ and $Ba_3GaAs_3$ compounds would have the smallest electrical conductivity, but the highest Seebeck coefficient along the [001] direction. Table 3 shows calculated Seebeck coefficient values for the title compounds at a temperature (T) equal to 300K and charge-carriers concentration (labeled n for electrons and p for holes) equal to $4 \times 10^{18} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ using the semi-classical Boltzmann theory, as implemented in the Boltztrap code [64]. From Table 3, one notes that the values of the Seebeck coefficients for the title compounds are of the same order of magnitude as those obtained for $Ca_3AlSb_3$, $Sr_3AlSb_3$ [65], $Sr_3AlAs_3$ and $Ba_3AlAs_3$ [23].

The hole and electron effective masses in an arbitrary direction infinitesimally close to the $\Gamma$-point for an orthorhombic system can be obtained through the following relationship [66]:

$$
m^{*}=\frac{1}{\cos ^{2}(\alpha) / \mathrm{m}_{[100]}^{*}+\cos ^{2}(\beta) / \mathrm{m}_{[010]}^{*}+\cos ^{2}(\gamma) / \mathrm{m}_{[001]}^{*}}
$$

where $\alpha, \beta$, and $\gamma$ are the angles between that specific direction and the [100], [010] and [001] crystalline directions, respectively. Figs. 4 and 5 illustrate 3D-representations of the crystalline direction dependencies of the effective masses of holes and electrons at the $\Gamma$-point and their cross-sections (2D-representations) in the (100), (010) and (001) crystalline planes. From these 3D- and 2D-represntations, it is obvious that the charge carrier effective masses of the title compounds are strongly anisotropic. Thus, transport parameters, such as mobility and Seebeck coefficient, are expected to be strongly anisotropic too. ### 3.2.3. Density of states

The calculated TDOS and PDOS are depicted in Fig. 6. The valence band of $\text{Sr}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ consists of six sub-bands labeled V1, V2, V3, V4, V5 and V6, while the valence band of $\text{Ba}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ is subdivided into seven sub-bands labeled V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, V6 and V7; the V7 sub-band is not shown for clarity of the figure. The V2 valence band group mainly originates from the overlapping As-4p and Ga-4p states, suggesting a significant covalent nature of the As–Ga chemical bond. #### 3.4.1. Monocrystalline elastic constants
The calculated full set monocrystalline elastic constants ($C_{ij}$s), viz. $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$, $C_{13}$, $C_{22}$, $C_{23}$, $C_{33}$, $C_{44}$, $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$, for the orthorhombic crystals Sr₃GaAs₃ and Ba₃GaAs₃, using the above-mentioned approach and settings, are given in Table 5. Unfortunately, to the best of authors knowledge, experimental and theoretical results concerning the elastic properties of the title compounds are still lacking in the scientific literature. Therefore, the present results are the first numerical estimations of the elastic moduli for the studied crystals. Some results can be extracted from Table 5 data.

1. The estimates values for the $C_{ij}$s of both studied compounds are smaller than 100 GPa, indicating the moderate resistance of these compounds to applied external deformations.
2. The $C_{ij}$s of Sr₃GaAs₃ are slightly higher than those of Ba₃GaAs₃, suggesting that the resistance to elastic deformations of Sr₃GaAs₃ is slightly higher than that of Ba₃GaAs₃. This indicates that values of the $C_{ij}$s do not change significantly with the radius of the alkali-earth atom in the title compounds.
3. $C_{11}$, $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$ reflect the resistance to uniaxial strains along the crystalline directions [100], [010] and [001], respectively; so, the difference between them is a measure of the anisotropy extent of the linear compressibility. Table 5 shows that the values of $C_{11}$, $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$ are somewhat different, indicating that the title materials are characterized by a relatively weak anisotropic linear compressibility, i.e., relatively weak directional dependence of the linear compressibility, a point we will return to later. The values of $C_{11}$, $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$, where$C_{11}>C_{22}>C_{33}$, suggest that the linear compressibility ($\beta$) along the [100] direction is smaller than that along the [010] direction which in turn is a bit smaller than that along the [001] direction; $\beta^{[100]}<\beta^{[010]}<\beta^{[001]}$.
4. The values of $C_{11}$, $C_{22}$ and $C_{33}$ are larger than those of $C_{44}$, $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$. Knowing that $C_{44}$, $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$ characterize the resistance to shear deformation in the (100), (010) and (001) planes, respectively, we expect that the title compounds are less resistant to shear deformations than to linear compression or attraction. On the other hand, $C_{44}$, $C_{55}$ and $C_{66}$ values are almost equal; so, one can draw a conclusion that the shear modulus of the title compounds is almost isotropic.
5. The values of all the $C_{ij}$s of both studied compounds satisfy the necessary and sufficient mechanical stability criteria for an orthorhombic crystal [69], confirming the mechanical stability of the explored compounds.
6. From the calculated $C_{ij}$s, one can deduce the velocities of elastic waves propagating inside the crystal. The calculated velocities of the elastic waves propagating along the three main crystalline directions [100], [010] and [001], using the well-known relationships [70] are listed in Table 5. One notes that the sound wave velocities of Sr₃GaAs₃ are somewhat greater than the corresponding ones of Ba₃GaAs₃, reflecting the fact that the elastic constants of Sr₃GaAs₃ are somewhat greater than those of Ba₃GaAs₃. On the other hand, transverse sound wave velocities are smaller than the longitudinal ones. ### 3.4.3. Elastic anisotropy
It is required to determine the extent of elastic anisotropy of solids as far as they are involved in applications because microcracks and mechanical failure can occur in solids with significant elastic anisotropy [78]. Some metrics have been developed to better highlight the elastic anisotropy extent of solids in order to find possible mechanisms that hopefully can prevent the occurrence of microcracks and mechanical failure. In the present work, to highlight the elastic anisotropy extent of the studied compounds, we have used two metrics that are widely used for this purpose, viz. universal anisotropy index and visualization of the crystalline direction dependence of the Young's modulus and linear compressibility.

(1) The universal anisotropy metric $A^{\text{U}}$ is given by the following expression: $A^{\text{U}} = 5\frac{G_V}{G_R} + \frac{B_V}{B_R} - 6$ [79]. $A^{\text{U}}$ is equal to zero in an isotropic crystal. Thus, the degree of deviation of $A^{\text{U}}$ from zero reflects the degree of elastic anisotropy. The calculated $A^{\text{U}}$ is equal to 0.037 in $\text{Sr}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ and 0.158 in $\text{Ba}_3\text{GaAs}_3$, suggesting that $\text{Sr}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ and $\text{Ba}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ will exhibit certain elastic anisotropy but $\text{Ba}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ will be much more elastically anisotropic than $\text{Sr}_3\text{GaAs}_3$.

(2) For an orthorhombic system, the dependencies of Young's modulus $(E)$ and linear compressibility $(\beta)$ on the crystal directions are expressed in the spherical coordinate system as follows [80]:

$$
\begin{align*}
\beta &= (S_{11} + S_{12} + S_{13})l_1^2 + (S_{12} + S_{22} + S_{23})l_2^2 \\
&+ (S_{13} + S_{23} + S_{33})l_3^2
\end{align*}
$$

Here $l_1$, $l_2$ and $l_3$ are the direction cosines, and $S_{ij}$s are the components of the compliance matrix. The three-dimensional (3D) closed surface (3D-contours) representing the crystal direction dependence of an isotropic elastic modulus will exhibit a perfect spherical shape. Thus, the degree of deviation of the shape of this closed surface from the spherical shape reflects the degree of anisotropy of the represented elastic modulus. 3D-contours of $E$ and $\beta$ as well as their planar contours (2D-contours) in the (100), (010) and (001) crystalline planes of the considered compounds are shown in Figs. 9 and 10, respectively. ## 4. Conclusion

In summary, the equilibrium crystal structure, elastic modules and related properties, electronic structures and linear optical properties of the ternary Zintl phases $\text{Sr}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ and $\text{Ba}_3\text{GaAs}_3$ are studied using DFT-based first-principles calculations. It is found that the PP-PW method with the GGA-PBEsol products accurate equilibrium lattice and positional parameters that are in excellent agreement with experimental data. The monocrystalline and polycrystalline elastic constants and related properties are evaluated. The title compounds are mechanically stable with moderate resistance to applied external deformations, brittle in nature and elastically somewhat anisotropic. From the energy band dispersions calculated using the FP-LAPW method with the TB-mBJ potential, it is found that both studied compounds are semiconductors with direct band gaps at the $\Gamma$-point, positioned in the visible sunlight spectrum. From analysis of diagrams of the density of states projected on orbitals and atoms, nature of the electronic states composing the valence and conduction bands is determined. It is found that the $s$ and $p$ states of the Ga and As atoms form the valence band, while the $\text{Sr}/\text{Ba}$ $d$-states form the bottom of the conduction band, the $\text{Ga-As}$ bond is dominantly of covalent nature and the binding of $AE$ (Sr/Ba) with the other constituent elements is mainly of ionic character. The calculated charge carrier effective masses at the energy band extremes exhibit a strong anisotropy. Linear optical coefficients are calculated for incident electromagnetic radiation polarized parallel to [100], [010] and [001] crystal directions. It is found that the optical properties will exhibit a noticeable anisotropy. The studied compounds have a strong absorption in the visible spectrum and Near-UV.
