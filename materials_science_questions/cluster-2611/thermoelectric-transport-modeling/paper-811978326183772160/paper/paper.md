# Shubnikov-de Haas Oscillations
in Fe- and Co-Doped HgSe under Pressure

By
E. A. NEIFELD, K. M. DEMCHUK, G. I. HARUS, A. E. BOUBNOVA,
L. I. DOMANSKAYA, G. L. SHTRAPENIN, and S. YU. PARANCHICH

(Received June 17, 1996; in revised form August 20, 1996)

We have investigated the effect of pressure on Shubnikov-de Haas oscillations in the gapless semiconductor HgSe doped with Fe and Co. For HgSe(Fe) an increase of pressure results in a decrease of the electron concentration in accordance with the Kane model for constant Fermi energy. In contrast, in HgSe(Co) the electron concentration is independent of pressure, i.e. the Fermi level is not pinned.

## 1. Introduction

Ions of 3d transition metals form isovalent substitutional impurities in II-VI semiconducting crystals. In wide-gap semiconductors with $\varepsilon_{\Gamma 6}>\varepsilon_{\Gamma 8}$ (energies $\varepsilon$ refer to band edge states at the zone center) the 3d levels fall, as a rule, into the energy gap. In zero-gap semiconductors $(\varepsilon_{\Gamma 6}<\varepsilon_{\Gamma 8})$ the d level lies in a background of band states. If the d states fall within the valence band, as for example in HgSe(Mn), the divalent Mn ions are electrically neutral and determine the magnetic properties only. However, if the d level lies above the bottom of the conduction band, as in the case of HgSe(Fe), the 3d ions act as donors, transferring electrons to band states. In this autoionization process the valence state of Fe changes: $Fe^{2+} \to Fe^{3+}+e^{-}$ . This happens for small concentrations $N_{Fe}$ of Fe ions until the Fermi energy $\varepsilon_{F}(n)$ approaches the d level. In HgSe(Fe) the d level energy is $\varepsilon_{d} ≈220 meV$ and the equality $\varepsilon_{F}(n_{0})=\varepsilon_{d}$ is reached at $n_{0} ≈4.5 ×10^{18} cm^{-3}$ [1]. For $N_{Fe}>n_{0}=N_{Fe}^{*}$ the so-called mixed-valency regime arises when a fraction of the Fe atoms remains divalent and the Fermi level is pinned at the resonant d level.

In zero-gap semiconductors hydrostatic pressure leads to a decrease in the absolute value of the energy gap $\varepsilon_{g}=|\varepsilon_{\Gamma 6}-\varepsilon_{\Gamma 8}|$ . As a consequence the electron effective mass and the density of conduction band states are decreased. The relation between electron concentration and the Fermi energy in the scope of the Kane model [2] is described by [3]

$$
\left(3 \pi^{2} n\right)^{2 / 3}=\frac{3}{Q} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}}(P)\right), \tag{1}
$$

where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}}(P)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}}^{0}-\beta P, Q$ is a Kane model parameter, $\beta=\partial \varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}} / \partial P$ is the band gap pressure coefficient. In the case of a fixed Fermi level the dependence of $n^{2 / 3}$ on $P$ should be linear.

In [4] is was shown that doping of HgSe with Cr, Co, and Ni is not accompanied by an increase of the electron density for impurity concentrations larger than a certain value $N^*$. It was assumed that these ions also form resonant donor states in the conduction band as is the case for HgSe(Fe). In the present paper we report the results of our studies of Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) oscillations in HgSe(Fe) and HgSe(Co) under hydrostatic pressure.

## 2. Experimental Details
Three samples were used in this study: HgSe(Fe) with Fe concentrations of $N_{\mathrm{Fe}}=5 \times 10^{19} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ (sample 1) and $N_{\mathrm{Fe}}=1 \times 10^{19} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ (sample 2) and HgSe(Co) with $N_{\mathrm{Co}}=5 \times 10^{18} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$. In the HgSe(Fe) samples the inequality $N_{\mathrm{Fe}}>N_{\mathrm{Fe}}^{*}$ was fulfilled; in the HgSe(Co) sample $N_{\mathrm{Co}}>N_{\mathrm{Co}}^{*}$ as well, assuming that $N_{\mathrm{Co}}^{*} \approx 1 \times 10^{18} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$ [4]. The samples were placed in a He gas pressure cell. Magnetoresistance measurements were carried out up to 5 kbar at 4.2 K in a superconducting solenoid under continuous scanning of the magnetic field.

## 3. Results and Discussion
Fig. 1a and b show the oscillating components of the longitudinal magnetoresistance of the two samples of HgSe(Fe) at different pressures. The positions of SdH maxima depend on pressure. The spin splitting of the oscillation maxima for sample 1 increases with increasing pressure. For sample 2 the splitting is not observed at atmospheric pressure, but appears at $P=3$ kbar. In Fig. 2 the magnetoresistance oscillations of HgSe(Co) are shown at zero pressure and 5 kbar. In this case, pressure has no effect on the position of the extrema in the magnetic field. In Table 1 we list the values of the conductivity $\sigma$, the electron concentration $n$ as determined from the SdH oscillation periods, the electron mobility $\mu(=\sigma / e n)$, and the Dingle temperature $T_{\mathrm{D}}$ for the three samples studied at different pressures.

Fig. 3 shows $n^{2 / 3}$ as a function of pressure for the three samples studied. For the HgSe(Fe) samples we find linear dependencies, in accordance with (1) for constant $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}(P)$. The pressure coefficients $\beta$ for the HgSe(Fe) samples are almost the same, $\beta=7.7 \mathrm{meV} / \mathrm{kbar}$ for sample 1 and $\beta=8.0 \mathrm{meV} / \mathrm{kbar}$ for sample 2.

For HgSe(Co) the electron concentration is nearly independent of pressure. Thus, according to (1) the Fermi level is increased from 150 to 163 meV in the pressure interval under study. Qualitatively, the different $n(P)$ behavior for HgSe(Fe) and HgSe(Co) shows that there is no resonant d level in HgSe(Co). This is consistent with EPR measurements [5], which show no evidence for the presence of $\mathrm{Co}^{3+}$ ions. Thus, we conclude that Co impurities do not act as donors in HgSe, and that the conduction electron density must originate from other defects.

Although the linear $n^{2 / 3}(P)$ dependence is valid for the both samples of HgSe(Fe), the Fermi level is pinned at different energy values. This difference, $\delta \varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}} \approx 5$ to $6 \mathrm{meV}$, is due to the spread of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{d}}$ values caused by random electrostatic fields of the charged centers. The spread lies within the estimated value $\Delta \varepsilon_{\mathrm{d}} \sim e^{2} n^{2 / 3} / \varkappa$, where $\varkappa$ is the dielectric constant.

We briefly consider the effect of pressure on the electron mobility and Dingle temperature. The electron relaxation time for scattering by random Coulomb centers is [7]

$$
\tau \sim \frac{k_{\mathrm{F}}^{3}}{N_{\mathrm{i}} m_{\mathrm{F}}}, \tag{2}
$$

where $k_{\mathrm{F}}^{3}=3 \pi^{2} n$, $N_{\mathrm{i}}$ is the concentration of charged centers. Taking into account that $n=N_{\mathrm{i}}$ for uncompensated samples, the mass $m_{\mathrm{F}}$ at the Fermi level is the only quantity

The reduction of the Dingle temperature $T_{D}$ with pressure in HgSe(Fe) is essentially
due to the decrease of $m_{F}$ in the mixed-valence regime as well. Unfortunately, it is diffi-
cult to theoretically estimate $T_{D}$, since, apart from the collisional broadening $\Gamma \sim h / \tau$ of
the Landau levels, other factors, for example the spatial nonuniformity of the electron
concentration, can contribute to $T_{D}$.

The present pressure studies of the electron concentration allow us to conclude that the
Co $(3d^{7})$ level is not a resonant donor state in HgSe, in contrast to the Fe $(3d^{6})$ level. The
background electron concentration in HgSe(Co) originated from nonstoichiometric defects
or the presence of other impurities. Optical studies in wide-gap II-VI semiconductors have
shown that the d level is shifting monotonically towards higher energy in the series from Cu
to Sc [8], with the exception of divalent Mn which has a very stable half-filled d shell. It may
be assumed that this tendency is also valid for zero-gap II-VI semiconductors. Thus, we can
expect that the d levels of divalent Ti, V and, possibly, Cr are located at higher energies
compared to the corresponding d level of Fe (see also Fig. 7 in [8]). Therefore, Ti and V are
possible candidates for forming resonant d levels in the zero-gap semiconductor HgSe.
