# Study of electronic structure and Compton profiles of PbS and PbSe

N.L. Heda $^{a}$ , A. Rathor $^{a}$ , V. Sharma $^{a}$ , G. Ahmed $^{a}$ , Y. Sharma $^{b}$ , B.L. Ahuja $^{a, *}$

$^{a}$ Department of Physics, University College of Science, M. L. Sukhadia University, Udaipur 313001, Rajasthan, India
$^{b}$ Department of Physics, Feroze Gandhi College, Rae Bareli 229001, UP, India

Received 23 August 2007; accepted 10 September 2007
Available online 14 September 2007

## Abstract
In this paper, we report the energy bands, density of states and the first ever Compton profiles of PbS and PbSe computed in the framework of spin-polarised relativistic Korringa-Kohn-Rostoker and $a b$ initio pseudopotential with Hartree-Fock and density functional theory schemes. In addition, we have computed the energy bands and density of states using full-potential linearised augmented plane wave method. To compare our theoretical data, we have also measured the first ever isotropic Compton profiles at an intermediate resolution using our $740 GBq(20 Ci)^{137} Cs$ Compton spectrometer. The Compton profiles scaled on equal-valence-electron-density show that PbSe is more covalent than PbS, which is in agreement with the present Mulliken's population data.

## 1. Introduction
Due to a great interest in the fundamental properties and technological importance of the IV-VI lead chalcogenides (PbX where X=S, Se and Te), a considerable experimental and the- oretical work has been undertaken. An earlier review up to1985 that mainly includes band structures, densities of states, electronic energy levels, X-ray photoemission spectra, etc. is reported by Cohen and Chelikowsky [1]. Lach-hab et al. [2] have studied the mechanical properties and the electronic structure of lead chalcogenides using the scalar-relativistic full-potential lin- earised augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method. The authors have employed the density functional theory (DFT) with the local density approximation (LDA) and the generalised gradi- ent approximation (GGA). Nabi et al. [3] have observed the dependences on hydrostatic pressure of the energy gaps for these chalcogenides using the FP-LAPW within the local spin den- sity approximation (LSDA). $A b$ initio calculations of the optical properties of the PbX have been reported by Delin et al. [4].

These calculations have been performed with a relativistic full- potential linear muffin-tin orbital (LMTO) method within the LDA. Wei and Zunger [5] have employed the general-potential linearised APW method with LDA to compute the electronic and structural anomalies in the lead chalcogenides. Using the CRYS- TAL code of Torino group, PbS has been studied by different groups [6-8] for its physical, elastic and electronic properties(except its momentum densities, energy bands, density of states, band gap, etc.).

Compton scattering has been used extensively over the years to probe the ground-state electron momentum distribution in metals, alloys and compounds [9]. This technique is insensitive to the surface effects or the crystal defects. The Compton pro- file, $J(p_{z})$ , is the one-dimensional projection of the ground-state momentum density $n(\vec{p})$ on the scattering vector ( $z$ -axis of the coordinate system) and is given as
$$J\left(p_{z}\right)=\iint n(\vec{p}) \mathrm{d} p_{x} \mathrm{~d} p_{y}\qquad(1)$$
where
$$n(\vec{p})=\sum_{i}\left|\int \psi_{i}(\vec{r}) \exp (-\mathrm{i} \vec{p} \cdot \vec{r}) \mathrm{d} r\right|^{2}$$

The summation extends over all the occupied states and $\psi_{i}(\vec{r})$ is the electron wave function in real space. Therefore, the $J(p_{z})$ contains the fingerprints of the Fermi surface, bonding, etc.

To compute the first ever theoretical Compton profiles, we have used the CRYSTAL03 package [10-12] within the framework of pseudopotential with (a) Hartree-Fock (HF), (b) DFT with LDA and GGA and (c) hybrid combination of DFT and HF schemes and also spin-polarised relativistic Korringa-Kohn-Rostoker (SPR-KKR) method [13]. It is worth mentioning here that for the heavy materials, the pseudopo- tential reduces the computational effort. They minimise the requirement of relativistic treatment of core electrons, which is difficult to implement within periodic Hartree-Fock theory [6]. In addition to momentum densities, we have also computed the energy bands and density of states (DOS) using pseudopo- tential, SPR-KKR and full-potential linearised augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) [14] techniques. In order to gain a systematic experimental insight into the electronic structure of PbS and PbSe and the relative nature of bonding in these compounds, in this paper, we also present the first ever experimental Compton profiles of these semiconductors. For the present measurements, we have used our $20 Ci^{137} Cs$ Compton spectrometer. The choice of polycrystalline samples was due to (a) difficulty in getting the large size (15 mm dia. and 3 mm thick) single crystals and (b) study of relative nature of bonding in these compounds using their equal-valence-electron-density (EVED) profiles.

## 2. Theory

### 2.1. Linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO) calculations

We have computed the directional Compton profiles for valence and core electrons using the $a b$ initio CRYSTAL03 pack age of Torino group [10-12]. This code is an implementation of Hartree-Fock (HF) method and is reliable for computing the electronic structure and associated ground-state properties. To calculate the electronic structure and the momentum densities of PbS and PbSe, a variety of schemes of exchange and correlation, viz. HF, DFT with LDA and GGA and Becke's three-parameter hybrid functional (B3LYP) embodied a posteriori in pseudopo- tential (PP) approach have been adopted. It is worth noting that the B3LYP hybrid function is a modified version of Becke's original three-parameter exchange-correlation functional [10].

The one electron Hamiltonian operator in PP-HF scheme is given as [10-12]
$$
\hat{h}_{\mathrm{PP}}=\hat{t}+\hat{c}+\hat{x}_{\mathrm{HF}}+\hat{e}_{\mathrm{PP}}
\tag{2}
$$
where $\hat{t}, \hat{c}, \hat{x}_{HF}$ and $\hat{e}_{PP}$ are kinetic, Coulomb, exchange and effective core pseudopotential (ECP) operators, respectively. Therefore, in case of HF the electron correlation effects are not included. In case of PP-DFT, the $\hat{x}_{HF}$ is replaced by Kohn-Sham exchange-correlation potential operator $(\hat{x}_{KS})$ .

For PbS, the ECP was used to describe the core part of Pb. TheGaussian basis sets were taken for $S^{2-}$ (all electrons) and $Pb^{2+}$ (valence only) from the work of Mian et al. [6]. In case of PbSe, the optimisation of basis sets within PP-HF theory was done for $Se^{2-}$ (all-electron) and $Pb^{2+}$ (valence). Initially, $Pb^{2+}$ basissets were taken from Mian et al. [6] while the basis sets of $Se^{2-}$  were taken from Ref. [15]. Due to non-availability of all-electron basis sets for $Pb$ , only their valence part was considered. We have optimised the basis sets of PbSe by using BILLY programme as mentioned in the CRYSTAL03 package. The optimised basis sets for PbSe are given in Table 1. The lattice constants for PbS and PbSe with space group $F m \overline{3} m$ (rock salt structure) were taken as 5.636 and $6.124 \AA$ [16], respectively. Using the standard truncation criteria for the CRYSTAL03 code, the self-consistent- field (SCF) calculations have been performed at 256 points in the irreducible Brillouin zone (BZ).

In PP-HF, the exact exchange of the HF [10] has been taken while the correlations were neglected. In the PP-DFT with the LDA approach, Dirac-Slater exchange [12] and Perdew-Zunger correlations [17] were adopted. In case of PP-DFT with the GGA, exchange of Becke [18] and Perdew-Wang correlations[19] were taken. In the PP-B3LYP approach, the standard Beckeexchange [18] and Lee-Yang-Parr correlations [20] with 20% mixing of the HF exchange were used.

### 2.2. Spin-polarised relativistic Korringa-Kohn-Rostoker(SPR-KKR) calculation

To compute the Compton profiles of PbS and PbSe, we have also used Green's function formalism SPR-KKR [13] in the atomic sphere approximation mode. In this scheme the multiple scattering theory has been adopted and the electronic structure is represented by the electronic Green's function $G(r, r^{\prime}, E)$ instead of Bloch function and eigen energies [13]. In our computations, the Vosko-Wilk-Nusair [21] LDA to model the exchange- correlation potential along with the BROYDEN algorithm have been used. For the self-consistency $834 \vec{k}$ have been considered in the $1 / 48$ th part of the Brillouin zone. The respective muffin-tin radii were taken to be 3.213 and $2.396 \AA$ for $Pb$ and $S$ in case of PbS, and 3.193 and $2.593 \AA$ for $Pb$ and $Se$ in case of PbSe. For the expansion of the Green function in local orbitals a cut-off of $l_{ max }=2$ was taken. Using the standard methodology, we have computed the majority spin $(J_{\uparrow})$ and minority spin $(J_{\downarrow})$ valencedirectional Compton profiles along the [1 00 $],[\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 0\end{array}]$ and $[\begin{array}{lll}1 & 1 & 1\end{array}]$  directions. On the contrary to magnetic Compton profiles, to evaluate the valence charge Compton profiles we have added the contribution of majority and minority spins. To get the total Compton profile in each principal direction, we have taken the core part from the free atom tables of Biggs et al. [22] and added it to the spherically averaged valence profile.

## 4. Results and discussion

In Figs. 1 and 2, we have presented the energy bands and DOS in the energy range −14 to +8 eV for PbS and PbSe, respectively, using FP-LAPW method. Separately, we have seen that the overall behaviour of the band structure computed using SPR-KKR, various schemes of LCAO and FP-LAPW are similar for both the compounds except for the value of their band gap. A resemblance between the present energy bands and those reported by Lach-hab et al. [2] and Delin et al. [4] was also observed. For both the compounds the minimum band gap is found at the point L. As can be seen from DOS curves, the Bloch states that have the sp components of S and Pb, dominantly, lie in the present energy range. The previous results of band gap data [2,3,5,29] at $L$ and $\Gamma$ points together with the present data are summarised in Table 3. Separately, we have seen that the band gap in both the samples using LCAO-PP-HF scheme is overestimated. This trend is consistent with the general observations (over estimation of band gap) about the HF calculations. Therefore, we have not incorporated the band gap due to PP-HF and also B3LYP (20% mixing of HF) in Table 3. It is worth mentioning here that to reproduce an exact experimental value of band gap [29], Wei and Zunger [5] have adjusted the potential in their calculation. The band gaps predicted by our LCAO calculations are larger than those reported by earlier workers [2,3,5]. The band gaps computed by us using SPR-KKR and FP-LAPW calculations are in agreement with the available data. The difference in the band gap values computed using LCAO schemes might be due to the pseudopotential basis sets for $Pb^{2+}$ employed in the LCAO schemes.

In Figs. 3 and 4, we have plotted the unconvoluted anisotropies of PbS and PbSe, respectively for (a) $J_{111}$-$J_{110}$, (b) $J_{111}$-$J_{100}$ and (c) $J_{110}$-$J_{100}$ as computed using various schemes of LCAO (namely PP with HF, DFT-LDA, DFT-GGA and B3LYP) and also SPR-KKR. From both the figures, it is seen that the overall shape of profiles is quite similar in all the LCAO-based calculations. In case of SPR-KKR the magnitude of anisotropies and their crossover on $p_{z}$ scale are significantly different than LCAO-based profiles. In case of PbS, the bond population between nearest-neighbour atoms (Pb–S) is negligible (0.005 electron) while that for PbSe it comes as 0.078 electrons. Therefore, due to relatively large amount of overlap charge in PbSe, it is more covalent than PbS. It may be noted that Mulliken’s population can be taken to be reliable in the present case because the overlap population is relatively low for both the compounds and the external atomic orbitals of Pb, S and Se are not too diffuse, viz. exponents of the external basis set ($\alpha$) was 0.3 for each Pb and S in case of PbS, while in case of PbSe these were 0.1176 and 0.1379 for Pb and Se, respectively.

To compare the nature of bonding in the isoelectronic compounds, one can use their Compton profiles at equal-valence-electron densities (EVED). The similarity in the equal-valence-electron-density (EVED) profiles (on $p_z/p_F$ scale) may suggest that to first order the wave function for bonding electrons in isoelectronic materials would be identical if written as a function of $r/a$, where $a$ is the lattice parameter. According to Reed and Eisenberger [30] the effect of bonding in isovalent compounds like semiconductors can be visualized with a reduction of momentum density at $p_z/p_F$=0 a.u. This method was very successful in detecting the nature of bonding in isovalent semiconductor compounds (see, for example, Refs. [30–32]). In Fig. 6, the scaled valence (EVED) profiles of both the isovalent compounds derived from their PP-DFT-LDA and experimental profiles are shown. All the EVED profiles were normalised to five electrons (valence) in the momentum range 0–7 a.u. It is seen from Fig. 6 that near $p_z$=0 a.u. both the theoretical and the experimental EVED profiles of PbSe are higher than that of PbS. It suggests more localisation of charge in PbSe in comparison to PbS in the bonding direction. Since sharpness of EVED $J(p_z)$ is measure of covalent character, we conclude that the PbSe is more covalent than the PbS. It is in agreement with the conclusions of our Mulliken’s population analysis.

### 5. Conclusions

The isotropic Compton profiles of lead chalcogenides namely PbS and PbSe are presented. A considerable amount of band structure data including energy bands, band gaps, density of states, momentum densities using *ab initio* pseudopotential, SPR-KKR and FP-LAPW schemes are presented. It is seen that the SPR-KKR data give a better agreement with the measurements as compared to other theories. An analysis of equal-valence-electron-density profiles confirms that PbSe is more covalent (hence less ionic) than PbS. The experimental directional Compton profiles of these rock salt compounds are required to yield more information about the utility of SPR-KKR in semiconductor compounds. Further, the all-electron basis sets of Pb may improve the band calculations using the LCAO scheme.
