# Momentum density distributions in $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$

Y Kubo† and S Wakoh‡

† Department of Physics. College of Humanities and Sciences. Nihon University.
Sakurajosui-3. Setagaya-ku. Tokyo 156. Japan
‡ University for Library and Information Science. Yatabe-machi, Ibaraki 305. Japan

Received 17 February 1986. in final form 15 May 1986

**Abstract.** Self-consistent energy band calculations of $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$ are carried out by the symmetrised augmented plane wave (SAPW) method. In the construction of a one-electron potential, the local-density approximation is used. Momentum density distributions determined by Compton scattering and positron annihilation are calculated by the SAPW method. These anisotropies are due not only to the partially occupied bands but also to the fully occupied ones. The calculated anisotropy in angular correlation curves of positron annihilation radiation is in good agreement with the recent experimental result. The corresponding calculated result in Compton profile is also in good agreement with the recent experimental result. But in contrast to the case of elemental transition metals, the calculated Compton profiles and angular correlation curves of positron annihilation radiation show anisotropies of different structures. It is shown that this feature originates from the localised d electron states of $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$.

## 1. Introduction

The metallic compounds $\text{Ni}_3\text{Al}$ and $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$, which crystallise in the $\text{L1}_2$ structure, show a Stoner-type behaviour in the magnetic properties. These materials show a gradual transition from Pauli paramagnetism to band ferromagnetism by varying the composition. The critical nickel concentration is about 74.6 at.% in $\text{Ni}_3\text{Al}$ and about 75.8 at.% in $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$. Their magnetic and related properties have been measured (de Boer *et al* 1969, Fawcett *et al* 1970, Fluitman *et al* 1973, de Dood and de Châtel 1973), but an adequate theoretical explanation of them remains to be given. Recently, the generalised spin density fluctuation theory (Moriya 1985) has been advanced. However, whether or not this theory can correctly account for the measured properties of $\text{Ni}_3\text{Al}$ and $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$ is still uncertain.

To perform realistic quantitative tests of theory, correct information on the electronic energy band structure is required. The electronic energy band structures of $\text{Ni}_3\text{Al}$ and $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$ were first calculated by Fletcher (1972) using Hubbard's method in a non-self-consistent scheme. However, in metallic compounds, a self-consistent calculation is particularly needed to obtain a correct Coulomb part of the one-electron potential. Recently, Buiting *et al* (1983) performed a self-consistent calculation on $\text{Ni}_3\text{Al}$ by the ASW method. The d band width of their $\text{Ni}_3\text{Al}$ is narrower than that of Fletcher (1972). In $\text{Ni}_3\text{Ga}$, the same trend is also found in both more recent self-consistent calculation by the LMTO method (Hayden *et al* 1986) and our calculation.

In order to study the band structures in metals and alloys, evaluation of the physical properties directly related to their ground states (Fermi surfaces, density of states, charge density distributions and momentum density distributions etc) is necessary. Compton scattering experiments and positron annihilation techniques are very fruitful methods for the study of electronic structures in metals and alloys, such as momentum density distributions (denoted MDD), shapes of Fermi surfaces etc (Williams 1977, Hautojärvi 1979). Recently, Itoh *et al* (1984) performed a measurement of the Compton profiles (denoted CP) in $\mathrm{Ni_3Ga}$. Very recently, Shiotani *et al* (1986) measured the angular correlation of positron annihilation radiation (denoted ACAR) in $\mathrm{Ni_3Ga}$. In a previous paper (Kubo and Wakoh 1983), we have published preliminary results for the MDD of $\mathrm{Ni_3Ga}$. In this paper more elaborate results will be shown. This paper is concentrated on the comparison between theory and experiment in the one-dimensional MDD, since the comparison between theory and experiment in the two-dimensional ACAR is given by Shiotani *et al*(1986).

In $\S 2$ the band structure of $\mathrm{Ni_3Ga}$ is calculated by a self-consistent APW method and the Fermi surface structure is decided. In $\S 3$ the computational procedure of the MDD for CP and ACAR are described. In $\S 4$, the theoretical results are given together with the comparisons between theory and experiment. In $\S 5$ the relation between the anisotropies in CP and ACAR and the electronic structure is discussed.

## 2. Band structure and Fermi surface

The symmetrised APW method is employed to calculate the energy bands and the wavefunctions in $\mathrm{Ni_3Ga}$. A crystal potential is determined self-consistently using the exchange correlation functional of Gunnarson and Lundqvist (1976). The radii of the APW spheres for Ni and Ga sites are determined in such a way that a nearest-neighbour pair touch, and that at the point of touching the potential values for Ni and Ga become equal. The lattice constant is assigned to be 6.7580 au, and the determined APW spheres for Ni and Ga are 2.2974 au and 2.4812 au respectively. In every iteration, we employed 35 $k$-points in $\frac{1}{48}$ of the Brillouin zone. The self-consistent APW scheme was carried out until the energy eigenvalues were fixed within 0.001 Ryd. The constant potential outside the muffin-tin spheres is determined by averaging the potential values in the region between the muffin-tin spheres.

In figure 1, the self-consistent energy band structure is shown. In the figure, the thirteen energetically lowest bands are fully occupied and the next six bands are partially occupied. The lowest three bands are sp-like ones, the next lowest six bands are p-d hybridised ones and the eight bands further up, which consist of localised electron states, are predominantly derived from d orbitals of Ni atoms. The 18th and 19th bands are sp-like ones and are occupied only in a small region surrounding the R point. The density of states curve is shown in figure 2. This result is very similar to that by Hayden *et al*(1986). By accommodating 33 electrons per unit cell, the Fermi energy (denoted $E_{\text{F}}$) is determined and shown in figures 1 and 2 by dotted lines. From the density of states at the $E_{\text{F}}$, the coefficient $\gamma$ of the electronic specific heat is estimated as $\gamma_{\text{th}}=3.2\ \text{mJ}\ \text{mol}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-2}$. This value is similar to the theoretical value $3.7\ \text{mJ}\ \text{mol}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-2}$ of Hayden *et al* (1986), while the observed values are $\gamma_{\text{ex}}=5.7$ (Fawcett *et al* 1970) and $\gamma_{\text{ex}}=6.0$ (de Dood and de Châtel 1973) in units of $\text{mJ}\ \text{mol}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-2}$. The theoretical values are substantially lower than the experimental values. This discrepancy may lead to the importance of enhanced magnetic

## 3. Calculations of momentum density distributions

Energy values and wavefunctions were calculated by the APW method at 35 *k*-points in $\frac{1}{48}$ of the Brillouin zone. The number of APWs used is about 270 and the maximum value of *l* is 6. In the calculations of the momentum wavefunctions, the wavefunctions within the APW spheres are obtained using *l* up to 4 (this restriction was ascertained to be a good approximation).

### 3.1. Compton profiles

The momentum wavefunction $\chi_{b,k}^{-}(k+K_{n})$ of CP at point *k* in the *b*th band with reciprocal lattice vector $K_{n}$ is determined for each of the 35 *k*-points up to $2109\ K_{n}$ Brillouin zones (this corresponds to $p(\text{max})\simeq8.0$ au as the cut-off of the momentum expansion). The Compton profile due to the valence electrons is calculated within the impulse approximation (Chew 1952) and is given by

$$
J^{-}(p_{z})=\int\int \mathrm{d}p_{x}\,\mathrm{d}p_{y}\sum_{b}\sum_{k}\theta(E_{\text{F}}-E_{b}(k))|\chi_{b,k}^{-}(p)|^{2}. \tag{1}
$$

The integration is carried out by the summation of $|\chi|^{2}$ over each occupied *k*-point, selected by the linear interpolation scheme in each cubic mesh (Kubo and Wakoh 1983). In the present case, 512 *k*-points are used in each cubic mesh. As for the contribution from the core electrons to the CP, it is evaluated from free-atom wavefunctions obtained by density functional formalism (Gunnarson and Lundqvist 1976).

### 3.2. Positron annihilation profiles

The one dimensional ACAR (denoted 1D-ACAR) is expressed in the independent particle model (Berko and Plaskett 1958) as

$$
J^{+}(p_{z})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-q}^{q} \mathrm{d}p_{x}\,\mathrm{d}p_{y}\sum_{b}\sum_{k}\theta(E_{\text{F}}-E_{b}(k))|\chi_{b,k}^{+}(p)|^{2}. \tag{2}
$$

The momentum wavefunction $\chi_{b,k}^{+}(k+K_{n})$ is determined at each of the 35 *k*-points up to only $515\ K_{n}$ Brillouin zones (this corresponds to $p(\text{max})\simeq5.0$ au as the cut-off of the momentum expansion), since the electron-positron momentum density distribution is less extended in the momentum space than that of the electrons themselves. In the calculation of the $\chi_{b,k}^{+}(k+K_{n})$ two kinds of positron wavefunctions are used. The first is expressed by only one angular momentum term ($l=0$) within the APW spheres and by a single wave ($K_{n}=0$) outside the spheres, and is denoted as $\psi_{\text{s}}^{+}(r)$. The second is expressed by three terms ($l=0$, 2 and 4) within the spheres and 33 plane waves outside the spheres, and is denoted $\psi_{\text{t}}^{+}(r)$. Other numerical values used here are the same as those in the case of the CP.

## 5. Discussions
The anisotropies in CP and those in ACAR are very different from each other, as seen in figures 5(b) and 7(b). This situation is in contrast with the case of elementary transition

metals (Wakoh and Yamashita 1973, Wakoh *et al* 1975a, b, 1976, Shiotani *et al* 1977). In the case of the transition metals, the topology of the Fermi surface dominantly contributes to the anisotropies in CP and ACAR. In the case of $Ni_3Ga$, however, the situation looks different. As conjectured from figures 5(b) and 7(b), partially occupied bands which construct the Fermi surfaces do not mainly contribute to the anisotropies in CP and ACAR. In order to examine this origin in detail, the band-by-band contributions to the anisotropies in the CP and the ACAR are evaluated for the $J(110)-J(100)$ of which experiments have been carried out and satisfactorily reproduced by the present theoretical calculations. The partial contributions to the anisotropies from the first band up to 17th band are shown in figure 9. The contributions from the 18th and 19th bands are negligibly small and not shown in the figure. Now in the case of the ACAR (Hautojärvi 1979), the positron preferentially annihilates with sp-like electrons and less preferentially annihilates with localised electrons. As seen in figure 9, the anisotropy curves from the 1st to the 3rd bands are similar in both the CP and the ACAR. This is due to their sp-like bands. The different shapes between the anisotropy curves in both cases begin to appear in the next eight bands (from the 4th to the 11th). In the next six bands (from the 12th to the 17th), the difference becomes remarkable. Furthermore, it is noted that the shape of the anisotropy in the ACAR does not vary much from the 12th band to the 17th band, in contrast with the case of the CP. This means that the topology of the Fermi surfaces does not contribute much to the anisotropy in the ACAR.

As mentioned in § 2, the six bands (from the 12th to the 17th) consist of strongly localised d electron states and this is regarded as the origin of the small variation of the anisotropy of the ACAR mentioned above. In order to make this feature clear, a folded MDD $M^{\pm}(p_y,p_z)$ to the first Brillouin zone which corresponds to a two-dimensional extension of LCW (Lock *et al* 1973) is calculated. This is given by the following equation (Berko *et al* 1980):

$$
M^{\pm}\left(p_{y}, p_{z}\right)=\sum_{K_{y}} \sum_{K_{z}} J^{\pm}\left(p_{y}+K_{y}, p_{z}+K_{z}\right)
\tag{3}
$$

$$
J^{\pm}\left(p_{y}+K_{y}, p_{z}+K_{z}\right)=J^{\pm}\left(q_{y}, q_{z}\right)=\int \mathrm{d} q_{x} \sum_{b} \sum_{k} \theta\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}-E_{b}(k)\right)\left|\chi_{b, k}^{ \pm}(q)\right|^{2}
\tag{4}
$$

where $K_y$ and $K_z$ are the components of the reciprocal lattice vectors on the $(p_y,p_z)$ plane. In the calculations of equation (4), the momentum wavefunctions $\chi_{b,k}^{\pm}(q)$ used in the calculations in equations (1) and (2) are used. The obtained results are shown in figures 10(a) and (b). The characteristic structures in both the $M^{+}(p_y,p_z)$ of ACAR and the $M^{-}(p_y,p_z)$ of CP are similar to each other, although the structure on the $M^{-}(p_y,p_z)$ is clearer than that on the $M^{+}(p_y,p_z)$. The structure of the $M^{-}(p_y,p_z)$ for CP is the reflection of the topology of the Fermi surface since the contributions from fully occupied bands give a flat surface. On the other hand, the structure of the $M^{+}(p_y,p_z)$ is due to the contributions from all occupied bands. In order to see this situation, the $M^{+}(p_y,p_z)$ constructed from fully occupied bands is also shown in figure 10(c). The basic structure of the $M^{+}(p_y,p_z)$ seen in figure 10(c) is similar to that in figure 10(b). This indicates that the fully occupied bands sufficiently contribute to the $M^{+}(p_y,p_z)$ and this feature is another indication of the small variation of the anisotropy of the ACAR in the contributions from the 14th to the 17th bands seen in figure 9.
