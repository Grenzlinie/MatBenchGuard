# A THERMODYNAMIC EVALUATION OF THE C-Fe-Ni SYSTEM

Armand Gabriel*, Per Gustafson** and Ibrahim Ansara***

* Laboratorie d'UGICARB-MORGON, Grenoble, France
** Div. of Physical Metallurgy, Stockholm, Sweden
*** LTPCM-ENSEEG, St. Martin d'Hères, France

ABSTRACT An evaluation of the C-Fe-Ni system has been made using a two-sublattice subregular model for the interstitial solution phases taking into account the magnetic contribution, and an ordinary subregular solution model for the liquid phase. A set of parameter values describing the Gibbs energy of each individual phase is given.

## INTRODUCTION

The present thermodynamic study of the C-Fe-Ni system is part of a more extensive study of Fe-based alloys performed by the SGTE group*. The Fe-Ni system has already been analysed by Chart (1) the C-Fe system by Gustafson (2) and the C-Ni system by Gabriel (3) and Gabriel et al. (4).

Available experimental data mainly concern the liquid/graphite and the fcc/graphite equilibria. The carbon activity in the fcc (5-13) and liquid (14) phases as a function of composition as well as the graphite solubility in the fcc (8) and liquid (15-19) phases are known with a high accuracy.

Additional information on the phase diagram can be obtained from the experimental determinations of the liquid/bcc and liquid/fcc equilibrium by Buckley and Hume-Rothery (20). The metastable equilibrium between cementite and bcc or fcc phase, has recently been investigated by Ko et al. (21) and Romig and Goldstein (22). Part of this information has been used in a previous assessment of the C-Fe-Ni system by Uhrenius (23). However, he did not consider the liquid phase, nor the magnetic effects in the bcc and fcc phases.

## THE THERMODYNAMIC MODEL

A model describing the thermodynamic properties of phases with several sublattices which was recently developed (24) will be applied in the present work. The model can also handle a contribution to the Gibbs energy due to magnetic ordering in the form suggested by Inden (25) and Hillert and Jarl (26). The Curie or Neel temperature and the average magnetic moment per atoms must be given as function of composition. A detailed description of its application to a ternary carbon system has already been published by Gustafson (27). A short review of the model and its application to the ternary C-Fe-Ni system will be given in the following.

### Fcc and bcc phases

The Gibbs energy for the bcc and fcc phases was described with a two-sublattice model. For one formula unit of an interstitial solution $(Fe,Ni)_a(Va,C)_c$ the sublattice model yields

$$G_{m}=y_{Ni}^{1}y_{Va}^{2}G_{Ni:Va}^{oh}+y_{Ni}^{1}y_{C}^{2o}G_{Ni:C}^{h}+y_{Fe}^{1}y_{Va}^{2}G_{Fe:Va}^{oh}+y_{Fe}^{1}y_{C}^{2o}G_{Fe:C}^{h}$$

where
$$G_{m}^{E}=y_{N i}^{1} y_{F e}^{1}\left(y_{C}^{2} L_{N i, F e: C}+y_{V a}^{2} L_{N i, F e: V a}\right)+y_{V a}^{2} y_{C}^{2}\left(y_{N i}^{1} L_{N i: V a, C}+y_{F e}^{1} L_{F e: V a, C}\right)$$

The symbols a and c are used to denote the number of sites on each sublattice. In the case of fcc a=c=1 and for bcc a=1 and b=3. $y_{i}^{s}$ denotes the site fraction of component i on sublattice s and s=2 denotes the interstitial sublattice. The subscript s will be omitted for clarity.
The parameter $^{O} G_{i: V a}^{h}$ is the Gibbs energy of component i in a hypothetical non-magnetic state.
The parameter $^{O} G_{i: C}^{h}$ is the Gibbs energy of a hypothetical non-magnetic state where all the interstitial sites are filled with carbon. All the $^{O} G$ values are given relative to the enthalpy of selected reference state for the elements at 298.15 K. This state is denoted by SER (Stable Element Reference). The term $G_{m}^{mo}$ represents the contribution due to magnetic ordering. The L parameters can be concentration dependent according to a Redlich-Kister polynomial.
The comma separates components that interact in the same sublattice and the colon separates components on different sublattices.

### Liquid phase
For the liquid phase the ordinary subregular solution model was chosen. A ternary parameter was introduced in the following form,
$$L_{F e, N i, C}=a+b T+\left(x_{F e}-x_{N i}\right) c$$

The value of a, b and c are derived from experimental data by an optimization procedure.

### Cementite
Cementite has an orthorhombic structure and is considered as stoichiometric with respect to carbon. It is described with a two-sublattice model $(Fe, Ni)_{3} C$ , yielding the following expression for the Gibbs energy of one mole of formula units,
$$G_{m}^{c e m}=y_{F e}{ }^{o} G_{F e: C}^{c e m}+y_{N i}{ }^{o} G_{N i: C}^{c e m}+3 R T\left(y_{F e} \ln y_{F e}+y_{N i} \ln y_{N i}\right)+y_{F e} y_{N i} L_{F e, N i: C}^{c e m}$$

The parameter $^{O} G_{F e: C}^{c e m}$ is the Gibbs energy of $Fe_{3} C$ and $^{O} G_{N i: C}^{c e m}$ is the Gibbs energy of $Ni_{3} C$ .

## SELECTION OF EXPERIMENTAL DATA AND EVALUATION OF THE PARAMETERS
The evaluation of the various parameters was made by means of a computer program for the optimization of thermodynamic parameters developed by Jansson (28,29). The optimization was performed with the selected set of ternary data now to be described and each piece of information was given a certain weight, reflecting the experimental uncertainty.

### The fcc phase
Gabriel et al. (30) have performed a critical analysis of the classical carburizing experiments and have applied their conclusions to an evaluation of the interaction parameters of the binary fcc phase in the Ni-C and Co-C systems (3). They concluded that carburizing experiments performed in silica tubes at high temperatures must be discarded when CH4/H2 gas mixtures have been used. They recommend the use of alumina tubes or a CO/CO₂ gas mixture, when possible. In agreement with their conclusions, the results of Natessan and Kassner (6) who used silica tubes and a $CH_{4}/H_{2}$ gas mixture, were discarded in the present work. Schenck and Kaiser (7) also used $CH_{4}/H_{2}$ gas mixtures. They did not give any details on their choice of reactor material. Their schematic picture shows a seal of a form reminding of $SiO_{2}$ tubes and their data were thus also discarde. Bodsworth et al. (12) did not give any details about their experimental procedure. However, Ellis et al. (31) seem to have used the same apparatus and the same gas mixture, CH4/H2. They found about 30 ppm of water in the circulating gas. We thus preferred to discard the data by Bodsworth as well as Ellis. Greenbank (5) used $CH_{4}/H_{2}$ gas mixtures and an iron-carbon cylinder of known composition. His technique seems to be adequate but he limited his experiments to the iron rich part of the system. Heckler and Winchell (13) used the capsule technique with a $CO/CO_{2}$ gas mixture. They also limited their study to the iron rich part, (< 30 wt% Ni).

We preferred to use the data of Wada et al. (10) and Ramanathan (11), who used alumina reactors and covered the entire Fe-Ni range. Smith's (8) data were derived from experiments performed in a silica tube, but with a $CO/CO_{2}$ gas mixture, which does not react with silica(30). His data were also used in the present assessment.

Smith (8), Wada et al. (10) and Ramanthan (11) all used iron specimens as reference for their carbon activity measurements but they used various analytical expressions for the calcu- lation of the carbon activity. For this reason, our optimization was performed using the car- bon content of the iron reference specimen as an experimental value and the carbon activity in each experiment was otpimized from the information on all the specimens present.

### The liquid phase
Foo and Lupis (14) measured the carbon solubility in liquid iron-nickel alloys at 1823 K at different carbon activities, using a $CO/CO_2$ gas mixture as a transporting medium. Their data show a relatively small scatter and were thus used in the optimization. The carbon acti- vity in each experiment was not prescribed in the optimization. Instead the carbon content of the pure liquid iron specimen was used as an experimental point.

The determinations of the graphite solubility in Fe-Ni melts reported by Schurmann (15), Uhrenius (16), Turkdogan (17), Miller and Elliott (18) and Ward and Wright (19) are in good agreement with each other, Only the three last series of data were used in the optimization because the others do not cover the entire Fe-Ni range.

### The bcc phase
The bcc phase is not stable in the binary C-Ni system. It was thus necessary to estimate the properties of the binary as well as the ternary bcc phase. The solubility of carbon in the ternary bcc phase is very low and unknown. The interaction parameter $L_{Ni:C,Va}^{bcc}$ was there- fore rather arbitrarily set to zero. The interaction parameter $L_{Fe,Ni:C}^{bcc}$ was assumed to be equal to the binary parameter $L_{Fe,Ni:Va}^{bcc}$. The parameter $G_{Ni:C}^{bcc}$ was then given a value which made the slope of the isoactivity lines for carbon in the iron rich bcc phase similar to the corresponding slope in the fcc phase.

### Cementite
#### Binary data
The thermodynamic properties of the binary $Ni_3C$ carbide are not well known. The available experimental data consist of the gas equilibrium measurements by Browning et al. (32) and Scheffer et al. (33) in the temperature range 499 - 588 K and 609 - 898 K, respectively, and the old measurement of the enthalpy of formation at room temperature published by Roth (34). Scheffer's data indicate that $Ni_3C$ could become stable with respect to fcc-Ni and graphite be- low 690 K. However, the more recent data by Browning et al. shows that $Ni_3C$ is metastable with respect to these phases at low temperatures as well. Only the high temperature points by Schef- fer et al. were included in the optimization. The data by Browning et al. seems more trust- worthy and were therefore given a stronger weight.

#### Ternary data
The partition coefficient of nickel between cementite and ferrite in iron rich alloys has been investigated many times. The experimental data by Ko et al. (21) Romig (22), Hultgren (35) and Sato et al. (36 are in general agreement even though the scatter in the data by Romig is very large.

The partition between cementite and austenite has been investigated by Romig and by Ko et al. in the temperature range 773 - 1003 K and 1073 -1373 K, respectively. Romig's data were not included in the optimization because of the rather large scatter in his data. All the parameter values describing the ternary C-Fe-Ni system are summarized in Appendix 1. The values which were obtained from the present optimization are marked with a star (*).

(10) and Ramanathan (11) are well reproduced by the present calculation, as shown in figures 1 to 5.

### Cementite
The present description of the binary $Ni_{3} C$ carbide is in reasonable agreement with the experimental data. The calculated enthalpy of formation at $298.15 ~K$ is $40.2 ~kJ / mol$ which is in good agreement with the experimental value, $(38.5+/-6 ~kJ / mol)$ , given by Roth (34). Fig.11 shows a part of the metastable C-Ni phase diagram plotted with carbon activity on the hori- zotal axis. The result of the present assessment is in good agreement with the equilibrium measurements performed by Browning et al. (32) but not with the measurements by Scheffer et al.(33).

Table II gives a comparison between the experimental partition of nickel between cemen- tite and bcc or fcc and values obtained from the present assessment. The latter values were calculated for the experimental nickel contents in bcc or fcc. The agreement is satisfactory except for the data on fcc/cem reported by Romig (22). However, his data show a very strong variation with the nickel content, which cannot be accounted for with the present model.

### CONCLUSION
The experimental information on the C-Fe-Ni system has been assessed after the exclusion of some information obtained with a technique which might have been inferior. The assessment is based on the most recent analyses of the binary Fe-Ni, Fe-C and Ni-C systems (1-3) and the new descriptions of the pure elements nickel, iron and carbon $(1,39,40)$ .

It has been possible to describe most of the selected experimental information within the accepted uncertainty by using a reasonably simple model.
