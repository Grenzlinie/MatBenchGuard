# A Calculation of Viscosities for Iron-Metalloid Liquids
YOSHITAKE NISHI and AKIRA YOSHIHIRO

Using the heat of vaporization, the free energy of mixing, the ionic radius, and the atomic radius, a method of calcu- lation is suggested for liquid viscosity. Furthermore, the supercooled liquid viscosities are calculated for Fe-B alloy. The critical cooling rate for glass formation is estimated by the use of the supercooled liquid viscosity and the homoge- neous nucleation theory for Fe-17 at. pct B alloy.

Based on the kinetic and thermodynamic points of view, the viscosity is an atomic scale measure of the thermal motion and structure of materials in the liquid state. There- fore, the viscosity has been known to be one of the most sensitive factors controlling the reaction and crystallization in the liquid. $^{1-4}$ However, it is not easy to measure the viscosity in the liquid alloys. Thus, we suggest a practical method to obtain the viscosity as a function of temperature.

Correction of Eyring Assumption. Using hole theory, $^{5}$  the apparent activation energy $Ev^{6-9}$ of viscosity has been cor related with a heat of vaporization Evap $^{10}$ in pure materials.
$$E v=\operatorname{Evap} / n\qquad[1]$$

Here $n$ is a constant. $n$ is $3.5 \pm 0.5$ for unassociated liquids and about $16 \pm 8$ for alloy liquids. Since metals are gener ally ionized in the liquid state, Eyring has suggested a following expression by the use of ionic radius $r * i$ andatomic radius $r * a.^{5}$

$$(\text{Evap}/Ev)(r * i/r * a)^3 = C \tag{2}$$

Here $C$ is a constant in the Eyring's theory, though $C$ is $4.8 \pm 3.5$ for metals, as shown in Figure 1. Thus, we suggest the size $(r * i/r * a)$ dependence on $C$. Two linear relations are shown in Figure 1. These relations are expressed by the following equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(\text{Evap}/Ev)(r * i/r * a)^3 &= 4.98 \log(r * i/r * a) \\
&+ 2.35 \quad \text{for metallic bond} \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(\text{Evap}/Ev)(r * i/r * a)^3 &= 3.44 \log(r * i/r * a) \\
&+ 1.15 \quad \text{for covalent bond} \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Here Eq. [3] is applied in Ib, IIb, and VIII elements of the periodic table and Eq. [4] is applied in IIIb, IVb, and Vb elements.

Viscosity of Liquid Alloys. $Ev$ is assumed to express by an Eq. [5] in Fe-P-C-B liquid alloy.⁵

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ev &= X(\text{Fe})Ev(\text{Fe}) + X(\text{P})Ev(\text{P}) + X(\text{C})Ev(\text{C}) \\
&+ X(\text{B})Ev(\text{B}) + Fm \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $X(\text{Fe})$, $X(\text{P})$, $X(\text{C})$, and $X(\text{B})$ are mole fractions of Fe, P, C, and B. The $Ev(\text{Fe})$ is $28.8$ kJ/mol for $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, estimated by Eq. [3]. Calculated by Eq. [4], the $Ev(\text{C})$, $Ev(\text{P})$, and $Ev(\text{B})$ are $100.3$ kJ/mol for C, $34.7$ kJ/mol for P, and $19.6$ kJ/mol for B, respectively. $Fm$ is a free energy of mixing. Using the interaction parameter of Fe-C, Fe-P, and P-C in Fe,¹¹ $Fm$ is estimated to be $-10.62$ kJ/mol for Fe-17.3 at. pct C alloy, $-17.22$ kJ/mol for Fe-17 at. pct P alloy, and $-18.10$ kJ/mol for Fe-13 at. pct P-7 at. pct C alloy, respectively. Thus, the calculated $Ev$ is $51.9$ kJ/mol for Fe-17.3 at. pct C alloy, $47.1$ kJ/mol for Fe-17.5 at. pct P alloy, and $56.6$ kJ/mol for Fe-13 at. pct P-7 at. pct C alloy. These values are in good agreement with the experimentally obtained $Ev$ ($52.5$, $46.5$, and $55.1$ kJ/mol for Fe-17.3 at. pct C,¹² Fe-17 at. pct P,¹³ and Fe-13 at. pct P-7 at. pct C¹² alloys).

Using Eq. [5], $Ev(\text{B})$, and $Fm(\text{Fe-B})$, $Ev$ is estimated for Fe-17 at. pct B alloy. If $Fm(\text{Fe-C})$ is assumed to be equal to the half $Fm$ of Fe-C, the $Ev$ is estimated to be $36.39$ kJ/mol. While the $Ev$ is $41.70$ kJ/mol for Fe-17 at. pct B alloy, if the $Fm(\text{Fe-B})$ is estimated by the use of the Kubaschewski equation¹⁴ and phase diagram¹⁵ and is about two times larger than that of Fe-C alloy.¹²

Considering the law of correspondence¹⁶ and the experimental result,¹⁷ the constant $\eta_0$ of the Arrhenius type equation of the viscosity $\eta$ is predicted to correlate $Ev$ in liquid alloys. Figure 2 shows the change in $\eta_0$ ($\text{mPa} \cdot \text{s}$) with $Ev$ (J/mol) of Fe-C, Fe-P, and Fe-P-C alloys together with various elements.⁶⁻⁹ These plots are expressed by the following equation:

$$\log \eta_0 = -3.30 \log Ev + 14.6 \tag{6}$$

The temperature dependence of the viscosity is expressed by the following equations in Fe-17 at. pct B alloy:

$$\log \eta = -0.451 + 1912/T \tag{7}$$

$$\log \eta = -0.664 + 2191/T \tag{8}$$

Both viscosities are $10^{0.856} \text{ mPa} \cdot \text{s}$ and $10^{0.852} \text{ mPa} \cdot \text{s}$ at the melting point (1462 K) and are $10^{0.773} \text{ mPa} \cdot \text{s}$ and $10^{0.756} \text{ mPa} \cdot \text{s}$ at 1562 K in Fe-17 at. pct B alloy.

Critical Cooling Rate for Glass Formation. The calculated critical cooling rate is approximately obtained by the use of the nose temperature $Tn$ and nose time $tn$ in TTT curves. $^{20}$ Figure 3 shows the TTT curves estimated by the use of Uhlmann's kinetic analysis $^{20}$ and the supercooled liquid viscosities. The calculated critical cooling rates are $2.75 \times 10^{5}$ K/s (using Eq. [9]) and $1.95 \times 10^{5}$ K/s (using Eq. [10]) for Fe-17 at. pct B alloy. These values are in good agreement with the experimentally obtained value $(2 \times 10^{5}\ \text{K/s})^{1}$.

Effect of Metalloids on TTT Curves of Crystallization. The calculated critical cooling rates agree with the experimental values of Fe-C, Fe-P, and Fe-P-C alloys. $^{1}$ Thus, the effects of metalloids on crystallization can be discussed. Considering TTT curves in Figure 3, the addition of P decreases $Tn$ and increases $tn$. The addition of B increases $tn$. Furthermore, the additions of P and C increase $tn$ and decrease $Tn$.

Using the heat of vaporization, the free energy of mixing, the ionic radius, and the atomic radius, a method of calculation is suggested for a liquid viscosity. Furthermore, the viscosities in the supercooled liquids are calculated. The critical cooling rate is estimated by the use of the supercooled liquid viscosity for Fe-17 at. pct B alloy.
