# CALCULATION OF THE FCC/LIQUID PHASE EQUILIBRIA IN THE FE-CU-NI SYSTEM

P. Spencer and K. Hack
Lehrstuhl für Metallurgie der Kernbrennstoffe und Theoretische Hüttenkunde der Rheinisch-
Westfälischen Technischen Hochschule Aachen
D-5100 Aachen, W.-Germany

Z. Moser and W. Zakulski
Institute for Metal Research
Polish Academy of Sciences
30-059 Krakow, Reymonta 25, Poland

(Presented at CALPHAD XIII, Grenoble France, May 1984)

## ABSTRACT
Previously evaluated data for fcc Cu-Ni and Fe-Ni alloys together with solidus/liquidus boundaries for the two systems have been used as basis for the evaluation of the thermochemical properties of liquid alloys. The selected data for the fcc and liquid phases have been combined with an assessment of the Cu-Fe system to calculate the fcc/liquid phase equilibria in the Fe-Cu-Ni system.

## 1. Introduction
In an earlier paper (1), the experimental determination of the thermodynamic properties of fcc Cu-Ni and Fe-Ni alloys from emf measurements was described. The experimental data were evaluated, together with the published results of other investigators, to provide mathematical descriptions for the thermodynamic properties of the two binary systems. These descriptions were combined with evaluated data for the Cu-Fe system (2) and with experimental values for ternary Fe-Cu-Ni alloys (3-6) to calculate firstly ternary enthalpy and excess entropy interaction coefficients for the fcc phase and then the miscibility gap in the fcc phase of the Fe-Cu-Ni system.

The present paper describes an extension of the work to include evaluation of published experimental values for the liquid phases of the Cu-Ni and Fe-Ni systems. The resulting self-consistent data sets for fcc and liquid alloys have been used in conjunction with evaluated data for the Cu-Fe system from Kubaschewski, Smith and Bailey (2) and with experimental phase boundary determinations for ternary Fe-Cu-Ni alloys from Nishizawa and Hasebe (6) to calculate fcc/liquid phase equilibria in the ternary system. Isothermal sections are presented for temperatures between 1373 K and 1673 K.

## 2. Review of data for liquid Cu-Ni alloys
The solidus/liquidus phase boundaries presented by Hansen (7) and by Hultgren et al. (8) have been used as basis for the present evaluation of the thermodynamic properties of the liquid phase of the Cu-Ni system. From the published experimental enthalpy of mixing and activity values for the liquid phase (9-15), temperature-independent $\Delta H_{m}^{E}$ and $\Delta S_{m}^{E}$ data have been derived.

The evaluated set of coefficients for a Redlich-Kister representation of the excess Gibbs energy of fcc and liquid Cu-Ni alloys is given in Table 1. It can be seen that the experimental $\Delta H_{m}$ values scatter widely (Fig. 2). However, since the corresponding data for the fcc phase show a maximum of approximately 2400 J/mol, it seems reasonable to assume that the $\Delta H_{m}$ values should not be more positive for liquid alloys than for solid and hence to select liquid $\Delta H_{m}$ values which have a maximum value a little more nega tive than 2400 J/mol. The evaluated enthalpy of mixing curve is based mainly on the experimental measurements due to Predel and Mohs (9) with some weight also being given to the combined results of other investigators (10-12). The evaluated activity data generally lie within 5 % of published experimental val- ues (Figure 3), although individual experimental points show greater departure (up to 13 %) from the selected data.

### 3. Review of data for liquid Fe-Ni alloys

The solidus/liquidus phase boundaries presented by Mrs. O. Kubaschewski (16) have been used as basis for the present evaluation of the thermodynamic properties of the liquid phase of the Fe-Ni system. From the published experi- mental enthalpy of mixing and activity values for the liquid phase (10, 17-30), temperature-independent $\Delta H_{m}$ and $\Delta S_{m}^{E}$ data have been derived, which when combined with the evaluated data for fcc alloys (1), enable the solidus/liquidus bound- aries of reference (16) to be reproduced within very close limits (Fig. 4). An evaluated set of coefficients for fcc and liquid Fe-Ni alloys is given in Table 1.

In Figures 5 and 6, comparison is made between the evaluated and experi- mentally determined enthalpies of mixing and activities respectively for liquid Fe-Ni alloys. The selected data are seen to reproduce the experimental values very well within the scatter of the different results.

### 4. Calculation of fcc/liquid equilibria for Fe-Cu-Ni alloys

The Redlich-Kister coefficients given in Table 1 for the Cu-Ni and Fe-Ni systems were combined with the corresponding evaluated data for the Cu-Fe system from reference (2) to provide an expression for the integral excess Gibbs energy of liquid Fe-Cu-Ni alloys. Calculation was then made of the liquid/fcc phase equilibria over a range of temperatures between 1373 K and 1673 K. In order to reproduce experimental liquid/fcc phase boundaries more closely, it was found necessary to introduce a ternary interaction term into the $G^{E}$ expression for the liquid phase. This term has a temperature-independent value of $-45000\ x_{Cu}x_{Fe}x_{Ni}$ J/mol.

A complete set of coefficients for the binary and ternary alloys is given in Table 1. Lattice stabilities for Cu, Fe, Ni are listed in Table 2. These data sets may be used in calculating thermochemical properties or phase boundaries in any of the systems.
Calculated isothermal sections at 50 K intervals between 1373 K and 1673 K are shown in Fig. 7 to 13, where comparison is also made with experimental and cal- culated results of Hasebe and Nishizawa (6) at different temperatures. The ex- perimental phase boundaries and tie-lines at 1423 K and 1523 K are quite well reproduced using the evaluated binary and ternary data. For higher temperatures, a feature of the calculated section is the rapid intrusion of the liquid phase field across a large portion of the ternary. This behaviour is consistent with the calculations made by Hasebe and Nishizawa, but the evaluation of the system made by Chart et al. (31) indicates that the effect is not so pronounced.
