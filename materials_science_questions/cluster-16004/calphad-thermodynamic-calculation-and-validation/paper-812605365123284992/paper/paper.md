# The role of Re in improving the oxidation-resistance of a Re modified PtAl coating on Mo-rich single crystal superalloy

### Highlights

- A novel Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was prepared on single-crystal superalloy with high amount of Mo.

- The high temperature oxidation-resistance was improved by the addition of Re to traditional $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coatings.

- The outward diffusion of Mo was effectively inhibited by Re to form $\sigma$-MoRe phases.

### Abstract

A novel Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was prepared on a Mo-rich Ni₃Al-based single crystal (SC) superalloy by electro-deposition of Re/Pt dual films and low-activity aluminizing. The isothermal oxidation behavior of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating and traditional $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was comparatively studied at 1100 °C. Apparent spallation of oxide scale was found on the surface of traditional $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h isothermal exposure, which mainly resulted from the evaporation of Mo-containing oxides. It is further found that the outward diffusion of Mo from the SC substrate was effectively inhibited in the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating by forming the $\sigma$-MoRe phase both at the coating/superalloy interface and grain boundaries. It is also revealed that the addition of Re can stabilise Mo to form the $\sigma$-MoRe phase, allowing the improved oxidation resistance of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating.

**Key words**: Superalloy; PtAl coatings; Oxidation; Diffusion barrier

### 1. Introduction

Thermal barrier coatings (TBCs) are the crucial part of the high temperature components in gas turbine engines. Generally, a typical TBC system consists of [1] a ceramic top coat for thermal insulation and a metallic bond coat for adhesion and oxidation resistance [2]. Among the available bond coats, it is widely reported that Pt modified $\beta$-NiAl coating shows superior high-temperature oxidation [3-5] and hot-corrosion resistance [6, 7] in comparison with MCrAlY (M:Ni, Co), $\gamma$-$\gamma$' and $\beta$-NiAl coatings. The high content of aluminum in Pt modified $\beta$-NiAl coating produces a dense and defect-free thermally grown oxide (TGO) scale (mainly $\alpha$-alumina) during the oxidation process [8, 9]. The addition of Pt enhances the adhesion of the oxide scale by inhibiting sulphur segregation at TGO/bond coat interface [10], increasing the outward diffusion of Al in the coating [11, 12] and reducing Al depletion during oxidation [13].

Single phase $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coatings have been applied to various superalloy substrates and their corresponding chemical behavior has also been reported. It is generally recognized that $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coatings exhibit low oxide growth rate during high temperature exposure, depending on the coating/substrate systems [3, 14-16]. A recently developed Mo-rich single crystal superalloy (IC21) appears of interest due to its significant mechanical properties at high temperatures [17]. However, it is reported that the diffusion of Mo can occur from the substrate to coating during high temperature oxidation, causing the evaporable Mo oxides ($\text{MoO}_3$ and $\text{MoO}_2$) to form on the coating surface [17, 18]. Subsequently, the continuous evaporation of the Mo oxides leads to early spallation of TGO and degrades oxidation resistance of the coating. It is thus important to develop a diffusion barrier between the coating and superalloy substrate to prohibit the detrimental diffusion of Mo and prevent the formation of Mo oxides. Tu et al. [18] have reported that the outward diffusion of Mo can be blocked by forming $\text{MoSi}_2$ precipitates in a Si-doped aluminide coating.

However, the formation of $MoSi_2$ precipitates diminished after 20 h oxidation at 1150 °C, indicating the limited blocking effects of Si on those Mo-rich superalloys. Therefore, an alternative diffusion barrier for Mo that exhibits enhanced durability and chemical stability is urgently needed.

Among the available candidates of effective diffusion barriers, Rhenium (Re) is very promising due to its low diffusivity and good thermal stability for long-term exposure at high temperatures [19-21]. Previous works have shown that Re in the electroplated Ni-Re diffusion barrier coating can inhibit the inward diffusion of Al from coating to substrate and the outward diffusion of Mo, Cr, Ta and Ni from substrate to coating. However, work concerned on Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating is somewhat sparse. The large mismatch of lattice constant between Pt and Re and the excessive hardness of Re restricts the interface adhesion between the Pt layer and Ni-Re layer, resulting in severe spallation of the coating after electrodeposition or vacuum heat treatment. Thus, the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating with good structure integrity and chemical stability has not been reported yet.

Therefore, in the present work, a novel low-content Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was deposited on a Mo-rich single-crystal superalloy by electroplating of Re/Pt dual films with strong adhesion to the substrate, followed by the low-activity gaseous phase aluminizing. This work aims to improve the oxidation resistance of $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating by introducing Re as a diffusion barrier to constrain the outward diffusion of Mo. The microstructure evolution and oxidation behavior of the coatings were investigated at 1100 °C. Besides, thermodynamic calculation was employed to evaluate the phase equilibrium in the coating using Thermo-Calc software [26] with the TCNi8 database. The associated mechanisms about the role of Re in diffusion barrier were also addressed and discussed.

## 2. Experimental procedure

Single-crystal superalloy IC21 rods with a diameter of 12 mm were used as substrate for the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coatings. The chemical compositions of IC21 are listed in Table 1. The alloy was cut into coupons of 2 mm in thickness. And holes of 1 mm in diameter were fabricated in the specimens for fixing the specimens during electro-deposition. Prior to electrodeposition, all the specimens were ground by 600-grit SiC papers, grit-blasted by alumina abrasives with an average size of 0.074 $\mu$m and ultrasonically cleaned in an acetone bath for 20 min.

A thin nickel layer ($\sim$1.5 $\mu$m in thickness) was electroplated on the IC21 substrate before NiRe plating in order to release the electroplating stress in the following process and increase the adhesion of coatings with the substrate. Subsequently, a NiRe layer ($\sim$1.5 $\mu$m in thickness) was electroplated from the aqueous solution containing 0.1 g/L $KReO_4$, 400 g/L $Ni(NH_2SO_3)_2{\cdot}4H_2O$, 30 g/L boric acid ($H_3BO_3$) with the addition of 30 g/L NiCl at 60 $^\circ$C. The pH of the electrolyte was corrected to 3.5–4.0 with potassium hydroxide (KOH). Finally, a thick Pt layer ($\sim$6 $\mu$m in thickness) was electrodeposited on the NiRe layer. The schematic illustration of the as-electroplated Ni/ NiRe/ Pt tri-layer coating is shown in Fig. 1. The tri-layer electrodeposited samples were annealed at 400 $^\circ$C for 2 h and 900 $^\circ$C for 4 h in vacuum to remove hydrogen and decrease the concentration of Pt on the surface.

The Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was then prepared by the conventional low-activity gaseous phase aluminizing at 870 $^\circ$C for 6 h. The chemical composition of the aluminizing pack was in a mixture of Fe-50 wt%Al powder and $NH_4HF_2$ activator (weight ratio 99:1). For comparison, a traditional $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was also prepared using the same method discussed above as the reference sample. After aluminizing, all the samples were ultrasonically cleaned in acetone bath and dried accordingly. Isothermal oxidation tests of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating and traditional $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating samples were conducted in a tube furnace at 1100 $^\circ$C.

Each sample was held in an alumina crucible and isothermally oxidized for 300 h.

The phases of the coatings before and after oxidation were identified by X-ray diffraction (XRD, D/MAX-2500, Rigaku Corporation, Japan) with Cu-$K_\alpha$ radiation. All coating samples were mounted in an epoxy resin, ground and polished to 1 $\mu$m surface finish. A further step of electrochemical polishing with colloidal silica was performed to minimize surface deformation for electron back scattered diffraction (EBSD) analysis. The surface morphology and cross-sectional microstructure of the samples were characterized by field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, Carl Zeiss, Germany) equipped with energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). SEM-based EBSD (25 kV) was used to reveal the grain structure of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-2100 F, JEOL, Japan) was employed to investigate the microstructure of Mo-Re precipitates and the cross-section specimens were prepared using a focused ion beam (FIB). Electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA, JXA-8230, JEOL, Japan) was used for elemental quantitative analysis.

Thermo-Calc software was employed to conduct phase equilibria calculations of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating via the CALPHAD approach based on minimizing the total Gibbs free energy of the coating system. The thermodynamic properties of phases presenting in the equilibrium state coatings were obtained from the TCNi8 thermodynamic database to obtain the fractions and compositions of the phases as a function of temperature.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1 Initial microstructures of the PtAl coatings

The cross-sectional microstructure of the as-electroplated coating is shown in Fig. 2(a)

and (b), where the thickness of the Ni layer, the NiRe layer and the Pt coating are approximately 2 µm, 1.5 µm and 6 µm, respectively. Sufficient bonding was achieved at the Pt/NiRe and NiRe/Ni interfaces, as shown in Fig. 2(b). Several fine pores can be seen at the interface between the Ni layer and substrate, which were presumably the alumina grits resulted from the grit blasting.

The XRD patterns of the as-prepared Re modified Ni-Pt-Al coating and traditional Ni-Pt-Al coating after low-activity gaseous phase aluminizing at 870 °C for 6 h are shown in Fig. 3. The β-NiAl and ζ-PtAl₂ peaks were clearly identified in both coatings, indicating that the aluminide coating mainly consisted of β-NiAl and ζ-PtAl₂ phases. This is due to the diffusion of Al from the aluminizing pack to substrate surface and the outward diffusion of Ni from the superalloy during the aluminizing allows the formation of thermodynamically stable phases [27].

Fig. 4(a) shows the cross-sectional microstructure of the as-prepared Re modified β-(Ni,Pt)Al coating. It can be seen that the coating consisted of three layers: the outer layer (labelled as layer 1) with the thickness of ~12 µm, the middle layer (labelled as layer 2) with the thickness of ~10 µm and the inner layer (labelled as layer 3) with the thickness of ~4 µm. The initial surface of the superalloy was hard to find. At high magnification (Fig. 4(b)), the layer 1 comprised white phase and dark phase, which were determined to be ζ-PtAl₂ phase and β-NiAl phase respectively by EDS. It is believed that the formation of the two phases (ζ-PtAl₂ in a matrix of β-NiAl) was due to the high content of Pt and outward diffusion of Ni from the substrate during aluminizing [28].

Fig. 4(c) (high magnification of the local area of layer 2 in Fig. 4(a)) was mostly composed of white phases, indicating that the layer 2 was enriched in PtAl₂ phases. It is likely because this layer was in the vicinity of the original electroplated Pt layer with higher content of Pt. Several bright fine precipitates formed in the highlighted

zone marked in Fig. 4(a). They are found to be Re-rich phase, as evidenced by the Re element mapping result in Fig. 4(e). In addition, Fig. 5(a) and (b) depicts the element profiles of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating sample. As shown in Fig. 5(a), small amount of Re with a concentration of ~1.6 at.% was found at the bottom of layer 2, showing a much lower Re content than the previous study [21, 24, 29]. It can be seen that the Re barrier layer almost stayed around the region where the original NiRe diffusion barrier was prepared (as shown in Fig. 4(c) and (e)), indicating the low diffusivity of Re during the aluminizing process. This is consistent with the earlier findings that Re exhibits high thermodynamic stability in $\beta$-NiAl [30].

The formation of layer 3 (dark contrast) can be attributed to the less intense inward diffusion of Pt during aluminizing, thereby forming a layer with less Pt in the bottom of the aluminide coating. At high magnification in Fig. 4(d), it can be seen that a small number of bright phases were dispersed in the layer 3. Combined with the evidence of Mo element mapping result in layer 3 (Fig. 4(f)), it can be inferred that a small amounts of Mo were diffused from the substrate to coating along the grain boundaries during the aluminizing process [31].

### 3.2 Microstructures of the coatings: after 300 h oxidation at $1100\ ^\circ$C

The XRD patterns of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating and traditional $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h isothermal oxidation are shown in Fig. (6). Besides $\alpha$-Al₂O₃ and $\beta$-NiAl phases, the $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al phase is also clearly identified in the coating. This suggests that the depletion of Al in the $\beta$-NiAl phase occurred during long term thermal exposure, leading to the formation of $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al phase. It should also be noted that some NiO phase and spinel (NiAl₂O₄) were found in the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating when compared to oxides formed in the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating. The presence of spinel and NiO phase is usually related to the depletion of Al in the coating. Therefore, it can be inferred that the depletion of Al in the Re modified

$\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was effectively inhibited during thermal exposure.

The photographs of the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating and Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating samples after 300 h of oxidation at $1100\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ are shown in Fig. 7(a) and (b), respectively. Apparent bulk spallation occurred at the edge of the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al sample. White and grey regions can be seen at the sample surface, suggesting that various oxides are formed on the coating surface. In contrast, no obvious spallation can be found on the Re modified sample and the sample surface exhibits a single contrast feature, indicating a uniform oxide scale on the coating.

Surface morphologies of both the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating and Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h oxidation are shown in Fig. 8(a) and (b). It can be seen that severe spallation of TGO occurred on the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating surface (Fig. 8(a)), whereas a dense oxide scale remains on the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating (Fig. 8(b)). High magnification of the spallation area was examined to further investigate the morphology of the exposure regions, as shown in Fig. 8(c) and (d). It can be seen that a great number of fine particles nucleated on the spalled surface. These particles were determined to be Al-rich oxides by EDS analysis. This means that new oxides nucleated and grew on the spalled area during thermal exposure, which is consistent with the previous study [32].

Fig. 9(a) shows the backscattered electron images of cross-sections of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h isothermal oxidation at $1100\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$. The Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating consisted of greyer phase and lighter phase, with the thickness of $\sim32\ \mu\text{m}$. A great number of bright fine precipitates were observed in the coating which were later identified to be MoRe-rich phases. It can be seen that the bright $\text{PtAl}_2$ phase in the as-prepared coating (Fig. 4(a)) were decomposed completely during 300 h oxidation because of the shortage of Al and Pt. A continuous oxide layer formed on the coating surface, with a thickness of $\sim3.5\ \mu\text{m}$.

The oxide scale was primarily composed of $Al_2O_3$ as identified by EDS analysis. Good bonding between the oxide scale and the coating is seen clearly. No evidence of interfacial cracks or pores can be found. Some small voids (known as "Kirkendal voids") formed beneath the continuous TGO scale due to the different diffusion flux of Al atoms and Ni atoms (Fig. 9(c)). Similar morphologies were reported in previous studies [12, 32, 33]. The rapid outward diffusion of Al to form $\alpha$-$Al_2O_3$ scale and inward diffusion of Al from the coating to the substrate occurred during oxidation. Meanwhile, the outward diffusion of Ni from the substrate to the coating was insufficient to supplement the consumption of Al. Consequently, a number of voids were left at the zone near the TGO during oxidation. Moreover, an inter-diffusion zone (IDZ) with rod-like precipitates and needle-like topologically closed-packed phases (TCP) appeared in the IC21 substrate after long-term oxidation, which likely resulted from inward diffusion of Al and outward diffusion of Ni [34, 35]. An apparent difference in terms of the dimensions of TCP precipitates in the two coatings was noticed. It can be seen that the TCP precipitates are larger and the volume fraction is smaller in the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating when compared to those in the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating. This indicates that the Re content in the coating can affect the nucleation and growth of the TCP precipitates, showing good consistency with the previous study [36]. The specific mechanism of Re barrier coating on the precipitation of TCP phases in IC21 superalloy will be studied in next article.

The element profiles of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating sample after 300 h is shown in Fig. 10. By comparing with the as-prepared coating (shown in Fig. 5(b)), the concentration of Al decreased from ~54 at.% to ~30 at.% and the concentration of Pt decreased from ~12 at.% to ~4.5 at.%. The composition changes primarily resulted from the outward diffusion of Al due to the growth of the protective oxide scale and the internal diffusion of both Al and Pt facilitated by the concentration gradient between the coating and substrate. It can be seen that the single $\beta$-NiAl

phase has transformed to $\beta$-NiAl (the lighter phase) + $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al (the greyer phase) two phase structure in the coating, due to the loss of Al during oxidation. It is also noted that the Mo content in the coating was very low and a MoRe-rich zone (as labeled in Fig. 9(a)) formed ~32 μm away from the coating surface. This MoRe-rich zone is close to the region where Re originally appeared in the as-prepared coating.

The cross-sectional morphology of the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h oxidation is shown in Fig. 9(b). The coating consisted of $\beta$-NiAl phase and $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al phase, which is similar to the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating. However, the TGO on the coating is discontinuous and not uniform. Some TGO was partially peeled off as seen in Fig. 9(d), indicating a poor oxide scale adhesion on the coating. Small amount of bright precipitates can be observed at the coating/substrate interface and were found to be NiMo-rich precipitates, indicating the diffusion of Mo from the substrate to the coating. The detailed chemical compositions of the precipitates in both the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating and Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h oxidation are listed in Table 2. It is believed that the poor adhesion and even partial spallation of the TGO was most likely caused by the volatilization of Mo oxides (MoO₃ and MoO₂), resulting from the outward diffusion of Mo [18, 37]. As a result, mixed oxides such as spinel and nickel oxides were formed. In addition, similar Kirkendal voids were also found beneath the coating, as shown in Fig. 9(d).

Fig. 11(a) and (b) depicts the microstructure of the highlighted area in Fig. 9(a) with higher magnification and corresponding element mappings, respectively. It can be seen that some bright globular precipitates were dispersed in the coating. These precipitates were enriched in Mo and Re, as seen from the EPMA analysis in Table 2. It should be noted that the MoRe-rich zone was far from the coating surface.

Fig. 12 shows the TEM microstructure of the MoRe-rich precipitate that is ~1.5 μm in width and ~3 μm in length. The selected area electron diffraction pattern (SAED)

exhibits two sets of diffraction patterns: a set of strong spots and a set of weak spots.
The precipitate is identified clearly by the strong spots to be an intermediate phase of $\sigma$-Mo$_{0.45}$Re$_{0.55}$ with a tetragonal structure, which is consistent with the previous reported study [38]. And the set of weak spots are due to double diffraction from the superimposition. The lattice parameters of the $\sigma$ phase was calculated to be $a = 8.89$ Å and $c = 5.57$ Å, while the lattice constants of the standard pure Mo$_{0.45}$Re$_{0.55}$ is $a = 9.61$ Å and $c = 4.92$ Å. Together with the EPMA results of the precipitate in Table 2, it can be proposed that the solid solution of Ni and Cr in <001> direction contributed to the extension of the crystal lattice in z-axis and the compression of the crystal lattice in x- and y-axis. Thus, combining the net effect of the above, it can be concluded that Re has a stabilizing effect on Mo by forming the $\sigma$-MoRe phase.

A schematic model was established to elucidate the mechanism of Re inhibiting Mo outward diffusion, as shown in Fig. 13. It is proposed that the majority of Mo was blocked in the coating closed to the coating/substrate interface by forming the $\sigma$-MoRe phase during thermal exposure. The remaining Mo (unblocked) was prohibited through their upward diffusion along the aluminide grain boundaries by Re, leaving small amount of precipitates of $\sigma$-MoRe in the coating as observed in Fig. 12. This mechanism is further supported by the evidenced of the EBSD results in Fig. 14. The FCC $\gamma$-Ni grains were colored in red and the $\sigma$-MoRe precipitates were colored in yellow. It can be seen clearly that the $\sigma$-MoRe precipitates were predominately distributed along the grain boundaries.

Since the detrimental diffusion of Mo was effectively inhibited in the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating, the protective oxide scale of $\alpha$-Al$_2$O$_3$ could form on the coating. But for the $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating, no evidence of Mo-rich precipitates can be found in the coating, This means that Mo has already diffused from the substrate to the coating surface during high-temperature oxidation and caused severe spallation of oxide scale,

as indeed seen in Fig. 9(b).

### 3.3 Calculations of equilibrium phase fractions and compositions

Given the complicated nature of the above oxidized system, phase equilibrium calculations can provide useful information on the compositions and fractions of various phases at given coating compositions under specified conditions. In view of this, the phase equilibrium of the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h oxidation was investigated in Thermo-Calc with the TCNi8 thermodynamic database. The composition of the coating is listed in Table 3 as analyzed by EPMA in the highlighted zone marked in Fig. 9(a). This composition was used to approach the evolution of phase fraction and composition against temperature (900–1200 °C).

The phase fraction and elemental compositions against temperature are plotted in Fig. 15. The system mainly consisted of three phases: $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al, $\beta$-NiAl and Mo(Re) at 1100 °C. The yellow line in Fig. 15(a) denotes Re(Cr) phase volume fraction, which is almost zero at 1100 °C. The result agreed well with the BSE morphology of the coating (shown in Fig. 9(a)). It can be seen from Fig. 15(b) and (c) that very limited Mo or Re exists in both $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al phase and $\beta$-NiAl phase, suggesting that Mo and Re can hardly be dissolved in these two phases. Fig. 15(d) depicts the elemental concentration of a MoRe-rich phase. The existence of $\sigma$-MoRe phase is also evidenced by TEM observation as shown in Fig. 12. Thus, the thermodynamic calculation agreed well with the experimental results, showing that the $\sigma$-MoRe phase is able to form at high temperatures.

### 4. Conclusions

The Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating was prepared on high Mo-content SC superalloy IC21 by electrodeposition and low-activity aluminizing. Isothermal oxidation behavior and microstructure evolution of the coating at 1100 °C were investigated.

Some conclusions are drawn as follows:

(1) Severe spallation of oxide scale occurred on the traditional $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after 300 h oxidation test. This is because the outward diffusion of Mo from the superalloy deteriorated the oxide scale adhesion. The Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating exhibited improved oxidation-resistance, with a continuous and uniform oxide scale forming at the coating surface

(2) The outward diffusion of Mo was effectively inhibited by reacting with Re to form the $\sigma$-MoRe phase both at the coating/superalloy interface and coating grain boundaries. The negative effect of Mo on oxide scale adhesion was retarded in the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating.

(3) The Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating after oxidation exhibits multi-phase structures from thermodynamic calculations, consisting of $\gamma'$-Ni₃Al, $\beta$-NiAl and Mo(Re) phases. This further supports the formation of $\sigma$-MoRe phase during oxidation in the Re modified $\beta$-(Ni,Pt)Al coating to inhibit the outward diffusion of Mo.
