# Deformation behavior of nickel-chromium alloys with special reference to the nature of solute distribution

M. Z. BUTT, U. SATTAR
Metal Research Laboratories, Department of Physics, P.O. Box 1072, Government College, Lahore-54000, Pakistan

The nature of solute distribution in the slip plane of a solid-solution crystal has a significant influence on the temperature dependence of the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) $\tau$ of the crystal. In the kink-pair nucleation model (KPN) of solid-solution hardening [1, 2], a parameter $W_{\mathrm{o}}$, which is a positive constant specific to the material and is inversely proportional to the slope of the straight line fitted to the $\tau$-$T$ data points appertaining to an alloy crystal at rather low temperatures in log-linear coordinates, increases with solute concentration $c$ if the distribution of solute atoms is random in the slip plane of crystals of a given binary solid-solution system. Experimental observations [3-6] however show that beyond a certain critical value $c_{\mathrm{m}}$ of solute concentration, $W_{\mathrm{o}}$ decreases with increase in $c$, indicating departure from random solute distribution in the slip plane of the alloy crystal. The values of $c_{\mathrm{m}}$ corresponding to maximum in $W_{\mathrm{o}}$-$c$ curve for a range of copper based binary solid-solutions were found to be 1 at. % Mn [3], 5 at. % Ge [4] 7 at. % Al [5] and 27 at. % Zn [6], which are quite lower than the first solubility limit of the respective solute metals in copper lattice. The critical solute concentration $c_{\mathrm{m}}$ was shown [7] to be strongly dependent on the size-misfit factor $\delta=(1/b)(\mathrm{d}b/\mathrm{d}c)$ in accord with the power-law $c_{\mathrm{m}}=K\delta^{-4.5}$, where $b$ is the lattice parameter and constant $K\approx5\times10^{-7}$ for the copper based solid-solutions referred to above.

Both nickel and chromium are transition elements, and form substitutional solid-solutions for a range of solute concentration up to 39.9 at. % Cr. In 1984 Clement *et al.* [8] studied the temperature dependence of the yield stress of polycrystalline nickel and of its alloys containing 5.6, 22.0 and 39.9 at. % Cr between 78 and 510 K. The main object of the present work was to examine their experimental data critically in terms of the KPN model of solid-solution hardening [1, 2], and to elucidate the nature of solute distribution in Ni-Cr alloy crystals.

In the KPN model of plastic flow in solid-solution crystals, Butt and Feltham [1, 2] visualized yielding to occur as a consequence of the breakaway of edge-dislocation segments from short rows of closely spaced solute-atom pinning points. These barriers were regarded as "smeared out" over the segment length in a manner somewhat similar to the "kink-pair mode of escape" of screw dislocations from a Peierls barrier [9]. By comparison, pinning of screw dislocations with solute atoms was considered too weak to result in effective barriers to glide. The CRSS $\tau$ of the solid solution, which depends on temperature $T$ and solute concentration $c$, is then given by the relation:

$$
\tau=\tau_{\mathrm{o}} \exp \left(-m k T / W_{\mathrm{o}}\right), \tag{1}
$$

which is valid over the temperature range where no diffusional effects occur in the crystal. Here $\tau_{\mathrm{o}}$ is the CRSS as $T\rightarrow0$ K, $m$ is a constant equal to 25, $k$ is Boltzmann constant, and $W_{\mathrm{o}}$ is a positive constant specific to the material. In terms of the microscopic parameters of the model, $W_{\mathrm{o}}=n\left(U c^{1 / 2} G b^{3}\right)^{1 / 2}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{o}}=4 U c^{1 / 2} / n b^{3}$, where $G$ is the shear modulus, $b$ is the length of the Burgers vector, and $U$ represents the energy expended per solute atom in the process of nucleation of a kink-pair of critical mean height $(1/2) nb$, in the initial breakaway of an edge-dislocation segment from a short row of solute-atom pinners to the saddle point. The value of $n$ is expected to be 4–6 in the case of concentrated face-centred cubic (FCC) and hexagonal close-packed (HCP) alloy crystals (low intrinsic lattice friction) whereas it would be somewhat smaller, i.e. 1–2 for body-centred cubic (BCC) alloy crystals (high intrinsic lattice friction).

As regards the temperature dependence of the CRSS of nominally pure FCC and HCP metals, Equation 1 will equally hold good if $c$ is taken equal to 1. In that case $U$ will be interpreted as the binding energy per interatomic spacing along the edge-dislocation segment with the extended groups of localized barriers, e.g. vacancies in above-equilibrium concentration, solid and gaseous residual impurities, dislocation debris formed during the stage of anelastic deformation of the specimen in the process of raising the stress from zero to the macro yield-point etc., and it should be looked at as an average value of $U_{i}, i=1,2,3,\dots$ etc., for all types of localized barriers taking part in the process of yielding [9]. The edge-dislocation segment involved in the unit activation process would move forward through a distance of several lattice spacings like that in dilute solid-solutions ($n\approx7$–10) [10] based on metals with low intrinsic lattice friction.

The length of the edge-dislocation segment $L_{\mathrm{o}} = b(4Gn/\tau_{\mathrm{o}})^{1/2}$ involved in the unit activation process at $T \to 0$ K, and the associated activation volume $v_{\mathrm{o}} = (1/4)nL_{\mathrm{o}}b^{2}$ [9, 10] were also evaluated, and are listed in Table I along with the values of $n$ and $U$.

One can readily note that the values of $n$ for both Ni and Ni-Cr crystals are in accord with the expectations, while $U$-values are also of the right order of magnitude. The binding energy per unit length of the edge-dislocation segment, i.e. $Uc^{1/2}$ [10], increases as solute concentration $c$ increases from 0 to 39.9 at.% Cr by about four fold. This makes the values of $L_{\mathrm{o}}$ and $n$ to decrease with increase in $c$, resulting in corresponding decrease in the activation volume $v_{\mathrm{o}}$. It is interesting to note that although binding energy per unit length of the edge-dislocation segment $Uc^{1/2}$ increases with increase in solute concentration $c$, yet the average binding energy per solute atom $U$ decreases as $c$ increases. It is due to the fact that a "partially flexible" dislocation line lying in the slip plane containing a concentration of solute atoms cannot be "fully" pinned at all the obstacles along its length; larger the number of solute atoms more "imperfect" will be dislocation-solute interaction, which makes the average binding energy per solute atom $U$ smaller as $c$ increases.

To explore the nature of solute distribution in the slip plane of Ni-Cr alloy crystals, we shall now examine the dependence of parameter $W_{\mathrm{o}}$ on the Cr concentration $c$. Fig. 2 describes such a relationship. The curve drawn through the data points has a maximum corresponding to a critical solute concentration $c_{\mathrm{m}} \approx 22.0$ at.% Cr. Below this critical value, $W_{\mathrm{o}}$ increases with the increase in $c$, as envisaged in the KPN model of plastic flow in

solid-solution crystals [1, 2], in which distribution of solute atoms is assumed to be random. The fall in $W_{\mathrm{o}}$ with further increase in $c$ beyond $c_{\mathrm{m}}$, indicates that solute distribution is no more random, and some sort of ordering occurs in the Ni-Cr crystals with $c \geq 22.0$ at. $\%$ Cr.

X-ray [13] and neutron [14] diffraction studies confirm a long range ordering tendency in Ni-33 at. $\%$ Cr crystals, corresponding to the $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Cr}$ stiochiometric compound. In fact $W_{\mathrm{o}}$ is a measure of the interaction energy between the edge-dislocation segment of length $L_{\mathrm{o}}$ and the solute atoms close to it in a solid-solution crystal. Theoretically, it should increase with solute concentration $c$ as $W_{\mathrm{o}} \propto c^{1 / 4}$ [12]. However, when ordering occurs in the vicinity of extended lattice defects e.g. jogs etc., the misfit energy as well as migration energy of the latter is reduced [15], which results in a deviation from $c^{1 / 4}$-dependence of $W_{\mathrm{o}}$.

From the preceeding discussion, we conclude as follows:

1. The KPN model of plastic flow in solid-solution crystals describes very well the temperature dependence of the CRSS of Ni-Cr alloys in the range 78-340 K.

2. It also helps to diagnose that the distribution of chromium atoms in nickel lattice is random below a critical solute concentration $c_{\mathrm{m}} \approx 22.0$ at. $\% \mathrm{Cr}$, and above that it becomes rather ordered.
