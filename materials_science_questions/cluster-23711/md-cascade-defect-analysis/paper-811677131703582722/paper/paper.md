# MOLECULAR DYNAMICS SIMULATIONS OF ION BEAM MIXING AT SUBTHRESHOLD ENERGIES IN METALLIC SUPERLATTICES. STRUCTURAL EFFECTS

A. M. MAZZONE
CNR - Istituto LAMEL, Via Castagnoli 1 - 40126 Bologna (Italy)

## ABSTRACT

This work presents results of molecular dynamics simulations of the intermixing of a heterostructure formed by a Fe-Ag bilayer of thickness of few tens of $\mathring{A}$. The intermixing is generated by As⁺ ions with energy of few eV. It has been found that the structure of the lattice has profound effects on mixing and disordering.

## INTRODUCTION

Ion beam mixing represents today a leading technique in the field of material preparation and many studies are being devoted to the characterization of the physical mechanisms leading to mixing.

A structure commonly used in mixing experiments consists of a multilayer alternating two different elements and their intermixing is generated by the bombardment of heavy ions. The current trend is towards the use of superlattices and the emphasis is on the understanding of the interplay of defects formation, the structural properties of the elemental components and the atomic arrangement in the structure being investigated. So far this goal appears to be achieved only on the experimental side whereas, owing to the evident complexity of these effects, their theoretical characterization is virtually absent.

In this work a molecular dynamics simulation method has been applied to the intermixing of a metallic Fe/Ag bilayer with a thickness of a few tens $\mathring{A}$. The purpose of the simulations is an analysis of the mechanisms of disordering and of their relationship with the structural properties of the lattice. It is well evident that the problem has many complex points and, as discussed below, the simulation has to be regarded for many aspects as a model calculation. Within these limits the simulation indicates that the structure of the bilayer has profound effects on mixing and disordering.

## THE SIMULATION METHOD

As mentioned in the Introduction the simulation cell is a thin heterostructure. A complex aspect of its study is the structural characterization. In fact under experimental conditions a layer is grown on an ordered substrate. Failures in the epitaxial growth may result into misfit dislocations. Furthermore lattice strains and interfacial defects are regularly formed. These structures are difficult to predict and, generally, very case-dependent. In fact, from a theoretical point of view it is to be expected that the addition of adjoining atoms has not negligible effects on the bonding mode and the atomic

arrangement in the planes near the surface. Such effects require complex molecular dynamics and quantum mechanical calculations. Furthermore on a practical ground many factors, such as contaminants and the state of the exposed surface, are known to be influential.

These uncertainties force the adoption of a simplified modelling for the geometry of the atomic arrangement. Owing to the large body of experimental and theoretical data available on defects and mixing in metallic targets a Fe/Ag bilayer has been chosen for the present series of simulations. It is added that at variance with experiments where combinations of similar lattices, generally of the zincblend type, are normally used this structure represents an interesting combination of two profoundly different lattices. In fact it consists of a bcc lattice (Fe) with a small cell parameter (2.87 Å) and of a fcc lattice (Ag) with an almost double cell parameter (4.04 Å).

In the simulation cell each sublattice has a cubic form and the two cubes overlap. Two arrangements of the atoms have been considered.In the first case (indicated as registered growth in the following) it is assumed a coherent growth of the top layer on the one underneath. In such case, according to [1], the atomic arrangement in the two lattices is formed by rows where the interatomic distances along the X axis parallel to the interface (the interface lies in a XY plane while the Z axis is perpendicular to this plane) is equal to ~ 4.04 Å and the distance between atomic rows along the Y axis is approximately 2.03 Å. Consequently large channels of section 2.03x4.04 Å² and axis parallel to the Z direction are formed in both lattices (in this configuration the X axis is aligned with the [110] axis of pure Fe and with the [100] axis of pure Ag. The Z axis is aligned with the [001] axis of both Fe and Ag). The second case (indicated as unregistered growth) is designed to illustrate the suppression of channelling effects. In this structure (in this case the X axis is aligned with the [100] axis of both Fe and Ag and the Z axis with the [001] axis of both metals) the interatomic distance along the X axis is 2.87 Å in Fe and 4.04 Å in Ag and the distance between rows along the Y axis is ~ 1.4 Å in Fe and 2.03 Å in Ag. Consequently the section of the channels parallel to the Z-axis sharply shrinks from 2.03x4.04 Å² to 1.4x2.87 Å² at the passage from Ag to Fe.

Even if the second case represents a more disordered structure the picture is not exhaustive. As discussed above, molecular dynamics simulations of surface reconstruction show that disordered structures of great atomic and electronic complexity are like to be formed in the planes near an interface [2]. To include this aspect in the simulation, according to a procedure common in mixing studies [3], an interfacial energy barrier $E_{int}$ is used. As the physics underlying such parameter is uncertain the value of $E_{int}$ is ill-defined. Reasonable estimates span between the heat of mixing [2] and the surface binding energies [3]. Consequently in the simulation $E_{int}$ has been treated as a free parameter and its values range from few tenths eV to some eV. The planar barrier model described in [3] has been adopted.

In the simulation cell the two sublattices have an identical linear dimension equal to ~ 40 Å and contain approximately the same number of atoms, that is 2000 atoms. The thickness of the cell along the Z direction is sufficient to contain the entire dynamics of the displacements. Periodic boundary conditions are applied along the X and Y axis to simulate a laterally infinite target.

The heterostructure is maintained at a given temperature by the action of a thermal bath. The heat exchange with the external reservoir is described by stochastic collisions according to the model proposed by Andersen [4]. Prior to the irradiation the cell is equilibrated at the wanted temperature (300 K) and the frequency of the stochastic collisions is adjusted in a such a way as to reproduce the correct values of the lattice vibrations of pure Fe and Ag. Such heat exchanges are maintained also during irradiation. Furthermore, for reasons of continuity with the external reservoir, the velocities and the displacements of the atoms on the two surfaces at the Z boundaries of the cell are constantly kept at the thermal values.No effect of the boundary conditions along Z or size-dependent effect has been observed and cells with 8000 atoms for each sublattices showed behaviours similar to the ones described below.

The disordering cascade and intermixing are generated by an ion entering the target through the exposed surface in the Fe lattice with a direction aligned with the Z axis. The ion mass (As⁺), intermediate between the ones of target components, has been chosen to generate cascades sufficiently dense to observe stable and reproducible trends in intermixing. As mentioned in the Introduction, the aim of this study is an analysis of basic mechanisms of disordering and mixing. To this purpose phenomena taking place in the eV range are known [5] to be of central importance as this is the range of the typical energies of defects migrations and of thermal spikes. Consequently ion energies of a few eV have been adopted in the simulations.

The numerical method and the evaluation of the potentials are the ones already used in [6] with the only differences that the Morse potentials have been replaced by the E.A.M potential [7] in the case of Ag and by the one given in [8] for Fe. This substitution should lead to realistic results as it is known that the potentials [7,8] accurately describe structural properties of the lattice in equilibrium and characteristic energies of vacancies and interstitials. As in [6], for heteropolar interactions the universal Ziegler-Biersack-Littmark potential has been used (spline-interpolants are used and the junction of the various potentials). A detailed discussion hetero- and omo- polar interactions can be found in [6].

For its evaluation the cell is divided in slabs of $5$ Å thickness and the number of atoms in each slab is counted. <p> represents the maximum deviation of the number of atoms detected in a slab with respect to the initial atomic content and its value is expressed as the ratio between the current and the initial content of the atoms in the slab.

The evolution of mixing follows an obvious trend. The incoming beam generates a cluster of atomic excitation in the top layer. This excitation propagates in the layer underneath and is gradually quenched through atom- collisions and heat exchange with the external reservoir. The more rapid cooling of the bottom layer is due to the fact that the ions stop in the top layer (or remain deposited on the exposed surface) so that less input energy is supplied to the bottom layer.

Three points represent the more significative results of the calculations and are worth being underlined. In the first place it is seen that the increase of the kinetic energies activates motions so that a flux appears across the interface. However the calculations show that the interaction of intermixing largely consists of repulsion between the incoming atoms and the ones of the host lattice. Owing to this repulsion the atoms entering the other layer are scattered back to the original layer and generally penetrate above the initial location. Similar effects are generated by reflections at the interfacial barrier. These mechanisms lead to a depletion of the planes near the surface and to an increase of concentration in the planes above. As shown by the data in Table 1 these disuniformities are conspicuous, they are formed simultaneously with the mixing flux and persist in time. Generally they appear in the Fe lattice as the lighter Fe atoms are scattered at a larger angle in the Fe/Ag collisions. Furthermore in this layer the overconcentrations are slightly more spatially concentrated than the corresponding depletions as the Fe atoms experience the repulsive action of the ions at the exposed surface and the one of the Ag atoms at the interface. For this reason the values of <p> are generally greater than 1. It is underlined that these damage structures were not previously known. It is added that de-mixing phenomena experimentally observed have been often explained on the basis of a Darken term in the diffusive motions. The repulsive interactions found by the simulations offer an alternative explanation for these effects.

In the second place it is known from experiments and molecular dynamics simulations (references can be found in [9]) that in pure metals there is an energy threshold below which stable defects can not be formed. This threshold is due to the lattice symmetries and its value generally falls in the range some tens eV. In our case the average kinetic energies are in the range 1 eV (the maxima are comparable to the energy of the incident beam. The point has been omitted for sake of brevity). However, in spite of these low values, the dynamics of the <D> and <Z> coefficients indicate stable disordering. It is seen in fact that while the values of $<E_k>$ are falling in the thermal range the displacements are not reabsorbed. The formation of stable damage is attributable to the suppression of the lattice symmetries around the interfacial planes. The disordered state of the matrix generates a scattering which effectively randomizes the paths of the moving particles and suppresses correlated recovery.It is mentioned that, similarly to our results, stable defects near the exposed surface of a thin silicon film have been found in molecular dynamics

simulations of atom deposition [2] for energies of the beam comparable to the of the ones of As⁺ ions in our simulations.

The third noticeable point is represented by the differences between the registered and the unregistered growth. In the first case the matching of Fe and Ag leads to the formation of large crystalline channels in both lattices and phenomena of the type of common channelling take place. The atoms are scattered in a softer mode. Mixing is enhanced and becomes more profound. As shown by the values of $<Z_{Fe}>$ and $<Z_{Ag}>$ the depth of the mixed fraction is approximately two-three times larger than in the case of the unregistered growth. The probability of back-scattering at the original layer and the formation of over-concentration are both reduced.

It is seen that, according to other theories and experiments [2], a sufficient high value of interfacial barrier can consistently reduce intermixing (penetration through the barrier is attributable to the high-energy atoms which, as mentioned above, have energy comparable to the one of ions). However even in this case the differences between the registered and the unregistered growth remain perceptible.

In conclusion this study shows in well-ordered heterostructures channelling effects which were not previously known. They enhance mixing and lead to more homogeneous atomic distribution. It is thought that the origin of many phenomena, such as the capability of mixing and low strain [10], observed in mixing experiments may be sought in mechanisms of this type.
