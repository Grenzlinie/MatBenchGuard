# DFT Study of Non-Metal Doping Effects on ORR Activity of Co–gN₄ Single-Atom Catalyst

## Problem background
The oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) is the cathode half-reaction in hydrogen fuel cells and its sluggish kinetics demand efficient catalysts. Single‑atom catalysts (SACs) such as cobalt coordinated by four nitrogen atoms embedded in a graphene sheet (Co–gN₄) are a promising class of non‑precious‑metal ORR catalysts. Doping the carbon network with non‑metal elements (B, N, Si, P, S) alters the electronic structure and can change the catalytic activity, but the systematic effects of different dopants on the ORR performance of Co–gN₄ have not been fully quantified. This task uses periodic density functional theory (DFT) to compute the adsorption free energies of the ORR intermediates (O₂, OOH, O, OH) and the resulting overpotentials for undoped Co–gN₄ and for the thermodynamically preferred doped configurations, thereby providing a quantitative picture of how doping influences the reaction energetics and the rate‑determining step.

## Approach
The computational approach employs periodic plane‑wave DFT with the GGA‑PBE exchange‑correlation functional and the Tkatchenko–Scheffler dispersion correction to account for van der Waals interactions. First, a periodic slab model of Co–gN₄ (a monolayer graphene sheet containing one Co atom coordinated to four N atoms) is constructed. Doped models are generated by substituting carbon atoms at symmetry‑distinct sites with each dopant (B, N, Si, P, S). Single‑point DFT calculations on all symmetry‑distinct doping sites provide formation energies; the lowest‑energy configuration for each dopant is selected as the representative doped catalyst. For the most stable B‑doped site (BNC‑1), models with 1, 2, and 3 boron atoms are also prepared to probe the effect of boron content. For every final model—undoped Co–gN₄, BNC‑1 (1B, 2B, 3B), NNC‑1, SiNC‑1, PNC‑1, SNC‑1—the clean surface and the surfaces with adsorbed O₂, OOH, O, and OH are optimized. Zero‑point energy and entropy corrections at 298.15 K are added to obtain free energies. From these, the free energy changes of the four electron‑transfer steps are computed, and the potential‑limiting step (the step with the smallest free‑energy drop) and the overpotential are derived. For the third‑period dopants (Si, P, S), site poisoning by O or OH may change the favored reaction pathway; the solver must determine, from the relative stabilities of the intermediates, the thermodynamically preferred ORR mechanism for each doped system and use it when computing the overpotential.
