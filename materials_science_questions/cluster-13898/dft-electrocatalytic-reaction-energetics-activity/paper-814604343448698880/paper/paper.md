# A Density Functional Theory Study on Mechanism of Electrochemical Oxygen Reduction on FeN₃-Graphene

Jing Zhang, ${ }^{a,z}$ Zhen Wang, ${ }^{b}$ and Zhenping Zhu ${ }^{c,z}$

${ }^{a}$ School of Chemical and Biological Engineering, Taiyuan University of Science and Technology, Taiyuan, Shanxi 030021, People's Republic of China
${ }^{b}$ College of Chemical Engineering, Shandong University of Technology, Zibo, Shandong 255049, People's Republic of China
${ }^{c}$ State Key Laboratory of Coal Conversion, Institute of Coal Chemistry, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Taiyuan, Shanxi 030001, People's Republic of China

Fe/N/C catalysts are proven to be highly active for oxygen reduction reaction (ORR). The detailed kinetic and thermodynamic ORR behavior on FeN₃-G (three-coordinated FeN₃ center embedded in graphene) based on density functional theory is investigated in this work. The results show that $O_2$ dissociation mechanism with a high active barrier is unfavorable. All ORR intermediates except $H_2O_2$ can be chemisorbed stably on the surface. $H_2O_2$ is easily dissociated, which indicates that a two-electron pathway is impossible. This conclusion is further supported by the calculations of OOH dissociation reaction barriers, which are extremely low. The results prove that FeN₃-G catalysts promote four-electron ORR. Given the high adsorption energy of the surface toward $O_2$, the $O_{2(ads)}$-to-$OOH_{(ads)}$ reaction has the highest reaction barrier in the whole reduction steps, which functions as the kinetic rate-determining step. However, free energy diagrams show that reduction of OH into $H_2O$ remains uphill for all positive electrode potential vs. the normal hydrogen electrode. The high endothermic $\Delta G$ value of $H_2O$ formation indicates this step is the most sluggish one and implies the highest resistance for the whole ORR. The FeN₃-G is not a well catalyst for ORR.

Fuel cells are efficient power generation sources with high efficiency and low pollutant emissions. Thus, fuel cells have attracted much interest and are expected to significantly contribute to efficient energy utilization. $^{1}$ The oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) at the cathode in this system limits the overall performance of fuel cells because of the low reaction rate and multi-electron reaction character of ORR. $^{2}$ ORR can proceed to two different pathways. One is a four-electron pathway, where $O_2$ is reduced to two $H_2O$. The other pathway is a two-electron reduction of $O_2$ to $H_2O_2$. The first pathway is always the desired pathway. $^{3-5}$ The most widely used catalysts to date are still Pt-based materials. $^{4,6}$ However, the high cost, low durability, and scarcity of Pt catalysts limit the commercial application of fuel cells.

Great efforts have been made in the past decades to develop new cheap and stable catalysts to replace Pt-based catalysts, $^{7-11}$ and various non-noble metal catalysts have been developed. $^{11}$ Among these catalysts, transition metal-coordinated nitrogen-doped carbons (M/N/C) are catalytically active and stable for ORR. The M/N/C catalysts have been extensively studied for several decades. Since 1964, when Jasinski $^{12}$ first reported that metal-$N_4$ chelates catalyzed the ORR, the M/N/C catalysts have been thoroughly studied as promising alternatives to platinum-based catalysts. $^{9,13-15}$ However, the catalytic activity and stability of M/N/C catalysts are generally still inferior to those of Pt-based catalyst. $^{7}$ A significant progress is achieved by Lefèvre et al., $^{13}$ who have synthesized microporous Fe/N/C catalysts. The current density of a cathode made with this iron-based catalyst can equal that of a Pt-based cathode at a cell voltage of larger than 0.9 V. However, this Fe/N/C catalyst has a much higher initial activity, but less stability. The works of Wu et al. $^{14}$ suggested the Fe or Co incorporation leads to a radical enhancement in the ORR activity and $4e^-$ selectivity. The X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) of the Fe-based catalysts shows a peak at $\sim$1.50 Å, indicating of coordination to a lighter element (either N or O). The ORR onset potential for the Fe-based catalysts was about 0.93 V versus the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE). The rotating ring-disk electrode study reveals that the $H_2O_2$ yield with Fe/N/C remains below 1% at all potentials. Recently, three dimensional carbon networks emerged to be effective in catalyzing ORR. An iron nitride/nitrogen doped-graphene aerogel hybrid is synthesized. $^{16}$ The hybrid also exhibits comparable catalytic activity as commercial Pt/C at the same catalyst loading. The onset potential of this hybrid was 0.01 V versus Ag/AgCl in alkaline solution, which was 20 mV more positive than that of commercial Pt/C. Nevertheless, the catalytic activities of 3D graphene frameworks based catalysts are generally still inferior to those of Pt-based. To bridge the remaining performance gap in the activity and stability between M/N/C and Pt-based catalysts, an active oxygen reduction site and a better understanding of the reaction mechanism are required. $^{14}$

Numerous studies have reported that the structure of metal-$N_4$ moiety favors the reduction of $O_2$ to $H_2O_2$, but the metal-$N_2$ active site has an ORR that is inclined toward the four-electron pathway to generate water. $^{17-24}$ Further studies have demonstrated that an $FeN_{2+2}$ structure that bridges two adjacent graphitic crystallites is a potential model for most catalytic sites in such Fe/N/C type catalysts. $^{13,18}$ Theoretical results have shown that both the four-coordinated $FeN_4$ center and the five-coordinated $Fe(CN)N_4$ center can be excellent catalysts for ORR with an excellent selectivity in four-electron reduction. $^{25}$ In addition, Byon et al. reported Fe/N/C catalysts based on modified graphene with an average Fe coordination of three N, which exhibit high ORR mass activity, high stability, and low $H_2O_2$ production in acids. $^{26}$ Density functional theory (DFT) calculation revealed that clusters that include $FeN_3$ defects can cleave the O-O bond spontaneously. $^{27}$

Obviously, previous studies have not reached a consensus on which type of metal-$N_x$ center is the optimal ORR active structure. Three types of $FeN_x$ (x can be equal to 2, 3 and 4) active centers are proposed experimentally in the current study. Several theoretical calculations have been applied to elucidate the active site structure and the reaction mechanism of M/N/C catalysts in ORR. $^{25,27-34}$ However, most of these studies focused on the carbon materials including $FeN_4$ active center. $^{25,27-31,34,35}$ In the present study, DFT calculations are applied to examine the ORR mechanism over the surface of an $FeN_3$ center embedded in graphene layer ($FeN_3$-G), which appears stable with a negative formation energy. $^{36}$ The present work emphasizes the diagnosis of the thermodynamic and kinetic behaviors of the involved elementary reaction steps. Particular attention is given to Gibbs free energy changes in the absence and presence of an external potential and on the active barriers of each elementary reaction steps. We then explore the most favorable ORR pathway and the thermodynamic and kinetic rate-determining steps.

## Computational Methods and Models

Methods.— All calculations were performed within the spin-unrestricted DFT framework as implemented in Dmol$^3$ code. $^{37,38}$ The

Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerh of parameterization of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) described the electronic exchange and correlation effects. $^{39,40}$ The all-electron-relativistic-core method was implemented to treat the relativistic effects. Double numerical plus po- larization function basis sets were used in the calculations. A smearing of 0.005 Ha to the orbital occupation was applied to achieve an ac- curate electronic convergence. The real-space global orbital cutoff radius was chosen as $4.6 \AA$ in the computations. A $5 \times 5 \times 1$ k-point sampling was used. The geometry convergence tolerance for energy change, max force, and max displacement were 0.00001 hartree, 0.002 hartree/Å, and 0.005, respectively. All atoms were allowed to relax during geometry optimization. To calculate the energy barrier of each elementary step, the complete LST (linear synchronous transit)/QST (quadratic synchronous transit) method was performed to search the transition state.

The adsorption energy $E_{a d s}$ is defined as $E_{a d s}=E_{t o t}-E_{\mathrm{FeN} 3-\mathrm{G}}-E_{X}$, where $E_{t o t}, E_{\mathrm{FeN} 3-\mathrm{G}}$, and $E_{X}$ are the total energies of the $\mathrm{FeN}_{3}-\mathrm{G}$ with an adsorbed $X$ species, the $\mathrm{FeN}_{3}-\mathrm{G}$ catalysts, and the isolated $X$ species, respectively. Negative adsorption energy indicates that the adsorbate molecule would be energetically favorable to be adducted to the $\mathrm{FeN}_{3^{-}}$ G catalyst. A simple estimate of effect of the dispersion correction to the adsorption energy of adsorbed ORR species was performed. The results showed that the gap was small between the values with and without the correction. The $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ adsorbed system had the maximum gap, 0.1 eV, which was neglected here. The conductor-like screening model (COSMO) was used to simulate the solvent environment in the computations. The COSMO is a continuum model in which the solute molecule forms a cavity within the dielectric continuum of permittivity. The dielectric constant was set to 78.54 for $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} .^{35}$

Following the approach developed by Nørskov et al., $^{41,42}$ free en ergies of the ORR intermediates can be calculated. The change in free energy for the elemental step is determined by the expression: $\Delta G$ $=\Delta \mathrm{E}+\Delta \mathrm{ZPE}-\mathrm{T} \Delta \mathrm{S}+\Delta \mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{pH}}+\Delta \mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{U}}+\Delta \mathrm{G}_{\text {field }}$, where $\Delta \mathrm{E}$ is the reaction energy based on DFT calculations, ZPE is the zero point energy, T is the temperature and equal to 300 K, $\Delta \mathrm{S}$ is the change in entropy. ZPE and S of the ORR intermediates are calculated based on the vibrational frequencies. $\Delta \mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{pH}}$ and $\Delta \mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{U}}$ are the free energy contributions due to variations in the pH value and electrode potential $\mathrm{U}$, respectively. $\Delta \mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{U}}=-\mathrm{neU}$, where $\mathrm{n}$ is the number of electrons transferred and $\mathrm{U}$ is the electrode potential vs. the normal hydrogen electrode (SHE). $\Delta \mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{pH}}=-\mathrm{k}_{\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{Tln}\left[\mathrm{H}^{+}\right]=\mathrm{pH} \times \mathrm{k}_{\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{T} \ln 10$, where $\mathrm{k}_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the Boltzmann constant. In this work, the pH value is assumed to be 0 for acidic medium. The effect of the electrochemical double layer has been proved subtle for the intermediates in ORR and does not affect the results much. $^{41}$ We will neglect this in our calculations. By setting the reference potential to be that of the standard hydrogen electrode, $^{41}$ the reaction $\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-}=1 / 2 \mathrm{H}_{2}$ (1 atm) is in equilibrium with the 0 $\mathrm{V}$ electro-potential and $\mathrm{pH}=0$ at $298 \mathrm{~K}$. Hence, the free energy of $1 / 2 \mathrm{H}_{2}$ can be used to instead of that of $\left(\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-}\right)$. The free energy of $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ was calculated in the gas phase at $300 \mathrm{~K}$ with a pressure of 0.035 bar since at this pressure gas phase $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ was in equilibrium with liquid water at $300 \mathrm{~K} .^{42}$ The free energy of $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ was obtained from the reaction $\mathrm{O}_{2}+2 \mathrm{H}_{2}=2 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ for which a free energy change is $4.92 \mathrm{eV}$.

Models.- All simulations are performed in a periodic $5 \times 5$ su percell of graphene including 50 atoms, and one carbon atom is sub- stituted by a Fe atom, and the three carbon atoms next to the doped-Fe atom are substituted by nitrogen atoms. In the vertical direction, a vacuum slab was set to at least $15 \AA$ to avoid interplanar interactions. The same supercell and $k$-point grids were used for all calculations to minimize systematic errors. The isolated $X$ species $\mathrm{O}_{2}, \mathrm{OOH}, \mathrm{OH}, \mathrm{O}$, $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{2}$, and $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ were optimized in a vacuum box of $15 \AA \times 15 \AA \times$ $15 \AA$.

After optimization, the $\mathrm{FeN}_{3}-\mathrm{G}$ structure was deformed locally. All the $\mathrm{FeN}_{3}$ moieties protrude from the graphene plane (Figure 1). The elevation of the Fe atom above the graphene plane is $1.36 \AA$, which is close to the results of Kattel et al. $(1.33 \AA)^{36}$ The $\mathrm{N}$ atoms are slightly displaced out of the plane by $0.43 \AA$. The three $\mathrm{Fe}-\mathrm{N}$ bonds are nearly equivalent, with an average bond length of $1.82 \AA$.

Possible catalytic pathways of ORR.— In an acid medium, the ORR on metal catalysts may follow two different mechanisms: $^{52}$

(A) Association mechanism, as expressed in the following:

$$
\mathrm{O}_{2}+^{*} \rightarrow \mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{ads})} \tag{1}
$$

$$
\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{ads})}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{OOH}_{(\mathrm{ads})} \tag{2}
$$

$$
\mathrm{OOH}_{(\mathrm{ads})} \rightarrow \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{ads})}+\mathrm{OH}_{(\mathrm{ads})} \tag{3}
$$

$$
\mathrm{OOH}_{(\mathrm{ads})}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{OH}_{(\mathrm{ads})} \tag{4}
$$

$$
\mathrm{OOH}_{(\mathrm{ads})}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}+\mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{ads})} \tag{5}
$$

$$
\mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{ads})}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{OH}_{(\mathrm{ads})} \tag{6}
$$

$$
\mathrm{OH}_{(\mathrm{ads})}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} \tag{7}
$$

where * denotes the free site on the catalyst, and subscript (ads) indicates the surface adsorption. Breaking the O-OH bond in $\text{OOH}_{(\text{ads})}$ is important because breaking this bond indicates a four-electron reaction pathway. $^{53}$ The adsorbed $\text{OOH}_{(\text{ads})}$ has three possible dissociation pathways, as shown in Eqs. 3–5. Alternatively, instead of the dissociation reaction, $\text{OOH}_{(\text{ads})}$ may combine with H to produce $H_2O_2$, which is a two-electron pathway.

$$
\mathrm{OOH}_{(\mathrm{ads})}+\mathrm{H}^{+}+\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{2} \tag{8}
$$

(B) Dissociation mechanism, where the first step is the direct dissociation of $O_2$. This reaction is expressed in Eq. 9

$$
\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{ads})} \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{ads})}, \tag{9}
$$

followed by steps 6 and 7.

According to the calculated results, $O_2$ dissociation is highly endothermic with a reaction energy of $0.56\,\text{eV}$, which indicates that the dissociation of $\text{O}_{2(\text{ads})}$ is energetically unfavorable and prevents the steps of further reducing the ORR in the dissociation mechanism. By contrast, the hydrogenation of $O_2$ into OOH easily occurs. The reaction energy ($\Delta\text{E}$) of this step is calculated as $-1.14\,\text{eV}$ which indicates that the reaction is exothermic. Therefore, the association mechanism is a favorable pathway to reduce $O_2$.

In a further hydrogenation step, the adsorbed OOH can be protonated to form either (i)O and H2O, as shown in Eq. 5, or (ii)two OH, as shown in Eq. 4.

If O and $H_2O$ are formed, the results show that the formed $H_2O$ molecule drifts from the Fe site, with the nearest distance between $H_2O$ and the surface that is larger than $3.00\,\text{Å}$, whereas the formed O atom tends to remain adsorbed at the Fe site for further reduction. This process is thermodynamically favorable with $\Delta\text{E}$ of $-3.14\,\text{eV}$. Moreover, further reduction of the resultant O species into OH and OH into $H_2O$ are characterized by negative $\Delta\text{E}$ values of $-1.77$ and $-0.77\,\text{eV}$, which revealed a highly exothermic characteristic and thermodynamic favorability.

(i)If two OH are formed, the two OH are adsorbed on two sides of the Fe atom. This hydrogenation reaction is also thermodynamically favorable with a $\Delta\text{E}$ of $-3.51\,\text{eV}$. The subsequent reaction is the reduction of OH into $H_2O$, and the whole ORR pathway follows as $\mathrm{O}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{OOH}\rightarrow2\mathrm{OH}\rightarrow\mathrm{OH}+\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}\rightarrow2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$.

After comparing the two pathways, the OOH hydrogenation reactions do not affect the ORR in this system significantly because the reactions no matter to generate $\mathrm{O}+\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ or $2\text{OH}$ is energy-favorable. Notably, the ORR pathways depend on not only the data, such as the aforementioned adsorption energy and reaction energy, but also the data, such as the activation barriers and Gibbs free energies for each of the proposed reduction reactions. Therefore, we calculate the active barriers and Gibbs free energies of all elementary steps.

Activation barriers.—The atomic structures of the initial state, transition state and final state for $O_2$ dissociation and four reduction steps along the ORR are presented in Figure 3. In addition, we calculated the activation energies ($\text{E}_\text{a}$) which is the difference between the energy of the transition state and the initial state.

According to the calculated $\text{E}_\text{a}$, $O_2$ dissociation (Figure 3a) has quite a high barrier of about $1.46\,\text{eV}$, which is obviously too high to overcome at the working conditions of a fuel cell. Therefore, the $O_2$ dissociation mechanism is an unfavorable pathway in terms of thermodynamics and kinetics. By contrast, the energy barrier of the $O_2$ hydrogenation into OOH (Figure 3b) is relatively easy to cross with a value of $0.96\,\text{eV}$. However, the barrier of OOH formation on the $\text{FeN}_3$-G system is higher than that on other $\text{FeN}_x$-G $(0.62\,\text{eV})^{28}$ catalysts and is also higher than that on non-metal nitrogen-doped graphene $(0.63\,\text{eV})$. $^{51,54}$ We speculate that this result is because of the strong adsorption between $O_2$ and the surface. If our speculation is correct, the ORR efficiency over the $\text{FeN}_3$-G catalysts can be increased by improving the $O_2$ adsorption and activation.

The hydrogenation barriers of OOH are very low regardless if $\mathrm{O}+\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ (Figure 3c) or two OH (Figure 3d) species are formed with only $0.16$ and $0.07\,\text{eV}$, respectively, which indicates that these two processes are quite favorable kinetically. Moreover, even the direct dissociation of OOH into $\mathrm{O+OH}$ is an exothermic process and has an extremely low active barrier of $0.03\,\text{eV}$ in the $\text{FeN}_3$-G model. The ease of breaking the O-OH bond suggests that the two-electron ORR pathway for generating $H_2O_2$ is impossible; the most favorable ORR pathway is the four-electron path.

Further reactions are performed by reducing O into OH (Figure 3e) and OH into $H_2O$ (Figure 3f). These two reactions are also characterized with low active barriers and exhibit values of $0.26$ and $0.64\,\text{eV}$, respectively, which indicate that these reactions proceed at high rates. Subsequently, the four-electron ORR product $H_2O$ is formed.

According to the calculated activation barriers, the most favorable four-electron ORR pathway is obtained. In the $\text{FeN}_3$-G model, the pathway is arranged in the following order: reduction of $O_2$ into OOH; dissociation of OOH to generate $H_2O$ and O species, two OH or O and OH; reduction of O into OH; and reduction of OH into the second $H_2O$ molecule. The highest barrier $(0.96\,\text{eV})$ appears at the first reduction of the $\text{O}_{2(\text{ads})}$-to-$\text{OOH}_{(\text{ads})}$ reaction, which functions as the kinetic rate-determining step.

## Conclusions
A DFT calculation on the detailed kinetic and thermodynamic behavior of ORR on $FeN_3$-G was performed. Both dissociation and association mechanisms were investigated. The pathways for both four-electron and two-electron reductions were discussed. The main results are as follows:

(1) The $O_2$ dissociation mechanism was impossible because the reaction of $O_2$ dissociation required absorbing much heat (0.56 eV) with a high barrier (1.46 eV), which was an unfavorable reaction in terms of thermodynamics and kinetics.

(2) The two-electron reduction product $H_2O_2$ cannot exist stably on the surface; $H_2O_2$ was dissociated with the O—O bond length that was about 70% larger than the corresponding distance in a free molecular $H_2O_2$. Therefore, the two-electron ORR pathway was impossible on $FeN_3$-G. This conclusion was further supported by the calculations of the O-O bond lengths of the adsorbed OOH (1.519 Å) and the active barriers of OOH dissociation. The dissociation barriers were extremely low with values of 0.03, 0.16, and 0.07 eV for the direct dissociation and two OOH hydrogenations, respectively.

(3) The four-electron ORR pathway was arranged in the following order: $O_2$$\to$$OOH$$\to$$O+H_2O$ (2OH or O+OH)$\to$$OH+H_2O$$\to$$2H_2O$. Given the strong adsorption between $O_2$ and the $FeN_3$-G, the first reduction step, the $O_{2(ads)}$-to-$OOH_{(ads)}$ reaction had the highest reaction barrier, which functioned as the kinetic rate-determining step. However, free energy diagrams show that reduction of OH into $H_2O$ remains uphill for all positive electrode potential, indicating this step is the most sluggish one and the highest resistance for the whole ORR. Therefore, the $FeN_3$-G is not a well catalyst for ORR.
