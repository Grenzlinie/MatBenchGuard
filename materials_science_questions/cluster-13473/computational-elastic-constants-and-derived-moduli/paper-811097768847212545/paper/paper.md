# Effect of temperature on elasticity of P doped silicon nanowires

Jing Wang

School of Physics Science and Technology, Xinjiang University, Urumqi, Xinjiang, China

## ABSTRACT
A semi-continuum approach is developed for mechanical analysis of a Phosphorus (P) doped silicon nanowire. Young’s modulus of the silicon (001) nanowire with different size along [100] direction is obtained by the developed semi-continuum approach. The results show that Young’s modulus of the P doped silicon nanowire decreases dramatically as the size of the width and thickness scaling down. The approach is extended to perform a mechanical analysis of the silicon nanowire at finite temperature. The dependence of young’s modulus of the P doped silicon nanowire on temperature is predicted, and it exhibits a negative temperature coefficient.

## 1. Introduction
Along with rapid advances in nanoelectromechanical system (NEMS) technologies, silicon nanostructures such as nanowires, nanobeams and nanofilms have attracted considerable attention due to their potential remarkable mechanical properties. In order to control the functionality, the mechanical properties of materials need to be well characterized with the decreasing characteristic dimensions. A number of efforts have been made to evaluate the mechanical properties of nanostructures. Theoretical analysis [1], atomistic or molecular dynamics (MD), finite element simulations, and experiments [2] have been performed to study the various properties of silicon nanostructures. The characteristics of doped silicon nanostructure have been investigated by first-principles calculations [3–5] and experiments [6–9]. Some experiments have proved that the mechanical properties of the nanostructures are size dependent as structure scaled down to nanoscale [1]. Theoretical investigations also reach the same conclusion. Among these, a semi-continuum approach is useful and simple [2], which key is to adopt the framework of continuum mechanics, but to extract the material properties and the constitutive relations from the atomistic description of the underlying local environment.

## 2. Three-dimensional model of a P doped silicon nanowire

The prototype of study is a three-dimensional silicon (001) nanowire, which is uni- form in width and thickness and infinite in length. A rectangular coordinate system is used with $x$ and $y$ axes along the length and the width directions, and $z$ axis along the thickness direction, as shown in Fig. 1. One layer thickness is assumed to be a silicon crystal cell with lattice parameter $4a\ ( = 5.43Å)$, thus the model for the Si nanowire has been assumed with $N\ (N = 1, 2, 3, ...,)$ crystal cell layers along the width and the same layers along the thickness.

The Keating model for microscopic elastic strain energy of the bond topology description of silicon is used in this work [10]. In Keating model, the strain energy of a given system is expressed as the sum of the nearest-neighbor and the noncen- tral next-nearest-neighbor interactions after deformation. The strain energy $U$ of a tetrahedron in a Si crystal cell is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{t e t} & =\frac{1}{2} k_{b \mathrm{Si}} \sum_{i=1}^{4}\left(\vec{R}_{0-i}^{2}-\vec{r}_{0-i}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2} k_{\theta \mathrm{Si}} \sum_{i=1, j>i}^{4}\left(\vec{R}_{0-i} \cdot \vec{R}_{0-j}-\vec{r}_{0-i} \cdot \vec{r}_{0-j}\right)^{2} \\
& =\sum_{i=1}^{4} U_{0-i}^{b}+\sum_{i=1, j>i}^{4} U_{0-i, 0-j}^{\theta},
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

where $\vec{r}_{0-i}$ and $\vec{r}_{0-j}$ are the equilibrium position vectors connecting atom 0 with its neighbors $i$ and $j$, $\vec{R}_{0-i}$ and $\vec{R}_{0-j}$ are the corresponding position vectors after deformation, $U_{0-i}^{b}$ is the energy of bond-stretching between atom 0 and $i$, and $U_{0-i,0-j}^{\theta}$ is the energy of bond-bending between the bond 0-$i$ and 0-$j$, respectively. The force constants $k_{b\mathrm{Si}}$ and $k_{\theta\mathrm{Si}}$ essentially describe the bond-stretching and bond-bending of silicon. The constants values of force constants sets $k_{b\mathrm{Si}}$ and $k_{\theta\mathrm{Si}}$ for silicon from Ref. [11] are used in this work, where $k_{b\mathrm{Si}}=6.187\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{N/m^{3}}$ and $k_{\theta\mathrm{Si}}=1.813\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{N/m^{3}}$.

The doping in a silicon nanowire is phosphorus (P). It is assumed that only a P atom substitute for a Si atom in a crystal cell shown in Fig. 2 and the structure is the same with a silicon crystal cell without doping.

The strain energy of a P-Si tetrahedron can also be given by the Eq. 1. And the force constants of P-Si bond are denoted by $k_{b\mathrm{P}}$ and $k_{\theta\mathrm{P}}$. The force constants sets can be obtained by comparing the energies of bond-stretching and bond-bending of Si-Si bond with the energies of P-Si bond, where $k_{b\mathrm{P}}=7.897\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{N/m^{3}}$ and $k_{\theta\mathrm{P}}=1.561\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{N/m^{3}}$.

The concentration of doping is assumed to be $\alpha$. Then the strain energy of a P doped silicon crystal cell can be expressed as

&-\ \gamma_{xy})^{2}\ +(\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy}+\varepsilon_{zz}-\gamma_{zx})^{2}\ +(\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy}-\varepsilon_{zz}+\gamma_{yz})^{2}\bigg]\\

&+\ \gamma_{zx}-\gamma_{yz})^{2}+(\varepsilon_{xx}+\varepsilon_{yy}+\varepsilon_{zz}-\gamma_{xy}-\gamma_{zx}+\gamma_{yz})^{2}\ +(\varepsilon_{xx}+\varepsilon_{yy}+\varepsilon_{zz}
\end{aligned}

$$
\begin{aligned}

$$

The total strain energy of the nanowire $U_{tot}$, can be obtained by the sum of all cells

$$
U_{t o t}=\sum_{i} \sum_{j} \sum_{l} U_{c e l l}.
$$

The average strain energy density $f$ is defined as the ratio of the total strain energy $U_{tot}$ to the volume $V$ of the nanowire, i.e., $f=U_{tot}/V$. Because the periodicity of the crystal lattice along $x$ direction, the periodic calculation element is chosen to contain $N^{2}(=1 \times N \times N)$ crystal cell. Thus the calculation element volume is $V=4 a \times h \times h$, where $h$ is the width and thickness of the nanowire. It is reasonable to define the nanowire width and thickness as the product of the thickness of one atom layer multiplied by the number of the atom layers, which gives $h=(4N+1)a$.

Since $\sigma_{xx}=E\varepsilon_{xx}$ and $\sigma_{ij}=\partial f/\partial \varepsilon_{ij}\ (i,j=1,2,...,6)$ for small deformation, where $i,j\ (=1,2,...,6)$ corresponding to $x,y,z,xy,yz$, and $zx$, respectively, Young's modulus $E$ of a P doped silicon nanowire along the $x$ direction is finally given by

$$
E=8 \alpha a^{4}\left(2 k_{b \mathrm{P}}+3 k_{\theta \mathrm{P}}\right)+8 a^{4}\left[\frac{N^{2}}{(4 N+1)^{2} a^{3}}-\alpha\right]\left(2 k_{b \mathrm{Si}}+3 k_{\theta \mathrm{Si}}\right)
$$

It is evident that Young's modulus of a P doped Si (001) nanowire is dependent of the number of atomic layers $N$ along the $y$ and $z$ directions and also the doping concentration. From the expression above, the contribution to Young's modulus can be divided obviously into two parts, the one is come from Si-Si bond and another is come from P-Si bond.

Based on Eq. 2, Young's modulus of the Si (001) nanowire is calculated as a function of the size of nanowire width and thickness with different doping concentration, along the high-symmetry [100] direction, as shown in Fig. 3. It is interesting to note that Young's modulus decreases dramatically as the size of the nanowire width and thickness scaling down to several nanometers with or without doping, which means that the nanowire is elastically softer than bulk and the variation of Young's modulus is consistent with earlier observation. At several nanometers, the tendency of the result is comparable with that obtained by MD simulation [12]. Meanwhile, as the size along the width and thickness direction is increased, it tends to become a constant value 125 GPa, which approaches the bulk value 130 GPa obtained by Wortman and Evans [13]. The variation of Young's modulus with the size along the width and thickness direction agrees qualitatively with the experimental result [2]. Thus the conclusion can be drawn that the approach is effective to study the elasticity of a silicon nanowire.

It is means that the doping has small effect on Young's modulus of silicon nanowire, and the doping effect should be considered at several nanometers.

## 3. Elasticity at finite temperature

It is well known that the mean interatomic distances increase with temperature, which is the intrinsic property for the thermal expansion of materials. The lattice parameter $a(T)$ under zero pressure was computed at various temperatures (100-1000 K) by using quasiharmonic model [14]. Fig. 4 shows the variation of the lattice parameter and the bond length of silicon at various temperatures, obtained from quasiharmonic approach. Since the lattice parameter is the function of temperature, the strain energy and the volume of the nanowire vary with temperature. Thus the variation of the Young's modulus would be dependent of the temperature besides the size along the nanowire width and thickness directions.

In the Keating model as introduced in Eq. 1, the lowest-order contribution to the strain energy was described correctly through quadratic terms in the distortion of the equilibrium geometry. Since the bonding curve of a crystal deviates consid- erably from the quadratic form for distortions of the crystal volume, it is obvious that the strain energy must be extended to include higher-order anharmonic terms for a reasonable description of deformations of the atomic structure. One way to include higher-order terms is to use force constants which depend explicitly on the local geometry. Within the local picture of a valence-force model, for homogeneous

## 4. Summary

In summary, a semi-continuum approach is developed for a multiscale analysis of a P doped silicon nanowire. Based on the Keating model, which strain energy considers the two-body and three-body interaction, the model of Young's modulus of P doped silicon nanowires is established. The model indicates that Young's modulus of P doped silicon nanowires is size-dependent, which also be influenced by the doping concentration.

The approach is also extended to perform a mechanical analysis of the P doped silicon nanowire at finite temperature. Based on quasiharmonic approximation, the temperature dependence of the lattice parameter and bond length of silicon has been coupled into the semi-continuum approach. The temperature-dependence of young's modulus of a P doped silicon nanowire is predicted, and the temperature coefficient of Young's modulus is negative and nonlinear.
