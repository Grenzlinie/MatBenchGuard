# Study of phonons and long-range order in Si-Ge superlattices using empirical interatomic potentials

K. E. Khor and S. Das Sarma
Joint Program for Advanced Electronic Materials, Department of Physics, University of Maryland,
College Park, Maryland 20742

(Received 28 January 1992; accepted 3 April 1992)

It is known that existing empirical potentials for semiconductors do not give good phonon spectra. They fail particularly in the aspect of transverse acoustical (TA) flattening. In this work we develop an empirical potential for the Si-Ge system which incorporates long-range interactions and accurately reproduces the phonon spectra, including TA flattening, of Si and Ge. This potential is based on our earlier theoretical work on a universal interatomic potential for semiconductors, which employs very few parameters. The validity of our potentials has already been confirmed in previous work where we have shown that elastic constants, cohesive energies, excess energies, equilibrium structural, and lattice parameters for several different monolayer superlattices are in very good agreement with the available first principles and experimental results. Long-range interactions are considered to be important in the understanding of superlattice phonons. A potential which can predict equilibrium structures accurately (which is essential for phonon studies), and at the same time calculate accurate phonon frequencies should be useful. We report on the results of the application of the potentials to the study of phonon spectra of Si-Ge strained layer superlattices. We also apply the potentials to the study of RH1 and RH2 long-range ordering in Si-Ge systems.

Empirical potentials have proven to be useful in modeling covalent systems. $^{1-7}$ They have been applied extensively in the study of growth, surfaces, microclusters, superlattices, strain, point and line defects, and alloys, to mention a few areas. It should be noted that these are all complex problems where traditional first principles techniques are generally limited in their usefulness. Cowley $^{8}$ pointed out that none of the potentials proposed by Tersoff, Stillinger and Weber, and Biswas and Hamann $^{2}$ is really satisfactory in the calculation of elastic properties. For example, these potentials do not produce the correct phonon dispersions (see Fig. 1). These potentials should, therefore, be used with some reservation in the study of strain effects. We have developed class potentials for Si, Ge, and Si-Ge which give elastic constants and their pressure derivatives, equilibrium states of Si-Ge strained layer superlattices (SLS) grown in the (100) direction, and predict bulk phase stabilities that agree very well with experimental values and local density (LD) calculations. $^{9}$ None of these potentials, including ours above, can reproduce the transverse acoustical (TA) flattening while at the same time giving the correct elastic constants; this is due to their short range nature. In this article, we extend our potential by introducing longer range interactions like those considered in the valence force field (VFF) model of McMurry et $a l.^{10}$ Long range interactions are important in the understanding of phonons of Si and Ge and their superlattices. $^{11}$ Our aim is to obtain a potential that can predict equilibrium structures accurately, (which is a prerequisite for accurate phonon studies), and at the same time calculate good phonon spectra. After testing the potential against these requirements, we apply it to a preliminary study of phonons in SLSs and long range RH1 and RH2 ordering in Si-Ge SLSs, where the (111) planes, occupied by only one type of atom, are arranged in the order AB-BA and BA-BA, respectively; the mechanism of this ordering has remained essentially unexplained despite much work having been done on it. $^{12}$ We hope that the potential will throw some light on this.

We write the following expressions for the cohesive energy $E_{\text{coh}}$, and interatomic potential $V_{ij}^{7,9}$

$$
E_{\text{coh}}=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{ij(i \neq j)} V_{ij}, \tag{1}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{ij} &= A \exp \left[ -\beta(r_{ij}-R_{i})^{\gamma} \right] \left( \exp(-\Theta r_{ij}) \right. \\
&\quad \left. - B_0 \frac{\exp(-\lambda r_{ij})}{Z_{i}^{\alpha}} \right) G(\Theta), \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
G(\Theta) &= 1 + \sum_{k \neq i,j} \Bigg[ \cos(\eta \Delta \Theta_{jik}) - 1 \\
&\quad - \omega \left( \frac{\Delta r_{ik}}{r_{eik}} + \frac{\Delta r_{ij}}{r_{eij}} \right) \sin(\Delta \Theta_{jik}) - \tau \frac{\Delta r_{ij} \Delta r_{ik}}{r_{eij} r_{eik}} \\
&\quad - \mu \sin(\Delta \Theta_{jik}) \sum_{n \neq k} \sin(\Delta \Theta_{jin}) \\
&\quad - v \sin(\Delta \Theta_{jik}) \sum_{m} \sin(\Delta \Theta_{mji}) \Bigg], \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\Delta r_{ij}=r_{ij}-r_{eij},
$$

They also saw RH2 ordering which is not reversible after annealing to a high temperature. The above results indicate very strongly, that if the RH1 phase is to be stabilized by strain, a long range potential, by itself, is not adequate and that if the potentials are modified <i>equally</i> for the ordered and disordered phases, the energy differences will remain small, when elastic constants are kept unchanged. We do not expect the presence of interfaces to change the results, as has been observed by Mader <i>et al.</i>12 for some of the conditions above.

We now carry out simulations to determine the type and sizes of changes (made only to the potential of the random phase), that will give energy differences of the necessary magnitude. Our calculation of the optical phonon frequency at the $\Gamma$ point for the zinc blende form of SiGe is $411.7$ cm$^{- 1}$, which is 2% above $402.5$ cm$^{- 1}$, the average of the frequencies obtained for pure Si and Ge. As seen from Table II, the elastic constants of SiGe are effectively equal to the corresponding averages for Si and Ge. Renucci <i>et al.</i>18 have carried out experimental measurements of the Raman spectra of $\text{Si}_{x}\text{Ge}_{1 - x}$ alloys for the whole range of Si concentration. Their results show that the optical phonon frequencies of pure Si and Ge, at the $\Gamma$ point are 523 and 303 cm$^{- 1}$, respectively, and they conclude from their results that the corresponding line for the ordered alloy with Si concentration at 0.5 should occur at 429 cm$^{- 1}$, which is about 4% more than the average of the two frequencies above. Calculated optical phonon frequencies at the $\Gamma$ point are 513.6, 291.3, and 411.7 cm$^{- 1}$, respectively, for Si, Ge, and zinc blende SiGe. We see that our calculations are consistent with experimental results. The frequency at the $\Gamma$ point of optical phonons is given by10

As can be seen from Table VII for the VFF constants, if the increase is in the bond-bending term $F_{\phi}$ only, then $F_{\phi}$ would be raised to 2.5 times its value. We obtained the following results for the disordered phase by increasing VFF parameters by 20% for (a) Ge only, the energy $E = 16$ meV/atom, (b) SiGe only $E = 16.5$ meV, (c) Ge and SiGe only $E = 17.3$, and (d) all three components, $E = 18$ meV. In Table VIII, we display the results for a number of simulations, where we change $F_{\phi}$, $f_{\phi\phi}^{\ast}$, and $f_{\phi\phi}$. These changes affect shear only [see Eqs. (4) and (5)], by the factor in the last row. In columns 1 and 2, instead of increasing the VFF parameters across the board, we put all the “frustration” into $F_{\phi}$ only and $F_{\phi}$ and $f_{\phi\phi}^{\ast}$, respectively. The energies and shear are similar. In the last column, we multiply $f_{\phi\phi}$ by 8 times, while keeping the factor for $F_{\phi}$ at 2.5. Changing the factor for $f_{\phi\phi}$, as can be seen from Eqs. (6) and (8), does not change the optical phonon frequencies at the $\Gamma$ point, but would only remove the TA flattening [see Eq. (8)] in the ordered system. A factor of 10 for $f_{\phi\phi}$ would double the frequency at the $X$ point. We see that the energy differences between the ordered phase, with $E = 16.9$ meV, and the results in Table VIII for the disordered phase are at most 6 meV/atom, despite substantial changes in the VFF parameters; if there is frustration in the disordered system, our results show that that it would have to be quite large (perhaps corresponding to an increase in shear of 2 times), to get the energy difference that is needed to stabilize the RH1 phase.

In conclusion, we now have an empirical potential which gives both phonon spectra and structure correctly. It should be useful for investigating phonons in strained layer SiGe superlattices grown in different directions and under varying stresses. In applying it to the existence of reversible RH1 order, our simulations show that energy differences between RH1 and the disordered phase “track” over a large range of situations, with magnitude of about 1 meV, which cannot explain the stability of the RH1 phase and its transition temperature; the long range order of our potential seems to favor disorder. Experimental optical phonon frequencies agree with our calculated results. Our simulations show that the transition temperature of the RH1 phase still remains unexplained.
