**Electrical and Optical Properties in the 180°
Ferroelectric Domain Wall of Tetragonal KNbO₃**

H. CHAIB, F. SCHLAPHOF, T. OTTO, and L. M. ENG*

Institute of Applied Photophysics, University of Technology,
Dresden D-01062 Dresden, Germany

(Received September 15, 2002)

The microscopic mechanism of spontaneous polarization and refractive indices in 180° fer-
roelectric domain walls of tetragonal Potassium niobate (KNbO₃) is discussed by using a
microscopic model. This model bases on the orbital approximation in correlation with the
dipole-dipole interaction due to the local field acting on all constituent ions within the domain
wall. It is found that the behavior of both the spontaneous polarization and refractive indices
depends on the thickness of the domain wall which was varied between 5–20 Å. Moreover,
the spontaneous polarization shows a hyperbolic tangent shape for domain walls of a larger
thickness which then vanishes at the center of the domain wall. The refractive indices suggest
the domain wall to act like a biaxial crystal resulting in refractive index profiles of a Gaussian
shape for domain walls of ~20 Å. This dramatically affects optical transmission through the
domain wall specifically for light being polarized parallel to the domain wall.

Keywords: Ferroelectric; 180° domain wall; KNbO₃; spontaneous polarization; refractive index;
electronic polarizability; dipole-dipole interaction; orbital approximation

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Potassium niobate (KNbO₃) is one of the most known ferroelectric mate-
rials. This material is especially interesting for linear and nonlinear optical
applications because of its large value of the spontaneous polarization. It
is a ferroelectric material belonging, as Barium titanate (BaTiO₃), to the
perovskite class and having a first order ferroelectric phase transition [1]
and field reversible spontaneous polarization below the Curie point. KNbO₃
(and comparable materials) exhibits a sequence of ferroelectric phase tran-
sitions: at high temperature, KNbO₃ is paraelectric having a cubic structure.

On cooling, this material undergoes successive structural phase transitions [1] all of them are strongly of first-order character showing a large ther- mal hysteresis [2], a remarkable optical anisotropy [3], a large sponta- neous polarization [1], as well as electrooptic and photorefractive activity [4–7].

In recent years, a number of experimental investigations were reported which focus on characterizing the ferroelectric domains and domain walls in $\mathrm{KNbO}_{3}$ [8] as well as in other ferroelectric materials [9–19]. Transmission electron microscopy, X-ray spectroscopy as well as scanning force micro- copy have been applied though with very limited success: In fact, not one of these inspections was able to deliver some experimental data on the physi- cal properties within the $180^{\circ}$ domain wall. To our knowledge the few data available on the domain wall properties have been deduced from theoretical calculations mainly focussing on the domain wall thickness, the (elastic) domain wall energy, or the domain wall structure and character [20]. More recent theoretical work in this respect reports on first principle calculations in order to compute the energy and thickness of the ferroelectric domain wall [21]. The common conclusion from these theoretical investigations claims that the $180^{\circ}$ domain wall in $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$, from the same family of $\mathrm{KNbO}_{3}$, must have a width of less than $20\ \mathring{A}$. Most probably it is this very thin thickness which so far might be the main reason for the difficulty in obtain- ing reliable experimental data on the physical properties within the domain wall.

Here we present a somewhat different theoretical approach for computing the spontaneous polarization and refractive indices within the $180^{\circ}$ domain wall of tetragonal $\mathrm{KNbO}_{3}$. The microscopic model takes into account the anisotropy in the electronic polarizability of all ions within the domain wall, their ionic shifts as well as the crystalline deformations. The model was pre- viously tested for the calculation of bulk properties of ferroelectric crystals like the ferroelectricity and optical anisotropy [22–24], as well as their lin- ear electro-optical coefficients [25–27] in mono-domain $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{KNbO}_{3}$ also the $180^{\circ}\ \mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ domain wall was calculated [28]. Based on those expe- riences, which agree excellently well with the corresponding experimental data, we initiate here the application of the orbital approximation in corre- lation with the dipole-dipole interaction for the ferroelectric domain wall in $\mathrm{KNbO}_{3}$. Further motivation for these calculations stems from the today's perspectives in applying optical measuring techniques in the optical near- field [9] to directly investigate the local absorption properties [29] as well as the chemical nature of bonding within the domain wall via tip-enhanced Raman spectroscopy [30].

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 discusses the dipole-dipole interaction due to the local field acting on the constituent ions taking into account the individual ionic shifts and crystalline deformations. Also a summary on the electronic polarizability of ions in the KNbO₃ unit cell is given for completeness, as well as the basic approach to our quantum mechanical calculation. Section 3 then presents the results of the spontaneous polarization, and local refractive indices calculated over the 180° domain wall in KNbO₃. We clearly differentiate these results for different widths of the domain wall. This section also contains the discussion of the above mentioned findings.

## 2. DESCRIPTION OF THE MODEL

As mentioned above the model is based on the dipole-dipole interaction in correlation with the quantum mechanical orbital approximation where every ion is modeled as consisting of a single electron and the effectively charged nucleus [23, 24, 27]. The model therefore accounts for the possible anisotropy in the electronic polarizability, the crystalline deformations, as well as the ionic shifts both in the bulk and over the domain wall.

The latter are of uttermost importance when modeling the 180° domain wall in a ferroelectric system. We therefore write the strains parallel to the pseudo-cubic [100], [010] and [001] directions as $\Delta_1$, $\Delta_2$ and $\Delta_3$, respectively, while the shearing concerning the (100), (010) and (001) planes are denoted as $\delta_1$, $\delta_2$ and $\delta_3$, respectively. The distance $\mathbf{r}_{\binom{\tilde{n},\tilde{m}}{i,j}}$ between the $i$-ion sitting in the $\tilde{n}(n_x,n_y,n_z)$ unit cell (the indices $(n_x,n_y,n_z)$ represent the coordinates of a unit cell in the whole lattice) and the $j$-ion which is found in the $\tilde{m}(m_x,m_y,m_z)$ unit cell follows as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{r}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)=& \left(\begin{array}{ccc}
1+\Delta_1 & \delta_3 & \delta_2 \\
\delta_3 & 1+\Delta_2 & \delta_1 \\
\delta_2 & \delta_1 & 1+\Delta_3
\end{array}\right) \mathbf{r}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)^0 \\
&+\left(\mathbf{s}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)-\mathbf{s}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i
\end{array}\right)\right),
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

with $\mathbf{r}(\binom{\tilde{n},\tilde{m}}{i,j})^0$ representing the interionic distance in the cubic phase between $i$-ion in unit cell $\tilde{n}$ and $j$-ion in unit cell $\tilde{m}$, and $\mathbf{s}(\binom{\tilde{n}}{i})$ and $\mathbf{s}(\binom{\tilde{m}}{j})$ denoting

the shifts of the $i$-ion of the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell and the $j$-ion of the $\tilde{m}$ unit cell, respectively.

The unit cell volume $v$ then becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
v= & a_{0}^{3}\left[\left(1+\Delta_{1}\right)\left(1+\Delta_{2}\right)\left(1+\Delta_{3}\right)-\left(1+\Delta_{1}\right)\left(\delta_{1}\right)^{2}\right. \\
& \left.-\left(1+\Delta_{2}\right)\left(\delta_{2}\right)^{2}-\left(1+\Delta_{3}\right)\left(\delta_{3}\right)^{2}+2 \delta_{1} \delta_{2} \delta_{3}\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

where $a_{0}$ is the lattice constant in the cubic phase.

The local field acting on $i$-ion of the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell in the $k$-direction is expressed as [31]

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{k i}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i
\end{array}\right)= & E_{k}^{\mathrm{ext}}+\sum_{j=1}^{5}\left(\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{3} \sum_{\tilde{m}} \frac{3 r_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right) r_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)-\delta_{k k^{\prime}} r^{2}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}\left\|\mathbf{r}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)\right\|^{5}} p_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{1}{3 \varepsilon_{0} v} p_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{n} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

In the last equation, $\mathbf{E}^{\text {ext }}$ represents an external electric field which is in general the sum of the bias electric field $\mathbf{E}^{\text {bias }}$ and the optical electric field $\mathbf{E}^{\text {opt }}, \mathbf{E}^{\text {ext }}=\mathbf{E}^{\text {bias }}+\mathbf{E}^{\text {opt }} . \delta_{k k^{\prime}}$ denotes the Kronecker symbol, and $p_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\ i, j\end{array}\right)$ the dipole moment along the $k^{\prime}$-direction, which can be expressed as

$$
p_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)=p_{k^{\prime}}^{e}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)+p_{k^{\prime}}^{i}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)
$$

with the two contributions $p_{k^{\prime}}^{e}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{m} \\ j\end{array}\right)$ and $p_{k^{\prime}}^{i}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\ i, j\end{array}\right)$ specifying the electronic dipole moment and the relative ionic dipole moment in the $k^{\prime}$-direction of the $j$-ion as seen by the $i$-ion, respectively. These dipole moments are calculated as

$$
p_{k^{\prime}}^{e}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{3} \alpha_{k^{\prime} l}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right) E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)
$$

and

$$
p_{k^{\prime}}^{i}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)=Z_{k^{\prime}}^{*}(j) e\left(s_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)-s_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i
\end{array}\right)\right)
$$

In equation (5), $\alpha_{k'l}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{m}\\\ j\end{pmatrix}$ represents the $(k'l)$ element of the electronic polarizability tensor of the $j$-ion of the $\tilde{m}$ unit cell given by [24–26]

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{k'l}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{m}\\\ j\end{pmatrix} &= \alpha^{\exp}(j)\bigg[\delta_{k'l} - \theta_{k'}(j)\bigg(\bigg(E^{\mathrm{loc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{m}\\\ j\end{pmatrix}\bigg)^2\delta_{k'l} \\
&\quad + 2E_{k'}^{\mathrm{loc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{m}\\\ j\end{pmatrix}E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{m}\\\ j\end{pmatrix}\bigg)\bigg]
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

Here $\alpha^{\exp}(j)$ represents the measured free electronic polarizability of the $j$-ion. These values are reported in reference 24 together with the coefficients $\theta_{k'}(j)$. Note that in order to obtain equation (7) which expresses the electronic polarizabilities of tetragonal $\mathrm{KNbO}_3$ as a function of electric field,we used the quantum mechanical orbital approximation. This manifests in treating the electrostatic energy of the core-shell system, introduced by the presence of the electric field, as a small perturbation within the Schrödinger equation. The quantum mechanical problem then is solved by applying the variational method. As a result we obtain the wave functions of the core-shell system under the presence of the electric field, which then in turn are used to compute the components of the electronic polarizability tensor for all orbitals involved for one specific ion. Finally, the total electronic polarizability of an ion is obtained by summing over all orbitals.

In equation (6), $Z_{k'}^*(j)$ represents the effective ionic charge along the $k'$ direction of the $j$-ion.

With this, equation (3) may then be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n}\\\ i\end{pmatrix} = E_{k}^{\mathrm{ext}} &= \sum_{j=1}^{5}\sum_{k'=1}^{3}\bigg(\sum_{\tilde{m}} \frac{3r_{k'}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix}r_{k}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix} - \delta_{kk'}r^2\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\left\|\mathbf{r}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix}\right\|^5} \\
&\quad + \frac{1}{3\varepsilon_0\nu}\delta_{kk'}\bigg)p_{k'}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\ \tilde{n}\\\ i,\ j\end{pmatrix} + \delta E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n}\\\ i\end{pmatrix},
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n}\\\ i\end{pmatrix} &= \sum_{j=1}^{5}\sum_{k'=1}^{3}\sum_{\tilde{m}} \frac{3r_{k'}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix}r_{k}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix} - \delta_{kk'}r^2\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\left\|\mathbf{r}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,j\end{pmatrix}\right\|^5} \\
&\quad \times \bigg(p_{k'}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{m}\\\ i,\ j\end{pmatrix} - p_{k'}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{n},\tilde{n}\\\ i,\ j\end{pmatrix}\bigg).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

Here $E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ i\end{array}\right)$ represents the contribution to the local field of the $i$-ion of the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell in $k$-direction due to the variation of the relative dipole moment between unit cells $\tilde{n}$ and $\tilde{m}$.

To evaluate the components of the local field of every ion in the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell we consider first the local field $E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ i\end{array}\right)$ neglecting the contribution from $\delta E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ i\end{array}\right)$ completely. $\delta E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ i\end{array}\right)$ will be added later, because this last contribution depends on the dipole moments of all other unit cells within the crystal (i.e. their local fields). Once $E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ i\end{array}\right)$ is evaluated for all unit cells in the crystal, we then superimpose $\delta E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ i\end{array}\right)$ to all the corresponding local field contributions $E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ i\end{array}\right)$ which makes a self-consistent calculation necessary.

Thus the local field present along the $k$-direction acting on $i$-ion of the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell can be written as

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{5} \sum_{l=1}^{3} S_{k l}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right) E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)=Q_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i
\end{array}\right)
\tag{10}
$$

with

$$
S_{k l}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)=\delta_{k l} \delta_{i j}-\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{3} T_{k k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right) \alpha_{k^{\prime} l}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)
\tag{11}
$$

and

$$
Q_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i
\end{array}\right)=E_{k}^{\mathrm{ext}}+\sum_{j=1}^{5} \sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{3} T_{k k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right) p_{k^{\prime}}^{i}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{n} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right),
\tag{12}
$$

where

$$
T_{k k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)=\sum_{\tilde{m}} \frac{3 r_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right) r_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)-\delta_{k k^{\prime}} r^{2}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}\left\|\mathbf{r}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)\right\|^{5}}+\frac{1}{3 \varepsilon_{0} v} \delta_{k k^{\prime}}.
\tag{13}
$$

By solving equation (10) for each unit cell $\tilde{n}$, we obtain the $(l, j)$ component, $E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ j\end{array}\right)$, for the local field after adding the contribution $\delta E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n} \\ j\end{array}\right)$. The total polarization $P_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{n}\end{array}\right)$ of the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell is expressed as

$$
P_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}
\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{v} \sum_{j=1}^{5}\left(p_{k}^{\mathrm{e}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)+p_{k}^{\mathrm{ion}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)\right)
\tag{14}
$$

with

$$
p_{k}^{\mathrm{ion}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)=Z_{k}^{*}(j) e s_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right) \tag{15}
$$

representing the absolute ionic dipole moment in the $k$-direction of the $j$-ion in the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell.

The connection between the optical dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{k l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\tilde{n})$ in the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell, polarization $P_{k}(\tilde{n})$ and the optical electric field $E_{l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}$ is expressed in the following way:

$$
\varepsilon_{k l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}=\delta_{k l^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0} v} \sum_{j=1}^{5} \sum_{l=1}^{3} \alpha_{k l}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right) \frac{\partial E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)}{\partial E_{l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}}. \tag{16}
$$

Note that $\partial E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{n}_{j}) / \partial E_{l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}$, representing the local electric field induced along the $l$-direction of the $j$-ion in the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell, by unit of the $l^{\prime}$-component of optical electric field, also consists of two contributions: the first one is obtained by solving the following equation:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{5} \sum_{l=1}^{3} S_{k l}\left(\begin{array}{cc}
\tilde{n} \\
i, & j
\end{array}\right) \frac{\partial E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)}{\partial E_{l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}}=\delta_{k l^{\prime}}, \tag{17}
$$

which is deduced from equation (10). The second contribution is given as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial \delta E_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)}{\partial E_{l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}}= & \sum_{j=1}^{5} \sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{3} \sum_{\tilde{m}} \frac{3 r_{k^{\prime}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right) r_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)-\delta_{k k^{\prime}} r^{2}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}\left\|\mathbf{r}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n}, \tilde{m} \\
i, j
\end{array}\right)\right\|^{5}} \\
& \times \sum_{l=1}^{3}\left(\alpha_{k l}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right) \frac{\partial E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)}{\partial E_{l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}}-\alpha_{k l}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right) \frac{\partial E_{l}^{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{n} \\
j
\end{array}\right)}{\partial E_{l^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{opt}}}\right) \tag{18}
\end{aligned}
$$

In electromagnetic theory [32], one proves that for light being polarized along the direction of the unit vector $\mathbf{u}(u_{1}, u_{2}, u_{3})$ and impinging on a dielectric medium characterized by the optical dielectric impermeability tensor $\tilde{\eta}^{\mathrm{opt}}=(\tilde{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{opt}})^{-1}$, the refractive index $n$ may be expressed as

$$
n=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{3} \sum_{l=1}^{3} \eta_{k l}^{\mathrm{opt}} u_{k} u_{l}\right)^{-1 / 2}. \tag{19}
$$

In the special case of light being polarized along the $k$-direction, the refractive index thus becomes $n_k = (\eta_{kk}^{\rm opt})^{-1/2}$.

## 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The calculation of the spontaneous polarization and the refractive indices over the $180^{\circ}$ domain wall in tetragonal $\mathrm{KNbO}_{3}$ are carried out by using the same numerical data at $270^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ of lattice parameters, effective ionic charges, and spontaneous ionic shits as in reference 24. In this study we consider the $180^{\circ}$ domain wall to coincide with the $yz$ plane in the bulk. The change of the spontaneous ionic shifts near the domain wall are given by [33]

$$
s_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)=\left(s_{k}(j)\right)^{0} \tanh \left(\frac{x\left(\begin{array}{c}
\tilde{m} \\
j
\end{array}\right)}{2 r_{\mathrm{c}}}\right),\qquad(20)
$$

where $x\left(\begin{array}{c}\tilde{m} \\ j\end{array}\right)$ is the $x$-position of the $j$-ion within the $\tilde{m}$ unit cell; $\left(s_{k}(j)\right)^{0}$ are the spontaneous ionic shifts in a homogeneous ideal crystal, and $r_{\mathrm{c}}$ represents the effective thickness of the domain wall (also called the *correlation radius of the order parameter*). These calculations are performed for a variety of effective thickness of the domain wall ranging between 5-20 $\mathring{A}$, as stated in an earlier theoretical work on tetragonal $\mathrm{BaTiO}_{3}$ by Zhirnov and quite commonly aceepted since then [4].

The spontaneous polarization in the $\tilde{n}$ unit cell, $P^{\rm spon}(\tilde{n})$, is defined as the total polarization, with the components $P_{k}(\tilde{n})$ being deduced from equation (14), in absence of any external field. The calculation shows, that only the 3-component of the spontaneous polarization $P_{3}^{\rm spon}(\tilde{n})$ is non-zero. The calculated values are reported in Fig. 1 for four different values of the effective thickness $r_{\mathrm{c}}$ of the domain wall ($r_{\mathrm{c}} = 5\ \mathring{A}$, $10\ \mathring{A}$, $15\ \mathring{A}$, $20\ \mathring{A}$) and show that for $|x| \gg 0$, the spontaneous polarization measures $-0.34\ \mathrm{C/m^2}$ and $+0.34\ \mathrm{C/m^2}$ for the $(-)$ region and $(+)$ region, respectively, in excel- lent agreement with the experimental data [34] of $\pm 0.30\ \mathrm{C/m^2}$. This holds when approaching the domain wall from both sides. In fact the 3-component of the spontaneous polarization measures exactly zero at the center of the domain wall with the absolute values left and right having opposite sign (see Fig. 1). ## 4. CONCLUSIONS

By using a microscopic model taking into account a quantum method based upon the orbital approximation and the dipole-dipole interaction due to the local field acting on the constituent ions, we have studied the ferroelectric and optical properties within $180^{\circ}$ ferroelectric domain walls of tetragonal

$KNbO_3$. The calculations show a dramatic dependence of spontaneous polarization and refractive indices on the effective domain wall thickness. For a large domain wall width, the behavior of the spontaneous polarization within the domain wall is quite similar to former theoretical work based on a three dimensional Landau-Ginzburg Model [38]. However, the model presented here gives different behaviors for the microscopic properties differentiating between the high and small width of the domain wall. Moreover, we find the spontaneous polarization to vanish in the middle of the domain wall as for $BaTiO_3$ at room temperature [28]. On the other hand, the $KNbO_3$ $180^\circ$ domain wall behaves like a biaxial crystal resulting in dramatic changes of the refractive indices $n_2$ and $n_3$. Therefore, the optical propagation properties over the domain wall should experience dramatic changes when using light with polarization parallel to the domain wall.
