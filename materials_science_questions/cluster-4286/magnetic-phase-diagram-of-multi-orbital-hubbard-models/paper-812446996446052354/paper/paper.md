# The possible ground states of doped holes in $(La_{1-x}Sr_x)_2CuO_4$

Shijie Xiong
Department of Physics, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210008, PR China

Received 12 March 1990; accepted for publication 22 May 1990
Communicated by A.A. Maradudin

On the basis of the Anderson lattice Hamiltonian, the ground states of the doped holes in $(La_{1-x}Sr_x)_2CuO_4$ are investigated by using a variational analysis. It is found that a single hole is localized within a linear cluster with length of about $8a$ ($a$ being the oxygen-oxygen spacing), and the hopping of holes produces conductivity with temperature dependence $\exp[-(c/k_{\mathrm{B}}T)^{1/4}]$ at low temperature (critical conductivity) and $T^{-1}\exp(-v/k_{\mathrm{B}}T)$ at higher temperature (thermal-excited hopping conductivity). The hole-cluster also breaks the AF correlation between the copper sites on its two sides, the AF coherence length is then proportional to $a/\sqrt{x}$. By increasing the doping, an infinite cluster may be formed to hold all the doped holes, the AF background is resolved into isolated spin-singlets or spins, and the Fermi-liquid-like features of the holes appear. The critical doping densities for these transitions are determined. Many unusual properties of this material can be quantitatively explained.

The investigation of the features of the doped holes in $(La_{1-x}Sr_x)_2CuO_4$ is of great interest for understanding the high $T_c$ phenomena. This problem has been studied from different points of view [1,2]. The purpose of this paper is to present a variational analysis of the ground states of the doped holes on the basis of the Anderson lattice Hamiltonian of the $CuO_2$ sheet. The starting point in choosing the trial wave-functions is the half-filled limit where the system is an insulating Heisenberg antiferromagnet. It is assumed that by doping the copper holes remain localized and the doped holes go to the oxygen orbitals. The size and the topological configuration of a single doped hole is determined. It is proven that by further doping the Fermi-liquid-like features of the oxygen holes appear and the system undergoes a series of phase transitions. Surprisingly, a wide range of experimental results can be explained by this approach even in a quantitative way. The applicability of this analysis to the electron-doped systems is discussed.

We write the Anderson lattice Hamiltonian [3] for the $CuO_2$ sheet as

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & \sum_{i, \sigma} \epsilon_{\mathrm{p}} a_{i \sigma}^{+} a_{i \sigma}+\sum_{j, \sigma} \epsilon_{\mathrm{d}} b_{j \sigma}^{+} b_{j \sigma}+\sum_{\substack{\langle i, i'\rangle \\
\sigma}} t_{1} a_{i \sigma}^{+} a_{i' \sigma} \\
& +\sum_{\substack{\langle i, j\rangle \\
\sigma}} t_{2}\left(a_{i \sigma}^{+} b_{j \sigma}+\text { h.c. }\right)+\sum_{j} U b_{j \uparrow}^{+} b_{j \uparrow} b_{j \downarrow}^{+} b_{j \downarrow}, \quad (1)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}$ are the oxygen and copper energy levels, $a_{i \sigma}^{+}$ ($a_{i \sigma}$) and $b_{j \sigma}^{+}$ ($b_{j \sigma}$) are creation (destruction) operators for oxygen electrons at site $i$ with spin $\sigma$ and copper electrons at site $j$ with spin $\sigma$, $t_2$ and $t_1$ are the nearest oxygen-copper and oxygen-oxygen hopping integrals, respectively. The Coulomb repulsion between electrons on copper sites is $U$. As in ref. [4], the Coulomb repulsion on the oxygen sites and the intersite Coulomb terms are not explicitly included in the Hamiltonian, we will take into account the effect of the oxygen on-site repulsion by excluding the double-occupation of the holes on one oxygen site in the trial hole wave-functions, and the intersite copper-oxygen repulsion only provides an energy shift for $\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}$ if the copper holes are assumed to remain localized.

It is well known that in the undoped case ($x=0$) the system is an insulating Heisenberg antiferromagnet [5]. There are presumably three subbands: the empty upper Hubbard subband, the full lower Hubbard subband and the full oxygen 2p-subband. A doped hole may be located at one of the full subbands, according to the values of $\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}$, $t_1$, $t_2$ and $U$ [6]. As many experiments on this material [7] in

dicate that the doped holes are of oxygen 2p char- acter, we assume that these holes are located at the oxygen 2p orbitals. Due to the existence of the hop- ping terms, the hole states tend to expand on the ox- ygen sublattice to reduce the kinetic energy. We as- sume that there is a cluster on the sublattice, including several connected oxygen sites, to hold a doped hole, and write the trial wave-function for the ground state of the hole as

$$
\left|\psi_{\sigma}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{i \sigma}|\Phi\rangle, \quad(2)
$$

where $n$ is the number of sites of the cluster, $|\Phi\rangle$ is the ground state in the undoped case. The topolog- ical structure and the size of the cluster can be de- termined by the variational principle. The energy shift of the system due to the formation of a hole cluster consists of three contributions:

$$
\Delta E=\Delta E_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}+\Delta E_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}+\Delta E_{\mathrm{AF}}, \quad(3)
$$

where $\Delta E_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}$ and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}$ are due to the copper-ox- ygen and oxygen-oxygen hopping terms in eq. (1), respectively, $\Delta E_{\mathrm{AF}}$ is the energy shift of the spin con figuration on the copper sublattice. $\Delta E_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}$ and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}$ can be calculated by the standard procedure:

$$
\Delta E_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}=\left\langle\psi_{\sigma}\left|H_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}\right| \psi_{\sigma}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Phi\left|H_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}\right| \Phi\right\rangle,
$$

$$
\Delta E_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}=\left\langle\psi_{\sigma}\left|H_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}\right| \psi_{\sigma}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Phi\left|H_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}\right| \Phi\right\rangle,
$$

where $H_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}$ are the copper-oxygen and oxygen-oxygen hopping terms in eq. (1). We have then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}} & =\frac{t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}\left(\frac{2}{n}-4+\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j} \frac{g_{j}\left(g_{j}-1\right)}{n^{2}}\right) \\
& +\frac{t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}} \sum_{j} \frac{g_{j}^{2}}{2 n^{2}}, \quad (4)
\end{aligned}
$$

where the sum for $j$ is taken over the copper sites which are the nearest neighbours of the oxygen sites belonging to the cluster, $g_{j}$ is the number of the clus ter sites nearest-neighbour to the $j$ th copper site. From the crystalline structure of the $\mathrm{CuO}_{2}$ sheet one has

$$
\sum_{j} g_{j}=2 n.
$$

Because in the present case the oxygen 2p level is sit- uated between the two Hubbard subbands, that is

$$
\epsilon_{d}+U-\epsilon_{p}>0, \quad \epsilon_{p}-\epsilon_{d}>0,
$$

$\Delta E_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}$ is minimized only if $g_{j}$ is identical for all $j$. For a finite cluster this condition is approximately satisfied when the cluster is a segment of a straight line at an angle of $45^{\circ}$ to the $a$ - or $b$-axis of the $\mathrm{CuO}_{2}$ sheet. For such a cluster, $g_{j}=2$, and we have

$$
\Delta E_{\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}}=\frac{t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}(3 / n-4)+\frac{2 t_{2}^{2}}{n\left(\overline{\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}\right)}, \quad (5)
$$

and

$$
\Delta E_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}=-2 t \text {. }
$$

Here the effect of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}$ on the shape has not been included because $t_{1} \ll t_{2}$ and it can be proven that for $n \lesssim 10$ the linear cluster still has the lowest energy even by inclusion of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{O}}$.

The formation of a hole-cluster changes the su- perexchange correlation of the nearby copper sites. In Anderson superexchange theory [8,9] the half- filled limit is the necessary condition for the ap- pearance of the AF superexchange $J$. In Zaanen and Sawatsky's derivation of superexchange for a two- band model [6], the mediating oxygen 2p orbital should be full so that the available phase space for the antiferromagnetic configuration is larger than the ferromagnetic one. When a pair of copper sites is mediated by an oxygen site belonging to a hole-clus- ter, these conditions are not satisfied. Now the cop- per-oxygen hoppings occur between the copper 3d orbitals and the hole-cluster orbital. According to Zaanen and Sawatsky's method, we are then left with a configuration-interaction problem with, as a basis set, the following hole states:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \phi_{1}=\left|\mathrm{L}_{\sigma} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{~L}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{2}=\left|\mathrm{R}_{\sigma} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \\
& \phi_{3}=\left|\mathrm{L}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{L}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{4}=\left|\mathrm{R}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \\
& \phi_{5}\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{L}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{6}=\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}} \mathrm{L}_{\sigma 0}\right\rangle, \\
& \phi_{7}=\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{8}=\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma 0}\right\rangle, \\
& \phi_{9}=\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{10}=\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{L}_{\sigma} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \\
& \phi_{11}=\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{L}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{12}=\left|\mathrm{C}_{\sigma} \mathrm{L}_{\sigma^{\prime}} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma 0}\right\rangle, \\
& \phi_{13}=\left|\mathrm{L}_{\sigma} \mathrm{L}_{\sigma^{\prime}} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma 0}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{14}=\left|\mathrm{L}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{~L}_{\sigma^{\prime}} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma}\right\rangle, \\
& \phi_{15}=\left|\mathrm{L}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle, \quad \phi_{16}=\left|\mathrm{L}_{\sigma} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma 0} \mathrm{R}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right\rangle,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\sigma_{0}$ is the spin of the hole-cluster, $\sigma$ and $\sigma^{\prime}$ are

the spins of two copper holes, L (R) denotes the left (right) copper hole and C is the cluster hole. For the AF configuration, $\sigma=-\sigma_{0}$ and $\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma_{0}$, only the states $\phi_{3}, \phi_{4}, \phi_{14}$ and $\phi_{16}$ are not allowed, and for the F configuration, $\sigma=-\sigma_{0}$ and $\sigma^{\prime}=-\sigma_{0}$, the forbidden states are $\phi_{1}, \phi_{2}, \phi_{13}$ and $\phi_{15}$. We immediately see that for these two spin configurations the scale of the available phase space is identical and the corresponding Hamiltonian matrices on the basis sets for them are equivalent. Thus, they have the same energy level, $E_{0}(\mathrm{AF})=E_{0}(\mathrm{~F})$. According to the definition of the superexchange [6], $J=E_{0}(\mathrm{AF})-E_{0}(\mathrm{~F})=0$, i.e. the AF correlation of the pair of copper sites is cut off by the hole-cluster. Another F configuration, $\sigma=\sigma_{0}$ and $\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma_{0}$, has a smaller phase space and then a higher energy, so we do not consider it here.

The energy cost for breaking an AF correlation of a nearest-neighbour pair on a spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ Heisenberg 2D square lattice is on average $0.329 J$ [10]. At the same time if a pair of the nearest-neighbour spins is isolated from the others, its exact ground state is the spin-singlet, $(1 / \sqrt{2})(|\uparrow \downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow \uparrow\rangle)$, and the cost for breaking the bond of a spin-singlet is
$$
\epsilon_{\text {bond }}=0.75 J, \quad(8)
$$
which is much larger than the average one. As a simple analysis we estimate the energy cost of such a breakdown of the AF correlation, which does not result in the destruction of the isolated spin-singlets, as
$$
\epsilon_{\text {link }}=\frac{1}{3}\left(4 \epsilon_{\text {average }}-\epsilon_{\text {bond }}\right)=0.19 J, \quad(9)
$$
where the terminology "link" is borrowed from the valence-bond description of the AF magnet [11]. For the low hole-density, the hole-clusters only break the links. Combining eqs. (3), (5), (6) and (9), we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E= & -2 t_{1}+\frac{t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}(3 / n-4) \\
& +\frac{t_{2}^{2}}{n\left(\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}\right)}+0.19 n J.
\end{aligned}
$$

Minimizing its yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
& n=\left[\frac{1}{0.19 J}\left(\frac{3}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}+\frac{2}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}}\right)\right]^{1 / 2} t_{2}, \\
& \Delta E=0.38 n J-2 t_{1}-\frac{4 t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Choosing a set of realistic values of the parameters from both the theoretical analysis [4] and the experimental measurements [12],
$$
\begin{aligned}
& t_{1}=0.2 \mathrm{eV}, \quad t_{2}=1.0 \mathrm{eV}, \quad \epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}=4 \mathrm{eV}, \\
& U=7 \mathrm{eV}, \quad J=0.12 \mathrm{eV},
\end{aligned}
$$
one obtains
$$
n \approx 8, \quad \Delta E \approx-1.37 \mathrm{eV}. \quad(13)
$$

If there are two holes doped, they may be situated at two isolated clusters or at one larger cluster. The energy cost for the isomer is $2 \Delta E$, while the cost for the latter is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E_{2}= & 0.19 n_{2} J-4 t_{1}+\frac{6 t_{1}}{n_{2}-1}+\frac{12 t_{2}^{2}}{n_{2}\left(\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}+U\right)} \\
& +\frac{8 t_{2}^{2}}{n_{2}\left(\overline{\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}}\right)}-\frac{8 t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}, \\
n_{2} \approx & 2\left[\frac{1}{0.19 J}\left(\frac{3 t_{1}}{2}+\frac{3 t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}}+\frac{2 t_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}}\right)\right]^{1 / 2},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{2}$ is the size of this larger cluster. In this calculation we exclude the double-occupation of the holes on one oxygen site in the trial wave-function, as implied ty the oxygen on-site repulsion. It can be seen that $\Delta E_{2}$ is slightly larger than $2 \Delta E$.

We conclude, then, that at low doping density the doped holes tend to be separated from each other, each hole is localized within a linear oxygen-cluster of length $l_{0}=8 a$ ( $a$ being the oxygen-oxygen spacing), at an angle of $45^{\circ}$ to the AF links, and the AF correlations between the copper sites on the two sides of the cluster are broken. As a result, the AF coherence length should be the average distance of the hole-clusters. Because the hole number per unit area is $x /$ $a^{2}$, the AF coherence length is approximately
$$
\xi \sim \frac{a}{\sqrt{x}}=\frac{2.8 \mathring{A}}{\sqrt{x}}. \quad(15)
$$

This is quantitatively consistent with the measurement [13]. If $\xi \sim l_{0}$, the long-range AF order is destroyed, the system undergoes the transition from AF phase to spin-glass phase. The doping density at this point is

$$
x_{\mathrm{AF}-\mathrm{SG}}=\left(a / l_{0}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}} \sim 0.015,
\tag{16}
$$
which is also consistent with the experimental data very well.

Although the ground state of a single hole is lo- calized, the hole can move among the possible states by the quantum or the thermal-excited hoppings. A lot of the cluster-states, with different sizes, shapes and at different positions and energy levels, are available for this motion. The distribution of these cluster-states is certainly random. The situation is similar to the motion of carriers in amorphous ma- terials [14]. According to the variable-range hop- ping theory [14], at low temperature there appears the so-called "critical conductivity" with the tem- perature dependence
$$
\sigma_{1} \propto \exp \left[-\left(c / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\right)^{1 / 4}\right].
\tag{17}
$$

If the temperature is raised, the conductivity due to the thermal-excited hoppings may exceed this criti- cal one. The hopping from one linear cluster of the ground state to its neighbouring one along the line by one oxygen-oxygen spacing is the easiest hopping. The mediate state for this hopping has an additional breakdown of the AF link at an end of the linear clus- ter, the energy barrier is then
$$
v=\epsilon_{\text {link }}=0.19 J \sim 0.023 \mathrm{eV},
\tag{18}
$$
and the conductivity behaves as
$$
\sigma_{2} \propto T^{-1} \exp \left(-v / k_{\mathrm{B}} T\right),
\tag{19}
$$
where the factor $T^{-1}$ is due to the scattering with phonons. The measurement of the dc conductivity in the $La_{2} CuO_{4}$ system [15] shows the behaviour of eq.(17) in the temperature range 4-25 K and the be- haviour of eq. (19) in the range 40-100 K. Espe- cially, the measured value of $v$ is $0.024 eV$ , strongly supporting the present arguments.

If the doping is further increased, the hole-clusters may be connected to each other and extended to the whole system. We consider, then, an infinite cluster which contains all the doped holes. By a similar way the energy shift due to the formation of such a clus- ter can be calculated for small $x$ ,
$$
\Delta E_{\mathrm{inf}}=\Delta E_{0}+\frac{\bar{g} N_{0}}{4}\left(\epsilon_{\mathrm{s}}-4 x t_{1}\right),
$$

$$
\Delta E_{0}=-\frac{4 x t_{2}^{2} N_{0}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}+U-\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}},
\tag{20}
$$
where $\bar{g}=4 N / N_{0}, N(N_{0})$ is the number of sites of the cluster (the oxygen sublattice). The ratio $\bar{g}$ can be regarded as the average coordination number of the cluster. By the consideration of eqs. (8) and (9), $\epsilon_{s}$ , the energy cost for breaking an AF correlation, is also $\bar{g}$ -dependent,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_{\mathrm{s}} & =\epsilon_{\text {link }}, \quad \text { if } \bar{g} \leqslant 3, \\
& =3 \epsilon_{\text {link }} / \bar{g}+(1-3 / \bar{g}) \epsilon_{\text {bond }}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$

Minimizing $\Delta E_{inf }$ with respect to $\bar{g}$ yields:
$$\text{if }x<\epsilon_{\text{link}} / 4 t_{1}: \quad \bar{g}=0\text{;}$$

$$\text{if }\epsilon_{\text{link}} / 4 t_{1}<x<\epsilon_{\text{bond}} / 4 t_{1}\text{ :}$$

$$\bar{g}=3, \quad \Delta E_{\mathrm{inf}}=\Delta E_{0}+3 N_{0}\left(\frac{1}{4} \epsilon_{\mathrm{link}}-x t_{1}\right) ;$$

$$\text{if }x>\epsilon_{\text{bond}} / 4 t_{1}\text{ :}$$

$$\begin{array}{r}
\bar{g}=4, \quad \Delta E_{\text {inf }}=\Delta E_{0}+N_{0}\left(\frac{3}{4} \epsilon_{\text {link }}+\frac{1}{4} \epsilon_{\text {bond }}-4 x t_{1}\right). \\
(22)
\end{array}$$

Therefore, when $x<\epsilon_{link } / 4 t_{1}$ , the infinite cluster is unstable, the doped holes are separated as men- tioned above. In the range of $\epsilon_{link } / 4 t_{1}<x<\epsilon_{bond } / 4 t_{1}$ ,an infinite cluster with the coordination number 3 may appear. At the same time, the structure with all the doped holes separated is also possible, its energy shift is
$$\Delta E_{\text {sep }}=\Delta E_{0}+2 N_{0}\left(n x \epsilon_{\text {link }}-x t_{1}\right).$$

By comparing $\Delta E_{inf }$ and $\Delta E_{sep }$ , we can determine which structure is more stable. In this way we obtain that in the doping range
$$
x_{\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{A}} \equiv \frac{\epsilon_{\text {bond }}}{4 t_{1}}>x>\max \left(\frac{\epsilon_{\text {link }}}{4 t_{1}}, \frac{3 \epsilon_{\text {mnk }}}{4 t_{1}+8 n \epsilon_{\text {link }}}\right) \equiv x_{\mathrm{SG}-\mathrm{L}}
\tag{23}
$$
the infinite cluster with $\bar{g}=3$ is established. In this phase all the AF links are broken and the AF back- ground is resolved into isolated spin-singlets, the traps for the doped holes are released and the band fea- tures are established. The phase can be regarded as a mixture of Fermi-liquid and spin-liquid, we willcall it liquid phase. Using the definitions in eq. (23) and the values in eq. (12), we obtain the following critical densities,

$$
\chi_{\mathrm{SG}-\mathrm{L}} \sim 0.0303, \quad \chi_{\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{A}} \sim 0.113. \tag{24}
$$

The systematic measurement of conductivity in the region of eq. (23) evidently shows the Fermi-liquid-like features [16]. Because the copper spins are combined into isolated singlets of small size, their magnetic moments cannot show. Thus, at low temperature, it is not easy to detect the static magnetic moments or the magnetic order, and the Curie behaviour of the susceptibility disappears. The measurement of susceptibility at low temperature in this doping region [16] and the muon-spin-rotation study of magnetism in $(La_{0.925} Sr_{0.075})_{2} CuO_{4}$ below 90 mK[17] clearly confirm these arguments. The topological structure of this phase is similar to that of the RVB states [18], except that now the links between the spin-singlets are all cut off, the singlets, hence, are the realistic pairs. Interestingly, the superconducting phase also falls into this doping region. This means that the singlet pairs of the localized Cu holes and the Cooper pairs of the itinerant holes co-exist. This fact can be interpreted from two different points of view: (i) The formation of the Cooper pairs is due to the leakage of the pairs from the localized sites to the itinerant sub-system, just like the leakage of the Cooper pairs across the S-N interface in the proximity effect [19]. This leakage of pairs of Cu holes leaves some Cu sites in the $3 d^{10}$ state. It may be a possible origin of superconductivity. (ii) The formation of the Cu singlet pairs dramatically suppresses the effect of $U$ on the itinerant holes, because an itinerant hole almost does not magnetically interact with a spin-singlet. The carriers seem to be free from the strong-correlation effect, and the role of the interactions with phonons arises and may become a dominant one. The latter point of view is likely to be a better explanation for the 214 system, if we consider the other basic facts, such as the isotope effect and the details of the phase diagrams [20]. Some further investigations on the former seem also necessary.

For this phase, if the temperature is raised, the bonds of the singlets can also be destroyed, because the phase with isolated spins has high entropy. The susceptibility then increases with temperature, as shown by the measurement [16].

When $x>\chi_{\mathrm{L}-\mathrm{A}}, \bar{g}$ takes its largest value, 4 . This means that the AF background is resolved into isolated spins, and the holes go through all the oxygen sites. Consequently, the static magnetic moments and the Curie behaviour of susceptibility are restored, and the metallic features remain, but the residual resistivity becomes larger due to the re-appearance of the magnetic interaction. These results are also well consistent with the measurements in this region [16]. This phase can be regarded as an alloy with isolated magnetic moments.

It is surprising that such a simple analysis can explain so many unusual properties of this material. An important factor for the formation of the liquid phase is the direct oxygen-oxygen hopping, as can be seen from eq. (23). For the electron-doping system, Nd-Ce-Cu-O, the copper electrons become itinerant via the presence of the 4f states of Nd or Ce atoms, while the oxygen electrons remain localized, a similar analysis is applicable.
