# A Microscopic Model of the FeAs Plane in the Iron-Based Superconductors: Orbital Degeneracy, Geometric Frustration and Structural Transition

Tao Li

Department of Physics, Renmin University of China, Beijing, 100872, P.R.China

(Received August 14, 2008)

We construct a two-band model for the FeAs plane in the newly discovered Iron-based superconductors based on quantum chemistry consideration and the magnetic correlation pattern observed by neutron scattering. The orbital degeneracy between the Fe $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbital and their covalence with the As 4p orbitals play a key role in the model. The model explains naturally the large geometric frustration which is needed for the formation of the magnetic order with a wave vector at $(\pi,0)$ and provides a natural understanding of the tetragonal-orthorhombic structural phase transition as a kinetic-energy-driven Jahn-Teller effect. An extended s-wave pairing is proposed for the superconducting state based on symmetry consideration.

The newly discovered Iron-based superconductors F-doped LaOFeAs¹) and its derivatives²⁻⁵) with rare earth substitution arose many interests in the community. Similar to the high-$T_c$ cuprates, the Iron-based superconductors also have a layered structure in which the role of the Copper oxygen plane in the high-$T_c$ cuprates is played by the FeAs plane. At the same time, both systems show similar phase diagram with antiferromagnetic ordered phase and superconducting phase next to each other.¹¹)

Despite these similarities, the cuprates and the pnictides have some important differences. For example, in the antiferromagnetic ordered phase of the undoped pnictides, the system is actually a bad metal rather than a strong insulator. At the same time, the magnetic ordering wave vector is located at $(\pi,0)$ rather than $(\pi,\pi)$. The experiments also report structural phase transition from tetragonal to orthorhombic symmetry at a temperature just above the Néel temperature.

The origin of superconductivity in the doped pnictides is hotly debated. Several groups have examined the band structure as calculated from Density functional theory(DFT) and its DMFT improvement.⁶⁻¹⁰) Not to one's surprise, most of the spectral weight close to the Fermi energy are contributed by the Fe 3d orbitals, as the Fe is the only ion in the system that posses a unclosed shell. Thus, a microscopic model for the system can be very complicated since all five of the Fe 3d orbitals are involved in principle. A relevant microscopic model, besides being as generic as possible, should first account for the peculiarities of the undoped pnictides: the system is neither a good band metal,though it has a unclosed 3d-shell, nor a good band insulator, though it has an even number of electrons per site.

Another constraint, or clue, for the relevant microscopic model is the magnetic order. On the square lattice, a magnetic order at $(\pi,0)$ is quite unusual. It is equally unusual that the system show metallic rather than insulating behavior in the magnetic ordered state. As the magnetic ordered phase is next to the superconducting phase, it is natural to expect the magnetic correlation to play a key role in the origin of superconductivity.

Some people tried to formulate a phenomenological theory based on the results of the DFT calculations.¹²⁻¹⁴) In these approaches, the existence of small electron pocket and small hole pocket found in DFT calculation and their nesting plays a key role to account for transport and magnetic properties of the parent compounds. Namely, the undoped system is by accident very close to a semi-metal and the instability caused by nesting between the small electron pocket and the small hole pocket gives rise to the magnetic order at $(\pi,0)$. Except being not generic (as there is no general reason to guarantee the existence and nesting of the small Fermi pockets), these approaches also suffers from inconsistency with the experiments, as the resistivity *decreases* rather than *increases* when the magnetic order builds up in the undoped system. At the same time, the concept of nesting becomes totally irrelevant to account for a ordered moment as large as one $\mu_B$ as found by the DFT calculation.

We propose a two-band tight-binding model for the low energy physics of the FeAs plane in this paper. The two bands, which is derived from the two degenerate Fe 3d orbitals, $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$, enjoy the largest itineracy among all the five Fe 3d bands as a result of the covalence between these two orbitals and the As $4p_x$ and $4p_y$ orbitals. Both the crystal field splitting and the covalence with the As 4p orbitals are important for singling out the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals as the relevant orbitals for low energy physics of the system. The peculiarities of the undoped system is interpreted as a result of filling two degenerate bands with two electrons per site. The covalence with the As 4p orbitals also explains naturally the large geometric frustration which is necessary for the formation of the magnetic order with a wave vector at $(\pi,0)$ as found in experiments. The hopping bridged by the As 4p orbitals is peculiar in that it is anisotropic. This anisotropy in kinetic energy provides an interesting way to break the degeneracy between the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ bands and lower the symmetry of the system from tetragonal to orthorhombic spontaneously via a kinetic-energy-driven Jahn-Teller process. Finally, we present some ar-

guments for an extended s-wave pairing in the supercon- ducting state based on symmetry consideration and the local magnetic correlation pattern.

There are five 3d orbitals. In principle, all these five 3d orbitals can contribute to the density of state at Fermi energy. However, not all the five 3d orbitals are equally important for the low energy physics. In the presence of crystal field, the five-fold degenerate Fe 3d atomic or- bitals will split according to the irreducible representa- tions of the crystal symmetry. At the same time, the bands derived from the five 3d orbitals have quite dif- ferent kinetic energy: the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ bands enjoy the largest itineracy among the five bands as a result of the hybridization of the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals with the As 4p orbitals. The remaining three orbitals, namely $3d_{xy}$, $3d_{3z^{2}-r^{2}}$ and $3d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ are more localized as their hybridization with the As 4p orbitals is much less effec- tive. Here we assume the crystal field splitting and the orbital selective hopping effect to be sufficiently strong to retain only the Fe $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals for the dis- cussion of the low energy physics.

In the FeAs plane of the undoped LaOFeAs, each Fe ion sits at the center of a squashed(along the normal of the FeAs plane) tetrahedron formed by four neighboring As ions(see Fig. 1). Using the lattice constants reported in the literature, $^{7)}$ one estimates that the Fe-As bond makes an angle of about 58.8 degree with the normal of the FeAs plane, while in a perfect tetrahedron the cor- responding angle should be 54.7 degree. Recent experi- ments have found the angle $\phi$ is material dependent and some material can even have angle $\phi$ slightly smaller than 54.7 degree, namely the tetrahedron is slightly elongatedrather than squashed along $z-axis.^{15)}$

In an ideal tetrahedron environment, the five fold de- generate Fe 3d orbitals will split into the $E_{g}$ doublet $(3d_{3z^{2}-r^{2}}$ and $3d_{xy})$ and the $t_{2g}$ triplet$(3d_{xz},3d_{yz}$ and $3d_{x^{2}-y^{2}})$. If we assume that the crystal field is con- tributed mainly by the four neighboring As ions, one would expect the two $E_{g}$ orbitals to have lower energy than the three $t_{2g}$ orbitals, as the electron cloud of the $E_{g}$ orbitals point toward the vacancy of the As ions while the electron cloud of the $t_{2g}$ orbitals point toward the As ions(see Fig. 2).

When the tetrahedron is squashed along the $z$ direc- tion, the degeneracy between the $3d_{3z^{2}-r^{2}}$ orbital and the $3d_{xy}$ orbital will be lifted and the $3d_{3z^{2}-r^{2}}$ orbital will have a lower energy than that of the $3d_{xy}$ orbital, as the electron cloud of the $3d_{3z^{2}-r^{2}}$ orbital has a better chance to avoid the As ions(see Fig. 2). Similarly, the de-generacy between the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbital and the $3d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital will be lifted and the $3d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital will have a higher energy than that of the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbital. However, as the squashing distortion is a secondary ef- fect, we expect the splitting between the $3d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital and the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbital to be much smaller than that between the $E_{g}$ doublet and the $t_{2g}$ triplet. We note that in the tetragonal structure of the FeAs plane, the degen- eracy between the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals is protected by symmetry.

The divalent Fe ions in the FeAs plane have six elec- trons in their 3d shell. If we assume the crystal splitting to be sufficiently large, then the $E_{g}$ doublet would be fully occupied and the $3d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital would be totally empty. This would leave us a very peculiar situation in which the Fermi energy lies within the two-fold degen- erate $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ bands which are both half filled. We think this is the reason that the parent compounds, with six electrons in five 3d orbitals, is neither a usual band insulator nor a usual band metal. However, as the

splitting between the $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ and the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbitals is small and even being negative in some material, one would wonder if we should include the $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbital in our description of the low energy physics of the system. Here we note in advance that the bands derived from $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals have much larger itineracy as compared to that of $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbital as a result of the indirect hopping bridged by As. Thus, we believe the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ bands have much more importance than the $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ band in the low energy physics.

In the following, we assume the crystal splitting to be large enough to neglect the filled $E_g$ doublet and the orbital selective hopping effect to be large enough to neglect the $3d_{x^2-y^2}$ band in the discussion of low energy physics. With this simplification, we are left with a model with two degenerate bands, $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$. In this model, each Fe site can accommodate at most four electrons in the two degenerate bands. In the parent compound, each Fe site have two electrons on average in the two degenerate bands and the system is thus half filled.

Now we construct a tight-binding model for the system. The interactions between the electrons on the same Fe site can be classified into three types, namely the intra-orbital Hubbard repulsion U , the spin independent part of the inter-orbital Hubbard repulsion $U_1$, and the spin dependent part of the inter-orbital Hubbard repulsion J(the usual Hund's rule coupling). From the definition of these terms, one easily see that the inequality $U > U_1 > \frac{J}{4} > 0$ should be satisfied. These interaction terms is represented by the model Hamiltonian

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{U}=U \sum_{i}(&n_{i, x z, \uparrow} n_{i, x z, \downarrow}+n_{i, y z, \uparrow} n_{i, y z, \downarrow}) \\
+U_{1} \sum_{i}(&n_{i, x z, \uparrow}+n_{i, x z, \downarrow})(n_{i, y z, \uparrow}+n_{i, y z, \downarrow}) \\
&-J \sum_{i} \vec{S}_{i, x z} \cdot \vec{S}_{i, y z}
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

in which $n_{i, x z, \uparrow}=c_{i, x z, \uparrow}^{\dagger} c_{i, x z, \uparrow}$ denotes the number of up spin electron in the $3d_{xz}$ orbital. $\vec{S}_{i, x z}=$ $\frac{1}{2} c_{i, x z, \alpha}^{\dagger} \vec{\sigma}_{\alpha, \beta} c_{i, x z, \beta}$ denotes the spin density on the $3d_{xz}$ orbital. The meaning of other terms in the equation is similar.

We now consider the kinetic energy of the Fe 3d electrons. The Fe 3d electron can hop between Fe sites either directly or through the indirect hopping process bridged by the As 4p orbitals(see Fig. 3). As can be easily seen from Fig. 3, the As-bridged hopping is much more effective for the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbital than for other Fe 3d orbitals, as their electron clouds have the best chance to reach the As ions which sit above(or below) the center of the Fe plaquette. Such a orbital selective hopping effect provides a natural explanation for the much larger itinerancy of the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ bands as compared to other 3d bands found in DFT calculations. $^{7)}$ Thus, we believe the kinetic energy of the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ bands comes mainly from the As-bridged hopping, while the itineracy of other Fe 3d bands still come from direct hopping. As we will show below, the As-bridged hopping provides a natural understanding for the large geometric frustration needed to account for the magnetic order at $(\pi, 0)$ on a square lattice, which is almost impossible if the direct hopping term dominates the kinetic energy.

The As ion has three 4p orbitals in its outmost shell. Since the $4p_z$ orbital is far away from the Fermi energy, we consider only the hopping path mediated by the $4p_x$ and $4p_y$ orbitals of As ion. As a result of the sign change between the four lobes of the $3d_{xz}$ orbital, an electron in $3d_{xz}$ orbital can hop only via the As $4p_x$ orbital. Similarly, an electron in $3d_{yz}$ orbital can hop only via the As $4p_y$ orbital. Thus, the indirect hopping mediated by the As $4p_x$ and $4p_y$ orbitals conserves the orbital index, namely, an electron initially on the Fe $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbital can only hop onto the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbital of destination Fe ions(the inclusion of the hopping path mediated by $4p_z$ orbital will break such a symmetry)(see Fig. 3).

Fig. 3. A schematic representation of the As 4p orbital aided hopping between neighboring Fe ions on the square lattice. Note that the As ion lies above or below the Fe plane. Simple symmetry consideration shows that the electron on Fe $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbital can hop only through the As $4p_x(4p_y)$ orbital. This explains the conservation of orbital index and the anisotropy of the hopping Hamiltonian. The electron wave function on the yellow(blue) lobes have positive(negative) values. $t_A$ denotes the $\sigma$-like hybridization between the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ and the $4p_x(4p_y)$orbital, $t_B$ denotes the $\pi$-like hybridization between the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$and the $4p_x(4p_y)$orbital.

Another peculiarity about the As ion aided effective hopping is that the hopping integral between next-nearest-neighboring Fe ions is anisotropic(the hopping between nearest neighboring Fe ions remains isotropic). As a result, the dispersion relation of the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ band has an orthorhombic rather than tetragonal symmetry(the tetragonal symmetry of the system is recovered when both bands are taken into account). This anisotropy originates from the anisotropic nature of the $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals. To be more specific, we introduce two kinds of hopping integral, $t_A$ and $t_B$, to denote the $\sigma$-like and $\pi$-like hybridization between the $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbital and the corresponding $4p_x(4p_y)$ orbital shown in Fig. 3. Then, the As-bridged hopping term between

neighboring Fe sites are given approximately by
$$
t=2 \frac{t_{A} t_{B}}{\Delta}, t_{1}=\frac{t_{A}^{2}}{\Delta}, t_{2}=\frac{t_{B}^{2}}{\Delta}, \tag{2}
$$
in which $t$ denotes the hopping integral between nearest neighboring(NN) Fe sites, $t_1$ and $t_2$ denote the hopping integrals between next nearest neighboring(NNN) Fe sites in the two diagonal directions, $\Delta$ denotes the energy splitting between the Fe $3d_{xz}(3d_{yz})$ orbital and the As $4p_x(4p_y)$ orbital. The factor of two for $t$ comes from the fact that there are two hopping paths between the nearest neighboring Fe ions.

Taking all these considerations into account, we arrive at the following model for the effective hopping between the Fe ions.
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{t}= & -t \sum_{i, \vec{\delta}, \sigma}\left[\left(c_{i, x z, \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i+\vec{\delta}, x z, \sigma}+c_{i, y z, \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i+\vec{\delta}, y z, \sigma}\right)+H . C .\right] \\
- & t_{1} \sum_{i, \sigma}\left[\left(c_{i, x z, \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i+\vec{\delta}^{\prime}, x z, \sigma}+c_{i, y z, \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i+\vec{\delta}^{\prime \prime}, y z, \sigma}\right)+H . C .\right] \\
- & t_{2} \sum_{i, \sigma}\left[\left(c_{i, x z, \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i+\vec{\delta}^{\prime \prime}, x z, \sigma}+c_{i, y z, \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i+\vec{\delta}^{\prime}, y z, \sigma}\right)+H . C .\right],
\end{aligned}
$$
in which $\vec{\delta}=\vec{e}_{x}$ or $\vec{e}_{y}$ denotes the nearest neighboring vectors on the square lattice form by Fe ions. $\vec{\delta}^{\prime}=\vec{e}_{x}+\vec{e}_{y}$ and $\vec{\delta}^{\prime \prime}=\vec{e}_{x}-\vec{e}_{y}$ denotes the next nearest neighboring vectors. From Fig. 3, it is easy to see that $t_{A}$ and $t_{B}$ have different signs. As a result, $t<0$ and $t_{1}, t_{2}>0$. However, on the square lattice, the sign of nearest neighboring hopping can be made positive with a suitable choice of gauge. In the below, we will take $t>0$.

The total Hamiltonian of the system is the sum of the on-site term $H_{U}$ and the hopping term $H_{t}$. Before looking at the phase diagram of the model, we first estimate the model parameters. According to DFT calculations, the Hubbard repulsion $U$ and $U_{1}$ are about $4 eV$ in magnitude and Hund's rule coupling $J$ is about $1 eV$ in magnitude. For the hopping terms, as both the Fe3 $d_{x z}-\mathrm{As} 4 p_{x}$ separation and the hopping integral between the two are of the order of $1 eV$, we estimate the effective hopping integral between the Fe ions mediated by the As ions to be also of the order of $1 eV$. Thus the system has a moderate level of electron correlation. Another important interaction parameter is the ratio between the NNN and NN hopping integral. In our model, the ratio is given by
$$
\frac{t_{1}}{t}=\frac{1}{2} \frac{t_{A}}{t_{B}}, \frac{t_{2}}{t}=\frac{1}{2} \frac{t_{B}}{t_{A}} . \tag{4}
$$

Thus, the NNN to NN hopping ratio is at least 0.5, much larger than that one would expect if there is only direct hopping.

A full solution of the model Hamiltonian is obviously impossible at the present stage. Here we contend with ourself with a mean field analysis of the model at half-filling and a qualitative analysis of the pairing symmetry in the superconducting state.

In the half filled case, each site is occupied on average by two electrons. Since the intra-orbital Hubbard repulsion $U$ is larger than the inter-orbital Hubbard repulsion $U_{1}$, the two electrons tend to occupy different orbital and the remaining Hund's rule coupling tends to align up the spin of the electrons on both orbitals. Thus each individual Fe ion carries approximately a unit spin angular momentum and shows two Bohr magneton of magnetic moment. The inclusion of the hopping terms between the Fe ions will induce antiferromagnetic spin exchange between neighboring Fe ions within each band. Since the frustration ratio is already quite large, it is a nontrivial problem what kind of magnetic order is favored for the realistic model parameter. To elucidate this problem, we conduct a mean field calculation on the magnetic order for the half-filled system. For simplicity, we restrict our calculation to collinear magnetic order with ordering wave vector at $(\pi, \pi)$ and $(\pi, 0)$. The mean field Hamiltonian, apart from the chemical potential term and the constant energy term, is given by
$$
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{MF}}=H_{t}-(2 U+J) \sum_{i} M_{i}\left(S_{i, x z}^{z}+S_{i, y z}^{z}\right), \quad(5)
$$
in which $M_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\langle\sum \sigma n_{i, x z, \sigma}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\sum \sigma n_{i, y z, \sigma}\rangle=$  $M \exp (i \vec{Q} \cdot \vec{R}_{i})$ is the order parameter for the magnetic order, $\vec{Q}=(\pi, \pi)$ or $(\pi, 0)$ is the ordering wave vector. From the mean field Hamiltonian, we find Hund's coupling acts to enhance the effect of the intra-orbital Hubbard $U$ term and in the following we take it into account by defining $\tilde{U}=U+\frac{J}{2}$.

The mean field self-consistent equation for the order parameter is given by
$$
1=\frac{2 \tilde{U}}{N} \sum_{\mathrm{k}}\left[\frac{\left(\xi_{\mathrm{k}}-E_{\mathrm{k}}^{+}\right) n_{\mathrm{k}}^{+}}{\Delta_{A F}^{2}+\left(\xi_{\mathrm{k}}-E_{\mathrm{k}}^{+}\right)^{2}}+\frac{\left(\xi_{\mathrm{k}}-E_{\mathrm{k}}^{-}\right) n_{\mathrm{k}}^{-}}{\Delta_{A F}^{2}+\left(\xi_{\mathrm{k}}-E_{\mathrm{k}}^{-}\right)^{2}}\right],
$$
in which the sum is over the reduced Brillouin zone of the corresponding magnetic order, $n_{\mathrm{k}}^{ \pm}=1-\theta\left(E_{\mathrm{k}}^{ \pm}\right), \Delta_{A F}=$ $\tilde{U} M, E_{\mathrm{k}}^{ \pm}$ is given by
$$
E_{\mathrm{k}}^{ \pm}=\frac{\xi_{\mathrm{k}}+\xi_{\mathrm{k}+\vec{Q}} \pm \sqrt{\left(\xi_{\mathrm{k}}-\xi_{\mathrm{k}+\vec{Q}}\right)^{2}+4 \Delta_{A F}^{2}}}{2}, \quad(7)
$$
in which $\xi_{\mathrm{k}}=\varepsilon_{x z, \mathrm{k}}-\mu$ and the chemical potential $\mu$ is determined by the half-filling condition.

We have solved the self-consistent equation numerically. Fig. 4 shows the critical value $\tilde{U}_{c}$ for the formation of magnetic order at wave vector $(\pi, \pi)$ and $(\pi, 0)$ as a function of $\frac{t_{A}}{t_{B}}$. We find the critical value for both orders increase with the frustration ratio $\frac{t_{A}}{t_{B}}$ and the order with a lower critical value is always more stable. Thus, for $\frac{t_{A}}{t_{B}}<2$, or $\frac{t_{1}}{t}<1$, the $(\pi, \pi)$ order is more stable, while for $\frac{t_{A}}{t_{B}}>2$, or $\frac{t_{1}}{t}>1$, the $(\pi, 0)$ order become more stable. The $(\pi, 0)$ order observed in experiment then indicates that the system is strongly frustrated. The As-bridged indirect hopping proposed in our model seems to be the only way to account for such strong geometrical frustration.

Finally we discuss the pairing symmetry of the super-
conducting phase. As in the cuprate superconductors,
the antiferromagnetic superexchange responsible for the
magnetic ordering at half-filling will also induce electron
pairing at finite doping in the singlet channel. Here we
present a qualitative analysis of the possible pairing sym-
metry in the singlet channel. In our model, the antifer-
romagnetic superexchange takes place within each band
and thus the singlet pairing induced by it is an intra-
band electron pairing. In the superconducting phase, the
orthorhombic distortion of the lattice is gone. However,
the $3d_{xz}$ and the $3d_{yz}$ bands individually still have an or-
thorhombic symmetry(see Fig. 6). Thus we should clas-
sify the intra-band electron pairing according to the or-
thorhombic rather than tetragonal point group. For the
orthorhombic point group $C_{2v}$, there are two irreducible
representations in the singlet channel, namely the d-wave
pairing and the extended s-wave pairing representation.
The sign structure for both pairing symmetry in real
space is illustrated in Fig. 6. In the d-wave pairing case,
the pairing amplitudes is odd with respect to the reflec-
tion in the two diagonal directions and thus the pairing
amplitudes in these two directions are forced to be zero.
In the extended s-wave pairing case, the pairing ampli-
tude is even with respect to both reflection and thus can
be nonzero in all direction.

As we have mentioned above, the $(\pi,0)$ magnetic order
at half-filling is stable only when $t_1 > t$. In such a case,
the superexchange in the diagonal direction with hopping
integral $t_1$ should be the strongest and we would expect
the strongest singlet pairing in this direction. This is ob-
viously inconsistent with the d-wave pairing symmetry
which enforces the pairing amplitude in the diagonal di-
rection to be zero. Thus we believe the extended s-wave
pairing is the most possible pairing symmetry, provided
that the magnetic exchange is the origin of the pairing.

The above argument can also be formulated in the
momentum space as follows. According to the weak cou-
pling analysis, the pairing gap induced by the exchange
of magnetic fluctuation peaked at momentum $\vec{Q}$ should
satisfy the following inequality on the Fermi surface$^{17)}$

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{k}} \Delta_{\mathrm{k}+\vec{Q}}<0. \tag{8}
$$

For $\vec{Q}=(\pi,0)$, this inequality is incompatible with the
d-wave pairing which has a gap function of the form
$\cos(\mathrm{k}_x)-\cos(\mathrm{k}_y)$, but can be compatible with the ex-
tended s-wave pairing if the pairing amplitudes along the

diagonal direction is large enough.

Up to now, we have neglected the inter-orbital couplings in our discussion of the superconductivity. In the presence of such couplings, the Cooper pairs in the two bands get entangled with each other. Such entanglement will inevitably increase the effective mass of the Cooper pair in each band and reduce the superfluid density. More specifically, Hund's rule coupling will induce entanglement between the Cooper pairs in the spin channel(in a way much alike the projection operator does in the construction of the matrix-product-type ground state for the AKLT model $^{16)}$), while the inter-orbital Hubbard interaction will induce entanglement in the charge channel. Since Hund's rule coupling tends to enlarge the spin value at each site and thus enhance the classical nature of the electron spin, it seems be at odds with the spin singlet pairing which is of totally quantum nature. Thus, in addition to the entanglement effect, Hund's rule coupling seems to also suppress electron pairing in the singlet channel. A detailed analysis of these interesting problems is left for the future works.

In summary, a microscopic model for the FeAs plane of the newly discovered Iron-based superconductors is proposed. In our model, the orbital degeneracy between the Fe $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals plays an essential role. The model predicts that the Fe $3d_{xz}$ and $3d_{yz}$ orbitals have the largest itineracy among the five Fe 3d orbitals as a result of their hybridization with the As 4p orbitals. The covalence with the As 4p orbitals also explains naturally the large geometric frustration which is necessary for the formation of the magnetic order with a wave vector at $(\pi,0)$. A kinetic-energy-driven Jahn-Teller mechanism is proposed for the structure transition accompanying the magnetic order. Based on symmetry consideration and the local magnetic correlation pattern, we propose an extended s-wave pairing for the superconducting state.
