# Magnetic Mechanism of Superconductivity in Coupled Spin-Fermion Systems

Masatoshi IMADA, Naoto NAGAOSA† and Yasuhiro HATSUGAI†

Department of Physics, College of Liberal Arts,
University of Saitama, Shimo-Okubo, Urawa 338
†Department of Applied Physics, Faculty of Engineering,
University of Tokyo, Hongo, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo 113

(Received July 4, 1988)

A coupled spin-fermion model is presented as a two-band model of high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ superconductivity. The experimental relevance of the model is discussed. The high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ superconductivity is discussed the continuity of the strong coupling superconductivity to the weak coupling region. The attractive interaction of holes seems to exist over a wide range of parameters from weak to strong coupling cases. The small transfer region is useful to understand the pairing mechanism in the relevant strong coupling region. The model includes the effective Hamiltonian of the single-band Hubbard model as a special case.

The discovery of high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ superconductors focused attention on mechanisms of superconductivity of purely electronic origin. In particular, possible mechanisms of high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ superconductivity were looked for from various points of two-band picture.$^{1-10)}$ Experimentally, it is not yet clear enough which orbitals are responsible for the conduction of holes.

Although a definite conclusion on the existence of the superconducting transition in any idealized electron model has not been obtained yet, several ideas have been proposed from insight obtained from various numerical efforts on Hubbard-type models. The attractive interaction between distortions of antiferromagnetic correlation around two holes was suggested in the $d$-$p_\sigma$ model. A possible origin of the Cooper pairing comes from the attractive interaction between two Kondo-like singlets$^{2)}$ through the antiferromagnetic correlation. The role of magnetic frustration was also pointed out.$^{2,4,7)}$

At the present stage of the understanding, we believe that it is important to introduce more general and clearer viewpoint to understand the essence of high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ superconductivity. One of the most fundamental questions is whether the superconducting state may be realized in a model with only repulsive electron-electron interaction.

To specify our viewpoint, we first remark that a common feature of high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ oxides is the existence of the Cu-O network with the magnetic moments at the Cu sites except the case of a relatively low-$T_\mathrm{c}$ material.$^{11)}$ The existence of the antiferromagnetic order near the superconducting state seems to be another common phenomenon.$^{12)}$ A recent NMR experiment also supports the importance of magnetic correlation in the superconducting state.$^{13)}$ Theoretically, it is important to establish that the magnetic mechanism may indeed lead to superconductivity.

In this letter, we propose a starting point to extract the essence of high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ superconductivity mainly based on the magnetic mechanism. We also emphasize the experimental relevance and reality of our model in the high-$T_\mathrm{c}$ oxides.

When we refer to several proposals for superconductivity based on the magnetic mechanism, we notice that there are three basic parameters $t$, $J_\mathrm{S}$ and $J_\mathrm{K}$ as is described in the following coupled spin-fermion Hamiltonian:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & -t \sum_{\langle i j\rangle, \sigma}\left(c_{i \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{j \sigma}+c_{j \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i \sigma}\right)+U \sum_{i} n_{i \uparrow} n_{i \downarrow} \\
& -J_{\mathrm{S}} / 2 \sum_{\langle k l\rangle} S_{k} \cdot S_{l}-J_{\mathrm{K}} / 2 \sum_{\langle i k\rangle} \sigma_{i} \cdot S_{k}, \quad (1)
\end{aligned}
$$

where the fermion at the $i$-th site with spin $\sigma$ is described by the creation (annihilation) operator $c_{i \sigma}^{\dagger}$ ($c_{i \sigma}$). The operator $\sigma_{i}$ denotes the

fermion's spin defined by $\sigma_{i}^{z}=c_{i \uparrow}^{\dagger} c_{i \uparrow}-c_{i \downarrow}^{\dagger} c_{i \downarrow}$, $\sigma_{i}^{+}=2 c_{i \uparrow}^{\dagger} c_{i \downarrow}$ and $\sigma_{i}^{-}=2 c_{i \downarrow}^{\dagger} c_{i \uparrow}$. The spin operator $S_{k}$ denotes the Pauli matrix. When $J_{\mathrm{S}}=U=0$, eq. (1) reduces to the Kondo lattice, so that it may be regarded as an extended Kondo lattice system.

When we consider the case of small $U$, this model describes a two-band model, one with strong interaction and the other with weak correlation. Equation (1) is derived when the level of the strongly correlated band is sufficiently lower than the weakly correlated band and the on-site interaction in the strongly correlated band is larger than the level difference. When one increases the density of fermions beyond the "half-filling", the strongly correlated system remains as half-filled, which constitutes the spin-1/2 antiferromagnetic Heisenberg system through the superexchange interaction $J_{\mathrm{S}}$. In the high- $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ oxides, these fermions in eq. (1) correspond to holes.

We next emphasize that eq. (1) also represents relevant models of real oxide superconductors. When we consider the case of conducting holes in oxygen sites, the lattice structure of the fermion system is different from the square lattice of the spin-1/2 Heisenberg system, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The Kondo coupling $J_{\mathrm{K}}$, which represents the exchange coupling of the nearest-neighbor $\mathrm{Cu}-\mathrm{O}$ bond, connects the nearest-neighbor spin and fermion site. Because each fermion site has two Kondo couplings to spin sites, we refer to this case as the $z_{\mathrm{K}}=2$ class.

We first consider the case of the $p_{\sigma}$ orbital. In this case, the relation of eq. (1) to the original $d-p_{\sigma}$ model in ref. 2 is satisfied approximately. The strong coupling expansion of the original extended Hubbard model ( $d-p_{\sigma}$ model) includes many other terms than eq. (1) up to the order of the superexchange interaction. It comes from the fact that the $d-p_{\sigma}$ model does not form a well-separated two-band system. However, we believe that the essence of the $d-p_{\sigma}$ model in the strong coupling region is included in eq. (1), when the hole concentration in the $p_{\sigma}$ orbital is small. $^{6)}$ We, of course, need to examine the correspondence between the $d-p_{\sigma}$ model and eq. (1) in more detail. This remains for further study.

Fig. 1. Lattice structure of $z_{\mathrm{K}}=2$ class. The fermion transfer is shown as broken lines and the solid lines illustrate the antiferromagnetic spin coupling.

When we consider the case of holes in $p_{\pi}$ orbitals, it might have rather large direct transfer in the $p_{\pi}$ band. The $p_{\pi}$ orbitals may either be in the $\mathrm{CuO}$ plane or out of the plane. This constitutes a well-separated band from the antiferromagnetic spin system constructed from the $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}-p_{\sigma}$ network. In the case of the $d-\rho_{\sigma}$ model, $J_{\mathrm{K}}$ is negative (antiferromagnetic), while $J_{\mathrm{K}}$ should be positive (ferromagnetic) in the $d-p_{\pi}$ model due to Hund's rule.

When holes occupy the $d_{3 z^{2}-r^{2}}$ orbital, the fermion system has the same square lattice as the Heisenberg system. Because the Kondo coupling, $J_{\mathrm{K}}$, connects the spin and fermion on the same site, we refer to this lattice as the $z_{\mathrm{K}}=1$ class. For $d_{3 z^{2}-r^{2}}$ orbitals, $J_{\mathrm{K}}$ must be positive (ferromagnetic).

We also note that $z_{\mathrm{K}}=1$ class with $J_{\mathrm{K}}=-\infty$ includes the so-called effective Hamiltonian of the single-band Hubbard model given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & -t^{\prime} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle, \sigma}\left(1-n_{i,-\sigma}\right) c_{i \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{j \sigma}\left(1-n_{j,-\sigma}\right) \\
& + \text { h.c. }-J_{\mathrm{S}} / 2 \sum_{\langle i j\rangle} S_{i} \cdot S_{j},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $t^{\prime}=t / 2$. Equation (2) is justified only in the limit of the strong onsite repulsion in the single-band Hubbard model. It is approximately valid under the constraint that $|J_{\mathrm{S}}|$ must be much smaller than $t$. If we start with the two-band model (1) with $J_{\mathrm{K}}=-\infty$, eq. (2) is derived in a more natural way. Parameter values $J_{\mathrm{S}}$ and $t$ may be taken independently, which seems difficult to be realistic within a one-band picture. Equation (1) is also suited to examine the validity of the one-band picture from more general point of view.

The microscopic derivation of eq. (1) to specify the relevant parameter values remains for further theoretical and experimental

cooperation. In any case, high-temperature superconductivity is located in the region $t \gg |J_{\mathrm{S}}|$ and $|J_{\mathrm{K}}| < |J_{\mathrm{S}}|$. Here, we consider eq. (1) from a more general point of view.

The first case to be considered is the weak coupling region $t \gg |J_{\mathrm{K}}|$ and $|J_{\mathrm{S}}| \gg |J_{\mathrm{K}}|$. Although high-$T_{\mathrm{c}}$ oxides are not located in this region, it provides a useful idea to understand the connection between the weak and the strong coupling region of superconductivity. It is also useful to establish the existence of superconductivity in a purely electronic system only with repulsive on-site interaction.

Although the existence of the long-range order in the Heisenberg system on a square lattice is a controversial issue, $^{14)}$ it has, at least, very long correlation of the antiferromagnetic order. Because $J_{\mathrm{K}}$ is small, we may assume that the fermion state does not cause serious effects on such ground states of the Heisenberg system. Therefore, the spin wave theory provides a good starting point in this weak coupling region.

If we apply the perturbational expansion for $J_{\mathrm{K}}$, the second-order process leads to the effective fermion-fermion interaction
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{eff}}= & -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k, k^{\prime}} \frac{\Gamma_{q}^{2} \omega_{q}}{\left(E_{k}-E_{k-q}\right)^{2}-\omega_{q}^{2}} \\
& \times c_{k^{\prime}+q, \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{k-q,-\sigma}^{\dagger} c_{k^{\prime},-\sigma} c_{k, \sigma},
\end{aligned}\quad (3)
$$
where
$$
\Gamma_{q}= \begin{cases}-J_{\mathrm{K}} / 2 \exp \left(-\theta_{q}\right) & \text { for } z_{\mathrm{K}}=1, \\ -J_{\mathrm{K}} / 2\left(\cos \left(q_{x} a / 2\right)\right. & \\ \left.+\cos \left(q_{y} a / 2\right)\right) \exp \left(-\theta_{q}\right) & \text { for } z_{\mathrm{K}}=2.\end{cases}
$$
Here, $\omega_{q}=4 J_{\mathrm{S}} \sqrt{1-\gamma(q)^{2}}, \quad \gamma(q)=(\cos q_{x} a+$ $\cos q_{y} a) / 2$ and $\tanh 2 \theta_{q}=\gamma(q)$ and $E_{k}$ is the conduction hole energy with wave number $k$.

An important difference from the phonon-mediated BCS-type interaction is that the effective interaction may oscillate from an attractive to a repulsive value in the real space, reflecting the dispersion of the antiferromagnetic spin wave. However, the framework of the formalism is parallel to the BCS theory. We simply need to replace the phonon with magnon excitations. The region $|J_{\mathrm{S}}| \ll t$ is the counterpart of the phonon-mediated BCS region. Because the coherence length is large, the mean-field theory provides an adequate theoretical approach when we assume a finite interlayer coupling. We should note that the order parameter is expressed as
$$
\sum_{k} f(k)\left\langle c_{k \uparrow}^{\dagger} c_{-k \downarrow}^{\dagger}\right\rangle,\quad (4)
$$
where $c_{k \sigma}$ is the Fourier transformation of $c_{i \sigma}$, and $f$ is the form factor. The spin-wave theory of a similar model was considered by Hida $^{6)}$ in case of the $z_{\mathrm{K}}=2$ class. In the weak coupling region, the coexistence of superconductivity and antiferromagnetism is a characteristic feature.

We expect a richer structure in the strong coupling region, which will be reported in detail elsewhere. A possible symmetry is the extended s-wave. In particular, in the region $t \sim |J_{\mathrm{K}}| > |J_{\mathrm{S}}|$, the attractive interaction of the extended singlet cloud around fermions is found.

We next consider the case $t=0$. It is clear that the superconducting state is not realized when $t$ is too small. However, effective interaction observed in the nonitinerant system is helpful to draw a physical picture of the pairing mechanism. Although another singlet-cloud mechanism works $^{15)}$ in the region $|J_{\mathrm{K}}| \sim t > |J_{\mathrm{S}}|$, the pairing mechanism revealed in the case $t=0$ remains qualitatively valid in a certain range of itinerant systems.

By the exact diagonalization technique of eq. (1) up to 24 sites, we calculated the binding energy of localized fermions in eq. (1) when $t=0$ and $U=0$. The binding energy $|E_{\mathrm{B}}|$ in an $L_{x} \times L_{y}$ lattice is calculated from $E_{\mathrm{B}}=2 E_{1}(L_{x}$, $L_{y})-E_{0}(L_{x}, L_{y})-E_{2}(L_{x}, L_{y})$, where $E_{n}(L_{x}, L_{y})$ denotes the energy of the $n$-fermion system in eq. (1) on an $L_{x} \times L_{y}$ lattice. The extrapolation to the thermodynamic limit is taken in systems with the free boundary condition in the $y$-direction and the periodic boundary condition in the $x$-direction.

The double extrapolation is performed by taking the limit $L_{y} \rightarrow \infty$ first and $L_{x} \rightarrow \infty$ next. In the case of $J_{\mathrm{K}}=-\infty$ for $z_{\mathrm{K}}=1$ class, for example, the extrapolated binding energy scaled by $J_{\mathrm{S}}$ is 0.65. When $J_{\mathrm{K}}=\infty$ and $z_{\mathrm{K}}=1$, $|E_{\mathrm{B}}| / J_{\mathrm{S}}=0.14$. In the case of $z_{\mathrm{K}}=1$, the largest binding energy is realized when two holes are at the nearest-neighbor sites.

The origin of the bound state in the case

$J_{\mathrm{K}}=-\infty$ and $z_{\mathrm{K}}=1$ is clearly the attractive interaction of two Kondo singlets induced by the elimination of antiferromagnetic bonds around the Kondo singlets. We will show in a separate paper that the spin-wave theory predicts the formation of the bound state semiquantitatively. $^{15)}$

Although the formation of the Kondo singlet is replaced with slightly different types of defect formation in a different class of $J_{\mathrm{K}}$ and $z_{\mathrm{K}}$, the origin of the attractive interaction always comes from the local disturbance to the diagonal and off-diagonal part of the spin-spin interaction around the hole. The tendency for the existence of the bound state was confirmed in most cases of $-\infty<J_{\mathrm{K}}<\infty$, $J_{\mathrm{S}}=-1$ and $z_{\mathrm{K}}=1,2$ in the thermodynamic limit. A detailed analysis of the attractive interaction for each class of systems will be reported in a separate paper. $^{15)}$

We emphasize that the added holes do not necessarily introduce the frustration in the host Heisenberg system. Even in the case $z_{\mathrm{K}}=2$, a classical concept of the frustration itself is not essential for the pairing mechanism. The dynamical exchange term of either $J_{\mathrm{K}}^{\pm}$or $J_{\mathrm{S}}^{\pm}$is crucial to realize the pairing. Therefore, we expect that the spin-1/2 system is the best choice to induce the pairing from the Heisenberg system.

When the transfer $t$ is too small as compared to the binding energy $E_{\mathrm{B}}$, it may lead to the phase separation with the condensation of fermions in the real space. With the increase of $t$, the transfer energy gain surpasses the energy of real space condensation, which leads to the delocalization of fermions. Because the binding energy $E_{\mathrm{B}}$ is positive in a wide range of $t,^{15)}$ it suggests that the superconducting state appear when holes are delocalized. It is likely that such a superconducting state is continuously connected to the superconductivity in the weak coupling region. We speculate that the order parameter of the superconducting state is expressed by eq. (4) in the whole region.

In the limit $J_{\mathrm{K}} \rightarrow-\infty$, a fermion site corresponds to a mobile hole in the spin substrate due to the formation of the spin-fermion Kondo singlet. In the Cooper pair in eq. (4), formation of the hole-hole bound state is essentially important. It should be contrasted with the RVB picture that the RVB singlet on the spin substrate or a hole itself forms a Cooper pair.

We clearly need further study towards more complete understanding of the superconductivity. In fact, we should note that the formation of the bound state is neither a sufficient nor a necessary condition for the condensation of a Cooper pair.

In summary, we presented a coupled spin-fermion system as a model for the high- $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ superconductivity. The coupled spin-fermion model provides a way to understand the superconductivity of the two-band systems in the whole region from a strong to weak coupling system. The existence of the attractive interaction of fermions in the absence of transfer suggests a clear pairing mechanism over a wide range of the strong coupling region.
