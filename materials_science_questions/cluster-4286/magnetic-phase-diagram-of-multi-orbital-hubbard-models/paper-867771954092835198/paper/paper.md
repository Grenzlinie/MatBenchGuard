# Noncollinear magnetism and spin-orbit coupling in $5d$ pyrochlore oxide $\text{Cd}_2\text{Os}_2\text{O}_7$

Hiroshi Shinaoka$^{1,2}$, Takashi Miyake$^{1,2}$, and Shoji Ishibashi$^{1,2}$

$^1$Nanosystem Research Institute "RICS", National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST), Umezono, Tsukuba 305-8568, Japan
$^2$Japan Science and Technology Agency (JST), CREST, Honcho, Kawaguchi, Saitama 332-0012, Japan
(Dated: July 16, 2021)

We investigated the electronic and magnetic properties of the pyrochlore oxide $\text{Cd}_2\text{Os}_2\text{O}_7$ using the density-functional theory plus on-site repulsion ($U$) method, and depict the ground-state phase diagram with respect to $U$. We conclude that the all-in/all-out non-collinear magnetic order is stable in a wide range of $U$. We also show that the easy-axis anisotropy arising from the spin-orbit (SO) coupling plays a significant role in stabilizing the all-in/all-out magnetic order. A pseudo gap was observed near the transition between the antiferromagnetic metallic and insulating phases. Finally, we discuss possible origins of the peculiar low-temperature$(T)$ properties observed in experiments.

PACS numbers: 75.47.Lx,71.15.Mb,71.70.Ej

Pyrochlore transition-metal oxides $A_2B_2O_7$ are a class of materials that have been extensively investigated for several decades [1]. Recently, particular attention has been paid to $5d$ transition-metal oxides, such as iridates ($A_2\text{Ir}_2\text{O}_7$), in search for unconventional phenomena that are induced by the competing spin-orbit (SO) coupling and electron correlation.

$\text{Cd}_2\text{Os}_2\text{O}_7$ is one of the few compounds whose low-$T$ magnetic structure has been experimentally determined among a number of magnetic $5d$ pyrochlore oxides. The $\text{Cd}^{2+}$ ion is non-magnetic, whereas the $\text{Os}^{5+}$ ion with a $5d^3$ configuration can be magnetic. This compound exhibits a purely electronic continuous metal-insulator transition (MIT) concurrently with a Néel ordering at $T_{\text{MIT}} \simeq 227$ K [2-5]. Magnetic-susceptibility [2, 3] and $\mu$SR measurements [6] suggested the Néel ordering, whereas powder neutron diffraction did not confirm the abovementioned observation [7]. Recently, Yamaura *et al.* have successfully detected a magnetic reflection at the wave vector of $\boldsymbol{q}=0$ using resonant X-ray scattering on high-quality single crystals [5]. They showed that only the so-called all-in/all-out magnetic order [see Fig. 1(a)] is compatible with the cubic symmetry of the crystal among the possible $\boldsymbol{q}=0$ magnetic orders. The all-in/all-out magnetic ordering has been suggested also in other $5d$ pyrochlore oxides, i.e, $\text{Nd}_2\text{Ir}_2\text{O}_7$ in experimental work [8] and $\text{Y}_2\text{Ir}_2\text{O}_7$ in theoretical work [9].

Despite the recent experimental progress, we are still far from fully understanding the electronic properties of this compound. In particular, several puzzling electronic properties have been reported experimentally: (1) In contrast to the opening of an optical gap of the order of $800$ cm$^{-1}$ ($\simeq 1100$ K) [10], the semiconducting gap continuously vanishes toward low $T$'s [2, 3]. This indicates the absence of a clear charge gap at low $T$'s which seemingly contradicts the semiconducting behavior of the resistivity up to $T_{\text{MIT}}$. (2) The Néel transition temperature of this compound ($=T_{\text{MIT}}$) is, to the beset of our knowledge, one of the highest among the magnetic pyrochlore oxides [1]. This observation is quite surprising because geometrical frustration tends to prevent long-range magnetic ordering. These puzzling characteristics and the similarity with the Ir oxides urged us to investigate this compound as a prototype of $5d$ pyrochlore oxides.

In this Letter, we investigate the ground-state properties of $\text{Cd}_2\text{Os}_2\text{O}_7$ using extensive LSDA+SO+$U$ calculations (LSDA denotes the local spin density approximation). We explore the ground-state phase diagram as a function of $U_{\text{eff}}$ ($\equiv U-J$). Here, $U_{\text{eff}}$ is an empirical parameter, and $U_{\text{eff}}=1$-$2$ eV is expected to be appropriate for spatially extended $5d$ orbitals [9]. We show that the all-in/all-out magnetic order is stable in an antiferromagnetic metal (AFM) and in an antiferromagnetic insulator (AFI). We observe that strong magnetic anisotropy arising from the SO coupling stabilizes the all-in/all-out magnetic order. Finally, we discuss possible origins of the experimentally-observed puzzling characteristics of

$Cd_2Os_2O_7$.

In the following calculations, we use a fully-relativistic two-component first-principles computational code, QMAS (Quantum MAterials Simulator) [11]. We employ the projector augmented-wave method [12] and the LSDA+SO+$U$ method [13-17]. The relativistic effect including the SO coupling is fully considered in solving the relativistic Kohn-Sham equation [16, 17]. In the following calculations, we adopt a face-centered cubic primitive unit cell for $Cd_2Os_2O_7$ containing two formula units, and restrict consideration to $\boldsymbol{q}=0$ magnetic ordering. Brillouin-zone integrations were performed using up to $12\times12\times12$ $k$-point samplings using the improved tetrahedron method [18]. We used a planewave cutoff energy of 40 Ry. The following calculations were done with the experimental lattice structure at 180 K: $a=10.1598$ Å and $x(\text{O}_1)=0.319$ [3]. Every Os atom is located at the center of an $\text{OsO}_6$ octahedron. For $x(\text{O}_1)>0.3125$, each oxygen octahedron is slightly compressed along the local $\langle111\rangle$ axis that connects the centers of the two neighboring Os tetrahedra.

To identify the antiferromagnetic ordering, we calculate the local magnetic moment projected on an Os atom, $m_{\text{Os}}$, by integrating the magnetic moment within a radius of 2.5 a.u. $(=1.323$ Å). The direct gap, $\Delta_{\text{D}}$, is defined as the minimum gap between the conduction and valence bands, which approximately corresponds to the optical gap. In the following discussion, $\Delta_{\text{C}}$ denotes the charge gap, which identifies the MIT.

Figure 1(b) shows the computed ground-state phase diagram with respect to $U_{\text{eff}}$. At small $U_{\text{eff}}$'s, the ground state is non-magnetic metal (NMM). By increasing $U_{\text{eff}}$, the ground state turns into the AFM phase at $U_{\text{eff}}\simeq0.75$ eV, and further into the AFI phase at $U_{\text{eff}}\simeq1.2$ eV. We found that the all-in/all-out magnetic order is the most stable in the entire parameter region of the AFM and AFI phases: Os moments with the same magnitude point toward or away from the centers of the tetrahedra along the local $\langle111\rangle$ axes as illustrated in Fig. 1(a). We obtained $m_{\text{Os}}\simeq0.8$-$1.1$ $\mu_{\text{B}}$/Os in the AFI phase, which is considerably smaller than 3 $\mu_{\text{B}}$/Os for the high-spin state.

Figures 2(a)-(b) show the calculated electronic band structures near the Fermi level. At $U_{\text{eff}}=0.0$ eV [Fig. 2(a)], the Fermi level lies in the half-filled $t_{2\text{g}}$ bands consisting of twelve Kramers(doubly)-degenerate bands. Note that the effect of the distortion of the octahedral crystal field is not strong enough to split the $t_{2\text{g}}$ manifold. As suggested by the previous LSDA studies [19, 20], the band structure looks semi-metallic on the high-symmetry lines. In fact, we obtained $\Delta_{\text{D}}\simeq0.035$ eV at $U_{\text{eff}}=0.0$ eV. Although $\Delta_{\text{D}}$ decreases with increasing $U_{\text{eff}}$, $\Delta_{\text{D}}$ remains non zero within the NMM phase as shown in Fig. 1(c). Figures 3(a)-(f) show the calculated electronic density of states. At $U_{\text{eff}}=0.0$ eV [Fig. 3(a)], one clearly see a sharp peak near the Fermi level, which may originate in low-lying flat bands. The low-energy structure of the density of states remains essentially unchanged within the NMM phase [see Fig. 3(b)].

Next, we discuss the magnetic properties of the AFI phase. It is well known that pyrochlore antiferromagnets tend to be frustrated when the spins are isotropic. This is clearly seen in the nearest-neighbor classical antiferromagnet which exhibits no phase transition and remains paramagnetic down to zero $T$ with a macroscopic degeneracy [22]. The ground-state manifold consists of degenerate states in each of which the summation of four spin moments vanishes on every tetrahedron. The degeneracy can be lifted by magnetic anisotropy which arises from the SO coupling. In particular, the all-in/all-out order is selected as the unique ground state by the local $\langle 111 \rangle$ easy-axis anisotropy [23-25].

Figure 4(a) shows magnetic anisotropy energies calculated for $U_{\text{eff}} = 1.25$ and 2.0 eV, respectively. Here $E_{\text{g}}(\theta)$ is the energy of the self-consistent solution that is obtained under the constraint that every Os moment is rotated from that of the ground state around the [001] axis, and its magnitude is equal to $m_{\text{Os}}$ in the ground state. One can clearly see that $E_{\text{g}}$ remarkably increases with the rotation by as large as about 40 meV/Os and 70 meV/Os for $U_{\text{eff}} = 1.25$ and 2.0 eV, respectively. This proves the existence of strong magnetic anisotropy in this compound even near the MIT.

To gain deeper insight into the nature of the magnetic anisotropy, we extend the analysis using a phenomenological model for the energy change associated with the rotations of the Os moments:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{H}= & J \sum_{\langle i, j\rangle, i<j} \vec{m}_{i} \cdot \vec{m}_{j}-A_{\text{sia}} \sum_{i}\left(\vec{m}_{i} \cdot \vec{\alpha}_{i}\right)^{2} \\
& +A_{\text{DM}} \sum_{\langle i, j\rangle, i<j} \vec{d}_{i j} \cdot\left(\vec{m}_{i} \times \vec{m}_{j}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\vec{m}_{i}$ is a unit direction vector of the magnetic moment at Os site $i$. Here $J$, $A_{\text{sia}}$, and $A_{\text{DM}}$ are the nearest-neighbor exchange interaction, the single-ion anisotropy, and the DM interaction, respectively. Note that $m_{\text{Os}}$ weakly depends on $U_{\text{eff}}$ in the AFI phase, and this effect is renormalized into the interaction parameters. The unit vector $\vec{\alpha}_{i}$ is along the local $\langle 111 \rangle$ axis at site $i$. The case where $A_{\text{sia}}>0$ corresponds to the easy-axis anisotropy. The unit vectors $\vec{d}_{i j}$ are the direction vectors of the DM interaction, which are the only one symmetry-allowed form [26][34]. In Ref. 26, the cases where $A_{\text{DM}}>0$ and $A_{\text{DM}}<0$ are distinctly referred to as the "direct case" and "indirect case"; the former favors the all-in/all-out ordering.

The model parameters can be extracted by fitting energies obtained in electronic structure calculations by Eq. (1). Hereafter, the energy is always measured per Os atom. In the following calculations, the magnitudes of the Os moments are fixed to $m_{\text{Os}}$ of the ground state. On the basis of Eq. (1), the energy difference between the all-in/all-out and 3-in/1-out states is given by

$$
E_{\mathrm{e}}(0)-E_{\mathrm{g}}(0)=J+2 \sqrt{2} A_{\mathrm{DM}}.
$$

Here, the 3-in/1-out state is the lowest excited state for $J>0$ and $A_{\text{sia}}>0$, which is obtained by flipping one of four non-equivalent spins in the all-in/all-out state [see Figs. 4(b) and (c)]. Changes in the energies of the all-in/all-out $[E_{\text{g}}(\theta)]$ and 3-in/1-out ordered states $[E_{\text{e}}(\theta)]$ are obtained respectively as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{g}}(\theta)-E_{\mathrm{g}}(0)= & E_{\mathrm{e}}(\theta)-E_{\mathrm{e}}(0) \\
& +\frac{4 \sqrt{2}}{3} A_{\mathrm{DM}}(1-\cos \theta),
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{e}}(\theta)-E_{\mathrm{e}}(0)=A_{\text{sia}}\left\{1-\frac{1}{9}(2 \cos \theta+1)^{2}\right\}, \quad (4)
$$

where $\theta$ denotes the rotation angle around the [001] axis.

Table I summarizes the model parameters extracted for $U_{\text{eff}} = 1.25$ and 2.0 eV. The values of $A_{\text{sia}}$ and $A_{\text{DM}}$ were obtained by fitting the magnetic anisotropy energies at small $\theta$'s, i.e., in the range of $0^\circ \leq \theta \leq 60^\circ$ [35]. We found that the values of $J$ and $A_{\text{sia}}$ are positive for both the abovementioned values of $U_{\text{eff}}$'s. The antiferromagnetic $J$ might originate in the superexchange coupling via the O site. The easy-axis anisotropy $A_{\text{sia}}$ is found to be even larger than $J$ for both $U_{\text{eff}}$'s. On the other hand, $A_{\text{DM}}$ is considerably smaller than $A_{\text{sia}}$, and vanishes as $U_{\text{eff}}$ increases. This might be because the DM interaction appears as a perturbation with respect to hopping between the nearest-neighboring Os atoms. These results suggest that the all-in/all-out order is mainly stabilized by the large easy-axis anisotropy rather than the DM interaction. In the case of $Y_2\text{Ir}_2\text{O}_7$ [9], the all-in/all-out ordering is ascribed to the direct DM interaction because $\text{Ir}^{4+}$ has an effective total angular momentum of $J_{\text{eff}} = 1/2$ [9, 27, 28][36]. The observation of the large $A_{\text{sia}}$ indicates that $\text{Os}^{5+}$ has a larger value of $J_{\text{eff}}$ ($J_{\text{eff}} > 1/2$).

Assuming that the real material is located near the MIT, e.g, $U_{\text{eff}} \simeq 1.25$ eV, the calculated results provide a natural explanation for the peculiar low-$T$ properties of this compound. (1) The large direct gap, $\Delta_{\text{D}}$, and small charge gap, $\Delta_{\text{C}}$, near the MIT are consistent with the experimental observation of a large optical gap and the absence of a clear charge gap at low $T$'s. In fact, $\Delta_{\text{D}} \simeq 0.17$ eV estimated at $U_{\text{eff}} = 1.25$ eV is comparable to the optical gap of about 0.1 eV observed below $T_{\text{MIT}}$ [10]. The *pseudo gap* is expected to develop below $T_{\text{MIT}}$ concurrently with the onset of the Néel ordering. The density of states vanishes toward low $T$'s in the wide energy range. This observation explains the semiconducting behavior of resistivity up to the high $T_{\text{MIT}}$. (2) The strong easy-axis anisotropy on the order of several tens meV stabilizes the all-in/all-out order cooperatively with the antiferromagnetic exchange interaction. This can account for the high antiferromagnetic transition temperature of this compound. The present result indicates that this compound has a magnetic gap of several tens meV. Further experiments are needed to detect the magnetic gap and confirm the present observation.

Before closing this Letter, we would like to comment on future work. Contrary to the recent theoretical proposals [29, 30], all the three phases in the phase diagram have a trivial $Z_2$ topological invariant. The band inversion in the AFM phase might suggest the presence of Dirac cones. A detailed analysis of the nature of the band inversion is a part of our future study. In contrast to $\text{Cd}_2\text{Os}_2\text{O}_7$, $\text{Hg}_2\text{Os}_2\text{O}_7$ remains metallic below the AF transition temperature [31]. This material might be located in the AFM phase. The absence of a clear charge gap below the continuous MIT associated with the Néel ordering is also observed in $\text{NaOsO}_3$ perovskite [32]. Clearly, first-principle studies on this related compound will be interesting.

In summary, we performed LSDA+SO+$U$ calculations to explore the ground-state properties of $\text{Cd}_2\text{Os}_2\text{O}_7$. We also found the all-in/all-out magnetic order as the ground state in a wide region of the phase diagram, supporting recent X-ray experimental results. We also showed that this magnetic order is stabilized by strong easy-axis anisotropy originating in spin-orbit coupling. Furthermore, we found a *pseudo gap* in the density of states near the metal-insulator transition. These numerical results provide a natural explanation for the puzzling low-temperature properties of this compound. The present result might open up new possibilities to explore Os compounds as a stimulating playground for the interplay of electron correlation and spin-orbit coupling.
