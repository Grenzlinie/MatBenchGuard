PHYSICAL REVIEW B 73, 052501 (2006)

# Long-range Coulomb effect on the antiferromagnetic ordering
in electron-doped cuprate superconductors

Xin-Zhong Yan
Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, P.O. Box 603, Beijing 100080, China
(Received 11 October 2005; published 8 February 2006)

Using mean-field theory, we illustrate the long-range Coulomb effect on the antiferromagnetic ordering in the electron-doped cuprate superconductors. Because of the Coulomb exchange effect, the magnitude of the total next-nearest-neighbor hopping increases appreciably with increasing the electron doping concentration, raising the frustration to the antiferromagnetic ordering. This provides a possible explanation to the Fermi surface evolution in the electron-doped cuprate $Nd_{2-x}Ce_{x}CuO_{4}$.

DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevB.73.052501
PACS number(s): 74.25.Jb, 71.10.Fd, 74.25.Ha, 74.72.-h

The Fermi surface (FS) evolution with electron doping in $Nd_{2-x}Ce_{x}CuO_{4}$ has been observed by angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) experiments. $^{1,2}$ Recently, much attention has been paid to understanding the physics of this phenomenon. $^{3-9}$ At low temperatures, the electron-doped cuprates are in the states of an antiferromagnetic (AF) phase within a wide doping region. $^{10,11}$ The FS evolution is therefore closely relevant to the antiferromagnetic correlation. From the framework of the Hubbard model, the doped electrons occupy the upper Hubbard band. According to the experimental observation, with increasing electron doping, the energy gap should decrease and eventually close up at the optimal doping, $x \approx 0.14$, where the AF phase terminates. Therefore, to interpret the experimental results, one needs to assume the on-site interaction $U$ in the Hubbard model to be dramatically decreasing with increasing electron doping concentration. This is a puzzle within the Hubbard model with constant on-site interaction. Some investigators have treated the doping dependence of $U$ by considering some kind of screening. $^{3,4,6}$

In this work, we explore the long-range Coulomb effect (LRCE) on the AF ordering. Due to the Coulomb exchange effect, the LRCE results in excess electron hopping. In especial, the magnitude of the total next-nearest-neighbor hopping increases appreciably with increasing the doping concentration. This effect leads to the decreasing of the energy gap and eventually frustrates the AF ordering.

We start with the following two-dimensional square lattice model:

$$
H=-\sum_{i j, \sigma} t_{i j} c_{i \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{j \sigma}+U \sum_{i} n_{i \uparrow} n_{i \downarrow}+\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \neq j} v_{i j} n_{i} n_{j}, \quad(1)
$$

where $t_{i j}$ denotes the hopping energy of an electron between the lattice sites $i$ and $j$, $c_{i \sigma}^{\dagger}$ ($c_{i \sigma}$) represents the electron creation (annihilation) operator of spin-$\sigma$ ($=\pm 1$ for both up and down spins, respectively) at site $i$, $n_{i \sigma}=c_{i \sigma}^{\dagger} c_{i \sigma}$, and $n_{i}=n_{i \uparrow}+n_{i \downarrow}$ is the electron density operator. Besides the on-site Hubbard repulsion $U$, we take into account the long-range Coulomb interaction in the third term. Such a similar type of Hamiltonian has been used to investigate the LRCE on the $d$-wave pairing for the hole-doped case. $^{12}$

In the hopping term, besides the nearest-neighbor (NN) hopping, the other hopping processes within a range needs to be included. The essential role of the next NN hopping for the validity of the single-band Hubbard model for describing the cuprates has been investigated by comparing it with the two-band $^{13}$ and three-band Hubbard models. $^{4}$ The particle-hole asymmetry in the cuprates can be understood by taking into account the next NN hopping. $^{14,15}$ By including the next and third NN hopping parameters in the types of $t-J$ and the Hubbard models, numerous studies have been carried out to explore the properties of the electron-doped cuprates. $^{3-9,16-19}$ Here we take two additional parameters for the fourth and fifth hopping, each of which is smaller than the former. In the unit of the NN hopping parameter, $t_{(1,0)} \equiv t=1$, the values of the four other parameters are given as $t_{(1,1)}=-0.325$, $t_{(2,0)}=0.17$, $t_{(2,1)}=-0.121$, and $t_{(2,2)}=-0.07$, where the subscripts instead of $ij$ denote the components of a vector of length $|\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}|$. The values of the next and third NN hopping parameters are approximately the same as that in the literatures. $^{3-9,16-19}$

For the long-range Coulomb interaction $v_{i j}$, we take the following form:

$$
v_{i j}=V \exp (-|\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}| / d) /|\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}|, \quad(2)
$$

where the length scale is in the unit of the lattice constant. The screening effect from the insulating background is absorbed in parameter $V$. Since we will use the mean-field (MF) theory, $v_{i j}$ should be an effective interaction in which the screening effect from the Hubbard electrons should be included in advance. Therefore, it is essentially finite ranged. The "long-range" is termed opposite to the on-site $U$. Here we set $V=1.0$ (in a unit of $t=1$) and $d=4$ for the present calculation. For the on-site $U$, we use $U=4.8$, which is within the range of the doping-dependent interaction adopted in the existing calculations. $^{3,4,6}$ The strength of $V$ with $V/U \approx 0.21$ is a typical one. $^{12}$

By the MF approximation, from the long-range Coulomb term, we get the exchange part of the self-energy as

$$

$$

where $N$ is the number of lattice sites, $v(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|)$ is the Fourier transform of $v_{i j}$, and $\langle\cdots\rangle$ denotes an average. This ex-

At low temperatures, the strong on-site Coulomb interaction leads to the AF ordering. In the case of the electron doping under consideration, the AF ordering is a commensurate spin-density wave. The order parameter is given by $\Delta=U\langle n_\downarrow(Q)-n_\uparrow(Q)\rangle/2N$, where $n_\sigma(Q)$ is the Fourier transform of the electron density of spin $\sigma$ at wave vector $Q=(\pi,\pi)$. In the present work, the quasiparticle states are analyzed by the MF theory.

Figure 1 shows the Fermi-level electron distributions in momentum space obtained by integrating the occupied spectral density in an energy window $0.15t$ at temperature $T=0.1t$ at various electron doping concentration $x$'s. At low doping, the FS (in the first quadrant of the Brillouin zone) appears as two small pockets centered at $(\pi,0)$ and $(0,\pi)$. With increasing doping, these pockets extend to larger squares. Such an evolution of the pockets reflects the occupation of the doped electrons at the upper Hubbard band. On the other hand, a small pocket begins to form around $(\pi/2,\pi/2)$ at $x\sim0.1$ and grows up with increasing doping. The formation of this pocket stems from the contribution of the lower Hubbard band; with increasing doping, the energy gap decreases and meanwhile the lower Hubbard band shifts up toward the FS. At high doping, the energy gap closes up, therefore the FS is a single curve centered at $(\pi,\pi)$ ($x=0.18$). Clearly, the present calculation reproduces the results of the existing calculations based on the doping-dependent $U$ Hubbard model$^{3,6}$ and is in agreement with the experimental observations. $^1$ By MF on the $t$-$t'$-$t''$-$U$ model, a constant $U$ actually results in a nearly constant energy gap in a wide region of the doping concentration, and hence cannot explain the FS evolution. Though $U$ is constant in the present calculation, but because of the LRCE, the energy gap decreases remarkably with increasing doping.

In Fig. 2, the MF Néel temperature $T_N$ (depicted by a solid line with circles) is shown as a function of the doping concentration $x$ and is compared with the experimental data. For illustrating the dependence on the Coulomb coupling strengths $U$ and $V$, we depict the results at various $V$ in the upper panel (a) and at various $U$ in the lower panel (b). The result sensitively depends on these parameters, especially at high doping. The best fit to the experimental data$^{11}$ is by the parameters $U=4.8$ and $V=1$ with the given hopping parameters fixed. The $V=0$ curve corresponds to the extended Hubbard model (EHM). The dependence of the EHM $T_N$ on $x$ is very weak in the region $0<x<0.2$. The difference between the solid line and the EHM is remarkable. In contrast to the EHM, $T_N$ given by the solid line decreases distinctly with increasing doping. In particular, it drops sharply at $x\approx0.141$. This doping concentration corresponds to the quantum critical point at which the zero-temperature AF transition terminates and is consistent with the experimental observation.$^{10,11}$ Also, the envelope of $T_N$ (depicted as a solid line) is in fairly good agreement with the experimental results for the pseudogap $\Delta_{\text{pg}}$ and its onset temperature $T^*$.$^{11}$ The MF Néel transition is associated with the appearance of the local magnetization, and hence the comparison between the MF $T_N$ and $T^*$ or $\Delta_{\text{pg}}$ is meaningful. $^4$ The MF order parameter is a measure of the local ordering in the pseudogap state, where the long-wavelength spin fluctuation is significant, similar to the case of superconducting ordering.$^{20,21}$

Why does the LRCE lead to $T_{N}$ decreasing with increasing doping? To answer this question, we consider the excess hopping parameter $t_{ij}^{x}$ due to the Coulomb exchange. This quantity can be written as
$$
t_{ij}^{x} \equiv t_{l}^{x}=\frac{v_{ij}}{2N}\sum_{k}\left\langle c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{k\uparrow}\right\rangle S_{l}(k),\qquad(4)
$$
where $S_{l}(k){=}\cos(l_{x}k_{x})\cos(l_{y}k_{y}){+}\cos(l_{y}k_{x})\cos(l_{x}k_{y})$ and again $l{=}(l_{x},l_{y})$ represents a vector of length $|\mathbf{i}{-}\mathbf{j}|$. The doping dependence of $t_{ij}^{x}$ originates from the Fermi distribution $\langle c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{k\uparrow}\rangle$. At low temperature, the contribution to the integral in Eq. (4) comes from the Fermi area. The Fermi surface varies with changing doping concentration. Especially, with increasing doping, the Fermi area increases considerably in the regions near the points $(\pi,0)$ and $(0,\pi)$ at which the upper Hubbard band reaches its minimum and where the energy dispersion is nearly flat. Therefore, the variation of $t_{l}^{x}$ stems predominantly from the integral in these regions. On the other hand, with the change of the Fermi surface, the factor $S_{l}(k)$ varies slowly for small $l$, but rapidly for large $l$. It is then easy to see the variation of $t_{l}^{x}$ with increasing doping at low temperature; for example, $t_{(1,0)}^{x}$ is almost unchanged for $S_{(1,0)}(\pi,0){=}0$; $t_{(1,1)}^{x}$ has a large variation because of $S_{(1,1)}(\pi,0){=}{-}2$. Since the original hopping parameter $t_{(1,1)}$ is negative, the magnitude of the total hopping parameter $t_{(1,1)}{+}t_{(1,1)}^{x}$ is enhanced with increasing doping. For large $l$, because of the cancellation from the destructive factor $S_{l}(k)$, not only the variation, but also the magnitude of $t_{l}^{x}$ are negligible small. At high temperature, since the integral in Eq. (4) is taken over a spreading area wider than the Fermi area, the behavior of $t_{l}^{x}$ is not so intuitive. Figure 3 exhibits the variations of the magnitudes of the effective hopping parameters $t_{l}{+}t_{l}^{x}$ as functions of the doping concentration $x$ at temperature $T{=}0.53t$. The quantity $\delta t_{l}(x)$ is defined as
$$
\delta t_{l}(x)=\left[t_{l}^{x}(x)-t_{l}^{x}(0)\right] \operatorname{sgn}\left[t_{l}+t_{l}^{x}(0)\right].\qquad(5)
$$

From Fig. 3, it is seen that except for $\delta t_{(1,0)}$ which is nearly constant, the other parameters vary approximately linearly with $x$. Especially, among those hopping parameters only the magnitude of $t_{(1,1)}{+}t_{(1,1)}^{x}{\equiv}t'$ increases appreciably with increasing $x$. This is consistent with the above analysis. A large hopping $t'$ can destroy the AF instability in the square lattice. $^{22}$ It is therefore clear that the AF order parameter and the transition temperature decrease with increasing doping concentration. Since the magnitude of $t'$ is determined by the strength of $V$, the sensitive dependence of $T_{N}$ on $V$ or $V/U$ can also be understood.

Shown in Fig. 4 is the parameter $t_{l}^{x}$ as a function of distance at the MF $T_{N}{=}0.39$ at $x{=}0.1$. It is seen that only the magnitudes of the first four parameters are appreciable; from the fifth to the one at distance four, all of them are very small; after that all of the other parameters are negligibly small. In a wide region on the temperature-doping concentration phase diagram, the parameter $t_{l}^{x}$ behaves almost the same as shown in Fig. 4, with only a visual change in the next NN hopping parameter. From the behavior of the parameter $t_{l}^{x}$, the range of the predominant Coulomb exchange effect seems to be shorter than 3. Of course, the result depends on the screening length $d$.

Finally, we show in Fig. 5 the staggered magnetization $m{=}2\Delta/U$ as a function of the doping concentration $x$ at $T{=}0.1$. The result of $U{=}5.6$ is obtained with another set of parameters $\{t_{l}\}{=}(1,{-}0.36,0.21,{-}0.158,{-}0.06)$, and $(U,V,d){=}(5.6,1.8,5)$, which reproduces a similar spectral density and phase diagram. In summary, we have investigated the long-range Cou- lomb effect on the antiferromagnetism in the electron-doped cuprate superconductors using the mean-field theory. Due to the Coulomb exchange, the magnitude of the total next- nearest-neighbor hopping parameter in especial increases ap- preciably with increasing the electron doping concentration. This leads to stronger frustration to the AF ordering at higher doping concentration. Therefore the transition temperature decreases with increasing doping. Consequently, AF phase terminates at a doping concentration $x$$\approx$0.14 inconsistent with the experiments. The present calculation gives a pos- sible explanation to the FS evolution in the electron-doped cuprate $\mathrm{Nd_{2-x}Ce_xCuO_4}$.
