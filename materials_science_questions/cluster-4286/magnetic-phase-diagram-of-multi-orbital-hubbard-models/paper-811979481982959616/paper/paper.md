# Two Band Mean Field Ferromagnetism

By
J.D. PATTERSON and D.E. KLEPPIN

Schneider et al. (1) have considered the effect of ferromagnetism in orbitally degenerate narrow bands by using Green's function techniques with truncation and with a square density of states. Starting from a similar Hamiltonian, we have made a mean field approximation and obtained magnetic solutions at absolute zero for a free electron like density of states. Our basic motivation was to calculate the effect of Hund's rule coupling terms within the context of a mean field theory. We found, as expected, that the addition of a Hund's rule term can increase the tendency to ferromagnetism.

The model Hamiltonian we use is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & \sum_{\substack{\alpha, \alpha^{1}, \sigma, a \\
\left(\alpha \neq \alpha^{1}\right)}} T_{\alpha, \alpha^{1}}^{a} C_{\alpha, a, \sigma}^{\dagger} C_{\alpha^{1}, a, \sigma}+\frac{U}{2} \sum_{\substack{\alpha, \sigma, \sigma^{1} a, a^{1} \\
\left(\sigma \neq \sigma^{1} \text { when } a=a^{1}\right)}} n_{\alpha, a, \sigma} n_{\alpha, a^{1}, \sigma^{1}}- \\
& -J \sum_{\substack{\alpha, a, a^{1} \\
\left(a \neq a^{1}\right)}}\left[\left(n_{\alpha, a+}-n_{\alpha, a-}\right)\left(n_{\alpha, a^{1}+}-n_{\alpha, a^{1}-}\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term describes the hopping between the sites (labeled by $\alpha$) and the second term describes the Coulomb repulsion between electrons on the same site. Different localized orbital states are labeled by a which is chosen to run from 1 to 2. We thus have a total of 4 states per site when spin $\sigma(= \pm)$ is included. The $C_{\alpha, a, \sigma}^{+}, C_{\alpha^{1}, a, \sigma}$ are electron creation and annihilation operators, respectively, and $n_{\alpha, \sigma}=$ $=C_{\alpha, a, \sigma}^{+} C_{\alpha, a, \sigma}$ is the occupation number operator for the site $\alpha$ with spin $\sigma$.
The third term makes it energetically favorable (when $J>0$) for the magnetic moments of the electrons on the same site to line up with parallel spins. This term can be regarded as a phenomenological Hund's rule term. Equation (1) can also be considered a special case of the Hamiltonian considered by Chao and Gutzwiller (2).

We make the Hartree-Fock approximation on this Hamiltonian (3) and further assume that the quantum statistical average of the operator $n_{\alpha, a, \sigma}$ (i. e. $<n_{\alpha, a, \sigma}>$)

equals $\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{a}, \sigma}$ a scalar number. Recasting the Hamiltonian in the Bloch representation, the electrons can be thought of as moving independently in bands labeled by a and in sub-bands labeled by $\sigma$. The energy of each electron is its band energy plus a coupling energy determined by the other bands and sub-bands. If the band energy is assumed to have a density of states per site $\frac{3}{2} \mathrm{~A}_{\mathrm{a}} \sqrt{\mathrm{E}}$, then Fermi-Dirac statistics at absolute zero implies

$$
\mathrm{n}_{1, \sigma}=\mathrm{A}_{1}\left[v+\mathrm{Un}_{1, \sigma}+2 \mathrm{~J}\left(\mathrm{n}_{2, \sigma}-\mathrm{n}_{2,-\sigma}\right)\right]^{3 / 2},
$$

$$
\mathrm{n}_{2, \sigma}=\mathrm{A}_{2}\left[v+\mathrm{Un}_{2, \sigma}+2 \mathrm{~J}\left(\mathrm{n}_{1, \sigma}-\mathrm{n}_{1,-\sigma}\right)\right]^{3 / 2},
$$

where

$$
\sum_{a, \sigma} n_{a, \sigma}=n \leqq 4
$$

with n the average number of electrons per site. The chemical potential at absolute zero $\mu_{0}$ is determined by equation (4) and $v$ is defined by $v=\mu_{0}-n U$. The energy per site u at absolute zero can also be readily evaluated as

$$
u=\frac{U n^{2}}{2}+\frac{3}{5} v n+\frac{U}{10}\left(n_{1+}^{2}+n_{1-}^{2}+n_{2+}^{2}+n_{2-}^{2}\right)+\frac{2 J}{5}\left(n_{1+}-n_{1-}\right)\left(n_{2+}-n_{2-}\right).(5)
$$

Finally the magnetization per site can be written as

$$
\mathrm{M}=\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\left(\mathrm{n}_{1+}-\mathrm{n}_{1-}+\mathrm{n}_{2+}-\mathrm{n}_{2-}\right),
$$

where $\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the Bohr magneton.

By numerically integrating equations (2), (3), (4) and using equation (6) we obtained Fig. 1 and 2. Using equation (5), the corresponding energies of magnetized and unmagnetized solutions are shown in Fig. 3 and 4. Note that the magnetized solutions have lower energies. For all figures the average number of electrons per site $\mathrm{n}=2, \mathrm{~A}_{1}=1.00, \mathrm{~A}_{2}=1.01, \mathrm{U}$ and $\mathrm{J}$ have the same arbitrary energy units, and $\mathrm{A}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{A}_{2}$ have the same (as $\mathrm{U}$ ) arbitrary energy units to the (-3/2) power. Both Fig. 1 and 2 show that as we increase either U or J, in an appropriate range, the magnetization at absolute zero increases. For fixed $\mathrm{J}$ and varying $\mathrm{U}$, Fig. 1 indicates that there appears to be a critical $U$ below which the system does not magnetize.
