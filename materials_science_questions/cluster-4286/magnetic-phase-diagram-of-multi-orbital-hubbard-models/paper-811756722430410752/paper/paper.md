## ABSTRACT

The properties of the charge imbalance and magnetism are investigated in the multilayered cuprates. The effective Hubbard constants are much reduced and show clear electron-hole asymmetry due to the screening effect. Phase diagrams are obtained, which may give the clue to search for the self-doped cuprates. An unexpected increase of the charge density upon doping in the layer with minority charge is predicted. The antiferromagnetism follows a two-step evolution with respective origin in the layer with minority charge, in contrast to the conventional doping dependence in the layer with major charge.

### 1. Introduction

The multilayered cuprates $(N>2)$ [1-8] have received much attention recently due to some exotic properties, in particular, the potential self-doped phenomenon in the four-layered $\mathrm{Ba_2Ca_3Cu_4O_8F_2}$ (F0234) [2-4]. The structure of the multilayered cuprates includes two inequivalent types of $\mathrm{CuO_2}$ planes: two outer planes (OP) with apical atoms and $N-2$ inner planes (IP) without apical atoms, comparing with the two equivalent planes in the bi-layered $\mathrm{Bi_2Sr_2Ca_{(1-x)}Y_xCu_2O_8}$ (Bi2212) [9]. Several different properties had been revealed based on the different apical atom F [2,3,6] or Hg (Tl, Cu) [10,11]. The nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) studies revealed that the doping density is different in the OP and IP [2,3,5-7], which means the charge imbalance occurs. The Knight shift measurements showed that the IP, and OP is underdoped, and heavily overdoped in the four-layered $(\mathrm{Cu_{1-x}C_x)Ba_2Ca_3Cu_4O_{12+y}}$, respectively [11]. While they are both underdoped in the $\mathrm{HgBa_2Ca_2Cu_3O_y}$ and $\mathrm{HgBa_2Ca_2Cu_4O_y}$ [7]. Similar behaviors had also been discovered in F0234 and $\mathrm{Ba_2Ca_2Cu_3O_6F_2}$ (F0223) [6]. The charge imbalance was found to increase upon the number of layers and increase almost linearly with the doping density [8]. On the other hand, the magnetic behaviors in the multilayered cuprates are more complicated. The inter-layered antiferromagnetism was found in the five-layered $\mathrm{HgBa_2Ca_4Cu_5O_y}$ (Hg1245) [5,7]. The IP is always antiferromagnetic (AF), while the OP changes from AF to paramagnetic upon the doping density. Even in three IPs, the antiferromagnetism shows clear inhomogeneous, compared with the almost homogeneous charge density [7].

A self-doped phenomenon was first reported by Chen et al. in F0234 [2]. Since the formal Cu valence should be +2 due to the full occupation of $\mathrm{F^{-1}}$ on the apical sites, F0234 is then expected to be a Mott insulator. However,clear superconductivity (SC) had been discovered. The self-doping, where the charges were transferred from the IP to the OP, was consequently proposed. Both the electron- and hole-like Fermi surface (FS) sheets were simultaneously found in this material. The splitting between the two FS pieces was significant along the nodal direction, in contrast with the two hole-like FS sheets coinciding together in Bi2212 [9]. This new phenomenon was further confirmed by the LDA + U calculation [3] and NMR measurements [4]. In addition, the inter-layered ferromagnetism between the OP and IP had also been reported. The magnitude of the antiferromagnetism in OP is almost two times as that in IP [4]. Unfortunately, the most recent NMR studies by Shimizu and collaborators [6] revealed that both the OP and IP in F0223 and F0234 are more likely hole doped due to the possible substitution of $\mathrm{O^{-2}}$ for $\mathrm{F^{-1}}$ at the apical sites. The inter-layered antiferromagnetism was obtained, reversed results compared with previous NMR measurement. It should be noted that the OP is about 15% hole-doped, which is much beyond the AF stability in

the conventional hole-doped cuprates. Then the questions that in what condition can the self-doped phenomenon exist and how to realize the magnetic properties are naturally interested.

Theoretically, several works had been carried out to investigate the multilayered cuprates. The enhancement of $T_{c}$ upon increasing the number of layers and the inhomogeneous charge distribution were proposed in the earlier study [12-14]. Based on an extended $t-J$ model, Mori et al. studied the overdoped three- and four-layered cuprates. The redistribution of charge between the OP and IP leads to the distinct SC magnitude [15]. They further studied the doping dependence of the inter-layered coupling under the Gutzwiller approximation, two different values of inter-layered hopping were obtained between the IP and IP, the IP and OP, respectively [16]. However, the full doping range, especially the potential self-doped cases, has not been considered. Moreover, the role of antiferromagnetism has never been taken into account.

In this Letter, we focus on three question:

(1) The behavior of the charge imbalance over a wide doping range;
(2) The condition of the self-doping; and
(3) The magnetic properties, which was absent in the previous studies.

The key is that the effective Hubbard constants are much reduced due to the screening effect and show clear electron-hole asymmetry. The obtained phase diagrams show that three regions of the occupation state can be divided into depending on the average doping density and the value of Madelung energy, which predicts the self-doped phenomenon to form first in the four-layered multilayered cuprates with positive charge imbalance. The charge imbalance shows the distinct doping dependence in respective region and clear dependence on the number of layers. An unexpected enhancement of charge density upon the doping in the layer with the minority charge is predicted. The antiferromagnetism in the layer with majority charge follows the conventional doping dependence. However, a two-step evolution of antiferromagnetism, originating from the intra-layered interaction and inter-layered coupling in the first- and second-step evolution respectively, is found in the layer with minority charge. Our theoretical results are qualitatively consistent with the experiments.

## 2. Theory

The Hubbard model with layered hopping is adopted. We introduce the Madelung potential $W$ in the OP to describe the influence of the apical atom [17,3], which leads to the inequivalence between the OP and IP. $W$ can be negative or positive (referred as N-based and P-based below, respectively) based on the different type of the apical atom. For example, the case with apical atom F is N-based, while the case with apical atom Hg, Tl or Cu is P-based.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\text {intra }}= & \left(-t \sum_{\langle i j\rangle_{1} \sigma \alpha}-t^{\prime} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle_{2} \sigma \alpha}-t^{\prime \prime} \sum_{\langle i j\rangle_{3} \sigma \alpha}\right)\left(c_{i \sigma \alpha}^{+} c_{j \sigma \alpha}+\text { h.c. }\right) \\
& +\sum_{i \sigma \alpha} \frac{U_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\alpha}}{2} n_{i \sigma \alpha} n_{i \bar{\sigma} \alpha}-\mu \sum_{i \sigma, \alpha} n_{i \sigma \alpha},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
H_{\text {inter }}=t_{\perp} \sum_{i \sigma\left\langle\alpha \alpha^{\prime}\right\rangle}\left(c_{i \sigma \alpha}^{+} c_{i \sigma \alpha^{\prime}}+\text { hc }\right),
$$

$$
H_{s}=W \sum_{i \sigma, \alpha=O P} n_{i \sigma \alpha} .
$$

Here, the subscript $\alpha$ denotes the layer index, and $\left\langle\alpha \alpha^{\prime}\right\rangle$ means the nearest-neighbor layers. The rest of the notations are standard.

Due to the weakened $U_{\text {eff }}^{\alpha}$ as shown below, it enables to approximate Eq. (1) by Hartree-Fock mean field theory. This model can be decoupled by defining the order parameter as [18]

$$
\left\langle c_{i \sigma \alpha}^{+} c_{i \sigma \alpha}\right\rangle=\frac{1-\delta_{\alpha}}{2}+\sigma(-1)^{i_{x}+i_{y}}(-1)^{\alpha} M_{\alpha},
$$

where $\delta_{\alpha}$ denotes the doping density in each layer (positive and negative for the hole- and electron-doped case, respectively). We use $\delta=\sum_{\alpha} \delta_{\alpha} / N$ as the average doping density. $M_{\alpha}=(-1)^{i_{x}+i_{y}}\left\langle S_{i \alpha}^{z}\right\rangle$, representing the AF order in respective layer, is also inequivalent. The charge imbalance, accounting for the charge density difference between the OP and IP, is defined as $y=\sum_{\sigma}\left(\left\langle c_{i \sigma}^{+} c_{i \sigma}\right\rangle_{O P}-\left\langle c_{i \sigma}^{+} c_{i \sigma}\right\rangle_{I P}\right)$. In the five-layered cuprates, one more parameter $y^{*}$ is needed to describe the charge imbalance between IP* (the middle inner plane) and OP, which is almost the same as $y$ as shown in the next section.

In consideration of two sublattices D and E (with corresponding operator $d$ and $e$, respectively) [19], the Hamiltonian can be expressed in the momentum space as follows

$$
\begin{aligned}
H= & \sum_{k \sigma \alpha} \epsilon_{k}^{\prime}\left(d_{k \sigma \alpha}^{+} e_{k \sigma \alpha}+\mathrm{hc}\right)+\sum_{k \sigma \alpha} \epsilon_{k}^{\alpha}\left(d_{k \sigma \alpha}^{+} d_{k \sigma \alpha}+e_{k \sigma \alpha}^{+} e_{k \sigma \alpha}\right) \\
& -\sum_{k \sigma \alpha} \sigma U_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\alpha} M_{\alpha}\left(d_{k \sigma \alpha}^{+} d_{k \sigma \alpha}-e_{k \sigma \alpha}^{+} e_{k \sigma \alpha}\right) \\
& +\sum_{k \sigma\left\langle\alpha \alpha^{\prime}\right\rangle} \epsilon_{\perp k}\left(d_{k \sigma \alpha}^{+} e_{k \sigma \alpha^{\prime}}\left(d_{k \sigma \alpha^{\prime}}\right)+e_{k \sigma \alpha}^{+} d_{k \sigma \alpha^{\prime}}\left(e_{k \sigma \alpha^{\prime}}\right)\right) .
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, the summation is restricted to the magnetic Brillouin zone (BZ): $-\pi<k_{x} \pm k_{y} \leqslant \pi . \epsilon_{k}^{O P}=\epsilon_{k}+W_{\text {eff }}$ with $W_{\text {eff }}=W+\left(U_{\text {eff }}^{O P}\left\langle n_{i}\right\rangle_{O P}-U_{\text {eff }}^{I P}\left\langle n_{i}\right\rangle_{I P}\right) / 2$, while $\epsilon_{k}^{I P}=\epsilon_{k} . \epsilon_{k}^{\prime}=-2 t\left(\cos k_{x}+\cos k_{y}\right)$, and $\epsilon_{k}=-4 t^{\prime} \cos k_{x} \cos k_{y}-2 t^{\prime \prime}\left(\cos 2 k_{x}+\cos 2 k_{y}\right)-\mu$ is the general tight banding dispersion for inter-, and intra-sublattice. The inter-layered hopping, neglecting the doping dependence [16], takes the form of $\epsilon_{\perp k}=-t_{\perp}\left(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y}\right)^{2} / 4$ as usual [20]. We do not pre-assume the layered magnetic correlation, which will be determined by the free energy minimization.

The key in our model Hamiltonian is that the adopted Hubbard constants are renormalized due to the strong screening effect as shown by the LDA + U calculation [3]. This means that the strong correlation effect has been partially taken into account. The in-

fluence of this screening effect can be simply approximated by a reduced $U_{\text{eff}}$ instead of the original value [21], that is,
$$
U_{\mathrm{eff}}=U /\left(1+U\langle P(q, 0)\rangle_{q}\right).\tag{6}
$$

Here, $P(q, 0)=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{k \eta \eta^{\prime}}\left(1 \pm \frac{\epsilon_{k}^{\prime} \epsilon_{k+q}^{\prime}+(U M)^{2}}{E_{k}^{3} E_{k+q}^{3}}\right) \frac{f\left(E_{k+q}^{\eta}\right)-f\left(E_{k}^{\eta^{\prime}}\right)}{E_{k}^{\eta}-E_{k+q}^{\eta^{\prime}}}(+, \text { and }- \text { for }$ $\eta=\eta^{\prime}$, and $\eta \neq \eta^{\prime}$, respectively) is the charge susceptibility, where $E_{k}^{3}=\sqrt{\left(\epsilon_{k}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+(U M)^{2}}$, and $E_{k}^{\eta}=\epsilon_{k} \pm E_{k}^{3}$. The average of $P(q, 0)$ is taken over the whole BZ. The validity of such treatment had been verified by the previous variational Monte Carlo calculations [21]. Moreover, its application on the electron-doped cuprates made great success [22]. $U_{\text {eff }}$ is reduced much both in the hole- and electron-doped cuprates as shown in Fig. 1. (also see Ref. [22]). Furthermore, the reduction with doping is much steeper in the hole-doped case than that in the electron-doped case. Correspondingly, the antiferromagnetism in the latter is more stable than that in the former when the doping density increases, consisting with experimental phase diagram [23]. In fact, the doping dependence can be well described by a second order exponential decay fitting $U_{\text {eff }}=A_{0}+A_{1} \exp \left(-\delta / B_{1}\right)+A_{2} \exp \left(-\delta / B_{2}\right)$.

At end of this section, we would like to point out that the $t-J$ model [15] is intrinsically limited in the present multilayered cuprates, especially in the self-doped situations due to the limitation of no double occupancy, though a particle-hole transformation is widely used in the mono-layered electron-doped cuprates [24]. In addition, the AF instability in the $t-J$ model is far beyond the experimental phase diagram unless a phenomenological parameter is introduced [25]. In this sense, the present Hamiltonian is more suitable for describing the AF state.

### 3. Numerical results

For simplicity, $t$ is taken as unit in this Letter, $t^{\prime} / t=-0.25$, $t^{\prime \prime} / t=0.1$, and $t_{\perp} / t=0.3$ [26]. $U_{\text {eff }}^{\alpha}$ depends on the corresponding doping density of respective layer, which will be self-consistent calculated. $W$ is positive and negative in the P- and N-based multilayered cuprates, respectively, while the charge imbalance $y$ is reversed. We will use their absolute values unless specified.

The phase diagrams are shown in Fig. 2. Apparently, there are three types of the electron occupation states depending on $W$ and $\delta$. In the N-based cases, both the OP and IP are hole-doped (HDHD) for $W<W_{c 1}$ at given doping density. The OP develops into non-doped, and electron-doped for $W_{c 1}<W<W_{c 2}$, and $W>W_{c 2}$, respectively, while the IP remains hole-doped (referred as NDHD, and EDHD, respectively). Similar behaviors are also found in P-based cases but with reversed OP and IP. $W_{c 1}$ and $W_{c 2}$ increase upon the doping. On the other hand, they increase with the number of layers in the N-based cases, and decrease in the P-based cases. However, the smallest $W_{c 2}$ can be found in the N-based self-doped four-layered cuprates. This means the self-doped phenomenon is expected to form easily in this condition, which may be why F0234 being considered as the self-doped cuprate previously [2,3]. In fact, the self-doped phenomenon is common in the multilayered cuprates if the proper parameters are chosen. To be EDHD state, large enough $W\left(>W_{c 2}\right)$ is needed to overcome the AF splitting. In addition, $W_{c 1}$ and $W_{c 2}$ also strongly depend on the bared $U$. Large $U$ means strong AF splitting, which enhances the critical value of $W$. Hence, the EDHD state favors small $U$ at given $W$ and $\delta$.

The clear difference between the N- and P-based cases can be found both in the three- and five-layered cuprates as shown in Fig. 2. Such difference is mainly due to the different charge transferring. There are two OPs and one IP in the three-layered cuprates. The electrons transfer from two OPs to one IP in the P-based case, in contrast to those transfer from one IP to two OPs in the N-based case. Therefor the IP in the P-based case enters the non-doped state and subsequent electron-doped state easily. Similar analysis can also be applied in the five-layered cases. In the four-layered cuprates, the number of OPs and IPs is the same. However, small

The charge imbalance and charge density show distinct doping dependence in the respective region as can be seen from Fig. 3. Three occupancy regions are divided by $\delta_{c1}$ and $\delta_{c2}$ in the N-based cases. It is worthwhile to point out that:

(1) In the region of $\delta < \delta_{c1}$ (EDHD region), the charge imbalance falls quickly when $\delta$ closes to $\delta_{c1}$. The most interesting is that the corresponding charge density in IP (with minority charge) in- creases upon $\delta$ in this condition. Such unconventional behavior has never been reported before. Detailed doping dependent experiments in this region are expected to check it. In fact, this anomaly is intimately related to the emergence of the an- tiferromagnetism due to the rapid enhancement of $U_{\text{eff}}^{OP}$, which will be clarified below;

(2) The region of $\delta_{c1} < \delta < \delta_{c2}$ (NDHD region) will be absent if the antiferromagnetism is not taken into account;

(3) When $\delta$ is large enough, the antiferromagnetism disappears due to the large hole concentration. The charge imbalance fol- lows the linear dependence on the average doping, which is consistent with the previous experiment [8]. In fact, the effec- tive Hubbard constant changes smoothly in this condition. As a result, the $t$-$J$ type description is also available, where the similar linear behavior had been reported by Mori et al. [15]. In this sense, their work is just the special case without AF order;

(4) The deviation between $y$ and $y^{*}$ (not shown) is indeed small in all regions in the five-layered cuprates, which is confirmed by the NMR experiment since the two NMR peaks overlap with each other [7]. Similar doping dependence of the charge imbalance and charge density can also be found in the P-based cases.

Fig. 4(a) shows the AF order in N-based three-layered cuprates. Our numerical results show that the inter-layered ferromagnetic, and AF correlation favors the EDHD, and other two states, re- spectively. This is consistent with the experimental NMR discov- ery [4,6]. When $W$ is small enough (for example 1.0) only the NDHD and HDHD states exist (also see Fig. 2(a)). $M_{OP}$ keeps al- most unchanged in the NDHD region and falls quickly after enter- ing the HDHD region. This is just an usual doping dependence of the AF order, which is originated from the intra-layered interac- tion. On the other hand, $M_{IP}$ shows a two-step evolution though the IP is always hole-doped. It drops rapidly until $\delta \sim 0.04$, and then decreases slowly to zero. The first step, mainly determined by its intra-layered interaction $U_{\text{eff}}^{IP}$, is also the usual doping de- pendence of the AF order in hole-doped case. However, in the second step, $U_{\text{eff}}^{IP}$ is not strong enough to stabilize the antifer- romagnetism due to the enhancement of $\delta_{IP}$. The antiferromag- netism does not originate from its intra-layered interaction any more, but from the inter-layered coupling. It decreases with the de- creasing $M_{OP}$ and disappears together with $M_{OP}$. When $W$ is large enough (for example $W=1.5$), the EDHD state emerges. $M_{OP}$ shows an usual evolution from electron-doped to hole-doped. While only the second-step evolution of $M_{IP}$ can be found due to the large enough $\delta_{IP}$. It should also been pointed out that the second-step evolution of $M_{IP}$ indeed includes two possibili- ties:

(1) In the EDHD state, $M_{IP}$ is induced by the inter-layered ferro- magnetic coupling, it vanishes when EDHD state turns to be NDHD;

(2) In the NDHD and HDHD state, $M_{IP}$ is induced by inter-layered AF coupling. Similar behaviors of AF orders can also be found in the N-based four- and five-layered cuprates as shown in Fig. 4(b) and (c).

In fact, the second-step evolution had been found experimentally. In F0234, the IP is 7% hole-doped, which is beyond the AF stability in the conventional mono-layered cuprates. However, it is still AF due to the inter-layered coupling [4]. In the five-layered case, a more order parameter $M_{IP^*}$ is needed to describe the AF order of IP*. $M_{IP^*}$ is generally smaller than $M_{IP}$ in the first step in the N-based case. While it disappears in the second step since $M_{IP}$ is not strong enough to produce AF order in the IP*.

The evolutions of the AF order in P-based multilayered cuprates are similar but with reversed OP and IP as shown in lower panels of Fig. 4. Of course, the deviation between the N- and P-based case is also clear due to the different charge transferring. Unlike the N-based case, the relationship between $M_{IP}$ and $M_{IP^*}$ shows more complex behavior in five-layered cuprate. If the inner layers are electron-doped, $M_{IP} > M_{IP^*}$. If they are non-doped, $M_{IP^*}$ is slightly larger than $M_{IP}$. This deviation is enlarged upon doping if both IP and IP* are hole-doped. This is well supported by the NMR exper-

iments [5,7], which shows that $M_{IP}$ and $M_{IP*}$ in deep underdoped Hg1245 is almost the same, while they show clear difference in moderate doped case.

At the end of this section, we discuss the influence of the antiferromagnetism on the charge distribution. The density of states (DOS) of the OP and IP in P-based four-layered cuprates is shown in Fig. 5 with $W=1.0$. Firstly, in the region of EDHD, the cases of $\delta=0.01$ and $\delta=0.03$ are compared in Fig. 5(a). For the IP layer, $E_{f}$ locates nearby the AF gap. A sharp decreasing of DOS is then expected as $E_{f}$ moves towards left upon doping. The total DOS in the IP below $E_{f}$ decreases fast. By comparison, it increases slightly in the OP. As a result, the quick decreasing of charge imbalance and slightly increasing of charge density in IP are not difficult to understand (also see Fig. 3). Secondly, in the region of NDHD, the cases of $\delta=0.04$ and $\delta=0.07$ are shown in Fig. 5(b). The total DOS below $E_{f}$ in OP shows clear decrease. However, in the IP, $E_{f}$ locates in the AF gap, which leads to the almost unchanged charge density. The doped holes enter the OP entirely. The charge imbalance, therefor, shows a quick increase. Finally, in the region of HDHD as shown in Fig. 5(c), $E_{f}$ locates below the AF gap. The total DOS below $E_{f}$ decreases upon doping both in the OP and IP. Similar analysis could be also applied on the other situations.

## 4. Conclusion and discussion

In this Letter, the charge imbalance and the AF order in the multilayered cuprates are studied in detail both for the P- and N-based cases. The effective Hubbard constants we adopted are strongly doping-dependent and show clear electron-hole asymmetry in consideration of the screening effect. Three regions of the occupancy state are found depending on the average doping and the value of the Madelung energy W. The smallest W to form the EDHD state is in the N-based self-doped four-layered cuprates. The charge imbalance shows interesting behaviors in the different regions due to the influence of antiferromagnetism. The charge density in the layer with minority charge may even increase upon the doping in the vicinity of the EDHD state. The evolution of antiferromagnetism is complicated. The conventional doping dependence in the layers with majority charge is found. However, the antiferromagnetism follows a two-step evolution in the layers with minority charge, which is induced by the intra-layered interaction, and inter-layered coupling for the first-, and second-step evolution, respectively.

Our theoretical investigation is restricted in the AF state, the SC is not considered. One could learn from the above calculations that the doping density in the OP and IP is different due to the influence of the Madelung energy. Thus two SC gaps in the multilayered cuprates come out naturally [15]. On the other hand, the suppressing of the SC in the IPs of Hg1245 due to the strong antiferromagnetism is also expected [5]. However, the SC gap along the electron-like FS sheet is about two times as that along the hole-like FS sheet in F0234 is not easy to comprehensive [2]. This probably implies that the inter-layered coupling plays an important role in the SC. More sophisticated treatments, such as the quantum tunneling [27] and pairing hoping effects [28], are expected to understand the nature of the SC in the multilayered cuprates.

This is beyond of the present Letter, and will be considered in future.
