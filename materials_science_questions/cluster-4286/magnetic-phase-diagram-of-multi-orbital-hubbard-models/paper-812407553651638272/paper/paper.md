# The generalized Gutzwiller method for $n \geqslant 2$ correlated orbitals: Itinerant ferromagnetism in d($e_{\rm g}$)- bands

J. Bünemann, W. Weber *

Institut für Physik, Universität Dortmund, Lebrstuhl für Theoretische Physik II, D-44221 Dortmund, Germany

## Abstract
Using the generalized Gutzwiller method we present results on the ferromagnetic behavior of extended Hubbard models with two degenerate d($e_{\rm g}$) orbitals. We find significant differences from the results obtained from Hartree-Fock theory.

**Keywords:** Correlated electron system; Hubbard model; Extended Hubbard Models; Gutzwiller method; Itinerant ferromagnetism

Recently, the Gutzwiller variational method has been generalized for $n \geqslant 2$ correlated orbitals per site [1]. In this paper we present studies of the itinerant ferromagnetism using a two-band Hubbard model with degenerate orbitals and general on-site interactions.

The one-band Gutzwiller variational wave function is given by [2]
$$
|\Psi\rangle \equiv g^{\hat{D}}\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle=\prod_{s=1}^{L}\left[1-(1-g) \hat{D}_{s}\right]\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle, \quad(1)
$$
where $|\Psi_{0}\rangle$ is an uncorrelated wave function on a lattice of $L$ sites and the operators $\hat{D}_{s} \equiv \hat{n}_{s \uparrow} \hat{n}_{s \downarrow}$ measure the double occupancy of sites $s$. By combinatorics, one is led to the hopping 'loss factors'
$$
q_{k} \equiv \frac{1}{m_{k}\left(1-m_{k}\right)}\left[\sqrt{\overline{m}_{k} \overline{m}_{0}}+\sqrt{\overline{m}_{k l} \overline{m}_{l}}\right]^{2}, \quad(2)
$$
where $m_{0}, m_{1}, m_{2}, m_{12}$ ($\overline{m}_{0}, \overline{m}_{1}, \overline{m}_{2}, \overline{m}_{12}$) are the respective gross (net) occupancies of sites: empty, spin-up, spin-down, double. There are relations between gross and net occupancies such as $\overline{m}_{1}=m_{1}-\overline{m}_{12}$ or $m_{0}=\overline{m}_{0}=1-\overline{m}_{1}-\overline{m}_{2}-\overline{m}_{12}$.

The extension to cases of $n \geqslant 2$ correlated orbitals $(a, b, \ldots)$ leads to $J_{n}=4^{n}$ different possibilities for a single site occupancy, and $K_{n}=J_{n}-(2 n+1)$ of them represent multiple ones. The basic idea of our generalization was to include all $K_{n}$ multiple occupancy operators.

Using the notation $a \uparrow \hat{=} 1, a \downarrow \hat{=} 2, b \uparrow \hat{=} 3, b \downarrow \hat{=} 4, ...$, the resulting generalized hopping loss factors have been found to be
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{k k}= & \frac{1}{m_{k}\left(1-m_{k}\right)}\left[\sqrt{\overline{m}_{k} \overline{m}_{0}}+\sum_{l}^{\prime} \sqrt{\overline{m}_{k l} \overline{m}_{l}}\right. \\
& \left.+\sum_{l, p}^{\prime \prime} \sqrt{\overline{m}_{k l p} \overline{m}_{l p}}+\sum_{l, p, q}^{\prime \prime \prime} \sqrt{\overline{m}_{k l p q} \overline{m}_{l p q}}+\cdots\right]^{2},
\end{aligned}\quad(3)
$$
$$
q_{k l}^{2}=q_{k k} q_{l l}.\quad(4)
$$

It is now possible to investigate extensions of the Hubbard model for arbitrary numbers of orbitals $\alpha, \beta$ and more general on-site interactions:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat{H}= & \sum_{\alpha, \beta, s, t, \sigma} T_{s t}^{\alpha \beta} \hat{\alpha}_{s \sigma}^{+} \hat{\beta}_{t \sigma}+\sum_{\alpha, \beta, s, \sigma, \sigma^{\prime}}^{\prime} U^{\alpha \beta} \hat{n}_{s \sigma}^{\alpha} \hat{n}_{s \sigma^{\prime}}^{\beta} \\
& +\sum_{\alpha, \beta, s, \sigma, \sigma^{\prime}}^{\prime} J^{\alpha \beta} \hat{\alpha}_{s \sigma}^{+} \hat{\beta}_{s \sigma^{\prime}}^{+} \hat{\alpha}_{s \sigma^{\prime}} \hat{\beta}_{s \sigma}.
\end{aligned}\quad(5)
$$

For our present study we have focussed on a ferromagnetic case; i.e. we have chosen $N$-particle wave functions $|\Psi\rangle,|\Psi_{0}\rangle$ which allow that $m_{1}=$ $m_{3}=m_{+}=\langle\hat{n}_{\uparrow}\rangle \neq\langle\hat{n}_{\downarrow}\rangle=m_{-}$. This spin-splitting is controlled by an additional variational parameter $\Delta$ ($m_{+}=m+\Delta ; m_{-}=m-\Delta$), representing the magnetization. We have studied the $n=2$ Hamiltonian with two $e_{\mathrm{g}}$-type orbitals on a simple cubic lattice and have included the first nearest neighbor (1NN) and $2 \mathrm{NN}$ hopping terms given by [3] $T_{d d \sigma}(1 \mathrm{NN})=1 \mathrm{eV}$, $T_{d d \sigma}(2 \mathrm{NN})=0.25 \mathrm{eV}, \quad$ and $\quad T_{d d \delta}: T_{d d \pi}: T_{d d \sigma}=$ $0.1: 0.3:-0.3: 1$. Further, there are three interaction parameters $U^{\alpha \alpha} \equiv U, U^{\alpha \beta} \equiv U^{\prime}$ and $J^{\alpha \beta} \equiv J$. We employ the relation $2 J=U-U^{\prime}$, which is valid for $e_{\mathrm{g}}$-orbitals in the limit of vanishing configuration interaction [4].

There are seven variational parameters representing the multiple occupancies: $f \equiv \bar{m}_{1234}, t_{+} \equiv$ $\bar{m}_{123}=\bar{m}_{134}, t_{-} \equiv \bar{m}_{124}=\bar{m}_{234}, d_{t+} \equiv \bar{m}_{13}, d_{-} \equiv$ $\bar{m}_{24} d_{d} \equiv \bar{m}_{12}=\bar{m}_{34}, d_{o} \equiv \bar{m}_{14}=\bar{m}_{23}$. Further, there are two different loss factors $q_{+} \equiv q_{11}=q_{33}=q_{13}=$ $q_{31}$ and $q_{-} \equiv q_{22}=q_{44}=q_{24}=q_{42}$, with

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{\mu}= & \frac{1}{m_{\mu}\left(1-m_{\mu}\right)}\left[\sqrt{\bar{m}_{\mu}}\left(\sqrt{\bar{m}_{0}}+\sqrt{d_{t \mu}}\right)\right. \\
& +\left(\sqrt{d_{d}}+\sqrt{d_{o}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\bar{m}_{(-\mu)}}+\sqrt{t_{\mu}}\right) \\
& \left.+\sqrt{t_{(-\mu)}}\left(\sqrt{f}+\sqrt{d_{t(-\mu)}}\right)\right]^{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The ground state energy function is

$$
\begin{aligned}
E= & q_{+} \bar{\varepsilon}\left(m_{+}\right)+q_{-} \bar{\varepsilon}\left(m_{-}\right) \\
& +2 U d_{d}+2 U^{\prime} d_{o}+\left(U^{\prime}-J\right)\left(d_{t+}+d_{t-}\right) \\
& +\left(2 U+4 U^{\prime}-2 J\right)\left(t_{+}+t_{-}+f\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

with $\bar{\varepsilon}(m)$ being the kinetic energy of the uncorrelated bands. Note that the number of electrons per atom is given by $e / a=4 m$. We have studied band fillings, which, for the paramagnetic case, are close to the biggest peak in the single particle DOS.

Fig. 1 shows the magnetization $\Delta$ as a function of $U$ (with $J=0.2 U$ ) for the values $m=0.35$ (Fig. 1(a)) and $m=0.3$ (Fig. 1(b)). Also shown are the values of $N\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)$. When $e / a$ is chosen so that $N\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)$ of the uncorrelated case coincides with the DOS peak $(m=$ $0.3)$, there exists a kind of Stoner criterion for the ferromagnetic instability. Then, $\Delta$ starts as a continuous function of $(U, U', J)$ (Fig. 1(b)). Away from this peak, there is a first-order transition to a state of finite magnetization before the Stoner criterion is met (Fig. 1(a)). We observe further discontinuous increases of the magnetization with increasing interaction $(U, U', J)$. These jumps are related to structures in the DOS which pass through the Fermi level with increasing splitting of majority and minority bands (see Fig. 1(a)).

A Hartree-Fock treatment of the model leads to onsets of ferromagnetism at much smaller values of the interaction parameters $U, U', J$ (Fig. 2). The first-order transition from $\Delta=0$ to finite $\Delta$ is also seen in HF theory; however, no further jumps in magnetization are observed.

Fig. 3. Phase diagram in the $J/U$ versus $U$ plane for the Gutzwiller results with $m=0.3$ (note that $U'=U-2J$).

The magnetic phase diagram (Fig. 3) elucidates the role of the on-site exchange for the formation of itinerant ferromagnetism.

In conclusion, our study of itinerant ferromagnetism indicates significant differences between the Gutzwiller method and the Hartree-Fock theory. We hope that the generalized Gutzwiller method will enable us to judge the quality of effective one-particle theories for itinerant magnetism such as spin density functional theory for 3D transition metals.
