# Size-dependent elastic properties of single-walled ZnO nanotubes:
A first-principles study

Yu-Hua Wen,⁽ᵃ⁾ Yang Zhang, Shun-Qing Wu, and Zi-Zhong Zhu
Department of Physics and Institute of Theoretical Physics and Astrophysics, Xiamen University, Xiamen
361005, China

(Received 3 January 2011; accepted 5 March 2011; published online 21 April 2011)

By means of first-principles calculations, we have investigated a size dependence of elastic modulus in single-walled ZnO nanotubes with armchair and zigzag forms. It is found that for these tubes the Young's modulus is increased dramatically with the increased diameters and inversely proportional to the Zn−O bond length. Further, the amount of charge transfer, calculated by the Bader analysis, is introduced to elucidate the strength of bonding between Zn and O atoms in these tubes. © 2011 American Institute of Physics. [doi:10.1063/1.3573508]

## I. INTRODUCTION
Over the past decade, zinc oxide (ZnO) materials have attracted a great deal of research because of their intrinsic semiconducting and piezoelectric properties.¹ ZnO exhibits a direct wide bandgap of 3.37 eV and a large excitation bind-ing energy (60 meV). Due to the noncentrosymmetric wurt-zite structure, ZnO is highly piezoelectric, playing a key role in building electromechanically coupled sensors and trans-ducers.² Experimentally, nanosized ZnO has a rich variety of morphologies and structures, including nanorods,³ nanobelts,⁴ nanowires,⁵ nanotubes,⁶ and nanorings,⁷ with demon-strated potential applications in nanoelectronics, piezoelectric devices, optoelectronics, chemical sensors, and nanoelectro-mechanical systems (NEMS).⁸,⁹ All of these applications require the knowledge of the structure and properties of ZnO nanostructures. In particular, it is of crucial importance to understand the mechanical properties of ZnO nanostructures for the efficient operation of ZnO based nanopiezoelectric devices.¹⁰

ZnO nanotubes (ZnO NTs), grown by present experi-mental approaches, are usually hollow nanowires with wurt-zite single crystal and have a typical diameter of 20−450 nm and thickness of 4−100 nm as well as the axis oriented along the [0001] direction.⁶,¹⁰ Although numerous and rapid pro-gresses have been achieved in synthesizing ZnO NTs and nanowires (NWs),⁵,⁶,¹⁰,¹¹ experimental investigations of the nanomechanical properties and their size dependence have been reported only recently. For example, both experimental and theoretical studies show that the Young's moduli of ZnO NWs are increased dramatically with decreasing diameters, and significantly higher than that of bulk ZnO.¹¹⁻¹³ This behavior is attributed to a surface stiffening effect dominat-ing at large surface-to-volume ratios.¹⁴,¹⁵ The same depend-ence remains valid in ZnO NTs.¹⁰,¹⁴⁻¹⁶ However, some experiments have verified that the Young's moduli of ZnO NWs are essentially independent of diameter and close to the bulk value down to diameters of 18 nm,¹⁷,¹⁸ whereas the ultimate strength increases for small diameter wires, and exhibits values up to 40 times that of bulk.¹⁸ The conflicting reports on the Young's moduli of ZnO NWs and their diame-ter dependence suggest that there call for different and more accurate ways to characterize the elastic properties of nanowires.

Although ZnO NWs have been extensively investigated over the past decade, to the best of our knowledge, a thorough study of elastic properties by a consideration of ZnO NTs with different sizes and structures is currently lacking. Espe-cially, a strong doubt arises spontaneously after successful synthesis of single-walled carbon NTs (SWCNTs), i.e., what will happen to these ZnO NTs when their thicknesses are reduced to one atom thick? Available first-principles calcula-tions predict that single-walled ZnO NTs (SWZONTs) can exist stably and might be realized by the solid-vapor phase process with carbon NTs as templates.¹⁹,²⁰ Additionally, they may transform into unusual fourfold-coordinated tubes under axial compression or tension.²¹,²² Therefore, SWZONTs could be expected to possess richer morphologies and struc-tures compared with carbon NTs. Although there are few recent works addressing the structure and stability of ZnO NTs, a comprehensive characterization of size-dependent me-chanical properties of these systems is still missing. In this pa-per, we focus on study of stability and size-dependent elasticity of SWZONTs by the density functional theory cal-culations, and explore the relation between elastic modulus bond length.

## II. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS
As is well known, SWCNTs, which can be viewed as a graphene sheet rolled into tubes, are usually indexed by a pair of integers (n, m) to represent their helicities.²³ Theoreti-cal studies have demonstrated that ZnO NTs will take the form of SWCNTs when their thicknesses decrease down to one atom thick.¹⁹⁻²¹ Therefore, the SWZONTs can be gener-ated in the same way as the SWCNTs. Here we have consid-ered two types of SWZONTs, namely, armchair (A-type) and zigzag (Z-type). The original structures of A-type (n, n) and Z-type (n, 0) SWZONTs are constructed by rolling up a

All SWZONTs are modeled in a tetragonal supercell and are infinitely long along their axes by applying periodic boundary conditions. The axial direction is along $z$ axis and a vacuum region of at least $10 \AA$ is applied in $x$ and $y$ axis to avoid the interactions between SWZONTs. The calculations are performed by Vienna $a b$ initio simulation package(VASP) $)^{24,25}$ based on the projector augmented wave (PAW) method $^{26}$ within density functional theory (DFT). The func tional proposed by Perdew and Wang 91 (PW 91) with gen- eralized gradient approximation (GGA) $^{27}$ is used to describe the exchange-correlation interactions. The cut-off of plane- wave energy is set to $500 eV$ , and the convergence for total energy is controlled to be smaller than $10^{-6} eV$ . For Bril louin-zone integrations, a $1 \times 1 \times 11 k$ -point mesh with gamma centered grid is used for all the SWZONTs studied.

Upon starting the axial strain, the SWZONTs are fully relaxed to their local minimum energy states by computing the Hellmann-Feynman (H-F) forces. $^{28}$ All atoms in the supercell are allowed to move freely until the maximum $H-F$ force is smaller than $0.005 eV / \AA$ . Subsequently, these NTs are subjected to a uniaxial loading process. The uniaxial strain is applied along the axial direction of SWZONTs to acquire their elastic responses. The applied strain range is from $-2.0 \%$ to $2.0 \%$ with an increment of $0.2 \%$ . At each strain step, the SWZONTs are also fully relaxed to their local minimum energy states.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Different from SWCNTs in which all of carbon atoms lie on a cylindrical surface, for the optimized SWZONTs, $Zn$ and O atoms are located at two different coaxial cylindrical surfa- ces (see Fig. 1) whose diameters are denoted as $d_{Zn}$ and $d_{O}$ . Zn(O) atoms shift inward (outward) along the tube's radius,similar to the relaxations of surface atoms in [0001] $ZnO$ nanowires. $^{29}$ For comparison of SWZONTs with different sizes and structures, a common diameter has been defined as

$$d=\frac{d_{\mathrm{Zn}}+d_{\mathrm{O}}}{2}.\qquad(1)$$

Figure 1 shows the size dependence of the binding energy for fully relaxed SWZONTs. It is seen that a strong size effect is evident for both types of tubes. By comparison of binding energies, SWZONT of armchair form is found to be more stable than that of zigzag form. The binding energies of armchair and zigzag NTs decrease monotonically with increased diameter and tend to that of $ZnO$ graphitic sheet when the diameter is infinite, in good agreement with a pre- vious study. $^{19}$ The above results can be elucidated by the curvature effects of tube that is, the strain energy becomes lower with decreased curvature (i.e., increased diameter) so that the total energy of the rolled-up $ZnO$ graphitic sheet is gradually close to that of the $ZnO$ graphitic sheet.

As one of the most important elastic properties, the Young's modulus $E$ is defined as the second derivative of the total energy with respect to the strain at the equilibrium state(i.e., at unstrained state) by the following equation $^{30}$ 

$$E=\frac{1}{\Omega_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2} U}{\partial \varepsilon^{2}}\right)_{\varepsilon=0},\qquad(2)$$

where $\Omega_{0}$ and $U$ are the equilibrium volume and binding energy, and $\varepsilon$ is the strain. By loading certain strains on the tube along its axial direction, one can obtain the total energy of the system as a function of the strain. The Young's modu- lus of this tube can be deduced finally from the strain-energy relation. Actually, the Young's modus calculated from this equation is equivalent to that from the first derivative of thestress with respect to the strain. $^{31}$

For both types of tubes, the elastic modulus is increased with increased diameter, similar to that of SWCNTs. $^{32}$ Additionally, the difference of elastic moduli between both types of SWZONTs is reduced with enlarged diameter. There is a significant difference lying between single-walled and "multi-walled" (or thick) ZnO NTs. For those ZnO NTs with thickness larger than $10 \AA$ , the Young's modulus is decreased with increased diameter and thickness. $^{14,15}$
The smallest value, namely 183 GPa or so, is remarkably larger than the maximum of SWZONTs obtained in our study. The opposite diameter dependent elastic modulus is attributed to the structural differences between single-walled and "multi-walled" ZnO NTs. For thick ZnO NTs, their crystalline structures belong to the wurtzite form, similar to that of ZnO bulk. They grew experimentally along the [0001] direction, with lateral $(10 \overline{1} 0)$ facets. $^{33,34}$ The size dependent elastic modulus originates from the surface stiffening effect. $^{11,12,14,15}$ However, as aforementioned, the structure of SWZONTs is analogous to that of SWCNTs. No surface stiffening effect occurs in these tubes.

Since the Young's modulus characterizes the strength of the forces between atoms that varies with the type of their bonding in a given material, it is relatively sensitive to the change of interatomic distance. Therefore, the Young's modulus should be expected to be closely associated with the bond length of this material. To explore the origin of size dependence of the elastic modulus of SWZONT, we have calculated $Zn-O$ bond lengths in these tubes. Note that the $Zn-O$ bond lengths inside each ZnO nanotube are not equal but their differences are small, whereas for the ZnO graphitic sheet, all of the $Zn-O$ bond lengths are equal to $1.896 \AA$ . Therefore, the average bond length is introduced to characterize the bonding strength between Zn and O atoms in the SWZONTs. Figure 3 shows the diameter dependence of the average $Zn-O$ bond length. It is seen from this figure that strong size effects are also evident for both types of SWZONTs. Their bond lengths are decreased with increased diameter, showing a strengthening bond in the tubes with larger diameters. Furthermore, the bond length of the armchair tube is clearly larger than that of zigzag tube.

By comparing of Fig. 3 with Fig. 2, one can see that there is a clear correlation between the Young's modulus and the bond length: the smaller the bond length, the higher the Young's modulus. Their relation has been inserted in Fig. 3, which reveals that the Young's modulus is inversely proportional to the bond length. This finding has further demonstrated that the elastic modulus is closely associated with the interatomic bonding strength. However, a spontaneous doubt is why the $Zn-O$ bond length is decreased with the enlarged tube diameter? To trace the origin of bonding strength, a detailed investigation of electronic distribution is urgent for quantitative description of interatomic interactions in the SWZONTs. A key approach is the study of the electron density changes taking place upon formation of SWZONTs. The electron density change $\Delta \rho$ is defined as the charge density difference between the SWZONT and the superposition of independent atomic charge densities placed at the atomic sites of the tube. This can give a picture of the direction and extent of electron transfer over the space. As a representative, the inset of Fig. 4 shows the contour plot of the electron density difference for the cross section of (4,4) SWZONT. Unambiguously, the picture of charge displacement indicates that a charge transfer occurs going from zinc toward oxygen atom. To obtain further the amount of the charge transfer $\Delta q$ , we have adopted an improved algorithm proposed by Tang et al. $^{35}$ to do the Bader analysis on the charge density grids. The $\Delta q$ measures the electron charge that, upon formation of the tube, is transferred from the zinc atoms to the oxygen ones. Figure 4 shows the diameter dependence of the charge transfer. The amount of charge transferred to the oxygen atom is increased remarkably as the tube diameter becomes larger. An increased amount of charge transfer indicates the enhancing ionic interactions between zinc and oxygen atoms. Due to the importance of ionic interactions in SWZONTs, the stronger bonding

strength between Zn and O atoms will lead to shorter bond length accordingly with the increased tube diameter.

## IV. CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we have investigated the elasticity of SWZONTs by using first-principles calculations based on DFT. Two types of tubes, namely armchair and zigzag forms, have been considered. It is demonstrated that in contrast to recent reports on thick ZnO NTs, the Young's moduli for both types of SWZONTs are increased as the tube diameters increase. Such size-dependent elastic modulus mainly arises from the Zn$-$O bonding strength in these tubes. The Young's modulus is found to be inversely proportional to the Zn$-$O bond length. The Bader analysis of charge density differences shows that the Zn$-$O bond length is closely associated with the charge transfer from Zn to O atoms. The amount of transferred charge is generally increased with enlarged diameter, resulting in the strengthening bond between Zn and O atoms.
