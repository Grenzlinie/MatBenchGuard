## Abstract
Recently, some experimental works have been done to synthesize various allotropes of boron. This paper provides a detailed discussion on the rupture mechanisms of different allotropes of boron. By using a reactive molecular dynamics model, probable types of fracture for borophene sheets are studied. Two dominant mechanisms are observed for borophene sheets; the cracking and nanopore creation. Obtained results are compared with the results for graphene and h-BN nano sheets, and it is concluded that the rupture mechanism is completely different in borophene sheets with respect to the graphene and h-BN sheets. In comparison to the graphene and h-BN nano sheets, the observations suggest that borophene may remain more stable through external loads because of creation of some nanopores and also local cracking instead of suddenly rupturing the graphene and h-BN sheets. As a significant perspective, the rupture mechanism of borophene sheets has been categorize into two groups; cracking and local pore creation. Cracking leads to more strain along the load direction, whereas local pore creation spend the imposed energy for breaking the internal bonds and so flowing the external energy into the various

## 1. Introduction
From the structural point of view, advanced 2D materials can be thought of as layered a thin section of bulk materials. Graphite was the first material from which various configurations were separated; the flat surfaces (graphene), the cylindrical tubes (carbon nanotubes) and the spherical shapes (fullerenes) [1]. Hexagonal boron nitride [2], silicene [3], germanene [4], stanene [5] and transition metal dichalcogenides [6] like MoS2 [7] are the most famous structures presented till now. After these configurations, boron attracted researchers because of specific physical properties. Boron has various allotropes such as nanotube, nanowire and different kinds of 2D sheets [8]. Due to the development of different approaches to simulate and synthesize graphene, the same methods are being used for boron and especially for boron sheets (also called borophene) [9-13]. These 2D sheets are comprised of hexagonal and triangular lattices, and they show unique metallic, auxetic and electrical characteristics [14]. The capacity of borophene to store Li/Na has the highest value in comparison to other two-dimensional materials that have already been proposed [15].

Unique experimental works have been conducted in this field recently, and such structures are named Borophene [16-18]. Boron sheets with the ratio η (the ratio of vacant hexagons to the number of atoms in a triangular lattice; in the range of 0.1-0.15 are the most stable structures which are found till now) [9-11]. From proposed structures based on theory, some structures have been successfully synthesized [16, 18-20]. For example, Borophene in the form of triangular lattice ($\eta=0$) and planar lattice ($\eta=\frac{1}{5}$) have been grown on the silver bed, recently [16, 18]. Also, $\gamma$ - borophen ($\eta=\frac{1}{6}$) was synthesized on copper foil [21] and silver substrate [16].

A number of studies on various borophene structures are limited. Peng et al [22] investigated mechanical characteristics of $\alpha-boron$ with $\eta=1/9$ by Density Functional Theory (DFT). By the same method, electrical, magnetic and optical characteristics of boron sheets were also investigated [23-26]. In another valuable work, mechanical characteristics of allotropes of boron were studied by molecular dynamics for the first time [27]. Since synthesize of boron allotropes is challenging and expensive, conducting theoretical studies on design of interested related systems is critically important.

Researchers found out that free edges and defects are two geometric factors which affect mechanical characteristics of 2D materials. As edges behave freely and like a layer, raising the density of free edges can be practically beneficial, and they can be used as locations for bonding with external agents and doping of 2D sheets [28, 29]. It is obvious that existence of defects causes mechanical weakening of 2D materials. Therefore comprehensive mechanical analysis is needed to prevent disruption of designed systems made of 2D materials. Due to the intrinsic structure of various allotropes of boron, there are different homogeneous slots, and the following paragraphs are intended to distinguish these intrinsic defects and systematically categorize them. It should be emphasized that to gain some qualitative insight into

the complex behavior of borophene sheets, we resort to numerical simulations. However, some analytic simulation could be made, and it will be inevitable because analytic solutions introduce more simple analyses and could be simplified to model large scale systems [30-33].

Figure 1 illustrates the top view of various borophene sheets. Borophene edges have an armchair and zigzag formations like other 2D materials. Although it was shown for graphene experimentally that zigzag edges are more stable than armchair edges [34], because of the strong metallic structure of borophene, for borophene it was shown that both formations are stable [18]. However, the effects of free edges in the application of this material in the future's advanced systems will be important. Except for S1 structure which has one row of atoms outside the sheet and one row inside of it, other structures are planar.

![](./images/813109817462751234_2.jpg)

**Figure 1.** Various structures of borophene which have been studied in this work

Investigating the effects of intrinsic defects in some allotropes of borophene would be beneficial. Unlike conventional vacancies were investigated in graphene, the vacancies in borophene sheets are not concluded from undesirable conditions in synthesizing, but rather from intrinsic minimum energy state of structure [16-18, 26, 35-37]. However, we call such vacancies as defect yet. Probably this kind of defect has the most influence on mechanical characteristics of borophene. In the future, a study on cracks and long defects can make a new contribution in this new field. Any kind of defect is effective on changing borophene sheets characteristics. Therefore, in this research effects of various defects on mechanical characteristics of borophene sheets are evaluated to open a path for conduction other new works in this field.

Dependent on the provided condition for deposition of borophene in the experimental environment, the presence of different kinds of defects can be predicted for the structure. Since most of practical structures of borophene have atoms outside of sheets, its related types of defects have more variety in comparison to graphene and boron nitride. After the discovery of a quasi-planar boron cluster with a hole in the middle of it, it was proved unlike graphene which has no defect in its ideal structure; we can have relatively large scale boron layers with a vacancy in it.

There exist some reports on mechanical characteristics of borophene; though they are all computational. Most of the works were performed by using DFT, and there are a few MD analyses. Since the structure S1 (it is shown in figure 1) has the most stability, most of the works were conducted on it. Mortazavi et al [26] studied the effects of loading direction on mechanical characteristics of Borophene by DFT. Modulus of elasticity for armchair and zigzag formations was calculated as 382 and

163 Gpa.nm, respectively. They also studied effects of point vacancy on mechanical characteristics of five different single-layer borophenes by MD method and ReaxFF potential. The reported values for elasticity modulus of the armchair and zigzag directions were 284 and 249 Gpa.nm, respectively.

Mechanical strength and stability of planar single-layer □-boron under high strain was studied by using DFT, and 224.6 Gpa.nm was reported for armchair edges [22]. By the same method, 179 and 398 Gpa.nm [18], 162 and 377 Gpa.nm [38], 382,3 and 154,1 Gpa.nm [39], 389 and 166 Gpa.nm [40] and 213.78 and 94.116 Gpa.nm [41] have been reported. Recently it was shown other structures of borophene also have special mechanical characters which should be investigated [26]. In that research, for structures S2, S3, S4 and S5, elasticity modulus for armchair formation was reported 190, 208, 186 and 214 Gpa.nm, respectively. For zigzag formation, these numbers reported as 210, 205, 167 and 217 Gpa.nm, respectively.

By considering these works, borophene sheets and their important applications, especially in the energy storage field and batteries, the defects and the mechanisms that cause more and more defects make this topic so important. In this work, effects of different geometric parameters on mechanical characteristics of borophene sheets would be evaluated. Mechanism of rupture in various allotropes of boron was studied. Since the number of free boron atoms on edges play a fundamental role in future applications, mechanism of rupture during the elongation test of sheets is focused in order to determine how many internal boundaries allowed to be formed, whereas the boron allotropes remain intact.

## 2. Atomistic model
It was mentioned before that borophene is more complicated than graphene structurally. This feature might explain why there are only limited numbers of studies on it by molecular dynamics method. ReaxFF potentials are the only

potentials that have been used to study borophene [37] because other potentials are incapable of modeling boron atoms in single element borophene structures.

Based on ReaxFF potential, the total energy of a system made of lots of atoms can be described as follows:

$$
E_{sys}=E_{bond}+E_{angle}+E_{torsion}+E_{VdWaals}+E_{Coulomb} \tag{1}
$$

Which:

$$
E_{bond}=k_{b}(r-r_{0})^{2}
$$

$$
E_{angle}=k_{v}(\varphi-\varphi_{0})^{2}
$$

$$
E_{torsion}=V_{2}(1-\cos(2\omega))+V_{3}(1-\cos(3\omega))
$$

$$
E_{VdWaals}=D_{ij}\left\{e^{\alpha_{ij}\left(1-\frac{r_{ij}}{r_{VdW}}\right)}-2e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{ij}\left(1-\frac{r_{ij}}{r_{VdW}}\right)}\right\} \tag{2}
$$

$$
E_{Coulomb}=C\frac{q_{i}q_{j}}{r_{ij}}
$$

These formulas are applicable for structures close to an equilibrium state. Van Duin et al. showed for change from non-bonding state to one, double and triple bonding system; we can have a relation between the degree of bonding and the length of bonding, in a way that in each round the level of bonding be updated [42-44]. All the interactions (valence and torsion angles) in ReaxFF are dependent on the level of bonding to become confident about not considering the energy after removal of bonding. Non-bonding interactions (van der Waals and Coulomb) irrespective of bonding are calculated between each pair of atoms and atoms which have non-bonding interactions and are very close to each other would be removed from calculations. This potential accounts polarization effects by using a geometry-dependent charge calculation scheme [42]. When this potential is compared to other potentials to describe non-bonding forces in borophene, the advantage of this

potential becomes clear. ReaxFF potential considers all non-bonding interactions between the pair atoms without taking to account the type of the linking. That is the reason famous potentials like Tersoff and AIREBO are not applicable for borophene, and ReaxFF is the best choice. The related coefficients are provided based on the work presented in the reference [43].

## 3. Results and discussion
### 3.1. Materials and methods

For more reliable simulation, all of the simulations have performed in equilibrium ensembles. However, some non-equilibrium molecular dynamics have been done to guarantee the obtained results. In the equilibrium molecular dynamics, where all presented results of the current work have been obtained, the effect of the size of sheets on the stress-strain diagrams have been studied at first and then for all simulations, the smallest length and width have been used so that the obtained results are converged truly. In the most cases, the length and width have been assumed as 5 nm.

The main simulation structure was as below:

First, the configurations are made by a handwritten program, and favorite structures are stabled based on various studies presented different allotropes of borophene. Then, an isothermal-isobaric (npt) ensemble in all direction (x, y, z) was applied. From the first step, stresses and strains were computed and printed. After equilibration, the previous ensemble removed and a deform strategy has been implemented that changes the volume of the simulation box during a dynamics run. Various strain rates have been used and it was observed that strain rate affect the mechanical properties of borophene sheets similar to what was seen in the case of graphene [45, 46]. However, for more brevity, all presented results are only based on 0.0001 (1/ps) for strain rate. Mentioned deform strategy has performed under a

new isothermal-isobaric (npt) ensemble, in the width direction (perpendicular to the elongation (deform)) of course. The strain has been calculated as

$$
\varepsilon=\delta\left(T-T_{e q}\right) \Upsilon \tag{3}
$$

Where $\delta$ is time step, T is the step that we want to calculate the strain, $T_{e q}$ is the number of steps that equilibration ensemble has been done and $\Upsilon$ is the strain rate.
The stress at each time step has calculated as the summation of stress of all atoms divided by the volume of simulated sheet.

### 3.3. Rupture mechanism
Figures 3-15 depict deformation of defect free state of various allotropes of borophene. Both armchair and zigzag edges are simulated and illustrated in these figures. The dimensions are scaled based on the real sizes, and all sheets are scaled in the figure so that all horizontal lengths are equal.

#### i) A comparison with graphene and h-BN
Figure 3 provides deformation of S1 configuration when the load is applied along zigzag and armchair edges. A common feature is that the failure occurred with the manifestation of some holes, the occurrence that cannot be seen in a tensile test of graphene (figure 4) or boron nitride (figure 5) sheets.

![](./images/813109817462751234_4.jpg)

![](./images/813109817462751234_5.jpg)

Figure 3. Deformation of defect free state of S1 configuration

Figures 4 and 5 depict the deformation of graphene and hexagonal boron nitride sheets along the elongation test. As the length of sheets grows simultaneously with elongation, the length of honey-combs increases and the height decreases. At a certain time, which is relative to a certain strain, the sheet surrenders. Figures show that the shape of rupturing is completely different from what was seen for borophene sheet. Because of more complementary flexibility (i.e. a flexibility without any defect formation on the sheet), graphene and h-BN sheets rupture later than borophene sheet.

![](./images/813109817462751234_6.jpg)

![](./images/813109817462751234_7.jpg)

Figure 4. Deformation of defect free state of graphene sheet

![](./images/813109817462751234_8.jpg)

Figure 5. Deformation of defect free state of boron nitride sheet

On the other hand, the yield strain of borophene sheets occurs sooner, but they remain in competition with an external load more than graphene and h-BN and then an ultimate flexibility of borophene sheets could be referred. For both graphene and h-BN sheets, two sides of the sheet were separated clearly. This occurance was not observed in borophene sheets even in sheets with considerable channels, such as S2, S3, S4 and S8 configurations.

These observations suggest that borophene may remain more stable through external loads because of the creation of some nanopores and also local cracking instead of rupturing in graphene and h-BN sheets. To gather further insights in these directions, in the following sections we focus on other allotropes of boron, which have different pores or channels in their initial stable structures.

Other configurations result in a regular nanopore for zigzag-types and cracking for armchair-types.

## 4. Conclusion

In this paper, mechanical properties of different types of borophene sheet were studied to have provided insights into the molecular scale mechanism of deformation of this 2D material.

In conclusion, in comparison to the graphene and h-BN nano sheets;

1.  A common feature is that in borophene sheets the failure occurred with the manifestation of some holes, the occurrence that cannot be seen in graphene or h-BN sheets.

2.  Because of more complementary flexibility (i.e. a flexibility without any defect formation on the sheet), graphene and h-BN sheets rupture suddenly and albeit later than borophene sheet.

3.  The yield strain of borophene sheets occurs sooner, but they remain in competition with an external load more than graphene and h-BN and then an ultimate flexibility of borophene sheets could be referred.

4.  For both graphene and h-BN sheets, two sides of the sheet were separated clearly. This feature was not observed in borophene sheets even in sheets with considerable channels, such as S2, S3, S4 and S8 configurations.

5.  The observations suggest that borophene may remain more stable through external loads because of the creation of some nanopores and also local cracking instead of rupturing in graphene and h-BN sheets.

Performed simulations for different types of boron allotropes concludes that;

1.  With respect to the armchair type, when loading applied along the zigzag direction, yielding was delayed.

2.  As a significant perspective, we can categorize the rupture mechanism of borophene sheets into two groups; cracking and local pore creation.

3.  In armchair-types, except for S1 and S4, cracking is the most observable deformation shape.

4.  Only zigzag-type of S4 and S8 have cracked finally.

5.  Cracking leads to more strain along the load direction, whereas local pore creation spends the imposed energy for breaking the internal bonds and so flowing the external energy into the various bonds into the sheet cause increasing the number of pores.

6.  For the armchair-types, the cracking is a dominant mechanism where for the zigzag-type the common mechanism is the creation of nanopores.

7.  Because of considerable strength of S1 configuration, neither the armchair-type nor the zigzag-type does not crack.

8.  For S8 configuration, due to some strong regions confined by a narrow channel, both types lead to cracking.

9.  Other configurations result in a regular nanopore for the zigzag-types and cracking for the armchair-types.

These impressive results may help to design a new class of borophene based semiconductors that remain stable even when are sustaining uncontrollable external stresses.

## Highlights

-   A detailed discussion on the rupture of different allotropes of boron was presented

-   For the zigzag-type the common mechanism is the creation of nanopores.
