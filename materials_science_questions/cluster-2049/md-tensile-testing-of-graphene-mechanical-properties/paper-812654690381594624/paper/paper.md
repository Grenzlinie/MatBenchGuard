Section 3. Calculation in Föppl-von Kármán Model :

From data in AFM and Fig. S7:

Average height of graphene covered bacteria before vacuum: $H = 0.279\ \mu\text{m}$

Average height of graphene covered bacteria after vacuum: $h = 0.186\ \mu\text{m}$

Average weight of wrinkles region $D_C = 1.1\ \mu\text{m}$

$$
{R_C}^2 = (R_C - D_C)^2 + \left(\frac{D_C}{2}\right)^2
$$

$$
R_C = 0.682\ \mu\text{m}
$$

$$
\text{Arcsine}\ \left(\frac{D_C}{2R}\right) = 0.938\ R
$$

Length of curve before vacuum: $L_C = 2 \times 0.938 \times 0.682 = 1.513\ \mu\text{m}$

Average width of wrinkles region $D_W = 0.628\ \mu\text{m}$

Pythagorean Theorem: ${R_W}^2 = (R_W - D_W)^2 + \left(\frac{D_W}{2}\right)^2$

$$
R_W = 0.358\ \mu\text{m}
$$

$$
\text{Arcsine}\ \left(\frac{D_W}{2R}\right) = 1.069\ R
$$

Length of curve after vacuum: $L_W = 2 \times 1.069 \times 0.358 = 0.765\ \mu\text{m}$

The compressive strain in the graphene film along the relaxed direction: $\varepsilon_0$

and $\varepsilon_g = (L_C - L_W)/L_C$, as $L_C = 1.513\ \mu\text{m}\quad and\quad L_W = 0.765\ \mu\text{m}$

So: $\varepsilon_g = 0.494$

The strain of the substrate: $\varepsilon$

$$
\varepsilon_g = \varepsilon/(\varepsilon + 1)
$$

$$
\varepsilon = \frac{\varepsilon_g}{1-\varepsilon_g} = 0.977
$$

$\lambda$ is the stretch of bacteria substrate after vacuum compare to before vacuum

So : $\varepsilon = \lambda - 1$

$$
\text{Prestretch factor:}S = \Lambda = \frac{1}{2}\ \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1 + \lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\ (1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1 + (1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right) = 2.657
$$

$$
E_g = 1TPa, v_g = 0.165
$$

Plane-strain modulus: $\bar{E} = E/(1 - v^2)$

We approximated the semi-rigid cell wall with Poisson's ratio value of 0.25. As the volume of the bacterial cell reduces, so does the surface area and the transverse diameter of graphene-on-bacteria.

Plane-strain modulus of the graphene film: $$\bar{E}_g = \frac{E_g}{1-v_g^2} = \frac{10^{12}}{1-0.165^2} = 1.028 \times 10^{12}$$

t is the thickness of the graphene: t = 0.355 nm

We have the wavelength modulus related equation: $$\lambda = 2\pi t(\frac{\bar{E}_g}{3S\bar{E}_B})^{1/3}$$

$$E_B = \bar{E}_B \times (1-{v_B}^2) = \left(\frac{\lambda}{2\pi t}\right)^{-3} \times \frac{\bar{E}_g}{3S}$$

Since the wavelength we get form FESEM and AFM is 32.4 and 34.3 nm respectively. The range of $E_B$ is 35.74 t0 42.40Mpa.

Average length of bacteria: L = 2.478 $\mu$m

Effective wavelength of wrinkles: $$\bar{L} = \frac{L}{3} = 0.826 \mu\text{m}$$

We have the wavelength modulus related equation: $$A = \frac{\bar{L}t}{\lambda}\left(\frac{8v}{3(1-v)^2}\right)^{1/2}$$

So we have: $$A = \frac{\bar{L}t}{\lambda}\left(\frac{8v}{3(1-v)^2}\right)^{1/2} = 7.279 \text{ nm} \cong 7.3 \text{ nm}$$

The linearization of pre-buckling state in classical buckling analysis showed:
$$D\nabla^4w + \sigma_0t\nabla^2w = -p$$
D is the bending stiffness of the plate with $D = Et^3/[12(1-v^2)]$, $\nabla^4$ is the bi-harmonic operator, and $\sigma_0$ is the compressive equi-biaxial pre-stress.

The periodic solution of this linearization and the exact solution for the normal deflection of the surface in classical buckling model based on von Karman plate equations gives the following periodic relationship:
The maximum perpendicular stress: $w = 2p/(\bar{E}_S k)$ (in magnitude) and k is wave number and is equal to $2\pi/\lambda$.

At $w \approx A$

$$
p_{max} \sim \frac{w_{max} \bar{E}_{S}}{2 t\left(\frac{\bar{E}_{g}}{3 S \bar{E}_{S}}\right)^{1 / 3}}=\frac{10 × 39 × 10^{6}}{2 × 0.355 ×\left(1-0.25^{2}\right) × 2.657 ×\left(\frac{1.028 × 10^{12}}{3 × 4.160 × 10^{7}}\right)^{1 / 3}}=40.18 \mathrm{Mpa}
$$

Where $w$ is the perpendicular displacement of the graphene, $P$ is the stress component by the bacterial cell wall acting perpendicular on graphene.

The young's modulus of cell membrane was estimated on the order of 1000 Pa. So the range of wavelength of wrinkles formatted on bacterial cell wall is 2738.08 nm (at 10000 Pa) to 5903.2 nm (at 1000 Pa).

$$
\left(\lambda=2 \pi t\left(\frac{\bar{E}_{B}}{3 S \bar{E}_{C e l l ~ m e m b r a n e}}\right)^{1 / 3}\right)
$$

The wavelength on raisin: $\lambda=2 \pi t\left(\frac{\bar{E}_{g}}{3 \bar{E}_{B}}\right)^{1 / 3}=2 \pi × 0.1 m m ×\left(\frac{30}{3}\right)^{1 / 3}=1.354 \mathrm{~mm}$

Section 4. Calculation of Strain in graphene from 2D peak shifts in Raman Spectroscopy

The Raman 2D frequency shift can be related to the strain in graphene sheet by:

$$
\frac{\Delta \omega}{\omega_{0}}=\gamma \cdot\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{p}}\right) \tag{1a}
$$

Where $\gamma$ =1.24 is the Güneisen parameter, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}$ is the axial strain, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{p}}$ is the relative strain in the perpendicular direction from the Poisson's ratio of graphene $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{p}}$ = -0.186 $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}$, $\omega_{0}$ is the Raman band frequency and $\Delta \omega$ the shift. Therefore, we obtain:

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}=\Delta \omega /\left[-\omega_{\mathrm{o}} \cdot \gamma \cdot(1-0.186)\right] \tag{1b}
$$

Before annealing, graphene exhibits a strong 2D-peak feature centered around $2675 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$, G-peak at $1578 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$; while graphene on bacterium exhibits redshifted 2D peak $(2665 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1})$ and G peak $(1566 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1})$.

The distributions of potential energy and strain are uniform in graphene except the region near two ends of bacteria. In the region near two ends of bacteria, there exist some wrinkles in graphene in our CGMD simulations, this is because there is no solution in our simulations and it should be without wrinkles when wrapping the bacteria in the solution as discussed in Ref. 7.

However, when we add the substrate, the distributions of potential energy and strain are not uniform and may induce a nucleated crack to form in graphene. As a word, wrapping the bacteria completely in graphene won't produce a stress field that induce a nucleated crack to form.

Crack nucleation

To determine location of crack nucleation under interior gas pressure, we construct the graphene-cell-SiO₂ system shown in Fig. 4(a) and equilibrate the system using molecular dynamics simulations. From the equilibrium configuration, we obtain the relation between stress and curvature: $\sigma_1/R_1 + \sigma_2/R_2 = p/t$, where $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ are the meridional and circumferential stress, p and t are the interior gas pressure and thickness of graphene, $1/R_1$ and $1/R_2$ are the meridional and circumferential curvature of this point. p, t, $R_1$ and $\sigma_1$ are known qualities with considering the equilibrium configuration of the system, Therefore, we have $\sigma_2 = (p/t - \sigma_1/R_1)R_2 \sim R_2$. From the above relation, we can know that the location where curvature of height profile is zero bear the maximum stress and will induce a crack.
