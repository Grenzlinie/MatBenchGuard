# Modes of Vibrations Due to Single Ion Substitutional Impurities in Alkali Halides

By
R. K. GUPTA and A. K. SINGH

Modes of vibrations due to single ion impurities in alkali halides are studied by Green's function technique. The Green functions computed on the basis of shell model parameters fitted to the neutron data are used in the computation. The modes of vibrations are computed by considering the vibrations of the defect and its six nearest neighbour ions, having $O_{\mathrm{h}}$ symmetry. Using group theory symmetry coordinates are constructed for the seven-atom defect space and analytical ex- pressions are derived for various modes of vibrations, in terms of Green's functions of the perfect lattice and the perturbation matrix as a result of the defect. Mass changes at the defect site as well as the changes in the short-range interaction due to the presence of the defect are taken into account. The change $\Delta A$ in the short range interaction parameter $A$ is fitted to the observed single ion frequencies in various alkali halides. A considerable softening of the nearest neighbour short- range interaction varying from $8\%$ in KBr:Cl to $64\%$ in KI:Na is found.

Die Schwingungsmoden einer isolierten Ionenstörstelle in Alkalihalogeniden werden mit der Technik Greenscher Funktionen untersucht. Bei den Rechnungen werden die auf der Grundlage von an Neutronenwerte angepaßten Schalenmodellparameter ermittelten Greenschen Funktionen benutzt. Die Schwingungsmoden werden unter Berücksichtigung des Defekts und seiner sechs nächsten Nachbarionen mit $O_{\mathrm{h}}$-Symmetrie berechnet. Mit der Gruppentheorie werden symmetrische Ko- ordinaten für den Defektraum der sieben Atome konstruiert, und analytische Ausdrücke werden für eine Reihe von Schwingungsmoden mit Greenschen Funktionen des perfekten Gitters und der Störungsmatrix als Ergebnis des Defekts abgeleitet. Dabei werden sowohl Massenänderungen am Defektplatz als auch Änderungen der kurzreichweitigen Wechselwirkung infolge des Defekts berücksichtigt. Die Änderung $\Delta A$ des kurzreichweitigen Wechselwirkungsparameters $A$ wird an die beobachteten Ein-Ionenfrequenzen in verschiedenen Alkalihalogeniden angepaßt. Es wird eine beträchtliche „Erweichung“ der kurzreichweitigen Wechselwirkung der nächsten Nachbarn von etwa $8\%$ in KBr:Cl auf $64\%$ in KJ:Na gefunden.

## 1. Introduction

A small concentration of the substitutional impurities in an otherwise perfect crystal may give rise to various new vibrational modes (local, gap, or resonance). A wide variety of experimental techniques, viz. the impurity induced infrared absorption, Raman spectroscopy, specific heat measurements, Mössbauer effect, neutron scatter- ing, tunneling between the superconductors etc. have been used to study these modes. The most thoroughly studied experimental method for investigating the dynamical properties of the imperfect crystals is the impurity induced infrared absorption. A second powerful tool that has been attracting much interest these days is the Raman spectroscopy.

There are a number of experimental studies which have shown the occurrence of these new vibrational modes. The first evidence of the appearance of the defect mode was furnished by Schaefer [1], later due to $\mathrm{H}^{-}$ions in alkaline earth fluorides [2] and boron and carbon [3,4] in silicon were studied. More recently local modes due to a number of substitutional impurities in II-VI and III-V semiconductors have

been observed. Resonance absorption bands were discovered in the far infrared in silver doped alkali halides by Sievers [5] and by Weber [6]. Detailed references are given in Maradudin [7], Klein [8] and Newman [9].

Experimental investigations of local modes due to U-centres in mixed alkali halides have been carried out by Mirlin and Reshina [10] and by Barth and Fritz [11]. Very recently much attention has been drawn to the study of defect modes due to pairing of impurities in alkali halides. Resonance modes due to $\text{Ag}^+$ ions pairs in NaCl [12], $\text{Na}^+$ ions pairs in KCl [13], $\text{F}^-$ ions pairs in NaCl [14], U-centre pairs in KCl, KBr, NaCl, and RbCl [15 to 17], gap modes in KI [18] are the various examples, where the defect modes due to pair impurities have been observed thus making the interactions between the impurity ions important. The theoretical investigations of these modes have been carried out by a few workers [19 to 24], either by Green function techniques or the molecular model method. In all these calculations, one has to fit the defect mode due to a single ion impurity to the experimental single ion resonance or localized modes, and thus, the change in the short-range interaction due to the presence of the defect is estimated.

In this paper we have carried out a systematic and detailed investigation of the defect modes due to single ion impurities in alkali halides by Green function techniques. The Green functions computed on the basis of shell-model parameters fitted to neutron data have been used in the computation. Using group theory, symmetry co-ordinates have been constructed, and analytical expressions derived for the various modes corresponding to the vibrations of the defect atom and its six nearest neighbours having $\text{O}_\text{h}$ symmetry, in terms of the Green functions of the perfect lattice and the perturbation matrix. Mass changes at the defect sites as well as the changes in the nearest neighbour short-range interaction due to the presence of the defect have been included in the perturbation matrix in the computation. The weakening in the short-range interaction has been adjusted to give a fit to the experimentally observed single ion frequencies. It is hoped, our results will find use in the experimental and theoret- ical investigations of the vibrational modes due to pair and more complex impurities in alkali halides and the technique can be extended to other ionic crystals. With this idea this investigation has been done.

### 2. Basic Theory

The details of the dynamical theory of crystals containing substitutional impurities is discussed at various places [7, 25, 26]. We will not go into the details here. It will however be appropriate to discuss the theory in brief for the sake of clarity of the formulation and the various symbols used in our computation. It is well known that in the harmonic approximation the equation of motion of a perfect crystal, consisting of $N$ unit cells each of which contains $r$ atoms, can be written as
$$
LU=0, \tag{1}
$$
where $L$ is a $3rN \times 3rN$ matrix whose elements are
$$
L_{\alpha\beta}(lk, l'k')=M_{k}\omega^{2}\delta_{ll'}\delta_{kk'}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(0)(lk, l'k') \tag{2}
$$
and $U$ is a $3rN$ element column vector whose components are the atomic displacements $U_{\alpha}(lk)$ and $U_{\alpha}(lk)$ denotes the $\alpha$-Cartesian component of the displacement of the $k$-th atom in the $l$-th unit cell of the crystal. If we assume that one of the lattice sites of the crystal is occupied by a substitutional impurity atom whose mass and interactions with its neighbours in the crystal differ from those of the atom it replaces, the corresponding equation for the perturbed crystal can be written as
$$
(L-\delta L)U=0. \tag{3}
$$

The elements of the matrix $\delta L$ are given by
$$\delta L_{\alpha \beta}\left(l k, l^{\prime} k^{\prime}\right)=\left(M_{k}-M_{l k}\right) \omega^{2} \delta_{l l^{\prime}} \delta_{k k^{\prime}} \delta_{l \beta}-\left[\varphi_{\alpha \beta}^{(0)}\left(l k, l^{\prime} k^{\prime}\right)-\varphi_{\alpha \beta}\left(l k, l^{\prime} k^{\prime}\right)\right]. \quad(4)$$

If the impurity atom interacts directly with $z$ atoms in the crystal, the matrix $\delta L$ in the most general case has $3 n \times 3 n$ nonzero elements, where $n=z+1$ is the number of lattice sites of the crystal directly influenced by the presence of the impurity atom including the impurity site itself. If the impurity is well localized, $n$ is a small number. Denoting by $G$ the perfect lattice Green function $L^{-1}$, (3) can be written as
$$G \delta L U=U.\qquad(5)$$

If we are interested in the study of the modes due to the impurity atom and a small number of atoms surrounding it, $G, \delta L$, and $U$ can be partitioned in terms of the defect space and the rest of the crystal. The localized vibrations are then obtained from the solution of the determinantal equation
$$|I-G J|=0, \quad(6)$$
where the $G$'s are the perfect lattice Green function matrices and the $J$'s are the perturbation matrices corresponding to the defect space. An explicit expression for the elements of the Green function matrix $G$ is given by
$$\begin{aligned}
G_{\alpha \beta}\left(l k ; l^{\prime} k^{\prime} ; \omega^{2}\right) & =\frac{1}{N\left(M_{k} M_{k^{\prime}}\right)^{1 / 2}} \sum_{\boldsymbol{K} j} \frac{e_{\alpha}(k \mid \boldsymbol{K} j) e_{\beta}^{*}\left(k^{\prime} \mid \boldsymbol{K} j\right)}{\omega^{2}-\omega_{j}^{2}(\boldsymbol{K})} \times \\
& \times \exp \left[i \boldsymbol{K} \cdot\left(X(l k)-X\left(l^{\prime} k^{\prime}\right)\right)\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad(7)$$

Here $\omega_{j}(K)$ is the frequency of the normal mode of the crystal described by the wave vector $K$ and the branch index $j$ and $e_{\alpha}(k \mid K j)$ is the $\alpha$-Cartesian component of the associated unit polarization vector.

### 3. Method of Calculation and Results

As mentioned in Section 2, the vibrational mode frequencies are obtained from the solution of the determinantal equation (6). We are interested in the study of single ion impurity frequencies. We consider the defect and its nearest neighbours as our defect space. The defect space consists of seven atoms, i.e. the defect atom at the origin $(0,0,0)$, six nearest neighbours at $(a / 2,0,0),(0, a / 2,0),(0,0, a / 2),(-a / 2,0,0)$, $(0,-a / 2,0)$, and $(0,0,-a / 2), a$ being the lattice constant. The Green function $G$ and the perturbation matrix $J$ are $21 \times 21$ matrices. The $21 \times 21$ Green function matrix for our $\mathrm{XY}_{6}$ defect space has the following 14 independent functions:
$$\left.\begin{array}{rl}
g_{0}=G_{x x}\left(000,-; 000,-; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{1}=G_{x x}\left(000,+; 000,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{2}=G_{x x}\left(000,-; 100,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{3}=G_{y y}\left(000,-; 100,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{4}=G_{x x}\left(000,+; 110,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{5}=G_{y y}\left(000,+; 110,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{6}=G_{z z}\left(000,+; 110,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{7}=G_{x x}\left(000,+; 200,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{8}=G_{y y}\left(000,+; 200,+; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{9}=G_{x x}\left(000,-; 110,-; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{10}=G_{y y}\left(000,-; 110,-; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{11}=G_{z z}\left(000,-; 110,-; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{12}=G_{x x}\left(000,-; 200,-; \omega^{2}\right), \\
g_{13}=G_{y y}\left(000,-; 200,-; \omega^{2}\right),
\end{array}\right\}\qquad(8)$$

The perturbation matrix $J$ contains the mass changes as a result of the defect at one of the lattice sites and the force constant changes between the defect and its neigh- bours. We consider the changes of the force constants between nearest neighbours only. We further consider the force constant change for the short-range overlap interaction only. The short-range interaction can be expressed in terms of the Keller- mann's coefficients [27] $A$ and $B$. We have neglected $\Delta B$ in comparison to $\Delta A$ in our derivations. These changes are denoted by
$$\left.egin{array}{c}
\Delta A=A \text { parameter for the defect-host bond } \
\quad-A \text { parameter for the perfect lattice }, \
\Delta B=B \text { parameter for the defect-host bond } \
\quad-B \text { parameter for the perfect lattice }.
\end{array}\right\}\qquad(9)$$

As shown in Fig. 1, our defect space has $O_{h}$ symmetry. The 21-dimensional represen tation can be shown to reduce to the following irreducible representations:
$$\Gamma_{21}=\mathrm{A}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}+\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{g}}+\mathrm{F}_{1 \mathrm{~g}}+\mathrm{F}_{2 \mathrm{~g}}+3 \mathrm{~F}_{1 \mathrm{u}}+\mathrm{F}_{2 \mathrm{u}}.\qquad(10)$$

Using standard character table for the $O_{h}$ group, the symmetry coordinates were constructed. The normalized symmetry coordinates for the $XY_{6}$ complex are displayed in Table 1. Using these symmetry coordinates the $21 \times 21,|I-G J|$ matrix was block diagonalized into various irreducible representations. We obtained the following condition for various modes of vibrations.

$\mathbf{A_{1g}}$ mode
$$1-\Delta A\left(g_{1}-g_{7}-4 g_{5}\right)=0, \quad(11 \mathrm{a})$$

$\mathbf{E_{g}}$ mode
$$1-\Delta A\left(g_{1}-g_{7}+2 g_{5}\right)=0, \quad(11 \mathrm{~b})$$

$\mathbf{F_{1g}}$ mode
$$1-\Delta B\left(g_{1}-g_{8}+2 g_{5}\right)=0, \quad(11 \mathrm{c})$$

$\mathbf{F_{2g}}$ mode
$$1-\Delta B\left(g_{1}-g_{8}-2 g_{5}\right)=0, \quad(11 \mathrm{~d})$$

$\mathbf{F_{1u}}$ mode
$$1-\varepsilon M \omega^{2} g_{0}-\Delta A\left[2 g_{0}+g_{1}-4 g_{2}+g_{7}\right)-\varepsilon M \omega^{2}\left(g_{1} g_{0}+g_{0} g_{7}-2 g_{2}^{2}\right)]=0, \quad(11 \mathrm{e})$$

$\mathbf{F_{2u}}$ mode
$$1-\Delta B\left(g_{1}-2 g_{6}+g_{8}\right)=0.\qquad(11f)$$

$\text{F}_{1\text{u}}$ mode
$$
\begin{aligned}
& 1-\varepsilon M \omega^{2} g_{1}-\Delta A\left[\left(2 g_{1}+g_{0}-4 g_{2}+g_{12}\right)-\right. \\
& \left.-\varepsilon M \omega^{2}\left(g_{0} g_{1}+g_{1} g_{12}-2 g_{2}^{2}\right)\right]=0,
\end{aligned} \tag{12e}
$$

$\text{F}_{2\text{u}}$ mode
$$
1-\Delta B\left(g_{0}-2 g_{11}+g_{13}\right)=0, \tag{12f}
$$

$$
\varepsilon M=M_{\text {host }}-M_{\text {defect }}.
$$

It is clear from the above equations that the frequency of the defect mode can be computed if the change $\Delta A$ in the force constant parameter $A$ is known and if on the other hand, the frequency of the defect mode is known, the value of $\Delta A$ can be computed. We have computed the values of $\Delta A$ fitting the observed single ion defect frequencies in various alkali halides using equations (11e) and (12e). This requires the knowledge of the various Green functions. The Green functions computed by using neutron fitted shell model have been used in our calculations.

The computed changes $\Delta A$ in $A$ parameters along with the $A$ parameters are displayed in Table 2. $\Delta A$ and $A$ are given in units of $e^{2} / 2 V$ of the given crystal; $e$ is the electronic charge and $V$ the volume of the unit cell. It is seen from Table 2 that the presence of the defect softens the nearest neighbour short-range force constant quite considerably. This weakening of the force constant varies from $8 \%$ in $\mathrm{KBr}: \mathrm{Cl}$ to $64 \%$ in $\mathrm{KI}: \mathrm{Na}$.

A systematic and detailed investigation of modes of vibrations due to single ion impurity in various alkali halide crystals has been presented. It is hoped, our results will find application in the study of single ion impurities in these crystals. Also equations (11) and (12) are quite general and can be used to study the resonance modes due to single ion impurities, specific heat measurements having the same symmetry, also in vibrational modes due to pair and more complex impurities in the crystals, the change in the short-range force constant due to a single impurity being one of the important factors in all these problems.
