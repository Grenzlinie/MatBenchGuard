# Molecular model for U centres in caesium iodide

N. KRISHNAMURTHY

Department of Physics, Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, India

Communicated by R. S. Krishnan; MS. received 22nd February 1966

Abstract. A molecular model has been developed to study the vibrations of U centres in caesium iodide. Employing the rigid ion model with nearest-neighbour short-range forces, the dynamical matrix of order $27 \times 27$ was solved to obtain the frequencies of the localized modes and the perturbed lattice modes. The results are compared with those obtained from the Green function method.

In a recent letter (Krishnamurthy and Haridasan 1966) the nature of the localized modes in the caesium iodide lattice was presented using the Green function method. Recently an elegant molecular model has been put forward by Jaswal (1965). The molecular model takes into account only the motion of the impurity and its nearest neighbours while the rest of the lattice is assumed to be at rest. This model lends support to the fact that in the local mode the lighter $H^{-}$or $D^{-}$centres alone oscillate in the potential well set up by the static lattice (Elliott *et al.* 1966) and also give us an idea of the weakening of the short-range forces between the defect and its nearest neighbours. Hence the theory of the molecular model for CsCl type structures has been developed and the results of our calculations for CsI are presented in this paper.

For a CsCl type structure each atom has as its nearest neighbours eight atoms of the other type at the corners of the cube. We label the central atom replaced by the impurity $0-r_{0}(000)$ and the eight neighbours $1-r_{0}(111), 2-r_{0}(\overline{1} \overline{1} \overline{1}), 3-r_{0}(\overline{1} 11), 4-r_{0}(1 \overline{1} \overline{1})$, $5-r_{0}(1 \overline{1} 1), 6-r_{0}(\overline{1} 1 \overline{1}), 7-r_{0}(11 \overline{1})$ and $8-r_{0}(\overline{1} \overline{1} 1)$, where $r_{0}$ is half the lattice constant and the first number denotes the value of $l$, the particle index. The dynamical matrix for this nine-atomic molecular problem can be written as

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}\left(l l^{\prime}\right)=\left(M_{l} M_{l^{\prime}}\right)^{-1 / 2}\left\{\frac{\partial^{2} \Phi}{\partial u_{\alpha}(l) \partial u_{\alpha^{\prime}}\left(l^{\prime}\right)}\right\}_{0} \tag{1}
$$

where $M_{l}$ is the mass of the $l$ th particle and $u_{\alpha}(l)$ is the $\alpha$ th component of the displacement of $l$.

The potential energy of interaction

$$
\Phi=\sum \frac{e(l) e\left(l^{\prime}\right)}{\left|r\left(l l^{\prime}\right)\right|}+V\left(\left|r\left(l l^{\prime}\right)\right|\right) \tag{2}
$$

takes into account the Coulomb interactions between the particles $l$ and $l^{\prime}$, having charges $e(l)$ and $e\left(l^{\prime}\right)$ and separated by $r\left(l l^{\prime}\right)$, and also the short-range interactions represented by $V(\left|r\left(l l^{\prime}\right)\right|)$. The short-range interactions for the first neighbours alone are completely specified by

$$
V^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}\right)_{0}=\frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}^{3}} \sqrt{ } 3 r_{0} B \tag{3}
$$


$$
V^{\prime \prime}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2} V}{\partial r^{2}}\right)_{0}=\frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}} A
\tag{4}
$$

for the host lattice and

$$
V_{\imath}{ }^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\partial V_{\imath}}{\partial r}\right)_{0}=\frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}} \sqrt{ } 3 r_{0} B^{\prime}
\tag{5}
$$

$$
V_{\imath}^{\prime \prime}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2} V_{\imath}}{\partial r^{2}}\right)_{0}=\frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}} A^{\prime}
\tag{6}
$$

for the defect lattice. $A$ and $B$ are easily obtained from the equilibrium condition and the compressibility data, from

$$
A=3 r_{0} \frac{C_{11}+2 C_{12}}{3} \frac{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}}{e^{2}}+\frac{2 \alpha}{3 \sqrt{ } 3}
\tag{7}
$$

and

$$
B=-\frac{\alpha}{3 \sqrt{ } 3}
\tag{8}
$$

where $\alpha=1.7627$ is the Madelung constant and $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ are the elastic constants.

The Coulomb contributions to the dynamical matrix elements $D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}(l l^{\prime})$ can be calculated for the following two cases: (i) $l=l^{\prime}$ and (ii) $l \neq l^{\prime}$.

For $l=l^{\prime}$

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha}{ }^{\prime \mathrm{C}}\left(l l^{\prime}\right)=M_{l}^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\left\{\sum\left[e(1) e(2) /\left|r_{12}\right|\right]\right\}}{\partial u_{\alpha}(l) \partial u_{\alpha^{\prime}}(l)}\right]_{0}
\tag{9}
$$

is zero owing to the symmetry of the crystal, as the summation involves the interaction of the ion $l$ with the rest of the crystal.

For $l \neq l^{\prime}$

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}{ }^{\mathrm{C}}\left(l l^{\prime}\right)=-\left(M_{l} M_{l^{\prime}}\right)^{-1 / 2} \frac{e(l) e\left(l^{\prime}\right)}{r_{0}{ }^{3}\left(l l^{\prime}\right)}\left\{\frac{r_{0 \alpha}\left(l l^{\prime}\right) r_{0 \alpha^{\prime}}\left(l l^{\prime}\right)}{r_{0}{ }^{2}\left(l l^{\prime}\right)}-\delta_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}\right\}
\tag{10}
$$

where $r_{0 \alpha}(l l^{\prime})$ is the $\alpha$ th component of the vector separating $l$ and $l^{\prime}$ in the equilibrium configuration.

To evaluate the corresponding quantities for short-range interaction, we assume $B^{\prime}=B$ for no relaxation around the defect. Then, for $l=l^{\prime}$, we obtain

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(00)=M_{0}{ }^{-1} \frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}} \frac{8}{3}\left(A^{\prime}+2 B\right)
$$

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(00)=0
$$

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(l l)=M_{l}^{-1} \frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}} \frac{1}{3}\left(A^{\prime}+7 A+16 B\right), \quad l=1 \text { to } 8
$$

$$
M_{l}^{-1 / 2} D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(l l)=-M_{0}{ }^{-1 / 2} D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(0 l).
$$

For $l \neq l^{\prime}$

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(0 l)=-\left(M_{0} M_{l}\right)^{-1 / 2}\left(\left(A^{\prime}-B\right) \frac{r_{\alpha}(0 l) r_{\alpha^{\prime}}(0 l)}{|r(0 l)|^{2}}+B \delta_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}\right) \frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}}
$$

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}\left(l l^{\prime}=0\right)
$$

unless at least $l$ or $l' = 0$ and, for example,

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(0 l)=-\left(M_{0} M_{l}\right)^{-1 / 2} \frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}} \frac{A^{\prime}+2 B}{3}
$$

$$
D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}{ }^{\mathrm{R}}(0 l)=-\left(M_{0} M_{l}\right)^{-1 / 2} \frac{e^{2}}{8 r_{0}{ }^{3}} \frac{A^{\prime}-B}{3} \text { for } l=1. \tag{11}
$$

By adding the Coulomb and short-range parts, we obtain a $27 \times 27$ real symmetric matrix, the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of which satisfy

$$
\omega_{i}{ }^{2} W_{\alpha}(l)=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime} l^{\prime}} D_{\alpha \alpha^{\prime}}\left(l l^{\prime}\right) W_{\alpha^{\prime}}\left(l^{\prime}\right) \tag{12}
$$

where the amplitude of an ion in a mode is given by

$$
u_{\alpha}(l)=\frac{W_{\alpha}(l)}{\sqrt{ } M_{l}}. \tag{13}
$$

The value of $A$ was obtained from $r_{0}=2 \cdot 282 \AA$ and the compressibility $k=1.441 \times 10^{11}$ dyn cm $^{-2}$ (Vallin *et al.* 1964) as 4.7452. With $\mathrm{H}^{-}$and $\mathrm{D}^{-}$as impurities in CsI, the eigenvalues and eigenvectors for a series of values of $A^{\prime}$ starting from $A^{\prime}=A$ were obtained on an NE803 computer. The largest root was found to be triply degen- erate, thereby representing the local mode, while the rest of the roots were below the maximum frequency of the CsI lattice. The amplitude of the impurity in these modes was practically zero or negligible compared with its neighbours, while in the local mode the relative amplitude of the nearest neighbours to that of the defect was very small

Our earlier calculations using the Green functions and the moments of the frequency spectra gave $\omega_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{D}}$ as 15.83 and $11 \cdot 19$, while the present model gives these as $12.5 \times 10^{13} \mathrm{sec}^{-1}$ and $8.8 \times 10^{13} \mathrm{sec}^{-1}$.

The larger values of the earlier results may be due to the poor convergence of the dimen- sionless moments employed in the calculations.
