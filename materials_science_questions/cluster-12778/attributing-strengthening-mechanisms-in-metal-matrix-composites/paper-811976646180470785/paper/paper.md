# Microstructures and Mechanical Properties of TiC Particle Reinforced TiAl Composites by Spark Plasma Sintering

YUE Yunlong,WU Haitao,WU Bo,WANG Zhijie,YIN Haiyan, SU Tong
(School of Materials Science and Engineering, Jinan University, Jinan 250022, China)

**Abstract:** Using spark plasma sintering(SPS) technique, TiC particle reinforced $\gamma$-TiAl composites were prepared with varying weight fraction of TiC powders. The effects of the TiC fractions and distributions on the properties of the composites were investigated. The composite containing 7wt% TiC had the optimum three-point bending strength of 842 MPa,which was 200 MPa greater than that of the unreinforced $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic. The degradation of the bending strength occurred in the composites containing more than 7wt% TiC and this was believed to be attributed to agglomerated particles of TiC, which acted as crack initiation and propagation sites. The increase of strength in TiC reinforced IMCs came from the grain refinement and the interaction of dislocations with the reinforcing particles. The bending strength of the IMC containing 7wt% TiC was theoretically estimated to increase by 85 MPa and 200 MPa, respectively, by the grain refinement and dislocation strengthening, the total of which was almost in accordance with the improvement in that of the unreinforced $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic when considering normal estimation errors.

**Key words:** TiC / TiAl composites; mechanical properties; grain refinement; dislocation strengthening; SPS sintering technique

## 1 Introduction

The intermetallic compound TiAl has been investigated as a future candidate for potential use in an elevated temperature material. Some of the positive characteristics of this compound are its oxidation resistance, low density of about 3.8 g/cm³ and high melting point of about 1773 K⁽¹·²⁾. However, its use has been limited because of its poor ductility and fracture toughness at room temperature and poor elevated temperature strength. To improve these properties, recently this compound has been made as a composite material containing a second phase such as boride, carbide, nitride, or oxide. For instance, TiB₂/TiAl composite⁽³·⁴⁾, SiC/TiAl composite⁽⁵⁾, Al₂O₃/TiAl composite⁽⁶⁾ and Ti₂AlC/TiAl composite⁽⁵⁾ have been studied to incorporate the high strength and stiffness of these ceramics into low density of TiAl alloy.

Recently, much work has been focused on the production of intermetallics or ceramics by spark plasma sintering (SPS). As a new rapid sintering technology with a high sintering speed, high reproducibility, safety, and reliability, SPS is receiving increasingly attention and widely used to fabricate the functionally graded materials, metal matrix composites and intermetallics⁽⁷⁻¹⁰⁾. In previous work⁽¹¹⁾, we have applied the SPS process to the fabrication of binary $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic. In this paper, TiC ceramic was used to strengthen $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic due to its high melting point of 3523 K and elastic modulus of 379 GPa, and TiC/TiAl composites with a varying weight fraction of TiC powders were prepared by SPS technique. The strengthening mechanisms causing the change in the mechanical property of the IMCs with the addition of TiC were firstly discussed.

## 2 Experimental

$\gamma$-TiAl was used as the matrix of the intermetallic matrix composites (IMCs). The $\gamma$-TiAl was produced using a mixture of pure titanium powder(99.8%) and particle size of less than 45 μm and pure aluminum powder with 99.8% purity and particle size of less than 45 μm by combustion synthesis technique⁽¹²⁾. TiC powders with particle size of 0.3 μm were used as reinforcements. Then the $\gamma$-TiAl and TiC powders were blended for 24 h with high uniformity for the IMCs. Weight fractions of the TiC powders used in this experiment were 0wt%, 5wt%, 6wt%, 7wt%, 8wt%, 9wt%,10wt%,15wt% and 20wt%, respectively, for $\gamma$-TiAl powders. ## 3 Results and Discussion

The microhardness increases steadily with increasing the weight fraction of TiC, as illustrated in Fig.1 and also error bars are given in the figure. This result shows that the microhardness of the IMCs under compressive loading is enhanced by the addition of TiC reinforcement. The value of the IMCs containing 20wt% TiC reaches as high as more than 750 MPa, which is about 200 MPa greater than that of the TiC-free IMC. The high degree uniformity of TiC particle distributions has significant effects on the hardness properties and effectively resists materials' deformation caused by compression.

![](./images/811976646180470785_2.jpg)

Fig.2 Effect of weight fraction of TiC on bending strength of the IMCs

Fig.2 shows a relationship between the bending strength and weight fraction of TiC. As clearly shown in this figure, the values of bending strengths increase with increasing the weight fraction of TiC till 7wt% and then decrease with the varying weight fraction of TiC more than 7wt%. The IMC containing 7wt% TiC has the best bending strength of 842 MPa, which is 200 MPa greater than that of the unreinforced $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic. With the addition of 20wt% TiC, the value of bending strength of the IMC even is less than 620 MPa, lower than that of the unreinforced $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic.

Why the bending strength of the IMCs with the addition of TiC more than 7wt% shows a decrease gradually, even poorer than that of the unreinforced $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic? It is found that more than 7wt% added TiC reinforcements easily congregate at the position of grain boundary shown in Fig.3. The clustered regions of the TiC particles seen especially in the IMCs containing greater weight fractions of TiC will have detrimental effects on the mechanical properties of the IMCs. In particular, toughing particles with zero spacing are potentially crack initiation and propagation sites$^{[13]}$ in the mechanical examinations. Therefore, the decrease in the mechanical properties of the IMCs containing more than 7wt% TiC may be attributed to the agglomeration of the TiC distributions.

Mechanical tests have indicated that the IMC containing 7wt% TiC shows a more excellent mechanical property than that of any other IMCs. The mechanisms causing the change in the mechanical property of the IMCs with the addition of TiC will be discussed below. Kampe *et al* have reported that the increase of strength in TiC reinforced IMCs comes from both indirect and direct sources$^{[13]}$. The former is due to the microstructure changes within the matrix of the IMCs, for example, reinforcement-derived grain refinement and interstitial solid solution strengthening. The latter is due to the interaction of dislocations with the reinforcing particles. The following equation has been proposed based on the additive strengthening principle:

$$\sigma_{c}=\sigma_{0}+\delta \sigma_{i}+\delta \sigma_{p} \tag{1}$$

where, $\sigma_{c}$ is the flow stress of the IMCs, $\sigma_{0}$ the flow stress of the unreinforced alloy, $\delta \sigma_{i}$ the increase in flow stress of the matrix due to the indirect effects of the reinforcement, and $\delta \sigma_{p}$ the increase in flow stress due to the direct effects of the reinforcement.

The following equation is obtained by rearranging Eq.(1):

$$\Delta \sigma=\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{0}=\delta \sigma_{i}+\delta \sigma_{p} \tag{2}$$

In the system of the IMCs reinforced by TiC, two kinds of strengthening mechanisms are fully considered, one of which is the grain refinement

mechanism and it is assumed that $\delta \sigma_{i}$ is derived solely from grain refinement. Thus, the following equation is obtained from the Hall-petch equation $^{[14]}$:
$$
\delta \sigma_{i}=k \cdot\left(d^{-1 / 2}-d_{0}^{-1 / 2}\right) \tag{3}
$$
where, $k$ is the Hall-Petch slope constant, $d$ the grain size of the IMCs, and $d_{0}$ the grain size of the unreinforced $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic. The value of $d_{0}$ can be estimated to be $8 \mu \mathrm{m}$ from the SEM images of $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic shown in Fig.4, while the value of $d$ is estimated to be $2 \mu \mathrm{m}$ from TEM images of the IMC containing $7 \mathrm{wt} \%$ TiC shown in Fig.5. In addition, the Hall-Petch slope constant, $k$, was taken as $2.4 \mathrm{MPa} \bullet$ $\mathrm{mm}^{1 / 2}$ based on the research of TiAl $^{[15]}$. When the values of $k, d$ and $d_{0}$ are substituted in Eq.(3), we obtain the value of $\delta \sigma_{i}$. Thus, $\delta \sigma_{i}$ for the IMCs containing $7 \mathrm{wt} \%$ TiC can be calculated to be $85 \mathrm{MPa}$ from the above equation.

Another is the dislocation strengthening mechanism and a series of dislocations are observed by means of TEM shown in Figs.6-8. It can be seen that the dislocations are formed as three types such as netlike dislocations, regular dislocations and cystiform dislocations. Therefore, the contribution of $\delta \sigma_{p}$ on the IMCs is derived mainly from dislocation strengthening. To quantitatively evaluate $\delta \sigma_{p}$, the following equation was proposed:
$$
\delta \sigma_{p}=C \mu b \rho_{t}^{1 / 2} \tag{4}
$$
where, $C$ is a constant of $1.25, \mu$ the elastic modulus of matrix, $b$ the matrix Burgers vector, and $\rho_{t}$ the dislocation density of composite. The dislocation density $\rho_{t}$ can be estimated by means of TEM and is calculated from the following equation:
$$
\rho=2 N / L T \tag{5}
$$
where, $N$ is the magnitude of linear dislocations intersectant with gridding lines in the selected area, $L$ the length of gridding lines divided by magnifying multiples of TEM, $T$ the thickness of the sample. The average of six examinations of dislocation density by

## 4 Conclusions
In this work, TiC reinforced $\gamma$-TiAl IMCs were produced using the spark plasma sintering process with varying the weight fraction of TiC particles. The effects of the TiC fractions and distributions on the properties

of the IMCs were investigated. The following results were obtained.

a) The microhardness increased steadily from approximately 550 MPa to 750 MPa with increasing the weight fraction of TiC from 0 to 20wt%. The bending strength of the IMCs had a maximum value of 843 MPa, which was ~200 MPa greater than that of the unreinforced $\gamma$-TiAl intermetallic, at 7wt% TiC, beyond which the strengths decreased. The degradation of the bending strengths in the IMCs containing higher weight fractions of TiC was attributed to high populations of touching TiC particles, which acted as initiation and propagation sites.

b) The increase of strength in TiC reinforced IMCs came from the reinforcement-derived grain refinement and the interaction of dislocations with the reinforcing particles. Theoretically, the strength of the IMCs containing 7wt% TiC was improved by 85 MPa, and 200 MPa respectively, caused by the grain refinement and dislocation strengthening, the total of which was almost in accordance with the practical results of tests considering normal estimation errors.
