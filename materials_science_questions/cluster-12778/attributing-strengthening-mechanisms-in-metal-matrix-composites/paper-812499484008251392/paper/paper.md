# Effect of Friction Stir Process Parameters on Mechanical Properties of Chrome Containing Leather Waste Reinforced Aluminium Based Composite

Shashi Prakash Dwivedi¹ · Rajeev Agrawal¹ · Shubham Sharma²

Received: 4 September 2020 / Revised: 30 December 2020 / Accepted: 13 January 2021 / Published online: 19 February 2021
© Korean Society for Precision Engineering 2021

## Abstract
Nowadays, there is a lot of environmental pollution due to the wastes generated by the industries. In particular, the waste generated by leather industries produces more environmental pollution. Chrome containing leather waste (CCLW) also causes a lot of environmental pollution. In this study, an attempt has been made to use CCLW as reinforcement with aluminium. Collagen powder was extracted from CCLW. Extracted collagen powder was used to develop aluminium based composite after ball-milling with alumina particles. The parameters of the friction stir process (FSP) are optimized by the Box-Behnken Design. The optimum combination of FSP parameters was found to be the number of tool pass of 1, tool rotational speed of 965.20 rpm and transverse speed of 23.69 mm/min. Tensile strength and hardness were found to be 162.89 MPa and 53.24 BHN, respectively at an optimum combination of FSP parameters. Uniform distribution of reinforcement particles has been also observed for the composite developed at an optimum combination of FSP parameters. Results showed that tensile strength and hardness of composite were enhanced by about 20.65 % and 23.81 % respectively with respect to the base material.

Keywords Leather waste · CCLW · Box-Behnken design · FSP · Ball-milling

## 1 Introduction
Waste emitted by industries is the main reason for pollution in the environment. These wastes generate various types of pollution. It mainly produces air pollution and soil pollution. Usually, these wastes lay outside. Due to their not being used again at the right time, they rot lying outside [1, 2]. Due to which various types of viruses are produced. These viruses are often fatal. In today’s time, efforts are being made to reuse these wastes in many ways. However, many efforts have been successful. But many times there has also been a failure to use them again [3].

Leather industries are currently manufacturing various types of items. The demand of these goods in the market is also very high. Chrome containing leather waste is also being used a lot in the manufacture of various types of items. However, almost all leather material is used to make objects. Still, some leather is left as waste. Similarly, chrome containing leather remains outside the industry as waste, which is called chrome containing leather waste (CCLW). This waste also gives rise to various types of pollution [4]. This waste gives rise to various types of viruses after rotting. This virus causes many types of disease by entering the body of people. Therefore, it becomes very important to use these wastes. The Cr in CCLW proves it is useful as reinforcement. In this study, Cr has been extracted from CCLW to develop a composite with aluminium [5].

The use of aluminium and aluminium-based alloys is becoming widespread in industries. Aluminium is used in buildings to make doors and windows. It is also used to fabricate various parts in automobile industries [6]. Due to its low density compared to iron, it is being used a lot in the aircraft industries. However, there are many aluminium alloys whose specific strength is very good. But many aluminium

There is various technique have been used to develop the composite such as stir casting technique. Composite is also developed by ultrasonic vibration effect. Different types of defects occur when the composite is developed by stir cast- ing technique or ultrasonic vibration effect. During the devel- opment of composite by the stir casting technique, various defects have been observed. These defects were produced inside the composite due to the stirrer. When the stirrer is injected inside the molten matrix material, some air also enters into the molten aluminium alloy with the stirrer. This gives rise to various types of defects such as porosity and blow holes in the composite during solidification. However, the possibility of defects in the composite developed by the ultrasonic vibration effect is negligible due to stirrer [8, 9]. During the development of composite by ultrasonic vibra- tion effect, ultrasonic waves spread quickly, propagate in all directions and easily penetrate high into the molten mate- rial. When a sinusoidal ultrasonic wave of finite-amplitude traverses a non-linear elastic material at the x frequency, a second harmonic is generated at the 2x frequency. Ultrasonic vibrations have a higher frequency of sound waves which is higher than the frequency of human hearing. Ultrasonic vibration is the physical vibration of the molecules of the medium in which the sound is travelling. Frequency range 20–60 kHz. This is greater than 20 kHz, or 20,000 cycles per second [10]. However, the grain structure of the composite is refined by ultrasonic waves. But, reinforcement particles distribution is still a big problem for the composite devel- oped under ultrasonic vibration effect. Therefore, in today’s time, the use of FSP technique is widely used to develop the composite. The main reason for its use in making composite is the formation of composite in the solid-state. Because of which the possibility of porosity or other defects such as shrinkage becomes negligible. In this study also there has been an attempt to develop the aluminium based composite by FSP [11]. Composite mechanical properties depend on grain size and grain structure. The surface contact area of grains increases due to the smaller size of the grain. Due to which tensile strength and hardness increases. The grain structure of the composite formed by FSP gets refined [12, 13]. While the grain size of the composite made by stir cast- ing is slightly coarser. Therefore, if the composite is made at optimum FSP parameters, the strength and hardness of the composite increase well. In this study also composite has been developed by optimizing FSP parameters [14, 15].

From the archival literature, it has been observed that the FSP technique has been used to develop aluminium based composite using various ceramic particles such as SiC, $Al_2O_3$, $B_4C$, $Si_3N_4$ etc. Further, chrome containing leather waste reinforced composite has been developed using stir casting technique in previously published work of author [8, 9]. Though, no researchers combined collagen and alumina to form a composite by FSP technique. In this study, first of all, the FSP parameters were optimized by RSM and then developed composite at the optimum parameters.

## 2 Investigated Materials and Experimental Methods

### 2.1 Formation of Collagen Powder from CCLW and Its Ball-Milling

Figure 1 shows the formation of collagen powder from CCLW. To use chrome containing leather waste as reinforce- ment, it was first cleaned. The CCLW was kept in sulphu- ric acid for one day. After that, it was kept in a solution of HCl and EDTA for 3 days. Then, it was kept in the solution of acetic acid and transformed into collagen fibrils. Colla- gen fibrils were left to dry for 24 h. After drying, collagen powder was obtained after ball milling. Collagen powder and alumina were ball-milled again to form in single entity powder [4].

### 2.2 Experimental Procedure

In the present study, AA6101 aluminium alloy is considered as matrix material. It contains about 98.9% of aluminium, 0.60% of Mg and 0.50% of Si. Tensile strength and hardness of the base plate were found to be 135 MPa and 43 BHN, respec- tively. The dimensions of the plates taken were 100 mm × 50 mm × 10 mm. A groove of 1 mm width and 3 mm depth was made in the aluminium plate with the help of a verti- cal milling machine. The groove was filled with a mixture of ball-milled collagen powder and $Al_2O_3$ particles of size ranging from 30 to 40 µm. To prevent reinforcement particle escapement, the groove has been formed. Further, the HSS tool without a pin was taken for capping operation to avoid reinforcement particles from absconding. HCHCr steel tool with 3 mm profile pin at one end was used for stirring purpose. FSP was performed on a vertical milling machine with the help of in-house developed fixture. Figure 2 shows the experimental procedure to develop the aluminium based composite [16–19]. For the selection of friction stir process parameters (number of tool pass, rotational speed and transverse speed), several experiments were carried out. In the pilot run, arbitrarily the rotational speed was chosen 450 rpm for the development of aluminium based composite and other parameters such as tool pass and transverse speed were kept constant. It was found that the mechanical properties such as tensile strength and hardness were not significantly improved. When rotational speed increased up to 800 rpm, some mechanical properties

### 3.2 Microstructural Investigation at Optimum FSP Parameters

The main reason for observing the microstructure image of the composite is to know whether reinforcement particles are distributed well in the matrix material. If the reinforcement particles are well mixed in the matrix material, then the mechanical properties of the matrix material increase well. In particular, there is a good increase in tensile strength and hardness. Whereas, if the reinforcement particles were not distributed well in the matrix material, the mechanical properties of the base material would not have grown well. The microstructure image of the surface composite developed at optimum FSP parameters (with a single tool pass, tool rotational speed and transverse speed at about 965.20 rpm and about 23.69 mm/min, respectively) can be seen from Fig. 4. The distribution of reinforcement particles was found to be good when the composite was built at optimum FSP parameters.

### 3.3 Tensile Strength Analysis

#### 3.3.1 Effect of Number of Tool Passes on Tensile strength

The effect of the number of tool passes on tensile strength can be seen from Fig. 5. Once the composite was made by a single tool pass, the value of tensile strength was maximum (170.42 MPa). Whereas, when composite was developed by passing tool with three passes, tensile strength was the lowest (155.23 MPa). In a single tool pass, fine grain structure obtained. Fine-grain always helps to increase tensile strength. Smaller grains have greater ratios of surface area to volume, which means a greater ratio of grain boundary to dislocations. The more grain boundaries that exist, the higher the strength becomes.

#### 3.3.2 Effect of Tool Rotational Speed on Tensile strength

The effect of tool rotational speed can be observed from Fig. 5 with tensile strength. Maximum tensile strength was found be 170.90 MPa at tool rotational speed of 965.20 rpm for the selected range of process parameters. Further, the tensile strength of the composite was found to be 162.52 MPa at tool rotational speed of 800 rpm. From this result, it can be concluded that tool rotational speed should be 965.20 rpm to develop the collagen and alumina reinforced aluminium based composite. ### 3.3.3 Effect of Transverse Speed on Tensile Strength

Figure 5 shows the effect of transverse speed on tensile strength. The tensile strength continuously increased with an increase in transverse speed. The best tensile strength (170.90 MPa) was obtained at the transverse speed of 23.69 mm/min. Increasing the transverse speed up to 23.69 mm/min led to a refinement in the grain structure of the composite which resulted in a good increase in ten- sile strength. Srivastava et al. [22] developed the $Si_{3}N_{4}$ reinforced aluminium based composite by FSP technique. They developed surface composite at tool rotational speed of 1000 rpm and transverse speed of 25 mm/min. Results showed that $22.89 \%$ tensile strength improved of aluminium alloy when composite was developed at the transverse speed of 25 mm/min. Present study results also showed that the tensile strength of $Al/Al_{2}O_{3}/collagen$ composite (developed at the transverse speed of 23.69 mm/min) improved by about20.65 %. Present results showed good agreement with Sriv- astava et al. results.

### 3.3.4 Confirmation Experiment

Figure 6 shows the ramp function graph. The optimum parameters of the FSP can be seen by the ramp function graph. From the ramp function graph, it can be concluded that the tensile strength value should be 170.90 MPa, if the composite developed by the FSP with single tool pass, the tool rotational speed of 965.20 rpm and the transverse speed of 23.69 mm/min. Confirmation experiment was carried out at the optimum combination of FSP parameters (number of tool pass of 1, tool rotational speed of 965.20 rpm and the transverse speed of 23.69 mm/min). Experimental tensile strength at an optimum combination of FSP parameters was found to be 162.89 MPa. Mathematical model result and the experimental result showed only $4.62 \%$ error. The hardness of the composite has been also investigated developed at an optimum combination of friction stir parameters. The hard- ness of composite was found to be 53.24 BHN. To observe the FSP parameters effect on aluminium alloy, the friction stir process has been also carried out on the aluminium alloy without mixing the reinforcement particles (collagen powder and $Al_{2}O_{3}$ ). Tensile strength and hardness of devel oped surface without mixing the reinforcement particles were found to be 144.82 Mpa and 49.98 BHN respectively. Tensile strength and hardness of base metal without mix- ing the reinforcement particles after FSP were improved by about $7.27 \%$ and $16.23 \%$ respectively. However, the tensilestrength and hardness of composite $(Al+collagen+Al_{2}O_{3})$  were enhanced by about $20.65 \%$ and $23.81 \%$ respectively.

## 4 Conclusions

Collagen powder can be extracted from CCLW and used as reinforcement particles. Collagen powder and alumina were combined by ball milling to develop aluminium based composite by FSP. Optimum parameters of the FSP were obtained using the Box Behnken design. Optimum parameters of FSP were found to be the number of tool pass of 1, tool rotational speed of 965.20 rpm and transverse speed of 24 mm/min. Tensile strength of composite developed at optimum parameters by FSP was found to be 162.89 MPa. The tensile strength and hardness of composite $(Al+collagen+Al_2O_3)$ were enhanced by about 20.65 % and 23.81 % respectively. XRD results showed that the presence of hard phases such as $Al_2O_3$ and $Cr_2O_3$ in the aluminium alloy was responsible for enhancing the tensile strength and hardness of the composite.
