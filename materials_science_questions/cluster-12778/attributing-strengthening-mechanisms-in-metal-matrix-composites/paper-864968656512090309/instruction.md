# Strengthening Mechanism Attribution in Nano-martensitic Ti Alloy

## Problem background
Titanium alloys are important lightweight structural materials. A promising approach to enhance their strength is to create a hierarchical nano-martensitic microstructure in metastable titanium alloys. Consider a water‑quenched Ti‑2.8Cr‑4.5Zr‑5.2Al alloy with a duplex microstructure consisting of equiaxed primary α grains and nanoscale α' martensite lamellae embedded in a β matrix. To understand the mechanical properties of this alloy, it is necessary to quantify the contributions of different strengthening mechanisms—solid solution, dislocation, precipitation, and interface strengthening—to the overall yield strength. This task requires computing those contributions from the measured microstructural parameters using classical strengthening models, whose exact formulations and parameters are provided below.

## Approach
The total yield strength of the water‑quenched (WQ) Ti‑2.8Cr‑4.5Zr‑5.2Al alloy is modelled as a linear sum of four independent strengthening contributions:

σ_y = σ_int + σ_dis + σ_ss + σ_prec

where:
- σ_int – interface strengthening (Hall‑Petch for primary α grains + interlamellar spacing strengthening for α'/β lamellae)
- σ_dis – dislocation (forest) strengthening
- σ_ss – solid solution strengthening
- σ_prec – precipitation strengthening (Orowan + CTE mismatch, combined in quadrature)

The microstructural parameters and alloy composition are taken from the experimental characterisation of the WQ alloy. All material constants and coefficient values are drawn from the literature and listed explicitly in the “Models and parameters” section.

### 4. Strengthening contribution formulas

#### Interface strengthening (σ_int)
σ_int = σ_GB + σ_lam

σ_GB = k_HP / √(D_αp)   (Hall‑Petch for primary α)

σ_lam = k_lam / √(t_α')   (interlamellar spacing strengthening for α'/β nanolamellae)

*Note:* Use consistent units – convert μm to m and nm to m as appropriate so that the result is in Pa, then convert to MPa.

#### Dislocation strengthening (σ_dis)
σ_dis = M · α · G · b · √ρ

#### Solid solution strengthening (σ_ss)
σ_ss = B_Cr · w_Cr + B_Al · w_Al

where w_Cr and w_Al are the weight percentages given in the composition table (Zr is treated as neutral and does not contribute).

#### Precipitation strengthening (σ_prec)
Precipitation strengthening is the result of two simultaneously active mechanisms, which are statistically combined by quadrature addition:

σ_prec = √( σ_oro² + σ_CTE² )

**Orowan mechanism (σ_oro):**

σ_oro = (C_oro · M · G · b) / (λ_oro) · ln(2·r_p / b)

**CTE mismatch mechanism (σ_CTE):**

σ_CTE = η · G · b · √( 12 · Δα · ΔT / (b · t_α') )

where η = 1.25 is an empirical constant that accounts for the dislocation density generated by thermal mismatch.

**Explanation of derived quantities:**
- λ_oro = β_oro · t_α' · ( 1/√(f_α')  –  1 )   (effective inter‑precipitate spacing for plate‑shaped particles)
- r_p = t_α' / 2 is taken as the equivalent radius of the plate‑like α' precipitates.

All quantities must be expressed in SI base units (or consistently) before performing the calculations, and the final results converted to MPa.
