# Freestanding Film Made by Necklace-like N-doped Hollow Carbon with Hierarchical Pores for High-performance Potassium-Ion Storage

Wenxiu Yang$^{a}\dagger$, Jinhui Zhou$^{a}\dagger$, Shuo Wang$^{a}$, Weiyu Zhang$^{a}$, Zichen Wang$^{a}$, Fan Lv$^{a}$, Kai Wang$^{a}$, Qiang Sun$^{a}$, and Shaojun Guo$^{a, b, c} *$

Potassium-ion batteries (PIBs) have drawn much attention owe to their low cost and high safety to replace the lithium-ion batteries (LIBs). However, it is still a grand challenge to design stable and high-capacity nanocarbon for efficient intercalation/deintercalation of large size K ions. Herein, we report a class of ultra-high pyrrolic/pyridinic-N-doped necklace-like hollow carbon (NHC) material as a novel free-standing anode for boosting PIBs in term of capacity, rate ability and durability. The as-made NHC film is featured with abundant hierarchical micro/meso/macro-pores, necklace-like hollow structure, ultra-high pyrrolic/pyridinic-N doping and high specific surface area, which could finally promote the intercalation/deintercalation of K ions, reduce the volume expansion and improve the stability in the PIBs. These new characteristics make the NHC deliver a high reversible specific capacity of 293.5 mAh/g at 100 mA/g, outstanding rate property (204.8 mAh/g at 2000 mA/g) and cycling performance (161.3 mAh/g at 1000 mA/g after 1600 cycles), which represent the best carbon-based non-metal materials for PIBs anode. The density functional theory (DFT) calculations demonstrate that pyrrolic and pyridinic-N doping can efficiently change the charge density distribution of carbon and promote the adsorption of $K^{+}$ to the NHC electrode, which promotes the K ion storage.

## Broader context
Up to now, potassium-ion batteries (PIBs) have been regarded as hopeful alternatives to lithium-ion batteries for their low-cost, nontoxic, abundant reserves and low redox potential of $K/K^{+}$. However, PIBs still suffer from great challenges due to the large K-ion radius, slow reaction dynamics and poor durability. In this work, we boost the capacity, rate ability and durability of PIBs by a class of ultra-high pyrrolic/pyridinic-N-doped necklace-like hollow carbon (NHC) free-standing material. We demonstrate theoretically and experimentally that the synergistic effect of the hierarchical pores, hollow nanofiber structure, ultra-high N doping and high specific surface area greatly enhance the electrochemical performance of the NHC anode. We believe that the present work would encourage further research for high-performance free-standing carbon-based metal-ion batteries anode materials, opening the new avenue for alkaline metal-ion batteries toward the potential commercialization.

## Introduction
Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) have been widely applied in portable electronic devices and electric vehicles because of their high energy and power densities.$^{1-5}$ However, the high cost, uneven distribution and the scarcity of lithium resources hinder their rapid development in grid storage. Developing low-cost, steady and safe metal-ion batteries is of great significance as promising alternatives to LIBs, such as sodium, magnesium, aluminium and potassium-ion batteries (PIBs).$^{6-15}$ In particular, PIBs have been regarded as the most promising alternative for LIBs due to the following advantages: low-cost, nontoxic, abundant reserves and low redox potential of $K/K^{+}$ (-2.93 V vs standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)).$^{16-18}$ Up to now, much attention has been drawn to design new anode materials for PIBs such as alloy-based materials, transition-metal sulphides, phosphides, MXene-based composites, and carbon-containing materials.$^{16, 19-23}$ However, the main challenge up against by PIBs is the larger diameter of the K ion (1.38 Å) than those of Li (0.76 Å) and Na (1.02 Å) ions, which may impede the intercalation of K ions into the electrode materials, leading to a poor capacity, rate performance, and cycle stability during the potassiation and depotassiation process.$^{11, 24-26}$ High specific surface area and porous carbonaceous composites have been regarded as attractive PIBs anode materials due to their high conductivity, chemical stability and expanded interlayer spacing, which can facilitate the accommodating of more

Herein we demonstrate a facial strategy to produce ultra-high pyrrolic/pyridinic-N doped necklace-like hollow carbon (NHC) material as a novel free-standing anode for boosting PIBs. The resulting free-standing NHC owns hierarchical micro/meso/macro-pores, necklace-like hollow structure, ultra-high pyrrolic/pyridinic-N doping and high specific surface area. Such unique characteristics make NHC perform a high reversible specific capacity of 293.5 mAh/g at 100 mA/g and outstanding rate properties (204.8 mAh/g at 2000 mA/g). The optimized NHC material is highly stable for PIBs, by retaining almost no decrease of capacity (161.3 mAh/g) over 1600 cycles at a high current density of 1000 mA/g, representing one of the best cycling stabilities of the carbon-based PIBs anode reported to date. Furthermore, first-principles calculations reveal that the charge density distribution of carbon substrate can be optimized by pyrrolic and pyridinic-N doping, suggesting an optimal binding energy of the K ion adsorption in this unique NHC nano-structure.

## Results and discussion

The as-prepared un-pyrolyzed electrospinned membrane was named as u-NHC. To describe the capacitive behaviour of the NHC materials, the CVs were carried out in the potential range from 0.01 to 2.5 V at scan rates from 0.1 to 1.0 mV/s (Figure 3a&S20). According to the power-law relationship between the current (i) and scan rate (v), $i = av^{b}$, where the parameter b-value can be determined by plotting the log(i) versus log(v) curves. In general, the b-value of 0.5 demonstrates an ideal diffusion-controlled process, while the b-value of 1.0 demonstrates a surface capacitive-controlled process. In this work, the b-value was calculated to be 0.67 and 0.90 for cathodic and anodic peaks, respectively, at the sweep rates between 0.1 and 1.0 mV/s, suggesting a mixed insertion and capacitive reactions for potassium-ion charge storage (Figure S20f). To evaluate the charge-transfer resistance and stability of NHC₂- NH₃/Ar electrode after the cycles, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis was employed. To further interpret the superior battery performance with high rate capability and excellent cyclability rendered by a surface-dominated process, the K ion adsorption abilities at different N-doping sites were simulated, and the relative adsorption energy (ΔEₐ) was calculated based on the DFT. Firstly, the ΔEₐ of the K atom on an ideal graphene layer was investigated to be **-0.63 eV** (Figure S21). Then, different co-doping sites (N5, N6, and NQ) were introduced and the corresponding ΔEₐ was calculated to be -3.05, -3.44, and -0.56 eV (Figure 4a-c), respectively, which indicates that the pyrrolic N and pyridinic N sites own significantly higher K-adsorption ability than the graphitic N site. The relative lower ΔEₐ of the NQ site should be attributed to the electron-rich structure of the graphene layer, which shows a negative effect on K adsorption.³⁸ Thanks for the ultrahigh pyrrolic/pyridinic-N-doping, a stronger tendency for K attraction appeared at the NHC₂-NH₃/Ar anode material, leading to the increased rate capability of the PIBs. Additionally, side and top views of electron density difference was calculated through subtracting the charge densities of K atoms and bare C atoms from those of the combined compounds to deeply understand the bonding characteristic of the adsorbed K atoms (Figure 4d-i, S21b and c). A net gain of charge can be observed in the four structures, indicating a charge transfer from the adsorbed K to its nearest C atoms, whereas the charge density tends to accumulate more around the N-doping sites in the N-doped nanocarbon structures.³⁹ Moreover, the tendency is more significant in the N5 and N6 than in NQ, suggesting a stronger K-adsorption in pyrrolic and pyridinic N doped structure. Therefore, we can conclude that the ultra-high pyrrolic/pyridinic-N-doping is beneficial to improve the electrochemical performance of the NHC₂-NH₃/Ar anode material, with enhanced K adsorption and optimized electron density distribution.

## Conclusions
In summary, we design and demonstrate a new method for making the necklace-like hollow carbon material with ultra-high pyridinic/pyrrolic-N doping, hierarchical micro/meso/macro-pore structure and large specific surface area for enhancing the K ion storage. The as-made free-standing film performs excellent electrochemical performances as PIBs anode materials in terms of a high reversible specific capacity of 293.5 mAh/g at 100 mA/g, outstanding rate property (204.8 mAh/g at 2000 mA/g) and cycling performance (161.3 mAh/g at 1000 mA/g after 1600 cycles). The CV measurements confirm that the K ion storage behaviour of NHC electrode is a surface-driven dominated process (55.8% at 0.1 mV/s to 82.4% at 1 mV/s). To interpret the correlations between the superior PIBs performance and the electronic-structure of NHC₂-NH₃/Ar, the first-principle was applied to calculate the binding energy of K ions at different N-doping sites, demonstrating the pyridinic-N and pyrrolic-N are in favour of the K ion adsorption.

## Experimental methods
Materials. Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) and Nafion (5 wt %) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Triethanolamine (TEA) was got from Beijing Tongguang Fine Chemical Company. Zinc nitrate hexahydrate (Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O) and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) were purchased from Beijing Chemical Reagent Company (Beijing, China). All chemicals were analytical grade and used as received. All aqueous solutions were prepared with ultrapure water from a Water Purifier System (Sichuan Water Purifier Co. Ltd., China).

Synthesis of ultra-high pyrrolic/pyridinic-N-doped necklace-like hollow carbon (NHC). Monodisperse ZnO nanospheres were firstly produced by a reported method.³¹ 1.5 g of ZnO nanospheres was dissolved into 4.0 mL of DMF by ultrasonicating for 0.5 h, named as solution A. Meanwhile, 0.75 g PAN was added into 3.5 mL of DMF and stirred for 0.5 h, named as solution B. Then, the A and B were mixed and further stirred for 12 h as the electrospinning precursors.
The precursor was filled into a plastic syringe with 22# needle and electrospinned with a flow rate of 0.12 mm/min, an applied voltage of 16 kV and distance between the nozzle tip and the collector of 13 cm. The aluminium foil was used as a collector with a rotating speed of 10 rpm/min. The as-prepared electrospinned samples were named as u-NHCₓ/ᵧ according to the mass ratio of ZnO to PAN (x:y). Thereafter, heat treatments were carried out with the u-NHCₓ/ᵧ at 200 °C for 6 h in a drying oven. The remaining brown product was heated at 250 and 500 °C for 2 h and 1 h, respectively, with a heating rate of 2 °C/min in NH₃. After that, the product was further heated up to 800 °C for 1 h with a heating rate of 2 °C/min in Ar. For simplicity, the resulting samples were named as NHCₓ/ᵧ-NH₃/Ar.
For comparison, the resulting electrospinning samples (u-NHC₂) were heated at 200 °C for 6 h in a drying oven, then pyrolysed at 250 (2 h) and X °C (1 h) with a heating rate of 2 °C/min in Ar and named as NHC₂-Ar-X (X=600, 700, 800 and 900 °C), respectively.
For simplicity, the resulting samples were named as NHCₓ/ᵧ-NH₃/Ar-T according to the mass ratio of ZnO to PAN (x:y), as well as the pyrolysis temperature (T °C).

To demonstrate the importance of the ZnO templates, pure PAN nanofibers were also produced under the same electrospinning conditions, and pyrolyzed with the same of the NHCₓ/ᵧ-NH₃/Ar, and named as PAN-NH₃/Ar.

Characterization. The morphologies of the resulting samples were observed by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, Hitachi SU8010). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) measurements were conducted on a JEM-2010 microscope operated at 200 kV and equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDS, Bruker Quantax). Scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) analyses were carried out using a Hitachi HD2700C (200 kV). The structure of materials was characterized by X-ray diffraction measurement (XRD, Rigaku, D/mas-RB) and Raman spectra (iHR550). The element content was analysed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Kratos XSAM 800, Cu-Kα radiation). The specific surface areas and pore distribution of the

resulting materials were measured according to the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) method.

Electrochemical Characterization. The CR2032 coin type half-cells were used to measure the electrochemical performances, which were assembled in an argon-filled glove box. The working electrode was made directly by the tablet machine and dried at $60\ ^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 10 h under vacuum, and the mass density of PIBS working electrodes is $0.6$-$1.4\ \text{mg/cm}^2$. Potassium metal was used as the reference electrode and counter electrode, and $0.8\ \text{M}\ \text{KPF}_6$ in a mixture of ethylene carbonate (EC) and diethyl carbonate (DEC) with a volume ratio of 1:1 as the electrolyte. Glass fiber (GF) from Whatman was used as the separator. The electrode was washed by diethyl carbonate (DEC) for more than 5 times and then quickly taken out from the argon-filled glove-box before the XRD and Raman measurements. LAND-CT2011A battery instrument was used to test the galvanostatic charge and discharge process at ambient temperature under different current densities, within the potential range of $0.01$-$2.5\ \text{V}$ ($vs.\ \text{K/K}^+$). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were recorded on a CHI 660e at a scan rate of $0.01\ \text{mV/s}$ within the same potential range.

DFT calculation. In this work, all the calculations were carried out based on using Vienna $Ab$ $initio$ Simulation Package (VASP), and Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (PBE-GGA) functional described by the projector augmented-wave (PAW) approach were adopted. The plane wave cutoff was set to $520\ \text{eV}$ with Hellmann-Feynman forces convergence criterion set to be lower than $0.01\ \text{eV}\ \mathring{\text{A}}^{-1}$ during geometrical optimization. A graphene supercell containing 72 atoms was used with vacuum layer of $20\ \mathring{\text{A}}$ to prevent possible interactions between the repeated layers. The Brillouin zone was sampled by Monkhorst-Pack method with Gamma centered due to $2\times2\times1$ due to the large lattice.
