# Structural and electronic properties of adsorbed nucleobases on pristine and Al-doped coronene in absence and presence of external electric fields: a computational study

Tooba Afshari¹ · Mohsen Mohsennia¹² ![](./images/812702953231089666_1.jpg)

Received: 17 July 2019 / Accepted: 7 November 2019
© Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2019

## Abstract
The adsorption of nucleobases (NBs) on pristine and aluminum-doped coronene (Al-coronene) has been studied theoretically using density functional theory (DFT) method in the presence/absence of external electric fields (EFs) having different strengths. The changes in geometric and electronic structures upon the adsorption were analyzed in order to reveal the sensitivity of pristine coronene and Al-coronene toward NBs. The results of theoretical calculations at wB97XD/6–31G(d,p) level of theory showed that the molecular adsorption of NBs on Al-coronene under the external EFs can induce significant change in Al-coronene conductivity. On the basis of calculated changes in band gap energy ($\Delta E_{\text{g}}$) and adsorption energy ($E_{\text{ads}}$), it was found that electronic properties of Al-coronene, especially in presence of the applied EFs, are very sensitive to the adsorption of NBs. Finally, electronic structures analysis and full characterization of the surface interactions were performed using the quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM) to elucidate the nature of NB adsorption interaction. As a result, the enhanced electrical properties of NBs/Al-coronene-adsorbed complexes in the presence of the applied external EFs indicated promising perspectives for fabrication of new NBs-sensing devices.

Keywords Adsorption · Nucleobases · Coronene · DFT · External electric fields

## Introduction
The genetic information in deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is encoded in terms of four nucleobases (NBs): adenine (A), guanine (G), thymine (T), and cytosine (C) (see Fig. 1). They operate as the basic units of the genetic codes for transferring the encoded information within DNA to proteins [1, 2]. G and A have a fused ring skeletal structure derived from purine ($\text{C}_{5}\text{H}_{4}\text{N}_{4}$) and are called the purine bases. The simple ring structure of C and T is derived from pyrimidine ($\text{C}_{4}\text{H}_{4}\text{N}_{2}$) and hence is called the pyrimidine bases. The A–T and C–G pairs form two and three hydrogen bonds, respectively. The hydrogen bonding between A–T and C–G pairs hold the two strands of DNA together [3, 4].

Biomolecule in the environment may be strongly adsorbed on the nanoparticle (NP) surfaces to make a tight bound layer of biomolecules. In recent years, the adsorption of NBs on proper surfaces has achieved particular attraction in a wealth of applications, such as DNA biosensors and protein biosensors [5]. Solid-state sensors are widely used for detection of biomolecules in the environment, and there are enormous researches devoted to the study of the NBs adsorption on the surface of different solids surfaces [6–14].

Several studies have been performed experimentally and theoretically to investigate the properties and adsorption behavior of nanoadsorbents for adsorption and detection of biological molecules [15–28]. Among the different nanostructured adsorbents, the versatile carbon family manomaterials including carbon nanotube, graphene, and coronene play increasingly important roles in the fabrication of nanoelectronic devices. Coronene ($\text{C}_{24}\text{H}_{12}$) is the new member in the carbon family with large two-dimensional network structures analogous to graphene. The coronene as a planar polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon, consisting of seven perfused benzene rings, has attracted great attention in many categories of science [29, 30].

Alternatively, the doping of nanostructured materials with foreign atoms having different electron negativity (e.g., Si, Al, Ga) was shown to be an efficient method to modify its chemical reactivity and electronic and adsorption properties. The experimental results on the electronic properties of doping of alkali-metals (e.g., Na and K) in polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) such as coronene using photoemission spectroscopy have been reported in the literature [31]. The doping of K into coronene under ultrahigh vacuum (UHV) conditions has been studied by low-temperature scanning tunneling microscopy and spectroscopy (STM/STS) [32]. Besides experimental works [33, 34], there are numerous theoretical studies [35-40] reporting increase in the chemical, physical, and electronic properties of nanoadsorbents by incorporating heteroatom dopants in their structure.

Among different dopant materials, doping with Al was frequently done to achieve enhanced mechanical, electrical, and gas-sensing properties. It was shown that the adsorbent capability and electronic properties of carbon family nanomaterials (e.g., graphene) for NBs can be considerably improved by incorporation of Al dopant [41-45]. However, to our knowledge, the influences of the Al doping on the electronic structure of coronenes have been rarely studied. The potential application of coronene and its doped forms for gas sensor fabrication was investigated during the last few years [46-48].

In addition to doping type and concentration of dopant impurities, presence of a static electric field (SEF) can effectively affect the electronic property and the corresponding adsorption capacity of adsorbents. Based on our literature survey, the applications of various nanostructured materials in different scientific fields (e.g., physics, chemistry, medicine, and health) have been frequently studied experimentally and theoretically under different EF strengths [49-54].

The experimental and computational studies made on the fabrication of chemical nanosensors have revealed that the physicochemical and adsorptive properties of adsorbent materials may be enhanced systematically under the applied SEF [51, 54]. In a recent study, we applied density functional theory (DFT) method to assess the high impact of the applied EFs on the adsorption characteristics of ethylene oxide molecules on the aluminum-doped coronene (Al-coronene) [55]. The results can indeed provide useful information about the influence of the doping and applied external EF on the coronene for exploring their new applications such as sensor fabrications.

In the present work, we have provided a detailed study on the sensitivity of both pristine coronene and Al-coronene toward adsorption of NBs. The influence of perpendicular EFs in the negative of the $x$-axis with different field strengths of 1.0 $\times 10^{-2}$ and $2.0 \times 10^{-2}$ a.u. (1 a.u. $= 5.14224 \times 10^{11}$ Vm$^{-1}$) [56] on the adsorption properties of NBs on the Al-coronene will be addressed. We also considered some possible orientations of the NBs with respect to the adsorbents. Furthermore, we will exploit the quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM) to characterize the nature of interaction existing between surfaces and NBs. According to results, the sensitivity of coronene for the NB detection could be observably improved by introducing the Al dopant especially in the presence of EFs.

## Computational methods

The structure of selected coronene containing 36 atoms is shown in Fig. 2. The geometry optimization and single-point energy calculation of pristine coronene and Al-coronene structures were performed via meta-hybrid wB97XD functional [57] using the Gaussian 09 program package [58]. Since it has been frequently emphasized that considering the dispersion forces leads to a significant improvement in the interaction energy [59, 60], the Pople-type 6-31G(d,p) basis set as implemented in the software package was also used during calculation [61]. The effect of EFs on the Al-coronene and NBs/Al-coronene-adsorbed complexes was investigated by applying the perpendicular EFs to the coronene plane in the negative of the $x$-axis with different field strengths. The calculations were performed using Gaussian 09 program package with the same basis set and using the "field" keyword [62]. The Hamiltonian of the molecular system is as follows [63]:

$$
H=H_{0}+H_{\text {int }} \tag{1}
$$

where $H_{0}$ and $H_{\text {int }}$ are the Hamiltonian of systems without and with the applied EF, respectively. $H_{\text {int }}$ can be expressed using the dipole approximation as:

$$
H_{\text {int }}=-\mu \times F \tag{2}
$$

where $\mu$ and $F$ are respectively the dipole moment of the molecule and the radiation field.

The frame of axes is shown in Fig. 2. The convergence criteria for energy, force, and displacement were $1 \times 10^{-6}$ Ha, $3 \times 10^{-4}$ Ha/Å, and $5 \times 10^{-3}$ Å, respectively.

The topological parameters based on the AIM theory was also carried out using AIMAll package [64, 65] to characterize the nature and interaction strength of NBs with the pristine coronene and Al-coronene in the presence/absence of the applied external EF.

We defined the adsorption energy ($E_{\text{ads}}$) as follows:

$$
E_{\text{ads}}=E_{(\text{NB+sheet})}-(E_{\text{sheet}}+E_{\text{NB}})+E_{\text{BSSE}}+E_{\text{ZPE}} \tag{3}
$$

where $E_{(\text{NB+sheet})}$, $E_{\text{sheet}}$, and $E_{\text{NB}}$ are the total energy of the adsorbed complex systems, the energy of isolated adsorbents (pristine coronene and Al-coronene), and the energy of the free NB molecules, respectively. In order to accurately assess the $E_{\text{ads}}$ values, all of them were corrected by considering the zero-point vibrational energy (ZPE) and basis set superposition error (BSSE) using the counterpoise method proposed by Boys and Bernardi [66]. The population analysis to evaluate the average total charge transfer ($Q_{\text{T}}$) between pristine coronene and Al-coronene and NBs was performed based on Mulliken scheme.

The band gap energy ($E_{\text{g}} = E_{\text{LUMO}} - E_{\text{HOMO}}$) as the energy difference between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) was obtained and reported [67-69].

The sensitivity of the pristine coronene and Al-coronene for detection of NBs was evaluated by the obtained $E_{\text{g}}$ change ($\Delta E_{\text{g}}\%$) after exposure to NBs in the following form:

$$
\Delta E\text{g}\% = [(Eg2-Eg1)/Eg1] \times 100 \tag{4}
$$

where $E_{\text{g1}}$ and $E_{\text{g2}}$ are $E_{\text{g}}$ values before and after adsorption of NBs.

## Results and discussion
### Adsorption of NBs on pristine coronene and Al-coronene

In order to compare the effect of different doped positions of coronene with Al atom on its adsorption properties, optimized structures of coronene with Al doping at the edge and non-edge positions were investigated and the results are shown in Fig. 3. As revealed from this figure, the doping in the non-edge position of coronene showed a drastic change on the planar structure of coronene. The protrusion formed on the planar structure of coronene due to the replacement of carbon atoms with larger atoms such as Al can provide better situation for further adsorption of the NBs. This deformation is associated with change in hybridization of coronene from $\text{sp}^2$ to $\text{sp}^3$ at the site of doped atom.

The effect of Al-doped positions on adsorption of the NBs on Al-coronene surface was examined, for instance, the adsorption characteristics of cytosine (C) on Al-coronene with different doped positions were calculated and the results are presented in Table 1 and Fig. 3. According to the results, the adsorption of C on the edge-doped coronene showed the lower bond length and higher $E_{\text{ads}}$, indicating a stronger binding between C and the Al-coronene surfaces. Therefore, Al doping in non-edge position of coronene as shown in Fig. 3a was considered for further calculations.

The binding energy of Al atom in Al-coronene in absence of the applied EF was obtained to be $-$21.81 eV, which can be compared with $-$ 21.42 and $-$ 21.09 eV as that obtained in presence of the applied EFs with field strength of $1.0 \times 10^{-2}$ and $2.0 \times 10^{-2}$ a.u., respectively. The results confirmed that the applied EFs had no significant influence on the binding energy of Al atom in Al-coronene. The NBs were placed at various positions on the top of pristine coronene and Al-coronene surface in different orientations. The optimized structures of the NBs, pristine coronene, and Al-coronene were obtained as shown in Figs. 4, 5, 6, and 7.

In our recent studies, we calculated the structural parameters and Mulliken charge for pristine coronene and Al-coronene in presence and absence of perpendicular external EFs in the negative of the $x$-axis with different strengths [55].

The different possible orientations of NB molecules were cheeked for adsorption on the surface of pristine coronene. Our investigation revealed that the A, T, G, and C molecules adsorbed on pristine coronene surfaces with a parallel orientation adsorption indicated higher $E_{\text{ads}}$ values compared with other possible orientations (see Table 2).

The results are shown in Fig. 8, in which the blue and red regions correspond to the most electron-poor and the most electron-rich regions, respectively. The graphical results showed the positive charge of Al atom (blue color) and the negative charge of C atoms (red color). The significant interaction of T with Al-coronene suggested a possible way for a great fabrication of NBs sensors.

The HOMO and LUMO energies are presented in Table 2. The results obviously showed that doping processes are strongly dependent on all the molecular orbital energy gaps between the HOMO and LUMO orbitals. After the doping process, the HOMO energy level of the Al-coronene was higher than pristine coronene, whereas their LUMO energy level was lower than coronene. In order to elucidate the influence of doping on the electronic properties of the systems, the density of state (DOS) plots for pristine coronene, NB/coronene, and NB/Al-coronene (A1, T1, G1, and C1) were calculated. The results of Table 2 revealed that the Al doping can break the coronene sublattice symmetry, resulting in reducing its $E_{\text{g}}$ by 0.51 eV. To further understand the adsorption characteristics of NBs on Al-coronene, the DOSs for NB/Al-coronene complexes at A1, T1, G1, and C1 configurations were calculated and are shown in Table 2 and Fig. 9. The DOS plots showed that there is a considerable hybridizing between NBs and Al-coronene orbitals which would lead to the change in electronic properties of Al-coronene upon adsorption of NBs. The $E_{\text{g}}$ of Al-coronene in the most stable configurations as shown in Figs. 4, 5, 6, and 7 (i.e., A1, T1, G1, and C1, respectively) was close to the $E_{\text{g}}$ of the NBs/coronene by a difference about 0.93, 1.31, 0.86, and 1.67 eV, respectively.

Table 2 indicates that the $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ values of A1, T1, G1, and C1 adsorbed complexes change about $-$6.72%, $-$11.82%, $-$7.01%, and $-$17.81%, respectively. The energy difference between Fermi level and vacuum level is called the work function ($\phi$). It is the least amount of energy required to take an electron away from the Fermi level to the vacuum level and is calculated using the following equation [70]:

$$
\phi = \underset{-\ \text{inf}}{E} - E_{\mathrm{f}} \tag{5}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{inf}}$ is the electrostatic potential at infinity, assumed to be zero because this discrepancy does not have a significant impact on the analysis of the relative values of the work function. $E_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the Fermi energy level, and its position ($T=0\ \text{K}$) for a molecule is approximately in the middle of the HOMO-LUMO energy gap [70].

The change in work function ($\Delta\phi$) is defined as follows:

$$
\Delta\phi = \phi_{(\text{complex})} - \phi_{(\text{adsorbents})} \tag{6}
$$

$\phi_{(\text{complex})}$ is the work function of the adsorbed complexes, and $\phi_{(\text{adsorbents})}$ is the work function of the adsorbents surfaces.

A positive value of $\nabla^{2} \rho(r)$ at the BCP indicates the depletion of the electron density along the bond path, proposing that the C(Al)...N(O) interactions should be classified as an electrostatic interaction. ## Conclusions
DFT calculations at wB97XD level were used to study the adsorption of NBs on pristine coronene and Al-coronene in the presence and absence of the applied external EF at different positions. We checked out the different possible adsorption sites of NBs on Al-coronene systems. While Al dopant and the applied EFs strength facilitates the adsorption of NBs, we found that in the Al-, Tl-, Gl-, and Cl-adsorbed complexes, the $\mathrm{N}_{1}$-side of $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{O}_{1}$-side of $\mathrm{T}, \mathrm{G}$, and $\mathrm{C}$ molecules interact appropriately with atom Al on the Al-coronene surface, indicating chemical interactions (electrostatic in nature). The results indicated that the adsorption of NBs imposes important effects on the electronic properties of Al-coronene, while it has little effect on the electronic properties of pristine coronene. Results of the $\Delta E_{\mathrm{g}}$ and DOS showed that the $\sigma$ values of adsorbed complexes increase significantly after adsorption of the NBs on Al-coronene surface in the presence of the applied EFs. As a result, according to the obtained findings in this work, Al-coronene under the external EFs can be a good candidate for adsorption and detection of NBs.
