## ABSTRACT

MXene is a potential candidate of the sensing electrode for flexible gas sensing devices and has attracted considerable attention. However, two-dimensional (2D) MXene Ti₃C₂Tₓ nanosheet is liable to stack together and lose the high specific surface area, which limits its gas sensing performance. In this work, a room-temperature flexible NO₂ sensor based on three-dimensional (3D) crumpled MXene sphere prepared by ultrasonic spray pyrolysis technology is developed. Then, the properties are further improved by designing 3D crumpled MXene sphere/ZnO composites sensitive electrode with high specific surface area and active sites. The selectivity of the flexible sensing device to NO₂ is further highlighted, and the response signal to 100 ppm NO₂ improves from 27.27 %-41.93 %, and the recovery characteristic substantially increases from ~30 % to ~100 %. The excellent NO₂ sensing properties of 3D crumpled MXene sphere/ZnO are attributed to the high surface area, abundance of edges and defects caused by folding, and the formation of MXene/ZnO p–n heterojunction. This work has introduced an idea to improve MXene gas-sensing properties and helped us to further understand the sensing process between MXene and target gas.

## 1. Introduction

MXenes, a large family of newly developing 2D materials, refers to 2D transition metal carbide, nitride, and carbonitride [1–3]. MXenes has shown the potential application value in many areas, such as gas/humidity sensing, energy storage systems, water purification, and EMI shielding [4–7]. According to the reported gas sensing work [8], a gas sensor based on pure 2D MXene nanosheet has a higher signal-to-noise ratio than those of the other 2D gas sensing materials, such as graphene, transition-metal dichalcogenides, and black phosphorus. Nevertheless, MXene will cause serious aggregation after drying and lose the large specific surface area. Thus, the response and selectivity of gas sensor based on pure 2D MXene nanosheet are very inferior, and further improvement is necessary to increase its practicality.

Some efforts have been made by researchers to sensitize MXene. In our previous work [9], we used alkaline treatment to improve the surface oxygen terminal to enhance ammonia sensing. Jung et al. used a similar approach to increase the selectivity of ethanol by adjusting the intercalation of metal ions [10]. In addition, organ-like MXene with polyaniline and MXene sheets with TiO₂ were recombined to improve the ethanol and ammonia sensing properties, respectively [11,12]. Yin et al. used the electrospinning technique to structure the 3D MXene framework to lower the detection limit of VOCs [13]. However, in these reports, only a certain unilateral feature of the sensor was improved. The comprehensive sensing performance of response, selectivity, and recovery characteristic should be further enhanced, and the detection range of gas species should be enlarged.

Here, we demonstrate the anti-aggregation 3D crumpled MXene Ti₃C₂Tₓ spheres by using ultrasonic spray pyrolysis approach to provide the high specific surface area of MXene, which fabricates the large adsorption surface of the target gas. Moreover, we compound ZnO nanoparticles with 3D crumpled MXene Ti₃C₂Tₓ spheres to create more activated adsorption sites. The above modifications have substantially improved the NO₂ sensitivity, selectivity, response and recovery properties of MXene sensor. ## 2. Experimental

### 2.1. Synthesis of 2D MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sheet

A 2D MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sheet was synthesized by etching $1.0\ \text{g}\ Ti_3AlC_2$, which was obtained from Jilin 11 Technology Co., Ltd. with a mixture of 40 mL of concentrated HCl and 1.0 g LiF at $40\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 24 h. After the reaction, the product was washed by centrifugation until the pH reached ~6. Then, the clay $Ti_3C_2T_x$ was dispersed by ultrasonic treatment in an ice bath for 1 h. Finally, a colloidal solution of delaminated $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sheets was obtained by centrifugation. A small amount of colloidal solution was vacuum-filtered, and the mass of MXene membrane was measured. The concentration of the colloidal solution of delaminated $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sheets was determined.

### 2.2. Synthesis of 3D crumpled MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere and 3D crumpled MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere/ZnO

Samples of 3D crumpled MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere and sphere/ZnO were prepared by ultrasonic spray pyrolysis technology [14,15]. MXene sheets ultrasonic spray precursor solution with 0.2 g poly- vinylpyrrolidone K30 (PVP-K30), MXene sheet ultrasonic spray precursor solution with $0.2\ \text{g}$ PVP-K30, and $0.675\ \text{g}\ Zn(CH_3COO)_2\cdot H_2O$ were atomized using an ultrasound generator. Then, the formed aerogel was uniformly delivered to $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ of tube furnace by using $N_2$ with a flow rate of $5\ \text{L}\ \text{min}^{-1}$ as the carrier gas. Lastly, 3D crumpled MXene sphere and $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere/ZnO powder were collected using an electrostatic collection device.

### 2.3. Material characterization

The morphology of MXene sheet, 3D crumpled MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere, and 3D crumpled MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere/ZnO were observed by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM; JEOL JSM-7500 F) with an acceleration voltage of 5 kV and transmission electron microscopy (TEM; JEM 2100 F) with an acceleration voltage of 200 kV. The Ti, C, O, and Zn element areal scanning analysis was tested by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) of transmission electron microscopy. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the powder were analyzed using Rigaku D/Max 2550 with Cu K$\alpha$ radiation ($\lambda =1.5418\ \mathring{\text{A}}$) in the $2\theta$ range of $5^\circ$-$80^\circ$. The surface characteristics of the samples were measured using an ESCALAB X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS) with an X-ray source (Al K$\alpha$ h$\nu=1,486.6\ \text{eV}$). The specific surface area of the prepared sensing materials was determined using Brunauer-Emmett-Teller nitrogen adsorption/desorption isotherms (Micromeritics Gemini VII).

### 2.4. Fabrication and sensing properties measurement of flexible gas sensing device

The PI flexible substrate ($10\times10\ \text{mm}$) with interdigitated Au/Ni electrodes was manufactured by Guangzhou Ji Sensing Technology Corporation. The width of electrodes and gaps were both 100 nm. Exactly 4 mg prepared 3D crumpled MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere (or 3D crumpled MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ sphere/ZnO powder) was dispersed in a drop of deionized water by ultrasonic treatment. After washing the substrate repeatedly with deionized water and ethanol and then masking, the aqueous dispersion was drop-cast onto the PI substrate and dried at $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$ in a vacuum oven to obtain the flexible gas sensing device (Fig. S1 (a)). The gas sensing properties were measured at $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$ by a static testing system by using the homemade fixture (Fig. S1(b)). The real-time resistance of the flexible gas sensing device was recorded using a FLUKE 8846A digital multimeter in air, and different concentrations of the target gas were produced by mixing a certain amount of the target gas with air.

### 2.5. Density functional theory (DFT) simulations

All the calculations were performed in the framework of the DFT with the projector augmented plane-wave method. The generalized gradient approximation proposed by Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof functional was selected for the exchange-correlation potential. The long-range van der Waals interaction was described using the DFT-D3 approach. The cut-off energy for plane wave was set to 400 eV. The Brillouin zone integration was performed at the Gamma point. All the structures were relaxed until the residual forces on the atoms have declined to less than $0.05\ \text{eV}/\mathring{\text{A}}$. The MXene/ZnO heterostructure is composed of ZnO (100) and MXene (001) planes.

## 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1 shows the schematic illustration of 3D crumpled MXene sphere and 3D crumpled MXene sphere/ZnO obtained through ultrasonic spray pyrolysis self-assembly. By using MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ colloid at a certain concentration as the precursor, $N_2$ was used as a carrier gas to carry the aerogel into the high-temperature tube furnace after ultrasonic atomization, and the moisture volatilized rapidly. Considering the sudden disappearance of internal stress, all the MXene sheets dispersed in single micro-fog particle collapsed inward isotropically and immediately self-assembled into 3D crumpled MXene (Figs. 2(a) and S2) [16], which were collected using the electrostatic collector along with the $N_2$ flow. Although the MXene sheets in a single micro-fog particle aggregated and lost some adsorption sites for gas molecules sensing, these losses were negligible compared with the large-area aggregation of the entire dispersions (Fig. S3). With an appropriate concentration of MXene $Ti_3C_2T_x$ colloid, the homogeneous structure of 3D crumpled MXene was formed by adjusting the size of micro-fog, $N_2$ flow rate, and the addition of the surfactant PVP-K30 [14]. The microstructure of the 3D crumpled MXene sphere was very clear in Fig. 2(b-c). The size of 3D crumpled MXene was stable at approximately $1\ \mu\text{m}$ (Fig. S4), and its surface was covered with ridge-like bulges caused by the collapse inwards and aggregation of MXene sheets. By zooming in further, the micromorphology of the MXene sheets on the surface of the 3D crumpled MXene sphere was observed, and the folds formed when they were stacked together (Fig. 2 (d)). In Fig. 2(e-g), the distribution of Ti, C, and O in the 3D crumpled MXene sphere is shown, and the results are consistent with that of the self-assembly of MXene sheets. To form a composite 3D crumpled MXene sphere with ZnO, we added a zinc acetate solution to the precursor, and the addition of the surfactant PVP-K30 was necessary for stabilizing the colloid. Although the ultrasonic atomization process was identical, many $Zn^+$ ions were observed in a single micro-fog particle. At high temperatures, ZnO nanoparticles with a uniform size appeared by thermal decomposition when MXene sheets collapsed inward and were tightly bonded to the surface of MXene sheets. MXene sheets with ZnO nanoparticles on the surface were self-assembled to form 3D crumpled MXene sphere/ZnO (Fig. 3(a-b)). The TEM images in Fig. 3(c-d) clearly show that ZnO nanoparticles were evenly distributed on the surface of the 3D crumpled MXene sphere. However, the self-assembly process was completely random, and both sides of MXene sheets were likely to be tightly bound to the ZnO nanoparticles. Fig. 3(e) vividly shows the co-existence of the folds and ZnO nanoparticles, and the lattice spacing of 0.284 nm corresponds to the (100) crystal plane spacing of ZnO. The results of the EDS map scanning of Ti, C, O, and Zn showed that zinc oxide particles were evenly distributed on the 3D crumpled MXene spheres, as shown in Fig. 3(f-i).

The valence bond states and changes of the surface of MXene sphere and MXene sphere/ZnO were analyzed by the XPS. Based on the survey spectra of XPS (Fig. S6(a)), the peak of Zn 2p₃/₂ and the further prominence of the peak of O 1s compared with the peak of Ti 2p were observed, indicating that ZnO successfully adhered to the surface of MXene sphere. All the sensors were placed in the corresponding target gases to respond for 1 min and then recovered in air for 5 min during the measurement of gas sensing properties of all the sensors. Various target gases such as ethanol, acetone, toluene, formaldehyde, ammonia, and NO₂ at the fixed concentration of 100 ppm were exposed to MXene sheets at 70 % RH, and the response were all below 1 %, as shown in Fig. 5(a). Through the same testing process, the response of sensors based on the MXene sphere and the MXene sphere/ZnO were both measured, and the response to ammonia and NO₂ had significant enhancements. After compounding the MXene sphere with ZnO, the response to NO₂ was increased from 27.27 %–41.93 %, and the selectivity of NO₂ became more prominent. All responses were improved as the concentration of NO₂ increased. The sensors based on 2D MXene sheets showed very low response to various gases and low differential selectivity, and the gas sensing properties of the unmodified MXene were limited by the limited adsorption sites on the surface and the loss of specific surface area. The synthesis of the 3D crumpled MXene sphere maintained the large specific surface area, and more adsorption sites were exposed. Considering that NO₂ is an oxidizing gas, the free electrons in 3D crumpled MXene sphere were seized after NO₂ was absorbed. Accordingly, the carrier concentration was reduced as reflected in the increase of the resistance of 3D crumpled MXene sphere [20]. The increase in the specific surface area did not cause a big increase in the response to ammonia and NO₂. Moreover, the 3D crumpled MXene sphere introduced many edges and defects (Fig. 7a), which played an important part in gas sensing [21-23]. DFT simulations were conducted to compute the adsorption energy of NO₂ molecules on the surface of MXene (-0.548 eV) and edge of MXene (-3.298 eV) in Fig. 8, which exhibited molecular configurations with strong adsorption energies. The simulation results support that the edge of MXene displayed stronger NO₂ molecule adsorption than the surface of MXene. During the testing of gas sensing properties, an unusual phenomenon occurred, as shown in Fig. 6(d-e). The resistance of the sensor based on the MXene sphere/ZnO was decreased in 100 ppm NO₂ and increased in 100 ppm ammonia, while $\Delta R/R$ had positive and negative values. However, when the sensor based on the MXene sphere was exposed to ammonia and NO₂, the resistance of the sensor was increased, and this finding is consistent with the previous report [8]. Considering this difference, the mechanism of the gas sensing of MXene can be further explored. The resistance of MXene sheets and spheres was at the order of magnitude of $10^{-1}$ and $10^1$ $\Omega$, respectively, and these materials had metallic electrical properties, thus explaining the overall positive response. After compositing with ZnO, the XPS spectra of the MXene sphere/ZnO showed that the TiO₂ proportion increased, the oxygen terminal increased, and the MXene sphere began to exhibit the nature of p-type semiconductor [31], thus forming heterogeneous p-n junction with ZnO nanoparticles on the surface. The depletion layer formed by the p-n junction promoted the drift transfer of electrons and holes. When the NO₂ molecules were adsorbed, NO₂ molecules gained electrons, and the depletion layer was further widened, thus improving the transmission efficiency and increasing the variation in resistance [32],

as indicated by the substantially improved response time and response value. Water molecules had the same n-doping effect as the ZnO nano- particles, and this effect further increased the response value [33].Moreover, the hydrogen bonding between water molecules and $NO_{2}$ molecules further promoted the absorption of $NO_{2}$ [34]. ## 4. Conclusion
In summary, the synthesis and modification of 3D crumpled MXene Ti3C2Tx sphere by the reliable and effective ultrasonic spray pyrolysis have been introduced, and other 2D materials can be modified in a similar way. The 3D crumpled MXene not only maintains the high specific surface area of MXene sheets but also provides a path for stable and uniform composites with other materials. The high specific surface area, the possibility of increasing the response sites, and high gas- sensitive materials enable us to apply 3D crumpled MXene spheres to gas sensors. In comparison with MXene sheets and 3D crumpled MXene spheres, the 3D crumpled MXene sphere/ZnO has achieved reversible $NO_{2}$ sensing, while significantly increasing the selectivity and response to $NO_{2}$ . The response to $100 ppm NO_{2}$ was enhanced from $27.27 \%$  $41.93 \%$ accompanied by the substantial increase in recovery rate from $30 \%$ to $100 \%$ . The high $NO_{2}$ sensing performance of the 3D crumpled MXene sphere/ZnO is ascribed to the defects, which are caused by folding and the appearance of $p-n$ heterojunction between the MXene sphere and ZnO nanoparticles. The 3D crumpled MXene sphere structure provides a new idea for the chemical sensing of MXenes and further promotes the application of MXenes in gas sensors, thus providing a reference for the exploration of the mechanism of MXene gas sensing.
