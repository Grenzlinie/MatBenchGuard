## ABSTRACT
The mechanism of Cs-NF₃ adsorption on the GaAs(100)-β2(2 × 4) reconstructed surface is investigated by using the first-principles method based on density functional theory. Cs-NF₃ adsorption cases at ten different sites named as D, D', T2, T2', T3, T3', T4, T4', H and H' with two opposite vertical directions are simulated. The results show that the vertical layered direction can remarkably affect surface properties. Compared with the NF₃-up models, the Cs-up models can possess lower adsorption energy, smaller work function and stronger dipole moment. Meanwhile, it is found that in the Cs-up models, the trench site T4 is the energy-preferred position for its lowest adsorption energy while the topmost As-dimer bridge site D is the most unstable for adsorption for its highest adsorption energy. Besides, the hollow site H' can achieve the maximum drop of work function. Furthermore, Mulliken charge distribution indicates that the NF₃ molecule gets negatively charged in Cs-up models while that is neutrally charged in NF₃-up models. Combined with the change of atomic geometric structure, it is inferred that the [Cs⁺-NF₃⁻] dipole is formed in Cs-up models while only the [Cs⁺-As⁻] dipole is formed in NF₃-up models. These results can help to understand Cs-NF₃ co-activation behavior for GaAs photocathode.

## 1. Introduction

GaAs is a typical III-V binary semiconductor material with a direct band gap of 1.42 eV at room temperature. Meanwhile, it is also an ideal material serving as negative electron affinity (NEA) photocathode due to its high quantum efficiency (QE), low thermal emission, high spin polarization and concentrated electron energy distribution, which leads to its numerous applications including image intensifiers for low light detection [1], photon-enhanced thermionic emission converters for solar energy conversion [2], unpolarized and polarized electron sources for accelerators [3], and vacuum microelectronic devices for THz detection [4]. Despite the successful development of NEA photocathode technology, the nature of NEA physical mechanism still lacks sufficient understanding. As for the practical application of NEA GaAs photocathodes, the main limitation is their short lifetime arising from the particular sensitivity to surface contaminants [5]. Even in the extremely high vacuum environment, their QE will gradually decrease with time [6]. It is universally believed that the degradation of photoemission performance for NEA GaAs photocathodes originate from two aspects: the release of alkali atoms and the adsorption of O-containing residual gases [7,8]. As the key factor in the preparation of NEA photocathodes, the activation technology has been continuously focused to improve the photoemission performance, especially the stability of NEA photocathode [9-12].

In the conventional preparation process of high QE GaAs photocathodes, the low-work-function materials, namely the mixtures of electropositive Cs and electronegative O₂ are exposed onto the atomic clean surface to achieve the NEA state [13]. Meanwhile, the alternative materials, such as Cs and NF₃ are also widely used to activate GaAs cathodes on the grounds that GaAs photocathodes activated with Cs and NF₃ can show better stability than those activated with Cs and O₂ [6,7,10,14]. Chanlek *et al* proved that the GaAs photocathodes activated with NF₃ have better stability and resistance than those activated with O₂ under exposure to CO, CO₂ and O₂ through experiments [10], whereas the chemical mechanism of the improved stability were not investigated. Cao *et al* utilized the high resolution photoelectron spectroscopy to investigate the interfacial chemistry on the Cs-NF₃ activated GaAs (110) surface and qualitatively explained the NEA formation based on the double dipole layer model, and it is found that there was no chemical interaction between fluorine and the substrate [15]. At present, with the development of epitaxy technique, compared with nonpolar GaAs (110) surface, GaAs (100) surface attracts more extensive interest and is applied to achieve higher QE and spin polarizability because of its polar surface and diverse reconstruction phases [16-18]. Meanwhile, previous researches on GaAs surface using first-principles calculation have focused on Cs or Cs-O adsorption rather than Cs-NF₃ adsorption [19-22]. Furthermore, there are no specialized theoretical researches about the Cs-NF₃ adsorption on the GaAs (100) reconstructed surface. Especially, the knowledge about the adsorption site and direction of Cs-NF₃ on the GaAs (100) reconstructed surface still lacks of understanding at an atomic level. In this paper, inspired by the experimental findings of Cs-NF₃ activated GaAs (100) photocathodes by Liu et al [14], we use ab-initio density functional theory (DFT) to investigate the co-adsorption of Cs-NF₃ on the GaAs (100)-β2(2 × 4) reconstructed surface, and the variation in surface atomic geometry and electrical properties with the different adsorption sites and directions are analyzed.

## 2. Calculation method and models

The first-principles calculations were on the basis of DFT, which was implemented in the Cambridge serial total energy package (CASTEP) software. The exchange-correlation interactions were treated by Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) in the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) functional [23,24]. The van der Waals (vdW) interactions based on dispersion correction method was included in the calculations. Since Cs element parameter is not covered by the method of Grimme, another available dispersion correction method proposed by Tkatchenko and Scheffler (TS) was adopted [25]. All energy calculations were performed in the reciprocal space, and through the convergence tests, the Brillouin zone integration was sampled by the Monkhorst-Pack grid with the 6 × 3 × 1 k points. The ultra-soft pseudopotential plane wave with a cutoff energy of 400 eV was adopted. The Broyden Flecher Goldfarb Shanno (BFGS) algorithm was used to finish the geometry optimizations, and the chosen convergence parameters are as follows: the energy tolerance was 1 × 10⁻⁵ eV/atom, the force tolerance was 0.03 eV/Å, the stress tolerance was 0.02 GPa and the maximum displacement was 0.001 Å. The valence electrons involved in the calculations included Ga: 3d¹⁰4s²4p¹, As: 4s²4p³, Zn: 3d¹⁰4s², Cs: 5s²5p⁶6s¹, N: 2s²2p³ and F: 2s²2p⁵.

As for the surface atomic structure, the GaAs (100)- β2 (2 × 4) structure is the most stable and concerned surface phase among the GaAs (100) reconstructed surfaces [26], and extensive researches about surface oxidation and Cs adsorption on the As-terminated GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) reconstructed surface have been carried out [19,20,27,28]. Besides, the As-stabilized GaAs (100)-(2 × 4) reconstruction surface was observed via Low Energy Electron Diffraction (LEED) in the preparation process of GaAs(100) photocathode [29]. In their experiments, this reconstruction surface without the residual carbon contaminations was achieved after treatment in HCl-isopropanol solution and subsequent annealing at 410~480 °C in ultrahigh vacuum. Accordingly, in this work, the slab model of GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) reconstructed surface containing two As dimers in the top layer and one As dimer in the third layer was built [26,28], as shown in Fig. 1. By means of a periodically repeated slab, the surface model of GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) reconstructed phase with the size of 7.99 Å×15.99 Å×22.89 Å was built. The surface consisted of 4 layers of As atoms and 3 layers of Ga atoms, wherein there were 28 As atoms, 21 Ga atoms and 1 Zn atom substituted for 1 Ga atom as the p-type dopant. Through the Zn-doping form, the p-type GaAs surface can possess smaller surface energy and work function, which is beneficial to achieve better photoemission performance [30]. In the optimization process, the top four layers above were allowed to fully relax and the remaining layers below were constrained and always fixed in the ideal position. The vacuum layer with a thickness of 15 Å along z-axis was used to avoid mirror interaction between the two periodic slab surfaces. In order to prevent transfer of surface charges and eliminate the artificial surface related quantum states, the As dangling bonds on the bottom of the model were saturate by the fractionally charged pseudo-hydrogen atoms with 0.75 valence electrons [31].

The side view and top view of the structure of the cleaned GaAs (100)-β2(2 × 4) reconstructed surface are shown in Fig. 1(a) and (b), respectively. On the top layer of the clean surface, two pairs of As dimers are formed and one pair of As dimers in the third layer is formed below the missing Ga atoms in the second layer. After model optimization, the bond lengths of As dimmers at the top and third layers were 2.50 Å, 2.49 Å, 2.58 Å, respectively. The work function of the GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) model with no adsorbed atoms was calculated to be 4.95 eV, in excellent agreement with the referenced experimental value of 5 eV [32]. On the basis of optimized GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) model, the surface models regarding the Cs-NF₃ dipole of two opposite directions located at the ten different adsorption sites, namely H, H', T2, T2', T3, T3', T4, T4', D and D' as shown in Fig. 1(b) on the reconstructed surface, were built. As examples, the side views of the Cs-NF₃ co-adsorption models with two opposite directions at the D' site are shown in Fig. 1(c) and (d). In all adsorption models, the initial distance between Cs and NF₃ was set to the same value before model optimization.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Adsorption energy and work function

In the traditional Cs-O activation process, the adsorbed tiny O atoms will tend to move under the Cs atoms layer [33]. Differing from the Cs-O adsorption, the Cs-NF₃ co-adsorption structure with two opposite directions can form on the GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) surface. On the GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) reconstructed surface, ten possible adsorption sites, labeled as H', H, T2, T2', T3; T3', T4, T4', D and D' were chosen to investigate the Cs-NF₃ co-adsorption mechanism of layered structures in two different directions, as shown in Fig. 2. The adsorption energy $E_{ads}$ of Cs atoms and NF₃ molecule can be calculated by the following formula [34,35]:

$$
E_{ads} = E_{GaAs: Cs/NF_3} - E_{GaAs} - E_{Cs} - E_{NF_3} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{GaAs: Cs/NF_3}$ represents the total energy of the surface slab model after adsorption, $E_{GaAs}$ represents the energy of the clean surface slab model before adsorption, $E_{Cs}$ and $E_{NF_3}$ is the energy of single Cs atom and NF₃ molecule. Fig. 2 shows the calculated adsorption energy of GaAs(100)-β2(2 × 4) reconstructed surface corresponding to the ten Cs-NF₃ adsorption sites with two oppsite directions. During geometry optimization, there were no chemical bond breaking and formation, indicating that no chemical reaction took place between the adatoms and GaAs surface.

As shown in Fig. 2, the calculation results of adsorption energy are all negative values, showing that the adsorption processes are all exothermic and then the adsorbed models are thermodynamically stable [34]. It can be first seen that in the Cs-up models, the T4 site exhibits the lowest adsorption energy, while the T3 and T4 sites show the similar adsorption energy in the NF₃-up models. Whether in the Cs-up models or NF₃-up models, the D site shows the highest adsorption energy, which indicates that the top As-dimer bridge is the most unstable position. Further calculated results show that, for the ten Cs-up adsorption models, the average adsorption energy is $-4.83$ eV, while for the ten NF₃-up adsorption models, the average adsorption energy is $-3.52$ eV. Compared with the adsorption energy of surface models with Cs atoms below, the adsorption energy of all surface models with Cs atoms above is lower at the same co-adsorption sites, which shows

Fig. 2 shows that the adsorption energy presents a relatively gentle change at the ten adsorption sites when Cs lies under $NF_3$, whereas the change is significant when Cs lies above $NF_3$, which indicates that the vertical relationship between Cs and $NF_3$ can play a major role in the adsorption energy. In addition, the adsorption site also affects the stability of the Cs-up models. Among the ten adsorption sites, the adsorption energy for Cs-$NF_3$ co-adsorption at the T4 site is $-6.34$ eV, representing the most stable adsorption site, while the As-dimer bridge sites D and D' with the higher adsorption energy are relatively unstable. Predictably, in the early stage of Cs-$NF_3$ co-adsorption on the GaAs (100)-$\beta2$ ($2\times4$) reconstructed surface, Cs atoms will tend to adsorb on the top of $NF_3$ molecules, especially at the T4 site. The T4 and other four better sites, including H', T3, T3' and T4' sites with lower adsorption energy than other sites are chosen to perform further analysis, wherein T3, T3', T4 and T4' sites were also commonly studied for Cs adsorption by other researchers [19,20].

For semiconductors, the work function $\varPhi$ is defined as the required minimum energy of electrons escaping from the bulk to the vacuum, which reflects the photoelectron emission capability [36-38], and this key parameter is expressed as $\varPhi = E_0 - E_F$ [38], where $E_0$ is the vacuum level and $E_F$ is the Fermi level. As for the clean GaAs(100)-$\beta2$ ($2\times4$) reconstructed surface, the work function is 4.95 eV, and the work function changes of the Cs-$NF_3$ adsorbed surface relative to the clean surface are shown in Fig. 3 for the five adsorption sites. It is found that all the work function values are decreased after the Cs/$NF_3$ adsorption in two opposite directions, which illustrates that the adsorption of Cs and $NF_3$ are favorable for the photoemission of GaAs(100)-$\beta2$ ($2\times4$) surface. More importantly, the decrease in work function is more remarkable when Cs is adsorbed above the $NF_3$ molecule at all the adsorption sites. In contrast to that of the $NF_3$-up surface models, the work function of the Cs-up surface models exhibits a further drop of 0.61 eV, 0.67 eV, 0.16 eV, 0.41 eV and 0.16 eV, respectively, at the corresponding adsorption sites H', T3, T3', T4 and T4'. Meanwhile, different adsorption sites lead to different work function. When Cs atoms and $NF_3$ molecule are adsorbed at the H' site in the Cs-up surface models, the decrease of work function reaches the maximum value of $-1.46$ eV. Coincidentally, the H' site is also the adsorption site with the lowest work function in the $NF_3$-up surface models.

In contrast, the As atoms transfer electrons to NF₃ in the Cs-up models and become less negatively charged. Meanwhile, the positively charged Cs atoms contribute to the formation of [Cs⁺-As⁻] dipoles between Cs and As atoms at surface. This differ- ence occurs in all five adsorption sites. The charge transfer cases in the Cs-up and NF₃-up surface models are illustrated in Fig. 5. When the Cs atom is adsorbed below the NF₃ molecule, the large Cs atom can pre- vent interaction of NF₃ molecule and surface GaAs atoms. It is rea- sonably believed that the adsorption case with Cs atoms above NF₃ is better for reducing the work function. The negatively charged NF₃ molecules adsorbed under the Cs atoms are conducive to form the ro- bust [Cs⁺-NF₃⁻] dipole moment with the orientation from vacuum to surface, which is beneficial to the escape of photoelectrons. It is clearly seen from Table 1 that the NF₃ molecule at the T4 site in the Cs-up models has the maximum negative charge, which means that the [Cs⁺- NF3⁻] dipole at the T4 site seems to be the most robust. In addition, it is worth noting that metallic Cs is a good getter material, but the posi- tively charged Cs atom is not [9], and therefore the dipole-induced activation layer formed by the more positively charged Cs⁺ and more negatively charged NF₃⁻ can better reduce the sticking coefficient of residual contaminants from the surrounding vacuum environment.

By means of the calculation method proposed by Hogan et al [19], the adsorption-induced dipoles can be analyzed to further make clear the quantitative relationship between charge redistribution and dipole moment. The charge difference $\Delta \rho(\boldsymbol{r})$ related to the spatial position $\boldsymbol{r}$ for the Cs-NF₃ adsorbed surface model can be calculated by the following equation:

$$
\Delta \rho(\boldsymbol{r})=\rho C s(\boldsymbol{r})+\rho N F_{3}(\boldsymbol{r})+\rho G a A s(\boldsymbol{r})-\rho C s / N F_{3} / G a A s(\boldsymbol{r})
\tag{3}
$$

where the $\rho Cs(\boldsymbol{r})$ and $\rho NF_{3}(\boldsymbol{r})$ is the electron density for the isolated Cs atom and the NF₃ molecule, $\rho GaAs(\boldsymbol{r})$ is that for the clean GaAs surface, and $\rho Cs/NF_{3}/GaAs(\boldsymbol{r})$ is that for the Cs/NF₃ co-adsorbed GaAs surface. Similar to the definition of the center-of-mass, the quantities of total positive charge $Q^{+}$ and total negative charge $Q^{-}$ are calculated as follows:

$$
Q^{+}=\sum_{i} \Delta \rho(r i) for \Delta \rho(r i)>0
\tag{4}
$$

$$
Q^{-}=\sum_{i} \Delta \rho(r i) for \Delta \rho(r i)<0
\tag{5}
$$

$$
d z=\left.\frac{\sum_{i} \Delta \rho(r i) z}{Q^{+}}\right|_{\Delta \rho(r i)>0}-\left.\frac{\sum_{i} \Delta \rho(r i) z}{Q^{-}}\right|_{\Delta \rho(r i)<0}
\tag{6}
$$

where $d_{z}$ represent the average dipole length, and $z$ is the coordinate value of the corresponding atom (the $z$ axis is denoted in Fig. 5 and $z=0$ is defined at the midpoint of the clean surface). The summation run over all points $\{\mathbf{r}_{i}\}$ of atoms in the whole slab model. After the average dipole charge $Q^{ \pm}$ is calculated, the surface dipole moment $P_{z}$ can be further obtained through $P z=|Q^{ \pm}| × d z$, where $d_{z}$ is the average dipole length.

The calculation results of $d_{z},|Q^{ \pm}|$ and $P_{z}$ are shown in Fig. 6. Re sults show that dipole moment with direction from vacuum to surface exists in all the model surface. It is found that, for the same adsorption sites, the average dipole length $d_{z}$ in the Cs-up models is slightly shorter than that in the NF₃-up models, indicating that the electrons from Cs atom are more attractive to NF₃ than the GaAs surface. In the NF₃-up models, the Cs atom prefers to exchange electrons with surface GaAs atom rather than NF₃ molecule. It is inferred that the length of [Cs⁺- NF₃⁻] dipole is shorter than that of the dipole formed by Cs and surface GaAs atom. Moreover, it is noted that the average dipole charge $|Q^{ \pm}|$ in the Cs-up model are remarkably larger than that in the NF₃-up models at the five adsorption sites. In other words, the adsorbed NF₃ below Cs can weaken the dipole between Cs and surface GaAs atom and in turn attract electrons from surface GaAs atom, which finally results in the increase of dipole moments and the decrease of work function in the Cs-up models. Meanwhile, the adsorption sites can affect the dipole moment. It is clear to see that the strongest dipole appears at the site H' and the weakest one appears at the site T3', which reflects the analo- gous change of work function as shown in Fig. 3.

### 3.3. Surface geometric structure

The geometric structure of the GaAs (100)-β2 (2 × 4) reconstructed surface can undergo the change by the adsorption of Cs atom and NF₃ molecule. As presented in above Fig. 1, the thickness of the first bilayer, the thickness of the second bilayer, the spacing between the first bilayer and the second bilayer are labeled as $D_1$, $D_2$, and $D_{12}$, respectively. For the Cs-up and NF₃-up surface models, the distances between the Cs atom and the NF₃ molecule are defined as $D_{\text{Cs - NF}_3}$ and $D_{\text{NF}_3\text{ - Cs}}$, respectively, where the coordinate position of the NF₃ molecule is treated as the average position of N atom and three F atoms. The calculated values of surface geometric structure after Cs-NF₃ co-adsorption are given in Fig. 7. It is found that $D_{12}$ and $D_2$ are almost unchanged because adsorbed atoms have little effect on the atoms located in the deep position of cathode material. Besides, $D_1$ is slightly decreased in the Cs-up models while that is slightly increased in the NF₃-up models. The possible reason is that in the structure of NF₃-up models, the bond-unsaturated As atoms in the first bilayer tend to receive more electrons from adjacent Cs atom and become more negatively charged, which can cause the As atoms attracted by the positively charged Cs atom and move toward the Cs atom. Whereas, in the Cs-up models, this kind of attractive force gets much weaker with the Cs atom far away from the surface. At the same time, the negatively charged As atoms move toward the Ga atoms under the repulsive force from the negatively charged NF₃ molecule, which results in the slight decrease of $D_1$. Furthermore, the most obvious difference between Cs-up and NF₃-up models is that on the whole, $D_{\text{Cs - NF}_3}$ in the Cs-up models is about 1–2 Å shorter than $D_{\text{NF}_3\text{ - Cs}}$ in the NF₃-up models. Differing from the electrically neutral NF₃ molecules in the NF₃-up models, the negatively charged NF₃ pulls Cs closer to itself in the Cs-up models, which explains the shorter distance between Cs and NF₃ in the Cs-up models. In combination with the charge distribution listed in Table 1, the shorter distance between Cs and NF₃ indicates that when Cs is located above NF₃, the NF₃ molecule can get more negatively charged, and the preferred $[\text{Cs}^+$-NF₃$^-]$ dipole will be formed, which is beneficial to the photoemission of GaAs photocathodes. Therefore, it is believed that the Cs-NF₃ co-activation process should end with the Cs exposure rather than the NF₃ exposure [14].

### 4. Conclusion

In this work, the adsorption characteristics of Cs atom and NF₃ molecule on GaAs(100)-β2 (2 × 4) reconstructed surface is investigated through first-principles on the basis of DFT. Ten adsorption sites with two oppositely vertical structures are involved and the calculation results indicate that the adsorption order of Cs/NF₃ in the vertical direction significantly impacts the surface properties. The Cs-up adsorption structure has the lower adsorption energy and work function than the NF₃-up adsorption structure, which means that at the end of the Cs-NF₃ co-adsorption process, the Cs exposure should be supplied rather than the NF₃ exposure. Among the ten adsorption sites in the Cs-up models, the topmost As-dimer bridge site D with the highest adsorption energy is the most unstable for adsorption, while the trench site T4 has the lowest adsorption energy. When Cs-NF₃ is adsorbed at the hollow site H′, the maximum work function drop can be obtained. In addition, the analyses of charge distribution, dipole moment and atomic geometry show that the $[\text{Cs}^+$-NF₃$^-]$ dipole is formed in the Cs-up structure. Unlike that in the Cs-up structure, the NF₃ molecule in the NF₃-up structure can hardly accept any electrons and only the $[\text{Cs}^+$-As$^-]$ dipole is formed. On the whole, the sites T4, T3 and H′ with stronger dipole moment and lower adsorption energy are relatively suitable for Cs-NF₃ adsorption on this GaAs(1 0 0) reconstructed surface with the Cs-up structure. It needs be noted that the NEA surface cannot be achieved by single Cs/NF₃ adsorption, and the adsorption of more cesium atoms and NF₃ molecules will be investigated to guild the optimal ratio of Cs/NF₃ in the future work.
