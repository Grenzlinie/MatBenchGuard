# Electronic study of carbon nanotube (6,0) doped with transtition metals: Copper, silver and gold

S. Dasilva* and R. López-Planes
Laboratorio de Física de Metales, Departamento de Física, Escuela de Ciencias, Universidad de Oriente, apartado postal 245, Cumaná, Venezuela

Abstract. In view of the unique chemical and physical properties of CNTs, it was proposed a theoretical study of carbon nanotube doped with metals: Cu, Ag and Au, to observe the electronic variations occasioned. Calculations were done using a Hybrid Functional (B3PW91) of Functional Density Theory and the LANL2DZ basis set. In all structures, it was calculated: band gap energy of the electrical conduction, electric charges NBO and adsorption energy of metals at the nanotube. It was found that in all positions of the metallic dopants, the value of the gap energy is increased. The metal position with small gap energy, the copper, was outside of the tube, however, for silver and gold it was inside. This indicates that it exists an intrinsic relationship of each metal with the nanotube. For metallic adsorption, there was a tendency to form physical bond interactions with the carbon nanotube.

Keywords: Nanotubes, transition metals, adsorption, doped

## 1. Introduction
Among the allotropic forms of carbon are found the carbon nanotubes. Their hexagonal lattice cylindrical structure has been of interest as one of the new materials, most coveted in areas of pure and applied sciences, because they possess excellent mechanical and thermal properties [1-4], and they are an excellent candidate for electronic applications [5,6]. One of the most striking features of these materials is that their properties are due to their structural arrangement governed by the chiral vector:

$$\boldsymbol{C}=n \boldsymbol{x}+m \boldsymbol{y} \tag{1}$$

where, $y$ are the primitive vectors of graphene and $(n,m)$ are the integral [7]. Through the estimation of integration values: $n$ and $m$ can be determined by diameter, defined by:

$$d_{t}=\frac{a\sqrt{n^{2}+nm+m^{2}}}{\pi \left|\boldsymbol{C}_{h}\right|} \tag{2}$$

is the distance between two carbon atoms with $sp^{2}$ hybridization. It's approximately $1.4200\ \mathring{A}$ and chiral angle $0^{\circ}$ is zigzag $(n,0)$, $30^{\circ}$ is armchair $(n,n)$ and between $0^{\circ}$ and $30^{\circ}$ are chiral $(n,m)$ [8].

## 4. Conclusions

All metals showed significant changes in the electronic structure of the carbon nanotube (6.0). In the case of copper, the increase in tube diameters was lower compared with the silver and gold, suggesting that there is an electronic resonance damping in the process of backbonding among them. For adsorption energies, the best place to generate bonds was inside the tube, result of stability generated by back bonding among metals and carbon nanotube. Best adsorptions it was performed by copper-doped, suggesting that the atomic radius metal plays an important role in the formation of interaction with the nanotubes, because as it increases its electronic period there is a decrease of adsorption energy. It was noted that all positions of the metallic dopants increased the value of Eg of pristine structure. The smaller gap position in the case of copper was the site Outside, for silver and gold it was the Inside site. This suggests that there is an intrinsic relationship of each metal with the nanotube.
