# A Monolayer Composite of h-BN Doped by a Nano Graphene Domain: as Sensitive Material for $\text{SO}_2$ Gas Detection

Guanya Wang, Jiabing Yu, Kai Zheng, Yexiong Huang, Xiandong Li, Xianping Chen, Member, IEEE and Lu-Qi Tao, Member, IEEE

Abstract—The development of two-dimensional materials with gas-sensitive properties has been a research focus in the field of materials science. Considering the excellent properties of graphene and hexagonal boron nitride, a monolayer composite is put forward and its potential for gas sensing has been investigated by density functional theory. Compared to common gases in the atmosphere and three other toxic gases, sulfur dioxide tends to have a strong mutual effect with this composite. Together with electronic analyses, it is proved that the composite presents excellent sensitivity toward sulfur dioxide molecules and the conductivity can be used as a vital parameter to diagnose the status of gas sensor. What's more, a co-adsorption model demonstrates a favorable performance in high moisture working environment. Thus, we can reasonably draw the conclusion that the presented composite could be an eligible candidate for sulfur dioxide gas detection.

Index Terms—First principles calculations, gas sensors, $\text{SO}_2$ detection, two-dimensional materials.

## I. INTRODUCTION
GAS sensors are of great significance for environmental protection and safety inspection. Sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$) is a poisonous and colorless pollutant. This gas not only generates serious harm to human health and environment [1], but also corrodes metal components of industrial equipment [2, 3].

Therefore, $\text{SO}_2$ gas sensor with high performance is crucial to security assurance and industrial monitor [4].

Sensing material plays a vital role in the performance of gas sensors [5]. During the past several decades, various materials have been exploited for gas sensing [6-10], among which two-dimensional materials have been showing great potentials because of their large surface-to-volume ratio and excellent physical properties [11]. In 2004, the first exfoliation of graphene [12] caused great repercussions in materials science. The prominent electrical properties of graphene have propelled massive efforts in exploring graphene-like analogues [13]. Hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN), a structural analogue of graphene, has very high thermal and chemical stability with a wide band gap [14]. Thus, its chemical inertness brings about the sluggish response of pure h-BN toward gas molecules. For the purpose of increasing the activity of h-BN to specific gases, many strategies have been proposed, including applying an external electric field [15], doping a heteroatom [16], constructing composite with other materials [17] and so on. To the best of our knowledge, setting up hybrid system combining heterogeneous materials is an effective way to gain desired properties [18]. Particularly, the hybridized domain composed of graphene and h-BN make a remarkable effect in band gap engineering [19-21].

In this letter, we assemble a two-dimensional composite by introducing a nano graphene domain, namely a pair of graphene-like C atoms, to h-BN in a substituted way. Then, its gas sensing performance is comprehensively investigated through density functional theory (DFT). Obtained results exhibit a strong interaction between $\text{SO}_2$ molecule and the composite. Contrast with atmospheric gases, i.e. nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$), oxygen ($\text{O}_2$), carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$), vapor ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), and three common toxic gases, i.e. nitric oxide (NO), nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$), carbonic oxide (CO), adsorption process of $\text{SO}_2$ on the composite is accompanied by biggish energy release and moderate charge transfer, which indicates it possesses great potential in $\text{SO}_2$ sensing applications.

$$E_{\text{ads}}=E_{(\text{sub+gas})}-E_{\text{sub}}-E_{\text{gas}} \tag{1}$$
where $E_{(\text{sub+gas})}$ represents the total energy of adsorption system, $E_{\text{sub}}$ and $E_{\text{gas}}$ denote the energy of pure substrate and isolated gas molecule. The more negative the adsorption energy, the more intense the interaction.

### III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The constructing mode of the composite from graphene and h-BN, marked as CBN-composite, is exhibited in Fig. 1. A set of B-N atoms in h-BN is replaced by a pair of C atoms with $sp^2$ hybridization. After geometry is fully relaxed, the honeycomb structure has a slight change with irregularly deformed hexagons. The C-C bond length of 1.386 Å in CBN-composite is shorter than either C-C bonds of graphene or B-N bonds of h-BN, which may be ascribed to the inhomogeneity of radius and electronegativity. What counts is that band gap energy is situated at a moderate value of 3.029 eV, which gives evidence of an obvious band gap regulation effect of the nano graphene domain on h-BN [16], and it facilitate electron transition from valance band to conduction band so that the C heteroatoms would increase chemical reactivity of pure h-BN accordingly. Meanwhile, redistribution of electrons caused by heteroatoms signifies possibility of appropriately large electron deficient region, where certain gases might have a tendency to be located at.

Adsorption behaviors of aforementioned eight gases on CBN-composite were simulated to investigate its sensing performance. For the purpose of preferably imitating all sorts of random cases in the actual adsorption process, all typical pre-adsorption models were initially constructed by considering adsorption site on substrate and orientation relation between gas molecule and substrate. Fig. 2 presents eight structurally optimized configurations with the most negative adsorption energy and depicts the adsorption energy and the charge transfer of corresponding adsorption configurations. For the optimal adsorption configuration of $N_2$, $O_2$, $CO_2$ and CO on CBN-composite, attributing to weaker van der Waals forces, gas molecule bonds are almost unvaried and the shortest distances between adsorbate and adsorbent are still at a large number. In conjunction with smaller magnitude energy changes, negligible transferred charges mean CBN-composite is undiscerning to these gases. In the case of $SO_2$, $H_2O$, NO and

Even though gas molecules are pulled toward the substrate by stronger attraction, the shortest distances are still longer than the sum of covalent radii [26] of corresponding atoms. Thus, no chemical bonds are formed in these four adsorption processes. On the basis of other researcher's studies, the adsorptive action of both pristine graphene and h-BN toward $SO_2$ are relatively weaker, with the binding energy of -0.246 eV [27] and -0.36 eV [28], respectively. By contrast, the hybrid system shows a better adsorptive effect toward $SO_2$ by releasing 0.47 eV energy. Since these four gases with stronger reaction all are prone to be situated above the pair of C atoms, we can view this doping region as an active site for adsorption. More significantly, in the light of a published report, the ideal adsorption energy should be in the range of 0.42-0.83 eV for a high-performance adsorbent [29]. Among these researched gases, only the interaction between $SO_2$ and CBN-composite is better enough to release this level of energy.

So as to gain further insights into the electronic properties of the interaction between $SO_2$ and CBN-composite, we calculated the partial density of states (PDOS) and the ELF, as shown in Fig. 3. It is clear that $SO_2$ molecule contribute a huge sharp peak to the electronic levels of total system under the conduction band minimum. Meanwhile, coupled states near Fermi level is another demonstration for strong interaction between $SO_2$ and CBN-composite. According to the following formula [30], the electric conductivity ($\sigma$) of CBN-composite would increases with the decrease of band gap value ($E_{\text{g}}$):

$$
\sigma \propto \mathrm{exp}(-E_{\mathrm{g}}/2kT) \tag{2}
$$

where $k$ is the Boltzmann constant and $T$ is the temperature. Because the adsorbate would lead to an impurity state in band gap, a large change of electric conductivity is expected due to the presence of $SO_2$. Consequently, it is desired the electric conductivity can be used as an important parameter to judge the status of gas sensor. Furthermore, from the ELF plots, we can see that there is no electron localization overlap between $SO_2$ and CBN-composite although a big deal of electron localization is concentrated. Its nature of physisorption indicates adsorbed $SO_2$ molecules could be released by means of physical desorption, thereby realizing the cycle use of gas sensor.

The humidity condition is inevitably a key factor affecting sensors performance on account of their widespread employment in various application scenarios. Considering the adsorption of $H_2O$ on CBN-composite may be strong enough to weaken the sensing effects toward $SO_2$, the co-adsorption situation of these two gases is simulated. Fig. 4 displays the co-adsorption results. From a visualized point of view, there is no significant difference for $SO_2$ between co-adsorption and single adsorption, while $H_2O$ is dislodged away from the active site. In terms of energy change, $SO_2$ still in the ideal range for a well adsorption behavior, while the energy released by $H_2O$ in co-adsorption system is less than half of single adsorption. We can deduce that the presence of $H_2O$ would not fluctuate the sensitivity of CBN-composite to $SO_2$, and, on the contrary, $SO_2$ would inhibit the adsorption of $H_2O$ on substrate. It can also be proved by the difference of redistributed charge magnitude in different situation. Hence, it can be theoretically concluded that gas sensor could still acquit itself well in high humidity circumstance.

## IV. CONCLUSIONS

In summary, DFT calculations demonstrate the monolayer CBN-composite we bring forward by combining graphene and h-BN possesses good electrical properties. Subsequently, a comprehensive investigation on its adsorption behaviors towards a series of gas molecules ($SO_2$, $N_2$, $O_2$, $CO_2$, $H_2O$, NO, NO₂ and CO) proves the selective sensing of CBN-composite toward $SO_2$, which is supported by reasonable adsorption energy and apparent charge transfer. Moreover, the impurity state appears near the Fermi level bear out the fairly strong interaction between $SO_2$ and CBN-composite, and adsorbed $SO_2$ can be detected by the change of conductivity. In the end, a co-adsorption model is built, and the results refute the fluctuation of water molecules on sensing performance. Based on these results, this composite shows up distinguished capability and well applicability in the field of $SO_2$ gas sensing.
