## ABSTRACT

The development of two-dimensional materials rise new options on the promising application of biosensors and DNA sequencing. In particular, molybdenum disulfide ($MoS_2$) material has become the focus of current research due to its unique properties and advantages. An in-depth understanding of the interfacial interaction between materials and biomolecules is critical in biomedical field. Therefore, the directional selection of double-stranded DNA (ds-DNA) on the $MoS_2$ surface was studied by molecular dynamics (MD) simulation in this work. The simulation results showed that double-strand DNA (ds-DNA) was vertically adsorbed on the $MoS_2$ surface through terminal bases, and the main driving forces is Van der Waals force. In addition, our results indicate that the length and base sequence of ds-DNA are independent of its selection of vertically adsorbed orientation, which may be beneficial for improving the development of fast DNA sequencing technology through MoS2 nanopore in the future. Moreover, the ds-DNA structure is still stable after adsorption on the surface of $MoS_2$. These simulation results greatly enhanced our understanding on the interaction between ds-DNA and $MoS_2$ and may promote the promising application of $MoS_2$ in the field of biosensor and DNA sequencing.

## 1. Introduction

Along with the discovery of the double helix structure [1], the decoding of the genetic code, and the mapping of the first complete genome have been studied by scientists [2]. The human awareness about the importance of DNA sequencing have been increased. In particular the rapid development of nanopore sequencing technology gives us a new option for disease diagnosis and treatment [3,4]. New-type nanomaterial and bioinformatics have provided new ideas and methods for biology, medicine and pharmacy [5-7]. Nanomaterial has extraordinary electronic characteristics, excellent mechanical strength, and large specific surface areas etc [8]. The electrical signals are generated by interacting with biomolecules, and the signal information is transmitted and outputted by the sensing system, which is of great significance for detecting biomolecules such as proteins and nucleotide sequences [9,10]. In recent years, with the discovery of layered graphene [11], the world of two-dimensional nanomaterial has been opened up, especially in the application of DNA sequencing and protein detection. Two-dimensional nanomaterial, such as graphene and transition metal sulfide, have unique physical, chemical and mechanical properties [12-14], which is advantageous for establishing biosensors. Nanometer sized graphene materials have been studied and applied in many aspects, such as drug carrier, protein detection, DNA or RNA sequencing [15-18] and so on. However, because of the graphene material structural defects that there is no inherent band gap [19], electronic applications are limited. Among the researches on many ultra-thin two-dimensional materials, $MoS_2$ of the transition metal disulfide compounds has become an excellent candidate to replace graphene as its innate advantages [8,20]. $MoS_2$ is a molecular layer composed of molybdenum atoms and sulfur atoms by means of covalent bond in the sandwich form of "S-Mo-S" [21]. It is mainly driven by autologous van der Waals force and then stacked together. Because the

var der Waals force between sulfur and molybdenum is weak, the friction coefficient of $MoS_2$ is low, which leads to good lubrication characteristics [22]. Nano-scale $MoS_2$ also has outstanding advantages of large specific surface area, better signal-to-noise ratio [23], easy modification and a 1.8 ev direct band gap [21]. Therefore, biosensors based on nano-level $MoS_2$ can be widely used in electronics [24], biology [25], medicine and other fields [26]. It could well predict the occurrence of nano-level events by detecting a single molecule or cell, so as to make diagnosis and accurate treatment. At the same time, Zhang's team [25] firstly found that single-layer $MoS_2$ nano-sheet had a high fluorescence quenching efficiency. Nano-scale $MoS_2$ sheet could be synthesized massively without being further processed. It greatly pro- motes the chance to develop a simple, fast, and low-cost nano-level probe diagnosis.

Based on this background, it is of great significance to explore the influence of the interfacial interaction between DNA and $MoS_2$. The aim of this work is mainly on the basis of previous study on the limitations of two-dimensional nanomaterial graphene in the electrochemical re- actions and semiconductor sensing application fields and limitation of gene sequencing [27-30]. During the sequencing process, the existence of strong adsorption between graphene and DNA was found, which limits the detection accuracy of DNA sequence information [17,31-33]. Simultaneously, Lu's group [34] found that interfacial interaction and adsorption behavior was weaker between $MoS_2$ and DNA compared with that between graphene sheet and DNA. Yet so far, the adsorption orientation and behavior of DNA on $MoS_2$ surface have been rare considered. The appearance of molecular simulation method [27] could greatly enhanced our understanding on the interaction between nano- material and biomolecules. Besides experiments, as a useful tool, MD simulation could help us to understand the molecular even atomic de- tails. It could help us to elucidate the molecular interaction between biomolecules and materials surface. However, it could not simulate the system as big as we observed in experiment with the limitation of computational resource. For example, the mechanism and dynamics of DNA loading into carbon nanotubes was proposed by Shen's group using MD simulation [15], and they also reveal the adsorption behavior and mechanism of SCA-1 protein on a calcite surface by MD simulation [35]. In this work, MD simulation was mainly used to learn and un- derstand the adsorption behavior and interaction mechanism of DNA on the surface of $MoS_2$.

## 2. Computing models and details

### 2.1. Model setup

The ds-DNA fragment was constructed by HyperChem software (Version 8.0, Hypercube, Inc), which was made up of 24 different deoxidized nucleotides (12-pair bases) with sequence 5'-ATCGATCGA TCG-3' and 3'-CGATCGATCGAT-5', and its structure was shown in Fig. 1(A). A deoxidized nucleotides molecule is consisted of three parts: a nitrogenous base, a deoxidized ribose and a phosphate [1]. The parameters of ds-DNA were taken from Charmm27 force field [36]. The single-layer $MoS_2$ model was built as our previous work [37] with Materials Studio (Accelrys software, Inc.), and it was set in the x-y surface with the center of mass in the origin (0, 0, 0) of the Cartesian coordinate, as shown in Fig. 1(B). The dimensions of a single layer $MoS_2$ was set a fixed value of 8.1 nm × 8.4 nm, and it contains 780 Mo atoms and 1560 S atoms, in which the charge of the Mo atom is 0.76e, and the charge of the S atom is −0.38e [38]. As well, the force field parameters of $MoS_2$ sheet was obtained from references, includes Lenard-Jones parameters $\varepsilon$ and $\sigma$ of Mo and S atoms [39].

### 2.2. Simulation details

The initial configurations were constructed with the orientation of ds-DNA either vertical to the $MoS_2$ layer surface or parallel to the $MoS_2$ layer surface, which denoted as DNA⊥$MoS_2$ and DNA∥$MoS_2$ config- urations. Both of the initial structures were displayed in Fig. 2. The minimum distance between the bottom of ds-DNA and the center of $MoS_2$ surface in z direction (vertical to $MoS_2$ surface) was about 15 Å.

All simulations were carried out in the TIP3P water box with the size of 8.3 nm × 9.6 nm × 8.6 nm by Gromacs-5.0 package [40]. Then, $K^+$ ions were added to the water solution by means of replacing some water molecules to neutralize the negative charge of the DNA. Periodic boundary conditions were used in all the simulations to keep the number of particles constant. After that, a 50000-steps energy mini- mization was carried out for the system and a 200 ns NVT ensemble was simulated for energy minimized system. The cut-off distance of non- bonded interaction was set to be 10 Å [36]. The long-range electrostatic force was calculated by the particle-mesh Ewald (PME) summation method [37]. The temperature was maintained at 300 K in way of Be- rendsen thermostat with a 0.1 ps time coupling constant [41], and the Lincs algorithm was used to constrain all-bonds [42]. The time step was set to be 2 fs. Finally, 200 ns simulation time was performed for the whole model system, and the simulated trajectory file was used to conduct data analysis.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Adsorption phenomenon between DNA and $MoS_2$

MD simulations were carried out to explore the absorption process between ds-DNA and $MoS_2$. One simulation is defined as DNA⊥$MoS_2$, where the main axis of DNA molecules is vertical to the x-y plane of $MoS_2$. The other simulation is defined as DNA∥$MoS_2$, where the main axis of DNA molecules is parallel to the x-y plane of $MoS_2$. As seen in Fig. 3, the terminal base of ds-DNA tends to form vertical orientation with $MoS_2$ surface in both systems after 200 ns MD simulation. This result indicates that ds-DNA tends to adsorbed on the surface of $MoS_2$ in an approximately vertical orientation, which is similar to previous re- search of Gu's group [43].

At the same time, the change of the angle between the central axis of the base pairs of ds-DNA and $MoS_2$ surface was plotted in Fig. 4. As seen in Fig. 4(A), ds-DNA would plumb steadily at about 78° on the $MoS_2$ surface after 100 ns MD simulation. The distance between the center of mass of each-pair bases in ds-DNA relative to $MoS_2$ surface was also measured during last 15 ns time. It was seen from Fig. 4(B) that the distance between the center of mass of each-pair bases and $MoS_2$ sur- face significantly increased from 0.1 nm to 3.4 nm. When the ds-DNA was stably perpendicular to the surface of $MoS_2$, the range change in distance curve was slight fluctuations that was similar to the change of angle between DNA molecule and $MoS_2$ surface. It indicates that the ds- DNA molecule tends to adsorb on $MoS_2$ surface with vertical orienta- tion. It also reveals that the adsorption interaction between DNA and $MoS_2$ surface is not very strong. In the experiment of Lu's group [34], they found weak physical interaction existed between ds-DNA and $MoS_2$, and it was consistent with our simulation result that ds-DNA could vertically adsorb on the surface of $MoS_2$ and constantly moved on the $MoS_2$ surface.

The adsorption residues in the range of 4 Å from $MoS_2$ with its main contributing atomic groups were counted during last 15 ns time. These data of DNA⊥$MoS_2$ and DNA∥$MoS_2$ systems were displayed in the Table 1.

From the Table 1, it could find that the nitrogenous base and deoxyribose atomic groups of ds-DNA interacted with $MoS_2$ surface in the range of 4 Å, and the adsorbed residues of two systems were shown in Fig. 5(A) and (B).

### 3.3. Effects of DNA length and base sequence

The effects of ds-DNA length and base sequence on the adsorption orientation of DNA molecule on the $MoS_2$ surface was also explored. The ds-DNA with sequence 5'-ATGCATGCATGC-3' and 3'-TACGTACG TACG-5' were putted on the top of the $MoS_2$ surface as a control group. To observe the influence of base sequence to the orientation on the $MoS_2$ surface, a new system was established that the sequence of ds-DNA's bases was changed to 5'-GCTAGCTAGCTA-3' and 3'-TAGCTAG CTAGC-5' for a 200 ns MD simulation. As shown in Fig. 7(A), the ds-DNA with the sequence changed was still adsorbed on the $MoS_2$ surface in an approximately $78^\circ$ orientation by terminal-base pairs. It implies that the base sequence was irrelevant to the orientation selection on the $MoS_2$ surface. Similarly, the length of ds-DNA changed as a single variable, and the original ds-DNA with 12 base pairs was considered as a control system, ds-DNA with 8 base pairs and 6 base pairs were set as two new systems to investigate the effect of ds-DNA length on the adsorption. Then, MD simulation was performed for 200 ns time in two new systems, and the orientation and conformation of ds-DNA with different-lengths on the $MoS_2$ surface were obtained, as displayed in Fig. 7(B) and (C). The simulation results show that the ds-DNA was finally adsorbed on the surface of $MoS_2$ by the terminal bases in an approximately $65^\circ$~$75^\circ$ orientation. This result indicates that the adsorption orientation of ds-DNA on the $MoS_2$ surface was independent with the length of the ds-DNA and prefer to adsorbed on $MoS_2$ surface close to vertical orientation. In summary, both of the double-stranded length and the base sequence has little effect to the adsorption orientation of ds-DNA on $MoS_2$ surface. This character may facilitate fast DNA sequencing through $MoS_2$ nanopore with slight orientation adjustment since the DNA is vertically translocate across the solid nanopore in DNA sequencing.

## 4. Conclusion
In this work, the adsorption orientation and behavior of ds-DNA on the surface of two-dimensional nanomaterial MoS₂ were studied by MD simulation. The simulation results showed that the ds-DNA interacts with the MoS₂ surface in a nearly 90° vertical orientation by the terminal bases regardless of the initial orientation. However, due to the relatively weak van der Waals force, the ds-DNA could continuously move on the MoS₂ surface in close to a 90° orientation. In the analysis process, we found that the adsorption orientation of ds-DNA was maintained around 90° from 100 ns simulation. The main driving force is van der Waals force between DNA and the MoS₂, and the ds-DNA structure is still stable after adsorbed on the surface of MoS₂. The orientation choice on the MoS₂ surface of ds-DNA is not affected with characters change of ds-DNA such as double-stranded length and base sequence, which may facilitate fast DNA sequencing through MoS₂ nanopore. The adsorption behavior of ds-DAN on the MoS₂ surface revealed the mechanism of interaction between two substances, paving the way for preparation of MoS₂ nanopore sequencing, which may provide valuable information for the applications of MoS₂ material in the field of biosensor and DNA sequencing.
