# Mixed-mode crack analysis in annular orthotropic planes using distributed dislocation technique

## Problem background
The task addresses the computation of stress intensity factors (SIFs) for mixed‑mode cracks in an annular plane made of a cylindrically orthotropic material (e.g., wood or extruded metals), where elastic properties differ along the radial and tangential directions. Such geometries arise in tree trunks, filament‑wound composites, and extruded cylinders. The analysis covers embedded cracks, inner/outer edge cracks, and multiple interacting cracks of arbitrary orientation, subjected to in‑plane loading. The goal is to implement the underlying theory and numerical solver to produce normalized Mode I and Mode II SIFs for a set of benchmark configurations and for orthotropic cases that demonstrate the effect of material anisotropy.

## Approach
The solution is built on a generalization of the classical Michell solution for cylindrical orthotropy. The Airy stress function for a plane‑stress orthotropic material leads to a fourth‑order partial differential equation that separates into radial and angular parts. The radial equation involves the material parameters β = E₂/E₁ and α = E₂/G₁₂ − 2βν₁₂. Its characteristic roots yield a rich set of fundamental solutions, including power‑law terms like r^(√β±1), r^(±√(1+α+β)), and more complex terms for higher Fourier modes. For a Volterra‑type edge dislocation (climb or glide) in an infinite orthotropic plane, closed‑form stress components are obtained. To satisfy the traction‑free boundary conditions on the inner and outer radii of an annular plane, the stresses generated by the dislocation are expanded in Fourier series on those circles, and the appropriate Michell solution terms with unknown coefficients are superimposed. This leads to a linear algebraic system for the coefficients, which is solved explicitly. For a cracked annular plane, the distributed dislocation technique is employed: cracks are modelled by continuously distributing climb and glide dislocation densities along the crack faces. The stress field due to all dislocations is combined with the intact stress field caused by the applied boundary tractions, and the traction‑free condition on each crack surface yields a system of Cauchy singular integral equations. These equations are discretized using Lobatto–Chebyshev quadrature with collocation points on the cracks and, for embedded cracks, supplemented by closure conditions. The resulting linear system is solved for the dislocation densities, from which the Mode I and Mode II SIFs are computed via the crack‑tip stress‑field formulas. The implementation also requires careful truncation and possible convergence acceleration of the Fourier series. The workflow first validates against isotropic reference cases (embedded radial crack under pure Mode I and Mode II, inner edge radial crack under both modes) and then computes SIFs for orthotropic annular planes (using material properties of Balsa and Douglas‑fir, with an isotropic counterpart as a baseline) under a prescribed non‑uniform inner‑edge loading.
