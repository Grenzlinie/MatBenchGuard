# Atomic scale friction of nanoscale clusters

Keita Ohno, Toshihiro Nitta, Jun Nakamura, $^{\text{a)}}$ and Akiko Natori $^{\text{b)}}$

Department of Electronic-Engineering, The University of Electro-Communications, 1-5-1 Chofugaoka,
Chofu, Tokyo 182-8585, Japan

(Received 19 January 2001; accepted 11 March 2004; published 18 August 2004)

We study both the activation energy for diffusion and dynamics of nanoscale clusters of a Frenkel–Kontrova chain subject to the microscopic friction, in connection with atomic friction phenomena. We clarify the atomistic mechanism for the presence of a magic size for diffusion and show how the magic size effects appear in the friction. When a cluster is pulled by a constant driving force, the critical force strength of depinning depends crucially on the cluster size, and it is smallest at the magic size. When a cluster is pulled by a spring at a constant velocity below the critical value, clusters show a stick-and-slip motion, and the maximum force of a spring also becomes smallest at the magic size. © 2004 *American Vacuum Society*. [DOI: 10.1116/1.1771681]

## I. INTRODUCTION

Scientists and engineers have long been intrigued by the phenomena of friction, and an ideal regime of friction, *su-perlubricity*, where friction completely vanishes, was found recently for incommensurately contacting surfaces sliding against each other. $^{1,2}$ On the other hand, possibilities of nearly frictionless sliding without energy dissipation were also suggested for mesoscopic solids interacting with anhar-monic forces, when they slide relative to each other. $^{3,4}$ This is caused by suppression of energy dissipation due to phonon excitation at a mesoscopic size less than the critical size. Sliding friction on solid surfaces was measured at the atomic scale by the quartz crystal microbalance method for xenon film on Ag(111) as a function of xenon coverage between 0.2 and 2.6 monolayers. $^{5}$ In the submonolayer coverage, the fric tion coefficient decreases as coverage decreases. This means that atoms in the gas phase slide more readily than atoms in the solid phase. Molecular dynamics simulations for this sys-tem reproduced the observed coverage dependence of the friction coefficient and demonstrated that the friction is dominated by phonon excitation for xenon film on $Ag(111).^{6}$ However, sliding friction of an individual nanoscale cluster on solid surfaces has not yet been resolved. The friction of nanoscale clusters is also an important issue in relation to nanomachines. $^{7}$

Hamilton, on the other hand, showed the presence of a magic size of clusters for diffusion, $^{8}$ based on the one dimensional Frenkel–Kontrova (FK) model. The activation energy for diffusion is smallest for the island size where the minimum energy configuration changes from zero to one dis-location. However, the way in which the magic size is related to the lattice misfit between an equilibrium interatomic dis-tance of a cluster and the lattice constant of a substrate and to ratio of the interatomic interaction to the interaction with a substrate is not clear.

On the other hand, the FK model subject to the micro-scopic friction has been applied extensively to studies of friction. The nonlinear response of an adsorbed layer on a periodic substrate to an external force has been studied via a two-dimensional FK model $^{9,10}$ or one-dimensional FK model. $^{11,12}$ Without control, the FK model exhibits four dif ferent regimes: (1) pinning state, (2) periodic stick–slip, (3) chaotic, and (4) periodic sliding, by changing only the initial condition of a cluster. $^{13}$ Furthermore, control method of fric tion of nanoscale clusters by external force has also been proposed $^{13}$ on the one-dimensional FK model. However, the interrelation between friction and the size effect of clusters has not yet been thoroughly investigated.

Our aim is to clarify the atomistic mechanism for the presence of the magic size and to investigate how the cluster size effects appear at the magic size in friction of clusters. We calculate the energy barrier for cluster diffusion based on the one-dimensional FK model. We obtained the analytical expression of the energy barrier in the case of strong inter-atomic interaction limit and found the condition of com-pletely vanishing activation energy for diffusion. In the case of intermediate interatomic interaction, we revealed how the magic size is related to the lattice misfit, comparing the nu-merical results with the analytical results. In connection with atomic friction phenomena, we study the cluster dynamics of a driven Frenkel–Kontrova chain subject to the microscopic friction and show how the cluster size effects appear at the magic size in friction.

## II. MODEL

We use a one-dimensional atomic chain in the FK model. The total interaction energy in the one-dimensional FK model is written as follows:

$$
E_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{N} U_{0}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{2 \pi x_{i}}{a}\right)\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1} \frac{k}{2}\left(x_{i+1}-x_{i}-b\right)^{2}.
\tag{1}
$$

The first term of Eq. (1) is a periodic potential from a sub-strate with a lattice constant $a$ and the second term is an elastic interaction between atoms with an elastic constant $k$ and an equilibrium interatomic distance $b$. Hereafter, we take $a$ as a unit of length and $U_{0}$ as a unit of energy, and our

system is characterized by three dimensionless parameters: lattice misfit $b/a$, the interaction ratio $ka^2/U_0$ of the elastic energy to the interaction energy $U_0$ with a substrate, and the cluster size $N$.

The dynamics of a cluster in the FK model is generally described by the Langevin equation:

$$
m\ddot{x}_i+m\mu\dot{x}_i=-\frac{\partial E_t}{\partial x_i}+F+f_i, \tag{2}
$$

where $m$ is the mass of an atom, $\mu$ is the linear friction coefficient representing the single particle energy exchange with the substrate, $E_t$ is the total interaction energy given by Eq. (1), and $F$ is the applied external force. Here, $f_i$ is a random force that is related to the linear friction coefficient $\mu$ by the fluctuation dissipation theorem $\langle f_i(t)-f_{i'}(t')\rangle =2\mu\ m k_B T\delta(t-t')$. Here, $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant and $T$ is an absolute temperature. In the latter part of this article, we take $m$ as unity and show only numerical results at $T=0$.

## III. ANALYSIS OF A MAGIC SIZE

We can calculate the activation energy for cluster diffusion analytically if the elastic constant $k$ is infinity, since no relaxation of interatomic distance is allowed and contribution of the second term in Eq. (1) vanishes. The coordinate of the $j$th atom in a cluster is given as $x_j=x_0-b(j-1)$, and hence the total interaction energy $E_t$ in Eq. (1) can be calculated analytically as

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_t=&U_0N-U_0\mathrm{Re}\left[\sum_{j=1}^N \exp(2\pi i(x_0-b(j-1)))\right] \\
=&\frac{U_0}{\sin(\pi b)}[N\sin(\pi b)-\cos(\pi(b+2x_0 \\
&-bN))\sin(\pi bN)]. \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

The activation energy $E_a$ for diffusion is given by the difference between the maximum and the minimum values of $E_t$ when $x_0$ is varied

$$
E_a=2U_0\frac{|\sin(\pi bN)|}{|\sin(\pi b)|}. \tag{4}
$$

When $b$ approaches an integer value $n$ ($b=na$), the activation energy is given as

$$
\lim_{b\rightarrow n} E_a=2U_0N, \tag{5}
$$

since each atom must overcome simultaneously the potential barrier of a substrate. On the contrary, when $bN$ is equal to an integer number $n$, $E_a$ vanishes, i.e., $E_a=0$ at $b=n/N$. This means the presence of the magic size cluster, which has vanishing activation energy for diffusion when the misfit $b/a$ takes a rational number, i.e., $b/a=n/N$. It should be noticed that superlubricity between contacting surfaces with lattice constants $a$ and $b$ occurs for the incommensurate condition, i.e., $b/a$ takes an irrational number. $^{1,2}$

In Fig. 1, the activation energy $E_a$ is shown as a function of lattice misfit $b/a$ for $N=8,9,10$. It is seen that the activation energy of the $N=9$ cluster, for example, vanishes at $b_0=8/9=0.889$ and the activation energy becomes smaller than those of $N=8$ and $N=10$ clusters in a certain range of lattice misfit $b/a$ around $b_0/a$. We show the activation energy $E_a$ in Fig. 2 as a function of cluster size $N$ at $b$ $=0.887$, a little less than $b_0$. It is seen that the activation energy is really smallest at $N=9$. This means $N=9$ is the magic size at this misfit value in the case of strong interatomic interaction limit. In the latter part of this article, we consider the case of $b/a<1.0$ to clarify the situation, although the inverse case can also be treated similarly. Since $b$ is less than unity, the zero activation energy condition for a cluster size $N_0$ is satisfied at $b_0$:

$$
b_0N_0=N_0-n, \tag{6}
$$

where $n$ is a positive integer, i.e., $N_0=n/(1-b_0)$. The range of $b$ around $b_0$ where $N_0$ is a magic size is given by the condition

$$
E_a(N_0)<E_a(N_0\pm1). \tag{7}
$$

Hence, the range of $b$ where $N_0$ is a magic size is given by the next relation

$$
b_0-\frac{1-b_0}{2N_0-1}<b<b_0+\frac{1-b_0}{2N_0+1}. \tag{8}
$$

## IV. NUMERICAL RESULTS

### A. Magic size

In this section, we study the magic size for diffusion in the case of a finite interaction ratio $ka^{2}/U_{0}$. Using numerical methods, the minimum energy configurations for one-dimensional clusters given by Eq. (1) were determined.⁸ Total energies of clusters with a dislocation were calculated by constraining single atoms to remain at a peak in the substrate potential. The typical results of this calculation are shown in Fig. 4 for $N=8,9,10$ in the case of $ka^{2}/U_{0}=800$ and $b/a$$=0.887$. For reference, the analytical result in the case of an infinite interaction ratio is also plotted at $N=9$ as a function of the coordinate of the top atom. The activation energy for diffusion can be calculated from Fig. 4 as the difference between the maximum and minimum energies, and the calculated result in Fig. 2 shows a dramatic minimum at the cluster size of nine atoms in the case of $ka^{2}/U_{0}=800$. In Fig. 2, numerical results for other values of $ka^{2}/U_{0}$ are also plotted and all the cases indicate that $N=9$ is the magic size at $b/a=0.887$. The activation energy for diffusion of $N=7,8$, $9,10,11$ clusters are shown in Fig. 5, as a function of misfit $b/a$ for $ka^{2}/U_{0}=800$. It is seen that a specific size becomes a magic size in a certain range of misfit $b/a$, just as analytical results in the case of strong interaction limit. In Fig. 3, we plot the calculated range of $b/a$ which gives a specific magic size, in the case of $ka^{2}/U_{0}=800$ and 400. Surprisingly, these ranges approximately coincide with those for strong interaction limit. This means the magic size is principally determined by the lattice misfit and the cluster relaxation effect is a subsidiary in this range of interaction ratio $ka^{2}/U_{0}$. The small deviation of the magic size from that for the strong interaction limit is thought to be caused by an increase of an average interatomic distance of a cluster contacted with a substrate, which has a larger lattice constant, i.e., increase of the effective lattice misfit. In Fig. 6, the activation energy for the diffusion of the $N=9$ cluster is shown as a function of the interaction ratio $ka^{2}/U_{0}$, for misfits $b/a=0.9,0.887$, and $0.875$. It is seen that the activation energy itself depends largely on the interaction ratio $ka^{2}/U_{0}$. As $ka^{2}/U_{0}$ approaches infinity, they approach corresponding analytical values. The asymptotic values for $b/a=0.9$ and $0.875$ in Fig. 6 are coincident accidentally, as seen from Fig. 1.

### B. Sliding friction

We calculated numerical solution of Eq. (2) at $T=0$, using the velocity Verlet method. As for the initial condition of a cluster, we adopted the minimum energy configuration since $T=0$ was assumed. When a cluster is pulled by a constant driving force, the critical force strength of depinning depends crucially on the cluster size, and it becomes smallest at the magic size as shown in Fig. 7.

When a cluster is pulled by a spring at a constant velocity $v_{s}$, on the other hand, the driving force $F$ on an atom can be written as $F=-K_{s}(x_{\text{cm}}-v_{s}t).^{9}$ Here, $K_{s}$ is the spring elastic constant and $x_{\text{cm}}$ is the position of the center-of-mass of a cluster. Stick-and-slip motion is obtained for $v_{s}$ less than the critical velocity $v_{c}$ and smooth sliding motion for $v_{s}>v_{c}$. The maximum spring force $F_{m}$ in stick-and-slip motion also becomes smallest at a magic size, as shown in Fig. 8. It should be noted that the energy dissipation occurs through the linear friction coefficient term in Eq. (2).

![](./images/812340794433208321_10.jpg)

FIG. 8. Maximum elastic force as a function of cluster size when a cluster is pulled by a spring at a constant velocity of $v_{s}=0.01\sqrt{U_{0}/m}$. Here, $b/a =0.887$, $ka^{2}/U_{0}=800$, $\mu=3\sqrt{U_{0}/ma^{2}}$, and $K_{s}a^{2}/U_{0}=10$ were assumed.

As for energy dissipation in the sliding state of a cluster when a constant external force $F$ is applied, a preliminary calculation shows that the effective friction coefficient $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ had a shallow minimum at a magic size for $F$ less than the critical value. Here, $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ is defined as $\mu_{\text{eff}}=F/mv_{d}$, where $v_{d}$ is the time averaged drift velocity of a center-of-mass coordinate $x_{\text{cm}}$ of a cluster. $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ takes a larger value than $\mu$ in Eq. (2) because of excitaion of internal oscillation of a cluster, and the difference $\mu_{\text{eff}}-\mu$ gives a measure of internal oscillation. However, more calculation is necessary to demonstrate definitely the magic size effect on sliding friction.

### V. CONCLUSION

We obtained analytically the magic size for cluster diffusion as a function of the lattice misfit, in the case of strong interatomic interaction limit. We showed from numerical calculation that the magic size is determined principally by the lattice misfit and the cluster relaxation effect in the case of a finite interatomic interaction is a subsidiary. Hence, we can predict a magic size for a given lattice misfit. Further, we showed both the critical force of depinning of a cluster in the case of a constant driving force and the maximum elastic force when a cluster is pulled by a spring becomes minima at a magic size. These suggest the magic size cluster is promising to reduce dramatically the friction force.

Now, we are promoting calculation of sliding friction to see the magic size effect.
