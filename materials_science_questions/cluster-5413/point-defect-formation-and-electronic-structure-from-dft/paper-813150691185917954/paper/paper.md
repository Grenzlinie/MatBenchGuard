# Interplay between gadolinium dopants and oxygen vacancies in ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$: A density functional theory plus Hubbard $U$ investigation

Wei Zhang $^{1}$ and Z. F. Hou $^{2,a)}$

$^{1}$Department of Criminal Science and Technology, Nanjing Forest Police College, Nanjing 210023, China
$^{2}$Department of Electronic Science, Xiamen University, Xiamen 361005, China

(Received 7 January 2014; accepted 13 March 2014; published online 26 March 2014)

The influence of gadolinium (Gd) doping on the oxygen vacancy $(V_{O})$ in monoclinic ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ have been studied by the first-principles calculations within the spin-polarized generalized gradient approximation plus Hubbard $U$ approach. It is found that the Gd dopant and $V_{O}$ show strong attractive interaction, resulting in a cooperative effect that the substitution of Gd for Hf $({\text{Gd}}_{{\text{Hf}}})$ would increase the probability of oxygen vacancies generation and vice versa. The ${\text{Gd}}_{{\text{Hf}}}$ is more energetically favorable to be next to the vacancy site of a three-coordinated oxygen (O3), forming a complex defect ${\text{Gd}}_{{\text{Hf}}}+V_{O}$. A single ${\text{Gd}}_{{\text{Hf}}}$ acts a hole donor and passivates the defect states of $V_{O}$. Our results suggest that the decrease of the $V_{O}$-related defect states observed in the photoluminescence spectra of Gd-${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ is because Gd doping passivates the defect states of $V_{O}$, rather than caused by decrease of $V_{O}$ concentration. Our findings would clarify the debate about the influence of Gd doping on the oxygen vacancies in ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$. © 2014 AIP Publishing LLC.

[http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4869539]

## I. INTRODUCTION

The high dielectric constant $(\kappa)$ gate dielectric has been widely studied as alternatives to the conventional gate oxide ${\text{SiO}}_{2}$ in metal-oxide-semiconductor devices. The hafnium-based oxide ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ with $\kappa$ value $\sim 25$ is currently considered as a leading candidate for replacing ${\text{SiO}}_{2}$, because it has good physical properties and the compatibility with standard manufacturing processes. $^{1-5}$ Similar to most transition metal oxides with ionic bonds, the electrical characteristics of ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ are often affected by the oxygen-related defects (e.g., oxygen vacancies) because of the formation of defect states inside the band gap. $^{6-11}$ Besides, ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ is also known as one of promising materials for resistive random access memories (ReRAM) due to the discovery of resistance switching phenomena. $^{12-14}$ It is believed that the localized conductive filaments (CFs) made of $V_{O}$s or metallic ions play crucial roles in the resistive switching behavior. However, the randomly formed CFs lead to the large variations of switching parameters in ReRAM devices. $^{15-17}$ The poor uniformity of resistive switching behaviors of ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ based ReRAM devices still restricts its wide applications. Therefore, it is import to understand the behavior of $V_{O}$s in ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ and so as to find proper ways to control the randomly formed CFs for the applications of ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ in electric device.

Much attention has been paid to the modification of ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ by the incorporation of a third-element such as Al, Sc, Y, La, Gd, and so on, because this doping was shown to passviate the defect states of adjacent oxygen vacancies, to suppresses the Fermi level pinning, to increase the crystallizing temperature, or to increase the dielectric constant. $^{18-38}$ Among them, Gd-doped ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ (Gd-${\text{HfO}}_{2}$) is one of the most widely studied. Losovyj et al. reported that Gd-${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ films deposited on silicon substrates change from monoclinic structure to fluorite cubic one with increasing Gd concentration. $^{21,22}$ This was supported by the results of density functional theory (DFT) calculations that Gd incorporation helps stabilize the cubic phase of ${\text{HfO}}_{2}.^{23}$ The improvement of capacitance voltage $C$-$V$ hysteresis and leakage current density in Gd-${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ have also been observed by several researcher groups. $^{27,30,32,34}$ Despite these extensive studies on Gd-${\text{HfO}}_{2}$, the influence of Gd doping on the $V_{O}$s of ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ is still in dispute. $^{34,35,38}$ Ji et al. reported that the incorporation of ${\text{Gd}}_{2}{\text{O}}_{3}$ into ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ can partly eliminate the negative $V_{O}$s. $^{34}$ Xiong et al. reported that the incorporation of Gd suppress the formation of $V_{O}$s. $^{35}$ However, Zhang et al. found that the Gd doping decrease the formation energy of $V_{O}$ and thus increase the concentrations of $V_{O}$s. $^{38}$ In order to better understand the influence of Gd doping on the behavior of $V_{O}$s and to contribute to the progress of ${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ based ReRAM devices, it is therefore important to clarify these discrepancies both experimentally and theoretically. In this paper, based on the spin-polarized generalized gradient approximation (GGA) plus Hubbard $U$ (GGA+$U$) method, we study the interplay between Gd dopants and $V_{O}$s. Furthermore, we investigate the electronic structures of Gd-${\text{HfO}}_{2}$ with and without $V_{O}$s.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: In Sec. II, we describe the calculation method used throughout this work. In Sec. III, we discuss the point defect of Gd substitution at the Hf site $({\text{Gd}}_{{\text{Hf}}})$, the interaction between ${\text{Gd}}_{{\text{Hf}}}$ and $V_{O}$, and the results for the complex defect ${\text{Gd}}_{{\text{Hf}}}+V_{O}$. Finally, the conclusion is given in Sec. IV.

## II. COMPUTATIONAL METHOD

All the calculations are performed using the projector augmented wave (PAW) method, $^{39}$ as implemented in the VASP code. $^{40,41}$ The exchange-correlation functional is treated by the GGA after Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof. $^{42}$

The obtained band gap $(E_g)$ of bulk monoclinic-HfO₂ ($m$-HfO₂) in our GGA calculations is 3.9 eV, which is smaller than the experimental optical $E_g$ of 5.68 eV.⁴³ To include the strong correlation effects of $4f$ electrons of Gd, we used the spin-polarized GGA plus Hubbard $U$ (GGA+$U$) method.⁴⁴ In the present study, we choose $U\mathrm{=7.5\ eV}$ and $J\mathrm{=0.6\ eV}$ for Gd $4f$ orbital according to Ref. 24, which has shown that the calculated positions of Gd $4f$ states in Gd-HfO₂ and hexagonal-close-packed (hcp) Gd obtained by this setup of $U$ and $J$ parameters are in good agreement with those in experiments.²⁴,⁴⁵ The point defects of $\text{Gd}_{\text{Hf}}$ and $V_\text{O}$ are constructed from a $2×2×2$ (i.e., 96-atom) supercell of $m$-HfO₂. A large plane-wave cutoff of 500 eV is used throughout. Convergence with respect to the $k$-point mesh size⁴⁶ is achieved by using $2×2×2$, $4×4×4$, and $9×9×5$ grids for the calculations of the supercell of $m$-HfO₂, hcp Gd, and hcp Hf, respectively. During the structural optimizations, the convergence criterium for the electronic energy is $10^{-5}$ eV, and atoms are relaxed until the Hellmann-Feynman forces are less than $0.01\ \text{eV/Å}$ while the lattice vectors of the supercell are fixed.

The formation energy $\Omega_f$ of a defect $D$ at a charge state of $q$ in HfO₂ is calculated according to a thermodynamic formula⁴⁷,⁴⁸ as below

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_{f}(D, q)=E(D, q)-E_{0}-\sum_{i} n_{i} \mu_{i}+q *\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}+E_{\mathrm{VBM}}\right), \\
(1)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $E_0$ and $E(D, q)$ are the total energies of the perfect and defective supercells of $m$-HfO₂, respectively. $n_i$ indicates the number of atoms of type $i$ (dopant atoms or constituent ones) that has been added into ($n_i>0$) or removed from ($n_i<0$) supercell when the defect is created. The Fermi energy $\varepsilon_{\text{ F }}$ is measured with respect to the valence band maximum (VBM) of $m$-HfO₂. The average electrostatic potential far from the defect is used as the reference to align the VBM of defective supercells with that of the defect free system.⁴⁹

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### A. Structural properties and transition energy levels of a single $\text{Gd}_{\text{Hf}}$

We first optimize the atomic structure of bulk $m$-HfO₂ and hcp Gd. As shown in Table I, the obtained structure parameters of these two bulk materials agree well with the experimental data⁵⁰⁻⁵² and other theoretical works.⁶,³³ The $\text{Gd}_{\text{Hf}}$ defect is constructed by replacing one of the lattice Hf atoms with a Gd atom. Figure 1 shows the bond lengths between Gd dopant and its nearest neighboring O atoms in the optimized structure of $m$-HfO₂ supercell with a single $\text{Gd}_{\text{Hf}}$. The Gd-O bond lengths in Gd-HfO₂ distribute in the range from $2.21\ \text{Å}$ to $2.37\ \text{Å}$ and they are slightly larger than the Hf-O bond lengths [from $2.05\ \text{Å}$ to $2.25\ \text{Å}$, shown in Fig. 1(a)] in perfect $m$-HfO₂. This suggests that the dopant Gd in Gd-HfO₂ tends to form the local structure characteristics of cubic Gd₂O₃.

$$
\Delta\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}-\mu_{i}^{0}, \tag{2}

where $\mu_{i}^{0}$ is the chemical potential of element $i$ in its most stable elemental phase. The upper boundary of chemical potential is

$$
\Delta\mu_{i}\leq0, \tag{3}

$$

$$
\begin{cases}

\end{cases} \tag{6}

The formation energy of a neutral $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ as a function of the chemical potentials of Gd and Hf in the above range is presented in Fig. 2(c). To incorporate Gd into $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$ more efficiently, the formation energy of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ is expected to at the lower value, resulting in $\mu_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{Gd}}$ to take the lower and upper boundaries, respectively. $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}$ consequently takes the upper limit, i.e., $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}^{0}$. These suggest that under the oxygen-rich condition the use of bulk Gd as dopant will be very efficient to dope Gd into $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$. Because in the thermodynamic equilibrium, the dependence of the concentration $c$ of a defect on its formation energy $\Omega_{f}$ and the temperature $T$ can be described by an expression of $c=c_{0}\exp^{-\Omega_{f}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T}$, where $c_{0}$ is a constant and $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ is Boltzmann's constant, $^{48}$ it is more interesting to examine the boundary for the formation energy of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ at the zero value. $$
\left.
\begin{array}{l}
{[\Delta H_{f}^{0}(\mathrm{HfO}_{2})+\mu_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{0}]\leq\mu_{\mathrm{Hf}}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{Gd}}^{0}-[E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}})-E_{0}],} \\
{[\Delta H_{f}^{0}(\mathrm{HfO}_{2})+\mu_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{0}]+[E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}})-E_{0}]\leq\mu_{\mathrm{Gd}}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{Gd}}^{0},}
\end{array}
\right\} \quad \text{for} \quad \Omega_{f}(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}})\leq0, \tag{7}
$$

where $E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}})$ is the total energy of $m$-HfO$_2$ supercell with a single $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$. From the upper limit of $\mu_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ in Eq. (7) and the requirement in Eq. (4), we can further derive the consequent value of $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}$ for the specific condition that the formation energy of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ is at the zero value, i.e., $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}=1/2$ $[\Delta H_{f}^{0}(\mathrm{HfO}_{2})+\mu_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{0}-\mu_{\mathrm{Gd}}^{0}+E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}})-E_{0}]+\mu_{\mathrm{O}}^{0}$. This value of $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}$ is shown in Fig. 2(b) and it corresponds to an oxygen-deficient condition. Because oxygen vacancies are detected usually in insufficiently oxidized $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$ films containing less oxygen than the stoichiometric $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$, $^{54}$ this value of $\mu_{\mathrm{O}}$ will be used to estimate the formation energy of oxygen vacancy in Secs. III B and III C unless otherwise stated.

Now we turn to discuss the stability of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ at the charged states. Figure 2(d) shows the formation energy of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ at different charge states as function of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}$. The lower limit of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}=0$ eV corresponds to the VBM, while the upper limit of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}=3.90$ eV is the bottom conduction band in the theoretical value of GGA level. The different charge states of the defect are distinguished by different slopes of the lines in Fig. 2(d). We can see that $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ at the charge state of $q=-1$ ($\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{1-}$) is more stable than that at the charge state of $q=-2$ ($\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{2-}$) for a wide range of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}}$. Only when the Fermi level is above the theoretical value of conduction band minimum (CBM), the $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{2-}$ has the lowest formation energy. Our calculations also predict that the reaction $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{2-}+\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{0}\rightarrow$ $2\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}^{1-}$ releases an energy of 4.14 eV. From Fig. 2(d), it is found that the transition levels of $(-2/-1)$ and $(-1/0)$ of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ are $4.107$ eV and $-0.029$ eV, respectively. The latter case is below the VBM, indicating that a single $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ acts as an electron acceptor.

### B. Interaction energies of gadolinium dopants and $V_{\text{Os}}$

It is well known that there are two distinct types of oxygen in $m$-HfO$_2$ according to the coordination, i.e., three-coordinated oxygens (O3) and four-coordinated oxygens (O4). The formation energy of $V_{\mathrm{O}}$ as a function of $\Delta\mu_{\mathrm{O}}$ is presented in Fig. 3(a). Our calculations predict that a neutral $V_{\mathrm{O}4}$ is energetically more stable than a neutral $V_{\mathrm{O}3}$ by $116$ meV, which are consistent with the previous theoretical work. $^{6,33}$ Under the oxygen-poor condition, the formation energy of $V_{\mathrm{O}4}$ is about $1.003$ eV.

To study the influence of gadolinium dopant on the $V_{\text{Os}}$, we calculate the interaction energy $E_{\text{int}}$ between a $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ and a $V_{\mathrm{O}}$ as follows:

$$
E_{\mathrm{int}}=E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}+V_{\mathrm{O}})+E_{0}-E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}})-E(V_{\mathrm{O}}), \tag{8}
$$

where $E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}+V_{\mathrm{O}})$, $E(\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}})$ and $E(V_{\mathrm{O}})$ are the total energies of the supercell containing a complex defect of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}+V_{\mathrm{O}}$, a single $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ and a single $V_{\mathrm{O}}$, respectively. $E(V_{\mathrm{O}})$ is taken as the total energy of a supercell with a single vacancy at the $\mathrm{O}4$ site because $V_{\mathrm{O}4}$ is more stable than $V_{\mathrm{O}3}$. The negative (positive) value of $E_{\text{int}}$ indicates that the interaction between a $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ and a $V_{\mathrm{O}}$ is attractive (repulsive). From Eq. (9), we can regard $E_{\text{int}}$ as the change in the formation energy of oxygen vacancy caused by Gd doping. On the other hand, $E_{\text{int}}$ also means the change in the formation energy of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ caused by the presence of $V_{\mathrm{O}}$, as seen from Eq. (10). The calculated values of $E_{\text{int}}$ versus the distance between $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{O}}$ are presented in Fig. 3(b). It can be seen that the $E_{\text{int}}$ are all negative, indicating the strong attractive interactions between $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{O}}$. They suggest that the formation energy of oxygen vacancy would be reduced significantly after Gd doping, which is consistent with a previous calculation. $^{38}$ The presence of oxygen vacancy would also reduce the formation energy of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$. In undoped $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$, the formation energies of monovacancy at different sites of $\mathrm{O}3$ atoms are almost the same even these $\mathrm{O}3$ atoms have slight different Hf-O bond lengths. In Gd-doped $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$, the most stable configuration of complex defect $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}+V_{\mathrm{O}}$ is formed by the removal of the $\mathrm{O}3$ atom with shortest Gd-O bond length [see Fig. 1(b)]. Figure 3(b) also shows that a neutral $V_{\mathrm{O}3}$ is much more stable than a neutral $V_{\mathrm{O}4}$ in Gd-doped $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$, which is in contrast to the situation in undoped $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$. This is because of the hole doping behavior of a $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}$ that traps the electrons donated by $V_{\mathrm{O}}$ and thus leads to a trend of positive charge state for $V_{\mathrm{O}}$. In undoped $\mathrm{HfO}_{2}$, a $V_{\mathrm{O}3}^{1+}$ is indeed found to be more stable than a $V_{\mathrm{O}4}^{1+}$. $^{6,55-58}$

## C. Electronic structures and relative stability of $\mathrm{Gd}_{\mathrm{Hf}}+V_{\mathrm{O}}$

## IV. CONCLUSION
In summary, the first-principles calculations are performed to study the influence of gadolinium doping on the oxygen vacancy of $HfO_2$. The calculations show that the substitution of Gd for Hf would increase the probability of $V_O$ generation and vice versa. A substitution of Gd for Hf acts as a hole donor and it can passivate the defect state of $V_O$ due to the charge compensation. A substitution of Gd for Hf is energetically favorable to be next to a vacancy site at the three-coordinated oxygen with shortest Gd-O bond length. For the resulting complex defect $Gd_{Hf}+V_O$, its charge state of $q=+1$ is more stable than the charge state of $q=+2$ in the most range of the Fermi level and the defect level of $(Gd_{Hf}+V_O)^{1+}$ locates within the valence band, in contrast to the situation of $V_O$ in undoped $HfO_2$. Our results suggest that the decrease of the $V_O$-related defect states observed in the photoluminescence spectra of Gd-$HfO_2$ is not caused by the decrease of $V_O$ concentration, but because the $Gd_{Hf}$ passivates the defect states of $V_O$ and thus changes the positions of the defect levels. Our results would clarify the debate about the influence of Gd doping on the oxygen vacancies in $HfO_2$.
