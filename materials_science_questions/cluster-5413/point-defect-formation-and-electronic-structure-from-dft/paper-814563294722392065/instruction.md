# DFT comparison of oxygen vacancy formation energy in crystalline and amorphous IGZO

## Problem background
In–Ga–Zn oxide (IGZO) is a semiconductor material widely used in thin-film transistors. Oxygen vacancies (missing oxygen atoms) are believed to create deep defect levels inside the band gap, which can degrade device reliability. Understanding how the crystalline order of IGZO affects the formation energy of these vacancies is important because higher crystallinity may suppress their formation. However, a direct, quantitative comparison of oxygen vacancy formation energies between crystalline and amorphous IGZO, using a common reference energy and a single computation, has not been established. This task reproduces that comparison by constructing a periodic model that contains both a crystalline and an amorphous IGZO slab, enabling a direct first-principles calculation of per-site neutral oxygen vacancy formation energies and the accompanying atomic relaxations.

## Approach
The workflow uses density functional theory (DFT) with the PBE functional and pseudopotentials that treat In 4d, Ga 3d, and Zn 3d electrons as valence. The computational experiment proceeds in three phases:

1. **Build the separate IGZO phases.** A 112‑atom crystalline IGZO model is constructed from the InGaO₃(ZnO) (m=1) unit cell and relaxed. An amorphous 112‑atom model is generated by melting the crystalline cell via *ab initio* molecular dynamics (3500 K, 7 ps) followed by step‑quenching and full relaxation.
2. **Assemble a crystalline/amorphous complex.** The two slabs are joined along the crystalline b-axis to form a 224‑atom supercell. Multiple starting configurations are generated, the structure is fully relaxed, and the lowest‑energy complex model is selected.
3. **Compute vacancy formation energies and displacements.** After obtaining the total energy of the pristine complex model and the chemical potential of oxygen (half the energy of an isolated O₂ molecule), each of the 128 oxygen atoms is removed, the defective structure is relaxed, and the neutral vacancy formation energy is calculated as E_form = E_tot(V_O) − E_tot(no defect) + μ_O. For every vacancy site the root-square-sum of atomic displacements relative to the pristine structure is also computed.

The calculations are designed to reveal how the formation energy varies between the crystalline region (far from the interface, where the lattice is ordered) and the amorphous region, and how atomic relaxations correlate with the energy cost of creating a vacancy.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Construct and relax crystalline IGZO model
- Role: process
- Action: Build a 112-atom crystalline IGZO model based on the InGaO3(ZnO) (m=1) unit cell. Relax atomic positions and cell parameters using DFT (PBE functional, single Γ-point, appropriate plane-wave cutoff). Report the optimized lattice constants.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/crystalline_model.xyz`

### Step 2: Generate amorphous IGZO model via ab initio MD
- Role: process
- Action: Starting from the 112-atom crystalline model, perform NVT ab initio molecular dynamics at 3500 K for 7 ps, then quench stepwise at 500 K/ps. Fully relax the quenched structure. The final amorphous model should have a density of approximately 6.16 g/cm³.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/amorphous_model.xyz`

### Step 3: Assemble and relax the crystalline/amorphous complex model
- Role: process
- Action: Combine the relaxed 112-atom crystalline slab and the 112-atom amorphous slab in a direction parallel to the crystalline b-axis, ensuring no interatomic distances <1.4 Å. Fully relax the 224-atom combined structure. Generate multiple initial arrangements (e.g., 10) and select the one with the lowest total energy.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/complex_model.xyz`

### Step 4: Compute oxygen chemical potential
- Role: process
- Action: Set up an isolated O2 molecule in a large box (≥10 Å vacuum) and compute its total energy using the same DFT settings (PBE, same cutoff, spin-polarized). The chemical potential μ_O is half of the O2 total energy.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/o2_energy.txt`

### Step 5: Compute total energy of the pristine complex model
- Role: process
- Action: Perform a final static DFT calculation on the relaxed complex model to obtain the total energy E_tot(no defect).
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/pristine_energy.txt`

### Step 6: Compute oxygen vacancy formation energies for all oxygen sites
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: For each of the 128 oxygen atoms in the complex model, remove that atom, fully relax the structure, and compute the total energy E_tot(V_O). Using the pristine energy and μ_O, calculate the neutral vacancy formation energy E_form = E_tot(V_O) - E_tot(no defect) + μ_O. Determine the distance from Interface A (the central interface) to the vacancy site; assign a negative distance for atoms in the crystalline region and a positive distance for atoms in the amorphous region.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/oxygen_vacancy_formation_energies.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Columns: site_id (int), region (string: 'crystalline' or 'amorphous'), distance_from_interface_A (float, Å, negative for crystalline side, positive for amorphous side), formation_energy_eV (float).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 7: Compute root-sum-square atomic displacements due to vacancy formation
- Role: scored
- Action: For each oxygen vacancy site, compute the root-sum-square displacement D_j over all atoms except the removed oxygen, using the relaxed positions of the pristine structure and the relaxed vacancy structure. D_j = sqrt( sum_i (r_vacancy_i - r_pristine_i)^2 ), where the sum runs over all atoms except the vacancy site.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/oxygen_vacancy_displacements.csv`
- Format: csv
- Contract: Columns: site_id (int), root_sum_square_displacement_angstrom (float).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

## How you are scored
A hidden checker will read your submitted CSV files and independently calculate two metrics:

1. **Regional formation energy averages.** For oxygen sites located well inside the crystalline region (distance < −5 Å from Interface A) and well inside the amorphous region (distance > 5 Å), it computes the mean formation energy. The observed difference between the two regions is compared to a hidden expected value, and a score is assigned based on how closely the computed average energies match the hidden criteria.
2. **Correlation with displacement.** The checker merges the formation energy and displacement tables by `site_id`, computes the Spearman rank correlation coefficient, and verifies that it satisfies a hidden condition.

Each of these two checks contributes a fraction of the total reward, with the formation energy check carrying the larger weight. The reward is monotonically increasing with solution quality; passing the hidden thresholds (or exceeding them) earns full credit, while results that deviate in the wrong direction receive proportionally lower credit.

Simply printing a pre‑computed number is not sufficient – the verifier recomputes the relevant statistics directly from your CSV data. All steps in the workflow are required, and the final score reflects only the contents of the two scored output files.
