# $\mathbf{F_S^+}$ and $\mathbf{F_S^+(OH^-)}$ defect centers at the $\mathbf{MgO(1\,0\,0)}$ surface: cluster and periodic calculations

Marion Ménétrey $^{\text{a}}$, Alexis Markovits $^{\text{a}}$, Christian Minot $^{\text{a}}$, Annalisa Del Vitto $^{\text{b}}$, Gianfranco Pacchioni $^{\text{b},*}$

$^{\text{a}}$ Laboratoire de Chimie Théorique, Université de Paris VI, 75252 Paris Cedex 05, France
$^{\text{b}}$ Dipartimento di Scienza dei Materiali, Istituto Nazionale per la Fisica della Materia, Università di Milano-Bicocca, Via R. Cozzi, 53-20125, Milano, Italy

Received 22 October 2003; accepted for publication 5 December 2003

## Abstract

We present a model of a new paramagnetic defect center which results from the interaction of atomic hydrogen with the MgO(1 0 0) surface. DFT calculations have been performed using periodic supercells and embedded cluster models where long-range polarization effects are included explicitly. The H atom promotes the creation of an oxygen vacancy (F center) by formation of the $F_S^+(OH^-)$ defect where an hydroxyl group is adsorbed near an electron trapped in an oxygen vacancy. This new center has some characteristics similar to those of the classical $F_S^+$ centers but a smaller formation energy; furthermore, being globally neutral, it can be treated also with supercell methods.

## 1. Introduction

Color centers at the MgO surface have been subject to a critical discussion in recent years [1-7]. These centers affect the chemical and physical properties of the surface. For example, the nearly defect-free MgO(1 0 0) single crystal surface is chemically inert towards molecular adsorbates that physisorb only at very low temperature [8,9]; on the contrary, a polycrystalline surface rich in color centers is highly reactive and promotes the formation of complex radical anions [10]. The natural abundance of color centers in high surface area MgO samples prepared by thermal decomposition of $Mg(OH)_2$ or $MgCO_3$ is too low to be monitored by standard spectroscopic techniques, like Electron Paramagnetic Resonance (EPR) or UV-VIS. On the other hand, the surface contains several defect sites, mostly of morphological nature, that can act as electron traps and lead to the formation of color centers. The coloring of MgO samples has been associated in the past with the presence of oxygen vacancies, or F centers, which can be neutral (F center, missing O atom), singly

charged (paramagnetic $F^{+}$ center, missing $O^{-}$ ion), or doubly charged ($F^{2+}$ center, missing $O^{2-}$ ion). Often the trapped electron is near an adsorbed proton, and the term $F_{S}(H^{+})$ center has been created to identify these defects [11]. The model of $F_{S}$ centers (the subscript S indicates that the center is located on the surface) was proposed by Tench and Nelson [12,13] more than 30 years ago as the obvious extension of the structure F centers in the bulk [14,15]. The existence of $F_{S}$ and $F_{S}^{+}$ centers has been proven on the surface of thin MgO films where they give rise to typical absorption bands measured in EELS spectra [16,17]; the nature and position of these bands has been assigned with the help of sophisticated ab initio calculations [18,19].

Things are partly different on polycrystalline materials. Here the number of color centers can be increased by X- or UV-rays bombardment [20,21], or by using softer methods like the deposition of alkali metals [22] or atomic hydrogen [6]. These species transfer their valence electron to a pre-existing trapping site giving a paramagnetic center and an adsorbed cation [23–25]. Atomic hydrogen is generated from molecular hydrogen which dissociates into a proton, $H^{+}$, and a hydride, $H^{-}$ [6,7]; under UV-irradiation $H^{-}$ transforms into a neutral H atom (which desorbs or further reacts) and an electron trapped near the $H^{+}$ ion. Therefore, these centers consist of a $(H^{+})(e^{-})$ pair stabilized at a low-coordinated MgO sites [6,7]. In this respect, the concept of F center has disappeared completely (in fact these centers are neutral and do not involve oxygen vacancies). These new models explain the presence of a large number of paramagnetic centers in additively colored samples [6,7]. In general, hydrogen is present on the surface of MgO in the form of OH groups or is introduced on purpose to create new color centers. It is only after high thermal treatment that all the H atoms are removed from the surface. The role of atomic hydrogen in favoring the generation of oxygen vacancies on oxide surfaces and on MgO in particular has not been addressed so far and is the central topic of this paper.

Another problem addressed in this study is related to the theoretical description of defect centers when the defects are associated to large dipole moments, as for $(H^{+})(e^{-})$ pairs, or are charged, as the $F_{S}^{+}$ centers. In cluster models one can include long-term polarization effects through a shell model embedding; in periodic calculations the treatment of charged supercells poses considerable technical problems and sometimes is simply unfeasible (in particular for surfaces). In this study we have studied the electronic structure of "classical" $F_{S}$ and $F_{S}^{+}$ defects at the surface of MgO by comparing results of embedded cluster and periodic supercell approaches (in this case only $F_{S}$ centers have been treated). We present a model of a new paramagnetic center which results from the interaction of atomic hydrogen with MgO. This new center has some characteristics similar to those of the $F_{S}^{+}$ centers but a smaller formation energy; furthermore, being neutral, it can be treated also with supercell methods.

The paper has been organized as follows. In Section 2 we describe the cluster and the periodic models and the theoretical methods used. Section 3 is divided into two parts. In Section 3.1 we describe the properties and formation energy of $F_{S}$, $F_{S}^{+}$, and $F_{S}^{2+}$ centers. In Section 3.2 we describe the new defect resulting from the adsorption of atomic H on the surface. The conclusions are summarized in the last section.

## 2. Details of calculations

The total energy and the electronic structure of the defect centers are calculated within the density functional theory (DFT) by solving the Kohn-Sham equations using the generalized gradient approximation (PW-91 exchange-correlation functional [26]).

### 2.1. Cluster calculations

The MgO surface is represented by a large $20×20×8$ cluster [27]; the system is divided in two main regions. Region I, in the center of the cluster, contains the quantum-mechanical (QM) part surrounded by interface ions and by a region of polarizable classical shell model ions. Region II is represented by fixed point charges which correctly reproduce the long-range electrostatic potential in region I. An interface between the QM cluster and

classical ions consists of a semi-local effective core potential (ECP) [28] associated with the $Mg^{2+}$ cations. The entire system, region I plus region II, is neutral. This hybrid scheme is implemented in the GUESS code [29] for the shell model part interfaced with the Gaussian98 code [30] for calculation of the QM cluster. The positions of all centers in region I, both QM and classical (cores and shells), have been optimized using the BFGS minimization technique [31].

A classical $F_S$ or $F_S^+$ center is obtained by removing the central O atom in the first layer. The $F_S$ centers at the (1 0 0) terrace were modeled using an $Mg_{25}O_{24}Mg^*_{32}$ QM cluster ($Mg^*$ represent the interface Mg atoms). A smaller QM cluster, $Mg_{13}O_{13}Mg^*_{19}$ has been used to simulate the reaction with an H atom. The standard 6-31G basis set has been used for the Mg and O atoms of the QM cluster [32,33]. No basis functions were set on the interface atoms or in the vacancy.

Optimal geometry, formation energies, spin distribution, hyperfine coupling constants, and optical transitions have been determined. The hyperfine interactions of the electron spin with the nuclear spin of the $^{25}Mg$ and $^1H$ nuclides, $\mathbf{H_{hfc}} = \mathbf{S} \cdot \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{I}$, is given in terms of the hyperfine matrix $\mathbf{A}$ which describes the coupling between the electron and the nuclear spins [34]. The components of $\mathbf{A}$ can be represented as the sum of an isotropic part, $a_{iso}$, related to the Fermi contact term, and the matrix $\mathbf{B}$ which represents the "classical" dipolar interaction between two magnetic (electron and nuclear) moments. Typical anisotropic interactions can be observed when the unpaired electron is in directional orbitals like p, d, f, etc. Here we focus only on the isotropic part of the matrix.

Optical transitions have been determined at the time-dependent DFT level (TD-DFT). In this method excitation energies are calculated from the poles of the frequency-dependent polarizability. Recent studies show that TD-DFT is more accurate than the configuration interaction single (CIS) method [35], and that it can provide transition energies with an accuracy similar to that of multireference configuration interaction or coupled-cluster methods [36]. However, Rydberg states [37] and in general transitions to states which are close to the conduction band edge [36] are poorly reproduced.

### 2.2. Periodic supercell calculations

For the periodic calculations we employed the VASP program [38,39] which uses a plane wave basis set (with a kinetic energy cut-off at 396 eV) and ultrasoft pseudopotentials [40] for the electron-ion interactions. The atoms within the supercell are relaxed until the energy difference between two subsequent steps is smaller than 0.001 eV. The calculations have been performed sampling the Brillouin zone in a $5 \times 5 \times 1$ grid. The MgO(1 0 0) surface has been modeled by three or four MgO layers; during geometry optimization, all atoms in the first surface layer were relaxed while only the vertical position of the atoms in the second layer were optimized. The atomic positions for the third layer were frozen at bulk values. The three-layer MgO slab reproduces well the band structure and the dimensions of the unit cell of bulk MgO. The defect centers have been described using a $2\sqrt{2} \times 2\sqrt{2}$ supercell, containing up to 64 atoms (four-layer film). This supercell corresponds to a defect coverage of 1/8. The surface-slabs are separated by a vacuum slab of $8 \mathring{A}$. The formation energy of an oxygen vacancy has been determined with respect to the energy of an $O(^3P)$ atom computed at the spin polarized level using an asymmetric box of $10 \times 11 \times 12 \mathring{A}^3$.

## 3. Results and discussions

### 3.1. The $F_S^{n+}$ centers

As a test of the reliability of the methods used we have considered the properties of the neutral oxygen vacancy computed in the two approaches, cluster and slab. This center has been the subject of several theoretical investigations [41-45]. The formation energy is defined as $E_f(F_S) = -E(\text{MgO; regular}) + E(\text{O; } ^3\text{P}) + E(\text{MgO; } F_S)$. In the cluster calculations $E_f$ is 9.31 eV, close to the slab value, $E_f = 9.12$ eV. Both values are in line with previously reported estimates [41,44,45]. The energy cost to create the vacancy is reduced if as a

reference we take an O atom adsorbed on the MgO surface; in this case the process considered corresponds to remove an O atom from its lattice position and to adsorb it on the surface to form a peroxo group [46]. Since the adsorption energy of an O atom to the MgO surface is of about 2.3 eV [46], the real cost to create the vacancy is of about 7 eV.

The geometry relaxation in a $F_S$ center is very similar in the cluster and slab approaches, Fig. 1, and presents a modest displacement of the nearest Mg ions outwards the vacancy and of the nearest O ions towards the vacancy. The electronic structure is characterized by the presence of two electrons trapped in the cavity, Fig. 2 [41–44]. The two electrons are associated to an energy state in the gap of the material. This level lies about 2 eV above the top of the valence band, near the middle of the gap, see Fig. 3.

The ionization of this center leads to the formation of a $F_S^+$ paramagnetic defect. The electronic structure of this charged defect can be treated in a periodic approach only with specific procedures not easy to apply and the description is limited to the cluster results. Here the formation energy, defined with respect to the removal of an $O^-$ ion, is $E_{\rm f}({\rm F}_S^+)=-E({\rm MgO};{\rm regular})+E({\rm O}^-;{}^2{\rm P})+E({\rm MgO};{\rm F}_S^+)$. $E_{\rm f}({\rm F}_S^+)=9.77$ eV, Table 1, is not too different from that of the neutral vacancy. The last center considered is the bare oxygen vacancy, ${\rm F_S^{2+}}$. If one defines the formation energy with respect to the removal of an ${\rm O^{2-}}$ ion, $E_{\rm f}({\rm F_S^{2+}}) = -E({\rm MgO}; {\rm regular}) + E({\rm O^{2-}}; {}^1S) + E({\rm MgO}; {\rm F_S^{2+}})$, the cost is of 20.83 eV. Here one should mention that the ${\rm O^{2-}}$ ion is unstable in the gas-phase, so that $E({\rm O^{2-}}; {}^1S)$ is affected by this problem (the energy can be computed with a finite basis set, but the basis set optimization would lead to the detachment of one electron leaving an ${\rm O^-}$ ion). Even considering this uncertainty, the formation energy of the ${\rm F_S^{2+}}$ center is very large, and the number of these centers on the as-grown material should be small. Not surprisingly, the perturbation of the electrostatic potential determined by the formation of the doubly charged center results in large displacements of the ions around the vacancy: the nearest Mg ions move outwards by $0.26\ \mathring{\rm A}$, the O ions move inwards by $0.13\ \mathring{\rm A}$, and the Mg ion in the second layer is displaced by $0.17\ \mathring{\rm A}$. The energy required to ionize the paramagnetic ${\rm F_S^+}$ center to give the ${\rm F_S^{2+}}$ is 4.15 eV. This shows that the ${\rm F_S^+}$ center is thermally very stable.

To summarize this section, the cluster and the periodic models give a very similar description of the neutral ${\rm F_S}$ center, showing that at a surface coverage of 1/8 does not result in a severe interaction of the vacancies. The properties of the ${\rm F_S^+}$ and ${\rm F_S^{2+}}$ centers are in line with previous reports [43–45]. The use of shell model embedding allows one to determine with considerable accuracy the energy required to ionize the neutral ${\rm F_S}$ center to form the paramagnetic ${\rm F_S^+}$ defect: the computed ${\rm IP}=2.18$ eV shows that ${\rm F_S}$ centers are thermally stable at RT, although they could lose one of the trapped electrons at higher temperatures or by reaction with adsorbed molecules.

### 3.2. The ${\rm F_S^+}(OH^-)$ centers

Under the name ${\rm F_S(H)^+}$ one generally includes oxygen vacancies which trap one electron in the vicinity of an adsorbed H atom [1]. Here we discuss a new kind of centers which can be formed by addition of an H atom to the MgO surface. The adsorption of an H atom can promote the formation of an oxygen vacancy, according to the following mechanism:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\rm MgO + H_{gas-phase}^\bullet} &\rightarrow {\rm MgO(H_{ads}^\bullet)} \\
&\rightarrow {\rm MgO(F_S^{+\bullet})(OH^-)}
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
$$

The process can be seen in two steps (the values reported in the following have been obtained with cluster models). First the H atom is adsorbed on a surface O ion forming a paramagnetic ${\rm OH^\bullet}$ group at the surface (the binding energy of the H atom to the surface oxide anion is 0.42 eV); then an ${\rm OH^-}$ ion is displaced from the surface and stabilized at a neighboring acid site like a ${\rm Mg^{2+}}$ cation leaving behind an oxygen vacancy with a trapped electron, i.e. a ${\rm F_S^+}$ center. Since this center is near an adsorbed ${\rm OH^-}$ group we name the center ${\rm F_S^+}({\rm OH^-})$, Fig. 5. In the minimum structure the Mg–OH bond distance is $1.94\ \mathring{\rm A}$ and the Mg–O–H angle $127.6^\circ$. Other geometrical parameters are compared with those of the ${\rm F_S^+}$ center in Fig. 4. The main difference between the ${\rm F_S^+}({\rm OH^-})$ center and the ${\rm F_S^+}$ defect described in the previous section is that (a) here the system is globally neutral and (b) the unpaired electron is near an adsorbed OH group. Another way to see the process is that an ${\rm O^-}$ ion is displaced from its lattice position and adsorbed on top of a ${\rm Mg^{2+}}$ cation; this corresponds to the formation of a ${\rm F_S^+}$ center and an ${\rm O_{ads}^-}$ ion (paramagnetic). Then, an H atom is added to the ${\rm O_{ads}^-}$ ion with formation of a diamagnetic ${\rm OH_{ads}^-}$ species. The interest for this center is twofold. On one side we are dealing with a neutral paramagnetic defect which can be described in a periodic approach; on the other side the energetics of reaction (1) can be of interest for the analysis of the formation of paramagnetic centers at the surface of MgO.

We consider first the cluster results. With this model we found that the structure of the new center, ${\rm F_S^+}({\rm OH^-})$, is a stable minimum when the ${\rm OH^-}$ group is not sitting on one of the Mg cations at the border of the vacancy (in this case in fact the geometry optimization results in a filled vacancy and in a H atom adsorbed on a five-coordinated O atom). The cost for creating the defect, 2.43 eV, is slightly higher if we compare the energy of the $F_S^+(OH^-)$ center with that of an H atom adsorbed on the MgO surface; in this case in fact one has to add the binding energy of the neutral H atom to the MgO surface, 0.42 eV. Thus, the formation of the $F_S^+(OH^-)$ center on the MgO(1 0 0) terraces is an endothermic process, but the energy cost is relatively small, definitely smaller than that needed to create a neutral $F_S$ or a charged $F_S^+$ center. Stated differently, the presence of atomic hydrogen strongly reduces the cost to create vacancies on the surface and it is possible that on some special sites of the surface (steps, kinks, etc.) the process requires a small energy cost or is even thermoneutral.

The $F_S^+(OH^-)$ center has an electronic structure which is reminiscent of that of the "classical" $F_S^+$ center. In particular, the unpaired electron is trapped in the vacancy and is not associated to the OH group or delocalized over the surface. This is shown in particular by two observable quantities, the spin density and the hyperfine coupling constants. Fig. 5 shows that the spin density is largely localized in the vacancy site. The second proof of the spin localization comes from the small values of the isotropic hyperfine coupling constants, $a_{iso}$, with the neighboring $^{25}$Mg isotopes, Table 1. At variance with the $F_S^+$ center, where the spin is symmetrically distributed in the cavity, here the presence of a vicinal OH group induces a polarization of the spin and results in slightly different $a_{iso}$ values: the two Mg atoms opposite to the OH group have $a_{iso}$ values of $-10.3$ and $-8.3$ G, while the remaining two surface Mg atoms have values of $-5.1$ and $-2.6$ G. It is interesting to mention that the $a_{iso}$ values for $^{25}$Mg are close to the experimentally determined hyperfine constants for paramagnetic color centers generated by exposure of MgO to atomic hydrogen, 10-12 G [1,7]. On the other hand, a tiny coupling, 0.1 G, is found with the H atom. This coupling is much smaller than that observed experimentally (2 G) [1], suggesting that the OH group is too far from the trapped electron. However, if one places the OH group near the vacancy this results in its incorporation in the cavity. Thus, while some features of the spin distribution are consistent with the observations, others are not. Recently it has been shown that most likely these couplings originate from $(H^+)(e^-)$ pairs formed at morphological irregularities on the surface [6,7]. Still, it is intriguing that the $F_S^+(OH^-)$ centers present a spin distribution quite similar to the observed one, at least for the Mg part.

The $F_S^+(OH^-)$ centers are stable and represent a deep trap for the electron: the vertical IP, 4.50 eV, is not too different from the vertical IP of the $F_S^+$ center, Table 1. This means that in principle the center can give rise to optical transitions in

the visible region of the spectrum, and account for the color change which usually accompanies the formation of paramagnetic centers on the surface. The transition energies, $T_{\mathrm{e}}$, have been computed at the TD-DFT level. The calculations have been performed also placing ghost functions in the center of the cavity to check that the results are not dependent on the choice of the basis set but the $T_{\mathrm{e}}$'s change at most by 0.02 eV by augmenting the basis set, Table 1. Two groups of transitions can be identified: two transitions with appreciable intensity ($f \approx 10^{-2}$) are found at 0.39 and 0.46 eV above the ground state. These transitions occur from singly-occupied s-like state to empty levels in the vacancy with p character. The next transition is found at 1.42 eV and is followed by three states at 1.57, 1.58 and 1.72 eV. All these states have weak oscillator strengths ($f \approx 10^{-3}$) and involve an excitation from an s-like state to a p-like state of the trapped electron mixed with p states belonging to the O atom of the $-\mathrm{OH}$ group. While these transitions can be responsible for the coloring of the sample, their intensity is rather low. It should be mentioned also that some uncertainty exists about the reliability of TD-DFT excitation energies for transitions to states with some conduction band character [36]. To summarize, the new $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}(\mathrm{OH}^{-})$defect can be classified as a color center but its optical properties are different from those of the classical $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}$one.

At variance with the $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}$center, which is difficult to treat in a periodic approach because of the net charge of the supercell, the $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}(\mathrm{OH}^{-})$defect is neutral and its properties can be studied with periodic boundary conditions. First we have considered the same arrangement of the $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}(\mathrm{OH}^{-})$used in the cluster calculations, Fig. 5. In this case we used a three layer MgO slab to represent the surface. The geometry optimization results in a similar structure, with a Mg-OH bond distance of $1.87 \AA$ and a Mg-O-H angle of $126^{\circ}$. The electronic structure is the same obtained with the embedded cluster: the unpaired electron is localized in the vacancy, Fig. 6, and the charge distribution corresponds to that of a $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}$center near an adsorbed $\mathrm{OH}^{-}$group. The formation energy of the defect, computed with respect to the free surface and an H atom at infinite distance, is 1.30 eV, i.e. 0.7 eV smaller than for the cluster results. One possible explanation is that the periodic repetition of the $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{S}}^{+}(\mathrm{OH}^{-})$defect on the surface results in attractive dipolar interactions which stabilize the defective surface. The DOS curves, Fig. 7, show that the unpaired electron is localized on a level which is about 1.8 eV above the top of the $\mathrm{O}_{2 \mathrm{p}}$ valence band and about 1 eV above a level corresponding to the $\mathrm{O}_{2 \mathrm{p}}$ orbitals of the OH group.

An important question is whether the stabilization of the electron trapped in the cavity ($\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}$ center) is related to the presence of the neighboring $\text{OH}^-$ group or not. Recent studies on the formation of $(\text{H}^\text{+})(\text{e}^-)$ pairs when atomic hydrogen is adsorbed on irregular low-coordinated sites on the MgO surface indicate the important role of short-range electrostatic interactions [6,7]. In order to answer this question we have considered a different model where the OH group and the vacancy are separated by a larger distance. This has been done by creating the oxygen vacancy on one side of a four-layer MgO slab and by adsorbing the OH group on the opposite side of slab. The analysis of the spin population clearly shows that the unpaired electron remains localized in the oxygen vacancy. The formation energy of the $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}(\text{OH}^-)$ defect, computed with the four-slab model and relaxing the $z$ coordinate of the Mg ion where the OH group is adsorbed, is 1.87 eV, i.e. about 0.57 eV higher than for the case where the $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}$ and the $(\text{OH}^-)$ groups are at short distance, indicating the existence of a direct electrostatic interaction between the two groups. On the other hand, the results have shown that the vicinity of the $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}$ and the $(\text{OH}^-)$ groups is not a necessary condition for the stabilization of the trapped electron.

An indirect consequence of these results is that a supercell model of the $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}(\text{OH}^-)$ center can be used to study adsorption and reactivity on paramagnetic color centers. In fact the new center exhibits some properties which are reminiscent of those of the "classical" $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}$ center. However, the presence of the vicinal OH group and the fact that the defect is globally neutral are features which can change the reactivity of the $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}(\text{OH}^-)$ center with respect to that of the $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}$ one.

## 4. Conclusions

The presence of oxygen vacancies (F centers) on the surface of MgO has been invoked to explain a large number of adsorption phenomena. Their existence on MgO thin films has been proved recently by accurate EELS experiments [16,17] in combination with ab initio calculations of the excitation energies [18,19]. In these experiments however, the number of F centers has been artificially increased by $\text{Ar}^\text{+}$ bombardment or by preparing films with excess of Mg [16,17]. The high formation energy of a neutral, $\text{F}_\text{S}$, or of charged, $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}$ and $\text{F}_\text{S}^{2+}$, centers is the reason for their low concentration on the as-grown material. Here we have computed the formation energies of these centers using two complementary methods, a periodic approach using sufficiently large supercells and an embedded cluster model where long-range polarization effects are included explicitly. This is particularly important for the charged defects which cannot be treated in a simple way in the periodic approach. The results confirm the substantial identity of the slab and cluster descriptions, and the high energy involved in the creation of these defects.

More subtle is the nature of color centers at the surface of polycrystalline MgO where the samples are often subject to chemical treatments, like adsorption of atomic or molecular hydrogen [6,7], alkali metals, etc. [22]. Experimentally it has been shown that the trapped electron is near an adsorbed proton [1]. Starting from the idea that the sites where electrons can be trapped are oxygen vacancies, it has been suggested that the centers consist of an oxygen vacancy with an adsorbed $\text{H}^\text{+}$ ion in the vicinity; the name $\text{F}_\text{S}(\text{H})^\text{+}$ has been coined to indicate these defects. Accurate ab initio calculations have shown that these centers exhibit spin properties consistent with the experimental ones [49]. More recently, based on new highly resolved EPR spectra and other chemical evidence, it has been shown that the centers generated by $\text{H}_2$ adsorption followed by UV irradiation consist of $(\text{H}^\text{+})(\text{e}^-)$ pairs formed at specific morphological sites [6,7]. These latter centers have nothing to do with oxygen vacancies. In this study we have shown that atomic hydrogen can promote the creation of paramagnetic oxygen vacancies by formation of adsorbed OH groups giving rise to a $\text{F}_\text{S}^\text{+}(\text{OH}^-)$ center. This defect, which has not been described previously, corresponds to a paramagnetic color center with properties not too different from those observed experimentally [1]. Based on the present results, it is not possible to conclude unambiguously that this represents another variant of trap-

ped electron on the MgO surface. However, the cost to create these new centers on the terrace sites of MgO is significantly lower than that required for the isolated $F_S^+$ vacancies. It is possible that in correspondence of morphological irregularities the energy cost will be even lower and that the presence of hydrogen will promote the generation of surface vacancies. More experimental work is needed in order to confirm this hypothesis.
