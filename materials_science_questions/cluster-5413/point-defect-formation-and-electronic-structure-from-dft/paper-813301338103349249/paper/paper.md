## ABSTRACT
First-principles calculations were carried out to study the difference in the segregation behavior of Pd on the LaO and FeO₂ terminated surfaces of LaFe₁₋ₓPdₓO₃₋ᵧ perovskite oxide. Without oxygen vacancy, the tendency of Pd to segregate near to the LaO- and FeO₂-terminated surfaces is rather weak. Oxygen vacancies enhance the surface segregation of Pd significantly at FeO₂ terminated surfaces, while they do not at LaO terminated surfaces. Combined with the previous thermodynamic calculations, we propose that Pd-containing perovskite catalyst should be prepared at high temperature to enhance the surface segregation of Pd and to form suitable surface geometries for rapid switching between oxidized and reduced states of Pd catalyst on perovskite support. The origin for the large difference in the segregation tendency of Pd between LaO and FeO₂ terminated surfaces is also discussed.

## 1. Introduction
The toxic exhaust emissions composed by CO, NOₓ, and hydrocarbons from the gasoline-fueled automotive vehicles can be converted to the harmless gases: CO₂, N₂ and H₂O simultaneously by the three-way catalyst. However, during the vehicle used, the agglomeration and growth of the precious metal particles precipitated on the surface, due to the converter exposed to heat, dramatically decreases the overall surface area, degrading the activity of the catalyst and wasting many precious metals. Thus, it is urgent and essential to find ways to suppress the growth of metal particles and the amount of precious metals used in the three-way catalyst. The precious-metal-containing perovskite catalyst has been found to be of the excellent durability because of the effect on sintering controlling [1–3]. So far some experiments have demonstrated that the intelligent catalyst can suppress the growth of the metal particles more efficiently than the conventional one due to the self-regenerative function of the precious metals [2–4]. TEM observation found that after aging at 900 °C, the sizes of Pd on the Al₂O₃ surface reach up to 120 nm, whereas only much smaller metallic particles can be detected on the LaFeO₃ (LFO) surface [4]. The similar phenomenon was observed on CaTiO₃ surface with Pt doped [3]. Based on XANES and EXAFS measurements, it is explained that Pd as one of the precious metals with high activity, replaces the iron atom on B site (the B site in the ABO₃ perovskite is originally occupied by transition metals such as Fe, Co, and Mn) in the LFO perovskite forming the solid-solution state under oxidized atmosphere; while in the reductive reactions, Pd segregates to and then moves out of the surface in the form of small nanoparticles with the size of 1–3 nm [5]. Therefore, the transformation of the state of Pd in the perovskite catalyst in such a cycle can effectively suppress the growth of the precious metal particle.

Although a mechanism [6] for understanding the rapid transformation of precious metals in perovskite catalyst in redox cycle is proposed based on the spinodal nano-decomposition, it is still difficult to explain the fast redox process associated with the Pd atoms doped deeply inside the matrix. One latest Density Functional Theory (DFT) study [7] concentrating on the properties of Pd on the LFO surface revealed that the oxygen vacancy ($V_O$) at the subsurface (LaO layer) can promote the segregation of Pd/Pt onto the surface. They also proposed a scenario that a LaPdO₃₋ᵧ layer formed close to the LaFeO₃ surface improves the Pd metal segregation without diffusing a very long distance in bulk. Actually, a latest TEM work [8] on the atomic-scale processes in Pd-LFO catalysts pointed out that the redox reactions between the formation of Pd particles on the Pd-LFO surface under reducing conditions and the dissolution of Pd particles into LFO under oxidizing conditions take place at spacially-limited area, especially at proximity of oxide surfaces, strongly supporting the mechanism

proposed by Hamada et al. [7]. Unfortunately, their work only investigated one type termination $(FeO_{2})$ system. It is known that LaO-terminated slab is another important type for LFO surface, but its properties, stability and the behavior of Pd at LaO-terminated surface is barely studied so far. Thus it is necessary to investigate LaO-terminated surfaces to unambiguously conclude that the "temporary-layer" $(LaPdO_{3-y})$ could be produced generally. Lee et al. [9] calculated the surface phase diagram of LFO from first-principles and showed that both the LaO and $FeO_{2}$ terminated surfaces appear as stable surface phases under given operating conditions. Accordingly, since the LaO-type surface is also evidenced stable at low oxygen chemical potential, it is necessary to study the properties and the stability of Pd at LaO terminated surfaces to make a complete conclusion of the former work [7]. On the other hand, up to this date, no first-principles works have been reported on the comparison of two-type terminated surfaces with Pd embedded, to the best of our knowledge.

In this work, based on the DFT, we performed first-principles calculations to investigate the segregation behavior of Pd on both LaO and $FeO_{2}$-type surfaces in $LaFe_{1-x}Pd_{x}O_{3-y}$ slab (LFP). Without $V_{O}$, Pd has very similar segregation energy on LaO- and $FeO_{2}$-terminated surfaces. However, the effects induced by $V_{O}$ on the segregation behavior of Pd in the two type terminations are obviously different. In the case of $FeO_{2}$-terminated surface, $V_{O}$ only appears near the surface wherever Pd is located, and improves the segregation tendency of Pd. On the other hand, in the LaO-terminated surface, the Pd-O pair forming in bulk restrains the segregation of Pd. Thus, the introduction of $V_{O}$ makes Pd less favorable for segregation in LaO-terminated slab. Our result indicates that during catalyst preparation, in which the precious metal-containing precursor is calcined at 1073 K for 2 h [2,3], Pd-containing oxide layers are formed at proximity of perovskite surfaces.

## 2. Model and computation

Our spin-polarized first-principles calculations were performed by using STATE [10,11] and QUANTUM ESPRESSO (QE) code [12]. We used ultrasoft pseudopotential (USPP) [13] to deal with the ion-electron interactions. The exchange and correlation potential was described by a generalized gradient approximation (GGA), and the PBE functional [14] is employed in both the STATE and QE calculations. The valence configurations for each atom and cutoff energies for plane-wave basis sets to represent wave functions and augmentation charge density are listed in Table 1. It is noted here that the DFT calculations with the Hubbard correction (DFT + U) is not used in the present paper. It is because by using the STATE code and the same parameters, Hamada et al. [7] have verified that the electronic structures with DFT + U are qualitatively the same and their conclusion is unchanged. A $GdFeO_{3}$-type orthorhombic unit cell shown in Fig. 1 was adopted to construct a surface of LFO perovskite, in which the local magnetic moments of Fe are arranged in the G-type anti-ferromagnetic order. The relaxed lattice constants for $a$, $b$, and $c$ in bulk LFO are presented in Table 2. It is found that the lattice constant relaxed by QE code is closer to the experimental results, while STATE overestimates the lattice constant slightly. The LFO (001) surface is modeled by a slab, which consists of five $FeO_{2}$ layers and four or five LaO layers depending on the surface termination and each $FeO_{2}$ layer includes two Fe atoms. The Brillouin-zone integration was performed within Monkhorst-Pack [15] scheme using a $(4\times4\times1)$ mesh. To minimize the interaction between two adjacent slabs, we inserted a vacuum region of $11\ \mathring{A}$. During the geometry optimizations, the two bottom layers are fixed (Fig. 2).

The solution energy of a substitutional solute Pd in the perovskite LFP systems with and without a $V_{O}$ is defined generally as follow:

$$
E^{\text{sol}} = E_{\text{LFP}}^{\text{tot}} - E_{\text{LFO}}^{\text{tot}} - E_{\text{Pd}}^{\text{bulk}} + E_{\text{Fe}}^{\text{bulk}} \tag{1}
$$

where $E_{\text{LFP}}^{\text{tot}}$ and $E_{\text{LFO}}^{\text{tot}}$ are the total energies of systems with and without a Pd atom; $E_{\text{Pd}}^{\text{bulk}}$ and $E_{\text{Fe}}^{\text{bulk}}$ denote the energies of the bulk Pd and bulk Fe, respectively. By this definition, negative formation energy means exothermic mixing. Thus, the segregation energy of Pd can be written as:

$$
E_{\text{Pd}}^{\text{seg}} = E_{\text{Pd}-\text{sf}}^{\text{sol}} - E_{\text{Pd}-\text{bulk}}^{\text{sol}} \tag{2}
$$

in which $E_{\text{Pd}-\text{sf}}^{\text{sol}}$ and $E_{\text{Pd}-\text{bulk}}^{\text{sol}}$ are the solution energies of Pd on the surface and inside the bulk of LFO. The formation energy of $V_{O}$ is calculated in reference to half of the gaseous $O_{2}$ as:

$$
E^{\text{f}} = E_{\text{P}-V_{0}}^{\text{tot}} - E_{\text{P}}^{\text{tot}} + \frac{1}{2}E_{\text{O}_{2}}^{\text{gas}} \tag{3}
$$

in which $E_{\text{P}-V_{0}}^{\text{tot}}$ and $E_{\text{P}}^{\text{tot}}$ represent the total energies of the perovskite surface with and without a $V_{O}$, respectively. The calculated binding energy of $O_{2}$ in our work is -5.68 eV in good agreement with the results (-5.66 eV [9] and -5.68 eV [17]) in other DFT calculations.

## 3. Results and discussion

We traced the energy changes of Pd segregating from the accommodation deeply inside to the top sites at both of two type terminated (001) LFO surfaces as shown in Fig. 2 (a) and (b) with and without $V_O$. All results are displayed in Fig. 3. We note here that although Pd at the 3rd $FeO_2$ layer is treated as the solid-solution as well as the initial state of segregation, the solution energies of Pd (in Table 3 (a) and (b)) in the third layer are different by using the two type terminated slab models by about 0.18 eV (about –0.1 eV in QE calculations), indicating unequal reference states. Therefore the calculated segregation energies may have an error of about 0.18 eV (or –0.10 eV in QE). To get more reliable reference energy, one needs to enlarge the thickness of the (001) slabs, which may costs much more computational time. Thus, in Fig. 3 we only present the relative energy of Pd in the first and third $FeO_2$-layer (the 3rd $FeO_2$- layer is taken as the reference site for Pd). From Fig. 3 (a), if the energy error induced by the thickness of the model of slab is taken into account, the relative energy difference of Pd on the LaO- and $FeO_2$-terminated surfaces is within 0.2 eV, hence the evaluated segregation tendency of Pd in the LaO-terminated surface is a little stronger than it in $FeO_2$-terminated surface. An excellent agreement is obtained with another DFT-GGA calculation carried out by QE code (Fig. 4).

We now turn to the effect induced by $V_O$ on the segregating behavior of Pd at the perovskite surface. For each site of Pd, all of the nonequivalent O sites are examined to find the most stable site for $V_O$. When Pd is located around the surface, $V_O$ tends to form at the LaO-layer beneath Pd atom on both LaO- and $FeO_2$-terminated surfaces with the formation energy of 2.89 and 1.42 eV, respectively. The formation energy of $V_O$ on the $FeO_2$-termination in the present work is well consistent with that (1.52 eV) gained in Ref. [7]. For the solid-solution state of Pd, the most favorable sites for $V_O$ are different in the two terminated surfaces. For convenience of comparison, we plotted in Fig. 4 some representative structures both for these two type surfaces with Pd embedded deeply and near the surface. In the case of LaO-terminated system, $V_O$ prefers staying with Pd rather than at any other sites near the surface. The total energy difference between the two is about 1.39 eV, indicating the formation of $V_O$ on the pure surface is rather difficult. In contrast, the topmost site is more favorable for the $V_O$ formation than the inside in the $FeO_2$-terminated system with the energy difference of about 0.41 eV. According to the results in Fig. 3 (a), the energy releases during the Pd segregation from the 3rd to the 1st $FeO_2$-layer are –0.21 and –0.89 eV in LaO- and $FeO_2$-terminated surface, respectively. Apparently, the $V_O$ at the subsurface significantly promotes the segregation of Pd in $FeO_2$-terminated system, whereas the formation of $V_O$ has no promotion but slightly

Accordingly, the segregation tendency of Pd denoted by $\sigma_{\text{ter.-surf.}}^{\text{seg}}$ at the two terminated surfaces with and without $V_O$ is evaluated in this order: $\sigma_{\text{FeO}_2 - V_O}^{\text{seg}} > \sigma_{\text{LaO}}^{\text{seg}} \approx \sigma_{\text{LaO}-V_O}^{\text{seg}} \approx \sigma_{\text{FeO}_2}^{\text{seg}}$. The calculated energy differences in segregation tendency among the last three items are closed to the energy error (0.18 eV) mentioned above, therefore we considered that the tendency of Pd to segregate to these three kinds of surfaces should be almost identical. The $V_O$-containing FeO₂-terminated surface is the most favorable place for Pd segregation.

To deeply understand the origin for the obvious differences in segregation tendency of Pd between the $V_O$-containing FeO₂-terminated surface and the other three surfaces, we calculated the relative energies of Pd in the relaxed and unrelaxed slabs, and showed the results in Fig. 3 (b). In unrelaxed slabs, all the atoms in the slab models are fixed at their ideally terminated LFO surfaces. Therefore, the relative energies calculated by using unrelaxed slabs indicate the electronic (or chemical) contribution to the segregation energies and the difference in the relative energies between relaxed and unrelaxed slabs indicate the elastic (or mechanical) contribution. Fig. 3 (b) indicates that the relative energies using relaxed and unrelaxed slabs are similar and therefore, we conclude that the chemical contribution determines the segregation tendency of Pd. To clarify the electronic origin for the segregation tendency, we presented the projected density of states (PDOS) of Pd in the unrelaxed systems in Fig. 5. When Pd stays inside the bulk, the degenerate $e_g$ states of Pd are singly occupied (Fig. 5 (a)). In the case of Pd segregated at the LaO-terminated surface (Fig. 5 (b)), the PDOS is almost the same as it in panel (a) near the Fermi energy, except a slight downward shift of the $e_g$ states beneath the Fermi level. The introduction of $V_O$ just below Pd at the LaO-terminated surface leads to stabilization of $d_{3z}^2 - r^2$ state beneath the Fermi level because it is the anti-bonding state between Pd $d_{3z}^2 - r^2$ state and O $p_z$ state (panel (c)). However, since there is another O just above Pd, an antibonding $d_{3z}^2 - r^2$ state appears nearly at the Fermi level. On the other hand, at the FeO₂-terminated surface, even without $V_O$, since O just above Pd is missing, the degeneracy of $e_g$ states is lifted and $d_{3z}^2 - r^2$ state is shifted downward relative to the $d_{x}^2 - y^2$ state, resulting in large peak related to $d_{3z}^2 - r^2$ state (panel (d)). When $V_O$ is introduced just under the Pd at the LaO-terminated surface, the hybridization between Pd $d_{3z}^2 - r^2$ state and O $p$ state is further reduced, leading to significant stabilization of $d_{3z}^2 - r^2$ state (panel (e)). Therefore, the effect of $V_O$ on the chemical bonding of Pd at the FeO₂-terminated surface is much more significant than that at the LaO-terminated surface.

From the surface phase diagram shown in Ref. [9], we can see that the LaO-terminated surface is predicted to be stable at lower temperature (773 K), while the FeO₂-terminated surface becomes dominant at high temperature (1500 K). According to our results, the segregation tendency of Pd seems weak in the perfect surfaces and $V_O$ significantly enhances the surface segregation of Pd onto the FeO₂-terminated surface. Thus, we predict that the formation of LaPdO₃−y layer is strongly enhanced at high temperature where the LaO-terminated surface is suppressed and the concentration of $V_O$ is increased. Precious-metal containing perovskite catalysts are prepared by calcination of the precious metal containing precursor at 1073 K for 2 h in air [2,3]. From our present study, we propose to make catalysts at higher calcination temperature to enhance the surface segregation of Pd and to form the LaPdO₃−y surface layers, which promote efficient redox reactions between the formation of Pd particles on the Pd-LFO surface under reducing conditions and the dissolution of Pd particles into LFO under oxidizing conditions.

## 4. Summary

To summarize, we have carried out a systematic investigation on the segregation behavior of Pd at LaFeO₃ perovskite surfaces by using the first-principles calculations based on DFT. Pd can segregate near to both of the LaO- and FeO₂-terminated surfaces with similar segregation tendency. We also found that oxygen vacancy promotes the surface segregation of Pd at FeO₂-terminated surface. On the contrast, the oxygen vacancy suppresses the segregation of Pd at LaO-terminated surface. The effects on the segregation behavior of Pd induced by V₀ have been discussed by electronic structure analysis. By combining the present results with the previous thermodynamic calculations, we propose that the catalyst preparation should be done under higher temperature to form Pd concentrated layers at perovskite catalyst surfaces, which promote efficient switching between Pd metal particles under reducing conditions and the oxidized Pd in LFP perovskite lattice under oxidizing conditions.
