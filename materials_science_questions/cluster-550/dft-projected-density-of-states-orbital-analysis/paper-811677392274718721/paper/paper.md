# Semiconducting behavior of type-I Si clathrate $K_8Ga_8Si_{38}^\dagger$

Motoharu Imai, $^{*a}$ Akira Sato, $^{b}$ Haruhiko Udono, $^{c}$ Yoji Imai $^{d}$ and Hiroyuki Tajima $^{e}$

Received 14th January 2011, Accepted 18th February 2011
DOI: 10.1039/c1dt10071h

A ternary type-I Si clathrate $K_8Ga_8Si_{38}$ has been revealed to be an indirect band gap semiconducting material with an energy gap $(E_g)$ of approximately 0.10 eV, which is much smaller than the calculated $E_g$ value that is 0.15 eV wider than $E_g$ of elemental Si with the diamond-type structure.

The group-14 type-I clathrates, whose general chemical formula is $A_8X_{46}$, have a characteristic crystal structure, the $Na_8Si_{46}$- type structure (cubic, space group: $P\overline{3}n$ (No. 223), $Z = 1$), in which X atoms form a cage framework that consists of face-shared $X_{20}$ dodecahedra and $X_{24}$ tetrakaidecahedra and in which A atoms locate inside the polyhedra as guests. $^{1}$ They have attracted attention as a thermoelectric, $^{2-6}$ semiconducting, $^{7}$ and superconducting material. $^{8-11}$

Ternary group-14 clathrates have been extensively studied as thermoelectrical materials $^{2-6}$ since Nolas et al. reported that $Sr_8Ga_{16}Ge_{30}$, in which Ga and Ge atoms form polyhedra network, exhibits low lattice thermal conductivity. $^{12}$ Recently, a dimensionless figure of merit $ZT$ in a semiconducting ternary Ge clathrate, $Ba_8Ga_{16}Ge_{30}$, reached 1.35 at 900 K, which demonstrates that it is a promising material for practical thermoelectric applications. $^{13}$ From the point of view of elemental abundance and cost, Si-based materials seem to be better than Ge-based materials. Ternary Si clathrates, however, have not shown high $ZT$ mainly because of their low Seebeck coefficient, which reflects their metallic conduction. $^{14,15}$

Adams et al. have indicated by the first principle calculation that a guest-free type-I Si clathrate $Si_{46}$ is a semiconducting material with an energy gap $(E_g)$ that is 0.7 eV wider than that of elemental Si with the diamond-type structure. $^{7}$ Si-based materials with wider $E_g$ than the diamond-phase Si are attractive for solar cell application since (1) a semiconducting material with $E_g$ of 1.4 eV, which is 0.3 eV wider than that of Si, is ideal for a single- junction solar cell, and (2) a Si-based material with $E_g$ that is wider than 1.4 eV can be a candidate top cell material for a tandem Si-based solar cell. $^{16}$

For the reasons mentioned above, semiconducting type-I Si clathrates are attractive for both thermoelectric and solar cell applications but their synthesis has not been thoroughly studied. There are two strategies for a synthesis of semiconducting type-I Si clathrates: a synthesis of guest-free Si clathrate and a synthesis of ternary Si clathrates. Although several efforts have been made for the synthesis of the guest free type-I Si clathrate, they have been unsuccessful; instead, guest-free type-II clathrates have been formed for both Si and Ge. $^{17-19}$ Ternary Si clathrates have been investigated theoretically and experimentally. The first-principle calculations have demonstrated that alkaline-earth-metal tetride type-I clathrates $Ae_8Tr_{16}Tt_{30}$ (Ae = Sr, Ba: Tr = Al, Ga, Tt = Si, Ge) are semiconducting materials. $^{20,21}$ In fact, the Ge clathrate $Ba_8Ga_{16}Ge_{30}$ has shown semiconducting conduction: a decrease of $\rho$ with temperature, $^{13,22,23}$ and its carriers have been successfully tuned by control of the Ga content. $^{23}$ The synthesis of semiconducting Si clathrates, however, has been unsuccessful. The resistivity $\rho$ in ternary Si clathrates, such as $Sr_8Ga_{16-x}Si_{30+x}$, $Ba_8Ga_{16-x}Si_{30+x}$ and $Ba_8Al_{16-x}Si_{30+x-y}\square_y$, where $\square$ denotes a vacancy, has shown metallic behavior: an increase of $\rho$ with temperature. $^{24,14,15}$ We believe that one of the reasons for metallic conduction is the content of Tr atoms, which has not reached 16, as discussed later. It is, therefore, necessary to synthesize a ternary Si clathrate in which the valence electrons of guest atoms can be compensated with lower Tr atom content, in order to obtain semiconducting Si clathrates. Although $K_8Ga_8Si_{38}$, in which valence electrons of K atoms are expected to be compensated by eight Ga atoms, was reported to be a semiconductor, $^{25}$ no data on physical properties was given in the paper.

In this letter, we report the synthesis and characterization of a ternary alkaline-metal Si clathrate $K_8Ga_8Si_{38}$ and its semiconducting behavior. First, we confirm that $K_8Ga_8Si_{38}$ is a semiconducting material on the basis of calculations. We then characterize the synthesized sample. Finally, we show that $K_8Ga_8Si_{38}$ is a semiconducting material by optical absorption and electrical resistivity measurements.

Fig. 1 illustrates the calculated density of states (DOS) of (a) Si₄₆, (b) K₈Si₄₆, and (c) K₈Ga₈Si₃₈. The energy at the top of the valence band or the energy of the corresponding states is chosen as an origin. Since the calculation within LDA systematically underestimates $E_{\text{g}}$ of semiconductors, we discuss $E_{\text{g}}$ using a relative value from the calculated $E_{\text{g}}$ value of the diamond-phase Si, not the absolute value of calculated $E_{\text{g}}$. Si₄₆ has the Fermi level $E_{\text{F}}$ in its $E_{\text{g}}$. The $E_{\text{g}}$ of Si₄₆ is approximately 0.67 eV wider than that of the diamond-phase Si, which is consistent with the results in Ref. 7. In the case of K₈Si₄₆, the shape of DOS is almost the same as that of Si₄₆. $E_{\text{F}}$ shifts to higher energy, as expected from the simple filling of the conduction band of Si₄₆ with eight electrons. As a result, $E_{\text{F}}$ is located in the states that correspond to the conduction band of Si₄₆, which indicates that K₈Si₄₆ is a metal. $E_{\text{g}}$ still exists approximately 0.68 eV below $E_{\text{F}}$, the value of which is 0.58 eV wider than that of Si. Compensation of eight valence electrons from K₈Si₄₆, therefore, results in a semiconducting Si clathrate.

One of the ways is a substitution of eight group-13 atoms for eight Si atoms. Fig. 1 (c) shows a DOS of K₈Ga₈Si₃₈. K₈Ga₈Si₃₈ has $E_{\text{F}}$ in $E_{\text{g}}$, which indicates that it is semiconducting. $E_{\text{g}}$ of K₈Ga₈Si₃₈ is 0.52 eV smaller than $E_{\text{g}}$ of Si₄₆, but 0.15 eV larger than that of the diamond-phase Si. The same scenario is expected to be applied to Ae₈Tr₁₆Tt₃₀, as shown by the previous calculations.²¹,²² In this case, we have to compensate for 16 valence electrons by substitution of 16 Tr atoms for 16 Tt atoms because an Ae atom has two valence electrons. For the Ge clathrates, it works, as described previously. It is, however, difficult to synthesize stoichiometric Ae₈Tr₁₆Si₃₀, and instead, ${\text{Ae}_{8}\text{Tr}_{16 - x}\text{Si}_{30 + x - y}\square_{y}}$ has been synthesized. In this case, $E_{\text{F}}$ is still expected to be in the conduction band because Tr content is insufficient to compensate for all of the valence electrons from the Ae atoms.

A calculated band structure of K₈Ga₈Si₃₈ indicates that K₈Ga₈Si₃₈ is an indirect gap semiconducting material with a valence band maximum at the Γ point and a conduction band minimum at the M point.²⁸,²⁹

The crystal structure of synthesized sample was determined to be the Na₈Si₄₆-type structure with a lattice parameter *a* of 10.4261(2) Å‡ (see Electronic Supplementary Information for details of the crystal structure†). The chemical formula determined from the X-ray diffractometry is $\text{K}_{8}\text{Ga}_{8.3(1)}\text{Si}_{37.7}$. These results are consistent with a previous report.²⁵

Fig. 2 shows $(\alpha h\nu)^{1/2}$ and $(\alpha h\nu)^{2}$ as a function of $h\nu$, where α and $h\nu$ are the optical absorption coefficient and photon energy, respectively. The indirect band gap $E_{\text{g}}^{\text{ind}}$ and direct band gap $E_{\text{g}}^{\text{dir}}$ were estimated to be 0.10 and 0.27 eV by fitting data to the following equations: $(\alpha h\nu)^{1/2} = A^{\text{ind}}(h\nu - E_{\text{g}}^{\text{ind}})$ and $(\alpha h\nu)^{2} = A^{\text{dir}}(h\nu - E_{\text{g}}^{\text{dir}})$, where $A^{\text{dir}}$ and $A^{\text{ind}}$ are constants, respectively.³⁰ Although the equation for $E_{\text{g}}^{\text{ind}}$ is an approximate one that does not separate the contributions from phonon-emission and phonon-absorption processes, we use this equation because of the lack of information on phonon energy. These results indicate that K₈Ga₈Si₃₈ is an indirect gap semiconductor with $E_{\text{g}}^{\text{ind}}$ of approximately 0.10 eV.

This suggests that electrical conduction is related to some kind of disorder such as defects because the variable-range hopping is observed in disordered semiconductors. $^{32}$

$E_{\mathrm{g}}$ obtained from the experiments is much smaller than the calculated $E_{\mathrm{g}}$. There are two plausible reasons: vacancies and disorder in occupancy. An effect of vacancies on the electronic structure has been discussed in the type-I clathrates $\mathrm{K}_{8} \mathrm{Ge}_{44} \square_{2}$, $\mathrm{C}_{8} \mathrm{Sn}_{44} \square_{2}$, and $\mathrm{Ba}_{8} \mathrm{Cu}_{5} \mathrm{Ge}_{40} \square_{1} \cdot{ }^{33-35}$ The vacancies in the cage framework make dangling bond (DB) states within $E_{\mathrm{g}}$. The calculation on $\mathrm{Ba}_{8} \mathrm{Cu}_{5} \mathrm{Ge}_{40} \square_{1}$ has demonstrated that the DB states locate at the top of the valence band, which results in a reduction of $E_{\mathrm{g}} \cdot{ }^{35}$ This scenario is expected to be applied to $\mathrm{K}_{8} \mathrm{Ga}_{8} \mathrm{Si}_{38}$. Since the disorder in occupancy of Ga atoms was not considered in the present calculation, it may affect the value of calculated $E_{\mathrm{g}}$. For a systematic discussion of the effects on $E_{\mathrm{g}}$, further study would be necessary.

## Conclusions
The type-I Si clathrate $\mathrm{K}_{8} \mathrm{Ga}_{8} \mathrm{Si}_{38}$ has been revealed to be an indirect band gap semiconductor with $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ of approximately 0.10 eV. This material is the first example to show clear semiconducting conduction in the type-I Si clathrates. The use of alkaline-metal atoms as guest atoms makes it possible to compensate the valence electrons by substitution of eight group-13 atoms for eight Si atoms. As a result, $\mathrm{K}_{8} \mathrm{Ga}_{8} \mathrm{Si}_{38}$ becomes semiconducting. It will be interesting to examine thermoelectric properties of $\mathrm{K}_{8} \mathrm{Ga}_{8} \mathrm{Si}_{38}$ because a narrow gap semiconductor is expected to be a good thermoelectric material.
