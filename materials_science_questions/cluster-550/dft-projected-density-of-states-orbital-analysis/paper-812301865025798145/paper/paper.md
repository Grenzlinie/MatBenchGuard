# de Haas-van Alphen Effect and Fermi Surfaces of LaNi₂Al₅

Kunihiko MAEZAWA*, Satoshi AKAMARU¹, Tomohiko KUWAI¹,
Yosikazu ISIKAWA¹, Junji SAKURAI¹ and Hisatomo HARIMA²

Toyama Prefectural University, Kosugi, Toyama 939-0398
¹Department of Physics, Toyama University, Toyama 930-8555
²The Institute of Scientific and Industrial Research, Osaka University, Ibaraki, Osaka 567-0047

(Received March 2, 1999)

Magnetoresistance and de Haas-van Alphen measurements have been performed to investigate the Fermi-surface property of LaNi₂Al₅, which is a reference material of the magnetic dense-Kondo compound CeNi₂Al₅. Thirteen branches with the frequencies from $1.25 \times 10^{6}$ to $101 \times 10^{6}$ Oe have been observed in the angular-resolved dHvA measurements, and the magnetoresistance shows the existence of open orbits. The results are well explained by the Fermi surfaces obtained from an FLAPW band-structure calculation.

KEYWORDS: LaNi₂Al₅, Magnetoresistance, de Haas-van Alphen effect, Fermi surface, FLAPW method, band theory

## §1. Introduction
LaNi₂Al₅ is a reference material of an isostructural magnetic dense-Kondo compound CeNi₂Al₅. Both LaNi₂Al₅ and CeNi₂Al₅ crystallize in the body-centered-orthorhombic PrNi₂Al₅-type crystal structure; the space group is #71(Immm) as shown in Fig. 1. There are two crystallographic sites for Al, then four crystallographic sites in this compound. The primitive cell contains one molecule.

CeNi₂Al₅ is a magnetic dense-Kondo compound with the Néel temperature of $2.6\mathrm{K}.^{1-5)}$ The magnetic structure is incommensurate and complicated amplitude-modulated with the propagation vector of (0.500, 0.405, 0.083) down to $400\mathrm{mK}.^{6)}$ This magnetic structure is transformed into a ferromagnetic or an induced paramagnetic state in the field of $90\mathrm{kOe}.^{7)}$ It is unusual that the amplitude-modulated structure can be allowed in the compound with localized moments of which the ground state is Kramers doublet.⁸) One of possible interpretations is that the magnetic $4f$ electrons correlate strongly to conduction electrons, and the magnetic structure reflects Fermi-surface properties, for example, through the nesting effect. Thus the fermiological study in CeNi₂Al₅ has been investigated by means of the de Haas-van Alphen effect in a separate paper.⁹) It is also necessary to compare the Fermi-surface properties of CeNi₂Al₅ with a reference material LaNi₂Al₅ in order to clarify the characteristics of the Fermi surface modified by the correlation between the $4f$ and conduction electrons. In this study, the transverse magnetoresistance and the de Haas-van Alphen (dHvA) effect have been performed to investigate the Fermi-surface properties of LaNi₂Al₅.

We have also calculated a band structure and Fermi surfaces of LaNi₂Al₅ by using FLAPW method to compare them with the experimental results. In recent progress, the self-consistent band calculation by an FLAPW method within the local density approximation (LDA) succeeded in determination of the Fermi surfaces of many rare-earth compounds, especially of the compounds with high-symmetric crystal structure.¹⁰,¹¹) It is also interesting in how well such a band-structure calculation works in the orthorhombic structure with four crystallographic sites.

## §2. Experimental
The single crystal of LaNi₂Al₅ was grown by the Czochralski method using a radio-frequency induction furnace. The residual resistivities were from 0.4 to 0.6 $\mu\Omega\cdot\mathrm{cm}$ and the residual resistivity ratios were from 26 to 29 for the principal axes. The magnetoresistance was measured at temperature of $0.15\mathrm{K}$ and in fields up to $130\mathrm{kOe}$ by a four-probe DC method. The dHvA effect was measured by a field-modulation technique in fields up to $120\mathrm{kOe}$ and at temperatures down to $0.45\mathrm{K}$, and the temperature dependence of amplitude of the dHvA signals at temperatures from 0.45 to $1.0\mathrm{K}$.

### 3.1 Magnetoresistance
Figures 2-4 show the transverse magnetoresistance $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$ in the current direction along the $a$-, $b$- and $c$-axes, respectively. Here $\Delta\rho$ is given by $\rho_H - \rho_0$, $\rho_H$ is the resistivity in an applied field $H$, and $\rho_0$ is the resistivity without $H$. The insets are angular dependence of $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$ in the fields of 70 and 130 kOe. The magnetoresistance in the (100) plane (Fig. 2) increases faster than linearly with increasing field, and it is not saturated except for the field direction along the $c$-axis. For the $c$-axis, $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$ shows a tendency to be saturated in the high-field region above 110 kOe. In the (010) plane (Fig. 3), $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$ is large and reaches the value of 3 to 4 in the field of 130 kOe. In this plane, $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$ increases monotonically in any field directions, being proportional to $H^{1.4-1.5}$. In the (001) plane (Fig. 4), $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$ is very small and less than one in the field of 130 kOe. Around the $a$-axis, $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$ shows a tendency to be saturated in the fields above 100 kOe though it is not clear around the $b$-axis.

§4. Band Calculation and Fermi Surfaces

Band-structure calculations are carried out using an FLAPW method with LDA for the exchange correlation potential. For the LDA, the formula proposed by Gunnarsson and Lundqvist $^{12)}$ is used. For the band-structure calculation, we used the program codes; TSPACE $^{13)}$ and KANSAI-94. The scalar relativistic effects are taken into account for all electrons, and the spin-orbit interactions are included for valence electrons as a second variational procedure.

![](./images/812301865025798145_6.jpg)

Fig. 6. Measured angular dependence of the de Haas-van Alphen frequencies for $LaNi_{2} Al_{5}$.

Lattice constants of $LaNi_{2} Al_{5}$ are used as follows; $a = 7.033$ Å, $b = 9.677$ Å and $c = 4.021$ Å. Muffin-tin (MT) radii are set as $0.2870\ a$ for La and $0.1534\ a$ for Ni and Al, where $a$ is a lattice constant of the $a$-axis. Core electrons (Xe-core except $5p^{6}$ for La, Ar-core for Ni and Ne-core for Al) are calculated inside the MT spheres in each self-consistent step. $5p^{6}$ electrons on La are calculated as valence electrons with the second energy window.

The LAPW basis functions are truncated at $|k+G_{i}| \leq 6.51\ (2\pi/a)$, where $G_{i}$ is the reciprocal lattice vector, corresponding to 449 LAPW functions at the $\Gamma$ point. The sampling points are uniformly distributed in the irreducible $1/8$th of the first Brillouin zone, 61 $k$-points for the potential convergence and 353 $k$-points for the final band structure. The first Brillouin zone of $LaNi_{2} Al_{5}$ is shown in Fig. 7 with the symmetry points and axes.

The calculated band structure and density of states are shown in Figs. 8 and 9, respectively. Ni $3d$ bands are located around $0.9$ Ry and have a large peak with a tail in the higher energy side. La $4f$ bands are located around $1.25$ Ry and hardly influence the Fermi level. From the density of states at the Fermi level, the electronic specific-heat coefficient $\gamma_{\text{band}}$ is obtained as $6.75\ \text{mJ/mol·K}^{2}$.

## §5. Discussions
First, we discuss the magnetoresistance. $LaNi_{2} Al_{5}$ is a compensated metal, hence it is expected that the transverse magnetoresistance increases with increasing field, being proportional to $H^{2}$ in the high-field limit $\omega_{c} \tau \gg 1.^{14)}$ For the field in the (100) and (010) planes, $\Delta \rho / \rho_{0}$ increases faster than linearly and reaches the value from 2 to 4 in the field of 130 kOe, except for the configuration of the field $H$ along the $c$-axis and the current $I$ along the $a$-axis. This result is consistent with the expectation that $LaNi_{2} Al_{5}$ is a compensated metal. This result also shows that the value of $\omega_{c} \tau$ is roughly estimated to be from 1.5 to 2 and thus the high-field condition is satisfied in the present measurements. For the following two configurations of $I$ and $H, \Delta \rho / \rho_{0}$ is small and shows a tendency to be saturated in the fields above 100 kOe. The first is that the current is directed to the $c$-axis $(I / / c)$ and the field $H$ to the $a$-axis $(H / / a)$, and the second is $I / / a$ and $H / / c$. These results suggest that the $b$-directional open orbits, which are perpendicular to the current direction, arise for $H / / a$ and $H / / c$, respectively. These results are well explained by the calculated Fermi surfaces shown in Fig. 10. That is, there are two $b$-directional open orbits for $H / / a$. One of them is the orbit through the neck at the $\Gamma$ point on the 19th band hole surface and the other is the orbit through the neck at the $S$ point on the 20th band electron surface. For $H / / c$, the $b$-directional open orbit also arises on the 20th surface, which connects from the belly centered at the middle of the $\Lambda$ axis to the neck at the $S$ point.
As seen in Fig. 10, the $[1 \overline{1} 0]$-directional open orbit on the 19th band surface and the $a$-directional open orbit on the 20th band surface are also expected for $H / /[110]$

Next, we compare the angular dependence of the mea- sured dHvA frequencies (Fig. 6) with that of the calcu- lated extremal cross-sectional areas (Fig. 11). It is very easy to find that the measured $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, $\delta$, $\zeta$, $\eta$, $\kappa$, $\lambda$, $\mu$ and $\omega$ branches correspond to the calculated a, b, g, d, z, h, k, l, m and w branches, respectively. The agreement is excellent both in the magnitude of the frequencies and in the regions where the signals are detected.

The experimental $\alpha$ branch, corresponding to the a branch, arises from the closed small pocket in the 20th band. The experimental $\delta$ branch, corresponding to the d branch, arises from the large closed surface in the 18th band. This Fermi surface is not completely spherical but accompanying humps for some directions, as seen in Fig. 10(a). For the field angle of about $30^\circ$ from the $a$-axis in the (001) plane, the d orbit passes on these humps. Such configuration makes the amplitude of the dHvA signal very week because of large curvature fac- tor.¹⁵) This is consistent with the experimental result as mentioned in §3. The experimental $\beta$ and $\gamma$ branches, corresponding to the b and g branches, respectively, arise from the necks of the 19th band surface. The $\zeta$, $\eta$, $\mu$ and $\omega$ branches arise from the large main orbits, z, h, m and w, of the 19th and the 20th band surfaces. Some of these orbits, b, g, z and h, change into the open orbits for the particular field directions within the (001) plane as discussed above. The $\kappa$ and $\lambda$ branches arise from the electron orbit, k, on the 19th band surface and the hole orbit, l, on the 20th band surface, respectively.

The theoretical o, p and q branches origi- nate in three kinds of neck in the 20th band surface in Fig. 10(c); the o branch is not shown in Fig. 11 because of the frequency less than $10^6$ Oe. The p and q branches are thought to be hardly detected due to the large curva- ture factors leading to too small intensities.¹⁵⁾ However some of the isolated points observed in the (100) and (010) planes presumably correspond to parts of the p and q branches. On the other hand, there is no exper- imental branch corresponding to the o branch. This o branch correlates strongly to the h branch, that is, the h branch should disappear in a narrow region around $40^\circ$ from the $b$-axis in the (001) plane caused by the existence of the neck of the o orbit, as shown in Fig. 11. The $\eta$ branch is, however, detected continuously in this region. So the small o orbit may not exist. As other discrepan- cies, the measured $\beta'$ and $\varepsilon$ branches have no theoretical correspondence in Fig. 11. One possible interpretation for the $\beta'$ branch is as follows; an additional extremal cross-sectional area. corresponding to the $\beta'$ branch, may appear in the vicinity of the b orbit on the neck-shaped surface if this neck is somewhat corrugated. An orbit, corresponding to the $\varepsilon$, may also appear in the vicinity of the z orbit, assuming a small change of the shape. The branch with the frequency of $101×10^6$ Oe measured in the (001) plane also has no theoretical correspondence.

The experimental electronic specific-heat coefficient $\gamma_{\text{exp}}$ is obtained as $8.0\ \text{mJ/mol·K}^2$,¹⁶⁾ then the mass en- hancement factor from the electronic specific-heat coef- ficients $\gamma_{\text{exp}}/\gamma_{\text{band}}$ is 1.2. Table 1 shows the dHvA fre- quencies and cyclotron masses in some magnetic field directions. The experimental cyclotron masses are 1.2 to 2.8 times as heavy as calculated ones. The mass en- hancement from the cyclotron masses seems to be larger than that from the electronic specific-heat coefficients, but the mass enhancement factor for the largest branch $\zeta(z)$ is in good agreement with $\gamma_{\text{exp}}/\gamma_{\text{band}}$.

In the recent Fermi surface studies for $LaAg^{10)}$ and $LaCu_2$,¹¹⁾ excellent agreements between the experi- ments and band calculations have been obtained by the FLAPW method. However, these compounds have high symmetric crystal structures (cubic in LaAg and hexagonal in $LaCu_2$). While, $LaNi_2Al_5$ is a body- centered-orthorhombic crystal structure with four crys- tallographic sites. The present excellent agreement of the angular dependence of the dHvA frequencies indi- cates that the FLAPW calculation within the LDA gives an accurate description on Fermi-surface properties even for such a low symmetric crystal. The established Fermi- surface property in $LaNi_2Al_5$ will be a great help to the following study in $CeNi_2Al_5$.

§6. Conclusions

Magnetoresistance and dHvA measurements and an FLAPW band-structure calculation are performed for $LaNi_2Al_5$ to clarify the Fermi-surface properties. The experimental results are explained well by the FLAPW results.
