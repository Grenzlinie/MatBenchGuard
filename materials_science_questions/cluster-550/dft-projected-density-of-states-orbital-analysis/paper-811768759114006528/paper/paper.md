# Effect of Pressure on Electronic Structures and Optical Properties of Rocksalt InN

Ming-yao Xu${}^{\text{a}}$, Ming Xu${}^{\text{b,c}*}$, Man-yi Duan${}^{\text{a,c}}$, Qing-ping Hu${}^{\text{a}}$

(Dated: Received on December 6, 2009; Accepted on April 6, 2010)

The electronic structures and optical properties of rocksalt indium nitride (InN) under pressure were studied using the first-principles calculation by considering the exchange and correlation potentials with the generalized gradient approximation. The calculated lattice constant shows good agreement with the experimental value. It is interestingly found that the band gap energy $E_{\text{g}}$ at the $\Gamma$ or X point remarkably increases with increasing pressure, but $E_{\text{g}}$ at the L point does not increase obviously. The pressure coefficient of $E_{\text{g}}$ is calculated to be 44 meV/GPa at the $\Gamma$ point. Moreover, the optical properties of rocksalt InN were calculated and discussed based on the calculated band structures and electronic density of states.

Key words: Density functional theory, Electronic structure, Optical property, Rocksalt InN

## I. INTRODUCTION

Indium nitride (InN) is an attractive material for long-wavelength optoelectronic and high-speed electronic devices because of its narrow direct band-gap energy [1] and superior carrier transport characteristics in a wide range of temperature [2]. At ambient temperature and pressure, InN usually crystallizes in the hexagonal wurtzite structure with the space group P63mc, although it can also crystallize in the zinc blende cubic structure with the space group F4-3m under certain conditions. Under high pressure, the wurtzite structure of InN experiences a phase transition to the rocksalt phase at the pressure of 10-14 GPa [3-9], like most of the four-coordinated binary compound semiconductors [10]. A number of theoretical and experimental studies have been focused on the phase transition, electronic structure, optical properties and elastic behaviors of wurtzite [11-16] and zinc blende InN [17-19], but the investigation on the optical properties of high-pressure phase InN (i.e., rocksalt InN under high pressure) is still lacking. Since the properties of semiconductor materials are sensitive to external pressure, the pressure is an important variable used to tune physical properties. However, to our knowledge, there is only few theoretical calculation on the electronic and optical properties of InN under pressure. This strongly attracts us to calculate the electronic and optical properties of rocksalt InN under high pressure.

In this work, we performed a detailed study of the electronic and optical properties of rocksalt InN under pressure by using first-principles calculations based on density functional theory (DFT) since such method is very effective for the investigation on the electronic structure and optical properties of materials [20]. The calculated crystal structure and electronic structure are effective in comparison to the experimental and other theoretical values. Furthermore, the optical properties under high pressure were discussed based on the band structures and electronic density of states.

## II. CALCULATION METHOD

First-principles calculations were performed by using pseudo-potential plane wave method based on the DFT. Based on the DFT, the CASTEP (Cambridge serial total energy package) code [21] in Materials Studio of Accelrys Inc. (version 4.2) was used. The ultra-soft pseudo-potential is used with $4\text{d}^{10}5\text{s}^{2}5\text{p}^{1}$ and $2\text{s}^{2}2\text{p}^{3}$ as the valence-electron configuration for the In and N atoms, respectively, to describe the electron-ion interaction. Electronic wavefunctions are expanded in terms of a discrete plane-wave basis set, and the exchange and correlation potentials are described with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the scheme of Perdew-Bueke-Ernzerhof (PBE) formula [22].

Finally, the electronic structures and optical properties were performed based on optimized geometry.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### A. Crystal structures

The obtained equilibrium lattice constant $a_0$ of rocksalt InN is $4.712\ \mathring{\text{A}}$ at zero pressure, in good agreement with the calculated value $(4.71\ \mathring{\text{A}})$ by Saib *et al.* [7]. Unfortunately, there are not experimental data at zero pressure at present. However, Xia *et al.* [3] measured the lattice constant $a$ of rocksalt InN to be $4.535{\pm}0.001\ \mathring{\text{A}}$ at 20 GPa by the X-ray diffraction, rather close to our calculated value of $4.562\ \mathring{\text{A}}$ at the same pressure. Figure 1 shows the relative volume of rocksalt InN under pressure, in our calculations, the $V_0$ is the unit cell volume of the wurtzite InN at 0 K and 0 Pa ($32.2\ \mathring{\text{A}}^3$/primitive cell). One can see that the relative volume decreases exponentially with the pressure, well consistent with the experimental data [3, 5]. This indicates the validity of the first-principles calculations based on the DFT with GGA for the crystal structure of rocksalt InN.

In comparison with the band structure and density of states of rocksalt InN under 0 and 20 GPa, one can see that the valence bands have a tendency of shift- ing to lower energy region and becoming broader when rocksalt InN is compressed. The main peaks of In5s, In5p, In4d, and N2p states are weakened, only the peak of N2s states is enhanced. On the contrary, the con- duction bands shift to higher energy region. This can be understood as follows: The inter-atomic spacing in rocksalt InN is expected to decrease as the pressure be- comes higher. Under such conditions, the wave func- tions overlap more strongly. Thus, there is an increase in the dispersion of the bands in $k$ space as well as in the bandwidths. As such, the bandgap is broadened at higher external pressures. Due to the larger bandgap of InN at 20 GPa, the conductivity reasonably decreases in this case.

Figure 4 presents the pressure variation of the di- rect bandgap at $\Gamma$, L, X points and indirect bandgap $E_{\text{g}}$ (L-$\Gamma$) for the rocksalt InN. It is seen that all the bandgaps almost linearly increase with pressure and the bandgap keeps indirect for InN up to the pressure of 40 GPa. $E_{\text{g}}$ at the $\Gamma$ and X point remarkably increase with increasing pressure, but the $E_{\text{g}}$ at the L point does not increase significantly. Generally, the pressure co- efficient $k{=}\text{d}E_{\text{g}}/\text{d}P$ is calculated by fitting $E_{\text{g}}(P)$ the linear equation [10]:

$$
E_{\text{g}}(P)=E_{\text{g}}(P_{T})+k(P-P_{T})+\alpha(P-P_{T})^{2} \tag{1}
$$

where $P_{T}{=}12$ GPa. The pressure coefficient has been calculated to be 44 meV/GPa for $E_{\text{g}}$ ($\Gamma$-$\Gamma$), 11 meV/GPa for $E_{\text{g}}$(L-L), 43 meV/GPa for $E_{\text{g}}$ (X-X), and 34 meV/GPa for $E_{\text{g}}$(L-$\Gamma$). The calculated pres- sure coefficient of $E_{\text{g}}$ ($\Gamma$-$\Gamma$) shows excellent agreement with the value of 41 meV/GPa by means of the LMTO method with LDA reported in Ref.[10], indicative of the validity of our calculation in electronic structures. This will guarantee the reliability for the following calcula- tion of optical properties based on the above electronic structures.

## C. Optical properties

The optical properties of a solid material are usu- ally described by the complex dielectric function $\varepsilon(\omega){=}\varepsilon_{1}(\omega){+}i\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$, which characterizes the linear re- sponse of the material to an electromagnetic radiation, and therefore governs the propagation behavior of ra- diation in a medium. The imaginary part $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ can be obtained from the electronic structure calculations by using the matrix elements between the occupied and unoccupied wave functions within the selection rules, and the real part $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ can be derived from $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ by the Kramer-Kronig relationship. All the other optical constants could be deduced from $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$.

Figure 5 show the calculated $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ and $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ of di- electric function, absorption coefficient $\alpha(\omega)$ and reflec- tivity $R(\omega)$ of rocksalt InN under 0, 20, and 40 GPa. The imaginary part of dielectric function $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ is the most important optical property for any materials. In Fig.5(a), the calculated results of $\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)$ were given. One can see, under 0 GPa, there are two main peaks at 4.6 and 10.0 eV. The peak at 4.6 eV mainly originates from the optical transitions between N2p states in valence band and In5p states in conduction band; while the peak at 10.0 eV can be due to the optical transitions from 5p and 5s states of In to N2p states. In addi-

tion, there is a weak peak at about 22.4 eV, which is from the optical transitions between In4d states and N2p states. When the pressure increases up to 20 and 40 GPa, the peaks shift to the high-energy region. The similar phenomena are also observed for the other opti- cal constants. Such variation can be attributed to the broadening of bandgap under pressure. On the other hand, the intensity of the peak at about 4.6 eV gradu- ally decreases with increasing pressure, due to the de- crease of the peak intensity of DOS.

The $\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)$ of dielectric function at the zero frequency limit is the static dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{1}(0)$, which is re lated to the reflective index measured at a frequency above the lattice vibration frequencies. The calculated values of $\varepsilon_{1}(0)$ are 11.5, 8.9, and 7.9 under different pres sure at 0, 20, and 40 GPa, respectively (see Fig.5(b)). Obviously, $\varepsilon_{1}(0)$ of rocksalt InN at 0 GPa is larger than 7.39 for wurtzite InN by Xu *et al.* [25] and 8.56 for zinc blende InN by Berrah *et al.* [26], implying its better ap- plication in dielectric material. The optical absorption spectra of rocksalt InN under 0, 20, and 40 GPa are shown in Fig.5(c). The absorption band shows three main peaks in the range of 0–40 eV. The blue shift of the absorption edge is clearly observed. The intensity of the peaks increases with increasing pressure. The reflectivity spectrum also shows three peaks. The host peak appears at about 13 eV; while the weakest peak at around 25 eV, corresponding to the electron transition between the lower part of valence bands and conduction bands.

## IV. CONCLUSION

A detailed investigation of the electronic structure, band structure, electronic density of states, and optical properties of rocksalt indium nitride under pressure has been performed using the first-principles method based on the density function theory. Our calculation shows that the lattice constant is in good agreement with the experimental and previous theoretical value. The high pressure causes the valence bands to shift to lower en- ergy region and the conduction bands to shift to higher energy region. Furthermore, the optical properties of rocksalt InN such as the dielectric function, absorption coefficient, and reflectivity were calculated under pres- sure, which are explained in terms of the computed elec- tronic structure. Our results are useful for the further experimental work of high-pressure rocksalt InN.
