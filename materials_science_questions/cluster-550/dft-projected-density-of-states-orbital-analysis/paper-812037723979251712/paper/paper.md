# Magnetism of zinc blende $\text{CrP}(0\ 0\ 1)$ surface

Gul Rahman, Sunglae Cho, Soon Cheol Hong*

Department of Physics, University of Ulsan, Ulsan 680-749, Republic of Korea

Available online 30 November 2006

## Abstract

The spin-polarized full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FLAPW) method in the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) is carried out for investigation on the magnetism of the zinc-blende $\text{CrP}(0\ 0\ 1)$ surface. We found that the Cr-terminated surface retains the half-metallic character, while the half-metallicity is destroyed for the P-terminated surface due to surface states, which originate from p electrons. The magnetic moment of Cr at the surface (sub-surface) is found to be enhanced (reduced) compared to that of the bulk-like Cr atom.

Half metallic ferromagnets are the most desirable materials for spintronic devices. These materials have one spin channel that is metallic, while the other spin channel has a gap at the Fermi level. Hence, 100% spin polarization of the conducting electron is expected from these materials. Groot et al. [1] first predicted the half-metallicity for NiMnSb and PtMnSb. Binary CrAs in the zinc-blende (zb) structure was reported to grow epitaxially on $\text{GaAs}(1\ 0\ 0)$ and found to be half metallic, experimentally and theoretically [2]. The electronic and magnetic properties of the CrAs surface were studied using the full-potential screened Korringa-Kohn-Roster (KKR) method and it was found that the Cr-terminated surface retains the half- metallic character of the bulk, while the As-termination destroys the gap in the minority spin band [3]. The synthesis of monolayer of zb CrSb on GaSb and GaAs substrates was also reported [4]. A first principles calcula- tion with the FLAPW method was done on bulk zb-CrP, one of Cr pnictides, and found that it is a half-metallic ferromagnet for the lattice constant of $5.48\ \text{\AA}$ ($a_{\text{ZB}}$), which is close to the lattice constant of $\text{GaP}$ ($5.45\ \text{\AA}$). It was also shown [6] by the plane-wave pseudopotential method that bulk zb-CrP is a half-metallic ferromagnet.

In practical application of CrP to spintronic devices, its surface and interface magnetisms may be rather more important than its bulk magnetism. In this study, we investigate the magnetism of $\text{CrP}(0\ 0\ 1)$ surface by using the most appropriate method for investigation of magnetism, the FLAPW method. Two lattice constants, $a_{\text{ZB}}$ and the experimental lattice constant of $\text{InP}$ ($5.89\ \text{\AA}$, $a_{\text{InP}}$), were used for the calculations. Two different ideal surface configurations, Cr- and P-terminated surfaces, were considered. For the lattice constant of $a_{\text{ZB}}$, we allowed the surface relaxation, but the surface relaxation was found to be negligible. The half-metallicity of the $\text{CrP}\ (0\ 0\ 1)$ surface was found to be retained for the Cr-terminated surfaces, but to be lost for the P-terminated surfaces for both lattice constants.

The spin-polarized FLAPW method [7], based on density functional theory in generalized gradient approx- imation (GGA) [8], is employed. The core electrons are treated fully relativistically, whereas the valence electrons are treated semirelativistically. The basis functions are expanded in terms of the spherical harmonics up to $l = 8$ inside each muffin-tin sphere and plane waves in the interstitial region, respectively.

The total energy of the CrP (0 0 1) surface was calculated as a function of relaxation in the [0 0 1] direction of the topmost Cr layer, fixing the 2D lattice constant as $a_{ZB}$. We relaxed the surface up to 2% in the inward and outward directions (not shown here), but we did not observe any surface relaxation. The atomic projected density of states (DOS) for the Cr-terminated surfaces with $a_{ZB}$ and $a_{\text{InP}}$ lattice constants are shown in Fig. 1(a). The DOSs of the surface and bulk-like center atoms are denoted by the solid and dotted lines, respectively. The Cr-terminated surfaces retain the half-metallic nature of bulk CrP for both the lattice constants. We see that the majority spins are metallic, while the minority spins are insulator and there are no surface states in the minority gap. Two noteworthy points in the figure are found. (1) The band gaps of the minority spins of $\sim$2.0 eV at the surfaces are wider than that of the bulk CrP (1.88 eV) [5] and (2) the gaps are almost the same for both the 2D lattice constants even though the d-band narrowing is observed at larger lattice constant of $a_{\text{InP}}$. We see that the d states of Cr are strongly localized and contribute mainly to the half-metallic nature. The Fermi level lies at the center of minority bands for $a_{\text{InP}}$, but in case of $a_{ZB}$ the Fermi level is shifted towards the conduction band edge. For both the lattice constants, it can be seen that the DOS of the surface is slightly different from that of the bulk-like center layer. No surface states are formed in the minority gap at the Cr-terminated surface because the exchange splitting of Cr is very large ($\sim$3.50 eV).

The atomic projected DOS for the P-terminated surfaces for both $a_{ZB}$ and $a_{\text{InP}}$ lattice constants are shown in Fig. 1(b). Here, the DOSs of the surface and bulk-like center atoms are denoted by solid and dotted lines, respectively. The DOS of the surface P atoms show large deviation from the bulk-like center P atoms. Such deviation at the P surface was also observed for the CrAs (0 0 1) surface [3]. As shown in Fig. 1(b), for the P-terminated surface, the half-metallic character is destroyed due to the surface states originated from the P surface atom, whose character is mainly p-like. In this case, d electrons are not involved in the bonding at the surface and dangling bonds are created at the P-terminated surface, which in turn creates surface- states that destroy the half-metallicity of the CrP. In the case of CrP(1 1 0) surface [9], no dangling bonds are created and CrP (1 1 0) retains the half-metallic nature of bulk CrP.

The magnetic moments of the Cr- and P-terminated surfaces for both the lattice constants are listed in Table 1. We see that the magnetic moments of Cr and P atoms at the surfaces are enhanced compared to those of the corresponding bulk-like center layers. Due to reduced co- ordination number, the Cr atoms at the surface lose electron charge towards the vacuum and their magnetic moment increases by about $0.33\,\mu_{\text{B}}$. The enhancement of the surface magnetic moments was also observed in spin density contours (not shown here), which show prominent eruption of spin-density towards the vacuum region. The charge transfers at the subsurface and bulk-like Cr atoms are the same, but the majority (minority) electrons are decreased (increased) for the Cr subsurface atom compared to the bulk-like Cr atom, which decreases the magnetic moment of the Cr subsurface atom.
