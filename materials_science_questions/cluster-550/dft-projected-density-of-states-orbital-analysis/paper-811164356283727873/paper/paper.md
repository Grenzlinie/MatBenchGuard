## A B S T R A C T

By means of the first-principles calculations, we performed a systematical investigation on the structure, electronic and mechanical properties of $\text{MBSi}_2$ (M = Li, Na, K and Rb) and $\text{BSi}_2$. The changes of lattice parameters and interatomic distance from $\text{BSi}_2$ to $\text{RbBSi}_2$ were discussed in detail. Both $\text{LiBSi}_2$ and $\text{NaBSi}_2$ with distorted $sp^3$ hybridization $\text{BSi}_2$ three period networks are semiconductors, whereas $\text{KBSi}_2$ and $\text{RbBSi}_2$ with mixtures of $sp^2$ and $sp^3$ hybridization are metallic because of the weaker interaction in two-dimensional $\text{BSi}_2$ net. Interestingly, a new $\text{BSi}_2$ phase with no guest atom, featuring a metallic behavior, is predicted to be dynamically and mechanically stable.

## 1. Introduction

Group-14 elements (C, Si, Ge and Sn) usually feature the interesting covalent bonded networks. Diamond is the typical representative element, in which, C atom is perfectly tetrahedral coordinated, yielding four-connected three-dimensional (3D) networks. The diamond-like structure has thus attracted extensive interests due to the unique physical properties and great significance for the fundamental studies of materials and chemistry [1–3]. The archetypal diamond-like network can accept the guest atoms, e.g. alkali and alkaline metals, to form a variety of compounds with unexpected structural and physical properties [4,5]. For example, $\text{Na}_8\text{Si}_{46}$ and $\text{K}_8\text{Si}_{46}$ [4] known since 1965, exhibit metallic feature, because the electrons donated by alkali-metal atoms are not required for the framework of four-bonded silicon atoms. $\text{Ba}_8\text{Si}_{46}$, synthesized with a mixture of $\text{BaSi}_2$ and Si at 3 GPa and 800 °C, was reported to be superconductor with a transition temperature 8 K [5]. When silicon atoms are partially substituted by trivalent Group 13 atoms, e.g. $\text{K}_8\text{Ga}_8\text{Si}_{38}$ [6] and $\text{K}_7\text{B}_7\text{Si}_{39}$ [7], an electronic balance can be achieved. These ternary compounds usually called as Zintl phase are expected to be promising semiconducting and thermoelectric materials [6–10]. Very recently, a tetrahedral $\text{LiBSi}_2$ (Space group: $P4_2/nmc$) was reported by Zeilinger et al. [11] through a mixture of LiB and Si at 900 °C under a pressure of 10 GPa. As shown in Fig. 1, $\text{LiBSi}_2$ features a unique strictly ordered $\text{BSi}_2$ framework with mutually orthogonal channels hosted by Li atoms, and was predicted to be a semiconductor with a band gap exceeding 1.0 eV. The novel $\text{BSi}_2$ networks in $\text{LiBSi}_2$ structure sparked our interests to explore the physical properties of $\text{MBSi}_2$ (M = Na, K and Rb). In order to understand the effect of guest species (Li, Na, K and Rb) inside the $\text{BSi}_2$ networks on the electronic transport properties, herein, a systematical first-principles investigation was carried out to unveil the trend in the structure, electronic and mechanical properties of the ternary phases $\text{MBSi}_2$ (M = Li-Rb). Additionally, the stability and electronic property of $\text{BSi}_2$ were discussed, which may be obtained by a thermal "degassing" process, just as the *allo*-Ge [1] and $\text{Si}_{24}$ [12].

## 2. Computational details

The calculations on $\text{MBSi}_2$ (M = Li, Na, K and Rb) were performed using CASTEP software package within the framework of density functional theory (DFT) [13]. The exchange and correlation functional was treated by generalized gradient approximations with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE) functional [14]. The electronic configurations for boron, silicon, lithium, sodium, potassium and rubidium elements are treated as B: $2s^22p^1$, Si: $3s^23p^2$, Li: $1s^22p^1$, Na: $2s^22p^63s^1$, K: $3s^23p^64s^1$, and Rb: $4s^24p^65s^1$, respectively. A cutoff energy of 450 eV and a $5 \times 5 \times 4$ k-point mesh was employed to ensure the convergence of the total energy and the residual forces within $1.0 \times 10^{-6}$ eV and $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ eV Å$^{-1}$, respectively. The lattice parameter $c$ is also in the sequence of $V(BSi_{2})< V(LiBSi_{2})< V(NaBSi_{2})< V(KBSi_{2})< V(RbBSi_{2})$, but there is an abruptly increase from $NaBSi_{2}$ to $KBSi_{2}$. On the other hand, lattice parameter, $a$, varies slightly by about 0.79-4.29% in comparison with $LiBSi_{2}$. Therefore, the atomic replacements in the sub-lattice lead to the anisotropic deformation due to the interatomic separations of guest atoms. As shown in Fig. 3b, from $BSi_{2}$ to $RbBSi_{2}$, the inter-atomic distances B-Si1, B-Si2, Si1-Si1 and Si2-Si2 change slightly. The greatest values of bond lengths B-Si1, B-Si2, Si1-Si1 and Si2-Si2 are shown in $NaBSi_{2}$, and $BSi_{2}$ has the shortest bond distances. While for $KBSi_{2}$ and $RbBSi_{2}$, the bond length of Si1-Si1 ($2.434\mathring{A}$ for $KBSi_{2}$ and $2.435\mathring{A}$ for $RbBSi_{2}$) and Si2-Si2 ($2.481\mathring{A}$ for $KBSi_{2}$ and $2.480\mathring{A}$ for $RbBSi_{2}$) are almost the same, but shorter than those of $LiBSi_{2}$ (Si1-Si1 $2.461\mathring{A}$ and Si2-Si2 $2.500\mathring{A}$), indicating a relatively stronger Si-Si interactions. On the contrary, a monotonous increase can be observed for M-B, M-Si and Si1-Si2, especially, sharply increasing from $NaBSi_{2}$ to $KBSi_{2}$. The longer separations of Si1-Si2 in $KBSi_{2}$ ($4.717\mathring{A}$) and $RbBSi_{2}$ ($5.085\mathring{A}$) indicate the weak interactions. As shown in Fig. 1b, the three dimensional (3D) $BSi_{2}$ network significantly changed from $LiBSi_{2}$ and $NaBSi_{2}$, to $KBSi_{2}$ and $RbBSi_{2}$ due to the large atomic radii difference. In the other word, the structure of $KBSi_{2}$ and $RbBSi_{2}$ can be viewed as a sandwich arrangement with alternation of two dimensional (2D) $BSi_{2}$ network and M (M = K and Rb) ions layers stacking along $c$ direction. B atoms remain fourfold coordination, while Si1 and Si2 atoms adopt threefold coordination. This interesting 3D-2D transition of $BSi_{2}$ network from $NaBSi_{2}$ to $KBSi_{2}$ may provide a potential route to synthesize 2D-$BSi_{2}$ by substituted the metal atom with larger one with the fascinating properties of 2D materials, such as the half-integer quantum Hall effect, the never falling conductivity, and massless carriers [20]. Furthermore, Mulliken charge analysis (as shown in Table 3) in $MBSi_{2}$ shows that one electron is donated from M atom (M = Li, Na, K and Rb) to B atom, forming $B^{-}$ ions, while the total charges of Si1 and Si2 atoms change very slightly. In this case, the valence electrons of B is closed to $4e$, isoelectronic with atom C, sharing similarity with atom Si, while in $BSi_{2}$, each B atom gains $-0.89e$ from Si1 ($0.46e$) and Si2 ($0.44e$).

The calculated band structure of $MBSi_{2}$ is shown in Fig. 4. Interestingly, both $LiBSi_{2}$ and $NaBSi_{2}$ exhibit semiconducting behaviors while the others are metal. $LiBSi_{2}$ shows an indirect band gap of 1.14 eV, in good agreement with previous report 1.1 eV [11]. The top of the valence band (VB) locates between Y and S point, while the bottom of the conduction band (CB) locates between X and U point in the Brillouin zone. More intriguingly, $NaBSi_{2}$ possesses a direct band gap of 0.73 eV, with the top of VB and the bottom of CB lying along Y-S. Due to the general underestimation of band gap of DFT, $NaBSi_{2}$ may be a good candidate for the applications of single-junction solar cells and photoelectrochemical devices.

The calculated electronic density of states (DOS) (Fig. 5) shows substantial overlap of Si and B states over the VB and CB, indicating relatively strong covalent B-Si bonding in $LiBSi_{2}$. The contribution of Li states is mainly in CB and can be neglected in VB. The lower states of VB are derived from $s$ orbital of B and Si atoms, and the upper states of VB are contributed by $p$ orbital of B and Si atoms. The strong $sp^{3}$ hybridization leads to a band gap at Fermi level between the bonding states and antibonding states, the bonding states are fully occupied, and the antibonding states remain open. The bonding configuration of $NaBSi_{2}$ is similar to $LiBSi_{2}$, due to their similar bonding environment, such as valence electrons, lattice parameters and bond lengths in the $BSi_{2}$ network, although the band gap is narrower than $LiBSi_{2}$. The DOS profile of $BSi_{2}$ is also similar to $LiBSi_{2}$, but $BSi_{2}$ exhibits a metallic feature with finite DOS values at Fermi level, which mainly contribute to the $p$ states of B and Si atoms. Comparing with $LiBSi_{2}$, the number of bonding orbital in $BSi_{2}$ is not changed, however, the number of valence electrons decreases to 88 (44 bands), leaving the top four valence bands unoccupied, introducing a hole-conducting behavior. Moving to $KBSi_{2}$ and $RbBSi_{2}$, their DOS profiles are analogous to each other, but quite different from $LiBSi_{2}$ and $NaBSi_{2}$. Both $KBSi_{2}$ and $RbBSi_{2}$ are metallic, the band gap between bonding states and antibonding states vanishes and the Si-$p$ states dominate the Fermi level. Their VB are also originated mostly from B and Si states, nevertheless an increasing contribution of K-3$p$ and Rb-4$p$ states can be observed. In comparison with $NaBSi_{2}$, the $s$-$p$ hybridization in $KBSi_{2}$ is not strong, and the splitting between $s$ and $p$ states becomes significant, indicating the mixture hybridization between $sp^{2}$ and $sp^{3}$. The clear separation between Si1 and Si2 atoms results in a 2D-like $SiB_{2}$ networks, analogous to the buckling silicene. As a result, the perpendicular $p_{z}$ orbital forms $\pi$ bonding and hence $\pi$ and $\pi^{*}$ bands, crossing over the Fermi level, which is further confirmed by the decomposed DOS of Si-$p$ orbitals. As shown in Fig. 6, in $NaBSi_{2}$, the contribution of $p_{x}$, $p_{y}$ and $p_{z}$ of Si atoms are almost equally, indicating the high degree $sp^{3}$ hybridization. While in $KBSi_{2}$, a predominant contribution of Si-$p_{z}$ states can be observed at Fermi level, supporting the $sp^{2}$ and $sp^{3}$ mixture

hybridization. This result is also coincident with the trend of bond lengths of Si1-Si1 and Si2-Si2 in $LiBSi_2$ and $NaBSi_2$, longer than those in $KBSi_2$ and $RbBSi_2$.

The mechanical properties, including the elastic constants $C_{ij}$s, bulk modulus ($B$) and shear modulus ($G$), are also calculated (as shown in Table 4) to check the stability of these phases. The requirement of mechanical stability in a tetragonal system leads to the following restrictions on the elastic constants, $C_{11}>0$, $C_{33}>0$, $C_{44}>0$, $C_{66}>0$, $(C_{11}-C_{12})>0$, $(C_{11}+C_{33}-2C_{13})>0$, [2 $(C_{11}+C_{12})+C_{33}+4C_{13}]>0$ [21]. The calculated elastic constants of $MBSi_2$ satisfy the stability constraints. A general tendency for $MBSi_2$ is $C_{11}>C_{33}$, which may be resulted from the relatively strong covalent bonding in $BSi_2$ network paralleling to the (0 0 1) plane. Especially, $LiBSi_2$ possesses the largest $C_{11}$, $C_{33}$ and $C_{44}$ among all the considered phases, reasonable agreement with high $sp^3$ hybridization in $BSi_2$ network. The $C_{ij}$s of $KBSi_2$ and $RbBSi_2$ are close to each other, but much smaller than $LiBSi_2$ and $NaBSi_2$, due to the weaker interaction between the neighboring 2D $BSi_2$ networks. The smallest $C_{44}$ value of $BSi_2$, 11 GPa, indicate its easier shear deformation than others. The calculated $B$ and $G$ values of $LiBSi_2$ are 103 and 86 GPa, respectively, the largest ones for all of the consideration phases, also well consistent with previous theoretical results 102 and 84 GPa. The bulk modulus of $BSi_2$, 96 GPa, is lower than $LiBSi_2$, but greater than $NaBSi_2$ 87 GPa. Due to the large separations of 2D $BSi_2$ network, $B$ values of $KBSi_2$ (43 GPa) and $RbBSi_2$ (42 GPa) are only about half of that of $NaBSi_2$. The $G$ value of $BSi_2$, $KBSi_2$ and $RbBSi_2$ are also small, 25-27 GPa. The ratio of $B/G$, proposed by Pugh, is usually used to predict the brittle or ductile behavior of materials. The critical value is 1.75, $B/G>1.75$ is ductile behavior, otherwise brittle manner. As shown in Table 4, $BSi_2$ shows the interesting ductile behavior, while the others are prone to brittleness.

## 4. Conclusions
In summary, a systematical investigation on the crystal structure, electronic and mechanical properties of $MBSi_2$ (M = Vc, Li, Na, K, Rb) has been performed within DFT framework. $BSi_2$ without guest atoms is predicted to be dynamically and mechanically stable. $LiBSi_2$ and $NaBSi_2$ feature 3D periodic structure with tetrahedral coordinated B and Si atoms, whereas $KBSi_2$ and $RbBSi_2$ feature weakly interacted $BSi_2$ 2D network separated by alkali metal atoms. $BSi_2$ is predicted to be a hole-conducting metal due to its electron deficient nature. $LiBSi_2$ and $NaBSi_2$ are both semiconductor with calculated band gap 1.14 and 0.73 eV, respectively, because of $sp^3$ hybridization of B and Si atoms. On the other hand, $KBSi_2$ and $RbBSi_2$ are predicted to be metallic, due to the mixture $sp^3$ and $sp^2$ hybridization. Mechanical properties of the considered phases are all discussed in detail.
