JOURNAL OF THE PHYSICAL SOCIETY OF JAPAN, Vol. 42, No. 2, FEBRUARY, 1977

# Energy Band Structures of Gd-Pnictides

Akira HASEGAWA and Akira YANASE

Department of Physics, Tohoku University, Sendai
(Received August 16, 1976)

Energy band structures of Gd-pnictides, GdX(X=N, P, As and Sb), known to show a transition from a semiconductor to a metal in the series from GdN to GdSb, are calculated using the APW method. It is found that the energy bands depend sensitively on a choice of the one-electron potential. Then, the calculation is carried out self-consistently. The calculated bands account qualitatively well for the observed trend of the conducting properties through the pnictides. The density of states agrees reasonably well with experimental results. The Fermi surface of metallic pnictides is calculated both for paramagnetic and ferromagnetic states. It consists of simple closed electron and hole surfaces. Relativistic effects are investigated using first-order perturbation theory. They do not affect seriously nonrelativistic bands.

## §1. Introduction
The rare-earth monopnictides have attracted interest, because of the great variety of their magnetic and electrical properties. $^{1,2)}$ In the present paper, we try to calculate the electronic energy band structures of Gd-pnictides, GdX (X=N, P, As and Sb), using the APW method. Gd-pnictides are chosen, because Gd is located in the middle of the rare-earth group in the periodic table, so that they are expected to show physical properties typical of the whole rare-earth monopnictides. Moreover, among the pnictides they have been the most exten- sively investigated experimentally.

An interesting property of Gd-pnictides is a transition from a semiconductor to a metal in the series from GdN to GdSb. Recent measure- ments of electrical properties on carefully prepared GdN samples suggest that GdN may be a semiconductor. $^{3)}$ It does not seem, how ever, that a precise value of the energy gap has been established. GdP is the only pnictide for which the growth of large single crystals has been successful. $^{4)}$ Measurements of electrical $^{5)}$ and optical $^{6)}$ properties suggest that GdP may be a semimetal. Measurements of photoemis- sion from $GdSb^{7)}$ clearly show that this com pound is a metal; there is a sharp Fermi edge at the high-energy end of the observed energy distribution of the photoemitted electrons. Note that the electronic specific heats of LaP and $LaSb,^{8)}$ whose electronic structures are expected to be similar to those of corresponding Gd-pnictides in a paramagnetic state, are comparable to that of the Ag metal. This fact may provide another evidence that the rare- earth monopnictides with heavy pnictogens are metallic. The first purpose of the present calculation is to investigate whether the band theory can account for this observed trend of the conducting properties through the pnictides.

Magnetic properties of Gd-pnictides are also interesting. GdN is a ferromagnet, whereas the other pnictides are an antiferromagnet. $^{1)}$ The antiferromagnetic ordering in the metallic pnictides can be destroyed by an external magnetic field, and a ferromagnetic ordering can be obtained. $^{9)}$ The Fermi surface may be affected appreciably by the external field, because the exchange interaction between the Bloch electrons and the localized spins of the 4f electrons in Gd is fairly large. Thus, a measure- ment of the de Haas-van Alphen effect should observe how the Fermi surface changes with a change of the magnetic ordering as the external field increases. This change of the Fermi surface with the external field, an interesting fermiology, has been recently studied in the magnetic compound MnP. $^{10)}$ For an analysis of experi mental results, the usual method is not ap- plicable, because it assumes that the Fermi surface is not affected by the external field. An extension of the method to the case where the Fermi surface changes with the external fieldhas been carried out by Kasuya. $^{11)}$

Whereas the study of the Fermi-surface change is difficult in MnP because of its complex crystal structure, it is rather easy in the metallic pnictides because they have the simple NaCl structure and thus a theoretical investigation of the Fermi surface is much easier. The second purpose of the present calculation is to calculate the Fermi surface of the metallic pnictides in a paramagnetic state. It will provide a basis for analizing experimental results of the de Haas-van Alphen effect in Gd-pnictides.

In the present paper, the APW band calculations are carried out in a self-consistent manner. Relativistic effects are important in such heavy elements as considered here, especially in Gd. These effects are taken into account using first-order perturbation theory.

As a result of calculations, the band structures which account qualitatively well for the observed trend of the conducting properties through the pnictides are obtained. The Fermi surface of metallic pnictides is simple; it consists of closed surfaces of electrons and holes. It is hoped that the present calculation will stimulate further experimental and theoretical investigations of Gd-pnictides and other rare-earth compounds.

## §2. Self-Consistent Calculation in the Nonrelativistic Limit

### 2.1 Calculation

Gd-pnictides crystallize in the NaCl structure. The reciprocal lattice is body-centred cubic, and the Brillouin zone is shown in Fig. 1. The symmetry points and axes are labelled according to the standard notation. $^{12)}$ The lattice constants and other parameters used in the calculation are given in Table I.

Fig. 1. The Brillouin zone of the face-centred cubic lattice.

For a self-consistent calculation, we follow a method proposed by Mattheiss et al. $^{14)}$ The full Stater $^{15)}$ exchange potential is used throughout the present calculation. The starting charge density is constructed along lines proposed by Matthiess. $^{16)}$ It is obtained from overlapping self-consistent atomic charge densities of Herman and Skillman $^{17)}$ calculated for the atomic configurations of $Gd(4 f^{7} 5 d^{1} 6 s^{2})$ and $X(n s^{2} n p^{3})$ , where $n=2,3,4$ and 5 for $X=N$ , P, As and Sb, respectively. The frozen-core approximation is adopted; the atomic charge densities of the core states (the xenon core and $4 f^{7}$ for $Gd$ , and the appropriate rare-gas core and $n s^{2}$ for $X$ ) are used to represent the core electrons in the crystal.

In each step of the iteration process, the wave functions and the energy eigen-values are calculated at six inequivalent points of high symmetry in $1 / 48$ of the Brillouin zone (uniformly distributed 32 points in the entire zone). About 120 basis functions are employed, and the size of the secular determinant is reduced by the aid of group theory. The new crystal charge density, $\rho(r)$ , is obtained by summation over occupied Bloch states, and then compared with the input charge density, $\rho'(r)$ . For selfconsistency, we require that for each APW sphere,
$$4 \pi \int_{0}^{R}\left|\rho^{\prime}(r)-\rho(r)\right| r^{2} \mathrm{~d} r<0.005, \quad(1)$$
where $R$ is the appropriate sphere radius. If this condition is not satisfied, then $70 \%$ of $\rho'(r)$ and $30 \%$ of $\rho(r)$ are used to construct a new potential for the next iteration. Self-consistency is achieved in 20 iterations in most cases, and yields energies stable to within 0.005 Ryd. Final accuracy of the self-consistent energies is, however, limited by the number of sampling

points in the Brillouin zone. According to the prescription of Mattheiss et al., $^{14)}$ the present calculation yields the band structures which are self-consistent to within 0.01 Ryd. This accuracy is sufficient for the purposes of the present calculation.

At the start of the self-consistent calculation, it has been found that the band structures of GdN and GdP calculated from the starting potential show an insulating character, whereas those of GdAs and GdSb show a metallic character. The band structures of GdN and GdP are similar to those of Eu-chalcogenides calculated by Cho. $^{18)}$

As the iteration proceeds, characters of the bands change appreciably. The valence-band width becomes larger, and the mixing effect of the conduction and valence bands becomes greater. Finally, the band gap of GdN is much reduced, and GdP becomes metallic. Both GdAs and GdSb remain metallic. The change of the band structures in GdN is the most drastic. Its band structures in the [100] direction at the first and final steps of the iteration are shown in Fig. 2.

The numerical calculations were performed at Computer Centre of the University of Tokyo and at Tohoku University Computer Centre.

### 2.2 Results in the nonrelativistic limit
Calculated results of the self-consistent band structures of Gd-pnictides along the principal symmetry directions in the Brillouin zone are shown in Fig. 3. As seen in Fig. 3, the band structures of all pnictides except GdN have a metallic character; the energy branches starting from the $\Gamma_{15}$ and $\Gamma_{25}'$ states overlap considerably around [100] directions. The energy bands of GdN do not have a metallic character, but have a semiconducting character. The energy gap between the conduction and valence bands is small, less than 0.2 eV.

In the metallic pnictides, the Fermi surface consists of three nearly spherical hole surfaces with different volumes centred at the $\Gamma$ point and three equivalent ellipsoidal electron surfaces at the X points. The numbers of electrons, $n_{\mathrm{e}}$, and of holes, $n_{\mathrm{h}}$, are the same; $n_{\mathrm{e}}=n_{\mathrm{h}}=$ 0.016, 0.018 and 0.021 per Gd atom in GdP, GdAs and GdSb, respectively.

These electrons and holes have small effective masses. In the case of GdSb, for instance, the averaged effective masses of the holes are $0.1 m$, $0.2 m$ and $0.3 m$, where $m$ is the free-electron mass; the holes with a larger Fermi surface have a larger effective mass. The effective masses of the electrons are $1.5 m$ in the $\Delta$ direction and $0.1 m$ both in the S and Z directions. The electrons and holes in GdP and GdAs have effective masses of magnitude of the same order as in GdSb.

The density-of-states curve is calculated for each pnictide by means of a method proposed by Rath and Freeman, $^{19)}$ and the results are shown in Fig. 4. The calculated values of the density of states at the Fermi energy are 3.0, 3.0 and 4.5 in units of states/Ryd. cell for GdP, GdAs and GdSb, respectively.

All these results are obtained in the nonrelativistic approximation. The relativistic effects may change more or less the results obtained here. In the next section, the changes of the band structures due to the relativistic effects are investigated.

## §3. Relativistic Effects
To investigate the relativistic effects on the band structures, we treat these effects within the framework of first-order perturbation theory.

![](./images/812268615909769218_3.jpg)

Fig. 3. Self-consistent energy bands of paramagnetic Gd-pnictides. The symmetry labels are for rotational operations about the Gd nucleus. $E_{f}$ denotes the Fermi energy.

In a representation correct to $\alpha^{2}$, where $\alpha$ is the fine structure constant $(\alpha=1 / 137)$, the perturbation Hamiltonian consists of three well-known terms, i.e. the mass-velocity term, the Darwin term and the spin-orbit interaction term. $^{20)}$ The matrix elements of these terms are calculated using the APW wave functions determined in the previous calculation. Integrations are restricted within the APW spheres, and contributions from the region between the spheres are neglected. Mixing of states is taken into account at degenerate states.

At the $\Gamma$ point, the spin-orbit interaction splits the $\Gamma_{15}$ state into two levels: a fourfold-degenerate $\Gamma_{8}^{-}$ state and a doubly-degenerate $\Gamma_{6}^{-}$ state. $^{21)}$ Thus, the hole Fermi surfaces may

![](./images/812268615909769218_4.jpg)

Fig. 4. Density of states of Gd-pnictides calculated from the self-consistent bands. $E_{f}$ denotes the Fermi energy.

be affected considerably. The calculated value of the spin-orbit splitting at the $\Gamma_{15}$ state in GdSb and GdAs is 0.065 Ryd and 0.035 Ryd, respectively. The spin-orbit splitting is negligibly small in GdP and GdN.

The spin-orbit interaction splits also the $X_{5}$ state into two levels, but it is found that the electron Fermi surface at X is affected only slightly.

In GdSb and GdAs, it is found that the $\Gamma_{6}^{-}$ state comes below the $\Gamma_{8}^{-}$ state. Therefore, one of the hole Fermi surfaces, the smallest one, tends to disappear due to the relativistic effects. According to this change of the hole surface, the carrier number is decreased by about $10 \%$.

The Fermi surface in GdSb and GdAs now consists of two nearly spherical hole surfaces centred at $\Gamma$ and three ellipsoidal electron surfaces at X. In GdP, the Fermi surface is not affected considerably, but remains almost the same as in the nonrelativistic case.

The relativistic effects on the valence bands of GdN are negligibly small, because the mixing of the Gd states into the valence bands is not so large as in the other pnictides, and in the N atom the relativistic effects are originally small. The relativistic energy shifts at the $X_{3}$ state in the conduction band is also small, $-0.015$ Ryd (0.2 eV), because this state consists largely of plane waves as well as the 5d state of Gd. Note that the energy branches starting from $X_{3}$ are of the nearly-free-electron type; the effective mass is $1.3 m$ and $0.2 m$ in the $\Delta$ and S directions, respectively. Thus, the band gap may be slightly decreased by the relativistic energy shift. In the present calculation, the band gap of the self-consistent bands almost disappears.

## §4. Comparison with Experiments and Discussions

As explained in $\S 2$, the self-consistent calculation yields the band structures which account qualitatively well for the observed trend of the conducting properties through the pnictides. In $\S 3$, the relativistic effects are investigated using first-order perturbation theory, and it is found that this qualitative results is not changed.

The calculated density of states at the Fermi energy is $3.0 \sim 4.5$ states/Ryd.cell for the three metallic pnictides. This calculated value should be compared with the experimental value for nonmagnetic La-pnictides, because the calculation has been carried out for a paramagnetic state. The experimental density of states at the Fermi energy derived from the electronic specific heat is 4.6 states/Ryd.cell both for LaP and LaSb. $^{8)}$ The agreement between theory and experiment is reasonably good.

Occupied portions of the density-of-states curve of GdSb, as shown in Fig. 4(d), have a shape similar to the experimental result derived from the measurement of photoemission. $^{7)}$ In the experimental density-of-states curve, a peak is found at 2 eV below the Fermi level. A maximum peak of the calculated result is located at about 1.5 eV below the Fermi level, in reasonably agreement with the experimental result.

The calculation shows that the Fermi surface of the metallic pnictides consists of the electron and hole surfaces. This property may be ob- served in an optical property, as measured on GdP. $^{5,6)}$ In the experimental works of refs. 5 and 6, the reflectivity of nonstoichiometric samples, $GdP_{x}(x \leqq 1)$ , was measured as a function of $x$ near $x=1$ . The concentration of free carriers, $n_{f}$ , was then deduced from theplasma frequency. It was found that $n_{f}$ is 0.07 per Gd atom at $x=1$ , and depends on $x$ only weakly as $x$ decreases; it is nearly constant in the range $0.93<x<1$ . This result is naturally understood by the Fermi surface obtained in the present calculation. If the carrier consists of electrons and holes as in the calculated bands, $n_{f}$ may remain nearly constant as $x$ decreases until the hole surfaces are filled up, because electrons and holes contribute to the plasmafrequency independently in the form, $n_{e} /|m_{e}^{*}|+$  $n_{h} /|m_{h}^{*}|$ , where $m_{e}^{*}$ and $m_{h}^{*}$ are the effectivemasses of electrons and holes, respectively. $^{22)}$  Therefore, an accurate measurement of the plasma frequency as a function of $x$ may provide a useful information on the Fermi surface in metallic pnictides. Further experi- mental investigations are desirable.

Topology of the Fermi surface of the metallic pnictides is simple. It is nearly spherical or ellipsoidal. There is no open orbit. An interest- ing fermiology related to the metallic pnictides is explained in the introduction. A critical(external) magnetic field, $H_{c}$ , at which the antiferromagnetic ordering is destroyed and the ferromagnetic ordering is produced, is estimated from the exchange constants for the localized $spins^{9,23)}$ as $H_{c}=90,190$ and 350 in units of kOe for GdP, GdAs and GdSb, respectively. It is not difficult to attain these magnetic fields by means of usual superconducting solenoids, especially for GdP and GdAs.

Qualitative features of the Fermi surface of the pnictides in an induced-ferromagnetic state are easily obtained on the basis of the calculated results in a paramagnetic state. Since the exchange interaction between the $n p$ state of the pnictogen and the $4 f$ state of $Gd$ is much smaller than those between the $6 ~s$ or $5 ~d$ states and the $4 f$ state in $Gd$ , a change of the hole surfaces at $\Gamma$ may be neglected in a lowest order approximation.

The electron surfaces at $X$ , however, consists largely of the $5 ~d$ state of $Gd$ , so that they may be affected by the $5 ~d-4 f$ exchange interaction. The exchange splitting due to this interaction is estimated as about 0.07 Ryd. $^{24)}$ As seen in Fig. 3, in a ferromagnetic state the $X_{3}$ state of the down-spin electron may be pushed up above the Fermi level, whereas that of the up-spin electron is pushed down. Thus, it is found that the Fermi surface of the down-spin electron may disappear, whereas the size of the Fermi surface of the up-spin electron is accordingly enlarged.

The calculated energy gap between the valence and conduction bands in GdN may be too small, though its precise magnitude has not been established experimentally. It seems that the self-consistent APW method adopted in the present calculation tends to give an insulator the energy gap which is usually smaller than an experimental value by about 1 eV; in the self-consistent bands of $KCl^{25)}$ and $EuS,^{26)}$ for instance, the calculated energy gap is smaller than experimental values by 1.2 eV and 1.0 eV, respectively. Thus, it is safe to suggest that the energy gap of GdN in reality may be about 1 eV.

The band structures of GdN in a ferromag- netic state is estimated in a similar manner. But, if the band gap is about 1 eV, as just suggested, a situation is critical. Then, the5d-4f exchange splitting is comparable to the energy gap, so that in a ferromagnetic state the third and fourth energy branches may overlap at the $X$ point, just as in the other pnictides, thus exhibiting a metallic character. We must be careful, however, to construct the band structures in magnetic semiconductors, because there the magnetic polaron effect plays an important role in the change of the bandstructures due to magnetic ordering. $^{27-29)}$  Experimental investigations on this point are also desirable.
