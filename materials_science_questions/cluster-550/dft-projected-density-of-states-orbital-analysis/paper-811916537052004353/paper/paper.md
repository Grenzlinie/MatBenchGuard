# Field electron emission enhancement of amorphous carbon through a niobium buffer layer

L. Xu, C. Wang, C. Q. Hu, W. X. Yu, Z. D. Zhao, and W. T. Zheng${}^{a)}$

Department of Materials Science, Key Laboratory of Automobile Materials of MOE, and State Key Laboratory of Integrated Optoelectronics, Jilin University, Changchun 130012, China

(Received 29 January 2008; accepted 30 March 2008; published online 13 June 2008)

Field electron emission for amorphous carbon ($a$-C) films deposited on Si (100) substrates with a niobium buffer layer is investigated. It is found that the Nb buffer layer can substantially improve the electron field emission properties of the $a$-C films, which can be attributed to an increase in the enhancement factor $\beta$ on the surface of the $a$-C films after insertion of the Nb layer. Moreover, the electron field emission can be further enhanced by annealing $a$-C/Nb/Si, which can be ascribed to the formation of NbC phase at the interface between $a$-C and Nb layer, revealed by x-ray diffraction for annealed $a$-C/Nb/Si. The first-principles calculated results show that the formation of NbC can lower the interface barrier and improve the back contact between Nb and $a$-C films, enhancing the field electron emission of $a$-C. © 2008 American Institute of Physics. [DOI: 10.1063/1.2939346]

## I. INTRODUCTION

Field electron emission from carbon related materials such as diamondlike carbon,${}^{1}$ diamond,${}^{2}$ carbon nanotubes,${}^{3}$ and amorphous carbon ($a$-C) films has been widely studied. These carbon-based emitters have been reported to show good electron field emission properties with a moderately low electric field${}^{4-6}$ because of their unique properties such as low work function and outstanding chemical inertness.${}^{7}$ $a$-C films is a promising field emitter that is very favorable to enhancing field emission. Some efforts have been made to improve the emission properties of $a$-C films and most work has focused on the surface treatment,${}^{8}$ doping,${}^{9-11}$ and the quality of the films in order to improve their field emission properties. However, so far, there have been only a few reports about how buffer layer between $a$-C film and substrate does influence the field emission of carbon films, where Ti,${}^{12}$ Au,${}^{13}$ Al, and Cu (Ref. 14) layer have been used as a buffer layer, respectively. Even though a few mechanisms have been proposed, the influence of buffer layer on field emission for $a$-C film is not well understood and the phenomena that are widely observed experimentally are not well explained yet. Therefore, further investigation is required to promote our understanding of these phenomena.

As a transition metal, niobium has numerous attractive properties, such as low density, good strength, and electric conductivity, which make it promising candidates for applications in the field of superconductivity.${}^{15,16}$ It is expected that the insertion of metallic Nb between $a$-C films and Si substrates may improve the field electron emission of $a$-C films due to high-quality contacts between $a$-C films and substrates and the formation of an effective conduction channel. However, up until now, no investigation concerning the effects of the niobium buffer layer on the field electron emission for $a$-C films has been performed.

In this work, the influence of a niobium buffer layer between $a$-C film and Si substrate on the field electron emission for $a$-C films, deposited by magnetron sputtering, is reported. Also, the first-principles calculations are used to reveal the formation of NbC phase at the interface between buffer and $a$-C layer and the mechanism for enhancing the field electron emission of $a$-C with a Nb buffer layer.

## II. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

A niobium layer with a thickness of 150 nm was deposited by direct current magnetron sputtering in Ar discharge at pressure of 0.8 Pa. Single-crystal Si (100) with a resistivity of $5\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}$ was used as substrate. The base pressure in the chamber was lower than $6\times 10^{-4}$ Pa. Before being introduced into the vacuum chamber, the Si substrates were cleaned ultrasonically in acetone and petroleum ether, consecutively, and boiled in mixed organic solvents in certain proportion. Finally, the Si substrate was put into HF acid to eliminate $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ on the surface of the substrate. As a result, $\mathrm{SiF}_{4}$ is formed to keep Si surface from oxidization. Before deposition, the substrate temperature was first raised up to $500\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (for 10 min) to eliminate SiF4, then was dropped and kept at the deposition temperature of $400\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. During deposition, discharge current on Nb target (purity of 99.999 at. %) was fixed at 0.25 A. The work pressure was always kept at 0.8 Pa. After deposition of Nb layer, $a$-C film with a thickness of 200 nm was deposited on the Nb layer using radio frequency magnetron sputtering pure graphite (99.99%) target with a diameter of 100 mm and thickness of 3 mm. The distance between the target and substrate holder was fixed at 80 mm, and substrate temperature, sputtering power, work pressure were kept at $200\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, 200 W, and 1.3 Pa, respectively. The as-deposited samples were annealed for 2 h at $1000\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ in a quartz-tube furnace, which had been evacuated to a pressure of $2\times 10^{-4}$ Pa to protect the film surface from oxidization during annealing treatment.

### III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns for as-deposited $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si, annealed $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si, respectively. For as-deposited $a$-C/Si [Fig. 1(a)] and annealed $a$-C/Si [Fig. 1(c)], only a noise background appears in XRD pattern in addition to the peaks at $2\theta{=}69^\circ$ and $62^\circ$ from Si (100) and Si (420), which means that the carbon film is amorphous. A peak at $2\theta{=}37.7^\circ$ from Nb (110) can be found in the XRD pattern [Fig. 1(b)] for as-deposited $a$-C/Nb/Si. In contrast, the peak from Nb (110) shifts to $37.4^\circ$ for annealed $a$-C/Nb/Si [Fig. 1(d)]. Leroy *et al.*$^{17}$ reported that upon annealing, the peak from Nb in XRD pattern shifted toward lower $2\theta$ values, which is in good agreement with our result. It is interesting that other peaks also appear in addition to the peak from Nb (110) in Fig. 1(d). The peaks at $2\theta{=}40.3^\circ$ and $58.9^\circ$ can be ascribed to NbC (200) and NbC (220), respectively, whereas the peak at $41.9^\circ$ can be attributed to $\text{Nb}_5\text{Si}_3$ (112), which means that NbC and $\text{Nb}_5\text{Si}_3$ phases are formed only in the film annealed, rather than as-deposited film.

Figure 2 shows the Raman spectra for as-deposited $a$-C/Si, $a$-C/Nb/Si, annealed $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si, respectively. For as-deposited $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si samples [Figs. 2(a) and 2(b)], there is only one symmetrical peak ($G$ band) at $1590\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, which is due to in-plane bond-stretching motion of pairs of the $sp^2$ carbon atoms.$^{18}$ However, after annealing another broad peak at $1348\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ ($D$ band) appears in addition to the peak at $1590\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ ($G$ band). This $D$ band is due to disorder-allowed phonon modes, which become Raman active as a result of destroying long-range order in amorphous graphitic materials. It is well known that the intensity ratio of the $D$ peak to the $G$ peak, $I(D)/I(G)$, is very sensitive to the structural change in $sp^2$ hybridized carbon domains, which can reflect the variation of $sp^2$/$sp^3$ bonding ratio in the $a$-C films. The presence of $D$ band around $1348\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ after annealing indicates that a conversion from $sp^3$ C to $sp^2$ C takes place in $a$-C films.$^{19}$ It should be noted that there is no appreciable difference in the spectral characteristic either between as-deposited $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si samples or between annealed $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si samples, which may imply that the insertion of Nb does not significantly change the bonding structure of the $a$-C films.

Figure 3 exhibits AFM images for as-deposited $a$-C/Si, $a$-C/Nb/Si, annealed $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si, respectively. The surface for the as-deposited $a$-C/Si film [Fig. 3(a)] is relatively smooth. However, as the niobium layer is introduced between $a$-C layer and Si substrate [Fig. 3(b)], the surface becomes rougher. As for annealed $a$-C/Nb/Si [Fig. 3(d)], its surface does not change remarkably comparing to the as-deposited $a$-C/Nb/Si. The root-mean-square roughness ($R_{\text{rms}}$) of the surfaces for four samples is listed in Table I. It can be seen that the roughness of as-deposited $a$-C/Si, $a$-C/Nb/Si, annealed $a$-C/Si and $a$-C/Nb/Si is 0.7, 5.7, 0.8, and 6.0 nm, respectively. This implies that the roughness of $a$-C films can be significantly influenced by the insertion of Nb layer, whereas it keeps almost unchanged for the annealing treatment.

It is well known that the enhancement factor $\beta$ can reflect the ability of the emitters to enhance the local electric field at the tip compared to the average macroscopic value. The bigger the enhancement factor $\beta$, the better the field emission property for $a$-C films. Therefore, the field electron emission enhancement of $a$-C films in the present case can be mainly attributed to an increase in the enhancement factor $\beta$ on the surface of $a$-C films after insertion of the Nb layer. In addition, a Nb metallic buffer layer, as a conduction channel, can enhance the transport of electrons from the substrates into $a$-C films during field emission, which may also contribute to the field electron emission enhancement of $a$-C films.

The field emission property of annealed $a$-C/Nb/Si, compared to as-deposited $a$-C/Nb/Si, has been remarkably improved. One possible reason may be attributed to the different surface morphologies, but Table I shows that there is no significant difference in the roughness between as-deposited and annealed $a$-C/Nb/Si. Hence, the enhancement caused by geometry could be negligible in the present study. The bonding structure is also an important factor to influence the emission behavior of $a$-C films. It has been reported that the more $sp^{2}$ content, the better field emission properties for $a$-C films. $^{21,22}$ Above, Raman analysis indicates that a conversion from $sp^{3}$ C to $sp^{2}$ C takes place in $a$-C/Nb films after annealing, which seems to be the reason for the field emission enhancement. However, it should be noted that the above Raman analysis indicates that $a$-C and $a$-C/Nb exhibit a similar bonding structure whenever after or before annealing, whereas the enhancement for annealed $a$-C and $a$-C/Nb comparing to as-deposited ones are quite different. In the case of $a$-C/Nb/Si, the $E_{0.5}$ varies from 5.5 to $4 \mathrm{~V} / \mu \mathrm{m}$ with a decrease in $27 \%$ after annealing, while for $a$-C/Si, it only changes from 7 to $6.3 \mathrm{~V} / \mu \mathrm{m}$ with a decrease in $10 \%$ after annealing, which may imply that the variation in the ratio of $sp^{2} / sp^{3}$ after heat treatment is not the main reason for the field emission enhancement for annealed $a$-C/Nb/Si.

It is worth noting that XRD patterns in Fig. 1 shows the formation of a new phase of NbC after annealing, which may play an important role in the field emission enhancement. In order to investigate the relationship between the NbC phase and field emission property, we have calculated the electronic structure of bulk NbC and Nb using DMOL3 code, which is based on density function theory (DFT). $^{23,24}$ In our DFT calculations, the Kohn-Sham wave functions are expanded in a double atomic basis set. The generalized gradient approximation with Perdew and Wang $^{25}$ correlation gradient correction is used to describe the exchange and correlation energy in all calculations. Self-consistent field procedure is done with a convergence criterion of $10^{-6}$ hartree. The structures of the Nb and NbC are shown in Figs. 6(a) (inset) and 6(b) (inset), respectively. The lattice constants are calculated to be 0.450 and 0.332 nm for NbC and Nb, respectively, both are in reasonable agreement with the experimental values. The total and partial density of states (DOS) for Nb and NbC are shown in Figs. 6(a) and 6(b), which exhibits that $d$ state dominates in the conduction band of Nb. In the DOS for NbC, there are two high-density peaks below the Fermi level, the lower one comes mostly from the $s$ state of C, with a very small contribution of $d$ component of Nb, and the peak near $-10 \mathrm{eV}$ is associated with the $p$ orbital of $\mathrm{C}$ as well as the $d$ orbital of $\mathrm{Nb}$. The overlap of the Nb-$d$ and C-$p$ states indicates that a significant covalent contribution is present in Nb-C bond, which can make a good contact between Nb layer and the $a$-C film. The shape of the DOS for NbC near the Fermi level [between $-7$ and $-4 \mathrm{eV}$ in Fig. 6(b)] is quite similar to that for bulk Nb in the same energy region and these states are formed mainly by the $d$ states of Nb, which implies that the electrons near the Fermi level of NbC are delocalized. Simultaneously, there is no band gap around Fermi energy for NbC. These characters indicate that bulk NbC is electric conductive. The Fermi level for bulk NbC is higher than bulk Nb [Fig. 6(b)], meaning that the work function of the NbC is lower than that of Nb. Indeed, according to our calculations, the work functions of bulk

Our simulation results suggest that the formation of NbC decreases the interface barrier and improves the back contact between Nb and $a$-C films, which is the dominant reason for the field emission enhancement of the annealed $a$-C/Nb/Si. ## IV. CONCLUSIONS

The effect of a niobium buffer layer between $a$-C film and Si substrate on the field electron emission for $a$-C films, deposited by magnetron sputtering, has been investigated. It is found that the electron field emission properties of $a$-C films can be remarkably improved by insertion of the Nb layer, which is associated with an increase in the enhancement factor $\beta$ on the surface of the $a$-C films. Furthermore, it is found that the annealing treatment can promote a formation of NbC phase at the interface between $a$-C and Nb layer and lead to a further increase in the electron field emission for $a$-C/Nb/Si, and the first-principles calculations are used to explain the field electron emission enhancement. The results show that the formation of NbC can lower the interface barrier and improve the back contact between Nb and $a$-C films, favoring the field electron emission enhancement. This investigation sheds a deep light on the field emission mechanism for $a$-C film coated by a buffer layer on the substrate.
