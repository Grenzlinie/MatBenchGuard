# Theoretical study of structural, electronic, and thermal properties of $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Nb})$ Laves alloys

A. Kellou $^{\mathrm{a}, *}$, T. Grosdidier $^{\mathrm{b}}$, C. Coddet $^{\mathrm{a}}$, H. Aourag $^{\mathrm{a}}$

Available online 25 December 2004

## Abstract
The full-potential linearized augmented plane waves (FP-LAPW) method using the generalized gradient approximation within the framework of density functional theory is applied to the study of the lattice parameters, bulk modulii, and densities of states of $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$, and their $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}_{1-{ }_{x}} \mathrm{Nb}_{x}$ ternary alloys having the $\mathrm{C} 15$-Laves structure. The quasi-harmonic Debye model, using a set of total energy versus molar volume obtained with the FP-LAPW method, is applied to the study of the thermal and vibrational effects. The temperature effect on the structural parameters, thermal expansions, heat capacities, Grüneisen parameters, and Debye temperatures are determined from the non-equilibrium Gibbs functions and compared to available data.

## 1. Introduction
Various intermetallic phases are being studied for their potential as new structural materials for high temperature applications. The familiar aluminides (Ti, Al or Fe based) are promising candidates for applications up to about $1000{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. Other less common intermetallic phases, such as the Laves phases, are being explored for use at much higher temperatures. Laves structures are indeed characterized by high melting points that could bring a promising potential field for applications in aerospace and technological sectors [1,2]. Of particular interest are the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{X}(\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Nb})$ compounds which have good creep and oxidation resistances [2-5]. However, the cubic $\mathrm{MgCu}_{2}$-type structure (C15) of these compounds, which occurs at the stoichiometric composition, is very hard and extremely brittle at ambient temperatures [4-6]. One potential solution to this problem is the use of an in-situ composite consisting of a ductile matrix phase. This provides improved toughness when reinforced by the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{X}$ Laves phase. For example, chromium-based alloys containing the Laves phase $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{X}$ have recently been the subject of several investigations [7-11]. Another potential advantage of the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ phase is found in $\mathrm{Cu}$ based alloys where its stable precipitation gives an improved balance between high temperature strength and thermal conductivity [12].

Several theoretical and experimental studies have been devoted to these materials in order to understand such features as the phase transformation, deformation, fracture, cracking behavior, oxidation resistance, phase stability, elastic properties, and thermal stress [13-21]. For example, the microstructure, cracking behaviour, thermal expansion, and mechanical properties of Laves phases in binary $\mathrm{Cr}-\mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Cr}-\mathrm{Zr}$ and ternary $\mathrm{Cr}-(\mathrm{Nb}, \mathrm{Zr})$ alloys have been the subject of several investigations [22-25]. Although several calculation methods have been succes- sively applied to predict the stable ground state structure of various Laves phases, these calculations are: (i) al- ways limited to the binary stoichiometric compounds and (ii) very often restricted to 0 K [27].

In order to avoid these limitations, the full-potential linearized augmented plane waves (FP-LAPW) method is applied in the present work to investigate the structural and electronic properties of ternary alloys in the C15- Laves phase. In addition, the quasi-harmonic Debye model, which is added within the framework of the FP- LAPW, is used to reproduce the temperature effects. Thus, the aim of this work is threefold: (i) testing the validity of the quasi-harmonic Debye model using the to- tal energy and molar volume set obtained with the FP-LAPW method and making it suitable for a large number of solids, (ii) highlighting the stability of ternary C15-Laves alloys for the Cr-Zr-Nb system, and (iii) giving electronic and vibrational properties of this phase since there is a lack of experimental data.

The parameters and technical details of the calcula- tions related to the FP-LAPW method and the quasi- harmonic Debye model are presented in the second section of this paper. The results are given and discussed in the third section within two subsections dealing suc- cessively with: (i) the structural and electronic properties and (ii) the thermal effects. The concluding remarks are presented in the fourth section.

## 2. Computational methods

All calculations in this study were done within the framework of the DFT, according to which the many- body problem of interacting electrons and nuclei was mapped to a series of one-electron equations, the so- called Kohn-Sham (KS) equations. The self-consistent calculations were carried out for all structures using a scalar-relativistic version of the FP-LAPW method [28]. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) approximation of Perdew et al. [29] (PBE) to the local density approximation was taken to include the ex- change-correlation energy to the total energy. Inside the muffin-tin spheres, the wavefunctions, electron charge densities, and potentials were expanded in terms of the spherical harmonics, while for the interstitial re- gion between the spheres plane-wave expansions were used. The spherical harmonics were expanded up to angular momenta $l_{max}=8$ inside the muffin-tin and $l_{max}=4$ for the interstitial region, to avoid any shape approximations. The core states were treated fully rela- tivistically in the frozen core approximation and the muffin-tin radii were taken as 2.10, 2.20, and 2.25 a.u. for Cr, Zr, and Nb, respectively. The present calcula- tions were done without spin-orbit coupling (SOC).

Laves phases generally crystallize into one of the three topologically close-packed structures: cubic C15 $(CuMg_{2})$, hexagonal C14 $(MgZr_{2})$, and C36 $(MgNi_{2})$. As the Laves phases are stabilized by size factor princi- ples and electronic structure, the $e/a$ ratio - defined, for transition elements, as the average number of $(p+d)$ electrons per atom - was identified as a key parameter in controlling the C14/C15 phase stability in $Cr_{2}Nb$ based transition-metal Laves alloys [30]. The lattice structure used in the calculations for the $Cr_{2}Zr$ and $Cr_{2}Nb$ compounds is similar to the $Cu_{2}Mg$ cubic Laves structure (C15) having a space group: $Fd3m$ (# 227). The C15 Laves phases have composition $AB_{2}$, where the A atoms are ordered as in diamond, and the B atoms form tetrahedra around the A atoms. The Cr(Zr,Nb) structures used in the calculation are like $Cu_{4}MgSn$ (F-43m, # 216), which is an ordered variant of the $Cu_{2}Mg$ structure. The 8-atom equipoint of the $Cr_{2}Mg$ type structure was subdivided into two different, or- dered, 4-point subsets in the $Cu_{4}MgSn$ structure. This structure is one of the most commonly observed Laves phases among the known ternary systems.

The correct temperature variation of the thermal properties can only be obtained by treating the lattice vibrations as quantized (phonons). The investigations of thermal effects in this study were done within the qua- si-harmonic Debye theory of crystals, without having to make extensive and complicated lattice dynamics calculations.

The isotropic approximation was used to determine the Debye temperature given as

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{D}}=\hbar\left(6 \pi^{2} V^{1 / 2} n\right)^{1 / 3} f(\sigma) \sqrt{\frac{B_{\mathrm{S}}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}^{2} M}}. \quad(1)
$$

In this equation, $V, M, n, B_{\mathrm{S}}, f(\sigma)$ and $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ are the molar volume, the molar mass, the number of atoms per for- mula unit, the adiabatic bulk modulus, a scaling func- tion that depends on the Poisson's ratio of the isotropic solid, and the Boltzmann constant, respec- tively. A simple way to use the Debye model is to con- sider that the adiabatic bulk modulus is equal to the isothermal bulk modulus, $B_{T}$, leading to the following equation:

$$
B_{\mathrm{S}} \approx B_{\mathrm{T}}=V\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2} E}{\mathrm{~d} V^{2}}\right). \quad (2)
$$

Here, $E$ is the total energy of the crystal at 0 K. Being given the energy of the considered phase $(E)$ as a func- tion of the molecular volume $(V)$ by using of the FP- LAPW method under static condition $(T=0$ K), the

quasi-harmonic Debye model allows: (i) to generate the Debye temperature $\theta_{\mathrm{D}}(V)$ from Eqs. (1) and (2), (ii) to obtain the non-equilibrium Gibbs function $G^{*}(V ; p, T)$:

$$G^{*}(V ; T, p)=E(V)+p V+A_{\mathrm{vib}}(T, \theta(V)),\tag{3}$$

where $A_{\mathrm{vib}}$ is the vibrational Helmholtz free energy given by the Debye model, and (iii) to minimize $G^{*}$ to derive the thermal equation of state (EOS), $V(p, T)$ and the chemical potential $G(p, T)$ of the corresponding phase [31]. The common thermodynamic parameters that depend on temperature and pressure are used to derive other macroscopic properties. A detailed description of this procedure can be found in [32] and references therein.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Structural and electronic properties of $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}_{1-x} N_{x}$ alloys

As a first step, a set of total energy calculation versus total volume, $E(V)$, for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Nb})$ alloys was carried out in order to determine the structural parameters. The corresponding values are fitted with universal Mur- naghan EOS. The obtained results for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ and $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ compounds are presented in Table 1. As shown in the table, the predicted lattice parameters are consistent with those measured experimentally by X-ray diffraction (room temperature) as well as with those calculated from theoretical methods. Equally good agreement is noticed between the measured and calculated bulk modulii. Moreover, the calculated results confirm that $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ is less compressible than $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$.

The variation of lattice parameters and bulk modulii with the Nb concentration $(x)$ is represented in Fig. 1(a) and (b), respectively. The lattice parameter of $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}_{1-x} \mathrm{Nb}_{x}$ alloys decreases with increasing the $\mathrm{Nb}$ concentration, whereas the bulk modulus increases. The linear variations of the lattice parameters and the bulk modulus with the Nb concentration indicate that the Vegard's law is valid for these alloys. Deviation from linearity, that would be represented by a bowing factor describing the structural disorder in the alloys, is not present; showing thereby that the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Nb})$ alloys are ordered in the entire range of composition regardless of the $\mathrm{Zr} / \mathrm{Nb}$ ratio.

The heats of formation of the studied alloys are determined as the difference of the C15 total energy and the sum of the body-centered cubic-$(b c c)$-Cr, hexagonal close packed-$(h c p)$-Zr, and $b c c$-Nb energies. Fig. 2 shows the dependence of the heat of formation with the Nb concentration $(x)$. The obtained values are always negative, which indicates that the formation of the C15-alloys is favored compared to the phase separation. Moreover, starting with $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$, the heat of formation decreases with $x$ from $0 \%$ to $50 \% \mathrm{Nb}$. In a similar manner, starting with $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$, the heat of formation decreases when Nb content is reduced from $100 \%$ to $50 \%$; the lowest value being obtained for $50 \mathrm{Zr}$ and $50 \mathrm{Nb} \%$. These are interesting results indicating that substitution of $\mathrm{Zr}$ on $\mathrm{Nb}$ sites in $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ or $\mathrm{Nb}$ on $\mathrm{Zr}$ sites in $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ does not destabilize the C15 structure. This is consistent with the recent determination of the 1573 K isothermal section of ternary phase diagrams established by Kim and Takasugi [38] Indeed, the authors found that the

Another way of clarifying the structural stability and the hardness of the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Nb})$ alloys is the determina- tion of their electronic structures.

Fig. 3 represents the total (DOS) and partial (PDOS) densities of states in the cases of $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}_{0.5} \mathrm{Nb}_{0.50}$, and $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$. All the DOS curves present two sets of peaks separated by the Fermi level localized in the vicin- ity of a fairly pronounced valley (deep minimum). The first set of peaks (in the low energy range) corresponds to the valence states whereas the second one (in the high energy range) represents the conduction states. The well- defined valley separating the bonding and antibonding states and the localisation of the Fermi level at the bond- ing-antibonding valley, was already reported in the lit- erature for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ [34] and $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ [39]. The discussion here only concentrates on the first set of peaks, since the second one is not well reproduced within the frame- work of the DFT which fails in dealing with excited states. The results in Fig. 3 indicate the metallic charac- ter in all these materials as well as the effects of the Zr/Nb concentration on the density and location of the Fermi level. The DOS at the Fermi level, $N(E_{\mathrm{F}})$, is 47.32 ,51.80 and 115.52 states/Ry for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}_{0.5} \mathrm{Nb}_{0.5}$, and $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$, respectively. The $N(E_{\mathrm{F}})$ of the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ com- pound is higher than that of the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ by more than a factor of 2. Note that our value for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ compares very well with the 115.8 states/Ry given by Ormeci et al. [39]. The deep minimum of the DOS is at the right of the Fermi level in $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ and quite displaced to the left in $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$. In fact, as illustrated in Fig. 3 for the case of $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}_{0.5} \mathrm{Nb}_{0.5}$, all the results for the $\mathrm{Cr}-\mathrm{Zr}-\mathrm{Nb}$ Laves phase investigated here showed a displacement of the Fermi level from the left to the right with increasing the Nb concentration. Clearly, the contribution of the Nb and the Zr atoms to the total DOS are quite differ- ent. In lower energies (full states), the PDOS profiles show that the Nb $d$-states contribution is more impor- tant than the Zr $d$-states. This confirms that the hybrid- ization between Cr and Nb atoms is effectively more important than the hybridization between Cr and Zr ones. This leads to higher potential hardness in the Cr- rich compounds, as already established from the bulk modulii (see Fig. 1(b)). It can be assumed, from the elec- tronic structures, that the ternary compounds $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathrm{Zr}_{x} \mathrm{Cr}_{1-x})$ in the C-15 Laves phase are more stable for Zr-rich concentration. This is consistent with the heats of formation that were previously shown in Fig. 2.

### 3.2. Thermal properties
Using a given set of total energy versus molar volume values, $E(V)$, and a numerical EOS, the thermal proper- ties were obtained for each alloy. As the Debye temper-ature in this model depends on the scaling function $f(\sigma)$ (see Eq. (1), in Section 2), values of the Poisson's ratios have to be introduced. In the literature, the reported Poisson's ratios are 0.32 [35] and 0.34 [40,41] for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ and $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$, respectively. These values were used in the present work and an averaged value of 0.33 is taken for the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Nb})$ alloys.

The lattice parameters and bulk modulii obtained with a numerical EOS, at $T=0$ K (static case) for $x=0,0.25,0.50,0.75,1.0$, were 7.138,7.086,7.039, $6.992,6.945$ Å and 177.84, 191.47, 204.18, 218.1,226.04 GPa, respectively. These results are in fairly good agreement with the Murnaghan EOS and the experi- mental results (see Table 1 and Fig. 1).

Since the C15-phases studied here exist only in the lower temperature range of the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathrm{Zr}, \mathrm{Nb})$ solids, the thermal properties were determined in the temperature range from 0 to 1500 K, where the quasi-harmonic model remains fully applicable. The effects of tempera- ture on the lattice parameters and bulk modulii are shown in Figs. 4(a) and (b), respectively. The lattice parameter of each alloy increases with increasing tem- perature but the rate of increase is more important for $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ above 600 K. In terms of the bulk modulus (Fig. 4(b)), there are clearly three categories of behav- ior. The effect of temperature on $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ is very moder- ate as the bulk modulus at 1500 K is still above220 GPa. For the Laves phases containing both Zr and Nb, the decrease in bulk modulus with tempera- ture is more drastic. Finally, for the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ alloy the $B$ value drops by more than $40\%$ between 0 and 1500 K. This indicates that $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Nb}$ stays a hard material at high temperature when compared to $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$. A very interesting point about these results is the fact that the rate of decrease does not appear to be dependent on the amount of Zr in the $\mathrm{Cr}-\mathrm{Zr}-\mathrm{Nb}$ Laves phases(Fig. 3(b)). Consequently, small amounts of Nb should be sufficient to improve significantly the hardness of the $\mathrm{Cr}_{2} \mathrm{Zr}$ compound. This interesting behavior was

Other properties which are related to the thermal effects are the thermal expansion, $\alpha(T)$, and the heat capacity, $C_V(T)$. As these two properties have anharmonic behaviors, one would not expect linear variation with Zr/Nb ratio. Fig. 5(a) represents the variation of the (volume) expansion coefficient, $\alpha(T)$, as function of the temperature. After a sharp increase, up to $\sim 300$ K, due the electronic contributions and roughly similar for all compositions, the behavior of the $Cr_2Nb$, $Cr_2Zr$, and $Cr_2(Zr,Nb)$ alloys are somehow quite different between 300 and 1500 K. As shown in Fig. 5(a), the $Cr_2Nb$ alloy has a fairly constant thermal expansion. Comparatively, the expansion coefficient of $Cr_2Zr$ exhibits a parabolic behavior at high temperature while the $Cr_2$–(Zr,Nb) ones show a linear dependence with the temperature. The values for the $Cr_2$–(Zr,Nb) alloys are lower than that for $Cr_2Zr$ and higher than the $Cr_2Nb$ ones.

Another vibrational property is the heat capacity and its dependence on temperature. The calculated values for the studied alloys are compiled in Table 2. The values follow the Debye model at low temperature $(C_V(T) \sim T^3)$ and the classical behavior $(C_V(T) \sim 3R$ for mono-atomic solids) is found at sufficient high temperatures; obeying Dulong and Petit's Rule. The specific heat capacity of the three materials at sufficient high temperature does not depend much on temperature and tends to approach $75\ \text{J}\ \text{mol}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-1}$.

The calculated properties at different temperatures are very sensitive to the vibrational modes. In the quasi-harmonic Debye model, the Grüneisen parameter, $\gamma_{\text{G}}(T)$, and the Debye temperature, $\Theta_{\text{D}}(T)$, are two key quantities. In Fig. 5(b), the Grüneisen parameter is plotted for the different temperature values at $p=0$ GPa. The Grüneisen parameter decreases with temperature for $\text{Cr}_2\text{Nb}$ while it increases for $\text{Cr}_2\text{Zr}$. The plotted values vary from 1.8 to 2.5, which show that the vibrational modes in $\text{Cr}_2\text{Nb}$ and $\text{Cr}_2\text{Zr}$ are quite different and sensitive to the $\text{Zr/Nb}$ ratio in the ternary compounds. The values of the Debye temperature are given in Table 3 for 0, 300, and 600 K. A very good agreement is found for the $\text{Cr}_2\text{Nb}$ compounds at 300 K between our calculated results and the experimental data of Thoma et al. [42]. Though the calculated value obtained by Mayer et al. [33] for $\text{Cr}_2\text{Nb}$ is higher than these results, the fact that they found a lower Debye temperature for $\text{Cr}_2\text{Zr}$ compared to $\text{Cr}_2\text{Nb}$ is consistent with our values. It is well known that hard materials exhibit elevated Debye temperatures. This is the case for the $\text{Cr}_2\text{Nb}$ and $\text{Cr}_2\text{Zr}$ compounds for all the temperature range. Finally, our calculations give a fairly accurate estimation of the Debye temperatures and depict well its evolution in $\text{Cr}_2(\text{Zr,Nb})$ alloys. The trend is to increase with the Nb concentration and to decrease with temperature.

## 4. Conclusion

The aim of this work was to use ab initio calculations (with the FP-LAPW method) to give new insights on structural, electronic, and thermal properties of binary and ternary C15-Laves compounds in the Cr–Zr–Nb system. The temperature effects have been defined using the quasi-harmonic Debye model exploiting the total energy calculations of the FP-LAPW method. The main results can be summarized as follows:

1. The calculated lattice parameter and bulk modulus of Cr₂Zr and Cr₂Nb are in good agreement with experimental data. The lattice parameters of the Cr₂(Zr,Nb) compounds decrease with Nb concentration, whereas the bulk modulii increase.
2. The calculated heats of formations show that the Cr–Zr–Nb system favors ternary C15-Cr₂(Zr,Nb) compounds formation over phase separation. In addition, the structural properties obey the Vegard’s law, indicating that Cr₂(Zr,Nb) are ordered alloys regardless of the Zr/Nb ratio.
3. The total densities of states have shown that the stability of the ternary Laves compound Cr₂(Zr–Nb) decreases with increasing Nb content. The partial densities of states (d-states) of the Nb atoms are more important than the Zr ones in the low energy range. This leads to a strong hybridization between Nb and Cr d-states.
4. Bulk modulus values as well as the type of hybridization between Cr–Nb and Cr–Zr suggest higher hardness potential for Cr₂Nb than for Cr₂(Zr,Nb), and finally for Cr₂Zr.
5. The quasi-harmonic Debye model was successfully applied to determine the thermal properties of the Cr₂(Zr,Nb) alloys in the 0–1500 K temperature range. Significant differences in properties were observed above 300 K.
6. The behavior of the Laves phases in the Cr–Zr–Nb system is very sensitive to the Zr/Nb ratio. Our results indicate that the effects of temperature on bulk modulus and volume expansions are very drastic in Cr₂Zr and less important in Cr₂Nb.

Finally, the approach presented here is a promising alternative for the determination of several properties in complex structures that are difficult and/or costly to obtain from experiments.
