# First Principles Study on ${\rm PbFe_{0.5}Nb_{0.5}O_3}$ *

WANG Yuan-Xu(王渊旭) $^{1,2,3**}$, WANG Chun-Lei(王春雷) $^{1,2}$, ZHAO Ming-Lei(赵明磊) $^{1}$, ZHANG Jia-Liang(张家良) $^{1}$

$^{1}$School of Physics and Microelectronics, Shandong University, Jinan 250100
$^{2}$Laboratory of Solid State Microstructures, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210008
$^{3}$Computational Materials Science Centre, National Institute for Materials Science, Tsukuba 305-0044, Japan

(Received 6 July 2004)

The full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FLAPW) method is used to study the crystal structure and electronic structure properties of ${\rm PbFe_{0.5}Nb_{0.5}O_3}$ (PFN). The optimized crystal structure, density of states, band structure and electron density distribution have been obtained to understand the ferroelectric behaviour of PFN. The analysis result of the density of states shows there is an obvious change of Nb $d$ states in the paraelectric-to-ferroelectric phase transition. The polarization result shows that the contribution to ferroelectricity of Nb atoms is larger than that of Fe atoms. In ferroelectric phase there is a hybridization of Fe $d$--O $p$ and Nb $d$--O $p$ in ferroelectric PFN. This is consistent with the result of the electronic band structure. This hybridization is responsible for the tendency to its ferroelectricity.

PACS: 77.80.-e, 77.15.Ap

Perovskite ${\rm ABO_3}$ materials have been extensively studied because of their technical importance and the fundamental interest in the physics of their phase transitions.$^{[1,2]}$ Most perovskite ferroelectrics that have been widely used are solid solutions (such as ${\rm PbZr_{1-x}Ti_xO_3}$, i.e. PZT) and complex compounds (two or more kinds of atoms occupy the same crystallographic positions, such as ${\rm PbMg_{1/3}Nb_{2/3}O_3}$). First principle calculations on these complicated systems are very interesting. Lead iron niobate ${\rm PbFe_{0.5}Nb_{0.5}O_3}$ (PFN) is a complex compound ferroelectric with Fe and Nb occupying the B-site in the ${\rm ABO_3}$ perovskite structure. Its ferroelectricity at room temperature was discovered in 1957 by Smolenskii $et$ $al$.$^{[3]}$ Since then, many researchers have studied its phase transition and other properties.$^{[4-7]}$ Apart from the bulk ceramic, the ferroelectric, dielectric and leakage current properties show that it is a potential material for nonvolatile ferroelectric memory and high dielectric constant DRAM applications.$^{[8]}$ At 376 K, it undergoes a ferroelectric phase transition from cubic phase to tetragonal phase.$^{[9]}$ The occupation of Fe and Nb in the site B was examined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM).$^{[8]}$ The (1/2, 1/2, 1/2) superlattice reflection plots which indicate the ordering of the occupation of irons in site B could not be found, thus confirming that Fe and Nb randomly distributed on site B. The fine structure was investigated in our previous paper.$^{[10]}$ To our knowledge, there is little investigation on the electronic structure of PFN by first principles calculation. In order to well know the origin of its ferroelectricity, we calculated its electronic structure (density of states, band structure) by the FLAPW method within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA).

The calculations presented in this work are performed within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA)$^{[11]}$ to density functional theory, using the FLAPW method (WIEN2k).$^{[12]}$ In order to calculate the electronic structure of ${\rm PbFe_{0.5}Nb_{0.5}O_3}$, we construct a supercell for it. In the [001] direction, there are two cells, ${\rm PbFeO_3}$ and ${\rm PbNbO_3}$. The system structure parameters are optimized by the first principle calculation. In the electronic structure calculation, we use our optimized parameters. The atomic sphere radii $R_i=2.0, 1.8, 1.9, 1.6$ are used for Pb, Fe, Nb, and O, respectively. We use $7\times7\times3$ meshes, which corresponds to 18 $k$ points in the irreducible Brillouin zone. and $RK_{\max}$ ($R_{mt}K_{\max}$, where $K_{\max}$ is the plane wave cutoff and $R_{mt}$ is the smallest among all atomic sphere radii) which controls the size of the basis sets in these calculations and is set to be 8.0 to calculate the GGA results.

Table 1 lists our theoretical optimized atomic coordinates. In order to understand the role of Fe and Nb in ferroelectric PFN, we calculate its electronic structure. Figure 2 shows the density of states (DOS) of paraelectric PFN. In Fig. 2, there is a sharp peak around 0 eV of Fe $d$. And for Nb $d$, there are three main peaks all above 0 eV. Thus, we can see from Fig. 2 that in paraelectric phase, the DOS peaks are at different energy points. Therefore, the electrons of these three atoms distribute in different energy ranges. This implies the covalent interaction between atoms B (Nb, Fe) and O is weak. The DOS of PFN in ferroelectric phase are given in Fig. 3. Compared to the paraelectric phase (Fig. 2), there is an obvious change of DOS for PFN. In particular for Nb $d$ electron states, the DOS energy shifting is large. In Fig. 3 for Nb $d$, there are mainly three peaks around $-5$, 0 and 2 eV. The highest peak is around 0 eV. During the transition from paraelectric to ferroelectric phase, the Nb $d$ electrons have an obvious change. In ferroelectric phase, the broad peaks from $-6$ to $-2$ eV of Nd and O $p$ are similar. This means the covalent interaction between Nb $d$ and O $p$ becomes very strong during the phase transition. Compared with Nb $d$, the DOS shape of Fe $d$ have a little change during the phase transition. This means that the state change of atoms Fe $d$ is small during the phase transition. Moreover, in Fig. 3, the DOS shapes of atoms Nb $d$ and Fe $d$ are very similar. There are two sharp peaks on about 0 and 2 eV and a broad peak in valence energy range. A similar character shows itself in the ferroelectric phase; Fe $d$ and Nb $d$ electrons at the same energy range increase greatly. In addition, in the energy range from $-6$ to $-2$ eV, B (Fe and Nb) $d$ and O $p$ have a broad DOS peak, and the peaks of the three atoms are similar. This means that there is a hybridization between B (Fe and Nb) $d$ and O $p$ and this hybridization weakens the short range repulsions and stabilizes the ferroelectricity. This strong hybridization also implies the interaction between atom B and O is highly covalent. In addition, the sharp peaks around of Fe $d$ and Nb $d$ indicate the ferroelectric PFN is more electrically conductive than normal ferroelectrics.

In order to know the contribution to ferroelectricity of atoms Nb and Fe respectively during phase transition, we calculate the spontaneous polarization of subcell $\text{PbFeO}_3$ and $\text{PbNbO}_3$ respectively by the Berry phase method. The polarization values

of $PbFeO_3$ is $18\,\mu\text{C/cm}^2$ and that of $PbNbO_3$ is $58\,\mu\text{C/cm}^2$. Thus, the polarization result shows that the contribution to ferroelectricity of Nb atoms is larger than that of Fe atoms. This result is the same to the analysis of DOS change of PFN during the phase transition from paraelectric to ferroelectric.

Fig. 4. The band structure of ferroelectric PFN.

Figure 4 shows the band structure of ferroelectric PFN. From $-6$ to $-2\,\text{eV}$, there are many bands. Compared with Fig. 3 in the same energy range, it can be seen that these bands are mainly from O $p$ and some of B $d$ electrons. These bands overlap each other. This means that there is a hybridization between the B $d$ and O $p$. The same conclusion has been obtained from the partial DOS analysis of B $d$ and O $p$. Around and above $0\,\text{eV}$, some conduction bands exist. Compared with Fig. 3, it can be assessed that these bands mainly consist of B $d$ and a little of O $p$. The existence of these bands indicates that ferroelectric PFN is more electrically conductive than normal ferroelectrics and this is consistent with the experimental result.$^{[13]}$ In Ref. [13], the authors show their experimental result of electric conductivity of PFN. In addition, there are two bands around $-9\,\text{eV}$. The two bands should come from Pb $6s$. The large dispersion of both the bands implies the free character. As in ferroelectric $PbTiO_3$, Pb $6s$ lies very close to the oxygen bands, which leads to the large Pb–O hybridization. Therefore, atom Pb in the ferroelectric PFN plays a similar role to that in pure $PbTiO_3$.

Figure 5 shows the electron density distribution of ferroelectric PFN in the (100) plane. In Fig. 5, we can see that the interaction between atom Fe and O or Nb and O is very strong. This means that there is strong hybridization between B (Ta and Nb) $d$ and O $p$.

In summary, we have performed FLAPW calculations to investigate the ferroelectricity of $PbFe_{0.5}Nb_{0.5}O_3$. The ground state structure and electronic properties are obtained. From the calculated DOS and electronic band structure, it is shown that during the paraelectric to ferroelectric phase transition, the states of Nb $d$ electrons has an obvious change and the interaction between Nb $d$ and O $p$ becomes very strong. The spontaneous polarization result shows that the contribution to ferroelectricity of Nb atoms is larger than that of Fe atoms. Moreover, in the ferroelectric phase, there is a strong hybridization between B (Fe and Nb) $d$ and O $p$ electrons. This hybridization is important to the ferroelectric stability of PFN. In addition, from the band structure analysis, the atom Pb has a significant effect on ferroelectricity of PFN and thus in ferroelectric PFN, atoms Pb play a important role as that in pure $PbTiO_3$.
