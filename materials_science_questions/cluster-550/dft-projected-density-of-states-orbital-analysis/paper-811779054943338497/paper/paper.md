# A first-principles investigation into the hydrogen bond interaction in β-HMX

CHEN Fang¹, ZHANG Hong¹,²*, ZHAO Feng³, MENG ChuanMin³ & CHENG XinLu¹

¹ Institute of Atomic and Molecular Physics, Sichuan University, Chengdu 610065, China;
² School of Physical Science and Technology, Sichuan University, Chengdu 610065, China;
³ Laboratory for Shock Wave and Detonation Physics Research, Southwest Institute of Fluid Physics, China Academy of Engineering Physics, Mianyang 621900, China

Received December 25, 2009; accepted January 21, 2010

A theoretical study of structural and electronic properties of β-HMX crystal is performed using density-functional theory (DFT). The total density of states (TDOS) is presented. The atomic orbit projected density of state (PDOS) from the p-type orbit of C, N, O and the s-type orbit of H is discussed. The study by analyzing the PDOS shows that the structure of β-HMX crystal possesses C—H···O intra- and inter-molecular hydrogen-bonding. There exists a hydrogen bonding between H₅-1s and O₁₂-2p orbits, between H₁₉-1s and O₂₈-2p orbits of intra molecules, and between H₁₉-1s and O₂₄-2p orbits of inter molecules. The Mulliken population analysis is also made.

structural property, hydrogen bond, β-HMX, population analysis

Octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine, a weak hydrogen-bonded molecular crystal, known as HMX, is an important and commonly used energetic ingredient in various high performance explosives and propellant formulations due to its thermal stability and high detonation velocity relative to other explosives. The term HMX usually stands for cyclotetramethylene-tetranitroamine or octogen (C₄H₈N₈O₈), which is a white crystal-line solid used mostly as a filler for polymer-bonded explosives [1]. HMX is known to exist in four crystalline phases, denoted as α, β, δ, and γ [2,3]. Figure 1(a) contains a three-dimensional rendering of the unit cell for β-HMX, which is the thermodynamically stable form under ambient conditions. Also, β-HMX has been determined to contain two C₄H₈N₈O₈ molecules per unit cell in a monoclinic lattice with space group P21/c by X-ray [4] and neutron [5] diffraction. In the β-phase lattice, each C₄H₈N₈O₈ molecule involves two NO₂ groups above and two below the ring plane. The unit cell of β-HMX crystal contains 56 atoms, and its experimental structural parameters are: $a$=6.54 Å, $b$=11.05 Å, $c$=8.70 Å, $β$=124.3° [5]. Hydrogen bonding often leads to highly ordered layering of molecular sheets in solids, which can be an important factor in determining the sensitivity of materials to shocking. But it is still an unclear issue that requires further experimental and theoretical studies.

β-HMX is widely used in propellants and explosives, and its macroscopic behavior is ultimately controlled by microscopic properties such as the electronic structure and interatomic forces. Thus, a systematic investigation into the basic properties of β-HMX as a solid energetic material appears very important in understanding its explosive properties. β-HMX has been intensively studied from both experimental and theoretical sides [6–17], including crystal properties, elastic properties, equations of states, and vibrational spectra. Lewis et al. [8] examined the electronic structure of the β-HMX using density functional theory

In this study, we report a systematic study of the structural and electronic properties of β-HMX from DFT. Our main purpose here is to examine the possible hydrogen bond interaction both intramolecular and intermolecular from the electronic properties, such as the total density of state (TDOS), atomic orbit projected density of state (PDOS) and Mulliken population analysis.

## 1 Computational details

The first-principles calculations for β-HMX were made using the CASTEP code [19] based on the DFT formalism [20,21]. The local density approximation (LDA) function proposed by Ceperley and Alder [22] and parameterized by Perdew and Zunder [23], named CA-PZ, was employed. The LDA limit is a reasonable approximation for the β-HMX crystal calculated here because HMX forms a molecular crystal largely via electrostatic interactions rather than via hydrogen-bonding interactions. The cutoff energy of plane waves was set to 400.0 eV. Brillouin zone sampling was performed by using the Monkhost-Pack scheme with a k-point grid of 2×2×2. The values of the kinetic energy cutoff and the k-point grid were determined to ensure the convergence of total energies. Starting from the above-mentioned experimental structures, we did the geometry relaxation to allow the ionic configurations, cell shape, and volume to change. For geometry optimization, ultrasoft Vanderbilt type pseudopotentials [24] were used given the following electronic configurations: C $2s^22p^2$, N $2s^22p^3$, O $2s^22p^4$ and H $1s^1$. In the geometry relaxation, the total energy of the system was converged less than $2.0×10^{-5}$ eV, the residual force less than 0.05 eV/Å, the displacement of atoms less than 0.002 Å, and the residual bulk stress less than 0.1 GPa. Based on the optimized structures, the TDOS, the atomic orbit PDOS and population analysis were evaluated. For the sake of a comparison with the experimental data of molecules in the crystal phase, the geometric parameters and the Mulliken population analysis of the single β-HMX molecule in a gaseous phase were calculated using B3LYP/6-31G(d,p). This level of theory (B3LYP/6-31G(d,p)) appeared adequate for optimizing the structure of similar hydrogen-bond systems [25–27]. The B3LYP method employs Becke's three-parameter hybrid exchange functional [28,29] as implemented by Stephens and his colleagues [30] and the correlation functional of Lee, Yang, and Parr [31].

## 2 Results and discussion

### 2.1 Geometry structures

The equilibrium structure for β-HMX crystal is shown in Figure 1. In the β-phase lattice, each $C_4H_8N_8O_8$ molecule has a so-called "chair" conformation in which two $NO_2$ groups are on one side of the $C_4N_4$ ring and the other two $NO_2$ groups are on the opposite side of the $C_4N_4$ ring. This conformation gives the molecule a center of inversion ($C_i$ symmetry). The calculated geometric parameters (bond lengths, bond angles) of the $C_4H_8N_8O_8$ molecule in the gas phase and in the crystal phase are listed in Table 1, along with the corresponding values from the experimental X-ray structure of molecules. The structure of β-HMX with atom numbering is shown in Figure 1(b). Comparing with the experimental data, we now scrutinize our calculated values from LDA. Our calculations overestimate $N_{18}—O_{25}$, $N_{18}—O_{24}$, and $N_8—C_2—H_6$ by 2.73%, 3.74% and 3.13%, respectively, while underestimating $H_5\cdots O_{12}$ by 2.28%. Such a discrepancy might be due to inaccurate determination for the hydrogen position in neutron diffraction. For example, the position labeled $H_5$ has a direct or indirect influence on the bond lengths and bond angles from the above reports. In general, our calculated geometric parameters in the crystal phase are in close agreement with the experimental data. Similarly, the calculated geometric parameters of the molecule in the gas phase overestimate $N_8—N_7$, $N_7—N_8—C_1$, and $N_{21}—C_{17}—N_{15}$ by 2.81%, 2.52% and 3.63%, respectively, overestimate $H_5\cdots O_{12}$ by 13.6%, and underestimate $N_8—N_7—O_{12}$ and $N_7—N_8—C_2$ by 2.46% and 3.21%. These changes of molecular geometry from the gas phase to the crystal phase are due to hydrogen bonding interactions and Fermi resonance present in the crystal lattice. We find that the above differences come mainly from the configuration of $C_1—N_8N_7O_{11}O_{12}—C_2$, indicating the group was strongly affected in the crystal phase. Compared to the experimental distance 2.19 Å of $H_5—O_{12}$, the distance for the intramolecular hydrogen bond is 2.14 Å in the crystal phase while the corresponding value is 2.487 Å in the gas phase.

From the hybridization, we can infer that parts of the charge transform from H 1s to O atoms, and there is a strong interaction between the O and H atom.

So the investigation into hydrogen bonds in the condensed phases is necessary. According to the charge-transfer theory, the more electron transfer, the more overlap between the valence electron orbits. From the data in Figure 2, we find that the valence electrons transfer from the weak electronegative H atom to the strong O atom between −7 eV and −6 eV, indicating the interactions occur in O and H atoms. The position of the hydrogen bond can be found from the electronic overlap in PDOS [32]. Here, we will analyze the hydrogen-bonding interaction from the PDOS of O 2p states and H 1s states. In Figure 3, we pre- sent the PDOS of the correlative O and H atoms in the β-HMX crystal. As can be expected, the resonance is found in the vicinity of Fermi level between $O_{12}$-2p and $H_{5}$-1s orbits, between $O_{24}$-2p and $H_{19}$-1s orbits, which is generally interpreted as evidence of hydrogen-bonding of non-bond atoms. Thus, the O-2p and H-1s orbits form the $C—H\cdots O$ intramolecular hydrogen bond and $C—H\cdots O$ intermolecular hydrogen bond, which agrees with the results of ref. [5] that $C—H_{5}\cdots O_{12}$ hydrogen bond distance was particularly short, 2.19 Å. Due to the symmetry of crystal structure, we also find the existence of intra molecular HB between $O_{28}$-2p and $H_{19}$-1s orbits.

To validate the reliability of our results, we have calcu- lated the Mulliken population analysis of the single β-HMX molecule in the gaseous phase and the crystallographically β-HMX molecule using B3LYP/6-31G (d, p) and LDA-CA- PZ, respectively. The corresponding characteristics of the atomic and bond populations of the constituent atoms and bonds are presented in Tables 2 and 3. A comparison be- tween the single β-HMX molecules and the bulk β-HMX molecules results is made. First, apart from the C atom, in the isolated molecule and the crystal, the charges on N at- oms in $-NO_{2}$ and all the H atoms are positive, whereas the N atoms in the ring skeleton and all O atoms carry negative charges. However, the positive charges on C atoms turn into negative from the isolated molecule in the gas phase to the crystal phase. For example, the results in Table 2 show in the single β-HMX molecules the atom $C_{1}$ and $C_{2}$ charges are 0.09 and 0.14, but in the bulk β-HMX molecules at $C_{1}$ and $C_{2}$ charge are −0.28 and −0.30. The difference in popu- lation between the single molecule and the bulk molecule points to strong intermolecular interactions in the bulk. Second, when the interatomic distances are less than the sum of the van der Waals, it can be regarded as hydrogen bonds formed [33]. The radii used in this study are H 1.20 Å and O 1.53 Å. The value of the bond population of $H_{5}\cdots O_{12}$ in the isolated molecule indicates 0.005, which is slightly less than that of the bulk, 0.01. This difference between the absolute values may be simply due to the difference be- tween the bond lengths of the $H_{5}\cdots O_{12}$ in the gas phase and the solid phase. In other words, the value of the bond length of $H_{5}\cdots O_{12}$ in the single molecule is 2.4866, which is larger than that of the crystal phase, 2.1403. The results suggest $C—H_{5}\cdots O_{12}—N$ intramolecular hydrogen bond interaction becomes stronger going from the gaseous phase to the crys- tal. Moreover, the $C—H_{19}\cdots O_{24}$ forms an intermolecular hydrogen bond, and the bond length and bond populations are 2.3441 Å and 0.01, respectively. ## 3 Conclusions

In summary, periodic DFT calculations and B3LYP/6-31G (d, p) are performed to study the structural and electronic properties of the energetic material $\beta$-HMX, and to disclose the origin of the hydrogen bond. The LDA/CA-PZ relaxed crystal structure and the calculated geometry of the $\beta$-HMX molecule in the gas phase match the experimental data. The calculated DOS shows that there is a hybridization of atomic orbits between O 2p and H 1s states in the energy range of $-7$ eV to $-6$ eV, indicating the interaction occur- ring between the O and H atom. The investigations of hy- drogen bond (HB) show the $\beta$-HMX crystal possesses intra- and inter-molecular hydrogen bonding of type $\mathrm{C-H\cdots O}$. For example, there are the intra-molecular HB between $\mathrm{H_{5}-1s}$ and $\mathrm{O_{12}-2p}$ orbits and between $\mathrm{H_{19}-1s}$ and $\mathrm{O_{28}-2p}$ orbits, and inter-molecular HB between $\mathrm{H_{19}-1s}$ and $\mathrm{O_{24}-2p}$ orbits. The population analysis shows that the donator turns into the acceptor for C atom from the isolated molecule in the gas phase to the crystal phase, and the differences of population suggest the existence of strong intermolecular interactions in the bulk. In addition, the analysis of $\mathrm{O-H}$ bonds population shows that the intramolecular interaction of hydrogen bonds in $\mathrm{C-H_{5}\cdots O_{12}-N}$ becomes stronger going from the gaseous phase to the crystal.
