# Surface electronic structure and magnetism of NiAs structured MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001)

J. I. Lee$^{\mathrm{a}}$
Department of Physics, Inha University, Incheon 402-751, Republic of Korea

S. C. Hong
Department of Physics, University of Ulsan, Ulsan 680-749, Republic of Korea

(Presented on 9 January 2007; received 30 October 2006; accepted 6 November 2006; published online 20 March 2007)

We investigated the surface electronic structure and magnetism of the NiAs structured MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001) using first-principles full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FLAPW) method within the generalized gradient approximation. We considered the Mn- and anion(As or Sb)-terminated surfaces for both the MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001) systems. The calculated magnetic moments of the surface Mn atoms for Mn-terminated MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001) systems are much enhanced to have $3.79\mu_{B}$ and $4.04\mu_{B}$, respectively, compared to the corresponding center layer values, $3.10\mu_{B}$ and $3.32\mu_{B}$. The anion atoms in the Mn- and anion-terminated systems have negative magnetic moments with the values between $-0.09\mu_{B}$ and $-0.18\mu_{B}$. The effects of surface termination and the band hybridization on the magnetism and the electronic structure are discussed with the calculated density of states. © 2007 American Institute of Physics. [DOI: 10.1063/1.2670093]

## I. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, the transition metal and anion binary compounds, $MX$ ($M$=Mn and Cr and $X$=As, Sb, N, and P), have attracted much attention for the possible application to the spintronic devices. Continenza et al. $^{1}$ studied the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of Mn pnictides, such as the MnAs, MnSb, and MnP, using the all-electron full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FLAPW) method. $^{2,3}$ The results showed that the NiAs structure is more stable than the zinc-blende (ZB) structure for MnAs and MnSb, and the ZB structured $MnX$ have larger magnetic moments with nearly half-metallic behavior. Hong et al. $^{4}$ also performed similar calculations for $MnX$ ($X$=N, P, As, and Sb) by psuedopotential method and obtained similar results.

For the ZB structured Cr and Mn binary compounds, there have been many electronic structure investigations since these compounds have the half-metallic properties which are useful for the spin transport device applications. $^{5-7}$ The surface electronic structures of the ZB MnAs(001) and CrAs(001) systems were also calculated to examine whether the half metallicity is maintained or not at the surfaces. It was found that the Mn(Cr)-terminated surface keeps and the As-terminated surface loses the half metallicity due to the dangling bonds of the surface As atoms.

In this study, we investigated the electronic structure and magnetism at the (0001) surfaces of the NiAs structured MnAs and MnSb which have ferromagnetic ground states, by means of the all-electron FLAPW (Refs. 2 and 3) method within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) (Ref. 8) to the exchange-correlation potential. We discuss the effects of surface termination and the hybridization between the anion-$p$ and Mn-$d$ and $d$-$d$ hybridizations between Mn atoms on the magnetism and electronic structures with the calculated density of states.

## II. METHOD OF CALCULATION

Since there are two types of surface termination along the (0001) plane, the Mn-terminated (Mn-Term) and anion $X$ (As or Sb)-terminated (As-Term and Sb-Term) surfaces for NiAs structured MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001) systems, we considered both of them. We took slabs consisted of 17- and 15-atomic layers to study the Mn-Term and As(Sb)-Term surfaces, respectively. The lattice constants were taken from the bulk equilibrium values given in Ref. 1.

The two-dimensional (2D) unit cell in the (0001) plane was chosen to be a rhombus with a side of $a$=7.000 a.u. for MnAs(0001) system and $a$=7.801 a.u. for MnSb(0001) one. The interlayer spacing between the Mn and As(Sb) atoms was chosen to be $c/4$, where $c$ is 2.607(2.638) a.u. for MnAs(Sb)(0001) slab. We have considered neither the surface relaxation nor the reconstruction.

The Kohn-Sham equation $^{9}$ was solved self-consistently in terms of FLAPW (Refs. 2 and 3) method within the GGA (Ref. 8) to the exchange-correlation potential. Core electrons were treated fully relativistically, while the conduction electrons were treated in the scalar relativistic approximation which takes into account all of relativistic terms except the spin-orbit coupling. $^{10}$ Lattice harmonics with angular momentum up to $l$=8 were employed to expand the charge density, potential, and wave functions inside the muffin-tin (MT) radii of 2.40 a.u. for Mn and Sb, and 2.00 a.u. for As atoms. A plane wave cutoff of 13.0 Ry was employed for the LAPW basis, corresponding to about 120 LAPW functions per atom.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

The spin-polarized layer-projected densities of states (LDOSs) of Mn-Term and As-Term of MnAs(0001) systems are presented in Figs. 1(a) and 1(b), and those for Mn-Term and Sb-Term of MnSb(0001) are presented in Figs. 2(a) and 2(b). The letters $S$, $S-1,\dots$, and $C$ denote the surface, subsurface, and center layers, respectively. If we compare the LDOS of the $\text{Mn}(C)$ atom in the Mn-Term of the MnAs(0001) system with that of MnSb(0001), the former has more clearly separated two main peaks than the latter one due to stronger Mn-$d$ and As-$p$ hybridizations, similar to the bulk case. However, the LDOSs for the $\text{Mn}(S)$'s in both systems have very narrow peak due to the surface termination which prevails the $p$-$d$ hybridization between surface Mn and subsurface anion atoms. We observe that the exchange splitting at the Mn-Term surfaces for both systems is increased about 0.8 eV compared with that of the center layer, which causes the increase of the magnetic moments of $\text{Mn}(S)$ atoms (Table I). We also observe sharp peaks at $\sim -1$ eV in the LDOS of surface anion atoms for both the MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001) systems which originate from the dangling bonds of the surface anion atoms.

The calculated magnetic moments for the Mn-Term and $X$ (As or Sb)-Term MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001) systems are presented in Table I. We found that the surface Mn atoms of the both systems have much enhanced magnetic values of $3.79\mu_B$ and $4.04\mu_B$, respectively, compared with the corresponding center layer values, $3.10\mu_B$ and $3.32\mu_B$, which are consistent with the bulk cases. The magnetic moments of the anion atoms in Mn-Term systems have negative values between $-0.12\mu_B$ and $-0.09\mu_B$. The surface As(Sb) atom of the As(Sb)-Term systems has also enhanced values of $-0.15\mu_B$ ($-0.18\mu_B$) compared with those of inner As(Sb) atoms. The $\text{Mn}(S-2)$ in the Mn-Term systems and $\text{Mn}(S-3)$ atoms in the As- and Sb-Term systems have smaller magnetic moments compared to the values of $\text{Mn}(C)$ atoms, due to the enhanced $p$-$d$ hybridization induced by the surface termination.

## IV. SUMMARY
We investigated the surface electronic structures and magnetism for the NiAs structured MnAs(0001) and MnSb(0001) system using the all-electron FLAPW method within the GGA. If we compare the LDOS for the $\text{Mn}(C)$ atom of Mn-Term MnAs(0001) with that of MnSb(0001) system, the former has more clearly separated two peaks than the latter one due to a stronger Mn-$d$ and As-$p$ hybridizations as in the bulk. However, the shape of the LDOS of the $\text{Mn}(S)$'s in both systems is narrowed and sharpened by the surface effect. The calculated magnetic moments for the surface Mn atoms of the Mn-Term MnAs(0001) ($3.79\mu_B$) and MnSb(0001) ($4.04\mu_B$) systems are about $23\%$ enhanced compared with the corresponding center layer values. The magnetic moments of the As(Sb) atoms in the As(Sb)-Term systems are negative with the values between $-0.18\mu_B$ and $-0.09\mu_B$, while the $\text{Mn}(S-3)$ atoms in the As-or Sb-Term systems have smaller values of $3.06\mu_B$ and $3.25\mu_B$, respectively.
