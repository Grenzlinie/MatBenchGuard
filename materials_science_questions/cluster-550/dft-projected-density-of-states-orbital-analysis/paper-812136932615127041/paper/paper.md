# First-principles study of the effects of Si doping on geometric and electronic structure of closed carbon nanotube

ZHOU Junzhe¹ & WANG Chongyu¹,²

1. Department of Physics, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100084, China;
2. International Centre for Materials Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shenyang 110016, China

Correspondence should be addressed to Zhou Junzhe (email: zjz02@mails.tsinghua.edu.cn)

## Abstract
The effects of Si doping on geometric and electronic structure of closed carbon nanotube (CNT) are studied by, a first-principles method, DMol. It is found that the local density of states at the Fermi level ($E_{\text{F}}$) increases due to the Si-doping and the non-occupied states above the $E_{\text{F}}$ go down toward the lower energy range under an external electronic field. In addition, due to the doping of Si, a sub-tip on the CNT cap is formed, which consisted of the Si atom and its neighbor C atoms. From these results it is concluded that Si-doping is beneficial to the CNT field emission properties.

**Keywords:** carbon nanotube, doping, electronic structure, field emission.

**DOI: 10.1360/982005-470**

Since the discovery of carbon nanotubes (CNTs)¹, CNT has been applied to various fields because of its excellent mechanical and electronic properties. In the aspect of its field emission properties, a high-intensity electron gun has been made using aligned carbon nanotubes by de Heer and co-workers². This strongly aroused research workers' interest in the field emission capability of CNTs. Some experiments showed that large aspect ratios of CNTs, together with high chemical stability and mechanical strength are advantageous for its applications to the field emitter. Gao and co-workers have discovered that the graphitized CNT arrays display good and stable field emitting properties³. Arrays of aligned large-area carbon nanotubes with different orientations have been obtained and studied by Chen and co-workers⁴. The work function at the caps of individual multiwalled CNTs has been measured for their field emission properties⁵. A fully sealed field-emission display has also been fabricated using single-wall CNT-organic binders in 1999⁶.

In order to understand the mechanism of field emission, related theoretical researches have been carried out. Based on experiments and calculations, Carroll suggested that the local properties of closed CNTs depend on the topological structures of the caps in 1997⁷. Buldum and Lu argued that as a field emitter closed CNTs are superior to open CNTs⁸. The effects of the change of density of state (DOS) on the field emission properties of CNTs under electric field were discussed by Kim and co-workers⁹. The calculations by Park and co-workers indicated that doping O to the cap of CNT benefited its field emission properties¹⁰. Using first principles method Akdim investigated the field emission properties of CNTs adsorbed by O₂¹¹. Maiti and co-workers theoretically explained the phenomenon found in experiments that the emitting current is enlarged by absorbing H₂O¹². Zhou argued that the HOMO-LUMO gap at the cap of CNTs is closely related to the field emission properties¹³,¹⁴. Zhang and co-workers examined the effect of introducing substitutional boron or nitrogen atom into a CNT cap on its field emission properties¹⁵. The Fowler-Nordheim's (FN) theory is conventionally applied to study the field emission of CNTs¹⁶. Bonard and co-workers found that the emitting CNTs followed the FN law at low emitted currents¹⁷, but the properties of field emission of CNTs are different from those of metal field emitting devices and do not follow FN theory exactly. A new mechanism about field emission is found by quantum-mechanical investigation for a micrometer-long single-wall CNT¹⁸.

Recently, the synthesis of $C_{59}$Si heterofullerenes has been reported¹⁹ and related theoretical calculations have been also carried out²⁰. In addition, the geometric and electronic structures of doping Si CNT have been studied²¹⁻²³. Because silicon prefers $\text{sp}^3$-like bonding to $\text{sp}^2$-like bonding while C-C prefers $\text{sp}^2$-like bonding to $\text{sp}^3$-like bonding in graphite, it can be expected that substituting C atoms by Si atoms in the CNT cap could improve its field emission properties. Also, silicon is widely used as substrate material while growing CNTs. The study of Si-doping into a CNT has practical meaning.

In this study, Si is chosen as an impurity to substitute C atoms in the cap of CNTs. The doping effects on the properties of field emission of CNTs are discussed by calculating its geometrical and electronic structures. According to FN theory, work function $\varphi$ and field enhancement factor $\beta$ are directly related to field emission. $\varphi$ is decided by the electronic structures of the emitter, while $\beta$ depends on the geometric structures. In this paper we will give further insight on the field emission mechanism of CNT concerning the $\varphi$ and the $\beta$ using first principles calculations.

## 1 Calculation methods and models
Two types of familiar closed CNTs ((5,5) and (9,0)) are investigated by doping. In the calculation a quantum-chemistry program DMol code²⁴, a generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of BLYP²⁵,²⁶ for the exchange-correlation energy, and the Double Numerical plus d-functions (DND) basis sets are adopted. The geometrical

optimization is performed for the two CNT systems. During relaxation, C atoms at the bottom of CNTs are fixed. The convergence is obtained when the difference in the total energies between two optimized steps is less than $10^{-5}$ Ha.

The (5,5) single-wall CNTs (Fig. 1(a)) contains 150 C atoms. Its open ends are closed by H atoms in order to eliminate dangling bonds. Four models corresponding to four kinds of Si doping are considered. Model I: at the topmost pentagon layer of the cap a C atom is substituted by an Si atom; model II, at the second top layer of the cap a C atom is replaced by a Si atom; model III, an Si atom is placed, instead of a C atom at the third top layer; model IV is the same as model II, but with two C atoms replaced by two Si atoms. It has been found that $C_{60}$ is doped by 2 Si atoms, and its energy is the lowest with stable $C_{58}Si_{2}$ isomer, where the two Si atoms are located at the para-sites of a hexagon belonging to the $C_{60}^{[20]}$.

The (9,0) single-wall CNTs (Fig. 1(b)) contains 183 C atoms with the open ends closed by H atoms. We consider two models: C atoms in the topmost hexagon layer of the cap are substituted by one Si atom and two Si atoms, for model V and VI respectively. For model VI the two Si atoms are at the para-sites of the hexagon.

## 2 Results and discussions

### 2.1 Formation energy

In order to investigate the stability of the doped system, we define $E_{form}$ as the formation energy as follows:
$$
E_{\text{form}}=(E_{\text{doping}}-E_{\text{pure}})-(E_{\text{Si}}-E_{\text{C}}), \tag{1}
$$
where $E_{\text{pure}}$ and $E_{\text{doping}}$ are the total energy of CNT before and after doping, respectively. $E_{\text{C}}$ and $E_{\text{Si}}$ are the chemical potentials of C and Si, respectively. In the present calculations, $E_{\text{C}}=-38.08$ Ha and $E_{\text{Si}}=-289.49$ Ha, where $E_{\text{si}}$ is the total energy per atom in the bulk Si, and $E_{\text{C}}$ is the total energy per atom in the undoped CNT. The results are listed in Table 1.

For (5,5) CNT, clearly the $E_{\text{form}}$ of model I is the lowest and $E_{\text{form}}$ increases with the distance of Si away from the cap. For model IV, the $E_{\text{form}}$ is the largest. The result indicates that single site substitution is more stable than the two site substitution at the cap of closed (5,5) CNTs. On the contrary, one more Si atom only changes $E_{\text{form}}$ from 1.24 to 1.68 eV for (9,0) CNTs. The optimization results also indicate that the doped (9,0) CNTs are stable for models V and VI. Therefore, it can be expected that there could be more Si dopants in (9,0) CNTs than in (5,5) CNTs.

Since the enhancement factor varies as the geometric structures change, Dyke$^{[27]}$ and co-workers expressed the enhancement factor $\beta$ as
$$
\beta=1/\alpha r, \tag{2}
$$
where $\alpha$ is a constant, and $r$ is the curvature radius of a tip of emitters. Although the field emission properties of CNTs is thought not to completely follow FN theory in a large electric field, it still takes effect in our calculation. As shown in Fig. 2, Si atom stretches outside the caps of CNTs. The Si doping induces a protrusion in the cap of CNTs, giving rise to a sub-tip with a smaller curvature radius $r$ which leads to a larger $\beta$ value.

We also investigated the effects of external electric field on the LDOS at the cap of CNTs by applying two different fields (0.5 V/Å and 1 V/Å respectively) to the CNTs. The results (Figs. 9 and 10) revealed that the LDOS

moves to the lower energy range under external field and the unoccupied states are high $E_{\text{F}}$ that provides more opportunities for electrons to occupy these states. Under the field of $1\ \text{V}/\text{\AA}$, the peak of anti-bonding states of the LDOS of undoped (5,5) CNTs moves from 0.8 eV above $E_{\text{F}}$ to $E_{\text{F}}$ and increased the LDOS at the Fermi level. This result is consistent with that from Kim's calculations $^{[9]}$. Under the field of $0.5\ \text{V}/\text{\AA}$, a peak of anti-bonding states at $E_{\text{F}}$ is found in a doped CNT but not in an undoped CNT. Therefore, it is anticipated that doped CNTs have lower turn-on voltages.

We define the work function as the difference in potential energy when an electron is moved from the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) in CNTs to the vacuum level. The values of the HOMO in these systems are listed in Tables 3 and 4. It can be seen that the difference in the HOMO between doped and undoped CNTs is only $0.03 - 0.04\ \text{eV}$. Electrons may be emitted through transition level under electric field: The emissions may take the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) as a channel. The difference in the LUMO between doped and undoped CNTs is about $10^{-1}\ \text{eV}$. Thus the modulation to LUMO by Si atoms could evidently influence the field emission properties of CNTs.

Based on the above discussions, effective work function $(E_{\text{eff}})$ is introduced as the difference between the LUMO and the Fermi level. The results (Tables 3 and 4) show that the $E_{\text{eff}}$ of a single Si doped (5,5) CNT is 0.11 eV less than that of undoped CNT, while the $E_{\text{eff}}$ of double Si doped (5,5) CNT is 0.16 eV less than that of the undoped CNT, also indicating that Si doping helps to decrease $E_{\text{eff}}$ and is beneficial to the field emission of CNTs.

The LDOS at the Fermi level are mainly from HOMO/LUMO which are composed mostly of the orbits of Si atoms (Figs. 12 and13), which is consistent with Figs. 5—9: LDOS are also mainly from Si. The electron affinity of Si (133.5 kJ/mol) is weaker than that of C (153.9 kJ/mol)[²⁸], which means that the electrons of Si atoms occupying around the Fermi level are easily emitted. Moreover, the amount of electrons to emitted increases with the increase in LDOS at $E_{\text{F}}$. Therefore, we deem that Si doping can decrease the work function. This idea is also supported by the effective work function from present calculations.

From the LDOS figures and the HOMO/LUMO figures, it is concluded that the double Si doping in $(9,0)$ CNTs can improve the performance of field emission by a "transition emission". It could be the best one for field emission among those doping modes.

## 3 Conclusion

In this study, we investigated the geometric and electronic structures of Si doped and undoped metallic CNTs $((5,5)$ and $(9,0))$. In the aspect of geometric structures, the doping makes the cap "sharper", leading charges to accumulate more easily at the cap and benefits the electrons emission. In the aspect of electronic structures, high doping electronic states are introduced by Si in the cap of CNTs, which greatly increases the LDOS around the Fermi level and decreases the work function. Furthermore, the unoccupied states above the Fermi level move to the lower energy range under external electric field. Our results of first-principles calculation indicated that Si doping is favorable for the field emission properties of CNTs.
