# DFT Projected Density of States Orbital Analysis in W20O58 Idealized Structure

## Problem background
Tungsten oxide W20O58 (a Magnéli phase with the general formula WnO3n−2, here n=20) has a complex crystal structure containing 78 atoms per unit cell. The unit cell is built from WO6 octahedra that can share corners or faces, leading to distorted octahedra and zigzag chains. The electronic properties of this material near the Fermi level are of fundamental interest, especially for understanding possible superconductivity. This work studies an idealized version of the structure where all octahedra are made perfect, and uses density functional theory to investigate which tungsten atoms dominate the electronic states at the Fermi level and to characterize the Fermi surface. The key open question is: among the 20 tungsten atoms in the unit cell, which ones contribute most strongly to the density of states at the Fermi level, and what does the Fermi surface look like?

## Approach
The approach is a first-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculation. Starting from the published crystallographic data for W20O58 (Magnéli 1949), an idealized crystal structure is constructed in which every WO6 octahedron is made perfect with a uniform oxygen‑oxygen distance of 2.68 Å, retaining the space group P2/m (a=23.39 Å, b=12.1 Å, c=3.78 Å, γ=95°). A self‑consistent ground‑state calculation is then performed with the full‑potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP‑LAPW) code Elk, using the GGA‑PBE exchange‑correlation functional and an 8×8×8 k‑point mesh. From the converged charge density the total electronic density of states (DOS) and the DOS projected onto the 5d orbitals of every tungsten atom are computed. The Kohn–Sham band structure is calculated along a high‑symmetry k‑point path (generated by the SeeK‑path tool), and the Fermi surface is constructed to determine the number of disconnected sheets. The final analysis combines the projected DOS and band structure to identify which tungsten atoms dominate the states near the Fermi level, and counts the Fermi surface sheets.

## Reproduction target
The task is to reproduce the DFT‑based electronic structure analysis for idealized W20O58 and report the following quantities:

- Compute the total density of states (DOS) as a function of energy (Fermi level set to zero).
- Compute the density of states projected onto the 5d orbitals of every tungsten atom (atom indices 1–20 as in the idealized structure).
- From these, determine the fractional contribution of the tungsten atoms that surround the voids formed by the zigzag chains — specifically atoms W1, W12, and W14 through W19 — to the total DOS at the Fermi level.
- Compute the electronic band structure along the high‑symmetry k‑path generated by SeeK‑path, and verify that the flat bands crossing the Fermi level are primarily composed of 5d orbitals from those same tungsten atoms.
- Generate the Fermi surface and determine the number of distinct Fermi surface sheets.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Construct idealized crystal structure
- Role: process
- Action: Obtain the published W20O58 crystal structure (Magnéli 1949) and construct the idealized structure where all WO6 octahedra are made perfect with an average O–O distance of 2.68 Å, forming space group P2/m with lattice parameters a=23.39 Å, b=12.1 Å, c=3.78 Å, γ=95°. Write the structure to a CIF file.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/idealized_structure.cif`

### Step 2: DFT self-consistent field calculation
- Role: process
- Action: Run the Elk DFT code using the GGA-PBE exchange-correlation functional on the idealized structure. Use an 8×8×8 k-point mesh in the irreducible Brillouin zone. Perform a self-consistent calculation to obtain the converged charge density and wavefunctions.
- Evidence: `/app/outputs/scf_convergence.log`

### Step 3: Compute total density of states
- Role: scored
- Action: From the DFT ground state, compute the total electronic density of states (DOS). Write a two-column space-separated file with Energy (eV, Fermi level at 0) and Total DOS (states/eV/unit cell).
- Output file: `/app/outputs/total_dos.dat`
- Format: txt
- Contract: Column 1: Energy (float, eV); Column 2: Total DOS (float, states/eV/unit cell).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 4: Compute projected density of states
- Role: scored (load-bearing)
- Action: Compute the density of states projected onto the 5d orbitals of every tungsten atom. Write a three-column space-separated file with Energy (eV), Atom index (integer), and PDOS (states/eV/atom) for all 20 tungsten atoms in the unit cell. The atom indices must match the idealized structure from step 01 (the atoms around the voids are expected to be W1, W12, W14–W19).
- Output file: `/app/outputs/projected_dos.dat`
- Format: txt
- Contract: Column 1: Energy (float, eV); Column 2: Atom index (integer, 1–20); Column 3: PDOS (float, states/eV/atom).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 5: Compute electronic band structure
- Role: scored
- Action: Compute the Kohn–Sham band structure along the high-symmetry k-point path generated by SeeK-path for the W20O58 structure. Write a three-column space-separated file with k-distance (Å⁻¹), Band index (integer), and Energy (eV). Set the Fermi level to zero.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/band_structure.dat`
- Format: txt
- Contract: Column 1: k-distance (float, Å⁻¹); Column 2: Band index (integer); Column 3: Energy (float, eV).
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

### Step 6: Compute Fermi surface and count sheets
- Role: scored
- Action: Generate the Fermi surface from the DFT results and determine the number of distinct Fermi surface sheets. Write a single-line text file stating the integer count.
- Output file: `/app/outputs/fermi_surface_sheets.txt`
- Format: txt
- Contract: Single line: 'Number of Fermi surface sheets: N' where N is an integer.
- Scoring: scored by hidden verifier

## How you are scored
Your submission is evaluated by a hidden verifier that independently processes each scored output file. The verifier recomputes derived quantities (for example, it recalculates the orbital fraction at the Fermi level from your raw total DOS and projected DOS data), checks the structural validity of your files, and compares your results against hidden reference values. Each scored artifact contributes a reward, and the final overall score is a weighted combination of these per‑artifact rewards. Scores reflect the accuracy and consistency of the computed quantities with the hidden references; merely printing a number without the underlying computational artifacts (the raw DOS, PDOS, band structure, and Fermi surface sheet count) will result in no credit. The reference thresholds and tolerances are not disclosed.
