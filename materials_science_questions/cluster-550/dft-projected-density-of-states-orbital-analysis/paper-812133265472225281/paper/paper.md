# Surface structure and energetics of oxygen and CO adsorption on $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001)

Jun Ren $^{\text{a}}$, Chun-Fang Huo $^{\text{a}}$, Jianguo Wang $^{\text{a},*}$, Yong-Wang Li $^{\text{a}}$, Haijun Jiao $^{\text{a,b},*}$

$^{\text{a}}$ State Key Laboratory of Coal Conversion, Institute of Coal chemistry, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Taiyuan 030001, China
$^{\text{b}}$ Leibniz-Institut für Organische Katalyse an der Universität Rostock, Albert-Einstein-Strasse 29a, 18059 Rostock, Germany

Received 16 April 2005; accepted for publication 19 September 2005
Available online 6 October 2005

## Abstract

Adsorption of oxygen and CO on Mo- and C-terminated $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001) has been investigated at the density functional level of theory. On the Mo-terminated surface, the most stable adsorption configuration (1) has atomic oxygen capping the threefold Mo hollow position over a second layer carbon atom ($V_\text{C}$, C vacancy site). On the C-terminated surface, the most stable adsorption configuration (8) has atomic oxygen capping the threefold Mo hollow site over a first layer carbon atom ($H_\text{C}$, C hollow site). For CO adsorption, the most stable configuration (17) has CO capping structure over carbon vacancy ($V_\text{C}$) on the Mo-terminated surface, but atop of the surface carbon with the formation of ketenylidene ($\text{C=C=O}$) species on the C-terminated surface (21). Analysis of density of states reveals the metallic character of the clean and oxygen doped surfaces and the activation of the surfaces by oxygen adsorption.

## 1. Introduction

Considerable attention has been focused in recent years on the chemical and physical properties of late transition metal carbides (TMC) because of their excellent catalytic properties that resemble group VIII metals in many hydrogen-involved reactions [1–9]. In particular, they also have an interesting combination of useful properties such as extreme hardness, high melting point and metallic conductivity [10]. In practical applications, the utility of these materials ranges from wear-resistant coatings to superconductors and heterogeneous catalysts [11]. Furthermore, they

have potential applications in hydrodenitrogen-ation (HDN) [12,13] and hydrodesulfurization (HDS) [14,15] processes. Recent studies indicated that molybdenum sulfide under reaction conditions is partially transformed into carbide ($\text{MoC}_x\text{S}_{2-x}$) where the HDS reactions occur [16]. Molybdenum carbide and nitride catalysts have shown the potential to replace sulfided Mo catalysts in HDS reactions [17–21]. It has been found that the carbides generally have higher HDS rates than the nitrides, which are twice larger than those of commercial catalysts [21]. Despite many investigations on catalytic reactivity, very little work has been done toward the fundamental understanding of the surface active sites of molybdenum carbides. Moreover, many questions concerning the catalytic nature of the surface active sites on TMC catalysts are not answered.

The $\alpha\text{-Mo}_2\text{C}(0001)$ surface has been characterized with low-energy electron diffraction (LEED), scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) [22]. It has been found that the composition and structure of $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}(0001)$ depend substantially on the cleaning procedure. The carbon terminated surface of $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}(0001)$ has been investigated with photoelectron spectroscopy (PES), and the valence band has been proposed to have hybrid nature, mostly composed of Mo 4d and C 2p orbitals, except for the region near the Fermi level ($E_\text{F}$), where the band is mostly composed of Mo 4d orbitals. It also has been shown that the valence PES spectrum gives a clear cut-off at $E_\text{F}$, indicating the metallic nature of $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ [23].

$\text{O}_2$ is widely employed as a probe for catalytically active sites of metal and metal carbide/nitride catalysts [24]. For example, molybdenum has been extensively studied by $\text{O}_2$ chemisorption [25]. Recently, $\text{O}_2$ adsorption is used as active site probe for $\gamma\text{-Mo}_2\text{N}$ [26], and Thompson et al. concluded that $\text{O}_2$ adsorption merely reflected the physical nature of the catalyst surface, rather than the chemical nature because of oxygen uptake values increase with increasing surface area. However, recent work on $\beta\text{-Mo}_2\text{C}$ has shown different results [27], for example, activity measurements for cumene hydrogenation over the $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ catalysts indicated that the $\text{O}_2$ uptake tracked the CO uptake, i.e. the catalysts with the higher CO uptake also gave a higher $\text{O}_2$ uptake. The ratio of $\text{O}_2$ (not O) uptake to CO uptake was about six, which may be attributed to $\text{O}_2$ chemisorption on powder $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ catalysts resulting in subsurface oxygen, either as a thin oxide layer or as an oxycarbide. In addition, it has been reported that the catalytic activities of $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ and other group VI TMC are often improved by slight oxidation of the surfaces. The activation of their surfaces has been explained by the formation of the oxycarbide layer [9,28].

Over the past years, quantum chemical calculations have emerged as a new tool to understand the structure of active surfaces, and to determine reaction mechanisms. With recent improvement, density functional theory (DFT) is capable of providing qualitative and, in many cases, quantitative insights into surface science and catalysis. In this paper, DFT calculations were carried out to study the adsorption of oxygen on the $\alpha\text{-Mo}_2\text{C}(0001)$ surface in order to get insight into their surfaces and structures. The adsorption of CO on the same surface has been investigated for comparison.

### 2. Methods and models

All calculations were done with the Cambridge sequential total energy package [29]. DFT calculations within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [30] were carried out to study oxygen adsorption on the surfaces of $\alpha\text{-Mo}_2\text{C}(0001)$. Ionic cores were described by the ultrasoft pseudopotential [31], and the Kohn–Sham one-electron states were expanded in a plane wave basis set up to 340 eV. The difference of the adsorption energy at the level of cutoff between 340 and 400 eV was about 0.04 eV. A Fermi smearing of 0.1 eV was utilized. Brillouin zone integration was approximated by a sum over special $k$-points chosen using the Monkhorst–Pack scheme [32]. A spin-restricted approach was used for clean surface models since polarization effects were found to be negligible. For atomic O and the open-shell molecular $\text{O}_2$, a spin-unrestricted approach (both O and $\text{O}_2$ in triplet ground states) was used. The convergence criteria for structure

optimization and energy calculation were set to (a) SCF tolerance of $2.0 \times 10^{-6}$ eV/atom, (b) energy tolerance of $2.0 \times 10^{-5}$ eV/atom, (c) maximum force tolerance of $0.05$ eV/Å, and (d) maximum displacement tolerance of $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ Å. A $3 \times 4 \times 1$ $k$-grid sampling within the Brillouin zones was used in the p(1×1) unit cell. We also tested the $k$-point sampling by using the $4 \times 5 \times 2$ Monkhorst–Pack meshes for the unit cell, and the change in energy is less than 0.03 eV.

Although the PBE functional can give reliable optimized geometry, it tends to overbind adsorbate on metal surface [33]. In the PBE parameterization the parameter $\kappa$, which determines the degree of non-locality of the GGA, is chosen so as to satisfy the Lieb–Oxford bound on the exchange energy for all possible electron densities ($\kappa=0.804$). However, it is observed that the adsorption energies for a number of atoms and molecules on 3d, 4d and 5d transition metal surfaces depend strongly on the choice of $\kappa$. For $\kappa=0.804$, the atoms and molecules are overbound by 0.2–0.6 eV [33]. Zhang and Yang [34] found that, without violating the Lieb–Oxford bound, the use of $\kappa=1.245$ (RPBE) gives the best atomization energies for 23 small molecules. The same effect has also been found for hydrogen adsorption on the Fe(111) surface [35]. Therefore, we further carried out the RPBE single point energy calculations on the PBE optimized geometries. Compared with PBE, RPBE lowers the oxygen adsorption energies systemically by about 0.5 eV (Tables 1, and 2). In the following sections, we use the RPBE energies for discussion, and the PBE values are provided for comparison.

Regarding the surface structure, only few experimental data are available. To model the surfaces, cleavage parallel to the (100) plane of clean $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C exposes unsaturated Mo and C atoms and threefold hollow sites are created. The metal atoms in $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C are in a slightly distorted hexagonal close packed (hcp) arrangement, with carbon atoms occupying orderly in the lattice octahedral vacancies. The bulk coordination of Mo is three, and that of carbon is six.

We model the $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001) surface with a stoichiometric slab consisting of six layers of Mo$_2$C in per $1 \times 1$ unit cell. The vacuum spacing between two repeated slabs is set to $10.29$ Å to ensure no significant interaction between the slabs. Mo-terminated $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001) with a p(1×1) slab of three Mo and three C layers (3Mo + 3C) gives twelve Mo atoms and six C atoms per cell, and the surface layer contains Mo atoms. The top four layers (2Mo + 2C) are relaxed, while the two bottom layers (1Mo + 1C) are fixed in their bulk positions. The calculated adsorption energy is defined in equation, $E_{\text{ads}} = E(\text{O}_2/\text{slab}) - [E(\text{slab}) + E(\text{O}_2)]$ where $E(\text{O}_2/\text{slab})$ is the total energy of the slab with one adsorbed $\text{O}_2$ molecule, $E(\text{slab})$ is the total energy of the corresponding clean $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ slab, and $E(\text{O}_2)$ is the total energy of free $\text{O}_2$. Therefore, the more negative the $E_{\text{ads}}$, the stronger the adsorption. The O coverage ($\theta$) is defined as the number of the adsorbed O atoms over the number of the first layer Mo atoms.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Bulk $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ and $\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ surface

The $\alpha$-$\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ phase has an orthorhombic crystal structure, in which the Mo atoms form a slightly distorted hexagonal close packed (hcp) lattice [36]. In this study, the crystal was cut at an orientation of (100), which is equivalent to the (0001) surface in hexagonal system. As shown in Fig. 1a, the structure of $\alpha$-$\text{Mo}_2\text{C}$ perpendicular to the [0001] direction shows a series of alternating Mo and C layers, suggesting that the surface can be either Mo- or C-terminated. Fig. 1 shows the ideal Mo-terminated (b) and C-terminated (c) surfaces of $\alpha$-$\text{Mo}_2\text{C}(0001)$. On the Mo-terminated surface (Fig. 1b), there are two individual Mo atoms with different coordination numbers on the surface. The first is Mo coordination with two C atoms with respect to the bulk ($\text{Mo}_1$ in Fig. 1b), and second is Mo coordination with one C atom ($\text{Mo}_2$ in Fig. 1b). In comparison with the bulk Mo atom, $\text{Mo}_1$ has one coordination vacancy and $\text{Mo}_2$ has two coordination vacancies. On the C-terminated surface (Fig. 1c), all of the Mo atoms are fully coordinated with respect to the bulk coordination. According to the Mo-termination (3Mo + 3C) and C-termination (3C + 3Mo) models, the Mo-terminated surface is energetically less stable than the

C-terminated surface by about 0.03 eV. Experi- mentally these two surfaces can be formed easily [22,37].

The calculated lattice parameters of the bulk $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C ($a$=$4.748$ Å, $b$=$6.026$ Å and $c$=$5.204$ Å) are very close to the experimental values ($a$=$4.729$ Å, $b$=$6.028$ Å and $c$=$5.197$ Å) [36]. Opti- mization relaxes surface Mo and C slightly, and their interlayer distances, Mo(first layer)- C(second layer) and Mo(first layer)-Mo(third layer), become shorter than those in bulk. In addi- tion, structure relaxation results in a rippled sur- face structure with the less bonded Mo$_2$ atoms at the ridge and Mo$_1$ atoms in the trough, and the altitude is about 0.09 Å. The calculated Mo-Mo distance at the "C hollow" sites with C atoms underneath is 3.137 Å and at the "C vacancy" sites is in average about 2.878 Å. The charge of Mo$_1$ with two C neighbors on $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001) is about 0.35e, more positive than that of Mo$_2$ with one C neighbor (0.23e). Therefore, it seems that increasing the C coordination of Mo atoms of the bulk surface leads to higher charge transfer from Mo to C. The direction of metal to carbon charge transfer is consistent with the previous the- oretical studies and measurements of near edge X-ray adsorption spectroscopy for the bulk carbides [19].

### 3.2. $O_2$ adsorption on Mo-terminated $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001)

First of all, it is to note that only atomic oxygen (O) adsorption has been found, despite the initial conformation with molecular oxygen (O$_2$), indicat- ing the spontaneous dissociation of O$_2$ on the sur- face. For the Mo-terminated surface, there are three types of Mo triangle for oxygen capping ($\mu^3$) adsorption, (i) three surface Mo atoms over a second layer C atom ($V_\text{C}$, C vacancy site), (ii) three Mo atoms over a second layer Mo atom ($H_\text{M}$, Mo hollow site), (iii) three surface Mo atoms over a second layer C atom ($H_\text{C}$, C hollow site), respectively (Fig. 1). Their combinations yield the corresponding adsorption sites. All possible adsorption sites were considered, and only the seven energy minimum structures in Fig. 2 are found and the calculated adsorption energies and structural parameters are listed in Table 1.

Configuration 1 has two adsorbed oxygen atoms over the C vacancies ($V_\text{C}$/$V_\text{C}$). Configura- tions 2 and 3 have two adsorbed oxygen atoms over the Mo hollows ($H_\text{M}$/$H_\text{M}$), but they differ in the arrangement of the oxygen atoms. Combing $V_\text{C}$ with $H_\text{M}$ gives two different $V_\text{C}$/$H_\text{M}$ configura- tions (4 and 5), and they differ also in the arrange- ment of the oxygen atoms. In addition, two configurations with $V_\text{C}$ and $H_\text{C}$ (6, $V_\text{C}$/$H_\text{C}$) as well as $H_\text{M}$ and $H_\text{C}$ (7, $H_\text{M}$/$H_\text{C}$) are found. However, the possible $H_\text{C}$/$H_\text{C}$ configurations can not be located, and one of the adsorbed oxygen atoms will occupy either $V_\text{C}$ or $H_\text{M}$ to result in 6 or 7 as more stable configurations.

As shown in Table 1, 1 ($V_\text{C}$/$V_\text{C}$) is the most sta- ble adsorption form, followed by 2 ($H_\text{M}$/$H_\text{M}$) with an energy difference of 0.38 eV, while 3 ($H_\text{M}$/$H_\text{M}$) and 4 ($V_\text{C}$/$H_\text{M}$) are almost equal in energy. In a recent experimental study [37], Clair et al. pro- posed that the most probable oxygen adsorption configuration on Mo-terminated $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001) is the $H_\text{M}$/$H_\text{M}$ form with the dissociated O atoms at the $H_\text{M}$ sites. However, this conclusion was deduced from the analogy between the HfC(111) [38] and $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001) surfaces. It is noteworthy

### 3.3. $O_2$ adsorption on C-terminated $\alpha$-$\mathrm{Mo}_{2}\mathrm{C}$ (0001)

According to the ideal truncation of the bulk structure, the threefold Mo hollows above second-layer carbon C vacancies ($V_{\mathrm{C}}$) are filled when the surface becomes C-terminated. These sites can therefore be ruled out as the oxygen adsorption sites. However, the $H_{\mathrm{M}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{C}}$ Mo threefold hollow sites and atop C sites are open on the C-terminated surface and should be considered as possible adsorption sites for oxygen, which results in the formation of the Mo–O bond and the C–O bond even in the initial stage of adsorption.

As shown in Fig. 3, four adsorption modes (8–11) are found as stable configurations and the computed bond parameters and adsorption energies are given in Table 2. Apart from the adsorption on the $H_{\mathrm{C}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{M}}$ sites like in case of the Mo-terminated surface, there is also bridge ($B_{\mathrm{M}}$) adsorption site on the C-terminated surface. $\mathbf{8}$ has two adsorbed oxygen atoms over the carbon hollow ($H_{\mathrm{C}}$), forming a sharp $(1\times 1)$ hexagonal pattern, in line with the LEED observations [37]. $\mathbf{9}$ has one oxygen atom over carbon hollow ($H_{\mathrm{C}}$) and one bridging with one Mo and one C, while $\mathbf{1 0}$ has two oxygen atoms simultaneously bridging one Mo atom and one C atom. $\mathbf{1 1}$ has one oxygen atom over molybdenum hollow ($H_{\mathrm{M}}$) and one bridging with two Mo atoms.

Other adsorption configurations are less stable. In $\mathbf{8}$, there are only Mo–O bonds and no direct C–O bonds available, while there are both Mo–O and C–O bonds in $\mathbf{9}$. This result is in good agreement with the findings by Clair et al. [22a] and Edamoto et al. [23], and they found that the adsorbed oxygen atoms interact with both Mo and C atoms to form an oxycarbide layer, and with the increase of the surface temperature, the C–O bonds break and the adsorbed oxygen atoms interact only with molybdenum. Consequently the surface carbon atoms do not burn off as CO, but instead oxygen atoms diffuse into the bulk.

Our results indicate that molecular $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ adsorbs dissociatively on the two surfaces, as found in experiment [23,37]. The energetically most favored adsorption site is the threefold C vacancy hollow site ($V_{\mathrm{C}}/V_{\mathrm{C}}$) on the Mo-terminated surface, and the threefold C hollow site ($H_{\mathrm{C}}/H_{\mathrm{C}}$) is the most stable adsorption site on the C-terminated surface. The adsorption energies in Tables 1 and 2 show that the molecular $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ adsorbs dissociatively on the Mo-terminated surface more easily than on the C-terminated surface, which indicates that Mo-terminated surface is more active [11–16].

### 3.4. Atomic oxygen adsorption on Mo- and C-terminated $\alpha$-$\mathrm{Mo}_{2}\mathrm{C}(0001)$

For the Mo-terminated surface, three different sites are considered: $V_{\mathrm{C}}$, $H_{\mathrm{M}}$, and $H_{\mathrm{C}}$. The computed structures and adsorption energies (which are relative to $\mathrm{O}_{2}/2$) are given in Fig. 4 and Table 3. We can see that the $V_{\mathrm{C}}$ position is the energetically most favored adsorption site, followed by $H_{\mathrm{M}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{C}}$ positions. In addition, we have also calculated the O adsorption on the C-terminated surface. The adsorption sites considered here are $H_{\mathrm{C}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{M}}$ sites. From Table 3, it is clear that the minimal total energy is still obtained in $H_{\mathrm{C}}$ site, and then followed with $B_{\mathrm{M}}$, and the energy difference between the two structures is 0.27 eV. These results are consistent with those of the molecular $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ adsorption.

### 3.5. CO adsorption on $\alpha-Mo_{2} C(0001)$

In addition to $O_{2}$ or O, CO adsorption on $\alpha-Mo_{2} C(0001)$ has been studied both experimentally [37] and theoretically [39]. As found experimentally, CO saturation coverage is about $14\%$ and $11\%$ on the Mo- and C-terminated surfaces, while there is about $50\%$ saturation coverage for oxygen adsorption based on the total number of surface Mo atoms. For comparison, all possible CO adsorbed sites on the two surfaces are considered.

As shown in Fig. 5, there are four stable CO adsorption forms on the Mo-terminated surface, $\mu^{3}$-form (17), bridge (18), atop $Mo_{1}$ (19) and atop $Mo_{2}$ (20), and the computed adsorption energies and structural parameters are listed in Table 4. The energetically most favored site is CO adsorption on the $V_{C}$ site with a tilted $\mu^{3}$-form adsorption configuration (17), followed by the bridge configuration (18) with the adsorption energies of $-2.23$ and $-2.17\ eV$, respectively, while the two atop configurations (19 and 20) are less stable ($-1.84$ and $-1.82\ eV$, respectively).

For CO adsorption on C-terminated surface, there also are four stable adsorption forms, atop C (21), atop Mo (22 and 23) and bridge (C, C) (24), as shown in Fig. 5. The computed adsorption energies and bond parameters are listed in Table 4. In addition, the similar surface species of $N{=}C{=}O$ on the $\gamma-Mo_{2}N(100)$ surface is proposed both experimentally [41] and theoretically [42]. ## 4. Electronic structure and density of states

Fig. 6 shows the partial density of states (PDOS) plots for the occupied bands of the adsorbed oxygen atoms on the Mo-terminated (a) and C-terminated (b) surfaces of $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001). The most stable surface structures 1 for the Mo-terminated surface and 8 for the C-terminated surface are used to interpret bonding of the oxygen interaction with the surfaces of $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C(0001).

For the surface (dotted lines), the valence band near the Fermi level contains mainly metal 4d character on the Mo-terminated surface (Fig. 6a), and on the C-terminated surface, the valence band has not only the metal 4d, but also some carbon 2p characters (Fig. 6b). These indicate that the two surfaces differ in the electronic structures, but have the common metallic nature.

For oxygen adsorption on both surfaces (solid lines), the 2p states of the adsorbed oxygen atom shifts to the higher energy region towards to the Fermi level, while those of surface Mo and C change hardly. However, valence bands for both adsorbate-covered surfaces still have high character of Mo 4d. This reveals that the oxidized Mo$_2$C surfaces still remain their metallic nature [23]. ### 5. Conclusion

Density functional theory calculations have been performed to study the adsorption of oxygen and CO on the $\alpha$-Mo$_2$C (0001) surface at 1/2 ML coverage. On the basis of the surface nature, there are Mo- and C-terminated surfaces. On both surfaces, the stable adsorption configurations have atomic oxygen rather than molecular oxygen, indicating the spontaneous dissociation of molecular oxygen into atomic oxygen upon adsorption. On the Mo-terminated surface, the most stable surface structure has the adsorbed atomic oxygen capping the triangular surface of three Mo atoms over a C vacancy $(1, V_C)$. On the C-terminated surface, the most stable surface structure has the adsorbed atomic oxygen capping the triangular surface of three Mo atoms over a C hollow $(8, H_C)$. Compared to atomic oxygen adsorption on 1/4 ML coverage, both configurations $\mathbf{1}$ and $\mathbf{8}$ have strong lateral interaction by about 0.32 and 0.34 eV per O$_2$, respectively.

The analysis of the partial density of states shows the metallic character of the clean surfaces, e.g. the valence band region is mainly contributed by the Mo 4d orbitals. Under the adsorption of atomic oxygen, the valence band region still maintains their metallic nature, and the valence band of the oxygen 2p orbitals shifts towards the Fermi level, indicating the activation of the surface structure by adsorbed oxygen. This is in nice agreement with the experimental findings.

As in case of oxygen adsorption, CO adsorption occupies the same $V_C$ sites as the most stable surface structure $(\mathbf{17})$ on the Mo-terminated surface. On the C-terminated surface, however, CO prefers the top site at surface carbon with the formation of ketenylidene ($C=C=O$) species $(\mathbf{21})$, in agreement with the recent experimental findings.
