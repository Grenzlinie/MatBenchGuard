## ABSTRACT

The tetragonal (s.g. $I4/mmm$; #139) $ThCr_2Si_2$ is widely known as a structural type of the broad family of the so-called 122-like ternary phases which includes now more than 800 members. Among them the superconducting iron-pnictides $AFe_2Pn_2$ (discovered in 2008, $A=$ alkali-earth metals) and the newest superconducting iron-chalcogenides $A'Fe_2Ch_2$ (discovered in 2010, $A'=$ alkali metals) have attracted recently enormous interest in this class of materials. Meanwhile, the data about the electronic, magnetic, and elastic properties of the $ThCr_2Si_2$ phase itself are still practically absent. Here, by means of first-principles calculations, the optimized structural parameters, spin ordering of the magnetic ground state, independent elastic constants, bulk, shear, and Young's moduli, elastic anisotropy indexes, total and partial densities of states, and inter-atomic bonding picture for $ThCr_2Si_2$ were obtained for the first time and analyzed in comparison with the aforementioned most popular 122-like systems $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$.

The tetragonal (s.g. $I4/mmm$; #139) phases with the general formula $M'M_2X_2$ (where $M'$, $M$ are metals, and $X$ are sp elements such as Ga, Si, Ge, P, As, Se, etc.) form a broad family of ternary systems which includes now more than 800 members [1,2]. Today these phases are most known as 122-like systems with a $ThCr_2Si_2$ structural type. These materials have attracted recently enormous interest after the discovery of superconductivity with the transition temperatures $T_C$ to 38 K in iron-pnictide systems $AFe_2Pn_2$ (where $A=$ alkali-earth metals; in 2008 [3]), and of the newest superconducting iron-chalcogenide systems $A'Fe_2Ch_2$ ($A'=$ alkali metals; with $T_C$ to 36 K) discovered in 2010 [4]. Many experiments were carried out and theories were formulated to understand various properties of these new materials; see reviews [5-9].

Meanwhile, the data about the basic electronic, magnetic, and elastic properties of the $ThCr_2Si_2$ phase itself are still practically absent.

In order to get a systematic insight into the structural, elastic, electronic, and magnetic properties of $ThCr_2Si_2$, we performed first-principles calculations of the optimized structural parameters, spin ordering of the magnetic ground state, elastic constants ($C_{ij}$), bulk modulus ($B$), compressibility ($\beta$), shear modulus ($G$), Young's modulus ($Y$), elastic anisotropy indexes, total and partial densities of states, and inter-atomic bonding picture for $ThCr_2Si_2$, which are analyzed in comparison with the aforementioned most popular 122-like systems $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$.

$ThCr_2Si_2$ crystallizes in a body-centered tetragonal structure with two formulas per unit cell [10-13]. The atomic positions are Th: $2a$ (0; 0; 0), Cr: $4d$ (0; 1/2; 1/4), and Si: $4e(0;0;z_{Si})$, where $z_{Si}$ are the so-called internal coordinates. The structure of $ThCr_2Si_2$ can be schematically described as a sequence of Th sheets and $[Cr_2Si_2]$ blocks consisting of $\{CrSi_4\}$ tetrahedrons: $...\ Th/[Cr_2Si_2]/Th/[Cr_2Si_2]\ ...$; see Fig. 1.

The available magnetic susceptibility experiments exhibit an antiferromagnetic (AFM) behavior of $ThCr_2Si_2$ with $T_N \sim 1770$ K and a magnetic moment (MM) of $2.18\ \mu_B$ per Cr atom [14]. Since the type of AFM long-range ordering is unknown, we searched for the ground state spin ordering among three different collinear commensurate in-plane AFM spin configurations (AFM1-AFM3, Fig. 1), as compared with non-magnetic (NM) and ferromagnetic (FM) solutions.

Our results are based on the first-principles calculations within the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) in a projector augmented wave (PAW) formalism [15,16]. Exchange and correlation were described by a non-local correction for LDA in the form of GGA [17]. The kinetic energy cutoff of 535 eV and k-mesh of $12\times12\times6$ were used. The geometry optimization was performed with the force cutoff of $2\ \text{meV}/\mathring{A}$.

At first, we found that the ground state for $ThCr_2Si_2$ is the spin configuration AFM3, for which the obtained MM ($\sim2.05$ $\mu_B$ per Cr atom, Table 1) agrees well with the experiment [14]. Here, the magnetic structure within each $[Cr_2Si_2]$ block consists of chains of parallel Cr spins that are coupled antiferromagnetically; in turn, the spin ordering is also antiferromagnetic between adjacent $[Cr_2Si_2]/[Cr_2Si_2]$ blocks; see Fig. 1. AFM3 ordering for $ThCr_2Si_2$ (which is known also as stripe-like magnetic ordering) corresponds to the typical spin alignment in 122-like iron-pnictides [5–9]. The other excited antiferromagnetic configurations, AFM1 and AFM2, have energies higher than the FM state, Table 1. Thus, for further comparative discussion we chose AFM2, FM, and NM states.

The calculated equilibrium structural parameters are listed in Table 2. The cell volume $V_o$ is smallest for the NM but largest for the AFM3 state. For the iron-based superconducting 122-like systems $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$, two structural indicators (the tetrahedral angles and the so-called anion height $\Delta z$, i.e. the distance of $Pn(Ch)$ atoms from the Fe plane), which correlate with the electronic properties and $T_C$, are widely discussed; see [5–9]. For $ThCr_2Si_2$, the Si–Cr–Si tetrahedral angles $\Theta_{1,2}$ ($109.3^\circ$–$109.8^\circ$) for the AFM3 state are closest to the ideal tetrahedron angle ($109.5^\circ$). The anion height $\Delta z$ is smallest for the NM and largest for the AFM3 state, but $\Delta z$ for $ThCr_2Si_2$ also lies close to the proposed [18] "optimal" factor $\Delta z = 1.38$ Å.

The calculated elastic parameters are presented in Table 3. The values of six independent elastic constants ($C_{ij}$) were evaluated by calculating the stress tensors on different deformations applied to the equilibrium lattice of the tetragonal unit cell, whereupon the dependence between the resulting energy change and the deformation was determined. For the NM phase, some of $C_{ij}$ are negative (i.e. this phase is mechanically unstable) – unlike the FM and AFM3 phases, for which all $C_{ij}$ are positive and satisfy the well-known Born's criteria (see [19]) for mechanically stable tetragonal crystals.

From the calculated constants $C_{ij}$, the macroscopic elastic parameters for FM and AFM3 $ThCr_2Si_2$ were evaluated, namely, the bulk modulus ($B$), shear modulus ($G$), compressibility ($\beta$), and Young's modulus ($Y$) – in three main approximations: Voigt (V) and Reuss (R) and Voigt–Reuss–Hill (VRH); see details in [20].

These data reveal that the bulk and shear modules for $ThCr_2Si_2$ (Table 3) are higher than for related iron-based 122-like systems $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$: so, for $KFe_2Se_2$ $B \sim 64$ GPa, $G \sim 38$ GPa [21], for $SrFe_2As_2$ $B \sim 62$ GPa, $G \sim 32$ GPa [22], for $CaFe_2As_2$ $B \sim 60$ GPa [23]. Besides, according to estimations of the so-called universal anisotropy index $A^U$ (for isotropic crystals $A^U = 0$; deviations of $A^U$ from zero define the extent of crystal anisotropy [24]) we found that $A^U = 0.23$, which is much smaller than for $KFe_2Se_2$($A^U = 1.08$ [21]) and indicates a considerable lowering of elastic anisotropy for $ThCr_2Si_2$. These facts can be explained taking into account the formation of a 3D-like system of directional covalent Si–Si and Si–Cr bonds for $ThCr_2Si_2$; see below.

Next, our data reveal that for the most stable AFM3 phase $G > B$, this implies that the parameter limiting the mechanical stability of $ThCr_2Si_2$ is the bulk modulus. For $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$ examined earlier [21,22], the inverse situation takes place: $B > G$. Since the shear modulus $G$ represents the resistance to plastic deformation while the bulk modulus $B$ represents the resistance to fracture, the value $B/G$ was proposed [25] as an empirical malleability measure of materials: if $B/G > 1.75$, a material behaves in a ductile manner, and vice versa: if $B/G < 1.75$, a material demonstrates brittleness. In our case, $B/G \sim 1$, therefore $ThCr_2Si_2$ should be brittle.

Let us discuss the electronic properties of $ThCr_2Si_2$. The total and site-projected $l$-decomposed densities of states (DOSs) for the most stable AFM3 phase are presented in Fig. 2. The lowest band (peak A, Fig. 2) lying in the range from $-10.2$ to $-6.5$ eV below the Fermi level ($E_F$) arises mainly from the Si 3s orbitals with some contributions from the Th s, p, d orbitals and is separated from the near-Fermi valence band by a gap. In turn, this broad valence band includes DOS peaks B–D, where the peak B is made up of contributions from the Si 3p + Th 6d states, and the peak C is of a mixed (Si 3p + Cr 3d + Th 6d) character. The occupied bands near the Fermi level (peak C) and the lowest part of the conduction band (peak D) are formed by contributions of the Cr 3d states with an admixture of the Th d, f states responsible for the metallic-like character of this system.

Besides, the Th 5f states participate actively in the formation of the valence band, Fig. 2. Thus, like metallic thorium [26] and some thorium compounds with light $sp$ atoms (H, B, C, N, O) [27-29], the Th 5f states for ThCr$_2$Si$_2$ are itinerant and partially occupied. The contributions from the Th 6d and Th 5f states to the near-Fermi region are comparable with each other and with the contributions of the Cr 3d states. So, the calculated partial densities of states at the Fermi level ($N(E_F)$), in states/eV $\cdot$ f.u.) are: $N(E_F)^{\text{Th 6d}} = 0.21$, $N(E_F)^{\text{Th 5f}} = 0.13$, $N(E_F)^{\text{Cr1 3d$\downarrow$}} = 0.29$, $N(E_F)^{\text{Cr1 3d$\uparrow$}} = 0.39$, $N(E_F)^{\text{Cr2 3d$\downarrow$}} = 0.33$, $N(E_F)^{\text{Cr2 3d$\uparrow$}} = 0.34$, where Cr$^1$ and Cr$^2$ denote the Cr atoms with antiparallel spin orientation. The contributions from other orbitals to $N(E_F)^{\text{total}}$ ($=2.54$ states/eV $\cdot$ f.u.) are much smaller.

Finally, to explain the aforementioned elastic properties for ThCr$_2$Si$_2$, let us discuss the peculiarities of inter-atomic bonding for this material. Usually, the ThCr$_2$Si$_2$-like $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$ phases are described as quasi-two-dimensional (2D) layered systems with a high-anisotropic inter-atomic bonding picture, where inside [Fe$_2$Pn$_2$(Ch$_2$)] blocks, strong directional covalent Fe-Pn(Ch) bonds form, whereas between the [Fe$_2$Pn$_2$(Ch$_2$)] blocks and $A(A')$ atomic sheets ionic bonds emerge owing to the $A(A') \rightarrow$ [Fe$_2$Pn$_2$(Ch$_2$)] charge transfer.

For ThCr$_2$Si$_2$ the bonding picture differs essentially. Indeed, for ThCr$_2$Si$_2$ together with covalent Cr-Si bonds inside [Cr$_2$Si$_2$] blocks (owing to hybridization of the Cr 3d-Si 3p states, see site-projected DOSs, Fig. 2), strong directional Si-Si bonds between the adjacent [Cr$_2$Si$_2$]/[Cr$_2$Si$_2$] blocks arise. This can be easily explained taking into account that the lengths of the aforementioned bonds ($d$(Si-Si) $\sim$2.54 Å between [Cr$_2$Si$_2$] blocks) are comparable with the sum of covalent radii of silicon ($\sim$2.22 Å) or with inter-atomic distances in crystalline silicon: $d$(Si-Si) $\sim$2.352 Å. Thus, ThCr$_2$Si$_2$ should be assumed as a 3D-like system, where directional covalent Cr-Si and Si-Si bonds appear inside [Cr$_2$Si$_2$] blocks and between the adjacent [Cr$_2$Si$_2$]/[Cr$_2$Si$_2$] blocks, respectively. This situation is well visible on the charge density map, Fig. 3.

In addition, to describe the ionic bonding for ThCr$_2$Si$_2$, we carried out a Bader [30] analysis. The effective atomic charges are: Th$^{1.557+}$, Cr$^{0.399+}$, and Si$^{1.178-}$. Thus, inside [Cr$_2$Si$_2$] blocks, ionic bonding arises between ions with opposite charges: Cr-Si, and the charge transfer ($\sim$1.6$e$) occurs from Th sheets to [Cr$_2$Si$_2$] blocks.

In conclusion, we presented a first-principles study of the tetragonal ThCr$_2$Si$_2$ which is widely known as a structural type of a broad family of the so-called 122-like ternary phases. Our analysis comprises optimized structural parameters, spin ordering of the magnetic ground state, elastic properties, total and partial densities of states, and inter-atomic bonding picture for ThCr$_2$Si$_2$- in comparison with those for the superconducting iron-pnictides $AFe_2Pn_2$ and the newest superconducting iron-chalcogenides $A'Fe_2Ch_2$ which have attracted recently enormous interest in this class of materials.

As a result, we found that the ThCr$_2$Si$_2$ phase, like $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$ systems: (i) is metallic-like, (ii) adopts complex so-called

stripe-like antiferromagnetic ordering, and (iii) behaves in a brittle manner.

On the other hand, we found also that unlike $AFe_2Pn_2$ and $A'Fe_2Ch_2$ systems, the $ThCr_2Si_2$ phase: (i) should be assumed as a 3D-like system owing to the presence of strong covalent Si–Si bonds between the adjacent $[Cr_2Si_2]/[Cr_2Si_2]$ blocks together with directional Cr–Si bonds inside the $[Cr_2Si_2]$ blocks; (ii) adopts enhanced elastic modules and lower elastic anisotropy; (iii) exhibits conductivity both in $[Cr_2Si_2]$ blocks and in Th sheets. Besides, the Th 5f states for $ThCr_2Si_2$ participate actively in the formation of the valence band and are itinerant and partially occupied.
