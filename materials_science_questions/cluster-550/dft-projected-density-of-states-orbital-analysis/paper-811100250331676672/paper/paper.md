In this paper, the effect of dilute charged impurity and external magnetic field on orbital-resolved density of states (DOS) and electronic heat capacity (EHC) of a monolayer hydrogenated graphene which is called chair-like graphane is investigated within the Harrison model and Green's function technique. The self-consistent Born approximation has been implemented to describe the effect of scattering between electrons and dilute charged impurities. Our results show that the graphane is a semiconductor and its band gap decreases with impurity and magnetic field. EHC reaches almost linearly to Schottky anomaly and does not change at low temperatures in the presence of impurity and magnetic field. Generally, EHC increases with the mentioned parameters. Surprisingly, impurity doping only affects the salient behavior of $p_y$ orbital contribution of carbon atoms due to the symmetry breaking.

Keywords: Graphane; Harrison model; electronic heat capacity; magnetic field; impurity.

PACS numbers: 75.25.Dk, 74.20.Pq, 75.75.-c, 68.80.Ck, 65.40.Ba

Also, because of the interesting properties of parallel magnetic field in a two- dimensional electron gas, the investigation of this effect is increased by physicists. In Ref. 31, the in-plane magnetic field effects on physical properties of two-dimensional electron gas have been investigated in the context of random phase approximation. Electrons in a system scatter from impurities with a scattering rate $\tau$. A characteristic energy scale $\hbar/\tau$ induces in the limit of zero doping, that is, at Dirac points. For this reason, impurities have a strong effect on physical properties of material for its applications. Many authors have shown that the impure graphane can be used in transistor devices due to its decreased band gap. Since the unit cell of graphane includes four atoms, the Green's function can be written as a $4 \times 4$ matrix. In the Matsubara formalism, $^{43}$ each element of Green's function matrix is defined as

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{\alpha \beta}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, \gamma) & =-\left\langle T_{\gamma} c_{k, \alpha}(\gamma) c_{k, \beta}^{\dagger}(0)\right\rangle \\
G_{\alpha \beta}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, i \omega_{n}) & =\int_{0}^{1 / k_{B} T} e^{i \omega_{n} \gamma} G_{\alpha \beta}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, \gamma) d \gamma,
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

where $T_{\gamma}$ is the time ordering operator, $c=a(b)$ and $\alpha, \beta$ refer to each sublattice atoms $C_{1}, C_{2}, H_{1}, H_{2}$ and $\gamma$ is the imaginary time. Also, $\omega_{n}=(2 n+1) \pi k_{B} T$ is the Fermionic Matsubaras frequency. The Green's function matrix of the system can be readily obtained by the following equation:

$$
\mathbf{G}_{0}\left(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, i \omega_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{i \omega_{n} \hat{\mathbf{I}}-H(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})}.
\tag{8}
$$

After substituting Eq. (1) into Eq. (8), the explicit form of Green's function matrix of this system has been found which is quite lengthy and has not been presented here. Using Born approximation in the scattering theory and $T$ matrix, $^{44}$ the electronic self-energy matrix of disordered system in the presence of finite but small density of impurity atoms, $n_{i}=N_{i} / N$, could be obtained as

$$
\sum\left(i \omega_{n}\right)=N_{i} \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{imp}}\left(i \omega_{n}\right)=\frac{n_{i} \nu_{i}}{1-\nu_{i} \mathbf{G}_{0}\left(i \omega_{n}\right)},
\tag{9}
$$

where $N$ is the number of unit cells and $\nu_{i}$ denotes the electronic on-site energy which shows the scattering potential strength. By neglecting interstice correlations, the self-consistent problem requires the solution of the below equation:

$$
\sum_{\alpha \alpha}=\frac{n_{i} \nu_{i}}{1-\nu_{i} G_{\alpha \alpha}(E)}=\frac{n_{i} \nu_{i}}{1-\nu_{i} G_{\alpha \alpha}\left(E+i 0^{+}-\sum(E)\right)}.
\tag{10}
$$

## 3. Density of States and Electronic Heat Capacity
The DOS can be calculated by tracing over the imaginary part of Green's function matrix. $^{45}$ By setting $i \omega_{n} \to E+i 0^{+}$ as a numerical calculation in which $0^{+}$ is a very small real number, total DOS can be eventuated
$$
D(E)=-\frac{1}{10 \pi N_{c}} \sum_{\mu, \mathbf{k}} \Im G_{\mu \mu}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, E+i 0^{+}) \tag{12}
$$
and
$$
D_{\zeta}^{C, H}(E)=-\frac{1}{2 \pi N_{c}} \sum_{\mu, \mathbf{k}} \Im G^{\mu \mu}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, E+i 0^{+}), \tag{13}
$$
where $\mu$ and $\zeta$ describe a sub-site $(C, H)$ and their orbitals, respectively. $N_{c}$ is the number of atoms in the unit cell. The EHC could be introduced by the following expression $^{46}$
$$
C(T)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} d E E D(E) \partial_{T} f(E) \tag{14}
$$
in which $D(E)$ calculated by Eq. (12) and $f(E)=1 /[e^{E / k_{B} T}+1]$ ($k_{B}$ is the Boltzmann constant) represents the Fermi-Dirac distribution function. By using Eqs. (12) and (14), the EHC would be obtained as
$$
C(T)=-\frac{1}{10 \pi N_{c} T^{2}} \sum_{\mu, \mathbf{k}} \Im \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} d E \frac{E^{2} e^{E / k_{B} T}}{\left(e^{E / k_{B} T}+1\right)^{2}} G_{\mu \mu}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, E+i 0^{+}) \tag{15}
$$
and
$$
C_{\zeta}^{C, H}(T)=-\frac{1}{2 \pi N_{c} T^{2}} \sum_{\mu, \mathbf{k}} \Im \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} d E \frac{E^{2} e^{E / k_{B} T}}{\left(e^{E / k_{B} T}+1\right)^{2}} G^{\mu \mu}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}, E+i 0^{+}). \tag{16}
$$

The study of $C(T)$ in the monolayer graphane lattice constitutes the main aim in this work. In the next section, the results of DOS and EHC are presented.
