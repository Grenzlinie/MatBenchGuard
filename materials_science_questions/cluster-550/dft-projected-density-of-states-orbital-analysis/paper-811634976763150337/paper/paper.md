**3D POLYMORPHS OF BORON NITRIDE: SCC-DFTB MODELING OF THE STABILITY AND STRUCTURAL, ELASTIC, AND ELECTRONIC CHARACTERISTICS**

A. N. Enyashin and A. L. Ivanovskii

UDC 541.16

The results of SCC-DFTB modeling of six known and hypothetical crystalline (3D) polymorphous modifications of boron nitride (BN) are presented. Their comparative stability and structural, elastic, and electronic characteristics are analyzed on the basis of the calculated data.

**Key words:** BN, 3D polymorphs, properties, modeling.

The arrival of the "era of carbon allotropes" [1], the most conspicuous events of which are associated with the discovery of quasi-zerodimensional (0D, fullerenes [2]), quasi-unidimensional (1D, nanotubes [3]), and quasi-two-dimensional (2D, graphene [4]) forms of carbon, is also marked by the synthesis (and assessment) of a multitude of new carbon allotropes (see the review [5]), and the search for analogous structures – polymorphous modifications (polymorphs) of other inorganic substances and compounds. In this area the greatest advances have been made in the development of theories about the polymorphism of the closest isoelectronic analog of carbon – boron nitride (BN).

At the present time the 0D-2D polymorphs of BN, including various types of fullerene-like molecules, nanotubes, and the so-called white graphene (atomic networks of boron nitride), have been investigated most (see [5-7]). In addition to these 0D-2D polymorphs, which are formed by boron and nitrogen atoms having coordination numbers of 2 or 3 with $sp^1$ and $sp^2$ types of hybridization respectively, there is great interest in the crystalline 3D ($sp^3$) forms of BN, the best-known representatives of which are the cubic (sphalerite-like, c-BN) and wurtzite-like (w-BN) nitrides of boron [8, 9].

In the present work we used a unified calculation method to make a comparative analysis of the stability, structural parameters, density, compression modulus, and electronic structure of five possible crystallographic 3D polymorphs of boron nitride containing $sp^3$-hybridized atoms and c-BN.

The following 3D structures of BN were studied. As indicated, the "classical" 3D ($sp^3$) polymorph of BN, cubic boron nitride (Borazon), was used as the starting structure (1). The next five polymorphs [Fig. 1, structures (2)-(6)] form crystalline 3D lattices of $sp^3$-hybridized B and N atoms and can be described as consisting of three main structural elements: single $sp^3$ atoms, tetragonal $B_2N_4$ rings, and/or $B_4N_4$ cubes. During construction of the respective crystal lattices the condition that the most energetically favorable alternative covalent B—N bonds are formed in the lattice was observed, i.e., each boron atom was located in an environment of nitrogen atoms and vice versa.

Thus, the polymorph (2) represents "stacks" of $B_2N_2$ rings, the tops of which are linked with the formation of covalent B—N bonds. The polymorph (3) (the structural analog of the carbon allotrope known as "supercubane" [2, 13]) is composed of $B_4N_4$ cubes, the tops of which are also linked by B—N bonds. The polymorph (4) consists of $B_4N_4$ cubes linked by single $sp^3$ boron (nitrogen) atoms, whereas the polymorph (5) can be represented as consisting of $B_2N_2$ rings having common tops.

From the obtained data it is possible to reach the following conclusions.

Depending on the structure of the crystal lattice, the investigated 3D polymorphs have substantially differing unit cell volumes $V_{\text{uc}}$ and, accordingly, densities $\rho$. The smallest value of $V_{\text{uc}}$ (maximum density) is given by c-BN, and the values of $V_{\text{uc}}$ (density $\rho$) for the remaining 3D polymorphs increase (decrease) in the order (2) $\rightarrow$ (6). The lightest (having minimum density) is the polymorph (6), in the structure of which the tetragonal $\text{B}_2\text{N}_2$ rings and $\text{B}_4\text{N}_4$ cubes are linked through single B or N atoms.

As follows from calculations of the $\Delta E_{\text{tot}}$ values [the total energy $E_{\text{tot}}$ of the modeled polymorphs in relation to $E_{\text{tot}}$ of c-BN (Table 1)], the least stable is the polymorph (3) consisting of the linked tops of the $\text{B}_4\text{N}_4$ cubes. The other 3D polymorphs of BN can be arranged in the order (2) $<$ (5) $<$ (6) $<$ (4) of increasing $\Delta E_{\text{tot}}$ values (decreasing stability). Thus, the 3D polymorphs containing $\text{B}_2\text{N}_2$ rings as structural elements proved more stable than the polymorphs containing $\text{B}_4\text{N}_4$ cubes.

The compression moduli $B$ of the investigated 3D ($sp^3$) polymorphs vary over a comparatively wide range: from 288 to 179 GPa. The largest values of the moduli $B$, compared with the compression modulus of c-BN, were obtained for the allotropes (2)-(4), and the smallest were obtained for the allotropes (5) and (6), which have the most complex lattices with the maximum number of atoms in the unit cell (Table 2).

It is seen from Fig. 2 that all the 3D polymorphs of boron nitride are broad-band semiconductors, where the top edge of the valence band and the bottom edge of the conduction band are formed by major contributions from the N$2p$ and B$2p$ states respectively. On the other hand the width of the forbidden gap (i.e., the conducting and optical characteristics) can change substantially depending on the type of 3D polymorph. From the data in Table 2, which gives the calculated values of $\Delta E_{\text{g}}$ [the change of the FG of the polymorphs (2)-(6) in relation to the FG of c-BN (~6.0-6.4 eV, see [14])], it follows that the $\Delta E_{\text{g}}$ values for the polymorphs (2), (3), and (6) are negative or close to zero, whereas the widths of the forbidden gaps for the other polymorphs (4) and (5) are appreciably larger than the FG of Borazon.

As a result the systematic calculations for the series of 3D ($sp^3$) polymorphs of BN by a single method have made it possible for the first time to reveal regularities in the variation of their stability and structural, elastic, and electronic characteristics in relation to the type of crystal lattice. The obtained results can clearly serve as useful reference points in the organization of researches on the directed synthesis of new BN materials with various functional characteristics.
