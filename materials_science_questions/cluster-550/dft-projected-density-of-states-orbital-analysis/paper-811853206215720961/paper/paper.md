## ABSTRACT
The electronic structures of the distrontium magnesium disilicate ($Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$(:$Eu^{2+}$)) materials were studied by a combined experimental and theoretical approach. The UV-VUV synchrotron radiation was applied in the experimental study while the electronic structures were investigated theoretically by using the density functional theory. The structure of the valence and conduction bands and the band gap energy of the material as well as the position of the $Eu^{2+}$ 4f ground state were calculated. The calculated band gap energy (6.7 eV) agrees well with the experimental value of 7.1 eV. The valence band consists mainly of the oxygen states and the bottom of the conduction band of the Sr states. The calculated occupied 4f ground state of $Eu^{2+}$ lies in the energy gap of the host though the position depends strongly on the Coulomb repulsion strength. The position of the 4f ground state with respect to the valence and conduction bands is discussed using the theoretical and experimental evidence available.

## 1. Introduction
The persistent luminescence materials and mechanisms are currently among the most popular subjects in the storage phosphor field. Their ability to produce thermally stimulated luminescence for several hours at room temperature is yet to be fully explained though several different mechanisms have been brought up (for the most recent works, *cf.* [1,2]). However, the ever more sophisticated applications demand the exact knowledge of the luminescence mechanism(s) and the trap identification. The problem of several energetic systems involved – host band structure, discrete energy levels of the (co-)dopants (*e.g.* $Eu^{2+}$, $R^{3+}$; R: rare earth) and those of defects – has to be solved before an agreement on the mechanism(s) can be reached. Recently, it has become evident that in order to explain the complex mechanism(s) involved theoretical methods have to be applied parallel to the experimental studies.

A new generation of materials has quite recently been introduced as alternatives for the alkaline earth aluminate based persistent luminescence materials [3,4]. The new materials include alkaline earth magnesium disilicates doped with $Eu^{2+}$ and trivalent rare earth ions, $M_2MgSi_2O_7$:$Eu^{2+}$,$R^{3+}$ (MCa, Sr, Ba; R: Nd, Dy, Tm). Their high chemical stability is accompanied with the efficient persistent luminescence.

In the present work, the luminescence processes as well as the band gap energy of the non-doped and $Eu^{2+}$ doped strontium magnesium disilicate ($Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$) materials were studied with the aid of the UV-VUV synchrotron radiation (SUPERLUMI/HASYLAB). Simultaneously, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out in order to estimate theoretically the electronic structure and to reveal the structure of the valence and conduction bands as well as the band gap energy. The position of the 4f ground state of the $Eu^{2+}$ ion in the energy gap of the host was determined as well. Eventually, the processes occurring in the persistent luminescence materials were discussed based on the combined experimental and theoretical results.

## 2. Experimental
The polycrystalline $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$:$Eu^{2+}$ material was prepared with a solid state reaction between stoichiometric amounts of $SrCO_3$, $Mg(NO_3)_2·6H_2O$, fumed $SiO_2$ and $Eu_2O_3$. The nominal concentration of $Eu^{2+}$ was 1 mol% of the strontium amount. The starting materials were ground to a homogeneous mixture in a ball mill. The mixtures were annealed in a reducing ($N_2$+10% $H_2$) atmosphere at $700\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 1 h and then at $1350\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 10 h. The

The excitation spectra of the $Eu^{2+}$ ion in the $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$ material were measured at 10K by using the synchrotron radiation with a 2-m McPherson $15^\circ$ normal incidence primary monochromator with a Hamamatsu R6358P photomultiplier at the SUPERLUMI station (Beamline I) of HASYLAB at DESY (Hamburg, Germany). Measurements for a short (22 ns) and long (86 ns) decay time were carried out with a delay of 2 and 82 ns after the excitation, respectively.

The electronic structures were calculated using the WIEN2k package [5]. WIEN2k is based on the full potential linearized augmented plane wave method, an approach which is among the most precise and reliable ways to calculate the electronic structure of solids. For the non-doped $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$ host material the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was employed. In order to describe better the strongly correlated 4f electrons of $Eu^{2+}$ the "fully localized limit" version of the localized density approximation (LDA+U) [6] and GGA+U as well as the hybrid [7] methods were used. The GGA+U and LDA+U methods require as an input the Coulomb repulsion strength (Hubbard parameter $U$) and the exchange parameter $J$ which can be related to Slater's integrals [8]. $U$ increases with increasing nuclear charge and valence state, whereas $J$ is almost independent on the number of d(f) electrons. $U$ and $J$ values of 7.62 and 0.68 eV, respectively, have been found to reproduce the experimentally observed splitting between the majority and minority spin states of $Gd^{3+}$ [9]. Since both $Gd^{3+}$ and $Eu^{2+}$ have the similar $4f^7$ ground state configuration, the dependence of the $Eu^{2+}$ doped system on the Hubbard parameter was calculated using selected $U$ values between 4.35 and 7.62 eV while the exchange parameter was fixed at 0.68 eV.

Spin-orbit coupling was included in the calculation of the $Eu^{2+}$ doped material. The number of $k$-points in the irreducible part of the Brillouin zone was 6 and 12 without and with the spin-orbit coupling, respectively. The number of basis functions used was always 1500.

## 3. Results and discussion

### 3.1. Crystal structure

All the $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$ materials studied were found to be free from impurities and to possess the tetragonal structure (space group: $P\bar{4}2_1m$, $Z=2$, no. 113, $a=7.996$ and $c=5.152$ Å [10]). The $Eu^{2+}$ ion is expected to substitute for $Sr^{2+}$ located in a single site in $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$ since the statistical ionic radii of the eight-fold coordinated species of $Eu^{2+}$ (1.25 Å) and $Sr^{2+}$ (1.26 Å [11]) are close to a perfect match. In contrast, $Eu^{2+}$ does not fit at all into the four-fold coordinated $Mg^{2+}$ site (0.57 Å). Therefore, one $Sr^{2+}$ per unit cell was replaced by $Eu^{2+}$ in the DFT calculations while keeping the unit cell dimensions intact.

### 3.2. Band structure

The synchrotron radiation excitation spectrum of the $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7:Eu^{2+}$ material at 10 K showed a significant increase in intensity due to the fundamental absorption of the host at *ca.* 175 nm (Fig. 1). The estimated band gap energy ($E_g$) was thus found to be *ca.* 7.1 eV. A significant increase in the (GGA) calculated density of states (DOS) of the pure $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$ material appears at 6.7 eV which value agrees very well with the increase in the excitation spectrum, the difference of 0.4 eV is not important when taken into account the experimental uncertainties.

With the present DFT calculations, the lowest-lying conduction band states of the non-doped $Sr_2MgSi_2O_7$ material are located at 5.0 eV while the same value for the $Eu^{2+}$ doped material is 5.5 eV. The calculated $E_g$ value was found to be independent of the $U$ parameter in the range studied. It should be noted that the experimental DOS (if the band in the excitation spectrum can be called as such) has also non-zero values at energies lower than 7.1 eV.

The valence band has mainly the O(2p) character, whereas the conduction band consists mostly of Sr – but also the Mg and Si – states (Fig. 1). The density of states at the bottom of the conduction band mostly results from strontium. The maximum DOS of Mg and Si is located deep in the conduction band. Some features of the calculated conduction band fine structure were observed also in the excitation spectrum of the non-doped material. No perfect match between the two results can be anticipated since the electron transfer can occur from anywhere in the valence band, not only from the top. The structure of the valence and conduction bands as well as the $E_g$ value did not change substantially when calculated using either the GGA(+U), LDA(+U) or hybrid methods. No significant improvement in the accuracy of the calculated band structure was thus achieved when employing different DFT methods presently in common use.

### 3.3. 4f ground state of $\boldsymbol{Eu^{2+}}$

The position of the 4f ground state of $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ can be experimentally determined when the $\text{O}^{2-}(2\text{p})-\text{Eu}^{3+}$ charge transfer energy ($ca$. 5 eV for $\text{Sr}_2\text{MgSi}_2\text{O}_7$:$\text{Eu}^{2+}$ [12]) is known. It is approximately the same as the energy difference between the top of the valence band and the 4f ground level of the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ ion [13]. The charge transfer is confirmed to take place between the $\text{O}^{2-}$ and $\text{Eu}^{3+}$ ions by the calculated $\text{O}^{2-}(2\text{p})$ character of the top of the valence band (see section 3.2.). However, there always remains an uncertainty of $ca$. 0.5 eV due to the broad band nature of the charge transfer absorption. As expected, the occupied (majority spin) $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f ground state is positioned in the energy gap of the host by the DFT calculations (Fig. 2). The position of the 4f ground state with respect to the valence and conduction band depends on the Coulomb repulsion strength ($U$ was varied in the range of 4.35–7.62 eV). The dependence is linear, with the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ ground state located closer to the valence band for the higher $U$ values (Fig. 3). The strong $U$ dependence provides a challenge in determining the absolute position of the 4f ground state with the DFT calculations since there are no straightforward methods to calculate the $U$ parameter.

The energy difference between the 4f ground state and the bottom of the conduction band can be tentatively deduced from experiments when the energy of the luminescence band due to the $4\text{f}^65\text{d}^1(^2\text{D})\rightarrow4\text{f}^7(^8\text{S}_{7/2})$ transition of $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ is known. Such a luminescence band in the $\text{Sr}_2\text{MgSi}_2\text{O}_7$:$\text{Eu}^{2+}$ material is observed at 470 nm (2.6 eV)[14]. Efficient electron transfer from the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 5d states to the conduction band followed by trapping of electrons is required for the appearance of the persistent luminescence [1,2]. Thus, the 5d states must be positioned close to or within the conduction band in this material. Moreover, the lowest $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 5d state has to be located below the conduction band as the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ luminescence is observed. Therefore, in combination with the wide band gap of the $\text{Sr}_2\text{MgSi}_2\text{O}_7$ host, it is confirmed that the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f ground state is not far from the conduction band and that the excited electrons at the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ ions which can be released to the conduction band act as the primary charge carriers responsible for the persistent luminescence. Unfortunately, the theoretical methods used do not describe the 5d states accurately and the calculated position is inside the conduction band.

Inclusion of the spin–orbit coupling leads to the broadening and splitting of the occupied $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f ground state (Fig. 2).

Fig. 3. Energy differences of the occupied $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f ground state to the valence and conduction bands in $\text{Sr}_2\text{MgSi}_2\text{O}_7$:$\text{Eu}^{2+}$ (GGA+U, spin–orbit coupling included).

However, neither the general structure of the valence and conduction bands nor the band gap energy is changed. The width of the 4f band is 0.6 and 0.2 eV with and without spin–orbit coupling, respectively. This is not in agreement with experimental results since the splitting of the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f ground state $(^8\text{S}_{7/2})$ with the inclusion of spin–orbit coupling is almost negligible. Such a strong broadening by the DFT calculations is caused by insufficient treatment of strong correlations of the 4f electrons. The unoccupied (minority spin) $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f states rise high in the conduction band and are broadened due to hybridization with the conduction band states. Therefore, the influence of spin–orbit coupling is not so pronounced for the unoccupied states. The position of the occupied $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f ground state is almost identical when calculated with the GGA+U, LDA+U and hybrid methods.

### 4. Conclusions

A significant increase in the calculated DOS of the pure $\text{Sr}_2\text{MgSi}_2\text{O}_7$ material was observed at 6.7 eV. This agrees well with the band gap energy deduced from the synchrotron radiation excitation spectrum, 7.1 eV. The valence band has mainly oxygen character, whereas the bottom of the conduction band consists mostly of the strontium states. The position of the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ 4f ground state in the energy gap of the host was approximately determined using the DFT method. The position of the 4f ground state is linearly dependent on the Coulomb repulsion strength. The high band gap energy value obtained in this work combined with the previously reported experimental and theoretical results confirm that the electrons are the main charge carriers mostly influencing the persistent luminescence process in this material.
