# Structural, electronic and elastic properties of YCu from first principles

G. Uğur¹, M. Çivi¹, Ş. Uğur¹, F. Soyalp², R. Ellialtıoğlu³

(1. Department of Physics, Gazi University, Teknikokullar, Ankara, 06500 Turkey; 2. Department of Physics, Yüzüncü Yıl University, Zeve Kampüsü, Van, 65080 Turkey;
3. Department of Physics Engineering, Hacettepe University, Beytepe, Ankara, 06800 Turkey)

Received 15 September 2008; revised 19 December 2008

**Abstract:** The structural, electronic and elastic properties of YCu compound in the B2 (CsCl) phase were investigated using the density functional theory (DFT) within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). The electronic density of states (DOS) obtained in this way accorded well with the results of a recent study utilizing the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FLAPW) method. We also found that the density of $d$-states at the Fermi energy was low. The calculated equilibrium properties such as lattice constant, bulk modulus and its first derivative, and the elastic constants were in good agreement with experimental and theoretical results.

**Keywords:** YCu, electronic band structure, density of states, elastic constants, bulk modulus, *ab initio*; rare earths

The intermetallic YCu alloy has attracted considerable attention during the past four decades due to its interesting combination of mechanical, electric, thermal and elastic properties⁽¹⁻¹⁵⁾. Recently the full-potential augmented plane wave (FLAPW) based calculations on YCu were reported by Morris et al.⁽¹⁵⁾ This alloy has been found to have significant polycrystalline ductility, in some cases exceeding 20% ductility in tension⁽¹⁵⁾. The YCu compound taken up for investigation crystallizes in the cesium chloride structure (B2 type) with the space group symmetry of $Pm3m$. Transition from B2 phase to orthorhombic B27 phase has been observed⁽¹⁶⁾ at about 140 K.

To understand some of the physical properties of YCu, a detailed description of its electronic structure is needed. In this paper, we have studied the electronic and elastic properties of YCu in B2 phase by using plane-wave pseudopotential method of the density functional theory (DFT) with generalized gradient approximation (GGA) functionals. The calculated structural parameters, elastic constants and electronic properties were compared with previous experimental and theoretical studies.

## 1 Theoretical methods

All calculations here on B2 YCu were performed using the Quantum ESPRESSO code⁽¹⁷⁾, which is based on the density functional theory (DFT)⁽¹⁸⁾. The electronic exchange correlation potential was calculated by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the scheme of Perdew-Wang⁽¹⁹⁾.

The wave functions were expanded into plane waves with kinetic energies of up to 40 Ryd. Self consistent solutions of the Kohn–Sham equations were obtained by employing a set of 47 k points within the irreducible part of the Brillouin zone.

## 2 Results

### 2.1 Structural and elastic properties

The equilibrium lattice parameter has been determined by minimizing the crystal total energy calculated for different values of the lattice parameter. Then, these energy values have been fitted to Murnaghan equation of state⁽²⁰⁾ in order to obtain the lattice constant, and the first-order pressure derivative of the bulk modulus $B'$. We found the derivative of the bulk modulus $B'$=5.61, and the lattice constant $a$=0.3471 nm. The calculated value of the lattice constant was in good agreement with the experimental value⁽¹⁵⁾.

Elastic properties of solids are closely related to many fundamental solid-state properties, such as Debye temperature, Grüneisen parameter, melting point, specific heat, thermal expansion coefficient and so on. Cubic crystals have only three independent elastic constants, namely $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$. These theoretical elastic constants are obtained from the energy variations caused by small strains applied to equilibrium lattice configuration. The bulk modulus $B$, $C_{44}$, and shear modulus $C'$=($C_{11}$-$C_{12}$)/2 are calculated, respectively⁽²¹⁾, from

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{V}=\frac{9}{2} B \delta^{2}
$$

where $V$ and $\Delta E$ are the volume of unstrained lattice cell and the energy variation from strain with vector $e=(e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{3}, e_{4}, e_{5}$, $e_{6}$) , respectively. $C_{44}$ can be calculated from

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{V}=\frac{3}{2} C_{44} \delta^{2}
$$

and $C'$ is given by

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{V}=6 C^{\prime} \delta^{2}+O \delta^{3}
$$

Then $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ can be deduced from these by using the expressions $C_{1}=(3 B+4 C') / 3$ and $C_{12}=(3 B-2 C') / 3$.

In this study, we have calculated 21 sets of $\Delta E / V$ by vary- ing $\delta$ from $-0.02$ to $0.02$ in steps of $0.002$. Then, we fitted these data by a parabola, and the elastic constants were obtained from quadratic coefficients. Our calculated elastic constants $(C_{11}, C_{12}, C_{44})$, and the bulk modulus $(B)$ are listed and compared with experimental $^{[15]}$ and theoretical results $^{[13-15]}$ in Table 1. In general, our results are in good agreement with the experimental $^{[15]}$ results. In particular, a maximum difference has been observed for our $C_{44}$ value which is $6\%$ off from the corresponding experimental $^{[15]}$ value of $32.3$ GPa. Whereas the agreement with the theoretical results is better $^{[14]}$, namely, the discrepancies for the elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$ in the B2 structure are $3\%$, $1\%$ and $5\%$, respectively.

### 2.2 Electronic properties
Fig. 1 shows the energy band structure of YCu with the GGA approximation. The energy band structure is calculated along the path that contains the highest number of high-symmetry points of the first Brillouin zone, namely, $\Gamma \to X \to$ $M \to R \to \Gamma \to M$. The Fermi level crosses the yttrium bands which contribute to the Fermi level, *i.e.* the YCu is metallic-like. The valance bands are dominated by the contribution of Y and Cu $p$-states. The total and partial DOS are plotted in Fig. 2. There is an energy region from around $-4$ eV to around $-2$ eV, consisting of Cu $d$-states. The Y $4d$-states contribute to the DOS at the Fermi level. At higher energies peaks correspond mainly to the Y $d$-states.

In YCu, the density of $d$-states at the Fermi energy is low. In contrast, for YMg (NiAl), the Y (Ni) $d$-states were reported to be partially occupied $^{[14,15]}$. The total DOS at Fermi level, $N(E_{F})$, was obtained as 1.32 states/eV, which agrees very well with the calculations of Ref.[8]. Consequently, there is very good agreement between the density of dominating $d$-states obtained in this work and the previously calculated results associated with this compound $^{[13-15]}$.
