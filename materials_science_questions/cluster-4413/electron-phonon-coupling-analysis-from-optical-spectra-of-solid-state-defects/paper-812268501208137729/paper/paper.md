## Abstract

The emission and absorption spectra of $Er^{3+}$ ions in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder are investigated at room temperature. The crystal field splitting of $^{4}I_{15/2}$, $^{4}I_{13/2}$, $^{4}I_{9/2}$ and $^{4}S_{3/2}$ multiplets of $Er^{3+}$ was calculated using a parameterized Hamiltonian including crystal field terms. A final standard deviation of $5.3\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ is obtained between 22 calculated and experimental Stark. The strength crystal field parameters of $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder are compared to those of erbium doped $Y_{2}O_{3}$ single crystal. In addition, the location of the ground and the first excited states of $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder is determined. The $^{4}I_{15/2}$ ground state of $Er^{3+}$ is located at $12750\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ below the valence band edge. The $^{4}I_{13/2}$ and $^{4}I_{11/2}$ multiplets are set in resonance with the valence band. A configuration coordinate diagram is proposed for $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder.

## 1. Introduction

The optical properties of rare earth sesquioxides ($Ln_{2}O_{3}$) have been extensively studied, since the oxides are known to be excellent laser host materials [1]. Most theoretical calculations of crystal field parameters of rare earth in 4f shell have been concentrated on single crystals. In the present study, we focus on the emission and absorption spectra of $Er^{3+}$ ions in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder. A crystal-field analysis is presented for the energy levels of $Er^{3+}(4f^{11})$. The calculated crystal field parameters are compared with those reported for $Er^{3+}$ ions in $Y_{2}O_{3}$ single crystal.

In the second part, we are interested by the location of the $Er^{3+}$ manifolds in the band gap of $Er_{2}O_{3}$. Indeed the location of the localized electronic states of ions in insulating compounds plays an important role in determining many of their optical properties. When there is resonance between the localized states and the impurity host band states, charge transfer with the formation of defects can produce large changes in the optical properties. It has been demonstrated for many of the rare earth ions in oxide hosts that their ground states actually lay below the top of the valence band (VB) [2]. In the present work, we determinate the position of the $^{4}I_{15/2}$ ground state and the first excited states of $Er^{3+}$ relative to the valence band of the $Er_{2}O_{3}$ host. A coordinate diagram model is proposed for $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder.

The sesquioxide $\mathrm{Er}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ is isostructural to $\mathrm{Y}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ and crys- tallizes in a cubic bixbyite structure with space group Ia3. In this lattice, two distinct sites are available for rare earth ions, one with point group $C_{2}$ and the second with point group $S_{6}$. According to the selection rules, the electric dipole tran- sitions are not allowed for the $\mathrm{Er}^{3+}$ ions occupying the $S_{6}$ sites due to its center of inversion. So, it can be assumed that f-f spectra are expected to show electric dipole transitions pertaining mainly to $\mathrm{Er}^{3+}$ ions in $C_{2}$ sites [4].

### 3. Absorption and fluorescence spectra

The $\mathrm{Er}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ powder spectra can be related to the $\mathrm{Er}^{3+}: \mathrm{Y}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ single crystal spectra. Indeed, using the relevant energy scheme established by Chang et al. [5] for $\mathrm{Er}^{3+}$ ions in $C_{2}$ sites symmetry center of $\mathrm{Y}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$, all emission and absorption lines have been identified in the spectra with some reassign- ments. The differences between the spectra are due to the host changes from $\mathrm{Er}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ powder to $\mathrm{Y}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ single crystal. The typ- ical line-width in $\mathrm{Er}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ powder is approximately $10 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$ at room temperature, which is in the same order as that in $\mathrm{Y}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}$ crystal. This suggests that the crystalline powder preserves the $C_{2}$ site symmetry, and there is no contribution of other symmetry sites.

Figs. 3 and 4 show the details of the $^{4} \mathrm{I}_{15 / 2}(\mathrm{Z}) \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{I}_{11 / 2}(\mathrm{~A})$ and the $^{4} \mathrm{I}_{15 / 2} \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{I}_{13 / 2}(\mathrm{Y})$ transitions, respectively.

The fluorescence spectra observed between 515 and 880 nm at room temperature present transitions from $^{4} \mathrm{~S}_{3 / 2}(\mathrm{E}) \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{I}_{15 / 2}$ (Fig. 5), $^{4} \mathrm{~F}_{9 / 2}(\mathrm{D}) \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{I}_{15 / 2}$ (Fig. 6), $^{4} \mathrm{I}_{9 / 2} \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{I}_{15 / 2}$ (Fig. 7) and $^{4} \mathrm{~S}_{3 / 2} \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{I}_{13 / 2}$ (Fig. 8). The $^{2} \mathrm{H}_{11 / 2}$ multiplet fluorescence intensity (515-540 nm) is very low even that the excitation energy is higher than this multiplet energy. The most intense fluorescence is that originating from $^{4} \mathrm{~S}_{3 / 2}$ multiplet.

The splitting of the $^{4} \mathrm{I}_{15 / 2}$ manifold can be established from fluorescence that originates from the lower energy Stark level of $^{4} \mathrm{~S}_{3 / 2}$ (Fig. 5). Transitions beginning with $\mathrm{E}_{1} \rightarrow Z_{1}$ through $\mathrm{E}_{1} \rightarrow Z_{8}$ are shown in Fig. 5 and listed in Table 1. The tran- sitions from the $\mathrm{E}_{2}$ Stark level to the $Z_{i}(i=1, \ldots, 8)$ Stark levels are then deduced from the same spectrum.

The Stark levels originating from the $^{4} \mathrm{~F}_{9 / 2}$ and $^{4} \mathrm{I}_{11 / 2}$ mul tiplets will not be included in the fitting procedure, since their spectra are not well resolved (Figs. 3 and 6, respectively).

It should be mentioned finally, that the Stark levels apper- taining to the $^{4} \mathrm{~S}_{3 / 2} \rightarrow{ }^{4} \mathrm{I}_{13 / 2}$ transitions (Fig. 8) were not used in the determination of the experimental energetic levels dia- gram because of the broadness of these transitions, so that the maximum of each one is not well resolved.

## 4. Energy level calculation

The one-electron crystal field (c.f.) Hamiltonian can be expressed as a sum of products between the real and imaginary ($B_q^k$ and $S_q^k$, respectively) parts of the c.f. parameters and function $(C_q^k)$ related to spherical harmonic $(Y_q^k)$ [6]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\text{c.f.}}= & \sum_{k=2,4,6} \sum_{q=0}^{k}[ B_{q}^{k}(C_{q}^{k}+(-1)^{q} C_{-q}^{k})+i S_{q}^{k}(C_{q}^{k}-(-1)^{q} \\
& \left.\times C_{-q}^{k}\right)]
\end{aligned}
$$

For the $C_2$ symmetry, the sum on $q$ runs over $q=0, \pm 2, \dots$, $\pm k$ (as is appropriate for $C_2$ symmetry) with the $C_2$-axis chosen as the quantization axis. We assume that the coordinate

The matrix elements of $C_q^k$ operators are calculated numerically [7]. The crystal field parameters $B_q^k$ and $S_q^k$ are regarded as empirical parameters. For this purpose, a least squares fitting program based on the Nelder and Mead [8] iterative routines was developed. The quality of the fits was determined by the root mean square (rms) deviation $\sigma$ (in $\text{cm}^{-1}$) defined as [9,10]:

$$
\sigma = \left[ \sum_{i} \frac{(E_i^{\text{cal}} - E_i^{\text{obs}})}{N - P} \right]^{1/2}
$$

where $E_i^{\text{obs}}$ and $E_i^{\text{cal}}$ are experimentally observed and calculated energies for level $i$, respectively, $N$ denotes the number of levels and $P$ is the number of parameters that are varied freely.

The starting set of crystal field parameters was taken from references [5] corresponding to the $\text{Er}^{3+}$ ions in $\text{Y}_2\text{O}_3$ single crystal.

The fitting procedure was then performed between 22 calculated and experimental Stark energy levels with a final rms deviation of $5.3\,\text{cm}^{-1}$. Since the final set of $B_q^k$ and $S_q^k$ changed relatively little from the starting set, it is not likely that we encounter false minima along the way. Furthermore the $\text{A}_i \rightarrow \text{Z}_j$ and $\text{D}_i \rightarrow \text{Z}_j$ transitions reported in Figs. 3 and 6, respectively, which are not included in the fitting procedure, can be assigned using the calculated Stark energy levels of $^4$I$_{15/2}$, $^4$I$_{11/2}$ and $^4$F$_{9/2}$ reported in Table 1 confirming our assignment.

Indeed, the absorption spectrum of the $^{4}I_{9/2}$ multiplet is overlapped with a large band involving the crystal host (VB). This large band is due to the host absorption of energy, corresponding to a transition of an electron from the $^{4}I_{15/2}$ ground state to the VB. If the energy excitation is less than $12750\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, this energy is absorbed by the valence band and by the $Er^{3+}$ excited states lying in this band. When the energy excitation becomes higher than $12750\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, the absorbance vanishes to zero. This locates the valence band edge at $E_{0}=12750\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (1.59 eV) (Fig. 9) from the $^{4}I_{15/2}$ ground state.

Now let as focus on the very low luminescence intensity related to the $^{2}H_{11/2}$ manifold even that the excitation energy is higher than this manifold.

It is well established that the $^{2}H_{11/2}$ multiplet of the $4f^{3}$ and $4f^{11}$ configurations belongs to a set of "abnormal" terms existing among the series of rare-earth ions, for which the crystal field levels cannot be satisfactory fitted by the standard procedure [12,13]. Where anomalous splitting of the $^{2}H_{11/2}$ multiplet is attributed by the authors to correlation effect.

This discrepancy between calculated and experimental Stark energy levels, which is generally present for the $^{2}H_{11/2}$ state, can be explicated in our case, by the presence of a strong electron-lattice coupling for this state. The $^{2}H_{11/2}$ state will be a reservoir of the $^{4}S_{3/2}$ state. For this reason, the most intense fluorescence is that originating from this $^{4}S_{3/2}$ manifold. In order to clarify the significant non-radiative relaxation between the $^{2}H_{11/2}$ and the $^{4}S_{3/2}$ multiplets, it is necessary to take into account lattice relaxation. This possible process can be illustrated with the energy level scheme in configuration coordinate space (Fig. 9).

From the above results, and discussion, we give a configuration diagram model for $Er^{3+}$ ions in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder (Fig. 9). The parabolas shown in the figure are not calculated theoretically, but are drawn for the qualitative discussion of the observed effects. This diagram explicates the absorption and the emission spectra of $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder.

## 6. Conclusions

In the present study, we report the room temperature absorption and fluorescence spectra of $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder. The spectra include the crystal field splitting of six multiplets, $^{2S+1}L_{J}$, of $Er^{3+}(4f^{11})$. All the transitions are well assigned to $Er^{3+}$ in $C_{2}$ site, the powder clearly shows that the symmetry of $Er^{3+}$ ion site remains cubic. A very satisfactory agreement is obtained between calculated and experimental energy Stark levels with a standard rms deviation of $5.3\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. The calculated c.f. parameters of $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder are in good agreement with those reported for $Er^{3+}$ in $Y_{2}O_{3}$ single crystal. However the $N_{V}$ strength parameter of $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder is slightly little than that of $Y_{2}O_{3}$ single crystal.

In addition, the location of the $^{4}I_{15/2}$ ground state of $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder is determined to be located at about $12750\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ from the valence band edge. We have also demonstrated that the $^{4}I_{13/2}$ and $^{4}I_{11/2}$ multiplets lay in resonance with the valence band whereas the $^{4}I_{9/2}$ multiplet is in quasi-resonance with this band. The low intensity emission from the $^{2}H_{11/2}$ multiplet can be explained by the presence of a strong electron-lattice coupling for this state. Finally, a configuration coordinate diagram is proposed for $Er^{3+}$ in $Er_{2}O_{3}$ powder, which explicates the absorption and emission spectra.
