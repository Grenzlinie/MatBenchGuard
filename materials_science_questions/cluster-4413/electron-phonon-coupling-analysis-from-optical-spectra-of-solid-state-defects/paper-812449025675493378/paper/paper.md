# EXCITONIC AND TIME-RESOLVED EDGE EMISSIONS OF IODINE-DOPED CUBIC ZnS CRYSTALS EXCITED BY AN EXCIMER LASER

T. Taguchi, T. Yokogawa and H. Yamashita*

Faculty of Engineering, Osaka University, Suita, Osaka 565, Japan

(Received 25 October 1983 by H. Kamimura)

Excitonic-emission lines and edge-emission bands of highly cubic-structured ZnS crystals doped with iodine, have been observed using an Xe-Cl excimer laser capable of band-to-band excitation at 77 or 4.2 K. A free-exciton line at 3.801 eV, and prominent bound-exciton lines at 3.792, 3.729 and 3.701 eV were resolved well. It is tentatively suggested that the 3.792 eV line is due to excitons bound to a relatively shallow donor, whilst both the 3.729 and 3.701 eV lines may be due to excitons bound to deep acceptors found in the self-activated blue-luminescence band. Time-resolved studies suggest that the edge emissions consist of LO-phonon-replicated free-to-bound and donor-acceptor pair recombinations, and that as a result a common acceptor is involved.

IN MOST II-VI COMPOUND CRYSTALS, the sharp emission lines due to bound-excitons and edge-emission bands are well-known features of the radiative recombination luminescence; they may be used to characterise extrinsic impurities and defects due to deviations from stoichiometry in the crystals, and to determine the energy levels of the donors and acceptors concerned.

In the past decade, despite the numerous reports [1] of the blue-, green- and red-luminescence band spectra in hexagonal or cubic ZnS phosphors and crystals, there has been very little information available on the excitonic- and edge-emission spectra in highly cubic-structured ZnS single crystals, except a few recent cathodoluminescent studies [2, 3]. This is because of the difficulties of growing satisfactory single crystals with nearly all cubic form and of finding suitable optical sources of short wavelength radiation.

In ZnS crystals doped with Cl, Samelson and Lempicki [4] reported at least two edge emission series with wavelengths between 330 and 350 nm, involving many LO-phonon replicas. Gross and Suslina [5] have attributed the edge emission observed in their hexagonal crystals to a donor-acceptor pair recombination. Their work, however, has not been until now followed up using photoluminescent investigations, and did not lead to unambiguous interpretations of the UV emission lines.

Most recently, we have particularly emphasised that crystals of ZnS grown by the iodine-transport method (so-called ZnS:I) show excellent cubic structure [6] having a lower stacking-fault density than the melt-grown ZnS and allowing the production of low-resistivity light-emitting-diode (LED) material capable of strong blue emission [7].

In order to get a deeper understanding of the recombination characteristics of the defect centres involved in cubic ZnS:I crystals, we attempted to evaluate the exciton and edge emissions appearing at 77 or 4.2 K. This should give the levels of excitons bound to donors or acceptors using a simple effective-mass argument [8] developed for CdTe, ZnTe and ZnSe. This paper thus describes the characteristics of the excitonic-emission lines and edge-emission bands excited by an Xe-Cl excimer laser with short wavelength of 308 nm (corresponding to 4.0 eV) at 77 and 4.2 K in cubic-ZnS single crystals. Freshly cleaved (110) faces of the crystal were used for all luminescence measurements. According to the PIXE (proton-induced X-ray emission) measurements for as-grown high-resistivity ZnS:I crystals, the impurity content was about 1 p.p.m. for As, Fe and Ni, and less than 1 p.p.m. for Nb, Cr, Co and I. After heat-treatment using the molten-Zn extraction technique at $960^{\circ}C$ for 122 hr, those residual impurities mentioned above were not detectable at p.p.m. levels, and Cu and Al were also not detectable at all. We also measured the excitonic-and edge-emission spectra to make a comparison with those in the melt-grown ZnS including a partly hexagonal modification. An Xe-Cl excimer laser was operated at a repetition rate of 15 Hz and a pulse width of 15 nsec, and at a weak power density of about $0.8\ \text{kW}\ \text{cm}^{-2}$ so as to avoid effects of high-density excitation by the laser. The signal from the crystals, detected by a high-sensitive UV photomultiplier (Hamamastu R 106UH) coupled to a single grating monochromator, was measured by a PAR

Figure 1 shows the excitonic- and edge-emission spectra obtained at 4.2 K in both as-grown high-resistivity (solid curve) and $n$-type low-resistivity (dotted curve) ZnS: I crystals. There appeared several excitonic (free or bound exciton) lines; $I_{\text{ex}}$ at 3.801 eV, $I_D$ at 3.792 eV, and $I_a$ and $I_\beta$ associated with longitudinal optical phonon (LO) replicas [4], having energy intervals of about 42 meV, at 3.729 and 3.701 eV, respectively. If the $I_D$ line is due to a free-exciton recombination, as proposed previously by Gezei and Woods [2] the exciton-binding energy is calculated to be 48 meV, assuming the band-gap energy $(E_g)$ of 3.84 eV at 4.2 K [9]. This value is certainly larger than the reported values of 28-40 meV [10, 11]. The position of the $A_1$ exciton peak was observed to be 326.4 nm at 4.2 K [10] and 326.6 nm at 77 K [12] respectively, in cubic crystals, from the exciton absorption and excitation measurements; it is thus reasonable to consider that the $I_{\text{ex}}$ line is ascribed to the $\Gamma_6-\Gamma_8$ free-exciton recombination. We therefore obtain the exciton-binding energy $(E_{\text{ex}})$ as about 40 meV, which is in good agreement with the previous data [10, 11].

From the $E_{\text{ex}}$ corresponding to the ground-state energy $(G)$ of $n=1$ excitons, a reduced exciton mass $(\mu_0)$ is estimated to be 0.203 using $G=e^2\mu_0/2h^2\epsilon_0^2n^2$, where $\epsilon_0$ is the static dielectric constant (8.3) [13] and $e$ the charge on a proton. The other exciton binding energies for the $I_D$, $I_a$ and $I_\beta$ lines are calculated to be 9, 72 and 100 meV, respectively.

After application of the molten-Zn extraction technique, the crystal exhibited low-resistivity $n$-type conduction, with a resistivity of about $10\ \Omega\mathrm{cm}$; the $I_D$ line was intensified much more and the $I_a$ line becomes well-resolved as seen in Fig. 1, whilst the $I_\beta$ line was a little decreased in intensity. Similar features in the excitonic spectrum were observed in the melt-grown ZnS. The $I_\beta$ line, however, was not found and the excitonic spectrum was shifted towards shorter wavelengths (higher photon energies) by about 0.5 nm, because of the presence of some hexagonal structured materials, where the $I_{\text{ex}}$ line was then situated at 3.807 eV at 4.2 K.

Assuming that the electron effective mass $m_e^*=0.39m_e$ [14] and the average hole effective mass, $m_h^*=0.42m_e$, deduced from $\mu_0$, the mass ratio $(a=m_e^*/m_h^*)$ is 0.95. The relationship between the neutral donor or acceptor bound-exciton emissions and the ionisation energies of localised centres in cubic-ZnS is as follows [8],

neutral-donor bound-exciton
$$
(D^0,X)=E_g-E_{\text{ex}}-0.1E_D,\tag{1}
$$

neutral-acceptor bound-exciton
$$
(A^0,X)=E_g-E_{\text{ex}}-0.12E_A,\tag{2}
$$

where $E_D$ and $E_A$ are the donor and acceptor ionisation energies, $E_g$ is the band-gap energy and $X$ denotes the free exciton.

Considering the variations in intensity of each exciton line following the molten-Zn extraction, it is reasonable to assume that the $I_D$ line is attributed to excitons bound to a neutral donor, which might be derived from the creation of $S$ vacancies, and that the $I_a$ and $I_\beta$ lines are ascribed to excitons bound to neutral acceptors, probably due to the reduction of the concentration of Zn vacancies or Zn-vacancy-related defects. One can thus estimate the donor $(E_D)$ and acceptor $(E_{A1}$ and $E_{A2})$ ionisation energies to be approximately 0.1, 0.6 and 0.83 eV, respectively, using equations (1) and (2). It seems that the donor depth estimated is relatively shallow, whilst two acceptor depths are extremely deep. It is, however, worth noting that in fact there are two acceptor levels observed in the self-activated blue-luminescence bands, with double peaks [7] separated by about 0.18 eV at 2.65 eV.

It is seen in Fig. 1 that two edge-emission bands (designated $A$ and $B$) appeared, together with

LO-phonon replicas displaced by about 40 meV; the zero-phonon lines of the $A$ and $B$ bands are respectively at 3.659 and 3.543 eV. These photon energies and band shapes are different from the results obtained in ZnS:Cl by Samelson and Lempicki [4] and by Gross and Suslina [5] in hexagonal ZnS, but the $A$ band seems to be near the $Q$ band series in cubic crystals reported by Gezei and Woods [2]. After the heat-treatment by molten-Zn extraction, the $A$ and $B$ bands are increased in intensity by about one or two orders of magnitude compared with their values in as-grown crystals. It is noted that after this heat-treatment neither $A$ nor $B$ band disappeared, but there are small changes in the LO-phonon sidebands. It may therefore be suggested that both bands possess a common recombination centre, perhaps an acceptor. The intensity ratio of $A_{1} / A_{0}$ and $B_{1} / B_{0}$ ($A_{0}$, $B_{0}$: zero-phonon line; $A_{1}$, $B_{1}$: 1-LO-phonon line) yields approximately 1.3, indicating the value of the electron-LO-phonon coupling strength $(S)$. The solid-bar lines indicated in Fig. 1 show each LO-phonon intensity, calculated using a Poisson distribution by $I_{n}=e^{-S} S^{n} / n!$, where $n$ is a number of LO-phonons. In the case of phonon interaction with electron transitions from the conduction band to a localised centre, the electron-LO-phonon coupling constant is generally expressed by [15],

$$
S=\frac{5}{16 a_{h}} \frac{e^{2}}{\hbar w_{\mathrm{LO}}}\left(\epsilon_{\infty}^{-1}-\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\right), \quad (3)
$$

where $a_{h}$ is the hole radius in an acceptor centre and $E_{A}=e^{2} / \epsilon_{0} a_{h}$, $E_{A}$ is an acceptor binding energy. $\hbar w_{\mathrm{LO}}$ is an LO-phonon energy and $\epsilon_{\infty}, \epsilon_{0}$ are the high frequency and static dielectric constants. Putting $S=1.3$ in equation (3), gives an acceptor level involved in both $A$ and $B$ edge-emissions to be about 0.2 eV above the valence band.

In order to clarify further the recombination processes resulting in the edge-emission, time-resolved spectral measurements were made at 77 and 4.2 K in $n$-type ZnS:I crystals. The results are described in Fig. 2. At 4.2 K, with increasing time after pulsed excitation the $B$ series shifts towards lower photon energies, whilst the $A$ series showed no apparent shift. After $5 \mu \mathrm{sec}$ delay, the $B_{0}$ peak was situated at about 3.481 eV; with further delay the band showed no measurable further shift. The results therefore give a direct proof that the $B$ band series is due to a distant donor-acceptor pair emission, and that the $A$ band series is due to an electronic transition between the conduction band and an acceptor level. With increasing temperature, the $A$ band becomes pronounced in intensity and, at 77 K, only the $A^{*}$ band, without LO-phonon structure and having a maximum at 3.584 eV, appeared; it showed no apparent shift towards lower photon energies with time after pulsed excitation. It is most likely that the band occurring at 77 K is due to the free-to-bound transition.

On the basis of the above results, plausible recombination processes are suggested for the $A$ and $B$ edge-emissions, as shown in the insert of Fig. 2. It is proposed that the $A$ band is concerned with the higher-energy series, whilst the $B$ band is due to the lower-energy series, usually observed in the edge-emission of II-VI compounds. An acceptor level is then calculated, from the peak position of the $A_{0}$ line, to be about 0.2 eV above the valence band, which is in good agreement with the value of 0.22 eV from the peak at 3.584 eV of the edge-emission band obtained at 77 K, assuming $E_{g}(77 \mathrm{~K})=3.8 \mathrm{eV}$, and also from the evaluation of $S$. It should be reasonable to consider on the other hand that an energy difference between the $A_{0}$ and $B_{0}$ line positions gives rise to a donor level which is estimated to be about 0.11 eV below the conduction band. Such a donor level seems to be identical with a 0.14 eV donor found in low-resistivity ZnS:I single crystals by Hall effect measurement [7] and also with the value of $E_{D}$ analysed from the $I_{D}$ donor bound-exciton line described in the present paper. Since the resultant acceptor energy level is deeper relative to the edge emissions in other II-VI compounds such as CdTe, CdS and ZnSe, the observed edge-emission band exhibits a strong LO-phonon coupling constant and as a result becomes broad band in shape. From the time-dependent energy-peak shift observed in the time-resolved spectra of the $B$ series at 4.2 K, the bound-to-bound reaction constant $W_{0}$ is calculated by the following relations [16],

$$
\ln W_{0}=4 E_{D} / E_{c \max }+\ln \left(1-E_{c \max } / E_{D}\right)-\ln t, \quad (4)
$$

where
$$W = W_0 \exp \left(-2r/a_e\right),$$

where $E_{c\text{max}}$ is the Coulomb energy found from the position of the emission peak at time $t$, $W$ is the transition probability, $r$ is a donor-acceptor pair distance, and $a_e$ is an electron orbit radius. Putting $E_{c\text{max}} =$ 0.06 eV at $5\ \mu\text{sec}$ and $E_D = 0.11$ eV in equation (4), $W_0$ is calculated to be $10^8\ \text{sec}^{-1}$, which is comparable to that for the edge emission in CdS $(4 \times 10^8\ \text{sec}^{-1})$ [17].

It is concluded that the edge-emission band appearing in cubic-ZnS:I crystals consists of the free-to-bound and distant donor-acceptor pair emission replicated by many LO-phonons; the latter indicates an electronic transition between a donor level at about 0.11 eV below the conduction band and an acceptor level at about 0.2 eV above the valence band. A simple argument concerning the bound-exciton emission analysis tentatively suggests the appearance of such a shallow-donor and deep-acceptor centre involved in the self-activated blue luminescence band where excitons are bound. We therefore believe the postulated effective mass ratio between the electrons and holes to be reasonable. Although the acceptor level of 0.2 eV seems to be distinctly shallower than the previous reported values, it is at present unknown whether or not the native acceptor-type defect should be taken into consideration as its chemical origin.
