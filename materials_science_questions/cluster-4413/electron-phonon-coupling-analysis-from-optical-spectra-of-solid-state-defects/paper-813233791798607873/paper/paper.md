## ABSTRACT
The electron-vibrational interaction (EVI) parameters such as Huang-Rhys factor, effective phonon energy and zero phonon line position were estimated using data from our recent reported work, on photoluminescence in rare earth doped complex hexafluorides phosphors $\text{LiMgBF}_6$, $\text{Li}_2\text{NaBF}_6$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{BF}_6$
Validity of results were established by modeling the emission line which was found to be in good agreement with experimental photoluminescence spectra.

## 1. Introduction
Ce activated compounds show excellent luminescent properties. A large number of Ce activated hexafluoride have been studied in past. $\text{LiCaAlF}_6$:Ce and $\text{LiSrAlF}_6$:Ce have been reported as leading candidate for tunable solid state laser in UV region [1]. $\text{LiCaAlF}_6$:Ce is a prominent luminescent material [2]. $\text{LiCaAlF}_6$:Ce was found to be excellent scintillators [3]. Recently we have reported Boro-fluoride $\text{LiMgBF}_6$:Ce and $\text{Li}_2\text{NaBF}_6$:Ce which are useful for scintillation application [4].

The lowest energy state of $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ ion has a single 4f electron outside closed shell. Spin orbit coupling causes a splitting into a $^2\text{F}_{5/2}$ ground state and a $^2\text{F}_{7/2}$ excited state separated by approximately $2000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Further, the ground state 5d configuration splits into 2 to 5 components due to crystal field [5]. Fig. 1 shows simplified energy level scheme of $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ ion. The emission spectrum of $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ consists of several unresolved emission band caused by transition from lowest 5d to different stark component of $^2\text{F}_{5/2}$ ground state and $^2\text{F}_{7/2}$ excited state. Since the spin orbit splitting of $^2\text{F}$ terms is significantly larger than stark splitting due to crystal field the Ce emission can be approximated by a doublet of two broad Gaussian shaped band separated typically by $2000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ [6]. The energy level scheme for $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ is shown in Fig. 1. Under the effect of crystal field $^2\text{F}_{5/2}$ ground state and $^2\text{F}_{7/2}$ excited state further splits into 3 and 4 levels respectively as indicated in Fig. 1 [7]. The position of 5d level depends strongly upon on crystalline environment. Thus $4\text{f}\rightarrow5\text{d}$ absorption and $5\text{d}\rightarrow4\text{f}$ emission depends upon the host and therefore it is important to analyze EVI and crystal field splitting parameters for 5d electron state of $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ in different hosts.

In present work we have estimated EVI parameters mainly Huang-Rhys factor, effective phonon energy and zero phonon line position for $\text{LiMgBF}_6$, $\text{Li}_2\text{NaBF}_6$ and $\text{Li}_3\text{BF}_6$ using the experimental data reported in reference [4,13].

Brik et al. have reported EVI in the 5d state of $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ ion in halosulphate phosphor [8]. Similarly, Bartwal et al. have reported electron-vibrational interaction in the $\text{Eu}^{2+}$ ion in 5d states of $\text{Mg}_x\text{Sr}_{1-x}\text{Al}_2\text{O}_4$:$\text{Eu}^{2+}$ [9]. ### 2. Theoretical outline

EVI chiefly depends upon type of host lattice. This model has been used to estimate EVI parameters in 5d state of $Ce^{3+}$ [8] and $Eu^{2+}$ [9]. It has been found that the model is successful in describing EVI parameters.

Shift in energy between emission and absorption band is known as stokes shift and it has magnitude $(2S - 1)\hslash\omega$.

$$
\Delta E_{S}=(2S - 1)\hslash\omega \tag{1}
$$

The constant $S$ is called Huang–Rhys coupling constant. It measures the strength of electron lattice coupling. $S < 1$ indicates weak coupling regime, $1 < S < 5$ indicates intermediate coupling regime and $S > 5$ indicates strong coupling regime [5]. $\hslash\omega$ is effective phonon energy. Another equation required to calculate $S$ and $\hslash\omega$ is

$$
\Gamma(T)=\sqrt{8\ln2}\hslash\omega\sqrt{S\coth\left(\frac{\hslash\omega}{2kT}\right)} \tag{2}
$$

Where $\Gamma(T)$ full width of half maxima (FWHM) of emission curve at absolute temperature T. The values of $\Delta E_{S}$ and $\Gamma(T)$ are obtained from experimental excitation and emission curve. The values of $S$ and $\hslash\omega$ are obtained by solving equations (1) and (2).

The validity of these values is checked by modeling the emission band shape and comparing it with experimental curve. The intensity of emission band at energy $E$ is given by expression:

$$
I=\frac{e^{-S}S^{m}}{m!}\left(1+S^{2}\frac{e^{\frac{-\hslash\omega}{kT}}}{m+1}\right) \tag{3}
$$

and

$$
m=\frac{\Delta E}{\hslash\omega}, \Delta E=E_{0}-E \tag{4}
$$

where $m$ is effective number of phonon involved in emission transition and $E_{0}$ is the zero phonon line position energy [12]. The point of intersection of emission and excitation curve indicates zero phonon line position.

Nazarov et al. has discussed this quantum mechanical description by means of single configurational coordinate model. This model utilizes the assumption that frequency of vibrational mode is same for both ground state and excited state as well as ground state parabola and excited state parabola have same curvature. Also anharmonicity and Jahn–Tellar interaction is neglected which may lead to complicated vibronic bands [10].

Puppalwar et al. have reported photoluminescence of Ce<sup>3+</sup> activated Li<sub>3</sub>BF<sub>6</sub> [13]. The excitation spectra of Li<sub>3</sub>BF<sub>6</sub>:Ce has prominent excitation peak at 241 nm and emission peak at 377 nm together with a shoulder at 422 nm. The emission of 377 nm is more intense than 422 nm. The phosphor is found to be emitting in UV region.

The room temperature excitation spectra of LiMgBF<sub>6</sub>:Ce, Li<sub>2</sub>NaBF<sub>6</sub>:Ce and Li<sub>3</sub>BF<sub>6</sub>:Ce are shown in Fig. 2 The experimental excitation band is decomposed into several individual Gaussians (marked with dashed lines in Fig. 2) and the lowest energy Gaussian band represents the lowest 5d state. The maxima of each of these Gaussian bands are marked on graphs in Fig. 2. The experimental emission spectra are shown in Fig. 3. FWHM for each of these graphs is noted. The intensity scales for both emission and excitation have been normalized to carry out comparative analysis of different compounds.

Fig. 4 shows emission and excitation spectra simultaneously, the position of point of intersection of emission and excitation curve indicates zero phonon line position. The extrapolated values are shown by dashed line in Fig. 4. Using the values of Stokes shift and FWHM of emission curve EVI parameters such as Huang–Rhys factor and effective phonon energy were obtained from equations (1) and equation (2). All of these values are shown in Table 1. It is observed that material analyzed in this work have large FWHM, large Stokes shift and large effective phonon energy.

In order to check the validity of obtained parameters the shape of emission spectra has been modeled using equations (3) and equation (4). One of the quantity $m$ which represents number of phonons is calculated using equation (4) and ZPL position. The modeled emission curve (shown with dotted line in Fig. 4) is found to match closely with experimental curve. This establishes the validity of estimated EVI parameters.

Doping of Ce3+ into these compounds requires charge compensation. There are two possible ways of charge compensation. One way is charge compensation by lithium vacancy. In Li<sub>2</sub>NaBF<sub>6</sub> and Li<sub>3</sub>BF<sub>6</sub> for each Ce ion substituting Na and Li two Li vacancies in host lattice compensates for extra charge of Ce ion. Similarly for LiMgBF<sub>6</sub> Ce3+ replaces Mg and one vacancy in lattice compensates for extra charge of Ce ion. Another possible could be O2- substituting one of the F ion near to Ce3+. The O2- may be introduced into lattice due to open air treatment during synthesis [14]. Table 2 compares the position of lowest excitation band shows shift toward lower energies from Li<sub>3</sub>BF<sub>6</sub> Li<sub>2</sub>NaBF<sub>6</sub> and LiMgBF<sub>6</sub>. Similarly stokes shift also shifts toward lower energy in same manner.

In this work the EVI parameters mainly Huang–Rhys factor, Effective phonon energy and Zero phonon line position were estimated for Ce<sup>3+</sup> doped complex hexafluoride phosphors namely LiMgBF<sub>6</sub>:Ce<sup>3+</sup>,Li<sub>2</sub>NaBF<sub>6</sub>:Ce<sup>3+</sup> and Li<sub>3</sub>BF<sub>6</sub>:Ce<sup>3+</sup> .The results are reported in Table 1. Making use of estimated values of Huang–Rhys factor, Effective phonon energy the shape of emission band was predicted and it was found to be in good agreement with experimental curve.
