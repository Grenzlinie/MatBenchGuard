# Diamond (001) Surface Reconstruction: Energy Gains and Dimer Bond Lengths via Tersoff Potential Minimization

## Problem background
Diamond surfaces exhibit structural reconstructions, notably the formation of dimers on the (001) face. Characterizing the energetics and geometry of these reconstructions is crucial for understanding diamond growth mechanisms at the atomic scale. This task addresses the energy minimization of the diamond (001) surface using the empirical Tersoff potential. We consider three candidate surface structures: the relaxed (1×1) slab, a symmetric (2×1) dimerized reconstruction, and an asymmetric (2×1)a variant with additional atomic displacements in deeper layers. The goal is to compute the energy gains and the dimer bond lengths for these reconstructions, thereby reproducing a set of benchmark simulation results.

## Crystal structure and slab construction
The diamond lattice has a cubic conventional cell with lattice constant a = 3.567 Å. The (001) surface is modelled by a slab consisting of 20 atomic layers; each layer contains 6×6 = 36 carbon atoms, giving a total of 720 atoms. The bottom four layers are kept fixed at bulk-terminated positions, while the atoms in the remaining 16 layers are allowed to move during energy minimization.

The surface coordinate system is defined as follows (see Fig. 2 of the source paper):
- x ∥ [1‾10] (i.e. along the [1‾10] crystallographic direction)
- y ∥ [110] (i.e. along the [110] direction)
- z ∥ [001] (normal to the surface, pointing outward)

The in‑plane order of the diamond (001) surface can be described by a two‑dimensional lattice with primitive vectors **a₁** = (a/√2)[1‾10] and **a₂** = (a/√2)[110]. Consecutive atomic layers along z are shifted relative to each other: even‑numbered layers (counting from the bottom) have atoms at positions  
(i d + 0, j d + 0)  
while odd‑numbered layers have atoms at  
(i d + d/2, j d + d/2)  
where d = a/√2 ≈ 2.522 Å, and i,j are integers ranging from 0 to 5 (for the 6×6 supercell). The interlayer spacing along [001] is a/4 ≈ 0.892 Å.  
The bulk‑terminated slab is generated by placing carbon atoms according to these rules, with the bottom four layers fixed at these bulk‑terminated positions.

## Structures to compute

1. **Bulk‑terminated and relaxed (1×1)**  
   Start from the ideal bulk‑terminated slab as defined above. First compute the total energy of the *unrelaxed* slab (with the bottom four layers fixed and the upper layers at bulk positions). Then perform an energy minimization (allowing the top 16 layers to relax) to obtain the **relaxed (1×1)** structure. Record the total energy of this relaxed slab.

2. **Symmetric (2×1) reconstruction**  
   In the topmost atomic layer, atoms are laterally displaced to form unbuckled dimers along the [110] direction (y‑axis). Pairs of adjacent surface atoms along y are moved closer together to a distance typical of a dimer bond (roughly 1.5 Å), thereby creating a (2×1) pattern of dimer rows. This configuration is then energy‑minimized using the Tersoff potential. Record the total energy and the relaxed atomic coordinates.

3. **Asymmetric (2×1)a reconstruction**  
   Starting from the symmetric (2×1) configuration just described, additionally pre‑move some atoms in the third layer that lie underneath the dimer rows. These third‑layer atoms are shifted laterally along the y‑direction ([110]) by a small amount (∼0.1–0.2 Å) to break symmetry, as suggested by the paper. After this initial displacement the structure is energy‑minimized, yielding the **asymmetric (2×1)a** reconstruction. Record the total energy and the relaxed atomic coordinates.

## Energy gains and dimer bond lengths
From the total energies obtained above, compute the following quantities:
- **Energy gain per surface atom for the (1×1) relaxation** = (E_unrelaxed_bulk − E_relaxed_1×1) / 36  
  (36 is the number of surface atoms).
- **Energy gain per dimer for the symmetric (2×1) reconstruction** = (E_relaxed_1×1 − E_sym_2×1) / 18  
  (18 is the number of dimers in the 6×6 supercell).
- **Energy gain per dimer for the asymmetric (2×1)a reconstruction** = (E_relaxed_1×1 − E_asym_2×1a) / 18.
- **Average dimer bond length** for both the symmetric and asymmetric structures, obtained by averaging the distances of all top‑layer dimer pairs in the relaxed XYZ files.

All energies must be expressed in electron‑volts (eV) and lengths in Å.

## Assets
- **LAMMPS**: Open‑source molecular dynamics package, available at https://lammps.sandia.gov (installable via conda or apt).  
- **Tersoff potential for carbon**: The parameter file `C.tersoff` is distributed with LAMMPS (parameterization from J. Tersoff, Phys. Rev. B **37**, 6991 (1988)). No other potentials or custom files are required.

## Workflow steps

### Step 1: Prepare initial configurations
- **Role**: process
- **Action**: Construct the three initial slab configurations in LAMMPS data format (720 atoms, periodic in x and y only, fixed bottom 4 layers). The configurations are:
  1. Bulk‑terminated (ideal positions).
  2. Symmetric (2×1) with top‑layer atoms pre‑displaced into dimer pairs along y.
  3. Asymmetric (2×1)a: like symmetric (2×1) plus a small lateral shift (along y) of the third‑layer atoms that sit under the dimer rows.
- **Evidence**: `/app/outputs/initial_structures.lmps` (one file containing the three configurations, or three separate LAMMPS data files).

### Step 2: Energy minimization for the symmetric (2×1) structure
- **Role**: scored (load‑bearing)
- **Action**: Perform energy minimization on the symmetric (2×1) initial configuration using the Tersoff potential. Save the relaxed atomic coordinates as **structure_2x1_sym.xyz** (XYZ format, 720 carbon atoms, coordinates in Å). Record the total energy after minimization.
- **Output file**: `/app/outputs/structure_2x1_sym.xyz`
- **Format**: txt (XYZ format: first line 720, second line comment, then lines “C x y z”).
- **Scoring**: scored by hidden verifier (shape check and recomputation of average dimer bond length).

### Step 3: Energy minimization for the asymmetric (2×1)a structure
- **Role**: scored (load‑bearing)
- **Action**: Perform energy minimization on the asymmetric (2×1)a initial configuration using the Tersoff potential. Save the relaxed atomic coordinates as **structure_2x1a_asym.xyz** (XYZ format, 720 atoms). Record the total energy after minimization.
- **Output file**: `/app/outputs/structure_2x1a_asym.xyz`
- **Format**: txt (XYZ format: first line 720, second line comment, then “C x y z”).
- **Scoring**: scored by hidden verifier (shape check and recomputation of average dimer bond length).

### Step 4: Energy minimization for the (1×1) slab
- **Role**: scored (load‑bearing)
- **Action**: Compute the total energy of the *unrelaxed* bulk‑terminated slab (bottom 4 layers fixed) as the reference bulk energy. Then energy‑minimize the slab to obtain the relaxed (1×1) structure. Save the relaxed coordinates as **structure_1x1_relaxed.xyz** (720 atoms) and record both the reference bulk energy and the minimized total energy.
- **Output file**: `/app/outputs/structure_1x1_relaxed.xyz`
- **Format**: txt (XYZ format, 720 atoms).
- **Scoring**: scored by hidden verifier (shape check).

### Step 5: Compute energy gains and dimer bond lengths
- **Role**: scored
- **Action**: From the recorded total energies, calculate:
  - energy_gain_1x1_per_surface_atom (eV)
  - energy_gain_2x1_per_dimer (eV)
  - energy_gain_2x1a_per_dimer (eV)
  From the two XYZ files, compute the average dimer bond length for the symmetric and asymmetric structures. Write all values into `/app/outputs/results.json` using the keys specified below.
- **Output file**: `/app/outputs/results.json`
- **Format**: json
- **Scoring**: scored by hidden verifier (energy gains are compared against benchmark values; internal consistency between total energies and gains is also checked).

## How you are scored
A hidden verifier inspects each artifact produced by your workflow. The scoring includes:

- **Shape audits** for the three XYZ files (correct number of atoms, parseable format).
- **Dimer bond length recomputation**: The verifier extracts the average dimer bond length directly from `structure_2x1_sym.xyz` and `structure_2x1a_asym.xyz` and compares them against the benchmark values from the paper. The bond length entries in `results.json` are not used for this check; they are recorded for reference only.
- **Energy gain verification**: The energy gains in `results.json` are compared against hidden benchmark values with predetermined tolerances. Additionally, the verifier checks that the reported gains are internally consistent with the total energies you provided (i.e., (E_1x1_relaxed − E_bulk_terminated)/36 ≈ energy_gain_1x1_per_surface_atom, etc.).

The final reward is a weighted sum of the individual checks. Performing the actual energy minimizations is essential—simply copying numbers will fail these checks. The verifier operates on the content of the files you write to `/app/outputs`; no other information is accessible to it.
