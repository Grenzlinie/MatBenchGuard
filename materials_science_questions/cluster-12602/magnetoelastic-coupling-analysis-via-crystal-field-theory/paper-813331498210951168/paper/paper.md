# Quadrupole Ordering and Rattling Motion of Clathrate Compound $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$

Genki ANO*, Mitsuhiro AKATSU, Koji ARAKI, Kazuo MATSUO, Yoshiaki TACHIKAWA, Keisuke MITSUMOTO, Takashi YAMAGUCHI, Yuichi NEMOTO, Terutaka GOTO, Naoya TAKEDA¹, Andreas DÖNNI², and Hideaki KITAZAWA²

Graduate School of Science and Technology, Niigata University, Niigata 950-2181, Japan
¹Faculty of Engineering, Niigata University, Niigata 950-2181, Japan
²National Institute for Materials Science, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0044, Japan

(Received March 11, 2011; accepted December 13, 2011; published online February 22, 2012)

We have investigated the quadrupole ordering of 4f-electrons and the rattling motion of guest rare-earth ions in the clathrate compound $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ using ultrasonic measurements. The elastic constants $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ and $C_{44}$ exhibit considerable softening at low temperatures. A minimum of $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ at $T_{\text{Q1}}=0.25$ K indicates antiferroquadrupole ordering associated with a $\Gamma_3$ doublet of a crystal-field ground state at the 8c site, while a minimum of $C_{44}$ at $T_{\text{Q2}}=60$ mK indicates the ferroquadrupole ordering of a $\Gamma_5$ triplet of a crystal-field ground state at the 4a site. The antiferroquadrupole phase depending on magnetic field direction shows a highly anisotropic behavior. The field-induced ordered phase due to the level crossing of the CEF ground state at the 8c site in magnetic fields along the [001] direction has been found. Furthermore, for the elastic constant $C_{44}$ and the attenuation coefficient $\alpha_{44}$ we found a frequency dependence at two different temperatures of 15 and 25 K, which we describe in terms of the Debye-type dispersion with an Arrhenius-type relaxation time $\tau=\tau_0\exp(E/k_{\text{B}}T)$ for two different activation energies of $E_{\text{L}}=102$ K and $E_{\text{H}}=198$ K. These ultrasonic dispersions are caused by the thermally activated rattling motions of guest rare-earth $\text{Pr}^{3+}$ ions at the 4a site of $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$.

KEYWORDS: quadrupole ordering, rattling, clathrate compound, $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$, elastic softening

## 1. Introduction

Rare-earth ternary compounds $R_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ ($R=$ La, Ce, Pr, Nd) with a clathrate structure are receiving much attention since the quadrupole ordering of 4f-electrons and the rattling motion of a guest rare-earth ion in an oversized cage have been reported.¹⁻⁴) The $\text{Cr}_{23}\text{C}_6$-type cubic structure of $R_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ with space group $Fm\overline{3}m$ (No. 225) has a primitive unit cell consisting of 12 $R$ ions, 80 Pd atoms, and 24 Ge atoms,⁵) where the rare-earth $R$ ions occupy the crystallographically nonequivalent 4a and 8c sites. The 4a site lies inside a cage built by 12 Pd atoms and 6 Ge atoms and possesses the site symmetry $O_h$, while the 8c site in a cage of 16 Pd atoms has the site symmetry $T_d$. The 4a sites form a face-centered cubic sublattice and the 8c sites form a simple cubic sublattice.

It was reported that the Ce-based compound $\text{Ce}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ possesses a $\Gamma_8$ quartet ground state for the crystalline electric field (CEF) level schemes at both sites 4a and 8c,⁶) and that the elastic constant $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ shows a considerable softening of 50% for temperature decreasing towards $T_{\text{Q}}=1.25$ K,²) where the onset of ferroquadrupole (FQ) ordering is accompanied by a spontaneous tetragonal lattice distortion. $\text{Ce}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ shows antiferromagnetic (AFM) ordering at $T_{\text{N}}=0.75$ K.⁷) The FQ transition occurs at the 8c site and the AFM transition at the 4a site. The isostructural compounds $\text{Ce}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Si}_6$ and $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Si}_6$ show the elastic softening of $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ and $C_{44}$, and antiferroquadrupole (AFQ) ordering at low temperatures with anisotropic magnetic phase diagrams.⁸⁻⁹)

The transverse ultrasonic $C_{44}$ mode of $\text{Ce}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ and $\text{La}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ shows a characteristic frequency dependence around 20 K in the elastic constant $C_{44}$ and the attenuation coefficient $\alpha_{44}$.²⁻³) This ultrasonic dispersion is caused by a thermally activated rattling motion of the guest rare-earth ions at the 4a site. The filled skutterudite $\text{PrOs}_4\text{Sb}_{12}$ with the cage structure also shows the ultrasonic dispersion due to the rattling.⁴) In order to verify the rattling in the clathrate compounds, spectroscopic measurements using Raman,¹⁰) neutron,¹¹⁻¹²) and x-ray¹³⁻¹⁴) methods have been carried out. Theoretical studies have also been conducted to explain the ultrasonic dispersion due to the rattling motions of local phonons being strongly coupled to low-lying optical phonons or conduction electrons.¹⁵⁻¹⁷) Although many investigations have been reported so far, the origin of the rattling in the clathrate compounds remains incompletely understood.

$\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ is an interesting clathrate compound showing the quadrupole ordering and the rattling motions. The ninefold degenerate $J=4$ multiplet of $\text{Pr}^{3+}$ ions is partly removed by CEF potentials at the 4a and 8c sites. Results of neutron scattering experiments on $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ revealed that a CEF ground state at the 8c site is a nonmagnetic $\Gamma_3$ doublet.⁶) The magnetic susceptibility of $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$, however, follows the Curie–Weiss law from 300 to 80 mK.¹⁸⁻¹⁹) Previous ultrasonic measurements on $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ showed a pronounced softening in the elastic constant $C_{44}$ down to 0.4 K, which is described in terms of the quadrupole susceptibility for the $\Gamma_5$ ground state system.²⁰) These experimental results imply that the magnetic $\Gamma_5$ triplet is a ground state for the CEF level scheme at the 4a site. The complete CEF level schemes at both sites 4a and 8c, and the nature of the quadrupole transitions in $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ still remain to be solved. The ultrasonic measurements of the transverse $C_{44}$ mode are of particularly importance to elucidate the rattling motions of the $\text{Pr}^{3+}$ ion at the 4a site.

In the present paper, we elucidate the CEF states at both sites 4a and 8c of $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ by means of temperature and

## 2. Experiment
A starting rod of ${\rm Pr_3Pd_{20}Ge_6}$ was prepared in a mono-arc furnace using stoichiometric nominal amounts of Pr with 5N purity (Ames Lab.), Pd with 3N, and Ge with 5N. Single crystals of ${\rm Pr_3Pd_{20}Ge_6}$ were grown using an electric furnace with dual ellipsoidal imaging mirrors. The light beam from a halogen lamp at a focal point is reflected by the ellipsoidal mirrors. The reflected beam focuses on a counter focal point, where the sample rod is mounted on a pulling shaft. The upper and lower shafts were rotated in opposite ways to agitate a zone-melted phase. The rod was slowly pulled down to grow a ${\rm Pr_3Pd_{20}Ge_6}$ single crystal. The crystal orientation was determined by the Laue x-ray method. A small amount of ${\rm Pr_3Pd_{20}Ge_6}$ material was powdered and measured by x-ray diffraction. Observed Bragg peaks could all be indexed with the expected ${\rm Cr_{23}C_6}$-type crystal structure. No additional peaks of an unwanted impurity phase were observed.

Ultrasonic measurements were carried out by the pulse-echo method. The ultrasonic wave velocity $v$ as a function of temperature and magnetic field was measured using the phase-comparison method based on mixer technologies. The attenuation coefficients of the successive ultrasonic echo signals were measured using a digital storage oscilloscope. Piezoelectric ${\rm LiNbO_3}$ plates of $36^\circ -Y$ cut and $X$ cut were used for the measurements of longitudinal and transverse waves, respectively. We adopted several types of ${\rm LiNbO_3}$ transducers with thicknesses of 200, 100, and $40\mu{\rm m}$. The ${\rm LiNbO_3}$ transducers were glued on plane parallel surfaces of the ${\rm Pr_3Pd_{20}Ge_6}$ sample using silicone bond materials. The elastic constant $C=\rho v^2$ for ${\rm Pr_3Pd_{20}Ge_6}$ was determined using the mass density of $\rho=10.29{\rm g/cm^3}$ for the lattice parameter of $a=1.2445{\rm nm}.^{21)}$

For low-temperature ultrasonic measurements, we used a handmade $^3$He-refrigerator down to 0.45 K and a top-loading dilution refrigerator (Oxford Kelvinox TLM) down to 20 mK. Magnetic fields were generated using superconducting magnets equipped to the refrigerators. We used a thermo-sensor of Cernox (Lake Shore) for detecting the specimen temperatures below room temperature down to 0.3 K and ${\rm RuO_2}$ tip resistances (Alps) down to 20 mK. We used an ac-resistance bridge of CRYO-CON model 32 (Cryogenic Control Systems) and that of AVS-47 (Picowatt) to accurately measure the low temperatures with the thermo-sensor resistances.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Temperature dependence of elastic constants
Figure 1 shows the temperature dependence of the elastic constants $C_{11}$, $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$, and $C_{44}$ of ${\rm Pr_3Pd_{20}Ge_6}$ at zero magnetic field. The elastic constant $C_{11}$ in Fig. 1(a) was measured by longitudinal ultrasonic waves with a frequency of 32 MHz. The elastic constants $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ in Fig. 1(b) and $C_{44}$ in Fig. 1(c) were measured by transverse waves with a frequency of 18 MHz. All the elastic constants shown in Fig. 1 exhibit a monotonic increase with decreasing temperature down to about 50 K and an elastic softening appears at low temperatures.

Between 6 and 0.3 K, we observe a softening of 3.7% for the longitudinal $C_{11}$ and a more pronounced softening of 20% for the transverse $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$. As displayed in the insets of Figs. 1(a) and 1(b), both $C_{11}$ and $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ increase again below a higher-temperature phase transition $T_{Q1}=0.25$ K and exhibit an anomaly at a lower-temperature phase transition $T_{Q2}=60$ mK.

Below 20 K, the transverse elastic constant $C_{44}$ shows an even larger softening of 29%. ### 3.2 Quadrupole susceptibility
The elastic softening in $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ and $C_{44}$, shown in Fig. 1, can be described in terms of the quadrupole susceptibility for CEF states. The elastic strain $\varepsilon_{\gamma\nu}$, which is injected by an ultrasonic pulse wave, couples to the electric

quadrupole $O_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}(j)$ with the same irreducible representation $\Gamma_{\gamma}$ at the $j$-th site. The quadrupole-strain interaction as a perturbation Hamiltonian for the system is written as $^{22)}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\mathrm{QS}}= & -\sum_{\Gamma_{\gamma}} g_{\Gamma} \sum_{j} O_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}(j) \varepsilon_{\Gamma_{\gamma}} \\
= & -g_{\Gamma_{3}} \sum_{j}\left[O_{u}(j) \varepsilon_{u}+O_{v}(j) \varepsilon_{v}\right] \\
& -g_{\Gamma_{5}} \sum_{j}\left[O_{y z}(j) \varepsilon_{y z}+O_{z x}(j) \varepsilon_{z x}+O_{x y}(j) \varepsilon_{x y}\right]. \quad(1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, we introduce the electric quadrupoles $O_{u}=(2 J_{z}^{2}-$ $J_{x}^{2}-J_{y}^{2}) / \sqrt{3}$ and $O_{v}=J_{x}^{2}-J_{y}^{2}$ with the $\Gamma_{3}$ representation and $O_{y z}=J_{y} J_{z}+J_{z} J_{y}, \ O_{z x}=J_{z} J_{x}+J_{x} J_{z}, \ $ and $O_{x y}=$ $J_{x} J_{y}+J_{y} J_{x}$ with the $\Gamma_{5}$. The counterpart of the symmetry elastic strains is defined as $\varepsilon_{u}=(2 \varepsilon_{z z}-\varepsilon_{x x}-\varepsilon_{y y}) / \sqrt{3}$ and $\varepsilon_{v}=\varepsilon_{x x}-\varepsilon_{y y}$ with the $\Gamma_{3}$ and $\varepsilon_{y z}, \varepsilon_{z x}$ , and as $\varepsilon_{x y}$ with the $\Gamma_{5}$. The summation of $\Sigma_{j}$ is over the $Pr^{3+}$ ions at the $j$-th site in unit volume and $g_{\Gamma}$ is a coupling constant for the quadrupole-strain interaction.

Because the intersite interaction of the electric quadru- poles among $Pr^{3+}$ ions affects the softening, we consider the intersite quadrupole interactions within the mean-field approximation as

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\mathrm{QQ}}= & -\sum_{\Gamma_{\gamma}} g_{\Gamma}^{\prime} \sum_{j}\left\langle O_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}\right\rangle O_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}(j) \\
= & -g_{\Gamma_{3}}^{\prime} \sum_{j}\left[\left\langle O_{u}\right\rangle O_{u}(j)+\left\langle O_{v}\right\rangle O_{v}(j)\right] \\
& -g_{\Gamma_{5}}^{\prime} \sum_{j}\left[\left\langle O_{y z}\right\rangle O_{y z}(j)+\left\langle O_{z x}\right\rangle O_{z x}(j)+\left\langle O_{x y}\right\rangle O_{x y}(j)\right]. \\
& (2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $g_{\Gamma}^{\prime}$ denotes a coupling constant and $\langle O_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}\rangle$ is the mean field of the quadrupole.

Taking into account the Hamiltonians of eqs. (1) and (2),we can describe the softening in the elastic constant $C_{\Gamma}$ as $^{22,23)}$

$$
C_{\Gamma}(T)=C_{\Gamma}^{0}-\frac{N g_{\Gamma}^{2} \chi_{\Gamma}(T)}{1-g_{\Gamma}^{\prime} \chi_{\Gamma}(T)}.\qquad(3)
$$

Here, $C_{\Gamma}^{0}$ describes the background and $N$ is the number of $Pr^{3+}$ ions in unit volume. In eq. (3), we introduce the quadrupole susceptibility $\chi_{\Gamma}(T)$ as

$$
-g_{\Gamma}^{2} \chi_{\Gamma}(T)=\left\langle\frac{\partial^{2} E_{i}}{\partial \varepsilon_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}^{2}}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}\left[\left\langle\left(\frac{\partial E_{i}}{\partial \varepsilon_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\partial E_{i}}{\partial \varepsilon_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}}\right\rangle^{2}\right]. \text { (4) }
$$

Here, $\langle A\rangle$ means the thermal average of $A$. The energy $E_{i}$ for the CEF eigenstate $|i\rangle$ in eq. (4) is calculated up to second-order perturbation for the infinitesimal elastic strain $\varepsilon_{\Gamma_{\gamma}}$. On the right-hand side of eq. (4), the first term, called the van Vleck term, is temperature-inde- pendent at low temperatures, whereas the second term, called the Curie term, is proportional to reciprocal temperature.

The CEF level schemes at the $4a$ and $8c$ sites in $Pr_{3} Pd_{20} Ge_{6}$ are different from each other. Furthermore, neutron diffraction experiments showed that, in the series of $R_{3} Pd_{20} X_{6}$ ( $X=Ge, Si$ ) systems, magnetic ordering at the $4a$ and $8c$ sites occurs independently at different temperatures. $^{24,25)}$ To calculate the elastic constants of $Pr_{3} Pd_{20} Ge_{6}$ with eq. (3), we have to take the sum of the contributions to the quadrupole susceptibilities from both sites with the correct occupation numbers, $N/3$ for the $4a$ site and $2N/3$ for the $8c$ site, and obtain the elastic constant $C_{\Gamma}(T)$ as

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\Gamma}(T)=C_{\Gamma}^{0} & -\frac{N}{3}\left[\frac{g_{\Gamma[4 \mathrm{a}]}^{2} \chi_{\Gamma[4 \mathrm{a}]}(T)}{1-g_{\Gamma[4 \mathrm{a}]}^{\prime} \chi_{\Gamma[4 \mathrm{a}]}(T)}\right] \\
& -\frac{2 N}{3}\left[\frac{g_{\Gamma[8 \mathrm{c}]}^{2} \chi_{\Gamma[8 \mathrm{c}]}(T)}{1-g_{\Gamma[8 \mathrm{c}]}^{\prime} \chi_{\Gamma[8 \mathrm{c}]}(T)}\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad(5)
$$

For the analysis of the experimentally observed softeningin Figs. 1(b) and 1(c) using eq. (5), note that $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$  is responsible for the $\Gamma_{3}$ -type electric quadrupole and $C_{44}$ isrelated to the $\Gamma_{5}$ -type one. The transverse $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$  mode propagating along $k \|[110]$ with a polarization vector $u \|[110]$ injects the elastic strain $\varepsilon_{v}=\varepsilon_{x x}-\varepsilon_{y y}$ , whichcouples to the electric quadrupole $O_{v}=J_{x}^{2}-J_{v}^{2}$ with the $\Gamma_{3}$ representation. The $C_{44}$ mode with $k \|[001]$ and $u \|[100]$  is responsible for the elastic strain $\varepsilon_{z x}$ , which is conjugateto the electric quadrupole $O_{z x}=J_{z} J_{x}+J_{x} J_{z}$ with the $\Gamma_{5}$  representation.

In order to analyze the elastic softening in Fig. 1, we needthe CEF levels for the $4a$ and $8c$ sites in $Pr_{3} Pd_{20} Ge_{6}$  described by the Hamiltonian as

$$H_{\mathrm{CEF}}=B_{4}\left(O_{4}^{0}+5 O_{4}^{4}\right)+B_{6}\left(O_{6}^{0}-21 O_{6}^{4}\right),\qquad(6)$$
 where $B_{l}$ and $O_{l}^{m}$ denote the CEF parameters and the Steven's operators, respectively. $^{26)}$ Neutron scattering and magnetization measurements on $Pr_{3} Pd_{20} Ge_{6}$ show the CEF level scheme $\Gamma_{5}(0 ~K)-\Gamma_{3}(5.4 ~K)-\Gamma_{4}(50 ~K)-\Gamma_{1}(113 ~K)$ atthe $4 a$ site for the CEF parameters $B_{4}=2.570 \times 10^{-2} ~K$  and $B_{6}=-3.860 \times 10^{-4} ~K$ , and the CEF level scheme $\Gamma_{3}(0 ~K)-\Gamma_{5}(6 ~K)-\Gamma_{4}(46 ~K)-\Gamma_{1}(111 ~K)$ at the $8 c$ site for $B_{4}=2.243 \times 10^{-2} ~K$ and $B_{6}=-4.363 \times 10^{-4} ~K^{6,27)}$ Uti lizing the CEF level schemes at the $4a$ and $8c$ sites, we calculate the quadrupole susceptibilities $-\chi_{\Gamma_{3}}$ for $O_{v}$ of $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ in Fig. 2(a) and $-\chi_{\Gamma_{5}}$ for $O_{z x}$ of $C_{44}$ in Fig. 2(b).

At low temperatures, $-\chi_{\Gamma_{3}[4 a]}$ for the $\Gamma_{5}$ triplet ground state at the $4 a$ site and $-\chi_{\Gamma_{3}[8 c]}$ for the $\Gamma_{3}$ doublet ground state at the $8c$ site are both dominated by the Curie term as shown in Fig. 2(a) by the dashed and dotted lines,respectively. In contrast, the quadrupole susceptibility $-\chi_{\Gamma_{5}}$  for $O_{z x}$ in Fig. 2(b) behaves in a different way depending onthe ground state at the $4a$ and $8c$ sites. $-\chi_{\Gamma_{5}[4 a]}$ for the $\Gamma_{5}$  triplet ground state at the $4a$ site exhibits the Curie-type softening as shown by the dashed line in Fig. 2(b), while -Xrs[sc] for the 8c site is dominated by the van Vleck term as shown by the dotted line in Fig. 2(b). The latter is due to the off-diagonal contributions of $O_{z x}$ from the $\Gamma_{3}$ ground state to the $\Gamma_{5}$ first excited state at $6 ~K$ .

A fit based on eq. (5) of the quadrupole susceptibilities of Fig. 2 is shown by solid lines in Figs. 1(b) and 1(c). The fit reproduces the experimentally observed softening of $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ and $C_{44}$ down to $0.8 ~K$ and provides the quadrupole-strain coupling constants $g_{\Gamma}$ and the intersite quadrupole coupling constants $g_{\Gamma}^{\prime}$ for $Pr_{3} Pd_{20} Ge_{6}$ . The fitting of $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ gives $|g_{\Gamma_{3}[4 a]}|=30 ~K, g_{\Gamma_{3}[4 a]}^{\prime}=0 ~K$ for the $4 a$ site and $|g_{\Gamma_{3}[8 c]}|=90 ~K, g_{\Gamma_{3}[8 c]}^{\prime}=-36 mK$ for the $8 c$ site. The fitting of $C_{44}$ gives $|g_{\Gamma_{5}[4 a]}|=31 ~K, g_{\Gamma_{5}[4 a]}^{\prime}=13 mK$ for the $4 a$ site and $|g_{\Gamma_{5}[8 c]}|=13 ~K, g_{\Gamma_{5}[8 c]}^{\prime}=-30 mK$ for the $8 c$ site. Because the direct product of the $\Gamma_3$ doublet is reduced to the sum of $\Gamma_3\otimes\Gamma_3=\Gamma_1\oplus\Gamma_2\oplus\Gamma_3$, the $\Gamma_3$ doublet ground state at the 8c site has the electric quadrupoles $O_u$ and $O_v$ with the $\Gamma_3$ representation and the magnetic octopole $T_{xyz}=\sqrt{15J_xJ_yJ_z}/6$ with the $\Gamma_2$. Here, the bar means sum of cyclic permutations on $x$, $y$, and $z$. The $\Gamma_3$ ground state at the 8c site, therefore, brings about the Curie-type softening in $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$, but leads to the van Vleck contribution in $C_{44}$ owing to quadrupole transition to excited states.

On the other hand, since the direct product of the $\Gamma_5$ triplet is reduced to the sum of $\Gamma_5\otimes\Gamma_5=\Gamma_1\oplus\Gamma_3\oplus\Gamma_4\oplus\Gamma_5$, the $\Gamma_5$ triplet for the 4a site possesses the magnetic dipoles $J_x$, $J_y$, and $J_z$ with the $\Gamma_4$ representation, and the electric quadrupoles $O_u$ and $O_v$ with the $\Gamma_3$, and $O_{yz}$, $O_{zx}$, and $O_{xy}$ with the $\Gamma_5$. The $\Gamma_5$ ground state at the 4a site, therefore, brings about the Curie term in the quadrupole susceptibility for both $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ and $C_{44}$ as well as in the magnetic susceptibility.

It is notable that neither the magnetic dipoles nor the $\Gamma_5$-type electric quadrupoles are expected for the $\Gamma_3$ doublet ground state at the 8c site. In the present experiments, at $T_{Q1}$ clear anomalies in $C_{11}$ and $(C_{11}-C_{12})/2$ have been observed, while little anomaly in both $C_{44}$ and magnetic susceptibility has been found. This result indicates that the phase transition at $T_{Q1}=0.25$ K is the quadrupole ordering related to the nonmagnetic doublet $\Gamma_3$ at the 8c site. $^{19)}$ Furthermore, the negative coupling constant $g_{\Gamma_3[8\text{c}]}'$ for the 8c site suggests that the transition at $T_{Q1}$ corresponds to the $\Gamma_3$-type AFQ ordering.

The phase transition at $T_{Q2}=60$ mK is responsible for the quadrupole ordering of the $\Gamma_5$ triplet ground state at the 4a site. The positive value of $g_{\Gamma_5[4\text{a}]}'$ suggests the $\Gamma_5$-type FQ ordering at $T_{Q2}$ at the 4a site. The ultrasonic attenuation in the $C_{44}$ mode around $T_{Q2}=60$ mK also indicates ultrasonic wave scatterings due to a ferroelastic domain associated with the FQ ordering below $T_{Q2}=60$ mK. Magnetic properties of the phase below $T_{Q2}$ have not yet been measured. Future magnetic susceptibility and neutron scattering experiments have to be awaited to elucidate the interplay of the magnetic dipole around the phase transition at $T_{Q2}$ in $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$.

$$

$$
\alpha_{44}(\omega)=\frac{\Delta C_{\mathrm{L}}}{2 \rho\left[v_{44}(\infty)\right]^{3}} \frac{\omega^{2} \tau_{\mathrm{L}}}{1+\omega^{2} \tau_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}}
$$

$$
+\frac{\Delta C_{\mathrm{H}}}{2 \rho\left[v_{44}(\infty)\right]^{3}} \frac{\omega^{2} \tau_{\mathrm{H}}}{1+\omega^{2} \tau_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}}. \tag{8}
$$

(7). $\omega$ is the angular frequency of the ultrasonic waves used in the experiments. It is worthwhile to compare the relaxation time due to the thermally activated rattling motions in the isostructural compounds La₃Pd₂₀Ge₆, Ce₃Pd₂₀Ge₆, and Nd₃Pd₂₀Ge₆, and the filled skutterudites LaOs₄Sb₁₂, PrOs₄Sb₁₂, NdOs₄Sb₁₂, and SmOs₄Sb₁₂ in the Arrhenius plot of Fig. 16. ⁴˙³²⁻³⁴⁾ The activation energies $E$ and the attempt times $\tau_{0}$ are both listed in Table II. For these systems, activation energies $E$ in the range of 100–200 K and attempt times $\tau_{0}$ of $5 \times 10^{- 11}$–$5 \times 10^{- 12}$ s are common features for the thermally activated rattling motions of the guest rare-earth ions in cages.

In the following, focusing on Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆, we discuss the observed attempt times $\tau_{0}$ and activation energies $E$. The characteristic energies $h/\tau_{0} = 1.1$ and 4.4 K in Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ and $h/\tau_{0} = 1.6$ K in Ce₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ are considerably smaller than $h/\tau_{0} = 24$ K in La₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ and $h/\tau_{0} = 19$ K in Nd₃Pd₂₀Ge₆. This result indicates that the coupling of the local phonon to the conduction electrons, which hybridize with the 4f¹ state of Ce³⁺ or 4f² state of Pr³⁺, may soften the frequencies of the local phonon, corresponding to the characteristic energy $h/\tau_{0}$. The theoretical study on the two-channel Kondo model by Kusunose et al.³⁵⁾ pointed out that the coupling of the local phonon to the heavy-fermion system results in a softening of the frequency of the local phonon. The small $h/\tau_{0}$ in Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ and Ce₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ is consistent with the theoretical work.

The activation energies $E$ of 102 and 198 K in Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ are comparable to $E = 197$ K in La₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ and $E = 120$ K in Nd₃Pd₂₀Ge₆. The relatively small activation energy $E = 70$ K in Ce₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ may be caused by the coupling of the local phonon to the heavy-fermion state with a strong cf mixing effect. The difference in the activation energy of Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ and Ce₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ may be caused by the cf mixing effect in Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ being relatively smaller than that in Ce₃Pd₂₀Ge₆.³⁶⁾

We comment on the reason why the ultrasonic dispersion in Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ is caused at the 4a site. In Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆, the cage at the 4a site is made up of Pd and Ge atoms with distances $d_{\text{Pr[4a]-Ge}} = 0.333$ nm and $d_{\text{Pr[4a]-Pd2}} = 0.307$ nm, and the other cage at the 8c site consists of Pd atoms with $d_{\text{Pr[8c]-Pd1}} = 0.287$ nm and $d_{\text{Pr[8c]-Pd2}} = 0.338$ nm.²¹⁾ Here, Pd1 and Pd2 denote Pd atoms located at the 32f and 48h sites, respectively. These indicate that the surrounding clearance of the guest Pr³⁺ ion in the cage at the 4a site is larger than that of the guest Pr³⁺ ion at the 8c site. Recently, it has been clarified that no sign of ultrasonic dispersion due to rattling motion is found in the $R_{3}\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Si}_{6}$ ($R =$ La, Ce, Pr, Nd) systems.⁸˙⁹⁾ Thus, the replacement of Si by Ge, constituting of the cage at the 4a site, leads to the appearance of ultrasonic dispersion in $R_{3}\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_{6}$ systems. Based on these results, we conclude that the ultrasonic dispersion is caused by the rattling motion at the 4a site.

Rattling is defined as the movement of the guest ion among stable states of the multiwell potential in an oversized cage by thermal activation.²˙³⁾ The ultrasonic dispersion due to the rattling motion is observed at two different temperatures 15 and 25 K, which indicates that the guest ion moves through in two different paths between the stable states. Double ultrasonic dispersion in different temperature regions has already been reported for NdOs₄Sb₁₂ and SmOs₄Sb₁₂.³³˙³⁴⁾ Therefore, in rattling systems, double ultrasonic dispersion seems to be a common feature.

In the series of $R_{3}\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_{6}$ systems, Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ is the first compound that was found to show ultrasonic dispersion due to thermally activated rattling motion in two different temperature regions. Our group is now conducting more precise ultrasonic experiments using high-frequency ultrasonic waves above 200 MHz to measure the possible ultrasonic dispersion in two different temperature regions for the compounds $R_{3}\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_{6}$ ($R =$ La, Ce, Nd).

## 4. Conclusions
In this paper, we report about low-temperature ultrasonic measurements on the clathrate compound Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆ to investigate the quadrupole ordering and rattling motion. The elastic constants $C_{11}$, ${({C_{11} - C_{12}})}/2$, and $C_{44}$ exhibit a low-temperature softening, which is described in terms of the quadrupole susceptibilities for the CEF levels with the ground state being the $\Gamma_{3}$ doublet at the 8c site and the $\Gamma_{5}$ triplet at the 4a site. ${({C_{11} - C_{12}})}/2$ shows anomalies at $T_{\text{Q1}} = 0.25$ K and $T_{\text{Q2}} = 60$ mK, while $C_{44}$ is insensitive at $T_{\text{Q1}}$ and has a sizable anomaly at $T_{\text{Q2}}$ only. This result suggests that the ordering at $T_{\text{Q1}}$ is caused by the $\Gamma_{3}$ ground state at the 8c site and the ordering at $T_{\text{Q2}}$ is due to the $\Gamma_{5}$ ground state at the 4a site.

The elastic softening in ${({C_{11} - C_{12}})}/2$ and $C_{44}$ is characterized by AFQ-type intersite interactions among the 8c sites of $g_{\Gamma_{3}{[{8c}]}}^{\prime} = {- 36}$ mK $< 0$, and FQ-type intersite interactions among the 4a sites of $g_{\Gamma_{5}{[{4a}]}}^{\prime} = 13$ mK $> 0$ K. This result infers that the $\Gamma_{3}$-type AFQ ordering occurs at the 8c site at $T_{\text{Q1}} = 0.25$ K, and the $\Gamma_{5}$-type FQ ordering occurs at the 4a site at $T_{\text{Q2}} = 60$ mK.

Ultrasonic measurements in magnetic fields along the three cubic high symmetry directions yield highly anisotropic magnetic phase diagrams for Pr₃Pd₂₀Ge₆. The AFQ phase II closes at critical fields $H_{c}$ between 1 and 2.5 T with magnetic anisotropy $H_{c{[{001}]}} > H_{c{[{110}]}} > H_{c{[{111}]}}$. The FQ ordering in phase III is expected to be accompanied by a spontaneous lattice distortion due to the cooperative Jahn-

Teller effect. Thermal expansion and x-ray measurements are required to confirm the FQ ordering in phase III in $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$. The field-induced ordered phase has been found in the fields along the [001] direction owing to the level crossing of the CEF ground state at the 8c site.

We have found ultrasonic dispersions in the $C_{44}$ mode around the two different temperatures 15 and 25 K, due to thermally activated rattling motions in $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$. There is a low-temperature region with an activation energy $E_{\text{L}} = 102$ K and an attempt time $\tau_{0\text{L}} = 4.2 \times 10^{-11}$ s, as well as a high-temperature region with $E_{\text{H}} = 198$ K and $\tau_{0\text{H}} = 1.1 \times 10^{-11}$ s. The rattling motions in the present compound $\text{Pr}_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ are comparable with the rattling in the isomorphic compounds $R_3\text{Pd}_{20}\text{Ge}_6$ ($R =$ La, Ce, Nd) and filled skutterudites.
