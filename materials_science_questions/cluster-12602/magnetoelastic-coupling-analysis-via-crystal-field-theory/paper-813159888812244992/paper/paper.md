## ABSTRACT
The field-induced entropy change and the magnetocaloric effect (MCE) of the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys Ni₂Mn₁₋ₓCuₓGa and Ni₂Mn₁₊ₓIn₁₋ₓ are studied theoretically on the basis of a phenomenological free energy. The free energy is expanded in powers of the mangetization $M$ and the tetragonal distortion $e_3$, which are the order parameters for the ferromagnetic and martensitic transitions, respectively. Through the magneto-structural interaction term in the free energy, magnetic fields can affect also $e_3$ to shift the martensitic first-order transition temperatures. This shift of the transition temperature results in large field-induced changes in the order parameters, which can be responsible for large MCEs. It is found that the resulting MCEs depend on the nature of the magneto-structural interaction. In Ni₂Mn₁₋ₓCuₓGa with the attractive magneto-structural interaction, magnetic fields cause the negative entropy change as in the normal MCE. In Ni₂Mn₁₊ₓIn₁₋ₓ with the repulsive interaction, on the other hand, magnetic fields can do the positive entropy change causing the inverse MCE. For both the alloy systems, the $x$ dependences of the refrigerant capacity (RC) are studied to find the compositions optimum for large MCEs. Applicability of the Maxwell relation to the system with the first-order transition is also studied by simulating the field-induced entropy changes on the basis of the Maxwell relation.

## 1. Introduction
Some ferromagnetic shape memory alloys with the Heusler-type structure exhibit both the ferromagnetic and martensitic transitions in each compound. Since the two phase transitions interfere with each other, those compounds demonstrate a variety of unusual magnetic and structural properties. In recent years, such exotic properties as well as their new functions have been investigated actively by many researchers. Also experiments on the magnetocaloric effect (MCE) have been performed for these alloy systems [1]. In fact, Krenke et al. [2]. found the inverse MCE in Ni–Mn–Sn alloys, where application of magnetic fields causes positive entropy changes in contrast to the negative one in the usual ferromagnets, while Liu et al. [3] found a giant MCE in some Ni–Mn–In-based alloys. At present, however, we have not yet succeeded in understanding the MCEs observed in the shape memory alloys, because the experimental analyses on the MCEs of the shape memory alloys have not yet been settled in addition to the insufficient understandings on the microscopic mechanism of the martensitic transition. The purpose of the present paper is to clarify the characteristics of the MCE of the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys from a theoretical standpoint.

Most phenomena characteristic of the shape memory alloys appear in or are caused by thermodynamically nonequilibrium states. For example, large magnetic-field-induced strains result from reorientations of martensitic variants by magnetic fields and the mixed phase of the marensitic and austenitic states often appears. Also the MCE in the shape memory alloys are affected by the existence of the nonequilibrium states. Such the nonequilibrium states are caused not only by supercooling or superheating of the system with the first order phase transition but also by various imperfections in the magneto-structural systems. In the present paper, however, we do not go into the nonequilibrium states, but confine ourselves to the study of the MCE in thermodynamically equilibrium states on the basis of the

The phase diagram which is spanned by the ferromagnetic austenite (Ferro-A) phase, ferromagnetic martensite (Ferro-M) phase, paramagnetic austenite (Para-A) phase, and paramagnetic martensite (Para-M) phase was first found for $\mathrm{Ni}_{2+x} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Ga}$ by Vasil'ev et al. [4] and Khovaylo et al. [5]. They also analyzed this phase diagram by using the phenomenologically introduced free energy, which was expanded in powers of the magnetization $\mathbf{M}$ as the order parameter of the ferromagnetic state and the strains as the order parameter of the martensite state. Kataoka et al. [6,7] proposed their free energy by emphasizing the role of the magneto-structural interaction given by the biquadratic term of $\mathbf{M}$ and the tetragonal strain $e_{3}$, and explained the observed phase diagrams of $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ and $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}$. As the result, it was proved that the magnetostructural transitions observed in $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ with $0.23<x<$ 0.30 are ascribed to an attractive magneto-structural interaction [6], while the reentrant ferromagnetism observed in $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x}$ $\mathrm{In}_{1-x}$ with $0.34<x<0.40$ is ascribed to a repulsive one [7]. In the present study, we are especially interested in the entropy, which has two different sources, i.e., the ferromagnetic transition and the martensitic transition. We will see how the two entropy changes by magnetic fields cooperate depending on the alloy systems. This will make clear the difference between the MCEs of the two alloy systems with the different magneto-structural interactions. We are also interested in the composition $x$ dependence of the MCE in both the alloy systems. The obtained result will help us to find compositions optimum for large MCEs.

Some authors of the MCEs already found a close relation between large MCEs and first-order magnetic phase transitions. In fact, the large MCEs are observed in $\mathrm{Gd}_{5} \mathrm{Si}_{2} \mathrm{Ge}_{2}$ [8] and $\mathrm{MnAs}_{1-x} \mathrm{Sb}_{x}$ [9], whose magnetic transitions are of the first order. The reason for this relation can be attributed mainly to the fact that the entropy of the magnetic system changes drastically and this large entropy changes are considerably affected by magnetic fields near the first-order phase transition temperatures. A similar phenomenon can be expected in the alloy systems interested herein, where the magnetic transitions are subjected to the first-order martensitic transitions through the magneto-structural interaction. In the present study, therefore, we are interested mainly in the MCE near the first-order magnetic and/or martensitic transition temperatures. As well known, the first-order transition (FOT) always accompanies the hysteresis in the magnetization processes, which makes the MCE feeble. We do not go into the study of the hysteresis effect on the MCE. Nonetheless, the MCE studied in the following will be able to clarify the origins of the anomalous behavior of the MCEs observed in the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys.

Experimentally, the field-induced entropy change has been analyzed by use of the Maxwell relation even for the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys exhibiting the FOT. Although those analyses succeeded in finding anomalously large MCEs in these alloys, the results of such the experimental analyses are found to behave quite differently from those calculated in the present theory. We, therefore, clarify the reason for the different behaviors of the entropy changes by simulating the analysis on the basis of the Maxwell relation.

In the next section, a phenomenological free energy in the presence of an external magnetic field is presented, which gives further the equilibrium conditions for the order parameters. By use of this free energy, the MCE is derived in Section 3. Along the derived MCE, the MCEs in $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ and $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}$ are investigated numerically in Sections 4 and 5, respectively. In Section 6, the conventional analyses of the entropy change by use of the Maxwell relation are simulated. Finally (in Section 7), discussion and conclusion are given.

## 2. Phenomenological free energy of the systems under external magnetic fields

In both the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x}$ $\mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ and $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}$, the order parameters to describe the observed phases are the martensitic distortion $e_{3}$ and the magnetization $M=|\mathbf{M}|$. As in the earlier studies [6,7], it is assumed that the magnetization $\mathbf{M}$ always directs to a crystal axis, say the $z$ axis, for the positive cubic anisotropy constant $K_{1}>0$. The martensitic distortion $e_{3}$ is defined by $e_{3}=\left(2 e_{z z}-e_{x x}-e_{y y}\right) / \sqrt{6}$ with the strain along the $i$ axis, $e_{i i}$, and $e_{3}>0$ represents an elongation along the $z$ axis as in both the alloys. It is further assumed that the external magnetic fields $H$ are applied to the crystal along the magnetic easy axis. Following the arguments made in [6] and [7], we expand the free energy in powers of $e_{3}$ and $M$ with taking into account the cubic crystal symmetry. For a phenomenological free energy per unit volume, we assume the following form as

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{tot}}= & F_{0}+\frac{1}{2} c_{2} e_{3}^{2}+\frac{1}{3} A_{3} e_{3}^{3}+\frac{1}{4} I_{4} e_{3}^{4}+\frac{1}{2} \chi^{-1} M^{2}+\frac{1}{4} J_{1} M^{4} \\
& +\frac{1}{2}\left(G_{2}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} G_{4}\right) e_{3}^{2} M^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(P_{3}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} P_{5}\right) e_{3}^{3} M^{2}+\frac{1}{2} Q_{1} e_{3}^{2} M^{4} \\
& -M H,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $c_{2}, A_{3}$ and $I_{4}$ are to be expressed in terms of various elastic constants, $\chi^{-1}$ and $J_{1}$ originate from the spin exchange energy, $G_{2}, G_{4}, P_{3}, P_{5}$, and $Q_{1}$ represent the magnitudes of various types of interaction between $e_{3}$ and $M$. The above $F_{\text {tot }}$ includes the Zeeman term $M H$, which affects not only the magnetic system but also the martensite system through the interactions between $e_{3}$ and $M$. The term $F_{0}$ is a free energy independent of $e_{3}$ and $M$, but dependent on temperature $T$. The term of $e_{3}^{2} M^{2}$ plays an important role in the MCE as well as in the phase diagram. In Eq. (1), the magnetostriction terms linear to $e_{3}$ and the volume change $e_{1}=\left(e_{x}+e_{y y}+e_{z z}\right) / \sqrt{3}$ were already neglected by the reasons given in Ref. [6]. Neglect of the linear terms will be considered in more detail later and in Appendix A.

The coefficients in the terms on the right hand side of Eq. (1) are regarded as constants independent of $T$ and concentration $x$ except for $c_{2}$ and $\chi^{-1}$. As usually, the simplest $x$ - and $T$-dependencies of $c_{2}$ and $\chi^{-1}$ are assumed as follows:

$$
c_{2}=\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}}{T_{\mathrm{M}}(1)}\left[T-T_{\mathrm{M}}(x)\right],
$$

$$
\chi^{-1}=\frac{c_{\mathrm{f}}}{T_{\mathrm{C}}(0)}\left[T-T_{\mathrm{C}}(x)\right],
$$

with

$$
T_{\mathrm{M}}(x)=T_{\mathrm{M}}(0)+\left[T_{\mathrm{M}}(1)-T_{\mathrm{M}}(0)\right] x,
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{C}}(x)=T_{\mathrm{C}}(0)+\left[T_{\mathrm{C}}(1)-T_{\mathrm{C}}(0)\right] x,
$$

where $c_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $c_{\mathrm{f}}$ are positive constants independent of $x$ and $T$, and $T_{\mathrm{M}}(x)$ and $T_{\mathrm{C}}(x)$ are fictitious (or sometimes real) second order transition temperatures of $e_{3}$ and $M$, respectively.

As in [6] and [7], we measure the various quantities in Eqs. (1)-(5) in their respective units as follows:

$$
t=T / T_{\mathrm{C}}(0),
$$

$$
t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)=T_{\mathrm{M}}(x) / T_{\mathrm{C}}(0)=t_{\mathrm{m}}(0)+\left[t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)-t_{\mathrm{m}}(0)\right] x,
$$

$$
t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)=T_{\mathrm{C}}(x) / T_{\mathrm{C}}(0)=1+\left[t_{\mathrm{c}}(1)-1\right] x,
$$

$$
\overline{e_{3}}=e_{3} / \sqrt{c_{\mathrm{m}} / I_{4}},
$$

$$
\bar{M}=M / \sqrt{c_{\mathrm{f}} / J_{1}},
$$

$$\bar{H}=H / c_{\mathrm{f}} \sqrt{c_{\mathrm{f}} / J_{1}},\qquad(11)$$

and
$$\bar{F}=F /\left(c_{\mathrm{f}}^{2} / J_{1}\right).\qquad(12)$$

Then, Eq. (1) is reduced to
$$\begin{aligned}
\bar{F}\left(\bar{e}_{3}, \bar{M}\right)= & \bar{F}_{0}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\left[t-t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)\right] \bar{M}^{2}+\frac{1}{4} \bar{M}^{4} \\
& +r\left\{\frac{1}{2 t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\left[t-t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)\right] \bar{e}_{3}^{2}+\frac{2}{3} a \bar{e}_{3}^{3}+\frac{1}{4} \bar{e}_{3}^{4}\right\} \\
& +\frac{1}{2} g \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \bar{M}^{2}+\frac{1}{3} p \bar{e}_{3}^{3} \bar{M}^{2}+\frac{1}{2} q \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \bar{M}^{4}-\bar{M} \bar{H},
\end{aligned}\qquad(13)$$
where the new coefficients were defined by
$$\begin{aligned}
& r=\left(\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}}{c_{\mathrm{f}}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{J_{1}}{I_{4}}\right), \quad a=\left(\frac{1}{2 \sqrt{c_{\mathrm{m}} I_{4}}}\right) A_{3}, \quad g=\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}}{c_{\mathrm{f}} I_{4}}\left(G_{2}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} G_{4}\right), \\
& p=\frac{1}{c_{\mathrm{f}}}\left(\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}} f}{I_{4}}\right)^{3 / 2}\left(P_{3}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} P_{5}\right) \quad \text { and } \quad q=\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}}{J_{1} I_{4}} Q_{1}.
\end{aligned}\qquad(14)$$

The anharmonic term $\bar{e}_{3}^{3}$ in Eq. (13) makes the martensitic transition become of the first order. The negative or positive sign of $g$ appearing in Eq. (13), respectively, gives the attraction or the repulsion between $\bar{e}_{3}^{2}$ and $\bar{M}^{2}$ .

Up to now, some authors have already presented the theories in which the terms linear to $e_{3}$ and/or $e_{1}$ play essential roles in a phase diagram [5] and an entropy change [10] of ferromagnetic shape memory alloys. These theories, therefore, do not agree with the present theory on the basis of the free energy (13) with no linear terms. The reason why the linear terms were neglected in our theory will be described in Appendix A.

The equilibrium order parameters $\bar{e}_{3}$ and $\bar{M}$ must be those which minimize $\bar{F}(\bar{e}_{3}, \bar{M})$ given by Eq. (13) at any values of $t$ and $\bar{H}$ ,so that the two satisfy the following equations:
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial \bar{F}\left(\bar{e}_{3}, \bar{M}\right)}{\partial \bar{M}}= & {\left[t-t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)\right] \bar{M}+\bar{M}^{3} } \\
& +g \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \bar{M}+(2 / 3) p \bar{e}_{3}^{3} \bar{M}+2 q \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \bar{M}^{3}-\bar{H}=0,
\end{aligned}\qquad(15)$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial \bar{F}\left(\bar{e}_{3}, \bar{M}\right)}{\partial \bar{e}_{3}}= & r\left\{\frac{1}{t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\left[t-t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)\right] \bar{e}_{3}+2 a \bar{e}_{3}^{2}+\bar{e}_{3}^{3}\right\} \\
& +g \bar{e}_{3} \bar{M}^{2}+p \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \bar{H}^{2}+q \bar{e}_{3} \bar{M}^{4}=0.
\end{aligned}\qquad(16)$$

## 3. Entropy change by magnetic fields and magnetocaloric effect
The total entropy per unit volume, $S_{tot }$ , is given by $S_{tot }=$  $-(\partial F_{tot } / \partial T)$ . Since $\bar{M}$ and $\bar{e}_{3}$ depend on $t$ , but should satisfy Eqs. (15) and (16), $S_{tot }$ is shown to have the form as
$$S_{\mathrm{tot}}=\left[c_{\mathrm{f}}^{2} / T_{\mathrm{C}}(0) J_{1}\right]\left(\bar{S}+\bar{S}_{0}\right),\qquad(17)$$

$$\bar{S}=\bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}+\bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}},\qquad(18)$$
where $\bar{S}_{f}$ and $\bar{S}_{m}$ are, respectively, the partial entropies depending on the ferromagnetic and martensitic orderings, while $\bar{S}_{0}$ is the entropy independent of the order parameters. Putting into mind Nernst's heat theorem in each of the ferromagnetic and martensi-tic systems, we can write the partial entropies as follows:
$$\bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}=-(1 / 2)\left(\bar{M}^{2}-\bar{M}_{0}^{2}\right),\qquad(19)$$

$$\bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}}=-\left[r / 2 t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)\right]\left(\bar{e}_{3}^{2}-\bar{e}_{30}^{2}\right),\qquad(20)$$

$$\bar{S}_{0}=\left(\frac{d \bar{F}_{0}(t)}{d t}\right)-\frac{1}{2} \bar{M}_{0}^{2}-\frac{r}{2 t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)} \bar{e}_{30}^{2}.\qquad(21)$$
where $\bar{M}_{0}$ and $\bar{e}_{30}$ are, respectively, the equilibrium magnetization and strain at absolute zero in the absence of magnetic fields. The terms of $\bar{M}_{0}^{2}$ and $\bar{e}_{30}^{2}$ in Eqs. (19)-(21) are not important for the MCE, but were taken into account in order to see the relative magnitudes of the partial entropies. It is noted here that $\bar{S}_{f}$ and $\bar{S}_{m}$ become constant independent of $t$ in the paramagnetic and austenite phases, respectively, while $\bar{S}_{0}$ should be 0 at absolute zero and depends on $t$ even above these transition temperatures to include all entropies independent of $\bar{H}$ .

When external magnetic fields are applied via an isothermal process, they affect the magnitudes of the equilibrium order para-meters, inducing the change in the entropy. From Eqs. (18)-(20), the field-induced entropy change at a fixed temperature $t, \Delta \bar{S}$ , is shown to be expressed by
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta \bar{S} & =\bar{S}_{\bar{H}}-\bar{S}_{\bar{H}=0} \\
& =\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}+\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}},
\end{aligned}\qquad(22)$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}} & =\bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}, \bar{H}}-\bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}, \bar{H}=0}, \\
& =-(1 / 2)\left(\bar{M}_{\bar{H}}^{2}-\bar{M}_{\bar{H}=0}^{2}\right)
\end{aligned}\qquad(23)$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}} & =\bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}, \bar{H}}-\bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}, \bar{H}=0}, \\
& =-\left[r / 2 t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)\right]\left(\bar{e}_{3, \bar{H}}^{2}-\bar{e}_{3, \bar{H}=0}^{2}\right),
\end{aligned}\qquad(24)$$
where $\bar{S}_{\bar{H}}$ etc. are the quantities under a magnetic field $\bar{H}$ .

The magnetic fields affect also the FOT temperature itself, so that the FOT temperature $T_{A}=T_{C}(0) t_{A}$ at $\bar{H}=0$ is shifted to $T_{B}=T_{C}(0) t_{B}$ at $\bar{H} \neq 0$ . Since the order parameters in the equili brium phases under $\bar{H}=0$ and $\bar{H} \neq 0$ drastically change between $T_{A}$ and $T_{B}$ , the MCE becomes extremely large between this temperature region, on which we concentrate our attention in the following. After the conventional study on the MCE [11], we define the refrigerant capacity, RC, as the index of the MCE strength, which is written as
$$\mathrm{RC}=\left(c_{\mathrm{f}}^{2} / J_{1}\right) \overline{\mathrm{RC}},\qquad(25)$$

$$\overline{\mathrm{RC}}=\int_{t_{\mathrm{B}}}^{t_{\mathrm{A}}} \Delta \bar{S} d t=\langle\Delta \bar{S}\rangle \Delta t_{\mathrm{ad}},\qquad(26)$$

$$\Delta t_{\mathrm{ad}}=\Delta T_{\mathrm{ad}} / T_{\mathrm{C}}(0)=t_{\mathrm{A}}-t_{\mathrm{B}},\qquad(27)$$
where $\langle\Delta \bar{S}\rangle$ is the mean value of $\Delta \bar{S}$ between $t_{A}$ and $t_{B}$ , and $\Delta T_{ad}$ is the adiabatic field-induced temperature change which actually corresponds to the field-induced shift of the FOT temperature. When $\Delta \bar{S}$ shows a trapezoid-like shape in the $\Delta \bar{S}-t$ plot as will be seen in Sections 4 and 5, we can have
$$\langle\Delta \bar{S}\rangle \simeq\left(\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{A}}+\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{B}}\right) / 2,\qquad(28)$$
where $\Delta \bar{S}_{A}$ and $\Delta \bar{S}_{B}$ are, respectively, the discontinuous changes of $\Delta \bar{S}$ at $t_{A}$ and $t_{B}$ in the $\Delta \bar{S}-t$ plot.

When both $\Delta t_{ad}$ and $\langle\Delta \bar{S}\rangle$ are negative, the system exhibits the normal MCE. When, on the other hand, both $\Delta t_{ad}$ and $\langle\Delta \bar{S}\rangle$ are positive, the system does the inverse MCE [1]. In the following Sections 4 and $5, \Delta \bar{S}$ and $\overline{RC}$ are calculated for $Ni_{2} Mn_{1-x} Cu_{x} Ga$ and $Ni_{2} Mn_{1+x} In_{1-x}$ , which will be found to be alloy systems exhibiting the normal and inverse MCEs, respectively.

## 4. Magnetocaloric effect in $Ni_{2} Mn_{1-x} Cu_{x} Ga$ 
The phase diagram of $Ni_{2} Mn_{1-x} Cu_{x} Ga$ [6] was already found to be very similar to that of $Ni_{2+x} Mn_{1-x} Ga$ [5], being spanned by the Ferro-M, Ferro-A, Para-M, and Para-A phases. The characteristic of these phase diagrams is the appearance of the magnetostructural transitions. We already analyzed this phase diagram on the basis of the free energy given by Eq. (13) under $\bar{H}=0$ . The values of the

unknown parameters in Eq. (13) were found by fitting the calculated phase diagram to the observed one. The observed phase boundaries between the Ferro-A and Para-A phases and between the Para-M and Para-A phases give

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_{\mathrm{c}}(1)=0.11, t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)=3.46 /\left[1+(8 / 9) a^{2}\right], \quad \text { and } \quad t_{\mathrm{m}}(0)=-3.68+t_{\mathrm{m}}(1) .
\end{aligned}
$$

With these constraints, other observed phase boundaries gave the parameter values as follows:

$$
r=0.324, \quad a=-0.706, \quad g=-0.547, \quad \text { and } \quad p=0.572, \quad (30)
$$

together with $q=0$. The ferromagnetic transition temperature of $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{MnGa}, T_{\mathrm{C}}(0)$, is known experimentally to be $373.0 \mathrm{~K}$ [6]. Then, $t$ in the numerical calculations can be translated into $T$ in the real unit, $\mathrm{K}$, through Eq. (6) while the other quantities $\bar{e}_{3}, \bar{M}, \bar{S}$, and $\overline{\mathrm{RC}}$ are remained to be dimensionless. It should be noted that $g$ is negative giving the attractive magneto-structural interaction. These parameter values are employed also in the following analyses of the MCE in this alloy system.

As an example, we consider the MCE of $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ with $x=0.26$, where the magnetostructural transition, i.e., the simultaneous transition of the ferromagnetic and martensitic states, occurs. Fig. 1 shows the calculated temperature dependences of $\bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}, \bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $\bar{S}$ at $\bar{H}=0$ and also those of the equilibrium $\bar{M}$ and $\bar{e}_{3}$ at $\bar{H}=0$ in its inset. As seen in the inset of this figure, this alloy exhibits the FOT at $T_{\mathrm{A}}=T_{\mathrm{C}}(0) t_{\mathrm{A}}$. As seen also in Fig. $1, \bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}}$ is considerably small compared with $\bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}$ at all temperatures. However, both $\bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}$ and $\bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}}$ contribute comparably to the discontinuous change at $T_{\mathrm{A}}$. This large entropy change is especially important for the large MCE, on which we concentrate our attention in the following considerations. Fig. 2 shows the calculated temperature dependence of $\bar{S}$ in the vicinity of $T_{\mathrm{A}}$ for some magnetic fields $\bar{H}$. As seen from this figure, the FOT still survives but the FOT temperature $T_{\mathrm{A}}$ at $\bar{H}=0$ is shifted upward to $T_{\mathrm{B}}\left(>T_{\mathrm{A}}\right)$ at $\bar{H} \neq 0$. In addition to the large shift of the FOT temperature, the magnetic field always slightly decrease the entropy. As the result, the magnetic field induces the entropy change $\Delta \bar{S}$ which is shown in Fig. 3. This figure shows that the entropy change $\Delta \bar{S}$ has large negative values only between $T_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{B}}$, and is always negative even in temperature regions other than that between $T_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{B}}$. The negative large entropy change given here originates from the fact that a magnetic field develops $\bar{M}$, which further develops $\bar{e}_{3}$ through the attractive magneto-structural interaction. Under the magnetic field, therefore, the martensite phase with a low entropy replaces the austenite phase with a high entropy in the absence of the magnetic field between $T_{\mathrm{A}}<T<T_{\mathrm{B}}$. This holds also for $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ with $x<0.23$ in which the martensitic transition occurs in the ferromagnetic state and for that with $x>0.30$ in which it does in the paramagnetic state. $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$, therefore, exhibits the normal MCE at any $x$.

## 6. Entropy change simulated by use of the Maxwell relation

Up to now, a large number of experiments on the MCE has been reported on the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ [13-16] and $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}$ [17-23]. In most of those reports, the observed data were analyzed by use of the Maxwell relation. However, applicability of the Maxwell relation to the magnetic system with the FOT have been often argued [24-29]. In fact, the MCE analyzed experimentally on the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys depends on authors and has not yet been settled. As demonstrated just below, the signs of $\Delta \bar{S}$ obtained in the preceding sections agree with those derived experimentally, but the calculated $\Delta \bar{S}$ and experimental $\Delta S$ behave quite differently. Especially, most of the entropy changes $\Delta S$ analyzed experimentally depend strongly on the magnetic field $H$ [13-22] in contrast to the calculated weak dependence as shown in Figs. 4 and 9. In the present theory, therefore, we do not go into a detailed quantitative comparison between the theory and experiments. Rather, we simulate the experimental analyses on the basis of the Maxwell relation with the aid of the phenomenological theory to clarify the reason for the insufficient agreement between the present theory and experiments.

In the experimental analyses, the field-induced entropy changes are estimated by substituting the measured temperature-dependent magnetization into the Maxwell relation, which is written as

$$
\Delta \bar{S}=\int_{0}^{\bar{H}}\left(\frac{\partial \bar{M}}{\partial t}\right)_{\bar{H}} d \bar{H}, \tag{33}
$$

in terms of the quantities in units defined in Sections 2 and 3. As will be proved in Appendix B, $\Delta \bar{S}$ derived from Eq. (33) contains both the entropy change of the ferromagnetic system $\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}$ and that of the martensitic system $\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{m}}$, so long as the ferromagnetic and martensitic systems interact with each other. In other words, $\Delta \bar{S}$ given by Eq. (33) corresponds to $\Delta \bar{S}$ itself given by Eqs. (22)-(24). Obviously, the relation (33) can hold for a phase with no FOT which causes discontinuous changes in thermodynamical quantities. Nevertheless, (33) has been extensively used in experimental analyses, because the discontinuity of the observed magnetization is usually smeared by various causes in real magnets.

In experimental analyses, the Maxwell relation is treated by replacing Eq. (33) by the approximate equation [11] as follows:

$$
\Delta \bar{S}=\sum_{i} \frac{1}{\Delta t_{i}}\left\{\bar{M}\left(t+\frac{\Delta t_{i}}{2}, \bar{H}_{i}\right)-\bar{M}\left(t-\frac{\Delta t_{i}}{2}, \bar{H}_{i}\right)\right\} \Delta \bar{H}_{i}, \tag{34}
$$

where $\Delta t_{i}$ is a sufficiently small temperature interval to find the temperature derivative of $\bar{M}$ and the summation is taken over $i$ in the region $0<\bar{H}_{i}<\bar{H}$. In the present simulations, we also employ Eq. (34), in which the magnetization calculated by use of Eqs. (15) and (16), instead of observed ones, is used to see the effect of the discontinuity in $\bar{M}$ vs. $t$ plot. Numerical calculations are done for $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}(x=0.26)$ with the same parameter values given by Eqs. (29) and (30) and $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}(x=0.37)$ with the same parameter values given by Eqs. (31) and (32). \end{aligned}\qquad(35)
$$

$$
\Delta \bar{S}=\frac{1}{\Delta t} \sum_{i}\left\{\bar{M}\left(t+\frac{\Delta t}{2}, \bar{H}_{i}\right)-\bar{M}\left(t-\frac{\Delta t}{2}, \bar{H}_{i}\right)\right\} \Delta \bar{H}_{i},
\qquad(36)
$$

where the temperature interval $\Delta t$ is again assumed to be sufficiently small and the summation is taken over $i$ within the region $0<\bar{H}_{i}<\bar{H}$. Eqs. (33) and (35) (or Eqs. (34) and (36)) extremely resemble to each other. However, the two equations are strongly different in the accuracy of the numerical calculations. In Eq. (34), the discontinuity (or discontinuity-like drastic change) in the magnetization was overlooked and various $t$-derivatives at different magnetic fields $\bar{H}_{i}$ in the vicinity of the FOT temperature accumulate numerical errors. In Eq. (36), on the other hand, the summation over $i$ can be safely taken so as to satisfy

$$
\sum_{i}\left\{\bar{M}\left(t+\frac{\Delta t}{2}, \bar{H}_{i}\right)-\bar{M}\left(t-\frac{\Delta t}{2}, \bar{H}_{i}\right)\right\} \Delta \bar{H}_{i} \propto \Delta t, \quad \Delta t \rightarrow 0,
\qquad(37)
$$

even if $\bar{M}$ has the discontinuity in the $\bar{M}$ vs. $\bar{H}$ plot. Again the simulations of $\Delta \bar{S}$ by use of Eq. (36) were made, where the calculated temperature dependence of $\bar{M}$, instead of the observed one, is substituted into Eq. (36). The calculated results verified numerically that Eq. (36) reproduces well the entropy changes $\Delta \bar{S}$ as shown in Figs. 3 and 8, in which $\Delta \bar{S}$ s have nonzero values at $t_{A}$ and $t_{B}$ even for $\bar{H} \to 0$ and show trapezoid-like shapes in the $\Delta \bar{S}$ vs. $t$ plots. To our knowledge, any experimental analysis by the use of Eq. (36) with observed magnetizations has not been reported until now.

### 7. Discussion and conclusion

The ferromagnetic shape memory alloys undergo the martensitic transition of the first order. Since the martensitic state couples to the ferromagnetic state, the martensitic transition accompanies also the first-order transition (FOT) in the magnetic system. External magnetic fields can affect the resultant magneto-structural transition, and change not only the order parameters but also the FOT temperature itself. As the result, magnetic fields can cause large changes in the entropy in the vicinity of the FOT temperature. The present study clarified also that the field-induced entropy change contains the contribution from the martensitic system comparable to that from the ferromagnetic system and its sign is dominated by the properties of the magneto-structural interaction. For quantitative analyses of the MCE in the ferromagnetic shape memory alloys, therefore, we must go into detail of the microscopic origin of the martensitic transition and its coupling to the ferromagnetic state in these alloys. Some authors [30-32] performed the first-principles calculation of the total energy including the band-electron and lattice energies in some ferromagnetic shape memory alloys to find that the energy of the martensite state can be lowered than that of the austenite state. According to their calculated results, the d-bands near the Fermi level play an important role in lowering the total energy, as in the band Jahn-Teller effect. In this mechanism, the band electrons which are itinerant rather than localized in the crystal are responsible for the entropy of the martensitic system [33,34]. Quite differently from this mechanism, other authors proposed a model of the martensitic transition, where strain variables localized at lattice sites are introduced and are expressed in terms of localized pseudo spins [35]. This model could reproduce the martensitic FOT by assuming a sufficiently strong interaction between the pseudo spins. On the basis of this model, Buchelnikov et al. [36] and Sokolovskiy et al. [37] made the Monte Carlo simulations of the ferromagnetic and martensitic transitions on $\mathrm{Ni}_{2+x} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Ga}$ and $\mathrm{Ni}-\mathrm{Mn}-\mathrm{In}-\mathrm{Y}$, respectively, and obtained the calculated field-induced entropy change compatible with the results of their experimental analyses by use of the Maxwell relation. In the latter mechanism of the martensitic transition, however, the introduced localized strain variables in the martensitic state have not been investigated experimentally. Moreover, the degree of the freedom belonging to the strain variable, which is related to the entropy of the martensitic system, is still of an assumption.

We focused our attention mainly on the large MCE accompanied by the FOT, which originates from the fact that a large discontinuous change in the entropy by the FOT can be affected by external magnetic fields. As well known, however, the FOT causes various hysteresis phenomena. In the vicinity of the FOT temperature, the phase equilibrium above the FOT temperature mixes with that below the temperature, or, in other words, the nonequilibrium phase is realized by superheating or supercooling. For such the nonequilibrium phase, the Maxwell relation does not hold. Moreover, distributions of martensitic variants and magnetic domains give rise to another difficulty in estimating the effect of the magnetic field. These prove that the measurement of the magnetization is insufficient for the estimation of the field-induced entropy change. This situation cannot be altered even if the experimental analyses are made by use of the Clausius-Clapeyron equation. It should be noted, however, that the large part of the field-induced entropy change comes from the difference between the entropies of the phases above and below the FOT temperature. (See Figs. 2 and 7.) This suggests that the change in the distribution of the two phases by the magnetic field can dominate the field-induced entropy change in the vicinity of the FOT temperature.

In conclusion, the MCEs in the ferromagnetic shape memory alloy systems $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ and $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}$ were studied by use of the phenomenological Landau free energy, which had been already confirmed to produce their observed phase diagrams. It was found that both the alloy systems can exhibit large entropy changes by magnetic fields, which originate from the field-induced shift of the martensitic transition temperature of the first-order. The signs of these large entropy changes were proved to be negative for the alloy system with the attractive magnetostructural interaction, $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$, while positive for that with the repulsive one, $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}$, as observed. The refrigerant capacity, RC, was calculated for both the alloy systems to find that the maximum RC of the alloys $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1-x} \mathrm{Cu}_{x} \mathrm{Ga}$ appears in alloys exhibiting the magnetostructural transitions, while that of the maximum RC of the alloys $\mathrm{Ni}_{2} \mathrm{Mn}_{1+x} \mathrm{In}_{1-x}$ appears in alloys exhibiting the reentrant ferromagnetism. It was also demonstrated that a careful treatment of the Maxwell relation is necessary to avoid an artifact in experimental analyses of the field-induced entropy change by using measured magnetizations.

### Appendix A. Smallness of the magnitudes of the terms linear to the strains

In this Appendix, the order estimations of the terms linear to the volume strain $e_{1}$ and the tetragonal strain $e_{3}$ are made in order to see that those linear terms are too small to dominate the interaction between the ferromagnetic and martensitic systems.

We start with the new total free energy $F_{\text {tot }}^{\prime}$ including the linear terms as
$$
F_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\prime}=F_{\mathrm{tot}}+\frac{1}{2} c_{1} e_{1}^{2}+B_{1} e_{1} M^{2}+B_{2} e_{3} M^{2}, \tag{A.1}
$$
where $F_{\text {tot }}$ is given by Eq. (1), $c_{1}$ is the bulk modulus, and $B_{1}$ and $B_{2}$ are the linear-coupling constants. By using the same units as in Eqs. (9) and (12), the dimensionless quantities are defined by
$$
\overline{e}_{1}=e_{1} / \sqrt{c_{\mathrm{m}} / I_{4}} \quad \text { and } \quad \overline{F}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\prime}=F_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\prime} /\left(c_{\mathrm{f}}^{2} / J_{1}\right). \tag{A.2}
$$

Then, Eq. (A.1) under no magnetic field is rewritten as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{F}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\prime}= & \overline{F}_{0}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\left[t-t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)\right] \overline{M}^{2}+\frac{1}{4} \overline{M}^{4} \\
& +r\left\{\frac{1}{2} \overline{c}_{1} \overline{e}_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2 t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\left[t-t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)\right] \overline{e}_{3}^{2}+\frac{2}{3} a \overline{e}_{3}^{3}+\frac{1}{4} \overline{e}_{3}^{4}\right\} \\
& +b_{1} \overline{e}_{1} \overline{M}^{2}+b_{2} \overline{e}_{3} \overline{M}^{2}+\frac{1}{2} g \overline{e}_{3}^{2} \overline{M}^{2}, \tag{A.3}
\end{aligned}
$$
where
$$
\overline{c}_{1}=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{\mathrm{m}}}, \quad b_{1}=\frac{1}{c_{\mathrm{f}}} \sqrt{\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}}{I_{4}}} B_{1}, \quad \text { and } \quad b_{2}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} \frac{1}{c_{\mathrm{f}}} \sqrt{\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}}{I_{4}}} B_{2}, \tag{A.4}
$$
and the unimportant terms of $p$ and $q$ in Eq. (13) have been already neglected.

In order to estimate the magnitudes of $b_{1}$ and $b_{2}$, we pay attention to the equilibrium strains in the absence of the interaction term quadratic to $\overline{e}_{3},(1 / 2) g \overline{e}_{3}^{2} \overline{M}^{2}$. The equilibrium conditions for the Ferro-A phase labeled here III,
$$
\frac{\partial \overline{F}^{\prime}}{\partial \overline{M}}=0 \quad \text { and } \quad \frac{\partial \overline{F}^{\prime}}{\partial \overline{e}_{1}}=\frac{\partial \overline{F}^{\prime}}{\partial \overline{e}_{3}}=0, \tag{A.5}
$$
determine the magnetostrictions in the phase III, $\overline{e}_{1, \mathrm{III}}$ and $\overline{e}_{3, \mathrm{III}}$, at a temperature $t$ satisfying $t_{\mathrm{m}}(x) \ll t<t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)$, which are

shown to satisfy the relations as

$$
\frac{b_{1}}{r} \simeq-\left[\frac{\bar{c}_{1}}{t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)-t}\right] \bar{e}_{1, \mathrm{III}},\qquad(A.6)
$$

$$
\frac{b_{2}}{r} \simeq-\frac{1}{t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\left[\frac{t-t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)}{t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)-t}\right] \bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{III}}.\qquad(A.7)
$$

On the other hand, the equilibrium conditions for the Para-M phase labeled here II,

$$
\overline{M}=0 \quad \text { and } \quad \frac{\partial \overline{F}^{\prime}}{\partial \bar{e}_{3}}=0,\qquad(A.8)
$$

determine the martensitic distortion in the phase II, $\bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{II}}$, at a temperature $t$ satisfying $t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)<t<t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)$, which is shown to be given by

$$
\bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{II}}=-\left\{a+\operatorname{sgn}(a) \sqrt{a^{2}+\left[t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)-t\right] / t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\right\}.\qquad(A.9)
$$

At low temperatures, $\bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{II}}$ well develops to become

$$
\bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{II}} \simeq-\operatorname{sgn}(a) \sqrt{t_{\mathrm{m}}(x) / t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)},\qquad(A.10)
$$

for $|a| \ll \sqrt{t_{\mathrm{m}}(x) / t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}$. This gives

$$
O\left(\bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{II}}\right)=O\left(10^{0}\right),\qquad(A.11)
$$

where $O(\alpha)$ denotes $n$ when $\alpha \simeq 10^{n}$ with any integer $n$. Also when $O(2 a)=O\left(10^{0}\right)$, Eq. (A.11) holds up to the FOT temperature. From Eqs. (A.11), (A.2) and (9), we can find the relations as

$$
O\left(\bar{e}_{1, \mathrm{III}}\right)=O\left(\frac{e_{1, \mathrm{III}}}{e_{3, \mathrm{II}}}\right) \quad \text { and } \quad O\left(\bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{III}}\right)=O\left(\frac{e_{3, \mathrm{III}}}{e_{3, \mathrm{II}}}\right).\qquad(A.12)
$$

As for $\bar{c}_{1}$ in Eq. (A.6), we estimate $O(\bar{c}_{1})$ as follows. In a cubic crystal, we have

$$
c_{1}=c_{11}+2 c_{12} \quad \text { and } \quad c_{2}=c_{11}-c_{12},\qquad(A.13)
$$

in terms of the elastic stiffness constants $c_{i j}$. Eqs. (2) and (A.13) imply

$$
O\left(\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}}{c_{11,0}-c_{12,0}}\right)=O\left(10^{0}\right),\qquad(A.14)
$$

where the subscripts 0 mean the quantities in the absence of the martensitic transition. This equation and Eqs. (A.4) and (A.13) give

$$
O\left(\bar{c}_{1}\right)=O\left(\frac{c_{11,0}+2 c_{12,0}}{c_{11,0}-c_{12,0}}\right).\qquad(A.15)
$$

When some elastic stiffness constants observed for some metals are employed, we can have

$$
O\left(\bar{c}_{1}\right) \simeq O\left(10^{1}\right).\qquad(A.16)
$$

Furthermore, a temperature $t$ in Eqs. (A.6) and (A.7) is chosen so that

$$
O\left(t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)-t\right)=O\left(10^{0}\right) \quad \text { and } \quad O\left(\frac{1}{t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\left[\frac{t-t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)}{t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)-t}\right]\right)=O\left(10^{0}\right),\quad(A.17)
$$

for which the magnetization well develops in the Ferro-A phase. In the usual ferromagnets with well developed magnetizations, magnetostrictions are known to have orders as

$$
O\left(e_{1, \mathrm{III}}\right), O\left(e_{3, \mathrm{III}}\right) \simeq O\left(10^{-4} \sim 10^{-6}\right),\qquad(A.18)
$$

On the other hand, martensitic distortions observed in the Para-M phase of the Heusler-type shape memory alloys have the order as

$$
O\left(e_{3, \mathrm{II}}\right) \simeq O\left(10^{-1}\right).\qquad(A.19)
$$

Substituting Eq. (A.12) and (A.16)-(A.19) into Eqs. (A.6) and (A.7), we arrive at

$$
O\left(b_{1} / r\right)=O\left(10^{-2} \sim 10^{-4}\right) \quad \text { and } \quad O\left(b_{2} / r\right)=O\left(10^{-3} \sim 10^{-5}\right).\qquad(A.20)
$$

When Eqs. (A.20) are taken into account in Eq. (A.3) with $g=0$, the linear terms $b_{1} \bar{e}_{1} \overline{M}^{2}$ and $b_{2} \bar{e}_{3} \overline{M}^{2}$ are proved to be very small, so that the former cannot be responsible for an effective interaction between the ferromagnetic and martensitic systems even with the aid of some higher order terms of $\bar{e}_{1}$ and $\bar{e}_{3}$ and the latter hardly affect the martensitic system in contradiction to the observed large interactions between the two systems. In fact, the phenomenological theory with such the small linear term $b_{2} \bar{e}_{3} \overline{M}^{2}$ as the dominant interaction between the two is unable to produce the observed phase diagram [6]. Nevertheless, the observed phase diagrams were successfully reproduced by taking into account the quadratic term $(1 / 2) g \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \overline{M}^{2}$ instead of the linear term [6,7]. The parameter values determined by fitting the phenomenologically predicted phase boundaries to the observed ones were already given by Eqs. (30) and (32). Comparisons between the values of $g$ in Eqs. (30) and (32) and those of $b_{1}$ and $b_{2}$ in Eq. (A.20) with the values of $r$ in Eqs. (30) and (32) lastly show that the linear terms can be neglected for the interaction between the ferromagnetic and martensitic systems.

Finally, we give a comment on the result that the lower-order term of $e_{3}, b_{2} \bar{e}_{3} \overline{M}^{2}$ , is much smaller than that of the higher-order term, $(1 / 2) g \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \overline{M}^{2}$ , in the series expansion of the free energy. It is well known that the anisotropic magnetostriction $e_{3}$ in usual ferromagnets is caused by the perturbation energy of the spin-orbit coupling and the energy of the dipole-dipole interaction, and the isotropic magnetostriction $e_{1}$ is caused by a change of the spin-exchange energy. These energies were already proved to be very small compared with the energies of the magnetic and lattice systems. In the present ferromagnetic-martensite system, however, there may exist quite different origins of the magnetostructural interaction. If, for example, the martensitic transition is caused by band-electrons as in the band Jahn-Teller effect, the martensitic distortion and the magnetization can interact strongly each other through the band-electrons, since the magnetization also is caused, partly at least, by the band electrons. If the quadratic term $(1 / 2) g e_{3}^{2} M^{2}$ reflects such the interaction, the values of $g$ can be expected to become large as those in Eqs. (30) and (32). This consideration suggests that terms in the free energy has different origins, and magnitudes of those terms do not always decrease with increasing the order of the order parameter in the series expansion of the free energy. Estimations of their true magnitudes must be postponed until the microscopic origin of the martensitic distortion will be clarified.

## Appendix B. Entropy derived from the Maxwell relation

It is proved here that the entropy change derived from Eq. (33) contains both the entropies of the ferromagnetic and martensitic systems and becomes the same as the entropy change $\Delta \bar{S}$ given by Eqs. (22)-(24).

First we consider the case where the FOT does not occur when the external magnetic field is increased from 0 to $\bar{H}$ at a fixed temperature $t$. Differentiating Eqs. (15) and (16) with respect to $t$ and $\bar{H}$, we obtain the following derivatives as

$$
\left(\frac{\partial \overline{M}}{\partial t}\right)_{\bar{H}}=\left\{-D_{22} \overline{M}+\left[r / t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)\right] D_{12} \bar{e}_{3}\right\} /\left(D_{11} D_{22}-D_{12}^{2}\right),\qquad(B.1)
$$

$$
\left(\frac{\partial \bar{e}_{3}}{\partial t}\right)_{\bar{H}}=\left\{D_{12} \overline{M}-\left[r / t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)\right] D_{11} \bar{e}_{3}\right\} /\left(D_{11} D_{22}-D_{12}^{2}\right),\qquad(B.2)
$$

$$
\left(\frac{\partial \overline{M}}{\partial \bar{H}}\right)_{t}=D_{22} /\left(D_{11} D_{22}-D_{12}^{2}\right),\qquad(B.3)
$$

$$
\left(\frac{\partial \bar{e}_{3}}{\partial \bar{H}}\right)_{t}=-D_{12} /\left(D_{11} D_{22}-D_{12}^{2}\right), \tag{B.4}
$$

where
$$
D_{11}=\left[t-t_{\mathrm{c}}(x)\right]+3 \bar{M}^{2}+g \bar{e}_{3}^{2}+(2 / 3) p \bar{e}_{3}^{3}+6 q \bar{e}_{3}^{2} \bar{M}^{2}, \tag{B.5}
$$

$$
D_{12}=2\left(g+p \bar{e}_{3}+2 q \bar{M}^{2}\right) \bar{e}_{3} \bar{M}, \tag{B.6}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{22}= & {\left[r / t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)\right]\left[\boldsymbol{t}-t_{\mathrm{m}}(x)\right]+4 a r \bar{e}_{3}+3 r \bar{e}_{3}^{2} } \\
& +g \bar{M}^{2}+2 p \bar{e}_{3} \bar{M}^{2}+q \bar{M}^{4}.
\end{aligned} \tag{B.7}
$$

By use of Eqs. (B.1) and (B.3), Eq. (33) is calculated as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \bar{S} & =\int_{\overline{M}(\bar{H}=0)}^{\bar{M}(\bar{H})}\left(\frac{\partial \bar{M}}{\partial t}\right)_{\bar{H}}\left(\frac{\partial \bar{H}}{\partial \overline{M}}\right)_{t} d \overline{M} \\
& =-\int_{\overline{M}(\bar{H}=0)}^{\bar{M}(\bar{H})} \overline{M} d \overline{M}+\left[\frac{r}{t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\right] \int_{\overline{M}(\bar{H}=0)}^{\bar{M}(\bar{H})}\left(\frac{D_{12}}{D_{22}}\right) \bar{e}_{3} d \overline{M}.
\end{aligned} \tag{B.8}
$$

With the use of Eqs. (B.3) and (B.4), on the other hand, we can have
$$
d \bar{M}=\left(\frac{\partial \bar{M}}{\partial \bar{H}}\right)_{t}\left(\frac{\partial \bar{H}}{\partial \bar{e}_{3}}\right)_{t} d \bar{e}_{3}=-\left(\frac{D_{22}}{D_{12}}\right) d \bar{e}_{3}. \tag{B.9}
$$

By substituting Eq. (B.9) into the second term in Eq. (B.8), the integrations in Eq. (B.8) can be done to result in
$$
\Delta \bar{S}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\bar{M}_{\bar{H}}^{2}-\bar{M}_{\bar{H}=0}^{2}\right]-\frac{r}{2 t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\left[\bar{e}_{3, \bar{H}}^{2}-\bar{e}_{3, \bar{H}=0}^{2}\right]. \tag{B.10}
$$

Second, we consider the case where the FOT occurs at a critical magnetic field $\bar{H}_{c}$ when the external magnetic field is increased from 0 to $\bar{H}$ at a fixed temperature $t$. By applying the Maxwell relation (33) to each of the phase I below $\bar{H}_{c}$ and the phase II above $\bar{H}_{c}$ and by adding another entropy change $\delta \bar{S}_{\bar{H}_{c}}$ caused by the latent heat transfer, the entropy change in this case can be written as
$$
\Delta \bar{S}=\int_{0}^{\bar{H}_{c}}\left(\frac{\partial \bar{M}}{\partial t}\right)_{\bar{H}} d \bar{H}+\int_{\bar{H}_{c}}^{\bar{H}}\left(\frac{\partial \bar{M}}{\partial t}\right)_{\bar{H}} d \bar{H}+\delta \bar{S}_{\bar{H}_{c}}, \tag{B.11}
$$

$$
\delta \bar{S}_{\bar{H}_{c}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left[\bar{M}_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{II}}^{2}+\frac{r}{t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)} \bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{II}}^{2}\right]-\left[\bar{M}_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{I}}^{2}+\frac{r}{t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)} \bar{e}_{3, \mathrm{I}}^{2}\right]\right\}, \tag{B.12}
$$
where $\bar{M}_{\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{I}(\mathrm{II})}$ etc. are the order parameters in the phases I (II) at $\bar{H}=\bar{H}_{c}$. The integrations in the two terms on Eq. (B.11) can be done by making the same procedures leading to Eq. (B.10). As the result, we finally arrive at
$$
\Delta \bar{S}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\bar{M}_{\bar{H}, \mathrm{II}}^{2}-\bar{M}_{\bar{H}=0, \mathrm{I}}^{2}\right]-\frac{r}{2 t_{\mathrm{m}}(1)}\left[\bar{e}_{3, \bar{H}, \mathrm{II}}^{2}-\bar{e}_{3, \bar{H}=0, \mathrm{I}}^{2}\right]. \tag{B.13}
$$

The entropy changes $\Delta \bar{S}$ given by Eqs. (B.10) and (B.13) are essentially the same as the one given by Eqs. (22)-(24). It is noted that the Maxwell relation gives only the entropy change in the ferromagnetic system $\Delta \bar{S}_{\mathrm{f}}$ when the ferromagnetic and martensitic systems do not interact with each other, i.e., $g=p=q=0$, for which $D_{12}=0$.
