# Spin-Lattice Coupling Coefficients $G_{i j}$ for
## Trigonal $Al_{2} O_{3}: Fe^{3+}$ and $ZnSiF_{6} \cdot 6 H_{2} O: Ni^{2+}$ Crystals $^{2}$ )

BY
WEN-CHEN ZHENG

By expressing the six spin-lattice coupling coefficients $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ in trigonal symmetry in terms of the coefficients $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ and $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ in cubic symmetry, the coefficients $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ for trigonal $\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}: \mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ and $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ crystals are calculated from the values of $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ and $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ obtained from a simple and uniform method within the framework of cubic symmetry approximation. The results for the $\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}: \mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ crystal is in agreement with the experimental findings, whereas for $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$, the agreement for part of coefficients is poor. The reasons for the disparity are discussed.

Выразая 6 константы $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ спин-решеточной связи тригонльной симметрии через константы $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ и $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ кубической сумметрии, расчётны константы $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ для тригонльным $\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}: \mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ и $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ кристаллам на основе величин $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ и $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ которые следуют из нашего лаконичного и единого образа при аппроксимции кубической симметрии. Расчётные результаты для кристаллу $\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}: \mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ совпадают с экспериментальными данными, однако парциальные константы для $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ плохо совпадают с экспериментальными результатами. Вопроси о эту погрешность были обсужденны.

## 1. Introduction

The interaction of phonons with paramagnetic spin systems which is characterised by the spin-lattice coupling coefficients $G_{i j}$ is of importance because the studies for these coefficients $G_{i j}$ can help us understand the mechanisms of relaxation processes and zero-field splitting. In the past few decades, a great many works have been done for the theoretical calculations of these coefficients. Among them, a uniform and simple method has been developed by us $[1,2]$ for calculating the coefficients $G_{11}$ and $G_{44}$ for $\mathrm{d}^{n}$ ions in cubic symmetry. By using the method, we calculated the coefficients $G_{11}$ and $G_{44}$ for $\mathrm{d}^{3}, \mathrm{~d}^{5}$, and $\mathrm{d}^{8}$ ions in some crystals with regular octahedron structure from the distinctive high-order perturbation formulas of zero-field splitting. All the calculated results show good agreement with the experimental findings [2 to 5]. However, when the symmetry is lowered, the number of independent coupling coefficients $G_{i j}$ increases and the calculation method becomes substantially complicated and difficult. Fortunately, if the low-symmetry distortion is small, in some cases the spin-lattice coupling coefficients $G_{i j}$ in low symmetry can be expressed approximately in terms of the coefficients $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ and $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ in cubic symmetry by applying the appropriate coordinate transformation. For example, Clare and Devine [6] calculated the six coefficients $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ for the trigonal ruby crystal from the values of $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ and $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of the cubic $\mathrm{MgO}: \mathrm{Cr}^{3+}$ crystal by transforming the values for $\mathrm{MgO}: \mathrm{Cr}^{3+}$ into trigonal coordinates.

The calculated results are in good agreement with the observed values. It appears that in some low-symmetry cases, our simple method can also be applicable to the calculation of the coefficients $G_{ij}$ by expressing them in terms of the values of $G_{11}^\mathrm{c}$ and $G_{44}^\mathrm{c}$ with a cubic symmetry approximation. In this paper, we will calculate the six coefficients $G_{ij}^\mathrm{t}$ for trigonal $\mathrm{Al_2O_3:Fe^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{ZnSiF_6 \cdot 6H_2O:Ni^{2+}}$ crystals by using the above simplified cubic model, in which the values of $G_{11}^\mathrm{c}$ and $G_{44}^\mathrm{c}$ are obtained from our method for both the crystals with the approximation of cubic symmetry. The results for $\mathrm{Al_2O_3:Fe^{3+}}$ show good agreement with the observed ones, suggesting that the simplified cubic model is true for this crystal. The results for the $\mathrm{ZnSiF_6 \cdot 6H_2O:Ni^{2+}}$ crystal are also compared with the experimental values and the disparity in part of the coefficients $G_{ij}^\mathrm{t}$, which does not mean the failure of this cubic model and our method of calculating the coefficients $G_{11}^\mathrm{c}$ and $G_{44}^\mathrm{c}$, is discussed.

## 2. Calculation of the Coefficients $G_{ij}^\mathrm{t}$ for $\mathrm{Al_2O_3:Fe^{3+}}$ Crystal

In the $\mathrm{Al_2O_3:Fe^{3+}}$ crystal, $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ions substitute for $\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ ions and are surrounding by an oxygen octahedron. The local symmetry of the $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ion is $\mathrm{C_3}$ and can be regarded as $\mathrm{D_{3d}}$ approximately. According to our method, for $\mathrm{d^5}$ ions in a regular octahedron, the spin-lattice coupling coefficients $G_{11}^\mathrm{c}$ and $G_{44}^\mathrm{c}$ obtained from the high-order perturbation formulas of zero-field splitting based on the dominant spin-orbit coupling mechanism are as follows [5]:

$$
G_{11}^\mathrm{c} = \frac{400}{3} \frac{D_q^2 \xi_\mathrm{d}^2}{P^2 G} - \frac{18}{5} eq \left(1 + \frac{3p}{eR_0}\right) \frac{\langle r^2 \rangle \xi_\mathrm{d}^3}{R_0^3 P^2 D}, \tag{1}
$$

$$
G_{44}^\mathrm{c} = -20 \frac{D_q^2 \xi_\mathrm{d}^2}{P^2 G} + \frac{9}{5} eq \left(1 + \frac{3\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{eR_0}}\right) \frac{\langle r^2 \rangle \xi_\mathrm{d}^3}{R_0^3 P^2 D}, \tag{2}
$$

where $\xi_\mathrm{d}$ is spin-orbit coupling coefficient, the point-charge-dipole model is applied and the Trees correction $\alpha$ is taken into account, so

$$
D_q = -eq \left(1 + \frac{5p}{eR_0}\right) \frac{\langle r^4 \rangle}{6R_0^5} \tag{3}
$$

($q$ is the ligand's charge and $p$ the dipole), and

$$
P = 7B + 7C + 2\alpha, \quad D = 17B + 5C + 6\alpha, \quad G = 10B + 5C + 20\alpha. \tag{4}
$$

For the $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ion, from the empirical d orbital obtained from the optical data for many crystals containing the $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ion, we have [7]

$$
\begin{align*}
B_0 &= 1130.22\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}, \quad C_0 = 4111.45\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}, \quad \xi_{\mathrm{d0}} = 588.946\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}, \\
\langle r^2 \rangle_0 &= 1.89039\ \text{at. units}, \quad \langle r^4 \rangle_0 = 11.46485\ \text{at. units}. \tag{5}
\end{align*}
$$

Introducing a parameter $N$ to denote the average covalency reduction, one gets [8]

$$
B = N^4 B_0, \quad C = N^4 C_0, \quad \langle r^k \rangle = N^2 \langle r^k \rangle_0, \quad \xi_\mathrm{d} = N^2 \xi_{\mathrm{d0}}. \tag{6}
$$

The parameters $N$, $p$, and $\alpha$ can be obtained from optical spectra. Thus, from the optical spectra of the $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3:\text{Fe}^{3+}$ crystal [9], we obtain $N = 0.903$, $p = 0.059eR_0$, and $\alpha = 43\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. The comparison of calculated and observed spectra is shown in Table 1. Substituting the parameters $N$, $p$, $\alpha$, and the structural data $R_0\ (= -0.5(R_1 + R_2) = 0.191\ \text{nm}$ [10]) of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ into the above formulas, we find in cubic approximation

$$
G_{11}^\text{c} = 5.25\ \text{cm}^{-1}, \quad G_{44}^\text{c} = -0.93\ \text{cm}^{-1}. \tag{7}
$$

By applying the appropriate coordinate transformation, i.e., rotating the cubic axis toward the $C_3$-axis, the coefficients $G_{ij}^\text{t}$ in trigonal symmetry can be expressed approximately in terms of the coefficients $G_{11}^\text{c}$ and $G_{44}^\text{c}$ as follows [6]:

$$
\begin{align*}
G_{11}^\text{t} &= \frac{1}{4} G_{11}^\text{c} + G_{44}^\text{c}, & G_{12}^\text{t} &= -\frac{1}{4} G_{11}^\text{c} - \frac{1}{3} G_{44}^\text{c}, & G_{13}^\text{t} &= -\frac{2}{3} G_{44}^\text{c}, \\
G_{44}^\text{t} &= \frac{1}{2} G_{11}^\text{c} + \frac{1}{3} G_{44}^\text{c}, & G_{14}^\text{t} &= G_{41}^\text{t} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4} G_{11}^\text{c} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3} G_{44}^\text{c}. \tag{8}
\end{align*}
$$

Thus, the coefficients $G_{ij}^\text{t}$ for the trigonal $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3:\text{Fe}^{3+}$ crystal can be calculated. The results are shown in Table 2. The observed values of $G_{ij}^\text{t}$ are obtained from the uniaxial pressure EPR experiments [11] and the elastic compliance constants $s_{ij}$ of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ crystals [12]. Considering the experimental errors and the approximation of (8), the agreement between calculation and experiment is satisfactory. It appears that the simplified cubic model and our method for calculating the coefficients $G_{11}^\text{c}$ and $G_{44}^\text{c}$ are true for the $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3:\text{Fe}^{3+}$ crystal.

### 3. Calculation of the Coefficients $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ for $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ Crystal

Similarly, for the $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}\left(\mathrm{d}^{8}\right)$ ion in $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ crystals, from our method, the coefficients $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ and $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ for $\mathrm{d}^{8}$ ions in cubic symmetry obtained from the high-order perturbation formulas of zero-field splitting are [2]

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}= & \frac{35}{12} \xi_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}\left[\frac{1}{W_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{W_{2}^{2}}\right]\left(\frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{t}}}{\partial \alpha}\right)_{0}-\frac{3}{2} \xi_{\mathrm{d}}^{3}\left[\frac{1}{W_{1}^{2} W_{3}}-\frac{1}{W_{1} W_{2} W_{3}}\right]\left[\left(\frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{s}}}{\partial \alpha}\right)_{0}\right. \\
& \left.+\frac{4}{5}\left(\frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{t}}}{\partial \alpha}\right)_{0}\right]-\frac{35}{24} \xi_{\mathrm{d}}^{3}\left[\frac{1}{W_{1}^{2} W_{2}}-\frac{2}{W_{1} W_{2}^{2}}-\frac{1}{W_{1}^{3}}\right]\left(\frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{t}}}{\partial \alpha}\right)_{0},{ }^{3)}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}= & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{12} \xi_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}\left[\frac{1}{W_{2}^{2}}-\frac{1}{W_{1}^{2}}\right]\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \beta}\right)_{0}+\frac{1}{2} \xi_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}\left[\frac{1}{W_{1} W_{3}}-\frac{1}{W_{2} W_{3}}\right]\left(\frac{\partial v^{\prime}}{\partial \beta}\right)_{0} \\
& -2 B \xi_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}\left[\frac{1}{W_{2} W_{3} W_{5}}+\frac{1}{W_{2}^{2} W_{5}}-\frac{3}{W_{1} W_{3} W_{4}}-\frac{3}{W_{2} W_{3} W_{4}}\right]\left(\frac{\partial v^{\prime}}{\partial \beta}\right)_{0},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $W_{i}(i=1$ to 5$)$ are the zero-order energy separations [2], and

$$
\left(\frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{t}}}{\partial \alpha}\right)_{0}=\frac{40}{7} D q, \quad\left(\frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{s}}}{\partial \alpha}\right)_{0}=-\frac{12}{7} e q\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle / R_{0}^{3},
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \beta}\right)_{0}=\frac{18 \sqrt{2}}{7} e q\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle / R_{0}^{3}+\frac{60 \sqrt{2}}{7} D q, \\
& \left(\frac{\partial v^{\prime}}{\partial \beta}\right)_{0}=-\frac{12}{7} e q\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle / R_{0}^{3}+\frac{30}{7} D q .
\end{aligned}
$$

Equations (11) and (12) are obtained from the point-charge model by taking only the effect of the ligands of the first coordination sphere into account. In fact, the distances from the paramagnetic ion to the ligands of the second coordination sphere are often so large that the influence of these ligands upon the zero-field splitting may be unimportant, as shown in a similar case in [14]. This point will also be supported by the following calculation, in which the dependences of zero-field splitting on hydrostatic pressure and stress for $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ and $\mathrm{NiSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ crystals can be explained satisfactorily from the contribution of nearest ligands.

By using the empirical d-orbital of the $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ ion [15] and introducing the average covalency reduction parameter $\mathrm{N}$ as above, we have

$$
\begin{gathered}
B=1208 N^{4} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}, \quad C=4459 N^{4} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}, \quad \xi_{\mathrm{d}}=636 N^{2} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}, \\
\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle=1.9804 N^{2} \text { at. units }, \quad\left\langle r^{4}\right\rangle=13.4043 N^{2} \text { at. units . }
\end{gathered}
$$

$^{3)}$ There is a misprint in [2]: the signs of the terms containing $\xi_{\mathrm{d}}^{3}$ in (12) should be changed because the sign of $\xi_{\mathrm{d}}$ in [2] is opposite to that in the formula of zero-field splitting $D$ in the original literature [13].

Spin-Lattice Coupling Coefficients $G_{ij}$ for Trigonal Crystals

Considering that the $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ and $\mathrm{NiSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ crystals have the same crystalline structure and very similar structural parameters [16], as well as that the hydrostatic pressure and stress dependences of zero-field splitting for $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ ions in both crystals are close to each other (for $\mathrm{NiSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}, \mathrm{d} D / \mathrm{d} P=0.834 \times 10^{-4}, \mathrm{~d} D / \mathrm{d} U=-1.66 \times 10^{-4}$, for $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}, \mathrm{d} D / \mathrm{d} P=0.834 \times 10^{-4}, \mathrm{~d} D / \mathrm{d} U=-1.83 \times 10^{-4}$, all are in units of $\mathrm{cm}^{-1} /\left(\mathrm{kg} / \mathrm{cm}^{2}\right)$ [17 to 19], the parameters $N(=0.9), q(=-2 e)$, and $R_{0}(=0.2048 \mathrm{~nm}$, the distance of $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}-\mathrm{O}^{2-}$) for $\mathrm{NiSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ crystal [20] can also be applied to the case of the $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ crystal. Substituting these parameters into the above formulas, one finds

$$
G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}=39 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}, \quad G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}=31 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}. \tag{14}
$$

Inserting these into (8), the six coefficients $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ for the trigonal $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ crystal can be calculated and are shown in Table 2. Comparing the calculated values with the observed ones in [21], it can be seen that the results of $G_{11}^{\mathrm{t}}, G_{44}^{\mathrm{t}}$, and $G_{14}^{\mathrm{t}}$ (or $G_{41}^{\mathrm{t}}$) are in rough agreement with the observed values, but the magnitude and, in particular, the signs of $G_{12}^{\mathrm{t}}$ and $G_{13}^{\mathrm{t}}$ are not consistent with the observed ones (see Table 2). In our opinion, our theoretical values may be more reasonable than the values in [21]. The reasons are as follows:

(i) Because the trigonal distortion is slight for this crystal, the value $\partial D / \partial \beta$ can be calculated from the theoretical value of $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$ or $G_{13}^{\mathrm{t}}$ and from the formula shown in [2], i.e.,

$$
\frac{\partial D}{\partial \beta} \approx\left(\frac{\partial D}{\partial \beta}\right)_{\beta=\beta_{0}}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{6} G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{9}{\sqrt{2}} G_{13}^{\mathrm{t}}=-131 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} / \mathrm{rad}. \tag{15}
$$

The result is very close to the observed values of $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}(\partial D / \partial \beta$ $\approx-111 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} / \mathrm{rad})$ and $\mathrm{NiSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} \quad(\partial D / \partial \beta \approx-128 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} / \mathrm{rad})$ obtained from the above hydrostatic pressure and stress dependences of zero-field splitting by using the method of macroscopic thermodynamic analysis given in [20]. This shows that our results can explain the hydrostatic pressure and stress dependences of EPR spectra for both crystals. However, the value of $\partial D / \partial \beta\left(\approx-57.5 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} / \mathrm{rad}\right)$ in [21] is not consistent with these observed values (by the way from the value of $G_{13}^{\mathrm{t}}$ given in [21], $\partial D / \partial \beta$ is not $-57.5 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} / \mathrm{rad}$, but $14.6 \pm 14 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} / \mathrm{rad}$. Clearly, the error is greater). In addition, considering that $D \approx\left(\beta-\beta_{0}\right)(\partial D / \partial \beta)_{0}$ and that the vibrational contribution to the zero-field splitting due to electron-phonon interaction at low temperature $(T=4.2 \mathrm{~K})$ can be omitted, we can obtain $\beta-\beta_{0} \approx 0.06^{\circ}$ from the theoretical value of $\partial D / \partial \beta$ and the observed value of $D$ $\left(=-0.134 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\right.$ [19]). The result can be supported by the following fact. From the trigonal distortion $\beta-\beta_{0}\left(=-0.07 \pm 0.07^{\circ}\right.$ [16]) of the $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ crystal at room temperature (we assume that the distortion $\beta-\beta_{0}$ remains unchanged when the $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ ion replaces the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ ion in $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ ) and that $\left(=0.06^{\circ}\right)$ at $4.2 \mathrm{~K}$, one can estimate that the thermal expansion is $\partial \beta / \partial T \approx-1 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{K}$. The sign and the order of magnitude of the result are close to those of $\mathrm{NiSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ crystal isomorphous to $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ crystal $\left(\partial \beta / \partial T \approx-2.9 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{K}\right.$ [17]) and can be regarded as reasonable.

(ii) Since the trigonal distortion for $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ is slight and hence the nearest surrounding of $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ ion is close to a regular octahedron, the value of $G_{13}^{\mathrm{t}}$ should be approximately equal to that of $G_{31}^{\mathrm{t}}\left(=-G_{11}^{\mathrm{t}}-G_{12}^{\mathrm{t}}\right.$ [6]). The point is consistent with our results and those for $\mathrm{Al}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3}: \mathrm{Cr}^{3+}$ [6], but is evidently different from those in [21] $\left(2.3 \pm 2.2 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}\right.$ compared with $-47.3 \pm 4.9 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$).

(iii) According to formula (6) in [21], the sign of $G_{13}^{\mathrm{t}}$ should be negative for $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6}$ $\cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ crystals, but the observed value in [21] is positive. It is noteworthy that our result of $G_{13}^{\mathrm{t}}$ is just a negative value.

From the above mentioned facts, the disparity for part of coefficients $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ does not mean the failure of the simplified cubic model and our calculation method for the coefficients $G_{11}^{\mathrm{c}}$ and $G_{44}^{\mathrm{c}}$. Considering that the experiment and data processing are complex for obtaining the observed values of six coefficients $G_{i j}^{\mathrm{t}}$ in trigonal $\mathrm{ZnSiF}_{6} \cdot 6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}: \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ crystal, there may be, in our opinion, some errors in experiment and processing in [21]. This point remains to be further verified.
