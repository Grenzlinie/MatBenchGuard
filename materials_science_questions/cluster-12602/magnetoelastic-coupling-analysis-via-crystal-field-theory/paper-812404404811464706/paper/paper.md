# Shifts of Curie temperature of epitaxial dysprosium films
C. Dufour $^{a, *}$ , K. Dumesnil $^{a}$ , Ph. Mangin $^{a}$ , G. Marchal $^{a}$ , M. Hennion $^{b}$
$^{a}$ L.M.P.S.M. Université Henri Poincaré-Nancy 1, B.P. 239, 54506 Vandoeuvre Cedex, France
$^{b}$ L.L.B., C.E.N. Saclay, 91191 Gif-sur-Yvette Cedex, France

## Abstract
From magnetic measurements and neutron scattering, dysprosium epitaxial films present an enhanced Curie temperature, when they are grown on erbium, and a reduced (even suppressed) one, when they are grown on yttrium. A magnetostrictive model reproduces the continuous increase of the Curie temperature with the $c$-axis strain induced by epitaxy.

Bulk hexagonal dysprosium exhibits at $T_{\mathrm{N}}=176 \mathrm{~K}$ a magnetic ordering from the paramagnetic state to a helical phase. The helical-ferromagnetic transition, driven by magnetostrictive effects, occurs at $T_{\mathrm{C}}=89 \mathrm{~K}$ [1]. Recently, spectacular shifts of the Curie temperature have been observed in epitaxial dysprosium films. For instance, the Curie temperature of dysprosium can be increased by several tens of degrees when dysprosium is grown on lutecium [2] or on erbium [3] (whose lattice parameters are smaller than those of dysprosium). In contrast, the ferromagnetic transition can be suppressed when it is grown on yttrium [3,4] (whose lattice parameters are larger than those of dysprosium).

In this paper, we present the experimental and the calculated evolution of the Curie temperature versus the $c$-axis induced epitaxial strain for dysprosium films of various thicknesses grown on yttrium and erbium.

The (0001) epitaxial dysprosium films were grown by Molecular Beam Epitaxy on $(11 \overline{2} 0)$ sapphire substrates. Following the experimental process proposed by Kwo et al. [5], a (110) niobium buffer was first deposited on the substrate. The dysprosium films, ranging in thickness from $100 \AA$ to $3500 \AA$ are sandwiched between two yttrium or two erbium thick layers.

Room temperature X-ray diffraction shows the good cristal quality with mosaic width around $0.3^{\circ}$ and enables one to determine the $\epsilon_{33} c$-axis epitaxial strain from the values of the $c$ parameters. This strain is positive when dysprosium is epitaxially deposited on erbium and negative when it is epitaxially deposited on yttrium. Its absolute value depends on the thickness of the dysprosium film.

The zero-field Curie temperature was determined by neutron diffraction and magnetic measurements (by extrapolation to zero field of the values of the critical field for which the ferromagnetic transition occurs) for a set of dysprosium films grown on yttrium and on erbium.

In fact, the magnetic phases present in dysprosium are the consequences of a balance between various energies: exchange, anisotropy and magnetostriction. In order to get a better understanding of the influence of epitaxy on these different terms (and therefore on the Curie temperature of dysprosium), we have developped a magnetostrictive model [6]. Let us consider a system X/Dy/X (where X=Y or Er). The total energy of this sandwich system is the sum of the elastic energies in dysprosium and in yttrium or erbium, of the magnetoelastic energy in dysprosium and the exchange energy in dysprosium. The exchange barrier which has to be overcome by the magnetostrictive energy to drive the ferromagnetic transition can be determined from exchange integral in the classical three planes model [7]. In the calculation of the magnetostrictive energies, three symmetry strains have to be taken into account: $\epsilon_{\alpha 1}$, $\epsilon_{\alpha 2}$ and $\epsilon_{\gamma}$ [8]. They correspond respectively to a pure expansion, to a cylindrical distortion and to an orthorhombic distortion along the easy magnetisation axis (a direction) in the basal plane.

The minimisation of the total energy respect to the strains permits one to determine the equilibrium strains and thus to calculate the thermal dependance of the elastic and magnetoelastic energy terms in the ferromagnetic and in the helical state.

One can draw the different energies versus temperature for bulk dysprosium and for the various epitaxial dysprosium films [6] and evaluate the influence of epitaxy on the various energies. In fact, the modification of the magnetostrictive energy is not the only consequence of epitaxy. A second consequence is the occurrence of an elastic energy in dysprosium and in the material by which it is clamped by: any strain in dysprosium is transmitted to the other rare earth, which increases the elastic energy in both materials and delays the magnetic transition driven by the magnetoelastic energy. A third consequence is the modification of the exchange term itself: the exchange energy is indeed very sensitive to details of the Fermi surface which depends itself on the lattice parameters.

The ferromagnetic transition occurs when the sum of the exchange and the magnetostrictive energies in the helical state becomes larger than the sum of the same energies in the ferromagnetic state. The Curie temperature, determined through this model, is shown versus the c-axis epitaxial strains in Fig. 2 (continuous line). The magnetostriction constants used in the model have been chosen from the values available in the literature for bulk dysprosium, in order to obtain the bulk Curie temperature for zero strain. The evolution of the calculated values is in qualitative agreement with the experimental results.

One should stress that the $\alpha$ distortions are the only strains induced by epitaxy in the dysprosium layers. The increase of $T_{\mathrm{C}}$ observed in some samples is mainly due to the contribution of epitaxial $\alpha$ strains to the magnetoelastic energy. The $\gamma$ orthorhombic distortion, which is usually believed to be the main driving force to the ferromagnetic transition in the bulk element, is not favoured by epitaxy. This clamping of the $\gamma$ mode contributes in any case to the stabilisation of the helical phase.
